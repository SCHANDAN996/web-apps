# BODMAS Explained Simply (Feynman Technique)

The Feynman Technique means: if you can explain an idea in simple words, as if to a child, you truly understand it. Let's do that with BODMAS.

## The Sweet Shop Problem

Imagine you run a sweet shop. A customer gives you this instruction:

"Take 2 boxes of laddoos, each box has 10 laddoos, and add 5 loose laddoos."

You write it as a maths expression:

**2 × 10 + 5**

Now two shop assistants calculate it differently:

- Assistant A goes left to right: 2 × 10 = 20, then 20 + 5 = **25** ✓
- Assistant B adds first: 10 + 5 = 15, then 2 × 15 = **30** ✗

Both used the same numbers but got different answers! A shop cannot run like this — maths must give **one correct answer every time**. That is why the world agreed on a fixed **order of operations**. In India we call it **BODMAS**.

## What BODMAS Stands For

| Letter | Meaning | Do it |
|---|---|---|
| **B** | Brackets ( ), { }, [ ] | First |
| **O** | Orders (powers, roots, e.g. 5², √9) | Second |
| **D** | Division (÷) | Third — left to right with M |
| **M** | Multiplication (×) | Third — left to right with D |
| **A** | Addition (+) | Last — left to right with S |
| **S** | Subtraction (−) | Last — left to right with A |

**Important:** D and M have equal rank — solve whichever comes first from the left. Same for A and S.

## Example 1 (the shop problem)

2 × 10 + 5
= 20 + 5 (multiplication before addition)
= **25**

## Example 2 (with a bracket)

2 × (10 + 5)
= 2 × 15 (bracket first)
= **30**

See how the bracket changes the answer completely!

## Example 3 (exam style)

24 ÷ 4 × 2 + 6 − 3
= 6 × 2 + 6 − 3 (division first, left to right)
= 12 + 6 − 3 (then multiplication)
= 18 − 3 (then addition)
= **15**

## The One-Line Memory Trick

**"Brackets first, then powers, then × and ÷ left-to-right, then + and − left-to-right."**

## Common Traps in Exams

1. **Adding before multiplying:** In 3 + 4 × 2, the answer is 11, not 14.
2. **Ignoring left-to-right:** In 20 ÷ 5 × 2, do division first (it comes first from the left): 4 × 2 = 8, not 20 ÷ 10 = 2.
3. **Bar (vinculum):** In expressions like 5 + 3̅ (a bar over numbers), treat the bar as a bracket and solve it first.

## Test Yourself (Explain It Back)

Close this file and explain to a friend — or to yourself aloud — why 6 + 3 × 4 equals 18 and not 36. If you can do it in one simple sentence, you have mastered BODMAS the Feynman way.
