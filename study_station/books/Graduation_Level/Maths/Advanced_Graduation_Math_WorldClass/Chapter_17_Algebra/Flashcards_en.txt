Card 1
Front: What is the standard form of a quadratic equation?
Back: ax² + bx + c = 0, where a, b, c are real numbers and a ≠ 0. If a = 0, the equation becomes linear, not quadratic.

Card 2
Front: What is the quadratic formula for solving ax² + bx + c = 0?
Back: x = [−b ± √(b² − 4ac)] / (2a)

Card 3
Front: What is the discriminant of a quadratic equation?
Back: D = b² − 4ac. It tells us the nature of the roots without solving the equation.

Card 4
Front: If D > 0, what is the nature of the roots?
Back: Two distinct real roots. (If D is a perfect square, the roots are rational; otherwise irrational.)

Card 5
Front: If D = 0, what is the nature of the roots?
Back: Two equal (coincident) real roots: x = −b / (2a).

Card 6
Front: If D < 0, what is the nature of the roots?
Back: No real roots — the roots are complex (imaginary).

Card 7
Front: What is the sum of the roots of ax² + bx + c = 0?
Back: Sum of roots = −b/a

Card 8
Front: What is the product of the roots of ax² + bx + c = 0?
Back: Product of roots = c/a

Card 9
Front: How do you form a quadratic equation from its roots α and β?
Back: x² − (α + β)x + αβ = 0, i.e., x² − (sum of roots)x + (product of roots) = 0.

Card 10
Front: Solve by factorisation: x² − 5x + 6 = 0
Back: (x − 2)(x − 3) = 0, so x = 2 or x = 3. Check: sum = 5 = −(−5)/1, product = 6 = 6/1.

Card 11
Front: What are the roots of x² − 9 = 0?
Back: x = 3 and x = −3, since x² = 9. This is a difference of squares: (x − 3)(x + 3) = 0.

Card 12
Front: How do you check whether a given value is a root of a quadratic equation?
Back: Substitute the value for x. If the left side equals 0, it is a root. Example: for x² − 4x + 3 = 0, putting x = 1 gives 1 − 4 + 3 = 0, so 1 is a root.

Card 13
Front: What is the maximum number of real roots a quadratic equation can have?
Back: Two. A polynomial equation of degree n has at most n real roots.

Card 14
Front: For what value of k does x² + kx + 9 = 0 have equal roots?
Back: Equal roots need D = 0: k² − 36 = 0, so k = ±6.

Card 15
Front: If one root of x² − 7x + k = 0 is 3, find k and the other root.
Back: Sum of roots = 7, so other root = 7 − 3 = 4. Product = k = 3 × 4 = 12.

Card 16
Front: What is the discriminant and nature of roots of 2x² − 4x + 3 = 0?
Back: D = (−4)² − 4(2)(3) = 16 − 24 = −8 < 0, so the equation has no real roots.

Card 17
Front: The sum of a number and its reciprocal is 10/3. What equation does the number x satisfy?
Back: x + 1/x = 10/3, which gives 3x² − 10x + 3 = 0. Solving: x = 3 or x = 1/3.

Card 18
Front: What is a common exam trap with the quadratic formula?
Back: Sign errors: forgetting that −b changes sign, or mishandling a negative b. Also, the whole numerator is divided by 2a, not just the square-root part.

Card 19
Front: If the roots of a quadratic equation are equal, what is each root in terms of a and b?
Back: Each root = −b/(2a), since D = 0 makes the square-root term vanish.

Card 20
Front: Which method is fastest when the equation factorises easily, and which always works?
Back: Factorisation (splitting the middle term) is fastest when roots are simple rationals; the quadratic formula works for every quadratic equation.
