# The Feynman Technique: Compound Interest Unveiled

Imagine you plant an apple seed. In the first year, it grows into a small tree and yields 10 apples. 
Under Simple Interest, someone takes away those 10 apples, and in the second year, only your original tree yields another 10 apples. Your reward is forever locked at 10 apples per year, regardless of time.

Now imagine Compound Interest. When your tree yields 10 apples, you do not eat them; you plant the seeds of all 10 apples! 
In the second year, your original tree produces 10 apples, but the 10 new saplings also begin to bear fruit! 
In the third year, the fruits of those fruits begin producing. 
This is why Albert Einstein famously dubbed compound interest the "Eighth Wonder of the World": He who understands it, earns it; he who does not, pays it.

### The Compounding Tree Visualized
Let's see what happens to Rs 1,000 at 10% per annum over 3 years:
1. **Year 1:**
   - Principal Rs 1,000 generates 10% = Rs 100.
   - Total interest so far = Rs 100. (Notice: for the 1st year, CI = SI exactly!)
2. **Year 2:**
   - The principal Rs 1,000 generates its standard Rs 100.
   - But the Year 1 interest of Rs 100 ALSO earns 10% = Rs 10!
   - Total interest for Year 2 alone = 100 + 10 = Rs 110.
   - Cumulative 2-year CI = 100 + 110 = Rs 210.
   - Compare with SI: 100 + 100 = Rs 200. The extra Rs 10 is the magic of compounding!
3. **Year 3:**
   - Original principal generates Rs 100.
   - The previous two Rs 100s both generate Rs 10 each = Rs 20.
   - The tiny Rs 10 from Year 2 generates 10% = Re 1!
   - Total interest for Year 3 alone = 100 + 20 + 1 = Rs 121.
   - Cumulative 3-year CI = 100 + 110 + 121 = Rs 331.
   - Compare with SI = Rs 300. The extra Rs 31 is (3 * 10 + 1), which directly corresponds to our 3 : 3 : 1 ratio!

### The Golden Multiplier Rule
Because interest multiplies rather than adds:
- If your money doubles in 5 years, it does NOT triple in 10 years; it QUADRUPLES (2 * 2 = 4 times)!
- In 15 years, it becomes 2 * 2 * 2 = 8 times!
- In 20 years, it becomes 16 times!
Compound interest transforms time into pure exponential power.
