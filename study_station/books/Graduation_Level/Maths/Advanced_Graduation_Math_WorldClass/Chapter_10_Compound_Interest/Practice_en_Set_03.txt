51. A sum of ₹8,000 is invested at 10% per annum compound interest, compounded annually. What is the amount after 2 years?
(a) ₹9,800 (b) ₹9,600 (c) ₹9,700 (d) ₹9,680
Answer: (d)
Solution: The tempting wrong option ₹9,600 comes from simple interest (8,000 + 2 × 800). For compound interest, Amount = 8,000 × (1.1)² = 8,000 × 1.21 = 9,680, so the answer is 9680.
Source: NCERT Class 8 Mathematics

52. The compound interest on ₹10,000 at 20% per annum for 2 years, compounded annually, is:
(a) ₹4,000 (b) ₹4,400 (c) ₹4,200 (d) ₹4,600
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is ₹4,000, which is the simple interest (10,000 × 20 × 2 / 100). Compound interest = 10,000 × (1.2)² − 10,000 = 14,400 − 10,000 = 4,400, so the answer is 4400.
Source: NCERT Class 8 Mathematics

53. The difference between compound interest and simple interest on ₹5,000 at 10% per annum for 2 years is:
(a) ₹25 (b) ₹75 (c) ₹50 (d) ₹100
Answer: (c)
Solution: For 2 years, CI − SI = P × (r/100)² = 5,000 × (0.1)² = 5,000 × 0.01 = 50. The trap ₹100 comes from wrongly doubling this difference, so the answer is 50.

54. At what rate per annum will ₹4,000 amount to ₹4,840 in 2 years under compound interest?
(a) 8% (b) 15% (c) 12% (d) 10%
Answer: (d)
Solution: 4,840 / 4,000 = 1.21 = (1.1)², so the rate is 10%. The trap 12% comes from mishandling the total interest of 840, so the answer is 10%.

59. The difference between CI and SI on a sum at 8% per annum for 2 years is ₹32. The sum is:
(a) ₹4,000 (b) ₹5,000 (c) ₹6,000 (d) ₹8,000
Answer: (b)
Solution: CI − SI = P × (0.08)² = P × 0.0064 = 32, giving P = 32 / 0.0064 = 5,000. The trap ₹4,000 comes from using 0.008 instead of 0.0064, so the answer is 5000.
Source: PYQ-style

60. A sum doubles itself in 5 years at compound interest. In how many years will it become 8 times itself at the same rate?
(a) 15 years (b) 25 years (c) 20 years (d) 10 years
Answer: (a)
Solution: 8 = 2³, so it needs 3 doubling periods = 3 × 5 = 15 years. The trap 20 years comes from assuming linear growth (4 × 5), so the answer is 15 years.
Source: PYQ-style

61. The compound interest on ₹25,000 at 12% per annum for 2 years, compounded annually, exceeds the simple interest by:
(a) ₹360 (b) ₹300 (c) ₹400 (d) ₹420
Answer: (a)
Solution: Difference = P × (r/100)² = 25,000 × (0.12)² = 25,000 × 0.0144 = 360. The trap ₹300 comes from using r = 12 directly instead of squaring the decimal, so the answer is 360.
Source: PYQ-style

63. The population of a town is 50,000. It increases by 10% in the first year and decreases by 10% in the second year. The population after 2 years is:
(a) 50,500 (b) 49,500 (c) 50,000 (d) 49,000
Answer: (b)
Solution: Population = 50,000 × 1.1 × 0.9 = 50,000 × 0.99 = 49,500. The trap 50,000 assumes the increase and decrease cancel out, so the answer is 49500.

64. The value of a machine depreciates at 10% per annum. If its present value is ₹81,000, its value 2 years ago was:

Answer: (a)
Solution: Past value = 81,000 / (0.9)² = 81,000 / 0.81 = 1,00,000. The trap ₹99,000 comes from adding back 10% of 81,000 twice, so the answer is 100000.

65. The compound interest on ₹5,000 at 10% per annum for 1.5 years, interest compounded annually, is:
(a) ₹775 (b) ₹750 (c) ₹762.50 (d) ₹800
Answer: (a)
Solution: Amount after 1 year = 5,000 × 1.1 = 5,500; interest for the next half year = 5,500 × 10 × 0.5 / 100 = 275; total CI = 500 + 275 = 775. The trap ₹750 applies simple interest on 5,000 for the whole period, so the answer is 775.

66. A sum invested at compound interest amounts to ₹4,410 in 2 years and ₹4,851 in 3 years. The rate of interest per annum is:
(a) 8% (b) 9% (c) 10% (d) 12%
Answer: (c)
Solution: Rate = (4,851 − 4,410) / 4,410 × 100 = 441 / 4,410 × 100 = 10%. The trap 9% comes from dividing 441 by 4,851 instead of 4,410, so the answer is 10%.

67. A sum amounts to ₹7,260 in 2 years and ₹7,986 in 3 years at compound interest. The sum is:

71. If the compound interest on a sum for 2 years at 10% per annum is ₹2,100, the simple interest on the same sum at the same rate for 2 years is:

72. A man borrows ₹20,000 at 10% per annum compound interest and repays ₹11,000 at the end of the first year. How much must he pay at the end of the second year to clear the debt?
(a) ₹13,000 (b) ₹12,000 (c) ₹12,500 (d) ₹12,100
Answer: (d)
Solution: After 1 year, debt = 20,000 × 1.1 = 22,000; after paying 11,000, balance = 11,000. After year 2, balance = 11,000 × 1.1 = 12,100. The trap ₹12,000 ignores interest on the balance, so the answer is 12100.

74. A sum becomes 1.44 times itself in 2 years at compound interest. The rate of interest per annum is:

Solution: (1 + r/100)² = 1.44, so 1 + r/100 = 1.2, giving r = 20%. The trap 22% comes from halving 44%, so the answer is 20%.

75. The simple interest on a sum for 2 years at 10% per annum is ₹1,600. The compound interest on the same sum at the same rate for 2 years is:
(a) ₹1,680 (b) ₹1,640 (c) ₹1,600 (d) ₹1,720
Answer: (a)
Solution: Principal = 1,600 × 100 / (10 × 2) = 8,000. CI = 8,000 × (1.21 − 1) = 8,000 × 0.21 = 1,680. The trap ₹1,640 adds only ₹40 extra instead of the correct ₹80 (interest on the first year's interest of 800), so the answer is 1680.
Source: PYQ-style
