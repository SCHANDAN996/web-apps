101. What is the compound interest on ₹8,000 at 10% per annum for 2 years, compounded annually?
(a) ₹1,680 (b) ₹1,600 (c) ₹1,700 (d) ₹1,720
Answer: (a)
Solution: The tempting wrong option ₹1,600 is the simple interest, which ignores interest on the first year's interest. Amount = 8000 × (1.1)² = 8000 × 1.21 = ₹9,680, so CI = 9680 − 8000 = ₹1,680.
Source: NCERT Class 8 Mathematics (Comparing Quantities)

102. The difference between compound interest and simple interest on ₹4,000 at 5% per annum for 2 years is:
(a) ₹5 (b) ₹15 (c) ₹10 (d) ₹20
Answer: (c)
Solution: Many students compute the full CI and SI separately and slip in arithmetic; the direct formula is faster. Difference for 2 years = P × r²/100² = 4000 × 25/10000 = ₹10.
Source: PYQ-style

103. What is the compound interest on ₹5,000 at 20% per annum for 2 years, compounded annually?
(a) ₹2,000 (b) ₹2,200 (c) ₹2,100 (d) ₹2,400
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap option ₹2,000 is the simple interest (5000 × 20 × 2/100). Amount = 5000 × (1.2)² = 5000 × 1.44 = ₹7,200, so CI = 7200 − 5000 = ₹2,200.

104. What is the compound interest on ₹10,000 at 10% per annum for 1 year, compounded half-yearly?
(a) ₹1,025 (b) ₹1,000 (c) ₹1,050 (d) ₹1,100
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap option ₹1,000 comes from using annual compounding; half-yearly compounding halves the rate to 5% and doubles the periods to 2. Amount = 10000 × (1.05)² = ₹11,025, so CI = ₹1,025.

106. A sum of ₹6,400 becomes ₹8,100 in 2 years at compound interest, compounded annually. The rate of interest per annum is:
(a) 10% (b) 12.5% (c) 12% (d) 15%
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is to divide the total gain 1700 by 6400 and halve it, which mixes SI logic with CI. Ratio 8100/6400 = 81/64, so (1 + r/100) = √(81/64) = 9/8, giving r = 12.5%.
Source: PYQ-style

107. In how many years will ₹1,000 become ₹1,331 at 10% per annum compound interest?
(a) 2 years (b) 4 years (c) 3 years (d) 5 years
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is to divide 331 by 100 and guess roughly 3.3 years; CI grows multiplicatively, not linearly. Since 1331/1000 = 1.331 = (1.1)³, the time is 3 years.

108. The compound interest on a certain sum at 10% per annum for 2 years is ₹2,100. The sum is:
(a) ₹9,000 (b) ₹10,500 (c) ₹10,000 (d) ₹11,000
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is to use 2100 = P × 20/100 (SI logic), giving ₹10,500. With CI, P × (1.21 − 1) = 2100, so P = 2100/0.21 = ₹10,000.

109. The difference between compound interest and simple interest on ₹5,000 at 10% per annum for 3 years is:
(a) ₹155 (b) ₹150 (c) ₹160 (d) ₹165
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is to extend the 2-year difference formula to 3 years, which misses the third year's compounding on accumulated interest. Difference = P × r² × (300 + r)/100³ = 5000 × 100 × 310/1000000 = ₹155.
Source: PYQ-style

110. The population of a town is 10,000. It increases by 10% in the first year and decreases by 10% in the second year. The population at the end of 2 years is:
(a) 10,000 (b) 10,100 (c) 9,800 (d) 9,900
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap option 10,000 assumes the increase and decrease cancel out, but the 10% decrease acts on the larger base 11,000. Population = 10000 × 1.1 × 0.9 = 9,900.
Source: NCERT Class 8 Mathematics (Comparing Quantities)

111. A machine worth ₹16,000 depreciates at 25% per annum. Its value after 2 years is:
(a) ₹9,000 (b) ₹8,000 (c) ₹9,500 (d) ₹10,000
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap option ₹8,000 subtracts 50% of the original value, treating depreciation as linear. Value = 16000 × (0.75)² = 16000 × 0.5625 = ₹9,000.

Solution: The trap option 8% ignores the extra compounding within the year. Effective rate = (1.04)² − 1 = 1.0816 − 1 = 0.0816, i.e. 8.16%.

122. A sum amounts to ₹14,400 in 2 years and ₹17,280 in 3 years at compound interest. The rate of interest and the sum are:
(a) 15%, ₹12,000 (b) 25%, ₹9,000 (c) 20%, ₹12,000 (d) 20%, ₹10,000
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is to take the difference ₹2,880 as simple interest and divide by ₹14,400 incorrectly framed; the ratio of successive amounts gives the growth factor directly. Rate = 17280/14400 − 1 = 1.2 − 1 = 20%, and P = 14400/1.44 = ₹10,000.
Source: PYQ-style

123. The difference between the compound interest compounded half-yearly and the interest compounded annually on ₹10,000 at 10% per annum for 1 year is:

Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap option ₹50 doubles the correct gap by misplacing the rate. Half-yearly CI = 10000 × (1.05)² − 10000 = ₹1,025; annual CI = ₹1,000; difference = ₹25.

124. The difference between compound interest and simple interest on ₹2,500 at 20% per annum for 3 years is:
(a) ₹300 (b) ₹310 (c) ₹340 (d) ₹320
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is to use the 2-year difference formula (which gives ₹100) or to mis-add the third year's extra interest. Difference = P × r² × (300 + r)/100³ = 2500 × 400 × 320/1000000 = ₹320.
Source: PYQ-style

125. A machine depreciates at 10% per annum. If its present value is ₹81,000, its value 2 years ago was:
(a) ₹90,000 (b) ₹95,000 (c) ₹1,02,000 (d) ₹1,00,000
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap option ₹90,000 adds back 10% of the present value twice, but depreciation acts on a shrinking base, so we must divide, not add. Value 2 years ago = 81000/(0.9)² = 81000/0.81 = ₹1,00,000.
Source: PYQ-style
