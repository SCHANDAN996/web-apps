26. The compound interest on ₹8,000 at 10% per annum for 2 years is:
(a) ₹1,600 (b) ₹1,800 (c) ₹1,720 (d) ₹1,680
Answer: (d)
Solution: The tempting wrong option ₹1,600 is the simple interest, which ignores interest on interest. Amount = 8000 × (1.1)² = 8000 × 1.21 = ₹9,680, so CI = 9680 − 8000, and the answer is ₹1,680.
Source: NCERT Class 8 Mathematics

27. A sum of ₹12,000 is invested at 5% per annum compound interest. The amount after 2 years is:
(a) ₹13,320 (b) ₹13,023 (c) ₹13,230 (d) ₹13,200
Answer: (c)
Solution: The wrong option ₹13,200 comes from adding simple interest of ₹1,200 directly. Amount = 12000 × (1.05)² = 12000 × 1.1025, so the answer is ₹13,230.

28. The difference between compound interest and simple interest on ₹10,000 at 10% per annum for 2 years is:
(a) ₹100 (b) ₹200 (c) ₹150 (d) ₹50
Answer: (a)
Solution: For 2 years, CI − SI = P × (r/100)² = 10000 × (0.1)² = ₹100. The trap is computing full CI (₹2,100) and SI (₹2,000) and mis-subtracting; the answer is ₹100.
Source: PYQ-style

29. At what rate percent per annum will ₹4,000 amount to ₹4,840 in 2 years, compounded annually?
(a) 9% (b) 10% (c) 8% (d) 12%
Answer: (b)
Solution: 4840/4000 = 1.21 = (1.1)², so 1 + r/100 = 1.1. The trap is dividing the total interest ₹840 by 2 years and principal to get 10.5% by simple-interest logic; the answer is 10%.

32. A sum becomes ₹6,600 in 2 years and ₹7,260 in 3 years at compound interest. The rate of interest per annum is:
(a) 10% (b) 9% (c) 8% (d) 12%
Answer: (a)
Solution: Interest earned in the third year = 7260 − 6600 = ₹660 on ₹6,600, so rate = 660/6600 × 100 = 10%. The trap is dividing 660 by 7260, which gives about 9.1%; the answer is 10%.

Solution: 8100/6400 = 81/64 = (9/8)², and 1 + 12.5/100 = 9/8, so time = 2 years. The trap is assuming 1 year since 6400 × 9/8 = 7200 looks close to 8100; the answer is 2 years.

39. A man borrows ₹20,000 at 10% per annum compound interest and repays ₹10,000 at the end of the first year. How much should he pay at the end of the second year to clear the debt?
(a) ₹12,100 (b) ₹13,200 (c) ₹12,000 (d) ₹11,000
Answer: (b)
Solution: After 1 year the debt is 20000 × 1.1 = ₹22,000; after paying ₹10,000, ₹12,000 remains, which grows to 12000 × 1.1 = ₹13,200. The trap is charging interest only on ₹10,000 for the second year, giving ₹11,000; the answer is ₹13,200.

Answer: (b)

42. A sum doubles itself in 4 years at compound interest. In how many years will it become 8 times itself at the same rate?
(a) 10 years (b) 12 years (c) 8 years (d) 16 years
Answer: (b)
Solution: 8 = 2³, so it needs 3 doubling periods = 3 × 4 = 12 years. The trap is linear thinking: 8 times means 4 × 4 = 16 years; the answer is 12 years.
