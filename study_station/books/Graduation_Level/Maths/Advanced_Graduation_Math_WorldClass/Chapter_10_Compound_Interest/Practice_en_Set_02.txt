Q26. (Medium) The difference between compound interest and simple interest on a certain sum of money for 2 years at 12% per annum is Rs 144. Find the sum.
(A) Rs 10,000  (B) Rs 9,500  (C) Rs 10,500  (D) Rs 11,000

Answer: (A)

Solution:
Using the standard 2-year difference formula: Diff = P * (R / 100)^2 => 144 = P * (12 / 100)^2 = P * (144 / 10000) => P = 144 * (10000 / 144) = Rs 10,000. Therefore, the correct value is Rs 10,000, corresponding to option (A).

Q27. (Medium) The difference between compound interest and simple interest on a sum of money for 3 years at 10% per annum is Rs 155. Find the sum.
(A) Rs 4,800  (B) Rs 5,000  (C) Rs 5,200  (D) Rs 5,500

Answer: (B)

Solution:
Using the 3-year difference formula: Diff = [P * R^2 * (300 + R)] / 10^6. Given Diff = 155, R = 10: 155 = [P * 100 * 310] / 1000000 = 31P / 1000 => P = (155 * 1000) / 31 = 5 * 1000 = Rs 5,000. Therefore, the correct value is Rs 5,000, corresponding to option (B).

Q28. (Medium) A sum of money placed at compound interest doubles itself in 6 years. In how many years will it become 16 times of itself at the same rate?
(A) 32 years  (B) 18 years  (C) 24 years  (D) 30 years

Answer: (C)

Solution:
Under compound interest, the multiplier increases exponentially: Money doubles (2^1 times) in 6 years. To become 16 = 2^4 times, required time = 4 * 6 = 24 years. Psychological Trap: A frequent trap is multiplying 6 * 16 / 2 = 48 years or applying simple interest arithmetic logic (15 * 6 = 90 years). Therefore, the correct value is 24 years, corresponding to option (C).
