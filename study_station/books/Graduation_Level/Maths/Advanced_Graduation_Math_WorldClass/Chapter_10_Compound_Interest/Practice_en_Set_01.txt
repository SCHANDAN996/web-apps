1. Find the compound interest on ₹10,000 at 10% per annum for 2 years, compounded annually.
(a) ₹2,100 (b) ₹2,200 (c) ₹2,000 (d) ₹1,900
Answer: (a)
Solution: Computing simple interest (10,000 × 10% × 2 = ₹2,000) is the tempting trap. For compound interest, Amount = 10,000 × (1.1)² = ₹12,100, so CI = 12,100 − 10,000 = ₹2,100. The answer is ₹2,100.
Source: NCERT Class 8 Mathematics

2. The compound interest on ₹8,000 at 5% per annum for 2 years, compounded annually, is:
(a) ₹800 (b) ₹840 (c) ₹810 (d) ₹820
Answer: (d)
Solution: Simple interest of 8,000 × 5% × 2 = ₹800 is the tempting wrong option. Compound interest = 8,000 × [(1.05)² − 1] = 8,000 × 0.1025 = ₹820. The answer is ₹820.

Answer: (d)
Solution: Adding simple interest of ₹2,000 to get ₹7,000 is the common trap. Amount = 5,000 × (1.2)² = 5,000 × 1.44 = ₹7,200. The answer is ₹7,200.

4. The compound interest on a sum for 2 years at 10% per annum is ₹525. The sum is:
(a) ₹2,000 (b) ₹2,500 (c) ₹3,000 (d) ₹2,625
Answer: (b)
Solution: Treating ₹525 as simple interest and dividing by 20% gives ₹2,625, the trap. Here P × [(1.1)² − 1] = 525, so P × 0.21 = 525, giving P = ₹2,500. The answer is ₹2,500.

5. At what rate per annum will ₹1,000 amount to ₹1,210 in 2 years, compounded annually?
(a) 10% (b) 11% (c) 12% (d) 10.5%
Answer: (a)
Solution: Computing (1,210 − 1,000)/2 = 10.5% per year is the SI-based trap. Here (1 + r)² = 1,210/1,000 = 1.21, so 1 + r = 1.1 and r = 10%. The answer is 10%.

(a) 9% (b) 10% (c) 8% (d) 12%
Answer: (b)
Solution: Using the full two-year growth instead of one year's growth misleads many. Interest in the second year = 7,260 − 6,600 = ₹660 on ₹6,600, so rate = 660/6,600 × 100 = 10%. The answer is 10%.

14. A sum of money doubles itself in 4 years at compound interest. In how many years will it become 8 times?
(a) 8 years (b) 12 years (c) 16 years (d) 10 years
Answer: (b)
Solution: Multiplying 4 × 2 = 8 years is the classic trap. 8 times = 2³, so it needs 3 doubling periods: 3 × 4 = 12 years. The answer is 12 years.

21. A sum of ₹10,000 is invested at 10% per annum for 1 year. What is the difference between the interest earned when compounded half-yearly and when compounded annually?
(a) ₹25 (b) ₹50 (c) ₹100 (d) ₹10
Answer: (a)
Solution: Assuming both methods give equal interest in 1 year is the trap. Annual CI = 10,000 × 0.10 = ₹1,000. Half-yearly: rate 5%, 2 periods, CI = 10,000 × [(1.05)² − 1] = 10,000 × 0.1025 = ₹1,025. Difference = 1,025 − 1,000 = ₹25. The answer is ₹25.
Source: PYQ-style

22. A man borrows ₹21,000 at 10% per annum compound interest. He repays ₹12,100 at the end of the first year. How much should he pay at the end of the second year to clear the debt?

Solution: Ignoring interest on the remaining balance and repaying ₹11,000 is the trap. After 1 year, debt = 21,000 × 1.1 = ₹23,100; after paying ₹12,100, balance = ₹11,000. Second year dues = 11,000 × 1.1 = ₹12,100. The answer is ₹12,100.
