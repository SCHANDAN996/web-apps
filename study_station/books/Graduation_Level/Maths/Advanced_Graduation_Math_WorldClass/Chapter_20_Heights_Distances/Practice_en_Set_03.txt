51. The angle of elevation of the top of a tower from a point on the ground is 30°. On walking 40 m towards the tower, the angle becomes 60°. The height of the tower is:
(a) 20√3 m (b) 40√3 m (c) 20 m (d) 10√3 m
Answer: (a)
Solution: The tempting error is to take the 40 m walked as the height itself. Let the height be h and the nearer distance be x. Then h = x√3 and h = (x + 40)/√3, so 3x = x + 40, giving x = 20 and h = 20√3 m, so the answer is 20√3 m.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Mathematics

52. From the top of a 60 m high tower, the angles of depression of two points on the ground on the same side are 30° and 60°. The distance between the two points is:
(a) 20√3 m (b) 40√3 m (c) 60√3 m (d) 80√3 m
Answer: (b)
Solution: Many students add the two distances instead of subtracting them. The nearer point is at 60/√3 = 20√3 m and the farther at 60√3 m, so the gap is 60√3 − 20√3 = 40√3 m, so the answer is 40√3 m.

53. The shadow of a pole is √3 times its height. The angle of elevation of the sun is:
(a) 30° (b) 45° (c) 60° (d) 15°
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is to invert the ratio and pick 60°. If the height is h, the shadow is h√3, so tan θ = h/(h√3) = 1/√3, giving θ = 30°, so the answer is 30°.

54. A ladder 15 m long leans against a wall making an angle of 60° with the ground. The height it reaches on the wall is:
(a) 7.5 m (b) 15/√3 m (c) 15√3 m (d) 15√3/2 m
Answer: (d)
Solution: The common mistake is using cosine instead of sine, which gives 7.5 m. Height = 15 × sin 60° = 15 × √3/2 = 15√3/2 m, so the answer is 15√3/2 m.

Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is to report only the final distance 100/√3 m. Initial distance = 100√3 m and final distance = 100/√3 m, so the boat moved 100√3 − 100/√3 = 200/√3 m, so the answer is 200/√3 m.

56. The angles of elevation of the top of a tower from two points at distances a and b from the foot, in line with it, are complementary. The height of the tower is:
(a) a + b (b) √(ab) (c) a/b (d) ab
Answer: (b)
Solution: The tempting wrong choice is ab, forgetting the square root. If the angles are θ and 90° − θ, then h = a tan θ and h = b cot θ; multiplying gives h² = ab, so h = √(ab), so the answer is √(ab).

Solution: The trap is to ignore the man's height and answer 20 m. Height above eye level = 20 × tan 45° = 20 m, so total height = 20 + 1.8 = 21.8 m, so the answer is 21.8 m.

59. The angle of elevation of a cloud from a point 60 m above a lake is 30°, and the angle of depression of its reflection is 60°. The height of the cloud above the lake is:
(a) 60 m (b) 120 m (c) 90 m (d) 180 m
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is to add 60 m twice and get 180 m. Let the cloud be h m above the lake and the horizontal distance be x. Then h − 60 = x/√3 and h + 60 = x√3; dividing gives (h + 60)/(h − 60) = 3, so h = 120 m, so the answer is 120 m.

60. A tower subtends an angle of 30° at a point on the ground. On moving 50 m closer, it subtends 45°. The height of the tower is:
(a) 50√3 m (b) 25(√3 − 1) m (c) 25(√3 + 1) m (d) 50(√3 − 1) m
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is to assume the height equals 50 m. Let the height be h; the nearer distance is h (since tan 45° = 1) and the farther distance is h√3. So h√3 − h = 50, giving h = 50/(√3 − 1) = 25(√3 + 1) m, so the answer is 25(√3 + 1) m.
Source: PYQ-style

72. From a point on the ground, the angle of elevation of the top of a tower is θ. If the tower were 10 m taller, the angle would be 2θ, and if 20 m taller, 3θ. This situation is best handled by:
(a) applying tan θ = h/d only (b) using tan 2θ and tan 3θ identities (c) assuming θ = 30° (d) assuming the distance equals the height

Solution: The trap is assuming θ = 30° without any justification. With three unknowns (height, distance, θ), one must expand tan 2θ = 2tan θ/(1 − tan²θ) and the corresponding tan 3θ formula and solve the resulting equations, so the answer is using tan 2θ and tan 3θ identities.

73. From the foot of a tower, the angle of elevation of the top of a building is 60°, and from the top of the tower (height 40 m), the angle of elevation of the top of the building is 30°. The height of the building is:

Solution: The trap is doubling 40 m to get 80 m. Let the building be H m tall at distance d. Then H = d√3 and H − 40 = d/√3 = H/3, so 2H/3 = 40, giving H = 60 m, so the answer is 60 m.

75. The angle of elevation of the top of a tower from a point is 45°. On walking 10 m towards the tower along a slope inclined at 15° to the horizontal, the angle of elevation becomes 60°. This problem requires:
(a) only tan 45° and tan 60° (b) resolving the 10 m walk into horizontal and vertical components (c) assuming the slope is horizontal (d) using sin 45° only
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is treating the 10 m as purely horizontal, which ignores the slope. The walk changes both the horizontal distance (by 10 cos 15°) and the observer's height (by 10 sin 15°), so both components must be resolved before applying tangent ratios, so the answer is resolving the 10 m walk into horizontal and vertical components.
