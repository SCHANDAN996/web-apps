101. The angle of elevation of the top of a tower from a point on the ground is 30°. On walking 40 m towards the tower, the angle becomes 60°. The height of the tower is:
(a) 20 m (b) 40√3 m (c) 40 m (d) 20√3 m
Answer: (d)
Solution: The tempting wrong choice is 40√3 m, which comes from wrongly treating the walked 40 m as the final distance. Let the height be h and the nearer distance be x. Then h = x tan 60° = x√3 and h = (x + 40) tan 30° = (x + 40)/√3. Equating gives 3x = x + 40, so x = 20 m and h = 20√3 m, so the answer is 20√3 m.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Mathematics, Some Applications of Trigonometry

102. From the top of a 60 m high tower, the angle of depression of a car on the road is 30°. The distance of the car from the foot of the tower is:
(a) 20√3 m (b) 30√3 m (c) 60√3 m (d) 60 m
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is 20√3 m, obtained by dividing 60 by √3, which reverses the ratio. Angle of depression 30° gives tan 30° = 60/d, so d = 60/tan 30° = 60√3 m, so the answer is 60√3 m.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Mathematics, Some Applications of Trigonometry

103. The shadow of a pole is equal to its height. The angle of elevation of the sun is:
(a) 90° (b) 30° (c) 60° (d) 45°
Answer: (d)
Solution: The tempting wrong choice is 60°, picked by confusing the shadow with the slant ray. If height h equals shadow s, then tan θ = h/s = 1, so θ = 45°, so the answer is 45°.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Mathematics, Some Applications of Trigonometry

104. A ladder 10 m long leans against a wall making 60° with the ground. The height it reaches on the wall is:
(a) 5√3 m (b) 5 m (c) 10√3 m (d) 10/√3 m
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is 5 m, which uses cos 60° instead of sin 60°. Height reached = 10 sin 60° = 10 × (√3/2) = 5√3 m, so the answer is 5√3 m.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Mathematics, Some Applications of Trigonometry

105. The angles of elevation of the top of a tower from two points on the same side at distances a and b from the foot are complementary. The height of the tower is:
(a) √(ab) (b) a + b (c) ab (d) (a + b)/2
Answer: (a)
Solution: The tempting wrong choice is (a + b)/2, the arithmetic mean, but complementary angles give a product relation. If the angles are θ and 90° − θ, then h = a tan θ and h = b cot θ. Multiplying gives h² = ab, so h = √(ab), so the answer is √(ab).
Source: PYQ-style

106. From the top of a cliff 100 m high, the angles of depression of two boats in the same line on the same side are 30° and 45°. The distance between the boats is:
(a) 100(√3 − 1) m (b) 100(√3 + 1) m (c) 100√3 m (d) 100 m
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is 100(√3 + 1) m, which adds the distances instead of subtracting, though both boats are on the same side. Nearer boat distance = 100/tan 45° = 100 m; farther = 100/tan 30° = 100√3 m. Difference = 100(√3 − 1) m, so the answer is 100(√3 − 1) m.
Source: PYQ-style

107. A kite is flying at a height of 60 m with its string making 30° with the horizontal. The length of the string (assumed straight) is:
(a) 30 m (b) 60√2 m (c) 60√3 m (d) 120 m
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is 60√3 m, which wrongly multiplies by √3. Here sin 30° = 60/L, so L = 60/sin 30° = 60/(1/2) = 120 m, so the answer is 120 m.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Mathematics, Some Applications of Trigonometry

108. The angle of elevation of a cloud from a point 60 m above a lake is 30°, and the angle of depression of its reflection is 60°. The height of the cloud above the lake is:
(a) 180 m (b) 90 m (c) 60 m (d) 120 m
Answer: (d)
Solution: The tempting wrong choice is 90 m, from adding 60 and 30 incorrectly. Let the cloud height be H and the horizontal distance be x. Then H − 60 = x tan 30° and H + 60 = x tan 60°. Dividing gives (H + 60)/(H − 60) = 3, so H + 60 = 3H − 180 and H = 120 m, so the answer is 120 m.
Source: PYQ-style

109. From a point on the ground the angle of elevation of the top of a tower is 45°. On walking 50 m away, the angle becomes 30°. The height of the tower is:
(a) 25(√3 + 1) m (b) 50(√3 + 1)/2 m (c) 50√3 m (d) 25(√3 − 1) m
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is 50√3 m, which ignores the walking-away condition. Since tan 45° = 1, the height h equals the nearer distance. Then h = (h + 50) tan 30° = (h + 50)/√3, so h√3 = h + 50, h(√3 − 1) = 50, and h = 50/(√3 − 1) = 25(√3 + 1) m, so the answer is 25(√3 + 1) m.
Source: PYQ-style

110. The angle of elevation of the top of a building from the foot of a tower 30 m high is 30°, and the angle of elevation of the top of the tower from the foot of the building is 60°. The height of the building is:
(a) 30√3 m (b) 10 m (c) 30 m (d) 90 m
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is 30√3 m, from multiplying instead of dividing. Let the distance between them be d. From the tower's data, d = 30/tan 60° = 30/√3 = 10√3 m. Building height = d tan 30° = 10√3 × (1/√3) = 10 m, so the answer is 10 m.
Source: PYQ-style

111. A man 1.8 m tall stands 20 m from a lamp post. The angle of elevation of the top of the post from his eyes is 45°. The height of the lamp post is:
(a) 18.2 m (b) 20 m (c) 21.8 m (d) 38.2 m
Answer: (c)
Solution: The tempting wrong choice is 20 m, which forgets the man's own height. Height above the man's eyes = 20 tan 45° = 20 m. Total height = 20 + 1.8 = 21.8 m, so the answer is 21.8 m.

112. From a point P on the ground, the angle of elevation of the top of a 100 m tower is 30°. The distance of P from the top of the tower is:
(a) 200 m (b) 200/√3 m (c) 100 m (d) 100√3 m
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is 100√3 m, which is the distance from the foot, not from the top. Slant distance = 100/sin 30° = 100/(1/2) = 200 m, so the answer is 200 m.

113. The angles of elevation of the top of a tower from two points on opposite sides of it are 45° and 60°. If the points are 100(1 + 1/√3) m apart, the height of the tower is:
(a) 50 m (b) 50√3 m (c) 100 m (d) 100√3 m
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is 100√3 m, from mishandling the sum of distances on opposite sides. The distances are h (for 45°) and h/√3 (for 60°). Sum: h(1 + 1/√3) = 100(1 + 1/√3), so h = 100 m, so the answer is 100 m.

114. A pole 6 m high casts a shadow 2√3 m long. The angle of elevation of the sun is:
(a) 30° (b) 45° (c) 60° (d) 75°
Answer: (c)
Solution: The tempting wrong choice is 30°, which swaps height and shadow. tan θ = 6/(2√3) = 3/√3 = √3, so θ = 60°, so the answer is 60°.

115. From the top of a 7 m high building, the angle of elevation of the top of a tower is 60° and the angle of depression of its foot is 45°. The height of the tower is:
(a) 7(√3 + 1) m (b) 14 m (c) 7√3 m (d) 7(√3 − 1) m
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is 7√3 m, which is only the part above the building. Distance = 7/tan 45° = 7 m. Extra height = 7 tan 60° = 7√3 m. Total = 7 + 7√3 = 7(√3 + 1) m, so the answer is 7(√3 + 1) m.

116. The angle of elevation of the top of a tower from a point is θ such that tan θ = 5/12. If the point is 120 m from the foot, the height of the tower is:
(a) 60 m (b) 50 m (c) 288 m (d) 25 m
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is 60 m, from halving 120 without applying the ratio. Height = 120 × tan θ = 120 × 5/12 = 50 m, so the answer is 50 m.

117. An aeroplane flying at 3000 m passes vertically above another aeroplane. From a point on the ground, their angles of elevation are 60° and 45°. The height of the lower aeroplane is:
(a) 3000/√3 m (b) 1500 m (c) 1000√3 m (d) 3000(√3 − 1) m
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is 3000/√3 m, which is the horizontal distance, not the lower plane's height. Horizontal distance = 3000/tan 60° = 3000/√3 = 1000√3 m. Lower height = 1000√3 × tan 45° = 1000√3 m, so the answer is 1000√3 m.

118. A tree breaks and bends so that its top touches the ground making 30° with it, at a distance of 10 m from the foot. The original height of the tree is:
(a) 20√3 m (b) 20/√3 m (c) 10/√3 m (d) 10√3 m
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is 20/√3 m, which is only the broken slant part, not the full original height. Standing part = 10 tan 30° = 10/√3 m; broken part = 10/cos 30° = 20/√3 m. Original height = 10/√3 + 20/√3 = 30/√3 = 10√3 m, so the answer is 10√3 m.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Mathematics, Some Applications of Trigonometry

119. The angle of depression of a ship from the top of a 75 m lighthouse is 30°. After some time the angle becomes 45° as the ship approaches. The distance travelled by the ship is:
(a) 75√3 m (b) 75(√3 + 1) m (c) 75(√3 − 1) m (d) 75 m
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is 75√3 m, which is only the initial distance. Initial distance = 75√3 m; final distance = 75 m. Distance travelled = 75√3 − 75 = 75(√3 − 1) m, so the answer is 75(√3 − 1) m.

120. From the top of a hill, the angles of depression of two consecutive kilometre stones on the same side on a straight road are 30° and 45°. The height of the hill is:
(a) √3/2 km (b) (√3 − 1)/2 km (c) 1 km (d) (√3 + 1)/2 km
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is √3/2 km, from a sign error in the difference of distances. Let the height be h; the distances are h√3 and h, and the stones are 1 km apart. So h√3 − h = 1, giving h(√3 − 1) = 1 and h = 1/(√3 − 1) = (√3 + 1)/2 km, so the answer is (√3 + 1)/2 km.
Source: PYQ-style

121. A tower subtends an angle θ at a point A on the ground, where tan θ = 1/3. At a point B, 100 m nearer the tower, it subtends 45°. The height of the tower is:
(a) 75 m (b) 50 m (c) 100 m (d) 150 m
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is 100 m, from assuming the nearer distance equals the walked distance. Let the height be h; at B the distance is h since tan 45° = 1. At A: h/(h + 100) = 1/3, so 3h = h + 100 and h = 50 m, so the answer is 50 m.
Source: PYQ-style

122. From the top of a tower of height h, the angles of depression of the top and bottom of a building of height b are 30° and 45° respectively. Then h/b equals:
(a) √3 (b) √3/(√3 − 1) (c) 3/(3 − √3) (d) (√3 + 1)/2
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is √3, from ignoring the building's own height. The distance d = h since tan 45° = 1. Also h − b = d tan 30° = h/√3. So b = h − h/√3 = h(√3 − 1)/√3, giving h/b = √3/(√3 − 1), so the answer is √3/(√3 − 1).
Source: PYQ-style

123. A flagstaff of height 5 m stands on a tower. From a point on the ground, the angles of elevation of the bottom and top of the flagstaff are 30° and 60°. The height of the tower is:
(a) 2.5 m (b) 5 m (c) 10 m (d) 5√3 m
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is 5 m, from assuming the flagstaff equals the tower. Let the tower height be h at distance d: h = d/√3 and h + 5 = d√3. Subtracting gives 5 = d√3 − d/√3 = 2d/√3, so d = 5√3/2 and h = d/√3 = 2.5 m, so the answer is 2.5 m.
Source: PYQ-style

124. Two poles of heights 4 m and 9 m stand on level ground. The angles of elevation of the top of each pole from the foot of the other are complementary. The distance between the poles is:
(a) 13 m (b) 6 m (c) 36 m (d) 5 m
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is 13 m, which simply adds the heights. If the angles are θ and 90° − θ, then 4 = d tan θ and 9 = d cot θ. Multiplying gives 36 = d², so d = 6 m, so the answer is 6 m.
Source: PYQ-style

125. From an aeroplane vertically above a straight road, the angles of depression of two consecutive milestones on opposite sides of the aeroplane are 30° and 60°. If consecutive milestones are 1 km apart, the height of the aeroplane is:
(a) √3 km (b) √3/4 km (c) 1/2 km (d) √3/2 km
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is √3/2 km, which comes from wrongly taking the stones as 2 km apart. The distances on opposite sides are h√3 (for 30°) and h/√3 (for 60°), and they sum to 1 km. So h√3 + h/√3 = 1, giving h(4/√3) = 1 and h = √3/4 km, so the answer is √3/4 km.
Source: PYQ-style
