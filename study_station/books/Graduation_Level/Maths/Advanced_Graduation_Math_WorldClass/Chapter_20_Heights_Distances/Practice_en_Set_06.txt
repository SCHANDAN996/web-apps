126. From a point A, the angle of elevation of the top of a tower is 30°. From a point B, 20 m closer to the tower on the same line, the angle of elevation is 60°. The height of the tower is:
(a) 20 m (b) 20√3 m (c) 10 m (d) 10√3 m
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is assuming the 20 m gap itself relates directly to the height by a single tangent, which tempts 20√3 m. Let the height be h and the nearer distance be x. Then h = x tan 60° and h = (x + 20) tan 30°. So x√3 = (x + 20)/√3, giving 3x = x + 20, x = 10 m, and h = 10√3 m. So the answer is 10√3 m.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Mathematics

127. Two poles stand 40 m apart on level ground. The angle of elevation of the top of each pole from the foot of the other is 30° and 60° respectively. The sum of their heights is:
(a) 160/√3 m (b) 80/√3 m (c) 80 m (d) 40√3 m
Answer: (a)
Solution: Many students compute only the taller pole (40√3 m) and stop, which is the trap. Heights are 40 tan 30° = 40/√3 m and 40 tan 60° = 40√3 m. Sum = 40(1/√3 + √3) = 40 × 4/√3 = 160/√3 m. So the answer is 160/√3 m.
Source: PYQ-style

128. From the top of a 100 m high cliff, the angles of depression of two boats on the same side, in the same line, are 45° and 30°. The distance between the boats is:
(a) 100√3 m (b) 100(√3 + 1) m (c) 100(√3 − 1) m (d) 100 m
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is adding the two distances, which gives 100(√3 + 1) m, but the boats are on the same side so we subtract. Distances from the foot are 100 cot 45° = 100 m and 100 cot 30° = 100√3 m. Difference = 100(√3 − 1) m. So the answer is 100(√3 − 1) m.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Mathematics

129. A balloon rises vertically above a fixed point. From a point P on the ground 50 m from that fixed point, the angle of elevation of the balloon changes from 30° to 60°. The height risen by the balloon is:
(a) 100/√3 m (b) 50/√3 m (c) 50√3 m (d) 100√3 m
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is taking only the final height 50√3 m instead of the rise. Initial height = 50 tan 30° = 50/√3 m; final height = 50 tan 60° = 50√3 m. Rise = 50(√3 − 1/√3) = 50 × 2/√3 = 100/√3 m. So the answer is 100/√3 m.

132. From a point 40 m from the foot of a tower, the angles of elevation of the top of the tower and the top of a flagstaff on it are 30° and 60° respectively. The height of the flagstaff is:
(a) 40√3 m (b) 80/√3 m (c) 40/√3 m (d) 80√3 m
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is reporting the total height 40√3 m, which is tower plus flagstaff. Tower height = 40 tan 30° = 40/√3 m; total height = 40 tan 60° = 40√3 m. Flagstaff = 40√3 − 40/√3 = 80/√3 m. So the answer is 80/√3 m.

133. From a point 20 m above a lake, the angle of elevation of a cloud is 30° and the angle of depression of its reflection in the lake is 60°. The height of the cloud above the lake is:
(a) 40 m (b) 30 m (c) 60 m (d) 20√3 m
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is forgetting that the reflection lies as far below the lake surface as the cloud is above it. Let the cloud be H m above the lake and the horizontal distance be d. Then (H − 20)/d = tan 30° and (H + 20)/d = tan 60°. Dividing: (H + 20)/(H − 20) = 3, so H + 20 = 3H − 60, H = 40 m. So the answer is 40 m.
Source: PYQ-style

134. From a point, the angle of elevation of the top of a tower is 30°. On walking 30 m towards the tower, the angle becomes 60°. The height of the tower is:
(a) 10√3 m (b) 15√3 m (c) 15 m (d) 30√3 m
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is halving 30 m to get 15 m as the height directly. With h = x tan 60° and h = (x + 30) tan 30°, we get 3x = x + 30, x = 15 m, h = 15√3 m. So the answer is 15√3 m.

135. From two points on the ground, 9 m and 16 m from the foot of a tower on the same line and same side, the angles of elevation of the top are complementary. The height of the tower is:
(a) 12 m (b) 25 m (c) 144 m (d) 7 m
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is adding the distances (25 m) instead of using the complementary-angle relation. If the angles are θ and 90° − θ, then h/9 = tan θ and h/16 = cot θ, so h² = 9 × 16 = 144, h = 12 m. So the answer is 12 m.

(a) 15 m/s (b) 60 m/s (c) 20 m/s (d) 30 m/s
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is using only one position's distance instead of the difference. Distances from the observer: 300√3 cot 60° = 300 m and 300√3 cot 30° = 900 m. Distance flown = 600 m in 20 s, speed = 30 m/s. So the answer is 30 m/s.

137. When the Sun's altitude falls from 60° to 30°, the shadow of a tower lengthens by 40 m. The height of the tower is:
(a) 40√3 m (b) 40 m (c) 20√3 m (d) 20 m
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is equating the 40 m increase with the full shadow at 30°, giving 40√3 m. Shadows are h cot 60° = h/√3 and h cot 30° = h√3. Increase = h(√3 − 1/√3) = 2h/√3 = 40, so h = 20√3 m. So the answer is 20√3 m.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Mathematics

138. From the top of a 60 m tower, the angles of depression of the top and bottom of a building are 30° and 60° respectively. The height of the building is:
(a) 20 m (b) 40 m (c) 30 m (d) 45 m
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is computing the depression distance to the top (60 − h) and reporting that as the answer. If the building is d m away, 60/d = tan 60° gives d = 60/√3 = 20√3 m. Then (60 − h)/d = tan 30°, so 60 − h = 20√3/√3 = 20, h = 40 m. So the answer is 40 m.

145. From a point A, the angle of elevation of the top of a tower is α, where tan α = 1/3. B is the midpoint of the line joining A to the foot of the tower. The angle of elevation β of the top from B satisfies:

147. A statue 2 m high stands on a pedestal 4 m high. The angle subtended by the statue at a point on the ground (in line with the foot of the pedestal) is greatest when the distance of the point from the foot of the pedestal is:

150. From a ship, the angle of elevation of the top of a 60 m lighthouse is 30°. After sailing straight towards the lighthouse, the angle of elevation becomes 60°. The distance sailed is:
(a) 120 m (b) 60√3 m (c) 20√3 m (d) 40√3 m

Solution: The trap is reporting the final distance from the lighthouse (20√3 m) instead of the distance sailed. Initial distance = 60 cot 30° = 60√3 m; final distance = 60 cot 60° = 20√3 m. Distance sailed = 60√3 − 20√3 = 40√3 m. So the answer is 40√3 m.
