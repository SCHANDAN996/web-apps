126. Two trains of lengths 120 m and 180 m run at speeds of 54 km/h and 36 km/h respectively in opposite directions on parallel tracks. In how much time will they cross each other?
(a) 10 s (b) 18 s (c) 15 s (d) 12 s
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is adding the speeds but forgetting to add the lengths; crossing requires covering 120 + 180 = 300 m. Relative speed = 54 + 36 = 90 km/h = 25 m/s. Time = 300/25, so the answer is 12 s.

127. Walking at 3/4 of his usual speed, a man reaches his office 20 minutes late. His usual time to reach the office is:
(a) 40 min (b) 50 min (c) 80 min (d) 60 min
Answer: (d)
Solution: The tempting error is taking 20 min as 1/4 of the usual time directly, which gives 80 min; actually when speed becomes 3/4, time becomes 4/3, so the extra 1/3 of usual time equals 20 min. Usual time = 20 × 3, so the answer is 60 min.

128. In a 100 m race, A beats B by 20 m. In a 200 m race at the same speeds, A beats B by:
(a) 40 m (b) 30 m (c) 20 m (d) 45 m
Answer: (a)
Solution: Many double the race distance but keep the margin at 20 m, which is wrong because the margin scales with distance. When A runs 100 m, B runs 80 m, so when A runs 200 m, B runs 160 m. The margin is 200 − 160, so the answer is 40 m.

Solution: The trap is averaging 15 km/h and 10 km/h as (15+10)/2 = 12.5 but then confusing it with the stream speed; the average of downstream and upstream speeds is indeed the boat speed. Downstream = 15 km/h, upstream = 10 km/h, boat speed = (15+10)/2, so the answer is 12.5 km/h.
