# The Feynman Technique: Time, Speed and Distance

## The Highway Marker Analogy
Imagine driving down an expressway marked with kilometer stones every 1 km. Speed is simply the count of kilometer stones you pass in one hour.
If you travel at 60 km/h, you pass 1 stone every minute.
At 90 km/h, you pass 1.5 stones every minute.

### The Conversion Secret: Why 5/18?
People memorize $5/18$ blindly.
Consider what 1 km/h truly means:
1 kilometer has 1,000 meters.
1 hour has 3,600 seconds.
So 1 km/h = 1,000 meters in 3,600 seconds = $1000 / 3600 = 10 / 36 = 5 / 18$ meters every second.
Whenever you see km/h and want m/s, you are shrinking hours into seconds, so you multiply by the smaller fraction $5/18$.

## Why Relative Speed Works
Imagine two cars moving toward each other on a single lane, one at 60 km/h and the other at 40 km/h.
From inside one car, the trees seem to move at 60 km/h, but the oncoming car rushes toward you at $60 + 40 = 100$ km/h!
The distance between them closes at the combined rate.
If both cars move in the same direction, the faster car only pulls away by the difference of speeds, $60 - 40 = 20$ km/h.

## The Mystery of Average Speed
If you drive 100 km to a town at 50 km/h and return at 100 km/h, is your average speed 75 km/h?
Absolutely not!
Going there takes $100 / 50 = 2$ hours.
Returning takes $100 / 100 = 1$ hour.
Total round trip = 200 km in $2 + 1 = 3$ hours.
$$\text{Average Speed} = \frac{200}{3} = 66.67 \text{ km/h}$$
You spent twice as much time crawling at 50 km/h as you did speeding at 100 km/h, so the slower speed drags the average down toward itself. That is the geometric intuition of the Harmonic Mean.
