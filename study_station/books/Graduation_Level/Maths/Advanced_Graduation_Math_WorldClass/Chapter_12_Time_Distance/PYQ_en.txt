# 📊 PYQ Analysis — Time & Distance (Advanced Level)

## How Exams Test This Chapter

Time & Distance is one of the most reliable scoring areas in UPSC CSAT, SSC CGL Tier-II, IBPS PO Mains, CAT and State PCS papers. The examiners rarely invent new concepts — they recycle the same handful of ideas and dress them up with psychological traps. Here is what the pattern looks like, in words:

**1. Average speed with equal distances vs equal times.**
The single most repeated idea. When distances are equal, average speed is the *harmonic mean* (2ab/(a+b)); when times are equal, it is the *arithmetic mean* ((a+b)/2). Examiners love giving "60 km/h going, 40 km/h returning" and planting 50 km/h as a trap option.

**2. Relative speed — trains, boats, and two people moving.**
Trains crossing poles, platforms, and each other; boats going upstream/downstream; two runners on a circular track. The trap: forgetting to convert km/h to m/s (×5/18), or adding speeds when they should be subtracted (same direction) and vice versa.

**3. The "late/early" family.**
"A man walks at 3/4 of his usual speed and is late by 20 minutes." This algebraic staple appears constantly in CSAT and banking papers. The trap is computing with the wrong base — the reduced speed makes time 4/3 of usual, so the *extra* 1/3 equals 20 minutes.

**4. Ratio-based questions.**
When speed ratio is given, time ratio is its inverse (for fixed distance). CAT and CGL Tier-II love hiding the whole solution inside one ratio inversion.

**5. Circular tracks and meetings.**
Two runners on a circular track meet every LCM-style interval: time to first meeting = track length ÷ relative speed; time to meet at the *starting point* = LCM of individual lap times. Examiners blur these two different questions on purpose.

**6. Escalator, stoppage, and "chasing" variants.**
A bus's speed excluding stoppages vs including them; a policeman chasing a thief with a head start. These reward setting up one clean equation rather than brute force.

### The Examiner's Favourite Traps (Psychological Hooks)

- **The arithmetic-mean bait:** whenever two speeds are given for a round trip, the midpoint of the two speeds is always offered as an option — and it is always wrong.
- **Unit-switch trap:** data in km/h, answer demanded in metres or seconds. The ×5/18 conversion is where most marks are lost.
- **Near-true statements:** in CSAT's statement-based questions, one statement is *almost* right — e.g., "average speed is 50 km/h" — testing whether you actually computed the harmonic mean.
- **Timeline confusion:** "A starts at 8 am, B starts at 9 am" — candidates forget the head-start distance covered in that one hour.
- **Negative wording:** "which of the following is NOT possible" — read the question twice before solving.

**Time strategy:** a standard train/boat question should take 30–45 seconds with the formula; a ratio or late/early question 45–60 seconds. If you are past 90 seconds, you have missed the shortcut — mark and move on.

## Top 10 High-Yield Questions (with Solutions)

**Q1.** A man travels from home to office at 40 km/h and returns along the same route at 60 km/h. His average speed for the whole journey is:
(a) 50 km/h (b) 48 km/h (c) 52 km/h (d) 45 km/h

**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** Equal distances → harmonic mean: 2×40×60/(40+60) = 4800/100 = 48 km/h. **Trap:** option (a) 50 is the arithmetic mean — the classic bait.
**Source: PYQ-style (CSAT pattern)**

**Q2.** A train 240 m long crosses a pole in 12 seconds. The speed of the train is:
(a) 72 km/h (b) 60 km/h (c) 80 km/h (d) 66 km/h

**Answer: (a)**
**Solution:** Speed = 240/12 = 20 m/s = 20 × 18/5 = 72 km/h. **Trap:** forgetting the m/s → km/h conversion leaves you hunting among wrong options.
**Source: PYQ-style (SSC CGL pattern)**

**Q3.** A train 150 m long crosses a platform 250 m long in 20 seconds. Its speed is:
(a) 72 km/h (b) 54 km/h (c) 90 km/h (d) 60 km/h

**Answer: (a)**
**Solution:** Total distance = 150 + 250 = 400 m. Speed = 400/20 = 20 m/s = 72 km/h. **Trap:** using only the train's length (150 m) gives 27 km/h — the platform must be added.
**Source: PYQ-style (Railway/Bank pattern)**

**Q4.** Walking at 3/4 of his usual speed, a man reaches his office 20 minutes late. His usual time is:
(a) 60 min (b) 75 min (c) 80 min (d) 90 min

**Answer: (a)**
**Solution:** Speed becomes 3/4, so time becomes 4/3 of usual. Extra time = (4/3 − 1)T = T/3 = 20 min → T = 60 min. **Trap:** taking 20 min as 1/4 of usual time (using the speed fraction instead of the time fraction) gives 80 min — option (c) is the bait.
**Source: PYQ-style (CSAT/IBPS pattern)**

**Q5.** A boat goes 30 km downstream in 2 hours and the same distance upstream in 3 hours. The speed of the stream is:
(a) 2.5 km/h (b) 5 km/h (c) 3 km/h (d) 2 km/h

**Answer: (a)**
**Solution:** Downstream speed = 15 km/h, upstream = 10 km/h. Stream speed = (15 − 10)/2 = 2.5 km/h. **Trap:** forgetting to divide by 2 gives 5 km/h — option (b) is the hook; that is the *difference*, not the stream speed.
**Source: PYQ-style (Bank PO pattern)**

**Q6.** Two trains of length 120 m and 180 m run towards each other on parallel tracks at 54 km/h and 36 km/h. The time taken to cross each other is:
(a) 10 s (b) 12 s (c) 15 s (d) 18 s

**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** Relative speed = 54 + 36 = 90 km/h = 25 m/s. Total length = 300 m. Time = 300/25 = 12 s. **Trap:** subtracting the speeds (same-direction logic) — opposite directions always *add*.
**Source: PYQ-style (SSC pattern)**

**Q7.** A and B start together on a circular track of 400 m, running in the same direction at 6 m/s and 4 m/s. After how long will they meet for the first time?
(a) 100 s (b) 200 s (c) 400 s (d) 66.67 s

**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** Same direction → relative speed = 6 − 4 = 2 m/s. First meeting when the faster gains one full lap: 400/2 = 200 s. **Trap:** adding the speeds (opposite-direction logic) gives 40 s; dividing 400 by 6 gives option (d) — both are planted.
**Source: PYQ-style (CAT/CSAT pattern)**

**Q8.** A thief steals a car at 2 pm and drives at 60 km/h. The owner discovers the theft at 3 pm and chases at 80 km/h. At what time does he catch the thief?
(a) 6 pm (b) 5 pm (c) 7 pm (d) 4 pm

**Answer: (a)**
**Solution:** Head start = 60 km (one hour). Relative speed = 80 − 60 = 20 km/h. Catch-up time = 60/20 = 3 h after 3 pm → 6 pm. **Trap:** measuring the 3 hours from 2 pm instead of 3 pm gives 5 pm — option (b) catches the careless.
**Source: PYQ-style (Bank/SSC pattern)**

**Q9.** Excluding stoppages, a bus's speed is 54 km/h; including stoppages, 45 km/h. How many minutes per hour does the bus stop?
(a) 9 min (b) 12 min (c) 10 min (d) 15 min

**Answer: (c)**
**Solution:** In one hour of running the bus covers 54 km; with stoppages it covers only 45 km, losing 9 km. Time to cover 9 km at 54 km/h = 9/54 h = 10 min. **Trap:** answering 9 min (option a) by confusing distance lost with time lost.
**Source: PYQ-style (CSAT pattern)**

**Q10.** The ratio of speeds of A and B is 3 : 4. A takes 30 minutes more than B to cover the same distance. The time taken by B is:
(a) 90 min (b) 120 min (c) 60 min (d) 100 min

**Answer: (a)**
**Solution:** Time ratio is inverse: T_A : T_B = 4 : 3. Difference of 1 part = 30 min → T_B = 3 × 30 = 90 min. **Trap:** using the speed ratio directly (3 : 4) for time gives 120 min for B — option (b) punishes those who forget to invert.
**Source: PYQ-style (CAT/CGL Tier-II pattern)**

## Key Takeaways for Revision

- Equal-distance average speed → harmonic mean; never the arithmetic mean.
- Trains: add lengths; opposite directions add speeds, same direction subtract.
- Late/early problems: convert the speed fraction into a time fraction first.
- Boats: stream = (downstream − upstream)/2; boat = (downstream + upstream)/2.
- Circular track: first meeting = lap ÷ relative speed; meeting at start = LCM of lap times.
- Always convert units before computing: km/h × 5/18 = m/s.
