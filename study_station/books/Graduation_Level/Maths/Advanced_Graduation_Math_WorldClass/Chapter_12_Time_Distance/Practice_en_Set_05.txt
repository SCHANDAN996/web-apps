101. A train 240 m long crosses a pole in 12 seconds. What is the speed of the train in km/h?
(a) 72 km/h (b) 60 km/h (c) 80 km/h (d) 90 km/h
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is forgetting to convert m/s to km/h, which gives 20 as a raw number. Speed = 240/12 = 20 m/s = 20 × 18/5 = 72 km/h, so the answer is 72 km/h.

102. A man covers a certain distance at 60 km/h and returns at 40 km/h. What is his average speed for the whole journey?
(a) 48 km/h (b) 50 km/h (c) 45 km/h (d) 52 km/h
Answer: (a)
Solution: The tempting wrong choice is the arithmetic mean 50 km/h, but average speed for equal distances is the harmonic mean: 2 × 60 × 40/(60 + 40) = 4800/100 = 48 km/h, so the answer is 48 km/h.

103. Two trains of lengths 150 m and 250 m run towards each other on parallel lines at 45 km/h and 55 km/h. In what time will they cross each other?
(a) 12 s (b) 16 s (c) 14.4 s (d) 18 s
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is adding the speeds wrongly or using only one train's length. Relative speed = 100 km/h = 100 × 5/18 m/s; total distance = 400 m; time = 400 × 18/500 = 14.4 s, so the answer is 14.4 s.

104. A car travels the first 160 km at 40 km/h and the next 160 km at 80 km/h. What is the average speed for the entire journey?
(a) 60 km/h (b) 58 km/h (c) 56 km/h (d) 53.33 km/h
Answer: (d)
Solution: Choosing 60 km/h (the simple mean) is the trap. Time taken = 160/40 + 160/80 = 4 + 2 = 6 h; average speed = 320/6 = 53.33 km/h, so the answer is 53.33 km/h.

105. A boat goes 30 km downstream in 2 hours and returns in 3 hours. What is the speed of the boat in still water?
(a) 13 km/h (b) 12.5 km/h (c) 12 km/h (d) 15 km/h
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is averaging 15 and 10 incorrectly as 13. Downstream speed = 15 km/h, upstream = 10 km/h; boat speed = (15 + 10)/2 = 12.5 km/h, so the answer is 12.5 km/h.

106. A train crosses a platform 200 m long in 20 seconds and a pole in 10 seconds. What is the length of the train?
(a) 200 m (b) 150 m (c) 250 m (d) 180 m
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is subtracting the times wrongly. Let length be L; speed = L/10. Then (L + 200)/20 = L/10, giving L + 200 = 2L, so L = 200 m, so the answer is 200 m.

107. A man walks at 5 km/h and reaches his office 10 minutes late. Walking at 6 km/h he reaches 5 minutes early. What is the distance to his office?
(a) 9 km (b) 6 km (c) 8 km (d) 7.5 km
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is using only the 10-minute lateness. Difference in time = 15 min = 1/4 h. Distance = (5 × 6)/(6 − 5) × 1/4 = 30/4 = 7.5 km, so the answer is 7.5 km.

120. A train passes two men walking in the same direction at 3 km/h and 6 km/h in 9 seconds and 10 seconds respectively. What is the length of the train?
(a) 90 m (b) 75 m (c) 100 m (d) 80 m
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is ignoring relative speed. Let train speed be v m/s and length L: L = 9(v − 3 × 5/18) = 10(v − 6 × 5/18). Solving 9v − 7.5 = 10v − 16.67 gives v = 55/6 m/s; L = 9(55/6 − 5/6) = 9 × 50/6 = 75 m, so the answer is 75 m.

123. Two trains of equal length cross a pole in 12 s and 15 s respectively. In what time will they cross each other when moving in opposite directions?
(a) 13⅓ s (b) 13.5 s (c) 14 s (d) 12.5 s
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is averaging 12 and 15 directly. Let each length be L; speeds are L/12 and L/15. Relative speed = L(1/12 + 1/15) = 3L/20; time to cross = 2L ÷ (3L/20) = 40/3 = 13⅓ s, so the answer is 13⅓ s.
Source: PYQ-style

(a) 60 km (b) 84 km (c) 72 km (d) 90 km

Solution: The trap is assuming the boat's still-water speed first without equations. Let boat speed be b: 6(b + 2) = 9(b − 2), so 3b = 30, b = 10 km/h; distance = 6 × 12 = 72 km, so the answer is 72 km.

125. A man walks a certain distance and rides back, taking 37 minutes in all. He could walk both ways in 55 minutes. How long would he take to ride both ways?
(a) 19 min (b) 20 min (c) 18 min (d) 22 min
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is halving 37 directly. Walking one way = 55/2 = 27.5 min; riding one way = 37 − 27.5 = 9.5 min; riding both ways = 19 min, so the answer is 19 min.
