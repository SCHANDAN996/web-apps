Q51. (Medium) A person spends 75% of his income. His income increases by 20% and his expenditure increases by 10%. By what percentage do his savings increase?
(A) 50%  (B) 40%  (C) 30%  (D) 25%

Answer: (A)

Solution:
Let initial income be 100, expenditure = 75, savings = 25. New income = 120. New expenditure = 75 * 1.10 = 82.5. New savings = 120 - 82.5 = 37.5. Increase in savings = 37.5 - 25 = 12.5. Percentage increase = (12.5 / 25) * 100% = 50%. Psychological Trap: A common blunder is taking 12.5 as percentage of income (12.5%) instead of original savings (50%). Therefore, the correct value is 50%, corresponding to option (A).

Q52. (Medium) A man spends 80% of his income. When his income is increased by 30%, he increases his expenditure by 37.5%. What is the percentage change in his savings?
(A) 5% increase  (B) 0% (No change)  (C) 10% decrease  (D) 7.5% increase

Answer: (B)

Solution:
Let income = 100, expenditure = 80, savings = 20. New income = 130. New expenditure = 80 + 80 * (3/8) = 80 + 30 = 110. New savings = 130 - 110 = 20. Change in savings = 0%. Therefore, the correct value is 0% (No change), corresponding to option (B).

Q53. (Medium) If the income tax is increased by 19%, the net income is reduced by 1%. Find the original rate of income tax.
(A) 4%  (B) 6%  (C) 5%  (D) 7.5%

Answer: (C)

Solution:
The increase in tax equals the reduction in net income. Thus, 19% of Tax = 1% of Net Income => Tax / Net Income = 1 / 19. Total Income = Tax + Net Income = 1 + 19 = 20. Rate of Tax = (1 / 20) * 100% = 5%. Psychological Trap: Students often divide 1 by 19 giving 5.26%, forgetting that Total Income = Tax + Net Income = 20. Therefore, the correct value is 5%, corresponding to option (C).
