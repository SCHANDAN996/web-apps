The Feynman Technique: Intuition Behind Percentage Dynamics

Think of percentages not as arbitrary numbers with a '%' sign, but as volume knobs on a stereo.

1. The Invariant Product (AB Rule):
Imagine you spend 100 on sugar every month.
If the price doubles (100% increase, ratio 2/1), you can only buy half the sugar (50% decrease, ratio 1/2).
Notice: +1/1 price shift leads to -1/(1+1) = -1/2 consumption shift!
If price goes up by 25% (+1/4), the denominator increases to 4 + 1 = 5, meaning consumption must drop by 1/5 (20%)!
Why? Because (5/4) * (4/5) = 1. The two fractions must always be exact reciprocals to cancel each other out!

2. The Asymmetry of Up vs Down:
If your investment goes up 50% and then drops 50%, did you break even?
No! You started with 100.
Up 50% takes you to 150.
Down 50% of 150 cuts 75 away, leaving you with only 75!
Because you gained 50% of a smaller base (100), but lost 50% of a larger base (150). The loss always wins on an equal percentage rebound.

3. The Income-Expenditure-Savings Fulcrum:
Think of Income as the total length of a balance beam with a fulcrum at the expenditure-savings split.
Any percentage push on Income is simply the center of gravity between how much the Expenditure side expands and how much the Savings side expands!
