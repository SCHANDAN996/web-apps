# Feynman Explanation: The Hardest Idea in Percentage

## The Concept That Tricks Everyone: "Percentage of a Percentage" (Successive Change)

Ask most students this: *"A price goes UP by 20%, then comes DOWN by 20%. Is it back where it started?"*

Almost everyone says **yes**. Almost everyone is **wrong**. Let's see why — with pizza. 🍕

---

## The Pizza Story

Meet Ravi. Ravi has **100 rupees** (a nice round number — remember this, it's our secret weapon).

**Monday:** Ravi's money **increases by 20%**.
- 20% of 100 = 20
- Ravi now has **120 rupees**. 🎉

**Tuesday:** Ravi's money **decreases by 20%**.
- Here's the trap! 20% of *what*?
- Not 20% of 100 — that money is gone. Now it's 20% of **120**.
- 20% of 120 = 24
- Ravi now has 120 − 24 = **96 rupees**. 😢

Ravi went up 20% and down 20%... and **lost 4 rupees**. Where did they go?!

**The secret:** Percentages don't have a fixed size. A percentage is like a rubber band — it stretches or shrinks depending on **what it's holding onto**. The "up 20%" grabbed onto 100. The "down 20%" grabbed onto 120. Same 20%, different victims.

---

## The Elevator Rule 🛗

Think of percentage change like an elevator:

> **Going up 1 floor and coming down 1 floor brings you home — but only if the floors are the same height. In percentages, every floor is a DIFFERENT height, because each floor is measured from where you're standing right now.**

So: **+20% then −20% ≠ 0**. It always leaves you slightly **lower** than where you started.

---

## The Unforgettable Trick: "The 100 Trick" 💯

Whenever a problem says "increases by X%, then changes by Y%", don't panic. Just **pretend the starting number is 100** — always.

- Start: 100
- Apply the first change to 100
- Apply the second change to the *new* number
- Whatever you end with **is your answer in percent**. Done. No algebra needed.

**Speed version (the formula):**
$$\text{Net change} = a + b + \frac{ab}{100}$$
(Use + for increase, − for decrease.)

Check Ravi: +20 + (−20) + (20 × −20)/100 = 20 − 20 − 4 = **−4%**. Ravi lost 4%. Matches the pizza story! ✅

**Memory hook:** *"Same up, same down — you still frown."* 😟
Equal percentage up and down **always** ends in a small loss, and the loss is exactly $\frac{x^2}{100}\%$. For 20%: 400/100 = **4% loss**. For 10%: **1% loss**. For 50%: **25% loss**.

---

## Why Toppers Love This

Examiners hide this trap everywhere:
- Population grows 10%, then falls 10% → net?
- Salary cut 25%, then raised 25% → back to normal? (No! 6.25% poorer.)
- Area of a rectangle: length +10%, breadth −10% → area drops 1%.

Once you see the rubber band, you can never be fooled again.

## 🧠 Your Turn (The Feynman Test)

Explain this to a friend — or your wall — in your own words:

> **A shopkeeper raises the price of a toy by 25%, then offers a 25% discount and claims "It's back to the original price!" Is he honest, or is he quietly cheating you — and by exactly how much?**

If you can answer that without a formula, using the 100 Trick, you've truly understood percentages. That's the Feynman way: *if you can't explain it simply, you don't understand it yet.*
