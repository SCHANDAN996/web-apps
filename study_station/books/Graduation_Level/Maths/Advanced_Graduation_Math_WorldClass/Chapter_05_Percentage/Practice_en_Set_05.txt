Q101. (Hard) In an examination, 65% of the candidates passed in Mathematics, 48% passed in Physics, and 30% passed in both. What percentage of candidates failed in both subjects?
(A) 17%  (B) 15%  (C) 18%  (D) 20%

Answer: (A)

Solution:
Percentage passing in at least one subject = n(M) + n(P) - n(M and P) = 65% + 48% - 30% = 83%. Percentage failing in both = 100% - 83% = 17%. Psychological Trap: Candidates often add 65% + 48% = 113% and then subtract 100% or misapply Venn union formula. Therefore, the correct value is 17%, corresponding to option (A).

Q102. (Hard) In an examination, 35% of the students failed in English, 25% failed in Mathematics, and 15% failed in both. If 440 students passed in both subjects, find the total number of students who appeared in the examination.
(A) 750  (B) 800  (C) 900  (D) 850

Answer: (B)

Solution:
Percentage failed in at least one subject = 35% + 25% - 15% = 45%. Percentage passed in both subjects = 100% - 45% = 55%. Given 55% = 440 => Total students = 440 / 0.55 = 800. Therefore, the correct value is 800, corresponding to option (B).

Q103. (Hard) A candidate who gets 20% marks in an examination fails by 30 marks, but another candidate who gets 32% gets 42 marks more than the passing marks. Find the percentage of marks required to pass.
(A) 28%  (B) 24%  (C) 25%  (D) 30%

Answer: (C)

Solution:
Difference in percentage = 32% - 20% = 12%. Difference in marks = 42 - (-30) = 72 marks. Maximum marks = 72 / 0.12 = 600 marks. Passing marks = 20% of 600 + 30 = 120 + 30 = 150 marks. Passing percentage = (150 / 600) * 100% = 25%. Psychological Trap: A common error is taking the marks difference as 42 - 30 = 12 instead of adding them (42 + 30 = 72) since one is below and one is above passing. Therefore, the correct value is 25%, corresponding to option (C).

Q105. (Hard) Fresh fruit contains 68% water and dry fruit contains 20% water. How many kilograms of dry fruit can be obtained from 100 kg of fresh fruit?
(A) 40 kg  (B) 32 kg  (C) 48 kg  (D) 50 kg

Answer: (A)

Solution:
Pulp content remains constant. In 100 kg fresh fruit, pulp = (100 - 68)% = 32% of 100 kg = 32 kg. In dry fruit, pulp is (100 - 20)% = 80%. Let dry fruit weight be W. 80% of W = 32 kg => W = 32 / 0.80 = 40 kg. Psychological Trap: Many students assume water loss is 68% - 20% = 48%, giving an incorrect answer of 52 kg. Therefore, the correct value is 40 kg, corresponding to option (A).

Q107. (Hard) In an alloy of 60 kg, there is 75% copper and the rest is zinc. How much zinc must be added to this alloy to make the percentage of copper 60%?
(A) 12 kg  (B) 18 kg  (C) 15 kg  (D) 20 kg

Answer: (C)

Solution:
Copper is constant. Initial copper = 60 * 0.75 = 45 kg. In the new mixture, 45 kg represents 60% of total weight W. W = 45 / 0.60 = 75 kg. Zinc to be added = 75 - 60 = 15 kg. Therefore, the correct value is 15 kg, corresponding to option (C).

Q109. (Hard) In a survey of 1,000 consumers, it was found that 720 like product Alpha and 450 like product Beta. What is the least number of consumers that must like both products?
(A) 170  (B) 150  (C) 180  (D) 200

Answer: (A)

Solution:
Using Venn formula: n(A U B) = n(A) + n(B) - n(A and B). Since total consumers is at most 1,000: 720 + 450 - n(A and B) <= 1,000 => 1,170 - n(A and B) <= 1,000 => n(A and B) >= 170. Therefore, the correct value is 170, corresponding to option (A).

Q115. (Hard) A watermelon weighing 20 kg contains 96% water by weight. After being kept in the sun for some days, some water evaporates so that it contains 95% water. What is its present weight?
(A) 15 kg  (B) 18 kg  (C) 16 kg  (D) 17.5 kg

Answer: (C)

Solution:
Pulp content is constant: 20 * (1 - 0.96) = 20 * 0.04 = 0.8 kg. In present state, water is 95%, so pulp is 5%. Let new weight be W. 5% of W = 0.8 kg => W = 0.8 / 0.05 = 16 kg. Psychological Trap: A deceptive trap is to think that a 1% drop in water (96% to 95%) causes only a 1% drop in watermelon weight (to 19.8 kg). In reality, the weight drops to 16 kg because pulp percentage doubles from 4% to 5%. Therefore, the correct value is 16 kg, corresponding to option (C).
