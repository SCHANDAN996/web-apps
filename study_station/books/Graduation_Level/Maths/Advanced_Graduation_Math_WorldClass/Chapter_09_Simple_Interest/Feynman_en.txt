# 🧒 The Feynman Explanation — Simple Interest

## The Hardest Concept: Why does the SAME rate give DIFFERENT interest every year?

Imagine you lend your friend Ravi ₹1,000. He promises to pay you 10% extra every year as a "thank you" for waiting.

**Year 1:** He owes you ₹100 extra. Easy.
**Year 2:** Another ₹100. Still easy.
**Year 10:** Still exactly ₹100.

Wait… something feels unfair, right? Your ₹1,000 has been sitting with him for TEN years, but the "thank you" never grows. It's like a plant that never gets taller, no matter how long you water it.

**That's Simple Interest.** The interest is ALWAYS calculated on the original ₹1,000 — the *principal* — never on the growing pile. The principal is frozen in time, like a photograph. The interest keeps getting clicked from that same old photo, year after year.

## The Story of the Two Piggy Banks 🐷🐷

Meena and Tina each got ₹1,000 on their birthday.

- **Meena** put hers in a "Simple" piggy bank at 10% per year.
- **Tina** put hers in a "Compound" piggy bank at 10% per year.

After 1 year: both have ₹1,100. Tie!

After 2 years: Meena has ₹1,200. Tina has ₹1,210. Tina is ahead — because HER second-year interest was calculated on ₹1,100, not ₹1,000.

After 20 years: Meena has ₹3,000. Tina has over ₹6,700!

The moral? **Simple Interest is honest but lazy.** It never lets your interest earn its own interest. That's why banks love lending on compound and why examiners love asking you to *compare* the two.

## The Unforgettable Trick: "The Frozen Photo" 📸

> **SI = P × R × T / 100**

Read it as a sentence: **"PeeRTi over 100"** — imagine your friend Preeti standing on a weighing scale that always shows 100.

- **P** = Principal (the frozen photo — never changes)
- **R** = Rate (how fast the "thank you" grows per year)
- **T** = Time (how many years the photo sits there)

**Memory hook:** *"Simple interest is a frozen photo — the principal never ages, so the interest never grows."*

And the three golden rearrangements (just cover what you want to find):

- **P = (SI × 100) / (R × T)**
- **R = (SI × 100) / (P × T)**
- **T = (SI × 100) / (P × R)**

One formula, four disguises. Master the triangle and you never need to memorise four formulas.

## One More Thing Exams Love

If a sum **doubles** in T years at simple interest, the rate is simply:

> **R = 100 / T**

Doubled in 8 years? Rate = 12.5%. Trebled? The interest earned is 2P, so R = 200/T. The pattern: **(n − 1) × 100 / T** for a sum becoming n times itself.

## 🤔 Now You Try (Feynman's Final Question)

*Ramesh lends ₹5,000 at 8% simple interest. His friend Suresh lends the same amount at 8% compound interest. After 2 years, who earned more — and by exactly how much?*

If you can answer that (hint: the difference is only in the second year's interest on ₹400), you've truly understood Simple Interest — not just memorised it.
