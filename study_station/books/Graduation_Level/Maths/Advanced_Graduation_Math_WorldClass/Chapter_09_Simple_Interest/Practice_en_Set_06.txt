126. A sum of money at simple interest amounts to ₹2,240 in 2 years and to ₹2,600 in 5 years. The sum is:
(a) ₹2,000 (b) ₹2,120 (c) ₹1,880 (d) ₹1,960
Answer: (a)
Solution: The tempting trap is to take ₹2,240 as the principal, but that already includes 2 years of interest. Interest for 3 years = 2600 − 2240 = ₹360, so yearly interest = ₹120; interest for 2 years = ₹240, hence principal = 2240 − 240 = ₹2,000, so the answer is 2000.
Source: PYQ-style

127. A man lent ₹8,000 in two parts, one at 4% and the other at 6% per annum simple interest. If the total annual interest is ₹380, the part lent at 4% is:
(a) ₹3,000 (b) ₹4,000 (c) ₹5,000 (d) ₹6,000
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is assuming equal division, which would give ₹400 interest, not ₹380. Let the 4% part be x: 0.04x + 0.06(8000 − x) = 380 gives 480 − 0.02x = 380, so x = ₹5,000, so the answer is 5000.
Source: PYQ-style

128. The simple interest on a sum for 3 years at 8% per annum is ₹88 less than the simple interest on the same sum for 5 years at 7% per annum. The sum is:
(a) ₹1,000 (b) ₹960 (c) ₹800 (d) ₹1,200
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is subtracting the rates directly (8% − 7% = 1%) and dividing, which ignores the different time periods. Interest difference = P(5×7 − 3×8)/100 = 11P/100 = 88, giving P = ₹800, so the answer is 800.
Source: PYQ-style

129. A sum becomes ₹7,250 in 3 years and ₹8,750 in 5 years at simple interest. The rate per cent per annum is:
(a) 10% (b) 12% (c) 18% (d) 15%
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is computing the rate on ₹7,250 as principal, but that amount includes interest. Interest for 2 years = 8750 − 7250 = ₹1,500, so yearly interest = ₹750 and principal = 7250 − 2250 = ₹5,000. Rate = 750×100/5000 = 15%, so the answer is 15%.

130. A person invests ₹12,000 at 10% per annum simple interest for a certain period and receives ₹3,600 as interest. The period is:
(a) 3 years (b) 4 years (c) 2 years (d) 5 years
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is dividing 3600 by 1200 and misreading the result. Yearly interest = 12000×10/100 = ₹1,200, so time = 3600/1200 = 3 years, so the answer is 3 years.

131. The simple interest on a certain sum at 5% per annum for 4 years is ₹400 more than the simple interest on the same sum at 4% per annum for 5 years. The sum is:
(a) ₹20,000 (b) No such sum exists (c) ₹10,000 (d) ₹40,000
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is solving 20P/100 − 20P/100 = 400 as if it had a finite answer. Both interests equal 20P/100, so their difference is 0 for every P and can never be ₹400, so the answer is No such sum exists.
Source: PYQ-style

132. A sum of ₹15,500 is lent partly at 8% and partly at 10% per annum simple interest. The total interest after one year is ₹1,450. The part lent at 10% is:
(a) ₹8,500 (b) ₹9,500 (c) ₹10,500 (d) ₹11,500
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is splitting the sum equally, which gives interest ₹1,390, not ₹1,450. Had the whole sum earned 8%, interest would be ₹1,240; the excess ₹210 comes from the extra 2% on the 10% part, so that part = 210×100/2 = ₹10,500, so the answer is 10500.
Source: PYQ-style

133. If the simple interest on a sum at 6% per annum for 6 years equals the simple interest on ₹9,000 at 8% per annum for 4 years, the sum is:
(a) ₹8,000 (b) ₹7,200 (c) ₹9,600 (d) ₹6,400
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is equating the products 6×6 and 8×4 while keeping the same principal. Interest on ₹9,000 = 9000×8×4/100 = ₹2,880; then P×6×6/100 = 2880 gives P = 288000/36 = ₹8,000, so the answer is 8000.
Source: PYQ-style

134. A sum doubles itself in 8 years at simple interest. In how many years will it become four times itself at the same rate?
(a) 16 years (b) 32 years (c) 20 years (d) 24 years
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is doubling the time to 16 years, but four times the amount means interest equal to three times the principal. Doubling needs interest = P in 8 years, so interest = 3P needs 24 years, so the answer is 24 years.

135. The rate of simple interest at which a sum becomes 5/4 of itself in 4 years is:
(a) 5% (b) 5.5% (c) 6.25% (d) 6%
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is taking 5/4 as a 25% rate instead of 25% total interest over 4 years. Interest = P/4 in 4 years, so rate = (P/4)×100/(P×4) = 6.25%, so the answer is 6.25%.

136. A man borrowed ₹25,000 at simple interest and paid ₹32,500 after 2 years 6 months. The rate per annum was:
(a) 10% (b) 11% (c) 9% (d) 12%

Solution: The trap is using 2 years instead of 2.5 years in the formula. Interest = 32500 − 25000 = ₹7,500; rate = 7500×100/(25000×2.5) = 750000/62500 = 12%, so the answer is 12%.

138. The simple interest on ₹4,800 invested for 2 years is ₹768. The rate of interest per annum is:

139. The simple interest on ₹7,500 from 4 February 2021 to 18 April 2021 at 8% per annum (ordinary simple interest, 365 days) is approximately:
(a) ₹118 (b) ₹120 (c) ₹122 (d) ₹125
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is counting 74 days by including both end dates. Days = 24 (Feb) + 31 (Mar) + 18 (Apr) = 73 days; SI = 7500×8×73/(100×365) = ₹120, so the answer is 120.

140. A lends ₹6,000 to B for 2 years and ₹3,000 to C for 4 years at the same simple interest rate, receiving ₹1,800 as total interest. The rate is:
(a) 8% (b) 6% (c) 7.5% (d) 9%
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is averaging the principals or the times instead of adding the principal-time products. Total interest = (6000×2 + 3000×4)×r/100 = 24000r/100 = 1800, so r = 7.5%, so the answer is 7.5%.
Source: PYQ-style

141. A sum was invested at simple interest at a certain rate for 3 years. Had it been invested at 4% higher rate, it would have fetched ₹720 more. The sum is:
(a) ₹7,200 (b) ₹5,000 (c) ₹6,000 (d) ₹4,800
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is dividing 720 by 4 to get 180 and stopping there. Extra interest = P×4×3/100 = 720, so P = 720×100/12 = ₹6,000, so the answer is 6000.
Source: PYQ-style

142. The simple interest on a sum is 16/25 of the principal, and the number of years equals the rate per cent. The rate is:
(a) 7% (b) 8% (c) 6% (d) 9%
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is setting r×t = 16 instead of 64. SI/P = r²/100 = 16/25, so r² = 64 and r = 8%, so the answer is 8%.

143. A man deposits ₹10,000 in a bank at 5% simple interest per annum. After how many years will the amount become ₹13,000?
(a) 5 years (b) 6 years (c) 4 years (d) 7 years

144. Two equal sums are lent at 6% and 9% simple interest. The first is lent for 2 years more than the second, yet both earn equal interest. The time for the second sum is:
(a) 5 years (b) 4 years (c) 3 years (d) 6 years
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is setting 6(t+2) = 9t and mis-solving it. 6t + 12 = 9t gives 3t = 12, so t = 4 years, so the answer is 4 years.

145. A sum of ₹12,500 amounts to ₹15,500 in 4 years at simple interest. If the rate is increased by 2% per annum, the new amount after 4 years would be:
(a) ₹16,500 (b) ₹16,000 (c) ₹16,250 (d) ₹17,000
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is adding 2% of the amount instead of extra interest on the principal. Original rate = 3000×100/(12500×4) = 6%; new rate 8% gives SI = 12500×8×4/100 = ₹4,000, amount = ₹16,500, so the answer is 16500.
Source: PYQ-style

146. A person borrows ₹8,000 at 12% per annum simple interest and repays it in two equal annual instalments of principal at the end of each year. The lender's total simple interest earned over the 2 years on the reducing balance is:
(a) ₹1,440 (b) ₹1,920 (c) ₹960 (d) ₹1,200
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is charging 12% on ₹8,000 for 2 full years (₹1,920), but the balance halves after year 1. Interest = 8000×12/100 + 4000×12/100 = 960 + 480 = ₹1,440, so the answer is 1440.

147. The simple interest on a sum at r% for r years is ₹256, and the principal is ₹1,600. The value of r is:

149. A trader lends ₹20,000 at 10% per annum simple interest for 3 years, then lends the entire amount received at 8% per annum simple interest for 2 more years. The total interest earned over 5 years is:
(a) ₹10,160 (b) ₹9,600 (c) ₹10,800 (d) ₹11,200
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is computing both interests on ₹20,000, which gives ₹6,000 + ₹3,200 = ₹9,200, or adding the rates wrongly. First SI = 20000×10×3/100 = ₹6,000; new principal = ₹26,000; second SI = 26000×8×2/100 = ₹4,160; total = ₹10,160, so the answer is 10160.
Source: PYQ-style

150. A sum of money amounts to ₹9,800 in 5 years and ₹12,005 in 8 years at the same simple interest rate. The rate per cent per annum is:
(a) 7% (b) 6% (c) 5% (d) 12%
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is dividing the 3-year interest by ₹9,800, which includes interest. Interest for 3 years = 12005 − 9800 = ₹2,205, so yearly interest = ₹735 and principal = 9800 − 5×735 = ₹6,125. Rate = 735×100/6125 = 12%, so the answer is 12%.
Source: PYQ-style
