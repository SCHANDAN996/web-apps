26. A sum of ₹8,400 is lent at simple interest for 3 years. If the total interest earned is ₹1,512, what is the annual rate of interest?
(a) 4% (b) 6% (c) 5% (d) 8%
Answer: (b)
Solution: The tempting wrong value 8% comes from dividing the interest by only one year's principal share instead of spreading it over 3 years. Use SI = PRT/100: 1512 = (8400 × R × 3)/100, so R = 151200/25200 = 6. So the answer is 6%.
Source: NCERT Class 8 Mathematics (Comparing Quantities)

27. At what rate percent per annum will a sum of ₹6,250 amount to ₹7,750 in 4 years at simple interest?
(a) 5% (b) 6% (c) 7% (d) 8%
Answer: (b)
Solution: Many students divide 1500 by 6250 and get 24%, forgetting to divide by the time period. Interest = 7750 − 6250 = ₹1,500. Rate = (1500 × 100)/(6250 × 4) = 150000/25000 = 6. So the answer is 6%.
Source: NCERT Class 8 Mathematics (Comparing Quantities)

28. In how many years will ₹4,500 earn the same simple interest at 8% per annum as ₹6,000 earns in 3 years at 5% per annum?
(a) 2 years (b) 4 years (c) 2.5 years (d) 3 years
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is assuming equal principals, which would make the times equal. Interest from the second sum = (6000 × 5 × 3)/100 = ₹900. For the first sum: 900 = (4500 × 8 × T)/100, so T = 90000/36000 = 2.5. So the answer is 2.5 years.
Source: PYQ-style

29. A sum of money doubles itself in 10 years at simple interest. In how many years will it triple itself at the same rate?
(a) 20 years (b) 15 years (c) 25 years (d) 30 years
Answer: (a)
Solution: The tempting wrong answer 30 years comes from assuming tripling takes 3 × 10 years, but doubling means earning interest equal to the principal once. Doubling in 10 years means rate = 10% per annum. Tripling needs interest = 2P, so T = (2P × 100)/(P × 10) = 20. So the answer is 20 years.
Source: PYQ-style

30. ₹12,000 is invested partly at 6% and partly at 9% per annum simple interest. If the total annual interest is ₹930, how much is invested at 9%?
(a) ₹7,000 (b) ₹6,000 (c) ₹5,000 (d) ₹8,000
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is splitting the money equally, which gives interest of ₹900, not ₹930. Let x be at 9%: 0.09x + 0.06(12000 − x) = 930, so 0.03x = 210, giving x = 7000. So the answer is ₹7,000.
Source: PYQ-style

31. The simple interest on a sum for 5 years is two-fifths of the sum. What is the rate of interest per annum?
(a) 6% (b) 10% (c) 8% (d) 12%
Answer: (c)
Solution: Students often take 2/5 as 40% and report that as the rate, ignoring the 5-year spread. SI = (2/5)P, so (P × R × 5)/100 = 2P/5, giving R = 40/5 = 8. So the answer is 8%.
Source: NCERT Class 8 Mathematics (Comparing Quantities)

32. A man borrowed ₹15,000 at 12% per annum simple interest. He repaid ₹9,000 after 2 years. How much should he pay at the end of the 4th year to clear the debt (interest calculated on the original principal throughout)?
(a) ₹12,600 (b) ₹14,400 (c) ₹13,200 (d) ₹13,800
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is recomputing interest on the reduced principal after repayment, but the question fixes interest on the original principal. Total interest for 4 years = (15000 × 12 × 4)/100 = ₹7,200; total due = ₹22,200. Amount to pay = 22200 − 9000 = 13200. So the answer is ₹13,200.
Source: PYQ-style

33. At a certain rate of simple interest, a sum amounts to ₹11,500 in 3 years and ₹12,500 in 5 years. What is the sum?
(a) ₹10,500 (b) ₹9,500 (c) ₹9,000 (d) ₹10,000
Answer: (d)
Solution: The tempting error is averaging the two amounts. Interest for 2 years = 12500 − 11500 = ₹1,000, so interest for 3 years = ₹1,500. Principal = 11500 − 1500 = 10000. So the answer is ₹10,000.
Source: PYQ-style

34. A sum amounts to ₹6,800 in 3 years and ₹8,000 in 5 years at simple interest. What is the rate percent per annum?
(a) 12% (b) 10% (c) 8% (d) 15%
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is dividing the 2-year interest by 5 years. Interest for 2 years = 8000 − 6800 = ₹1,200, so yearly interest = ₹600. Principal = 6800 − 3 × 600 = ₹5,000. Rate = (600 × 100)/5000 = 12. So the answer is 12%.
Source: PYQ-style

35. A sum was put at simple interest for 4 years. Had the rate been 2% per annum higher, it would have fetched ₹480 more. The sum is:
(a) ₹6,000 (b) ₹5,000 (c) ₹6,500 (d) ₹7,500
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is dividing 480 by 2% alone, which ignores the 4-year period and gives ₹24,000. Extra interest = (P × 2 × 4)/100 = 480, so P = 48000/8 = 6000. So the answer is ₹6,000.
Source: PYQ-style

36. The simple interest on ₹9,600 for 2 years 6 months at 8% per annum is:
(a) ₹1,536 (b) ₹1,728 (c) ₹1,920 (d) ₹2,016
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is using 2 years instead of 2.5 years, which gives ₹1,536. SI = (9600 × 8 × 2.5)/100 = 9600 × 20/100 = 1920. So the answer is ₹1,920.
Source: NCERT Class 8 Mathematics (Comparing Quantities)

37. A sum becomes ₹13,200 in 4 years and ₹15,600 in 7 years at simple interest. The rate of interest per annum is:
(a) 10% (b) 6% (c) 5% (d) 8%
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is treating ₹2,400 as one year's interest. Interest for 3 years = 15600 − 13200 = ₹2,400, so yearly interest = ₹800. Principal = 13200 − 4 × 800 = ₹10,000, and rate = (800 × 100)/10000 = 8. So the answer is 8%.
Source: PYQ-style

38. Ramesh lends ₹20,000 to Suresh at 10% per annum simple interest for 3 years. Suresh lends the same amount to Mahesh at 12% per annum simple interest for the same period. Suresh's gain is:
(a) ₹1,000 (b) ₹1,500 (c) ₹2,000 (d) ₹1,200
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is computing the gain on the full amount including interest. Gain = difference of interests = (20000 × (12 − 10) × 3)/100 = 20000 × 6/100 = 1200. So the answer is ₹1,200.
Source: PYQ-style

39. A certain sum earns simple interest of ₹1,750 in 7 years. Had the interest been 2% more per annum, how much more would it have earned, given the principal is ₹5,000?
(a) ₹700 (b) ₹600 (c) ₹500 (d) ₹800
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is applying 2% to the interest ₹1,750 instead of to the principal. Extra interest = (5000 × 2 × 7)/100 = 700. So the answer is ₹700.
Source: PYQ-style

40. What sum of money will amount to ₹9,300 in 3 years at 8% per annum simple interest?
(a) ₹7,500 (b) ₹7,200 (c) ₹7,000 (d) ₹7,800
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is subtracting 24% of ₹9,300 from ₹9,300, which wrongly applies the rate to the amount instead of the principal. Amount = P(1 + 24/100) = 1.24P, so P = 9300/1.24 = 7500. So the answer is ₹7,500.
Source: PYQ-style

41. The difference between the simple interests received from two banks on ₹8,000 for 2 years is ₹320. What is the difference between their rates of interest?
(a) 2.5% (b) 1.5% (c) 1% (d) 2%
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is dividing 320 by 8000 and reporting 4%, forgetting the 2-year factor. Difference in interest = (P × difference in rate × T)/100, so 320 = (8000 × d × 2)/100, giving d = 32000/16000 = 2. So the answer is 2%.
Source: PYQ-style

42. A sum of ₹5,600 is divided into two parts such that the simple interest on the first part at 5% for 4 years equals the simple interest on the second part at 4% for 5 years. The first part is:
(a) ₹2,800 (b) ₹2,600 (c) ₹3,000 (d) ₹3,200
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is dividing the sum in the ratio 5:4 directly instead of using the full rate-time products. Let parts be x and 5600 − x: x × 5 × 4 = (5600 − x) × 4 × 5, so 20x = 20(5600 − x), giving x = 2800. So the answer is ₹2,800.
Source: PYQ-style

43. At simple interest, a sum becomes 5/4 of itself in 5 years. The rate percent per annum is:
(a) 4% (b) 8% (c) 6% (d) 5%
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is taking 5/4 as 125% and reporting 25% as the rate. Interest = P/4 over 5 years, so R = (P/4 × 100)/(P × 5) = 100/20 = 5. So the answer is 5%.
Source: NCERT Class 8 Mathematics (Comparing Quantities)

44. A person invests ₹6,400 at 7.5% per annum simple interest. After how many years will the interest be ₹1,920?
(a) 3 years (b) 4 years (c) 5 years (d) 6 years
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is computing 1920/6400 = 30% and dividing by 7.5 incorrectly as 30 − 7.5. T = (1920 × 100)/(6400 × 7.5) = 192000/48000 = 4. So the answer is 4 years.
Source: NCERT Class 8 Mathematics (Comparing Quantities)

45. ₹10,000 is deposited at simple interest for 2 years. The rate is 6% for the first year and 8% for the second year. The total interest is:
(a) ₹1,200 (b) ₹1,400 (c) ₹1,600 (d) ₹1,480
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is adding interest on the grown amount in the second year, which is compound interest thinking and gives ₹1,480. In simple interest the principal stays fixed: SI = (10000 × 6 × 1)/100 + (10000 × 8 × 1)/100 = 600 + 800 = 1400. So the answer is ₹1,400.
Source: PYQ-style

46. The simple interest on a sum at 9% per annum for 4 years is ₹2,880. What would be the simple interest on the same sum at 12% per annum for 3 years?
(a) ₹3,200 (b) ₹2,880 (c) ₹3,600 (d) ₹2,400
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is scaling the interest by the rate ratio 12/9 only, ignoring the changed time. Principal = (2880 × 100)/(9 × 4) = ₹8,000. New SI = (8000 × 12 × 3)/100 = 2880. So the answer is ₹2,880.
Source: PYQ-style

47. A sum of money at simple interest amounts to ₹4,640 in 2 years and ₹5,120 in 4 years. The sum is:
(a) ₹4,000 (b) ₹4,160 (c) ₹4,200 (d) ₹4,320
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is halving ₹5,120 or averaging the two amounts. Interest for 2 years = 5120 − 4640 = ₹480, so interest for the first 2 years is also ₹480. Principal = 4640 − 480 = 4160. So the answer is ₹4,160.
Source: PYQ-style

48. If the rate of simple interest increases from 8% to 10% per annum, a person's yearly income from a fixed deposit increases by ₹650. The deposit is:
(a) ₹30,000 (b) ₹35,000 (c) ₹32,500 (d) ₹40,000
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is dividing 650 by 10% or by 8% instead of by the difference of 2%. Increase = P × 2/100 = 650, so P = 650 × 50 = 32500. So the answer is ₹32,500.
Source: PYQ-style

49. A lent ₹6,000 to B for 2 years and ₹4,000 to C for 4 years at the same rate of simple interest, and received ₹2,800 in all as interest. The rate per annum is:
(a) 8% (b) 6% (c) 10% (d) 5%
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is treating the two loans as one ₹10,000 loan for an average of 3 years. Total interest = (6000 × R × 2 + 4000 × R × 4)/100 = (12000R + 16000R)/100 = 280R. Setting 280R = 2800 gives R = 10. So the answer is 10%.
Source: PYQ-style

50. A sum was put at simple interest at a certain rate for 3 years. Had it been put at 4% higher rate, it would have fetched ₹720 more. The sum is:
(a) ₹5,000 (b) ₹8,000 (c) ₹7,200 (d) ₹6,000
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is dividing 720 by 4% alone, which ignores the 3-year period and gives ₹18,000. Extra interest = (P × 4 × 3)/100 = 720, so P = 72000/12 = 6000. So the answer is ₹6,000.
Source: PYQ-style
