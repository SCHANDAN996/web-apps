76. A sum of money at simple interest amounts to ₹8,150 in 3 years and to ₹8,540 in 4 years. The sum is:
(a) ₹6,980 (b) ₹6,900 (c) ₹6,500 (d) ₹7,000
Answer: (a)
Solution: The interest for 1 year = 8,540 − 8,150 = ₹390, so interest for 3 years = ₹1,170 and principal = 8,150 − 1,170 = ₹6,980. The tempting wrong choice 7,000 comes from rounding the amount instead of subtracting the yearly interest, so the answer is ₹6,980.
Source: PYQ-style

77. A certain sum is invested at simple interest at 6% per annum. In how many years will the sum double itself?
(a) 16⅔ years (b) 20 years (c) 18 years (d) 15 years
Answer: (a)
Solution: For the sum to double, SI must equal the principal, so 100 = (100 × 6 × T)/100, giving T = 100/6 = 16⅔ years. The trap 15 years comes from premature rounding of 100/6, so the answer is 16⅔ years.
Source: NCERT Class 8 Mathematics (concept)

78. The simple interest on a sum for 5 years is two-fifths of the sum. The rate of interest per annum is:
(a) 8% (b) 6% (c) 10% (d) 12%
Answer: (a)
Solution: Let the sum be P; SI = (2/5)P = (P × R × 5)/100, so R = (2 × 100)/(5 × 5) = 8%. The tempting wrong choice 10% arises from dividing by 4 years instead of 5, so the answer is 8%.
Source: PYQ-style

79. A man borrowed ₹8,000 at 10% per annum simple interest and immediately lent the whole sum at 12% per annum simple interest. His gain per year is:
(a) ₹160 (b) ₹140 (c) ₹120 (d) ₹180
Answer: (a)
Solution: Gain per year = interest earned − interest paid = (8,000 × 12 × 1)/100 − (8,000 × 10 × 1)/100 = 960 − 800 = ₹160. The trap 120 comes from applying the 2% rate difference to a wrong principal, so the answer is ₹160.
Source: PYQ-style

80. A sum of ₹12,500 amounts to ₹15,500 in 4 years at simple interest. The rate of interest per annum is:
(a) 5% (b) 8% (c) 6% (d) 7%
Answer: (c)
Solution: SI = 15,500 − 12,500 = ₹3,000, so rate = (3,000 × 100)/(12,500 × 4) = 6%. The tempting wrong choice 5% comes from dividing by the amount 15,500 instead of the principal 12,500, so the answer is 6%.
Source: PYQ-style

81. The simple interest on a certain sum at 8% per annum for 6 years is ₹1,200 less than the sum itself. The sum is:
(a) ₹2,500 (b) ₹2,200 (c) ₹2,000 (d) ₹2,307.69
Answer: (d)
Solution: SI = (P × 8 × 6)/100 = 0.48P; given P − 0.48P = 1,200, so 0.52P = 1,200 and P = ₹2,307.69. The trap 2,500 comes from wrongly setting 0.48P = 1,200, so the answer is ₹2,307.69.
Source: PYQ-style

82. A sum was put at simple interest at a certain rate for 2 years. Had it been put at 3% higher rate, it would have fetched ₹360 more. The sum is:
(a) ₹6,000 (b) ₹7,200 (c) ₹6,500 (d) ₹5,000
Answer: (a)
Solution: Extra interest = (P × 3 × 2)/100 = 360, so P = (360 × 100)/6 = ₹6,000. The tempting wrong choice 5,000 comes from a factor error while rearranging the formula, so the answer is ₹6,000.

83. The simple interest on ₹7,000 at a certain rate for 3 years and on ₹5,000 at double that rate for 2 years together amount to ₹2,050. The original rate of interest is:
(a) 4% (b) 6% (c) 5% (d) 8%
Answer: (c)
Solution: Total SI = (7,000 × R × 3)/100 + (5,000 × 2R × 2)/100 = 210R + 200R = 410R = 2,050, so R = 5%. The trap 4% comes from adding the two principals before applying the rates, so the answer is 5%.
Source: PYQ-style

84. A person invests ₹6,000 at 5% per annum and ₹4,000 at 8% per annum, both at simple interest. After how many years will the total interest be ₹1,860?
(a) 2 years (b) 5 years (c) 4 years (d) 3 years
Answer: (d)
Solution: Yearly interest = (6,000 × 5)/100 + (4,000 × 8)/100 = 300 + 320 = ₹620, so time = 1,860/620 = 3 years. The tempting wrong choice 4 years comes from mixing up the two interest components, so the answer is 3 years.
Source: PYQ-style

85. A sum is invested at simple interest at 6% per annum for the first 3 years, 8% per annum for the next 4 years and 10% per annum for the period beyond 7 years. The total interest on ₹4,000 for 10 years is:
(a) ₹2,800 (b) ₹3,200 (c) ₹3,000 (d) ₹3,400
Answer: (b)
Solution: SI = (4,000/100) × (6×3 + 8×4 + 10×3) = 40 × (18 + 32 + 30) = 40 × 80 = ₹3,200. The trap 3,000 comes from applying 10% for only 2 years instead of 3, so the answer is ₹3,200.
Source: PYQ-style

86. A sum of money becomes 7/5 of itself in 4 years at simple interest. The rate of interest per annum is:
(a) 10% (b) 8% (c) 12% (d) 15%
Answer: (a)
Solution: SI = (7/5)P − P = (2/5)P, so (2/5)P = (P × R × 4)/100, giving R = (2 × 100)/(5 × 4) = 10%. The tempting wrong choice 8% comes from dividing by 5 years instead of 4, so the answer is 10%.
Source: PYQ-style

87. ₹2,000 is invested partly at 4% and partly at 6% simple interest. The total annual interest is ₹100. The amount invested at 6% is:
(a) ₹1,000 (b) ₹500 (c) ₹800 (d) ₹1,200
Answer: (a)
Solution: Let x be invested at 6%; then 6x + 4(2,000 − x) = 10,000, so 2x = 2,000 and x = ₹1,000. The trap 500 comes from assuming an equal split and halving again, so the answer is ₹1,000.
Source: PYQ-style

88. The simple interest on a sum of money is 1/9 of the principal and the number of years is equal to the rate percent per annum. The rate percent is:
(a) 3% (b) 3⅓% (c) 4% (d) 5%
Answer: (b)
Solution: Let rate = time = x; then (P × x × x)/100 = P/9, so x² = 100/9 and x = 10/3 = 3⅓%. The tempting wrong choice 3% comes from taking x² = 9 instead of 100/9, so the answer is 3⅓%.
Source: PYQ-style

89. A lent ₹5,000 to B for 2 years and ₹3,000 to C for 4 years at the same rate of simple interest and received ₹2,200 in all as interest. The rate of interest per annum is:
(a) 5% (b) 8% (c) 10% (d) 7%
Answer: (c)
Solution: Total SI = (5,000 × R × 2)/100 + (3,000 × R × 4)/100 = 100R + 120R = 220R = 2,200, so R = 10%. The trap 8% comes from adding the principals and averaging the times wrongly, so the answer is 10%.
Source: PYQ-style

90. A sum at simple interest at 13.5% per annum amounts to ₹2,502.50 after 4 years. The sum is:
(a) ₹1,525 (b) ₹1,575 (c) ₹1,625 (d) ₹1,675
Answer: (c)
Solution: Amount = P(1 + 13.5 × 4/100) = 1.54P = 2,502.50, so P = 2,502.50/1.54 = ₹1,625. The tempting wrong choice 1,575 comes from using a wrong multiplier, so the answer is ₹1,625.
Source: PYQ-style

91. If the simple interest on a sum of money for 2 years at 5% per annum is ₹50, what is the compound interest on the same sum at the same rate for the same time?
(a) ₹54 (b) ₹52 (c) ₹52.50 (d) ₹51.25
Answer: (d)
Solution: Principal = (50 × 100)/(5 × 2) = ₹500; CI = 500[(1.05)² − 1] = 500 × 0.1025 = ₹51.25. The trap 52 comes from adding a flat ₹2 to the simple interest instead of the interest-on-interest of ₹1.25, so the answer is ₹51.25.
Source: PYQ-style

92. A man invests a certain sum at 6% per annum simple interest and earns ₹900 as interest in 5 years. If he had invested the same sum at 8% per annum for 4 years, the interest would have been:
(a) ₹900 (b) ₹1,000 (c) ₹960 (d) ₹1,080
Answer: (c)
Solution: Principal = (900 × 100)/(6 × 5) = ₹3,000; new SI = (3,000 × 8 × 4)/100 = ₹960. The tempting wrong choice 1,080 comes from keeping 5 years with the new rate, so the answer is ₹960.
Source: PYQ-style

93. The difference between the simple interest received from two different banks on ₹1,500 for 3 years is ₹135. The difference between their rates of interest is:
(a) 2% (b) 3% (c) 2.5% (d) 3.5%
Answer: (b)
Solution: 135 = (1,500 × ΔR × 3)/100, so ΔR = (135 × 100)/(1,500 × 3) = 3%. The trap 2% comes from forgetting to account for the 3 years, so the answer is 3%.

94. A sum of ₹10,000 is lent in two parts such that the simple interest on the first part at 5% for 4 years equals the simple interest on the second part at 6% for 5 years. The first part is:
(a) ₹4,000 (b) ₹5,000 (c) ₹7,000 (d) ₹6,000
Answer: (d)
Solution: Let the first part be x; then 20x = 30(10,000 − x), so 50x = 3,00,000 and x = ₹6,000. The tempting wrong choice 5,000 comes from assuming equal division, so the answer is ₹6,000.

95. A sum of money at simple interest trebles itself in 15 years. It will become 5 times itself in:
(a) 35 years (b) 25 years (c) 30 years (d) 40 years
Answer: (c)
Solution: Trebling means SI = 2P in 15 years, so yearly interest = 2P/15; to become 5 times, SI must be 4P, needing 4P ÷ (2P/15) = 30 years. The trap 25 years comes from wrongly scaling 15 × (5/3), so the answer is 30 years.
Source: PYQ-style

96. The simple interest on a sum of money at 4% per annum for 2 years is ₹80. The compound interest on the same sum for the same period at the same rate is:
(a) ₹82.40 (b) ₹82 (c) ₹84 (d) ₹81.60
Answer: (d)
Solution: Principal = (80 × 100)/(4 × 2) = ₹1,000; CI = 1,000[(1.04)² − 1] = 1,000 × 0.0816 = ₹81.60. The tempting wrong choice 82 comes from rounding the interest-on-interest of ₹1.60 up to ₹2, so the answer is ₹81.60.
Source: PYQ-style

97. A person deposited ₹400 for 2 years, ₹550 for 4 years and ₹1,200 for 6 years at the same rate of simple interest. He received a total simple interest of ₹1,020. The rate of interest per annum is:
(a) 8% (b) 10% (c) 12% (d) 15%
Answer: (b)
Solution: Total SI = (R/100)(400×2 + 550×4 + 1,200×6) = (R/100)(800 + 2,200 + 7,200) = 102R = 1,020, so R = 10%. The trap 8% comes from adding the deposits and using an average time, so the answer is 10%.
Source: PYQ-style

98. A sum of ₹1,550 was lent partly at 5% and partly at 8% simple interest. The total interest received after 3 years was ₹300. The ratio of money lent at 5% to that at 8% is:
(a) 8 : 5 (b) 16 : 15 (c) 5 : 8 (d) 31 : 6
Answer: (b)
Solution: Let x be lent at 5%; then 15x + 24(1,550 − x) = 30,000, so 9x = 7,200 and x = 800, giving the ratio 800 : 750 = 16 : 15. The tempting wrong choice 5 : 8 comes from simply reversing the rates, so the answer is 16 : 15.
Source: PYQ-style

99. What annual instalment will discharge a debt of ₹4,200 due in 5 years at 10% simple interest?
(a) ₹600 (b) ₹800 (c) ₹750 (d) ₹700
Answer: (d)
Solution: Let the instalment be x; the five instalments earn interest for 4, 3, 2, 1 and 0 years, so x[5 + (10/100)(4+3+2+1+0)] = 4,200, giving 6x = 4,200 and x = ₹700. The trap 800 comes from mishandling the interest on instalments, so the answer is ₹700.
Source: PYQ-style

100. A sum of money amounts to ₹5,200 in 5 years and to ₹5,680 in 7 years at simple interest. The rate of interest per annum is:
(a) 4% (b) 6% (c) 5% (d) 8%
Answer: (b)
Solution: Interest for 2 years = 5,680 − 5,200 = ₹480, so yearly interest = ₹240 and principal = 5,200 − 5 × 240 = ₹4,000; rate = (240 × 100)/4,000 = 6%. The tempting wrong choice 5% comes from using a wrong principal obtained by mis-subtraction, so the answer is 6%.
Source: PYQ-style
