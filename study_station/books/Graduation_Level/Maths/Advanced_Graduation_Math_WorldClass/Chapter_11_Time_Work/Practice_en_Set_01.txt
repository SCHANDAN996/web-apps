1. A can do a piece of work in 10 days. In how many days can he do the same work if he works at half his usual efficiency?
(a) 20 days (b) 5 days (c) 15 days (d) 25 days
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is to halve the time instead of doubling it — lower efficiency means more time, not less. Half efficiency means half the work per day, so the work takes twice as long: 10 × 2 = 20 days. So the answer is 20 days.
Source: NCERT Class 8 Mathematics (Direct and Inverse Proportions)

2. A can complete a work in 12 days. What part of the work does he complete in one day?
(a) 1/6 (b) 1/8 (c) 1/10 (d) 1/12
Answer: (d)
Solution: The tempting error is to confuse one day's work with the total days. One day's work is the reciprocal of the total time taken, which is 1/12. So the answer is 1/12.
Source: NCERT Class 8 Mathematics

3. A can do a work in 6 days and B can do the same work in 12 days. Working together, in how many days will they finish it?
(a) 9 days (b) 4 days (c) 8 days (d) 3 days
Answer: (b)
Solution: Many students wrongly average the times and pick a value near 9 days. One day's combined work = 1/6 + 1/12 = 2/12 + 1/12 = 3/12 = 1/4, so together they need 4 days. So the answer is 4 days.
Source: PYQ-style

4. A and B together can do a work in 8 days. A alone can do it in 12 days. In how many days can B alone do it?
(a) 24 days (b) 20 days (c) 16 days (d) 18 days
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is to subtract the days directly (12 − 8 = 4), which is meaningless. B's one-day work = 1/8 − 1/12 = 3/24 − 2/24 = 1/24, so B alone takes 24 days. So the answer is 24 days.
Source: PYQ-style

5. If 5 men can do a work in 20 days, in how many days can 10 men do the same work?
(a) 40 days (b) 5 days (c) 15 days (d) 10 days
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is to double the days along with the men, but men and days are inversely proportional. Total work = 5 × 20 = 100 man-days; with 10 men, days = 100/10 = 10. So the answer is 10 days.
Source: NCERT Class 8 Mathematics (Direct and Inverse Proportions)

6. A can do a work in 15 days. How much work does he do in 5 days?
(a) 1/2 (b) 1/5 (c) 1/3 (d) 2/3
Answer: (c)
Solution: The tempting wrong choice is 1/5, which confuses the number of days worked with the fraction done. In 5 days A completes 5 × (1/15) = 5/15 = 1/3 of the work. So the answer is 1/3.
Source: NCERT Class 8 Mathematics

7. A is twice as efficient as B. If B can do a work in 20 days, in how many days can A do it?
(a) 15 days (b) 40 days (c) 5 days (d) 10 days
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is to double the time for the more efficient worker; higher efficiency means less time. A works at double B's rate, so A takes 20/2 = 10 days. So the answer is 10 days.
Source: PYQ-style

8. A can do a work in 10 days and B in 15 days. What fraction of the work do they complete together in one day?
(a) 1/25 (b) 1/6 (c) 1/5 (d) 1/12
Answer: (b)
Solution: A common slip is to add the denominators and get 1/25. Combined one-day work = 1/10 + 1/15 = 3/30 + 2/30 = 5/30 = 1/6. So the answer is 1/6.
Source: PYQ-style

9. 8 men can complete a work in 12 days. After 4 days, how much work is left?
(a) 1/3 (b) 2/3 (c) 3/4 (d) 1/2
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is to report the work done (1/3) instead of the work left. In 4 days the work done = 4/12 = 1/3, so work left = 1 − 1/3 = 2/3. So the answer is 2/3.
Source: PYQ-style

10. A pipe can fill a tank in 6 hours. What part of the tank is filled in 2 hours?
(a) 1/3 (b) 1/2 (c) 1/4 (d) 2/3
Answer: (a)
Solution: The tempting error is to invert the ratio and pick 1/2. In 2 hours the pipe fills 2 × (1/6) = 1/3 of the tank. So the answer is 1/3.
Source: NCERT Class 8 Mathematics

11. A can do a work in 20 days and B in 30 days. Working together, in how many days will they complete the work?
(a) 25 days (b) 12 days (c) 10 days (d) 15 days
Answer: (b)
Solution: Averaging gives 25 days, which is the classic trap — combined work is always faster than either alone. One-day work = 1/20 + 1/30 = 3/60 + 2/60 = 5/60 = 1/12, so they take 12 days. So the answer is 12 days.
Source: PYQ-style

12. If A can do a work in 4 days and B in 6 days, together they finish the work in:
(a) 2.4 days (b) 5 days (c) 2 days (d) 3 days
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is to average 4 and 6 to get 5 days. One-day work = 1/4 + 1/6 = 3/12 + 2/12 = 5/12, so time = 12/5 = 2.4 days. So the answer is 2.4 days.
Source: PYQ-style

13. 12 workers can build a wall in 15 days. How many workers are needed to build it in 10 days?
(a) 18 (b) 20 (c) 16 (d) 8
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is to reduce workers when days reduce; fewer days needs more workers. Total work = 12 × 15 = 180 worker-days; workers needed = 180/10 = 18. So the answer is 18.
Source: NCERT Class 8 Mathematics (Direct and Inverse Proportions)

14. A does a work in 18 days. He works for 6 days and leaves. What fraction of the work remains?
(a) 2/3 (b) 1/3 (c) 1/2 (d) 3/4
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is to answer 1/3, which is the work done, not the work left. Work done in 6 days = 6/18 = 1/3, so remaining = 1 − 1/3 = 2/3. So the answer is 2/3.
Source: PYQ-style

15. A and B together can do a work in 12 days, B and C in 15 days, and C and A in 20 days. In how many days can A alone do the work?
(a) 24 days (b) 36 days (c) 30 days (d) 20 days
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is to average the three pair-times, which mixes up everyone's rates. Adding the pair rates: 2(A + B + C) = 1/12 + 1/15 + 1/20 = 5/60 + 4/60 + 3/60 = 12/60 = 1/5, so A + B + C = 1/10. Then A's rate = 1/10 − 1/15 = 3/30 − 2/30 = 1/30, so A alone takes 30 days. So the answer is 30 days.
Source: PYQ-style

16. A can do 1/3 of a work in 5 days. In how many days can he complete the whole work?
(a) 10 days (b) 8 days (c) 12 days (d) 15 days
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is to multiply 5 by 1/3 instead of dividing by it. If 1/3 of the work takes 5 days, the whole work takes 5 × 3 = 15 days. So the answer is 15 days.
Source: NCERT Class 8 Mathematics

17. A pipe can fill a tank in 8 hours and another pipe can empty it in 12 hours. If both are opened together, in how many hours will the tank be filled?
(a) 24 hours (b) 10 hours (c) 20 hours (d) 16 hours
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is to add the rates instead of subtracting the emptying pipe. Net filling per hour = 1/8 − 1/12 = 3/24 − 2/24 = 1/24, so the tank fills in 24 hours. So the answer is 24 hours.
Source: PYQ-style

18. A is 50% more efficient than B. If B takes 18 days to do a work, how many days will A take?
(a) 9 days (b) 27 days (c) 15 days (d) 12 days
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is to take 50% of 18 and subtract, getting 9 days — efficiency and time are not related that way. A's rate is 1.5 times B's, so A's time = 18/1.5 = 12 days. So the answer is 12 days.
Source: PYQ-style

19. 6 men or 10 women can do a work in 20 days. In how many days can 6 men and 10 women together do it?
(a) 8 days (b) 15 days (c) 12 days (d) 10 days
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is to add the days (20 + 20 = 40) or average them. 6 men alone take 20 days and 10 women alone take 20 days; together they form two such teams, so time = 20/2 = 10 days. So the answer is 10 days.
Source: PYQ-style

20. A can do a work in 9 days. He works alone for 3 days and then B joins him. What fraction of the work was left when B joined?
(a) 1/2 (b) 1/3 (c) 2/3 (d) 3/4
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is to state the work done (1/3) instead of the work left. In 3 days A completes 3/9 = 1/3, so the fraction left = 1 − 1/3 = 2/3. So the answer is 2/3.
Source: PYQ-style

21. A and B together can do a work in 12 days. They work together for 4 days, after which A leaves and B completes the remaining work alone in 20 days. In how many days can B alone do the whole work?
(a) 24 days (b) 20 days (c) 30 days (d) 36 days
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is to treat B's 20 days as his full capacity. In 4 days the pair completes 4/12 = 1/3 of the work, leaving 2/3, which B does in 20 days. So B's full time = 20 × (3/2) = 30 days. So the answer is 30 days.
Source: PYQ-style

22. A can do a work in 15 days and B in 20 days. They work together for 4 days. What fraction of the work is left?
(a) 7/15 (b) 2/3 (c) 8/15 (d) 1/2
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is to report the work done (7/15) instead of the work left. Combined rate = 1/15 + 1/20 = 4/60 + 3/60 = 7/60, so in 4 days they finish 28/60 = 7/15. Work left = 1 − 7/15 = 8/15. So the answer is 8/15.
Source: PYQ-style

23. A, B and C can do a work in 10, 12 and 15 days respectively. Working together, in how many days will they finish it?
(a) 5 days (b) 4 days (c) 6 days (d) 3 days
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is to average the three times, giving a value near 12. Combined rate = 1/10 + 1/12 + 1/15 = 6/60 + 5/60 + 4/60 = 15/60 = 1/4, so they finish in 4 days. So the answer is 4 days.
Source: PYQ-style

24. A can do a work in 24 days. B is 60% more efficient than A. In how many days can B do the same work?
(a) 18 days (b) 12 days (c) 15 days (d) 10 days
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is to reduce 24 by 60% and get a value near 10 days. B's rate = 1.6 × A's rate, so B's time = 24/1.6 = 15 days. So the answer is 15 days.
Source: PYQ-style

25. Two pipes can fill a tank in 20 minutes and 30 minutes respectively, while a third pipe can empty it in 60 minutes. If all three are opened together, in how many minutes will the tank be filled?
(a) 10 minutes (b) 15 minutes (c) 18 minutes (d) 12 minutes
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is to add all three rates, forgetting the third pipe empties the tank. Net rate = 1/20 + 1/30 − 1/60 = 3/60 + 2/60 − 1/60 = 4/60 = 1/15, so the tank fills in 15 minutes. So the answer is 15 minutes.
Source: PYQ-style
