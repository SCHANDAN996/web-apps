51. A can do a piece of work in 12 days and B can do it in 18 days. They work together for 4 days, after which A leaves. In how many more days will B finish the remaining work?
(a) 6 days (b) 10 days (c) 5 days (d) 8 days
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is to subtract only A's share from the work instead of the combined share. Combined rate = 1/12 + 1/18 = 5/36 per day, so in 4 days they complete 20/36 = 5/9, leaving 4/9. B alone needs (4/9) ÷ (1/18) = 8 days, so the answer is 8 days.
Source: PYQ-style

52. A is twice as efficient as B. Together they finish a work in 18 days. In how many days can A alone finish the work?
(a) 54 days (b) 36 days (c) 27 days (d) 24 days
Answer: (c)
Solution: The tempting error is to simply double 18 to get 36, ignoring the efficiency ratio. Let B's rate be x, so A's rate is 2x and combined 3x = 1/18, giving x = 1/54. A's rate = 2/54 = 1/27, so the answer is 27 days.
Source: PYQ-style

53. A and B can do a work in 20 and 30 days respectively. They work on alternate days, starting with A. In how many days will the work be completed?
(a) 23 days (b) 24 days (c) 22 days (d) 25 days
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is to average the two times and get 25 days. In each 2-day cycle the work done is 1/20 + 1/30 = 1/12, so after 11 cycles (22 days) 11/12 is done, leaving 1/12. On day 23, A does 1/20, leaving 1/30, which B completes on day 24, so the answer is 24 days.
Source: PYQ-style

54. 12 men can complete a work in 15 days. After working for 5 days, 4 men leave. In how many total days will the work be completed?
(a) 22 days (b) 25 days (c) 18 days (d) 20 days
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is to assume the remaining work still takes 10 days with fewer men. Total work = 12 × 15 = 180 man-days; 60 man-days are done in 5 days, leaving 120. With 8 men, that takes 120/8 = 15 more days, giving a total of 5 + 15 = 20 days, so the answer is 20 days.
Source: PYQ-style

55. A can do a work in 10 days and B can do it in 15 days. Working together, in how many days will they finish the work?
(a) 5 days (b) 9 days (c) 6 days (d) 8 days
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is to average 10 and 15 to get 12.5 or to add the denominators. Combined rate = 1/10 + 1/15 = 3/30 + 2/30 = 5/30 = 1/6 per day, so the answer is 6 days.
Source: NCERT Class 8 Mathematics

56. Pipe A can fill a tank in 6 hours and pipe B can empty it in 10 hours. If both are opened together, in how many hours will the tank be filled?
(a) 12 hours (b) 16 hours (c) 18 hours (d) 15 hours
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is to take the difference of the times, 10 − 6 = 4 hours. Net rate = 1/6 − 1/10 = 5/30 − 3/30 = 2/30 = 1/15 per hour, so the answer is 15 hours.
Source: PYQ-style

57. Pipe A can fill a tank in 8 hours, pipe B in 12 hours, and pipe C can empty it in 24 hours. If all three are opened together, in how many hours will the tank be filled?
(a) 6 hours (b) 8 hours (c) 4.8 hours (d) 12 hours
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is to add all three rates, forgetting that C empties the tank. Net rate = 1/8 + 1/12 − 1/24 = 3/24 + 2/24 − 1/24 = 4/24 = 1/6 per hour, so the answer is 6 hours.
Source: PYQ-style

58. A alone can do a work in 16 days. With B's help, the same work is done in 10 days. In how many days can B alone do the work?
(a) 24 days (b) 26⅔ days (c) 30 days (d) 20 days
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is to subtract the days directly, 16 − 10 = 6. B's rate = 1/10 − 1/16 = 8/80 − 5/80 = 3/80 per day, so B alone takes 80/3 days, so the answer is 26⅔ days.
Source: PYQ-style

59. 8 men or 12 women can do a work in 25 days. In how many days can 6 men and 11 women do the same work?
(a) 18 days (b) 20 days (c) 15 days (d) 12 days
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is to treat men and women as equally efficient. Since 8 men = 12 women, 1 man = 1.5 women, so 6 men + 11 women = 9 + 11 = 20 women-equivalents. 12 women take 25 days, so 20 women take 12 × 25/20 = 15 days, so the answer is 15 days.
Source: PYQ-style

60. A can do 1/3 of a work in 5 days and B can do 1/2 of the same work in 9 days. Working together, in how many days will they complete the work?
(a) 9 days (b) 10 days (c) 8 days (d) 90/11 days
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is to use 5 and 9 days directly as their full-work times. A's full time = 15 days and B's = 18 days, so combined rate = 1/15 + 1/18 = 11/90 per day, so the answer is 90/11 days.
Source: PYQ-style

61. A works twice as fast as B, and B works twice as fast as C. If all three together finish a work in 7 days, in how many days can C alone finish it?
(a) 49 days (b) 56 days (c) 42 days (d) 28 days
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is to multiply 7 by 4, taking only A's ratio. Let C's rate be x; then B = 2x and A = 4x, so 7x = 1/7 and x = 1/49, so the answer is 49 days.
Source: PYQ-style

62. A and B together can do a work in 12 days, B and C in 15 days, and A and C in 20 days. In how many days can A alone do the work?
(a) 24 days (b) 30 days (c) 36 days (d) 40 days
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is to average the three pair-times. Adding the three pair rates: 2(A + B + C) = 1/12 + 1/15 + 1/20 = 12/60 = 1/5, so A + B + C = 1/10. A's rate = 1/10 − 1/15 = 1/30, so the answer is 30 days.
Source: PYQ-style

63. A can complete a work in 24 days. He works alone for 8 days, then B joins him and together they finish the remaining work in 8 days. In how many days can B alone do the whole work?
(a) 18 days (b) 20 days (c) 24 days (d) 36 days
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is to credit B with the full 16 days instead of only the 8 joint days. In 8 days A does 8/24 = 1/3, leaving 2/3, so the joint rate is (2/3)/8 = 1/12 per day. B's rate = 1/12 − 1/24 = 1/24, so the answer is 24 days.
Source: PYQ-style

64. 6 men can do a work in 10 days and 8 women can do the same work in 15 days. In how many days can 3 men and 4 women together do the work?
(a) 12 days (b) 8 days (c) 15 days (d) 10 days
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is to add the group rates without scaling to the actual number of workers. One man's rate = 1/60 and one woman's rate = 1/120, so 3 men and 4 women together do 3/60 + 4/120 = 1/20 + 1/30 = 1/12 per day, so the answer is 12 days.
Source: PYQ-style

65. Pipe A can fill a tank in 10 hours and pipe B in 15 hours. Both are opened together for 3 hours, after which B is closed. How much more time will A need to fill the tank?
(a) 5 hours (b) 4 hours (c) 6 hours (d) 3 hours
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is to compute only A's share in the first 3 hours and ignore B's contribution. In 3 hours together they fill 3 × (1/10 + 1/15) = 3 × 1/6 = 1/2, leaving 1/2. A alone needs (1/2) ÷ (1/10) = 5 hours, so the answer is 5 hours.
Source: PYQ-style

66. A does a work in 20 days. B is 25% more efficient than A. In how many days will B do the same work?
(a) 16 days (b) 15 days (c) 18 days (d) 25 days
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is to reduce 20 days by 25% to get 15 days — efficiency and time are inversely, not directly, related. B's rate = 1.25 × (1/20) = 1/16 per day, so the answer is 16 days.
Source: PYQ-style

67. A and B together can finish a work in 12 days. A works alone for 6 days, then B finishes the remaining work in 21 days. In how many days can A alone finish the whole work?
(a) 18 days (b) 24 days (c) 30 days (d) 20 days
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is to treat the 6 and 21 days as solo full-work times. Think of it as 6 joint days plus 15 extra days of B: 6 joint days complete 6/12 = 1/2, so B's 15 extra days do the other 1/2, giving B's rate = 1/30. Then A's rate = 1/12 − 1/30 = 5/60 − 2/60 = 3/60 = 1/20, so the answer is 20 days.
Source: PYQ-style

68. 15 men can build a wall in 20 days working 8 hours a day. How many hours a day must 25 men work to build the same wall in 12 days?
(a) 6 hours (b) 12 hours (c) 8 hours (d) 10 hours
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is to set up the proportion with hours directly instead of through total man-hours. Total work = 15 × 20 × 8 = 2400 man-hours, so required hours per day = 2400/(25 × 12) = 8, so the answer is 8 hours.
Source: NCERT Class 8 Mathematics

69. A can do a work in 12 days. B is 50% more efficient than A. A and B start together, but after 3 days A leaves. In how many total days is the work completed?
(a) 5 days (b) 6 days (c) 7 days (d) 5.5 days
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is to use 12 days for B as well. B's time = 12/1.5 = 8 days; in 3 days together they do 3 × (1/12 + 1/8) = 3 × 5/24 = 5/8, leaving 3/8. B alone needs (3/8) ÷ (1/8) = 3 more days, giving 3 + 3 = 6 days, so the answer is 6 days.
Source: PYQ-style

70. Three pipes A, B and C can fill a tank in 4, 6 and 12 hours respectively. A is opened first, B is opened after 1 hour, and C is opened after 2 hours. In how many hours from the start will the tank be full?
(a) 2.5 hours (b) 3 hours (c) 2⅔ hours (d) 3⅓ hours
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is to assume all three pipes run from the start. In hour 1, A fills 1/4 = 3/12; in hour 2, A and B fill 1/4 + 1/6 = 5/12, so 8/12 = 2/3 is done and 1/3 remains. All three together fill 3/12 + 2/12 + 1/12 = 1/2 per hour, needing (1/3) ÷ (1/2) = 2/3 hour more, a total of 2 + 2/3 hours, so the answer is 2⅔ hours.
Source: PYQ-style

71. A is 25% more efficient than B. If B can do a work in 30 days, in how many days will A and B together do it?
(a) 12 days (b) 13⅓ days (c) 15 days (d) 14 days
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is to take A's time as 30 − 25% of 30 = 22.5 days. A's rate = 1.25 × (1/30) = 1/24, so combined rate = 1/24 + 1/30 = 5/120 + 4/120 = 9/120 = 3/40 per day, giving 40/3 days, so the answer is 13⅓ days.
Source: PYQ-style

72. A and B can do a work in 10 and 15 days. They start together, but A leaves 2 days before completion. In how many days is the work finished?
(a) 7.2 days (b) 6 days (c) 8 days (d) 6.5 days
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is to assume both work for the full time. Let the total time be T; B works all T days and A works (T − 2) days, so T/15 + (T − 2)/10 = 1. Multiplying by 30: 2T + 3T − 6 = 30, so 5T = 36 and T = 7.2, so the answer is 7.2 days.
Source: PYQ-style

73. 4 men and 6 women can do a work in 8 days, while 3 men and 7 women can do it in 10 days. In how many days can 10 women do the work?
(a) 30 days (b) 36 days (c) 45 days (d) 40 days
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is to average the two scenarios. Equating work: 8(4M + 6W) = 10(3M + 7W) gives 32M + 48W = 30M + 70W, so 2M = 22W and 1M = 11W. Then 4M + 6W = 50 women-equivalents finish in 8 days, so the work is 400 woman-days and 10 women need 40 days, so the answer is 40 days.
Source: PYQ-style

74. A pipe can fill a tank in 12 hours. Due to a leak at the bottom, it takes 18 hours to fill. In how many hours can the leak alone empty the full tank?
(a) 20 hours (b) 36 hours (c) 24 hours (d) 30 hours
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is to take the difference 18 − 12 = 6 hours as the leak's time. Net rate = 1/18, so the leak's rate = 1/12 − 1/18 = 3/36 − 2/36 = 1/36 per hour, so the answer is 36 hours.
Source: PYQ-style

75. A, B and C can do a work in 12, 18 and 24 days respectively. They work together and earn ₹3,900 for the job. What is B's share if the payment is divided in proportion to work done?
(a) ₹1,200 (b) ₹1,500 (c) ₹1,300 (d) ₹1,000
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is to divide in the ratio of days (12 : 18 : 24) instead of the ratio of rates. Rate ratio = 1/12 : 1/18 : 1/24 = 6 : 4 : 3, so B's share = 4/13 × 3900 = ₹1,200, so the answer is ₹1,200.
Source: PYQ-style
