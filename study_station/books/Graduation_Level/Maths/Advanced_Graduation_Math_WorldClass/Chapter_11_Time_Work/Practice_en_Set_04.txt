Questions 76–100: Medium

76. A can do a piece of work in 12 days and B can do it in 18 days. They work together for 4 days, after which A leaves. In how many more days will B finish the remaining work?
(a) 6 days (b) 8 days (c) 7 days (d) 5 days
Answer: (b)
Solution: Combined rate = 1/12 + 1/18 = 5/36 per day, so in 4 days they complete 20/36 = 5/9, leaving 4/9. The trap is dividing the remaining work by the combined rate even though A has left. B alone works at 1/18 per day, so time = (4/9) × 18 = 8, so the answer is 8 days.
Source: PYQ-style

77. A is twice as efficient as B. Together they finish a work in 12 days. In how many days can A alone finish the work?
(a) 20 days (b) 16 days (c) 18 days (d) 24 days
Answer: (c)
Solution: Let B's rate be x, then A's rate is 2x; combined 3x = 1/12, so x = 1/36 and A's rate = 1/18. The trap is doubling 12 to get 24, which gives B's time, not A's. A alone takes 18 days, so the answer is 18 days.
Source: NCERT Class 8 Mathematics

78. A and B can do a work in 20 and 30 days respectively. They start together, but A leaves 5 days before completion. In how many days is the work finished?
(a) 15 days (b) 16 days (c) 14 days (d) 17 days
Answer: (a)
Solution: In the last 5 days B works alone, completing 5/30 = 1/6 of the work. The remaining 5/6 was done together at 1/20 + 1/30 = 1/12 per day, taking (5/6) × 12 = 10 days. The trap is subtracting 5 from the together-time of 12 days. Total time = 10 + 5 = 15, so the answer is 15 days.
Source: PYQ-style

79. 12 men can complete a work in 18 days. After working for 6 days, 4 more men join. In how many more days will the work be completed?
(a) 9 days (b) 10 days (c) 11 days (d) 12 days
Answer: (a)
Solution: Total work = 12 × 18 = 216 man-days. In 6 days, 12 men do 72 man-days, leaving 144 man-days. The trap is forgetting that 4 men joined and dividing 144 by 12. With 16 men, time = 144/16 = 9, so the answer is 9 days.
Source: NCERT Class 8 Mathematics

80. A can do a work in 15 days, B in 20 days. They work on alternate days, starting with A. In how many days will the work be completed?
(a) 17 days (b) 16 days (c) 17½ days (d) 18 days
Answer: (a)
Solution: In each 2-day cycle, work done = 1/15 + 1/20 = 7/60. After 8 cycles (16 days), 56/60 is done, leaving 4/60 = 1/15, which A completes exactly on day 17. The trap is adding a full extra day for the remainder. The work finishes exactly on day 17, so the answer is 17 days.
Source: PYQ-style

81. A pipe can fill a tank in 12 hours and another can empty it in 20 hours. If both are opened together, in how many hours will the tank be filled?
(a) 32 hours (b) 28 hours (c) 24 hours (d) 30 hours
Answer: (d)
Solution: Net rate = 1/12 − 1/20 = (5 − 3)/60 = 1/30 per hour. The trap is adding the rates instead of subtracting the emptying pipe, which gives 7.5 hours. Time = 30 hours, so the answer is 30 hours.
Source: NCERT Class 8 Mathematics

82. A and B together can do a work in 8 days. A alone can do it in 12 days. They work together for 3 days, then A leaves. How long will B take to finish the rest?
(a) 15 days (b) 20 days (c) 18 days (d) 12 days
Answer: (a)
Solution: B's rate = 1/8 − 1/12 = 1/24 per day. In 3 days together they finish 3/8, leaving 5/8. The trap is using A's rate 1/12 for the remaining work, giving 7.5 days. B needs (5/8) × 24 = 15, so the answer is 15 days.
Source: PYQ-style

83. 8 men or 12 women can do a work in 25 days. In how many days can 6 men and 11 women do the same work?
(a) 20 days (b) 18 days (c) 22 days (d) 15 days
Answer: (d)
Solution: 8 men = 12 women, so 1 man = 1.5 women; hence 6 men = 9 women and the team equals 9 + 11 = 20 women. The trap is adding 6 + 11 = 17 workers directly without converting men into women. Work = 12 × 25 = 300 woman-days, so time = 300/20 = 15, so the answer is 15 days.
Source: PYQ-style

84. A takes 50% more time than B to do a work. If together they take 18 days, how long does B alone take?
(a) 45 days (b) 27 days (c) 36 days (d) 30 days
Answer: (d)
Solution: A takes 1.5 times B's time, so B's rate is 1.5 times A's rate; take A's rate as 2x and B's as 3x. Combined 5x = 1/18, so x = 1/90 and B's rate = 3/90 = 1/30. The trap is computing 18 × 1.5 = 27 directly. B alone takes 30 days, so the answer is 30 days.
Source: PYQ-style

85. A can do a work in 18 days. He works alone for 6 days, then B joins and together they finish the work in 8 more days. In how many days can B alone do the work?
(a) 24 days (b) 30 days (c) 36 days (d) 40 days
Answer: (c)
Solution: A works for all 14 days, completing 14/18 = 7/9, so B does 2/9 in 8 days. B's rate = (2/9)/8 = 1/36 per day. The trap is counting only 6 days of A's work instead of all 14. B alone takes 36 days, so the answer is 36 days.
Source: PYQ-style

86. Two pipes can fill a tank in 10 and 15 hours respectively. A third pipe empties it in 30 hours. If all three are opened, in how many hours will the tank fill?
(a) 5 hours (b) 6 hours (c) 8 hours (d) 7.5 hours
Answer: (d)
Solution: Net rate = 1/10 + 1/15 − 1/30 = (3 + 2 − 1)/30 = 4/30 = 2/15 per hour. The trap is adding all three rates, which gives 1/5 per hour and 5 hours. Time = 15/2 = 7.5, so the answer is 7.5 hours.
Source: NCERT Class 8 Mathematics

87. A is 60% more efficient than B. If B can do a work in 32 days, in how many days will A do it?
(a) 18 days (b) 22 days (c) 20 days (d) 24 days
Answer: (c)
Solution: A's rate = 1.6 × B's rate, so A's time = 32/1.6 = 20 days. The trap is reducing 32 by 60%, which wrongly gives about 13 days, because efficiency and time are inversely related. A takes 20 days, so the answer is 20 days.

88. A, B and C can do a work in 12, 15 and 20 days respectively. They work together for 2 days, then C leaves. In how many more days will A and B finish the work?
(a) 5 days (b) 6 days (c) 4 days (d) 7 days
Answer: (c)
Solution: Combined rate of all three = 1/12 + 1/15 + 1/20 = 12/60 = 1/5, so in 2 days they finish 2/5, leaving 3/5. The trap is continuing with the three-person rate after C leaves. A and B together work at 1/12 + 1/15 = 3/20 per day, so time = (3/5) ÷ (3/20) = 4, so the answer is 4 days.
Source: PYQ-style

89. 15 men can complete a work in 20 days working 8 hours a day. In how many days can 10 men complete the same work working 10 hours a day?
(a) 20 days (b) 25 days (c) 22 days (d) 24 days
Answer: (d)
Solution: Total work = 15 × 20 × 8 = 2400 man-hours. With 10 men at 10 hours/day, days = 2400/(10 × 10) = 24. The trap is ignoring hours and computing 15 × 20/10 = 30 days. The answer is 24 days.

90. A can do a work in 10 days, B in 15 days. They work together but A leaves after some days, and B completes the remaining work in 5 days. For how many days did they work together?
(a) 4 days (b) 3 days (c) 5 days (d) 6 days
Answer: (a)
Solution: In the last 5 days alone B does 5/15 = 1/3 of the work, so together they did 2/3 at rate 1/10 + 1/15 = 1/6 per day, taking (2/3) × 6 = 4 days. The trap is assuming A left exactly after 5 days. They worked together for 4 days, so the answer is 4 days.

91. A and B can do a work in 12 days, B and C in 15 days, A and C in 20 days. In how many days can C alone do the work?
(a) 40 days (b) 70 days (c) 50 days (d) 60 days
Answer: (d)
Solution: Adding the three pair rates: 2(A + B + C) = 1/12 + 1/15 + 1/20 = 1/5, so A + B + C = 1/10 per day. C's rate = 1/10 − 1/12 = 1/60. The trap is forgetting to halve the sum of the pair rates, which doubles every answer. C alone takes 60 days, so the answer is 60 days.
Source: PYQ-style

92. A tank has a leak that empties it in 8 hours. A tap fills it at 12 litres per minute. With the leak open, the tank fills in 24 hours. What is the capacity of the tank?
(a) 2880 litres (b) 5760 litres (c) 4320 litres (d) 8640 litres
Answer: (c)
Solution: If the tap alone fills the tank in T hours, then 1/T − 1/8 = 1/24, so 1/T = 1/6 and T = 6 hours. The trap is multiplying the tap rate by 24 hours, which ignores the leak and gives 8640 litres. Capacity = 12 × 60 × 6 = 4320 litres, so the answer is 4320 litres.
Source: PYQ-style

93. A can do a work in 16 days and B can do it in 48 days. They work together for 4 days, then C joins and the work finishes in 4 more days. In how many days can C alone do the work?
(a) 10 days (b) 12 days (c) 14 days (d) 16 days
Answer: (b)
Solution: A and B together work at 1/16 + 1/48 = 1/12 per day, so in 4 days they finish 1/3, leaving 2/3. All three finish this in 4 days, so their combined rate is 1/6 per day and C's rate = 1/6 − 1/12 = 1/12. The trap is crediting C with the whole remaining work instead of subtracting A and B's share. C alone takes 12 days, so the answer is 12 days.
Source: PYQ-style

94. A alone can finish a work in 18 days and B alone in 24 days. They work on alternate days starting with B. On which day will the work be completed?
(a) 20⅔ day (b) 21st day (c) 21½ day (d) 20th day
Answer: (a)
Solution: Per 2-day cycle, work done = 1/18 + 1/24 = 7/72. After 10 cycles (20 days), 70/72 is done, leaving 2/72 = 1/36. Day 21 is B's turn and B works at 1/24 per day, needing (1/36)/(1/24) = 2/3 of a day. The trap is adding a full 21st day. The work ends on the 20⅔ day, so the answer is 20⅔ day.
Source: PYQ-style

95. 4 men and 6 women can complete a work in 8 days, while 3 men and 7 women can complete it in 10 days. In how many days will 10 women complete it?
(a) 40 days (b) 50 days (c) 45 days (d) 35 days
Answer: (a)
Solution: Let a man's daily work be m and a woman's be w: 4m + 6w = 1/8 and 3m + 7w = 1/10. Eliminating m gives 10w = 4/10 − 3/8 = 1/40. The trap is solving for the man's rate when the question asks about women. Ten women do 1/40 per day, so the answer is 40 days.
Source: PYQ-style

96. A can do a work in 25 days. He works for 5 days and then B finishes the remaining work in 16 days. In how many days can A and B together do the whole work?
(a) 10 days (b) 100/9 days (c) 200/9 days (d) 12 days
Answer: (b)
Solution: In 5 days A completes 5/25 = 1/5, leaving 4/5, which B does in 16 days, so B's rate = 1/20 per day. Combined rate = 1/25 + 1/20 = 9/100. The trap is averaging 25 and 20 instead of adding the rates. Together they take 100/9 days, so the answer is 100/9 days.

97. Pipe A fills a tank in 6 hours, pipe B in 8 hours. They are opened alternately for 1 hour each, starting with A. In how many hours will the tank be full?
(a) 6½ hours (b) 6¾ hours (c) 7 hours (d) 7¼ hours
Answer: (b)
Solution: Per 2-hour cycle, 1/6 + 1/8 = 7/24 is filled. After 3 cycles (6 hours), 21/24 is filled, leaving 3/24 = 1/8. Hour 7 is A's turn, and A fills 1/6 per hour, needing (1/8)/(1/6) = 3/4 hour. The trap is giving the 7th hour to B. Total = 6¾ hours, so the answer is 6¾ hours.
Source: PYQ-style

Answer: (b)
Solution: In the final 5 days alone B does 5/15 = 1/3 of the work, so together they completed 2/3 at rate 1/10 + 1/15 = 1/6 per day, taking (2/3) × 6 = 4 days. The trap is assuming A worked for the same 5 days that B worked alone. A worked for 4 days, so the answer is 4 days.

99. A is thrice as good a workman as B and finishes a work 40 days earlier than B. In how many days can they finish the work together?
(a) 12 days (b) 18 days (c) 15 days (d) 20 days
Answer: (c)
Solution: If A takes x days, B takes 3x days, and 3x − x = 40 gives x = 20, so A takes 20 days and B takes 60 days. Combined rate = 1/20 + 1/60 = 1/15. The trap is taking x = 40/3 by dividing the difference by the efficiency ratio. Together they take 15 days, so the answer is 15 days.
Source: PYQ-style

100. A, B and C can complete a work in 10, 12 and 15 days respectively. All start together, but A leaves after 2 days and B leaves 3 days before completion. In how many days is the work completed?
(a) 6 days (b) 7 days (c) 8 days (d) 9 days
Answer: (b)
Solution: Let total time be T days; A works 2 days, B works (T − 3) days, C works all T days: 2/10 + (T − 3)/12 + T/15 = 1. Multiplying by 60: 12 + 5(T − 3) + 4T = 60, so 9T = 63 and T = 7. The trap is assuming all three work for the full T days. The work is completed in 7 days, so the answer is 7 days.
Source: PYQ-style
