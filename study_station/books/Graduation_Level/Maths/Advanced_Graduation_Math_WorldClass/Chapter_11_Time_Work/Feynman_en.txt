# 🧒 Feynman Explanation: Time & Work

## The Hardest Concept: "Why do we ADD rates but never add times?"

This is where 9 out of 10 students slip. A does a job in 10 days, B in 15 days — and the brain screams "10 + 15 = 25 days together!" Wrong. Together they finish in **6 days**. Let's see why, with a story.

---

## 🍕 The Pizza Shop Story

Imagine Ravi runs a pizza shop. Alone, he can bake **60 pizzas in 10 hours**. His friend Meena, alone, bakes the same 60 pizzas in **15 hours**.

Now the boss says: "A wedding order! 60 pizzas, both of you work together. How long?"

Little Chintu, age 12, says: "10 + 15 = 25 hours!"

But wait — if they work **together**, how can it take *longer* than Ravi alone (10 hours)? That's like saying two runners together run slower than one. Impossible!

**Here's the secret:** Don't count *time*. Count **pizzas per hour** — the *speed*.

- Ravi's speed: 60 ÷ 10 = **6 pizzas/hour**
- Meena's speed: 60 ÷ 15 = **4 pizzas/hour**
- Together: 6 + 4 = **10 pizzas/hour** 🎉

Time = 60 pizzas ÷ 10 per hour = **6 hours**. Done!

---

## 💡 The Big Idea

**Speeds (rates) can be added. Times cannot.**

Time is like the *length of a race*. Speed is *how fast you run*. Two people running together — their speeds combine, not their race lengths.

That's why the formula is:

> **1/Time together = 1/Time of A + 1/Time of B**

We're adding *rates* (work per day), and the "1/time" form is just rate wearing a disguise.

---

## 🪄 The Unforgettable Trick: "LCM is the Total Pizza"

Whenever two people finish a job in **a** and **b** days:

1. Take **LCM(a, b)** = total work (the "total pizzas").
2. Divide to get each person's daily speed.
3. Add speeds, divide total work by combined speed.

**Example:** A = 10 days, B = 15 days → LCM = 60 units.
A does 6/day, B does 4/day → together 10/day → **60 ÷ 10 = 6 days.** ✔

**Memory hook:** *"Don't marry the days, marry the LCM!"* 💍

A tap fills a tank in 4 hours. Another tap fills it in 6 hours. Both open together — how long to fill the tank?

*(Hint: LCM of 4 and 6 is 12. Speeds are 3 and 2 per hour. Together 5 per hour. So…?)*

**Answer: 12 ÷ 5 = 2.4 hours = 2 hours 24 minutes.** Did you get it? If yes — congratulations, you now think like Feynman! 🚀
