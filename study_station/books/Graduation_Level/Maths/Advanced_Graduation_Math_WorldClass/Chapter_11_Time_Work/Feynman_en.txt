# The Feynman Technique: Time & Work

## The Bricklaying Wall Analogy
Imagine you have to build a brick wall. Instead of thinking of "1 complete job" as an abstract fraction like 1/10 or 1/15, imagine the wall contains a specific count of bricks.

### Why LCM is Pure Genius
Suppose Alice can build the wall in 10 days and Bob can build the same wall in 15 days.
What is the common multiple of 10 and 15? It is 30 bricks!
Now look at what each person does every day:
- Alice lays $30 / 10 = 3$ bricks per day.
- Bob lays $30 / 15 = 2$ bricks per day.
Notice that the fractions vanished completely.

When Alice and Bob work side by side:
- Together they lay $3 + 2 = 5$ bricks every single day.
- To build the entire 30-brick wall, they need $30 / 5 = 6$ days!

## The Departure Illusion: Leaving Before Completion
What happens if Alice leaves 2 days before the wall is finished?
Beginners set up complex algebraic equations with $(T - 2)$.
Consider the intuitive approach:
If Alice had NOT left, how many extra bricks would she have added in those 2 days?
Alice lays 3 bricks a day, so in 2 days she would lay $2 \times 3 = 6$ extra bricks.
Imagine making the wall 6 bricks taller!
Total bricks = $30 + 6 = 36$ bricks.
Now both Alice and Bob work together for the whole time laying 5 bricks a day:
$$\text{Total Days} = \frac{36}{5} = 7.2 \text{ days}$$
By simply adding the virtual bricks Alice would have laid, the entire problem solves in one line without algebra.
