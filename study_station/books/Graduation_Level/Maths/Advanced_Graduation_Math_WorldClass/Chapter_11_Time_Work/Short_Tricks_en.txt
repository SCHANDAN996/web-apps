# Exam Short Tricks: Time & Work

## Trick 1: The Offset Square Root Formula
When A alone takes $a$ days more than $(A + B)$ together, and B alone takes $b$ days more than $(A + B)$ together:
$$T_{A+B} = \sqrt{a \cdot b}$$
- **Example:** A takes 8 days more than A+B together, B takes 18 days more than A+B together.
  $$T_{A+B} = \sqrt{8 \cdot 18} = \sqrt{144} = 12 \text{ days}$$
  Time for A alone = $12 + 8 = 20$ days; Time for B alone = $12 + 18 = 30$ days.

## Trick 2: The 'Leaving Before Completion' Hack
When someone leaves $k$ days BEFORE scheduled completion, DO NOT form linear equations with $(T - k)$.
Instead, add their prospective work to the total work and divide by combined efficiency:
$$T = \frac{\text{Total Work} + k \cdot E_{\text{departing}}}{\sum E}$$

---

## Trick 3: Efficiency Percentage to Time Ratio
If A is $p\%$ more efficient than B:
$$E_A : E_B = (100 + p) : 100$$
$$T_A : T_B = 100 : (100 + p)$$
- **Example:** A is 60% more efficient than B.
  $$E_A : E_B = 160 : 100 = 8 : 5 \implies T_A : T_B = 5 : 8$$

## Trick 4: Fast Equivalence for Men and Women ('OR' to 'AND')
If $m$ men OR $w$ women take $D$ days to complete a work, then $m_1$ men AND $w_1$ women take:
$$T = \frac{D}{\frac{m_1}{m} + \frac{w_1}{w}} \text{ days}$$
