101. A can do a piece of work in 12 days and B can do it in 18 days. They work together for 4 days, after which A leaves. In how many more days will B finish the remaining work?
(a) 10 days (b) 6 days (c) 8 days (d) 12 days
Answer: (c)
Solution: The tempting wrong value 10 days comes from computing B's remaining time as if A had never helped. Combined rate = 1/12 + 1/18 = 5/36 per day, so in 4 days they complete 20/36 = 5/9, leaving 4/9. B alone needs (4/9)/(1/18) = 8 days, so the answer is 8 days.
Source: PYQ-style

102. A is twice as efficient as B. Together they finish a work in 14 days. In how many days can A alone finish it?
(a) 18 days (b) 28 days (c) 21 days (d) 24 days
Answer: (c)
Solution: Many students wrongly double 14 to get 28. Let B's rate be x, so A's rate is 2x and combined 3x = 1/14, giving x = 1/42. A alone takes 1/(2x) = 21 days, so the answer is 21 days.
Source: PYQ-style

103. 12 men can complete a work in 18 days. After 6 days, 6 more men join. In how many total days will the work be completed?
(a) 12 days (b) 16 days (c) 15 days (d) 14 days
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is to assume the remaining work still takes 12 more days with the original team. Total work = 12 × 18 = 216 man-days; in 6 days, 72 man-days are done, leaving 144. With 18 men, remaining time = 144/18 = 8 days, so total time = 6 + 8 = 14 days, so the answer is 14 days.
Source: PYQ-style

104. A and B together can do a work in 8 days, B and C in 12 days, and A and C in 16 days. In how many days can A, B and C together do the work?
(a) 48/7 days (b) 6 days (c) 7 days (d) 96/13 days
Answer: (d)
Solution: A common error is averaging 8, 12 and 16 to get 12 days. Adding the three pair rates: 2(A+B+C) = 1/8 + 1/12 + 1/16 = 6/48 + 4/48 + 3/48 = 13/48, so the combined rate is 13/96 per day. Time = 96/13 days, so the answer is 96/13 days.
Source: PYQ-style

105. A can do a work in 10 days. He works for 4 days, then B finishes the rest in 9 days. In how many days can B alone do the whole work?
(a) 12 days (b) 15 days (c) 20 days (d) 18 days
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is to compute B's time from the full work instead of the remaining work. A does 4/10 = 2/5 of the work, leaving 3/5, which B does in 9 days. B's full time = 9 × 5/3 = 15 days, so the answer is 15 days.
Source: PYQ-style

106. Pipe A can fill a tank in 6 hours and pipe B can empty it in 9 hours. If both are opened together, in how many hours will the tank be filled?
(a) 12 hours (b) 15 hours (c) 18 hours (d) 16 hours
Answer: (c)
Solution: Students often add the times to get 15 hours. Net rate = 1/6 − 1/9 = 3/18 − 2/18 = 1/18 per hour, so the tank fills in 18 hours, so the answer is 18 hours.
Source: PYQ-style

107. 6 men or 9 women can do a work in 18 days. In how many days will 4 men and 6 women do the same work?
(a) 13.5 days (b) 12 days (c) 15 days (d) 16 days
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is to average the headcounts without converting efficiencies. Since 6 men = 9 women, 1 man = 1.5 women, so 4 men + 6 women = 6 + 6 = 12 women. If 9 women take 18 days, 12 women take 18 × 9/12 = 13.5 days, so the answer is 13.5 days.
Source: PYQ-style

108. A can do a work in 20 days. He works alone for 5 days, then B joins him and together they finish the work in 6 more days. In how many days can B alone do the whole work?
(a) 15 days (b) 12 days (c) 40/3 days (d) 10 days
Answer: (c)
Solution: The tempting error is to treat B's 6 days of joint work as B's solo pace. A does 5/20 = 1/4 alone, leaving 3/4; in the 6 joint days A adds 6/20 = 3/10, so B does 3/4 − 3/10 = 9/20 in 6 days. B's rate = 9/120 = 3/40 per day, so B alone takes 40/3 days, so the answer is 40/3 days.
Source: PYQ-style

109. A and B can do a work in 20 and 30 days respectively. They work on alternate days, starting with A. In how many days will the work be completed?
(a) 23 days (b) 24 days (c) 25 days (d) 26 days
Answer: (b)
Solution: Many wrongly take the average of 20 and 30, getting 25. In each 2-day cycle, work done = 1/20 + 1/30 = 5/60 = 1/12. After 12 cycles, i.e. 24 days, the work is exactly complete, so the answer is 24 days.
Source: PYQ-style

110. Two pipes can fill a tank in 12 hours and 18 hours. A leak at the bottom empties the full tank in 36 hours. If all three operate together, in how many hours will the tank fill?
(a) 6 hours (b) 9 hours (c) 8 hours (d) 12 hours
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is to ignore the leak and compute only the two filling pipes, getting 36/5 = 7.2 hours. Net rate = 1/12 + 1/18 − 1/36 = 3/36 + 2/36 − 1/36 = 4/36 = 1/9 per hour, so the tank fills in 9 hours, so the answer is 9 hours.
Source: PYQ-style

111. 4 men or 6 women can do a work in 20 days. In how many days will 2 men and 3 women do the same work?
(a) 20 days (b) 18 days (c) 15 days (d) 24 days
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is to halve the team and double the time without converting efficiencies. Since 4 men = 6 women, 2 men = 3 women, so 2 men + 3 women = 6 women, which is exactly the team that takes 20 days. So the answer is 20 days.
Source: PYQ-style

112. A can do a work in 25 days. B is 25% more efficient than A. In how many days will B do the same work?
(a) 18 days (b) 22 days (c) 24 days (d) 20 days
Answer: (d)
Solution: The classic trap is reducing 25 days by 25% to get 18.75 days — efficiency and time are inversely related, not directly. B's rate = 1.25 × (1/25) = 1/20 per day, so B takes 20 days, so the answer is 20 days.
Source: PYQ-style

113. A and B together can finish a work in 12 days. A alone can finish it in 20 days. If they are paid ₹3,200 for the work, what is B's share?
(a) ₹1,600 (b) ₹1,280 (c) ₹1,920 (d) ₹2,000
Answer: (b)
Solution: Many split the money equally (₹1,600 each), ignoring that wages follow work done. B's rate = 1/12 − 1/20 = 2/60 = 1/30, so the ratio of work A : B = 1/20 : 1/30 = 3 : 2. B's share = (2/5) × 3200 = ₹1,280, so the answer is ₹1,280.
Source: PYQ-style

114. A can complete a work in 16 days. He works for 4 days and then leaves. B completes the remaining work in 16 days. In how many days can A and B together complete the whole work?
(a) 10 days (b) 64/7 days (c) 12 days (d) 80/7 days
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is to assume B alone takes 16 days for the whole work. A does 4/16 = 1/4, leaving 3/4, which B does in 16 days, so B alone needs 64/3 days. Combined rate = 1/16 + 3/64 = 7/64, so together they take 64/7 days, so the answer is 64/7 days.
Source: PYQ-style

115. Two pipes can fill a tank in 12 and 18 minutes. Both are opened together, but after 3 minutes the first pipe is closed. How much longer will the second pipe take to fill the tank?
(a) 9 minutes (b) 12 minutes (c) 13.5 minutes (d) 10.5 minutes
Answer: (d)
Solution: The tempting wrong value 9 minutes comes from subtracting 3 minutes from 12. In 3 minutes both pipes fill 3(1/12 + 1/18) = 3 × 5/36 = 5/12, leaving 7/12. The second pipe needs (7/12)/(1/18) = 10.5 minutes, so the answer is 10.5 minutes.
Source: PYQ-style

116. 20 men can build a wall in 15 days working 8 hours a day. In how many days will 25 men build the same wall working 10 hours a day?
(a) 10 days (b) 12 days (c) 15 days (d) 9.6 days
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is to scale only by men and ignore hours, getting 12 days. Total work = 20 × 15 × 8 = 2400 man-hours. Required days = 2400/(25 × 10) = 9.6 days, so the answer is 9.6 days.
Source: PYQ-style

117. A is 50% more efficient than B, and C is 25% more efficient than A. If B alone can do a work in 36 days, in how many days will C alone do it?
(a) 19.2 days (b) 20 days (c) 18 days (d) 24 days
Answer: (a)
Solution: Students often add the percentages to get 75% and compute 36/1.75 ≈ 20.6 days. A's rate = 1.5 × B's rate and C's rate = 1.25 × A's rate = 1.875 × B's rate. C's time = 36/1.875 = 19.2 days, so the answer is 19.2 days.
Source: PYQ-style

118. A, B and C can do a work in 10, 12 and 15 days respectively. They start together, but A leaves after 2 days and B leaves 3 days before completion. In how many days is the work finished?
(a) 5 days (b) 6 days (c) 8 days (d) 7 days
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is to assume all three work the whole time, giving 4 days. Let total time be T days: A works 2 days, B works (T − 3) days, C works T days, so 2/10 + (T − 3)/12 + T/15 = 1. Multiplying by 60: 12 + 5(T − 3) + 4T = 60, giving 9T = 63 and T = 7, so the answer is 7 days.
Source: PYQ-style

119. A pipe can fill a tank in 8 hours. Due to a leak at the bottom, it takes 12 hours to fill. In how many hours will the leak alone empty the full tank?
(a) 16 hours (b) 20 hours (c) 24 hours (d) 30 hours
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is to subtract the times, getting 12 − 8 = 4 hours. Leak's rate = 1/8 − 1/12 = 3/24 − 2/24 = 1/24 per hour, so the leak empties the tank in 24 hours, so the answer is 24 hours.
Source: PYQ-style

120. 5 men and 3 boys can do a work in 12 days, and 3 men and 5 boys can do the same work in 12 days. In how many days will 4 men and 4 boys do it?
(a) 10 days (b) 11 days (c) 12 days (d) 14 days
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is to average the two teams' headcounts. Since both teams take equal time, 5m + 3b = 3m + 5b, giving m = b, so each team equals 8b per day and total work = 12 × 8b = 96b. A team of 4 men and 4 boys works at 8b per day, needing 96b/8b = 12 days, so the answer is 12 days.
Source: PYQ-style

121. A can do a work in 12 days and B in 18 days. They start together, but A leaves 3 days before the work is completed. In how many days is the work finished?
(a) 9 days (b) 8 days (c) 10 days (d) 11 days
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is to assume A works throughout, giving the joint time 36/5 = 7.2 days. Let total time be T: B works all T days and A works (T − 3) days, so (T − 3)/12 + T/18 = 1. Multiplying by 36: 3(T − 3) + 2T = 36, giving 5T = 45 and T = 9, so the answer is 9 days.
Source: PYQ-style

122. A can do a work in 12 days, B in 16 days. They work alternately, one day each, starting with B. In how many days will the work be completed?
(a) 13.75 days (b) 14.25 days (c) 14 days (d) 13.5 days
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is to use the average time of 14 days. In each 2-day cycle work done = 1/16 + 1/12 = 7/48; after 6 cycles (12 days), 42/48 is done, leaving 6/48. On day 13, B does 3/48, leaving 3/48 = 1/16; on day 14, A needs (1/16)/(1/12) = 3/4 day. Total = 13.75 days, so the answer is 13.75 days.
Source: PYQ-style

123. A tank is filled by three pipes A, B, C. A and B together fill it in 6 hours, B and C in 10 hours, and A and C in 7.5 hours. In how many hours does pipe A alone fill the tank?
(a) 10 hours (b) 8 hours (c) 12 hours (d) 15 hours
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is to assume A alone equals the A+B time of 6 hours. Sum of pair rates: 2(A+B+C) = 1/6 + 1/10 + 1/7.5 = 5/30 + 3/30 + 4/30 = 12/30 = 2/5, so the combined rate is 1/5. A's rate = 1/5 − 1/10 = 1/10, so A alone takes 10 hours, so the answer is 10 hours.
Source: PYQ-style

124. A contractor employs 30 men to finish a work in 24 days. After 8 days, only one-fourth of the work is done. How many extra men must he employ to finish on time?
(a) 10 men (b) 15 men (c) 25 men (d) 20 men
Answer: (b)
Solution: The tempting wrong value 20 men comes from misjudging the remaining work. One-fourth done by 30 men in 8 days means total work = 30 × 8 × 4 = 960 man-days; remaining 720 man-days must be done in 16 days, needing 720/16 = 45 men. Extra men = 45 − 30 = 15, so the answer is 15 men.
Source: PYQ-style

125. A, B and C working alone can finish a work in 8, 12 and 24 days. All three start together. After 2 days A leaves; 2 days later B also leaves. C completes the rest. In how many total days is the work finished?
(a) 10 days (b) 9 days (c) 8 days (d) 12 days
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is to apply the joint rate of all three for the whole duration. First 2 days: all three do 2(1/8 + 1/12 + 1/24) = 2 × 6/24 = 1/2; next 2 days: B and C do 2(1/12 + 1/24) = 2 × 3/24 = 1/4, so 3/4 is done and 1/4 remains. C alone needs (1/4)/(1/24) = 6 days, giving total = 2 + 2 + 6 = 10 days, so the answer is 10 days.
Source: PYQ-style
