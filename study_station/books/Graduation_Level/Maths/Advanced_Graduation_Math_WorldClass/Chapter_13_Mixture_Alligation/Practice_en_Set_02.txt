26. A vessel contains 60 litres of milk. 12 litres of milk is taken out and replaced with water. This process is repeated once more. How much milk remains in the vessel?
(a) 36 litres (b) 42 litres (c) 38.4 litres (d) 40 litres
Answer: (c)
Solution: The tempting wrong answer 36 litres comes from subtracting 12 litres twice (60 − 24), which ignores that the second removal takes out a mixture, not pure milk. Using the repeated replacement formula, milk left = 60 × (1 − 12/60)² = 60 × 16/25 = 38.4 litres, so the answer is 38.4 litres.
Source: PYQ-style

27. In what ratio must rice at ₹40 per kg be mixed with rice at ₹60 per kg to get a mixture worth ₹48 per kg?
(a) 2 : 3 (b) 3 : 1 (c) 1 : 2 (d) 3 : 2
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is to reverse the differences and write 8 : 12 = 2 : 3, which swaps the two quantities. By alligation, the ratio is (60 − 48) : (48 − 40) = 12 : 8 = 3 : 2, so the answer is 3 : 2.
Source: NCERT Class 8 Mathematics (concept), PYQ-style

28. A mixture of 40 litres has milk and water in the ratio 3 : 1. How much water must be added to make the ratio 2 : 1?
(a) 5 litres (b) 4 litres (c) 6 litres (d) 8 litres
Answer: (a)
Solution: Choosing 10 litres is a common error from assuming the water must double from 10 to 20 litres; but doubling water changes the ratio to 30 : 20 = 3 : 2, not 2 : 1. Milk = 30 litres, water = 10 litres; for ratio 2 : 1, water needed = 30/2 = 15 litres, so add 15 − 10 = 5 litres, so the answer is 5 litres.
Source: PYQ-style

29. A dishonest milkman mixes water with milk and gains 25% by selling at cost price. What is the percentage of water in the mixture?
(a) 20% (b) 25% (c) 15% (d) 33.33%
Answer: (a)
Solution: The tempting answer 25% confuses gain percent with water percentage. If he sells 1 litre of mixture at the cost of 1 litre of milk and gains 25%, then the cost of the mixture is 100/125 = 4/5 of milk's cost, meaning milk is 4/5 and water is 1/5 = 20% of the mixture, so the answer is 20%.
Source: PYQ-style

30. Two vessels A and B contain milk and water in the ratios 5 : 3 and 2 : 3 respectively. In what ratio should the mixtures be combined to get a new mixture with milk and water in the ratio 1 : 1?
(a) 2 : 1 (b) 4 : 5 (c) 1 : 1 (d) 3 : 2
Answer: (b)
Solution: Picking 1 : 1 is the trap of assuming equal parts balance the ratios; equal parts give milk fraction (5/8 + 2/5)/2 = 41/80, not 1/2. Milk fractions are 5/8 and 2/5, target is 1/2; by alligation, A : B = (1/2 − 2/5) : (5/8 − 1/2) = 1/10 : 1/8 = 4 : 5. Check: 4 parts of A and 5 parts of B give milk = 4 × 5/8 + 5 × 2/5 = 2.5 + 2 = 4.5 out of 9, exactly half, so the answer is 4 : 5.
Source: PYQ-style

31. A 100-litre mixture contains 20% alcohol. How much alcohol must be added to make it 25% of the new mixture?
(a) 5 litres (b) 8 litres (c) 6.67 litres (d) 10 litres
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap answer 5 litres treats 25% − 20% = 5% of 100 litres, ignoring that adding alcohol increases the total volume. Water stays fixed at 80 litres; in the new mixture water is 75%, so total = 80/0.75 = 106.67 litres, and alcohol added = 6.67 litres, so the answer is 6.67 litres.
Source: PYQ-style

32. A grocer mixes two varieties of tea costing ₹180 and ₹240 per kg in the ratio 2 : 3. At what price per kg should he sell the mixture to gain 20%?
(a) ₹240 (b) ₹264 (c) ₹259.20 (d) ₹252
Answer: (c)
Solution: Choosing ₹264 comes from taking the average price ₹220 and adding 20% of ₹240, a base confusion. Cost of mixture = (2 × 180 + 3 × 240)/5 = 1080/5 = ₹216 per kg; selling price = 216 × 1.2 = ₹259.20, so the answer is ₹259.20.
Source: PYQ-style

33. A vessel contains 40 litres of pure milk. 8 litres of milk is drawn out and replaced with water, and this process is repeated once more. How much milk is left in the vessel?
(a) 24 litres (b) 25.6 litres (c) 28 litres (d) 26.4 litres
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap answer 24 litres subtracts 8 litres twice (40 − 16), forgetting that the second draw removes a mixture. Milk left = 40 × (1 − 8/40)² = 40 × 16/25 = 25.6 litres, so the answer is 25.6 litres.
Source: PYQ-style

34. A mixture contains spirit and water in the ratio 5 : 2. When 14 litres of water is added, the ratio becomes 5 : 4. Find the initial quantity of the mixture.
(a) 56 litres (b) 42 litres (c) 49 litres (d) 35 litres
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is to assume the 14 litres changes both components, while only water changes. Spirit is unchanged: 5x/(2x + 14) = 5/4 gives 20x = 10x + 70, so x = 7. Initial mixture = 7x = 49 litres, so the answer is 49 litres.
Source: PYQ-style

35. In what ratio must water be mixed with milk costing ₹36 per litre so that the mixture, sold at ₹36 per litre, yields a profit of 20%?
(a) 1 : 5 (b) 1 : 6 (c) 5 : 1 (d) 1 : 4
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap answer 1 : 6 uses 36/1.2 = 30 and then writes 30 : 6 = 5 : 1 reversed. Cost price of mixture = 36/1.2 = ₹30 per litre; by alligation between water (₹0) and milk (₹36): water : milk = (36 − 30) : (30 − 0) = 6 : 30 = 1 : 5, so the answer is 1 : 5.
Source: PYQ-style

36. A vessel is full of 80 litres of milk. 16 litres is withdrawn and replaced with water, then 16 litres of the mixture is again withdrawn and replaced with water. What is the ratio of milk to water finally?
(a) 16 : 9 (b) 9 : 7 (c) 3 : 2 (d) 5 : 3
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap 3 : 2 comes from subtracting 32 litres of milk directly (48 : 32). Milk left = 80 × (1 − 16/80)² = 80 × 16/25 = 51.2 litres; water = 80 − 51.2 = 28.8 litres. Ratio = 51.2 : 28.8 = 16 : 9, so the answer is 16 : 9.
Source: PYQ-style

37. Two alloys contain copper and zinc in the ratios 5 : 3 and 3 : 5 respectively. Equal weights of both alloys are melted together. What is the ratio of copper to zinc in the new alloy?
(a) 5 : 3 (b) 1 : 1 (c) 3 : 5 (d) 2 : 1
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is to pick 5 : 3 by assuming the first alloy dominates. Take 8 kg of each: copper = 5 + 3 = 8 kg, zinc = 3 + 5 = 8 kg, giving copper : zinc = 8 : 8 = 1 : 1, so the answer is 1 : 1.
Source: PYQ-style

38. A milkman has 20 litres of milk containing 10% water. How much pure milk should he add so that water becomes 5% of the new mixture?
(a) 10 litres (b) 25 litres (c) 15 litres (d) 20 litres
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap 10 litres assumes halving the percentage needs half the volume added. Water = 2 litres stays fixed; it must be 5% of the new total, so new total = 2/0.05 = 40 litres. Milk added = 40 − 20 = 20 litres, so the answer is 20 litres.
Source: PYQ-style

39. A jar contains a mixture of two liquids P and Q in the ratio 7 : 5. When 9 litres of mixture is drawn off and replaced with Q, the ratio becomes 7 : 9. How many litres of P were in the jar initially?
(a) 28 litres (b) 24 litres (c) 35 litres (d) 21 litres
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is to apply the ratio change to the whole jar instead of only the 9 litres removed. Let total = 12x; P initially = 7x. After the draw, P = 7x − (7/12)(9) = 7x − 21/4, and the new ratio gives P = 7/16 × 12x = 21x/4. Setting 7x − 21/4 = 21x/4 gives 28x − 21 = 21x, so x = 3. Initial P = 7 × 3 = 21 litres, so the answer is 21 litres.
Source: PYQ-style

40. A trader mixes 26 kg of rice at ₹20 per kg with 30 kg of rice at ₹36 per kg and sells the mixture at ₹30 per kg. What is his profit percentage?
(a) 10% (b) 8% (c) 5% (d) 12%
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap 10% comes from averaging prices as (20 + 36)/2 = 28 and mis-weighting the profit. Cost = 26 × 20 + 30 × 36 = 520 + 1080 = ₹1600 for 56 kg, i.e. 200/7 per kg. Profit per kg = 30 − 200/7 = 10/7, so profit % = (10/7)/(200/7) × 100 = 5%, so the answer is 5%.
Source: PYQ-style

41. A container has 40 litres of a 30% salt solution. How much water must evaporate to make it a 40% solution?
(a) 8 litres (b) 15 litres (c) 12 litres (d) 10 litres
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap 13.3 litres comes from computing 40 × (40 − 30)/30, treating percentages as additive. Salt = 12 litres stays fixed; it must be 40% of the remaining solution, so remaining = 12/0.4 = 30 litres. Water evaporated = 40 − 30 = 10 litres, so the answer is 10 litres.
Source: PYQ-style

42. In a 729-litre mixture of milk and water, the ratio is 7 : 2. How much water must be added to make the ratio 7 : 3?
(a) 100 litres (b) 72 litres (c) 81 litres (d) 90 litres
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is to scale 729 by 1/9 directly or to add water proportional to the whole mixture. Milk = 7/9 × 729 = 567 litres, water = 162 litres. For ratio 7 : 3, water = 3/7 × 567 = 243 litres. Water to add = 243 − 162 = 81 litres, so the answer is 81 litres.
Source: PYQ-style

43. A chemist mixes two solutions of acid strength 40% and 70% to obtain 60 litres of 55% acid solution. How much of the 40% solution is used?
(a) 30 litres (b) 25 litres (c) 20 litres (d) 35 litres
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap 20 litres assigns the larger difference to the larger quantity, reversing the alligation rule. By alligation, ratio of 40% to 70% solution = (70 − 55) : (55 − 40) = 15 : 15 = 1 : 1, so each is 30 litres, so the answer is 30 litres.
Source: PYQ-style

44. A vessel contains a mixture of petrol and kerosene in the ratio 3 : 2. After adding 6 litres of petrol, the ratio becomes 2 : 1. Find the initial quantity of kerosene.
(a) 8 litres (b) 12 litres (c) 10 litres (d) 14 litres
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is to let both components change or to mis-solve the equation. Kerosene is unchanged: (3x + 6)/(2x) = 2/1 gives 3x + 6 = 4x, so x = 6. Kerosene = 2x = 12 litres, so the answer is 12 litres.
Source: PYQ-style

45. A mixture of 125 gallons contains wine and water in the ratio 3 : 2. How much water must be added so that the new mixture contains 50% water?
(a) 20 gallons (b) 25 gallons (c) 30 gallons (d) 15 gallons
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap 50 gallons doubles the water from 50 to 100, but that makes water 100/175 ≈ 57%, not 50%. Wine = 3/5 × 125 = 75 gallons stays fixed and must be 50% of the new mixture, so new total = 75/0.5 = 150 gallons. Water added = 150 − 125 = 25 gallons, so the answer is 25 gallons.
Source: PYQ-style

46. Two containers of equal capacity are filled with milk-water mixtures in the ratios 3 : 1 and 5 : 3. Both are poured into a larger vessel. What is the ratio of milk to water in the large vessel?
(a) 11 : 5 (b) 2 : 1 (c) 7 : 3 (d) 8 : 7
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap 2 : 1 adds numerators and denominators (3 + 5 : 1 + 3 = 8 : 4) without equalising the bases. Take capacity 8 litres (LCM of 4 and 8): first container has milk 6, water 2; second has milk 5, water 3. Total milk : water = 11 : 5, so the answer is 11 : 5.
Source: PYQ-style

47. A shopkeeper sells a mixture of two sugars costing ₹30 and ₹42 per kg at ₹43.20 per kg, gaining 8%. In what ratio did he mix the cheaper sugar with the dearer sugar?
(a) 4 : 3 (b) 1 : 5 (c) 1 : 1 (d) 3 : 2
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is to apply alligation on the selling price ₹43.20 directly instead of the cost price. Cost price of mixture = 43.20/1.08 = ₹40 per kg. Ratio = (42 − 40) : (40 − 30) = 2 : 10 = 1 : 5, so the answer is 1 : 5.
Source: PYQ-style

48. A butler steals wine from a butt containing 50% spirit and replaces it with wine containing 20% spirit. The resulting mixture has 35% spirit. What fraction of the butt did he replace?
(a) 1/4 (b) 1/3 (c) 2/5 (d) 1/2
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is to average 50 and 20 to get 35 and conclude "half" without any method; the correct tool is alligation. By alligation between 50% and 20% for a mean of 35%: original : replaced = (35 − 20) : (50 − 35) = 15 : 15 = 1 : 1, so the replaced fraction is 1/2, so the answer is 1/2.
Source: PYQ-style

49. A 60-litre mixture of milk and water has 25% water. After removing some mixture and replacing it with pure milk, the water content drops to 15%. How much mixture was removed?
(a) 36 litres (b) 20 litres (c) 30 litres (d) 24 litres
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is to compute 10% of 60 = 6 litres or to scale water linearly. Water initially = 15 litres; finally water = 15% of 60 = 9 litres, so 6 litres of water was removed. Removed mixture was 25% water, so quantity removed = 6/0.25 = 24 litres, so the answer is 24 litres.
Source: PYQ-style

50. Alloy X contains gold and silver in the ratio 2 : 3 and alloy Y contains them in the ratio 3 : 7. In what ratio should X and Y be mixed so that the new alloy has gold and silver in the ratio 1 : 2?
(a) 1 : 2 (b) 5 : 3 (c) 7 : 3 (d) 1 : 1
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap 1 : 1 assumes equal amounts average the ratios; equal amounts give gold fraction (2/5 + 3/10)/2 = 7/20, not 1/3. Gold fractions: X = 2/5, Y = 3/10, target = 1/3. By alligation, X : Y = (1/3 − 3/10) : (2/5 − 1/3) = 1/30 : 1/15 = 1 : 2, so the answer is 1 : 2.
Source: PYQ-style
