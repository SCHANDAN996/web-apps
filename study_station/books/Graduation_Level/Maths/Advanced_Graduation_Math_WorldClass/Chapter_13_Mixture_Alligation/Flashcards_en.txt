Card 1
Front: What is the Alligation Rule formula?
Back: (Cheaper quantity : Dearer quantity) = (d − m) : (m − c), where c = cheaper price, d = dearer price, m = mean price.

Card 2
Front: In alligation, what does the diagonal difference give you?
Back: The ratio of quantities (not prices) in which the two ingredients must be mixed.

Card 3
Front: What is the mnemonic to remember which difference pairs with which quantity in the alligation cross?
Back: "CROSS means OPPOSITE" — the difference (d − m) sits opposite the cheaper price, so it gives the cheaper quantity's share. Diagonals cross over!

Card 4
Front: A mixture has milk and water in ratio 5:1. What fraction of the mixture is water?
Back: 1/(5+1) = 1/6 of the mixture.

Card 5
Front: Formula for repeated replacement: if x litres is replaced n times from V litres of pure liquid, final quantity of pure liquid = ?
Back: V × (1 − x/V)ⁿ

Card 6
Front: In the repeated replacement formula, what happens to the total volume of the mixture after each operation?
Back: It stays constant (V litres) — only the proportion of pure liquid decreases.

Card 7
Front: Two varieties of rice at ₹40/kg and ₹60/kg are mixed to get ₹48/kg. What is the mixing ratio?
Back: (60 − 48) : (48 − 40) = 12 : 8 = 3 : 2 (cheaper : dearer).

Card 8
Front: What is the mnemonic for repeated replacement problems?
Back: "DRAIN and RETAIN" — each drain retains a factor (1 − x/V); raise it to the number of drains n.

Card 9
Front: If a vessel contains only water and you must find the ratio to mix two milk-water mixtures, what value do you place at the corners of the alligation cross?
Back: The milk (or water) fractions of each mixture; water alone = 0, pure milk = 1.

Card 10
Front: Mixture A has 60% milk, mixture B has 30% milk. In what ratio should they be mixed to get 45% milk?
Back: (45 − 30) : (60 − 45) = 15 : 15 = 1 : 1.

Card 11
Front: How do you convert "milk : water = 7 : 2" into a milk percentage for alligation?
Back: Milk fraction = 7/9 ≈ 77.78%; use fractions directly in the cross to avoid decimals.

Card 12
Front: A dishonest milkman sells at cost price but mixes water. If he gains 25%, what fraction of the mixture is water?
Back: Water fraction = 25/125 = 1/5, i.e., milk : water = 4 : 1. (When sold at CP, Gain% = water/milk × 100, so water fraction = Gain% / (100 + Gain%).)

Card 13
Front: What is the key trap in "sold at cost price with profit" adulteration problems?
Back: The profit comes entirely from the free water — profit% = (water quantity / milk quantity) × 100, not water/total.

Card 14
Front: From 40 L of pure milk, 4 L is replaced by water 3 times. Milk left?
Back: 40 × (1 − 4/40)³ = 40 × (0.9)³ = 29.16 L.

Card 15
Front: When two mixtures are mixed in ratio a : b, what is the weighted average concentration formula?
Back: Mean concentration = (a·C₁ + b·C₂) / (a + b)

Card 16
Front: What is the mnemonic to remember the alligation cross layout?
Back: "BIG top-right, SMALL top-left, MEAN in the MIDDLE" — dearer (big) and cheaper (small) at top corners, mean in the centre, differences on the diagonals.

Card 17
Front: If the price of the mixture equals the cheaper ingredient's price, what does alligation tell you?
Back: The dearer ingredient's share is zero — the mixture is purely the cheaper ingredient.

Card 18
Front: A 100 L mixture has milk : water = 3 : 2. How much water must be added to make the ratio 3 : 4?
Back: Milk = 60 L stays fixed; new water needed = 60 × 4/3 = 80 L; existing water = 40 L; add 40 L.

Card 19
Front: In "add water to change ratio" problems, which quantity stays constant?
Back: The quantity of the component NOT being added (here, milk). Anchor the ratio to it.

Card 20
Front: Can alligation be applied to speed, profit%, and averages — or only to mixtures?
Back: To any weighted average of two groups: average speed, average marks, profit%, interest% — the cross always gives the ratio of the group sizes.
