# Chapter 13: Mixture and Alligation (Comprehensive Graduation Level Foundation)

## 1. The Core Principle of Alligation
Alligation is a mathematical shortcut derived from the weighted average formula. When two ingredients of differing values or concentrations are blended together to produce a mixture of intermediate mean value, the ratio in which the ingredients must be mixed is governed by the Rule of Alligation:
$$\frac{\text{Quantity of Cheaper Ingredient}}{\text{Quantity of Dearer Ingredient}} = \frac{d - m}{m - c}$$

Where:
- $c$: Unit value or concentration of the cheaper/lower ingredient.
- $d$: Unit value or concentration of the dearer/higher ingredient.
- $m$: Mean unit value or target concentration of the mixture.

### Structural Alligation Cross:
```
Price of Cheaper (c)          Price of Dearer (d)
                  \          /
                   Mean (m)
                  /          \
          (d - m)              (m - c)
```
- Ratio of Quantities: $\text{Quantity}_c : \text{Quantity}_d = (d - m) : (m - c)$.

---

## 2. Mandatory Rules and Consistency Requirements
1. **Consistency of Economic Metric:** All three entities ($c$, $d$, and $m$) must be expressed in the exact same metric.
   - If $c$ and $d$ are Cost Prices (CP), $m$ MUST be the Cost Price of the mixture.
   - **Crucial Exam Trap:** When the Selling Price (SP) of the mixture is stated along with a profit percentage $P\%$, NEVER use the SP directly in the alligation cross! First determine the Cost Price of the mixture:
     $$m = \text{CP}_{\text{mix}} = \frac{\text{SP}_{\text{mix}}}{1 + \frac{P}{100}}$$
2. **Consistency of Concentration Metric:**
   - If mixing two liquid solutions, focus consistently on either the solute (e.g. Milk percentage) or the solvent (Water percentage) across all three terms.

---

## 3. Repeated Dilution / Replacement Mechanics
Consider a container initially filled with $x$ units of pure liquid (e.g., pure milk or alcohol). Suppose $y$ units of the liquid are drawn out and replaced with an equal quantity of water, and this process is repeated $n$ times in succession.
$$\text{Quantity of Pure Liquid Remaining} = x \left( 1 - \frac{y}{x} \right)^n$$
$$\frac{\text{Remaining Pure Liquid}}{\text{Initial Volume (or Total Mixture)}} = \left( 1 - \frac{y}{x} \right)^n$$

### Reverse Ratio Formulation:
If the ratio of pure liquid to water after $n$ operations is $a : b$:
$$\frac{\text{Pure Liquid Left}}{\text{Total Volume}} = \frac{a}{a + b} = \left( 1 - \frac{y}{x} \right)^n$$
Taking the $n$-th root provides an immediate algebraic solution for $x$ or $y$.

---

## 4. Partial Replacement in Multi-Component Solutions
When a mixture contains liquids in ratio $a : b$:
1. If $k$ liters of mixture is drawn off, the quantities removed are strictly in the proportion $a : b$:
   - Liquid A removed = $k \cdot \frac{a}{a + b}$.
   - Liquid B removed = $k \cdot \frac{b}{a + b}$.
2. The remaining mixture inside the container continues to possess the original ratio $a : b$.
3. When $k$ liters of pure Liquid B is added back:
   - The quantity of Liquid A remains completely unchanged from its post-withdrawal level.
   - This invariant allows direct single-variable linear equations.

---

## 5. Universal Applications of Alligation
The Rule of Alligation applies to any quantity expressible as a weighted average:
1. **Profit and Loss:**
   - Cheaper: Profit/Loss % on Lot 1 ($P_1$).
   - Dearer: Profit/Loss % on Lot 2 ($P_2$).
   - Mean: Overall Profit % on entire consignment ($P_{\text{overall}}$).
   - Resulting ratio represents the **Cost Prices (CP)** of the two lots.
2. **Simple Interest:**
   - Cheaper: Rate $R_1$, Dearer: Rate $R_2$, Mean: Overall effective rate $R_{\text{net}}$.
   - Resulting ratio represents the **Principal Sums** invested.
3. **Speed and Distance:**
   - Cheaper: Speed $S_1$, Dearer: Speed $S_2$, Mean: Average speed $S_{\text{avg}}$.
   - Resulting ratio represents the **Time Durations** spent at each speed!
4. **Weighted Averages of Groups:**
   - Average score of group 1 ($A_1$), Average score of group 2 ($A_2$), Mean score ($A_{\text{net}}$).
   - Resulting ratio represents the **Number of Persons** in each group.
