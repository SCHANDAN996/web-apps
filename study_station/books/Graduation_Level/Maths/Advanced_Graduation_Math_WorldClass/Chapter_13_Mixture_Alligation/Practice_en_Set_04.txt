76. A vessel contains 60 litres of milk. 12 litres of milk is taken out and replaced with water. This process is repeated once more. How much milk remains in the vessel?
(a) 36 litres (b) 38.4 litres (c) 40 litres (d) 42 litres
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is subtracting 12 litres twice to get 36 litres, which ignores that the second removal takes out a mixture, not pure milk. Milk left = 60 × (1 − 12/60)² = 60 × 16/25 = 38.4 litres, so the answer is 38.4 litres.
Source: NCERT Class 8 Mathematics (concept), PYQ-style

77. Two vessels A and B contain milk and water in the ratios 5 : 3 and 2 : 3 respectively. In what ratio should the mixtures be taken from A and B to form a mixture containing milk and water in the ratio 1 : 1?
(a) 4 : 5 (b) 2 : 1 (c) 1 : 2 (d) 5 : 4
Answer: (a)
Solution: Milk fractions are 5/8 and 2/5; the target is 1/2. The trap is averaging the ratios or simplifying 1/10 : 1/8 carelessly to 1 : 2. By alligation, A : B = (1/2 − 2/5) : (5/8 − 1/2) = 1/10 : 1/8 = 4 : 5, so the answer is 4 : 5.
Source: PYQ-style

78. A grocer mixes two varieties of rice costing ₹40/kg and ₹60/kg in the ratio 2 : 3. At what price per kg should he sell the mixture to gain 25%?
(a) ₹65 (b) ₹67.50 (c) ₹62.50 (d) ₹60
Answer: (a)
Solution: Cost of mixture = (2×40 + 3×60)/5 = 260/5 = ₹52/kg. The trap is applying 25% gain on a guessed average of ₹50 to get ₹62.50. Selling price = 52 × 1.25 = ₹65, so the answer is ₹65.
Source: PYQ-style

79. A 40-litre mixture of milk and water contains 10% water. How much water must be added to make the water 20% of the new mixture?
(a) 4 litres (b) 8 litres (c) 6 litres (d) 5 litres
Answer: (d)
Solution: Milk = 36 litres and stays constant. The trap is taking 20% of 40 = 8 litres and adding 4 litres, which ignores that the total grows. If milk is 80% of the new mixture, total = 36/0.8 = 45 litres, so water added = 5 litres, so the answer is 5 litres.
Source: PYQ-style

80. In what ratio must tea at ₹62/kg be mixed with tea at ₹72/kg so that the mixture is worth ₹64.50/kg?
(a) 3 : 1 (b) 1 : 3 (c) 2 : 3 (d) 3 : 2
Answer: (a)
Solution: By alligation, ratio = (72 − 64.50) : (64.50 − 62) = 7.5 : 2.5 = 3 : 1. The trap is reversing the differences to get 1 : 3, which would price the mix nearer the dearer tea, so the answer is 3 : 1.
Source: PYQ-style

81. A dishonest milkman sells milk at cost price but mixes water to gain 25%. What is the percentage of water in the mixture?
(a) 25% (b) 20% (c) 15% (d) 33⅓%
Answer: (b)
Solution: To gain 25% while selling at cost price, each litre of mixture sold must contain only 1/1.25 = 4/5 litre of milk. The trap is answering 25%, confusing gain percent with water percent. Water = 1/5 = 20%, so the answer is 20%.
Source: PYQ-style

82. A can contains a mixture of two liquids P and Q in the ratio 7 : 5. When 9 litres of mixture is drawn off and the can is filled with Q, the ratio becomes 7 : 9. How many litres of P did the can initially hold?
(a) 20 litres (b) 21 litres (c) 10 litres (d) 25 litres
Answer: (b)
Solution: P is unchanged by refilling with Q. Initially P : total = 7 : 12; finally P : total = 7 : 16. The 9 litres drawn carried P in the ratio 7/12, so P lost = 9 × 7/12 = 21/4 litres; if total = T, then 7T/12 − 7T/16 = 7T/48 = 21/4, giving T = 36. The trap is reporting the total 36 litres instead of P's share. P initially = 7/12 × 36 = 21 litres, so the answer is 21 litres.
Source: PYQ-style

83. Three vessels of equal capacity contain mixtures of milk and water in the ratios 3 : 2, 7 : 3 and 11 : 4 respectively. The contents of all three are mixed. What is the ratio of milk to water in the final mixture?
(a) 29 : 61 (b) 61 : 30 (c) 5 : 3 (d) 61 : 29
Answer: (d)
Solution: Take capacity 90 (LCM of 5, 10, 15). Milk = 54 + 63 + 66 = 183; water = 36 + 27 + 24 = 87. Ratio = 183 : 87 = 61 : 29. The trap is adding ratios term-wise to get 7 : 3, which ignores differing denominators, so the answer is 61 : 29.
Source: PYQ-style

84. A mixture of 125 gallons of wine and water contains 20% water. How much water must be added so that water becomes 25% of the new mixture?
(a) 8.33 gallons (b) 10 gallons (c) 6.25 gallons (d) 12.5 gallons
Answer: (a)
Solution: Wine = 100 gallons, constant. The trap is adding 5% of 125 = 6.25 gallons, forgetting the total grows. If wine is 75% of the new mixture, total = 100/0.75 = 133.33, so water added = 8.33 gallons, so the answer is 8.33 gallons.
Source: PYQ-style

85. In what ratio must a grocer mix two varieties of pulses costing ₹15/kg and ₹20/kg so that by selling the mixture at ₹19.80/kg he gains 10%?
(a) 3 : 2 (b) 2 : 3 (c) 1 : 1 (d) 4 : 3
Answer: (b)
Solution: Cost price of mixture = 19.80/1.10 = ₹18/kg. The trap is applying alligation on ₹19.80 directly, mixing up selling price with cost price. By alligation, cheaper : dearer = (20 − 18) : (18 − 15) = 2 : 3, so the answer is 2 : 3.
Source: PYQ-style

86. A vessel is full of 80 litres of milk. 8 litres is drawn off and replaced with water, and this operation is performed three times in all. Find the quantity of milk finally left.
(a) 54 litres (b) 60 litres (c) 56 litres (d) 58.32 litres
Answer: (d)
Solution: Milk left = 80 × (1 − 8/80)³ = 80 × (9/10)³ = 80 × 0.729 = 58.32 litres. The trap is subtracting 8 litres three times to get 56 litres, treating each draw as pure milk, so the answer is 58.32 litres.
Source: PYQ-style

87. Two alloys contain copper and zinc in the ratios 5 : 3 and 1 : 3. How many kg of the first alloy must be melted with 8 kg of the second so that the new alloy has copper and zinc in equal amounts?
(a) 12 kg (b) 16 kg (c) 18 kg (d) 14 kg
Answer: (b)
Solution: The second alloy (8 kg) has copper 2 kg and zinc 6 kg, so 4 kg more copper than zinc is needed. Each kg of the first alloy contributes 5/8 − 3/8 = 1/4 kg net copper, so 4 ÷ 1/4 = 16 kg is required. The trap is using the copper fraction 5/8 alone instead of the net difference, so the answer is 16 kg.
Source: PYQ-style

88. A jar full of whisky contains 40% alcohol. A part of this whisky is replaced by another containing 19% alcohol, and now the percentage of alcohol is 26%. What fraction of the whisky was replaced?
(a) 2/5 (b) 1/3 (c) 3/5 (d) 2/3
Answer: (d)
Solution: By alligation between 40% and 19% to get 26%: parts = (26 − 19) : (40 − 26) = 7 : 14 = 1 : 2. So the new (19%) whisky is 2/3 of the final mixture, meaning 2/3 was replaced. The trap is answering 1/3, the share of the original whisky left, so the answer is 2/3.
Source: PYQ-style

89. Milk and water are mixed in vessel A in the ratio 5 : 2 and in vessel B in the ratio 8 : 5. In what ratio should quantities be taken from A and B to form a mixture with milk and water in the ratio 9 : 4?
(a) 5 : 3 (b) 2 : 7 (c) 7 : 2 (d) 3 : 5
Answer: (c)
Solution: Milk fractions: A = 5/7, B = 8/13, target = 9/13. By alligation: (9/13 − 8/13) : (5/7 − 9/13) = 1/13 : 2/91 = 7 : 2. The trap is comparing water fractions and inverting to 2 : 7, so the answer is 7 : 2.
Source: PYQ-style

90. A trader has 50 kg of rice, part of which he sells at 8% profit and the rest at 18% profit. He gains 14% on the whole. What quantity was sold at 18% profit?
(a) 35 kg (b) 30 kg (c) 25 kg (d) 20 kg
Answer: (b)
Solution: By alligation on profits: (18 − 14) : (14 − 8) = 4 : 6 = 2 : 3, so the 18% portion is 3/5 of 50 = 30 kg. The trap is assigning the larger share to the 8% part, giving 20 kg, so the answer is 30 kg.
Source: PYQ-style

91. A 729-litre mixture of milk and water is in the ratio 7 : 2. How much water must be added to make the ratio 7 : 3?
(a) 90 litres (b) 72 litres (c) 81 litres (d) 63 litres
Answer: (c)
Solution: Milk = 7/9 × 729 = 567 litres and stays constant. For the ratio 7 : 3, total = 567 × 10/7 = 810 litres, so water added = 810 − 729 = 81 litres. The trap is adding water in proportion to the old total without keeping milk fixed, so the answer is 81 litres.
Source: PYQ-style

92. A mixture contains spirit and water in the ratio 5 : 3. If it contains 4 litres more spirit than water, how much spirit must be added so that the ratio becomes 5 : 2?
(a) 5 litres (b) 3 litres (c) 6 litres (d) 4 litres
Answer: (a)
Solution: A difference of 2 parts = 4 litres, so spirit = 10 litres and water = 6 litres. Water stays fixed; for the ratio 5 : 2, spirit must be 6 × 5/2 = 15 litres, so spirit to add = 15 − 10 = 5 litres. The trap is adding water or keeping spirit fixed, so the answer is 5 litres.
Source: PYQ-style

93. A sum of ₹36 is made up of 90 coins, some of 25 paise and the rest of 50 paise. How many 50-paise coins are there?
(a) 36 (b) 48 (c) 42 (d) 54
Answer: (d)
Solution: Let the 50-paise coins be y; then 25(90 − y) + 50y = 3600, giving 25y = 1350, so y = 54. The trap is assuming equal numbers of both coins, which gives a total of ₹33.75, not ₹36, so the answer is 54.
Source: PYQ-style

94. A vessel contains a mixture of milk and water in the ratio 4 : 1. 20% of the mixture is removed and replaced with water. What is the new ratio of milk to water?
(a) 16 : 9 (b) 3 : 2 (c) 4 : 1 (d) 16 : 11
Answer: (a)
Solution: Take 100 litres: milk 80, water 20. Removing 20% removes milk 16 and water 4, leaving milk 64, water 16; adding 20 litres of water gives milk 64, water 36, ratio 64 : 36 = 16 : 9. The trap is assuming the ratio is unchanged because only water is added back, so the answer is 16 : 9.
Source: PYQ-style

95. In what ratio must water be mixed with milk costing ₹12 per litre to obtain a mixture worth ₹8 per litre?
(a) 1 : 3 (b) 2 : 1 (c) 1 : 2 (d) 3 : 1
Answer: (c)
Solution: Water costs ₹0. By alligation: water : milk = (12 − 8) : (8 − 0) = 4 : 8 = 1 : 2. The trap is reversing to 2 : 1, which would price the mix at only ₹4 per litre, so the answer is 1 : 2.
Source: PYQ-style

96. A butler stole wine from a butt of sherry containing 30% spirit and replaced it with wine containing 12% spirit. The butt then had 18% spirit. How much of the butt did he steal?
(a) 1/3 (b) 1/2 (c) 3/4 (d) 2/3
Answer: (d)
Solution: By alligation between 30% and 12% to get 18%: (18 − 12) : (30 − 18) = 6 : 12 = 1 : 2. The stolen-and-replaced portion is the 12% wine's share = 2/3. The trap is reporting 1/3, the share of original wine left, so the answer is 2/3.

97. Two containers of equal capacity are full of milk-water mixtures in the ratios 3 : 1 and 5 : 3. Both are emptied into a third vessel. What is the ratio of milk to water in the third vessel?
(a) 11 : 5 (b) 8 : 3 (c) 13 : 11 (d) 5 : 11
Answer: (a)
Solution: Take capacity 8 (LCM of 4 and 8). Milk = 6 + 5 = 11; water = 2 + 3 = 5; ratio = 11 : 5. The trap is adding the ratios term-wise to get 8 : 4 = 2 : 1, which ignores the different denominators, so the answer is 11 : 5.

98. A shopkeeper mixes 30 kg of wheat at ₹20/kg with some wheat at ₹26/kg and sells the mixture at ₹24.20/kg, gaining 10%. How many kg of the dearer wheat did he use?
(a) 25 kg (b) 20 kg (c) 15 kg (d) 30 kg
Answer: (c)
Solution: Cost price of mixture = 24.20/1.10 = ₹22/kg. The trap is using ₹24.20 as the cost price, which distorts the ratio. By alligation, cheaper : dearer = (26 − 22) : (22 − 20) = 4 : 2 = 2 : 1, so dearer wheat = 30/2 = 15 kg, so the answer is 15 kg.
Source: PYQ-style

99. A cask full of wine is emptied by drawing 9 litres and refilling with water, repeated twice more (three operations). If the final ratio of wine to water is 343 : 169, what was the capacity of the cask?
(a) 64 litres (b) 81 litres (c) 72 litres (d) 90 litres
Answer: (c)
Solution: Wine fraction = 343/512 = (7/8)³, so each operation retains 7/8, i.e., 1/8 of the contents is drawn each time. The trap is treating 343 : 169 as a simple ratio and ignoring the cube structure. If 9 litres = 1/8 of capacity, capacity = 72 litres, so the answer is 72 litres.
Source: PYQ-style

100. A mixture of 40 litres has milk and water in the ratio 3 : 1. How much of the mixture must be drawn off and replaced with water so that the final ratio of milk to water becomes 1 : 1?
(a) 20/3 litres (b) 10 litres (c) 40/3 litres (d) 15 litres
Answer: (c)
Solution: Milk = 30 litres; finally milk must be half of the still 40-litre mixture = 20 litres, so 10 litres of milk must be removed. Each litre of mixture drawn removes 3/4 litre of milk, so draw = 10 × 4/3 = 40/3 litres. The trap is drawing 10 litres of mixture, which removes only 7.5 litres of milk, so the answer is 40/3 litres.
Source: PYQ-style
