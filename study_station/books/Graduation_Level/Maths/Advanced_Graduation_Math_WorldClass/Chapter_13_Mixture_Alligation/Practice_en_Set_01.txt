1. In what ratio must rice at ₹40 per kg be mixed with rice at ₹60 per kg to get a mixture worth ₹50 per kg?
(a) 3 : 2 (b) 2 : 1 (c) 1 : 2 (d) 1 : 1
Answer: (d)
Solution: By the alligation rule, ratio = (60 − 50) : (50 − 40) = 10 : 10 = 1 : 1. The tempting wrong choice 2 : 1 comes from reversing the differences, but the cheaper rice's share equals the gap between the dearer price and the mean, so the answer is 1 : 1.
Source: NCERT Class 8 Mathematics (Comparing Quantities)

2. A mixture contains milk and water in the ratio 5 : 1. What is the percentage of water in the mixture?
(a) 20% (b) 16.67% (c) 25% (d) 15%
Answer: (b)
Solution: Total parts = 5 + 1 = 6, so water = 1/6 = 16.67%. Many students wrongly compute 1/5 = 20% by comparing water to milk instead of water to the total mixture, so the answer is 16.67%.
Source: NCERT Class 8 Mathematics (Comparing Quantities)

3. How many litres of water must be added to 10 litres of pure milk so that milk becomes 80% of the mixture?
(a) 2 litres (b) 2.5 litres (c) 3 litres (d) 4 litres
Answer: (b)
Solution: Milk stays 10 litres and must be 80% of the total, so total = 10/0.8 = 12.5 litres; water added = 12.5 − 10 = 2.5 litres. The trap answer 2 litres comes from taking 20% of 10 directly, ignoring that the percentage applies to the new total, so the answer is 2.5 litres.
Source: PYQ-style

4. Two varieties of sugar cost ₹30/kg and ₹45/kg. In what ratio should they be mixed to get a mixture costing ₹40/kg?
(a) 3 : 1 (b) 2 : 1 (c) 1 : 1 (d) 1 : 2
Answer: (d)
Solution: Ratio = (45 − 40) : (40 − 30) = 5 : 10 = 1 : 2. The reversed ratio 2 : 1 is the classic trap; remember the cheaper variety's share is proportional to the gap on the dearer side, so the answer is 1 : 2.
Source: NCERT Class 8 Mathematics (Comparing Quantities)

5. A 60-litre mixture has milk and water in the ratio 2 : 1. How much milk is in the mixture?
(a) 20 litres (b) 40 litres (c) 30 litres (d) 45 litres
Answer: (b)
Solution: Milk = (2/3) × 60 = 40 litres. The wrong option 20 litres is the water quantity, which students pick when they misread which component is asked, so the answer is 40 litres.

6. A grocer mixes 20 kg of wheat at ₹25/kg with 30 kg of wheat at ₹35/kg. What is the cost price of the mixture per kg?
(a) ₹30 (b) ₹32 (c) ₹31 (d) ₹29
Answer: (c)
Solution: Total cost = 20×25 + 30×35 = 500 + 1050 = ₹1550; total weight = 50 kg; price = 1550/50 = ₹31. The trap ₹30 is the simple average of the two prices, which works only for equal quantities, so the answer is ₹31.

7. A vessel contains 40 litres of milk. 8 litres is drawn out and replaced with water. How much milk remains?
(a) 30 litres (b) 34 litres (c) 32 litres (d) 36 litres
Answer: (c)
Solution: Removing 8 litres of pure milk leaves 40 − 8 = 32 litres of milk; adding water does not change the milk quantity. The trap 30 litres comes from subtracting 8 and then wrongly adjusting again for the added water, so the answer is 32 litres.

8. In what ratio must water be mixed with milk costing ₹48 per litre so that the mixture costs ₹36 per litre?
(a) 1 : 2 (b) 1 : 4 (c) 1 : 3 (d) 2 : 3
Answer: (c)
Solution: Water costs ₹0. Ratio (water : milk) = (48 − 36) : (36 − 0) = 12 : 36 = 1 : 3. The trap 1 : 2 arises from comparing 12 with 24 instead of 36, so the answer is 1 : 3.

9. A mixture of 45 litres has milk and water in the ratio 4 : 1. How much water must be added to make the ratio 3 : 2?
(a) 15 litres (b) 12 litres (c) 10 litres (d) 18 litres
Answer: (a)
Solution: Milk = (4/5)×45 = 36 litres, water = 9 litres. For ratio 3 : 2 with milk fixed at 36, water needed = (2/3)×36 = 24 litres, so add 24 − 9 = 15 litres. The trap 10 litres comes from wrongly scaling the milk too, so the answer is 15 litres.

10. Tea at ₹80/kg is mixed with tea at ₹120/kg in the ratio 3 : 1. What is the price of the mixture per kg?
(a) ₹95 (b) ₹90 (c) ₹100 (d) ₹105
Answer: (b)
Solution: Price = (3×80 + 1×120)/4 = (240 + 120)/4 = 360/4 = ₹90. The trap ₹100 is the plain average of 80 and 120, valid only for a 1 : 1 mix, so the answer is ₹90.

13. In what ratio must a grocer mix two varieties of pulses costing ₹60/kg and ₹90/kg to get a mixture worth ₹75/kg?
(a) 3 : 2 (b) 2 : 1 (c) 1 : 2 (d) 1 : 1
Answer: (d)
Solution: Ratio = (90 − 75) : (75 − 60) = 15 : 15 = 1 : 1. Since ₹75 is exactly midway between ₹60 and ₹90, equal quantities are needed; the reversed-looking options are distractors, so the answer is 1 : 1.

14. A 30-litre mixture of milk and water contains 10% water. How much pure milk should be added to make water 5% of the mixture?
(a) 20 litres (b) 30 litres (c) 25 litres (d) 15 litres
Answer: (b)
Solution: Water = 3 litres (constant). For water to be 5%, total = 3/0.05 = 60 litres, so milk added = 60 − 30 = 30 litres. The trap 15 litres comes from halving the mixture instead of doubling it, so the answer is 30 litres.

16. A shopkeeper mixes 15 kg of rice at ₹20/kg with 10 kg of rice at ₹30/kg. At what price per kg should he sell the mixture to gain 25%?
(a) ₹30 (b) ₹28 (c) ₹32 (d) ₹35
Answer: (a)
Solution: Cost = (15×20 + 10×30)/25 = (300 + 300)/25 = ₹24/kg. Selling price = 24 × 1.25 = ₹30. The trap ₹28 comes from applying 25% gain to the wrong base cost, so the answer is ₹30.

20. From a 50-litre cask of pure wine, 5 litres is drawn and replaced with water. This is done once more. How much wine remains?
(a) 40.5 litres (b) 40 litres (c) 41 litres (d) 42 litres
Answer: (a)
Solution: Wine left = 50 × (1 − 5/50)² = 50 × (0.9)² = 50 × 0.81 = 40.5 litres. The trap 40 litres assumes 5 litres of wine is removed each time, but the second draw removes a diluted mixture, so the answer is 40.5 litres.
Source: PYQ-style

21. A milkman mixes water with 90 litres of milk and gains 20% by selling the mixture at the cost price of milk. What is the ratio of water to milk in the mixture?
(a) 1 : 4 (b) 1 : 5 (c) 1 : 6 (d) 2 : 9
Answer: (b)
Solution: Selling at milk's cost price with 20% gain means the mixture sold contains 20% free water relative to milk paid for: water : milk = 20 : 100 = 1 : 5. The trap 1 : 4 comes from taking 20% of the total mixture instead of the milk, so the answer is 1 : 5.

23. A jar full of whisky contains 40% alcohol. A part of it is replaced by another whisky containing 19% alcohol, and the mixture now has 26% alcohol. What fraction of the jar was replaced?
(a) 2/3 (b) 1/3 (c) 2/5 (d) 3/5
Answer: (a)
Solution: By alligation, ratio of original to replacement = (26 − 19) : (40 − 26) = 7 : 14 = 1 : 2, so replacement is 2/3 of the jar. The trap 1/3 is the share of the original liquid, not the replaced part, so the answer is 2/3.

24. Two vessels contain milk and water in the ratios 5 : 2 and 3 : 4 respectively. Equal quantities from both vessels are mixed. What is the ratio of milk to water in the new mixture?
(a) 41 : 29 (b) 8 : 6 (c) 29 : 41 (d) 7 : 7
Answer: (a)
Solution: Take 35 litres (LCM of 7 and 7) from each: milk = 25 + 15 = 40... more precisely milk = (5/7)×35 + (3/7)×35 = 25 + 15 = 40, water = 10 + 20 = 30; ratio 40 : 30 = 4 : 3. Checking with unit quantities: milk = 5/7 + 3/7 = 8/7, water = 2/7 + 4/7 = 6/7, giving 8 : 6 = 4 : 3 per equal unit; scaling to option form, milk : water = 41 : 29 is wrong — recompute: 5/7 + 3/7 = 8/7 and 2/7 + 4/7 = 6/7, so ratio = 8 : 6 = 4 : 3. Since 4 : 3 is not listed, use LCM 49: milk = 35 + 21 = 56... The correct computation per litre of each: milk = 5/7 + 3/7 = 8/7, water = 6/7; ratio 8:6. Among options, 41 : 29 matches ratios 5:2 and 3:4 only if denominators differ — they do not, so the intended answer with equal quantities is 8 : 6, which simplifies to 4 : 3; the closest listed correct form is 41 : 29 only for unequal denominators, which is not the case here. Hence the answer is 41 : 29 is incorrect; the answer is 8 : 6.
Answer: (b)
Solution: Take 1 litre from each vessel: milk = 5/7 + 3/7 = 8/7 litre, water = 2/7 + 4/7 = 6/7 litre, so milk : water = 8 : 6 = 4 : 3. The trap 41 : 29 is used when the denominators of the two ratios differ, but here both ratios have 7 parts, so the answer is 8 : 6.

25. A dishonest milkman professes to sell milk at cost price but mixes water to gain 25%. What is the percentage of water in the mixture?
(a) 15% (b) 25% (c) 20% (d) 33.33%
Answer: (c)
Solution: To gain 25% while selling at cost price, every 100 litres of milk is stretched to 125 litres of mixture by adding 25 litres of water; water percentage = 25/125 = 20%. The trap 25% treats water as a fraction of the milk rather than of the total mixture, so the answer is 20%.
