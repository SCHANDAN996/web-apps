51. A 40-litre mixture contains milk and water in the ratio 3:1. How much water must be added so that the ratio becomes 1:1?
(a) 10 litres (b) 20 litres (c) 15 litres (d) 25 litres
Answer: (b)
Solution: Milk = 3/4 × 40 = 30 L, water = 10 L. The trap is adding 10 L, which only matches the original water quantity, not the milk. For a 1:1 ratio, water must equal milk = 30 L, so water to add = 30 − 10 = 20 litres, so the answer is 20 litres.
Source: NCERT Class 8 Mathematics (Ratio applications), PYQ-style

52. Rice at ₹40 per kg is mixed with rice at ₹60 per kg to get a mixture worth ₹48 per kg. In what ratio are they mixed?
(a) 3:2 (b) 2:3 (c) 1:1 (d) 4:3
Answer: (a)
Solution: By alligation, ratio = (60 − 48) : (48 − 40) = 12 : 8 = 3:2. The tempting wrong choice 2:3 comes from writing the differences in reverse order; the cheaper quantity must be paired with the larger difference, so the answer is 3:2.
Source: PYQ-style (Alligation rule)

53. A vessel contains 60 litres of pure milk. 12 litres are drawn out and replaced with water, and this operation is repeated once more. How much milk remains?
(a) 36 litres (b) 40 litres (c) 38.4 litres (d) 42 litres
Answer: (c)
Solution: Milk left = 60 × (1 − 12/60)² = 60 × (4/5)² = 60 × 16/25 = 38.4 L. The trap is subtracting 12 L twice to get 36 L, but the second draw removes a mixture, not pure milk, so the answer is 38.4 litres.
Source: PYQ-style (Replacement formula)

54. A 35-litre mixture has milk and water in the ratio 4:3. How much milk must be added to make the ratio 3:2?
(a) 2 litres (b) 5 litres (c) 3 litres (d) 2.5 litres
Answer: (d)
Solution: Milk = 20 L, water = 15 L. Let x litres of milk be added: (20 + x)/15 = 3/2, so 40 + 2x = 45 and x = 2.5. The trap is using the original milk figure 20 in the new ratio directly; only water stays fixed, so the answer is 2.5 litres.
Source: PYQ-style

55. A 25-litre solution contains alcohol and water in the ratio 2:3. How much alcohol must be added to make the ratio 3:2?
(a) 12.5 litres (b) 10 litres (c) 15 litres (d) 7.5 litres
Answer: (a)
Solution: Alcohol = 10 L, water = 15 L. Let x be added: (10 + x)/15 = 3/2, so 20 + 2x = 45 and x = 12.5. The trap is inverting the target ratio; water is unchanged at 15 L, so the answer is 12.5 litres.
Source: PYQ-style

56. Two vessels contain milk and water in the ratios 3:2 and 5:3 respectively. Equal quantities are drawn from both and mixed. The ratio of milk to water in the new mixture is:
(a) 5:8 (b) 8:5 (c) 31:49 (d) 49:31
Answer: (d)
Solution: Milk fraction = 3/5 + 5/8 = 24/40 + 25/40 = 49/80; water = 31/80. Ratio = 49:31. The trap is simply adding the ratio terms to get 8:5, which ignores that the two mixtures have different total parts, so the answer is 49:31.
Source: PYQ-style (SSC CGL pattern)

57. In what ratio must a 20% alcohol solution be mixed with a 50% alcohol solution to obtain a 35% solution?
(a) 2:1 (b) 1:2 (c) 3:2 (d) 1:1
Answer: (d)
Solution: By alligation, ratio = (50 − 35) : (35 − 20) = 15 : 15 = 1:1. The trap is assuming the stronger solution must dominate; since 35% is exactly midway, equal parts are needed, so the answer is 1:1.
Source: PYQ-style

58. A milkman buys milk at ₹50 per litre, mixes water (free) into it, and sells the mixture at ₹50 per litre, gaining 25%. The ratio of milk to water in the mixture is:
(a) 5:1 (b) 2:1 (c) 3:1 (d) 4:1
Answer: (d)
Solution: Cost price of 1 litre of mixture = 50/1.25 = ₹40. By alligation, milk : water = 40 : (50 − 40) = 40 : 10 = 4:1. The trap is taking the profit 25% directly as the water share; profit is on cost, not on selling price, so the answer is 4:1.
Source: PYQ-style (Dishonest milkman type)

59. From a vessel of 100 litres of pure milk, 20 litres are replaced with water, and this is done three times in all. The quantity of milk left is:
(a) 40 litres (b) 51.2 litres (c) 48.8 litres (d) 52 litres
Answer: (b)
Solution: Milk left = 100 × (1 − 20/100)³ = 100 × (0.8)³ = 51.2 L. The trap is subtracting 20 L three times to get 40 L; each replacement removes only 20% of the remaining milk, so the answer is 51.2 litres.
Source: PYQ-style (Repeated replacement)

60. Tea at ₹120 per kg is mixed with tea at ₹180 per kg in the ratio 2:3. The price per kg of the mixture is:
(a) ₹150 (b) ₹160 (c) ₹156 (d) ₹148
Answer: (c)
Solution: Mean price = (2 × 120 + 3 × 180)/5 = (240 + 540)/5 = 780/5 = ₹156. The trap is taking the simple average ₹150, which ignores the unequal weights, so the answer is ₹156.
Source: PYQ-style (Weighted average)

61. A 60-litre mixture has milk and water in the ratio 2:1. After removing 15 litres of the mixture and adding 15 litres of water, the ratio of milk to water becomes:
(a) 2:1 (b) 3:2 (c) 1:1 (d) 4:3
Answer: (c)
Solution: Milk = 40 L, water = 20 L. Removed mixture has 10 L milk and 5 L water. Milk left = 30 L; water = 20 − 5 + 15 = 30 L. Ratio = 1:1. The trap is assuming the ratio is unchanged because equal volume was replaced; only water was added back, so the answer is 1:1.
Source: PYQ-style

62. Gold of 22 carat is mixed with gold of 18 carat to obtain 20 carat gold. The required ratio is:
(a) 2:1 (b) 1:1 (c) 1:2 (d) 3:1
Answer: (b)
Solution: By alligation, ratio = (20 − 18) : (22 − 20) = 2 : 2 = 1:1. The trap is thinking the higher carat must be used more; 20 is the exact mean of 18 and 22, so the answer is 1:1.
Source: PYQ-style

63. How much water must be added to 90 litres of a 10% salt solution to reduce it to a 5% solution?
(a) 45 litres (b) 60 litres (c) 90 litres (d) 100 litres
Answer: (c)
Solution: Salt = 9 L, which stays constant. For 5% concentration, total solution = 9/0.05 = 180 L, so water added = 180 − 90 = 90 L. The trap is halving the volume to 45 L; halving the concentration requires doubling the total volume, so the answer is 90 litres.
Source: PYQ-style

64. A mixture contains milk and water in the ratio 5:3. When 16 litres of the mixture is removed and replaced with water, the ratio becomes 3:5. The original quantity of the mixture was:
(a) 40 litres (b) 32 litres (c) 48 litres (d) 36 litres
Answer: (a)
Solution: Let total = V. Milk after = 5V/8 − 10; water after = 3V/8 − 6 + 16 = 3V/8 + 10. Setting (5V/8 − 10)/(3V/8 + 10) = 3/5 gives 25V/8 − 50 = 9V/8 + 30, so 2V = 80 and V = 40. The trap is forgetting that the removed 16 L also carries away 10 L of milk, so the answer is 40 litres.
Source: PYQ-style (SSC CGL pattern)

65. Mixture A has milk and water in the ratio 4:1 and mixture B in the ratio 2:3. They are combined in the ratio 1:2. The milk-to-water ratio in the final mixture is:
(a) 6:5 (b) 7:8 (c) 8:7 (d) 2:1
Answer: (c)
Solution: Take 1 part of A and 2 parts of B. Milk = 4/5 + 2 × (2/5) = 8/5; water = 1/5 + 2 × (3/5) = 7/5. Ratio = 8:7. The trap is averaging the two ratios directly; B is used in double quantity, so the answer is 8:7.
Source: PYQ-style

66. A 40-litre solution contains 25% alcohol. How much pure alcohol must be added to make it a 40% solution?
(a) 10 litres (b) 8 litres (c) 12 litres (d) 15 litres
Answer: (a)
Solution: Alcohol = 10 L. Let x be added: (10 + x)/(40 + x) = 0.4, so 10 + x = 16 + 0.4x, giving 0.6x = 6 and x = 10. The trap is computing 15% of 40 = 6 L; the added alcohol also increases the total volume, so the answer is 10 litres.
Source: PYQ-style

67. A grocer mixes 4 kg of wheat at ₹30 per kg with 6 kg at ₹45 per kg. The price per kg of the mixture is:
(a) ₹37.50 (b) ₹39 (c) ₹40 (d) ₹38
Answer: (b)
Solution: Mean price = (4 × 30 + 6 × 45)/10 = (120 + 270)/10 = ₹39. The trap is the plain average ₹37.50, which wrongly gives equal weight to both lots, so the answer is ₹39.
Source: PYQ-style (Weighted average)

68. A milkman mixes water with milk and sells the mixture at the price of pure milk, making a profit of 20%. The percentage of water in the mixture is:
(a) 16⅔% (b) 20% (c) 25% (d) 15%
Answer: (a)
Solution: Water fraction = profit/(100 + profit) = 20/120 = 1/6 = 16⅔%. The trap is answering 20%, but profit is reckoned on the cost (the milk part), not on the total mixture, so the answer is 16⅔%.
Source: PYQ-style (Classic milkman problem)

69. From 50 litres of pure milk, 5 litres are drawn out and replaced with water; this is repeated once more. The milk left in the vessel is:
(a) 40 litres (b) 45 litres (c) 42 litres (d) 40.5 litres
Answer: (d)
Solution: Milk left = 50 × (1 − 5/50)² = 50 × (0.9)² = 40.5 L. The trap is subtracting 5 L twice to get 40 L; the second draw removes a diluted mixture, so the answer is 40.5 litres.
Source: PYQ-style (Replacement formula)

70. A 45-litre mixture has milk and water in the ratio 4:1. If 9 litres of the mixture is removed and 9 litres of water is added, the new ratio of milk to water is:
(a) 16:9 (b) 9:16 (c) 3:2 (d) 2:1
Answer: (a)
Solution: Milk = 36 L, water = 9 L. Removed: 7.2 L milk and 1.8 L water. Milk left = 28.8 L; water = 9 − 1.8 + 9 = 16.2 L. Ratio = 28.8 : 16.2 = 16:9. The trap is ignoring the milk lost in the removed portion, so the answer is 16:9.
Source: PYQ-style

71. How many kilograms of rice at ₹42 per kg must be mixed with 25 kg of rice at ₹60 per kg so that the mixture is worth ₹50 per kg?
(a) 30 kg (b) 35 kg (c) 32.5 kg (d) 31.25 kg
Answer: (d)
Solution: By alligation, ratio of cheaper to dearer = (60 − 50) : (50 − 42) = 10 : 8 = 5:4. So cheaper rice = 25 × 5/4 = 31.25 kg. The trap is inverting the ratio to get 20 kg; the larger difference pairs with the cheaper variety, so the answer is 31.25 kg.
Source: PYQ-style

72. A 40-litre mixture contains wine and water in the ratio 3:2. How much water must be added so that the ratio becomes 2:3?
(a) 20 litres (b) 15 litres (c) 24 litres (d) 25 litres
Answer: (a)
Solution: Wine = 24 L, water = 16 L. For ratio 2:3, water needed = 24 × 3/2 = 36 L, so water to add = 36 − 16 = 20 L. The trap is adding 8 L to merely reverse the original gap; wine stays fixed at 24 L, so the answer is 20 litres.
Source: PYQ-style

73. Alloy P contains copper and zinc in the ratio 5:2 and alloy Q in the ratio 3:4. Equal weights of both are melted together. The ratio of copper to zinc in the new alloy is:
(a) 8:6 (b) 3:4 (c) 4:3 (d) 1:1
Answer: (c)
Solution: Copper = 5/7 + 3/7 = 8/7; zinc = 2/7 + 4/7 = 6/7. Ratio = 8:6 = 4:3. The trap is adding the ratio terms to get 8:6 without reducing, or assuming the ratios cannot be added — here both alloys have 7 parts, so direct addition works and reduces to 4:3, so the answer is 4:3.
Source: PYQ-style (Alloy mixture)

74. A 60% alcohol solution is mixed with a 30% alcohol solution to obtain a 45% solution. If 30 litres of the 60% solution is used, how much of the 30% solution is needed?
(a) 20 litres (b) 30 litres (c) 45 litres (d) 60 litres
Answer: (b)
Solution: By alligation, ratio = (45 − 30) : (60 − 45) = 15 : 15 = 1:1. Hence 30 litres of the 30% solution is needed. The trap is assuming the weaker solution must be used in double quantity; 45% is the exact midpoint, so the answer is 30 litres.
Source: PYQ-style

75. A milkman has 20 litres of pure milk. He sells 4 litres and replaces it with water, then again sells 4 litres of the mixture and replaces it with water. The quantity of milk now left is:
(a) 12 litres (b) 12.8 litres (c) 13 litres (d) 14 litres
Answer: (b)
Solution: Milk left = 20 × (1 − 4/20)² = 20 × (0.8)² = 12.8 L. The trap is subtracting 4 L twice to get 12 L; after the first replacement the drawn liquid is a mixture containing only 80% milk, so the answer is 12.8 litres.
Source: PYQ-style (Repeated replacement)
