126. Two vessels contain milk and water in the ratios 5:2 and 7:6 respectively. Equal quantities are drawn from both vessels and mixed together. What is the ratio of milk to water in the new mixture?
(a) 12:8 (b) 34:57 (c) 57:34 (d) 55:36
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is simply adding the ratios to get 12:8, which ignores that the vessels have different total parts (7 and 13). Take 91 litres (LCM of 7 and 13) from each: milk = 65 + 49 = 114, water = 26 + 42 = 68, giving 114:68 = 57:34, so the answer is 57:34.

Answer: (c)
Solution: The tempting wrong approach is subtracting 4 × 4 = 16 litres to get 24 litres, but after each replacement the drawn liquid is a mixture, not pure milk. Milk left = 40 × (1 − 4/40)⁴ = 40 × (0.9)⁴ = 40 × 0.6561 = 26.244, so the answer is 26.244 litres.
