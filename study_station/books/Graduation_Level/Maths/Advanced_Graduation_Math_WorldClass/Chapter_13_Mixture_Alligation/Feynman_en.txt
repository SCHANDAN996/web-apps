# The Feynman Technique: Mixture & Alligation

## The Seesaw / Balance Beam Analogy
Imagine a playground seesaw. A heavier adult sits close to the center pivot, while a lighter child must sit far out on the opposite end to balance the beam.
Alligation is nothing more than that exact seesaw balance!
The mean price (or concentration) is the center pivot (fulcrum).
The distance from the cheaper price to the mean is $(m - c)$.
The distance from the dearer price to the mean is $(d - m)$.
To keep the financial value balanced, the weights (quantities) must be inversely proportional to their distances from the pivot:
$$\text{Quantity}_c \times (m - c) = \text{Quantity}_d \times (d - m)$$
Rearranging yields the Rule of Alligation:
$$\frac{\text{Quantity}_c}{\text{Quantity}_d} = \frac{d - m}{m - c}$$

## The Wine and Water Illusion
Suppose you have a cask filled with 40 liters of pure wine. You steal 4 liters of wine and replace it with 4 liters of water.
Now you have 36 liters of wine and 4 liters of water. The fraction of wine left is $36 / 40 = 9/10 = (1 - 4/40)$.
What happens if you steal 4 liters again?
This second time, you are NOT stealing 4 liters of pure wine! You are stealing 4 liters of the mixture, which is 90% wine and 10% water.
So you only steal $4 \times 0.9 = 3.6$ liters of wine.
The wine remaining is $36 - 3.6 = 32.4$ liters, which is exactly $40 \times (9/10) \times (9/10) = 40 \times (1 - 4/40)^2$.
Each replacement multiplies the remaining wine by the same factor $(1 - y/x)$. That is why the formula is an exponential decay.
