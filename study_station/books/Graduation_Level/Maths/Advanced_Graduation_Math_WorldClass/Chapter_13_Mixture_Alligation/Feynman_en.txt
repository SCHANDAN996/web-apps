# 🧒 Feynman Explains: The Alligation Cross — The "Seesaw" of Mixtures

## The Story of the Two Lemonade Stalls

Imagine you're 12 years old, and it's a hot summer day. Your street has two lemonade stalls.

**Stall A** sells lemonade at **₹10 per glass** — cheap, watery stuff.
**Stall B** sells lemonade at **₹25 per glass** — the fancy, extra-lemony one.

Now your uncle, who runs a juice shop, says: *"I want to sell lemonade at ₹18 per glass. I'll mix the cheap one and the fancy one. But in what ratio should I mix them?"*

You could do algebra. Set up equations. Groan. OR — you could use the trick that toppers use to solve this in **5 seconds flat**.

## The Seesaw Trick (This Is All Alligation Really Is!)

Think of a **seesaw** in a park.

- The cheap lemonade (₹10) sits on the **left end**.
- The fancy lemonade (₹25) sits on the **right end**.
- Your target price (₹18) is the **balance point** in the middle — where the seesaw perfectly balances.

Now here's the magic: **the closer the balance point is to one side, the MORE of that side you need.**

- Distance from ₹18 to ₹25 (the fancy side) = 25 − 18 = **7**
- Distance from ₹18 to ₹10 (the cheap side) = 18 − 10 = **8**

Now — and this is the part everyone forgets — **you CROSS the distances!**

- Amount of **cheap** lemonade = distance to the **fancy** side = **7 parts**
- Amount of **fancy** lemonade = distance to the **cheap** side = **8 parts**

**Ratio = 7 : 8.** Done. No equations. No x and y.

```
   ₹10  ────────  ₹18  ────────  ₹25
  (cheap)      (target)       (fancy)
     │             │             │
     └── 8 ──→  FANCY amount     │
     │             │             │
     └── 7 ←──  CHEAP amount ────┘
```

## Why Does the Crossing Work? (The One-Line Secret)

Because a seesaw balances when **weight × distance** is equal on both sides. The side that's *farther* from the balance point needs *less* weight; the side that's *closer* needs *more*. So the distances automatically swap to become the quantities. That's it — the entire Alligation Rule is just a playground seesaw wearing a maths costume.

## 🎯 The Unforgettable Trick: **"FAR side, NEAR amount — CROSS it!"**

Say it like a chant: *"Cross the gap, get the ratio!"*

Whatever exam throws at you — milk & water, two alloys, two interest rates, two average marks, two speeds — it's always the same seesaw:

1. Put the **two given values** at the ends.
2. Put the **target (mean) value** in the middle.
3. **Subtract** each end from the middle.
4. **Cross** the answers → that's your ratio.

## One Last Thing — Can YOU Balance This Seesaw?

A grocer mixes rice at **₹40/kg** with rice at **₹65/kg** to get a mixture worth **₹55/kg**. In what ratio does he mix them?

*(Balance the seesaw. Cross the gaps. If you got 2 : 3 — congratulations, you just mastered the hardest concept in this chapter in under five minutes. Feynman would be proud.)*
