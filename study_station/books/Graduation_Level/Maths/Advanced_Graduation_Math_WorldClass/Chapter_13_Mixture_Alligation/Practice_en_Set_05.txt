101. A vessel contains 60 litres of milk. 12 litres of milk is taken out and replaced with water. This process is repeated once more. How much milk remains in the vessel?
(a) 36 litres (b) 38.4 litres (c) 40 litres (d) 42 litres
Answer: (b)
Solution: The tempting wrong answer 36 litres wrongly subtracts 12 litres twice from 60. Each replacement removes 12/60 = 1/5 of the milk present, so milk left = 60 × (4/5) × (4/5) = 60 × 16/25 = 38.4 litres, so the answer is 38.4 litres.
Source: PYQ-style

102. In what ratio must rice costing ₹40 per kg be mixed with rice costing ₹60 per kg so that the mixture costs ₹48 per kg?
(a) 2 : 3 (b) 2 : 1 (c) 1 : 2 (d) 3 : 2
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap answer 2 : 3 comes from writing the differences in the same order as the prices. By alligation, cheaper : dearer = (60 − 48) : (48 − 40) = 12 : 8 = 3 : 2, so the answer is 3 : 2.
Source: PYQ-style

103. A mixture of 40 litres has milk and water in the ratio 3 : 1. How much water must be added to make the ratio 2 : 1?
(a) 4 litres (b) 5 litres (c) 6 litres (d) 8 litres
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap answer 4 litres comes from wrongly scaling both parts. Milk = 30 litres, water = 10 litres; new ratio 2 : 1 means water = 30/2 = 15 litres, so water added = 15 − 10 = 5 litres, so the answer is 5 litres.

104. A grocer mixes two varieties of tea costing ₹180 per kg and ₹240 per kg in the ratio 2 : 3. At what price per kg should he sell the mixture to gain 25%?
(a) ₹250 (b) ₹260 (c) ₹280 (d) ₹270
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap answer ₹260 comes from averaging the prices first and then adding 25% incorrectly. Cost price of mixture = (2 × 180 + 3 × 240)/5 = 1080/5 = ₹216; selling price = 216 × 1.25 = ₹270, so the answer is ₹270.
Source: PYQ-style

105. A can contains a mixture of two liquids A and B in the ratio 7 : 5. When 9 litres of mixture is drawn off and replaced with B, the ratio becomes 7 : 9. How many litres of A did the can initially hold?
(a) 21 litres (b) 20 litres (c) 10 litres (d) 25 litres
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap answer 25 comes from assuming the total is 36 litres without checking. A removed = 9 × 7/12 = 21/4 litres; A falls from 7T/12 to 7T/16, so 7T(1/12 − 1/16) = 21/4, giving 7T/48 = 21/4 and T = 36 litres. A initially = 36 × 7/12 = 21 litres, so the answer is 21 litres.
Source: PYQ-style

106. Two vessels contain milk and water in the ratios 5 : 3 and 2 : 3. In what ratio should the mixtures be taken so that the new mixture has milk and water in equal quantities?
(a) 5 : 4 (b) 2 : 1 (c) 1 : 2 (d) 4 : 5
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap answer 1 : 2 comes from inverting the alligation differences. Milk fractions are 5/8 and 2/5, target 1/2; first : second = (1/2 − 2/5) : (5/8 − 1/2) = 1/10 : 1/8 = 4 : 5. Check: milk = 4 × 5/8 + 5 × 2/5 = 2.5 + 2 = 4.5 out of 9, exactly half, so the answer is 4 : 5.
Source: PYQ-style

107. A dishonest milkman sells milk at cost price but mixes water to gain 25%. What is the percentage of water in the mixture?
(a) 25% (b) 15% (c) 20% (d) 33.33%
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap answer 25% confuses gain percentage with water percentage. Gain of 25% means 1 litre of mixture contains milk worth 1/1.25 = 0.8 litre, so water = 0.2 litre, i.e. 20%, so the answer is 20%.

108. How many litres of water must be added to 16 litres of a 25% alcohol solution to make it a 20% solution?
(a) 3 litres (b) 5 litres (c) 4 litres (d) 6 litres
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap answer 5 litres comes from solving 4/(16 + x) = 1/5 incorrectly. Alcohol = 4 litres stays constant: 4/(16 + x) = 20/100 gives 16 + x = 20, so x = 4 litres, so the answer is 4 litres.

109. A mixture contains spirit and water in the ratio 5 : 3. If 16 litres of the mixture is removed and replaced with water, the ratio becomes 3 : 5. Find the total quantity of the original mixture.
(a) 30 litres (b) 32 litres (c) 40 litres (d) 48 litres
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap answer 32 litres comes from equating spirit fractions without accounting for the removed mixture. Spirit removed = 16 × 5/8 = 10 litres; original spirit 5T/8 becomes 3T/8, so 5T/8 − 10 = 3T/8, giving 2T/8 = 10 and T = 40 litres, so the answer is 40 litres.
Source: PYQ-style

110. In what ratio must a grocer mix sugar at ₹36 per kg with sugar at ₹45 per kg so that by selling the mixture at ₹44 per kg he gains 10%?
(a) 2 : 3 (b) 3 : 2 (c) 4 : 5 (d) 5 : 4
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap answer 4 : 5 reverses the ratio. Cost price of mixture = 44/1.1 = ₹40; by alligation, (45 − 40) : (40 − 36) = 5 : 4, so the answer is 5 : 4.

111. A jar full of whisky contains 40% alcohol. A part of this is replaced with another whisky containing 19% alcohol, and now the jar has 26% alcohol. What fraction of the jar was replaced?
(a) 1/3 (b) 2/3 (c) 2/5 (d) 3/5
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap answer 1/3 inverts the alligation parts. By alligation on alcohol percentages: (26 − 19) : (40 − 26) = 7 : 14 = 1 : 2, so the new whisky forms 2/3 of the jar, meaning 2/3 was replaced, so the answer is 2/3.

112. Milk and water are mixed in vessel A in the ratio 5 : 2 and in vessel B in the ratio 8 : 5. In what ratio should quantities be taken from A and B to form a mixture with milk and water in the ratio 9 : 4?
(a) 2 : 7 (b) 7 : 2 (c) 3 : 5 (d) 5 : 3
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap answer 2 : 7 reverses the alligation result. Milk fractions: A = 5/7, B = 8/13, target = 9/13; A : B = (9/13 − 8/13) : (5/7 − 9/13) = 1/13 : 2/91 = 7 : 2, so the answer is 7 : 2.

114. Two alloys contain copper and zinc in the ratios 3 : 4 and 5 : 9. Equal weights of both alloys are melted together. What is the ratio of copper to zinc in the new alloy?
(a) 8 : 13 (b) 4 : 5 (c) 17 : 11 (d) 11 : 17
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap answer 8 : 13 comes from simply adding the ratio terms 3 + 5 and 4 + 9. Take 14 g of each alloy (LCM of 7 and 14): copper = 6 + 5 = 11 g, zinc = 8 + 9 = 17 g, so the answer is 11 : 17.

115. A vessel is filled with liquid, 3 parts of which are water and 5 parts syrup. How much of the mixture must be drawn off and replaced with water so that the mixture becomes half water and half syrup?
(a) 1/3 (b) 1/4 (c) 1/5 (d) 1/7
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap answer 1/4 comes from halving the syrup difference directly. Syrup must fall from 5/8 to 1/2, i.e. by 1/8 of the vessel; each replacement of fraction x removes syrup 5x/8, so 5x/8 = 1/8, giving x = 1/5, so the answer is 1/5.
Source: PYQ-style

116. A trader has 50 kg of pulses, part of which he sells at 8% profit and the rest at 18% profit. He gains 14% on the whole. How much was sold at 18% profit?
(a) 30 kg (b) 25 kg (c) 35 kg (d) 20 kg
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap answer 20 kg assigns the larger alligation part to the wrong portion. By alligation: (18 − 14) : (14 − 8) = 4 : 6 = 2 : 3, so the 18% portion is 3/5 of 50 = 30 kg, so the answer is 30 kg.

119. A sum of ₹390 was invested partly at 8% and partly at 12% simple interest. The total annual interest received was ₹38. How much was invested at 12%?
(a) ₹130 (b) ₹170 (c) ₹190 (d) ₹200
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap answer ₹130 wrongly assigns the larger alligation part to the 8% portion. Solve directly: 8x + 12(390 − x) = 3800 gives 4680 − 4x = 3800, so x = 220 at 8% and the 12% portion = 390 − 220 = ₹170, so the answer is ₹170.

120. A container has 80 litres of milk. From this, 8 litres of milk is taken out and replaced by water. This action is repeated two more times. What is the amount of milk left?
(a) 54 litres (b) 56 litres (c) 58.32 litres (d) 60 litres
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap answer 56 litres wrongly subtracts 8 litres three times. Each operation retains 72/80 = 9/10 of the milk, so milk left = 80 × (9/10)³ = 80 × 0.729 = 58.32 litres, so the answer is 58.32 litres.

121. Three vessels of equal capacity contain mixtures of milk and water in the ratios 3 : 2, 7 : 3 and 11 : 4 respectively. The contents of all three are mixed together. What is the ratio of milk to water in the final mixture?
(a) 61 : 29 (b) 29 : 61 (c) 21 : 19 (d) 19 : 21
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap answer 29 : 61 inverts milk and water. Take capacity 90 (LCM of 5, 10, 15): milk = 54 + 63 + 66 = 183, water = 36 + 27 + 24 = 87, and 183 : 87 = 61 : 29, so the answer is 61 : 29.

122. A vessel contains a mixture of milk and water in the ratio 4 : 1. Some quantity is drawn off and replaced with water, and the resulting ratio is 3 : 2. What fraction of the mixture was drawn off?
(a) 1/5 (b) 2/5 (c) 1/4 (d) 1/3
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap answer 1/5 comes from comparing milk fractions without scaling. Milk falls from 4/5 to 3/5 of the total; if fraction x is replaced, milk left = 4/5 × (1 − x) = 3/5, so 1 − x = 3/4 and x = 1/4, so the answer is 1/4.

123. A milk vendor has two cans. The first contains 25% water and the rest milk; the second contains 50% water. How much mixture should he take from each can to get 12 litres of mixture with water and milk in the ratio 3 : 5?
(a) 6 litres, 6 litres (b) 8 litres, 4 litres (c) 5 litres, 7 litres (d) 4 litres, 8 litres
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap answer 4 litres, 8 litres applies alligation inconsistently. Milk fractions are 3/4 and 1/2, target milk = 5/8; by alligation, (5/8 − 1/2) : (3/4 − 5/8) = 1/8 : 1/8 = 1 : 1, so 6 litres from each can, so the answer is 6 litres, 6 litres.
Source: PYQ-style

124. A butler stole wine from a cask containing 32% spirit and replaced it with wine containing only 18% spirit. The cask then had 24% spirit. What fraction of the original wine did the butler steal?
(a) 4/7 (b) 3/7 (c) 5/7 (d) 1/2
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap answer 3/7 gives the fraction remaining instead of the fraction stolen. By alligation: original : replacement = (24 − 18) : (32 − 24) = 6 : 8 = 3 : 4, so the replacement (stolen amount) = 4/7 of the cask, so the answer is 4/7.
Source: PYQ-style
