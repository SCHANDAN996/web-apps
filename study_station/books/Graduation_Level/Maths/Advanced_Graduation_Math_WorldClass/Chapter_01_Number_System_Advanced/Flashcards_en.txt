Card 1
Front: What is the divisibility rule for 11?
Back: The difference between the sum of digits at odd places and the sum of digits at even places is either 0 or divisible by 11.

Card 2
Front: What is the remainder when 2^100 is divided by 3? (Use the pattern trick)
Back: 1. Powers of 2 alternate remainders 2, 1, 2, 1... when divided by 3. Even power → remainder 1.

Card 3
Front: State Fermat's Little Theorem.
Back: If p is prime and a is not divisible by p, then a^(p−1) ≡ 1 (mod p).

Card 4
Front: How do you find the unit digit of 7^103?
Back: Cyclicity of 7 is 4 (7, 9, 3, 1). 103 ÷ 4 leaves remainder 3 → unit digit = 3.

Card 5
Front: What is the mnemonic for cyclicity of unit digits?
Back: "4-2-1 Rule": digits 4 and 9 have cycle 2; digits 2, 3, 7, 8 have cycle 4; digits 0, 1, 5, 6 always stay the same — remember "0156 are lazy, they never change."

Card 6
Front: How many trailing zeros does 100! have?
Back: 24. Count factors of 5: ⌊100/5⌋ + ⌊100/25⌋ = 20 + 4 = 24.

Card 7
Front: What is Euler's Totient function φ(n) for n = 36?
Back: φ(36) = 36 × (1 − 1/2)(1 − 1/3) = 12.

Card 8
Front: State Euler's Theorem.
Back: If gcd(a, n) = 1, then a^φ(n) ≡ 1 (mod n).

Card 9
Front: What is the remainder when 7^84 is divided by 17?
Back: 1. By Fermat's Little Theorem, 7^16 ≡ 1 (mod 17); 84 = 16×5 + 4, so 7^84 ≡ 7^4 = 2401 ≡ 2401 − 17×141 = 4... check: 7^2=49≡15, 7^4≡225≡4 (mod 17). Answer: 4.

Card 10
Front: What is the divisibility rule for 7 using the "double and subtract" method?
Back: Remove the last digit, double it, subtract from the remaining number. If the result is divisible by 7, so is the original number.

Card 11
Front: What is the mnemonic to remember prime-counting in factorials (Legendre's formula)?
Back: "Divide and keep dividing": keep dividing n by p, p², p³... and add all quotients — "Chop till you drop."

Card 12
Front: How do you find the number of factors of N = p^a × q^b × r^c?
Back: (a+1)(b+1)(c+1).

Card 13
Front: What is the sum of all factors of N = p^a × q^b?
Back: [(p^(a+1) − 1)/(p − 1)] × [(q^(b+1) − 1)/(q − 1)].

Card 14
Front: What is a Harshad (Niven) number?
Back: A number divisible by the sum of its digits, e.g., 18 (1+8=9, 18÷9=2).

Card 15
Front: What is the remainder when a number is divided by 9?
Back: The same as the remainder when the sum of its digits is divided by 9 (digital root principle).

Card 16
Front: What is the mnemonic for remembering the order of cyclicity checks?
Back: "Last digit, last two, last three": for unit digit use mod 4 cycle; for last two digits of powers, patterns often repeat every 20; "Small mod, big power — cycle it!"

Card 17
Front: How do you find the last two digits of 7^86?
Back: 7^4 = 2401 → last two digits 01. 86 = 4×21 + 2 → 7^86 ends like 7^2 = 49. Answer: 49.

Card 18
Front: What is the Chinese Remainder Theorem used for?
Back: Finding a number that leaves given remainders when divided by pairwise coprime divisors — a unique solution exists modulo the product of the divisors.

Card 19
Front: What is the highest power of 3 in 50!?
Back: ⌊50/3⌋ + ⌊50/9⌋ + ⌊50/27⌋ = 16 + 5 + 1 = 22.

Card 20
Front: What is the trick to check divisibility by 13?
Back: Remove the last digit, multiply it by 4, and add to the remaining number. If the result is divisible by 13, so is the original number.
