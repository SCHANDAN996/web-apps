76. What is the remainder when 7^84 is divided by 342?
(a) 49 (b) 1 (c) 7 (d) 343
Answer: (b)
Solution: Note that 7^3 = 343 ≡ 1 (mod 342). Since 84 = 3 × 28, we get 7^84 = (7^3)^28 ≡ 1^28 = 1 (mod 342). The tempting wrong option 7 comes from stopping at the first power instead of using the cube cycle, so the answer is 1.
Source: PYQ-style

77. The number 3^6 × 5^4 × 7^2 has how many factors that are perfect squares?
(a) 48 (b) 36 (c) 24 (d) 12
Answer: (c)
Solution: A perfect-square factor needs even exponents: 3^6 allows 0, 2, 4, 6 (4 choices); 5^4 allows 0, 2, 4 (3 choices); 7^2 allows 0, 2 (2 choices). Total = 4 × 3 × 2 = 24. The trap option 12 comes from halving the exponents without adding 1, so the answer is 24.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Mathematics

78. Find the unit digit of 1! + 2! + 3! + … + 100!.
(a) 3 (b) 4 (c) 7 (d) 9
Answer: (a)
Solution: From 5! onward every factorial ends in 0, so only 1! + 2! + 3! + 4! = 1 + 2 + 6 + 24 = 33 matters, giving unit digit 3. The trap option 4 comes from wrongly including the unit digit of 5!, so the answer is 3.
Source: PYQ-style

79. What is the largest power of 12 that divides 50!?
(a) 24 (b) 48 (c) 47 (d) 22
Answer: (d)
Solution: 12 = 2^2 × 3. In 50!: power of 2 = 25 + 12 + 6 + 3 + 1 = 47, so power of 2^2 = ⌊47/2⌋ = 23; power of 3 = 16 + 5 + 1 = 22. The limiting factor is 3. The trap option 24 comes from using the 2^2 count and ignoring that 3 is scarcer, so the answer is 22.
Source: PYQ-style

80. If N = 2^4 × 3^3 × 5^2, how many factors of N are odd?
(a) 8 (b) 20 (c) 16 (d) 12
Answer: (d)
Solution: Odd factors exclude the prime 2, so they come from 3^3 × 5^2: (3+1)(2+1) = 12. The trap option 20 comes from including the power of 2 in the count, so the answer is 12.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Mathematics

81. The HCF of two numbers is 13 and their LCM is 2730. If one number is 195, find the other.
(a) 195 (b) 169 (c) 182 (d) 210
Answer: (c)
Solution: Product of numbers = HCF × LCM = 13 × 2730 = 35490. Other number = 35490 / 195 = 182. The trap option 169 = 13^2 looks neat but fails the product check, so the answer is 182.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Mathematics

82. What is the remainder when 2^1000 is divided by 7?
(a) 1 (b) 6 (c) 2 (d) 4
Answer: (c)
Solution: Powers of 2 mod 7 cycle as 2, 4, 1 with period 3. Since 1000 = 3 × 333 + 1, 2^1000 ≡ 2^1 = 2 (mod 7). The trap option 1 comes from assuming 1000 is divisible by 3 (its digit sum is 1, so it is not), so the answer is 2.
Source: PYQ-style

83. A number when divided by 5 leaves remainder 3, and when divided by 7 leaves remainder 5. What is the smallest such positive number?
(a) 18 (b) 33 (c) 38 (d) 68
Answer: (b)
Solution: Both conditions have divisor − remainder = 2, so N + 2 is divisible by both 5 and 7, i.e., by 35. Smallest N = 35 − 2 = 33. The trap option 38 comes from adding 3 instead of subtracting 2, so the answer is 33.
Source: PYQ-style

84. How many zeros are there at the end of 125!?
(a) 25 (b) 32 (c) 30 (d) 31
Answer: (d)
Solution: Trailing zeros = ⌊125/5⌋ + ⌊125/25⌋ + ⌊125/125⌋ = 25 + 5 + 1 = 31. The trap option 30 forgets the extra factor contributed by 125 = 5^3, so the answer is 31.
Source: PYQ-style

85. If the number 4567x32 is divisible by 8, what is the sum of all possible values of x?
(a) 10 (b) 15 (c) 20 (d) 25
Answer: (c)
Solution: Divisibility by 8 depends on the last three digits x32. Testing x = 0 to 9: 032, 232, 432, 632, 832 are divisible by 8, so x ∈ {0, 2, 4, 6, 8} and the sum is 20. The trap option 10 comes from taking only half the valid digits, so the answer is 20.
Source: NCERT Class 8 Mathematics

86. What is the remainder when (17^23 + 19^23) is divided by 36?
(a) 12 (b) 18 (c) 0 (d) 24
Answer: (c)
Solution: For odd n, a^n + b^n is divisible by a + b. Here a + b = 17 + 19 = 36 and n = 23 is odd, so the sum is divisible by 36, leaving remainder 0. The trap option 18 comes from halving 36 without reason, so the answer is 0.
Source: PYQ-style

87. The sum of the digits of a two-digit number is 11. If the digits are reversed, the number increases by 27. Find the original number.
(a) 47 (b) 56 (c) 65 (d) 74
Answer: (a)
Solution: Let the number be 10a + b with a + b = 11. Reversal adds 9(b − a) = 27, so b − a = 3. Solving, a = 4, b = 7, number = 47. The trap option 74 is the reversed number itself, so the answer is 47.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Mathematics

88. How many numbers between 100 and 1000 are divisible by both 6 and 9?
(a) 50 (b) 51 (c) 49 (d) 100
Answer: (a)
Solution: A number divisible by both 6 and 9 is divisible by LCM(6, 9) = 18. Multiples of 18 from 108 to 990: count = (990 − 108)/18 + 1 = 50. The trap option 100 comes from dividing 900 by 9 instead of using the LCM, so the answer is 50.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Mathematics

89. What is the value of 1/(1×2) + 1/(2×3) + … + 1/(99×100)?
(a) 99/100 (b) 98/99 (c) 1 (d) 100/99
Answer: (a)
Solution: Each term 1/(n(n+1)) = 1/n − 1/(n+1), so the series telescopes to 1 − 1/100 = 99/100. The trap option 1 comes from assuming the series sums to a whole, so the answer is 99/100.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Mathematics

90. The product of three consecutive positive integers is always divisible by which of the following?
(a) 4 (b) 6 (c) 9 (d) 12
Answer: (b)
Solution: Among any three consecutive integers, one is divisible by 3 and at least one is even, so the product is divisible by 2 × 3 = 6. The trap option 12 fails (for example, 1×2×3 = 6 is not divisible by 12), so the answer is 6.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Mathematics

91. What is the smallest number which when increased by 7 becomes divisible by 12, 18 and 24?
(a) 79 (b) 65 (c) 72 (d) 137
Answer: (b)
Solution: LCM(12, 18, 24) = 72. Required number = 72 − 7 = 65. The trap option 72 is the LCM itself, forgetting the "increased by 7" condition, so the answer is 65.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Mathematics

92. Find the last two digits of 7^86.
(a) 01 (b) 49 (c) 43 (d) 07
Answer: (b)
Solution: Powers of 7 mod 100 cycle as 07, 49, 43, 01 with period 4. Since 86 = 4 × 21 + 2, 7^86 ends like 7^2 = 49. The trap option 01 comes from rounding 86 to the nearest multiple of 4, so the answer is 49.
Source: PYQ-style

93. A number N has exactly 15 factors. Which of the following can be its prime factorisation form (p, q distinct primes)?
(a) p^15 (b) p q^7 (c) p^3 q^4 (d) p^4 q^2
Answer: (d)
Solution: The number of factors is the product of (exponent + 1) terms. For p^4 q^2 it is 5 × 3 = 15, which matches. The trap option p^15 gives 16 factors and p^3 q^4 gives 20, so the answer is p^4 q^2.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Mathematics

94. What is the remainder when 1^5 + 2^5 + 3^5 + … + 100^5 is divided by 4?
(a) 3 (b) 1 (c) 2 (d) 0
Answer: (d)
Solution: Mod 4, even terms contribute 0 (since 2^5 = 32 ≡ 0), while odd terms give 1^5 ≡ 1 and 3^5 ≡ 3. Among 1 to 100 there are 25 odd numbers ≡ 1 and 25 ≡ 3 (mod 4), summing to 25(1 + 3) = 100 ≡ 0 (mod 4). The trap option 2 comes from miscounting the odd terms, so the answer is 0.
Source: PYQ-style

95. The LCM of two numbers is 495 and their HCF is 5. If the sum of the numbers is 100, find the difference of the numbers.
(a) 10 (b) 20 (c) 30 (d) 40
Answer: (a)
Solution: Let the numbers be 5a and 5b with gcd(a, b) = 1. Then 5ab = 495 gives ab = 99, and 5(a + b) = 100 gives a + b = 20. The coprime pair is 9 and 11, so the numbers are 45 and 55. The trap option 20 misreads a + b as the difference, so the answer is 10.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Mathematics

96. How many integers from 1 to 300 are divisible by neither 3 nor 5?
(a) 150 (b) 160 (c) 140 (d) 180
Answer: (b)
Solution: Divisible by 3: 100; by 5: 60; by 15: 20. Divisible by 3 or 5 = 100 + 60 − 20 = 140, so neither = 300 − 140 = 160. The trap option 140 is the count divisible by at least one of them, so the answer is 160.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Mathematics

97. If 2^(2x−1) = 8^(3−x), find x.
(a) 2 (b) 3 (c) 4 (d) 5
Answer: (a)
Solution: 8^(3−x) = 2^(9−3x). Equating exponents: 2x − 1 = 9 − 3x, so 5x = 10 and x = 2. The trap option 5 comes from mishandling 5x = 10, so the answer is 2.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Mathematics

98. What is the greatest four-digit number which when divided by 15, 25, 40 and 75 leaves remainder 9 in each case?
(a) 9609 (b) 9909 (c) 9999 (d) 9009
Answer: (a)
Solution: LCM(15, 25, 40, 75) = 600. The greatest four-digit multiple of 600 is 9600; adding the remainder gives 9600 + 9 = 9609. The trap option 9909 fails because 9900 is not a multiple of 600, so the answer is 9609.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Mathematics

99. The number 10^N − 1 (N digits, all 9s) is always divisible by which of the following for every positive integer N?
(a) 7 (b) 11 (c) 9 (d) 13
Answer: (c)
Solution: 10^N − 1 = 999…9 = 9 × (111…1), so it is always divisible by 9. Divisibility by 11 holds only for even N, and by 7 or 13 only for specific N, so the answer is 9.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Mathematics

100. If a and b are positive integers such that a^2 − b^2 = 45, how many ordered pairs (a, b) are possible?
(a) 5 (b) 2 (c) 4 (d) 3
Answer: (d)
Solution: (a − b)(a + b) = 45 with both factors odd, which works for all factor pairs of 45: (1, 45), (3, 15), (5, 9), giving (a, b) = (23, 22), (9, 6), (7, 2). The trap option 5 counts reversed factor pairs as separate solutions, so the answer is 3.
Source: PYQ-style
