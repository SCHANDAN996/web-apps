Questions 1–20: Easy | Questions 21–25: Medium

1. What is the smallest prime number?
(a) 0 (b) 1 (c) 2 (d) 3
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is 1, which many students wrongly call prime; 1 has only one factor, so it is neither prime nor composite. A prime number has exactly two factors, and 2 has factors 1 and 2 only, so the answer is 2.
Source: NCERT Class 6 Mathematics

2. Which of the following is a rational number?
(a) 0.75 (b) √2 (c) π (d) √3
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is π, which looks like 22/7 but is actually irrational since it is non-terminating and non-repeating. 0.75 = 3/4, a ratio of two integers, so the answer is 0.75.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Mathematics

3. The face value of 7 in 47,532 is:
(a) 7000 (b) 700 (c) 70 (d) 7
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is 7000, which is the place value, not the face value. Face value of a digit is the digit itself regardless of position, so the answer is 7.
Source: NCERT Class 6 Mathematics

4. The place value of 5 in 3,56,821 is:
(a) 50000 (b) 500 (c) 5000 (d) 5
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is 5, which is the face value. The digit 5 is in the ten-thousands place, so its place value is 5 × 10000 = 50000, so the answer is 50000.
Source: NCERT Class 6 Mathematics

5. How many prime numbers are there between 1 and 10?
(a) 6 (b) 3 (c) 5 (d) 4
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is counting 1 as prime, which gives 5. The primes between 1 and 10 are 2, 3, 5, 7 — four numbers, so the answer is 4.
Source: NCERT Class 6 Mathematics

6. Which of the following numbers is divisible by 3?
(a) 124 (b) 235 (c) 451 (d) 342
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is checking the last digit instead of the digit sum. Sum of digits of 342 is 3+4+2 = 9, divisible by 3, so the answer is 342.
Source: NCERT Class 6 Mathematics

7. The HCF of 12 and 18 is:
(a) 3 (b) 6 (c) 2 (d) 36
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is 36, which is the LCM, not the HCF. Factors of 12 are 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 12 and of 18 are 1, 2, 3, 6, 9, 18; the highest common factor is 6, so the answer is 6.
Source: NCERT Class 6 Mathematics

8. The LCM of 4 and 6 is:
(a) 12 (b) 24 (c) 8 (d) 2
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is 24, the product of 4 and 6, which works only when numbers are coprime. Multiples of 4: 4, 8, 12… and of 6: 6, 12…, so the answer is 12.
Source: NCERT Class 6 Mathematics

9. What is the remainder when 47 is divided by 6?
(a) 4 (b) 5 (c) 7 (d) 6
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is 7, obtained by wrong division. Since 6 × 7 = 42 and 47 − 42 = 5, the remainder is 5, so the answer is 5.
Source: NCERT Class 6 Mathematics

10. Which of the following is an irrational number?
(a) 0.333… (b) 22/7 (c) √5 (d) 1.25
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is 0.333…, which is repeating and hence rational (equal to 1/3). √5 is non-terminating and non-repeating, so the answer is √5.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Mathematics

11. The number 1 is:
(a) Both prime and composite (b) Composite (c) Prime (d) Neither prime nor composite
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is calling 1 prime because it is divisible only by 1 and itself — but that gives only one factor, not two. By definition 1 is neither prime nor composite, so the answer is "Neither prime nor composite".
Source: NCERT Class 6 Mathematics

12. Which number is divisible by both 2 and 3?
(a) 345 (b) 123 (c) 234 (d) 451
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is 123, whose digit sum 6 is divisible by 3 but it is odd, so not divisible by 2. 234 is even and its digit sum 9 is divisible by 3, so the answer is 234.
Source: NCERT Class 6 Mathematics

13. The sum of the first 10 natural numbers is:
(a) 55 (b) 45 (c) 50 (d) 60
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is 50, from wrongly using n × 5. Using n(n+1)/2 = 10 × 11/2 = 55, so the answer is 55.
Source: NCERT Class 8 Mathematics

14. The unit digit of 7² is:
(a) 9 (b) 3 (c) 7 (d) 1
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is 7, the unit digit of 7¹. Since 7² = 49, the unit digit is 9, so the answer is 9.
Source: NCERT Class 8 Mathematics

15. How many factors does 12 have?
(a) 4 (b) 5 (c) 6 (d) 8
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is counting only prime factors. Factors of 12 are 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 12 — six in total, so the answer is 6.
Source: NCERT Class 6 Mathematics

16. The smallest composite number is:
(a) 1 (b) 2 (c) 4 (d) 6
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is 2, which is prime, not composite. A composite number has more than two factors; 4 has factors 1, 2, 4, so the answer is 4.
Source: NCERT Class 6 Mathematics

17. Which of the following fractions is equivalent to 3/4?
(a) 5/8 (b) 6/9 (c) 8/12 (d) 9/12
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is 6/9, which equals 2/3, not 3/4. Since 9/12 = (3×3)/(4×3) = 3/4, the answer is 9/12.
Source: NCERT Class 6 Mathematics

18. The value of 2⁵ is:
(a) 10 (b) 32 (c) 25 (d) 64
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is 10, from wrongly computing 2 × 5. Since 2⁵ = 2×2×2×2×2 = 32, the answer is 32.
Source: NCERT Class 8 Mathematics

19. Which of the following is a whole number but not a natural number?
(a) 0 (b) 0.5 (c) 1 (d) −1
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is −1, which is an integer but not a whole number. Natural numbers start from 1 and whole numbers start from 0, so the answer is 0.
Source: NCERT Class 6 Mathematics

20. The greatest two-digit number is:
(a) 100 (b) 90 (c) 98 (d) 99
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is 100, which is a three-digit number. The greatest number with exactly two digits is 99, so the answer is 99.
Source: NCERT Class 6 Mathematics

21. The HCF of two numbers is 11 and their LCM is 330. If one number is 55, the other number is:
(a) 66 (b) 60 (c) 77 (d) 110
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is 110, from doubling 55 without using the product rule. Using HCF × LCM = product of numbers: 11 × 330 = 55 × x, so x = 3630/55 = 66, so the answer is 66.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Mathematics

22. What is the unit digit of 3⁴⁵?
(a) 1 (b) 3 (c) 7 (d) 9
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is 9, obtained by taking 45 mod 4 = 1 and then wrongly picking the last cyclicity entry. Unit digits of powers of 3 cycle as 3, 9, 7, 1; since 45 = 4×11 + 1, the unit digit matches the first in the cycle, so the answer is 3.
Source: PYQ-style

23. The remainder when 2¹⁰ is divided by 7 is:
(a) 1 (b) 2 (c) 4 (d) 6
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is 4, from stopping at 2⁶ ÷ 7. Since 2³ = 8 leaves remainder 1 on division by 7, we get 2¹⁰ = 2⁹ × 2, which leaves remainder 1 × 2 = 2, so the answer is 2.
Source: PYQ-style

24. How many numbers between 100 and 300 are divisible by 7?
(a) 27 (b) 28 (c) 29 (d) 30
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is 29, from including 300 wrongly or miscounting endpoints. First multiple of 7 after 100 is 105, last before 300 is 294; count = (294 − 105)/7 + 1 = 27 + 1 = 28, so the answer is 28.
Source: PYQ-style

25. If the number 3A56B is divisible by 45, then the values of A and B are:
(a) A = 5, B = 0 (b) A = 8, B = 0 (c) A = 4, B = 0 (d) A = 4, B = 5
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is picking B = 5 without checking the digit-sum condition for 9; with B = 5 the digit sum is 19 + A, and A = 4 gives 23, not divisible by 9. Divisibility by 45 needs divisibility by both 5 and 9; with B = 0 the digit sum is 14 + A, so A = 4 makes it 18, and 34560 ÷ 45 = 768 exactly, so the answer is A = 4, B = 0.
Source: PYQ-style
