101. Two numbers are in the ratio 3 : 5. If 9 is added to each, the ratio becomes 5 : 8. The smaller number is:
(a) 72 (b) 81 (c) 63 (d) 54
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is mis-cross-multiplying the equation. Correctly, 8(3x + 9) = 5(5x + 9) gives 24x + 72 = 25x + 45, so x = 27. The smaller number is 3 × 27 = 81, so the answer is 81.
Source: PYQ-style

102. If A : B = 2 : 3 and B : C = 4 : 5, then A : C is:
(a) 2 : 5 (b) 8 : 12 (c) 3 : 5 (d) 8 : 15
Answer: (d)
Solution: Many students wrongly combine 2 : 3 and 4 : 5 as 2 : 5 directly, which ignores that B must be made common. Make B equal: A : B = 8 : 12 and B : C = 12 : 15, so the answer is 8 : 15.
Source: NCERT Class 8 Mathematics

103. The ratio of incomes of A and B is 5 : 4 and the ratio of their expenditures is 3 : 2. If each saves ₹1,600, the income of A is:
(a) ₹5,000 (b) ₹6,400 (c) ₹8,000 (d) ₹4,000
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is assuming savings also follow the ratio 5 : 4, which is false. Let incomes be 5x and 4x, expenditures 3y and 2y; then 5x − 3y = 1600 and 4x − 2y = 1600. Solving gives x = 800, so A's income is 5 × 800 = ₹4,000, and the answer is ₹4,000.
Source: PYQ-style

104. ₹6,200 is divided among A, B and C so that A gets half of what B gets, and B gets twice what C gets. B's share is:
(a) ₹3,100 (b) ₹1,550 (c) ₹2,066.67 (d) ₹1,240
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is taking the ratio as 1 : 2 : 1 instead of chaining correctly. Let C get x; then B gets 2x and A gets x, so total = 4x = 6200 and x = 1550. B's share is 2 × 1550 = ₹3,100, so the answer is ₹3,100.
Source: PYQ-style

105. The ratio of boys to girls in a school is 7 : 8. If the number of boys increases by 20% and girls increase by 10%, the new ratio is:
(a) 21 : 22 (b) 7 : 8 (c) 21 : 20 (d) 14 : 11
Answer: (a)
Solution: Students often add the percentages to the ratio terms directly, which is wrong. New boys = 7 × 1.20 = 8.4 and new girls = 8 × 1.10 = 8.8, giving 84 : 88 = 21 : 22, so the answer is 21 : 22.
Source: PYQ-style

106. If (3a + 5b) : (3a − 5b) = 5 : 1, then a : b is:
(a) 2 : 5 (b) 3 : 5 (c) 5 : 2 (d) 5 : 3
Answer: (c)
Solution: A frequent slip is writing the final ratio upside down after simplification. Cross-multiplying, 3a + 5b = 15a − 25b, so 30b = 12a and a : b = 30 : 12 = 5 : 2, so the answer is 5 : 2.
Source: PYQ-style

107. The fourth proportional to 6, 8 and 15 is:
(a) 24 (b) 18 (c) 20 (d) 12
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is computing the third proportional (8²/6) instead of the fourth. For the fourth proportional, 6 : 8 :: 15 : x, so x = (8 × 15)/6 = 20, and the answer is 20.
Source: NCERT Class 8 Mathematics

108. The mean proportional between 0.08 and 0.18 is:
(a) 0.0144 (b) 0.13 (c) 0.12 (d) 0.16
Answer: (c)
Solution: Many students take the arithmetic mean 0.13, which is the trap. Mean proportional = √(0.08 × 0.18) = √0.0144 = 0.12, so the answer is 0.12.
Source: NCERT Class 8 Mathematics

109. If x : y = 3 : 4, then (7x + 3y) : (7x − 3y) equals:
(a) 4 : 3 (b) 33 : 9 (c) 11 : 9 (d) 11 : 3
Answer: (d)
Solution: Substituting x = 3, y = 4 directly is fastest; the trap is algebraic mishandling or leaving the ratio unreduced as 33 : 9. Here 7x + 3y = 33 and 7x − 3y = 9, so the ratio is 33 : 9 = 11 : 3, and the answer is 11 : 3.
Source: PYQ-style

110. The prices of a scooter and a TV are in the ratio 7 : 5. If the scooter costs ₹8,000 more than the TV, the price of the TV is:
(a) ₹28,000 (b) ₹20,000 (c) ₹32,000 (d) ₹24,000
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is dividing 8000 by 7 or 5 instead of the difference of ratio terms. Difference = 7 − 5 = 2 parts = ₹8,000, so 1 part = ₹4,000 and the TV costs 5 × 4,000 = ₹20,000, so the answer is ₹20,000.
Source: PYQ-style

111. A bag contains 50 paise, 25 paise and 10 paise coins in the ratio 5 : 9 : 4, amounting to ₹206. The number of 25 paise coins is:
(a) 200 (b) 160 (c) 400 (d) 360
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is treating the coin ratio as the value ratio. Value ratio = (5 × 50) : (9 × 25) : (4 × 10) = 250 : 225 : 40 paise, total 515 paise-units, and 20600/515 = 40. Number of 25 paise coins = 9 × 40 = 360, so the answer is 360.
Source: PYQ-style

112. If a : b = 2 : 3, b : c = 4 : 5 and c : d = 6 : 7, then a : d is:
(a) 2 : 7 (b) 16 : 35 (c) 48 : 105 (d) 8 : 21
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is multiplying only the outer terms or leaving the answer unreduced as 48 : 105. Here a/d = (2/3) × (4/5) × (6/7) = 48/105 = 16/35, so the answer is 16 : 35.
Source: PYQ-style

113. The ratio of two numbers is 4 : 7. If each number is increased by 4, the ratio becomes 3 : 5. The sum of the numbers is:
(a) 88 (b) 44 (c) 132 (d) 176
Answer: (a)
Solution: A common slip is cross-multiplying with the wrong pairing. (4x + 4)/(7x + 4) = 3/5 gives 20x + 20 = 21x + 12, so x = 8; the numbers are 32 and 56, and the answer is 88.
Source: PYQ-style

114. Zinc and copper are in the ratio 5 : 3 in alloy A and 3 : 2 in alloy B. Equal weights of A and B are melted together; the ratio of zinc to copper in the new alloy is:
(a) 8 : 5 (b) 5 : 8 (c) 49 : 31 (d) 47 : 33
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is adding the ratios term by term (8 : 5), which ignores the different total parts. Take 40 units (LCM of 8 and 5) of each: zinc = 25 + 24 = 49 and copper = 15 + 16 = 31, so the answer is 49 : 31.
Source: PYQ-style

115. ₹1,180 is divided among A, B and C such that A : B = 3 : 4 and B : C = 5 : 6. C's share is:
(a) ₹300 (b) ₹480 (c) ₹400 (d) ₹500
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is combining the ratios as 3 : 4 : 6 without making B common. A : B = 15 : 20 and B : C = 20 : 24, so A : B : C = 15 : 20 : 24 with 59 parts; each part = 1180/59 = 20. C's share = 24 × 20 = ₹480, so the answer is ₹480.
Source: PYQ-style

116. If 2A = 3B = 4C, then A : B : C is:
(a) 2 : 3 : 4 (b) 6 : 4 : 3 (c) 4 : 3 : 2 (d) 3 : 4 : 6
Answer: (b)
Solution: The classic trap is answering 2 : 3 : 4, copying the coefficients. Let 2A = 3B = 4C = k; then A = k/2, B = k/3, C = k/4, and multiplying by 12 gives 6 : 4 : 3, so the answer is 6 : 4 : 3.
Source: PYQ-style

117. The ratio of the ages of a father and son is 7 : 2. After 10 years the ratio will be 9 : 4. The father's present age is:
(a) 42 years (b) 49 years (c) 35 years (d) 28 years
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is assuming the difference of ratio terms stays proportional to the age difference. (7x + 10)/(2x + 10) = 9/4 gives 28x + 40 = 18x + 90, so x = 5 and the father is 7 × 5 = 35 years old, so the answer is 35 years.
Source: PYQ-style

118. If (a + b) : (b + c) : (c + a) = 6 : 7 : 8 and a + b + c = 14, then c equals:
(a) 6 (b) 7 (c) 8 (d) 5
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is equating c directly with one ratio term. The three expressions sum to 2(a + b + c) = 28, matching 21 parts, so 1 part = 4/3 and a + b = 8. Then c = 14 − 8 = 6, so the answer is 6.
Source: PYQ-style

119. A sum of money is divided among P, Q and R in the ratio 1/2 : 1/3 : 1/4. If the total is ₹1,300, Q's share is:
(a) ₹400 (b) ₹300 (c) ₹600 (d) ₹450
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is using 1/2 : 1/3 : 1/4 as if the fractions were whole parts. Multiply by LCM 12 to get 6 : 4 : 3, total 13 parts; Q's share = (4/13) × 1300 = ₹400, so the answer is ₹400.
Source: PYQ-style

120. The third proportional to 9 and 12 is:
(a) 18 (b) 15 (c) 16 (d) 21
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is computing the mean proportional (√108) instead. Third proportional to 9 and 12: 9 : 12 :: 12 : x, so x = 144/9 = 16, and the answer is 16.
Source: NCERT Class 8 Mathematics

121. A and B started a business with capitals in the ratio 4 : 5. A invested for 8 months and B for 12 months. The profit-sharing ratio A : B is:
(a) 4 : 5 (b) 2 : 3 (c) 16 : 25 (d) 8 : 15
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is ignoring the time-weighting of capital and answering 4 : 5. A's effective capital-months = 4 × 8 = 32 and B's = 5 × 12 = 60, giving 32 : 60 = 8 : 15, so the answer is 8 : 15.
Source: PYQ-style

122. The ratio of the number of students in three classes is 2 : 3 : 5. If 20 students are added to each class, the ratio becomes 4 : 5 : 7. The total number of students initially was:
(a) 120 (b) 100 (c) 140 (d) 160
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is solving with only one equation; use the fact that differences between classes stay constant. Since the gaps are 1 and 2 parts in both ratios, the part size is unchanged, so 2x + 20 = 4x gives x = 10. Initial total = 10 × 10 = 100, so the answer is 100.
Source: PYQ-style

123. A mixture contains milk and water in the ratio 5 : 3. When 16 litres of water is added, the ratio becomes 5 : 7. The quantity of milk in the mixture is:
(a) 20 litres (b) 25 litres (c) 30 litres (d) 40 litres
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is changing both terms; only water changes, so milk stays fixed. With milk = 5x and water = 3x, 5x/(3x + 16) = 5/7 gives 35x = 15x + 80, so x = 4. Milk = 5 × 4 = 20 litres, so the answer is 20 litres.
Source: PYQ-style

124. If p : q : r = 1 : 2 : 4, then √(5p² + q² + r²) : q is:
(a) 5 : 2 (b) √21 : 2 (c) 3 : 1 (d) 2 : 1
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is forgetting to square the ratio terms inside the root. Let p = 1, q = 2, r = 4; then 5p² + q² + r² = 5 + 4 + 16 = 25 and √25 = 5, so the answer is 5 : 2.
Source: PYQ-style

125. Three containers have milk-water mixtures in ratios 2 : 3, 3 : 4 and 4 : 5. Equal volumes from all three are mixed. The ratio of milk to water in the resulting mixture is:
(a) 157 : 203 (b) 3 : 4 (c) 9 : 11 (d) 401 : 544
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is adding the ratios term by term (9 : 12), which ignores different total parts. Take 315 units (LCM of 5, 7, 9) from each: milk = 126 + 135 + 140 = 401 and water = 189 + 180 + 175 = 544, so the answer is 401 : 544.
Source: PYQ-style
