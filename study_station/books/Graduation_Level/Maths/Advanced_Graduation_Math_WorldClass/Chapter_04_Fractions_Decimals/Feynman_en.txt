# 🧒 Feynman Explanation: Fractions & Decimals

## The Hardest Concept: Why does 0.999… equal exactly 1? (And what it teaches us about fractions & decimals)

Imagine you're 12 years old, and your friend Ravi bets you ₹100:

> "I'll give you the money if you can tell me a number that sits *between* 0.999… (going on forever) and 1."

You think hard. 0.9999? No — 0.999… has *infinite* nines, so it's bigger. 0.99999? Still smaller. You try and try… and realize something shocking: **there is no number between them. They are the same number.**

Ravi owes you ₹100. Here's why.

---

## The Pizza Story 🍕

You and two friends order a pizza. You cut it into 3 equal pieces. Each person gets **1/3**.

Now write 1/3 as a decimal:

$$1 \div 3 = 0.3333\ldots \text{ (the 3s never stop)}$$

Now add all three pieces back together — in fractions:

$$\frac{1}{3} + \frac{1}{3} + \frac{1}{3} = \frac{3}{3} = 1 \text{ whole pizza ✅}$$

But in decimals:

$$0.333\ldots + 0.333\ldots + 0.333\ldots = 0.999\ldots$$

The pizza didn't change! Three friends ate **one whole pizza**, whether you count in fractions or decimals. So:

$$0.999\ldots = 1$$

**The punchline:** A fraction and a decimal are just *two languages* describing the same thing — like calling water "पानी" or "water." The pizza doesn't care what language you speak.

---

## The Real Lesson (this is what exams actually test)

Every fraction is a **division waiting to happen**:

- $\frac{1}{4}$ means "1 roti shared by 4 people" = 0.25 each
- $\frac{3}{8}$ means "3 chocolates shared by 8 kids" = 0.375 each

And every decimal is a **fraction in disguise**:

- 0.75 = 75/100 = 3/4
- 0.125 = 125/1000 = 1/8

Some fractions become **terminating** decimals (they stop: 1/4 = 0.25), and some become **recurring** decimals (they repeat forever: 1/3 = 0.333…). The rule is beautiful:

> A fraction p/q (in lowest terms) gives a **terminating** decimal only if the denominator q has **no prime factors other than 2 and 5**.

Why 2 and 5? Because our number system is base-10, and 10 = 2 × 5. Denominators made of only 2s and 5s can always be "padded" into 10, 100, 1000…

- 1/8 → 8 = 2³ ✅ terminates (0.125)
- 1/20 → 20 = 2² × 5 ✅ terminates (0.05)
- 1/6 → 6 = 2 × **3** ❌ repeats forever (0.1666…)
- 1/7 → 7 is a stranger ❌ repeats (0.142857142857…)

---

## 🎯 The Unforgettable Trick: "The 2-5 Party Rule" 🎉

> **"Only 2 and 5 are invited to the Decimal Party. If the denominator brings any other prime friend (3, 7, 11…), the party never ends — the decimal repeats forever!"**

Picture it: the denominator is a host. If the guest list is only 2s and 5s, the party wraps up cleanly (terminating decimal). If a 3 or 7 sneaks in, the party goes on all night (recurring decimal). 🥳🔁

**Memory hook:** *"Two-Five arrive, decimals survive. Three-Seven gatecrash, decimals thrash (repeat)!"*

---

## One More Feynman Gem: Comparing Fractions Like a Child

Which is bigger: 5/8 or 7/11? Don't panic. Just **cross-multiply** — multiply diagonally and compare:

$$5 \times 11 = 55 \quad \text{vs} \quad 7 \times 8 = 56$$

56 > 55, so **7/11 is bigger**. 

Why does this work? You're secretly converting both to a common denominator (88): 5/8 = 55/88 and 7/11 = 56/88. The trick just skips writing the denominators. Once you *see* the machinery, the trick is yours forever.
