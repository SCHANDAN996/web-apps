Card 1
Front: What is a proper fraction? Give an example.
Back: A fraction where numerator < denominator, e.g. 3/7. Its value is always less than 1.

Card 2
Front: What is the difference between an improper fraction and a mixed fraction?
Back: Improper: numerator ≥ denominator (e.g. 9/4). Mixed: whole number + proper fraction (e.g. 2¼). Both represent the same value.

Card 3
Front: How do you compare two fractions quickly using cross-multiplication?
Back: For a/b vs c/d, compare a×d with c×b. If a×d > c×b, then a/b > c/d.

Card 4
Front: What is the mnemonic to remember the order of operations in fraction simplification?
Back: BODMAS — Brackets, Orders, Division, Multiplication, Addition, Subtraction. "Big Old Dogs Make Awesome Snacks."

Card 5
Front: How do you convert a mixed fraction to an improper fraction?
Back: (Whole × Denominator + Numerator) / Denominator. E.g. 3⅖ = (3×5+2)/5 = 17/5.

Card 6
Front: Which fractions give terminating decimals?
Back: Fractions whose denominator (in lowest terms) has only prime factors 2 and/or 5. E.g. 3/8 = 0.375 (8 = 2³).

Card 7
Front: Which fractions give non-terminating recurring decimals?
Back: Fractions whose denominator (in lowest terms) has any prime factor other than 2 and 5. E.g. 5/6 = 0.8333… (6 = 2×3).

Card 8
Front: How do you convert 0.777… (pure recurring) to a fraction?
Back: Put the repeating digit(s) over as many 9s: 0.777… = 7/9.

Card 9
Front: How do you convert a mixed recurring decimal like 0.1666… to a fraction?
Back: (Whole number formed by all digits − non-repeating part) / (as many 9s as repeating digits followed by as many 0s as non-repeating digits). 0.1666… = (16−1)/90 = 15/90 = 1/6.

Card 10
Front: What are the decimal values of 1/2 to 1/10 worth memorising?
Back: 1/2 = 0.5, 1/3 = 0.333…, 1/4 = 0.25, 1/5 = 0.2, 1/6 = 0.1666…, 1/7 = 0.142857142857… (repeating), 1/8 = 0.125, 1/9 = 0.111…, 1/10 = 0.1. Learn 1/3, 1/6, 1/9 as the "3-6-9 repeating family"; 1/7 repeats a 6-digit block.

Card 11
Front: What is the LCM–HCF trick for adding fractions?
Back: Take LCM of denominators as the common denominator, scale each numerator, then add. Simplify the result by dividing by HCF of numerator and denominator.

Card 12
Front: How do you multiply fractions? What about division?
Back: Multiply: numerator × numerator, denominator × denominator. Division: multiply by the reciprocal — "Keep, Change, Flip" (keep first, change ÷ to ×, flip the second fraction).

Card 13
Front: What is the rule for comparing fractions with the same numerator?
Back: The fraction with the smaller denominator is larger. E.g. 5/7 > 5/9.

Card 14
Front: What is the rule for comparing fractions with the same denominator?
Back: The fraction with the larger numerator is larger. E.g. 7/11 > 4/11.

Card 15
Front: How do you arrange decimals in ascending order quickly?
Back: Equalise the number of decimal places by adding zeros, then compare as whole numbers. E.g. 0.5, 0.45, 0.505 → 500, 450, 505 → 0.45 < 0.5 < 0.505.

Card 16
Front: What happens to a fraction's value when the same number is added to both numerator and denominator (proper fraction)?
Back: For a proper fraction, the value increases (moves toward 1). E.g. 3/5 < 4/6 < 5/7. For an improper fraction, it decreases toward 1.

Card 17
Front: What is the shortcut for finding a fraction between two fractions a/b and c/d?
Back: Add numerators and denominators: (a+c)/(b+d) always lies between them. E.g. between 1/2 and 2/3 lies 3/5.

Card 18
Front: How do you multiply a decimal by 10, 100, 1000? And divide?
Back: Multiply: shift the decimal point right by the number of zeros. Divide: shift it left. E.g. 3.456 × 100 = 345.6; 3.456 ÷ 100 = 0.03456.

Card 19
Front: What is the mnemonic for converting fraction ↔ percentage?
Back: "F to P, ×100; P to F, ÷100" — "Fractions grow up (×100) to become Percentages; Percentages shrink down (÷100) to become Fractions."

Card 20
Front: What is the value of 0.999… and why?
Back: Exactly 1. Because 0.999… = 9/9 = 1 (pure recurring decimal rule).
