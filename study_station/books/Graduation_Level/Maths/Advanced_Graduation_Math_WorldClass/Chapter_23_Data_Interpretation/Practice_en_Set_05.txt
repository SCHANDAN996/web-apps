101. The table below shows the production (in thousands) of two products by a factory:
Years 2019, 2020, 2021, 2022, 2023 — Product A: 120, 150, 180, 200, 240; Product B: 100, 130, 160, 190, 210.
What is the total production of Product A over the five years (in thousands)?
(a) 870 (b) 890 (c) 910 (d) 850
Answer: (b)
Solution: Add the five values of Product A: 120 + 150 + 180 + 200 + 240 = 890. The option 910 is a trap for those who accidentally add Product B's last value twice or misread 240 as 260. So the answer is 890.
Source: PYQ-style

102. Using the same table (Product A: 120, 150, 180, 200, 240 for 2019–2023), what is the percentage increase in the production of Product A from 2019 to 2023?
(a) 80% (b) 90% (c) 120% (d) 100%
Answer: (d)
Solution: Increase = 240 − 120 = 120. Percentage increase = (120/120) × 100 = 100%. The trap is 120%, which comes from dividing the increase by the final value's difference wrongly or confusing the new value 240 with the increase. So the answer is 100%.

122. In a school of 1800 students, the ratio of boys to girls is 5:4. If 20% of the boys and 25% of the girls play cricket, cricket players form approximately what percentage of the school?
(a) 22.2% (b) 20.0% (c) 24.4% (d) 25.0%
Answer: (a)
Solution: Boys = (5/9) × 1800 = 1000; girls = 800. Cricket players = 20% of 1000 + 25% of 800 = 200 + 200 = 400. Percentage = (400/1800) × 100 ≈ 22.2%. The trap is 22.5%, from simply averaging 20% and 25% without weighting by the 5:4 ratio. So the answer is 22.2%.

123. The population of a town is 80,000. It increases by 10% in the first year and then decreases by 5% in the second year. What is the population at the end of the second year?
(a) 84,000 (b) 83,600 (c) 84,400 (d) 83,000
Answer: (b)
Solution: After the first year: 80,000 × 1.10 = 88,000. After the second year: 88,000 × 0.95 = 83,600. The trap is 84,000, from assuming net change is +5% (10% − 5%) on the original population, ignoring that the 5% decrease applies to the increased base. So the answer is 83,600.
Source: PYQ-style

125. A shopkeeper's monthly revenue (in ₹) and expense ratio (expenses as a percentage of revenue) are: January revenue 50,000, expenses 70%; February revenue 60,000, expenses 75%; March revenue 55,000, expenses 80%. What is the total profit over the three months (in ₹)?
(a) 41,000 (b) 40,000 (c) 42,000 (d) 39,500
Answer: (a)
Solution: Profits: January = 30% of 50,000 = 15,000; February = 25% of 60,000 = 15,000; March = 20% of 55,000 = 11,000. Total = 15,000 + 15,000 + 11,000 = 41,000. The trap is 42,000, from using the expense percentages as profit percentages in one month by mistake. So the answer is 41,000.
