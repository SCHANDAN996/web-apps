126. The table shows the production (in lakh tonnes) of wheat in a state over 5 years: 2019: 42, 2020: 48, 2021: 45, 2022: 54, 2023: 60. The production in 2023 is what percent more than the average production of 2019 and 2020?
(a) 36% (b) 30% (c) 33.33% (d) 40%
Answer: (c)
Solution: The tempting wrong option 30% comes from comparing 60 with 50 (a rough midpoint) instead of the exact average. Average of 2019 and 2020 = (42 + 48)/2 = 45. Increase = 60 − 45 = 15, and 15/45 × 100 = 33.33%, so the answer is 33.33%.
Source: PYQ-style

127. A pie chart shows the expenditure of a family: Food 30%, Rent 25%, Education 15%, Transport 10%, Savings 20%. If the total monthly income is ₹80,000 and the entire income is distributed as shown, by how much does the expenditure on Food exceed that on Education and Transport together?
(a) ₹6,000 (b) ₹10,000 (c) ₹8,000 (d) ₹4,000
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is to compute Food minus only Education (30% − 15% = 15% = ₹12,000), ignoring Transport. Education + Transport = 25%, so the difference is 30% − 25% = 5% of ₹80,000, so the answer is ₹4,000.
Source: PYQ-style

128. The table shows the number of bikes sold by a dealer: 2020: 150, 2021: 180, 2022: 216, 2023: 270. In which year was the percentage increase in sales over the previous year the highest?
(a) 2020 (b) 2021 (c) 2022 (d) 2023
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is to pick 2023 only because its absolute increase (54) is the largest without checking percentages — though here the percentage check is decisive. Increases: 2021 = 30/150 = 20%, 2022 = 36/180 = 20%, 2023 = 54/216 = 25%. The highest percentage increase is in 2023, so the answer is 2023.
Source: PYQ-style

129. The ratio of imports to exports of a country in 2020 was 3:2 and in 2021 it was 5:4. If exports in 2020 were ₹120 crore and imports in 2021 were ₹250 crore, what is the ratio of total trade (imports + exports) in 2020 to that in 2021?
(a) 2:3 (b) 3:4 (c) 4:5 (d) 5:6
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is to add the ratios directly (3+2 : 5+4 = 5:9), ignoring that the ratio units differ across years. 2020: exports = 120, so imports = 3/2 × 120 = 180, total = 300. 2021: imports = 250, so exports = 4/5 × 250 = 200, total = 450. Ratio = 300:450, so the answer is 2:3.
Source: PYQ-style

130. The table shows the number of employees in three departments of a firm: HR: 40, Tech: 120, Sales: 80. If 25% of HR, 40% of Tech and 35% of Sales employees are women, what percent of the total employees are men?
(a) 62.5% (b) 64.17% (c) 65.83% (d) 67.5%
Answer: (b)
Solution: The tempting wrong option 65.83% comes from averaging the men percentages (75%, 60%, 65%), which ignores that department sizes differ. Women = 10 + 48 + 28 = 86 out of 240 employees, so men = 154. Percentage of men = 154/240 × 100, so the answer is 64.17%.
Source: PYQ-style

131. A company's revenue grew by 20% in 2021, fell by 10% in 2022 and grew by 25% in 2023. If the revenue in 2020 was ₹500 crore, what was it at the end of 2023?
(a) ₹675 crore (b) ₹700 crore (c) ₹750 crore (d) ₹725 crore
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is to add the percentages (20 − 10 + 25 = 35%) and apply it in one step; the correct method is successive multiplication on changing bases. 500 × 1.20 = 600; 600 × 0.90 = 540; 540 × 1.25 = 675, so the answer is ₹675 crore.
Source: PYQ-style

Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is to pick Maths because 85 is the largest raw mark, ignoring the different maximum marks. Percentages: Maths 85%, Science 72%, English = 60/75 = 80%, Hindi = 54/60 = 90%, so the answer is Hindi.

133. A pie chart (total 360°) represents the budget of a project. The angle for Labour is 108°, for Material 144°, for Transport 54° and the rest for Overheads. If the total budget is ₹50 lakh, what is the expenditure on Overheads?
(a) ₹12.5 lakh (b) ₹5 lakh (c) ₹7.5 lakh (d) ₹10 lakh
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is to divide the Overheads angle by 100 instead of 360, treating degrees as percents directly. Overheads angle = 360 − (108 + 144 + 54) = 54°. Expenditure = 54/360 × 50 = ₹7.5 lakh, so the answer is ₹7.5 lakh.
Source: PYQ-style

134. The population of a town was 80,000 in 2021. It increased by 10% in 2022 and decreased by 5% in 2023. What is the net percentage change in population from 2021 to 2023?
(a) 4.5% decrease (b) 4.5% increase (c) 5% increase (d) 5% decrease
Answer: (b)
Solution: The tempting wrong option 5% increase comes from simply subtracting 10 − 5 = 5, ignoring that the 5% decrease applies to the increased base. Population 2022 = 88,000; population 2023 = 88,000 × 0.95 = 83,600. Net change = 3,600/80,000, so the answer is 4.5% increase.
Source: PYQ-style

135. The table shows the number of cars sold by a dealer: 2019: 250, 2020: 200, 2021: 300, 2022: 350, 2023: 400. The average sale of 2021, 2022 and 2023 is what percent of the total sale of all five years?
(a) 23.33% (b) 21% (c) 26.67% (d) 25%
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is to divide the 3-year average by the 3-year total instead of the 5-year total. Average of 2021–2023 = (300 + 350 + 400)/3 = 350. Total of five years = 250 + 200 + 300 + 350 + 400 = 1,500. Required percent = 350/1500 × 100, so the answer is 23.33%.
Source: PYQ-style

136. In a company, the ratio of officers to clerks is 3:7. The average salary of officers is ₹60,000 and that of clerks is ₹36,000. What is the average salary of all employees?
(a) ₹42,000 (b) ₹45,600 (c) ₹44,000 (d) ₹43,200
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is to take the simple average of the two salaries, ₹48,000, ignoring the 3:7 weights. Weighted average = (3 × 60,000 + 7 × 36,000)/10 = 4,32,000/10, so the answer is ₹43,200.
Source: PYQ-style

137. The bar graph shows exports (in ₹ crore) of two products: Product X: 2021: 80, 2022: 100, 2023: 120; Product Y: 2021: 120, 2022: 110, 2023: 100. In which year was the combined export of X and Y the highest, and what was its value?
(a) 2022, ₹210 crore (b) 2021, ₹200 crore (c) 2023, ₹220 crore (d) 2022, ₹220 crore
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is to assume the totals are equal because X rises as Y falls — but X rises by 20 each year while Y falls by only 10. Totals: 2021 = 200, 2022 = 210, 2023 = 220, so the answer is 2023, ₹220 crore.
Source: PYQ-style

138. A table gives the cost price and profit percentage of four items: Item P: CP ₹400, profit 25%; Item Q: CP ₹600, profit 20%; Item R: CP ₹500, profit 12%; Item S: CP ₹800, profit 16%. Which item gives the highest absolute profit?
(a) Item P (b) Item Q (c) Item R (d) Item S
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is to pick Item P because it has the highest profit percentage (25%), ignoring the cost price base. Absolute profits: P = ₹100, Q = ₹120, R = ₹60, S = 16% of 800 = ₹128. The largest is ₹128, so the answer is Item S.

139. The line graph shows the temperature (°C) of a city at 6-hour intervals: 6 AM: 18, 12 PM: 30, 6 PM: 26, 12 AM: 20. What is the average rate of change of temperature per hour between 6 AM and 12 PM?
(a) 1.5°C/hour (b) 2.5°C/hour (c) 2°C/hour (d) 3°C/hour
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is to divide the rise of 12°C by the number of readings (4) instead of the elapsed hours (6). Rise = 30 − 18 = 12°C over 6 hours, giving 12/6, so the answer is 2°C/hour.

(a) 360 (b) 270 (c) 330 (d) 300

Solution: The trap is to apply 40% and 50% to the total 1,800 directly, ignoring the stream shares. Science = 450 students, girls = 180; Commerce = 360 students, girls = 180. Total = 180 + 180, so the answer is 360.

142. The table shows the price of a share (₹) at month-end: Jan: 250, Feb: 240, Mar: 260, Apr: 255, May: 270. An investor bought 100 shares at the average price of Jan and Feb and sold all at the average price of Apr and May. What is the total profit?
(a) ₹1,500 (b) ₹2,000 (c) ₹1,750 (d) ₹2,250
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is to use the Jan price for buying and the May price for selling, giving 20 × 100 = ₹2,000. Buy average = (250 + 240)/2 = 245; sell average = (255 + 270)/2 = 262.5. Profit = 17.5 × 100, so the answer is ₹1,750.

143. The ratio of male to female employees in a firm is 5:3. If 60% of males and 40% of females are graduates, what percentage of all employees are non-graduates?
(a) 55% (b) 50% (c) 52.5% (d) 47.5%
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is to average the non-graduate percentages (40% and 60%) to get 50%, ignoring the 5:3 weights. Take 500 males and 300 females: graduates = 300 + 120 = 420, so non-graduates = 380 out of 800. Percentage = 380/800 × 100, so the answer is 47.5%.

147. In a survey of 1,200 people, 60% like tea, 50% like coffee and 30% like both. How many people like neither tea nor coffee?
(a) 300 (b) 240 (c) 180 (d) 360
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is to add 60% + 50% = 110% and conclude that 10% like neither, forgetting the overlap. People liking at least one = 60% + 50% − 30% = 80% of 1,200 = 960. Neither = 1,200 − 960, so the answer is 240.
Source: PYQ-style

148. The bar graph shows the number of books published by a press: Fiction 320, Non-fiction 240, Children 160, Academic 280. If Fiction books increase by 25% and Academic books decrease by 10% next year, while others stay the same, what will be the total number of books next year?
(a) 1,080 (b) 1,052 (c) 1,100 (d) 1,124
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is to apply the 25% increase to the total or to forget the Academic decrease. New Fiction = 320 × 1.25 = 400; new Academic = 280 × 0.90 = 252. Total = 400 + 240 + 160 + 252, so the answer is 1,052.

149. The table shows the marks of two students in four tests (each out of 50): Student A: 40, 35, 45, 38; Student B: 42, 38, 40, 44. By what percentage is B's total score more than A's total score?
(a) 3.16% (b) 4.2% (c) 3.8% (d) 5%
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is to divide the difference by B's total instead of A's total, since A is the base of comparison. A's total = 158, B's total = 164, difference = 6. Percentage more = 6/158 × 100 ≈ 3.8%, so the answer is 3.8%.

150. A man earns ₹50,000 a month. He spends ₹15,000 on rent and saves ₹10,000, both fixed in absolute terms; the remaining amount is split between Food and Travel in the ratio 3:2. After a 10% salary cut, with rent and savings unchanged, what is his new Food expense?
(a) ₹15,000 (b) ₹12,000 (c) ₹13,500 (d) ₹10,000
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is to take the original Food share (3/5 of 25,000 = ₹15,000) and forget that rent and savings stay fixed while the salary falls. New salary = ₹45,000; fixed items = 15,000 + 10,000 = 25,000; remaining = 20,000. Food = 3/5 × 20,000, so the answer is ₹12,000.
Source: PYQ-style
