Card 1
Front: What is the core skill tested in Data Interpretation?
Back: Extracting data from tables/graphs and converting it into percentages, ratios, averages or comparisons — calculation speed with approximation.

Card 2
Front: What is the formula for percentage change?
Back: % Change = (New Value − Old Value) / Old Value × 100

Card 3
Front: In a pie chart, how many degrees represent 1% of the total?
Back: 3.6° (since 360° = 100%)

Card 4
Front: How do you convert a pie-chart angle into a percentage?
Back: Percentage = (Angle ÷ 360°) × 100

Card 5
Front: What is the mnemonic for remembering the DI solving order?
Back: "READ before you CALC" — Read the question, Estimate, Approximate, Decide; only then Calculate.

Card 6
Front: What does the mnemonic "FLOP" stand for in fraction-to-percentage conversions?
Back: Fractions Learned Once, Percentages follow — memorize 1/2 to 1/12 conversions (e.g., 1/8 = 12.5%, 1/6 = 16.67%).

Card 7
Front: What is 1/7 as a percentage (approximately)?
Back: 14.28%

Card 8
Front: What is 1/11 as a percentage (approximately)?
Back: 9.09%

Card 9
Front: If a value increases by x% and then decreases by x%, what is the net change?
Back: Net decrease of x²/100 % (successive change formula: a + b + ab/100, with b negative)

Card 10
Front: In a bar graph question asking "which year had maximum growth", what trap should you avoid?
Back: Confusing absolute increase with percentage increase — always check which one the question asks.

Card 11
Front: What is the quick rule for comparing two ratios a/b and c/d?
Back: Cross-multiply: compare a×d with c×b. The larger product indicates the larger ratio.

Card 12
Front: When is approximation safe in DI calculations?
Back: When answer options are far apart (gap > 5–10%); round to nearest convenient numbers and estimate.

Card 13
Front: In a table with "total" given, how do you find what % one item is of the total?
Back: (Item value ÷ Total) × 100 — often faster by comparing item to 10% or 25% benchmarks of the total.

Card 14
Front: What is the average of a data set, and its quick-estimation trick?
Back: Sum ÷ Count. Trick: assume a round number as base average, then adjust by mean of deviations.

Card 15
Front: In a line graph showing two variables, what does the steeper slope indicate?
Back: Faster rate of change (growth or decline) over that period — not necessarily a higher value.

Card 16
Front: What is the mnemonic "DI = D minus I" trick for caselet/paragraph DI?
Back: Data minus Irrelevance — first strike out sentences with no numbers; only 2–3 numeric lines usually matter.

Card 17
Front: How do you quickly compute "A is what percent more than B"?
Back: (A − B)/B × 100. Trap: the base is B (the one after "than"), not A.

Card 18
Front: In a mixed graph (bar + line), what does each axis usually represent?
Back: Bars = absolute values (one axis), Line = percentage/ratio (secondary axis) — always read both axis labels first.

Card 19
Front: What is the rule for "percentage points" vs "percent"?
Back: A rise from 20% to 25% is 5 percentage points, but a 25% percent increase ((25−20)/20 × 100).

Card 20
Front: What is the first thing to check before solving any DI set?
Back: Units and scale — thousands vs lakhs vs crores, percentages vs absolute values, and the base year.
