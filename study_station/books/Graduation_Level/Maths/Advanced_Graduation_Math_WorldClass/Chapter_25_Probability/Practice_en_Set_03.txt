51. A bag contains 4 red and 6 blue balls. Two balls are drawn at random without replacement. What is the probability that both are red?
(a) 2/15 (b) 4/25 (c) 1/5 (d) 3/10
Answer: (a)
Solution: The tempting wrong option 4/25 comes from assuming replacement, i.e. (4/10)². Without replacement, P = (4/10) × (3/9) = 12/90 = 2/15, so the answer is 2/15.

52. Two dice are thrown together. What is the probability that the sum of the numbers is 9?
(a) 1/6 (b) 5/36 (c) 1/12 (d) 1/9
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is counting only (3,6) and (4,5), forgetting their reverses. Favourable outcomes are (3,6), (6,3), (4,5), (5,4) = 4 out of 36, giving 4/36 = 1/9, so the answer is 1/9.

53. A card is drawn from a well-shuffled deck of 52 cards. What is the probability that it is either a king or a heart?
(a) 17/52 (b) 15/52 (c) 13/52 (d) 4/13
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is adding 4 kings + 13 hearts = 17, which double-counts the king of hearts. By inclusion-exclusion: 4 + 13 − 1 = 16 favourable cards, and 16/52 = 4/13, so the answer is 4/13.

54. The probability that A solves a problem is 1/2 and that B solves it is 1/3. If both try independently, what is the probability that the problem is solved?
(a) 1/6 (b) 5/6 (c) 2/3 (d) 1/2
Answer: (c)
Solution: The tempting wrong option 5/6 comes from adding 1/2 + 1/3, which double-counts the overlap. P(solved) = 1 − P(neither solves) = 1 − (1/2)(2/3) = 1 − 1/3 = 2/3, so the answer is 2/3.

55. Three coins are tossed simultaneously. What is the probability of getting at least two heads?
(a) 3/8 (b) 1/2 (c) 5/8 (d) 7/8
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is counting only exactly two heads (3 cases) and forgetting HHH. Favourable: HHT, HTH, THH, HHH = 4 out of 8, giving 1/2, so the answer is 1/2.

56. A number is chosen at random from the first 100 natural numbers. What is the probability that it is divisible by 4 or 6?
(a) 8/25 (b) 33/100 (c) 37/100 (d) 41/100
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is adding 25 + 16 = 41, ignoring common multiples of 12. Multiples of 4: 25; of 6: 16; of 12: 8. Total = 25 + 16 − 8 = 33, so the answer is 33/100.

57. A speaks truth in 75% of cases and B in 80% of cases. In what percentage of cases are they likely to contradict each other in stating the same fact?
(a) 45% (b) 30% (c) 40% (d) 35%
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is computing agreement instead of contradiction. Contradiction = A true and B false, or A false and B true = (3/4)(1/5) + (1/4)(4/5) = 3/20 + 4/20 = 7/20 = 35%, so the answer is 35%.

Solution: The trap is multiplying fractions like (5/9)(4/8) while ignoring combinations. Favourable = C(5,2) × C(4,1) = 10 × 4 = 40; total = C(9,3) = 84. Probability = 40/84 = 10/21, so the answer is 10/21.

62. A box contains 10 bulbs, of which 3 are defective. Two bulbs are drawn at random without replacement. What is the probability that at least one is defective?
(a) 7/15 (b) 3/10 (c) 8/15 (d) 2/5
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is adding P(first defective) + P(second defective), which double-counts. Use the complement: P(none defective) = (7/10)(6/9) = 42/90 = 7/15; required = 1 − 7/15 = 8/15, so the answer is 8/15.

Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is adding 0.4 + 0.5 = 0.9, ignoring the overlap. P(A ∪ B) = 0.4 + 0.5 − 0.2 = 0.7, so the answer is 0.7.

70. A and B throw a die alternately. The first to throw a 6 wins. If A starts, what is the probability that A wins?
(a) 6/11 (b) 1/2 (c) 5/11 (d) 7/12
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap 1/2 assumes equal chances, ignoring A's first-move advantage. P(A wins) = 1/6 + (5/6)(5/6)(1/6) + … = (1/6)/(1 − 25/36) = (1/6)/(11/36) = 6/11, so the answer is 6/11.

71. Three machines produce 50%, 30% and 20% of items with defect rates 2%, 3% and 5% respectively. An item is found defective. What is the probability it came from the first machine?
(a) 2/29 (b) 1/2 (c) 5/29 (d) 10/29
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap 1/2 assumes the largest producer is most likely, ignoring defect rates. By Bayes' theorem: (0.5 × 0.02)/(0.5 × 0.02 + 0.3 × 0.03 + 0.2 × 0.05) = 0.01/0.029 = 10/29, so the answer is 10/29.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Mathematics

Solution: The trap is checking only small numbers and guessing 1/4. x² ≡ 1 (mod 4) exactly when x is odd; there are 10 odd numbers in 1–20, giving 10/20 = 1/2, so the answer is 1/2.
