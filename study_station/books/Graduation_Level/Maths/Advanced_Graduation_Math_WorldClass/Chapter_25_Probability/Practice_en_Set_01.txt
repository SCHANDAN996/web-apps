1. A fair coin is tossed once. What is the probability of getting a head?
(a) 1/4 (b) 1/3 (c) 1/2 (d) 1
Answer: (c)
Solution: A coin has two equally likely outcomes, head and tail. The tempting wrong option 1/4 wrongly assumes four outcomes, which would apply to two coins, not one. Favourable outcomes = 1, total outcomes = 2, so the answer is 1/2.

2. A fair die is rolled once. What is the probability of getting the number 5?
(a) 1/2 (b) 1/6 (c) 1/3 (d) 5/6
Answer: (b)
Solution: A die has 6 equally likely faces. The option 5/6 is the probability of NOT getting 5, which is the trap here. Only one face shows 5, so the answer is 1/6.

3. A bag contains 3 red and 2 blue balls. One ball is drawn at random. What is the probability that it is red?
(a) 2/5 (b) 3/2 (c) 1/2 (d) 3/5
Answer: (d)
Solution: Total balls = 3 + 2 = 5, red balls = 3. The option 2/5 is the probability of drawing a blue ball, the common trap of picking the wrong colour. Probability of red = 3/5, so the answer is 3/5.

4. What is the probability of an impossible event?
(a) 1 (b) 1/2 (c) 0 (d) Cannot be determined
Answer: (c)
Solution: An impossible event has zero favourable outcomes, so its probability is 0. The option 1 is the probability of a sure event, which is the trap for students who confuse the two extremes. Hence the answer is 0.

5. What is the probability of a sure (certain) event?
(a) 1 (b) 1/2 (c) 0 (d) 2
Answer: (a)
Solution: A sure event includes all possible outcomes, so its probability is 1. The option 0 belongs to an impossible event, the reverse trap. Probability always lies between 0 and 1, so the answer is 1.

Answer: (a)
Solution: There are 4 kings in 52 cards, so probability = 4/52 = 1/13. The option 1/52 is the trap of counting only one specific king (say the king of hearts) instead of all four. Simplifying 4/52, the answer is 1/13.

7. Two coins are tossed simultaneously. What is the probability of getting exactly one head?
(a) 1/4 (b) 1 (c) 3/4 (d) 1/2
Answer: (d)
Solution: The sample space is HH, HT, TH, TT — four outcomes. Exactly one head occurs in HT and TH, i.e., 2 outcomes. The option 1/4 is the trap of counting only HT and forgetting TH. Probability = 2/4, so the answer is 1/2.

9. If P(E) = 0.3, what is P(not E)?
(a) 0.3 (b) 0.7 (c) 1 (d) 0
Answer: (b)
Solution: P(not E) = 1 − P(E) = 1 − 0.3 = 0.7. The option 0.3 is the trap of simply repeating the given value without subtracting from 1. Hence the answer is 0.7.

Answer: (a)
Solution: Three tosses give 2³ = 8 outcomes, and only HHH is all heads. The option 1/4 is the trap of using 2² = 4 outcomes, i.e., counting only two tosses. So the answer is 1/8.

22. A bag contains 4 red, 3 green and 5 blue balls. Two balls are drawn at random without replacement. What is the probability that both are red?
(a) 2/11 (b) 4/33 (c) 1/12 (d) 1/11
Answer: (d)
Solution: Total balls = 12. P(first red) = 4/12 and P(second red | first red) = 3/11, giving 4/12 × 3/11 = 12/132 = 1/11. The trap is using 4/12 × 4/12 (with replacement), which ignores that the first ball is not put back. So the answer is 1/11.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Mathematics

23. If P(A) = 0.5, P(B) = 0.4 and P(A ∩ B) = 0.2, what is P(A ∪ B)?
(a) 0.9 (b) 0.7 (c) 0.6 (d) 0.8
Answer: (b)
Solution: P(A ∪ B) = P(A) + P(B) − P(A ∩ B) = 0.5 + 0.4 − 0.2 = 0.7. The option 0.9 is the trap of forgetting to subtract the intersection, which double-counts the overlap. So the answer is 0.7.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Mathematics

24. Three coins are tossed. What is the probability of getting at least one head?
(a) 7/8 (b) 3/8 (c) 1/2 (d) 5/8
Answer: (a)
Solution: "At least one head" = 1 − P(no head) = 1 − P(TTT) = 1 − 1/8 = 7/8. The option 3/8 is the trap of counting only the outcomes with exactly one head. Using the complement rule, the answer is 7/8.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Mathematics

25. A and B are independent events with P(A) = 0.6 and P(B) = 0.5. What is P(A ∩ B)?
(a) 0.1 (b) 1.1 (c) 0.3 (d) 0.55
Answer: (c)
Solution: For independent events, P(A ∩ B) = P(A) × P(B) = 0.6 × 0.5 = 0.3. The option 1.1 is the trap of adding the probabilities, which is impossible since probability cannot exceed 1. So the answer is 0.3.
