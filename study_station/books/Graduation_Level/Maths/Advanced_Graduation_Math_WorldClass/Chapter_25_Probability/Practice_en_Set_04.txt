76. A bag contains 5 red and 4 blue balls. Two balls are drawn at random without replacement. What is the probability that both are red?
(a) 1/3 (b) 10/36 (c) 5/9 (d) 5/18
Answer: (d)
Solution: The tempting answer 10/36 comes from wrongly treating the draws as independent with replacement. Without replacement, P(both red) = (5/9) × (4/8) = 20/72 = 5/18, so the answer is 5/18.

77. Two dice are thrown together. What is the probability that the sum of the numbers is 9?
(a) 5/36 (b) 1/6 (c) 1/9 (d) 1/12
Answer: (c)
Solution: Favourable outcomes for sum 9 are (3,6), (4,5), (5,4), (6,3), i.e. 4 outcomes out of 36. The trap is counting only 3 pairs by ignoring order. So the answer is 1/9.

78. A card is drawn from a well-shuffled deck of 52 cards. What is the probability that it is either a king or a heart?
(a) 15/52 (b) 17/52 (c) 4/13 (d) 3/13
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is adding 4 kings + 13 hearts = 17, which double-counts the king of hearts. Correct count = 4 + 13 − 1 = 16, so probability = 16/52 = 4/13, so the answer is 4/13.

79. The probability that A solves a problem is 1/2 and that B solves it is 1/3. If both try independently, what is the probability that the problem is solved?
(a) 5/6 (b) 1/6 (c) 2/3 (d) 1/3
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is adding 1/2 + 1/3 = 5/6, which double-counts the overlap. P(solved) = 1 − P(neither solves) = 1 − (1/2)(2/3) = 1 − 1/3 = 2/3, so the answer is 2/3.

80. Three coins are tossed simultaneously. What is the probability of getting at least two heads?
(a) 1/2 (b) 3/8 (c) 5/8 (d) 7/8
Answer: (a)
Solution: Outcomes with at least two heads: HHT, HTH, THH, HHH = 4 out of 8. The trap is counting only exactly two heads (3/8) and forgetting HHH. So the answer is 1/2.

Solution: Multiples of 4: 25; multiples of 6: 16; multiples of 12 (double-counted): 8. Total = 25 + 16 − 8 = 33. The trap is ignoring the overlap of multiples of 12. So the answer is 33/100.

100. Three persons A, B and C are seated randomly in a row. What is the probability that A and B sit together?
(a) 2/3 (b) 1/3 (c) 1/2 (d) 5/6
Answer: (a)
Solution: Total arrangements = 3! = 6. Treat A and B as one unit: 2! × 2! = 4 arrangements. So P = 4/6 = 2/3. The trap 1/3 counts only one internal order of A and B. So the answer is 2/3.
