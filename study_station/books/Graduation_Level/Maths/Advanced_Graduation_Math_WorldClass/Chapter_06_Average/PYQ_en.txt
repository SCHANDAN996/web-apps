# 📊 PYQ Analysis — Average (Advanced Level)

## How Exams Test This Chapter

Average is one of the most reliable scoring areas in UPSC CSAT, SSC CGL Tier-II, IBPS PO Mains and CAT. Examiners rarely ask a plain "find the average" question at this level — they wrap the concept inside a story. The recurring patterns are:

- **Replacement problems** — one person leaves, another joins; average age/weight changes. The trap is forgetting that the *total* changes by (new − old), not by the average change alone.
- **Weighted average** — two groups with different sizes merged. The trap is taking a simple mean of the two averages.
- **Consecutive numbers** — average of consecutive integers/odd/even numbers equals the middle term. The trap is miscounting how many terms exist.
- **Inclusion/exclusion of a wrong value** — a misread number is corrected; the trap is adjusting the average instead of the total.
- **Average speed** — the classic trap: average of speeds is NOT the average speed when distances are equal (use harmonic mean).
- **Statement-based questions** (CSAT/PCS) — "the average of the remaining…", where you must set up one clean equation instead of computing everything.

**Examiner's favourite psychological hooks:**
1. *Almost-correct statements* — e.g., "average increases by 2, so each member's age increases by 2" (false; only the total rises by 2 × n).
2. *Chronology confusion* — average age "3 years ago" vs "3 years hence"; students add/subtract from the wrong base.
3. *Similar-sounding names* — average of first n naturals (n+1)/2 vs average of first n even numbers (n+1).
4. *Negative wording* — "which is NOT possible" questions on weighted averages.

**Time advice:** aim for 45–60 seconds per question. If a question needs more than two equations, you are probably missing the total-sum shortcut.

## Top 10 High-Yield Questions (with Solutions)

**Q1.** The average age of 30 students is 15 years. If the teacher's age is included, the average becomes 16 years. Find the teacher's age.
(a) 44 years (b) 46 years (c) 45 years (d) 48 years
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** Total of students = 30 × 15 = 450. New total = 31 × 16 = 496. Teacher's age = 496 − 450 = 46. *Trap:* students often compute 16 + 15 = 31 or just 16 + 30. Remember: teacher = new average + (old count × increase) = 16 + 30 × 1 = 46.
**Source: PYQ-style (SSC CGL pattern)**

**Q2.** The average weight of 8 persons increases by 2.5 kg when a new person replaces one weighing 65 kg. Find the new person's weight.
(a) 85 kg (b) 80 kg (c) 75 kg (d) 90 kg
**Answer: (a)**
**Solution:** Increase in total = 8 × 2.5 = 20 kg. New person = 65 + 20 = 85 kg. *Trap:* multiplying 2.5 by the wrong count or subtracting 20 instead of adding.
**Source: PYQ-style (Bank PO pattern)**

**Q3.** The average of five consecutive even numbers is 24. Find the largest number.
(a) 28 (b) 26 (c) 30 (d) 32
**Answer: (a)**
**Solution:** For consecutive numbers, average = middle (3rd) term = 24. Largest = 24 + 4 = 28. *Trap:* taking 24 as the first term and adding 8.
**Source: PYQ-style**

**Q4.** The average of 11 results is 50. The average of the first 6 is 49 and that of the last 6 is 52. Find the 6th result.
(a) 56 (b) 55 (c) 54 (d) 58
**Answer: (a)**
**Solution:** 6th result = (6 × 49 + 6 × 52) − 11 × 50 = (294 + 312) − 550 = 56. *Trap:* the 6th result is counted in *both* groups — that overlap is exactly the hook of this classic question.
**Source: PYQ-style (SSC/CSAT pattern)**

**Q5.** A batsman has an average of 40 runs after 20 innings. How many runs must he score in the 21st innings to raise his average to 41?
(a) 60 (b) 61 (c) 62 (d) 59
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** Required = 21 × 41 − 20 × 40 = 861 − 800 = 61. Shortcut: new average + old count × increase = 41 + 20 × 1 = 61. *Trap:* answering 41 + 1 = 42.
**Source: PYQ-style (cricket-average is a perennial favourite)**

**Q6.** A man travels 60 km at 30 km/h and returns the same 60 km at 20 km/h. His average speed for the whole journey is:
(a) 25 km/h (b) 24 km/h (c) 26 km/h (d) 22 km/h
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** Equal distances → harmonic mean = 2 × 30 × 20 / (30 + 20) = 1200/50 = 24 km/h. *Trap:* the arithmetic mean 25 km/h is option (a) — placed there deliberately to catch the hurried.
**Source: PYQ-style (UPSC CSAT pattern)**

**Q7.** The average of 20 numbers is zero. At most how many of them can be greater than zero?
(a) 0 (b) 1 (c) 10 (d) 19
**Answer: (d)**
**Solution:** If 19 numbers are positive, the 20th can be a single negative number balancing their total. But all 20 cannot be positive (sum would exceed zero). So maximum = 19. *Trap:* this is a logic question wearing an average costume — CSAT loves this disguise.
**Source: PYQ-style (CSAT reasoning pattern)**

**Q8.** The average age of a group of 10 members is 25 years. After 5 years, what will be the average age of the same group?
(a) 30 years (b) 28 years (c) 35 years (d) 25 years
**Answer: (a)**
**Solution:** Every member ages 5 years, so the average also rises by exactly 5: 25 + 5 = 30. *Trap:* adding 5 × 10 = 50 to the average. Time shifts the average by the time elapsed — nothing more.
**Source: PYQ-style**

**Q9.** The average marks of 100 students were 40. Later it was found that one score of 83 was misread as 38. Find the correct average.
(a) 40.45 (b) 40.50 (c) 40.25 (d) 41.45
**Answer: (a)**
**Solution:** Correction to total = 83 − 38 = +45. Correct average = 40 + 45/100 = 40.45. *Trap:* adjusting the average by (83 − 38)/83 or similar misplacements; always fix the *total*, then divide.
**Source: PYQ-style (Bank/SSC pattern)**

**Q10.** The average of the first 50 natural numbers is:
(a) 25 (b) 25.5 (c) 26 (d) 24.5
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** Average of first n naturals = (n + 1)/2 = 51/2 = 25.5. *Trap:* confusing with n/2 = 25, or with the average of the first 50 *even* numbers, which is 51. Similar formulas side by side — the examiner's oldest trick.
**Source: NCERT Class 10 Maths (Statistics) / PYQ-style**

## Key Takeaways for the Exam Hall

- Convert every average problem into a **total-sum problem** first; 80% of traps dissolve.
- For replacement/inclusion: **new member = new average ± (old count × change)**.
- For equal-distance speed: **harmonic mean**, never arithmetic.
- For consecutive terms: **average = middle term**; count terms carefully.
- In CSAT logic-type questions, think about *possibility* (max/min), not computation.
- When two groups merge, use the **alligation cross** — it is faster than equations.
