76. The mean of 20 observations is 15. If each observation is increased by 3, the new mean is:
(a) 15 (b) 45 (c) 60 (d) 18
Answer: (d)
Solution: The tempting option is 45, which comes from multiplying 15 by 3 — but adding 3 to each value shifts the mean by exactly 3, not by a factor. New mean = 15 + 3 = 18, so the answer is 18.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Statistics

77. The median of the data 12, 7, 15, 9, 21, 4, 18 is:
(a) 9 (b) 15 (c) 12 (d) 10.5
Answer: (c)
Solution: Many students pick 15 by taking the middle value of the unsorted list — the trap is skipping the sorting step. Arranged in order: 4, 7, 9, 12, 15, 18, 21; the 4th value is 12, so the answer is 12.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Statistics

78. For the data 3, 5, 7, 5, 9, 5, 11, the mode is:
(a) 7 (b) 9 (c) 5 (d) 6.4
Answer: (c)
Solution: Option 6.4 is the mean, a common confusion between averages. The mode is the most frequent value; 5 appears three times, more than any other value, so the answer is 5.

79. If the mean of x, x+2, x+4, x+6, x+8 is 11, then x equals:
(a) 5 (b) 7 (c) 9 (d) 11
Answer: (b)
Solution: Students often set x = 11 directly, forgetting the mean equals the middle term of this arithmetic sequence. Mean = x + 4 = 11, giving x = 7, so the answer is 7.

80. The range of the data 25, 18, 42, 33, 10, 27 is:
(a) 32 (b) 42 (c) 10 (d) 26
Answer: (a)
Solution: Option 42 tempts those who pick the maximum and forget to subtract the minimum. Range = 42 − 10 = 32, so the answer is 32.

81. The variance of the data 2, 4, 6, 8, 10 is:
(a) 6 (b) 40 (c) 8 (d) 10
Answer: (c)
Solution: Option 40 is the sum of squared deviations, the trap of forgetting to divide by n. Mean = 6; squared deviations are 16, 4, 0, 4, 16; variance = 40/5 = 8, so the answer is 8.

82. If the variance of a data set is 16, its standard deviation is:
(a) 8 (b) 4 (c) 256 (d) 2
Answer: (b)
Solution: Option 256 comes from squaring instead of taking the square root — a reversal trap. Standard deviation = √16 = 4, so the answer is 4.

83. The mean of first 10 natural numbers is:
(a) 55 (b) 5 (c) 6 (d) 5.5
Answer: (d)
Solution: Option 55 is the sum, not the mean — the classic trap. Mean = 55/10 = 5.5, so the answer is 5.5.

84. If each value of a data set is multiplied by 4, the standard deviation becomes:
(a) 4 times (b) unchanged (c) 2 times (d) 16 times
Answer: (a)
Solution: Option 16 tempts those who apply the variance rule to standard deviation. Multiplying data by k multiplies the standard deviation by |k| (variance by k²), so the standard deviation becomes 4 times, and the answer is 4 times.

85. The median of first 12 natural numbers is:
(a) 6 (b) 7 (c) 6.5 (d) 13
Answer: (c)
Solution: Option 6 tempts those who take only the 6th term. With an even count, median = average of 6th and 7th terms = (6+7)/2 = 6.5, so the answer is 6.5.

86. For a moderately skewed distribution, if mean = 26 and median = 22, the mode is approximately:
(a) 30 (b) 18 (c) 20 (d) 14
Answer: (d)
Solution: Option 18 comes from wrongly averaging mean and median. Using Mode = 3Median − 2Mean = 66 − 52 = 14, so the answer is 14.

87. The coefficient of variation of a data set with mean 50 and standard deviation 10 is:
(a) 5% (b) 20% (c) 25% (d) 500%
Answer: (b)
Solution: Option 5% results from inverting the ratio (mean divided by standard deviation). CV = (SD/Mean) × 100 = (10/50) × 100 = 20%, so the answer is 20%.

88. The mean deviation about the mean for the data 4, 8, 10, 12, 16 is:
(a) 4 (b) 3.6 (c) 3.2 (d) 16
Answer: (c)
Solution: Option 16 is the sum of absolute deviations — the trap of forgetting to divide by n. Mean = 10; absolute deviations are 6, 2, 0, 2, 6, summing to 16; mean deviation = 16/5 = 3.2, so the answer is 3.2.

89. If the sum of squares of deviations from 10 is minimum for a data set, then 10 is the:
(a) mode (b) mean (c) median (d) range
Answer: (b)
Solution: Median is tempting because it minimises the sum of absolute deviations — but the question says squared deviations. The sum of squared deviations is minimum about the mean, so the answer is mean.

90. The geometric mean of 4 and 16 is:
(a) 6 (b) 10 (c) 12 (d) 8
Answer: (d)
Solution: Option 10 is the arithmetic mean, the usual confusion. GM = √(4 × 16) = √64 = 8, so the answer is 8.

91. The harmonic mean of 2 and 4 is:
(a) 3 (b) 8/3 (c) 2√2 (d) 3.5
Answer: (b)
Solution: Option 3 is the arithmetic mean and 2√2 the geometric mean — both are traps. HM = 2/(1/2 + 1/4) = 2/(3/4) = 8/3, so the answer is 8/3.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Statistics

92. For any two positive numbers, which relation always holds?
(a) AM ≥ GM ≥ HM (b) GM ≥ AM ≥ HM (c) AM ≤ GM ≤ HM (d) HM ≥ GM ≥ AM
Answer: (a)
Solution: The reversed inequality in the first option is the classic trap. The standard result is AM ≥ GM ≥ HM, with equality only when all numbers are equal, so the answer is AM ≥ GM ≥ HM.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Statistics

93. In a frequency distribution, the class mark of the class 20–30 is:
(a) 25 (b) 10 (c) 30 (d) 20
Answer: (a)
Solution: Option 10 is the class width, not the class mark. Class mark = (20 + 30)/2 = 25, so the answer is 25.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Statistics

94. The mode of a grouped distribution is calculated using the class with:
(a) the smallest frequency (b) the middle frequency (c) zero frequency (d) the highest frequency
Answer: (d)
Solution: The middle frequency belongs to the median class, a common mix-up. The modal class is the one with the highest frequency, so the answer is the highest frequency.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Statistics

95. If the mean of 5 observations is 20 and one observation 24 is removed, the mean of the remaining observations is:

96. The variance of the data 5, 5, 5, 5, 5 is:
(a) 0 (b) 25 (c) 5 (d) 1
Answer: (a)
Solution: Option 5 tempts those who confuse the constant value with spread. All values are identical, so there is no dispersion; variance = 0, so the answer is 0.

97. If the standard deviation of n observations is 6 and each observation is decreased by 4, the new standard deviation is:
(a) 6 (b) 2 (c) 10 (d) 24
Answer: (a)
Solution: Option 2 tempts those who subtract 4 from the standard deviation too. Adding or subtracting a constant does not change dispersion, so the standard deviation stays 6, and the answer is 6.

98. The median of the data 5, 8, 3, 9, 12, 7 is:
(a) 7 (b) 8 (c) 7.5 (d) 6.5
Answer: (c)
Solution: Option 7 tempts those who forget the even-count rule and just pick a middle value. Sorted: 3, 5, 7, 8, 9, 12; median = (7 + 8)/2 = 7.5, so the answer is 7.5.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Statistics

99. For a data set, the sum of deviations of all values from the mean is always:
(a) positive (b) negative (c) equal to the mean (d) zero
Answer: (d)
Solution: "Positive" is tempting because deviations sound like magnitudes — but signed deviations cancel out. By definition of the mean, the sum of deviations from it is always zero, so the answer is zero.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Statistics

100. The mean of 100 observations is 40. Later it was found that one observation was wrongly taken as 30 instead of 50. The correct mean is:
(a) 41 (b) 40.2 (c) 39.8 (d) 40.5
Answer: (b)
Solution: Option 41 tempts those who add the full difference of 20 to the mean instead of spreading it over 100 observations. Corrected sum = 4000 − 30 + 50 = 4020; correct mean = 4020/100 = 40.2, so the answer is 40.2.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Statistics
