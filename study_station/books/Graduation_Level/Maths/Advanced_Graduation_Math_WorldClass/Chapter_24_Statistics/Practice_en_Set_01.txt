1. The arithmetic mean of the first 10 natural numbers is:
(a) 5.5 (b) 5 (c) 6 (d) 4.5
Answer: (a)
Solution: The tempting wrong option 5 comes from taking the middle of 1 and 10 without averaging. Sum of first n natural numbers is n(n+1)/2 = 10×11/2 = 55, so mean = 55/10 = 5.5, so the answer is 5.5.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Mathematics (Statistics)

2. The median of the data set 3, 7, 5, 9, 1 is:
(a) 3 (b) 5 (c) 7 (d) 9
Answer: (b)
Solution: The tempting wrong option 7 comes from picking the middle value without arranging the data. Arranging in ascending order: 1, 3, 5, 7, 9; the middle (3rd) value is 5, so the answer is 5.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Mathematics (Statistics)

3. The mode of the data 2, 3, 4, 3, 5, 3, 6 is:
(a) 2 (b) 3 (c) 5 (d) 4
Answer: (b)
Solution: The tempting wrong option 4 appears only once but sits in the middle of the list. The mode is the most frequent value; 3 occurs three times, more than any other value, so the answer is 3.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Mathematics (Statistics)

4. The range of the data set 12, 25, 7, 18, 30 is:
(a) 18 (b) 25 (c) 23 (d) 30
Answer: (c)
Solution: The tempting wrong option 30 is just the maximum value, not the range. Range = maximum − minimum = 30 − 7 = 23, so the answer is 23.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Mathematics (Statistics)

5. If the mean of 5 observations is 20, the sum of the observations is:
(a) 4 (b) 25 (c) 15 (d) 100
Answer: (d)
Solution: The tempting wrong option 4 comes from dividing instead of multiplying. Sum = mean × number of observations = 20 × 5 = 100, so the answer is 100.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Mathematics (Statistics)

6. The median of an even number of observations is:
(a) the middle observation (b) the mean of all observations (c) the largest observation (d) the mean of the two middle observations
Answer: (d)
Solution: The tempting wrong option "the middle observation" applies only when the count is odd. For an even count there is no single middle value, so we average the two central values after arranging in order, so the answer is the mean of the two middle observations.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Mathematics (Statistics)

7. The mean of the data 4, 8, 12, 16, 20 is:
(a) 10 (b) 14 (c) 12 (d) 16
Answer: (c)
Solution: The tempting wrong option 16 is the second-largest value, often picked by hasty reading. Sum = 4+8+12+16+20 = 60; mean = 60/5 = 12, so the answer is 12.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Mathematics (Statistics)

8. Which measure of central tendency is most affected by extreme values?
(a) Median (b) Mode (c) Mean (d) Range is not a measure of central tendency; the correct choice among these is Mean
Answer: (c)
Solution: The tempting wrong option Median feels central but it depends only on position, not magnitude. The mean uses every value, so one very large or very small observation pulls it strongly, so the answer is Mean.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Mathematics (Statistics)

9. The mode of the data 5, 5, 7, 7, 9 is:
(a) Both 5 and 7 (b) 7 (c) 5 (d) 9
Answer: (a)
Solution: The tempting wrong option 5 appears first, but frequency must be compared, not order. Both 5 and 7 occur twice, the highest frequency, making the data bimodal, so the answer is Both 5 and 7.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Mathematics (Statistics)

10. If each observation of a data set is increased by 3, the mean:
(a) remains the same (b) increases by 3 (c) increases by 3 times (d) decreases by 3
Answer: (b)
Solution: The tempting wrong option "increases by 3 times" confuses addition with multiplication. Adding a constant k to every value raises the mean by exactly k, so the mean increases by 3, so the answer is increases by 3.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Mathematics (Statistics)

11. The median of the data 10, 20, 30, 40, 50, 60 is:
(a) 35 (b) 30 (c) 40 (d) 25
Answer: (a)
Solution: The tempting wrong option 30 is the third value, but with 6 observations there is no single middle. Median = mean of 3rd and 4th values = (30+40)/2 = 35, so the answer is 35.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Mathematics (Statistics)

12. The mean of first 5 even natural numbers is:
(a) 5 (b) 8 (c) 7 (d) 6
Answer: (d)
Solution: The tempting wrong option 5 comes from confusing even numbers with the first 5 natural numbers. First 5 even numbers are 2, 4, 6, 8, 10; sum = 30; mean = 30/5 = 6, so the answer is 6.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Mathematics (Statistics)

13. In a frequency distribution, the class mark of the class 10–20 is:
(a) 10 (b) 15 (c) 20 (d) 30
Answer: (b)
Solution: The tempting wrong option 20 is just the upper limit. Class mark = (lower limit + upper limit)/2 = (10+20)/2 = 15, so the answer is 15.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Mathematics (Statistics)

14. The sum of deviations of observations from their arithmetic mean is always:
(a) positive (b) negative (c) zero (d) one
Answer: (c)
Solution: The tempting wrong option "positive" seems natural since deviations look like distances, but deviations carry signs. By definition the mean balances the data, so positive and negative deviations cancel exactly, so the answer is zero.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Mathematics (Statistics)

15. If the mean of x, x+2, x+4, x+6, x+8 is 11, then x equals:
(a) 5 (b) 6 (c) 7 (d) 8
Answer: (c)
Solution: The tempting wrong option 8 comes from subtracting the wrong total from 55. Sum = 5x + 20 = 55, so 5x = 35 and x = 7, so the answer is 7.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Mathematics (Statistics)

16. The empirical relationship among mean, median and mode is:
(a) Mode = 3 Median − 2 Mean (b) Mode = 2 Median − 3 Mean (c) Mode = 3 Mean − 2 Median (d) Mode = Mean + Median
Answer: (a)
Solution: The tempting wrong option "Mode = 3 Mean − 2 Median" swaps the positions of mean and median, a classic confusion. The verified empirical relation is Mode = 3 Median − 2 Mean, so the answer is Mode = 3 Median − 2 Mean.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Mathematics (Statistics)

17. The variance of the data 2, 4, 6, 8, 10 is:
(a) 6 (b) 10 (c) 8 (d) 4
Answer: (c)
Solution: The tempting wrong option 4 is the standard deviation, not the variance — a very common slip. Mean = 6; squared deviations are 16, 4, 0, 4, 16; variance = 40/5 = 8, so the answer is 8.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Mathematics (Statistics)

18. The standard deviation is the:
(a) square of variance (b) positive square root of variance (c) half of variance (d) twice the variance
Answer: (b)
Solution: The tempting wrong option "square of variance" reverses the relationship. Standard deviation is defined as the positive square root of the variance, bringing units back to the original scale, so the answer is positive square root of variance.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Mathematics (Statistics)

19. If every observation of a data set is multiplied by 2, the standard deviation becomes:
(a) unchanged (b) doubled (c) half (d) four times
Answer: (b)
Solution: The tempting wrong option "four times" applies to variance, not standard deviation. Multiplying data by k multiplies the standard deviation by |k|, so it doubles, so the answer is doubled.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Mathematics (Statistics)

20. The mean of the squares of the first 5 natural numbers is:
(a) 9 (b) 10 (c) 12 (d) 11
Answer: (d)
Solution: The tempting wrong option 9 is the square of the mean of 1–5, not the mean of squares. Squares are 1, 4, 9, 16, 25; sum = 55; mean = 55/5 = 11, so the answer is 11.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Mathematics (Statistics)

21. The mean of 100 observations is 50. Later it was found that one observation was misread as 83 instead of 53. The correct mean is:
(a) 49.7 (b) 50.3 (c) 49.5 (d) 50.5
Answer: (a)
Solution: The tempting wrong option 50.3 comes from adding the difference instead of subtracting it. Wrong sum = 5000; correct sum = 5000 − 83 + 53 = 4970; correct mean = 4970/100 = 49.7, so the answer is 49.7.
Source: PYQ-style (SSC CGL pattern)

22. The median of the data 24, 36, 46, 17, 18, 25, 35 is:
(a) 24 (b) 36 (c) 35 (d) 25
Answer: (d)
Solution: The tempting wrong option 35 is the middle value of the unarranged list, a classic trap. Arranged: 17, 18, 24, 25, 35, 36, 46; the 4th value is 25, so the answer is 25.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Mathematics (Statistics)

23. The mean of 5 numbers is 18. If one number is excluded, the mean becomes 16. The excluded number is:
(a) 18 (b) 20 (c) 24 (d) 26
Answer: (d)
Solution: The tempting wrong option 20 comes from averaging the two means, which has no basis. Total of 5 numbers = 90; total of remaining 4 = 64; excluded number = 90 − 64 = 26, so the answer is 26.
Source: PYQ-style (Bank exam pattern)

24. For a moderately skewed distribution, if Mean = 20 and Mode = 26, the Median is:
(a) 22 (b) 21 (c) 23 (d) 24
Answer: (a)
Solution: The tempting wrong option 24 comes from wrongly averaging mode and mean directly. Using Mode = 3 Median − 2 Mean: 26 = 3M − 40, so 3M = 66 and M = 22, so the answer is 22.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Mathematics (Statistics)

25. The variance of a data set is 25. If each observation is increased by 5, the new variance is:
(a) 25 (b) 50 (c) 30 (d) 625
Answer: (a)
Solution: The tempting wrong option 30 comes from adding 5 to the variance, but adding a constant shifts all values equally without changing their spread. Variance is unchanged by addition or subtraction of a constant, so the answer is 25.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Mathematics (Statistics)
