101. The LCM of two numbers is 495 and their HCF is 5. If one number is 45, find the other number.
(a) 55 (b) 65 (c) 60 (d) 50
Answer: (a)
Solution: The tempting trap is to divide 495 by 45 and forget the HCF, which gives 11. Use the rule: product of two numbers = LCM × HCF. Other number = (495 × 5) ÷ 45 = 2475 ÷ 45 = 55, so the answer is 55.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Mathematics

102. Find the least number which when divided by 12, 15, 20 and 27 leaves a remainder of 7 in each case.
(a) 540 (b) 553 (c) 527 (d) 547
Answer: (d)
Solution: Many students stop at LCM(12, 15, 20, 27) = 540 and pick it, but the remainder 7 must be added. Required number = 540 + 7 = 547, so the answer is 547.
Source: PYQ-style

103. The HCF of two numbers is 11 and their LCM is 7700. If one of the numbers is 275, the other number is:
(a) 279 (b) 283 (c) 318 (d) 308
Answer: (d)
Solution: A common slip is dividing 7700 by 275 (= 28) and ignoring the HCF. Other number = (11 × 7700) ÷ 275 = 84700 ÷ 275 = 308, so the answer is 308.
Source: PYQ-style

104. Three bells ring at intervals of 18, 24 and 32 minutes. If they ring together at 10 a.m., when will they next ring together?
(a) 2:48 p.m. (b) 3:12 p.m. (c) 2:24 p.m. (d) 3:36 p.m.
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is taking the HCF instead of the LCM of the intervals. LCM(18, 24, 32) = 288 minutes = 4 hours 48 minutes, and 10 a.m. + 4 h 48 min = 2:48 p.m., so the answer is 2:48 p.m.
Source: PYQ-style

105. The ratio of two numbers is 3 : 4 and their HCF is 4. Their LCM is:
(a) 48 (b) 16 (c) 12 (d) 24
Answer: (a)
Solution: The numbers are 3 × 4 = 12 and 4 × 4 = 16. The trap is taking the LCM of 3 and 4 (= 12) directly and forgetting the HCF multiplier. LCM(12, 16) = 48, so the answer is 48.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Mathematics

106. Find the greatest number that divides 148 and 246 leaving remainders 4 and 6 respectively.
(a) 24 (b) 16 (c) 12 (d) 48
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is taking the HCF of 148 and 246 directly, which is only 2. Subtract the remainders first: 148 − 4 = 144 and 246 − 6 = 240, and HCF(144, 240) = 48, which exceeds both remainders so it is valid. So the answer is 48.
Source: PYQ-style

107. The LCM of two numbers is 48 and the numbers are in the ratio 2 : 3. The sum of the numbers is:
(a) 28 (b) 40 (c) 32 (d) 64
Answer: (b)
Solution: Let the numbers be 2x and 3x; their LCM is 6x = 48, so x = 8. The trap is reporting x = 8 or just the larger number 24. The numbers are 16 and 24, and their sum is 40, so the answer is 40.
Source: PYQ-style

108. Find the smallest number which when increased by 5 is exactly divisible by 16, 24, 36 and 54.
(a) 427 (b) 432 (c) 437 (d) 869
Answer: (a)
Solution: LCM(16, 24, 36, 54) = 432. The trap is answering 432 itself, but the number must be 5 less than a multiple of the LCM. Required number = 432 − 5 = 427, so the answer is 427.
Source: PYQ-style

109. The product of two numbers is 2028 and their HCF is 13. The number of such pairs is:
(a) 1 (b) 3 (c) 2 (d) 4
Answer: (c)
Solution: Let the numbers be 13a and 13b with a, b coprime. Then 169ab = 2028, so ab = 12. The coprime pairs are (1, 12) and (3, 4) — the trap is counting (2, 6), which is not coprime. So the answer is 2.
Source: PYQ-style

110. Six bells toll at intervals of 2, 4, 6, 8, 10 and 12 seconds. In 30 minutes, how many times do they toll together (excluding the start)?
(a) 4 (b) 10 (c) 16 (d) 15
Answer: (d)
Solution: LCM(2, 4, 6, 8, 10, 12) = 120 seconds = 2 minutes. The trap is counting the starting toll and answering 16. In 30 minutes they toll together 30 ÷ 2 = 15 times after the start, so the answer is 15.
Source: PYQ-style

111. The HCF and LCM of two numbers are 21 and 4641 respectively. If one number lies between 200 and 300, that number is:
(a) 231 (b) 273 (c) 147 (d) 441
Answer: (b)
Solution: The numbers must be 21a and 21b with coprime a, b and ab = 4641 ÷ 21 = 221 = 13 × 17. The coprime pairs are (1, 221) and (13, 17), giving the numbers (21, 4641) or (273, 357); the trap is picking 147 = 21 × 7, but 7 does not divide 221. Only 273 lies between 200 and 300, so the answer is 273.
Source: PYQ-style

112. Find the greatest number of four digits which is divisible by 15, 25, 40 and 75.
(a) 9000 (b) 9600 (c) 9800 (d) 9975
Answer: (b)
Solution: LCM(15, 25, 40, 75) = 600. The trap is taking 9999 and rounding wrongly. Largest four-digit multiple of 600: 9999 ÷ 600 = 16.66, so 16 × 600 = 9600, so the answer is 9600.
Source: PYQ-style

113. The sum of two numbers is 216 and their HCF is 27. How many such pairs of numbers exist?
(a) 4 (b) 3 (c) 2 (d) 6
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is counting every pair summing to 8. With 27a + 27b = 216, we get a + b = 8 with a, b coprime; only (1, 7) and (3, 5) qualify, since (2, 6) and (4, 4) share common factors. So the answer is 2.
Source: PYQ-style

114. Find the least number which when divided by 5, 6, 7 and 8 leaves remainder 3 in each case but is divisible by 9.
(a) 843 (b) 2523 (c) 1683 (d) 3363
Answer: (c)
Solution: LCM(5, 6, 7, 8) = 840, so candidates are 840k + 3: 843, 1683, 2523… The trap is picking 843, but 8 + 4 + 3 = 15, not divisible by 9. For 1683, the digit sum is 1 + 6 + 8 + 3 = 18, divisible by 9, so the answer is 1683.
Source: PYQ-style

115. The LCM of two numbers is 20 times their HCF. The sum of HCF and LCM is 2520. If one number is 480, the other is:
(a) 120 (b) 400 (c) 600 (d) 800
Answer: (c)
Solution: Let HCF = h; then LCM = 20h and 21h = 2520, so h = 120 and LCM = 2400. The trap is using 2520 directly as the LCM. Other number = (120 × 2400) ÷ 480 = 600, so the answer is 600.
Source: PYQ-style

116. A rectangular courtyard 3.78 m long and 5.25 m wide is to be paved with square tiles of the same size. The minimum number of tiles required is:
(a) 430 (b) 440 (c) 450 (d) 460
Answer: (c)
Solution: Convert to cm: 378 and 525. The trap is using the LCM or adding the dimensions; for the largest square tile use HCF(378, 525) = 21 cm. Number of tiles = (378 × 525) ÷ (21 × 21) = 18 × 25 = 450, so the answer is 450.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Mathematics

117. The HCF of two numbers is 8. Which of the following can never be their LCM?
(a) 48 (b) 60 (c) 56 (d) 24
Answer: (b)
Solution: The LCM must always be a multiple of the HCF. The trap is checking divisibility among the numbers instead of against the HCF. 24, 48 and 56 are multiples of 8, but 60 ÷ 8 = 7.5, so the answer is 60.
Source: PYQ-style

118. Find the greatest number which on dividing 1657 and 2037 leaves remainders 6 and 5 respectively.
(a) 123 (b) 305 (c) 235 (d) 127
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is computing the HCF of 1657 and 2037 directly. Subtract the remainders: 1657 − 6 = 1651 and 2037 − 5 = 2032; then HCF(1651, 2032) = 127, since 2032 − 1651 = 381, 1651 = 381 × 4 + 127 and 381 = 127 × 3. So the answer is 127.
Source: PYQ-style

119. Three numbers are in the ratio 1 : 2 : 3 and their HCF is 12. The numbers are:
(a) 12, 24, 48 (b) 4, 8, 12 (c) 12, 24, 36 (d) 24, 48, 72
Answer: (c)
Solution: The numbers are 12 × 1, 12 × 2, 12 × 3 = 12, 24, 36. The trap is the set 24, 48, 72, whose HCF is 24, not 12. So the answer is 12, 24, 36.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Mathematics

120. The product of the HCF and LCM of two numbers is 9072. If one number is 72, the other number is:
(a) 126 (b) 108 (c) 144 (d) 168
Answer: (a)
Solution: Product of the numbers = HCF × LCM = 9072. The trap is hunting for the HCF and LCM separately. Other number = 9072 ÷ 72 = 126, and indeed HCF(72, 126) = 18 with LCM = 504 gives 18 × 504 = 9072, so the answer is 126.
Source: PYQ-style

121. Find the least number which when divided by 16, 18, 20 and 25 leaves remainder 4 in each case, but when divided by 7 leaves no remainder.
(a) 18004 (b) 7204 (c) 14404 (d) 3604
Answer: (a)
Solution: LCM(16, 18, 20, 25) = 3600, so the number is 3600k + 4. The trap is picking 3604 (k = 1), but 3604 ÷ 7 leaves remainder 1. We need 3600k + 4 divisible by 7; since 3600 ≡ 2 (mod 7), we need 2k + 4 ≡ 0, i.e. k ≡ 5 (mod 7), so k = 5 gives 18004 = 7 × 2572. So the answer is 18004.
Source: PYQ-style

122. The LCM of three different numbers is 120. Which of the following cannot be their HCF?
(a) 12 (b) 35 (c) 15 (d) 8
Answer: (b)
Solution: The HCF must always divide the LCM. The trap is checking the options carelessly. 8, 12 and 15 all divide 120, but 35 does not divide 120, so the answer is 35.
Source: PYQ-style

123. Four runners start together at the same point on a 1200 m circular track and run at 200, 300, 400 and 150 metres per minute. After how much time will all four first be together again at the starting point?
(a) 12 min (b) 36 min (c) 60 min (d) 24 min
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is taking the LCM of the speeds instead of the lap times. Lap times are 1200/200 = 6, 1200/300 = 4, 1200/400 = 3 and 1200/150 = 8 minutes, and LCM(6, 4, 3, 8) = 24 minutes. So the answer is 24 min.
Source: PYQ-style

124. Let N be the greatest number that divides 1305, 4665 and 6905 leaving the same remainder in each case. The sum of the digits of N is:
(a) 7 (b) 4 (c) 6 (d) 5
Answer: (b)
Solution: N divides the differences: 4665 − 1305 = 3360 and 6905 − 4665 = 2240. The trap is taking the HCF of the original numbers. HCF(3360, 2240) = 1120, and the digit sum of 1120 is 1 + 1 + 2 + 0 = 4, so the answer is 4.
Source: PYQ-style

125. The HCF of two numbers is 23 and the sum of the two numbers is 276. How many pairs of such numbers are possible?
(a) 2 (b) 3 (c) 4 (d) 6
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is counting every pair summing to 12. With 23a + 23b = 276, we get a + b = 12 with a, b coprime; only (1, 11) and (5, 7) qualify, since (2, 10), (3, 9), (4, 8) and (6, 6) share common factors. So the answer is 2.
Source: PYQ-style
