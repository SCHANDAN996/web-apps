1. What is the HCF of 12 and 18?
(a) 3 (b) 36 (c) 9 (d) 6
Answer: (d)
Solution: The factors of 12 are 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 12 and of 18 are 1, 2, 3, 6, 9, 18. The tempting wrong option 36 is actually the LCM, not the HCF. The greatest common factor is 6, so the answer is 6.
Source: NCERT Class 6 Mathematics

2. What is the LCM of 4 and 6?
(a) 24 (b) 12 (c) 10 (d) 2
Answer: (b)
Solution: Multiples of 4 are 4, 8, 12, 16… and of 6 are 6, 12, 18…. The trap option 24 is the product of 4 and 6, which works only when numbers are coprime. The least common multiple is 12, so the answer is 12.
Source: NCERT Class 6 Mathematics

3. The HCF of two coprime numbers is:
(a) 0 (b) their product (c) 1 (d) their sum
Answer: (c)
Solution: Coprime numbers share no common factor other than 1. The trap is choosing their product, which is their LCM, not HCF. By definition the HCF of coprime numbers is 1, so the answer is 1.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Mathematics

4. What is the LCM of 5 and 7?
(a) 35 (b) 1 (c) 12 (d) 70
Answer: (a)
Solution: Since 5 and 7 are coprime, their LCM equals their product, 5 × 7 = 35. The trap option 70 is double the LCM, a common slip when multiplying by 2 unnecessarily. So the answer is 35.
Source: NCERT Class 6 Mathematics

5. What is the HCF of 24 and 36?
(a) 6 (b) 72 (c) 18 (d) 12
Answer: (d)
Solution: 24 = 2³ × 3 and 36 = 2² × 3². HCF takes the lowest powers: 2² × 3 = 12. The trap option 72 is the LCM, not the HCF. So the answer is 12.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Mathematics

6. The product of two numbers is 180 and their HCF is 6. Their LCM is:
(a) 30 (b) 36 (c) 1080 (d) 60
Answer: (a)
Solution: LCM × HCF = product of numbers, so LCM = 180 ÷ 6 = 30. The trap option 1080 comes from multiplying 180 by 6 instead of dividing. So the answer is 30.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Mathematics

7. What is the LCM of 8 and 12?
(a) 4 (b) 96 (c) 24 (d) 48
Answer: (c)
Solution: 8 = 2³ and 12 = 2² × 3. LCM takes highest powers: 2³ × 3 = 24. The trap option 96 is the product 8 × 12, valid only for coprime numbers. So the answer is 24.
Source: NCERT Class 6 Mathematics

8. The HCF of 15, 25 and 35 is:
(a) 1 (b) 15 (c) 5 (d) 525
Answer: (c)
Solution: 15 = 3 × 5, 25 = 5², 35 = 5 × 7. The only common factor is 5. The trap option 15 is a factor of only one number, not all three. So the answer is 5.
Source: NCERT Class 6 Mathematics

9. Which of the following is the LCM of 2, 3 and 4?
(a) 6 (b) 12 (c) 24 (d) 8
Answer: (b)
Solution: LCM of 2, 3, 4 = 2² × 3 = 12. The trap option 24 is the product of all three, ignoring that 4 already contains 2. So the answer is 12.
Source: NCERT Class 6 Mathematics

10. If HCF of two numbers is 1, the numbers are called:
(a) coprime (b) composite (c) prime (d) twin primes
Answer: (a)
Solution: Numbers whose HCF is 1 are called coprime (relatively prime); they need not themselves be prime, e.g. 8 and 9. The trap option "prime" confuses coprime pairs with prime numbers. So the answer is coprime.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Mathematics

11. What is the HCF of 7 and 13?
(a) 7 (b) 13 (c) 1 (d) 91
Answer: (c)
Solution: Both 7 and 13 are prime and distinct, so they share no factor except 1. The trap option 91 is their product, which is the LCM. So the answer is 1.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Mathematics

12. The LCM of 10 and 15 is:
(a) 5 (b) 25 (c) 150 (d) 30
Answer: (d)
Solution: 10 = 2 × 5 and 15 = 3 × 5, so LCM = 2 × 3 × 5 = 30. The trap option 5 is the HCF, not the LCM. So the answer is 30.
Source: NCERT Class 6 Mathematics

13. The smallest number exactly divisible by 6, 8 and 12 is:
(a) 48 (b) 24 (c) 36 (d) 96
Answer: (b)
Solution: 6 = 2 × 3, 8 = 2³, 12 = 2² × 3; LCM = 2³ × 3 = 24. The trap option 48 is a common multiple but not the least. So the answer is 24.
Source: NCERT Class 6 Mathematics

14. The greatest number that divides 28 and 42 exactly is:
(a) 14 (b) 7 (c) 21 (d) 84
Answer: (a)
Solution: 28 = 2² × 7 and 42 = 2 × 3 × 7; HCF = 2 × 7 = 14. The trap option 84 is the LCM, which is a multiple, not a divisor. So the answer is 14.
Source: NCERT Class 6 Mathematics

15. LCM × HCF of two numbers equals:
(a) sum of the numbers (b) difference of the numbers (c) product of the numbers (d) square of the HCF
Answer: (c)
Solution: For any two numbers, LCM × HCF = product of the two numbers. The trap options sum and difference have no such fixed relation. So the answer is product of the numbers.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Mathematics

16. What is the HCF of 0 and 6?
(a) 6 (b) 0 (c) 1 (d) not defined
Answer: (a)
Solution: Every number divides 0, so the common divisors of 0 and 6 are the divisors of 6; the greatest is 6. The trap option "not defined" applies to HCF of 0 and 0, not 0 and 6. So the answer is 6.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Mathematics

17. The LCM of 9 and 12 is:
(a) 3 (b) 108 (c) 18 (d) 36
Answer: (d)
Solution: 9 = 3² and 12 = 2² × 3; LCM = 2² × 3² = 36. The trap option 108 is the product 9 × 12, wrong because the numbers are not coprime. So the answer is 36.
Source: NCERT Class 6 Mathematics

18. Which pair of numbers is coprime?
(a) 8 and 15 (b) 14 and 21 (c) 12 and 18 (d) 9 and 27
Answer: (a)
Solution: 8 = 2³ and 15 = 3 × 5 share no common factor, so HCF = 1. The other pairs share factors 7, 6 and 9 respectively, which is the trap. So the answer is 8 and 15.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Mathematics

19. The HCF of 16, 24 and 40 is:
(a) 4 (b) 16 (c) 2 (d) 8
Answer: (d)
Solution: 16 = 2⁴, 24 = 2³ × 3, 40 = 2³ × 5; common lowest power is 2³ = 8. The trap option 4 divides all three but is not the greatest such divisor. So the answer is 8.
Source: NCERT Class 6 Mathematics

20. The LCM of 1 and any number n is:
(a) n (b) 1 (c) 2n (d) n²
Answer: (a)
Solution: Every number is a multiple of 1, so the least common multiple of 1 and n is n itself. The trap option 1 confuses LCM with HCF. So the answer is n.
Source: NCERT Class 6 Mathematics

21. The HCF of two numbers is 11 and their LCM is 770. If one number is 77, the other is:
(a) 70 (b) 121 (c) 110 (d) 100
Answer: (c)
Solution: Product of numbers = HCF × LCM = 11 × 770 = 8470. Other number = 8470 ÷ 77 = 110. The trap option 121 comes from wrongly adding or squaring 11 instead of using the product formula. So the answer is 110.
Source: PYQ-style (SSC pattern)

22. The smallest number which when divided by 4, 6 and 8 leaves remainder 2 in each case is:
(a) 48 (b) 24 (c) 50 (d) 26
Answer: (d)
Solution: LCM of 4, 6, 8 = 24; required number = 24 + 2 = 26. The trap option 24 forgets to add the common remainder 2. So the answer is 26.
Source: PYQ-style (SSC CGL pattern)

23. Three bells ring at intervals of 12, 15 and 20 minutes. If they ring together now, after how many minutes will they ring together again?
(a) 30 (b) 60 (c) 120 (d) 240
Answer: (b)
Solution: LCM of 12, 15, 20 = 2² × 3 × 5 = 60 minutes. The trap option 120 is a common multiple but double the least one. So the answer is 60.
Source: PYQ-style (Railway/SSC pattern)

24. The greatest number of four digits which is divisible by 15, 25 and 40 is:
(a) 9800 (b) 9600 (c) 9900 (d) 9990
Answer: (b)
Solution: LCM of 15, 25, 40 = 600. Largest 4-digit number 9999 ÷ 600 gives quotient 16, so 600 × 16 = 9600. The trap option 9990 is divisible by 15 but not by 40. So the answer is 9600.
Source: PYQ-style (SSC CGL pattern)

25. The ratio of two numbers is 3 : 4 and their HCF is 4. Their LCM is:
(a) 12 (b) 48 (c) 24 (d) 36
Answer: (b)
Solution: Numbers are 3 × 4 = 12 and 4 × 4 = 16. LCM of 12 and 16 = 48. The trap option 12 is just the smaller number, not the LCM. So the answer is 48.
Source: PYQ-style (Bank exam pattern)
