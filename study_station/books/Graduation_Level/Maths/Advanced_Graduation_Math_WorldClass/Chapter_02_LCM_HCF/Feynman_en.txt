The Story of the Two Train Tracks and the Universal Measuring Stick

Imagine you have two very long train sets running side by side.
Track A has stations spaced every 12 meters apart.
Track B has stations spaced every 18 meters apart.

Both tracks start at the exact same station: Station 0.
As the trains chug forward, the passengers wonder: "At which station will both trains stop together next?"
Track A stops at: 12, 24, 36, 48, 60, 72...
Track B stops at: 18, 36, 54, 72...
Look at that! Both trains stop together at 36 meters, then at 72 meters!
The VERY FIRST place they meet is 36 meters.
That first common station is the Least Common Multiple (LCM). LCM is the future meetup point of different cycles.

Now imagine a totally different problem. You are a carpenter.
You have two wooden boards: one is 12 meters long, and the other is 18 meters long.
You want to cut BOTH boards into smaller equal pieces with ZERO wood wasted.
What is the LONGEST wooden ruler you can use to measure both boards?
- A 1-meter ruler works.
- A 2-meter ruler works (12 = 6*2, 18 = 9*2).
- A 3-meter ruler works (12 = 4*3, 18 = 6*3).
- A 6-meter ruler works (12 = 2*6, 18 = 3*6).
Can you use a 9-meter ruler? It cuts 18, but it ruins 12!
So the biggest possible ruler is 6 meters.
That longest universal ruler is the Highest Common Factor (HCF). HCF is the biggest common building block!

The mind-blowing connection between them:
Multiply the boards: 12 * 18 = 216.
Multiply the HCF and LCM: 6 * 36 = 216!
They are exact mirrors of each other: The biggest common ruler multiplied by the first common meeting point equals the product of the two original numbers!
