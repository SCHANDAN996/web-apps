# Number Series & Logic

## Chapter Outline & Learning Goals

By the end of this chapter, you will be able to:

1. Identify the hidden rule in any number series within seconds — difference-based, ratio-based, alternating, or mixed.
2. Solve missing-term and wrong-term series questions at Graduation level (SSC CGL Tier-II, IBPS PO Mains, CAT, CSAT).
3. Handle letter series and alphanumeric series using position logic.
4. Apply logical reasoning tools — syllogisms, coding-decoding, direction sense, blood relations — that share the same "pattern-hunting" brain muscle.
5. Recognise the traps examiners set: near-correct options, double patterns, and misleading first differences.

---

## The Hook: The Candidate Who Beat the Clock

Picture two candidates in a bank PO exam, facing the same 5-question series set. One starts subtracting terms, scribbling rough work, and is still on the first series after two minutes. The other finishes all five in about 90 seconds. Her secret? She never "calculated" the series. She **classified** it first.

That is the entire philosophy of this chapter: **Don't solve. Classify first, then solve.**

Every number series asked in Indian competitive exams belongs to one of about **8 families**. Once you can spot the family in 5 seconds, the answer takes another 10. The toppers are not faster calculators — they are faster classifiers.

## Why Students Struggle (And Why You Won't)

Most aspirants open a series question and immediately start subtracting consecutive terms. Sometimes that works. Often it doesn't — and then panic sets in, 2 minutes vanish, and the question is skipped in frustration.

The struggle happens because of one missing habit: **a fixed checking order**. Doctors follow a diagnostic sequence; pilots follow a checklist. You need one too. We will build it in this chapter.

🧠 **Active Recall:** Before reading further, write down: what do you usually do first when you see a number series? Be honest — is it a method, or a guess?

## Chunk 1: The Classification Checklist (Your 5-Second Scan)

When a series appears on screen, run this mental checklist **in order**:

1. **Is it growing fast?** (Terms doubling or more) → Think *multiplication, powers, or ×n ± k*.
2. **Is it growing slowly / steadily?** → Think *addition/subtraction of differences*.
3. **Does it rise and fall?** → Think *alternating operations or two interleaved series*.
4. **Are the terms perfect squares/cubes or near them?** → Think *n², n³, n² ± k*.
5. **Are the terms fractions or decimals?** → Think *division or ×0.5 patterns*.

This single checklist resolves the large majority of exam series. Memorise it as:

> **"Fast-Slow-Wavy-Square-Fraction"** — five doors; every series hides behind one.

🧠 **Active Recall:** Close the book. Recite the five doors in order. Which door do you check first, and why?

## Chunk 2: Family 1 — Difference Series (The Slow Door)

**Basic idea:** The gap between consecutive terms follows its own pattern.

**Example:** 4, 7, 12, 19, 28, ?
- Differences: 3, 5, 7, 9 → increasing by 2
- Next difference: 11 → Answer: **39**

**Second-level differences:** Sometimes the first differences show nothing, but the *differences of differences* do.

**Example:** 2, 6, 12, 20, 30, ?
- First differences: 4, 6, 8, 10 → second differences: 2, 2, 2 (constant!)
- Next first difference: 12 → Answer: **42**
- (Notice: this is also n² + n: 1²+1=2, 2²+2=6 … 6²+6=42. Many series have two valid logics — both give the same answer.)

**Exam trap:** The first two differences look constant, tempting you to stop — but the third difference breaks the pattern. Always compute differences for *all* given terms before predicting.

🧠 **Active Recall:** For the series 3, 8, 15, 24, 35 — compute first differences. What do you see? (Answer: 5, 7, 9, 11 — odd numbers; next term 48.)

---

## Chunk 3: Family 2 — Ratio & Mixed Operations (The Fast Door)

When terms grow rapidly, divide instead of subtracting.

**Example:** 3, 6, 18, 72, 360, ?
- Ratios: ×2, ×3, ×4, ×5 → next ×6 → Answer: **2160**

**Mixed operation family:** ×a + b, where a and b may themselves change.

**Example:** 2, 5, 11, 23, 47, ?
- Rule: ×2 + 1 each time → 47×2 + 1 = **95**

**Example (tougher):** 4, 9, 20, 43, 90, ?
- ×2+1, ×2+2, ×2+3, ×2+4 → next ×2+5 → 90×2+5 = **185**

**Mnemonic:** 🗝️ **"Multiply first, then look for the leftover."** When growth is fast, always test ×2, ×3, ×1.5 first, then check what's added or subtracted on top.

🧠 **Active Recall:** In 5, 11, 23, 47, 95 — what is the rule? Say it aloud before checking. (×2+1.)

---

## Chunk 4: Family 3 — Squares, Cubes & Their Neighbours (The Square Door)

Memorise squares to 30 and cubes to 15 — non-negotiable for Graduation level.

**Example:** 2, 5, 10, 17, 26, ?
- These are 1²+1, 2²+1, 3²+1, 4²+1, 5²+1 → next 6²+1 = **37**

**Example:** 7, 26, 63, 124, 215, ?
- 2³−1, 3³−1, 4³−1, 5³−1, 6³−1 → next 7³−1 = **342**

**Trap alert:** Examiners love n³ ± n, n² ± n hybrids. Example: 0, 6, 24, 60, 120 → n³ − n (1³−1=0, 2³−2=6 …) → next 6³−6 = **210**.

🧠 **Active Recall:** What is 12³? 17²? If you paused more than 2 seconds, schedule a squares/cubes drill tonight.

---

## Chunk 5: Family 4 — Alternating & Interleaved Series (The Wavy Door)

Two independent series are woven together: odd positions follow one rule, even positions another.

**Example:** 2, 10, 4, 15, 8, 20, 16, ?
- Odd positions: 2, 4, 8, 16 (×2) | Even positions: 10, 15, 20 (+5)
- Next is even position → 20 + 5 = **25**

**Alternating operations:** +3, ×2, +3, ×2 …

**Example:** 5, 8, 16, 19, 38, 41, ?
- +3, ×2, +3, ×2, +3 → next ×2 → 41×2 = **82**

**How to spot:** If differences alternate in sign or jump wildly, split the series into two.

🧠 **Active Recall:** In 3, 6, 5, 10, 9, 18, ? — what are the two interleaved rules? (Odd positions 3, 5, 9: differences 2, 4 — doubling, i.e. ×2−1; even positions 6, 10, 18: differences 4, 8 — doubling, i.e. ×2−2. Next odd difference is 8 → 9 + 8 = **17**.)

---

## Chunk 6: Family 5 — Special & Hybrid Series

**Prime numbers:** 2, 3, 5, 7, 11, 13, 17 … (and primes ± k variants).

**Fibonacci-type:** Each term = sum of previous two. 1, 1, 2, 3, 5, 8, 13 … Exam variant: a, b, a+b, a+2b … or "sum of previous two ± k".

**Example:** 3, 5, 9, 17, 33, ?
- Each term = previous ×2 − 1, OR differences double: 2, 4, 8, 16 → next diff 32 → **65**. (Again, two logics, one answer.)

**Digit-based series (CAT/CSAT favourite):** Pattern in digits, not values. E.g., 11, 13, 17, 19, 23 — primes; or series where you reverse digits, sum digits, etc.

🧠 **Active Recall:** Name all five families (doors) without looking. Then name the two "special" families from this chunk.

## Chunk 7: Wrong-Term Series (Find the Impostor)

Instead of a missing term, one term in the series is **wrong**. Strategy:

1. Compute the pattern assuming the series is correct.
2. The term that breaks the pattern is the impostor — but verify that *replacing* it makes everything consistent.

**Example:** 4, 9, 19, 38, 79, 159
- Test the rule ×2+1: 4×2+1 = 9 ✓; 9×2+1 = 19 ✓; 19×2+1 = 39 ✗ — but the series shows 38.
- Check the rest: if the term were 39, then 39×2+1 = 79 ✓ and 79×2+1 = 159 ✓. Replacing 38 with 39 makes the whole series consistent.
- **Impostor: 38** (it should be 39).

**Trap:** The wrong term is rarely at the ends — examiners hide it in the middle so both halves look plausible.

## Chunk 8: Letter & Alphanumeric Series

Convert letters to positions instantly: **A=1 … Z=26.** Memorise anchor points: **E=5, J=10, O=15, T=20, Z=26** (multiples of 5) — compute any letter from the nearest anchor.

**Example:** B, E, H, K, ?
- Positions: 2, 5, 8, 11 → +3 each → next 14 = **N**

**Example (increasing gaps):** A, C, F, J, O, ?
- Differences: +2, +3, +4, +5 → next +6 → O(15)+6 = 21 = **U**

**Alphanumeric:** Z9, X16, V25, T36, ?
- Letters: Z, X, V, T → −2 each → next **R**; Numbers: 9, 16, 25, 36 = 3², 4², 5², 6² → next 7² = 49 → **R49**

🧠 **Active Recall:** What is the position of Q? Of L? Use the anchors (O=15, J=10) — don't count from A.

## Chunk 9: The Logic Toolkit — Same Brain, New Costumes

Number series trains one skill: **spot the rule from examples**. The same skill powers the whole reasoning section:

**Syllogisms:** Rules like "All A are B" are just patterns over sets. Draw Venn circles; accept only conclusions true in *every* valid diagram. Trap: "Some A are B" does NOT mean "Some A are not B."

**Coding-Decoding:** Letter shifting is a number series on alphabet positions. If CAT → DBU, that's +1 per letter. If CAT → FDW, that's +3. Treat it exactly like a letter series.

**Direction Sense:** Track (x, y) coordinates. North = +y, East = +x. "Turns left" = rotate 90° anticlockwise. Final displacement = √(x² + y²) — Pythagoras, not guesswork.

**Blood Relations:** Draw a mini family tree the moment the question starts. Mark gender with symbols (□ male, ○ female). Never hold relations in your head — that's where errors breed.

**Seating Arrangement / Puzzles:** Fix the most constrained person first (the one with the most clues). Build outward. This is the "anchor first" rule — identical to using anchor letters in alphabet series.

🧠 **Active Recall:** What single skill unites series, coding, syllogisms and puzzles? (Answer: extracting a rule from given information and applying it consistently.)

---

## Chunk 10: Exam Strategy — The 30-Second Rule

- **First pass (≤30 sec per question):** Run the five-door checklist. If the family reveals itself, solve and move on.
- **If nothing clicks in 30 seconds:** Mark and skip. Series questions have diminishing returns — a fresh look in the second pass often cracks them instantly.
- **Wrong-term questions:** Budget 45–60 seconds; they need full verification.
- **Never** compute beyond the given terms before predicting — wasted effort if the pattern is simple.

**Mnemonic for the whole chapter:** 🐘 **"D-R-S-A-L" — "Doctor SAaL"**
- **D**ifference (slow door)
- **R**atio (fast door)
- **S**quares/Cubes (square door)
- **A**lternating (wavy door)
- **L**etters→Numbers (alphabet door)

## Summary Table

| Family | Signal | First Test | Example Answer |
|---|---|---|---|
| Difference | Slow, steady growth | Subtract consecutive terms | 4,7,12,19,28 → 39 |
| Second difference | First diffs look random | Subtract the differences | 2,6,12,20,30 → 42 |
| Ratio/Mixed | Fast growth (×2 or more) | Divide consecutive terms | 3,6,18,72 → 360 |
| Squares/Cubes ± k | Terms near n² or n³ | Compare with 1,4,9,16… / 1,8,27… | 2,5,10,17,26 → 37 |
| Alternating | Rise-fall or wild jumps | Split odd/even positions | 5,8,16,19,38,41 → 82 |
| Fibonacci-type | Each term ≈ sum of previous | Add last two terms | 3,5,9,17,33 → 65 |
| Letter series | Alphabets | Convert to positions (anchors E,J,O,T,Z) | B,E,H,K → N |
| Wrong term | One impostor | Verify rule across ALL terms | Impostor usually mid-series |
