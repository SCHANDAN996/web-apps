# Feynman Explains: Mensuration — The Pizza That Fooled Everyone

## The Hardest Idea: Why Area and Perimeter Play Tricks on You

Let me tell you about the day a pizza shop nearly tricked me.

I walked in hungry and saw a deal: "One 12-inch pizza for ₹300, or TWO 6-inch pizzas for ₹300!" Two pizzas for the price of one? Obviously the better deal, right?

Wrong. And here's why — this is the single hardest idea in mensuration, the one that fools even toppers in SSC CGL and CAT:

**When a shape doubles in size, its area does NOT double. It becomes FOUR times bigger.**

## The Pizza Math (Do It With Me)

A "12-inch pizza" means the *diameter* is 12 inches, so the radius is 6 inches.

- Area of the big pizza = π × 6² = **36π**
- Area of one small pizza (radius 3) = π × 3² = **9π**
- Two small pizzas = 18π

So the "two pizzas" deal gives you only **half** the pizza of the single big one! The shop doubled the *number* of pizzas but halved the *size* — and area cares about size, not count.

## Why Does This Happen? Think of a Square of Chocolate

Imagine a chocolate bar that is 1 piece wide and 1 piece tall. That's 1 piece of chocolate.

Now make it 2 wide and 2 tall. How many pieces? Not 2 — it's **4**! Because you grew in *two directions at once*: sideways AND upwards. Each direction multiplies.

- Double the length → area × 2
- Double the width too → area × 2 again
- Total: 2 × 2 = **4 times**

That's why every area formula has something *squared* or *two lengths multiplied*: πr², side², length × breadth, ½ × base × height. Area is always "length times length." Perimeter is just "length" — that's why doubling a square only doubles its perimeter but quadruples its area.

## The Unforgettable Trick: "Area is a SQUARE deal" 🟦

Whenever you see an area formula, ask: *"Where are my two lengths?"*

- Circle: π × r × **r** — two radii hiding in there!
- Rectangle: length × breadth — obvious two.
- Triangle: ½ × base × height — two again.
- Volume? **Three** lengths: l × b × h. That's why doubling a cube's side makes volume **8 times** bigger (2×2×2).

**Memory hook:** *"Perimeter walks in a line (×2), Area dances on a floor (×4), Volume fills a room (×8)."* Line–Floor–Room: 2, 4, 8.

## One More Feynman Secret: Units Are Your Lie Detector

If your answer for an area comes out in "cm" instead of "cm²", you made a mistake — you forgot to multiply two lengths. Units never lie. In the exam, this 2-second check catches silly errors before they cost you marks.
