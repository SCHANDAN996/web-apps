76. The length of a rectangle is increased by 20% and its breadth is decreased by 20%. What is the percentage change in its area?
(a) No change (b) 4% decrease (c) 4% increase (d) 2% decrease
Answer: (b)
Solution: The tempting answer is "no change", assuming +20% and −20% cancel out, but percentage changes multiply, not add. Net change = 20 − 20 − (20×20)/100 = −4%, so the answer is 4% decrease.
Source: NCERT Class 8 Mathematics (Mensuration)

77. A wire bent into a square encloses an area of 484 cm². If the same wire is bent into a circle, the area enclosed is (take π = 22/7):
(a) 528 cm² (b) 594 cm² (c) 462 cm² (d) 616 cm²
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is using the side 22 cm as the radius directly, which gives an inflated area. Side = √484 = 22 cm, so wire length = 88 cm; 2πr = 88 gives r = 14 cm, and area = (22/7)×14×14 = 616 cm², so the answer is 616 cm².
Source: PYQ-style

78. The diagonals of a rhombus are 24 cm and 10 cm. Its perimeter is:
(a) 68 cm (b) 48 cm (c) 34 cm (d) 52 cm
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is adding the diagonals (24 + 10 = 34) and treating it as the perimeter. Half-diagonals are 12 and 5, so side = √(12² + 5²) = 13 cm, and perimeter = 4 × 13 = 52 cm, so the answer is 52 cm.
Source: NCERT Class 8 Mathematics (Mensuration)

79. A circular park of radius 21 m has a path 7 m wide running around it on the outside. The area of the path is (take π = 22/7):
(a) 1232 m² (b) 1078 m² (c) 924 m² (d) 1386 m²
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is computing the outer circle's area (22/7 × 28² = 2464) and forgetting to subtract the park. Path area = π(28² − 21²) = (22/7)(784 − 441) = (22/7)(343) = 1078 m², so the answer is 1078 m².
Source: NCERT Class 10 Mathematics (Areas Related to Circles)

80. The area of an equilateral triangle is 16√3 cm². Its perimeter is:
(a) 24 cm (b) 12 cm (c) 36 cm (d) 48 cm
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is taking the side as 16 cm directly from the area figure. (√3/4)a² = 16√3 gives a² = 64, so a = 8 cm, and perimeter = 3 × 8 = 24 cm, so the answer is 24 cm.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Mathematics (Heron's Formula)

81. A cylinder and a cone have the same base radius and the same height. If the volume of the cone is 150 cm³, the volume of the cylinder is:
(a) 450 cm³ (b) 300 cm³ (c) 600 cm³ (d) 150 cm³
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is doubling the cone's volume, but a cylinder holds three times a cone of the same base and height, not twice. Volume of cylinder = 3 × 150 = 450 cm³, so the answer is 450 cm³.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Mathematics (Surface Areas and Volumes)

82. The curved surface area of a cylinder is 440 cm² and its height is 10 cm. Its volume is (take π = 22/7):
(a) 1232 cm³ (b) 1400 cm³ (c) 1540 cm³ (d) 1760 cm³
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is treating 440 as the total surface area and subtracting imaginary circular ends. From 2πrh = 440, r = 440×7/(2×22×10) = 7 cm, so volume = πr²h = (22/7)×49×10 = 1540 cm³, so the answer is 1540 cm³.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Mathematics (Surface Areas and Volumes)

83. The radius of a sphere is doubled. Its volume becomes:
(a) 2 times (b) 4 times (c) 8 times (d) 6 times
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is answering 4 times by confusing volume with surface area, which does become 4 times. Volume varies as r³, so doubling r multiplies volume by 2³ = 8, so the answer is 8 times.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Mathematics (Surface Areas and Volumes)

84. A hall is 15 m long, 12 m wide and 5 m high. The cost of painting its four walls and ceiling at ₹8 per m² is:
(a) ₹3600 (b) ₹2880 (c) ₹3960 (d) ₹3240
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is painting the floor too, which adds 180 m² and inflates the cost to ₹3960. Area = ceiling (15×12 = 180) + walls 2(15+12)×5 = 270, total 450 m², and cost = 450 × 8 = ₹3600, so the answer is ₹3600.
Source: NCERT Class 8 Mathematics (Mensuration)

85. The area of a trapezium is 384 cm² and the distance between its parallel sides is 16 cm. If one parallel side is 28 cm, the other is:
(a) 22 cm (b) 20 cm (c) 18 cm (d) 24 cm
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is forgetting the factor ½ and solving (28 + b)×16 = 384, which gives an absurd value. ½(28 + b)×16 = 384 gives 28 + b = 48, so b = 20 cm, so the answer is 20 cm.
Source: NCERT Class 8 Mathematics (Mensuration)

86. A solid metallic sphere of radius 6 cm is melted and recast into a cylinder of radius 4 cm. The height of the cylinder is:
(a) 9 cm (b) 13.5 cm (c) 27 cm (d) 18 cm
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is equating surface areas instead of volumes, but melting conserves volume, not area. (4/3)π(6³) = π(4²)h gives 288 = 16h, so h = 18 cm, so the answer is 18 cm.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Mathematics (Surface Areas and Volumes)

87. The perimeter of a semicircular protractor is 36 cm. Its diameter is (take π = 22/7):
(a) 10.5 cm (b) 14 cm (c) 21 cm (d) 7 cm
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is treating 36 cm as the full circumference πd, which gives a wrong diameter. Perimeter of semicircle = πr + 2r = r(22/7 + 2) = 36r/7 = 36, so r = 7 cm and diameter = 14 cm, so the answer is 14 cm.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Mathematics (Areas Related to Circles)

88. A cube of side 10 cm has a cube of side 4 cm cut out from one corner. The total surface area of the remaining solid is:
(a) 632 cm² (b) 568 cm² (c) 600 cm² (d) 584 cm²
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is subtracting the small cube's face areas, but the three new inner faces exactly replace the three removed outer faces. Total surface area stays 6 × 10² = 600 cm², so the answer is 600 cm².
Source: PYQ-style

89. The ratio of the volumes of two spheres is 27 : 64. The ratio of their surface areas is:
(a) 3 : 4 (b) 9 : 16 (c) 27 : 64 (d) 81 : 256
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is copying the volume ratio 27 : 64, or the radius ratio 3 : 4, directly. Radii ratio = ∛27 : ∛64 = 3 : 4, and surface area ratio = 3² : 4² = 9 : 16, so the answer is 9 : 16.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Mathematics (Surface Areas and Volumes)

90. A rectangular plot 50 m × 40 m has two cross-paths each 2 m wide, one along the length and one along the breadth. The area of the paths is:
(a) 184 m² (b) 180 m² (c) 176 m² (d) 200 m²
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is adding 100 + 80 = 180 m², which double-counts the 2×2 intersection square. Path area = 100 + 80 − 4 = 176 m², so the answer is 176 m².
Source: NCERT Class 7 Mathematics (Perimeter and Area)

91. The slant height of a cone is 13 cm and its base radius is 5 cm. Its volume is (take π = 22/7):
(a) 282.9 cm³ (b) 220 cm³ (c) 408.6 cm³ (d) 314.3 cm³
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is using the slant height 13 cm as the vertical height in the volume formula. Height = √(13² − 5²) = 12 cm, so volume = (1/3)×(22/7)×25×12 = 2200/7 ≈ 314.3 cm³, so the answer is 314.3 cm³.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Mathematics (Surface Areas and Volumes)

92. A wheel makes 1000 revolutions in covering 88 km. The radius of the wheel is (take π = 22/7):
(a) 14 m (b) 7 m (c) 1.4 m (d) 0.7 m
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is forgetting to convert km to m, which gives an absurd radius. Distance per revolution = 88000/1000 = 88 m = 2πr, so r = 88×7/44 = 14 m, so the answer is 14 m.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Mathematics (Areas Related to Circles)

93. The area of a rhombus is 240 cm² and one of its diagonals is 16 cm. The length of each side is:
(a) 20 cm (b) 17 cm (c) 15 cm (d) 13 cm
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is treating 16 cm as the side directly. Other diagonal = 2×240/16 = 30 cm; half-diagonals 8 and 15 give side = √(8² + 15²) = 17 cm, so the answer is 17 cm.
Source: NCERT Class 8 Mathematics (Mensuration)

94. A hemispherical bowl of internal radius 9 cm is full of liquid. This liquid is poured into cylindrical bottles of radius 3 cm and height 6 cm. The number of bottles filled completely is:
(a) 8 (b) 12 (c) 9 (d) 13.5
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is miscomputing the hemisphere volume as (4/3)πr³ instead of (2/3)πr³, which doubles the count. Bowl volume = (2/3)π(9³) = 486π; bottle volume = π×9×6 = 54π; 486/54 = 9, so the answer is 9.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Mathematics (Surface Areas and Volumes)

95. The length, breadth and height of a cuboid are in the ratio 5 : 3 : 2 and its total surface area is 558 cm². Its volume is:
(a) 720 cm³ (b) 640 cm³ (c) 900 cm³ (d) 810 cm³
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is computing volume from the ratio numbers directly (5×3×2 = 30) without finding the scale factor. TSA = 2(15 + 6 + 10)x² = 62x² = 558 gives x = 3, so dimensions are 15, 9, 6 and volume = 810 cm³, so the answer is 810 cm³.
Source: NCERT Class 8 Mathematics (Mensuration)

96. A sector of a circle of radius 14 cm has a central angle of 90°. The perimeter of the sector is (take π = 22/7):
(a) 44 cm (b) 36 cm (c) 50 cm (d) 58 cm
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is giving only the arc length (22 cm) and forgetting the two radii. Arc = (90/360)×2×(22/7)×14 = 22 cm; perimeter = 22 + 14 + 14 = 50 cm, so the answer is 50 cm.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Mathematics (Areas Related to Circles)

97. The base of a right prism is a right-angled triangle with legs 6 cm and 8 cm, and the height of the prism is 10 cm. Its total surface area is:
(a) 288 cm² (b) 240 cm² (c) 264 cm² (d) 312 cm²
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is forgetting the two triangular bases and reporting only the lateral area 240 cm². Hypotenuse = 10 cm; lateral area = (6+8+10)×10 = 240 cm²; bases = 2×½×6×8 = 48 cm²; total = 288 cm², so the answer is 288 cm².
Source: PYQ-style

98. If the area of a square is equal to the area of a circle, then the ratio of the perimeter of the square to the circumference of the circle is:
(a) 2 : √π (b) √π : 2 (c) π : 2 (d) 1 : 1
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is assuming equal areas mean equal boundaries, which is false — a circle encloses a given area with the least boundary. With a² = πr², a = r√π; ratio = 4a : 2πr = 4r√π : 2πr = 2 : √π, so the answer is 2 : √π.
Source: PYQ-style

99. A cone of height 24 cm and base radius 7 cm is reshaped into a sphere. The radius of the sphere is:
(a) ∛294 cm (b) 7 cm (c) ∛98 cm (d) 14 cm
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is equating heights or radii instead of volumes. Cone volume = (1/3)π×49×24 = 392π; sphere (4/3)πR³ = 392π gives R³ = 294, so R = ∛294 cm, so the answer is ∛294 cm.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Mathematics (Surface Areas and Volumes)

100. The area of the largest triangle that can be inscribed in a semicircle of radius r is:
(a) r²/2 (b) 2r² (c) πr²/2 (d) r²
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is choosing 2r² by taking base 2r and height 2r, but the maximum height inside a semicircle is only r. Largest triangle has base = diameter 2r and height = r, so area = ½×2r×r = r², so the answer is r².
Source: PYQ-style
