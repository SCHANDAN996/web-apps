101. The distance between the points (3, −2) and (−1, 1) is
(a) 4 (b) 5 (c) √7 (d) 25
Answer: (b)
Solution: The tempting wrong choice 25 is the value under the square root before taking the root — a classic trap. Distance = √[(3−(−1))² + (−2−1)²] = √[16 + 9] = √25 = 5, so the answer is 5.

102. The midpoint of the segment joining (4, 7) and (−2, 3) is
(a) (2, 5) (b) (3, 5) (c) (1, 2) (d) (1, 5)
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is adding the x-coordinates without dividing by 2, which gives 2 and leads to the wrong option. Midpoint = ((4−2)/2, (7+3)/2) = (1, 5), so the answer is (1, 5).

103. The slope of the line passing through (2, 3) and (6, 11) is
(a) 4 (b) 8 (c) 1/2 (d) 2
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is computing run over rise (4/8 = 1/2) instead of rise over run. Slope = (11−3)/(6−2) = 8/4 = 2, so the answer is 2.

104. The equation of a line with slope 3 and y-intercept −2 is
(a) y = 3x − 2 (b) y = −2x + 3 (c) y = 3x + 2 (d) 3y = x − 2
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is swapping slope and intercept, giving y = −2x + 3. Using y = mx + c with m = 3 and c = −2 gives y = 3x − 2, so the answer is y = 3x − 2.

105. The distance of the point (3, 4) from the origin is
(a) 5 (b) 7 (c) 12 (d) 25
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is adding 3 + 4 = 7, but distance uses the Pythagorean form. Distance = √(3² + 4²) = √25 = 5, so the answer is 5.

106. The point which divides the segment joining (1, 2) and (7, 8) in the ratio 1 : 2 internally is
(a) (4, 5) (b) (5, 6) (c) (3, 4) (d) (2, 3)
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is using the ratio 2 : 1 instead of 1 : 2, which gives (5, 6). By the section formula, x = (1·7 + 2·1)/3 = 3, y = (1·8 + 2·2)/3 = 4, so the answer is (3, 4).

107. The area of the triangle with vertices (0, 0), (4, 0) and (0, 3) is
(a) 12 (b) 7 (c) 6 (d) 24
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is forgetting the factor 1/2, giving 12. Area = ½ × base × height = ½ × 4 × 3 = 6, so the answer is 6.

108. The lines 2x + 3y = 5 and 4x + 6y = 7 are
(a) intersecting (b) coincident (c) parallel (d) perpendicular
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is calling them coincident because the coefficients are proportional; but the constants are not in the same ratio (5/7 ≠ 1/2). Slopes are equal (−2/3 each) but intercepts differ, so the lines are parallel, and the answer is parallel.

112. The distance between the parallel lines 3x + 4y + 5 = 0 and 3x + 4y + 15 = 0 is
(a) 10 (b) 2 (c) 5 (d) 4
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is taking the difference of constants (10) as the distance without dividing by √(a² + b²). Distance = |15 − 5|/√(9 + 16) = 10/5 = 2, so the answer is 2.

113. If the points (1, 2), (3, k) and (5, 10) are collinear, then k equals
(a) 6 (b) 4 (c) 8 (d) 5
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is averaging 2 and 10 to get 6 by luck but for the wrong reason; properly, slope between first two points equals slope between last two: (k−2)/2 = (10−k)/2, giving k − 2 = 10 − k, so k = 6. The answer is 6.

114. The equation of the line passing through (1, 2) and perpendicular to 2x − y + 3 = 0 is

Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is keeping the same slope (parallel) instead of the perpendicular slope. Given slope is 2, so perpendicular slope is −1/2; line: y − 2 = −1/2(x − 1), i.e. x + 2y − 5 = 0, so the answer is x + 2y − 5 = 0.

119. The value of k for which the lines kx + 3y + 1 = 0 and 2x + y + 3 = 0 are perpendicular is

123. The equation of the circle passing through (0, 0), (4, 0) and (0, 3) is
(a) x² + y² − 4x − 3y = 0 (b) x² + y² + 4x + 3y = 0 (c) x² + y² − 4x − 3y + 1 = 0 (d) x² + y² = 25
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is assuming the circle is centred at the origin since one point is the origin. Substituting (0, 0) into x² + y² + Dx + Ey + F = 0 gives F = 0; (4, 0) gives D = −4; (0, 3) gives E = −3, so the equation is x² + y² − 4x − 3y = 0, and the answer is x² + y² − 4x − 3y = 0.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Mathematics, Conic Sections

124. A line passes through (2, 2) and cuts off intercepts on the axes whose sum is 9. If both intercepts are positive and equal in magnitude of the form a and (9 − a), the equation of the line when the intercepts are 3 and 6 is
(a) 2x + y = 6 (b) x + 2y = 6 (c) x + y = 9 (d) 2x + y = 9
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is swapping the intercepts, which gives x + 2y = 6 — but that line does not pass through (2, 2) with intercepts 3 and 6 in the right order; check: for intercepts a = 3 (on x-axis) and b = 6 (on y-axis), the line is x/3 + y/6 = 1, i.e. 2x + y = 6, and indeed 2(2) + 2 = 6. The answer is 2x + y = 6.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Mathematics, Straight Lines

125. The reflection of the point (4, 1) in the line y = x is the point P, and the reflection of P in the y-axis is Q. The coordinates of Q are
(a) (4, −1) (b) (1, 4) (c) (−4, 1) (d) (−1, 4)
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is reflecting (4, 1) directly in the y-axis, which gives (−4, 1). First, reflection in y = x swaps coordinates: P = (1, 4). Then reflection in the y-axis negates the x-coordinate: Q = (−1, 4), so the answer is (−1, 4).
