126. In a triangle ABC, the sides are in the ratio 3 : 4 : 5 and the perimeter is 48 cm. What is the area of the triangle?
(a) 96 cm² (b) 84 cm² (c) 72 cm² (d) 108 cm²
Answer: (a)
Solution: The tempting wrong option 84 cm² comes from taking the sides as 12, 16, 20 but then miscomputing half of 12 × 14. Sides are 3k, 4k, 5k with 12k = 48, so k = 4 and the sides are 12, 16, 20. Since 3-4-5 is a right triangle, area = ½ × 12 × 16 = 96, so the answer is 96 cm².

127. The angles of a triangle are in the ratio 2 : 3 : 7. The triangle is:
(a) acute-angled (b) right-angled (c) obtuse-angled (d) equilateral
Answer: (c)
Solution: Many pick right-angled assuming one angle must be 90°, but the angles are 2x, 3x, 7x with 12x = 180°, so x = 15° and the angles are 30°, 45°, 105°. Since 105° > 90°, the triangle is obtuse-angled, so the answer is obtuse-angled.

128. In a circle of radius 10 cm, a chord is at a distance of 6 cm from the centre. The length of the chord is:
(a) 16 cm (b) 12 cm (c) 8 cm (d) 14 cm
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is answering 8 cm, which is only half the chord (the segment from foot of perpendicular to one end). Half-chord = √(10² − 6²) = √64 = 8 cm, so full chord = 2 × 8 = 16, so the answer is 16 cm.

130. The diagonals of a rhombus are 24 cm and 10 cm. Its side length is:
(a) 13 cm (b) 17 cm (c) 26 cm (d) 12 cm
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is using the full diagonals in Pythagoras, giving √(24² + 10²) = 26. Diagonals of a rhombus bisect each other at right angles, so half-diagonals are 12 and 5, and side = √(12² + 5²) = √169 = 13, so the answer is 13 cm.

132. In triangle ABC, angle A = 50° and angle B = 60°. The bisectors of angles B and C meet at point O. Angle BOC is:

133. A regular polygon has each interior angle equal to 156°. The number of its sides is:
(a) 12 (b) 15 (c) 18 (d) 20
Answer: (b)

136. The area of a trapezium is 180 cm² and the distance between its parallel sides is 12 cm. If one parallel side is 18 cm, the other is:
(a) 12 cm (b) 10 cm (c) 14 cm (d) 16 cm
Answer: (a)
Solution: The tempting wrong option 14 cm comes from forgetting the factor ½ in the area formula. Area = ½ × (sum of parallel sides) × height, so 180 = ½ × (18 + x) × 12, giving 18 + x = 30 and x = 12, so the answer is 12 cm.

137. In a circle, two chords AB and CD intersect inside the circle at P. If AP = 4 cm, PB = 9 cm and CP = 6 cm, then PD is:
(a) 5 cm (b) 6 cm (c) 7 cm (d) 8 cm

Solution: The trap is adding or subtracting the segments instead of using the intersecting chords property. By that property, AP × PB = CP × PD, so 4 × 9 = 6 × PD, giving PD = 36/6 = 6, so the answer is 6 cm.

(a) 100° (b) 200° (c) 50° (d) 25°

Solution: The tempting wrong option 100° assumes the angles are equal, but the angle at the centre is double the angle at the circumference standing on the same arc. Hence the required angle = 100°/2 = 50°, so the answer is 50°.

(a) 462 cm² (b) 154 cm² (c) 308 cm² (d) 616 cm²

Solution: The trap is equating areas instead of perimeters. Side of square = √484 = 22 cm, so wire length = 88 cm. Then 2πr = 88 gives r = 14 cm, and circle area = (22/7) × 14 × 14 = 616, so the answer is 616 cm².
