101. In a triangle ABC, the angles are in the ratio 2 : 3 : 4. The largest angle of the triangle is:
(a) 60° (b) 80° (c) 90° (d) 100°
Answer: (b)
Solution: Many students divide 180° by 4 and get 45°, then multiply wrongly. The correct method: let angles be 2x, 3x, 4x. Then 2x + 3x + 4x = 180°, so 9x = 180°, x = 20°. Largest angle = 4x = 80°, so the answer is 80°.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Mathematics

102. Two parallel lines are cut by a transversal. If one pair of interior angles on the same side of the transversal measures 65° and x, then x equals:
(a) 65° (b) 105° (c) 115° (d) 125°
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is assuming interior angles on the same side are equal — they are supplementary, not equal. So x = 180° − 65° = 115°, so the answer is 115°.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Mathematics

103. The exterior angle of a regular polygon is 24°. The number of sides of the polygon is:
(a) 12 (b) 15 (c) 18 (d) 20
Answer: (b)
Solution: A common error is dividing 360° by the interior angle instead of the exterior angle. Number of sides = 360° ÷ 24° = 15, so the answer is 15.
Source: NCERT Class 8 Mathematics

104. In a circle of radius 10 cm, a chord of length 16 cm is drawn. The perpendicular distance of the chord from the centre is:
(a) 4 cm (b) 6 cm (c) 8 cm (d) 5 cm
Answer: (b)
Solution: Students often forget to halve the chord and use 16 directly, getting an impossible result. The perpendicular bisects the chord, so half-chord = 8 cm. Distance = √(10² − 8²) = √36 = 6 cm, so the answer is 6 cm.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Mathematics

105. The diagonals of a rhombus are 24 cm and 10 cm. Its side length is:
(a) 13 cm (b) 12 cm (c) 17 cm (d) 26 cm
Answer: (a)
Solution: The tempting error is using the full diagonals in Pythagoras, giving 26 cm. Diagonals bisect each other at right angles, so half-diagonals are 12 cm and 5 cm. Side = √(12² + 5²) = √169 = 13 cm, so the answer is 13 cm.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Mathematics

106. In triangle ABC, D and E are midpoints of AB and AC respectively. If BC = 14 cm, then DE equals:
(a) 7 cm (b) 10.5 cm (c) 28 cm (d) 3.5 cm
Answer: (a)
Solution: By the Midpoint Theorem, the segment joining midpoints of two sides is parallel to the third side and half its length. Some students wrongly double BC. DE = 14 ÷ 2 = 7 cm, so the answer is 7 cm.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Mathematics

107. The angle subtended by an arc at the centre of a circle is 100°. The angle subtended by the same arc at any point on the remaining part of the circle is:
(a) 100° (b) 200° (c) 25° (d) 50°
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is doubling 100° to get 200°, reversing the theorem. The angle at the centre is twice the angle at the circumference, so the required angle = 100° ÷ 2 = 50°, so the answer is 50°.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Mathematics

108. Two circles of radii 5 cm and 3 cm touch each other externally. The distance between their centres is:
(a) 2 cm (b) 4 cm (c) 15 cm (d) 8 cm
Answer: (d)
Solution: Students confuse external touch (add radii) with internal touch (subtract radii) and pick 2 cm. For external touching, distance = 5 + 3 = 8 cm, so the answer is 8 cm.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Mathematics

109. In a right-angled triangle, the hypotenuse is 25 cm and one leg is 7 cm. The other leg is:
(a) 18 cm (b) 20 cm (c) 24 cm (d) 32 cm
Answer: (c)
Solution: A frequent slip is adding 25² + 7² instead of subtracting. Other leg = √(25² − 7²) = √(625 − 49) = √576 = 24 cm, so the answer is 24 cm.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Mathematics

110. The sum of interior angles of a polygon with 10 sides is:
(a) 1260° (b) 1440° (c) 1800° (d) 1080°
Answer: (b)
Solution: Some students use n × 180° instead of (n − 2) × 180°, getting 1800°. Sum = (10 − 2) × 180° = 8 × 180° = 1440°, so the answer is 1440°.
Source: NCERT Class 8 Mathematics

111. In triangle PQR, PQ = PR and angle Q = 70°. Then angle P equals:
(a) 70° (b) 55° (c) 110° (d) 40°
Answer: (d)
Solution: Since PQ = PR, the angles opposite them are equal: angle R = angle Q = 70°. The trap is assuming angle P = 70° too. Angle P = 180° − 70° − 70° = 40°, so the answer is 40°.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Mathematics

112. The length of the tangent drawn from a point 13 cm away from the centre of a circle of radius 5 cm is:
(a) 12 cm (b) 10 cm (c) 8 cm (d) 18 cm
Answer: (a)
Solution: The radius is perpendicular to the tangent at the point of contact, forming a right triangle. Students who add 13 and 5 get 18 cm wrongly. Tangent = √(13² − 5²) = √144 = 12 cm, so the answer is 12 cm.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Mathematics

113. ABCD is a cyclic quadrilateral. If angle A = 85°, then angle C equals:
(a) 95° (b) 85° (c) 105° (d) 170°
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is thinking opposite angles of a cyclic quadrilateral are equal. They are supplementary: angle C = 180° − 85° = 95°, so the answer is 95°.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Mathematics

114. The area of an equilateral triangle with side 6 cm is:
(a) 9√3 cm² (b) 12√3 cm² (c) 18√3 cm² (d) 36√3 cm²
Answer: (a)
Solution: Area = (√3/4) × side² = (√3/4) × 36 = 9√3 cm². The common error is using (√3/2) × side², which gives 18√3 cm². So the answer is 9√3 cm².
Source: NCERT Class 9 Mathematics

115. In triangle ABC, angle B = 90°, AB = 8 cm, BC = 6 cm. The length of the median from B to hypotenuse AC is:
(a) 5 cm (b) 4 cm (c) 6 cm (d) 10 cm
Answer: (a)
Solution: Hypotenuse AC = √(8² + 6²) = 10 cm. In a right triangle, the median to the hypotenuse equals half the hypotenuse. Students who forget this property attempt heavy calculations. Median = 10 ÷ 2 = 5 cm, so the answer is 5 cm.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Mathematics

116. The ratio of the areas of two similar triangles is 9 : 16. The ratio of their corresponding sides is:
(a) 9 : 16 (b) 81 : 256 (c) 3 : 4 (d) 4 : 3
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is copying the area ratio as the side ratio. Sides are in the ratio of square roots: √9 : √16 = 3 : 4, so the answer is 3 : 4.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Mathematics

117. A ladder 13 m long leans against a wall, with its foot 5 m from the wall. The height it reaches on the wall is:
(a) 8 m (b) 10 m (c) 12 m (d) 18 m
Answer: (c)
Solution: Height = √(13² − 5²) = √(169 − 25) = √144 = 12 m. Students who misidentify the hypotenuse get wrong values like 8 m. So the answer is 12 m.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Mathematics

118. The number of diagonals in a polygon of 12 sides is:
(a) 44 (b) 66 (c) 60 (d) 54
Answer: (d)
Solution: Diagonals = n(n − 3)/2 = 12 × 9/2 = 54. The error is using n(n − 1)/2 (which counts all segments), giving 66. So the answer is 54.
Source: NCERT Class 8 Mathematics

119. In a circle, two equal chords are 8 cm from the centre. If the radius is 17 cm, the length of each chord is:
(a) 15 cm (b) 24 cm (c) 30 cm (d) 34 cm
Answer: (c)
Solution: Half-chord = √(17² − 8²) = √(289 − 64) = √225 = 15 cm. The trap is reporting 15 cm as the chord length, forgetting to double it. Chord = 2 × 15 = 30 cm, so the answer is 30 cm.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Mathematics

120. The angles of a quadrilateral are in the ratio 1 : 2 : 3 : 4. The smallest angle is:
(a) 36° (b) 30° (c) 40° (d) 45°
Answer: (a)
Solution: Sum of angles of a quadrilateral is 360°, not 180° — using 180° is the common trap. Let angles be x, 2x, 3x, 4x; then 10x = 360°, x = 36°. Smallest angle = 36°, so the answer is 36°.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Mathematics

121. In triangle ABC, AB = 6 cm, BC = 8 cm, CA = 10 cm. A point D lies on BC such that AD bisects angle A. Then BD equals:
(a) 3.6 cm (b) 3 cm (c) 4 cm (d) 4.5 cm
Answer: (b)
Solution: By the Angle Bisector Theorem, BD : DC = AB : AC = 6 : 10 = 3 : 5. The trap is using AB : BC instead of AB : AC. BD = (3/8) × 8 = 3 cm, so the answer is 3 cm.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Mathematics

122. Two chords AB and CD of a circle intersect inside the circle at point P. If AP = 4 cm, PB = 6 cm and CP = 3 cm, then PD equals:
(a) 2 cm (b) 4.5 cm (c) 12 cm (d) 8 cm
Answer: (d)
Solution: For intersecting chords, AP × PB = CP × PD. Students who set up AP × CP = PB × PD get 4.5 cm wrongly. So 4 × 6 = 3 × PD, giving PD = 24 ÷ 3 = 8 cm, so the answer is 8 cm.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Mathematics

123. In triangle ABC, the medians AD, BE and CF meet at G. If AD = 18 cm, then AG equals:
(a) 6 cm (b) 9 cm (c) 12 cm (d) 15 cm
Answer: (c)
Solution: The centroid divides each median in the ratio 2 : 1, with the longer part towards the vertex. The trap is taking one-third of 18, i.e. 6 cm, which is GD, not AG. AG = (2/3) × 18 = 12 cm, so the answer is 12 cm.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Mathematics

124. From an external point P, a tangent PT and a secant PAB are drawn to a circle, where the secant cuts the circle at A and B (A nearer to P). If PT = 12 cm and PA = 8 cm, then PB equals:
(a) 10 cm (b) 18 cm (c) 16 cm (d) 14 cm
Answer: (b)
Solution: By the tangent-secant theorem, PT² = PA × PB. The trap is using PB − PA (the chord AB) in place of PB. So 144 = 8 × PB, giving PB = 18 cm, so the answer is 18 cm.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Mathematics

125. In triangle ABC, D is a point on BC such that BD : DC = 2 : 1. If area of triangle ABC is 60 cm², then area of triangle ABD is:
(a) 20 cm² (b) 30 cm² (c) 45 cm² (d) 40 cm²
Answer: (d)
Solution: Triangles ABD and ADC share the same height from A, so their areas are in the ratio of bases BD : DC = 2 : 1. The trap is taking half of 60, i.e. 30 cm². Area of ABD = (2/3) × 60 = 40 cm², so the answer is 40 cm².
Source: NCERT Class 9 Mathematics
