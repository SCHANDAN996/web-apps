101. If the roots of x² − 8x + 15 = 0 are α and β, then the value of α² + β² is
(a) 64 (b) 28 (c) 30 (d) 34
Answer: (d)
Solution: The tempting option 64 comes from squaring the sum of roots directly, forgetting to subtract 2αβ. Here α + β = 8 and αβ = 15, so α² + β² = (α + β)² − 2αβ = 64 − 30 = 34, so the answer is 34.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Mathematics

102. For what value of k does the equation x² − kx + 9 = 0 have equal roots?
(a) 3 (b) 9 (c) 6 (d) 12
Answer: (c)
Solution: Many students pick 9, confusing the constant term with the discriminant condition. Equal roots require k² − 4(1)(9) = 0, so k² = 36 and k = ±6; among the options, 6 is listed, so the answer is 6.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Mathematics

103. The sum of the first 20 terms of the AP 3, 7, 11, … is
(a) 840 (b) 820 (c) 800 (d) 860
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap option 800 comes from using nd instead of (n−1)d in the sum formula. Here S₂₀ = 20/2 × [2(3) + 19(4)] = 10 × 82 = 820, so the answer is 820.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Mathematics

104. If one root of x² − 6x + k = 0 is 4, then k equals
(a) 8 (b) −8 (c) 6 (d) 10
Answer: (a)
Solution: The option −8 tempts those who make a sign slip while transposing. Substituting x = 4 gives 16 − 24 + k = 0, so k = 8, so the answer is 8.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Mathematics

105. The 10th term of the GP 2, 6, 18, … is
(a) 6⁹ (b) 2 × 3⁹ (c) 3⁹ (d) 2 × 3¹⁰
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is 2 × 3¹⁰, which uses rⁿ instead of rⁿ⁻¹. The nth term is arⁿ⁻¹ = 2 × 3⁹, so the answer is 2 × 3⁹.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Mathematics

106. The discriminant of 2x² + 5x + 3 = 0 is
(a) 25 (b) −1 (c) 49 (d) 1
Answer: (d)
Solution: Option 49 tempts those who compute b² + 4ac instead of b² − 4ac. Here D = 25 − 24 = 1, so the answer is 1.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Mathematics

107. If the sum of the roots of a quadratic equation is 5 and the product is 6, the equation is
(a) x² − 5x − 6 = 0 (b) x² − 5x + 6 = 0 (c) x² + 5x + 6 = 0 (d) x² + 5x − 6 = 0
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap option x² + 5x + 6 = 0 ignores that the coefficient of x is the negative of the sum of roots. The equation is x² − (sum)x + product = 0, i.e. x² − 5x + 6 = 0, so the answer is x² − 5x + 6 = 0.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Mathematics

108. The sum of the infinite GP 1 + 1/2 + 1/4 + … is
(a) 1 (b) 3/2 (c) 2 (d) 4
Answer: (c)
Solution: Some students pick 3/2 by adding only the first few terms. With first term 1 and common ratio 1/2, S = a/(1 − r) = 1/(1/2) = 2, so the answer is 2.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Mathematics

109. The roots of x² − 5x + 6 = 0 are
(a) 1 and 6 (b) −2 and −3 (c) 2 and 3 (d) −1 and −6
Answer: (c)
Solution: The pair 1 and 6 tempts because 1 × 6 = 6, but 1 + 6 = 7 ≠ 5. Factorising gives (x − 2)(x − 3) = 0, so the answer is 2 and 3.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Mathematics

110. How many terms of the AP 5, 9, 13, … must be taken so that their sum is 230?
(a) 12 (b) 8 (c) 9 (d) 10
Answer: (d)
Solution: Using Sₙ = n/2 [2a + (n−1)d] gives 230 = n/2 [10 + 4(n−1)] = n(2n + 3), so 2n² + 3n − 230 = 0, giving n = 10 (the negative root −11.5 is rejected). The option 9 tempts from an arithmetic slip in the quadratic, so the answer is 10.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Mathematics

111. If α and β are roots of 3x² − 6x + 2 = 0, then 1/α + 1/β equals
(a) 3 (b) 2 (c) 6 (d) 1/3
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is 2, which is just the sum of the roots. Here 1/α + 1/β = (α + β)/αβ = 2/(2/3) = 3, so the answer is 3.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Mathematics

112. The 15th term of the AP whose first term is 7 and common difference is −2 is
(a) −21 (b) −19 (c) −23 (d) 21
Answer: (a)
Solution: The option −23 comes from using nd instead of (n−1)d. Here a₁₅ = 7 + 14(−2) = 7 − 28 = −21, so the answer is −21.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Mathematics

113. The nature of the roots of x² + 4x + 5 = 0 is
(a) real and distinct (b) imaginary (c) real and equal (d) rational
Answer: (b)
Solution: Students who miscompute the discriminant as 36 pick real and distinct. Here D = 16 − 20 = −4 < 0, so the roots are imaginary, so the answer is imaginary.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Mathematics

114. The sum of the series 1 + 2 + 3 + … + 50 is
(a) 1225 (b) 1250 (c) 1325 (d) 1275
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap 1250 comes from computing 50 × 50/2, forgetting the (n+1) factor. Using n(n+1)/2 = 50 × 51/2 = 1275, so the answer is 1275.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Mathematics

115. If the roots of x² − kx + 16 = 0 are in the ratio 1 : 4, then k equals
(a) 10 (b) 6 (c) 12 (d) 8
Answer: (a)
Solution: The option 8 tempts those who report the larger root instead of the sum. Let the roots be m and 4m; then 4m² = 16 gives m = 2, so the roots are 2 and 8 and k = 2 + 8 = 10, so the answer is 10.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Mathematics

116. The third term of a GP is 12 and the sixth term is 96. The common ratio is
(a) 2 (b) 8 (c) 4 (d) 3
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap 8 comes from dividing 96 by 12 directly. Since ar⁵/ar² = r³ = 96/12 = 8, we get r = 2, so the answer is 2.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Mathematics

117. The value of x satisfying √(x + 4) = x − 2 is
(a) 3 (b) 5 (c) 5 and 0 (d) 0
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is accepting both roots after squaring. Squaring gives x + 4 = x² − 4x + 4, i.e. x² − 5x = 0, so x = 0 or 5; but x = 0 fails the original equation since √4 ≠ −2, so the answer is 5.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Mathematics

118. The sum of the first n terms of an AP is 3n² + 2n. Its common difference is
(a) 2 (b) 5 (c) 6 (d) 3
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap 3 comes from reading the coefficient of n² directly. Since Sₙ = (d/2)n² + …, we have d/2 = 3, so d = 6, so the answer is 6.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Mathematics

119. If x² + bx + c = 0 has roots 3 and −5, then b + c equals
(a) −13 (b) 13 (c) −17 (d) 17
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap 13 comes from a sign error on c. Sum of roots = −2 gives b = 2; product = −15 gives c = −15, so b + c = 2 − 15 = −13, so the answer is −13.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Mathematics

120. The 8th term of the sequence 1, 4, 9, 16, … is
(a) 56 (b) 49 (c) 64 (d) 72
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap 56 comes from treating it as an AP with growing differences. The nth term is n², so the 8th term is 64, so the answer is 64.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Mathematics

121. If α and β are the roots of x² − 3x + 1 = 0, then the value of α³ + β³ is
(a) 27 (b) 18 (c) 9 (d) 21
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap 27 comes from cubing the sum of roots directly. Using α³ + β³ = (α + β)³ − 3αβ(α + β) = 27 − 3(1)(3) = 18, so the answer is 18.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Mathematics

122. The sum of all two-digit numbers divisible by 7 is
(a) 721 (b) 735 (c) 742 (d) 728
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap 735 comes from taking 14 terms by wrongly counting the last term. The two-digit multiples of 7 are 14, 21, …, 98, an AP with n = (98 − 14)/7 + 1 = 13 terms, so the sum is 13/2 × (14 + 98) = 13 × 56 = 728, so the answer is 728.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Mathematics

123. If the equation x² − (k + 2)x + 2k = 0 has one root equal to 2, then the other root is
(a) k + 2 (b) 2k (c) k (d) 2 − k
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap 2k is the product of the roots, not the other root. Since the product of roots is 2k and one root is 2, the other root is 2k/2 = k, so the answer is k.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Mathematics

124. Three numbers in GP have sum 21 and product 216. The middle number is
(a) 9 (b) 7 (c) 12 (d) 6
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap 7 comes from dividing the sum 21 by 3, assuming an AP. Let the numbers be a/r, a, ar; their product a³ = 216 gives a = 6, so the answer is 6.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Mathematics

125. If the sum of the first p terms of an AP equals the sum of the first q terms (p ≠ q), then the sum of the first (p + q) terms is
(a) 0 (b) pq (c) p + q (d) (p + q)/2
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap p + q assumes the two equal sums simply add up. From Sₚ = S_q we get (p − q)[2a + (p + q − 1)d] = 0, and since p ≠ q, 2a + (p + q − 1)d = 0; then S_{p+q} = (p+q)/2 × [2a + (p+q−1)d] = 0, so the answer is 0.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Mathematics
