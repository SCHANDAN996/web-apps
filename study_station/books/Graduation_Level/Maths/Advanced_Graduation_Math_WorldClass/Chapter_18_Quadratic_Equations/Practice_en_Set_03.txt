51. If the roots of x² − 8x + k = 0 differ by 2, then the value of k is
(a) 15 (b) 16 (c) 12 (d) 20
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is assuming the roots are 4 and 6, which differ by 2 but give product 24. Let the roots be α and α + 2; then 2α + 2 = 8 gives α = 3, so the roots are 3 and 5 and k = 3 × 5 = 15, so the answer is 15.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Mathematics

52. The sum of the first 20 terms of the AP 5, 9, 13, … is
(a) 760 (b) 800 (c) 860 (d) 900
Answer: (c)
Solution: Many students compute the 20th term as 5 + 20 × 4 = 85, forgetting the formula uses (n − 1)d. Here a = 5, d = 4, so a₂₀ = 5 + 19 × 4 = 81 and Sum = 20/2 × (5 + 81) = 860, so the answer is 860.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Mathematics

53. If α and β are roots of 2x² − 5x + 3 = 0, then the value of α² + β² is
(a) 25/4 (b) 7/4 (c) 13/4 (d) 9/4
Answer: (c)
Solution: The tempting error is squaring the sum directly to get 25/4, which ignores the cross term. α + β = 5/2 and αβ = 3/2, so α² + β² = (α + β)² − 2αβ = 25/4 − 3 = 13/4, so the answer is 13/4.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Mathematics

54. The 3rd term of a GP is 12 and the 6th term is 96. The common ratio is
(a) 2 (b) 3 (c) 4 (d) 8
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is dividing 96 by 12 to get 8 and picking it as the ratio, but that quotient equals r³, not r. Since a₆/a₃ = r³ = 8, we get r = 2, so the answer is 2.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Mathematics

55. For what value of p does the equation x² + px + 16 = 0 have equal roots?
(a) ±8 (b) 8 (c) −8 only (d) 4
Answer: (a)
Solution: Many candidates pick only 8, forgetting that p can be negative since p² appears in the discriminant. Equal roots need p² − 64 = 0, so p = ±8, so the answer is ±8.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Mathematics

56. The sum of the infinite GP 1 + 1/3 + 1/9 + … is
(a) 5/3 (b) 4/3 (c) 2 (d) 3/2
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is applying a/(1 − r) with r = 3 instead of r = 1/3, which gives an absurd negative value. Here a = 1, r = 1/3, so S = 1/(1 − 1/3) = 3/2, so the answer is 3/2.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Mathematics

57. If the roots of x² − 6x + k = 0 are in the ratio 1 : 2, then k equals
(a) 8 (b) 9 (c) 6 (d) 12
Answer: (a)
Solution: A common slip is taking the roots as 1 and 2 directly, but their sum is 3, not 6. Let the roots be m and 2m; then 3m = 6 gives m = 2, so the roots are 2 and 4 and k = 8, so the answer is 8.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Mathematics

58. How many terms of the AP 3, 7, 11, … must be taken so that the sum is 406?
(a) 15 (b) 13 (c) 12 (d) 14
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is dropping a factor while simplifying n/2 × [6 + (n − 1)4] = 406. This reduces to 2n² + n − 406 = 0, giving n = (−1 + 57)/4 = 14, so the answer is 14.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Mathematics

59. The discriminant of 3x² − 2x + 5 = 0 is
(a) −64 (b) 64 (c) 56 (d) −56
Answer: (d)
Solution: The tempting mistake is computing 4 + 60 = 64 by adding instead of subtracting 4ac. D = (−2)² − 4(3)(5) = 4 − 60 = −56, so the answer is −56.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Mathematics

60. The sum of the series 1² + 2² + 3² + … + 10² is
(a) 385 (b) 285 (c) 325 (d) 420
Answer: (a)
Solution: Many students use n(n + 1)/2 = 55, which is the sum of the numbers, not their squares. Sum of squares = n(n + 1)(2n + 1)/6 = 10 × 11 × 21/6 = 385, so the answer is 385.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Mathematics

61. If one root of x² − kx + 12 = 0 is 3, then the other root and k are
(a) 6 and 7 (b) 4 and 7 (c) 4 and 9 (d) 6 and 9
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is finding the other root as 12/3 = 4 but then adding wrongly to get k = 8 or 9. Product of roots = 12 gives the other root as 4, and sum = 3 + 4 = 7 = k, so the answer is 4 and 7.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Mathematics

62. The 10th term of the series 2, 6, 18, 54, … is
(a) 3⁹ (b) 2 × 3¹⁰ (c) 2 × 3⁹ (d) 6 × 3⁸
Answer: (c)
Solution: The common slip is using a × rⁿ instead of a × rⁿ⁻¹, which gives 2 × 3¹⁰. Here a = 2, r = 3, so a₁₀ = 2 × 3⁹, so the answer is 2 × 3⁹.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Mathematics

63. The quadratic equation whose roots are 2 + √3 and 2 − √3 is
(a) x² − 4x + 7 = 0 (b) x² + 4x + 1 = 0 (c) x² − 4x + 1 = 0 (d) x² − 4x − 1 = 0
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is computing the product as 1 but then writing +4x, forgetting the sum enters with a minus sign. Sum = 4 and product = 4 − 3 = 1, so the equation is x² − 4x + 1 = 0, so the answer is x² − 4x + 1 = 0.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Mathematics

64. The sum of the first n terms of an AP is 3n² + 5n. Its common difference is
(a) 3 (b) 6 (c) 8 (d) 5
Answer: (b)
Solution: Many pick 3, the coefficient of n², but the common difference is twice that coefficient. First term = S₁ = 8 and S₂ = 22, so the second term is 14 and d = 14 − 8 = 6, so the answer is 6.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Mathematics

65. If x² − 11x + 28 = 0 and y² − 13y + 42 = 0, then the relation between x and y (taking the larger root of each) is
(a) x > y (b) x < y (c) x ≥ y (d) x = y
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is comparing 4 with 6, the smaller roots, and concluding x < y, but the question fixes the larger roots. The larger root of the first equation is 7 and of the second is also 7, so the answer is x = y.
Source: PYQ-style (IBPS PO pattern)

66. The value of √(12 + √(12 + √(12 + …))) is
(a) 3 (b) 4 (c) 6 (d) 12
Answer: (b)
Solution: The tempting answer is 3, from mishandling the equation as x² = 12. Let x equal the whole expression; then x² = 12 + x, so x² − x − 12 = 0 gives x = 4 or −3, and since x > 0, the answer is 4.
Source: PYQ-style (SSC CGL pattern)

67. The sum of all two-digit numbers divisible by 7 is
(a) 728 (b) 735 (c) 742 (d) 700
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is starting the AP at 7, which is not a two-digit number, or ending at 105. The two-digit multiples of 7 form the AP 14, 21, …, 98 with n = (98 − 14)/7 + 1 = 13 terms, so Sum = 13/2 × (14 + 98) = 13 × 56 = 728, so the answer is 728.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Mathematics

68. If the sum of the roots of ax² + bx + c = 0 equals the sum of the reciprocals of the roots, then
(a) a = b (b) b = c (c) a = c (d) ab = c
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is equating the sum of roots to the product instead of to the sum of reciprocals. Sum of roots = −b/a and sum of reciprocals = (α + β)/αβ = −b/c; equating gives −b/a = −b/c, hence a = c, so the answer is a = c.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Mathematics

69. The next term of the series 2, 3, 5, 9, 17, 33, … is
(a) 65 (b) 66 (c) 63 (d) 64
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is treating it as pure doubling and answering 64, but each term is 2 × previous − 1. Check: 2 × 2 − 1 = 3, 2 × 3 − 1 = 5, …, 2 × 33 − 1 = 65, so the answer is 65.
Source: PYQ-style (SSC pattern)

70. For the equation x² − (k + 2)x + 2k = 0, the roots are
(a) k and −2 (b) −k and −2 (c) 1 and 2k (d) k and 2
Answer: (d)
Solution: The tempting error is factoring as (x + k)(x + 2), which gives the sum with the wrong sign. Factoring: x² − (k + 2)x + 2k = (x − k)(x − 2) = 0, so the roots are k and 2, so the answer is k and 2.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Mathematics

71. The 15th term of an AP is 45 and the 25th term is 75. The first term is
(a) 9 (b) 5 (c) 6 (d) 3
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is computing d = (75 − 45)/25 by dividing by 25 instead of by the gap between the terms. d = (75 − 45)/(25 − 15) = 3, and then a = 45 − 14 × 3 = 3, so the answer is 3.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Mathematics

72. If α and β are roots of x² − 5x + 6 = 0, the equation whose roots are α + 1 and β + 1 is
(a) x² − 5x + 12 = 0 (b) x² − 7x + 10 = 0 (c) x² − 7x + 12 = 0 (d) x² − 6x + 12 = 0
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is keeping the old product 6 and only adding 2 to the sum. New sum = 5 + 2 = 7 and new product = αβ + (α + β) + 1 = 6 + 5 + 1 = 12, giving x² − 7x + 12 = 0, so the answer is x² − 7x + 12 = 0.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Mathematics

73. The sum of the series 5 + 55 + 555 + … up to n terms is 5/81 × [10(10ⁿ − 1) − 9n]. For n = 3, the sum is
(a) 555 (b) 615 (c) 605 (d) 625
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is using rough shortcuts like 3 × 200 instead of adding the actual terms. Directly, 5 + 55 + 555 = 615, and the formula gives 5/81 × [10 × 999 − 27] = 5/81 × 9963 = 615, so the answer is 615.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Mathematics

74. The minimum value of x² − 6x + 13 is
(a) 3 (b) 4 (c) 13 (d) 9
Answer: (b)
Solution: The tempting answer is 13, the constant term, but the minimum occurs at the vertex, not at x = 0. Completing the square gives (x − 3)² + 4, whose minimum is 4 at x = 3, so the answer is 4.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Mathematics

75. In a GP, the sum of the first three terms is 21 and their product is 216. The middle term is
(a) 3 (b) 6 (c) 9 (d) 12
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is dividing 21 by 3 to get 7 as the middle term, but 21 is the sum, not the product. Taking the terms as a/r, a, ar, the product a³ = 216 gives a = 6, and r = 2 satisfies 3 + 6 + 12 = 21, so the answer is 6.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Mathematics
