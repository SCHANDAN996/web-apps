# The Magic of "Undoing" — The Feynman Technique

Richard Feynman (1918–1988) was an American physicist and Nobel Prize winner, famous not only for his science but for his ability to explain difficult ideas in simple words. His learning method — now called the **Feynman Technique** — is one of the most powerful tools for exam preparation. Its core idea is the magic of "undoing": taking a concept apart and rebuilding it in your own simple language, as if you were teaching it to a child.

## The Four Steps

**Step 1: Choose a topic.**
Pick one concept from your syllabus — for example, "Why does the monsoon occur?" or "What is the Fundamental Right to Equality?" Write the topic name at the top of a blank page.

**Step 2: Explain it in simple words.**
Write an explanation as if you were teaching a 12-year-old student. Use plain language, short sentences, and everyday examples. Do not copy the textbook — use your own words.

**Step 3: Find the gaps.**
Wherever you get stuck, hesitate, or have to use vague words like "somehow" or "basically" — that is a gap in your understanding. Go back to your NCERT book or notes and re-study only that part.

**Step 4: Simplify and repeat.**
Rewrite your explanation until it is clear, short, and complete. If you can explain it simply without looking at the book, you truly know it — and you will remember it in the exam hall.

## Why "Undoing" Works

- **It exposes false confidence.** Reading a chapter feels like learning, but explaining it reveals what you actually know.
- **It builds memory.** Rebuilding an idea in your own words creates stronger memory than re-reading.
- **It matches the exam.** SSC, Railway, and Bank exams test whether you understand concepts, not whether you memorised sentences.

## How to Use It for Government Exams

1. After finishing a chapter, close the book and explain the main ideas aloud or on paper.
2. For static GK (rivers, articles of the Constitution, battles), explain *why* a fact is true, not just *what* it is.
3. Keep a "gap notebook": every time Step 3 reveals a weakness, note it and revise it within 24 hours.
4. Practise with a study partner — teaching a friend is the fastest version of this technique.

**Remember:** If you cannot explain it simply, you have not understood it yet. Undo it, rebuild it, and it is yours forever.
