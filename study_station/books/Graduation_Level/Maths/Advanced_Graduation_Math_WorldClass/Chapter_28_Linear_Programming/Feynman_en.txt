# Feynman Technique — Linear Programming

## The Hardest Idea: "Why is the best answer always at a corner?"

Students learn the rule "check the corner points" but do not see why it works. Once you see it, you will never forget it.

## A Story: Meena's Bakery

Meena bakes cakes and cookies. Each cake needs 2 hours of oven time and each tray of cookies needs 1 hour; she has 10 oven hours a day. She also has only 8 kg of flour: a cake uses 1 kg, a tray of cookies 2 kg. A cake earns ₹300 and a tray of cookies ₹200.

Every possible plan — 1 cake and 3 trays, 2 cakes and 2 trays, and so on — is a point on a graph. The plans she can actually make form a shaded shape: the **feasible region**.

Now imagine a ruler lying across the graph for "profit = ₹1,000". Slide it outwards to "₹1,200", "₹1,400"… The profit line keeps moving in the same direction, parallel to itself. The last place it still touches the shaded shape is the best plan. A straight ruler leaving a shape with straight edges always leaves it **at a corner** (or along a whole edge, when two corners tie).

For Meena, the corners are (0, 0), (5, 0), (0, 4) and (4, 2). Profit there is ₹0, ₹1,500, ₹800 and ₹1,600. The best plan is 4 cakes and 2 trays of cookies, earning ₹1,600.

## The Unforgettable Trick: "Slide the ruler, stop at a corner"

1. Draw the constraints and shade the region that obeys all of them.
2. List the corner points (where two boundary lines meet inside the region).
3. Put each corner into Z. The biggest value is the maximum, the smallest is the minimum.
