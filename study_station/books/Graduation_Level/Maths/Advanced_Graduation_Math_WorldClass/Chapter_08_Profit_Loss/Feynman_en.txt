# 🧒 Profit & Loss — Explained Like You're 12

## The Hardest Concept: "Profit % is always on Cost Price — but the question tricks you into using Selling Price!"

Meet **Rohan**, a 12-year-old who runs a lemonade stall. 🍋

Rohan buys lemons, sugar and cups for **₹100** (this is his **Cost Price — CP**, the money that went *out* of his pocket). He sells all the lemonade for **₹120** (this is his **Selling Price — SP**, the money that came *in*).

"How much profit did you make?" asks his friend.

"₹20!" says Rohan proudly.

"And your profit *percent*?"

Rohan thinks: "₹20 out of ₹120 I collected… so 20/120 = 16.67%!"

**WRONG!** ❌ And this is exactly the trap where lakhs of exam candidates fall every year.

## Why is it wrong?

Think of it like this: **Percentage always asks "compared to WHAT?"**

Profit is the *reward* for the money you **risked and spent**. You didn't risk ₹120 — you risked only the ₹100 you took out of your piggy bank. So the profit must be measured against **₹100 (CP)**, not ₹120.

> **Profit % = 20/100 = 20%** ✅

## 🍕 The Pizza Memory Trick

Imagine your **Cost Price is the pizza you BOUGHT**, and profit is the **extra slice** someone gives you free.

If you bought a pizza cut into **10 slices (CP = 10)** and got **2 slices free (profit = 2)**, would you say "I got 2 free out of 12"? No! You'd say "I got 2 free for every 10 I bought" — **2/10 = 20%**.

**The free slices are always counted against what you PAID for, never against the total on your plate.**

## The Golden Rule (tattoo this on your brain 🧠)

> **% ka Baap = CP**
> Every percentage in this chapter — profit%, loss%, discount% (discount% is on Marked Price, the only exception!) — sits on the **base it belongs to**. Profit and loss belong to CP. Always. No exceptions.

## The Exam Trap in Action

Exam question: *"A shopkeeper sells an article for ₹120 and gains 20%. Find CP."*

Trap: Students do 20% of 120 = 24, then 120 − 24 = ₹96. ❌

Feynman way: 20% profit means SP = 120% of CP. So CP = 120 × 100/120 = **₹100**. ✅

See? The number 120 in the question is a **bait** — it looks like the base, but the base is hiding.
