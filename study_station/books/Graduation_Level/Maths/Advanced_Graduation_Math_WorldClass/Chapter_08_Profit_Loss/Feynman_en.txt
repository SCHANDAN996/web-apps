# Feynman Technique: Profit & Loss Intuition

## 1. Why Cost Price Is the Sole Anchor of Reality
Think of Cost Price (CP) as the amount of energy or raw capital you invested into the universe. The Selling Price (SP) is simply the return signal.
- If you planted 100 seeds (CP = 100) and harvested 125 seeds (SP = 125), your harvest rate is 25/100 = 25%.
- If someone says "I made a 20% profit on selling price", they are looking at the harvest, not the seed. 25 seeds profit out of 125 harvest seeds is 20%. But your capital only grew based on the 100 seeds you started with! Always tie profit back to what you originally risked (CP).

## 2. The Illusion of the Marked Price (MP)
Marked Price is a psychological decoy. A shopkeeper marks an article at Rs 200, gives you a "generous" 40% discount, and sells it to you for Rs 120.
- If the original cost was Rs 100, the merchant still enjoys a Rs 20 profit (20%).
- The discount was entirely funded by the artificial markup.
- That is why the equation MP / CP = (100 + P) / (100 - D) is so powerful: it balances the merchant's real greed (Profit above CP) with the buyer's psychological satisfaction (Discount below MP).

## 3. The Mathematics of False Weights
Why does a merchant who gives 900 grams instead of 1,000 grams make an 11.11% profit, and not 10%?
- The merchant only parted with 900 grams of goods (his actual cost).
- He collected money equivalent to 1,000 grams.
- The 100 grams he withheld is pure profit generated on an investment of ONLY 900 grams!
- Profit % = (100 / 900) * 100 = 11.11%. The base of profit is what actually left his inventory, not what he promised on the scale.

## 4. The Equal SP Paradox: Why +20% and -20% Never Break Even
Suppose you sell two cars for Rs 1,20,000 each. On one you make 20% profit, on the other you lose 20%. Why don't they cancel out?
- For the profitable car: CP * 1.20 = 1,20,000 => CP = Rs 1,00,000 (Profit = Rs 20,000).
- For the loss car: CP * 0.80 = 1,20,000 => CP = Rs 1,50,000 (Loss = Rs 30,000).
- Total investment = 1,00,000 + 1,50,000 = Rs 2,50,000. Total revenue = Rs 2,40,000.
- Net loss = Rs 10,000. Net Loss % = (10,000 / 2,50,000) * 100 = 4% = (20/10)^2 %.
- The loss was calculated on a LARGER base (Rs 1,50,000), while the profit was calculated on a SMALLER base (Rs 1,00,000). The heavier loss inevitably dominates!
