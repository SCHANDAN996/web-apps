1. A shopkeeper buys a pen for ₹50 and sells it for ₹65. What is his profit percentage?
(a) 25% (b) 15% (c) 30% (d) 20%
Answer: (c)
Solution: Profit = 65 − 50 = ₹15. Profit % = (15/50) × 100 = 30%. The tempting wrong choice 15% comes from writing the profit amount itself as the percentage without dividing by the cost price, so the answer is 30%.
Source: NCERT Class 7 Mathematics (Comparing Quantities)

2. An article is bought for ₹200 and sold for ₹180. What is the loss percentage?
(a) 11.11% (b) 20% (c) 10% (d) 9%
Answer: (c)
Solution: Loss = 200 − 180 = ₹20. Loss % = (20/200) × 100 = 10%. The trap option 11.11% comes from dividing the loss by the selling price (20/180) instead of the cost price; loss % is always calculated on CP, so the answer is 10%.
Source: NCERT Class 7 Mathematics (Comparing Quantities)

3. If the cost price of an article is ₹400 and it is sold at a profit of 25%, what is the selling price?
(a) ₹425 (b) ₹450 (c) ₹480 (d) ₹500
Answer: (d)
Solution: Profit = 25% of 400 = ₹100. SP = 400 + 100 = ₹500. The trap option ₹425 comes from adding only 25 rupees instead of 25 percent of the cost price, so the answer is ₹500.
Source: NCERT Class 8 Mathematics (Comparing Quantities)

4. A trader sells a chair for ₹720 and gains 20%. What is the cost price?
(a) ₹576 (b) ₹620 (c) ₹600 (d) ₹650
Answer: (c)
Solution: SP = 120% of CP, so CP = 720 × 100/120 = ₹600. The trap option ₹576 comes from subtracting 20% of 720 (i.e., 720 − 144), but the 20% gain is on CP, not on SP, so the answer is ₹600.
Source: NCERT Class 8 Mathematics (Comparing Quantities)

7. By selling an article for ₹550, a man gains 10%. What is his profit in rupees?
(a) ₹60 (b) ₹55 (c) ₹45 (d) ₹50
Answer: (d)
Solution: CP = 550 × 100/110 = ₹500. Profit = 550 − 500 = ₹50. The trap option ₹55 comes from taking 10% of the selling price 550, but gain percent is on CP, so the answer is ₹50.

8. The cost price of 12 articles equals the selling price of 10 articles. Find the profit percentage.
(a) 15% (b) 16.67% (c) 25% (d) 20%
Answer: (d)
Solution: Let CP of 1 article = ₹1. CP of 12 = ₹12 = SP of 10, so SP of 1 = ₹1.20. Profit % = (0.20/1) × 100 = 20%. The trap option 16.67% comes from computing 2/12 instead of 2/10; the gain of 2 articles is measured against the 10 articles' CP base, so the answer is 20%.
Source: PYQ-style (SSC CGL pattern)

9. A shopkeeper sells an article at ₹480, making a profit of 20%. At what price should he sell it to make a profit of 30%?
(a) ₹500 (b) ₹520 (c) ₹510 (d) ₹540
Answer: (b)
Solution: CP = 480 × 100/120 = ₹400. New SP = 400 × 130/100 = ₹520. The trap option ₹540 comes from wrongly adding 30% of 480's base; always work from CP, so the answer is ₹520.
Source: PYQ-style (SSC CHSL pattern)

10. If an article is sold at a gain of 5% instead of a loss of 5%, the seller gets ₹20 more. Find the cost price.
(a) ₹100 (b) ₹200 (c) ₹250 (d) ₹150
Answer: (b)
Solution: The swing between a 5% loss and a 5% gain is 10% of CP, so 10% of CP = ₹20, giving CP = ₹200. The trap option ₹100 comes from treating the swing as only 5% instead of 5% + 5% = 10%, so the answer is ₹200.
Source: PYQ-style (IBPS pattern)

11. A man buys two articles for ₹800 each. He sells one at a profit of 20% and the other at a loss of 20%. What is his overall result?
(a) Loss of ₹64 (b) No profit, no loss (c) Profit of ₹64 (d) Loss of ₹32
Answer: (b)
Solution: SP of the first article = 800 × 1.2 = ₹960; SP of the second = 800 × 0.8 = ₹640. Total SP = ₹1600 = total CP, so there is no profit and no loss. The trap option "loss of ₹64" applies to the different classic case where the two selling prices are equal; here the cost prices are equal, so gains and losses cancel exactly, and the answer is no profit, no loss.
Source: PYQ-style (SSC CGL pattern)

16. A trader marks his goods 25% above cost price and allows a discount of 10%. What is his profit percentage?

Answer: (a)
Solution: Let CP = ₹100. MP = ₹125. SP = 125 × 0.9 = ₹112.50. Profit = 12.5%. The trap option 15% comes from simply subtracting 25 − 10, but the discount acts on the marked price, not the cost price, so the answer is 12.5%.

19. By selling a cycle for ₹2400, a shopkeeper loses 20%. At what price should he sell it to gain 10%?

Answer: (a)

20. A single discount equivalent to two successive discounts of 10% and 20% is:
(a) 32% (b) 30% (c) 25% (d) 28%
Answer: (d)
Solution: Equivalent discount = 10 + 20 − (10 × 20)/100 = 30 − 2 = 28%. The trap option 30% comes from simply adding the two discounts, but the second discount applies to the already reduced price, so the answer is 28%.
Source: PYQ-style (Bank exam pattern)

21. A shopkeeper sells an article at a 10% discount on the marked price and still gains 20%. If the cost price is ₹300, what is the marked price?
(a) ₹400 (b) ₹396 (c) ₹360 (d) ₹420
Answer: (a)
Solution: SP = 300 × 1.2 = ₹360. Since SP = 90% of MP, MP = 360 × 100/90 = ₹400. The trap option ₹396 comes from adding 10% to 360, but the discount of 10% is on MP, so MP must be larger than SP by a factor of 100/90; the answer is ₹400.
Source: PYQ-style (SSC CGL pattern)

22. A man sells an article at a profit of 25%. If he had bought it at 20% less and sold it for ₹10.50 less, he would have gained 30%. Find the original cost price.
(a) ₹50 (b) ₹60 (c) ₹40 (d) ₹45
Answer: (a)
Solution: Let CP = x. Original SP = 1.25x. New CP = 0.8x; new SP = 0.8x × 1.3 = 1.04x. Given 1.25x − 1.04x = 10.50, so 0.21x = 10.50, giving x = ₹50. The trap option ₹60 comes from mis-setting the new SP as 1.3x instead of 1.3 × 0.8x, so the answer is ₹50.
Source: PYQ-style (IBPS pattern)

23. A dishonest dealer sells goods at cost price but uses a weight of 800 g instead of 1 kg. What is his profit percentage?
(a) 20% (b) 25% (c) 15% (d) 22.5%
Answer: (b)
Solution: He charges for 1000 g but gives goods costing only 800 g. Profit % = (200/800) × 100 = 25%. The trap option 20% comes from dividing 200 by 1000; profit is measured on what he actually spent (800 g worth), not on what he claims, so the answer is 25%.
Source: PYQ-style (SSC CGL pattern)

24. A trader allows a discount of 15% on the marked price. To gain 19%, by what percentage above the cost price should he mark the goods?
(a) 35% (b) 30% (c) 34% (d) 40%
Answer: (d)
Solution: Let CP = ₹100. Required SP = ₹119. Since SP = 85% of MP, MP = 119 × 100/85 = ₹140. Mark-up = 40%. The trap option 34% comes from adding 19 + 15, but the discount is on MP while the gain is on CP, so they cannot be added directly; the answer is 40%.
Source: PYQ-style (Bank PO pattern)

25. A person buys 60 oranges for ₹100 and sells 50 oranges for ₹100. What is his profit or loss percentage?
(a) 20% profit (b) 20% loss (c) 16.67% profit (d) 25% profit
Answer: (a)
Solution: CP per orange = 100/60 = ₹5/3; SP per orange = 100/50 = ₹2. Profit per orange = 2 − 5/3 = ₹1/3. Profit % = (1/3)/(5/3) × 100 = 20%. The trap option 16.67% comes from dividing the profit by the selling price per orange; profit % is always on CP, so the answer is 20% profit.
Source: PYQ-style (SSC pattern)
