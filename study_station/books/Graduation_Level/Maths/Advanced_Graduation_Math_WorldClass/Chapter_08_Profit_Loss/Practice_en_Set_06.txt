126. A trader marks his goods 40% above cost price and gives two successive discounts of 10% and 20%. What is his profit or loss percentage?
(a) 0.8% loss (b) 0.8% profit (c) 2% profit (d) 2% loss
Answer: (b)
Solution: The tempting error is to add the discounts as 30% and compute 140 − 30% = 98, i.e. a 2% loss, but successive discounts multiply, not add. Effective price = 140 × 0.9 × 0.8 = 100.8, so the answer is 0.8% profit.
Source: PYQ-style

127. A shopkeeper sells an article at ₹720 after a discount of 20% on the marked price and still gains 20%. If he sells at the marked price, what is the profit percentage?
(a) 50% (b) 45% (c) 60% (d) 40%
Answer: (a)
Solution: Many wrongly add 20% + 20% = 40%. Marked price = 720/0.8 = ₹900 and cost price = 720/1.2 = ₹600. Selling at the marked price gives profit = 300/600 = 50%, so the answer is 50%.
Source: PYQ-style

128. A dishonest dealer uses a weight of 900 g instead of 1 kg and also marks up his goods 20% above cost, then gives a 10% discount. Find his actual profit percentage.
(a) 20% (b) 24% (c) 36% (d) 32%
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is to ignore the false weight and get 8% from 120 × 0.9 = 108. Let the cost of 1 kg be ₹100; the dealer delivers 900 g costing ₹90 but charges 100 × 1.2 × 0.9 = ₹108. Profit = 18 on 90 = 20%, so the answer is 20%.
Source: PYQ-style

129. The cost price of two articles is the same. One is sold at 20% profit and the other at 20% loss. The difference between the selling prices is ₹400. Find the cost price of each article.
(a) ₹800 (b) ₹1000 (c) ₹1200 (d) ₹2000
Answer: (b)
Solution: A common slip is to take 400 as 20% of CP giving ₹2000, but the gap between 120% and 80% of CP is 40% of CP. So 0.4 × CP = 400, giving CP = ₹1000, so the answer is ₹1000.
Source: PYQ-style

130. A man buys two articles for ₹1800 each. He sells one at 30% profit and the other at 20% loss. What is his overall profit or loss percentage?
(a) No profit no loss (b) 10% profit (c) 5% loss (d) 5% profit
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is misreading the average of 30 and 20 as 10% profit; since the cost prices are equal, the net result is the average of +30% and −20%, which is +5%. Net = (30 − 20)/2 = 5% profit, so the answer is 5% profit.
Source: PYQ-style

131. An article is sold at a profit of 25%. If the cost price were 20% less and the selling price ₹60 less, the profit would be 25%. Find the original cost price.
(a) ₹300 (b) ₹200 (c) ₹400 (d) ₹240
Answer: (d)
Solution: Let CP = x; then SP = 1.25x. New CP = 0.8x and new SP = 1.25x − 60 must equal 1.25 × 0.8x = x, so 0.25x = 60 and x = 240. Verify: SP = 300, new CP = 192, new SP = 240, profit = 48/192 = 25%, so the answer is ₹240.
Source: PYQ-style

132. A trader sells an article at 10% loss. If he had sold it for ₹54 more, he would have gained 8%. Find the cost price.
(a) ₹300 (b) ₹250 (c) ₹350 (d) ₹400
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is dividing 54 by 10% or 8% separately. The difference between an 8% gain and a 10% loss is 18% of CP, so 0.18 × CP = 54, giving CP = ₹300, so the answer is ₹300.

133. A wholesaler sells 30 pens at the marked price of 20 pens to a retailer, who then sells them at the marked price. Find the retailer's profit percentage.
(a) 40% (b) 45% (c) 50% (d) 60%
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is taking 10/30 = 33.3% as the profit. The retailer pays for 20 pens and receives 30; with marked price m, cost = 20m and revenue = 30m. Profit = 10m/20m = 50%, so the answer is 50%.

Answer: (d)

135. By selling 33 metres of cloth, a shopkeeper gains the selling price of 11 metres. Find his profit percentage.
(a) 25% (b) 66.67% (c) 50% (d) 33.33%
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is 11/33 = 33.33%, but the gain equals the SP of 11 m while the cost base is the SP of 33 m minus the gain, i.e. the SP of 22 m. Profit% = 11/22 = 50%, so the answer is 50%.
Source: PYQ-style

136. A shopkeeper cheats by 10% while buying (taking 1100 g for the price of 1000 g) and by 10% while selling (giving 900 g for the price of 1000 g). Find his total profit percentage.
(a) 22.22% (b) 21% (c) 20% (d) 25%
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is adding 10% + 10% = 20% or multiplying 1.1 × 1.1 = 1.21 to get 21%. He pays the cost of 1000 g but receives 1100 g, and for every 900 g he delivers he charges the price of 1000 g; effective gain factor = 1100/900 = 11/9. Profit = 2/9 = 22.22%, so the answer is 22.22%.
Source: PYQ-style

137. The marked price of an article is ₹500. After two successive discounts of 20% and x%, it is sold for ₹320. Find x.
(a) 20% (b) 30% (c) 25% (d) 15%
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is assuming the total discount of 36% splits as 20% + 16%. After the first discount the price is 500 × 0.8 = 400; then 400(1 − x/100) = 320 gives 1 − x/100 = 0.8, so x = 20, so the answer is 20%.
Source: PYQ-style

138. A single discount equivalent to three successive discounts of 20%, 25% and 10% is:
(a) 55% (b) 45% (c) 50% (d) 46%
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is adding 20 + 25 + 10 = 55%. Net factor = 0.8 × 0.75 × 0.9 = 0.54, so the single discount = 100 − 54 = 46%, so the answer is 46%.
Source: PYQ-style

139. A man sells an article at 5% above cost price. If he had bought it at 5% less and sold it for ₹2 less, he would have gained 10%. Find the original cost price.
(a) ₹200 (b) ₹150 (c) ₹100 (d) ₹400
Answer: (d)
Solution: Let CP = x; then SP = 1.05x. New CP = 0.95x and new SP = 1.05x − 2 must equal 1.1 × 0.95x = 1.045x, so 0.005x = 2 and x = 400. Verify: SP = 420, new CP = 380, new SP = 418, gain = 38/380 = 10%, so the answer is ₹400.
Source: PYQ-style

140. A trader allows a discount of 15% on the marked price and still makes a profit of 19%. By what percentage is the marked price above the cost price?
(a) 35% (b) 38% (c) 40% (d) 42%
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is adding 15 + 19 = 34%. MP × 0.85 = CP × 1.19, so MP/CP = 1.19/0.85 = 1.4, i.e. the marked price is 40% above cost, so the answer is 40%.

146. A man sells two horses for ₹9900 each, gaining 10% on one and losing 10% on the other. His overall result is:
(a) 1% loss (b) 1% profit (c) 2% loss (d) No profit no loss
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is assuming the equal gain and loss cancel out. When the selling prices are equal with x% gain and x% loss, there is always a loss of x²/100 % = 100/100 = 1%, so the answer is 1% loss.
Source: PYQ-style

149. A shopkeeper gives 4 articles free on the purchase of 12 articles and still earns a profit of 20%. By what percentage is the marked price above the cost price?
(a) 50% (b) 40% (c) 60% (d) 75%
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is treating 4/16 = 25% as the discount and adding 20% to get 45%. The buyer pays for 12 but gets 16, so SP per article = 12m/16 = 0.75m where m is the marked price. Given 0.75m = 1.2 × CP, m = 1.6 × CP, i.e. 60% above, so the answer is 60%.
Source: PYQ-style

150. A man bought an article and sold it at a gain of 10%. If he had bought it at 20% less and sold it for ₹10 more, the gain would have been 40%. Find the original cost price.
(a) ₹400 (b) ₹500 (c) ₹600 (d) ₹800
Answer: (b)
Solution: Let CP = x; then SP = 1.1x. New CP = 0.8x and new SP = 1.1x + 10 must equal 1.4 × 0.8x = 1.12x, so 0.02x = 10 and x = 500. Verify: SP = 550, new CP = 400, new SP = 560, gain = 160/400 = 40%, so the answer is ₹500.
Source: PYQ-style
