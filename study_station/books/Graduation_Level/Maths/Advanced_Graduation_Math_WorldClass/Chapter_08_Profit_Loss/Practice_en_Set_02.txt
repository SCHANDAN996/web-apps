26. A shopkeeper buys an article for ₹800 and marks it 25% above the cost price. If he allows a discount of 10% on the marked price, what is his profit percentage?
(a) 11.25% (b) 10% (c) 15% (d) 12.5%
Answer: (d)
Solution: The tempting wrong answer is 15%, obtained by simply subtracting 10% from 25%, but the discount applies to the marked price, not the cost price. Marked price = 800 × 1.25 = ₹1000; selling price = 1000 × 0.90 = ₹900. Profit = 900 − 800 = ₹100 on ₹800, so the answer is 12.5%.
Source: NCERT Class 8 Mathematics (Comparing Quantities)

27. A trader sells two articles at ₹990 each. On one he gains 10% and on the other he loses 10%. What is his overall result?
(a) No profit, no loss (b) Profit of 1% (c) Loss of 1% (d) Loss of 2%
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is assuming equal gain and loss cancel out to zero, but the cost prices differ. CP of first = 990/1.10 = ₹900; CP of second = 990/0.90 = ₹1100. Total CP = ₹2000, total SP = ₹1980, so loss = 20/2000 = 1%, so the answer is a loss of 1%.
Source: PYQ-style

28. By selling an article for ₹720, a man loses 10%. At what price should he sell it to gain 20%?
(a) ₹880 (b) ₹960 (c) ₹900 (d) ₹1000
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is computing 720 × 1.20 = ₹864, ignoring that the 10% loss is measured on the cost price, not on ₹720. CP = 720/0.90 = ₹800. Required SP = 800 × 1.20, so the answer is ₹960.
Source: NCERT Class 8 Mathematics (Comparing Quantities)

29. The cost price of 15 articles equals the selling price of 12 articles. What is the profit percentage?
(a) 20% (b) 25% (c) 30% (d) 15%
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is taking the difference 3 over 15, giving 20%, but profit must be measured on the cost base. Let CP of each article be ₹1; then SP of 12 articles = ₹15, so SP per article = ₹1.25. Profit = ₹0.25 on CP ₹1, so the answer is 25%.
Source: PYQ-style

30. A dishonest dealer sells goods at cost price but uses a weight of 900 g instead of 1 kg. What is his profit percentage?
(a) 10% (b) 11.11% (c) 9.09% (d) 12.5%
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is answering 10%, taking the shortfall over 1000 g, but profit is reckoned on what the dealer actually spends. He pays for 900 g but charges for 1000 g, so profit = 100/900 × 100, so the answer is 11.11%.

31. A shopkeeper marks his goods 40% above cost price and allows a discount of 15%. What is his profit percentage?
(a) 19% (b) 25% (c) 21% (d) 16%
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is subtracting 15 from 40 to get 25%, but the discount acts on the inflated marked price. Let CP = ₹100; MP = ₹140; SP = 140 × 0.85 = ₹119, so the answer is 19%.

32. An article is sold at a profit of 12%. Had it been sold for ₹54 more, the profit would have been 21%. What is the cost price?
(a) ₹500 (b) ₹650 (c) ₹540 (d) ₹600
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is dividing ₹54 by 21% or 12% instead of by the difference of the two profit rates. The extra ₹54 corresponds to 21% − 12% = 9% of CP, so CP = 54 × 100/9, so the answer is ₹600.
Source: PYQ-style

33. A man buys 11 oranges for ₹10 and sells 10 oranges for ₹11. What is his profit percentage?
(a) 21% (b) 19% (c) 20% (d) 22%
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is quoting 10% by comparing ₹10 and ₹11 directly, ignoring the different quantities. CP per orange = 10/11 and SP per orange = 11/10; profit % = [(121 − 100)/110] ÷ (10/11) × 100 = 21%, so the answer is 21%.
Source: PYQ-style

34. A retailer buys a washing machine for ₹15,000 and spends ₹1,500 on transport and ₹500 on installation. At what price must he sell it to gain 20%?
(a) ₹18,000 (b) ₹21,000 (c) ₹20,400 (d) ₹19,800
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is applying 20% to ₹15,000 alone, giving ₹18,000, but overheads are part of the effective cost price. Effective CP = 15000 + 1500 + 500 = ₹17,000; SP = 17000 × 1.20, so the answer is ₹20,400.
Source: NCERT Class 8 Mathematics (Comparing Quantities)

35. A trader allows a discount of 20% on the marked price and still makes a profit of 25%. By what percentage is the marked price above the cost price?
(a) 50% (b) 60% (c) 56.25% (d) 45%
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is adding 20% and 25% to get 45%, but the two percentages act on different bases. Let CP = ₹100; SP = ₹125; since SP = 0.80 × MP, MP = 125/0.80 = ₹156.25, so the answer is 56.25%.
Source: PYQ-style

36. Selling an article for ₹1,140 earns a profit percentage equal in magnitude to the loss percentage earned by selling it for ₹1,260. What is the cost price?
(a) ₹1,150 (b) ₹1,220 (c) ₹1,180 (d) ₹1,200
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is trying to set up two separate equations; when gain% equals loss% in magnitude, the CP is simply the midpoint of the two selling prices. CP = (1140 + 1260)/2, so the answer is ₹1,200.
Source: PYQ-style

37. A shopkeeper sells an article at a 10% discount and gains 8%. If the marked price is ₹600, what is the cost price?
(a) ₹480 (b) ₹500 (c) ₹520 (d) ₹540
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is reducing ₹600 by 10% and then by 8% again, mixing up the bases. SP = 600 × 0.90 = ₹540; CP = 540/1.08, so the answer is ₹500.

38. A person sold an article at a loss of 8%. Had he sold it for ₹144 more, he would have gained 10%. What is the cost price?

Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is dividing ₹144 by 10% or 8% alone; the ₹144 bridges the gap from −8% to +10%, i.e. 18% of CP. CP = 144 × 100/18, so the answer is ₹800.

39. The profit earned by selling an article for ₹1,050 is equal to the loss incurred by selling it for ₹850. What is the cost price?
(a) ₹950 (b) ₹975 (c) ₹925 (d) ₹1,000
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is averaging the profit and loss amounts instead of the prices; equal profit and loss means the CP lies exactly midway between the two selling prices. CP = (1050 + 850)/2, so the answer is ₹950.
Source: PYQ-style

40. A grocer mixes 20 kg of rice at ₹40 per kg with 30 kg of rice at ₹60 per kg and sells the mixture at ₹62.40 per kg. What is his profit percentage?
(a) 18% (b) 20% (c) 22% (d) 24%
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is averaging the two cost rates to ₹50 per kg, which ignores the unequal quantities. Total CP = 20 × 40 + 30 × 60 = 800 + 1800 = ₹2,600 for 50 kg, i.e. ₹52 per kg. Profit = 62.40 − 52 = ₹10.40 on ₹52 = 20%, so the answer is 20%.
Source: PYQ-style

41. A trader sells an article at a profit of 20%. If he had bought it at 20% less and sold it for ₹75 less, he would have gained 25%. What is the original cost price?
(a) ₹300 (b) ₹350 (c) ₹375 (d) ₹400
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is applying the 25% gain to the original cost price instead of the reduced cost price. Let CP = x; original SP = 1.2x; new CP = 0.8x and new SP = 1.2x − 75 = 1.25 × 0.8x = x. Solving 1.2x − 75 = x gives 0.2x = 75, so the answer is ₹375.
Source: PYQ-style

42. A shopkeeper offers three successive discounts of 10%, 20% and 25%. What is the single equivalent discount?
(a) 55% (b) 50% (c) 48% (d) 46%
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is adding the discounts to get 55%, but each successive discount acts on the already reduced price. Net price factor = 0.90 × 0.80 × 0.75 = 0.54, so equivalent discount = 46%, so the answer is 46%.

43. A man buys a cycle for ₹1,400 and sells it at a loss of 15%. What is the selling price?
(a) ₹1,190 (b) ₹1,210 (c) ₹1,160 (d) ₹1,240
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is computing 15% of ₹1,400 as ₹200 instead of ₹210, a common arithmetic slip. Loss = 1400 × 0.15 = ₹210; SP = 1400 − 210, so the answer is ₹1,190.

44. By selling 45 lemons for ₹40, a man loses 20%. How many lemons should he sell for ₹24 to gain 20%?
(a) 16 (b) 22 (c) 20 (d) 18
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is scaling the lemon count directly with price, ignoring the change from loss to gain. CP of 45 lemons = 40/0.8 = ₹50, so CP per lemon = ₹10/9; to gain 20%, SP per lemon = (10/9) × 1.2 = ₹4/3. Number sold for ₹24 = 24 ÷ (4/3), so the answer is 18.
Source: PYQ-style

45. A dealer marks his goods 25% above cost price and gives one article free with every 9 articles sold. What is his profit percentage?
(a) 15% (b) 12.5% (c) 10% (d) 11.11%
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is ignoring the free article and quoting 25%, but the free article raises the effective cost. Let CP per article = ₹100; for every 10 articles handed over, cost = ₹1,000 while only 9 are paid for at ₹125 each, giving revenue ₹1,125. Profit = 125/1000, so the answer is 12.5%.
Source: PYQ-style

46. An article was sold for ₹2,400 at a profit of 20%. If the cost price had been 10% lower and the selling price unchanged, what would the profit percentage be?

Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is adding 10% to 20% to get 30%, but the lower cost price changes the base of the profit ratio. Original CP = 2400/1.2 = ₹2,000; new CP = 2000 × 0.9 = ₹1,800. Profit = 600/1800, so the answer is 33.33%.

50. A shopkeeper cheats by 10% while buying (takes 1100 g for the price of 1000 g) and cheats by 10% while selling (gives 900 g for the price of 1000 g). What is his overall profit percentage?
(a) 21% (b) 20% (c) 25% (d) 22.22%
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is adding the two cheats to get 20%, but the gains compound on different quantities. Suppose goods cost ₹1 per gram; he pays ₹1,000 and receives 1,100 g, and he charges ₹1,000 for every 900 g, so for 1,100 g he charges 1000 × 1100/900 ≈ ₹1,222.22. Profit ≈ 222.22/1000, so the answer is 22.22%.
Source: PYQ-style
