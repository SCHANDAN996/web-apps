Q51. (Medium) A shopkeeper marks his goods at 40% above the cost price and allows a discount of 25% on the marked price. In addition, for cash payment, he gives an extra discount of 10%. What is his overall profit or loss percentage?
(A) 5.5% loss  (B) 5.5% gain  (C) 5% loss  (D) 4.5% loss

Answer: (A)

Solution:
Assuming Cost Price = 100. Marked Price = 140. Applying first discount of 25%: Price becomes 140 * 0.75 = 105. Applying cash discount of 10%: Selling Price = 105 * 0.90 = 94.50. Net result = 94.50 - 100 = -5.50, which is a 5.5% loss. Therefore, the correct value is 5.5% loss, corresponding to option (A).

Q52. (Medium) A cloth merchant uses a metre rod that is actually 90 cm long while measuring cloth sold. If he claims to sell cloth at cost price, what is his profit percentage?
(A) 10%  (B) 11.11%  (C) 12.5%  (D) 9.09%

Answer: (B)

Solution:
The customer pays for 100 cm, but receives only 90 cm. The merchant's outlay is for 90 cm, while his return is for 100 cm. Profit = 100 - 90 = 10 cm on a base of 90 cm. Profit percentage = (10 / 90) * 100 = 100 / 9 = 11.11%. Psychological Trap: Examinees often divide 10 by 100 to get 10%, forgetting that the 90 cm actually given constitutes the true cost outlay. Therefore, the correct value is 11.11%, corresponding to option (B).

Q53. (Medium) A trader buys two horses for Rs 19,500. He sells one at a loss of 20% and the other at a gain of 15%. If each horse is sold for the same price, find the cost price of the horse sold at a loss.
(A) Rs 8,000  (B) Rs 11,000  (C) Rs 11,500  (D) Rs 12,000

Answer: (C)

Solution:
Let cost prices be C1 and C2. Since Selling Prices are equal: 0.80 * C1 = 1.15 * C2 => C1 / C2 = 1.15 / 0.80 = 23 / 16. Total ratio parts = 23 + 16 = 39 parts = Rs 19500 => 1 part = Rs 500. Cost Price of horse sold at a loss = 23 * 500 = Rs 11,500. Therefore, the correct value is Rs 11,500, corresponding to option (C).
