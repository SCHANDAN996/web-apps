51. A trader marks his goods 40% above the cost price and allows a discount of 10% on the marked price. What is his profit percentage?
(a) 30% (b) 24% (c) 28% (d) 26%
Answer: (d)
Solution: The tempting wrong answer is 30%, obtained by simply subtracting 40 − 10, but discount applies to the marked price, not the cost price. Let CP = 100, so MP = 140 and SP = 140 × 0.90 = 126, so the answer is 26%.
Source: PYQ-style

52. A shopkeeper sells an article at ₹720 after giving a discount of 20% on the marked price. If he still earns a profit of 25%, what is the cost price of the article?
(a) ₹540 (b) ₹600 (c) ₹640 (d) ₹576
Answer: (d)
Solution: Many students wrongly divide 720 by 1.20, but 20% is the discount on the marked price, not the profit rate. SP = ₹720 and profit = 25%, so CP = 720 ÷ 1.25, so the answer is ₹576.
Source: PYQ-style

53. By selling an article for ₹1,440, a man loses 10%. At what price should he sell it to gain 15%?
(a) ₹1,760 (b) ₹1,800 (c) ₹1,920 (d) ₹1,840
Answer: (d)
Solution: A common trap is to add 10% and 15% to ₹1,440 directly, ignoring that the base changes. CP = 1440 ÷ 0.90 = ₹1,600 and required SP = 1600 × 1.15, so the answer is ₹1,840.
Source: PYQ-style

54. The cost price of 20 articles equals the selling price of 16 articles. What is the profit percentage?
(a) 20% (b) 25% (c) 30% (d) 33⅓%
Answer: (b)
Solution: Students often take 4/20 = 20%, but profit is calculated on the cost price of the articles sold. Let CP of each article be ₹1; then SP of one article = 20/16 = ₹1.25, giving a profit of 0.25 on ₹1, so the answer is 25%.
Source: PYQ-style

55. A dishonest dealer sells goods at cost price but uses a weight of 900 g instead of 1 kg. What is his profit percentage?
(a) 10% (b) 9⅑% (c) 11⅑% (d) 12½%
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap answer is 10%, which treats the 100 g shortfall as a fraction of 1000 g; profit must be measured on what the dealer actually spends. He pays for 900 g but is paid for 1000 g, so profit = 100/900 × 100, so the answer is 11⅑%.
Source: PYQ-style

56. An article is sold at a profit of 20%. If both the cost price and selling price were ₹100 less, the profit would be 4% more. Find the cost price.
(a) ₹500 (b) ₹700 (c) ₹800 (d) ₹600
Answer: (d)
Solution: Let CP = x, so SP = 1.2x; the new condition gives 1.2x − 100 = 1.24(x − 100). Solving, 0.04x = 24, so the answer is ₹600.
Source: PYQ-style

57. A man buys two articles for ₹1,800 each. He sells one at a profit of 20% and the other at a loss of 20%. What is his overall result?
(a) No profit, no loss (b) Profit of 4% (c) Loss of 4% (d) Profit of 2%
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is to assume a 4% loss by analogy with the equal-selling-price rule, but here the cost prices are equal, not the selling prices. Total CP = ₹3,600 and total SP = 2160 + 1440 = ₹3,600, so the answer is no profit, no loss.
Source: PYQ-style

58. Two articles are sold at ₹990 each. On one there is a gain of 10% and on the other a loss of 10%. What is the overall result?
(a) No profit, no loss (b) Loss of 1% (c) Gain of 1% (d) Loss of 2%
Answer: (b)
Solution: The tempting answer is no profit no loss, but with equal selling prices and equal opposite percentages there is always a loss of rate²/100 percent. Here loss = 10²/100 = 1% (CPs are 900 and 1100, total CP 2000, total SP 1980), so the answer is a loss of 1%.
Source: PYQ-style

59. A trader allows two successive discounts of 20% and 10%. What single discount is equivalent to these?
(a) 30% (b) 26% (c) 32% (d) 28%
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is to add the discounts to get 30%, but the second discount applies to the already reduced price. Net SP factor = 0.80 × 0.90 = 0.72, so the equivalent single discount is 1 − 0.72, so the answer is 28%.
Source: PYQ-style

60. A shopkeeper marks his goods 25% above cost price. What maximum discount can he offer so that he neither gains nor loses?
(a) 18% (b) 20% (c) 22% (d) 25%
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap answer is 25%, but the discount is on the marked price, which is larger than the cost price. Let CP = 100 and MP = 125; for no profit no loss SP = 100, so discount = 25 on 125, so the answer is 20%.
Source: PYQ-style

61. A retailer buys 40 pens for the price of 36 pens and sells each pen at the price he paid per pen in the deal. What is his profit percentage?
(a) 11⅑% (b) 10% (c) 12½% (d) 9%
Answer: (a)
Solution: Suppose the deal price per pen is ₹1; then he pays ₹36 for 40 pens, so CP per pen = ₹0.90 while SP = ₹1. Profit = 0.10/0.90 × 100, so the answer is 11⅑%.
Source: PYQ-style

62. The selling price of an article is ⅞ of its marked price. If the cost price is ₹560 and the marked price is ₹800, what is the profit percentage?
(a) 22% (b) 25% (c) 20% (d) 28%
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is computing profit on the marked price, which would wrongly give 17.5%. SP = ⅞ × 800 = ₹700, profit = 700 − 560 = ₹140, and 140/560 × 100, so the answer is 25%.
Source: PYQ-style

63. A man sold an article at a loss of 8%. Had he sold it for ₹72 more, he would have gained 10%. Find the cost price.
(a) ₹360 (b) ₹450 (c) ₹400 (d) ₹480
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is treating the 18% gap as a percentage of the selling price; it is 10 + 8 = 18% of the cost price. So CP = 72 ÷ 0.18, so the answer is ₹400.
Source: PYQ-style

64. A fruit vendor buys oranges at 6 for ₹10 and sells them at 4 for ₹9. What is his profit percentage?
(a) 30% (b) 35% (c) 40% (d) 32%
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is comparing single-fruit prices without a common quantity; use the LCM of 6 and 4, i.e. 12 oranges. CP of 12 = ₹20 and SP of 12 = 3 × 9 = ₹27, giving profit 7 on 20, so the answer is 35%.
Source: PYQ-style

65. A trader sells an article at ₹2,300, earning a profit equal to 25% of the selling price. What is the cost price?
(a) ₹1,725 (b) ₹1,610 (c) ₹1,840 (d) ₹1,750
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is treating the 25% as being on the cost price; here it is on the selling price. Profit = 25% of 2300 = ₹575, so CP = 2300 − 575, so the answer is ₹1,725.
Source: PYQ-style

66. A wholesaler allows a discount of 15% on the marked price and still makes a profit of 19%. By what percentage is the marked price above the cost price?
(a) 35% (b) 38% (c) 40% (d) 42%
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is adding 15 + 19 = 34%, which ignores the different bases. Let CP = 100, so SP = 119 and MP = 119 ÷ 0.85 = 140, so the answer is 40%.
Source: PYQ-style

67. A man buys a cycle for ₹1,400 and sells it at a loss of 15%. With the money received, he buys another cycle and sells it at a gain of 15%. What is his overall gain or loss?
(a) Loss of ₹21 (b) No profit, no loss (c) Gain of ₹31.50 (d) Loss of ₹31.50
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is assuming the two 15% changes cancel out; they do not because the bases differ. First SP = 1400 × 0.85 = ₹1,190 and second SP = 1190 × 1.15 = ₹1,368.50, so the answer is a loss of ₹31.50.
Source: PYQ-style

68. The ratio of cost price to selling price of an article is 5 : 6. What is the profit percentage?
(a) 16⅔% (b) 25% (c) 20% (d) 18%
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is computing 1/6 = 16⅔%, which wrongly uses the selling price as the base. Profit = 6 − 5 = 1 part on CP of 5 parts, giving 1/5 × 100, so the answer is 20%.
Source: PYQ-style

69. A shopkeeper cheats by 10% while buying (takes 1100 g for the price of 1000 g) and also cheats by 10% while selling (gives 900 g for the price of 1000 g). What is his overall profit percentage?
(a) 22²⁄₉% (b) 21% (c) 20% (d) 25%
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is adding 10 + 10 = 20%. He receives 1100 g for the price of 1000 g and sells only 900 g for the price of 1000 g, so profit = (1100 − 900)/900 × 100 = 200/9, so the answer is 22²⁄₉%.
Source: PYQ-style

70. An article was sold for ₹1,150 after a discount, yielding a profit of 15%. If no discount had been given, the profit would have been 30%. What was the marked price?
(a) ₹1,250 (b) ₹1,300 (c) ₹1,350 (d) ₹1,400
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is applying 30% to ₹1,150 instead of first finding the cost price. CP = 1150 ÷ 1.15 = ₹1,000, and without discount SP = MP = 1000 × 1.30, so the answer is ₹1,300.
Source: PYQ-style

71. A trader mixes 20 kg of rice at ₹30 per kg with 30 kg of rice at ₹40 per kg and sells the mixture at ₹42 per kg. What is his profit percentage?
(a) 18% (b) 15% (c) 16⅔% (d) 20%
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is averaging the prices as ₹35 instead of using the weighted mean. Total CP = 600 + 1200 = ₹1,800 for 50 kg, so CP per kg = ₹36 and profit = 6/36 × 100, so the answer is 16⅔%.
Source: PYQ-style

72. A man sells an article at a profit of 12%. If he had bought it at 10% less and sold it for ₹37.50 more, he would have gained 30%. Find the original cost price.
(a) ₹750 (b) ₹650 (c) ₹700 (d) ₹600
Answer: (a)
Solution: Let CP = x; original SP = 1.12x, new CP = 0.9x, and the new SP must be 1.30 × 0.9x = 1.17x. Then 1.17x − 1.12x = 37.50 gives 0.05x = 37.50, so the answer is ₹750.
Source: PYQ-style

73. A dealer offers a 10% discount on a sofa and still earns a profit of 20%. If the cost price is ₹2,400, what is the marked price?
(a) ₹3,200 (b) ₹3,000 (c) ₹3,100 (d) ₹3,300
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is adding 20% and 10% to get 2400 × 1.30 = ₹3,120, which mixes the bases. SP = 2400 × 1.20 = ₹2,880 and MP = 2880 ÷ 0.90, so the answer is ₹3,200.
Source: PYQ-style

74. By selling 45 lemons, a vendor gains the selling price of 5 lemons. What is his profit percentage?
(a) 12½% (b) 11⅑% (c) 9% (d) 10%
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is computing 5/45 = 11⅑%, which uses the wrong base. Gain = SP of 5 lemons, so CP of 45 = SP of 40 lemons; taking SP of one lemon as ₹1, profit = ₹5 on ₹40, so the answer is 12½%.
Source: PYQ-style

75. A shopkeeper buys an article for ₹800. He wants a profit of 20% after allowing a discount of 20% on the marked price. At what price should he mark the article?
(a) ₹1,150 (b) ₹1,250 (c) ₹1,200 (d) ₹1,280
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is marking at 800 × 1.40 = ₹1,120 by adding the two percentages, which ignores that the discount is on the marked price. Required SP = 800 × 1.20 = ₹960 and MP = 960 ÷ 0.80, so the answer is ₹1,200.
Source: PYQ-style
