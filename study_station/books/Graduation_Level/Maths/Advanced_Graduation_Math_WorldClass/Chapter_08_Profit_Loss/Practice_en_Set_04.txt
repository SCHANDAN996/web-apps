76. A shopkeeper buys an article for ₹800 and marks it 40% above the cost price. He then gives a discount of 10% on the marked price. What is his profit percentage?
(a) 26% (b) 28% (c) 24% (d) 30%
Answer: (a)
Solution: The tempting wrong answer 30% comes from simply subtracting 40 − 10, which ignores that the discount applies to the marked price, not the cost. Marked price = 800 × 1.40 = ₹1120. Selling price = 1120 × 0.90 = ₹1008. Profit = 1008 − 800 = ₹208, so profit % = 208/800 × 100 = 26%, so the answer is 26%.
Source: NCERT Class 8 Mathematics (Comparing Quantities)

77. A trader sells two articles at ₹990 each. On one he gains 10% and on the other he loses 10%. What is his overall result?
(a) No profit, no loss (b) Gain of 1% (c) Loss of 1% (d) Loss of 2%
Answer: (c)
Solution: Many assume equal gain and loss cancel out, giving no profit no loss — that is the trap, because the cost prices differ. CP of first = 990/1.10 = ₹900; CP of second = 990/0.90 = ₹1100. Total CP = ₹2000, total SP = ₹1980. Loss = ₹20 on ₹2000 = 1%, so the answer is a loss of 1%.
Source: PYQ-style (SSC CGL pattern)

78. By selling an article for ₹720, a man loses 10%. At what price should he sell it to gain 15%?
(a) ₹960 (b) ₹900 (c) ₹940 (d) ₹920
Answer: (d)
Solution: A common error is computing 720 × 1.15 = ₹828, which applies the gain to the selling price instead of the cost price. CP = 720/0.90 = ₹800. Required SP = 800 × 1.15 = ₹920, so the answer is ₹920.
Source: NCERT Class 8 Mathematics (Comparing Quantities)

79. The cost price of 20 articles equals the selling price of 16 articles. What is the profit percentage?
(a) 20% (b) 25% (c) 30% (d) 16⅔%
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap answer 20% comes from 4/20, but profit is always reckoned on the cost price of the articles sold. Let CP of each article be ₹1. CP of 16 articles = ₹16, SP of 16 articles = CP of 20 = ₹20. Profit = ₹4 on ₹16 = 25%, so the answer is 25%.
Source: PYQ-style (SSC/Bank pattern)

80. A dishonest dealer sells goods at cost price but uses a weight of 900 g instead of 1 kg. What is his profit percentage?
(a) 11⅑% (b) 10% (c) 9⅑% (d) 12%
Answer: (a)
Solution: The tempting answer 10% comes from 100/1000, but profit % is calculated on what the dealer actually spends, i.e. the 900 g he gives. He pays for 900 g and receives the price of 1000 g. Profit % = 100/900 × 100 = 11⅑%, so the answer is 11⅑%.
Source: PYQ-style (SSC CGL pattern)

81. A man buys 12 oranges for ₹10 and sells 10 oranges for ₹12. What is his profit percentage?
(a) 40% (b) 36% (c) 20% (d) 44%
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is comparing ₹12 with ₹10 directly and saying 20%, ignoring the different quantities. CP per orange = 10/12 = ₹5/6; SP per orange = 12/10 = ₹6/5. Profit per orange = 6/5 − 5/6 = 11/30. Profit % = (11/30)/(5/6) × 100 = 44%, so the answer is 44%.
Source: NCERT Class 8 Mathematics (Comparing Quantities)

82. An article is sold at a profit of 20%. Had it been sold for ₹60 more, the profit would have been 30%. What is the cost price?
(a) ₹500 (b) ₹600 (c) ₹650 (d) ₹700
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is treating ₹60 as 30% of CP, which ignores the original 20% profit already included. The extra ₹60 represents the difference of 10% of CP. So CP = 60 × 100/10 = ₹600, so the answer is ₹600.
Source: PYQ-style (Bank PO pattern)

83. A shopkeeper allows a discount of 20% and still earns a profit of 20%. By what percentage is the marked price above the cost price?
(a) 40% (b) 44% (c) 50% (d) 60%
Answer: (c)
Solution: The tempting answer 40% (20 + 20) is wrong because the discount is on the marked price while profit is on the cost price. Let CP = ₹100, then SP = ₹120. Since SP = 80% of MP, MP = 120/0.80 = ₹150. Mark-up = 50%, so the answer is 50%.
Source: PYQ-style (SSC CGL pattern)

84. Successive discounts of 20% and 10% are equivalent to a single discount of:
(a) 30% (b) 32% (c) 25% (d) 28%
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is adding 20 + 10 = 30%, but the second discount acts on the reduced price. Net price factor = 0.80 × 0.90 = 0.72, so the single equivalent discount = 100 − 72 = 28%, so the answer is 28%.
Source: NCERT Class 8 Mathematics (Comparing Quantities)

85. A retailer buys 40 pens for the price of 36 pens. If he sells all 40 pens at the same rate per pen as his cost rate, what is his profit percentage?
(a) 10% (b) 12% (c) 11⅑% (d) 9%
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap answer 10% uses 4/40, but profit is measured on the cost, which covers only 36 pens. He pays for 36 pens and sells 40 at the same rate. Profit = price of 4 pens on cost of 36 pens = 4/36 × 100 = 11⅑%, so the answer is 11⅑%.
Source: PYQ-style (SSC pattern)

86. A man sold an article at a loss of 8%. Had he sold it for ₹144 more, he would have gained 10%. What is the cost price of the article?
(a) ₹720 (b) ₹800 (c) ₹880 (d) ₹900
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is taking ₹144 as 10% of CP, forgetting the 8% loss must first be recovered. The swing from −8% to +10% is 18% of CP. So CP = 144 × 100/18 = ₹800, so the answer is ₹800.
Source: PYQ-style (Bank Clerk/PO pattern)

87. The marked price of an article is ₹1500. After two successive discounts of 10% and 20%, what is the selling price?
(a) ₹1080 (b) ₹1050 (c) ₹1100 (d) ₹1120
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is taking a flat 30% off, giving ₹1050, but successive discounts multiply. SP = 1500 × 0.90 × 0.80 = ₹1080, so the answer is ₹1080.
Source: NCERT Class 8 Mathematics (Comparing Quantities)

88. A trader marks his goods 25% above cost price and allows a discount of 12%. What is his profit percentage?
(a) 11% (b) 10% (c) 12% (d) 13%
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is 25 − 12 = 13%, which wrongly subtracts percentages on different bases. Let CP = ₹100; MP = ₹125; SP = 125 × 0.88 = ₹110. Profit = 10%, so the answer is 10%.
Source: PYQ-style (SSC CGL pattern)

89. By selling 45 lemons for ₹40, a man loses 20%. How many lemons should he sell for ₹24 to gain 20%?
(a) 16 (b) 18 (c) 20 (d) 22
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is scaling 45 lemons directly by price without adjusting for the loss-to-gain swing. SP of 45 lemons = ₹40 at 20% loss, so CP of 45 lemons = 40/0.80 = ₹50. For 20% gain, SP of 45 lemons should be ₹60. So for ₹24 he must sell 45 × 24/60 = 18 lemons, so the answer is 18.
Source: PYQ-style (SSC CGL pattern)

90. A shopkeeper sells an article at ₹480 after giving a discount of 20% on the marked price. If the cost price is ₹400, what is the difference between the marked price and the cost price?
(a) ₹150 (b) ₹180 (c) ₹200 (d) ₹220
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is adding 20% of ₹480 (₹96) to get ₹576, but the discount is on the marked price, so ₹480 is 80% of MP. MP = 480/0.80 = ₹600. Difference = 600 − 400 = ₹200, so the answer is ₹200.
Source: NCERT Class 8 Mathematics (Comparing Quantities)

91. A man sells an article at a profit of 10%. Had he bought it at 10% less than what he paid and sold it for ₹9 less, he would have gained 20%. What is the original cost price?
(a) ₹400 (b) ₹450 (c) ₹500 (d) ₹550
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is applying the 20% gain to the original cost price instead of the reduced one. Let CP = x. Original SP = 1.10x. New CP = 0.90x; new SP = 0.90x × 1.20 = 1.08x. Given 1.10x − 1.08x = 9, so 0.02x = 9 and x = ₹450, so the answer is ₹450.
Source: PYQ-style (CAT/SSC CGL pattern)

92. A vendor buys bananas at 6 for ₹5 and sells them at 5 for ₹6. What is his profit percentage?
(a) 40% (b) 30% (c) 44% (d) 36%
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is comparing ₹6 and ₹5 and saying 20%, ignoring quantity. LCM of 6 and 5 is 30 bananas: CP = 30 × 5/6 = ₹25; SP = 30 × 6/5 = ₹36. Profit = ₹11 on ₹25 = 44%, so the answer is 44%.
Source: NCERT Class 8 Mathematics (Comparing Quantities)

93. An article was sold for ₹1440 after allowing a discount of 20%. What discount should be offered on the same marked price so that the selling price becomes ₹1620?
(a) 10% (b) 12% (c) 8% (d) 15%
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is computing the discount on ₹1440 instead of the marked price. MP = 1440/0.80 = ₹1800. Required discount = 1800 − 1620 = ₹180, which is 180/1800 × 100 = 10%, so the answer is 10%.
Source: PYQ-style (Bank PO pattern)

94. A shopkeeper cheats by 10% while buying (takes 1100 g for the price of 1 kg) and also cheats by 10% while selling (gives 900 g for the price of 1 kg). What is his overall profit percentage?
(a) 20% (b) 21% (c) 22²⁄₉% (d) 25%
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is adding 10 + 10 = 20%, but the two cheats multiply. He pays for 1000 g but receives 1100 g, and charges for 1000 g while giving 900 g. Profit % = (1100/900 − 1) × 100 = 200/900 × 100 = 22²⁄₉%, so the answer is 22²⁄₉%.
Source: PYQ-style (SSC CGL pattern)

95. The selling price of an article is ⁵⁄₄ times its cost price. If the cost price increases by 20% and the selling price remains the same, what is the new profit percentage?
(a) 10% (b) 5% (c) 8⅓% (d) 4⅙%
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is subtracting 20% from 25% to get 5%, but the base changes. Let CP = ₹100, SP = ₹125. New CP = ₹120. New profit = 5/120 × 100 = 4⅙%, so the answer is 4⅙%.
Source: PYQ-style (CAT pattern)

96. A man gains the selling price of 5 pens by selling 25 pens. What is his profit percentage?
(a) 25% (b) 20% (c) 16⅔% (d) 30%
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is 5/25 = 20%, but profit equals SP of 5 pens, so SP of 25 pens = CP of 25 + SP of 5, meaning CP of 25 = SP of 20. Let SP per pen = ₹1: SP of 25 = ₹25, CP of 25 = ₹20. Profit = 5/20 × 100 = 25%, so the answer is 25%.
Source: PYQ-style (SSC pattern)

97. A trader offers a discount of 15% on the marked price and still makes a profit of 19%. What would be his profit percentage if he sold at the marked price?
(a) 38% (b) 45% (c) 42% (d) 40%
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is adding 15 + 19 = 34% or scaling the percentages wrongly. Let CP = ₹100; SP = ₹119 = 85% of MP, so MP = 119/0.85 = ₹140. Selling at MP gives profit = 40%, so the answer is 40%.
Source: PYQ-style (IBPS PO pattern)

98. An article is listed at ₹900. A customer pays ₹612 after two successive discounts, the first being 20%. What is the second discount?
(a) 15% (b) 12% (c) 10% (d) 18%
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is dividing ₹612 by ₹900 and treating the total 32% as the second discount. After the first discount: 900 × 0.80 = ₹720. Second discount = 720 − 612 = ₹108, i.e. 108/720 × 100 = 15%, so the answer is 15%.
Source: NCERT Class 8 Mathematics (Comparing Quantities)

99. A man sells an article at 5% above its cost price. If he had bought it at 5% less than what he paid and sold it for ₹2 less, he would have gained 10%. What is the original cost price?
(a) ₹400 (b) ₹500 (c) ₹600 (d) ₹800
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is applying the 10% gain to the original CP. Let CP = x. Original SP = 1.05x. New CP = 0.95x; new SP = 0.95x × 1.10 = 1.045x. Given 1.05x − 1.045x = 2, so 0.005x = 2 and x = ₹400, so the answer is ₹400.
Source: PYQ-style (CAT/SSC CGL pattern)

100. A fruit seller makes a profit of 20% by selling mangoes at a certain price. If he charges ₹1.20 more per mango, he would make a profit of 50%. What is the cost price per mango?
(a) ₹3 (b) ₹5 (c) ₹6 (d) ₹4
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is treating ₹1.20 as 50% of CP, ignoring the existing 20% profit. The extra ₹1.20 equals the difference of 30% of CP. CP = 1.20 × 100/30 = ₹4, so the answer is ₹4.
Source: PYQ-style (Bank Clerk pattern)
