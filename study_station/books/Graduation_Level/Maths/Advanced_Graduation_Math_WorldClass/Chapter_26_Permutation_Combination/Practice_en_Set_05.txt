101. In how many ways can 6 different books be arranged on a shelf so that two particular books are always together?
(a) 120 (b) 48 (c) 240 (d) 720
Answer: (c)
Solution: Treat the two particular books as one unit, giving 5 units arranged in 5! = 120 ways; the two books can swap places in 2! = 2 ways. The trap is answering 120 and forgetting the internal arrangement of the pair. Total = 120 × 2 = 240, so the answer is 240.

102. How many 4-digit numbers can be formed using the digits 1, 2, 3, 4, 5 without repetition?
(a) 120 (b) 625 (c) 24 (d) 60
Answer: (a)
Solution: This is a permutation of 5 digits taken 4 at a time: ⁵P₄ = 5 × 4 × 3 × 2 = 120. The tempting wrong option 625 = 5⁴ wrongly allows repetition. So the answer is 120.

103. In how many ways can a committee of 4 be chosen from 9 people?
(a) 3024 (b) 126 (c) 36 (d) 84
Answer: (b)
Solution: Selection only, so use combination: ⁹C₄ = 9!/(4!5!) = 126. The trap is 3024, which is ⁹P₄ and wrongly counts arrangements of the committee. So the answer is 126.

104. How many words can be formed using all the letters of the word "BANANA"?
(a) 720 (b) 360 (c) 120 (d) 60
Answer: (d)
Solution: There are 6 letters with A repeated 3 times and N repeated 2 times, so arrangements = 6!/(3!2!) = 720/12 = 60. The trap is 720, which ignores the repeated letters. So the answer is 60.

105. In how many ways can 5 boys and 3 girls be seated in a row so that no two girls sit together?
(a) 40320 (b) 14400 (c) 7200 (d) 2880
Answer: (b)
Solution: Arrange the 5 boys first in 5! = 120 ways; this creates 6 gaps, and the 3 girls go into 3 of these gaps in ⁶P₃ = 120 ways. Total = 120 × 120 = 14400. The trap 40320 = 8! ignores the restriction. So the answer is 14400.

106. From 6 men and 4 women, a committee of 5 is to be formed with at least 2 women. In how many ways can this be done?
(a) 120 (b) 246 (c) 186 (d) 252
Answer: (c)
Solution: Cases: 2 women 3 men = ⁴C₂×⁶C₃ = 6×20 = 120; 3 women 2 men = ⁴C₃×⁶C₂ = 4×15 = 60; 4 women 1 man = ⁴C₄×⁶C₁ = 6. Total = 120 + 60 + 6 = 186. The trap 252 = ¹⁰C₅ ignores the condition. So the answer is 186.

107. How many diagonals does a polygon with 10 sides have?
(a) 45 (b) 40 (c) 90 (d) 35
Answer: (d)
Solution: Diagonals = n(n−3)/2 = 10×7/2 = 35. The trap 45 = ¹⁰C₂ counts all line segments joining vertices, including the 10 sides. So the answer is 35.

108. In how many ways can the letters of "LEADING" be arranged so that the vowels always come together?
(a) 720 (b) 360 (c) 144 (d) 576
Answer: (a)
Solution: Vowels E, A, I form one block; together with consonants L, D, N, G there are 5 units arranged in 5! = 120 ways, and the 3 vowels arrange inside the block in 3! = 6 ways. The trap 576 comes from using 4! × 4! by miscounting the units. Total = 120 × 6 = 720, so the answer is 720.

109. How many 3-digit even numbers can be formed from the digits 1, 2, 3, 4, 5 without repetition?
(a) 24 (b) 60 (c) 36 (d) 12
Answer: (a)
Solution: The units digit must be 2 or 4, giving 2 choices; the remaining two places are filled from the other 4 digits in ⁴P₂ = 12 ways. Total = 2 × 12 = 24. The trap 60 = ⁵P₃ ignores the evenness condition. So the answer is 24.

110. In how many ways can 8 people be seated around a circular table?
(a) 40320 (b) 5760 (c) 5040 (d) 720
Answer: (c)
Solution: Circular arrangements of n people = (n−1)! = 7! = 5040. The trap 40320 = 8! treats it as a linear arrangement and overcounts rotations. So the answer is 5040.

111. If ⁿC₈ = ⁿC₆, find the value of n.
(a) 14 (b) 12 (c) 48 (d) 2
Answer: (a)
Solution: If ⁿCᵣ = ⁿCₛ with r ≠ s, then r + s = n, so n = 8 + 6 = 14. The trap 48 comes from multiplying instead of adding. So the answer is 14.

112. How many words of 4 letters can be formed from the letters of "EXAMINATION" if no letter is repeated?
(a) 336 (b) 720 (c) 840 (d) 1680
Answer: (d)
Solution: The distinct letters of EXAMINATION are E, X, A, M, I, N, T, O — 8 in all. Choosing and arranging 4 of them gives ⁸P₄ = 8 × 7 × 6 × 5 = 1680. The trap 336 = ⁸P₃ uses only 3 letters. So the answer is 1680.

113. A box contains 5 red and 4 blue balls. In how many ways can 3 balls be drawn so that at least one is red?
(a) 74 (b) 80 (c) 84 (d) 60
Answer: (b)
Solution: Total ways to draw 3 from 9 = ⁹C₃ = 84; ways with no red (all blue) = ⁴C₃ = 4. Required = 84 − 4 = 80. The trap 74 comes from subtracting the wrong complementary count. So the answer is 80.

114. In how many ways can 10 identical coins be distributed among 4 children if each child gets at least one coin?
(a) 120 (b) 286 (c) 84 (d) 220
Answer: (c)
Solution: Give each child 1 coin first; distribute the remaining 6 identical coins among 4 children freely: C(6+4−1, 4−1) = ⁹C₃ = 84. The trap 286 = ¹³C₃ forgets the "at least one" adjustment. So the answer is 84.

115. How many numbers between 100 and 1000 have all distinct digits?
(a) 648 (b) 900 (c) 720 (d) 504
Answer: (a)
Solution: These are the 3-digit numbers with distinct digits: hundreds digit has 9 choices (1–9), tens digit 9 choices (0 plus remaining), units digit 8 choices. Total = 9 × 9 × 8 = 648. The trap 900 counts all 3-digit numbers ignoring distinctness. So the answer is 648.

116. In how many ways can 4 boys and 4 girls be seated in a row alternately?
(a) 40320 (b) 576 (c) 2304 (d) 1152
Answer: (d)
Solution: Two patterns exist: starting with a boy or starting with a girl. Boys arrange in 4! = 24 and girls in 4! = 24, so total = 2 × 24 × 24 = 1152. The trap 576 = 24 × 24 counts only one starting pattern. So the answer is 1152.

(a) 120 (b) 31 (c) 325 (d) 720

Solution: Signals using 1, 2, 3, 4 or 5 flags: ⁵P₁ + ⁵P₂ + ⁵P₃ + ⁵P₄ + ⁵P₅ = 5 + 20 + 60 + 120 + 120 = 325. The trap 120 counts only signals using all 5 flags. So the answer is 325.

118. From a group of 7 men and 6 women, 5 persons are to be selected so that at least 3 men are included. In how many ways can this be done?
(a) 861 (b) 756 (c) 525 (d) 630
Answer: (b)
Solution: Cases: 3 men 2 women = ⁷C₃×⁶C₂ = 35×15 = 525; 4 men 1 woman = ⁷C₄×⁶C₁ = 35×6 = 210; 5 men = ⁷C₅ = 21. Total = 525 + 210 + 21 = 756. The trap 861 adds an extra overlapping case. So the answer is 756.

120. In how many ways can 5 different prizes be given to 4 students if each student can receive any number of prizes?

Answer: (a)
Solution: Each prize can go to any of the 4 students independently, giving 4⁵ = 1024 ways. The trap 625 = 5⁴ reverses base and power, treating students as choices for prizes. So the answer is 1024.

121. How many 6-digit numbers can be formed from the digits 0, 1, 2, 3, 4, 5 without repetition which are divisible by 10?

Answer: (a)
Solution: Divisibility by 10 forces the units digit to be 0. The remaining 5 digits fill the first five places in 5! = 120 ways. The trap 600 = 6! − 5! counts all 6-digit numbers from these digits without the divisibility condition. So the answer is 120.

123. How many words can be formed from the letters of "DAUGHTER" so that the vowels are never together?
(a) 4320 (b) 40320 (c) 36000 (d) 31680
Answer: (c)
Solution: Total arrangements = 8! = 40320. Arrangements with vowels A, U, E together: treat them as one block, giving 6 units in 6! = 720 ways times 3! = 6 internal arrangements = 4320. Required = 40320 − 4320 = 36000. The trap 4320 is exactly the complementary case that must be subtracted. So the answer is 36000.

124. A polygon has 44 diagonals. How many sides does it have?
(a) 10 (b) 9 (c) 12 (d) 11
Answer: (d)
Solution: n(n−3)/2 = 44 gives n² − 3n − 88 = 0, i.e. (n − 11)(n + 8) = 0, so n = 11. The trap 12 comes from solving n(n−3) = 44 instead of n(n−3) = 88. So the answer is 11.

125. In how many ways can 5 boys and 5 girls be seated around a circular table so that no two girls sit together?
(a) 3628800 (b) 2880 (c) 14400 (d) 5760
Answer: (b)
Solution: Arrange the 5 boys around the circle in (5−1)! = 24 ways; this creates exactly 5 gaps between them, and the 5 girls occupy these gaps in 5! = 120 ways. Total = 24 × 120 = 2880. The trap 3628800 = 9! treats it as an unrestricted circular arrangement. So the answer is 2880.
