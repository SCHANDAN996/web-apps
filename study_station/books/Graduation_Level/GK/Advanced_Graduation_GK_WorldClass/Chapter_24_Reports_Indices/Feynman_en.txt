# 🧒 Feynman Technique — Reports & Indices

## The Most Confusing Concept: "Who Publishes What — and Why Do Two Similar-Sounding Reports Have Different Publishers?"

Ask any aspirant: *"Who publishes the Global Hunger Index?"* and watch the confusion begin. Some say UN. Some say FAO. Some say WHO. The correct answer — **Concern Worldwide (Ireland) and Welthungerhilfe (Germany), two NGOs** — surprises almost everyone. And that surprise is exactly what the examiner exploits.

So let's fix this the Feynman way.

---

## Explain It Like I'm 12

Imagine your school holds a **"Best Student" competition**. Now, who decides the winner?

- The **Principal** can announce one ranking (official authority).
- A **newspaper** can publish its own ranking (media house).
- A **parents' association** can publish another (an NGO/private body).

All three rankings are about the *same students*, but the **judge is different** — so the method, the criteria, and the credibility are different.

Reports and indices work exactly like this:

| "Judge" type | Real-world example |
|---|---|
| **UN bodies** (the Principal) | UNDP → Human Development Index; FAO → State of Food Security |
| **Financial institutions** (the Bank manager) | World Bank → Ease of Doing Business (now discontinued); IMF → World Economic Outlook |
| **NGOs / private foundations** (the Parents' association) | Global Hunger Index → Concern Worldwide + Welthungerhilfe; Press Freedom Index → Reporters Without Borders; Corruption Perceptions Index → Transparency International |

**The golden insight:** An index is *not* automatically a UN product just because it sounds serious and global. Many famous indices are published by **private organisations and NGOs**. The examiner's favourite trap is to swap the publisher — "Global Hunger Index is published by FAO" — and most students fall for it because FAO *does* deal with food.

---

## A Concrete Daily-Life Example

Think of **movie ratings**:

- **Censor Board certificate** = official government body (like a UN report).
- **IMDb rating** = a private platform's score (like Transparency International's index).
- **A film critic's blog ranking** = an NGO-style independent assessment (like Reporters Without Borders).

If someone asks, *"Who rated this movie 8.5?"* — you must ask **which platform**, not assume. Same movie, different raters, different scores. Same hunger problem, different publishers, different indices.

---

## The Unforgettable Trick: **"NGO ka Naam, NGO ka Kaam"**

Whenever an index measures something **critical of governments** — corruption, press freedom, hunger, human rights — ask yourself: *"Would governments happily publish a ranking that embarrasses themselves?"* Usually **no**. So these are mostly **NGO/private** publications:

- **Corruption** → Transparency International (NGO, Berlin)
- **Press Freedom** → Reporters Without Borders (NGO, Paris)
- **Hunger** → Concern Worldwide + Welthungerhilfe (NGOs)
- **Human Freedom** → Cato Institute + Fraser Institute (think tanks)

And the **"development/economy"** sounding ones are mostly **UN/institutional**:

- **HDI** → UNDP
- **World Economic Outlook** → IMF
- **Global Gender Gap** → World Economic Forum (a foundation — remember: WEF is *not* a UN body!)

**One-line hook:** *"Sharam wale index NGO dete hain, vikas wale UN."* (Embarrassing indices come from NGOs; development ones from the UN.)
