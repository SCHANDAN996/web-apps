# Biology – Human Body

## The Hook: A Heart That Never Rests

Right now, as you read this line, your heart has already beaten about 70 times in the last minute. It will do this roughly 100,000 times today, pumping thousands of litres of blood — without you ever asking it to. In 1628, an English physician named **William Harvey** proved that blood *circulates* in a closed loop, overturning centuries of medical belief. Every competitive exam still asks about this system — because the human body is the one machine every examiner knows you carry into the exam hall.

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this chapter, you will be able to:
1. Identify the structure and functions of major human organ systems.
2. Recall key facts about blood, the heart, the brain, bones, muscles and glands.
3. Spot the examiner's favourite traps — swapped numbers, reversed functions, and look-alike terms.
4. Answer statement-based questions (the Graduation-level pattern) with confidence.

## 1. The Cell – The Basic Unit of Life

Every system begins here. The **cell** is the structural and functional unit of life.

- **Cell theory** was given by **Schleiden and Schwann** (1838–39); **Rudolf Virchow** (1855) added that all cells arise from pre-existing cells.
- **Robert Hooke** (1665) first observed cells (in cork).
- The **cell membrane** is selectively permeable; the **nucleus** is the control centre and contains **chromosomes** (humans have **46**, i.e., 23 pairs).
- **Mitochondria** = powerhouse of the cell (site of ATP/energy production).
- **Ribosomes** = protein factories. **Lysosomes** = "suicidal bags" (contain digestive enzymes).
- Plant cells have a **cell wall** (cellulose) and **chloroplasts**; animal cells do not.

🧠 **Active Recall:** Who gave the cell theory? Which organelle is called the powerhouse? How many chromosomes does a normal human cell have?

## 2. Blood – The River Inside You

Blood is a fluid connective tissue. An average adult has about **5–6 litres** of blood (roughly 7–8% of body weight).

**Components:**
- **Plasma** (~55%): mostly water; carries nutrients, hormones, waste.
- **Red Blood Cells (RBC / Erythrocytes):** carry oxygen via **haemoglobin** (an iron-containing pigment). Formed in **bone marrow**; lifespan ~**120 days**; destroyed in the **spleen** ("graveyard of RBCs"). RBCs in mammals have **no nucleus**.
- **White Blood Cells (WBC / Leucocytes):** soldiers of the body — fight infection (immunity).
- **Platelets (Thrombocytes):** help in **blood clotting**.

**Blood Groups:** Discovered by **Karl Landsteiner** (1900–01) — ABO system.
- **O negative** = universal donor; **AB positive** = universal recipient.
- **Rh factor** also discovered by Landsteiner (in Rhesus monkeys).

**pH of blood:** slightly alkaline, about **7.4**.

🧠 **Active Recall:** Which cell has no nucleus? Where are RBCs destroyed? Who is the universal donor — and don't confuse it with the universal recipient!

## 3. The Circulatory System – Heart and Vessels

- The **heart** is a four-chambered muscular organ: 2 **atria** (upper) + 2 **ventricles** (lower).
- **Double circulation:** pulmonary (heart ↔ lungs) and systemic (heart ↔ body).
- **Arteries** carry blood *away* from the heart (usually oxygenated — exception: **pulmonary artery** carries deoxygenated blood).
- **Veins** carry blood *toward* the heart (usually deoxygenated — exception: **pulmonary vein** carries oxygenated blood).
- **Capillaries** are the thinnest vessels — site of exchange.
- Normal **blood pressure: 120/80 mm Hg** (systolic/diastolic), measured with a **sphygmomanometer**.
- Normal resting **heart rate: ~72 beats/minute**.
- The largest artery is the **aorta**.
- **William Harvey** discovered blood circulation; **ECG** records heart's electrical activity.

🧠 **Active Recall:** Which artery carries deoxygenated blood? What do the two numbers in 120/80 mean?

---

## 4. The Respiratory System

- Breathing organs: **lungs** (right lung has 3 lobes, left has 2 — to make room for the heart).
- Gas exchange happens in tiny air sacs called **alveoli**.
- The windpipe is the **trachea**; it branches into **bronchi**, then **bronchioles**.
- The **diaphragm** is the main muscle of breathing.
- Oxygen binds to **haemoglobin** forming oxyhaemoglobin; carbon monoxide binds far more strongly (why CO poisoning is deadly).

---

## 5. The Digestive System

- Digestion begins in the **mouth** — salivary **amylase (ptyalin)** starts starch digestion.
- Food travels via the **oesophagus** to the **stomach**, where **HCl** and **pepsin** act on proteins.
- Most digestion and absorption occurs in the **small intestine** (the longest part of the alimentary canal, ~6 metres); finger-like **villi** absorb nutrients.
- **Liver** — the **largest gland** of the body — secretes **bile** (stored in the **gall bladder**), which emulsifies fats. Bile contains **no enzyme**.
- **Pancreas** — a mixed gland — secretes digestive enzymes and the hormones **insulin** and **glucagon** (blood sugar control).
- The **large intestine** absorbs water; undigested waste leaves via the rectum.

🧠 **Active Recall:** Where does most absorption occur? Which gland is the largest? What does bile do despite having no enzyme?

## 6. The Nervous System and the Brain

- The functional unit is the **neuron** — the longest cell in the body.
- The system divides into the **central nervous system** (brain + spinal cord) and **peripheral nervous system**.
- **Brain parts:**
  - **Cerebrum** — largest part; thinking, memory, voluntary actions.
  - **Cerebellum** — balance and coordination (affected by alcohol).
  - **Medulla oblongata** — involuntary actions: heartbeat, breathing, swallowing.
  - **Hypothalamus** — hunger, thirst, body temperature; links nervous and endocrine systems.
- The brain is protected by **meninges** and the skull; the spinal cord runs through the vertebral column.
- **Reflex actions** are controlled by the **spinal cord**.

---

## 7. The Skeletal and Muscular Systems

- An adult human skeleton has **206 bones**; a newborn has about 300 (many fuse with age).
- The **longest and strongest bone: femur** (thigh bone).
- The **smallest bone: stapes** (in the middle ear).
- The **skull** has 22 bones; the **backbone (vertebral column)** has **33 vertebrae**.
- Bones are made strong by **calcium and phosphorus**; **vitamin D** helps calcium absorption.
- **Muscles:** the body has over 600 muscles. The **largest** muscle is the **gluteus maximus**; the **busiest** are the eye muscles.
- Muscle fatigue is caused by accumulation of **lactic acid**.

🧠 **Active Recall:** 206 bones — but how many in a newborn? Longest bone vs smallest bone — name both.

---

## 8. Excretory System

- The **kidneys** (a pair) filter blood; their functional unit is the **nephron** (about a million per kidney).
- **Urea** is the main nitrogenous waste in humans, formed in the **liver**.
- Urine passes: kidney → **ureter** → **urinary bladder** → **urethra**.
- **Dialysis** is the artificial filtration of blood in kidney failure.
- Skin also excretes (sweat — water and salts); lungs excrete CO₂ and water vapour.

---

## 9. Endocrine System – The Chemical Messengers

| Gland | Hormone | Key Function |
|---|---|---|
| Pituitary ("master gland") | Growth hormone | Growth; excess → gigantism, deficiency → dwarfism |
| Thyroid | Thyroxine | Metabolism; needs **iodine**; deficiency → goitre |
| Pancreas (islets of Langerhans) | Insulin | Lowers blood sugar; deficiency → **diabetes mellitus** |
| Adrenal | Adrenaline | "Fight or flight" emergency hormone |
| Parathyroid | Parathormone | Calcium balance |
| Testes / Ovaries | Testosterone / Oestrogen | Sexual characteristics |

- The **pituitary** is called the master gland, but it is itself controlled by the **hypothalamus**.
- **Insulin** was discovered by **Banting and Best** (1921).

---

## 10. Sense Organs and Skin

- **Eye:** image forms on the **retina**; the **iris** controls light entry; **rods** (dim light) and **cones** (colour) are photoreceptors. **Vitamin A** deficiency → night blindness.
- **Ear:** hearing + balance; contains the smallest bone (stapes).
- **Skin:** the **largest organ** of the body; sense of touch, temperature regulation.
- **Tongue:** taste buds detect sweet, sour, salty, bitter (and umami).

---

## 11. Vitamins, Deficiency and Disease (Quick Table)

| Vitamin | Chemical Name | Deficiency Disease |
|---|---|---|
| A | Retinol | Night blindness |
| B1 | Thiamine | Beri-beri |
| B12 | Cobalamin | Anaemia |
| C | Ascorbic acid | Scurvy |
| D | Calciferol | Rickets (children), osteomalacia (adults) |
| K | Phylloquinone | Poor blood clotting |

- Vitamin **C and B-complex** are water-soluble; **A, D, E, K** are fat-soluble.
- **Protein** deficiency → **kwashiorkor** and **marasmus** (in children).

## 🧠 Mnemonics (Memory Hooks)

1. **"ADEK are FAT"** — Fat-soluble vitamins: **A, D, E, K** (the rest dissolve in water).
2. **"O gives, AB receives"** — O⁻ is the universal **donor**, AB⁺ the universal **recipient**.
3. **"Cerebrum thinks, Cerebellum balances, Medulla keeps you alive."** — the three big brain parts in one line.

## ⚠️ Caution: Examiner's Trap

**Trap 1 — "लगभग सही कथन" (Almost-True Statement):**
*"Arteries always carry oxygenated blood."* Sounds right — but the **pulmonary artery** carries deoxygenated blood from heart to lungs. The rule is about *direction* (away from heart), not oxygen. Similarly, "all veins carry deoxygenated blood" fails at the pulmonary vein.

**Trap 2 — "समान नाम/संख्या भ्रम" (Similar Numbers Confusion):**
Examiners swap **universal donor (O⁻)** with **universal recipient (AB⁺)**, or **206 bones (adult)** with **~300 (newborn)**, or **120/80 BP** with **120 days (RBC lifespan)**. Read every number twice before answering.

**Trap 3 — "विपरीत कार्य-कारण" (Reverse Causation):**
*"Insulin increases blood sugar."* — Reversed! Insulin **lowers** blood sugar; **glucagon** raises it. Similarly, bile does not *digest* fat with enzymes — it *emulsifies* fat (breaks it into droplets) and contains **no enzyme at all**.
