Card 1
Front: Which is the largest gland in the human body, and what is its most famous digestive product?
Back: The liver. It produces bile, which emulsifies fats. Bile is stored and concentrated in the gallbladder — it is not made there.

Card 2
Front: Which blood cells have no nucleus in mature humans, and why does that matter?
Back: Red blood cells (erythrocytes). Losing the nucleus gives more room for haemoglobin, increasing oxygen-carrying capacity — but it also means RBCs cannot divide or repair themselves, so they live only about 120 days.

Card 3
Front: Mnemonic trick — how do you remember the cranial nerves count and the order of the first few?
Back: There are 12 pairs of cranial nerves. A classic mnemonic: "On Old Olympus's Towering Top, A Finn And German Viewed Some Hops" — Olfactory, Optic, Oculomotor, Trochlear, Trigeminal, Abducens, Facial, Auditory (Vestibulocochlear), Glossopharyngeal, Vagus, Spinal Accessory, Hypoglossal.

Card 4
Front: Examiner's trap — "Bile is produced in the gallbladder." True or false?
Back: False. Bile is produced by the liver; the gallbladder only stores and concentrates it. Exams repeatedly swap "produced" and "stored" to catch careless readers.

Card 5
Front: Which is the longest and strongest bone in the human body, and which is the smallest?
Back: Longest/strongest: femur (thigh bone). Smallest: stapes in the middle ear.

Card 6
Front: How many chambers does the human heart have, and which chamber pumps blood to the whole body?
Back: Four chambers — two atria and two ventricles. The left ventricle pumps oxygenated blood into the aorta for the whole body; it has the thickest muscular wall.

Card 7
Front: What is the normal resting heart rate and normal blood pressure of a healthy adult?
Back: Heart rate: about 72 beats per minute. Blood pressure: about 120/80 mm Hg (systolic/diastolic).

Card 8
Front: Which vitamin is synthesized in human skin with the help of sunlight, and which disease results from its deficiency?
Back: Vitamin D. Its deficiency causes rickets in children and osteomalacia in adults (weak, soft bones).

Card 9
Front: Mnemonic trick — how do you quickly recall the fat-soluble vitamins?
Back: "KEDA" or "ADEK" — Vitamins A, D, E, K are fat-soluble; the rest (B-complex and C) are water-soluble. Fat-soluble vitamins can be stored in the body, so excess can be toxic.

Card 10
Front: Which part of the brain controls balance and posture, and which part controls breathing and heartbeat?
Back: Balance and posture: cerebellum. Breathing, heartbeat and other involuntary vital functions: medulla oblongata (part of the brainstem).

Card 11
Front: Examiner's trap — "Arteries always carry oxygenated blood." Where does this statement fail?
Back: It fails at the pulmonary artery, which carries deoxygenated blood from the right ventricle to the lungs. Similarly, the pulmonary vein carries oxygenated blood. Arteries are defined by carrying blood away from the heart, not by oxygen content.

Card 12
Front: Which is the largest organ of the human body, and the largest internal organ?
Back: Skin is the largest organ overall; the liver is the largest internal organ (and largest gland).

Card 13
Front: What is the functional unit of the kidney called, and roughly how many are there per kidney?
Back: The nephron — about 1 million (10 lakh) nephrons per kidney. It filters blood and forms urine.

Card 14
Front: Which hormone regulates blood sugar, which gland secretes it, and what disease follows its deficiency?
Back: Insulin, secreted by the beta cells of the islets of Langerhans in the pancreas. Its deficiency (or ineffectiveness) causes diabetes mellitus.

Card 15
Front: Which blood group is called the universal donor and which the universal recipient?
Back: O negative (O−) is the universal donor; AB positive (AB+) is the universal recipient. Trap: exams often drop the Rh factor and just say "O" and "AB" — read the options carefully.

Card 16
Front: What is the powerhouse of the cell, and which organelle is called the "suicide bag"?
Back: Powerhouse: mitochondrion (site of cellular respiration/ATP production). Suicide bag: lysosome, because its enzymes can digest the cell itself.

Card 17
Front: How many bones does an adult human skeleton have, and why do infants have more?
Back: An adult has 206 bones. Infants are born with about 300; many fuse during growth (e.g., skull bones, sacrum).

Card 18
Front: Which is the master gland of the endocrine system, and which gland controls it?
Back: The pituitary gland is called the master gland because it controls other endocrine glands — but it is itself regulated by the hypothalamus of the brain.

Card 19
Front: What is the average total blood volume in a healthy adult human body?
Back: About 5 litres (roughly 7–8% of body weight). Plasma is about 55% of blood; the rest is formed elements (RBCs, WBCs, platelets).

Card 20
Front: Which enzyme in saliva begins digestion, and on which nutrient does it act?
Back: Salivary amylase (ptyalin) — it begins the digestion of starch (carbohydrates) right in the mouth.
