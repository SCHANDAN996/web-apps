101. Which enzyme present in saliva begins the digestion of starch?
(a) Ptyalin (b) Pepsin (c) Lipase (d) Trypsin
Answer: (a)
Solution: Pepsin acts on proteins in the stomach, not in the mouth, so it is a tempting but wrong choice. Ptyalin (salivary amylase) secreted by salivary glands converts starch into maltose in the mouth, so the answer is Ptyalin.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Biology

102. The functional unit of the kidney is:
(a) Neuron (b) Alveolus (c) Nephron (d) Glomerulus
Answer: (c)
Solution: Glomerulus is only a part of the nephron, not the whole functional unit — a common trap. The nephron is the structural and functional unit of the kidney, so the answer is Nephron.

103. Which blood group is called the universal donor?
(a) AB positive (b) O positive (c) AB negative (d) O negative
Answer: (d)
Solution: O positive is a near-correct trap; it can donate to all positive groups but not to negative groups. O negative lacks A, B and Rh antigens, making it the universal donor, so the answer is O negative.

104. Insulin is secreted by which cells of the pancreas?
(a) Alpha cells (b) Delta cells (c) Beta cells (d) Acinar cells
Answer: (c)
Solution: Alpha cells secrete glucagon, which raises blood sugar — the opposite action and a classic confusion. Beta cells of the islets of Langerhans secrete insulin, so the answer is Beta cells.

106. Which vitamin is synthesized in human skin on exposure to sunlight?
(a) Vitamin A (b) Vitamin D (c) Vitamin C (d) Vitamin K
Answer: (b)
Solution: Vitamin K is synthesized by gut bacteria, not skin — a frequent mix-up. Ultraviolet rays convert 7-dehydrocholesterol in skin into Vitamin D, so the answer is Vitamin D.

107. The exchange of gases in the lungs takes place in the:
(a) Alveoli (b) Bronchioles (c) Bronchi (d) Trachea
Answer: (a)
Solution: Bronchioles only conduct air; they do not exchange gases, which is the trap here. Alveoli provide a thin, large surface for diffusion of oxygen and carbon dioxide, so the answer is Alveoli.

Answer: (b)
Solution: Pulmonary artery carries deoxygenated blood despite being an artery — the usual vein/artery assumption is the trap. The pulmonary vein brings oxygenated blood to the left atrium, so the answer is Pulmonary vein.

121. Consider the following statements about blood circulation:
1. The pulmonary artery carries deoxygenated blood.
2. The pulmonary vein carries oxygenated blood.
3. The aorta carries deoxygenated blood to the body.
Which of the above statements are correct?
(a) 1, 2 and 3 (b) 2 and 3 only (c) 1 and 3 only (d) 1 and 2 only
Answer: (d)
Solution: Statement 3 is the trap — the aorta always carries oxygenated blood from the left ventricle. Statements 1 and 2 correctly describe the pulmonary vessels, so the answer is 1 and 2 only.

123. A person with blood group AB negative can receive blood from which of the following groups?
(a) Only AB negative (b) All blood groups (c) AB negative, A negative, B negative and O negative (d) Only O negative
Answer: (c)
Solution: "All blood groups" is the trap — AB positive is the universal recipient, not AB negative, because Rh-positive blood cannot be given to an Rh-negative person. AB negative can receive from all negative groups, so the answer is AB negative, A negative, B negative and O negative.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Biology

124. Arrange the following events of digestion in correct chronological order:
1. Absorption of nutrients in the small intestine
2. Action of ptyalin on starch
3. Action of pepsin on proteins
4. Emulsification of fats by bile
(a) 2 → 3 → 4 → 1 (b) 2 → 4 → 3 → 1 (c) 3 → 2 → 4 → 1 (d) 2 → 3 → 1 → 4
Answer: (a)
Solution: Placing absorption before bile action is the chronological trap in the last option. Digestion begins in the mouth, then stomach, then bile acts in the duodenum, and absorption follows, so the answer is 2 → 3 → 4 → 1.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science

Answer: (a)
Solution: Statement 3 is the trap — memory and intelligence belong to the cerebrum, while the cerebellum handles balance and coordination. Statements 1 and 2 are correct, so the answer is 1 and 2 only.
