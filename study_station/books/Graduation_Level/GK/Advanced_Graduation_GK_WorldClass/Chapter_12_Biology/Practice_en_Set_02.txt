Questions 26–50: Medium

26. Which enzyme present in saliva begins the digestion of starch?
(a) Ptyalin (b) Pepsin (c) Lipase (d) Trypsin
Answer: (a)
Solution: Pepsin acts in the stomach on proteins, not in the mouth, so it is the tempting wrong choice. Salivary amylase, also called ptyalin, converts starch into maltose in the mouth, so the answer is ptyalin.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Biology

27. The functional unit of the human kidney is the:
(a) Neuron (b) Alveolus (c) Nephron (d) Villus
Answer: (c)
Solution: Neuron (nerve cell) and nephron sound similar, which is the examiner's trap; alveoli belong to lungs and villi to the intestine. Filtration of blood and urine formation occur in the nephron, so the answer is nephron.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Biology

28. Which blood group is called the universal donor?
(a) AB positive (b) AB negative (c) O negative (d) O positive
Answer: (c)
Solution: Many students pick O positive, forgetting that the Rh factor also matters in transfusion. O negative blood has neither A/B antigens nor Rh antigen, so the answer is O negative.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Biology

29. The exchange of gases in the lungs takes place in the:
(a) Bronchi (b) Bronchioles (c) Trachea (d) Alveoli
Answer: (d)
Solution: Bronchioles are only air passages, a common confusion with the actual exchange surface. The thin-walled alveoli surrounded by capillaries are the site of gas exchange, so the answer is alveoli.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Biology

30. Which hormone regulates blood calcium levels by decreasing them?
(a) Parathyroid hormone (b) Aldosterone (c) Insulin (d) Calcitonin
Answer: (d)
Solution: Parathyroid hormone is the trap because it also deals with calcium but raises blood calcium, the opposite action. Calcitonin from the thyroid lowers blood calcium, so the answer is calcitonin.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Biology

31. The largest gland in the human body is the:
(a) Liver (b) Thyroid (c) Pancreas (d) Pituitary
Answer: (a)
Solution: The pituitary is often wrongly chosen because it is called the master gland, but it is tiny. The liver is the largest gland and performs bile secretion and detoxification, so the answer is liver.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Biology

32. Which part of the brain controls balance and coordination of the body?
(a) Cerebrum (b) Hypothalamus (c) Medulla oblongata (d) Cerebellum
Answer: (d)
Solution: Cerebrum and cerebellum are similar names used as a trap; the cerebrum handles thinking and voluntary actions. The cerebellum coordinates movement and balance, so the answer is cerebellum.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Biology

33. The normal resting heart rate of a healthy adult human is approximately:
(a) 40–50 beats per minute (b) 140–160 beats per minute (c) 110–130 beats per minute (d) 60–100 beats per minute
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is 40–50, which applies only to trained athletes at rest, not average adults. The standard normal range is 60–100 beats per minute, so the answer is 60–100 beats per minute.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Biology

34. Which vitamin is synthesized in human skin on exposure to sunlight?
(a) Vitamin A (b) Vitamin C (c) Vitamin K (d) Vitamin D
Answer: (d)
Solution: Vitamin K is the distractor because it is also made in the body, but by gut bacteria, not skin. Ultraviolet light converts a cholesterol derivative in skin into vitamin D, so the answer is vitamin D.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Biology

35. The pacemaker of the human heart is the:
(a) AV node (b) SA node (c) Bundle of His (d) Purkinje fibres
Answer: (b)
Solution: The AV node is the tempting wrong answer since it also conducts impulses, but it only relays them. The sinoatrial node initiates each heartbeat, so the answer is SA node.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Biology

36. Which of the following carries oxygenated blood from the lungs to the heart?
(a) Pulmonary artery (b) Vena cava (c) Aorta (d) Pulmonary vein
Answer: (d)
Solution: The classic trap is the pulmonary artery — arteries usually carry oxygenated blood, but this one carries deoxygenated blood to the lungs. The pulmonary vein returns oxygenated blood to the left atrium, so the answer is pulmonary vein.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Biology

37. Insulin is secreted by which cells of the pancreas?
(a) Beta cells (b) Alpha cells (c) Delta cells (d) Acinar cells
Answer: (a)
Solution: Alpha cells are the trap because they also sit in the islets of Langerhans, but they secrete glucagon, which raises blood sugar. Beta cells secrete insulin, so the answer is beta cells.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Biology

38. The total number of bones in an adult human skeleton is:
(a) 300 (b) 208 (c) 206 (d) 186
Answer: (c)
Solution: 300 is the trap — infants have about 300 bones, which fuse as they grow. The adult skeleton has 206 bones, so the answer is 206.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Biology

39. Which part of the nephron is mainly responsible for ultrafiltration?
(a) Loop of Henle (b) Glomerulus (c) Distal convoluted tubule (d) Collecting duct
Answer: (b)
Solution: The loop of Henle is tempting because it handles concentration of urine, but that is reabsorption, not filtration. High-pressure filtration occurs across the glomerular capillaries into Bowman's capsule, so the answer is glomerulus.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Biology

40. Which blood cells are primarily responsible for immunity?
(a) Red blood cells (b) Platelets (c) White blood cells (d) Plasma cells only
Answer: (c)
Solution: Platelets are the distractor since they are also cell fragments in blood, but they function in clotting. Leukocytes defend the body against pathogens, so the answer is white blood cells.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Biology

41. The longest bone in the human body is the:
(a) Tibia (b) Humerus (c) Femur (d) Fibula
Answer: (c)
Solution: The humerus is a trap because it is the longest bone of the arm, and students confuse arm with whole body. The femur (thigh bone) is the longest and strongest bone, so the answer is femur.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Biology

42. Which of the following statements about human respiration is correct?
(a) The diaphragm relaxes and domes up during exhalation (b) The diaphragm contracts during exhalation (c) Ribs move upward during exhalation (d) Lung volume increases during exhalation
Answer: (a)
Solution: Statement one is the almost-correct trap — the diaphragm contracts during inhalation, not exhalation, a classic reversal. During exhalation the diaphragm relaxes and moves upward, reducing thoracic volume, so the answer is that the diaphragm relaxes and domes up during exhalation.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Biology

43. Bile, which helps in fat digestion, is stored in the:
(a) Liver (b) Gall bladder (c) Pancreas (d) Duodenum
Answer: (b)
Solution: The liver is the tempting answer because bile is produced there, but the question asks where it is stored. Bile is concentrated and stored in the gall bladder, so the answer is gall bladder.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Biology

44. The master gland of the human endocrine system is the:
(a) Thyroid (b) Pituitary gland (c) Adrenal gland (d) Pineal gland
Answer: (b)
Solution: The thyroid is a common wrong pick because it is the largest endocrine gland, but size is not the criterion here. The pituitary controls other endocrine glands through its tropic hormones, so the answer is pituitary gland.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Biology

45. Which of the following is the correct sequence of the passage of food through the human digestive tract?
(a) Mouth → Oesophagus → Stomach → Small intestine → Large intestine (b) Mouth → Stomach → Oesophagus → Small intestine → Large intestine (c) Mouth → Oesophagus → Small intestine → Stomach → Large intestine (d) Mouth → Oesophagus → Stomach → Large intestine → Small intestine
Answer: (a)
Solution: The last option is the chronological trap — placing the large intestine before the small intestine reverses the actual order of digestion and absorption. Food passes from mouth to oesophagus, stomach, small intestine and then large intestine, so the answer is mouth → oesophagus → stomach → small intestine → large intestine.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Biology

46. Which protein in red blood cells carries oxygen?
(a) Albumin (b) Haemoglobin (c) Fibrinogen (d) Myosin
Answer: (b)
Solution: Myosin is the similar-sounding trap, but it is a muscle contraction protein, not an oxygen carrier. Haemoglobin binds oxygen in the lungs and releases it in tissues, so the answer is haemoglobin.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Biology

47. The structural and functional unit of the nervous system is the:
(a) Neuron (b) Nephron (c) Axon (d) Synapse
Answer: (a)
Solution: Nephron is the near-identical name trap, but it belongs to the kidney; axon is only a part of the cell. The neuron is the complete structural and functional unit of the nervous system, so the answer is neuron.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Biology

48. Which organ in the human body produces urea?
(a) Kidney (b) Pancreas (c) Liver (d) Spleen
Answer: (c)
Solution: The kidney is the classic trap because it excretes urea in urine, but excretion is not production. Urea is formed in the liver from ammonia via the urea cycle, so the answer is liver.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Biology

49. The average volume of blood in a healthy adult human body is about:
(a) 2–3 litres (b) 4–6 litres (c) 8–10 litres (d) 10–12 litres
Answer: (b)
Solution: The 2–3 litre option is a trap arising from confusion with blood plasma volume alone. Total blood volume in an average adult is roughly 4–6 litres, so the answer is 4–6 litres.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Biology

50. Which of the following statements about the human heart is correct?
(a) The right ventricle pumps deoxygenated blood to the lungs (b) The left ventricle pumps blood to the lungs (c) The heart has three chambers (d) Valves allow blood to flow in both directions
Answer: (a)
Solution: The second statement is the almost-correct trap — the left ventricle is the strongest chamber, but it pumps oxygenated blood to the body, not the lungs. The right ventricle sends deoxygenated blood to the lungs through the pulmonary artery, so the answer is that the right ventricle pumps deoxygenated blood to the lungs.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Biology
