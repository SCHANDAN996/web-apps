# 📊 PYQ Analysis — Biology: Human Body (Advanced Level)

## 1. How Exams Ask Questions from This Chapter

Human Body is one of the most dependable areas of Biology in UPSC, SSC CGL/CHSL, IBPS, RRB NTPC and State PCS prelims. The questions are almost always **fact-recall with a twist** — the examiner knows you have read the NCERT basics, so the marks are decided by traps, not by obscure facts.

**Sub-topics that appear again and again:**

- **Blood and circulation** — blood groups (ABO, Rh factor), universal donor/recipient, functions of RBC/WBC/platelets, normal blood pressure (120/80 mmHg), haemoglobin, ECG.
- **Digestive system** — enzymes and their substrates (ptyalin, pepsin, trypsin, lipase), where digestion of each nutrient begins, role of bile (emulsification, not an enzyme), longest part of the alimentary canal (small intestine).
- **Vitamins and deficiency diseases** — the single most repeated cluster: scurvy (C), rickets (D), beriberi (B1), night blindness (A), pellagra (B3). Also which vitamins are fat-soluble (A, D, E, K) and which vitamin is synthesised in the skin by sunlight (D).
- **Endocrine glands and hormones** — insulin (pancreas, lowers blood sugar), thyroxine (thyroid, iodine needed), adrenaline ("fight or flight"), pituitary as "master gland", largest endocrine gland (thyroid).
- **Nervous system** — neuron as structural unit, CNS vs PNS, largest part of the brain (cerebrum), reflex arc, cerebellum and balance.
- **Skeletal and muscular systems** — 206 bones in an adult, smallest bone (stapes in the ear), longest bone (femur), hardest substance in the body (tooth enamel).
- **Excretory and respiratory systems** — nephron as the functional unit of the kidney, dialysis, exchange of gases in alveoli, normal respiratory rate.
- **Cell basics** — mitochondria (powerhouse), ribosomes (protein synthesis), cell membrane, DNA location.

## 2. Exam-wise Tendencies (in words, not numbers)

- **UPSC (Prelims):** Prefers conceptual and statement-based questions — e.g., which hormone does what, why a deficiency occurs, or linking a vitamin to its chemical name. Negative phrasing ("Which of the following is NOT correct") is common.
- **SSC CGL / CHSL:** Direct one-liners — "Which gland is called the master gland?", "Normal human body temperature?" (37°C / 98.6°F). Speed matters more than depth.
- **Banking (IBPS/SBI):** Fewer questions, usually vitamins, blood groups, or disease–deficiency matching.
- **Railway (RRB NTPC/Group D):** Very high repetition of classic facts — 206 bones, stapes, universal donor O-negative, insulin and diabetes.
- **State PCS:** Mixes static facts with health schemes; statement-based pairs (Assertion–Reason) appear occasionally.

## 3. The Examiner's Traps (जाल) — Know These Before the Exam

1. **Almost Correct Statement:** "Bile is an enzyme secreted by the liver that digests fats." Sounds right — but bile is **not an enzyme**; it only emulsifies fats. Similarly: "Universal donor is O-positive" — wrong, it is **O-negative**.
2. **Chronological / Sequence Confusion:** Order of digestion (mouth → oesophagus → stomach → small intestine → large intestine), or the sequence of the reflex arc. Examiners swap two steps.
3. **Similar-Sounding Names:** Beriberi (B1) vs pellagra (B3) vs scurvy (C); thyroxine vs insulin; cerebrum vs cerebellum. Options are deliberately placed side by side.
4. **Negative Phrasing:** "Which of the following is NOT a function of the liver?" Under time pressure, candidates pick the correct function instead of the wrong one. Underline NOT in the question paper.
5. **Unit / Number Swap:** Blood pressure 120/80 vs 80/120; body temperature 37°C vs 37°F; 206 bones (adult) vs ~300 (infant). Numbers are the cheapest trap to set.
6. **Organ–Function Mismatch:** "Urine formation occurs in the ureter" — the ureter only carries urine; the **nephron in the kidney** forms it.

## 4. The 15/45-Second Rule

- **15 seconds:** Pure recall facts — master gland, 206 bones, vitamin C deficiency, universal donor. If you have revised flashcards, answer instantly and bank time.
- **45 seconds:** Statement-based or match-the-following questions (vitamin–disease pairs, gland–hormone pairs). Read every option fully; the trap is usually in the last option you would have skipped.
- **Skip:** A question asking for an obscure hormone's exact chemical structure or a rare syndrome — mark and move; negative marking makes blind guessing costly.

## 5. Ten High-Yield PYQ-Style Questions with Solutions

**Q1.** Which blood group is called the universal donor?
(a) AB-positive (b) O-positive (c) O-negative (d) AB-negative
**Answer:** (c)
**Solution:** O-negative blood has neither A/B antigens nor the Rh antigen, so it can be transfused to anyone. Trap type: **Almost Correct** — O-positive is the common wrong pick because people forget the Rh factor.
**Source:** NCERT Class 11 Biology (Body Fluids and Circulation)

**Q2.** Deficiency of vitamin B1 (thiamine) causes:
(a) Scurvy (b) Beriberi (c) Pellagra (d) Rickets
**Answer:** (b)
**Solution:** Beriberi is caused by thiamine (B1) deficiency; pellagra is B3, scurvy is C, rickets is D. Trap type: **Similar-Sounding Names** — all four options are classic deficiency diseases.
**Source:** NCERT Class 12 Biology / standard nutrition science

**Q3.** Which of the following is NOT a function of the liver?
(a) Production of bile (b) Storage of glycogen (c) Production of insulin (d) Detoxification
**Answer:** (c)
**Solution:** Insulin is produced by the beta cells of the pancreas, not the liver. Trap type: **Negative Phrasing** — candidates in a hurry pick (a) because it is a true liver function.
**Source:** NCERT Class 11 Biology

**Q4.** The functional unit of the kidney is the:
(a) Neuron (b) Nephron (c) Alveolus (d) Villus
**Answer:** (b)
**Solution:** The nephron filters blood and forms urine. Neuron (nerve cell) and nephron are deliberately paired. Trap type: **Similar-Sounding Names**.
**Source:** NCERT Class 11 Biology (Excretory Products and their Elimination)

**Q5.** Bile juice helps in digestion by:
(a) Acting as an enzyme that breaks down fat molecules (b) Emulsifying fats into small droplets (c) Digesting proteins in the stomach (d) Absorbing water in the colon
**Answer:** (b)
**Solution:** Bile contains no enzymes; it emulsifies fats, increasing surface area for lipase. Trap type: **Almost Correct** — option (a) is the classic wrong statement.
**Source:** NCERT Class 11 Biology (Digestion and Absorption)

**Q6.** Arrange the parts of the alimentary canal in the correct order of food passage:
(a) Mouth → Stomach → Oesophagus → Small intestine (b) Mouth → Oesophagus → Stomach → Small intestine (c) Oesophagus → Mouth → Stomach → Small intestine (d) Mouth → Oesophagus → Small intestine → Stomach
**Answer:** (b)
**Solution:** Food travels mouth → oesophagus → stomach → small intestine → large intestine. Trap type: **Chronological Confusion** — options (a) and (d) swap adjacent organs.
**Source:** NCERT Class 11 Biology

**Q7.** The smallest bone in the human body is the:
(a) Femur (b) Stapes (c) Tibia (d) Humerus
**Answer:** (b)
**Solution:** The stapes, in the middle ear, is the smallest bone; the femur is the longest — both are offered together to test whether you read "smallest" or "longest". Trap type: **Similar-Sounding / Paired Facts**.
**Source:** NCERT-based standard anatomy

**Q8.** Which gland is known as the "master gland" of the human body?
(a) Thyroid (b) Adrenal (c) Pituitary (d) Pancreas
**Answer:** (c)
**Solution:** The pituitary secretes hormones that regulate other endocrine glands. The thyroid is the *largest* endocrine gland — a frequent confusion. Trap type: **Almost Correct paired fact**. *(15-second question — answer instantly.)*
**Source:** NCERT Class 11 Biology (Chemical Coordination)

**Q9.** Match the hormone with its source gland: Insulin — ?
(a) Thyroid (b) Pancreas (c) Adrenal (d) Pituitary
**Answer:** (b)
**Solution:** Insulin is secreted by the beta cells of the islets of Langerhans in the pancreas; it lowers blood glucose. Trap type: **Organ–Function Mismatch** — adrenaline (adrenal) and thyroxine (thyroid) are the distractors. *(45-second question if framed as a four-pair matching set — verify each pair before marking.)*
**Source:** NCERT Class 11 Biology

**Q10.** Normal human blood pressure is approximately:
(a) 80/120 mmHg (b) 120/80 mmHg (c) 140/60 mmHg (d) 100/100 mmHg
**Answer:** (b)
**Solution:** Systolic pressure (~120) is written first, diastolic (~80) second. Trap type: **Number Swap** — option (a) reverses the two values.
**Source:** NCERT Class 11 Biology (Body Fluids and Circulation)

## 6. Revision Takeaway

Revise in **pairs of confusable facts** (donor vs recipient, cerebrum vs cerebellum, B1 vs B3, largest gland vs master gland, 206 adult vs ~300 infant bones) — because that is exactly how the examiner builds options. For every fact you memorise, ask: "What is the almost-correct wrong version of this?" That question is your real PYQ preparation.
