🪄 MEMORY HOOKS & SHORT TRICKS — Indian Polity (Advanced)

---

**BOX 1 — Fundamental Rights: "RACE-EC"**
Six Fundamental Rights (Articles 12–35):
**R**ight to Equality (14–18) | **A**nti-exploitation / Right against Exploitation (23–24) | **C**ultural & Educational Rights (29–30) | **E**… wait, fix the order — say it as a sentence:
**"Every Religious Person Can Challenge Exploitation"**
- **E**quality (14–18)
- **R**eligion — Freedom of Religion (25–28)
- **P**rotection — Constitutional Remedies (32)
- **C**ultural & Educational (29–30)
- **C**ivil — Freedom (19–22)
- **E**xploitation — Right against (23–24)
Trap: Right to Property is NOT here anymore — it was deleted by the 44th Amendment (1978) and is now only a legal right under Article 300A.

---

**BOX 2 — Article 32: "Ambedkar's Heart & Soul"**
Dr. B.R. Ambedkar called Article 32 the **"heart and soul of the Constitution."**
Hook: *"If your rights are hurt, Ambedkar gives you his HEART — Article 32."*
Trap: Article 226 (High Courts) is actually WIDER than Article 32 — HC can act for legal rights too, SC only for Fundamental Rights.

---

**BOX 3 — Writs: "HMPQC — He Must Produce Quo-Certiorari"**
Five writs under Articles 32 & 226:
- **H**abeas Corpus — "produce the body" (against illegal detention)
- **M**andamus — "we command" (to a public official/body)
- **P**rohibition — issued by a higher court to a LOWER court to stop proceedings
- **Q**uo Warranto — "by what authority?" (challenges a person's claim to a public office)
- **C**ertiorari — higher court quashes a lower court's order
Hook: *"The HMPQC police: Habeas frees the body, Mandamus commands the officer, Prohibition stops the judge, Quo Warranto checks the chair, Certiorari cancels the verdict."*
Trap: Prohibition = BEFORE the decision; Certiorari = AFTER the decision.

---

**BOX 4 — DPSP: "Gandhi's Irish Stew, Article 36–51"**
DPSP = Part IV, Articles 36–51, borrowed from **Ireland** (which took it from Spain).
Hook: *"The Irish stew has no legal salt — DPSPs are NOT enforceable by courts (Article 37)."*
Trap: Article 44 (Uniform Civil Code), Article 40 (Panchayats), Article 50 (separation of judiciary from executive) are all DPSPs — examiners love asking "which of these is a DPSP?"

---

**BOX 5 — Fundamental Duties: "11 Duties, 42nd + 86th"**
Originally **10 duties** added by the **42nd Amendment, 1976** (Swaran Singh Committee), inspired by the **USSR**. The **86th Amendment, 2002** added the **11th** (parent/guardian to provide education to child aged 6–14).
Hook: *"42 brought ten, 86 made eleven — like a cricket team completed late."*
Trap: Fundamental Duties apply only to CITIZENS, not foreigners; Fundamental Rights (some) apply to all persons.

---

**BOX 6 — President's Election: "MP + MLA, but NOT MLC"**
Electoral college = elected MPs of both Houses + elected MLAs of States + UTs of Delhi & Puducherry.
Hook: *"The President is chosen by the 'Lok Sabha family' — MLCs (Legislative Councils) are NOT invited to the wedding."*
Trap: Nominated members of Parliament do NOT vote in Presidential election but DO vote in Vice-Presidential election.

---

**BOX 7 — Money Bill: "Speaker's Signature, Article 110"**
Only the **Lok Sabha Speaker** certifies a Money Bill (Article 110). Rajya Sabha can only suggest — it must return the bill within **14 days**.
Hook: *"Money speaks only one language — the Speaker's. RS gets 14 days to window-shop, then it's gone."*
Trap: The President cannot return a Money Bill for reconsideration (it is introduced only with his prior recommendation).

---

**BOX 8 — Amendment: "368 — Half the House, Half the States"**
Article 368: Special majority = majority of total membership + 2/3 of members present and voting. For federal features (e.g., election of President, distribution of powers), ratification by **half the States** is also needed.
Hook: *"To bend the Constitution you need 368 muscles — and for federal bones, half the states must nod."*
Trap: Article 368 is in Part XX — and the "basic structure" doctrine (Kesavananda Bharati, 1973) limits even this power.

---

**BOX 9 — Emergency Trio: "352 War, 356 Failure, 360 Wallet"**
- **352** — National Emergency (war/external aggression/armed rebellion)
- **356** — President's Rule (failure of constitutional machinery in a State)
- **360** — Financial Emergency (never proclaimed so far)
Hook: *"352 = soldiers, 356 = state government collapses, 360 = empty treasury (never used — the wallet never fully emptied)."*
Trap: After the 44th Amendment, "internal disturbance" was replaced by "armed rebellion" in Article 352.

---

**BOX 10 — Schedules: "12 Schedules — TEARS OF OLD PM"**
A classic memory string for the 12 Schedules:
**T**erritory (1) | **E**moluments (2) | **A**ffirmations/Oaths (3) | **R**ajya Sabha seats (4) | **S**cheduled Areas (5) | **O**ther tribal areas — Assam, Meghalaya, Tripura, Mizoram (6) | **F**ederal lists — Union/State/Concurrent (7) | **O**fficial languages (8) | **L**and reforms (9) | **D**efection — Anti-Defection (10) | **P**anchayats (11) | **M**unicipalities (12)
Hook: *"TEARS OF OLD PM — a politician cries when the 10th Schedule (defection) catches him."*
Trap: Originally there were only **8 Schedules**; 9th (1st Amendment, 1951), 10th (52nd, 1985), 11th & 12th (73rd & 74th, 1992) were added later.

---

**BOX 11 — Panchayati Raj: "73 = Village, 74 = City"**
73rd Amendment → Panchayats (Part IX, 11th Schedule, 29 subjects); 74th Amendment → Municipalities (Part IXA, 12th Schedule, 18 subjects).
Hook: *"73 comes first, village comes first — India lives in its villages before its cities."*
Trap: Both came into force in **1993** (24 April and 1 June respectively) — not 1992 (the year of the Acts).

---

**BOX 12 — CAG & AG: "The Twin Auditors — 148 & 76"**
CAG of India — Article **148** (audits Union + States); Attorney General — Article **76** (chief legal advisor).
Hook: *"CAG counts the money (148), AG argues the law (76) — one holds the calculator, the other the Constitution."*
Trap: CAG audits but is removed like a Supreme Court judge; AG holds office during the pleasure of the President and is NOT a member of the Cabinet.

---

**BOX 13 — Borrowed Features: "The Constitution's World Tour"**
- **UK** — Parliamentary system, Rule of Law, single citizenship
- **USA** — Fundamental Rights, Judicial Review, impeachment of President
- **Ireland** — DPSP, nomination of Rajya Sabha members
- **Canada** — Federation with strong Centre, residuary powers with Centre
- **Australia** — Concurrent List, joint sitting
- **Germany (Weimar)** — Emergency provisions
- **USSR** — Fundamental Duties, Five-Year Plan ideals
- **South Africa** — Amendment procedure, election of Rajya Sabha members
- **France** — Republic, ideals of liberty-equality-fraternity
- **Japan** — Procedure established by law (Article 21)
Hook: *"The Constitution is a well-travelled suitcase — but the tag on the handle is Indian."*
Trap: "Due process of law" is American; India originally chose Japan's "procedure established by law" — but Maneka Gandhi (1978) imported due-process spirit into Article 21.

---

**BOX 14 — Key Articles Number Chain: "14-19-21-32 = Ladder of Liberty"**
14 (equality before law) → 19 (six freedoms) → 21 (life & personal liberty) → 32 (remedies).
Hook: *"Climb the ladder: 14 says all are equal, 19 sets you free, 21 protects your life, 32 lets you fight for all three."*
Trap: Articles 20 and 21 can NEVER be suspended — even during Emergency (44th Amendment).

---

**BOX 15 — Constitutional Bodies Quick-Fire: "EC-UPSC-FC-CAG = 324, 315, 280, 148"**
- Election Commission — **324**
- UPSC — **315**
- Finance Commission — **280**
- CAG — **148**
Hook: *"Countdown to power: 324 counts votes, 315 picks officers, 280 splits the money, 148 checks the bill."*
Trap: Finance Commission is set up every **5 years** by the President; Election Commission and UPSC are permanent.

## ⏭️ SKIP STRATEGY — When to Skip a Question

**Tip 1 — The "One Blank Link" Rule:**
If a question asks you to match FOUR pairs (e.g., four borrowed features with four countries, or four Schedules with their subjects) and you are sure of only two but totally blank on one — **skip immediately**. In matching questions, one unknown link destroys the whole chain, and guessing between two remaining arrangements is a 50-50 coin toss with negative marking. A blank costs 0; a wrong guess costs marks.

**Tip 2 — The "Exact Number Trap" Rule:**
If a question demands a precise figure you only vaguely remember — exact percentage of votes, exact number of subjects in the 11th Schedule (29), exact days for Rajya Sabha to return a Money Bill (14) — and two options differ by just one digit (e.g., 13 vs 14 days), **skip unless you can recall the hook**. Examiners deliberately place the "almost-right" number next to the right one; vague memory always picks the trap.

**Tip 3 — The "Statement Soup" Rule:**
If a question has 4–5 long statements (I, II, III, IV) about committees, amendments, or case names (Golaknath, Kesavananda, Minerva Mills) and you cannot verify even ONE statement confidently, **skip and return only if time remains**. Statement-based polity questions are designed so that one swapped year (1973 vs 1976) flips the answer. Attempt them only when you can mentally tag each statement TRUE/FALSE with a reason — never on "it sounds right."
