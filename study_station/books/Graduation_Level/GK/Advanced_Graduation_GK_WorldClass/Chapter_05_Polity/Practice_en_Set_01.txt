1. The Constitution of India was adopted by the Constituent Assembly on:
(a) 2 October 1950 (b) 15 August 1947 (c) 26 January 1950 (d) 26 November 1949
Answer: (d)
Solution: Many students confuse the date of adoption with the date of enforcement. The Constitution was adopted on 26 November 1949 (celebrated as Constitution Day), while it came into force on 26 January 1950, so the answer is 26 November 1949.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Indian Constitution at Work

2. Who was the Chairman of the Drafting Committee of the Constituent Assembly?
(a) Dr. Rajendra Prasad (b) Dr. B.R. Ambedkar (c) Jawaharlal Nehru (d) Sardar Vallabhbhai Patel
Answer: (b)
Solution: Dr. Rajendra Prasad is the tempting wrong option because he was the President of the Constituent Assembly, not the Drafting Committee Chairman. Dr. B.R. Ambedkar chaired the Drafting Committee, so the answer is Dr. B.R. Ambedkar.

3. The idea of the Preamble of the Indian Constitution was borrowed from the Constitution of:
(a) United States of America (b) Canada (c) Ireland (d) United Kingdom
Answer: (a)
Solution: Ireland is a tempting wrong option because the Directive Principles were borrowed from Ireland, not the Preamble. The idea of the Preamble came from the US Constitution, so the answer is United States of America.

4. How many Fundamental Rights are currently guaranteed by the Constitution of India?
(a) Five (b) Eight (c) Seven (d) Six
Answer: (d)
Solution: Seven is the tempting wrong option because originally there were seven Fundamental Rights. The Right to Property was removed by the 44th Amendment (1978), leaving six, so the answer is Six.

5. Which Article of the Constitution abolishes untouchability?
(a) Article 14 (b) Article 15 (c) Article 17 (d) Article 19
Answer: (c)
Solution: Article 15 is the tempting wrong option because it prohibits discrimination on grounds of religion, race, caste, sex or place of birth, which sounds similar but is not about untouchability. Article 17 abolishes untouchability, so the answer is Article 17.

6. The Directive Principles of State Policy are contained in which Part of the Constitution?
(a) Part IVA (b) Part III (c) Part IV (d) Part V
Answer: (c)
Solution: Part III contains Fundamental Rights and Part IVA contains Fundamental Duties — both are frequent confusions. Directive Principles are in Part IV (Articles 36–51), so the answer is Part IV.

7. Fundamental Duties were added to the Constitution by which amendment?
(a) 42nd Amendment (b) 44th Amendment (c) 24th Amendment (d) 52nd Amendment
Answer: (a)
Solution: The 44th Amendment is a tempting option because it also made major changes, but it removed the Right to Property from Fundamental Rights. Fundamental Duties were added by the 42nd Amendment, 1976, so the answer is 42nd Amendment.

8. Who is known as the 'Father of the Indian Constitution'?
(a) Mahatma Gandhi (b) Dr. B.R. Ambedkar (c) Dr. Rajendra Prasad (d) B.N. Rau
Answer: (b)
Solution: B.N. Rau is the tempting wrong option because he was the Constitutional Adviser who prepared the initial draft. Dr. B.R. Ambedkar, as Drafting Committee Chairman, is called the Father of the Indian Constitution, so the answer is Dr. B.R. Ambedkar.

9. The President of India is elected by:
(a) Direct election by the people (b) An electoral college of elected members of both Houses of Parliament and State Legislative Assemblies (c) Members of Lok Sabha only (d) Members of Rajya Sabha only
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is assuming all MPs vote — nominated members of Parliament and members of Legislative Councils do not vote. The electoral college consists of elected MPs of both Houses and elected MLAs of States and the Union Territories of Delhi and Puducherry, so the answer is an electoral college of elected members of both Houses of Parliament and State Legislative Assemblies.

10. The minimum age to become the President of India is:
(a) 25 years (b) 30 years (c) 40 years (d) 35 years
Answer: (d)
Solution: 25 years is the minimum age for Lok Sabha membership and 30 years for Rajya Sabha — both are common confusions. For the President it is 35 years, so the answer is 35 years.

11. Which Article empowers the President to promulgate ordinances when Parliament is not in session?
(a) Article 123 (b) Article 213 (c) Article 356 (d) Article 360
Answer: (a)
Solution: Article 213 is a tempting trap because it deals with the same ordinance power, but for the Governor of a State, not the President. Article 123 covers the President's ordinance power, so the answer is Article 123.

12. The Rajya Sabha is also known as:
(a) House of the People (b) Council of States (c) Upper Chamber of States (d) Federal House of Elders
Answer: (b)
Solution: House of the People is the tempting wrong option, but that is the Lok Sabha. The Rajya Sabha is the Council of States, so the answer is Council of States.

13. What is the maximum strength of the Lok Sabha as fixed by the Constitution?
(a) 545 (b) 550 (c) 552 (d) 560
Answer: (c)
Solution: 545 misleads many because it resembles the current elected strength of the House. The constitutional maximum is 552 (530 from States, 20 from Union Territories, 2 nominated Anglo-Indians), so the answer is 552.

14. Money Bills can be introduced only in:
(a) Rajya Sabha (b) Either House of Parliament (c) Lok Sabha (d) A joint sitting of both Houses
Answer: (c)
Solution: A joint sitting under Article 108 is never possible for a Money Bill, which is a trap option. Under Article 110, a Money Bill can be introduced only in the Lok Sabha, so the answer is Lok Sabha.

15. Who presides over a joint sitting of the two Houses of Parliament?
(a) The President (b) The Vice-President (c) The Chief Justice of India (d) The Speaker of the Lok Sabha
Answer: (d)
Solution: The Vice-President is the tempting wrong option because he presides over the Rajya Sabha, but not over a joint sitting. The Speaker of the Lok Sabha presides over a joint sitting, so the answer is the Speaker of the Lok Sabha.

16. The concept of Judicial Review in India is borrowed from:
(a) United Kingdom (b) Australia (c) United States of America (d) Ireland
Answer: (c)
Solution: The UK is a wrong but tempting choice because many Indian parliamentary features come from Britain, but the UK follows parliamentary sovereignty. Judicial Review was borrowed from the US Constitution, so the answer is United States of America.

17. Which of the following writs is issued to quash the order of a lower court?
(a) Mandamus (b) Certiorari (c) Habeas Corpus (d) Prohibition
Answer: (b)
Solution: Prohibition is the closest trap — it is issued to stop a lower court before it gives a decision, while Certiorari is issued after a decision to quash it. Since the question asks about quashing an order, the answer is Certiorari.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Indian Constitution at Work

18. The Panchayati Raj system was constitutionalised by which amendment?
(a) 42nd Amendment (b) 52nd Amendment (c) 74th Amendment (d) 73rd Amendment
Answer: (d)
Solution: The 74th Amendment is the classic trap — it deals with Municipalities (urban local bodies), not Panchayats. The 73rd Amendment (1992) constitutionalised Panchayati Raj, so the answer is 73rd Amendment.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Indian Constitution at Work

19. The Election Commission of India is a:
(a) Constitutional body (b) Statutory body (c) Executive body (d) Advisory body
Answer: (a)
Solution: Bodies like NITI Aayog are executive bodies, which confuses students into picking non-constitutional options. The Election Commission is established under Article 324 of the Constitution, so the answer is Constitutional body.

20. Which Article deals with the amendment of the Constitution?
(a) Article 360 (b) Article 356 (c) Article 370 (d) Article 368
Answer: (d)
Solution: Article 356 (President's Rule) and Article 360 (Financial Emergency) are number-confusion traps. Article 368 deals with the power and procedure of constitutional amendment, so the answer is Article 368.

21. Consider the following statements about the Preamble:
1. The word 'Secular' was added by the 42nd Amendment, 1976.
2. The Preamble is enforceable in courts of law.
3. The Preamble was held to be a part of the Constitution in the Kesavananda Bharati case.
Which of the statements given above is/are correct?
(a) 1 only (b) 1 and 3 only (c) 2 and 3 only (d) 1, 2 and 3
Answer: (b)
Solution: Statement 2 is the trap — it sounds plausible but the Preamble is non-justiciable, i.e., not enforceable in courts. Statements 1 and 3 are correct, so the answer is 1 and 3 only.

22. Which of the following pairs is correctly matched?
(a) Concurrent List — Government of India Act, 1919 (b) Directive Principles — Constitution of Ireland (c) Emergency provisions — Constitution of Canada (d) Fundamental Duties — Constitution of Japan
Answer: (b)
Solution: The traps: the Concurrent List came from Australia, Fundamental Duties came from the USSR, and Emergency provisions came from Germany. Directive Principles were borrowed from Ireland, so the answer is Directive Principles — Constitution of Ireland.

23. Arrange the following events in chronological order:
1. First meeting of the Constituent Assembly
2. Adoption of the National Flag
3. Enforcement of the Constitution
4. Adoption of the Constitution
(a) 2-1-4-3 (b) 1-4-2-3 (c) 1-2-4-3 (d) 2-4-1-3
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is assuming the National Flag was adopted before the Constituent Assembly first met. The Assembly first met on 9 December 1946, the National Flag was adopted on 22 July 1947, the Constitution was adopted on 26 November 1949 and enforced on 26 January 1950, so the answer is 1-2-4-3.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Indian Constitution at Work

24. Which of the following is NOT a condition for election as the President of India?
(a) He must be a citizen of India (b) He must be a member of the Lok Sabha at the time of election (c) He must have completed 35 years of age (d) He must be qualified for election as a member of the Lok Sabha
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is confusing 'qualified to be a member of Lok Sabha' with 'must actually be a member'. The President need not be a sitting member of any House; he must only be qualified for election to the Lok Sabha, so the answer is that he must be a member of the Lok Sabha at the time of election.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Indian Constitution at Work

25. Consider the following statements about the Attorney General of India:
1. He is appointed by the President under Article 76.
2. He has the right to vote in Parliament.
3. He must be qualified to be appointed a judge of the Supreme Court.
Which of the statements given above is/are correct?
(a) 1 only (b) 1, 2 and 3 (c) 1 and 3 only (d) 2 and 3 only
Answer: (c)
Solution: Statement 2 is the trap — the Attorney General has the right to speak and take part in the proceedings of Parliament but has no right to vote. Statements 1 and 3 are correct, so the answer is 1 and 3 only.
