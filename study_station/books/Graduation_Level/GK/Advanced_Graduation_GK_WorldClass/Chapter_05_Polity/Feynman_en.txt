# 🧒 Feynman Technique — Indian Polity

## The Hardest Concept Made Simple: **Basic Structure Doctrine & Article 368**

Most aspirants can recite "Article 368 deals with amendment of the Constitution" — but freeze when asked: *"Can Parliament amend ANYTHING in the Constitution?"* Let's fix that forever.

---

### Explain It Like I'm 12

Imagine the Constitution is a **house** your family lives in.

- **Parliament (Article 368)** is like the family members who can **renovate** the house — repaint walls, add a room, change the windows. That's an *amendment*.
- But can they **demolish the foundation pillars** and still call it the same house? **No.** If you remove the foundation, the house collapses — it's no longer the same house.

In **Kesavananda Bharati v. State of Kerala (1973)**, the Supreme Court said exactly this: Parliament can amend the Constitution (even Fundamental Rights), **but it cannot destroy or damage its "basic structure"** — the foundation pillars like:

- Supremacy of the Constitution
- Democratic and republican form of government
- Secularism
- Federal character
- Separation of powers
- Judicial review
- Free and fair elections

⚠️ **The trap:** The Constitution itself never lists these pillars. The Court decides case by case what counts as "basic structure." So if an exam option says *"The basic structure is defined in Article 368"* — it's **wrong**.

---

### The Daily-Life Example

Think of **editing a photo** on your phone:
- Cropping, filters, brightness = **amendments** (allowed).
- Deleting the photo itself and claiming it's "just an edit" = **destroying the basic structure** (not allowed).

Parliament's power under Article 368 is the editing app — powerful, but it cannot delete the photo.

---

### 🪄 Unforgettable Trick

**"368 can RENOVATE, not DEMOLISH — Kesavananda holds the key (1973)."**

Or remember the pillars with: **"S-D-S-F-S-J-E"** → *"Supreme Democracy Secures Federalism, Separation, Judiciary-review, Elections."*
