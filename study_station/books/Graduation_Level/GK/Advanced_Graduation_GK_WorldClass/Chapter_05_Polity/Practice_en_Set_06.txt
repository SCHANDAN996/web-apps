Questions 126–150: Hard

126. Which of the following statements about the 42nd Constitutional Amendment Act, 1976 is correct?
(a) It reduced the tenure of the Lok Sabha from five years to four years (b) It added the words 'Socialist', 'Secular' and 'Integrity' to the Preamble (c) It abolished the Fundamental Duties from the Constitution (d) It transferred education from the State List to the Union List
Answer: (b)
Solution: The tempting wrong option is the first one — the 42nd Amendment actually extended the Lok Sabha tenure from five to six years, not reduced it. It added 'Socialist', 'Secular' and 'Integrity' to the Preamble, inserted Fundamental Duties, and moved education to the Concurrent List, so the answer is that it added the three words to the Preamble.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Indian Constitution at Work

127. Consider the following statements about a Money Bill:
1. It can be introduced only in the Lok Sabha.
2. The Rajya Sabha can amend it but cannot reject it.
3. The Speaker's decision on whether a bill is a Money Bill is final.
Which of the statements given above is/are correct?
(a) 1 and 3 only (b) 1 and 2 only (c) 2 and 3 only (d) 1, 2 and 3
Answer: (a)
Solution: Statement 2 is the trap — the Rajya Sabha can neither amend nor reject a Money Bill; it can only make recommendations within 14 days, which the Lok Sabha may accept or reject. Statements 1 and 3 are correct under Article 110, so the answer is 1 and 3 only.
Source: M. Laxmikanth, Indian Polity

128. The doctrine of 'Basic Structure' of the Constitution was propounded by the Supreme Court in which case?
(a) Golaknath v. State of Punjab (b) S.R. Bommai v. Union of India (c) Minerva Mills v. Union of India (d) Kesavananda Bharati v. State of Kerala
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is the first option — Golaknath (1967) held that Parliament cannot amend Fundamental Rights, but it did not propound the basic structure doctrine. That doctrine was laid down in Kesavananda Bharati (1973), and Minerva Mills (1980) only reaffirmed it, so the answer is Kesavananda Bharati v. State of Kerala.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Indian Constitution at Work

129. Which of the following pairs is correctly matched?
(a) Article 360 — Financial Emergency (b) Article 356 — National Emergency (c) Article 352 — Financial Emergency (d) Article 365 — Armed Rebellion
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap lies in the middle options, which swap the two emergencies: Article 352 deals with National Emergency and Article 356 with President's Rule. Article 360 provides for Financial Emergency, which has never been proclaimed in India, so the answer is Article 360 — Financial Emergency.
Source: M. Laxmikanth, Indian Polity

130. With reference to the office of the Vice-President of India, consider the following statements:
1. He is elected by an electoral college consisting of members of both Houses of Parliament, including nominated members.
2. He can be removed by a resolution of the Lok Sabha agreed to by the Rajya Sabha.
Which of the statements given above is/are correct?
(a) 2 only (b) 1 only (c) Both 1 and 2 (d) Neither 1 nor 2
Answer: (b)
Solution: Statement 2 is the trap — the removal resolution must originate in the Rajya Sabha and then be agreed to by the Lok Sabha, not the other way round. Statement 1 is correct: unlike the President's electoral college, the Vice-President's includes nominated members of Parliament, so the answer is 1 only.
Source: M. Laxmikanth, Indian Polity

131. The 73rd Constitutional Amendment Act, 1992 added which of the following to the Constitution?
(a) Part IX-A and the Twelfth Schedule (b) Part IX and the Eleventh Schedule (c) Part VIII and the Tenth Schedule (d) Part IX and the Twelfth Schedule
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is the second option — Part IX-A and the Twelfth Schedule relate to Municipalities and were added by the 74th Amendment, not the 73rd. The 73rd Amendment added Part IX on Panchayats and the Eleventh Schedule with 29 subjects, so the answer is Part IX and the Eleventh Schedule.

132. Which of the following is NOT a ground for disqualification of a Member of Parliament under the Tenth Schedule (Anti-Defection Law)?
(a) Criticising the party's policies in a public speech (b) Voting contrary to the party whip without prior permission (c) Abstaining from voting contrary to the party whip without prior permission (d) Voluntarily giving up membership of the political party
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is the third option — abstaining from voting against the whip is indeed a ground for disqualification, just like voting against it. Merely criticising party policy publicly, without giving up membership or defying a whip in the House, is not a ground under the Tenth Schedule, so the answer is criticising the party's policies in a public speech.
Source: M. Laxmikanth, Indian Polity

133. Consider the following statements about the Attorney General of India:
1. He must be qualified to be appointed a judge of the Supreme Court.
2. He has the right to vote in either House of Parliament.
3. He holds office during the pleasure of the President.
Which of the statements given above is/are correct?
(a) 1 and 2 only (b) 1 and 3 only (c) 2 and 3 only (d) 1, 2 and 3
Answer: (b)
Solution: Statement 2 is the trap — the Attorney General has the right to speak and take part in the proceedings of either House under Article 88 but has no right to vote. Statements 1 and 3 are correct under Article 76, so the answer is 1 and 3 only.

134. The enforcement of Fundamental Rights through the Supreme Court under Article 32 can be suspended during:
(a) a National Emergency proclaimed on any of its grounds (b) Financial Emergency only (c) President's Rule in a state (d) no circumstances whatsoever
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is the last option — Article 359 expressly empowers the President to suspend the enforcement of Fundamental Rights (except Articles 20 and 21 after the 44th Amendment) during a National Emergency. This suspension applies to an emergency proclaimed on any ground, including armed rebellion, so the answer is a National Emergency proclaimed on any of its grounds.
Source: M. Laxmikanth, Indian Polity

135. Which of the following schedules of the Constitution contains provisions regarding the administration and control of Scheduled Areas and Scheduled Tribes?
(a) Fourth Schedule (b) Sixth Schedule (c) Fifth Schedule (d) Seventh Schedule
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is the Sixth Schedule — it deals with autonomous district councils in the tribal areas of Assam, Meghalaya, Tripura and Mizoram, a related but distinct subject. The Fifth Schedule specifically covers the administration and control of Scheduled Areas and Scheduled Tribes in other states, so the answer is the Fifth Schedule.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Indian Constitution at Work

136. With reference to a joint sitting of the two Houses of Parliament under Article 108, consider the following statements:
1. It can be summoned for a Money Bill.
2. It is presided over by the Speaker of the Lok Sabha.
3. The Constitution (Amendment) Bills are excluded from the provision of joint sitting.
Which of the statements given above is/are correct?
(a) 1 and 2 only (b) 2 and 3 only (c) 1 and 3 only (d) 1, 2 and 3
Answer: (b)
Solution: Statement 1 is the trap — a joint sitting cannot be summoned for a Money Bill because the Lok Sabha alone has the final say over it. Statements 2 and 3 are correct: the Speaker presides, and constitutional amendment bills under Article 368 are excluded from joint sitting, so the answer is 2 and 3 only.
Source: M. Laxmikanth, Indian Polity

137. The concept of 'Concurrent List' in the Indian Constitution was borrowed from the Constitution of:
(a) Canada (b) Australia (c) Ireland (d) United States of America
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is the first option — from Canada we borrowed the federation with a strong centre and residuary powers with the Union, not the Concurrent List. The Concurrent List, along with the joint sitting provision, was borrowed from Australia, so the answer is Australia.

138. Which of the following statements about the Comptroller and Auditor General of India is correct?
(a) He is appointed by the Prime Minister (b) He is eligible for reappointment to the same office after retirement (c) He holds office for a term of five years or up to the age of 60 years (d) He can be removed from office in the same manner as a judge of the Supreme Court
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is the third option — the CAG holds office for six years or up to the age of 65 years, whichever is earlier, not five years or 60 years. He is appointed by the President, is not eligible for further government office after ceasing to hold office, and is removed like a Supreme Court judge, so the answer is that he can be removed in the same manner as a judge of the Supreme Court.
Source: M. Laxmikanth, Indian Polity

139. Consider the following pairs regarding the amendment procedure:
1. Amendment by simple majority — creation of new states
2. Amendment by special majority — Fundamental Rights
3. Amendment by special majority plus ratification by half the states — election of the President
Which of the pairs given above is/are correctly matched?
(a) 1 and 2 only (b) 1 and 3 only (c) 1, 2 and 3 (d) 2 and 3 only
Answer: (c)
Solution: All three pairs are correctly matched. Creation of new states needs only a simple majority and is not deemed an amendment under Article 368; Fundamental Rights need a special majority; and the election of the President requires special majority plus ratification by at least half the state legislatures, so the answer is 1, 2 and 3.
Source: M. Laxmikanth, Indian Polity

140. The first constitutional amendment that curtailed the right to property as a Fundamental Right was the:
(a) Twenty-Fourth Amendment Act, 1971 (b) Fourth Amendment Act, 1955 (c) First Amendment Act, 1951 (d) Forty-Fourth Amendment Act, 1978
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is the last option — the 44th Amendment (1978) removed the right to property from the Fundamental Rights altogether and made it a legal right under Article 300A, but the process of curtailing it began much earlier. The First Amendment (1951) inserted Articles 31A and 31B and the Ninth Schedule to protect land reform laws from judicial review, so the answer is the First Amendment Act, 1951.
Source: M. Laxmikanth, Indian Polity

141. Which of the following is correct regarding the ordinance-making power of the President under Article 123?
(a) An ordinance can amend the Constitution (b) An ordinance must be laid before both Houses and ceases to operate six weeks after their reassembly unless approved earlier (c) An ordinance can be issued only when both Houses are in session (d) The maximum life of an ordinance is one year
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is the second option — the maximum life of an ordinance is six months plus six weeks, not one year. An ordinance cannot amend the Constitution, is issued when Parliament is not in session, and must be approved by both Houses within six weeks of reassembly, so the answer is that it ceases to operate six weeks after reassembly unless approved earlier.
Source: M. Laxmikanth, Indian Polity

143. The 'Instrument of Instructions' contained in the Government of India Act, 1935 was incorporated in the Indian Constitution as:
(a) Fundamental Rights (b) Directive Principles of State Policy (c) Fundamental Duties (d) Emergency Provisions
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is the second option — Fundamental Rights were inspired by the USA's Bill of Rights, not the 1935 Act. The Directive Principles of State Policy were described by the framers as resembling the Instrument of Instructions issued under the Government of India Act, 1935, so the answer is Directive Principles of State Policy.

144. The Speaker of the Lok Sabha can be removed from office by:
(a) a resolution of the Lok Sabha passed by a majority of all the then members, after a 14-day notice (b) an order of the President on the advice of the Prime Minister (c) a resolution of the Rajya Sabha passed by a two-thirds majority (d) a simple majority of members present and voting in the Lok Sabha, without any notice
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is the first option — removal of the Speaker is deliberately made harder than ordinary business, so a mere majority of members present and voting is not enough. Under Article 94, the Speaker is removed by a resolution passed by a majority of all the then members of the Lok Sabha (effective majority), and a 14-day prior notice is mandatory, so the answer is a resolution passed by a majority of all the then members after a 14-day notice.
Source: M. Laxmikanth, Indian Polity

145. Which Article of the Constitution empowers Parliament to make laws on subjects in the State List during a National Emergency?
(a) Article 250 (b) Article 249 (c) Article 248 (d) Article 252
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is the second option — Article 249 empowers Parliament to legislate on State List subjects when the Rajya Sabha passes a resolution in the national interest, not during an emergency. Article 250 specifically empowers Parliament to make laws on State List subjects while a Proclamation of Emergency is in operation, so the answer is Article 250.
Source: M. Laxmikanth, Indian Polity

146. Consider the following statements about the Zonal Councils:
1. They are constitutional bodies established under Article 263.
2. The Union Home Minister is the chairman of all the Zonal Councils.
3. There are five Zonal Councils in India.
Which of the statements given above is/are correct?
(a) 1, 2 and 3 (b) 1 and 2 only (c) 1 and 3 only (d) 2 and 3 only

147. The power of judicial review in India is:
(a) Explicitly confined to Article 13 only (b) Not available against constitutional amendments (c) Derived from Articles 13, 32, 226, 136 and other provisions (d) Exercised only by the Supreme Court
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is the second option — after Kesavananda Bharati, constitutional amendments can be struck down if they damage the basic structure, so judicial review does extend to amendments. Judicial review flows from several provisions including Articles 13, 32, 136, 226 and 227, and is exercised by both the Supreme Court and the High Courts, so the answer is that it is derived from Articles 13, 32, 226, 136 and other provisions.
Source: M. Laxmikanth, Indian Polity

148. With reference to the 101st Constitutional Amendment Act, 2016, consider the following statements:
1. It introduced the Goods and Services Tax in India.
2. It inserted Article 246A giving both Parliament and state legislatures power to make laws on GST.
3. It abolished the GST Compensation Cess from the very date of enactment.
Which of the statements given above is/are correct?
(a) 1 and 3 only (b) 1, 2 and 3 (c) 2 and 3 only (d) 1 and 2 only
Answer: (d)
Solution: Statement 3 is the trap — the GST Compensation to States Act, 2017 provided for a compensation cess for five years (later extended), so it was not abolished at enactment. 149. Which of the following correctly describes the 'pith and substance' doctrine?
(a) A law is invalid if it incidentally encroaches upon a subject in another list (b) Repugnancy between Union and State laws is always resolved in favour of the State law (c) The true nature and character of legislation determines its validity, and incidental encroachment on another list does not invalidate it (d) A law is valid only if it strictly avoids touching any subject in another list
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is the first option — it states the exact opposite of the doctrine; incidental encroachment does not invalidate a law if in pith and substance it falls within the legislature's competence. The doctrine examines the true nature and object of the legislation, so the answer is that the true nature and character of legislation determines its validity.
Source: M. Laxmikanth, Indian Polity

150. Consider the following statements about the 106th Constitutional Amendment Act, 2023 (Nari Shakti Vandan Adhiniyam):
1. It reserves one-third of seats for women in the Lok Sabha and state legislative assemblies.
2. The reservation comes into effect immediately upon enactment.
3. The reservation will operate for 15 years from its commencement.
Which of the statements given above is/are correct?
(a) 1 and 3 only (b) 1 and 2 only (c) 2 and 3 only (d) 1, 2 and 3
Answer: (a)
Solution: Statement 2 is the trap — the reservation takes effect only after the delimitation exercise conducted following the first census after the Act's commencement, not immediately. Statements 1 and 3 are correct: one-third reservation in the Lok Sabha and state assemblies, operative for 15 years, so the answer is 1 and 3 only.
Source: The Constitution (One Hundred and Sixth Amendment) Act, 2023
