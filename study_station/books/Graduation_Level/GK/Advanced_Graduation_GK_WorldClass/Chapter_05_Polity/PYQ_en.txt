# 📊 PYQ Analysis — Indian Polity (Advanced Level)

## 1. How Exams Test This Chapter

Indian Polity is one of the most dependable scoring areas across UPSC (Prelims), SSC CGL/CHSL, Banking (IBPS/SBI), Railway (NTPC/Group D) and State PCS prelims. The questions are almost never about obscure facts — they test whether you have read the **same core topics precisely**. Aspirants lose marks not because they don't know Polity, but because they know it *approximately*.

**Sub-topics that recur most often (in words, not numbers):**

- **Preamble, Citizenship and Fundamental Rights** — the single most fertile ground. Examiners love Article 14 vs 19 confusion, "reasonable restrictions", and which rights are available to foreigners.
- **Constitutional Amendments & Schedules** — especially the 42nd, 44th, 52nd, 73rd, 74th, 86th and 101st Amendments, and what each Schedule contains.
- **President, PM, Parliament** — ordinance power (Art 123), money bill (Art 110), joint sitting (Art 108), impeachment procedure.
- **Judiciary** — writs (Art 32 vs 226), jurisdiction of Supreme Court vs High Court, appointment of judges.
- **Federal features** — Seventh Schedule lists, Article 356, Finance Commission vs GST Council.
- **Local bodies & special officers** — Panchayati Raj structure, CAG, Election Commission, UPSC (the body), Attorney General.
- **Borrowed features of the Constitution** — a favourite of SSC and Railway.

**Exam-wise flavour:**

- **UPSC / State PCS:** statement-based questions ("Which of the statements given above is/are correct?"), conceptual depth, comparison between offices.
- **SSC / Railway:** direct one-liners — article numbers, amendment years, "who appoints whom".
- **Banking:** fewer Polity questions, but when they come, they are about constitutional bodies, recent amendments and basic structure.

## 2. The Examiner's Traps (जाल) — Learn to Spot Them

| Trap Type | How It Looks | Example Pattern |
|---|---|---|
| **Almost Correct** | A statement that is 90% right but one word is wrong | "The President can promulgate ordinances when *either House* is not in session" — correct; trap version says "only when both Houses are not in session" |
| **Chronological Confusion** | Events/amendments in wrong order | 42nd (1976) vs 44th (1978) Amendment swapped; Fundamental Duties "originally" in the Constitution |
| **Similar Names** | Offices/bodies that sound alike | Finance Commission (Art 280) vs GST Council (Art 279A); Art 32 vs Art 226 |
| **Negative Phrasing** | "Which is NOT…", "Which is NOT a feature of…" | "Which of the following is NOT a Fundamental Right?" — one option is a legal right dressed as an FR |
| **Borrowed-Feature Mix-up** | Wrong country attributed | "Directive Principles borrowed from UK" (actually Ireland) |
| **Number Swap** | Article/Schedule numbers interchanged | Art 368 (amendment) vs Art 370; Ninth vs Tenth Schedule |

---

## 3. The 15/45-Second Rule

- **15 seconds:** Direct factual recall — article number, amendment year, "who appoints the CAG". If you know it, mark it; if not, skip immediately. Never burn time here.
- **45 seconds:** Statement-based or multi-statement questions. Budget one pass of reading + elimination. If two statements remain doubtful after 45 seconds, mark your best elimination and move on — do not re-read.

## 4. Top 10 High-Yield PYQ-Style Questions (with Trap Analysis)

**Q1.** Which Article of the Indian Constitution empowers the President to promulgate ordinances?
(a) Article 111 (b) Article 123 (c) Article 143 (d) Article 356
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** Article 123 gives the President ordinance-making power when Parliament is not in session. Article 111 is assent to bills — the classic "similar number" trap. Article 143 is advisory jurisdiction; 356 is President's Rule.
**Trap type:** Number Swap | **Rule:** 15 seconds
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q2.** The concept of Directive Principles of State Policy was borrowed from the Constitution of:
(a) USA (b) UK (c) Ireland (d) Canada
**Answer: (c)**
**Solution:** DPSPs came from the Irish Constitution (which itself borrowed from Spain). UK gave us parliamentary government and rule of law — the most common wrong pick.
**Trap type:** Borrowed-Feature Mix-up | **Rule:** 15 seconds
**Source:** NCERT Class 11 (Indian Constitution at Work)

**Q3.** Which of the following is NOT a Fundamental Right under the Indian Constitution?
(a) Right to Equality (b) Right to Constitutional Remedies
(c) Right to Property (d) Right against Exploitation
**Answer: (c)**
**Solution:** The Right to Property was removed from Fundamental Rights by the 44th Amendment (1978) and made a legal right under Article 300A. The negative phrasing makes candidates rush past "NOT".
**Trap type:** Negative Phrasing + Chronological Confusion | **Rule:** 45 seconds
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q4.** The 73rd Constitutional Amendment Act is related to:
(a) Municipalities (b) Panchayati Raj Institutions
(c) Anti-defection law (d) Goods and Services Tax
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** 73rd Amendment (1992) → Panchayats; 74th → Municipalities. The 73/74 pair is the most exploited "similar names" trap in SSC and Railway exams. 52nd is anti-defection; 101st is GST.
**Trap type:** Similar Names | **Rule:** 15 seconds
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q5.** Consider the following statements:
1. Fundamental Duties were originally part of the Constitution as adopted in 1949.
2. They were added by the 42nd Amendment Act, 1976, on the recommendation of the Swaran Singh Committee.
Which of the above is/are correct?
(a) 1 only (b) 2 only (c) Both 1 and 2 (d) Neither 1 nor 2
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** Statement 1 is the trap — Fundamental Duties (Article 51A, Part IVA) were NOT in the original Constitution; they were inserted in 1976. Statement 2 is fully correct.
**Trap type:** Almost Correct + Chronological Confusion | **Rule:** 45 seconds — read each statement separately, never judge them together.
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q6.** A Money Bill can be introduced only in:
(a) Rajya Sabha (b) Lok Sabha
(c) Either House of Parliament (d) A joint sitting of both Houses
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** Under Article 110, a Money Bill can originate only in the Lok Sabha, and only on the President's recommendation. "Either House" is the tempting wrong option because ordinary bills can start anywhere.
**Trap type:** Almost Correct | **Rule:** 15 seconds
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q7.** Which writ is issued by a court to a public authority asking it to perform a duty it has failed to perform?
(a) Habeas Corpus (b) Mandamus (c) Prohibition (d) Certiorari
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** Mandamus = "we command" — directs a public official/body to do its legal duty. Habeas Corpus (produce the body) is the most commonly confused option. Prohibition and Certiorari apply to lower courts/tribunals.
**Trap type:** Similar Names | **Rule:** 15 seconds
**Source:** NCERT Class 11 (Indian Constitution at Work)

**Q8.** The Tenth Schedule of the Indian Constitution deals with:
(a) Languages (b) Anti-defection provisions
(c) Panchayats (d) Allocation of seats in Rajya Sabha
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** Tenth Schedule = anti-defection (added by the 52nd Amendment, 1985). Languages are the Eighth Schedule — the classic number swap. Panchayats = Eleventh Schedule; Rajya Sabha seats = Fourth Schedule.
**Trap type:** Number Swap | **Rule:** 15 seconds
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q9.** Who among the following is appointed by the President of India?
1. Comptroller and Auditor General
2. Attorney General for India
3. Chief Election Commissioner
Select the correct answer:
(a) 1 and 2 only (b) 2 and 3 only (c) 1 and 3 only (d) 1, 2 and 3
**Answer: (d)**
**Solution:** All three are appointed by the President — CAG (Art 148), Attorney General (Art 76), CEC (Art 324). The trap is assuming one of them is appointed by Parliament or the PM.
**Trap type:** Almost Correct (partial-knowledge trap) | **Rule:** 45 seconds
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q10.** Which of the following rights is available ONLY to citizens of India and not to foreigners?
(a) Article 21 — Protection of life and personal liberty
(b) Article 19 — Freedom of speech and expression
(c) Article 14 — Equality before law
(d) Article 25 — Freedom of religion
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** Article 19 freedoms are reserved for citizens only. Articles 14, 21 and 25 are available to all persons, including foreigners. This citizen-vs-person distinction is a perennial UPSC/PCS favourite.
**Trap type:** Almost Correct | **Rule:** 45 seconds
**Source:** PYQ-style

## 5. Quick Revision Takeaways

1. **Article numbers are non-negotiable** for SSC/Railway: 32, 110, 123, 148, 280, 324, 356, 368 — know them cold.
2. **Amendment-year pairs** (42nd/44th, 73rd/74th, 52nd/86th/101st) are the highest-frequency trap zone.
3. In **statement questions**, treat each statement as an independent true/false question before combining.
4. For **"NOT" questions**, underline the negative word mentally before reading options — most errors here are reading errors, not knowledge errors.
5. Apply **15/45 seconds** ruthlessly: Polity questions are either instant recall or structured elimination — there is no third category.
