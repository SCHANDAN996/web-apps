101. Which Article of the Indian Constitution deals with the impeachment of the President?
(a) Article 61 (b) Article 56 (c) Article 72 (d) Article 74
Answer: (a)
Solution: Article 56 covers the President's term of office, which tempts many candidates, but impeachment procedure is laid down in Article 61. Article 72 is about pardoning power and Article 74 about the Council of Ministers, so the answer is Article 61.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Political Science (Indian Constitution at Work)

102. The idea of a Concurrent List in the Indian Constitution was borrowed from which country?
(a) Australia (b) Ireland (c) Canada (d) United States
Answer: (a)
Solution: Canada is a common trap because federalism with a strong Centre came from Canada, but the Concurrent List was borrowed from Australia. Ireland gave Directive Principles and the US gave fundamental rights, so the answer is Australia.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Political Science (Indian Constitution at Work)

103. Who presides over a joint sitting of the two Houses of Parliament summoned under Article 108?
(a) The President (b) The Speaker of the Lok Sabha (c) The Vice-President (d) The Chief Justice of India
Answer: (b)
Solution: The Vice-President is tempting because he chairs the Rajya Sabha, but a joint sitting is presided over by the Speaker of the Lok Sabha, or in his absence the Deputy Speaker, so the answer is the Speaker of the Lok Sabha.
Source: M. Laxmikanth, Indian Polity

104. The 42nd Constitutional Amendment Act (1976) added which of the following words to the Preamble?
(a) Sovereign, Socialist, Republic (b) Socialist, Secular, Fraternity (c) Democratic, Secular, Integrity (d) Socialist, Secular, Integrity
Answer: (d)
Solution: "Fraternity" is the trap because it is in the Preamble, but it was there since 1950; the 42nd Amendment added Socialist, Secular and Integrity. Sovereign, Democratic and Republic were original words, so the answer is Socialist, Secular, Integrity.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Political Science (Indian Constitution at Work)

105. A Money Bill can be introduced only in:
(a) The Rajya Sabha (b) The Lok Sabha (c) Either House of Parliament (d) A joint sitting of both Houses
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Either House" tempts because ordinary bills can start anywhere, but under Article 109 a Money Bill can be introduced only in the Lok Sabha and only on the President's recommendation, so the answer is the Lok Sabha.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Political Science (Indian Constitution at Work)

106. Which Schedule of the Constitution contains provisions for the administration of tribal areas in Assam, Meghalaya, Tripura and Mizoram?
(a) Fifth Schedule (b) Sixth Schedule (c) Seventh Schedule (d) Ninth Schedule
Answer: (b)
Solution: The Fifth Schedule is the trap because it also deals with scheduled areas and tribes, but it applies to other states; the Sixth Schedule covers autonomous tribal councils in Assam, Meghalaya, Tripura and Mizoram, so the answer is the Sixth Schedule.
Source: M. Laxmikanth, Indian Polity

107. The Fundamental Duties were incorporated into the Constitution on the recommendation of which committee?
(a) Swaran Singh Committee (b) Sarkaria Commission (c) Punchhi Commission (d) Shah Commission
Answer: (a)
Solution: Sarkaria and Punchhi dealt with Centre-State relations and are frequent traps, but Fundamental Duties (Article 51A) were added by the 42nd Amendment, 1976, on the Swaran Singh Committee's recommendation, so the answer is the Swaran Singh Committee.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Political Science (Indian Constitution at Work)

108. Under Article 368, a Constitutional Amendment Bill requires:
(a) Simple majority in both Houses (b) Approval of the President only (c) Special majority plus ratification by all states (d) Special majority in both Houses
Answer: (d)
Solution: Ratification by states is tempting but applies only to certain provisions (like election of the President, distribution of powers), and even then by half the states, not all; the general rule is a special majority in each House, so the answer is special majority in both Houses.
Source: M. Laxmikanth, Indian Polity

109. The concept of "Procedure established by law" in Article 21 was borrowed from the Constitution of:
(a) Japan (b) United States (c) United Kingdom (d) Germany
Answer: (a)
Solution: The United States is the classic trap because it gave "due process of law," but "procedure established by law" in Article 21 was borrowed from Japan, so the answer is Japan.

110. Who among the following is the ex-officio Chairman of the Rajya Sabha?
(a) The Vice-President (b) The President (c) The Prime Minister (d) The Speaker of the Lok Sabha
Answer: (a)
Solution: The Speaker is a trap for those who confuse the two Houses, but under Article 64 the Vice-President is the ex-officio Chairman of the Rajya Sabha, so the answer is the Vice-President.

111. The minimum age to become a member of the Rajya Sabha is:
(a) 21 years (b) 25 years (c) 35 years (d) 30 years
Answer: (d)
Solution: 25 years is the tempting wrong option because it is the minimum age for the Lok Sabha; for the Rajya Sabha Article 84 prescribes 30 years, while 35 is for President and Governor, so the answer is 30 years.
Source: M. Laxmikanth, Indian Polity

112. Which Article empowers the Supreme Court to issue writs for the enforcement of Fundamental Rights?
(a) Article 13 (b) Article 226 (c) Article 32 (d) Article 143
Answer: (c)
Solution: Article 226 is the trap because High Courts issue writs under it, but the question asks about the Supreme Court, which acts under Article 32 — called by Ambedkar the "heart and soul" of the Constitution — so the answer is Article 32.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Political Science (Indian Constitution at Work)

113. The 73rd Constitutional Amendment Act, 1992 deals with:
(a) Municipalities (b) Scheduled Tribes (c) Cooperative Societies (d) Panchayati Raj Institutions
Answer: (d)
Solution: Municipalities is the trap because it came in the same year, but through the 74th Amendment; the 73rd Amendment gave constitutional status to Panchayati Raj Institutions and added the Eleventh Schedule, so the answer is Panchayati Raj Institutions.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Political Science (Indian Constitution at Work)

114. A National Emergency under Article 352 was proclaimed for the first time in:
(a) 1975 (b) 1965 (c) 1971 (d) 1962
Answer: (d)
Solution: 1975 is tempting because it is the most discussed emergency, but the first proclamation came in October 1962 during the India-China war on grounds of external aggression, so the answer is 1962.
Source: M. Laxmikanth, Indian Polity

115. The Finance Commission is constituted by the President under which Article?
(a) Article 263 (b) Article 300 (c) Article 280 (d) Article 312
Answer: (c)
Solution: Article 263 is the trap because it provides for the Inter-State Council, but the Finance Commission, constituted every five years to recommend distribution of tax revenues, comes under Article 280, so the answer is Article 280.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Political Science (Indian Constitution at Work)

116. Which of the following is NOT a Fundamental Right under the Indian Constitution?
(a) Right to Property (b) Right to Freedom of Religion (c) Right to Equality (d) Right against Exploitation
Answer: (a)
Solution: Right to Property is the correct pick because the 44th Amendment, 1978 removed it from Part III and made it a legal right under Article 300A; the other three remain Fundamental Rights, so the answer is Right to Property.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Political Science (Indian Constitution at Work)

117. The President can nominate how many members to the Rajya Sabha?
(a) 2 (b) 12 (c) 10 (d) 14
Answer: (b)
Solution: 2 is the trap because that was the number of Anglo-Indian members nominated to the Lok Sabha (abolished by the 104th Amendment); under Article 80, twelve members from literature, science, art and social service are nominated to the Rajya Sabha, so the answer is 12.
Source: M. Laxmikanth, Indian Polity

118. The doctrine of "Basic Structure" of the Constitution was propounded by the Supreme Court in:
(a) Golaknath v. State of Punjab (b) Kesavananda Bharati v. State of Kerala (c) Minerva Mills v. Union of India (d) S.R. Bommai v. Union of India
Answer: (b)
Solution: Golaknath is the trap because it dealt with amendability of Fundamental Rights, but the Basic Structure doctrine was laid down in the Kesavananda Bharati case (1973); Minerva Mills only reaffirmed it, so the answer is Kesavananda Bharati v. State of Kerala.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Political Science (Indian Constitution at Work)

119. Who decides whether a bill is a Money Bill or not?
(a) The President (b) The Speaker of the Lok Sabha (c) The Chairman of the Rajya Sabha (d) The Finance Minister
Answer: (b)
Solution: The President is tempting because his recommendation is needed to introduce a Money Bill, but under Article 110 the Speaker's decision on whether a bill is a Money Bill is final, so the answer is the Speaker of the Lok Sabha.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Political Science (Indian Constitution at Work)

120. The Tenth Schedule of the Constitution, dealing with anti-defection, was added by which amendment?
(a) 42nd Amendment (b) 44th Amendment (c) 52nd Amendment (d) 61st Amendment
Answer: (c)
Solution: The 42nd Amendment is the trap because it was the largest amendment, but the Tenth Schedule on disqualification for defection was inserted by the 52nd Amendment, 1985; the 61st lowered the voting age to 18, so the answer is the 52nd Amendment.
Source: M. Laxmikanth, Indian Polity

121. Consider the following statements:
1. The 86th Amendment Act, 2002 made elementary education a Fundamental Right.
2. It inserted Article 21A into the Constitution.
3. It changed the subject of Article 45 to early childhood care and education.
Which of the statements given above are correct?
(a) 1 and 2 only (b) 2 and 3 only (c) 1 and 3 only (d) 1, 2 and 3
Answer: (d)
Solution: Many candidates doubt statement 3, but the 86th Amendment did all three: it inserted Article 21A (free and compulsory education for ages 6–14), modified Article 45 to cover early childhood care up to age 6, and added a Fundamental Duty under Article 51A(k), so the answer is 1, 2 and 3.
Source: M. Laxmikanth, Indian Polity

122. Arrange the following in chronological order of their establishment:
1. National Human Rights Commission
2. Central Vigilance Commission (statutory status)
3. National Commission for Women
4. National Commission for Minorities
(a) 1, 2, 3, 4 (b) 3, 1, 4, 2 (c) 3, 4, 1, 2 (d) 4, 3, 1, 2
Answer: (c)
Solution: The chronological-confusion trap here is placing the NHRC first; the correct order is National Commission for Women (1992, January), National Commission for Minorities (1992, May), NHRC (1993), and CVC given statutory status (2003), so the answer is 3, 4, 1, 2.
Source: M. Laxmikanth, Indian Polity

123. Consider the following statements about the Attorney General of India:
1. He is appointed by the President under Article 76.
2. He has the right to vote in Parliament.
3. He must be qualified to be appointed a judge of the Supreme Court.
Which of the statements given above is/are correct?
(a) 1 only (b) 2 and 3 only (c) 1 and 3 only (d) 1, 2 and 3
Answer: (c)
Solution: Statement 2 is the almost-correct trap: the Attorney General has the right to speak and take part in proceedings of either House but has NO right to vote; statements 1 and 3 are correct, so the answer is 1 and 3 only.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Political Science (Indian Constitution at Work)

124. Which of the following pairs is correctly matched?
(a) Article 352 — President's Rule (b) Article 356 — National Emergency (c) Article 360 — Financial Emergency (d) Article 365 — Financial Emergency
Answer: (c)
Solution: The similar-numbers trap swaps the emergencies: Article 352 is National Emergency, Article 356 is President's Rule, and Article 365 covers failure to comply with Union directions; Financial Emergency under Article 360 has never been proclaimed, so the answer is Article 360 — Financial Emergency.
Source: M. Laxmikanth, Indian Polity

125. Consider the following statements:
1. The 101st Constitutional Amendment Act introduced the Goods and Services Tax.
2. It inserted Article 246A giving both Parliament and state legislatures power to make laws on GST.
3. It abolished the GST Council after one year of its creation.
Which of the statements given above is/are correct?
(a) 1 only (b) 1 and 2 only (c) 2 and 3 only (d) 1, 2 and 3
Answer: (b)
Solution: Statement 3 is the almost-correct trap: the 101st Amendment (2016) itself created the GST Council under Article 279A as a permanent constitutional body, so it was never abolished; statements 1 and 2 are correct, so the answer is 1 and 2 only.
Source: M. Laxmikanth, Indian Polity
