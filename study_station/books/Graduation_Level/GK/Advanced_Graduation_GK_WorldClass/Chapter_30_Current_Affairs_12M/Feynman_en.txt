# Feynman Technique — Current Affairs (October 2025 – October 2026)

## Explain it to a 12-year-old

Current affairs is the "news diary" of the country. Instead of reading every news story, we keep a diary with nine pages — awards, appointments, sports, defence, space, economy, environment, schemes and the world. Every time something important happens, we write one line on the right page: **what happened, when, where, and one number or "first".**

For example, on the Sports page we write: *"Asian Games 2026, Aichi-Nagoya (Japan): India 4th, 85 medals, 21 gold."* On the Environment page: *"October 2026: Ramsar sites reach 103 — Sukhna Lake and Sauj Jheel added."* One line, four hooks. That is all an exam asks.

## Why it works

- The brain remembers **stories with a place and a number** much better than long paragraphs.
- Grouping by theme means one question ("Who is the new Army chief?") reminds you of its neighbours (Navy and Air Force chiefs).
- Writing the month next to each fact protects you from the "as of when" trap.

## Blurting Sheet

Close the book and write, from memory, one line for each of the nine themes. Then open the Key Facts table and tick what you got right. Anything missing goes on a sticky note for tomorrow.
