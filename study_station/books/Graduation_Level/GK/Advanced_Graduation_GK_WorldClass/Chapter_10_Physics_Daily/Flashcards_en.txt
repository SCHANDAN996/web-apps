Card 1
Front: Why does a wet finger make a "singing" sound on a glass rim?
Back: The wet finger sticks and slips on the rim (stick-slip friction), making the glass vibrate at its natural frequency, which we hear as a musical note.

Card 2
Front: Why can a hot drink crack a glass tumbler?
Back: The inside of the glass heats and expands faster than the outside (thermal shock), and the uneven expansion creates stress that cracks the glass.

Card 3
Front: Why does a red-hot iron look "red" in the dark?
Back: At high temperature, the iron emits visible light of its own; the colour of this emitted light depends on the temperature of the iron.

Card 4
Front: Why does a wet road look shiny from a distance?
Back: Water on the road forms a smooth surface that reflects light like a mirror, so the road appears bright or shiny.

Card 5
Front: Why does a red rose look red in white light?
Back: The petals absorb most colours of white light and reflect mainly red light, so the rose appears red.

Card 6
Front: Why does a wet shirt look darker than a dry shirt?
Back: Water fills the gaps between fibres, so less light is reflected back to our eyes and the shirt appears darker.

Card 7
Front: Why does a black shirt look black?
Back: The shirt absorbs almost all colours of light and reflects very little, so it appears black.

Card 8
Front: Why does a white shirt look white?
Back: It reflects all colours of light almost equally, so the combined reflected light appears white.

Card 9
Front: Why does a yellow shirt look yellow in white light?
Back: It reflects mainly yellow light and absorbs the other colours, so it appears yellow.

Card 10
Front: What colour does a red object appear in green light?
Back: It appears nearly black, because the red object absorbs green light and there is no red light to reflect.

Card 11
Front: Why does a glass of water sing at a lower pitch when more water is added?
Back: More water makes the vibrating system heavier, so its natural frequency decreases and the pitch becomes lower.

Card 12
Front: Why do we see colours at all?
Back: Objects reflect some colours of light and absorb others; the reflected colours entering our eyes decide the colour we see.
