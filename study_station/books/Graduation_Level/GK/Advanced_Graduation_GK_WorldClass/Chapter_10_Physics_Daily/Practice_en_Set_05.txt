101. A person standing in a moving bus falls forward when the bus suddenly stops. This is due to:
(a) Inertia of rest (b) Inertia of direction (c) Inertia of motion (d) Gravitational pull
Answer: (c)
Solution: The tempting choice is inertia of rest, but the body was already moving with the bus, so the upper body continues moving forward when the lower body stops with the bus. This is inertia of motion, so the answer is inertia of motion.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Science

102. Which colour of light is scattered the most by the atmosphere?
(a) Red (b) Yellow (c) Green (d) Violet
Answer: (d)
Solution: Many students pick red because the sky is blue, but scattering is inversely proportional to the fourth power of wavelength, so shorter wavelengths scatter most. Violet scatters even more than blue; the sky looks blue because our eyes are more sensitive to blue and sunlight contains less violet. So the answer is violet.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science

103. The sky appears blue because of:
(a) Reflection of sunlight (b) Refraction of sunlight (c) Dispersion of sunlight (d) Scattering of sunlight
Answer: (d)
Solution: Dispersion is tempting because it also involves colours, but dispersion splits white light through a prism, while the blue sky results from air molecules scattering shorter wavelengths. So the answer is scattering of sunlight.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science

104. An oil film on water shows colours due to:
(a) Diffraction (b) Interference (c) Polarisation (d) Scattering
Answer: (b)
Solution: Scattering is tempting since colours are involved, but thin-film colours arise when light reflected from the top and bottom surfaces of the film superpose, producing constructive and destructive interference. So the answer is interference.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Physics

105. A convex lens of focal length 20 cm forms a real, inverted, same-size image when the object is placed at:
(a) 40 cm (b) 20 cm (c) 10 cm (d) Infinity
Answer: (a)
Solution: Placing the object at the focus (20 cm) is tempting, but that gives an image at infinity. A same-size real image forms only when the object is at 2F, i.e., 40 cm. So the answer is 40 cm.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science

106. The power of a lens of focal length 50 cm is:
(a) +2 D (b) +0.5 D (c) +5 D (d) +50 D
Answer: (a)
Solution: Using f = 50 cm directly in P = 1/f is the trap; the focal length must be in metres. P = 1/0.5 m = +2 D. So the answer is +2 D.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science

107. Which mirror is used as a rear-view mirror in vehicles?
(a) Plane mirror (b) Concave mirror (c) Cylindrical mirror (d) Convex mirror
Answer: (d)
Solution: Concave mirror is tempting because it magnifies, but it gives an inverted real image for distant objects and has a narrow field of view. A convex mirror always gives an erect, diminished image with a wide field of view. So the answer is convex mirror.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science

108. The SI unit of electric current is:
(a) Volt (b) Ampere (c) Ohm (d) Coulomb
Answer: (b)
Solution: Coulomb is tempting since current involves charge, but coulomb is the unit of charge, not current. Current is charge per unit time and its SI unit is the ampere. So the answer is ampere.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science

109. The commercial unit of electrical energy is:
(a) Joule (b) Kilowatt-hour (c) Kilowatt (d) Watt
Answer: (b)
Solution: Kilowatt is tempting because electricity bills mention "units", but kilowatt is a unit of power, not energy. One commercial unit equals one kilowatt-hour = 3.6 × 10⁶ J. So the answer is kilowatt-hour.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science

110. A fuse wire is made of a material having:
(a) High melting point and high resistance (b) Low melting point and high resistance (c) Low melting point and low resistance (d) High melting point and low resistance
Answer: (b)
Solution: High melting point is tempting because the wire carries current, but a fuse must melt quickly when current exceeds the safe limit, so it needs a low melting point with relatively high resistance to heat up fast. So the answer is low melting point and high resistance.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science

111. The phenomenon responsible for the twinkling of stars is:
(a) Total internal reflection (b) Scattering (c) Dispersion (d) Atmospheric refraction
Answer: (d)
Solution: Scattering is tempting since it explains the sky's colour, but twinkling occurs because starlight passes through layers of air of varying density, causing continuous refraction and apparent shifting of the star's position. So the answer is atmospheric refraction.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science

112. Planets do not twinkle because:
(a) They are very far away (b) They reflect sunlight (c) They act as extended sources of light (d) They have no atmosphere
Answer: (c)
Solution: Reflecting sunlight is tempting since planets do shine by reflected light, but that is not why they don't twinkle. Being close, planets appear as extended sources, and the fluctuations from different points average out. So the answer is they act as extended sources of light.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science

113. The device used to detect and measure small electric currents in a circuit is:
(a) Galvanometer (b) Voltmeter (c) Ammeter (d) Barometer
Answer: (a)
Solution: Ammeter is tempting because it measures current, but an ammeter measures larger currents in a circuit; a galvanometer detects and measures small currents and is the basis of both ammeter and voltmeter. So the answer is galvanometer.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Physics

114. The magnetic field inside a long straight solenoid carrying current is:
(a) Zero (b) Maximum at the ends (c) Uniform and parallel to the axis (d) Inversely proportional to length
Answer: (c)
Solution: Maximum at the ends is tempting because field lines crowd there outside, but inside a long solenoid the field is strong, uniform and directed along the axis. So the answer is uniform and parallel to the axis.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science

115. Fleming's left-hand rule is used to find the direction of:
(a) Force on a current-carrying conductor (b) Magnetic field (c) Induced current (d) Electric field
Answer: (a)
Solution: Induced current is tempting, but that is given by Fleming's right-hand rule — a classic examiner's trap pairing the two rules. The left-hand rule (motor rule) gives the force on a current-carrying conductor in a magnetic field. So the answer is force on a current-carrying conductor.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science

116. The frequency of AC supply in India is:
(a) 220 Hz (b) 60 Hz (c) 100 Hz (d) 50 Hz
Answer: (d)
Solution: 220 Hz is tempting because 220 V is the supply voltage, but voltage and frequency are different quantities. Indian domestic AC supply is 220 V at 50 Hz. So the answer is 50 Hz.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science

117. Which of the following is used in the treatment of cancer?
(a) Carbon-14 (b) Cobalt-60 (c) Uranium-235 (d) Iodine-131 for imaging bones
Answer: (b)
Solution: Carbon-14 is tempting as a famous isotope, but it is used for radiocarbon dating, not treatment. Gamma rays from cobalt-60 are used in radiotherapy to destroy cancer cells. So the answer is cobalt-60.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Physics

118. The working of a hydraulic brake is based on:
(a) Bernoulli's principle (b) Pascal's law (c) Archimedes' principle (d) Stoke's law
Answer: (b)
Solution: Archimedes' principle is tempting since fluids are involved, but it deals with buoyancy. Hydraulic brakes transmit pressure equally in all directions through a confined fluid, which is Pascal's law. So the answer is Pascal's law.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Physics

119. A ship made of iron floats on water because:
(a) Iron is lighter than water (b) Its weight equals its volume (c) Water exerts no force on it (d) Its average density is less than water
Answer: (d)
Solution: "Iron is lighter than water" is the tempting wrong statement — iron is denser than water. The hollow shape encloses air, making the ship's average density less than water, so buoyancy supports it. So the answer is its average density is less than water.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Science

120. The loudness of sound depends on:
(a) Frequency (b) Wavelength (c) Amplitude (d) Velocity
Answer: (c)
Solution: Frequency is tempting, but frequency determines pitch, not loudness. Loudness depends on the amplitude of vibration — greater amplitude means louder sound. So the answer is amplitude.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Science

121. Consider the following statements about total internal reflection:
1. Light must travel from denser to rarer medium.
2. The angle of incidence must be greater than the critical angle.
3. It is the principle behind the brilliance of a diamond.
Which of the above are correct?
(a) 1, 2 and 3 (b) 2 and 3 only (c) 1 and 3 only (d) 1 and 2 only
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is to think statement 3 is unrelated, but a diamond's small critical angle (about 24°) causes repeated total internal reflection, giving its sparkle. All three statements are correct, so the answer is 1, 2 and 3.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Physics

122. An electric bulb is rated 100 W, 220 V. Its resistance while glowing is:
(a) 100 Ω (b) 484 Ω (c) 220 Ω (d) 48400 Ω
Answer: (b)
Solution: Dividing 220 by 100 is the tempting shortcut, but R = V²/P = 220 × 220 / 100 = 484 Ω. So the answer is 484 Ω.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science

123. A ray of light passes from air into glass. Which of the following remains unchanged?
(a) Speed (b) Wavelength (c) Direction of propagation (d) Frequency
Answer: (d)
Solution: Wavelength is tempting because it is a wave property, but both speed and wavelength decrease in glass; even the direction changes unless incidence is normal. Only frequency, fixed by the source, remains unchanged. So the answer is frequency.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Physics

124. Match the instruments with what they measure:
1. Barometer — P. Humidity
2. Hygrometer — Q. Atmospheric pressure
3. Ammeter — R. Electric current
(a) 1-Q, 2-R, 3-P (b) 1-P, 2-Q, 3-R (c) 1-Q, 2-P, 3-R (d) 1-R, 2-P, 3-Q
Answer: (c)
Solution: The similar-sounding names barometer and hygrometer are the examiner's trap — barometer measures atmospheric pressure and hygrometer measures humidity, while an ammeter measures current. So the answer is 1-Q, 2-P, 3-R.
Source: PYQ-style

125. A concave mirror of focal length 15 cm has an object placed 10 cm from it. The image formed is:
(a) Real, inverted and magnified (b) Virtual, erect and magnified (c) Real, inverted and diminished (d) Virtual, erect and diminished
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Real and magnified" is tempting because concave mirrors magnify, but that happens only when the object is beyond the focus. Here the object (10 cm) lies between the pole and the focus (15 cm), so the image is virtual, erect and magnified — the shaving-mirror case. So the answer is virtual, erect and magnified.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science
