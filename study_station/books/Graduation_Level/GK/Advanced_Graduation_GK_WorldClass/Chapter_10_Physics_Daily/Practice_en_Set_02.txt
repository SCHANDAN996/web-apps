26. A person standing in a moving bus falls forward when the bus suddenly stops. This is due to:
(a) Inertia of rest (b) Inertia of motion (c) Inertia of direction (d) Gravitational pull
Answer: (b)
Solution: The tempting wrong choice is inertia of rest, which applies when a body at rest resists motion, e.g., a passenger jerking backward when a bus starts. Here the passenger's upper body was moving with the bus and continues moving when the bus stops, which is inertia of motion, so the answer is inertia of motion.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Science (Force and Laws of Motion)

27. Which of the following statements about friction is correct?
(a) Friction always acts in the direction of motion (b) Friction is independent of the nature of surfaces in contact (c) Friction opposes relative motion between surfaces (d) Friction increases when surfaces are polished
Answer: (c)
Solution: The almost-correct trap is the first statement; friction never aids motion, it always opposes relative motion or the tendency of relative motion. Polishing reduces friction, and friction depends strongly on the nature of surfaces, so the answer is that friction opposes relative motion between surfaces.
Source: NCERT Class 8 Science (Friction)

28. A convex mirror is used as a rear-view mirror in vehicles because it:
(a) Forms a virtual, erect and diminished image with a wide field of view (b) Forms a real and magnified image (c) Forms a virtual and magnified image (d) Converges light to a single point
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is the magnified image option; a magnified image would mislead the driver about distance. A convex mirror always gives a virtual, erect, diminished image and covers a wide field of view, so the answer is virtual, erect and diminished image with a wide field of view.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science (Light – Reflection and Refraction)

29. The sky appears blue because of:
(a) Reflection of blue light from oceans (b) Refraction of sunlight in the atmosphere (c) Dispersion of sunlight by water droplets (d) Scattering of shorter wavelengths by air molecules
Answer: (d)
Solution: Dispersion by water droplets is the tempting trap, but that explains the rainbow, not the blue sky. Air molecules scatter shorter wavelengths (blue) much more than longer ones (Rayleigh scattering), so the answer is scattering of shorter wavelengths by air molecules.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science (Human Eye and the Colourful World)

30. An iron nail sinks in water but a large iron ship floats. This is best explained by:
(a) The ship is lighter than the nail (b) The nail has greater surface tension (c) Water exerts no buoyant force on the nail (d) The ship's average density is less than that of water
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is saying the ship is lighter; a ship is far heavier than a nail, so weight alone cannot explain floating. Because of its hollow shape, the ship's average density is less than water, so buoyancy balances its weight, and the answer is that the ship's average density is less than that of water.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Science (Gravitation)

31. Which colour of light deviates the most while passing through a glass prism?
(a) Red (b) Violet (c) Yellow (d) Green
Answer: (b)
Solution: The common trap is red, because red is scattered least in the sky and students confuse the two phenomena. In a prism, refractive index is highest for the shortest wavelength, so violet bends the most, and the answer is violet.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science (Human Eye and the Colourful World)

32. A fuse wire used in domestic circuits is made of a material with:
(a) High melting point and high resistance (b) Low melting point and high resistance (c) Low melting point and low resistance (d) High melting point and low resistance
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is high melting point, which would defeat the purpose since the wire must melt quickly on excess current. A fuse wire (tin-lead alloy) has a low melting point and relatively high resistance so it heats and melts first, so the answer is low melting point and high resistance.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science (Electricity)

33. The loudness of sound depends on:
(a) Amplitude of the wave (b) Frequency of the wave (c) Wavelength of the wave (d) Speed of the wave
Answer: (a)
Solution: Frequency is the tempting trap, but frequency determines pitch, not loudness. Loudness is related to the energy of vibration, which depends on amplitude, so the answer is amplitude of the wave.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Science (Sound)

34. When a ball is thrown vertically upward, at the highest point its:
(a) Velocity and acceleration are both zero (b) Velocity and acceleration are both maximum (c) Velocity is maximum and acceleration is zero (d) Velocity is zero but acceleration is g downward
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is claiming both velocity and acceleration are zero; gravity never stops acting, so acceleration remains g throughout the flight. Only the instantaneous velocity becomes zero at the top, so the answer is velocity is zero but acceleration is g downward.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Science (Gravitation)

35. Which of the following correctly matches the device with its working principle?
(a) Electric motor – electromagnetic induction (b) Electric bell – magnetic effect used in a generator (c) Transformer – heating effect of current (d) Electric generator – conversion of mechanical energy into electrical energy
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is pairing the motor with electromagnetic induction; the motor works on the force on a current-carrying conductor in a magnetic field, while induction is the generator's principle. A generator converts mechanical energy into electrical energy, so the answer is the generator pairing.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science (Magnetic Effects of Electric Current)

36. A person with myopia (short-sightedness) can see clearly:
(a) Distant objects only (b) Both near and distant objects (c) Nearby objects only (d) Neither near nor distant objects
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is distant objects only, which describes hypermetropia, the opposite defect; examiners often swap the two. In myopia the image forms in front of the retina, so only nearby objects are seen clearly, and the answer is nearby objects only.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science (Human Eye and the Colourful World)

37. The SI unit of power is:
(a) Joule (b) Newton (c) Watt (d) Pascal
Answer: (c)
Solution: Joule is the tempting trap because power and energy are closely related, but joule measures energy or work, not the rate of doing work. Power is work per unit time and its SI unit is the watt, so the answer is watt.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Science (Work and Energy)

38. Water pipes in cold countries sometimes burst in winter because:
(a) Water expands on freezing below 4°C (b) Water contracts on freezing (c) Ice is denser than water (d) Pipes expand on cooling
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is that water contracts on freezing, which is true for most liquids but not water; water shows anomalous expansion below 4°C. Ice is less dense and occupies more volume, exerting pressure that bursts pipes, so the answer is water expands on freezing below 4°C.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Physics (Thermal Properties of Matter)

39. The working of a hydraulic brake in vehicles is based on:
(a) Bernoulli's principle (b) Archimedes' principle (c) Pascal's law (d) Stoke's law
Answer: (c)
Solution: Archimedes' principle is the trap since both deal with fluids, but it concerns buoyancy, not pressure transmission. Hydraulic brakes transmit pressure equally through an enclosed fluid, which is Pascal's law, so the answer is Pascal's law.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Physics (Mechanical Properties of Fluids)

40. Which of the following electromagnetic waves has the shortest wavelength?
(a) Gamma rays (b) Microwaves (c) X-rays (d) Radio waves
Answer: (a)
Solution: X-rays are the tempting trap because they are well known for high energy, but gamma rays lie beyond X-rays in the spectrum. The order of decreasing wavelength is radio, microwave, infrared, visible, ultraviolet, X-ray, gamma, so the answer is gamma rays.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Physics (Electromagnetic Waves)

41. A body weighs more at the poles than at the equator because:
(a) The value of g is greater at the poles (b) The Earth is perfectly spherical (c) The poles are closer to the Sun (d) The equator has a stronger magnetic field
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is the perfectly spherical statement; the Earth is actually flattened at the poles, making the polar radius smaller. Since g is inversely related to the square of the radius (and rotation reduces g at the equator), g is greater at the poles, so the answer is that the value of g is greater at the poles.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Physics (Gravitation)

42. The phenomenon of total internal reflection is used in:
(a) Periscope (b) Simple microscope (c) Optical fibres (d) Photographic camera
Answer: (c)
Solution: The periscope is the trap because it also manipulates light, but it uses ordinary reflection by plane mirrors or prisms, not total internal reflection as its defining principle. Optical fibres guide light by repeated total internal reflection, so the answer is optical fibres.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Physics (Ray Optics)

43. When a current-carrying conductor is placed in a magnetic field, the direction of force on it is given by:
(a) Fleming's right-hand rule (b) Fleming's left-hand rule (c) Right-hand thumb rule (d) Lenz's law
Answer: (b)
Solution: Fleming's right-hand rule is the classic trap; it applies to generators (induced current), not to force on a conductor. The force on a current-carrying conductor (motor principle) is found by Fleming's left-hand rule, so the answer is Fleming's left-hand rule.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science (Magnetic Effects of Electric Current)

44. The kinetic energy of a body is doubled when its velocity is increased by a factor of:
(a) 2 (b) √2 (c) 4 (d) 1/2
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is the factor 2, which ignores that kinetic energy varies with the square of velocity. To double KE, velocity must rise by √2 (about 1.41 times), so the answer is √2.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Science (Work and Energy)

45. Which statement about sound waves is correct?
(a) Sound can travel through vacuum (b) Sound travels faster in air than in steel (c) Sound waves are transverse waves (d) Sound is a longitudinal mechanical wave
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is the transverse wave statement; light is transverse, and students often transfer that property to sound. Sound needs a medium and propagates as compressions and rarefactions, making it a longitudinal mechanical wave, so the answer is that sound is a longitudinal mechanical wave.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Science (Sound)

46. The device used to measure electric current in a circuit is:
(a) Ammeter (b) Voltmeter (c) Galvanometer used as an ohmmeter (d) Rheostat
Answer: (a)
Solution: The voltmeter is the tempting trap because it is also connected in circuits, but it measures potential difference and is connected in parallel. Current is measured by an ammeter connected in series, so the answer is ammeter.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science (Electricity)

47. A piece of paper and a stone dropped simultaneously in a vacuum will:
(a) Reach the ground at different times (b) Reach the ground at the same time (c) The paper will float (d) The stone will fall faster
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is that the stone falls faster, which is true in air due to air resistance but not in a vacuum. In a vacuum all bodies fall with the same acceleration g regardless of mass, so the answer is they reach the ground at the same time.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Science (Gravitation)

48. The heating element of an electric iron is usually made of nichrome because it has:
(a) Low resistance and low melting point (b) High resistance and low melting point (c) High resistance and high melting point (d) Low resistance and high melting point
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is high resistance with low melting point, which would make the element melt like a fuse. A heating element must produce heat (high resistance) without melting (high melting point), so the answer is high resistance and high melting point.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science (Electricity)

49. The image formed by a plane mirror is always:
(a) Real, inverted and same size (b) Real, erect and magnified (c) Virtual, inverted and diminished (d) Virtual, erect and same size
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is real and inverted, which confuses plane mirror images with those of concave mirrors in some positions. A plane mirror always forms a virtual, erect image of the same size behind the mirror, so the answer is virtual, erect and same size.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science (Light – Reflection and Refraction)

50. Which of the following is an example of a non-contact force?
(a) Gravitational force (b) Muscular force (c) Frictional force (d) Force applied by a spring
Answer: (a)
Solution: The spring force is the trap because a stretched spring seems to act at a distance, but it requires physical contact with the body. Gravitational force acts between bodies without any contact, so the answer is gravitational force.
Source: NCERT Class 8 Science (Force and Pressure)
