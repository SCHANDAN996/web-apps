Questions 76–100: Medium

76. A person standing in a moving bus falls forward when the bus suddenly stops. This is explained by
(a) Newton's third law of motion (b) Newton's first law of motion (c) Newton's second law of motion (d) the law of gravitation
Answer: (b)
Solution: The tempting wrong choice is the third law, because the situation involves an action (braking) and a reaction (falling), but no pair of forces on two bodies is involved. The upper body tends to continue its state of motion while the feet stop with the bus — this is inertia of motion, which is Newton's first law, so the answer is Newton's first law of motion.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Science (Force and Laws of Motion)

77. A convex mirror is preferred as a rear-view mirror in vehicles because
(a) it gives a magnified image (b) it gives a real image (c) it gives an erect, diminished image with a wider field of view (d) it converges light from behind
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is "magnified image" — a magnified image would mislead the driver about distance, so it cannot be the reason. A convex mirror always forms a virtual, erect and diminished image and covers a wider field of view, letting the driver see more traffic, so the answer is it gives an erect, diminished image with a wider field of view.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science (Light – Reflection and Refraction)

78. The sky appears blue because
(a) the atmosphere absorbs blue light (b) the sky reflects the colour of the oceans (c) shorter wavelengths are scattered more by air molecules (d) blue light travels faster than red light
Answer: (c)
Solution: "The sky reflects the oceans" is a popular but wrong belief — the sky is blue even far inland and at night over oceans it is dark. Rayleigh scattering is inversely proportional to the fourth power of wavelength, so blue (shorter wavelength) scatters most, so the answer is shorter wavelengths are scattered more by air molecules.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science (Human Eye and the Colourful World)

79. An electric fuse wire is made of a material having
(a) high melting point and high resistance (b) low melting point and high resistance (c) low melting point and low resistance (d) high melting point and low resistance
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is "high melting point" — a fuse must melt quickly when current exceeds the safe limit, so a high melting point would defeat its purpose. Fuse wire (tin-lead alloy) has a low melting point and relatively high resistance so it heats and melts first, so the answer is low melting point and high resistance.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science (Magnetic Effects of Electric Current)

80. When a ray of light passes from air into glass, it bends
(a) away from the normal because glass is rarer (b) it does not bend at all (c) away from the normal because glass is denser (d) towards the normal because glass is denser
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap pairs the correct direction with the wrong reason or vice versa; remember light slows down in a denser medium and bends towards the normal. Glass is optically denser than air, so the ray bends towards the normal, so the answer is towards the normal because glass is denser.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science (Light – Reflection and Refraction)

81. The loudness of sound depends on
(a) amplitude (b) wavelength (c) frequency (d) velocity
Answer: (a)
Solution: Frequency is the tempting wrong option, but frequency determines pitch, not loudness — a common confusion in exams. Loudness is related to the energy of the wave, which depends on the square of the amplitude, so the answer is amplitude.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Science (Sound)

82. A body weighs more at the poles than at the equator because
(a) the earth is flat at the equator (b) the earth rotates faster at the poles (c) the value of g is greater at the poles (d) the poles are closer to the sun
Answer: (c)
Solution: "The earth rotates faster at the poles" is the trap — rotation is fastest at the equator, and centrifugal effect there reduces g further. The earth is flattened at the poles, so polar radius is smaller and g is greater there, making weight more, so the answer is the value of g is greater at the poles.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Science (Gravitation)

83. Which of the following is used in a pressure cooker to cook food faster?
(a) Lowering of boiling point due to pressure (b) Increase in thermal conductivity of the vessel (c) Decrease in specific heat of water (d) Increase of boiling point of water due to increased pressure
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is "lowering of boiling point" — that happens at high altitudes where pressure is low, which is why cooking is slow there. Inside a pressure cooker, steam raises the pressure, raising water's boiling point above 100°C, so food cooks faster, so the answer is increase of boiling point of water due to increased pressure.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Physics (Thermal Properties of Matter)

84. The working of an optical fibre is based on
(a) refraction (b) interference (c) dispersion (d) total internal reflection
Answer: (d)
Solution: Refraction is the tempting wrong answer because light does refract when entering the fibre, but the guiding of light along the fibre is not by refraction. Light strikes the core-cladding boundary at angles greater than the critical angle and undergoes repeated total internal reflection, so the answer is total internal reflection.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Physics (Ray Optics)

85. A transformer works on the principle of
(a) mutual induction (b) self-induction (c) Ohm's law (d) Joule's heating
Answer: (a)
Solution: Self-induction is the trap — it operates in a single coil opposing its own current change, whereas a transformer has two coils. A changing current in the primary induces an emf in the secondary through mutual induction, so the answer is mutual induction.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Physics (Electromagnetic Induction)

86. A transformer cannot work on
(a) direct current (b) alternating current (c) high voltage AC (d) low voltage AC
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is to think transformers fail at low voltage — they work on AC of any voltage as long as the current changes. A steady direct current produces no changing magnetic flux, so no emf is induced in the secondary, so the answer is direct current.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Physics (Electromagnetic Induction)

87. The device used to measure electric current in a circuit is
(a) ammeter, connected in series (b) voltmeter, connected in series (c) ammeter, connected in parallel (d) voltmeter, connected in parallel
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is the connection: a voltmeter measures potential difference and is connected in parallel, and candidates often mix the two. Current must pass through the measuring device, so an ammeter is connected in series, so the answer is ammeter, connected in series.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science (Electricity)

88. Which colour of light deviates the most while passing through a prism?
(a) Red (b) Violet (c) Yellow (d) Green
Answer: (b)
Solution: Red is the trap because it appears first in the spectrum sequence VIBGYOR, but red deviates the least. Refractive index is highest for the shortest wavelength, so violet bends the most, so the answer is violet.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science (Human Eye and the Colourful World)

89. The SI unit of power is
(a) joule (b) newton (c) watt (d) pascal
Answer: (c)
Solution: Joule is the tempting wrong option because power and energy are often confused; joule is the unit of work or energy. Power is the rate of doing work, measured in joules per second, called the watt, so the answer is watt.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Science (Work and Energy)

90. One kilowatt-hour (kWh) is a unit of
(a) energy (b) power (c) force (d) current
Answer: (a)
Solution: Power is the trap because "kilowatt" appears in the name, but multiplying power by time gives energy. 1 kWh = 3.6 × 10⁶ joules and is the commercial unit of electrical energy, so the answer is energy.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science (Electricity)

91. An echo is heard distinctly only if the reflecting surface is at a minimum distance of about (at normal temperature)
(a) 34.4 m (b) 17.2 m (c) 10 m (d) 5 m
Answer: (b)
Solution: 34.4 m is the trap — it is the total distance travelled by sound (to the wall and back) in 0.1 s, not the distance of the wall. With sound speed about 344 m/s and persistence of hearing 0.1 s, the wall must be at least 344 × 0.1 / 2 = 17.2 m away, so the answer is 17.2 m.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Science (Sound)

92. The image formed by a plane mirror is
(a) virtual, erect and same size (b) real, erect and same size (c) virtual, inverted and diminished (d) real, inverted and magnified
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Real, erect and same size" is the trap — the image looks as if it is behind the mirror, but the rays do not actually meet there. A plane mirror always forms a virtual, erect, laterally inverted image of the same size, so the answer is virtual, erect and same size.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science (Light – Reflection and Refraction)

93. Which of the following phenomena explains the twinkling of stars?
(a) dispersion (b) scattering (c) total internal reflection (d) atmospheric refraction
Answer: (d)
Solution: Scattering is the trap because it explains blue sky and red sunsets, but twinkling is different. Starlight passes through layers of air of varying density, and continuous refraction shifts the apparent position of the star, causing twinkling, so the answer is atmospheric refraction.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science (Human Eye and the Colourful World)

94. A person suffering from myopia (short-sightedness) is corrected using
(a) convex lens (b) concave lens (c) cylindrical lens (d) bifocal lens
Answer: (b)
Solution: Convex lens is the trap — it corrects hypermetropia (long-sightedness), the opposite defect. In myopia the image forms in front of the retina, and a concave (diverging) lens shifts it back onto the retina, so the answer is concave lens.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science (Human Eye and the Colourful World)

95. The escape velocity from the surface of the earth is approximately
(a) 7.9 km/s (b) 3.0 km/s (c) 9.8 km/s (d) 11.2 km/s
Answer: (d)
Solution: 7.9 km/s is the trap — it is the orbital velocity of a satellite close to the earth, not the escape velocity. Escape velocity is √2 times the orbital velocity, about 11.2 km/s, so the answer is 11.2 km/s.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Physics (Gravitation)

96. When a body is taken from the equator to the poles, its weight
(a) increases (b) decreases (c) remains the same (d) first increases then decreases
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Remains the same" is the trap — mass remains constant, but weight depends on g. Since g is maximum at the poles (smaller radius, no rotational effect), weight increases, so the answer is increases.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Science (Gravitation)

97. The heating element of an electric iron is made of
(a) copper (b) aluminium (c) tungsten (d) nichrome
Answer: (d)
Solution: Tungsten is the trap — it is used in incandescent bulb filaments because of its very high melting point, not in irons. Nichrome has high resistivity and does not oxidise easily at high temperatures, making it ideal for heating elements, so the answer is nichrome.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science (Electricity)

98. Sound waves cannot travel through
(a) water (b) vacuum (c) air (d) steel
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is to think sound travels fastest in air and hence only in air — in fact it travels fastest in solids like steel. Sound is a mechanical wave needing a material medium, so it cannot travel through a vacuum, so the answer is vacuum.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Science (Sound)

99. The focal length of a convex lens is 50 cm. Its power is
(a) +0.5 D (b) −2 D (c) +2 D (d) +50 D
Answer: (c)
Solution: +0.5 D is the trap — it comes from forgetting to convert centimetres to metres before taking the reciprocal. Power P = 1/f (in metres) = 1/0.5 = +2 dioptres, positive for a convex lens, so the answer is +2 D.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science (Light – Reflection and Refraction)

100. A ball is thrown vertically upwards. At the highest point, its
(a) velocity and acceleration are both zero (b) velocity is maximum and acceleration is zero (c) velocity is zero but acceleration is g downward (d) velocity and acceleration are both maximum
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Velocity and acceleration are both zero" is the classic trap — if acceleration were zero at the top, the ball would never come back down. At the highest point the velocity is momentarily zero, but gravity still acts with acceleration g downward, so the answer is velocity is zero but acceleration is g downward.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Science (Motion)
