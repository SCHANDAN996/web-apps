# 📊 PYQ Analysis — Physics in Everyday Life

## 1. How Exams Ask Questions from This Chapter

Questions on everyday physics appear across UPSC (Prelims GS), SSC (CGL/CHSL/GD), Banking (IBPS/SBI GA), Railway (NTPC/Group D) and State PCS exams. The pattern is remarkably consistent: examiners rarely ask derivations or numericals here — they test whether you can connect a **daily-life observation to the correct physical principle**, and whether you can resist a **plausible-sounding wrong principle**.

The most repeatedly tested sub-topics, in words rather than numbers:

- **Optics in daily life** — the single richest source: why the sky is blue, why the sun appears red at sunrise/sunset, mirage, twinkling of stars, rainbow formation, why a swimming pool looks shallower, diamond's sparkle, rear-view and side mirrors of vehicles.
- **Surface tension, capillarity, viscosity** — spherical raindrops, soap cleaning, oil spreading on water, kerosene rising in a wick, blotting paper.
- **Pressure and fluids** — why dams are thicker at the bottom, why a sharp knife cuts better, why it is easier to swim in sea water, hydraulic brakes/lifts, drinking through a straw.
- **Heat and thermometry** — sea breeze/land breeze, why a metallic tile feels colder than a wooden one, thermos flask, pressure cooker, white clothes in summer.
- **Sound** — echo, why we hear sound better at night (refraction of sound), ultrasound applications, why lightning is seen before thunder is heard.
- **Electricity at home** — fuse wire, earthing, series vs parallel in domestic wiring, why birds on a wire are not electrocuted.
- **Gravitation and motion** — feeling heavier/lighter in a lift, why a falling object and a horizontally thrown object hit the ground together, banking of roads, cream separation by centrifuge.

## 2. Exam-wise Flavour (in words)

- **UPSC / State PCS:** statement-based questions ("Consider the following statements…"), often mixing two correct principles with one subtly wrong one. Conceptual depth is higher; application to technology (RADAR, SONAR, optical fibre, MRI) is common.
- **SSC (CGL/CHSL):** direct one-liners — "The blue colour of the sky is due to…", "Which mirror is used as a rear-view mirror?" Speed matters more than depth.
- **Railway (NTPC/Group D):** very direct factual recall, frequently repeated across shifts — units, instruments, simple phenomena.
- **Banking:** fewer questions, but when they appear they are usually instrument–principle matching (e.g., hydrometer — density of liquids) or simple everyday applications.

## 3. The Examiner's Traps (Trap Types)

1. **Almost-Correct Statement:** "The sky appears blue because of *reflection* of sunlight by dust particles." — The phenomenon is real, the mechanism is wrong (it is **scattering**, specifically Rayleigh scattering). This is the most common trap in this chapter.
2. **Mirror/Lens Swap:** Convex vs concave confusion — rear-view mirror (convex, wider field), shaving mirror and dentist's mirror (concave, magnified image), street lights (convex). Examiners swap the two in options.
3. **Chronological/Sequence Confusion:** "Thunder is heard before lightning is seen" — reversal of the actual order (light travels far faster than sound).
4. **Negative Phrasing:** "Which of the following is **NOT** an application of total internal reflection?" — options include mirage, optical fibre, sparkling diamond, and the correct answer is something like "twinkling of stars" (which is refraction through turbulent atmosphere). Students misread NOT and pick a true application.
5. **Similar-Name Confusion:** Scattering vs dispersion vs diffraction; conduction vs convection vs radiation; SONAR vs RADAR (sound vs radio waves).
6. **Unit Trap:** Pressure asked in pascal but an option given in bar or atm; power in kW vs kWh (energy). kWh as a unit of *energy*, not power, is a classic.
7. **Half-True Reasoning:** "A pressure cooker cooks faster because pressure inside is low" — the direction is reversed; pressure is *high*, raising the boiling point of water.

## 4. High-Yield PYQ-Style Questions with Solutions

**Q1.** The blue colour of the sky is due to —
(a) Reflection of sunlight by clouds
(b) Scattering of sunlight by air molecules
(c) Dispersion of sunlight by water droplets
(d) Absorption of blue light by the atmosphere
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** Air molecules scatter shorter (blue) wavelengths most strongly — Rayleigh scattering. Trap type: **Almost-Correct** — (a) and (c) name real optical phenomena but the wrong mechanism. Dispersion by droplets explains the rainbow, not the sky.
*Source: NCERT Class 10 Science (Human Eye and the Colourful World)*

**Q2.** Which of the following is NOT based on total internal reflection?
(a) Sparkling of a diamond
(b) Working of an optical fibre
(c) Twinkling of stars
(d) Mirage in a desert
**Answer: (c)**
**Solution:** Twinkling is caused by atmospheric **refraction** through layers of varying density. Diamond, optical fibre and mirage all involve total internal reflection. Trap type: **Negative Phrasing** — three options are true applications; misreading "NOT" costs the mark.
*Source: NCERT Class 10/12 Physics*

**Q3.** A pressure cooker cooks food faster because —
(a) the pressure inside is low, so water boils at a lower temperature
(b) the pressure inside is high, raising the boiling point of water
(c) heat cannot escape from the cooker at all
(d) steam absorbs all the heat from the flame
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** Higher pressure raises water's boiling point above 100°C, so food cooks at a higher temperature. Trap type: **Half-True Reasoning** — option (a) reverses the pressure direction; it describes what happens at high altitude, where cooking is *slower*.
*Source: NCERT Class 11 Physics (Thermal Properties)*

**Q4.** The side-view mirror of a car carries the warning "Objects in the mirror are closer than they appear" because it is a —
(a) concave mirror (b) plane mirror (c) convex mirror (d) convex lens
**Answer: (c)**
**Solution:** A convex mirror gives a wider field of view but forms a diminished image, making objects look farther than they are. Trap type: **Mirror Swap** — concave mirrors magnify and are used for shaving/dental mirrors, not vehicle side mirrors.
*Source: NCERT Class 10 Science (Light)*

**Q5.** It is easier to swim in sea water than in river water because —
(a) sea water is colder
(b) sea water has waves that push the swimmer
(c) the density of sea water is higher, giving greater upthrust
(d) river water has more friction
**Answer: (c)**
**Solution:** Dissolved salts raise the density of sea water, so buoyant force (Archimedes' principle) is larger. Trap type: **Almost-Correct** — (b) sounds experientially true but is not the physics reason.
*Source: NCERT Class 9 Science (Gravitation — Buoyancy)*

**Q6.** Lightning is seen before thunder is heard because —
(a) lightning occurs before thunder is produced
(b) light travels much faster than sound
(c) sound is absorbed by clouds
(d) thunder is produced at a greater height
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** Both occur simultaneously; light (~3 × 10⁸ m/s) vastly outruns sound (~340 m/s in air). Trap type: **Chronological Confusion** — option (a) reverses cause and effect.
*Source: NCERT Class 9 Science (Sound)*

**Q7.** Which instrument works on Archimedes' principle?
(a) Barometer (b) Hydrometer (c) Ammeter (d) Thermometer
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** A hydrometer floats higher or lower depending on liquid density — a direct application of buoyancy. Trap type: **Similar-Name Confusion** — barometer (atmospheric pressure) is the tempting wrong pick.
*Source: PYQ-style*

**Q8.** A bird sitting on a high-voltage bare wire is not electrocuted because —
(a) its body is a perfect insulator
(b) there is no potential difference between its two feet
(c) the current chooses the ground instead
(d) its feathers repel electricity
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** Current flows only across a potential difference; both feet are at nearly the same potential on the same wire. Trap type: **Almost-Correct** — (a) is false; the bird's body conducts, but no circuit to a different potential is completed.
*Source: PYQ-style*

**Q9.** In domestic wiring, appliances are connected in parallel because —
(a) it saves wire
(b) each appliance then gets the full mains voltage and works independently
(c) it increases the total resistance
(d) it reduces the current in the circuit to zero
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** Parallel connection gives every appliance 220 V and lets each be switched independently; a fault in one does not break the others. Trap type: **Half-True Reasoning** — parallel *decreases* equivalent resistance, so (c) is reversed.
*Source: NCERT Class 10 Science (Electricity)*

**Q10.** The kilowatt-hour (kWh) is a unit of —
(a) power (b) electric current (c) electrical energy (d) potential difference
**Answer: (c)**
**Solution:** kWh = power × time = energy; 1 kWh = 3.6 × 10⁶ J, the "unit" on electricity bills. Trap type: **Unit Trap** — students see "kilowatt" and pick power.
*Source: NCERT Class 10 Science (Electricity)*

## 5. The 15/45-Second Rule Applied

**15-second questions (instant recall — answer and move on):**
- Q4 (convex side mirror), Q6 (light faster than sound), Q10 (kWh = energy). These are pure one-fact recalls. If you know it, mark it in under 15 seconds; if you don't, no amount of staring will help — flag and move.

**45-second questions (need one reasoning step — worth the time):**
- Q2 (NOT + total internal reflection): you must verify each of the four options against the principle — budget the full 45 seconds, and underline "NOT" before reading options.
- Q8 (bird on wire): requires constructing the potential-difference argument; guessing between (a) and (b) is where most marks are lost, so spend the time.

**Strategy:** In a mixed GK section, bank the 15-second optics/units questions first, then return to statement-based and negative-phrasing questions with the time saved. Never spend 45 seconds on a pure recall item you simply don't know — that is a skip, not a solve.
