# Physics in Everyday Life

## The Hook: Why Your Pressure Cooker Whistles Before Lunch

Every morning, millions of Indian kitchens perform a physics experiment. A pressure cooker whistles, and dal that would take an hour on an open flame is ready in fifteen minutes. Why? Because trapped steam raises the pressure inside, and higher pressure raises the boiling point of water — so food cooks at a temperature above 100°C. No magic. Just physics, quietly running your home, your phone, your fan, and even the rainbow after monsoon rain.

Examiners know this. That is why "everyday physics" questions appear again and again in SSC CGL, IBPS, Railway and State PCS exams — not as formulas, but as *situations*: Why does a swimmer float better in the Dead Sea? Why does the sky look blue? Why does a falling cat twist its body? This chapter turns your daily life into a question bank you already own.

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this chapter, you will be able to:

1. Explain 20+ everyday phenomena using the correct physical principle.

3. Spot the examiner's favourite traps — reversed cause-effect and "almost correct" statements.
4. Answer statement-based questions on light, sound, heat and fluids with confidence.

## 1. Mechanics Around You

### Why a Falling Object and You Reach the Ground Together

Galileo's insight still rules: in a vacuum, all objects fall at the same rate regardless of mass, because acceleration due to gravity (g ≈ 9.8 m/s²) acts equally on all. Air resistance is what makes a feather lag behind a stone — not gravity.

**Inertia in daily life:**
- You lurch **forward** when a bus brakes suddenly — your body tends to continue its state of motion (Newton's First Law).
- You are pushed **backward** when the bus starts suddenly — your body tends to stay at rest.
- Dust comes off a carpet when beaten — the carpet moves, the dust stays put due to inertia of rest.

### Action–Reaction (Newton's Third Law)

- A rocket rises because exhaust gases push down and the gases push the rocket up.
- Walking: you push the ground backward; the ground pushes you forward (friction makes this grip possible).
- A gun recoils backward when a bullet fires forward — **conservation of momentum**.

### Why a Cricket Fielder Pulls Hands Backward While Catching

By increasing the *time* of impact, the fielder reduces the *force* on the palms (Impulse = Force × Time). Same logic: cars have crumple zones, and athletes land on cushioned floors.

## 2. Pressure and Fluids

### The Pressure Cooker (Again — It Is That Important)

Boiling point of water **increases with pressure**. Inside a sealed cooker, pressure rises above atmospheric, water boils at roughly 120°C, and food cooks faster. At high altitudes (hill stations), the opposite happens: atmospheric pressure is low, water boils below 100°C, and cooking takes *longer*.

### Why a Sharp Knife Cuts Better

Pressure = Force ÷ Area. A sharp edge has a tiny area, so the same force creates enormous pressure. Same reason: nails have pointed tips, camels have broad feet (to walk on sand), and school bags have wide straps.

### Floating, Sinking and Archimedes

- A body floats when the **buoyant force** (weight of displaced fluid) equals or exceeds its weight.
- An iron nail sinks, but an iron ship floats — the ship's hollow shape displaces a huge volume of water, so average density stays below water's.
- The Dead Sea's extremely salty water is denser than the human body, so swimmers float effortlessly.
- **Hydrometers** (used to test milk purity or battery acid) work on Archimedes' principle.

### Surface Tension and Capillarity

- Water droplets are spherical — surface tension pulls the surface into minimum area.
- Oil rises in a lamp wick, and water rises in soil to plant roots — **capillary action**.
- Soap reduces water's surface tension, letting it spread into fabric — that is why soap cleans.

🧠 **Active Recall:** At a hill station, does water boil above or below 100°C? Why does a ship float while a nail sinks? What force makes a water drop spherical?

## 3. Heat in Daily Life

### Three Modes of Heat Transfer

| Mode | Medium needed? | Daily example |
|---|---|---|
| Conduction | Yes (solids) | Metal spoon heating in hot tea |
| Convection | Yes (fluids) | Sea breeze, room heater warming air |
| Radiation | **No** | Sun's heat reaching Earth through vacuum |

### Everyday Applications

- **White clothes in summer, dark in winter:** white reflects radiant heat; dark absorbs it.
- **Thermos flask:** vacuum between double walls stops conduction and convection; silvered walls reflect radiation.
- **Sea breeze:** land heats faster than water by day → warm air rises over land → cool air flows in from the sea. At night, the pattern reverses (land breeze).
- **Water as coolant in car radiators:** water has a very high **specific heat capacity** — it absorbs lots of heat with little temperature rise.

🧠 **Active Recall:** Which mode of heat transfer needs no medium? Why is water used in car radiators? Which way does air flow during a daytime sea breeze?

## 4. Light: The Examiner's Favourite

### The Big Four Phenomena

1. **Reflection** — light bounces off surfaces. Mirrors, periscopes.
2. **Refraction** — light bends when changing medium. A straw looks bent in water; a pool looks shallower than it is; stars twinkle; the Sun is visible slightly before actual sunrise and after actual sunset (atmospheric refraction).
3. **Dispersion** — white light splits into colours through a prism. The **rainbow** is dispersion + internal reflection inside raindrops.
4. **Scattering** — tiny particles redirect light. The **sky is blue** because blue light scatters most (Rayleigh scattering); the **Sun looks red at sunrise/sunset** because light travels a longer path and blue is scattered away, leaving red.

### Mirrors and Lenses in Devices

- **Rear-view mirror of vehicles:** convex mirror — wider field of view, erect diminished image.
- **Shaving mirror / dentist's mirror / headlights:** concave mirror — converging, magnified or focused beam.
- **Optical fibres:** **total internal reflection** — used in endoscopy and high-speed internet cables.
- **Camera and human eye:** convex lens forms a real, inverted image on film/retina.

### Vision Defects

| Defect | Sees clearly | Corrected by |
|---|---|---|
| Myopia (short-sightedness) | Near objects | Concave lens |
| Hypermetropia (long-sightedness) | Distant objects | Convex lens |
| Presbyopia (old age) | Neither well | Bifocal lens |
| Astigmatism | Distorted | Cylindrical lens |

🧠 **Active Recall:** Why is the sky blue but the setting sun red? Which mirror is in a car's rear-view? Which phenomenon runs an optical fibre?

## 5. Sound Around You

- Sound is a **mechanical, longitudinal** wave — it needs a medium. It cannot travel through vacuum (light can).
- Speed: fastest in **solids**, then liquids, slowest in gases (~332 m/s in air at 0°C).
- **Echo:** heard distinctly only if reflected sound reaches the ear at least ~0.1 second after the original — the reflecting surface must be roughly **17 metres** away or more.
- **Ultrasound** (above 20,000 Hz): sonar, medical ultrasonography, cleaning delicate instruments.
- **Infrasound** (below 20 Hz): produced by earthquakes, elephants and whales.
- **Doppler effect:** the pitch of a train's whistle rises as it approaches and falls as it recedes — the basis of radar speed guns and medical blood-flow imaging.

🧠 **Active Recall:** Can sound travel on the Moon? Why? What minimum distance gives a distinct echo? Which effect explains the changing pitch of a passing train?

## 6. Electricity and Magnetism at Home

- **Fuse wire:** low melting point, high resistance alloy (tin-lead); melts and breaks the circuit when current exceeds safe limits.
- **Why birds sitting on a single high-voltage wire don't get shocked:** no potential difference across their body — current needs a voltage difference to flow.
- **Earthing:** provides a low-resistance path for leakage current, protecting you from shocks.
- **Transformer:** works on **electromagnetic induction** (Faraday's law) — steps voltage up or down; works only on AC, never DC.
- **Electric motor:** converts electrical energy → mechanical energy (fans, mixers). **Generator/dynamo:** mechanical → electrical.
- **MRI scanner:** uses strong magnetic fields and radio waves — no X-rays.

🧠 **Active Recall:** Why doesn't a bird on a wire get electrocuted? On what principle does a transformer work? Why does a fuse melt?

## 7. Modern Physics in Daily Life

- **Microwave oven:** microwaves make water molecules vibrate, heating food from within.
- **Refrigerator:** a refrigerant evaporates inside (absorbing heat) and condenses outside (releasing it) — latent heat in action.
- **LED bulbs:** light from electron transitions in a semiconductor — far more efficient than filament bulbs.
- **Solar cell:** photovoltaic effect — light energy converted directly into electricity.
- **X-rays:** high-energy electromagnetic waves used for imaging bones; discovered by **Wilhelm Röntgen (1895)**.
- **Laser:** coherent, monochromatic, highly directional light — used in surgery, barcode scanners, fibre communication.

## 🧷 Mnemonics

1. **"VIBGYOR"** — colours of the rainbow in order: Violet, Indigo, Blue, Green, Yellow, Orange, Red. (Violet deviates most, red least.)
2. **"My-Hyper: Con-Con"** — **My**opia → **Con**cave lens; **Hyper**metropia → **Con**vex lens.
3. **"Solid Shouts Loudest"** — sound travels fastest in **Solids**, then liquids, then gases.
4. **"Cooker = Pressure ↑, Boiling ↑"** — more pressure, higher boiling point; hills = reverse.

## ⚠️ Caution: Examiner's Trap

**Trap 1 — Reverse Causation (विपरीत कार्य-कारण):**
*"Water boils at a lower temperature on hills because the pressure cooker works faster there."* Both halves are twisted. On hills, atmospheric pressure is **low**, so boiling point **falls** — and cooking is **slower**, which is exactly why pressure cookers are *more useful* there. The examiner swaps cause and effect.

**Trap 2 — Almost-Correct Statement (लगभग सही कथन):**
*"The sky appears blue because the atmosphere reflects blue light from the oceans."* Sounds plausible, but the true reason is **scattering** of sunlight by air molecules (Rayleigh scattering) — reflection and oceans have nothing to do with it. One wrong word ("reflects" instead of "scatters") makes the whole statement false.

**Trap 3 — Similar Names (समान नाम):**
**Conduction vs Convection vs Radiation**, and **Reflection vs Refraction vs Diffraction** — examiners place these side by side in options. Remember: only **radiation** needs no medium; only **refraction** involves a change of speed between media.
