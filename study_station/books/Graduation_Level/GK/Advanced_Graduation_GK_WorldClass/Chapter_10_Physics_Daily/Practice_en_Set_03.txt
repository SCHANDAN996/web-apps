Questions 51–75: Medium

51. A person standing in a moving bus falls forward when the bus suddenly stops. This is explained by
(a) Newton's third law of motion (b) Newton's second law of motion (c) Newton's first law of motion (d) Law of conservation of energy
Answer: (c)
Solution: The tempting choice is the third law, since the bus "acts" and the passenger "reacts", but no action-reaction pair is involved here. The body tends to continue its state of motion due to inertia of motion, which is Newton's first law, so the answer is Newton's first law of motion.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Science

52. It is easier to roll a barrel than to pull it along the road because
(a) the surface area decreases while rolling (b) sliding friction is smaller than rolling friction (c) the full weight acts while pulling (d) rolling friction is smaller than sliding friction
Answer: (d)
Solution: Many students pick the surface-area option, but friction is nearly independent of contact area; the real reason is that rolling friction is much smaller than sliding friction, so the answer is rolling friction is smaller than sliding friction.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Physics

53. A sudden fall in barometer reading indicates
(a) stormy weather (b) calm weather (c) dry weather (d) cold wave
Answer: (a)
Solution: A rapid fall in atmospheric pressure means a low-pressure system is approaching, which brings storms. Calm and dry weather are associated with steady or high pressure, so the answer is stormy weather.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Physics

54. An iron needle floats on the surface of water due to
(a) buoyancy (b) surface tension (c) viscosity (d) capillarity
Answer: (b)
Solution: Buoyancy is the tempting trap, but the needle's density is greater than water, so buoyancy alone cannot support it. The water's surface film behaves like a stretched membrane due to surface tension, so the answer is surface tension.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Physics

55. The sky appears blue because of
(a) reflection of light (b) scattering of light (c) refraction of light (d) dispersion of light
Answer: (b)
Solution: Dispersion is the common trap, but dispersion splits white light into colours, as in a rainbow. Shorter blue wavelengths are scattered most by air molecules (Rayleigh scattering), so the answer is scattering of light.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science

56. The danger signals are red because red light
(a) has the highest frequency (b) has the shortest wavelength (c) travels fastest in air (d) is scattered the least
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is the shortest-wavelength option — red actually has the longest wavelength in visible light, which is why it is scattered the least and visible from far away, so the answer is it is scattered the least.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science

57. A convex mirror is used as a rear-view mirror in vehicles because it
(a) gives a magnified image (b) gives a real image (c) converges light rays (d) gives an erect image with a wider field of view
Answer: (d)
Solution: The magnified-image option tempts many, but a convex mirror always forms a diminished, erect, virtual image covering a wide area, so the answer is it gives an erect image with a wider field of view.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science

58. When a ray of light passes from air into water, it bends
(a) away from the normal (b) towards the normal (c) along the surface (d) and does not deviate at all
Answer: (b)
Solution: Water is optically denser than air, so light slows down and bends towards the normal. Bending away from the normal happens when going from water to air, so the answer is towards the normal.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science

59. The twinkling of stars is due to
(a) reflection (b) total internal reflection (c) atmospheric refraction (d) diffraction
Answer: (c)
Solution: Total internal reflection is the trap — it explains mirages and sparkling diamonds, not twinkling. Starlight passes through layers of air of varying density, causing continuous refraction, so the answer is atmospheric refraction.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science

60. A diamond sparkles because of
(a) total internal reflection (b) refraction only (c) scattering (d) dispersion only
Answer: (a)
Solution: Diamond has a very high refractive index and a small critical angle (about 24°), so light entering it undergoes repeated total internal reflection and emerges brilliantly, so the answer is total internal reflection.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Physics

61. The working principle of an optical fibre is
(a) refraction (b) scattering (c) total internal reflection (d) interference
Answer: (c)
Solution: Light entering the fibre strikes the core-cladding boundary at angles greater than the critical angle and is totally internally reflected along the fibre, so the answer is total internal reflection.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Physics

62. Sound travels fastest in
(a) steel (b) water (c) air (d) vacuum
Answer: (a)
Solution: Vacuum is the trap — sound cannot travel in vacuum at all since it needs a medium. Sound travels fastest in solids because particles are closely packed, so the answer is steel.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Science

63. The pitch of a sound depends on its
(a) amplitude (b) wavelength only (c) frequency (d) speed
Answer: (c)
Solution: Amplitude is the tempting option, but amplitude determines loudness, not pitch. Higher frequency means a shriller (higher-pitched) sound, so the answer is frequency.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Science

64. An echo is heard distinctly only if the reflecting surface is at least about 17 m away because
(a) the human ear retains sound for about 0.1 s (b) sound needs 17 m to form (c) sound travels 17 m per second (d) reflection needs a large surface
Answer: (a)
Solution: The persistence of hearing is about 0.1 s; in this time sound covers about 34 m (to and fro), so the minimum one-way distance is about 17 m, so the answer is the human ear retains sound for about 0.1 s.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Science

65. In a pressure cooker, food cooks faster because
(a) pressure decreases the boiling point (b) steam absorbs all impurities (c) heat cannot escape at all (d) increased pressure raises the boiling point of water
Answer: (d)
Solution: The first option is the classic trap — pressure cookers work by raising, not lowering, the boiling point, so water boils above 100°C and food cooks faster, so the answer is increased pressure raises the boiling point of water.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Physics

66. Water pipes in cold countries sometimes burst in winter because
(a) water contracts on freezing (b) ice is heavier than water (c) pipes contract more than ice (d) water expands on freezing
Answer: (d)
Solution: Water shows anomalous expansion; on freezing below 4°C it expands, exerting pressure on pipe walls. Ice is actually lighter (less dense) than water, so the answer is water expands on freezing.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Physics

67. A metal rim is heated before being fitted on a wooden wheel because on heating the rim
(a) expands and slips on easily (b) contracts and grips tightly (c) becomes soft (d) loses weight
Answer: (a)
Solution: Metals expand on heating; the expanded rim fits over the wheel and contracts on cooling to grip tightly. Contraction happens on cooling, not heating, so the answer is it expands and slips on easily.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Physics

68. The unit of power is
(a) joule (b) watt (c) newton (d) pascal
Answer: (b)
Solution: Joule is the trap — it is the unit of work or energy, not power. Power is the rate of doing work, measured in watts (joule per second), so the answer is watt.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Science

69. One kilowatt-hour (kWh) is a unit of
(a) power (b) current (c) force (d) energy
Answer: (d)
Solution: Power is the common trap because "kilowatt" appears in the name, but kWh = power × time, which equals energy (1 kWh = 3.6 × 10⁶ J), so the answer is energy.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science

70. The fuse wire in an electric circuit is made of a material having
(a) high melting point and high resistance (b) low melting point and high resistance (c) low melting point and low resistance (d) high melting point and low resistance
Answer: (b)
Solution: A fuse must melt quickly when excess current flows, so it needs a low melting point, and it must produce enough heat, so it needs relatively high resistance (e.g., tin-lead alloy), so the answer is low melting point and high resistance.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science

71. The device used to measure electric current in a circuit is
(a) ammeter (b) voltmeter (c) galvanometer only (d) ohmmeter
Answer: (a)
Solution: A voltmeter measures potential difference and is connected in parallel, while an ammeter measures current and is connected in series, so the answer is ammeter.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science

72. A transformer works on the principle of
(a) self-induction (b) mutual induction (c) heating effect of current (d) magnetic effect of current
Answer: (b)
Solution: A changing current in the primary coil induces an emf in the secondary coil through mutual induction; it works only on AC, not DC, so the answer is mutual induction.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Physics

73. Which of the following electromagnetic waves has the shortest wavelength?
(a) X-rays (b) ultraviolet rays (c) gamma rays (d) microwaves
Answer: (c)
Solution: X-rays is the tempting trap, but the order of decreasing wavelength is radio > microwave > infrared > visible > ultraviolet > X-rays > gamma rays, so the answer is gamma rays.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Physics

74. The ozone layer absorbs which radiation coming from the Sun?
(a) ultraviolet (b) infrared (c) visible light (d) radio waves
Answer: (a)
Solution: Infrared is the trap — it is associated with the greenhouse effect, not ozone. The ozone layer in the stratosphere absorbs harmful ultraviolet radiation, so the answer is ultraviolet.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Physics

75. A geostationary satellite revolves around the Earth with a period of
(a) 12 hours (b) 48 hours (c) 24 hours (d) 365 days
Answer: (c)
Solution: A geostationary satellite orbits above the equator with the same period as the Earth's rotation, so it appears stationary relative to the ground; its period is 24 hours, so the answer is 24 hours.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Physics
