# 🧒 The Feynman Corner — Physical Geography of India

## The Concept Everyone Gets Wrong: Why does the windward side of the Western Ghats flood with rain while the Deccan Plateau, just a few kilometres away, stays dry?

Students memorise "rain shadow region" as a two-word answer, but in the exam hall they freeze when the question is twisted: *"Pune receives less rainfall than Mumbai because…"* or *"Which region lies in the rain shadow of the Western Ghats?"* Let's fix this forever.

---

## Explain It Like I'm 12

Imagine you are carrying a heavy school bag full of water bottles (that's the **moisture-laden monsoon wind** coming from the Arabian Sea). In front of you is a huge wall — the **Western Ghats**, rising 1,000–1,500 m like a giant staircase.

To cross the wall, you must climb. As you climb, the air gets colder (temperature drops about 6.5°C for every 1,000 m you rise). Cold air cannot hold as much water as warm air — just like a cold glass "sweats" water droplets on a hot day. So the wind is **forced to drop its water** on the way up. That's why the western slopes — Mumbai, Goa, coastal Karnataka, Kerala — get drenched with 200–400 cm of rain.

Now you've crossed the wall. Your bag is **empty**. You descend the other side — and descending air actually gets **warmer and drier**, so it *absorbs* moisture instead of releasing it. The land on this side — the Deccan Plateau (Pune, Solapur, rain-shadow Maharashtra, interior Karnataka, Telangana) — gets only about 60–70 cm of rain.

**One line to remember:** The mountain *wrings out* the cloud like a wet towel on one side; the other side gets the already-squeezed towel.

---

## The Daily-Life Anchor

Think of a **wet sponge pressed against a wall**:
- Side facing you (windward) = water dripping out = heavy rain.
- Side behind the wall (leeward) = dry = rain shadow.

Every time you see "leeward," "rain shadow," "orographic rainfall," or "Western Ghats" in a question, picture that sponge.

---

## The Unforgettable Trick

**"Ghats WRING, Plateau DRY"**
- **W**indward side → **R**ain heavy (Mumbai ~200+ cm)
- **L**eeward side → rain **shadow** (Pune ~70 cm)

And the examiner's favourite trap pair:
- **Mumbai vs Pune** — same latitude, same monsoon, opposite rainfall. Why? Mumbai is windward, Pune is leeward. If an option says "Pune is farther from the sea" — that's the trap; distance is small, the **mountain barrier** is the real reason.

**Trap alert:** The same logic applies to the **Bay of Bengal branch** — the Khasi-Jaintia hills force the wind up, giving **Mawsynram/Cherrapunji** the world's heaviest rainfall, while the plateau beyond stays comparatively dry. Examiners swap "Arabian Sea branch" and "Bay of Bengal branch" in options to catch you.
