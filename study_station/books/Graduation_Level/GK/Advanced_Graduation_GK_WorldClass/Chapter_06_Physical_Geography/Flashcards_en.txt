Card 1
Front: Which is the highest peak of India, and in which range does it lie?
Back: Kangchenjunga (8,586 m), in the Himalayas on the India–Nepal border (Sikkim side). Students often confuse it with Mount Everest, but Everest lies entirely in Nepal–Tibet, not in India.

Card 2
Front: Which is the only major Indian river that flows westward into the Arabian Sea through a rift valley, and which river shares this feature?
Back: The Narmada, along with the Tapi (Tapti). Both flow west through rift valleys between the Vindhya–Satpura ranges; most other peninsular rivers flow east into the Bay of Bengal.

Card 3
Front: The Himalayas are divided into three parallel ranges. Name them from north to south.
Back: Great Himalaya (Himadri) → Lesser Himalaya (Himachal) → Outer Himalaya (Shiwalik). Mnemonic: "Great Lesser Shiwalik" — north to south, highest to lowest.

Card 4
Front: What is the mnemonic to remember the order of Himalayan ranges from north to south?
Back: "Himadri–Himachal–Shiwalik" or the trick "G-L-S" (Great, Lesser, Shiwalik). Remember: altitude decreases as you move south.

Card 5
Front: Examiner's trap: "The Deccan Plateau slopes from east to west." True or false?
Back: False. The Deccan Plateau slopes from west to east — that's why major rivers like Godavari, Krishna, and Kaveri flow eastward into the Bay of Bengal. Examiners reverse the direction to trap you.

Card 6
Front: Which is the largest tributary of the Ganga by volume of water, and which tributary joins the Ganga at Prayagraj?
Back: The Ghaghara (Karnali) carries the largest volume of water among the Ganga's tributaries. The Yamuna — the longest tributary — joins the Ganga at Prayagraj (Triveni Sangam). Don't confuse "longest" with "largest by volume".

Card 7
Front: Which pass connects Srinagar with Leh, and which pass connects Manali with Leh?
Back: Zoji La connects Srinagar–Leh; Rohtang Pass connects Manali–Leh. Examiners often swap these two in options.

Card 8
Front: What are "Duns" and name two famous examples.
Back: Longitudinal valleys lying between the Lesser Himalaya and the Shiwaliks. Examples: Dehradun, Kotli Dun, Patli Dun.

Card 9
Front: Which is the oldest landmass of India, and which is the youngest?
Back: The Peninsular Plateau (part of Gondwanaland) is the oldest; the Himalayan mountains and the Northern Plains are the youngest formations.

Card 10
Front: Which river is known as the "Sorrow of Bengal" and which as the "Sorrow of Bihar"?
Back: Damodar — Sorrow of Bengal; Kosi — Sorrow of Bihar. (Kosi is notorious for frequent course changes causing floods in Bihar.)

Card 11
Front: Examiner's trap: "The Western Ghats are higher than the Eastern Ghats and form a continuous chain." True or false?
Back: True. Western Ghats are higher (Anamudi, 2,695 m) and continuous; Eastern Ghats are lower and discontinuous, cut by rivers like Godavari and Krishna. The trap version swaps the two.

Card 12
Front: What is the highest peak of the Western Ghats and of the Eastern Ghats?
Back: Western Ghats: Anamudi (2,695 m, Kerala). Eastern Ghats: Jindhagada (Arma Konda, ~1,690 m, Andhra Pradesh). Don't confuse Anamudi with Doddabetta (Nilgiri Hills).

Card 13
Front: Which is the only river in India that crosses the Tropic of Cancer twice?
Back: The Mahi. It originates in Madhya Pradesh, flows into Rajasthan, re-enters Gujarat, and drains into the Gulf of Khambhat.

Card 14
Front: What is the mnemonic to remember the east-flowing peninsular rivers from north to south?
Back: "M-G-K-K" — Mahanadi, Godavari, Krishna, Kaveri. Trick: "My Great King Kaveri" flows north to south into the Bay of Bengal.

Card 15
Front: Between which two hill ranges does the Narmada flow?
Back: Between the Vindhya range (north) and the Satpura range (south), through a rift valley, emptying into the Gulf of Khambhat.

Card 16
Front: Which is the largest freshwater lake in India, and how is it different from Chilika?
Back: Wular Lake (Jammu & Kashmir) is the largest freshwater lake; Chilika (Odisha) is the largest brackish-water lagoon. Examiners swap "freshwater" and "saltwater" between them.

Card 17
Front: What are the three divisions of the Northern Plains from west to east (by river systems)?
Back: Punjab Plains (Indus tributaries), Ganga Plains, and Brahmaputra Plains. Also classified as Bhabar, Terai, Bhangar, and Khadar by deposition zones.

Card 18
Front: What is the difference between Bhangar and Khadar?
Back: Bhangar = older alluvium, higher terraces, less fertile, contains kankar nodules. Khadar = newer alluvium, floodplains, renewed by annual floods, more fertile.

Card 19
Front: Which Indian island group is of coral origin and which is of volcanic origin?
Back: Lakshadweep — coral (atoll) origin; Andaman & Nicobar — volcanic/tectonic origin (Barren Island has India's only active volcano).

Card 20
Front: Where do the Eastern and Western Ghats meet, and which peak is located there?
Back: At the Nilgiri Hills (Tamil Nadu). Doddabetta (2,637 m) is the highest peak of the Nilgiris — often confused with Anamudi, which is in the Western Ghats proper.
