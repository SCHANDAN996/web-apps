# Physical Geography of India

## 🌟 The Hook: A Mountain That Is Still Growing

Every year, Mount Everest grows by roughly 4 millimetres. Why? Because the Indian tectonic plate is still pushing into the Eurasian plate — the same collision that began about 50 million years ago and lifted the Himalayas out of an ancient sea called the **Tethys Sea**. Fossils of sea creatures are still found in Himalayan rocks. The land you will study in this chapter is not dead stone — it is alive, moving, and examiners love asking about it.

## 1. India's Location and Size — The Frame of the Map

- India lies entirely in the **Northern Hemisphere**, between **8°4′ N and 37°6′ N** latitude and **68°7′ E and 97°25′ E** longitude.
- The **Tropic of Cancer (23°30′ N)** passes through **8 states**: Gujarat, Rajasthan, Madhya Pradesh, Chhattisgarh, Jharkhand, West Bengal, Tripura, and Mizoram.
- India is the **7th largest country** in the world by area (about 32.87 lakh sq km) — roughly **2.4% of the world's land area**.
- The **82°30′ E longitude** (passing near Mirzapur, Uttar Pradesh) is India's Standard Meridian. Indian Standard Time (IST) is **5 hours 30 minutes ahead of GMT**.
- North–South extent: **3,214 km**; East–West extent: **2,933 km**.
- Land frontier: about **15,200 km**; coastline (mainland + islands): about **7,516.6 km** (mainland alone: 6,100 km).

## 2. The Himalayas — The Young Fold Mountains

The Himalayas are **young fold mountains**, formed by the collision of the **Indian Plate** with the **Eurasian Plate**. They stretch about **2,400 km** from the Indus gorge in the west to the Brahmaputra gorge in the east.

### The Three Parallel Ranges (North to South)

| Range | Also Called | Average Height | Key Features |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Great Himalaya (Himadri)** | Inner Himalaya | ~6,000 m | Highest peaks: Everest (Nepal), Kanchenjunga, Nanda Devi; permanently snow-covered; passes like Zoji La, Shipki La |
| **Middle/Lesser Himalaya (Himachal)** | Lower Himalaya | 3,700–4,500 m | Pir Panjal, Dhauladhar, Mahabharat ranges; hill stations: Shimla, Mussoorie, Nainital; Kashmir and Kullu valleys |
| **Outer Himalaya (Shiwalik)** | Sub-Himalaya | 900–1,100 m | Youngest range; made of unconsolidated sediments; **duns** (valleys) like Dehradun, Kotli, Patli |

Between the Shiwaliks and the Lesser Himalaya lie the flat-floored valleys called **duns** — a favourite one-liner question.

### Regional Divisions of the Himalayas (West to East)

1. **Punjab/Kashmir Himalaya** — between Indus and Satluj
2. **Kumaon Himalaya** — between Satluj and Kali
3. **Nepal Himalaya** — between Kali and Teesta
4. **Assam Himalaya** — between Teesta and Dihang (Brahmaputra)

### Highest Peaks You Must Know

- **Kanchenjunga (8,586 m)** — highest peak of India (in Sikkim).
- **Nanda Devi (7,816 m)** — highest peak entirely within India (Uttarakhand).
- **K2 / Godwin-Austen (8,611 m)** — lies in the **Karakoram** range (PoK region), NOT in the Himalaya proper. This is a classic trap!
- **Mount Everest (8,849 m)** — in Nepal, on the Nepal–China border.

🧠 **Active Recall:** Which range contains the hill stations? What is a "dun"? Is K2 a Himalayan peak?

### 📝 Mnemonic 1 — Ranges from North to South
**"Great Men Sing"** → **G**reat Himalaya → **M**iddle Himalaya → **S**hiwalik

## 3. The Northern Plains — The Gift of the Rivers

- Formed by the alluvium deposited by the **Indus, Ganga, and Brahmaputra** river systems — about **7 lakh sq km** of some of the most fertile land on Earth.
- About **2,400 km long** and **240–320 km wide**.

### Divisions of the Northern Plains

1. **Bhabar** — narrow belt (8–16 km) of pebbles at the foot of the Shiwaliks; streams disappear here.
2. **Terai** — marshy, wet belt south of Bhabar where streams re-emerge; dense forests and rich wildlife.
3. **Bhangar** — older alluvium, slightly elevated, contains **kankar** (calcareous) deposits.
4. **Khadar** — newer alluvium, renewed by floods every year; the most fertile zone.

### Regional Names

- **Punjab Plains** (Indus system — doabs like Bist Doab, Bari Doab)
- **Ganga Plains** (UP, Bihar, West Bengal)
- **Brahmaputra Plains** (Assam) — contains **Majuli**, one of the world's largest river islands.

🧠 **Active Recall:** Which is more fertile — Bhangar or Khadar? Where do streams disappear, and where do they reappear?

### 📝 Mnemonic 2 — Plains from Mountain to River
**"Big Tigers Bring Kill"** → **B**habar → **T**erai → **B**hangar → **K**hadar

## 4. The Peninsular Plateau — India's Oldest Landmass

The Peninsular Plateau is part of the ancient **Gondwanaland** — one of the oldest and most stable landmasses on Earth. It is composed mainly of **igneous and metamorphic rocks**.

### Major Divisions

| Division | Location / Key Facts |
|---|---|
| **Central Highlands** | North of the Narmada; includes Malwa Plateau, Bundelkhand, Baghelkhand, Chotanagpur Plateau; rivers flow towards the **north-east** (Chambal, Betwa, Ken, Son) |
| **Deccan Plateau** | South of the Narmada; triangular; bounded by the **Satpura–Mahadeo–Maikal** range in the north, Western Ghats in the west, Eastern Ghats in the east; slopes gently **eastward** — so most rivers (Godavari, Krishna, Kaveri) flow east into the Bay of Bengal |
| **Meghalaya Plateau** | An extension of the Peninsular block, separated by the **Rajmahal–Garo gap** (fault); includes Garo, Khasi, Jaintia hills |
| **Deccan Trap** | Formed by volcanic lava flows; black **regur soil** — ideal for cotton |

### The Ghats

- **Western Ghats (Sahyadri)**: continuous, higher (average 900–1,600 m). Highest peak: **Anamudi (2,695 m)** in Kerala — the highest peak of South India. Other peaks: Doddabetta (Nilgiris).
- **Eastern Ghats**: discontinuous, lower, cut by rivers. Highest peak: **Jindhagada (1,690 m)** in Andhra Pradesh (often confused with Mahendragiri in Odisha).
- The Western and Eastern Ghats meet at the **Nilgiri Hills**.

### Key Plateau Facts

- **Narmada** and **Tapi** are the major **west-flowing** rivers — they flow through **rift valleys** (fault-created valleys), which is why they do NOT form deltas; they form **estuaries**.
- **Chotanagpur Plateau** is India's mineral heartland (Jharkhand).
- Highest peak of the Aravalli: **Guru Shikhar (1,722 m)** on Mount Abu, Rajasthan. The Aravallis are among the **oldest fold mountains** in the world — now highly eroded (residual mountains).

🧠 **Active Recall:** Why do the Narmada and Tapi form estuaries instead of deltas? Which is older — the Himalaya or the Aravalli?

### 📝 Mnemonic 3 — West-flowing rivers
**"NaTa"** → **Na**rmada, **Ta**pi (the two big west-flowers; remember Sabarmati and Mahi as smaller ones)

## 5. The Indian Desert, Coastal Plains, and Islands

### The Thar Desert (Marusthali)

- Lies in western Rajasthan; extends into Pakistan.
- Receives less than 150 mm rainfall; **Luni** is the only significant river — it is **inland drainage** (does not reach the sea; ends in the Rann of Kutch).
- Features: sand dunes (barchans), saline lakes like **Sambhar Lake** (Rajasthan) — India's largest inland saltwater lake.

### Coastal Plains

| | Western Coastal Plain | Eastern Coastal Plain |
|---|---|---|
| Width | Narrow | Wide |
| Sub-divisions | Konkan (Maharashtra–Goa), Kannad (Karnataka), Malabar (Kerala) | Utkal (Odisha), Andhra Plain, Coromandel (Tamil Nadu) |
| Rivers | Short, swift, estuary-forming | Long, delta-forming |
| Key feature | Backwaters (**kayals**) of Kerala | Deltas of Mahanadi, Godavari, Krishna, Kaveri; **Chilika Lake** (Odisha) — largest coastal lagoon/brackish water lake of India |

### The Islands

- **Andaman & Nicobar Islands**: in the Bay of Bengal; an elevated portion of submarine mountains. **North Andaman's Saddle Peak (737 m)** is the highest point. India's only active volcano: **Barren Island**. The **Ten Degree Channel** separates the Andamans from the Nicobars.
- **Lakshadweep Islands**: in the Arabian Sea; **coral** origin (atolls). The **Eight Degree Channel** separates Minicoy from the Maldives; the **Nine Degree Channel** separates Minicoy from the main Lakshadweep group. Administrative capital: **Kavaratti**.

🧠 **Active Recall:** Which channel separates Andaman from Nicobar? What is the origin of Lakshadweep — volcanic, coral, or tectonic?

## ⚠️ Caution: Examiner's Trap

**Trap 1 — "Almost-True Statement" (लगभग सही कथन):**
"K2 is the highest peak of the Himalayas in India." — 90% believable, but **wrong on two counts**: K2 lies in the **Karakoram** range (not the Himalaya), and India's highest Himalayan peak is **Kanchenjunga**. Examiners swap "Karakoram" and "Himalaya" constantly.

**Trap 2 — Reverse Causation (विपरीत कार्य-कारण):**
"The Narmada flows west because the Deccan Plateau slopes westward." — False. The Deccan slopes **eastward**; the Narmada flows west because it occupies a **rift valley** (a fault trough), not because of the general slope. Cause and effect are deliberately reversed.

**Trap 3 — Chronological Confusion (कालानुक्रमिक भ्रम):**
"The Aravallis are younger than the Himalayas." — The reverse is true. The Aravallis are among the **oldest** fold mountains (now worn down to residual hills); the Himalayas are the **youngest** fold mountains. Watch for "older/younger" swaps.

**Trap 4 — Negative Phrasing (नकारात्मक वाक्यांश):**
"Which of the following is NOT a part of the Peninsular Plateau?" — options may include the Meghalaya Plateau (which IS part of it, separated by the Rajmahal–Garo gap) and the Punjab Plains (which is NOT). The hidden "NOT" changes everything — underline it before answering.
