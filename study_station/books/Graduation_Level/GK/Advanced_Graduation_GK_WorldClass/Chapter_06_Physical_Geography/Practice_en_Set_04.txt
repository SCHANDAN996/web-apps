Questions 76–100: Medium

76. Which of the following statements about the Western Ghats is correct?
(a) They are lower than the Eastern Ghats and are highly fragmented (b) They are higher than the Eastern Ghats and form a continuous chain (c) They are a series of residual hills separated by river valleys (d) They merge with the Himalayas in the north
Answer: (b)
Solution: The tempting wrong choice describes the Eastern Ghats, which are indeed lower and fragmented by rivers like the Godavari and Krishna. The Western Ghats (Sahyadri) are higher, continuous, and run parallel to the western coast, so the answer is that they are higher than the Eastern Ghats and form a continuous chain.
Source: NCERT Class 11 India Physical Environment

77. The highest peak of the Eastern Ghats is
(a) Anaimudi (b) Doddabetta (c) Mahendragiri (d) Kalsubai
Answer: (c)
Solution: Anaimudi and Doddabetta are traps from the Western Ghats (Anaimudi is the highest peak of South India; Doddabetta is in the Nilgiris), and Kalsubai is the highest peak of Maharashtra in the Western Ghats. Mahendragiri (about 1,501 m) in Odisha is the highest peak of the Eastern Ghats, so the answer is Mahendragiri.
Source: NCERT Class 11 India Physical Environment

78. The 'Duns' are
(a) Longitudinal valleys between the Lesser Himalaya and the Shiwaliks (b) Deep gorges in the Eastern Himalayas (c) Glacial lakes of the Trans-Himalaya (d) Coastal plains of Kerala
Answer: (a)
Solution: Duns like Dehradun, Kotli Dun and Patli Dun are longitudinal valleys formed between the Lesser Himalaya and the Shiwalik range; they are not gorges, lakes or coastal features, so the answer is longitudinal valleys between the Lesser Himalaya and the Shiwaliks.
Source: NCERT Class 11 India Physical Environment

79. Which river is known as the 'Sorrow of Bengal'?
(a) Damodar (b) Hooghly (c) Kosi (d) Teesta
Answer: (a)
Solution: Kosi is a trap because it is called the 'Sorrow of Bihar', not Bengal, due to its frequent course changes. The Damodar, once notorious for devastating floods in West Bengal, is called the 'Sorrow of Bengal', so the answer is Damodar.
Source: NCERT Class 11 India Physical Environment

80. Consider the following statements about the Deccan Plateau:
1. It is bounded by the Satpura range in the north.
2. It slopes from west to east.
3. Most of its rivers flow into the Bay of Bengal.
Which of the statements given above are correct?
(a) 1 and 2 only (b) 1, 2 and 3 (c) 1 and 3 only (d) 2 and 3 only
Answer: (b)
Solution: All three statements are correct: the Satpura forms the northern boundary, the general slope is west to east (shown by the east-flowing Mahanadi, Godavari, Krishna and Kaveri), and most rivers drain into the Bay of Bengal, so the answer is 1, 2 and 3.
Source: NCERT Class 11 India Physical Environment

81. The Barren Island, India's only active volcano, is located in
(a) Lakshadweep (b) Arabian Sea off Gujarat (c) Gulf of Mannar (d) Andaman Sea
Answer: (d)
Solution: Lakshadweep is a trap because those islands are coral in origin, not volcanic. Barren Island lies in the Andaman Sea, east of the Andaman group, and is India's only active volcano, so the answer is Andaman Sea.
Source: NCERT Class 11 India Physical Environment

82. Which of the following rivers does NOT form a delta?
(a) Mahanadi (b) Godavari (c) Narmada (d) Krishna
Answer: (c)
Solution: The Mahanadi, Godavari and Krishna are east-flowing rivers that build large deltas on the Bay of Bengal coast. The Narmada flows west through a rift valley and forms an estuary instead of a delta, so the answer is Narmada.
Source: NCERT Class 11 India Physical Environment

83. The 'Malwa Plateau' is primarily located in which state?
(a) Maharashtra (b) Madhya Pradesh (c) Rajasthan (d) Gujarat
Answer: (b)
Solution: The Malwa Plateau, of volcanic origin with black regur soil, lies mainly in western Madhya Pradesh (Indore, Ujjain region), though small parts extend into Rajasthan, so the answer is Madhya Pradesh.
Source: NCERT Class 11 India Physical Environment

84. Arrange the following Himalayan peaks from west to east:
1. Nanga Parbat 2. Nanda Devi 3. Kanchenjunga 4. Namcha Barwa
(a) 1-2-3-4 (b) 2-1-3-4 (c) 1-3-2-4 (d) 2-3-1-4
Answer: (a)
Solution: This is a chronological/spatial trap: Nanga Parbat is in the westernmost (Kashmir) Himalaya, Nanda Devi in Uttarakhand, Kanchenjunga on the Nepal–Sikkim border, and Namcha Barwa at the eastern syntaxial bend in Arunachal Pradesh, so the answer is 1-2-3-4.
Source: NCERT Class 11 India Physical Environment

85. The Shiwalik range is known in Nepal as the
(a) Mahabharat Lekh (b) Dhauladhar (c) Churia range (d) Pir Panjal
Answer: (c)
Solution: Dhauladhar and Pir Panjal are traps because they belong to the Lesser/Great Himalayan system of Himachal and Jammu & Kashmir, not the Shiwaliks. In Nepal the Shiwaliks are called the Churia range, so the answer is Churia range.
Source: NCERT Class 11 India Physical Environment

86. Which plateau is known as the 'Mineral Heartland of India'?
(a) Deccan Plateau (b) Malwa Plateau (c) Chota Nagpur Plateau (d) Bundelkhand Plateau
Answer: (c)
Solution: The Chota Nagpur Plateau (Jharkhand and adjoining areas) holds rich deposits of coal, iron ore, mica, bauxite and copper, earning it the title of India's mineral heartland, so the answer is Chota Nagpur Plateau.
Source: NCERT Class 11 India Physical Environment

87. The river that flows between the Vindhya and Satpura ranges is
(a) Tapi (b) Narmada (c) Chambal (d) Betwa
Answer: (b)
Solution: Tapi is a trap because it flows south of the Satpura, between the Satpura and the Ajanta range. The Narmada flows westward in a rift valley between the Vindhyas (north) and the Satpuras (south), so the answer is Narmada.
Source: NCERT Class 11 India Physical Environment

88. Consider the following statements about the Northern Plains:
1. They are formed by the alluvial deposits of the Indus, Ganga and Brahmaputra systems.
2. The 'Bhabar' belt lies south of the 'Terai' belt.
3. 'Bhangar' is older alluvium lying above the flood level.
Which of the statements given above are correct?
(a) 1 and 3 only (b) 1 and 2 only (c) 2 and 3 only (d) 1, 2 and 3
Answer: (a)
Solution: Statement 2 is the trap: the Bhabar (pebble-studded belt where streams disappear) lies at the foot of the Shiwaliks, north of the marshy Terai belt, not south of it. Statements 1 and 3 are correct, so the answer is 1 and 3 only.
Source: NCERT Class 11 India Physical Environment

89. The 'Karewa' deposits of Kashmir are famous for the cultivation of
(a) Cardamom (b) Tea (c) Coffee (d) Saffron
Answer: (d)
Solution: Karewas are lacustrine (lake) deposits of the Kashmir Valley, and their soil is ideal for saffron (kesar) cultivation, for which the region is famous, so the answer is Saffron.
Source: NCERT Class 11 India Physical Environment

90. Which of the following is the largest brackish water lagoon in India?
(a) Pulicat Lake (b) Chilika Lake (c) Vembanad Lake (d) Wular Lake
Answer: (b)
Solution: Wular is a trap because it is a freshwater lake, not a lagoon, and Vembanad is the longest lake but not the largest lagoon. Chilika Lake in Odisha, at the mouth of the Daya river, is India's largest brackish water lagoon, so the answer is Chilika Lake.
Source: NCERT Class 11 India Physical Environment

91. The 'Duar' region (Terai belt) of the Eastern Himalaya is mainly associated with which states?
(a) Punjab and Haryana (b) Bihar and Uttar Pradesh (c) Uttarakhand and Himachal (d) West Bengal and Assam
Answer: (d)
Solution: The Duars are the floodplains and foothill belt of the Eastern Himalaya, stretching across northern West Bengal and Assam, famous for tea gardens, so the answer is West Bengal and Assam.

92. Which pass connects Srinagar with Leh?
(a) Zoji La (b) Banihal (c) Shipki La (d) Nathu La
Answer: (a)
Solution: Banihal is a trap because it connects Jammu with Srinagar across the Pir Panjal, not Srinagar with Leh. Zoji La, in the Zaskar range, links Srinagar to Leh (Ladakh), so the answer is Zoji La.
Source: NCERT Class 11 India Physical Environment

93. The Godavari is often called the 'Dakshin Ganga' because
(a) It is the longest river of India (b) It is the holiest river of South India (c) It originates from the Gangotri glacier (d) It is the longest river of Peninsular India with a large basin
Answer: (d)
Solution: The Ganga remains India's longest river, so the first option is a trap. The Godavari (about 1,465 km) is the longest river of Peninsular India with the largest peninsular basin, hence the name 'Dakshin Ganga' (Ganga of the South), so the answer is that it is the longest river of Peninsular India with a large basin.
Source: NCERT Class 11 India Physical Environment

94. Consider the following statements about the Thar Desert:
1. It is bounded by the Aravalli range in the east.
2. The Luni is the only major river of the region.
3. It receives rainfall mainly from the northeast monsoon.
Which of the statements given above are correct?
(a) 1 and 3 only (b) 2 and 3 only (c) 1 and 2 only (d) 1, 2 and 3
Answer: (c)
Solution: Statement 3 is the trap: the Thar receives scanty rain from the southwest monsoon (the Aravallis lie parallel to the winds, giving little orographic rain), not the northeast monsoon. Statements 1 and 2 are correct, so the answer is 1 and 2 only.
Source: NCERT Class 11 India Physical Environment

95. The 'Palghat Gap' connects
(a) Tamil Nadu and Andhra Pradesh (b) Karnataka and Kerala (c) Kerala and Tamil Nadu (d) Maharashtra and Karnataka
Answer: (c)
Solution: The Palghat (Palakkad) Gap is a major break in the Western Ghats through which road and rail link Palakkad (Kerala) with Coimbatore (Tamil Nadu), so the answer is Kerala and Tamil Nadu.

96. Which of the following rivers originates from the Amarkantak plateau?
(a) Mahanadi (b) Kaveri (c) Narmada (d) Tapi
Answer: (c)
Solution: Kaveri (Talakaveri, Brahmagiri) and Tapi (Multai, Betul) are traps with different origins. The Narmada rises from the Amarkantak plateau in Madhya Pradesh (the Son also rises nearby), so the answer is Narmada.
Source: NCERT Class 11 India Physical Environment

97. The 'Meghalaya Plateau' is geologically a part of
(a) The Himalayan mountain system (b) The Peninsular Plateau separated by the Malda gap (c) The Indo-Gangetic plain (d) The Deccan lava plateau
Answer: (b)
Solution: The Meghalaya Plateau (Garo–Khasi–Jaintia hills) is an eastward extension of the Peninsular block, detached from the Chota Nagpur Plateau by the Malda (Rajmahal) gap through which the Ganga flows, so the answer is the Peninsular Plateau separated by the Malda gap.
Source: NCERT Class 11 India Physical Environment

98. Which of the following statements about the Aravalli range is correct?
(a) It is a young fold mountain still rising (b) It is one of the oldest fold mountains of the world, now highly eroded (c) Its highest peak is Anaimudi (d) It runs parallel to the east coast of India
Answer: (b)
Solution: The 'young fold mountain' option is a trap describing the Himalayas, and Anaimudi belongs to the Western Ghats. The Aravallis are among the world's oldest fold mountains, now worn down to residual hills, with Guru Shikhar (Mount Abu) as the highest peak, so the answer is that it is one of the oldest fold mountains of the world, now highly eroded.
Source: NCERT Class 11 India Physical Environment

99. The 'Khadar' and 'Bhangar' refer to
(a) Types of black soil (b) Types of desert dunes (c) Newer and older alluvium of the plains (d) Himalayan vegetation zones
Answer: (c)
Solution: Khadar is the newer alluvium of floodplains, renewed almost every year by floods, while Bhangar is the older alluvium on higher terraces above flood level, so the answer is newer and older alluvium of the plains.
Source: NCERT Class 11 India Physical Environment

100. Which river forms the boundary between the Central Highlands and the Deccan Plateau?
(a) Chambal (b) Betwa (c) Son (d) Narmada
Answer: (d)
Solution: The Narmada, flowing through its rift valley, is conventionally taken as the dividing line between the Central Highlands (to the north) and the Deccan Plateau (to the south), so the answer is Narmada.
Source: NCERT Class 11 India Physical Environment
