101. Which of the following is the correct sequence of Himalayan ranges from north to south?
(a) Lesser Himalaya → Greater Himalaya → Shiwalik (b) Greater Himalaya → Shiwalik → Lesser Himalaya (c) Shiwalik → Greater Himalaya → Lesser Himalaya (d) Greater Himalaya → Lesser Himalaya → Shiwalik
Answer: (d)
Solution: The tempting wrong option reverses the order by placing the Lesser Himalaya first; the Shiwalik is the southernmost and youngest range, not the middle one. From north to south the order is Greater Himalaya (Himadri), Lesser Himalaya (Himachal), then Shiwalik, so the answer is Greater Himalaya → Lesser Himalaya → Shiwalik.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Geography (India Physical Environment)

102. The Deccan Plateau is bounded on the west by which mountain range?
(a) Eastern Ghats (b) Aravalli Range (c) Satpura Range (d) Western Ghats
Answer: (d)
Solution: The Eastern Ghats bound the plateau on the east, which is the common confusion; the Aravallis lie far to the northwest and the Satpuras to the north. The Deccan Plateau rises steeply along its western edge as the Western Ghats, so the answer is Western Ghats.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Geography (India Physical Environment)

103. Which river flows through a rift valley between the Vindhya and Satpura ranges?
(a) Tapi (b) Godavari (c) Narmada (d) Mahanadi
Answer: (c)
Solution: The Tapi also flows in a rift valley, which tempts many candidates, but it lies south of the Satpura range, not between the Vindhyas and Satpuras. The Narmada flows westward through the rift valley bounded by the Vindhyas to the north and the Satpuras to the south, so the answer is Narmada.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Geography (India Physical Environment)

104. The highest peak of the Aravalli Range is:
(a) Guru Shikhar (b) Anaimudi (c) Dhupgarh (d) Doddabetta
Answer: (a)
Solution: Dhupgarh is the highest peak of the Satpura range and Anaimudi of the Western Ghats — both are frequent traps because they are also famous peaks. Guru Shikhar (about 1,722 m) on Mount Abu is the highest point of the Aravallis, so the answer is Guru Shikhar.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Geography (India Physical Environment)

105. Which of the following rivers does NOT form a delta at its mouth?
(a) Mahanadi (b) Narmada (c) Krishna (d) Kaveri
Answer: (b)
Solution: The Mahanadi, Krishna and Kaveri are east-flowing rivers that deposit sediment and build deltas on the Bay of Bengal coast. The Narmada is a west-flowing river that empties into the Gulf of Khambhat through an estuary, so the answer is Narmada.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Geography (India Physical Environment)

106. The 'Duns' are:
(a) Longitudinal valleys between the Lesser Himalaya and the Shiwaliks (b) Glacial lakes of Ladakh (c) Sand dunes of the Thar Desert (d) Deep gorges of the Brahmaputra
Answer: (a)
Solution: The word sounds like 'dunes', which misleads candidates toward the Thar option, but Duns have nothing to do with sand. Duns such as Dehradun, Kotli Dun and Patli Dun are longitudinal valleys lying between the Lesser Himalaya and the Shiwalik ranges, so the answer is longitudinal valleys between the Lesser Himalaya and the Shiwaliks.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Geography (India Physical Environment)

107. Which soil type is formed by the weathering of basaltic lava rocks and is ideal for cotton cultivation?
(a) Alluvial soil (b) Laterite soil (c) Black soil (d) Red soil
Answer: (c)
Solution: Red soil also forms from rock weathering, which makes it tempting, but it develops on crystalline rocks and is poor for cotton compared to regur soil. Black (regur) soil forms from Deccan basalt and retains moisture well, making it ideal for cotton, so the answer is Black soil.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Geography

108. The Brahmaputra is known as the 'Tsangpo' in:
(a) India (b) Bangladesh (c) Tibet (d) Bhutan
Answer: (c)
Solution: In Bangladesh the river is called the Jamuna, which is the classic trap in this question. In Tibet the Brahmaputra flows eastward under the name Tsangpo before taking a great bend to enter India, so the answer is Tibet.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Geography (India Physical Environment)

109. Which pass connects Srinagar with Leh?
(a) Zoji La (b) Shipki La (c) Nathu La (d) Banihal
Answer: (a)
Solution: Banihal is tempting because it also lies in Jammu and Kashmir, but it connects the Jammu region with the Kashmir Valley, not Srinagar with Leh. Zoji La is the pass on the Srinagar–Leh route through the Greater Himalaya, so the answer is Zoji La.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Geography (India Physical Environment)

110. The largest brackish water lagoon in India, Chilika Lake, is located in:
(a) Andhra Pradesh (b) West Bengal (c) Kerala (d) Odisha
Answer: (d)
Solution: Kerala is tempting because of its famous backwaters, but those are a chain of lagoons, not the single largest one. Chilika Lake lies on the Odisha coast at the mouth of the Daya river, so the answer is Odisha.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Geography (India Physical Environment)

111. Which of the following is the correct chronological order of the formation of India's physiographic divisions, from oldest to youngest?
(a) Himalayas → Peninsular Plateau → Indo-Gangetic Plain (b) Peninsular Plateau → Himalayas → Indo-Gangetic Plain (c) Indo-Gangetic Plain → Peninsular Plateau → Himalayas (d) Peninsular Plateau → Indo-Gangetic Plain → Himalayas
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap option places the Himalayas first, but the Himalayas are young fold mountains formed by the collision of the Indian and Eurasian plates, while the Peninsular block is one of the oldest landmasses on Earth. The plain formed last from sediments deposited by rivers after the Himalayas rose, so the answer is Peninsular Plateau → Himalayas → Indo-Gangetic Plain.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Geography (India Physical Environment)

112. The river known as the 'Sorrow of Bengal' is:
(a) Hooghly (b) Damodar (c) Kosi (d) Teesta
Answer: (b)
Solution: The Kosi is the classic trap here — it is called the 'Sorrow of Bihar', not Bengal, because of its frequent course changes and floods. The Damodar, notorious for devastating floods in the Bengal basin, is called the 'Sorrow of Bengal', so the answer is Damodar.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Geography (India Physical Environment)

113. Which of the following pairs is correctly matched?
(a) Mahendragiri — Western Ghats (b) Anaimudi — Eastern Ghats (c) Doddabetta — Nilgiri Hills (d) Kanchenjunga — Lesser Himalaya
Answer: (c)
Solution: Anaimudi is the highest peak of the Western Ghats, not the Eastern Ghats, and Mahendragiri lies in the Eastern Ghats, not the Western Ghats — both are deliberate swaps. Doddabetta is the highest peak of the Nilgiri Hills, so the answer is Doddabetta — Nilgiri Hills.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Geography (India Physical Environment)

114. The 'Bhabar' belt of the northern plains is characterised by:
(a) Fine alluvium and intensive agriculture (b) Older alluvium with calcareous deposits (c) Marshy and swampy land (d) Pebbles and boulders where streams disappear underground
Answer: (d)
Solution: The marshy description fits the Terai belt, which lies just south of the Bhabar and is the usual confusion. In the Bhabar belt, rivers descending from the mountains deposit pebbles and boulders, and streams vanish into the porous deposits, so the answer is pebbles and boulders where streams disappear underground.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Geography (India Physical Environment)

115. Which river originates from the Amarkantak plateau in Madhya Pradesh?
(a) Mahanadi (b) Narmada (c) Tapi (d) Godavari
Answer: (b)
Solution: The Mahanadi is the tempting wrong option because it also rises in central India, but it originates near Sihawa in the highlands of Chhattisgarh, not at Amarkantak; the Tapi rises near Multai in Betul district. The Narmada rises from the Amarkantak plateau in Anuppur district of Madhya Pradesh, so the answer is Narmada.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Geography (India Physical Environment)

116. Laterite soil is most suitable for which of the following after proper manuring?
(a) Tea, coffee and cashew (b) Wheat (c) Cotton (d) Sugarcane
Answer: (a)
Solution: Cotton is the trap because it is strongly associated with black soil, not laterite soil which is leached and acidic. Laterite soils of high-rainfall areas, when manured, support plantation crops like tea, coffee, rubber and cashew, so the answer is tea, coffee and cashew.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Geography

117. The Gulf of Mannar and Palk Strait separate India from:
(a) Sri Lanka (b) Maldives (c) Myanmar (d) Indonesia
Answer: (a)
Solution: The Maldives is tempting because it is also an island neighbour, but it is separated from India by the Eight Degree Channel in the Arabian Sea. The Palk Strait and Gulf of Mannar lie between Tamil Nadu and Sri Lanka, so the answer is Sri Lanka.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Geography (Contemporary India)

118. Which of the following is a west-flowing river of Peninsular India?
(a) Subarnarekha (b) Godavari (c) Pennar (d) Sabarmati
Answer: (d)
Solution: The Godavari, Pennar and Subarnarekha all flow eastward into the Bay of Bengal, which is the general pattern of Peninsular drainage and the basis of the trap. The Sabarmati rises in the Aravallis and flows southwest into the Gulf of Khambhat, so the answer is Sabarmati.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Geography (India Physical Environment)

119. The 'Karewas' of Kashmir are famous for the cultivation of:
(a) Tea (b) Apple only (c) Saffron (d) Spices like cardamom
Answer: (c)
Solution: Apple orchards are common in Kashmir, which makes that option tempting, but Karewa deposits are specifically famed for one high-value crop. Karewas are lacustrine deposits of the Kashmir Valley on which saffron (zafran) is cultivated, so the answer is saffron.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Geography (India Physical Environment)

120. Which of the following statements about the Eastern Ghats is correct?
(a) They are continuous and higher than the Western Ghats (b) They run parallel to the Arabian Sea coast (c) They are discontinuous and dissected by rivers flowing into the Bay of Bengal (d) They form the western edge of the Deccan Plateau
Answer: (c)
Solution: The first option reverses reality — it is the Western Ghats that are continuous and higher, which is the examiner's favourite swap. The Eastern Ghats are broken into discontinuous blocks by rivers like the Godavari, Krishna and Kaveri flowing to the Bay of Bengal, so the answer is that they are discontinuous and dissected by rivers.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Geography (India Physical Environment)

121. Consider the following statements:
1. The Peninsular Plateau is composed of old crystalline, igneous and metamorphic rocks.
2. The Himalayas are young fold mountains formed by the convergence of the Indo-Australian and Eurasian plates.
3. The Indo-Gangetic Plain is older than the Peninsular Plateau.
Which of the statements given above is/are correct?
(a) 1 and 2 only (b) 2 and 3 only (c) 1 and 3 only (d) 1, 2 and 3
Answer: (a)
Solution: Statement 3 is the trap — it sounds plausible because the plain looks ancient, but it is geologically the youngest feature, built of recent alluvium deposited after the Himalayas rose. Statements 1 and 2 correctly describe the ancient Peninsular block and the young fold mountains, so the answer is 1 and 2 only.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Geography (India Physical Environment)

122. Arrange the following peaks from west to east:
1. Nanga Parbat
2. Nanda Devi
3. Kanchenjunga
4. Namcha Barwa
(a) 2, 1, 3, 4 (b) 1, 2, 3, 4 (c) 1, 3, 2, 4 (d) 2, 3, 1, 4
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap options swap Nanga Parbat and Nanda Devi; Nanga Parbat lies at the western syntaxial bend of the Himalaya in Kashmir, far west of Nanda Devi in Uttarakhand. Moving eastward the order is Nanga Parbat, Nanda Devi, Kanchenjunga (Sikkim–Nepal border) and Namcha Barwa at the eastern bend, so the answer is 1, 2, 3, 4.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Geography (India Physical Environment)

123. Consider the following statements about the Narmada and Tapi rivers:
1. Both flow through rift valleys.
2. Both form large deltas on the west coast.
3. Both flow westward into the Arabian Sea.
Which of the statements given above is/are correct?
(a) 1, 2 and 3 (b) 1 and 3 only (c) 2 and 3 only (d) 1 and 2 only
Answer: (b)
Solution: Statement 2 is the trap — candidates assume all major rivers form deltas, but west-flowing rivers descending steeply to the coast form estuaries, not deltas. Both rivers flow through rift valleys and empty westward into the Arabian Sea, so the answer is 1 and 3 only.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Geography (India Physical Environment)

124. Match the following:
P. Bhangar — 1. Newer alluvium of flood plains
Q. Khadar — 2. Older alluvium of terrace-like features
R. Terai — 3. Marshy belt south of Bhabar
S. Bhabar — 4. Pebble belt at the foot of the Shiwaliks
(a) P-1, Q-2, R-4, S-3 (b) P-1, Q-2, R-3, S-4 (c) P-2, Q-1, R-3, S-4 (d) P-2, Q-1, R-4, S-3
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap options swap Bhangar and Khadar; remember that Khadar is the newer alluvium renewed by annual floods, while Bhangar is the older alluvium lying above the flood level. The Terai is the marshy belt south of the pebbly Bhabar, so the answer is P-2, Q-1, R-3, S-4.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Geography (India Physical Environment)

125. Consider the following statements:
1. The Andaman and Nicobar Islands are believed to be the elevated portions of submarine mountains.
2. Lakshadweep Islands are of coral origin.
3. Barren Island, India's only active volcano, is located in the Lakshadweep group.
Which of the statements given above is/are correct?
(a) 2 and 3 only (b) 1 and 2 only (c) 1 and 3 only (d) 1, 2 and 3
Answer: (b)
Solution: Statement 3 is the trap — Barren Island is real and is India's only active volcano, but it lies in the Andaman group, not Lakshadweep, which is the examiner's deliberate misplacement. The Andaman–Nicobar chain is an extension of submarine mountain ranges and Lakshadweep is built of coral, so the answer is 1 and 2 only.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Geography (India Physical Environment)
