Questions 26–50: Medium

26. Which of the following statements about the Deccan Plateau is correct?
(a) It is bounded by the Satpura Range in the south (b) It slopes gently from west to east (c) It is composed mainly of alluvial deposits (d) Its highest peak is Anamudi
Answer: (b)
Solution: The tempting wrong option claims Anamudi is the plateau's highest peak, but Anamudi (2,695 m) lies in the Anaimalai Hills of the Western Ghats, not on the Deccan Plateau proper. The plateau is bounded by the Satpuras in the north, not south, and is made of ancient crystalline and Deccan trap rocks, not alluvium. Most peninsular rivers flow eastward into the Bay of Bengal, proving the plateau slopes from west to east, so the answer is that it slopes gently from west to east.
Source: NCERT Class 11 India Physical Environment

27. The Barren Island, India's only active volcano, is located in which tectonic setting?
(a) On the mid-oceanic ridge of the Arabian Sea (b) On the continental shelf off the Odisha coast (c) On a transform fault in the Bay of Bengal (d) At the subduction zone of the Indian plate beneath the Burmese plate
Answer: (d)
Solution: The tempting wrong option places it on a mid-oceanic ridge, but Barren Island is not related to sea-floor spreading. It lies in the Andaman Sea where the Indian plate subducts beneath the Burmese microplate, creating a volcanic island arc. This subduction-related volcanism makes it India's only active volcano, so the answer is the subduction zone of the Indian plate beneath the Burmese plate.
Source: NCERT Class 11 India Physical Environment

28. Arrange the following Himalayan ranges from north to south: 1. Himadri 2. Shiwalik 3. Himachal 4. Trans-Himalaya
(a) 4, 1, 3, 2 (b) 1, 4, 3, 2 (c) 4, 3, 1, 2 (d) 1, 3, 2, 4
Answer: (a)
Solution: The tempting wrong option starts with Himadri, a chronological-spatial confusion trap; the Himadri (Greater Himalaya) is not the northernmost. The northernmost is the Trans-Himalaya (Karakoram, Ladakh, Zaskar), followed southward by the Himadri, then the Himachal (Lesser Himalaya), and finally the Shiwaliks, so the answer is 4, 1, 3, 2.

29. Which river is known as the "Sorrow of Bengal"?
(a) Damodar (b) Hooghly (c) Kosi (d) Teesta
Answer: (a)
Solution: The tempting wrong option is Kosi, but Kosi is called the "Sorrow of Bihar", a classic similar-name trap. The Damodar, flowing through Jharkhand and West Bengal, was notorious for devastating floods in the Bengal plains before the Damodar Valley Corporation tamed it, so the answer is Damodar.

30. The Narmada and Tapi rivers flow westward mainly because they flow through:
(a) Structural depressions formed by rift valleys (faults) (b) Glacial valleys carved during the Ice Age (c) Wind gaps created by river capture (d) Coastal plains sloping towards the Arabian Sea
Answer: (a)
Solution: The tempting wrong option suggests a simple westward coastal slope, but the real cause is structural. The Narmada flows between the Vindhyas and Satpuras and the Tapi between the Satpuras and Ajanta range, both through rift valleys formed by faulting during the breakup of Gondwanaland, so the answer is structural depressions formed by rift valleys.
Source: NCERT Class 11 India Physical Environment

31. Which of the following is the correct sequence of soil types from the Himalayan foothills towards the peninsular interior?
(a) Red soil → Black soil → Alluvial soil → Mountain soil (b) Alluvial soil → Black soil → Red soil → Mountain soil (c) Mountain soil → Alluvial soil → Black soil → Red soil (d) Mountain soil → Red soil → Black soil → Alluvial soil
Answer: (c)
Solution: The tempting wrong option reverses the sequence, a spatial-confusion trap. Moving south from the foothills: mountain (forest) soils in the Himalayas, alluvial soils of the northern plains, black (regur) soils of the Deccan trap region, and red soils of the weathered crystalline peninsular interior, so the answer is Mountain soil → Alluvial soil → Black soil → Red soil.

32. The Brahmaputra is known as the "Tsangpo" in:
(a) Nepal (b) Bhutan (c) Tibet (d) Myanmar
Answer: (c)
Solution: The tempting wrong option is Bhutan, but in Bhutan the river is not called Tsangpo; this is a similar-region name trap. The Brahmaputra originates near Lake Mansarovar and flows eastward in Tibet as the Tsangpo before taking a U-turn at Namcha Barwa to enter India, so the answer is Tibet.

33. Which statement about the Western Coastal Plain is correct?
(a) It is a submerged coast with estuaries (b) It is wider than the Eastern Coastal Plain (c) It has large deltas of the Mahanadi and Godavari (d) It extends from Kanyakumari to the Sundarbans
Answer: (a)
Solution: The tempting wrong option mentions Mahanadi and Godavari deltas, but those deltas are on the Eastern Coastal Plain, a classic east-west confusion trap. The Western Coastal Plain is narrow, lies between the Western Ghats and the Arabian Sea, and is a submerged coast featuring estuaries rather than deltas, so the answer is that it is a submerged coast with estuaries.

34. The "Duns" are:
(a) Glacial moraines in the Karakoram (b) Sand dunes of the Thar desert (c) Deep gorges of the peninsular rivers (d) Longitudinal valleys between the Lesser Himalaya and the Shiwaliks
Answer: (d)
Solution: The tempting wrong option confuses Duns with Thar sand dunes, a similar-sound trap. Duns are flat-floored longitudinal structural valleys lying between the Lesser Himalaya and the Shiwalik range, such as Dehradun, Kotli Dun and Patli Dun, formed by river deposits, so the answer is longitudinal valleys between the Lesser Himalaya and the Shiwaliks.
Source: NCERT Class 11 India Physical Environment

35. Which of the following rivers does NOT form a delta?
(a) Mahanadi (b) Narmada (c) Krishna (d) Godavari
Answer: (b)
Solution: The tempting wrong options are the east-flowing rivers, but the Mahanadi, Krishna and Godavari all build large deltas on the Bay of Bengal coast. The Narmada, flowing westward through a rift valley with a steep gradient and tidal estuary, deposits its sediment in the Gulf of Khambhat without forming a delta, so the answer is Narmada.

36. The highest peak of the Aravalli Range is:
(a) Guru Shikhar (b) Dhupgarh (c) Mahendragiri (d) Kalsubai
Answer: (a)
Solution: The tempting wrong option is Dhupgarh, but Dhupgarh (1,350 m) is the highest peak of the Satpura Range, a similar-name trap. Guru Shikhar (1,722 m) on Mount Abu in Rajasthan is the highest point of the Aravallis, so the answer is Guru Shikhar.

37. Which of the following correctly matches the pass with the region it connects?
(a) Zoji La – Srinagar to Leh (b) Shipki La – Kashmir to Kabul (c) Nathu La – Uttarakhand to Tibet (d) Banihal – Sikkim to Tibet
Answer: (a)
Solution: The tempting wrong options misplace the passes, a location-confusion trap: Shipki La is in Himachal Pradesh (Sutlej route to Tibet), Nathu La connects Sikkim to Tibet, and Banihal connects Jammu to Srinagar. Zoji La lies on the Srinagar–Leh highway connecting the Kashmir Valley with Ladakh, so the answer is Zoji La – Srinagar to Leh.

38. The laterite soils of India are most suitable for which crop with proper manuring?
(a) Wheat (b) Cotton (c) Tea and coffee (d) Sugarcane
Answer: (c)
Solution: The tempting wrong option is cotton, but cotton thrives in black regur soils, not laterite, a soil-crop mismatch trap. Laterite soils, formed by intense leaching in high-rainfall areas of the Western Ghats and Eastern Ghats, are acidic and poor in fertility but support tea, coffee, rubber and cashew with manuring, so the answer is tea and coffee.

39. Which of the following statements about the Lakshadweep islands is correct?
(a) They are of volcanic origin (b) They are an extension of the Aravalli Range (c) They lie in the Bay of Bengal (d) They are coral islands built on a submerged ridge
Answer: (d)
Solution: The tempting wrong option claims volcanic origin, but that describes Barren Island in the Andamans, an origin-confusion trap. The Lakshadweep group lies in the Arabian Sea and consists of coral atolls built by polyps on the submerged Lakshadweep–Chagos ridge, so the answer is that they are coral islands built on a submerged ridge.

40. The river which flows between the Vindhya and Satpura ranges is:
(a) Chambal (b) Betwa (c) Narmada (d) Mahi
Answer: (c)
Solution: The tempting wrong options are Chambal and Betwa, but both are north-flowing tributaries of the Yamuna draining the Malwa plateau, not inter-montane rivers. The Narmada flows westward through a rift valley flanked by the Vindhyas to the north and the Satpuras to the south, so the answer is Narmada.

41. Which of the following is the largest physiographic division of India by area?
(a) The Himalayan Mountains (b) The Peninsular Plateau (c) The Northern Plains (d) The Coastal Plains
Answer: (b)
Solution: The tempting wrong option is the Northern Plains because of their dense population and prominence, but area-wise they are smaller. The Peninsular Plateau, covering roughly 16 lakh sq km including the Deccan Plateau and Central Highlands, is the largest physiographic division of India, so the answer is the Peninsular Plateau.

42. The "Bhabar" belt of the northern plains is characterised by:
(a) Fine alluvium and intensive agriculture (b) Pebble deposits where streams disappear underground (c) Marshy lands with rich vegetation (d) Old alluvium with kankar nodules
Answer: (b)
Solution: The tempting wrong option describes the Terai (marshy, vegetated), but the Terai lies south of the Bhabar, a zonal-sequence trap. The Bhabar is a narrow belt along the Himalayan foothills where rivers deposit pebbles and coarse debris and their water disappears below the surface, so the answer is pebble deposits where streams disappear underground.
Source: NCERT Class 11 India Physical Environment

43. Which plateau is known as the "Mineral Heartland of India"?
(a) Malwa Plateau (b) Chota Nagpur Plateau (c) Deccan Plateau (d) Meghalaya Plateau
Answer: (b)
Solution: The tempting wrong option is the Deccan Plateau because of its vast size, but size is not the criterion here. The Chota Nagpur Plateau, spread over Jharkhand and adjoining areas, holds rich deposits of coal, iron ore, mica, bauxite and manganese, earning it the title of India's mineral heartland, so the answer is Chota Nagpur Plateau.

44. The Godavari is called the "Dakshin Ganga" because:
(a) It originates from the Gangotri glacier (b) It joins the Ganga at Prayagraj (c) It is the longest peninsular river with a large basin like the Ganga (d) It is the holiest river of South India
Answer: (c)
Solution: The tempting wrong option cites holiness, but the comparison is geographical, not religious, a reasoning trap. The Godavari, about 1,465 km long, is the longest river of peninsular India and drains a vast basin across Maharashtra, Telangana and Andhra Pradesh, resembling the Ganga in scale, so the answer is that it is the longest peninsular river with a large basin like the Ganga.

45. Which of the following statements about the Shiwalik range is correct?
(a) It is the oldest and highest range of the Himalayas (b) It is also called the Inner Himalaya (c) It contains the highest peaks like Kanchenjunga (d) It is composed of unconsolidated sediments and is prone to earthquakes and landslides
Answer: (d)
Solution: The tempting wrong option calls it the Inner Himalaya, but that name belongs to the Himadri (Greater Himalaya), a similar-name trap. The Shiwaliks are the youngest, outermost range made of unconsolidated river-borne sediments, making them highly prone to landslides and seismic damage, so the answer is that it is composed of unconsolidated sediments and is prone to earthquakes and landslides.

46. The river Luni, which drains into the Rann of Kutch, is an example of:
(a) An antecedent river (b) A consequent river of the east coast (c) An inland drainage river (d) A superimposed river
Answer: (c)
Solution: The tempting wrong option is antecedent drainage, but antecedent rivers like the Indus and Sutlej cut across rising mountains and reach the sea. The Luni originates in the Aravallis and loses itself in the salty marshes of the Rann of Kutch without reaching the ocean, making it a classic case of inland drainage, so the answer is an inland drainage river.

47. Which of the following hill ranges separates the Narmada and Tapi valleys?

Answer: (a)

48. The Karewa formations of the Kashmir Valley are famous for the cultivation of:
(a) Cardamom (b) Apple only (c) Tea (d) Saffron
Answer: (d)
Solution: The tempting wrong option is apple, which is grown in Kashmir but is not uniquely tied to Karewa deposits. Karewas are lacustrine (lake) deposits of silt and clay in the Kashmir Valley, and their well-drained terraces are ideal for saffron (kesar) cultivation, for which Kashmir is world-famous, so the answer is saffron.

49. Which of the following statements about the Eastern Ghats is correct?
(a) They are higher and more continuous than the Western Ghats (b) They are discontinuous and dissected by rivers like the Godavari and Krishna (c) They run parallel to the Arabian Sea coast (d) Their highest peak is Anamudi
Answer: (b)
Solution: The tempting wrong option claims Anamudi as their highest peak, but Anamudi is in the Western Ghats; the Eastern Ghats' highest peak is Jindhagada (Arma Konda) in Andhra Pradesh. The Eastern Ghats run along the Bay of Bengal and are broken into discontinuous blocks by the Mahanadi, Godavari and Krishna rivers, so the answer is that they are discontinuous and dissected by rivers like the Godavari and Krishna.
Source: NCERT Class 11 India Physical Environment

50. The "Meghalaya Plateau" is geologically a part of:
(a) The Himalayan orogenic belt (b) The Deccan lava plateau (c) The Indo-Gangetic alluvial plain (d) The Peninsular block separated by the Malda gap
Answer: (d)
Solution: The tempting wrong option links it to the Himalayas because of its location in the north-east, a regional-association trap. Geologically the Meghalaya (Shillong) Plateau is an eastward extension of the Peninsular block, separated from the Chota Nagpur Plateau by the Malda gap (Rajmahal–Garo gap) through which the Ganga-Brahmaputra sediments pass, so the answer is the Peninsular block separated by the Malda gap.
