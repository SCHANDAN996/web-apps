Questions 76–100: Medium

76. Which of the following correctly describes the difference between 4G LTE and 5G NR?
(a) 5G uses only low-frequency bands while 4G uses only high-frequency bands (b) 4G is a satellite-based technology while 5G is purely terrestrial (c) 5G NR supports network slicing and ultra-reliable low-latency communication, unlike 4G LTE (d) 5G eliminates the need for base stations entirely
Answer: (c)
Solution: The tempting wrong option claims 5G uses only high-frequency bands; in fact 5G operates across low, mid and high (mmWave) bands, so that statement is false. 5G still requires base stations (small cells), and both 4G and 5G are terrestrial cellular technologies. Network slicing and URLLC are defining new capabilities of 5G NR, so the answer is that 5G NR supports network slicing and ultra-reliable low-latency communication.
Source: PYQ-style

77. In the context of biotechnology, what is the primary function of the CRISPR-Cas9 system?
(a) Sequencing the entire genome of an organism (b) Editing specific DNA sequences by cutting them at targeted locations (c) Producing monoclonal antibodies in large quantities (d) Cloning whole organisms from somatic cells
Answer: (b)
Solution: Many candidates confuse CRISPR with DNA sequencing, but sequencing reads DNA while CRISPR modifies it — that is the trap. Monoclonal antibodies are produced by hybridoma technology, and cloning uses nuclear transfer. CRISPR-Cas9 uses a guide RNA to direct the Cas9 enzyme to cut DNA at a chosen site, so the answer is editing specific DNA sequences by cutting them at targeted locations.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Biology (Biotechnology)

78. India's NavIC system provides coverage primarily over which region?
(a) The entire globe (b) The whole of Asia and Africa (c) Only the Indian mainland (d) India and up to 1,500 km beyond its borders
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is assuming NavIC is global like GPS; it is a regional system, not a global one. It is also wider than just the mainland, covering the surrounding region. NavIC (IRNSS) with seven satellites covers India and a region extending about 1,500 km beyond its borders, so the answer is India and up to 1,500 km beyond its borders.
Source: ISRO official website

79. Which of the following is the correct chronological order of these Indian space milestones?
(a) Aryabhata launch → Mangalyaan → Chandrayaan-1 → Chandrayaan-3 (b) Chandrayaan-1 → Aryabhata launch → Mangalyaan → Chandrayaan-3 (c) Aryabhata launch → Chandrayaan-1 → Mangalyaan → Chandrayaan-3 (d) Mangalyaan → Aryabhata launch → Chandrayaan-1 → Chandrayaan-3
Answer: (c)
Solution: This is a chronological-confusion trap: Mangalyaan (2013) came after Chandrayaan-1 (2008), not before, which eliminates the options placing Mangalyaan earlier. Aryabhata was launched in 1975, Chandrayaan-1 in 2008, Mangalyaan in 2013 and Chandrayaan-3 in 2023, so the answer is Aryabhata launch → Chandrayaan-1 → Mangalyaan → Chandrayaan-3.
Source: ISRO official website

80. What does the term "quantum supremacy" refer to?
(a) A quantum computer that is cheaper than classical computers (b) The use of quantum mechanics in nuclear weapons (c) The point at which a quantum computer performs a task that a classical computer cannot do in feasible time (d) A quantum network connecting all countries
Answer: (c)
Solution: The tempting wrong option equates supremacy with cost, but the term has nothing to do with price. It also does not refer to weapons or networks. Quantum supremacy (or quantum advantage) is the demonstrated ability of a quantum device to solve a problem infeasible for classical supercomputers, so the answer is the point at which a quantum computer performs a task that a classical computer cannot do in feasible time.
Source: PYQ-style

81. In blockchain technology, what is a "smart contract"?
(a) A self-executing program on the blockchain that runs when predefined conditions are met (b) A legally signed paper contract stored digitally (c) A contract between two cryptocurrency miners (d) An agreement between a government and a tech company
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is thinking of a smart contract as a digitised legal document; it is code, not a scanned agreement. It is also unrelated to miner agreements or government deals. A smart contract is self-executing code deployed on a blockchain that automatically enforces terms when conditions are satisfied, so the answer is a self-executing program on the blockchain that runs when predefined conditions are met.
Source: PYQ-style

82. Which Indian mission aims to send humans into space on an indigenous spacecraft?
(a) Aditya-L1 (b) Shukrayaan (c) Gaganyaan (d) NISAR
Answer: (c)
Solution: The similar-names trap: Aditya-L1 studies the Sun from the L1 point, NISAR is a joint NASA-ISRO radar satellite, and Shukrayaan is a proposed Venus mission — none carries humans. Gaganyaan is ISRO's human spaceflight programme using the HLVM3 rocket, so the answer is Gaganyaan.
Source: ISRO official website

83. What is the primary purpose of a "geosynchronous transfer orbit" (GTO)?
(a) To serve as an intermediate orbit for moving satellites toward geostationary orbit (b) To keep a satellite fixed over one point on Earth permanently (c) To place satellites in low Earth orbit for imaging (d) To send probes to the Moon
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is confusing GTO with geostationary orbit itself; a satellite in GTO is not fixed over one point. GTO is an elliptical transfer orbit from which a satellite's own engine circularises it into geostationary orbit, so the answer is to serve as an intermediate orbit for moving satellites toward geostationary orbit.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Physics (Gravitation)

84. Which of the following statements about mRNA vaccines is correct?
(a) They introduce a weakened live virus into the body (b) They carry genetic instructions that prompt cells to produce a viral protein, triggering immunity (c) They alter the recipient's DNA permanently (d) They contain inactivated toxins of bacteria
Answer: (b)
Solution: The tempting wrong option claims mRNA vaccines alter DNA permanently; mRNA never enters the cell nucleus and does not integrate into the genome, so that is false. They also contain no live virus or toxins. mRNA vaccines deliver instructions for making a viral antigen that trains the immune system, so the answer is that they carry genetic instructions that prompt cells to produce a viral protein, triggering immunity.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Biology (Health and Disease)

85. India's first indigenously developed aircraft carrier, commissioned in 2022, is:
(a) INS Viraat (b) INS Vikramaditya (c) INS Vikrant (d) INS Vishal
Answer: (c)
Solution: The similar-names trap: INS Vikramaditya is a Russian-built (refitted) carrier, INS Viraat was a British-built carrier decommissioned in 2017, and INS Vishal is only a proposed future carrier. The indigenously built carrier commissioned in September 2022 is INS Vikrant, so the answer is INS Vikrant.

86. What is "Li-Fi"?
(a) A satellite navigation augmentation system (b) A type of optical fibre used in undersea cables (c) A lithium-based battery technology (d) A wireless communication technology that uses visible light to transmit data
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is associating "Li" with lithium batteries; here "Li" stands for light. Li-Fi (Light Fidelity) transmits data through rapid modulation of LED light, detected by photodetectors, so the answer is a wireless communication technology that uses visible light to transmit data.
Source: PYQ-style

87. Which of the following correctly pairs the Indian supercomputer series with its developing institution?
(a) PARAM series — ISRO (b) PARAM series — DRDO (c) PARAM series — BARC (d) PARAM series — C-DAC
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap lies in the similar-sounding institutions: BARC developed the ANUPAM series and DRDO works on defence systems, not the PARAM line. The PARAM series of supercomputers, beginning with PARAM 8000 in 1991, was developed by the Centre for Development of Advanced Computing, so the answer is PARAM series — C-DAC.
Source: C-DAC official website

88. In nuclear reactors, what is the function of a moderator?
(a) To slow down fast neutrons to sustain the chain reaction (b) To absorb neutrons and shut down the reaction (c) To cool the reactor core (d) To convert uranium into plutonium
Answer: (a)
Solution: The tempting wrong option describes a control rod's job (absorbing neutrons), not a moderator's — a classic confusion. Coolants remove heat, which is a separate component. A moderator such as heavy water or graphite slows fast neutrons to thermal energies so fission can continue, so the answer is to slow down fast neutrons to sustain the chain reaction.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Physics (Nuclei)

89. Which of the following is an example of a "zoonotic" disease?
(a) Rabies (b) Diabetes mellitus (c) Hypertension (d) Anaemia
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is picking a common serious disease without checking its origin; diabetes, hypertension and anaemia are non-communicable and not transmitted from animals. Zoonotic diseases jump from animals to humans, and rabies is transmitted through the bite of infected animals, so the answer is Rabies.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Biology (Health and Disease)

90. The term "Internet of Things" (IoT) best describes:
(a) A faster version of the World Wide Web (b) A network of physical objects embedded with sensors and software that exchange data over the internet (c) A government database of all internet users (d) A social media platform for devices
Answer: (b)
Solution: The tempting wrong option reduces IoT to a faster web, but IoT is about connected physical devices, not browsing speed. It is neither a user database nor a social platform. IoT refers to everyday objects with sensors, connectivity and software that collect and share data, so the answer is a network of physical objects embedded with sensors and software that exchange data over the internet.
Source: PYQ-style

91. Which fuel is used in India's Prototype Fast Breeder Reactor (PFBR) at Kalpakkam?
(a) Natural uranium (b) Heavy water (c) Thorium alone (d) Mixed oxide (plutonium-uranium oxide) fuel
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is choosing thorium because India's three-stage programme is thorium-focused; thorium is the third stage, while the PFBR belongs to the second stage. Heavy water is a moderator, not a fuel. The PFBR uses mixed oxide fuel containing plutonium and uranium oxides, so the answer is mixed oxide (plutonium-uranium oxide) fuel.
Source: DAE/IGCAR official information

92. What does "edge computing" primarily aim to achieve?
(a) Processing data closer to where it is generated to reduce latency (b) Replacing all cloud servers with satellites (c) Increasing the size of data centres (d) Encrypting all internet traffic
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is assuming edge computing is about bigger centralised data centres; it is the opposite — decentralised processing. It is unrelated to satellites or encryption as its core purpose. Edge computing processes data near the source device, cutting latency and bandwidth use, so the answer is processing data closer to where it is generated to reduce latency.
Source: PYQ-style

93. Which of the following statements about stem cells is correct?
(a) They are a type of red blood cell (b) They are found only in embryos (c) They cannot divide by mitosis (d) They can differentiate into specialised cell types
Answer: (d)
Solution: The tempting wrong option restricts stem cells to embryos, but adult (somatic) stem cells also exist in bone marrow and other tissues. Stem cells actively divide and are unrelated to red blood cells as a category. Their defining property is the ability to self-renew and differentiate into specialised cells, so the answer is they can differentiate into specialised cell types.

94. The Chandrayaan-3 mission (2023) achieved a soft landing near which region of the Moon?
(a) The lunar north pole (b) The south polar region (c) The equatorial Mare Imbrium (d) The far side of the Moon
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is the far side, which was China's Chang'e-4 achievement, not Chandrayaan-3's. The landing was not at the north pole or in an equatorial mare. Chandrayaan-3's Vikram lander touched down near the lunar south polar region in August 2023, so the answer is the south polar region.
Source: ISRO official website

95. In artificial intelligence, "machine learning" is best defined as:
(a) Programming computers with explicit rules for every situation (b) Robots that physically assemble machines (c) Systems that improve their performance on a task by learning from data (d) Storing large amounts of data on servers
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is the explicit-rules option, which describes traditional rule-based programming — precisely what machine learning moves away from. It is not about physical robots or mere storage. Machine learning enables systems to learn patterns from data and improve without being explicitly programmed for each case, so the answer is systems that improve their performance on a task by learning from data.

96. Which organisation is responsible for the development of the BrahMos missile?
(a) A collaboration between India and the USA (b) ISRO alone (c) Hindustan Aeronautics Limited alone (d) A joint venture between India's DRDO and Russia's NPO Mashinostroyenia

97. What is the main advantage of a "reusable launch vehicle" (RLV)?
(a) It reduces the cost of access to space by allowing recovery and reuse of hardware (b) It can carry heavier payloads than any other rocket (c) It eliminates the need for fuel (d) It can travel to other stars
Answer: (a)
Solution: The tempting wrong option claims heavier payloads, but reusability often trades some payload capacity for recovery hardware. RLVs still need fuel and cannot reach other stars. The core benefit is cost reduction through recovering and reflying stages, as demonstrated by ISRO's RLV-LEX landing experiments, so the answer is it reduces the cost of access to space by allowing recovery and reuse of hardware.
Source: ISRO official website

98. Which of the following correctly describes "gene therapy"?
(a) Surgery to remove defective organs (b) Treating disease by introducing, removing or altering genetic material within a patient's cells (c) Vaccination against genetic disorders (d) Using antibiotics to kill mutated bacteria
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is vaccination, which prevents infectious disease and cannot correct inherited genetic defects. Gene therapy is neither surgery nor antibiotic treatment. It works by modifying genetic material inside a patient's cells to treat or prevent disease, so the answer is treating disease by introducing, removing or altering genetic material within a patient's cells.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Biology (Biotechnology)

99. The "dark web" is best described as:
(a) All websites that have been shut down (b) The part of the internet accessible only through special software like Tor and not indexed by standard search engines (c) Government websites with restricted access (d) The portion of the internet used only for video streaming
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is confusing the dark web with the deep web or with defunct sites; shut-down sites are simply offline, not dark web. It is also not limited to government or streaming content. The dark web consists of encrypted networks reachable via tools like Tor and invisible to normal search engines, so the answer is the part of the internet accessible only through special software like Tor and not indexed by standard search engines.
Source: PYQ-style

100. Which of the following is the correct sequence of India's three-stage nuclear power programme?
(a) Pressurised heavy water reactors → Fast breeder reactors → Thorium-based reactors (b) Fast breeder reactors → Pressurised heavy water reactors → Thorium-based reactors (c) Thorium-based reactors → Fast breeder reactors → Pressurised heavy water reactors (d) Pressurised heavy water reactors → Thorium-based reactors → Fast breeder reactors
Answer: (a)
Solution: This is a chronological-confusion trap: thorium reactors come last, not first or second, because plutonium from stage one and uranium-233 bred in stage two are needed first. Homi Bhabha's programme begins with natural-uranium PHWRs, moves to plutonium-fuelled fast breeders, and ends with thorium-based reactors, so the answer is Pressurised heavy water reactors → Fast breeder reactors → Thorium-based reactors.
Source: DAE official website
