Questions 51–75: Medium

51. Which of the following correctly describes the principle behind a LiDAR system?
(a) It uses radio waves to measure the distance of objects (b) It uses magnetic field variations to detect metallic objects (c) It uses sound waves to map underwater terrain (d) It uses laser pulses and measures the time taken for reflected light to return
Answer: (d)
Solution: The tempting wrong option is the radio-wave one, which describes RADAR, not LiDAR — examiners often swap the two. LiDAR (Light Detection and Ranging) emits laser pulses and calculates distance from the time-of-flight of the reflected light, so the answer is that it uses laser pulses and measures the time taken for reflected light to return.
Source: PYQ-style

52. In the context of nuclear reactors in India, the Prototype Fast Breeder Reactor (PFBR) being built at Kalpakkam uses which combination of fuel and coolant?
(a) Uranium-235 fuel and heavy water coolant (b) Plutonium-uranium mixed oxide fuel and liquid sodium coolant (c) Thorium fuel and molten salt coolant (d) Enriched uranium fuel and light water coolant
Answer: (b)
Solution: Heavy water is used in India's pressurised heavy water reactors (PHWRs), which lures many students into picking the first option. The PFBR at Kalpakkam is a 500 MWe reactor using plutonium-uranium mixed oxide (MOX) fuel with liquid sodium as coolant, so the answer is plutonium-uranium mixed oxide fuel and liquid sodium coolant.
Source: PYQ-style

53. Which of the following statements about CRISPR-Cas9 gene editing is correct?
(a) It can only edit genes in bacterial cells (b) It was originally discovered as a plant defence mechanism against fungi (c) It uses a guide RNA to direct the Cas9 protein to a specific DNA sequence (d) It edits RNA permanently without ever touching DNA
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is the statement about a plant defence mechanism — CRISPR was discovered as part of the bacterial immune system against viruses (bacteriophages), not in plants. In CRISPR-Cas9, a guide RNA leads the Cas9 nuclease to the target DNA sequence where it makes a cut, so the answer is that it uses a guide RNA to direct the Cas9 protein to a specific DNA sequence.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Biology

54. The term 'quantum supremacy' refers to:
(a) The complete replacement of classical computers by quantum computers (b) The ability of a quantum computer to perform a task that is practically impossible for classical computers (c) The use of quantum mechanics in making superconductors (d) The achievement of absolute zero temperature in quantum labs
Answer: (b)
Solution: The second option is the trap — quantum supremacy does not mean classical computers become obsolete; it is demonstrated for a specific, often contrived, computational task. It means a quantum device performs a calculation infeasible for any classical computer, so the answer is the ability of a quantum computer to perform a task that is practically impossible for classical computers.
Source: PYQ-style

55. Which of the following diseases is caused by a prion?
(a) Kala-azar (b) Japanese encephalitis (c) Creutzfeldt-Jakob disease (d) Chikungunya
Answer: (c)
Solution: Japanese encephalitis and chikungunya are viral diseases and kala-azar is caused by the protozoan Leishmania — all tempting because they appear in the same GK lists. Prions are misfolded proteins that cause neurodegenerative diseases such as Creutzfeldt-Jakob disease in humans and BSE (mad cow disease) in cattle, so the answer is Creutzfeldt-Jakob disease.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Biology

56. In India's three-stage nuclear power programme, the third stage is based primarily on which fuel cycle?
(a) Uranium-Plutonium cycle (b) Thorium-Uranium-233 cycle (c) Plutonium-Uranium-238 cycle (d) Enriched uranium cycle
Answer: (b)
Solution: The first option tempts students because the uranium-plutonium cycle drives stages one and two. The third stage, envisioned by Homi Bhabha, uses India's abundant thorium, which is converted into fissile uranium-233 in breeder reactors, so the answer is the Thorium-Uranium-233 cycle.
Source: PYQ-style

57. Which of the following correctly pairs a navigation satellite system with its operating country?
(a) NavIC — India (b) Galileo — China (c) BeiDou — European Union (d) GLONASS — Japan
Answer: (a)
Solution: The first two options are deliberately swapped — BeiDou is Chinese and Galileo belongs to the European Union, a classic similar-names trap. NavIC (IRNSS) is India's regional navigation system operated by ISRO, so the answer is NavIC — India.
Source: PYQ-style

58. What is the primary function of 'reverse transcriptase' enzyme?
(a) Synthesis of DNA from an RNA template (b) Synthesis of RNA from a DNA template (c) Cutting DNA at specific recognition sites (d) Joining two DNA fragments together
Answer: (a)
Solution: The second option describes transcription by RNA polymerase and is the common trap. Reverse transcriptase, found in retroviruses like HIV, synthesises complementary DNA using RNA as the template, so the answer is synthesis of DNA from an RNA template.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Biology

59. Consider the following statements about 5G technology:
1. It operates only in the millimetre wave band.
2. It offers significantly lower latency than 4G.
3. It can support network slicing for different applications.
Which of the statements given above is/are correct?
(a) 2 and 3 only (b) 1 and 2 only (c) 1 and 3 only (d) 1, 2 and 3
Answer: (a)
Solution: Statement 1 is the trap — 5G uses sub-6 GHz bands as well as millimetre waves, so "only" makes it false. Statements 2 and 3 are correct: 5G reduces latency to around a millisecond and supports network slicing, so the answer is 2 and 3 only.
Source: PYQ-style

60. The 'Bharatiya Nirdeshak Dravya' (BND) programme of CSIR-NPL is related to:
(a) Certified reference materials for quality testing (b) Satellite-based navigation standards (c) Standardisation of nuclear fuel (d) Calibration of defence equipment
Answer: (a)
Solution: The navigation option is a trap planted by the word "Nirdeshak" (which also appears in NavIC's full name). BNDs are certified reference materials developed by CSIR-National Physical Laboratory to ensure accuracy and traceability in measurements and quality testing, so the answer is certified reference materials for quality testing.
Source: PYQ-style

61. Which of the following best describes 'blockchain' technology?
(a) A centralised database managed by a single authority (b) A type of quantum encryption used in banking (c) A distributed ledger where records are stored in cryptographically linked blocks across multiple nodes (d) A cloud storage system for large files
Answer: (c)
Solution: The first option is the exact opposite of blockchain and traps students who equate all databases. Blockchain is a decentralised, distributed ledger in which each block is cryptographically linked to the previous one and copies are maintained across nodes, so the answer is a distributed ledger where records are stored in cryptographically linked blocks across multiple nodes.
Source: PYQ-style

62. In the context of vaccines, mRNA vaccines work by:
(a) Introducing a weakened live virus into the body (b) Injecting ready-made antibodies against the virus (c) Delivering genetic instructions for cells to produce a viral protein that triggers an immune response (d) Introducing inactivated toxin molecules
Answer: (c)
Solution: The first option describes traditional live-attenuated vaccines and is the tempting wrong choice. mRNA vaccines deliver messenger RNA encoding a viral antigen (such as the spike protein); the body's cells produce that protein, provoking immunity, so the answer is delivering genetic instructions for cells to produce a viral protein that triggers an immune response.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Biology

63. Which Indian mission is designed to study the Sun from the Lagrange point L1?
(a) Chandrayaan-3 (b) Gaganyaan (c) Aditya-L1 (d) XPoSat
Answer: (c)
Solution: XPoSat is a trap because it is also an astronomy mission, but it studies X-ray polarisation from cosmic sources, not the Sun. Aditya-L1, launched by ISRO in September 2023, is India's first solar observation mission placed in a halo orbit around the Sun-Earth L1 point, so the answer is Aditya-L1.
Source: PYQ-style

64. Which of the following is an example of a 'zoonotic' disease?
(a) Nipah virus infection (b) Malaria (c) Typhoid (d) Tuberculosis
Answer: (a)
Solution: Malaria is the trap — although transmitted by mosquitoes, it is a vector-borne human disease, not classically zoonotic since the parasite cycles between humans and mosquitoes. Nipah virus jumps from animals (fruit bats, pigs) to humans, making it zoonotic, so the answer is Nipah virus infection.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Biology

65. The term 'Internet of Things' (IoT) refers to:
(a) A government portal for digital services (b) A faster version of the World Wide Web (c) A social media platform for devices (d) A network of physical objects embedded with sensors and software that exchange data over the internet
Answer: (d)
Solution: The second option is a vague trap — IoT is not about web speed but about connectivity of physical devices. IoT means everyday physical objects with sensors, processing ability and software that connect and exchange data with other devices over the internet, so the answer is a network of physical objects embedded with sensors and software that exchange data over the internet.
Source: PYQ-style

66. Which of the following statements about stem cells is correct?
(a) Adult stem cells are always totipotent (b) Embryonic stem cells are pluripotent and can differentiate into nearly all cell types (c) Stem cells cannot divide by mitosis (d) Only plant cells can act as stem cells
Answer: (b)
Solution: The first option is the trap — adult stem cells are multipotent, not totipotent; totipotency belongs to the zygote and early cleavage cells. Embryonic stem cells are pluripotent, capable of forming almost all body cell types, so the answer is that embryonic stem cells are pluripotent and can differentiate into nearly all cell types.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Biology

67. India's first indigenous aircraft carrier, commissioned in September 2022, is:
(a) INS Vishal (b) INS Vikramaditya (c) INS Viraat (d) INS Vikrant
Answer: (d)
Solution: INS Vikramaditya is the trap — it is a Russian-built (refitted) carrier, not indigenous; INS Viraat was decommissioned in 2017. INS Vikrant (IAC-1), built at Cochin Shipyard, was commissioned in September 2022, so the answer is INS Vikrant.
Source: PYQ-style

68. Which of the following correctly describes 'superconductivity'?
(a) The property of a material to conduct electricity with zero resistance below a critical temperature (b) The ability of a material to conduct heat faster than metals (c) The property of semiconductors at room temperature (d) The phenomenon of light travelling faster in a vacuum
Answer: (a)
Solution: The third option tempts students who confuse superconductors with semiconductors — the two are entirely different. Superconductivity is the complete loss of electrical resistance (and expulsion of magnetic fields) in certain materials below a critical temperature, so the answer is the property of a material to conduct electricity with zero resistance below a critical temperature.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Physics

69. The 'Gaganyaan' mission of ISRO aims to:
(a) Send a probe to Mars (b) Land an Indian astronaut on the Moon (c) Establish a permanent Indian space station by 2025 (d) Send an Indian crewed spacecraft to low Earth orbit
Answer: (d)
Solution: The lunar option is a chronological-confusion trap — a crewed Moon landing is a stated long-term aspiration (by 2040), not the Gaganyaan objective. Gaganyaan is ISRO's human spaceflight programme to carry a crew to low Earth orbit (about 400 km) and bring them back safely, so the answer is to send an Indian crewed spacecraft to low Earth orbit.
Source: PYQ-style

70. Which of the following is used as a moderator in a Pressurised Heavy Water Reactor (PHWR)?
(a) Graphite (b) Liquid sodium (c) Heavy water (D₂O) (d) Beryllium
Answer: (c)
Solution: Graphite is the trap — it is the moderator in RBMK and some early reactors, and liquid sodium is a coolant in fast breeders, not a moderator. PHWRs, the workhorse of India's nuclear programme, use heavy water as both moderator and coolant, so the answer is heavy water (D₂O).
Source: NCERT Class 12 Physics

71. Which of the following statements about graphene is correct?
(a) It is a three-dimensional allotrope of carbon (b) It is heavier than steel for the same volume (c) It is an electrical insulator (d) It is a single layer of carbon atoms arranged in a hexagonal lattice
Answer: (d)
Solution: The first option is the trap — diamond is the three-dimensional carbon allotrope, while graphene is two-dimensional. Graphene is a one-atom-thick sheet of carbon atoms in a hexagonal (honeycomb) lattice, and it is an excellent conductor, so the answer is that it is a single layer of carbon atoms arranged in a hexagonal lattice.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Chemistry

72. In the context of artificial intelligence, 'deep learning' is based on:
(a) Simple if-then rule-based programming (b) Manual statistical tables (c) Artificial neural networks with multiple layers (d) Analog computing circuits
Answer: (c)
Solution: The first option describes early expert systems, which is the trap for students who equate all AI with rule-based logic. Deep learning uses multi-layered artificial neural networks that learn hierarchical features from large data, so the answer is artificial neural networks with multiple layers.

73. Which of the following pairs of an Indian spaceport/launch vehicle and its feature is correctly matched?
(a) LVM3 — ISRO's heaviest operational launch vehicle (b) PSLV — exclusively a human-rated launch vehicle (c) SSLV — designed for launching heavy geostationary satellites (d) GSLV Mk I — uses only solid propellant stages
Answer: (a)
Solution: The first option is the trap — PSLV is a workhorse for polar satellite launches, not human-rated; LVM3 (GSLV Mk III) is the vehicle being human-rated for Gaganyaan. LVM3 is ISRO's heaviest operational rocket, capable of lifting about 4 tonnes to GTO, so the answer is LVM3 — ISRO's heaviest operational launch vehicle.
Source: PYQ-style

74. Which of the following techniques is used for DNA fingerprinting?
(a) X-ray crystallography of blood cells (b) Polymerase chain reaction and analysis of variable number tandem repeats (c) Chromatography of serum proteins (d) Ultracentrifugation of plasma
Answer: (b)
Solution: X-ray crystallography is the trap — it was used to determine DNA's structure (Rosalind Franklin's work), not for fingerprinting individuals. DNA fingerprinting amplifies DNA by PCR and analyses highly variable regions such as VNTRs/STRs, so the answer is polymerase chain reaction and analysis of variable number tandem repeats.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Biology

75. Consider the following statements about hydrogen fuel cells:
1. They generate electricity through the electrochemical reaction of hydrogen and oxygen.
2. The only by-products are water and heat.
3. They require hydrogen to be burned in air like a conventional engine.
Which of the statements given above is/are correct?
(a) 1, 2 and 3 (b) 2 and 3 only (c) 1 only (d) 1 and 2 only
Answer: (d)
Solution: Statement 3 is the trap — fuel cells do not burn hydrogen; combustion would produce nitrogen oxides and waste energy as in an engine. Fuel cells convert chemical energy directly into electricity electrochemically, emitting only water and heat, so the answer is 1 and 2 only.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Chemistry
