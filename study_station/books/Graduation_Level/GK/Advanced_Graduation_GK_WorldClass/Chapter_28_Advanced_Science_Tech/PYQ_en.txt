# 📊 PYQ Analysis — Advanced Science & Technology

## 1. How Exams Ask Questions from This Chapter

Across UPSC (Prelims), SSC CGL/CHSL, Banking (IBPS/SBI), Railway (NTPC/Group D) and State PCS prelims, Advanced Science & Technology questions follow a **stable, recognisable pattern** even though the exact topics rotate. The analysis below is based on the *nature* of questions repeatedly seen in these exams — not on invented counts.

**What examiners consistently test:**

- **Definitional clarity** — What exactly is 5G, blockchain, CRISPR, quantum computing, AI/ML, nanotechnology? One-line definitions with one word changed are the favourite trap.
- **Application vs. technology matching** — Which technology is used in which field (e.g., LiDAR in remote sensing, RFID in tracking, GIS in mapping)?
- **Institutions and missions** — ISRO, DRDO, BARC, CSIR labs, and missions like Gaganyaan, Chandrayaan, Aditya-L1, NQM (National Quantum Mission).
- **India-specific achievements** — vaccines, supercomputers (PARAM series), nuclear reactors, missile systems (Agni, Prithvi, BrahMos).
- **Concept pairs that look alike** — AI vs ML vs Deep Learning; 4G vs 5G; gene editing vs cloning; virus vs bacterium; LEO vs GEO orbits.

**Sub-topics that recur most often (in words, not numbers):**

| Sub-topic | How often it appears | Typical form |
|---|---|---|
| Space technology (ISRO missions, orbits, launch vehicles) | Very frequent | Match-the-following, statement-based |
| Biotechnology (stem cells, GM crops, gene editing) | Very frequent | "Which of the following is correct?" |
| IT & emerging tech (AI, blockchain, 5G, IoT, quantum) | Rising sharply in recent years | Definition + application |
| Defence technology (missiles, DRDO systems) | Frequent in SSC/Railway/Defence exams | Direct factual |
| Nuclear & energy tech | Moderate | Statement-based |
| Nanotechnology & materials | Occasional but predictable | One-liner factual |

**Exam-wise flavour:**

- **UPSC / State PCS:** Statement-based, conceptual, often linking tech with environment, ethics, or economy ("With reference to…, consider the following statements").
- **SSC / Railway:** Direct one-liners — full forms, inventors, mission names, "which is India's first…".
- **Banking:** Tech-in-banking angle — UPI, digital rupee (CBDC), cybersecurity terms, plus current-affairs-linked static questions.

## 2. The Examiner's Traps (जाल) — With Examples

### Trap 1: Almost-Correct Statement
A statement that is 90% true but fails on one word.
> "5G technology operates only on low-band spectrum." — Wrong: 5G uses low, mid **and** high (mmWave) bands.

### Trap 2: Chronological Confusion
Swapping the order of missions, inventions, or versions.
> "Chandrayaan-2 was India's first lunar mission." — Wrong: Chandrayaan-1 (2008) was first; Chandrayaan-2 (2019) was second.

### Trap 3: Negative Phrasing
"Which of the following is **NOT** correct…" — candidates misread under time pressure and pick the correct statement.

### Trap 4: Similar-Sounding Names
- **Agni vs Akash** (ballistic missile vs surface-to-air missile)
- **PARAM vs ANURAG** (supercomputer series)
- **LEO vs GEO vs MEO** orbits
- **AI vs ML vs Deep Learning** (nested subsets — Deep Learning ⊂ ML ⊂ AI)

### Trap 5: Overgeneralisation
Words like *all, only, always, never* usually signal a wrong statement.
> "Blockchain can only be used for cryptocurrencies." — Wrong: supply chains, land records, voting pilots, etc.

### Trap 6: Attribution Swap
Crediting the wrong organisation or country.
> "BrahMos is a joint venture of India and Israel." — Wrong: it is India–**Russia** (named after Brahmaputra + Moskva rivers).

## 3. Top 10 High-Yield PYQ-Style Questions with Solutions

**Q1.** With reference to 5G technology, consider the following statements:
1. It can support data speeds significantly higher than 4G.
2. It operates exclusively on millimetre-wave spectrum.
3. It enables network slicing for different applications.
Which of the statements given above is/are correct?
(a) 1 only (b) 1 and 3 only (c) 2 and 3 only (d) 1, 2 and 3
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** Statements 1 and 3 are correct. Statement 2 is the **Almost-Correct trap** — 5G uses low, mid and high (mmWave) bands, not mmWave exclusively.
**Trap type:** Almost-Correct | **Source:** PYQ-style

**Q2.** Which of the following is **NOT** a feature of blockchain technology?
(a) Decentralised ledger (b) Immutability of recorded data
(c) Central authority controls all transactions (d) Cryptographic security
**Answer: (c)**
**Solution:** Blockchain is defined by the *absence* of a central controlling authority. Note the **NOT** — read the stem twice before answering.
**Trap type:** Negative Phrasing | **Source:** PYQ-style

**Q3.** CRISPR-Cas9 technology is primarily associated with:
(a) Gene editing (b) Quantum computing (c) Satellite navigation (d) Nuclear fusion
**Answer: (a)**
**Solution:** CRISPR-Cas9 is a gene-editing tool that cuts DNA at targeted locations. The other options are **Similar-Name distractors** from unrelated high-yield topics.
**Trap type:** Similar-Sounding Names | **Source:** NCERT Class 12 Biology (biotechnology chapter)

**Q4.** Consider the following pairs:
1. Agni — Ballistic missile
2. Akash — Surface-to-air missile
3. BrahMos — Supersonic cruise missile
Which of the pairs given above is/are correctly matched?
(a) 1 and 2 only (b) 2 and 3 only (c) 1 and 3 only (d) 1, 2 and 3
**Answer: (d)**
**Solution:** All three pairs are correct. Agni is a ballistic missile family, Akash is a surface-to-air missile, and BrahMos is an Indo-Russian supersonic cruise missile.
**Trap type:** Similar-Sounding Names (Agni/Akash swap is the classic error) | **Source:** PYQ-style

**Q5.** Which of the following best describes the relationship between Artificial Intelligence (AI), Machine Learning (ML) and Deep Learning (DL)?
(a) AI is a subset of ML, which is a subset of DL
(b) DL is a subset of ML, which is a subset of AI
(c) AI, ML and DL are completely independent fields
(d) ML is a subset of DL, which is a subset of AI
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** The nesting is DL ⊂ ML ⊂ AI. Options (a) and (d) reverse the order — a pure **Chronological/Hierarchical Confusion** trap.
**Trap type:** Hierarchical Confusion | **Source:** PYQ-style

**Q6.** India's Aditya-L1 mission is designed to study:
(a) The Moon's south pole (b) The Sun (c) Mars' atmosphere (d) Asteroids
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** Aditya-L1 is ISRO's solar observation mission, placed near the Lagrange point L1 of the Sun–Earth system. Do not confuse it with Chandrayaan (lunar) or Mangalyaan (Mars).
**Trap type:** Similar-Sounding Names | **Source:** PYQ-style

**Q7.** Which of the following statements about stem cells is correct?
(a) Stem cells can differentiate into specialised cell types
(b) Stem cells are found only in embryos
(c) Stem cells cannot divide
(d) All stem cells are totipotent
**Answer: (a)**
**Solution:** Stem cells can self-renew and differentiate. Adult stem cells also exist (so (b) is wrong), they do divide (so (c) is wrong), and only the earliest embryonic cells are totipotent — adult stem cells are multipotent (so (d) is the **Overgeneralisation trap**).
**Trap type:** Overgeneralisation | **Source:** NCERT Class 12 Biology

**Q8.** A satellite in Geostationary Orbit (GEO) appears stationary over the equator because:
(a) It does not move relative to the Sun
(b) Its orbital period equals Earth's rotation period
(c) It is outside Earth's gravitational field
(d) It orbits over the poles
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** A GEO satellite orbits at ~35,786 km with a period of 24 hours, matching Earth's rotation, so it appears fixed over one equatorial point. Option (d) confuses GEO with polar orbit — a classic **Similar-Concept trap**.
**Trap type:** Similar-Sounding Concepts (GEO vs Polar) | **Source:** NCERT Class 11 Physics (Gravitation)

**Q9.** With reference to quantum computing, the basic unit of information is:
(a) Bit (b) Byte (c) Qubit (d) Pixel
**Answer: (c)**
**Solution:** Quantum computers use **qubits**, which can exist in superposition of 0 and 1. Option (a) is the **Almost-Correct trap** — a bit is the unit of *classical* computing.
**Trap type:** Almost-Correct | **Source:** PYQ-style

**Q10.** Which organisation developed the PARAM series of supercomputers in India?
(a) ISRO (b) C-DAC (c) DRDO (d) BARC
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** The PARAM series was developed by the Centre for Development of Advanced Computing (C-DAC), Pune. ISRO/DRDO/BARC are **Attribution-Swap distractors** — all are famous Indian S&T bodies, which is exactly why the trap works.
**Trap type:** Attribution Swap | **Source:** PYQ-style

## 4. The 15/45-Second Rule Applied

**Rule:** If a question is direct factual (full form, mission name, inventor), decide within **15 seconds** — you either know it or you don't. If it is statement-based or match-the-following, allow up to **45 seconds** to eliminate options; beyond that, mark and move on.

**Example 1 — 15-second question (Q6 above, Aditya-L1):**
This is pure recall. If you know Aditya-L1 = Sun mission, answer in under 15 seconds. If you don't, no amount of thinking will help — guessing between Moon/Sun wastes time. **Decide fast, move on.**

**Example 2 — 45-second question (Q1 above, 5G statements):**
Statement-based questions reward methodical elimination:
- ~15 sec: Read the stem, note "correct" vs "NOT correct".
- ~15 sec: Judge statement 2 — the word "exclusively" is a red flag; eliminate options containing 2 (i.e., (c) and (d)).
- ~15 sec: Confirm between (a) and (b) by checking statement 3 (network slicing — a genuine 5G feature). Answer (b).
Total: ~45 seconds. **Do not exceed this** — if still unsure at 45 seconds, mark your best elimination and proceed.

---

## 5. Key Takeaways for the Exam Hall

1. **Read the stem twice** when you see NOT/EXCEPT/INCORRECT — negative phrasing causes more losses than lack of knowledge.
2. **Distrust absolute words** (only, all, always, never) in statement-based questions.
3. **Prepare comparison tables** for confusing pairs: Agni/Akash, LEO/GEO, AI/ML/DL, 4G/5G, virus/bacterium.
4. **Link current affairs to static concepts** — a mission in the news (e.g., a new ISRO launch) pulls its *static* background (orbits, launch vehicles, L1 point) into the exam.
5. **Apply 15/45 strictly** — Advanced S&T questions are scoring only if you don't let one tricky statement-based question eat 2 minutes.
