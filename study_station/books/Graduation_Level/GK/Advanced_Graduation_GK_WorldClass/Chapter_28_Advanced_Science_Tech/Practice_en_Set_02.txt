Questions 26–50: Medium

26. Which of the following statements about CRISPR-Cas9 is correct?
(a) It is a technique used only for cloning animals (b) It is a gene-editing tool adapted from a bacterial immune system (c) It edits proteins directly without touching DNA (d) It was developed by ISRO for satellite imaging
Answer: (b)
Solution: The tempting wrong option is the protein-editing claim — CRISPR works on DNA, not proteins, so that statement is almost right in spirit but factually wrong. CRISPR-Cas9 is derived from a bacterial defence mechanism against viruses, where Cas9 cuts DNA at a guided location, so the answer is that it is a gene-editing tool adapted from a bacterial immune system.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Biology (Biotechnology)

27. India's first indigenous aircraft carrier, commissioned in September 2022, is:
(a) INS Vikramaditya (b) INS Vikrant (c) INS Viraat (d) INS Arihant
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is INS Vikramaditya, which sounds similar but is a Russian-built (refitted Admiral Gorshkov) carrier, not indigenous. INS Arihant is a nuclear submarine, not a carrier. The indigenously built carrier commissioned in September 2022 is INS Vikrant, so the answer is INS Vikrant.
Source: Indian Navy official records (2022)

28. Which of the following correctly describes the difference between 4G and 5G networks?
(a) 5G offers higher data speeds and lower latency than 4G (b) 5G works only on satellite communication (c) 4G has lower latency than 5G (d) 5G cannot support Internet of Things devices
Answer: (a)
Solution: The tempting wrong option reverses the latency comparison — in fact 5G's key advantage is much lower latency (around 1 millisecond target) along with higher speeds and massive device connectivity for IoT. So the answer is that 5G offers higher data speeds and lower latency than 4G.
Source: ITU IMT-2020 specifications

29. The Chandrayaan-3 mission achieved a soft landing near which region of the Moon in August 2023?
(a) The far side of the Moon (b) The north polar region (c) The equatorial region (d) The south polar region
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is the far side of the Moon — that was China's Chang'e-4, not Chandrayaan-3. Chandrayaan-3's Vikram lander touched down near the lunar south polar region on 23 August 2023, making India the first country to soft-land there, so the answer is the south polar region.
Source: ISRO official mission report (2023)

30. Which of the following is an example of a non-conventional (renewable) source of energy?
(a) Natural gas (b) Coal (c) Solar energy (d) Diesel
Answer: (c)
Solution: Coal, natural gas and diesel are all fossil fuels, which are conventional and non-renewable. Solar energy is renewable and non-conventional, so the answer is solar energy.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science (Sources of Energy)

31. Consider the following statements about blockchain technology:
1. It is a distributed ledger maintained across multiple computers.
2. Once recorded, data in a block can be easily altered by a single user.
Which of the statements given above is/are correct?
(a) 2 only (b) 1 only (c) Both 1 and 2 (d) Neither 1 nor 2
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is statement 2, which is almost plausible but wrong — the defining feature of blockchain is immutability; altering a block would require changing all subsequent blocks across the network, which a single user cannot do. Statement 1 is correct, so the answer is 1 only.
Source: NITI Aayog discussion paper on Blockchain

32. The term 'Li-Fi' in communication technology refers to:
(a) Data transmission using visible light (b) A faster version of Bluetooth (c) Satellite-based internet service (d) A type of fibre optic cable
Answer: (a)
Solution: The tempting wrong option is fibre optic cable — both involve light, but fibre uses guided light inside cables while Li-Fi transmits data wirelessly through visible light from LEDs (Visible Light Communication). So the answer is data transmission using visible light.
Source: NCERT-based general science (communication technology)

33. Which Indian mission aims to send humans into space on an indigenous spacecraft?
(a) Aditya-L1 (b) Mangalyaan (c) Chandrayaan (d) Gaganyaan
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is Aditya-L1, which is also a recent ISRO mission but is a solar observation mission, not a human spaceflight one. Mangalyaan is the Mars orbiter and Chandrayaan is the lunar programme. The human spaceflight programme is Gaganyaan, so the answer is Gaganyaan.
Source: ISRO official programme details

34. Which of the following diseases is caused by a virus?
(a) Typhoid (b) Tuberculosis (c) Cholera (d) COVID-19
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap here is that all four are infectious diseases, but tuberculosis (Mycobacterium), cholera (Vibrio cholerae) and typhoid (Salmonella typhi) are bacterial. COVID-19 is caused by the SARS-CoV-2 virus, so the answer is COVID-19.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Science (Why Do We Fall Ill)

35. The 'PARAM' series in India is associated with:
(a) Satellites (b) Missiles (c) Supercomputers (d) Nuclear reactors
Answer: (c)
Solution: The tempting wrong option is missiles, because names like Prithvi and Agni dominate defence news — but PARAM is the series of supercomputers developed by C-DAC (Centre for Development of Advanced Computing), starting with PARAM 8000 in 1991. So the answer is supercomputers.
Source: C-DAC official records

36. Which of the following correctly matches the missile with its type?
(a) Agni-V – Anti-tank missile (b) BrahMos – Supersonic cruise missile (c) Prithvi – Submarine-launched ballistic missile (d) Akash – Intercontinental ballistic missile
Answer: (b)
Solution: The traps are mismatched categories: Agni-V is an intercontinental-range ballistic missile, Prithvi is a surface-to-surface ballistic missile, and Akash is a surface-to-air missile. BrahMos is an Indo-Russian supersonic cruise missile, so the answer is BrahMos – Supersonic cruise missile.
Source: DRDO official records

37. In biotechnology, 'Bt cotton' is cotton that:
(a) Contains a gene from the bacterium Bacillus thuringiensis giving resistance to bollworms (b) Is grown without any water (c) Is a naturally occurring wild variety of cotton (d) Contains genes taken from another cotton plant only
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is the last option — Bt cotton is transgenic, meaning the gene comes from a different species altogether, the soil bacterium Bacillus thuringiensis, whose Cry protein is toxic to bollworm larvae. So the answer is that it contains a gene from Bacillus thuringiensis giving resistance to bollworms.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Biology (Biotechnology: Principles and Processes)

38. Which of the following is the correct chronological order of these Indian space events?
1. Launch of Aryabhata satellite
2. Launch of Chandrayaan-1
3. Mars Orbiter Mission (Mangalyaan)
(a) 1, 3, 2 (b) 2, 1, 3 (c) 1, 2, 3 (d) 3, 2, 1
Answer: (c)
Solution: The chronological-confusion trap swaps the Mars and Moon missions. Aryabhata was launched in 1975, Chandrayaan-1 in 2008, and Mangalyaan in 2013, so the correct order is 1, 2, 3.
Source: ISRO official mission chronology

39. 'Quantum computing' differs from classical computing primarily because it:
(a) Does not require electricity (b) Uses faster silicon chips than normal computers (c) Uses qubits that can exist in superposition of states (d) Stores data only in the cloud
Answer: (c)
Solution: The tempting wrong option is faster silicon chips — quantum computers are not merely faster classical machines; their fundamental unit, the qubit, exploits superposition and entanglement, allowing certain problems to be solved in fundamentally new ways. So the answer is that it uses qubits that can exist in superposition of states.
Source: Standard physics/computer science reference

40. The 'National Mission on Quantum Technologies and Applications' in India is implemented under the overall framework of:
(a) The Ministry of Defence (b) The Department of Science and Technology (c) The Ministry of Railways (d) The Ministry of Agriculture
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is the Ministry of Defence, since quantum tech has defence uses — but the National Mission on Quantum Technologies and Applications is a civilian science mission under the Department of Science and Technology. So the answer is the Department of Science and Technology.
Source: Government of India budget announcements (2020)

41. Which of the following statements about stem cells is correct?
(a) They are found only in embryos (b) They can differentiate into specialised cell types (c) They cannot divide (d) They are a type of virus used in therapy
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is "found only in embryos" — adult (somatic) stem cells also exist, for example in bone marrow. The defining property of stem cells is their ability to self-renew and differentiate into specialised cells, so the answer is that they can differentiate into specialised cell types.
Source: NCERT Class 11–12 Biology

42. India's Aditya-L1 mission, launched in September 2023, is designed to study:
(a) Mars (b) The Moon (c) The Sun (d) Jupiter
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is the Moon, because Aditya-L1 launched close to Chandrayaan-3 in time, causing chronological confusion. Aditya-L1 is India's first dedicated solar observatory, placed at the Lagrange point L1 to study the Sun, so the answer is the Sun.
Source: ISRO official mission details (2023)

43. Which of the following is used as a moderator in a nuclear reactor?
(a) Mercury (b) Petrol (c) Liquid nitrogen (d) Heavy water
Answer: (d)
Solution: The tempting wrong options are simply other liquids; the moderator's job is to slow down fast neutrons to sustain the chain reaction, and heavy water (D2O) — used in India's pressurised heavy water reactors — is an excellent moderator. So the answer is heavy water.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Physics (Nuclei)

44. The 'NavIC' system of India is:
(a) A regional satellite navigation system (b) A missile defence shield (c) A weather forecasting satellite (d) An undersea cable network
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is weather forecasting, since INSAT satellites do that — but NavIC (Navigation with Indian Constellation, formerly IRNSS) is India's regional navigation satellite system with seven satellites, similar in purpose to GPS. So the answer is a regional satellite navigation system.
Source: ISRO official records

45. Consider the following statements about Artificial Intelligence:
1. Machine learning is a subset of artificial intelligence.
2. Deep learning is a subset of machine learning.
Which of the statements given above is/are correct?
(a) 2 only (b) 1 only (c) Both 1 and 2 (d) Neither 1 nor 2
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is to treat these as competing or parallel fields. In fact the hierarchy is nested: AI is the broad field, machine learning is a subset of AI, and deep learning (using neural networks) is a subset of machine learning. Both statements are correct, so the answer is Both 1 and 2.
Source: Standard computer science reference

46. Which of the following pairs is correctly matched?
(a) INS Vikrant – Nuclear submarine (b) INS Arihant – Nuclear-powered ballistic missile submarine (c) INS Chakra – Aircraft carrier (d) INS Kolkata – Conventional submarine
Answer: (b)
Solution: The similar-names trap swaps vessel categories: INS Vikrant is an aircraft carrier, INS Chakra is a nuclear-powered attack submarine, and INS Kolkata is a guided-missile destroyer. INS Arihant is India's indigenous nuclear-powered ballistic missile submarine (SSBN), so the answer is INS Arihant – Nuclear-powered ballistic missile submarine.
Source: Indian Navy official records

47. The 'Genome India Project' aims to:
(a) Develop genetically modified food crops (b) Clone endangered Indian animals (c) Catalogue genetic variation across the Indian population (d) Map all rivers of India using satellites
Answer: (c)
Solution: The tempting wrong option is cloning, a common confusion with anything involving genes. The Genome India Project, coordinated by IISc and partner institutions, sequences thousands of Indian genomes to build a reference catalogue of genetic diversity for healthcare and research, so the answer is that it catalogues genetic variation across the Indian population.
Source: Department of Biotechnology, Government of India

48. Which of the following is a geostationary orbit characteristic?
(a) The satellite appears stationary relative to a point on the equator (b) The satellite passes over both poles every orbit (c) The orbital period is 90 minutes (d) The orbit lies at about 800 km altitude
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is the polar-orbit description — passing over both poles describes a polar sun-synchronous orbit (around 700–900 km, ~90–100 minute period), not a geostationary one. A geostationary satellite orbits at about 35,786 km above the equator with a 24-hour period, appearing fixed in the sky, so the answer is that the satellite appears stationary relative to a point on the equator.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Physics (Gravitation)

49. 'Green hydrogen' refers to hydrogen produced by:
(a) Gasification of coal (b) Steam reforming of natural gas (c) Electrolysis of water using renewable electricity (d) Burning biomass in open air
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is steam reforming of natural gas — that produces 'grey' hydrogen, the commonest industrial method, but it emits carbon dioxide. Green hydrogen is made by splitting water through electrolysis powered by renewable energy, as targeted under India's National Green Hydrogen Mission (2023), so the answer is electrolysis of water using renewable electricity.
Source: Ministry of New and Renewable Energy (National Green Hydrogen Mission, 2023)

50. Which of the following statements about the LIGO-India project is correct?
(a) It is a deep-sea exploration vessel (b) It is a lunar rover project of ISRO (c) It is a particle accelerator near Mumbai (d) It is a gravitational-wave observatory being set up in India in collaboration with the USA
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is the particle accelerator — LIGO detects gravitational waves using laser interferometry, not particle collisions. LIGO-India (approved in 2023, to be built in Maharashtra) will join the US LIGO detectors in observing gravitational waves, so the answer is that it is a gravitational-wave observatory being set up in India in collaboration with the USA.
Source: Department of Atomic Energy / LIGO-India official records (2023)
