# Advanced Science & Technology (उन्नत विज्ञान एवं प्रौद्योगिकी)

## The Hook: A Rocket That Landed Backwards

On 23 August 2023, India did something no nation had ever done — it soft-landed a spacecraft near the **south pole of the Moon**. Chandrayaan-3's Vikram lander touched down, and India became only the **fourth country** to soft-land on the Moon (after the USSR, USA, and China) and the **first** to land near the lunar south pole. The mission cost less than many Hollywood movies. That is the spirit of Indian science and technology — frugal, precise, and exam-relevant.

Every competitive exam — SSC CGL, IBPS PO, State PCS — now asks questions from Science & Technology (विज्ञान एवं प्रौद्योगिकी). The good news: the facts are static, finite, and highly predictable. Master this chapter once, revise it three times, and these marks are yours.

## Part 1: India's Space Programme

### 1.1 The Foundations

- **ISRO** (Indian Space Research Organisation) — formed **15 August 1969**, headquarters **Bengaluru**. It superseded INCOSPAR (1962), set up under **Dr. Vikram Sarabhai**, the "Father of the Indian Space Programme."
- First Indian satellite: **Aryabhata** (1975) — launched by the **USSR**, not by India. Classic trap!
- First Indian launch vehicle: **SLV-3** (1980) — placed the **Rohini** satellite in orbit. Project director: **Dr. A.P.J. Abdul Kalam**.
- **Satish Dhawan Space Centre (SDSC), Sriharikota** (Andhra Pradesh) — India's main launch port.
- **Thumba** (Kerala) — where it all began; the first sounding rocket was launched in **1963**. TERLS (Thumba Equatorial Rocket Launching Station) is here.

🧠 **Active Recall:** Who launched Aryabhata — India or the USSR? Which vehicle put Rohini in orbit, and who led that project?

### 1.2 Launch Vehicles — The Workhorses

| Vehicle | Type | Key Point |
|---|---|---|
| PSLV | Polar Satellite Launch Vehicle | ISRO's "workhorse"; famous for **104 satellites in one launch (2017)** — a world record at the time |
| GSLV | Geosynchronous Satellite Launch Vehicle | Uses an indigenous **cryogenic** upper stage — the **CE-7.5** engine powers the GSLV Mk II |
| LVM3 (GSLV Mk III) | Heaviest Indian rocket | Uses the more powerful **CE-20** cryogenic engine; launched **Chandrayaan-2 and Chandrayaan-3**; chosen for **Gaganyaan** |
| SSLV | Small Satellite Launch Vehicle | For small payloads, quick turnaround |

**Mnemonic — "P-G-L-S" = "Please Go Launch Satellites"** (PSLV → GSLV → LVM3 → SSLV). Note: this is just a memory aid for the four vehicle names — it is **not** an order of lifting power (SSLV actually carries the smallest payloads, while LVM3 is the heaviest lifter).

### 1.3 Landmark Missions

- **Chandrayaan-1 (2008)**: PSLV launch; carried the **Moon Impact Probe**; its instruments helped confirm **water molecules on the Moon**.
- **Mars Orbiter Mission / Mangalyaan (2013)**: India became the **first Asian nation** to reach Mars orbit and the **first nation in the world to do so in its first attempt**.
- **Chandrayaan-2 (2019)**: Orbiter succeeded; the Vikram lander crashed during descent.
- **Chandrayaan-3 (2023)**: Successful soft landing near the lunar south pole. Lander **Vikram**, rover **Pragyan**. Landing site named **Shiv Shakti Point**.
- **Aditya-L1 (2023)**: India's first mission to study the **Sun**, placed at **Lagrange Point 1** (~1.5 million km from Earth).
- **Gaganyaan**: India's upcoming **human spaceflight** mission; four astronaut-designates (IAF test pilots) announced in 2024.

🧠 **Active Recall:** Which mission reached Mars in the first attempt? What is Aditya-L1, and where does it sit?

### 1.4 Navigation & Others

- **NavIC** (Navigation with Indian Constellation), earlier **IRNSS** — India's own regional navigation system, **7 satellites**, covering India and ~1,500 km beyond. Compare: GPS (USA), GLONASS (Russia), Galileo (EU), BeiDou (China).
- **IN-SPACe** — nodal agency to promote private sector participation in space (HQ: Ahmedabad).
- **NSIL** (NewSpace India Ltd) — ISRO's commercial arm.

## Part 2: Nuclear Science & Energy

### 2.1 The Basics

- **Nuclear fission**: a heavy nucleus (Uranium-235, Plutonium-239) splits → energy. Used in reactors and atom bombs.
- **Nuclear fusion**: light nuclei (hydrogen isotopes) join → energy. Powers the **Sun**; basis of the hydrogen bomb.
- **Homi J. Bhabha** — Father of the Indian nuclear programme. **BARC** (Bhabha Atomic Research Centre), Mumbai, is named after him.
- India's first nuclear reactor: **Apsara (1956)** — also Asia's first (outside the USSR). Located at BARC.
- **Three-stage nuclear power programme** (Bhabha's vision): Stage 1 — Pressurised Heavy Water Reactors (natural uranium) → Stage 2 — Fast Breeder Reactors (plutonium) → Stage 3 — Thorium-based reactors. India holds among the world's largest **thorium** reserves (monazite sands of the Kerala coast).

### 2.2 Key Facts

- **NPCIL** (Nuclear Power Corporation of India Ltd) operates India's nuclear power plants.
- Major plants: **Tarapur** (Maharashtra — India's first nuclear power station, 1969), **Kudankulam** (Tamil Nadu — largest, built with Russian collaboration), Kalpakkam (Tamil Nadu — Prototype Fast Breeder Reactor), Rawatbhata (Rajasthan), Narora (UP), Kakrapar (Gujarat).
- **Pokhran tests**: Pokhran-I ("Smiling Buddha", **1974**) — peaceful nuclear explosion; Pokhran-II ("Operation Shakti", **1998**) — five tests, after which India declared itself a nuclear-weapon state.
- India follows a **"No First Use"** nuclear doctrine.

**Mnemonic — "T-K-K-R-N-K" for plants:** "**T**arapur **K**udankulam **K**alpakkam **R**awatbhata **N**arora **K**akrapar" — remember "Two Kings Keep Royal Nuclear Keys."

🧠 **Active Recall:** What was Smiling Buddha? Which stage of Bhabha's programme uses thorium?

## Part 3: Defence Technology

### 3.1 Missiles — IGMDP

The **Integrated Guided Missile Development Programme (IGMDP)** was launched in **1983** under **Dr. A.P.J. Abdul Kalam** ("Missile Man of India"). It produced five missile systems:

| Missile | Type | Range Class |
|---|---|---|
| **Prithvi** | Surface-to-surface, short range | 150–350 km |
| **Agni** | Surface-to-surface ballistic | Agni-V: 5,000+ km (ICBM class) |
| **Akash** | Surface-to-air | ~25–30 km |
| **Nag** | Anti-tank guided | ~4 km |
| **Trishul** | Short-range surface-to-air | ~9 km |

**Mnemonic — "P-A-A-N-T":** "**P**rithvi **A**gni **A**kash **N**ag **T**rishul" — "PAAN, the five-leaf missile bouquet."

- **BrahMos**: Indo-**Russian** joint venture supersonic **cruise** missile; name from **Brah**maputra + **Mos**kva rivers. Flies at about Mach 3. Can be launched from land, sea, and air.
- **Ballistic vs Cruise** (favourite trap!): Ballistic missiles follow a high arcing trajectory (like a thrown stone — Agni, Prithvi); cruise missiles fly low, powered throughout, like a small aircraft (BrahMos, Nirbhay).

### 3.2 Organisations & Systems

- **DRDO** (Defence Research and Development Organisation) — formed **1958**, HQ New Delhi.
- **INS Arihant** — India's first indigenous **nuclear-powered ballistic missile submarine (SSBN)**.
- **INS Vikrant** — India's first indigenously built **aircraft carrier** (commissioned 2022). Don't confuse with the older INS Vikrant (decommissioned 1997).
- **Tejas** — indigenous Light Combat Aircraft (LCA), built by HAL.
- **Agni-P (Agni Prime)**, **Pralay**, **Hypersonic Technology Demonstrator Vehicle (HSTDV)** — newer systems.

🧠 **Active Recall:** Is BrahMos ballistic or cruise? Which country partners India in BrahMos? What class of vessel is INS Arihant?

## Part 4: Biotechnology & Health

- **DNA** — Deoxyribonucleic acid; double helix structure described by **Watson and Crick (1953)** (using Rosalind Franklin's X-ray data).
- **Human Genome Project** — completed 2003; mapped all human genes.
- **Stem cells** — undifferentiated cells that can become specialised cells.
- **Vaccines**: **BCG** (tuberculosis — developed by Calmette and Guérin); **DPT** (diphtheria, pertussis, tetanus); **OPV** (oral polio vaccine — **Albert Sabin**); **Covaxin** — India's indigenous COVID-19 vaccine by **Bharat Biotech** with ICMR; **Covishield** — by Serum Institute of India (Oxford-AstraZeneca formula).
- **CERVAVAC** — India's first indigenously developed quadrivalent HPV vaccine (against cervical cancer), by Serum Institute of India.
- **Cloning**: **Dolly the sheep (1996)** — first mammal cloned from an adult somatic cell.
- **Bt Cotton** — the only GM crop approved for commercial cultivation in India.

## Part 5: IT, Emerging Tech & Institutions

- **Supercomputers**: India's **PARAM** series (built by **C-DAC**, Pune). First: **PARAM 8000 (1991)**. Recent systems include **PARAM Siddhi-AI** and machines under the **National Supercomputing Mission**.
- **Internet**: **Vint Cerf** is called the "Father of the Internet" (TCP/IP). The **WWW** was invented by **Tim Berners-Lee (1989)** at CERN.
- **5G** launched in India in **October 2022**.
- **Semiconductors**: India Semiconductor Mission (2021); chip fabrication plants approved in Gujarat (e.g., the Tata-PSMC fab at Dholera, approved 2024).
- **Quantum**: **National Quantum Mission** approved **2023** (~₹6,000 crore) — quantum computing, communication, sensing, materials.
- **AI**: **IndiaAI Mission** approved **2024**.
- **Key institutions**: **CSIR** (1942), **IITs** (first: IIT Kharagpur, 1951), **IISc Bengaluru** (1909), **TIFR** (1945, founded by Bhabha), **NITI Aayog** (2015, replaced the Planning Commission).

**Mnemonic — "Father figures":** Sarabhai = **Space**, Bhabha = **Bombs/Nuclear**, Kalam = **Missiles**, Vint Cerf = **Internet**, Berners-Lee = **Web**. Remember: "**S**pace **B**omb **K**alam **V**ery **B**right" (Sarabhai, Bhabha, Kalam, Vint, Berners-Lee).

🧠 **Active Recall:** Who built the PARAM series? In which year was the WWW invented, and by whom?

## ⚠️ Caution: Examiner's Trap

**Trap 1 — "लगभग सही कथन" (Almost-true statement):** "Aryabhata was India's first satellite, launched by ISRO in 1975." Sounds perfect — but Aryabhata was launched by the **USSR** (from Kapustin Yar), not by India. India's own first launch was **SLV-3/Rohini (1980)**. Examiners swap the launcher to catch the half-prepared.

**Trap 2 — "समान नाम/वर्ष" (Similar names/dates):** **Chandrayaan-2 vs Chandrayaan-3** — both had a lander named Vikram and rover Pragyan, but only Chandrayaan-3 (2023) landed successfully. Similarly, **INS Vikrant** (old, decommissioned 1997) vs **INS Vikrant** (new indigenous carrier, 2022). Always check which one the question means.

**Trap 3 — "विपरीत कार्य-कारण / श्रेणी भ्रम" (Category reversal):** "BrahMos is a ballistic missile developed entirely by India." Two errors: BrahMos is a **cruise** missile, and it is an **Indo-Russian** joint venture. Examiners love flipping ballistic ↔ cruise and "indigenous" ↔ "joint venture."
