126. Consider the following statements about the Chandrayaan-3 mission:
1. Its propulsion module carried the SHAPE payload for Earth observation.
2. The Vikram lander successfully performed a "hop experiment" on the lunar surface.
3. The mission landed near the lunar north pole.
Which of the statements given above is/are correct?
(a) 2 and 3 only (b) 1, 2 and 3 (c) 1 and 3 only (d) 1 and 2 only
Answer: (d)
Solution: Statement 3 is the trap — Chandrayaan-3 landed near the lunar SOUTH pole (Shiv Shakti Point, about 69°S), not the north pole; examiners swap the poles. SHAPE was indeed on the propulsion module and Vikram hopped about 40 cm and landed again, so the answer is 1 and 2 only.
Source: ISRO official mission updates, 2023

127. With reference to India's semiconductor ecosystem, consider the following:
1. The India Semiconductor Mission was launched under the Ministry of Electronics and IT.
2. India's first semiconductor fab approved under the mission is being set up at Dholera, Gujarat, by Tata Electronics in partnership with PSMC of Taiwan.
3. Silicon is the only material permitted for use in fabs approved under the mission.
Which of the statements given above is/are correct?
(a) 1 and 2 only (b) 2 only (c) 1 and 3 only (d) 1, 2 and 3
Answer: (a)
Solution: Statement 3 is fabricated — the scheme also covers compound semiconductors (gallium nitride, silicon carbide) and ATMP/OSAT units, not silicon alone. ISM is under MeitY and the Tata-PSMC fab at Dholera was approved in 2024, so the answer is 1 and 2 only.
Source: MeitY / India Semiconductor Mission, 2024

128. The term "Event Horizon", sometimes in news, is best associated with:
(a) The boundary around a black hole beyond which nothing, not even light, can escape (b) The boundary of the observable universe beyond which light has not yet reached us (c) The outermost layer of the Sun's corona visible during a total eclipse (d) The altitude at which the International Space Station orbits the Earth
Answer: (a)
Solution: The observable-universe boundary is the tempting trap — that is the particle horizon, a different concept; examiners exploit the word "horizon". The event horizon marks the point of no return around a black hole, imaged for M87* by the Event Horizon Telescope in 2019, so the answer is the boundary around a black hole beyond which nothing, not even light, can escape.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Physics / EHT Collaboration

130. With reference to CRISPR-Cas9, consider the following statements:
1. It uses a guide RNA to direct the Cas9 protein to a specific DNA sequence.
2. Emmanuelle Charpentier and Jennifer Doudna received the Nobel Prize in Chemistry for its development.
3. It can edit only somatic cells and cannot modify germline cells.
Which of the statements given above is/are correct?
(a) 1, 2 and 3 (b) 1 only (c) 1 and 2 only (d) 2 and 3 only
Answer: (c)
Solution: Statement 3 is the trap — CRISPR can technically edit germline cells and embryos (the 2018 He Jiankui case proved this); the restriction is regulatory, not technical. Guide RNA targeting and the 2020 Chemistry Nobel to Charpentier and Doudna are correct, so the answer is 1 and 2 only.
Source: Nobel Prize official website, 2020

131. The "NISAR" satellite, a joint mission of ISRO and NASA, is designed primarily to:
(a) Map Earth's land and ice surfaces using dual-frequency synthetic aperture radar (b) Provide navigation signals as a replacement for NavIC (c) Study the Sun's corona and solar wind (d) Detect gravitational waves from merging neutron stars

Solution: The solar-study option is the trap — that describes Aditya-L1; examiners pair recent ISRO missions with each other's objectives. NISAR carries L-band and S-band radar to observe ecosystems, ice mass and hazards, so the answer is mapping Earth's land and ice surfaces using dual-frequency synthetic aperture radar.
Source: NASA/ISRO official mission pages

133. Which of the following correctly describes "quantum supremacy"?
(a) A quantum computer breaking all existing encryption standards (b) A quantum computer performing a computation that is practically infeasible for classical supercomputers (c) A quantum computer operating at absolute zero temperature (d) A quantum network transmitting data faster than light
Answer: (b)
Solution: The encryption-breaking option is the common media exaggeration — no encryption standard has been broken — and faster-than-light transfer violates relativity. Quantum supremacy, claimed by Google's Sycamore in 2019, means a quantum device solving a task infeasible for classical machines, so the answer is a computation practically infeasible for classical supercomputers.
Source: Nature (Google AI Quantum, 2019)

139. The term "dark matter" is best described as matter that:
(a) Does not emit, absorb or reflect electromagnetic radiation but exerts gravitational effects (b) Exists only in the cores of dead stars (c) Is composed entirely of black holes (d) Emits light only in the infrared spectrum
Answer: (a)
Solution: The black-hole option is the trap — primordial black holes were once hypothesised as dark matter candidates, but observations rule them out as the dominant component; examiners present a discarded hypothesis as fact. Dark matter is inferred from galaxy rotation curves and gravitational lensing, so the answer is matter that does not emit, absorb or reflect electromagnetic radiation but exerts gravitational effects.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Physics / CERN

145. Which of the following best explains why a "qubit" is fundamentally different from a classical bit?
(a) A qubit stores data magnetically while a bit stores it electrically (b) A qubit processes data faster because it is made of superconductors (c) A qubit can store two bits of classical information permanently (d) A qubit can exist in a superposition of 0 and 1 states simultaneously until measured
Answer: (d)
Solution: The two-bits option is the trap — Holevo's bound shows one qubit cannot reliably carry more than one classical bit of extractable information; examiners misuse "superposition means two values stored". The defining feature is superposition along with entanglement, so the answer is that a qubit can exist in a superposition of 0 and 1 states simultaneously until measured.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Physics / IBM Quantum documentation

(a) 1 and 3 only (b) 2 only (c) 1 and 2 only (d) 1, 2 and 3
