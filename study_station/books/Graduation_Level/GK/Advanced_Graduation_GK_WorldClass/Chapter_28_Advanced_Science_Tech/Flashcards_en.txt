Card 1
Front: Which Indian missile uses a cryogenic upper stage, and what is a cryogenic engine's basic fuel combination?
Back: GSLV (Geosynchronous Satellite Launch Vehicle) uses a cryogenic upper stage. A cryogenic engine burns liquid hydrogen (fuel) with liquid oxygen (oxidiser), both stored at extremely low temperatures.

Card 2
Front: What does "NavIC" stand for, and how many satellites make up its operational constellation?
Back: Navigation with Indian Constellation — India's regional navigation system (earlier IRNSS). It has 7 satellites: 3 in geostationary orbit and 4 in inclined geosynchronous orbit.

Card 3
Front: Memory trick — how can you instantly recall the 7 NavIC satellites' orbit split?
Back: Remember "3 stand still, 4 tilt": 3 GEO (fixed over one point) + 4 GSO (inclined, figure-of-8 ground track). "3-4 = 7" completes the constellation.

Card 4
Front: Examiner's trap — "Chandrayaan-2's lander Vikram successfully soft-landed on the Moon." True or false?
Back: False. Chandrayaan-2's orbiter works fine, but lander Vikram crash-landed in September 2019. The successful soft landing was Chandrayaan-3 (23 August 2023) — examiners swap the mission numbers to trap you.

Card 5
Front: What is the difference between 5G's "latency" and "bandwidth"?
Back: Bandwidth = how much data flows per second (speed); latency = the delay before data starts moving (responsiveness). 5G targets latency as low as ~1 millisecond, crucial for remote surgery and autonomous vehicles.

Card 6
Front: Which organisation developed India's COVID-19 vaccine Covaxin, and what type of vaccine is it?
Back: Bharat Biotech (with ICMR-NIV). It is an inactivated (killed virus) vaccine — unlike Covishield, which is a viral-vector vaccine made by Serum Institute of India with Oxford-AstraZeneca.

Card 7
Front: What is "quantum supremacy" in simple terms?
Back: The point where a quantum computer solves a problem that no classical supercomputer could solve in any practical time. Quantum computers use qubits, which can be 0 and 1 simultaneously (superposition).

Card 8
Front: Memory trick — how to remember the order of India's three-stage nuclear power programme?
Back: "Pressurised → Fast → Thorium" = "P-F-T" or "Please Fasten Tightly". Stage 1: PHWR (natural uranium), Stage 2: Fast Breeder Reactors (plutonium), Stage 3: Thorium-based reactors.

Card 9
Front: Examiner's trap — "DRDO developed the Akash missile; it is a surface-to-air missile." Which similar-sounding missile is air-to-air, and why do exams confuse them?
Back: Astra is India's beyond-visual-range air-to-air missile. Examiners pair "Akash" with "air-to-air" because both start with 'A' and relate to the sky — but Akash is surface-to-air (ground-launched, hits aircraft).

Card 10
Front: What is CRISPR-Cas9, and who won the Nobel Prize for it?
Back: A gene-editing tool that cuts DNA at precise locations ("molecular scissors"). Emmanuelle Charpentier and Jennifer Doudna won the 2020 Nobel Prize in Chemistry for developing it.

Card 11
Front: What does "Li-Fi" use to transmit data, and how is it different from Wi-Fi?
Back: Li-Fi uses visible light (LED intensity modulation) instead of radio waves. It can be faster and more secure (light can't pass through walls), but needs line of sight.

Card 12
Front: Which Indian supercomputer series is built indigenously under the National Supercomputing Mission, and name one?
Back: The PARAM series (by C-DAC). Examples: PARAM Siddhi-AI, PARAM Shivay (IIT-BHU), PARAM Shakti (IIT Kharagpur).

Card 13
Front: What is the "Internet of Things" (IoT) in one line, with one everyday example?
Back: Everyday physical objects embedded with sensors and internet connectivity that collect and exchange data — e.g., a smart meter sending electricity usage directly to the power company.

Card 14
Front: What is blockchain's core feature that makes records tamper-resistant?
Back: Data is stored in cryptographically linked blocks distributed across many computers; altering one block breaks the chain and is rejected by the network. It is decentralised — no single authority controls it.

Card 15
Front: What is the difference between AI, Machine Learning, and Deep Learning?
Back: AI = broad goal of machines mimicking intelligence. Machine Learning = a subset of AI where systems learn from data. Deep Learning = a subset of ML using multi-layered neural networks (inspired by the human brain).

Card 16
Front: Memory trick — how to recall the full form sequence of ISRO's launch vehicles from smallest to largest?
Back: "S-P-G-L": SLV → ASLV → PSLV → GSLV → LVM3. Think "Small People Grow Large" — capacity rises from ~40 kg (SLV) to 4+ tonnes to GTO (LVM3).

Card 17
Front: What is "green hydrogen," and how is it produced?
Back: Hydrogen produced by splitting water (electrolysis) using renewable electricity — zero carbon emissions. "Grey hydrogen" comes from natural gas; "blue" is grey plus carbon capture.

Card 18
Front: Examiner's trap — "Bharat Biotech's Covaxin is an mRNA vaccine." True or false, and which vaccine actually used mRNA technology?
Back: False. Covaxin is an inactivated-virus vaccine. mRNA vaccines were Pfizer-BioNTech and Moderna. Examiners swap vaccine platforms because all were in the news together.

Card 19
Front: What is the "Aditya-L1" mission, and where is it parked?
Back: India's first solar observation mission (ISRO, launched September 2023). It sits at the Lagrange Point L1, about 1.5 million km from Earth toward the Sun, giving an uninterrupted view of the Sun.

Card 20
Front: What does "LVM3" stand for, and which major missions has it launched?
Back: Launch Vehicle Mark-3 (formerly GSLV Mk-III) — ISRO's heaviest rocket with a cryogenic upper stage. It launched Chandrayaan-2, Chandrayaan-3, and OneWeb satellite batches.
