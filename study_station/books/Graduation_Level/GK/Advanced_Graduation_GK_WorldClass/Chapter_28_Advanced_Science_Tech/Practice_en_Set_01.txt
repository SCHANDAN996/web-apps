1. What does the term 'CRISPR-Cas9' primarily refer to?
(a) A gene-editing technology (b) A supercomputer developed by India (c) A satellite navigation system (d) A type of nuclear reactor
Answer: (a)
Solution: The tempting trap is the supercomputer option, since India has the PARAM series, but CRISPR has nothing to do with computing. CRISPR-Cas9 is a gene-editing tool derived from a bacterial defence mechanism that cuts DNA at precise locations, so the answer is a gene-editing technology.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Biology

2. India's first indigenously developed aircraft carrier, commissioned in 2022, is:
(a) INS Arihant (b) INS Vikrant (c) INS Viraat (d) INS Vikramaditya
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is INS Vikramaditya, which sounds similar but is a Russian-origin carrier, while INS Arihant is a nuclear submarine, not a carrier. INS Vikrant (IAC-1) was built at Cochin Shipyard and commissioned in September 2022, so the answer is INS Vikrant.
Source: PYQ-style

3. The 'Chandrayaan-3' mission achieved a soft landing near which region of the Moon?
(a) North Pole (b) South Polar region (c) Equatorial region (d) Far side of the Moon
Answer: (b)
Solution: The far side option is the trap — that feat belongs to China's Chang'e-4, not Chandrayaan-3. The Vikram lander touched down near the lunar south polar region on 23 August 2023, a world first, so the answer is the South Polar region.
Source: PYQ-style

4. Which of the following is India's first solar mission?
(a) AstroSat (b) Mangalyaan (c) Chandrayaan-2 (d) Aditya-L1
Answer: (d)
Solution: AstroSat is the trap — it is an astronomy observatory, not a solar mission, while Mangalyaan went to Mars. Aditya-L1, launched in September 2023, studies the Sun from the Lagrange point L1, so the answer is Aditya-L1.
Source: PYQ-style

5. 'Blockchain' technology is best described as:
(a) A centralised banking database (b) A social media algorithm (c) A type of computer virus (d) A distributed, immutable digital ledger
Answer: (d)
Solution: The centralised database option is the trap because blockchain is precisely the opposite — decentralised and distributed. Its records (blocks) are cryptographically linked and cannot be altered retroactively, so the answer is a distributed, immutable digital ledger.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Computer Science (supplementary)

6. The Gaganyaan mission of ISRO aims to:
(a) Demonstrate human spaceflight capability (b) Send an unmanned probe to Venus (c) Build a permanent space station (d) Land a rover on Mars
Answer: (a)
Solution: The Venus probe option is the trap — that mission is Shukrayaan, a different ISRO proposal. Gaganyaan is ISRO's programme to carry Indian astronauts to low Earth orbit, so the answer is to demonstrate human spaceflight capability.
Source: PYQ-style

7. Which organisation developed the COVID-19 vaccine 'Covaxin' in collaboration with ICMR?
(a) Serum Institute of India (b) Dr. Reddy's Laboratories (c) Bharat Biotech (d) Biocon
Answer: (c)
Solution: The Serum Institute is the classic trap — it produced Covishield (Oxford-AstraZeneca), not Covaxin. Covaxin was developed by Bharat Biotech with ICMR-NIV, so the answer is Bharat Biotech.
Source: PYQ-style

8. 5G technology primarily differs from 4G in terms of:
(a) Only better voice call quality (b) Use of satellite phones only (c) Higher speed, lower latency and greater device connectivity (d) Elimination of mobile towers
Answer: (c)
Solution: The tower-elimination option is the trap — 5G actually needs more small cells, not fewer towers. 5G offers much higher data speeds, latency near 1 millisecond, and massive IoT connectivity, so the answer is higher speed, lower latency and greater device connectivity.
Source: PYQ-style

9. The term 'Li-Fi' refers to data transmission through:
(a) Sound waves (b) Radio waves (c) Visible light (d) Magnetic fields
Answer: (c)
Solution: Radio waves is the standard contrast trap — that is how Wi-Fi works, not Li-Fi. Li-Fi (Light Fidelity) uses visible light from LEDs for high-speed data transmission, so the answer is visible light.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Physics (EM spectrum applications)

10. India's first indigenous mRNA COVID-19 vaccine was developed by:
(a) Gennova Biopharmaceuticals (b) Panacea Biotec (c) Biological E (d) Zydus Cadila
Answer: (a)
Solution: Zydus Cadila is the trap — its ZyCoV-D is a DNA vaccine, and the DNA versus mRNA distinction is the examiner's favourite confusion here. Gennova's GEMCOVAC-19 was India's first indigenous mRNA vaccine, so the answer is Gennova Biopharmaceuticals.
Source: PYQ-style

11. What is 'quantum computing' fundamentally based on?
(a) Classical binary bits only (b) Optical fibre transmission (c) Qubits that can exist in superposition (d) Vacuum tube technology
Answer: (c)
Solution: The classical-bits option is the trap because that describes ordinary computers, where a bit is strictly 0 or 1. Quantum computers use qubits that exploit superposition and entanglement, so the answer is qubits that can exist in superposition.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Physics (modern physics concepts)

12. The 'NavIC' system of India is related to:
(a) Weather forecasting (b) Naval warfare (c) Satellite-based navigation (d) Deep-sea exploration
Answer: (c)
Solution: The 'Nav' prefix tempts the naval-warfare trap, but NavIC has nothing to do with the Navy. NavIC (Navigation with Indian Constellation), earlier called IRNSS, is India's regional satellite navigation system, so the answer is satellite-based navigation.
Source: PYQ-style

13. Which of the following orbits is used by India's geosynchronous communication satellites such as the GSAT series?
(a) LEO at about 400 km (b) MEO at about 8,000 km (c) GSO at about 36,000 km (d) Polar orbit at about 700 km
Answer: (c)
Solution: The polar orbit is the trap — remote-sensing satellites use polar orbits near 700 km, not communication satellites. Geosynchronous/geostationary orbits lie at roughly 35,786 km above the equator, so the answer is GSO at about 36,000 km.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Physics (gravitation)

14. Which of the following is a surface-to-surface ballistic missile developed under India's Integrated Guided Missile Development Programme?
(a) Tejas (b) Prithvi (c) Dhanush (d) Pinaka
Answer: (b)
Solution: The traps are all DRDO-linked but not ballistic missiles: Tejas is a fighter aircraft, Pinaka a rocket artillery system, and Dhanush an artillery gun. Prithvi is a surface-to-surface ballistic missile from the IGMDP, so the answer is Prithvi.
Source: PYQ-style

15. The term 'Internet of Things (IoT)' refers to:
(a) A faster version of the World Wide Web (b) A network of physical devices connected and exchanging data via the internet (c) A government database of citizens (d) A type of computer hardware
Answer: (b)
Solution: The 'faster web' option is the trap — IoT is not about browsing speed but about connected objects. It means everyday physical devices embedded with sensors and software that exchange data over the internet, so the answer is a network of physical devices connected and exchanging data via the internet.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Computer Science (supplementary)

16. India's first 3D-printed post office was inaugurated in 2023 in which city?
(a) Pune (b) Hyderabad (c) Bengaluru (d) Ahmedabad
Answer: (c)
Solution: Hyderabad is a tempting trap because it hosts many tech firsts, but this one is not among them. India's first 3D-printed post office opened in Bengaluru, Karnataka, in August 2023, built in about 43 days, so the answer is Bengaluru.
Source: PYQ-style

17. Which Indian institution is the nodal agency for the 'National Quantum Mission' approved in 2023?
(a) ISRO (b) CSIR (c) DRDO (d) Department of Science and Technology
Answer: (d)
Solution: ISRO and DRDO are traps because they handle space and defence respectively, not this mission. The National Quantum Mission (approved April 2023) is implemented by the Department of Science and Technology, so the answer is the Department of Science and Technology.
Source: PYQ-style

18. 'Green hydrogen' is produced by:
(a) Steam methane reforming (b) Biomass burning (c) Coal gasification (d) Electrolysis of water using renewable energy
Answer: (d)
Solution: Steam methane reforming is the trap — it yields 'grey' hydrogen, while coal gasification gives 'brown/black' hydrogen; the colour-coding confuses many. Green hydrogen comes from electrolysis powered by renewable energy, so the answer is electrolysis of water using renewable energy.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Chemistry (hydrogen)

19. The 'PARAM' series in India is associated with:
(a) Missiles (b) Supercomputers (c) Satellites (d) Submarines
Answer: (b)
Solution: The missile option is the trap because India's missile names (Prithvi, Agni) dominate defence news. PARAM is India's series of indigenous supercomputers developed by C-DAC, so the answer is supercomputers.
Source: PYQ-style

20. The INS Arihant class of submarines of the Indian Navy is powered by:
(a) Nuclear reactors (b) Diesel-electric engines only (c) Gas turbines (d) Solar-assisted batteries
Answer: (a)
Solution: The diesel-electric option is the trap — conventional submarines like the Kalvari class use diesel-electric propulsion, but Arihant does not. INS Arihant is a nuclear-powered ballistic missile submarine (SSBN), so the answer is nuclear reactors.
Source: PYQ-style

21. Consider the following statements about Chandrayaan missions:
1. Chandrayaan-1 discovered evidence of water molecules on the Moon.
2. Chandrayaan-2 was a sample-return mission.
3. Chandrayaan-3 carried a lander and a rover but no orbiter propulsion module for science payloads.
Which of the above is/are correct?
(a) 2 and 3 only (b) 1 and 2 only (c) 1, 2 and 3 (d) 1 only
Answer: (d)
Solution: Statement 2 is the trap — Chandrayaan-2 was an orbiter-lander-rover mission, not a sample-return mission; statement 3 is also wrong because Chandrayaan-3's propulsion module carried the SHAPE science payload. Only statement 1 is correct, so the answer is 1 only.
Source: PYQ-style

22. With reference to India's missile programmes, arrange the following in chronological order of their first successful tests:
1. Agni-I 2. Prithvi 3. BrahMos 4. Agni-V
(a) 1-2-3-4 (b) 2-3-1-4 (c) 2-1-3-4 (d) 1-3-2-4
Answer: (c)
Solution: The chronological-confusion trap is placing Agni before Prithvi because Agni is more famous. Prithvi was first tested in 1988, Agni in 1989, BrahMos in 2001, and Agni-V in 2012, so the answer is 2-1-3-4.
Source: PYQ-style

23. Which of the following pairs is/are correctly matched?
1. NISAR — India-US joint Earth observation satellite
2. XPoSat — India's X-ray polarimeter satellite
3. SSLV — India's heaviest launch vehicle
(a) 1 and 2 only (b) 2 and 3 only (c) 1, 2 and 3 (d) 1 and 3 only
Answer: (a)
Solution: Statement 3 is the trap — SSLV is the Small Satellite Launch Vehicle, while LVM3 (GSLV Mk III) is India's heaviest launch vehicle. NISAR is a NASA-ISRO joint mission and XPoSat is India's X-ray polarimetry satellite, so the answer is 1 and 2 only.
Source: PYQ-style

24. Consider the following statements about stem cells:
1. Embryonic stem cells are pluripotent.
2. Adult stem cells are totipotent.
3. Induced pluripotent stem cells can be generated from adult somatic cells.
Which of the above is/are correct?
(a) 1 only (b) 1 and 3 only (c) 2 and 3 only (d) 1, 2 and 3
Answer: (b)
Solution: Statement 2 is the trap — adult stem cells are multipotent, not totipotent; totipotency belongs to the zygote and early embryonic cells. Embryonic stem cells are pluripotent and iPSCs are made by reprogramming adult cells, so the answer is 1 and 3 only.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Biology

25. With reference to nuclear energy in India, which of the following is correct about the three-stage nuclear power programme?
(a) Stage 1 uses thorium directly in fast breeder reactors (b) Stage 3 uses enriched uranium in pressurised heavy water reactors (c) All stages use imported uranium only (d) Stage 2 uses plutonium from Stage 1 in fast breeder reactors
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is reversing the stages: Stage 1 uses natural uranium in PHWRs producing plutonium, and Stage 3 uses thorium-232 to breed uranium-233. Stage 2 uses the plutonium from Stage 1 in fast breeder reactors, so the answer is Stage 2 uses plutonium from Stage 1 in fast breeder reactors.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Physics (nuclear energy)
