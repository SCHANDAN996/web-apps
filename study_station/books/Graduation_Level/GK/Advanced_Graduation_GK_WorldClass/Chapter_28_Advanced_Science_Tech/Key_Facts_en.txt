# Key Facts & Figures — Advanced Science & Tech

## Part 1: High-Yield Facts

| Fact | Description | Examiner's Trap | Trap Type |
|---|---|---|---|
| ISRO established | 15 August 1969; founder-chairman Dr. Vikram Sarabhai; HQ Bengaluru | Confusing ISRO (1969) with INCOSPAR (1962) or DOS (1972) | Similar dates |
| First Indian satellite | Aryabhata (1975), launched by Soviet Union | Trap: calling it launched by India/PSLV | Near-correct statement |
| SLV-3 | First Indian-made launch vehicle; launched Rohini (1980); led by A.P.J. Abdul Kalam | Confusing SLV-3 with ASLV/PSLV | Similar names |
| PSLV | Workhorse rocket; first flight 1993; record 104 satellites in one launch (2017) | Trap: attributing 104-satellite record to GSLV | Wrong attribution |
| GSLV Mk III (LVM3) | Heaviest Indian rocket; used for Chandrayaan-2 & 3, Gaganyaan | Confusing GSLV (cryogenic) with PSLV (solid/liquid stages) | Technical confusion |
| Chandrayaan-1 (2008) | Discovered water molecules on Moon (via M3 instrument); carried Moon Impact Probe | Trap: crediting Chandrayaan-2 with water discovery | Chronology trap |
| Chandrayaan-3 (2023) | Soft landing near lunar south pole (23 Aug 2023); lander Vikram, rover Pragyan | Confusing lander/rover names with Chandrayaan-2 (same names) | Similar names |
| Aditya-L1 (2023) | India's first solar observation mission; placed at Lagrange point L1 | Trap: calling it a Moon mission or saying it orbits the Sun's surface | Concept confusion |
| Gaganyaan | India's first human spaceflight programme; Indian astronauts are called Gaganyatris | Trap: saying India already sent humans to space (Rakesh Sharma flew on Soviet Soyuz, 1984) | Near-correct statement |
| Rakesh Sharma | First Indian in space (1984), aboard Soviet Soyuz T-11 | Trap: calling him a NASA/ISRO mission astronaut | Wrong attribution |
| NISAR | Joint NASA–ISRO radar satellite | Trap: calling it purely ISRO or purely NASA | Attribution trap |
| Nuclear power regulator | AERB (Atomic Energy Regulatory Board), est. 1983 | Confusing AERB with DAE or NPCIL | Similar acronyms |
| First nuclear test | Pokhran-I, 1974 ("Smiling Buddha"); Pokhran-II, 1998 ("Operation Shakti") | Swapping the years or code names | Chronology trap |
| India's nuclear doctrine | No First Use (NFU); credible minimum deterrence | Trap: "first use if attacked by conventional weapons" | Negative/near-correct statement |
| Three-stage nuclear programme | Homi Bhabha's plan: PHWR → Fast Breeder → Thorium-based reactors | Trap: saying stage 3 uses uranium (it uses thorium) | Technical confusion |
| NPCIL | Operates India's nuclear power plants; HQ Mumbai | Confusing NPCIL with BARC (research) or UCIL (mining) | Similar acronyms |
| Largest nuclear power station | Kudankulam (Tamil Nadu), built with Russian cooperation | Trap: Tarapur (oldest, Maharashtra) vs Kudankulam (largest) | Superlative confusion |
| DRDO | Established 1958; HQ New Delhi; develops missiles, radars, defence systems | Confusing DRDO (R&D) with Ordnance Factories (production) | Role confusion |
| Integrated Guided Missile Programme | Led by A.P.J. Abdul Kalam; produced Agni, Prithvi, Akash, Nag, Trishul | Trap: including BrahMos in IGMDP (it is an Indo-Russian JV, 1998) | Membership trap |
| Agni-V | Longest-range Indian missile (ICBM-class, 5,000+ km); nuclear-capable | Trap: calling Agni-V a cruise missile (it is ballistic) | Category confusion |
| BrahMos | Indo-Russian supersonic cruise missile; named after Brahmaputra + Moskva rivers | Trap: calling it ballistic or fully indigenous | Category/attribution trap |
| INS Arihant | India's first indigenous nuclear-powered ballistic missile submarine (SSBN) | Confusing with INS Chakra (leased Russian nuclear sub) or INS Vikrant (aircraft carrier) | Similar names |
| INS Vikrant (IAC-1) | First indigenously built aircraft carrier, commissioned 2022 | Confusing with old INS Vikrant (decommissioned 1997) | Same-name trap |
| LCA Tejas | Indigenous light combat aircraft; developed by ADA/HAL | Trap: calling it a joint venture or imported design | Attribution trap |
| National Supercomputing Mission | Deploys indigenous supercomputers built by C-DAC (PARAM series) across academic and research institutions | Confusing the mission's indigenous PARAM systems with imported weather supercomputers | Attribution trap |
| Pratyush & Mihir | High-performance computers dedicated to weather and climate research, under the Ministry of Earth Sciences (MoES) | Trap: placing them under the National Supercomputing Mission or under ISRO | Attribution trap |
| First Indian supercomputer | PARAM 8000 (1991), developed by C-DAC, Pune | Trap: attributing it to ISRO or DRDO | Wrong attribution |
| Quantum mission | National Quantum Mission approved 2023 (quantum computing, communication, sensing) | Trap: confusing with National Mission on Interdisciplinary Cyber-Physical Systems | Similar names |
| 5G in India | Launched October 2022; spectrum bands include 600 MHz–26 GHz | Trap: saying India developed 5G hardware fully indigenously | Overstatement trap |
| NavIC (IRNSS) | India's regional navigation system; 7 satellites; covers India + 1,500 km beyond | Trap: calling it global (GPS is global; NavIC is regional) | Near-correct statement |
| GPS vs NavIC vs GLONASS vs Galileo vs BeiDou | USA, India, Russia, EU, China respectively | Swapping country–system pairs | Matching trap |
| BIRAC | Biotechnology Industry Research Assistance Council — supports biotech startups | Confusing BIRAC with DBT (parent dept.) or ICMR (medical research) | Similar acronyms |
| First COVID-19 vaccine (indigenous) | Covaxin — Bharat Biotech + ICMR/NIV; Covishield — Serum Institute (Oxford-AstraZeneca licence) | Trap: calling Covishield fully indigenous | Attribution trap |
| Human Genome Project | Completed 2003; India launched Genome India Project (2020) | Confusing global HGP with Genome India Project | Chronology trap |
| Stem cell research regulator | ICMR guidelines; DBT-ICMR joint guidelines | Trap: saying stem cell therapy is fully approved for all uses | Overstatement trap |
| Green Revolution father (India) | M.S. Swaminathan; world: Norman Borlaug | Swapping the two names | Similar-name trap |
| White Revolution | Verghese Kurien (Operation Flood, Amul) | Confusing with Green Revolution figures | Cross-domain trap |
| NITI Aayog | Replaced Planning Commission (2015); promotes innovation via Atal Innovation Mission | Trap: saying NITI Aayog allocates funds like Planning Commission | Role confusion |
| Atal Tinkering Labs | Under Atal Innovation Mission, NITI Aayog | Trap: attributing to Ministry of Education directly | Attribution trap |
| Semiconductor mission | India Semiconductor Mission (2021) under MeitY | Trap: placing it under Ministry of Heavy Industries | Ministry confusion |
| National AI Mission / INDIAai | Under MeitY; AI research and skilling | Confusing with NITI Aayog's AI strategy paper (2018) | Attribution trap |
| C-DAC | Centre for Development of Advanced Computing, est. 1988, Pune | Confusing with CDAC Noida or NIC | Similar acronyms |
| IN-SPACe | Regulator/promoter for private space sector (2020), HQ Ahmedabad | Trap: saying it replaced ISRO | Role confusion |
| NSIL | NewSpace India Ltd — commercial arm of ISRO (2019) | Confusing NSIL with IN-SPACe (commercial vs regulator) | Similar names |
| Antrix Corporation | ISRO's older commercial arm (1992) | Trap: saying Antrix still handles all commercial launches | Outdated-fact trap |
| BARC | Bhabha Atomic Research Centre, Mumbai; founded by Homi Bhabha (as AEET, 1954) | Confusing BARC (research) with NPCIL (power plants) | Role confusion |
| Homi J. Bhabha | Father of Indian nuclear programme; first chairman, Atomic Energy Commission | Trap: crediting Vikram Sarabhai (space) with nuclear programme | Cross-domain trap |
| C.V. Raman | Nobel Prize 1930 (Physics) — Raman Effect; National Science Day: 28 February | Trap: wrong year or wrong field | Fact-swap trap |
| Indian Nobel laureates in science | C.V. Raman (Physics), Har Gobind Khorana (Medicine), Venkatraman Ramakrishnan (Chemistry) | Including Amartya Sen (Economics) in "science Nobel" lists | Category trap |
| CSIR | Council of Scientific & Industrial Research, est. 1942; largest R&D organisation | Confusing CSIR with ICAR (agriculture) or ICMR (medical) | Similar acronyms |
| National Science Day | 28 February (Raman Effect discovery, 1928) | Trap: confusing with National Technology Day (11 May — Pokhran-II) | Date-swap trap |
| National Technology Day | 11 May (Pokhran-II, 1998; also first flight of Hansa aircraft) | Swapping with Science Day | Date-swap trap |

## Part 2: Time Management (15/45-Second Rule)

| Topic | Type | Time | Score Maximizer? |
|---|---|---|---|
| ISRO missions, satellites, launch vehicles | Fact-based | 15 sec | ✅ Yes |
| Missiles (Agni, Prithvi, BrahMos, Akash, Nag) | Fact-based | 15 sec | ✅ Yes |
| Nuclear programme (tests, reactors, doctrine) | Fact-based | 15 sec | ✅ Yes |
| Space agencies & their countries (NASA, ESA, Roscosmos, CNSA, JAXA) | Fact-based | 15 sec | ✅ Yes |
| Navigation systems (NavIC, GPS, GLONASS, Galileo, BeiDou) | Fact-based | 15 sec | ✅ Yes |
| Science days, awards, Nobel laureates | Fact-based | 15 sec | ✅ Yes |
| Organisations & acronyms (DRDO, BARC, CSIR, IN-SPACe, NSIL) | Fact-based | 15 sec | ✅ Yes |
| Statement-based questions on nuclear doctrine / space policy | Analytical | 45 sec | ❌ No |
| Matching questions (missile–type, satellite–purpose, scientist–contribution) | Analytical | 45 sec | ❌ No |
| Chronology questions (order of missions/tests) | Analytical | 45 sec | ❌ No |
| Assertion–reason on technology applications (biotech, AI, quantum) | Analytical | 45 sec | ❌ No |

**Strategy:** Attempt all 15-second fact questions first — they are pure recall and high-accuracy. Return to 45-second analytical questions only after securing the fact-based marks.
