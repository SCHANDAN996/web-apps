51. Which Article of the Indian Constitution deals with the impeachment of the President?
(a) Article 56 (b) Article 61 (c) Article 72 (d) Article 74
Answer: (b)
Solution: Article 56 is the tempting wrong choice because it deals with the President's term of office and resignation, which students often confuse with removal. Impeachment of the President for violation of the Constitution is specifically provided under Article 61, so the answer is Article 61.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Political Science (Indian Constitution at Work)

52. The 42nd Constitutional Amendment Act, 1976 added which of the following words to the Preamble?
(a) Socialist, Secular, Integrity (b) Socialist, Secular, Fraternity (c) Sovereign, Secular, Integrity (d) Democratic, Secular, Unity
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Fraternity" is the trap because it was already in the original Preamble of 1950, not added in 1976. The 42nd Amendment added three words — Socialist, Secular and Integrity — so the answer is Socialist, Secular, Integrity.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Political Science (Indian Constitution at Work)

53. Under which Article can the Parliament amend the Constitution?
(a) Article 356 (b) Article 360 (c) Article 368 (d) Article 370
Answer: (c)
Solution: Article 356 (President's Rule) and Article 360 (Financial Emergency) are traps as they deal with emergencies, not amendment. The power and procedure of constitutional amendment lies in Article 368, so the answer is Article 368.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Political Science (Indian Constitution at Work)

54. The concept of 'Procedure Established by Law' in Article 21 was borrowed from the Constitution of which country?
(a) Ireland (b) USA (c) UK (d) Japan
Answer: (d)
Solution: USA is the tempting wrong answer because the American Constitution uses "due process of law," which sounds similar but is a different doctrine. The expression "procedure established by law" in Article 21 was taken from the Japanese Constitution, so the answer is Japan.
Source: M. Laxmikanth, Indian Polity

55. Which of the following is NOT a condition for the President's office under the Constitution?
(a) He should not be a member of either House of Parliament (b) He should not hold any office of profit (c) He is entitled to use his official residence without payment of rent (d) He must be a member of the Rajya Sabha at the time of election
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is that students assume the President must belong to some House like the Prime Minister must. In fact, a sitting member of Parliament or a state legislature must vacate the seat upon entering the President's office; membership of the Rajya Sabha is not required, so the answer is "He must be a member of the Rajya Sabha at the time of election."
Source: M. Laxmikanth, Indian Polity

56. The minimum age prescribed for election as a member of the Rajya Sabha is:
(a) 21 years (b) 25 years (c) 30 years (d) 35 years
Answer: (c)
Solution: 25 years is the trap because it is the minimum age for the Lok Sabha and State Assemblies, and students mix the two. For the Rajya Sabha the Constitution prescribes 30 years, so the answer is 30 years.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Political Science (Indian Constitution at Work)

57. Who presides over a joint sitting of the two Houses of Parliament summoned under Article 108?
(a) The President (b) The Vice-President (c) The Speaker of the Lok Sabha (d) The Chief Justice of India
Answer: (c)
Solution: The President is the tempting wrong option because he summons the joint sitting, but summoning is not presiding. Article 118(4) provides that the Speaker of the Lok Sabha presides over a joint sitting, so the answer is the Speaker of the Lok Sabha.
Source: M. Laxmikanth, Indian Polity

58. The Fundamental Duties were incorporated in the Constitution on the recommendation of which committee?
(a) Swaran Singh Committee (b) Shah Commission (c) Sarkaria Commission (d) Punchhi Commission
Answer: (a)
Solution: Sarkaria and Punchhi Commissions are traps as both dealt with Centre–State relations, not duties. The Swaran Singh Committee recommended Fundamental Duties, added by the 42nd Amendment in 1976, so the answer is the Swaran Singh Committee.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Political Science (Indian Constitution at Work)

59. Which Article empowers the Supreme Court to issue writs for the enforcement of Fundamental Rights?
(a) Article 13 (b) Article 32 (c) Article 226 (d) Article 143
Answer: (b)
Solution: Article 226 is the classic trap because it also concerns writs, but it belongs to the High Courts, not the Supreme Court. Article 32, called the "heart and soul of the Constitution" by Dr. Ambedkar, empowers the Supreme Court, so the answer is Article 32.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Political Science (Indian Constitution at Work)

60. The idea of a Concurrent List in the Indian Constitution was borrowed from:
(a) Canada (b) Australia (c) Ireland (d) Weimar Germany
Answer: (b)
Solution: Canada is the trap because the federation with a strong centre and residuary powers came from Canada, and students extend it wrongly to the Concurrent List. The Concurrent List was borrowed from Australia, so the answer is Australia.
Source: M. Laxmikanth, Indian Polity

61. A Money Bill can be introduced only in:
(a) The Rajya Sabha (b) A joint sitting of both Houses (c) The Lok Sabha (d) Either House of Parliament
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Either House" is the trap because ordinary bills can indeed be introduced in either House, and students generalise this to Money Bills. Under Article 109, a Money Bill can be introduced only in the Lok Sabha, so the answer is the Lok Sabha.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Political Science (Indian Constitution at Work)

62. Who decides whether a bill is a Money Bill or not?
(a) The President (b) The Speaker of the Lok Sabha (c) The Chairman of the Rajya Sabha (d) The Finance Minister
Answer: (b)
Solution: The President is the tempting wrong option because he gives assent to bills and can return them, but certification is not his function. Under Article 110(3), the Speaker of the Lok Sabha decides whether a bill is a Money Bill, and his decision is final, so the answer is the Speaker of the Lok Sabha.
Source: M. Laxmikanth, Indian Polity

63. The 73rd Constitutional Amendment Act, 1992 deals with:
(a) Scheduled and Tribal Areas (b) Municipalities (c) Co-operative Societies (d) Panchayati Raj Institutions
Answer: (d)
Solution: Municipalities is the trap because the 74th Amendment, passed in the same year, deals with urban local bodies, and the pair is frequently confused. The 73rd Amendment gave constitutional status to Panchayati Raj Institutions, so the answer is Panchayati Raj Institutions.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Political Science (Indian Constitution at Work)

64. Which Schedule of the Constitution contains the provisions for the administration of tribal areas in Assam, Meghalaya, Tripura and Mizoram?
(a) Fifth Schedule (b) Ninth Schedule (c) Seventh Schedule (d) Sixth Schedule
Answer: (d)
Solution: The Fifth Schedule is the trap because it also concerns Scheduled Areas and tribes, but it applies to tribal areas in other states, not these four. The Sixth Schedule covers Assam, Meghalaya, Tripura and Mizoram, so the answer is the Sixth Schedule.
Source: M. Laxmikanth, Indian Polity

65. The Comptroller and Auditor General of India is appointed by:
(a) The Prime Minister (b) The Parliament (c) The President (d) The Chief Justice of India
Answer: (c)
Solution: Parliament is the trap because the CAG's reports go to Parliament and he is called its watchdog, leading students to assume Parliament appoints him. Under Article 148, the CAG is appointed by the President, so the answer is the President.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Political Science (Indian Constitution at Work)

66. How many times has a Financial Emergency under Article 360 been proclaimed in India so far?
(a) Never (b) Twice (c) Once (d) Three times
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Once" is the trap because students confuse the Financial Emergency with the National Emergency, which has been proclaimed three times (1962, 1971, 1975). A Financial Emergency under Article 360 has never been proclaimed in India, so the answer is Never.
Source: M. Laxmikanth, Indian Polity

67. The doctrine of 'Basic Structure' of the Constitution was propounded by the Supreme Court in:
(a) Golaknath v. State of Punjab (b) S.R. Bommai v. Union of India (c) Minerva Mills v. Union of India (d) Kesavananda Bharati v. State of Kerala
Answer: (d)
Solution: Golaknath is the trap because it dealt with whether Parliament can amend Fundamental Rights and immediately preceded the basic structure debate. The basic structure doctrine was laid down in Kesavananda Bharati v. State of Kerala (1973), so the answer is Kesavananda Bharati v. State of Kerala.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Political Science (Indian Constitution at Work)

68. The Vice-President of India is elected by an electoral college consisting of:
(a) All members of both Houses of Parliament (b) Elected members of both Houses of Parliament only (c) Members of Parliament and State Legislative Assemblies (d) Elected members of Parliament and State Assemblies
Answer: (a)
Solution: The option mentioning State Assemblies is the trap because the President's electoral college includes state legislators, and students extend it to the Vice-President. The Vice-President is elected by all members — elected and nominated — of both Houses of Parliament only, so the answer is all members of both Houses of Parliament.
Source: M. Laxmikanth, Indian Polity

69. Which Article provides for the establishment of a Finance Commission?
(a) Article 263 (b) Article 312 (c) Article 300 (d) Article 280
Answer: (d)
Solution: Article 263 is the trap because it provides for an Inter-State Council, another body students confuse with financial distribution. Article 280 mandates the Finance Commission every five years, so the answer is Article 280.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Political Science (Indian Constitution at Work)

70. The power of judicial review in India is:
(a) Derived from several provisions and limited by the Constitution itself (b) Available only for Fundamental Rights (c) Explicitly unlimited like in the USA (d) Not available against constitutional amendments
Answer: (a)
Solution: The "unlimited like in the USA" option is the trap because judicial review is often taught as an American import, but in India it is narrower and constitutionally circumscribed. Indian judicial review flows from Articles 13, 32, 226, 227 and others and is subject to constitutional limits, so the answer is that it is derived from several provisions and limited by the Constitution itself.
Source: M. Laxmikanth, Indian Polity

71. Which of the following pairs is correctly matched?
(a) Article 17 — Abolition of titles (b) Article 23 — Prohibition of human trafficking and forced labour (c) Article 18 — Abolition of untouchability (d) Article 25 — Protection of minorities' educational rights
Answer: (b)
Solution: The first two options are traps with swapped numbers: Article 17 abolishes untouchability and Article 18 abolishes titles, a classic examiner's interchange. Article 23 prohibits traffic in human beings and forced labour, so the answer is Article 23 — Prohibition of human trafficking and forced labour.

72. The Attorney General of India holds office:
(a) For a fixed term of five years (b) For a fixed term of six years (c) During the pleasure of the President (d) Till the age of 65 years
Answer: (c)
Solution: The five-year term is the trap because many constitutional offices (President, Governors, legislatures) have five-year terms, and students assume the same here. The Constitution prescribes no fixed term for the Attorney General; he holds office during the pleasure of the President under Article 76, so the answer is during the pleasure of the President.
Source: M. Laxmikanth, Indian Polity

73. Which Amendment Act reduced the voting age from 21 years to 18 years?
(a) 61st Amendment Act (b) 44th Amendment Act (c) 86th Amendment Act (d) 42nd Amendment Act
Answer: (a)
Solution: The 42nd and 44th Amendments are traps because they are the most famous amendments of the same era and students pick them by familiarity. The 61st Amendment Act, 1988 reduced the voting age from 21 to 18 years, so the answer is the 61st Amendment Act.
Source: M. Laxmikanth, Indian Polity

74. The residuary powers of legislation in India rest with:
(a) The Parliament (b) The State Legislatures (c) The President (d) The Supreme Court
Answer: (a)
Solution: State Legislatures is the trap because in the USA and Australia residuary powers lie with the states, and students apply that federal logic to India. Following the Canadian model, Article 248 and Entry 97 of the Union List give residuary powers to Parliament, so the answer is the Parliament.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Political Science (Indian Constitution at Work)

75. Which of the following is a correct statement about a Constitutional Amendment Bill under Article 368?
(a) It requires prior recommendation of the President for introduction (b) It can be introduced in either House of Parliament without prior recommendation of the President (c) It can be introduced only in the Lok Sabha (d) It must be ratified by half of the state legislatures in all cases
Answer: (b)
Solution: The Lok Sabha-only option is the trap because Money Bills need the Lok Sabha route and presidential recommendation, and students extend those rules to amendment bills. An amendment bill can be introduced in either House without prior presidential recommendation, and state ratification is needed only for specific federal provisions, so the answer is that it can be introduced in either House of Parliament without prior recommendation of the President.
Source: M. Laxmikanth, Indian Polity
