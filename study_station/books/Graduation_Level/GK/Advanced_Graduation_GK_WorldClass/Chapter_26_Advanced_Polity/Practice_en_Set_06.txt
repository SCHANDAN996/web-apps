126. With reference to the 'Basic Structure' doctrine, which of the following correctly represents the chronological order of the cases?
P. Golaknath v. State of Punjab
Q. Kesavananda Bharati v. State of Kerala
R. Minerva Mills v. Union of India
S. Shankari Prasad v. Union of India
(a) S, Q, P, R (b) P, S, Q, R (c) P, Q, S, R (d) S, P, Q, R
Answer: (d)
Solution: The tempting trap is placing Golaknath before Shankari Prasad because Golaknath is more famous; actually Shankari Prasad (1951) came first, then Golaknath (1967), Kesavananda Bharati (1973), and Minerva Mills (1980). So the answer is S, P, Q, R.
Source: M. Laxmikanth, Indian Polity

127. Which of the following statements about the 42nd Constitutional Amendment Act, 1976 is NOT correct?
(a) It empowered the President to send back the advice of the Council of Ministers once for reconsideration. (b) It extended the term of Lok Sabha and State Assemblies from 5 to 6 years. (c) It added the words 'Socialist', 'Secular' and 'Integrity' to the Preamble. (d) It added Fundamental Duties on the recommendation of the Swaran Singh Committee.
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is confusing the 42nd and 44th Amendments: the 42nd made the President bound by ministerial advice, but the power to return the advice once for reconsideration was added by the 44th Amendment (1978), not the 42nd. The other three are genuine 42nd Amendment provisions. So the answer is that the statement about sending advice back for reconsideration is not correct.
Source: M. Laxmikanth, Indian Polity

128. The power of the Supreme Court to decide disputes between the Centre and the States falls under its:
(a) Original jurisdiction (b) Appellate jurisdiction (c) Advisory jurisdiction (d) Writ jurisdiction
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is advisory jurisdiction, since inter-governmental questions sound like 'advice'; but Article 131 gives the Supreme Court exclusive original jurisdiction in Centre–State and inter-State disputes, while advisory jurisdiction is under Article 143. So the answer is original jurisdiction.
Source: NCERT Class 11, Indian Constitution at Work

129. Which of the following pairs is correctly matched?
(a) Article 352 — Financial Emergency (b) Article 356 — National Emergency (c) Article 365 — President's Rule on failure of constitutional machinery (d) Article 360 — Financial Emergency
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is the close numbering: Article 352 is National Emergency, Article 356 is President's Rule for failure of constitutional machinery, and Article 365 covers failure to comply with Union directions — only Article 360 correctly denotes Financial Emergency. So the answer is Article 360 — Financial Emergency.
Source: M. Laxmikanth, Indian Polity

130. Consider the following statements about a Money Bill:
1. It can be introduced only in the Lok Sabha.
2. The Speaker's decision on whether a bill is a Money Bill is final.
3. The Rajya Sabha can reject a Money Bill.
4. The President can return a Money Bill for reconsideration.
Which of the statements given above are correct?
(a) 1 and 2 only (b) 1, 2, 3 and 4 (c) 1, 2 and 3 only (d) 1, 2 and 4 only
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is statement 4: under Article 111 the President may return a bill for reconsideration only if it is NOT a Money Bill, so a Money Bill cannot be returned; statement 3 is also wrong because the Rajya Sabha can only recommend amendments within 14 days and cannot reject it. Statements 1 and 2 are correct under Article 110. So the answer is 1 and 2 only.
Source: M. Laxmikanth, Indian Polity

131. The 'Doctrine of Pith and Substance' is applied by courts to:
(a) Resolve conflicts between entries of the Union List and State List (b) Test the validity of delegated legislation (c) Determine whether a law violates Fundamental Rights (d) Interpret the Preamble
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is Fundamental Rights, where doctrines like 'severability' and 'eclipse' apply; pith and substance is used when a legislature incidentally encroaches on another list's field, to see the law's true nature. So the answer is resolving conflicts between entries of the Union List and State List.
Source: M. Laxmikanth, Indian Polity

132. Which of the following is NOT a condition for the Parliament to legislate on a subject in the State List?
(a) When the Supreme Court directs Parliament under Article 141 (b) During a National Emergency under Article 250 (c) When Rajya Sabha passes a resolution by two-thirds majority under Article 249 (d) When two or more states request Parliament under Article 252
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is that Article 141 makes Supreme Court law binding, but it does not empower Parliament to legislate on State subjects; the genuine routes are Articles 249, 250, 252, 253 (international agreements) and 356. So the answer is when the Supreme Court directs Parliament under Article 141.
Source: M. Laxmikanth, Indian Polity

133. With reference to the Attorney General of India, consider the following statements:
1. He is appointed by the President on the advice of the Chief Justice of India.
2. He has the right to speak in either House of Parliament without being a member.
3. He holds office during the pleasure of the President.
Which of the statements given above is/are correct?
(a) 1 and 2 only (b) 2 and 3 only (c) 1 and 3 only (d) 1, 2 and 3
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is statement 1: the Attorney General is appointed by the President, but there is no constitutional requirement of the Chief Justice's advice — that confusion arises from judicial appointments. He can speak in either House (Article 88) and holds office during the President's pleasure. So the answer is 2 and 3 only.
Source: M. Laxmikanth, Indian Polity

134. The 73rd Constitutional Amendment Act added which of the following to the Constitution?
(a) Tenth Schedule and Part IX-A (b) Twelfth Schedule and Part IX-A (c) Ninth Schedule and Part IX (d) Eleventh Schedule and Part IX
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is the Twelfth Schedule and Part IX-A, which belong to the 74th Amendment (municipalities), not the 73rd; the 73rd Amendment (1992) added Part IX and the Eleventh Schedule with 29 subjects for Panchayats. So the answer is Eleventh Schedule and Part IX.
Source: M. Laxmikanth, Indian Polity

135. Which of the following statements about the Vice-President of India is correct?
(a) He is elected by an electoral college consisting of elected members of both Houses of Parliament only. (b) He is the ex-officio Chairman of the Rajya Sabha. (c) He acts as President for a maximum period of six months. (d) He can be removed by a resolution of the Lok Sabha agreed to by the Rajya Sabha.
Answer: (b)
Solution: The traps: the electoral college includes all members (elected and nominated) of both Houses; removal begins in the Rajya Sabha, not the Lok Sabha; and there is no six-month limit on acting as President (that limit applies to the gap for electing a new President). He is the ex-officio Chairman of the Rajya Sabha under Article 64. So the answer is that he is the ex-officio Chairman of the Rajya Sabha.
Source: M. Laxmikanth, Indian Polity

136. The concept of 'Concurrent List' in the Indian Constitution was borrowed from:
(a) The Constitution of Ireland (b) The Constitution of the USA (c) The Constitution of Canada (d) The Constitution of Australia
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is Canada, from which we borrowed the federation with a strong centre and residuary powers with the Centre; the Concurrent List, however, was borrowed from Australia. So the answer is the Constitution of Australia.
Source: M. Laxmikanth, Indian Polity

138. Under which Article can the Parliament create a new All-India Service?
(a) Article 311 (b) Article 315 (c) Article 320 (d) Article 312
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is Article 311, which deals with dismissal and protection of civil servants, and Article 315, which provides for Public Service Commissions; Article 312 empowers Parliament to create new All-India Services if the Rajya Sabha passes a resolution by two-thirds majority. So the answer is Article 312.
Source: M. Laxmikanth, Indian Polity

139. Which of the following correctly describes the status of the Ninth Schedule after the I.R. Coelho case (2007)?
(a) Laws placed in the Ninth Schedule are completely immune from judicial review. (b) Laws placed in the Ninth Schedule after 24 April 1973 are open to judicial review if they violate the basic structure. (c) The Ninth Schedule was declared unconstitutional. (d) Only laws relating to land reforms can be placed in the Ninth Schedule.
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is complete immunity, which was the position after Waman Rao for pre-1973 laws; in I.R. Coelho, the Supreme Court held that laws inserted into the Ninth Schedule after 24 April 1973 (the Kesavananda date) can be tested against the basic structure. So the answer is that post-24 April 1973 laws are open to review if they violate the basic structure.
Source: M. Laxmikanth, Indian Polity

140. The quorum to constitute a meeting of either House of Parliament is:
(a) One-fifth of the total membership of the House (b) Fifty members of the House (c) One-fourth of the total membership of the House (d) One-tenth of the total membership of the House
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is one-fifth, a commonly guessed fraction; Article 100(3) fixes the quorum at one-tenth of the total number of members of the House, including the presiding officer. So the answer is one-tenth of the total membership of the House.
Source: M. Laxmikanth, Indian Polity

141. Which of the following statements about the Finance Commission is correct?
(a) It is constituted by the President every fifth year under Article 280. (b) Its recommendations are binding on the Union Government. (c) It is a statutory body constituted under the Finance Commission Act, 1951. (d) It recommends the distribution of only the income tax revenue between the Centre and States.
Answer: (a)
Solution: The traps: the Finance Commission is a constitutional, not statutory, body; its recommendations are advisory, not binding; and it covers distribution of net proceeds of shareable taxes, not just income tax. Article 280 mandates its constitution every fifth year or earlier. So the answer is that it is constituted by the President every fifth year under Article 280.
Source: M. Laxmikanth, Indian Polity

142. In which case did the Supreme Court hold that 'secularism' is a part of the basic structure of the Constitution?
(a) Kesavananda Bharati case (b) Minerva Mills case (c) Indira Sawhney case (d) S.R. Bommai case
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is Kesavananda Bharati, which created the basic structure doctrine but did not list secularism explicitly; in S.R. Bommai v. Union of India (1994), the Court expressly held secularism to be part of the basic structure. So the answer is the S.R. Bommai case.
Source: M. Laxmikanth, Indian Polity

143. Consider the following statements about the 52nd Constitutional Amendment (Anti-Defection Law):
1. It added the Tenth Schedule to the Constitution.
2. The decision of the presiding officer on disqualification is subject to judicial review.
3. An independent member is disqualified if he joins any political party after election.
Which of the statements given above are correct?
(a) 1, 2 and 3 (b) 1 and 3 only (c) 1 and 2 only (d) 2 and 3 only
Answer: (a)
Solution: All three are correct: the 52nd Amendment (1985) added the Tenth Schedule; after the Kihoto Hollohan case (1992), the presiding officer's decision is open to judicial review; and an independent member who joins a party after election is disqualified. So the answer is 1, 2 and 3.
Source: M. Laxmikanth, Indian Polity

144. The 'Collegium system' for appointment of judges of the Supreme Court evolved through:
(a) The Constitution (99th Amendment) Act (b) The National Judicial Appointments Commission Act, 2014 (c) A series of Supreme Court judgements known as the Three Judges Cases (d) The Memorandum of Procedure issued by the President
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is the 99th Amendment, which actually created the NJAC to replace the collegium and was struck down in 2015; the collegium system itself evolved through the First (1981), Second (1993) and Third (1998) Judges Cases. So the answer is the Three Judges Cases.
Source: M. Laxmikanth, Indian Polity

145. Which of the following is NOT a Fundamental Duty under Article 51A?
(a) To abide by the Constitution and respect its ideals and institutions (b) To pay taxes honestly and on time (c) To safeguard public property and to abjure violence (d) To develop scientific temper, humanism and the spirit of inquiry and reform
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is that paying taxes sounds like a civic duty, but it is not among the eleven Fundamental Duties in Article 51A; the other three are verbatim duties from the clauses of Article 51A. So the answer is to pay taxes honestly and on time.
Source: NCERT Class 11, Indian Constitution at Work

146. With reference to the Governor of a State, consider the following statements:
1. The same person can be appointed as Governor of two or more states.
2. The Governor's emoluments are charged on the Consolidated Fund of the State.
3. The Governor can reserve a bill passed by the State Legislature for the consideration of the President.
Which of the statements given above are correct?
(a) 1 and 3 only (b) 2 and 3 only (c) 1, 2 and 3 (d) 1 and 2 only
Answer: (c)
Solution: All three are correct: the 7th Amendment (1956) allowed one person to be Governor of multiple states; the Governor's emoluments are charged on the state's Consolidated Fund; and Article 200 permits reserving bills for the President. So the answer is 1, 2 and 3.

147. The power of judicial review of the Supreme Court under Article 32 can be suspended:
(a) By the President at any time by an executive order (b) By the Parliament through a simple majority law (c) During the operation of a National Emergency, as provided by the Constitution (d) It can never be suspended under any circumstances
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is 'never suspended', since Article 32 is itself a Fundamental Right; but under Article 359, during a National Emergency the President may suspend the right to move courts for enforcement of Fundamental Rights (except Articles 20 and 21 after the 44th Amendment). So the answer is during the operation of a National Emergency, as provided by the Constitution.
Source: M. Laxmikanth, Indian Polity

148. Which of the following committees is exclusively a committee of the Lok Sabha?
(a) Public Accounts Committee (b) Estimates Committee (c) Committee on Public Undertakings (d) Committee on Welfare of Scheduled Castes and Scheduled Tribes
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is the Public Accounts Committee, which is famous but includes members from both Houses (15 from Lok Sabha, 7 from Rajya Sabha); the Estimates Committee consists of 30 members, all from the Lok Sabha only. So the answer is the Estimates Committee.
Source: M. Laxmikanth, Indian Polity

149. Consider the following statements about the 86th Constitutional Amendment Act, 2002:
1. It made elementary education a Fundamental Right under Article 21A.
2. It changed the subject of Article 45 to early childhood care and education.
3. It added a new Fundamental Duty for parents to provide education opportunities to children aged 6 to 14 years.
Which of the statements given above are correct?
(a) 2 and 3 only (b) 1 and 3 only (c) 1, 2 and 3 (d) 1 and 2 only
Answer: (c)
Solution: All three changes were made by the 86th Amendment: Article 21A (right to education for ages 6–14), modified Article 45 (early childhood care until age 6), and a new clause in Article 51A for parents/guardians. So the answer is 1, 2 and 3.

150. Which of the following statements about the Comptroller and Auditor General of India is NOT correct?
(a) He is appointed by the President and holds office for 6 years or up to 65 years of age, whichever is earlier. (b) He is eligible for appointment to any office under the Government of India after retirement. (c) His salary and conditions of service are determined by Parliament. (d) He can be removed from office in the same manner as a judge of the Supreme Court.
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is that the other three statements are accurate, making them attractive; but the CAG is expressly barred from holding any office under the Union or a State government after ceasing to hold office, to safeguard independence. So the answer is that he is eligible for appointment after retirement — this statement is not correct.
Source: M. Laxmikanth, Indian Polity
