# 🪄 Memory Hooks & Short Tricks — Advanced Indian Polity

---

**Box 1 — Borrowed Features of the Constitution**
> **"AUS-CAN-GER-IRE-JAP-RUS-SA-UK-USA went to a party"**
- **AUS**tralia → Concurrent List, Joint Sitting
- **CAN**ada → Federation with strong Centre, Residuary powers with Centre
- **GER**many (Weimar) → Emergency provisions
- **IRE**land → DPSP, Nomination of Rajya Sabha members, President's election method
- **JAP**an → Procedure established by law
- **RUS**sia (USSR) → Fundamental Duties, Five-Year Plans
- **SA** (South Africa) → Amendment procedure, Rajya Sabha election
- **UK** → Parliamentary system, Rule of law, Single citizenship
- **USA** → Fundamental Rights, Judicial Review, Impeachment of President

---

**Box 2 — Fundamental Rights Articles (12–35)**
> **"12-13 define, 14-18 Equality, 19-22 Freedom, 23-24 Exploitation, 25-28 Religion, 29-30 Culture, 32 Remedy"**
- 14–18 = Right to **E**quality
- 19–22 = Right to **F**reedom
- 23–24 = Against **E**xploitation
- 25–28 = **R**eligious freedom
- 29–30 = **C**ultural & Educational
- 32 = Constitutional **R**emedies ("heart and soul" — Ambedkar)
- Trick: **E-F-E-R-C-R** → "**E**very **F**reedom **E**xists **R**equiring **C**onstitutional **R**emedy"

---

**Box 3 — Writs (Article 32 / 226)**
> **"He-Man-Quo-Pro-Cert"** — the superhero squad:
- **He**beas Corpus → "produce the body"
- **Man**damus → "we command" (to public authority)
- **Quo** Warranto → "by what authority?" (public office)
- **Pro**hibition → stop lower court *before* judgment
- **Cert**iorari → quash order *after* judgment
- Trap: Prohibition = preventive (before), Certiorari = curative (after).

---

**Box 4 — President's Election & Removal**
> **"52 creates, 54 elects, 61 removes"**
- Article 52 → Office of President
- Article 54 → Election (Electoral College: elected MPs + elected MLAs of states and of the UTs of Delhi, Puducherry and Jammu & Kashmir)
- Article 61 → Impeachment (only ground: **violation of the Constitution**)
- Trick: **"54 selects, 61 ejects."**

---

**Box 5 — Money Bill (Article 110)**
> **"Speaker decides, Lok Sabha introduces, Rajya Sabha only watches for 14 days"**
- Only Lok Sabha introduces it; Speaker's certificate is final.
- Rajya Sabha can only recommend within **14 days** — Lok Sabha may reject all suggestions.
- Trick: **"Money speaks only in Lok Sabha; RS gets a 14-day window seat."**

---

**Box 6 — Emergency Trio (352, 356, 360)**
> **"352 War, 356 State, 360 Wallet"**
- 352 → National Emergency (war/external aggression/**armed rebellion**)
- 356 → President's Rule (failure of constitutional machinery in a state)
- 360 → Financial Emergency (never proclaimed so far)
- Trick: **"Nation fights (352), State fails (356), Finance falls (360)."**

---

**Box 7 — Amendment Categories (Article 368)**
> **"Simple, Special, Special + Half the States"**
- Simple majority → e.g., creation of new states, citizenship
- Special majority → most provisions (FRs, DPSP)
- Special majority + ratification by half the states → federal provisions (President's election, SC/HC, Seventh Schedule, Article 368 itself)
- Trick: **"Touch the federation, take the states along."**

---

**Box 8 — Panchayati Raj (73rd Amendment)**
> **"243 has 29 friends in Schedule 11"**
- 73rd Amendment → Part IX (Articles 243–243O), **Eleventh Schedule** with **29 subjects**
- 74th Amendment → Part IXA, **Twelfth Schedule** with **18 subjects**
- Trick: **"Village 29, Town 18 — villages get more work!"**

---

**Box 9 — Schedules of the Constitution (12)**
> **"1 Territory, 2 Salary, 3 Oath, 4 RS-Seats, 5-6 Tribal Areas, 7 Lists, 8 Languages, 9 Land Laws, 10 Defection, 11-12 Local Bodies"**
- 8th Schedule → 22 languages
- 9th Schedule → Article 31B (added by 1st Amendment, 1951)
- 10th Schedule → Anti-Defection (52nd Amendment, 1985)
- Trick: **"9 protects laws, 10 punishes party-hoppers."**

---

**Box 10 — Fundamental Duties**
> **"42nd Amendment, 1976, Swaran Singh Committee, 10 duties; 86th Amendment, 2002 added the 11th (education of child)"**
- Borrowed from **USSR**; Article **51A**, Part **IVA**
- Trick: **"42 gave 10, 86 gave 1 more — 11 duties, non-justiciable (not enforceable by courts)."**

---

**Box 11 — CAG, AG, EC, UPSC (Constitutional Bodies)**
> **"CAG 148 audits, EC 324 conducts, UPSC 315 recruits, AG 76 advises"**
- CAG → Article 148 (audit of Centre & States)
- Election Commission → Article 324
- UPSC → Articles 315–323
- Attorney General → Article 76 (highest law officer, can attend either House, no vote)
- Trick: **"148 counts money, 324 counts votes, 315 counts candidates, 76 counts arguments."**

---

**Box 12 — Vice-President & Rajya Sabha**
> **"VP = ex-officio Chairman of RS; elected by BOTH Houses (elected + nominated members)"**
- Trap: President's electoral college has only **elected** members; VP's includes **nominated** members too.
- Trick: **"President — elected only; VP — everyone votes."**

---

**Box 13 — Centre–State Lists (Seventh Schedule)**
> **"Now 100 Union, 61 State, 52 Concurrent — originally 97, 66, 47"**
- Union List: **100** subjects (originally 97 in 1950)
- State List: **61** subjects (originally 66)
- Concurrent List: **52** subjects (originally 47)
- The **42nd Amendment (1976)** shifted **5 subjects** from the State List to the Concurrent List (e.g., education, forests, protection of wild animals and birds).
- Trick: **"97-66-47 grew into 100-61-52 — the 42nd moved 5 from State to Concurrent."**

## ⏭️ Skip Strategy — When to Skip a Question

1. **Multi-person / multi-committee recall questions:** If a question asks for the chairman of a lesser-known committee (e.g., which committee recommended a specific panchayat reform) and you cannot recall it within 15 seconds, **skip immediately** — guessing among four similar-sounding names (Balwant Rai Mehta / Ashok Mehta / L.M. Singhvi / Gadgil) has a high negative-marking risk.

2. **Exact-number traps:** Questions demanding precise figures — exact number of subjects in a list, exact amendment number for a minor change, exact article of an obscure body. If you are unsure between two adjacent numbers (e.g., 243G vs 243H), **skip** rather than gamble; these are designed as precision traps.

3. **"Which of the following is NOT correct" with four dense statements:** If even one statement involves a fact you have never seen (an unfamiliar case name, a date, a borrowed-feature pairing), **skip** — one unknown statement makes elimination unreliable, and negative marking punishes blind attempts.
