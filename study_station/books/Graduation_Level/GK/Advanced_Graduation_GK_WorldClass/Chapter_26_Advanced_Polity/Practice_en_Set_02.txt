26. Which Article of the Indian Constitution deals with the impeachment of the President?
(a) Article 56 (b) Article 123 (c) Article 72 (d) Article 61
Answer: (d)
Solution: Article 56 is the tempting wrong option because it deals with the President's term of office and resignation, which sounds close to removal. The impeachment procedure is specifically laid down in Article 61, while Article 72 covers pardoning power and Article 123 covers ordinances, so the answer is Article 61.

27. The 42nd Constitutional Amendment Act, 1976 is often called the 'Mini Constitution'. Which of the following changes was made by the 44th Amendment Act, 1978 and NOT by the 42nd Amendment?
(a) Adding the words 'Socialist' and 'Secular' to the Preamble (b) Requiring the President to act on the written advice of the Cabinet before proclaiming Emergency under Article 352 (c) Adding Fundamental Duties under Part IV-A (d) Inserting free legal aid under Article 39A
Answer: (b)
Solution: This is a classic chronological trap: the 42nd Amendment made sweeping changes, so candidates assume everything listed came from it. But the requirement of the Cabinet's written advice for proclaiming Emergency under Article 352 was inserted by the 44th Amendment Act, 1978, while the Preamble words, Fundamental Duties and Article 39A all came from the 42nd, so the answer is the written advice of the Cabinet requirement under Article 352.

28. Under which Article can the Parliament amend the Constitution by way of addition, variation or repeal of any provision?
(a) Article 360 (b) Article 356 (c) Article 370 (d) Article 368
Answer: (d)
Solution: Article 356 is tempting because of its similar number, but it deals with President's Rule, and Article 360 deals with Financial Emergency. Article 368 in Part XX lays down the power and procedure of constitutional amendment, so the answer is Article 368.

29. The doctrine of 'Basic Structure' of the Constitution was propounded by the Supreme Court in which case?
(a) Golaknath v. State of Punjab (b) Shankari Prasad v. Union of India (c) Minerva Mills v. Union of India (d) Kesavananda Bharati v. State of Kerala
Answer: (d)
Solution: Golaknath (1967) is the tempting wrong option because it held that Parliament cannot amend Fundamental Rights, but it did not propound the Basic Structure doctrine. That doctrine was laid down in Kesavananda Bharati (1973) and only reaffirmed in Minerva Mills (1980), so the answer is Kesavananda Bharati v. State of Kerala.
Source: PYQ-style

30. Which of the following pairs is correctly matched?
(a) Article 19 — Right to Constitutional Remedies (b) Article 32 — Abolition of Untouchability (c) Article 14 — Right to Freedom of Religion (d) Article 21 — Protection of Life and Personal Liberty
Answer: (d)
Solution: Examiners frequently swap Articles 32 and 19: Article 32 is the Right to Constitutional Remedies and Article 19 guarantees the six freedoms. Article 14 is equality before law and Article 17 abolishes untouchability, so the answer is Article 21 — Protection of Life and Personal Liberty.

31. The concept of 'Concurrent List' in the Indian Constitution was borrowed from which country?
(a) Australia (b) Canada (c) United States of America (d) Ireland
Answer: (a)
Solution: Canada is the tempting wrong answer because the federal scheme and residuary powers came from Canada. The Concurrent List, however, was borrowed from Australia, while Ireland gave us the Directive Principles, so the answer is Australia.

32. How many members can the President nominate to the Rajya Sabha?
(a) 12 (b) 10 (c) 2 (d) 14
Answer: (a)
Solution: The number 2 is a trap — it refers to the earlier nomination of Anglo-Indian members to the Lok Sabha, discontinued by the 104th Amendment. The President nominates 12 members to the Rajya Sabha from the fields of literature, science, art and social service, so the answer is 12.

33. A Money Bill can be introduced only in:
(a) The Rajya Sabha (b) The Lok Sabha (c) A joint sitting of both Houses (d) Either House of Parliament
Answer: (b)
Solution: 'Either House' is tempting because ordinary bills can originate in either House. Under Article 109, however, a Money Bill can be introduced only in the Lok Sabha, and that too on the recommendation of the President, so the answer is the Lok Sabha.

34. Who decides whether a bill is a Money Bill or not?
(a) The President of India (b) The Chairman of the Rajya Sabha (c) The Speaker of the Lok Sabha (d) The Chief Justice of India
Answer: (c)
Solution: The President is a tempting choice because his recommendation is needed to introduce a Money Bill. Under Article 110, the final decision on whether a bill is a Money Bill rests with the Speaker of the Lok Sabha, and this decision cannot be questioned in any court, so the answer is the Speaker of the Lok Sabha.

35. The 73rd Constitutional Amendment Act is related to:
(a) Municipalities (b) Cooperative Societies (c) Panchayati Raj Institutions (d) Anti-Defection
Answer: (c)
Solution: Municipalities is the classic trap because it came through the very next amendment — the 74th Amendment Act, 1992. The 73rd Amendment (1992) gave constitutional status to Panchayati Raj Institutions and added the Eleventh Schedule, so the answer is Panchayati Raj Institutions.

36. Which Schedule of the Constitution contains the provisions regarding anti-defection?
(a) Eighth Schedule (b) Ninth Schedule (c) Tenth Schedule (d) Twelfth Schedule
Answer: (c)
Solution: The Ninth Schedule is tempting because it too was added by an amendment (the 1st, 1952) and shields certain laws from judicial review. The anti-defection law, added by the 52nd Amendment Act, 1985, is contained in the Tenth Schedule, so the answer is the Tenth Schedule.

37. The minimum age to become a member of the Rajya Sabha is:
(a) 30 years (b) 25 years (c) 21 years (d) 35 years
Answer: (a)
Solution: 25 years is the tempting wrong answer because it is the minimum age for the Lok Sabha and State Assemblies. For the Rajya Sabha and State Legislative Councils the minimum age is 30 years, while 35 years is the minimum for the President, so the answer is 30 years.

38. Which Article empowers the Supreme Court to issue writs for the enforcement of Fundamental Rights?
(a) Article 143 (b) Article 32 (c) Article 226 (d) Article 13
Answer: (b)
Solution: Article 226 is the deliberate trap — it empowers High Courts to issue writs, and for a wider range of rights than just Fundamental Rights. The question asks about the Supreme Court, which acts under Article 32, called the 'heart and soul' of the Constitution by Dr. Ambedkar, so the answer is Article 32.

39. The President of India can declare a Financial Emergency under:
(a) Article 360 (b) Article 356 (c) Article 352 (d) Article 365
Answer: (a)
Solution: Articles 352 and 356 are the usual confusions — they deal with National Emergency and President's Rule respectively. Financial Emergency under Article 360 has never been declared in India so far, so the answer is Article 360.

40. Which of the following is NOT a Fundamental Right under the Indian Constitution?
(a) Right to Property (b) Right to Freedom (c) Right to Equality (d) Right against Exploitation
Answer: (a)
Solution: Right to Property is the correct pick because the 44th Amendment Act, 1978 removed it from the list of Fundamental Rights and made it a legal right under Article 300A. The other three remain Fundamental Rights under Articles 14–18, 19–22 and 23–24, so the answer is Right to Property.

41. The idea of Directive Principles of State Policy was borrowed from the Constitution of:
(a) USA (b) UK (c) Ireland (d) Germany
Answer: (c)
Solution: The UK is tempting because India borrowed the parliamentary system and rule of law from it. The Directive Principles of State Policy in Part IV, however, were borrowed from the Irish Constitution, which itself took them from Spain, so the answer is Ireland.

42. Who was the Chairman of the Drafting Committee of the Constituent Assembly?
(a) Dr. B.R. Ambedkar (b) Dr. Rajendra Prasad (c) Jawaharlal Nehru (d) Sardar Vallabhbhai Patel
Answer: (a)
Solution: Dr. Rajendra Prasad is the classic trap because he was the President of the Constituent Assembly, a role often confused with the chairmanship of the Drafting Committee. The Drafting Committee, formed on 29 August 1947, was chaired by Dr. B.R. Ambedkar, so the answer is Dr. B.R. Ambedkar.

43. The Constitution of India was adopted by the Constituent Assembly on:
(a) 26 January 1950 (b) 26 November 1949 (c) 15 August 1947 (d) 26 January 1949
Answer: (b)
Solution: 26 January 1950 is the tempting wrong answer — that is when the Constitution came into force, celebrated as Republic Day. The Constitution was adopted and signed on 26 November 1949, now observed as Constitution Day, so the answer is 26 November 1949.

44. Which Article of the Constitution deals with the appointment of the Governor of a State?
(a) Article 153 (b) Article 155 (c) Article 163 (d) Article 167
Answer: (b)
Solution: Article 153 is tempting because it provides that there shall be a Governor for each State, but it does not cover the appointment itself. The appointment of the Governor by the President is provided under Article 155, while Article 163 deals with the Council of Ministers, so the answer is Article 155.

45. The Union Public Service Commission submits its annual report to:
(a) The Prime Minister (b) The Chief Justice of India (c) The Parliament directly (d) The President
Answer: (d)
Solution: 'Parliament directly' is a trap — the report does reach Parliament, but only through the President, who causes it to be laid before each House with a memorandum on cases where the Commission's advice was not accepted. Under Article 323 the UPSC submits its report to the President, so the answer is the President.

46. Which Amendment Act lowered the voting age from 21 years to 18 years?
(a) 42nd Amendment Act (b) 44th Amendment Act (c) 61st Amendment Act (d) 73rd Amendment Act
Answer: (c)
Solution: The 42nd Amendment is tempting because it made sweeping changes in 1976, but it did not touch the voting age. The reduction of voting age from 21 to 18 years for Lok Sabha and Assembly elections was done by the 61st Amendment Act, 1988, so the answer is the 61st Amendment Act.

47. The 'Right to Education' as a Fundamental Right under Article 21A was inserted by which amendment?
(a) 86th Amendment Act, 2002 (b) 91st Amendment Act, 2003 (c) 93rd Amendment Act, 2005 (d) 52nd Amendment Act, 1985
Answer: (a)
Solution: The 93rd Amendment is a near-miss trap — it dealt with reservation in educational institutions under Article 15(5), not the Right to Education. Article 21A, providing free and compulsory education to children aged 6–14, was inserted by the 86th Amendment Act, 2002, so the answer is the 86th Amendment Act, 2002.

48. Who among the following is the ex-officio Chairman of the Rajya Sabha?
(a) The Speaker of the Lok Sabha (b) The President (c) The Vice-President (d) The Prime Minister
Answer: (c)
Solution: The President is tempting because he is a part of Parliament and appoints many high officials. Under Article 64, however, the Vice-President of India is the ex-officio Chairman of the Rajya Sabha, so the answer is the Vice-President.

49. The total number of Fundamental Duties in the Indian Constitution at present is:
(a) 10 (b) 12 (c) 9 (d) 11
Answer: (d)
Solution: 10 is the classic trap because the 42nd Amendment (1976) originally added ten Fundamental Duties. The 86th Amendment (2002) added the eleventh duty — a parent or guardian providing education to their child between 6 and 14 years — so the answer is 11.
Source: PYQ-style

50. Which of the following emergencies was declared on the ground of 'internal disturbance'?
(a) National Emergency of 1962 (b) National Emergency of 1975 (c) National Emergency of 1971 (d) Financial Emergency of 1991
Answer: (b)
Solution: The 1971 emergency is tempting because it too was a National Emergency, but like the 1962 emergency it was declared on the ground of external aggression. Only the 1975 emergency was proclaimed on the ground of 'internal disturbance' (changed to 'armed rebellion' by the 44th Amendment), and no Financial Emergency has ever been declared, so the answer is the National Emergency of 1975.
