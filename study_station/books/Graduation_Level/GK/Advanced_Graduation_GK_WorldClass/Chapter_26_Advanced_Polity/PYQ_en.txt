# 📊 PYQ ANALYSIS — ADVANCED INDIAN POLITY

## 1. How Exams Ask Questions from This Chapter

Polity is one of the most dependable areas across UPSC (Prelims), SSC CGL/CHSL, Banking (IBPS/SBI), Railway (NTPC/Group D) and State PCS prelims. The questions are overwhelmingly **static** — they test the Constitution as it is written, not current events. The pattern analysis below is based on the *nature* of questions repeatedly seen in these exams, not on any invented count.

**What examiners repeatedly target:**

- **Articles and their exact content** — "Which Article deals with…?" is the single most common frame. Favourite zones: Articles 14–32 (Fundamental Rights), 74–75 (Council of Ministers), 110 (Money Bill), 123/213 (Ordinances), 280 (Finance Commission), 324 (Election Commission), 352/356/360 (Emergencies), 368 (Amendment).
- **Borrowed features of the Constitution** — matching a feature (e.g., Directive Principles, Concurrent List, Judicial Review) with the country it was borrowed from.
- **Amendments** — especially the 42nd, 44th, 52nd (Anti-Defection), 61st (voting age 21→18), 73rd/74th (Panchayati Raj/Municipalities), 86th (Article 21A), 101st (GST).
- **Schedules** — how many there are (12), and what the 8th, 9th and 10th Schedules contain.
- **Constitutional vs. statutory bodies** — Election Commission, UPSC, Finance Commission, CAG vs. NITI Aayog, NHRC, CVC, Lokpal.
- **Parliamentary procedure** — Money Bill vs. ordinary Bill, joint sitting (Article 108), Question Hour, motions (calling attention, adjournment, no-confidence).
- **Federal structure** — three lists, residuary powers (Centre, Article 248), Article 356 misuse and the S.R. Bommai judgment.
- **Judiciary** — writs (habeas corpus, mandamus, prohibition, certiorari, quo warranto), original vs. appellate jurisdiction of the Supreme Court, judicial review.
- **Panchayati Raj** — 73rd Amendment provisions: three tiers, reservation for women, State Election Commission, 29 subjects in the 11th Schedule.

**Exam-wise flavour (qualitative):**

- **UPSC Prelims** — conceptual and statement-based; "Which of the statements given above is/are correct?" dominates. Tests *why* a provision exists, not just *what* it says.
- **SSC CGL/CHSL** — direct factual recall: Articles, Amendments, Schedules, borrowed features, tenures and appointments.
- **Banking (IBPS/SBI)** — fewer polity questions, but when asked they are simple: constitutional bodies, recent amendments, basic structure doctrine.
- **Railway NTPC/Group D** — one-liner facts: who appoints whom, term lengths, which Article, which Schedule.
- **State PCS** — mirrors UPSC style at lower difficulty, plus state-specific provisions (Governor, State Legislature, State PSC).

## 2. The Examiner's Traps (जाल) — With Examples

### Trap 1: Almost-Correct Statement
A statement that is 90% right but fails on one word.

> "The President can promulgate an Ordinance when **either House or both Houses** are not in session."
✅ Correct — Article 123 says "except when both Houses of Parliament are in session." The trap version says "only when **both** Houses are not in session," which is wrong: an Ordinance can be issued even if one House is sitting.

### Trap 2: Chronological Confusion
Swapping dates, amendment numbers, or the order of events.

> "The 44th Amendment (1978) added the words 'Socialist' and 'Secular' to the Preamble."
❌ Wrong — it was the **42nd Amendment (1976)**. The 44th Amendment *retained* those words but reversed many 42nd-Amendment changes. Examiners love pairing 42nd/44th, 73rd/74th, and 52nd/61st.

### Trap 3: Negative Phrasing
"Which of the following is **NOT**…" — under exam pressure candidates read it as "which is."

> "Which of the following is NOT a Fundamental Right?"
(a) Right to Equality (b) Right to Constitutional Remedies (c) Right to Property (d) Right against Exploitation
✅ (c) — Right to Property was removed from Fundamental Rights by the 44th Amendment (1978) and is now a legal right under Article 300A. This is one of the most repeated trap questions across exams.

### Trap 4: Similar-Sounding Names / Numbers
Article 32 vs. 226 (both writ jurisdictions — Supreme Court vs. High Courts); Article 352 vs. 356 (National Emergency vs. President's Rule); CAG (Article 148) vs. AG (Article 76); 8th Schedule (languages) vs. 10th Schedule (anti-defection).

### Trap 5: Constitutional vs. Statutory Body Swap
> "NITI Aayog is a constitutional body."
❌ Wrong — NITI Aayog was created by an executive resolution (2015). Election Commission (Art. 324), UPSC (Art. 315), Finance Commission (Art. 280) and CAG (Art. 148) are constitutional. NHRC, CVC, Lokpal and CBI are statutory/executive — a favourite confusion set.

### Trap 6: "Borrowed From" Mismatch
> "The Concurrent List was borrowed from the Constitution of Canada."
❌ Wrong — Concurrent List came from **Australia**. From Canada we borrowed federation with a strong Centre and residuary powers with the Centre. Directive Principles — Ireland; Judicial Review and Fundamental Rights — USA; Parliamentary system — UK.

## 3. High-Yield Practice Questions (PYQ-Style) With Solutions

**Q1.** Which Article of the Indian Constitution empowers the President to promulgate Ordinances?
(a) Article 111 (b) Article 123 (c) Article 143 (d) Article 213
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** Article 123 gives the President ordinance-making power when Parliament is not in session; Article 213 is the parallel power of the Governor — the classic similar-number trap. Article 111 is assent to Bills; Article 143 is advisory jurisdiction.
**Trap type:** Similar-Sounding Numbers | **Source:** PYQ-style

**Q2.** The words 'Socialist', 'Secular' and 'Integrity' were added to the Preamble by which amendment?
(a) 24th Amendment (b) 42nd Amendment (c) 44th Amendment (d) 52nd Amendment
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** The 42nd Amendment (1976), often called the "Mini-Constitution," inserted these words. The 44th Amendment (1978) is the tempting wrong answer — it undid many 42nd-Amendment provisions but kept these words.
**Trap type:** Chronological Confusion | **Source:** PYQ-style

**Q3.** Which of the following is NOT a constitutional body?
(a) Election Commission (b) Finance Commission (c) NITI Aayog (d) Union Public Service Commission
**Answer: (c)**
**Solution:** NITI Aayog was set up by a Union Cabinet resolution in 2015; it has no mention in the Constitution. The other three are created by Articles 324, 280 and 315 respectively.
**Trap type:** Negative Phrasing + Body-Swap | **Source:** PYQ-style

**Q4.** The concept of Directive Principles of State Policy was borrowed from the Constitution of:
(a) USA (b) Ireland (c) Canada (d) Australia
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** DPSPs came from the Irish Constitution (which itself drew from Spain). Australia is the distractor — it gave us the Concurrent List and joint sitting.
**Trap type:** Borrowed-From Mismatch | **Source:** NCERT Class 11 (Indian Constitution at Work)

**Q5.** Under which Article can the Supreme Court issue writs for the enforcement of Fundamental Rights?
(a) Article 13 (b) Article 32 (c) Article 226 (d) Article 368
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** Article 32 (called by Dr. Ambedkar the "heart and soul" of the Constitution) empowers the Supreme Court; Article 226 empowers High Courts — and is wider, since High Courts can issue writs for other legal rights too.
**Trap type:** Similar-Sounding Numbers | **Source:** PYQ-style

**Q6.** A Money Bill can be introduced only in:
(a) Rajya Sabha (b) Lok Sabha (c) Either House (d) A joint sitting
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** Under Article 110, a Money Bill can originate only in the Lok Sabha, and only on the President's recommendation. The Speaker's certification of a Bill as a Money Bill is final.
**Trap type:** Almost-Correct ("either House" sounds plausible) | **Source:** PYQ-style

**Q7.** The 73rd Constitutional Amendment is related to:
(a) Municipalities (b) Panchayati Raj (c) Anti-Defection (d) Voting age
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** 73rd (1992) → Panchayati Raj; 74th → Municipalities; 52nd → Anti-Defection (10th Schedule); 61st → voting age reduced from 21 to 18. All four are routinely swapped with each other.
**Trap type:** Chronological/Number Confusion | **Source:** PYQ-style

**Q8.** Which Schedule of the Constitution contains provisions regarding disqualification on grounds of defection?
(a) 8th Schedule (b) 9th Schedule (c) 10th Schedule (d) 12th Schedule
**Answer: (c)**
**Solution:** The 10th Schedule (added by the 52nd Amendment, 1985) deals with anti-defection. 8th = languages; 9th = laws immune from judicial review (added by 1st Amendment, 1951); 12th = municipalities.
**Trap type:** Schedule Swap | **Source:** PYQ-style

**Q9.** Residuary powers of legislation under the Indian Constitution rest with:
(a) State Legislatures (b) Parliament (c) President (d) Supreme Court
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** Article 248 and Entry 97 of the Union List give residuary powers to Parliament — a feature borrowed from Canada, unlike the USA where residuary powers lie with the states.
**Trap type:** Borrowed-From / Federal Confusion | **Source:** NCERT Class 11 (Indian Constitution at Work)

**Q10.** The doctrine of 'Basic Structure' of the Constitution was propounded by the Supreme Court in:
(a) Golaknath case (b) Kesavananda Bharati case (c) Minerva Mills case (d) S.R. Bommai case
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** Kesavananda Bharati v. State of Kerala (1973) held that Parliament may amend the Constitution but cannot alter its basic structure. Minerva Mills (1980) *applied* the doctrine; Golaknath (1967) preceded it; Bommai (1994) concerned Article 356.
**Trap type:** Chronological Confusion | **Source:** PYQ-style

## 4. The 15/45-Second Rule Applied

**Rule:** If a question is pure recall (Article number, Schedule, borrowed feature), decide within **15 seconds** — you either know it or you don't; mark and move. If it is statement-based or multi-concept, allow up to **45 seconds** of structured elimination, never more.

**Applied to Q3 (NITI Aayog):** This is pure recall — one fact ("NITI Aayog = executive resolution, 2015"). ⏱️ **15-second question.** Do not re-read the options hoping for a memory; flag and move on if unsure.

**Applied to Q10 (Basic Structure):** Four similar case names demand careful matching of case → year → holding. ⏱️ **45-second question.** Eliminate Golaknath (1967, before the doctrine) and Bommai (Article 356), then choose between Kesavananda (propounded) and Minerva Mills (applied). If still unsure at 45 seconds, mark Kesavananda — statistically the most-tested answer for "propounded" — and move on.

**General skip strategy for Polity:**
- Skip (return later) any question mixing **three or more amendments** or asking "arrange in chronological order" — these eat time.
- Never skip direct Article/Schedule questions without attempting — they are the fastest marks in the paper.
- In negative-phrasing questions, underline "NOT/EXCEPT" mentally before reading options; most Polity errors are reading errors, not knowledge gaps.
