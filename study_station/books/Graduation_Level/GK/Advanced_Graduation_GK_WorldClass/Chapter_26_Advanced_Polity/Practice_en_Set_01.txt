1. Which Article of the Indian Constitution deals with the amendment procedure?
(a) Article 356 (b) Article 360 (c) Article 368 (d) Article 370
Answer: (c)
Solution: Article 356 deals with President's Rule and Article 360 with financial emergency — both are tempting because they appear together in emergency discussions, but neither concerns amendment. Article 368 in Part XX lays down the power and procedure of Parliament to amend the Constitution, so the answer is Article 368.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Indian Constitution at Work

2. The concept of 'Procedure Established by Law' in Article 21 was borrowed from the Constitution of which country?
(a) Japan (b) USA (c) UK (d) Ireland
Answer: (a)
Solution: Many students pick the USA, but the USA gave us 'Due Process of Law', which is the trap here. The expression 'Procedure Established by Law' in Article 21 was borrowed from the Japanese Constitution, so the answer is Japan.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Indian Constitution at Work

3. Who presides over a joint sitting of the two Houses of Parliament summoned under Article 108?
(a) The President (b) The Speaker of the Lok Sabha (c) The Vice-President (d) The Chief Justice of India
Answer: (b)
Solution: The Vice-President is tempting because he chairs the Rajya Sabha, but a joint sitting is not a Rajya Sabha session. Under Article 118(4), the Speaker of the Lok Sabha presides over a joint sitting summoned by the President under Article 108, so the answer is the Speaker of the Lok Sabha.
Source: M. Laxmikanth, Indian Polity

4. The Fundamental Duties were added to the Constitution by which amendment?
(a) 24th Amendment (b) 52nd Amendment (c) 44th Amendment (d) 42nd Amendment
Answer: (d)
Solution: The 44th Amendment is a common wrong pick because it also dealt with emergency-era corrections, but it did not add Fundamental Duties. The 42nd Amendment Act, 1976 inserted Part IV-A (Article 51A) on the recommendation of the Swaran Singh Committee, so the answer is the 42nd Amendment.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Indian Constitution at Work

5. Which of the following is NOT a Fundamental Right under the Indian Constitution?
(a) Right to Equality (b) Right to Property (c) Right to Constitutional Remedies (d) Right against Exploitation
Answer: (b)
Solution: Right to Property is the classic trap — it was a Fundamental Right under Article 31 until the 44th Amendment, 1978 deleted it and made it a legal right under Article 300A. Hence it is no longer a Fundamental Right, so the answer is Right to Property.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Indian Constitution at Work

6. The minimum age to become a member of the Rajya Sabha is:
(a) 21 years (b) 30 years (c) 25 years (d) 35 years
Answer: (b)
Solution: 25 years is tempting because it is the minimum age for the Lok Sabha and State Assemblies — a frequent confusion. Article 84 prescribes 30 years for the Rajya Sabha, so the answer is 30 years.
Source: M. Laxmikanth, Indian Polity

7. The idea of the Directive Principles of State Policy was borrowed from the Constitution of:
(a) Canada (b) Australia (c) Germany (d) Ireland
Answer: (d)
Solution: Canada is a distractor because we borrowed the federation-with-strong-centre scheme from it, not DPSPs. The Directive Principles in Part IV were borrowed from the Irish Constitution (which itself took them from the Spanish Constitution), so the answer is Ireland.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Indian Constitution at Work

8. A Money Bill can be introduced only in:
(a) The Rajya Sabha (b) The Lok Sabha (c) Either House (d) A joint sitting
Answer: (b)
Solution: 'Either House' tempts students who forget the special status of Money Bills. Under Article 109, a Money Bill can be introduced only in the Lok Sabha, and only on the recommendation of the President, so the answer is the Lok Sabha.
Source: M. Laxmikanth, Indian Polity

9. Who is known as the 'Father of the Indian Constitution'?
(a) Jawaharlal Nehru (b) Sardar Vallabhbhai Patel (c) Dr. Rajendra Prasad (d) Dr. B. R. Ambedkar
Answer: (d)
Solution: Dr. Rajendra Prasad is the tempting wrong option because he was the President of the Constituent Assembly, but presiding is not drafting. Dr. B. R. Ambedkar chaired the Drafting Committee and is honoured as the Father of the Indian Constitution, so the answer is Dr. B. R. Ambedkar.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Indian Constitution at Work

10. The Constitution of India was adopted by the Constituent Assembly on:
(a) 15 August 1947 (b) 2 October 1950 (c) 26 January 1950 (d) 26 November 1949
Answer: (d)
Solution: 26 January 1950 is the classic chronological trap — that is when the Constitution came into force, not when it was adopted. The Constituent Assembly adopted the Constitution on 26 November 1949 (now celebrated as Constitution Day), so the answer is 26 November 1949.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Indian Constitution at Work

11. Which Schedule of the Constitution contains the list of recognised languages?
(a) Seventh Schedule (b) Ninth Schedule (c) Eighth Schedule (d) Tenth Schedule
Answer: (c)
Solution: The Seventh Schedule is tempting because it contains lists — but those are the Union, State and Concurrent legislative lists. The Eighth Schedule lists the recognised languages (originally 14, now 22), so the answer is the Eighth Schedule.
Source: M. Laxmikanth, Indian Polity

12. The Tenth Schedule of the Constitution deals with:
(a) Panchayati Raj (b) Anti-defection provisions (c) Municipalities (d) Official languages
Answer: (b)
Solution: Panchayati Raj (Eleventh Schedule) and Municipalities (Twelfth Schedule) are near-identical distractors added by the 73rd and 74th Amendments. The Tenth Schedule, added by the 52nd Amendment, 1985, contains the anti-defection law, so the answer is anti-defection provisions.
Source: M. Laxmikanth, Indian Polity

13. Who has the power to decide whether a Bill is a Money Bill or not?
(a) The Speaker of the Lok Sabha (b) The President (c) The Chairman of the Rajya Sabha (d) The Finance Minister
Answer: (a)
Solution: The President seems plausible since his recommendation is needed to introduce a Money Bill, but recommendation is not certification. Under Article 110(3), the decision of the Speaker of the Lok Sabha on whether a Bill is a Money Bill is final, so the answer is the Speaker of the Lok Sabha.
Source: M. Laxmikanth, Indian Polity

14. The Preamble of the Indian Constitution declares India to be all of the following EXCEPT:
(a) Sovereign (b) Socialist (c) Federal (d) Secular
Answer: (c)
Solution: 'Socialist' and 'Secular' are traps in reverse — students often think they were always in the Preamble, but they were added by the 42nd Amendment, 1976; still, they are in the Preamble today. The word 'Federal' does not appear in the Preamble at all, so the answer is Federal.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Indian Constitution at Work

15. Under which Article can the President promulgate an Ordinance when Parliament is not in session?
(a) Article 352 (b) Article 213 (c) Article 123 (d) Article 143
Answer: (c)
Solution: Article 213 is the near-perfect trap — it is the identical ordinance power, but of the Governor of a State, not the President. Article 123 empowers the President to promulgate ordinances when either House is not in session, so the answer is Article 123.
Source: M. Laxmikanth, Indian Polity

16. The Fundamental Rights in the Indian Constitution are inspired by the Constitution of:
(a) UK (b) Canada (c) USA (d) Australia
Answer: (c)
Solution: The UK tempts students because our parliamentary system comes from there, but Britain has no codified fundamental rights charter. Part III of our Constitution is modelled on the Bill of Rights of the USA, so the answer is USA.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Indian Constitution at Work

17. How many members can the President nominate to the Rajya Sabha?
(a) 12 (b) 10 (c) 2 (d) 14
Answer: (a)
Solution: 2 is the old trap — that was the Anglo-Indian nomination to the Lok Sabha (abolished by the 104th Amendment). Under Article 80, the President nominates 12 members to the Rajya Sabha from fields like literature, science, art and social service, so the answer is 12.
Source: M. Laxmikanth, Indian Polity

18. The 'Basic Structure' doctrine of the Constitution was propounded by the Supreme Court in:
(a) Golaknath case (b) Minerva Mills case (c) Kesavananda Bharati case (d) Maneka Gandhi case
Answer: (c)
Solution: The Golaknath case (1967) is the tempting predecessor — it held Parliament could not amend Fundamental Rights, but it did not propound the basic structure doctrine. That doctrine was laid down in the Kesavananda Bharati case (1973), so the answer is Kesavananda Bharati case.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Indian Constitution at Work

19. Which Article empowers the Supreme Court to issue writs for the enforcement of Fundamental Rights?
(a) Article 32 (b) Article 226 (c) Article 136 (d) Article 141
Answer: (a)
Solution: Article 226 is the classic confusion — it is the writ power too, but of the High Courts, and it covers legal rights as well. Article 32 empowers the Supreme Court directly and is itself a Fundamental Right, called by Ambedkar the 'heart and soul' of the Constitution, so the answer is Article 32.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Indian Constitution at Work

20. The Vice-President of India is elected by:
(a) Elected members of both Houses only (b) Members of both Houses of Parliament (c) An electoral college including State Legislatures (d) Members of the Lok Sabha only
Answer: (b)
Solution: Option (c) is the trap — it describes the President's electoral college, which includes elected MLAs of States. The Vice-President is elected by members of both Houses of Parliament (elected and nominated) voting by proportional representation, so the answer is members of both Houses of Parliament.
Source: M. Laxmikanth, Indian Polity

21. Consider the following statements about a Constitutional Amendment Bill:
1. It can be introduced in either House of Parliament.
2. It requires prior recommendation of the President.
3. There is no provision for a joint sitting in case of disagreement.
Which of the statements given above is/are correct?
(a) 1 and 3 only (b) 1 and 2 only (c) 2 and 3 only (d) 1, 2 and 3
Answer: (a)
Solution: Statement 2 is the almost-correct trap — prior presidential recommendation applies to Money Bills and some other bills, not to Constitutional Amendment Bills under Article 368. An amendment bill can be introduced in either House, and Article 108's joint sitting does not apply to it, so the answer is 1 and 3 only.
Source: M. Laxmikanth, Indian Polity

22. Which of the following pairs is correctly matched?
(a) Anti-defection — Twelfth Schedule (b) Panchayats — Tenth Schedule (c) Municipalities — Eleventh Schedule (d) Concurrent List — Seventh Schedule
Answer: (d)
Solution: The three wrong options form a schedule-shuffling trap: anti-defection is the Tenth Schedule, Panchayats the Eleventh, and Municipalities the Twelfth — each option swaps them by one. The Concurrent List is indeed in the Seventh Schedule, so the answer is Concurrent List — Seventh Schedule.
Source: M. Laxmikanth, Indian Polity

23. Consider the following statements about the Attorney General of India:
1. He is appointed by the President under Article 76.
2. He has the right to speak in either House of Parliament but no right to vote.
3. He must be qualified to be appointed a judge of the Supreme Court.
Which of the statements given above is/are correct?
(a) 1 and 2 only (b) 2 and 3 only (c) 1 and 3 only (d) 1, 2 and 3
Answer: (d)
Solution: Students often reject statement 3, confusing the Attorney General with the Solicitor General or assuming a lower bar; in fact Article 76 requires Supreme Court judge qualifications. All three statements correctly describe the Attorney General's position, so the answer is 1, 2 and 3.
Source: M. Laxmikanth, Indian Polity

24. Arrange the following events in correct chronological order:
1. Golaknath case
2. Kesavananda Bharati case
3. 42nd Constitutional Amendment
4. Minerva Mills case
(a) 2-1-4-3 (b) 2-1-3-4 (c) 1-3-2-4 (d) 1-2-3-4
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is placing the 42nd Amendment before Kesavananda; in fact the 42nd Amendment (1976) was Parliament's response to the basic structure doctrine of 1973. The correct order is Golaknath (1967), Kesavananda (1973), 42nd Amendment (1976), Minerva Mills (1980), so the answer is 1-2-3-4.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Indian Constitution at Work

25. Consider the following statements about the Speaker of the Lok Sabha:
1. He continues in office even after the dissolution of the Lok Sabha until the new House elects a Speaker.
2. He can be removed only by a resolution passed by a majority of all the then members of the Lok Sabha.
3. His salary is charged on the Consolidated Fund of India.
Which of the statements given above is/are correct?
(a) 1 and 2 only (b) 1, 2 and 3 (c) 1 and 3 only (d) 2 and 3 only
Answer: (b)
Solution: Statement 1 looks wrong to many because ministers cease with dissolution, but the Speaker is a deliberate exception — he vacates office only just before the first meeting of the new Lok Sabha. Removal needs an effective majority (all the then members) after 14 days' notice, and his salary is charged on the Consolidated Fund, so the answer is 1, 2 and 3.
Source: M. Laxmikanth, Indian Polity
