101. Which Article of the Indian Constitution deals with the appointment of the Chief Minister of a State?
(a) Article 163 (b) Article 165 (c) Article 164 (d) Article 167
Answer: (c)
Solution: Article 163 provides for the Council of Ministers to aid and advise the Governor, which tempts many candidates, but the actual appointment of the Chief Minister is made by the Governor under Article 164(1). Article 165 concerns the Advocate-General and Article 167 the duties of the Chief Minister, so the answer is Article 164.
Source: M. Laxmikanth, Indian Polity

102. The concept of 'Judicial Review' in the Indian Constitution is borrowed from which country?
(a) United Kingdom (b) Ireland (c) Canada (d) United States of America
Answer: (d)
Solution: Candidates often pick the UK because of parliamentary sovereignty, but in Britain courts cannot strike down Acts of Parliament; judicial review is an American feature (Marbury v. Madison, 1803). Canada gave us the federation with a strong centre and Ireland the Directive Principles, so the answer is the United States of America.
Source: NCERT Class 11, Indian Constitution at Work

103. Which of the following is NOT a condition for the disqualification of a Member of Parliament under the Tenth Schedule?
(a) Voluntarily giving up membership of the political party (b) Criticising the party leadership publicly in a press conference (c) Abstaining from voting contrary to the party whip without prior permission (d) Voting contrary to the party whip without prior permission
Answer: (b)
Solution: The Tenth Schedule (Anti-Defection Law) covers voluntarily giving up membership and voting or abstaining against the whip without permission; mere public criticism of the leadership, however unpopular, is not a ground for disqualification. Hence the answer is criticising the party leadership publicly in a press conference.
Source: M. Laxmikanth, Indian Polity

104. The 91st Constitutional Amendment Act, 2003 is related to which of the following?
(a) Limiting the size of the Council of Ministers to 15% of the legislature (b) Right to Education (c) Reservation for Economically Weaker Sections (d) Goods and Services Tax
Answer: (a)
Solution: The Right to Education came through the 86th Amendment (2002), EWS reservation through the 103rd Amendment (2019), and GST through the 101st Amendment (2016) — a classic chronological trap. The 91st Amendment capped the Council of Ministers at 15% of the total strength of the House, so the answer is limiting the size of the Council of Ministers to 15% of the legislature.
Source: M. Laxmikanth, Indian Polity

105. Who among the following presides over a joint sitting of the two Houses of Parliament summoned under Article 108?
(a) The President of India (b) The Speaker of the Lok Sabha (c) The Vice-President of India (d) The Chief Justice of India
Answer: (b)
Solution: The President only summons the joint sitting and the Vice-President has no role in it, which confuses many. The Speaker of the Lok Sabha presides, and in his absence the Deputy Speaker, so the answer is the Speaker of the Lok Sabha.
Source: M. Laxmikanth, Indian Polity

106. Under which Article can the President of India promulgate an Ordinance when Parliament is not in session?
(a) Article 123 (b) Article 213 (c) Article 352 (d) Article 356
Answer: (a)
Solution: Article 213 is the tempting wrong choice because it also deals with ordinances, but it applies to State Governors, not the President. Article 352 is National Emergency and Article 356 is President's Rule, so the answer is Article 123.
Source: NCERT Class 11, Indian Constitution at Work

107. The maximum life of an ordinance promulgated by the President is:
(a) Six months (b) Six weeks (c) Six months and six weeks (d) One year
Answer: (c)
Solution: An ordinance ceases to operate six weeks after Parliament reassembles, and since the maximum gap between two sessions can be six months, the maximum possible life is six months plus six weeks. Choosing only six months or only six weeks is the common trap, so the answer is six months and six weeks.
Source: M. Laxmikanth, Indian Polity

108. Which committee of Parliament examines the report of the Comptroller and Auditor General of India regarding public undertakings?
(a) Public Accounts Committee (b) Committee on Public Undertakings (c) Estimates Committee (d) Rules Committee
Answer: (b)
Solution: The Public Accounts Committee also examines CAG reports, which is the trap, but it deals with government departments and appropriation accounts; CAG reports on public undertakings go to the Committee on Public Undertakings. Hence the answer is the Committee on Public Undertakings.
Source: M. Laxmikanth, Indian Polity

109. The 'Basic Structure' doctrine of the Indian Constitution was propounded by the Supreme Court in which case?
(a) Golaknath v. State of Punjab (b) Kesavananda Bharati v. State of Kerala (c) Minerva Mills v. Union of India (d) S.R. Bommai v. Union of India
Answer: (b)
Solution: Golaknath (1967) held that Parliament cannot amend Fundamental Rights, which is close but not the basic structure doctrine; Minerva Mills (1980) only applied and strengthened it. The doctrine was first propounded in Kesavananda Bharati (1973), so the answer is Kesavananda Bharati v. State of Kerala.
Source: NCERT Class 11, Indian Constitution at Work

110. Which of the following pairs is correctly matched?
(a) Article 360 — President's Rule in a State (b) Article 368 — Power of Parliament to amend the Constitution (c) Article 356 — Financial Emergency (d) Article 352 — Failure of constitutional machinery in a State
Answer: (b)
Solution: The three emergency articles are a classic confusion trap: Article 352 is National Emergency, Article 356 is President's Rule (failure of constitutional machinery), and Article 360 is Financial Emergency. Only the amendment provision is correctly matched, so the answer is Article 368 — Power of Parliament to amend the Constitution.
Source: M. Laxmikanth, Indian Polity

111. The salary and allowances of the Comptroller and Auditor General of India are charged upon:
(a) The Consolidated Fund of India (b) The Contingency Fund of India (c) The Public Account of India (d) The Consolidated Fund of the States
Answer: (a)
Solution: The Contingency Fund is for unforeseen expenditure and the Public Account holds money like provident funds, neither of which carries charged expenditure. Like the President, judges and the UPSC chairman, the CAG's salary is charged on the Consolidated Fund of India and is not votable, so the answer is the Consolidated Fund of India.
Source: M. Laxmikanth, Indian Polity

112. Which of the following bodies is a constitutional body?
(a) NITI Aayog (b) Union Public Service Commission (c) National Human Rights Commission (d) Central Bureau of Investigation
Answer: (b)
Solution: NITI Aayog is an executive creation, the NHRC is a statutory body under the 1993 Act, and the CBI derives powers from the Delhi Special Police Establishment Act, 1946 — all non-constitutional. The UPSC is established directly under Article 315, so the answer is the Union Public Service Commission.
Source: NCERT Class 11, Indian Constitution at Work

113. The 73rd Constitutional Amendment Act added which Schedule to the Constitution?
(a) Ninth Schedule (b) Tenth Schedule (c) Eleventh Schedule (d) Twelfth Schedule
Answer: (c)
Solution: The Eleventh and Twelfth Schedules are easily confused: the 73rd Amendment (Panchayats) added the Eleventh Schedule with 29 subjects, while the 74th Amendment (Municipalities) added the Twelfth Schedule with 18 subjects. Hence the answer is the Eleventh Schedule.
Source: M. Laxmikanth, Indian Polity

114. Who is the ex-officio Chairman of the Rajya Sabha?
(a) The Vice-President of India (b) The President of India (c) The Prime Minister of India (d) The Chief Justice of India
Answer: (a)
Solution: The President is part of Parliament but never presides over either House, which is the tempting error. Under Article 64, the Vice-President of India is the ex-officio Chairman of the Rajya Sabha, so the answer is the Vice-President of India.
Source: NCERT Class 11, Indian Constitution at Work

115. A Money Bill can be introduced only in:
(a) The Rajya Sabha (b) A joint sitting of both Houses (c) Either House of Parliament (d) The Lok Sabha
Answer: (d)
Solution: Under Article 109, a Money Bill can be introduced only in the Lok Sabha and only on the recommendation of the President; the Rajya Sabha can merely suggest amendments within 14 days. Hence the answer is the Lok Sabha.
Source: NCERT Class 11, Indian Constitution at Work

116. The final authority to decide whether a particular bill is a Money Bill or not is:
(a) The Speaker of the Lok Sabha (b) The President of India (c) The Chairman of the Rajya Sabha (d) The Supreme Court of India
Answer: (a)
Solution: Many candidates pick the President because his recommendation is needed to introduce a Money Bill, but certification is not his function. Under Article 110(3), the decision of the Speaker of the Lok Sabha is final, so the answer is the Speaker of the Lok Sabha.
Source: M. Laxmikanth, Indian Polity

117. Which Article empowers the Supreme Court to issue writs for the enforcement of Fundamental Rights?
(a) Article 32 (b) Article 13 (c) Article 226 (d) Article 143
Answer: (a)
Solution: Article 226 also concerns writs but belongs to High Courts, not the Supreme Court — the classic similar-provision trap. Article 32, called the "heart and soul" of the Constitution by Dr. Ambedkar, empowers the Supreme Court, so the answer is Article 32.
Source: NCERT Class 11, Indian Constitution at Work

118. The total number of ministers, including the Prime Minister, in the Union Council of Ministers shall not exceed:
(a) 10% of the total strength of the Lok Sabha (b) 15% of the total strength of the Lok Sabha (c) 15% of the total strength of both Houses combined (d) 20% of the total strength of the Lok Sabha
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is "both Houses combined" — the 91st Amendment (2003) pegged the ceiling only to the strength of the Lok Sabha, not the combined Houses. The limit is 15% of the total number of members of the Lok Sabha, so the answer is 15% of the total strength of the Lok Sabha.
Source: M. Laxmikanth, Indian Polity

119. Which of the following is a subject in the Concurrent List?
(a) Railways (b) Police (c) Education (d) Agriculture
Answer: (c)
Solution: Railways is in the Union List, while Police and Agriculture are in the State List — a frequent list-confusion trap. Education was transferred from the State List to the Concurrent List by the 42nd Amendment Act, 1976, so the answer is Education.
Source: NCERT Class 11, Indian Constitution at Work

120. The impeachment of the President of India can be initiated in:
(a) Either House of Parliament (b) The Rajya Sabha only (c) The Lok Sabha only (d) A joint sitting of both Houses
Answer: (a)
Solution: Unlike a Money Bill or an ordinary bill, the impeachment charge under Article 61 may be preferred by either House of Parliament, with a 14-day notice signed by at least one-fourth of its members. Hence the answer is either House of Parliament.
Source: M. Laxmikanth, Indian Polity

121. Consider the following statements about a Constitutional Amendment Bill under Article 368:
1. It can be introduced only in the Lok Sabha.
2. It requires a prior recommendation of the President.
3. It can be introduced by a private member.
Which of the statements given above is/are correct?
(a) 1 and 2 only (b) 3 only (c) 1 and 3 only (d) 1, 2 and 3
Answer: (b)
Solution: Statements 1 and 2 are the tempting traps because they are true for Money Bills, not amendment bills: an amendment bill can be introduced in either House and needs no prior presidential recommendation. It can, however, be introduced by a minister or a private member, so only statement 3 is correct and the answer is 3 only.
Source: M. Laxmikanth, Indian Polity

122. Consider the following statements about the Attorney General of India:
1. He is a member of the Union Cabinet.
2. He has the right to speak in either House of Parliament without being a member.
3. He holds office during the pleasure of the President.
Which of the statements given above is/are correct?
(a) 1 and 2 only (b) 1, 2 and 3 (c) 1 and 3 only (d) 2 and 3 only
Answer: (d)
Solution: Statement 1 is the trap: the Attorney General attends Cabinet meetings only when invited and is not a member of the Cabinet. Under Article 88 he may speak in either House without a vote, and under Article 76 he holds office during the pleasure of the President, so the answer is 2 and 3 only.
Source: M. Laxmikanth, Indian Polity

123. Arrange the following constitutional amendment acts in chronological order of their enactment:
1. 42nd Amendment (Mini Constitution)
2. 44th Amendment (reversing emergency-era changes)
3. 52nd Amendment (Anti-Defection Law)
4. 61st Amendment (voting age reduced to 18)
(a) 1-2-3-4 (b) 2-1-3-4 (c) 1-3-2-4 (d) 2-1-4-3
Answer: (a)
Solution: The chronological trap lies between the 44th and 52nd Amendments: the 42nd came in 1976, the 44th in 1978, the 52nd in 1985, and the 61st in 1988 (effective 1989). The correct sequence is 1-2-3-4, so the answer is 1-2-3-4.
Source: M. Laxmikanth, Indian Polity

124. Consider the following statements about the Governor of a State:
1. The same person can be appointed as Governor of two or more States.
2. The Governor must be a resident of the State to which he is appointed.
3. The Governor's emoluments cannot be diminished during his term of office.
Which of the statements given above is/are correct?
(a) 1 and 2 only (b) 2 only (c) 1, 2 and 3 (d) 1 and 3 only
Answer: (d)
Solution: Statement 2 is the trap: the Constitution prescribes citizenship and a minimum age of 35 (Article 157) but no domicile requirement for the Governor. The 7th Amendment (1956) allowed one person to be Governor of two or more States, and Article 158 protects his emoluments during the term, so the answer is 1 and 3 only.
Source: M. Laxmikanth, Indian Polity

125. Consider the following statements about the Finance Commission of India:
1. It is constituted by the President every fifth year under Article 280.
2. Its recommendations are binding on the Union Government.
3. It recommends the distribution of net proceeds of taxes between the Union and the States.
Which of the statements given above is/are correct?
(a) 1 and 2 only (b) 2 and 3 only (c) 1, 2 and 3 (d) 1 and 3 only
Answer: (d)
Solution: Statement 2 is the trap: the Finance Commission's recommendations are advisory in nature, not binding, though they carry great weight. It is a constitutional body set up by the President every five years (or earlier) under Article 280 and recommends tax devolution between the Union and States, so the answer is 1 and 3 only.
Source: M. Laxmikanth, Indian Polity
