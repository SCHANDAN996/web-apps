Card 1
Front: Which Article lets the President seek the Supreme Court's advisory opinion — and is the Court bound to give it?
Back: Article 143. The Court is not bound to answer — it may decline to give its opinion — and the opinion, when given, is advisory, not binding on the President.

Card 2
Front: A Money Bill is certified by whom — and can that decision be challenged in court?
Back: The Speaker of the Lok Sabha certifies it (Article 110). In the Aadhaar case (K.S. Puttaswamy), the Supreme Court held that the Speaker's decision on a Money Bill is open to judicial review.

Card 3
Front: Which Amendment is called the "Mini-Constitution" and what did it add to the Preamble?
Back: The 42nd Amendment (1976). It added "Socialist", "Secular" and "Integrity" to the Preamble.

Card 4
Front: Trick: How do you instantly recall the three words added to the Preamble by the 42nd Amendment?
Back: Mnemonic "SSI" — Socialist, Secular, Integrity. Remember: "42 gave SSI to India."

Card 5
Front: Can the President return a Constitutional Amendment Bill for reconsideration?
Back: No. After the 24th Amendment (1971), the President must give assent to a Constitutional Amendment Bill. An ordinary bill can be returned once (suspensive veto).

Card 6
Front: Examiner's trap: "The 73rd and 74th Amendments were passed in 1992 and came into force in 1993" — true or false?
Back: True. Both passed in December 1992; the 73rd came into force on 24 April 1993 and the 74th on 1 June 1993. Trap: questions swap the two dates or the two amendments.

Card 7
Front: Which Schedule contains the anti-defection law, and which Amendment inserted it?
Back: The Tenth Schedule, inserted by the 52nd Amendment (1985). The 91st Amendment (2003) deleted the "split" provision (one-third exemption).

Card 8
Front: Trick: How to remember the original 10 Fundamental Duties come from Article 51A?
Back: Mnemonic: "51A = duties Added by the 42nd Amendment, inspired by the USSR." The 11th duty (child's education) was added by the 86th Amendment, 2002.

Card 9
Front: Which Fundamental Right did Dr. Ambedkar call the "heart and soul" of the Constitution?
Back: Article 32 — the Right to Constitutional Remedies (writ jurisdiction of the Supreme Court).

Card 10
Front: Examiner's trap: "Article 368 amendment procedure applies to creation of new states under Article 3" — correct?
Back: No. Article 4 declares that laws under Articles 2 and 3 (admission/formation of states, boundary changes) are NOT deemed amendments under Article 368 — they need only a simple majority.

Card 11
Front: How many types of emergencies does the Constitution provide, and under which Articles?
Back: Three: National Emergency (Article 352), President's Rule/State Emergency (Article 356), and Financial Emergency (Article 360 — never proclaimed so far).

Card 12
Front: After the 44th Amendment (1978), on what ground alone can a National Emergency be proclaimed?
Back: "Armed rebellion" replaced "internal disturbance". Also, the President can proclaim it only on the written recommendation of the Cabinet.

Card 13
Front: Which Article provides for a joint sitting of the two Houses, who summons and presides over it, and which bills can never go to a joint sitting?
Back: Article 108. The President summons it; the Lok Sabha Speaker presides. Money Bills (Article 110) and Constitutional Amendment Bills (Article 368) cannot go to a joint sitting.

Card 14
Front: Trick: How to recall the six Fundamental Rights (freedoms) of Article 19(1)?
Back: Mnemonic "SAAMRP" — Speech and expression, Assembly (peaceful), Association, Movement (free, throughout India), Residence (anywhere in India), Profession (any occupation/trade/business). Note: the right to property was removed from Article 19 by the 44th Amendment (now Article 300A).

Card 15
Front: Who decides whether a member of Parliament is disqualified on grounds other than defection — and whose opinion is binding?
Back: The President decides under Article 103, but must act according to the opinion of the Election Commission. For defection (Tenth Schedule), the Speaker/Chairman decides.

Card 16
Front: What is the maximum gap allowed between two sessions of Parliament, and which Article implies it?
Back: Six months. Article 85(1) requires that the gap between the last sitting of one session and the first sitting of the next not exceed six months.

Card 17
Front: Examiner's trap: "The Vice-President is a member of the Rajya Sabha" — true or false?
Back: False. The Vice-President is the ex-officio Chairman of the Rajya Sabha (Article 64) but is NOT a member of it — hence he cannot vote except a casting vote.

Card 18
Front: Which Amendment lowered the voting age from 21 to 18, and which Article did it change?
Back: The 61st Amendment (1988), amending Article 326 (elections on the basis of adult suffrage).

Card 19
Front: Under which Article can Parliament amend the Constitution, and which case fixed the "basic structure" limit?
Back: Article 368. Kesavananda Bharati v. State of Kerala (1973) held Parliament cannot alter the Constitution's basic structure.

Card 20
Front: Trick: How to remember which writ means what?
Back: Mnemonic: "Habeas = have the body; Mandamus = we command (a public official); Prohibition = stop before; Certiorari = quash after; Quo warranto = by what authority?" Remember: "Prohibition prevents, Certiorari corrects."
