# 📊 PYQ Analysis — Awards & Honours (English)

## 1. How Exams Ask Questions from This Chapter

Awards & Honours is a **high-frequency, low-effort** chapter across SSC CGL/CHSL, IBPS PO/Clerk, RRB NTPC, and State PCS prelims. Questions are almost always **one-liner factual recalls** — the examiner's real weapon is not depth but **confusion between similar-looking facts**. Based on the pattern of official papers, questions cluster around these recurring sub-topics:

- **Bharat Ratna** — first recipients (1954: C. Rajagopalachari, S. Radhakrishnan, C.V. Raman), posthumous awards, youngest recipient (Sachin Tendulkar, 2014), foreign recipients (Khan Abdul Ghaffar Khan 1987, Nelson Mandela 1990).
- **Padma Awards** — hierarchy (Vibhushan > Bhushan > Shri), the 1954 institution year, the rule that Bharat Ratna/Padma awards cannot be used as titles (Article 18 context).
- **Nobel Prize Indians** — Rabindranath Tagore (Literature, 1913, first Asian), C.V. Raman (Physics, 1930), Mother Teresa (Peace, 1979), Amartya Sen (Economics, 1998), Kailash Satyarthi (Peace, 2014). Trap: confusing field or year.
- **Sports awards** — Major Dhyan Chand Khel Ratna (renamed from Rajiv Gandhi Khel Ratna in 2021), Arjuna Award (1961), Dronacharya Award (1985, coaches), Dhyan Chand Award (lifetime, 2002).
- **Gallantry awards** — Param Vir Chakra > Maha Vir Chakra > Vir Chakra (wartime); Ashoka Chakra > Kirti Chakra > Shaurya Chakra (peacetime). First PVC: Major Somnath Sharma (1947, posthumous).
- **Film & literature** — Dadasaheb Phalke Award (first: Devika Rani, 1969), Jnanpith Award (first: G. Sankara Kurup, Malayalam, 1965), Sahitya Akademi Award.
- **International awards** — Nobel categories (6), Pulitzer, Booker, Oscar; Gandhi Peace Prize; UN awards.

## 2. Exam-Wise Pattern (in words, not numbers)

- **SSC (CGL/CHSL/MTS):** Direct matching questions — "Who received X award in year Y?" Bharat Ratna and sports awards dominate.
- **Banking (IBPS/SBI):** Recent award winners (last 1–2 years) mixed with static firsts; often appears in GA section as "Which of the following is correctly matched?"
- **Railway (NTPC/Group D):** Simplest level — first recipients, award fields, Nobel laureates.
- **State PCS:** Adds state-specific awards and national awards won by personalities of that state.

## 3. The Examiner's Traps (Jaal Types)

1. **Almost Correct:** "Rabindranath Tagore won the Nobel Prize for Literature in **1911**" — correct person, correct field, wrong year (1913).
2. **Chronological Confusion:** Swapping institution years — Arjuna Award 1961 vs Dronacharya 1985 vs Dhyan Chand Award 2002.
3. **Similar Names:** Khel Ratna vs Arjuna vs Dhyan Chand Award; Kirti Chakra vs Shaurya Chakra; Jnanpith vs Sahitya Akademi.
4. **Negative Phrasing:** "Which of the following is **NOT** a peacetime gallantry award?" — students misread and pick the wartime one.
5. **Field Swap:** "C.V. Raman won the Nobel in Chemistry" (it was Physics, 1930, for the Raman Effect).
6. **Renaming Trap:** Old name "Rajiv Gandhi Khel Ratna" in options when the question asks the current name (Major Dhyan Chand Khel Ratna, renamed 2021).

## 4. Top 10 High-Yield PYQ-Style Questions

**Q1.** Who was the first recipient of the Bharat Ratna along with two others in 1954?
(a) Jawaharlal Nehru (b) C. Rajagopalachari (c) B.R. Ambedkar (d) Vallabhbhai Patel
**Answer:** (b)
**Solution:** In 1954 the first Bharat Ratnas went to C. Rajagopalachari, S. Radhakrishnan and C.V. Raman. Nehru received it in 1955 — a classic chronological trap.
**Trap type:** Chronological Confusion | **Source:** PYQ-style

**Q2.** The first Nobel Prize won by an Indian was in which field?
(a) Physics (b) Peace (c) Literature (d) Medicine
**Answer:** (c)
**Solution:** Rabindranath Tagore won the 1913 Nobel in Literature for *Gitanjali* — the first Asian Nobel laureate. Many students wrongly pick Physics thinking of C.V. Raman (1930).
**Trap type:** Similar Names | **Source:** PYQ-style

**Q3.** Which of the following is NOT a peacetime gallantry award?
(a) Ashoka Chakra (b) Kirti Chakra (c) Vir Chakra (d) Shaurya Chakra
**Answer:** (c)
**Solution:** Vir Chakra is a **wartime** gallantry award. The peacetime series is Ashoka → Kirti → Shaurya Chakra. Read "NOT" carefully.
**Trap type:** Negative Phrasing | **Source:** PYQ-style

**Q4.** The Arjuna Award was instituted in which year?
(a) 1956 (b) 1961 (c) 1985 (d) 2002
**Answer:** (b)
**Solution:** Arjuna Award — 1961; Dronacharya — 1985; Dhyan Chand Award — 2002. Examiners deliberately place all three years as options.
**Trap type:** Chronological Confusion | **Source:** PYQ-style

**Q5.** Who was the first recipient of the Dadasaheb Phalke Award (1969)?
(a) Satyajit Ray (b) Devika Rani (c) Prithviraj Kapoor (d) B.N. Sircar
**Answer:** (b)
**Solution:** Devika Rani received the first Dadasaheb Phalke Award in 1969. Satyajit Ray received it in 1984 — the "almost correct" distractor.
**Trap type:** Almost Correct | **Source:** PYQ-style

**Q6.** The first Jnanpith Award (1965) was given for a work in which language?
(a) Hindi (b) Bengali (c) Malayalam (d) Urdu
**Answer:** (c)
**Solution:** G. Sankara Kurup won the first Jnanpith for *Odakkuzhal* (Malayalam). First Hindi Jnanpith: Sumitranandan Pant (1968, *Chidambara*).
**Trap type:** Almost Correct | **Source:** PYQ-style

**Q7.** The Rajiv Gandhi Khel Ratna Award was renamed in 2021 as:
(a) Major Dhyan Chand Khel Ratna Award (b) Dhyan Chand Lifetime Award (c) Arjuna Khel Ratna (d) National Sports Ratna
**Answer:** (a)
**Solution:** Renamed in August 2021 after hockey legend Major Dhyan Chand. Do not confuse with the separate "Dhyan Chand Award" for lifetime achievement (2002).
**Trap type:** Similar Names | **Source:** PYQ-style

**Q8.** Who was the first Param Vir Chakra awardee?
(a) Major Somnath Sharma (b) Lance Naik Albert Ekka (c) Capt. Vikram Batra (d) 2/Lt Arun Khetarpal
**Answer:** (a)
**Solution:** Major Somnath Sharma, 1947 (posthumous, Battle of Badgam). All other options are also PVC winners — a deliberate similar-facts trap.
**Trap type:** Similar Names | **Source:** PYQ-style

**Q9.** Amartya Sen received the Nobel Prize in 1998 for:
(a) Peace (b) Literature (c) Economic Sciences (d) Physics
**Answer:** (c)
**Solution:** Nobel in Economic Sciences, 1998, for contributions to welfare economics. The Peace option lures students who confuse him with Mother Teresa (1979) or Satyarthi (2014).
**Trap type:** Field Swap | **Source:** PYQ-style

**Q10.** Which of the following foreigners has received the Bharat Ratna?
(a) Muhammad Ali Jinnah (b) Nelson Mandela (c) Aung San Suu Kyi (d) Malala Yousafzai
**Answer:** (b)
**Solution:** Nelson Mandela (1990) and Khan Abdul Ghaffar Khan (1987) are the two non-citizen Bharat Ratna recipients. Jinnah never received it — a frequent rumour-based trap.
**Trap type:** Almost Correct | **Source:** PYQ-style

## 5. The 15/45-Second Rule Applied

**Q3 above — 15-second question:** The moment you see "NOT a peacetime gallantry award," recall the two triads (Param/Maha/Vir = war; Ashoka/Kirti/Shaurya = peace). Vir Chakra stands out instantly. **Do not spend more than 15 seconds** — this is pure recall; thinking longer adds no value.

**Q7 above — 45-second question:** The renaming question needs care because two options contain "Dhyan Chand." Budget up to 45 seconds: (1) recall the 2021 renaming, (2) eliminate "Lifetime Award" (that's the 2002 Dhyan Chand Award), (3) confirm "Major Dhyan Chand Khel Ratna." If after 45 seconds you are still unsure between two Dhyan Chand options, mark the one with "Khel Ratna" in it and move on — never leave it blank if there is no negative marking, and skip entirely if there is and you cannot eliminate at least two options.

**General rule for this chapter:** ~80% of Awards questions are 15-second recalls. Reserve 45-second thinking only for matching-type ("which pair is correct?") and NOT/EXCEPT questions, where misreading costs more than ignorance.

## 6. Preparation Takeaways

- Master the **"firsts" list** — first Bharat Ratna, first PVC, first Phalke, first Jnanpith, first Nobel. These recur across all exams.
- Learn awards in **triads and hierarchies** (gallantry, Padma, sports) — examiners test the order, not just the names.
- Note **renamings and years of institution** separately; they are the cheapest traps to fall for.
- For recent winners, revise only the last 1–2 years before the exam; static firsts never change.
