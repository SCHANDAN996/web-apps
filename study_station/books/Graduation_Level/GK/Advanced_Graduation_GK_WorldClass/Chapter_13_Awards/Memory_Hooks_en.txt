🎯 HOOK 1: Bharat Ratna — Maximum 3 per year
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
"RATNA = 3 gems only!"
Think of a crown with exactly THREE jewels — no matter how many deserve it, only 3 Bharat Ratnas can be given in one year. Article 18(1) bars titles, but Bharat Ratna is not a title at all — it is not used as a prefix or suffix with the name, so Article 18(1) simply does not apply to it.

Bharat Ratna → Padma Vibhushan → Padma Bhushan → Padma Shri
(B → V → B → S — "Bhaiya, Very Big Salute!")

🎯 HOOK 3: First Bharat Ratna (1954)
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
"CRR — C. Rajagopalachari, Radhakrishnan, Raman"
Three greats in the very first year: the last Governor-General, the philosopher-President, and the scientist. Remember: "Rajaji, Radha, Raman — 1954 ka Ratna dhamaka!"

⏭️ SKIP STRATEGY — When to Leave the Question
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

1. **The "Year + Recipient + Field" triple trap:** If a question asks "Who won the Jnanpith Award in 1987 for Hindi literature?" and you cannot recall even the decade confidently, SKIP instantly. Award-year combinations are pure memory — no logic can rescue you, and a wild guess costs negative marks.

2. **The "All four look foreign" Nobel question:** If all four options are unfamiliar foreign Nobel laureates and you don't recognise even one, skip. Never gamble between four unknown names — the probability of a correct blind guess (25%) is worse than the negative marking penalty.

3. **The "Which award is NOT matched correctly" question:** If you can verify only 2 of the 4 pairs, attempt it (elimination works). But if you can verify only 1 pair or none, skip — "NOT matched" questions punish partial knowledge because the wrong pair is designed to look almost right.

4. **The 15/45 rule:** Give an award question 15 seconds. If the answer doesn't surface, mark it and move on. Return only if time remains — award facts either click instantly or they don't; staring longer never helps.
