# Key Facts & Figures — Awards & Honours

## Part 1: High-Yield Facts

| Fact | Description | Examiner's Trap | Trap Type |
|---|---|---|---|
| Bharat Ratna | Highest civilian award of India; instituted 1954; max 3 per year (exceptions: 1999 — 4; 2024 — 5) | "Max 4 per year" is wrong; Padma awards have no such cap | Nearly-correct statement |
| First Bharat Ratna (1954) | C. Rajagopalachari, S. Radhakrishnan, C.V. Raman | Nehru was NOT in the first batch (he got it in 1955) | Chronology confusion |
| Youngest Bharat Ratna | Sachin Tendulkar (2014, age 40) | Often confused with youngest Padma awardees | Similar names/facts |
| First foreigner Bharat Ratna | Khan Abdul Ghaffar Khan (1987); also Nelson Mandela (1990) | Mandela called "first foreigner" — wrong | Nearly-correct statement |
| Posthumous Bharat Ratna | Lal Bahadur Shastri (1966) was the first posthumous recipient | Confused with first posthumous Padma | Chronology confusion |
| Bharat Ratna to non-citizen | Mother Teresa (1980) — naturalised Indian citizen; Khan Abdul Ghaffar Khan was a Pakistani citizen | "Only citizens can receive it" — false | Absolute statement trap |
| Padma hierarchy | Padma Vibhushan > Padma Bhushan > Padma Shri (2nd, 3rd, 4th highest civilian) | Order often shuffled in options | Order swap |
| Padma Vibhushan categories | Peacetime only; no posthumous bar (posthumous allowed) | "Padma awards cannot be given posthumously" — false (allowed, except rare) | Nearly-correct statement |
| Awards announced on | Republic Day (Padma) each year; Bharat Ratna announced separately | "Announced on Independence Day" — wrong | Date confusion |
| Award suspension | Padma awards suspended 1977–80 and 1992–95 (court cases) | Rarely asked but appears in advanced papers | Obscure fact |
| Gandhi Peace Prize | Instituted 1995 by the Government of India to commemorate the 125th birth anniversary of Mahatma Gandhi (1994); ₹1 crore | Confused with Indira Gandhi Peace Prize (1986); "instituted in 1994" — wrong | Similar names / Date confusion |
| Indira Gandhi Prize | For Peace, Disarmament and Development; instituted 1986 | Swapped with Gandhi Peace Prize year | Similar names |
| Jnanpith Award | Highest literary award; instituted 1961; first to G. Sankara Kurup (Malayalam, 1965) | "First to a Hindi writer" — wrong (first Hindi: Sumitranandan Pant, 1968) | Nearly-correct statement |
| First woman Jnanpith | Ashapurna Devi (Bengali, 1976) | Confused with Mahadevi Verma (1982, first Hindi woman) | Similar names |
| Sahitya Akademi Award | 24 languages (22 scheduled + English + Rajasthani); instituted 1954 | "22 languages" is the trap | Number trap |
| Saraswati Samman | Instituted 1991 by K.K. Birla Foundation; for literary work in any of 22 scheduled languages | Confused with Vyas Samman (also Birla Foundation) | Similar names |
| Vyas Samman | For Hindi literary work; instituted 1991 | Swapped with Saraswati Samman | Similar names |
| Dadasaheb Phalke Award | Highest film award; instituted 1969; first to Devika Rani (1969) | "First to Dadasaheb Phalke himself" — wrong | Nearly-correct statement |
| National Film Awards | Given by Directorate of Film Festivals; Best Feature Film = Swarna Kamal (Golden Lotus) | Confused with Dadasaheb Phalke (lifetime achievement) | Category confusion |
| Arjuna Award | For outstanding sportspersons; instituted 1961 | Confused with Dronacharya (coaches) | Similar names |
| Dronacharya Award | For coaches; instituted 1985 | "For players" — wrong | Category confusion |
| Major Dhyan Chand Khel Ratna | Highest sports honour; renamed from Rajiv Gandhi Khel Ratna in 2021; first (1991–92): Viswanathan Anand | Old name still used in options | Name-change trap |
| Dhyan Chand Award | Lifetime achievement in sports; instituted 2002 | Confused with Khel Ratna | Similar names |
| Tenzing Norgay National Adventure Award | For adventure sports (land, water, air, lifetime) | Rarely asked; confused with Arjuna | Category confusion |
| Rashtriya Khel Protsahan Puruskar | For sports promotion (corporates, institutions) | Mixed with individual awards | Category confusion |
| Nobel Prize fields | Physics, Chemistry, Medicine, Literature, Peace, Economics (Economics added 1968 by Sveriges Riksbank) | "6 original fields" — wrong; Economics is not original | Nearly-correct statement |
| Nobel Peace Prize venue | Oslo, Norway; all others in Stockholm, Sweden | "All in Stockholm" — wrong | Nearly-correct statement |
| First Indian Nobel | Rabindranath Tagore (Literature, 1913) — first Indian citizen; Ronald Ross (Medicine, 1902) was born in India but was British | C.V. Raman often picked (he was first in science, 1930) | Similar names |
| Indian / India-connected Nobel laureates | Ronald Ross (1902, born in India), Rudyard Kipling (1907, born in India), Rabindranath Tagore (1913), C.V. Raman (1930), Hargobind Khorana (1968), Mother Teresa (1979), S. Chandrasekhar (1983), Amartya Sen (1998), V.S. Naipaul (2001, Trinidad-born of Indian origin), Venkatraman Ramakrishnan (2009), Kailash Satyarthi (2014), Abhijit Banerjee (2019) | Ross/Kipling were British citizens; Naipaul/Banerjee citizenship status questioned in traps | Citizenship trap |
| First Indian woman Nobel | Mother Teresa (Peace, 1979) | Confused with first Indian woman in other awards | Category confusion |
| Nobel cannot be posthumous | Since 1974 rule (exceptions: Dag Hammarskjöld 1961, Erik Axel Karlfeldt 1931, Ralph Steinman 2011 — died before announcement) | "Never posthumous" is nearly right but has exceptions | Absolute statement trap |
| Max Nobel per prize | Shared by max 3 persons | "Max 2" — wrong | Number trap |
| Pulitzer Prize | US award for journalism, literature, music; instituted 1917 (Joseph Pulitzer's will) | Confused with Booker (UK) | Similar names |
| Booker Prize | UK; for English fiction; instituted 1969; first Indian-origin winner: Salman Rushdie (1981); first Indian woman: Arundhati Roy (1997, The God of Small Things) | "First Indian winner = Arundhati Roy" — wrong | Nearly-correct statement |
| International Booker | For translated fiction; Geetanjali Shree won 2022 (Tomb of Sand, Hindi) | Confused with Booker Prize proper | Similar names |
| Oscar (Academy Awards) | Instituted 1929; first Indian: Bhanu Athaiya (Costume Design, Gandhi, 1982) | Satyajit Ray got Honorary Oscar 1992 — not first | Chronology confusion |
| Satyajit Ray Oscar | Honorary Award, 1992 (lifetime achievement) | "Competitive Oscar" — wrong | Category confusion |
| A.R. Rahman Oscars | 2 Oscars for Slumdog Millionaire (2009): Best Original Score, Best Original Song | "One Oscar" — wrong | Number trap |
| Grammy | Music award by Recording Academy (US); first Indian: Ravi Shankar | Confused with Oscar | Similar names |
| Ramon Magsaysay Award | "Asia's Nobel"; instituted 1957 (Philippines); first Indian: Vinoba Bhave (1958) | Called "Asia's highest award" loosely; first winner trap | Similar names |
| Right Livelihood Award | "Alternative Nobel"; Sweden, 1980 | Confused with actual Nobel | Similar names |
| Fields Medal | "Nobel of Mathematics"; every 4 years; age limit under 40 | "Annual award" — wrong; no Nobel in Mathematics | Nearly-correct statement |
| Abel Prize | Mathematics, Norway, annual (Nobel-style) | Confused with Fields Medal | Similar names |
| Bharat Ratna design | Peepal leaf-shaped medallion; no cash; cannot be used as title (Art. 18(1)) | "Can be used as prefix/suffix" — unconstitutional | Constitutional trap |
| Article 18 | Abolishes titles; Bharat Ratna/Padma are not "titles" per SC (Balaji Raghavan case, 1996) | "Awards violate Article 18" — wrong per SC | Constitutional trap |
| National Sports Awards selection | Committee under Ministry of Youth Affairs & Sports | "Given by President directly without committee" — wrong | Process trap |
| Gallantry awards | Param Vir Chakra > Maha Vir Chakra > Vir Chakra (wartime); Ashoka Chakra > Kirti Chakra > Shaurya Chakra (peacetime) | Order and wartime/peacetime swap | Order swap |
| First Param Vir Chakra | Major Somnath Sharma (1947, posthumous) | Confused with first Ashoka Chakra | Similar names |
| Ashoka Chakra (award) | Highest peacetime gallantry; instituted 1952 | Confused with Ashoka Chakra on flag | Similar names |
| Bharat Ratna 2019 | Pranab Mukherjee, Nanaji Deshmukh (posthumous), Bhupen Hazarika (posthumous) | Year/name swaps | Chronology confusion |
| Bharat Ratna 2024 | Karpoori Thakur (posthumous), L.K. Advani, P.V. Narasimha Rao, Charan Singh, M.S. Swaminathan — 5 in one year (record) | "Max 3 rule" violated — exception year | Number trap |
| Shanti Swarup Bhatnagar Prize | Top Indian science award (CSIR); instituted 1958; for under-45 scientists | Confused with civilian awards | Category confusion |
| Vigyan Ratna / Rashtriya Vigyan Puraskar | New national science awards announced 2023 (replacing older schemes) | Old scheme names still in options | Name-change trap |
| Param Vishisht Seva Medal | Distinguished service (military), peacetime | Confused with gallantry medals | Category confusion |
| UN awards to Indians | e.g., UN Champions of the Earth (PM Modi, 2018) | Year traps | Chronology confusion |
| Legion of Honour | France's highest honour; given to several Indians (e.g., PM Modi, Grand Cross, 2023) | Confused with Order of the British Empire | Similar names |
| Bharat Ratna to institutions | Never given to institutions (only individuals) | "Given to organisations" — wrong | Absolute statement trap |

## Part 2: Time Management (15/45-Second Rule)

| Topic | Type | Time Rule | Score Maximizer? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Bharat Ratna recipients & years | Fact-based | 15 sec | ✅ Yes |
| Padma award hierarchy & rules | Fact-based | 15 sec | ✅ Yes |
| Sports awards (Khel Ratna, Arjuna, Dronacharya) | Fact-based | 15 sec | ✅ Yes |
| Nobel laureates (Indian) & fields | Fact-based | 15 sec | ✅ Yes |
| Literary awards (Jnanpith, Sahitya Akademi, Booker) | Fact-based | 15 sec | ✅ Yes |
| Film awards (Phalke, Oscar, National Film) | Fact-based | 15 sec | ✅ Yes |
| Gallantry award order (wartime vs peacetime) | Fact-based | 15 sec | ✅ Yes |
| International awards (Magsaysay, Pulitzer, Fields) | Fact-based | 15 sec | ✅ Yes |
| Statement-based: "Which of these is correct about Bharat Ratna rules?" | Analytical | 45 sec | ⚠️ Attempt only if sure |
| Match-the-following (award ↔ field ↔ first winner) | Analytical | 45 sec | ⚠️ High trap density |
| Chronology ordering (award institution years) | Analytical | 45 sec | ⚠️ Skip if 2+ years unknown |
| Renamed awards (Khel Ratna, Vigyan Puraskar) | Fact-based | 15 sec | ✅ Yes |
| Constitutional angle (Article 18, SC rulings) | Analytical | 45 sec | ⚠️ Attempt if Polity strong |
| Negative questions ("Which is NOT...") | Analytical | 45 sec | ⚠️ Read twice; underline NOT |

**Strategy:** Awards questions are ~80% fact-recall. Answer fact-based ones in 15 seconds and bank time for statement/matching questions. Never guess on chronology-ordering questions — they carry the highest trap density.
