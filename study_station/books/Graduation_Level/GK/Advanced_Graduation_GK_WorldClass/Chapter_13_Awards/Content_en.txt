# Awards & Honours

## The Medal That Was Returned

In 1915, the British Crown knighted a Bengali poet. Four years later, after the Jallianwala Bagh massacre of 1919, that same poet wrote a furious letter to the Viceroy and **gave the knighthood back**. That poet was **Rabindranath Tagore** — the first non-European to win the Nobel Prize (Literature, 1913).

Why start here? Because awards are never just metal and certificates. They carry politics, history, and national emotion — and examiners know it. Every year, SSC, Banking, and State PCS papers quietly slip in questions from awards: *Who was the first? Which award came first? Who returned which honour?* This chapter makes sure those marks are yours.

## 🎯 Learning Objectives

By the end of this chapter, you will be able to:

4. Spot the examiner's favourite traps: similar names, wrong years, and "NOT" questions.

## Part 1: India's Civilian Awards — The Big Four

Instituted in **1954**, India's civilian awards are announced every year on **Republic Day (26 January)**. In order of precedence:

| Rank | Award | Limit / Note |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Bharat Ratna** | Maximum **3 per year** |
| 2 | **Padma Vibhushan** | For exceptional and distinguished service |
| 3 | **Padma Bhushan** | Distinguished service of a high order |
| 4 | **Padma Shri** | Distinguished service in any field |

**Key facts about the Bharat Ratna:**
- First awarded in **1954** to **three** people: **C. Rajagopalachari, Dr. S. Radhakrishnan, and Dr. C.V. Raman**.
- **First woman:** Indira Gandhi (1971).
- **Youngest recipient:** Sachin Tendulkar (2014, age 40).
- **First non-citizen of India:** Khan Abdul Ghaffar Khan (1987); Nelson Mandela received it in 1990.
- It can be awarded **posthumously** — and since 2011 it is open to "any field of human endeavour" (the change allowed sportspersons like Tendulkar).

🧠 **Active Recall:** Close the book. Name the three first Bharat Ratna winners. Who was the first foreigner? What is the annual cap?

## Part 2: Gallantry Awards — For Bravery

Divided into **wartime** and **peacetime** categories:

**Wartime (in order):** Param Vir Chakra → Maha Vir Chakra → Vir Chakra
**Peacetime (in order):** Ashoka Chakra → Kirti Chakra → Shaurya Chakra

- **Param Vir Chakra (PVC):** India's **highest** wartime gallantry award. Designed by Savitri Khanolkar. First awarded to **Major Somnath Sharma** (posthumous, 1947 Indo-Pak war).
- **Ashoka Chakra:** Highest **peacetime** gallantry award. Instituted in 1952 (as Ashoka Chakra Class I, renamed in 1967).

🧠 **Active Recall:** Which is higher — Vir Chakra or Ashoka Chakra? (Trick question: they belong to different categories — wartime vs peacetime. Examiners love this confusion.)

## Part 3: Sports Awards

| Award | For | Started |
|---|---|---|
| **Major Dhyan Chand Khel Ratna** | Highest sporting honour (spectacular performance) | 1991–92 (as Rajiv Gandhi Khel Ratna; **renamed in 2021**) |
| **Arjuna Award** | Consistent outstanding performance | **1961** |
| **Dronacharya Award** | Coaches | **1985** |
| **Dhyan Chand Award** | Lifetime contribution to sports | 2002 |

- First Khel Ratna: chess legend **Viswanathan Anand** (1991–92).
- The first Arjuna Awards (1961) went to 20 sportspersons, including footballer **P.K. Banerjee** and athlete **Makhan Singh**.

**Mnemonic 🪄 — "KADC":** **K**hel Ratna → **A**rjuna → **D**ronacharya → **D**hyan Chand. Khel Ratna is King; the Coach (Dronacharya) trains the warrior (Arjuna).

## Part 4: Literature, Cinema & Arts

- **Jnanpith Award:** India's highest literary honour, instituted **1961**; first awarded **1965** to **G. Sankara Kurup** (Malayalam) for *Odakkuzhal*. First woman: **Ashapurna Devi** (Bengali, 1976).
- **Sahitya Akademi Award:** Since **1955**, for books in 24 recognised languages.
- **Dadasaheb Phalke Award:** Highest honour in **Indian cinema**, instituted **1969**; first recipient: **Devika Rani** (1969).
- **Sangeet Natak Akademi Award:** For music, dance, and drama (since 1952).

🧠 **Active Recall:** Jnanpith was *instituted* in 1961 but *first given* in 1965 — why does this gap matter? Because examiners ask both years separately!

**Mnemonic 🪄 — "61-65, Kurup took five":** Jnanpith born in '61, but Kurup had to wait till '65.

---

## Part 5: International Awards Indians Must Know

- **Nobel Prize:** From Alfred Nobel's will (1895); first awarded **1901**. Indian/India-linked laureates: **Rabindranath Tagore** (Literature, 1913), **C.V. Raman** (Physics, 1930), **Hargobind Khorana** (Medicine, 1968), **Mother Teresa** (Peace, 1979), **Subrahmanyan Chandrasekhar** (Physics, 1983), **Amartya Sen** (Economics, 1998), **Kailash Satyarthi** (Peace, 2014), **Abhijit Banerjee** (Economics, 2019).
- **Gandhi Peace Prize:** Instituted by the Government of India in **1995** (125th birth anniversary of Gandhi ji); first recipient: **Julius Nyerere** (Tanzania).
- **Bharat Ratna to foreigners:** Khan Abdul Ghaffar Khan (1987), Nelson Mandela (1990).

**Mnemonic 🪄 — "TR-KC-SKS":** Tagore → Raman → Khorana → Chandrasekhar → Teresa → Sen → Satyarthi. *"Tagore's Raman Knew Chandrasekhar; Teresa Served Kailash Selflessly."*

## 🔗 Static-Dynamic Link

Awards are static in structure but dynamic in recipients. The **list of winners changes every Republic Day** — so for the *latest* Bharat Ratna, Padma, and Khel Ratna winners, check the current-affairs section of the app. What never changes: the **order of precedence, the years of institution, and the "firsts"** — and those are exactly what this chapter locks in. (One structural change worth remembering: the Rajiv Gandhi Khel Ratna was **renamed Major Dhyan Chand Khel Ratna in 2021**.)

## ⚠️ Caution: Examiner's Trap

**Trap 1 — Chronological Confusion:**
"Jnanpith Award was first given in 1961." — *Sounds right, is wrong.* Instituted in 1961, **first awarded in 1965**. Examiners swap "instituted" and "first awarded" years constantly. Same trick with Arjuna (1961) vs Dronacharya (1985).

**Trap 2 — Similar Names:**
**Dronacharya** (for **coaches**) vs **Dhyan Chand** (lifetime achievement) vs **Khel Ratna** (top performance). All three are sports awards with similar-sounding names. Also: **Devika Rani** (first Phalke) is confused with other early actresses.

**Trap 3 — Negative Phrasing:**
"Which of the following is **NOT** a peacetime gallantry award?" — Students see "Vir Chakra" and mark it as correct because it *is* a gallantry award, forgetting the question asked for the odd one out. Always underline NOT/EXCEPT before answering.
