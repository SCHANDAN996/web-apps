# 📊 PYQ Analysis — Environment & Ecology (Basic)

**Note on method:** We do not have access to a verified question-by-question database, so this section deliberately avoids year-wise frequency tables, percentage weightages, or "N questions in 10 years" claims. Instead, it analyses the *pattern* — what kinds of questions examiners repeatedly ask from this chapter, which sub-topics dominate, and the traps they set. This is what actually helps you in the exam hall.

---

## 1. How Exams Use This Chapter

**UPSC (Prelims):** Environment is one of the heaviest static areas. Questions are statement-based (2–4 statements), often mixing a correct fact with a subtle distortion. Favourite zones: ecosystems and biomes, biodiversity hotspots, national parks/tiger reserves, conventions (Ramsar, CITES, CBD, Montreal/Kyoto/Paris), and pollution basics.

**SSC (CGL/CHSL/GD):** Direct one-liners. "Which gas is responsible for ozone depletion?", "Where is X National Park?", "What is the full form of UNEP?" Memory-based, low analysis.

**Banking (IBPS/SBI):** Environment appears mostly through schemes, reports, and conventions — often with a current-affairs wrapper. Static basics (greenhouse gases, Ramsar sites) appear in the GA section.

**Railway (NTPC/Group D):** NCERT-level direct facts: food chains, biomes, pollutants, firsts (first national park, first biosphere reserve).

**State PCS:** Mirrors UPSC pattern but adds state-specific content — state animal/bird, wetlands and parks of that state.

---

## 2. Sub-Topics That Recur Most (in words, not numbers)

- **Conventions & protocols** — Ramsar, CITES, Montreal, Kyoto, Paris, CBD, Stockholm. Examiners love swapping years, host cities, and what each convention covers.
- **Protected areas** — National Parks vs Wildlife Sanctuaries vs Biosphere Reserves vs Conservation Reserves: who declares them, what activities are allowed.
- **Biodiversity hotspots** — criteria (endemism + habitat loss) and India's four hotspots.
- **Pollution** — air (CO, SO₂, NOx, SPM), water (BOD, eutrophication, biomagnification), and specific disasters/landmarks (Minamata, Bhopal).
- **Ecosystem basics** — food chains, trophic levels, 10% energy law, ecological pyramids.
- **Climate** — greenhouse gases, ozone layer (formation vs depletion), global warming vs ozone hole confusion.
- **Institutions** — UNEP, IUCN, MoEFCC, CPCB; IUCN Red List categories.

---

## 3. The Examiner's Trap Types

1. **Almost Correct:** "Montreal Protocol deals with climate change" — it deals with ozone-depleting substances; climate change is UNFCCC's domain.
2. **Chronological Confusion:** Swapping years — Stockholm 1972, Rio 1992, Kyoto 1997, Paris 2015. One wrong year makes the statement false.
3. **Negative Phrasing:** "Which of the following is NOT a greenhouse gas?" — students misread and pick a GHG.
4. **Similar Names:** Jim Corbett vs Dudhwa; CITES vs CBD; biosphere reserve vs national park.
5. **Category Swap:** IUCN categories (Vulnerable/Endangered/Critically Endangered) shuffled for a species.
6. **Overgeneralisation:** "All biosphere reserves are national parks" — absolute words like *all/only/never* are usually wrong.

## 4. Top 10 High-Yield PYQ-Style Questions (with solutions)

**Q1.** Which of the following is NOT a greenhouse gas?
(a) Carbon dioxide (b) Methane (c) Nitrous oxide (d) Oxygen
**Answer:** (d)
**Solution:** CO₂, CH₄ and N₂O are all greenhouse gases. Oxygen (O₂) does not absorb infrared radiation. *Trap: Negative phrasing — read "NOT" twice before answering.*
**Source:** NCERT Class 11 Chemistry / PYQ-style

**Q2.** The Montreal Protocol (1987) is related to:
(a) Control of transboundary hazardous waste
(b) Protection of the ozone layer
(c) Reduction of greenhouse gas emissions
(d) Conservation of wetlands
**Answer:** (b)
**Solution:** Montreal Protocol phases out ozone-depleting substances (CFCs etc.). Hazardous waste = Basel Convention; wetlands = Ramsar; GHG = Kyoto/Paris. *Trap: Almost correct — all four options are real conventions.*
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q3.** Which of the following is the correct order of trophic levels in a grassland ecosystem?
(a) Grass → Deer → Lion
(b) Lion → Deer → Grass
(c) Deer → Grass → Lion
(d) Grass → Lion → Deer
**Answer:** (a)
**Solution:** Producer → primary consumer (herbivore) → secondary consumer (carnivore). *Trap: Chronological/sequence confusion.*
**Source:** NCERT Class 12 Biology

**Q4.** Consider the following statements about Biosphere Reserves in India:
1. They are declared under the Wildlife Protection Act, 1972.
2. They are part of UNESCO's Man and Biosphere (MAB) programme.
Which is/are correct?
(a) 1 only (b) 2 only (c) Both (d) Neither
**Answer:** (b)
**Solution:** Biosphere reserves are declared under the UNESCO MAB programme, not under the WPA 1972 (national parks and sanctuaries are). *Trap: Almost correct — statement 1 sounds plausible.*
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q5.** The "10% law" of energy transfer in ecosystems was given by:
(a) Charles Elton (b) Raymond Lindeman (c) E.P. Odum (d) A.G. Tansley
**Answer:** (b)
**Solution:** Lindeman (1942) proposed that only about 10% of energy passes to the next trophic level. Elton gave ecological pyramids; Tansley coined "ecosystem". *Trap: Similar names of ecologists.*
**Source:** NCERT Class 12 Biology

**Q6.** Minamata disease is caused by poisoning of:
(a) Lead (b) Cadmium (c) Mercury (d) Arsenic
**Answer:** (c)
**Solution:** Mercury poisoning from industrial discharge in Minamata Bay, Japan. Cadmium causes Itai-itai disease — the classic paired trap. *Trap: Similar names.*
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q7.** Which of the following is a biodiversity hotspot in India?
(a) Thar Desert (b) Western Ghats (c) Deccan Plateau (d) Gangetic Plain
**Answer:** (b)
**Solution:** India's four hotspots: Himalaya, Indo-Burma, Western Ghats & Sri Lanka, and Sundaland (Nicobar Islands). *Trap: Almost correct — all are real Indian physiographic regions.*
**Source:** NCERT / PYQ-style

**Q8.** Ramsar Convention (1971) is associated with:
(a) Wetlands of international importance
(b) Endangered species trade
(c) Ozone layer protection
(d) Marine pollution
**Answer:** (a)
**Solution:** Ramsar (Iran) covers wetlands. Endangered species trade = CITES. *Trap: Convention swap.*
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q9.** ⏱️ *15-second rule:* The gas mainly responsible for the Bhopal gas tragedy (1984) was:
(a) Methyl isocyanate (b) Carbon monoxide (c) Chlorine (d) Ammonia
**Answer:** (a)
**Solution:** MIC leaked from the Union Carbide plant. This is a pure recall fact — if you know it, answer in under 15 seconds and bank time for statement-based questions. If you don't, mark and move; don't burn 90 seconds guessing between four chemical names.
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q10.** ⏱️ *45-second rule:* Consider the following:
1. Keoladeo National Park — Rajasthan
2. Kanha National Park — Madhya Pradesh
3. Jim Corbett National Park — Uttarakhand
Which of the above are correctly matched?
(a) 1 and 2 only (b) 2 and 3 only (c) 1 and 3 only (d) 1, 2 and 3
**Answer:** (d)
**Solution:** All three pairs are correct. Multi-statement matching questions deserve up to 45 seconds: verify each pair individually before committing. The trap variant swaps one state (e.g., "Keoladeo — Gujarat"), so check every line even if the first two look right.
**Source:** PYQ-style

---

## 5. The 15/45-Second Rule for This Chapter

- **15 seconds:** Direct recall — full forms, "which gas", "which disease", "which convention". Know it or skip it; no middle ground.
- **45 seconds:** Statement-based and match-the-following questions. Budget time to test each statement separately; examiners plant the error in the *last* statement, counting on you to stop checking early.
- **Skip immediately:** Questions asking for exact counts that change yearly (number of Ramsar sites, tiger numbers) unless you know the figure *with its year*.
