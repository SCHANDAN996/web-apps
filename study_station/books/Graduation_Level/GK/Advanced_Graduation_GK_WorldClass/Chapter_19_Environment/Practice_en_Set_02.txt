Questions 26–50: Medium

26. Which of the following is the correct sequence of ecological organisation from smallest to largest?
(a) Population → Organism → Community → Ecosystem → Biosphere (b) Population → Community → Organism → Biosphere → Ecosystem (c) Organism → Community → Population → Ecosystem → Biosphere (d) Organism → Population → Community → Ecosystem → Biosphere
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is option (a), which looks plausible but wrongly places population below organism — a population is a group of organisms of the same species, so it must come after organism. The correct hierarchy is organism → population → community → ecosystem → biosphere, so the answer is option (d).
Source: NCERT Class 12 Biology

27. In an ecosystem, the 10% law of energy transfer was given by:
(a) Charles Elton (b) Raymond Lindeman (c) Eugene Odum (d) Arthur Tansley
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is Charles Elton, who is famous for the concept of the ecological pyramid and food chain, not the energy-transfer law. Raymond Lindeman (1942) proposed that only about 10% of energy is transferred from one trophic level to the next, so the answer is Raymond Lindeman.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Biology

28. Which of the following ecosystems has the highest primary productivity?
(a) Desert (b) Tundra (c) Temperate grassland (d) Tropical rainforest
Answer: (d)
Solution: The tempting wrong choice is temperate grassland, which is productive but limited by seasonal rainfall and temperature. Tropical rainforests have year-round sunlight, high rainfall and dense vegetation, giving them the highest net primary productivity among terrestrial ecosystems, so the answer is tropical rainforest.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Biology

29. The term 'ecosystem' was coined by:
(a) E.P. Odum (b) A.G. Tansley (c) E. Haeckel (d) Carl Linnaeus
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is Ernst Haeckel, who coined the word 'ecology' (1869), not 'ecosystem'. A.G. Tansley coined the term 'ecosystem' in 1935, so the answer is A.G. Tansley.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Biology

30. Which of the following is a secondary consumer in a grassland food chain: grass → grasshopper → frog → snake → eagle?
(a) Grasshopper (b) Snake (c) Frog (d) Eagle
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is snake — many students count trophic levels starting from the grasshopper instead of the producer. Grass is the producer, grasshopper the primary consumer, frog the secondary consumer, snake the tertiary consumer and eagle the quaternary consumer, so the answer is frog.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Biology

31. The pyramid of energy in any ecosystem is:
(a) Always upright (b) Always inverted (c) Sometimes upright, sometimes inverted (d) Spindle-shaped
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is option (c), which is true for pyramids of number and biomass (e.g., inverted biomass pyramid in a pond), and students often generalise it. Because energy always decreases at each successive trophic level, the pyramid of energy is always upright, so the answer is always upright.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Biology

32. Which of the following is a man-made (artificial) ecosystem?
(a) Pond (b) Crop field (c) Forest (d) Ocean
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is pond — an aquarium is artificial, but a natural pond is a self-sustaining natural ecosystem. A crop field is maintained by human inputs like ploughing, seeds, irrigation and fertilisers, making it an artificial ecosystem, so the answer is crop field.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science

33. Ozone in the stratosphere protects life on Earth by absorbing:
(a) Infrared radiation (b) Ultraviolet radiation (c) Visible light (d) Radio waves
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is infrared radiation — that is absorbed mainly by greenhouse gases like carbon dioxide and water vapour, causing warming. Stratospheric ozone absorbs harmful ultraviolet (UV-B) radiation from the Sun, so the answer is ultraviolet radiation.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science

34. The Montreal Protocol (1987) is related to:
(a) Control of greenhouse gas emissions (b) Conservation of biodiversity (c) Protection of the ozone layer (d) Control of hazardous waste movement
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is greenhouse gas emissions, which belongs to the Kyoto Protocol (1997) — students often confuse the two climate-related treaties. The Montreal Protocol phases out ozone-depleting substances like CFCs, so the answer is protection of the ozone layer.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Chemistry

35. Which gas is the largest contributor to the enhanced greenhouse effect from human activities?
(a) Methane (b) Chlorofluorocarbons (c) Nitrous oxide (d) Carbon dioxide
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is methane, which has a much higher warming potential per molecule, tempting students to pick it. However, carbon dioxide is emitted in far larger quantities and contributes the most to the enhanced greenhouse effect overall, so the answer is carbon dioxide.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Chemistry

36. The biological magnification of DDT is highest in:
(a) Top carnivores (b) Primary consumers (c) Producers (d) Decomposers
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is decomposers — although they break down dead matter, DDT concentration is measured along the food chain, not in decomposers. Being non-biodegradable, DDT accumulates and magnifies at each trophic level, reaching maximum concentration in top carnivores, so the answer is top carnivores.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science

37. Which of the following is a biodegradable pollutant?
(a) Plastic bags (b) DDT (c) Sewage (d) Mercury compounds
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is DDT — students sometimes assume all 'natural-looking' chemicals degrade, but DDT is famously non-biodegradable. Sewage consists of organic matter that microbes can decompose, making it biodegradable, so the answer is sewage.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science

38. The 'Red Data Book' contains information about:
(a) Economically important plants (b) Red-coloured birds (c) Endangered species of plants and animals (d) Species discovered in the last decade
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is economically important plants — students confuse the Red Data Book with trade or economic lists. Published under the IUCN, the Red Data Book lists species according to their extinction risk, so the answer is endangered species of plants and animals.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Biology

39. Which of the following is an in-situ method of biodiversity conservation?
(a) Botanical garden (b) Biosphere reserve (c) Zoological park (d) Seed bank
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is seed bank — it conserves genetic material, but outside the natural habitat, so it is ex-situ. A biosphere reserve protects species within their natural habitat, which is in-situ conservation, so the answer is biosphere reserve.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Biology

40. The largest reservoir of carbon on Earth is:
(a) Atmosphere (b) Oceans (c) Sedimentary rocks (carbonate rocks) (d) Fossil fuels
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is oceans — they are the largest *active* carbon reservoir in day-to-day cycling, which misleads many students. The greatest total store of carbon is locked in carbonate sedimentary rocks like limestone, so the answer is sedimentary rocks.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Biology

41. In the nitrogen cycle, the conversion of ammonia into nitrites and nitrates is called:
(a) Nitrification (b) Nitrogen fixation (c) Denitrification (d) Ammonification
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is ammonification, which is the reverse-sounding process of converting organic nitrogen into ammonia. Conversion of ammonia first to nitrite (by Nitrosomonas) and then to nitrate (by Nitrobacter) is nitrification, so the answer is nitrification.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Biology

42. Which bacteria convert nitrates back into free nitrogen gas?
(a) Pseudomonas (b) Nitrosomonas (c) Rhizobium (d) Azotobacter
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is Rhizobium — it is the best-known nitrogen bacterium, but it *fixes* nitrogen in legume root nodules rather than releasing it. Denitrification, converting nitrates into free nitrogen, is carried out by bacteria such as Pseudomonas, so the answer is Pseudomonas.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Biology

43. Eutrophication of a water body is primarily caused by an excess of:
(a) Heavy metals (b) Oil spills (c) Suspended solids (d) Nitrates and phosphates
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is heavy metals — they cause toxicity (like Minamata disease from mercury), not algal blooms. Nutrient enrichment by nitrates and phosphates from fertilisers and sewage triggers algal growth and oxygen depletion, so the answer is nitrates and phosphates.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Biology

44. The Minamata disease in Japan was caused by the consumption of fish contaminated with:
(a) Mercury (b) Lead (c) Cadmium (d) Arsenic
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is cadmium, which causes Itai-itai disease — also in Japan — and students frequently swap the two. Minamata disease resulted from methylmercury released into Minamata Bay and accumulated in fish, so the answer is mercury.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Chemistry

45. Which of the following is the correct chronological order of these environmental events?
(a) Kyoto Protocol → Stockholm Conference → Rio Earth Summit → Paris Agreement (b) Rio Earth Summit → Stockholm Conference → Paris Agreement → Kyoto Protocol (c) Stockholm Conference → Kyoto Protocol → Rio Earth Summit → Paris Agreement (d) Stockholm Conference → Rio Earth Summit → Kyoto Protocol → Paris Agreement
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is option (c), which swaps Rio (1992) and Kyoto (1997) — a classic chronological confusion. The correct order is Stockholm 1972, Rio Earth Summit 1992, Kyoto Protocol 1997 and Paris Agreement 2015, so the answer is option (d).
Source: PYQ-style

46. The 'Chipko Movement' is associated with:
(a) Conservation of water in Rajasthan (b) Protection of forests by hugging trees (c) Protection of the Silent Valley (d) Opposition to nuclear power plants
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is the Silent Valley movement — both are famous Indian environmental movements and are often confused. The Chipko Movement (1973, Garhwal Himalayas, associated with Sunderlal Bahuguna) involved villagers hugging trees to prevent felling, so the answer is protection of forests by hugging trees.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Biology

47. Which of the following pairs is correctly matched?
(a) Bishnoi community — protection of blackbuck and khejri trees (b) Appiko movement — Rajasthan (c) Silent Valley — Himachal Pradesh (d) Narmada Bachao Andolan — Kerala
Answer: (a)
Solution: The traps are the other pairs: Appiko began in Karnataka (Western Ghats), Silent Valley is in Kerala, and Narmada Bachao Andolan concerns Gujarat–Madhya Pradesh. The Bishnoi community of Rajasthan is famous for protecting blackbuck and khejri trees, so the answer is option (a).
Source: NCERT Class 12 Biology

48. The phenomenon of 'algal bloom' in a lake leads to:
(a) Increase in dissolved oxygen (b) Increase in fish population (c) Decrease in dissolved oxygen (d) Improved water clarity
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is increase in dissolved oxygen — algae do photosynthesise, which tempts students. But when the bloom dies, decomposing bacteria consume large amounts of oxygen, sharply reducing dissolved oxygen and killing fish, so the answer is decrease in dissolved oxygen.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Biology

49. Which of the following is a greenhouse gas that is also responsible for ozone layer depletion?
(a) Carbon dioxide (b) Methane (c) Sulphur dioxide (d) Chlorofluorocarbons (CFCs)
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is sulphur dioxide — it is a major air pollutant causing acid rain, but it neither warms the planet significantly nor depletes ozone. CFCs act as greenhouse gases and also release chlorine that destroys stratospheric ozone, so the answer is chlorofluorocarbons.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Chemistry

50. In a food chain, decomposers are essential because they:
(a) Recycle nutrients back into the soil (b) Produce food by photosynthesis (c) Control the population of herbivores (d) Convert solar energy into chemical energy
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is controlling herbivore populations — that is the role of carnivores (predators), not decomposers. Bacteria and fungi break down dead organic matter and release minerals back into the soil for producers to reuse, so the answer is recycle nutrients back into the soil.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science
