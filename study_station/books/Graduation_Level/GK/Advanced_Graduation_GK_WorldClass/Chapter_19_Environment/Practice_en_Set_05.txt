101. Which of the following is the correct sequence of ecological organisation from smallest to largest?
(a) Population → Organism → Community → Ecosystem → Biosphere (b) Population → Community → Organism → Biosphere → Ecosystem (c) Organism → Community → Population → Ecosystem → Biosphere (d) Organism → Population → Community → Ecosystem → Biosphere
Answer: (d)
Solution: The tempting wrong option starts with population, but a population is a group of organisms of the same species, so the organism must come first. The hierarchy is organism → population → community → ecosystem → biosphere, so the answer is Organism → Population → Community → Ecosystem → Biosphere.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Biology

102. In an ecosystem, the 10 percent law of energy transfer was given by:
(a) Raymond Lindeman (b) Charles Elton (c) Eugene Odum (d) Arthur Tansley
Answer: (a)
Solution: Charles Elton is the tempting wrong option because he gave the concept of the ecological pyramid and food chain, not the energy-transfer law. Raymond Lindeman (1942) proposed that only about 10 percent of energy passes from one trophic level to the next, so the answer is Raymond Lindeman.

103. The term 'ecosystem' was coined by:
(a) R. Mishra (b) E.P. Odum (c) E. Haeckel (d) A.G. Tansley
Answer: (d)
Solution: Ernst Haeckel is the tempting wrong option, but he coined the term 'ecology' (1869), not 'ecosystem'. The term 'ecosystem' was coined by British ecologist A.G. Tansley in 1935, so the answer is A.G. Tansley.

104. Which of the following is a primary consumer in a grassland ecosystem?
(a) Grass (b) Snake (c) Frog (d) Grasshopper
Answer: (d)
Solution: Grass is the tempting wrong option, but it is a producer, not a consumer. Primary consumers are herbivores that feed directly on producers; the grasshopper eats grass, so the answer is Grasshopper.

105. The pyramid of energy in any ecosystem is always:
(a) Inverted (b) Upright (c) Spindle-shaped (d) Sometimes upright, sometimes inverted
Answer: (b)
Solution: The tempting wrong option is 'sometimes upright, sometimes inverted', which is true for pyramids of number and biomass (e.g., in a pond or tree ecosystem), but not for energy. Since energy always decreases at each successive trophic level, the pyramid of energy is always upright, so the answer is Upright.

106. Which of the following biomes has the highest biodiversity?
(a) Tundra (b) Temperate grassland (c) Tropical rainforest (d) Desert
Answer: (c)
Solution: Temperate grassland is a tempting wrong option because it is highly productive, but its species diversity is limited. Tropical rainforests, with warm temperatures and high rainfall year-round, support the greatest variety of species, so the answer is Tropical rainforest.

107. The ozone layer is found mainly in which layer of the atmosphere?
(a) Troposphere (b) Stratosphere (c) Mesosphere (d) Thermosphere
Answer: (b)
Solution: Troposphere is the tempting wrong option because it is the lowest layer where weather occurs, but ozone there is a pollutant, not a protective layer. The protective ozone layer lies mainly in the stratosphere, about 15–35 km above the surface, so the answer is Stratosphere.

108. Which gas is primarily responsible for the depletion of the ozone layer?
(a) Carbon dioxide (b) Chlorofluorocarbons (c) Methane (d) Sulphur dioxide
Answer: (b)
Solution: Carbon dioxide is the tempting wrong option because it is the main greenhouse gas, but it does not destroy ozone. Chlorofluorocarbons release chlorine atoms in the stratosphere that break down ozone molecules, so the answer is Chlorofluorocarbons.

111. Which of the following is a non-renewable natural resource?
(a) Solar energy (b) Wind energy (c) Forest (d) Coal
Answer: (d)
Solution: Forest is the tempting wrong option because forests can be destroyed, but they regenerate naturally and are classed as renewable resources. Coal takes millions of years to form and cannot be replenished on a human timescale, so the answer is Coal.

112. Biomagnification refers to:
(a) Increase in population of a species (b) Increase in concentration of toxic substances at successive trophic levels (c) Increase in biomass of producers (d) Increase in body size of organisms
Answer: (b)
Solution: Increase in biomass of producers is the tempting wrong option because the word sounds similar, but it has nothing to do with toxins. Biomagnification is the rising concentration of non-degradable pollutants like DDT and mercury at higher trophic levels, so the answer is Increase in concentration of toxic substances at successive trophic levels.

113. Which of the following is an example of ex-situ conservation?

Solution: Biosphere reserve is the tempting wrong option, but national parks, sanctuaries and biosphere reserves all protect species in their natural habitat, i.e., in-situ. A botanical garden conserves plants outside their natural habitat, so the answer is Botanical garden.

116. Eutrophication of a water body is caused mainly by excess of:
(a) Heavy metals (b) Nitrates and phosphates (c) Suspended solids (d) Oil spills
Answer: (b)
Solution: Heavy metals are the tempting wrong option because they pollute water, but they cause toxicity, not nutrient enrichment. Excess nitrates and phosphates from fertilisers and sewage trigger algal blooms and oxygen depletion, so the answer is Nitrates and phosphates.

117. The biological oxygen demand (BOD) of polluted water is:
(a) Higher than that of clean water (b) Lower than that of clean water (c) Equal to that of clean water (d) Zero
Answer: (a)
Solution: Lower than that of clean water is the tempting wrong option because pollution reduces dissolved oxygen, but BOD measures the oxygen microbes need to decompose organic matter. More organic pollution means microbes consume more oxygen, so the answer is Higher than that of clean water.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Biology

121. Consider the following statements:
1. The pyramid of biomass in a pond ecosystem is upright.
2. The pyramid of numbers in a tree ecosystem is inverted.
Which of the statements given above is/are correct?
(a) 2 only (b) 1 only (c) Both 1 and 2 (d) Neither 1 nor 2
Answer: (a)
Solution: Statement 1 is the trap: in a pond, the biomass of phytoplankton at any moment is less than that of the consumers they support, so the pyramid of biomass there is inverted, not upright. In a tree ecosystem, one tree supports many insects, making the number pyramid inverted, so the answer is 2 only.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Biology

123. Consider the following statements about biosphere reserves in India:

2. Nilgiri was the first biosphere reserve of India.

Solution: Statement 1 is the trap: biosphere reserves are designated under UNESCO's Man and Biosphere Programme, not under the Wildlife Protection Act, which governs national parks and sanctuaries. Nilgiri (1986) was indeed India's first biosphere reserve, so the answer is 2 only.

124. Which of the following correctly describes the difference between a food chain and a food web?
(a) A food chain has more trophic levels than a food web (b) A food chain operates only in aquatic ecosystems (c) A food web is a network of interconnected food chains (d) A food web contains only producers and decomposers
Answer: (c)
Solution: The first option is the tempting wrong one, but trophic levels depend on the ecosystem, not on whether it is a chain or web. In nature, organisms eat and are eaten by many species, so interlinked food chains form a food web, so the answer is A food web is a network of interconnected food chains.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Biology

125. Consider the following statements:
1. Lichens are indicators of sulphur dioxide pollution.
2. Taj Mahal is affected by acid rain caused by sulphur dioxide and nitrogen oxides.
Which of the statements given above is/are correct?
(a) 1 only (b) 2 only (c) Neither 1 nor 2 (d) Both 1 and 2
Answer: (d)
Solution: Some candidates reject statement 1 thinking lichens indicate only air quality generally, but lichens are specifically sensitive to sulphur dioxide and disappear in polluted areas. Acid rain from sulphur dioxide and nitrogen oxides (largely from the Mathura refinery region) has yellowed the Taj Mahal's marble, so the answer is Both 1 and 2.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Chemistry
