126. Which of the following best describes an ecological niche?
(a) The physical space occupied by an organism (b) The total number of species in a community (c) The functional role and position of a species in its ecosystem, including its habitat and resource use (d) The area where two ecosystems meet
Answer: (c)
Solution: The tempting wrong option is the first one, which describes only the habitat (the "address"), not the niche. A niche is the species' "profession" — its functional role, feeding habits, activity time, and interactions, so the answer is the functional role and position of a species in its ecosystem.

127. In an aquatic ecosystem, the order of trophic levels in a grazing food chain is correctly represented as:
(a) Phytoplankton → Zooplankton → Small fish → Large fish (b) Zooplankton → Phytoplankton → Small fish → Large fish (c) Small fish → Zooplankton → Phytoplankton → Large fish (d) Phytoplankton → Small fish → Zooplankton → Large fish
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is the second option, which reverses producer and primary consumer — a classic chronological/sequence confusion. Producers (phytoplankton) always come first, eaten by zooplankton, then small fish, then large fish, so the answer is Phytoplankton → Zooplankton → Small fish → Large fish.

129. Which of the following statements about ecological pyramids is correct?
(a) The pyramid of energy is always upright (b) The pyramid of numbers in a parasitic food chain is always upright (c) The pyramid of biomass in a pond ecosystem is always upright (d) The pyramid of biomass in a forest ecosystem is always inverted

Solution: The trap is the first option — in a pond, the biomass pyramid is inverted because phytoplankton have low standing biomass but high turnover. The pyramid of energy can never be inverted since energy always decreases upward, so the answer is the pyramid of energy is always upright.

150. In a food chain, if producers contain 10,000 kcal of energy, the energy available to tertiary consumers (fourth trophic level) would be approximately:
(a) 10 kcal (b) 100 kcal (c) 1,000 kcal (d) 1 kcal
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is 100 kcal, which is the energy at the secondary consumer level — an off-by-one trophic-level error. Applying the 10% law thrice: 10,000 → 1,000 (primary) → 100 (secondary) → 10 kcal (tertiary), so the answer is 10 kcal.
