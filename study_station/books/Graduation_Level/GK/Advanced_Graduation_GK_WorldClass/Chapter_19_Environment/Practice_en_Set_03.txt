51. Which of the following best defines an "ecosystem"?
(a) A group of organisms of the same species living together (b) A functional unit of nature where living organisms interact among themselves and with the physical environment (c) The total area occupied by a biome (d) The physical environment excluding all living organisms
Answer: (b)
Solution: The tempting wrong choice is the first option, which defines a "population", not an ecosystem. An ecosystem includes both biotic and abiotic components interacting as a functional unit, so the answer is a functional unit of nature where living organisms interact among themselves and with the physical environment.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Biology

52. In an ecosystem, the maximum amount of energy is available at the level of:
(a) Primary consumers (b) Secondary consumers (c) Decomposers (d) Producers
Answer: (d)
Solution: Many students pick decomposers because they act on all trophic levels, but decomposers do not form a trophic level in the energy pyramid. Energy decreases at each successive level (10% law), so the maximum energy is with producers, and the answer is producers.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Biology

53. According to Lindeman's 10 percent law, if producers store 10,000 kcal of energy, the energy available to secondary consumers will be:
(a) 1,000 kcal (b) 500 kcal (c) 10 kcal (d) 100 kcal
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is stopping after one transfer and choosing 1,000 kcal, which is the energy at the primary consumer level. Secondary consumers are two transfers away: 10,000 → 1,000 → 100 kcal, so the answer is 100 kcal.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Biology

54. Which of the following is a primary consumer in a grassland ecosystem?
(a) Grass (b) Frog (c) Grasshopper (d) Snake
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap here is the frog, which eats the grasshopper and is therefore a secondary consumer. Primary consumers are herbivores feeding directly on producers, so the answer is grasshopper.

56. Which of the following ecosystems has the highest primary productivity?
(a) Desert (b) Tropical rainforest (c) Temperate grassland (d) Tundra
Answer: (b)
Solution: Temperate grasslands look productive but are limited by water and temperature. Tropical rainforests have year-round warmth, rainfall and dense vegetation, giving the highest primary productivity, so the answer is tropical rainforest.

57. In a food chain, the flow of energy is:
(a) Bidirectional (b) Unidirectional (c) Cyclic (d) Random
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is "cyclic", which describes the movement of nutrients (biogeochemical cycles), not energy. Energy flows one way — from the sun to producers to consumers — and is lost as heat, so the answer is unidirectional.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Biology

58. Which of the following is an example of a detritus food chain starting material?
(a) Green leaves on a tree (b) Sunlight (c) Phytoplankton (d) Dead organic matter
Answer: (d)
Solution: Green leaves and phytoplankton belong to the grazing food chain, which is the tempting wrong choice. The detritus food chain begins with dead organic matter decomposed by detritivores, so the answer is dead organic matter.

59. The pyramid of energy in any ecosystem is:
(a) Always inverted (b) Always upright (c) Sometimes upright, sometimes inverted (d) Spindle-shaped
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is "sometimes inverted", which is true for pyramids of biomass (e.g., in oceans) and numbers (e.g., a tree ecosystem), but energy always decreases upward. The pyramid of energy is always upright, so the answer is always upright.

60. In an aquatic ecosystem, the pyramid of biomass may be inverted because:
(a) Producers are small, short-lived phytoplankton with high turnover (b) Producers are absent (c) Consumers do not eat producers (d) Decomposers dominate the ecosystem
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is assuming producers are absent; phytoplankton are present but tiny and reproduce rapidly, so at any moment their standing biomass can be less than that of consumers. The answer is that producers are small, short-lived phytoplankton with high turnover.

61. Which biogeochemical cycle does NOT have a gaseous phase?
(a) Phosphorus cycle (b) Nitrogen cycle (c) Carbon cycle (d) Oxygen cycle
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is the nitrogen cycle, which does have gaseous phases (N₂, N₂O). The phosphorus cycle is purely sedimentary, moving through rocks, soil and water, so the answer is phosphorus cycle.

62. The process by which nitrogen gas is converted into ammonia by certain bacteria is called:
(a) Nitrification (b) Denitrification (c) Nitrogen fixation (d) Ammonification
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is nitrification, which converts ammonia into nitrites and nitrates — the opposite direction. Converting atmospheric N₂ into ammonia is nitrogen fixation, so the answer is nitrogen fixation.

63. Which bacteria convert ammonia into nitrites and then nitrates in the soil?
(a) Rhizobium and Azotobacter (b) Clostridium and Bacillus (c) Pseudomonas and Thiobacillus (d) Nitrosomonas and Nitrobacter
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is Rhizobium, which fixes nitrogen but does not perform nitrification. Nitrosomonas converts ammonia to nitrite and Nitrobacter converts nitrite to nitrate, so the answer is Nitrosomonas and Nitrobacter.

64. The ozone layer is found mainly in which layer of the atmosphere?
(a) Troposphere (b) Thermosphere (c) Mesosphere (d) Stratosphere
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is the troposphere, where weather occurs and where ground-level ozone is a pollutant — a classic confusion. The protective ozone layer lies in the stratosphere, so the answer is stratosphere.

65. Which of the following is the chief cause of ozone layer depletion?
(a) Carbon dioxide (b) Methane (c) Chlorofluorocarbons (CFCs) (d) Sulphur dioxide
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is carbon dioxide, which is the main greenhouse gas but does not deplete ozone. CFCs release chlorine atoms in the stratosphere that break down ozone, so the answer is chlorofluorocarbons (CFCs).

68. The Biological Oxygen Demand (BOD) of a water body increases when:
(a) The water is pure and clean (b) Dissolved oxygen increases (c) Organic pollution in the water increases (d) Temperature of water decreases
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is linking high BOD with high dissolved oxygen; actually high BOD means microbes consume more oxygen, lowering dissolved oxygen. BOD rises with more organic pollution, so the answer is organic pollution in the water increases.

72. Which of the following is the correct sequence of ecological succession on bare rock?
(a) Lichens → Mosses → Herbs → Shrubs → Forest (b) Mosses → Lichens → Shrubs → Herbs → Forest (c) Herbs → Lichens → Mosses → Shrubs → Forest (d) Forest → Shrubs → Herbs → Mosses → Lichens
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is placing mosses before lichens; lichens are the pioneer species on bare rock because they can grow without soil and help form it. The correct sequence is lichens → mosses → herbs → shrubs → forest, so the answer is lichens → mosses → herbs → shrubs → forest.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Biology

73. The term "biodiversity hotspot" requires a region to have:
(a) Only a large number of species (b) High endemism and significant habitat loss (c) Only high rainfall (d) Absence of human population
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is "only a large number of species"; species richness alone does not qualify a hotspot. Norman Myers' criteria require at least 1,500 endemic vascular plant species and at least 70% loss of original habitat, so the answer is high endemism and significant habitat loss.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Biology

74. Which of the following pairs is correctly matched?

Answer: (c)
Solution: The traps mix up dates and places: Silent Valley was in Kerala (1970s–80s) and Appiko in Karnataka (1983). The Chipko Movement began in 1973 in the Garhwal Himalaya (present Uttarakhand), so the answer is Chipko Movement — 1973 — Garhwal, Uttarakhand region.

75. Which gas is mainly responsible for acid rain?
(a) Sulphur dioxide and nitrogen oxides (b) Carbon dioxide and methane (c) Ozone and carbon monoxide (d) Ammonia and hydrogen sulphide
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is carbon dioxide, which forms only weak carbonic acid in normal rain and is not the cause of acid rain. SO₂ and NOx form sulphuric and nitric acids, so the answer is sulphur dioxide and nitrogen oxides.
