# Key Facts & Figures — Environment & Ecology (Basic)

## Part 1: High-Yield Facts

| Fact | Description | Examiner's Trap | Trap Type |
|---|---|---|---|
| Term "Ecology" coined by | Ernst Haeckel (1866), German biologist | Confused with E.P. Odum ("Father of Modern Ecology") | Similar names |
| Term "Ecosystem" coined by | A.G. Tansley (1935) | Confused with Haeckel (who coined "ecology") | Similar names |
| Father of Ecology (India) | Ramdeo Misra | Confused with P. Maheshwari (father of Indian embryology) | Similar names |
| Levels of organisation | Organism → Population → Community → Ecosystem → Biome → Biosphere | Order shuffled in options (community/population swapped) | Sequence confusion |
| Biosphere term | Coined by Eduard Suess (geologist); popularised by Vernadsky | Suess vs Vernadsky swapped | Similar names |
| Habitat vs Niche | Habitat = address (where it lives); Niche = profession (functional role) | Definitions interchanged in statements | Statement trap |
| 10% energy law | Only ~10% energy transfers to next trophic level (Lindeman, 1942) | Options offer 1%, 20%, 90% | Numerical trap |
| Energy flow direction | Unidirectional (one-way), never cyclic | "Energy cycles in ecosystem" — false statement | Negative statement |
| Nutrient cycling | Biogeochemical cycles are cyclic (unlike energy) | "Both energy and nutrients flow unidirectionally" — false | Statement trap |
| Ecological pyramids | Energy pyramid is ALWAYS upright | "Energy pyramid can be inverted" — false; number/biomass pyramids can be inverted (e.g., tree ecosystem, parasitic food chain) | Statement trap |
| Inverted biomass pyramid | Seen in aquatic (pond/sea) ecosystems — phytoplankton reproduce fast | Applied wrongly to grassland | Context trap |
| Producers | Green plants, algae, cyanobacteria (autotrophs) | Fungi listed as producers — fungi are decomposers | Category trap |
| Decomposers | Bacteria and fungi; convert organic → inorganic | Earthworms called decomposers — they are detritivores | Category trap |
| Food chain vs Food web | Food web = interconnected food chains; more stable | "Food chain is more stable than food web" — false | Statement trap |
| Grazing vs Detritus food chain | GFC starts from living plants; DFC starts from dead organic matter | DFC said to start from producers | Statement trap |
| Ecological succession | Primary (bare rock, no soil) vs Secondary (after disturbance, soil present) | Primary succession said to be faster — secondary is faster | Statement trap |
| Pioneer species | First colonisers — lichens and mosses on bare rock | Grasses listed as pioneers of bare rock | Category trap |
| Climax community | Final stable community of succession | Called "unstable/transitional" | Negative statement |
| Hydrosere vs Xerosere | Hydrosere: succession in water; Xerosere: on dry/bare rock | Terms interchanged | Similar names |
| Biome | Largest terrestrial ecological unit (tundra, taiga, desert, grassland) | Biome confused with ecosystem (smaller) | Category trap |
| Hotspot criteria (Conservation International) | ≥1,500 endemic plant species AND ≥70% habitat loss | Only endemism mentioned, habitat loss omitted | Incomplete statement |
| Biodiversity hotspots in India | 4 — Himalaya, Indo-Burma, Western Ghats & Sri Lanka, Sundaland (Nicobar) | Number given as 2 or 3; Eastern Ghats listed as hotspot | Numerical trap |
| In-situ conservation | National parks, wildlife sanctuaries, biosphere reserves (natural habitat) | Zoo/botanical garden called in-situ — they are ex-situ | Category trap |
| Ex-situ conservation | Zoos, botanical gardens, seed banks, gene banks, cryopreservation | Biosphere reserve called ex-situ | Category trap |
| National Park vs Sanctuary | NP: no human activity/grazing; Sanctuary: limited rights may be allowed | Differences reversed | Statement trap |
| First National Park in India | Jim Corbett (1936, as Hailey NP), Uttarakhand | Kaziranga or Gir given as first | Similar names |
| Biosphere Reserve zones | Core (no activity) → Buffer (research/tourism limited) → Transition (human settlements) | Zone order shuffled | Sequence confusion |
| First Biosphere Reserve of India | Nilgiri (1986) | Nanda Devi or Sundarbans given as first | Similar names |
| Red Data Book | IUCN publication listing threatened species | Attributed to WWF or UNEP | Institution trap |
| IUCN threat categories (order) | Extinct → Extinct in Wild → Critically Endangered → Endangered → Vulnerable → Near Threatened → Least Concern | CR/EN/VU order shuffled | Sequence confusion |
| Keystone species | Disproportionately large effect on ecosystem (e.g., fig trees, sea otters) | Confused with flagship/umbrella species | Similar names |
| Indicator species | Signal environmental quality (lichens indicate air pollution/SO₂) | Lichens said to indicate water pollution | Context trap |
| Eutrophication | Nutrient (N, P) enrichment → algal bloom → oxygen depletion | Called "increase in dissolved oxygen" — it decreases DO | Statement trap |
| Biomagnification | Toxin concentration rises up the food chain (DDT, mercury) | Said to decrease at higher trophic levels | Statement trap |
| Minamata disease | Mercury poisoning (Japan) | Matched with cadmium (that's Itai-itai) | Similar names |
| Itai-itai disease | Cadmium poisoning (Japan) | Matched with mercury | Similar names |
| Bhopal gas tragedy (1984) | Methyl isocyanate (MIC) leak | Gas given as chlorine or ammonia | Similar names |
| Ozone layer location | Stratosphere | Troposphere given (tropospheric ozone is a pollutant) | Context trap |
| Ozone depleting substances | CFCs, halons, methyl bromide | CO₂ listed as main ODS — it is a GHG, not major ODS | Category trap |
| Montreal Protocol (1987) | Ozone-depleting substances phase-out | Confused with Kyoto (climate/GHG) or Paris (2015) | Treaty confusion |
| Kyoto Protocol (1997, in force 2005) | Binding GHG reduction targets for developed countries | Said to cover ozone or biodiversity | Treaty confusion |
| Paris Agreement (2015) | Limit warming well below 2°C, pursue 1.5°C | Target given as "below 1°C" or "zero emissions by 2030" | Numerical trap |
| Rio Earth Summit (1992) | UNFCCC, CBD, Agenda 21; Rio Declaration | CBD said to be from Stockholm 1972 | Treaty confusion |
| Stockholm Conference (1972) | First UN conference on human environment; UNEP born | Called the conference that created UNFCCC | Treaty confusion |
| UNEP headquarters | Nairobi (Kenya) | Geneva or New York given | Similar names |
| Greenhouse gases | CO₂, CH₄, N₂O, water vapour, CFCs, O₃ | O₂ or N₂ listed as GHG | Category trap |
| Largest natural GHG contributor | Water vapour (by abundance); CO₂ main anthropogenic | "CO₂ is the only greenhouse gas" — false | Statement trap |
| Global warming potential | CO₂ = 1 (reference); CH₄ much higher per molecule | CH₄ said to have lower GWP than CO₂ | Statement trap |
| Acid rain pH | Below 5.6 (normal rain ~5.6 due to CO₂) | Threshold given as 7.0 | Numerical trap |
| Acid rain gases | SO₂ and NOx | CO₂ given as main cause | Category trap |
| BOD | Biological Oxygen Demand — higher BOD = more organic pollution | "High BOD means clean water" — false | Statement trap |
| EIA | Environmental Impact Assessment — mandatory before projects | Called a post-project audit | Statement trap |
| Chipko Movement (1973) | Tree-hugging, Gaura Devi prominent; linked to Garhwal (Uttarakhand) | Leader given as Medha Patkar (Narmada) | Similar names |
| Appiko Movement | Karnataka version of Chipko (1983) | Placed in Kerala or Rajasthan | Context trap |
| Narmada Bachao Andolan | Medha Patkar, against Sardar Sarovar dam | Linked to Chipko leaders | Similar names |
| Silent Valley Movement | Kerala — saved tropical rainforest from hydel project | Placed in Himachal or Assam | Context trap |
| World Environment Day | 5 June (since 1973, from Stockholm 1972) | Given as 22 April (Earth Day) or 16 Sept (Ozone Day) | Date confusion |
| Earth Day | 22 April | Confused with Environment Day | Date confusion |
| Ozone Day | 16 September (Montreal Protocol signing) | Confused with 5 June | Date confusion |
| Wetlands Day | 2 February (Ramsar Convention 1971) | Ramsar placed in Iran — it is in Iran (correct), but year given as 1972 | Date confusion |
| Ramsar Convention (1971) | Wetlands of international importance | Called a biodiversity or desertification treaty | Treaty confusion |
| CITES (1973) | International trade in endangered species | Confused with CBD (1992) | Treaty confusion |
| CBD (1992) | Conservation of biological diversity; Nagoya Protocol (2010) on access & benefit-sharing | Nagoya linked to climate change | Treaty confusion |
| Project Tiger | 1973, launched from Jim Corbett NP | Year given as 1972 (that's Wildlife Protection Act) | Date confusion |
| Project Elephant | 1992 | Confused with Project Tiger year | Date confusion |
| Wildlife Protection Act | 1972 | Given as 1973 | Date confusion |
| Environment (Protection) Act | 1986 (after Bhopal; under Article 253, Rio/Stockholm commitments) | Given as 1974 (that's Water Act) | Date confusion |
| Water (Prevention & Control of Pollution) Act | 1974 | Confused with Air Act 1981 | Date confusion |
| Air (Prevention & Control of Pollution) Act | 1981 | Confused with Water Act 1974 | Date confusion |
| Forest Conservation Act | 1980 | Given as 1986 | Date confusion |
| Article 48A | State shall protect environment (DPSP, 42nd Amendment 1976) | Called a Fundamental Right | Category trap |
| Article 51A(g) | Fundamental Duty to protect environment | Called a DPSP | Category trap |
| Article 21 | Right to clean environment read into Right to Life (judiciary) | Said to be explicitly written in original Constitution | Statement trap |
| 42nd Amendment (1976) | Added 48A, 51A(g); "socialist, secular, integrity" to Preamble | Attributed to 44th Amendment | Date confusion |
| CPCB | Central Pollution Control Board, under Water Act 1974 | Said to be created by EPA 1986 | Date confusion |
| National Green Tribunal | NGT Act 2010; HQ New Delhi | Year given as 2012 or HQ given as Mumbai | Date confusion |
| Eco-sensitive zones | Buffer around protected areas (notified under EPA 1986) | Said to be declared under Wildlife Act 1972 | Category trap |
| Mangroves importance | Coastal protection, breeding grounds, carbon sinks; Sundarbans largest delta mangrove | Called "freshwater ecosystems only" | Statement trap |
| Coral bleaching | Loss of zooxanthellae due to heat stress | Called "coral death by oil spills only" | Statement trap |
| Carbon sink vs source | Forests/oceans absorb CO₂ (sink); burning releases (source) | Ocean called only a source | Statement trap |
| Carbon credit | 1 credit = 1 tonne CO₂ reduced | Given as 100 kg or 1000 tonnes | Numerical trap |
| Ecological footprint | Land/water area needed to sustain a population's consumption | Confused with carbon footprint (only GHG) | Similar names |
| Edge effect | Greater species diversity at ecosystem boundaries | Said to reduce diversity | Statement trap |
| Ecotone | Transition zone between two ecosystems | Confused with ecotype or biome | Similar names |
| Standing crop | Biomass present at a given time in an area | Confused with standing state (nutrients in soil) | Similar names |
| Photochemical smog | NOx + sunlight + hydrocarbons; PAN formed; Los Angeles type | Confused with classical/London smog (SO₂ + smoke) | Similar names |
| PAN | Peroxyacetyl nitrate — component of photochemical smog | Called a greenhouse gas | Category trap |
| Taj Mahal protection | Marble damaged by acid rain (SO₂ from Mathura refinery) | Cause given as CO₂ or dust only | Category trap |
| Ganga Action Plan | Launched 1985 (later Namami Gange 2014) | Year given as 1986 | Date confusion |
| Tiger reserve count | Numbers change with notifications — verify current figure before quoting | Static numbers quoted from old sources | Outdated data |
| Ramsar sites in India | Count rises with new designations — verify latest | Old counts quoted | Outdated data |
