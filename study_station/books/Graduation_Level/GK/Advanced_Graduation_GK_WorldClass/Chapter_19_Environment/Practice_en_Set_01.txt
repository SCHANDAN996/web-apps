1. Which of the following is the basic unit of ecological study?
(a) Organism (b) Population (c) Community (d) Ecosystem
Answer: (a)
Solution: The organism is the fundamental unit of ecology because all ecological interactions begin at the level of the individual living being. Population, community and ecosystem are higher levels built upon organisms, so the answer is Organism.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Biology

2. The term "ecology" was coined by:
(a) Charles Darwin (b) Carl Linnaeus (c) Ernst Haeckel (d) Alexander von Humboldt
Answer: (c)
Solution: Darwin proposed natural selection and Linnaeus gave binomial nomenclature, which tempts students, but neither coined "ecology". Ernst Haeckel coined the term in 1866 from the Greek "oikos" (house), so the answer is Ernst Haeckel.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Biology

3. Which of the following is an abiotic component of an ecosystem?
(a) Bacteria (b) Green plants (c) Fungi (d) Sunlight
Answer: (d)
Solution: Bacteria, plants and fungi are all living (biotic) components. Sunlight is a non-living physical factor, so the answer is Sunlight.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Biology

4. In a food chain, the maximum energy is available at the level of:
(a) Producers (b) Herbivores (c) Top carnivores (d) Decomposers
Answer: (a)
Solution: Energy decreases at each successive trophic level, so top carnivores have the least energy, not the most. Producers trap solar energy directly and hold the maximum energy, so the answer is Producers.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Biology

5. According to the 10 percent law of energy transfer, if producers store 10,000 J of energy, the energy available to primary consumers will be:
(a) 10,000 J (b) 10 J (c) 100 J (d) 1,000 J
Answer: (d)
Solution: Only about 10 percent of energy passes to the next trophic level; 100 J would be the secondary consumer level, which is the common trap. 10 percent of 10,000 J is 1,000 J, so the answer is 1,000 J.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Biology

6. Which of the following is a primary consumer in a grassland ecosystem?
(a) Lion (b) Grass (c) Vulture (d) Deer
Answer: (d)
Solution: Grass is a producer and the lion is a secondary or tertiary consumer. The deer eats grass directly, making it a herbivore and primary consumer, so the answer is Deer.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Biology

7. Decomposers in an ecosystem are mainly:
(a) Green plants and algae (b) Herbivores (c) Bacteria and fungi (d) Carnivores
Answer: (c)
Solution: Green plants are producers, not decomposers. Bacteria and fungi break down dead organic matter and recycle nutrients, so the answer is Bacteria and fungi.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Biology

8. The ozone layer is found mainly in which layer of the atmosphere?
(a) Troposphere (b) Thermosphere (c) Mesosphere (d) Stratosphere
Answer: (d)
Solution: The troposphere is where weather occurs and is the lowest layer, which confuses many students. The ozone layer lies in the stratosphere, roughly 15–35 km above Earth, so the answer is Stratosphere.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Geography

9. Which gas is primarily responsible for the depletion of the ozone layer?
(a) Carbon dioxide (b) Nitrogen (c) Methane (d) Chlorofluorocarbons
Answer: (d)
Solution: Carbon dioxide causes global warming, not ozone depletion — a classic examiner trap mixing the two issues. Chlorofluorocarbons release chlorine atoms that destroy ozone, so the answer is Chlorofluorocarbons.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Chemistry

10. The gas most responsible for the greenhouse effect is:
(a) Carbon dioxide (b) Nitrogen (c) Oxygen (d) Hydrogen
Answer: (a)
Solution: Oxygen and nitrogen make up most of the atmosphere but do not trap heat significantly. Carbon dioxide is the principal greenhouse gas from human activity, so the answer is Carbon dioxide.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Chemistry

11. Which of the following is a renewable source of energy?
(a) Coal (b) Solar energy (c) Petroleum (d) Natural gas
Answer: (b)
Solution: Coal, petroleum and natural gas are fossil fuels that take millions of years to form. Solar energy is naturally replenished, so the answer is Solar energy.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science

12. The variety of life forms found on Earth is called:
(a) Ecosystem (b) Biome (c) Biodiversity (d) Habitat
Answer: (c)
Solution: A habitat is the place where an organism lives, and a biome is a large regional ecosystem — both are traps. The variety of genes, species and ecosystems is biodiversity, so the answer is Biodiversity.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Biology

13. Which of the following is an example of a natural ecosystem?
(a) Aquarium (b) Crop field (c) Pond (d) Garden
Answer: (c)
Solution: An aquarium, crop field and garden are all man-made (artificial) ecosystems. A pond maintains itself without human intervention, so the answer is Pond.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Biology

14. The World Environment Day is celebrated on:
(a) 5 June (b) 22 April (c) 16 September (d) 11 July
Answer: (a)
Solution: 22 April is Earth Day and 16 September is Ozone Day — similar-sounding observances that examiners use as traps. World Environment Day is 5 June, so the answer is 5 June.
Source: UNEP official website

15. Which of the following organisms is a producer?
(a) Mushroom (b) Amoeba (c) Earthworm (d) Algae
Answer: (d)
Solution: Mushroom is a fungus and hence a decomposer, which is the tempting wrong choice. Algae perform photosynthesis and produce their own food, so the answer is Algae.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Biology

16. Acid rain is mainly caused by the emission of:
(a) Carbon dioxide and carbon monoxide (b) Methane and ozone (c) Sulphur dioxide and nitrogen oxides (d) Chlorine and fluorine
Answer: (c)
Solution: Carbon dioxide causes global warming, not acid rain — a frequent confusion. Sulphur dioxide and nitrogen oxides form sulphuric and nitric acids in rainwater, so the answer is Sulphur dioxide and nitrogen oxides.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Chemistry

17. In an ecosystem, the flow of energy is:
(a) Cyclic (b) Unidirectional (c) Bidirectional (d) Random
Answer: (b)
Solution: Nutrients cycle in an ecosystem, which makes "cyclic" the attractive trap. Energy flows one way — from the sun to producers to consumers — and is lost as heat, so the answer is Unidirectional.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Biology

18. Which of the following is a biodegradable waste?
(a) Plastic bag (b) Glass bottle (c) Vegetable peels (d) Aluminium can
Answer: (c)
Solution: Plastic, glass and aluminium resist microbial decomposition and persist for years. Vegetable peels are broken down by decomposers quickly, so the answer is Vegetable peels.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science

19. The place where an organism lives is called its:
(a) Habitat (b) Niche (c) Biome (d) Community
Answer: (a)
Solution: A niche is the functional role of an organism, not its address — a classic habitat-versus-niche trap. The physical place where an organism lives is its habitat, so the answer is Habitat.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Biology

20. Which of the following is a secondary consumer?
(a) Grass (b) Grasshopper (c) Frog (d) Hawk eating a snake
Answer: (c)
Solution: Grass is a producer and the grasshopper is a primary consumer. The frog eats the grasshopper, placing it at the third trophic level as a secondary consumer, so the answer is Frog.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Biology

21. Consider the following statements:
1. The pyramid of energy is always upright.
2. The pyramid of numbers can be inverted in some ecosystems.
Which of the statements given above is/are correct?
(a) 1 only (b) 2 only (c) Neither 1 nor 2 (d) Both 1 and 2
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is assuming all ecological pyramids behave alike. Energy always decreases upward, so its pyramid is always upright, while the number pyramid can be inverted, as in a tree supporting many parasites. Both statements are correct, so the answer is Both 1 and 2.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Biology

22. Consider the following statements about a food chain:
1. Grass → Deer → Lion
2. In this chain, the lion is a primary consumer.
Which of the statements given above is/are correct?
(a) 2 only (b) 1 only (c) Both 1 and 2 (d) Neither 1 nor 2
Answer: (b)
Solution: Statement 2 is the trap: the deer, not the lion, is the primary consumer because it eats the producer. The lion is a secondary consumer, so only statement 1 is correct, and the answer is 1 only.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Biology

23. Which of the following pairs is correctly matched?
(a) Montreal Protocol — Ozone-depleting substances (b) Kyoto Protocol — Ozone depletion (c) Montreal Protocol — Climate change (d) Rio Summit — Ozone depletion
Answer: (a)
Solution: The examiner swaps the two protocols deliberately: the Kyoto Protocol deals with greenhouse gases and climate change, not ozone. The Montreal Protocol (1987) phases out ozone-depleting substances, so the answer is Montreal Protocol — Ozone-depleting substances.
Source: UNEP official website

24. Consider the following statements:
1. Eutrophication is caused by excess nutrients like nitrates and phosphates in water bodies.
2. Eutrophication increases the dissolved oxygen level in water.
Which of the statements given above is/are correct?
(a) 2 only (b) 1 only (c) Both 1 and 2 (d) Neither 1 nor 2
Answer: (b)
Solution: Statement 2 is the trap: algal blooms from eutrophication actually deplete dissolved oxygen as the algae decay, killing aquatic life. Only statement 1 is correct, so the answer is 1 only.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Biology

25. Arrange the following levels of biological organisation in the correct ascending order:
1. Community 2. Organism 3. Ecosystem 4. Population
(a) 2, 4, 1, 3 (b) 2, 1, 4, 3 (c) 4, 2, 1, 3 (d) 2, 4, 3, 1
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is placing community before population; a community is made of many populations, so population must come first. The correct order is Organism → Population → Community → Ecosystem, so the answer is 2, 4, 1, 3.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Biology
