# 📊 PYQ Analysis — Medieval Indian History

## 1. How Exams Ask Questions from This Chapter

Medieval Indian History (roughly 8th–18th century) is a **steady, medium-weight** area across UPSC, SSC CGL/CHSL, Banking, Railway and State PCS prelims. It is not the heaviest part of history (Ancient and Modern usually carry more), but it is the **most trap-dense** — examiners love this chapter because it is full of similar names, overlapping dynasties, and easily confused chronologies.

**What the pattern looks like, in words:**

- **UPSC / State PCS:** Conceptual and statement-based questions — land revenue systems, Bhakti-Sufi thought, administrative terms, art and architecture comparisons. "Which of the statements given above is/are correct?" format dominates.
- **SSC (CGL/CHSL/GD):** Direct factual one-liners — battles, founders, travellers, books and authors, tombs and monuments.
- **Banking (IBPS/SBI):** Fewer questions, but when they come, they are simple facts — firsts, founders, famous buildings.
- **Railway (NTPC/Group D):** High volume of direct facts, often repeated themes across shifts — Delhi Sultanate rulers, Mughal emperors in order, Vijayanagara, famous travellers.

## 2. Sub-Topics That Recur Most Often

1. **Mughal emperors in sequence** — Babur to Aurangzeb: who came after whom, major battles (Panipat 1526, Khanwa 1527, Chausa 1539, Bilgram 1540, Haldighati 1576).
2. **Delhi Sultanate dynasties in order** — Slave → Khilji → Tughlaq → Sayyid → Lodi, and their founders.
3. **Travellers and their accounts** — Ibn Battuta (Tughlaq period), Al-Biruni (came with Mahmud of Ghazni), Marco Polo (South India), Nicolo Conti, Abdur Razzaq (Vijayanagara), Bernier and Tavernier (Mughals).
4. **Books and authors** — Baburnama, Akbarnama and Ain-i-Akbari (Abul Fazl), Tuzuk-i-Jahangiri, Padshahnama, Tughlaqnama.
5. **Bhakti and Sufi saints** — Kabir, Guru Nanak, Chaitanya, Mirabai, Tulsidas, Surdas; Chishti and Suhrawardi orders; who was contemporary of which ruler.
6. **Architecture** — Qutub Minar (started by Aibak, completed by Iltutmish), Alai Darwaza, Tughlaqabad, Humayun's Tomb, Taj Mahal, Red Fort, Fatehpur Sikri; who built what and where.
7. **Administration and revenue** — Iqta system, Zabt/Dahsala under Akbar, Mansabdari, Jagirdari; terms like Diwan, Wazir, Barid.
8. **Vijayanagara and Bahmani kingdoms** — Krishnadevaraya, Amuktamalyada, Ashtadiggajas, Hampi.
9. **Religious policy of rulers** — Akbar's Din-i-Ilahi and Sulh-i-kul, Aurangzeb's reimposition of Jizya (1679), Akbar's abolition of Jizya (1564).
10. **Firsts and lasts** — First battle of Panipat, last Mughal emperor (Bahadur Shah Zafar II), first Sultan to introduce token currency (Muhammad bin Tughlaq).

## 3. Examiner's Trap Types (with Examples)

### Trap 1: Almost-Correct Statement
A statement that is 90% right but fails on one word.
> "The Qutub Minar was completed by Qutbuddin Aibak."
Aibak only **started** it; **Iltutmish** completed it. Students who half-remember pick this.

### Trap 2: Chronological Confusion
Options list events/battles and ask for the correct order, or swap two adjacent rulers.
> Panipat (1526) → Khanwa (1527) → Ghaghra (1529). Examiners swap Khanwa and Ghaghra, or place Chausa before Khanwa.

### Trap 3: Similar-Sounding Names
> **Baburnama** (Babur's autobiography, in Turkish) vs **Baburnama's Persian translation** (by Abdur Rahim Khan-i-Khanan). Or **Ain-i-Akbari** vs **Akbarnama** — both by Abul Fazl, but Ain-i-Akbari is the third volume/statistical appendix.

### Trap 4: Negative Phrasing
> "Which of the following was **NOT** a contemporary of Akbar?" or "Which ruler did **NOT** build any part of the Qutub complex?" Under time pressure, students miss the NOT and pick the first true statement.

### Trap 5: Traveller–Ruler Mismatch
> Ibn Battuta visited during **Muhammad bin Tughlaq's** reign, not Alauddin Khilji's. Al-Biruni came with **Mahmud of Ghazni**, not Muhammad Ghori. These pairings are swapped constantly.

### Trap 6: Wrong Attribution of Policy
> Jizya was **abolished by Akbar (1564)** and **reimposed by Aurangzeb (1679)** — options often credit abolition to Jahangir or reimposition to Shah Jahan.

### Trap 7: Language of a Text
> Baburnama was written in **Turkish (Chagatai)**, not Persian. Tuzuk-i-Jahangiri is in Persian. Examiners bank on the assumption "Mughal court = Persian = everything."

### Trap 8: Dynasty–Monument Mismatch
> Alai Darwaza — **Alauddin Khilji**, not a Mughal. Humayun's Tomb — built by **Hamida Banu Begum (his widow)**, not by Humayun himself.

## 4. High-Yield Practice Questions (PYQ-Style) with Trap Analysis

**Q1.** Who completed the construction of the Qutub Minar?
(a) Qutbuddin Aibak (b) Iltutmish (c) Balban (d) Alauddin Khilji
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** Aibak began construction (first storey) but died; Iltutmish completed it. **Trap: Almost-Correct** — Aibak is the tempting answer because his name is attached to the monument.
*Source: NCERT Class 7 History (Our Pasts II)*

**Q2.** Arrange in chronological order: (1) Battle of Khanwa (2) First Battle of Panipat (3) Battle of Ghaghra (4) Battle of Chausa
(a) 2-1-3-4 (b) 1-2-3-4 (c) 2-3-1-4 (d) 2-1-4-3
**Answer: (a)**
**Solution:** Panipat 1526 → Khanwa 1527 → Ghaghra 1529 → Chausa 1539 (Humayun vs Sher Shah). **Trap: Chronological Confusion** — Chausa is often placed before Ghaghra.
*Source: PYQ-style*

**Q3.** Ibn Battuta, the Moroccan traveller, visited India during the reign of:
(a) Alauddin Khilji (b) Muhammad bin Tughlaq (c) Firoz Shah Tughlaq (d) Akbar
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** Ibn Battuta arrived in 1333 and was made Qazi of Delhi by Muhammad bin Tughlaq; his account is the *Rihla*. **Trap: Traveller–Ruler Mismatch.**
*Source: NCERT Class 7 History*

**Q4.** Which of the following was NOT written by Abul Fazl?
(a) Akbarnama (b) Ain-i-Akbari (c) Tuzuk-i-Jahangiri (d) None — all are his works
**Answer: (c)**
**Solution:** Tuzuk-i-Jahangiri is Jahangir's own memoir. **Trap: Negative Phrasing + Similar Names** — students skim "NOT" and pick (a).
*Source: NCERT Class 7/12 History themes*

**Q5.** The Mansabdari system was introduced by:
(a) Babur (b) Akbar (c) Jahangir (d) Shah Jahan
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** Akbar organised the nobility into mansabs (rank by zat and sawar). **Trap: Almost-Correct** — Jahangir/Shah Jahan modified it, which lures students.
*Source: NCERT Class 7 History*

**Q6.** Baburnama, the autobiography of Babur, was originally written in:
(a) Persian (b) Arabic (c) Turkish (d) Urdu
**Answer: (c)**
**Solution:** Written in Chagatai Turkish; translated into Persian by Abdur Rahim Khan-i-Khanan under Akbar. **Trap: Language of a Text.**
*Source: NCERT Class 7 History*

**Q7.** Who among the following reimposed Jizya on non-Muslims?
(a) Akbar (b) Jahangir (c) Shah Jahan (d) Aurangzeb
**Answer: (d)**
**Solution:** Aurangzeb reimposed Jizya in 1679; Akbar had abolished it in 1564. **Trap: Wrong Attribution of Policy.**
*Source: NCERT Class 7 History*

**Q8.** The Ashtadiggajas (eight literary gems) adorned the court of:
(a) Akbar (b) Krishnadevaraya (c) Harshavardhana (d) Shivaji
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** Krishnadevaraya of Vijayanagara patronised eight Telugu poets; he wrote *Amuktamalyada*. **Trap: Similar-Sounding** — Akbar's "Navratnas" is the look-alike concept.
*Source: NCERT Class 7 History*

**Q9.** Which Sufi order was most popular in India, with saints like Moinuddin Chishti and Nizamuddin Auliya?
(a) Suhrawardi (b) Chishti (c) Naqshbandi (d) Qadiri
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** The Chishti silsila (Ajmer, Delhi) was the most influential in the Sultanate era. **Trap: Almost-Correct** — Suhrawardi was strong in Sindh/Multan and is the common wrong pick.
*Source: NCERT Class 7 History*

**Q10.** The Battle of Haldighati (1576) was fought between Akbar's forces (under Man Singh) and:
(a) Rana Pratap (b) Rana Sanga (c) Maharana Amar Singh (d) Udai Singh
**Answer: (a)**
**Solution:** Rana Pratap of Mewar resisted at Haldighati. **Trap: Similar Names** — Rana Sanga fought Babur at Khanwa (1527); examiners swap the two Ranas.
*Source: NCERT Class 7 History*

## 5. The 15/45-Second Rule Applied

**The rule:** If a question is a direct fact you know cold, answer in **15 seconds** and bank the time. If it needs reasoning (chronology, statement-matching), give it **45 seconds** — no more. Beyond 45 seconds, mark and move on.

**Applied to Q2 (chronology of battles):** This is a 45-second question. Do not guess instantly — write the years mentally: Panipat 1526, Khanwa 1527, Ghaghra 1529, Chausa 1539. The moment you recall Chausa is Humayun–Sher Shah (1539), it must be last; only option (a) fits. Spending 45 seconds here is worth it because the question is fully solvable.

**Applied to Q3 (Ibn Battuta):** This is a 15-second question if you have memorised the traveller–ruler pair. If the pair does not come to mind within 15 seconds, do not burn 60 seconds "almost remembering" — mark it, move on, return in the review pass. Half-remembered traveller questions are where negative marking happens.

## 6. Exam-Wise Strategy Notes

- **UPSC/PCS:** Prioritise *why* questions — why Akbar's revenue system mattered, why Bhakti spread, comparisons between Sultanate and Mughal administration. Statement-based traps dominate.
- **SSC:** Memorise the sequence lists (dynasties, Mughals, battles with years) and traveller–book pairs. Most questions are one-line recalls.
- **Railway:** Expect repeated themes across shifts — Qutub Minar, Panipat battles, Taj Mahal, first/last facts. High repetition means high return on revision.
- **Banking:** Low frequency; revise only the top-20 facts (founders, firsts, famous monuments, books) rather than the whole chapter.

## 7. What to Revise Last-Minute

1. Dynasty order + founders (Slave to Lodi; six Great Mughals).
2. Battle–year–opponents table (Panipat, Khanwa, Chausa, Bilgram, Haldighati, Talikota 1565, Plassey is modern — do not mix).
3. Traveller → period → book.
4. Book → author → language.
5. Monument → builder → completer (where different).
6. Policy facts: Jizya abolished 1564 / reimposed 1679; Din-i-Ilahi 1582; token currency and capital shift — Muhammad bin Tughlaq.
