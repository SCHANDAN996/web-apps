Questions 26–50: Medium

26. Who was the founder of the Delhi Sultanate's Mamluk (Slave) dynasty?
(a) Iltutmish (b) Qutb-ud-din Aibak (c) Balban (d) Razia Sultan
Answer: (b)
Solution: Iltutmish is a tempting choice because he consolidated the Sultanate and is often called its real founder, but the dynasty was founded in 1206 by Qutb-ud-din Aibak, a slave-general of Muhammad Ghori. So the answer is Qutb-ud-din Aibak.
Source: NCERT Class 7 History (Our Pasts II)

27. The Iqta system, a land-grant-based revenue assignment, was institutionalised in the Delhi Sultanate primarily by:
(a) Alauddin Khalji (b) Iltutmish (c) Firoz Shah Tughlaq (d) Muhammad bin Tughlaq
Answer: (b)
Solution: Alauddin Khalji is tempting because of his famous revenue and market reforms, but the Iqta system — assigning revenue of a territory to officers in lieu of salary — was organised under Iltutmish. So the answer is Iltutmish.
Source: NCERT Class 7 History (Our Pasts II)

28. Which traveller visited India during the reign of Muhammad bin Tughlaq and wrote the 'Rihla'?
(a) Marco Polo (b) Al-Biruni (c) Nicolo Conti (d) Ibn Battuta
Answer: (d)
Solution: Al-Biruni is tempting as a famous foreign writer on India, but he came with Mahmud of Ghazni in the 11th century and wrote Kitab-ul-Hind. Ibn Battuta, a Moroccan traveller, came during Muhammad bin Tughlaq's reign and wrote the Rihla. So the answer is Ibn Battuta.
Source: NCERT Class 7 History (Our Pasts II)

29. Alauddin Khalji's market control policy primarily aimed at:
(a) Promoting foreign trade (b) Maintaining a large standing army at low cost (c) Encouraging peasant production (d) Financing temple construction
Answer: (b)
Solution: Promoting trade sounds plausible, but the price-control regulations were designed so that soldiers could live on low salaries, enabling Alauddin to maintain a huge standing army cheaply. So the answer is maintaining a large standing army at low cost.
Source: NCERT Class 7 History (Our Pasts II)

30. The 'Chalisa' (Group of Forty), a powerful corps of Turkish nobles, was broken up by:
(a) Iltutmish (b) Balban (c) Razia Sultan (d) Kaiqubad
Answer: (b)
Solution: Iltutmish is tempting because he created the Chalisa, but it was Balban who destroyed its power to strengthen the crown's authority. So the answer is Balban.

31. Which Sultan shifted the capital from Delhi to Daulatabad (Devagiri)?
(a) Alauddin Khalji (b) Ghiyasuddin Tughlaq (c) Muhammad bin Tughlaq (d) Firoz Shah Tughlaq
Answer: (c)
Solution: Firoz Shah Tughlaq is tempting as he founded several cities, but the famous and disastrous capital transfer to Daulatabad around 1327 was ordered by Muhammad bin Tughlaq. So the answer is Muhammad bin Tughlaq.

32. The Vijayanagara Empire was founded in 1336 by:
(a) Harihara and Bukka (b) Devaraya I and Devaraya II (c) Krishnadevaraya and Achyutaraya (d) Narasimha and Tirumala
Answer: (a)
Solution: Krishnadevaraya is tempting as the empire's most famous ruler, but the empire was founded by the brothers Harihara and Bukka of the Sangama dynasty, guided by sage Vidyaranya. So the answer is Harihara and Bukka.
Source: NCERT Class 7 History (Our Pasts II)

33. The Battle of Talikota (1565) was fought between the Vijayanagara Empire and:
(a) The Bahmani Kingdom alone (b) The Mughals (c) The combined armies of the Deccan Sultanates (d) The Portuguese
Answer: (c)
Solution: The Bahmani Kingdom alone is tempting because it was Vijayanagara's old rival, but by 1565 the Bahmani state had split, and it was the combined forces of Bijapur, Golconda, Ahmadnagar and Bidar that defeated Vijayanagara at Talikota. So the answer is the combined armies of the Deccan Sultanates.
Source: NCERT Class 7 History (Our Pasts II)

34. Babur's victory in the First Battle of Panipat (1526) was largely due to:
(a) Effective use of artillery and the Tulughma tactic (b) Superior cavalry numbers (c) Naval support (d) Alliance with the Rajputs
Answer: (a)
Solution: Superior numbers is tempting, but Babur's army was actually smaller than Ibrahim Lodi's; his field artillery and the Tulughma flanking tactic decided the battle. So the answer is effective use of artillery and the Tulughma tactic.
Source: NCERT Class 7 History (Our Pasts II)

35. Humayun lost his empire to Sher Shah Suri after defeats at:
(a) Panipat and Khanwa (b) Chausa and Kannauj (c) Ghaghra and Sirhind (d) Chanderi and Daurah
Answer: (b)
Solution: Panipat and Khanwa are tempting as famous Mughal battles, but those were Babur's victories. Humayun was defeated by Sher Shah at Chausa (1539) and Kannauj/Bilgram (1540), forcing his exile. So the answer is Chausa and Kannauj.
Source: NCERT Class 7 History (Our Pasts II)

36. The Grand Trunk Road (Sadak-e-Azam) was rebuilt and extended by:
(a) Akbar (b) Alauddin Khalji (c) Sher Shah Suri (d) Shah Jahan
Answer: (c)
Solution: Akbar is tempting because of his vast administrative works, but the rebuilding of the Grand Trunk Road from Sonargaon to the Indus region, with sarais and shade trees, was Sher Shah Suri's achievement. So the answer is Sher Shah Suri.
Source: NCERT Class 7 History (Our Pasts II)

37. Akbar abolished the Jizya tax on non-Muslims in the year:
(a) 1556 (b) 1564 (c) 1575 (d) 1582
Answer: (b)
Solution: 1582 is tempting because that is when Din-i-Ilahi was propounded, but Jizya was abolished earlier, in 1564; the pilgrimage tax had been abolished in 1563. So the answer is 1564.
Source: NCERT Class 7 History (Our Pasts II)

38. The Ibadat Khana (Hall of Prayer) where scholars of different religions debated was built by Akbar at:
(a) Agra (b) Delhi (c) Fatehpur Sikri (d) Lahore
Answer: (c)
Solution: Agra is tempting as Akbar's early capital, but the Ibadat Khana was constructed in 1575 at Fatehpur Sikri, where inter-faith discussions were held. So the answer is Fatehpur Sikri.
Source: NCERT Class 7 History (Our Pasts II)

39. The Mansabdari system under Akbar combined which two ranks?
(a) Diwan and Bakhshi (b) Jagir and Khalisa (c) Suba and Sarkar (d) Zat and Sawar
Answer: (d)
Solution: Jagir and Khalisa are tempting as related revenue terms, but every mansabdar held a dual rank — Zat (personal status and salary) and Sawar (number of cavalrymen to maintain). So the answer is Zat and Sawar.
Source: NCERT Class 7 History (Our Pasts II)

40. Which Mughal emperor was known as 'Zinda Pir' (Living Saint) for his austere lifestyle?
(a) Jahangir (b) Aurangzeb (c) Bahadur Shah I (d) Shah Jahan
Answer: (b)
Solution: Shah Jahan is tempting due to his piety in building mosques, but it was Aurangzeb who lived an austere life, sewed caps, copied the Quran, and was called Zinda Pir. So the answer is Aurangzeb.
Source: NCERT Class 7 History (Our Pasts II)

41. Consider the following statements about the Bhakti movement:
1. Guru Nanak founded Sikhism in the Punjab region.
2. Kabir's verses are preserved in the Guru Granth Sahib and the Bijak.
3. Chaitanya Mahaprabhu was a Bhakti saint of Bengal associated with Krishna devotion.
Which of the above are correct?
(a) 1, 2 and 3 (b) 2 and 3 only (c) 1 and 3 only (d) 1 and 2 only
Answer: (a)
Solution: Some may doubt statement 2, but Kabir's compositions do appear in the Guru Granth Sahib as well as the Bijak; all three statements are historically accurate. So the answer is 1, 2 and 3.

42. The Sufi order (silsila) that became most popular in India, associated with saints like Moinuddin Chishti and Nizamuddin Auliya, was:
(a) Suhrawardi (b) Qadiri (c) Naqshbandi (d) Chishti
Answer: (d)
Solution: Suhrawardi is tempting as it was also present in India, mainly in Sindh and Punjab, but the order that gained the widest following, with khanqahs at Ajmer and Delhi, was the Chishti silsila. So the answer is Chishti.

43. Arrange the following battles in correct chronological order:
1. Battle of Khanwa
2. First Battle of Panipat
3. Battle of Ghaghra
4. Battle of Chanderi
(a) 2, 1, 4, 3 (b) 1, 2, 3, 4 (c) 2, 4, 1, 3 (d) 4, 2, 1, 3
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is placing Chanderi before Khanwa; the correct sequence is Panipat (1526), Khanwa (1527), Chanderi (1528), Ghaghra (1529), which is 2, 1, 4, 3. So the answer is 2, 1, 4, 3.

44. The 'Ain-i-Akbari', which details Akbar's administration, was written by:
(a) Badauni (b) Faizi (c) Abul Fazl (d) Nizamuddin Ahmad
Answer: (c)
Solution: Badauni is tempting as another famous historian of Akbar's court, but he wrote Muntakhab-ut-Tawarikh and was critical of Akbar; the Ain-i-Akbari, the third volume of the Akbarnama, was authored by Abul Fazl. So the answer is Abul Fazl.
Source: NCERT Class 7 History (Our Pasts II)

45. Which Rajput ruler fought against Babur in the Battle of Khanwa (1527)?
(a) Rana Pratap (b) Maldeo of Marwar (c) Man Singh (d) Rana Sanga
Answer: (d)
Solution: Rana Pratap is tempting as the most famous Rajput resister, but he fought Akbar at Haldighati (1576); it was Rana Sanga of Mewar who faced Babur at Khanwa. So the answer is Rana Sanga.

(a) Aurangzeb (b) Bahadur Shah I (c) Farrukhsiyar (d) Muhammad Shah
Answer: (c)

47. Consider the following statements about Aurangzeb's reign:
1. He reimposed Jizya in 1679.
2. He was crowned twice, in 1658 and 1659.
3. He executed the ninth Sikh Guru, Guru Tegh Bahadur.
Which of the above are correct?
(a) 1 and 2 only (b) 1 and 3 only (c) 1, 2 and 3 (d) 2 and 3 only
Answer: (c)
Solution: Statement 2 may seem doubtful, but Aurangzeb indeed held two coronations, a hurried one in 1658 and a formal one in 1659; Jizya was reimposed in 1679 and Guru Tegh Bahadur was executed in 1675. So the answer is 1, 2 and 3.
Source: NCERT Class 7 History (Our Pasts II)

(a) Introducing gunpowder artillery (b) Signing a treaty with the Portuguese (c) Converting the kingdom to a new faith (d) Administrative reforms and founding a madrasa at Bidar
Answer: (d)
Solution: Introducing artillery is tempting as a Deccan-era innovation, but Mahmud Gawan is remembered for his administrative and revenue reforms and for building the famous madrasa at Bidar. So the answer is administrative reforms and founding a madrasa at Bidar.

(a) Qutb-ud-din Aibak (b) Iltutmish (c) Balban (d) Alauddin Khalji
Answer: (b)
Solution: Alauddin Khalji is tempting due to his economic regulations, but the silver tanka and copper jital standard currency was introduced by Iltutmish. So the answer is Iltutmish.

50. The Mughal painting school reached its zenith under which emperor, who employed masters like Bishan Das and Abul Hasan?
(a) Akbar (b) Jahangir (c) Shah Jahan (d) Humayun
Answer: (b)
Solution: Akbar is tempting because he established the imperial atelier and commissioned the Hamzanama, but Mughal painting reached its peak of refinement and naturalism under Jahangir, a great connoisseur. So the answer is Jahangir.
