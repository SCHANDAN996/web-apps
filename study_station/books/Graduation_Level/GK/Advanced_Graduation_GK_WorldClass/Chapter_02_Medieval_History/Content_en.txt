# Medieval Indian History

## 🎯 The Hook: A Kingdom Won by a Cannon That Never Fired

In 1526, on the plains of Panipat, an army of barely 12,000 men faced a force of nearly 100,000 soldiers and 1,000 war elephants. By every rule of medieval warfare, the smaller army should have been annihilated. Yet by sunset, Ibrahim Lodi lay dead on the battlefield, and a Central Asian adventurer named Babur had founded an empire that would rule India for over 300 years. His secret? Gunpowder, field artillery, and the *tulughma* tactic — technology and strategy defeating sheer numbers.

This is the story of Medieval India: a period where battles were won not just by swords, but by ideas, administration, and sometimes by a single betrayal. Master this chapter, and you master one of the most frequently tested areas of every competitive exam.

## 🧱 Chunk 1: The Delhi Sultanate (1206–1526)

### The Slave (Mamluk) Dynasty, 1206–1290

After Muhammad Ghori's death in 1206, his slave-general **Qutbuddin Aibak** founded the Delhi Sultanate. He began the construction of the **Qutub Minar** (completed by Iltutmish) and died in 1210 while playing *chaugan* (polo).

**Iltutmish** (1211–1236) was the real consolidator: he introduced the silver *tanka* and copper *jital* coins, organized the **Chalisa** (Corps of Forty nobles), and completed the Qutub Minar.

**Razia Sultan** (1236–1240) — the first and only woman ruler of Delhi — was killed after a rebellion. **Balban** (1266–1287) crushed the Chalisa, introduced *sijda* (prostration) and *paibos* (kissing the feet), and established the theory of kingship as divine (*Zil-i-Ilahi*).

### The Khalji Dynasty, 1290–1320

**Alauddin Khalji** (1296–1316) was the most powerful Sultan:
- First Sultan to attack South India (his general **Malik Kafur** raided Devagiri, Warangal, Dwarasamudra, Madurai).
- Introduced **market control policy** (price regulation), *dagh* (branding of horses), and *chehra* (descriptive roll of soldiers).
- First Sultan with a permanent standing army paid in cash.
- Built the **Alai Darwaza** and Siri Fort.

### The Tughlaq Dynasty, 1320–1414

- **Muhammad bin Tughlaq** (1325–1351): shifted the capital from Delhi to **Daulatabad**, introduced **token currency** (bronze coins) — both experiments failed disastrously. Ibn Battuta, the Moroccan traveller, visited his court.
- **Firoz Shah Tughlaq** (1351–1388): built canals, imposed *jaziya* on Brahmins too, and brought two Ashokan pillars to Delhi.
- **Timur** invaded Delhi in **1398**, shattering the Sultanate.

### The Sayyid (1414–1451) and Lodi (1451–1526) Dynasties

The Lodis were the first **Afghan** dynasty of Delhi. **Sikandar Lodi** founded **Agra** (1504). **Ibrahim Lodi** was defeated and killed by Babur at the **First Battle of Panipat, 1526**.

## 🐘 Chunk 2: The Mughal Empire (1526–1857)

### Babur (1526–1530)

- Won **Panipat (1526)** against Ibrahim Lodi, **Khanwa (1527)** against Rana Sanga, **Ghaghra (1529)** against the Afghans.
- Wrote his memoirs, the **Tuzuk-i-Baburi (Baburnama)**, in **Turkish**.
- First to use gunpowder and artillery systematically in India.

### Humayun (1530–1540, 1555–1556)

Defeated by **Sher Shah Suri** at **Chausa (1539)** and **Kannauj (1540)**; fled to Persia; regained the throne in 1555; died in 1556 falling from the stairs of his library (**Sher Mandal**, Purana Qila).

### The Sur Interregnum: Sher Shah Suri (1540–1545)

A brilliant administrator in just five years:
- Built the **Grand Trunk Road** (Sonargaon to Peshawar) with *sarais* (rest houses).
- Introduced the silver **Rupiya** — the ancestor of the modern rupee.
- Land revenue based on measurement (*zabt* system precursor).
- Built his tomb at **Sasaram** (Bihar).

### Akbar (1556–1605)

- Crowned at 13 under regent **Bairam Khan**; won the **Second Battle of Panipat (1556)** against **Hemu**.
- Abolished **jaziya (1564)** and the pilgrimage tax.
- Founded **Din-i-Ilahi (1582)** — a syncretic faith, not a mass religion.
- **Mansabdari system**: civil-military ranking (*zat* = personal rank, *sawar* = cavalry rank).
- **Navaratnas** (nine gems) of his court: Birbal, Tansen, Todar Mal, Abul Fazl, Faizi, Man Singh, Abdul Rahim Khan-i-Khana, Fakir Aziao-Din, Mullah Do-Piyaza.
- Built **Fatehpur Sikri**, Buland Darwaza (to commemorate the Gujarat victory).

### Jahangir (1605–1627)

- Known for justice ("chain of justice" — *zanjir-i-adl*).
- Executed **Guru Arjan Dev** (1606), the fifth Sikh Guru.
- **Nur Jahan** wielded real power; English ambassador **Sir Thomas Roe** visited his court (1615).

### Shah Jahan (1628–1658)

- The golden age of Mughal architecture: **Taj Mahal** (in memory of Mumtaz Mahal), **Red Fort**, **Jama Masjid** (Delhi), **Peacock Throne**.
- French traveller **Bernier** and Italian **Manucci** visited during his era.
- Imprisoned by Aurangzeb at Agra Fort; died 1666.

### Aurangzeb (1658–1707)

- Reimposed **jaziya (1679)**; executed **Guru Tegh Bahadur** (1675), the ninth Sikh Guru.
- Longest Deccan campaigns — died at **Ahmednagar (1707)**; the empire began crumbling.
- Called *Zinda Pir* (living saint); his reign saw the maximum territorial extent of the empire.

### Decline and the Later Mughals

After 1707, weak successors, the invasions of **Nadir Shah (1739)** — who took away the **Peacock Throne and Kohinoor** — and **Ahmad Shah Abdali** (Third Battle of Panipat, **1761**, against the Marathas) destroyed the empire. The last Mughal, **Bahadur Shah Zafar II**, was exiled to Rangoon after the 1857 revolt.

## 🕉️ Chunk 3: Vijayanagara & Bahmani Kingdoms

### Vijayanagara Empire (1336–1646)

- Founded by **Harihara and Bukka** (Sangama dynasty) in 1336, guided by sage **Vidyaranya**.
- Greatest ruler: **Krishnadevaraya** (1509–1529, Tuluva dynasty) — wrote *Amuktamalyada* (Telugu); his court had the **Ashtadiggajas** (eight Telugu poets).
- Foreign travellers: **Nicolo Conti** (Italian), **Abdur Razzaq** (Persian), **Domingo Paes** and **Fernao Nuniz** (Portuguese).
- Destroyed after the **Battle of Talikota (1565)** by the combined Deccan Sultanates.

### Bahmani Kingdom (1347–1527)

- Founded by **Alauddin Hasan Bahman Shah** (1347); capital later shifted from Gulbarga to **Bidar**.
- Famous minister: **Mahmud Gawan**.
- Broke into five Deccan Sultanates: **Bijapur, Golconda, Ahmadnagar, Bidar, Berar**.

## 🙏 Chunk 4: Bhakti and Sufi Movements

### Bhakti Saints

- **Shankaracharya** (8th century, pre-medieval but foundational) — Advaita.
- **Ramanuja** (11th–12th century) — Vishishtadvaita.
- **Kabir** (1440–1510) — disciple of Ramananda; dohas in the *Bijak*; rejected both Hindu and Muslim orthodoxy.
- **Guru Nanak** (1469–1539) — founder of Sikhism.
- **Chaitanya Mahaprabhu** (1486–1534) — Bengal Vaishnavism, kirtans.
- **Tulsidas** (1532–1623) — *Ramcharitmanas* (Awadhi), contemporary of Akbar.
- **Mirabai** — Rajput princess, devotee of Krishna.
- **Surdas** — *Sursagar*; devotee of Krishna.
- **Namdev, Tukaram, Eknath** — Maharashtra (Varkari tradition, Pandharpur).

### Sufi Orders (Silsilas)

- **Chishti** (most influential in India): **Khwaja Moinuddin Chishti** (Ajmer), **Nizamuddin Auliya** (Delhi), **Baba Farid** (Pakpattan).
- **Suhrawardi**: Bahauddin Zakariya (Multan).
- Sufi centres were called **khanqahs**; their gatherings of devotional music — **sama**.

## 🏰 Chunk 5: Art, Architecture & Administration — Quick Comparisons

| Feature | Delhi Sultanate | Mughals |
|---|---|---|
| Architecture style | Arcuate (arch & dome), red sandstone, calligraphy | Charbagh gardens, pietra dura, double dome |
| Landmark | Qutub Minar, Alai Darwaza | Taj Mahal, Red Fort, Fatehpur Sikri |
| Coinage | Tanka (silver), Jital (copper) | Rupiya (silver, from Sher Shah), Mohur (gold) |
| Revenue | Iqta system | Zabt/Dahsala (Todar Mal) |
| Language of court | Persian | Persian |

## ⚠️ Caution: Examiner's Trap

**Trap 1 — Chronological Confusion (कालानुक्रमिक भ्रम):** "Alauddin Khalji introduced token currency." ❌ Wrong — it was **Muhammad bin Tughlaq**. Examiners love swapping the two most "experimental" Sultans. Remember: Khalji = market control; Tughlaq = token currency + capital shift.

**Trap 2 — Almost-Correct Statement (लगभग सही कथन):** "Akbar abolished jaziya in 1564 and founded Din-i-Ilahi in 1564." The first half is right, but Din-i-Ilahi came in **1582**. One wrong digit makes the whole statement false — read every date.

**Trap 3 — Similar Names (समान नाम):** **Sher Mandal** (where Humayun died) vs **Sher Shah** (who defeated Humayun); **Battle of Khanwa (1527)** vs **Battle of Chausa (1539)**. Also: Guru **Arjan Dev** was executed by **Jahangir**, but Guru **Tegh Bahadur** by **Aurangzeb** — the classic swap.

## 🔗 Static-Dynamic Link

The **Grand Trunk Road**, rebuilt by Sher Shah Suri in the 16th century, still lives on as India's **NH-19/NH-44 corridor** (the old NH-1 and NH-2) — one of Asia's oldest and longest road networks, now part of the Golden Quadrilateral. Similarly, the **Rupiya** Sher Shah introduced in 1540 is the direct ancestor of the modern Indian **Rupee (₹)** — a 480-year-old legacy in your wallet.
