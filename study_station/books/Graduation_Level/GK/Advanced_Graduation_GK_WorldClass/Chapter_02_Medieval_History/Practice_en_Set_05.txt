101. Who founded the city of Agra and made it his capital in 1506?
(a) Babur (b) Sikandar Lodi (c) Ibrahim Lodi (d) Bahlul Lodi
Answer: (b)
Solution: The tempting wrong option is Babur, because Agra is strongly associated with the Mughals, but Babur only captured Agra in 1526 after Panipat. Sikandar Lodi founded Agra in 1504 and shifted his capital there in 1506 to control the Rajputana region better, so the answer is Sikandar Lodi.
Source: NCERT Class 7 History (Our Pasts II)

102. The First Battle of Panipat (1526) was fought between Babur and which ruler?
(a) Medini Rai (b) Rana Sanga (c) Ibrahim Lodi (d) Daulat Khan Lodi
Answer: (c)
Solution: Rana Sanga is the trap — Babur fought him at Khanwa in 1527, not at Panipat, and Daulat Khan Lodi merely invited Babur to invade India. The First Battle of Panipat was fought on 21 April 1526 between Babur and Sultan Ibrahim Lodi, who was killed on the battlefield, so the answer is Ibrahim Lodi.
Source: NCERT Class 7 History (Our Pasts II)

103. Which Mughal emperor introduced the 'Dahsala' or 'Zabti' system of land revenue?
(a) Babur (b) Humayun (c) Akbar (d) Jahangir
Answer: (c)
Solution: Sher Shah Suri's earlier reforms tempt many students, but the formal Dahsala system — fixing revenue on the basis of average produce and prices of the previous ten years — was introduced by Akbar in 1580 with the help of Raja Todar Mal, so the answer is Akbar.
Source: NCERT Class 7 History (Our Pasts II)

104. The 'Din-i-Ilahi' was propounded by Akbar in which year?
(a) 1575 (b) 1582 (c) 1592 (d) 1579
Answer: (b)
Solution: 1575 is the trap because that is when the Ibadat Khana was built at Fatehpur Sikri, and 1579 saw the Mahzar decree. Din-i-Ilahi, a syncretic ethical order rather than a new religion, was formally proclaimed in 1582, so the answer is 1582.
Source: NCERT Class 7 History (Our Pasts II)

105. Who wrote the 'Akbarnama' and 'Ain-i-Akbari'?
(a) Faizi (b) Badauni (c) Abul Fazl (d) Nizamuddin Ahmad
Answer: (c)
Solution: Faizi is the tempting wrong option since he was Abul Fazl's brother and Akbar's poet laureate, but he did not write these works. Abul Fazl authored the Akbarnama, whose third volume is the Ain-i-Akbari, a detailed statistical account of Akbar's empire, so the answer is Abul Fazl.
Source: NCERT Class 7 History (Our Pasts II)

106. The Battle of Haldighati (1576) was fought between Akbar's forces and which Rajput ruler?
(a) Rana Sanga (b) Maharana Pratap (c) Rana Udai Singh (d) Raja Man Singh
Answer: (b)
Solution: Raja Man Singh is the trap — he actually led Akbar's army in this battle, so he cannot be the opponent, and Rana Sanga died decades earlier in 1528. The Battle of Haldighati was fought in June 1576 between the Mughal army under Man Singh and Maharana Pratap of Mewar, so the answer is Maharana Pratap.
Source: NCERT Class 7 History (Our Pasts II)

107. Which Sikh Guru was executed on the orders of Jahangir?
(a) Guru Nanak (b) Guru Arjan Dev (c) Guru Angad (d) Guru Tegh Bahadur
Answer: (b)
Solution: Guru Tegh Bahadur is the classic trap — he too was executed, but by Aurangzeb in 1675, not by Jahangir. Guru Arjan Dev, the fifth Guru who compiled the Adi Granth, was executed in 1606 on Jahangir's orders after blessing the rebel prince Khusrau, so the answer is Guru Arjan Dev.
Source: NCERT Class 7 History (Our Pasts II)

108. The Peacock Throne (Takht-i-Taus) was taken away from India by which invader?
(a) Nadir Shah (b) Ahmad Shah Abdali (c) Timur (d) Babur
Answer: (a)
Solution: Ahmad Shah Abdali is the tempting option because of his repeated invasions and the Third Battle of Panipat, but he came later. Nadir Shah of Persia invaded in 1739, defeated Muhammad Shah at Karnal, and carried away the Peacock Throne and the Koh-i-Noor diamond, so the answer is Nadir Shah.
Source: NCERT Class 8 History (Our Pasts III)

109. Who among the following was known as 'Zinda Pir' (Living Saint)?
(a) Shah Jahan (b) Murad (c) Dara Shikoh (d) Aurangzeb
Answer: (d)
Solution: Dara Shikoh tempts students because of his deep interest in Sufism and the Upanishads, but he was never called Zinda Pir. Aurangzeb earned this title because of his austere personal life, simple dress, and strict religious observance, so the answer is Aurangzeb.
Source: NCERT Class 7 History (Our Pasts II)

110. The 'Jizya' tax, abolished by Akbar, was reimposed by which Mughal emperor?
(a) Aurangzeb (b) Shah Jahan (c) Jahangir (d) Bahadur Shah I
Answer: (a)
Solution: Shah Jahan is a common wrong guess because of his orthodox reputation, but he did not reimpose jizya. Akbar abolished it in 1564, and Aurangzeb reimposed it in 1679 on non-Muslims, a step that deepened opposition to his rule, so the answer is Aurangzeb.
Source: NCERT Class 7 History (Our Pasts II)

111. Who founded the Vijayanagara Empire in 1336?
(a) Narasimha and Tirumala (b) Krishnadevaraya and Achyutaraya (c) Devaraya I and Devaraya II (d) Harihara and Bukka
Answer: (d)
Solution: Krishnadevaraya is the trap because he is the most famous Vijayanagara ruler, but he belonged to the Tuluva dynasty nearly two centuries later. The Sangama brothers Harihara I and Bukka Raya I founded the empire in 1336 with the guidance of the sage Vidyaranya, so the answer is Harihara and Bukka.
Source: NCERT Class 7 History (Our Pasts II)

112. The Bahmani Kingdom was founded in 1347 by whom?
(a) Muhammad bin Tughlaq (b) Firuz Shah Bahmani (c) Alauddin Hasan Bahman Shah (d) Mahmud Gawan
Answer: (c)
Solution: Mahmud Gawan is the tempting wrong option since he was the Bahmani kingdom's most famous figure, but he was a wazir (minister), not the founder. Alauddin Hasan Bahman Shah (Hasan Gangu) founded the kingdom in 1347 with its capital at Gulbarga, so the answer is Alauddin Hasan Bahman Shah.
Source: NCERT Class 7 History (Our Pasts II)

113. Which Delhi Sultan transferred his capital from Delhi to Daulatabad?
(a) Alauddin Khalji (b) Firuz Shah Tughlaq (c) Muhammad bin Tughlaq (d) Ghiyasuddin Balban
Answer: (c)
Solution: Firuz Shah Tughlaq is the trap because of the similar dynastic name, but he founded new cities like Hisar and Firozabad rather than shifting the capital. Muhammad bin Tughlaq ordered the transfer of the capital from Delhi to Devagiri, renamed Daulatabad, around 1327 — a decision that failed badly, so the answer is Muhammad bin Tughlaq.
Source: NCERT Class 7 History (Our Pasts II)

114. The 'Iqta' system of land assignment was institutionalised in the Delhi Sultanate by which ruler?
(a) Qutbuddin Aibak (b) Iltutmish (c) Balban (d) Alauddin Khalji
Answer: (b)
Solution: Alauddin Khalji tempts students because of his revenue reforms, but he actually curtailed iqta assignments through measurement and centralisation. Iltutmish organised the empire into iqtas assigned to officers (iqtadars) in lieu of salary, making the system a regular institution, so the answer is Iltutmish.
Source: NCERT Class 7 History (Our Pasts II)

115. Who was the only woman ruler of the Delhi Sultanate?
(a) Chand Bibi (b) Razia Sultan (c) Nur Jahan (d) Rani Durgavati
Answer: (b)
Solution: Nur Jahan is the trap — she wielded enormous power as Jahangir's wife but was never a crowned sovereign, while Chand Bibi and Durgavati were regional warrior-queens, not Delhi rulers. Razia Sultan, daughter of Iltutmish, ruled Delhi from 1236 to 1240, so the answer is Razia Sultan.
Source: NCERT Class 7 History (Our Pasts II)

116. The famous iron pillar near the Qutub Minar is associated with which earlier ruler?
(a) Chandragupta II (b) Samudragupta (c) Harshavardhana (d) Ashoka
Answer: (a)
Solution: Ashoka is the tempting wrong option because he is famous for pillars, but his pillars are polished sandstone edicts, not iron. The rust-resistant iron pillar at Mehrauli bears an inscription of a ruler called Chandra, identified with the Gupta emperor Chandragupta II (Vikramaditya), so the answer is Chandragupta II.
Source: NCERT Class 6 History (Our Pasts I)

117. Which traveller visited India during the reign of Muhammad bin Tughlaq and wrote the 'Rihla'?
(a) Marco Polo (b) Al-Biruni (c) Nicolo Conti (d) Ibn Battuta
Answer: (d)
Solution: Al-Biruni is the trap — he wrote the famous 'Kitab-ul-Hind', but he came to India in the 11th century with Mahmud of Ghazni, centuries earlier. The Moroccan traveller Ibn Battuta served as a qazi under Muhammad bin Tughlaq and described his experiences in the Rihla, so the answer is Ibn Battuta.
Source: NCERT Class 7 History (Our Pasts II)

118. The Second Battle of Panipat (1556) was fought between the forces of Akbar and whom?
(a) Hemu (b) Sikandar Shah Suri (c) Ibrahim Lodi (d) Adil Shah Suri
Answer: (a)
Solution: Ibrahim Lodi is the chronological-confusion trap — he fought the First Battle of Panipat in 1526 against Babur. In the Second Battle of Panipat (5 November 1556), the young Akbar's forces under Bairam Khan defeated Hemu, the general of Adil Shah Suri, who had briefly captured Delhi, so the answer is Hemu.
Source: NCERT Class 7 History (Our Pasts II)

119. The medieval stretch of the route later called the Grand Trunk Road, connecting Bengal with the northwest, was rebuilt by which ruler?
(a) Akbar (b) Sher Shah Suri (c) Chandragupta Maurya (d) Alauddin Khalji
Answer: (b)
Solution: Akbar is a tempting guess because the Mughals maintained the road, but they only repaired and extended it. Sher Shah Suri rebuilt the ancient route from Sonargaon in Bengal to the Indus region, planting trees, wells, and sarais along it, so the answer is Sher Shah Suri.
Source: NCERT Class 7 History (Our Pasts II)

120. The 'Mansabdari' system of the Mughals primarily determined what?
(a) Rank, salary and military obligations of officers (b) Trade duties (c) Religious hierarchy (d) Land revenue rates
Answer: (a)
Solution: Land revenue rates is the trap — that was the function of the Zabti/Dahsala system, not mansabdari. Under the mansabdari system, every officer held a mansab (rank) with zat (personal status and salary) and sawar (cavalry to be maintained), fixing his pay and military obligations, so the answer is rank, salary and military obligations of officers.
Source: NCERT Class 7 History (Our Pasts II)

121. Consider the following statements about the Mughal emperor Jahangir:
1. He executed the fifth Sikh Guru, Guru Arjan Dev.
2. He issued twelve edicts (dastur-ul-amal) at his accession, including banning the levy of cesses like tamgha and mir bahri.
3. He introduced the Din-i-Ilahi.
Which of the statements given above is/are correct?
(a) 1, 2 and 3 (b) 2 and 3 only (c) 1 and 3 only (d) 1 and 2 only
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is statement 3, which sounds plausible because Jahangir was Akbar's son, but Din-i-Ilahi was Akbar's creation of 1582 and died with him. Statements 1 and 2 are correct: Jahangir executed Guru Arjan Dev in 1606 and issued twelve ordinances at accession abolishing certain cesses, so the answer is 1 and 2 only.

122. Arrange the following battles in correct chronological order:
1. Battle of Khanwa
2. Battle of Chausa
3. First Battle of Panipat
4. Battle of Ghaghra
(a) 1, 3, 2, 4 (b) 1, 4, 3, 2 (c) 3, 4, 1, 2 (d) 3, 1, 4, 2
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is placing Khanwa before Panipat because both involve Babur; in fact Panipat (1526) came first, then Khanwa against Rana Sanga (1527), then Ghaghra against the Afghans (1529). Chausa (1539), where Sher Shah defeated Humayun, came last, so the answer is 3, 1, 4, 2.
Source: PYQ-style

123. With reference to medieval Indian painting, consider the following statements:
1. The Hamzanama was painted during Akbar's reign.
2. Jahangir's reign is considered the golden age of Mughal miniature painting.
3. Aurangzeb was a great patron of painting and established royal ateliers.
Which of the statements given above is/are correct?
(a) 2 and 3 only (b) 1 only (c) 1 and 2 only (d) 1, 2 and 3
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is statement 3 — because Aurangzeb ruled long and expanded the empire, students assume he patronised all arts, but he withdrew court patronage from painting, and artists dispersed to regional courts. The Hamzanama was indeed painted under Akbar, and Mughal painting reached its refinement peak under Jahangir, so the answer is 1 and 2 only.
Source: NCERT Class 7 History (Our Pasts II)

124. Consider the following pairs of travellers and the rulers during whose reign they visited India:
1. Marco Polo — Pandyan kingdom of Madurai
2. Ibn Battuta — Muhammad bin Tughlaq
3. Nicolo Conti — Vijayanagara under Devaraya I
4. Domingo Paes — Krishnadevaraya
How many pairs given above are correctly matched?
(a) All four (b) Only one (c) Only three (d) Only two
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is to doubt pair 1 or pair 3 because they are less famous, but all four are correct: Marco Polo visited the Pandyan realm around 1293, Ibn Battuta served Muhammad bin Tughlaq, the Venetian Nicolo Conti visited Vijayanagara in Devaraya I's time, and the Portuguese horse-trader Domingo Paes described Krishnadevaraya's court, so the answer is all four.
Source: PYQ-style

125. With reference to the Bhakti and Sufi movements, consider the following statements:
1. The Chishti silsila was introduced in India by Khwaja Moinuddin Chishti, who settled at Ajmer.
2. Guru Nanak's teachings were compiled in the Adi Granth by Guru Arjan Dev.
3. Kabir's verses are found only in the Guru Granth Sahib and nowhere else.
Which of the statements given above is/are correct?
(a) 1 only (b) 2 and 3 only (c) 1, 2 and 3 (d) 1 and 2 only
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is statement 3 — the word 'only' makes it false, since Kabir's verses also appear in the Bijak compiled by the Kabirpanthis and in the Dadu Panthi collections, not solely in the Sikh scripture. Statements 1 and 2 are correct: Moinuddin Chishti established the Chishti order at Ajmer, and Guru Arjan Dev compiled the Adi Granth in 1604, so the answer is 1 and 2 only.
Source: NCERT Class 7 History (Our Pasts II)
