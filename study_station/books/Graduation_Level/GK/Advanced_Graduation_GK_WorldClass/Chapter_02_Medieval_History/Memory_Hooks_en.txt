# 🪄 Memory Hooks & Short Tricks — Medieval Indian History

---

**Box 1 — The Delhi Sultanate Dynasties (in order)**
**"Sunny Kids Took Silly Lunch"**
**S**lave (Mamluk) → **K**hilji → **T**ughlaq → **S**ayyid → **L**odi
⚡ Trap alert: Sayyid comes BEFORE Lodi — examiners love swapping these two.

---

**Box 2 — Mughal Emperors (the "Big Six")**
**"Boys Have A Joyful Summer Afternoon"**
**B**abur → **H**umayun → **A**kbar → **J**ahangir → **S**hah Jahan → **A**urangzeb
⚡ Trap alert: Shah Jahan sits between Jahangir and Aurangzeb — don't skip him.

---

**Box 3 — Babur's Four Battles (in order)**
**"P-K-C-G: Panipat Kept Conquering Ghaghra"**
**P**anipat (1526) → **K**hanwa (1527) → **C**handeri (1528) → **G**haghra (1529)
⚡ Careful! Chanderi (1528) comes BEFORE Ghaghra (1529) — the years climb in order.

---

**Box 4 — Alauddin Khilji's Market Reforms**
**"SHE-MAN controlled the market"**
**SH**ahna-i-Mandi (market officer) — remember a "She-Man" policing prices!
Pair it with: Diwan-i-Riyasat (commerce dept). If the question says "price control," think Khilji, NOT Akbar.

---

**Box 5 — Akbar's Navratnas (Nine Gems)**
**"Birbal's wit, Tansen's tunes, Todar Mal's accounts"**
Anchor the three most-asked gems first: **Birbal**, **Tansen**, **Todar Mal** — then add Abul Fazl, Faizi, Raja Man Singh, Abdul Rahim Khan-i-Khana, Fakir Aziao-Din, Mulla Do-Piyaza.
⚡ Trap: Tulsidas was NOT a Navratna — he was a contemporary poet but not in Akbar's court.

---

**Box 6 — Land Revenue Systems**
**"ZABT the DAH"**
Akbar's **Zabt/Dahsala** system — Todar Mal's settlement based on ten-year averages.
Hook: "Todar Mal counted for TEN years (Dahsala = dah = ten)."

---

**Box 7 — Battles of Panipat (all three)**
**"Babur Built, Bairam Boosted, Abdali Broke"**
1st (1526): Babur vs Ibrahim Lodi — Mughal rule begins
2nd (1556): Bairam Khan (for Akbar) vs Hemu
3rd (1761): Ahmad Shah Abdali vs Marathas — Maratha expansion checked

---

**Box 8 — Iqtadari vs Jagirdari**
**"IQTADAR = Sultan's power; JAGIR = Mughal's gift"**
Iqta → Delhi Sultanate; Jagir → Mughals.
⚡ Classic trap: examiners swap the two systems between the two periods.

---

**Box 9 — Bhakti Saints (North India)**
**"KRN-C — KaRan NCe"**
**K**abir, **R**aidas (Ravidas), **N**anak, **C**haitanya
Hook: "Kabir wove, Raidas cobbled, Nanak farmed truth, Chaitanya danced."
⚡ Trap: Chaitanya Mahaprabhu = Bengal/Odisha, NOT Maharashtra (that's the Varkari tradition: Dnyaneshwar, Tukaram, Namdev, Eknath).

---

**Box 10 — Sufi Orders in India**
**"CSNQ — Cool Saints Never Quarrel"**
**C**hishti, **S**uhrawardi, **N**aqshbandi, **Q**adiri
Hook: Chishti = music-loving (sama), Naqshbandi = orthodox, opposed Akbar's liberalism.
⚡ Trap: Suhrawardis accepted government posts; Chishtis stayed away from courts.

---

**Box 11 — Vijayanagara's Founders**
**"Hari-Bukka Sangama"** — say it like a chant!
Harihara and Bukka, of the **Sangama** dynasty, founded Vijayanagara (1336), guided by **Vidyaranya**.
⚡ Trap: Krishnadevaraya was TULUVA dynasty, NOT the founder — the most common examiner trap in this chapter.

---

**Box 12 — Krishnadevaraya's Court Poets**
**"Ashtadiggajas = 8 elephants holding up his court"**
Eight Telugu poets. Remember **Allasani Peddana** — the chief, honoured as Andhra Kavita Pitamaha; his famous work is **Manucharitram**.
Krishnadevaraya himself wrote **Amuktamalyada** (Telugu). Hook: "The king wrote Amuktamalyada; his poet wrote Manucharitram."
⚡ Trap: examiners swap these two works between the king and the poet.

---

**Box 13 — Travellers to India (who came when)**
**"Polo went South, Battuta went Delhi, Biruni came with the invader."**
- Marco Polo → Pandya kingdom (South, ~1290s)
- Ibn Battuta → Muhammad bin Tughlaq's court
- Al-Biruni → came with Mahmud of Ghazni (Kitab-ul-Hind)
- Nicolo Conti, Abdur Razzaq → Vijayanagara (Deva Raya II era)

---

**Box 14 — Mughal Architecture Quick-Map**
**"Akbar = Red, Shah Jahan = White"**
Akbar → red sandstone (Fatehpur Sikri, Agra Fort)
Shah Jahan → white marble (Taj Mahal, Moti Masjid)
⚡ Trap: Humayun's Tomb was built by his widow (Haji Begum), NOT by Akbar himself — first garden-tomb, precursor of the Taj.

---

**Box 15 — Din-i-Ilahi & Ibadat Khana**
**"Akbar's Ibadat Khana (1575) → Din-i-Ilahi (1582)"**
Hook: "First he DEBATED (Ibadat Khana), then he DECIDED (Din-i-Ilahi)."
⚡ Trap: Din-i-Ilahi was NOT a mass religion — it had very few followers (Birbal was the only Hindu among them).

---

## ⏭️ Skip Strategy — When to Skip a Question

**Tip 1 — The "Four Founders" Freeze:**
If a question asks about founders of four different dynasties (e.g., "Who founded the Sayyid dynasty?") and you blank on even ONE option's dynasty, **skip immediately**. Guessing between Khizr Khan, Bahlul Lodi, and Qutbuddin Aibak under pressure has near-random odds — the names are deliberately similar. Come back only if time remains.

**Tip 2 — The Date-Cluster Trap:**
Questions asking for the EXACT year of a mid-tier event (e.g., "In which year was the Battle of Chanderi fought?") where options are 1526 / 1527 / 1528 / 1529 — if you haven't memorized the sequence, **skip**. These are designed so that every option is a real Babur battle year; elimination is impossible without exact recall.

**Tip 3 — The "Statement I & II" Overload:**
In statement-based questions mixing Bhakti/Sufi facts (e.g., "Statement I: Chaitanya belonged to the Varkari tradition…"), if you must evaluate more than two unfamiliar statements, **skip and mark for review**. These questions punish partial knowledge — one wrong statement evaluation flips the entire answer. Attempt only if you can confidently judge at least all-but-one statement.
