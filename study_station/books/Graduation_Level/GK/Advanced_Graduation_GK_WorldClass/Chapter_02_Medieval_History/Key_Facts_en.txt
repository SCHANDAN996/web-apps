# Key Facts & Figures — Medieval Indian History

## Part 1: High-Yield Facts

| Fact | Description | Examiner's Trap | Trap Type |
|---|---|---|---|
| First Battle of Tarain (1191) | Prithviraj Chauhan defeated Muhammad Ghori | Confused with Second Battle of Tarain (1192) where Ghori won | Chronology confusion |
| Second Battle of Tarain (1192) | Ghori defeated Prithviraj; foundation of Muslim rule in North India | Swapped with 1191 result | Result reversal |
| Battle of Chandawar (1194) | Ghori defeated Jaichandra of Kannauj | Confused with Tarain battles | Similar-name confusion |
| Qutbuddin Aibak (1206–1210) | Founder of Slave (Mamluk) Dynasty; "Lakh Baksh"; died playing Chaugan (polo) | Called builder of completed Qutub Minar (he only began it) | Half-true statement |
| Qutub Minar | Started by Aibak, completed by Iltutmish; repaired by Firoz Shah Tughlaq | Attributed wholly to Aibak or wholly to Iltutmish | Attribution trap |
| Iltutmish (1211–1236) | Real consolidator of Delhi Sultanate; introduced Iqta system, silver Tanka & copper Jital; set up Chalisa (Group of Forty) | Chalisa credited to Balban (he destroyed it) | Reversed attribution |
| Razia Sultan (1236–1240) | First and only woman ruler of Delhi; daughter of Iltutmish | Called wife of Iltutmish or first woman ruler of "India" loosely | Relationship trap |
| Balban (1266–1287) | Theory of Kingship (Zil-i-Ilahi); Sijda & Paibos; destroyed Chalisa; established Diwan-i-Arz | Sijda-Paibos credited to other sultans | Practice attribution |
| Alauddin Khilji (1296–1316) | Market control policy; first Sultan to attack South India (via Malik Kafur); Diwan-i-Mustakharaj; permanent standing army | Market reforms credited to Muhammad bin Tughlaq | Policy swap |
| Malik Kafur | Alauddin's general, led Deccan campaigns | Called an independent ruler | Status trap |
| Alauddin's conquest | First Delhi Sultan to cross Vindhyas; captured Devagiri, Warangal, Dwarasamudra, Madurai (raids) | Claimed he annexed South permanently | Exaggeration trap |
| Siri Fort | Built by Alauddin Khilji (Delhi) | Confused with Tughlaqabad (Ghiyasuddin) or Firozabad | City-founder mix-up |
| Muhammad bin Tughlaq (1325–1351) | Token currency (bronze/copper); shifted capital Delhi→Daulatabad; Diwan-i-Kohi (agriculture); Ibn Battuta visited | Token currency called successful (it failed); Ibn Battuta placed in Akbar's court | Outcome & visitor trap |
| Ibn Battuta | Moroccan traveller; wrote Rihla; came during MBT's reign | Confused with Marco Polo (Venice, Pandyan kingdom) or Nicolo Conti | Traveller-period mix |
| Firoz Shah Tughlaq (1351–1388) | Canals, Diwan-i-Khairat, Diwan-i-Bandagan; brought Ashokan pillars to Delhi; imposed Jaziya strictly | Called a liberal ruler who abolished Jaziya | Character reversal |
| Timur's invasion (1398) | Invaded during Nasiruddin Mahmud Tughlaq's reign | Placed during Lodhi period | Chronology trap |
| Sayyid Dynasty (1414–1451) | Founded by Khizr Khan | Founder confused with Bahlul Lodhi | Dynasty-founder swap |
| Lodhi Dynasty (1451–1526) | First Afghan (Pashtun) dynasty of Delhi; founded by Bahlul Lodhi | Called first "Turkish" dynasty | Origin trap |
| Sikandar Lodhi | Founded Agra (1504); introduced Gaz-i-Sikandari | Agra's founding credited to Akbar | City attribution |
| First Battle of Panipat (1526) | Babur defeated Ibrahim Lodhi; used artillery (Tulughma tactics) | Confused with Panipat 1556 & 1761 | Same-place battles |
| Babur (1526–1530) | Wrote Tuzuk-i-Baburi (Baburnama) in Turkish | Language stated as Persian | Language trap |
| Battle of Khanwa (1527) | Babur defeated Rana Sanga | Confused with Chanderi (1528, vs Medini Rai) | Battle sequence |
| Humayun (1530–40, 1555–56) | Lost to Sher Shah at Chausa (1539) & Kannauj (1540); died falling from library stairs (Sher Mandal) | Death cause given as battle | Cause trap |
| Sher Shah Suri (1540–1545) | Grand Trunk Road (Sadak-e-Azam), Sarais, Rupiya (silver), Patta & Qabuliyat, land measurement | GT Road credited to Akbar or the British | Attribution trap |
| Battle of Chausa (1539) | Sher Shah defeated Humayun; Humayun escaped using a water skin (mashak) | Result reversed | Result reversal |
| Second Battle of Panipat (1556) | Akbar's forces (Bairam Khan) defeated Hemu | Hemu called a Mughal general | Side confusion |
| Akbar (1556–1605) | Ended Jaziya (1564) & pilgrimage tax (1563); Din-i-Ilahi (1582); Mansabdari system; Ibadat Khana (1575) | Din-i-Ilahi called a new religion with mass conversion (it had few followers) | Exaggeration trap |
| Battle of Haldighati (1576) | Man Singh (Akbar) vs Maharana Pratap; Pratap not decisively defeated | Called a clear Mughal conquest of Mewar | Outcome trap |
| Mansabdari | Rank system: Zat (personal rank) & Sawar (cavalry) | Zat/Sawar meanings swapped | Terminology swap |
| Todar Mal | Akbar's revenue minister; Dahsala/Zabti system; wrote Ain-i-Akbari data under Abul Fazl's authorship | Ain-i-Akbari credited to Todar Mal | Authorship trap |
| Abul Fazl | Wrote Akbarnama & Ain-i-Akbari; killed by Bir Singh Bundela (at Jahangir's instigation) | Confused with Faizi (his brother, poet) | Person swap |
| Jahangir (1605–1627) | Chain of Justice; Nur Jahan's influence; executed Guru Arjan Dev (1606); English visitors: William Hawkins, Sir Thomas Roe | Guru Arjan's execution placed under Aurangzeb | Ruler swap |
| Sir Thomas Roe | English ambassador to Jahangir's court (1615–18) | Placed in Akbar's or Shah Jahan's court | Court-period trap |
| Shah Jahan (1628–1658) | Taj Mahal, Red Fort, Jama Masjid, Peacock Throne; Moti Masjid (Agra) | Moti Masjid (Delhi, by Aurangzeb) confused with Agra's | Monument mix-up |
| Aurangzeb (1658–1707) | Reimposed Jaziya (1679); executed Guru Tegh Bahadur (1675); called Zinda Pir; long Deccan wars vs Marathas | Jaziya reimposition dated to Akbar's era | Chronology trap |
| Guru Tegh Bahadur | 9th Sikh Guru, executed by Aurangzeb (1675) | Confused with Guru Arjan Dev (5th, Jahangir) | Guru-number trap |
| Shivaji | Coronation 1674 (Raigad); Ashtapradhan council; Chauth & Sardeshmukhi taxes | Chauth called a Mughal tax | Tax-origin trap |
| Chauth & Sardeshmukhi | Chauth = 1/4 of revenue; Sardeshmukhi = additional 1/10 | Percentages swapped | Numerical swap |
| Battle of Plassey context | Medieval period ends conventionally with Mughal decline; Bahadur Shah Zafar = last Mughal (d. 1862) | Aurangzeb called last Mughal emperor | "Last ruler" trap |
| Vijayanagara Empire (1336) | Founded by Harihara & Bukka (Sangama dynasty); capital Hampi on Tungabhadra | River stated as Krishna | Geography trap |
| Krishnadevaraya (1509–1529) | Tuluva dynasty; wrote Amuktamalyada (Telugu); Ashtadiggajas in court | Amuktamalyada language given as Sanskrit/Kannada | Language trap |
| Battle of Talikota (1565) | Deccan Sultanates defeated Vijayanagara (Rama Raya killed) | Called a Mughal victory | Participant trap |
| Bahmani Kingdom (1347) | Founded by Alauddin Hasan Bahman Shah; later split into 5 Deccan Sultanates | Founder confused with Zafar Khan (Gujarat) | Founder swap |
| Mahmud Gawan | Bahmani minister; expanded kingdom; executed (1481) | Called a Bahmani sultan | Status trap |
| Bhakti: Kabir | Disciple of Ramananda; dohas in Bijak; nirguna bhakti | Called a saguna saint | Doctrine trap |
| Bhakti: Guru Nanak (1469–1539) | Founder of Sikhism; contemporary of Babur & Lodhis | Placed in Akbar's era | Chronology trap |
| Bhakti: Chaitanya Mahaprabhu | Bengal Vaishnavism; kirtans; devotee of Krishna | Linked to Maharashtra (that was Tukaram/Namdev) | Region swap |
| Bhakti: Mirabai | Rajput princess; devotee of Krishna | Called devotee of Rama | Deity swap |
| Bhakti: Tulsidas | Wrote Ramcharitmanas (Awadhi) in Akbar's era | Language stated as Sanskrit/Braj | Language trap |
| Sufi: Chishti order | Khwaja Moinuddin Chishti (Ajmer); Nizamuddin Auliya (Delhi) | Nizamuddin placed in Ajmer | Centre swap |
| Sufi: Suhrawardi order | Bahauddin Zakariya (Multan) | Confused with Chishti saints | Order swap |
| Amir Khusrau | "Tuti-e-Hind" (Parrot of India); associated with Chishti order; saw 7 sultans' reigns | Called a Mughal court poet | Period trap |
| Alai Darwaza | Built by Alauddin Khilji; first true arch/dome example | Credited to Qutbuddin Aibak | Attribution trap |
| Charminar (1591) | Built by Muhammad Quli Qutb Shah (Hyderabad) | Credited to Akbar | Builder trap |
| Gol Gumbaz | Bijapur; Adil Shahi dynasty; huge dome | Placed in Delhi/Agra | Location trap |
| Iqta system | Land revenue assignment to officers (iqtadars); started by Iltutmish | Called land ownership grant | Concept trap |
| Jaziya | Tax on non-Muslims; abolished by Akbar (1564), reimposed by Aurangzeb (1679) | Dates reversed | Date reversal |
| Zabt/Dahsala | Akbar's land revenue system; 10-year average (Todar Mal) | Credited to Sher Shah alone | Attribution trap |
| Sarai | Rest houses on highways (Sher Shah) | Called military forts | Function trap |
| Panchayati administration of Cholas | Not medieval-north; used as distractor in Sultanate polity questions | Mixed into Delhi Sultanate features | Cross-topic trap |

## Part 2: Time Management (15/45-Second Rule)

| Topic | Question Type | Time | Score Maximizer? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Dynasty founders & capitals | Fact-based | 15 sec | ✅ Yes |
| Battles (year, winner, loser) | Fact-based | 15 sec | ✅ Yes |
| Travellers & their periods | Fact-based | 15 sec | ✅ Yes |
| Books & authors (language) | Fact-based | 15 sec | ✅ Yes |
| Monuments & builders | Fact-based | 15 sec | ✅ Yes |
| Bhakti/Sufi saints & doctrines | Fact-based | 15 sec | ✅ Yes |
| Tax names (Jaziya, Chauth, Zakat, Kharaj) | Fact-based | 15 sec | ✅ Yes |
| Chronology ordering (4 events) | Analytical | 45 sec | ❌ No |
| Statement-based (I & II correct?) | Analytical | 45 sec | ❌ No |
| Administrative systems (Iqta, Mansabdari, Zabt) comparison | Analytical | 45 sec | ❌ No |
| Match-the-following (ruler–reform, saint–region) | Analytical | 45 sec | ❌ No |
| Cause-effect (decline of Sultanate/Mughals) | Analytical | 45 sec | ❌ No |

**Strategy:** Attempt all 15-sec fact questions first (battles, founders, books, travellers). Reserve 45-sec slots for chronology and statement questions. Skip any question mixing two dynasties' policies unless both are certain.
