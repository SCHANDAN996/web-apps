1. Who founded the Delhi Sultanate in 1206 CE?
(a) Iltutmish (b) Qutb-ud-din Aibak (c) Balban (d) Muhammad Ghori
Answer: (b)
Solution: Muhammad Ghori conquered northern India but died in 1206 without naming an heir in India; his slave-general Qutb-ud-din Aibak then declared himself Sultan at Lahore, founding the Mamluk (Slave) dynasty and the Delhi Sultanate. So the answer is Qutb-ud-din Aibak.
Source: NCERT Class 7 History (Our Pasts II)

2. The Qutub Minar was completed by which ruler?
(a) Qutb-ud-din Aibak (b) Iltutmish (c) Alauddin Khalji (d) Firoz Shah Tughlaq
Answer: (b)
Solution: Aibak only began the construction and completed the first storey; the tempting trap is to credit him with the whole tower. Iltutmish added three more storeys and completed it, and Firoz Shah Tughlaq later repaired the top storeys after lightning damage. So the answer is Iltutmish.
Source: NCERT Class 7 History (Our Pasts II)

3. Who was the first and only woman ruler of the Delhi Sultanate?
(a) Razia Sultan (b) Nur Jahan (c) Chand Bibi (d) Rani Durgavati
Answer: (a)
Solution: Nur Jahan wielded power as Jahangir's wife but never ruled as sovereign, which is the common confusion. Razia, daughter of Iltutmish, ruled Delhi from 1236 to 1240. So the answer is Razia Sultan.
Source: NCERT Class 7 History (Our Pasts II)

4. The market control policy (price regulation) of the Delhi Sultanate is associated with:
(a) Balban (b) Alauddin Khalji (c) Muhammad bin Tughlaq (d) Sikandar Lodi
Answer: (b)
Solution: Muhammad bin Tughlaq is remembered for token currency and shifting the capital, not price control — a frequent mix-up. Alauddin Khalji fixed prices of essential goods in Delhi's markets to maintain a large standing army at low cost. So the answer is Alauddin Khalji.
Source: NCERT Class 7 History (Our Pasts II)

5. Who introduced the token currency of bronze and copper coins?
(a) Muhammad bin Tughlaq (b) Alauddin Khalji (c) Firoz Shah Tughlaq (d) Ibrahim Lodi
Answer: (a)
Solution: Alauddin's famous experiment was market regulation, not token currency. Muhammad bin Tughlaq issued bronze/copper tokens at par with silver tankas around 1329–30; the scheme failed due to widespread counterfeiting. So the answer is Muhammad bin Tughlaq.
Source: NCERT Class 7 History (Our Pasts II)

6. The First Battle of Panipat (1526) was fought between Babur and:
(a) Rana Sanga (b) Sikandar Lodi (c) Hemu (d) Ibrahim Lodi
Answer: (d)
Solution: Rana Sanga fought Babur at Khanwa in 1527, not Panipat — a classic chronological trap. At Panipat on 21 April 1526, Babur's artillery and cavalry tactics defeated and killed Sultan Ibrahim Lodi. So the answer is Ibrahim Lodi.
Source: NCERT Class 7 History (Our Pasts II)

7. The Battle of Khanwa (1527) was fought between Babur and:
(a) Ibrahim Lodi (b) Medini Rai (c) Rana Sanga (d) Mahmud Lodi
Answer: (c)
Solution: Ibrahim Lodi belongs to Panipat 1526; do not merge the two battles. At Khanwa, Babur defeated the Rajput confederacy led by Rana Sanga of Mewar. So the answer is Rana Sanga.
Source: NCERT Class 7 History (Our Pasts II)

8. Who wrote the Baburnama (Tuzuk-i-Baburi)?
(a) Babur (b) Abul Fazl (c) Gulbadan Begum (d) Badauni
Answer: (a)
Solution: Gulbadan Begum wrote the Humayunnama, which is the similar-name trap. Babur wrote his memoirs, the Baburnama, in Chagatai Turkish. So the answer is Babur.
Source: NCERT Class 7 History (Our Pasts II)

9. Sher Shah Suri defeated Humayun at the Battle of Chausa in:
(a) 1526 (b) 1539 (c) 1540 (d) 1556
Answer: (b)
Solution: 1540 is tempting because that is the year of the Battle of Kannauj (Bilgram), where Sher Shah again defeated Humayun — the two defeats are often confused. Chausa came first, in 1539. So the answer is 1539.
Source: NCERT Class 7 History (Our Pasts II)

10. The Grand Trunk Road (Sadak-e-Azam) was rebuilt and extended by:
(a) Akbar (b) Alauddin Khalji (c) Sher Shah Suri (d) Aurangzeb
Answer: (c)
Solution: Akbar consolidated the empire but the road's restoration with sarais, trees and wells is credited to Sher Shah Suri, who rebuilt the ancient route from Sonargaon to the Indus region. So the answer is Sher Shah Suri.
Source: NCERT Class 7 History (Our Pasts II)

11. Akbar abolished the jizya tax in which year?
(a) 1556 (b) 1562 (c) 1579 (d) 1564
Answer: (d)
Solution: 1562 is tempting because Akbar abolished the tax on Hindu pilgrims that year — the two abolitions are a standard trap. The jizya on non-Muslims was abolished in 1564. So the answer is 1564.
Source: NCERT Class 11 History (Themes in Indian History II)

12. The Din-i-Ilahi was propounded by Akbar in:
(a) 1575 (b) 1579 (c) 1605 (d) 1582
Answer: (d)
Solution: 1575 is the year of the Ibadat Khana (House of Worship) at Fatehpur Sikri, which is the chronological confusion here. Akbar promulgated the Din-i-Ilahi in 1582 as a syncretic ethical code. So the answer is 1582.
Source: NCERT Class 11 History (Themes in Indian History II)

13. The Ain-i-Akbari, which details Akbar's administration, was written by:
(a) Badauni (b) Faizi (c) Abul Fazl (d) Nizamuddin Ahmad
Answer: (c)
Solution: Badauni wrote the Muntakhab-ut-Tawarikh, a work critical of Akbar — the two authors are often swapped. Abul Fazl wrote the Akbarnama, whose third volume is the Ain-i-Akbari. So the answer is Abul Fazl.
Source: NCERT Class 11 History (Themes in Indian History II)

14. The Second Battle of Panipat (1556) was fought between the forces of Akbar and:
(a) Sikandar Shah Suri (b) Ibrahim Lodi (c) Hemu (d) Daulat Khan Lodi
Answer: (c)
Solution: Sikandar Shah Suri was defeated by Humayun in 1555, not at Panipat 1556. Hemu, the general of Adil Shah Suri, was defeated and killed by Akbar's forces under Bairam Khan on 5 November 1556. So the answer is Hemu.
Source: NCERT Class 7 History (Our Pasts II)

15. Who built the Taj Mahal?
(a) Shah Jahan (b) Jahangir (c) Akbar (d) Aurangzeb
Answer: (a)
Solution: Jahangir built the tomb of Akbar at Sikandra and Itimad-ud-Daulah's tomb belongs to Nur Jahan's patronage — both are distractors. Shah Jahan built the Taj Mahal at Agra in memory of Mumtaz Mahal between 1632 and 1653. So the answer is Shah Jahan.
Source: NCERT Class 7 History (Our Pasts II)

16. The Mughal emperor who was called 'Zinda Pir' (living saint) was:
(a) Jahangir (b) Shah Jahan (c) Bahadur Shah I (d) Aurangzeb
Answer: (d)
Solution: Jahangir is known as a lover of justice (chain of justice), which is a tempting but wrong association. Aurangzeb earned the title 'Zinda Pir' for his austere, orthodox personal life. So the answer is Aurangzeb.
Source: NCERT Class 7 History (Our Pasts II)

17. The Vijayanagara Empire was founded in 1336 by:
(a) Krishnadevaraya and Achyutaraya (b) Harihara and Bukka (c) Devaraya I and Devaraya II (d) Narasimha and Tirumala
Answer: (b)
Solution: Krishnadevaraya was the empire's greatest ruler (Tuluva dynasty), not its founder — the most common trap. The Sangama brothers Harihara and Bukka founded Vijayanagara in 1336. So the answer is Harihara and Bukka.
Source: NCERT Class 7 History (Our Pasts II)

18. The Battle of Talikota (1565) led to the decline of which empire?
(a) Vijayanagara Empire (b) Bahmani Sultanate (c) Mughal Empire (d) Maratha Empire
Answer: (a)
Solution: The Bahmani Sultanate had already fragmented into the Deccan Sultanates, which is the tempting confusion. At Talikota (Rakshasa-Tangadi), the combined Deccan Sultanates defeated Vijayanagara, killing its regent Aliya Rama Raya. So the answer is the Vijayanagara Empire.
Source: NCERT Class 7 History (Our Pasts II)

19. Guru Nanak, the founder of Sikhism, was born at:
(a) Amritsar (b) Kartarpur (c) Talwandi (d) Anandpur Sahib
Answer: (c)
Solution: Kartarpur is where Guru Nanak spent his final years and Amritsar was founded by Guru Ram Das — both are traps. Guru Nanak was born in 1469 at Talwandi (now Nankana Sahib, Pakistan). So the answer is Talwandi.
Source: NCERT Class 7 History (Our Pasts II)

20. Shivaji was crowned (Rajyabhishek) at Raigad in:
(a) 1665 (b) 1680 (c) 1674 (d) 1689
Answer: (c)
Solution: 1680 is tempting because it is the year of Shivaji's death — a classic date-swap trap. His formal coronation as Chhatrapati took place on 6 June 1674 at Raigad. So the answer is 1674.
Source: NCERT Class 7 History (Our Pasts II)

21. Consider the following statements about the Iqta system:
1. Iqtadars were given land grants in lieu of salary.
2. Iltutmish regularised the iqta system as the backbone of Sultanate administration.
3. Iqtas were hereditary from the time of their introduction.
Which of the statements given above is/are correct?
(a) 1 and 3 only (b) 2 and 3 only (c) 1 and 2 only (d) 1, 2 and 3
Answer: (c)
Solution: Statement 3 is the trap: early iqtas were transferable assignments of revenue, not hereditary property; hereditary claims grew only later under weaker rulers like Firoz Shah Tughlaq. Statements 1 and 2 correctly describe the system consolidated under Iltutmish. So the answer is 1 and 2 only.
Source: PYQ-style

22. Arrange the following travellers in the correct chronological order of their visit to India:
1. Ibn Battuta
2. Al-Biruni
3. Marco Polo
4. Nicolo Conti
(a) 3, 2, 1, 4 (b) 2, 1, 3, 4 (c) 2, 3, 1, 4 (d) 2, 3, 4, 1
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is placing Marco Polo after Ibn Battuta; Polo visited south India around 1292–93, while Ibn Battuta came in 1333 under Muhammad bin Tughlaq. Al-Biruni came with Mahmud of Ghazni's era (11th century), and Conti visited Vijayanagara in the 15th century. So the answer is 2, 3, 1, 4.
Source: PYQ-style

23. Consider the following statements about Akbar's Mansabdari system:
1. Every mansabdar had a zat rank and a sawar rank.
2. The zat rank determined the number of cavalrymen the mansabdar maintained.
3. Mansabdars were paid through jagirs (revenue assignments).
Which of the statements given above is/are correct?
(a) 1 and 2 only (b) 1 and 3 only (c) 2 and 3 only (d) 1, 2 and 3
Answer: (b)
Solution: Statement 2 is the almost-correct trap: it is the sawar rank, not the zat rank, that fixed the number of horsemen to be maintained; zat fixed personal status and salary. Statements 1 and 3 are correct. So the answer is 1 and 3 only.
Source: NCERT Class 11 History (Themes in Indian History II)

24. Match the following battles with their years:
1. Battle of Tarain (Second) — 1192
2. Battle of Chandawar — 1194
3. Battle of Panipat (First) — 1526
4. Battle of Haldighati — 1576
Which of the above are correctly matched?
(a) 1 and 2 only (b) 1, 2 and 3 only (c) 2, 3 and 4 only (d) 1, 2, 3 and 4
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap lies in confusing the First Battle of Tarain (1191), which Prithviraj Chauhan won, with the Second (1192), which Muhammad Ghori won; Chandawar followed in 1194 against Jaichandra. Panipat 1526 and Haldighati 1576 (Akbar's forces vs Maharana Pratap) are also correctly paired. So the answer is 1, 2, 3 and 4.
Source: PYQ-style

25. Consider the following statements about the Bhakti and Sufi movements:
1. The Chishti silsila was the most influential Sufi order in India.
2. Shaikh Nizamuddin Auliya was a contemporary of Alauddin Khalji in Delhi.
3. The Chishtis accepted royal service and land grants from the Sultans.
Which of the statements given above is/are correct?
(a) 2 and 3 only (b) 1 and 2 only (c) 1 and 3 only (d) 1, 2 and 3
Answer: (b)
Solution: Statement 3 is the trap: the Chishtis deliberately kept aloof from the state, refusing court service and grants, unlike the Suhrawardis who accepted them. Nizamuddin Auliya (1238–1325) indeed lived in Delhi during Alauddin Khalji's reign, and the Chishti order was the most popular in India. So the answer is 1 and 2 only.
Source: NCERT Class 11 History (Themes in Indian History II)
