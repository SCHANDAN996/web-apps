# 🪄 Memory Hooks & Short Tricks — Indian Economy (Basic)

---

**Hook 1 — Sectors of the Economy**
> **"Papa Sells Toys"** → **P**rimary, **S**econdary, **T**ertiary
> Papa (Primary = farming) Sells (Secondary = industry that processes what is grown) Toys (Tertiary = services like shops and transport).
> Order check: raw → factory → service.

---

**Hook 2 — National Income: GDP vs GNP**
> **"GNP = GDP + NR"** — GNP is GDP plus **N**et factor income from abroad (**R**est of the world).
> Trick: **G**NP has an extra letter, so it has an extra term. "NRI money" is the difference.

---

**Hook 3 — NNP = GNP − Depreciation**
> **"Net means the machine got old."**
> Whenever you see **N**et, subtract **D**epreciation (wear & tear of machines).
> NNP = GNP − Depreciation. Net National = "after the machines aged."

---

**Hook 4 — Base Year of GDP (India)**
> **"2011-12: The Eleven-Twelve Economy."**
> As of 2024, India's GDP base year is **2011–12**. Remember: "India's economy stands on 11-12" — like two legs, 11 and 12.

---

**Hook 5 — Who Estimates National Income?**
> **"CSO Counts, NSO Speaks."**
> **CSO** (Central Statistics Office) calculates national income; since 2019, CSO + NSSO merged into **NSO** (National Statistical Office).
> Trick: **S**tatistics **O**ffice — the office that does the statistics.

---

**Hook 6 — Inflation Types by Speed**
> **"Creeping cat, Walking man, Running horse, Galloping cheetah — then Hyper, the storm."**
> Correct order of speed: **Creeping → Walking → Running → Galloping → Hyperinflation**.
> Creeping is the slowest (about 2–3% a year). **Galloping** inflation is very fast (often double or triple digit) but still a separate stage; **Hyperinflation** is the final, out-of-control stage where prices rise extremely rapidly.
> If the question asks "which is fastest/most severe?" — the answer is **Hyperinflation**, not galloping. Galloping is the cheetah; hyperinflation is the storm that follows.

---

**Hook 7 — Demand-Pull vs Cost-Push Inflation**
> **"Pull = People, Push = Producers."**
> Demand-**Pull**: too much money chasing too few goods — **people** pulling prices up.
> Cost-**Push**: producers' costs (wages, oil, raw material) **push** prices up.

---

**Hook 8 — RBI's Tools (Quantitative)**
> **"CRR SLR Repo Bank — RBI's Four Pillars Stand."**
> **C**RR (cash with RBI), **S**LR (liquid assets with itself), **R**epo Rate (RBI lends to banks), **B**ank Rate.
> Reverse Repo = banks park money **with** RBI. "Reverse = banks give, Repo = banks take."

---

**Hook 9 — Repo vs Reverse Repo (never confuse again)**
> **"REpo = REceive loan."** Banks **receive** loans from RBI at the **Repo** rate.
> Reverse Repo = RBI receives deposits from banks. The word "Reverse" flips the direction.

---

**Hook 10 — Fiscal Policy vs Monetary Policy**
> **"FM taxes, RBI rates."**
> **F**iscal = **F**inance Ministry (budget, tax, spending).
> **M**onetary = **M**int Street = RBI (interest rates, money supply).
> Exam trap: CRR/Repo questions are NEVER fiscal policy.

---

**Hook 11 — Direct vs Indirect Tax**
> **"Direct hits your Door, Indirect hides in the Item."**
> Direct tax (income tax) — paid straight from your pocket, can't be shifted.
> Indirect tax (GST) — hidden in the price of the item, burden shifts to the buyer.

---

**Hook 12 — GST Slabs**
> **"5-12-18-28: India's GST Elevator."**
> Four main slabs: **5%, 12%, 18%, 28%** (plus 0% on essentials).
> Picture an elevator with only these four floors. Luxury/sin goods ride to the top floor (28% + cess).

---

**Hook 13 — Poverty Lines (Committees)**
> **"Tendulkar counted calories, Rangarajan revised the ration."**
> **Tendulkar Committee** (2009) — moved beyond just calories to include health & education spending.
> **Rangarajan Committee** (2014) — revised the poverty line upward again.
> Order trick: **T** comes before **R** — Tendulkar first, Rangarajan after.

---

**Hook 14 — Five Year Plans & NITI Aayog**
> **"1951 Plans began, 2015 NITI began."**
> Planning Commission: **1950** (First Plan 1951–56). NITI Aayog replaced it on **1 January 2015**.
> Trick: **NITI** = **N**ational **I**nstitution for **T**ransforming **I**ndia — "Transforming" is the key word examiners test.

---

**Hook 15 — HDI's Three Dimensions**
> **"Long Life, Learning, Living"** — the **3 L's**.
> Health (life expectancy), Education (schooling years), Standard of living (GNI per capita).
> Published by **UNDP** — "UN Develops People."

---

## ⏭️ Skip Strategy — When to Skip a Question

**1. The "Exact Number" Trap:**
If a question asks for a precise figure you only half-remember — e.g., "What was India's exact poverty ratio as per the Tendulkar Committee?" or "What is the current CRR?" — and you can't recall it confidently, **skip immediately**. Numbers that change with every RBI policy or report are the highest-risk guesses. Never burn 60 seconds negotiating with your memory.

**2. The "Committee-Year" Combo:**
Questions pairing a committee with its exact year (Dandekar? Lakdawala? Tendulkar? Rangarajan?) are all-or-nothing. If you remember the committee's *work* but not the *year*, don't guess between two adjacent years — the options are designed so the wrong year "feels" right. Skip and bank the time.

**3. The "Four Statements" Monster:**
If a question gives four statements about monetary tools (CRR, SLR, Repo, Bank Rate) and asks "which are correct," and you're unsure about even **one** statement, the elimination math collapses — you'd be guessing among multiple combos. Mark it, move on, and return only if time remains. One uncertain statement = one skipped question, not one lost mark.

**Golden Rule:** In economy, confusion between two *similar-sounding* terms (GDP/GNP, Repo/Reverse Repo, Fiscal/Monetary) means the examiner has already won half the battle. If the hook doesn't fire within 15 seconds, skip — your mnemonics either know it instantly or they don't.
