Card 1
Front: Which sector contributes the largest share to India's GDP — agriculture, industry, or services?
Back: The services sector (tertiary sector) contributes the largest share, roughly half or more of India's Gross Value Added. Agriculture employs the most people but contributes far less to GDP.

Card 2
Front: Who is called the "Father of Indian Economic Planning"?
Back: M. Visvesvaraya — he wrote "Planned Economy for India" (1934). Note: the Planning Commission itself was set up in 1950 with Nehru as its first chairman.

Card 3
Front: What replaced the Planning Commission on 1 January 2015?
Back: NITI Aayog (National Institution for Transforming India). Its chairperson is the Prime Minister; it has a Vice-Chairperson and a CEO.

Card 4
Front: What is the difference between GDP and GNP in one line?
Back: GDP measures output within India's borders; GNP = GDP + net factor income from abroad (income earned by Indians abroad minus income earned by foreigners in India).

Card 5
Front: Which organisation releases India's official GDP data, and what is its base year (as of 2024)?
Back: The National Statistical Office (NSO), under the Ministry of Statistics and Programme Implementation (MoSPI). Base year: 2011–12.

Card 6
Front: Trick: How do you remember the three sectors of the economy in order?
Back: "A-I-S" → Agriculture (primary), Industry (secondary), Services (tertiary) — remember "All Indians Serve". Primary = extraction from nature, secondary = manufacturing, tertiary = services.

Card 7
Front: Examiner's trap: "National Income = NNP at which price — market price or factor cost?"
Back: National Income = NNP at factor cost (not market price). NNP at market price minus net indirect taxes gives NNP at factor cost. Examiners swap "market price" and "factor cost" to trap you.

Card 8
Front: What is "disguised unemployment" and in which Indian sector is it most common?
Back: A situation where more people are employed than actually needed — removing some workers does not reduce output. Most common in Indian agriculture.

Card 9
Front: Which Five-Year Plan was based on the Harrod-Domar model, and which on the Mahalanobis model?
Back: First Plan (1951–56) → Harrod-Domar model (focus: agriculture). Second Plan (1956–61) → Mahalanobis model (focus: heavy industries).

Card 10
Front: What is the difference between fiscal deficit and revenue deficit?
Back: Fiscal deficit = total expenditure − total receipts excluding borrowings (i.e., total borrowing requirement). Revenue deficit = revenue expenditure − revenue receipts. Fiscal deficit is the broader measure.

Card 11
Front: Trick: How do you remember the hierarchy GDP → NDP → GNP → NNP?
Back: "G minus D = N" — subtract Depreciation to go from Gross to Net; adjust for "income from abroad" to switch between Domestic and National. Mnemonic: "Depreciation makes Gross Nervous (Net)."

Card 12
Front: Examiner's trap: Is the RBI's repo rate the rate at which RBI lends to banks, or borrows from banks?
Back: Repo rate = rate at which RBI lends short-term funds to banks (against securities). Reverse repo = rate at which RBI borrows/absorbs funds from banks. Examiners deliberately swap the two.

Card 13
Front: What does "inflation" measured by CPI differ from WPI in India?
Back: CPI (Consumer Price Index) tracks retail prices paid by consumers and includes services; WPI tracks wholesale prices of goods only (no services). RBI's inflation targeting uses CPI.

Card 14
Front: What is the "poverty line" concept associated with the Tendulkar Committee?
Back: The Tendulkar Committee (2009) revised poverty estimation to include expenditure on food, education, and health, moving beyond the earlier calorie-based norm. (Earlier: Lakdawala Committee; later: Rangarajan Committee, 2014.)

Card 15
Front: What is "stagflation"?
Back: A rare combination of stagnant growth (high unemployment) plus high inflation occurring together — normal policy tools worsen one problem while fixing the other.

Card 16
Front: What was the main thrust of the 1991 New Economic Policy?
Back: Liberalisation, Privatisation, and Globalisation (LPG) — ending the Licence Raj, opening to foreign investment, and reducing state control, triggered by the 1991 balance-of-payments crisis.

Card 17
Front: What is the difference between CRR and SLR?
Back: CRR (Cash Reserve Ratio): share of deposits banks must keep with the RBI in cash. SLR (Statutory Liquidity Ratio): share banks must keep with themselves in liquid assets (cash, gold, approved securities).

Card 18
Front: What is "per capita income" and how is it calculated?
Back: Per capita income = National Income ÷ total population. It is an average and does not show income inequality — a common examiner trap in statement-based questions.

Card 19
Front: Which index measures depreciation-adjusted national output, and why is it preferred for growth comparisons?
Back: NDP/NNP (Net Domestic/Net National Product) — because it subtracts depreciation (consumption of fixed capital), giving a truer picture of sustainable output than gross measures.

Card 20
Front: What is "mixed economy" in the Indian context?
Back: Coexistence of public and private sectors — adopted via the Industrial Policy Resolutions (notably 1948 and 1956). India is neither fully capitalist nor fully socialist in economic structure.
