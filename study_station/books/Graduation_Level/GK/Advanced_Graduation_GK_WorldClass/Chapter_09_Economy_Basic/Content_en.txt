# Indian Economy (Basic)

## The Hook: The Night India Almost Ran Out of Money

In June 1991, India had foreign exchange reserves to pay for barely **two weeks of imports**. The government had to secretly airlift **67 tonnes of gold** to London (Bank of England) and Tokyo (Bank of Japan) as collateral for an emergency loan. That humiliation forced India to open its economy on 24 July 1991 — the day the modern Indian economy was born. Every question on "LPG reforms" in your exam traces back to that single night.

If you understand *why* 1991 happened, half of Indian Economy becomes a story instead of a list.

## 1. Types of Economies

An economy answers three questions: **what to produce, how to produce, for whom to produce**. Based on who answers them:

| Type | Decisions taken by | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Capitalist (Market) | Private players, price mechanism | USA |
| Socialist (Command) | The State | Former USSR, North Korea |
| Mixed | Both — market with government regulation | **India** |

India chose the **mixed economy** model at Independence — private enterprise allowed, but the State controls key sectors and corrects market failures.

🧠 **Active Recall:** Why is India called a mixed economy and not a socialist economy, even though the word "Socialist" is in our Preamble? (Hint: the 42nd Amendment added the word, but private property and markets were never abolished.)

## 2. Sectors of the Economy

- **Primary sector** — directly uses natural resources: agriculture, fishing, mining, forestry.
- **Secondary sector** — transforms raw material: manufacturing, construction.
- **Tertiary sector** — services: banking, IT, transport, education, trade.

**The Indian paradox:** Agriculture employs the largest share of workers (~45%) but contributes the smallest share to GDP (~18%), while services contribute the largest share (~55%) of GDP. Examiners love reversing these two figures — read every statement twice.

A related classification:
- **Organised vs Unorganised** sector (registered, regulated vs informal).
- **Public vs Private** sector (ownership-based).

🧠 **Active Recall:** A fisherman, a biscuit factory, and a bank — place each in its sector. Now: which sector contributes most to India's GDP, and which employs most people?

## 3. National Income: GDP, GNP and Friends

This is the highest-yield block of the chapter.

- **GDP (Gross Domestic Product):** total value of final goods and services produced **within India's borders** in a year — regardless of who produces it (an Indian farmer or a foreign company in Pune both count).
- **GNP (Gross National Product):** GDP + **Net Factor Income from Abroad (NFIA)** — income earned by Indians abroad minus income earned by foreigners in India.
  - **GNP = GDP + NFIA**
- **NNP = GNP − Depreciation** (wear and tear of capital, also called "consumption of fixed capital").
- **Per Capita Income = National Income ÷ Population.**

**Base vs current prices:** GDP at *constant (base-year) prices* removes inflation → "real GDP". GDP at *current prices* includes inflation → "nominal GDP".

**Who calculates?** The **National Statistical Office (NSO)**, under the **Ministry of Statistics and Programme Implementation (MoSPI)**. The current base year for national accounts is **2011–12**.

**Memory Hook 1 — "GDP is Domestic, GNP is National":**
> **D**omestic = within the **D**esh (country's borders). **N**ational = by the **N**ation's people, wherever they are.

🧠 **Active Recall:** A Japanese company earns profit from its factory in Chennai. Does it enter India's GDP? India's GNP? (GDP — yes; GNP — no, it is subtracted as factor income paid abroad.)

## 4. Planning in India: From Planning Commission to NITI Aayog

**Chronology (learn it cold — examiners scramble it):**

1. **1934** — M. Visvesvaraya's book *Planned Economy for India* (first attempt at planning ideas).
2. **1938** — **National Planning Committee** set up under Congress President **Subhas Chandra Bose**, with **Jawaharlal Nehru** as its chairman.
3. **1944** — **Bombay Plan** (by industrialists: J.R.D. Tata, G.D. Birla and others).
4. **1950** — **Planning Commission** established by a **Cabinet resolution** (15 March 1950) — an **extra-constitutional, non-statutory** body. PM was its ex-officio chairman.
5. **1951** — First Five-Year Plan launched (based on the **Harrod–Domar model**; focus: agriculture).
6. **1956** — Second Plan (**Mahalanobis model**; focus: heavy industries).
7. **2015** — Planning Commission replaced by **NITI Aayog** (National Institution for Transforming India), from **1 January 2015**, again by Cabinet resolution. PM = Chairperson; has a Vice-Chairperson and CEO. **No power to allocate funds** — it is a think tank promoting **cooperative federalism**.

**Memory Hook 2 — "34-38-44-50-51-56-15":**
> *Visvesvaraya thought (34), Bose-Nehru planned (38), Bombay industrialists dreamt (44), Commission came (50), Plans rolled (51), Mahalanobis industrialised (56), NITI replaced (15).*

🧠 **Active Recall:** Both the Planning Commission and NITI Aayog were created by what instrument? Why does that matter for "constitutional body" trap questions?

## 5. The 1991 LPG Reforms

**Cause:** Balance of Payments crisis — forex reserves down to ~2 weeks of imports; gold pledged abroad; IMF conditional loan.

**The response (24 July 1991, Budget by Dr. Manmohan Singh, PM P.V. Narasimha Rao):**

- **L — Liberalisation:** end of "Licence Raj" — industrial licensing abolished for most industries.
- **P — Privatisation:** disinvestment in PSUs, greater private sector role.
- **G — Globalisation:** rupee devalued, trade opened, FDI welcomed, import duties cut.

**Memory Hook 3 — "LPG cylinder of 1991":**
> Just as an LPG cylinder replaced the smoky chulha, **L**iberalisation, **P**rivatisation, **G**lobalisation replaced the smoky Licence Raj.

**New term:** The post-1991 economy is often called the "New Economic Policy" (NEP).

🧠 **Active Recall:** Which of L, P, G deals with ending industrial licensing? Which with disinvestment? Which with FDI?

## 6. Fiscal Policy vs Monetary Policy

| | Fiscal Policy | Monetary Policy |
|---|---|---|
| Made by | Government (Finance Ministry) | **RBI** |
| Tools | Taxation, government spending, borrowing, deficit | Repo rate, reverse repo, CRR, SLR, OMO |
| Annual event | **Union Budget** (1 February) | Bi-monthly **Monetary Policy Committee (MPC)** review |
| Target | Growth, welfare, redistribution | **Inflation control** (CPI target: 4% ± 2%) |

**Key rates (concepts, not current values — values change, so learn what each means):**
- **Repo rate:** rate at which RBI lends to banks. ↑ repo = costlier loans = less money = inflation cools.
- **Reverse repo:** rate at which RBI borrows from banks.
- **CRR (Cash Reserve Ratio):** % of deposits banks must keep with RBI (no interest).
- **SLR (Statutory Liquidity Ratio):** % banks must keep in liquid assets (gold, govt securities) with themselves.

**MPC:** 6 members (3 RBI + 3 government nominees), headed by the RBI Governor; set up in **2016** under the amended RBI Act, 1934.

🧠 **Active Recall:** Inflation is rising. Should RBI raise or cut the repo rate? Which body — Finance Ministry or MPC — takes that call?

## 7. Inflation and Its Types

**Inflation** = sustained rise in the general price level; measured mainly by **CPI (Consumer Price Index)** and **WPI (Wholesale Price Index)**. WPI is released by the **Office of the Economic Adviser, DPIIT (Ministry of Commerce)**; CPI by NSO (MoSPI).

Types by cause:
- **Demand-pull inflation:** too much money chasing too few goods.
- **Cost-push inflation:** rising input costs (oil, wages) push prices up.
- **Stagflation:** stagnation + inflation — high prices *with* high unemployment and slow growth (the worst combination).
- **Deflation:** sustained fall in prices.
- **Disinflation:** fall in the *rate* of inflation (prices still rising, but slower) — **do not confuse with deflation!**

**Memory Hook 4 — "Dis-inflation ≠ De-flation":**
> **Dis**inflation = speed **dis**tal gaya (slower rise). **De**flation = prices actually **de**scend.

🧠 **Active Recall:** Prices rose 8% last year and 5% this year. Is this deflation or disinflation?

## 8. Poverty, Unemployment and Inequality (Core Terms)

- **Poverty line history:** Dadabhai Naoroji (early estimate) → **Dandekar & Rath (1971)** → **Alagh Committee (1979)** → **Lakdawala Committee (1993)** → **Tendulkar Committee (2009)** → **Rangarajan Committee (2014)**. The Tendulkar methodology was the last officially adopted one.
- **Unemployment types:** disguised (agriculture — more workers than needed), seasonal, structural, frictional, cyclical.
- **Inequality measures:** **Gini coefficient** (0 = perfect equality, 1 = perfect inequality); **Lorenz curve** is its graphical form.
- **HDI:** published by **UNDP** — combines health (life expectancy), education, and income (GNI per capita).

---

## 9. Banking & Budget Basics

- **RBI:** established **1 April 1935** (RBI Act, 1934), on the recommendation of the **Hilton Young Commission (1926)**; nationalised in **1949**; HQ — **Mumbai**.
- **Nationalisation of banks:** **14 banks in 1969**, **6 more in 1980**.
- **Budget (Article 112):** the "Annual Financial Statement". Presented on **1 February** (since 2017; earlier it was the last working day of February). Railway Budget merged with the Union Budget in **2017**.
- **Fiscal deficit** = total expenditure − total receipts (excluding borrowings). **Revenue deficit** = revenue expenditure − revenue receipts.

## ⚠️ Caution: Examiner's Trap

**Trap 1 — "लगभग सही कथन" (Almost-true statement):**
*"Agriculture contributes the largest share to India's GDP."* Sounds plausible because most Indians farm — but services contribute the largest share (~55%); agriculture is largest only in **employment**. The examiner swaps "employment" and "GDP contribution".

**Trap 2 — "कालानुक्रमिक भ्रम" (Chronological confusion):**
Options like *"NITI Aayog was established in 2014"* or *"Bank nationalisation happened in 1980 (14 banks)"*. Correct: NITI Aayog — **1 January 2015**; 1969 — **14 banks**, 1980 — **6 banks**. Dates and counts are deliberately shuffled.

**Trap 3 — "विपरीत कार्य-कारण" (Reverse causation):**
*"RBI reduces the repo rate to control inflation."* Reversed! To control inflation, RBI **raises** the repo rate (dear money policy). Cutting repo stimulates growth but can fuel inflation.

**Trap 4 — "समान नाम" (Similar names):**
**Disinflation vs Deflation**; **GDP vs GNP**; **Planning Commission vs National Planning Committee (1938)**. One word changes the entire answer.
