# 📊 PYQ Analysis — Indian Economy (National Income & GDP)

## 1. How Exams Ask Questions from This Chapter

Questions from National Income and GDP appear in almost every SSC, Railway, Banking and State exam. The pattern is predictable:

- **Definition-based questions**: What is GDP, GNP, NNP, NDP? Which one includes net factor income from abroad? Which one deducts depreciation?
- **Method-based questions**: The three methods of measuring national income — Product (Value Added) Method, Income Method, and Expenditure Method. Exams often ask which method adds up factor payments (Income Method) or which avoids double counting (Value Added Method).
- **Institution-based questions**: Who estimates national income in India? (National Statistical Office, NSO, under the Ministry of Statistics and Programme Implementation — earlier the Central Statistical Organisation, CSO.)
- **Base year questions**: The current base year for national income estimates in India is 2011–12. Exams love asking this directly.
- **Concept-difference questions**: GDP vs GNP, market price vs factor cost, nominal vs real GDP, GDP deflator.
- **Statement-based questions** (especially in SSC CGL and Bank exams): Two or three statements about GDP/GNP are given and you must pick the correct combination.

## 2. The Examiner's Trap Types

1. **Almost Correct but Wrong Option**: An option says "GNP = GDP + Depreciation" — looks plausible, but the correct relation is GNP = GDP + Net Factor Income from Abroad (NFIA). Depreciation converts Gross to Net, not Domestic to National.
2. **Timeline Confusion**: Mixing up old and new base years (2004–05 vs 2011–12), or CSO vs NSO (NSO was formed in 2019 by merging CSO and NSSO).
3. **Similar-Sounding Terms**: National Income vs Per Capita Income; GDP at market price vs GDP at factor cost; Nominal GDP vs Real GDP. The examiner swaps one word to make a wrong statement.
4. **Negative Statements**: "Which of the following is NOT included in GDP?" — students misread and pick an item that IS included. Read the NOT carefully.
5. **Transfer Payment Trap**: Pensions, scholarships, and unemployment allowances are transfer payments and are NOT counted in national income. This is a favourite trap.

## 3. The 15-Second / 45-Second Rule

- **15-second questions**: Direct definitions — "What is the base year of national income estimates?" or "GNP = GDP + ?" Answer instantly from memory.
- **45-second questions**: Statement-based or calculation-type questions (e.g., given GDP at market price and indirect taxes/subsidies, find GDP at factor cost). Read all statements, eliminate the clearly wrong ones, then verify the remaining ones. Never spend more than 45 seconds — mark and move on.

## 4. Top 10 High-Yield PYQ-Style Questions with Solutions

**Q1. Which of the following is included in the calculation of GDP of a country?**
(a) Sale of an old house
(b) Pension paid to retired government employees
(c) Services of a doctor in a government hospital
(d) Value of vegetables grown for self-consumption at home

**Answer: (c)**
**Solution:** GDP includes the value of currently produced goods and services. A doctor's service is a current productive service, so it counts. Sale of an old house is not current production (only the broker's commission would count). Pension is a transfer payment — excluded. Self-consumed produce is generally not part of market GDP estimates.
**Source: PYQ-style**

**Q2. GNP is equal to:**
(a) GDP + Depreciation
(b) GDP + Net Factor Income from Abroad
(c) GDP − Indirect Taxes
(d) NNP + Subsidies

**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** GNP = GDP + Net Factor Income from Abroad (NFIA). Depreciation converts Gross to Net, not Domestic to National — option (a) is the classic trap.
**Source: PYQ-style**

**Q3. National Income in India is estimated by:**
(a) Reserve Bank of India
(b) NITI Aayog
(c) National Statistical Office (NSO)
(d) Ministry of Finance

**Answer: (c)**
**Solution:** The National Statistical Office (NSO), under the Ministry of Statistics and Programme Implementation, releases national income estimates. It was formed in 2019 by merging CSO and NSSO. RBI and NITI Aayog do not estimate national income.
**Source: PYQ-style**

**Q4. The base year currently used for calculating national income in India is:**
(a) 2004–05
(b) 2011–12
(c) 2014–15
(d) 2017–18

**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** The current base year for national income and GDP estimates in India is 2011–12. Option (a) 2004–05 was the earlier base year — a common trap.
**Source: PYQ-style**

**Q5. Which method of measuring national income adds up wages, rent, interest and profit?**
(a) Product Method
(b) Income Method
(c) Expenditure Method
(d) Value Added Method

**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** The Income Method sums all factor incomes — wages (labour), rent (land), interest (capital) and profit (entrepreneurship). The Product/Value Added Method sums output value; the Expenditure Method sums C + I + G + (X − M).
**Source: PYQ-style**

**Q6. Which of the following is a transfer payment?**
(a) Salary of a school teacher
(b) Old-age pension
(c) Payment to a factory worker
(d) Fees of a lawyer

**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** Old-age pension is a transfer payment — money given without any current productive service in return — so it is excluded from national income. Salary, wages and fees are payments for services rendered.
**Source: PYQ-style**

**Q7. NNP at factor cost is also known as:**
(a) Gross Domestic Product
(b) National Income
(c) Personal Income
(d) Disposable Income

**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** National Income is defined as NNP at factor cost. Personal Income and Disposable Income are different concepts relating to household receipts.
**Source: PYQ-style**

**Q8. Real GDP is GDP measured at:**
(a) Current year prices
(b) Constant (base year) prices
(c) Market prices of the previous year
(d) International prices

**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** Real GDP is measured at constant (base year) prices, removing the effect of inflation. GDP at current prices is Nominal GDP — option (a) is the trap.
**Source: PYQ-style**

**Q9. GDP at market price minus indirect taxes plus subsidies equals:**
(a) GDP at factor cost
(b) GNP at market price
(c) NDP at market price
(d) Personal Income

**Answer: (a)**
**Solution:** GDP at factor cost = GDP at market price − Indirect Taxes + Subsidies. This conversion between market price and factor cost is frequently tested.
**Source: PYQ-style**

**Q10. Double counting in national income estimation is avoided by using:**
(a) The Income Method
(b) The Value Added Method
(c) The Expenditure Method
(d) The Transfer Method

**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** The Value Added Method counts only the value added at each stage of production, avoiding double counting of intermediate goods. There is no "Transfer Method" — option (d) is a distractor.
**Source: PYQ-style**

## 5. Revision Takeaways from the Pattern

- Memorise the core identities: **GNP = GDP + NFIA**, **Net = Gross − Depreciation**, **Factor Cost = Market Price − Indirect Taxes + Subsidies**, **National Income = NNP at factor cost**.
- Remember the three methods of measuring national income and what each one adds up.
- Fix the institutional facts: **NSO** estimates national income; base year **2011–12**.
- Transfer payments (pension, scholarship, subsidy to individuals) are **excluded** from national income — this trap appears repeatedly.
- In negative questions ("NOT included"), underline the NOT before answering.
- For statement-based questions, eliminate options containing even one false statement — never verify all statements one by one if elimination is faster.
