26. Which of the following best describes a "mixed economy"?
(a) An economy where both public and private sectors coexist (b) An economy where only the private sector operates (c) An economy fully controlled by the government (d) An economy based only on agriculture
Answer: (a)
Solution: A tempting wrong choice is the fully government-controlled economy, which describes a socialist/command economy, not a mixed one. India follows a mixed economy where public and private sectors operate side by side, so the answer is an economy where both public and private sectors coexist.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Indian Economic Development

27. The National Income of India is estimated and published by which body?
(a) Reserve Bank of India (b) NITI Aayog (c) National Statistical Office (NSO), Ministry of Statistics and Programme Implementation (d) Ministry of Finance
Answer: (c)
Solution: Many candidates pick the Ministry of Finance because it presents the Budget and Economic Survey, but it does not compute national income. National income estimates are released by the National Statistical Office under MoSPI, so the answer is the National Statistical Office (NSO).
Source: NCERT Class 12 Macroeconomics

28. Which sector of the Indian economy is also called the "service sector"?
(a) Primary sector (b) Tertiary sector (c) Secondary sector (d) Quaternary mining sector
Answer: (b)
Solution: The primary sector (agriculture) is a common trap because it employs the most people, but employment share is not the same as the service classification. Services such as banking, transport and trade form the tertiary sector, so the answer is the tertiary sector.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Economics (Sectors of the Indian Economy)

29. GDP at market price minus net indirect taxes gives:
(a) GNP at factor cost (b) Per capita income (c) NDP at market price (d) GDP at factor cost
Answer: (d)
Solution: A frequent confusion is with NDP, which requires subtracting depreciation, not indirect taxes. Market price includes indirect taxes and excludes subsidies; removing net indirect taxes converts market price to factor cost, so the answer is GDP at factor cost.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Macroeconomics

30. Which of the following is NOT included in the calculation of GDP of India?
(a) Salary of a school teacher in Delhi (b) Output of a textile factory in Surat (c) Services of a housewife in her own home (d) Fees earned by a doctor in Mumbai
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is to think all productive work counts; however, GDP counts only market-valued production. Unpaid household services are non-market activities and are excluded from GDP, so the answer is services of a housewife in her own home.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Macroeconomics

31. The New Economic Policy of liberalisation, privatisation and globalisation was launched in India in:
(a) 1991 (b) 1985 (c) 1980 (d) 2001
Answer: (a)
Solution: Chronological confusion is common here: 1980 and 1985 saw only limited piecemeal reforms, not the structural shift. The comprehensive LPG reforms came with the July 1991 crisis-response package under P. V. Narasimha Rao and Manmohan Singh, so the answer is 1991.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Indian Economic Development

32. Which Five-Year Plan is known as the "Mahalanobis Plan" for its focus on heavy industries?
(a) First Five-Year Plan (b) Third Five-Year Plan (c) Second Five-Year Plan (d) Fourth Five-Year Plan
Answer: (c)
Solution: The First Plan is the trap because it came first chronologically, but it was based on the Harrod-Domar model and focused on agriculture. The Second Plan (1956–61) used P. C. Mahalanobis's model emphasising heavy and basic industries, so the answer is the Second Five-Year Plan.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Indian Economic Development

33. NITI Aayog replaced the Planning Commission on:
(a) 1 January 2015 (b) 26 January 2015 (c) 15 August 2014 (d) 1 April 2015
Answer: (a)
Solution: 26 January is tempting because of Republic Day, and 15 August 2014 is when the PM announced the intention from the Red Fort — but announcement is not formation. NITI Aayog was constituted by a Cabinet resolution on 1 January 2015, so the answer is 1 January 2015.

34. In India, the "poverty line" estimation was historically based on:
(a) Minimum wages fixed by states (b) Income tax exemption limits (c) Calorie intake norms (d) Per capita GDP
Answer: (c)
Solution: Per capita GDP is a trap because it measures average income, not poverty thresholds. Indian poverty estimation (from the 1970s task force onwards) used calorie norms — 2400 kcal rural and 2100 kcal urban per day — converted into expenditure, so the answer is calorie intake norms.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Indian Economic Development

35. Which committee recommended the shift to a consumption-expenditure based poverty estimation using the Mixed Reference Period?
(a) Tendulkar Committee (b) Lakdawala Committee (c) Rangarajan Committee (d) Kelkar Committee
Answer: (a)
Solution: The Rangarajan Committee is the near-correct trap — it came later (2012) and revised the methodology further, but the Mixed Reference Period shift was introduced by the Suresh Tendulkar Committee (2009), so the answer is the Tendulkar Committee.
Source: PYQ-style

36. "Disguised unemployment" is most commonly found in India in:
(a) The IT sector (b) The railways (c) The banking sector (d) The agriculture sector
Answer: (d)
Solution: Organised sectors like banking are traps because they have visible unemployment issues, but disguised unemployment means more workers than needed with zero marginal product. This is characteristic of Indian family farming, so the answer is the agriculture sector.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Economics

37. Which of the following is a direct tax?
(a) Goods and Services Tax (b) Corporation tax (c) Customs duty (d) Excise duty
Answer: (b)
Solution: GST is the tempting wrong option because it is the most discussed tax, but it is an indirect tax on consumption. Corporation tax is levied directly on company profits and cannot be shifted, so the answer is corporation tax.

38. The Goods and Services Tax (GST) was implemented across India from:
(a) 1 July 2017 (b) 1 April 2016 (c) 1 January 2018 (d) 1 April 2017
Answer: (a)
Solution: 1 April is a trap because financial years begin then and the Constitutional Amendment came in 2016 — but the rollout was deferred. GST was launched at midnight on 1 July 2017, so the answer is 1 July 2017.

39. Under GST, the tax on inter-state supply of goods is called:
(a) CGST (b) IGST (c) SGST (d) UTGST
Answer: (b)
Solution: CGST and SGST are traps because they apply together — but only to intra-state supplies. Integrated GST (IGST) is levied by the Centre on inter-state supplies and imports, so the answer is IGST.

40. The Reserve Bank of India was nationalised in:
(a) 1935 (b) 1947 (c) 1950 (d) 1949
Answer: (d)
Solution: 1935 is the classic trap — it is the year RBI was established (as a private shareholders' bank), not nationalised. RBI was nationalised on 1 January 1949, so the answer is 1949.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Macroeconomics

41. Which of the following is a qualitative (selective) credit control measure of the RBI?
(a) Repo rate (b) Cash Reserve Ratio (c) Open Market Operations (d) Margin requirements
Answer: (d)
Solution: Repo rate and CRR are traps because they are the most famous RBI tools, but they are quantitative measures affecting the overall volume of credit. Margin requirements selectively control credit for specific purposes, so the answer is margin requirements.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Macroeconomics

42. The minimum reserve system of note issue in India requires the RBI to maintain gold and foreign securities worth at least:
(a) ₹100 crore (b) ₹200 crore (c) ₹500 crore (d) ₹1000 crore
Answer: (b)
Solution: ₹100 crore is tempting as a round figure, but the statutory floor is higher. Under the minimum reserve system (adopted 1956), RBI must hold ₹200 crore in gold and foreign securities (of which gold at least ₹115 crore), so the answer is ₹200 crore.
Source: PYQ-style

43. Which bank is known as the "bankers' bank" in India?
(a) State Bank of India (b) EXIM Bank (c) NABARD (d) Reserve Bank of India
Answer: (d)
Solution: SBI is the trap because it is the largest commercial bank, but size does not make it the bankers' bank. The RBI keeps the cash reserves of scheduled banks and provides them refinancing, so the answer is the Reserve Bank of India.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Macroeconomics

44. The Fiscal Responsibility and Budget Management (FRBM) Act was passed in:
(a) 2003 (b) 2000 (c) 2005 (d) 2008
Answer: (a)
Solution: 2005 is the trap because the FRBM rules and targets became operational around then, but the Act itself was enacted in 2003 to institutionalise fiscal discipline, so the answer is 2003.

45. "Revenue deficit" in the Union Budget means:
(a) Revenue expenditure minus revenue receipts (b) Total expenditure minus total receipts (c) Fiscal deficit minus interest payments (d) Capital expenditure minus capital receipts
Answer: (a)
Solution: Total expenditure minus total receipts is the trap — that is the fiscal deficit, not the revenue deficit. Revenue deficit measures the excess of revenue expenditure over revenue receipts, so the answer is revenue expenditure minus revenue receipts.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Macroeconomics

46. Which of the following is a capital receipt of the Government of India?
(a) Income tax collection (b) Disinvestment proceeds from selling PSU shares (c) GST collection (d) Interest received on loans
Answer: (b)
Solution: Interest received is the trap — it is a non-tax receipt but still a revenue receipt because it neither creates liability nor reduces assets. Disinvestment reduces government assets, making it a capital receipt, so the answer is disinvestment proceeds from selling PSU shares.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Macroeconomics

47. The term "stagflation" refers to a situation of:
(a) High growth with high inflation (b) Falling prices with rising output (c) Stagnant growth combined with high inflation and unemployment (d) High employment with low inflation
Answer: (c)
Solution: High growth with high inflation is the trap because both words contain "high", but stagflation is the unusual combination of stagnation plus inflation. It describes stagnant output, high unemployment and rising prices together, so the answer is stagnant growth combined with high inflation and unemployment.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Macroeconomics

48. The base year currently used for calculating India's GDP (new series) is:
(a) 2004–05 (b) 2014–15 (c) 2011–12 (d) 2017–18
Answer: (c)
Solution: 2014–15 is tempting because the new series was released in 2015, but the base year adopted was 2011–12. The CSO (now NSO) revised the GDP series with base year 2011–12, so the answer is 2011–12.

49. Which institution is the apex body for agricultural and rural credit in India?
(a) RBI (b) NABARD (c) SEBI (d) SIDBI
Answer: (b)
Solution: SIDBI is the similar-name trap — it refinances small industries (MSMEs), not agriculture. NABARD, established in 1982, is the apex institution for agricultural and rural development finance, so the answer is NABARD.

50. The "Gini coefficient" is used to measure:
(a) Inflation (b) Foreign exchange reserves (c) Economic growth (d) Income inequality
Answer: (d)
Solution: Economic growth is the trap because Gini is often discussed alongside growth debates, but it does not measure growth. The Gini coefficient ranges from 0 (perfect equality) to 1 (perfect inequality) and measures income or wealth distribution, so the answer is income inequality.
