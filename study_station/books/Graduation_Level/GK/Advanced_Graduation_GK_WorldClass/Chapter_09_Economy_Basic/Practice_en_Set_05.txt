101. Which of the following best describes a "mixed economy"?
(a) An economy where only the private sector operates (b) An economy fully controlled by the government (c) An economy where both public and private sectors coexist (d) An economy based only on agriculture
Answer: (c)
Solution: Options describing only one sector depict pure capitalist or pure socialist systems, which is the trap. A mixed economy combines features of both, with public and private sectors operating side by side, as India adopted after Independence, so the answer is an economy where both public and private sectors coexist.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Indian Economic Development

102. The National Development Council (NDC) was set up in which year?
(a) 1952 (b) 1951 (c) 1955 (d) 1950
Answer: (a)
Solution: The tempting wrong option 1950 is the year the Planning Commission was formed — a classic chronological confusion trap. The NDC was established on 6 August 1952 to secure cooperation of states in planning, so the answer is 1952.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Indian Economic Development

103. Which sector of the Indian economy is also called the "service sector"?
(a) Quaternary sector (b) Tertiary sector (c) Primary sector (d) Secondary sector
Answer: (b)
Solution: The primary sector deals with raw materials and the secondary sector with manufacturing, which are the traps. The tertiary sector provides services such as banking, transport, education and trade, so the answer is the tertiary sector.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Economics

104. The New Economic Policy of 1991 is associated with which three components?
(a) Liberalisation, Nationalisation, Subsidisation (b) Liberalisation, Privatisation, Globalisation (c) Nationalisation, Regulation, Protectionism (d) Demonetisation, Digitisation, Globalisation
Answer: (b)
Solution: Nationalisation is the opposite of privatisation and is the trap for confused candidates. The 1991 reforms under P.V. Narasimha Rao and Manmohan Singh rested on LPG — Liberalisation, Privatisation and Globalisation, so the answer is Liberalisation, Privatisation, Globalisation.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Indian Economic Development

105. Who is known as the "Father of Indian Economic Planning"?
(a) Jawaharlal Nehru (b) Dadabhai Naoroji (c) P.C. Mahalanobis (d) M. Visvesvaraya
Answer: (d)
Solution: P.C. Mahalanobis is the tempting trap — he designed the Second Five-Year Plan model, not the title of father of planning. Sir M. Visvesvaraya earned the title for his 1934 book "Planned Economy for India", so the answer is M. Visvesvaraya.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Indian Economic Development

106. The Second Five-Year Plan (1956–61) was based on which model?
(a) Lewis model (b) Mahalanobis model (c) Harrod-Domar model (d) Gadgil model
Answer: (b)
Solution: The Harrod-Domar model influenced the First Plan, which is the common confusion. The Second Plan emphasised heavy industries and was based on the P.C. Mahalanobis model, so the answer is the Mahalanobis model.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Indian Economic Development

107. The Gadgil formula is associated with which of the following?
(a) Fixation of minimum support prices (b) Setting of interest rates by banks (c) Calculation of the poverty line (d) Distribution of central assistance to states
Answer: (d)
Solution: The poverty-line option is the trap because D.R. Gadgil also wrote on poverty, but the formula bearing his name is about plan assistance. The Gadgil formula (1969) laid down criteria for distributing central plan assistance among states, so the answer is distribution of central assistance to states.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Indian Economic Development

108. The concept of the "poverty line" in India was first estimated on the basis of:
(a) Income alone (b) Calorie intake (c) Land holding (d) Literacy level
Answer: (b)
Solution: Income-based measures came later and are the trap. Early poverty estimation in India (Dandekar-Rath, later the Planning Commission) used calorie intake norms — 2400 calories rural and 2100 calories urban per day, so the answer is calorie intake.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Indian Economic Development

109. The Tendulkar Committee (2009) was constituted to estimate:
(a) Fiscal deficit (b) Poverty (c) Black money (d) Unemployment
Answer: (b)
Solution: It is often confused with committees on black money or fiscal matters, which is the trap. The Suresh Tendulkar Committee (2009) revised the methodology for estimating poverty in India, moving to a broader consumption basket, so the answer is poverty.
Source: PYQ-style

110. Which of the following is NOT a function of the Reserve Bank of India?
(a) Formulating the Union Budget (b) Banker to the government (c) Controller of credit (d) Issuing currency notes
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is assuming all financial functions belong to the central bank. The Union Budget is prepared by the Ministry of Finance, while RBI issues currency, acts as banker to government and controls credit, so the answer is formulating the Union Budget.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Macroeconomics

111. The Reserve Bank of India was nationalised in which year?
(a) 1935 (b) 1947 (c) 1950 (d) 1949
Answer: (d)
Solution: The year 1935 is the classic trap — it is the establishment year, not nationalisation. RBI was nationalised on 1 January 1949, so the answer is 1949.
Source: RBI official website (rbi.org.in)

112. In which year were 14 major commercial banks nationalised in India?
(a) 1991 (b) 1955 (c) 1980 (d) 1969
Answer: (d)
Solution: The year 1980 is the trap — that is when 6 more banks were nationalised. On 19 July 1969, the Indira Gandhi government nationalised 14 banks with deposits above ₹50 crore, so the answer is 1969.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Indian Economic Development

113. The Goods and Services Tax (GST) in India was implemented from:
(a) 1 April 2016 (b) 1 January 2018 (c) 1 April 2017 (d) 1 July 2017
Answer: (d)
Solution: The date 1 April 2017 is a tempting trap as it marks the start of that financial year. GST came into force on 1 July 2017 following the 101st Constitutional Amendment, so the answer is 1 July 2017.
Source: GST Council / CBIC official records

114. Which article of the Constitution was inserted by the 101st Amendment for GST?
(a) Article 246A (b) Article 280 (c) Article 368 (d) Article 312
Answer: (a)
Solution: Article 280 deals with the Finance Commission — a similar-sounding trap. Article 246A gives Parliament and state legislatures concurrent power to make laws on GST, so the answer is Article 246A.
Source: Constitution of India (101st Amendment Act, 2016)

115. The Fiscal Responsibility and Budget Management (FRBM) Act was passed in:
(a) 2000 (b) 2005 (c) 2003 (d) 2008
Answer: (c)
Solution: It is often confused with the year 2000 when the Kelkar Committee recommended it, which is the trap. The FRBM Act was enacted in 2003 to institutionalise fiscal discipline and set deficit targets, so the answer is 2003.
Source: Ministry of Finance, Government of India

116. Which of the following is a direct tax?
(a) GST (b) Income tax (c) Customs duty (d) Excise duty
Answer: (b)
Solution: GST, customs and excise are indirect taxes whose burden passes to consumers — the trap. Income tax is levied directly on the person or entity earning income and its burden cannot be shifted, so the answer is income tax.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Macroeconomics

117. The term "disguised unemployment" is most commonly associated with which sector in India?
(a) Agriculture (b) Information technology (c) Manufacturing (d) Banking
Answer: (a)
Solution: It is sometimes confused with seasonal unemployment, which also occurs in agriculture but is a different concept — the trap. Disguised unemployment, where more people work than needed and marginal productivity is near zero, is typical of Indian agriculture, so the answer is agriculture.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Indian Economic Development

118. Which committee is associated with banking sector reforms in the 1990s?
(a) Narasimham Committee (b) Kelkar Committee (c) Sarkaria Commission (d) Tendulkar Committee
Answer: (a)
Solution: The Sarkaria Commission dealt with Centre-State relations — a name-similarity trap. The Narasimham Committees (1991 and 1998) recommended banking reforms including prudential norms and reduced SLR/CRR, so the answer is the Narasimham Committee.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Indian Economic Development

119. The "Green Revolution" in India primarily began in which decade?
(a) 1950s (b) 1980s (c) 1990s (d) 1960s
Answer: (d)
Solution: The 1950s is the trap, as that decade saw land reforms and the First Plan's agriculture focus, not HYV technology. The Green Revolution began in the mid-1960s with high-yielding variety seeds of wheat and rice in Punjab, Haryana and western UP, so the answer is the 1960s.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Indian Economic Development

120. Which institution replaced the Planning Commission in 2015?
(a) National Development Council (b) NITI Aayog (c) Finance Commission (d) Inter-State Council
Answer: (b)
Solution: The NDC was not the replacement but became defunct alongside, which is the common confusion. NITI Aayog (National Institution for Transforming India) replaced the Planning Commission on 1 January 2015, so the answer is NITI Aayog.
Source: NITI Aayog official website (niti.gov.in)

121. Consider the following statements about the Finance Commission:
1. It is a constitutional body under Article 280.
2. It is constituted every five years by the President.
3. Its recommendations are binding on the government.
Which of the statements given above is/are correct?
(a) 2 and 3 only (b) 1 and 3 only (c) 1 and 2 only (d) 1, 2 and 3
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is statement 3, which sounds plausible but is wrong — the Finance Commission's recommendations are advisory, not binding. Statements 1 and 2 are correct, so the answer is 1 and 2 only.
Source: Constitution of India, Article 280

122. With reference to inflation, "stagflation" refers to a situation of:
(a) High inflation with high growth (b) High inflation with stagnant growth and high unemployment (c) Low inflation with low growth (d) Deflation with high unemployment
Answer: (b)
Solution: The tempting wrong option pairs high inflation with high growth, which is simply demand-pull inflation — the trap. Stagflation combines stagnation (slow growth, high unemployment) with inflation, so the answer is high inflation with stagnant growth and high unemployment.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Macroeconomics

123. Consider the following statements about NITI Aayog:
1. The Prime Minister is its ex-officio Chairperson.
2. It has the power to allocate funds to states.
3. Its full form is National Institution for Transforming India.
Which of the statements given above is/are correct?
(a) 2 and 3 only (b) 1 and 2 only (c) 1 and 3 only (d) 1, 2 and 3
Answer: (c)
Solution: Statement 2 is the trap — unlike the Planning Commission, NITI Aayog does NOT allocate funds; it is a policy think tank. Statements 1 and 3 are correct, so the answer is 1 and 3 only.
Source: NITI Aayog official website (niti.gov.in)

124. The "Hindu rate of growth", a term for India's low GDP growth of around 3.5%, was coined by:
(a) Amartya Sen (b) Jagdish Bhagwati (c) Raj Krishna (d) V.K.R.V. Rao
Answer: (c)
Solution: Amartya Sen is a tempting trap due to his fame in welfare economics. Economist Raj Krishna coined the term "Hindu rate of growth" in 1978 to describe India's slow growth from the 1950s to the 1980s, so the answer is Raj Krishna.
Source: PYQ-style

125. Which of the following correctly matches the Five-Year Plan with its main focus?
(a) First Plan — Agriculture; Second Plan — Heavy industries (b) Second Plan — Agriculture; First Plan — Heavy industries (c) First Plan — Removal of poverty (d) Third Plan — Garibi Hatao
Answer: (a)
Solution: Options reversing the First and Second Plan focus are the classic trap, and Garibi Hatao belongs to the Fifth Plan, not the Third. The First Plan (1951–56) focused on agriculture and the Second Plan (1956–61) on heavy industries, so the answer is First Plan — Agriculture; Second Plan — Heavy industries.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Indian Economic Development
