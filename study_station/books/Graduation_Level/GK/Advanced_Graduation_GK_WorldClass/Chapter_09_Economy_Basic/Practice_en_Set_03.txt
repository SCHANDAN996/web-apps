51. Which of the following best describes a "mixed economy"?
(a) An economy where only the private sector operates (b) An economy where both public and private sectors coexist (c) An economy fully controlled by the government (d) An economy based only on agriculture
Answer: (b)
Solution: A fully government-controlled economy is a socialist/command economy, which is the tempting wrong choice here. India follows a mixed economy where public and private sectors operate side by side, so the answer is an economy where both public and private sectors coexist.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Indian Economic Development

52. The Planning Commission of India was replaced by NITI Aayog in which year?
(a) 2015 (b) 2014 (c) 2016 (d) 2017
Answer: (a)
Solution: The chronological trap is 2014, the year the new government was formed, but NITI Aayog was actually constituted on 1 January 2015 by a Union Cabinet resolution, so the answer is 2015.

53. Who is the ex-officio Chairperson of NITI Aayog?
(a) President of India (b) Prime Minister (c) Finance Minister (d) Vice-Chairperson appointed by RBI
Answer: (b)
Solution: Many candidates pick the Finance Minister because economic bodies are often linked to the finance ministry, but the Prime Minister chairs NITI Aayog, just as he chaired the Planning Commission, so the answer is Prime Minister.

54. Which sector of the Indian economy is also called the "service sector"?
(a) Tertiary sector (b) Secondary sector (c) Primary sector (d) Quaternary sector
Answer: (a)
Solution: The secondary sector (manufacturing) is a common confusion, but services such as banking, transport and trade belong to the tertiary sector, which contributes the largest share to India's GDP, so the answer is tertiary sector.

55. GDP at market price minus net indirect taxes gives:
(a) GNP at market price (b) GDP at factor cost (c) NDP at factor cost (d) National Income
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is GNP, which requires adding net factor income from abroad, not subtracting taxes. Market price minus net indirect taxes (indirect taxes minus subsidies) yields factor cost, so the answer is GDP at factor cost.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Macroeconomics

56. Which of the following is NOT included in the calculation of GDP?
(a) Salary of a government school teacher (b) Value of wheat sold by a farmer in the market (c) Fees earned by a doctor in private practice (d) Household work done by a homemaker
Answer: (d)
Solution: A government teacher's salary is included and tempts candidates to think all services count, but unpaid household work by a homemaker is a non-market activity excluded from GDP, so the answer is household work done by a homemaker.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Macroeconomics

57. The first Five-Year Plan of India was based on which model?
(a) Mahalanobis model (b) Gadgil model (c) Harrod-Domar model (d) Lewis model
Answer: (c)
Solution: The Mahalanobis model is the classic trap because it is famous, but it guided the Second Five-Year Plan; the First Plan (1951–56) was based on the Harrod-Domar model with focus on agriculture, so the answer is Harrod-Domar model.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Indian Economic Development

58. Which Five-Year Plan gave priority to heavy industries?
(a) First Five-Year Plan (b) Sixth Five-Year Plan (c) Fourth Five-Year Plan (d) Second Five-Year Plan
Answer: (d)
Solution: The First Plan is tempting because it came first, but it focused on agriculture and irrigation; the Second Plan (1956–61), based on the Mahalanobis model, prioritised heavy and basic industries, so the answer is Second Five-Year Plan.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Indian Economic Development

59. The New Economic Policy of 1991 in India is associated with which three components?
(a) Demonetisation, Digitisation, Decentralisation (b) Nationalisation, Regulation, Subsidisation (c) Liberalisation, Privatisation, Globalisation (d) Industrialisation, Urbanisation, Modernisation
Answer: (c)
Solution: Nationalisation sounds plausible because it was a major policy of earlier decades, but the 1991 reforms under P.V. Narasimha Rao and Manmohan Singh rested on LPG — Liberalisation, Privatisation and Globalisation, so the answer is Liberalisation, Privatisation, Globalisation.

60. The Reserve Bank of India was nationalised in which year?
(a) 1949 (b) 1947 (c) 1935 (d) 1955
Answer: (a)
Solution: 1935 is the trap — it is the year RBI was established (1 April 1935), not nationalised. RBI was nationalised on 1 January 1949, so the answer is 1949.

(a) 6 (b) 12 (c) 20 (d) 14

Solution: 20 is the trap — it is the total after the second round in 1980 when 6 more banks were nationalised. In July 1969, 14 major commercial banks were nationalised, so the answer is 14.

62. Which of the following is a direct tax?
(a) Goods and Services Tax (b) Corporation tax (c) Customs duty (d) Excise duty
Answer: (b)
Solution: GST is the tempting wrong option because it is the most discussed tax, but it is an indirect tax on consumption. Corporation tax is levied directly on company profits, so the answer is Corporation tax.

(a) 1 April 2016 (b) 1 January 2018 (c) 1 July 2017 (d) 15 August 2017

64. Under GST, the tax on inter-state supply of goods is called:
(a) CGST (b) SGST (c) IGST (d) UTGST

Solution: CGST and SGST together apply to intra-state supplies, which is the confusion point; Integrated GST (IGST) is levied on inter-state supplies and imports, so the answer is IGST.

67. Which of the following measures inflation at the wholesale level in India?

69. The repo rate is the rate at which:
(a) RBI lends to commercial banks (b) Commercial banks lend to each other (c) RBI borrows from commercial banks (d) Government borrows from RBI
Answer: (a)
Solution: The reverse repo rate, at which RBI borrows from banks, is the mirror-image trap. Repo rate is the rate at which RBI lends short-term funds to commercial banks against securities, so the answer is RBI lends to commercial banks.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Macroeconomics

70. Which of the following is NOT a function of the Reserve Bank of India?
(a) Issuer of currency (b) Banker to the government (c) Accepting deposits from the general public (d) Controller of credit
Answer: (c)
Solution: RBI issues currency, acts as banker to government and controls credit, but it does not deal directly with the general public for deposits — that is done by commercial banks, so the answer is accepting deposits from the general public.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Macroeconomics

71. Fiscal deficit is equal to:
(a) Total expenditure minus total revenue receipts (b) Total expenditure minus total receipts excluding borrowings (c) Revenue expenditure minus revenue receipts (d) Total receipts minus total expenditure
Answer: (b)
Solution: Revenue deficit (revenue expenditure minus revenue receipts) is the near-identical trap. Fiscal deficit equals total expenditure minus total receipts excluding borrowings, and it indicates the government's total borrowing requirement, so the answer is total expenditure minus total receipts excluding borrowings.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Macroeconomics

75. The term "Green Revolution" in India is primarily associated with:
(a) Increase in foodgrain production through HYV seeds (b) Increase in milk production (c) Increase in fish production (d) Increase in horticulture output
Answer: (a)
Solution: Milk production (Operation Flood/White Revolution) is the classic similar-name trap. The Green Revolution of the mid-1960s raised foodgrain output through high-yielding variety seeds, fertilisers and irrigation, so the answer is increase in foodgrain production through HYV seeds.
