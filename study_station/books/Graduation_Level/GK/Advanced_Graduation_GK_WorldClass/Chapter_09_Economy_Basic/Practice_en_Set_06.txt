126. Which of the following best describes "stagflation"?
(a) High growth with low inflation (b) High inflation with stagnant growth and high unemployment (c) Low inflation with high unemployment (d) Deflation with rapid industrial growth
Answer: (b)
Solution: The tempting trap is low inflation with high unemployment, which describes an ordinary recessionary slowdown, not stagflation. Stagflation is the unusual combination of rising prices with stagnant output and joblessness, seen globally in the 1970s after the oil shocks, so the answer is high inflation with stagnant growth and high unemployment.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Introductory Macroeconomics

127. The Phillips Curve originally depicted an inverse relationship between:
(a) Interest rate and investment (b) Inflation and unemployment (c) Money supply and price level (d) Exchange rate and exports
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is money supply and price level, which is the Quantity Theory of Money, not the Phillips Curve. A.W. Phillips showed that as unemployment falls, wage and price inflation tends to rise, so the answer is inflation and unemployment.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Introductory Macroeconomics

128. Which of the following is NOT a function of the Reserve Bank of India?
(a) Issuer of currency (b) Banker to the government (c) Accepting deposits from the general public (d) Controller of credit
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is issuer of currency, which sounds like a commercial activity but is indeed an RBI function. RBI is a bankers' bank and does not deal directly with the public, so the answer is accepting deposits from the general public.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Introductory Macroeconomics

129. The one-rupee note in India is issued by:
(a) Reserve Bank of India (b) NITI Aayog (c) State Bank of India (d) Ministry of Finance, Government of India
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is Reserve Bank of India, which issues all other currency notes. The one-rupee note and coins are issued by the Government of India and bear the signature of the Finance Secretary, not the RBI Governor, so the answer is Ministry of Finance, Government of India.
Source: RBI official publications

130. "Dear money" refers to money that is:
(a) Available only at high interest rates (b) Easily available at low interest rates (c) Counterfeit currency in circulation (d) Foreign currency held by residents
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is easily available at low interest rates, which is "cheap money" — the exact opposite. Dear money means a tight money policy in which credit is costly, so the answer is available only at high interest rates.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Introductory Macroeconomics

131. If the Cash Reserve Ratio (CRR) is increased by the RBI, the immediate effect is:
(a) Increase in money supply (b) Increase in lending capacity of banks (c) Decrease in lendable resources of banks (d) Decrease in repo rate
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is increase in lending capacity, which reverses the logic — a higher CRR locks more funds with the RBI. With less money left to lend, credit contracts, so the answer is decrease in lendable resources of banks.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Introductory Macroeconomics

132. The difference between fiscal deficit and revenue deficit gives:
(a) Primary deficit (b) Effective revenue deficit (c) Monetised deficit (d) Capital expenditure
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is primary deficit, which is fiscal deficit minus interest payments — a confusion between two similar formulas. Fiscal deficit minus revenue deficit equals capital expenditure, so the answer is capital expenditure.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Introductory Macroeconomics

133. Primary deficit is calculated as:
(a) Fiscal deficit minus revenue deficit (b) Revenue deficit minus interest payments (c) Total expenditure minus total revenue (d) Fiscal deficit minus interest payments
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is fiscal deficit minus revenue deficit, which gives capital expenditure, not primary deficit. Primary deficit shows borrowing excluding interest payments on past loans, so the answer is fiscal deficit minus interest payments.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Introductory Macroeconomics

134. Which of the following is a direct tax?
(a) Goods and Services Tax (b) Excise duty (c) Customs duty (d) Corporation tax
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is Goods and Services Tax, which is paid at the time of purchase but its burden is shifted to consumers, making it indirect. Corporation tax is paid directly by companies on their income and cannot be shifted, so the answer is corporation tax.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Indian Economic Development

135. The Goods and Services Tax in India was implemented from:
(a) 1 July 2017 (b) 1 April 2016 (c) 1 January 2018 (d) 1 April 2017
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is 1 April 2017, which sounds plausible because financial years begin in April, but GST actually commenced mid-year. The 101st Constitutional Amendment enabled it and the rollout date was 1 July 2017, so the answer is 1 July 2017.
Source: GST Council / Government of India

136. The GST Council is chaired by:
(a) The Prime Minister (b) The Union Finance Minister (c) The RBI Governor (d) The Vice-Chairman of NITI Aayog
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is the Prime Minister, who heads NITI Aayog's Governing Council — a similar-body confusion. Article 279A provides that the Union Finance Minister chairs the GST Council, so the answer is the Union Finance Minister.
Source: Constitution (101st Amendment) Act, 2016

137. Which of the following is included in the calculation of National Income by the income method?
(a) Transfer payments like old-age pensions (b) Mixed income of self-employed (c) Sale of second-hand goods (d) Capital gains from sale of shares
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is old-age pensions, but transfer payments involve no current production and are excluded. Mixed income of self-employed persons is a genuine factor income, so the answer is mixed income of self-employed.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Introductory Macroeconomics

138. GDP at factor cost plus net indirect taxes equals:
(a) GNP at market price (b) GDP at market price (c) NDP at factor cost (d) National income
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is GNP at market price, which additionally requires net factor income from abroad — an almost-correct statement. Moving from factor cost to market price only needs indirect taxes minus subsidies, so the answer is GDP at market price.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Introductory Macroeconomics

139. The New Economic Policy of 1991 in India is associated with which trio of reforms?
(a) Liberalisation, Privatisation, Globalisation (b) Nationalisation, Regulation, Protection (c) Demonetisation, Digitisation, Decentralisation (d) Planning, Budgeting, Auditing
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is nationalisation, regulation, protection, which describes the pre-1991 licence-permit regime — the reverse of the reforms. The 1991 crisis response rested on the LPG trio, so the answer is Liberalisation, Privatisation, Globalisation.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Indian Economic Development

140. The Planning Commission was replaced by NITI Aayog in:
(a) 2014 (b) 2016 (c) 2015 (d) 2017
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is 2014, the year the new government took office — a chronological confusion. NITI Aayog was actually constituted by a Cabinet resolution on 1 January 2015, so the answer is 2015.
Source: Government of India resolution, 1 January 2015

141. Which Five-Year Plan is known as the "Gadgil Plan"?
(a) First Five-Year Plan (b) Fourth Five-Year Plan (c) Sixth Five-Year Plan (d) Second Five-Year Plan
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is the Second Plan, which is famous but is based on the Mahalanobis model — a similar-name confusion. The Fourth Plan (1969–74) was based on D.R. Gadgil's strategy, so the answer is Fourth Five-Year Plan.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Indian Economic Development

142. The Second Five-Year Plan was based on which model?
(a) Harrod-Domar model (b) Lewis model (c) Mahalanobis model (d) Solow model
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is the Harrod-Domar model, which underlay the First Plan — a chronological confusion between plans. P.C. Mahalanobis designed the Second Plan's heavy-industry strategy, so the answer is Mahalanobis model.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Indian Economic Development

143. "Disguised unemployment" is most commonly found in India in:
(a) The information technology sector (b) The organised industrial sector (c) The banking sector (d) The agricultural sector
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is the organised industrial sector, where surplus labour is usually retrenched rather than hidden. On family farms more workers are engaged than needed, with marginal productivity near zero, so the answer is the agricultural sector.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Indian Economic Development

144. Which of the following measures of money supply is known as "narrow money"?
(a) M1 (b) M3 (c) M2 (d) M4
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is M3, which is actually "broad money" — an almost-correct confusion since both are RBI aggregates. M1, comprising currency with the public, demand deposits and other deposits with RBI, is narrow money, so the answer is M1.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Introductory Macroeconomics

145. Open Market Operations by the RBI refer to:
(a) Opening new bank branches in rural areas (b) Fixing prices of essential commodities (c) Buying and selling government securities (d) Regulating stock exchanges
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is regulating stock exchanges, which is SEBI's domain, not RBI's — an institutional-name confusion. OMOs are RBI's purchase and sale of government securities to control liquidity, so the answer is buying and selling government securities.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Introductory Macroeconomics

146. The repo rate is the rate at which:
(a) RBI lends to commercial banks against securities (b) Commercial banks lend to each other overnight (c) RBI borrows from commercial banks (d) Banks lend to their prime customers
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is RBI borrowing from commercial banks, which is the reverse repo rate — a classic similar-name trap. Repo is the rate at which banks borrow short-term funds from RBI against government securities, so the answer is RBI lends to commercial banks against securities.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Introductory Macroeconomics

147. The Task Force on Implementation of the FRBM Act, which first recommended a comprehensive Goods and Services Tax for India, was headed by:
(a) Raghuram Rajan (b) C. Rangarajan (c) Vijay Kelkar (d) Y.V. Reddy
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is C. Rangarajan, a famous economist associated with poverty estimation and central banking — a near-miss name. The Kelkar Task Force (2004) first recommended a nationwide GST, so the answer is Vijay Kelkar.
Source: Government of India / Kelkar Task Force Report

148. The FRBM Act, 2003 primarily deals with:
(a) Fiscal discipline and deficit targets (b) Foreign exchange management (c) Banking regulation (d) Monetary policy framework
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is foreign exchange management, which belongs to FEMA — a similar-acronym confusion. FRBM stands for Fiscal Responsibility and Budget Management and sets deficit and debt targets, so the answer is fiscal discipline and deficit targets.
Source: FRBM Act, 2003, Government of India

149. Which of the following is NOT a component of the current account of the Balance of Payments?
(a) Foreign direct investment (b) Invisible services trade (c) Merchandise trade (d) Remittances
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is remittances, which seem like capital flows but are current transfers recorded in the current account. FDI creates long-term assets and liabilities and belongs to the capital account, so the answer is foreign direct investment.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Introductory Macroeconomics

150. "Poverty line" estimation in India was historically based on the methodology of which expert group, whose 2011-12 estimates were widely used?
(a) Lakdawala Committee (b) Rangarajan Committee (c) Dandekar-Rath study (d) Tendulkar Committee
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is the Rangarajan Committee, which later (2014) revised the poverty line upward — a chronological confusion. The official 2011-12 poverty estimates used the Suresh Tendulkar methodology, so the answer is Tendulkar Committee.
Source: Planning Commission poverty estimates, 2011-12
