Questions 76–100: Medium

76. Which of the following best describes "disguised unemployment"?
(a) Workers employed in jobs below their skill level (b) A situation where removing some workers does not reduce total output (c) Workers who are unemployed but report themselves as employed (d) Seasonal unemployment in the tourism industry
Answer: (b)
Solution: The tempting trap is the first option, which describes underemployment by skill mismatch, not disguised unemployment. Disguised unemployment, common in Indian agriculture, means the marginal productivity of some workers is zero, so removing them leaves output unchanged. So the answer is a situation where removing some workers does not reduce total output.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Indian Economic Development

77. The "Base Year" currently used for calculating India's GDP (with 2011-12 prices) is:
(a) 2004-05 (b) 2011-12 (c) 2014-15 (d) 2017-18
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is 2014-15, which is often in the news as a policy milestone year, but it is not the base year. The Central Statistics Office revised the national accounts base year to 2011-12 in January 2015. So the answer is 2011-12.
Source: Ministry of Statistics and Programme Implementation (MoSPI)

78. Which institution releases the estimates of National Income in India?
(a) National Statistical Office (NSO) (b) NITI Aayog (c) Ministry of Finance (d) Reserve Bank of India
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is the Reserve Bank of India, because it publishes many monetary statistics, but national income estimation is not its function. The National Statistical Office (formed by merging CSO and NSSO in 2019) under MoSPI releases national income estimates. So the answer is National Statistical Office (NSO).
Source: MoSPI, Government of India

79. In the context of inflation, "stagflation" refers to:
(a) High inflation with high growth (b) Low inflation with low growth (c) Falling prices with rising employment (d) High inflation combined with stagnant growth and high unemployment
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is high inflation with high growth, which sounds plausible because inflation often accompanies growth, but stagflation is precisely the unusual combination of stagnation plus inflation. It describes an economy with high inflation, stagnant output and rising unemployment. So the answer is high inflation combined with stagnant growth and high unemployment.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Introductory Macroeconomics

80. Which of the following is NOT a function of the Reserve Bank of India?
(a) Banker to the Government (b) Framing of the Union Budget (c) Custodian of foreign exchange reserves (d) Issuer of currency
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is banker to the Government, which students sometimes doubt, but RBI does act as banker to both Central and State governments. The Union Budget is prepared by the Ministry of Finance, not RBI. So the answer is framing of the Union Budget.
Source: RBI official website (rbi.org.in)

81. The "Twin Balance Sheet problem" in the Indian context refers to:
(a) Fiscal deficit and current account deficit together (b) Stressed balance sheets of both banks and corporates (c) Revenue deficit and capital deficit (d) Trade deficit and budget deficit
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is fiscal deficit and current account deficit together, which describes the "twin deficits" problem — a different concept often confused with this one. The Twin Balance Sheet problem, highlighted in the Economic Survey 2016-17, refers to over-leveraged companies and banks burdened with bad loans. So the answer is stressed balance sheets of both banks and corporates.
Source: Economic Survey 2016-17, Government of India

82. Which of the following taxes was subsumed under the Goods and Services Tax (GST)?
(a) Income Tax (b) Basic Customs Duty (c) Central Excise Duty (d) Stamp Duty on property registration
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is Basic Customs Duty, since customs duty is levied on goods like excise, but it remains outside GST (IGST is charged in addition on imports). Central Excise Duty was subsumed into GST from 1 July 2017. So the answer is Central Excise Duty.
Source: GST Council / CBIC, Government of India

83. The "Fiscal Deficit" of the Government of India is calculated as:
(a) Total expenditure minus total revenue receipts (b) Revenue expenditure minus revenue receipts (c) Total receipts minus total expenditure (d) Total expenditure minus total receipts excluding borrowings
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is total expenditure minus total revenue receipts, which ignores that borrowings are also receipts; subtracting only revenue receipts overstates the concept. Fiscal deficit equals total expenditure minus total receipts excluding borrowings, i.e., the government's actual borrowing requirement. So the answer is total expenditure minus total receipts excluding borrowings.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Introductory Macroeconomics

84. Which Five-Year Plan is associated with the slogan "Garibi Hatao"?
(a) Third Five-Year Plan (b) Sixth Five-Year Plan (c) Fourth Five-Year Plan (d) Fifth Five-Year Plan
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is the Fourth Plan, because it coincided with Indira Gandhi's rise, but the "Garibi Hatao" slogan of the 1971 elections became the theme of the Fifth Five-Year Plan (1974-79). So the answer is the Fifth Five-Year Plan.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Indian Economic Development

85. The "Liquidity Adjustment Facility" (LAF) of RBI consists of:
(a) CRR and SLR requirements (b) Open Market Operations only (c) Repo and Reverse Repo operations (d) Bank Rate and Marginal Standing Facility only
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is CRR and SLR requirements, since these are also liquidity tools, but they are standing reserve requirements, not the LAF. The LAF, introduced on Narasimham Committee recommendations, operates through repo and reverse repo auctions. So the answer is Repo and Reverse Repo operations.
Source: RBI official website (rbi.org.in)

86. Which of the following sectors contributes the largest share to India's Gross Value Added (GVA)?
(a) Agriculture and allied activities (b) Services (c) Mining and quarrying (d) Industry
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is agriculture, because it employs the largest share of workers, which students confuse with output share. The services sector contributes the largest share (over half) of India's GVA. So the answer is Services.
Source: NSO, National Accounts Statistics

87. The "Phillips Curve" depicts the relationship between:
(a) Interest rate and investment (b) Inflation and unemployment (c) Money supply and price level (d) Exchange rate and exports
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is money supply and price level, which describes the quantity theory of money, a related but distinct idea. The Phillips Curve shows an inverse short-run relationship between inflation and unemployment. So the answer is inflation and unemployment.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Introductory Macroeconomics

88. Consider the following statements about the "Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code, 2016":
1. It provides a time-bound resolution process for corporate insolvency.
2. The National Company Law Tribunal (NCLT) is the adjudicating authority for companies.
3. It replaced the SARFAESI Act, 2002 completely.
Which of the above statements is/are correct?
(a) 2 and 3 only (b) 1, 2 and 3 (c) 1 and 3 only (d) 1 and 2 only
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is statement 3, which sounds like a natural consequence of a new code, but SARFAESI continues to operate alongside the IBC and was not repealed. Statements 1 and 2 are correct: IBC mandates a time-bound corporate resolution process with NCLT as adjudicator for companies. So the answer is 1 and 2 only.
Source: Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code, 2016 (ibbi.gov.in)

89. "Helicopter money" refers to:
(a) Printing and distributing money directly to the public to boost demand (b) Government borrowing from the RBI through treasury bills (c) Foreign aid received for disaster relief (d) Subsidies given to the aviation sector
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is government borrowing from the RBI through treasury bills, which is deficit financing — a real concept but different from helicopter money. Helicopter money, a term popularised by Milton Friedman, means direct money transfers to the public financed by money creation. So the answer is printing and distributing money directly to the public to boost demand.
Source: Economic Survey 2016-17, Government of India

90. Which committee recommended the introduction of the Goods and Services Tax in India for the first time?
(a) Chelliah Committee (b) Raja Committee (c) Kelkar Task Force (2004) (d) Sarkaria Commission
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is the Chelliah Committee, since it carried out major tax reforms in 1991, but it focused on direct taxes and MODVAT, not GST. The Kelkar Task Force (2004) first recommended a comprehensive national GST. So the answer is Kelkar Task Force (2004).
Source: Ministry of Finance, Government of India

91. The "Gini Coefficient" is used to measure:
(a) Income inequality (b) Inflation (c) Economic growth (d) Fiscal deficit
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is economic growth, because inequality and growth are discussed together in development economics, but the Gini Coefficient specifically measures income (or consumption) inequality, ranging from 0 (perfect equality) to 1 (perfect inequality). So the answer is income inequality.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Indian Economic Development

92. Which of the following is a "direct tax"?
(a) Corporation Tax (b) Goods and Services Tax (c) Customs Duty (d) Excise Duty
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is Goods and Services Tax, which is a tax on spending but is legally an indirect tax on consumption whose burden can be shifted. Corporation Tax is levied directly on company profits and its burden cannot be shifted. So the answer is Corporation Tax.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Introductory Macroeconomics

93. The term "Core Sector" in the Index of Eight Core Industries does NOT include:
(a) Automobiles (b) Cement (c) Coal (d) Natural Gas
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is cement, because it feels like a construction item rather than a "core" industry, but cement is one of the eight core industries. The eight are: coal, crude oil, natural gas, refinery products, fertilisers, steel, cement and electricity. So the answer is Automobiles.
Source: Office of the Economic Adviser, DPIIT

94. "Open Market Operations" by the RBI are conducted to:
(a) Allocate credit to priority sectors (b) Regulate the flow of credit and liquidity in the economy (c) Fix the exchange rate of the rupee permanently (d) Supervise the functioning of commercial banks
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is supervising commercial banks, since RBI does supervise banks, but that is a regulatory function, not what OMOs do. Open Market Operations involve buying and selling government securities to manage liquidity and influence interest rates. So the answer is to regulate the flow of credit and liquidity in the economy.
Source: RBI official website (rbi.org.in)

95. The "New Economic Policy" of 1991 was anchored on which three pillars?
(a) Nationalisation, Regulation, Protection (b) Liberalisation, Privatisation, Globalisation (c) Planning, Subsidy, Self-reliance (d) Demonetisation, Digitisation, Formalisation
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is planning, subsidy and self-reliance, which summarises the pre-1991 strategy and is the exact opposite of the 1991 reforms. The New Economic Policy of July 1991 rested on Liberalisation, Privatisation and Globalisation (LPG). So the answer is Liberalisation, Privatisation, Globalisation.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Indian Economic Development

96. Which of the following correctly arranges the sectors in decreasing order of employment share in India (as per PLFS trends)?
(a) Agriculture > Services > Industry (b) Agriculture > Industry > Services (c) Industry > Services > Agriculture (d) Services > Industry > Agriculture
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is Services > Industry > Agriculture, which correctly shows the output (GVA) order but not the employment order — a classic examiner confusion between output share and employment share. Agriculture still employs the largest share of workers, followed by services, then industry. So the answer is Agriculture > Services > Industry.
Source: Periodic Labour Force Survey (PLFS), NSO

97. The "Monetary Policy Committee" (MPC) of India consists of how many members?
(a) 4 (b) 5 (c) 6 (d) 8
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is 5, since students remember "three external members" and miscount the total. The MPC has 6 members — three from RBI (including the Governor as chair) and three external members appointed by the Government. So the answer is 6.
Source: RBI Act, 1934 (as amended in 2016)

98. "Crowding out" in macroeconomics refers to:
(a) Private investment falling due to increased government borrowing raising interest rates (b) Foreign firms driving out domestic firms (c) Population pressure reducing per capita income (d) Excess labour supply reducing wages
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is foreign firms driving out domestic firms, which uses the everyday meaning of "crowding out" in markets, but in macroeconomics the term is specific. It means government borrowing raises interest rates, thereby reducing private investment. So the answer is private investment falling due to increased government borrowing raising interest rates.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Introductory Macroeconomics

99. Which of the following is the correct chronological order of these events?
1. Establishment of the Reserve Bank of India
2. Nationalisation of the Reserve Bank of India
3. Nationalisation of 14 major commercial banks
4. Establishment of NABARD
(a) 1, 3, 2, 4 (b) 2, 1, 3, 4 (c) 1, 2, 3, 4 (d) 2, 1, 4, 3
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is placing RBI's nationalisation before its establishment, which reverses the first two events. RBI was established in 1935, nationalised in 1949, 14 major banks were nationalised in 1969, and NABARD was set up in 1982. So the answer is 1, 2, 3, 4.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Indian Economic Development / RBI history

100. The "Marginal Standing Facility" (MSF) allows banks to borrow from RBI:
(a) At a rate lower than the repo rate (b) For long-term infrastructure lending (c) Without any collateral (d) Overnight, against government securities, at a rate above the repo rate
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is borrowing at a rate lower than the repo rate, because students assume all RBI lending to banks is at repo rate; MSF is a penal facility, so its rate is kept above the repo rate (typically repo + 25 basis points). It provides overnight liquidity against SLR securities. So the answer is overnight, against government securities, at a rate above the repo rate.
Source: RBI official website (rbi.org.in)
