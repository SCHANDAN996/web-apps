126. The Nagoya Protocol on Access and Benefit-Sharing was adopted in which year and under which Convention?
(a) 2002, Convention on Biological Diversity (b) 2014, Ramsar Convention (c) 2010, UNFCCC (d) 2010, Convention on Biological Diversity
Answer: (d)
Solution: The tempting trap is pairing the correct year 2010 with UNFCCC; the Nagoya Protocol deals with genetic resources and benefit-sharing, which fall under the Convention on Biological Diversity, not the climate convention. It was adopted at COP-10 of the CBD in Nagoya, Japan, in October 2010 and entered into force in 2014, so the answer is 2010, Convention on Biological Diversity.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Chemistry (Environmental Chemistry) / CBD official records

127. Consider the following statements about the Kigali Amendment:
1. It amends the Montreal Protocol.
2. It aims to phase down hydrofluorocarbons (HFCs).
3. It was adopted in 2016 in Rwanda.
Which of the statements given above are correct?
(a) 1, 2 and 3 (b) 2 and 3 only (c) 1 and 3 only (d) 1 and 2 only
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is to assume HFCs belong to the Kyoto Protocol only; in fact the Kigali Amendment (October 2016, Kigali, Rwanda) brought HFC phase-down under the Montreal Protocol because HFCs were introduced as substitutes for ozone-depleting substances. All three statements are correct, so the answer is 1, 2 and 3.
Source: UNEP Ozone Secretariat records

128. The Cartagena Protocol on Biosafety primarily deals with:
(a) Safe handling, transport and use of living modified organisms (LMOs) (b) Phase-out of chlorofluorocarbons (c) Conservation of wetlands of international importance (d) Trade in endangered species of wild fauna and flora
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is confusing it with CITES, which deals with trade in endangered species; the Cartagena Protocol (2000, entered into force 2003) is a supplementary agreement to the CBD governing transboundary movement of living modified organisms produced by modern biotechnology, so the answer is safe handling, transport and use of LMOs.
Source: CBD official records

129. Which of the following correctly matches the convention with its headquarters/secretariat location?
(a) UNFCCC — Geneva (b) Convention on Biological Diversity — Montreal (c) Basel Convention — Vienna (d) Ramsar Convention — Paris
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is placing UNFCCC in Geneva; the UNFCCC secretariat is actually in Bonn, Germany, while the CBD secretariat is in Montreal, Canada. The Basel Convention secretariat is in Geneva and Ramsar's is in Gland, Switzerland, so the answer is Convention on Biological Diversity — Montreal.
Source: Official secretariat websites of the respective conventions

130. The Minamata Convention is associated with which pollutant, and the disease it is named after was first observed in which country?
(a) Mercury; Japan (b) Lead; United States (c) Cadmium; Japan (d) Arsenic; Bangladesh
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is cadmium, which caused Itai-itai disease, also in Japan; Minamata disease, however, was caused by methylmercury poisoning from industrial wastewater in Minamata Bay, Japan. The Minamata Convention on Mercury was adopted in 2013 and entered into force in 2017, so the answer is mercury; Japan.
Source: UNEP Minamata Convention records

131. Arrange the following in correct chronological order of adoption:
1. Stockholm Convention on POPs
2. Montreal Protocol
3. Basel Convention
4. Vienna Convention
(a) 2, 4, 3, 1 (b) 4, 2, 3, 1 (c) 4, 3, 2, 1 (d) 2, 3, 4, 1
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is swapping the Vienna Convention and the Montreal Protocol; the Vienna Convention for the Protection of the Ozone Layer came first in 1985, followed by the Montreal Protocol in 1987, the Basel Convention in 1989, and the Stockholm Convention in 2001, so the answer is 4, 2, 3, 1.
Source: UNEP official records

132. The Paris Agreement's central temperature goal is to hold the increase in global average temperature to:
(a) Well below 3°C above pre-industrial levels (b) Below 2°C measured from 1990 levels (c) Exactly 1.5°C with no flexibility (d) Well below 2°C above pre-industrial levels, pursuing efforts to limit it to 1.5°C
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is the rigid "exactly 1.5°C" option; the Agreement's Article 2 sets a dual goal — well below 2°C above pre-industrial levels while pursuing efforts toward 1.5°C. It was adopted at COP-21 in December 2015 and entered into force in November 2016, so the answer is well below 2°C, pursuing efforts to limit it to 1.5°C.
Source: UNFCCC Paris Agreement text

133. Under the Kyoto Protocol, the first commitment period covered which years?
(a) 2005–2012 (b) 2008–2012 (c) 2013–2020 (d) 1997–2005
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is 2005–2012, mixing the year of entry into force (2005) with the commitment period; the first commitment period actually ran from 2008 to 2012, while the second (Doha Amendment) covered 2013–2020, so the answer is 2008–2012.
Source: UNFCCC Kyoto Protocol records

134. Which of the following is NOT one of the three Rio Conventions adopted/opened at the 1992 Earth Summit?
(a) Stockholm Convention on Persistent Organic Pollutants (b) Convention on Biological Diversity (c) UN Convention to Combat Desertification (d) UNFCCC
Answer: (a)

135. The 'Common But Differentiated Responsibilities' (CBDR) principle is most directly associated with:
(a) The Ramsar Convention (b) CITES (c) The Basel Convention (d) The UNFCCC and the Rio Declaration (Principle 7)
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is attributing CBDR to any environmental treaty generically; it is explicitly enshrined in Principle 7 of the 1992 Rio Declaration and operationalised in the UNFCCC, recognising that developed countries bear greater historical responsibility for emissions, so the answer is the UNFCCC and the Rio Declaration.
Source: Rio Declaration on Environment and Development, 1992

136. The Rotterdam Convention is based on which procedure for trade in hazardous chemicals and pesticides?
(a) Advance Informed Agreement (b) Emissions Trading (c) Prior Informed Consent (PIC) (d) Clean Development Mechanism
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is Advance Informed Agreement, which belongs to the Cartagena Protocol on Biosafety; the Rotterdam Convention (1998, in force 2004) uses the Prior Informed Consent procedure for certain hazardous chemicals and pesticides in international trade, so the answer is Prior Informed Consent.
Source: Rotterdam Convention official text

137. Which gas is NOT among the six greenhouse gases covered under the Kyoto Protocol basket?
(a) Sulphur hexafluoride (SF6) (b) Perfluorocarbons (PFCs) (c) Carbon monoxide (CO) (d) Nitrous oxide (N2O)
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is thinking every carbon compound is a Kyoto gas; the six Kyoto gases are CO2, CH4, N2O, HFCs, PFCs and SF6 — carbon monoxide is an air pollutant but not a listed greenhouse gas in the Protocol, so the answer is carbon monoxide.
Source: UNFCCC Kyoto Protocol, Annex A

138. The Ramsar Convention (1971) originally focused on wetlands primarily as:
(a) Habitats of waterfowl (b) Sources of freshwater for agriculture (c) Carbon sinks (d) Sites for mangrove afforestation
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is projecting modern climate language (carbon sinks) onto a 1971 treaty; the Ramsar Convention, signed in the Iranian city of Ramsar, was originally framed around the conservation of wetlands as habitats of waterfowl, later broadening to wise use of all wetlands, so the answer is habitats of waterfowl.

140. The 'Aichi Biodiversity Targets' were adopted under which framework and were to be achieved by which year?

141. The Kunming-Montreal Global Biodiversity Framework (2022) includes the '30x30' target, which refers to:
(a) Reducing emissions by 30% by 2030 (b) Protecting 30 endangered species by 2030 (c) Conserving at least 30% of the world's land and ocean areas by 2030 (d) Mobilising 30 billion dollars by 2030
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is the emissions-reduction reading borrowed from climate negotiations; Target 3 of the Kunming-Montreal GBF, adopted at CBD COP-15, commits parties to effectively conserve and manage at least 30 per cent of terrestrial, inland water, coastal and marine areas by 2030, so the answer is conserving at least 30% of land and ocean by 2030.

142. Which of the following pairs is INCORRECTLY matched?
(a) Basel Convention — Hazardous wastes and their disposal (b) Stockholm Convention — Persistent organic pollutants (c) Vienna Convention — Protection of the ozone layer (d) Montreal Protocol — Greenhouse gas emission reduction targets
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is that the Montreal Protocol does affect climate indirectly, tempting candidates to accept it; however, binding greenhouse gas reduction targets were the hallmark of the Kyoto Protocol, while the Montreal Protocol phases out ozone-depleting substances, so the incorrectly matched pair is Montreal Protocol — greenhouse gas emission reduction targets.

143. The concept of 'Nationally Determined Contributions' (NDCs) is a feature of:
(a) The Paris Agreement (b) The Kyoto Protocol (c) The Montreal Protocol (d) The Basel Convention
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is the Kyoto Protocol, which instead imposed top-down binding targets on developed (Annex I) countries; the Paris Agreement introduced bottom-up NDCs, submitted by all parties and updated every five years, so the answer is the Paris Agreement.
Source: UNFCCC Paris Agreement, Article 4

144. CITES regulates international trade in endangered species through a system of appendices. Species threatened with extinction, for which commercial trade is generally prohibited, are listed in:
(a) Appendix III (b) Appendix II (c) Appendix I (d) Appendix IV
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is Appendix II, which covers species not necessarily threatened with extinction but requiring controlled trade; Appendix I lists species threatened with extinction where commercial trade is prohibited except in exceptional circumstances. CITES has only three appendices, so the answer is Appendix I.
Source: CITES official text
