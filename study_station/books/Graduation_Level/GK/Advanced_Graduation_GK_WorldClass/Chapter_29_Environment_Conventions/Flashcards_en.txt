Card 1
Front: Which 1987 protocol phased out ozone-depleting substances, and what is its most famous amendment that added HFCs?
Back: The Montreal Protocol (1987); the Kigali Amendment (2016) added HFCs, entering into force in 2019.

Card 2
Front: Which 1992 Rio "Earth Summit" convention deals specifically with greenhouse gases, and what is its ultimate objective?
Back: UNFCCC — to stabilize greenhouse gas concentrations at a level preventing dangerous human interference with the climate system.

Card 3
Front: Kyoto Protocol committed developed countries to cut emissions. Which "Annex" of the UNFCCC listed these countries?
Back: Annex I countries (industrialized nations and economies in transition). Trap: Annex II = countries that provide financial aid; don't confuse the two.

Card 4
Front: Which 2015 agreement aims to keep warming "well below 2°C" and pursue 1.5°C, and what mechanism does it use instead of binding targets?
Back: The Paris Agreement; it uses Nationally Determined Contributions (NDCs) — voluntary, self-set targets reviewed every 5 years.

Card 5
Front: Memory trick: How do you remember the three Rio Conventions of 1992?
Back: "CBD-UNFCCC-UNCCD" — remember "Climate, Creatures, Cracked land": Climate change (UNFCCC), Biodiversity (CBD), Desertification (UNCCD).

Card 6
Front: Which convention controls transboundary movement of hazardous wastes, and in which year was it adopted?
Back: The Basel Convention, adopted 1989, in force 1992.

Card 7
Front: Basel, Rotterdam, Stockholm — what single word links what each regulates?
Back: "Waste–Trade–Toxins": Basel = hazardous Wastes, Rotterdam = prior informed consent for Trade in hazardous chemicals, Stockholm = Persistent Organic Pollutants (POPs).

Card 8
Front: Which convention protects wetlands of international importance, and where was it signed?
Back: The Ramsar Convention (1971), signed at Ramsar, Iran. Montreux Record lists threatened Ramsar sites.

Card 9
Front: Examiner's trap: Is the Kyoto Protocol's second commitment period called the "Doha Amendment" or the "Paris Amendment"?
Back: The Doha Amendment (2012) — it covers 2013–2020. Paris is a separate agreement (2015), not an amendment to Kyoto.

Card 10
Front: Which treaty regulates international trade in endangered species, and what do its Appendices I, II, III signify?
Back: CITES (1973, Washington). Appendix I = trade banned (most threatened); Appendix II = trade regulated; Appendix III = protected by at least one country asking cooperation.

Card 11
Front: Which protocol under the CBD deals with biosafety and living modified organisms (LMOs)?
Back: The Cartagena Protocol on Biosafety (2000). Trap: Nagoya Protocol (2010) deals with access and benefit-sharing of genetic resources, not biosafety.

Card 12
Front: Memory trick: How to remember Cartagena vs Nagoya?
Back: "Cartagena = Caution (safety of GMOs); Nagoya = Negotiate (sharing benefits of genetic resources)."

Card 13
Front: Which 1979 convention protects migratory animals, and by what other name is it known?
Back: The Bonn Convention — Convention on the Conservation of Migratory Species of Wild Animals (CMS).

Card 14
Front: Which Vienna-based treaty (1985) was the precursor to the Montreal Protocol, and what did it establish?
Back: The Vienna Convention for the Protection of the Ozone Layer — a framework for cooperation; actual phase-out targets came later in the Montreal Protocol.

Card 15
Front: Examiner's trap: Does the Paris Agreement legally bind countries to achieve their NDC targets?
Back: No — NDCs are not legally enforceable outcomes; countries are bound to prepare, communicate, and pursue them, but not penalized for missing targets.

Card 16
Front: Which convention targets the "Dirty Dozen" chemicals, and name any two of them.
Back: The Stockholm Convention (2001) on POPs. Examples: DDT, PCBs, dioxins, aldrin.

Card 17
Front: Which 2013 convention addresses mercury pollution, and after which Japanese city is it named?
Back: The Minamata Convention on Mercury — named after Minamata, Japan, where mercury poisoning (Minamata disease) occurred.

Card 18
Front: What does UNCCD combat, and which is the only legally binding Rio convention on land?
Back: Desertification and land degradation; UNCCD (1994) is the only legally binding international agreement linking environment and development to sustainable land management.

Card 19
Front: Quick recall: Which convention's secretariat is in Montreal, and which in Bonn?
Back: CBD secretariat is in Montreal; UNFCCC and UNCCD secretariats are in Bonn (Germany).

Card 20
Front: Memory trick: How to remember the order Vienna → Montreal → Kyoto → Paris?
Back: "Very Many Kings Party" — Vienna (1985), Montreal (1987), Kyoto (1997), Paris (2015): ozone framework → ozone phase-out → binding emission cuts → universal climate pledges.
