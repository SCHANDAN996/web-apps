26. The Montreal Protocol, often called the most successful environmental treaty, primarily deals with the phase-out of:
(a) Greenhouse gases (b) Persistent organic pollutants (c) Ozone-depleting substances (d) Hazardous wastes
Answer: (c)
Solution: Many students confuse the Montreal Protocol with the Kyoto Protocol because both are climate-related treaties; however, Kyoto targeted greenhouse gases while Montreal targeted substances like CFCs and halons that deplete the ozone layer. The Montreal Protocol was signed in 1987 and came into force in 1989, so the answer is ozone-depleting substances.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Chemistry (Environmental Chemistry)

27. The Kigali Amendment (2016) to the Montreal Protocol aims to phase down:
(a) Chlorofluorocarbons (b) Hydrochlorofluorocarbons (c) Hydrofluorocarbons (d) Methyl bromide
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap here is choosing CFCs or HCFCs, which were already covered by the original Montreal Protocol and its earlier amendments. The Kigali Amendment specifically targets HFCs, which do not harm the ozone layer but are powerful greenhouse gases, so the answer is hydrofluorocarbons.
Source: PYQ-style

28. The Basel Convention is concerned with the control of:
(a) International trade in endangered species (b) Transboundary movements of hazardous wastes and their disposal (c) Conservation of wetlands of international importance (d) Prior informed consent for hazardous chemicals in trade
Answer: (b)
Solution: Options naming chemicals and endangered species are traps because they describe the Rotterdam Convention and CITES respectively. The Basel Convention (1989, in force 1992) regulates transboundary movement of hazardous wastes, so the answer is transboundary movements of hazardous wastes and their disposal.

29. The Ramsar Convention (1971) is associated with the conservation of:
(a) Wetlands (b) Mangroves (c) Coral reefs (d) Biosphere reserves
Answer: (a)
Solution: Mangroves and coral reefs are tempting because they are also ecosystems needing protection, but the Ramsar Convention, signed in the Iranian city of Ramsar, is specifically the Convention on Wetlands of International Importance, so the answer is wetlands.

30. Which of the following correctly pairs the convention with its year of adoption?
(a) Basel Convention – 1972 (b) Vienna Convention – 1992 (c) Ramsar Convention – 1985 (d) Stockholm Convention on POPs – 2001
Answer: (d)
Solution: This is a chronological-confusion trap: the Vienna Convention is 1985, Ramsar is 1971, and Basel is 1989, so all three distractors swap well-known years. The Stockholm Convention on Persistent Organic Pollutants was indeed adopted in 2001 and entered into force in 2004, so the answer is Stockholm Convention on POPs – 2001.

31. The 'Prior Informed Consent' (PIC) procedure in international trade of certain hazardous chemicals and pesticides is a feature of the:
(a) Rotterdam Convention (b) Basel Convention (c) Stockholm Convention (d) Minamata Convention
Answer: (a)
Solution: Basel is the tempting wrong choice because it also deals with hazardous materials, but Basel governs waste movement while the PIC procedure for hazardous chemicals and pesticides in international trade belongs to the Rotterdam Convention (1998), so the answer is Rotterdam Convention.

32. The Minamata Convention, which entered into force in 2017, deals with:
(a) Lead (b) Mercury (c) Cadmium (d) Arsenic
Answer: (b)
Solution: The convention is named after Minamata city in Japan, where mercury poisoning (Minamata disease) caused severe health damage; students sometimes pick lead or cadmium because they are also heavy-metal pollutants. The Minamata Convention (adopted 2013) addresses mercury throughout its life cycle, so the answer is mercury.

33. CITES, the Convention on International Trade in Endangered Species of Wild Fauna and Flora, was signed in:
(a) 1992 (b) 1982 (c) 1973 (d) 1971
Answer: (c)
Solution: The year 1971 is a trap because it belongs to the Ramsar Convention, and 1992 belongs to the Rio Earth Summit conventions. CITES was signed in Washington D.C. in 1973 and entered into force in 1975, so the answer is 1973.

34. The United Nations Framework Convention on Climate Change (UNFCCC) was adopted at:
(a) Stockholm Conference, 1972 (b) Paris Conference, 2015 (c) Kyoto Conference, 1997 (d) Rio Earth Summit, 1992
Answer: (d)
Solution: Kyoto and Paris are traps because they are protocols/agreements under the UNFCCC, not the framework convention itself. The UNFCCC was adopted at the Rio de Janeiro Earth Summit in 1992 and entered into force in 1994, so the answer is Rio Earth Summit, 1992.

35. Under the Kyoto Protocol, the principle that placed a greater burden on developed nations for emission reduction is known as:
(a) Common But Differentiated Responsibilities (b) Polluter Pays Principle (c) Precautionary Principle (d) Absolute Liability
Answer: (a)
Solution: The Polluter Pays Principle is a tempting trap because it also assigns responsibility for environmental harm, but it is a general liability principle, not the burden-sharing basis of Kyoto. The Kyoto Protocol operationalised Common But Differentiated Responsibilities (CBDR) from the UNFCCC, so the answer is Common But Differentiated Responsibilities.
Source: PYQ-style

36. The Paris Agreement (2015) requires countries to submit their climate action plans known as:
(a) National Biodiversity Action Plans (b) Intended Emission Reduction Certificates (c) National Adaptation Programmes of Action (d) Nationally Determined Contributions
Answer: (d)
Solution: National Adaptation Programmes of Action is a trap because it is a real UNFCCC instrument, but it applies to least developed countries' adaptation needs, not the core Paris pledge mechanism. Under the Paris Agreement each party submits Nationally Determined Contributions, so the answer is Nationally Determined Contributions.

37. The Paris Agreement's central temperature goal is to hold global warming well below:
(a) 1°C above pre-industrial levels (b) 2°C above pre-industrial levels, pursuing efforts towards 1.5°C (c) 3°C above pre-industrial levels (d) 0.5°C above pre-industrial levels
Answer: (b)
Solution: Choosing only 1.5°C is the classic trap, because 1.5°C is the aspirational effort, not the headline threshold. The agreement's stated goal is to hold the increase well below 2°C while pursuing efforts to limit it to 1.5°C, so the answer is 2°C above pre-industrial levels, pursuing efforts towards 1.5°C.

38. The Convention on Biological Diversity (CBD) was opened for signature at the Earth Summit in:
(a) 1992 (b) 1987 (c) 1997 (d) 2002
Answer: (a)
Solution: The year 1987 is a trap because it belongs to the Montreal Protocol, and 1997 belongs to Kyoto. The CBD was one of the two conventions opened for signature at the 1992 Rio Earth Summit, so the answer is 1992.

39. The Nagoya Protocol is a supplementary agreement to the:
(a) Convention on Biological Diversity (b) UNFCCC (c) Basel Convention (d) CITES
Answer: (a)
Solution: UNFCCC is a tempting trap because the Kyoto and Paris agreements are its supplementary instruments, creating a similar-name confusion. 40. The Cartagena Protocol deals with:
(a) Access to genetic resources and benefit sharing (b) Biosafety and safe handling of living modified organisms (c) Phase-out of ozone-depleting substances (d) Control of mercury emissions
Answer: (b)
Solution: Access and benefit-sharing is the trap because it describes the Nagoya Protocol, which students often mix up with Cartagena since both supplement the CBD. The Cartagena Protocol (2000) governs biosafety and transboundary movement of living modified organisms, so the answer is biosafety and safe handling of living modified organisms.

41. The Stockholm Convention initially listed a group of notorious persistent organic pollutants popularly called the:
(a) Hazardous Eight (b) Toxic Twenty (c) Dirty Dozen (d) Black List
Answer: (c)
Solution: The other names sound plausible but are fabricated distractors; the well-known term in environmental literature is the 'Dirty Dozen'. The Stockholm Convention (2001) initially targeted 12 POPs including DDT, aldrin and dioxins, so the answer is Dirty Dozen.

42. Which of the following is NOT a greenhouse gas covered under the Kyoto Protocol's basket of gases?
(a) Carbon dioxide (b) Methane (c) Sulphur dioxide (d) Nitrous oxide
Answer: (c)
Solution: This is a negative-statement trap; sulphur dioxide is a major air pollutant causing acid rain, which makes it feel like it belongs in a climate treaty. The Kyoto basket covers CO2, methane, nitrous oxide, HFCs, PFCs and SF6, so the answer is sulphur dioxide.

43. The Vienna Convention (1985) is significant because it:
(a) Fixed binding targets for CFC reduction (b) Established the carbon trading mechanism (c) Provided the framework for the Montreal Protocol on ozone protection (d) Created the list of wetlands of international importance
Answer: (c)
Solution: Binding CFC targets is the trap, because those targets were set by the Montreal Protocol, not the Vienna Convention. The Vienna Convention for the Protection of the Ozone Layer (1985) created the framework under which the Montreal Protocol was negotiated, so the answer is that it provided the framework for the Montreal Protocol on ozone protection.

44. India's 'Bharat Stage' emission norms draw their conceptual basis from standards originally developed in:
(a) Japan (b) The United States (c) Brazil (d) Europe
Answer: (d)
Solution: The United States is a tempting trap because US emission standards are also globally famous, but India's Bharat Stage norms are explicitly modelled on European (Euro) norms. Bharat Stage norms correspond to Euro standards with a time lag, so the answer is Europe.

(a) Paris Agreement (b) Kunming-Montreal Global Biodiversity Framework (c) Kigali Amendment (d) Ramsar Strategic Plan

47. Which conference is regarded as the first major global conference on the human environment, held in 1972?

Answer: (d)
Solution: Rio is the classic trap because it is the most famous environmental summit, but it took place in 1992, twenty years later. The 1972 Stockholm Conference was the first UN conference on the human environment and led to the creation of UNEP, so the answer is Stockholm Conference.

48. The concept of 'Common But Differentiated Responsibilities' was formally enshrined in:
(a) The Montreal Protocol (b) The UNFCCC at Rio, 1992 (c) The Basel Convention (d) The Ramsar Convention
Answer: (b)
Solution: The Montreal Protocol is a trap because it did apply differentiated phase-out schedules for developing countries, but the principle itself was formally articulated earlier. CBDR was enshrined as Principle 7 of the Rio Declaration and embedded in the UNFCCC in 1992, so the answer is the UNFCCC at Rio, 1992.

49. The Bonn Convention is another name for the Convention on the Conservation of:

Answer: (d)

50. Consider the following statements about the Montreal Protocol:
Statement 1: It is the only UN treaty that has achieved universal ratification.
Statement 2: It was amended at Kigali to include greenhouse gases that do not deplete ozone.
Statement 3: It established the principle of common but differentiated responsibilities for the first time.
Which of the statements given above is/are correct?
(a) 1 and 2 only (b) 2 and 3 only (c) 1 only (d) 1, 2 and 3
Answer: (a)
Solution: Statement 3 is the trap: CBDR was first enshrined in the Rio Declaration/UNFCCC (1992), not created by the Montreal Protocol, though the Protocol applied differentiated timelines. Statements 1 and 2 are correct — the Protocol is universally ratified and the Kigali Amendment added HFCs, so the answer is 1 and 2 only.
