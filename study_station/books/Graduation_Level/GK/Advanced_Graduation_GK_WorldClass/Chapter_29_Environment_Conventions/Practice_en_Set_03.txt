Questions 51–75: Medium

51. The Montreal Protocol was signed in which year?
(a) 1985 (b) 1989 (c) 1987 (d) 1992
Answer: (c)
Solution: The Vienna Convention (1985) is the tempting wrong choice because it also deals with ozone, but it is only a framework convention. The actual protocol with binding phase-out targets was signed in 1987, so the answer is 1987.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Chemistry (Environmental Chemistry)

52. The Kigali Amendment to the Montreal Protocol deals with the phase-down of:
(a) HFCs (b) Halons (c) CFCs (d) Methyl bromide
Answer: (a)
Solution: CFCs and halons were already covered by the original Montreal Protocol, so they are traps. The Kigali Amendment (2016) targets hydrofluorocarbons (HFCs), which are powerful greenhouse gases, so the answer is HFCs.
Source: UNEP Ozone Secretariat

53. The Basel Convention is concerned with the control of:
(a) Trade in endangered species (b) Transboundary movements of hazardous wastes (c) Persistent organic pollutants (d) Ozone-depleting substances
Answer: (b)
Solution: The Stockholm Convention (POPs) and CITES (endangered species) are similar-sounding traps. The Basel Convention (1989) regulates transboundary movement of hazardous wastes and their disposal, so the answer is transboundary movements of hazardous wastes.
Source: Basel Convention Secretariat, UNEP

54. The Stockholm Convention (2001) originally listed how many chemicals in the "dirty dozen"?
(a) 10 (b) 12 (c) 14 (d) 16
Answer: (b)
Solution: The number 10 is tempting because later additions changed the list, but the original 2001 convention targeted 12 persistent organic pollutants known as the "dirty dozen", so the answer is 12.
Source: Stockholm Convention Secretariat

55. The Earth Summit (Rio Summit) of 1992 was held in which city?
(a) Johannesburg (b) Kyoto (c) Rio de Janeiro (d) Paris
Answer: (c)
Solution: Johannesburg hosted the 2002 World Summit on Sustainable Development (Rio+10), which creates chronological confusion. The 1992 UN Conference on Environment and Development was held in Rio de Janeiro, so the answer is Rio de Janeiro.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Political Science (Environment and Natural Resources)

56. The Kyoto Protocol was adopted in 1997 but entered into force in:
(a) 2000 (b) 2002 (c) 2008 (d) 2005
Answer: (d)
Solution: 2008 is a trap because the first commitment period began in 2008. The protocol entered into force on 16 February 2005 after Russia's ratification satisfied the 55% emissions condition, so the answer is 2005.
Source: UNFCCC official records

57. The Paris Agreement aims to limit global warming to well below:
(a) 1°C above pre-industrial levels (b) 2°C above pre-industrial levels (c) 3°C above pre-industrial levels (d) 4°C above pre-industrial levels
Answer: (b)
Solution: 1°C is a trap because the agreement mentions pursuing efforts toward 1.5°C, but the main binding goal is well below 2°C above pre-industrial levels, so the answer is 2°C.
Source: UNFCCC, Paris Agreement text (2015)

58. CITES, which regulates international trade in endangered species, was signed in:
(a) 1971 (b) 1982 (c) 1975 (d) 1973
Answer: (d)
Solution: 1975 is tempting because CITES entered into force that year, but it was signed in Washington D.C. in 1973, so the answer is 1973.
Source: CITES official website

59. The Ramsar Convention (1971) is associated with the conservation of:
(a) Wetlands (b) Mangroves (c) Coral reefs (d) Tropical forests
Answer: (a)
Solution: Mangroves are a trap since many Ramsar sites are mangroves, but the convention covers all wetlands of international importance. It was signed at Ramsar, Iran, so the answer is wetlands.
Source: Ramsar Convention Secretariat

60. The Convention on Biological Diversity (CBD) was opened for signature at:
(a) Stockholm Conference, 1972 (b) Paris Conference, 2015 (c) Johannesburg Summit, 2002 (d) Rio Earth Summit, 1992
Answer: (d)
Solution: Stockholm 1972 is a trap as the first major UN environment conference, but CBD was one of the two conventions (with UNFCCC) opened for signature at the 1992 Rio Earth Summit, so the answer is Rio Earth Summit, 1992.
Source: CBD Secretariat, UNEP

61. The Nagoya Protocol is associated with:
(a) Biosafety of living modified organisms (b) Access and benefit-sharing of genetic resources (c) Phase-out of POPs (d) Reduction of mercury emissions
Answer: (b)
Solution: Biosafety is the trap because the Cartagena Protocol (also under CBD) covers living modified organisms. The Nagoya Protocol (2010) deals with access to genetic resources and fair sharing of benefits, so the answer is access and benefit-sharing of genetic resources.
Source: CBD Secretariat

62. The Cartagena Protocol on Biosafety is a supplementary agreement to:
(a) Convention on Biological Diversity (b) Montreal Protocol (c) UNFCCC (d) Basel Convention
Answer: (a)
Solution: The Basel Convention is a trap because it also deals with transboundary movement, but of hazardous waste. The Cartagena Protocol (2000) on safe transfer of living modified organisms is supplementary to the CBD, so the answer is Convention on Biological Diversity.
Source: CBD Secretariat

63. The Minamata Convention deals with which pollutant?
(a) Mercury (b) Lead (c) Arsenic (d) Cadmium
Answer: (a)
Solution: Lead is a tempting distractor, but the convention is named after Minamata disease in Japan, caused by mercury poisoning. Adopted in 2013, it controls mercury emissions and use, so the answer is mercury.
Source: UNEP, Minamata Convention Secretariat

64. The first UN Conference on the Human Environment (1972) was held at:
(a) Geneva (b) Stockholm (c) Nairobi (d) Vienna
Answer: (b)
Solution: Nairobi is a trap because UNEP (created by this conference) is headquartered there. The 1972 conference itself was held in Stockholm, Sweden, so the answer is Stockholm.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Political Science

65. The Vienna Convention (1985) is related to:
(a) Biodiversity conservation (b) Climate change (c) Protection of the ozone layer (d) Hazardous waste management
Answer: (c)
Solution: Climate change is a trap since ozone and climate issues are often confused. The Vienna Convention is the framework for protecting the ozone layer, under which the Montreal Protocol was later adopted, so the answer is protection of the ozone layer.
Source: UNEP Ozone Secretariat

66. Under the Paris Agreement, countries submit their climate action plans known as:
(a) National Action Plans (b) Intended Mitigation Targets (c) National Adaptation Programmes (d) Nationally Determined Contributions
Answer: (d)
Solution: National Action Plan on Climate Change is India's domestic plan (2008) and is a trap. Under the Paris Agreement, each country files Nationally Determined Contributions (NDCs), so the answer is Nationally Determined Contributions.
Source: UNFCCC

67. The Rotterdam Convention is based on which principle for trade in hazardous chemicals?
(a) Polluter Pays Principle (b) Common But Differentiated Responsibilities (c) Precautionary Principle (d) Prior Informed Consent
Answer: (d)
Solution: Polluter Pays is a general environmental law principle and a trap. The Rotterdam Convention (1998) requires Prior Informed Consent (PIC) before exporting listed hazardous chemicals, so the answer is Prior Informed Consent.
Source: Rotterdam Convention Secretariat

68. The concept of "Common But Differentiated Responsibilities" (CBDR) is a cornerstone of:
(a) Montreal Protocol (b) CITES (c) UNFCCC (d) Ramsar Convention
Answer: (c)
Solution: The Montreal Protocol is a trap because it gave developing countries grace periods, but CBDR is a formal principle of the UNFCCC (1992), recognising differing capacities of developed and developing nations, so the answer is UNFCCC.
Source: UNFCCC, Principle 7 of Rio Declaration

69. Agenda 21, a blueprint for sustainable development, was adopted at:
(a) Stockholm, 1972 (b) Rio de Janeiro, 1992 (c) Kyoto, 1997 (d) Copenhagen, 2009
Answer: (b)
Solution: Kyoto 1997 is a chronological trap. Agenda 21 was a non-binding action plan adopted at the 1992 Rio Earth Summit (UNCED), so the answer is Rio de Janeiro, 1992.
Source: UN Department of Economic and Social Affairs

70. The Bonn Convention (CMS) deals with the conservation of:
(a) Migratory species of wild animals (b) Wetlands of international importance (c) Trade in endangered species (d) Marine pollution from ships
Answer: (a)
Solution: CITES is the classic similar-name trap, but it regulates trade, not migration. The Convention on Migratory Species (Bonn Convention, 1979) protects migratory wild animals, so the answer is migratory species of wild animals.
Source: CMS Secretariat, UNEP

71. The second commitment period of the Kyoto Protocol ran from:
(a) 2008–2012 (b) 2015–2025 (c) 2013–2020 (d) 2016–2030
Answer: (c)
Solution: 2008–2012 is the trap because it was the first commitment period. The Doha Amendment established the second commitment period from 2013 to 2020, so the answer is 2013–2020.
Source: UNFCCC

72. India ratified the Paris Agreement in which year?
(a) 2016 (b) 2015 (c) 2017 (d) 2018
Answer: (a)
Solution: 2015 is tempting because the agreement was adopted in December 2015, but India ratified it on 2 October 2016 (Gandhi Jayanti), so the answer is 2016.
Source: Ministry of Environment, Forest and Climate Change, Government of India

73. The "30x30" target — protecting 30% of land and oceans by 2030 — is part of:
(a) Kunming-Montreal Global Biodiversity Framework (b) Paris Agreement (c) Kigali Amendment (d) Sendai Framework
Answer: (a)
Solution: The Paris Agreement is a trap as the most famous recent environmental deal, but the 30x30 target belongs to the Kunming-Montreal Global Biodiversity Framework adopted at CBD COP15 in 2022, so the answer is Kunming-Montreal Global Biodiversity Framework.
Source: CBD Secretariat

74. Which of the following pairs is correctly matched?
(a) Basel Convention — Ozone depletion (b) Ramsar Convention — Migratory species (c) Stockholm Convention — Persistent organic pollutants (d) Vienna Convention — Hazardous waste
Answer: (c)
Solution: Each wrong option swaps the subject of another real convention, a classic examiner's trap: Basel covers hazardous waste, Ramsar covers wetlands, and Vienna covers ozone. Only the Stockholm Convention–POPs pairing is correct, so the answer is Stockholm Convention — Persistent organic pollutants.
Source: UNEP convention secretariats

75. The United Nations Framework Convention on Climate Change (UNFCCC) holds its Conference of Parties (COP) meetings:
(a) Once every two years (b) Once every five years (c) Twice a year (d) Annually
Answer: (d)
Solution: Five years is a trap because NDC updates under the Paris Agreement follow a five-year cycle. The UNFCCC COP itself meets every year, so the answer is annually.
Source: UNFCCC official website
