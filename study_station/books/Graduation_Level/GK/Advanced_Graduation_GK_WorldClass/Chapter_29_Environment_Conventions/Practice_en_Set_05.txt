101. The Montreal Protocol, signed in 1987, primarily deals with the phase-out of substances that deplete which layer of the atmosphere?
(a) Troposphere (b) Ionosphere (c) Ozone layer (d) Mesosphere
Answer: (c)
Solution: The Montreal Protocol (1987) targets ozone-depleting substances like CFCs and halons; the troposphere is where weather occurs and is not the target. It is considered the most successful environmental treaty, so the answer is the ozone layer.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Chemistry (Environmental Chemistry)

102. The Kigali Amendment (2016) to the Montreal Protocol aims to phase down which group of substances?
(a) Chlorofluorocarbons (b) Hydrofluorocarbons (c) Halons (d) Carbon tetrachloride
Answer: (b)
Solution: CFCs and halons were already covered by the original Montreal Protocol, which is the tempting wrong choice; the Kigali Amendment specifically targets HFCs, which are potent greenhouse gases though not ozone-depleting, so the answer is hydrofluorocarbons.
Source: Ozone Secretariat, UNEP (ozone.unep.org)

103. The Basel Convention is concerned with the control of transboundary movements of:
(a) Persistent organic pollutants (b) Endangered species (c) Ozone-depleting substances (d) Hazardous wastes and their disposal
Answer: (d)
Solution: The Stockholm Convention covers persistent organic pollutants and CITES covers endangered species, which are common confusions; the Basel Convention (1989) regulates hazardous waste movement, so the answer is hazardous wastes and their disposal.
Source: Basel Convention official site (basel.int)

104. The Stockholm Convention (2001) deals with:
(a) Greenhouse gas reduction (b) Wetland conservation (c) Persistent organic pollutants (d) Biodiversity access and benefit sharing
Answer: (c)
Solution: The Ramsar Convention covers wetlands and the Nagoya Protocol covers benefit sharing, which are traps; the Stockholm Convention targets the "dirty dozen" POPs like DDT and dioxins, so the answer is persistent organic pollutants.
Source: Stockholm Convention official site (pops.int)

105. The Ramsar Convention (1971) is associated with the conservation of:
(a) Wetlands of international importance (b) Mangroves (c) Coral reefs (d) Tropical rainforests
Answer: (a)
Solution: Mangroves and coral reefs have no dedicated global convention of this kind, which tempts guesswork; the Ramsar Convention, signed in the Iranian city of Ramsar, protects wetlands, so the answer is wetlands of international importance.
Source: Ramsar Convention official site (ramsar.org)

106. The Paris Agreement (2015) aims to limit global warming to well below:
(a) 1°C above pre-industrial levels (b) 4°C above pre-industrial levels (c) 3°C above pre-industrial levels (d) 2°C above pre-industrial levels
Answer: (d)
Solution: 1.5°C is the aspirational target, not the main threshold, which is the classic trap; the agreement's central goal is well below 2°C while pursuing efforts toward 1.5°C, so the answer is 2°C above pre-industrial levels.
Source: UNFCCC official site (unfccc.int)

107. The Kyoto Protocol was adopted in which year?
(a) 1992 (b) 1997 (c) 2002 (d) 2005
Answer: (b)
Solution: 2005 is when the Protocol entered into force, not when it was adopted — a chronological trap; the Protocol was adopted in Kyoto, Japan, in 1997, so the answer is 1997.
Source: UNFCCC official site (unfccc.int)

108. CITES, the convention regulating international trade in endangered species, is also known as the:
(a) Vienna Convention (b) Washington Convention (c) Geneva Convention (d) Bonn Convention
Answer: (b)
Solution: The Vienna Convention concerns ozone layer protection and the Bonn Convention concerns migratory species, which are similar-name traps; CITES was signed in Washington D.C. in 1973, so the answer is the Washington Convention.
Source: CITES official site (cites.org)

109. The Convention on Biological Diversity (CBD) was opened for signature at which event?
(a) Stockholm Conference 1972 (b) Rio Earth Summit 1992 (c) Johannesburg Summit 2002 (d) Paris Conference 2015
Answer: (b)
Solution: The 1972 Stockholm Conference was the first UN environment conference but produced no biodiversity treaty, which is the trap; the CBD was opened for signature at the 1992 Rio Earth Summit, so the answer is Rio Earth Summit 1992.
Source: CBD official site (cbd.int)

110. The Nagoya Protocol is associated with:
(a) Hazardous waste trade (b) Access to genetic resources and fair sharing of benefits (c) Ozone-depleting substances (d) Climate finance
Answer: (b)
Solution: Hazardous waste belongs to Basel and ozone substances to Montreal, which are confusions; the Nagoya Protocol (2010) is a supplementary agreement to the CBD on access and benefit sharing, so the answer is access to genetic resources and fair sharing of benefits.
Source: CBD official site (cbd.int)

111. The Cartagena Protocol deals with the safe transfer and handling of:
(a) Living modified organisms (b) Nuclear materials (c) Hazardous chemicals in trade (d) Migratory birds
Answer: (a)
Solution: Hazardous chemicals in trade fall under the Rotterdam Convention, which is the tempting confusion; the Cartagena Protocol (2000) is a biosafety protocol to the CBD covering living modified organisms, so the answer is living modified organisms.
Source: CBD official site (cbd.int)

112. The Rotterdam Convention is based on which principle for trade in hazardous chemicals?
(a) Polluter pays principle (b) Common but differentiated responsibilities (c) Prior Informed Consent (d) Precautionary emissions trading
Answer: (c)
Solution: Polluter pays and CBDR are general environmental principles, not the operational mechanism of this convention; the Rotterdam Convention works through the Prior Informed Consent procedure, so the answer is Prior Informed Consent.
Source: Rotterdam Convention official site (pic.int)

113. The Vienna Convention (1985) is significant because it:
(a) Set binding targets for CFC reduction (b) Banned DDT globally (c) Provided the framework for protecting the ozone layer (d) Established the carbon credit market
Answer: (c)
Solution: Binding CFC targets came under the Montreal Protocol, not Vienna — a frequent trap; the Vienna Convention is the framework convention under which the Montreal Protocol was made, so the answer is that it provided the framework for protecting the ozone layer.
Source: Ozone Secretariat, UNEP (ozone.unep.org)

114. The UNFCCC was adopted at Rio in 1992 and entered into force in:
(a) 1992 (b) 2005 (c) 1997 (d) 1994
Answer: (d)
Solution: 1992 is the adoption year, not the entry-into-force year — a chronological trap; the UNFCCC entered into force in March 1994, so the answer is 1994.
Source: UNFCCC official site (unfccc.int)

115. The Minamata Convention addresses pollution and health risks from:
(a) Lead (b) Arsenic (c) Mercury (d) Cadmium
Answer: (c)
Solution: Lead and cadmium are heavy metals but not the subject of this treaty, which tempts guesswork; the convention is named after Minamata disease in Japan, caused by mercury poisoning, so the answer is mercury.
Source: Minamata Convention official site (mercuryconvention.org)

116. The Bonn Convention (CMS) is concerned with the conservation of:
(a) Marine oil pollution (b) Migratory species of wild animals (c) Antarctic mineral resources (d) World heritage sites
Answer: (b)
Solution: World heritage sites fall under the 1972 UNESCO convention, which is a similar-name confusion; the Bonn Convention (1979) protects migratory species, so the answer is migratory species of wild animals.
Source: CMS official site (cms.int)

117. Under the Paris Agreement, countries submit their climate action plans known as:
(a) National Adaptation Programmes (b) Green Climate Pledges (c) National Emission Ceilings (d) Nationally Determined Contributions
Answer: (d)
Solution: National Adaptation Programmes exist under the UNFCCC but are not the core Paris mechanism, which is the trap; each party submits Nationally Determined Contributions, updated every five years, so the answer is Nationally Determined Contributions.
Source: UNFCCC official site (unfccc.int)

118. The first UN Conference on the Human Environment (1972) was held in:
(a) Geneva (b) Rio de Janeiro (c) Stockholm (d) Nairobi
Answer: (c)
Solution: Rio hosted the 1992 Earth Summit, which is the most common confusion; the landmark 1972 conference was held in Stockholm, Sweden, so the answer is Stockholm.
Source: UNEP official site (unep.org)

119. The concept of "Common But Differentiated Responsibilities" (CBDR) is a principle of:
(a) UNFCCC (b) Basel Convention (c) CITES (d) Ramsar Convention
Answer: (a)
Solution: Basel deals with waste trade and has no CBDR principle, which is the trap; CBDR recognizes that developed nations bear greater historical responsibility for emissions under the climate regime, so the answer is UNFCCC.
Source: UNFCCC official site (unfccc.int)

120. India ratified the Kigali Amendment to the Montreal Protocol in which year?
(a) 2021 (b) 2018 (c) 2016 (d) 2023
Answer: (a)
Solution: 2016 is when the amendment was adopted in Kigali, not when India ratified it — a chronological trap; India ratified the Kigali Amendment in September 2021, so the answer is 2021.
Source: Ministry of Environment, Forest and Climate Change, Government of India (moef.gov.in)

121. Consider the following statements:
1. The Montreal Protocol has been ratified by all UN member states, making it the first universally ratified treaty in UN history.
2. The Kigali Amendment treats HFCs as ozone-depleting substances.
Which of the statements given above is/are correct?
(a) Neither 1 nor 2 (b) 2 only (c) Both 1 and 2 (d) 1 only
Answer: (d)
Solution: Statement 2 is the trap — HFCs are powerful greenhouse gases but do not deplete ozone; they were included because they replaced CFCs. Statement 1 is correct as the Montreal Protocol achieved universal ratification, so the answer is 1 only.
Source: Ozone Secretariat, UNEP (ozone.unep.org)

122. Arrange the following conventions in chronological order of adoption:
1. Ramsar Convention
2. Stockholm Convention on POPs
3. Vienna Convention
4. Basel Convention
Select the correct order:
(a) 1-3-4-2 (b) 3-1-4-2 (c) 1-4-3-2 (d) 3-4-1-2
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is swapping Vienna (1985) and Basel (1989); the correct sequence is Ramsar 1971, Vienna 1985, Basel 1989, Stockholm 2001, so the answer is 1-3-4-2.
Source: Official convention websites (ramsar.org, ozone.unep.org, basel.int, pops.int)

123. Consider the following pairs:
1. Cartagena Protocol — Biosafety
2. Nagoya Protocol — Access and benefit sharing
3. Aarhus Convention — Persistent organic pollutants
Which of the pairs given above is/are correctly matched?
(a) 1 and 2 only (b) 2 and 3 only (c) 1 and 3 only (d) 1, 2 and 3
Answer: (a)
Solution: Pair 3 is the trap — the Aarhus Convention concerns access to information, public participation and justice in environmental matters, while POPs belong to the Stockholm Convention; pairs 1 and 2 are correct, so the answer is 1 and 2 only.
Source: CBD official site (cbd.int); UNECE (unece.org)

124. Consider the following statements about the Paris Agreement:
1. It entered into force in November 2016.
2. It requires all countries to accept legally binding absolute emission reduction targets set by the UN.
Which of the statements given above is/are correct?
(a) 2 only (b) 1 only (c) Both 1 and 2 (d) Neither 1 nor 2
Answer: (b)
Solution: Statement 2 is the trap — the Paris Agreement is built on self-determined NDCs, not UN-imposed absolute targets, unlike the Kyoto approach for developed countries; it did enter into force on 4 November 2016, so the answer is 1 only.
Source: UNFCCC official site (unfccc.int)

125. Match the following conventions with their headquarters/secretariat locations:
1. Basel Convention — Geneva
2. CITES — Geneva
3. UNFCCC — Bonn
4. Ramsar Convention — Gland
Which of the above are correctly matched?
(a) 1, 2 and 3 only (b) 2, 3 and 4 only (c) 1, 2, 3 and 4 (d) 1, 3 and 4 only
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is assuming all environment secretariats sit in Geneva; in fact Basel and CITES are in Geneva, UNFCCC in Bonn, and Ramsar in Gland (Switzerland), so all four are correctly matched and the answer is 1, 2, 3 and 4.
Source: Official convention websites (basel.int, cites.org, unfccc.int, ramsar.org)
