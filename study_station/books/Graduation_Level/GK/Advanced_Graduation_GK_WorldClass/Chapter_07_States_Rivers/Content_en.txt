# States & Rivers

## 🌊 The Hook: A River That Changed Its Mind

In 2008, the Kosi river in Bihar suddenly shifted its course by over 100 kilometres, flooding five districts and displacing nearly 3 million people. Newspapers called it the "Sorrow of Bihar" — a title the river has carried for over a century. But here's what most aspirants miss: the Kosi didn't "break" its banks. It simply did what rivers in the Himalayan plains have always done — it wandered. Understanding *why* rivers behave the way they do, and *which state* they flow through, is exactly what examiners test you on, year after year.

Rivers are not just geography. They are the reason states were named, cities were built, and civilisations survived. Master this chapter, and you master one of the most frequently asked areas of Indian Geography.

## 1. The Two Great River Systems

Indian rivers are divided into two families:

**Himalayan Rivers** — Indus, Ganga, Brahmaputra (and their tributaries)
- **Perennial**: fed by glaciers AND monsoon rain, so they flow all year.
- Young, fast, and carry enormous silt — they build deltas and flood plains.
- They form **gorges** in the mountains and **meanders** in the plains.

**Peninsular Rivers** — Godavari, Krishna, Kaveri, Mahanadi, Narmada, Tapi, etc.
- Mostly **seasonal**: dependent on monsoon rain only.
- Older, slower, with shallow valleys.
- Most flow **east** into the Bay of Bengal; the famous exceptions — **Narmada and Tapi** — flow **west** into the Arabian Sea through rift valleys.

## 2. The Ganga System — The Examiner's Favourite

**Origin:** The Bhagirathi rises from the **Gangotri Glacier (Gaumukh), Uttarakhand**. At **Devprayag**, the Bhagirathi meets the **Alaknanda** — and only from here is the river called **Ganga**.

**The Five Prayags (Panch Prayag) of Uttarakhand — in order from upstream:**
1. **Vishnuprayag** — Alaknanda + Dhauliganga
2. **Nandprayag** — Alaknanda + Nandakini
3. **Karnaprayag** — Alaknanda + Pindar
4. **Rudraprayag** — Alaknanda + Mandakini
5. **Devprayag** — Alaknanda + Bhagirathi → **Ganga is born**

🪄 **Mnemonic:** "**V**ery **N**aughty **K**ids **R**un **D**aily" — Vishnuprayag, Nandprayag, Karnaprayag, Rudraprayag, Devprayag.

**States the Ganga flows through:** Uttarakhand → Uttar Pradesh → Bihar → Jharkhand (touches) → West Bengal. In Bangladesh it joins the Brahmaputra and is called the **Padma**.

**Major left-bank tributaries** (from the Himalayas): Ramganga, Gomti, Ghaghara, Gandak, Kosi.
**Major right-bank tributaries** (from the Peninsula): Yamuna (the largest tributary), Son, Chambal–Betwa–Ken (via Yamuna).

**Key facts examiners love:**
- **Yamuna** meets Ganga at **Prayagraj (Triveni Sangam)** — with the mythical Saraswati.
- **Kosi** = "Sorrow of Bihar" (frequent course changes).
- **Damodar** = "Sorrow of Bengal" (a tributary of the Hooghly system).
- **Hooghly** is the distributary of Ganga on which **Kolkata** stands; the main stream continues as Padma.
- **Farakka Barrage** (West Bengal) diverts Ganga water into the Hooghly.

🧠 **Active Recall:** At which prayag does the Ganga get its name? Which tributary is the largest? What is the Ganga called in Bangladesh?

## 3. The Indus System — The River That Gave India Its Name

**Origin:** Near **Lake Mansarovar** (Tibet), from the Bokhar Chu glacier. Flows through **Ladakh (Leh lies on its bank)**, then enters Pakistan and empties into the Arabian Sea near Karachi.

**The five tributaries that gave Punjab its name (Punj = five, ab = water):**
- **Jhelum** — from Verinag spring (J&K)
- **Chenab** — formed by Chandra + Bhaga at Tandi (Himachal Pradesh); the **largest** of the five
- **Ravi** — from Rohtang Pass region (Himachal Pradesh)
- **Beas** — from Beas Kund near Rohtang Pass; joins the **Sutlej at Harike (Punjab)**
- **Sutlej** — from Rakshastal near Mansarovar (Tibet); enters India through the Shipki La pass

🪄 **Mnemonic:** "**J**ust **C**rossed **R**iver **B**y **S**wimming" — Jhelum, Chenab, Ravi, Beas, Sutlej.

**Indus Waters Treaty (1960):** Brokered by the World Bank between India and Pakistan. India got exclusive rights over the **eastern rivers — Ravi, Beas, Sutlej**; Pakistan over the western rivers — Indus, Jhelum, Chenab (with limited use rights for India).

🧠 **Active Recall:** Which three rivers were allotted to India under the 1960 treaty? Where does the Beas join the Sutlej?

## 4. The Brahmaputra — The Male River

**Origin:** Chemayungdung Glacier near **Lake Mansarovar (Tibet)**, where it is called **Tsangpo**. It takes a dramatic U-turn (the "Great Bend") around **Namcha Barwa** peak, enters Arunachal Pradesh as the **Siang/Dihang**, and becomes the **Brahmaputra** in Assam after the Dibang and Lohit join it.

**States:** Arunachal Pradesh → Assam → (Bangladesh, where it is called **Jamuna**) → joins the Ganga (Padma) → the combined stream is the **Meghna**.

**Special facts:**
- Brahmaputra means "son of Brahma" — considered the only "male" river in India.
- **Majuli Island** (Assam) on the Brahmaputra is among the world's largest river islands.
- It carries more water than the Ganga during monsoon and causes severe Assam floods.

## 5. The Great Peninsular Rivers

| River | Origin | States (river course) | Mouth | Nickname / Note |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Godavari** | Trimbakeshwar, Nashik (Maharashtra) | Maharashtra, Telangana, Andhra Pradesh | Bay of Bengal | "Dakshin Ganga" / "Vriddha Ganga" — longest Peninsular river |
| **Krishna** | Mahabaleshwar (Maharashtra) | Maharashtra, Karnataka, Telangana, Andhra Pradesh | Bay of Bengal | Koyna, Tungabhadra, Bhima tributaries |
| **Kaveri** | Talakaveri, Brahmagiri Hills (Karnataka) | Karnataka, Tamil Nadu | Bay of Bengal | "Ganga of the South"; Hogenakkal & Shivanasamudra falls |
| **Mahanadi** | Sihawa, Chhattisgarh | Chhattisgarh, Odisha | Bay of Bengal | Hirakud Dam — one of the world's longest earthen dams |
| **Narmada** | Amarkantak (Madhya Pradesh) | MP, Maharashtra, Gujarat | Gulf of Khambhat (Arabian Sea) | Flows west through a rift valley; Sardar Sarovar Dam |
| **Tapi (Tapti)** | Betul, Madhya Pradesh | MP, Maharashtra, Gujarat | Gulf of Khambhat | Second west-flowing river; Surat lies on its bank |
| **Mahi** | Vindhyas, MP | MP, Rajasthan, Gujarat | Gulf of Khambhat | Crosses the Tropic of Cancer twice |
| **Luni** | Aravalli (near Ajmer, Rajasthan) | Rajasthan | Rann of Kutch | Saline "Salt River"; doesn't reach the sea properly |

⚠️ **Note:** The Godavari *basin* extends into Chhattisgarh, Odisha, Madhya Pradesh and Puducherry (Karaikal), but the river itself flows only through Maharashtra, Telangana and Andhra Pradesh. Examiners love to test this difference between "basin states" and "course states."

**East-flowing vs West-flowing — remember:** Only **Narmada and Tapi** are major west-flowing rivers that form **estuaries** (not deltas). All major east-flowing rivers form **deltas**.

🧠 **Active Recall:** Which river is called Dakshin Ganga? Which river crosses the Tropic of Cancer twice? Why do Narmada and Tapi form estuaries instead of deltas?

## 🔗 Static-Dynamic Link

River interlinking and water disputes keep this static topic in the news. The **Ken–Betwa Link Project** (connecting two Yamuna tributaries across MP and UP) is India's first river-interlinking project under implementation. The **Kaveri water dispute** between Karnataka and Tamil Nadu, and the **Indus Waters Treaty** discussions between India and Pakistan, regularly appear in current affairs — but the underlying rivers, tributaries, and treaty provisions are pure static GK. ## ⚠️ Caution: Examiner's Trap

**Trap 1 — Almost-Correct Statement (लगभग सही कथन):**
"The Ganga is formed at Devprayag by the confluence of the Alaknanda and the Mandakini."
→ 90% correct — but the Mandakini joins at **Rudraprayag**. At Devprayag it is the **Bhagirathi**. One swapped name makes the whole statement wrong.

**Trap 2 — Similar Names (समान नाम):**
- **Mandakini** (joins Alaknanda at Rudraprayag) vs **Mandakini** of Chitrakoot (a different river in MP/UP).
- The **Ghaghara** is known as the **Sarayu** in its **lower reaches** in Uttar Pradesh — and **Ayodhya stands on this very river**. Don't be fooled into treating Ghaghara and Sarayu as two unrelated rivers in "same/different" questions.
- **Tapi** (Tapti) vs **Tungabhadra** — both start with T, but one flows west, the other joins the Krishna.

**Trap 3 — Negative Phrasing (नकारात्मक वाक्यांश):**
"Which of the following rivers does NOT flow through Madhya Pradesh?" — Options: Narmada, Tapi, Chambal, **Kaveri**. Under time pressure, candidates pick a river that *does* flow through MP. Always underline the NOT.

**Trap 4 — Reverse Direction (विपरीत दिशा):**
"Narmada flows eastward and forms a delta on the Bay of Bengal." — Sounds plausible, but Narmada flows **west** and forms an **estuary** on the Gulf of Khambhat. Direction + landform is a classic double-trap.
