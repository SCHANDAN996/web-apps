# 📊 PYQ Analysis — States & Rivers

## 1. How Exams Ask Questions from This Chapter

Across SSC (CGL/CHSL/GD), Banking (IBPS/SBI), Railway (NTPC/Group D) and State PCS prelims, questions from "States & Rivers" follow a small set of repeatable patterns. The examiner rarely asks a plain "which river flows through X?" — instead, the same fact is wrapped in a trap. The recurring question families are:

- **River–State matching**: "Which of the following pairs is correctly matched?" — one pair is deliberately wrong (e.g., Tungabhadra–Tamil Nadu instead of Karnataka/Andhra Pradesh).
- **Origin and mouth**: source glaciers (Gangotri, Yamunotri, Siachen/Nubra region for the Indus system) and where rivers empty (Bay of Bengal vs Arabian Sea).
- **Left-bank vs right-bank tributaries**: especially of the Ganga (Yamuna, Son — right bank; Kosi, Gandak, Ghaghara — left bank) and the Godavari.
- **River ordering**: longest river *within* India (Ganga), east-flowing vs west-flowing (Narmada, Tapi into the Arabian Sea).
- **Dams and cities on banks**: Hirakud (Mahanadi, Odisha), Sardar Sarovar (Narmada), Nagarjuna Sagar (Krishna); cities like Varanasi (Ganga), Nashik (Godavari), Jabalpur (Narmada), Surat (Tapi).
- **Inter-state rivers and disputes**: Cauvery (Karnataka–Tamil Nadu), Krishna, Ravi–Beas — asked as "which states are involved?"
- **National Waterways**: NW-1 (Ganga, Prayagraj–Haldia) is a favourite.
- **Statement-based questions** (PCS style): two or three statements where one is "almost correct."

## 2. Sub-topics That Recur Most Often

Based on the pattern of past papers, examiners return again and again to:

1. **Ganga system and its tributaries** — the single most-tested cluster; left/right bank classification and the order of confluence (Prayagraj, etc.).
2. **Peninsular rivers** — Godavari ("Dakshin Ganga," longest peninsular river), Krishna and Cauvery rise in the Western Ghats and flow east; the Mahanadi rises in the Maikala range of Chhattisgarh. Examiners exploit exactly this difference in origin.
3. **West-flowing rivers** — Narmada and Tapi flowing through rift valleys into the Gulf of Khambhat; estuary vs delta is a classic discriminator.
4. **Indus system** — Indus, Jhelum, Chenab, Ravi, Beas, Sutlej; the Indus Waters Treaty (1960) allocation.
5. **Brahmaputra system** — entry into India as the Dihang/Siang, Majuli river island, tributaries like Subansiri, Lohit, Teesta.
6. **Dams, cities, waterfalls on rivers** — Jog Falls (Sharavathi), Hogenakkal (Cauvery), Dhuandhar (Narmada).

## 3. Exam-wise Emphasis (in words, not numbers)

- **State PCS**: statement-based and conceptual — drainage patterns, estuary vs delta, river linking, inter-state disputes. Fewer direct one-liners.
- **SSC CGL / CHSL**: direct factual one-liners — origin, tributary, dam, city on bank. Speed matters most here.
- **Banking (IBPS/SBI)**: rivers appear inside static GK — national waterways, dams, and rivers in the news (disputes, projects).
- **Railway (NTPC/Group D)**: simplest level — "Which river is called the Sorrow of Bihar?" (Kosi), "On which river is Delhi situated?" (Yamuna).

## 4. The Examiner's Traps (Jaal-Prakaar)

| Trap type | How it looks | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Almost Correct** | One word changed in a true statement | "The Narmada forms a delta at its mouth" — false; it forms an *estuary* |
| **Chronological Confusion** | Order of confluences or events swapped | Yamuna joining Ganga *before* Ghaghara — correct; reversing the order is the trap |
| **Similar Names** | Gandak vs Ghaghara, Tapi vs Tungabhadra, Mahanadi vs Mahananda | "Tungabhadra is a tributary of the Godavari" — false; it joins the *Krishna* |
| **Negative Phrasing** | "Which of the following is NOT…" | "Which is NOT a west-flowing river?" — students misread and pick Narmada |
| **Superlative Swap** | Longest/largest interchanged | "Godavari is the longest river of India" — false; longest *peninsular* river |
| **Geography Flip** | Left bank ↔ right bank, east ↔ west flowing, wrong origin | "Kosi is a right-bank tributary of the Ganga" — false; it is left-bank. "The Mahanadi rises in the Western Ghats" — false; it rises in Chhattisgarh |

## 5. The 15/45-Second Rule

- **15 seconds**: direct recall questions — origin, dam, city on bank, "Sorrow of…" nicknames. If you know it, mark it; if not, skip immediately. Do not re-read.
- **45 seconds**: matching lists, statement-based, and negative-phrasing questions. Budget the full time to check *each* option against the trap table above. Never answer a "NOT" question in under 30 seconds — misreading the negation is the costliest error in this chapter.

## 6. Ten High-Yield PYQ-Style Questions with Solutions

**Q1.** Which of the following rivers flows through a rift valley?
(a) Godavari (b) Narmada (c) Krishna (d) Mahanadi
**Answer:** (b)
**Solution:** The Narmada flows west through a rift valley between the Vindhya and Satpura ranges and empties into the Gulf of Khambhat. The other three are east-flowing rivers with deltas. *Trap: Superlative/geography flip — students pick Godavari because it is the longest peninsular river.*
**Source:** NCERT Class 9 Geography (Contemporary India I)

**Q2.** Which of the following is NOT a left-bank tributary of the Ganga?
(a) Kosi (b) Gandak (c) Ghaghara (d) Son
**Answer:** (d)
**Solution:** The Son joins the Ganga from the south (right bank), along with the Yamuna. Kosi, Gandak and Ghaghara are left-bank tributaries from the Himalayas. *Trap: Negative phrasing — misreading "NOT" leads to picking Kosi.*
**Source:** NCERT Class 11 Geography (India Physical Environment)

**Q3.** The Tungabhadra is a tributary of which river?
(a) Godavari (b) Cauvery (c) Krishna (d) Mahanadi
**Answer:** (c)
**Solution:** The Tungabhadra, formed by the Tunga and Bhadra streams in Karnataka, joins the Krishna. *Trap: Similar names — Tungabhadra is confused with Tapi or placed in the Godavari basin.*
**Source:** NCERT Class 11 Geography (India Physical Environment)

**Q4.** Which river is known as the "Sorrow of Bihar"?
(a) Gandak (b) Kosi (c) Ghaghara (d) Bagmati
**Answer:** (b)
**Solution:** The Kosi frequently shifts course and causes devastating floods in north Bihar, earning this nickname. (The Damodar was called the "Sorrow of Bengal.") *Trap: Similar names — Gandak and Bagmati also flood Bihar, but the standard nickname belongs to Kosi.*
**Source:** NCERT Class 11 Geography (India Physical Environment)

**Q5.** The Indus Waters Treaty (1960) allocated which three eastern rivers primarily to India?
(a) Indus, Jhelum, Chenab (b) Ravi, Beas, Sutlej
(c) Chenab, Ravi, Beas (d) Jhelum, Sutlej, Ravi
**Answer:** (b)
**Solution:** Under the 1960 treaty brokered by the World Bank, the eastern rivers Ravi, Beas and Sutlej were allocated to India; the western rivers Indus, Jhelum and Chenab largely to Pakistan. *Trap: Almost correct — option (c) mixes one western river (Chenab) with two eastern ones.*
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q6.** Which of the following pairs is correctly matched?
(a) Hirakud Dam — Godavari (b) Sardar Sarovar Dam — Narmada
(c) Nagarjuna Sagar Dam — Cauvery (d) Bhakra Nangal Dam — Ravi
**Answer:** (b)
**Solution:** Sardar Sarovar is on the Narmada in Gujarat. Hirakud is on the Mahanadi (Odisha), Nagarjuna Sagar on the Krishna, and Bhakra Nangal on the Sutlej. *Trap: Almost correct — every wrong option pairs a real dam with a neighbouring river.*
**Source:** NCERT Class 11 Geography (India Physical Environment)

**Q7.** Which is the longest peninsular river of India?
(a) Krishna (b) Narmada (c) Godavari (d) Mahanadi
**Answer:** (c)
**Solution:** The Godavari (about 1,465 km), rising at Trimbakeshwar near Nashik, is the longest peninsular river and is called the "Dakshin Ganga." *Trap: Superlative swap — "longest river of India" would be the Ganga; the qualifier "peninsular" is the key word.*
**Source:** NCERT Class 9 Geography (Contemporary India I)

**Q8.** The Brahmaputra enters India (Arunachal Pradesh) under which name?
(a) Tsangpo (b) Dihang (Siang) (c) Lohit (d) Subansiri
**Answer:** (b)
**Solution:** The river is the Tsangpo in Tibet; it enters Arunachal Pradesh as the Dihang (Siang) and becomes the Brahmaputra after the Dibang and Lohit join it. *Trap: Chronological/name confusion — Tsangpo is the Tibetan name, not the entry name in India.*
**Source:** NCERT Class 11 Geography (India Physical Environment)

**Q9.** National Waterway-1 (NW-1) runs on which river and between which places?
(a) Ganga — Prayagraj to Haldia (b) Brahmaputra — Dibrugarh to Sadiya
(c) Godavari — Nashik to Rajahmundry (d) Yamuna — Delhi to Prayagraj
**Answer:** (a)
**Solution:** NW-1 is the Prayagraj–Haldia stretch of the Ganga–Bhagirathi–Hooghly system, declared in 1986. NW-2 is on the Brahmaputra (Sadiya–Dhubri). *Trap: Similar names — option (b) reverses the NW-2 terminals and mislabels the waterway.*
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q10.** Consider the following statements:
1. The Narmada and Tapi form estuaries rather than deltas.
2. The Cauvery rises in the Brahmagiri hills of Karnataka.
3. The Chambal is a tributary of the Ganga.
Which of the above is/are correct?
(a) 1 and 2 only (b) 2 and 3 only (c) 1 only (d) 1, 2 and 3
**Answer:** (a)
**Solution:** Statements 1 and 2 are correct: west-flowing Narmada and Tapi form estuaries, and the Cauvery rises at Talakaveri in the Brahmagiri hills. Statement 3 is false — the Chambal joins the *Yamuna*, not the Ganga directly. *Trap: Almost correct — statement 3 is the classic single-word flip that converts "all correct" into a wrong answer. Use the full 45 seconds here.*
**Source:** NCERT Class 11 Geography (India Physical Environment)

## 7. Key Takeaways for the Exam Hall

- Read "NOT/EXCEPT" questions twice; underline the negation mentally before looking at options.
- For matching questions, eliminate the pair you are *sure* is wrong first — usually only one option survives.
- Memorise the left/right bank split of the Ganga and the Indus Waters Treaty allocation; these two facts alone answer a large share of trap questions.
- Estuary (Narmada, Tapi) vs delta (Ganga, Godavari, Krishna, Cauvery, Mahanadi) is the examiner's favourite discriminator — never confuse them.
- Remember the origin exception: Godavari, Krishna and Cauvery rise in the Western Ghats, but the Mahanadi rises in the Maikala range of Chhattisgarh.
