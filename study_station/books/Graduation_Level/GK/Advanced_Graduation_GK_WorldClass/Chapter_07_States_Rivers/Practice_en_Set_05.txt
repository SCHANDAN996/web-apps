101. Which river forms the boundary between Uttar Pradesh and Bihar for a considerable stretch?
(a) Ghaghara (b) Son (c) Kosi (d) Gandak
Answer: (a)
Solution: The Gandak and Kosi join the Ganga from the north but do not form a long state boundary, which is the tempting confusion. The Ghaghara (Sarayu) flows along the Uttar Pradesh–Bihar border for a long stretch before joining the Ganga near Chhapra, so the answer is Ghaghara.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Geography (India Physical Environment)

102. The Kosi river is known as the "Sorrow of Bihar" mainly because of —
(a) frequent floods and shifting of its course (b) its highly polluted water (c) its drying up in summer (d) construction of dams on it
Answer: (a)
Solution: Pollution and drying up are not the reason, though they sound plausible. The Kosi carries a huge silt load from Nepal, deposits it on its own bed, and repeatedly shifts westward, causing devastating floods — hence "Sorrow of Bihar", so the answer is frequent floods and shifting of its course.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Geography (India Physical Environment)

103. The Chambal river is a tributary of which river?
(a) Ganga (b) Son (c) Betwa (d) Yamuna
Answer: (d)
Solution: Many students mark Ganga because the Chambal's water ultimately reaches the Ganga system — that is the trap. The Chambal directly joins the Yamuna in Etawah district of Uttar Pradesh, so the answer is Yamuna.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Geography (India Physical Environment)

104. Which of the following rivers flows through a rift valley?
(a) Narmada (b) Godavari (c) Krishna (d) Kaveri
Answer: (a)
Solution: Godavari, Krishna and Kaveri flow over the Deccan plateau in normal graded valleys, which tempts students who think all peninsular rivers behave alike. The Narmada flows westward through a rift valley between the Vindhya and Satpura ranges, so the answer is Narmada.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Geography (India Physical Environment)

105. The river Brahmaputra is known as "Tsangpo" in —
(a) Tibet (b) Myanmar (c) Bangladesh (d) Bhutan
Answer: (a)
Solution: In Bangladesh the Brahmaputra is called the Jamuna, which is the tempting wrong option. In Tibet it flows eastward as the Tsangpo before taking a U-turn near Namcha Barwa to enter India, so the answer is Tibet.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Geography (India Physical Environment)

106. Which river is called the "Dakshin Ganga" (Ganga of the South)?
(a) Krishna (b) Kaveri (c) Mahanadi (d) Godavari
Answer: (d)
Solution: The Kaveri is often wrongly chosen because of its sacred status in the south. The Godavari, being the longest peninsular river with the largest peninsular basin, is called the Dakshin Ganga, so the answer is Godavari.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Geography (Contemporary India)

107. The Indus river originates near which lake?
(a) Tso Moriri (b) Rakshastal (c) Pangong Tso (d) Mansarovar
Answer: (d)
Solution: Rakshastal lies close to Mansarovar and confuses many candidates. The Indus rises from a glacier near Bokhar Chu in the Kailash range close to Lake Mansarovar in Tibet, so the answer is Mansarovar.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Geography (India Physical Environment)

108. Which of the following is a left-bank tributary of the Ganga?
(a) Yamuna (b) Son (c) Gandak (d) Chambal
Answer: (c)
Solution: The Yamuna, Son and Chambal all join the Ganga system from the right (southern) side, which is the trap. The Gandak rises in Nepal and joins the Ganga from the left near Patna, so the answer is Gandak.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Geography (India Physical Environment)

109. The Tapi (Tapti) river empties into the —
(a) Bay of Bengal (b) Palk Strait (c) Gulf of Mannar (d) Arabian Sea
Answer: (d)
Solution: Students who assume all major rivers drain into the Bay of Bengal pick that option. The Tapi is a west-flowing river that empties into the Arabian Sea through the Gulf of Khambhat, so the answer is Arabian Sea.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Geography (Contemporary India)

110. The Mahanadi river originates in which state?
(a) Odisha (b) Chhattisgarh (c) Jharkhand (d) Madhya Pradesh
Answer: (b)
Solution: Odisha is the tempting answer because the Mahanadi's delta and the Hirakud dam are there. The river actually rises near Sihawa in the Dhamtari district of Chhattisgarh and then flows into Odisha, so the answer is Chhattisgarh.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Geography (India Physical Environment)

111. The Bhakra Nangal dam is built on which river?
(a) Beas (b) Ravi (c) Sutlej (d) Chenab
Answer: (c)
Solution: The Beas is tempting because the Beas-Sutlej link project mixes the two names in memory. The Bhakra Nangal dam stands on the Sutlej in Himachal Pradesh, creating the Gobind Sagar reservoir, so the answer is Sutlej.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Geography (Contemporary India)

112. Which river forms the famous Jog (Gersoppa) Falls?
(a) Netravati (b) Kali (c) Sharavati (d) Tungabhadra
Answer: (c)
Solution: The Kali river of Karnataka is the confusing similar name. The Jog Falls, one of India's highest plunge waterfalls, is formed by the Sharavati river in Karnataka, so the answer is Sharavati.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Geography (Contemporary India)

113. The "Sorrow of Bengal" is the name given to which river?
(a) Hooghly (b) Teesta (c) Mahananda (d) Damodar
Answer: (d)
Solution: The Hooghly is tempting as Bengal's best-known river, but it is a distributary, not a flood menace of this scale. The Damodar, notorious for devastating floods in West Bengal before its valley corporation tamed it, is called the Sorrow of Bengal, so the answer is Damodar.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Geography (Contemporary India)

114. Which of the following rivers does NOT originate in India?
(a) Mahanadi (b) Godavari (c) Brahmaputra (d) Sabarmati
Answer: (c)
Solution: The Mahanadi, Godavari and Sabarmati all rise within India, so picking any of them is the trap. The Brahmaputra originates in Tibet (Chemayungdung glacier) and enters India through Arunachal Pradesh, so the answer is Brahmaputra.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Geography (India Physical Environment)

115. The Sardar Sarovar dam is located on which river and in which state?
(a) Tapi, Gujarat (b) Narmada, Madhya Pradesh (c) Narmada, Gujarat (d) Mahi, Rajasthan
Answer: (c)
Solution: Madhya Pradesh tempts because most of the Narmada flows there and the submergence affects it. The Sardar Sarovar dam itself is built on the Narmada at Kevadia in Gujarat, so the answer is Narmada, Gujarat.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Geography (Contemporary India)

116. Which river is known as the "Lifeline of Madhya Pradesh"?
(a) Chambal (b) Narmada (c) Betwa (d) Ken
Answer: (b)
Solution: The Chambal and Betwa are important but drain only parts of the state, which is the trap. The Narmada flows across the heart of Madhya Pradesh and is called its lifeline, so the answer is Narmada.
Source: PYQ-style

117. The confluence of the Alaknanda and Bhagirathi rivers takes place at —
(a) Rudraprayag (b) Karnaprayag (c) Devprayag (d) Vishnuprayag
Answer: (c)
Solution: Rudraprayag (Alaknanda–Mandakini) and Karnaprayag (Alaknanda–Pindar) are the classic confusing similar names. The Bhagirathi meets the Alaknanda at Devprayag, where the combined stream takes the name Ganga, so the answer is Devprayag.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Geography (India Physical Environment)

118. Which is the largest river island in India, located in the Brahmaputra?
(a) Majuli (b) Divar Island (c) Sagar Island (d) Srirangapatna
Answer: (a)
Solution: Srirangapatna is a tempting Kaveri island, but it is far smaller. Majuli in Assam, formed by the Brahmaputra, is India's largest river island, so the answer is Majuli.
Source: PYQ-style

119. The Hirakud dam, one of the longest dams in the world, is built on the —
(a) Mahanadi (b) Godavari (c) Krishna (d) Damodar
Answer: (a)
Solution: The Damodar tempts because of the Damodar Valley Corporation's many dams. The Hirakud dam stands on the Mahanadi near Sambalpur in Odisha, so the answer is Mahanadi.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Geography (Contemporary India)

120. Which of the following pairs is correctly matched?
(a) Mettur Dam – Godavari (b) Tehri Dam – Alaknanda (c) Nagarjuna Sagar – Krishna (d) Rihand Dam – Betwa
Answer: (c)
Solution: Tehri is on the Bhagirathi (not Alaknanda), Mettur is on the Kaveri, and Rihand is on the Rihand river — each is an almost-correct trap. Nagarjuna Sagar is correctly built on the Krishna, so the answer is Nagarjuna Sagar – Krishna.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Geography (Contemporary India)

121. Consider the following statements about the Narmada river:
1. It originates from the Amarkantak plateau.
2. It flows through a rift valley between the Vindhyas and the Satpuras.
3. It forms an estuary before meeting the sea.
Which of the statements given above are correct?
(a) 1 and 2 only (b) 1, 2 and 3 (c) 1 and 3 only (d) 2 and 3 only
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is statement 3 — students assume peninsular rivers form only deltas, but west-flowing rivers like the Narmada form estuaries, not deltas. All three statements are correct, so the answer is 1, 2 and 3.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Geography (India Physical Environment)

122. Arrange the following tributaries of the Ganga from west to east:
1. Gandak 2. Yamuna 3. Kosi 4. Son
(a) 4, 2, 1, 3 (b) 4, 1, 2, 3 (c) 2, 1, 4, 3 (d) 2, 4, 1, 3
Answer: (d)
Solution: The spatial confusion trap is placing the Son before the Yamuna; the Son joins the Ganga east of the Yamuna's confluence at Prayagraj. West to east the order is Yamuna, Son, Gandak, Kosi, so the answer is 2, 4, 1, 3.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Geography (India Physical Environment)

123. Which of the following statements about the Krishna river is NOT correct?
(a) It originates near Mahabaleshwar in Maharashtra (b) It flows through Maharashtra, Karnataka, Telangana and Andhra Pradesh (c) It forms a delta on the Bay of Bengal coast (d) It is a west-flowing river that ends in an estuary
Answer: (d)
Solution: The first three statements are all correct and lure students into picking one of them. The Krishna is an east-flowing river that forms a delta before entering the Bay of Bengal, so the claim that it is west-flowing is wrong — hence the answer is "It is a west-flowing river that ends in an estuary".
Source: NCERT Class 11 Geography (India Physical Environment)

124. Consider the following pairs:
1. Luni – inland drainage of Rajasthan
2. Ghaggar – seasonal river of Haryana
3. Mahi – crosses the Tropic of Cancer twice
Which of the pairs given above are correctly matched?
(a) 1 and 2 only (b) 1, 2 and 3 (c) 1 and 3 only (d) 2 and 3 only
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is pair 3 — students confuse the Mahi with the Narmada, but it is the Mahi that crosses the Tropic of Cancer twice. All three pairs are correctly matched, so the answer is 1, 2 and 3.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Geography (India Physical Environment)

125. The Indus Waters Treaty (1960) allocated the eastern rivers to India. Which set of rivers was allocated to India?
(a) Indus, Jhelum, Chenab (b) Ravi, Beas, Sutlej (c) Jhelum, Chenab, Ravi (d) Indus, Beas, Sutlej
Answer: (b)
Solution: The tempting wrong option lists the western rivers (Indus, Jhelum, Chenab), which were allocated to Pakistan — a classic almost-correct trap. India received unrestricted use of the eastern rivers Ravi, Beas and Sutlej, so the answer is Ravi, Beas, Sutlej.
Source: PYQ-style
