126. Consider the following statements about the Regulating Act of 1773:
1. It designated the Governor of Bengal as the Governor-General of Bengal.
2. It established a Supreme Court at Calcutta in 1774.
3. It subordinated the Governors of Bombay and Madras to the Governor-General in all matters without exception.
Which of the statements given above is/are correct?
(a) 1 and 3 only (b) 2 and 3 only (c) 1 and 2 only (d) 1, 2 and 3
Answer: (c)
Solution: Statement 3 is the tempting trap: the presidencies were subordinated only in matters of war and diplomacy, not in all matters, and they retained the right to appeal to the Court of Directors in emergencies. Statements 1 and 2 are correct — Warren Hastings became the first Governor-General of Bengal and the Supreme Court was established at Calcutta in 1774, so the answer is 1 and 2 only.
Source: NCERT Class 8 History (Our Pasts III)

127. The 'Subsidiary Alliance' system was most systematically and extensively used to expand British paramountcy by which Governor-General?
(a) Warren Hastings (b) Lord Wellesley (c) Lord Hastings (d) Lord Cornwallis
Answer: (b)
Solution: Cornwallis is a tempting wrong choice because he is associated with the Permanent Settlement, not Subsidiary Alliance. Although the system was used earlier by Dupleix and formalised in treaties before him, Lord Wellesley (1798–1805) used it most extensively, bringing Hyderabad, Awadh, the Maratha states and others under it, so the answer is Lord Wellesley.
Source: NCERT Class 8 History (Our Pasts III)

128. Arrange the following events in correct chronological order:
1. First Anglo-Mysore War
2. Battle of Buxar
3. First Anglo-Sikh War
4. Third Anglo-Maratha War
(a) 1, 2, 3, 4 (b) 2, 1, 4, 3 (c) 2, 4, 1, 3 (d) 1, 2, 4, 3
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is placing the First Anglo-Mysore War (1767–69) before Buxar; the Battle of Buxar was fought in 1764, before the Mysore war. The correct sequence is Buxar (1764), First Anglo-Mysore War (1767–69), Third Anglo-Maratha War (1817–18), First Anglo-Sikh War (1845–46), so the answer is 2, 1, 4, 3.

129. With reference to the Permanent Settlement of 1793, consider the following statements:
1. It was introduced by Lord Cornwallis in Bengal, Bihar and Orissa.
2. Under it, the zamindars were recognised as owners of the land.
3. The revenue demand fixed on the zamindars could be revised upwards by the government every ten years.
Which of the statements given above is/are correct?
(a) 2 and 3 only (b) 1, 2 and 3 (c) 1 only (d) 1 and 2 only
Answer: (d)
Solution: Statement 3 is the trap: the essence of the Permanent Settlement was that the state's revenue demand was fixed permanently, with no provision for periodic revision, which is why the government's share of rising agricultural income shrank over time. Statements 1 and 2 are correct — Cornwallis introduced it in Bengal, Bihar and Orissa and zamindars were made proprietors of the land, so the answer is 1 and 2 only.
Source: NCERT Class 8 History (Our Pasts III)

130. The Ryotwari system was first introduced on a large scale by Thomas Munro in which region?
(a) Madras Presidency (b) Bengal Presidency (c) Bombay Presidency (d) United Provinces
Answer: (a)
Solution: Bombay is a tempting wrong answer because the Ryotwari system was later extended there under Elphinstone and Wingate. Thomas Munro first introduced it in the Baramahal district and then extended it across the Madras Presidency around 1820, so the answer is Madras Presidency.

131. Consider the following pairs:

2. Arya Samaj — Swami Dayananda Saraswati, 1875
3. Ramakrishna Mission — Swami Vivekananda, 1897

Which of the pairs given above are correctly matched?
(a) 2, 3 and 4 only (b) 1, 2 and 3 only (c) 1, 2, 3 and 4 (d) 1 and 2 only
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap lies in confusing the Brahmo Samaj's founding year with the Atmiya Sabha (1815); the Brahmo Samaj itself was founded in 1828. All four pairs are correctly matched — Brahmo Samaj (1828), Arya Samaj (1875, Bombay), Ramakrishna Mission (1897) and the Aligarh Movement of Sir Syed Ahmad Khan, so the answer is 1, 2, 3 and 4.

132. Who among the following founded the Satyashodhak Samaj in 1873?
(a) Gopal Krishna Gokhale (b) Gopal Ganesh Agarkar (c) Mahadev Govind Ranade (d) Jyotiba Phule

134. The leader of the Revolt of 1857 at Kanpur was:
(a) Tantia Tope (b) Begum Hazrat Mahal (c) Kunwar Singh (d) Nana Saheb
Answer: (d)
Solution: Tantia Tope is the tempting trap because he fought alongside Nana Saheb and later recaptured Gwalior, but the leadership at Kanpur rested with Nana Saheb, the adopted son of Peshwa Baji Rao II. Kunwar Singh led in Bihar (Jagdishpur) and Begum Hazrat Mahal in Lucknow, so the answer is Nana Saheb.

135. Consider the following statements about the Government of India Act 1858:
1. It transferred the administration of India from the East India Company to the British Crown.
2. It created the office of the Secretary of State for India.
3. It abolished the office of the Governor-General and replaced it with that of the Viceroy.
Which of the statements given above is/are correct?

Answer: (b)
Solution: Statement 3 is the trap: the Act did not abolish the Governor-General's office; the Governor-General simply came to be additionally titled 'Viceroy' as the Crown's representative, and Canning became the first Viceroy. Statements 1 and 2 are correct, so the answer is 1 and 2 only.

136. The Indian National Congress was founded in 1885. Its first session was held at:
(a) Calcutta, presided over by W.C. Bonnerjee (b) Calcutta, presided over by Dadabhai Naoroji (c) Bombay, presided over by W.C. Bonnerjee (d) Bombay, presided over by A.O. Hume
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is naming A.O. Hume as president; Hume was the founder-general secretary, not the president of the first session. The first session was held at Gokuldas Tejpal Sanskrit College, Bombay, in December 1885 under the presidentship of W.C. Bonnerjee, so the answer is Bombay, presided over by W.C. Bonnerjee.

142. The Non-Cooperation Movement was withdrawn by Mahatma Gandhi in February 1922 after which incident?
(a) Chauri Chaura incident (b) Jallianwala Bagh massacre (c) Moplah rebellion (d) Bardoli Satyagraha
Answer: (a)
Solution: Bardoli is a tempting wrong answer because it is linked to the 1920s, but the Bardoli Satyagraha was a later peasant movement of 1928 led by Vallabhbhai Patel. Gandhi withdrew the Non-Cooperation Movement after a mob burnt a police station at Chauri Chaura (Gorakhpur district, UP) on 5 February 1922, killing 22 policemen, so the answer is Chauri Chaura incident.
Source: NCERT Class 12 History (Themes in Indian History III)

143. Consider the following statements about the Swaraj Party:
1. It was founded in 1923 by C.R. Das and Motilal Nehru.
2. Its aim was to enter the legislative councils and obstruct the government from within.
3. It was formed by leaders who opposed Gandhi's decision to launch the Civil Disobedience Movement.
Which of the statements given above is/are correct?
(a) 1 and 2 only (b) 2 and 3 only (c) 1 and 3 only (d) 1, 2 and 3
Answer: (a)
Solution: Statement 3 is the chronological trap: the Swaraj Party was formed in January 1923 after the withdrawal of Non-Cooperation, years before the Civil Disobedience Movement (1930) was even conceived. Statements 1 and 2 are correct — the 'pro-changers' Das and Motilal Nehru advocated council entry to wreck the reforms from within, so the answer is 1 and 2 only.

144. The Simon Commission (1928) was boycotted in India primarily because:
(a) It proposed the partition of Bengal (b) It recommended the abolition of dyarchy (c) It was headed by a Conservative politician (d) It had no Indian member
Answer: (d)
Solution: Abolition of dyarchy is a tempting trap — dyarchy was indeed abolished later, but by the Government of India Act 1935, not by the Simon Commission. The all-white commission under Sir John Simon was boycotted with the slogan 'Simon Go Back' because not a single Indian was included in a body meant to decide India's constitutional future, so the answer is that it had no Indian member.

146. The Poona Pact of 1932 was an agreement between Mahatma Gandhi and B.R. Ambedkar concerning:
(a) The partition of India (b) Separate electorates for Muslims (c) The abolition of untouchability (d) Reserved seats for Depressed Classes within the general electorate
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is 'separate electorates' — the Communal Award of Ramsay MacDonald had granted separate electorates to the Depressed Classes, which Gandhi opposed with a fast unto death. The Poona Pact replaced separate electorates with reserved seats for the Depressed Classes in joint electorates, so the answer is reserved seats for Depressed Classes within the general electorate.
Source: NCERT Class 12 History (Themes in Indian History III)

Solution: Statement 3 is the trap: the Constituent Assembly under the Cabinet Mission Plan was to be elected indirectly by the provincial legislative assemblies, not directly by the people.
