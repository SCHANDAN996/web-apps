101. The Treaty of Allahabad (1765) was signed between Robert Clive and which of the following?
(a) Shah Alam II and Shuja-ud-Daulah (b) Mir Jafar and Siraj-ud-Daulah (c) Mir Qasim and Balaji Baji Rao (d) Haider Ali and the Nizam of Hyderabad
Answer: (a)
Solution: Mir Jafar and Siraj-ud-Daulah belong to the earlier Plassey phase (1757), which is the tempting confusion. The Treaty of Allahabad followed Buxar and was concluded with the Mughal Emperor Shah Alam II and the Nawab of Awadh Shuja-ud-Daulah, granting the Company the Diwani of Bengal, so the answer is Shah Alam II and Shuja-ud-Daulah.
Source: NCERT Class 8 History (Our Pasts III)

102. The 'Doctrine of Lapse' was most vigorously applied during the tenure of which Governor-General?
(a) Lord Dalhousie (b) Lord Hastings (c) Lord Wellesley (d) Lord Canning
Answer: (a)
Solution: Wellesley is associated with Subsidiary Alliance, which is the similar-sounding trap. Under Dalhousie (1848–56), states like Satara, Jhansi and Nagpur were annexed on the ground of failure of natural heirs, so the answer is Lord Dalhousie.

103. The first session of the Indian National Congress (1885) was held at Bombay under the presidentship of:
(a) A. O. Hume (b) Surendranath Banerjee (c) Dadabhai Naoroji (d) W. C. Bonnerjee
Answer: (d)
Solution: A. O. Hume is the tempting wrong option because he founded the Congress, but he was its General Secretary, not the first president. The first session was presided over by Womesh Chandra Bonnerjee, so the answer is W. C. Bonnerjee.

Answer: (b)
Solution: 1909 tempts because of the Morley-Minto Reforms of that year, but the annulment was announced by King George V at the Delhi Durbar in December 1911, when the capital was also shifted to Delhi, so the answer is 1911.

106. The Lucknow Pact of 1916 was an agreement between the Congress and:
(a) The Hindu Mahasabha (b) The Depressed Classes (c) The Khilafat Committee (d) The Muslim League
Answer: (d)
Solution: The Khilafat Committee alliance came later (1920–22) and is the chronological trap. The 1916 Lucknow Pact brought the Congress and the Muslim League together and also reunited the Moderates and Extremists, so the answer is the Muslim League.

107. The Jallianwala Bagh massacre (13 April 1919) occurred during protests against which Act?
(a) The Vernacular Press Act (b) The Ilbert Bill (c) The Arms Act (d) The Rowlatt Act
Answer: (d)
Solution: The Vernacular Press Act (1878) and Arms Act (1878) are Lytton-era measures and form the chronological trap. The Amritsar gathering protested the repressive Rowlatt Act of 1919, so the answer is the Rowlatt Act.

109. The Chauri Chaura incident (1922) led Gandhi to withdraw which movement?
(a) The Khilafat Movement (b) The Non-Cooperation Movement (c) The Civil Disobedience Movement (d) The Quit India Movement
Answer: (b)
Solution: The Khilafat Movement ran parallel and is the tempting confusion, but after the killing of 22 policemen at Chauri Chaura on 5 February 1922, Gandhi suspended the Non-Cooperation Movement, so the answer is the Non-Cooperation Movement.

110. The Simon Commission (1928) was boycotted in India primarily because:
(a) It proposed the partition of Bengal (b) It had no Indian member (c) It recommended separate electorates (d) It abolished the Diwani rights
Answer: (b)
Solution: Separate electorates came with the 1909 Act and are the chronological trap. The all-white Simon Commission, sent to review the 1919 Act, contained no Indian member, provoking nationwide 'Simon Go Back' protests, so the answer is that it had no Indian member.

111. The Dandi March (1930) covered the distance from Sabarmati Ashram to Dandi in approximately how many days?
(a) 24 days (b) 10 days (c) 40 days (d) 60 days
Answer: (a)
Solution: The march began on 12 March 1930 and reached Dandi on 6 April 1930, a journey of about 240 miles in roughly 24 days; the round figures 10 and 40 are distractors, so the answer is 24 days.

113. The Government of India Act, 1935 introduced which of the following for the first time?
(a) Dyarchy in the provinces (b) Separate electorates for Muslims (c) Provincial autonomy (d) The office of the Viceroy
Answer: (c)
Solution: Dyarchy in provinces came with the 1919 Act — the classic chronological trap. The 1935 Act abolished provincial dyarchy and introduced provincial autonomy (1937 elections), so the answer is provincial autonomy.

116. The Cabinet Mission Plan (1946) proposed which structure for India?
(a) Complete partition into two sovereign states (b) A unitary state with provincial councils (c) A three-tier federation with a weak centre (d) Direct British rule for ten more years
Answer: (c)
Solution: Partition was the Mountbatten Plan (June 1947), the chronological trap. The Cabinet Mission rejected Pakistan and proposed a three-tier federation with the centre limited to defence, foreign affairs and communications, so the answer is a three-tier federation with a weak centre.

121. Consider the following events: P. Surat Split, Q. Partition of Bengal, R. Formation of the Muslim League, S. Morley-Minto Reforms. Which is the correct chronological order?
(a) Q, R, P, S (b) P, Q, R, S (c) R, Q, P, S (d) Q, P, R, S
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is placing the Surat Split (1907) before the Partition of Bengal (1905) because the Split is often discussed first in books. The correct order is: Partition of Bengal (1905), Muslim League (December 1906), Surat Split (1907), Morley-Minto Reforms (1909), so the answer is Q, R, P, S.
Source: NCERT Class 8 History (Our Pasts III)

122. Which of the following statements about the Regulating Act of 1773 is correct?
(a) It abolished the East India Company's trade monopoly completely (b) It introduced Dyarchy in the provinces (c) It transferred power from the Company to the British Crown (d) It designated the Governor of Bengal as Governor-General of Bengal and established a Supreme Court at Calcutta
Answer: (d)
Solution: The Crown takeover (1858) and Dyarchy (1919) are the classic almost-correct traps; the Charter Act of 1813, not 1773, ended the trade monopoly. The Regulating Act made the Bengal Governor the Governor-General (Warren Hastings being the first) and set up the Supreme Court at Calcutta in 1774, so the answer is that it designated the Governor of Bengal as Governor-General and established a Supreme Court at Calcutta.
Source: NCERT Class 8 History (Our Pasts III)

123. The 'Forward Bloc' was founded in 1939 by Subhas Chandra Bose after resigning from which position?
(a) Presidentship of the Indian National Congress (b) Chief Ministership of Bengal (c) Membership of the Viceroy's Executive Council (d) Editorship of 'The Statesman'
Answer: (a)
Solution: Bose never held the other offices listed, which are pure distractors. After defeating Gandhi's candidate Pattabhi Sitaramayya at Tripuri (1939) and failing to get cooperation, Bose resigned the Congress presidency and founded the Forward Bloc in May 1939, so the answer is Presidentship of the Indian National Congress.

124. Which of the following pairs is correctly matched?
(a) Permanent Settlement — Bombay Presidency (b) Ryotwari System — Bengal Presidency (c) Permanent Settlement — Madras Presidency (d) Mahalwari System — North-Western Provinces and Punjab
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is swapping the regions: Permanent Settlement (1793) applied to Bengal, Bihar and Orissa, while Ryotwari was introduced in Madras and Bombay. The Mahalwari system, a modified village-based settlement, operated in the North-Western Provinces, parts of Central India and Punjab, so the answer is Mahalwari System — North-Western Provinces and Punjab.
Source: NCERT Class 8 History (Our Pasts III)

125. Consider the following statements about the Revolt of 1857: P. It began at Meerut on 10 May 1857. Q. Bahadur Shah Zafar was proclaimed Emperor of India. R. Nana Saheb led the revolt at Jhansi. Which statements are correct?
(a) P and Q only (b) Q and R only (c) P and R only (d) P, Q and R
Answer: (a)
Solution: Statement R is the almost-correct trap: Nana Saheb led the revolt at Kanpur, while Jhansi was led by Rani Lakshmibai — the similar-name confusion examiners exploit. The revolt did begin at Meerut on 10 May 1857 and the sepoys proclaimed Bahadur Shah Zafar as Emperor, so the answer is P and Q only.
