# 📊 PYQ Analysis — Modern Indian History

## 1. How Exams Test This Chapter

Modern Indian History is one of the most dependable scoring areas across UPSC, SSC CGL/CHSL, IBPS, RRB NTPC and State PCS prelims. Examiners do not ask you to write essays — they test whether you can **distinguish between look-alike facts**: two similar sessions of the Congress, two similarly named newspapers, two leaders with overlapping roles, two events separated by only a year or two.

The questions cluster around a few repeatedly mined sub-topics:

- **Governor-Generals and their reforms/events** — who introduced what (Permanent Settlement, Subsidiary Alliance, Doctrine of Lapse, railways, postal system, Widow Remarriage Act).
- **The Revolt of 1857** — causes, centres and leaders, and why it failed.
- **The Indian National Congress** — foundation, early moderates vs extremists, famous sessions (Surat 1907, Lucknow 1916, Lahore 1929, Karachi 1931, Tripuri 1939) and their presidents.
- **Gandhian movements in sequence** — Champaran (1917), Kheda and Ahmedabad (1918), Rowlatt Satyagraha (1919), Non-Cooperation (1920–22), Civil Disobedience/Dandi (1930), Quit India (1942).
- **Revolutionary movement** — organisations (Anushilan Samiti, HSRA), and figures like Bhagat Singh, Chandrashekhar Azad, Surya Sen.
- **Newspapers and journals** — who founded which paper (Kesari, Young India, Harijan, Al-Hilal, New India, Commonweal).
- **Acts and constitutional developments** — 1773, 1784, 1858, 1861, 1892, 1909, 1919, 1935, 1947 — what each Act did.
- **Socio-religious reformers** — Brahmo Samaj, Arya Samaj, Ramakrishna Mission, Aligarh Movement, and their founders.
- **Partition and Independence** — Cripps Mission, Cabinet Mission, Mountbatten Plan, Radcliffe Line.

## 2. The Examiner's Traps (जाल)

**Trap 1 — Almost Correct Statement.** Four statements about one event; three are perfectly true, one has a single word altered — a wrong year, a wrong place, "first" instead of "second". Example pattern: "The Dandi March began from Sabarmati Ashram on 12 March 1930 and ended on 6 April 1930" — change one date and the statement becomes the trap.

**Trap 2 — Chronological Confusion.** "Arrange in chronological order: Surat Split, Lucknow Pact, Gandhi-Irwin Pact, Poona Pact." The events are close enough (1907, 1916, 1931, 1932) that a hazy memory fails. Examiners deliberately pick pairs only one year apart (Gandhi-Irwin Pact 1931 vs Poona Pact 1932).

**Trap 3 — Similar Names.** Kesari vs Maratha (both Tilak); Young India vs New India (Gandhi vs Annie Besant); Al-Hilal vs Comrade (Azad vs M.N. Roy). The options are built from the *other* leader's paper.

**Trap 4 — Negative Phrasing.** "Which of the following was **NOT** a cause of the Revolt of 1857?" or "Who did **NOT** participate in the Quit India Movement?" Under time pressure, candidates forget the "NOT" and pick the first true statement.

**Trap 5 — Founder vs Associated Person.** "Who founded the Ramakrishna Mission?" — Swami Vivekananda, not Ramakrishna Paramahamsa. The mission is *named after* one person but *founded by* another. Same pattern: Servants of India Society (Gokhale, not Gandhi), Forward Bloc (Subhas Chandra Bose, after leaving Congress).

**Trap 6 — Session vs President mismatch.** "The 1929 Lahore Session, where Purna Swaraj was declared, was presided over by —" Jawaharlal Nehru, not Gandhi or Patel. Examiners pair a famous session with its *less* famous president.

## 3. High-Yield PYQ-Style Questions with Solutions

**Q1.** The Permanent Settlement was introduced in Bengal by:
(a) Warren Hastings (b) Lord Cornwallis (c) Lord Wellesley (d) Lord Hastings
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** Lord Cornwallis introduced the Permanent Settlement in 1793. Trap type: **Similar Names** — option (d) "Lord Hastings" exploits confusion with Warren Hastings; Wellesley is the distractor for Subsidiary Alliance.
**Source:** NCERT Class 8 History (Our Pasts III)

**Q2.** Arrange in chronological order: (i) Champaran Satyagraha (ii) Dandi March (iii) Non-Cooperation Movement (iv) Quit India Movement
(a) i, iii, ii, iv (b) i, ii, iii, iv (c) iii, i, ii, iv (d) i, iii, iv, ii
**Answer: (a)**
**Solution:** Champaran 1917 → Non-Cooperation 1920 → Dandi 1930 → Quit India 1942. Trap type: **Chronological Confusion** — options (b) and (c) swap the middle two events.
**Source:** NCERT Class 8 History (Our Pasts III)

**Q3.** Who among the following did **NOT** preside over any session of the Indian National Congress?
(a) A.O. Hume (b) W.C. Bonnerjee (c) Surendranath Banerjee (d) Dadabhai Naoroji
**Answer: (a)**
**Solution:** A.O. Hume founded the Congress (1885) but never presided over a session. W.C. Bonnerjee presided over the first session (Bombay, 1885); Banerjee presided in 1895 and 1902; Naoroji in 1886, 1893 and 1906. Trap type: **Negative Phrasing + Founder vs President**.
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q4.** The newspaper 'Kesari' was started by:
(a) Mahatma Gandhi (b) Bal Gangadhar Tilak (c) Annie Besant (d) Gopal Krishna Gokhale
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** Tilak started Kesari (Marathi) and Maratha (English). Gandhi's papers were Young India and Harijan; Annie Besant's was New India. Trap type: **Similar Names**.
**Source:** NCERT Class 8 History (Our Pasts III)

**Q5.** The Doctrine of Lapse was applied most extensively under:
(a) Lord Dalhousie (b) Lord Canning (c) Lord Ripon (d) Lord Curzon
**Answer: (a)**
**Solution:** Dalhousie (1848–56) annexed Satara, Jhansi, Nagpur and others under the Doctrine of Lapse. Canning was the first Viceroy after 1858 — a frequent confusion. Trap type: **Almost Correct** (right era, wrong man).
**Source:** NCERT Class 8 History (Our Pasts III)

**Q6.** The Surat Split of the Indian National Congress occurred in:
(a) 1905 (b) 1906 (c) 1907 (d) 1908
**Answer: (c)**
**Solution:** The Congress split into Moderates and Extremists at the Surat Session of 1907. 1905 is the Partition of Bengal — the classic distractor. Trap type: **Chronological Confusion** (two famous events, two years apart).
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q7.** Who founded the Forward Bloc in 1939?
(a) Jawaharlal Nehru (b) Subhas Chandra Bose (c) Sardar Patel (d) C. Rajagopalachari
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** Bose founded the Forward Bloc after resigning the Congress presidency following the Tripuri crisis (1939). Trap type: **Founder vs Associated Person**.
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q8.** The Gandhi-Irwin Pact was signed in:
(a) 1930 (b) 1931 (c) 1932 (d) 1935
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** Signed on 5 March 1931, it suspended the Civil Disobedience Movement; Gandhi then attended the Second Round Table Conference. 1930 (Dandi March) and 1932 (Poona Pact) are the planted confusions. Trap type: **Chronological Confusion**.
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q9.** Which Act introduced dyarchy in the provinces?
(a) Government of India Act 1909 (b) Government of India Act 1919 (c) Government of India Act 1935 (d) Indian Councils Act 1892
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** The 1919 Act (Montagu-Chelmsford Reforms) introduced dyarchy in provinces; the 1935 Act introduced provincial autonomy and proposed a federation. Trap type: **Almost Correct** — 1919 vs 1935 is the most repeated Act-confusion in exams.
**Source:** NCERT Class 8 History (Our Pasts III)

**Q10.** The Radcliffe Line demarcated the boundary between India and:
(a) Pakistan (b) China (c) Myanmar (d) Afghanistan
**Answer: (a)**
**Solution:** Sir Cyril Radcliffe drew the boundary between India and Pakistan (including East Pakistan) in 1947. The Durand Line (Afghanistan) and McMahon Line (China) are the standard distractors. Trap type: **Similar Names** (boundary lines).
**Source:** PYQ-style

## 4. The 15/45-Second Rule in Action

**Applied to Q4 (Kesari):** This is a **15-second question**. The moment you see "Kesari", the answer "Tilak" should fire instantly — this is a pure memory pair. If you spend more than 15 seconds here, you are over-thinking; mark Tilak and move on. The examiner's trap only works on candidates who *hesitate* between Gandhi and Tilak.

**Applied to Q2 (chronology of Gandhian movements):** This is a **45-second question**. Do not rush. Write the four years in the margin — 1917, 1920, 1930, 1942 — then match to options. Candidates who answer from "feel" pick (b) because Dandi "feels" earlier than Non-Cooperation. The 45 seconds spent writing dates converts a gamble into a certainty.

**Rule of thumb:** single-fact recall (founder, year, newspaper) = 15 seconds; arrangement, multi-statement, or "NOT" questions = 45 seconds, and always re-read the stem once for the word "NOT" before marking.

## 5. What This Means for Your Preparation

- Master **paired facts** (person ↔ newspaper, session ↔ president, Act ↔ provision, movement ↔ year). Most questions are one half of a pair.
- Build a **personal chronology chain** of 1917–1942 Gandhian events and 1905–1919 nationalist events; chronology questions appear in some form in nearly every exam cycle.
- For every famous institution, learn the **founder separately from the person it is named after or associated with**.
- Practise reading stems twice whenever you see "NOT", "EXCEPT", or "incorrect" — negative phrasing is the cheapest trap the examiner sets.
