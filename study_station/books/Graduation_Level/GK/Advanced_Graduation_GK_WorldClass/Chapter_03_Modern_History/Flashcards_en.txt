Card 1
Front: Which event is called the "First War of Independence" by some historians, and who first popularised that label?
Back: The Revolt of 1857. V.D. Savarkar popularised the term "First War of Independence" in his 1909 book; British writers called it the "Sepoy Mutiny."

Card 2
Front: The Battle of Plassey (1757) is famous — but which later battle (1764) actually established British political control over Bengal, Bihar and Orissa?
Back: The Battle of Buxar (1764), where the English defeated the combined forces of Mir Qasim, Shuja-ud-Daula and Shah Alam II. It led to the Treaty of Allahabad (1765) and the Diwani rights of Bengal.

Card 3
Front: Who founded the Indian National Congress, and in which year and city was its first session held?
Back: A.O. Hume, a retired British civil servant, founded it in 1885. The first session was held in Bombay (Gokuldas Tejpal Sanskrit College), presided over by W.C. Bonnerjee.

Card 4
Front: Mnemonic trick: How can you quickly recall the order of the three major Gandhian mass movements — 1920, 1930, 1942?
Back: Remember "N-C-Q" → Non-Cooperation (1920), Civil Disobedience (1930, Dandi March), Quit India (1942). Trick: "Nehru Chacha Quit" — N, C, Q in sequence.

Card 5
Front: Examiner's trap: Was the "Doctrine of Lapse" introduced by Lord Dalhousie, or merely applied most aggressively by him?
Back: Trap! The doctrine was not invented by Dalhousie — it was an earlier East India Company policy, but Dalhousie (1848–56) applied it most extensively (Satara, Jhansi, Nagpur, Sambalpur). Options saying he "created" it are wrong.

Card 6
Front: Which 1905 event triggered the Swadeshi and Boycott movements, and who announced its reversal in 1911?
Back: The Partition of Bengal by Lord Curzon (announced July 1905, effective October 1905). Its annulment was announced by King George V at the Delhi Durbar of 1911.

Card 7
Front: Who was the first Indian to become a member of the British House of Commons, and which organisation did he co-found in India?
Back: Dadabhai Naoroji (elected 1892, Liberal, Finsbury Central). He co-founded the Indian National Congress and propounded the "Drain of Wealth" theory in "Poverty and Un-British Rule in India."

Card 8
Front: The "Safety Valve Theory" is associated with the founding of which organisation, and which leader is linked to this claim?
Back: The Indian National Congress (1885). The theory — that Congress was a British-sanctioned outlet for Indian discontent — is associated with Lala Lajpat Rai's criticism and later extremist/Marxist historians.

Card 9
Front: Examiner's trap: The Jallianwala Bagh massacre — who gave the firing order, and who was the Lieutenant Governor of Punjab whose name often confuses candidates?
Back: Brigadier-General Reginald Dyer ordered the firing (13 April 1919). The Lieutenant Governor was Sir Michael O'Dwyer — examiners swap "Dyer" and "O'Dwyer" deliberately. (O'Dwyer was later assassinated by Udham Singh in 1940.)

Card 10
Front: Which session of the Congress (1929) declared "Purna Swaraj" as its goal, and who presided over it?
Back: The Lahore Session, December 1929, presided over by Jawaharlal Nehru. 26 January 1930 was then observed as the first Independence Day.

Card 11
Front: Mnemonic trick: How to remember the sequence of Viceroys' major events — 1857 Revolt, Bengal Partition, Jallianwala Bagh?
Back: "Canning-Curzon-Chelmsford" in C-C-C order: Canning (1857 Revolt, last Governor-General/first Viceroy), Curzon (Partition of Bengal 1905), Chelmsford (Jallianwala Bagh 1919). Trick: "Crown Crushed Countrymen."

Card 12
Front: What was the immediate cause (greased cartridges aside) that sparked the Revolt of 1857 at Barrackpore, and who was the sepoy involved?
Back: Mangal Pandey, of the 34th Bengal Native Infantry, attacked British officers at Barrackpore on 29 March 1857 over the greased-cartridge issue; he was hanged on 8 April 1857.

Card 13
Front: Which Act of 1858 transferred power from the East India Company to the British Crown, and who became the first Viceroy?
Back: The Government of India Act 1858. Lord Canning became the first Viceroy of India.

Card 14
Front: Who gave the slogan "Swaraj is my birthright and I shall have it," and which two organisations did he found?
Back: Bal Gangadhar Tilak. He founded the Deccan Education Society (1884) and the Home Rule League (1916, at Poona) — Annie Besant founded the other Home Rule League at Madras the same year.

Card 15
Front: Examiner's trap: The "Two-Nation Theory" — was it first articulated in the 1940 Lahore Resolution?
Back: Trap! The Lahore Resolution (1940) demanded separate states, but the two-nation idea was articulated earlier — notably by Sir Syed Ahmad Khan and in M.A. Jinnah's presidential address at Lahore itself. Also, the 1940 resolution never used the word "Pakistan."

Card 16
Front: Which revolutionary threw a bomb in the Central Legislative Assembly in 1929 "to make the deaf hear," and with whom?
Back: Bhagat Singh and Batukeshwar Dutt (8 April 1929). They deliberately threw the bomb away from people and courted arrest to use the trial as a propaganda platform.

Card 17
Front: What were the three members of the Cabinet Mission (1946), and what was its key proposal?
Back: Pethick-Lawrence, Stafford Cripps and A.V. Alexander. It proposed a three-tier federation with grouping of provinces and rejected a fully sovereign Pakistan; its failure led to Direct Action Day (16 August 1946).

Card 18
Front: Which movement did Gandhi launch after the failure of the Cripps Mission, and what was its famous slogan?
Back: The Quit India Movement (August 1942), launched after Cripps' failure (March 1942). Slogan: "Do or Die" (Karo ya Maro), given at the Bombay session (Gowalia Tank Maidan).

Card 19
Front: Who was the first President of the Indian National Congress, and who presided over the 1907 Surat Session where the party split?
Back: W.C. Bonnerjee (1885, Bombay). The Surat Split (1907) occurred during the session presided over by Rash Behari Ghosh — the Moderates and Extremists separated.

Card 20
Front: Which Governor-General introduced the Permanent Settlement (1793), and in which region?
Back: Lord Cornwallis introduced the Permanent Settlement in Bengal, Bihar and Orissa — fixing land revenue permanently with zamindars as owners.
