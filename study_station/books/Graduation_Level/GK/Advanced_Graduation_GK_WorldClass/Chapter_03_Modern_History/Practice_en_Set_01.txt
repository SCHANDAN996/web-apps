1. Who founded the Brahmo Sabha in 1828, later renamed Brahmo Samaj?
(a) Debendranath Tagore (b) Keshab Chandra Sen (c) Raja Rammohan Roy (d) Ishwar Chandra Vidyasagar
Answer: (c)
Solution: Debendranath Tagore is the tempting wrong option because he led the Samaj after Roy and gave it a formal shape, which makes candidates confuse founder with successor. Raja Rammohan Roy founded the Brahmo Sabha in 1828, so the answer is Raja Rammohan Roy.
Source: NCERT Class 8 History (Our Pasts III)

2. The Revolt of 1857 began at which cantonment on 10 May 1857?
(a) Barrackpore (b) Meerut (c) Kanpur (d) Lucknow
Answer: (b)
Solution: Barrackpore is the trap because Mangal Pandey's incident happened there in March 1857, but that was a precursor, not the start of the general revolt. The sepoys of Meerut rose on 10 May 1857 and marched to Delhi, so the answer is Meerut.
Source: NCERT Class 8 History (Our Pasts III)

3. Who was the first Governor-General of independent India?
(a) C. Rajagopalachari (b) Lord Mountbatten (c) Jawaharlal Nehru (d) Dr. Rajendra Prasad
Answer: (b)
Solution: C. Rajagopalachari is the trap because he was the first Indian and last Governor-General, not the first of independent India. Lord Mountbatten served as the first Governor-General of independent India from August 1947, so the answer is Lord Mountbatten.
Source: NCERT Class 8 History (Our Pasts III)

4. The Swadeshi Movement was launched primarily in response to which event?
(a) Jallianwala Bagh massacre (b) Partition of Bengal in 1905 (c) Rowlatt Act of 1919 (d) Simon Commission of 1928
Answer: (b)
Solution: The Rowlatt Act is the trap because it triggered the nationwide satyagraha of 1919, but the Swadeshi Movement began fourteen years earlier. Lord Curzon's partition of Bengal in 1905 provoked the Swadeshi and Boycott movements, so the answer is Partition of Bengal in 1905.
Source: NCERT Class 8 History (Our Pasts III)

5. Who gave the slogan "Swaraj is my birthright and I shall have it"?
(a) Aurobindo Ghosh (b) Bipin Chandra Pal (c) Lala Lajpat Rai (d) Bal Gangadhar Tilak
Answer: (d)
Solution: Bipin Chandra Pal is the trap because he was part of the Lal-Bal-Pal extremist trio, so candidates often mix up their slogans. The famous declaration on swaraj belongs to Bal Gangadhar Tilak, so the answer is Bal Gangadhar Tilak.

6. The Indian National Congress was founded in which year?
(a) 1919 (b) 1857 (c) 1905 (d) 1885
Answer: (d)
Solution: 1857 is the trap because candidates associate the birth of nationalism with the revolt year. The Congress was founded in 1885 with A.O. Hume's initiative and W.C. Bonnerjee as its first president, so the answer is 1885.

7. Who was known as the "Father of the Indian Renaissance"?
(a) Raja Rammohan Roy (b) Swami Dayananda Saraswati (c) Swami Vivekananda (d) Henry Derozio
Answer: (a)
Solution: Swami Vivekananda is the trap because of his towering role in reviving Indian thought, but he belongs to a later generation. Raja Rammohan Roy is conventionally called the Father of the Indian Renaissance, so the answer is Raja Rammohan Roy.

8. The Jallianwala Bagh massacre took place on which date?
(a) 23 March 1931 (b) 6 April 1919 (c) 13 April 1919 (d) 10 April 1919
Answer: (c)
Solution: 23 March 1931 is the trap because it is the martyrdom date of Bhagat Singh, Rajguru and Sukhdev, often confused in date-based questions. General Dyer's troops fired on the gathering at Amritsar on Baisakhi day, 13 April 1919, so the answer is 13 April 1919.
Source: NCERT Class 8 History (Our Pasts III)

9. Who founded the Arya Samaj in 1875?
(a) Swami Shraddhanand (b) Swami Dayananda Saraswati (c) Lala Hansraj (d) Mahatma Hansraj
Answer: (b)
Solution: Swami Shraddhanand is the trap because of the similar honorific "Swami" and his later leadership of Arya Samaj-linked movements like Shuddhi. The Arya Samaj was founded by Swami Dayananda Saraswati at Bombay in 1875, so the answer is Swami Dayananda Saraswati.

10. The Non-Cooperation Movement was withdrawn by Gandhi after which incident?
(a) Kakori robbery (b) Jallianwala Bagh (c) Chauri Chaura (d) Dandi March
Answer: (c)
Solution: Kakori is the trap because it was a violent revolutionary incident of 1925, but it occurred after the movement had already ended. The mob violence at Chauri Chaura in February 1922, where policemen were burnt alive, led Gandhi to suspend the movement, so the answer is Chauri Chaura.
Source: NCERT Class 8 History (Our Pasts III)

11. Who was the first President of the Indian National Congress?
(a) Dadabhai Naoroji (b) A.O. Hume (c) W.C. Bonnerjee (d) Surendranath Banerjee
Answer: (c)
Solution: A.O. Hume is the classic trap because he founded the Congress, and candidates assume the founder was also the first president. The first session at Bombay in 1885 was presided over by W.C. Bonnerjee, so the answer is W.C. Bonnerjee.

12. The Dandi March of 1930 was undertaken to break which law?
(a) Rowlatt Act (b) Arms Act (c) Vernacular Press Act (d) Salt Law
Answer: (d)
Solution: The Rowlatt Act is the trap because it is the most famous repressive law of the era, but Gandhi's 1930 march targeted the salt tax. Gandhi marched from Sabarmati to Dandi to break the Salt Law, so the answer is Salt Law.

13. Who founded the Ramakrishna Mission in 1897?

Answer: (d)
Solution: Ramakrishna Paramahamsa is the trap because the Mission bears his name, but he died in 1886 and never founded an organisation. His disciple Swami Vivekananda founded the Ramakrishna Mission in 1897, so the answer is Swami Vivekananda.

14. The "Doctrine of Lapse" was associated with which Governor-General?
(a) Lord Wellesley (b) Lord Hastings (c) Lord Canning (d) Lord Dalhousie
Answer: (d)
Solution: Lord Wellesley is the trap because he too expanded British power aggressively, but through the Subsidiary Alliance system. The Doctrine of Lapse, annexing states without a natural male heir, was applied by Lord Dalhousie, so the answer is Lord Dalhousie.

Solution: Rabindranath Tagore is the trap because of his association with national songs, but he composed "Jana Gana Mana", not "Vande Mataram". Bankim Chandra Chatterjee wrote Anandamath in 1882, so the answer is Bankim Chandra Chatterjee.

21. Consider the following statements about the Cabinet Mission Plan of 1946:
1. It proposed a three-tier federation with grouping of provinces.
2. It provided for the partition of India into two dominions.
3. It rejected the demand for a fully sovereign Pakistan.
Which of the statements given above is/are correct?
(a) 1 and 3 only (b) 2 only (c) 1 and 2 only (d) 1, 2 and 3
Answer: (a)
Solution: Statement 2 is the trap: candidates assume every 1946 plan involved partition, but the Cabinet Mission explicitly tried to preserve a united India and rejected a sovereign Pakistan; partition came with the Mountbatten Plan of June 1947. Statements 1 and 3 are correct, so the answer is 1 and 3 only.
Source: PYQ-style

22. Arrange the following events in chronological order:
1. Surat Split of the Congress
2. Formation of the Muslim League
3. Partition of Bengal
4. Morley-Minto Reforms
(a) 3, 2, 1, 4 (b) 2, 3, 1, 4 (c) 3, 1, 2, 4 (d) 2, 1, 3, 4
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is placing the Muslim League (1906) before the Partition of Bengal (1905) because both belong to the same turbulent period. The correct order is Partition of Bengal (1905), Muslim League (1906), Surat Split (1907), Morley-Minto Reforms (1909), so the answer is 3, 2, 1, 4.
Source: PYQ-style

23. Which of the following pairs is correctly matched?
(a) Mahalwari System — Thomas Munro (b) Ryotwari System — Lord Dalhousie (c) Permanent Settlement — Lord Cornwallis (d) Subsidiary Alliance — Lord Hastings
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is the Ryotwari–Dalhousie pairing: Ryotwari was devised by Thomas Munro in Madras, while Dalhousie is linked to the Doctrine of Lapse; similarly Mahalwari is linked to Holt Mackenzie and Subsidiary Alliance to Wellesley. Only the Permanent Settlement of 1793 under Lord Cornwallis is correctly matched, so the answer is Permanent Settlement — Lord Cornwallis.
Source: NCERT Class 8 History (Our Pasts III)

24. Consider the following statements about the Government of India Act, 1935:
1. It introduced provincial autonomy.
2. It abolished dyarchy in the provinces.
3. It provided for an All-India Federation that actually came into being in 1937.
Which of the statements given above is/are correct?
(a) 1 and 2 only (b) 1, 2 and 3 (c) 2 and 3 only (d) 1 and 3 only
Answer: (a)
Solution: Statement 3 is the trap: the 1935 Act did provide for an All-India Federation, but that federation never came into existence because the princely states did not join, so the statement is false. Provincial autonomy was introduced and provincial dyarchy was abolished, so the answer is 1 and 2 only.
Source: PYQ-style

25. Who among the following was NOT associated with the Ghadar Party?
(a) Lala Har Dayal (b) Sohan Singh Bhakna (c) Vinayak Damodar Savarkar (d) Kartar Singh Sarabha
Answer: (c)
Solution: Kartar Singh Sarabha is the tempting wrong answer because he is more famous as a Bhagat Singh associate, but he was in fact a young Ghadar activist executed in 1915. Savarkar was linked to Abhinav Bharat in India and London, not the San Francisco-based Ghadar Party, so the answer is Vinayak Damodar Savarkar.
