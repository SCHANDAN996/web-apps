76. The Ilbert Bill controversy (1883) arose during the Viceroyalty of:
(a) Lord Lytton (b) Lord Lansdowne (c) Lord Dufferin (d) Lord Ripon
Answer: (d)
Solution: The tempting option is Lord Lytton, but Lytton's tenure is remembered for the Vernacular Press Act and the Arms Act of 1878, not the Ilbert Bill. The Ilbert Bill, which sought to allow Indian judges to try European offenders, was introduced during Lord Ripon's tenure (1880–84) and provoked furious European opposition, so the answer is Lord Ripon.
Source: NCERT Class 12 History (Themes in Indian History Part III)

77. The 'Drain of Wealth' theory was systematically propounded by:
(a) Dadabhai Naoroji (b) Gopal Krishna Gokhale (c) M.G. Ranade (d) R.C. Dutt
Answer: (a)
Solution: R.C. Dutt is a tempting distractor because he wrote the 'Economic History of India' criticising British rule, but the systematic Drain of Wealth theory was developed by Dadabhai Naoroji in 'Poverty and Un-British Rule in India', so the answer is Dadabhai Naoroji.

78. The Hunter Commission (1882) was appointed to enquire into:
(a) Police administration (b) Local self-government (c) Education (d) Currency reform
Answer: (c)
Solution: The Hunter Commission of 1882, headed by W.W. Hunter, reviewed the progress of education since Wood's Despatch of 1854; it was appointed by Lord Ripon. Police and currency commissions came much later, so the answer is Education.

79. Which of the following pairs is correctly matched?
(a) Aligarh Movement — 1885 (b) Arya Samaj — 1867 (c) Ramakrishna Mission — 1902 (d) Brahmo Samaj — 1828
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap lies in the near-correct dates: Arya Samaj was founded in 1875 (not 1867), the Ramakrishna Mission in 1897 (not 1902), and the Aligarh Movement began with the Scientific Society in 1864 and the MAO College in 1875 (not 1885). The Brahmo Samaj was indeed founded by Raja Rammohan Roy in 1828, so the answer is Brahmo Samaj — 1828.
Source: NCERT Class 12 History

80. The first session of the Indian National Congress (1885) was presided over by:
(a) A.O. Hume (b) Dadabhai Naoroji (c) Surendranath Banerjee (d) W.C. Bonnerjee
Answer: (d)
Solution: A.O. Hume is the tempting wrong answer because he founded the Congress, but he was its General Secretary, not the president of the first session. The Bombay session of December 1885 was presided over by Womesh Chandra Bonnerjee, so the answer is W.C. Bonnerjee.

81. The Partition of Bengal (1905) was officially annulled in:
(a) 1909 (b) 1911 (c) 1912 (d) 1916

Solution: 1912 is a tempting distractor because the capital was shifted from Calcutta to Delhi in 1912, but the annulment of the Partition was announced earlier, at the Delhi Durbar of December 1911 by King George V, so the answer is 1911.

(a) Vancouver (b) London (c) San Francisco (d) Singapore

83. The Lucknow Pact (1916) was an agreement between:
(a) Congress and the Muslim League (b) Moderates and Extremists only (c) Congress and the British Government (d) Hindu Mahasabha and Muslim League
Answer: (a)
Solution: The reunion of Moderates and Extremists also happened at Lucknow in 1916, which makes that option tempting, but the 'Lucknow Pact' specifically refers to the joint Congress–League scheme of reforms accepting separate electorates, so the answer is Congress and the Muslim League.

84. The Rowlatt Act (1919) authorised the government to:
(a) Detain persons without trial (b) Censor the press (c) Ban political parties (d) Levy new taxes
Answer: (a)
Solution: Press censorship is tempting because the Vernacular Press Act did that, but the Rowlatt Act's core provision was preventive detention — imprisonment without trial for up to two years — which Gandhi called the 'Black Act', so the answer is Detain persons without trial.

85. The Jallianwala Bagh massacre (13 April 1919) occurred on the day of:
(a) Diwali (b) Holi (c) Baisakhi (d) Guru Nanak Jayanti

86. Which of the following events occurred FIRST?
(a) Chauri Chaura incident (b) Moplah rebellion (c) Non-Cooperation Movement launch (d) Jallianwala Bagh massacre
Answer: (d)
Solution: This is a chronological trap: the Non-Cooperation Movement was launched in 1920, the Moplah rebellion in 1921, and Chauri Chaura in February 1922. The Jallianwala Bagh massacre of April 1919 precedes all of them, so the answer is Jallianwala Bagh massacre.

87. The Swaraj Party (1923) was founded by:
(a) Motilal Nehru and C.R. Das (b) Gandhi and Patel (c) Tilak and Pal (d) Nehru and Bose
Answer: (a)
Solution: Nehru and Bose are tempting because they later founded the Independence for India League, but after the Gaya session split the Swaraj Party was formed by Chittaranjan Das and Motilal Nehru to contest council elections, so the answer is Motilal Nehru and C.R. Das.

88. The Simon Commission (1928) was boycotted because:
(a) It proposed partition of India (b) It supported separate electorates (c) It recommended abolition of dyarchy (d) It had no Indian member
Answer: (d)
Solution: The all-white Simon Commission, appointed to review the 1919 Act, contained no Indian member, sparking nationwide protests with the slogan 'Simon Go Back'; Lala Lajpat Rai died after injuries in a lathi charge during the Lahore protest, so the answer is It had no Indian member.

89. The Nehru Report (1928) demanded:
(a) Dominion Status (b) Complete independence (c) Separate electorates (d) A federal republic
Answer: (a)
Solution: Complete independence is the tempting trap — that demand came with the Lahore Resolution of December 1929 after the British ignored the Nehru Report. The Nehru Report itself demanded Dominion Status with a responsible government, so the answer is Dominion Status.

91. The Second Round Table Conference (1931) was attended by Gandhi as the representative of:
(a) The British Government (b) The Depressed Classes (c) The Indian National Congress (d) The Muslim League
Answer: (c)
Solution: The Depressed Classes option is tempting because B.R. Ambedkar attended the conference, but he represented the Depressed Classes separately; Gandhi attended as the sole representative of the Congress following the Gandhi–Irwin Pact, so the answer is The Indian National Congress.

92. The Communal Award (1932) was announced by:
(a) Lord Irwin (b) Winston Churchill (c) Lord Willingdon (d) Ramsay MacDonald
Answer: (d)
Solution: Lord Willingdon is tempting because he was Viceroy in 1932, but the Communal Award granting separate electorates to the Depressed Classes was announced by British Prime Minister Ramsay MacDonald, leading to Gandhi's fast and the Poona Pact, so the answer is Ramsay MacDonald.

93. Under the Poona Pact (1932), the Depressed Classes were granted:
(a) Separate electorates (b) Nomination to all legislatures (c) Reserved seats in joint electorates (d) A separate province
Answer: (c)
Solution: Separate electorates is the trap — that was the provision of the Communal Award which Gandhi opposed through his fast. The Poona Pact between Gandhi and Ambedkar replaced it with reserved seats (148) for the Depressed Classes within joint electorates, so the answer is Reserved seats in joint electorates.

94. The Government of India Act, 1935 introduced dyarchy at:
(a) The Centre (b) The Provinces (c) Both levels (d) Neither level
Answer: (a)
Solution: 'The Provinces' is the classic trap: the 1919 Act had introduced dyarchy in the provinces, and the 1935 Act abolished it there by granting provincial autonomy. The 1935 Act proposed dyarchy at the Centre, though it never came into operation, so the answer is The Centre.

95. The Congress ministries formed after the 1937 provincial elections resigned in 1939 over:
(a) The Communal Award (b) India being declared a party to World War II without consultation (c) The Cripps proposals (d) The Wavell Plan
Answer: (b)
Solution: The Cripps proposals came in 1942, after the resignations, making it a chronological trap. The Congress ministries resigned in October–November 1939 because Viceroy Linlithgow declared India a belligerent in World War II without consulting Indian leaders, so the answer is India being declared a party to World War II without consultation.

100. The Mountbatten Plan (3 June 1947) provided for:
(a) A united confederation of India (b) Partition of India into two dominions (c) Immediate transfer of power to the Constituent Assembly (d) A plebiscite across all provinces
Answer: (b)
Solution: A united confederation is tempting because the Cabinet Mission Plan (1946) had proposed a three-tier federation, but the Mountbatten Plan of 3 June 1947 accepted partition into two dominions — India and Pakistan — with boundary commissions under Cyril Radcliffe, so the answer is Partition of India into two dominions.
