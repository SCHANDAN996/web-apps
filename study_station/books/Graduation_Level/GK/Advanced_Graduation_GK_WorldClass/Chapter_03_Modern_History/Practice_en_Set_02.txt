Questions 26–50: Medium

26. The Regulating Act of 1773 designated the Governor of Bengal as the Governor-General of Bengal. Who was the first person to hold this office?
(a) Lord Cornwallis (b) Warren Hastings (c) Lord Clive (d) Lord Wellesley
Answer: (b)
Solution: Robert Clive is the tempting wrong choice because he won Plassey and twice governed Bengal, but he left India in 1767, before the Act. The Regulating Act 1773 made Warren Hastings the first Governor-General of Bengal, so the answer is Warren Hastings.
Source: NCERT Class 8 History (Our Pasts III)

27. Which of the following correctly arranges these events in chronological order?
P. Battle of Buxar
Q. Battle of Plassey
R. Third Battle of Panipat
S. Treaty of Allahabad
(a) R, P, Q, S (b) R, Q, P, S (c) Q, P, R, S (d) Q, R, P, S
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is placing Panipat (1761) after Plassey; in fact Panipat (1761) came after Plassey (1757) but before Buxar (1764), and the Treaty of Allahabad (1765) followed Buxar. The correct order is Plassey, Panipat, Buxar, Allahabad, so the answer is Q, R, P, S.
Source: NCERT Class 8 History (Our Pasts III)

28. The 'Doctrine of Lapse', under which states without a natural male heir were annexed, is associated with which Governor-General?
(a) Lord Dalhousie (b) Lord Hastings (c) Lord Wellesley (d) Lord Canning
Answer: (a)
Solution: Wellesley is the trap because he expanded British territory aggressively, but his tool was the Subsidiary Alliance, not lapse. Satara (1848), Jhansi (1853) and Nagpur (1854) were annexed under Dalhousie's Doctrine of Lapse, so the answer is Lord Dalhousie.
Source: NCERT Class 8 History (Our Pasts III)

29. Consider the following statements about the Revolt of 1857:
1. It began at Meerut on 10 May 1857.
2. Mangal Pandey's action at Barrackpore occurred in March 1857.
3. Bahadur Shah Zafar was declared the symbolic leader and later exiled to Rangoon.
Which of the statements given above are correct?
(a) 1 and 2 only (b) 1, 2 and 3 (c) 1 and 3 only (d) 2 and 3 only
Answer: (b)
Solution: All three statements are accurate: the sepoys rose at Meerut on 10 May 1857, Mangal Pandey attacked British officers at Barrackpore on 29 March 1857, and Bahadur Shah II was proclaimed Emperor and exiled to Rangoon after the revolt's suppression. So the answer is 1, 2 and 3.
Source: NCERT Class 8 History (Our Pasts III)

30. The 'Permanent Settlement' of 1793 was introduced in Bengal by:
(a) Warren Hastings (b) William Bentinck (c) Lord Wellesley (d) Lord Cornwallis
Answer: (d)
Solution: Warren Hastings is the tempting option because he experimented with five-yearly revenue farming, but the fixed Permanent Settlement with zamindars was concluded by Cornwallis in 1793. So the answer is Lord Cornwallis.

33. Which of the following pairs is correctly matched?
(a) Arya Samaj — Swami Vivekananda (b) Ramakrishna Mission — Swami Dayananda (c) Theosophical Society — Annie Besant (founder) (d) Aligarh Movement — Sir Syed Ahmad Khan
Answer: (d)
Solution: The traps swap founders: Arya Samaj was founded by Dayananda Saraswati (1875), the Ramakrishna Mission by Vivekananda (1897), and the Theosophical Society was founded by Blavatsky and Olcott (1875), with Annie Besant as a later leader. The Aligarh Movement was indeed led by Sir Syed Ahmad Khan, so the answer is the Aligarh Movement pair.
Source: NCERT Class 8 History (Our Pasts III)

34. The Indian National Congress was founded in 1885. Its first session was held at:
(a) Calcutta (b) Poona (c) Madras (d) Bombay
Answer: (d)
Solution: Poona is the trap because the session was originally planned there but shifted due to a cholera outbreak; it was finally held at Gokuldas Tejpal Sanskrit College, Bombay, presided over by W.C. Bonnerjee. So the answer is Bombay.
Source: NCERT Class 10 History (India and the Contemporary World / Our Pasts)

35. Who among the following was known as the 'Grand Old Man of India' and propounded the Drain of Wealth theory?
(a) Dadabhai Naoroji (b) Gopal Krishna Gokhale (c) Surendranath Banerjee (d) Pherozeshah Mehta
Answer: (a)
Solution: Gokhale is tempting as a moderate economist, but the Drain of Wealth theory was systematically presented by Dadabhai Naoroji in 'Poverty and Un-British Rule in India'. So the answer is Dadabhai Naoroji.

36. The Partition of Bengal (1905) was announced during the Viceroyalty of:
(a) Lord Curzon (b) Lord Dufferin (c) Lord Ripon (d) Lord Minto
Answer: (a)
Solution: Minto is the trap because the Swadeshi movement continued into his tenure and the Morley-Minto Reforms came in 1909, but the partition itself was announced by Curzon in July 1905 and took effect on 16 October 1905. So the answer is Lord Curzon.
Source: NCERT Class 8 History (Our Pasts III)

37. The Muslim League was founded in 1906 at:
(a) Lucknow (b) Dhaka (c) Karachi (d) Lahore
Answer: (b)
Solution: Lucknow is tempting because of the 1916 Lucknow Pact, but the All-India Muslim League was founded at Dhaka in December 1906 during the session of the All-India Muhammadan Educational Conference. So the answer is Dhaka.

38. The Surat Split of the Congress (1907) was primarily a division between:
(a) Congress and Muslim League (b) Moderates and Extremists (c) Swarajists and No-changers (d) Left and Right wings
Answer: (b)
Solution: Swarajists versus No-changers is the trap, but that split occurred in 1922–23 over council entry. The 1907 Surat session split the Congress into Moderates and Extremists over the Swadeshi programme and leadership. So the answer is Moderates and Extremists.
Source: NCERT Class 8 History (Our Pasts III)

39. The Ghadar Party was founded in 1913 at San Francisco, USA, primarily by:
(a) Bhagat Singh and Chandrashekhar Azad (b) V.D. Savarkar and Shyamji Krishna Varma (c) Rash Behari Bose and Sachindranath Sanyal (d) Lala Har Dayal and Sohan Singh Bhakna
Answer: (d)
Solution: Savarkar and Krishna Varma are the trap, but they were associated with London-based India House. The Ghadar Party in San Francisco was founded by Lala Har Dayal with Sohan Singh Bhakna as its president. So the answer is Lala Har Dayal and Sohan Singh Bhakna.

40. The Jallianwala Bagh massacre took place on:
(a) 6 April 1919 (b) 13 April 1919 (c) 10 April 1919 (d) 23 March 1931
Answer: (b)
Solution: 6 April 1919 is the trap — that was the day of the nationwide hartal against the Rowlatt Act. The massacre by General Dyer's troops occurred on Baisakhi day, 13 April 1919, so the answer is 13 April 1919.

41. Which of the following was NOT one of the three main movements launched by Gandhi between 1920 and 1942?
(a) Swadeshi Movement (b) Civil Disobedience Movement (c) Quit India Movement (d) Non-Cooperation Movement
Answer: (a)
Solution: The Swadeshi Movement (1905–08) predates Gandhi's leadership of the national movement; he returned to India in 1915. Non-Cooperation (1920), Civil Disobedience (1930) and Quit India (1942) were his three mass movements, so the answer is the Swadeshi Movement.
Source: NCERT Class 10 History (India and the Contemporary World II)

42. The Dandi March (Salt Satyagraha) of 1930 covered the distance from Sabarmati Ashram to Dandi in approximately:
(a) 150 miles in 15 days (b) 100 miles in 10 days (c) 375 miles in 30 days (d) 240 miles in 24 days
Answer: (d)
Solution: Gandhi and 78 followers marched about 240 miles (roughly 385 km) from Sabarmati to Dandi between 12 March and 6 April 1930 — about 24 days. So the answer is 240 miles in 24 days.
Source: NCERT Class 10 History (India and the Contemporary World II)

43. The Poona Pact of 1932 was an agreement between:
(a) Gandhi and Ambedkar (b) Gandhi and Irwin (c) Nehru and Jinnah (d) Patel and Rajendra Prasad
Answer: (a)
Solution: The Gandhi-Irwin Pact (1931) is the trap because of the similar pairing format, but it concerned the suspension of Civil Disobedience. The Poona Pact (September 1932) between Gandhi and B.R. Ambedkar replaced separate electorates for Depressed Classes with reserved seats in joint electorates. So the answer is Gandhi and Ambedkar.
Source: NCERT Class 10 History (India and the Contemporary World II)

44. The Government of India Act 1935 provided for:
1. Provincial autonomy
2. Dyarchy at the Centre
3. An All-India Federation
(a) 1 and 2 only (b) 1 and 3 only (c) 1, 2 and 3 (d) 2 and 3 only
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is assuming dyarchy continued in the provinces; the 1935 Act abolished provincial dyarchy (introduced by the 1919 Act) and introduced provincial autonomy, while proposing dyarchy at the Centre and an All-India Federation (which never came into force). All three provisions were in the Act, so the answer is 1, 2 and 3.
Source: NCERT Class 8 History (Our Pasts III)

47. The 'Quit India' resolution was passed by the Congress at Bombay on:
(a) 8 August 1942 (b) 15 August 1942 (c) 9 August 1942 (d) 26 January 1942
Answer: (a)
Solution: 9 August 1942 is the trap — that is when Gandhi and other leaders were arrested, marking the movement's start. The resolution itself was passed at the AICC session at Gowalia Tank, Bombay, on 8 August 1942. So the answer is 8 August 1942.

48. The Cabinet Mission (1946) consisted of three members. Which of the following was NOT a member?
(a) Lord Mountbatten (b) Sir Stafford Cripps (c) A.V. Alexander (d) Lord Pethick-Lawrence
Answer: (a)
Solution: Mountbatten is the trap because he dealt with the transfer of power, but he arrived as Viceroy only in March 1947, after the Cabinet Mission. The Mission (March–June 1946) comprised Pethick-Lawrence, Cripps and Alexander, so the answer is Lord Mountbatten.

50. Who among the following founded the 'Servants of India Society' in 1905?

Solution: Gandhi is the trap because he later joined and was influenced by the Society, but it was founded by his political guru Gopal Krishna Gokhale in 1905 at Poona to train dedicated national workers. So the answer is Gopal Krishna Gokhale.
