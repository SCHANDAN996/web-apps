76. The famous 'Dashavatara' (ten incarnations) cave, with large sculpted panels of Vishnu's incarnations, is found at which site?
(a) Ellora Caves (b) Elephanta Caves (c) Ajanta Caves (d) Badami Caves
Answer: (a)
Solution: Elephanta tempts because of its colossal Trimurti, but the celebrated Dashavatara cave (Cave 15), with grand panels of Vishnu's ten incarnations, is at Ellora in Maharashtra. Ajanta is famed mainly for paintings and Badami for Chalukyan caves, so the answer is Ellora Caves.
Source: NCERT Class 11 An Introduction to Indian Art

77. Which classical dance form uses the 'Chowk' and 'Tribhanga' as its basic body positions?
(a) Odissi (b) Manipuri (c) Bharatanatyam (d) Kathak
Answer: (a)
Solution: Bharatanatyam tempts because of its geometric poses, but the square stance 'Chowk' and the three-bend posture 'Tribhanga' are the signature positions of Odissi, inspired by temple sculptures of Odisha. Kathak uses straight stances and Manipuri uses soft rounded movements, so the answer is Odissi.
Source: NCERT Class 11 An Introduction to Indian Art

78. The Gandhara school of art flourished mainly under the patronage of which ruler?
(a) Ashoka (b) Kanishka (c) Harshavardhana (d) Samudragupta
Answer: (b)
Solution: Ashoka is tempting because he spread Buddhism, but Gandhara art, with its Greco-Buddhist features, reached its peak under the Kushana ruler Kanishka in the 1st–2nd century CE. Ashoka belongs to the Mauryan period, centuries earlier, so the answer is Kanishka.

79. Which of the following stupas is famous for its four ornate 'toranas' (gateways) depicting Jataka tales?
(a) Sanchi Stupa (b) Dhamek Stupa (c) Bharhut Stupa (d) Amaravati Stupa
Answer: (a)
Solution: Bharhut is tempting because it also had carved railings and gateways, but the four exquisitely carved toranas narrating Jataka stories belong to the Great Stupa at Sanchi, built under Ashoka and enlarged later. Bharhut's remains are now largely in museums, so the answer is Sanchi Stupa.

80. The 'Vesara' style of temple architecture is chiefly associated with which dynasty?
(a) Pallavas (b) Chandellas (c) Cholas (d) Chalukyas of Badami
Answer: (d)
Solution: The Pallavas tempt because they pioneered Dravida stone temples, but the Vesara style — a blend of Nagara and Dravida features — is credited to the Chalukyas of Badami, seen at Pattadakal and Aihole. Cholas and Chandellas worked in Dravida and Nagara styles respectively, so the answer is Chalukyas of Badami.

81. Which Mughal emperor is credited with establishing the 'Karkhana' (royal atelier) that produced the Hamzanama paintings?
(a) Babur (b) Jahangir (c) Akbar (d) Shah Jahan
Answer: (c)
Solution: Jahangir is tempting because Mughal painting reached its finest naturalism under him, but the large illustrated Hamzanama project on cloth was commissioned by Akbar in his royal atelier, supervised by masters like Mir Sayyid Ali. Jahangir's reign came later, so the answer is Akbar.

82. The 'Gita Govinda' of Jayadeva is most closely associated with the origin of which classical dance?
(a) Kathakali (b) Kuchipudi (c) Odissi (d) Mohiniyattam
Answer: (c)
Solution: Kuchipudi tempts because it too draws on Vaishnava themes, but Jayadeva's Gita Govinda, composed in 12th-century Odisha, is integral to the Odissi repertoire and its ashtapadis are still sung in performances. Kathakali and Mohiniyattam belong to Kerala traditions, so the answer is Odissi.

83. Which site provides the earliest evidence of a Buddhist 'chaitya hall' cut in rock?

Answer: (d)
Solution: Karle tempts because it has the largest and most famous chaitya hall, but the earliest rock-cut chaitya halls date to around the 2nd century BCE at Bhaja (and Kondane) in Maharashtra. Karle's great chaitya is later, around the 1st century CE, so the answer is Bhaja.

84. The 'Kalamkari' textile art, using a pen-like tool for hand-painting on cloth, is traditionally associated with which state?
(a) Gujarat (b) Rajasthan (c) Andhra Pradesh (d) West Bengal
Answer: (c)
Solution: Gujarat tempts because of its rich textile traditions like patola, but Kalamkari — from 'kalam' (pen) and 'kari' (work) — is the hand-painted textile art of Andhra Pradesh, centred at Srikalahasti and Machilipatnam. The other states have different textile specialities, so the answer is Andhra Pradesh.

85. Which of the following is a correct chronological order of these architectural works?
(a) Shore Temple – Iron Pillar – Gol Gumbaz – Qutub Minar (b) Iron Pillar (Mehrauli) – Shore Temple (Mahabalipuram) – Qutub Minar – Gol Gumbaz (c) Qutub Minar – Iron Pillar – Shore Temple – Gol Gumbaz (d) Iron Pillar – Qutub Minar – Shore Temple – Gol Gumbaz
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is placing the Qutub Minar before the Shore Temple; the Shore Temple (Pallava, c. 700–728 CE) predates the Qutub Minar (begun 1199 CE) by centuries. The Iron Pillar is Gupta-era (4th–5th century CE) and Gol Gumbaz is 17th century, so the answer is Iron Pillar – Shore Temple – Qutub Minar – Gol Gumbaz.
Source: NCERT Class 11 An Introduction to Indian Art

86. The 'Sattriya' dance tradition was introduced by which saint-reformer?
(a) Chaitanya Mahaprabhu (b) Shankaradeva (c) Ramanuja (d) Vallabhacharya
Answer: (b)
Solution: Chaitanya Mahaprabhu tempts because he too spread Vaishnavism in eastern India, but Sattriya was created in the 15th–16th century by the Assamese saint Shankaradeva as part of worship in the sattras (monasteries) of Assam. Chaitanya belonged to Bengal, so the answer is Shankaradeva.

87. The bronze 'Nataraja' images reached their artistic zenith under which dynasty?
(a) Cholas (b) Cheras (c) Pandyas (d) Pallavas

88. Which ragas are traditionally associated with the late night time in Hindustani music?
(a) Bhairav and Bhairavi (b) Darbari Kanada and Malkauns (c) Bhupali and Yaman (d) Desh and Bihag
Answer: (b)
Solution: Yaman tempts because it is a famous evening raga, but the time theory assigns deep-night ragas like Darbari Kanada and Malkauns to the late-night period. Bhairav is a morning raga and Yaman an early-evening one, so the answer is Darbari Kanada and Malkauns.

89. The 'Phad' scroll painting tradition, depicting folk deities like Pabuji and Devnarayan, belongs to which state?
(a) Madhya Pradesh (b) Rajasthan (c) Odisha (d) Bihar
Answer: (b)
Solution: Madhya Pradesh tempts because of its tribal art like Gond painting, but Phad is a long horizontal scroll painting tradition of Rajasthan, used by Bhopa singers to narrate epics of folk deities Pabuji and Devnarayan. Odisha has Pattachitra and Bihar has Madhubani, so the answer is Rajasthan.

90. Which statement about the Ajanta paintings is correct?
(a) They were executed mainly in the fresco technique using oil colours (b) They span two phases, with the later phase under Vakataka patronage (c) They were all painted during the Gupta period (d) They depict only scenes from the Ramayana
Answer: (b)
Solution: The tempting error is calling them purely Gupta works; Ajanta has an early phase (2nd–1st century BCE, Satavahana era) and a later phase (5th–6th century CE) under Vakataka patronage, notably of Harishena. The technique is tempera, not oil fresco, and themes are Buddhist Jatakas, so the answer is that they span two phases with the later one under Vakataka patronage.

Solution: Shaivites tempt because many famous temples are Shaiva, but the Dilwara Temples (11th–13th century) are Jain temples, built by ministers and merchants of the Chalukya (Solanki) period, famed for their intricate marble ceilings. 98. The 'Madhubani' (Mithila) painting tradition is characterised by which feature?
(a) Miniature portraits of Mughal courtiers (b) Use of gold leaf on cloth (c) Fresco painting on temple walls (d) Double-line borders and filling of all empty space with motifs
Answer: (d)
Solution: Gold leaf tempts because of Tanjore painting's fame, but Madhubani of Bihar is marked by double-line borders, bold natural colours and the convention of leaving no empty space — gaps are filled with flowers, birds and geometric motifs. It is done on walls and paper, not temple frescoes, so the answer is double-line borders and filling of all empty space with motifs.

100. Which statement about the Konark Sun Temple is correct?
(a) It is located on the banks of the Ganga in Varanasi (b) It was built by the Chola king Rajaraja I (c) It is designed as a colossal chariot of Surya with twelve pairs of wheels (d) It was constructed entirely of white marble
Answer: (c)
Solution: Rajaraja I tempts because he built the great Brihadisvara temple, but Konark was built by Narasimhadeva I of the Eastern Ganga dynasty in the 13th century, conceived as Surya's chariot with twelve pairs of carved wheels and seven horses. It stands in Odisha and is built of khondalite stone, so the answer is that it is designed as a colossal chariot of Surya with twelve pairs of wheels.
