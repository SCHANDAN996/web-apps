101. The famous "Lion Capital" of Ashoka, adopted as the National Emblem of India, was originally erected at which place?
(a) Sanchi (b) Lumbini (c) Bodh Gaya (d) Sarnath
Answer: (d)
Solution: Sanchi is tempting because it has the most famous Ashokan stupas and gateways, but the Lion Capital crowned the pillar at Sarnath, where the Buddha delivered his first sermon. It was adopted as the National Emblem on 26 January 1950, so the answer is Sarnath.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Themes in Indian History

102. The Gandhara school of art is distinguished from the Mathura school primarily by which feature?
(a) Use of red spotted sandstone (b) Greco-Roman influence and use of schist stone (c) Exclusive depiction of Jain Tirthankaras (d) Absence of Buddha images
Answer: (b)
Solution: Red spotted sandstone is the trap — it belongs to the Mathura school, not Gandhara. Gandhara art shows strong Greek-Roman influence (wavy hair, muscular bodies, drapery) and used bluish-grey schist, so the answer is Greco-Roman influence and use of schist stone.

121. Consider the following statements about the Mathura school of art:
1. It used spotted red sandstone.
2. It produced images of Buddha, Bodhisattvas and Jain Tirthankaras.
3. It flourished only under the Gupta dynasty.
Which of the statements given above is/are correct?
(a) 1 and 2 only (b) 2 and 3 only (c) 1 and 3 only (d) 1, 2 and 3

Solution: The trap is statement 3, which sounds plausible because Gupta art is famous, but Mathura art flourished earlier under the Kushanas (Kanishka's era) and continued into the Gupta period — "only under the Guptas" makes it wrong. Statements 1 and 2 are correct, so the answer is 1 and 2 only.

122. Arrange the following architectural monuments in chronological order of their construction:
1. Shore Temple, Mahabalipuram
2. Brihadisvara Temple, Thanjavur
3. Kailasa Temple, Ellora
4. Great Stupa, Sanchi
(a) 4, 1, 2, 3 (b) 4, 3, 1, 2 (c) 1, 4, 3, 2 (d) 4, 1, 3, 2
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is placing Ellora's Kailasa temple before the Shore Temple; the Pallava Shore Temple (c. 700–728 CE, Narasimhavarman II) predates the Rashtrakuta Kailasa temple (c. 757–783 CE, Krishna I). Sanchi (3rd century BCE) is earliest and Thanjavur (1010 CE) latest, so the answer is 4, 1, 3, 2.

1. It is a hybrid of the Nagara and Dravida styles.
2. It is associated mainly with the Chalukyas of Badami and the Hoysalas.

(a) 1 and 2 only (b) 2 and 3 only (c) 1, 2 and 3 (d) 1 and 3 only
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is to doubt statement 3, since Halebidu looks purely Dravidian, but Hoysala temples are classified as Vesara — the Deccan hybrid of Nagara and Dravida features developed under the Chalukyas of Badami and refined by the Hoysalas.
