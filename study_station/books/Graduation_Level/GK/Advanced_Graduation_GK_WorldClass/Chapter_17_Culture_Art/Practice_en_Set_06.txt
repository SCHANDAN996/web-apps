126. With reference to the Sattriya dance form, consider the following statements:
1. It was introduced by the Vaishnavite saint Srimanta Sankardeva in the 15th–16th century.
2. It originated in the Satras (monasteries) of Assam.
3. It was recognised as a classical dance by the Sangeet Natak Akademi in the year 2000.
Which of the statements given above is/are correct?
(a) 1 and 2 only (b) 2 and 3 only (c) 1 and 3 only (d) 1, 2 and 3
Answer: (d)
Solution: The common trap is to doubt statement 3 because Sattriya was recognised much later than the other seven classical forms, making candidates assume the date is wrong. All three statements are correct: Sankardeva created it within Assam's Satras, and the Sangeet Natak Akademi granted it classical status in 2000, so the answer is 1, 2 and 3.
Source: PYQ-style

127. The 'Dashavatara' theme is most prominently depicted in which of the following classical dance forms, where the dancer traditionally begins the recital with an invocation behind a hand-held curtain?
(a) Kathak (b) Kathakali (c) Bharatanatyam (d) Manipuri
Answer: (b)
Solution: Manipuri is tempting because it is also Vaishnavite, but it centres on Radha-Krishna Ras Lila, not Dashavatara, and uses no curtain entry. Kathakali of Kerala famously depicts the Dashavatara and uses the 'tiranokku' curtain for dramatic character entry, so the answer is Kathakali.
Source: NCERT Class 11 – An Introduction to Indian Art

128. Consider the following pairs of folk theatres and their states:

Which of the pairs given above are correctly matched?
(a) 1, 2 and 4 only (b) 1 and 3 only (c) 2, 3 and 4 only (d) 1, 2, 3 and 4

129. Which of the following correctly describes the 'Vesara' style of temple architecture?
(a) A hybrid style combining features of Nagara and Dravida, developed under the Chalukyas and Rashtrakutas of the Deccan (b) A style confined to the Himalayan region with wooden roofs (c) The style of rock-cut Buddhist chaityas at Ajanta (d) The Indo-Islamic style of the Delhi Sultanate
Answer: (a)
Solution: Option (b) is a trap because Himalayan wooden temples exist, but they are not called Vesara. Vesara is the Deccan hybrid of Nagara and Dravida idioms, seen at Pattadakal and in Rashtrakuta works like the Kailasa temple at Ellora, so the answer is the hybrid Deccan style.
Source: NCERT Class 11 – An Introduction to Indian Art

130. With reference to the Ajanta paintings, consider the following statements:
1. They were executed exclusively in the fresco technique on dry plaster.
2. The themes are drawn mainly from the Jataka tales.
3. The caves span both the Hinayana and Mahayana phases of Buddhism.
Which of the statements given above is/are correct?
(a) 1 and 2 only (b) 2 and 3 only (c) 1 and 3 only (d) 1, 2 and 3
Answer: (b)
Solution: Statement 1 is the trap: Ajanta paintings were done in the tempera technique on prepared plaster, not true fresco on wet/dry plaster alone, so 'exclusively fresco' is wrong. The Jataka themes and the two-phase (Hinayana and Mahayana) chronology are correct, so the answer is 2 and 3 only.
Source: NCERT Class 11 – An Introduction to Indian Art

131. The 'Gandhara school' of art is distinguished from the 'Mathura school' primarily by which of the following?
(a) Use of spotted red sandstone and depiction of only Jain themes (b) Greco-Roman influence, use of bluish-grey schist, and realistic depiction of Buddha with wavy hair and muscular features (c) Complete absence of Buddha images (d) Patronage exclusively by the Gupta rulers
Answer: (b)
Solution: Option (a) is a trap because spotted red sandstone belongs to the Mathura school, not Gandhara. Gandhara art shows strong Greco-Roman influence, used grey schist, and portrayed Buddha with Apollo-like features, so the answer is the Greco-Roman influenced school.
Source: NCERT Class 11 – An Introduction to Indian Art

133. Which of the following statements about the Dhrupad tradition of Hindustani classical music is correct?
(a) It is the oldest surviving form of Hindustani vocal music, associated with the devotional temple tradition and patronised by Raja Man Singh Tomar of Gwalior (b) It is a light classical form that emerged in the 19th century courts of Lucknow (c) It was created by Amir Khusrau as a Sufi devotional genre (d) It is performed only with percussion accompaniment and no melodic instruments
Answer: (a)
Solution: Option (c) is a trap because Amir Khusrau is linked with Qawwali and Khayal innovations, not Dhrupad. Dhrupad is the oldest surviving Hindustani vocal form, rooted in temple singing and patronised by Man Singh Tomar, so the answer is the statement about the oldest form and Gwalior patronage.

136. Which of the following correctly matches the classical dance form with its traditional accompanying percussion instrument?
(a) Kathakali — Chenda (b) Bharatanatyam — Tabla (c) Odissi — Mridangam (d) Kathak — Maddalam
Answer: (a)
Solution: The traps are the cross-pairings: Bharatanatyam uses the mridangam, Odissi uses the pakhawaj/mardala, and Kathak uses the tabla, while the maddalam belongs to Kerala traditions. Kathakali is accompanied by the chenda, so the answer is Kathakali — Chenda.

139. The 'Gharana' system in Hindustani classical music refers to:
(a) A stylistic lineage or school of music associated with a particular region and passed down through discipleship (b) A guild of temple musicians in South India (c) A royal decree fixing the ragas to be sung in each season (d) A classification of musical instruments by material
Answer: (a)
Solution: Option (b) is a trap because temple musician guilds existed in the South, but the term gharana is specific to Hindustani music's hereditary stylistic lineages such as Gwalior, Agra and Jaipur. The correct meaning is a stylistic lineage transmitted through discipleship, so the answer is the lineage-based definition.

141. Which of the following statements about the Chola bronze sculptures is correct?
(a) They depict only Buddhist deities (b) They were made using the lost-wax (cire perdue) technique (c) They were cast in a single solid piece without any hollow core (d) The Nataraja icon shows Shiva dancing within a square frame
Answer: (b)
Solution: Option (d) is a trap because the Nataraja is shown within a circular halo of flames (prabhamandala), not a square frame. Chola bronzes were cast by the lost-wax technique and are famously hollow-cast, so the answer is the lost-wax technique statement.

149. Which of the following correctly distinguishes 'Khayal' from 'Dhrupad' in Hindustani music?
(a) Khayal allows greater melodic improvisation and ornamentation, and became prominent from the 18th century, while Dhrupad is older, austere and composition-bound (b) Khayal is older and more rigid than Dhrupad (c) Khayal is sung only in temples, while Dhrupad is a court form (d) Khayal uses no tabla accompaniment, unlike Dhrupad
Answer: (a)
Solution: Option (b) is the trap because it reverses the chronology: Dhrupad is the older, more austere form, while Khayal, popularised in the 18th century by figures like Niyamat Khan (Sadarang), permits freer improvisation. The correct distinction is the improvisation-based one, so the answer is the statement contrasting improvisation and antiquity.
Source: PYQ-style

1. The First Buddhist Council was held at Rajgriha under the patronage of Ajatashatru.

Which of the statements given above is/are correct?
(a) 1 and 2 only (b) 1, 2 and 3 (c) 1 and 3 only (d) 2 and 3 only
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is a chronological confusion: candidates often swap the venues of the Third (Pataliputra, under Ashoka) and Fourth (Kashmir/Kundalavana, under Kanishka) councils. All three statements here are correctly stated, so the answer is 1, 2 and 3.
