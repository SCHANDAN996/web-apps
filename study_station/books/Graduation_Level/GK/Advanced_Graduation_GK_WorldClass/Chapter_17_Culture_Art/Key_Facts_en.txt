# Key Facts & Figures — Indian Culture & Art

## Part 1: High-Yield Facts

| Fact | Description | Examiner's Trap | Trap Type |
|---|---|---|---|
| First Classical Language | Tamil was the first language to get Classical status (2004) | Sanskrit listed as "first" classical language | Chronology confusion |
| Classical Languages (6) | Tamil, Sanskrit, Telugu, Kannada, Malayalam, Odia | Pali or Hindi inserted in the list | Similar-name trap |
| Odia Classical Status | Odia got classical status in 2014 — the 6th and latest | Malayalam (2013) shown as the latest | Chronology confusion |
| Classical Dances (8) | Bharatanatyam, Kathak, Kathakali, Kuchipudi, Odissi, Manipuri, Mohiniyattam, Sattriya | Chhau or Bhangra listed as classical | Similar-name trap |
| Sattriya Origin | Assam; introduced by saint Sankaradeva (15th–16th century) | Attributed to Manipur | State-swap trap |
| Bharatanatyam Origin | Tamil Nadu; linked to Devadasi tradition, codified by the Tanjore Quartet | Attributed to Andhra Pradesh | State-swap trap |
| Kathak Origin | Uttar Pradesh (North India); Jaipur, Lucknow, Banaras gharanas | Called a "temple dance of Kerala" | Region-swap trap |
| Kuchipudi Origin | Andhra Pradesh; village Kuchipudi in Krishna district | Confused with Bharatanatyam (both South Indian) | Similar-name trap |
| Mohiniyattam vs Kathakali | Both from Kerala; Mohiniyattam = graceful solo (female), Kathakali = elaborate costume/mask dance-drama | Features interchanged between the two | Feature-swap trap |
| UNESCO Intangible Heritage (first from India) | Kutiyattam (Sanskrit theatre, Kerala) — proclaimed 2008 | Yoga (2016) shown as the first | Chronology confusion |
| UNESCO List Entries | Include Vedic Chanting (2008), Ramlila (2008), Chhau dance (2010), Kalbelia (2010), Yoga (2016), Kumbh Mela (2017), Durga Puja (2021), Garba (2023) | Garba shown as from Rajasthan instead of Gujarat | State-swap trap |
| Ajanta Caves | Maharashtra; Buddhist; famous for paintings (frescoes/tempera), c. 2nd century BCE–6th century CE | Called "Hindu caves" or confused with Ellora | Theme-swap trap |
| Ellora Caves | Maharashtra; 34 caves — Buddhist, Hindu, Jain; Kailasa temple (Cave 16) built under Rashtrakuta Krishna I | Kailasa temple attributed to Pallavas/Cholas | Dynasty-swap trap |
| Elephanta Caves | Island near Mumbai; mainly Shaivite; famous Trimurti sculpture | Trimurti called a "painting" | Medium-swap trap |
| Khajuraho Temples | Madhya Pradesh; Chandela dynasty; Nagara style; Kandariya Mahadeva temple | Attributed to Pallavas | Dynasty-swap trap |
| Konark Sun Temple | Odisha; built by Narasimhadeva I (Eastern Ganga); chariot of Surya; "Black Pagoda" | Attributed to Cholas; "Black Pagoda" swapped with "White Pagoda" | Dynasty/nickname trap |
| Brihadeeswara Temple | Thanjavur, Tamil Nadu; built by Rajaraja Chola I; Dravidian style | Attributed to Rajendra Chola I (who built Gangaikonda Cholapuram) | Similar-name trap |
| Shore Temple | Mamallapuram (Mahabalipuram); Pallavas under Narasimhavarman II | Attributed to Cholas | Dynasty-swap trap |
| Nagara vs Dravida vs Vesara | Nagara = North (curvilinear shikhara); Dravida = South (pyramidal vimana); Vesara = Deccan mix (Chalukyas/Hoysalas) | Shikhara/vimana terms interchanged | Terminology-swap trap |
| Sanchi Stupa | Madhya Pradesh; originally built by Ashoka (3rd century BCE); toranas added under Satavahanas | Entire stupa credited to Ashoka alone | Partial-truth trap |
| Nalanda University | Bihar; flourished under Guptas (Kumaragupta I); visited by Hiuen Tsang and Xuanzang's account studied | Hiuen Tsang shown visiting during Harsha only / Fa-Hien during Harsha | Traveller-period trap |
| Fa-Hien vs Hiuen Tsang | Fa-Hien visited during Chandragupta II; Hiuen Tsang during Harshavardhana | Periods swapped | Chronology confusion |
| Gandhara vs Mathura Art | Gandhara = Greco-Buddhist, grey schist, Buddha with Greek features; Mathura = indigenous, red spotted sandstone | Materials and features swapped | Feature-swap trap |
| Amaravati School | Andhra region; white marble/limestone; narrative panels | Confused with Gandhara material | Material-swap trap |
| Indus Valley Dancing Girl | Bronze statuette, lost-wax (cire perdue) technique, from Mohenjo-daro | Called a terracotta figure | Material-swap trap |
| Harappan Seals | Mostly steatite; unicorn most common motif | "Horse" shown as most common motif | Negative/fact trap |
| Mughal Painting Peak | Reached zenith under Jahangir; Akbar founded the atelier (Tutinama, Hamzanama) | Akbar shown as the peak patron | Chronology confusion |
| Rajasthani vs Pahari Painting | Rajasthani = Rajput courts (Mewar, Marwar, Bundi); Pahari = hill states (Basohli, Kangra, Guler) | Kangra listed under Rajasthani | Region-swap trap |
| Kangra Painting Theme | Krishna legends (Gita Govinda), delicate lines, soft colours | Called "court portraits of Mughals" | Theme-swap trap |
| Warli Painting | Tribal art of Maharashtra; geometric figures, white on ochre | Attributed to Madhya Pradesh (which has Gond/Bhil art) | State-swap trap |
| Madhubani (Mithila) Painting | Bihar; done by women; natural dyes; GI-tagged | Attributed to Odisha (which has Pattachitra) | State-swap trap |
| Pattachitra | Odisha; cloth-based scroll painting, Jagannath themes | Confused with Kalamkari (Andhra/Telangana) | Similar-name trap |
| Hindustani vs Carnatic | Hindustani = North, Persian influence, gharanas; Carnatic = South, kritis of Trinity (Tyagaraja, Muthuswami Dikshitar, Syama Sastri) | Trinity composers listed under Hindustani | Region-swap trap |
| Tansen | Court musician of Akbar; associated with raga creation (e.g., Miyan ki Malhar tradition) | Placed in Jahangir's court | Chronology confusion |
| Bismillah Khan | Shehnai maestro; Bharat Ratna (2001); played at Red Fort on 15 Aug 1947 | Instrument swapped with sarod/sitar | Instrument-swap trap |
| Ravi Shankar vs Vilayat Khan | Ravi Shankar = sitar; Vilayat Khan = sitar (Imdadkhani gharana); both sitar — trap pairs them with sarod (Amjad Ali Khan) | Instrument-swap trap | Instrument-swap trap |
| Bharat Ratna to Musicians | M.S. Subbulakshmi (1998) — first musician to receive it | Lata Mangeshkar (2001) shown as first musician | Chronology confusion |
| Natyashastra | Attributed to Bharata Muni; foundational text on dramaturgy/dance/music | Attributed to Kalidasa | Author-swap trap |
| Kalidasa's Works | Abhijnanashakuntalam, Meghaduta, Raghuvamsha, Kumarasambhava | "Mrichchhakatika" (by Shudraka) attributed to Kalidasa | Author-swap trap |
| Sangam Literature | Tamil; three Sangams per tradition; Tolkappiyam = oldest Tamil grammar | Tolkappiyam called an "epic" (Silappadikaram is the epic) | Genre-swap trap |
| Silappadikaram & Manimekalai | Tamil epics by Ilango Adigal and Sattanar respectively | Authors swapped | Author-swap trap |
| Mahabharata Compilation | Traditionally attributed to Vyasa; Bhagavad Gita is part of Bhishma Parva | Gita called a "separate Upanishad" | Classification trap |
| Puranas Count | 18 Mahapuranas; Vishnu, Bhagavata, Matsya among them | Count given as 16 or 20 | Number trap |
| Buddhist Councils | 1st — Rajgriha (Ajatashatru); 2nd — Vaishali; 3rd — Pataliputra (Ashoka); 4th — Kundalvana, Kashmir (Kanishka) | Patron kings swapped between councils | Chronology confusion |
| Jain Councils | 1st — Pataliputra (Sthulabhadra); 2nd — Vallabhi (Devardhigani, canon written down) | Locations swapped with Buddhist councils | Similar-name trap |
| Tripitaka | Buddhist canon in Pali: Vinaya, Sutta, Abhidhamma Pitaka | Called "Sanskrit texts" | Language-swap trap |
| Jain Agamas | Canonical texts in Ardhamagadhi (Prakrit) | Language given as Pali | Language-swap trap |
| Char Dham (Adi Shankaracharya) | Badrinath, Dwarka, Puri, Rameswaram — four mathas | Kedarnath/Gangotri (Chhota Char Dham, Uttarakhand) mixed in | Similar-name trap |
| Ajanta Cave Paintings Technique | Tempera on prepared plaster surface | Called "oil paintings" | Medium-swap trap |
| Iron Pillar, Mehrauli | Erected in honour of Vishnu, attributed to Chandragupta II period; rust-resistant | Attributed to Ashoka | Dynasty-swap trap |
| Qutub Minar | Started by Qutbuddin Aibak, completed by Iltutmish; repaired by Firoz Shah Tughlaq | Entirely credited to Aibak or to Iltutmish alone | Partial-truth trap |
| Buland Darwaza | Built by Akbar at Fatehpur Sikri to commemorate Gujarat victory | Called "entrance of Jama Masjid, Delhi" | Location-swap trap |
| Taj Mahal | Built by Shah Jahan; chief architect Ustad Ahmad Lahauri (traditional attribution) | Attributed to Jahangir or Aurangzeb | Ruler-swap trap |
| Gol Gumbaz | Bijapur (Vijayapura), Karnataka; Adil Shahi dynasty; second-largest dome of its kind | Attributed to Mughals | Dynasty-swap trap |
| Hampi | Capital of Vijayanagara Empire; Virupaksha temple; UNESCO site (1986) | Attributed to Cholas | Dynasty-swap trap |
| First UNESCO World Heritage Sites in India (1983) | Ajanta, Ellora, Agra Fort, Taj Mahal | Khajuraho (1986) shown as first batch | Chronology confusion |
| Santiniketan | Founded by Rabindranath Tagore; UNESCO World Heritage Site (2023) | Confused with Visva-Bharati's founding year (1921) | Date trap |
| GI Tag Concept | Protects origin-linked crafts (e.g., Chanderi saree, Pochampally ikat, Banarasi silk) | GI called a "patent on the product" | Definition trap |
| Sangeet Natak Akademi | National academy of music, dance, drama (est. 1953); gives Akademi Awards | Confused with Lalit Kala Akademi (fine arts, 1954) or Sahitya Akademi (literature, 1954) | Institution-swap trap |
| National School of Drama | Delhi; under Ministry of Culture | Called a "private institution" | Classification trap |
| Festivals–State Match | Bihu (Assam), Onam (Kerala), Pongal (TN), Lohri (Punjab), Chhath (Bihar/UP), Hornbill (Nagaland) | Hornbill shown as Manipur's festival | State-swap trap |
| Yoga Day | International Day of Yoga — 21 June (UN, from 2015) | Date given as 21 May / 20 June | Date trap |
