Card 1
Front: Which classical dance form of India traces its origins to the temples of Tamil Nadu and was earlier known as Sadir?
Back: Bharatanatyam. It was revived and renamed in the 20th century by Rukmini Devi Arundale and others.

Card 2
Front: Which is the only classical dance form that originated in North India and shows strong Mughal-Persian influence?
Back: Kathak. Its name comes from "Kathakars" (storytellers); its three famous gharanas are Lucknow, Jaipur, and Banaras.

Card 3
Front: Which dance form from Kerala uses elaborate facial makeup, green face paint (pacha vesham), and no spoken dialogue?
Back: Kathakali. It depicts stories from the Ramayana, Mahabharata, and Puranas through mudras and expressions.

Card 4
Front: Which classical dance was performed by the Maharis (devadasis) of the Jagannath Temple, Puri?
Back: Odissi — the classical dance of Odisha, known for its tribhanga (three-bend) posture.

Card 5
Front: Which two classical dance forms come from Andhra Pradesh and Manipur respectively?
Back: Kuchipudi (Andhra Pradesh) and Manipuri (Manipur). Manipuri is based on the Ras Lila of Radha-Krishna.

Card 6
Front: Which classical dance form was created by the Vaishnavite saint Srimanta Sankardeva in Assam?
Back: Sattriya. It originated in the 15th–16th century in the monasteries (sattras) of Assam and got classical status in 2000.

Card 7
Front: How many classical dance forms does the Sangeet Natak Akademi currently recognize — and what is the mnemonic to remember them?
Back: Eight. Mnemonic: "B-K-K-K-M-O-S-M" → Bharatanatyam, Kathak, Kathakali, Kuchipudi, Manipuri, Odissi, Sattriya, Mohiniyattam. (Trick: "Bharat Ki Kalayein — Kathak, Kathakali, Kuchipudi; Manipuri, Odissi, Sattriya, Mohiniyattam.")

Card 8
Front: Examiner's trap: Mohiniyattam belongs to which state — Tamil Nadu or Kerala?
Back: Kerala. The similar-sounding names confuse candidates: Bharatanatyam = Tamil Nadu, but Mohiniyattam = Kerala (dance of the enchantress Mohini).

Card 9
Front: Which ancient Sanskrit text, attributed to Bharata Muni, is the foundational treatise on Indian performing arts?
Back: Natya Shastra (c. 200 BCE–200 CE). It covers drama, dance, and music; the concept of "Rasa" comes from it.

Card 10
Front: Who wrote the Abhinaya Darpana, a key text on gestures used especially in Bharatanatyam?
Back: Nandikeshvara. Abhinaya Darpana deals with hand gestures (mudras) and body movements.

Card 11
Front: Which are the two major traditions of Indian classical music, and how do they differ in region?
Back: Hindustani (North India — influenced by Persian music) and Carnatic (South India — more devotional, composition-based). The split is traced to around the 13th–14th century.

Card 12
Front: Who is considered the father of the Khayal style of Hindustani music — and what was Tansen actually known for?
Back: Amir Khusro is traditionally credited with developing the Khayal style (later shaped by Sadarang). Tansen, one of Akbar's Navaratnas, was a Dhrupad maestro, associated with ragas like Miyan ki Malhar and Miyan ki Todi.

Card 13
Front: Which is India's highest honour in the field of performing arts, given by the Sangeet Natak Akademi?
Back: Sangeet Natak Akademi Fellowship (Akademi Ratna). The Akademi was established in 1953 as India's national academy for music, dance, and drama.

Card 14
Front: Which folk theatre form of Uttar Pradesh is based on the life of Lord Rama and is inscribed on UNESCO's Intangible Cultural Heritage list?
Back: Ramlila. It was inscribed on UNESCO's Representative List of Intangible Cultural Heritage in 2008.

Card 15
Front: Examiner's trap: Is Chhau a single dance or multiple styles — and which states claim it?
Back: Three styles: Mayurbhanj Chhau (Odisha), Seraikella Chhau (Jharkhand), and Purulia Chhau (West Bengal). Purulia and Seraikella use masks; Mayurbhanj does not. Chhau is UNESCO-listed (2010).

Card 16
Front: Which Indian festival from Kerala, featuring the snake-boat race, is associated with the Onam harvest celebration?
Back: Vallamkali (snake boat race) during Onam — the harvest festival honouring the legendary King Mahabali.

Card 17
Front: Which painting school flourished under Mughal patronage, and which ruler is called its greatest patron?
Back: The Mughal school of miniature painting; Jahangir is considered its greatest patron. It began under Humayun with Persian artists Mir Sayyid Ali and Abdus Samad.

Card 18
Front: Which folk painting style from Bihar is traditionally done by women on walls and uses natural dyes and double-line borders?
Back: Madhubani (Mithila) painting. It has a GI tag and depicts deities, nature, and weddings; famous artists include Sita Devi and Ganga Devi.

Card 19
Front: Mnemonic card: How can you quickly recall the four UNESCO-recognized intangible heritage elements often asked with Indian culture — Yoga, Kumbh Mela, Durga Puja, and Garba?
Back: Trick "Y-K-D-G" → Yoga (2016), Kumbh Mela (2017), Durga Puja of Kolkata (2021), Garba of Gujarat (2023) — all on UNESCO's Intangible Cultural Heritage list. Remember: "Yogi Kumbh mein Durga ke saath Garba karte hain."

Card 20
Front: Which ancient university in Bihar, a UNESCO World Heritage Site (2016), attracted scholars like Xuanzang (Hiuen Tsang) from China?
Back: Nalanda Mahavihara. It flourished under the Gupta and Pala periods; Xuanzang studied and taught there in the 7th century CE.
