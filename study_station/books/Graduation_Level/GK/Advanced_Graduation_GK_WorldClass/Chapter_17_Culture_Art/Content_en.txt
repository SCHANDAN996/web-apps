# Indian Culture & Art

## The Hook: A Song That Refused to Die

In 1911, when the British held a grand Durbar in Delhi, a song was sung that many newspapers reported as a tribute to King George V. That song was **"Jana Gana Mana"** — written by Rabindranath Tagore. Decades later, on **24 January 1950**, the Constituent Assembly adopted it as India's National Anthem, and Bankim Chandra Chattopadhyay's "Vande Mataram" was given equal status as the National Song. One poem, written in a colonised country, outlived the empire itself.

That is the power of Indian culture — and that is exactly why examiners love this chapter. Culture questions look easy, but they are full of traps: similar names, wrong centuries, swapped dancers and dance forms. This chapter will make you trap-proof.

## 1. Classical Dances of India

The **Sangeet Natak Akademi** currently recognises **eight** classical dance forms. (Note: the Ministry of Culture also includes Chhau in its list, but exam questions almost always follow the Akademi's eight.)

| Dance Form | State of Origin |
|---|---|
| Bharatanatyam | Tamil Nadu |
| Kathak | Uttar Pradesh (North India) |
| Kathakali | Kerala |
| Mohiniyattam | Kerala |
| Kuchipudi | Andhra Pradesh |
| Odissi | Odisha |
| Manipuri | Manipur |
| Sattriya | Assam |

**Key details examiners test:**

- **Bharatanatyam** — oldest classical form; rooted in the *Natya Shastra* tradition; temple dance of Tamil Nadu (earlier called Sadir/Dasi Attam).
- **Kathak** — the only classical form with strong **Mughal/Persian influence**; famous gharanas: **Jaipur, Lucknow, Banaras** (Raigarh is also counted).
- **Kathakali** — elaborate face makeup and costumes; stories from the epics; "Katha" = story, "Kali" = play.
- **Kuchipudi** — named after **Kuchipudi village** in Andhra Pradesh; combines dance and dialogue (dance-drama).
- **Sattriya** — introduced by the saint **Srimanta Sankardeva** (15th–16th century) in Assam's monasteries (*sattras*); recognised as classical in **2000** — the newest of the eight.
- **Odissi** — famous for the **tribhanga** (three-bend) posture; linked to the Jagannath temple tradition.

🧠 **Active Recall:** Close the book. Which two classical dances come from Kerala? Which one is the newest, and who founded it?

### 🪄 Mnemonic 1: The Eight Dances
**"Bharat Kee Kala, Mera Kala, Odisha Manipur Sat"**
**B**haratanatyam – **K**athak – **K**athakali – **M**ohiniyattam – **K**uchipudi – **O**dissi – **M**anipuri – **S**attriya

## 2. Classical Music: Two Great Streams

Indian classical music split into two systems around the 12th–13th century:

| Feature | Hindustani (North) | Carnatic (South) |
|---|---|---|
| Influence | Persian/Mughal influence | Purely indigenous, devotional |
| Key figure | Tansen (Akbar's court) | Trinity: Tyagaraja, Muthuswami Dikshitar, Syama Sastri |
| Improvisation | High (raga elaboration) | Composition-based (kriti) |
| Main forms | Khayal, Dhrupad, Thumri, Tappa | Kriti, Varnam, Padam |

**Gharanas (Hindustani)** — stylistic schools named after places: **Gwalior** (oldest khayal gharana), **Agra, Jaipur-Atrauli, Kirana, Patiala, Rampur-Sahaswan, Mewati**.

**Legendary musicians to remember:**
- **Tansen** — one of Akbar's Navaratnas; born Ramtanu Pandey; associated with Gwalior.
- **Bismillah Khan** — Shehnai; Bharat Ratna (2001).
- **M.S. Subbulakshmi** — Carnatic vocalist; first musician to receive the Bharat Ratna (1998).
- **Ravi Shankar** — Sitar; popularised Indian music globally.
- **Bhimsen Joshi** — Kirana gharana; Bharat Ratna (2008).

🧠 **Active Recall:** Name the Carnatic Trinity. Which gharana is considered the oldest for khayal singing?

## 3. Painting Traditions

- **Ajanta Caves (Maharashtra)** — Buddhist murals, roughly 2nd century BCE – 6th century CE; made with the **fresco/tempera** technique.
- **Mughal miniature** — flourished under **Akbar, Jahangir, Shah Jahan**; Jahangir's reign was its golden age; artists like **Basawan and Daswanth** (Akbar's court).
- **Rajput painting** — schools: **Mewar, Marwar, Bundi, Kishangarh** (famous for the *Bani Thani* painting by Nihal Chand — the "Indian Mona Lisa"), Kangra, Basohli (Pahari schools).
- **Madhubani (Mithila) painting** — Bihar; done traditionally by women; geometric patterns, natural dyes.
- **Tanjore painting** — Tamil Nadu; gold foil, embedded stones, religious themes.
- **Mysore, Pattachitra (Odisha), Kalamkari (Andhra Pradesh), Warli (Maharashtra)** — other frequently asked folk/tribal styles.

### 🪄 Mnemonic 2: Rajput Schools
**"MuMa BuKi KaBa"** — **Mu**ewar, **Ma**rwar, **Bu**ndi, **Ki**shangarh, **Ka**ngra, **Ba**sohli.

🧠 **Active Recall:** Where is the Bani Thani style from? Which caves hold Buddhist murals in Maharashtra?

## 5. Languages, Scripts & Literature

- The **Eighth Schedule** of the Constitution lists **22 scheduled languages**. Originally 14; Sindhi added (21st Amendment, 1967); Konkani, Manipuri, Nepali (71st Amendment, 1992); Bodo, Dogri, Maithili, Santhali (92nd Amendment, 2003).
- **Classical languages** (six): **Tamil** (first, 2004), Sanskrit (2005), Kannada and Telugu (2008), Malayalam (2013), Odia (2014).
- **Sangam literature** — ancient Tamil literature; three Sangams traditionally associated with Madurai.
- **Natya Shastra** — Bharata Muni; the foundational text on performing arts.
- **Kamasutra** — Vatsyayana; **Arthashastra** — Kautilya (Chanakya); examiners swap these authors deliberately.

### 🪄 Mnemonic 3: Classical Languages (in order)
**"Ta Sa Ka Te Ma O"** — **Ta**mil, **Sa**nskrit, **Ka**nnada, **Te**lugu, **Ma**layalam, **O**dia.

## 7. Architecture & Sculpture — Quick Anchors

- **Indus Valley** — Great Bath (Mohenjo-daro), Dancing Girl (bronze, lost-wax technique), Priest-King statue.
- **Mauryan** — Lion Capital of Sarnath (Ashoka) — our National Emblem; polished sandstone pillars.
- **Sun Temple, Konark** (Odisha) — built by **Narasimhadeva I** (Eastern Ganga dynasty, 13th century); the "Black Pagoda."
- **Khajuraho** (Madhya Pradesh) — Chandela dynasty; Kandariya Mahadeva temple.
- **Brihadeeswarar Temple** (Thanjavur) — built by **Rajaraja Chola I** (completed 1010 CE); UNESCO "Great Living Chola Temples."
- **Qutub Minar** — begun by **Qutb-ud-din Aibak**, completed by **Iltutmish** — a classic trap pair.
- **Hampi** — Vijayanagara Empire ruins (Karnataka); **Fatehpur Sikri** — Akbar's capital; Buland Darwaza.

## 🔗 Static-Dynamic Link

Culture is static, but its *recognition* keeps moving. **Garba of Gujarat** was inscribed on UNESCO's Intangible Heritage list in **2023**, and **Durga Puja in Kolkata** in **2021**. Recent inscriptions are hot favourites in current-affairs-linked static questions — always check the latest UNESCO list before your exam.

## ⚠️ Caution: Examiner's Trap

**Trap 1 — "Almost-True Statement" (लगभग सही कथन):**
*"Qutub Minar was built entirely by Qutb-ud-din Aibak."* — 90% believable, but false. Aibak only **began** it; **Iltutmish completed** it, and Firoz Shah Tughlaq later repaired/added storeys. Examiners love single-builder statements for multi-builder monuments.

**Trap 2 — "Similar Names" (समान नाम/वर्ष):**
**Kathak vs Kathakali vs Kuchipudi** — all start with "K", all classical, but from three different states (UP, Kerala, Andhra Pradesh). Similarly, **Mohiniyattam (Kerala) vs Manipuri (Manipur)** — both start with "M". Never answer by the "feel" of the name; answer by the state you memorised.

**Trap 3 — "Chronological Confusion" (कालानुक्रमिक भ्रम):**
Classical language declaration years: Tamil **2004**, Sanskrit **2005**, Kannada & Telugu **2008**, Malayalam **2013**, Odia **2014**. A question may say "Sanskrit was the first language to get classical status" — false; it was Tamil, a year earlier. One-year gaps are deliberate bait.
