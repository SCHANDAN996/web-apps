Questions 76–100: Medium

76. The headquarters of the International Labour Organization (ILO) is located in —
(a) New York (b) Paris (c) Geneva (d) Vienna
Answer: (c)
Solution: Many UN bodies sit in New York, which tempts that option, but the ILO, founded in 1919 under the Treaty of Versailles and now a UN specialised agency, has always been based in Geneva, Switzerland, so the answer is Geneva.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Political Science (International Organisations)

77. Which of the following is NOT a permanent member of the UN Security Council?
(a) France (b) Germany (c) Russia (d) United Kingdom
Answer: (b)
Solution: Germany is a frequent non-permanent member and a G4 aspirant, which makes it look plausible, but the five permanent members (P5) with veto power are the USA, Russia, China, France and the UK, so the answer is Germany.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Political Science (International Organisations)

78. The International Monetary Fund (IMF) was established following which conference?
(a) Yalta Conference (b) San Francisco Conference (c) Dumbarton Oaks Conference (d) Bretton Woods Conference
Answer: (d)
Solution: The San Francisco Conference (1945) produced the UN Charter, a classic chronological trap, but the IMF and the World Bank were conceived at the Bretton Woods Conference of 1944, so the answer is Bretton Woods Conference.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Political Science (International Organisations)

79. The term of a non-permanent member of the UN Security Council is —
(a) 1 year (b) 3 years (c) 2 years (d) 5 years
Answer: (c)
Solution: Five years is the term of the UN Secretary-General, a common confusion, but the ten non-permanent members of the Security Council are elected by the General Assembly for two-year terms, so the answer is 2 years.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Political Science (International Organisations)

80. Which UN organ is described as the "World Court"?
(a) UN General Assembly (b) UN Trusteeship Council (c) UN Economic and Social Council (d) International Court of Justice
Answer: (d)
Solution: The General Assembly is often called a "world parliament," which misleads some candidates, but the principal judicial organ of the UN, seated at The Hague, is the International Court of Justice, so the answer is International Court of Justice.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Political Science (International Organisations)

81. The World Trade Organization (WTO) replaced which body on 1 January 1995?
(a) GATT (b) UNCTAD (c) IMF (d) World Bank
Answer: (a)
Solution: UNCTAD still exists as a UN body on trade and development, so it cannot be the answer; the WTO succeeded the General Agreement on Tariffs and Trade (GATT, 1947) after the Uruguay Round, so the answer is GATT.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Economics (Indian Economic Development)

82. The headquarters of UNESCO is located in —
(a) Geneva (b) Rome (c) Paris (d) London
Answer: (c)
Solution: Geneva hosts WHO and ILO, which creates a similar-cities trap, but the UN Educational, Scientific and Cultural Organization (UNESCO, 1945) is headquartered in Paris, so the answer is Paris.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Political Science (International Organisations)

83. How many judges does the International Court of Justice have?
(a) 15 (b) 11 (c) 9 (d) 21
Answer: (a)
Solution: Nine is the number of judges on the US Supreme Court, a tempting cross-confusion, but the ICJ consists of 15 judges elected for nine-year terms by the General Assembly and the Security Council, so the answer is 15.
Source: Official website of the International Court of Justice (icj-cij.org)

84. Which of the following pairs is correctly matched?
(a) FAO — Rome (b) WHO — Vienna (c) IMF — Geneva (d) UNICEF — Washington D.C.
Answer: (a)
Solution: WHO is in Geneva, the IMF in Washington D.C. and UNICEF in New York, so each of those pairings is wrong; the Food and Agriculture Organization (FAO) is headquartered in Rome, so the answer is FAO — Rome.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Political Science (International Organisations)

85. The Secretary-General of the UN is appointed by the General Assembly on the recommendation of —
(a) The Security Council (b) The Economic and Social Council (c) The International Court of Justice (d) The Trusteeship Council
Answer: (a)
Solution: The General Assembly appoints, but only upon recommendation, and candidates often wrongly pick ECOSOC; under the UN Charter the Security Council recommends the Secretary-General to the General Assembly, so the answer is the Security Council.
Source: UN Charter, Article 97

86. Which organisation is known as the "lender of last resort" for countries facing balance-of-payments crises?
(a) World Bank (b) IMF (c) WTO (d) ADB
Answer: (b)
Solution: The World Bank funds long-term development projects, which confuses many, but short-term balance-of-payments support to member countries is the core role of the International Monetary Fund, so the answer is IMF.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Economics (Indian Economic Development)

87. The headquarters of the World Health Organization (WHO) is in —
(a) Paris (b) New York (c) Geneva (d) The Hague
Answer: (c)
Solution: The Hague hosts the ICJ and Paris hosts UNESCO, both tempting, but the WHO, established in 1948, is headquartered in Geneva, so the answer is Geneva.
Source: Official website of WHO (who.int)

88. Which of the following countries is NOT a member of SAARC?
(a) Bhutan (b) Maldives (c) Afghanistan (d) Myanmar
Answer: (d)
Solution: Afghanistan surprises many because it is not in "South" Asia strictly, yet it joined SAARC in 2007; Myanmar belongs to ASEAN, not SAARC, so the answer is Myanmar.
Source: Official website of SAARC (saarc-sec.org)

89. The New Development Bank (NDB), associated with BRICS, is headquartered in —
(a) Beijing (b) New Delhi (c) Shanghai (d) Moscow
Answer: (c)
Solution: Beijing is tempting because of China's role and the AIIB being there, but the BRICS New Development Bank, established in 2015, is headquartered in Shanghai, so the answer is Shanghai.
Source: Official website of the New Development Bank (ndb.int)

90. The Universal Declaration of Human Rights was adopted by the UN General Assembly in —
(a) 1945 (b) 1948 (c) 1950 (d) 1956
Answer: (b)
Solution: 1945 is the year of the UN Charter itself, a classic chronological trap, but the Universal Declaration of Human Rights was adopted on 10 December 1948 in Paris, so the answer is 1948.
Source: Official website of the United Nations (un.org)

91. Which principal organ of the UN suspended its operations in 1994 after the independence of Palau?
(a) Economic and Social Council (b) Trusteeship Council (c) Secretariat (d) Security Council
Answer: (b)
Solution: ECOSOC remains fully active, which rules it out; the Trusteeship Council suspended operations on 1 November 1994 when the last trust territory, Palau, became independent, so the answer is Trusteeship Council.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Political Science (International Organisations)

92. The Asian Development Bank (ADB) is headquartered in —
(a) Manila (b) Bangkok (c) Jakarta (d) Kuala Lumpur
Answer: (a)
Solution: Bangkok hosts ESCAP, a similar-sounding regional body that traps candidates, but the Asian Development Bank (1966) is headquartered in Manila, Philippines, so the answer is Manila.
Source: Official website of ADB (adb.org)

93. Which of the following is a specialised agency of the United Nations?
(a) UNICEF (b) FAO (c) UNDP (d) UNHCR
Answer: (b)
Solution: UNICEF, UNDP and UNHCR are funds and programmes of the General Assembly, not specialised agencies — a frequent classification trap; the FAO is a specialised agency linked to the UN through ECOSOC, so the answer is FAO.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Political Science (International Organisations)

94. The headquarters of the Organization of the Petroleum Exporting Countries (OPEC) is in —
(a) Riyadh (b) Baghdad (c) Geneva (d) Vienna
Answer: (d)
Solution: Riyadh tempts because Saudi Arabia dominates OPEC, but OPEC, founded in Baghdad in 1960, has its headquarters in Vienna, Austria, so the answer is Vienna.
Source: Official website of OPEC (opec.org)

95. How many members did the UN have at its founding in 1945?
(a) 51 (b) 45 (c) 55 (d) 60
Answer: (a)
Solution: The figure 193 (current membership) is well known and distracts, while 45 is simply invented; the UN was founded with 51 original member states, so the answer is 51.
Source: Official website of the United Nations (un.org)

96. The International Atomic Energy Agency (IAEA) is headquartered in —
(a) Vienna (b) Geneva (c) Paris (d) Rome
Answer: (a)
Solution: Geneva hosts many UN bodies and is the usual wrong guess, but the IAEA (1957), the "Atoms for Peace" agency, is headquartered in Vienna, so the answer is Vienna.
Source: Official website of IAEA (iaea.org)

97. Which country was the last to join the United Nations as its 193rd member?
(a) Switzerland (b) Montenegro (c) Timor-Leste (d) South Sudan
Answer: (d)
Solution: Switzerland (2002) and Montenegro (2006) joined late, which makes them tempting, but South Sudan became the 193rd member of the UN on 14 July 2011, so the answer is South Sudan.
Source: Official website of the United Nations (un.org)

98. The headquarters of the International Court of Justice is located in —
(a) Geneva (b) New York (c) The Hague (d) Brussels
Answer: (c)
Solution: New York hosts most principal UN organs, and the ICJ is the only one not located there — a classic trap; the ICJ sits at the Peace Palace in The Hague, Netherlands, so the answer is The Hague.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Political Science (International Organisations)

99. Which of the following statements about the World Bank is correct?
(a) It was established in 1955 after the Bandung Conference (b) It was created at the Bretton Woods Conference along with the IMF (c) It is headquartered in Geneva (d) It primarily gives short-term loans for balance-of-payments crises
Answer: (b)
Solution: Short-term balance-of-payments lending is the IMF's job, not the World Bank's — the most tempting wrong statement; the World Bank (IBRD, 1944) was created at Bretton Woods alongside the IMF and is headquartered in Washington D.C., so the answer is that it was created at the Bretton Woods Conference along with the IMF.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Economics (Indian Economic Development)

100. The Association of Southeast Asian Nations (ASEAN) was founded in which year?
(a) 1957 (b) 1985 (c) 1977 (d) 1967
Answer: (d)
Solution: 1957 is the year of the Treaty of Rome (EEC), a chronological confusion trap, but ASEAN was founded by the Bangkok Declaration on 8 August 1967 with five original members, so the answer is 1967.
Source: Official website of ASEAN (asean.org)
