# International Organizations

## The Hook: A Palace Built on Hope

In April 1945, as World War II still raged in the Pacific, delegates from 50 nations gathered in San Francisco with one audacious idea: never again. The League of Nations — humanity's first attempt at a world parliament — had collapsed after failing to stop Hitler, Mussolini, and Imperial Japan. Yet instead of giving up, the world tried again. On 24 October 1945, the United Nations was born. Today, that single date — **UN Day** — is one of the most frequently asked facts in competitive exams, and this chapter will make sure you never miss it.

Why does this matter to you? Because every major exam — SSC CGL, IBPS PO, State PCS — asks questions from international organizations: headquarters, founding years, member counts, and the classic trap of confusing similar-sounding bodies (UNCTAD vs. UNICEF, ILO vs. IMO). Master this chapter and those marks are yours.

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this chapter, you will be able to:

## Part 1: The United Nations — The Big One

### Birth of the UN

- **Founded:** 24 October 1945 (celebrated as UN Day)
- **Charter signed:** 26 June 1945, San Francisco, USA
- **Headquarters:** New York City, USA
- **Members:** 193 (South Sudan, 2011, was the most recent to join)
- **Official languages:** 6 — Arabic, Chinese, English, French, Russian, Spanish

**Mnemonic for the 6 languages:** "**A**ll **C**lever **E**xam **F**ighters **R**ock **S**SC" — Arabic, Chinese, English, French, Russian, Spanish.

### The Six Principal Organs

| Organ | Headquarters | Key Fact |
|---|---|---|
| General Assembly | New York | All 193 members; "parliament of nations" |
| Security Council | New York | 15 members: 5 permanent + 10 non-permanent |
| ECOSOC | New York | 54 members; economic & social matters |
| Trusteeship Council | New York | Suspended operations in 1994 (Palau's independence) |
| International Court of Justice | **The Hague, Netherlands** | Only principal organ NOT in New York |
| Secretariat | New York | Headed by the Secretary-General |

🧠 **Active Recall:** Close the book. Which UN organ sits in The Hague? How many permanent members does the Security Council have? Name them.

**The P5 (Permanent Five):** USA, UK, France, Russia, China — each holds **veto power**.

**Mnemonic for P5:** "**U**ncle **U**sed **F**rench **R**olls **C**arefully" — USA, UK, France, Russia, China.

## Part 2: UN Specialized Agencies — The Examiner's Goldmine

This is where exams strike hardest. Learn this table cold:

| Agency | Full Form | HQ | Founded |
|---|---|---|---|
| FAO | Food and Agriculture Organization | Rome, Italy | 1945 |
| UNESCO | UN Educational, Scientific and Cultural Organization | Paris, France | 1945 |
| WHO | World Health Organization | Geneva, Switzerland | 1948 |
| ILO | International Labour Organization | Geneva, Switzerland | 1919 |
| IMF | International Monetary Fund | Washington D.C., USA | 1944 |
| World Bank (IBRD) | International Bank for Reconstruction and Development | Washington D.C., USA | 1944 |
| ICAO | International Civil Aviation Organization | Montreal, Canada | 1944 |
| IMO | International Maritime Organization | London, UK | 1948 |
| WIPO | World Intellectual Property Organization | Geneva, Switzerland | 1967 |
| UNICEF | UN Children's Fund | New York, USA | 1946 |
| WTO | World Trade Organization | Geneva, Switzerland | 1995 |

**Key traps to note:**
- **ILO (1919)** is the *oldest* — it predates the UN itself (it was a League of Nations body).
- **IMF and World Bank** were born at the **Bretton Woods Conference (1944)** — both in Washington D.C.
- **WTO (1995)** replaced **GATT (1947)** — a classic chronological trap.
- **UNICEF is a fund, not a specialized agency** — a subtle distinction examiners exploit.

🧠 **Active Recall:** Without looking — where are FAO, UNESCO, and WHO headquartered? Which agency is oldest? What did WTO replace?

**Mnemonic for the "Geneva Gang":** "**W**e **I**nvite **W**orld **W**orkers" — WHO, ILO, WIPO, WTO are all in Geneva.

## Part 4: Economic & Financial Bodies

- **IMF (1944, Washington D.C.):** Stability of exchange rates, balance-of-payments support. Members: 190+.
- **World Bank Group (1944, Washington D.C.):** Five institutions — IBRD, IDA, IFC, MIGA, ICSID. Focus: development and poverty reduction.
- **WTO (1995, Geneva):** Successor to GATT; administers global trade rules; 164 members.
- **ADB (Asian Development Bank, 1966):** HQ **Manila, Philippines**.
- **AIIB (Asian Infrastructure Investment Bank, 2016):** HQ **Beijing, China**.
- **NDB (New Development Bank, 2015):** BRICS bank; HQ **Shanghai, China**.

**Trap alert:** ADB is in **Manila**, not Tokyo. AIIB is in **Beijing**, NDB in **Shanghai** — examiners love swapping these.

## Part 5: Judicial, Humanitarian & Miscellaneous Bodies

- **International Court of Justice (ICJ):** The Hague, Netherlands — settles disputes *between states*.
- **International Criminal Court (ICC):** Also The Hague — prosecutes *individuals* for genocide, war crimes. Established by the **Rome Statute (1998)**, functioning from 2002. **India is not a member.**
- **Interpol:** HQ **Lyon, France** (not Paris!).
- **Amnesty International:** London, UK (Nobel Peace Prize 1977).
- **Red Cross (ICRC):** Geneva, Switzerland (founded 1863; Nobel Peace Prizes 1917, 1944, 1963).
- **UNHCR:** Geneva, Switzerland (Nobel Peace Prize 1954, 1981).

🧠 **Active Recall:** ICJ vs. ICC — what's the difference? Where is Interpol? Which humanitarian body has won the Nobel Peace Prize three times?

## 🔗 Static-Dynamic Link

- **NATO expansion:** Finland joined in 2023 and Sweden in 2024, taking NATO to 32 members — the biggest recent change in any organization's membership. Expect this in current-affairs-linked static questions.
- **BRICS expansion (2024):** Egypt, Ethiopia, Iran, Saudi Arabia, and the UAE joined, expanding the bloc beyond its original five.
- **G20 presidency:** India held the G20 presidency in 2023, and the **African Union became a permanent G20 member** — linking regional bodies to global economic governance.

## ⚠️ Caution: Examiner's Trap

**Trap 1 — Similar Names (समान नाम):** *UNESCO vs. UNICEF vs. UNCTAD vs. UNDP.* Examiners place all four in options with different headquarters. Remember: UNESCO → Paris, UNICEF → New York, UNDP → New York, UNCTAD → Geneva.

**Trap 2 — Chronological Confusion (कालानुक्रमिक भ्रम):** *"GATT was replaced by the WTO in 1947."* Sounds plausible — but GATT was *created* in 1947 and replaced by WTO in **1995**. The trap swaps founding and replacement years.

**Trap 3 — Almost-Correct Statement (लगभग सही कथन):** *"All six principal organs of the UN are headquartered in New York."* Five are — but the **ICJ sits in The Hague**. One word makes the whole statement false.

**Trap 4 — Negative Phrasing (नकारात्मक वाक्यांश):** *"Which of the following is NOT a UN specialized agency?"* — and the answer is **UNICEF** (it's a fund/programme). Read the "NOT" twice before answering.

## Summary

The UN system anchors this chapter: 1945, New York, 193 members, six organs (ICJ in The Hague). Specialized agencies cluster by city — Geneva (WHO, ILO, WIPO, WTO), Washington D.C. (IMF, World Bank), Paris (UNESCO), Rome (FAO). Regional bodies: SAARC–Kathmandu, ASEAN–Jakarta, EU & NATO–Brussels, OPEC–Vienna, African Union–Addis Ababa. Financial newcomers: ADB–Manila, AIIB–Beijing, NDB–Shanghai.
