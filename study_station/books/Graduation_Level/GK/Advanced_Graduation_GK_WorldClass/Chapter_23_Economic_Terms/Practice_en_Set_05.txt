101. Which of the following best describes "stagflation"?
(a) High growth with high inflation (b) Stagnant growth combined with high inflation and unemployment (c) Falling prices with rising output (d) Rapid growth with low unemployment
Answer: (b)
Solution: The tempting wrong option is high growth with high inflation, which is simply demand-pull inflation during a boom, not stagflation. Stagflation, a term popularised after the 1970s oil shocks, means stagnation (low growth, high unemployment) occurring together with inflation, so the answer is stagnant growth combined with high inflation and unemployment.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Macroeconomics

102. The "Laffer Curve" illustrates the relationship between:
(a) Tax rates and tax revenue (b) Inflation and unemployment (c) Income and consumption (d) Money supply and price level
Answer: (a)
Solution: The tempting wrong option is inflation and unemployment, which is the Phillips Curve, not the Laffer Curve — a classic similar-concept trap. The Laffer Curve shows that beyond a certain tax rate, higher rates reduce revenue by discouraging work and compliance, so the answer is tax rates and tax revenue.
Source: PYQ-style

103. In national income accounting, "transfer payments" are:
(a) Included in GDP because they are government expenditure (b) Included in GDP at market prices only (c) Excluded from GDP because no goods or services are produced in return (d) Counted as investment expenditure
Answer: (c)
Solution: The tempting wrong option is inclusion in GDP as government expenditure, but only government purchases of goods and services enter GDP. Transfer payments like pensions and scholarships are one-way payments with no current production in exchange, so the answer is excluded from GDP because no goods or services are produced in return.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Macroeconomics

104. "Disguised unemployment" is most commonly found in India in:
(a) The organised manufacturing sector (b) The IT services sector (c) The agricultural sector (d) Public administration
Answer: (c)
Solution: The tempting wrong option is organised manufacturing, where surplus labour is usually retrenched openly. Disguised unemployment means more workers are engaged than needed, so removing some does not reduce output — typical of Indian family farming, so the answer is the agricultural sector.

105. Which of the following is a "flow" variable?
(a) Wealth of a household (b) Capital stock of a firm (c) Money supply on a given date (d) National income
Answer: (d)
Solution: The tempting wrong options are wealth, capital stock and money supply, all of which are measured at a point of time and are stock variables. National income is measured over a period of time (usually a year), so the answer is national income.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Macroeconomics

106. "Core inflation" in India is measured by excluding which items from the CPI?
(a) Housing and health (b) Food and fuel (c) Clothing and footwear (d) Education and transport
Answer: (b)
Solution: The tempting wrong options include housing or transport, but these remain in core inflation. Core inflation strips out the volatile food and fuel groups to reveal the underlying price trend, so the answer is food and fuel.
Source: PYQ-style

107. The "impossible trinity" (trilemma) in international economics states that a country cannot simultaneously have:
(a) Free trade, fixed exchange rate and high growth (b) Convertible currency, high reserves and low interest rates (c) Low inflation, low unemployment and low fiscal deficit (d) Free capital movement, fixed exchange rate and independent monetary policy
Answer: (d)
Solution: The tempting wrong option is the low inflation–unemployment–deficit mix, which is a policy dilemma but not the trilemma. The Mundell-Fleming trilemma says only two of the three — free capital flows, fixed exchange rate, sovereign monetary policy — can coexist, so the answer is free capital movement, fixed exchange rate and independent monetary policy.
Source: PYQ-style

108. "Helicopter money" refers to:
(a) Direct distribution of money to the public financed by money creation (b) Government borrowing from the RBI through bonds (c) Foreign aid received by the central government (d) Emergency credit lines to banks
Answer: (a)
Solution: The tempting wrong option is government borrowing through bonds, which is deficit financing in the conventional sense, not helicopter money. Helicopter money, a term associated with Milton Friedman, means printing money and giving it directly to citizens to boost demand, so the answer is direct distribution of money to the public financed by money creation.
Source: PYQ-style

109. If the RBI wants to reduce liquidity in the banking system, it should:
(a) Reduce the repo rate (b) Increase the Cash Reserve Ratio (c) Purchase government securities in the open market (d) Reduce the Statutory Liquidity Ratio
Answer: (b)
Solution: The tempting wrong options are cutting the repo rate and buying securities, but both inject liquidity rather than absorb it — a classic reverse-logic trap. Raising the CRR forces banks to park more funds with the RBI, reducing lendable resources, so the answer is increase the Cash Reserve Ratio.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Macroeconomics

110. The "Gini coefficient" of 0 and 1 respectively indicate:
(a) Perfect inequality and perfect equality (b) Zero poverty and full poverty (c) Perfect equality and perfect inequality (d) No growth and maximum growth
Answer: (c)
Solution: The tempting wrong option reverses the two values, which is the most common examiner trap on this topic. A Gini of 0 means everyone has equal income (perfect equality) and 1 means one person has all the income (perfect inequality), so the answer is perfect equality and perfect inequality.
Source: PYQ-style

111. "Fiscal drag" occurs when:
(a) High fiscal deficit crowds out private investment (b) Interest payments consume most of the revenue (c) Government spending rises faster than revenue (d) Inflation pushes taxpayers into higher tax brackets, reducing disposable income
Answer: (d)
Solution: The tempting wrong option is crowding out, which is a real concept but a different one — a similar-name trap. Fiscal drag refers to the automatic rise in tax burden as nominal incomes rise with inflation under progressive taxation, so the answer is inflation pushes taxpayers into higher tax brackets, reducing disposable income.
Source: PYQ-style

112. Which of the following is an example of a "merit good"?
(a) Cigarettes (b) Primary education (c) Luxury cars (d) Petrol
Answer: (b)
Solution: The tempting wrong option is cigarettes, which is a demerit good whose consumption governments discourage. Merit goods like primary education and vaccination are under-consumed if left to the market, so the state promotes them, so the answer is primary education.
Source: PYQ-style

113. The "base effect" in inflation measurement refers to:
(a) The influence of the price level of the corresponding period a year ago on the current inflation rate (b) The weight of food items in the index (c) The revision of the base year of the index (d) The minimum support price effect on farm goods
Answer: (a)
Solution: The tempting wrong option is revision of the base year, which is a statistical exercise unrelated to the base effect — a similar-sounding trap. The base effect means current inflation looks high or low partly because prices a year ago were unusually low or high, so the answer is the influence of the price level of the corresponding period a year ago on the current inflation rate.
Source: PYQ-style

114. "Seigniorage" is best defined as:
(a) Revenue earned by the government/central bank from issuing currency (b) Profit earned by commercial banks on loans (c) Tax collected on imports of gold (d) Dividend paid by RBI to the government
Answer: (a)
Solution: The tempting wrong option is the RBI dividend, which is a transfer of surplus, not seigniorage. Seigniorage is the difference between the face value of money and its cost of production, accruing to the issuer, so the answer is revenue earned by the government/central bank from issuing currency.
Source: PYQ-style

115. Under the "quantity theory of money" (Fisher's version), if money supply doubles while velocity and output are constant, the price level will:
(a) Double (b) Remain unchanged (c) Halve (d) Rise by 50 per cent
Answer: (a)
Solution: The tempting wrong option is a 50 per cent rise, which confuses proportionate change with a partial one. From MV = PT, with V and T constant, P moves in direct proportion to M, so doubling M doubles P, so the answer is double.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Macroeconomics

116. "Crowding out" in macroeconomics refers to:
(a) Foreign firms displacing domestic firms (b) Automation reducing employment (c) Imports replacing domestic production (d) Government borrowing raising interest rates and reducing private investment
Answer: (d)
Solution: The tempting wrong option is imports replacing domestic output, which is a trade phenomenon, not the fiscal concept. Crowding out occurs when heavy government borrowing pushes up interest rates, making credit costlier for private investors, so the answer is government borrowing raising interest rates and reducing private investment.
Source: PYQ-style

117. Which of the following correctly describes "Gross National Product" (GNP)?
(a) GDP at constant prices (b) GDP minus depreciation (c) GDP plus indirect taxes minus subsidies (d) GDP plus net factor income from abroad
Answer: (d)
Solution: The tempting wrong option is GDP minus depreciation, which gives Net Domestic Product, not GNP — a classic formula-confusion trap. GNP adds net factor income earned from abroad to GDP, so the answer is GDP plus net factor income from abroad.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Macroeconomics

118. A "regressive tax" is one which:
(a) Takes a larger proportion of income from the poor than from the rich (b) Increases in rate as income rises (c) Is levied only on luxury goods (d) Is levied on wealth rather than income
Answer: (a)
Solution: The tempting wrong option is a rising rate with income, which describes a progressive tax — the exact opposite, a frequent reversal trap. A regressive tax burdens lower incomes proportionally more, as with uniform indirect taxes on essentials, so the answer is takes a larger proportion of income from the poor than from the rich.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Indian Economic Development

119. The "Phillips Curve" in its original form shows:
(a) A positive relationship between inflation and growth (b) An inverse relationship between savings and investment (c) A direct relationship between money supply and interest rates (d) An inverse relationship between unemployment and wage/price inflation
Answer: (d)
Solution: The tempting wrong option is inflation and growth, which is not what Phillips studied. A.W. Phillips found that low unemployment historically coincided with high wage inflation and vice versa, so the answer is an inverse relationship between unemployment and wage/price inflation.
Source: PYQ-style

120. "Twin deficits" hypothesis links the fiscal deficit with:
(a) Revenue deficit (b) Current account deficit (c) Capital account deficit (d) Primary deficit
Answer: (b)
Solution: The tempting wrong option is revenue deficit, which is a component of fiscal deficit, not its twin. The twin deficits idea holds that a high fiscal deficit spills over into a high current account deficit through higher imports and interest rates, so the answer is current account deficit.
Source: PYQ-style

121. Consider the following statements about "liquidity trap":
1. It occurs when interest rates are very low and monetary policy becomes ineffective.
2. In a liquidity trap, people prefer to hold money rather than bonds.
3. It is associated with the Keynesian analysis of the Great Depression.
Which of the above are correct?
(a) 1 and 2 only (b) 2 and 3 only (c) 1, 2 and 3 (d) 1 and 3 only
Answer: (c)
Solution: The tempting wrong options drop one statement each, but all three hold: at near-zero rates, bond prices can only fall, so people hoard money, rate cuts lose power, and Keynes used this idea to explain Depression-era policy failure. All three statements are correct, so the answer is 1, 2 and 3.
Source: PYQ-style

122. Arrange the following monetary aggregates of India from the narrowest to the broadest:
1. M1
2. M2
3. M3
4. M4
(a) 2-1-3-4 (b) 1-2-3-4 (c) 1-3-2-4 (d) 4-3-2-1
Answer: (b)
Solution: The tempting wrong option reverses the order, a chronological/ordering trap. M1 (currency plus demand deposits) is narrowest; M2 adds savings deposits with post offices, M3 adds time deposits with banks, and M4 adds total post office deposits, so the answer is 1-2-3-4.
Source: PYQ-style

123. If the marginal propensity to consume (MPC) in an economy is 0.8, the value of the investment multiplier is:
(a) 4 (b) 8 (c) 5 (d) 0.2
Answer: (c)
Solution: The tempting wrong option is 4, which results from wrongly using MPC itself (1/0.8 = 1.25 is another slip) or confusing the formula. The multiplier is 1/(1 − MPC) = 1/0.2 = 5, so the answer is 5.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Macroeconomics

124. Consider the following statements about "Purchasing Power Parity" (PPP):
1. PPP exchange rates equalise the price of an identical basket of goods across countries.
2. India's GDP measured at PPP is smaller than its GDP at market exchange rates.
Which of the above is/are correct?
(a) Neither 1 nor 2 (b) 2 only (c) Both 1 and 2 (d) 1 only
Answer: (d)
Solution: The tempting wrong option is "both", but statement 2 is false — because goods and services are cheaper in India, GDP at PPP is larger, not smaller, than at market exchange rates; this is the classic trap. Statement 1 correctly defines PPP, so the answer is 1 only.
Source: PYQ-style

125. Consider the following statements about the "GDP deflator":
1. It is the ratio of nominal GDP to real GDP multiplied by 100.
2. Unlike CPI, it covers all goods and services produced domestically, including capital goods.
3. It includes the prices of imported consumer goods.
Which of the above are correct?
(a) 1 and 2 only (b) 2 and 3 only (c) 1 and 3 only (d) 1, 2 and 3
Answer: (a)
Solution: The tempting wrong option is "all three", but statement 3 is false — the GDP deflator covers only domestically produced output, so import prices are excluded, unlike CPI which includes them; this is the examiner's trap. Statements 1 and 2 are correct, so the answer is 1 and 2 only.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Macroeconomics
