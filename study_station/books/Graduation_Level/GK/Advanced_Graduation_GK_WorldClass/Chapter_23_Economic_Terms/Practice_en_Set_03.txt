Questions 51–75: Medium

51. Which of the following best describes 'stagflation'?
(a) High growth with low inflation (b) Rapid inflation caused by excess demand (c) Falling prices with rising output (d) Stagnant growth combined with high inflation and unemployment
Answer: (d)
Solution: Many students pick the excess-demand option because inflation is involved, but that describes demand-pull inflation, not stagflation. Stagflation is the unusual combination of a stagnant economy, high unemployment and high inflation together, so the answer is stagnant growth combined with high inflation and unemployment.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Macroeconomics

52. The 'Laffer Curve' illustrates the relationship between:
(a) Tax rates and tax revenue (b) Inflation and unemployment (c) Interest rates and savings (d) Money supply and price level
Answer: (a)
Solution: The inflation-unemployment trade-off is the Phillips Curve, a common confusion. The Laffer Curve shows that beyond a certain tax rate, revenue falls as compliance and work incentives decline, so the answer is tax rates and tax revenue.
Source: PYQ-style

53. 'Helicopter money' refers to:
(a) Money spent on defence aviation (b) Emergency loans given to banks (c) Foreign aid received by a country (d) Printing and distributing money directly to the public to stimulate demand
Answer: (d)
Solution: The defence-aviation option is a literal-word trap. Helicopter money, a term popularised by Milton Friedman, means central bank money given directly to citizens to boost spending, so the answer is printing and distributing money directly to the public to stimulate demand.
Source: PYQ-style

54. Which of the following is NOT a function of money?
(a) Medium of exchange (b) Measure of value (c) Standard of deferred payment (d) Source of income
Answer: (d)
Solution: The first three options are the classical functions of money, so they are tempting distractors. Money facilitates exchange, valuation and future payments but is not itself a source of income, so the answer is source of income.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Macroeconomics

55. The 'Gini coefficient' measures:
(a) Inflation inequality between sectors (b) Gender disparity in education (c) Income or wealth inequality within a nation (d) Regional imbalance in development
Answer: (c)
Solution: The other options sound plausible because all involve disparities, but the Gini coefficient specifically quantifies income or wealth distribution, ranging from 0 (perfect equality) to 1 (perfect inequality), so the answer is income or wealth inequality within a nation.
Source: PYQ-style

56. 'Crowding out' in economics occurs when:
(a) Exports fall due to rising imports (b) Private investment falls because of increased government borrowing (c) Population growth reduces per capita income (d) Banks refuse loans to small borrowers
Answer: (b)
Solution: The population option is a trap based on the everyday meaning of "crowding". In macroeconomics, crowding out means heavy government borrowing raises interest rates and reduces private investment, so the answer is private investment falls because of increased government borrowing.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Macroeconomics

57. Which of the following is an example of a 'regressive tax'?
(a) Income tax with rising slabs (b) Corporate tax on profits (c) Wealth tax on large estates (d) A uniform sales tax on essential goods
Answer: (d)
Solution: Income tax with rising slabs is progressive, the opposite of what is asked — a classic trap. A uniform sales tax takes a larger share of a poor person's income than a rich person's, so the answer is a uniform sales tax on essential goods.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Indian Economic Development

58. The 'impossible trinity' (trilemma) states that a country cannot simultaneously have:
(a) Free trade, low inflation and high growth (b) Fiscal deficit, current account deficit and low debt (c) Fixed exchange rate, free capital movement and independent monetary policy (d) Public sector, private sector and joint sector
Answer: (c)
Solution: The first option mixes unrelated desirable goals and is a distractor. The Mundell-Fleming trilemma says only two of the three — fixed exchange rate, free capital flows, sovereign monetary policy — can coexist, so the answer is fixed exchange rate, free capital movement and independent monetary policy.
Source: PYQ-style

59. 'Seigniorage' refers to:
(a) Tax collected on imports (b) Profit earned by the government from issuing currency (c) Interest paid on government bonds (d) Revenue from disinvestment
Answer: (b)
Solution: The import-tax option describes customs duty, a similar-sounding revenue source that traps many candidates. Seigniorage is the difference between the face value of money and its cost of production, so the answer is profit earned by the government from issuing currency.
Source: PYQ-style

60. Which concept describes a market with a single buyer?
(a) Monopoly (b) Oligopoly (c) Monopsony (d) Duopoly
Answer: (c)
Solution: Monopoly is the tempting wrong answer, but it means a single seller, not a single buyer — a classic similar-name trap. A market with one buyer is a monopsony, so the answer is monopsony.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Microeconomics

61. The 'paradox of thrift' suggests that:
(a) Saving more always makes individuals richer (b) Banks fail when people deposit too much (c) If everyone saves more simultaneously, aggregate demand and income may fall (d) Thrift leads to higher inflation
Answer: (c)
Solution: The first option is the almost-correct trap — saving helps an individual but not necessarily the whole economy. Keynes argued that universal higher saving cuts consumption, demand and hence national income, so the answer is if everyone saves more simultaneously, aggregate demand and income may fall.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Macroeconomics

62. 'Core inflation' excludes which of the following?
(a) Manufactured goods
(b) Food and fuel prices
(c) Housing costs
(d) Services
Answer: (b)
Solution: Manufactured goods are actually part of core inflation, making that option a trap. Core inflation removes volatile food and fuel components to reveal the underlying price trend, so the answer is food and fuel prices.
Source: PYQ-style

63. The 'quantity theory of money' is expressed by the equation:
(a) MV = PT (b) MP = VT (c) MT = PV (d) PQ = MV
Answer: (a)
Solution: The other equations are shuffled versions designed to confuse. Fisher's equation states money supply times velocity equals price level times transactions, so the answer is MV = PT.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Macroeconomics

64. 'Dutch disease' refers to:
(a) Decline of manufacturing after a natural resource boom raises the currency's value (b) A pandemic affecting trade (c) Excessive dependence on imports (d) Collapse of the banking sector
Answer: (a)
Solution: The pandemic option exploits the word "disease" literally — a trap. Dutch disease describes how resource export earnings appreciate the currency and hurt other export industries, so the answer is decline of manufacturing after a natural resource boom raises the currency's value.
Source: PYQ-style

65. Which of the following best defines 'opportunity cost'?
(a) The money spent on a good (b) The value of the next best alternative foregone (c) The total cost of production (d) The sunk cost of a project
Answer: (b)
Solution: The money-spent option is the accounting cost, a common confusion. Opportunity cost is what you give up — the next best alternative — when making a choice, so the answer is the value of the next best alternative foregone.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Microeconomics

66. 'Moral hazard' arises when:
(a) Banks lend only to government (b) Interest rates are too high (c) A party takes more risks because it is protected from the consequences (d) Depositors withdraw money in panic
Answer: (c)
Solution: The bank-run option describes a liquidity crisis, not moral hazard — a tempting distractor. Moral hazard occurs when insurance or bailouts encourage reckless behaviour, so the answer is a party takes more risks because it is protected from the consequences.
Source: PYQ-style

67. The 'Kuznets curve' hypothesises that as an economy develops:
(a) Agriculture's share keeps rising (b) Inflation steadily declines (c) Savings rate continuously falls (d) Inequality first rises and then falls
Answer: (d)
Solution: The inflation option is unrelated to Kuznets and traps candidates who confuse it with the Phillips curve. Kuznets proposed an inverted-U relationship between development and inequality, so the answer is inequality first rises and then falls.
Source: PYQ-style

68. 'Adverse selection' in insurance markets means:
(a) Insurers charging uniform premiums (b) High-risk individuals being more likely to buy insurance than low-risk ones (c) Insurance companies making excess profits (d) Government regulating premiums
Answer: (b)
Solution: Uniform premiums are the cause, not the meaning, of adverse selection — a subtle trap. Because of asymmetric information, those most likely to claim are most eager to buy cover, so the answer is high-risk individuals being more likely to buy insurance than low-risk ones.
Source: PYQ-style

69. Which of the following is a 'flow' variable?
(a) National income (b) Money supply at a point in time (c) National wealth (d) Capital stock
Answer: (a)
Solution: Wealth, money supply and capital stock are all measured at a point in time, making them stock variables — the trap here. National income is measured over a period, so the answer is national income.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Macroeconomics

70. The 'Phillips curve' shows an inverse relationship between:
(a) Inflation and unemployment (b) Growth and inequality (c) Savings and investment (d) Exports and imports
Answer: (a)
Solution: Growth-inequality is the Kuznets relationship, a frequent confusion in exams. The Phillips curve depicts the short-run trade-off between inflation and unemployment, so the answer is inflation and unemployment.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Macroeconomics

71. 'Tobin's q' ratio compares:
(a) Market value of a firm's assets to their replacement cost (b) Exports to imports (c) Tax revenue to GDP (d) Debt to equity
Answer: (a)
Solution: The debt-equity option is a familiar financial ratio that lures candidates. Tobin's q guides investment decisions by comparing market valuation with replacement cost, so the answer is market value of a firm's assets to their replacement cost.
Source: PYQ-style

72. 'Purchasing Power Parity' (PPP) exchange rate is based on:
(a) The price of an identical basket of goods in different countries (b) Gold reserves of countries (c) Interest rate differentials (d) Trade balances
Answer: (a)
Solution: Interest-rate differentials relate to interest rate parity, a similar-sounding concept that traps many. PPP compares what a common basket costs across countries, so the answer is the price of an identical basket of goods in different countries.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Macroeconomics

73. 'Engel's law' states that as income rises:
(a) The tax burden falls (b) Total savings fall (c) Spending on luxuries falls (d) The proportion of income spent on food falls
Answer: (d)
Solution: The savings option reverses actual behaviour and is a distractor. Engel observed that food's share of the budget shrinks as households get richer, so the answer is the proportion of income spent on food falls.
Source: PYQ-style

74. 'Liquidity trap' occurs when:
(a) Banks run out of cash (b) Interest rates are so low that monetary policy becomes ineffective (c) Inflation erodes all savings (d) Foreign capital stops flowing in
Answer: (b)
Solution: The banks-running-out-of-cash option confuses liquidity trap with a bank run — a classic trap. In a liquidity trap, people hoard money despite near-zero rates, blunting monetary policy, so the answer is interest rates are so low that monetary policy becomes ineffective.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Macroeconomics

75. 'Okun's law' describes the relationship between:
(a) Wages and productivity (b) Money supply and inflation (c) Unemployment and GDP growth (d) Interest rates and investment
Answer: (c)
Solution: The money-inflation option belongs to the quantity theory of money, a tempting confusion. Okun's law links a fall in unemployment to faster-than-trend GDP growth, so the answer is unemployment and GDP growth.
Source: PYQ-style
