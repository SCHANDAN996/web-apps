Questions 76–100: Medium

76. Which of the following best describes "stagflation"?
(a) Stagnant growth combined with high inflation and unemployment (b) High growth with high inflation (c) Falling prices with rising output (d) Low inflation with low interest rates
Answer: (a)
Solution: The tempting wrong choice is the idea that inflation always accompanies high growth; stagflation is precisely the abnormal case where inflation persists despite a stagnant economy and rising unemployment, as seen in the 1970s oil-shock economies. It combines stagnation with inflation, so the answer is stagnant growth combined with high inflation and unemployment.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Economics (Introductory Macroeconomics)

77. The "Laffer Curve" illustrates the relationship between:
(a) Inflation and unemployment (b) Tax rates and tax revenue (c) Money supply and price level (d) Income and consumption
Answer: (b)
Solution: A common trap is confusing it with the Phillips Curve, which links inflation and unemployment. The Laffer Curve shows that beyond a certain tax rate, revenue falls because high rates discourage work and encourage evasion, so the answer is tax rates and tax revenue.
Source: PYQ-style

78. In national income accounting, "transfer payments" are:
(a) Included in GDP as government expenditure (b) Counted as investment (c) Excluded from GDP because no goods or services are produced in return (d) Added to exports
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is to treat all government spending as part of GDP; only government purchases of goods and services count. Pensions, scholarships and subsidies are one-way payments with no current production in return, so they are excluded from GDP, and the answer is that they are excluded because no goods or services are produced in return.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Economics (Introductory Macroeconomics)

79. "Liquidity trap", a term associated with Keynes, refers to a situation where:
(a) Banks run out of cash reserves (b) Interest rates are so low that monetary policy becomes ineffective (c) Foreign exchange reserves are exhausted (d) Inflation erodes all savings
Answer: (b)
Solution: The misleading option is the literal image of banks lacking cash. In a liquidity trap, people prefer holding money at near-zero interest rates, so further increases in money supply do not lower rates or stimulate demand; monetary policy loses traction, so the answer is that interest rates are so low that monetary policy becomes ineffective.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Economics (Introductory Macroeconomics)

80. The "Gini coefficient" measures:
(a) Inflation volatility (b) Trade openness (c) Income or wealth inequality within a nation (d) Fiscal deficit as a share of GDP
Answer: (c)
Solution: A frequent confusion is with the Lorenz curve's other uses or with poverty headcount ratios. The Gini coefficient ranges from 0 (perfect equality) to 1 (perfect inequality) and summarises the Lorenz curve, so the answer is income or wealth inequality within a nation.
Source: PYQ-style

81. Which of the following is an example of a "regressive tax"?
(a) Income tax with rising slabs (b) Wealth tax on large estates (c) A uniform sales tax on essential goods (d) Corporate tax on profits
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is assuming all indirect taxes are proportional. A uniform tax on essentials takes a larger share of a poor person's income than of a rich person's, making it regressive in effect, so the answer is a uniform sales tax on essential goods.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Economics (Indian Economic Development)

82. "Crowding out" in macroeconomics occurs when:
(a) Exports displace domestic production (b) Population growth reduces per capita income (c) Foreign firms drive out domestic firms (d) Government borrowing raises interest rates and reduces private investment
Answer: (d)
Solution: The tempting wrong reading is about foreign competition displacing domestic firms. Crowding out is a fiscal-monetary phenomenon: heavy government borrowing pushes up interest rates, leaving less room for private investment, so the answer is that government borrowing raises interest rates and reduces private investment.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Economics (Introductory Macroeconomics)

83. The "Phillips Curve" suggests, in the short run:
(a) A positive relation between inflation and growth (b) An inverse relation between inflation and unemployment (c) A direct relation between money supply and output (d) No relation between wages and prices
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is to extend it to the long run, where economists argue the trade-off vanishes (vertical long-run Phillips curve). In the short run it shows lower unemployment accompanying higher inflation, so the answer is an inverse relation between inflation and unemployment.
Source: PYQ-style

84. "Purchasing Power Parity" (PPP) exchange rates are used primarily to:
(a) Fix currency values to gold (b) Determine import duties (c) Compare living standards and real output across countries (d) Set interest rates across borders
Answer: (c)
Solution: The misleading option is that PPP is a currency-fixing mechanism; it is not an exchange-rate regime. PPP adjusts for price-level differences so that GDP and incomes can be compared meaningfully across countries, so the answer is to compare living standards and real output across countries.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Economics (Indian Economic Development)

85. Which of the following is NOT a function of money?
(a) Generator of inflation (b) Measure of value (c) Standard of deferred payment (d) Medium of exchange
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap lies in the negative wording of the question — students often pick a genuine function in haste. The classical functions are medium of exchange, measure of value, store of value and standard of deferred payment; inflation generation is not a function of money, so the answer is generator of inflation.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Economics (Introductory Macroeconomics)

86. "Fiscal drag" refers to:
(a) Rising tax burdens due to inflation pushing incomes into higher brackets (b) Delays in passing the budget (c) The burden of public debt repayment (d) Slow growth caused by high deficits
Answer: (a)
Solution: The tempting wrong choice is the general idea of deficits slowing growth. Fiscal drag specifically describes how inflation moves nominal incomes into higher tax brackets under an unindexed progressive tax system, raising the effective tax take, so the answer is rising tax burdens due to inflation pushing incomes into higher brackets.
Source: PYQ-style

87. In the Human Development Index, which of the following is NOT a component?
(a) Life expectancy at birth (b) Expected and mean years of schooling (c) GNI per capita (PPP) (d) Infant mortality rate
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is that infant mortality sounds like a health indicator and is used in other indices, but HDI's health dimension uses life expectancy only. The three dimensions are health, education and standard of living, so the answer is infant mortality rate.
Source: UNDP Human Development Reports (concept as in NCERT Class 11 Economics)

88. "Demonetisation" means:
(a) Withdrawal of a currency unit's status as legal tender (b) Printing of new currency notes (c) Devaluation of currency against foreign currencies (d) Conversion of black money into white
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is confusing demonetisation with devaluation, which concerns the external value of currency. Demonetisation strips specified notes of legal tender status, as India did with ₹500 and ₹1000 notes in November 2016, so the answer is withdrawal of a currency unit's status as legal tender.
Source: PYQ-style

89. The "multiplier effect" in Keynesian economics implies that:
(a) Money supply always multiplies prices (b) Taxes multiply faster than income (c) An initial rise in investment leads to a larger rise in national income (d) Exports multiply imports
Answer: (c)
Solution: The misleading option links the multiplier to money and prices, which belongs to the quantity theory instead. The investment multiplier (1/MPS) shows income rising by a multiple of the initial investment, so the answer is that an initial rise in investment leads to a larger rise in national income.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Economics (Introductory Macroeconomics)

90. "Core inflation" differs from headline inflation because it excludes:
(a) Food and fuel prices due to their volatility (b) All imported goods (c) Services sector prices (d) Administered prices only
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is to think core inflation excludes administered prices or imports; the actual criterion is volatility, not origin. Food and fuel prices swing sharply with weather and global markets, so they are stripped out to reveal the underlying trend, and the answer is food and fuel prices due to their volatility.
Source: PYQ-style

91. Which of the following correctly orders the sectors by their historical share in India's GDP (largest first) in recent decades?
(a) Agriculture, Industry, Services (b) Industry, Services, Agriculture (c) Services, Industry, Agriculture (d) Services, Agriculture, Industry
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is chronological confusion — agriculture dominated at Independence, but the structure has long since shifted. Services contribute the largest share of GDP, followed by industry, with agriculture third (though still the largest employer), so the answer is Services, Industry, Agriculture.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Economics (Indian Economic Development)

92. "Moral hazard" in banking and insurance refers to:
(a) Fraud by bank employees (b) Increased risk-taking when one party is protected from the consequences (c) Collapse of banks due to rumours (d) Wilful default on loans
Answer: (b)
Solution: The tempting wrong choice is wilful default, which is a deliberate act, not the behavioural shift moral hazard describes. When deposit insurance or bailouts shield parties from losses, they take bigger risks, so the answer is increased risk-taking when one party is protected from the consequences.
Source: PYQ-style

93. The "impossible trinity" (trilemma) states that a country cannot simultaneously have:
(a) Free trade, low inflation and high growth (b) Full employment, price stability and growth (c) Fiscal surplus, low debt and high spending (d) Fixed exchange rate, free capital movement and independent monetary policy
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is the similar-sounding list of desirable macro goals; the trilemma is specifically about open-economy policy choices. A country can attain only two of the three: fixed exchange rate, free capital mobility and sovereign monetary policy, so the answer is fixed exchange rate, free capital movement and independent monetary policy.
Source: PYQ-style

94. "Disguised unemployment" is most commonly found in India in:
(a) The IT sector (b) Banking (c) Public administration (d) Agriculture
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is to associate it with any overstaffed office; the textbook case is rural. In Indian agriculture, more people work on family farms than needed, so removing some workers would not reduce output — marginal productivity is near zero — and the answer is agriculture.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Economics (Indian Economic Development)

95. Which of the following is a "flow" variable?
(a) National income (b) Money supply at a point in time (c) National wealth (d) Capital stock
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is money supply, which feels dynamic but is measured as a stock at a point in time. Flow variables are measured over a period — income, investment, savings per year — so the answer is national income.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Economics (Introductory Macroeconomics)

96. "Helicopter money" refers to:
(a) Emergency airlifting of cash to banks (b) Direct money creation by the central bank distributed to the public to stimulate demand (c) Foreign aid in the form of cash (d) Printing currency to repay external debt
Answer: (b)
Solution: The tempting wrong choice is the literal image of transporting cash. Coined from Milton Friedman's metaphor, helicopter money means the central bank creates money and channels it directly to people or government spending to lift demand, so the answer is direct money creation by the central bank distributed to the public to stimulate demand.
Source: PYQ-style

97. The " Kuznets Curve" hypothesises that as an economy develops, inequality:
(a) Falls continuously (b) First falls, then rises (c) Remains constant (d) First rises, then falls (inverted-U shape)
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is reversing the sequence — the curve is an inverted U, not a U. Simon Kuznets argued inequality rises in early industrialisation as workers move to higher-productivity sectors, then declines with wider education and welfare, so the answer is first rises, then falls (inverted-U shape).
Source: PYQ-style

98. "Seigniorage" is:
(a) A tax on imported luxury goods (b) The profit a government earns from issuing currency (c) Interest paid on foreign reserves (d) Revenue from disinvestment
Answer: (b)
Solution: The misleading option is disinvestment revenue, which is a one-time fiscal receipt, not a monetary concept. Seigniorage is the difference between the face value of money and its cost of production, accruing to the issuing authority, so the answer is the profit a government earns from issuing currency.
Source: PYQ-style

99. Which of the following pairs is correctly matched?
(a) Okun's Law — inflation and money supply (b) Engel's Law — savings rise with income (c) Say's Law — demand creates its own supply (d) Okun's Law — unemployment and GDP growth
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is Say's Law, which is deliberately reversed here — Say's Law states that supply creates its own demand, not the reverse. Okun's Law empirically links a fall in unemployment with faster GDP growth, so the answer is Okun's Law — unemployment and GDP growth.
Source: PYQ-style

100. "Twin deficits" hypothesis links a country's fiscal deficit with its:
(a) Current account deficit (b) Revenue deficit (c) Primary deficit (d) Capital account surplus
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is pairing fiscal deficit with revenue or primary deficit, which are components of the fiscal deficit itself, not a separate "twin". The hypothesis holds that high government borrowing spills over into a wider current account deficit, so the answer is current account deficit.
Source: PYQ-style
