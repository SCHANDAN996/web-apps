Card 1
Front: Which economic indicator measures the total value of final goods and services produced within a country's borders in a year?
Back: GDP (Gross Domestic Product). "Within borders" is the key — it counts production by anyone inside the country, regardless of nationality.

Card 2
Front: If an Indian citizen works in the USA, which Indian aggregate captures that income — GDP or GNP? And where do remittances sent home go?
Back: The citizen's income earned abroad enters GNP via Net Factor Income from Abroad (GNP = GDP + NFIA). But remittances sent home are current transfers — they raise Gross National Disposable Income (GNDI = GNP + net current transfers from abroad), not GNP. Examiner's trap: don't credit remittances to GNP.

Card 3
Front: What is the difference between NNP at factor cost and NNP at market price?
Back: NNP at factor cost = NNP at market price − Net Indirect Taxes (indirect taxes − subsidies). Factor cost shows what producers actually receive.

Card 4
Front: Which inflation measure does the RBI now use as its primary policy target — WPI or CPI?
Back: CPI (Consumer Price Index). Since 2016, RBI targets CPI (Combined) inflation at 4% ± 2% under the flexible inflation-targeting framework.

Card 5
Front: A country imports more goods than it exports. Is this a "trade deficit" or a "current account deficit"?
Back: Trade deficit — it refers only to goods (merchandise). Current account deficit is broader: goods + services + income + transfers. Trap: examiners swap these two.

Card 6
Front: What does "stagflation" mean, and why is it a policy nightmare?
Back: Stagnation + inflation together: high inflation with high unemployment and slow growth. Normal tools fail — cutting rates to boost jobs worsens inflation.

Card 7
Front: Which type of unemployment occurs when workers' skills no longer match available jobs due to technological change?
Back: Structural unemployment. Trap: don't confuse with frictional (between jobs) or cyclical (recession-linked) unemployment.

Card 8
Front: What is the "Laffer Curve" trying to show about tax rates and revenue?
Back: Beyond an optimal tax rate, raising taxes further reduces revenue (people evade or work less). Revenue is zero at both 0% and 100% tax rates.

Card 9
Front: Mnemonic trick: How do you remember the formula chain GDP → GNP → NNP?
Back: "G-N-N: Add NFIA, then subtract Depreciation." GNP = GDP + NFIA; NNP = GNP − Depreciation. Remember: "Foreign income first, wear-and-tear last."

Card 10
Front: Mnemonic trick: How do you recall the four factors of production and their rewards?
Back: Land–Labour–Capital–Entrepreneur earn Rent–Wages–Interest–Profit. Mnemonic: "Lazy Lions Catch Elephants — Rewards Wait In Pairs."

Card 11
Front: What is "disguised unemployment," and in which Indian sector is it most commonly found?
Back: More workers than needed — removing some doesn't reduce output. Classic example: Indian agriculture, where marginal productivity of extra labour is near zero.

Card 12
Front: What is the difference between "fiscal deficit" and "revenue deficit"?
Back: Fiscal deficit = total expenditure − total receipts (excluding borrowings) — shows total borrowing need. Revenue deficit = revenue expenditure − revenue receipts — shows borrowing for day-to-day spending. Trap: fiscal deficit is the bigger, broader figure.

Card 13
Front: What does the Gini coefficient measure, and what do values 0 and 1 mean?
Back: Income/wealth inequality. 0 = perfect equality, 1 = perfect inequality. Trap: examiners reverse the direction — higher Gini means MORE inequality.

Card 14
Front: What is "quantitative easing," and when do central banks use it?
Back: Central bank buys bonds/securities to inject money into the economy when interest rates are already near zero — used to fight deflation/recession.

Card 15
Front: Which committees are linked to India's poverty line estimation, and what is the trap?
Back: The Tendulkar Committee (2009) methodology has been the officially referenced poverty estimation method; the Rangarajan Committee (2014) proposed a higher poverty line. Trap: examiners swap Tendulkar and Rangarajan findings.

Card 16
Front: What is "core inflation," and why do policymakers track it?
Back: Inflation excluding volatile food and fuel prices. It reveals underlying demand-driven price pressure, guiding monetary policy better than headline inflation.

Card 17
Front: What is the "multiplier effect" in Keynesian economics?
Back: An initial increase in spending (e.g., government investment) leads to a larger total increase in national income, as one person's spending becomes another's income. Multiplier = 1/(1 − MPC).

Card 18
Front: Examiner's trap: "GDP at constant prices" — is this nominal GDP or real GDP?
Back: Real GDP. Constant prices remove the effect of inflation; nominal GDP uses current prices. Trap: questions swap "current" and "constant" to test this.

Card 19
Front: What is "helicopter money," and how does it differ from quantitative easing?
Back: Direct distribution of money to the public (or financing government spending by printing money) to boost demand. Unlike QE (asset purchases via banks), it puts cash straight into people's hands.

Card 20
Front: What does the "Phillips Curve" show, and what is its famous exception?
Back: An inverse relationship between inflation and unemployment in the short run. Exception: stagflation (1970s), where both rose together, breaking the curve.
