# 📊 PYQ Analysis — Economic Terms & Concepts

## 1. How Exams Test This Chapter

Economics terminology is one of the most reliable scoring areas across UPSC (Prelims), SSC CGL/CHSL, IBPS/SBI PO-Clerk, RRB NTPC and State PCS prelims. Examiners do not ask you to *derive* anything — they test whether you can **distinguish look-alike terms** and **match concepts to the right authority, formula or example**.

The recurring question styles are:

1. **Definition match** — "Which term describes a fall in the general price level?" (Deflation). Straight recall; these are your 15-second questions.
2. **Statement-based (UPSC/Bank style)** — two or three statements about GDP vs GNP, repo vs reverse repo, CPI vs WPI, with one subtly wrong clause.
3. **Pair matching** — tax → direct/indirect; budget term → definition; poverty line → committee.
4. **Numerical anchor** — current base years, FRBM targets, SLR/CRR ranges, GST slabs. These change over time, so always verify against the latest official notification before the exam.
5. **Institution attribution** — who publishes what (NSO, RBI, NITI Aayog, IMF, World Bank) and which index belongs to which body.

## 2. Sub-Topics That Recur Most Often

- **National income aggregates**: GDP, GNP, NNP, NDP, factor cost vs market price, base year, real vs nominal GDP. The single most repeated cluster.
- **Inflation & price indices**: CPI vs WPI, headline vs core inflation, stagflation, deflation, disinflation, inflation targeting (4% ± 2% band under the RBI Act framework).
- **Monetary policy tools**: repo, reverse repo, CRR, SLR, MSF, bank rate, OMO, MPC composition (6 members, 3 external).
- **Fiscal terms**: fiscal deficit, revenue deficit, primary deficit, effective revenue deficit, FRBM Act, direct vs indirect taxes, GST structure.
- **Banking terms**: NPA classification, priority sector lending, Basel norms, moral suasion, liquidity adjustment facility.
- **External sector**: BoP, current vs capital account, FDI vs FII/FPI, exchange rate regimes, devaluation vs depreciation.
- **Poverty, unemployment & inequality**: poverty line committees (Tendulkar, Rangarajan), types of unemployment, Gini coefficient, Lorenz curve.
- **Budget vocabulary**: budget types (balanced/surplus/deficit), gender budget, outcome budget, vote on account, interim budget, cess vs surcharge.

## 3. Exam-Wise Emphasis (Qualitative)

- **UPSC / State PCS**: statement-based questions on national income, inflation targeting, BoP and fiscal deficits; expects conceptual precision, not numbers alone.
- **SSC CGL/CHSL**: one-liner definitions, full forms, "which committee", direct/indirect tax classification, budget terms.
- **Banking (IBPS/SBI/RBI)**: monetary policy tools, NPA, Basel III, priority sector, CPI-based inflation target — the heaviest terminology load.
- **Railway (NTPC/Group D)**: simplest layer — full forms (GDP, NITI, SLR), basic definitions, who heads which body.

## 4. The Examiner's Traps (with Examples)

### Trap 1 — Almost Correct Statement
A statement is 90% right; one word is flipped.

> **Q.** Consider the following statements:
> 1. GDP measures the value of goods and services produced within a country's territory.
> 2. GNP = GDP + Net factor income from abroad.
> 3. If net factor income from abroad is positive, GDP exceeds GNP.
>
> (a) 1 and 2 only (b) 2 and 3 only (c) 1 and 3 only (d) 1, 2 and 3
>
> **Answer: (a)**
> **Solution:** Statements 1 and 2 are correct. Statement 3 reverses the logic: if net factor income from abroad is *positive*, **GNP exceeds GDP**, not the other way round. The trap is the inverted inequality in an otherwise familiar formula.
> **Trap type:** Almost Correct
> **Source:** PYQ-style (concept: NCERT Class 12 Macroeconomics)

### Trap 2 — Chronological / Sequence Confusion
Committees, acts and base years get shuffled.

> **Q.** Arrange the following in chronological order:
> 1. Nationalisation of 14 major commercial banks
> 2. Establishment of NITI Aayog
> 3. Introduction of LAF (Liquidity Adjustment Facility)
> 4. Nationalisation of RBI
>
> (a) 4-1-3-2 (b) 1-4-3-2 (c) 4-3-1-2 (d) 1-4-2-3
>
> **Answer: (a)**
> **Solution:** RBI was nationalised in 1949; 14 banks were nationalised in 1969; LAF was introduced in 2000 (on Narasimham Committee II recommendations); NITI Aayog replaced the Planning Commission in 2015. Distractors swap 1949 and 1969 — the classic decade-confusion trap.
> **Trap type:** Chronological Confusion
> **Source:** PYQ-style (facts: RBI official history; NITI Aayog, Govt. of India)

### Trap 3 — Negative Phrasing
"Which of the following is **NOT**..." — candidates misread under time pressure.

> **Q.** Which of the following is NOT a direct tax?
> (a) Income tax (b) Corporation tax (c) Goods and Services Tax (d) Wealth tax (when levied)
>
> **Answer: (c)**
> **Solution:** GST is an indirect tax — its burden can be shifted to the consumer. Income tax, corporation tax and wealth tax are paid directly by the person/entity on whom they are imposed. Many candidates pick (d) because wealth tax sounds unusual; the negative phrasing is the real trap.
> **Trap type:** Negative Phrasing
> **Source:** PYQ-style (concept: NCERT Class 11/12; CBIC)

### Trap 4 — Similar-Sounding Terms
Repo vs reverse repo; devaluation vs depreciation; disinflation vs deflation.

> **Q.** A situation where the rate of inflation is falling but prices are still rising is called:
> (a) Deflation (b) Disinflation (c) Stagflation (d) Reflation
>
> **Answer: (b)**
> **Solution:** Disinflation = falling inflation *rate* (e.g., from 6% to 4%) with prices still rising. Deflation (a) means prices actually fall — the most common wrong pick. Stagflation (c) combines stagnation with inflation.
> **Trap type:** Similar Terms
> **Source:** PYQ-style (concept: NCERT Class 12 Macroeconomics)

### Trap 5 — Institution Attribution
> **Q.** In India, the Consumer Price Index (CPI) for inflation targeting is released by:
> (a) RBI (b) Ministry of Finance (c) National Statistical Office (NSO) (d) NITI Aayog
>
> **Answer: (c)**
> **Solution:** CPI (Combined) is compiled and released by the NSO under the Ministry of Statistics and Programme Implementation. RBI *uses* it for inflation targeting but does not publish it — the (a) option exploits exactly this association.
> **Trap type:** Institution Attribution
> **Source:** PYQ-style (fact: MoSPI/NSO official releases)

### Trap 6 — Formula Disguise
> **Q.** Primary deficit is equal to:
> (a) Fiscal deficit + interest payments (b) Fiscal deficit − interest payments
> (c) Revenue deficit − interest payments (d) Total expenditure − total receipts
>
> **Answer: (b)**
> **Solution:** Primary deficit = Fiscal deficit − Interest payments. Option (a) flips the sign — the most planted distractor in budget-terminology questions.
> **Trap type:** Almost Correct (sign flip)
> **Source:** PYQ-style (concept: Union Budget documents, Ministry of Finance)

### Trap 7 — Current vs Capital Account
> **Q.** Which of the following is recorded in the capital account of the Balance of Payments?
> (a) Export of software services (b) Remittances from Indians abroad
> (c) Foreign Direct Investment (d) Import of crude oil
>
> **Answer: (c)**
> **Solution:** FDI is a capital account transaction. Exports/imports of goods and services and remittances (transfers) belong to the current account. Remittances (b) are the favourite distractor because they involve foreign money.
> **Trap type:** Similar Terms (account classification)
> **Source:** PYQ-style (concept: RBI BoP manual; NCERT Class 12)

### Trap 8 — NPA Timeline
> **Q.** A bank loan is classified as a Non-Performing Asset (NPA) when interest or principal remains overdue for more than:
> (a) 30 days (b) 60 days (c) 90 days (d) 180 days
>
> **Answer: (c)**
> **Solution:** As per RBI norms, a loan becomes NPA when overdue for more than 90 days. The 180-day option is a legacy distractor (the norm was tightened from 180 to 90 days in 2004) — examiners exploit outdated memory.
> **Trap type:** Outdated Fact / Chronological Confusion
> **Source:** PYQ-style (fact: RBI Master Circular on IRACP norms)

### Trap 9 — Real vs Nominal
> **Q.** If nominal GDP rises by 8% and prices rise by 5%, real GDP growth is approximately:
> (a) 13% (b) 3% (c) 5% (d) 8%
>
> **Answer: (b)**
> **Solution:** Real growth ≈ nominal growth − inflation = 8% − 5% = 3%. Option (a) tempts those who add instead of subtract.
> **Trap type:** Formula Disguise
> **Source:** PYQ-style (concept: NCERT Class 12 Macroeconomics)

### Trap 10 — Tax Devolution vs Grants
> **Q.** The distribution of tax revenues between the Union and the States is recommended by:
> (a) NITI Aayog (b) Finance Commission (c) GST Council (d) Inter-State Council
>
> **Answer: (b)**
> **Solution:** The Finance Commission (Article 280) recommends tax devolution every five years. The GST Council (c) decides GST rates and compensation, not overall devolution — the most common confusion since 2017.
> **Trap type:** Institution Attribution
> **Source:** PYQ-style (fact: Constitution of India, Article 280)

## 5. The 15/45-Second Rule Applied

**15-second questions** — pure recall, no statements to cross-check. Answer and move on.

> *Example:* "Stagflation refers to — (a) high growth with low inflation (b) stagnation with inflation (c) falling prices (d) rising employment." → You know it or you don't; 15 seconds, mark (b), move on. Never re-read a definition question.

**45-second questions** — statement-based or chronological; budget the time to verify each clause.

> *Example (Trap 1 above):* Read each statement separately. Statement 1 ✓ (definition). Statement 2 ✓ (formula). Statement 3 — pause: positive NFIA means GNP > GDP, so the statement is false. Eliminate (b), (c), (d) in one pass. Total: ~40 seconds. If after 45 seconds two options still survive, mark the most probable, flag it, and move on — do not let one question eat two questions' time.

**Skip signals:** a question asking for an exact current figure (CRR %, fiscal deficit target) you haven't revised recently — skip rather than guess blindly in negative-marking exams; attempt only if you can eliminate two options.

## 6. Takeaways for Revision

1. Master the **GDP→GNP→NNP chain** and the **deficit family** (fiscal, revenue, primary, effective revenue) — these two clusters alone cover a large share of terminology questions.
2. Maintain a one-page sheet of **"who publishes what"** (NSO, RBI, Finance Ministry, NITI Aayog, IMF, World Bank) — attribution traps are the examiner's favourite.
3. For every pair of similar terms (repo/reverse repo, deflation/disinflation, devaluation/depreciation, FDI/FPI), write the difference in one line of your own words.
4. Verify all **time-sensitive numbers** (policy rates, base years, FRBM targets) from official sources close to your exam date; this chapter's static concepts are stable, but its numbers are not.
