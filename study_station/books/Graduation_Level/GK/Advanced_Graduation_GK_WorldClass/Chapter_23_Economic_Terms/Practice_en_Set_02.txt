Questions 26–50: Medium

26. Which of the following best describes 'stagflation'?
(a) High growth with low inflation (b) Low inflation with falling interest rates (c) Rapid deflation with rising employment (d) Stagnant growth combined with high inflation and unemployment
Answer: (d)
Solution: Stagflation is the unusual combination of economic stagnation (slow or zero growth, high unemployment) with high inflation. The tempting wrong option reverses the relationship — low inflation with growth is a healthy economy, not stagflation. The term became famous after the 1970s oil crisis, so the answer is stagnant growth combined with high inflation and unemployment.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Macroeconomics

27. The 'Laffer Curve' illustrates the relationship between:
(a) Inflation and unemployment (b) Tax rates and tax revenue (c) Money supply and price level (d) Interest rates and investment
Answer: (b)
Solution: The Phillips Curve, not the Laffer Curve, shows the inflation–unemployment trade-off; this is a classic similar-names trap. The Laffer Curve shows that beyond a certain tax rate, revenue falls as compliance and work incentives decline, so the answer is tax rates and tax revenue.
Source: PYQ-style

28. In national income accounting, 'transfer payments' are:
(a) Included in GDP because they involve government spending (b) Counted twice to avoid underestimation (c) Excluded from GDP because no goods or services are produced in return (d) Added to exports while calculating national income
Answer: (c)
Solution: Many students assume all government spending enters GDP — that is the trap. Only expenditure on goods and services counts; pensions, scholarships and unemployment benefits are one-way transfers with no production in return, so they are excluded from GDP, and the answer is that they are excluded because no goods or services are produced in return.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Macroeconomics

29. 'Fiscal deficit' of the Government of India is defined as:
(a) Total expenditure minus total receipts excluding borrowings (b) Revenue expenditure minus revenue receipts (c) Total expenditure minus revenue receipts (d) Interest payments minus tax revenue
Answer: (a)
Solution: The tempting wrong option describes the revenue deficit, not the fiscal deficit — a frequent confusion. Fiscal deficit measures total borrowing requirement, so the answer is total expenditure minus total receipts excluding borrowings.
Source: PYQ-style

30. Which of the following is an example of 'regressive tax'?
(a) Income tax with rising slabs (b) A uniform sales tax on essential commodities (c) Corporate tax on profits (d) Wealth tax on large assets
Answer: (b)
Solution: A uniform tax on essentials takes a larger share of a poor person's income than a rich person's, making it regressive in effect. Progressive income tax, the tempting wrong option, does the opposite, so the answer is a uniform sales tax on essential commodities.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Indian Economic Development

31. The 'Phillips Curve' suggests that in the short run:
(a) Inflation and unemployment move in opposite directions (b) Inflation and growth always rise together (c) Money supply has no effect on prices (d) Unemployment is independent of demand
Answer: (a)
Solution: The Phillips Curve shows an inverse short-run relationship between inflation and unemployment. Do not confuse it with the Laffer Curve (taxes) — a similar-names trap, so the answer is that inflation and unemployment move in opposite directions.
Source: PYQ-style

32. 'Disguised unemployment' is most commonly found in India in:
(a) The organised manufacturing sector (b) The IT services sector (c) The banking sector (d) The agricultural sector
Answer: (d)
Solution: Disguised unemployment exists where more workers are engaged than needed and removing some does not reduce output — typical of Indian family farming. The organised sectors listed as distractors have defined staffing, so the answer is the agricultural sector.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Indian Economic Development

33. Which of the following is NOT a function of money?
(a) Creation of fiscal deficit (b) Measure of value (c) Standard of deferred payment (d) Medium of exchange
Answer: (a)
Solution: Money's classical functions are medium of exchange, unit of account, store of value and standard of deferred payments. Fiscal deficit is a budget concept, unrelated to money's functions, so the answer is creation of fiscal deficit.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Macroeconomics

34. 'Open Market Operations' by the RBI refer to:
(a) Buying and selling of government securities to control money supply (b) Fixing the repo rate for banks (c) Licensing of new banks (d) Printing of currency notes
Answer: (a)
Solution: OMOs involve the RBI buying or selling government securities to inject or absorb liquidity. Fixing the repo rate, the tempting wrong option, is a separate policy tool, so the answer is buying and selling of government securities to control money supply.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Macroeconomics

35. The 'Gini coefficient' measures:
(a) Inflation volatility (b) Industrial productivity (c) Foreign exchange reserves (d) Income inequality within a country
Answer: (d)
Solution: The Gini coefficient ranges from 0 (perfect equality) to 1 (perfect inequality) and measures income or wealth distribution. It is often confused with the Human Development Index, which measures broader welfare, so the answer is income inequality within a country.
Source: PYQ-style

36. 'Dumping' in international trade means:
(a) Importing banned goods illegally (b) Exporting goods at prices below cost or below domestic price (c) Imposing quotas on imports (d) Devaluing currency to boost exports
Answer: (b)
Solution: Dumping is selling abroad below normal value, often to capture markets; importing countries counter it with anti-dumping duties. Currency devaluation, the tempting wrong option, is a different trade strategy, so the answer is exporting goods at prices below cost or below domestic price.
Source: PYQ-style

37. Which of the following is a 'direct tax' in India?
(a) Goods and Services Tax (b) Customs duty (c) Income tax (d) Excise duty on fuel
Answer: (c)
Solution: Direct taxes are paid by the person on whom they are levied and cannot be shifted — income tax and corporate tax qualify. GST, customs and excise are indirect taxes whose burden shifts to consumers, so the answer is income tax.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Indian Economic Development

38. 'Core inflation' excludes which of the following?
(a) Manufactured goods (b) Housing costs (c) Food and fuel prices (d) Services
Answer: (c)
Solution: Core inflation strips out volatile food and fuel components to reveal the underlying price trend. Students often wrongly think it excludes manufactured goods — that is the trap, so the answer is food and fuel prices.
Source: PYQ-style

39. The 'multiplier effect' in Keynesian economics refers to:
(a) The effect of tax cuts on imports (b) The proportional rise in national income from an initial increase in investment (c) The rise in money supply due to RBI note printing (d) The increase in exports due to devaluation
Answer: (b)
Solution: The investment multiplier (k = 1/(1−MPC)) shows how an initial spending injection raises income by a larger amount through successive rounds of consumption. It is not about note printing, so the answer is the proportional rise in national income from an initial increase in investment.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Macroeconomics

40. 'Balance of Payments' of a country records:
(a) All economic transactions between residents and the rest of the world in a period (b) Only exports and imports of goods (c) Only foreign investment flows (d) The government's budget transactions
Answer: (a)
Solution: BoP is wider than the balance of trade — it includes goods, services, transfers and capital flows. The tempting wrong option describes only the trade account, so the answer is all economic transactions between residents and the rest of the world in a period.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Macroeconomics

41. Which of the following statements about GDP and GNP is correct?
(a) GDP = GNP + Net factor income from abroad (b) GNP = GDP + Net factor income from abroad (c) GNP is always greater than GDP (d) GDP and GNP are identical for every country
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap lies in reversing the formula and in assuming GNP always exceeds GDP — for countries paying more income to foreigners, GNP is smaller. The correct identity is GNP = GDP + Net factor income from abroad, so the answer is that GNP equals GDP plus net factor income from abroad.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Macroeconomics

42. 'Liquidity trap' refers to a situation where:
(a) Interest rates are so low that monetary policy becomes ineffective (b) Banks refuse to lend despite high demand (c) RBI runs out of foreign exchange (d) Inflation rises despite money supply contraction
Answer: (a)
Solution: In a liquidity trap, people prefer holding cash at near-zero interest rates, so further monetary easing fails to stimulate demand — a Keynesian concept. It is not about bank refusal to lend, so the answer is that interest rates are so low that monetary policy becomes ineffective.
Source: PYQ-style

43. The 'poverty line' in India was historically estimated using:
(a) Per capita land holding (b) Ownership of consumer durables (c) Literacy levels (d) Calorie intake-based consumption expenditure
Answer: (d)
Solution: Indian poverty estimation (Lakdawala and Tendulkar committees) was based on consumption expenditure linked to calorie norms. Literacy and asset ownership, the tempting distractors, are deprivation indicators, not the poverty line basis, so the answer is calorie intake-based consumption expenditure.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Indian Economic Development

44. 'Crowding out' occurs when:
(a) Exports fall due to imports (b) Foreign firms replace domestic firms (c) Private investment falls because government borrowing raises interest rates (d) Public sector banks merge
Answer: (c)
Solution: Heavy government borrowing can push up interest rates and absorb loanable funds, reducing private investment — this is crowding out. It has nothing to do with foreign firms replacing domestic ones, so the answer is that private investment falls because government borrowing raises interest rates.
Source: PYQ-style

45. Which of the following is counted in the 'capital account' of the Balance of Payments?
(a) Export of software services (b) Remittances from workers abroad (c) Foreign direct investment (d) Import of crude oil
Answer: (c)
Solution: FDI is a capital account transaction; services exports, remittances and oil imports all belong to the current account — a frequent classification trap, so the answer is foreign direct investment.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Macroeconomics

46. 'Marginal cost' is defined as:
(a) Total cost divided by output (b) The addition to total cost from producing one more unit (c) Fixed cost per unit of output (d) The cost of the first unit produced
Answer: (b)
Solution: Marginal cost measures the change in total cost when output rises by one unit. Total cost divided by output, the tempting wrong option, is average cost — a classic confusion, so the answer is the addition to total cost from producing one more unit.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Microeconomics

47. The 'Human Development Index' combines which three dimensions?
(a) Health, education, and standard of living (b) Income, trade, and industry (c) Growth, inflation, and employment (d) Agriculture, services, and manufacturing
Answer: (a)
Solution: HDI, published by UNDP, combines life expectancy (health), schooling (education) and GNI per capita (standard of living). The sectoral composition of GDP, a tempting distractor, is unrelated, so the answer is health, education, and standard of living.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Indian Economic Development

48. 'Devaluation' of a currency differs from 'depreciation' in that devaluation:
(a) Is decided by commercial banks (b) Happens only in free markets (c) Always increases inflation (d) Is a deliberate official reduction under a fixed exchange rate system
Answer: (d)
Solution: Devaluation is a government/central bank action under fixed rates, while depreciation is market-driven under floating rates — a subtle distinction examiners love to test, so the answer is that it is a deliberate official reduction under a fixed exchange rate system.
Source: PYQ-style

49. Which of the following best defines 'opportunity cost'?
(a) The money spent on producing a good (b) The sunk cost of a project (c) The total cost of all alternatives (d) The value of the next best alternative foregone
Answer: (d)
Solution: Opportunity cost is what you give up — the next best alternative — not the sum of all alternatives, which is the tempting wrong option. Sunk costs are irrelevant to current decisions, so the answer is the value of the next best alternative foregone.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Microeconomics

50. 'Monetary policy' in India is primarily formulated by:
(a) The Ministry of Finance (b) NITI Aayog (c) The Reserve Bank of India (d) The Finance Commission
Answer: (c)
Solution: The RBI, through its Monetary Policy Committee, sets the repo rate and manages liquidity; the Ministry of Finance handles fiscal policy — a common institutional confusion, so the answer is the Reserve Bank of India.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Macroeconomics
