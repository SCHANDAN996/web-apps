# Economic Terms & Concepts

## The Hook: A Note That Became Worthless Overnight

On 8 November 2016, at 8 PM, ₹500 and ₹1000 notes — nearly 86% of India's currency in circulation — stopped being legal tender. In one speech, words like *legal tender*, *liquidity*, *money supply* and *black money* jumped out of textbooks and into every Indian household. That is the power of economic vocabulary: it is not abstract theory, it is the language in which a country's biggest decisions are announced. And that is exactly why examiners love this chapter — one confused term (say, GDP vs GNP, or repo vs reverse repo) is enough to turn a sure mark into a negative one.

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this chapter you will be able to:
- Define and distinguish the 40+ economic terms most frequently asked in SSC CGL, IBPS PO/Clerk, and State PCS prelims.
- Spot the classic traps: near-identical pairs (inflation/deflation, fiscal/monetary, devaluation/depreciation).
- Connect static terms to institutions (RBI, NITI Aayog, IMF, World Bank) and to the Budget.
- Answer statement-based questions where one wrong word makes a statement false.

## 1. National Income: The Big Family

### GDP — Gross Domestic Product
The total money value of all **final** goods and services produced **within the domestic territory** of a country in a year. "Final" matters — the value of wheat is not counted separately if the bread's value is already counted (avoids **double counting**).

### GNP — Gross National Product
GNP = GDP + **Net Factor Income from Abroad (NFIA)** — i.e., income earned by Indians abroad minus income earned by foreigners in India.

### NNP, and the "at factor cost / market price" ladder
- NNP = GNP − **Depreciation** (wear and tear of capital; also called Capital Consumption Allowance).
- **Market Price → Factor Cost**: subtract **Net Indirect Taxes** (indirect taxes − subsidies).
- **National Income** = NNP at Factor Cost.

### Which measure does India use as the headline?
Until 2015, India's headline growth measure was **NNP at factor cost** (national income). With the new series (base year 2011–12) introduced in 2015, **GDP at market prices** became the headline indicator, in line with international practice.

### Real vs Nominal GDP
- **Nominal GDP**: measured at current prices — includes inflation.
- **Real GDP**: measured at constant (base-year) prices — shows true growth.
- **GDP Deflator** = (Nominal GDP ÷ Real GDP) × 100 — a measure of price rise.

### Who measures it in India?
The **National Statistical Office (NSO)**, under the Ministry of Statistics and Programme Implementation (MoSPI). The current base year for GDP is **2011–12**. GDP is now measured by **GVA (Gross Value Added)** at basic prices plus product taxes minus subsidies.

## 2. Inflation and Its Family

**Inflation** = sustained rise in the general price level, i.e., fall in the purchasing power of money.

### Types by speed
- **Creeping** (mild, ~2–3%), **Walking/Trotting**, **Running**, **Galloping/Hyperinflation** (prices rising extremely fast — e.g., Germany 1923, Zimbabwe 2008).

### Types by cause
- **Demand-pull inflation**: too much money chasing too few goods.
- **Cost-push inflation**: rise in input costs (wages, oil, raw material).
- **Stagflation**: stagnation + inflation — high inflation with high unemployment and low growth. The hardest to cure.
- **Deflation**: sustained fall in prices (worse than mild inflation — signals weak demand).
- **Disinflation**: fall in the *rate* of inflation (prices still rising, but slower). **Trap word!**
- **Reflation**: deliberate policy to expand output by stimulating demand.
- **Core inflation**: inflation excluding food and fuel (volatile items).

### How it is measured in India
- **CPI (Consumer Price Index)** — measures retail prices; the RBI's inflation target is based on **CPI (Combined)**: **4% ± 2%** (i.e., 2%–6% band) under the inflation-targeting framework.
- **WPI (Wholesale Price Index)** — wholesale prices; base year **2011–12**; does **not** include services.
- CPI is released by NSO; WPI by the Office of the Economic Adviser, Ministry of Commerce & Industry.

🧠 **Active Recall:** Which index excludes services? What is the RBI's CPI target band? Differentiate deflation and disinflation in one line each.

## 3. Money and Banking Terms

### Money supply measures (RBI)
- **M1** = Currency with public + Demand deposits + Other deposits with RBI (most liquid; "narrow money").
- **M2** = M1 + Savings deposits of post office savings banks.
- **M3** = M1 + Time deposits with banks ("broad money" — the most commonly used aggregate).
- **M4** = M3 + All post office deposits.

### Key RBI instruments
- **Repo Rate**: rate at which RBI lends short-term funds to banks against securities. ↑ Repo = costlier loans = less money in the economy.
- **Reverse Repo**: rate at which RBI borrows from banks (absorbs liquidity). Always **lower** than repo.
- **CRR (Cash Reserve Ratio)**: % of a bank's deposits kept with RBI **in cash** — earns no interest.
- **SLR (Statutory Liquidity Ratio)**: % of deposits kept by the bank **with itself** in liquid assets (cash, gold, approved securities).
- **Bank Rate**: long-term lending rate of RBI; aligned with the **MSF (Marginal Standing Facility)** rate, which is repo + a fixed margin.
- **Open Market Operations (OMO)**: buying/selling government securities to manage liquidity.
- **MPC (Monetary Policy Committee)**: 6 members (3 RBI + 3 government-nominated), headed by the RBI Governor; decides the repo rate; meets at least 4 times a year.

### Other banking terms
- **NPA (Non-Performing Asset)**: a loan on which interest/principal is overdue for **more than 90 days**.
- **Priority Sector Lending (PSL)**: 40% of Adjusted Net Bank Credit for domestic scheduled commercial banks.
- **Legal tender**: money that must be accepted by law (notes/coins issued under RBI Act/Government of India).
- **Liquidity trap**: when interest rates are so low that monetary policy stops working (people hoard cash).

🧠 **Active Recall:** Which is higher — repo or reverse repo? What does a CRR hike do to money supply? Define NPA in one line.

## 4. Budget and Fiscal Terms

- **Fiscal policy**: government's taxation and spending policy (Budget). **Monetary policy**: RBI's control of money supply and interest rates. Do not mix them up — the examiner's favourite trap.
- **Revenue deficit** = Revenue expenditure − Revenue receipts.
- **Fiscal deficit** = Total expenditure − Total receipts **excluding borrowings** = the government's total borrowing requirement.
- **Primary deficit** = Fiscal deficit − Interest payments.
- **Effective revenue deficit** = Revenue deficit − Grants for creation of capital assets.
- **Direct tax**: burden cannot be shifted (Income tax, Corporation tax). **Indirect tax**: burden can be shifted (GST, customs duty).
- **GST**: introduced **1 July 2017** via the **101st Constitutional Amendment Act, 2016**; a destination-based, indirect tax; GST Council is a constitutional body (Article 279A) chaired by the Union Finance Minister.
- **FRBM Act, 2003**: targets fiscal discipline — aimed to limit fiscal deficit and eliminate revenue deficit.
- **Disinvestment**: government selling its stake in PSUs. **Strategic disinvestment** = transferring management control.
- **Crowding out**: heavy government borrowing pushes up interest rates and reduces private investment.

🧠 **Active Recall:** Fiscal deficit minus interest payments = ? GST came via which amendment? Who chairs the GST Council?

## 5. External Sector Terms

- **Balance of Trade (BoT)**: exports − imports of **goods only**.
- **Balance of Payments (BoP)**: record of **all** economic transactions with the rest of the world — has two accounts: **Current account** (goods, services, income, transfers) and **Capital account** (FDI, FII/FPI, loans, reserves).
- **Devaluation**: deliberate lowering of currency value by the government (fixed-rate system). **Depreciation**: fall in value through market forces (floating system). India devalued the rupee notably in **1966, 1991** (July 1991, in two steps).
- **FDI vs FII/FPI**: FDI = long-term investment with management interest; FPI = portfolio investment in shares/bonds ("hot money", can exit quickly).
- **Forex reserves**: held by RBI — include foreign currency assets, gold, SDRs, and reserve position in the IMF.
- **SDR (Special Drawing Rights)**: IMF-created international reserve asset (1969); its value is based on a basket of currencies (US dollar, euro, Chinese yuan, Japanese yen, pound sterling).
- **Purchasing Power Parity (PPP)**: exchange rate that equalises the price of an identical basket of goods across countries.

🧠 **Active Recall:** BoT covers goods only — true or false? Devaluation vs depreciation — who does what? Name the SDR basket currencies.

## 6. Growth, Development and Welfare Terms

- **Economic growth**: rise in output/income (quantitative). **Economic development**: growth + improvement in living standards, health, education (qualitative).
- **HDI (Human Development Index)**: published by **UNDP** (since 1990); three dimensions — health (life expectancy), education, standard of living (GNI per capita). Created by **Mahbub ul Haq** and **Amartya Sen**.
- **Poverty line in India**: earlier estimated by the Planning Commission; key committees — **Tendulkar Committee (2009)** and **Rangarajan Committee (2014)**.
- **Gini coefficient**: measures income inequality (0 = perfect equality, 1 = perfect inequality). Related: **Lorenz curve**.
- **Phillips curve**: inverse relationship between inflation and unemployment (short run).
- **Laffer curve**: beyond a point, higher tax rates reduce tax revenue.
- **Trickle-down theory**: benefits of growth at the top eventually reach the poor.
- **Inclusive growth**: growth whose benefits reach all sections — a stated goal of Indian planning since the 11th/12th Five-Year Plans.
- **Green GDP**: GDP adjusted for environmental degradation.

## 8. Classic Economic Thinkers & Terms (Quick Hits)

- **Adam Smith** — "Wealth of Nations" (1776); *invisible hand*; father of modern economics.
- **John Maynard Keynes** — "General Theory" (1936); demand management; deficit spending in depression.
- **Amartya Sen** — Nobel 1998; capability approach; welfare economics.
- **Abhijit Banerjee** — Nobel 2019 (with Esther Duflo and Michael Kremer); randomised controlled trials for poverty alleviation.
- **Dadabhai Naoroji** — "Drain of Wealth" theory; first to estimate India's national income.
- **V.K.R.V. Rao** — pioneering scientific estimate of India's national income.

## 🧩 Mnemonics

**1. Money supply ladder — "CDT" for M1:** **C**urrency with public + **D**emand deposits + o**T**her deposits with RBI. Then remember: M3 = M1 + **T**ime deposits ("M3 has Time").

**2. Repo vs Reverse Repo — "Repo Pours, Reverse Removes":** Repo **pours** money into banks (RBI lends); Reverse repo **removes** money (RBI borrows/absorbs).

**3. Budget deficits — "R-F-P, each subtracts more":** **R**evenue deficit (revenue gap) → **F**iscal deficit (total borrowing) → **P**rimary deficit (fiscal minus interest). Remember: *"Real Fiscal Problems"*.

**4. HDI dimensions — "HEL":** **H**ealth, **E**ducation, **L**iving standard.

## 🔗 Static-Dynamic Link

Static terms keep re-entering the news: the **repo rate** changes at every MPC meeting; **CPI inflation** is reported monthly; the **fiscal deficit** number is announced in every Union Budget (1 February); and **GDP growth** figures are released quarterly by NSO. When you read a headline like "RBI holds repo rate" or "fiscal deficit target revised", map it instantly to the definitions above — that habit converts current affairs into free revision of this static chapter.

## ⚠️ Caution: Examiner's Trap

**Trap 1 — "लगभग सही कथन" (Almost-true statement):** "WPI includes services along with goods." Sounds plausible, but WPI covers **goods only** — services are in CPI. Similarly: "Reverse repo is higher than repo" — always false; reverse repo is lower.

**Trap 2 — "विपरीत कार्य-कारण" (Reverse causation):** "A rise in repo rate increases money supply in the economy." Wrong direction — a repo hike makes borrowing costlier and **contracts** money supply. Examiners flip the direction of CRR/SLR/repo effects to catch rote learners.

**Trap 3 — "समान नाम/वर्ष" (Similar names/years):** Devaluation vs depreciation; disinflation vs deflation; GDP vs GNP; fiscal vs monetary policy; Planning Commission (1950) vs NITI Aayog (2015). Also: RBI established **1935**, nationalised **1949** — the two years are routinely swapped in options.

**Trap 4 — "नकारात्मक वाक्यांश" (Hidden negative):** "Which of the following is **NOT** a direct tax?" — GST is the answer, but in a hurry candidates pick income tax. Underline NOT/EXCEPT before reading options.
