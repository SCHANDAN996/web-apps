1. Which of the following best defines Gross Domestic Product (GDP)?
(a) Total market value of all final goods and services produced within a country's borders in a given period (b) Total value of all goods and services produced by a country's citizens abroad (c) Total value of all intermediate and final goods produced in a country (d) Total income earned by the government through taxes
Answer: (a)
Solution: Option (c) is the trap — GDP counts only final goods to avoid double counting, not intermediate goods. Option (b) describes GNP, not GDP. GDP measures production within the geographical boundary regardless of who produces it, so the answer is the total market value of all final goods and services produced within a country's borders in a given period.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Macroeconomics

2. What does GNP stand for?
(a) General Net Production (b) General National Price (c) Gross Net Profit (d) Gross National Product
Answer: (d)
Solution: GNP is Gross National Product — the value of final goods and services produced by a country's residents, including income earned abroad, minus income earned by foreigners domestically. The other options are invented terms, so the answer is Gross National Product.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Macroeconomics

3. Net National Product (NNP) is obtained by subtracting what from GNP?
(a) Depreciation (b) Subsidies (c) Indirect taxes (d) Net factor income from abroad
Answer: (a)
Solution: Option (d) is the trap — net factor income from abroad is what converts GDP into GNP, not GNP into NNP. NNP = GNP minus depreciation (also called consumption of fixed capital), so the answer is depreciation.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Macroeconomics

4. Inflation is best described as:
(a) A one-time increase in the price of a single commodity (b) A sustained rise in the general price level of goods and services (c) A fall in the value of exports (d) An increase in unemployment
Answer: (b)
Solution: Option (a) is the trap — a rise in the price of one commodity is not inflation; inflation refers to the general price level rising persistently over time. It reduces the purchasing power of money, so the answer is a sustained rise in the general price level of goods and services.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Macroeconomics

5. Which index is used in India as the primary measure of retail inflation?
(a) Consumer Price Index (b) Wholesale Price Index (c) Index of Industrial Production (d) GDP deflator
Answer: (a)
Solution: Option (b) is the trap — WPI was historically used, but since 2014–16 the RBI's inflation targeting framework uses CPI (Combined) as the headline measure of retail inflation. WPI tracks wholesale prices, not what consumers pay, so the answer is Consumer Price Index.
Source: RBI / Ministry of Statistics and Programme Implementation

6. Stagflation refers to a situation of:
(a) High growth with low inflation (b) Stagnant growth combined with high inflation and unemployment (c) Rapid deflation with rising employment (d) Falling prices with rising output
Answer: (b)
Solution: Stagflation is the unusual combination of economic stagnation (slow or no growth, high unemployment) with high inflation — a situation that standard policy tools struggle to fix. The other options describe normal or deflationary conditions, so the answer is stagnant growth combined with high inflation and unemployment.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Macroeconomics

7. Deflation means:
(a) A sustained fall in the general price level (b) A decrease in the rate of inflation (c) A fall in the value of the rupee against the dollar (d) A reduction in government expenditure
Answer: (a)
Solution: Option (b) is the classic trap — a decrease in the rate of inflation is disinflation, not deflation. Deflation is an actual, sustained decline in the general price level, so the answer is a sustained fall in the general price level.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Macroeconomics

8. Fiscal policy in India is primarily the responsibility of:
(a) Reserve Bank of India (b) NITI Aayog (c) Ministry of Finance, Government of India (d) Securities and Exchange Board of India
Answer: (c)
Solution: Option (a) is the trap — the RBI conducts monetary policy, not fiscal policy. Fiscal policy (taxation and government spending, presented through the Union Budget) is framed by the Ministry of Finance, so the answer is Ministry of Finance, Government of India.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Macroeconomics

9. The repo rate is the rate at which:
(a) RBI borrows from commercial banks (b) Commercial banks lend to each other overnight (c) RBI lends short-term funds to commercial banks against securities (d) Banks lend to their most creditworthy customers
Answer: (c)
Solution: Option (a) is the trap — the rate at which RBI borrows from banks is the reverse repo rate, the mirror image of the repo rate. The repo rate is the rate at which RBI provides short-term liquidity to banks against government securities, so the answer is RBI lends short-term funds to commercial banks against securities.
Source: Reserve Bank of India

10. Cash Reserve Ratio (CRR) is:
(a) The portion of deposits banks must invest in government securities (b) The portion of deposits banks must keep as cash with the RBI (c) The minimum cash a bank must keep in its own vault for daily operations (d) The reserve maintained by RBI for printing currency
Answer: (b)
Solution: Option (a) is the trap — mandatory investment in government and approved securities is the Statutory Liquidity Ratio (SLR), not CRR. CRR is the percentage of a bank's net demand and time liabilities kept as cash balance with the RBI, so the answer is the portion of deposits banks must keep as cash with the RBI.
Source: Reserve Bank of India

11. Which of the following is a direct tax?
(a) Goods and Services Tax (b) Customs duty (c) Excise duty (d) Income tax
Answer: (d)
Solution: A direct tax is one whose burden cannot be shifted to another person — income tax is paid directly by the earner. GST, customs duty and excise duty are indirect taxes whose burden passes to consumers, so the answer is income tax.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Indian Economic Development

12. The Goods and Services Tax (GST) was implemented in India on:
(a) 1 April 2016 (b) 1 January 2018 (c) 1 July 2017 (d) 15 August 2017
Answer: (c)
Solution: Options (a) and (d) are chronological traps — the Constitutional (101st Amendment) Act came in 2016, but GST actually rolled out at midnight on 1 July 2017. It subsumed most central and state indirect taxes, so the answer is 1 July 2017.
Source: GST Council / Government of India

13. Fiscal deficit is defined as:
(a) Total expenditure minus total revenue receipts (b) Total expenditure minus total receipts excluding borrowings (c) Revenue expenditure minus revenue receipts (d) Interest payments minus subsidies
Answer: (b)
Solution: Option (a) is the trap — subtracting only revenue receipts from total expenditure gives the revenue deficit, not the fiscal deficit. Fiscal deficit equals total expenditure minus total non-debt receipts (revenue receipts plus non-debt capital receipts), showing the government's total borrowing requirement, so the answer is total expenditure minus total receipts excluding borrowings.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Macroeconomics

14. The balance of payments of a country records:
(a) Only exports and imports of goods (b) All economic transactions between residents of a country and the rest of the world in a period (c) Only foreign investment flows (d) The government's foreign borrowings only
Answer: (b)
Solution: Option (a) is the trap — trade in goods alone is just the balance of trade, one component of the current account. The balance of payments is a comprehensive record of all economic transactions (goods, services, income, transfers and capital flows) with the rest of the world, so the answer is all economic transactions between residents of a country and the rest of the world in a period.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Macroeconomics

15. Which organisation releases the World Development Report?
(a) International Monetary Fund (b) United Nations Development Programme (c) World Trade Organization (d) World Bank
Answer: (d)
Solution: Option (a) is the similar-name trap — the IMF publishes the World Economic Outlook, not the World Development Report. The World Bank publishes the World Development Report annually, so the answer is World Bank.
Source: World Bank official website

16. The Human Development Index is published by:
(a) World Bank (b) United Nations Development Programme (c) World Economic Forum (d) International Labour Organization
Answer: (b)
Solution: HDI combines life expectancy, education and per capita income into a single measure. It is published by the UNDP in its Human Development Report, not by the World Bank or WEF, so the answer is United Nations Development Programme.
Source: UNDP official website

17. Which of the following is an example of a capital receipt for the Government of India?
(a) Income tax collection (b) GST collection (c) Interest received on loans given to states (d) Disinvestment proceeds from selling equity in a public sector company
Answer: (d)
Solution: Options (a), (b) and (c) are revenue receipts — they recur and create no liability nor reduce assets. Disinvestment reduces the government's assets and is therefore a capital receipt, so the answer is disinvestment proceeds from selling equity in a public sector company.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Macroeconomics

18. The term 'disinvestment' refers to:
(a) RBI selling government securities in the open market (b) Foreign companies withdrawing investment from India (c) Government selling its stake in public sector enterprises (d) Reduction of capital expenditure in the budget
Answer: (c)
Solution: Option (a) is the trap — RBI selling securities is open market operations, a monetary policy tool, not disinvestment. Disinvestment means the government reducing its shareholding in PSUs, so the answer is government selling its stake in public sector enterprises.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Indian Economic Development

19. Which sector of the Indian economy is also called the service sector?
(a) Tertiary sector (b) Secondary sector (c) Primary sector (d) Quaternary sector
Answer: (a)
Solution: The primary sector covers agriculture and extraction, the secondary sector covers manufacturing and industry, and the tertiary sector covers services such as banking, transport and trade. The tertiary sector is the largest contributor to India's GDP, so the answer is tertiary sector.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Economics

20. Per capita income is calculated by:
(a) Multiplying national income by population (b) Dividing GDP by total exports (c) Dividing national income by total population (d) Dividing total tax revenue by population
Answer: (c)
Solution: Per capita income is an average — national income divided by the total population of the country. It is used to compare living standards across countries, so the answer is dividing national income by total population.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Economics

21. If the RBI wants to control inflation, which combination of actions would it most likely take?
(a) Increase repo rate and increase CRR (b) Reduce repo rate and reduce CRR (c) Reduce repo rate and increase CRR (d) Increase repo rate and reduce CRR
Answer: (a)
Solution: Options (c) and (d) are traps because they mix contradictory tools — one easing and one tightening. To fight inflation the RBI tightens liquidity: a higher repo rate makes borrowing costlier and a higher CRR locks away more bank funds, reducing money supply, so the answer is increase repo rate and increase CRR.
Source: Reserve Bank of India

22. Consider the following statements about GDP and GNP:
Statement 1: GNP = GDP + Net factor income from abroad.
Statement 2: If net factor income from abroad is negative, GNP exceeds GDP.
Which of the statements is/are correct?
(a) Only Statement 2 (b) Only Statement 1 (c) Both Statement 1 and Statement 2 (d) Neither Statement 1 nor Statement 2
Answer: (b)
Solution: Statement 2 is the trap — it reverses the logic: if net factor income from abroad is negative, GNP is less than GDP, not more. Statement 1 correctly states the identity GNP = GDP + NFIA, so the answer is only Statement 1.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Macroeconomics

23. A 'regressive tax' is one which:
(a) Takes a larger percentage of income from the poor than from the rich (b) Takes a larger percentage of income from the rich than from the poor (c) Takes the same absolute amount from everyone (d) Increases as the price of goods increases
Answer: (a)
Solution: Option (b) is the trap — that describes a progressive tax, the exact opposite. A regressive tax burdens lower-income groups proportionally more (for example, the same indirect tax on a necessity weighs heavier on a poor household), so the answer is takes a larger percentage of income from the poor than from the rich.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Indian Economic Development

24. Arrange the following in the correct sequence of increasing scope:
1. Balance of trade
2. Current account balance
3. Balance of payments
(a) 3, 2, 1 (b) 2, 1, 3 (c) 1, 2, 3 (d) 1, 3, 2
Answer: (c)
Solution: This is a scope-confusion trap: the balance of trade covers only goods; the current account adds services, income and transfers; the balance of payments adds the capital and financial account on top. Each successive measure is broader, so the answer is 1, 2, 3.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Macroeconomics

25. Which of the following correctly distinguishes the bank rate from the repo rate?
(a) Bank rate is for short-term lending with collateral; repo rate is for long-term lending without collateral (b) Bank rate applies to borrowers; repo rate applies to depositors (c) Both are identical rates set by the Ministry of Finance (d) Bank rate is the rate for long-term lending by RBI without collateral; repo rate is short-term lending against securities
Answer: (d)
Solution: Option (a) is the trap — it swaps the two definitions, a classic similar-concept confusion. The bank rate is the rate at which RBI provides long-term funds to banks without collateral, while the repo rate is for short-term lending against securities; both are set by the RBI, not the Finance Ministry, so the answer is bank rate is the rate for long-term lending by RBI without collateral; repo rate is short-term lending against securities.
Source: Reserve Bank of India
