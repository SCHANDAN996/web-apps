Questions 126–150: Hard

126. Consider the following statements about the Phillips Curve:
1. It shows a short-run inverse relationship between inflation and unemployment.
2. In the long run, the curve is vertical at the natural rate of unemployment.
Which of the statements given above is/are correct?
(a) 1 only (b) 2 only (c) Neither 1 nor 2 (d) Both 1 and 2
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is to assume the trade-off holds permanently; it exists only in the short run because expectations adjust over time. In the long run the curve is vertical at the natural rate, so both statements are correct and the answer is Both 1 and 2.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Macroeconomics

127. Stagflation refers to a situation where:
(a) High inflation coexists with high unemployment and stagnant growth (b) Inflation rises while unemployment falls (c) Prices fall along with output (d) Growth is high with zero inflation
Answer: (a)
Solution: The tempting wrong choice reverses the Phillips trade-off, but stagflation is precisely the breakdown of that trade-off — rising prices with rising joblessness, as seen in the 1970s oil shocks. So the answer is high inflation coexisting with high unemployment and stagnant growth.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Macroeconomics

128. If the Cash Reserve Ratio is increased by the RBI, the immediate effect on the economy is:
(a) Decrease in the lending capacity of banks (b) Increase in money supply (c) Fall in the repo rate automatically (d) Increase in government borrowing
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is to confuse CRR with the repo rate; changing CRR does not move the repo rate automatically. A higher CRR locks more funds with the RBI, shrinking lendable resources, so the answer is decrease in the lending capacity of banks.
Source: RBI official publications

129. Which of the following best describes 'seigniorage'?
(a) Tax on luxury imports (b) Interest paid on government bonds (c) Profit earned by the government from issuing currency (d) Fee charged for currency exchange
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is to treat it as a tax; seigniorage is not a tax but the difference between the face value of money and its cost of production. So the answer is profit earned by the government from issuing currency.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Macroeconomics

130. The 'Laffer Curve' illustrates the relationship between:
(a) Tax rates and tax revenue (b) Inflation and unemployment (c) Interest rates and savings (d) Exchange rates and exports
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is to confuse it with the Phillips Curve, which deals with inflation and unemployment. The Laffer Curve shows that beyond an optimal tax rate, revenue falls, so the answer is tax rates and tax revenue.
Source: Standard macroeconomics literature (PYQ-style)

131. Consider the following statements about GDP deflator and CPI:
1. GDP deflator covers all domestically produced goods and services.
2. CPI includes imported consumer goods.
3. GDP deflator uses a fixed basket of goods.
Which of the statements given above is/are correct?
(a) 2 and 3 only (b) 1 and 2 only (c) 1 and 3 only (d) 1, 2 and 3
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is statement 3 — it is the CPI, not the GDP deflator, that uses a fixed basket; the deflator's basket changes with current production. Statements 1 and 2 are correct, so the answer is 1 and 2 only.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Macroeconomics

132. 'Helicopter money' refers to:
(a) Subsidies for the aviation sector (b) Printing currency to finance government deficit directly (c) Foreign aid received by a country (d) Emergency loans from the IMF
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is the literal-sounding aviation option; the term, coined by Milton Friedman, means direct money creation to stimulate demand. So the answer is printing currency to finance government deficit directly.
Source: Standard macroeconomics literature (PYQ-style)

133. If the marginal propensity to consume (MPC) is 0.8, the value of the investment multiplier is:
(a) 4 (b) 1.25 (c) 8 (d) 5
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is 1.25, which results from wrongly dividing MPC by (1 − MPC) instead of using the correct formula 1/(1 − MPC) = 1/0.2. So the answer is 5.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Macroeconomics

134. Which of the following is an example of 'transfer payment' in national income accounting?
(a) Salary of a government school teacher (b) Wages paid to construction workers on a highway (c) Payment for purchase of defence equipment (d) Old-age pension paid by the government
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is the teacher's salary — it is factor income for services rendered and counts in GDP, unlike a pension which involves no current production. So the answer is old-age pension paid by the government.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Macroeconomics

135. The 'impossible trinity' (trilemma) in international economics states that a country cannot simultaneously have:
(a) Free trade, low inflation and high growth (b) Fixed exchange rate, free capital mobility and independent monetary policy (c) Fiscal surplus, current account surplus and capital account surplus (d) Low taxes, high spending and low debt
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is the fiscal option, which sounds plausible but is not the trilemma; the Mundell-Fleming trilemma concerns exchange rate, capital flows and monetary policy. So the answer is fixed exchange rate, free capital mobility and independent monetary policy.
Source: Standard international economics literature (PYQ-style)

136. Arrange the following events in the Indian economy in chronological order:
1. Nationalisation of 14 major commercial banks
2. Establishment of the Reserve Bank of India
3. Launch of the New Economic Policy (LPG reforms)
4. Nationalisation of the Imperial Bank to form SBI
(a) 1-2-4-3 (b) 4-2-1-3 (c) 2-1-4-3 (d) 2-4-1-3
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is placing bank nationalisation before SBI's formation; RBI came in 1935, SBI in 1955, bank nationalisation in 1969, and LPG reforms in 1991. So the answer is 2-4-1-3.
Source: RBI official publications

137. 'Core inflation' excludes which of the following from the price index?
(a) Food and fuel items (b) All manufactured goods (c) Services sector prices (d) Housing costs
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is to think core inflation excludes services; in fact it strips out the volatile food and fuel components to reveal underlying price trends. So the answer is food and fuel items.
Source: RBI official publications

138. Which of the following would cause the aggregate demand curve to shift to the right?
(a) Increase in income tax rates (b) Appreciation of the domestic currency (c) Increase in government expenditure (d) Increase in the cash reserve ratio
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is currency appreciation, which makes exports costlier and actually reduces net exports, shifting demand leftward. Higher government spending directly raises aggregate demand, so the answer is increase in government expenditure.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Macroeconomics

139. 'Tobin's q' ratio compares:
(a) Money supply to foreign reserves (b) Exports to imports of a country (c) Tax revenue to GDP (d) Market value of a firm's assets to their replacement cost
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is to associate it with trade ratios because of its unfamiliar name; Tobin's q guides investment decisions by comparing market valuation with replacement cost. So the answer is market value of a firm's assets to their replacement cost.
Source: Standard macroeconomics literature (PYQ-style)

140. Consider the following statements about 'fiscal drag':
1. It occurs when inflation pushes taxpayers into higher tax brackets.
2. It increases disposable income of households.
Which of the statements given above is/are correct?
(a) 2 only (b) 1 only (c) Both 1 and 2 (d) Neither 1 nor 2
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is statement 2 — fiscal drag reduces, not increases, disposable income because bracket creep raises the effective tax burden. Only statement 1 is correct, so the answer is 1 only.
Source: Standard public finance literature (PYQ-style)

141. The 'Gini coefficient' of 0 and 1 respectively indicate:
(a) Perfect inequality and perfect equality (b) Zero growth and maximum growth (c) Zero poverty and full poverty (d) Perfect equality and perfect inequality
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is reversing the two ends; a coefficient of 0 means everyone has equal income while 1 means one person holds all income. So the answer is perfect equality and perfect inequality.
Source: Standard development economics literature (PYQ-style)

142. Which of the following best describes 'quantitative easing'?
(a) Central bank purchase of long-term securities to inject liquidity (b) Raising interest rates to control inflation (c) Reducing government expenditure (d) Imposing quotas on imports
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is the import-quota option, which confuses 'quantitative' with trade quantity restrictions; QE is an unconventional monetary tool of large-scale asset purchases. So the answer is central bank purchase of long-term securities to inject liquidity.
Source: Standard monetary economics literature (PYQ-style)

143. In the context of the balance of payments, 'errors and omissions' are included to:
(a) Record smuggling transactions officially (b) Account for foreign aid (c) Balance the accounts due to unrecorded or misrecorded transactions (d) Adjust for exchange rate changes
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is the smuggling option — illegal trade is not officially recorded anywhere; the entry is a statistical residual ensuring the BoP sums to zero. So the answer is balancing the accounts due to unrecorded or misrecorded transactions.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Macroeconomics

144. 'Crowding out' effect refers to:
(a) Exit of foreign firms due to high taxes (b) Migration of workers from rural to urban areas (c) Fall in private investment due to increased government borrowing raising interest rates (d) Reduction in exports due to domestic demand
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is the foreign-firms option, which sounds like firms being pushed out; the term specifically describes government borrowing soaking up loanable funds and raising interest rates. So the answer is fall in private investment due to increased government borrowing raising interest rates.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Macroeconomics

145. Which of the following pairs is correctly matched?
(a) Okun's Law — relationship between unemployment and GDP growth (b) Okun's Law — relationship between inflation and money supply (c) Say's Law — demand creates its own supply (d) Keynes's Law — supply creates its own demand
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is the reversed Say's Law statement — Say's Law says supply creates its own demand, not the reverse. Okun's Law links unemployment and output growth, so the answer is Okun's Law — relationship between unemployment and GDP growth.
Source: Standard macroeconomics literature (PYQ-style)

146. If a country's nominal GDP grows by 10% and the GDP deflator rises by 6%, the approximate real GDP growth is:
(a) 16% (b) 10% (c) 6% (d) 4%
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is 16%, obtained by wrongly adding the two rates; real growth is approximately nominal growth minus inflation (10 − 6). So the answer is 4%.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Macroeconomics

147. 'Dutch Disease' refers to:
(a) Banking crisis caused by bad loans (b) Hyperinflation caused by war financing (c) Decline of manufacturing due to a boom in a natural resource sector (d) Trade deficit caused by cheap imports
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is the trade-deficit option; the term originated when Dutch gas exports strengthened the currency and hurt manufacturing competitiveness. So the answer is decline of manufacturing due to a boom in a natural resource sector.
Source: Standard international economics literature (PYQ-style)

148. Consider the following statements about the 'twin deficit' hypothesis:
1. It links the fiscal deficit with the current account deficit.
2. A higher fiscal deficit may lead to a higher current account deficit.
Which of the statements given above is/are correct?
(a) Both 1 and 2 (b) 2 only (c) 1 only (d) Neither 1 nor 2
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is to think the two deficits are unrelated; government dissaving can spill into higher imports and external borrowing, linking both deficits. Both statements are correct, so the answer is Both 1 and 2.
Source: Standard macroeconomics literature (PYQ-style)

149. Which of the following is NOT a function of money?
(a) Medium of exchange (b) Store of value (c) Hedge against inflation in all circumstances (d) Measure of value
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is that the first three are the classical functions, so the odd one must be chosen carefully; money loses real value during inflation and is not a guaranteed hedge. So the answer is hedge against inflation in all circumstances.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Macroeconomics

150. The 'Fisher equation' relates:
(a) Nominal interest rate, real interest rate and expected inflation (b) Money supply, velocity, price level and output (c) Exchange rate and interest rate differential (d) Tax rate and tax revenue
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is the second option, which is the Fisher quantity theory equation of exchange (MV = PY), not the Fisher equation of interest rates. The Fisher equation states nominal rate ≈ real rate + expected inflation, so the answer is nominal interest rate, real interest rate and expected inflation.
Source: Standard monetary economics literature (PYQ-style)
