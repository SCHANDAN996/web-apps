26. Which of the following is the highest peacetime gallantry award of India?
(a) Ashoka Chakra (b) Param Vir Chakra (c) Maha Vir Chakra (d) Kirti Chakra
Answer: (a)
Solution: The Param Vir Chakra is tempting because it is the highest gallantry award overall, but it is awarded for wartime valour, not peacetime. The Ashoka Chakra is the highest peacetime gallantry award, so the answer is Ashoka Chakra.
Source: Ministry of Defence, Government of India

27. The Integrated Defence Staff (IDS) headquarters is located at:
(a) South Block, New Delhi (b) Sena Bhawan, New Delhi (c) Kashmir House is wrong; it is at Kashmir House — correct location is Kashmir House, New Delhi (d) Kashmir House, New Delhi
Answer: (d)
Solution: South Block houses the Ministry of Defence, and Sena Bhawan is the Army Headquarters, so both are traps. The HQ Integrated Defence Staff functions from Kashmir House, New Delhi, so the answer is Kashmir House, New Delhi.
Source: Ministry of Defence (ids.nic.in)

28. Which missile of India is a submarine-launched ballistic missile (SLBM)?
(a) Prithvi-II (b) K-4 (c) Agni-V (d) Akash
Answer: (b)
Solution: Prithvi-II and Agni-V are surface-to-surface ballistic missiles launched from land, and Akash is a surface-to-air missile, so all three are traps. The K-4 is an SLBM developed for the Arihant-class submarines, so the answer is K-4.
Source: DRDO, Ministry of Defence

29. The Defence Research and Development Organisation (DRDO) was formed in which year?
(a) 1947 (b) 1984 (c) 1962 (d) 1958
Answer: (d)
Solution: 1947 is tempting as the year of independence, and 1962 is tempting due to the Sino-Indian war that spurred defence reforms, but DRDO was formed in 1958 by merging the Technical Development Establishments with the Defence Science Organisation, so the answer is 1958.
Source: DRDO official website (drdo.gov.in)

30. Which of the following is India's first indigenously built aircraft carrier?
(a) INS Vikrant (2013) (b) INS Viraat (c) INS Vikramaditya (d) INS Jalashwa
Answer: (a)
Solution: INS Vikramaditya is tempting but it is a refurbished Russian carrier (Admiral Gorshkov), and INS Viraat was British-built. INS Vikrant, commissioned in 2022, was built at Cochin Shipyard, so the answer is INS Vikrant (2013).
Source: Indian Navy official website

31. The Chief of Defence Staff (CDS) of India also heads which department?
(a) Department of Defence (b) Department of Defence Production (c) Department of Military Affairs (d) Department of Ex-Servicemen Welfare
Answer: (c)
Solution: The Department of Defence is headed by the Defence Secretary, which is the common trap. The CDS heads the Department of Military Affairs created in 2019, so the answer is Department of Military Affairs.
Source: Ministry of Defence, Government of India

32. Which command of the Indian Armed Forces is a tri-services command?
(a) Western Command (b) Southern Command (c) Andaman and Nicobar Command (d) Training Command
Answer: (c)
Solution: Western and Southern Commands are single-service commands of the Army and are the usual traps. The Andaman and Nicobar Command, set up in 2001 at Port Blair, is India's only tri-services theatre command, so the answer is Andaman and Nicobar Command.
Source: Ministry of Defence, Government of India

33. The Agni-V missile has an approximate range of:
(a) 700 km (b) 2,500 km (c) 3,500 km (d) Over 5,000 km
Answer: (d)
Solution: 3,500 km is tempting because it matches Agni-III's range, a classic similar-name confusion. Agni-V is an intercontinental-range ballistic missile with a range of over 5,000 km, so the answer is over 5,000 km.
Source: DRDO, Ministry of Defence

34. Which of the following is NOT a Central Armed Police Force (CAPF)?
(a) Border Security Force (b) Indo-Tibetan Border Police (c) National Security Guard (d) Central Reserve Police Force
Answer: (c)
Solution: BSF, ITBP and CRPF are all CAPFs under the Ministry of Home Affairs. The NSG is a federal contingency force under MHA but is not classified as a CAPF, so the answer is National Security Guard.
Source: Ministry of Home Affairs, Government of India

35. The 'Sagar Kavach' exercise is related to:
(a) Anti-submarine warfare (b) Deep-sea mining (c) Naval air defence (d) Coastal security
Answer: (d)
Solution: Anti-submarine warfare is tempting because Sagar Kavach is a naval-sounding exercise, but it is a coastal security exercise conducted by the Indian Navy with coastal states and other agencies after the 26/11 attacks, so the answer is coastal security.
Source: Indian Navy / Ministry of Defence

36. Which of the following statements about the Territorial Army is correct?
(a) It is a full-time regular force (b) It functions under the Ministry of Home Affairs (c) It is a part-time volunteer force that supports the regular Army (d) It was raised in 1962
Answer: (c)
Solution: The 1962 option is a chronological trap — the Territorial Army was raised in 1949, not 1962. It is a part-time volunteer force of employed citizens who relieve the regular Army from static duties, so the answer is that it is a part-time volunteer force that supports the regular Army.
Source: Ministry of Defence / Territorial Army official website

37. The 'Prithvi' missile series was developed under which programme?
(a) Ballistic Missile Defence Programme (b) Integrated Guided Missile Development Programme (c) Project Devil only (d) Nuclear Command Programme
Answer: (b)
Solution: Project Devil is tempting because it was an early DRDO missile project, but it was a precursor, not the parent programme. Prithvi, Agni, Akash, Nag and Trishul were all developed under the IGMDP launched in 1983 under Dr. A.P.J. Abdul Kalam, so the answer is Integrated Guided Missile Development Programme.
Source: DRDO official website

38. India's first nuclear test (1974) was codenamed:
(a) Smiling Buddha (b) Operation Shakti (c) Operation Vijay (d) Pokhran-II
Answer: (a)
Solution: Operation Shakti is the classic trap — it was the codename of the 1998 Pokhran-II tests, not 1974. The 1974 test at Pokhran was codenamed Smiling Buddha, so the answer is Smiling Buddha.
Source: Department of Atomic Energy, Government of India

39. Which force guards the India–Pakistan and India–Bangladesh borders?
(a) Indo-Tibetan Border Police (b) Border Security Force (c) Sashastra Seema Bal (d) Assam Rifles
Answer: (b)
Solution: SSB is tempting due to its similar name, but it guards the Nepal and Bhutan borders. The BSF guards the Pakistan and Bangladesh borders, so the answer is Border Security Force.
Source: Ministry of Home Affairs, Government of India

40. The 'BrahMos' missile is a joint venture between India and:
(a) France (b) Israel (c) Russia (d) United States
Answer: (c)
Solution: Israel is tempting because of the Barak missile cooperation with India. BrahMos is a joint venture between DRDO of India and NPO Mashinostroyenia of Russia, named after the Brahmaputra and Moskva rivers, so the answer is Russia.
Source: BrahMos Aerospace official website

41. Which of the following correctly matches the missile with its type?
(a) Nag — surface-to-air missile (b) Akash — anti-tank missile (c) Astra — air-to-air missile (d) Trishul — submarine-launched missile
Answer: (c)
Solution: The pairings are deliberately swapped as traps: Nag is anti-tank, Akash is surface-to-air, and Trishul was a short-range surface-to-air missile. Astra is India's beyond-visual-range air-to-air missile, so the answer is Astra — air-to-air missile.
Source: DRDO official website

42. The National Cadet Corps (NCC) was established in which year?
(a) 1948 (b) 1947 (c) 1950 (d) 1962
Answer: (a)
Solution: 1950 is a chronological trap linked to the Republic's first year. The NCC was established in 1948 under the NCC Act, 1948, so the answer is 1948.
Source: NCC official website / Ministry of Defence

43. Which of the following is the correct order of Army ranks from highest to lowest?
(a) General, Brigadier, Colonel, Major (b) General, Colonel, Brigadier, Major (c) General, Major General, Colonel, Brigadier (d) Field Marshal, General, Major General, Brigadier
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap options place Colonel above Brigadier or Brigadier above Major General, which reverses the actual hierarchy. The correct descending order is Field Marshal, General, Major General, Brigadier, so the answer is Field Marshal, General, Major General, Brigadier.
Source: Indian Army official website

44. INS Arihant is significant because it is:
(a) India's first aircraft carrier (b) India's first indigenously built nuclear-powered ballistic missile submarine (c) India's first diesel-electric submarine (d) India's first stealth frigate
Answer: (b)
Solution: The aircraft carrier option is a trap — that distinction belongs to INS Vikrant (1961, British-built). INS Arihant is India's first indigenous nuclear-powered ballistic missile submarine (SSBN), completing the nuclear triad, so the answer is India's first indigenously built nuclear-powered ballistic missile submarine.
Source: Ministry of Defence / Indian Navy

45. The 'Cold Start' doctrine is associated with:
(a) Indian Navy's maritime strategy (b) Indian Army's proactive conventional war strategy (c) Indian Air Force's high-altitude operations (d) India's nuclear doctrine
Answer: (b)
Solution: The nuclear doctrine option is tempting because doctrines are often discussed together, but India's nuclear doctrine is based on No First Use and credible minimum deterrence. Cold Start refers to the Army's doctrine of swift, limited conventional strikes, so the answer is Indian Army's proactive conventional war strategy.
Source: PYQ-style (defence studies literature)

46. Which of the following statements about India's nuclear doctrine is correct?
(a) India follows No First Use with credible minimum deterrence (b) India follows a first-use policy (c) India's doctrine permits use against non-nuclear states (d) The doctrine was first announced in 1974
Answer: (a)
Solution: The 1974 option is a chronological trap — the doctrine was formalised in 2003, building on the 1999 draft. India's doctrine rests on No First Use and credible minimum deterrence, so the answer is that India follows No First Use with credible minimum deterrence.
Source: Ministry of External Affairs, Government of India

47. The 'Dhanush' missile is:
(a) A ship-launched variant of Prithvi (b) An air-to-air missile (c) An anti-tank guided missile (d) A cruise missile
Answer: (a)
Solution: The cruise missile option is tempting because Dhanush is often confused with BrahMos. Dhanush is the naval (ship-launched) variant of the Prithvi ballistic missile, so the answer is a ship-launched variant of Prithvi.
Source: DRDO official website

48. Which organisation is responsible for guarding India's border with Myanmar?
(a) Border Security Force (b) Indo-Tibetan Border Police (c) Sashastra Seema Bal (d) Assam Rifles
Answer: (d)
Solution: ITBP is tempting but it guards the India–China border. The Assam Rifles, under the Ministry of Home Affairs (operational control of the Army), guards the India–Myanmar border, so the answer is Assam Rifles.
Source: Ministry of Home Affairs, Government of India

49. The 'Tejas' aircraft is:
(a) An imported fighter from France (b) An indigenously developed light combat aircraft (c) A transport aircraft (d) An unmanned aerial vehicle
Answer: (b)
Solution: The French import option is a trap referring to Rafale. Tejas is an indigenously developed single-engine light combat aircraft designed by ADA and built by HAL, so the answer is an indigenously developed light combat aircraft.
Source: HAL / DRDO official websites

50. Which of the following pairs of defence exercises and partner countries is correctly matched?
(a) Yudh Abhyas — India and USA (b) Varuna — India and Japan (c) Malabar — India and France (d) Indra — India and Australia
Answer: (a)
Solution: The options deliberately swap partners: Malabar involves India, USA, Japan and Australia; Varuna is with France; Indra is with Russia. Yudh Abhyas is the India–USA army exercise, so the answer is Yudh Abhyas — India and USA.
Source: Ministry of Defence, Government of India
