# Indian Defence & Security

## The Hook: A Mountain That Changed Everything

In May 1999, Indian soldiers climbed icy ridges at 16,000+ feet in Kargil, fighting an enemy sitting above them — a tactical nightmare. Operation Vijay succeeded, but it taught India a hard lesson: defence is not just about brave soldiers; it is about intelligence, weapons, borders, and policy working together. That is exactly what this chapter covers — the complete architecture of how India protects itself, and exactly the facts examiners love to twist.

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this chapter, you will be able to:

1. Describe the structure of India's armed forces and paramilitary forces.
2. Recall key defence organisations (DRDO, ISRO's defence role, nuclear command).
3. List major missiles, aircraft, ships, and their categories.
4. Explain India's nuclear doctrine and major defence exercises.
5. Spot and avoid the examiner's favourite traps in defence questions.

## 1. The Structure of India's Defence Forces

India's defence rests on three professional services plus supporting forces:

- **Indian Army** — headed by the Chief of Army Staff (COAS), headquartered in New Delhi. Organised into 7 commands (6 operational + 1 training).
- **Indian Navy** — headed by the Chief of Naval Staff (CNS). Three commands: Western (Mumbai), Eastern (Visakhapatnam), Southern (Kochi — the training command).
- **Indian Air Force** — headed by the Chief of Air Staff (CAS). Seven commands, including Western (New Delhi), Eastern (Shillong), Southern (Thiruvananthapuram), Central (Prayagraj), South-Western (Gandhinagar), plus Training and Maintenance commands.

Above them all sits the **Chief of Defence Staff (CDS)**, a post created in 2019 (first CDS: General Bipin Rawat). The CDS heads the Department of Military Affairs and acts as the principal military advisor to the government. The **Supreme Commander** of the armed forces is the **President of India**.

🧠 **Active Recall:** Who is the Supreme Commander of India's armed forces? How many commands does the Navy have, and which one handles training?

## 2. Paramilitary and Central Armed Police Forces (CAPF)

Examiners love confusing these. Learn the clean classification:

| Force | Role | Ministry |
|---|---|---|
| BSF (Border Security Force) | Borders with Pakistan & Bangladesh | Home Affairs |
| CRPF (Central Reserve Police Force) | Internal security, anti-Naxal ops | Home Affairs |
| CISF (Central Industrial Security Force) | Airports, metros, vital installations | Home Affairs |
| ITBP (Indo-Tibetan Border Police) | India–China border | Home Affairs |
| SSB (Sashastra Seema Bal) | Nepal & Bhutan borders | Home Affairs |
| Assam Rifles | North-East, India–Myanmar border | Home Affairs (administrative), Army (operational) |
| Coast Guard | Maritime zones, coastal security | **Defence** |

**Memory Hook — "B-C-C-I-S"**: BSF, CRPF, CISF, ITBP, SSB — the five CAPFs under the Home Ministry. Remember: **Coast Guard is the odd one out** — it works under the Ministry of Defence.

🧠 **Active Recall:** Which force guards the India–China border? Under which ministry does the Coast Guard operate?

## 3. Defence Research & Production

- **DRDO** (Defence Research and Development Organisation) — formed in **1958** by merging existing bodies; headquartered in New Delhi. It develops missiles, radars, aircraft systems, and more.
- **Integrated Guided Missile Development Programme (IGMDP)** — launched in 1983 under **Dr. A.P.J. Abdul Kalam** (the "Missile Man of India"). It produced the five famous missiles below.

### The Missile Family (Mnemonic: **"P-A-N-A-T"**)

| Missile | Type | Range (approx.) |
|---|---|---|
| **P**rithvi | Surface-to-surface, short range | 150–350 km |
| **A**kash | Surface-to-air | ~25–30 km |
| **N**ag | Anti-tank, "fire and forget" | ~4 km |
| **A**gni (I–V) | Ballistic, surface-to-surface | 700 km to 5,000+ km |
| **T**rishul | Short-range surface-to-air | ~9 km |

Later additions: **BrahMos** (Indo-Russian supersonic cruise missile, named after the Brahmaputra and Moskva rivers), **Astra** (air-to-air), **Nirbhay** (subsonic cruise missile).

🧠 **Active Recall:** Which missile is anti-tank? What does the name "BrahMos" combine?

## 4. Nuclear Doctrine and Command Structure

India conducted nuclear tests in **1974 (Pokhran-I, "Smiling Buddha")** and **1998 (Pokhran-II, "Operation Shakti")**.

India's nuclear doctrine rests on three pillars:

1. **No First Use (NFU)** — India will not be the first to use nuclear weapons.
2. **Credible Minimum Deterrence** — enough weapons to deter, not to race.
3. **Massive retaliation** to a nuclear attack on Indian territory or forces.

The **Nuclear Command Authority (NCA)** has two councils: the **Political Council** (chaired by the **Prime Minister** — the sole authority to order nuclear use) and the **Executive Council** (chaired by the National Security Advisor).

**Mnemonic — "PM Presses, NSA Plans"**: the Political Council (PM) decides; the Executive Council (NSA) executes.

🧠 **Active Recall:** What does NFU stand for? Who chairs the Political Council of the NCA?

## 5. Key Platforms: Ships, Submarines, Aircraft

- **INS Vikrant** — India's first **indigenously built** aircraft carrier, commissioned in **2022**. (INS Vikramaditya, from Russia, is the other carrier.)
- **INS Arihant** — India's first indigenous **nuclear-powered ballistic missile submarine (SSBN)**, completing the nuclear triad (land, air, sea).
- **Tejas** — indigenous Light Combat Aircraft (LCA), developed by HAL with DRDO.
- **Rafale** — 36 fighter jets acquired from France (Dassault); first squadron at Ambala.
- **S-400 Triumf** — long-range air defence system from Russia.

The **nuclear triad** means the ability to launch nuclear weapons from land (Agni missiles), air (aircraft), and sea (Arihant-class submarines).

## ⚠️ Caution: Examiner's Trap

**Trap 1 — Similar Names (समान नाम):** *Surya Kiran* (Nepal) vs *Mitra Shakti* (Sri Lanka) vs *Garuda Shakti* (Indonesia). All end in "Shakti/Kiran" and all are army exercises. The examiner swaps the partner country. Fix: memorise the mnemonic above, not just the list.

**Trap 2 — Almost-True Statement (लगभग सही कथन):** "The Coast Guard functions under the Ministry of Home Affairs." Sounds right (all other CAPFs are under Home), but the Coast Guard is under the **Ministry of Defence**. One word flips the answer.

**Trap 3 — Chronological Confusion (कालानुक्रमिक भ्रम):** Pokhran-I (1974) vs Pokhran-II (1998); DRDO (1958) vs IGMDP (1983). Questions ask "which happened first" or attach the wrong year to a correct event. Always anchor: Buddha smiled in '74, Shakti in '98.

**Trap 4 — Negative Phrasing (नकारात्मक वाक्यांश):** "Which of the following is NOT a surface-to-air missile?" Akash and Trishul are; Nag is anti-tank. Under time pressure, students mark Akash. Read the NOT twice.
