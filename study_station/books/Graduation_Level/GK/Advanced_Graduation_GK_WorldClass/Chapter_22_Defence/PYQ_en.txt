# 📊 PYQ Analysis — Indian Defence & Security

## 1. How Exams Ask Questions from This Chapter

Across UPSC (Prelims), SSC CGL/CHSL, Banking (IBPS/SBI), Railway (NTPC/Group D) and State PCS prelims, questions from Indian Defence & Security follow a stable pattern. The chapter is **fact-dense but concept-light** — examiners test memory of names, numbers, places and chronology, not analysis. The recurring sub-topics are:

- **Missile systems** — the single most repeated area. Examiners love asking the *type* of a missile (surface-to-air vs. surface-to-surface vs. anti-tank), its *range class*, and which agency developed it (DRDO). Prithvi, Agni, Akash, Nag, Trishul, BrahMos and Astra appear again and again.
- **Integrated Guided Missile Development Programme (IGMDP)** — its five missiles and its association with Dr. A.P.J. Abdul Kalam.
- **Nuclear tests** — Pokhran-I (1974, "Smiling Buddha") vs. Pokhran-II (1998, "Operation Shakti"). The trap is almost always the year or the code-name.
- **Defence commands and headquarters** — which command is where (e.g., Northern Command — Udhampur, Southern Naval Command — Kochi), and the tri-services commands (Andaman & Nicobar Command, Strategic Forces Command).
- **Ranks and equivalents** — Army–Navy–Air Force rank equivalence (e.g., Colonel = Captain = Group Captain).
- **Joint exercises** — matching exercise names with partner countries (Malabar, Yudh Abhyas, Varuna, Indra, Mitra Shakti).
- **Paramilitary / CAPF forces** — which force guards which border (BSF–Pakistan/Bangladesh, ITBP–China, SSB–Nepal/Bhutan, Assam Rifles–India–Myanmar border) and their parent ministries.
- **Defence production & organisations** — DRDO (1958, HQ New Delhi), HAL, BEL, ordnance factories, and the role of the Chief of Defence Staff (CDS).
- **Wars and operations** — 1947–48, 1962, 1965, 1971, Kargil 1999 (Operation Vijay), and operations like Meghdoot (Siachen) and Cactus (Maldives).

## 2. Exam-wise Emphasis (in words, not numbers)

- **UPSC Prelims:** statement-based questions on missiles, exercises, and organisations; often pairs a correct fact with a subtly wrong one.
- **SSC CGL/CHSL:** direct one-liners — range of a missile, HQ of a command, year of a war, first CDS.
- **Banking (IBPS/SBI):** favours recent defence news anchored to static facts — exercises, CDS, theatre commands, defence corridors.
- **Railway (NTPC/Group D):** simplest level — "who developed", "where is HQ", "which force guards which border".
- **State PCS:** mixes national facts with state-linked items (e.g., ordnance factories, regimental centres located in that state).

## 3. The Examiner's Traps (जाल)

1. **Almost Correct Statement** — "Agni-V is a surface-to-air missile." Everything sounds right except the category. Always check the *class* of the weapon.
2. **Chronological Confusion** — Pokhran-I (1974) vs. Pokhran-II (1998); DRDO formed 1958 vs. IGMDP launched 1983. Examiners swap years between two similar events.
3. **Similar-Sounding Names** — Prithvi vs. Prahaar; Akash (SAM) vs. Astra (air-to-air); Nag (anti-tank) vs. Nirbhay (cruise). One letter changes the answer.
4. **Negative Phrasing** — "Which of the following is NOT a ballistic missile?" Students miss the NOT and pick the first true statement.
5. **Ministry/Command Swap** — Assam Rifles is *administratively* under the Ministry of Home Affairs but *operationally* under the Army — a classic two-layer trap.
6. **Range Inflation** — options differ only in range figures (e.g., 150 km vs. 250 km vs. 350 km for Prithvi-II). Learn ranges in bands, not exact decimals.

## 4. Top 10 High-Yield PYQ-Style Questions with Solutions

**Q1.** Which of the following is a surface-to-air missile?
(a) Prithvi (b) Agni (c) Akash (d) Nag
**Answer: (c)**
**Solution:** Akash is a medium-range surface-to-air missile. Prithvi and Agni are surface-to-surface ballistic missiles; Nag is an anti-tank guided missile.
**Trap type:** Similar-Sounding Names (Akash vs. Astra also appears as a distractor).
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q2.** India's first nuclear test, code-named "Smiling Buddha", was conducted in:
(a) 1971 (b) 1974 (c) 1998 (d) 1965
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** Pokhran-I took place on 18 May 1974 at Pokhran, Rajasthan. The 1998 tests (Pokhran-II) were code-named Operation Shakti.
**Trap type:** Chronological Confusion (1974 vs. 1998).
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q3.** Which of the following is NOT a ballistic missile?
(a) Agni-II (b) Prithvi-III (c) BrahMos (d) Dhanush
**Answer: (c)**
**Solution:** BrahMos is a supersonic *cruise* missile, developed jointly with Russia. Agni, Prithvi and Dhanush are ballistic missiles.
**Trap type:** Negative Phrasing — read the NOT twice before answering.
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q4.** The Integrated Guided Missile Development Programme (IGMDP) was associated with which scientist?
(a) Homi J. Bhabha (b) Vikram Sarabhai (c) A.P.J. Abdul Kalam (d) Raja Ramanna
**Answer: (c)**
**Solution:** Dr. Kalam led the IGMDP (launched 1983), which produced Prithvi, Agni, Akash, Nag and Trishul. Bhabha and Sarabhai belong to the nuclear/space programmes.
**Trap type:** Similar-Sounding Names (all four are famous scientists).
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q5.** The Andaman and Nicobar Command is:
(a) a purely naval command (b) India's only tri-services theatre command
(c) under the Strategic Forces Command (d) headquartered at Port Blair but commanded by the Air Force only
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** Set up in 2001 at Port Blair, it is India's first and only tri-services (geographical theatre) command. The Strategic Forces Command handles nuclear assets and is functional, not geographical.
**Trap type:** Almost Correct Statement — (d) is half-true on HQ but wrong on command.
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q6.** Which force guards the India–China border (Line of Actual Control)?
(a) BSF (b) ITBP (c) SSB (d) Assam Rifles
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** The Indo-Tibetan Border Police guards the LAC. BSF guards the Pakistan and Bangladesh borders; SSB guards the Nepal and Bhutan borders; Assam Rifles guards the India–Myanmar border.
**Trap type:** Ministry/Command Swap — all four are CAPFs, so the pairing must be memorised exactly.
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q7.** The rank of "Group Captain" in the Indian Air Force is equivalent to which Army rank?
(a) Major (b) Lieutenant Colonel (c) Colonel (d) Brigadier
**Answer: (c)**
**Solution:** Colonel (Army) = Captain (Navy) = Group Captain (IAF). Brigadier = Commodore = Air Commodore.
**Trap type:** Almost Correct — (b) tempts those who confuse Colonel with Lt. Colonel.
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q8.** Operation Meghdoot is associated with:
(a) Kargil (b) Siachen Glacier (c) Maldives (d) Sri Lanka
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** Launched in 1984, Operation Meghdoot secured the Siachen Glacier. Kargil 1999 = Operation Vijay; Maldives 1988 = Operation Cactus; Sri Lanka = Operation Pawan.
**Trap type:** Chronological Confusion + Similar Names (all are famous operations).
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q9.** Exercise "Malabar" is conducted between the navies of:
(a) India and France (b) India, USA and Japan (c) India and Russia (d) India and Sri Lanka
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** Malabar began as an India–US bilateral exercise (1992); Japan became a permanent partner in 2015. India–France = Varuna; India–Russia = Indra.
**Trap type:** Similar-Sounding Names — exercise-country pairing is the most common mismatch trap.
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q10.** The Defence Research and Development Organisation (DRDO) was established in:
(a) 1948 (b) 1958 (c) 1968 (d) 1972
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** DRDO was formed in 1958 by merging the Technical Development Establishments with the Defence Science Organisation; HQ is New Delhi.
**Trap type:** Chronological Confusion — 1948 (post-Independence institutions) is the favourite wrong option.
**Source:** PYQ-style

## 5. The 15/45-Second Rule Applied

**Rule:** If a question is a direct fact you know (name, year, HQ), answer within **15 seconds** and move on. If it is statement-based or negative-phrased, allow up to **45 seconds** — never more; mark and return later.

**Example A — 15-second question (Q6 above):** "Which force guards the India–China border?" This is a single memorised pairing. If you know ITBP, click and go in under 15 seconds. Do not re-read the options — BSF/SSB/Assam Rifles pairings are either in your memory or not; extra time adds nothing.

**Example B — 45-second question (Q3 above):** "Which is NOT a ballistic missile?" Here the danger is the negative phrasing plus four plausible names. Spend the full 45 seconds: (1) underline NOT mentally, (2) classify each option — Agni (ballistic), Prithvi (ballistic), BrahMos (cruise), Dhanush (ballistic), (3) confirm only one is cruise. If after 45 seconds two options still look possible, mark it and move on — do not gamble between two.

**Skip strategy for this chapter:** Skip (a) range-figure questions where options differ by small numbers and you only remember the band, and (b) "which of the following pairs is/are correctly matched" questions where one pair is unknown — these consume time with low success odds. Attempt first all direct fact questions (missile type, HQ, year, exercise partner), then return to statement-based ones.
