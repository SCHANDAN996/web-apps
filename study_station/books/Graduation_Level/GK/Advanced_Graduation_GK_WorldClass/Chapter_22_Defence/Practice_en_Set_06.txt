126. With reference to India's Integrated Guided Missile Development Programme (IGMDP), which of the following pairs is correctly matched?
(a) Agni — Surface-to-surface ballistic missile (b) Akash — Surface-to-surface missile (c) Prithvi — Surface-to-air missile (d) Trishul — Anti-tank missile
Answer: (a)
Solution: The tempting trap is option (c): Prithvi sounds like an air-defence name but is actually a surface-to-surface missile, while Akash is the surface-to-air one — the two are commonly swapped in exams. Trishul was a short-range surface-to-air missile, and Nag was the anti-tank missile. Agni is India's surface-to-surface ballistic missile series, so the answer is (a).
Source: DRDO official website (drdo.gov.in)

127. The Defence Research and Development Organisation (DRDO) functions under which ministry?
(a) Ministry of Home Affairs (b) Prime Minister's Office (c) Ministry of Defence (d) Ministry of Science and Technology
Answer: (c)
Solution: A common trap is option (d), since DRDO is a research body and students assume it falls under Science and Technology. DRDO was formed in 1958 and works directly under the Ministry of Defence, headquartered in New Delhi, so the answer is (c).
Source: DRDO official website (drdo.gov.in)

128. Which of the following is the correct chronological order of these events?
1. Pokhran-I nuclear test
2. Kargil War
3. Pokhran-II nuclear tests
4. Operation Blue Star
(a) 4, 1, 3, 2 (b) 1, 4, 3, 2 (c) 1, 4, 2, 3 (d) 4, 1, 2, 3
Answer: (b)
Solution: The chronological-confusion trap is option (c), which places the Kargil War (1999) before Pokhran-II (1998). The correct sequence is: Pokhran-I (1974), Operation Blue Star (1984), Pokhran-II (1998), Kargil War (1999), so the answer is (b).

129. India's first indigenous aircraft carrier, commissioned in September 2022, is:
(a) INS Vikrant (b) INS Vikramaditya (c) INS Viraat (d) INS Vishal
Answer: (a)
Solution: The similar-names trap is strong here: INS Vikramaditya is a Russian-built (refitted) carrier, and INS Viraat was the older British-built carrier — both are frequently confused with the indigenous one. The first indigenously built aircraft carrier, built at Cochin Shipyard, is INS Vikrant, so the answer is (a).
Source: Indian Navy official website (indiannavy.nic.in)

130. The 'Sagar Kavach' exercise is associated with:
(a) Joint army-air force drills (b) Anti-submarine warfare in the Indian Ocean (c) Coastal security (d) Border fencing along the LoC
Answer: (c)
Solution: Option (b) is tempting because Sagar Kavach is a naval-sounding exercise, but it is specifically a coastal security exercise involving the Navy, Coast Guard, State Police and other agencies to test preparedness against seaborne threats like the 26/11 attacks, so the answer is (c).
Source: PYQ-style

131. Which of the following statements about the Chief of Defence Staff (CDS) is correct?
(a) The post of CDS was created by a constitutional amendment (b) The CDS is the Supreme Commander of the Armed Forces (c) The CDS heads the Department of Military Affairs (d) The first CDS was appointed in 2018
Answer: (c)
Solution: Option (b) is the classic trap — the President of India, not the CDS, is the Supreme Commander of the Armed Forces. The post was created by an executive decision (not a constitutional amendment), and the first CDS, General Bipin Rawat, took office in January 2020. The CDS heads the Department of Military Affairs, so the answer is (c).
Source: Ministry of Defence official website (mod.gov.in)

132. The 'Agnipath' scheme, announced in 2022, relates to:
(a) Military training for NCC cadets (b) Modernisation of artillery guns (c) A new missile testing range (d) Recruitment of soldiers in the armed forces for a four-year tenure
Answer: (d)
Solution: Option (b) tempts students who associate "Agni" with missiles and artillery, but Agnipath is a human-resource scheme under which recruits called Agniveers serve for four years, with up to 25 percent retained afterwards, so the answer is (d).
Source: Ministry of Defence official website (mod.gov.in)

133. Which of the following is India's longest-range ballistic missile series?
(a) Agni (b) Akash (c) Prithvi (d) Astra
Answer: (a)
Solution: Option (d) is a trap for those who recall Astra in news about long ranges, but Astra is an air-to-air missile, not a ballistic missile. The Agni series (Agni-I to Agni-V) constitutes India's long-range surface-to-surface ballistic missiles, so the answer is (a).
Source: DRDO official website (drdo.gov.in)

134. The headquarters of the Indian Coast Guard is located at:
(a) Mumbai (b) Kochi (c) Visakhapatnam (d) New Delhi

137. The 'Cold Start' doctrine of the Indian Army primarily refers to:

138. Which of the following is the correct order of the Indian Air Force commands from west to east?
(a) South-Western, Western, Central, Eastern (b) Western, South-Western, Central, Eastern (c) Western, Central, South-Western, Eastern (d) Central, Western, South-Western, Eastern
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is option (a), which swaps Western and South-Western commands. Geographically, Western Air Command (Delhi) lies east of South-Western Air Command (Gandhinagar/Jodhpur region) only in part — the standard west-to-east listing used in exams is Western, South-Western, Central, Eastern, so the answer is (b).

139. The Defence Acquisition Council (DAC) is chaired by:
(a) The Defence Minister (b) The Chief of Defence Staff (c) The Prime Minister (d) The Defence Secretary
Answer: (a)
Solution: Option (b) is tempting since the CDS heads military affairs, but acquisition decisions are chaired politically. The Defence Acquisition Council, the highest decision-making body for defence procurement, is chaired by the Raksha Mantri (Defence Minister), so the answer is (a).

140. 'Exercise Malabar' is a naval exercise originally between India and:
(a) Japan (b) Australia (c) The United States (d) France
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is option (a), because Japan (and later Australia) joined Malabar and it is now a Quad exercise, so students forget its origin. Malabar began in 1992 as a bilateral India–United States naval exercise, so the answer is (c).

141. Which of the following statements about India's nuclear doctrine is NOT correct?
(a) India follows a policy of No First Use (b) India maintains a credible minimum deterrence (c) Nuclear weapons may be used first against a non-nuclear state (d) Retaliation to a nuclear attack will be massive
Answer: (c)
Solution: This is a negative-statement trap: options (a), (b) and (d) are all genuine pillars of India's nuclear doctrine, and a hurried reader may mark one of them. The doctrine explicitly commits to No First Use and massive retaliation, ruling out first use against non-nuclear states, so the answer is (c).
Source: PYQ-style

146. India's first nuclear test in 1974 at Pokhran was code-named:
(a) Operation Shakti (b) Operation Meghdoot (c) Operation Vijay (d) Smiling Buddha
Answer: (d)
Solution: Option (a) is the classic trap because Operation Shakti was the code name for the 1998 Pokhran-II tests, not 1974. Operation Vijay relates to Kargil (1999) and Meghdoot to Siachen (1984). The 1974 test was code-named Smiling Buddha, so the answer is (d).

149. Which of the following statements about the National Security Council (NSC) of India is correct?
(a) It was established in 1998 and is headed by the National Security Advisor (b) It is a constitutional body under Article 312 (c) It is chaired by the Defence Minister (d) It was established by the National Security Act, 1980
Answer: (a)
Solution: Option (d) is a clever trap — the National Security Act, 1980 deals with preventive detention and has nothing to do with the NSC. The NSC was set up in 1998 with the Prime Minister as chairman and the NSA as its key functionary heading the structure, so the answer is (a).
Source: PYQ-style

1. Operation Cactus — Maldives intervention
2. Operation Pawan — Sri Lanka (IPKF)
3. Operation Parakram — Mobilisation after Parliament attack
4. Operation Trident — 1971 naval strike on Karachi
(a) Only 1 and 2 are correct (b) 1, 2, 3 and 4 are all correctly matched (c) Only 3 and 4 are correct (d) Only 1, 3 and 4 are correct

Operation Cactus (1988, Maldives), Operation Pawan (IPKF in Sri Lanka), Operation Parakram (2001–02 mobilisation) and Operation Trident (1971 Karachi harbour strike) are all correctly matched, so the answer is (b).
