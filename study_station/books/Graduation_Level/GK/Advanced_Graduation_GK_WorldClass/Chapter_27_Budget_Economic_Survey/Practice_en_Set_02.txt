Questions 26–50: Medium

26. Under Article 112 of the Constitution, the Annual Financial Statement shall be caused to be laid before both Houses of Parliament by:
(a) The Finance Minister (b) The Prime Minister (c) The President (d) The Comptroller and Auditor General
Answer: (c)
Solution: The Finance Minister is the tempting choice because he physically presents the Budget in the Lok Sabha, but Article 112 states that the President shall cause the Annual Financial Statement to be laid before both Houses. So the answer is the President.
Source: Constitution of India, Article 112

27. The Economic Survey of India is prepared under the guidance of the Chief Economic Adviser by which department?
(a) Department of Revenue (b) Department of Expenditure (c) NITI Aayog (d) Department of Economic Affairs
Answer: (d)
Solution: NITI Aayog is a tempting wrong choice because it handles policy think-tank work, but the Economic Survey is prepared by the Economics Division of the Department of Economic Affairs, Ministry of Finance. So the answer is the Department of Economic Affairs.
Source: Ministry of Finance (mof.gov.in)

28. The Economic Survey is traditionally presented in Parliament:
(a) Along with the Union Budget (b) One day before the Union Budget (c) During the Winter Session (d) After the Budget is passed
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Along with the Union Budget" is the trap — the Survey reviews the past year and is tabled separately, traditionally one day before the Budget. So the answer is one day before the Union Budget.
Source: Ministry of Finance / PIB

29. Fiscal deficit is best described as:
(a) Revenue expenditure minus revenue receipts (b) Total expenditure minus total revenue receipts (c) Total expenditure minus total receipts excluding borrowings (d) Fiscal deficit minus interest payments
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is total expenditure minus revenue receipts, which wrongly ignores non-debt capital receipts; fiscal deficit equals total expenditure minus total non-debt receipts. So the answer is total expenditure minus total receipts excluding borrowings.
Source: Union Budget documents, Ministry of Finance

30. Primary deficit is calculated as:
(a) Revenue deficit minus interest payments (b) Fiscal deficit minus interest payments (c) Total expenditure minus revenue deficit (d) Fiscal deficit plus interest payments
Answer: (b)
Solution: Adding interest payments is the tempting error; primary deficit removes the interest burden of past borrowings, so it equals fiscal deficit minus interest payments. So the answer is fiscal deficit minus interest payments.
Source: Union Budget documents, Ministry of Finance

31. The FRBM Act, 2003 primarily mandates the Government to:
(a) Reduce fiscal and revenue deficits and set debt targets (b) Balance the Budget every year (c) Abolish plan expenditure (d) Present the Budget in February
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Balance the Budget every year" is the trap — the FRBM Act sets targets and escape clauses for reducing deficits and debt, not an annual balanced-budget rule. So the answer is reduce fiscal and revenue deficits and set debt targets.
Source: FRBM Act 2003, Ministry of Finance

32. The Union Budget of India is presented in the Lok Sabha on which date since 2017?
(a) 31 January (b) Last working day of February (c) 15 February (d) 1 February
Answer: (d)
Solution: The last working day of February was the old convention, a classic chronological trap; since 2017 the Budget is presented on 1 February. So the answer is 1 February.
Source: PIB / Ministry of Finance

33. From which Budget was the Railway Budget merged with the Union Budget?
(a) 2016–17 (b) 2018–19 (c) 2015–16 (d) 2017–18
Answer: (d)
Solution: 2016–17 is the trap because the merger was announced and decided in 2016, but it took effect from Budget 2017–18, ending a 92-year separate Railway Budget tradition. So the answer is 2017–18.
Source: PIB / Ministry of Railways

34. The classification of expenditure into 'Plan' and 'Non-Plan' was discontinued from which Budget?
(a) 2015–16 (b) 2017–18 (c) 2018–19 (d) 2016–17
Answer: (b)
Solution: 2015–16 tempts because the Planning Commission was replaced by NITI Aayog in 2015, but the Plan/Non-Plan distinction in the Budget was removed from 2017–18, replaced by revenue and capital classification. So the answer is 2017–18.
Source: Ministry of Finance

35. Which of the following is a capital receipt of the Government of India?
(a) Income tax (b) Disinvestment proceeds (c) Interest receipts on loans (d) GST collections
Answer: (b)
Solution: Interest receipts tempt because they involve loans, but they are revenue receipts; disinvestment reduces government assets and is a non-debt capital receipt. So the answer is disinvestment proceeds.
Source: Union Budget documents, Ministry of Finance

36. Revenue deficit means:
(a) Revenue expenditure exceeds revenue receipts (b) Capital expenditure exceeds capital receipts (c) Total expenditure exceeds total receipts (d) Fiscal deficit exceeds revenue deficit
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is the total expenditure definition, which describes fiscal deficit, not revenue deficit; revenue deficit concerns only the revenue account. So the answer is revenue expenditure exceeds revenue receipts.
Source: Union Budget documents, Ministry of Finance

37. The 'Halwa Ceremony' before the Budget marks:
(a) The signing of the Budget by the President (b) The presentation of the Economic Survey (c) The start of the lock-in period for Budget printing officials (d) The passing of the Appropriation Bill
Answer: (c)
Solution: The President's signature is a trap — the President does not sign the Budget before presentation; the Halwa ceremony marks the beginning of the lock-in of officials involved in printing the Budget to maintain secrecy. So the answer is the start of the lock-in period for Budget printing officials.
Source: PIB / Ministry of Finance

38. The Appropriation Bill is passed to:
(a) Levy new taxes (b) Amend the FRBM Act (c) Authorise withdrawal of money from the Consolidated Fund of India (d) Present the Economic Survey
Answer: (c)
Solution: Levying new taxes is the trap — that is done through the Finance Bill; the Appropriation Bill authorises expenditure from the Consolidated Fund after the demands for grants are voted. So the answer is authorise withdrawal of money from the Consolidated Fund of India.
Source: Constitution of India, Articles 114–116

39. The Finance Bill is related to which Article of the Constitution?
(a) Article 112 (b) Article 114 (c) Article 110 (d) Article 280
Answer: (c)
Solution: Article 112 tempts because it defines the Annual Financial Statement, but the Finance Bill is a Money Bill, and Money Bills are defined under Article 110. So the answer is Article 110.
Source: Constitution of India

40. The first Union Budget of independent India was presented by:
(a) John Mathai (b) R. K. Shanmukham Chetty (c) Morarji Desai (d) C. D. Deshmukh
Answer: (b)
Solution: John Mathai is the trap because he presented the 1949–50 Budget and was an early finance minister, but the first Budget of independent India (1947–48) was presented by R. K. Shanmukham Chetty on 26 November 1947. So the answer is R. K. Shanmukham Chetty.
Source: Ministry of Finance / PIB

41. Who has presented the maximum number of Union Budgets in India?
(a) P. Chidambaram (b) Pranab Mukherjee (c) Yashwant Sinha (d) Morarji Desai
Answer: (d)
Solution: P. Chidambaram is a tempting modern answer, but Morarji Desai holds the record with ten Budgets, the highest by any finance minister. So the answer is Morarji Desai.
Source: Ministry of Finance records

42. The 'Gender Budget Statement' in the Union Budget:
(a) Reports allocations for schemes benefiting women (b) Is a separate Budget for women (c) Is prepared by the Ministry of Women and Child Development alone (d) Was introduced in 2020
Answer: (a)
Solution: "A separate Budget for women" is the classic trap — the Gender Budget Statement (introduced in 2005–06) is part of the Union Budget and reports the share of allocations meant for women across ministries. So the answer is reports allocations for schemes benefiting women.
Source: Union Budget documents, Ministry of Finance

43. Effective capital expenditure is defined as:
(a) Capital expenditure minus interest payments (b) Capital expenditure minus disinvestment (c) Revenue expenditure plus capital expenditure (d) Capital expenditure plus grants-in-aid for creation of capital assets
Answer: (d)
Solution: Subtracting disinvestment is the trap; effective capital expenditure adds grants-in-aid given to states and others for creating capital assets, since those too build assets. So the answer is capital expenditure plus grants-in-aid for creation of capital assets.
Source: Union Budget documents, Ministry of Finance

44. In the election year 2024, the full Economic Survey for 2023–24 was presented in Parliament on:
(a) 31 January 2024 (b) 30 January 2024 (c) 1 February 2024 (d) 22 July 2024
Answer: (d)
Solution: 31 January is the trap because that is the usual Survey date, but 2024 was an election year with only an Interim Budget in February, so the full Economic Survey 2023–24 was tabled on 22 July 2024, a day before the full Budget. So the answer is 22 July 2024.
Source: PIB / Ministry of Finance

45. An Interim Budget is presented when:
(a) The fiscal deficit crosses the FRBM target (b) General elections are due and a full Budget is left to the new government (c) The Finance Minister resigns (d) The GDP growth falls below 5 percent
Answer: (b)
Solution: The fiscal deficit trap confuses the Interim Budget with FRBM escape clauses; an Interim Budget (Vote on Account) is presented in an election year so the incoming government presents the full Budget. So the answer is general elections are due and a full Budget is left to the new government.
Source: PIB / Ministry of Finance

46. Which committee's recommendations led to the merger of the Railway Budget with the Union Budget?
(a) Bibek Debroy Committee (b) Chelliah Committee (c) Kelkar Committee (d) N. K. Singh Committee
Answer: (a)
Solution: The N. K. Singh Committee is the trap — it reviewed the FRBM framework, not the Railway Budget; the Bibek Debroy Committee on railway restructuring recommended the merger. So the answer is the Bibek Debroy Committee.
Source: Ministry of Railways / PIB

47. The 'Vote on Account' allows the government to:
(a) Withdraw funds for a short period pending full Budget approval (b) Levy new taxes for the full year (c) Skip parliamentary approval of expenditure (d) Present the Economic Survey
Answer: (a)
Solution: Levying new taxes is the trap — a Vote on Account deals only with expenditure, not taxation; it permits withdrawal from the Consolidated Fund for a limited period (usually two months) until the full Budget is passed. So the answer is withdraw funds for a short period pending full Budget approval.
Source: Constitution of India, Article 116

48. The Economic Survey 2022–23 projected India's real GDP growth for 2023–24 in the range of:
(a) 6.0–6.8 percent (b) 5.0–5.5 percent (c) 7.0–7.8 percent (d) 8.0–8.5 percent
Answer: (a)
Solution: The higher ranges tempt because India was called the fastest-growing major economy, but the Survey's baseline projection for 2023–24 was 6.0–6.8 percent, with 6.5 percent as the midpoint. So the answer is 6.0–6.8 percent.
Source: Economic Survey 2022–23, Ministry of Finance

49. Which of the following statements about the Economic Survey is correct?
(a) It is an annual review of the economy tabled by the Ministry of Finance, without constitutional mandate (b) It is a statutory report of the CAG (c) It is a constitutional document under Article 112 (d) It must be approved by the Rajya Sabha
Answer: (a)
Solution: Article 112 is the trap — that covers the Annual Financial Statement (Budget), not the Survey; the Economic Survey is a non-constitutional, non-statutory annual document prepared by the Ministry of Finance. So the answer is it is an annual review of the economy tabled by the Ministry of Finance, without constitutional mandate.
Source: Ministry of Finance

50. The 'Guillotine' in the Budget process refers to:
(a) Rejection of the Finance Bill (b) The President's assent to the Budget (c) Putting all outstanding demands for grants to vote together without discussion (d) Cutting of the fiscal deficit
Answer: (c)
Solution: Rejection of the Finance Bill is the trap — the guillotine concerns demands for grants, not the Finance Bill; when time runs out, the Speaker puts all remaining demands to vote together, which is called the guillotine. So the answer is putting all outstanding demands for grants to vote together without discussion.
Source: Parliamentary procedure, Lok Sabha Secretariat
