# Budget & Economic Survey

## 🎯 The Hook: A Briefcase That Became a Tablet

On 1 February 2021, Finance Minister Nirmala Sitharaman walked into Parliament carrying not the traditional leather briefcase (*bahi-khata* had already replaced it in 2019), but a tablet wrapped in a red cloth — India's Budget had gone fully paperless. That small moment captures a big truth: the Union Budget is not just a speech, it is a **constitutional ritual** with fixed players, fixed vocabulary, and fixed traps that examiners love. SSC, Banking and State PCS exams regularly ask questions from this single event — and most students lose marks not on the numbers, but on the **static skeleton** underneath: Article 112, the "Annual Financial Statement," the difference between Budget and Economic Survey.

Master the skeleton once, and every year's Budget becomes easy.

---

## 📌 Learning Objectives

By the end of this chapter you will be able to:

1. Define the Union Budget and the Economic Survey, and state their constitutional/legal basis.
2. Explain the full Budget-making process, from halwa ceremony to Appropriation and Finance Bills.
3. Distinguish between key deficit terms (fiscal, revenue, primary, effective revenue).
4. Classify Budget documents and types of budgets (interim, vote-on-account, gender, outcome).
5. Recall who presented which historic Budget and why it mattered.
6. Spot and defeat the examiner's favourite traps.

## 1. What Exactly Is the Budget?

The word "Budget" comes from the French *bougette*, meaning a small leather bag. But here is the first exam fact: **the word "Budget" does not appear anywhere in the Indian Constitution.**

**Article 112** of the Constitution calls it the **"Annual Financial Statement"** — a statement of the *estimated* receipts and expenditure of the Government of India for a financial year (1 April to 31 March).

Key points:

- It is an **estimate**, not an account of actual spending (that is the job of the Comptroller and Auditor General, CAG).
- It is presented in the **Lok Sabha** by the Finance Minister, traditionally on **1 February** (since 2017; earlier it was the last working day of February).
- Since 2017, the **Railway Budget was merged** with the General Budget, ending a 92-year-old separate tradition (separate since 1924, on the recommendation of the Acworth Committee).
- The Budget is laid before **both Houses**, but only the Lok Sabha votes on Demands for Grants.

🧠 **Active Recall:** Which Article calls the Budget the "Annual Financial Statement"? In which year was the Railway Budget merged with the General Budget?

## 2. The Economic Survey — The Budget's Elder Sibling

The **Economic Survey** is an annual document of the Ministry of Finance, prepared under the guidance of the **Chief Economic Adviser (CEA)**, and tabled in Parliament **one day before the Union Budget** (usually 31 January).

- It **reviews the economy's performance** over the past year and flags challenges ahead.
- It is **not binding** on the government — it is an advisory, analytical document.
- It was first presented in **1950–51**, was part of Budget documents until 1964, and has been **separated and presented a day before the Budget since 1964**.
- Since 2021–22, it has often been presented in **two volumes** (Volume 1: conceptual/forward-looking; Volume 2: data and sectoral review).

**The crucial contrast (a favourite exam trap):**

| Feature | Economic Survey | Union Budget |
|---|---|---|
| Nature | Review of the past year | Estimates for the coming year |
| Constitutional status | No constitutional mention | Article 112 |
| Prepared under | Chief Economic Adviser | Finance Minister / Budget Division, DEA |
| Tabled on | Day before the Budget | 1 February |
| Binding? | No | Yes (after Parliament passes it) |

🧠 **Active Recall:** Who guides the preparation of the Economic Survey? Is the Survey constitutionally mandated?

## 3. The Budget-Making Process — Step by Step

Think of it as a relay race with five laps:

**Lap 1 — Preparation (September–January):** The Budget Division of the **Department of Economic Affairs (Ministry of Finance)** issues circulars to all ministries; ministries send estimates; the Finance Minister holds pre-Budget consultations with states, industry, farmers, economists.

**Lap 2 — The Halwa Ceremony:** About a week before the Budget, a traditional **halwa ceremony** is held in the Finance Ministry (North Block). After it, officials involved in printing/preparation enter a **"lock-in"** — they stay isolated in the basement to maintain secrecy until the Budget is presented.

**Lap 3 — Presentation (1 February):** The Finance Minister presents the Budget in the Lok Sabha with the **Budget Speech** (in two parts: Part A — policies and schemes; Part B — tax proposals). No discussion happens on the day of presentation.

**Lap 4 — Discussion and Voting:**
- **General Discussion** on the Budget as a whole (both Houses).
- Lok Sabha then examines **Demands for Grants** ministry-wise. Only Lok Sabha votes on these; Rajya Sabha can only discuss. (Note: the Annual Financial Statement itself is not a Money Bill — it is the **Appropriation Bill and the Finance Bill**, which follow it, that are Money Bills under Article 110, so the Rajya Sabha's role over them is limited.)
- **Guillotine:** On the last day of the voting period, the Speaker puts all remaining demands to vote at once — this is called the "guillotine."

**Lap 5 — Passing the Bills:**
- **Appropriation Bill** — authorises withdrawal of money from the **Consolidated Fund of India** (Article 114). Without it, not a rupee can be spent.
- **Finance Bill** — gives legal effect to tax proposals (Article 265: no tax without authority of law).
- The Budget process is complete only when both Bills are passed and receive the President's assent.

🧠 **Active Recall:** What is the "guillotine" in parliamentary terms? Which Bill authorises expenditure from the Consolidated Fund?

## 4. The Three Funds of the Government of India

Examiners love this trio:

1. **Consolidated Fund of India (Article 266(1))** — all revenues, loans raised, and recoveries. No withdrawal without parliamentary approval. The largest fund.
2. **Contingency Fund of India (Article 267)** — the government's "emergency wallet," held at the disposal of the **President**, for unforeseen expenditure pending parliamentary approval. The corpus was raised from ₹500 crore to **₹30,000 crore in 2021**.
3. **Public Account of India (Article 266(2))** — money where the government acts as a banker (provident funds, small savings, deposits). **No parliamentary approval needed** for withdrawals.

**Mnemonic 🪄 — "CCP: Consolidated needs Consent, Public doesn't":**
- **C**onsolidated → Parliament's **C**onsent needed.
- **C**ontingency → President's purse.
- **P**ublic → **P**ermission-free.

## 5. The Deficit Family — Know Each Member

This is the highest-yield concept cluster of the chapter.

- **Budget Deficit** = Total Expenditure − Total Receipts (rarely asked now, but know it).
- **Revenue Deficit** = Revenue Expenditure − Revenue Receipts. Shows the government is borrowing for day-to-day expenses.
- **Fiscal Deficit** = Total Expenditure − Total Receipts (excluding borrowings). **It equals the government's total borrowing requirement** — the most watched number.
- **Primary Deficit** = Fiscal Deficit − Interest Payments. Shows the deficit of the *current* year's policies, removing the burden of past debt.
- **Effective Revenue Deficit** = Revenue Deficit − Grants for creation of capital assets (introduced in Budget 2011–12).

**Mnemonic 🪄 — "Father Pays Interest":**
**P**rimary Deficit = **F**iscal Deficit − **I**nterest. (P = F − I)

**Mnemonic 🪄 — "Fiscal = Full Borrowing":** Fiscal deficit is the *full* borrowing the government needs.

🧠 **Active Recall:** If Fiscal Deficit is ₹16 lakh crore and interest payments are ₹10 lakh crore, what is the Primary Deficit? (₹6 lakh crore.)

## 6. Types of Budgets and Special Vocabulary

- **Interim Budget:** Presented in an election year by the outgoing government; covers expenses until the new government presents a full Budget. (E.g., February 2024 — an Interim Budget; the full Budget followed in July 2024.)
- **Vote-on-Account:** A special provision (Article 116) allowing the government to withdraw money for essential expenditure (usually 2 months) until the full Budget is passed. It deals only with **expenditure**, not taxation — a classic trap.
- **Outcome Budget:** Links outlays to measurable outputs/outcomes (introduced 2005–06).
- **Gender Budget:** Highlights allocations for women (first introduced 2005–06).
- **Zero-Based Budgeting:** Every expense must be justified from zero each year, not incremented from last year.
- **Performance Budgeting:** Links funds to results/performance (first introduced in India in 1969–70, on the Administrative Reforms Commission's recommendation).

**Charged Expenditure:** Spending **not voted** by Parliament (discussed but not voted) — e.g., salaries of the President, judges of the Supreme Court and High Courts, CAG, Speaker/Deputy Speaker; interest on debt. **Non-votable = Charged.**

## 7. Historic Budgets — The Hall of Fame

- **First Budget of India:** 1860, by **James Wilson** (British India's first Finance Member; he also introduced income tax in India).
- **First Budget of Independent India:** 26 November 1947, by **R. K. Shanmukham Chetty** (first Finance Minister). Famous for his line: "I rise to present the first Budget of a free and independent India."
- **Most Budgets presented:** **Morarji Desai** — 10 Budgets (a record).
- **Longest Budget speech:** Nirmala Sitharaman, 2020 (about 2 hours 40 minutes).
- **1991 "Epochal Budget":** Presented by **Manmohan Singh** (PM: P. V. Narasimha Rao) — launched Liberalisation, Privatisation, Globalisation (LPG reforms).
- **1997 "Dream Budget":** P. Chidambaram — slashed tax rates.
- **2017:** Budget date advanced to 1 February; Railway Budget merged.
- **2019:** *Bahi-khata* (red cloth ledger) replaced the briefcase.
- **2021:** First fully **paperless (digital) Budget**.

**Mnemonic 🪄 — "Wilson 1860, Chetty 1947, Morarji Ten, Manmohan Ninety-One":** Chant it as a rhythm — it covers the four most-asked history facts.

🧠 **Active Recall:** Who presented the maximum number of Union Budgets? What was special about the 1991 Budget?

## 8. FRBM Act — The Fiscal Discipline Law

The **Fiscal Responsibility and Budget Management (FRBM) Act, 2003** (effective 2004) obliges the government to:

- Reduce fiscal and revenue deficits to set targets,
- Present four statements with the Budget: **Macro-Economic Framework Statement, Medium-Term Fiscal Policy Statement, Fiscal Policy Strategy Statement,** and **Medium-Term Expenditure Framework Statement**,
- Limit government guarantees and borrowing.

The FRBM framework was amended in 2018 on the recommendations of the **N. K. Singh Committee**, shifting focus to a **debt-to-GDP anchor** (central government debt target of 40% of GDP; general government 60%).

## 9. 🔗 Static-Dynamic Link

- **GST and the Budget:** Since GST (1 July 2017) subsumed most indirect taxes, the Budget's indirect-tax portion shrank — customs duties remain the main indirect-tax lever in the Budget speech. This is why post-2017 Budget questions focus more on **direct taxes and allocations**.
- **Interim Budget 2024 → Full Budget 2024:** The February 2024 exercise was an Interim Budget; the full Budget for 2024–25 was presented in July 2024 after the general elections. So **two Budgets were presented in 2024** — one interim (February) and one full (July) — a fact examiners can test directly.
- **Economic Survey 2023–24** was tabled in July 2024 (with the full Budget), not January — a one-off scheduling twist examiners can exploit.

## ⚠️ Caution: Examiner's Trap

**Trap 1 — "लगभग सही कथन" (Almost-True Statement):**
*"The Economic Survey is presented under Article 112 of the Constitution."* — 90% of students nod along because Article 112 is strongly linked to "Budget day." But Article 112 covers only the **Annual Financial Statement (Budget)**; the Survey has **no constitutional basis**. One swapped document name kills the statement.

**Trap 2 — "नकारात्मक वाक्यांश" (Hidden Negative):**
*"Which of the following is NOT a part of charged expenditure?"* — Students rush and pick a charged item (e.g., CAG's salary) as the answer to "which IS charged." Read the NOT twice. Salaries of **Ministers** are voted; salaries of the **President, SC/HC judges, CAG** are charged.

**Trap 3 — "कालानुक्रमिक भ्रम" (Chronological Confusion):**
*"The Railway Budget was merged with the General Budget in 2016."* — The *decision* and announcements happened in 2016, but the **first merged Budget was presented on 1 February 2017**. Similarly: Budget date shifted to 1 February in **2017**, not 2016. Examiners exploit the one-year gap between decision and implementation.

**Trap 4 — "समान नाम/वर्ष" (Similar Names):**
**Appropriation Bill vs Finance Bill**: Appropriation = spending authority (Article 114); Finance = taxation authority (Article 265 link). Also **Revenue Deficit vs Fiscal Deficit**: fiscal includes capital items; revenue does not.

**Trap 5 — "बजट खुद मनी बिल है" (The Budget Itself Is a Money Bill):**
*"The Union Budget is a Money Bill under Article 110."* — Wrong. The Budget (Annual Financial Statement) is a statement of estimates, not a Bill at all. It is the **Appropriation Bill** and the **Finance Bill** — the two Bills that follow the Budget — that are Money Bills under Article 110. Examiners swap "Budget" for "Finance Bill" to catch the careless reader.
