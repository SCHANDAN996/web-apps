# 🪄 Memory Hooks & Short Tricks — Budget & Economic Survey

---

**Box 1 — Budget vs Economic Survey (Who comes first?)**
> **"SURVEY the land, then BUILD the budget."**
> Economic Survey comes FIRST (usually 31 January), Union Budget SECOND (1 February). You survey the economy's health before writing its prescription. If a question asks the order, remember: **Doctor checks patient (Survey) → writes medicine (Budget).**

---

**Box 2 — Budget Date: 1 February**
> **"FM's New Year party is one month late."**
> Since 2017, the Budget is presented on **1 February** (earlier it was the last working day of February). Trick: **"Feb 1 = Full One"** — the full budget on the 1st.

---

**Box 3 — Article 112 = Annual Financial Statement**
> **"112 is the emergency number — and the Budget is the nation's financial emergency plan!"**
> The word "Budget" is NOT in the Constitution; Article **112** calls it the **Annual Financial Statement**. Trap alert: examiners ask "Which article mentions the Budget?" — answer: none by that name; it's Article 112.

---

**Box 4 — Three Funds of India (Articles 266, 267)**
> **"CON-PA-CA: Consolidated, Public, Contingency — the government's three wallets."**
> - **Con**solidated Fund (Art. 266(1)) — main wallet, needs Parliament's approval to spend.
> - **P**ublic **A**ccount (Art. 266(2)) — government as banker (PF, small savings); NO parliamentary approval needed.
> - **C**ontingency Fund (Art. 267) — emergency wallet, at the President's disposal; its corpus was raised from ₹500 crore to **₹30,000 crore** by a 2021 amendment.
> Trick: **"266 has twins (Consolidated + Public), 267 is the lone ranger (Contingency)."**

---

**Box 5 — FRBM Act Year: 2003**
> **"In 2003, the government went on a Fiscal Diet."**
> **F**iscal **R**esponsibility and **B**udget **M**anagement Act = **2003** (targets from 2004). Trap: don't confuse with the Finance Commission (constitutional, Art. 280) — FRBM is a **statute**, not constitutional.

---

**Box 6 — Fiscal Deficit Formula**
> **"Fiscal Deficit = Total Expenditure − Total Receipts EXCEPT borrowings."**
> Hook: **"How much did you borrow to run the house?"** — that gap IS the fiscal deficit. Remember the ladder: **Revenue Deficit < Fiscal Deficit (usually)**, and **Primary Deficit = Fiscal Deficit − Interest Payments** ("Primary = Fiscal minus the Price of past sins/interest").

---

**Box 7 — Who presents the Budget?**
> **"The FM holds the briefcase, but the President's permission opens it."**
> Budget is presented by the **Finance Minister** in Lok Sabha with the **President's recommendation** (Art. 113 for demands for grants). First Budget of independent India: **R. K. Shanmukham Chetty (1947)** — hook: **"Chetty Cut the First Cheque."**

---

**Box 8 — Economic Survey: Who prepares it?**
> The Survey is prepared by the **Chief Economic Adviser's** team in the **Department of Economic Affairs, Ministry of Finance** — NOT by NITI Aayog, NOT by RBI. Trap: examiners love offering "NITI Aayog" as the answer. Hook: **"DEA delivers the Survey; FM delivers the Budget."**

---

**Box 9 — Railway Budget merger: 2017**
> **"In 2017, the Railway train merged into the Budget highway."**
> Railway Budget (separate since **1924**, on the **Acworth Committee's** recommendation) was merged with the General Budget from **2017–18**. Hook: **"92 years of separate tracks, one junction in 2017."**

---

**Box 10 — Vote on Account vs Interim Budget**
> **"Vote on Account = pocket money; Interim Budget = full wallet; Full Budget = the new salary."**
> - **Vote on Account**: only expenditure, only ~2 months, no tax changes.
> - **Interim Budget**: income + expenditure for the transition period, but no major policy announcements.
> - Election-year trick: **"In election season, the government only withdraws pocket money."**

---

**Box 11 — Cut Motions (Demands for Grants)**
> **"Policy says NO, Economy says LESS, Token says ₹100."**
> - **Policy Cut** — reduce demand to **₹1** (total disapproval).
> - **Economy Cut** — reduce by a **specific amount** (spend less).
> - **Token Cut** — reduce by **₹100** (vent a grievance).
> Hook: **"1 rupee = I reject you; 100 rupees = I'm just complaining."**

---

**Box 12 — Guillotine**
> **"When time runs out, the GUILLOTINE falls — all remaining demands passed without discussion."**
> On the last allotted day, pending Demands for Grants are put to vote together. Hook: **"Guillotine chops debate, not the budget."**

---

**Box 13 — Halwa Ceremony**
> **"Before the bitter numbers, the sweet halwa."**
> The **Halwa Ceremony** marks the start of Budget document printing at North Block; after it, officials stay in **lock-in** (quarantine) till presentation. Hook: **"Halwa first, then house arrest."**

---

**Box 14 — Finance Bill vs Money Bill (Art. 110)**
> **"Money talks only in the Lok Sabha."**
> - **Money Bill (Art. 110)**: deals ONLY with taxes, borrowing, and money in/out of the Consolidated Fund. The **Speaker certifies** whether a bill is a Money Bill; Rajya Sabha can only **suggest** changes within **14 days** — Lok Sabha may accept or reject them.
> - **Finance Bill**: the bill that gives effect to the Budget's financial (tax) proposals. A Finance Bill that contains **only** Article 110 matters is certified as a Money Bill; if it carries other provisions too, it is treated as an ordinary financial bill and Rajya Sabha gets full powers over it.
> Trap: "The entire Budget is one Money Bill" — wrong. The Budget process includes Demands for Grants, the Appropriation Bill AND the Finance Bill; only the Finance Bill (if it sticks to Art. 110 matters) is certified as a Money Bill.

---

**Box 15 — Appropriation Bill order**
> **"First DEMAND, then APPROPRIATE, then TAX (Finance Bill)."**
> Sequence: Budget speech → General Discussion → **Demands for Grants** (Lok Sabha only) → **Appropriation Bill** (money out of Consolidated Fund) → **Finance Bill** (taxes). Hook: **"Ask (Demand) → Take (Appropriate) → Collect (Finance)."**

---

## ⏭️ Skip Strategy — When to Skip a Question

**Tip 1 — The "exact figure" trap.**
If a question asks for a precise number you haven't verified (e.g., "What was the fiscal deficit target as % of GDP in Budget 2024-25?" or "How many pages was the Economic Survey?"), and you can't recall it confidently — **skip immediately**. These questions are pure recall with no elimination logic; guessing between 4.9%, 5.1%, 5.6%, 5.9% is a coin toss with negative marking.

**Tip 2 — The "current-year scheme allocation" trap.**
Questions like "How much was allocated to Scheme X in the latest Budget?" change every year and are the most commonly outdated facts in any book. If the figure isn't one you revised in the last 30 days, **skip** — examiners deliberately mix the previous year's allocation as a wrong option.

**Tip 3 — The "two-statement both-plausible" trap.**
Statement-based questions on FRBM targets, debt-to-GDP glide paths, or Survey themes where BOTH statements sound correct (e.g., "Statement I: FRBM Act was enacted in 2003. Statement II: It is a constitutional amendment.") — if you can verify only one statement and the second is a maybe, **skip rather than gamble**. One wrong statement kills the whole question; attempt only when you can decisively confirm or reject each statement.
