Questions 1–20: Easy | Questions 21–25: Medium

1. The Union Budget of India is presented under which Article of the Constitution?
(a) Article 110 (b) Article 114 (c) Article 112 (d) Article 116
Answer: (c)
Solution: Article 110 defines a Money Bill, which is the tempting confusion here, but the Budget (Annual Financial Statement) is mandated by Article 112. Article 114 covers Appropriation Bills and Article 116 covers votes on account. So the answer is Article 112.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Indian Constitution at Work

2. The Economic Survey of India is presented in Parliament by whom?
(a) The President (b) The Finance Minister (c) The RBI Governor (d) The Prime Minister
Answer: (b)
Solution: The RBI Governor presents monetary policy, not the Survey, which is a common mix-up. The Economic Survey is prepared by the Department of Economic Affairs under the Chief Economic Adviser and tabled by the Finance Minister. So the answer is the Finance Minister.
Source: Ministry of Finance, Government of India

3. The Economic Survey is usually presented in Parliament on which day relative to the Union Budget?
(a) One week after (b) On the same day (c) One day before the Budget (d) One month after
Answer: (c)
Solution: Many assume the Survey comes after the Budget because it reviews the economy, but it actually reviews the previous year and is tabled one day before the Budget (31 January, with the Budget on 1 February). So the answer is one day before the Budget.
Source: Ministry of Finance, Government of India

4. The Union Budget is traditionally presented on which date since 2017?
(a) 1 February (b) 28 February (c) 31 January (d) 15 March
Answer: (a)
Solution: 28 February was the traditional date for decades, which is the trap; since 2017 the Budget has been presented on 1 February to allow earlier implementation from 1 April. So the answer is 1 February.
Source: Ministry of Finance, Government of India

5. Fiscal deficit is best defined as:
(a) Total revenue minus total expenditure (b) Interest payments minus subsidies (c) Revenue expenditure minus revenue receipts (d) Total expenditure minus total receipts excluding borrowings
Answer: (d)
Solution: Option (c) describes revenue deficit, the classic confusion. Fiscal deficit equals total expenditure minus total receipts excluding borrowings, and it indicates the government's total borrowing requirement. So the answer is total expenditure minus total receipts excluding borrowings.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Introductory Macroeconomics

6. The first Union Budget of independent India was presented by:
(a) R. K. Shanmukham Chetty (b) John Mathai (c) Morarji Desai (d) C. D. Deshmukh
Answer: (a)
Solution: John Mathai is the trap because he presented the 1949–50 Budget and was the first full-term Finance Minister, but the first Budget of independent India (1947–48) was presented by R. K. Shanmukham Chetty. So the answer is R. K. Shanmukham Chetty.
Source: Ministry of Finance, Government of India

7. A Money Bill can be introduced only in:
(a) Rajya Sabha (b) Lok Sabha (c) Either House (d) A joint sitting
Answer: (b)
Solution: A joint sitting is impossible for Money Bills, which is a frequent trap; under Article 109 a Money Bill can be introduced only in the Lok Sabha, and the Rajya Sabha can only make recommendations within 14 days. So the answer is Lok Sabha.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Indian Constitution at Work

8. Revenue deficit means:
(a) Capital expenditure exceeds capital receipts (b) Fiscal deficit minus interest payments (c) Revenue expenditure exceeds revenue receipts (d) Total expenditure exceeds total receipts
Answer: (c)
Solution: Option (b) defines primary deficit, the tempting confusion. Revenue deficit is simply the excess of revenue expenditure over revenue receipts, showing the government is borrowing for consumption. So the answer is revenue expenditure exceeds revenue receipts.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Introductory Macroeconomics

9. Primary deficit is calculated as:
(a) Fiscal deficit plus interest payments (b) Revenue deficit minus interest payments (c) Fiscal deficit minus interest payments (d) Total expenditure minus revenue receipts
Answer: (c)
Solution: Adding interest payments to the fiscal deficit is the common error; primary deficit actually removes interest payments from the fiscal deficit to show the current year's borrowing need excluding past debt servicing. So the answer is fiscal deficit minus interest payments.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Introductory Macroeconomics

10. The Economic Survey is prepared under the guidance of:
(a) The NITI Aayog Vice-Chairman (b) The Finance Secretary (c) The RBI Deputy Governor (d) The Chief Economic Adviser
Answer: (d)
Solution: The Finance Secretary sounds plausible but heads the Finance Ministry's administration, not the Survey. The Economic Survey is prepared by the Economic Division of the Department of Economic Affairs under the Chief Economic Adviser. So the answer is the Chief Economic Adviser.
Source: Ministry of Finance, Government of India

11. Which of the following is a capital receipt for the government?
(a) Income tax (b) GST collection (c) Dividends from PSUs (d) Disinvestment proceeds
Answer: (d)
Solution: Dividends from PSUs feel like capital income but are non-tax revenue receipts, which is the trap. Disinvestment reduces government assets and is therefore a capital receipt, while taxes and dividends are revenue receipts. So the answer is disinvestment proceeds.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Introductory Macroeconomics

12. The FRBM Act was enacted in which year?
(a) 1991 (b) 2003 (c) 2000 (d) 2014
Answer: (b)
Solution: 1991 is tempting because of the reforms era, but the Fiscal Responsibility and Budget Management Act was enacted in 2003 to institutionalise fiscal discipline and deficit targets. So the answer is 2003.
Source: Ministry of Finance, Government of India

13. The 'Vote on Account' provision is found in which Article?
(a) Article 112 (b) Article 114 (c) Article 116 (d) Article 123
Answer: (c)
Solution: Article 112 relates to the Annual Financial Statement itself, the usual confusion. Article 116 empowers the Lok Sabha to make grants in advance (vote on account) pending completion of the budget process. So the answer is Article 116.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Indian Constitution at Work

14. Which tax was subsumed under the Goods and Services Tax introduced in 2017?
(a) Customs duty (b) Central Excise duty (c) Income tax (d) Securities Transaction Tax
Answer: (b)
Solution: Customs duty is the trap because it is an indirect tax, but it was not subsumed under GST. Central Excise duty, Service Tax, VAT and several other indirect taxes were subsumed; direct taxes like income tax were untouched. So the answer is Central Excise duty.
Source: Ministry of Finance, Government of India

15. The 'Interim Budget' is presented when:
(a) The fiscal deficit crosses the target (b) General elections are due and a new government will take office (c) The Budget is rejected by Rajya Sabha (d) GDP growth falls below 5 percent
Answer: (b)
Solution: A Budget cannot be rejected by the Rajya Sabha since it is a Money Bill, which makes that option a trap. An Interim Budget is presented in an election year so the outgoing government can meet expenses until the new government presents a full Budget. So the answer is when general elections are due.
Source: Ministry of Finance, Government of India

16. Which committee's recommendations led to the FRBM Act?
(a) Narasimham Committee (b) Vijay Kelkar Task Force on FRBM (c) Kelkar Committee (d) Sarkaria Commission
Answer: (b)
Solution: The Narasimham Committee dealt with banking sector reforms, a frequent mix-up. The FRBM framework drew on the recommendations of the Vijay Kelkar Task Force set up in 2002. So the answer is the Vijay Kelkar Task Force on FRBM.
Source: Ministry of Finance, Government of India

17. Effective capital expenditure is defined as:
(a) Capital expenditure minus grants for capital assets (b) Fiscal deficit minus revenue deficit (c) Revenue expenditure plus capital expenditure (d) Capital expenditure plus grants-in-aid for creation of capital assets
Answer: (d)
Solution: Subtracting grants is the tempting error; effective capital expenditure adds grants-in-aid for creation of capital assets to capital expenditure, since such grants also build assets though booked as revenue expenditure. So the answer is capital expenditure plus grants-in-aid for creation of capital assets.
Source: Ministry of Finance, Government of India

18. The 'Halwa Ceremony' before the Budget marks:
(a) The start of the Budget printing and lock-in of officials (b) The President's assent to the Budget (c) The tabling of the Economic Survey (d) The GST Council meeting
Answer: (a)
Solution: The ceremony is often confused with the Survey's tabling, but it is held in North Block to mark the commencement of Budget document printing, after which officials involved stay in a lock-in to maintain secrecy. So the answer is the start of the Budget printing and lock-in of officials.
Source: Ministry of Finance, Government of India

19. Which of the following is NOT a component of revenue expenditure?
(a) Loans given to states (b) Subsidies (c) Salaries and pensions (d) Interest payments
Answer: (a)
Solution: Interest payments feel like capital items but are revenue expenditure, which is the trap. Loans given to states create assets (recoverable) and are capital expenditure, while interest, subsidies and salaries are revenue expenditure. So the answer is loans given to states.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Introductory Macroeconomics

20. The Budget documents are presented in Parliament by the Finance Minister under the name:
(a) Consolidated Fund Statement (b) Annual Financial Statement (c) Public Account Report (d) Contingency Statement
Answer: (b)
Solution: The Consolidated Fund is where receipts are credited, not the Budget's name, which confuses many. Article 112 calls the Budget the Annual Financial Statement, showing estimated receipts and expenditure for the year. So the answer is Annual Financial Statement.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Indian Constitution at Work

21. Consider the following statements about the Economic Survey:
1. It is a constitutional document mandated by Article 112.
2. Since 2021–22 it has been presented in a single volume.
3. It reviews the performance of the economy in the preceding financial year.
Which of the statements given above is/are correct?
(a) 2 and 3 only (b) 1 and 2 only (c) 1 and 3 only (d) 1, 2 and 3
Answer: (a)
Solution: Statement 1 is the trap: Article 112 mandates the Annual Financial Statement (Budget), not the Survey, which is only an executive document with no constitutional backing. Statements 2 and 3 are correct — the Survey moved to a single-volume format from 2021–22 and reviews the preceding year's economy. So the answer is 2 and 3 only.
Source: Ministry of Finance, Government of India

22. Arrange the following stages of the Union Budget in Parliament in correct chronological order:
1. Voting on Demands for Grants
2. General Discussion
3. Passing of the Appropriation Bill
4. Passing of the Finance Bill
(a) 2-1-4-3 (b) 1-2-3-4 (c) 2-1-3-4 (d) 1-2-4-3
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is placing the Finance Bill before the Appropriation Bill; expenditure must first be authorised by the Appropriation Bill before taxation measures in the Finance Bill are passed. The correct sequence is general discussion, voting on demands, Appropriation Bill, then Finance Bill. So the answer is 2-1-3-4.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Indian Constitution at Work

23. Consider the following statements:
1. Fiscal deficit can never exceed total government borrowings in a year.
2. A high revenue deficit implies borrowing is being used for asset creation.
3. Monetised deficit refers to the RBI printing currency to finance the deficit.
Which of the statements given above is/are correct?
(a) 3 only (b) 1 only (c) 1 and 3 only (d) 2 and 3 only
Answer: (a)
Solution: Statement 2 is the tempting trap — a high revenue deficit actually means borrowing funds consumption, not asset creation. Statement 1 is wrong because fiscal deficit equals total borrowings, not a ceiling on them. Only statement 3 correctly describes monetised deficit. So the answer is 3 only.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Introductory Macroeconomics

24. Which of the following pairs is correctly matched?
(a) Article 112 — Appropriation Bill (b) Article 266 — Finance Bill (c) Article 116 — Annual Financial Statement (d) Article 110 — Money Bill definition
Answer: (d)
Solution: The almost-correct pairings are the trap: Article 112 is the Annual Financial Statement, Article 116 is votes on account, and Article 266 deals with the Consolidated Fund and Public Account, not the Finance Bill. Only Article 110 correctly matches the definition of a Money Bill. So the answer is Article 110 — Money Bill definition.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Indian Constitution at Work

25. Consider the following statements about the Budget presentation:
1. The Railway Budget was merged with the Union Budget from 2017–18.
2. The Budget date was advanced to 1 February from 2017.
3. The merger of the Railway Budget ended a practice that began in 1924.
Which of the statements given above is/are correct?
(a) 1 and 2 only (b) 2 and 3 only (c) 1 and 3 only (d) 1, 2 and 3
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is to doubt statement 3, but the separate Railway Budget indeed began in 1924 on the Acworth Committee's recommendation and ended with the 2017–18 merger. All three statements are correct. So the answer is 1, 2 and 3.
Source: Ministry of Finance, Government of India
