# 📊 PYQ Analysis — Budget & Economic Survey

## 1. How Exams Ask Questions from This Chapter

Budget and Economic Survey questions appear across UPSC, SSC (CGL/CHSL), Banking (IBPS/SBI), Railway (NTPC) and State PCS exams, but the **style differs sharply by exam**:

- **UPSC / State PCS:** Conceptual and statement-based questions — "Which of the following statements about the Fiscal Responsibility and Budget Management (FRBM) Act is/are correct?" They test *understanding* of deficit types, tax structure, and budget documents, not just numbers.
- **SSC / Railway:** Direct factual recall — "Who presents the Union Budget?", "Under which Article is the Annual Financial Statement presented?", "When was the Budget date shifted to 1 February?"
- **Banking (IBPS/SBI/RRB):** Current-year figures dominate — fiscal deficit target, capital expenditure, new schemes announced, tax slabs. These exams ask the *latest* Budget's numbers, so this chapter must be revised every February.

**Recurring sub-topics (in words, not invented counts):**
- Constitutional provisions: Article 112 (Annual Financial Statement), Article 110 (Money Bill), Article 266 (Consolidated Fund)
- Types of deficits: fiscal, revenue, primary, effective revenue deficit — and their formulas
- FRBM Act targets and escape clauses
- Budget documents: Demands for Grants, Appropriation Bill, Finance Bill, Vote on Account vs Interim Budget
- Direct vs indirect taxes; GST's place in the Budget
- Economic Survey: who prepares it, when it is tabled, its two-volume structure (historically), key themes
- History: first Budget of independent India (R.K. Shanmukham Chetty, 1947), first Budget of British India (James Wilson, 1860), Budget date shift (2017), merger of Railway Budget (2017), paperless Budget (2021)

## 2. The Examiner's Traps (जाल-प्रकार)

**Trap 1 — Almost Correct Statement:** "The Economic Survey is presented by the Finance Minister." *Almost* right — it is prepared by the Chief Economic Adviser's team and tabled by the Finance Minister; the *authorship* belongs to the CEA. Exams swap "presents/tables/prepares" to catch skimmers.

**Trap 2 — Chronological Confusion:** "The Railway Budget was merged with the Union Budget in 2016." The merger took effect from Budget **2017–18** (announced 2016). Similarly: Budget date shifted to 1 February from **2017**; first Budget 1947 vs first *British India* Budget 1860.

**Trap 3 — Negative Phrasing:** "Which of the following is **NOT** a type of budget deficit?" Options include fiscal, revenue, primary — and a decoy like "trade deficit" (a real concept, but not a *budget* deficit). Underline NOT before reading options.

**Trap 4 — Similar-Sounding Names:** Fiscal deficit vs revenue deficit vs primary deficit. Primary deficit = fiscal deficit **minus interest payments** — exams swap the two formulas.

**Trap 5 — Article Swapping:** Article 112 (Annual Financial Statement) vs Article 110 (Money Bill) vs Article 266 (Consolidated Fund). One-digit changes create wrong options.

**Trap 6 — Vote on Account vs Interim Budget:** An Interim Budget (election year) can alter taxes; a Vote on Account only authorises expenditure. Exams blur the two.

## 3. The 15/45-Second Rule

- **15 seconds:** Direct recall questions — Article numbers, who presented the first Budget, deficit formulas. If you know it, mark it; never re-read.
- **45 seconds:** Statement-based or "which is NOT correct" questions. Budget for two passes: first pass eliminates obvious wrongs, second pass compares the two survivors word-by-word against the trap types above.
- **Skip signal:** A question asking a *specific current-year figure* (e.g., exact fiscal deficit % target) that you haven't revised this year — mark and move; guessing between 5.1% and 5.9% is a coin toss that costs negative marks.

## 4. Ten High-Yield PYQ-Style Questions with Solutions

**Q1.** The Annual Financial Statement (Union Budget) is presented under which Article of the Constitution?
(a) Article 110 (b) Article 112 (c) Article 266 (d) Article 280
**Answer:** (b)
**Solution:** Article 112 mandates the Annual Financial Statement. Article 110 defines Money Bills, Article 266 covers the Consolidated Fund, Article 280 the Finance Commission — the classic article-swap trap.
**Trap type:** Article Swapping | **Source:** PYQ-style

**Q2.** Who presented the first Budget of independent India?
(a) Jawaharlal Nehru (b) John Mathai (c) R.K. Shanmukham Chetty (d) Morarji Desai
**Answer:** (c)
**Solution:** Chetty presented it on 26 November 1947. John Mathai presented the next two — a near-name trap. James Wilson presented the first Budget of British India (1860).
**Trap type:** Similar-Sounding Names | **Source:** PYQ-style

**Q3.** Primary deficit is calculated as:
(a) Fiscal deficit minus interest payments
(b) Total expenditure minus total receipts
(c) Fiscal deficit minus revenue receipts
(d) Revenue deficit minus interest payments
**Answer:** (a)
**Solution:** Primary deficit = Fiscal deficit − Interest payments. Option (b) describes fiscal deficit loosely — the almost-correct trap.
**Trap type:** Almost Correct | **Source:** NCERT Class 12 Economics (Introductory Macroeconomics)

**Q4.** From which year was the Union Budget presented on 1 February instead of the last working day of February?
(a) 2015 (b) 2016 (c) 2017 (d) 2019
**Answer:** (c)
**Solution:** The shift took effect from Budget 2017–18, the same year the Railway Budget was merged. Exams offer 2016 (the announcement year) as the decoy.
**Trap type:** Chronological Confusion | **Source:** PYQ-style

**Q5.** Which of the following is NOT a type of budget deficit?
(a) Fiscal deficit (b) Revenue deficit (c) Trade deficit (d) Primary deficit
**Answer:** (c)
**Solution:** Trade deficit (imports > exports) is a balance-of-payments concept, not a budget deficit. The negative phrasing makes hasty readers pick a real deficit type.
**Trap type:** Negative Phrasing | **Source:** NCERT Class 12 Economics

**Q6.** The Economic Survey of India is prepared under the guidance of:
(a) The Finance Minister
(b) The RBI Governor
(c) The Chief Economic Adviser
(d) The NITI Aayog Vice-Chairman
**Answer:** (c)
**Solution:** The CEA's team in the Department of Economic Affairs prepares it; the Finance Minister only *tables* it in Parliament. The "presents vs prepares" swap is the trap.
**Trap type:** Almost Correct | **Source:** PYQ-style

**Q7.** The FRBM Act, 2003 primarily deals with:
(a) Monetary policy targets
(b) Fiscal deficit and debt management targets
(c) Foreign exchange reserves
(d) Disinvestment of PSUs
**Answer:** (b)
**Solution:** FRBM sets targets for fiscal deficit, revenue deficit and government debt. Monetary policy (option a) is RBI's domain — a domain-confusion trap.
**Trap type:** Almost Correct | **Source:** PYQ-style

**Q8.** A Vote on Account differs from an Interim Budget because a Vote on Account:
(a) Can introduce new tax proposals
(b) Only authorises withdrawal of funds for expenditure for a short period
(c) Is presented only by the President
(d) Covers the full financial year
**Answer:** (b)
**Solution:** A Vote on Account is merely Parliament's permission to draw from the Consolidated Fund for a few months; it cannot change taxes. An Interim Budget can. Option (a) reverses the truth.
**Trap type:** Similar-Sounding Names | **Source:** PYQ-style

**Q9.** The Railway Budget was merged with the Union Budget from which financial year?
(a) 2015–16 (b) 2016–17 (c) 2017–18 (d) 2019–20
**Answer:** (c)
**Solution:** The merger (recommended by the Bibek Debroy Committee) took effect from Budget 2017–18, ending a 92-year separate Railway Budget tradition. 2016–17 is the announcement-year decoy.
**Trap type:** Chronological Confusion | **Source:** PYQ-style

**Q10.** Which document contains the estimates of expenditure of each ministry requiring Parliament's vote?
(a) Finance Bill (b) Demands for Grants (c) Appropriation Bill (d) Receipts Budget
**Answer:** (b)
**Solution:** Demands for Grants are ministry-wise expenditure estimates voted by the Lok Sabha. The Appropriation Bill comes *after* voting — the sequence trap. The Finance Bill carries tax proposals.
**Trap type:** Chronological Confusion | **Source:** PYQ-style

## 5. Revision Priorities Before the Exam

1. Memorise the four deficit formulas and Article 110/112/266 cold — these are pure 15-second marks.
2. Fix the timeline: 1860 (Wilson) → 1947 (Chetty) → 2003 (FRBM) → 2017 (1 Feb + Railway merger) → 2021 (paperless).
3. For Banking exams, revise the **latest** Budget's headline numbers in the last week before the exam — never trust memory older than the current February.
