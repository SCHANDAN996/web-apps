101. Which of the following is the correct order of electromagnetic waves in increasing wavelength?
(a) Gamma rays < Ultraviolet < X-rays < Visible < Microwaves < Infrared < Radio waves (b) Radio waves < Microwaves < Infrared < Visible < Ultraviolet < X-rays < Gamma rays (c) X-rays < Gamma rays < Ultraviolet < Visible < Infrared < Radio waves < Microwaves (d) Gamma rays < X-rays < Ultraviolet < Visible < Infrared < Microwaves < Radio waves
Answer: (d)
Solution: The tempting option reverses the entire spectrum — it lists decreasing wavelength, not increasing. Gamma rays have the shortest wavelength and radio waves the longest, so the answer is Gamma rays < X-rays < Ultraviolet < Visible < Infrared < Microwaves < Radio waves.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Physics

102. The SI unit of electric current is defined based on the force between two parallel current-carrying conductors. This unit is:
(a) Coulomb (b) Volt (c) Ampere (d) Ohm
Answer: (c)
Solution: Coulomb is the unit of charge, volt of potential difference, and ohm of resistance. The ampere is the SI base unit of current, historically defined via the force between two parallel conductors, so the answer is Ampere.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science

103. Which vitamin deficiency causes night blindness?
(a) Vitamin C (b) Vitamin D (c) Vitamin A (d) Vitamin B12
Answer: (c)
Solution: Vitamin C deficiency causes scurvy and vitamin D deficiency causes rickets — both are common distractors. Vitamin A is essential for the pigment rhodopsin in the retina, so the answer is Vitamin A.
Source: NCERT Class 6 Science

104. The pH of a neutral solution at 25°C is:
(a) 0 (b) 7 (c) 14 (d) 1
Answer: (b)
Solution: Students often confuse pH 0 and 14 as "neutral" extremes, but those are strongly acidic and strongly basic respectively. At 25°C, pure water has equal hydrogen and hydroxide ion concentrations, so the answer is 7.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science

105. Which of the following diseases is caused by a virus?
(a) Tuberculosis (b) Cholera (c) Typhoid (d) Measles
Answer: (d)
Solution: Tuberculosis, cholera and typhoid are all bacterial diseases — a classic examiner trap listing three bacterial diseases with one viral one. Measles is caused by the measles virus, so the answer is Measles.

106. The phenomenon of light bending as it passes from one medium to another is called:
(a) Reflection (b) Diffraction (c) Refraction (d) Dispersion
Answer: (c)
Solution: Reflection is bouncing back of light, and dispersion is splitting into colours — both sound plausible but describe different phenomena. Bending due to change in speed between media is refraction, so the answer is Refraction.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science

107. Which gas is commonly known as laughing gas?
(a) Nitric oxide (b) Nitrous oxide (c) Nitrogen dioxide (d) Ammonia
Answer: (b)
Solution: Nitric oxide and nitrous oxide have confusingly similar names — a deliberate examiner trap. Nitrous oxide (N₂O) is laughing gas, used as an anaesthetic, so the answer is Nitrous oxide.

108. The powerhouse of the cell is:
(a) Ribosome (b) Mitochondrion (c) Nucleus (d) Golgi apparatus
Answer: (b)
Solution: The nucleus controls the cell and ribosomes make proteins, but neither produces energy. Mitochondria generate ATP through respiration, so the answer is Mitochondrion.

109. Which of the following is used as a moderator in a nuclear reactor?
(a) Uranium-235 (b) Heavy water (c) Cadmium (d) Plutonium
Answer: (b)
Solution: Cadmium is a trap — it is used in control rods to absorb neutrons, not to slow them. Heavy water (D₂O) slows down fast neutrons as a moderator, so the answer is Heavy water.

110. The chemical formula of washing soda is:
(a) NaHCO₃ (b) NaOH (c) Na₂CO₃·10H₂O (d) CaOCl₂
Answer: (c)
Solution: NaHCO₃ is baking soda — the similar name is a classic trap. Washing soda is sodium carbonate decahydrate, so the answer is Na₂CO₃·10H₂O.

111. Which blood group is called the universal donor?
(a) AB positive (b) O negative (c) AB negative (d) O positive
Answer: (b)
Solution: AB positive is the universal recipient, not donor — the two are frequently swapped by examiners. O negative blood lacks A, B and Rh antigens, so the answer is O negative.

112. The speed of sound is maximum in:
(a) Air (b) Steel (c) Vacuum (d) Water
Answer: (b)
Solution: Vacuum is a trap — sound cannot travel through vacuum at all since it needs a medium. Sound travels fastest in solids because particles are closely packed, so the answer is Steel.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Science

113. Which of the following is a non-renewable source of energy?
(a) Natural gas (b) Wind energy (c) Solar energy (d) Biogas
Answer: (a)
Solution: Biogas sounds similar to natural gas but is renewable, made from organic waste — a deliberate confusion. Natural gas is a fossil fuel formed over millions of years, so the answer is Natural gas.

114. The instrument used to measure atmospheric pressure is:
(a) Barometer (b) Thermometer (c) Ammeter (d) Hydrometer
Answer: (a)
Solution: A hydrometer measures liquid density and an ammeter measures current — both are plausible-sounding distractors. Atmospheric pressure is measured by a barometer, so the answer is Barometer.

115. Which of the following acids is present in vinegar?
(a) Acetic acid (b) Citric acid (c) Lactic acid (d) Oxalic acid
Answer: (a)
Solution: Citric acid is found in citrus fruits and lactic acid in curd — both are common food-acid traps. Vinegar is a 4–8% solution of acetic acid, so the answer is Acetic acid.

116. The process by which plants lose water vapour through stomata is called:
(a) Photosynthesis (b) Respiration (c) Guttation (d) Transpiration
Answer: (d)
Solution: Guttation is a tempting trap — it is the loss of water as liquid droplets, not vapour. Loss of water vapour through stomata is transpiration, so the answer is Transpiration.

118. The full form of DNA is:
(a) Deoxyribose Nucleic Acid (b) Deoxyribose Nitric Acid (c) Dinucleic Acid (d) Deoxyribonucleic Acid

Solution: The first option is a near-correct trap — "Deoxyribose Nucleic Acid" splits the word wrongly. The correct expansion is Deoxyribonucleic Acid, so the answer is Deoxyribonucleic Acid.

119. Which of the following is the hardest naturally occurring substance?
(a) Quartz (b) Graphite (c) Diamond (d) Corundum
Answer: (c)
Solution: Graphite is a trap — it is also pure carbon like diamond but is one of the softest minerals. Diamond's tetrahedral carbon bonding makes it the hardest natural substance, so the answer is Diamond.

120. The ozone layer is mainly found in which layer of the atmosphere?
(a) Troposphere (b) Stratosphere (c) Mesosphere (d) Thermosphere
Answer: (b)
Solution: The troposphere is a trap because weather and most atmospheric phenomena occur there, but ozone concentrated there is a pollutant. The protective ozone layer lies in the stratosphere, so the answer is Stratosphere.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Geography

121. Consider the following statements:
1. Antibiotics are effective against viral infections.
2. Penicillin was discovered by Alexander Fleming.
3. Viruses can reproduce only inside a host cell.
Which of the statements given above is/are correct?
(a) 1 and 2 only (b) 2 and 3 only (c) 1 and 3 only (d) 1, 2 and 3
Answer: (b)
Solution: Statement 1 is the trap — antibiotics work against bacteria, not viruses, which is why they are useless in common colds. Fleming discovered penicillin in 1928 and viruses are obligate intracellular parasites, so the answer is 2 and 3 only.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Biology

122. Match the following scientists with their contributions:
1. C.V. Raman — Scattering of light
2. Homi J. Bhabha — Nuclear physics
3. S. Chandrasekhar — Stellar structure limit
Which of the above are correctly matched?
(a) 1 and 2 only (b) 2 and 3 only (c) 1, 2 and 3 (d) 1 and 3 only
Answer: (c)
Solution: All three pairings are correct — the trap in such questions is assuming at least one must be wrong. Raman won the Nobel Prize for the Raman Effect, Bhabha founded India's nuclear programme, and Chandrasekhar gave the mass limit for white dwarfs, so the answer is 1, 2 and 3.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Physics

123. Arrange the following events of space exploration in chronological order:
1. Launch of Sputnik 1
2. First human in space (Yuri Gagarin)
3. First human landing on the Moon
(a) 1 – 3 – 2 (b) 2 – 1 – 3 (c) 1 – 2 – 3 (d) 3 – 2 – 1
Answer: (c)
Solution: Chronological confusion is the trap here — many assume Gagarin flew before Sputnik. Sputnik 1 was launched in 1957, Gagarin orbited Earth in 1961, and Apollo 11 landed on the Moon in 1969, so the answer is 1 – 2 – 3.
Source: PYQ-style

124. Consider the following statements about photosynthesis:
1. The light reaction occurs in the grana of chloroplasts.
2. The dark reaction requires direct sunlight to proceed.
3. Oxygen released comes from the splitting of water.
Which of the statements given above is/are correct?
(a) 1 and 2 only (b) 1, 2 and 3 (c) 2 and 3 only (d) 1 and 3 only
Answer: (d)
Solution: Statement 2 is an almost-correct trap — the dark reaction does not need light directly; it uses ATP and NADPH from the light reaction. Light reactions occur in grana and photolysis of water releases oxygen, so the answer is 1 and 3 only.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Biology

125. Consider the following statements about nuclear energy:
1. Nuclear fission is the process used in atomic power plants.
2. The Sun produces energy by nuclear fission.
3. In a chain reaction, neutrons released by fission cause further fissions.
Which of the statements given above is/are correct?
(a) 1 and 2 only (b) 2 and 3 only (c) 1 and 3 only (d) 1, 2 and 3
Answer: (c)
Solution: Statement 2 is the classic trap — the Sun produces energy by nuclear fusion of hydrogen into helium, not fission. Power plants use controlled fission sustained by neutron chain reactions, so the answer is 1 and 3 only.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Physics
