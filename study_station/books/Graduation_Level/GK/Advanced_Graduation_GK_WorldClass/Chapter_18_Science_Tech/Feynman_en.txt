# 🧒 The Feynman Corner: Science & Technology (Basic)

## The Concept That Confuses Everyone: Why Don't Antibiotics Work on Viruses?

Ask any aspirant: *"You have a viral fever. The doctor refuses to give you antibiotics. Why?"* Most students memorise the line "antibiotics don't work on viruses" — but in the exam, when the question is twisted into a statement like *"Antibiotics kill both bacteria and viruses"* or *"Viral infections can be cured by a full course of antibiotics,"* they fall straight into the trap. Let's fix this forever, the Feynman way.

---

## Step 1: Explain It Like You're 12

Imagine your body is a house.

- **A bacterium is a full, living intruder.** It has its own kitchen (cell machinery), its own walls (cell wall), and it cooks its own food and makes its own copies. It is *alive* and independent.
- **A virus is not even a proper intruder — it's just a stolen recipe card.** It has no kitchen, no walls, no life of its own. It slips into *your* kitchen (your body cells) and forces *your* machinery to cook thousands of copies of it.

Now, an **antibiotic is a weapon that attacks the intruder's kitchen** — it breaks the bacterial cell wall, or jams its protein-making machinery. That works beautifully on bacteria, because bacteria *have* those things.

But a virus has **no kitchen of its own**. It uses *yours*. So an antibiotic has nothing to attack. Shooting at the virus's "kitchen" would mean shooting at *your own cells*. That's why antibiotics are useless against the flu, common cold, dengue, or COVID-19.

**One-line summary:** *Antibiotics break bacterial machinery. Viruses have no machinery of their own — they borrow ours.*

---

## Step 2: The Daily-Life Example

Think of it this way:

- **Bacteria** = a thief who sets up his own stove in your garden and starts cooking. You can destroy his stove (antibiotic) and he's finished.
- **Virus** = a thief who sneaks into *your* kitchen and cooks using *your* stove. You can't burn down the stove — it's yours! You need a different strategy: lock the doors (vaccines train your security guards/immune system) or stop him from multiplying (antiviral drugs).

This is also why **vaccines** matter so much for viruses: since we can't easily kill viruses with drugs, we train the body's "security guards" (antibodies) in advance using a harmless preview of the intruder.

---

## Step 3: The Unforgettable Trick

> **"Bacteria Build, Viruses Borrow."**
>
> - **B**acteria **B**uild their own cell wall & machinery → **B**roken by anti**b**iotics.
> - **V**iruses **B**orrow your cell's machinery → **V**accines & anti**v**irals needed, antibiotics fail.

And the exam trap-buster:

> **"Fever + Cold + Flu = Virus = No Antibiotic."**
> If a question says antibiotics cure the common cold — it's **wrong**, every single time.

**Bonus trap alert:** Bacteria are *living* (they reproduce on their own); viruses sit on the **border of living and non-living** — they are "dead" outside a host and "alive" only inside one. Examiners love asking exactly this.
