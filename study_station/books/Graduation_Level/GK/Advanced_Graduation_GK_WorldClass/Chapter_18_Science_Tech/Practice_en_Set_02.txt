Questions 26–50: Medium

26. Which of the following pairs of vitamin and its deficiency disease is correctly matched?
(a) Vitamin A – Scurvy (b) Vitamin D – Rickets (c) Vitamin C – Night blindness (d) Vitamin B12 – Beriberi
Answer: (b)
Solution: The tempting wrong options swap the classic pairs: scurvy is caused by Vitamin C deficiency and night blindness by Vitamin A deficiency, so the first two options reverse the facts. Beriberi is caused by Vitamin B1 (thiamine) deficiency, not B12. Vitamin D deficiency causes rickets in children, so the answer is Vitamin D – Rickets.
Source: NCERT Class 6 Science

27. The SI unit of electric current is:
(a) Ampere (b) Volt (c) Ohm (d) Watt
Answer: (a)
Solution: Volt measures potential difference, Ohm measures resistance, and Watt measures power — all related to electricity but none is the unit of current. Electric current is measured in Ampere, so the answer is Ampere.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science

28. Which gas is commonly known as 'laughing gas'?
(a) Nitrogen dioxide (b) Nitrous oxide (c) Nitric oxide (d) Ammonia
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap here is the similar names of nitrogen oxides: nitric oxide (NO) and nitrogen dioxide (NO2) are not laughing gas. Nitrous oxide (N2O) is called laughing gas and is used as a mild anaesthetic, so the answer is Nitrous oxide.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science

29. The pH value of a neutral solution at 25°C is:
(a) 7 (b) 14 (c) 0 (d) 1
Answer: (a)
Solution: Students often confuse the ends of the pH scale: 0 is strongly acidic and 14 is strongly alkaline. A neutral solution like pure water has pH 7 at 25°C, so the answer is 7.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science

30. Which of the following diseases is caused by a virus?
(a) Tuberculosis (b) Cholera (c) Typhoid (d) Measles
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is that all four are common communicable diseases, but tuberculosis, cholera and typhoid are all caused by bacteria. Measles is caused by a virus (a paramyxovirus), so the answer is Measles.
Source: NCERT Class 8 Science

31. The phenomenon responsible for the blue colour of the sky is:
(a) Reflection (b) Refraction (c) Dispersion (d) Scattering of light
Answer: (d)
Solution: Dispersion (splitting of white light by a prism) is the tempting wrong answer because it also involves colours, but it is not why the sky is blue. Shorter blue wavelengths are scattered most by air molecules (Rayleigh scattering), so the answer is Scattering of light.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science

32. Which blood cells are primarily responsible for fighting infections in the human body?
(a) Red blood cells (b) White blood cells (c) Platelets (d) Plasma cells
Answer: (b)
Solution: Red blood cells carry oxygen and platelets help in clotting — both are tempting because they are abundant and well-known. White blood cells (leucocytes) defend the body against infections, so the answer is White blood cells.
Source: NCERT Class 7 Science

33. The chemical formula of washing soda is:
(a) Na2CO3·10H2O (b) NaHCO3 (c) NaOH (d) CaOCl2
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is the similar name: baking soda is NaHCO3, and caustic soda is NaOH — both get confused with washing soda. Washing soda is sodium carbonate decahydrate, so the answer is Na2CO3·10H2O.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science

34. Which of the following is a non-renewable source of energy?
(a) Solar energy (b) Natural gas (c) Wind energy (d) Biogas
Answer: (b)
Solution: Biogas is the tempting wrong option because it involves gas like natural gas, but biogas is produced from organic waste and is renewable. Natural gas is a fossil fuel formed over millions of years, so the answer is Natural gas.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science

35. The instrument used to measure atmospheric pressure is:
(a) Hydrometer (b) Thermometer (c) Barometer (d) Ammeter
Answer: (c)
Solution: Hydrometer (density of liquids) and ammeter (current) are similar-sounding instruments that confuse candidates. A barometer measures atmospheric pressure, so the answer is Barometer.
Source: NCERT Class 8 Science

36. Which organ in the human body produces insulin?
(a) Liver (b) Kidney (c) Pancreas (d) Spleen
Answer: (c)
Solution: The liver is the tempting wrong answer because it regulates blood glucose by storing glycogen, but it does not secrete insulin. Insulin is secreted by the beta cells of the islets of Langerhans in the pancreas, so the answer is Pancreas.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science

37. The speed of light in vacuum is approximately:
(a) 3 × 10⁸ m/s (b) 3 × 10⁶ m/s (c) 3 × 10¹⁰ m/s (d) 3 × 10⁴ m/s
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is the power of ten — all options look alike, and candidates often misremember the exponent. The speed of light in vacuum is about 3 × 10⁸ m/s, so the answer is 3 × 10⁸ m/s.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science

38. Which of the following metals is liquid at room temperature?
(a) Mercury (b) Sodium (c) Aluminium (d) Gallium only in solid form
Answer: (a)
Solution: Sodium is the tempting wrong answer because it is soft and reactive, but it is solid at room temperature. Mercury is the only metal that remains liquid at room temperature under standard conditions, so the answer is Mercury.
Source: NCERT Class 8 Science

39. The process by which plants lose water vapour through their leaves is called:
(a) Photosynthesis (b) Respiration (c) Transpiration (d) Guttation
Answer: (c)
Solution: Photosynthesis and respiration are tempting because they are the most familiar plant processes, but neither describes water loss. The loss of water vapour mainly through stomata of leaves is transpiration, so the answer is Transpiration.
Source: NCERT Class 7 Science

40. Which of the following is used as a moderator in a nuclear reactor?
(a) Uranium-235 (b) Cadmium rods (c) Plutonium (d) Heavy water
Answer: (d)
Solution: Cadmium rods are the tempting wrong answer, but they are control rods that absorb neutrons, not slow them down; uranium and plutonium are fuels. Heavy water (D2O) slows down fast neutrons as a moderator, so the answer is Heavy water.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science

41. The deficiency of iodine in the human body causes:
(a) Anaemia (b) Goitre (c) Rickets (d) Scurvy
Answer: (b)
Solution: Anaemia is the tempting wrong answer because it is the most common deficiency disease, but it is caused by iron deficiency. Iodine deficiency enlarges the thyroid gland causing goitre, so the answer is Goitre.
Source: NCERT Class 8 Science

42. Which of the following waves is used in a microwave oven?
(a) Infrared waves (b) Radio waves (c) Ultraviolet rays (d) Microwaves
Answer: (d)
Solution: Infrared is the tempting wrong answer because it is associated with heat, but microwave ovens do not heat food by infrared radiation. Microwaves make water molecules vibrate, producing heat inside the food, so the answer is Microwaves.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Physics

43. The chemical name of common salt is:
(a) Sodium carbonate (b) Potassium chloride (c) Sodium chloride (d) Calcium carbonate
Answer: (c)
Solution: Potassium chloride is the tempting wrong answer because it is also a chloride salt used as a salt substitute. Common table salt is sodium chloride (NaCl), so the answer is Sodium chloride.
Source: NCERT Class 7 Science

44. Which part of the human brain controls balance and coordination of the body?
(a) Cerebrum (b) Cerebellum (c) Medulla oblongata (d) Hypothalamus
Answer: (b)
Solution: The cerebrum is the tempting wrong answer because it is the largest part of the brain, but it handles thinking and voluntary actions, not balance. The cerebellum coordinates movement and maintains balance, so the answer is Cerebellum.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science

45. Which of the following is the correct order of the planets from the Sun?
(a) Mercury, Venus, Earth, Mars (b) Venus, Mercury, Earth, Mars (c) Mercury, Earth, Venus, Mars (d) Mercury, Venus, Mars, Earth
Answer: (a)
Solution: This is a chronological/sequence trap: the options swap Venus and Earth or Venus and Mercury to catch careless recall. The correct order outward from the Sun is Mercury, Venus, Earth, Mars, so the answer is Mercury, Venus, Earth, Mars.
Source: NCERT Class 8 Science

46. The filament of an electric bulb is made of:
(a) Copper (b) Nichrome (c) Aluminium (d) Tungsten
Answer: (d)
Solution: Nichrome is the tempting wrong answer because it is used in heating elements like irons and heaters, but it is not used for bulb filaments. Tungsten's very high melting point (about 3422°C) makes it ideal for filaments, so the answer is Tungsten.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science

47. Which of the following is an example of a chemical change?
(a) Melting of ice (b) Dissolving salt in water (c) Evaporation of water (d) Rusting of iron
Answer: (d)
Solution: Dissolving salt in water is the tempting wrong answer because the salt seems to disappear, but it is a reversible physical change. Rusting forms a new substance (iron oxide) and cannot be reversed easily, so the answer is Rusting of iron.
Source: NCERT Class 7 Science

48. The universal blood donor group is:
(a) AB positive (b) B positive (c) O negative (d) AB negative
Answer: (c)
Solution: AB positive is the tempting wrong answer, but it is the universal recipient, not the donor — a classic examiner's swap. O negative blood can be given to people of any blood group, so the answer is O negative.
Source: NCERT Class 7 Science

49. Which of the following acids is present in vinegar?
(a) Citric acid (b) Lactic acid (c) Acetic acid (d) Tartaric acid
Answer: (c)
Solution: Citric acid is the tempting wrong answer because vinegar tastes sour like citrus fruits, but citrus fruits contain citric acid, not vinegar. Vinegar is a 4–8% solution of acetic acid (ethanoic acid), so the answer is Acetic acid.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science

50. The unit of frequency is:
(a) Hertz (b) Joule (c) Newton (d) Pascal
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is that all four are SI units, so candidates who memorise units without quantities get confused: Joule is energy, Newton is force, and Pascal is pressure. Frequency is measured in Hertz (cycles per second), so the answer is Hertz.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Science
