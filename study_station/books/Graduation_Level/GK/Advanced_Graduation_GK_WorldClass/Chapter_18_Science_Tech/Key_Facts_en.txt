# Key Facts & Figures — Science & Technology (Basic)

## Part 1: High-Yield Facts

| Fact | Description | Examiner's Trap | Trap Type |
|---|---|---|---|
| SI unit of force | Newton (N) = kg·m/s² | Dyne is CGS unit, not SI | Similar-name confusion |
| SI unit of work/energy | Joule (J) | Calorie is heat unit, not SI energy unit | Similar-name confusion |
| SI unit of power | Watt (W) = J/s | Horsepower (746 W) is not SI | Unit mix-up |
| SI unit of pressure | Pascal (Pa) = N/m² | Bar and atm are non-SI units | Unit mix-up |
| SI unit of frequency | Hertz (Hz) | Decibel measures sound intensity, not frequency | Concept swap |
| SI unit of electric current | Ampere (A) — a base unit | Volt is derived unit, not base | Base vs derived trap |
| 7 SI base units | metre, kilogram, second, ampere, kelvin, mole, candela | "Litre" and "gram" are not base units | Negative-statement trap |
| Speed of light in vacuum | 3 × 10⁸ m/s | Sound needs a medium; light does not | Concept swap |
| Speed of sound in air (approx.) | ~332 m/s at 0°C | Faster in solids than in gases — order reversed in options | Order reversal |
| Light year | Unit of distance (~9.46 × 10¹² km) | Trap: "unit of time" | Classic trap |
| Acceleration due to gravity (g) | 9.8 m/s² on Earth's surface | g is maximum at poles, minimum at equator | Location reversal |
| Newton's First Law | Law of inertia | Inertia ∝ mass, not velocity | Concept swap |
| Newton's Third Law | Action–reaction act on different bodies | Trap: "they cancel each other" | Nearly-true statement |
| Convex mirror | Always virtual, erect, diminished image | Used as rear-view mirror (wide field) | Application swap |
| Concave mirror | Real/virtual depending on object position | Used in torches, shaving mirrors, solar cookers | Application swap |
| Convex lens | Converging lens; used for hypermetropia | Myopia corrected by concave lens | Defect–lens swap |
| Myopia (short-sightedness) | Corrected by concave (diverging) lens | Image forms in front of retina | Nearly-true statement |
| Hypermetropia (long-sightedness) | Corrected by convex (converging) lens | Image forms behind retina | Nearly-true statement |
| Total internal reflection | Light goes denser → rarer, angle > critical angle | Basis of optical fibres and diamond sparkle | Cause reversal |
| Rainbow formation | Dispersion + total internal reflection in droplets | Primary rainbow: red on top | Order reversal |
| Scattering of light | Blue sky due to Rayleigh scattering | Red appears at sunrise/sunset (longest path) | Concept swap |
| Ohm's Law | V = IR | Valid only at constant temperature | Condition trap |
| Resistance series vs parallel | Series: R = R₁+R₂; Parallel: 1/R = 1/R₁+1/R₂ | Household wiring is parallel | Application trap |
| Fuse wire | Low melting point, high resistance (tin-lead alloy) | Connected in series with live wire | Placement trap |
| AC frequency in India | 50 Hz, 220 V supply | Trap: 60 Hz (USA standard) | Country mix-up |
| Electromagnetic waves | Do not need medium; travel at light speed | Sound is mechanical, not EM | Category trap |
| X-rays | Discovered by Wilhelm Roentgen (1895) | Trap: Becquerel (radioactivity) | Discoverer swap |
| Radioactivity | Discovered by Henri Becquerel (1896) | Trap: Curie coined the term later | Discoverer swap |
| Penicillin | Alexander Fleming (1928) | Trap: Florey/Chain developed it | Discoverer swap |
| Telephone | Alexander Graham Bell (1876) | Trap: Edison (phonograph, bulb) | Inventor swap |
| Vitamin C deficiency | Scurvy | Trap: rickets (Vitamin D) | Deficiency swap |
| Vitamin D deficiency | Rickets (children), osteomalacia (adults) | Synthesised in skin by sunlight | Deficiency swap |
| Vitamin B12 deficiency | Pernicious anaemia | Only vitamin containing a metal (cobalt) | Fact swap |
| Vitamin A deficiency | Night blindness (xerophthalmia) | Trap: beriberi (B1) | Deficiency swap |
| Vitamin K | Essential for blood clotting | Trap: "produced only from food" — gut bacteria also make it | Nearly-true statement |
| Insulin | Discovered by Banting & Best (1921); controls blood sugar | Secreted by beta cells of islets of Langerhans (pancreas) | Gland–hormone swap |
| Universal blood donor | O negative | Universal recipient: AB positive | Donor–recipient swap |
| Blood groups discovered by | Karl Landsteiner (1900–01) | Rh factor found in Rhesus monkey | Discoverer trap |
| Normal human body temperature | 37°C (98.6°F) | Measured by clinical thermometer | Value trap |
| Normal blood pressure | 120/80 mm Hg | Measured by sphygmomanometer | Instrument swap |
| pH of human blood | ~7.4 (slightly alkaline) | pH < 7 acidic, > 7 basic | Value trap |
| Largest gland in human body | Liver | Largest organ (overall): skin | Organ swap |
| Largest cell in human body | Ovum (female egg cell) | Longest cell: neuron | Cell swap |
| Smallest bone | Stapes (ear) | Largest bone: femur (thigh) | Bone swap |
| Number of bones in adult human | 206 | Infant has ~300 (fuse later) | Value trap |
| Powerhouse of the cell | Mitochondria | Trap: ribosome (protein synthesis) | Function swap |
| Photosynthesis site | Chloroplast (chlorophyll) | Releases O₂, absorbs CO₂ | Gas swap |
| DNA structure | Double helix — Watson & Crick (1953) | Rosalind Franklin's X-ray data contributed | Discoverer trap |
| Full form: AIDS | Acquired Immuno Deficiency Syndrome | Caused by HIV (virus), not bacteria | Cause swap |
| Full form: LASER | Light Amplification by Stimulated Emission of Radiation | Word-order trap in options | Acronym trap |
| Full form: RADAR | Radio Detection and Ranging | Trap: SONAR uses sound waves | Acronym/tech swap |
| Full form: MRI | Magnetic Resonance Imaging | Uses magnetic fields, not X-rays | Tech swap |
| Antibiotics | Effective against bacteria, not viruses | Trap: "antibiotics cure common cold" | Nearly-true statement |
| Vaccine for smallpox | Edward Jenner (1796) — first vaccine | Trap: Pasteur (rabies) | Discoverer swap |
| BCG vaccine | Against tuberculosis | Trap: polio (OPV/IPV) | Vaccine–disease swap |
| Greenhouse gases | CO₂, CH₄, N₂O, water vapour, CFCs | O₂ and N₂ are NOT greenhouse gases | Negative-statement trap |
| Ozone layer | In stratosphere; absorbs UV-B | Ozone hole maximum over Antarctica | Location trap |
| Acid rain | pH < 5.6; caused by SO₂ and NOx | Trap: CO₂ blamed as main cause | Cause swap |
| CNG main component | Methane (CH₄) | Trap: LPG = propane/butane | Fuel swap |
| Hard water | Contains Ca²⁺ and Mg²⁺ salts | Does not lather with soap | Concept trap |
| Common salt | NaCl; iodised salt prevents goitre | Goitre due to iodine deficiency | Cause trap |
| Baking soda | Sodium bicarbonate (NaHCO₃) | Washing soda = Na₂CO₃·10H₂O | Chemical swap |
| Dry ice | Solid CO₂ | Trap: "solid water at low temperature" | Classic trap |
| Rusting of iron | Oxidation in presence of O₂ + moisture | Galvanisation = zinc coating prevents it | Process trap |
| pH scale | 0–14; 7 neutral | Trap: "pH of pure water is 0" | Value trap |
| Nuclear fission | Heavy nucleus splits (reactors, atom bomb) | Nuclear fusion powers the Sun | Process swap |
| India's first nuclear test | Pokhran, 1974 ("Smiling Buddha") | Pokhran-II was 1998 | Year confusion |
| Father of Indian nuclear programme | Homi J. Bhabha | Trap: Vikram Sarabhai (space) | Person swap |
| Father of Indian space programme | Vikram Sarabhai | ISRO founded 1969; HQ Bengaluru | Person/year trap |
| India's first satellite | Aryabhata (1975, launched by USSR) | Trap: Bhaskara (1979) | Satellite swap |
| Chandrayaan-3 | Soft-landed near lunar south pole (23 Aug 2023) | Trap: Chandrayaan-2 lander crashed (2019) | Mission confusion |
| Aditya-L1 | India's first solar observation mission (2023) | L1 = Lagrange point 1, ~1.5 million km from Earth | Concept trap |
| Gaganyaan | India's human spaceflight programme | Astronauts called "Vyomanauts" | Programme trap |
| GPS (USA) vs NavIC (India) | NavIC: 7 satellites, regional | Trap: GLONASS is Russian, Galileo is EU | System–country swap |
| 5G vs 4G | Higher speed, lower latency | Trap: "5G needs no towers" | Nearly-true statement |
| Internet vs WWW | Internet = network; WWW = Tim Berners-Lee (1989) | Trap: "internet invented by Berners-Lee" | Classic trap |
| Computer: father of | Charles Babbage (analytical engine) | First programmer: Ada Lovelace | Person swap |
| 1 Byte | 8 bits | 1 KB = 1024 bytes (not 1000) | Value trap |
| CPU full form | Central Processing Unit | ALU + Control Unit are its parts | Acronym trap |
| RAM vs ROM | RAM volatile; ROM non-volatile | Trap: "RAM retains data without power" | Concept swap |
