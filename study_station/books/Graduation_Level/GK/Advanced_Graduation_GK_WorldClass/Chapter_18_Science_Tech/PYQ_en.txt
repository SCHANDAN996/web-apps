# 📊 PYQ Analysis — Science & Technology (Basic)

## How Exams Test This Chapter

Across SSC (CGL/CHSL), Banking (IBPS/SBI), Railway (NTPC/Group D) and State PCS papers, Science & Technology questions from this chapter follow a stable, recognisable pattern. The examiner rarely asks deep theory; instead, he tests whether you can recall a **precise fact under pressure** and resist **look-alike options**.

### Sub-topics That Recur Most Often

- **Units and measurements** — SI units, derived units of common quantities (force, work, power, pressure, frequency).
- **Physics in daily life** — why the sky is blue, mirage, surface tension, capillarity, why a ship floats, pressure cookers, optical instruments.
- **Chemistry basics** — pH scale, common acids/bases in household items, alloys and their composition, gases (laughing gas, tear gas, marsh gas), metals and non-metals.
- **Biology basics** — vitamins and deficiency diseases, human organs and their functions, blood groups, photosynthesis, cell organelles, common diseases and their causative agents (bacterial vs viral).
- **Inventions and scientists** — who invented/discovered what (telephone, X-rays, penicillin, dynamite, radio).
- **Space and defence basics** — ISRO milestones, missile names, satellite types (asked as static facts, not current affairs).

### Exam-wise Flavour

- **State PCS**: conceptual and statement-based — "Which of the following statements is/are correct?" Often links science to everyday phenomena or environment.
- **SSC / Railway**: direct one-liner factual recall — units, inventions, vitamins, scientific instruments.
- **Banking**: fewer questions, but when asked, they favour units, abbreviations, and simple physics/chemistry facts.

### The Examiner's Traps (जाल)

1. **Almost Correct Statement** — "Mitochondria is the powerhouse of the cell" is right; the trap version swaps in "Ribosome" or changes one word (e.g., "Vitamin C deficiency causes night blindness" — it causes scurvy; night blindness is Vitamin A).
2. **Chronological Confusion** — inventions/discoveries shuffled in time (telephone vs radio vs X-ray), or ISRO missions placed in the wrong order.
3. **Similar-Sounding Names** — mitosis vs meiosis, arteries vs veins, speed vs velocity, mass vs weight, echo vs reverberation.
4. **Negative Phrasing** — "Which of the following is **NOT** a bacterial disease?" Students misread and pick a bacterial one.
5. **Unit Swap** — asking the SI unit of power but placing "Joule" (work) as the most attractive option.

## Top 10 High-Yield PYQ-Style Questions with Solutions

**Q1. Which of the following is the SI unit of electric current?**
(a) Volt (b) Ampere (c) Ohm (d) Watt
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** Ampere is the SI base unit of electric current. Volt (potential difference), Ohm (resistance) and Watt (power) are derived units — classic **Unit Swap** trap.
**Source:** NCERT Class 10 Science

**Q2. Night blindness is caused by the deficiency of which vitamin?**
(a) Vitamin B12 (b) Vitamin C (c) Vitamin A (d) Vitamin D
**Answer: (c)**
**Solution:** Vitamin A (retinol) deficiency causes night blindness. Vitamin C deficiency causes scurvy — the **Almost Correct** distractor. Vitamin D → rickets; B12 → anaemia.
**Source:** NCERT Class 12 Biology / PYQ-style

**Q3. Which of the following diseases is NOT caused by a virus?**
(a) Measles (b) Tuberculosis (c) Polio (d) Influenza
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** Tuberculosis is caused by the bacterium *Mycobacterium tuberculosis*. Measles, polio and influenza are viral. Note the **Negative Phrasing** trap — read "NOT" twice before answering.
**Source:** NCERT Class 9 Science / PYQ-style

**Q4. The blue colour of the sky is due to:**
(a) Reflection of sunlight (b) Scattering of light (c) Refraction of light (d) Dispersion of light
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** Rayleigh scattering — shorter (blue) wavelengths scatter more in the atmosphere. Refraction (bending) and dispersion (splitting, as in a rainbow) are the tempting **Similar-Concept** traps.
**Source:** NCERT Class 10 Science

**Q5. Who invented the telephone?**
(a) Thomas Edison (b) Guglielmo Marconi (c) Alexander Graham Bell (d) Nikola Tesla
**Answer: (c)**
**Solution:** Bell patented the telephone in 1876. Marconi is associated with radio, Edison with the phonograph and electric bulb — **Similar-Sounding Names** trap among inventors.
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q6. Which gas is commonly known as 'laughing gas'?**
(a) Nitrogen dioxide (b) Nitrous oxide (c) Carbon monoxide (d) Methane
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** Nitrous oxide (N₂O) is laughing gas, used as an anaesthetic. Methane is 'marsh gas' — a frequent **name-confusion** trap.
**Source:** NCERT Class 10 Science / PYQ-style

**Q7. Arrange the following in chronological order of their discovery/invention:**
1. X-rays  2. Telephone  3. Penicillin  4. Dynamite
(a) 2, 4, 1, 3 (b) 4, 2, 1, 3 (c) 2, 1, 4, 3 (d) 4, 1, 2, 3
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** Dynamite (Nobel, 1867) → Telephone (Bell, 1876) → X-rays (Röntgen, 1895) → Penicillin (Fleming, 1928), i.e. 4, 2, 1, 3. Option (a) is the **Chronological Confusion** trap — it looks right if you only remember "telephone is old, penicillin is recent" and forget that dynamite predates the telephone. Always fix at least two anchor dates before ordering.
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q8. The working principle of a pressure cooker is based on:**
(a) Decrease of boiling point with pressure (b) Increase of boiling point with pressure (c) Increase of melting point with pressure (d) Charles's law of volumes
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** Higher pressure inside raises water's boiling point above 100°C, so food cooks faster. Option (a) is the **Almost Correct** reversal trap.
**Source:** NCERT Class 11 Physics / PYQ-style

**Q9. Which of the following is the powerhouse of the cell?**
(a) Ribosome (b) Nucleus (c) Mitochondrion (d) Golgi apparatus
**Answer: (c)**
**Solution:** Mitochondria produce ATP. Ribosomes make proteins, the nucleus controls the cell, Golgi packages materials — **Similar-Sounding Names** trap among organelles.
**Source:** NCERT Class 9 Science

**Q10. Brass is an alloy of:**
(a) Copper and tin (b) Copper and zinc (c) Iron and chromium (d) Aluminium and magnesium
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** Brass = copper + zinc. Copper + tin makes **bronze** — the most common alloy-confusion trap in SSC/Railway papers. Iron + chromium relates to stainless steel.
**Source:** NCERT Class 10 Science / PYQ-style

## The 15/45-Second Rule Applied

**Q1 (SI unit of current) — 15-second question.**
This is pure recall. If you know Ampere, mark it and move on in under 15 seconds. Do not re-read the options; Volt/Ohm/Watt are there only to create doubt. Spending 45 seconds here steals time from statement-based questions.

**Q7 (chronological order) — 45-second question.**
This deserves the full 45 seconds. Write the anchor dates in the margin: Dynamite 1867, Telephone 1876, X-rays 1895, Penicillin 1928. Then match the sequence. Rushing this question almost guarantees falling into the trap option (a), which looks right if you only remember "telephone is old, penicillin is recent."

**Rule of thumb:** direct recall (units, inventors, vitamins) = 15 seconds; ordering, statement-matching, NOT-type questions = 45 seconds. If a question needs more than 45 seconds, mark it for review and move ahead.

---

## Key Takeaways for the Exam Hall

- Read "NOT/EXCEPT" questions twice before touching the options.
- For every vitamin, alloy, gas and inventor, learn the **paired trap** (A↔C, brass↔bronze, marsh↔laughing gas, Bell↔Edison).
- Fix 4–5 anchor years for inventions; chronological questions become mechanical.
- Units: memorise the SI base units cold — they are guaranteed 15-second marks.
