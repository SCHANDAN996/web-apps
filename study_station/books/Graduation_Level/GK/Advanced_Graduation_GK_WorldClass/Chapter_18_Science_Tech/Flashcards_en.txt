Card 1
Front: Why does the sky appear blue, and which scientist explained this phenomenon?
Back: Due to Rayleigh scattering — shorter blue wavelengths of sunlight scatter more in the atmosphere than longer red wavelengths. Explained by Lord Rayleigh (C.V. Raman explained the blue colour of the sea similarly).

Card 2
Front: Which vitamin is synthesised in human skin on exposure to sunlight, and what disease does its deficiency cause?
Back: Vitamin D (calciferol). Its deficiency causes rickets in children and osteomalacia in adults.

Card 3
Front: A person with blood group O is called a "universal donor" — but which blood group is the universal recipient?
Back: AB (positive, strictly AB+). O can donate to all groups because it lacks A and B antigens; AB can receive from all because it has no anti-A or anti-B antibodies.

Card 4
Front: What is the chemical name of common salt, and what products are obtained when its solution is electrolysed in the chlor-alkali process?
Back: Sodium chloride (NaCl). The chlor-alkali process is the electrolysis of brine (concentrated NaCl solution), producing chlorine gas (Cl₂) at the anode, hydrogen gas (H₂) at the cathode, and sodium hydroxide (NaOH) in solution. No acid is involved in this process.

Card 5
Front: Which gas is known as "laughing gas", and what is its chemical formula?
Back: Nitrous oxide, N₂O. Used as an anaesthetic and in aerosol propellants.

Card 6
Front: Mnemonic trick: How do you remember the order of planets from the Sun?
Back: "My Very Educated Mother Just Served Us Noodles" — Mercury, Venus, Earth, Mars, Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus, Neptune.

Card 7
Front: Mnemonic trick: How do you recall the colours of the visible spectrum in order?
Back: "VIBGYOR" — Violet, Indigo, Blue, Green, Yellow, Orange, Red. Violet has the shortest wavelength, red the longest.

Card 8
Front: Examiner's trap: "The image formed by a plane mirror is real and erect." True or false?
Back: False. A plane mirror forms a VIRTUAL, erect, laterally inverted image of the same size. Examiners swap "virtual" with "real" — only concave mirrors and convex lenses can form real images.

Card 9
Front: Examiner's trap: Which is heavier — 1 kg of iron or 1 kg of cotton? And why do people get confused?
Back: Both weigh the same (1 kg). The trap exploits density intuition: iron is denser, so equal VOLUMES would differ, but equal MASSES are equal. Read "1 kg" carefully before answering.

Card 10
Front: Which organelle is called the "powerhouse of the cell", and what does it produce?
Back: Mitochondria. It produces ATP (adenosine triphosphate) through cellular respiration.

Card 11
Front: What is the pH value of pure water at 25°C, and what does a pH below 7 indicate?
Back: pH 7 (neutral). Below 7 = acidic; above 7 = basic (alkaline). The pH scale runs from 0 to 14.

Card 12
Front: Which Indian scientist is known as the "Missile Man of India", and which missile programme did he lead?
Back: Dr. A.P.J. Abdul Kalam. He led the Integrated Guided Missile Development Programme (IGMDP), which developed Agni, Prithvi, Akash, Nag and Trishul missiles.

Card 13
Front: What does "ISRO" stand for, where is its headquarters, and in which year was it established?
Back: Indian Space Research Organisation; headquarters in Bengaluru; established in 1969 (founder: Dr. Vikram Sarabhai).

Card 14
Front: Which metal is liquid at room temperature, and what is its common use?
Back: Mercury (Hg). Used in thermometers and barometers. (Gallium and caesium also melt near room temperature — a common trap option.)

Card 15
Front: What is the SI unit of electric current, and which instrument measures it?
Back: Ampere (A), measured by an ammeter (connected in series). Trap: voltmeter measures potential difference and is connected in parallel.

Card 16
Front: Which vitamin deficiency causes night blindness, and what is its chemical name?
Back: Vitamin A (retinol). Trap: scurvy is Vitamin C deficiency, beriberi is Vitamin B1 — examiners shuffle these pairs.

Card 17
Front: What is the speed of light in vacuum, and who proposed that nothing can travel faster than it?
Back: Approximately 3 × 10⁸ m/s (299,792,458 m/s exactly). Albert Einstein, in the Special Theory of Relativity (1905).

Card 18
Front: Which gas is most abundant in Earth's atmosphere, and what is its approximate percentage?
Back: Nitrogen — about 78%. Oxygen is about 21%, argon ~0.93%, CO₂ ~0.04%. Trap: many students wrongly pick oxygen.

Card 19
Front: What is the full form of "DNA", and who proposed its double-helix structure?
Back: Deoxyribonucleic acid. The double-helix model was proposed by James Watson and Francis Crick (1953), using Rosalind Franklin's X-ray data.

Card 20
Front: Which Indian nuclear test series of 1974 was code-named "Smiling Buddha", and where was it conducted?
Back: India's first nuclear test, conducted at Pokhran, Rajasthan, on 18 May 1974. (The 1998 tests were "Operation Shakti" — Pokhran-II; examiners often mix the two.)
