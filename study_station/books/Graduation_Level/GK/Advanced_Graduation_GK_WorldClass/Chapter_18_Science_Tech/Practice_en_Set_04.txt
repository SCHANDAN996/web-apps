76. Which of the following is the SI unit of electric current?
(a) Ohm (b) Volt (c) Ampere (d) Watt
Answer: (c)
Solution: Volt measures potential difference, Ohm measures resistance, and Watt measures power — all tempting because they belong to the same family of electrical units. The SI unit of electric current is the Ampere, so the answer is Ampere.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science

77. The phenomenon of bending of light as it passes from one medium to another is called:
(a) Reflection (b) Refraction (c) Diffraction (d) Dispersion
Answer: (b)
Solution: Reflection (light bouncing back) is the tempting wrong choice since both involve a change in light's path, but reflection occurs at a surface without entering the new medium. Bending due to change in speed between media is refraction, so the answer is Refraction.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science

78. Which vitamin is synthesized in the human skin on exposure to sunlight?
(a) Vitamin D (b) Vitamin C (c) Vitamin A (d) Vitamin K
Answer: (a)
Solution: Vitamin K is a common trap because it is also synthesized in the body — but by gut bacteria, not by skin in sunlight. Sunlight converts 7-dehydrocholesterol in skin into Vitamin D, so the answer is Vitamin D.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Biology

79. The pH value of a neutral solution at 25°C is:
(a) 0 (b) 7 (c) 14 (d) 1
Answer: (b)
Solution: Students often confuse the extremes of the scale: 0 is strongly acidic and 14 is strongly alkaline. A neutral solution such as pure water has equal H⁺ and OH⁻ concentrations, giving pH 7, so the answer is 7.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science

80. Which of the following gases is the main component of biogas?
(a) Carbon dioxide (b) Hydrogen (c) Nitrogen (d) Methane
Answer: (d)
Solution: Carbon dioxide is present in biogas and is the usual trap, but it is only a minor component. Biogas typically contains 50–70% methane produced by anaerobic digestion, so the answer is Methane.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science

81. The chemical formula of washing soda is:
(a) NaHCO₃ (b) Na₂CO₃·10H₂O (c) NaOH (d) CaOCl₂
Answer: (b)
Solution: NaHCO₃ (baking soda) is the classic trap because of the similar name. Washing soda is sodium carbonate decahydrate, Na₂CO₃·10H₂O, so the answer is Na₂CO₃·10H₂O.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science

82. Which organelle is known as the powerhouse of the cell?
(a) Ribosome (b) Nucleus (c) Mitochondrion (d) Golgi apparatus
Answer: (c)
Solution: The nucleus is tempting as the "control centre," but it directs the cell rather than supplying energy. Mitochondria produce ATP through cellular respiration, so the answer is Mitochondrion.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Science

83. The speed of light in vacuum is approximately:
(a) 3 × 10¹⁰ m/s (b) 3 × 10⁶ m/s (c) 3 × 10⁸ m/s (d) 3 × 10⁴ m/s
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap lies in the exponent: 3 × 10⁶ and 3 × 10¹⁰ look similar under exam pressure. The accepted value is 299,792,458 m/s, rounded to 3 × 10⁸ m/s, so the answer is 3 × 10⁸ m/s.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Science

84. Which of the following diseases is caused by a virus?
(a) Measles (b) Cholera (c) Tuberculosis (d) Typhoid
Answer: (a)
Solution: Tuberculosis, cholera and typhoid are all tempting because they are major communicable diseases, but all three are bacterial. Measles is caused by a virus, so the answer is Measles.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Science

85. The device used to measure atmospheric pressure is:
(a) Thermometer (b) Ammeter (c) Hydrometer (d) Barometer
Answer: (d)
Solution: Hydrometer is the trap — it measures relative density of liquids, not pressure. A barometer measures atmospheric pressure, so the answer is Barometer.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Physics

86. Which blood group is called the universal donor?
(a) AB positive (b) AB negative (c) O negative (d) O positive
Answer: (c)
Solution: AB positive is the classic trap — it is the universal recipient, not donor. O negative blood lacks A, B and Rh antigens, so it can be given to anyone; the answer is O negative.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Biology

87. The process by which plants lose water vapour through leaves is called:
(a) Photosynthesis (b) Respiration (c) Guttation (d) Transpiration
Answer: (d)
Solution: Guttation is the trap — it also involves water loss from leaves, but as liquid droplets, not vapour. Loss of water as vapour mainly through stomata is transpiration, so the answer is Transpiration.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Biology

88. Which of the following is a non-renewable source of energy?
(a) Solar energy (b) Wind energy (c) Coal (d) Hydel energy
Answer: (c)
Solution: All four are energy sources, which makes the classification the real test. Solar, wind and hydel are naturally replenished; coal is a fossil fuel formed over millions of years, so the answer is Coal.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science

89. The atomic number of an element is equal to the number of:
(a) Protons (b) Nucleons (c) Neutrons (d) Mesons
Answer: (a)
Solution: Nucleons (protons + neutrons) give the mass number, which is the tempting confusion. Atomic number equals the number of protons in the nucleus, so the answer is Protons.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Science

90. Which of the following correctly matches the scientist with the discovery?
(a) Darwin — Theory of evolution (b) Newton — Theory of relativity (c) Einstein — Laws of motion (d) Mendel — Discovery of X-rays
Answer: (a)
Solution: The traps swap famous names: relativity belongs to Einstein, laws of motion to Newton, and X-rays to Röntgen. Darwin proposed the theory of evolution by natural selection, so the answer is Darwin — Theory of evolution.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Biology

91. The filament of an electric bulb is usually made of:
(a) Tungsten (b) Aluminium (c) Copper (d) Nichrome
Answer: (a)
Solution: Nichrome is the trap because it is indeed a heating element — but in heaters and irons, not bulb filaments. Tungsten's very high melting point (about 3422°C) suits incandescent filaments, so the answer is Tungsten.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science

92. Which of the following statements about sound is correct?
(a) Sound travels faster in solids than in gases (b) Sound travels fastest in vacuum (c) Sound cannot travel through liquids (d) Sound travels at the same speed in all media
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is the reversed claim that sound travels fastest in vacuum — in fact sound cannot travel in vacuum at all. Sound moves fastest in solids, slower in liquids and slowest in gases, so the answer is that sound travels faster in solids than in gases.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Science

93. The deficiency of iodine in the human body causes:
(a) Scurvy (b) Anaemia (c) Rickets (d) Goitre
Answer: (d)
Solution: Anaemia is tempting because it is also a deficiency disorder, but it results from iron deficiency. Iodine deficiency enlarges the thyroid gland causing goitre, so the answer is Goitre.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Biology

94. Which of the following is used as a moderator in a nuclear reactor?
(a) Uranium-235 (b) Heavy water (c) Cadmium (d) Plutonium
Answer: (b)
Solution: Cadmium is the trap — it is used in reactors but as control rods to absorb neutrons, not to slow them. Heavy water (D₂O) slows fast neutrons as a moderator, so the answer is Heavy water.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Physics

95. The full form of LASER is:
(a) Light Absorption by Stimulated Emission of Radiation (b) Light Amplification by Spontaneous Emission of Radiation (c) Light Amplification by Stimulated Emission of Radiation (d) Light Amplification by Stimulated Emission of Rays
Answer: (c)
Solution: The traps alter one word each: "Absorption" reverses the process, "Spontaneous" is the wrong emission type, and "Rays" replaces Radiation. The correct expansion is Light Amplification by Stimulated Emission of Radiation, so the answer is Light Amplification by Stimulated Emission of Radiation.
Source: PYQ-style

96. Which of the following is the correct chronological order of these space milestones?
(a) Sputnik 1 — Aryabhata — Apollo 11 (b) Apollo 11 — Sputnik 1 — Aryabhata (c) Aryabhata — Sputnik 1 — Apollo 11 (d) Sputnik 1 — Apollo 11 — Aryabhata
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap reverses Sputnik and Apollo: Sputnik 1 (1957) was the first artificial satellite, Apollo 11 landed humans on the Moon in 1969, and India's Aryabhata launched in 1975. The correct sequence is Sputnik 1 — Apollo 11 — Aryabhata, so the answer is Sputnik 1 — Apollo 11 — Aryabhata.
Source: PYQ-style

97. Which of the following is a greenhouse gas?
(a) Oxygen (b) Carbon dioxide (c) Nitrogen (d) Argon
Answer: (b)
Solution: Nitrogen and oxygen are traps because they dominate the atmosphere, but being diatomic homonuclear molecules they do not absorb infrared radiation. Carbon dioxide traps heat and is a major greenhouse gas, so the answer is Carbon dioxide.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Chemistry

98. The instrument used to record the waves generated by an earthquake is:
(a) Seismograph (b) Barograph (c) Richter scale (d) Lactometer
Answer: (a)
Solution: The Richter scale is the deliberate trap — it is a scale that quantifies earthquake magnitude, not an instrument that records waves. A seismograph detects and records seismic waves, so the answer is Seismograph.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Geography

99. Which of the following pairs is correctly matched?
(a) Penicillin — Alexander Fleming (b) Telephone — Thomas Edison (c) Radio — Guglielmo Marconi (d) Both Penicillin — Alexander Fleming and Radio — Guglielmo Marconi
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is the telephone pairing: it was invented by Alexander Graham Bell, not Edison, so that single pairing is wrong. Fleming discovered penicillin (1928) and Marconi pioneered radio, so the answer is that both the penicillin and radio pairs are correct.
Source: PYQ-style

100. In the human body, insulin is produced by:
(a) Liver (b) Pancreas (c) Kidney (d) Pituitary gland
Answer: (b)
Solution: The pituitary is tempting as the "master gland" producing many hormones, but not insulin. Insulin is secreted by the beta cells of the islets of Langerhans in the pancreas, so the answer is Pancreas.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Biology
