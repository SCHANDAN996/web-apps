# Science & Technology (Basic)

## The Hook: A Missed Bus That Changed Medicine Forever

In 1928, Alexander Fleming went on holiday and left a petri dish of bacteria uncovered near an open window. When he returned, he noticed a mould had killed the bacteria around it. Most scientists would have thrown the dish away. Fleming asked *why* — and the world got **penicillin**, the first antibiotic, which has since saved hundreds of millions of lives.

That is the heart of Science & Technology: **curiosity + observation + application**. And that is exactly what your exam tests — not PhD-level depth, but sharp awareness of basic scientific facts, discoveries, instruments, and India's technological milestones.

## 1. Branches of Science — Who Studies What?

Science is divided into three great streams:

- **Physics** — matter, energy, motion, force, light, sound, heat, electricity.
- **Chemistry** — composition, structure and reactions of substances.
- **Biology** — living organisms (Botany = plants, Zoology = animals).

## 2. SI Units — The Language of Measurement

The **International System of Units (SI)** has seven base units. Exams ask these directly:

| Quantity | SI Unit | Symbol |
|---|---|---|
| Length | metre | m |
| Mass | kilogram | kg |
| Time | second | s |
| Electric current | ampere | A |
| Temperature | kelvin | K |
| Amount of substance | mole | mol |
| Luminous intensity | candela | cd |

**Important derived units:**

- Force → **newton (N)**
- Pressure → **pascal (Pa)**
- Work/Energy → **joule (J)**
- Power → **watt (W)**
- Electric charge → **coulomb (C)**
- Potential difference → **volt (V)**
- Resistance → **ohm (Ω)**
- Frequency → **hertz (Hz)**
- Capacitance → **farad (F)**

🧠 **Active Recall:** What is the SI unit of (a) pressure, (b) power, (c) frequency? If you hesitated on any, re-read the table now.

## 5. Everyday Physics & Chemistry — Concepts Exams Love

**Physics quick facts:**
- Light travels fastest in vacuum (~3 × 10⁸ m/s).
- Sound needs a medium; it cannot travel in vacuum. Sound travels fastest in **solids**, slowest in **gases**.
- A rainbow is caused by **dispersion, refraction and internal reflection** of sunlight in water droplets.
- The sky appears blue due to **scattering of light** (Rayleigh scattering).
- Diamond sparkles due to **total internal reflection**.
- A fuse wire has **low melting point and high resistance**.
- Convex mirrors are used as rear-view mirrors (wider field of view); concave mirrors in torches and shaving mirrors.

**Chemistry quick facts:**
- pH scale: 0–14; below 7 = acidic, 7 = neutral, above 7 = basic.
- Common acids: HCl (stomach), citric acid (citrus fruits), acetic acid (vinegar), lactic acid (curd/sour milk).
- Baking soda = sodium bicarbonate (NaHCO₃); washing soda = sodium carbonate (Na₂CO₃).
- Hardest natural substance = **diamond** (pure carbon); graphite (also carbon) is a good conductor of electricity.
- Most abundant gas in the atmosphere = **nitrogen (~78%)**, then oxygen (~21%).
- Ozone (O₃) layer absorbs harmful **ultraviolet (UV)** radiation.

**Biology quick facts:**
- Powerhouse of the cell = **mitochondria**.
- Photosynthesis occurs in **chloroplasts** (chlorophyll pigment).
- Universal blood donor = **O negative**; universal recipient = **AB positive**.
- Blood groups discovered by **Karl Landsteiner** (ABO system).
- Normal human body temperature ≈ **37°C (98.6°F)**.
- Largest gland in the human body = **liver**; largest organ = **skin**.
- Insulin (discovered by Banting & Best, 1921) regulates blood sugar; its deficiency causes **diabetes**.
- Vitamin C deficiency → scurvy; Vitamin D → rickets; Vitamin A → night blindness; Vitamin B1 → beriberi; iron → anaemia; iodine → goitre.

## 6. India's Technology Milestones (Static Core)

**Space:**
- **ISRO** established: 15 August 1969; headquarters — Bengaluru.
- First Indian satellite: **Aryabhata** (1975, launched by the USSR).
- **SLV-3** (1980) launched Rohini — India's first indigenous launch.
- **Chandrayaan-1** (2008) — discovered water molecules on the Moon.
- **Mars Orbiter Mission (Mangalyaan, 2013)** — India became the first Asian nation to reach Mars orbit, and the first in the world to do so in its first attempt.
- **Chandrayaan-3** (2023) — India became the **fourth country to soft-land on the Moon** and the **first to land near the lunar south pole** (Vikram lander, Pragyan rover).
- **Aditya-L1** (2023) — India's first solar observation mission.

**Nuclear:**
- India's first nuclear test: **"Smiling Buddha"**, Pokhran, 1974.
- Second series of tests: **Pokhran-II (Operation Shakti)**, 1998.
- **BARC** (Bhabha Atomic Research Centre), Mumbai — premier nuclear research centre.

**Defence missiles (integrated guided missile programme):**
- **Agni** — surface-to-surface ballistic missile series.
- **Prithvi** — short-range surface-to-surface.
- **Akash** — surface-to-air.
- **Nag** — anti-tank.
- **Trishul** — short-range surface-to-air.

🔗 **Static–Dynamic Link:** The static facts above (ISRO's founding, Aryabhata, missile names) never change — but exams often link them to recent missions. For example, questions on Chandrayaan-3 (2023) still test the static fact that ISRO was founded in 1969 and that Aryabhata was the first satellite. Learn the static spine; current affairs will hang on it.

🧠 **Active Recall:** Arrange chronologically: Aryabhata, Chandrayaan-1, ISRO's establishment, Mangalyaan. (Answer: 1969 → 1975 → 2008 → 2013.)

## ⚠️ Caution: Examiner's Trap

**Trap 1 — "लगभग सही कथन" (Almost-Correct Statement)**
*Example:* "Sound travels fastest in gases." — Sounds plausible because we hear sound through air, but it is **false**: sound travels fastest in **solids**. Examiners flip one word in a true fact. Read every statement word by word.

**Trap 2 — "समान नाम/वर्ष" (Similar Names/Years)**
*Example:* Confusing **Edward Jenner** (smallpox vaccine) with **Alexander Fleming** (penicillin), or **Louis Pasteur** (rabies vaccine) with **Robert Koch** (TB bacterium). Also: Aryabhata (1975) vs. Bhaskara-I (1979). Make a personal "confusable pairs" list.

**Trap 3 — "नकारात्मक वाक्यांश" (Hidden Negative)**
*Example:* "Which of the following is **NOT** a vector quantity?" Students rush and pick the first vector they see. Circle the NOT/EXCEPT in the question before looking at options.

**Trap 4 — "विपरीत कार्य-कारण" (Reverse Causation)**
*Example:* "Diabetes causes insulin deficiency." — Actually, insulin deficiency/resistance **causes** diabetes. Examiners reverse cause and effect in biology and physics statements.
