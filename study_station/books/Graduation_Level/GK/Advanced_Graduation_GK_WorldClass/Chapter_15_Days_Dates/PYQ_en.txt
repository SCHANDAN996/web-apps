# 📊 PYQ Analysis — Important Days & Dates (English)

## 1. How Exams Ask Questions from This Chapter

Important Days & Dates is a **pure-recall, low-calculation** chapter. Across UPSC (Prelims), SSC (CGL/CHSL/GD), Banking (IBPS/SBI), Railways (NTPC/Group D) and State PCS prelims, questions from this chapter appear in a small but **remarkably predictable** band. The examiner does not test depth — he tests **precision of memory** and your ability to resist look-alike options.

The recurring question formats are:

1. **Direct date recall** — "When is National Voters' Day observed?" (One day, one date.)
2. **Day ↔ Theme matching** — especially for days whose *theme changes every year* (e.g., World Health Day, World Environment Day). Exams ask either the fixed date or the theme of a specific year.
3. **Founder / first-celebration year** — "In which year was International Yoga Day first observed?" (2015 — after the UNGA resolution of December 2014.)
4. **Chronology / ordering** — "Arrange these days in the order they fall in a year."
5. **Statement-based (UPSC/PCS style)** — two or three statements about a day, its proclaiming body (UN, WHO, UNESCO, Government of India), and its year of origin; you must pick the correct combination.
6. **Negative phrasing** — "Which of the following pairs is NOT correctly matched?"

## 2. Sub-Topics That Recur Most (in words, not numbers)

- **UN-declared international days** — the single richest vein. Environment Day (5 June), Yoga Day (21 June), Human Rights Day (10 December), Women's Day (8 March), Water Day (22 March), Earth Day (22 April), Literacy Day (8 September), Peace Day (21 September), Food Day (16 October), Children's Day (20 November, UN) — these are asked again and again across all exams.
- **Indian national days** — Republic Day (26 January), Independence Day (15 August), Gandhi Jayanti (2 October, *also* International Day of Non-Violence — a favourite trap), National Voters' Day (25 January), National Youth Day (12 January, Swami Vivekananda's birth anniversary), National Science Day (28 February, Raman Effect discovery), Kisan Diwas (23 December, Chaudhary Charan Singh's birth anniversary), Good Governance Day (25 December, Atal Bihari Vajpayee's birth anniversary), Constitution Day (26 November), Hindi Diwas (14 September), Engineer's Day (15 September, M. Visvesvaraya's birth anniversary), Teacher's Day (5 September, Dr. Radhakrishnan's birth anniversary).
- **Defence-related days** — Armed Forces Flag Day (7 December), Navy Day (4 December), Air Force Day (8 October), Army Day (15 January), Kargil Vijay Diwas (26 July), Vijay Diwas (16 December).
- **Health and awareness days** — World AIDS Day (1 December), World Cancer Day (4 February), World Health Day (7 April, WHO's founding day), Anti-Tobacco Day (31 May), World Blood Donor Day (14 June).
- **"First observed in which year" questions** — Yoga Day (2015), National Voters' Day (2011), Constitution Day (2015 as a formal observance), Good Governance Day (2014).

## 3. Exam-Wise Flavour (qualitative)

- **UPSC / State PCS**: statement-based, often linking a day to its **proclaiming body** (UNGA vs WHO vs UNESCO) or to a **theme of a particular year**. Rarely asks bare dates alone.
- **SSC (CGL/CHSL/GD)**: the heaviest direct-date question bank. Loves Indian national days and their **associated personalities** (whose birth/death anniversary is it?).
- **Banking (IBPS/SBI)**: prefers **themes of the current/recent year** and UN days; often clubbed with current affairs.
- **Railways (NTPC/Group D)**: simple one-liner date recall; defence days and national days dominate.

## 4. The Examiner's Traps (with examples)

**Trap 1 — Almost Correct (near-miss date):** Options like 21 June / 22 June / 21 July / 20 June for Yoga Day. The correct fact is known, but the option set is built from *plausible neighbours*.

**Trap 2 — Chronological Confusion:** Days that cluster together — e.g., World Environment Day (5 June), World Oceans Day (8 June), World Blood Donor Day (14 June), International Yoga Day (21 June). Exams swap these within June.

**Trap 3 — Same-name, different-day confusion:** Children's Day in India is **14 November** (Nehru's birthday); the UN's World Children's Day is **20 November**. Similarly, National Youth Day (12 January, India) vs International Youth Day (12 August, UN). This is the single most exploited confusion.

**Trap 4 — Dual identity:** Gandhi Jayanti (2 October) is *also* the International Day of Non-Violence; 24 October is UN Day *and* World Development Information Day. Examiners ask the "other" identity.

**Trap 5 — Negative Phrasing:** "Which pair is NOT correctly matched?" — three correct pairs lull you; the fourth has a one-day shift (e.g., National Science Day – 29 February instead of 28 February).

**Trap 6 — Founder/Year swap:** "International Yoga Day was first observed in 2014" (wrong — resolution was 2014, first observance 2015).

## 5. Top 10 High-Yield Questions (PYQ-style, with solutions)

**Q1.** International Yoga Day is observed on —
(a) 20 June (b) 21 June (c) 22 June (d) 21 July
**Answer:** (b)
**Solution:** 21 June — the summer solstice, the longest day in the Northern Hemisphere. First observed in **2015** after the UNGA resolution of December 2014, proposed by PM Narendra Modi. **Trap type:** Almost Correct. **Source:** PYQ-style.

**Q2.** National Science Day (28 February) commemorates —
(a) The launch of Aryabhata satellite (b) C.V. Raman's discovery of the Raman Effect (c) Homi Bhabha's birth anniversary (d) The establishment of ISRO
**Answer:** (b)
**Solution:** It marks the discovery of the **Raman Effect** on 28 February 1928, for which C.V. Raman won the Nobel Prize (1930). **Trap type:** Almost Correct (all options are real science milestones). **Source:** PYQ-style.

**Q3.** Which of the following pairs is NOT correctly matched?
(a) National Voters' Day – 25 January (b) Constitution Day – 26 November (c) Hindi Diwas – 14 September (d) National Youth Day – 14 January
**Answer:** (d)
**Solution:** National Youth Day is **12 January** (Swami Vivekananda's birth anniversary), not 14 January. The other three pairs are correct. **Trap type:** Negative Phrasing + one-day shift. **Source:** PYQ-style.

**Q4.** 2 October is observed internationally as —
(a) World Peace Day (b) International Day of Non-Violence (c) International Day of Tolerance (d) World Humanitarian Day
**Answer:** (b)
**Solution:** The UN declared Gandhi Jayanti (2 October) the **International Day of Non-Violence** in 2007. World Peace Day is 21 September — the classic distractor. **Trap type:** Dual identity. **Source:** PYQ-style.

**Q5.** World Environment Day is observed on —
(a) 22 April (b) 5 June (c) 22 March (d) 16 September
**Answer:** (b)
**Solution:** 5 June, led by UNEP since 1973 (Stockholm Conference legacy, 1972). 22 April is Earth Day, 22 March is World Water Day, 16 September is Ozone Day — all four are deliberately planted as environment-family confusables. **Trap type:** Chronological Confusion. **Source:** PYQ-style.

**Q6.** Kargil Vijay Diwas is observed on —
(a) 16 December (b) 26 July (c) 7 December (d) 15 January
**Answer:** (b)
**Solution:** 26 July marks the end of the Kargil conflict (Operation Vijay, 1999). 16 December is Vijay Diwas (1971 war) — the two "Vijay" days are a standard swap trap. **Trap type:** Same-name confusion. **Source:** PYQ-style.

**Q7.** National Voters' Day was first observed in —
(a) 2009 (b) 2010 (c) 2011 (d) 2015
**Answer:** (c)
**Solution:** First observed on **25 January 2011**, marking the foundation day of the Election Commission of India (25 January 1950). **Trap type:** Founder/Year swap. **Source:** PYQ-style.

**Q8.** World Health Day (7 April) marks —
(a) The adoption of the Universal Declaration of Human Rights (b) The founding of the World Health Organization in 1948 (c) The first polio vaccination campaign (d) The signing of the Alma-Ata Declaration
**Answer:** (b)
**Solution:** 7 April 1948 is the day the WHO Constitution came into force. **Trap type:** Almost Correct. **Source:** PYQ-style.

**Q9.** Consider the following statements:
1. International Day of Peace is observed on 21 September.
2. World Children's Day (UN) is observed on 14 November.
Which of the above is/are correct?
(a) 1 only (b) 2 only (c) Both 1 and 2 (d) Neither 1 nor 2
**Answer:** (a)
**Solution:** Statement 1 is correct. Statement 2 is the classic trap — **14 November is India's Children's Day** (Nehru's birthday); the UN's World Children's Day is **20 November**. **Trap type:** Same-name, different-day confusion. **Source:** PYQ-style.

**Q10.** Good Governance Day (25 December) is observed on the birth anniversary of —
(a) Pandit Madan Mohan Malaviya (b) Atal Bihari Vajpayee (c) Sardar Vallabhbhai Patel (d) Lal Bahadur Shastri
**Answer:** (b)
**Solution:** Observed since **2014** on Atal Bihari Vajpayee's birthday (Malaviya shares the date, which is itself a distractor). Kisan Diwas (23 December) honours Chaudhary Charan Singh — another December cluster trap. **Trap type:** Chronological Confusion. **Source:** PYQ-style.

## 6. The 15/45-Second Rule Applied

**Q1 (Yoga Day) — 15-second question.** The date 21 June is among the most memorised facts in Indian exam prep. If you know it, mark and move in under 15 seconds. **Do not** re-read the options — options (a), (c), (d) exist only to create doubt in a mind that hesitates. Spending 40 seconds here is a net loss.

**Q9 (Children's Day statements) — 45-second question.** Statement-based items demand a deliberate two-step check: (i) verify each statement *independently* before looking at combinations; (ii) specifically ask yourself "is this the Indian day or the UN day?" Budget the full 45 seconds — this is exactly the question type where rushing converts a known fact into a wrong answer.

**Rule of thumb for the chapter:** direct date recall = 15 seconds, no negotiation; statement/matching/negative-phrasing items = up to 45 seconds, because the trap is in the *construction*, not the fact.

## 7. Preparation Takeaways

1. Build a **month-wise master list** (January–December) — chronology questions become free marks.
2. Maintain a separate **"confusable pairs" list**: Children's Day (14 Nov vs 20 Nov), Youth Day (12 Jan vs 12 Aug), Vijay Diwas (16 Dec) vs Kargil Vijay Diwas (26 Jul), Earth Day (22 Apr) vs Environment Day (5 Jun).
3. For every day, learn **three hooks**: date, proclaiming body/first year, and associated person. Exams rotate among these three.
4. In negative-phrasing questions, verify **all four pairs** — the wrong pair is usually off by just one day.
