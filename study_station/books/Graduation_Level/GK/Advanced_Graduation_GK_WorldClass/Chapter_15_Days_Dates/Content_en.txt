# Important Days & Dates

## 🎯 Why This Chapter Can Win You 2–3 Free Marks

In 1947, when the United Nations was barely two years old, its founders faced a strange problem: how do you make 50+ nations, speaking dozens of languages, care about the same cause on the same day? Their answer was the "International Day" — a shared calendar of causes. Today, that calendar has become one of the **most predictable scoring areas** in SSC CGL, IBPS PO, RRB NTPC and State PCS prelims. The questions are direct, the facts never change, and yet most aspirants lose these marks — not because they don't know the days, but because they **confuse similar-looking dates**. This chapter fixes exactly that.

## 📌 Learning Objectives

By the end of this chapter, you will be able to:
1. Recall all high-yield national and international days with their exact dates.
2. Distinguish between confusing pairs (e.g., National Youth Day vs. International Youth Day).
3. Identify the **theme-based and chronology-based traps** examiners set.
4. Connect static days to their historical origins (the "why" behind the date).

## 1️⃣ January – March: The Year Opens with India-Heavy Dates

**January** is dominated by Indian observances:
- **9 January – Pravasi Bharatiya Divas (NRI Day)**: commemorates Mahatma Gandhi's return from South Africa to India on 9 January 1915.
- **12 January – National Youth Day**: birth anniversary of Swami Vivekananda (born 1863).
- **15 January – Army Day**: marks the day in 1949 when Field Marshal K.M. Cariappa took over as the first Indian Commander-in-Chief of the Indian Army.
- **25 January – National Voters' Day**: the Election Commission of India was established on 25 January 1950.
- **26 January – Republic Day**: the Constitution came into force on 26 January 1950. (This date was chosen because 26 January 1930 was observed as "Purna Swaraj Day.")
- **30 January – Martyrs' Day (Shaheed Diwas)**: Mahatma Gandhi was assassinated on 30 January 1948.

**February**:
- **4 February – World Cancer Day**
- **13 February – World Radio Day** (UNESCO; UN Radio was established on this date in 1946)
- **21 February – International Mother Language Day** (UNESCO; honours the 1952 language movement martyrs of Dhaka, Bangladesh)
- **28 February – National Science Day**: C.V. Raman announced the **Raman Effect** on 28 February 1928 (Nobel Prize 1930).

**March**:
- **8 March – International Women's Day**
- **15 March – World Consumer Rights Day** (J.F. Kennedy's consumer rights address, 1962)
- **22 March – World Water Day** (UN, since 1993)
- **23 March – Shaheed Diwas (Martyrs' Day)**: Bhagat Singh, Rajguru and Sukhdev were hanged on 23 March 1931. ⚠️ Note: India has **two** Martyrs' Days — 30 January and 23 March.
- **24 March – World Tuberculosis Day** (Robert Koch announced the discovery of the TB bacillus on 24 March 1882)

🧠 **Active Recall**: Close the book. Which two dates are both called Martyrs' Day in India, and whom does each honour? What event does National Science Day commemorate?

## 2️⃣ April – June: Environment, Health and Labour

- **7 April – World Health Day**: marks the founding of the **WHO in 1948**.
- **14 April – Ambedkar Jayanti** (birth anniversary of Dr. B.R. Ambedkar, born 1891)
- **22 April – Earth Day** (first observed in 1970)
- **1 May – International Labour Day / May Day**: commemorates the 1886 Haymarket affair in Chicago. Also **Maharashtra Day and Gujarat Day** (both states formed on 1 May 1960).
- **31 May – World No Tobacco Day** (WHO)
- **5 June – World Environment Day** (UN, since 1974; linked to the 1972 Stockholm Conference)
- **21 June – International Day of Yoga**: first observed in 2015 after India's proposal at the UN; 21 June is the summer solstice, the longest day in the Northern Hemisphere.
- **26 June – International Day against Drug Abuse and Illicit Trafficking**

🧠 **Active Recall**: Yoga Day is on 21 June — but in which year was it first celebrated? Which day falls on 1 May besides Labour Day?

## 3️⃣ July – September: Independence Season and Literacy

- **11 July – World Population Day** (inspired by the "Day of Five Billion," 11 July 1987)
- **15 August – Independence Day** (1947)
- **19 August – World Photography Day**; also **World Humanitarian Day** (commemorates the 2003 UN headquarters bombing in Baghdad)
- **29 August – National Sports Day**: birth anniversary of hockey legend **Major Dhyan Chand** (born 1905).
- **5 September – Teachers' Day (India)**: birth anniversary of Dr. Sarvepalli Radhakrishnan (born 1888). ⚠️ **World Teachers' Day is 5 October** (UNESCO) — a classic confusing pair.
- **8 September – International Literacy Day** (UNESCO)
- **14 September – Hindi Diwas**: the Constituent Assembly adopted Hindi in Devanagari script as the official language of the Union on 14 September 1949.
- **15 September – Engineer's Day (India)**: birth anniversary of Sir M. Visvesvaraya (born 1861). Also **International Day of Democracy** (UN).
- **16 September – International Day for the Preservation of the Ozone Layer** (Montreal Protocol signed 16 September 1987)

🧠 **Active Recall**: Write down the difference between Teachers' Day (India) and World Teachers' Day. Why is Hindi Diwas on 14 September?

## 4️⃣ October – December: UN, Gandhi and Human Rights

- **2 October – Gandhi Jayanti** (born 1869) — also **International Day of Non-Violence** (UN, since 2007). Also **Lal Bahadur Shastri Jayanti** (born 1904).
- **8 October – Indian Air Force Day** (IAF established 8 October 1932)
- **9 October – World Post Day** (Universal Postal Union established 1874)
- **16 October – World Food Day** (FAO founded 16 October 1945)
- **24 October – United Nations Day** (UN Charter came into force 24 October 1945)
- **31 October – National Unity Day (Rashtriya Ekta Diwas)**: birth anniversary of Sardar Vallabhbhai Patel (born 1875).
- **14 November – Children's Day (India)**: birth anniversary of Jawaharlal Nehru (born 1889). ⚠️ **World Children's Day is 20 November** (UN).
- **26 November – Constitution Day (Samvidhan Divas)**: the Constitution was **adopted** on 26 November 1949 (it came into force on 26 January 1950 — don't mix these up!). Also **National Law Day**.
- **1 December – World AIDS Day**
- **4 December – Navy Day** (commemorates Operation Trident, 1971 war)
- **7 December – Armed Forces Flag Day**
- **10 December – Human Rights Day** (Universal Declaration of Human Rights adopted 10 December 1948)
- **23 December – National Farmers' Day (Kisan Diwas)**: birth anniversary of former PM Chaudhary Charan Singh (born 1902).

🧠 **Active Recall**: Constitution adopted vs. Constitution enforced — which dates? Which two leaders share 2 October as their birth anniversary?

## 5️⃣ Mnemonics That Actually Work

**Mnemonic 1 — "Vivekananda's Youth at 12"**: National **Youth** Day = **12** January (Swami Vivekananda's birthday). Contrast: International Youth Day = **12 August**. Remember: *"12-12 Youth Twins — Jan for India, Aug for the World."*

**Mnemonic 2 — "Gandhi's 2-30 Rule"**: Gandhi Jayanti = **2** October; Martyrs' Day (Gandhi's death) = **30** January. *"Born on 2, martyred on 30."*

**Mnemonic 3 — "WHO-FAO-UN: 7-16-24"**: World **H**ealth Day = **7** April (WHO), World **F**ood Day = **16** October (FAO), **UN** Day = **24** October. *"Health at 7, Food at 16, Nations at 24."*

**Mnemonic 4 — "Science on 28, Water on 22"**: National Science Day = **28** February; World Water Day = **22** March. *"Feb ends with science (28), March flows with water (22)."*

## ⚠️ Caution: Examiner's Trap

**Trap 1 — Similar Names / Similar Dates (समान नाम/तिथि का भ्रम)**
The examiner's favourite weapon is the "twin pair": National Youth Day (12 Jan) vs. International Youth Day (12 Aug); Teachers' Day India (5 Sep) vs. World Teachers' Day (5 Oct); Children's Day India (14 Nov) vs. World Children's Day (20 Nov). In the options, both dates of the pair will appear. **Rule: if the question says "National/India," pick the Indian personality's birthday; if it says "World/International," pick the UN date.**

**Trap 2 — Chronological Confusion (कालानुक्रमिक भ्रम)**
"Constitution Day is celebrated on 26 January because the Constitution was adopted on this day." — This statement is **90% correct-sounding but doubly wrong**: Constitution Day is 26 **November**, and 26 January marks **enforcement**, not adoption. Examiners love swapping "adopted" and "came into force."

**Trap 3 — Negative Phrasing (नकारात्मक वाक्यांश)**
"Which of the following is **NOT** correctly matched?" — with pairs like (a) Yoga Day–21 June (b) Earth Day–22 April (c) Ozone Day–16 September (d) Human Rights Day–12 December. Three are right; one date is shifted by two days. Under time pressure, students misread "NOT" and pick the first correct pair. **Always circle the word NOT/EXCEPT before reading options.**

## 🔗 Static-Dynamic Link

Static dates often re-enter the news through **annual themes**. For example, every World Environment Day (5 June) and International Yoga Day (21 June) is celebrated with a new theme and host country/city — examiners frequently ask the *current year's theme* in the same question slot where they previously asked the static date. When you revise this chapter, also glance at the current year's themes for 4–5 major UN days via the official UN website (un.org/en/observances).
