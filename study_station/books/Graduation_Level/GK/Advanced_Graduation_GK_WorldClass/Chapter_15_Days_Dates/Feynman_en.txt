# 🧒 The Feynman Corner — Important Days & Dates

## The Most Confusing Concept: "Fixed Date" vs "Floating Day"

Ask any aspirant: *"When is National Voters' Day?"* — quick answer: **25 January**. Easy.
Now ask: *"When is World Leprosy Day?"* — silence. Because it is **not a fixed date** — it is observed on the **last Sunday of January** (in India, anti-leprosy day is also marked on 30 January, Gandhi's martyrdom day).

This is the single biggest trap in this chapter: **some days fall on the same date every year, while others "float" — tied to a weekday (second Sunday, last Friday, first Monday).** Examiners love mixing the two.

## Explaining It Like You're 12

Think of your birthday. It falls on a **fixed date** — say 15 August — every single year, no matter what day of the week it is. That's how **Republic Day (26 January)** or **World Environment Day (5 June)** work.

Now think of **"the second Sunday of May"** (Mother's Day). It has no fixed date — in one year it's 12 May, in another 14 May. It *floats* with the calendar. Some important days work exactly like this:

**Floating days (tied to a weekday):**

| Day | Rule |
|---|---|
| World Leprosy Day | Last Sunday of January |
| Mother's Day | Second Sunday of May |
| Father's Day | Third Sunday of June |

**Fixed days (same date every year):**

| Day | Date |
|---|---|
| National Safe Motherhood Day | 11 April |
| World Thalassemia Day | 8 May |
| Engineer's Day (India) | 15 September (M. Visvesvaraya's birthday) |

**Daily-life example:** Imagine two friends. Ravi celebrates his birthday on 10 March *always*. Meena celebrates hers on "the first Sunday of March." If you memorise both as "March," you'll get Meena's wrong every year. Exams do exactly this to you.

## The Unforgettable Trick

> **"Date is fate, weekday floats."**
>
> If the day's name contains a **person, event, or number** (Gandhi, Visvesvaraya, Constitution, Voters) → it's almost always a **fixed date**.
> If the day is about a **cause or awareness campaign** (leprosy, safety weeks) → check whether it's a **floating weekday** before answering.

Second trick for confusing pairs:
> **"Health before AIDS, Nurses before Doctors."**
> World Health Day = **7 April** (WHO founded 1948), World AIDS Day = **1 December**. National Doctors' Day = **1 July** (Dr. B.C. Roy), International Nurses Day = **12 May** (Florence Nightingale's birthday). Birthdays of real people = fixed dates — always.
