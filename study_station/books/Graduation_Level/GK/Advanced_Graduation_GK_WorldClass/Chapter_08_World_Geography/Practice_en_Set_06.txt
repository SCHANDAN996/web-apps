126. Which of the following pairs of ocean currents is correctly matched with its nature?
(a) Benguela Current — Cold (b) Labrador Current — Warm (c) Kuroshio Current — Cold (d) Oyashio Current — Warm
Answer: (a)
Solution: The tempting trap is the Labrador Current, which sounds tropical but is actually a cold current flowing down from the Arctic along Canada's coast. The Kuroshio is warm and the Oyashio is cold, so those pairs are reversed. The Benguela Current off south-west Africa is genuinely cold, so the answer is Benguela Current — Cold.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Geography (Fundamentals of Physical Geography)

127. The Great Barrier Reef, the world's largest coral reef system, is located off the coast of which Australian state?
(a) Western Australia (b) Queensland (c) New South Wales (d) Victoria
Answer: (b)
Solution: Many students pick Western Australia because of the Ningaloo Reef there, which is a similar-feature trap. The Great Barrier Reef stretches over 2,300 km along the north-eastern coast of Australia, off Queensland, so the answer is Queensland.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Geography

128. Which of the following seas is almost completely landlocked and connects to the Atlantic Ocean only through the narrow Strait of Gibraltar?
(a) Mediterranean Sea (b) Caspian Sea (c) Red Sea (d) Black Sea
Answer: (a)
Solution: The Black Sea is a trap because it is also nearly enclosed, but it connects via the Bosphorus to the Mediterranean, not directly to the Atlantic at Gibraltar. The Caspian is a lake with no ocean connection. The sea linked to the Atlantic by the Strait of Gibraltar is the Mediterranean Sea, so the answer is Mediterranean Sea.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Geography

129. The 'Ring of Fire', a zone of frequent earthquakes and volcanic eruptions, is associated with which ocean?
(a) Atlantic Ocean (b) Indian Ocean (c) Arctic Ocean (d) Pacific Ocean
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is the Indian Ocean because of the 2004 tsunami, but that was a single subduction event, not a volcanic arc belt. The Ring of Fire is the horseshoe-shaped belt of plate boundaries around the Pacific Ocean, so the answer is Pacific Ocean.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Geography (Fundamentals of Physical Geography)

130. Which strait separates the continents of Asia and North America?
(a) Bering Strait (b) Davis Strait (c) Bosphorus Strait (d) Malacca Strait
Answer: (a)
Solution: Davis Strait is a similar-name trap — it lies between Greenland and Canada, both in North America. The Bosphorus separates Europe and Asia within Turkey. The strait between Russia (Asia) and Alaska (North America) is the Bering Strait, so the answer is Bering Strait.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Geography

131. The world's deepest oceanic trench, the Mariana Trench, lies in which part of the Pacific Ocean?
(a) Eastern Pacific, off Peru (b) North-eastern Pacific, near Alaska (c) South Pacific, near New Zealand (d) Western Pacific, east of the Philippines
Answer: (d)
Solution: The Peru-Chile Trench off South America is the trap, as it is also very deep but not the deepest. The Mariana Trench, containing the Challenger Deep (about 10,900 m), lies in the western Pacific east of the Philippines and the Mariana Islands, so the answer is Western Pacific, east of the Philippines.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Geography

132. Which of the following countries does the Tropic of Capricorn NOT pass through?
(a) Brazil (b) Australia (c) Argentina (d) Zambia
Answer: (d)
Solution: This is a negative-wording trap — three of the four are correct, so read carefully. The Tropic of Capricorn crosses Brazil, Argentina, Chile, Paraguay, Australia, Namibia, Botswana, South Africa, Mozambique and Madagascar. Zambia lies entirely north of the line, so the answer is Zambia.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Geography

133. The 'Roaring Forties' refer to:
(a) Frequent cyclones in the Bay of Bengal (b) A period of rapid industrial growth in Europe (c) Warm ocean currents near 40°N latitude (d) Strong westerly winds in the Southern Hemisphere between 40°S and 50°S
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is the cyclone option, since the term sounds like violent storms. The Roaring Forties are the powerful, unobstructed westerly winds blowing between latitudes 40°S and 50°S, where little land blocks them, so the answer is strong westerly winds in the Southern Hemisphere between 40°S and 50°S.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Geography

134. Which of the following rivers crosses the Equator twice?
(a) Mekong (b) Amazon (c) Nile (d) Congo
Answer: (d)
Solution: The Amazon is the tempting trap because it lies along the Equator, but it flows roughly east-west and does not cross it twice. The Congo River arcs north and then south of the Equator, crossing it two times, so the answer is Congo.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Geography

135. The Pampas, a vast temperate grassland, is primarily located in which country?
(a) South Africa (b) Argentina (c) Kazakhstan (d) Canada
Answer: (b)
Solution: South Africa is the trap because it has the Velds, a similar temperate grassland — a classic similar-feature confusion. Kazakhstan has steppes and Canada has prairies. The Pampas are the fertile grasslands of Argentina (and Uruguay), so the answer is Argentina.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Geography

136. Which of the following statements about the Dead Sea is correct?
(a) It is the saltiest water body on Earth (b) It is located between Egypt and Sudan (c) It lies at the lowest land elevation on Earth (d) It drains into the Mediterranean Sea
Answer: (c)
Solution: The saltiest-water claim is the almost-correct trap — the Dead Sea is extremely saline, but hypersaline water bodies like Don Juan Pond in Antarctica are saltier. It lies between Jordan and Israel and has no outlet. Its surface, about 430 m below sea level, is the lowest land point on Earth, so the answer is that it lies at the lowest land elevation on Earth.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Geography

137. The 'Foehn' and 'Chinook' are examples of:
(a) Cold polar winds (b) Monsoon winds (c) Sea breezes (d) Warm, dry local winds descending mountain slopes
Answer: (d)
Solution: The cold-wind option is a trap because winds descending mountains are often assumed cold, like the Mistral. The Foehn (Alps) and Chinook (Rockies) are warm, dry winds that descend the leeward side of mountains after losing moisture on the windward side, so the answer is warm, dry local winds descending mountain slopes.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Geography

138. Which country is the largest producer of coffee in the world?
(a) Colombia (b) Vietnam (c) Brazil (d) Ethiopia
Answer: (c)
Solution: Colombia is the classic trap because of its famous coffee branding, and Vietnam leads in robusta specifically. But by total production, Brazil has been the world's largest coffee producer for over a century, so the answer is Brazil.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Geography (Fundamentals of Human Geography)

139. The Strait of Malacca connects which two water bodies?
(a) Andaman Sea (Indian Ocean) and South China Sea (Pacific Ocean) (b) Red Sea and Arabian Sea (c) Mediterranean Sea and Black Sea (d) Persian Gulf and Gulf of Oman
Answer: (a)
Solution: The Persian Gulf option is a trap because it describes the Strait of Hormuz, another famous chokepoint. The Strait of Malacca, between Malaysia and Indonesia's Sumatra, links the Andaman Sea with the South China Sea, so the answer is Andaman Sea (Indian Ocean) and South China Sea (Pacific Ocean).
Source: NCERT Class 11 Geography

140. Which of the following African countries is landlocked?
(a) Kenya (b) Zambia (c) Mozambique (d) Somalia
Answer: (b)
Solution: Somalia is the trap — its long coastline on the Horn of Africa is easy to forget. Kenya and Mozambique both have Indian Ocean coasts. Zambia, in south-central Africa, is surrounded by other countries with no coastline of its own, so the answer is Zambia.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Geography

141. The 'Midnight Sun' phenomenon can be observed in which of the following countries?
(a) Japan (b) Norway (c) New Zealand (d) Brazil
Answer: (b)
Solution: Japan is the trap because it is called the 'Land of the Rising Sun', a similar-name confusion with a different meaning. The Midnight Sun occurs beyond the Arctic and Antarctic Circles in summer; northern Norway lies within the Arctic Circle, so the answer is Norway.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Geography

142. Which of the following is the correct order of the Earth's atmospheric layers from the surface upward?
(a) Troposphere, Mesosphere, Stratosphere, Thermosphere, Exosphere (b) Stratosphere, Troposphere, Mesosphere, Exosphere, Thermosphere (c) Troposphere, Stratosphere, Mesosphere, Thermosphere, Exosphere (d) Troposphere, Stratosphere, Thermosphere, Mesosphere, Exosphere
Answer: (c)
Solution: The last option is the trap — it swaps the mesosphere and thermosphere, a common sequence confusion. The correct order upward is troposphere, stratosphere, mesosphere, thermosphere, exosphere, so the answer is Troposphere, Stratosphere, Mesosphere, Thermosphere, Exosphere.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Geography (Fundamentals of Physical Geography)

143. The world's largest hot desert, the Sahara, is roughly equal in area to which of the following?
(a) Western Europe (b) India (c) The contiguous United States (d) Australia
Answer: (c)
Solution: Australia is the trap because it is also famously arid, but it is much smaller. The Sahara covers about 9 million sq km, comparable to the area of the contiguous United States, so the answer is the contiguous United States.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Geography

144. Which of the following pairs of a country and its capital is INCORRECTLY matched?
(a) Canada — Ottawa (b) Switzerland — Bern (c) Australia — Sydney (d) New Zealand — Wellington
Answer: (c)
Solution: This is a negative-wording trap asking for the wrong pair. Sydney is Australia's largest city and a very common wrong guess, but Australia's capital is Canberra. Ottawa, Bern and Wellington are all correct capitals, so the answer is Australia — Sydney.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Geography

145. The 'El Niño' phenomenon is associated with:
(a) Unusual warming of the central and eastern equatorial Pacific Ocean (b) Melting of Arctic sea ice (c) Unusual cooling of the eastern Pacific Ocean (d) Strengthening of the Indian monsoon every year
Answer: (a)
Solution: The cooling option is the trap — that describes La Niña, the opposite phase, a classic paired-concept confusion. El Niño is the periodic warming of surface waters in the central and eastern equatorial Pacific, often weakening the Indian monsoon, so the answer is unusual warming of the central and eastern equatorial Pacific Ocean.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Geography

146. Which of the following mountain ranges is the longest above-water mountain range in the world?
(a) Andes (b) Himalayas (c) Rockies (d) Alps
Answer: (a)
Solution: The Himalayas are the trap because they are the highest, and students often confuse 'highest' with 'longest'. The Andes stretch about 7,000 km along South America's western edge, making them the longest continental mountain range, so the answer is Andes.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Geography

147. The 'Great Lakes' of North America include all of the following EXCEPT:
(a) Lake Michigan (b) Lake Great Bear (c) Lake Superior (d) Lake Huron
Answer: (b)
Solution: This is a negative-wording trap. Lake Great Bear is a large Canadian lake, which makes it sound plausible, but it is not one of the five Great Lakes. The five are Superior, Michigan, Huron, Erie and Ontario, so the answer is Lake Great Bear.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Geography

148. Which of the following countries has the largest number of time zones?
(a) France (b) United States (c) Russia (d) China
Answer: (a)
Solution: Russia is the strong trap because it spans 11 time zones, the most of any single contiguous landmass. But France, counting its overseas territories spread across the globe, covers 12 time zones — the most of any country, so the answer is France.

149. The 'Bamboo Curtain' historically referred to the political boundary associated with which country?
(a) Soviet Union (b) China (c) North Korea (d) Vietnam
Answer: (b)
Solution: The Soviet Union is the trap because of the similar-sounding 'Iron Curtain', a classic similar-name confusion. The 'Bamboo Curtain' referred to the Cold War isolation of communist China, so the answer is China.
Source: PYQ-style

150. Which of the following statements about the International Date Line is correct?
(a) It is a perfectly straight line along 180° longitude (b) Crossing it westward subtracts one calendar day (c) It passes through the Bering Strait and deviates to avoid splitting countries (d) It coincides exactly with the Prime Meridian
Answer: (c)
Solution: The straight-line claim is the almost-correct trap — the Date Line roughly follows 180° but zigzags to keep island nations and territories on the same day. Crossing westward adds a day, not subtracts. It passes through the Bering Strait with deviations, so the answer is that it passes through the Bering Strait and deviates to avoid splitting countries.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Geography
