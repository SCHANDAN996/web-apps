Card 1
Front: Which is the only continent through which the Equator, Tropic of Cancer, and Tropic of Capricorn all pass?
Back: Africa. The Equator crosses its middle, the Tropic of Cancer crosses the Sahara in the north, and the Tropic of Capricorn crosses Namibia, Botswana, and South Africa in the south.

Card 2
Front: Which ocean is the deepest, and what is its deepest point called?
Back: The Pacific Ocean; its deepest point is the Mariana Trench (Challenger Deep), about 11,000 metres deep, near Guam.

Card 3
Front: Which is the largest desert in the world — and is it hot or cold?
Back: The Antarctic Desert (a cold desert) is the largest. Among hot deserts, the Sahara is the largest. Examiners exploit this hot/cold confusion.

Card 4
Front: Which is the longest river in the world, and which river carries the largest volume of water?
Back: The Nile (about 6,650 km) is the longest; the Amazon carries the largest discharge of water. Exams often swap these two to trap you.

Card 5
Front: Mnemonic trick: How can you quickly recall the five Great Lakes of North America?
Back: Use "HOMES" — Huron, Ontario, Michigan, Erie, Superior. Lake Superior is the largest freshwater lake by surface area.

Card 6
Front: Which is the largest landlocked country in the world?
Back: Kazakhstan. A common trap answer is Mongolia, which is landlocked but smaller in area.

Card 7
Front: Which strait separates Asia from North America?
Back: The Bering Strait, separating Russia (Siberia) from Alaska (USA). The International Date Line passes through it.

Card 8
Front: Which is the highest waterfall in the world, and where is it located?
Back: Angel Falls (about 979 m), located in Venezuela on the Churún River, a tributary of the Caroní.

Card 9
Front: Examiner's trap: "The Tropic of Capricorn passes through Australia, Brazil, and South Africa" — is this fully correct?
Back: Yes, it is correct — but the trap version replaces one country (e.g., "Peru"). The Tropic of Capricorn passes through Chile and Argentina too, but NOT Peru. Always verify each country listed.

Card 10
Front: Which is the deepest lake in the world, and roughly how much of the world's fresh surface water does it hold?
Back: Lake Baikal (Russia), about 1,642 m deep, holding roughly one-fifth of the world's unfrozen fresh surface water.

Card 11
Front: Which is the only sea in the world without a coastline (land boundary)?
Back: The Sargasso Sea in the North Atlantic, bounded only by ocean currents. It is named after Sargassum seaweed.

Card 12
Front: Mnemonic trick: How do you remember the order of the continents by area (largest to smallest)?
Back: Asia, Africa, North America, South America, Antarctica, Europe, Australia. Remember: Europe is 6th and Australia is the smallest — a classic exam swap is placing Australia before Europe.

Card 13
Front: Which mountain range is the longest in the world above sea level, and which is the longest overall?
Back: The Andes (South America, about 7,000 km) is the longest above sea level; the mid-ocean ridge system is the longest overall (about 65,000 km, mostly underwater).

Card 14
Front: Which country has the most time zones, and why is the obvious answer (Russia) wrong?
Back: France, with 12 time zones, because of its overseas territories spread across the globe. Russia has 11 — the "obvious" trap answer.

Card 15
Front: What is the Ring of Fire, and why does it matter for exams?
Back: A horseshoe-shaped zone around the Pacific Ocean with frequent earthquakes and volcanic eruptions; about 75% of the world's active volcanoes lie here. Questions link it to Japan, Indonesia, and Chile.

Card 16
Front: Which is the smallest ocean, and which is the shallowest?
Back: The Arctic Ocean is both the smallest and the shallowest of the five oceans.

Card 17
Front: Examiner's trap: "The Dead Sea is the lowest point on Earth's land surface" — true or false, and what is the common confusion?
Back: True — its shore is about 430 m below sea level. The trap: confusing it with Death Valley (lowest in North America) or calling the Dead Sea a "sea" — it is actually a salt lake between Israel and Jordan.

Card 18
Front: Do the Equator and the Prime Meridian meet on land in any African country?
Back: No — they meet in the Gulf of Guinea (Atlantic Ocean), off the coast of West Africa. The trap option is usually Ghana: Ghana lies close to the Prime Meridian but entirely north of the Equator, so it is crossed by neither line's meeting point.

Card 19
Front: Which is the largest island in the world, and why is Australia not counted?
Back: Greenland (Denmark). Australia is classified as a continent, not an island — a favourite examiner distinction.

Card 20
Front: Which canal connects the Atlantic and Pacific Oceans, and which connects the Mediterranean to the Red Sea?
Back: The Panama Canal connects the Atlantic and Pacific; the Suez Canal connects the Mediterranean Sea to the Red Sea. Exams often swap the two.
