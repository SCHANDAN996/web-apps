# 🧒 Feynman Technique — World Geography (Basic)

## The Concept Everyone Gets Wrong: Why Do We Have Seasons?

Ask most students why summer is hot, and they will say: *"Because the Earth is closer to the Sun."* It sounds logical. It is also **completely wrong** — and examiners know it. This is one of the most frequently trapped facts in SSC, Banking and PCS prelims.

### Explain It Like I'm 12

Imagine you are sitting in front of a room heater on a winter night.

- If you sit **straight, facing the heater**, your face gets maximum heat.
- If you **tilt your chair back** and look at the ceiling, the same heater now hits your body at a slant — the heat spreads over a larger area, and you feel less warm.

The heater never moved. **You** tilted. That is exactly what Earth does.

Earth's axis is tilted at about **23.5° from the perpendicular to its orbital plane** (the plane on which Earth revolves around the Sun). As Earth revolves around the Sun in one year:

- When the **Northern Hemisphere tilts toward** the Sun, sunlight falls on it more directly (like facing the heater) → **summer** in India, while the Southern Hemisphere gets slanting rays → winter in Australia.
- Six months later, the Northern Hemisphere tilts **away** → winter for us, summer for Australia.

### The Killer Fact (This Is the Trap)

Earth is actually **closest to the Sun (perihelion) around 3–4 January** — in the middle of *our winter*! And **farthest (aphelion) around 4 July** — in *our summer*. If distance caused seasons, January would be India's hottest month. It isn't. So:

> **Seasons are caused by the TILT of the axis + revolution, NOT by distance from the Sun.**

### A Concrete Daily-Life Check

On Christmas Day (25 December), people in Delhi wear sweaters while people in Sydney, Australia enjoy summer weather. Same planet, same distance from the Sun, opposite seasons. Distance cannot explain this. Tilt can.

### The Unforgettable Trick

**"TILT, not TRIP."**
The seasons depend on Earth's **tilt** (23.5°), not on the **trip** (how far along the orbit / how close to the Sun) Earth has travelled.

Memory hook for the dates:
- **21 June** — Summer Solstice: Sun directly over the **Tropic of Cancer** (longest day in Northern Hemisphere).
- **22 December** — Winter Solstice: Sun directly over the **Tropic of Capricorn**.
- **21 March & 23 September** — Equinoxes: Sun directly over the **Equator**; day = night everywhere ("equi-nox" = equal night).

Trick: **"Cancer cooks us in June, Capricorn cools us in December."**

### Why Examiners Love This

They will offer you four options where three mention "distance from the Sun" or swap the solstice dates and tropics. A student who memorised without understanding falls; a student who understood the heater example smiles and moves on in 15 seconds.
