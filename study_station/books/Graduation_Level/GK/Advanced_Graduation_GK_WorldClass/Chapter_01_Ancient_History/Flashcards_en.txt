Card 1
Front: Which Harappan site yielded the famous bronze "Dancing Girl" statue, and which technique was used to cast it?
Back: Mohenjo-daro; it was cast using the lost-wax (cire perdue) technique.

Card 2
Front: The Rig Veda was composed in which region, and what was the area called in the text itself?
Back: The Sapta Sindhu region (land of seven rivers) — the Punjab–Haryana–western UP belt.

Card 3
Front: Which Veda is a collection of melodies/chants meant for singing during sacrifices?
Back: The Sama Veda — its verses are largely drawn from the Rig Veda and set to music.

Card 4
Front: Memory trick — how do you recall the order of the four Vedas?
Back: "RiYaSaA" (Rig → Yajur → Sama → Atharva). Rig is the oldest; Atharva the latest.

Card 5
Front: Where did Buddha deliver his first sermon, and what is that event called?
Back: At Sarnath (near Varanasi); it is called the Dhammachakkapavattana — "Turning of the Wheel of Dharma."

Card 6
Front: Mahavira and Buddha were contemporaries — but who died first, and where did each attain nirvana?
Back: Buddha died first — his mahaparinirvana (c. 483 BCE) was at Kushinagar. Mahavira attained nirvana later (c. 468 BCE) at Pavapuri near Rajgriha.

Card 7
Front: Examiner's trap — "The Fourth Buddhist Council was held at Kundalavana, Kashmir under Kanishka." True or false, and what is the usual trap?
Back: True. The trap: examiners swap it with the First Council (Rajgriha, under Ajatashatru) or Third (Pataliputra, under Ashoka). Fix the pairs: 1st–Rajgriha, 2nd–Vaishali, 3rd–Pataliputra, 4th–Kashmir.

Card 8
Front: Which Greek ambassador wrote "Indica" and at whose court did he serve?
Back: Megasthenes, at the court of Chandragupta Maurya (Pataliputra).

Card 9
Front: Which Ashokan inscription expresses his remorse after the Kalinga War?
Back: Major Rock Edict XIII (13) — it describes the suffering caused by the Kalinga War (261 BCE).

Card 10
Front: Examiner's trap — "Ashoka's inscriptions were deciphered by Alexander Cunningham." What is wrong?
Back: The Brahmi script of Ashoka's edicts was deciphered by James Prinsep (1837). Cunningham was the first Director-General of the Archaeological Survey — a classic name-swap trap.

Card 11
Front: Who founded the Mauryan Empire, and which text by Kautilya guides us on its administration?
Back: Chandragupta Maurya (c. 322 BCE, with Chanakya's help); Kautilya's Arthashastra.

Card 12
Front: Which dynasty immediately succeeded the Mauryas, and who was its founder?
Back: The Shunga dynasty, founded by Pushyamitra Shunga (who killed the last Mauryan king Brihadratha).

Card 13
Front: Kanishka started which era in 78 CE, and which school of art flourished under him?
Back: The Shaka era (78 CE); the Gandhara school of art (Greco-Buddhist style) flourished under him.

Card 14
Front: Which Gupta ruler is called the "Napoleon of India," and by whom?
Back: Samudragupta — so called by historian V.A. Smith, based on his conquests recorded in the Allahabad Pillar Inscription (Prayaga Prashasti) by Harisena.

Card 15
Front: Memory trick — how do you recall the sequence of the 16 Mahajanapadas' most famous four?
Back: "A-K-M-V" — Avanti, Kosala, Magadha, Vatsa — the four that fought for supremacy, with Magadha finally winning.

Card 16
Front: Which Chinese pilgrims visited India during the reigns of Chandragupta II and Harshavardhana respectively?
Back: Fa-Hien (Fa-Xian) during Chandragupta II's reign; Hiuen Tsang (Xuanzang) during Harshavardhana's reign.

Card 17
Front: The Iron Pillar at Mehrauli, which has not rusted for over 1,500 years, is associated with which ruler?
Back: Chandragupta II (Vikramaditya) — it bears an inscription to a king "Chandra," identified with him.

Card 18
Front: Which Sangam-era Tamil epic tells the story of Kannagi and Kovalan, and who wrote it?
Back: Silappadikaram, written by Ilango Adigal. (Note: the title "fifth Veda" is traditionally given to the Mahabharata, not to this epic.)

Card 19
Front: Examiner's trap — "Ajanta paintings depict only Buddhist themes, while Ellora has only Hindu caves." What is the trap?
Back: Half-trap: Ajanta is exclusively Buddhist, but Ellora has Buddhist, Hindu AND Jain caves (including the Kailasa temple, Cave 16, built by Rashtrakuta Krishna I). Examiners exploit the "only" word.

Card 20
Front: Who was the last ruler of the Nanda dynasty, defeated by Chandragupta Maurya?
Back: Dhana Nanda — the Nandas were the first non-Kshatriya dynasty of Magadha, founded by Mahapadma Nanda.
