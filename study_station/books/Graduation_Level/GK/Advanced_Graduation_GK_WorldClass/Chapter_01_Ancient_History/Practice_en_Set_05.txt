101. The Harappan site of Dholavira is located in which present-day state?
(a) Rajasthan (b) Gujarat (c) Haryana (d) Punjab
Answer: (b)
Solution: Dholavira lies on Khadir Bet island in the Rann of Kutch, Gujarat, and is famous for its water reservoirs. Rajasthan is a trap because Kalibangan, another Harappan site, is in Rajasthan.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Themes in Indian History Part I

102. Which Veda is a collection of melodies and chants used in sacrifices?
(a) Rigveda (b) Atharvaveda (c) Samaveda (d) Yajurveda
Answer: (c)
Solution: The Samaveda sets Rigvedic hymns to music for ritual chanting. The Atharvaveda contains spells and medicine — a common confusion trap.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Themes in Indian History Part I

103. The famous Gayatri Mantra is found in which Veda?
(a) Atharvaveda (b) Yajurveda (c) Samaveda (d) Rigveda
Answer: (d)
Solution: The Gayatri Mantra, addressed to the solar deity Savitri, appears in the Rigveda (Mandala 3). Do not assume it belongs to the later Atharvaveda merely because of its ritual use today.

104. The first Buddhist Council after the Buddha's death was held at:
(a) Vaishali (b) Rajagriha (c) Pataliputra (d) Kashmir
Answer: (b)
Solution: The First Council (c. 483 BCE) met at Rajagriha under Ajatashatru. Vaishali hosted the Second Council — a classic chronological confusion trap.

105. Who was the founder of the Mauryan Empire?
(a) Dasharatha (b) Bindusara (c) Ashoka (d) Chandragupta Maurya
Answer: (d)
Solution: Chandragupta Maurya founded the empire c. 322 BCE with Chanakya's guidance, overthrowing the Nandas. Ashoka was his grandson — a similar-names trap.

106. Megasthenes, who wrote 'Indica', was an ambassador in the court of:
(a) Chandragupta Maurya (b) Bindusara (c) Ashoka (d) Chandragupta I
Answer: (a)
Solution: Megasthenes was the Seleucid ambassador to Chandragupta Maurya's court at Pataliputra. Chandragupta I (Gupta ruler) is a deliberate similar-name trap — the two lived about 600 years apart.

107. Ashoka's inscriptions were deciphered for the first time by:
(a) James Prinsep (b) William Jones (c) Alexander Cunningham (d) Max Muller
Answer: (a)
Solution: James Prinsep deciphered the Brahmi script of Ashokan edicts in 1837. Cunningham founded the Archaeological Survey; Max Muller translated Sanskrit texts — a frequent mix-up.

108. The Kalinga War, which transformed Ashoka, was fought in which regnal year of Ashoka?
(a) 4th year (b) 8th year (c) 12th year (d) 13th year
Answer: (b)
Solution: Rock Edict XIII states the Kalinga War occurred in the 8th year of Ashoka's consecration (c. 261 BCE). The 13th year is a trap — it is the edict number, not the regnal year.

109. Which dynasty ruled Magadha immediately before the Mauryas?
(a) Shishunaga (b) Nanda (c) Haryanka (d) Kanva
Answer: (b)
Solution: The Nanda dynasty, with Mahapadma Nanda and his sons, ruled Magadha before Chandragupta Maurya overthrew them. The Haryankas (Bimbisara, Ajatashatru) came earlier — a chronological trap.

110. The Gupta ruler who assumed the title 'Vikramaditya' after defeating the Shakas was:
(a) Samudragupta (b) Chandragupta II (c) Kumaragupta I (d) Skandagupta
Answer: (b)
Solution: Chandragupta II took the title Vikramaditya after destroying Shaka power in western India. Samudragupta was his father, famous for the Allahabad Pillar inscription.

111. The Allahabad Pillar Inscription (Prayaga Prashasti) was composed by:
(a) Banabhatta (b) Kalidasa (c) Harisena (d) Vatsyayana
Answer: (c)
Solution: Harisena, Samudragupta's court poet, composed the Prayaga Prashasti. Banabhatta wrote Harshacharita for Harsha — a similar-role trap across two centuries.

112. Fa-Hien, the Chinese pilgrim, visited India during the reign of:
(a) Harshavardhana (b) Kanishka (c) Ashoka (d) Chandragupta II
Answer: (d)
Solution: Fa-Hien travelled in India c. 399–414 CE, during Chandragupta II's reign. Hiuen Tsang came during Harsha's reign — the two Chinese pilgrims are a classic confusion pair.

113. The Fourth Buddhist Council, which split Buddhism into Hinayana and Mahayana, was held under:
(a) Kanishka (b) Ashoka (c) Harsha (d) Dharmapala
Answer: (a)
Solution: The Fourth Council met at Kundalvana (Kashmir) under Kanishka, formalising the Hinayana–Mahayana division. Ashoka presided over the Third Council at Pataliputra — a frequent trap.

114. Which of the following is the oldest of the four Vedas?
(a) Samaveda (b) Yajurveda (c) Rigveda (d) Atharvaveda
Answer: (c)
Solution: The Rigveda (c. 1500–1200 BCE) is the oldest Veda, containing 1,028 hymns in ten mandalas. The other three Vedas were compiled later.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Themes in Indian History Part I

115. The 'Sangam' literature is associated with which language?
(a) Sanskrit (b) Tamil (c) Pali (d) Prakrit
Answer: (b)
Solution: Sangam literature comprises early Tamil texts composed in assemblies (sangams) of poets in South India. Pali is associated with Buddhist texts — a common confusion.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Themes in Indian History Part I

116. The Iron Pillar at Mehrauli, which has not rusted for centuries, is associated with which ruler?
(a) Ashoka (b) Chandragupta II (c) Samudragupta (d) Harsha
Answer: (b)
Solution: The Mehrauli Iron Pillar inscription refers to a ruler 'Chandra', identified with Chandragupta II of the Gupta dynasty. Ashoka's pillars are of polished sandstone, not iron — a material-based trap.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Themes in Indian History Part I

117. Which site has yielded evidence of a ploughed field from the Harappan period?
(a) Kalibangan (b) Mohenjo-daro (c) Lothal (d) Harappa
Answer: (a)
Solution: Kalibangan (Rajasthan) yielded a ploughed field with furrow marks, the earliest such evidence. Lothal is famous for its dockyard — a site-feature confusion trap.

118. The concept of 'Dhamma' propagated by Ashoka primarily meant:
(a) A new religion (b) Conversion to Buddhism (c) A code of moral and ethical conduct (d) A system of taxation
Answer: (c)
Solution: Ashoka's Dhamma was an ethical code — tolerance, non-violence, respect for elders — not Buddhist conversion or a new religion. The 'conversion' option is the examiner's favourite trap.

119. Who among the following was the 24th Tirthankara of Jainism?

Answer: (d)
Solution: Mahavira was the 24th and last Tirthankara; Parshvanatha was the 23rd and Rishabhanatha the first. The similar names of Tirthankaras are a standard trap.

120. The capital of the Haryanka king Bimbisara was:
(a) Pataliputra (b) Kaushambi (c) Rajagriha (d) Vaishali
Answer: (c)
Solution: Bimbisara ruled from Rajagriha (Girivraja); Pataliputra became the capital later under Udayin. The two capitals are a classic chronological confusion.

122. Arrange the following rulers in correct chronological order:
1. Bimbisara
2. Ashoka
3. Chandragupta Maurya
4. Mahapadma Nanda
(a) 1-3-4-2 (b) 1-4-3-2 (c) 4-1-3-2 (d) 4-3-1-2
Answer: (b)
Solution: Bimbisara (Haryanka) → Mahapadma Nanda → Chandragupta Maurya → Ashoka is the correct sequence. The Nandas immediately preceded the Mauryas — the most common chronological trap in this chapter.

123. Consider the following statements about Ashokan edicts:

Which of the statements given above is/are correct?

Solution: All three are correct. Most edicts use Brahmi; the northwestern inscriptions (Kandahar) use Greek and Aramaic; Rock Edict XIII records the Kalinga War and Ashoka's remorse.
