# 📊 PYQ Analysis — Ancient Indian History

*(Pattern analysis based on the recurring style of questions in UPSC, SSC CGL/CHSL, Banking, Railway and State PCS examinations. This section describes trends in words, not invented statistics.)*

---

## 1. How Exams Ask Questions from This Chapter

Ancient Indian History is a **permanent fixture** in the General Studies/GA section of almost every major exam. The weightage is moderate but highly predictable — examiners return again and again to the same clusters:

| Sub-topic | How often it appears | Typical question style |
|---|---|---|
| Indus Valley Civilization | Very frequent | Site–feature matching, "which is NOT true" statements |
| Vedic Age & Buddhism/Jainism | Very frequent | Text–author matching, councils, patrons |
| Mauryan Empire | Very frequent | Ashoka's edicts, inscriptions, officials, Kalinga war |
| Gupta Period | Frequent | "Golden Age" claims, scholars, administration |
| Sangam Age & South India | Moderate | Kingdom–capital matching, literature |
| Foreign travellers | Frequent | Traveller–ruler–period matching |
| Art, architecture & inscriptions | Rising trend (esp. UPSC) | Statement-based, multi-statement questions |

**Exam-wise flavour:**
- **UPSC / State PCS:** Multi-statement analytical questions ("Consider the following statements…"), conceptual links (e.g., Ashokan edicts + Kautilya + Megasthenes).
- **SSC CGL/CHSL:** Direct one-liners — capitals, founders, books, travellers, battles.
- **Banking (IBPS/SBI):** Short factual questions, usually from Indus Valley, Buddhism, Mauryas.
- **Railway (NTPC/Group D):** Simple matching — dynasty–founder, site–river, text–subject.

## 2. The Examiner's Traps (जाल) — Know Them Before the Exam

1. **Almost Correct Statement** — One word is changed: "Ashoka's edicts were written in **Sanskrit**" (they were mostly in Prakrit, Brahmi script). Read every word.
2. **Chronological Confusion** — Options list events/people in scrambled order: Buddha–Mahavira contemporaries; Megasthenes in Chandragupta Maurya's court, Fa-Hien in Chandragupta II's court.
3. **Similar-Sounding Names** — Chandragupta Maurya vs Chandragupta I (Gupta); Bindusara vs Bimbisara; Kanishka vs Kanishka-era Gandhara art.
4. **Negative Phrasing** — "Which of the following is **NOT**…", "All **EXCEPT**…" — the most common cause of silly mistakes.
5. **Traveller–Court Mismatch** — Fa-Hien ↔ Chandragupta II, Hiuen Tsang ↔ Harshavardhana, Megasthenes ↔ Chandragupta Maurya, I-Tsing ↔ Nalanda (7th century). Swapping these is the classic trap.
6. **Site–Feature Swap** — Great Bath ↔ Mohenjo-daro, Dockyard ↔ Lothal, Granary ↔ Harappa. Options deliberately cross these.
7. **Council Confusion** — Buddhist councils (Rajgriha, Vaishali, Pataliputra, Kundalavana) vs Jain councils (Pataliputra, Vallabhi) — presidents and patrons get swapped.

## 3. Top 10 High-Yield Questions (PYQ-style) with Solutions

**Q1.** The Great Bath of the Indus Valley Civilization was discovered at:
(a) Harappa (b) Mohenjo-daro (c) Lothal (d) Kalibangan
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** The Great Bath is the signature structure of Mohenjo-daro (Sindh, on the Indus). Lothal is famous for its **dockyard** — the favourite swap option. **Trap type: Site–Feature Swap.**
**Source:** NCERT Class 12 (Themes in Indian History, Part I)

**Q2.** Megasthenes, the author of *Indica*, visited the court of:
(a) Ashoka (b) Bindusara (c) Chandragupta Maurya (d) Chandragupta II
**Answer: (c)**
**Solution:** Megasthenes was the ambassador of Seleucus Nicator to Chandragupta Maurya's court at Pataliputra. Option (d) is the trap — Fa-Hien came during Chandragupta II's reign. **Trap type: Traveller–Court Mismatch.**
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q3.** Which of the following is NOT a correct match?
(a) Fa-Hien — Chandragupta II (b) Hiuen Tsang — Harshavardhana
(c) Megasthenes — Ashoka (d) I-Tsing — Nalanda
**Answer: (c)**
**Solution:** Megasthenes belonged to Chandragupta Maurya's court, not Ashoka's. The other three pairs are correct. **Trap type: Negative Phrasing + Traveller Mismatch.**
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q4.** The Fourth Buddhist Council, which split Buddhism into Hinayana and Mahayana, was held under:
(a) Ashoka (b) Kanishka (c) Harshavardhana (d) Kalasoka
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** The Fourth Council was held at Kundalavana (Kashmir) under Kanishka. Ashoka presided over the **Third** Council at Pataliputra — the classic confusion. **Trap type: Council Confusion.**
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q5.** Ashoka's inscriptions were deciphered for the first time by:
(a) James Prinsep (b) William Jones (c) Max Müller (d) Alexander Cunningham
**Answer: (a)**
**Solution:** James Prinsep deciphered the Brahmi script of Ashokan edicts in 1837. Cunningham (Archaeological Survey) and Max Müller (Sanskrit texts) are the distractors. **Trap type: Similar-Sounding Scholars.**
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q6.** Arrange the following in chronological order:
1. Alexander's invasion 2. Kalinga War 3. Kanishka's accession 4. Rise of the Guptas
(a) 1-2-3-4 (b) 2-1-3-4 (c) 1-3-2-4 (d) 2-1-4-3
**Answer: (a)**
**Solution:** Alexander (326 BCE) → Kalinga War (261 BCE) → Kanishka (1st–2nd century CE) → Guptas (4th century CE). **Trap type: Chronological Confusion.**
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q7.** The *Arthashastra*, a treatise on statecraft, is attributed to:
(a) Kalidasa (b) Kautilya (c) Banabhatta (d) Patanjali
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** Kautilya (Chanakya/Vishnugupta), Chandragupta Maurya's minister, wrote the *Arthashastra*. Banabhatta wrote *Harshacharita* — a frequent pairing trap. **Trap type: Text–Author Swap.**
**Source:** NCERT Class 12

**Q8.** Which Gupta ruler issued the largest number of gold coins and assumed the title 'Vikramaditya' after defeating the Shakas?
(a) Chandragupta I (b) Samudragupta (c) Chandragupta II (d) Kumaragupta
**Answer: (c)**
**Solution:** Chandragupta II defeated the Western Kshatrapas (Shakas) and took the title Vikramaditya. Samudragupta is the trap option because of his Allahabad Pillar conquests. **Trap type: Almost Correct / Similar Names.**
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q9.** The dockyard of the Indus Valley Civilization was found at:
(a) Lothal (b) Dholavira (c) Banawali (d) Ropar
**Answer: (a)**
**Solution:** Lothal (Gujarat, on the Bhogava river) had a tidal dockyard — evidence of maritime trade. Dholavira is known for its water-management system and city division into three parts. **Trap type: Site–Feature Swap.**
**Source:** NCERT Class 12

**Q10.** Consider the following statements about the Sangam Age:
1. The Sangam literature was composed in Tamil.
2. The three early kingdoms were Chera, Chola and Pandya.
3. *Silappadikaram* was written by Ilango Adigal.
Which of the above are correct?
(a) 1 and 2 only (b) 2 and 3 only (c) 1 and 3 only (d) 1, 2 and 3
**Answer: (d)**
**Solution:** All three statements are correct — Sangam texts are in Old Tamil; the three crowned kingdoms (muventar) were Chera, Chola, Pandya; *Silappadikaram* is by Ilango Adigal (its twin epic *Manimekalai* is by Sattanar). **Trap type: Multi-statement (UPSC style).**
**Source:** NCERT Class 11/12

## 4. The 15/45-Second Rule in Action

**Rule:** If a question is a direct recall (one fact), answer within **15 seconds**. If it needs statement-checking or chronology, give it up to **45 seconds** — never more. If still unsure, mark and move on.

**Applied to Q1 (Great Bath):** This is pure recall — "Great Bath = Mohenjo-daro" should be a reflex. **Target: under 15 seconds.** Do not re-read the options; Lothal is planted to make you hesitate.

**Applied to Q6 (Chronology):** This needs ordering four events. Fix two anchors first — Alexander (326 BCE) is earliest, Guptas (4th century CE) are latest. That alone eliminates (b), (c) and (d). **Target: 30–45 seconds.** If you cannot recall even one anchor date, skip immediately — guessing chronology blind wastes time and invites negative marking.

**Applied to Q10 (Multi-statement):** Check each statement independently; if all three feel familiar and none has a swapped word, commit. **Target: 45 seconds maximum.**

## 5. Quick Revision Pointers from PYQ Patterns

- Memorise the **traveller–ruler table** and the **Buddhist council table** — they appear in some form almost every cycle.
- Indus Valley **site–feature pairs** (Mohenjo-daro, Harappa, Lothal, Kalibangan, Dholavira, Chanhudaro, Banawali, Ropar) are the single most repeated cluster.
- For "NOT/EXCEPT" questions, underline the negative word before reading options.
- In chronology questions, anchor the **earliest and latest** items first — most options collapse with just two anchors.
