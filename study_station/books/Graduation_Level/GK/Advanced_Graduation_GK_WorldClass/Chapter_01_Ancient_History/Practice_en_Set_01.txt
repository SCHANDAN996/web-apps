Questions 1–20: Easy | Questions 21–25: Medium

1. The Harappan site of Lothal, famous for its dockyard, is located in which present-day state?
(a) Rajasthan (b) Punjab (c) Haryana (d) Gujarat
Answer: (d)
Solution: Lothal, on the Bhogava river (a tributary of the Sabarmati), is in Gujarat and is noted for its tidal dockyard and bead-making industry. Rajasthan has Kalibangan, which often confuses students.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Themes in Indian History Part I

2. Which metal was NOT known to the people of the Indus Valley Civilization?
(a) Copper (b) Bronze (c) Iron (d) Gold
Answer: (c)
Solution: The Harappans used copper, bronze, gold and silver but did not know iron; iron came into use in India during the later Vedic period. This is a classic examiner trap.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Themes in Indian History Part I

3. The Great Bath was discovered at which Harappan site?
(a) Harappa (b) Chanhudaro (c) Dholavira (d) Mohenjo-daro
Answer: (d)
Solution: The Great Bath, a large water-tight brick tank probably used for ritual bathing, was found at Mohenjo-daro in Sindh (Pakistan).
Source: NCERT Class 11 Themes in Indian History Part I

4. The Rig Veda consists of how many mandalas (books)?
(a) 8 (b) 12 (c) 10 (d) 16
Answer: (c)
Solution: The Rig Veda, the oldest Veda, has 10 mandalas containing 1,028 hymns (suktas); the Gayatri mantra occurs in the third mandala.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Themes in Indian History Part I

5. The famous Gayatri Mantra is addressed to which deity?
(a) Savitri (b) Agni (c) Indra (d) Varuna
Answer: (a)
Solution: The Gayatri Mantra (Rig Veda, Mandala 3) is addressed to Savitri, the solar deity. Indra is the most frequently invoked Rig Vedic god overall, which is the usual trap.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Themes in Indian History Part I

6. Gautama Buddha delivered his first sermon, known as the Dhammachakkapavattana, at:
(a) Bodh Gaya (b) Sarnath (c) Kushinagar (d) Lumbini
Answer: (b)
Solution: Buddha attained enlightenment at Bodh Gaya, gave his first sermon at Sarnath, and died at Kushinagar; Lumbini is his birthplace. Exams frequently swap these four events — a chronological confusion trap.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Themes in Indian History Part I

7. Mahavira, the 24th Tirthankara of Jainism, was born at:
(a) Pava (b) Kundagrama (c) Vaishali (d) Rajagriha
Answer: (b)
Solution: Mahavira was born at Kundagrama near Vaishali; he died at Pavapuri. Kundagrama was part of the Vaishali republic, so Vaishali is the tempting wrong option.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Themes in Indian History Part I

8. Which of the following was the first capital of the Magadha kingdom?
(a) Rajagriha (b) Pataliputra (c) Vaishali (d) Champa
Answer: (a)
Solution: Rajagriha (Girivraja) was the early capital of Magadha under Bimbisara; the capital was later shifted to Pataliputra by Udayin. This sequence is a favourite chronological trap.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Themes in Indian History Part I

9. Alexander invaded India in which year?
(a) 323 BCE (b) 326 BCE (c) 305 BCE (d) 261 BCE
Answer: (b)
Solution: Alexander crossed into the Indian subcontinent in 326 BCE and fought Porus on the Jhelum (Hydaspes). 305 BCE is the Seleucus–Chandragupta treaty year, a common confusion.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Themes in Indian History Part I

10. The Arthashastra, a treatise on statecraft, is traditionally attributed to:
(a) Megasthenes (b) Banabhatta (c) Kalidasa (d) Kautilya
Answer: (d)
Solution: The Arthashastra is attributed to Kautilya (Chanakya/Vishnugupta), minister of Chandragupta Maurya. Megasthenes wrote Indica — the similar-name trap.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Themes in Indian History Part I

11. Ashoka's inscriptions were deciphered for the first time by:
(a) William Jones (b) Max Muller (c) James Prinsep (d) Alexander Cunningham
Answer: (c)
Solution: James Prinsep deciphered the Brahmi script of Ashokan edicts in 1837. Cunningham is called the father of Indian archaeology — a frequent name-confusion trap.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Themes in Indian History Part I

12. The Kalinga War, which transformed Ashoka, was fought in which regnal year of Ashoka?
(a) 4th year (b) 20th year (c) 12th year (d) 8th year
Answer: (d)
Solution: Rock Edict XIII records that the Kalinga War took place in the 8th regnal year of Ashoka (261 BCE), after which he embraced Buddhism and dhamma-vijaya.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Themes in Indian History Part I

13. Which dynasty was founded by Chandragupta I?
(a) Maurya (b) Gupta (c) Vardhana (d) Chalukya
Answer: (b)
Solution: Chandragupta I (c. 319–320 CE) founded the Gupta imperial line and started the Gupta Era. Do not confuse him with Chandragupta Maurya — a classic similar-names trap.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Themes in Indian History Part II

14. Samudragupta's military achievements are recorded in the Allahabad Pillar Inscription composed by:
(a) Kalidasa (b) Ravikirti (c) Banabhatta (d) Harisena
Answer: (d)
Solution: The Allahabad (Prayaga) Prashasti was composed by Harisena, Samudragupta's court poet. Ravikirti composed Pulakeshin II's Aihole inscription — the usual distractor.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Themes in Indian History Part II

15. Fa-Hien, the Chinese pilgrim, visited India during the reign of:
(a) Samudragupta (b) Kanishka (c) Harshavardhana (d) Chandragupta II
Answer: (d)
Solution: Fa-Hien visited during Chandragupta II's reign (c. 399–414 CE); Hiuen Tsang came during Harsha's reign. Exams routinely swap the two Chinese travellers.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Themes in Indian History Part II

16. The Iron Pillar at Mehrauli, famous for its rust-resistant quality, is associated with which ruler?
(a) Ashoka (b) Samudragupta (c) Chandragupta II (d) Harshavardhana
Answer: (c)
Solution: The Mehrauli Iron Pillar inscription refers to a king called Chandra, identified with Chandragupta II (Vikramaditya) of the Gupta dynasty.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Themes in Indian History Part II

17. Kanishka, the famous Kushana ruler, started which era in 78 CE?
(a) Saka Era (b) Vikrama Era (c) Gupta Era (d) Kalachuri Era
Answer: (a)
Solution: The Saka Era beginning 78 CE is associated with Kanishka's accession. The Vikrama Era starts 57–58 BCE and the Gupta Era 319–320 CE — a chronological trap.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Themes in Indian History Part II

18. The Sangam literature is composed in which language?
(a) Tamil (b) Prakrit (c) Sanskrit (d) Pali
Answer: (a)
Solution: Sangam literature, the earliest South Indian literature, is in Old Tamil and describes the Chera, Chola and Pandya kingdoms.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Themes in Indian History Part I

19. The Third Buddhist Council, which sent missionaries abroad, was held under whose patronage?
(a) Kanishka (b) Ashoka (c) Harsha (d) Ajatashatru
Answer: (b)
Solution: The Third Council at Pataliputra (c. 250 BCE) was held under Ashoka, presided by Moggaliputta Tissa. Kanishka patronised the Fourth Council — the standard confusion.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Themes in Indian History Part I

20. The rock-cut caves of Ajanta, famous for paintings, were chiefly patronised by which dynasty?
(a) Cholas (b) Satavahanas and Vakatakas (c) Pallavas (d) Rashtrakutas
Answer: (b)
Solution: The Ajanta caves (Maharashtra) were excavated mainly under Satavahana and later Vakataka patronage. The Rashtrakutas built the Kailasa temple at Ellora — a nearby-site trap.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Themes in Indian History Part II

21. Consider the following statements about the Indus Valley Civilization:
1. The people worshipped the Mother Goddess.
2. They knew the use of the ploughshare.
3. They built their cities entirely of stone.
Which of the statements given above is/are correct?
(a) 1 and 2 only (b) 2 and 3 only (c) 1 and 3 only (d) 1, 2 and 3
Answer: (a)
Solution: Harappans worshipped a Mother Goddess and used ploughshares, but their cities were built of burnt and mud bricks, not stone — statement 3 is the almost-correct trap.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Themes in Indian History Part I

22. Arrange the following events in correct chronological order:
1. Alexander's invasion of India
2. Accession of Ashoka
3. Mahaparinirvana of Buddha
4. Rise of the Gupta Empire
(a) 3, 2, 1, 4 (b) 1, 3, 2, 4 (c) 3, 1, 2, 4 (d) 1, 2, 3, 4
Answer: (c)
Solution: Buddha's death (c. 483 BCE) precedes Alexander's invasion (326 BCE), then Ashoka's accession (c. 268 BCE), and the Guptas rose around 319–320 CE. This is a chronological-confusion trap.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Themes in Indian History Part I

23. Consider the following pairs:
1. Milindapanho — Dialogue between Menander and Nagasena
2. Harshacharita — Banabhatta
3. Mudrarakshasa — Kalidasa
Which of the pairs given above is/are correctly matched?
(a) 1, 2 and 3 (b) 2 and 3 only (c) 1 and 3 only (d) 1 and 2 only
Answer: (d)
Solution: Milindapanho records the Menander–Nagasena dialogue and Harshacharita is by Banabhatta; but Mudrarakshasa was written by Vishakhadatta, not Kalidasa — pair 3 is the trap.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Themes in Indian History Part II

24. With reference to Ashoka's Dhamma, consider the following statements:
1. It advocated complete renunciation of worldly life.
2. It emphasised tolerance among religious sects.
3. Dhamma Mahamatras were appointed to propagate it.
Which of the statements given above is/are correct?
(a) 1 and 2 only (b) 2 and 3 only (c) 1 only (d) 1, 2 and 3
Answer: (b)
Solution: Ashoka's Dhamma stressed tolerance and ethical conduct, spread by Dhamma Mahamatras; it never demanded renunciation of worldly life — statement 1 is the almost-correct trap.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Themes in Indian History Part I

25. Consider the following statements about Harshavardhana:
1. He was defeated by Pulakeshin II on the banks of the Narmada.
2. His court poet Banabhatta wrote the Kadambari.
3. He convened a grand assembly at Kannauj attended by Hiuen Tsang.
Which of the statements given above is/are correct?
(a) 1, 2 and 3 (b) 2 and 3 only (c) 1 and 3 only (d) 1 and 2 only
Answer: (a)
Solution: All three are correct: Pulakeshin II checked Harsha at the Narmada, Banabhatta wrote Kadambari and Harshacharita, and Hiuen Tsang attended Harsha's Kannauj assembly.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Themes in Indian History Part II
