Questions 76–100: Medium

76. The famous Hathigumpha inscription, which records the achievements of King Kharavela, is located in which state?
(a) Andhra Pradesh (b) Karnataka (c) Madhya Pradesh (d) Odisha
Answer: (d)
Solution: The Hathigumpha inscription of Kharavela, the Chedi (Mahameghavahana) ruler of Kalinga, is carved in the Udayagiri caves near Bhubaneswar, Odisha. Andhra Pradesh is a trap because Kharavela's campaigns reached the Krishna-Godavari region.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Themes in Indian History Part I

77. Which of the following pairs is correctly matched?
(a) Milindapanho — dialogue between Ashoka and Upagupta (b) Milindapanho — dialogue between Kanishka and Vasumitra (c) Milindapanho — dialogue between Menander and Nagasena (d) Milindapanho — dialogue between Pushyamitra and Patanjali
Answer: (c)
Solution: The Milindapanho (Questions of Milinda) records the philosophical dialogue between the Indo-Greek king Menander (Milinda) and the Buddhist monk Nagasena. The similar royal names in the other options are a classic examiner's trap.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Themes in Indian History Part I

78. The Gandhara school of art primarily shows the influence of which artistic tradition?
(a) Persian (b) Chinese (c) Egyptian (d) Greco-Roman
Answer: (d)
Solution: Gandhara art, flourishing under the Kushanas, blended Indian Buddhist themes with Greco-Roman (Hellenistic) techniques — wavy hair, muscular bodies, draped garments. Persian influence is the tempting wrong option because Gandhara lay on the north-western frontier.
Source: NCERT Class 11 An Introduction to Indian Art

79. Consider the following statements about the Mathura school of art:
1. It used spotted red sandstone.
2. It produced images of Buddha, Bodhisattvas, and also Jain tirthankaras.
3. It was entirely patronised by foreign rulers.
Which of the statements given above is/are correct?
(a) 2 and 3 only (b) 1 and 2 only (c) 1 and 3 only (d) 1, 2 and 3
Answer: (b)
Solution: Mathura art used spotted red sandstone and produced Buddhist, Jain, and Brahmanical images. Statement 3 is wrong — it received patronage from local devotees, guilds, and royalty alike, not only foreign rulers.
Source: NCERT Class 11 An Introduction to Indian Art

80. The term 'Sandhivigrahika' in ancient Indian administration referred to the official in charge of:
(a) Revenue collection (b) War and peace (foreign affairs) (c) Royal household (d) Religious endowments
Answer: (b)
Solution: The Sandhivigrahika was the minister for peace and war, i.e., foreign affairs, mentioned in Gupta-era inscriptions such as those of Samudragupta's court. Revenue collection belonged to officials like the Akshapataladhikrita.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Themes in Indian History Part I

81. Which Gupta ruler is known to have performed the Ashvamedha sacrifice and issued gold coins depicting the sacrificial horse?
(a) Chandragupta I (b) Chandragupta II (c) Samudragupta (d) Kumaragupta I
Answer: (c)
Solution: Samudragupta performed the Ashvamedha after his extensive conquests and issued Ashvamedha-type gold coins. Chandragupta I is a trap because he founded the Gupta era and first issued gold coins, but the horse-sacrifice coins belong to Samudragupta.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Themes in Indian History Part I

82. The Allahabad Pillar Inscription (Prayaga Prashasti) was composed by:
(a) Banabhatta (b) Vatsabhatti (c) Kalidasa (d) Harishena
Answer: (d)
Solution: The Prayaga Prashasti, eulogising Samudragupta, was composed by his court poet and minister Harishena. Banabhatta wrote Harshacharita for Harshavardhana — a frequent confusion trap.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Themes in Indian History Part I

83. Which of the following inscriptions provides information about the administration of the Chola village assemblies?
(a) Maski inscription (b) Aihole inscription (c) Junagadh inscription (d) Uttaramerur inscription
Answer: (d)
Solution: The Uttaramerur inscriptions of Parantaka I detail the functioning of Chola village sabhas, committees (variyams), and election by lot (kudavolai system). The Aihole inscription relates to Pulakeshin II — a common mix-up.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Themes in Indian History Part II

84. The Aihole inscription, which mentions the defeat of Harshavardhana, was composed by:
(a) Ravikirti (b) Harishena (c) Banabhatta (d) Dandin
Answer: (a)
Solution: The Aihole prashasti of Pulakeshin II was composed by his court poet Ravikirti; it records Pulakeshin's victory over Harshavardhana on the Narmada. Harishena belongs to Samudragupta's court — a deliberate similar-name trap.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Themes in Indian History Part II

85. Which Chola ruler founded the city of Gangaikondacholapuram to commemorate his victory over the Pala kingdom of Bengal?
(a) Rajaraja I (b) Rajendra I (c) Rajadhiraja I (d) Kulottunga I
Answer: (b)
Solution: Rajendra I led the northern expedition up to the Ganga, assumed the title 'Gangaikonda Chola', and built Gangaikondacholapuram with its great Brihadishvara-style temple. Rajaraja I, his father, built the Thanjavur Brihadishvara temple — a classic father-son confusion.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Themes in Indian History Part II

86. The Brihadishvara temple at Thanjavur was built by:
(a) Rajendra I (b) Rajaraja I (c) Vijayalaya (d) Aditya I
Answer: (b)
Solution: Rajaraja I completed the Brihadishvara (Rajarajeshvara) temple at Thanjavur around 1010 CE. Rajendra I built a similar temple at Gangaikondacholapuram — the examiner's favourite chronological/name trap.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Themes in Indian History Part II

87. Which of the following statements about the Sangam literature is correct?
(a) It was composed in Sanskrit under Pallava patronage (b) It deals exclusively with religious philosophy (c) It is Tamil literature composed in assemblies of poets (d) It was written during the Gupta period
Answer: (c)
Solution: Sangam literature is early Tamil literature composed in three legendary assemblies (sangams) of poets at Madurai, dealing with love (akam) and war (puram) themes. It is neither Sanskrit nor exclusively religious.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Themes in Indian History Part I

88. The terms 'akam' and 'puram' in Sangam poetry refer respectively to:
(a) Love and war (b) War and love (c) Trade and agriculture (d) Kingship and justice
Answer: (a)
Solution: Akam poetry deals with inner/love themes, while puram deals with outer themes such as war, kingship, and public life. Option (b) reverses the order — a deliberate trap for those who memorise carelessly.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Themes in Indian History Part I

89. Who among the following foreign travellers visited India during the reign of Chandragupta II (Vikramaditya)?
(a) Megasthenes (b) Fa-Hien (c) Hiuen Tsang (d) Al-Biruni
Answer: (b)
Solution: The Chinese pilgrim Fa-Hien visited India during Chandragupta II's reign (early 5th century CE). Megasthenes came in Chandragupta Maurya's time and Hiuen Tsang in Harsha's — the similar ruler names make this a standard trap.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Themes in Indian History Part I

90. Hiuen Tsang, the Chinese pilgrim, visited India during the reign of:
(a) Harshavardhana (b) Chandragupta II (c) Pulakeshin II (d) Dharmapala
Answer: (a)
Solution: Hiuen Tsang (Yuan Chwang) stayed in India (c. 630–644 CE) during Harshavardhana's reign and described Kannauj, Nalanda, and the Prayaga assembly. Chandragupta II is the trap because of Fa-Hien's visit.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Themes in Indian History Part II

91. The Nalanda University was patronised by which of the following rulers?
1. Kumaragupta I
2. Harshavardhana
3. Dharmapala
Select the correct answer:
(a) 1 and 2 only (b) 2 and 3 only (c) 1 and 3 only (d) 1, 2 and 3
Answer: (d)
Solution: Kumaragupta I is traditionally credited with founding Nalanda; Harsha granted villages for its upkeep; the Pala king Dharmapala also patronised it. All three supported the university at different times.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Themes in Indian History Part II

92. The 'Tripitaka' literature is associated with which religion?
(a) Jainism (b) Buddhism (c) Ajivikas (d) Charvaka
Answer: (b)
Solution: The Tripitakas — Vinaya, Sutta, and Abhidhamma Pitakas — are the canonical texts of Buddhism, compiled in Pali. Jain canonical texts are called Agamas, which is the common confusion.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Themes in Indian History Part I

93. The first Buddhist Council was held at Rajagriha during the reign of:
(a) Bimbisara (b) Ashoka (c) Kalasoka (d) Ajatashatru
Answer: (d)
Solution: The First Buddhist Council (c. 483 BCE) met at Rajagriha under Ajatashatru of the Haryanka dynasty, soon after the Buddha's death. Bimbisara, his father, is the trap; Kalasoka presided over the Second Council at Vaishali.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Themes in Indian History Part I

94. Which of the following is the correct chronological order of the Buddhist Councils' venues?
(a) Rajagriha – Pataliputra – Vaishali – Kundalavana (b) Vaishali – Rajagriha – Pataliputra – Kundalavana (c) Rajagriha – Vaishali – Pataliputra – Kundalavana (d) Rajagriha – Vaishali – Kundalavana – Pataliputra
Answer: (c)
Solution: First Council at Rajagriha (Ajatashatru), Second at Vaishali (Kalasoka), Third at Pataliputra (Ashoka), Fourth at Kundalavana, Kashmir (Kanishka). The other options scramble this sequence — a chronological-confusion trap.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Themes in Indian History Part I

95. The Fourth Buddhist Council, which led to the split into Hinayana and Mahayana, was held under the patronage of:
(a) Ashoka (b) Menander (c) Kanishka (d) Harshavardhana
Answer: (c)
Solution: The Fourth Council at Kundalavana (Kashmir) under Kanishka formalised the Mahayana-Hinayana division. Ashoka patronised the Third Council at Pataliputra — the most common trap here.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Themes in Indian History Part I

96. In Jainism, the term 'Jina' literally means:
(a) The conqueror (b) The renouncer (c) The teacher (d) The enlightened householder
Answer: (a)
Solution: 'Jina' means conqueror — one who has conquered the senses and inner passions; followers are Jains. Mahavira was the 24th Tirthankara, not the founder of Jainism (Parshvanatha, the 23rd, preceded him).
Source: NCERT Class 12 Themes in Indian History Part I

97. Who was the 23rd Tirthankara of Jainism?
(a) Rishabhanatha (b) Neminatha (c) Mahavira (d) Parshvanatha
Answer: (d)
Solution: Parshvanatha was the 23rd Tirthankara, who preached the four vows; Mahavira, the 24th, added celibacy as the fifth. Rishabhanatha was the first Tirthankara — a frequent numbering trap.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Themes in Indian History Part I

98. The Kailasanatha temple at Ellora was built during the reign of which dynasty?
(a) Rashtrakutas (b) Chalukyas of Badami (c) Pallavas (d) Cholas
Answer: (a)
Solution: The rock-cut Kailasanatha temple at Ellora was built by the Rashtrakuta king Krishna I in the 8th century CE. The Pallavas built the Kailasanatha temple at Kanchipuram — the identical temple name is a deliberate trap.
Source: NCERT Class 11 An Introduction to Indian Art

99. Consider the following statements about the Indus Valley Civilization:
1. The Great Bath was found at Harappa.
2. The bronze dancing girl was found at Mohenjo-daro.
3. Lothal was a major port city with a dockyard.
Which of the statements given above is/are correct?
(a) 1 and 2 only (b) 2 and 3 only (c) 1 and 3 only (d) 1, 2 and 3
Answer: (b)
Solution: Statement 1 is wrong — the Great Bath was found at Mohenjo-daro, not Harappa. The bronze dancing girl (Mohenjo-daro) and Lothal's dockyard are correct. This is an almost-correct-statement trap.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Themes in Indian History Part I

100. The site of Dholavira, known for its sophisticated water management system and three-part city division, is located in:
(a) Gujarat (b) Rajasthan (c) Punjab (d) Haryana
Answer: (a)
Solution: Dholavira lies on Khadir island in the Rann of Kutch, Gujarat; it was inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 2021. Its reservoirs and citadel-middle-lower town division are distinctive. Rajasthan is a trap because Kalibangan lies there.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Themes in Indian History Part I
