Questions 26–50: Medium

26. The Hathigumpha inscription, which gives a detailed account of the conquests of a Kalinga ruler, belongs to:
(a) Ashoka (b) Samudragupta (c) Kharavela (d) Harshavardhana
Answer: (c)
Solution: The Hathigumpha inscription at Udayagiri (Odisha) records the achievements of Kharavela, the Chedi (Mahameghavahana) ruler of Kalinga. Ashoka's records are rock and pillar edicts, not Hathigumpha — a common confusion.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Themes in Indian History Part I

27. Which of the following pairs is correctly matched?
(a) Junagadh Inscription — of Chandragupta II (b) Allahabad Pillar Inscription — composed by Kalidasa (c) Milindapanho — dialogue between Menander and Nagasena (d) Aihole Inscription — composed by Banabhatta
Answer: (c)
Solution: Milindapanho records the dialogue between Indo-Greek king Menander (Milinda) and the Buddhist monk Nagasena. The Allahabad inscription was composed by Harisena, Junagadh belongs to Rudradaman, and Aihole was composed by Ravikirti — the other options are classic similar-name traps.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Themes in Indian History Part I

28. The concept of 'Dhamma' as propounded by Ashoka primarily meant:
(a) conversion of all subjects to Buddhism (b) a new religion founded by Ashoka (c) a code of moral and ethical conduct including tolerance and non-violence (d) strict enforcement of Buddhist monastic rules on the public
Answer: (c)
Solution: Ashoka's Dhamma was an ethical code — tolerance, non-violence, respect for elders and all sects — not a new religion nor forced conversion. The trap is assuming Dhamma equals Buddhism; Ashoka respected all sects.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Themes in Indian History Part I

29. Which ruler is known to have performed the Ashvamedha sacrifice twice?
(a) Pushyamitra Shunga (b) Samudragupta (c) Gautamiputra Satakarni (d) Kanishka
Answer: (a)
Solution: Pushyamitra Shunga, founder of the Shunga dynasty, is credited with performing two Ashvamedha sacrifices. Samudragupta performed it once, commemorated on his Ashvamedha-type coins.
Source: PYQ-style

30. The Gandhara school of art is distinguished by:
(a) exclusive use of spotted red sandstone (b) complete absence of Buddha images (c) strong Greco-Roman influence and depiction of Buddha in human form (d) its origin in the region of Mathura
Answer: (c)
Solution: Gandhara art blended Greek and Roman techniques with Indian themes and produced some of the earliest anthropomorphic images of Buddha, using bluish-grey schist. Red sandstone and Mathura origin describe the Mathura school — a frequent mix-up.
Source: NCERT Class 11 An Introduction to Indian Art

31. Consider the following statements about the Sangam literature:
1. The Sangam assemblies were held under the patronage of the Pandya kings at Madurai.
2. Tolkappiyam is a work on Tamil grammar.
3. Silappadikaram was written by Ilango Adigal.
Which of the statements given above are correct?
(a) 1 and 2 only (b) 1, 2 and 3 (c) 1 and 3 only (d) 2 and 3 only
Answer: (b)
Solution: All three statements are correct: Sangams were patronised by Pandyas at Madurai, Tolkappiyam is the earliest Tamil grammar, and Silappadikaram (the story of Kannagi) was composed by Ilango Adigal. Its twin epic Manimekalai by Sattanar is the usual confusion.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Themes in World History / Tamil literary tradition

32. The Kalinga War, which transformed Ashoka's policy, is described in which of his edicts?
(a) Major Rock Edict XIII (b) Major Rock Edict X (c) Pillar Edict VII (d) Minor Rock Edict I
Answer: (a)
Solution: Major Rock Edict XIII vividly describes the death and suffering in the Kalinga War and Ashoka's remorse. Notably, this edict is omitted at the Kalinga-region sites (Dhauli, Jaugada), replaced by separate edicts — a subtle examiner trap.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Themes in Indian History Part I

33. Arrange the following rulers in correct chronological order:
1. Kanishka 2. Menander 3. Pushyamitra Shunga 4. Rudradaman
(a) 2, 3, 1, 4 (b) 3, 2, 1, 4 (c) 3, 2, 4, 1 (d) 2, 3, 4, 1
Answer: (b)
Solution: Pushyamitra Shunga (c. 185 BCE) → Menander (c. 165–145 BCE) → Kanishka (c. 78 CE onwards) → Rudradaman (c. 130–150 CE). Chronological-confusion questions of this type are a standard examiner trap.
Source: PYQ-style

34. The term 'Gahapati' in early Indian texts referred to:
(a) a royal official collecting land revenue (b) a wandering Buddhist monk (c) the head of a household, often a landowner and controller of family property (d) a guild of merchants
Answer: (c)
Solution: Gahapati (grihapati) denoted the householder head of family, typically a substantial landowner in Pali texts. Merchant guilds were called shreni; revenue officials had other titles.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Themes in Indian History Part I

35. Which Gupta ruler assumed the title 'Vikramaditya' after defeating the Shakas?
(a) Chandragupta I (b) Samudragupta (c) Kumaragupta I (d) Chandragupta II
Answer: (d)
Solution: Chandragupta II defeated the Shaka (Western Kshatrapa) rulers and took the title Vikramaditya. The trap is Chandragupta I, who founded the Gupta era and took the title Maharajadhiraja but did not fight the Shakas.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Themes in Indian History Part I

36. The Fourth Buddhist Council, which split Buddhism into Hinayana and Mahayana, was held during the reign of:
(a) Ashoka (b) Kalashoka (c) Harshavardhana (d) Kanishka
Answer: (d)
Solution: The Fourth Buddhist Council was held at Kundalvana (Kashmir) under Kanishka, where the Hinayana–Mahayana division crystallised. Ashoka presided over the Third Council at Pataliputra — the most common confusion here.
Source: PYQ-style

37. In the Mauryan administration, the officer in charge of the city administration (nagaraka) was assisted by committees described in the account of:
(a) Fa-Hien (b) I-Tsing (c) Hiuen Tsang (d) Megasthenes
Answer: (d)
Solution: Megasthenes, the Seleucid ambassador to Chandragupta Maurya's court, described in his Indica the six committees managing Pataliputra's municipal affairs. Fa-Hien and Hiuen Tsang came centuries later, in Gupta and Harsha times respectively.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Themes in Indian History Part I

38. Which of the following statements about the Harappan civilisation is NOT correct?
(a) The Great Bath was found at Mohenjo-daro (b) The Harappan script has been conclusively deciphered (c) A dockyard was discovered at Lothal (d) Granaries have been found at Harappa and Mohenjo-daro
Answer: (b)
Solution: The Harappan script remains undeciphered to this day — this is the almost-correct-statement trap. The Great Bath (Mohenjo-daro), the Lothal dockyard, and granaries are all well-established findings.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Themes in Indian History Part I

39. The Prayaga Prashasti (Allahabad Pillar Inscription) provides information about the military campaigns of:
(a) Chandragupta Maurya (b) Bimbisara (c) Skandagupta (d) Samudragupta
Answer: (d)
Solution: Composed by Harisena, the Prayaga Prashasti eulogises Samudragupta's conquests, classifying rulers into categories such as Aryavarta kings (uprooted) and Dakshinapatha kings (defeated and released).
Source: NCERT Class 12 Themes in Indian History Part I

40. The Ajanta paintings primarily depict:
(a) Jataka tales and events from the life of Buddha (b) scenes from the Mahabharata war (c) the court life of the Satavahanas (d) Shaivite mythology exclusively
Answer: (a)
Solution: The Ajanta murals (mostly 2nd century BCE to 6th century CE) illustrate Jataka stories and Buddha's life. They are Buddhist in theme, not Mahabharata or Shaivite — a deliberate distractor.
Source: NCERT Class 11 An Introduction to Indian Art

41. Who among the following was the first Tirthankara of Jainism?
(a) Rishabhanatha (b) Mahavira (c) Parshvanatha (d) Neminatha
Answer: (a)
Solution: Rishabhanatha (Adinatha) was the first Tirthankara; Parshvanatha was the 23rd and Mahavira the 24th. Examiners exploit the fact that Mahavira is the most famous, though he was the last, not the first.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Themes in Indian History Part I

42. The Satavahanas issued coins predominantly made of:
(a) gold (b) lead and potin (c) silver only (d) copper only
Answer: (b)
Solution: The Satavahanas are notable for coins of lead and potin (an alloy), besides some silver. Abundant gold coinage is associated with the Kushanas and Guptas — a frequent metal-confusion trap.
Source: PYQ-style

43. The Chinese pilgrim Fa-Hien visited India during the reign of:
(a) Chandragupta I (b) Chandragupta II (c) Samudragupta (d) Harshavardhana
Answer: (b)
Solution: Fa-Hien came to India (c. 399–414 CE) during Chandragupta II's reign, mainly to collect Buddhist texts. Hiuen Tsang visited under Harshavardhana — the classic pilgrim-ruler pairing trap.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Themes in Indian History Part I

44. Which of the following Harappan sites is located in present-day Afghanistan?
(a) Shortughai (b) Dholavira (c) Banawali (d) Kalibangan
Answer: (a)
Solution: Shortughai, on the Oxus (Amu Darya) in Afghanistan, was a Harappan trading outpost, probably for lapis lazuli. Dholavira is in Gujarat, Banawali in Haryana, and Kalibangan in Rajasthan.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Themes in Indian History Part I

45. The 'Tripitaka', the canonical literature of Buddhism, is written in:
(a) Sanskrit (b) Prakrit (c) Pali (d) Apabhramsha
Answer: (c)
Solution: The three Pitakas — Vinaya, Sutta, and Abhidhamma — were composed in Pali. Jain canonical texts were in Prakrit (Ardhamagadhi), which is the usual confusion.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Themes in Indian History Part I

46. Consider the following statements about Harshavardhana:
1. His court poet Banabhatta wrote the Harshacharita.
2. He was defeated by the Chalukya king Pulakeshin II on the banks of the Narmada.
3. He organised a grand assembly at Kannauj which was attended by Hiuen Tsang.
Which of the statements given above are correct?
(a) 1 and 2 only (b) 2 and 3 only (c) 1 and 3 only (d) 1, 2 and 3
Answer: (d)
Solution: All three are correct: Banabhatta authored Harshacharita and Kadambari; Pulakeshin II checked Harsha's southern expansion at the Narmada (recorded in the Aihole inscription); and the Kannauj assembly honoured Hiuen Tsang.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Themes in Indian History Part I

47. The land grants to Brahmanas during the Gupta period were recorded on:
(a) copper plates (b) rock edicts (c) silver scrolls (d) wooden tablets
Answer: (a)
Solution: Gupta and post-Gupta land grants (agrahara, brahmadeya) were inscribed on copper plates, which were durable legal documents. Rock edicts are characteristic of Ashoka's much earlier period.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Themes in Indian History Part I

48. Which of the following pairs of ancient ports and their regions is correctly matched?
(a) Bharuch (Barygaza) — east coast (b) Muziris — Malabar coast (c) Tamralipti — west coast (d) Arikamedu — Gujarat coast
Answer: (b)
Solution: Muziris was the famous spice-trade port on the Malabar (south-west) coast. Barygaza/Bharuch was on the west coast, Tamralipti on the east (Bengal), and Arikamedu near Puducherry on the Coromandel coast — all deliberately swapped here.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Themes in Indian History Part I

49. The doctrine of 'Syadvada' (the theory of 'may be' or conditional predication) is associated with:
(a) Buddhism (b) Ajivikas (c) Jainism (d) Charvakas
Answer: (c)
Solution: Syadvada, the doctrine that every judgement is true only conditionally, is a Jaina epistemological principle, linked to Anekantavada (many-sidedness of reality). The Ajivikas followed fatalism (niyati) under Makkhali Gosala.
Source: PYQ-style

50. The earliest evidence of cotton cultivation in the Indian subcontinent comes from:
(a) Kalibangan (b) Mehrgarh (c) Lothal (d) Ropar
Answer: (b)
Solution: Mehrgarh (Balochistan), a Neolithic site, provides the earliest evidence of cotton cultivation in the subcontinent. The Greeks called the Indus region's cloth 'Sindon'; later Harappan sites also grew cotton, but Mehrgarh is earliest.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Themes in Indian History Part I
