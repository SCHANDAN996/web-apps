Questions 76–100: Medium

76. Who wrote the novel "The Guide", which was also adapted into a famous Hindi film?
(a) Mulk Raj Anand (b) Khushwant Singh (c) Raja Rao (d) R. K. Narayan
Answer: (d)
Solution: The tempting option is Mulk Raj Anand, another major Indian English novelist of the same era, but he wrote "Untouchable" and "Coolie", not "The Guide". "The Guide" (1958) is by R. K. Narayan and was adapted into the 1965 film "Guide" starring Dev Anand, so the answer is R. K. Narayan.
Source: NCERT Class 11 English (Indian Writing in English)

77. "The God of Small Things", winner of the Booker Prize in 1997, was written by:
(a) Arundhati Roy (b) Kiran Desai (c) Jhumpa Lahiri (d) Anita Desai
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is Kiran Desai, who also won the Booker Prize (2006, for "The Inheritance of Loss") — examiners exploit this confusion between two Indian Booker-winning women. "The God of Small Things" (1997) is Arundhati Roy's debut novel, so the answer is Arundhati Roy.
Source: PYQ-style

78. Who is the author of the Sanskrit play "Abhijnana Shakuntalam"?
(a) Banabhatta (b) Vishakhadatta (c) Kalidasa (d) Shudraka
Answer: (c)
Solution: The tempting wrong option is Banabhatta, but he wrote prose romances "Kadambari" and "Harshacharita", not plays. "Abhijnana Shakuntalam" is Kalidasa's celebrated drama about Shakuntala and King Dushyanta, so the answer is Kalidasa.
Source: NCERT Class 12 (Themes in Indian History)

79. "A Suitable Boy", one of the longest novels published in a single volume, was written by:
(a) Amitav Ghosh (b) Salman Rushdie (c) Vikram Seth (d) Rohinton Mistry
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is Salman Rushdie, whose "Midnight's Children" is equally famous, but that novel deals with partition-era magical realism, not the story of Mrs Rupa Mehra's search for a match for her daughter. "A Suitable Boy" (1993) is by Vikram Seth, so the answer is Vikram Seth.
Source: PYQ-style

80. The autobiography "Wings of Fire" belongs to which Indian President?
(a) Dr. Rajendra Prasad (b) K. R. Narayanan (c) Dr. S. Radhakrishnan (d) Dr. A. P. J. Abdul Kalam
Answer: (d)
Solution: The tempting option is Dr. S. Radhakrishnan because he was a famous writer-President, but his works are philosophical, like "Indian Philosophy". "Wings of Fire" (1999) is the autobiography of Dr. A. P. J. Abdul Kalam, so the answer is Dr. A. P. J. Abdul Kalam.
Source: PYQ-style

81. "Gitanjali", for which Rabindranath Tagore won the Nobel Prize in 1913, is a collection of:
(a) Short stories (b) Plays (c) Essays (d) Poems
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is "short stories" because Tagore wrote famous stories too, but the Nobel citation honoured his poetry. "Gitanjali" (Song Offerings) is a collection of poems, translated into English by Tagore himself, so the answer is poems.
Source: NCERT Class 10 English

82. Who wrote "Anandamath", the novel that contains the song "Vande Mataram"?
(a) Bankim Chandra Chattopadhyay (b) Sarat Chandra Chattopadhyay (c) Rabindranath Tagore (d) Ishwar Chandra Vidyasagar
Answer: (a)
Solution: The tempting option is Tagore because of his association with national songs (he wrote "Jana Gana Mana"), which is a classic examiner's confusion between the two national songs. "Anandamath" (1882) is by Bankim Chandra Chattopadhyay, so the answer is Bankim Chandra Chattopadhyay.
Source: NCERT Class 10 History (Nationalism in India)

83. "The Discovery of India" was written by Jawaharlal Nehru during his imprisonment at:
(a) Cellular Jail, Andaman (b) Ahmednagar Fort (c) Yerwada Jail (d) Naini Jail
Answer: (b)
Solution: The tempting option is Cellular Jail, strongly associated with political prisoners (especially Veer Savarkar), but Nehru was never imprisoned there. "The Discovery of India" was written at Ahmednagar Fort prison during 1944–46, so the answer is Ahmednagar Fort.
Source: NCERT Class 12 History

84. Who authored the book "India Wins Freedom"?
(a) Maulana Abul Kalam Azad (b) Muhammad Ali Jinnah (c) Sardar Vallabhbhai Patel (d) C. Rajagopalachari
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is Sardar Patel, whose role in integration makes his name tempting for any freedom-related title, but he did not write this memoir. "India Wins Freedom" is the autobiographical account of Maulana Abul Kalam Azad, so the answer is Maulana Abul Kalam Azad.
Source: PYQ-style

85. "Malgudi Days", a collection of short stories set in a fictional South Indian town, was written by:
(a) Manohar Malgonkar (b) Ruskin Bond (c) Mulk Raj Anand (d) R. K. Narayan
Answer: (d)
Solution: The tempting option is Ruskin Bond, another beloved short-story writer, but his stories are set in the Himalayan foothills, not Malgudi. The fictional town of Malgudi is the creation of R. K. Narayan, so the answer is R. K. Narayan.
Source: PYQ-style

86. The book "Hind Swaraj" (1909), presenting his philosophy of non-violence and critique of modern civilisation, was written by:
(a) Bal Gangadhar Tilak (b) Mahatma Gandhi (c) Gopal Krishna Gokhale (d) Aurobindo Ghosh
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is Tilak, a contemporary nationalist writer, but he is associated with "Gita Rahasya", written in Mandalay prison. "Hind Swaraj" was written by Mahatma Gandhi aboard a ship in 1909, so the answer is Mahatma Gandhi.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Political Science

87. "Train to Pakistan", a novel set during the Partition of 1947, was written by:
(a) Khushwant Singh (b) Bhisham Sahni (c) Amrita Pritam (d) Saadat Hasan Manto
Answer: (a)
Solution: The tempting option is Bhisham Sahni, whose "Tamas" is also a famous Partition novel — a deliberate similar-theme confusion. "Train to Pakistan" (1956) is by Khushwant Singh, so the answer is Khushwant Singh.
Source: PYQ-style

88. Who wrote the epic poem "Meghaduta" (The Cloud Messenger)?
(a) Bharavi (b) Kalidasa (c) Magha (d) Bhavabhuti
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is Bhavabhuti, a famous Sanskrit dramatist ("Uttararamacharita"), but he wrote plays, not lyric poetry. "Meghaduta", where an exiled yaksha sends a message through a cloud, is by Kalidasa, so the answer is Kalidasa.
Source: NCERT Class 12 (Themes in Indian History)

89. "My Experiments with Truth" is the autobiography of Mahatma Gandhi, originally written in which language?
(a) Gujarati (b) English (c) Hindi (d) Marathi
Answer: (a)
Solution: The tempting option is English because most readers know the book through its English translation, which is exactly the examiner's trap. Gandhi wrote it originally in Gujarati as "Satya na Prayogo", so the answer is Gujarati.
Source: NCERT Class 12 History

90. The novel "Godaan", depicting the life of a poor peasant Hori, was written by:
(a) Mahadevi Verma (b) Harivansh Rai Bachchan (c) Premchand (d) Jaishankar Prasad
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is Jaishankar Prasad, a great Hindi writer, but he is known for poetry and drama like "Kamayani", not peasant novels. "Godaan" (1936) is Munshi Premchand's masterpiece, so the answer is Premchand.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Hindi

91. "The Argumentative Indian", a collection of essays on Indian history and identity, was written by:
(a) Ramachandra Guha (b) Amartya Sen (c) Shashi Tharoor (d) Bipan Chandra
Answer: (b)
Solution: The tempting option is Ramachandra Guha, a prolific historian-essayist ("India After Gandhi"), but this particular essay collection is by Nobel laureate economist Amartya Sen, so the answer is Amartya Sen.
Source: PYQ-style

92. Who wrote "Pather Panchali", the Bengali novel adapted into Satyajit Ray's famous film?
(a) Tarashankar Bandyopadhyay (b) Sarat Chandra Chattopadhyay (c) Bibhutibhushan Bandyopadhyay (d) Manik Bandyopadhyay
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is the cluster of similar Bengali surnames — Tarashankar and Manik Bandyopadhyay are deliberately confusing near-names. "Pather Panchali" (1929) is by Bibhutibhushan Bandyopadhyay, so the answer is Bibhutibhushan Bandyopadhyay.
Source: PYQ-style

93. "Glimpses of World History" was written by Jawaharlal Nehru in the form of:
(a) Letters to his daughter (b) A diary (c) Speeches (d) Newspaper articles
Answer: (a)
Solution: The tempting option is "a diary" because the book was written in prison, but its actual form is a series of letters written to Indira Gandhi — the same format as his "Letters from a Father to His Daughter". So the answer is letters to his daughter.
Source: NCERT Class 8 History

94. The book "The Inheritance of Loss", winner of the Man Booker Prize 2006, was written by:
(a) Kiran Desai (b) Anita Desai (c) Arundhati Roy (d) Vikram Seth
Answer: (a)
Solution: The classic trap is Anita Desai, who is Kiran Desai's mother and a celebrated novelist herself — examiners exploit this mother-daughter confusion. "The Inheritance of Loss" is by Kiran Desai, so the answer is Kiran Desai.
Source: PYQ-style

95. Who is the author of "Arthashastra", the ancient Indian treatise on statecraft?
(a) Panini (b) Kautilya (c) Patanjali (d) Manu
Answer: (b)
Solution: The tempting option is Manu, associated with "Manusmriti", a text on law and society — a similar ancient-text confusion. "Arthashastra" on politics and economics is attributed to Kautilya (Chanakya), so the answer is Kautilya.
Source: NCERT Class 12 (Themes in Indian History)

96. "Ignited Minds", a book addressed to the youth of India, was written by:
(a) Dr. Manmohan Singh (b) Dr. A. P. J. Abdul Kalam (c) Narendra Modi (d) Dr. S. Radhakrishnan
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is Dr. S. Radhakrishnan, another scholar-President, but his works are on philosophy and religion. "Ignited Minds" (2002) is by Dr. A. P. J. Abdul Kalam, so the answer is Dr. A. P. J. Abdul Kalam.
Source: PYQ-style

97. The novel "Midnight's Children", which won the Booker of Bookers, was written by:
(a) Rohinton Mistry (b) V. S. Naipaul (c) Salman Rushdie (d) Amitav Ghosh
Answer: (c)
Solution: The tempting option is V. S. Naipaul, a Nobel-winning Indian-origin writer ("A House for Mr Biswas"), but "Midnight's Children" (1981), about children born at the midnight of Independence, is by Salman Rushdie, so the answer is Salman Rushdie.
Source: PYQ-style

98. "Rajtarangini", a historical chronicle of the kings of Kashmir, was written by:
(a) Jayadeva (b) Kalidasa (c) Bilhana (d) Kalhana
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is Kalidasa — the similar-sounding name Kalhana vs Kalidasa is a deliberate examiner's confusion. "Rajtarangini" (12th century) is by Kalhana, so the answer is Kalhana.
Source: NCERT Class 12 (Themes in Indian History)

99. Who wrote the book "Why I Am an Atheist" while in prison?
(a) Ram Prasad Bismil (b) Chandrashekhar Azad (c) Sukhdev (d) Bhagat Singh
Answer: (d)
Solution: The tempting option is Ram Prasad Bismil, who also wrote in prison, but he wrote poetry and his autobiography. "Why I Am an Atheist" (1930) is the famous essay by Bhagat Singh written in Lahore jail, so the answer is Bhagat Singh.
Source: PYQ-style

100. "The White Tiger", winner of the Man Booker Prize 2008, was written by:
(a) Neel Mukherjee (b) Jeet Thayil (c) Aravind Adiga (d) Mohsin Hamid
Answer: (c)
Solution: The tempting options are other contemporary South Asian novelists, but "The White Tiger", narrated by the driver Balram Halwai, is the debut novel of Aravind Adiga, so the answer is Aravind Adiga.
Source: PYQ-style
