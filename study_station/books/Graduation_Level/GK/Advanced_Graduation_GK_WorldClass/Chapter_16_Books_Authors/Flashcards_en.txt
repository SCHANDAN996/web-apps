Card 1
Front: Who wrote "The God of Small Things," the Booker Prize-winning novel of 1997?
Back: Arundhati Roy. It was her debut novel and won the Booker Prize in 1997.

Card 2
Front: Which ancient Sanskrit play by Kalidasa tells the story of King Dushyanta and Shakuntala?
Back: Abhijnanashakuntalam (The Recognition of Shakuntala).

Card 3
Front: "The Guide," later adapted into a famous Hindi film, was written by which Indian English novelist?
Back: R. K. Narayan. He is also famous for the fictional town of Malgudi.

Card 4
Front: Which book by Jawaharlal Nehru was written as letters to his daughter Indira from prison?
Back: Glimpses of World History. (Trap: "The Discovery of India" was also written in prison, but it is not a series of letters.)

Card 5
Front: Who authored "Anandamath," the novel that contains the song "Vande Mataram"?
Back: Bankim Chandra Chatterjee (1882).

Card 6
Front: "Midnight's Children," which won the Booker of Bookers, was written by whom?
Back: Salman Rushdie (Booker Prize 1981; Booker of Bookers 1993).

Card 7
Front: Which Mughal emperor wrote his own memoir, the "Tuzuk-i-Jahangiri"?
Back: Jahangir. (Trap: Babur wrote Baburnama; Jahangir's memoir is Tuzuk-i-Jahangiri.)

Card 8
Front: Who translated the "Gitanjali" into English, helping Tagore win the Nobel Prize in 1913?
Back: Rabindranath Tagore himself translated it into English.

Card 9
Front: Mnemonic trick: How can you remember the authors of "India Wins Freedom" and "Discovery of India"?
Back: "Azad wins Freedom, Nehru Discovers India" — Maulana Abul Kalam Azad wrote "India Wins Freedom"; Jawaharlal Nehru wrote "The Discovery of India."

Card 10
Front: Who wrote "Pather Panchali," the Bengali novel adapted into Satyajit Ray's film?
Back: Bibhutibhushan Bandyopadhyay.

Card 11
Front: "My Experiments with Truth" is the autobiography of which leader?
Back: Mahatma Gandhi. It was originally written in Gujarati.

Card 12
Front: Which book by Amartya Sen links economics with social choice and won attention after his 1998 Nobel?
Back: "Development as Freedom" (1999).

Card 13
Front: Examiner's trap: "The Inheritance of Loss" — Kiran Desai or Anita Desai?
Back: Kiran Desai (Booker Prize 2006). Anita Desai, her mother, wrote "Clear Light of Day" and "Fire on the Mountain" — a classic mother-daughter confusion trap.

Card 14
Front: Who wrote "A Suitable Boy," one of the longest novels published in a single volume?
Back: Vikram Seth (1993).

Card 15
Front: "Train to Pakistan," a novel about Partition, was written by which journalist-author?
Back: Khushwant Singh (1956).

Card 16
Front: Mnemonic trick: How to remember who wrote "Godan," "Gitanjali," and "Gora"?
Back: "Premchand's Godan, Tagore's Gitanjali and Gora" — Godan (1936) is Premchand's Hindi classic; both Gitanjali and Gora are by Rabindranath Tagore.

Card 17
Front: Who wrote "The Argumentative Indian"?
Back: Amartya Sen (2005).

Card 18
Front: Examiner's trap: "Wings of Fire" — is it by A. P. J. Abdul Kalam alone?
Back: It is the autobiography of A. P. J. Abdul Kalam, written with Arun Tiwari (1999). Questions sometimes credit only Kalam, which is accepted, but the co-author is Arun Tiwari.

Card 19
Front: Which ancient Tamil epic was written by Ilango Adigal?
Back: Silappadikaram. (Its twin epic, Manimekalai, was written by Seethalai Saathanar.)

Card 20
Front: Who authored "Hind Swaraj" (1909), a critique of modern civilization?
Back: Mahatma Gandhi. It was written in Gujarati aboard a ship during his return from London to South Africa.
