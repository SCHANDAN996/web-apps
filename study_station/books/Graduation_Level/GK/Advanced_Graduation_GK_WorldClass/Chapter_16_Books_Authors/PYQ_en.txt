# 📊 PYQ Analysis — Books & Authors (English)

## 1. How Exams Ask Questions from This Chapter

Across SSC (CGL/CHSL/MTS/GD), Banking (IBPS/SBI), Railway (NTPC/Group D) and State PCS prelims, Books & Authors questions follow a small set of recurring patterns. The chapter is **static**, so the same facts recycle for years — the examiner's creativity lies in the *traps*, not the content.

**Recurring sub-topics that appear again and again:**

1. **Classic Indian English writing** — *The Guide*, *The God of Small Things*, *Midnight's Children*, *The White Tiger*, *Train to Pakistan*.
2. **Hindi literature giants** — Premchand (*Godaan*, *Gaban*), Mahadevi Verma, Harivansh Rai Bachchan (*Madhushala*), Ramdhari Singh Dinkar (*Rashmirathi*, *Kurukshetra*).
3. **Books by national leaders** — *My Experiments with Truth* (Gandhi's autobiography), *The Discovery of India* (Nehru), *An Autobiography* (Nehru), *Wings of Fire* (Kalam).
4. **Ancient/Sanskrit works** — *Arthashastra* (Kautilya), *Abhijnanasakuntalam* (Kalidasa), *Mrichchhakatika* (Shudraka), *Rajatarangini* (Kalhana).
5. **Award-linked books** — Booker, Jnanpith, Sahitya Akademi, Pulitzer winners and their famous works.
6. **Foreign classics frequently asked** — *War and Peace* (Tolstoy), *A Brief History of Time* (Hawking), *The Communist Manifesto* (Marx & Engels), *Mein Kampf* (Hitler).
7. **Books by sportspersons** — *Playing It My Way* (Sachin Tendulkar), *The Test of My Life* (Yuvraj Singh), *A Shot at History* (Abhinav Bindra).

**Exam-wise flavour (qualitative):**

- **State PCS:** statement-based questions, matching lists (Book–Author), ancient texts, award winners.
- **SSC CGL/CHSL:** direct one-liners — "Who wrote X?" — with near-identical distractors.
- **Banking (IBPS/SBI):** recent award books + autobiographies of public figures.
- **Railway:** simplest form — famous book ↔ famous author, often Hindi literature.

## 2. The Examiner's Traps (Jaal-Prakar)

| Trap Type | How It Looks in This Chapter |
|---|---|
| **Almost Correct** | *Godaan* attributed to "Mahadevi Verma" instead of Premchand — both are Hindi greats, so it feels right. |
| **Chronological Confusion** | Asking which book came first — *Gaban* (1931) vs *Godaan* (1936); or confusing *The Discovery of India* (1946, written in jail) with *Glimpses of World History*. |
| **Similar Names** | Arundhati Roy vs Anita Desai; *Midnight's Children* (Rushdie) vs other "Midnight" titles; Kalidasa vs Kalhana. |
| **Negative Phrasing** | "Which of the following is NOT written by Kalidasa?" — students misread and pick a genuine Kalidasa work. |
| **Language Swap** | Original language asked: *Gitanjali* was written in Bengali (Tagore translated it himself into English). |
| **Co-authorship** | *The Communist Manifesto* — Marx **and** Engels; options often give only Marx. |

## 3. Top 10 High-Yield PYQ-Style Questions (with Solutions)

**Q1.** Who is the author of *The God of Small Things*?
(a) Anita Desai (b) Arundhati Roy (c) Kiran Desai (d) Jhumpa Lahiri
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** Arundhati Roy won the Booker Prize (1997) for this debut novel. Trap: **Similar Names** — Kiran Desai is also a Booker winner (*The Inheritance of Loss*, 2006), so examiners place both in options.
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q2.** *Godaan*, considered one of the greatest Hindi novels, was written by:
(a) Mahadevi Verma (b) Munshi Premchand (c) Harivansh Rai Bachchan (d) Ramdhari Singh Dinkar
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** Premchand's *Godaan* (1936) is his masterpiece. Trap: **Almost Correct** — Mahadevi Verma is a Hindi literary giant, but a poet, not the novelist of *Godaan*.
**Source:** NCERT Class 10 Hindi / PYQ-style

**Q3.** Which of the following is NOT a work of Kalidasa?
(a) Abhijnanasakuntalam (b) Meghaduta (c) Mrichchhakatika (d) Raghuvamsha
**Answer: (c)**
**Solution:** *Mrichchhakatika* (The Little Clay Cart) is by **Shudraka**, not Kalidasa. Trap: **Negative Phrasing** — read the "NOT" first, then eliminate.
**Source:** NCERT Class 12 (Themes in Indian History) / PYQ-style

**Q4.** *My Experiments with Truth* is the autobiography of:
(a) Jawaharlal Nehru (b) M.K. Gandhi (c) B.R. Ambedkar (d) Subhas Chandra Bose
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** Gandhi's autobiography, originally written in Gujarati, covers his life up to the early 1920s. Trap: **Similar Names** — Nehru's autobiography is *An Autobiography*; don't confuse the two leaders' life-writings.
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q5.** *Arthashastra*, the ancient treatise on statecraft, is attributed to:
(a) Kalhana (b) Banabhatta (c) Kautilya (Chanakya) (d) Megasthenes
**Answer: (c)**
**Solution:** Kautilya (Chanakya/Vishnugupta) wrote the *Arthashastra*. Trap: **Similar Names** — Megasthenes wrote *Indica* (a foreign traveller's account), a frequent distractor.
**Source:** NCERT Class 12 History / PYQ-style

**Q6.** *Wings of Fire* is the autobiography of which Indian President?
(a) Dr. Rajendra Prasad (b) Dr. S. Radhakrishnan (c) Dr. A.P.J. Abdul Kalam (d) Dr. Zakir Husain
**Answer: (c)**
**Solution:** Kalam's *Wings of Fire* (1999) traces his journey from Rameswaram to ISRO/DRDO. Trap: **Almost Correct** — all four are Presidents, so the option list itself is the trap.
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q7.** Who wrote *Midnight's Children*?
(a) V.S. Naipaul (b) Salman Rushdie (c) Vikram Seth (d) Amitav Ghosh
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** Rushdie's *Midnight's Children* (1981) won the Booker Prize. Trap: **Similar Names** — all four are celebrated Indian-English novelists; Vikram Seth wrote *A Suitable Boy*, Amitav Ghosh the *Ibis* trilogy.
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q8.** *Rajatarangini*, the chronicle of the kings of Kashmir, was composed by:
(a) Kalidasa (b) Kalhana (c) Bilhana (d) Bana
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** Kalhana wrote *Rajatarangini* in Sanskrit (12th century CE). Trap: **Similar Names** — Kalidasa/Kalhana/Bilhana sound alike; Bana wrote *Harshacharita* and *Kadambari*.
**Source:** NCERT Class 12 History / PYQ-style

**Q9.** *The Communist Manifesto* was authored by:
(a) Karl Marx alone (b) Friedrich Engels alone (c) Karl Marx and Friedrich Engels (d) Vladimir Lenin
**Answer: (c)**
**Solution:** Published in 1848, it was jointly written by Marx and Engels. Trap: **Co-authorship** — option (a) tempts because Marx is the more famous name.
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q10.** *A Brief History of Time*, the landmark popular-science book, was written by:
(a) Stephen Hawking (b) Carl Sagan (c) Richard Dawkins (d) Brian Cox
**Answer: (a)**
**Solution:** Hawking's 1988 classic explains cosmology for lay readers. Trap: **Similar Names** — Carl Sagan (*Cosmos*) is the most common wrong pick.
**Source:** PYQ-style

## 4. The 15/45-Second Rule Applied

**The rule:** If a question is a direct fact you know cold → answer in **15 seconds** and move on. If it needs elimination or has a trap (negative phrasing, similar names) → give it up to **45 seconds**, never more. Beyond 45 seconds, mark and skip.

**Applied to Q1 (15-second question):** "Author of *The God of Small Things*" is a pure recall fact. If you know Arundhati Roy → mark (b) in under 15 seconds. Do **not** re-read the options; the Desai/Lahiri distractors only create doubt if you linger.

**Applied to Q3 (45-second question):** The "NOT" forces verification of all four options. Budget the full 45 seconds: recall *Abhijnanasakuntalam* ✓ Kalidasa, *Meghaduta* ✓ Kalidasa, *Raghuvamsha* ✓ Kalidasa → *Mrichchhakatika* is the odd one (Shudraka). If after 45 seconds you still can't confirm, mark (c) as best-elimination and move on — never burn 2 minutes on one static GK question.

**Strategy summary:** In this chapter, most questions are 15-second recall; the rest are 45-second elimination questions. Never guess blindly on negative-phrasing questions — re-read the stem once.
