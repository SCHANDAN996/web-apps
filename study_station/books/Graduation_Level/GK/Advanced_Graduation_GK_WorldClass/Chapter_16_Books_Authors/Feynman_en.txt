# The Feynman Corner: Books & Authors

## The Most Confusing Fact in This Chapter

Ask any aspirant what trips them up in Books & Authors, and the answer is almost always the same: **"The book was written by X, but in which language, and who translated it?"**

The classic trap is **Gitanjali**. Everyone knows Tagore won the Nobel Prize in 1913 for it. But here is where students fall:

- Gitanjali was **originally written in Bengali** (published 1910).
- Tagore **translated it into English himself** (published 1912).
- The Nobel was awarded for the **English version**, *Gitanjali: Song Offerings*.

So if the exam asks "Gitanjali was originally written in which language?" and you answer "English" because the Nobel was for the English version — you lose the mark. The examiner is counting on exactly that confusion.

## Explaining It Like You're 12

Imagine you write a poem in Hindi for your school magazine. Your poem is so good that your English teacher asks you to translate it into English so the whole country can read it. You translate it **yourself** — nobody else does it for you. Later, you win a national prize, and the certificate mentions the **English** version.

Now, if someone asks, "What language did you *originally* write the poem in?" — the answer is still **Hindi**. The prize doesn't change where the poem was born.

That's Tagore and Gitanjali. **Original: Bengali. Translator: Tagore himself. Nobel: for the English version.** Three different facts, three different possible questions — and the examiner mixes them up on purpose.

The same trap appears elsewhere:

- **Premchand** is a pen name — his real name was **Dhanpat Rai Srivastava**. (First pen name: "Nawab Rai.")
- **Munshi** is a title, not part of his name.
- *Godan*, *Gaban*, *Nirmala* — all Premchand. But *Maila Anchal*? That's **Phanishwar Nath Renu**. Similar era, similar themes — classic examiner swap.

## The Unforgettable Trick: "BOAT"

For any famous book, fix **four anchors** in memory — **B-O-A-T**:

- **B** — Book name
- **O** — Original language
- **A** — Author (real name, not just pen name)
- **T** — Translator (if any)

If you can fill all four, no trap option can touch you. Example:

| Book | Original | Author | Translator |
|---|---|---|---|
| Gitanjali | Bengali | Rabindranath Tagore | Tagore himself |
| Godan | Hindi | Premchand (Dhanpat Rai) | — |
| Anandamath | Bengali | Bankim Chandra Chatterjee | — |

## One Question for You

If the Nobel Prize was given for the English *Gitanjali*, why is "Bengali" the correct answer to "Gitanjali was originally written in which language?" — explain it to a friend in two sentences. If you can, the concept is yours forever.
