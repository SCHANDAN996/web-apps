# 📊 PYQ Analysis — Indian Constitution (Basic)

## 1. How Exams Ask Questions from This Chapter

Across UPSC (Prelims), SSC (CGL/CHSL/MTS), Banking (IBPS/SBI), Railway (NTPC/Group D) and State PCS prelims, this chapter is a **permanent fixture** — every cycle of every major exam carries questions from it. The pattern, in words:

- **Articles and their content** dominate: "Which Article deals with…?" is the single most repeated question frame. Articles 12–35 (Fundamental Rights), 368 (Amendment), 352/356/360 (Emergency), 74/75 (Council of Ministers), 32 vs 226 (writs) are the evergreen zones.
- **Sources of the Constitution** ("Borrowed features") appear constantly in SSC and Railway — matching features with countries (e.g., Directive Principles ← Ireland, Fundamental Rights ← USA, Parliamentary system ← UK).
- **Chronology questions**: order of events — Cabinet Mission (1946), Constituent Assembly first meeting (9 Dec 1946), adoption (26 Nov 1949), commencement (26 Jan 1950). Exams love swapping two dates.
- **Amendments**: 42nd (1976, "Mini-Constitution"), 44th (1978), 52nd (Anti-Defection, 1985), 61st (voting age 21→18, 1989), 73rd/74th (Panchayati Raj/Municipalities, 1992), 86th (Right to Education, 2002), 101st (GST, 2016).
- **Statement-based questions** (UPSC/PCS style): "Which of the following statements is/are correct?" — usually built on one almost-correct statement.
- **Negative phrasing**: "Which of the following is NOT a Fundamental Right?" — a classic trap frame.

## 3. Exam-Wise Flavour

- **UPSC Prelims**: conceptual, statement-based, "almost correct" traps; links Constitution with current debates.
- **SSC CGL/CHSL**: direct factual recall — Articles, dates, borrowed features, amendments.
- **Banking (IBPS/SBI)**: lighter — Preamble, Fundamental Rights, recent amendments, basic structure.
- **Railway NTPC/Group D**: one-liner facts — who, when, which Article, which Schedule.
- **State PCS**: mix of SSC-style facts + UPSC-style statements; often state-linked (Governor, State Legislature).

## 4. The Examiner's Traps (with examples)

**Trap 1 — Almost Correct Statement.**
"Fundamental Rights are absolute and cannot be suspended under any circumstances."
❌ Wrong — they are not absolute (reasonable restrictions exist) and can be suspended during Emergency (except Articles 20 & 21). The sentence *sounds* right because FRs are "guaranteed."

**Trap 2 — Chronological Confusion.**
"The Constitution was adopted on 26 January 1950."
❌ Adopted on **26 November 1949**; *commenced/enforced* on 26 January 1950. Exams swap these two dates deliberately.

**Trap 3 — Similar Names / Numbers.**
Article 32 (writs — Supreme Court) vs Article 226 (writs — High Courts). Dr. Ambedkar called **Article 32** the "heart and soul" of the Constitution — not Article 226, not Article 21.

**Trap 4 — Negative Phrasing.**
"Which of the following is NOT a Fundamental Duty?" — students misread "NOT" under time pressure. Underline negatives before answering.

**Trap 5 — Wrong Attribution.**
"The idea of Directive Principles was borrowed from the USA." ❌ — DPSP came from **Ireland**; USA gave Fundamental Rights and judicial review.

**Trap 6 — Half-Right Pairs.**
"The 42nd Amendment added 'Secular' and 'Socialist' to the Preamble" ✅ — but paired with "…and was passed in 1978" ❌ (it was 1976; 1978 was the 44th).

## 5. Top 10 High-Yield PYQ-Style Questions (with solutions)

**Q1.** The words "Socialist" and "Secular" were added to the Preamble by which amendment?
(a) 42nd Amendment, 1976 (b) 44th Amendment, 1978 (c) 52nd Amendment, 1985 (d) 24th Amendment, 1971
**Answer: (a)**
**Solution:** The 42nd Amendment (1976), called the "Mini-Constitution," inserted "Socialist," "Secular" and "Integrity" into the Preamble. Trap type: Chronological Confusion — 44th (1978) is the tempting distractor.
**Source:** PYQ-style (NCERT Class 11 Polity — Indian Constitution at Work)

**Q2.** Which Article did Dr. B.R. Ambedkar call the "heart and soul" of the Constitution?
(a) Article 21 (b) Article 32 (c) Article 226 (d) Article 14
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** Article 32 — the right to constitutional remedies (writs via the Supreme Court). Trap type: Similar Numbers — Article 226 gives the same writ power to High Courts, but Ambedkar's phrase belongs to Article 32.
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q3.** The concept of Directive Principles of State Policy was borrowed from:
(a) USA (b) UK (c) Ireland (d) Canada
**Answer: (c)**
**Solution:** DPSP ← Ireland (which itself borrowed from Spain). USA gave Fundamental Rights; UK gave the parliamentary system; Canada gave federation with a strong centre. Trap type: Wrong Attribution.
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q4.** The Constitution of India was adopted by the Constituent Assembly on:
(a) 26 January 1950 (b) 26 November 1949 (c) 15 August 1947 (d) 9 December 1946
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** Adopted 26 Nov 1949 (celebrated as Constitution Day); enforced 26 Jan 1950 (Republic Day). 9 Dec 1946 was the Assembly's first meeting. Trap type: Chronological Confusion.
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q5.** Which of the following is NOT a Fundamental Right under the current Constitution?
(a) Right to Equality (b) Right to Property (c) Right against Exploitation (d) Right to Constitutional Remedies
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** Right to Property was removed from Fundamental Rights by the 44th Amendment (1978) and made a legal right under Article 300A. Trap type: Negative Phrasing + Almost Correct (it *was* once an FR).
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q6.** The minimum voting age was reduced from 21 to 18 years by:
(a) 42nd Amendment (b) 44th Amendment (c) 61st Amendment (d) 73rd Amendment
**Answer: (c)**
**Solution:** The 61st Amendment (1989) amended Article 326. Trap type: Similar Numbers — amendment numbers are classic distractors; link "61 → 18" as a memory hook.
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q7.** Which Schedule of the Constitution contains the list of recognised languages?
(a) Seventh Schedule (b) Eighth Schedule (c) Ninth Schedule (d) Tenth Schedule
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** Eighth Schedule — 22 languages (as of 2024). Seventh = Union/State/Concurrent lists; Ninth = laws immune from judicial review; Tenth = Anti-Defection. Trap type: Similar Numbers.
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q8.** Under which Article can the President proclaim a National Emergency?
(a) Article 352 (b) Article 356 (c) Article 360 (d) Article 368
**Answer: (a)**
**Solution:** Article 352 = National Emergency; 356 = President's Rule in a state; 360 = Financial Emergency; 368 = Amendment procedure. Trap type: Similar Numbers — the 35x/36x cluster is a favourite confusion zone.
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q9.** The idea of the Preamble of the Indian Constitution was borrowed from:
(a) UK (b) USA (c) Australia (d) USSR
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** The Preamble's idea came from the **USA**; its ideals of justice, liberty, equality and fraternity echo the French Revolution. Trap type: Wrong Attribution.
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q10.** Who was the Chairman of the Drafting Committee of the Constituent Assembly?
(a) Jawaharlal Nehru (b) Dr. Rajendra Prasad (c) Dr. B.R. Ambedkar (d) Sardar Patel
**Answer: (c)**
**Solution:** Dr. B.R. Ambedkar chaired the Drafting Committee (set up 29 Aug 1947). Dr. Rajendra Prasad was President of the Constituent Assembly — the most common wrong pick. Trap type: Similar Names / role-swap.
**Source:** PYQ-style

## 6. The 15/45-Second Rule Applied

**⏱ 15-Second Question — Q4 (adoption date):** If you have the two dates (26 Nov 1949 / 26 Jan 1950) locked in memory, this is a pure recall hit — answer in under 15 seconds and bank the time. If you hesitate between them, mark and move; don't burn 90 seconds reconstructing the timeline mid-paper.

**⏱ 45-Second Question — Q5 (NOT a Fundamental Right):** Negative-phrasing questions demand a slow, careful read. Budget up to 45 seconds: underline "NOT," then verify each option against the FR list (Equality 14–18, Freedom 19–22, Exploitation 23–24, Religion 25–28, Cultural-Educational 29–30, Remedies 32). The trap (Right to Property *was* an FR) punishes rushed reading — this is where 45 seconds is well spent.

**Rule of thumb:** Direct recall (dates, Articles, borrowed features) = 15 seconds. Negative phrasing, statement-based, or "which pair is correct" = up to 45 seconds. Anything beyond 45 seconds — mark, skip, return in the second pass.

## 7. Key Takeaways for Revision

1. Master the **Article clusters**: 12–35, 352/356/360, 32/226, 368 — these are the highest-yield zones.
2. Lock the **four dates**: 9 Dec 1946 → 26 Nov 1949 → 26 Jan 1950 (and 29 Aug 1947 for the Drafting Committee).
3. Make a one-line **borrowed-features table** and revise it weekly — it converts directly into marks.
4. Practise reading **negative questions** slowly; most "silly mistakes" in this chapter are misread NOTs.
5. For every amendment, remember **number + year + one-line change** — exams attack all three.
