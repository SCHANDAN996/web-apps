# 🪄 Memory Hooks & Short Tricks — Indian Constitution (Basic)

---

**Box 1 — Preamble Words Order**
*"We the SOLDIERs of Justice"*
**S**overeign **S**ocialist **S**ecular **D**emocratic **R**epublic — then **J**ustice, **L**iberty, **E**quality, **F**raternity.
Hook: A soldier fights for the Republic and carries JLEF in his pocket.

---

**Box 2 — Sources of the Constitution**
*"I Cried UGC-ICG"*
- **I**reland → Directive Principles + Nomination of Rajya Sabha members
- **C**anada → Federation with a strong Centre
- **U**SA → Fundamental Rights, Judicial Review, Impeachment of President
- **G**ermany (Weimar) → Emergency provisions
- **I**ndia (Government of India Act, 1935) → Federal scheme, Public Service Commissions
- **G**oI Act 1935 → the single largest source of borrowed features
Hook: *"India Cried UGC-ICG"* — one cry, all the major sources.

---

**Box 3 — Fundamental Rights (Articles 12–35)**
*"14-19-23-25-29-32"*
Sing it like a phone number: **Equality (14–18), Freedom (19–22), Exploitation (23–24), Religion (25–28), Culture-Education (29–30), Remedies (32)**.
Hook: "At 14 you're equal, at 19 you're free, at 23 no one exploits you, at 25 you pray, at 29 you learn, at 32 you complain to court."

---

**Box 4 — Article 32 = "Heart and Soul"**
Dr. B.R. Ambedkar called Article 32 the **heart and soul** of the Constitution.
Hook: *"When your RIGHTS are hurt, your HEART goes straight to the Supreme Court (Art 32)."*
Trap alert: Art 226 (High Courts) is wider in scope but is NOT called heart-and-soul.

---

**Box 5 — Writs: "HCM QP"**
**H**abeas Corpus, **C**ertiorari, **M**andamus, **Q**uo Warranto, **P**rohibition.
- Habeas = "Have the body" (produce the person)
- Mandamus = "We command" (to a public official)
- Certiorari = "Certify the record" (quash a lower court order)
- Prohibition = "Stop before you act" (prevent a lower court)
- Quo Warranto = "By what authority?" (challenge an office-holder)
Hook: *"The CM's Habitual Quarrelsome Politics"* → H-C-M-Q-P.

---

**Box 6 — DPSP Articles (36–51)**
*"39-40-44-47-48-50-51"*
- **39A** → Equal justice and free legal aid
- **40** → Village Panchayats
- **44** → Uniform Civil Code
- **47** → Nutrition & prohibition of liquor
- **48** → Cow protection, scientific agriculture
- **50** → Separate judiciary from executive
- **51** → International peace
Hook: *"At 39 get a lawyer, at 40 join the panchayat, at 44 one law for all, at 47 eat healthy, at 48 save the cow, at 50 judges go solo, at 51 make world peace."*

---

**Box 7 — Fundamental Duties (Article 51A)**
*"42nd Amendment, 1976, 10 duties — on the Swaran Singh Committee's recommendation; 86th Amendment, 2002 added the 11th (child's education)."*
Hook: *"42 gave 10, 86 gave 1 more — 10+1 = 11 duties."*
Trap: Duties were borrowed from the **USSR**, not the USA.

---

**Box 8 — Schedules: "TEARS OF OLD PM"**
12 Schedules:
**T**erritory(1) **E**moluments(2) **A**ffirmations/Oaths(3) **R**ajya Sabha seats(4) **S**cheduled Areas(5) **O**ther tribal areas — Assam/Meghalaya/Tripura/Mizoram(6) **F**ederal lists(7) **O**fficial Languages(8) **L**and reforms(9) **D**efection/Anti-defection(10) **P**anchayats(11) **M**unicipalities(12).
Hook: *"Tears of the Old PM"* — one phrase, all 12.

---

**Box 9 — Parts of the Constitution**
*"The Union(Part V) met the States(Part VI), got Citizenship(Part II), gave Rights(Part III), Directions(Part IV), Duties(Part IVA), then Elections(Part XV), Emergency(Part XVIII), Amendment(Part XX), and finally Short Title & Repeals(Part XXII)."*
Hook: A love story — Union meets States, and the wedding has 22 parts (originally).
Trap alert: The **12 Schedules are separate** — they are NOT a Part. Part XXII is "Short Title, Commencement, Authoritative Text in Hindi and Repeals".

---

**Box 10 — Amendment: Article 368**
*"368 = 2 routes under the Article: Special majority, and Special majority + ratification by half the States."*
Hook: *"3-6-8: 2 ways inside, 1 way outside."* Some provisions (e.g., creation of new states, changes to the Fifth/Sixth Schedules) can be amended by a **simple majority** — but those are **outside Article 368** and are not treated as constitutional amendments under it.
Trap: Constitutional amendment bills need **no joint sitting** (Art 108 doesn't apply) and the President **cannot refuse** assent (24th Amendment, 1971).

---

**Box 11 — Emergency Trio (352, 356, 360)**
*"352 = War (National), 356 = State failure (President's Rule), 360 = Wallet empty (Financial)."*
Hook: *"352 the nation burns, 356 the state government falls, 360 the money's gone."*
Trap: Financial Emergency (360) has **never** been declared in India.

---

**Box 12 — Constitutional Bodies Quick-Count**
Election Commission (324), UPSC (315–323), Finance Commission (280), CAG (148), AG (76), SC (124), HC (214).
Hook: *"76 advises the President, 148 audits the money, 280 divides the money, 315 recruits the officers, 324 runs the elections."*

---

**Box 13 — Citizenship Articles 5–11 (Part II)**
*"5 at commencement, 6 migrants from Pakistan, 7 migrants to Pakistan, 8 Indians abroad, 9 no foreign citizenship, 10 continuance, 11 Parliament's power."*
Hook: *"5 born here, 6 came here, 7 went there, 8 live abroad, 9 can't be double, 10 keep going, 11 Parliament decides."*

---

**Box 14 — Six-Month Minister Rule**
Hook: *"One person, one seat — but a Minister gets 6 months to find one."*
Remember: A non-member can be a Minister (including PM/CM) for **6 months maximum** without being elected to the legislature (Art 75(5) / Art 164(4)).

---

**Box 15 — "Borrowed but Blended"**
Hook: *"India didn't copy the Constitution — it cooked a khichdi: British parliamentary system, American rights, Irish directives, Canadian federalism, Soviet duties, German emergency, Australian concurrent list, South African amendment procedure."*
One dish, eight countries — examiners love asking "borrowed from where?"

---

## ⏭️ Skip Strategy — When to Leave the Question

1. **"Which of the following pairs is correctly matched?" (source-borrowing questions):** If you can verify only 3 of 4 pairs and the 4th involves a country you can't recall (e.g., "concurrent list — Australia or Canada?"), **skip**. These questions are designed so one wrong pair kills the answer — guessing has negative expected value.

2. **Exact Article-number questions with look-alike options** (e.g., Art 311 vs 312 vs 315): If you haven't memorised the number, don't guess between adjacent articles — examiners deliberately place consecutive numbers as options. Skip unless you can eliminate at least two.

3. **Chronology/statement questions on amendments** (e.g., "Arrange: 42nd, 44th, 52nd, 61st Amendment"): If you can't place even one amendment confidently, the permutation count makes blind guessing nearly useless. Skip and return only if time remains after attempting direct factual questions.
