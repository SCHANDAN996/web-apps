51. Which Article of the Indian Constitution deals with the amendment procedure?
(a) Article 356 (b) Article 360 (c) Article 368 (d) Article 370
Answer: (c)
Solution: Article 356 (President's Rule) and Article 360 (financial emergency) are tempting because all three are frequently tested together, but they deal with emergencies, not amendments. Article 368 in Part XX lays down the power and procedure of Parliament to amend the Constitution, so the answer is Article 368.

52. The idea of a Constituent Assembly for India was first put forward by
(a) M. N. Roy (b) Mahatma Gandhi (c) Jawaharlal Nehru (d) B. R. Ambedkar
Answer: (a)
Solution: Nehru is tempting because he moved the Objectives Resolution, and Ambedkar because he chaired the Drafting Committee, but neither first proposed the Assembly. The demand for a Constituent Assembly was first articulated by M. N. Roy in 1934, so the answer is M. N. Roy.

53. The Constitution of India was adopted by the Constituent Assembly on
(a) 26 November 1949 (b) 26 January 1950 (c) 15 August 1947 (d) 26 January 1949
Answer: (a)
Solution: 26 January 1950 is the classic trap — that is when the Constitution came into force, not when it was adopted. The Constituent Assembly adopted and enacted the Constitution on 26 November 1949, now celebrated as Constitution Day, so the answer is 26 November 1949.

59. Which of the following is NOT a writ mentioned in Article 32 of the Constitution?
(a) Habeas Corpus (b) Mandamus (c) Certiorari (d) Caveat
Answer: (d)
Solution: Certiorari sounds unfamiliar and is tempting as the odd one out, but it is a genuine writ used to quash orders of lower courts. The five writs are Habeas Corpus, Mandamus, Prohibition, Certiorari and Quo Warranto; caveat is merely a caution filed in civil proceedings, so the answer is Caveat.

(a) 25 years (b) 30 years (c) 35 years (d) 40 years

61. The President of India is elected by an electoral college consisting of
(a) Elected members of Parliament and elected members of State Legislative Assemblies (b) Elected members of both Houses of Parliament only (c) All members of Parliament and State Legislatures (d) Elected members of Parliament, State Assemblies and Union Territory Councils
Answer: (a)
Solution: Option (c) is tempting but wrong because nominated members of Parliament and members of Legislative Councils do not vote in the presidential election. 62. Who presides over a joint sitting of the two Houses of Parliament summoned under Article 108?
(a) The President (b) The Vice-President (c) The Chief Justice of India (d) The Speaker of the Lok Sabha
Answer: (d)
Solution: The Vice-President is tempting because he chairs the Rajya Sabha, but a joint sitting is not a Rajya Sabha session. Article 118 provides that the Speaker of the Lok Sabha presides over a joint sitting summoned by the President to resolve a deadlock, so the answer is the Speaker of the Lok Sabha.

Answer: (c)
Solution: "Either House" is tempting because ordinary bills can indeed be introduced in either House. Under Article 109, a Money Bill can be introduced only in the Lok Sabha and only on the recommendation of the President, so the answer is the Lok Sabha.

Answer: (d)

68. Which of the following is a correct chronological order of these constitutional developments?
(a) Cripps Mission — Cabinet Mission Plan — Mountbatten Plan — Indian Independence Act (b) Cabinet Mission Plan — Cripps Mission — Mountbatten Plan — Indian Independence Act (c) Cripps Mission — Mountbatten Plan — Cabinet Mission Plan — Indian Independence Act (d) Cabinet Mission Plan — Mountbatten Plan — Cripps Mission — Indian Independence Act
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap here is chronological confusion: the Cabinet Mission (1946) is often misremembered as preceding the Cripps Mission. The correct sequence is Cripps Mission (1942), Cabinet Mission Plan (1946), Mountbatten Plan (June 1947) and Indian Independence Act (July 1947), so the answer is option (a).
