Questions 26–50: Medium

26. The idea of the Preamble to the Indian Constitution was borrowed from the Constitution of which country?
(a) United Kingdom (b) United States of America (c) Ireland (d) Canada
Answer: (b)
Solution: The tempting wrong choice is Ireland, but India borrowed the Directive Principles, not the Preamble, from Ireland. The concept of a Preamble was taken from the Constitution of the United States of America, so the answer is United States of America.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Political Science (Indian Constitution at Work)

27. The Constituent Assembly of India was first convened on which date?
(a) 9 December 1946 (b) 15 August 1947 (c) 26 January 1950 (d) 26 November 1949
Answer: (a)
Solution: A chronological trap: 26 November 1949 is when the Constitution was adopted, and 26 January 1950 is when it came into force. The first sitting of the Constituent Assembly was held on 9 December 1946, so the answer is 9 December 1946.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Political Science (Indian Constitution at Work)

28. Who was the permanent Chairman (President) of the Constituent Assembly of India?
(a) Dr. Rajendra Prasad (b) Dr. B.R. Ambedkar (c) Jawaharlal Nehru (d) Dr. Sachchidananda Sinha
Answer: (a)
Solution: Dr. Sachchidananda Sinha is the trap — he was only the temporary (interim) chairman of the first meeting. Dr. Rajendra Prasad was elected the permanent President of the Constituent Assembly on 11 December 1946, so the answer is Dr. Rajendra Prasad.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Political Science (Indian Constitution at Work)

29. The Objectives Resolution in the Constituent Assembly was moved by whom?
(a) Dr. B.R. Ambedkar (b) Sardar Vallabhbhai Patel (c) Maulana Abul Kalam Azad (d) Jawaharlal Nehru
Answer: (d)
Solution: Dr. Ambedkar is a tempting distractor because he chaired the Drafting Committee, but he did not move the Objectives Resolution. Jawaharlal Nehru moved it on 13 December 1946, and it was adopted on 22 January 1947, so the answer is Jawaharlal Nehru.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Political Science (Indian Constitution at Work)

30. Which Article of the Indian Constitution abolishes untouchability?
(a) Article 14 (b) Article 15 (c) Article 17 (d) Article 19
Answer: (c)
Solution: Articles 14 and 15 are near-miss traps: Article 14 guarantees equality before law and Article 15 prohibits discrimination on specified grounds, but neither abolishes untouchability. Article 17 abolishes untouchability and forbids its practice in any form, so the answer is Article 17.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Political Science (Indian Constitution at Work)

31. The concept of 'Procedure established by law' in Article 21 was borrowed from the Constitution of which country?
(a) United States of America (b) Japan (c) United Kingdom (d) Germany
Answer: (b)
Solution: The USA is the trap because it gave India judicial review and fundamental rights, but the American phrase is 'due process of law'. The expression 'procedure established by law' was borrowed from the Japanese Constitution, so the answer is Japan.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Political Science (Indian Constitution at Work)

32. Which of the following is NOT a Fundamental Right under the Indian Constitution?
(a) Right to Property (b) Right to Equality (c) Right against Exploitation (d) Right to Constitutional Remedies
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is assuming Right to Property is still a Fundamental Right; it was removed from Part III by the 44th Amendment Act, 1978 and made a legal right under Article 300A. The other three remain Fundamental Rights, so the answer is Right to Property.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Political Science (Indian Constitution at Work)

33. The Directive Principles of State Policy are contained in which Part of the Constitution?
(a) Part III (b) Part IVA (c) Part IV (d) Part V
Answer: (c)
Solution: Part III is the tempting wrong answer because it deals with Fundamental Rights, which are often confused with DPSPs. Directive Principles are enshrined in Part IV (Articles 36–51), so the answer is Part IV.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Political Science (Indian Constitution at Work)

34. Fundamental Duties were added to the Constitution by which amendment?
(a) 24th Amendment (b) 44th Amendment (c) 42nd Amendment (d) 52nd Amendment
Answer: (c)
Solution: The 44th Amendment is the trap — it was passed in 1978 to undo several 42nd Amendment changes, but it did not add Fundamental Duties. The 42nd Amendment Act, 1976 inserted Part IVA (Article 51A) on the recommendation of the Swaran Singh Committee, so the answer is 42nd Amendment.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Political Science (Indian Constitution at Work)

35. Who is known as the 'Father of the Indian Constitution'?
(a) Dr. B.R. Ambedkar (b) Mahatma Gandhi (c) Dr. Rajendra Prasad (d) Jawaharlal Nehru
Answer: (a)
Solution: Dr. Rajendra Prasad is a similar-role trap since he presided over the Constituent Assembly, but he is not called the Father of the Constitution. Dr. B.R. Ambedkar, as Chairman of the Drafting Committee, is honoured with that title, so the answer is Dr. B.R. Ambedkar.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Political Science (Indian Constitution at Work)

36. The Constitution of India came into force on which date?
(a) 26 November 1949 (b) 15 August 1947 (c) 2 October 1950 (d) 26 January 1950
Answer: (d)
Solution: 26 November 1949 is the classic chronological trap — that is the date of adoption, not enforcement. The Constitution came into force on 26 January 1950, commemorated as Republic Day, so the answer is 26 January 1950.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Political Science (Indian Constitution at Work)

37. Which Article empowers the President of India to promulgate ordinances when Parliament is not in session?
(a) Article 123 (b) Article 213 (c) Article 356 (d) Article 360
Answer: (a)
Solution: Article 213 is the similar-number trap — it concerns ordinance-making by State Governors, not the President. Article 123 empowers the President to promulgate ordinances when either House of Parliament is not in session, so the answer is Article 123.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Political Science (Indian Constitution at Work)

38. The words 'Socialist' and 'Secular' were added to the Preamble by which amendment?
(a) 44th Amendment, 1978 (b) 42nd Amendment, 1976 (c) 52nd Amendment, 1985 (d) 61st Amendment, 1989
Answer: (b)
Solution: The 44th Amendment is the trap because it also dealt with the Preamble-era changes, but it only altered Article 368 and other provisions, not the Preamble's words. 'Socialist', 'Secular' and 'Integrity' were inserted by the 42nd Amendment Act, 1976, so the answer is 42nd Amendment, 1976.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Political Science (Indian Constitution at Work)

39. Under which Article can the President declare a National Emergency?
(a) Article 356 (b) Article 352 (c) Article 360 (d) Article 370
Answer: (b)
Solution: Article 356 is the near-miss trap — it provides for President's Rule in a State due to failure of constitutional machinery, not a National Emergency. Article 352 deals with National Emergency on grounds of war, external aggression or armed rebellion, so the answer is Article 352.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Political Science (Indian Constitution at Work)

40. The minimum age to become the President of India is:
(a) 25 years (b) 30 years (c) 40 years (d) 35 years
Answer: (d)
Solution: 25 years is the trap — that is the minimum age for membership of the Lok Sabha and State Assemblies. Article 58 requires a presidential candidate to have completed 35 years of age, so the answer is 35 years.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Political Science (Indian Constitution at Work)

41. Which of the following schedules of the Constitution deals with the allocation of seats in the Rajya Sabha to States and Union Territories?
(a) Third Schedule (b) Fourth Schedule (c) Seventh Schedule (d) Tenth Schedule
Answer: (b)
Solution: The Seventh Schedule is the tempting wrong choice because it is the most frequently asked schedule, but it deals with the Union, State and Concurrent Lists. The Fourth Schedule allocates Rajya Sabha seats among States and Union Territories, so the answer is Fourth Schedule.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Political Science (Indian Constitution at Work)

42. The Anti-Defection Law is contained in which Schedule of the Constitution?
(a) Eighth Schedule (b) Ninth Schedule (c) Twelfth Schedule (d) Tenth Schedule
Answer: (d)
Solution: The Ninth Schedule is the trap — it protects certain laws from judicial review and has nothing to do with defection. The Tenth Schedule, added by the 52nd Amendment Act, 1985, contains the Anti-Defection Law, so the answer is Tenth Schedule.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Political Science (Indian Constitution at Work)

43. Who administers the oath of office to the President of India?
(a) The Vice-President (b) The Prime Minister (c) The Speaker of the Lok Sabha (d) The Chief Justice of India
Answer: (d)
Solution: The Vice-President is a similar-office trap — the Vice-President is sworn in by the President, not the other way round. Under Article 60, the Chief Justice of India (or the senior-most judge available) administers the oath to the President, so the answer is the Chief Justice of India.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Political Science (Indian Constitution at Work)

44. The concept of Concurrent List in the Indian Constitution was borrowed from which country?
(a) Australia (b) Canada (c) Ireland (d) South Africa
Answer: (a)
Solution: Canada is the trap because India borrowed the federal scheme and residuary powers from it, not the Concurrent List. The Concurrent List, along with the joint sitting of both Houses, was borrowed from the Australian Constitution, so the answer is Australia.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Political Science (Indian Constitution at Work)

45. Which Article of the Constitution deals with the amendment procedure?
(a) Article 360 (b) Article 370 (c) Article 368 (d) Article 312
Answer: (c)
Solution: Article 370 is the similar-number trap — it granted special status to Jammu and Kashmir and is unrelated to amendments. Article 368 in Part XX lays down the power and procedure of Parliament to amend the Constitution, so the answer is Article 368.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Political Science (Indian Constitution at Work)

46. The Right to Education as a Fundamental Right was inserted by which amendment?
(a) 74th Amendment, 1992 (b) 73rd Amendment, 1992 (c) 86th Amendment, 2002 (d) 42nd Amendment, 1976
Answer: (c)
Solution: The 73rd and 74th Amendments are traps — they constitutionalised Panchayats and Municipalities, not education. The 86th Amendment Act, 2002 inserted Article 21A, making free and compulsory education of children aged 6–14 a Fundamental Right, so the answer is 86th Amendment, 2002.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Political Science (Indian Constitution at Work)

47. How many Fundamental Duties are currently listed in Article 51A of the Constitution?
(a) 10 (b) 9 (c) 12 (d) 11
Answer: (d)
Solution: 10 is the classic trap — the 42nd Amendment, 1976 originally added ten duties, but the count changed later. The 86th Amendment, 2002 added the eleventh duty (parent/guardian to provide education to their child), so the answer is 11.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Political Science (Indian Constitution at Work)

48. The Union Public Service Commission (UPSC) is provided for under which Article?
(a) Article 280 (b) Article 315 (c) Article 324 (d) Article 338
Answer: (b)
Solution: Article 324 is the similar-body trap — it deals with the Election Commission, not the UPSC. Article 315 provides for a Public Service Commission for the Union and for each State, so the answer is Article 315.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Political Science (Indian Constitution at Work)

49. Which of the following statements about the Preamble is correct?
(a) It is a source of power to the legislature (b) It is enforceable in courts of law (c) It is part of the Constitution but not enforceable in courts (d) It can override the provisions of the Constitution
Answer: (c)
Solution: The tempting wrong statement is that the Preamble is enforceable — in the Kesavananda Bharati case (1973) the Supreme Court held it to be part of the Constitution, yet it is neither a source of power nor enforceable in courts. Hence the correct statement is that it is part of the Constitution but not enforceable in courts, so the answer is that option.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Political Science (Indian Constitution at Work)

50. The idea of a 'Republic' in the Indian Preamble implies that:
(a) The head of the State is elected, directly or indirectly, for a fixed period (b) The head of the State is elected directly by the people (c) The head of the State is hereditary (d) The head of the State is nominated by the Prime Minister
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is 'elected directly by the people' — the Indian President is elected indirectly by an electoral college, so direct election is not essential to a republic. A republic means the head of State is elected (directly or indirectly) for a fixed tenure rather than holding office hereditarily, so the answer is that the head of the State is elected, directly or indirectly, for a fixed period.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Political Science (Indian Constitution at Work)
