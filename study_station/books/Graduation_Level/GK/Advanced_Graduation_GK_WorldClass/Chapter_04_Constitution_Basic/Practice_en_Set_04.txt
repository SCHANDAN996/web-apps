Questions 76–100: Medium

76. Which Article of the Indian Constitution deals with the amendment procedure?
(a) Article 356 (b) Article 368 (c) Article 360 (d) Article 370
Answer: (b)
Solution: Article 356 (President's Rule) and Article 360 (Financial Emergency) are tempting because all three numbers look similar, but they deal with emergencies, not amendments. Article 368 in Part XX lays down the power and procedure of Parliament to amend the Constitution, so the answer is Article 368.

77. The concept of 'Procedure Established by Law' in Article 21 was borrowed from the Constitution of which country?
(a) Japan (b) USA (c) UK (d) Ireland
Answer: (a)
Solution: The USA is the tempting wrong choice because its 'Due Process of Law' sounds similar, but India borrowed 'Procedure Established by Law' specifically from Japan. The UK influenced the parliamentary system and Ireland the Directive Principles, so the answer is Japan.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Political Science (Indian Constitution at Work)

78. Which of the following pairs is correctly matched?
(a) Fundamental Duties — Part IV (b) Directive Principles — Part IVA (c) Fundamental Rights — Part III (d) Union Territories — Part V
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is the swapping of Parts IV and IVA: Directive Principles are in Part IV while Fundamental Duties are in Part IVA, so the first two options are reversed. Union Territories are covered in Part VIII, not Part V. Fundamental Rights are correctly placed in Part III, so the answer is Fundamental Rights — Part III.

79. The 42nd Constitutional Amendment (1976) added which words to the Preamble?
(a) Socialist, Secular, Fraternity (b) Socialist, Secular, Integrity (c) Democratic, Secular, Integrity (d) Sovereign, Socialist, Republic
Answer: (b)
Solution: 'Fraternity' is the tempting distractor because it appears in the Preamble, but it was there since 1950 and was not added in 1976. 'Sovereign' and 'Republic' were also original words. The 42nd Amendment added Socialist, Secular and Integrity, so the answer is Socialist, Secular, Integrity.

80. Who presides over a joint sitting of the two Houses of Parliament summoned under Article 108?
(a) The President (b) The Vice-President (c) The Chief Justice of India (d) The Speaker of the Lok Sabha
Answer: (d)
Solution: The President is the tempting wrong answer because he summons the joint sitting, but summoning and presiding are different functions. The Vice-President chairs only the Rajya Sabha. The Speaker of the Lok Sabha presides over a joint sitting, so the answer is the Speaker of the Lok Sabha.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Political Science (Indian Constitution at Work)

81. The Fundamental Duties were incorporated into the Constitution on the recommendation of which committee?
(a) Sarkaria Commission (b) Shah Commission (c) Swaran Singh Committee (d) Balwant Rai Mehta Committee
Answer: (c)
Solution: The Sarkaria Commission is the tempting distractor because it is frequently asked, but it dealt with Centre–State relations, not duties. The Swaran Singh Committee recommended Fundamental Duties, added by the 42nd Amendment in 1976, so the answer is the Swaran Singh Committee.

82. Under which Article can the President promulgate an ordinance when Parliament is not in session?
(a) Article 123 (b) Article 213 (c) Article 143 (d) Article 352
Answer: (a)
Solution: Article 213 is the classic trap because it also concerns ordinances, but it applies to Governors of States, not the President. Article 143 is advisory jurisdiction and Article 352 is National Emergency. The President's ordinance power is in Article 123, so the answer is Article 123.

83. Which Schedule of the Constitution contains provisions for the administration of tribal areas in Assam, Meghalaya, Tripura and Mizoram?
(a) Fifth Schedule (b) Ninth Schedule (c) Seventh Schedule (d) Sixth Schedule
Answer: (d)
Solution: The Fifth Schedule is the tempting wrong option because it also deals with Scheduled Areas and tribes, but it applies to other States, not these four. The Sixth Schedule covers tribal areas of Assam, Meghalaya, Tripura and Mizoram, so the answer is the Sixth Schedule.

84. The idea of a Constituent Assembly for India was first put forward by whom?
(a) Mahatma Gandhi (b) Jawaharlal Nehru (c) M. N. Roy (d) B. R. Ambedkar
Answer: (c)
Solution: Nehru is the tempting distractor because he moved the Objectives Resolution and is strongly associated with constitution-making, but the first formal demand for a Constituent Assembly was made by M. N. Roy in 1934, so the answer is M. N. Roy.

85. Which Article empowers the Supreme Court to issue writs for the enforcement of Fundamental Rights?
(a) Article 32 (b) Article 226 (c) Article 136 (d) Article 131
Answer: (a)
Solution: Article 226 is the tempting wrong choice because it also concerns writs, but it empowers High Courts, not the Supreme Court. Article 136 is special leave and Article 131 is original jurisdiction. Dr. Ambedkar called Article 32 the 'heart and soul' of the Constitution, so the answer is Article 32.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Political Science (Indian Constitution at Work)

86. The 73rd Constitutional Amendment Act, 1992 deals with:
(a) Municipalities (b) Co-operative Societies (c) Panchayats (d) Scheduled Castes welfare
Answer: (c)
Solution: Municipalities is the tempting distractor because it was enacted in the same year, but that was the 74th Amendment — a classic chronological confusion trap. The 73rd Amendment gave constitutional status to Panchayati Raj institutions, so the answer is Panchayats.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Political Science (Indian Constitution at Work)

87. Which of the following is NOT a Fundamental Right under the Constitution?
(a) Right to Equality (b) Right to Property (c) Right to Freedom of Religion (d) Right against Exploitation
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is that Right to Property was originally a Fundamental Right under Article 31, which makes it feel correct, but the 44th Amendment (1978) removed it and made it a legal right under Article 300A. The other three remain Fundamental Rights, so the answer is Right to Property.

88. The minimum age to become a member of the Lok Sabha is:
(a) 21 years (b) 30 years (c) 25 years (d) 35 years
Answer: (c)
Solution: 30 years is the tempting wrong answer because it is the minimum age for the Rajya Sabha — a similar-numbers trap. 35 years is for the President. For the Lok Sabha the minimum age is 25 years under Article 84, so the answer is 25 years.

89. The Directive Principles of State Policy are contained in which Articles?
(a) Articles 12–35 (b) Articles 36–51 (c) Articles 51A (d) Articles 52–78
Answer: (b)
Solution: Articles 12–35 is the tempting distractor because it is the most quoted range, but it covers Fundamental Rights. Article 51A alone lists Fundamental Duties. Directive Principles span Articles 36 to 51 in Part IV, so the answer is Articles 36–51.

90. Who was the Chairman of the Drafting Committee of the Constituent Assembly?
(a) Dr. B. R. Ambedkar (b) Dr. Rajendra Prasad (c) Jawaharlal Nehru (d) Sardar Patel
Answer: (a)
Solution: Dr. Rajendra Prasad is the classic similar-role trap because he chaired the Constituent Assembly itself, but chairing the Assembly and chairing the Drafting Committee are different posts. Dr. B. R. Ambedkar headed the Drafting Committee, so the answer is Dr. B. R. Ambedkar.

91. The Constitution of India was adopted by the Constituent Assembly on:
(a) 26 November 1949 (b) 26 January 1950 (c) 15 August 1947 (d) 26 January 1949
Answer: (a)
Solution: 26 January 1950 is the tempting wrong answer because it is Republic Day, but that is when the Constitution came into force, not when it was adopted. The Assembly adopted the Constitution on 26 November 1949, now celebrated as Constitution Day, so the answer is 26 November 1949.

92. Which Article provides for the abolition of untouchability?
(a) Article 17 (b) Article 15 (c) Article 14 (d) Article 18
Answer: (a)
Solution: Article 18 is the tempting distractor because it sits next to Article 17 and also abolishes something, but it abolishes titles, not untouchability. Articles 14 and 15 deal with equality and non-discrimination. Article 17 abolishes untouchability, so the answer is Article 17.

93. A Money Bill can be introduced only in:
(a) The Lok Sabha (b) The Rajya Sabha (c) Either House (d) A joint sitting
Answer: (a)
Solution: 'Either House' is the tempting wrong option because most ordinary bills can be introduced in either House, but Article 109 restricts Money Bills to the Lok Sabha, and the Speaker certifies whether a bill is a Money Bill, so the answer is the Lok Sabha.

94. The concept of Judicial Review in India was borrowed from:
(a) UK (b) Australia (c) Canada (d) USA
Answer: (d)
Solution: The UK is the tempting distractor because India borrowed heavily from it, but Britain follows parliamentary sovereignty, not strong judicial review. Judicial review, along with the independence of the judiciary, was borrowed from the USA, so the answer is the USA.

95. How many Fundamental Duties are currently listed in Article 51A?
(a) 10 (b) 9 (c) 12 (d) 11
Answer: (d)
Solution: 10 is the classic trap because the 42nd Amendment (1976) originally added ten duties, but the 86th Amendment (2002) added one more — the duty of a parent or guardian to provide education to a child aged 6–14 — making eleven, so the answer is 11.

96. The President of India is elected by an electoral college consisting of:
(a) Elected members of both Houses of Parliament only (b) Elected members of both Houses of Parliament and elected members of State Legislative Assemblies (c) All members of Parliament and State Legislatures (d) Elected members of Parliament, State Assemblies and Legislative Councils
Answer: (b)
Solution: 'All members of Parliament and State Legislatures' is the tempting wrong option because it sounds comprehensive, but nominated members and Legislative Council members do not vote in the presidential election. Only elected MPs and elected MLAs of States and of Delhi and Puducherry form the electoral college, so the answer is elected members of both Houses of Parliament and elected members of State Legislative Assemblies.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Political Science (Indian Constitution at Work)

97. Which Amendment lowered the voting age from 21 to 18 years?
(a) 42nd Amendment (b) 44th Amendment (c) 73rd Amendment (d) 61st Amendment
Answer: (d)
Solution: The 42nd Amendment is the tempting distractor because it is the most famous amendment, but it dealt with the Preamble, duties and emergency-era changes. The 61st Amendment Act, 1988 lowered the voting age to 18, so the answer is the 61st Amendment.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Political Science (Indian Constitution at Work)

98. The Ninth Schedule was added to the Constitution by which Amendment?
(a) 42nd Amendment, 1976 (b) Seventh Amendment, 1956 (c) First Amendment, 1951 (d) 52nd Amendment, 1985
Answer: (c)
Solution: The 52nd Amendment is the tempting wrong choice because it also added a Schedule, but that was the Tenth Schedule on anti-defection — a similar-fact trap. The very First Amendment of 1951 added the Ninth Schedule to protect land reform laws, so the answer is the First Amendment, 1951.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Political Science (Indian Constitution at Work)

99. Which Article deals with the impeachment of the President of India?
(a) Article 56 (b) Article 61 (c) Article 72 (d) Article 74
Answer: (b)
Solution: Article 56 is the tempting distractor because it concerns the President's term of office and the numbers are close, but impeachment procedure is laid down in Article 61. Article 72 covers pardoning power and Article 74 the Council of Ministers, so the answer is Article 61.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Political Science (Indian Constitution at Work)

100. The Concurrent List in the Seventh Schedule was borrowed from the Constitution of:
(a) Canada (b) South Africa (c) Ireland (d) Australia
Answer: (d)
Solution: Canada is the tempting wrong answer because India borrowed federalism with a strong Centre from it, but the scheme of the Concurrent List specifically came from Australia. Ireland gave the Directive Principles and South Africa the amendment procedure, so the answer is Australia.
