Card 1
Front: Which single day did the Constituent Assembly hold its first meeting — and why do exams confuse it with another date?
Back: 9 December 1946. The trap: 13 December 1946 was when Nehru moved the Objectives Resolution, and 26 November 1949 was adoption — three different dates.

Card 2
Front: The Constitution was adopted on 26 November 1949, so why do we celebrate Republic Day on 26 January 1950?
Back: 26 January marked Purna Swaraj Day (1930, Lahore Session). The Constitution came into force on 26 January 1950 to honour that date.

Card 3
Front: How long did the Constituent Assembly take to complete the Constitution?
Back: 2 years, 11 months and 18 days.

Card 4
Front: Who is called the "Father of the Indian Constitution," and who was the Assembly's President?
Back: Dr. B.R. Ambedkar (Chairman, Drafting Committee); Dr. Rajendra Prasad was President of the Constituent Assembly. Examiners swap these two roles.

Card 5
Front: Which country's Constitution inspired our Parliamentary system, rule of law, and single citizenship?
Back: The United Kingdom (British Constitution).

Card 6
Front: Mnemonic trick: How do you remember that Fundamental Rights came from the USA?
Back: "US gives you RIGHTS" — the USA is the source of Fundamental Rights, judicial review, and impeachment of the President.

Card 7
Front: From which country did we borrow the Directive Principles of State Policy?
Back: Ireland (which itself borrowed the idea from the Spanish Constitution).

Card 8
Front: Mnemonic trick: How to recall that the Emergency provisions came from Germany?
Back: "GERMANY = Emergency" — both start with similar sounds; Weimar Germany's constitution inspired Article 352-type provisions.

Card 9
Front: Which words were added to the Preamble by the 42nd Amendment (1976), often called the "Mini Constitution"?
Back: Socialist, Secular, and Integrity. The 42nd Amendment is the only amendment to have changed the Preamble.

Card 10
Front: Examiner's trap: Is the Preamble a part of the Constitution?
Back: Yes — held in the Kesavananda Bharati case (1973). Earlier, in Berubari Union (1960), the Supreme Court had said it was NOT a part — the trap is quoting the older verdict.

Card 11
Front: How many Articles and Schedules did the original Constitution (1950) have?
Back: 395 Articles, 22 Parts, and 8 Schedules.

Card 12
Front: Which Schedule contains the list of 22 official languages?
Back: The Eighth Schedule. Originally it had 14 languages; 8 more were added by later amendments.

Card 13
Front: Examiner's trap: Which Schedule deals with anti-defection — and which with Panchayats?
Back: Anti-defection = Tenth Schedule (52nd Amendment, 1985); Panchayats = Eleventh Schedule (73rd Amendment, 1992). Exams frequently swap the 10th, 11th, and 12th Schedules.

Card 14
Front: Who was the Constitutional Advisor to the Constituent Assembly?
Back: B.N. Rau (Sir Benegal Narsing Rau).

Card 15
Front: Which two members of the Drafting Committee are most often asked besides Ambedkar?
Back: N. Gopalaswami Ayyangar and Alladi Krishnaswami Ayyar (the committee had 7 members total).

Card 16
Front: From where did India borrow the idea of a Republic and the ideals of liberty, equality, fraternity in the Preamble?
Back: The French Revolution / French Constitution.

Card 17
Front: Which is the longest written constitution in the world, and roughly how much did it cost to make?
Back: The Indian Constitution; it cost about ₹64 lakh to produce.

Card 18
Front: Who hand-wrote (calligraphed) the original Constitution, and who illustrated it?
Back: Prem Behari Narain Raizada did the calligraphy; Nandalal Bose and his team from Santiniketan illustrated it.

Card 19
Front: Mnemonic trick: How to remember the source of Concurrent List and joint sitting of Parliament?
Back: "AUStralia = AUSpicious sharing" — Australia gave us the Concurrent List and joint sitting of both Houses.

Card 20
Front: On what date was the Constitution finally signed by members of the Assembly, and what else happened that day?
Back: 24 January 1950 — the same day the national anthem (Jana Gana Mana) and national song (Vande Mataram) were adopted. Note: Rajendra Prasad was elected first President of India shortly before this and assumed office on 26 January 1950 — do not confuse the dates.
