1. The Constitution of India was adopted by the Constituent Assembly on:
(a) 15 August 1947 (b) 2 October 1950 (c) 26 January 1950 (d) 26 November 1949
Answer: (d)
Solution: Many students confuse the date of adoption with the date of enforcement. The Constitution was adopted on 26 November 1949 (celebrated as Constitution Day), while it came into force on 26 January 1950, so the answer is 26 November 1949.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Indian Constitution at Work

2. The Constitution of India came into force on:
(a) 26 November 1949 (b) 15 August 1947 (c) 26 January 1950 (d) 14 November 1949
Answer: (c)
Solution: The tempting wrong choice is 26 November 1949, which is the date of adoption, not enforcement. The Constitution commenced on 26 January 1950, chosen to commemorate Purna Swaraj Day (26 January 1930), so the answer is 26 January 1950.

3. Who was the Chairman of the Drafting Committee of the Constituent Assembly?
(a) Jawaharlal Nehru (b) Sardar Vallabhbhai Patel (c) Dr. Rajendra Prasad (d) Dr. B. R. Ambedkar
Answer: (d)
Solution: Dr. Rajendra Prasad is a common trap — he was the President of the Constituent Assembly, not the Drafting Committee. The Drafting Committee was chaired by Dr. B. R. Ambedkar, so the answer is Dr. B. R. Ambedkar.

4. The Constituent Assembly of India was formed under which plan?
(a) Cripps Plan (b) Wavell Plan (c) Cabinet Mission Plan (d) Mountbatten Plan
Answer: (c)
Solution: The Cripps Plan (1942) proposed a constitution-making body but was rejected; the actual formation happened under the Cabinet Mission Plan of 1946, so the answer is Cabinet Mission Plan.

6. The idea of the Preamble to the Indian Constitution was borrowed from the Constitution of:
(a) United States of America (b) Ireland (c) United Kingdom (d) Canada
Answer: (a)
Solution: Ireland is a trap because the Directive Principles, not the Preamble, came from Ireland. The idea of the Preamble was borrowed from the US Constitution, so the answer is United States of America.

7. The words "Socialist" and "Secular" were added to the Preamble by which amendment?
(a) 24th Amendment (b) 42nd Amendment (c) 44th Amendment (d) 52nd Amendment
Answer: (b)
Solution: The 44th Amendment is a trap — it reversed several 42nd Amendment provisions but did not add these words. The 42nd Constitutional Amendment (1976) added "Socialist", "Secular" and "Integrity" to the Preamble, so the answer is 42nd Amendment.

8. The Directive Principles of State Policy in the Indian Constitution are borrowed from:
(a) USA (b) Germany (c) Australia (d) Ireland
Answer: (d)
Solution: The Directive Principles were taken from the Irish Constitution, which itself drew them from the Spanish Constitution, so the answer is Ireland.

9. Fundamental Rights are enshrined in which Part of the Indian Constitution?
(a) Part II (b) Part IV-A (c) Part IV (d) Part III
Answer: (d)
Solution: Part IV is a trap — it contains Directive Principles, not Fundamental Rights. Fundamental Rights are in Part III (Articles 12–35), so the answer is Part III.

10. How many Fundamental Rights are currently available under the Indian Constitution?
(a) Six (b) Five (c) Seven (d) Eight
Answer: (a)
Solution: Seven is the classic trap — originally there were seven, but the Right to Property was removed from Fundamental Rights by the 44th Amendment (1978) and made a legal right under Article 300A. Currently there are six, so the answer is Six.

11. The concept of "Procedure established by law" in Article 21 was borrowed from:
(a) USA (b) UK (c) Japan (d) Canada
Answer: (c)
Solution: The USA is a trap — from the USA we borrowed "due process of law" (as interpreted in Maneka Gandhi case), not the phrase itself. The expression "procedure established by law" was taken from the Japanese Constitution, so the answer is Japan.

12. The Fundamental Duties were added to the Constitution on the recommendation of which committee?
(a) Swaran Singh Committee (b) Shah Commission (c) Sarkaria Commission (d) Balwant Rai Mehta Committee
Answer: (a)
Solution: The Sarkaria Commission dealt with Centre–State relations — a similar-sounding trap. Fundamental Duties were added by the 42nd Amendment (1976) on the Swaran Singh Committee's recommendation, so the answer is Swaran Singh Committee.

13. Fundamental Duties are contained in which Article of the Constitution?
(a) Article 51A (b) Article 51 (c) Article 50 (d) Article 52
Answer: (a)
Solution: Article 51 is a near-miss trap — it deals with promotion of international peace under Directive Principles. Fundamental Duties are in Article 51A, Part IV-A, so the answer is Article 51A.

14. Which Article of the Constitution abolishes untouchability?
(a) Article 17 (b) Article 15 (c) Article 16 (d) Article 14
Answer: (a)
Solution: Articles 14–16 deal with equality before law, prohibition of discrimination and equal opportunity respectively — close in theme but not the answer. Article 17 abolishes untouchability, so the answer is Article 17.

15. The Right to Constitutional Remedies is provided under:
(a) Article 19 (b) Article 21 (c) Article 226 (d) Article 32
Answer: (d)
Solution: Article 226 is a trap — it empowers High Courts to issue writs, but it is not itself a Fundamental Right. Article 32, called the "heart and soul of the Constitution" by Dr. Ambedkar, guarantees the Right to Constitutional Remedies, so the answer is Article 32.

(a) 9 December 1946 (b) 13 December 1946 (c) 11 December 1946 (d) 26 January 1947
Answer: (a)
Solution: 13 December 1946 is a chronological trap — that is when Nehru moved the Objectives Resolution, not the first meeting. The Constituent Assembly first met on 9 December 1946, so the answer is 9 December 1946.

19. The Objectives Resolution in the Constituent Assembly was moved by:
(a) Dr. B. R. Ambedkar (b) Sardar Patel (c) Jawaharlal Nehru (d) Maulana Abul Kalam Azad
Answer: (c)
Solution: Jawaharlal Nehru moved the Objectives Resolution on 13 December 1946; it was adopted on 22 January 1947 and later became the basis of the Preamble, so the answer is Jawaharlal Nehru.

20. The Indian Constitution is described as:
(a) Wholly rigid (b) Wholly flexible (c) Unwritten (d) A blend of rigidity and flexibility
Answer: (d)
Solution: Some provisions can be amended by simple majority (flexible) while others need special majority, and some additionally need ratification by half the states (rigid). Hence the Constitution is a blend of rigidity and flexibility, so the answer is a blend of rigidity and flexibility.

21. Consider the following statements:
1. The Preamble is enforceable in a court of law.
2. The Preamble is a part of the Constitution as held in the Kesavananda Bharati case.
Which of the statements given above is/are correct?
(a) 2 only (b) 1 only (c) Both 1 and 2 (d) Neither 1 nor 2
Answer: (a)
Solution: Statement 1 is the trap — it sounds plausible but the Preamble is not justiciable and cannot be enforced in courts. In Kesavananda Bharati v. State of Kerala (1973), the Supreme Court held the Preamble to be a part of the Constitution, so the answer is 2 only.

22. Which of the following pairs is correctly matched?
(a) Fundamental Rights — Government of Ireland (b) Emergency provisions — Government of USA (c) Parliamentary system — Government of UK (d) Amendment procedure — Government of Canada
Answer: (c)
Solution: Each wrong option pairs a feature with a near-correct but wrong country: Fundamental Rights came from the USA, Emergency provisions from Germany (Weimar Constitution), and the amendment procedure from South Africa. The parliamentary system was indeed borrowed from the UK, so the answer is Parliamentary system — Government of UK.

23. The 86th Constitutional Amendment (2002) is related to:
(a) Right to Property (b) Panchayati Raj (c) Right to Education (d) Anti-Defection Law
Answer: (c)
Solution: The 73rd Amendment (Panchayati Raj) and 52nd Amendment (Anti-Defection) are similar-numbered traps. The 86th Amendment inserted Article 21A making free and compulsory education for children aged 6–14 a Fundamental Right, so the answer is Right to Education.

24. Under which Article can the President declare a National Emergency?
(a) Article 356 (b) Article 352 (c) Article 360 (d) Article 365
Answer: (b)
Solution: Article 356 (President's Rule in a state) and Article 360 (Financial Emergency) are close-number traps. National Emergency on grounds of war, external aggression or armed rebellion is proclaimed under Article 352, so the answer is Article 352.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Indian Constitution at Work

25. Which of the following is NOT a Fundamental Right under the Indian Constitution?
(a) Right to Equality (b) Right to Property (c) Right to Freedom of Religion (d) Right against Exploitation
Answer: (b)
Solution: The Right to Property is the deliberate trap — it was a Fundamental Right under Article 31 originally, but the 44th Amendment (1978) deleted it and made it a constitutional/legal right under Article 300A. The other three remain Fundamental Rights, so the answer is Right to Property.
