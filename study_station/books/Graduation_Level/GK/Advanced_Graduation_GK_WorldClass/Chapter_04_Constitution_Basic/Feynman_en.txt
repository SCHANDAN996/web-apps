The Feynman Technique: Indian Constitution (Basic)

The Hardest Concept: The Preamble

The Preamble is like the "cover of a book" for the Constitution. Just as a book cover tells you what the book is about, the Preamble tells you what the Constitution is about — its aims and its values.

Think of it like this: the Preamble is the "cover" of the Constitution. It tells you what the Constitution is about.

Now write down everything you remember about this concept of the Preamble without looking up.

Now, a question: What is the Preamble of the Constitution?

Explain it in your own words, as if you were teaching a younger student:

- The Preamble is the introduction to the Constitution of India.
- It begins with the words "WE, THE PEOPLE OF INDIA", which means the Constitution gets its power from the people, not from any king or foreign authority.
- It declares India to be a SOVEREIGN, SOCIALIST, SECULAR, DEMOCRATIC REPUBLIC.
- It promises to secure for all citizens: JUSTICE (social, economic and political), LIBERTY (of thought, expression, belief, faith and worship), EQUALITY (of status and opportunity), and FRATERNITY (assuring the dignity of the individual and the unity and integrity of the nation).
- The Preamble is based on the Objectives Resolution moved by Jawaharlal Nehru in the Constituent Assembly in 1946.
- The words "Socialist", "Secular" and "Integrity" were added to the Preamble by the 42nd Constitutional Amendment in 1976.
- In the Kesavananda Bharati case (1973), the Supreme Court held that the Preamble is a part of the Constitution.

Check yourself: Did you explain why the Preamble is called the "identity card" or "key" of the Constitution? Did you remember which words were added in 1976? If you got stuck anywhere, go back, read that part again, and explain it once more in simpler words — that is the Feynman way.
