1. Which of the following is the chemical symbol for sodium?
(a) So (b) Na (c) Sd (d) N
Answer: (b)
Solution: Many students pick "So" assuming symbols come from English names, but sodium's symbol comes from its Latin name "natrium". The symbol for sodium is Na, so the answer is Na.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Science

2. The pH value of a neutral solution at 25°C is:
(a) 0 (b) 14 (c) 7 (d) 1
Answer: (c)
Solution: A pH of 0 or 1 indicates a strong acid and 14 a strong base; these are tempting extremes. Pure water at 25°C is neutral, so the answer is 7.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science

3. Which gas is commonly known as laughing gas?
(a) Nitrogen dioxide (b) Ammonia (c) Nitric oxide (d) Nitrous oxide
Answer: (d)
Solution: Nitric oxide (NO) and nitrogen dioxide (NO₂) are similar-sounding names that confuse candidates, but neither is laughing gas. Laughing gas is N₂O, so the answer is nitrous oxide.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Chemistry

4. The atomic number of an element is equal to the number of:
(a) Neutrons (b) Nucleons (c) Electrons plus neutrons (d) Protons
Answer: (d)
Solution: Mass number (protons + neutrons) is a common confusion with atomic number. Atomic number counts only protons, so the answer is protons.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Science

5. Which acid is present in vinegar?
(a) Acetic acid (b) Lactic acid (c) Citric acid (d) Oxalic acid
Answer: (a)
Solution: Citric acid (citrus fruits) and lactic acid (curd) are tempting because they occur in common foods, but vinegar is a 4–8% solution of acetic acid. So the answer is acetic acid.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science

6. The chemical formula of washing soda is:
(a) NaHCO₃ (b) CaOCl₂ (c) NaOH (d) Na₂CO₃·10H₂O
Answer: (d)
Solution: NaHCO₃ is baking soda, the classic trap pair with washing soda. Washing soda is hydrated sodium carbonate, so the answer is Na₂CO₃·10H₂O.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science

7. Which of the following metals is liquid at room temperature?
(a) Sodium (b) Mercury (c) Aluminium (d) Gallium only in solid form
Answer: (b)
Solution: Gallium melts near body temperature but is solid at normal room temperature, which makes it a tempting distractor. The metal liquid at room temperature is mercury, so the answer is mercury.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science

8. Rusting of iron is an example of:
(a) Reduction (b) Sublimation (c) Neutralisation (d) Oxidation
Answer: (d)
Solution: Rust is hydrated iron(III) oxide formed when iron combines with oxygen and moisture; "reduction" tempts because it is the opposite term. Rusting is oxidation, so the answer is oxidation.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science

9. Which of the following is a noble gas?
(a) Argon (b) Oxygen (c) Nitrogen (d) Chlorine
Answer: (a)
Solution: Nitrogen seems unreactive and tempts many, but it is not a noble gas. Argon belongs to Group 18, so the answer is argon.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science

10. The chemical name of common salt is:
(a) Sodium chloride (b) Sodium carbonate (c) Potassium chloride (d) Calcium chloride
Answer: (a)
Solution: Potassium chloride is sold as a salt substitute and misleads some candidates. Common table salt is NaCl, so the answer is sodium chloride.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science

11. Which gas is released when zinc reacts with dilute hydrochloric acid?
(a) Oxygen (b) Hydrogen (c) Chlorine (d) Carbon dioxide
Answer: (b)
Solution: Carbon dioxide tempts because it is released with carbonates and acids, not metals. Metals above hydrogen in the reactivity series displace hydrogen from acids, so the answer is hydrogen.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science

12. The modern periodic table is arranged on the basis of:
(a) Atomic mass (b) Number of neutrons (c) Density (d) Atomic number
Answer: (d)
Solution: Mendeleev used atomic mass, which is the chronological trap here. Moseley's modern periodic law uses atomic number, so the answer is atomic number.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Chemistry

13. Which of the following is used as an antacid?
(a) Sodium chloride (b) Hydrochloric acid (c) Magnesium hydroxide (d) Acetic acid
Answer: (c)
Solution: Hydrochloric acid is the trap — it is the stomach acid being neutralised, not the remedy. Milk of magnesia neutralises excess acid, so the answer is magnesium hydroxide.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science

14. The isotopes of an element have the same number of:
(a) Protons (b) Neutrons (c) Nucleons (d) Mass number
Answer: (a)
Solution: Isotopes differ in neutrons and mass number, so those options are traps. They share the same proton count, so the answer is protons.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Science

15. Which of the following is the hardest naturally occurring substance?
(a) Graphite (b) Quartz (c) Diamond (d) Iron
Answer: (c)
Solution: Graphite is a trap because it is also pure carbon but is soft and slippery. Diamond's tetrahedral covalent network makes it the hardest natural substance, so the answer is diamond.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science

16. Brass is an alloy of:
(a) Copper and tin (b) Iron and chromium (c) Copper and zinc (d) Aluminium and copper
Answer: (c)
Solution: Copper and tin make bronze — the classic similar-name trap with brass. Brass is copper plus zinc, so the answer is copper and zinc.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science

17. Which of the following gases is the main component of biogas?
(a) Methane (b) Carbon dioxide (c) Hydrogen (d) Nitrogen
Answer: (a)
Solution: Carbon dioxide is present in biogas and tempts many, but it is the minor component. Biogas is roughly 50–75% methane, so the answer is methane.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science

18. An aqueous solution turns blue litmus red. The solution is:
(a) Basic (b) Acidic (c) Neutral (d) Amphoteric
Answer: (b)
Solution: The colour-direction trap is common: acids turn blue litmus red, bases turn red litmus blue. Blue turning red indicates acid, so the answer is acidic.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science

19. Which element is essential for the manufacture of ammonia by the Haber process along with nitrogen?
(a) Hydrogen (b) Oxygen (c) Chlorine (d) Carbon
Answer: (a)
Solution: The Haber process combines N₂ and H₂ over an iron catalyst; oxygen would oxidise, not form ammonia. So the answer is hydrogen.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Chemistry

20. The chemical formula of quicklime is:
(a) Ca(OH)₂ (b) CaO (c) CaCO₃ (d) CaSO₄
Answer: (b)
Solution: Ca(OH)₂ is slaked lime — the almost-correct trap since both are "lime". Quicklime is calcium oxide, so the answer is CaO.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science

21. Consider the following statements about the pH scale:
P. pH of human blood is approximately 7.4.
Q. pH below 7 indicates a basic solution.
R. Universal indicator gives different colours at different pH values.
Which of the above statements are correct?
(a) P and Q only (b) Q and R only (c) P and R only (d) P, Q and R
Answer: (c)
Solution: Statement Q is the trap — it reverses the scale; below 7 means acidic, not basic. Blood pH is about 7.4 and universal indicator does show a colour range, so the answer is P and R only.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science

22. Which of the following pairs is correctly matched?
(a) Plaster of Paris — CaSO₄·½H₂O (b) Baking soda — Na₂CO₃ (c) Bleaching powder — Ca(OH)₂ (d) Slaked lime — CaO
Answer: (a)
Solution: Each wrong option swaps a familiar formula: baking soda is NaHCO₃, bleaching powder is CaOCl₂, and slaked lime is Ca(OH)₂. Only Plaster of Paris as calcium sulphate hemihydrate is correct, so the answer is Plaster of Paris — CaSO₄·½H₂O.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science

23. Arrange the following metals in decreasing order of reactivity: potassium, iron, gold, zinc.
(a) Gold > Iron > Zinc > Potassium (b) Potassium > Iron > Zinc > Gold (c) Zinc > Potassium > Iron > Gold (d) Potassium > Zinc > Iron > Gold
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is swapping zinc and iron; zinc sits above iron in the reactivity series. Potassium is most reactive and gold least, so the answer is Potassium > Zinc > Iron > Gold.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science

24. Consider the following statements about allotropes of carbon:
P. Diamond is a good conductor of electricity.
Q. Graphite conducts electricity due to free electrons.
R. Fullerene (C₆₀) has a buckminsterfullerene structure.
Which of the above statements are correct?
(a) P and Q only (b) Q and R only (c) P and R only (d) P, Q and R
Answer: (b)
Solution: Statement P is the trap — diamond has no free electrons and is an insulator, despite being carbon like graphite. Graphite conducts and C₆₀ is buckminsterfullerene, so the answer is Q and R only.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science

25. A metal M reacts with cold water to form its hydroxide and hydrogen gas. M is most likely:
(a) Copper (b) Iron (c) Sodium (d) Silver
Answer: (c)
Solution: Iron reacts only with steam, not cold water, which is the tempting trap; copper and silver do not react with water at all. Alkali metals react vigorously with cold water, so the answer is sodium.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science
