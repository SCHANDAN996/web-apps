# 🧒 Feynman Technique — General Chemistry

## The Hardest Concept Made Simple: **Why Does the pH Scale Work the Way It Does? (Acids, Bases & the "Backwards" Numbers)**

Most aspirants can recite "pH 7 is neutral, below 7 is acid, above 7 is base." But the moment the examiner asks *"If pH drops from 5 to 3, how many times more acidic is the solution?"* — most students freeze. Let's fix that forever, the Feynman way.

---

### Step 1: Explain it like you're talking to a 12-year-old

Imagine acidity is measured by counting **how many "H⁺ soldiers"** are floating in a glass of water.

- More H⁺ soldiers → stronger acid.
- Fewer H⁺ soldiers → more basic.

Now here's the weird part: the pH number is **backwards**. It's like a golf score — the *lower* the number, the *stronger* the acid.

- pH 2 = a huge army of H⁺ soldiers (strong acid, like lemon juice).
- pH 7 = soldiers and anti-soldiers (OH⁻) are perfectly balanced (pure water).
- pH 12 = almost no H⁺ soldiers left; the OH⁻ anti-soldiers have taken over (strong base, like soap solution).

And the real trap: **each step on the pH scale is ×10, not +1.**

- pH 3 has **10 times** more H⁺ than pH 4.
- pH 3 has **100 times** more H⁺ than pH 5.

So if pH falls from 5 to 3, the solution became **100 times more acidic** — not "2 times" and not "2 units."

---

### Step 2: A concrete daily-life example

Think of **your phone's battery percentage, inverted**:

- Battery 100% = full power. pH 0 = full acid power.
- Now imagine a battery where dropping from 50% to 40% doesn't mean losing 10% power — it means losing **90%** of your power, because each 10-point drop is a ×10 collapse.

That's pH. Small number changes = giant chemistry changes. This is why a tiny shift in blood pH (normally 7.35–7.45) is a medical emergency — even 0.3 units means the H⁺ soldier count has roughly **doubled**.

---

### Step 3: The Unforgettable Trick 🪄

**"pH is a REVERSE MULTIPLYING scale."**

> **"Down 1 = Ten times stronger. Down 2 = Hundred. Down 3 = Thousand."**

Chant it: *"One down, ten up. Two down, hundred up."*

Examiner's favourite trap: *"Solution X has pH 4, Solution Y has pH 6. X is how many times more acidic?"*
Your brain now answers instantly: 2 steps down → **100 times**. Option (b). Done in 10 seconds.
