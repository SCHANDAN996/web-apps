Card 1
Front: Why does the pH of pure water change with temperature even though it stays neutral?
Back: Ionization of water increases with temperature, so Kw rises and neutral pH falls below 7 (e.g., ~6.5 at 60°C). Neutral means [H⁺] = [OH⁻], not necessarily pH 7.

Card 2
Front: Which gas is called "laughing gas", and what is its chemical formula?
Back: Nitrous oxide, N₂O. It is used as an anaesthetic and as a propellant in whipped-cream cans.

Card 3
Front: Mnemonic trick: How do you remember the reactivity series of common metals?
Back: "Please Stop Calling Me A Zebra, I Like Her Call Smart Goat" — Potassium, Sodium, Calcium, Magnesium, Aluminium, Zinc, Iron, Lead, Hydrogen, Copper, Silver, Gold.

Card 4
Front: Examiner's trap: Is rusting of iron a physical or chemical change — and what exactly is rust?
Back: A chemical change. Rust is hydrated ferric oxide, Fe₂O₃·xH₂O — not simply Fe₂O₃. Both oxygen and moisture are required; either alone does not cause rusting.

Card 5
Front: Which element is common to all acids, and which ion do acids release in water?
Back: Hydrogen. Acids release H⁺ ions (as hydronium, H₃O⁺) in aqueous solution — this is the Arrhenius definition.

Card 6
Front: Why is an aqueous solution of CuSO₄ acidic even though it is a salt?
Back: It is a salt of a strong acid (H₂SO₄) and a weak base (Cu(OH)₂), so it hydrolyses to give an acidic solution (pH < 7).

Card 7
Front: Mnemonic trick: How do you recall the colours of universal indicator across the pH scale?
Back: Follow the rainbow (ROYGBIV) order: Red (strong acid) → Orange → Yellow → Green (neutral, pH 7) → Blue → Indigo → Violet (strong base). Remember: Red = acid, Violet = alkali.

Card 8
Front: Which metal is liquid at room temperature, and which non-metal is also liquid?
Back: Mercury (Hg) is the liquid metal; bromine (Br₂) is the only liquid non-metal at room temperature.

Card 9
Front: Examiner's trap: Does a catalyst increase the yield of a reversible reaction?
Back: No. A catalyst only speeds up attainment of equilibrium by lowering activation energy; it does not shift equilibrium or change the yield.

Card 10
Front: What is the difference between an atom's mass number and its atomic number?
Back: Atomic number (Z) = number of protons; mass number (A) = protons + neutrons. Isotopes have the same Z but different A.

Card 11
Front: Why does an antacid relieve acidity, and name one common antacid compound?
Back: Antacids are mild bases that neutralise excess HCl in the stomach. Examples: magnesium hydroxide (milk of magnesia) or sodium bicarbonate.

Card 12
Front: Which allotrope of carbon conducts electricity, and why is that surprising?
Back: Graphite. It is a non-metal yet conducts electricity because each carbon has one delocalised free electron between its hexagonal layers.

Card 13
Front: What is the chemical name and formula of washing soda, and how does it differ from baking soda?
Back: Washing soda is sodium carbonate decahydrate, Na₂CO₃·10H₂O; baking soda is sodium hydrogen carbonate, NaHCO₃. Washing soda is more alkaline.

Card 14
Front: In the modern periodic table, what property decides the order of elements, and how many groups and periods are there?
Back: Atomic number (Moseley's law). The table has 18 groups (vertical columns) and 7 periods (horizontal rows).

Card 15
Front: Why is hydrogen peroxide stored in dark or wax-lined bottles?
Back: It decomposes into water and oxygen in the presence of light and rough surfaces; dark bottles slow this decomposition.

Card 16
Front: Examiner's trap: Is the dissolution of common salt in water a chemical change?
Back: No — it is a physical change. No new substance forms, and salt can be recovered by evaporation. Don't confuse it with reactions like quicklime + water, which are chemical.

Card 17
Front: What is the pH range of human blood, and which buffer system maintains it?
Back: About 7.35–7.45 (slightly alkaline), maintained mainly by the carbonic acid–bicarbonate (H₂CO₃/HCO₃⁻) buffer.

Card 18
Front: Which gas is produced when a metal reacts with a dilute acid, and how is it tested?
Back: Hydrogen gas. It burns with a "pop" sound when a burning splinter is brought near the mouth of the test tube.

Card 19
Front: What is galvanisation, and why does zinc protect iron even when the coating is scratched?
Back: Coating iron with a layer of zinc. Zinc is more reactive, so it corrodes preferentially (sacrificial protection), keeping the iron safe even at scratches.

Card 20
Front: Mnemonic trick: How do you remember the first 20 elements in order?
Back: "Hi He Likes Beer But Could Not Offer Full Nine Songs. Na Mg Al SiPS ClAr" — H, He, Li, Be, B, C, N, O, F, Ne, Na, Mg, Al, Si, P, S, Cl, Ar, K, Ca.
