51. Which of the following statements about isotopes is correct?
(a) Isotopes have the same mass number but different atomic numbers (b) Isotopes always have identical chemical and physical properties (c) Isotopes have different numbers of protons and neutrons both (d) Isotopes have the same atomic number but different mass numbers
Answer: (d)
Solution: The tempting wrong option reverses the definition — same mass number with different atomic numbers describes isobars, not isotopes. Isotopes are atoms of the same element (same number of protons, hence same atomic number) with different numbers of neutrons, hence different mass numbers. They have identical chemical properties but may differ in physical properties, so the answer is that isotopes have the same atomic number but different mass numbers.

52. The pH of a solution is 3. If its hydrogen ion concentration is made 100 times smaller, the new pH will be:
(a) 1 (b) 5 (c) 6 (d) 300
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is to subtract 100 from pH or to think pH changes by 100 units. pH is logarithmic: reducing H⁺ concentration by a factor of 100 (10²) raises pH by exactly 2 units, from 3 to 5, so the answer is 5.

53. Which gas is produced when zinc reacts with dilute sulphuric acid?
(a) Oxygen (b) Sulphur dioxide (c) Hydrogen sulphide (d) Hydrogen
Answer: (d)
Solution: The tempting wrong option is sulphur dioxide, which is released when metals react with hot concentrated sulphuric acid, not the dilute acid. With dilute H₂SO₄, zinc undergoes a typical metal–acid displacement: Zn + H₂SO₄ → ZnSO₄ + H₂, so the answer is hydrogen.

54. Arrange the following in increasing order of reactivity: gold, iron, sodium, copper.
(a) Sodium < Iron < Copper < Gold (b) Gold < Copper < Iron < Sodium (c) Copper < Gold < Iron < Sodium (d) Iron < Copper < Sodium < Gold
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap option simply reverses the correct order, listing the most reactive metal first. In the reactivity series, sodium is highly reactive, iron is moderately reactive, copper is low, and gold is among the least reactive metals, so the increasing order is gold < copper < iron < sodium, and the answer is gold < copper < iron < sodium.

55. Which of the following is an amphoteric oxide?
(a) Na₂O (b) SO₂ (c) Al₂O₃ (d) CaO
Answer: (c)
Solution: Sodium oxide and calcium oxide are basic oxides, and sulphur dioxide is acidic — these are the tempting distractors. Aluminium oxide reacts with both acids and bases (with HCl it forms AlCl₃, with NaOH it forms sodium aluminate), making it amphoteric, so the answer is Al₂O₃.

56. The number of electrons in an atom of element X is 17. Its valency and position in the periodic table are:
(a) Valency 7, Group 17 (b) Valency 1, Group 7 (c) Valency 1, Group 17 (d) Valency 7, Group 7
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is to call the valency 7 because the atom has 7 valence electrons. With configuration 2, 8, 7, the atom (chlorine) gains one electron to complete its octet, so its valency is 1, and with 7 valence electrons it belongs to Group 17, so the answer is valency 1, Group 17.

57. Which of the following statements about the Modern Periodic Table is correct?
(a) Elements are arranged in increasing order of atomic mass (b) Isotopes are placed in separate positions (c) There are 7 groups and 18 periods (d) Elements are arranged in increasing order of atomic number
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap options recall Mendeleev's table: atomic mass ordering was Mendeleev's basis, and the group–period numbers are reversed (there are 18 groups and 7 periods). Moseley's work established atomic number as the basis of the modern table, and isotopes share one position, so the answer is that elements are arranged in increasing order of atomic number.

59. Which of the following is used as a moderator in a nuclear reactor?

60. The chemical formula of washing soda is:

61. Which of the following pairs is correctly matched?
(a) Laughing gas — Nitrogen dioxide (b) Marsh gas — Ethane (c) Tear gas — Chloropicrin (d) Producer gas — Pure carbon monoxide
Answer: (c)
Solution: The traps are familiar gas nicknames mismatched: laughing gas is nitrous oxide (N₂O), not nitrogen dioxide; marsh gas is methane, not ethane; producer gas is a mixture of CO and N₂, not pure CO. Tear gas commonly refers to chloropicrin (CCl₃NO₂) among similar agents, so the answer is tear gas — chloropicrin.

62. The oxidation number of sulphur in H₂SO₄ is:
(a) +4 (b) +2 (c) +6 (d) −2
Answer: (c)
Solution: The tempting wrong option is +4, which is sulphur's oxidation state in SO₂ or H₂SO₃ — a similar-compound trap. In H₂SO₄: 2(+1) + x + 4(−2) = 0, giving x = +6, so the answer is +6.

63. Which of the following is NOT a greenhouse gas?
(a) Oxygen (b) Methane (c) Nitrous oxide (d) Carbon dioxide
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is to pick nitrous oxide, assuming only common gases count — but N₂O is a potent greenhouse gas. Carbon dioxide, methane, and nitrous oxide all absorb infrared radiation; oxygen is a major atmospheric gas that does not act as a greenhouse gas, so the answer is oxygen.

64. Hardness of water is primarily due to the presence of:
(a) Calcium and magnesium salts (b) Sodium and potassium salts (c) Iron and aluminium salts (d) Chloride and sulphate ions only
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap option names chloride and sulphate ions, but anions alone do not cause hardness — it depends on the cations. Temporary and permanent hardness are caused by dissolved bicarbonates, chlorides, and sulphates of calcium and magnesium, so the answer is calcium and magnesium salts.

65. Which of the following statements about catalysts is correct?
(a) A catalyst increases the rate of reaction by lowering activation energy (b) A catalyst changes the equilibrium position of a reversible reaction (c) A catalyst is consumed during the reaction (d) A catalyst increases the energy of the products
Answer: (a)
Solution: The tempting wrong option claims the catalyst shifts equilibrium — in fact a catalyst speeds up both forward and backward reactions equally, so equilibrium is reached faster but its position is unchanged. A catalyst works by providing an alternative path of lower activation energy and remains chemically unchanged, so the answer is that it increases the rate by lowering activation energy.

68. Which of the following is the correct order of increasing atomic size?
(a) Na < Mg < Al < Si (b) Mg < Na < Si < Al (c) Al < Si < Na < Mg (d) Si < Al < Mg < Na
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap option lists the elements in simple left-to-right periodic order, ignoring that size decreases across a period. Across Period 3, increasing nuclear charge pulls electrons closer, so size decreases from Na to Si; hence increasing size runs in reverse: Si < Al < Mg < Na, so the answer is Si < Al < Mg < Na.

70. Which of the following statements about graphite is correct?
(a) It is a bad conductor of electricity (b) It has a layered structure with weak forces between layers (c) Each carbon atom is bonded to four other carbon atoms (d) It is harder than diamond
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is the four-bonds statement, which describes diamond, not graphite — a classic allotrope confusion. In graphite each carbon bonds to three others, forming hexagonal layers held by weak van der Waals forces; the free electrons make it a good conductor and the sliding layers make it soft, so the answer is that it has a layered structure with weak forces between layers.

72. Which of the following is an example of a disproportionation reaction?
(a) 2H₂O₂ → 2H₂O + O₂ (b) Zn + CuSO₄ → ZnSO₄ + Cu (c) CaCO₃ → CaO + CO₂ (d) CH₄ + 2O₂ → CO₂ + 2H₂O
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap options are a displacement, a decomposition, and a combustion reaction — none involve one element being both oxidised and reduced. In hydrogen peroxide, oxygen has oxidation state −1; in water it becomes −2 (reduction) and in O₂ it becomes 0 (oxidation), so the same element undergoes both, making it a disproportionation reaction, and the answer is 2H₂O₂ → 2H₂O + O₂.
