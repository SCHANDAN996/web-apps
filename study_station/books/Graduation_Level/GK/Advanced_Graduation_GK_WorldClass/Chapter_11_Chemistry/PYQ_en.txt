# 📊 PYQ Analysis — General Chemistry (Advanced Level)

## 1. How Exams Test This Chapter

General Chemistry is one of the most reliable scoring areas in the General Awareness / General Science section of UPSC (Prelims GS), SSC (CGL, CHSL, MTS, GD), Banking (IBPS, SBI), Railway (NTPC, Group D) and State PCS exams. The questions are almost always **fact-recall or one-step application** — no long calculations. The examiner's real weapon is not difficulty, but **confusion**: similar-sounding chemicals, almost-correct statements, and reversed cause-effect pairs.

Across exams, the questions cluster around these recurring sub-themes:

- **Acids, Bases & Salts** — pH of common substances, indicators, antacids, baking soda vs washing soda, plaster of Paris.
- **Metals & Non-metals** — reactivity series, corrosion (rusting, galvanisation), alloys and their composition, extraction basics.
- **Carbon & Its Compounds** — allotropes (diamond, graphite, fullerene), hydrocarbons, functional groups, soaps and detergents.
- **Atoms, Molecules & the Periodic Table** — atomic number/mass, isotopes/isobars/isotones, periodic trends, Mendeleev vs Modern table.
- **Chemical Reactions & Equations** — types of reactions (combination, decomposition, displacement, redox), oxidation–reduction identification.
- **Chemistry in Everyday Life** — fuels (LPG, CNG composition), gases (laughing gas, tear gas, marsh gas), fertilisers, common chemical names vs formulas.
- **Air, Water & Pollution** — composition of air, hard water, acid rain, greenhouse gases, ozone layer.

## 2. Sub-topic Weightage (Qualitative)

Based on the pattern of questions asked across these exams:

- **Highest frequency:** Acids–bases–salts, metals/alloys, and everyday-life chemistry (common names and formulas). Nearly every paper touches at least one of these.
- **Moderate frequency:** Periodic table and atomic structure, carbon compounds, types of chemical reactions.
- **Occasional but predictable:** Pollution and environmental chemistry (acid rain, ozone, greenhouse gases) — these appear in cycles, often after environmental news.

**Exam-wise flavour:**
- **UPSC / State PCS:** Conceptual and statement-based — "Which of the statements is/are correct?", application to daily life and environment.
- **SSC / Railway:** Direct one-liners — formulas, common names, pH values, alloy composition.
- **Banking:** Lighter load; mostly everyday chemistry, metals, and basic definitions.

## 3. The Examiner's Traps (with Examples)

### Trap 1: Almost-Correct Statement
One word is changed in an otherwise true statement.
> "Graphite is a good conductor of electricity because each carbon atom has one delocalised (free) electron that can move within its layers." ✅ True.
> Trap version: "Diamond conducts electricity because of free electrons." ❌ — diamond has no free electrons; all four valence electrons of each carbon atom are locked in covalent bonds.

### Trap 2: Similar-Sounding Names / Formula Confusion
- Baking soda = NaHCO₃; Washing soda = Na₂CO₃·10H₂O; Caustic soda = NaOH. Options deliberately mix these.
- Laughing gas = N₂O (nitrous oxide), not NO₂ or NO.

### Trap 3: Chronological / Discovery Confusion
- Mendeleev's table (1869) was based on **atomic mass**; Moseley's Modern table (1913) on **atomic number**. Questions swap the two scientists or the two properties.

### Trap 4: Negative Phrasing
- "Which of the following is **NOT** an allotrope of carbon?" — students in a hurry pick the first true allotrope they see (diamond) instead of the odd one out.

### Trap 5: Reversed Cause–Effect
- "Acid rain is caused by ozone depletion." ❌ — acid rain is caused by SO₂ and NOₓ; ozone depletion is caused by CFCs. The two environmental issues are deliberately swapped.

### Trap 6: Composition Half-Truths
- "Brass is an alloy of copper and zinc" ✅; trap option: "Brass is an alloy of copper and tin" ❌ (that is bronze). LPG is mainly propane + butane; CNG is mainly methane — frequently interchanged.

## 4. Top 10 High-Yield PYQ-Style Questions (with Solutions)

**Q1.** Which of the following is the chemical formula of washing soda?
(a) NaHCO₃ (b) Na₂CO₃·10H₂O (c) NaOH (d) CaOCl₂
**Answer:** (b)
**Solution:** Washing soda is sodium carbonate decahydrate, Na₂CO₃·10H₂O. (a) is baking soda and (c) is caustic soda — the classic similar-names trap.
**Trap type:** Similar-Sounding Names | **Source:** NCERT Class 10 Science, Ch. 2

**Q2.** Which gas is commonly known as 'laughing gas'?
(a) Nitric oxide (b) Nitrogen dioxide (c) Nitrous oxide (d) Ammonia
**Answer:** (c)
**Solution:** Laughing gas is nitrous oxide, N₂O, used as a mild anaesthetic. NO and NO₂ are decoys placed to exploit formula confusion.
**Trap type:** Formula Confusion | **Source:** PYQ-style

**Q3.** The Modern Periodic Law is based on:
(a) Atomic mass (b) Atomic number (c) Density (d) Valency
**Answer:** (b)
**Solution:** Moseley (1913) showed that atomic number, not atomic mass, is the fundamental property. Mendeleev's table used atomic mass — the two are routinely swapped in options.
**Trap type:** Chronological Confusion | **Source:** NCERT Class 10 Science, Ch. 5

**Q4.** Which of the following is NOT an allotrope of carbon?
(a) Diamond (b) Graphite (c) Fullerene (d) Quartz
**Answer:** (d)
**Solution:** Quartz is silicon dioxide (SiO₂), not a form of carbon. The negative phrasing makes hurried candidates tick (a).
**Trap type:** Negative Phrasing | **Source:** NCERT Class 10 Science, Ch. 4

**Q5.** Galvanisation is the process of coating iron with:
(a) Copper (b) Zinc (c) Aluminium (d) Tin
**Answer:** (b)
**Solution:** A thin layer of zinc protects iron from rusting; zinc corrodes preferentially, shielding the iron. Tin coating (tinning) is used for food cans — a frequent decoy.
**Trap type:** Almost-Correct (similar processes) | **Source:** NCERT Class 10 Science, Ch. 3

**Q6.** The pH of a neutral solution at 25°C is:
(a) 0 (b) 14 (c) 7 (d) 1
**Answer:** (c)
**Solution:** pH 7 is neutral; below 7 acidic, above 7 basic. Note the hidden condition "at 25°C" — the pH scale is temperature-dependent, a detail advanced papers exploit.
**Trap type:** Almost-Correct Statement | **Source:** NCERT Class 10 Science, Ch. 2

**Q7.** Brass is an alloy of:
(a) Copper and tin (b) Copper and zinc (c) Iron and chromium (d) Aluminium and copper
**Answer:** (b)
**Solution:** Brass = Cu + Zn; bronze = Cu + Sn; stainless steel = Fe + Cr (+Ni). Alloy pairs are the single most repeated composition trap.
**Trap type:** Composition Half-Truth | **Source:** NCERT Class 10 Science, Ch. 3

**Q8.** Acid rain is mainly caused by the release of:
(a) CO₂ and CO (b) SO₂ and NOₓ (c) CFCs (d) Methane
**Answer:** (b)
**Solution:** Sulphur dioxide and nitrogen oxides form sulphuric and nitric acids in rainwater. CFCs cause ozone depletion — the two issues are deliberately interchanged.
**Trap type:** Reversed Cause–Effect | **Source:** NCERT Class 10 Science, Ch. 2 / Class 11 Chemistry

**Q9.** Which of the following reactions is an example of a displacement reaction?
(a) CaO + H₂O → Ca(OH)₂ (b) 2KClO₃ → 2KCl + 3O₂
(c) Zn + CuSO₄ → ZnSO₄ + Cu (d) NaOH + HCl → NaCl + H₂O
**Answer:** (c)
**Solution:** More reactive zinc displaces copper from its salt solution. (a) is combination, (b) decomposition, (d) neutralisation/double displacement — each option is a different reaction type by design.
**Trap type:** Category Confusion | **Source:** NCERT Class 10 Science, Ch. 1

**Q10.** The main component of CNG is:
(a) Propane (b) Butane (c) Methane (d) Ethane
**Answer:** (c)
**Solution:** CNG is compressed natural gas, chiefly methane (CH₄). LPG (propane + butane) is offered as the decoy — fuels are a favourite swap pair.
**Trap type:** Composition Half-Truth | **Source:** PYQ-style

## 5. The 15/45-Second Rule Applied

**The rule:** If a question is pure recall (formula, common name, alloy), decide within **15 seconds** — you either know it or you don't; mark and move. If it is statement-based or negative-phrased, allow up to **45 seconds** to parse every option carefully.

**Applied to Q4 (allotropes, negative phrasing):** This is a 45-second question. The word "NOT" changes everything. Read each option, mentally tag diamond ✅, graphite ✅, fullerene ✅, quartz ❌ — then answer. Spending 30 extra seconds here converts a likely error into a sure mark.

**Applied to Q6 (pH of neutral solution):** This is a 15-second question. pH 7 is instant recall. Do not overthink the "25°C" clause into doubting yourself — recognise it as a recall item, answer, and bank the saved 30 seconds for statement-based questions elsewhere in the paper.

**Strategy summary:**
- Formula/common-name/alloy/pH questions → 15 seconds, first instinct is usually right.
- "Which is NOT…", "Which statements are correct…", cause–effect pairs → full 45 seconds, read all four options before committing.
- Never leave chemistry questions unattempted where there is no negative marking; where there is, skip only if you cannot eliminate even two options.

## 6. Key Takeaways for Revision

1. Master the **formula–common name table** (sodium, calcium, chlorine compounds) — it feeds the most questions.
2. Learn **alloy compositions in pairs** (brass/bronze, steel/stainless steel, solder, amalgam) because options always pair them.
3. Memorise **gas nicknames** (laughing, marsh, tear, producer, water gas) with exact formulas.
4. For every environmental issue, fix the **correct culprit gas**: acid rain → SO₂/NOₓ; ozone hole → CFCs; greenhouse → CO₂/CH₄.
5. Practise reading **negative questions slowly** — underline NOT/EXCEPT before looking at options.
