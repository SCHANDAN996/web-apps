101. Which of the following is used as a moderator in a nuclear reactor?
(a) Heavy water (b) Cadmium (c) Uranium-235 (d) Plutonium
Answer: (a)
Solution: Cadmium is a tempting option, but cadmium rods are control rods that absorb neutrons to regulate the reaction — they do not slow neutrons down. A moderator slows fast neutrons to thermal speeds so fission can continue, and heavy water (D₂O) is a standard moderator, so the answer is heavy water.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Physics (Nuclei) / PYQ-style

102. The pH of a 0.001 M HCl solution is:
(a) 1 (b) 2 (c) 11 (d) 3
Answer: (d)
Solution: The value 11 is a trap for those who subtract from 14 unnecessarily — that applies only when converting pOH to pH. HCl is a strong acid and dissociates completely, so [H⁺] = 10⁻³ M and pH = −log(10⁻³) = 3, so the answer is 3.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Chemistry (Equilibrium)

103. Which gas is known as 'laughing gas'?
(a) Nitrous oxide (b) Nitric oxide (c) Nitrogen dioxide (d) Dinitrogen pentoxide
Answer: (a)
Solution: The similar names of nitrogen oxides are a classic examiner's trap. Nitric oxide (NO) is a colourless gas involved in signalling, while nitrous oxide (N₂O) is the anaesthetic called laughing gas, so the answer is nitrous oxide.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Chemistry (The p-Block Elements)

104. The oxidation number of sulphur in H₂SO₄ is:
(a) +4 (b) +6 (c) +2 (d) −2
Answer: (b)
Solution: The value +4 is the trap — it is sulphur's oxidation state in H₂SO₃, not H₂SO₄. Taking H as +1 and O as −2: 2(+1) + x + 4(−2) = 0, giving x = +6, so the answer is +6.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Chemistry (Redox Reactions)

105. Which of the following is an amphoteric oxide?
(a) Na₂O (b) CaO (c) SO₂ (d) Al₂O₃
Answer: (d)
Solution: Na₂O and CaO are basic oxides and SO₂ is acidic, so none of them fits. Aluminium oxide reacts with both acids and bases, making it amphoteric, so the answer is Al₂O₃.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Chemistry (The p-Block Elements)

106. The chemical formula of washing soda is:
(a) NaHCO₃ (b) Na₂CO₃ (c) NaOH (d) Na₂CO₃·10H₂O
Answer: (d)
Solution: Na₂CO₃ is the trap — it is soda ash (anhydrous sodium carbonate), not washing soda. Washing soda is the decahydrate, Na₂CO₃·10H₂O, while NaHCO₃ is baking soda, so the answer is Na₂CO₃·10H₂O.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Chemistry (Acids, Bases and Salts)

107. Which of the following has the highest electronegativity?
(a) Fluorine (b) Oxygen (c) Chlorine (d) Nitrogen
Answer: (a)
Solution: Chlorine is tempting because it has the highest electron affinity, but electronegativity and electron affinity are different concepts — a frequent examiner's trap. Fluorine is the most electronegative element (3.98 on the Pauling scale), so the answer is fluorine.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Chemistry (Classification of Elements)

108. In the electrolysis of brine, the gas liberated at the cathode is:
(a) Oxygen (b) Hydrogen (c) Chlorine (d) Nitrogen
Answer: (b)
Solution: Chlorine is the trap — it is liberated at the anode, not the cathode. In the chlor-alkali process, hydrogen gas is released at the cathode and NaOH remains in solution, so the answer is hydrogen.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Chemistry (Acids, Bases and Salts)

109. The IUPAC name of CH₃–CH(OH)–CH₃ is:
(a) Propanone (b) Propan-1-ol (c) Propanal (d) Propan-2-ol
Answer: (d)
Solution: Propan-1-ol is the trap for those who ignore the position of the functional group. The –OH group is on the middle carbon, so numbering gives it position 2, so the answer is propan-2-ol.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Chemistry (Organic Chemistry – Basic Principles)

110. Which vitamin is synthesized in the human skin on exposure to sunlight?
(a) Vitamin A (b) Vitamin C (c) Vitamin D (d) Vitamin K
Answer: (c)
Solution: Most vitamins must come from the diet, which makes the dietary vitamins tempting but wrong here. Vitamin D (cholecalciferol) is produced in the skin when 7-dehydrocholesterol is exposed to ultraviolet sunlight, so the answer is vitamin D.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Chemistry (Biomolecules)

111. The number of electrons in ¹⁸O²⁻ (atomic number of O = 8) is:
(a) 8 (b) 18 (c) 10 (d) 16
Answer: (c)
Solution: The mass number 18 is a trap — electrons depend on atomic number and charge, not mass number. A neutral O atom has 8 electrons; the 2− charge means 2 extra electrons, giving 10, so the answer is 10.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Chemistry (Structure of Atom)

112. Which of the following is a greenhouse gas?
(a) Oxygen (b) Methane (c) Nitrogen (d) Argon
Answer: (b)
Solution: Oxygen, nitrogen and argon are the main atmospheric gases and do not absorb infrared radiation significantly. Methane is a potent greenhouse gas, far more effective per molecule than CO₂, so the answer is methane.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Chemistry (Environmental Chemistry)

113. The bond present in a nitrogen molecule (N₂) is:
(a) Single bond (b) Triple bond (c) Double bond (d) Ionic bond
Answer: (b)
Solution: A double bond is the trap for those who confuse N₂ with O₂. Each nitrogen atom has 5 valence electrons and shares 3 pairs with the other nitrogen atom, forming a triple bond (N≡N), which explains nitrogen's inertness, so the answer is triple bond.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Chemistry (Chemical Bonding)

114. Which metal is liquid at room temperature?
(a) Sodium (b) Gallium (c) Mercury (d) Caesium
Answer: (c)
Solution: Gallium and caesium are traps — they melt near 30 °C and 28 °C respectively, slightly above standard room temperature (25 °C). Mercury (melting point −38.8 °C) is the only metal that is unambiguously liquid at room temperature, so the answer is mercury.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Chemistry (The d- and f-Block Elements)

115. The process of coating iron with zinc to prevent rusting is called:
(a) Anodising (b) Electroplating (c) Galvanisation (d) Alloying
Answer: (c)
Solution: Electroplating is a tempting but broader term; the specific process of coating iron or steel with a layer of zinc is called galvanisation. Zinc acts as a sacrificial metal, so the answer is galvanisation.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Chemistry (Metals and Non-metals)

116. Which of the following is the lightest element?
(a) Hydrogen (b) Helium (c) Lithium (d) Deuterium
Answer: (a)
Solution: Helium is a trap because it is used in balloons and feels 'light', but hydrogen (atomic mass ≈ 1) is the lightest element. Deuterium is an isotope of hydrogen, not a separate element, so the answer is hydrogen.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Chemistry (Hydrogen)

117. The common name of calcium sulphate hemihydrate is:
(a) Gypsum (b) Quicklime (c) Plaster of Paris (d) Limestone
Answer: (c)
Solution: Gypsum (CaSO₄·2H₂O) is the trap — it is the dihydrate, not the hemihydrate. Plaster of Paris is CaSO₄·½H₂O, obtained by heating gypsum, so the answer is Plaster of Paris.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Chemistry (Acids, Bases and Salts)

118. Which of the following acids is present in vinegar?
(a) Acetic acid (b) Citric acid (c) Lactic acid (d) Tartaric acid
Answer: (a)
Solution: Citric acid (citrus fruits) and lactic acid (curd) are similar everyday acids that act as traps. Vinegar is a 5–8% solution of acetic acid (ethanoic acid) in water, so the answer is acetic acid.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Chemistry (Acids, Bases and Salts)

119. The atomic number of an element is 17. Its valency is:
(a) 8 (b) 1 (c) 7 (d) 2
Answer: (b)
Solution: The value 7 is the trap — it is the number of valence electrons, not the valency. The electronic configuration is 2, 8, 7; the atom needs 1 electron to complete its octet, so its valency is 1, so the answer is 1.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Science (Structure of the Atom)

120. Which of the following is used in the manufacture of safety matches?
(a) White phosphorus (b) Red phosphorus (c) Phosphorus pentoxide (d) Phosphoric acid
Answer: (b)
Solution: White phosphorus is the trap — it is highly reactive and poisonous, so it is unsafe for matches. Red phosphorus, being stable and non-poisonous, is coated on the striking surface of matchboxes, so the answer is red phosphorus.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Chemistry (The p-Block Elements)

121. Consider the following statements about the Modern Periodic Table:
Statement 1: Elements are arranged in increasing order of atomic mass.
Statement 2: There are 7 periods and 18 groups.
Statement 3: Group 18 elements are called noble gases.
Which of the above statements is/are correct?
(a) 1 and 2 only (b) 2 and 3 only (c) 1 and 3 only (d) 1, 2 and 3
Answer: (b)
Solution: Statement 1 is the almost-correct trap — the modern table arranges elements by atomic number (Moseley's law), not atomic mass; atomic mass was Mendeleev's basis. Statements 2 and 3 are correct, so the answer is 2 and 3 only.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Chemistry (Classification of Elements)

122. Which of the following metal oxides is basic in nature?
(a) CO₂ (b) SO₃ (c) Al₂O₃ (d) CaO
Answer: (d)
Solution: Al₂O₃ is the trap — it is amphoteric, reacting with both acids and bases, not purely basic. CO₂ and SO₃ are non-metal oxides and are acidic, while calcium oxide is a metal oxide that reacts with water to form a base, so the answer is CaO.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Chemistry (Metals and Non-metals)

123. Arrange the following in increasing order of their acidic strength:
1. H₂O 2. H₂S 3. H₂Se 4. H₂Te
(a) 4 < 3 < 2 < 1 (b) 2 < 1 < 3 < 4 (c) 1 < 2 < 3 < 4 (d) 1 < 3 < 2 < 4
Answer: (c)
Solution: The reverse order is the trap for those who apply electronegativity logic incorrectly. Down Group 16, the H–E bond weakens as the atom size increases, so the hydride releases H⁺ more easily and acidic strength increases: H₂O < H₂S < H₂Se < H₂Te, so the answer is 1 < 2 < 3 < 4.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Chemistry (The p-Block Elements)

124. Consider the following statements about isotopes:
Statement 1: Isotopes have the same number of protons but different numbers of neutrons.
Statement 2: Isotopes have identical chemical properties.
Statement 3: Isotopes always have identical physical properties.
Which of the above statements is/are correct?
(a) 1 only (b) 1, 2 and 3 (c) 2 and 3 only (d) 1 and 2 only
Answer: (d)
Solution: Statement 3 is the trap — isotopes differ in mass, so physical properties like density, boiling point and diffusion rate differ (e.g., heavy water vs ordinary water). Chemical properties depend on electron configuration and are identical, so statements 1 and 2 are correct, so the answer is 1 and 2 only.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Science (Structure of the Atom)

125. In the reaction: MnO₂ + 4HCl → MnCl₂ + Cl₂ + 2H₂O, which substance is reduced?
(a) MnO₂ (b) HCl (c) MnCl₂ (d) Cl₂
Answer: (a)
Solution: HCl is the trap — chlorine's oxidation state rises from −1 in HCl to 0 in Cl₂, so HCl is oxidised, not reduced. Manganese goes from +4 in MnO₂ to +2 in MnCl₂, a gain of electrons, meaning MnO₂ is reduced, so the answer is MnO₂.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Chemistry (Redox Reactions)
