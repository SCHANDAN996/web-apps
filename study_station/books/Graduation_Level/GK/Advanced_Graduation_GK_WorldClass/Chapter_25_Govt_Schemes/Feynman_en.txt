# 🧒 Feynman Technique — Government Schemes & Initiatives

## The Most Confusing Concept: Central Sector Scheme vs Centrally Sponsored Scheme

Ask any aspirant the difference between these two, and watch them freeze. Both are "Central Government schemes." Both are announced by the same ministries. Both appear in the same Budget documents. Yet examiners love to swap them — and thousands of students lose marks every year on exactly this confusion.

Let me explain it the way I'd explain it to a 12-year-old.

---

## The Pocket-Money Story

Imagine your father gives you ₹100 for a school project.

**Case 1 — Central Sector Scheme:**
Father says: "Here is the full ₹100. I have decided exactly what project you will make, how you will make it, and I will check it myself." You have no say. The money is 100% his, the design is 100% his, the control is 100% his.

That is a **Central Sector Scheme** — the Central Government pays **100% of the cost**, designs it fully, and implements it directly through its own machinery. States are just the ground where it happens. Examples: **PM-KISAN** (₹6,000/year straight to farmers' accounts), **Pradhan Mantri Kisan Sampada Yojana**, **Ujjwala Yojana**, **Atal Pension Yojana**.

**Case 2 — Centrally Sponsored Scheme:**
Father says: "I will give ₹60, but your mother must add ₹40. Also, mother will decide the colours and buy the material, because she knows your school better."

That is a **Centrally Sponsored Scheme (CSS)** — the Centre and the **State share the cost** (commonly 60:40; for the 8 North-Eastern and 2 Himalayan states — Uttarakhand and Himachal Pradesh — it is 90:10; for Union Territories, the central share is generally higher, up to 100% for UTs without a legislature). The **State implements** it. Examples: **MGNREGA** (wage cost largely central, but material/unemployment allowance shared with states), **Pradhan Mantri Awas Yojana–Gramin**, **National Health Mission**, **Samagra Shiksha Abhiyan**, **Mid-Day Meal (PM POSHAN)**.

---

## The One-Line Test

> **"Who opens the wallet fully?"**
> - Centre opens it **fully** → Central **Sector** Scheme.
> - Centre and State **split** the wallet → Centrally **Sponsored** Scheme.

Memory hook: **"Sector = Solo, Sponsored = Shared."** (S-S pairs on both sides — impossible to mix up.)

---

## The Examiner's Trap

The classic trap question: *"MGNREGA is a Central Sector Scheme — True or False?"* It sounds right because the Centre funds most of it. **False.** It is a Centrally Sponsored Scheme because states share part of the cost (material component, unemployment allowance) and implement it through Gram Panchayats.

Second trap: **PM-KISAN** is 100% centrally funded — it is a **Central Sector Scheme**, not sponsored. Examiners swap these two constantly.

Third trap: funding ratios. Plain states = **60:40**, NE + Himalayan states (Uttarakhand, Himachal Pradesh) = **90:10**. Note: **Jammu & Kashmir is now a Union Territory** (since 2019), so it follows the UT funding pattern, not the Himalayan-state 90:10 pattern. A question will quietly change "90:10" to "80:20" — that option is wrong.

Then open the book and check what you missed. Whatever you missed is exactly what the examiner will ask.
