# 📊 PYQ Analysis — Sports & Competitions

## How Exams Ask Questions from This Chapter

Sports GK is a **low-volume but high-certainty** area: exams ask few questions, but those questions are almost always direct fact-recall. Aspirants who revise a compact list of trophies, venues, and "firsts" convert nearly every sports question; those who read widely but vaguely lose marks to near-identical options.

### Recurring Sub-Topics (in words, not numbers)

- **Trophies and associated sports** — the single most repeated theme across SSC, Banking, Railway and State PCS. Durand Cup, Santosh Trophy, Ranji Trophy, Dhyan Chand Trophy, Thomas/Uber Cup, Davis Cup appear again and again.
- **Grand Slam tennis** — which tournament is played on which surface (clay/grass/hard), and the calendar order of the four Slams.
- **Olympics basics** — motto, rings and their colours, first modern Games (Athens 1896), India's first individual gold (Abhinav Bindra, Beijing 2008), hockey golds.
- **Venue–event matching** — where a particular edition of the Asian Games, Commonwealth Games, or Cricket World Cup was held.
- **"First" facts** — first Indian woman Olympic medallist (Karnam Malleswari, Sydney 2000), first cricket World Cup (1975, England), first FIFA World Cup (1930, Uruguay).
- **Sports terms and player counts** — number of players per side, terms like "LBW", "checkmate", "deuce", "bogey".
- **National Sports Day / awards** — 29 August (Dhyan Chand's birthday), Major Dhyan Chand Khel Ratna, Arjuna Award.

### Exam-Wise Tendencies

- **SSC (CGL/CHSL/GD):** trophy–sport matching and player counts dominate; options are designed with same-category distractors (four real trophies, three of them for the wrong sport).
- **Banking (IBPS/SBI):** recent winners and hosts of major events, plus sports awards; often clubbed with current affairs, but the static skeleton (trophy lists, Grand Slam surfaces) is what gets tested repeatedly.
- **Railway (NTPC/Group D):** very direct one-liners — "Which sport is associated with the Davis Cup?" — with minimal twisting.
- **State PCS:** adds state-specific angles (state games, stadiums, sportspersons from that state) on top of the standard list.

### The Examiner's Traps (Jaal-Prakaar)

1. **Almost Correct (लगभग-सही):** "Wimbledon is played on clay" — only the surface is wrong. All four options name real Slams; only one pairs the right surface.
2. **Chronological Confusion (कालक्रम भ्रम):** swapping the order of Grand Slams in a calendar year, or the years of two World Cups. Options differ only in sequence.
3. **Similar Names (मिलते-जुलते नाम):** Thomas Cup vs. Davis Cup (both team events, but badminton vs. tennis); Ranji Trophy vs. Duleep Trophy (both cricket, but different formats of competition).
4. **Negative Phrasing (नकारात्मक वाक्य):** "Which of the following is NOT associated with hockey?" — three correct pairs and one wrong; students in a hurry pick the first correct pair they see.
5. **Person–Achievement Swap:** attributing the first individual Olympic gold to a hockey player, or crediting Malleswari with a gold instead of a bronze.

### The 15/45-Second Rule

- **15 seconds:** pure recall items — trophy–sport pairs, player counts, motto of the Olympics. If you have revised the list, answer instantly; if not, mark and move. Thinking longer does not help recall.
- **45 seconds:** statement-based or negative-phrasing questions, and anything involving chronology (order of Slams, sequence of hosts). Here, read every option fully — the trap lives in the option you skip.

## Top 10 High-Yield Questions (PYQ-style) with Solutions

**Q1. The Durand Cup is associated with which sport?**
(a) Hockey (b) Football (c) Cricket (d) Badminton
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** The Durand Cup, started in 1888 at Shimla, is one of the oldest football tournaments in the world. Hockey is the tempting wrong option because Indian sports GK is hockey-heavy — a classic Almost Correct trap.
**Trap type:** Almost Correct | **Rule:** 15 seconds
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q2. Which Grand Slam tennis tournament is played on clay courts?**
(a) Wimbledon (b) US Open (c) French Open (d) Australian Open
**Answer: (c)**
**Solution:** The French Open (Roland Garros) is the only Slam on clay. Wimbledon is grass; the US and Australian Opens are hard courts. Wimbledon is the most famous Slam, so it attracts hasty clicks — Almost Correct.
**Trap type:** Almost Correct | **Rule:** 15 seconds
**Source:** NCERT-adjacent static GK / PYQ-style

**Q3. Arrange the four tennis Grand Slams in their calendar-year order:**
(a) Australian Open – French Open – Wimbledon – US Open
(b) French Open – Australian Open – Wimbledon – US Open
(c) Australian Open – Wimbledon – French Open – US Open
(d) Wimbledon – French Open – Australian Open – US Open
**Answer: (a)**
**Solution:** January (Australian Open) → May–June (French Open) → June–July (Wimbledon) → August–September (US Open). Every option uses the same four names; only the sequence differs — Chronological Confusion. Take the full 45 seconds and verify each pair.
**Trap type:** Chronological Confusion | **Rule:** 45 seconds
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q4. The Thomas Cup is associated with:**
(a) Tennis (b) Badminton (c) Table Tennis (d) Hockey
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** Thomas Cup = men's world team badminton; Uber Cup = women's. The Davis Cup (tennis) is the similar-name distractor examiners love. Remember: **T**homas = shu**tt**le.
**Trap type:** Similar Names | **Rule:** 15 seconds
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q5. Who won India's first individual Olympic gold medal?**
(a) Milkha Singh (b) Leander Paes (c) Abhinav Bindra (d) Rajyavardhan Singh Rathore
**Answer: (c)**
**Solution:** Abhinav Bindra, 10m air rifle, Beijing 2008. Rathore (silver, Athens 2004) and Paes (bronze, Atlanta 1996) are real medallists — the trap swaps the colour of the medal, not the name.
**Trap type:** Person–Achievement Swap | **Rule:** 15 seconds
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q6. Which of the following is NOT correctly matched?**
(a) Ranji Trophy – Cricket
(b) Santosh Trophy – Football
(c) Dhyan Chand Trophy – Hockey
(d) Davis Cup – Badminton
**Answer: (d)**
**Solution:** The Davis Cup belongs to tennis, not badminton. In negative-phrasing questions, students often stop reading after confirming (a) is correct — read all four before answering.
**Trap type:** Negative Phrasing | **Rule:** 45 seconds
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q7. The first modern Olympic Games (1896) were held in:**
(a) Paris (b) London (c) Athens (d) Rome
**Answer: (c)**
**Solution:** Athens, Greece — the ancient home of the Olympics. Paris is the classic distractor because the modern Games were revived by the Frenchman Pierre de Coubertin. Almost Correct by association.
**Trap type:** Almost Correct | **Rule:** 15 seconds
**Source:** NCERT / PYQ-style

**Q8. Karnam Malleswari won an Olympic medal in which sport?**
(a) Boxing (b) Weightlifting (c) Wrestling (d) Shooting
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** Bronze in weightlifting at Sydney 2000 — the first Indian woman to win an Olympic medal. Wrestling and boxing are tempting because later women medallists (Sakshi Malik, Mary Kom, Lovlina Borgohain) came from those sports.
**Trap type:** Similar Names / Person–Achievement Swap | **Rule:** 15 seconds
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q9. The first FIFA World Cup (1930) was hosted and won by:**
(a) Brazil (b) Uruguay (c) Argentina (d) Italy
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** Uruguay hosted and won the inaugural 1930 World Cup. Brazil is the reflex answer because of its five titles — a textbook Almost Correct trap built on fame, not fact.
**Trap type:** Almost Correct | **Rule:** 15 seconds
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q10. National Sports Day in India is celebrated on 29 August to mark the birth anniversary of:**
(a) Milkha Singh (b) Dhyan Chand (c) Kapil Dev (d) P. T. Usha
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** Major Dhyan Chand, the hockey legend, was born on 29 August 1905. The highest sports honour is also named after him (Major Dhyan Chand Khel Ratna Award, renamed from Rajiv Gandhi Khel Ratna in 2021).
**Trap type:** Similar Names | **Rule:** 15 seconds
**Source:** PYQ-style

## Revision Takeaway

Master three compact lists — **trophies by sport, Grand Slam surfaces and order, and India's Olympic "firsts"** — and you cover the large majority of what exams actually ask. Spend your 45 seconds only on chronology and "NOT"-type questions; everything else in this chapter is a 15-second recall item.
