Questions 1–20: Easy | Questions 21–25: Medium

1. The Olympic Games are held once in how many years?
(a) Two years (b) Three years (c) Five years (d) Four years
Answer: (d)
Solution: The modern Olympic Games follow a four-year cycle called an Olympiad, a tradition maintained since the first modern Games in 1896. Two years is tempting because Summer and Winter Olympics alternate every two years, but each edition itself is quadrennial, so the answer is four years.
Source: IOC official website

2. In which city were the first modern Olympic Games held in 1896?
(a) Paris (b) Rome (c) Athens (d) London
Answer: (c)
Solution: Paris is a common trap because the modern Olympic movement was revived by the Frenchman Pierre de Coubertin, but the first Games were held in Athens, Greece, the ancient home of the Olympics, so the answer is Athens.
Source: IOC official website

3. How many rings are there in the Olympic symbol?
(a) Five (b) Four (c) Six (d) Seven
Answer: (a)
Solution: The Olympic symbol has five interlaced rings representing the five inhabited continents united by sport. Four is tempting as it matches the four-year cycle, but the symbol has five rings, so the answer is five.
Source: IOC official website

4. Which country has won the most ICC Cricket World Cups (ODI)?
(a) India (b) West Indies (c) England (d) Australia
Answer: (d)
Solution: West Indies is tempting because it won the first two World Cups (1975, 1979), but Australia has won the tournament the most times, including 1987, 1999, 2003, 2007, 2015 and 2023, so the answer is Australia.
Source: ICC official website

5. The term "LBW" is associated with which sport?
(a) Cricket (b) Football (c) Hockey (d) Tennis
Answer: (a)
Solution: LBW stands for "Leg Before Wicket", a mode of dismissal in cricket when the ball strikes the batsman's leg in line with the stumps. It has no connection with hockey or football, so the answer is cricket.
Source: NCERT-based GK / MCC Laws of Cricket

6. How many players are there in a football (soccer) team on the field?
(a) 9 (b) 10 (c) 12 (d) 11
Answer: (d)
Solution: A football team fields 11 players including the goalkeeper. Nine is tempting as it is the number in baseball, but football has eleven players per side, so the answer is 11.
Source: FIFA Laws of the Game

7. The Durand Cup is associated with which sport in India?
(a) Football (b) Hockey (c) Cricket (d) Badminton
Answer: (a)
Solution: The Durand Cup, started in 1888, is one of the oldest football tournaments in the world and is held in India. Hockey is tempting because India has many hockey trophies like the Aga Khan Cup, but the Durand Cup belongs to football, so the answer is football.
Source: Durand Cup official records

8. Who is known as the "Flying Sikh" of India?
(a) Milkha Singh (b) P.T. Usha (c) Gagan Narang (d) Kapil Dev
Answer: (a)
Solution: P.T. Usha is a tempting distractor as she is called the "Payyoli Express" and is also a legendary sprinter, but the title "Flying Sikh" belongs to sprinter Milkha Singh, so the answer is Milkha Singh.
Source: Standard GK / Sports autobiography records

9. In which sport is the term "Love" used for zero score?
(a) Badminton (b) Tennis (c) Table Tennis (d) Squash
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Love" meaning zero is a scoring term of lawn tennis, believed to derive from the French "l'oeuf" (egg, symbolising zero). Badminton is tempting since it is also a racket sport, but the term belongs to tennis, so the answer is tennis.
Source: ITF official rules

10. The Thomas Cup is associated with which sport?
(a) Table Tennis (b) Badminton (c) Lawn Tennis (d) Golf
Answer: (b)
Solution: The Thomas Cup is the world men's team championship in badminton. Lawn tennis is tempting because its team event, the Davis Cup, sounds similar, but the Thomas Cup is for badminton, so the answer is badminton.
Source: BWF official website

11. Which Indian won the first individual Olympic gold medal for India?
(a) Rajyavardhan Singh Rathore (b) Vijender Singh (c) Abhinav Bindra (d) Sushil Kumar
Answer: (c)
Solution: Rajyavardhan Rathore is tempting as he won India's first individual Olympic silver in shooting (Athens 2004), but the first individual gold was won by Abhinav Bindra in 10m air rifle at Beijing 2008, so the answer is Abhinav Bindra.
Source: IOC official records

12. The Wimbledon Championship is played on which surface traditionally?
(a) Clay (b) Grass (c) Hard court (d) Carpet
Answer: (b)
Solution: Clay is tempting because the French Open is played on clay, but Wimbledon, the oldest tennis tournament, is played on grass courts, so the answer is grass.
Source: Wimbledon official website

13. How many players of one team are on the court in a volleyball match?
(a) Five (b) Six (c) Seven (d) Eight
Answer: (b)
Solution: A volleyball team has six players on the court at a time. Five is tempting as it is the number in basketball, but volleyball fields six players, so the answer is six.
Source: FIVB official rules

14. The Santosh Trophy is associated with which sport?
(a) Hockey (b) Football (c) Cricket (d) Kabaddi
Answer: (b)
Solution: The Santosh Trophy is India's premier inter-state football championship. Hockey is tempting because of trophies like the Rangaswamy Cup, but the Santosh Trophy is a football competition, so the answer is football.
Source: AIFF official website

15. In which year did India win its first Cricket World Cup (ODI)?
(a) 1975 (b) 1987 (c) 1983 (d) 2011
Answer: (c)
Solution: 2011 is tempting as India's most recent home World Cup win, and 1975 was the first World Cup ever (won by West Indies), but India's first title came under Kapil Dev in 1983, so the answer is 1983.
Source: ICC official records

16. The term "Checkmate" belongs to which game?
(a) Bridge (b) Polo (c) Billiards (d) Chess
Answer: (d)
Solution: "Checkmate" ends a game of chess when the king cannot escape attack; it comes from the Persian "shah mat" (the king is helpless). Bridge is tempting as another indoor mind game, but the term is from chess, so the answer is chess.
Source: FIDE official rules

17. Which country hosted the 2010 Commonwealth Games?
(a) Australia (b) England (c) India (d) Canada
Answer: (c)
Solution: The 2010 Commonwealth Games were held in New Delhi, India — the first time India hosted the event. Australia is tempting as a frequent CWG host, but the 2010 edition was in India, so the answer is India.
Source: Commonwealth Games Federation records

18. A marathon race covers approximately what distance?
(a) 26.2 km (b) 36.2 km (c) 42.2 km (d) 52.2 km
Answer: (c)
Solution: The standard marathon distance is 42.195 km (about 42.2 km), fixed at the 1908 London Olympics. 26.2 is tempting because it is the distance in miles, not kilometres, so the answer is 42.2 km.
Source: World Athletics official rules

19. The Dronacharya Award is given for excellence in which field?
(a) Sports coaching (b) Sports playing (c) Sports journalism (d) Sports administration
Answer: (a)
Solution: The Dronacharya Award honours outstanding coaches, named after the guru of the Pandavas and Kauravas. Sports playing is tempting because the Major Dhyan Chand Khel Ratna is for players, but Dronacharya is for coaching, so the answer is sports coaching.
Source: Ministry of Youth Affairs & Sports, Government of India

20. In hockey, how many players of one team are on the field?
(a) 9 (b) 10 (c) 12 (d) 11
Answer: (d)
Solution: A hockey team fields 11 players including the goalkeeper, the same as football. Ten is tempting because indoor hockey uses fewer players, but standard field hockey has eleven, so the answer is 11.
Source: FIH official rules

21. Consider the following statements about the Grand Slam tennis tournaments:
1. The Australian Open is played on hard courts.
2. The French Open is played on grass courts.
3. The US Open is played on hard courts.
Which of the statements given above is/are correct?
(a) 1 and 3 only (b) 1 and 2 only (c) 2 and 3 only (d) 1, 2 and 3
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is statement 2, which sounds plausible because Wimbledon is on grass, but the French Open (Roland Garros) is played on clay. The Australian Open and US Open are both hard-court events, so statements 1 and 3 are correct, and the answer is 1 and 3 only.
Source: Official websites of the four Grand Slam tournaments

22. Match the trophies with their sports:
1. Ranji Trophy — Cricket
2. Aga Khan Cup — Hockey
3. Davis Cup — Badminton
Which of the above pairs is/are correctly matched?
(a) 1 only (b) 2 and 3 only (c) 1 and 2 only (d) 1, 2 and 3
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is pair 3: the Davis Cup belongs to lawn tennis, not badminton — badminton's equivalent team event is the Thomas Cup, and the similar-sounding names cause confusion. The Ranji Trophy is India's domestic cricket championship and the Aga Khan Cup is a hockey tournament, so pairs 1 and 2 are correct, and the answer is 1 and 2 only.
Source: BCCI / Hockey India / ITF official records

23. Arrange the following events of Indian sport in chronological order:
1. India's first Olympic hockey gold as an independent nation
2. India's first Cricket World Cup victory
3. Abhinav Bindra's individual Olympic gold
(a) 2, 1, 3 (b) 1, 2, 3 (c) 1, 3, 2 (d) 3, 2, 1
Answer: (b)
Solution: The chronological trap lies in swapping the hockey gold and the cricket World Cup: independent India's first hockey gold came at London 1948, the cricket World Cup win was in 1983, and Bindra's gold was at Beijing 2008. Thus the correct order is 1948, 1983, 2008, so the answer is 1, 2, 3.
Source: IOC and ICC official records

24. With reference to the Major Dhyan Chand Khel Ratna Award, consider the following statements:
1. It is India's highest sporting honour for sportspersons.
2. It was earlier known as the Rajiv Gandhi Khel Ratna Award.
3. It is given only for Olympic sports.
Which of the statements given above is/are correct?
(a) 1 and 2 only (b) 2 and 3 only (c) 1 only (d) 1, 2 and 3
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is statement 3: the award is not restricted to Olympic sports — chess players like Viswanathan Anand (its first recipient in 1991–92) have received it. It is indeed the highest sporting honour and was renamed after hockey legend Dhyan Chand in 2021, so statements 1 and 2 are correct, and the answer is 1 and 2 only.
Source: Ministry of Youth Affairs & Sports, Government of India

25. Consider the following statements about the Olympic Games:
1. The five rings of the Olympic flag represent the five inhabited continents.
2. The Olympic motto is "Citius, Altius, Fortius".
3. The Winter Olympics and Summer Olympics are held in the same year.
Which of the statements given above is/are correct?
(a) 1 and 3 only (b) 1 and 2 only (c) 2 and 3 only (d) 1, 2 and 3
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is statement 3: since 1994 the Winter and Summer Olympics have alternated every two years and are no longer held in the same year. The rings do symbolise the five inhabited continents and the Latin motto means "Faster, Higher, Stronger" (with "Communiter" added in 2021), so statements 1 and 2 are correct, and the answer is 1 and 2 only.
Source: IOC official website
