Card 1
Front: Which country has won the most FIFA World Cups, and how many?
Back: Brazil — 5 titles (1958, 1962, 1970, 1994, 2002). Germany and Italy follow with 4 each.

Card 2
Front: The Olympic rings are five — but what do their colours actually represent?
Back: The five continents (Africa, Americas, Asia, Europe, Oceania). The colours (blue, yellow, black, green, red on white) were chosen because every national flag contains at least one of them — not one colour per continent.

Card 3
Front: Which is the oldest Grand Slam tennis tournament?
Back: Wimbledon (first held 1877). Order of age: Wimbledon → US Open (1881) → French Open (1891) → Australian Open (1905).

Card 4
Front: Memory trick: How do you recall the four tennis Grand Slams in calendar order?
Back: "AO-FR-WI-US" — remember "Australia Opens, France Rolls, Wimbledon Inspires, US Seals" — Australian Open (Jan), French Open (May–Jun), Wimbledon (Jun–Jul), US Open (Aug–Sep).

Card 5
Front: Who was the first Indian to win an individual Olympic gold medal?
Back: Abhinav Bindra — 10m Air Rifle shooting, Beijing 2008. (Trap: India's first Olympic golds were in hockey, but those were team events.)

Card 6
Front: Examiner's trap: Is the Thomas Cup associated with men's or women's badminton?
Back: Men's team badminton championship. The women's equivalent is the Uber Cup. Examiners swap these two to confuse.

Card 7
Front: Which trophy is awarded to the winner of the India–Australia Test cricket series?
Back: Border–Gavaskar Trophy, named after Allan Border (Australia) and Sunil Gavaskar (India).

Card 8
Front: How many players are there on each side in a hockey team on the field?
Back: 11 (including the goalkeeper). Same as football and cricket — a common confusion point with basketball (5) and volleyball (6).

Card 9
Front: Which country hosted the first modern Olympic Games, and in which year?
Back: Greece (Athens), 1896. The Winter Olympics began later, in 1924 at Chamonix, France.

Card 10
Front: Who is known as the "Flying Sikh" of India?
Back: Milkha Singh — sprinter who finished 4th in the 400m at the 1960 Rome Olympics.

Card 11
Front: Examiner's trap: Dronacharya Award vs Arjuna Award — which is for coaches?
Back: Dronacharya Award is for coaches; Arjuna Award is for sportspersons' performance. Examiners frequently interchange them.

Card 12
Front: Which is the highest sports honour of India, and what was it renamed in 2021?
Back: Major Dhyan Chand Khel Ratna Award — earlier called Rajiv Gandhi Khel Ratna, renamed in 2021 after hockey legend Dhyan Chand.

Card 13
Front: Memory trick: How to remember the number of players in team games?
Back: "5-6-7-9-11 ladder": Basketball 5, Volleyball 6, Kabaddi 7, Baseball 9, Cricket/Football/Hockey 11.

Card 14
Front: Which Indian woman won an Olympic medal in badminton at two consecutive Olympics?
Back: P.V. Sindhu — silver at Rio 2016 and bronze at Tokyo 2020. (Saina Nehwal won bronze at London 2012.)

Card 15
Front: The Durand Cup, one of Asia's oldest football tournaments, is played in which country?
Back: India — started in 1888 at Shimla, making it one of the oldest football tournaments in the world.

Card 16
Front: In which sport is the term "LBW" used, and what does it stand for?
Back: Cricket — "Leg Before Wicket," a mode of dismissal when the ball hits the batsman's leg in line with the stumps.

Card 17
Front: Which country won the first Cricket World Cup in 1975, and who were the runners-up?
Back: West Indies beat Australia at Lord's. West Indies also won in 1979 — the first two editions.

Card 18
Front: Neeraj Chopra's historic Olympic gold came in which event and at which Games?
Back: Javelin throw, Tokyo 2020 (held in 2021) — India's first Olympic medal in athletics, and a gold at that.

Card 19
Front: Examiner's trap: Is the Ryder Cup associated with golf or tennis?
Back: Golf — a biennial team contest between Europe and the USA. The tennis equivalent team event is the Davis Cup (men) / Billie Jean King Cup (women).

Card 20
Front: What is the distance of a full marathon, and why the odd figure?
Back: 42.195 km (26 miles 385 yards) — standardised from the 1908 London Olympics course, which started at Windsor Castle and finished in front of the royal viewing box at White City Stadium.
