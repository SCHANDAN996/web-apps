# Sports & Competitions

## The Hook: A Medal That Took 41 Years

In 1980, the Indian men's hockey team won its 8th Olympic gold in Moscow. Then came a drought so long that an entire generation of Indians grew up never seeing their country on an Olympic hockey podium. It took **41 years** — until Tokyo 2020 (held in 2021) — for the men's team to win bronze again. That single gap tells you everything about why examiners love sports GK: it is full of **dates, firsts, venues, and names** that are easy to ask and easy to get wrong.

This chapter turns that chaos into a system.

## Part 1: The Olympic Games — The Mother of All Events

### Ancient Roots

The Olympic Games began in **776 BCE at Olympia, Greece**, held in honour of Zeus. They were abolished in **393 CE** by Roman Emperor Theodosius I.

### Modern Revival

- Revived by **Pierre de Coubertin** (France), often called the "Father of the Modern Olympics."
- **First modern Olympics: Athens, 1896.**
- The **International Olympic Committee (IOC)** was founded in **1894**, headquartered at **Lausanne, Switzerland**.
- Olympic motto: **"Citius, Altius, Fortius"** (Faster, Higher, Stronger) — "Communiter" (Together) was added in 2021.
- Five rings (designed by Coubertin, 1913) represent the five inhabited continents; every national flag contains at least one of the six colours (five rings + white background).

### Summer vs Winter vs Youth

- Summer and Winter Olympics each occur every **4 years**, staggered 2 years apart since 1994.
- **First Winter Olympics: Chamonix, France, 1924.**
- **First Youth Olympics: Singapore, 2010.**

### India at the Olympics

- India's first participation: **Paris 1900** — **Norman Pritchard** won **two silver medals** in athletics (the first Olympic medals by an athlete representing India).
- First **team** participation: Antwerp 1920.
- Men's hockey golds: **1928, 1932, 1936, 1948, 1952, 1956, 1964, 1980** (8 golds — a record in Olympic hockey).
- **Abhinav Bindra** — first Indian to win an **individual Olympic gold** (10m Air Rifle, Beijing 2008).
- **Neeraj Chopra** — gold in **javelin throw, Tokyo 2020** — India's first Olympic medal in athletics since Norman Pritchard, and first athletics gold.

🧠 **Active Recall:** Who founded the IOC and in which year? How many Olympic hockey golds has India won, and in which years? Who was India's first individual Olympic gold medallist?

## Part 2: Asian Games & Commonwealth Games

### Asian Games (Asiad)

- First edition: **New Delhi, 1951** — India was the first host.
- Governed by the **Olympic Council of Asia (OCA)**, headquartered in Kuwait.
- Held every **4 years**.
- Motto: **"Ever Onward."**
- India hosted again in **1982 (New Delhi)** — the edition that introduced the mascot **Appu the elephant** and brought colour television to India.

### Commonwealth Games (CWG)

- First held as the **British Empire Games in 1930, Hamilton, Canada**.
- Renamed over time: British Empire and Commonwealth Games (1954), British Commonwealth Games (1970), Commonwealth Games (1978).
- Held every 4 years; open to nations of the Commonwealth.
- India hosted **CWG 2010 in New Delhi** — the first time India hosted the Games.

🧠 **Active Recall:** Which city hosted the first Asian Games? What was the original name of the Commonwealth Games, and where was it first held?

## Part 3: National Games & Indian Sports Structure

- **National Games of India**: first held as the **Indian Olympic Games in 1924 (Lahore)**; renamed National Games from the 1940 edition. Held under the Indian Olympic Association (IOA).
- **Indian Olympic Association (IOA)**: formed in **1927**; first president — **Sir Dorabji Tata**.
- **Sports Authority of India (SAI)**: established **1984**, under the Ministry of Youth Affairs and Sports.
- **Major Dhyan Chand Khel Ratna Award** — India's highest sporting honour (renamed from Rajiv Gandhi Khel Ratna in 2021).
- **Arjuna Award** (instituted 1961) — for outstanding achievement in sports.
- **Dronacharya Award** (1985) — for coaches.
- **National Sports Day: 29 August** — Major Dhyan Chand's birthday.

## Part 5: Playing Fields & Terminology

- Cricket — pitch; terms: LBW, googly, yorker, silly point.
- Hockey — field; terms: bully-off, penalty corner, scoop.
- Football — field/pitch; terms: offside, hat-trick, penalty kick.
- Badminton — court; terms: shuttle, smash, deuce.
- Tennis — court; terms: ace, deuce, love, Grand Slam (Australian Open, French Open, Wimbledon, US Open).
- Boxing — ring; terms: knockout, uppercut, weight categories.
- Chess — board; terms: checkmate, stalemate, gambit, Grandmaster.
- Golf — course; terms: birdie, eagle, bogey, par.
- Shooting — range; Archery — range/field; Swimming — pool.

**Grand Slam order in a calendar year:** Australian Open → French Open (Roland Garros) → Wimbledon → US Open.

## Mnemonics 🪄

1. **"A-F-W-U" for Tennis Slams** — "**A**ussie **F**rench **W**imbledon **U**S" — the calendar order of the four Grand Slams.
2. **"T-U-B" for Badminton team cups** — **T**homas (men), **U**ber (women), **B**adminton. "Thomas and Uber play Badminton."
3. **Hockey golds "28-32-36, 48-52-56, 64, 80"** — remember as three pairs (28-32-36), three pairs (48-52-56), then two singles (64, 80). Chant it like a phone number.

## ⚠️ Caution: Examiner's Trap

**Trap 1 — Chronological Confusion (कालानुक्रमिक भ्रम):** "The first Winter Olympics were held in 1896 at Chamonix." — Two true halves stitched into one false statement. 1896 = first **Summer** Olympics (Athens); Chamonix = **1924**. Examiners love swapping dates between sibling events.

**Trap 2 — Similar Names (समान नाम):** **Thomas Cup vs Davis Cup** — both are men's world team championships, but Thomas = **badminton**, Davis = **tennis**. Similarly, **Durand Cup (football)** vs **Duleep Trophy (cricket)** — both start with 'D' and are Indian domestic tournaments. Read the sport name twice before answering.

**Trap 3 — Almost-Correct Statement (लगभग सही कथन):** "Norman Pritchard was India's first individual Olympic gold medallist." — 90% plausible, but he won **silver** (two of them). The first individual **gold** was Abhinav Bindra (2008). One wrong word — gold vs silver — is the entire trap.
