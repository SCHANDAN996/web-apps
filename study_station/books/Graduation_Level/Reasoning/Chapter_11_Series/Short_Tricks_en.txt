# 🪄 Short Tricks & Mnemonics — Series

---

**Box 1 — The "DIFF First" Rule**
> **"Dumb Friends Inhale First"** → **D-I-F-F**: Always check **DIFFerences First** before anything else.
> Most number series crack open at the difference level. Never stare at the raw numbers for more than 5 seconds.

---

**Box 2 — The Difference Ladder**
> **"One floor missing? Climb again!"**
> If 1st differences look random, take **2nd differences**. Still random? 3rd. A constant at any level = polynomial series. Most exam series die at floor 2.

---

**Box 3 — Ratio vs. Difference Detector**
> **"Big jumps? MULTIPLY. Small steps? SUBTRACT."**
> If numbers grow slowly (5, 8, 12, 17) → think +/−.
> If numbers explode (3, 6, 24, 120) → think ×/÷ or factorial.

---

**Box 4 — The Alternating Twins**
> **"Odd-Even twins never mix!"**
> When differences go up-down-up-down, split the series into **odd-position** and **even-position** sub-series. Two innocent series hiding in one costume.

---

**Box 5 — Square & Cube Radar**
> **"Memorize 1–30 squares, 1–12 cubes — or stay slow."**
> Numbers like 26, 35, 48, 63, 99 scream "square ± 1" (25+1, 36−1, 49−1, 64−1, 100−1). If a number sits next to a perfect square/cube, the examiner built it that way on purpose.

---

**Box 6 — Letter Series = Number Series**
> **"A=1, B=2 … Z=26. Letters are just numbers wearing masks."**
> Convert letters to positions instantly. **"EJOTY"** — E(5), J(10), O(15), T(20), Y(25) — the five checkpoints that let you locate any letter in 2 seconds without counting on fingers.

---

**Box 7 — Reverse EJOTY Trick**
> **"From Z, count backwards: Z=26, and pairs sum to 27."**
> Opposite letters: A↔Z, B↔Y, C↔X … (sum = 27). Remember **"AZad BoY CoX DWar EVen FUll GT HS IR JQ KP LO MN"** — opposite-pair questions become instant.

---

**Box 8 — The ×2 ± k Family**
> **"Double trouble with a tip."**
> Series like 3, 7, 15, 31, 63 → each term = previous ×2 + 1. Pattern: **×2 +1, ×2 −1, ×2 +2…** When differences double each time (4, 8, 16, 32), suspect "×2 ± something" immediately.

---

**Box 9 — Prime & Composite Alarm**
> **"2, 3, 5, 7, 11… — the VIP queue."**
> If differences or terms are 2, 3, 5, 7, 11, 13 — it's primes. If 4, 6, 8, 9, 10 — composites. Examiners love hiding prime gaps inside innocent-looking series.

---

**Box 10 — The Wrong-Number Hunter**
> **"In 'find the wrong term', trust the majority."**
> Build the pattern from **3–4 terms that agree**, then evict the one rebel. Never start from the suspect — start from the crowd.

---

**Box 11 — Mixed Operation Sandwich**
> **"+2, ×3, +2, ×3… — the exam sandwich."**
> When one operation fails everywhere, test **two alternating operations**. Write them above the gaps: +, ×, +, ×. If it fits twice, it will fit thrice.

---

**Box 12 — Alphabet Gap Pattern**
> **"Count the skips, not the letters."**
> In A, D, H, M — gaps are +3, +4, +5. The gaps themselves form a series. Always write the gap numbers above the arrows; the real pattern lives there.

---

**Box 13 — The 15-Second Smell Test**
> **"If it smells like nothing in 15 seconds, it's a trap."**
> Genuine exam series reveal their skeleton fast. If differences, ratios, squares, and splitting ALL fail in 15 seconds, it's either a rare pattern or a misprint — mark and move.

---

**Box 14 — Last-Term Verification**
> **"A pattern that fits 4 terms but breaks the 5th is a lie."**
> Always verify your rule against **every given term**, especially the last one. Examiners plant "almost-patterns" that fit 4 terms and collapse on the 5th — that's the trap option.

---

**Box 15 — Fraction/Decimal Series**
> **"Decimals are integers in disguise — multiply by 10 or 100."**
> 0.5, 1.5, 4.5, 13.5 → ×10 → 5, 15, 45, 135 → clearly ×3. Strip the decimal, solve, restore.

---

## ⏭️ Skip Strategy — When to Walk Away

**Tip 1 — The 15/45 Rule:**
Give a series **15 seconds** to reveal its type (difference / ratio / square / split). If identified, you get **45 more seconds** to finish. If the type itself is invisible at 15 seconds — **skip immediately, mark for review**. In many exams such as SSC and Banking, a series question carries the same 1–2 marks as a quick coding or analogy question; don't donate 3 minutes to it.

**Tip 2 — Skip the "Double-Alternating + Wrong Term" Combo:**
Questions asking for the *wrong term* in a *long* series (7+ terms) with alternating patterns have the highest error-to-time ratio. If your first pattern attempt fails, don't try a second — skip. Return only if time remains after the paper.

**Tip 3 — Never Guess Between Two "Fitting" Patterns:**
If two different rules both produce a valid next term (common trap: difference-pattern vs. square-pattern giving different answers), the question is designed to punish guessers. Unless one rule fits **all** terms and the other doesn't, skip rather than gamble — negative marking makes a 50-50 guess a losing bet.
