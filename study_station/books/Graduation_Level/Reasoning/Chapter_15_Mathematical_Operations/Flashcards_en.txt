Card 1
Front: In Mathematical Operations questions, what is the very first thing you should do before solving?
Back: Decode the symbol substitutions and rewrite the entire expression in normal mathematical form. Never try to compute while the symbols are still coded.

Card 2
Front: Which rule decides the order of calculation after you decode the symbols?
Back: BODMAS — Brackets, Orders (powers/roots), Division, Multiplication, Addition, Subtraction. Division and Multiplication have equal priority (left to right), as do Addition and Subtraction.

Card 3
Front: What is the popular mnemonic to remember the order of operations?
Back: "BODMAS" — expand it as Bracket → Of/Orders → Division → Multiplication → Addition → Subtraction. Some remember it as "Big Dogs Make Awesome Snacks."

Card 4
Front: In "interchange of signs" questions, what shortcut saves the most time?
Back: Don't rewrite the whole expression mentally. Interchange only the two given signs, keep everything else identical, then apply BODMAS strictly.

Card 5
Front: If '+' means '×' and '×' means '+', what is 4 + 3 × 2?
Back: Decode: 4 × 3 + 2 = 12 + 2 = 14. Trap: solving the original expression first (getting 10 or 14 by wrong order) — always decode before computing.

Card 6
Front: Examiner's trap: In "which equation is correct after interchanging signs" questions, what mistake do most students make?
Back: They check only one or two options, or they apply BODMAS loosely (adding before dividing). The correct method: test every option with strict BODMAS until one balances exactly.

Card 7
Front: In "interchange of numbers" questions (e.g., interchange 3 and 5), what must you be careful about?
Back: Interchange the digits everywhere they appear in the expression — including inside two-digit numbers if the question says so. Read whether digits or numbers are being swapped.

Card 8
Front: What is the fastest way to solve "balance the equation by choosing correct signs" questions?
Back: Work backwards from the answer options: substitute each option's signs into the equation and check with BODMAS. Estimating the result's rough size first eliminates 1–2 options instantly.

Card 9
Front: Mnemonic trick: How do you remember that Division and Multiplication are equal-priority partners?
Back: Remember "DM are twins, AS are twins" — within each pair, solve left to right, whoever comes first wins. BODMAS order applies only between pairs.

Card 10
Front: In symbol-substitution questions, why should you write the decoded expression on paper instead of solving mentally?
Back: Because one missed substitution (e.g., forgetting that '−' also changed) silently corrupts the whole answer. Writing takes 5 seconds and prevents the most common error in this chapter.

Card 11
Front: If 'P' means '+', 'Q' means '−', 'R' means '×', 'S' means '÷', how do you evaluate 8 R 2 S 4 P 3?
Back: Decode: 8 × 2 ÷ 4 + 3. Apply BODMAS: 16 ÷ 4 = 4, then 4 + 3 = 7.

Card 12
Front: Examiner's trap: A question says "if '×' means '÷' and '÷' means '×'" — what subtle error do students commit?
Back: They swap only one direction (converting × to ÷ but leaving ÷ as ÷). Substitution is always two-way: every occurrence of both symbols must be exchanged.

Card 13
Front: In "correct the equation by interchanging two signs" questions, what is the systematic approach?
Back: Take each option's pair of signs, swap them in the equation, and check with BODMAS. Start with options swapping ÷ and × or + and −, as these most often fix imbalances.

Card 14
Front: What should you check first when an equation has brackets along with coded symbols?
Back: Decode symbols first, then solve the bracket contents completely before touching anything outside — brackets always outrank every operation.

Card 15
Front: In questions where letters like A, B, C, D replace operators, what is the safest solving habit?
Back: Make a small key table (A = +, B = −, …) at the top of your rough work, then translate the expression symbol by symbol against the key before calculating.

Card 16
Front: How do you handle "which of the following is correct" questions where each option is a full equation?
Back: Evaluate the LHS of each option independently with BODMAS and compare with the RHS. Stop the moment one matches — but verify the match is exact, not approximate.

Card 17
Front: What is the 15/45-second rule for this chapter?
Back: Pure symbol-substitution questions should take ~15–20 seconds (decode + compute). Sign-interchange or option-testing questions may take ~45 seconds; if an option-testing question exceeds 60 seconds, mark it and move on.

Card 18
Front: Why do examiners love putting '÷' in Mathematical Operations questions?
Back: Because division forces non-integer intermediate values when students apply the wrong order, producing tempting wrong options. Strict BODMAS (divide before add/subtract) is the only defence.

Card 19
Front: In "interchange two numbers to make the equation correct" questions, what quick filter rejects wrong options?
Back: Check the units digit (last digit) of both sides after the proposed interchange. If the last digits can't match, reject the option without full calculation.

Card 20
Front: What single habit eliminates most errors in this entire chapter?
Back: Two-step discipline: (1) fully decode/rewrite the expression, (2) compute strictly by BODMAS. Mixing the two steps — decoding while calculating — is the root cause of nearly all mistakes.
