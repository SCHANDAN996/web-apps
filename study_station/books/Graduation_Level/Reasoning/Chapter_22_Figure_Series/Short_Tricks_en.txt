# 🪄 Short Tricks & Mnemonics — Figure Series / Classification

---

**Box 1 — The "ROAM" Patrol**
> **R**otation → **O**bjects (count) → **A**dd/Delete → **M**irror/Move
> "When a figure series confuses you, send the ROAM patrol!" Check these four in order — most series questions crack within these four checks.

---

**Box 2 — "Clock Thief" Rule**
> "The thief steals time — 45°, 90°, 135°…"
> If an element rotates, immediately check: is the angle **constant** (45° each time) or **stealing more each step** (45→90→135)? Rotation questions are almost always one of these two.

---

**Box 3 — "Odd One Wears No Uniform"**
> In classification, line up the figures like soldiers. The odd one breaks the **uniform rule**: closed vs open, curved vs straight, even vs odd lines, symmetry vs no symmetry. Find the uniform first — the rebel reveals itself.

---

**Box 4 — "Count Before You Shout"**
> Never guess a series by "feel". Count: sides, dots, lines, arrows, shaded parts. Numbers don't lie; eyes do. If counts go 2→3→4→5, the answer must have 6 — even if it "looks wrong".

---

**Box 5 — "Mirror Mirror, Water Water"**
> Mirror image = **left-right flip** (like a page turned sideways).
> Water image = **top-bottom flip** (like a reflection in a lake).
> Mnemonic: *"Mirror stands beside you, water lies below you."*

---

**Box 6 — "The Shading Sandwich"**
> Shading often moves like a sandwich being eaten: corner → side → centre, or alternates black-white-black. Track the **shaded region alone** — ignore everything else for 5 seconds.

---

**Box 7 — "Two Trains, Two Tracks"**
> When two elements move in one figure, they run on **independent tracks**. Track the arrow separately from the dot. Never try to watch both at once — your brain will derail.

---

**Box 8 — "Position Ladder: 1-2-3-1-2-3"**
> An element hopping corners often climbs a ladder: moves 1 step, then 2, then 3… or cycles 1-2-3-1-2-3. Whisper the ladder aloud while solving — it locks the pattern in.

---

**Box 9 — "New Baby, Old One Dies"**
> In add/delete series: a **new element appears** each step while the **oldest vanishes** (FIFO — First In, First Out). Think of a queue at a ticket window: new person joins at the back, front person leaves.

---

**Box 10 — "Symmetry Is the Lazy Examiner's Trap"**
> If 3 of 4 figures have an axis of symmetry and one doesn't — that's your answer. Examiners love this because students check shapes, not symmetry. Check symmetry **second**, right after counting.

---

**Box 11 — "Inside-Outside Gossip"**
> When one element sits inside another, ask: does the inner shape become the outer shape next? ("The insider gets promoted!") This swap-pattern is a classic series trick.

---

**Box 12 — "The 15-Second Siren"**
> If after 15 seconds you can't name the rule in one sentence ("arrow rotates 90° clockwise"), the pattern is multi-layered. Mark it, move on, return later. Siren rules save marks.

---

**Box 13 — "Options Are Witnesses"**
> Stuck between two logics? Interrogate the **options**: eliminate any option that breaks the count rule, then the rotation rule. Usually only one witness survives both questions.

---

**Box 14 — "Same Family, Different Face"**
> In classification, figures made of the **same elements rearranged** belong together; the one with a **different element** (curve instead of line, 4 sides instead of 3) is the outsider. Family first, face second.

---

**Box 15 — "Alternate Means Alternate"**
> If figure 1→3→5 follows one rule and 2→4→6 another, it's an **alternating series**. Odd seats and even seats watch different movies! Split the series before you panic.

---

## ⏭️ Skip Strategy — When to Leave the Question

1. **The 15-Second Rule:** If you cannot state the governing rule in one line within 15 seconds, skip and mark for review. Multi-pattern figures (rotation + addition + shading together) are designed to eat 2–3 minutes — they cost more than they're worth in round one.

2. **Two-Rule Deadlock:** If two different logics both "fit" and lead to two different options, don't gamble immediately. Skip, finish the paper, and return — fresh eyes usually spot which rule the examiner intended (when in doubt, prefer the simpler rule).

3. **Heavy Embedded Figures:** If a classification question has 5+ overlapping elements per figure and you must trace lines to compare, skip it in the first pass. Attempt only if time remains — these are high-effort, low-reward traps, and a wrong guess also costs negative marks.
