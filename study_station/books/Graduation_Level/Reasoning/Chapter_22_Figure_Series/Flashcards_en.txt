Card 1
Front: What is the very first thing you should check when a figure series question appears on screen?
Back: The number of elements (lines, dots, shapes) in each figure. Counting elements first instantly reveals arithmetic patterns (+1, −1, ×2) and eliminates half the options in seconds.

Card 2
Front: In a series, a dot moves 1 step, then 2 steps, then 3 steps clockwise. What comes next?
Back: 4 steps clockwise. Movement in figure series often follows an increasing arithmetic progression (1, 2, 3, 4…), not a constant step — always check whether the step size itself is growing.

Card 3
Front: What is the "45-90-135" rotation pattern, and how do you spot it?
Back: An element rotates by 45° in the first step, 90° in the second, 135° in the third (adding 45° each time). Spot it by tracking one marked corner/arrow and measuring the angle change between consecutive figures.

Card 4
Front: A figure has an arrow rotating 90° clockwise every step, but in one option it rotates 90° anti-clockwise with everything else identical. What trap is this?
Back: The "direction reversal" trap — examiners place a mirror-rotation option that matches in every detail except rotation direction. Always fix the direction (clockwise/anti-clockwise) before checking magnitude.

Card 5
Front: What mnemonic helps you remember the full checklist for analysing any figure series?
Back: "N-R-M-A-D" — Number of elements, Rotation, Movement (position), Addition/Deletion of elements, Design change (shading, shape swap). Run through NRMAD before picking an answer.

Card 6
Front: In classification (odd-one-out) questions, four figures look similar. What is the safest strategy to find the odd one?
Back: Test one property at a time across ALL figures (rotation, number of sides, enclosed regions, line orientation). The odd figure is the one that fails a property the other three share — never decide on a property that splits them 2-2.

Card 7
Front: Three figures can be obtained from each other by rotation, but one cannot. Is that one the odd figure?
Back: Yes — this is the classic "rotation vs reflection" trap. Figures related by rotation are the same shape; a figure related only by mirror reflection is the odd one. Check with your hand: rotate the page, don't flip it mentally.

Card 8
Front: What does the mnemonic "CLOSE" stand for when checking classification figures?
Back: Count (elements/sides), Lines (parallel/perpendicular), Orientation (rotation/reflection), Shading (black/white/hatched), Enclosed regions. CLOSE the case by testing each property on all four figures.

Card 9
Front: In a series, the number of sides of the main figure goes 3, 4, 5, 6. What is the next figure, and what trap should you avoid?
Back: A 7-sided figure (heptagon). Trap: options often include a hexagon repeated or a circle (treated as "infinite sides") — the pattern is +1 side, so only the heptagon fits.

Card 10
Front: A small circle alternates between inside and outside the main square: in, out, in, out. What is the rule called and what comes next?
Back: Alternation (ABAB pattern) of position. Next: the circle goes inside. Alternation can apply to position, shading, or shape — check all three when you see a two-state flip.

Card 11
Front: In a series, one element is added at each step, but the oldest element is also removed. What is this pattern called?
Back: "Add-and-drop" (or shift) pattern — total count stays constant while elements cycle through. Track each element's birth and death across frames instead of just counting totals.

Card 12
Front: Why is "the figure looks different" a dangerous reason in classification questions?
Back: Because visual difference is subjective; examiners design figures where the odd one differs by a precise rule (e.g., the only figure where the dot is on the left of the line). Always name the exact rule — if you can't state it in words, you haven't found it.

Card 13
Front: In a 5-figure series, figures 1, 3, 5 follow one rule and figures 2, 4 follow another. What pattern is this?
Back: Interleaved (alternate) series — two independent sub-series woven together. Solve odd positions and even positions separately; the answer continues the sub-series of the last figure's position.

Card 14
Front: An element moves clockwise along the corners of a square: top-left, top-right, bottom-right… Where does it go next, and what is the common wrong answer?
Back: Bottom-left (continuing clockwise). Common wrong answer: top-left (students assume it "restarts") — the element only restarts after completing the full cycle, not mid-path.

Card 15
Front: What is the "shading progression" pattern and how many states can it have?
Back: Shading cycles through states like white → hatched → black → white… It can have 2, 3, or more states. Count the states in the given figures first; then the next figure's shading is simply the next state in the cycle.

Card 16
Front: Examiner's trap: in a series, two properties change simultaneously (rotation + element count). An option gets the rotation right but the count wrong. How do you avoid picking it?
Back: Verify ALL changing properties before finalising. Make a mini-table: for each frame, note count, rotation, position. Reject any option that fails even one property — a "half-right" figure is the most common trap option.

Card 17
Front: In classification, all four figures contain a triangle, a circle, and a square. What should you check next to find the odd one?
Back: The relative arrangement: which shape is inside which, which touches which, and the order (clockwise/anti-clockwise) of the shapes. When elements are identical, the odd-one-out rule always hides in arrangement or orientation.

Card 18
Front: What is a "mirror image pair" trap in figure classification?
Back: Three figures are rotations of one original; the fourth is its mirror image. Since mirror images cannot be obtained by rotation alone, the mirrored figure is the odd one. Quick test: fix one feature, then check if the sequence of remaining features runs clockwise in all — the mirror runs anti-clockwise.

Card 19
Front: How much time should you spend on one figure series question before skipping, per the time rule?
Back: About 30–45 seconds. If no pattern emerges after checking Number, Rotation, and Movement (the N-R-M of NRMAD), mark it and move on — return only if time remains. Figure questions are pattern-instant: you either see it fast or not at all.

Card 20
Front: A series shows the main figure rotating 90° clockwise while an inner symbol rotates 90° anti-clockwise. What is this called?
Back: Dual (opposing) rotation — two elements rotate independently in opposite directions. Solve each element's path separately and combine; options typically trap you by rotating both in the same direction.
