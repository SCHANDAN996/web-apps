51. In a figure series, a dot moves one corner clockwise in each step, while a small circle moves two corners anticlockwise in each step. If the dot is at the top-left corner and the circle is at the top-right corner in step 1, where will both be in step 5?
(a) Dot at top-left, circle at top-right (b) Dot at top-right, circle at bottom-right (c) Dot at bottom-right, circle at bottom-left (d) Dot at bottom-left, circle at top-left
Answer: (a)
Solution: The tempting wrong answer assumes both elements end at different corners. The dot moves 1 corner clockwise per step, so after 4 steps it completes a full cycle and returns to top-left. The circle moves 2 corners anticlockwise per step, so after 4 steps it has moved 8 corners — two full cycles — and returns to top-right. Both are back at their starting corners, so the answer is dot at top-left, circle at top-right.

52. A series shows a square containing a triangle. In each successive figure, the triangle rotates 45° clockwise and alternates between shaded and unshaded. If figure 1 has an unshaded triangle pointing up, what does figure 5 show?
(a) Shaded triangle pointing down (b) Unshaded triangle pointing up (c) Unshaded triangle pointing down (d) Shaded triangle pointing up
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is to track only the rotation and forget the shading alternation. After 4 rotations of 45°, the triangle has turned 180° and points down. The shading alternates each step — unshaded, shaded, unshaded, shaded, unshaded — so figure 5 is unshaded. The answer is unshaded triangle pointing down.

54. A figure series shows arrows: up, right, down, left, up, … What is the 10th figure?
(a) Left-pointing arrow (b) Up-pointing arrow (c) Right-pointing arrow (d) Down-pointing arrow
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is to count 10 rotation steps from the first figure instead of using the cycle. The pattern repeats every 4 figures: positions 1, 5, 9 point up and positions 2, 6, 10 point right. Since 10 = 4 × 2 + 2, the 10th figure matches position 2, so the answer is the right-pointing arrow.

(a) Heptagon with 3 dots (b) Hexagon with 2 dots (c) Heptagon with 2 dots (d) Octagon with 2 dots

Solution: The tempting wrong answer keeps the hexagon or forgets to reduce the dots. The outer figure goes 3, 4, 5, 6 sides, so next is 7 sides (heptagon). The dots go 6, 5, 4, 3, so next is 2 dots. Both patterns apply together, so the answer is heptagon with 2 dots.
