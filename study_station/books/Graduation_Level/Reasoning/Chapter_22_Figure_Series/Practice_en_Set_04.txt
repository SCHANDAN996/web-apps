76. In a figure series, a dot moves one corner clockwise in each step, while a cross moves two corners anticlockwise in each step. If the dot is at the top-left and the cross is at the top-right in the first figure, where will both be in the fourth figure?
(a) Dot at bottom-left, cross at bottom-left (b) Dot at bottom-right, cross at top-left (c) Dot at bottom-right, cross at bottom-left (d) Dot at top-right, cross at bottom-right
Answer: (a)
Solution: The tempting wrong choice tracks only the dot correctly but moves the cross just one corner anticlockwise per step. The dot moves one corner clockwise: top-left → top-right → bottom-right → bottom-left, so in figure 4 it is at the bottom-left. The cross moves two corners anticlockwise: top-right → bottom-left → top-right → bottom-left, so in figure 4 it is also at the bottom-left. The answer is dot at bottom-left, cross at bottom-left.
Source: PYQ-style

77. A series shows a triangle, square, pentagon, hexagon, and so on, each figure having one more side than the previous. Which figure comes next after the hexagon?
(a) A heptagon (7-sided figure) (b) A circle (c) An octagon inside a hexagon (d) A star
Answer: (a)
Solution: The tempting wrong choice is the circle, which students pick thinking the sides "merge into a curve", but the rule is simply +1 side per step: 3, 4, 5, 6, so the next figure has 7 sides. A heptagon is the 7-sided polygon, so the answer is a heptagon.
Source: PYQ-style

78. In a series of squares, the number of small circles inside increases as 1, 2, 4, 8, … How many circles will the next square contain?
(a) 16 (b) 12 (c) 14 (d) 10
Answer: (a)
Solution: The tempting wrong choice is 12, obtained by adding 4 to 8 as if the increase were constant. In fact the count doubles each time (×2): 1, 2, 4, 8, so the next term is 16. The answer is 16 circles.
Source: PYQ-style

79. A figure rotates 45° clockwise in each step. After how many steps will it return to its original orientation?
(a) 4 (b) 8 (c) 6 (d) 12
Answer: (b)
Solution: The tempting wrong choice is 4, which assumes a 90° rotation. A full rotation is 360°, and 360 ÷ 45 = 8, so the figure returns to its original orientation after 8 steps. The answer is 8.
Source: PYQ-style

80. In a series, an arrow alternately points up, right, down, left, up, right, … Which direction will it point in the 10th figure?
(a) Up (b) Left (c) Down (d) Right
Answer: (d)
Solution: The tempting wrong choice is down, obtained by wrongly dividing 10 by 4 and taking the quotient instead of the remainder. The pattern repeats every 4 figures; 10 = 4 × 2 + 2, so the 10th figure matches the 2nd position, which is right. The answer is right.
Source: PYQ-style

81. Five figures are given. Four of them have a line of symmetry; one does not. Which is the odd one out?
(a) An equilateral triangle with a median drawn (b) A parallelogram with one diagonal drawn (c) A square with both diagonals drawn (d) A circle with a diameter drawn
Answer: (b)
Solution: The tempting wrong choice is the square, because its two diagonals look "busier", but a square has four lines of symmetry. A general parallelogram has no line of symmetry at all, and drawing one diagonal does not create one. The answer is the parallelogram with one diagonal.
Source: NCERT Class 7 Mathematics

82. In a classification question, four figures show a shape divided into parts equal to the number of its sides (triangle into 3, square into 4, pentagon into 5, hexagon into 6). One figure shows a square divided into 3 parts. Which is the odd one?
(a) Pentagon divided into 5 parts (b) Triangle divided into 3 parts (c) Square divided into 3 parts (d) Hexagon divided into 6 parts
Answer: (c)
Solution: The tempting wrong choice is the triangle, picked because 3 is the smallest number, but it follows the rule perfectly. The rule is "parts equal to sides"; the square divided into 3 parts breaks it since a square has 4 sides. The answer is the square divided into 3 parts.
Source: PYQ-style

83. A series shows a black sector of a circle growing by 90° clockwise each step: 90°, 180°, 270°, … What will the fifth figure show?
(a) A blank circle (b) A fully black circle (c) A half-black circle (d) A 90° black sector at the original position
Answer: (d)
Solution: The tempting wrong choice is the fully black circle, obtained by adding 90° to 270° to get 360° for the fourth figure and assuming the growth continues. After 360° (full circle) the cycle restarts, so the fifth figure again shows a 90° sector. The answer is a 90° black sector at the original position.
Source: PYQ-style

84. In a series, two elements swap positions in each step: the element at the top moves to the bottom and vice versa, while a third element stays fixed in the centre. If figure 1 has a star on top and a moon at the bottom, what does figure 3 show?
(a) Moon on top, star at bottom (b) Moon on top, star in the centre (c) Star on top, moon at bottom (d) Star and moon both in the centre
Answer: (c)
Solution: The tempting wrong choice is moon on top, which is figure 2, not figure 3. Each step swaps the two elements, so odd-numbered figures repeat figure 1: figure 3 has the star on top and the moon at the bottom. The answer is star on top, moon at bottom.
Source: PYQ-style

85. A figure series shows the number of dots on the perimeter of a square increasing by 2 each step: 4, 6, 8, 10, … How many dots will the sixth figure have?
(a) 14 (b) 12 (c) 16 (d) 18
Answer: (a)
Solution: The tempting wrong choice is 12, obtained by adding 2 only once to 10. The sequence is 4, 6, 8, 10, 12, 14, so the sixth term is 14. The answer is 14 dots.
Source: PYQ-style

86. Four figures show a letter-like shape rotating 90° anticlockwise each step. If the first figure shows an "L" upright, what will the fourth figure look like?
(a) L rotated 90° anticlockwise (b) L rotated 270° anticlockwise (c) L rotated 180° (d) L upright again
Answer: (b)
Solution: The tempting wrong choice is 180°, which is the rotation after only two steps. After three steps of 90° anticlockwise, the total rotation is 270° anticlockwise, so the fourth figure shows L rotated 270° anticlockwise. The answer is L rotated 270° anticlockwise.
Source: PYQ-style

87. In a classification set, four figures contain exactly two identical smaller shapes inside a larger shape, while one contains two different shapes inside. Which is the odd one out?
(a) A square containing two small squares (b) A triangle containing a small circle and a small square (c) A circle containing two small circles (d) A rectangle containing two small rectangles
Answer: (b)
Solution: The tempting wrong choice is the circle, picked because circles have no sides, but the rule concerns the inner shapes being identical, not the outer shape. Only the triangle holds two different inner shapes. The answer is the triangle containing a small circle and a small square.
Source: PYQ-style

88. A series alternates shading: the first figure is unshaded, the second half-shaded, the third fully shaded, the fourth half-shaded, the fifth unshaded. What is the sixth figure?
(a) Half-shaded (b) Fully shaded (c) Unshaded (d) Quarter-shaded
Answer: (a)
Solution: The tempting wrong choice is fully shaded, obtained by continuing the increase without noticing the reversal. The shading rises then falls symmetrically: none, half, full, half, none, so the next step rises again to half. The answer is half-shaded.
Source: PYQ-style

89. In a series, a small square moves along the sides of a bigger square: top side, right side, bottom side, left side, top side, … On which side will it be in the 9th figure?
(a) Right side (b) Bottom side (c) Top side (d) Left side
Answer: (c)
Solution: The tempting wrong choice is the left side, obtained by taking 9 ÷ 4 and misreading the remainder. The cycle length is 4, and 9 = 4 × 2 + 1, so the 9th figure matches position 1, the top side. The answer is the top side.
Source: PYQ-style

90. Four figures are given: a hexagon with 6 dots, a pentagon with 5 dots, a square with 4 dots, and a triangle with 4 dots. Which is the odd one out?
(a) Hexagon with 6 dots (b) Pentagon with 5 dots (c) Square with 4 dots (d) Triangle with 4 dots
Answer: (d)
Solution: The tempting wrong choice is the square, because 4 dots appear twice and students assume the duplicate must be wrong in the outer shape. The rule is "dots equal to sides"; a triangle has 3 sides but carries 4 dots, breaking the rule. The answer is the triangle with 4 dots.
Source: PYQ-style

91. A figure series shows a line segment inside a circle rotating 30° clockwise each step. Starting from the vertical position, what angle will the line make with the vertical in the 7th figure?
(a) 180° (b) 150° (c) 120° (d) 90°
Answer: (a)
Solution: The tempting wrong choice is 150°, which is the rotation after five steps, not six. From figure 1 to figure 7 there are six moves of 30°, giving 180°, so the line is again vertical but inverted, i.e. 180° from the start. The answer is 180°.
Source: PYQ-style

92. In a series, each figure adds one more arrow than the previous, and the arrows alternate direction: figure 1 has one right arrow, figure 2 has two left arrows, figure 3 has three right arrows. What does figure 5 show?
(a) Five left arrows (b) Four right arrows (c) Five right arrows (d) Six left arrows
Answer: (c)
Solution: The tempting wrong choice is five left arrows, picked by assuming the direction simply flips with count. Odd-numbered figures carry right arrows and even-numbered figures carry left arrows, and the count equals the figure number, so figure 5 has five right arrows. The answer is five right arrows.
Source: PYQ-style

93. A classification question shows five figures. In four, the inner shape has one side fewer than the outer shape; in one, the inner shape has the same number of sides as the outer. Which is the odd one out?
(a) Square outside, triangle inside (b) Square outside, square inside (c) Hexagon outside, pentagon inside (d) Pentagon outside, square inside
Answer: (b)
Solution: The tempting wrong choice is the square-triangle pair, because the difference of one side is least obvious there, but it obeys the rule. The square-within-square has equal sides inside and out, breaking the "one side fewer" rule. The answer is the square outside with a square inside.
Source: PYQ-style

94. In a series, a shaded triangle occupies positions at the corners of an imaginary square, moving clockwise one corner per step, and alternates between pointing up and pointing down. If figure 1 has an upward triangle at the top-left, what is figure 5?
(a) Downward triangle at top-left (b) Upward triangle at top-left (c) Upward triangle at bottom-right (d) Downward triangle at bottom-right
Answer: (b)
Solution: The tempting wrong choice is the downward triangle at top-left, which ignores that the orientation alternates with each step. After four moves the position returns to top-left, and four alternations return the orientation to upward, so figure 5 matches figure 1. The answer is the upward triangle at top-left.
Source: PYQ-style

95. A series shows figures with 2, 3, 5, 8, 13 line segments. How many segments will the next figure have?
(a) 21 (b) 20 (c) 18 (d) 24
Answer: (a)
Solution: The tempting wrong choice is 18, obtained by adding 5 (the last difference) to 13. The sequence is Fibonacci-type: each term is the sum of the previous two, so 8 + 13 = 21. The answer is 21 segments.
Source: PYQ-style

96. Four figures show a clock-like dial with a single hand pointing at 12, 3, 6, and 9 respectively. What comes next?
(a) Hand pointing at 10 (b) Hand pointing at 3 (c) Hand pointing at 6 (d) Hand pointing at 12
Answer: (d)
Solution: The tempting wrong choice is 10, picked by students who treat the positions as numbers increasing by 3 and overshoot. The hand moves a quarter-turn clockwise each step: 12 → 3 → 6 → 9 → 12, completing the cycle. The answer is the hand pointing at 12.
Source: PYQ-style

97. In a classification set, four figures can be drawn in one stroke without lifting the pen or retracing, and one cannot. Which is the odd one out?
(a) A square (b) A triangle with a line from one vertex to the midpoint of the opposite side (c) A circle (d) A rectangle with both diagonals
Answer: (d)
Solution: The tempting wrong choice is the triangle with the extra line, which looks complex but has exactly two odd vertices and can be drawn in one stroke. A rectangle with both diagonals has four odd vertices (each corner has degree 3), so it cannot be drawn in one stroke. The answer is the rectangle with both diagonals.
Source: PYQ-style

98. A series shows a large square divided into 1, 4, 9, and 16 equal small squares respectively. How many small squares will the next figure contain?
(a) 20 (b) 24 (c) 25 (d) 36
Answer: (c)
Solution: The tempting wrong choice is 20, obtained by treating the increase as a simple addition of 4 each time. The counts are perfect squares: 1², 2², 3², 4², so the next figure has 5² = 25 small squares. The answer is 25.
Source: NCERT Class 8 Mathematics

99. In a series, the number of horizontal lines increases by one each step (1, 2, 3, …) while the number of vertical lines decreases by one each step (4, 3, 2, …). What does the fifth figure contain?
(a) 5 horizontal and 1 vertical line (b) 4 horizontal and 1 vertical line (c) 5 horizontal and 0 vertical lines (d) 6 horizontal and 0 vertical lines
Answer: (c)
Solution: The tempting wrong choice is 5 horizontal and 1 vertical, obtained by decreasing the vertical count only three times instead of four. Horizontal lines: 1, 2, 3, 4, 5; vertical lines: 4, 3, 2, 1, 0. The answer is 5 horizontal and 0 vertical lines.
Source: PYQ-style

100. Four figures show a star with 5, 6, 7, and 8 points respectively, each star also containing as many dots as its points. One figure shows a 6-pointed star with 5 dots. Which is the odd one out?
(a) 5-pointed star with 5 dots (b) 8-pointed star with 8 dots (c) 7-pointed star with 7 dots (d) 6-pointed star with 5 dots
Answer: (d)
Solution: The tempting wrong choice is the 5-pointed star, picked because it is the smallest, but it follows the rule exactly. The rule is "dots equal to points"; the 6-pointed star with only 5 dots violates it. The answer is the 6-pointed star with 5 dots.
Source: PYQ-style
