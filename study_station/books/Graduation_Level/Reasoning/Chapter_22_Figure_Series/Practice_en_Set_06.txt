126. In a figure series, a square contains a dot and a cross. The dot moves one corner clockwise each step, while the cross moves two corners anticlockwise each step. If in the first figure the dot is at the top-left and the cross is at the top-right, where will both be in the fifth figure?
(a) Dot at top-left, cross at top-right (b) Dot at bottom-left, cross at bottom-right (c) Dot at top-right, cross at bottom-left (d) Dot at top-left, cross at bottom-left
Answer: (a)
Solution: The tempting trap is to track only one element and assume the other stays put. The dot moves 1 corner clockwise per step: after 4 moves (figure 5) it returns to top-left. The cross moves 2 corners anticlockwise per step: after 4 moves it has moved 8 corners = 2 full rounds, so it is back at top-right. So the answer is dot at top-left, cross at top-right.

127. A series shows triangles with 1, 2, 4, 7, 11 inner line segments respectively. How many inner segments will the next figure have?
(a) 14 (b) 15 (c) 17 (d) 16
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is to assume doubling (1, 2, 4, 8…) and pick a value near 15. Actually the differences are +1, +2, +3, +4, so the next difference is +5, giving 11 + 5 = 16. So the answer is 16.

Solution: The trap is to classify by orientation (vertical/horizontal/diagonal), which is a misleading surface feature. Orientation is irrelevant; the real property is whether the line is a diameter. 129. A figure series rotates an arrow 45° clockwise in step 1, 90° in step 2, 135° in step 3. If the arrow initially points up, where does it point after step 4 (rotation 180°)?
(a) Down (b) Left (c) Up (d) Right
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is to assume a constant 45° rotation each step, which would wrongly suggest down. The rotations grow by 45° each time: +45°, +90°, +135°, +180°. Cumulative positions measured clockwise from up are 45°, 135°, 270°, and then 270° + 180° = 450° ≡ 90°, which points right. So the answer is right.

142. In a classification set, three figures can be drawn in one stroke without lifting the pen, and one cannot. Which is the odd one?
(a) A square with both diagonals (b) A pentagon (c) A rectangle (d) A triangle
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is to classify by number of sides. The real property is one-stroke drawability: a figure can be drawn in one stroke only if it has 0 or 2 odd-degree vertices. A square with both diagonals has 4 odd-degree vertices, so it cannot be drawn in one stroke; the plain polygons can. So the answer is a square with both diagonals.

150. A complex series combines three rules: the outer figure rotates 90° clockwise each step; an inner dot moves one position anticlockwise each step; and shading toggles on/off every step. Figure 1: unshaded square (flat top), dot at top. What is figure 3?
(a) Shaded square rotated 180°, dot at bottom (b) Shaded square rotated 90°, dot at left (c) Unshaded square rotated 90°, dot at right (d) Unshaded square rotated 180°, dot at bottom
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is to apply only one or two of the three rules — a classic triple-relationship question. Rotation: after 2 steps the square has turned 180°. Dot: 2 anticlockwise moves from top → left → bottom. Shading toggles each step: figure 1 unshaded, figure 2 shaded, figure 3 unshaded. All three rules together fix the choice, so the answer is unshaded square rotated 180°, dot at bottom.
