# 🪄 Short Tricks & Mnemonics — Analogy

---

**Box 1 — The "BRIDGE" Rule**
Before solving, build a **B.R.I.D.G.E.** between the first pair:
**B**uild a sentence → **R**elate the two words → **I**dentify the exact relation → **D**o the same to the answer pair → **G**rab the match → **E**xit.
*"No bridge, no answer — never jump straight to options!"*

---

**Box 2 — "Doctor : Patient" Sentence Trick**
Always make a **sentence**, not just a feeling.
Doctor *treats* Patient → so Teacher *teaches* Student.
If your sentence doesn't fit the option word-for-word, reject it — even if it "feels close."

---

**Box 3 — The "PART-WHOLE" Song**
*"Is it a piece, or the whole pizza?"*
Wheel : Car (part : whole) vs. Car : Wheel (whole : part).
**Order matters!** Flip the order and the answer flips too. Sing it before you mark it.

---

**Box 4 — "TOOL-ACTION" Hammer Trick**
Ask: *"What does it DO?"*
Knife : Cut | Pen : Write | Needle : Stitch.
If the option is Knife : Sharp — that's a **property**, not an action. Wrong drawer, wrong answer!

---

**Box 5 — The "OPPOSITE LADDER" for Antonym Analogies**
Hot : Cold :: Day : ?
Climb down the ladder of meaning — don't slide sideways.
Hot : Cold is **exact opposite**. So pick Night, not "Evening" (that's a neighbour, not an opposite).
*"Neighbours are traps; opposites are answers."*

---

**Box 6 — "DEGREE" Thermometer Trick**
Warm : Hot :: Cool : ?
The relation is **intensity** — turn up the heat by the same amount!
Cool → Cold (same jump as Warm → Hot).
Don't jump to "Freezing" — that's overheating the thermometer.

---

**Box 7 — "ANIMAL HOUSE" Mnemonic**
For animal-based analogies, remember **H.S.Y.S.**:
**H**abitat (Lion : Den), **S**ound (Dog : Bark), **Y**oung one (Cow : Calf), **S**tructure/group (Fish : Shoal).
Four cages — find which cage the question belongs to first!

---

**Box 8 — The "COUNTRY-CAPITAL-CURRENCY" Triangle**
Three corners of the same triangle:
Country ↔ Capital | Country ↔ Currency | Capital ↔ Currency.
India : New Delhi : Rupee.
**Trap alert:** Japan : Yen is country : currency; Japan : Tokyo is country : capital. Read which side of the triangle is asked!

---

**Box 9 — "WORKER-WORKPLACE" Office Rule**
Chef : Kitchen :: Surgeon : ?
Ask: *"Where does he WORK?"* → Operation Theatre.
Not "Hospital" (too broad — the whole building), not "Patient" (that's the object, not the place).
**Precision beats proximity.**

---

**Box 10 — The "GRAMMAR LOCK" Trick**
The answer pair must match the **part of speech** of the question pair.
If the pair is Noun : Verb (Bird : Fly), reject Noun : Noun options instantly.
*"Lock the grammar first, then check the meaning."*

---

**Box 11 — "MIRROR ORDER" Warning**
If A : B is Cause : Effect, the answer must also be Cause : Effect — never Effect : Cause.
Disease : Pain :: Exercise : Fitness ✔
Disease : Pain :: Fitness : Exercise ✘ (mirror-flipped trap!)
*"Check the direction of the arrow before you shoot."*

---

**Box 12 — The "ELIMINATE 3" Blitz**
In tough analogies, don't hunt the right answer — **kill the wrong ones**:
1. Kill wrong relation type.
2. Kill wrong order.
3. Kill wrong grammar.
Whatever survives is your answer. Three kills, one survivor!

---

**Box 13 — "KING-KINGDOM" Memory Palace**
For classification analogies (Thing : Category):
Apple : Fruit :: Spinach : Vegetable.
Remember the palace rule: *"Every king belongs to exactly ONE kingdom."*
If a word fits two kingdoms (Tomato : Fruit/Vegetable), the examiner is baiting you — check which kingdom the question pair used.

---

**Box 14 — The "15-Second Sentence" Sprint**
Read pair → make sentence → scan options with the same sentence.
Total budget: **15 seconds**. If your sentence fits two options, refine the sentence (add one more detail: *"A doctor treats a patient in a clinic"*). Refinement breaks the tie.

---

## ⏭️ Skip Strategy — When to Leave the Question

1. **The 15-Second Rule:** If you cannot form a clear connecting sentence within 15 seconds, mark it and move on. Analogy questions are designed to be quick — a blank mind after 15 seconds means the relation is obscure, and extra staring rarely helps.

2. **The Double-Fit Trap:** If two options both seem to fit even after refining your sentence once, skip it. This usually signals an ambiguous or multi-relation question — the risk of a negative mark outweighs the gain. Return only if time remains at the end.

3. **The Unknown-Word Alarm:** If either word of the main pair (or two or more option words) is unfamiliar vocabulary, skip immediately. You cannot build a bridge with unknown bricks — guessing here is pure coin-flip, and coin-flips lose marks in negative-marking exams.
