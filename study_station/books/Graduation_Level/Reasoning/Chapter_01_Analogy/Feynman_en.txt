# 🧒 The Feynman Way — Analogy Explained Like You're 12

## The Hardest Idea in Analogy (Made Simple)

Most students think Analogy is about *words*. It isn't. Analogy is about **relationships**. The words are just costumes; the relationship is the actor inside.

**A : B :: C : ?** really means:

> "Whatever B is to A, find the word that is the *same thing* to C."

The trap? There is almost always more than one possible relationship, and the examiner puts a "looks-right" option that matches a *weaker* relationship. Your job is to find the **strongest, most specific** relationship — the one that works in exactly one direction, with no exceptions.

---

## A Story: The Shoe Shop

Imagine 12-year-old Meera goes to a shoe shop with her father.

Father says: **"Foot : Shoe :: Hand : ?"**

Meera shouts, "Ring!" — because a ring goes on the hand, just like a shoe goes on the foot.

The shopkeeper smiles. "But a ring goes on a *finger*, not the whole hand. A shoe covers the *whole foot*. What covers the whole hand?"

"**Glove!**" says Meera.

That is the entire secret of Analogy. "Ring" matched a *loose* relationship (worn on the body part). "Glove" matched the *exact* relationship (a covering worn over the whole body part). In the exam, "Ring" will be sitting there as option (b), smiling at you. Don't fall for it.

## The Unforgettable Trick: **"Make a Sentence, Then Swap-Test It"**

**Step 1 — Sentence:** Turn A : B into one precise sentence.
*Example:* Doctor : Hospital → "A doctor **works in** a hospital."

**Step 2 — Swap-test:** Read the sentence backwards. Does it still make sense?
"A hospital works in a doctor" — nonsense. Good. A one-directional relationship is a strong one.

**Step 3 — Apply the *same sentence* to C, word for word.**
Teacher : ? → "A teacher works in a…" → **School.** Done.

**Step 4 — Eliminate the impostor.** If two options fit, your sentence is too loose. Tighten it:
"Bird : Nest" is not "a bird lives in a nest" (a bird also lives in trees). It's "**a bird builds a nest to lay eggs in**." Now only one option will survive.

> 🧠 **Memory hook:** *"Loose sentence, lost mark. Tight sentence, top rank."*

## The Four Relationship Families (Blurting Checklist)

1. **Function** — Knife : Cut (tool → its job)
2. **Part–Whole** — Petal : Flower, Chapter : Book
3. **Degree / Intensity** — Warm : Hot, Angry : Furious (watch the *order* — small→big or big→small?)
4. **Grammar & Wordplay** — Synonym, Antonym, Singular–Plural, letter-shift (C A T → D B U is +1,+1,+1)

Advanced exams love mixing these: a pair that looks like synonyms but is actually *degree* (Glad : Ecstatic), or a letter-shift hidden inside a word pair.
