Card 1
Front: What is the very first step you should take before looking at the options in an Analogy question?
Back: Form a clear sentence linking the given pair (e.g., "A is a tool used by B"). Only then test the options against that exact relationship.

Card 2
Front: In "Doctor : Patient :: Lawyer : ?", why is "Client" correct and "Court" wrong?
Back: The relationship is "professional : person served." A court is the workplace, not the person served — a classic near-correct trap.

Card 3
Front: What is the mnemonic "T-P-F-C" used for in Analogy?
Back: To recall the four most-tested relationship families: Tool–User, Part–Whole, Function, Cause–Effect. Run options through these first.

Card 4
Front: In "Bird : Nest :: Lion : ?", what trap does the option "Forest" set?
Back: "Forest" is the lion's habitat, but a nest is a *built shelter/home*, so the parallel answer is "Den." Habitat vs. dwelling is a favourite examiner trap.

Card 5
Front: What is the "reverse order" trap in Analogy?
Back: The answer pair must match the *direction* of the given pair. If the question is Cause : Effect, an option giving Effect : Cause (e.g., Disease : Germ instead of Germ : Disease) is wrong even if the words are related.

Card 6
Front: In "Book : Pages :: Wall : ?", why is "Bricks" preferred over "Cement"?
Back: A book is *composed of* pages (part–whole, countable units). Bricks are the countable units of a wall; cement is a binding material, not a constituent unit.

Card 7
Front: What mnemonic helps you remember the degree/intensity relationship type?
Back: "Warm : Hot" — think "D-I-S": Degree, Intensity, Scale. Example: Warm : Hot :: Cool : Cold (same quality, higher degree).

Card 8
Front: In number analogies like "8 : 72 :: 9 : ?", what two operations should you test first?
Back: Multiplication and squaring patterns: 8 × 9 = 72, so 9 × 10 = 90; or n : n(n+1). Always check simple ×, ², ²±k before complex rules.

Card 9
Front: Why is "Knife : Cut :: Pen : Write" a better analogy than "Knife : Cut :: Pen : Ink"?
Back: The relationship is tool : function. "Ink" is what a pen *contains*, not its function — a material-vs-function trap.

Card 10
Front: In "Doctor : Stethoscope :: Carpenter : ?", what relationship type is being tested?
Back: Worker : Tool. The answer is a tool the carpenter uses (e.g., Saw). Watch for options naming the carpenter's *product* (furniture) instead.

Card 11
Front: What is the "gender pair" relationship, and give one example.
Back: Masculine : Feminine pairs, e.g., Bull : Cow :: Horse : Mare. Trap: options may give young ones (calf, foal) instead of the female.

Card 12
Front: In "Moon : Satellite :: Earth : ?", why is "Planet" correct?
Back: It is a member : category relationship — the Moon *is a* satellite; the Earth *is a* planet. Options like "Sun" or "Star" test whether you confuse category with association.

Card 13
Front: What should you do when two options both seem to fit an Analogy?
Back: Tighten the relationship sentence — add specificity (e.g., not just "related to" but "young one of"). The more precise sentence eliminates the weaker option.

Card 14
Front: In letter analogies like "ABC : ZYX :: DEF : ?", what is the underlying rule?
Back: Opposite/reverse alphabet positions (A↔Z, B↔Y, C↔X). So DEF → WVU. Remember: A+Z = B+Y = 27 — the "27 rule" mnemonic.

Card 15
Front: What is the examiner's trap in "Ophthalmologist : Eye :: Cardiologist : ?" when "Body" is an option?
Back: The relationship is specialist : organ treated. "Body" is too general; the answer is "Heart." Overly broad options are a standard trap in profession analogies.

Card 16
Front: In "Water : Thirst :: Food : ?", what parallel word completes the pair?
Back: Hunger. Water quenches thirst; food satisfies hunger — a remedy : need relationship. Trap options: "Bread" (a type of food, wrong direction).

Card 17
Front: What is the "synonym vs. antonym" trap in word analogies?
Back: If the given pair are synonyms (e.g., Begin : Commence), the answer must also be synonyms — an antonym pair that merely "relates" the same way in reverse is wrong. Always label the pair's relationship before scanning options.

Card 18
Front: In "Thermometer : Temperature :: Barometer : ?", what is tested and what is the trap?
Back: Instrument : quantity measured → answer: (Atmospheric) Pressure. Trap: "Weather" — a barometer indicates weather but *measures* pressure; instrument analogies demand the measured quantity.

Card 19
Front: What quick check should you run on the grammatical form of an Analogy answer?
Back: Parts of speech must match: if the given pair is Noun : Verb (tool : action), reject options that are Noun : Noun. Form mismatch is an instant eliminator.

Card 20
Front: In "India : Tiger :: USA : ?", why must you verify the exact relationship before answering?
Back: The relationship is country : *national animal*, so the answer is "Bald Eagle" — wait, that's the national bird; the US national mammal is the American Bison (2016). The trap: assuming "national symbol" loosely instead of the precise category.
