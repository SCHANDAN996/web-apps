# 🧒 The Feynman Way — Syllogism (Advanced)

## The Hardest Part, Made Simple

The hardest thing in Syllogism is not drawing circles. It is this one idea:

> **"What is POSSIBLE is not the same as what is DEFINITE."**

Almost every wrong answer in advanced Syllogism happens because a student sees something that *could* be true and marks it as *definitely* true. Let me tell you a story.

## The Story of Ravi and the School Bags 🎒

Ravi is 12. His teacher says two things about the students in his school:

1. **All** students who play cricket carry a red bag.
2. **Some** students who carry a red bag wear caps.

Now Ravi's friend says: *"So some cricket players must wear caps!"*

Is he right? Let's check. Draw it:

- A circle of **cricket players** sitting fully **inside** the red-bag circle. (Rule 1)
- A small patch of **cap-wearers** overlapping the red-bag circle. (Rule 2)

But wait — where is that cap patch? It could touch the cricket circle… or it could sit in a corner of the red-bag circle, far away from the cricket players. The rules never said the caps belong to cricketers!

So "some cricket players wear caps" is **possible** — but not **definite**. And in Syllogism, **only definite conclusions follow.** Ravi's friend fell into the examiner's favourite trap.

## The Golden Rule 🏆

> **A conclusion is correct only if it is true in EVERY possible diagram you can draw.**
> If you can draw even ONE picture where the statements are true but the conclusion is false — the conclusion does NOT follow.

## The Unforgettable Trick: "The Stingy Friend Test" 🧅

Imagine the conclusion is a friend asking you for money. You are a very stingy person. You will give money **only if you are forced to** — if there is no way out.

- **"All A are B" + "All B are C" → "All A are C"?** You are forced. The A-circle is locked inside B, B is locked inside C. No escape. ✅ PAY (conclusion follows).
- **"Some A are B" + "Some B are C" → "Some A are C"?** You can easily arrange the circles so A and C never meet. Not forced. ❌ DON'T PAY (conclusion does not follow).
- **Possibility questions** (new pattern: "Some A being C is a possibility")? Now flip the rule: if you can draw even ONE picture where it happens without breaking the statements — the possibility **follows**. ✅

**Memory line:** *"Definite = forced in all diagrams. Possibility = allowed in one diagram."*

## Three Traps the Examiner Sets 🪤

1. **The Almost-True Trap:** "Some cats are dogs" looks silly, but if the statements force it, it is correct. Never use real-world knowledge — only the statements.
2. **The Either-Or Trap:** "Either conclusion I or II follows" works only when (a) **neither conclusion follows on its own** (each one fails the every-diagram test — note: they may still be *possible*, just not *definite*), (b) they are a complementary pair (like "All A are B" / "Some A are not B", or "Some A are B" / "No A is B"), and (c) they talk about the same two terms.
3. **The Negative-Statement Trap:** One negative statement ("No A is B") can never produce a positive definite conclusion. If the conclusion is positive and a statement is negative — reject it instantly.
