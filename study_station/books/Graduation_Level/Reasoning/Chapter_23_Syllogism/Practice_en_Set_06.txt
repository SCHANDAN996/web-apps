126. Statements: All pens are books. Some books are chairs. No chair is a table.
Conclusions: I. Some pens are not tables. II. Some books are not tables. III. No pen is a chair.
(a) Only I follows (b) I and II follow (c) Only III follows (d) Only II follows
Answer: (d)
Solution: The tempting trap is conclusion I: "All pens are books" plus "Some books are chairs" gives no definite link between pens and tables, and a definite negative cannot come from two positive statements, so I fails; III also fails since pens may or may not be chairs. The books that are chairs are definitely not tables, so the answer is "Only II follows".

127. Statements: Some dogs are cats. All cats are rats. No rat is a lion.
Conclusions: I. Some dogs are not lions. II. All cats being lions is a possibility. III. Some rats are dogs.
(a) Only I and III follow (b) Only II and III follow (c) Only I follows (d) All follow
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is conclusion II: since all cats are rats and no rat is a lion, cats can never be lions, and a possibility cannot contradict a definite negative, so II fails. The dogs that are cats are rats and hence not lions, so I follows, and some dogs being rats means some rats are dogs, so III follows; the answer is "Only I and III follow".
