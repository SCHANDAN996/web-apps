101. Statements: All pens are books. Some books are tables.
Conclusions: I. Some pens are tables. II. Some books are pens.
(a) Only I follows (b) Only II follows (c) Both I and II follow (d) Neither I nor II follows
Answer: (b)
Solution: The tempting trap is Conclusion I — "All pens are books" plus "Some books are tables" does not force any overlap between pens and tables, since the books that are tables may be entirely outside the pens. Conclusion II is a valid conversion of "All pens are books" (All A are B implies Some B are A). So the answer is Only II follows.
Source: PYQ-style

102. Statements: Some cats are dogs. All dogs are animals.
Conclusions: I. Some cats are animals. II. All animals are dogs.
(a) Neither I nor II follows (b) Only II follows (c) Both I and II follow (d) Only I follows
Answer: (d)
Solution: Conclusion II is the trap — "All dogs are animals" cannot be reversed into "All animals are animals' subset dogs"; conversion only gives "Some animals are dogs". Conclusion I follows because the cats that are dogs must be animals. So the answer is Only I follows.

125. Statements: Some trains are buses. Some buses are cars. All cars are vehicles. No vehicle is a cycle.
Conclusions: I. Some buses are vehicles. II. Some trains are not cycles. III. All buses being cycles is a possibility.
(a) Only I follows (b) Only I and II follow (c) Only I and III follow (d) All I, II and III follow

Solution: Conclusions II and III are traps. II fails because the trains that are buses may not be the buses that are cars — the trains may lie outside vehicles entirely, so nothing definite about trains and cycles follows. III fails because the buses that are cars are vehicles and cannot be cycles, so "all buses are cycles" is impossible. I follows since the buses that are cars are vehicles. So the answer is Only I follows.
