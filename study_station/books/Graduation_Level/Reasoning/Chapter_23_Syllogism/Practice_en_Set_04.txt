76. Statements: All pens are books. Some books are chairs.
Conclusions: I. Some chairs are pens. II. Some books are pens.
(a) Only I follows (b) Both I and II follow (c) Only II follows (d) Neither I nor II follows
Answer: (c)
Solution: The tempting trap is conclusion I, which looks plausible because pens and chairs both touch "books", but the two book-groups may be completely separate, so I does not definitely follow. Since all pens are books, some books are certainly pens. Hence only II follows.

77. Statements: No dog is a cat. Some cats are rats.
Conclusions: I. Some rats are not dogs. II. No rat is a dog.
(a) Neither I nor II follows (b) Only I follows (c) Both I and II follow (d) Only II follows
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is conclusion II — a universal negative cannot be drawn from a particular statement, so II is too strong. The rats that are cats can never be dogs, so "some rats are not dogs" definitely holds. Hence only I follows.
