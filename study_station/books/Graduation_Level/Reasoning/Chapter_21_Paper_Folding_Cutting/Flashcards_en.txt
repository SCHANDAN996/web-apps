Card 1
Front: When a paper is folded once and a hole is punched, how many holes appear after unfolding?
Back: Two holes — the punch passes through both layers, and the two holes are mirror images across the fold line.

Card 2
Front: What is the single most important line to track in every paper folding problem?
Back: The fold line (axis of symmetry) — every cut or punch is reflected across it when the paper is unfolded.

Card 3
Front: If a paper is folded twice (in half, then in half again) and one hole is punched, how many holes appear on full unfolding?
Back: Four holes — each fold doubles the number of layers, so 2 × 2 = 4 holes, symmetric about both fold lines.

Card 4
Front: What is the "layer-counting" trick for predicting the number of holes?
Back: Count the layers of paper at the punched point after all folds; the number of holes after unfolding equals the number of layers the punch passed through.

Card 5
Front: Mnemonic trick: what does "FOLD" stand for as a solving checklist?
Back: F – Find the fold lines; O – Order of unfolding (reverse of folding); L – Locate the cut/punch; D – Duplicate by mirror reflection across each fold.

Card 6
Front: In which order must you unfold the paper while solving — the same order as folding or reverse?
Back: Reverse order — always undo the last fold first, reflecting the cuts step by step back to the original sheet.

Card 7
Front: A punch is made exactly ON the fold line. How many holes appear after one unfold?
Back: Only one hole — a punch on the fold line is its own mirror image, so it does not duplicate.

Card 8
Front: Examiner's trap: a cut is made at the folded corner where two fold lines meet. What do most students get wrong?
Back: They count it as 4 separate cuts; actually the cut lies on both axes, so reflections overlap and fewer distinct cuts appear (often a single symmetric shape at the centre).

Card 9
Front: How does a triangular fold (diagonal fold) change the reflection rule?
Back: The mirror axis is the diagonal, so holes reflect across the diagonal line — positions swap between the two triangular halves, not top-bottom or left-right.

Card 10
Front: When a shape is cut from a folded edge (not the interior), what appears after unfolding?
Back: A symmetric whole shape centred on the fold line — e.g., cutting a half-heart on the fold gives a full heart.

Card 11
Front: Mnemonic trick: what does "MIRROR" remind you in paper folding questions?
Back: "Every cut Makes Its Reflection, Repeated Over each fold, in Reverse order" — reflect the cut across each fold line while unfolding backwards.

Card 12
Front: If a paper is folded into quarters and a circle is punched through all layers near the outer corner, where do the four holes lie?
Back: Symmetrically placed in all four quadrants — mirror images across both the horizontal and vertical fold lines.

Card 13
Front: Examiner's trap: options show the correct number of holes but wrong positions. What check saves you?
Back: Verify symmetry — the final pattern must be perfectly symmetric about every fold line; any option breaking symmetry is wrong even if the hole count matches.

Card 14
Front: What changes when the paper is folded and then a shape is CUT OUT from an open (unfolded) edge instead of a folded edge?
Back: The cut does not duplicate across that edge — it appears as separate identical cuts on each layer, mirrored only across the actual fold lines.

Card 15
Front: In "paper cutting" questions (fold + cut along a line), how do you decide the final outline?
Back: Unfold step by step in reverse, mirroring the cut boundary across each fold line; the union of all mirrored cuts gives the final pattern.

Card 16
Front: A sheet is folded three times (into eighths). One punch is made away from all fold lines. How many holes result?
Back: Eight holes — 2³ = 8 layers, and a punch away from fold lines duplicates across all three axes.

Card 17
Front: Why do examiners place a punch very close to (but not on) a fold line in options?
Back: To trap students who merge it with its reflection — two distinct holes close together appear, not one; distance from the fold is preserved in the mirror image.

Card 18
Front: What is the fastest first step when you see a paper folding & cutting question in the exam?
Back: Count the folds and mark each fold line's direction (horizontal/vertical/diagonal) — this fixes the number of reflections and the axes before you even look at the cut.
