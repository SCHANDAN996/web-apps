# Paper Folding & Cutting — The Feynman Method

## The Hard Concept: The Hard Part of Paper Folding & Cutting

Imagine you are making a paper plane. You fold a flat sheet, and something interesting happens: the paper is still the same 2D sheet, but every fold creates a **line of symmetry**. Whatever you do on one side of the fold gets **mirrored** on the other side when you unfold it.

That single idea — *a fold is a mirror* — is the whole secret of this chapter. The hard part is not the folding; it is keeping track of **how many mirrors** you have created and **in which order** they reflect.

## Explain It Like You're Teaching a Friend

Take a square sheet of paper.

1. **Fold it once** (say, left half onto right half). Now punch one hole through the folded paper. When you open it, how many holes do you see? **Two** — because the fold acted like a mirror, and the punch went through two layers.
2. **Fold it twice** (left to right, then top to bottom). Now the paper has **four layers**. One punch = **four holes** when unfolded, placed symmetrically about both fold lines.
3. **The rule:** each fold doubles the number of layers (if the fold covers the whole sheet). So *n* full folds → 2ⁿ layers → one punch makes 2ⁿ holes.

Cutting works the same way. If you cut a shape along a folded edge, the unfolded paper shows that shape **reflected** across the fold line — like a butterfly's two wings.

## The Order Matters

Folds are undone in **reverse order** — the last fold is opened first. So when you track a hole or a cut:

- Start from the final folded figure.
- Unfold the **last** fold first: mirror every hole/cut across that fold line.
- Then unfold the next fold: mirror **all** the holes (old + new) across that line.
- Repeat until the sheet is fully open.

Skipping the reverse order is the most common mistake in exams.

## Cutting on a Fold Edge vs. Away from It

- **Cut on the fold line:** the shape opens into a symmetric figure (a half-heart cut on the fold becomes a full heart).
- **Cut away from the fold:** the shape appears as two (or more) separate mirror copies.

## Quick Self-Test (Explain Aloud)

Without looking above, explain to an imaginary student:

1. Why does one punch make 4 holes after two folds?
2. Why must you unfold in reverse order?
3. A circle is punched near a corner of a twice-folded square — where will the four holes appear?

If you can answer all three in simple words — *layers double, mirrors reflect, last fold opens first* — you have truly understood the concept. If you stumble, go back to the step where your explanation broke and rebuild it. That is the Feynman Method: **if you can't explain it simply, you haven't understood it yet.**

## One-Line Summary

**Every fold is a mirror; every punch multiplies by the number of layers; always unfold in reverse order.**
