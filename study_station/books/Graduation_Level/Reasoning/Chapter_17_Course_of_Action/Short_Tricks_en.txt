# 🪄 Short Tricks & Mnemonics — Statement & Course of Action

## Mnemonic Boxes

**Box 1 — The "P.L.A.N." Test (Is the action valid?)**
Every valid Course of Action must pass **P.L.A.N.**:
- **P** — **P**ractical (can it actually be done?)
- **L** — **L**egal (within law and rules?)
- **A** — **A**ttacks the problem (does it solve the issue in the statement?)
- **N** — **N**ot extreme (not harsh, not a knee-jerk reaction?)
Fail any one letter → reject that action.

---

**Box 2 — "Doctor, Not Judge"**
Be a **doctor**, not a judge. A doctor *treats* the disease (solves the problem); a judge *punishes*. In CoA questions, actions that only punish, blame, or take revenge are almost always wrong. Actions that *fix* the problem are right.

---

**Box 3 — "Root or Fruit?"**
Ask: does the action attack the **root** cause or just pluck the **fruit** (symptom)?
- "Employees are late" → "Cut salary" = plucking fruit ❌
- "Improve transport / revise timings after study" = attacking root ✅

---

**Box 4 — The "Baby-Bathwater" Rule**
Never throw the **baby out with the bathwater**. Actions like "shut down the department," "cancel the scheme," "ban all protests" discard the good with the bad → always wrong.

---

**Box 5 — "E-X-T-R-E-M-E = E-X-I-T"**
Any action containing extreme words — *all, every, completely, immediately dismiss, ban forever, shoot, demolish* — should **EXIT** your answer choices. Extreme = Eliminate.

---

**Box 6 — "Follows the Statement, Not Your Feelings"**
Chant: *"The statement is the whole universe."* Your personal opinion, general knowledge, or sympathy must not enter. If the statement says the sky is green, the action must deal with a green sky.

---

**Box 7 — The "Two-Key" Rule for "Both Follow"**
"Both follow" needs **TWO keys**, not one. Check each action **independently**. Students mark "both" because action II is good and action I "seems okay." One weak action = "both" is dead.

---

**Box 8 — "Either–Or = Same Coin, Two Faces"**
"Either I or II follows" only when both actions are:
1. Both valid, **and**
2. **Mutually exclusive** (doing one makes the other impossible/pointless) — two faces of the same coin.
If both can be done together → answer is "both," never "either."

---

**Box 9 — "Government Glasses"**
Wear **Government Glasses**: judge the action as a responsible administrator would — impartial, lawful, welfare-oriented, long-term. If the action sounds like something a panicked or biased person would do, reject it.

---

**Box 10 — "The 3-Second Veto Words"**
Veto on sight: **immediately dismiss, arrest all, ban, boycott, punish severely, close permanently, shoot, demolish, expel everyone**. 90% of actions containing these are traps.

---

**Box 11 — "Investigate First" (The Sherlock Rule)**
When the statement reports an allegation, complaint, or suspected problem, the correct action almost always begins with **"constitute a committee / inquire / verify facts."** Sherlock investigates before he acts — so should the administrator.

---

**Box 12 — "Problem-Solution Handshake"**
The action must **shake hands** with the exact problem in the statement. Statement about water shortage → action about electricity = no handshake = rejected, no matter how noble it sounds.

---

**Box 13 — "Neither Is the Default Doubt"**
When confused between "only I" and "neither," re-read action I with the P.L.A.N. test. Examiners love making action I *sound* official ("authorities should look into the matter") while being vague and toothless — vague actions without concrete steps are usually rejected.

---

**Box 14 — "Long-Term Beats Short-Term"**
If one action is a temporary patch (distribute compensation, one-time relief) and another fixes the system (reform policy, build infrastructure), the **systemic** one is stronger. But remember: both can follow if they work together (relief now + reform later).

---

## ⏭️ Skip Strategy — When to Skip

1. **The 20-Second Rule:** If after 20 seconds you cannot clearly classify even ONE of the two actions as valid/invalid, mark for review and move on. CoA questions are quick wins when clear, time-traps when vague — don't let one vague question eat 2 minutes.

2. **Skip "Emotionally Loaded" Statements for Later:** Statements about religion, politics, or sensitive social issues are deliberately worded to trigger your personal bias. If you feel yourself agreeing/disagreeing emotionally, flag it and return with a cool head — bias is the examiner's biggest trap here.

3. **Skip Double-Negative Confusers:** If the statement AND the actions both contain negatives ("should not fail to…", "cannot be avoided unless…"), and you can't untangle them in one clean read, skip and return. Rewriting them positively takes 40+ seconds — do it only in the second pass, never on the first.
