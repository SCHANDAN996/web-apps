Card 1
Front: What is the very first thing you must check about a "course of action" before anything else?
Back: Whether it is a practical, logical step that directly addresses the problem stated — not whether it is morally ideal or emotionally appealing.

Card 2
Front: A course of action says "the government should punish the culprits." Should it follow?
Back: No — unless the statement establishes who the culprits are and that a problem exists. Vague or punitive actions without a stated problem do not follow.

Card 3
Front: What is the "Problem → Solution" test in Statement & Course of Action?
Back: First identify the problem in the statement. A course of action follows only if it logically reduces or solves that specific problem.

Card 4
Front: What mnemonic reminds you of the three qualities a valid course of action must have?
Back: "P-L-P" — Practical, Logical, Problem-linked. If any one fails, the action does not follow.

Card 5
Front: What is the "EXTREME" trap in course-of-action questions?
Back: Actions using words like "all," "ban completely," "immediately dismiss," or "never allow" are usually too extreme and do not follow.

Card 6
Front: A course of action suggests a long-term measure (e.g., "improve education policy") for an immediate crisis (e.g., "exam paper leaked"). Does it follow?
Back: No — a long-term measure cannot solve an immediate problem. The action must match the urgency and time-frame of the problem.

Card 7
Front: What mnemonic helps you reject wrong courses of action quickly?
Back: "E-V-R" — Eliminate actions that are Extreme, Vague, or Repetitive (already implied/done in the statement).

Card 8
Front: If a course of action merely repeats what the statement already says is happening, does it follow?
Back: No — a redundant action adds nothing new and does not follow.

Card 9
Front: Examiner's trap: a course of action sounds morally correct ("everyone should be kind to the poor"). Why is it usually wrong?
Back: Moral or idealistic suggestions are not practical administrative actions. The test is logic and practicality, not virtue.

Card 10
Front: Examiner's trap: two courses of action are given, and one is a strong step while the other is a mild enquiry. When do "both follow"?
Back: Only when both independently address the problem — e.g., immediate relief (short-term) plus an enquiry to prevent recurrence (long-term). Don't mark "both" just because both sound nice.

Card 11
Front: In "either I or II follows" cases, what is the typical structure of the two actions?
Back: The two actions are mutually exclusive or contradictory responses to the same problem (e.g., "increase production" vs. "import goods") — only one can logically be chosen.

Card 12
Front: Should a course of action be judged by whether it is legally or constitutionally valid?
Back: Yes — an action that violates law, the Constitution, or basic rights never follows, even if it seems effective.

Card 13
Front: A statement reports a one-time, minor incident. A course of action proposes a permanent policy change. Does it follow?
Back: Usually no — the response must be proportionate to the scale of the problem. Overreaction is a classic trap.

Card 14
Front: What is the difference between a "course of action" and an "assumption" question?
Back: Course of action asks what should be DONE (future step); assumption asks what is already BELIEVED (hidden premise) in the statement.

Card 15
Front: If the statement itself already announces a decision, does a course of action suggesting the same decision follow?
Back: No — suggesting what is already decided is redundant. Actions must be new, logical next steps.

Card 16
Front: What quick check exposes a "negative/escape" course of action (e.g., "people should stop going out")?
Back: Ask: does it solve the problem or merely avoid it? Escapist or defeatist actions that ignore the root cause do not follow.

Card 17
Front: How much time should you ideally spend on one Statement & Course of Action question in the exam?
Back: About 30–40 seconds — identify the problem, apply the P-L-P test to each action, and eliminate extreme/vague options.

Card 18
Front: A course of action begins with "the authorities should look into the matter." When does such an enquiry action follow?
Back: When the facts or causes in the statement are unclear or disputed — investigation is valid only when more information is genuinely needed before acting.
