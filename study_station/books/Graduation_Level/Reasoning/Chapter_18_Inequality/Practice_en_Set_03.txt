Questions 51–75: Medium

51. Statements: P > Q ≥ R = S; S < T ≤ U
Conclusions: I. P > S  II. Q ≥ T
(a) Neither I nor II follows (b) Only II follows (c) Both I and II follow (d) Only I follows
Answer: (d)
Solution: The tempting trap is conclusion II: since Q ≥ R = S and S < T, we only know Q ≥ S < T, so Q and T cannot be compared — many students wrongly assume Q ≥ T. For I, P > Q ≥ R = S gives P > S directly, so only conclusion I follows.
Source: PYQ-style

52. Statements: A ≥ B > C; C = D ≥ E
Conclusions: I. A > E  II. B ≥ D
(a) Only I follows (b) Both I and II follow (c) Only II follows (d) Neither I nor II follows
Answer: (b)
Solution: From A ≥ B > C = D ≥ E, we get A > E, so I follows. For II, B > C = D means B > D, and a strict greater-than always satisfies a "greater than or equal to" conclusion, so II also follows — the trap is thinking II fails because the chain gives B > D rather than B = D. Both conclusions follow.
Source: PYQ-style

Solution: X > Y = Z ≥ W gives X > W, so I follows. For II, Z ≥ W < V ≤ U — the chain breaks at W because the sign reverses, so Z and U cannot be compared; assuming Z < U is the examiner's trap. Only conclusion I follows.
