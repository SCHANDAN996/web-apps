🪄 SHORT TRICKS & MNEMONICS — INEQUALITY

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
🧠 MNEMONIC 1 — "The Hungry Crocodile"
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
The crocodile always opens its mouth toward the BIGGER number.
A > B → crocodile eats A (A is bigger).
A < B → crocodile eats B.
Never read the symbol — just ask: "Whom is the croc eating?"

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
🧠 MNEMONIC 2 — "GOLD Chain" (for combining signs)
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
**G**reater signs **O**nly **L**ink in the same **D**irection.
A > B > C → you may conclude A > C.
A > B < C → the chain BREAKS at the opposite sign → NO relation between A and C.
Same direction = chain survives. Opposite direction = chain snaps.

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
🧠 MNEMONIC 3 — "The Gatekeeper ≥"
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
≥ and ≤ are gatekeepers: they allow EQUALITY through.
A ≥ B means A is bigger OR equal — never "strictly bigger."
Trap: if the conclusion says A > B but the statement gives A ≥ B, it is NOT definitely true (A = B is possible).

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
🧠 MNEMONIC 4 — "One Strong Sign Makes the Chain Strong"
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
> and < are STRONG signs; ≥ and ≤ are WEAK signs.
In a same-direction chain, the conclusion follows the STRONGEST sign present:
A ≥ B ≥ C → conclusion A ≥ C ✔ (all signs weak → weak conclusion).
A ≥ B > C → conclusion A > C ✔ (one strong sign makes the whole chain strong).
A > B ≥ C → conclusion A > C ✔ (strong sign present).
Rule: if even one strict sign (> or <) appears in the chain, the conclusion is strict; only when ALL signs are weak (≥/≤) is the conclusion weak.

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
🧠 MNEMONIC 5 — "Either-Or: The Twins Rule"
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
Either-Or works ONLY when three conditions hold — remember **S-C-N**:
**S**ame pair of elements in both conclusions,
**C**omplementary signs (one ≥ and one <, or one > and one ≤),
**N**o definite relation possible from the statement.
If all three pass → "either I or II follows." Miss even one → both false.

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
🧠 MNEMONIC 6 — "The Mirror Flip"
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
A > B is the same as B < A. Flip the sentence, flip the sign.
When statements mix directions (P > Q, R < Q), rewrite everything facing ONE way (R < Q → Q > R) before chaining. Most "no relation" errors come from not flipping.

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
🧠 MNEMONIC 7 — "Coded Inequality: Decode First, Think Later"
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
In coded questions (A @ B means A > B), write the decoder on your rough sheet FIRST:
@ → >, # → <, $ → =, % → ≥, & → ≤
Then translate the ENTIRE question before solving. Solving while decoding = guaranteed silly mistake.

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
🧠 MNEMONIC 8 — "The Dead-End Sign"
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
When chaining A > B < C, the < sign is a dead end — you cannot cross it.
Trick: draw the chain on paper; the moment two arrowheads POINT AT EACH OTHER (→ ←), stop. Any conclusion across that gap is false.

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
🧠 MNEMONIC 9 — "Equal is the Silent Killer"
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
Examiners hide = inside ≥ and ≤.
Statement: A ≥ B, B ≥ A → actually means A = B!
Whenever the same pair appears with opposite weak signs, check: the only possibility is equality. Conclusions claiming A > B or A < B are both false.

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
🧠 MNEMONIC 10 — "One Chain, One Verdict"
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
Before checking any conclusion, build ONE master chain:
A > B = C ≥ D > E
Now every conclusion is answered by reading positions on this single line — never re-read the statements for each conclusion. Build once, answer four.

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
🧠 MNEMONIC 11 — "Negative Conclusions Need a Full Bridge"
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
Conclusion "A is NOT greater than C" (A ≤ C) is true only if the chain gives ≤ or = the whole way. If even one link is missing or reversed, the negative conclusion collapses. Don't assume "not greater" just because "greater" failed — check the actual bridge.

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
🧠 MNEMONIC 12 — "The 10-Second Scan"
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
Before solving, scan the two conclusions:
- Different element pairs with no connecting chain? → instant "neither follows."
- Same pair, complementary signs, no relation? → likely "either-or."
- Same pair, one strong one weak? → check whether any strict sign appears in the chain.
These three patterns cover the most common question types — a useful rule of thumb, not a fixed ratio.

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
🧠 MNEMONIC 13 — "Write Big, Think Small"
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
On the rough sheet, write symbols HUGE: > ≥ = ≤ <.
Most errors are not logic errors — they are reading ≥ as >. Big symbols = zero misreads.

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
⏭️ SKIP STRATEGY — WHEN TO SKIP
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

1. **The 15-Second Chain Rule:** If you cannot build the master chain within 15 seconds (too many elements, mixed directions, 4+ statements), mark it and move on. Return only if time remains — a tangled 6-element chain can eat 2 minutes for 1 mark.

2. **Coded Inequality with 5+ Symbols:** If the code table has five or more symbols AND the statements are long, skip on the first pass. Decode-and-translate errors are costly; attempt these only in the second round with a fresh mind.

3. **Double Either-Or Traps:** If both conclusions look like either-or candidates but the pairs are slightly different (A vs C in one, A vs D in the other), do NOT guess — this is the examiner's favourite trap. Verify the "same pair" condition carefully or skip. A wrong either-or guess costs 1 mark plus negative marking; a skip costs nothing.
