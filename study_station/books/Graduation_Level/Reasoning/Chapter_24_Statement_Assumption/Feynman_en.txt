# Feynman Technique — Statement & Assumption

## The Hardest Concept, Explained Like You're 12

The hardest thing in this chapter is not finding an assumption — it's understanding **what an assumption actually is**. Let me tell you a story.

### The Story of Rohan and the Umbrella ☔

One morning, Rohan looked out of the window and said to his mother:

> **"I should take an umbrella to school today."**

That's his **statement** — what he *said* out loud.

But wait. Rohan never mentioned rain. He never said "clouds are gathering." He never said "the weather report predicts a storm." So why did he talk about an umbrella?

Because hidden *inside* his sentence, there are things he **believed but didn't say**:

1. *It might rain today.* (Otherwise, why an umbrella?)
2. *An umbrella can protect him from rain.* (Otherwise, why carry it?)

These hidden beliefs are **assumptions**. An assumption is something the speaker **takes for granted** — something that must be true in his mind, or his statement would make no sense at all.

### The Magic Test: "Break the Statement"

Here's how a 12-year-old can find any assumption. Take the hidden idea and **pretend it's false**. If the statement collapses like a house of cards, it was a real assumption.

- Suppose it **cannot** rain today. Then Rohan saying "take an umbrella" is nonsense. 💥 Statement broken → so "it might rain" **is** an assumption.
- Suppose umbrellas **cannot** protect from rain. Again, nonsense. 💥 Broken → so that **is** an assumption too.
- Now try: "Rohan's umbrella is red." Pretend it's false — the umbrella is blue. Does his statement break? No! He'd still carry it. ✅ Statement survives → so the colour is **NOT** an assumption.

**That's the whole chapter in one game:** *Kill the idea. If the statement dies with it, it was an assumption.*

### The Examiner's Trap 🪤

The examiner loves to fool you with two kinds of fake assumptions:

1. **The "Too Much" trap:** Statement — "Buy Shine Soap for glowing skin." Fake assumption — "Shine Soap is the *best* soap in the market." The speaker never claimed it's the best — only that it helps skin glow. Words like *best, only, all, never* are alarm bells. 🚨
2. **The "Restatement" trap:** Simply repeating the statement in different words is NOT an assumption. An assumption must be *hidden*, not *repeated*.

### The Unforgettable Trick: **"The Umbrella Test"** ☂️

Whenever you see a Statement & Assumption question, ask yourself:

> **"If this assumption were FALSE, would the speaker's umbrella still open?"**

- Statement collapses → **Assumption is implicit (valid).** ✅
- Statement survives happily → **Assumption is NOT implicit.** ❌

And remember the three quick rejections:
- ❌ Too extreme (only / all / best / never)
- ❌ Just a restatement of the statement
- ❌ A conclusion (what follows *after*) — an assumption comes *before*, in the speaker's mind
