Card 1
Front: What exactly is an "assumption" in a Statement & Assumption question?
Back: An assumption is an unstated, hidden idea that the speaker must have taken for granted while making the statement. It is never written in the statement — it lies behind it.

Card 2
Front: If an assumption is "probably true" but not essential for the statement, is it implicit?
Back: No. An assumption is implicit only if the statement collapses or loses its purpose without it. "Possible" or "desirable" is not enough — it must be necessary.

Card 3
Front: What is the quickest test to check whether an assumption is implicit?
Back: The Negation Test: negate the assumption. If negating it makes the statement meaningless or self-defeating, the assumption is implicit; if the statement still stands, it is not.

Card 4
Front: Which mnemonic reminds you that an assumption must be hidden AND necessary?
Back: "H-N rule" — Hidden but Necessary. If the idea is already stated, it can't be an assumption; if it isn't necessary, it isn't implicit.

Card 5
Front: What mnemonic helps you reject extreme assumptions instantly?
Back: "AES — Avoid Extreme Statements." Assumptions containing words like all, none, always, never, only, completely are almost always NOT implicit.

Card 6
Front: A statement says: "Buy X soap — it makes skin glow." What is the hidden assumption?
Back: That people desire glowing skin (and that advertising can influence buyers). The appeal works only if this desire exists.

Card 7
Front: In an advertisement or appeal, what is almost always implicitly assumed?
Back: That the advertisement/appeal will be read, heard, or noticed by people and can influence their behaviour. Without this, issuing it would be pointless.

Card 8
Front: In a notice like "Trespassers will be prosecuted," what is assumed?
Back: That people can read and understand the notice, and that the threat of prosecution can deter them. Otherwise the notice serves no purpose.

Card 9
Front: Examiner's trap: an assumption merely restates the statement in different words. Is it implicit?
Back: No — this is a classic trap. A restatement is not an assumption; an assumption must be something unstated that underlies the statement, not a paraphrase of it.

Card 10
Front: Examiner's trap: an assumption gives a reason or explanation for why the statement's event happened. Should you pick it?
Back: No. Assumptions about causes or past reasons are usually not implicit. The assumption must support the statement's purpose going forward, not explain its origin.

Card 11
Front: A statement is an instruction: "Reach the station an hour early." What is assumed?
Back: That the instruction can be followed and that following it serves the intended purpose (e.g., avoiding missing the train). Instructions assume feasibility and usefulness.

Card 12
Front: If an assumption contradicts the statement, can it ever be implicit?
Back: Never. A contradictory assumption destroys the statement. Implicit assumptions must be consistent with, and supportive of, the statement.

Card 13
Front: Should you use your own real-world knowledge of facts to judge assumptions?
Back: No. Judge only from the speaker's viewpoint within the statement. Even if an assumption is factually false in reality, it is implicit if the speaker clearly believed it.

Card 14
Front: A government announcement says: "The scheme will benefit farmers." What is typically assumed?
Back: That farmers need such benefit and that the scheme can actually reach and help them. Announcements assume the claimed outcome is achievable and desired.

Card 15
Front: What is the difference between an assumption and a conclusion in these questions?
Back: An assumption precedes the statement (what the speaker took for granted before speaking); a conclusion follows from it (what can be derived after). Don't mix the two — examiners swap them as a trap.

Card 16
Front: When both assumptions seem implicit, what should you check before choosing "both"?
Back: Check each independently with the Negation Test. "Both" is correct only if each one is individually necessary — not because they sound similar or related.

Card 17
Front: A statement says: "Join our coaching to clear SSC." Which assumption is a trap: (a) people want to clear SSC, or (b) only this coaching can clear SSC?
Back: (b) is the trap — "only" makes it extreme and unnecessary. (a) is implicit because the appeal is pointless if nobody wants to clear SSC.

Card 18
Front: How much time should you ideally spend on one Statement & Assumption question in the exam?
Back: About 30–40 seconds. Read the statement once, apply the Negation Test to each assumption, and move on — don't re-read endlessly; these questions reward a fixed method, not deep thought.
