# 🪄 Short Tricks & Mnemonics — Statement & Assumption

---

**BOX 1 — "The Sleeping Pill" 💊**
An assumption is what the author **SWALLOWED before speaking** — he never said it, but he digested it. If the idea is *already awake in the statement* (explicitly written), it is NOT an assumption. Assumptions sleep under the statement, never on top of it.

---

**BOX 2 — "GAP = Good Assumption Passes" 🕳️**
**G**oes unstated, **A**ligned with the author's intent, **P**robably true. If any letter fails — it's stated, it contradicts, or it's a wild guess — the assumption falls into the gap and dies.

---

**BOX 3 — "The Waiter Rule" 🍽️**
A statement is the dish served; the assumption is the kitchen you never see. You don't need to see the kitchen to know one exists — but you can't assume the kitchen also serves Chinese! Only assume what the dish *requires*, not what it *might* require.

---

**BOX 4 — "NEGATION NINJA" 🥷**
Stuck between two assumptions? **Negate each one.** If negating it *kills* the statement's purpose → implicit. If the statement survives the negation → not implicit. The ninja test never lies.

---

**BOX 5 — "ALWAYS-NEVER Graveyard" ⚰️**
Words like *always, never, all, none, only, forever* in an assumption = dig its grave immediately. Real-world assumptions are humble; extreme words are the examiner's favourite trap.

---

**BOX 6 — "The Advertisement Ghost" 👻**
Every ad/notice/appeal silently assumes: **(a) people will read/hear it, (b) people will respond.** If an assumption says "people will notice this notice" — it's implicit. The ghost behind every announcement!

---

**BOX 7 — "Advice = Disease + Cure" 💊**
Whenever someone advises/warns/requests, two assumptions are automatically implicit: **(1) the problem exists or may exist, (2) the advice can be followed.** Remember: no doctor prescribes medicine for a disease that can't exist and can't be treated.

---

**BOX 8 — "Don't Marry the Statement" 💍**
An assumption that merely *repeats* the statement in new words is a parrot, not an assumption. If it just restates → reject. Assumptions must be **one step behind**, never standing on the same step.

---

**BOX 9 — "Author's Shoes, Not Yours" 👟**
Ask: "What must **the speaker** believe to say this?" — NOT "What do **I** know about the world?" Your general knowledge is a trap. Even if the statement is factually wrong, judge assumptions from the author's mind only.

---

**BOX 10 — "The Either-Or Twins" 👯**
When two assumptions are **mutually exclusive** (both can't be true together) but each alone could justify the statement → answer is **"either I or II is implicit."** Twins: only one can come to the party, but either is welcome.

---

**BOX 11 — "Cause-Effect Confusion Killer" 🔀**
If an assumption states the *effect* of the statement as if it were the *cause* (or vice versa), reject it. The examiner loves flipping the arrow. Check: does the assumption come **before** (implicit) or **after** (conclusion) the statement?

---

**BOX 12 — "15-Second Gate" ⏱️**
Read statement → 5 sec. Negation test on Assumption I → 5 sec. Same on II → 5 sec. If you're still confused at second 16, the question is a time-trap — mark and move.

---

**BOX 13 — "Desirability ≠ Assumption" 🌈**
"Something should be done" is a *wish*, not an assumption. Assumptions are about what **is** believed, not what **ought to be**. The moment an assumption sounds like moral advice, show it the exit.

---

## ⏭️ Skip Strategy — When to Skip

1. **The 15-Second Fog Rule:** If after reading the statement twice you still can't identify the author's *intent* (suggestion? warning? announcement?), skip it. Statement-Assumption questions without clear intent eat 2+ minutes and still end in a guess.

2. **The Double-Negative Trap:** If both the statement and assumptions are loaded with negatives ("It is not true that X is not unnecessary…"), skip on first pass. Decode it only in the second round — mistranslating one "not" flips the entire answer.

3. **The Philosophy Bomb:** If the statement is abstract/philosophical (about life, society, morality) with no concrete actor or action, and both assumptions "feel" right — skip. These are the lowest-accuracy questions in the section; invest those seconds in coding or series questions instead.
