# 🪄 Short Tricks & Mnemonics — Advanced Puzzles (Multi-condition)

---

**Box 1 — "TABLE Before TALK"**
Before solving any puzzle, **T**ranslate every clue, **A**rrange a table/grid, **B**lock fixed positions first, **L**ink the chains, **E**liminate leftovers. Never start "talking" through clues in your head — draw the table first.

---

**Box 2 — "FIX the FIRM"**
**F**ixed clues first (direct statements: "A sits at the left end"), **I**ndirect clues next ("A sits two places from B"), **X**-clues (negative: "A is NOT next to C") last. FIRM = Fixed → Indirect → Relative → Maybe (case-based).

---

**Box 3 — "The Two-Case Trap"**
When a clue gives two possibilities, draw **both cases side by side** like twins. The moment one twin breaks a rule, kill it instantly. Mnemonic: *"Two twins enter, one twin leaves."* Never carry a dead case forward — it eats 2 minutes.

---

**Box 4 — "NEGATIVE = NET"**
Negative clues ("P is not the doctor", "Q doesn't like red") are a **NET** — they don't place anyone, they only catch wrong options. **N**ever **E**xpect a negative clue to **T**ell a position. Use them at the END to filter, not at the start to build.

---

**Box 5 — "CHAIN Gang"**
Link relative clues into chains: "A > B, B > C, C > D" becomes one gang: **A-B-C-D**. A chain of 4 clues = 1 picture. Mnemonic: *"Don't collect clues, ARREST them into a chain gang."*

---

**Box 6 — "The 3-Column Cop"**
For floor/box/day puzzles, always make exactly 3 columns: **Position | Person | Attribute**. The Cop asks every clue: "Which column do you belong to?" A clue that fits no column is a trap — park it.

---

**Box 7 — "ENDs are your Friends"**
In linear/circular arrangements, fill the **ends and extremes first** (leftmost, rightmost, tallest, shortest, top floor, ground floor). *"Friends sit at the ENDs, strangers in the middle."* Extremes have fewer neighbours = fewer possibilities = faster fixing.

---

**Box 8 — "COUNT the CROWN"**
In "how many persons between X and Y" questions, the CROWN rule: **C**ount positions, **R**emove the two endpoints, **O**nly the middle matters, **W**atch for "immediate" vs "somewhere", **N**ever count X and Y themselves. The crown sits BETWEEN two kings, never ON them.

---

**Box 9 — "FLOOR = F-L-O-O-R"**
For floor puzzles: **F**ix ground/top first, **L**ocate the anchor person, **O**dd-gap clues ("two floors between") next, **O**verlay negative clues, **R**ead the question only after the grid is full. *"Build the building from the ground, not from the roof."*

---

**Box 10 — "The 90-Second Judge"**
A multi-condition puzzle is a **case, not a question**. Judge it in 90 seconds: if the table is 70% full → finish all 5 sub-questions (each now costs only 15 sec). If the table is still empty after 90 sec → the case is hostile, walk away. *"A good judge decides fast."*

---

**Box 11 — "MIRROR the MONTH"**
For month/date puzzles with years, draw a vertical mirror timeline (Jan top → Dec bottom). Clues like "before March but after the person who joined in January" become arrows on the mirror. *"Time puzzles are solved on a timeline, never in the mind."*

---

**Box 12 — "CIRCLE = CLOCK"**
In circular seating, pick any one person as **12 o'clock** and rotate the whole circle mentally. Facing centre = left is anticlockwise; facing outside = left is clockwise. Mnemonic: *"Inside-in, outside-out — flip the clock about."* Most exam traps hide exactly here.

---

**Box 13 — "GRIDLOCK the GUESS"**
Never guess-place a person. If stuck, ask: "Which cell has only ONE candidate left?" Sudoku-style elimination beats intuition. *"When the grid locks, count the candidates — don't gamble."*

---

**Box 14 — "READ the QUESTION First (RQ-First)"**
In a 5-question puzzle set, glance at the questions BEFORE solving. If Q1 asks "Who sits third to the left of X?", you know X must be fixed early. *"The questions tell you which clues matter most."*

---

## ⏭️ Skip Strategy — When to Walk Away

**Tip 1 — The 15-Second Pattern Test:** If after reading all clues once you cannot identify even ONE fixed/anchor clue within 15 seconds, the puzzle is case-heavy. Mark it, move on, return only if time remains.

**Tip 2 — The 3-Case Alarm:** If your setup branches into **3 or more parallel cases**, skip immediately. In exam conditions, 3-case puzzles consume 4–6 minutes for uncertain gain — the worst marks-per-minute trade in the reasoning section.

**Tip 3 — The Half-Table Rule:** If 2 minutes are gone and your table is less than 50% filled, abandon the puzzle mid-way without guilt. A half-solved puzzle yields 0 marks; those 2 minutes invested in 3 easy questions yield 3 marks. *"Sunk time is not a reason to stay — it's a reason to leave."*
