# Advanced Puzzles (Multi-condition) — The Feynman Way

## The Birthday Party Disaster

Imagine you're 12 years old, and your mom puts YOU in charge of seating 8 guests at your birthday dinner. Sounds easy? Here's the catch:

- Aunty Priya **won't sit next to** Uncle Raj (they argued last Diwali).
- Grandpa **must sit at the head** of the table.
- Your best friend Sam **wants to sit beside you**, but **not opposite** Grandpa.
- Cousin Meena sits **exactly between** Sam and Aunty Priya.
- The twins, Ravi and Kavi, **always sit together**.

One rule? Easy. But FIVE rules at once? That's a **multi-condition puzzle** — and it's exactly what SSC and Banking exams throw at you, just dressed up as "8 people sit around a circular table facing the centre…"

## The Big Secret: Puzzles Are Just Detective Work

Here's what most students do wrong: they try to hold all 5 conditions in their head at the same time. The brain can't juggle that many balls. So they panic, guess, and lose marks.

A detective never solves a case by thinking about all the clues at once. A detective asks: **"Which clue is the LOUDEST?"**

The loudest clue is the one that fixes something **exactly** — no "maybe", no "either/or". In our party:

- "Grandpa **must** sit at the head" → BOOM. Fixed. Draw it. This is your **anchor**.
- "Ravi and Kavi **always** sit together" → treat them as **one block** (glue them!).
- "Meena sits **exactly between** Sam and Priya" → another fixed pattern.

Only AFTER placing the loud clues do you touch the quiet ones ("not opposite", "not next to"). Negative conditions are **erasers** — they remove possibilities. Positive conditions are **pencils** — they draw things in. Pencil first, eraser later.

## The Unforgettable Trick: "LOUD → GLUE → ERASE"

1. **LOUD** — Find the condition that fixes a position 100%. Place it first. (Exact words: *must, exactly, immediately, at the end, second to the right of*.)
2. **GLUE** — Stick together people/things that must be a pair or block. Now 8 people become 6 units. Smaller puzzle!
3. **ERASE** — Apply the "NOT" conditions last. They only make sense once things are drawn.

And one golden habit: **draw, don't think.** A rough table, a row of 8 boxes, a small grid — the moment conditions live on paper, your brain is free to reason instead of remember. If a condition has two possibilities ("A sits 2nd or 4th"), make **two small diagrams** and let a later clue kill one of them. That's not cheating — that's strategy.

## Why This Works (The 12-Year-Old Test)

If you can seat your birthday guests without a family fight, you can solve any multi-condition puzzle — because the exam is the same party, only with "persons P, Q, R, S…" instead of your relatives. The names change. The detective method doesn't.
