Card 1
Front: What is the very first thing you should do before solving a multi-condition puzzle?
Back: Read ALL conditions once without writing, identify the fixed (definite) clues, and choose the right table/diagram format (grid, row, or circular) before placing anything.

Card 2
Front: In a seating puzzle, which clues should you place first — direct or indirect?
Back: Direct/definite clues first (e.g., "A sits 2nd to the left of B"). Indirect clues ("A sits somewhere to the left of B") are noted aside and used later.

Card 3
Front: What is a "possibility table" and when do you use it?
Back: When no clue fixes a position, draw 2–3 parallel cases (Case I, Case II) and keep eliminating. Use it when the puzzle has multiple valid starting points.

Card 4
Front: Mnemonic trick: how do you remember the order of solving a puzzle?
Back: "F-I-N-E": Fixed clues first → Indirect clues noted → Negative clues ("not") applied → Eliminate cases. Solve F-I-N-E, and the puzzle turns out fine!

Card 5
Front: Why are negative clues ("P is NOT next to Q") so powerful in multi-condition puzzles?
Back: They eliminate options without fixing anything. Apply them after placing direct clues — they often collapse two possible cases into one.

Card 6
Front: In a floor-based puzzle (8 floors), what is the safest representation?
Back: Draw a vertical column numbered 8 (top) to 1 (bottom). Never assume floor 1 is at top — the ground floor is floor 1 unless stated otherwise.

Card 7
Front: Examiner's trap: "A sits three places away from B" — what mistake do students make?
Back: They count 3 seats between A and B. "Three places away" means a gap of 3 positions (2 persons between them). Read gap wording very carefully.

Card 8
Front: In circular seating, what changes when people face OUTWARD instead of inward?
Back: Left and right reverse. For outward-facing people, "immediate left" moves anticlockwise (for inward-facing people it moves clockwise). Always mark the facing direction first.

Card 9
Front: What is the "linking chain" technique in multi-variable puzzles (person–city–colour–profession)?
Back: Find the one entity mentioned in the most clues, fix it first, then attach other attributes to it like links of a chain until the grid fills.

Card 10
Front: Mnemonic trick: how to handle "only/only if" conditions in selection puzzles?
Back: Remember "ONLY = MUST": "Only P goes" means P is compulsory; "P goes only if Q goes" means Q is compulsory whenever P is selected (P → Q).

Card 11
Front: Examiner's trap: in "A is taller than B but shorter than C", what error is common?
Back: Students reverse the chain. Correct order: C > A > B. Always convert each statement into an inequality immediately to avoid mid-solution confusion.

Card 12
Front: When should you abandon a case in the possibility method?
Back: The moment any single condition is violated — don't "adjust" the case. One contradiction = delete that case entirely and continue with the surviving one.

Card 13
Front: In scheduling puzzles (days/months), what is the most reliable first step?
Back: Fix the clues that mention exact dates/days or exact gaps ("X on 15th", "3 days between P and Q"). Relative clues ("before", "after") come later.

Card 14
Front: What does "between A and B" usually NOT guarantee in a linear row?
Back: It does not mean immediately between. Unless "exactly between" or "midway" is stated, the person just lies somewhere in the interval — a classic trap.

Card 15
Front: How do you handle a puzzle where two people have identical clue sets?
Back: Mark them as interchangeable (X/Y) in the same slot-pair. Questions will only ask what is true for both — don't waste time deciding which is which.

Card 16
Front: What is the time rule for a 5-question puzzle set in SSC CGL/Bank exams?
Back: Invest a maximum of 4–5 minutes for the whole set. If the base arrangement isn't fixed in 2.5–3 minutes, skip and return — a stuck puzzle eats easy marks elsewhere.

Card 17
Front: In box/stack puzzles, what does "two boxes between P and Q" precisely mean?
Back: Exactly two boxes in the gap, so P and Q are 3 positions apart (e.g., positions 2 and 5). Confusing "boxes between" with "positions apart" is a frequent error.

Card 18
Front: Why should you re-read all conditions after completing the arrangement?
Back: To verify every single condition against the final grid. One overlooked "not" clue can make all 5 questions wrong — a 30-second check saves the whole set.

Card 19
Front: In blood-relation + puzzle hybrids, what should be drawn separately?
Back: Draw the family tree first, completely, before touching the seating/arrangement part. Mixing both in one diagram causes relationship-direction errors.

Card 20
Front: What is the single biggest reason students lose marks in multi-condition puzzles?
Back: Starting to place clues before reading all of them — they commit to a wrong case early. Read fully, pick the strongest clue, and only then draw.
