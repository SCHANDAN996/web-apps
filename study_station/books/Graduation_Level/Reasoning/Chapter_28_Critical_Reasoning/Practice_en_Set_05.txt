101. Statement: "All employees who work overtime receive a bonus. Some employees who receive a bonus are promoted." Which conclusion definitely follows?
(a) No employee who works overtime is promoted (b) All promoted employees work overtime (c) Some employees who work overtime are promoted (d) Some employees who receive a bonus work overtime
Answer: (d)
Solution: The tempting option links promotion to overtime workers, but "some bonus-receivers are promoted" says nothing about overtime — the promoted ones may be non-overtime bonus-receivers. From "all overtime workers receive a bonus," it directly follows that some bonus-receivers are overtime workers, so the answer is that some employees who receive a bonus work overtime.

102. Argument: "The city should ban all private cars from the city centre because this will reduce air pollution." Which of the following, if true, most weakens the argument?
(a) Most air pollution in the city centre comes from factories and buses, not private cars (b) Other cities have banned cars from their centres (c) Air pollution is harmful to health (d) Some citizens support the ban
Answer: (a)
Solution: Options supporting the ban or giving background do not weaken it. If most pollution comes from factories and buses, banning private cars will not meaningfully reduce pollution, which directly undercuts the stated reason, so the answer is that most pollution comes from factories and buses, not private cars.

103. Statement: "Whenever it rains, the match is cancelled. The match was cancelled." What can be concluded?
(a) It rained (b) It did not rain (c) The match may or may not have been cancelled due to rain (d) Rain is the only reason matches are cancelled
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is affirming the consequent — assuming cancellation proves rain. Rain guarantees cancellation, but cancellation can have other causes, so rain is not certain; the answer is that the match may or may not have been cancelled due to rain.

104. Argument: "Our school introduced a new teaching method, and this year exam results improved. Therefore, the new method caused the improvement." Which assumption underlies this argument?
(a) Students liked the new method (b) Exam results always improve every year (c) The new method is used in other schools (d) No other significant change affecting results occurred during this period
Answer: (d)
Solution: A correlation-to-causation argument needs the assumption that no alternative explanation exists. If another change, such as an easier exam or a better intake, caused the improvement, the argument collapses, so the answer is that no other significant change affecting results occurred during this period.

105. Statement: "Only those who register before Friday can attend the workshop. Ravi attended the workshop." What follows?
(a) Ravi may not have registered at all (b) Ravi registered after Friday (c) Registration date cannot be determined (d) Ravi registered before Friday
Answer: (d)
Solution: "Only those who register before Friday can attend" makes early registration a necessary condition for attendance. Since Ravi attended, he must have registered before Friday, so the answer is that Ravi registered before Friday.

(a) Meena may not actually be kind (b) Kindness and honesty are not shown to be relevant qualifications for the job (c) Other candidates may also be kind (d) The speaker may be biased
Answer: (b)
Solution: The core flaw is irrelevance: personal virtues are offered as a reason to hire without linking them to job requirements. Even if she is kind, that alone does not justify hiring, so the answer is that kindness and honesty are not shown to be relevant qualifications for the job.

107. Statement: "In a survey, 80% of people who exercised daily reported good health." Which conclusion is most justified?
(a) People with good health always exercise (b) There is an association between daily exercise and reported good health in the survey (c) Exercise causes good health (d) 20% of exercisers are unhealthy
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is converting survey correlation into causation or reversing it. The data only supports an association within the surveyed group, so the answer is that there is an association between daily exercise and reported good health in the survey.

108. Argument: "Company X's profits rose after it cut its workforce. Therefore, cutting the workforce increased profits." Which of the following, if true, most strengthens the argument?
(a) Sales, prices, and costs other than wages remained essentially unchanged during the period (b) Company X plans further cuts (c) The laid-off workers were unhappy (d) Company X's competitors also saw profit changes
Answer: (a)
Solution: Strengthening a causal claim means ruling out rival causes. If all other profit-affecting factors stayed constant, the workforce cut is more plausibly the cause, so the answer is that sales, prices, and other costs remained essentially unchanged.

109. Statement: "All lawyers in this firm are graduates. Some graduates in this firm are not lawyers." Which Venn-style relationship is consistent with this?
(a) The two sets are disjoint (b) Graduates are a subset of lawyers (c) Lawyers and graduates are identical sets (d) The set of lawyers is a proper subset of graduates
Answer: (d)
Solution: "All lawyers are graduates" puts lawyers inside graduates; "some graduates are not lawyers" means graduates extend beyond lawyers, making lawyers a proper subset. The identical-sets option contradicts the second statement, so the answer is that the set of lawyers is a proper subset of graduates.

110. Argument: "Most successful entrepreneurs wake up early. Therefore, waking up early leads to success." The reasoning error is:
(a) Circular reasoning (b) Confusing correlation with causation (c) Appeal to authority (d) Hasty generalisation from one case
Answer: (b)
Solution: The argument moves from an observed association — early rising among the successful — to a causal claim without evidence of causation. This is the classic correlation-causation error, so the answer is confusing correlation with causation.

111. Statement: "If the bill is passed, taxes will rise. Taxes did not rise." What follows?
(a) The bill was passed (b) The bill may have been passed (c) The bill was not passed (d) Taxes are unrelated to the bill
Answer: (c)
Solution: This is valid contraposition (modus tollens): "if passed then taxes rise" plus "taxes did not rise" entails the bill was not passed. The trap is treating the conditional as uncertain after the consequent fails, so the answer is that the bill was not passed.

116. Argument: "We should not trust Dr. Shah's research on nutrition because he once failed a chemistry exam in college." This is an example of:
(a) Straw man (b) Slippery slope (c) False dilemma (d) Ad hominem
Answer: (d)
Solution: The argument attacks the person — an old exam failure — rather than the research itself. This is a textbook ad hominem fallacy, so the answer is ad hominem.

117. Statement: "Either the project is delayed or the budget is increased. The budget was not increased." What follows?
(a) The project is not delayed (b) The project is delayed (c) The budget will be increased later (d) Nothing follows
Answer: (b)
Solution: In an either-or statement, ruling out one alternative confirms the other. Since the budget was not increased, the project must be delayed, so the answer is that the project is delayed.

119. Statement: "All A are B. No B is C." Which conclusion follows?
(a) All C are A (b) Some A are C (c) No A is C (d) Some B are A
Answer: (c)
Solution: Since every A is inside B and B has no overlap with C, A cannot overlap with C either. The trap is assuming partial overlap; the definite conclusion is that no A is C, so the answer is that no A is C.

(a) The committee members are experts (b) A well-written proposal would not have been rejected by the committee (c) The proposal was long (d) The committee always rejects proposals
Answer: (b)
Solution: To infer poor writing from rejection, one must assume rejection implies poor writing — that is, a well-written proposal would have been accepted. Without this, other reasons for rejection remain open, so the answer is that a well-written proposal would not have been rejected by the committee.

123. Statement: "Only if the witness testifies will the accused be convicted. The accused was not convicted." What follows?
(a) The witness did not testify (b) The witness may or may not have testified (c) The witness refused to testify (d) The witness testified
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is treating a necessary condition as sufficient. Testimony is necessary for conviction, but the absence of conviction tells us nothing about whether the witness testified — the testimony could have occurred yet failed to convince, so the answer is that the witness may or may not have testified.

124. Argument: "In Town A, households with more books have children with higher test scores. To raise scores, the government should distribute free books to all households." The argument is weakest because:
(a) Books are expensive (b) Town A is small (c) Some children do not read (d) It assumes the mere presence of books causes higher scores, ignoring factors like parental education that may drive both
Answer: (d)
Solution: The argument confuses a correlation — books in the home and high scores — with causation; a common underlying factor such as parental education could explain both, so simply distributing books may not raise scores. Hence the answer is that it assumes the mere presence of books causes higher scores while ignoring factors like parental education.
Source: PYQ-style (Critical reasoning pattern)

125. Statement: "If the machine overheats, it shuts down. If it shuts down, production stops. Production did not stop." What follows?
(a) The machine shut down but did not overheat (b) The machine overheated (c) The machine did not shut down and did not overheat (d) Nothing definite follows
Answer: (c)
Solution: This is a chained conditional with double contraposition: production not stopping implies no shutdown, and no shutdown implies no overheating. The trap is denying only one link; both negations follow, so the answer is that the machine did not shut down and did not overheat.
