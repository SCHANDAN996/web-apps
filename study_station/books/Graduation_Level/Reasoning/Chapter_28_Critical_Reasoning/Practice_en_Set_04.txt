76. Statement: "Our company should switch entirely to electric vehicles for its delivery fleet, because electric vehicles produce zero tailpipe emissions." Which of the following, if true, most weakens the argument?
(a) Electric vehicles are more expensive to purchase than diesel vehicles (b) Electric vehicles require less frequent maintenance than diesel vehicles (c) Some employees prefer driving diesel vehicles (d) The electricity used to charge the fleet is generated mainly from coal-fired power plants
Answer: (d)
Solution: Option (a) is a tempting trap because higher cost seems like a weakness, but the argument is specifically about emissions, not cost. If the charging electricity comes from coal, the overall emissions may not fall, directly undercutting the stated reason. So the answer is (d).

77. Statement: "All successful entrepreneurs in this survey woke up before 6 AM. Therefore, waking up before 6 AM leads to entrepreneurial success." The reasoning is flawed because it:
(a) confuses correlation with causation (b) relies on an unrepresentative sample of entrepreneurs (c) uses emotionally loaded language (d) appeals to an unqualified authority
Answer: (a)
Solution: Option (b) is tempting since surveys can be biased, but the core flaw named here is treating an observed association (early rising among the successful) as proof that early rising causes success. That is the classic correlation-causation error. So the answer is (a).

78. Argument: "City X banned plastic bags, and within a year plastic waste in its landfills fell by 30%. Therefore, the ban caused the reduction." Which of the following, if true, most strengthens the argument?
(a) Plastic bags are harmful to marine life (b) City X also ran a public awareness campaign about recycling (c) Other cities that did not ban plastic bags saw no change in plastic waste during the same period (d) Some shopkeepers in City X opposed the ban
Answer: (c)
Solution: Option (b) is the trap — it introduces an alternative cause, which weakens rather than strengthens. A control comparison showing unchanged waste in cities without the ban supports the ban as the cause. So the answer is (c).

79. Statement: "Only students who scored above 90% in mathematics were admitted to the advanced physics programme. Ravi was admitted to the advanced physics programme." What can be concluded with certainty?
(a) Ravi scored the highest marks in mathematics in his class (b) Ravi scored above 90% in mathematics (c) Ravi is exceptionally good at physics (d) Ravi will succeed in the programme
Answer: (b)
Solution: Option (a) is the trap — "above 90%" does not mean the highest in class. Since admission required a score above 90% and Ravi was admitted, his score must have exceeded 90%. So the answer is (b).

80. Argument: "We must either increase taxes or cut public services. Since nobody wants public services cut, we must increase taxes." The argument assumes that:
(a) increasing taxes is popular among voters (b) there is no third option for balancing the budget (c) public services are currently overfunded (d) tax increases always improve public services
Answer: (b)
Solution: Option (d) is tempting but irrelevant — the argument is about necessity, not outcomes. A false-dilemma argument works only if the two options presented are truly exhaustive, so it assumes no third option exists. So the answer is (b).

81. Statement: "A study found that people who drink one glass of red wine daily have healthier hearts than those who drink none." Which of the following most weakens the conclusion that red wine improves heart health?

Answer: (c)
Solution: Option (a) is the trap — it supports the wine-health link rather than weakening it. If wine drinkers' better health comes from wealth-related factors, the wine itself may not be the cause. So the answer is (c).

Solution: Option (a) is tempting but cost says nothing about quality of learning. If online students differ systematically (less study time), the lower scores reflect the students' circumstances, not the medium's inferiority. So the answer is (c).

84. Statement: "If the monsoon is normal, crop production will rise. Crop production did not rise this year." What can be concluded?
(a) The monsoon was not normal (b) The monsoon was normal (c) Crop production will rise next year (d) Farmers used poor quality seeds
Answer: (a)
Solution: Option (d) is the trap — it invents an outside explanation the logic does not address. By modus tollens, if normal monsoon implies rising production and production did not rise, the monsoon was not normal. So the answer is (a).

Answer: (a)
Solution: Option (b) is the trap — the argument needs only that space leadership brings respect, not that it is the sole route. The recommendation to spend money presupposes that global respect is valuable enough to justify the cost. So the answer is (a).

86. Statement: "All engineers in this firm who know Python were given the automation project. Some engineers who know Python were also given the web project." What must be true?
(a) All engineers given the web project know Python (b) Some engineers given the automation project were also given the web project (c) No engineer knows both Python and Java (d) Engineers without Python knowledge received no projects
Answer: (b)
Solution: Option (a) is the trap — it reverses the direction of "some"; knowing Python does not follow from getting the web project. Since all Python-knowing engineers got the automation project and some of them also got the web project, some automation-project engineers also got the web project. So the answer is (b).

87. Argument: "Crime in the city rose after the new mayor took office, so the mayor's policies have failed." Which of the following identifies the flaw in this reasoning?
(a) It assumes that because one event followed another, the first caused the second (b) It attacks the mayor personally rather than the policies (c) It relies on statistics from an unreliable source (d) It generalises from a single case of crime
Answer: (a)
Solution: Option (b) is the trap — there is no personal attack here, only a causal claim. The flaw is post hoc reasoning: inferring causation merely from sequence in time. So the answer is (a).

95. Argument: "Most accidents happen within five kilometres of home, so driving near home is more dangerous than driving far away." The flaw in this argument is that it:
(a) relies on data from only one city (b) assumes all accidents are equally serious (c) fails to define what counts as an accident (d) ignores that most driving occurs close to home, so more accidents there are expected
Answer: (d)
Solution: Option (b) is the trap — accident severity is irrelevant to the location claim. The argument ignores base rates: if most driving happens near home, most accidents will too, without near-home driving being riskier per trip. So the answer is (d).

96. Statement: "All members of the chess club are students. Some students are members of the drama society. No member of the drama society is a member of the chess club." Which of the following must be true?
(a) All students are members of the chess club (b) No student is a member of both clubs (c) Some students are not members of the chess club (d) All chess club members are also drama society members
Answer: (c)
Solution: Option (b) is the trap — the statements only bar drama members from the chess club; they do not discuss chess members joining drama, and "must be true" requires certainty. Since some students are drama members and no drama member is in the chess club, those students are students outside the chess club. So the answer is (c).

97. Argument: "The new traffic law reduced accidents at junctions where cameras were installed. Therefore, cameras should be installed at every junction in the country." Which of the following is an assumption underlying this argument?
(a) Cameras are the cheapest road safety measure available (b) All drivers dislike traffic cameras (c) The reduction in accidents at camera junctions was not caused by other simultaneous changes (d) Every junction in the country has the same traffic volume
Answer: (c)
Solution: Option (d) is the trap — the argument generalises across junctions but does not require identical traffic volumes, only that cameras work. For the recommendation to follow, the observed reduction must actually be due to the cameras, not confounding factors. So the answer is (c).

98. Statement: "Either the project will be completed on time or the client will impose a penalty. The client did not impose a penalty." What follows?
(a) The project was delayed (b) The project was completed on time (c) The client cancelled the contract (d) The project was abandoned
Answer: (b)
Solution: Option (a) is the trap — delay without penalty would contradict the given "either-or" statement, so it cannot be concluded. By disjunctive syllogism, since the penalty did not occur, the other alternative must hold: the project was completed on time. So the answer is (b).

99. Argument: "A famous actor endorsed this brand of tea, so it must be of superior quality." This argument commits the fallacy of:
(a) appeal to irrelevant authority (b) attacking the person instead of the argument (c) circular reasoning (d) hasty generalisation
Answer: (a)
Solution: Option (d) is the trap — no sample or generalisation from instances is involved here. An actor's expertise lies in acting, not tea quality, so using the endorsement as proof of quality is an appeal to irrelevant authority. So the answer is (a).

100. Statement: "In a survey, employees who worked from home reported higher job satisfaction than office-based employees. The company concluded that working from home causes higher satisfaction." Which of the following, if true, most weakens the conclusion?
(a) Home-based employees saved commuting time (b) Employees were allowed to choose their work arrangement, and those already more satisfied with their jobs tended to choose working from home (c) Office-based employees had longer working hours on paper (d) The survey was conducted online
Answer: (b)
Solution: Option (a) is the trap — saving commuting time could support the causal story rather than weaken it. If satisfaction came first and drove the choice to work from home, the direction of causation is reversed, undermining the company's conclusion. So the answer is (b).
