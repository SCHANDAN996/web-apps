# 🪄 Short Tricks & Mnemonics — Critical Reasoning

---

**BOX 1 — The "C-P-A" Skeleton**
Every argument is a body: **C**onclusion = head, **P**remise = legs, **A**ssumption = the invisible spine.
> *"No spine, no stand."* If you can't find the hidden assumption, the argument can't stand — and neither can your answer.

---

**BOX 2 — "WEAKEN = Attack the Bridge"**
To weaken, don't attack the premises (they're given as true). Attack the **bridge** between premise and conclusion.
> Mnemonic: **"Don't burn the house, cut the bridge."**
The house (premise) stays; the connection (assumption) collapses.

---

**BOX 3 — "STRENGTHEN = Add a Pillar"**
A strengthen option is a new pillar under the same bridge.
> **"Same bridge, new pillar."** If an option talks about a *different* bridge (new topic), it's out — no matter how true it sounds.

---

**BOX 4 — The "NEGATION TEST" for Assumptions**
Assume the opposite of the option. If the argument **dies**, that option is the assumption.
> **"Kill it to check it."** Negate → argument collapses → correct answer. Negate → argument survives → wrong option.

---

**BOX 5 — "MUST-BE-TRUE stays in the room"**
For inference/conclusion questions, the answer must be built **only** from the passage.
> **"If it needs outside air, it isn't there."** Any option importing outside facts, extreme words (*always, never, all*), or future predictions = reject.

---

**BOX 6 — "Extreme Words = Extreme Danger"**
In inference questions, circle: *all, none, always, never, only, completely.*
> **"Absolutes are absolutely suspicious."** The correct inference usually whispers (*may, some, likely*); the trap shouts.

---

**BOX 7 — "FLAW = Find the Leap"**
A flawed argument **leaps** from evidence to conclusion. Name the leap:
> **"Where's the jump?"** Common leaps: part→whole, one case→all cases, correlation→causation, old data→future claim.

---

**BOX 8 — "C vs C: Correlation is not Causation"**
Two things happening together ≠ one causing the other.
> **"Ice cream and drowning both rise in summer — ice cream doesn't drown people."** Third factor (heat) is the hidden culprit. Look for the "third factor" option.

---

**BOX 9 — "PARADOX: Both Sides True"**
In paradox questions, never deny either fact. The answer **holds both hands**.
> **"Don't pick a side, build the bridge."** Correct option explains *how both can be true together*.

---

**BOX 10 — "BOLD FACE = Role Play"**
For boldface questions, ask of each bold part: *Is it the main conclusion, a premise, or an opposing point?*
> **"Give every bold line a job title."** Then match job titles to options — ignore the topic, match the **role**.

---

**BOX 11 — "EVALUATE = The Yes/No Test"**
For "which is most useful to evaluate?" questions, the right option is a question whose **Yes and No answers push the argument in opposite directions**.
> **"Yes helps, No hurts — that's the one."** If Yes/No both leave the argument unchanged, reject.

---

**BOX 12 — "CONCLUSION sits at the WHY test"**
Ask "WHY should I believe this?" — the statement that *answers* is the premise; the statement that *needs* answers is the conclusion.
> **"Because → premise. Therefore → conclusion."** Signal words: *because, since* (premise) vs *thus, hence, so* (conclusion).

---

**BOX 13 — "STRONG Argument = Direct + Relevant"**
In statement-argument questions, a strong argument hits the issue **directly** with a real reason.
> **"Emotion is weak, reason is strong."** Options based on fear, tradition, or "everyone does it" = weak arguments.

---

**BOX 14 — "COURSE OF ACTION: Practical, Legal, Logical"**
A valid course of action must pass three gates:
> **"Can we? May we? Does it solve?"** — feasible, lawful/fair, and actually addresses the problem. Extreme or revenge-style actions = reject.

---

**BOX 15 — "Read the Question FIRST"**
Before the passage, read the question stem.
> **"Know the hunt before the forest."** Weaken-hunt, assumption-hunt, and inference-hunt need different eyes — 5 seconds here saves 30 later.

---

## ⏭️ Skip Strategy — When to Leave the Question

1. **The 40-Second Rule:** If after 40 seconds you haven't identified the conclusion and the question type, **mark and move on**. Critical Reasoning questions eat time silently — a stuck CR question costs you two easy questions elsewhere.

2. **Two options both "feel right":** If after applying the Negation Test / Extreme-Words check you still can't separate two options, **skip rather than gamble** — examiners design exactly one such trap pair per question, and blind guessing here has a negative-marking cost.

3. **Long passage + Boldface/Evaluate type:** If the passage is 6+ lines *and* the question is a boldface or evaluate type (the most time-hungry formats), attempt it **only in the second pass**. First finish all short inference/assumption questions, then return with fresh time.
