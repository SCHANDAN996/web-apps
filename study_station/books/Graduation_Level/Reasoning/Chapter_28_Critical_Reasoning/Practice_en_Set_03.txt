51. Statement: "The government should ban all private vehicles in city centres to reduce air pollution." Which of the following, if true, most weakens this argument?
(a) Air pollution is a serious health hazard in cities (b) Many cities abroad have banned private vehicles (c) Most city-centre pollution comes from industrial units, not private vehicles (d) Public transport is cheaper than private vehicles
Answer: (c)
Solution: The tempting trap is the claim that air pollution is a serious health hazard, which supports the ban rather than weakening it — always check whether the question asks to weaken or strengthen. The argument assumes private vehicles are the main pollution source; if industrial units cause most pollution, banning vehicles will not achieve the goal, so the answer is that most city-centre pollution comes from industrial units.
Source: PYQ-style (Critical Reasoning — Weaken the Argument)

52. Statement: "All successful entrepreneurs are risk-takers. Some risk-takers fail in their ventures." Which conclusion definitely follows?
(a) No definite conclusion about entrepreneurs failing follows (b) Some entrepreneurs fail in their ventures (c) All risk-takers are entrepreneurs (d) All who fail are risk-takers
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is the conclusion that some entrepreneurs fail: it feels plausible, but "some risk-takers fail" does not say those risk-takers are entrepreneurs — the two sets may not overlap there. Reversing "all entrepreneurs are risk-takers" is also invalid, so the answer is that no definite conclusion about entrepreneurs failing follows.
Source: PYQ-style (Critical Reasoning — Syllogistic Conclusion)

53. Statement: "Students who study with a fixed timetable score higher in exams." Which assumption is implicit?
(a) A fixed timetable can influence exam performance (b) Students without timetables never score well (c) Timetables are easy to prepare (d) Exams test only memory
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is the claim that students without timetables never score well: "never" is an extreme word, and extreme assumptions are rarely implicit. The statement claims a causal link between timetables and higher scores, which presupposes that timetables can affect performance, so the answer is that a fixed timetable can influence exam performance.
Source: PYQ-style (Critical Reasoning — Implicit Assumption)

54. Statement: "Company X launched a costly advertising campaign, yet its sales fell." Which of the following best explains the paradox?
(a) Advertising is a waste of money (b) Advertising always increases sales (c) Company X has many competitors (d) The campaign ran during an economic slowdown when overall demand collapsed
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is the existence of many competitors: that is a standing fact and does not explain why sales fell specifically after the campaign. A paradox is resolved by a new factor that reconciles both facts; an economic slowdown explains high spending plus falling sales, so the answer is that the campaign ran during an economic slowdown.
Source: PYQ-style (Critical Reasoning — Resolve the Paradox)

55. Statement: "Only those who practise daily can master classical music. Ravi has mastered classical music." What follows?
(a) Daily practice guarantees mastery (b) Ravi practises daily (c) Ravi may practise daily (d) Ravi is talented
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is "Ravi may practise daily": "only those who practise daily" makes practice a necessary condition, so mastery forces daily practice — "may" understates a certainty. Claiming that practice guarantees mastery reverses necessity into sufficiency, another classic trap, so the answer is that Ravi practises daily.
Source: PYQ-style (Critical Reasoning — Necessary Condition)

56. Statement: "Online education will completely replace classroom teaching within five years." Which of the following, if true, most strengthens this claim?
(a) Many students own smartphones (b) Some teachers dislike technology (c) Classroom teaching is centuries old (d) Internet penetration is rising rapidly and schools are already shifting core courses online
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is smartphone ownership: it only shows access to a device, not a trend toward replacement of classrooms. Strengthening needs evidence that the predicted shift is already underway; rising internet use plus schools moving core courses online does that, so the answer is that internet penetration is rising and schools are shifting courses online.
Source: PYQ-style (Critical Reasoning — Strengthen the Argument)

57. Statement: "Whenever the factory runs the night shift, the nearby hospital reports more cases of sleep disorders." Conclusion drawn: "The night shift causes sleep disorders in the neighbourhood." The reasoning is flawed because:
(a) Hospitals always exaggerate reports (b) Sleep disorders are hereditary (c) It assumes correlation proves causation, ignoring factors like night-shift noise or stress (d) Night shifts are illegal
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is the heredity point: even if sleep disorders are partly hereditary, that does not identify the logical flaw in this particular argument. The argument jumps from "two things occur together" to "one causes the other" without ruling out alternatives, so the answer is that it assumes correlation proves causation.
Source: PYQ-style (Critical Reasoning — Flawed Reasoning)

58. Statement: "If the monsoon is normal, crop production rises. Crop production has not risen this year." What follows?
(a) The monsoon was not normal (b) The monsoon was normal (c) Nothing definite follows about the monsoon (d) Farmers used poor seeds
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is "nothing definite follows": that would be true if production had risen (affirming the consequent), but here the consequent is denied. From "if P then Q" and "not Q", logic gives "not P" (modus tollens), so the answer is that the monsoon was not normal.
Source: PYQ-style (Critical Reasoning — Modus Tollens)

59. Statement: "The city should build more flyovers to end traffic jams." Which assumption is implicit?
(a) Flyovers are cheap to build (b) The city has unlimited funds (c) People will stop using cars (d) Traffic jams are caused mainly by insufficient road capacity at junctions
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is the cheapness of flyovers: cost is irrelevant to whether the proposal will work. Recommending flyovers presupposes that jams stem from capacity bottlenecks that flyovers can relieve, so the answer is that jams are caused mainly by insufficient road capacity at junctions.
Source: PYQ-style (Critical Reasoning — Implicit Assumption)

60. Statement: "All lawyers in this firm are graduates. Some graduates in this firm are not advocates." Which conclusion definitely follows?
(a) Some lawyers are not advocates (b) All advocates are graduates (c) Some graduates in the firm may not be lawyers (d) No lawyer is a graduate
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is "some lawyers are not advocates": the graduates who are not advocates could still all be non-lawyers, so nothing about lawyers follows. Since "all lawyers are graduates" does not mean all graduates are lawyers, the answer is that some graduates in the firm may not be lawyers.
Source: PYQ-style (Critical Reasoning — Syllogism)

61. Statement: "A survey found that people who eat breakfast daily are healthier." Claim: "Eating breakfast causes good health." Which of the following most weakens the claim?
(a) Health-conscious people both eat breakfast and exercise regularly (b) Breakfast cereals are fortified with vitamins (c) Some people skip breakfast (d) Breakfast is the first meal of the day
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is vitamin fortification: it supports a health link rather than weakening it. A third factor — health consciousness — producing both breakfast-eating and good health breaks the causal claim, so the answer is that health-conscious people both eat breakfast and exercise regularly.
Source: PYQ-style (Critical Reasoning — Third-Factor Weakening)

62. Statement: "Unless the accused confesses, we cannot convict him." Which of the following is logically equivalent?
(a) If the accused does not confess, he cannot be convicted (b) If the accused confesses, he will be convicted (c) The accused will confess (d) Confession is sufficient for conviction
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is "if he confesses, he will be convicted": "unless P, not Q" makes confession necessary, not sufficient — confessing does not guarantee conviction. The correct restatement of a necessary condition is that without confession there is no conviction, so the answer is that if the accused does not confess, he cannot be convicted.
Source: PYQ-style (Critical Reasoning — Unless Condition)

63. Statement: "Our competitor's product failed in the market, so our similar product will surely succeed." The argument is weakest because:
(a) Success of one product never predicts another (b) It ignores that the competitor's failure may have been due to poor marketing, not the product type (c) Competitors always copy each other (d) The market is unpredictable
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is "the market is unpredictable": it is vague and does not pinpoint the flaw. The argument assumes the competitor failed because of the product type; if the real cause was marketing, the conclusion collapses, so the answer is that it ignores alternative causes of the competitor's failure.
Source: PYQ-style (Critical Reasoning — Weakness in Argument)

64. Statement: "Most toppers of the exam studied from NCERT books." Conclusion: "Studying from NCERT books guarantees a top rank." The error in the conclusion is:
(a) Surveys are unreliable (b) NCERT books are too easy (c) Toppers never reveal their methods (d) It confuses a common factor among toppers with a guarantee of success
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is doubting the survey: the survey itself is not being questioned; the misuse of its finding is. "Most toppers used X" does not mean "using X makes one a topper" — many non-toppers also used NCERT, so the answer is that it confuses a common factor with a guarantee of success.
Source: PYQ-style (Critical Reasoning — Hasty Generalisation)

65. Statement: "The railway should increase fares to improve services." Which of the following, if true, most supports this proposal?
(a) Fares have not risen for three years (b) Past fare hikes were directly followed by measurable improvements in punctuality and cleanliness (c) Passengers dislike fare hikes (d) Road transport is costlier than rail
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is the three-year gap since the last hike: that does not show hikes improve services. Evidence that previous hikes actually produced better services directly supports the causal claim, so the answer is that past fare hikes were followed by measurable service improvements.
Source: PYQ-style (Critical Reasoning — Strengthen the Proposal)

66. Statement: "Either the project was delayed due to funding issues or due to staff shortage. The project had adequate funding." What follows?
(a) Nothing can be concluded (b) The project was not delayed (c) Funding issues caused the delay (d) The project was delayed due to staff shortage
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is "nothing can be concluded": with an either-or statement, eliminating one branch forces the other, since the delay itself is given in the statement. Adequate funding rules out the first branch, so the answer is that the project was delayed due to staff shortage.
Source: PYQ-style (Critical Reasoning — Disjunctive Syllogism)

67. Statement: "Banning plastic bags alone will solve the city's waste problem." Which of the following best exposes the flaw?
(a) Paper bags are a good alternative (b) Plastic bags are harmful to animals (c) Plastic bags form only a small fraction of the city's total waste (d) Other cities have banned plastic bags
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is the harm to animals: it supports the ban but does not test whether the ban solves the overall waste problem. If plastic bags are only a small fraction of total waste, banning them cannot "solve" the problem, so the answer is that plastic bags form only a small fraction of the city's total waste.
Source: PYQ-style (Critical Reasoning — Overstated Solution)

68. Statement: "No honest officer has ever been transferred from this district. Officer Meena has been transferred from this district." What follows?
(a) Meena took bribes (b) Meena is not honest (c) Nothing definite follows about Meena (d) All transferred officers in the country are dishonest
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is "nothing definite follows": that would apply if the premise were "all transferred officers are dishonest", but here the premise is "no honest officer is transferred", whose contrapositive is "whoever is transferred is not honest". Since Meena has been transferred, the answer is that Meena is not honest.
Source: PYQ-style (Critical Reasoning — Contrapositive)

69. Statement: "People who drink green tea daily live longer." Which additional information would most strengthen a causal interpretation?
(a) Green tea is popular in many countries (b) Green tea contains antioxidants (c) The longevity difference persists even after accounting for diet, income and exercise (d) Some green-tea drinkers also smoke
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is the antioxidant point: it suggests a mechanism but does not rule out confounding factors in the observed data. Controlling for other variables isolates green tea's effect, so the answer is that the difference persists even after accounting for diet, income and exercise.
Source: PYQ-style (Critical Reasoning — Controlled Variables)

70. Statement: "If the team wins today, it qualifies for the finals. The team qualified for the finals." What follows?
(a) The team won today (b) The team may have qualified through another route (c) The team lost today (d) The match was drawn
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is concluding the team won today: affirming the consequent is a classic fallacy, since other results could also secure qualification. Qualifying does not prove today's win, so the answer is that the team may have qualified through another route.
Source: PYQ-style (Critical Reasoning — Affirming the Consequent Trap)

71. Statement: "A famous actor endorsed a brand of honey, so the honey must be pure and healthy." The argument commits the fallacy of:
(a) Appeal to authority — an actor's fame is not evidence about product quality (b) Circular reasoning (c) False dilemma (d) Slippery slope
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is circular reasoning: the conclusion is not merely restating the premise; it relies on an irrelevant authority. Trusting a celebrity's word on a matter outside his expertise is appeal to authority, so the answer is appeal to authority.
Source: PYQ-style (Critical Reasoning — Fallacy Identification)

72. Statement: "Crime rate in the city rose after the new police commissioner took charge, so the commissioner is incompetent." Which of the following most weakens this argument?
(a) The previous commissioner was popular (b) The commissioner is highly decorated (c) Crime is a serious issue (d) A state-wide crime surge occurred in the same period, including cities with unchanged policing
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is the commissioner's decorations: they refer to past performance, not current effectiveness. A state-wide surge shows crime rose even without this commissioner, breaking the causal link, so the answer is that a state-wide crime surge occurred in the same period.
Source: PYQ-style (Critical Reasoning — Alternative Cause)

73. Statement: "Only candidates with a valid admit card may enter the exam hall. Sita entered the exam hall." What follows?
(a) Admit cards are checked strictly (b) Sita has a valid admit card (c) Sita bribed the guard (d) Sita may have a valid admit card
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is "Sita may have a valid admit card": "only" makes the admit card a necessary condition, so entry guarantees it — "may" wrongly weakens a certain inference. Since Sita entered and entry requires the card, the answer is that Sita has a valid admit card.
Source: PYQ-style (Critical Reasoning — Necessary Condition)

74. Statement: "To reduce road accidents, the speed limit on highways should be lowered from 100 to 80 km/h." Which assumption is implicit?
(a) A significant share of highway accidents is linked to high speeds (b) Highways are poorly maintained (c) Lower speeds save fuel (d) Drivers always obey speed limits
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is assuming drivers always obey limits: the proposal needs only enough compliance to matter, not universal obedience — "always" is an extreme word. The proposal presupposes speed is a major accident factor, so the answer is that a significant share of highway accidents is linked to high speeds.
Source: PYQ-style (Critical Reasoning — Implicit Assumption)

75. Statement: "All poets are imaginative. Some imaginative people are not writers. Ram is a poet." Which conclusion definitely follows?
(a) Some poets are not writers (b) Ram is a writer (c) Ram is imaginative (d) Ram is not a writer
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is "some poets are not writers": the imaginative non-writers may all lie outside the poets, so nothing about poets and writers follows. Since all poets are imaginative and Ram is a poet, the definite conclusion is that Ram is imaginative.
Source: PYQ-style (Critical Reasoning — Syllogism)
