1. Statement: "All students who study regularly pass the exam." Ravi studies regularly. What can be concluded?
(a) Ravi does not need to study (b) Ravi may fail the exam (c) Ravi will definitely pass the exam (d) Ravi is the best student
Answer: (c)
Solution: The statement is a universal rule: regular study leads to passing, and Ravi satisfies the condition. The tempting wrong choice introduces doubt, but a universal premise leaves no room for "may fail", so the answer is that Ravi will definitely pass the exam.

2. Statement: "Only those who are members can enter the club." Sita entered the club. What follows?
(a) Sita is a guest (b) Sita knows the owner (c) Sita is a member (d) Sita broke the rules
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Only members can enter" means entry implies membership, so Sita's entry proves she is a member. The trap is assuming she could be a guest, but the rule permits no exceptions, so the answer is that Sita is a member.

(a) It rained (b) The ground was wet (c) It did not rain (d) It may or may not have rained
Answer: (d)
Solution: Cancellation could have other causes; affirming the consequent is a classic fallacy. The trap is concluding rain with certainty, but the rule only works from rain to cancellation, not backwards, so the answer is that it may or may not have rained.

21. Argument: "Our school's results improved after we hired a new principal, so the new principal caused the improvement." Which option points out the flaw most precisely?
(a) The principal may be experienced (b) The improvement may have resulted from other changes, such as a new syllabus or better intake of students that year (c) School results depend on many factors (d) The principal works very hard
Answer: (b)
Solution: The flaw is post hoc reasoning — assuming sequence proves causation. Naming concrete alternative causes is more precise than the vague "many factors" trap, so the answer is that the improvement may have resulted from other changes, such as a new syllabus or better intake of students that year.

24. Statement: "All successful candidates cleared the cut-off. Some who cleared the cut-off were not selected." Conclusion I: "Some successful candidates were not selected." Conclusion II: "All successful candidates cleared the cut-off." Which follows?
(a) Only I (b) Neither I nor II (c) Both I and II (d) Only II
Answer: (d)
Solution: Conclusion II merely restates the first premise, so it holds, while Conclusion I contradicts the meaning of "successful" since those not selected cannot be successful candidates. The trap is mixing the two groups through the shared cut-off, so the answer is that only II follows.
