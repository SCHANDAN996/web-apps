# 📊 PYQ Analysis — Blood Relations (Coded/Complex)

## 1. How Exams Ask This Chapter

Across SSC (CGL, CHSL, MTS), Banking (IBPS PO/Clerk, SBI PO), Railways (RRB NTPC, Group D) and State PCS prelims, Blood Relations is a **permanent fixture** of the reasoning section. It almost never disappears from a paper — typically 1–3 questions per shift in SSC/Railway and 2–5 questions (often as a coded set) in Banking prelims.

The chapter appears in **four recurring formats**:

1. **Direct relationship puzzle** — "Pointing to a photograph, Ravi said…" One speaker, one chain of relations, one question.
2. **Coded relations** — Symbols defined (A + B means A is the father of B; A − B means A is the sister of B…), then an expression must be decoded or an expression must be *chosen* that proves a stated relationship.
3. **Family-tree puzzle (Banking favourite)** — A paragraph describes 6–8 family members across 3 generations; 3–5 questions follow (How is X related to Y? How many females? Who is the wife of Z?).
4. **Mixed with other topics** — Blood relations embedded inside seating arrangement, direction sense ("a man walks towards his sister's house…"), or syllogism-style statements.

## 2. Sub-topics That Repeat Most Often

- **Gender-hidden names**: Names like Kiran, Suman, Kamal, Neeraj that do not reveal gender — the examiner deliberately withholds gender so that careless solvers assume it.
- **"Only son / only daughter" clauses**: "My mother's only son" = the speaker himself (if male). Missing the word *only* is the single most common error.
- **Maternal vs paternal side confusion**: Mother's brother (maternal uncle) vs father's brother (paternal uncle) — options are designed so both look plausible.
- **In-law relations**: Wife's brother, husband's sister, son's wife — Banking exams love these.
- **Coded chains of 3–4 symbols**: e.g., P × Q + R − S. The trap is reading left-to-right without fixing each pair's gender first.
- **Generation-count questions**: "How many generations are there?" or "How many married couples?" — solvers forget that a single person can belong to two generations' counts in different questions.

## 3. Examiner's Traps (Psychological Hooks)

1. **The almost-true option**: If the answer is "maternal uncle", one option will be "paternal uncle" and another "cousin". The trap works because solvers build the tree correctly but flip one link at the end.
2. **Assumed gender**: The statement never says whether the speaker is male or female; two answer options differ only on this. Correct approach: check if the question even *can* be answered, or whether the relation is gender-independent.
3. **Pronoun back-reference**: Long sentences like "She is the daughter of the wife of the only son of my father's father" — the examiner counts on you losing track of "my". Always start from "my father's father" and build outward.
4. **Symbol overload in coded questions**: Defining 5–6 symbols when only 3 are needed for the actual question — a pure time-waster trap.
5. **Negative phrasing**: "Which of the following does NOT establish that M is the mother of N?" — solvers pick the first expression that *does* establish it.

## 4. Exam-wise Distribution (Qualitative)

- **SSC CGL/CHSL/MTS, RRB**: Mostly direct photograph/pointing questions and short coded questions; difficulty easy-to-moderate; 1 question per shift is common.
- **IBPS/SBI PO & Clerk**: Coded relations and full family-tree puzzles dominate; often 3–5 questions from one paragraph; difficulty moderate-to-high with gender-hidden names.
- **State PCS prelims**: Direct relations plus one coded question; difficulty moderate.

## 5. Top 10 High-Yield PYQ-Style Questions (with Solutions & Trap Analysis)

---

**Q1.** Pointing to a photograph, a man said, "She is the daughter of my grandfather's only son." How is the woman in the photograph related to the man?

(a) Sister (b) Cousin (c) Aunt (d) Mother

**Answer: (a)**

**Solution:** Grandfather's *only* son = the man's father. Father's daughter = his sister.
**Trap:** Missing "only". If grandfather had several sons, she could be a cousin. The word "only" collapses the chain to one path.
**Source:** PYQ-style

---

**Q2.** If A + B means A is the father of B; A − B means A is the brother of B; A × B means A is the mother of B, then which of the following shows that P is the maternal uncle of R?

(a) P + Q × R (b) P − Q × R (c) P × Q + R (d) P − Q + R

**Answer: (b)**

**Solution:** P − Q means P is the *brother* of Q, so P is male. Q × R means Q is the mother of R. P is the brother of R's mother = maternal **uncle**. Option (d) fails because Q + R makes Q the father of R, so P would be the paternal uncle.
**Trap:** Options (b) and (d) differ only in the last symbol — the examiner counts on you not checking whether Q becomes R's mother or father.
**Source:** PYQ-style

---

**Q3.** Introducing a boy, a girl said, "He is the son of the only daughter of the father of my paternal uncle." How is the boy related to the girl?

(a) Brother (b) Cousin (c) Nephew (d) Son

**Answer: (b)**

**Solution:** Father of my paternal uncle = my (paternal) grandfather. The *only* daughter of my grandfather = my father's sister, i.e., my aunt. Son of my aunt = my **cousin**.
**Trap:** Solvers take "daughter of the father of my uncle" as the girl's mother, getting "brother". The chain must be read strictly right-to-left, and "only daughter" fixes that this daughter is not the girl's mother (the mother would be a daughter-*in-law*, not a daughter, of the grandfather).
**Source:** PYQ-style

---

**Q4.** A family has 6 members: P, Q, R, S, T, U. P is the father of Q. R is the mother of S. S is the son of P. T is the wife of S. U is the daughter of S. How is R related to U?

(a) Mother (b) Grandmother (c) Aunt (d) Sister

**Answer: (b)**

**Solution:** P (father) and R (mother) are parents of Q and S. S's daughter is U, so R is U's grandmother.
**Trap:** Rushing to "mother" because R is introduced as "mother of S" — the question asks about U, one generation down.
**Source:** PYQ-style

---

**Q5.** Pointing to a woman, Rohan said, "Her mother's only daughter is my mother." How is Rohan related to the woman?

(a) Son (b) Nephew (c) Brother (d) Grandson

**Answer: (a)**

**Solution:** "Her mother's only daughter" = the woman herself (she is the only daughter). So the woman *is* Rohan's mother. Rohan is her son.
**Trap:** The sentence folds back on itself — "only daughter" makes the woman and "my mother" the same person. Solvers who build a two-person tree land on "nephew".
**Source:** PYQ-style

---

**Q6.** If M × N means M is the brother of N; M + N means M is the wife of N; M ÷ N means M is the daughter of N, then in the expression A + B × C ÷ D, how is A related to D?

(a) Daughter-in-law (b) Sister-in-law (c) Daughter (d) Mother

**Answer: (a)**

**Solution:** B × C → B is the brother of C. C ÷ D → C is the daughter of D, so B and C are both children of D, i.e., B is D's son. A + B → A is the wife of B, hence wife of D's son = D's **daughter-in-law**.
**Trap:** Two-step gender fixing: C's gender (daughter) confirms B is D's son; then A's relation to D follows. Solvers who stop at "A is wife of B" pick "sister-in-law".
**Source:** PYQ-style
**⏱ Skip rule:** 3-symbol chains like this are solvable in 30–40 seconds with a mini family tree — attempt. Skip only if the chain has 4+ symbols *and* a negative question.

---

**Q7.** Kiran is the brother of Suman. Suman is the daughter of Rajesh. Kamal is the son of Rajesh. How is Kiran related to Kamal?

(a) Brother (b) Cousin (c) Uncle (d) Cannot be determined

**Answer: (a)**

**Solution:** Suman and Kamal are both children of Rajesh; Kiran is Suman's brother, hence also Rajesh's child. Kiran (male) and Kamal (male) are brothers.
**Trap:** "Cannot be determined" tempts solvers who think the parents' marriage isn't stated — but shared parent Rajesh is enough.
**Source:** PYQ-style

---

**Q8.** A said to B, "Your father's only sister is my mother." How is A related to B?

(a) Cousin (b) Brother (c) Nephew (d) Uncle

**Answer: (a)**

**Solution:** B's father's only sister = B's paternal aunt. She is A's mother, so A is the child of B's paternal aunt — A and B are **cousins** (A is B's aunt's child).
**Trap:** Solvers who misread "your father's sister" as "your mother" land on "brother". Note the statement is fully determinate here: the aunt is explicitly A's mother, so no sibling/cousin ambiguity arises.
**Source:** PYQ-style

---

**Q9.** In a family of three generations, there are two married couples. F is the grandfather of H. G is the mother of H. E is the father-in-law of G. How many male members are definitely in the family?

(a) 1 (b) 2 (c) 3 (d) Cannot be determined

**Answer: (d)**

**Solution:** E (male, father-in-law of G) and F (male, grandfather) — note E and F may be the *same person* (G's husband's father = H's grandfather). H's gender is unstated; G's husband's existence is implied but he may or may not be counted among named members. Hence "definitely male" cannot be fixed.
**Trap:** Solvers count E, F, and H's father as 3 males, ignoring that E and F can coincide and H's gender is hidden.
**Source:** PYQ-style
**⏱ Skip rule:** "Cannot be determined" family-count questions with overlapping identities are classic 45-second skips in SSC — high ambiguity, low reward.

---

**Q10.** Pointing to a lady, a man said, "The son of her only brother is the brother of my wife." How is the lady related to the man?

(a) Mother-in-law (b) Sister of father-in-law (c) Aunt (d) Mother

**Answer: (b)**

**Solution:** Brother of my wife = my brother-in-law (wife's brother). He is the son of the lady's only brother → the lady's brother is my wife's father → the lady is the sister of my father-in-law.
**Trap:** The chain crosses two families (speaker's wife's side and the lady's side). Solvers who stay in one family pick "aunt" or "mother-in-law".
**Source:** PYQ-style

---

## 6. The 15-Second / 45-Second Rule — Review

- **15-second questions**: Direct "pointing to a photograph" with ≤3 links and the word "only" clearly placed (Q1, Q5 type). Solve instantly by reading right-to-left from "my".
- **45-second questions**: Coded expressions with ≤3 symbols (Q2, Q6 type) and short family paragraphs with ≤5 members. Draw a 3-line tree and answer.
- **SKIP immediately (return later if time permits)**:
  - 4+ symbol coded chains with a negative question ("does NOT establish").
  - "Cannot be determined" traps with overlapping identities (Q9 type).
  - Questions where the symbol table and options appear inconsistent — mark the closest intended option and move on; never argue with the paper.
- **Golden rule**: One blood-relation question should never consume more than 60 seconds. The tree either forms quickly or the question is designed to eat your time — that *is* the trap.
