76. If P + Q means P is the father of Q; P − Q means P is the mother of Q; P × Q means P is the brother of Q; then which expression shows that A is the maternal uncle of C?
(a) A × B − C (b) A + B − C (c) A × B + C (d) A − B × C
Answer: (a)
Solution: The tempting choice A + B − C makes A the father, not the uncle. In A × B − C, A is the brother of B and B is the mother of C, so A is the maternal uncle of C, so the answer is A × B − C.
Source: PYQ-style

77. If M × N means M is the sister of N; M + N means M is the father of N; M ÷ N means M is the mother of N; then in the expression A ÷ B + C × D, how is A related to D?
(a) Mother (b) Aunt (c) Grandmother (d) Sister
Answer: (c)
Solution: Many candidates stop midway and call A the mother of D, but the chain is longer. A is mother of B, B is father of C, and C is sister of D, so C and D are children of B; hence A is the grandmother of D, so the answer is Grandmother.
Source: PYQ-style

78. If A @ B means A is the wife of B; A # B means A is the son of B; A $ B means A is the daughter of B; then in P # Q @ R $ S, how is P related to S?
(a) Nephew (b) Son (c) Brother (d) Grandson
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is to read P as the son of S directly. P is the son of Q, Q is the wife of R, and R is the daughter of S, so P is the child of S's daughter R, making P the grandson of S, so the answer is Grandson.

Solution: Option M + N × R makes M a brother, i.e., an uncle, not an aunt. In M − N × R, M is the sister of N and N is the father of R, so M is the paternal aunt of R, so the answer is M − N × R.
