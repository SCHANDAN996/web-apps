# 📊 PYQ Analysis — Mirror & Water Images (Advanced Level)

## 1. How Exams Ask This Chapter

Mirror & Water Images is a **small but guaranteed** chapter in Non-verbal Reasoning. It does not appear in huge numbers, but when it appears, it is almost always a **quick-scoring question** — provided the student has practised the lateral-inversion rule. The questions are purely visual: a figure, word, number, or clock is given, and four options show possible images.

**Typical question formats across exams:**

1. **Word/Letter images** — A word (e.g., QUALITY, REASONING) or a letter cluster is shown with a mirror placed on the right/left; choose the correct mirror image.
2. **Number images** — A number like 2476 or a digital-style number; find its mirror or water image.
3. **Clock-based images** — A clock shows a time; find the time as seen in a mirror (uses the 11:60 / 12:60 subtraction rule) or in water.
4. **Figure images** — A geometric figure with an arrow, dot, or shaded part; identify the correct lateral inversion.
5. **Mixed alphanumeric** — Combinations like "A2B4" where both letter shapes and order reversal are tested together.

## 2. Sub-topic Weightage (Qualitative)

- **Most frequent:** Mirror image of words/letters and of figures — this is the core of the chapter and appears in nearly every exam cycle that includes this topic.
- **Moderately frequent:** Clock mirror-time questions (very popular in SSC and Banking because they can be solved by formula in seconds).
- **Less frequent but regular:** Water images — usually one question, often of a number or a simple figure, testing top-bottom inversion.
- **Occasional:** Mirror placed on the **left side** or **below** the object — these are the trap variants (see Section 4).

## 3. Exam-wise Tendencies

- **SSC (CGL, CHSL, MTS, GD):** Word/letter mirror images and clock-time questions dominate. Options are designed so that two options differ only in one letter's orientation — a classic near-correct trap.
- **Banking (IBPS, SBI):** Fewer questions, but when asked, they involve alphanumeric strings and sometimes symbols; speed matters because reasoning sections are time-pressured.
- **Railway (NTPC, Group D):** Simple figure and number images; difficulty is low, so accuracy is the differentiator.
- **State PCS prelims:** Occasionally clubbed with paper-folding or embedded figures; figure-based mirror images preferred.

## 4. Examiner's Traps (Psychological Hooks)

1. **Near-correct option:** Three letters inverted correctly, one letter (usually b/d/p/q or a curved letter like S) left un-inverted. Students who check only the first and last letters fall for it.
2. **Order-reversal confusion:** An option shows each letter correctly inverted but in the **original order** (no left-right swap). Students who memorise letter shapes but forget that the whole string reverses pick this.
3. **Mirror position trick:** The mirror line is drawn on the **left** or **below** the figure, but options assume the standard right-side mirror. Reading the question's mirror-line symbol (the hatched line) is essential.
4. **Mirror vs Water confusion:** The question asks for a **water image**, but one option is the perfect **mirror image** — placed deliberately as option (a) to catch hurried readers.
5. **Clock trap:** For mirror time, students subtract from 12:00 instead of 11:60 (or 12:60 when minutes are 00), getting an answer that is one hour off — and that wrong answer is always present in the options.
6. **Symmetric-letter illusion:** Letters like A, H, I, M, O, T, U, V, W, X, Y look the same in a mirror; examiners build words where only one asymmetric letter decides the answer, rewarding careful checking.

## 5. Top 10 High-Yield PYQ-Style Questions (with Solutions & Trap Analysis)

**Q1.** Choose the correct mirror image of the word: **QUALITY** (mirror on the right).
(a) YTILAUQ (b) YTILAUQ with reversed Q-tail and inverted L (c) QUILATY (d) YTILAUQ with normal Q
**Answer:** (b)
**Solution:** In a mirror image, the order reverses AND each asymmetric letter is laterally inverted. Q's tail flips to the other side, and **L is asymmetric** — its foot must point the opposite way in the image. A, U, I, T, Y are vertically symmetric so they look unchanged. Option (a) reverses order but keeps letters un-inverted — the order-reversal trap.
**Trap:** Option (a) catches students who only reverse the spelling; option (d) catches those who forget Q's tail flips.
**Source:** PYQ-style (SSC pattern)

**Q2.** A clock shows **3:40**. What time will its mirror image show?
(a) 8:20 (b) 9:20 (c) 8:40 (d) 9:40
**Answer:** (a)
**Solution:** Mirror time = 11:60 − 3:40 = 8:20. (Equivalently 12:00 − 3:40 = 8:20.)
**Trap:** Option (b) 9:20 comes from wrongly subtracting from 12:60 — the classic clock trap.
**Source:** PYQ-style (SSC/Banking pattern)

**Q3.** Choose the correct water image of the number: **2476**
(a) 2476 flipped top-bottom (b) 6742 (c) 2476 as it is (d) 6742 flipped
**Answer:** (a)
**Solution:** Water image inverts top and bottom only — the left-right order of digits stays the same, but each digit flips vertically. Option (b) reverses the order, which is a mirror-image effect, not water.
**Trap:** Option (b) is the mirror-vs-water confusion trap.
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q4.** Choose the correct mirror image of: **REASON** (mirror on the right).
(a) NOSAER with inverted N, E, R, S (b) NOSAER (c) REASON (d) NOSAER with normal S
**Answer:** (a)
**Solution:** Order reverses to N-O-S-A-E-R, and the asymmetric letters **N, E, R, S** must flip laterally (capital E is NOT vertically symmetric — its horizontal bars must face the opposite way). Only O and A remain unchanged. S in particular is a favourite trap letter — its mirror form is reversed.
**Trap:** Option (d) has everything right except S — the near-correct trap.
**Source:** PYQ-style (SSC pattern)

**Q5.** A clock shows **6:00**. Its mirror image will show:
(a) 6:00 (b) 12:00 (c) 5:60 (d) 11:60
**Answer:** (a)
**Solution:** When minutes are 00, subtract from 12:00 → 12:00 − 6:00 = 6:00. A clock pointing straight up-down is symmetric about the vertical axis.
**Trap:** Options (c) and (d) are meaningless times planted for formula-blind students.
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q6.** The mirror is placed on the **left** of the figure "→ P". The mirror image will be:
(a) P with arrow pointing left, on the right side of P (b) P with arrow pointing right (c) mirror-P with arrow pointing left (d) unchanged figure
**Answer:** (c)
**Solution:** Whether the mirror is left or right, lateral inversion still occurs — the side nearest the mirror appears nearest in the image. The arrow (closer to the mirror) stays closer, and P flips.
**Trap:** Option (a) keeps P un-inverted — students assume a left mirror changes the inversion rule. It does not.
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q7.** Choose the correct mirror image of: **b d p q**
(a) q p d b with each letter inverted (b) b d p q (c) q p d b (d) p q b d
**Answer:** (a)
**Solution:** b↔d and p↔q interchange under lateral inversion, and the order reverses. So "b d p q" becomes inverted-q, inverted-p, inverted-d, inverted-b = q p d b in flipped forms.
**Trap:** Option (c) reverses order but not the letters — the most common error with these four letters.
**Source:** PYQ-style (Banking pattern)

**Q8.** A clock shows **2:30** in a mirror. What is the actual time?
(a) 9:30 (b) 10:30 (c) 9:00 (d) 10:00
**Answer:** (a)
**Solution:** The rule works both ways: actual = 11:60 − 2:30 = 9:30.
**Trap:** Students who think the reverse direction needs a different formula get lost; the subtraction is symmetric.
**Source:** PYQ-style (SSC pattern)

**Q9.** Choose the correct water image of a figure: an arrow pointing **up** with a dot on its **left**.
(a) Arrow down, dot on left (b) Arrow down, dot on right (c) Arrow up, dot on right (d) Arrow up, dot on left
**Answer:** (a)
**Solution:** Water image flips top↔bottom only: the up-arrow becomes a down-arrow, but the dot's left-right position is unchanged.
**Trap:** Option (b) applies a mirror-style left-right flip too — the mirror-vs-water trap again.
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q10.** Choose the correct mirror image of: **A2B4** (mirror on the right).
(a) 4B2A with B inverted, digits 2 and 4 flipped, A unchanged (b) 4B2A (c) A2B4 (d) 2A4B
**Answer:** (a)
**Solution:** Order reverses to 4-B-2-A; **A is vertically symmetric so it stays unchanged**, B flips laterally, and digits 2 and 4 take their laterally inverted forms.
**Trap:** Option (b) reverses order but ignores digit inversion — digits are NOT symmetric (except 0, 1, 8 in most fonts).
**Source:** PYQ-style (Banking pattern)

## 6. The 15-Second / 45-Second Rule — Reviewed Against PYQ Patterns

- **15-second questions (attempt immediately):** Word/letter mirror images of short words (Q1, Q4 type), clock mirror-time by formula (Q2, Q5, Q8 type), and symmetric-letter words. These are pure rule application — under 15 seconds with practice.
- **45-second questions (attempt, but with a time cap):** Figure-based images with multiple elements (arrow + dot + shading, Q9 type) and alphanumeric strings with digit inversion (Q7, Q10 type). These need element-by-element checking.
- **SKIP candidates:**
  - **Q6-type (unusual mirror position):** If the mirror is on the left/below AND the figure is complex, the visualisation cost is high. If you cannot settle it in 45 seconds, mark and move on — the risk of picking the near-correct trap is high.
  - **Q7-type (b/d/p/q clusters):** When all four options differ only in subtle letter flips, this becomes an eyesight test, not a reasoning test. If your first check doesn't clearly eliminate three options, skip and return later.
- **General rule:** Never spend more than 45 seconds on any image question. These questions carry the same marks as easier ones; a skipped image question costs less than a rushed error elsewhere.

## 7. Key Takeaways

1. Master the two rules: **mirror = left-right inversion**, **water = top-bottom inversion**. Everything else is application.
2. For clocks, memorise: **mirror time = 11:60 − given time** (use 12:00 when minutes are 00).
3. Always locate the **mirror line** in the question before looking at options.
4. Eliminate options by checking the **two or three asymmetric letters/elements** first — never check letter by letter from the start. Remember: A, H, I, M, O, T, U, V, W, X, Y are vertically symmetric; **B, C, D, E, F, G, J, K, L, N, P, Q, R, S, Z are not**.
5. The wrong options are not random; they are built from the exact mistakes described in Section 4. Knowing the traps is half the preparation.
