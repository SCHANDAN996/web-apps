1. You are a team leader. Two of your team members have a serious personal conflict that is affecting the project's progress. What should you do?
(a) Ignore it as it is their personal matter (b) Talk to both separately and then together to resolve the conflict (c) Report both to senior management immediately (d) Remove both from the project
Answer: (b)
Solution: Ignoring the conflict lets it grow, and escalating or removing members without a hearing is unfair and demoralising. A good decision-maker first understands both sides and mediates. So the answer is (b).
Source: PYQ-style

2. You find a wallet containing money and an identity card on a road. What will you do?
(a) Keep the money and throw the wallet (b) Hand it over to the nearest police station (c) Leave it where it is (d) Ask your friends what to do
Answer: (b)
Solution: Keeping the money is dishonest and punishable, while leaving it helps no one. The lawful and ethical action is to deposit it with the police so it reaches the owner. So the answer is (b).
Source: PYQ-style

3. As a supervisor, you notice a worker repeatedly violating safety rules despite warnings. What is the most appropriate action?
(a) Ignore it since no accident has happened yet (b) Scold him publicly in front of all workers (c) Take disciplinary action as per rules after documenting the violations (d) Transfer him to another department quietly
Answer: (c)
Solution: Ignoring safety violations risks lives, and public humiliation or quiet transfer does not correct behaviour. Rule-based documented disciplinary action is fair and preventive. So the answer is (c).

4. You are travelling by train and see an elderly person standing while you have a reserved seat. What should you do?
(a) Continue sitting as the seat is reserved for you (b) Pretend to be asleep (c) Ask the ticket checker to find him a seat (d) Offer your seat to the elderly person
Answer: (d)
Solution: Reservation gives a right, but decision-making questions test empathy and social responsibility. Offering the seat is the humane and expected response. So the answer is (d).

5. Your close friend asks you to leak an internal office document to him. What will you do?
(a) Refuse politely and explain that it is against office rules (b) Give it to him because friendship comes first (c) Give it but ask him not to tell anyone (d) Complain about him to the police
Answer: (a)
Solution: Leaking a confidential document is misconduct regardless of who asks, and a police complaint is disproportionate. Refusing politely while explaining the rule balances duty and friendship. So the answer is (a).

(a) Take action as per examination rules (b) Warn him and let him continue (c) Snatch his paper and beat him (d) Ignore it to avoid trouble
Answer: (a)
Solution: A mere warning or ignoring it is unfair to honest students, and physical punishment is illegal. Following the prescribed examination rules is the correct, lawful response. So the answer is (a).

Answer: (c)

9. As a police officer on duty, you see a crowd beating a suspected thief. What should you do?
(a) Join the crowd since the man is a thief (b) Walk away as it is a mob matter (c) Stop the crowd, rescue the man and take legal action as per law (d) Record a video and leave
Answer: (c)
Solution: Mob justice is illegal; even a suspect has the right to due process. A police officer must protect life and hand the matter to law. So the answer is (c).

Answer: (b)
Solution: Public confrontation harms office discipline, and silence demotivates the worker. Recognising the subordinate through proper channels is the mature decision. So the answer is (b).

11. You are the head of an office and must choose one of two equally qualified candidates for a promotion; one is your relative. What should you do?
(a) Promote your relative (b) Decide strictly on merit and performance records, keeping the process transparent (c) Promote neither to avoid controversy (d) Ask the relative to resign
Answer: (b)
Solution: Favouring a relative is nepotism, and promoting neither punishes both unfairly. A transparent merit-based decision is the ethical standard tested in such questions. So the answer is (b).

21. You are a district officer. A scheme benefits 500 families, but funds can cover only 300 this year. What is the best decision?
(a) Give benefits to the first 300 applicants regardless of need (b) Select the 300 most needy families using transparent, pre-declared criteria and plan for the rest next year (c) Divide the funds equally so everyone gets a token amount (d) Cancel the scheme for this year
Answer: (b)
Solution: First-come-first-served and token division ignore need, and cancellation wastes the scheme. Transparent need-based criteria with a future plan is the most defensible administrative decision. So the answer is (b).

Answer: (c)
Solution: The tempting trap is that a "small risk" seems acceptable, but safety norms are non-negotiable because the potential loss is catastrophic. Transparency plus rescheduling is correct. So the answer is (c).
