# 🪄 Short Tricks & Mnemonics — Decision Making

---

**Box 1 — The "BOSS" Order of Decision Making**
Whenever a case study lands on your desk, follow the BOSS:
- **B** — Break the problem into facts vs. opinions
- **O** — Options: list every possible course of action
- **S** — Stakeholders: who is affected?
- **S** — Select the most ethical + practical option
🗣️ *"Be the BOSS, don't just guess!"*

---

**Box 2 — "ELPASO" for Course of Action questions**
A strong course of action must be:
- **E**thical
- **L**egal
- **P**ractical
- **A**chievable
- **S**afe (no harm to anyone)
- **O**bjective (not emotional)
🗣️ *"If your action can't cross the ELPASO border, it's a wrong answer!"*

---

**Box 3 — The "No Extreme, No Emotion" Rule**
In decision-making questions, options with words like *immediately dismiss, punish harshly, ignore completely, resign at once* are almost always wrong. Extreme = Eliminate.
🗣️ *"Extreme options are drama queens — they never get selected."*

---

**Box 4 — "FIRE" Test for Assumptions**
An assumption is valid only if it is:
- **F**ounded in the statement
- **I**mplicit (hidden, not stated)
- **R**easonable
- **E**ssential (statement collapses without it)
🗣️ *"If the assumption isn't FIRE, don't hire it!"*

---

**Box 5 — The "Middle Path Magnet"**
When two options are opposite extremes (do nothing vs. take harsh action), the correct answer usually sits in the middle: *investigate, discuss, form a committee, seek more information.*
🗣️ *"The examiner hides the answer in the middle of the road."*

---

**Box 6 — "PEEL" the Case Study**
- **P**roblem — what exactly went wrong?
- **E**vidence — what facts are given?
- **E**valuate — weigh each option
- **L**ong-term — pick the option good for the long run, not a quick patch
🗣️ *"PEEL the case like a banana — the answer is inside."*

---

**Box 7 — The "Villain Words" List**
Options containing these words are usually traps: *bribe, threaten, hide, blame others, manipulate records, favour relatives, suppress the complaint.*
🗣️ *"If the option sounds like a movie villain, it's the wrong answer."*

---

**Box 8 — "RULER" for Administrative Decisions**
A good administrator's decision is:
- **R**ule-based
- **U**nbiased
- **L**awful
- **E**mpathetic
- **R**eviewed (open to correction)
🗣️ *"A good officer is a RULER — but never rules like a dictator."*

---

**Box 9 — The "Both-Can-Be-True" Trap**
In statement–conclusion questions, if two conclusions contradict each other, neither follows automatically — check which one is *directly* supported. Don't pick both just because both "sound nice."
🗣️ *"Two friends fighting? Don't invite both to the answer party."*

---

**Box 10 — "WHO" Before "WHAT"**
Before choosing an action, ask **WHO** is responsible for acting. If the option makes *you* do something outside your authority (e.g., a clerk changing government policy), reject it.
🗣️ *"Stay in your lane — the examiner loves authority traps."*

---

**Box 11 — The "Short-term vs Long-term Seesaw"**
Quick fixes (transfer the complainant, close the case, give verbal warning) lose to long-term solutions (systemic reform, proper inquiry, training). The seesaw always tips toward the long term.
🗣️ *"Band-aid options fall off in the exam."*

---

**Box 12 — "3-Second Ethical Scan"**
Before marking, ask: *Would this decision look fine on tomorrow's newspaper front page?* If it would cause a scandal, it's wrong.
🗣️ *"The Front-Page Test — the examiner's favourite filter."*

---

**Box 13 — "Data Before Drama"**
If a passage gives data/facts and an option is purely emotional or opinion-based, data wins. Decision-making questions reward logic, not feelings.
🗣️ *"Drama gets applause, data gets marks."*

---

## ⏭️ Skip Strategy — When to Leave the Question

1. **The 20-Second Fog Rule:** If after 20 seconds you cannot even identify *what type* of question it is (course of action / assumption / case study), mark it for review and move on. Decision-making questions reward clarity, not stubbornness.

2. **The "All Options Look Right" Trap:** If all four options feel equally correct, you have missed a keyword in the passage (usually *not, except, least*). Re-read the stem once — if still foggy, skip. Negative-marking exams punish coin-flips.

3. **The Long Case-Study Last Rule:** If a case study has 6+ lines and 4 sub-questions, attempt it **last** in the reasoning section. It eats 3–4 minutes; secure quick marks from shorter questions first, then return with a fresh mind.
