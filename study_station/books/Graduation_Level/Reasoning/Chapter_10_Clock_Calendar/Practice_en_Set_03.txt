51. At what time between 4 o'clock and 5 o'clock will the hands of a clock be together?
(a) 20 minutes past 4 (b) 22 minutes past 4 (c) 21 9/11 minutes past 4 (d) 19 1/11 minutes past 4
Answer: (c)
Solution: The tempting answer 20 minutes past 4 ignores that the hour hand also moves while the minute hand catches up. At 4 o'clock the minute hand is 20 minute-spaces behind; it gains 55 minute-spaces in 60 minutes, so it needs 20 × 60/55 = 240/11 = 21 9/11 minutes. So the answer is 21 9/11 minutes past 4.
Source: PYQ-style

52. The angle between the two hands of a clock at 7:20 is
(a) 100° (b) 110° (c) 90° (d) 120°
Answer: (a)
Solution: Many students compute only the minute hand position and get 120°, forgetting the hour hand has moved past 7. Angle = |30H − 5.5M| = |30×7 − 5.5×20| = |210 − 110| = 100°. So the answer is 100°.
Source: PYQ-style

53. How many times in a day are the hands of a clock at right angles?
(a) 22 (b) 24 (c) 44 (d) 48
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap answer 48 assumes 2 right angles per hour for 24 hours, but between 2–4 and 8–10 o'clock one right angle is lost in each pair of hours. In 12 hours the hands are at right angles 22 times, so in 24 hours it is 44 times. So the answer is 44.

54. A clock gains 5 minutes every hour. If it is set right at 6 a.m., what will it show when the correct time is 6 p.m. the same day?
(a) 6:55 p.m. (b) 7:00 p.m. (c) 7:05 p.m. (d) 6:05 p.m.
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is to add only 5 minutes, treating the gain as a one-time error. In 12 hours the clock gains 12 × 5 = 60 minutes, so it shows 6 p.m. + 60 minutes = 7:00 p.m. So the answer is 7:00 p.m.

55. What was the day of the week on 15 August 1947?
(a) Wednesday (b) Thursday (c) Saturday (d) Friday
Answer: (d)
Solution: A common trap is to count 1946 as a leap year; 1946 is not divisible by 4, so it is ordinary. Counting odd days from a known reference (1 January 1947 was Wednesday) and adding odd days up to 15 August 1947 gives 2 more odd days, i.e. Friday. So the answer is Friday.

56. The reflex angle between the hands of a clock at 10:25 is
(a) 192.5° (b) 197.5° (c) 180° (d) 162.5°
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is to report the smaller angle 162.5° when the question asks for the reflex angle. Smaller angle = |30×10 − 5.5×25| = |300 − 137.5| = 162.5°, so reflex angle = 360° − 162.5° = 197.5°. So the answer is 197.5°.

57. If today is Monday, what day will it be after 62 days?
(a) Friday (b) Saturday (c) Sunday (d) Monday
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is to divide by 7 and use the quotient instead of the remainder. 62 ÷ 7 gives remainder 6, so 62 days later is 6 days after Monday, which is Sunday. So the answer is Sunday.

58. At what time between 9 and 10 o'clock will the hands of a clock be in opposite directions?
(a) 16 4/11 minutes past 9 (b) 15 minutes past 9 (c) 17 minutes past 9 (d) 18 2/11 minutes past 9
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap answer 15 minutes past 9 assumes the hour hand stays fixed at 9. The hands must be 30 minute-spaces apart; at 9 the minute hand is 45 spaces behind, so it must gain 15 spaces: 15 × 60/55 = 180/11 = 16 4/11 minutes. So the answer is 16 4/11 minutes past 9.

59. How many leap years are there in 400 consecutive years of the Gregorian calendar?
(a) 97 (b) 96 (c) 99 (d) 100
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap answer 100 divides 400 by 4 directly, ignoring that century years must be divisible by 400. In 400 years there are 100 multiples of 4, but 3 century years (like 1700, 1800, 1900) are not leap, giving 97 leap years. So the answer is 97.
Source: PYQ-style

60. A watch loses 4 minutes every hour. It was set right at 9 a.m. What is the correct time when the watch shows 5 p.m.?
(a) 5:32 p.m. (b) 5:36 p.m. (c) 5:30 p.m. (d) 5:34 2/7 p.m.
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is to add 8 × 4 = 32 minutes directly, but the watch's 8 hours are slow hours, not true hours. The watch covers 56 minutes in 60 true minutes, so 8 watch-hours = 8 × 60/56 = 60/7 = 8 4/7 true hours, i.e. 8 hours 34 2/7 minutes. So the answer is 5:34 2/7 p.m.
Source: PYQ-style

61. On what dates of March 2005 did Friday fall, given that 1 March 2005 was a Tuesday?
(a) 3, 10, 17, 24, 31 (b) 4, 11, 18, 25 (c) 5, 12, 19, 26 (d) 4, 11, 18, 25, 31
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap option adds 31, but 31 March is 30 days after 1 March, i.e. 2 odd days, making it Thursday, not Friday. If 1 March is Tuesday, the first Friday is 4 March, then 11, 18 and 25. So the answer is 4, 11, 18, 25.

62. The angle between the hands of a clock at 2:30 is
(a) 105° (b) 100° (c) 110° (d) 95°
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap answer 100° comes from misplacing the hour hand; at 2:30 it has moved halfway from 2 to 3. Angle = |30×2 − 5.5×30| = |60 − 165| = 105°. So the answer is 105°.

63. If 1 January 2001 was a Monday, what day was 1 January 2005?
(a) Friday (b) Thursday (c) Sunday (d) Saturday
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is to count all four years as ordinary years; 2004 is a leap year and contributes 2 odd days. Odd days = 1 + 1 + 1 + 2 = 5, so Monday + 5 = Saturday. So the answer is Saturday.

64. How many times in 12 hours do the hands of a clock coincide?
(a) 11 (b) 13 (c) 10 (d) 12
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap answer 12 assumes one coincidence per hour, but between 11 and 1 the hands coincide only once, at 12 o'clock. Hence in 12 hours they coincide 11 times. So the answer is 11.

65. A clock strikes once at 1 o'clock, twice at 2 o'clock and so on. How many times does it strike in 24 hours?
(a) 78 (b) 156 (c) 136 (d) 144
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap answer 78 counts only 12 hours. In 12 hours the clock strikes 1 + 2 + … + 12 = 78 times, so in 24 hours it strikes 2 × 78 = 156 times. So the answer is 156.

66. What was the day of the week on 26 January 1950?
(a) Wednesday (b) Thursday (c) Friday (d) Tuesday
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is to treat 1900 as a leap year while counting odd days; 1900 is a century year not divisible by 400, so it is ordinary. Using the standard odd-day count up to 26 January 1950 gives 4 odd days from the reference Monday, giving Thursday. So the answer is Thursday.

67. At what time between 5 and 6 o'clock will the hands of a clock be at right angles for the first time?
(a) 10 minutes past 5 (b) 10 10/11 minutes past 5 (c) 11 minutes past 5 (d) 12 minutes past 5
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap answer 10 minutes past 5 keeps the hour hand fixed at 5. The minute hand must be 15 spaces behind the hour hand; at 5 it is 25 spaces behind, so it must gain 10 spaces: 10 × 60/55 = 120/11 = 10 10/11 minutes. So the answer is 10 10/11 minutes past 5.

68. If the day before yesterday was Wednesday, what day will it be the day after tomorrow?
(a) Sunday (b) Monday (c) Saturday (d) Friday
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is to count four days forward from Wednesday and land on Monday, but the gap from day before yesterday to day after tomorrow is only 4 days total from Wednesday: Wednesday + 4 = Sunday. Day before yesterday = Wednesday means today is Friday, so the day after tomorrow is Sunday. So the answer is Sunday.

69. The minute hand of a clock overtakes the hour hand at intervals of 65 minutes of correct time. How much does the clock gain or lose per day?
(a) Loses 10 10/143 minutes (b) Loses 5 5/11 minutes (c) Gains 5 5/11 minutes (d) Gains 10 10/143 minutes
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is to mark "loses" because 65 minutes is shorter than the true coincidence interval of 65 5/11 minutes; a shorter interval means the clock's hands move faster, so the clock gains. The clock gains 5/11 minute per 65 true minutes, i.e. (1440/65) × (5/11) = 7200/715 = 10 10/143 minutes per day. So the answer is gains 10 10/143 minutes.
Source: PYQ-style

70. Which of the following years is a leap year?
(a) 1900 (b) 1800 (c) 2100 (d) 2000
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap options 1900, 2100 and 1800 are divisible by 4 but are century years not divisible by 400, so they are ordinary years. 2000 is divisible by 400, so it is a leap year. So the answer is 2000.

71. The angle between the hands of a clock at 8:45 is
(a) 7.5° (b) 15° (c) 22.5° (d) 30°
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap answer 22.5° comes from placing the hour hand exactly at 8; at 8:45 it has moved three-quarters of the way to 9. Angle = |30×8 − 5.5×45| = |240 − 247.5| = 7.5°. So the answer is 7.5°.

72. If 5 March of a year is Tuesday, what day is 5 July of the same year (non-leap year)?
(a) Saturday (b) Thursday (c) Friday (d) Wednesday
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is to count March as 30 days or to include both end dates in the count. Days from 5 March to 5 July = 26 + 30 + 31 + 30 + 5 = 122 days; 122 mod 7 = 3 odd days, so Tuesday + 3 = Friday. So the answer is Friday.
Source: PYQ-style

73. Between 3 and 4 o'clock, at what time are the hands of a clock exactly opposite to each other?
(a) 50 minutes past 3 (b) 48 minutes past 3 (c) 49 1/11 minutes past 3 (d) 47 3/11 minutes past 3
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap answer 48 minutes past 3 rounds off the fraction and loses exactness. At 3 o'clock the minute hand is 15 spaces behind; to be opposite it must be 30 spaces ahead, i.e. gain 45 spaces: 45 × 60/55 = 540/11 = 49 1/11 minutes. So the answer is 49 1/11 minutes past 3.
Source: PYQ-style

74. The calendar of the year 2007 can be used again in which year?
(a) 2016 (b) 2017 (c) 2018 (d) 2015
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap answer 2016 is a leap year, so its calendar cannot match the ordinary year 2007 even though odd days seem close. Adding odd days year by year from 2007, the total becomes a multiple of 7 in 2018, which is also an ordinary year. So the answer is 2018.
Source: PYQ-style

75. A clock is set right at 5 a.m. It loses 16 minutes in 24 hours. What will be the correct time when the clock shows 10 p.m. on the fourth day?
(a) 10:48 p.m. (b) 10:52 p.m. (c) 11:15 p.m. (d) 11:00 p.m.
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is to compute the loss on 89 hours as if they were true hours; the 89 hours shown are slow-clock hours. From 5 a.m. day 1 to 10 p.m. day 4 the clock shows 89 hours; since 23 hours 44 minutes of this clock equal 24 true hours, 89 clock-hours correspond to 90 true hours, i.e. 11 p.m. So the answer is 11:00 p.m.
