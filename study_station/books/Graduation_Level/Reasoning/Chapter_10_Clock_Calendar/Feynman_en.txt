# 🧒 The Feynman Way — Clock & Calendar, Explained Like You're 12

## The Story: Ravi's Birthday Puzzle

Imagine your friend Ravi says: *"My birthday was on a **Tuesday** this year. What day will it fall on next year?"*

You might think — "How would I know? I'd need a calendar!" But here's the secret: **you don't need a calendar. You need to count how many extra days a year throws at you.**

A year has 365 days. Now, a week has 7 days. Divide 365 by 7:

365 = 52 weeks + **1 leftover day**

That 1 leftover day is called an **odd day**. It's like buying 52 full boxes of 7 toffees, and 1 toffee is left rolling around loose. That loose toffee pushes every date forward by **one day** next year. So Ravi's birthday next year = Tuesday + 1 = **Wednesday**. Done!

But wait — in a **leap year** (366 days), there are **2 loose toffees** (odd days). So the date jumps forward by **two days**. That's the whole game of calendars: **count the loose toffees (odd days), and the weekday shifts by that many.**

## The Clock Version of the Same Trick

Now look at a clock. The minute hand is a fast runner, the hour hand is a slow walker.

- The minute hand sprints: **6° per minute** (360° ÷ 60).
- The hour hand strolls: **0.5° per minute** (30° per hour ÷ 60).

So the minute hand gains on the hour hand at **5.5° per minute** — like a fast runner gaining on a slow one in a circular race.

**The angle between the hands at H hours M minutes:**

$$\text{Angle} = |30H - 5.5M|$$

If the answer is more than 180°, subtract it from 360° (because the angle between two hands is always the *smaller* one).

**Example:** Angle at 3:20?
|30×3 − 5.5×20| = |90 − 110| = **20°**. That's it — one line!

## The Unforgettable Trick: "The Loose Toffee Rule" 🍬

> **Every ordinary year drops 1 loose toffee (odd day). Every leap year drops 2. Collect the toffees, take remainder with 7, and shift the weekday forward.**

And for clocks, remember the **"Race Track Rule"**: the minute hand gains 5.5° every minute — so hands coincide every **65⁵⁄₁₁ minutes** (not 60!), and in 12 hours they meet only **11 times**, not 12. Why 11? Because between 11 and 1 o'clock, they meet just once — at 12. That "missing" meeting is the examiner's favourite trap!

**Mirror trick for clocks:** Time in mirror = **11:60 − actual time** (use 12:00 as 11:60). Mirror of 4:30? 11:60 − 4:30 = **7:30**.
