101. At what time between 4 and 5 o'clock will the hands of a clock be together?
(a) 21 9/11 minutes past 4 (b) 20 minutes past 4 (c) 22 minutes past 4 (d) 19 1/11 minutes past 4
Answer: (a)
Solution: The tempting answer 20 minutes past 4 ignores that the hour hand also moves while the minute hand catches up. At 4:00 the hour hand is 20 minute-spaces ahead; the minute hand gains 55 spaces in 60 minutes, so time = 20 × 60/55 = 240/11 = 21 9/11 minutes, so the answer is 21 9/11 minutes past 4.
Source: PYQ-style

102. How many times in a day are the hands of a clock at right angles?
(a) 22 (b) 44 (c) 24 (d) 48
Answer: (b)
Solution: The tempting 48 assumes 2 right angles per hour × 24 hours, but between 2–3, 3–4, 8–9 and 9–10 o'clock only one right angle occurs each, removing 4 from 48. In 12 hours the hands are at right angles 22 times, so in 24 hours it is 44, so the answer is 44.
Source: PYQ-style

103. The angle between the hands of a clock at 7:20 is:
(a) 90° (b) 120° (c) 110° (d) 100°
Answer: (d)
Solution: Angle = |30H − 11M/2| = |30×7 − 11×20/2| = |210 − 110| = 100°. The tempting 110° comes from wrongly adding the two terms instead of subtracting, so the answer is 100°.
Source: PYQ-style

104. A clock gains 5 minutes every hour. If it is set right at 6 AM, what time will it show when the correct time is 6 PM the same day?
(a) 7:00 PM (b) 7:05 PM (c) 6:55 PM (d) 6:05 PM
Answer: (a)
Solution: The tempting 6:05 PM applies only one hour's gain. In 12 hours the clock gains 12 × 5 = 60 minutes, so it shows 6 PM + 60 min = 7:00 PM, so the answer is 7:00 PM.
Source: PYQ-style

105. At what time between 9 and 10 o'clock will the hands be in a straight line but not together?
(a) 18 min past 9 (b) 15 min past 9 (c) 16 4/11 min past 9 (d) 14 6/11 min past 9
Answer: (c)
Solution: At 9:00 the hands are 45 minute-spaces apart; for a straight line (opposite) the gap must be 30 spaces, so the minute hand must gain 15 spaces. Time = 15 × 60/55 = 180/11 = 16 4/11 minutes, so the answer is 16 4/11 minutes past 9.

106. What was the day of the week on 15 August 1947?
(a) Thursday (b) Friday (c) Saturday (d) Wednesday
Answer: (b)
Solution: Counting odd days: 1600 years = 0, 300 years = 1, 46 years = 11 leap + 35 ordinary = 57 days ≡ 1, and days up to 15 August 1947 = 227 ≡ 3. Total = 0+1+1+3 = 5 ≡ Friday, so the answer is Friday.
Source: PYQ-style

107. If today is Monday, what day will it be after 61 days?
(a) Saturday (b) Sunday (c) Friday (d) Thursday
Answer: (a)
Solution: 61 ÷ 7 leaves remainder 5. Monday + 5 days = Saturday. The tempting Friday comes from counting only 4 extra days, so the answer is Saturday.

108. How many leap years are there in 400 consecutive years of the Gregorian calendar?
(a) 99 (b) 96 (c) 100 (d) 97
Answer: (d)
Solution: The tempting 100 divides 400 by 4 but ignores the century rule: years 100, 200, 300 are not leap years while 400 is. So 100 − 3 = 97, so the answer is 97.
Source: PYQ-style

109. The minute hand of a clock overtakes the hour hand at intervals of 65 minutes of correct time. How much does the clock gain or lose per day?
(a) Loses 10 10/143 min (b) Gains 5 5/11 min (c) Gains 10 10/143 min (d) Loses 5 5/11 min
Answer: (c)
Solution: In a correct clock the hands meet every 65 5/11 minutes; here they meet in 65 minutes, so the clock runs fast. Gain in 65 minutes = 5/11 min, so per day = (5/11) × (24×60/65) = 720/143 = 10 10/143 minutes, so the answer is gains 10 10/143 min.

110. What is the angle between the hands at 3:40?
(a) 120° (b) 140° (c) 130° (d) 110°
Answer: (c)
Solution: Angle = |30×3 − 11×40/2| = |90 − 220| = 130°. The tempting 120° forgets that the hour hand has moved past 3, so the answer is 130°.

111. If 1 January 2001 was a Monday, what day was 1 January 2004?
(a) Wednesday (b) Thursday (c) Tuesday (d) Friday
Answer: (b)
Solution: The shift from 1 Jan 2001 to 1 Jan 2004 covers the years 2001, 2002 and 2003, all ordinary years with 1 odd day each, giving 3 odd days. Monday + 3 = Thursday, so the answer is Thursday.

112. Between 5 and 6 o'clock, at what time are the hands exactly opposite each other?
(a) 54 6/11 min past 5 (b) They are never opposite between 5 and 6 (c) 55 min past 5 (d) 1 hr exactly, i.e. 6:00
Answer: (b)
Solution: The tempting 54 6/11 min past 5 is the time the hands coincide, not oppose. For opposition the minute hand must gain 55 spaces on the hour hand, needing 60 minutes, which lands exactly at 6:00 — outside the interval, so the answer is they are never opposite between 5 and 6.
Source: PYQ-style

113. A leap year starting with which day will have 53 Sundays?
(a) Wednesday (b) Friday (c) Saturday or Sunday (d) Monday
Answer: (c)
Solution: A leap year has 2 odd days, so the first two days of the year occur 53 times. For 53 Sundays, Sunday must be among the first two days, i.e. the year starts on Saturday or Sunday, so the answer is Saturday or Sunday.
Source: PYQ-style

114. The reflex angle between the hands at 10:25 is:
(a) 180° (b) 162.5° (c) 197.5° (d) 192.5°
Answer: (c)
Solution: Smaller angle = |30×10 − 11×25/2| = |300 − 137.5| = 162.5°. The tempting 162.5° is the smaller angle, but the question asks for the reflex angle = 360° − 162.5° = 197.5°, so the answer is 197.5°.

115. On what dates of March 2005 did Friday fall, if 1 March 2005 was a Tuesday?
(a) 4, 11, 18, 25 (b) 3, 10, 17, 24, 31 (c) 5, 12, 19, 26 (d) 2, 9, 16, 23, 30
Answer: (a)
Solution: 1 March is Tuesday, so the first Friday is 4 March; adding 7 repeatedly gives 4, 11, 18, 25. The next would be 1 April, outside March, so the answer is 4, 11, 18, 25.

116. A watch loses 3 minutes per hour. It is set right at 9 AM. What is the correct time when the watch shows 5 PM?
(a) 5:24 PM (b) 5:25 5/19 PM (c) 5:20 PM (d) 5:30 PM
Answer: (b)
Solution: The tempting 5:24 PM simply adds 8 × 3 = 24 minutes, but the watch's hours are slow hours: the watch covers only 57 minutes per true hour. True elapsed time = 480 × 60/57 = 9600/19 = 505 5/19 minutes = 8 h 25 5/19 min, so the correct time is 5:25 5/19 PM, so the answer is 5:25 5/19 PM.
Source: PYQ-style

117. How many times do the hands of a clock coincide in 12 hours?
(a) 13 (b) 12 (c) 10 (d) 11
Answer: (d)
Solution: The tempting 12 assumes one coincidence per hour, but between 11 and 1 the hands meet only once (at 12:00). So in 12 hours they coincide 11 times, so the answer is 11.

118. If the day before yesterday was Wednesday, what day will it be the day after tomorrow?
(a) Friday (b) Monday (c) Saturday (d) Sunday
Answer: (d)
Solution: Day before yesterday = Wednesday, so yesterday = Thursday and today = Friday; then tomorrow = Saturday and the day after tomorrow = Sunday. The trap is miscounting the chain by one step, which gives Saturday, so the answer is Sunday.

119. The calendar of the year 2007 can be used again in which year?
(a) 2013 (b) 2018 (c) 2012 (d) 2017
Answer: (b)
Solution: 2007 is an ordinary year; count odd days forward: 2007→2008 (+1), 2008 leap (+2), then +1 each year. Cumulative odd days reach a multiple of 7 in 2018 (1+2+1+1+1+2+1+1+1+2+1 = 14). So the calendar repeats in 2018, so the answer is 2018.
Source: PYQ-style

120. At 6:15, the angle between the hands of a clock is:
(a) 97.5° (b) 100° (c) 95° (d) 90°
Answer: (a)
Solution: The tempting 90° assumes the hour hand is exactly at 6, but at 6:15 it has moved 7.5° ahead. Angle = |30×6 − 11×15/2| = |180 − 82.5| = 97.5°, so the answer is 97.5°.
Source: PYQ-style

121. A clock is set right at 5 AM. It loses 16 minutes in 24 hours. What will be the correct time when the clock indicates 10 PM on the 4th day?
(a) 11:00 PM (b) 11:15 PM (c) 10:45 PM (d) 11:30 PM
Answer: (a)
Solution: From 5 AM day 1 to 10 PM day 4 the slow clock shows 89 hours. The slow clock covers 23 h 44 min (= 1424 min) per true 24 h (1440 min). True time elapsed = 89 × 1440/1424 = 90 hours, and 5 AM + 90 hours = 11 PM on the 4th day, so the answer is 11:00 PM.
Source: PYQ-style

122. How many times in a day are the hands of a clock in a straight line (coinciding or opposite)?
(a) 42 (b) 48 (c) 46 (d) 44
Answer: (d)
Solution: The tempting 48 adds 24 coincidences + 24 oppositions, but in 24 hours the hands coincide only 22 times and are opposite 22 times. Total = 22 + 22 = 44, so the answer is 44.

123. The last day of a century cannot be:
(a) Monday (b) Sunday (c) Friday (d) Tuesday
Answer: (d)
Solution: 100 years contain 5 odd days (Friday), 200 years contain 3 (Wednesday), 300 years contain 1 (Monday), 400 years contain 0 (Sunday). So a century can end only on Friday, Wednesday, Monday or Sunday — never Tuesday, Thursday or Saturday, so the answer is Tuesday.
Source: PYQ-style

124. At what time between 2 and 3 o'clock are the hands of a clock at right angles?
(a) 32 8/11 min past 2 (b) 38 2/11 min past 2 (c) 27 3/11 min past 2 (d) 40 min past 2
Answer: (c)
Solution: At 2:00 the minute hand is 10 spaces behind the hour hand; for a right angle it must end up 15 spaces ahead, i.e. gain 25 spaces. Time = 25 × 60/55 = 300/11 = 27 3/11 minutes. The tempting 32 8/11 min comes from using a gain of 30 spaces, so the answer is 27 3/11 min past 2.
Source: PYQ-style

125. If 1st January of a non-leap year is a Sunday, then how many Mondays and how many Sundays does that year have?
(a) 52 Mondays, 53 Sundays (b) 53 Mondays, 52 Sundays (c) 53 Mondays, 53 Sundays (d) 52 Mondays, 52 Sundays
Answer: (a)
Solution: A non-leap year has 52 weeks + 1 odd day, so only the first day of the year occurs 53 times. Since the year starts on Sunday, there are 53 Sundays and 52 Mondays. The tempting 53 Mondays reverses the logic, so the answer is 52 Mondays, 53 Sundays.
Source: PYQ-style
