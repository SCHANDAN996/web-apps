51. Statement: All pens are books. All books are chairs.
Conclusions:
I. All pens are chairs.
II. All chairs are pens.
(a) Neither I nor II follows (b) Only II follows (c) Both I and II follow (d) Only I follows
Answer: (d)
Solution: The tempting wrong choice is "Both follow" — students often reverse a valid chain and assume the converse also holds, but chairs may include many things other than pens, so II fails. From "All pens are books" and "All books are chairs", the chain gives "All pens are chairs", so I follows. Hence the answer is Only I follows.
Source: PYQ-style

52. Statement: Some dogs are cats. Some cats are rats.
Conclusions:
I. Some dogs are rats.
II. Some cats are dogs.
(a) Only II follows (b) Only I follows (c) Both I and II follow (d) Neither I nor II follows
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is assuming two "some" statements with a common middle term connect the extremes — the "some cats" in each premise may be entirely different groups, so I fails. However, "Some dogs are cats" directly converts to "Some cats are dogs", so II follows. Hence the answer is Only II follows.
