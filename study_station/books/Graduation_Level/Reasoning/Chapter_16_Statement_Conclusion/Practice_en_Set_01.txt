1. Statement: All pens are books. All books are chairs.
Conclusions: I. All pens are chairs. II. All chairs are pens.
(a) Only conclusion I follows (b) Only conclusion II follows (c) Both I and II follow (d) Neither I nor II follows
Answer: (a)
Solution: The tempting wrong choice is "Both follow" — students often reverse the chain and assume chairs must also be pens, but "All books are chairs" does not mean all chairs are books or pens. From the two universal statements, pens ⊆ books ⊆ chairs, so every pen is a chair, while conclusion II reverses the relation without support. Only conclusion I follows, so the answer is "Only conclusion I follows".
Source: PYQ-style

2. Statement: Some dogs are cats. Some cats are rats.
Conclusions: I. Some dogs are rats. II. Some cats are dogs.
(a) Only conclusion I follows (b) Both I and II follow (c) Only conclusion II follows (d) Neither I nor II follows
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is conclusion I — two "some" statements sharing a middle term never guarantee a connection between the extremes, so dogs and rats may have no overlap at all. Conclusion II is simply the conversion of "Some dogs are cats", which is always valid. Only conclusion II follows, so the answer is "Only conclusion II follows".
