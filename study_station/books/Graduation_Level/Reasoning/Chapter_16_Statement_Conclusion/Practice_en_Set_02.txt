Questions 26–50: Medium

26. Statement: All pens are books. All books are chairs.
Conclusions:
I. All pens are chairs.
II. All chairs are pens.
(a) Only I follows (b) Only II follows (c) Both I and II follow (d) Neither I nor II follows
Answer: (a)
Solution: The tempting trap is Conclusion II — students assume that if all pens are chairs, the reverse also holds. But "all chairs are pens" cannot be derived; chairs may include many things beyond pens. Since pens ⊆ books ⊆ chairs, every pen is definitely a chair, so the answer is that only Conclusion I follows.
Source: PYQ-style

27. Statement: Some dogs are cats. Some cats are rats.
Conclusions:
I. Some dogs are rats.
II. Some cats are dogs.
(a) Only I follows (b) Both I and II follow (c) Neither I nor II follows (d) Only II follows
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is Conclusion I — two "some" statements with a common middle term (cats) never yield a definite conclusion linking dogs and rats; the groups may not overlap at all. However, "some dogs are cats" directly converts to "some cats are dogs" by the rule of conversion. So the answer is that only Conclusion II follows.
