1. In a class of 60 students, 35 play cricket, 28 play football and 12 play both. How many students play neither game?
(a) 9 (b) 12 (c) 7 (d) 15
Answer: (a)
Solution: The tempting wrong answer is 12, but that is the number playing both, not neither. Students playing at least one game = 35 + 28 − 12 = 51, so neither = 60 − 51 = 9. So the answer is 9.
Source: PYQ-style

2. In a survey, 50 people like tea, 40 like coffee and 20 like both. How many like only tea?
(a) 30 (b) 20 (c) 50 (d) 10
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is answering 50, which includes those who like both. Only tea = 50 − 20 = 30. So the answer is 30.

3. Which Venn diagram best represents: Men, Fathers, Doctors?
(a) Three separate circles (b) Two circles inside one big circle (c) One circle inside another (d) Three intersecting circles
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is drawing separate circles, but some men are fathers, some fathers are doctors and some men are doctors, with no group fully inside another. So the answer is three intersecting circles.
Source: PYQ-style

4. Which Venn diagram best represents: Women, Mothers, Sisters?
(a) Three separate circles (b) Two intersecting circles inside a big circle (c) Three intersecting circles (d) Two separate circles inside a big circle
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is three intersecting circles, but every mother and every sister is a woman, so both must lie inside the women circle; since some mothers are sisters, the two inner circles intersect. So the answer is two intersecting circles inside a big circle.
Source: PYQ-style

5. Which diagram best represents: Animals, Dogs, Cats?
(a) Two intersecting circles inside a big circle (b) Two separate circles inside a big circle (c) Three intersecting circles (d) One circle inside another, third separate
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is drawing the inner circles intersecting, but no dog is a cat. All dogs and all cats are animals, so the two inner circles are disjoint. So the answer is two separate circles inside a big circle.

7. Out of 80 students, 50 passed in Maths, 40 passed in Science and 15 failed in both. How many passed in both subjects?
(a) 30 (b) 20 (c) 15 (d) 25
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is treating 15 as those passing both, but 15 failed both. Passing at least one = 80 − 15 = 65, so both = 50 + 40 − 65 = 25. So the answer is 25.

Solution: The trap is intersecting circles, but every part of India lies in Asia and all of Asia lies in the world, so the circles are fully nested. So the answer is India inside Asia inside World.

(a) 35 (b) 43 (c) 27 (d) 23
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is adding all three numbers to get 43, which double-counts the overlap. At least one = 20 + 15 − 8 = 27. So the answer is 27.

13. In a colony of 120 families, 70 read newspaper A, 50 read newspaper B and 30 read both. How many read neither?
(a) 30 (b) 20 (c) 40 (d) 10
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is stopping at 90, the number reading at least one newspaper. Neither = 120 − (70 + 50 − 30) = 120 − 90 = 30. So the answer is 30.

15. In a school, 60% of students play cricket and 50% play hockey. If every student plays at least one game, what percentage play both?
(a) 20% (b) 10% (c) 15% (d) 5%
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is subtracting 50 from 60 without using the union. Both = 60 + 50 − 100 = 10%. So the answer is 10%.

17. Out of 50 students, 30 like music, 25 like dance and 10 like both. How many like exactly one of the two?
(a) 25 (b) 30 (c) 35 (d) 40
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is answering 45, which is "at least one", or 10, which is "both". Only music = 20, only dance = 15, so exactly one = 20 + 15 = 35. So the answer is 35.

21. In a class of 100 students, 40 play cricket, 30 play football and 20 play hockey; 10 play cricket and football, 8 play football and hockey, 5 play cricket and hockey, and 3 play all three games. How many play none of the three games?
(a) 25 (b) 28 (c) 32 (d) 30
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is forgetting to add back the all-three group, which gives 33 instead of 30. At least one = 40 + 30 + 20 − 10 − 8 − 5 + 3 = 70, so none = 100 − 70 = 30. So the answer is 30.
Source: PYQ-style

22. In a survey of 200 people, 120 read magazine X, 90 read magazine Y and 40 read neither. How many read both magazines?
(a) 50 (b) 40 (c) 60 (d) 30
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is answering 40, which is the "neither" figure, not "both". Reading at least one = 200 − 40 = 160, so both = 120 + 90 − 160 = 50. So the answer is 50.

23. In a group, 45% like tea, 35% like coffee and 15% like both. What percentage like neither?
(a) 30% (b) 25% (c) 35% (d) 20%
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is subtracting the 15% "both" from 100 directly, giving 85%. At least one = 45 + 35 − 15 = 65%, so neither = 100 − 65 = 35%. So the answer is 35%.

24. In a class, 18 students play only cricket, 12 play only football and 8 play both. If 5 students play neither, what is the total strength of the class?
(a) 40 (b) 38 (c) 43 (d) 46
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is forgetting the "neither" group and answering 38. Total = 18 + 12 + 8 + 5 = 43. So the answer is 43.

25. In an examination, 65% passed in English, 55% passed in Maths and 25% failed in both. What percentage passed in both subjects?
(a) 50% (b) 45% (c) 35% (d) 40%
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is computing 65 + 55 − 100 = 20%, which ignores the 25% who failed both. Passed in at least one = 100 − 25 = 75%, so both = 65 + 55 − 75 = 45%. So the answer is 45%.
