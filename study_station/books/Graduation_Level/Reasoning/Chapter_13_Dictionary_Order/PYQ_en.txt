# 📊 PYQ Analysis — Dictionary Order (Verbal Reasoning)

## 1. How Exams Test This Chapter

Dictionary Order is a **low-frequency but high-certainty** chapter. It does not appear in every shift, but when it does, it is one of the most reliably scorable questions in the reasoning section — the answer is fully determined by the alphabet, with no ambiguity.

**Where it appears:**
- **SSC (CGL, CHSL, MTS, GD):** Typically 1 question in the reasoning section, placed among the easy opening questions. SSC favours the "arrange and find the middle/last word" format.
- **Banking (IBPS, SBI):** Rare as a standalone question; the skill is absorbed into alphabetical series and word-arrangement puzzles.
- **Railway (NTPC, Group D):** Appears occasionally, usually as a direct "which word comes first/last in a dictionary" question.
- **State exams:** Direct dictionary-order questions are common at the constable/clerk level.

## 2. Sub-topics That Recur (in words, not numbers)

1. **Direct dictionary arrangement** — 4–5 words given; arrange them as in a dictionary and identify the word at a given position (first, last, middle, second, etc.). *This is the dominant pattern.*
2. **Which word comes first / last** — without full arrangement; a pure comparison task.
3. **Words differing only at a late letter** — e.g., "Manage, Manner, Manual, Mango" — the trap is stopping the comparison too early.
4. **Letter-position coding mixed with dictionary order** — e.g., "if the letters of each word are reversed, which word comes first?" — a two-step variant seen at CGL level.
5. **Sequence of words as in a dictionary, then a counting question** — "how many words lie between X and Y?"

## 3. The Examiner's Traps (Psychological Hooks)

- **Premature comparison:** Words sharing a long common prefix (e.g., "Principal, Principle, Print, Priority"). Students who compare only 2–3 letters mis-order them. The deciding letter may be the 4th or 5th.
- **Near-identical spellings:** "Accept / Except", "Advice / Advise" — the trap is reading the word you *expect*, not the word written.
- **Position confusion:** The question asks for the word that comes **last**, but option (a) is the word that comes **first** — placed first to catch students who stop after finding the first word.
- **Middle-word ambiguity:** With 5 words, the middle is the 3rd; students who arrange only partially pick the 2nd or 4th.
- **Two-step reversal:** "Write each word in reverse, then arrange alphabetically" — students forget the reversal applies to *every* word before comparison.

## 4. High-Yield Model Questions (PYQ-style) with Solutions

**Q1.** Arrange the following words in dictionary order and find the word that comes in the middle:
(1) Qualify (2) Quarter (3) Quarrel (4) Quantity (5) Quench
(a) Qualify (b) Quarter (c) Quarrel (d) Quantity
**Answer: (c)**
**Solution:** All start with "Qu". Compare the third letter: Qualify/Quantity/Quarrel/Quarter have 'a', Quench has 'e', so Quench is last. Among the "Qua" words, the 4th letter decides: Qualify 'l', Quantity 'n', Quarrel 'r', Quarter 'r'. Since 'l' < 'n' < 'r', order so far: Qualify → Quantity → then the two "Quar" words. Quarrel vs Quarter: 5th letter 'r' vs 't', and 'r' < 't', so Quarrel → Quarter. Full order: Qualify → Quantity → Quarrel → Quarter → Quench. With 5 words, the middle is the 3rd = **Quarrel**. **Trap:** Quarrel vs Quarter — both start "Quar"; the 5th letter decides (r < t). Picking Quantity means the "Quar" pair was never separated.

**Q2.** Which of the following words will come **last** in a dictionary?
(a) Principal (b) Principle (c) Print (d) Priority
**Answer: (d)**
**Solution:** All start "Pri". 4th letter: Principal 'n', Principle 'n', Print 'n', Priority 'o'. Since 'o' > 'n', Priority comes after all three "Prin" words and is last. **Trap:** Students compare only "Prin" and pick Print; the 4th letter of Priority settles it immediately.

**Q3.** Arrange in dictionary order: (1) Manage (2) Manner (3) Mango (4) Manual
(a) 1, 2, 3, 4 (b) 1, 3, 2, 4 (c) 2, 1, 3, 4 (d) 1, 3, 4, 2
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** All share "Man". 4th letter: Manage 'a', Mango 'g', Manner 'n', Manual 'u'. Since 'a' < 'g' < 'n' < 'u', the order is Manage → Mango → Manner → Manual = 1, 3, 2, 4. **Trap:** "Manner" looks like it should precede "Mango" — but 'g' < 'n'.

**Q4.** Which word comes **first** in a dictionary?
(a) Excite (b) Exclaim (c) Exclude (d) Excuse
**Answer: (a)**
**Solution:** All start "Exc". 4th letter: Excite 'i', Exclaim 'l', Exclude 'l', Excuse 'u'. Since 'i' < 'l' < 'u', Excite comes first. (For the record, the full order is Excite → Exclaim → Exclude → Excuse; Exclaim beats Exclude at the 5th letter, 'a' < 'u'.) **Trap:** This question punishes hasty letter-position recall — always verify that 'i' (9th letter) comes before 'l' (12th).

**Q5.** If each word is written with its letters in reverse order, which will come first in a dictionary?
(a) STOP (b) POST (c) SPOT (d) TOPS
**Answer: (a)**
**Solution:** Reversed: STOP→POTS, POST→TSOP, SPOT→TOPS, TOPS→SPOT. Alphabetical order of the reversed words: POTS → SPOT → TOPS → TSOP. First = POTS, i.e., the original word STOP. **Trap:** Students who skip reversing and compare the originals pick POST.

**Q6.** In dictionary order, how many of the following words come between "Market" and "Master"?
Marble, Marvel, Masculine, Mask, Meadow
(a) 1 (b) 2 (c) 3 (d) 4
**Answer: (c)**
**Solution:** Market = "Mark…", Master = "Mast…". A word lies between them only if it comes after "Mark" and before "Mast". Check each: Marble (Mar-b) — 'b' < 'k', comes *before* Market, excluded. Marvel (Mar-v) — 'v' > 'k', after Market ✓, and "Mar" < "Mas", before Master ✓. Masculine (Mas-c) — 'c' < 't', before Master ✓, and "Mas" > "Mar", after Market ✓. Mask (Mas-k) — 'k' < 't', before Master ✓, after Market ✓. Meadow (Me) — 'e' > 'a', comes *after* Master, excluded. Count = Marvel, Masculine, Mask = **3**. **Trap:** Boundary words (Marble before Market; Meadow after Master) must be excluded — this is the classic counting trap.

**Q7.** Which word will come **second** in dictionary order?
(a) Frighten (b) Friction (c) Friendly (d) Frugal
**Answer: (c)**
**Solution:** All start "Fr". 3rd letter: Frighten 'i', Friction 'i', Friendly 'i', Frugal 'u' — so Frugal is last. Among the three "Fri" words, compare the 4th letter: Frighten 'g', Friction 'c', Friendly 'e'. Since 'c' < 'e' < 'g', the order is Friction → Friendly → Frighten → Frugal. Second = **Friendly**. **Trap:** Three words tied until the 4th letter — partial comparison gives the wrong "second".

**Q8.** Arrange in dictionary order and pick the last word:
(1) Humble (2) Humid (3) Humour (4) Hundred
(a) Humble (b) Humid (c) Humour (d) Hundred
**Answer: (d)**
**Solution:** Humble (Hum-b), Humid (Hum-i), Humour (Hum-o), Hundred (Hun). Compare the 3rd letter: 'm' (Humble/Humid/Humour) vs 'n' (Hundred). Since 'n' > 'm', Hundred comes after all three "Hum" words and is last. **Trap:** "Hundred" looks shorter/simpler and students assume short words come first — length is irrelevant; letters decide.

## 5. The 15-Second / 45-Second Rule — Applied

- **Attempt in ≤15 seconds:** Direct "first/last word" questions where one word separates from the pack at a single deciding letter (Q2, Q8 type — in Q2 the 4th letter 'o' vs 'n' settles it at a glance). Spot the deciding letter, mark, move on.
- **Attempt in ≤45 seconds:** Full arrangement of 4–5 words with a shared prefix (Q1, Q3, Q7 type). Write the deciding letters in a column; do not arrange mentally.
- **SKIP and return:** Two-step variants (reverse-then-arrange, Q5 type) and between-counting questions (Q6 type) when they appear late in the section — they carry the same 1 mark but double the error risk under time pressure. Attempt them only in the second pass.

## 6. Bottom Line

Dictionary Order questions are **pattern-fixed**: the examiner can only vary the shared-prefix length and the position asked. Master the "compare letter-by-letter, never stop early" habit, and every question in this chapter becomes a guaranteed mark in under 45 seconds.
