Card 1
Front: What is the very first step when arranging words in dictionary order?
Back: Compare the first letter of every word. The word whose first letter comes earliest in the alphabet (A→Z) is placed first. Only when first letters are identical do you move to the second letter.

Card 2
Front: Two words share the same first three letters. How do you decide which comes first?
Back: Move to the fourth letter and compare. Keep shifting right letter by letter until you find the first position where the letters differ — that letter decides the order.

Card 3
Front: "CAT" and "CATALOGUE" — which appears first in a dictionary, and why?
Back: CAT comes first. Rule: when one word is a complete prefix of another (all letters match but one word ends), the shorter word comes first.

Card 4
Front: In "which word comes FIRST" questions, what is the fastest elimination trick?
Back: Scan only the first letters of all options. Eliminate every word whose first letter is not the alphabetically earliest. Usually only 1–2 words survive, saving you from full comparison.

Card 5
Front: In "which word comes LAST" questions, where do most students waste time?
Back: They compare all words fully. Instead, find the alphabetically latest first letter; if several words share it, compare only those words' second letters, and so on.

Card 6
Front: What is the mnemonic to remember the dictionary-order working rule?
Back: "Left to Right, First Difference Decides" — always scan letters from left to right, and the FIRST position where two words differ settles their order. Never look further right once a difference is found.

Card 7
Front: What mnemonic helps you recall the short-word rule?
Back: "Short before Long" — if one word is entirely contained at the start of another (e.g., PEN vs PENCIL), the shorter one sits earlier in the dictionary.

Card 8
Front: Examiner's trap: "APPLE, APPLICATION, APPLY, APPLAUSE" — what mistake do students make here?
Back: They stop after "APPL" and guess. All four share A-P-P-L; the 5th letter decides: APPLAUSE (A), APPLE (E), APPLICATION (I), APPLY (Y). Since A < E < I < Y, the correct order is: APPLAUSE < APPLE < APPLICATION < APPLY.

Card 9
Front: Examiner's trap: a question asks "which word comes THIRD in dictionary order" — what is the hidden danger?
Back: Students arrange the words and pick the third, but forget to check whether the question wants dictionary order or reverse dictionary order. Always underline FIRST/LAST and the position number before solving.

Card 10
Front: How do you handle words that differ only in capital vs small letters in exam questions?
Back: In competitive exams, treat all letters as the same case — dictionary order ignores capitalization. Compare letters purely by alphabet position.

Card 11
Front: When arranging 5 words, how many letters deep do you usually need to go?
Back: Rarely beyond the 2nd or 3rd letter. Examiners design options so that early letters separate most words; only one or two pairs need deeper comparison.

Card 12
Front: What is "reverse dictionary order" and how is it tested?
Back: Words arranged Z→A (last dictionary word first). If asked "which comes first in reverse order," simply find the word that would come LAST in normal dictionary order — no need to rearrange everything.

Card 13
Front: In letter-position questions (e.g., "which letter is 5th from the left"), what quick method avoids errors?
Back: Write the alphabet positions as numbers (A=1 … Z=26) above the letters, then count directly. Numbering beats finger-counting and prevents off-by-one mistakes.

Card 14
Front: What is the EJOTY trick and how does it help in dictionary questions?
Back: EJOTY marks letters at positions 5, 10, 15, 20, 25 (E, J, O, T, Y). These anchor points let you instantly locate any letter's position, speeding up first-letter comparisons.

Card 15
Front: If options are "MANGO, MANGOES, MANGROVE, MANAGE", which comes first?
Back: MANAGE. All four share MAN; compare the 4th letter: MANAGE (A), MANGO/MANGOES (G), MANGROVE (R). Since A < G < R, MANAGE comes first. (Full order: MANAGE < MANGO < MANGOES < MANGROVE — MANGO before MANGOES by the short-word rule.)

Card 16
Front: Why should you never compare the second letters of two words whose first letters already differ?
Back: Dictionary order is decided at the FIRST point of difference only. If first letters differ, later letters are irrelevant — comparing them wastes time and can trick you into a wrong answer.

Card 17
Front: In "arrange as in a dictionary and find the middle word" questions, what is the shortcut for 5 words?
Back: You need the 3rd word. Often you can identify it by ranking only first letters; full arrangement of all five is unnecessary if first letters are all distinct.

Card 18
Front: What should you do in the last 10 seconds if a dictionary-order question looks long?
Back: Compare only first letters of the options and match with the answer choices. If that fails, compare second letters of the tied words. Never start writing full arrangements under time pressure — guess after one-letter elimination.

Card 19
Front: Do numbers or hyphens inside words change dictionary order in exam questions?
Back: Such cases almost never appear in SSC/Bank exams. Standard rule if they do: numbers come before letters, and hyphens/spaces are ignored. Focus your practice on plain alphabetic words.

Card 20
Front: What single habit prevents most dictionary-order errors?
Back: Writing the words vertically, one under another, aligning letters in columns. The first column where letters differ becomes visually obvious, eliminating careless comparison slips.
