# 🪄 Short Tricks & Mnemonics — Dictionary Order

---

**Box 1 — "ALPHABET ARMY"**
The alphabet is your army of 26 soldiers standing in a fixed queue. A is the General (position 1), Z is the last recruit (position 26). No soldier ever leaves his position — so in any dictionary question, just ask: *"Who stands first in the army queue?"*

---

**Box 2 — "EJOTY: The Five Checkpoints"**
**E-J-O-T-Y = 5-10-15-20-25.** Remember it as *"Enjoy On The Yacht"* — five checkpoints of the alphabet highway. If you know these five, any letter's position is at most 4 steps away. M? O is 15, so M is 13. R? T is 20, so R is 18.

---

**Box 3 — "First Letter First, Rest Can Wait"**
When arranging words, shout the rule: *"Don't read the whole word — read letter by letter!"* Compare the 1st letters of ALL words first. Only the tied words go to the 2nd letter round. It's a knockout tournament, letter by letter.

---

**Box 4 — "The SHORT Word Wins"**
If one word is completely inside another (CAT vs CATTLE), the **shorter word comes first** — because in a dictionary, "nothing" beats "something." Mnemonic: *"The short guest enters the house first."* CAT → CATALOGUE → CATTLE? No wait — compare letter 4: nothing (CAT) < A (CATALOGUE) < T (CATTLE). Nothing first!

---

**Box 5 — "ZEBRA Goes Last"**
Picture a zebra standing at the very end of the dictionary. Any word starting with Z is almost always last. Quick elimination: in "which word comes last" questions, hunt for Z, Y, X, W first — don't waste time on words starting with A–M.

---

**Box 6 — "A-M-N-Z: The Two Halves"**
The alphabet has two halves: **A–M (1–13)** and **N–Z (14–26)**. Mnemonic: *"AM before noon, NZ after noon."* If a question asks "which letter is 13th," it's M — the last letter of the morning!

---

**Box 7 — "Reverse = 27 Minus"**
Position from the right (Z-side) = **27 − position from left**. Mnemonic: *"27 is the magic mirror."* K is 11th from left → 27 − 11 = 16th from right. One subtraction, zero counting backwards.

---

**Box 8 — "Vowel Vendetta"**
Vowels A-E-I-O-U sit at positions **1-5-9-15-21**. Mnemonic: *"All Elephants In Orange Underwear"* — odd positions mostly! Handy when options contain vowel-starting words: A always beats E, E always beats I.

---

**Box 9 — "The Twin Trap"**
When two words share a long prefix (PERFORM, PERFUME), don't panic — walk letter by letter until they **divorce**. PER-F-O vs PER-F-U: they split at the 5th letter. O (15) < U (20), so PERFORM first. Mnemonic: *"Follow the twins until they fight."*

---

**Box 10 — "Dictionary = Phone Contact List"**
Your phone sorts names exactly like a dictionary. Imagine typing each word into your contacts — whose name pops up first when you scroll? That mental picture kills confusion in "arrange in dictionary order" questions.

---

**Box 11 — "Middle Letter M-N"**
The exact middle of the alphabet is between **M (13) and N (14)**. Mnemonic: *"M and N are the two goalposts of the alphabet midfield."* Any "middle letter" question is solved instantly with this pair.

---

**Box 12 — "Count on Fingers: +1 Rule"**
"How many letters between X and Y?" = position(Y) − position(X) − **1**. Mnemonic: *"Between means the fence, not the houses."* Between D(4) and H(8): 8 − 4 − 1 = 3 letters (E, F, G). Forgetting the −1 is the examiner's favourite trap!

---

**Box 13 — "Last-Letter Lottery"**
In "which word comes LAST" questions, don't arrange everything. Just find the word whose **first letter is biggest** in the alphabet. If two tie, move to the next letter — but only between the tied words. You solve a 5-word question by reading maybe 6 letters total.

---

## ⏭️ Skip Strategy — When to Skip

1. **The 15-Second Rule:** If after 15 seconds you haven't identified *what type* of question it is (arrangement / position / between-letters), mark it and move on. Dictionary questions are meant to be 20–30 second earners — a confusing one is a time trap.

2. **Skip the "Long List, All Similar" question:** If 5 words share the same first 3–4 letters (e.g., STRAIN, STRAIT, STRAND, STRANGE, STRATA) and the exam is in its final minutes, skip it on the first pass. These demand letter-by-letter comparison up to the 4th–5th letter — high accuracy cost under pressure. Return only if time remains.

3. **Skip "position from right" chains without EJOTY:** If a question stacks two operations ("7th to the right of the 10th letter from the right end") and you can't recall the 27-minus trick instantly, don't count on fingers under time pressure — one miscount = −0.5. Flag it, solve it calmly in the second round.
