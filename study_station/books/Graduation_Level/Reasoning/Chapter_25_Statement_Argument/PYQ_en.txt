# PYQ Analysis — Statement and Argument

## 1. How Often It Appears

Statement–argument questions appear regularly in banking (IBPS/SBI PO), insurance, some SSC papers and state PCS prelims. Bank PO papers usually carry a few questions from the "critical reasoning" family, of which this is one. Exact yearly counts vary, so this analysis describes the pattern rather than quoting numbers.

## 2. Sub-topic Weightage (qualitative)

- **Policy questions (ban, make compulsory, allow)** — the most common format.
- **Economic issues (subsidies, taxes, privatisation)** — frequent in bank papers.
- **Social issues (education, health, environment)** — common in all exams.
- **"Both strong" answers** — increasingly common at Graduation level.

## 3. Exam-wise Tendencies

- **Banking:** economy and governance topics; both-strong and only-one-strong answers.
- **SSC:** simpler social issues; weak arguments are easy to spot by extreme words.
- **State PCS:** governance and rights issues; arguments are longer.

## 4. Ten High-Yield Questions (PYQ-style, with solutions)

**1.** Should helmets be compulsory for two-wheeler riders? I. Yes, they reduce head injuries. II. No, they spoil hairstyles. → **Only I** (II is trivial).
**2.** Should the retirement age be raised to 65? I. Yes, experienced staff can serve longer. II. No, it reduces openings for the young. → **Both** (real benefit and real cost).
**3.** Should India stop all imports? I. Yes, then everything will be made in India. II. No, India needs crude oil imports. → **Only II** (I is an unfounded assumption).
**4.** Should all shops stay open 24 hours? I. Yes, shopkeepers will become very rich. II. No, all customers will stop shopping at night. → **Neither**.
**5.** Should the government ban firecrackers? I. Yes, they cause pollution. II. No, other countries also use them. → **Only I** (II is not a reason by itself).
**6.** Should private cars be banned in city centres? I. Yes, it reduces jams and pollution. II. No, public transport is not yet enough. → **Both**.
**7.** Should mobile towers be removed from residential areas? I. Yes, all diseases are caused by towers. II. No, it would weaken mobile service. → **Only II** (I uses "all").
**8.** Should students get grace marks? I. Yes, every student with grace marks becomes a doctor. II. No, grace marks are written in red ink. → **Neither** (absurd and irrelevant).
**9.** Should cash transactions above a limit be banned? I. Yes, large hidden payments become harder. II. No, people without bank access may face difficulty. → **Both**.
**10.** Should India reduce forest cover for industry? I. Yes, factories look more modern than forests. II. No, forests protect water, soil and biodiversity. → **Only II**.

Hook in most of these: the weak argument sounds confident; the student who judges by confidence instead of reason loses the mark.

## 5. Skip Guidance (15-second / 45-second rule)

- Questions 4 and 8 above should take under 15 seconds once you spot "all", "every" or irrelevance.
- If two serious-looking arguments still confuse you after 45 seconds (like question 9), mark and return.

## 6. Examiner's Traps Seen Again and Again

1. Judging by personal opinion.
2. Accepting emotional or dramatic arguments.
3. Missing that both arguments are strong.
