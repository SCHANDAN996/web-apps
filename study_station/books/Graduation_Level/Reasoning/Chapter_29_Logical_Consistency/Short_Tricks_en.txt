# 🪄 Short Tricks & Mnemonics — Logical Consistency

---

## 🧠 Mnemonic 1: The "ALL-SOME-NO" Triangle

**"Aunt Sally Never Argues"**
- **A**LL → **S**OME → **N**O → **A**rgue (contradiction)
- The three statement types (All, Some, No) and the fourth step: check for contradiction.
- Whenever a consistency question appears, chant: *"Aunt Sally Never Argues"* — list each statement as ALL / SOME / NO, then hunt for the pair that argues (contradicts).

---

## 🧠 Mnemonic 2: Consistency vs. Truth

**"Friends can LIE together, but they can't FIGHT together."**
- Consistent statements = friends telling the same lie (all false, but no clash).
- Inconsistent statements = friends fighting (they contradict each other).
- **Truth doesn't matter — only harmony matters.** A set of false statements can still be consistent!

---

## 🧠 Mnemonic 3: The Contradiction Pairs

**"A-O fight, E-I fight"** → remember **"AO, EI"** — the only two deadly pairs.
- **A** (All S is P) contradicts **O** (Some S is not P)
- **E** (No S is P) contradicts **I** (Some S is P)
- Spot one of these two pairs → the set is inconsistent.
- ⚠️ Don't confuse these with look-alikes:
  - **A–E** ("All S is P" vs. "No S is P") are **contraries** — they cannot both be true, but both can be false. They clash on truth, but they are NOT strict contradictions.
  - **I–O** ("Some S is P" vs. "Some S is not P") are **subcontraries** — they cannot both be false, but both can be true. They do NOT contradict each other.
- For consistency questions, only the A–O and E–I pairs guarantee a logical contradiction.

---

## 🧠 Mnemonic 4: "Some" Never Means "All"

**"SOME is a shy guest — it never claims the whole house."**
- "Some cats are black" does NOT mean "All cats are black."
- Examiner's trap: treating "Some" as "All" or "Some not" as "No."
- Rule: **Some = at least one, possibly more — never assume the whole group.**

---

## 🧠 Mnemonic 5: The Negation Flip

**"FLIP the coin: ALL↔SOME-NOT, NO↔SOME"**
- Negation of "All are" = "Some are not" (NOT "None are"!)
- Negation of "No S is P" = "Some S is P"
- Classic trap: students negate "All" with "No." Remember — **the opposite of ALL is not NONE, it's SOME-NOT.**

---

## 🧠 Mnemonic 6: Consistency Check Order

**"P-C-N: Pair, Clash, Nullify"**
- **P**air up the statements (convert each to ALL/SOME/NO form)
- **C**lash check — does any pair contradict?
- **N**ullify — if a clash exists, the set is inconsistent; if not, consistent.
- Three steps, every time. Never skip the Pair step.

---

## 🧠 Mnemonic 7: "Only" = Reverse Arrow

**"ONLY is a U-turn sign 🔄"**
- "Only A are B" = "All B are A" (the arrow REVERSES!)
- "Only graduates are eligible" → "All eligible are graduates."
- Trap: reading it as "All A are B." When you see ONLY, make a U-turn.

---

## 🧠 Mnemonic 8: Possibility vs. Certainty

**"CAN is cheap, MUST is costly."**
- A conclusion with "can be / possibly" is cheap — easy to satisfy, usually consistent.
- A conclusion with "must be / definitely" is costly — needs full proof.
- In consistency questions, **possibility statements survive; certainty statements die first.**

---

## 🧠 Mnemonic 9: The Common-Term Bridge

**"No bridge, no conclusion."**
- Two statements can only clash or combine if they share a **common term** (the bridge).
- "All A are B" + "All C are D" → no common term → they can NEVER contradict each other.
- Before checking contradiction, ask: *"Is there a bridge between these statements?"*

---

## 🧠 Mnemonic 10: Assumption = Hidden Foundation

**"Assumption is the floor under the statement — remove it, the statement falls."**
- Test: negate the assumption. If the statement collapses, it was a real assumption.
- **Negation Test = the bulldozer test.** If bulldozing the assumption destroys the building (statement), it was load-bearing.

---

## 🧠 Mnemonic 11: Strong vs. Weak Arguments

**"A strong argument goes to the ROOT, a weak one goes to the FRUIT."**
- Strong: addresses the core issue, practical, logical, long-term.
- Weak: emotional, trivial, example-based, "because I said so."
- Trap words that signal WEAK: *"some people feel…", "it has always been…", "in one village…"*

---

## 🧠 Mnemonic 12: The "Both Consistent" Trap

**"Two strangers in a lift don't fight — but they're not friends either."**
- Two statements can be consistent (no contradiction) WITHOUT one following from the other.
- Consistent ≠ Conclusion follows. Don't upgrade "no clash" into "therefore true."

---

## 🧠 Mnemonic 13: Course of Action — "PRACTICAL"

**"Is it P-R-A-C? Practical, Realistic, Addresses the Cause?"**
- A valid course of action must be **P**ractical, **R**ealistic, and **A**ddress the **C**ause (not just the symptom).
- Reject actions that are extreme ("shut down all schools"), illegal, or only cosmetic.

---

## 🧠 Mnemonic 14: The 3-Statement Chain

**"Chain breaks at the weakest SOME."**
- All + All = All (strong chain)
- All + Some = Some (chain weakens)
- Some + Some = NO definite conclusion (chain breaks)
- **Two "Some" statements can never produce a definite conclusion together.**

---

## ⏭️ Skip Strategy — When to Skip

### Tip 1: The 20-Second Rule
If after reading all statements you cannot convert them into ALL/SOME/NO form within **20 seconds**, mark and skip. Consistency questions with 4+ tangled statements eat 2–3 minutes — return only if time remains.

### Tip 2: Skip "Double-Negative" Puzzles on First Pass
Statements like *"It is not true that no A are not B"* require two negation flips. High error risk under pressure. **Attempt these only in the second round** — one wrong flip flips the answer.

### Tip 3: Skip When Options Are Near-Identical
If options differ only by one word ("must" vs. "can", "all" vs. "some"), the question is designed to trap. If you can't spot the decisive word difference in **15 seconds**, skip — guessing here is risky because the trap option is usually the most attractive one.
