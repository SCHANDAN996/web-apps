101. Statement: All pens are books. All books are chairs. Conclusion I: All pens are chairs. Conclusion II: Some chairs are pens.
(a) Only II follows (b) Both I and II follow (c) Only I follows (d) Neither follows
Answer: (b)
Solution: The tempting error is to accept only Conclusion I and forget that "All pens are chairs" automatically guarantees "Some chairs are pens" by conversion. Since pens are inside books and books are inside chairs, pens are inside chairs, so the answer is that both I and II follow.

102. Statement: Some dogs are cats. No cat is a rat. Conclusion I: Some dogs are not rats. Conclusion II: No dog is a rat.
(a) Only I follows (b) Only II follows (c) Both follow (d) Neither follows
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is Conclusion II — a universal negative cannot be drawn when only part of the dogs overlaps with cats. The dogs that are cats cannot be rats, so "Some dogs are not rats" is definite, while other dogs may be rats, so the answer is that only I follows.
