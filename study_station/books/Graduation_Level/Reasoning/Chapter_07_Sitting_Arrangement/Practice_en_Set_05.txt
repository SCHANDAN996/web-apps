101. Eight persons A, B, C, D, E, F, G and H sit around a circular table facing the centre. A sits second to the left of B. C sits opposite A. D is an immediate neighbour of C but not of A. E sits third to the right of D. Who sits opposite B?
(a) G (b) E (c) F (d) D
Answer: (b)
Solution: The tempting wrong choice is D, but D sits next to C, not opposite B. Place B, then A second to B's left; C is opposite A; D must neighbour C on the side away from A (the other side makes E land on A's seat); E third to D's right then lands exactly opposite B, so the answer is E.
Source: PYQ-style

102. Six persons P, Q, R, S, T and U sit in a row facing north. P sits at one end. Q sits second to the right of P. R sits between Q and S. T is not adjacent to P. Who sits at the other end?
(a) U (b) R (c) S (d) T
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is placing U at the far end by filling seats mechanically. From P's end the order is P, _, Q, R, S, _; T cannot sit next to P, so T takes the last seat and U sits beside P, so the answer is T.

103. In a circular arrangement of 8 people facing the centre, M sits third to the left of N, and O sits second to the right of N. How many people sit between M and O on the shorter arc?
(a) Two (b) One (c) Four (d) Three
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is counting seats instead of people between. From N, M is 3 seats anti-clockwise and O is 2 seats clockwise, so the gap spans 5 positions one way and 3 the other; the shorter arc contains 2 people, so the answer is Two.

104. A, B, C, D, E and F sit in two rows of three facing each other (row 1 faces south, row 2 faces north). A faces D. B sits to the right of A. E faces B. Who faces C?
(a) D (b) F (c) E (d) A
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is mixing up left-right for south-facing persons. A faces D and B faces E, so the three facing pairs are fixed as A–D, B–E, and the only remaining pair is C with F, so the answer is F.

105. Seven persons sit in a row facing north. G sits fourth from the left. H sits to the immediate right of G. I sits exactly between H and J. If J sits at the right end, how many seats are between G and J?
(a) Three (b) One (c) Two (d) Four
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is answering three by counting inclusively. G is 4th, H is 5th, J is 7th (right end), so I sits 6th; between G (4th) and J (7th) there are exactly seats 5 and 6, so the answer is Two.

108. Five persons V, W, X, Y, Z sit in a row facing south. V sits in the middle. W sits to the immediate right of V. X sits to the immediate left of V. Who sits second from the right end of the row as seen by a viewer facing them?
(a) Y (b) V (c) X (d) W
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is ignoring the facing direction: for a south-facing person, their left is the viewer's right. V is in the middle, W sits on V's right (viewer's left) and X on V's left (viewer's right), making X second from the viewer's right end, so the answer is X.

111. In a row of 10 persons facing north, J sits fourth from the left and K sits fourth from the right. How many people sit between J and K?
(a) Two (b) Three (c) None (d) One
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is computing K's position as 6th from the left by forgetting the +1 in the conversion. Fourth from the right in 10 seats is position 10 − 4 + 1 = 7, so between positions 4 and 7 lie positions 5 and 6, so the answer is Two.

119. Eight persons sit at a square table, two per side, all facing the centre. A sits on the north side. B sits on the side opposite A's side. C sits to the immediate right of A. D sits on the same side as B, at B's left. Who sits diagonally opposite C?
(a) A (b) B (c) D (d) Cannot be determined
Answer: (b)
Solution: The tempting wrong answer is D, but D sits at the south-west seat, which is diagonally opposite the north-east seat, not C's. A faces the centre (southward), so A's right is the viewer's west; for C to fit at A's right, A must be at the north-east seat and C at the north-west seat. Similarly B must be at the south-east seat with D at the south-west. The seat diagonally opposite C (north-west) is the south-east seat, so the answer is B.
