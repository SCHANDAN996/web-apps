# 🪄 Short Tricks & Mnemonics — Sitting Arrangement (Complex)

---

**Box 1 — "FACE the CIRCLE"**
> **F**acing **A**nd **C**entre **E**verything — Inward-facing circle? Everyone faces the centre. Left = clockwise, Right = anti-clockwise. Say it: *"FACE the circle, or the circle faces you out!"*

---

**Box 2 — "OUT is ODD"**
> Outward-facing circle? Everything flips — Left becomes anti-clockwise, Right becomes clockwise. Memory hook: *"When they look OUT, the rules turn OUT-side down."*

---

**Box 3 — "The Anchor First"**
> Never place people randomly. Find the line with the MOST links ("A sits third to the left of B, who faces C…") and drop that **anchor** first. Chant: *"Anchor down, or drown!"*

---

**Box 4 — "Two Chairs, One Truth"**
> Stuck between two possible positions? Draw **both** mini-diagrams. One will die when a later clue contradicts it. *"Two chairs enter, one truth leaves."*

---

**Box 5 — "LEFT-RIGHT Mirror Trick"**
> In a row facing **North**: Left = West (your left on paper), Right = East.
> Facing **South**: mirror it — Left = East, Right = West.
> Mnemonic: *"South is a selfie — everything's mirrored!"*

---

**Box 6 — "Immediate = Glue, Between = Sandwich"**
> "Immediate neighbour" = glued pair — treat them as ONE block.
> "X sits between Y and Z" = sandwich — X is the filling, order of Y/Z unknown until another clue.
> *"Glue the pairs, sandwich the middles."*

---

**Box 7 — "Second to the Right = Skip One"**
> "Second to the right" means skip exactly ONE seat. Count: neighbour (1st), next (2nd). Never place directly adjacent — that's the classic trap. *"Second means skip one, or you're done!"*

---

**Box 8 — "Rectangle: Everyone Has Two Neighbours"**
> In a rectangle or square seating loop, **every** person — corner or middle of a side — has exactly **2** immediate neighbours (one on the left, one on the right). The real difference: corner people face along a side, while side-middle people may face inward/outward — read the facing clue carefully. *"Corner or side, two neighbours ride."*

---

**Box 9 — "The Negative Clue Goldmine"**
> "A does NOT sit next to B" feels useless — but after placing 5 people, it often solves everything. Write negatives as ❌ marks on seats. *"Negatives are slow poison — they kill options last."*

---

**Box 10 — "Blood-Relation Seating: Family Tree First"**
> If the puzzle mixes seating + relations, draw the **family tree BEFORE the circle**. Placing people first = chaos. *"Tree before table — always."*

---

**Box 11 — "Opposite = Halfway Round"**
> In an 8-person circle, opposite = 4 seats away; in 6-person, 3 away. Quick check: *"Opposite is half the party away."*

---

**Box 12 — "The 3-Clue Rule"**
> If after reading ALL clues you can't place even 3 people with certainty, the puzzle is a multi-case monster — budget your time accordingly. *"No three in thirty? Mark and flee."*

---

**Box 13 — "Fixed Ends First (Row Puzzles)"**
> In linear rows, clues like "P sits at an extreme end" are gold — only 2 options. Lock ends, then work inward. *"Ends first, middle follows."*

---

**Box 14 — "One Diagram, One Pencil Rule"**
> Redrawing the diagram for every sub-question wastes 40+ seconds. Make ONE clean master diagram with initials only (A, B, C…). *"One diagram to rule them all."*

---

## ⏭️ Skip Strategy — When to Walk Away

**Tip 1 — The 15-Second Scan:** Read all clues once. If you cannot identify a single definite anchor (fixed position or glued pair) within 15 seconds, **mark it and move on** — return only after finishing easier questions.

**Tip 2 — The Two-Case Explosion:** If a puzzle forces you into 2 parallel diagrams and after 60 seconds neither case has collapsed, **skip**. Complex arrangement sets with multiple cases are time-traps designed to eat 5+ minutes for 1 mark.

**Tip 3 — Partial Credit Thinking:** In a 5-question puzzle set, if you've solved the seating for 6 of 8 people, attempt the 2–3 questions answerable from your partial diagram and **skip the rest**. Never restart the whole puzzle for one stubborn sub-question — the 15/45-second rule applies: 15 seconds to decide the approach, 45 seconds per sub-question maximum.
