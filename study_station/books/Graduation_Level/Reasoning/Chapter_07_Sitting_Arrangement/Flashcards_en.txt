Card 1
Front: In a circular arrangement where everyone faces the centre, which side is a person's "left"?
Back: Anticlockwise direction. When facing the centre, left = anticlockwise, right = clockwise. This is the single most-used rule in circle puzzles.

Card 2
Front: How do left and right flip when people face OUTWARD (away from the centre) in a circle?
Back: They reverse: left = clockwise, right = anticlockwise. Always check the facing direction before marking any neighbour.

Card 3
Front: What is the very first thing you should fix before placing anyone in a seating puzzle?
Back: A reference point — either a fixed person ("A sits at one end") or a direction marker (North/top). Every other position is then plotted relative to it.

Card 4
Front: In a row of people facing North, a person's right hand points which way on your diagram?
Back: To your right (East). For North-facing rows, the diagram's left/right matches the person's left/right. For South-facing rows, it mirrors.

Card 5
Front: What does "A sits second to the right of B" actually mean in terms of positions?
Back: Exactly one person sits between A and B, and A is on B's right side. "Second to the right" = gap of one seat, not adjacent.

Card 6
Front: What is the difference between "immediate neighbour" and "sits to the right of"?
Back: "Immediate neighbour" means adjacent (no gap). "Sits to the right of" (without "immediate") can mean anywhere on that side — a classic trap.

Card 7
Front: Mnemonic trick: how do you remember the facing-direction rule for circles?
Back: "IN-AC, OUT-C" — facing INward, left is AntiClockwise; facing OUTward, left is Clockwise. Say it once before every circle puzzle.

Card 8
Front: Mnemonic trick: how do you quickly handle "between" clues like "C sits between A and B"?
Back: Use the "sandwich rule": draw A–C–B as a block, but remember it can flip to B–C–A unless another clue fixes the order. Keep both cases open.

Card 9
Front: Examiner's trap: "P is third to the left of Q" in a circle of 8 — what mistake do most students make?
Back: They count Q itself as position 1. Correct method: move 3 seats from Q excluding Q. Counting the starting person shifts the whole arrangement by one seat.

Card 10
Front: Examiner's trap: in a two-row puzzle (Row 1 facing South, Row 2 facing North), what goes wrong when matching "opposite" pairs?
Back: Students assume left/right is the same for both rows. South-facing people have mirrored left/right, so "left of X" in Row 1 points the opposite way on the diagram compared to Row 2.

Card 11
Front: When a puzzle gives a negative clue like "D is NOT next to E", how should you use it?
Back: Park it. Negative clues rarely fix a position alone; use them at the end to eliminate one of your open cases. Never start a diagram with a negative clue.

Card 12
Front: In a rectangular table with people at corners and mid-sides, what must you confirm before applying left/right rules?
Back: Who faces the centre and who faces outward (or a mix). Mixed-facing rectangles are the most error-prone format — mark every arrow on the diagram first.

Card 13
Front: What is the "definite case vs possible case" distinction in seating questions?
Back: Some questions ask what MUST be true (true in every valid arrangement), others what CAN be true (true in at least one). Solve all valid cases before answering "must be" questions.

Card 14
Front: How many people sit between two persons who are "opposite" each other in a circle of 10?
Back: 4 on each side. In a circle of N (even), opposite means N/2 seats apart — here 5 apart, leaving 4 people in between on either arc.

Card 15
Front: When should you draw two parallel diagrams instead of one?
Back: When a clue gives two equally possible placements (e.g., "A is at an end" in a row). Carry both cases forward and drop the one that contradicts a later clue.

Card 16
Front: In a linear row with unknown total strength, what clue type fixes the length of the row?
Back: Position-from-both-ends clues: "X is 7th from the left and 12th from the right" → total = 7 + 12 − 1 = 18. Remember to subtract 1 so X isn't counted twice.

Card 17
Front: Why should you never assume gender or profession pairings unless stated?
Back: Examiners add attribute layers (city, colour, profession) separately from seating. Assuming links that aren't given creates false constraints and collapses the correct case.

Card 18
Front: What is the fastest way to verify a completed seating arrangement?
Back: Re-read each clue one by one and tick it against the diagram — 20 seconds of checking catches the one flipped left/right that ruins all 5 questions of the set.

Card 19
Front: In "facing each other" two-row puzzles, if A faces B, and C is immediate right of A, whom does C face?
Back: The person immediately to B's left (from B's perspective). Opposite positions shift in mirrored directions — always trace it on the diagram, never in your head.

Card 20
Front: What is the ideal time budget for a 5-question complex seating set in the exam?
Back: 4–6 minutes total. If the diagram isn't complete in 3 minutes, mark it for review and move on — a half-solved arrangement still answers "must be true" questions sometimes.
