126. In a row of students, Ravi is 12th from the left and 18th from the right. If 5 more students join the row at the right end, what is Ravi's new position from the right?
(a) 18th (b) 22nd (c) 13th (d) 23rd
Answer: (d)
Solution: The tempting answer is 18th, which ignores the new students entirely. Total students initially = 12 + 18 − 1 = 29; after 5 join at the right, total = 34. Ravi is still 12th from the left, so from the right he is 34 − 12 + 1 = 23rd, so the answer is 23rd.
Source: PYQ-style

127. In a class, A is ranked 8th from the top and B is ranked 15th from the bottom. If they interchange their positions, A becomes 20th from the top. How many students are there in the class?
(a) 28 (b) 35 (c) 27 (d) 34
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is computing 8 + 15 − 1 = 22 or 8 + 20 − 1 = 27 by mixing A's old rank with the wrong end. After interchange, A sits at B's seat, which is 20th from the top and 15th from the bottom. Total = 20 + 15 − 1 = 34, so the answer is 34.
Source: PYQ-style

128. In a row of 40 students, P is 10th from the left and Q is 12th from the right. How many students are sitting between P and Q?
(a) 18 (b) 19 (c) 20 (d) 17
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is converting Q's position wrongly and getting 19 or 20. Q's position from the left = 40 − 12 + 1 = 29. Students between positions 10 and 29 = 29 − 10 − 1 = 18, so the answer is 18.

(a) B (b) A (c) C (d) E

Solution: The trap is hesitating because E and D's relation to B is not directly stated. From the data the chain is complete: B < A < C < D < E, so B is clearly the shortest, so the answer is B.

136. In a row of 45 boys, Ajay is 9th from the left and Vijay is 15th from the right. Rakesh sits exactly in the middle of Ajay and Vijay. What is Rakesh's position from the left?
(a) 21st (b) 20th (c) 22nd (d) 19th
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is averaging 9 and 15 directly, which mixes positions counted from opposite ends. Vijay from the left = 45 − 15 + 1 = 31. The middle of positions 9 and 31 = (9 + 31)/2 = 20, so the answer is 20th.

139. In a queue of 36 persons, A is 8th from the front and B is 9th from the back. C stands exactly between A and B. How many persons stand between A and C?

Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is halving the total gap without locating B's position from the front first. B from the front = 36 − 9 + 1 = 28, so persons between A and B = 28 − 8 − 1 = 19, and C sits at the middle, the 18th position. Persons between A (8th) and C (18th) = 18 − 8 − 1 = 9, so the answer is 9.

146. In a row of boys, Harish is 10th from the left and Suresh is 15th from the right. When they interchange positions, Harish becomes 18th from the left. What is the total number of boys, and how many boys sit between them originally?
(a) 32 total, 8 between (b) 32 total, 7 between (c) 33 total, 7 between (d) 33 total, 8 between
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is computing total as 18 + 15 = 33 without subtracting the one shared seat. After interchange, Harish occupies Suresh's seat: 18th from the left and 15th from the right, so total = 18 + 15 − 1 = 32. Suresh's original position from the left = 32 − 15 + 1 = 18, and boys between the 10th and 18th positions = 18 − 10 − 1 = 7, so the answer is 32 total, 7 between.
Source: PYQ-style

147. In a row of 42 students, the position of Karan from the left is the same as the position of Arjun from the right, and there are 10 students between them with Karan to the left of Arjun. What is Karan's position from the left?
(a) 16th (b) 14th (c) 17th (d) 15th
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is halving 42 − 10 = 32 and misplacing the result. Let Karan be at position x from the left; Arjun is then at position x from the right, i.e., 43 − x from the left. The gap gives (43 − x) − x − 1 = 10, so 42 − 2x = 10 and x = 16, so the answer is 16th.
Source: PYQ-style

150. In a class of 24 students, Rohit's rank from the top is a perfect square and his rank from the bottom is also a perfect square, and his rank from the top is less than his rank from the bottom. What is his rank from the top?
(a) 4 (b) 16 (c) 9 (d) 1
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is picking 16 because it is a familiar perfect square, without checking the bottom rank. Rank from bottom = 24 − top + 1 = 25 − top; for top = 9, bottom = 16, both perfect squares with top less than bottom, while top = 16 gives bottom = 9, violating the condition. So the answer is 9.
