76. In a row of students, Ravi is 12th from the left end and 18th from the right end. How many students are there in the row?
(a) 28 (b) 30 (c) 31 (d) 29
Answer: (d)
Solution: Total = position from left + position from right − 1 = 12 + 18 − 1 = 29. The tempting answer 30 comes from simply adding 12 and 18, which double-counts Ravi, so the answer is 29.

77. In a class, Priya ranks 8th from the top and 35th from the bottom. How many students are in the class?
(a) 42 (b) 41 (c) 43 (d) 44
Answer: (a)
Solution: Total = 8 + 35 − 1 = 42. Adding 8 and 35 directly gives 43, which counts Priya twice, so the answer is 42.

78. In a row of 40 boys, Aman is 15th from the left. What is his position from the right?
(a) 27th (b) 25th (c) 24th (d) 26th
Answer: (d)
Solution: Position from right = Total − position from left + 1 = 40 − 15 + 1 = 26. Forgetting the +1 gives the tempting wrong answer 25, so the answer is 26th.

79. In a queue, Suresh is 10th from the front and Mahesh is 15th from the back. If there are 30 people in the queue, how many people stand between them?
(a) 4 (b) 7 (c) 5 (d) 6
Answer: (c)
Solution: Mahesh's position from front = 30 − 15 + 1 = 16. People between 10th and 16th = 16 − 10 − 1 = 5. Subtracting directly (16 − 10 = 6) counts one endpoint, so the answer is 5.

80. In a row of girls facing north, Kavya is 9th from the left and Ritu is 12th from the right. If they interchange their positions, Kavya becomes 16th from the left. How many girls are in the row?
(a) 27 (b) 26 (c) 28 (d) 29
Answer: (a)
Solution: After interchange, Kavya occupies Ritu's original seat, which is 16th from left and 12th from right. Total = 16 + 12 − 1 = 27. Using Kavya's old position 9 with 12 gives the wrong 20, so the answer is 27.

81. Five friends P, Q, R, S, T scored different marks. P scored more than Q but less than R. S scored more than T but less than Q. Who scored the highest?
(a) P (b) Q (c) R (d) S
Answer: (c)
Solution: From the conditions: R > P > Q > S > T. R is at the top of the chain. P looks tempting because it beats Q, but R beats P, so the answer is R.

82. In a row of 50 students, Vikram is 20th from the left and Sunil is 22nd from the right. How many students are between them?
(a) 7 (b) 10 (c) 8 (d) 9
Answer: (c)
Solution: Sunil from left = 50 − 22 + 1 = 29. Students between 20th and 29th = 29 − 20 − 1 = 8. Direct subtraction gives 9, which includes one of the two boys, so the answer is 8.

85. In a row, A is 7th from the left and B is 9th from the right. They swap positions, after which A becomes 14th from the left. What is B's new position from the right?
(a) 15th (b) 16th (c) 14th (d) 17th
Answer: (b)
Solution: After the swap, A sits at B's old seat: 14th from left and 9th from right, so total = 14 + 9 − 1 = 22. B now sits at A's old seat, 7th from left, so from right = 22 − 7 + 1 = 16. Assuming B's new right position equals A's new left position (14) is the trap, so the answer is 16th.
Source: PYQ-style

88. In an examination, Ashok ranked 16th from the top and 29th from the bottom among the students who passed. 6 students failed and 4 were absent. What was the total number of students enrolled?
(a) 50 (b) 52 (c) 54 (d) 56
Answer: (c)
Solution: Passed students = 16 + 29 − 1 = 44. Total enrolled = 44 + 6 + 4 = 54. Stopping at 44 or adding only the failed (50) are the traps, so the answer is 54.

Solution: Total = 8 + 13 + 4 = 25, since the 4 boys between them are additional to the two ranked boys. Adding only 8 + 13 = 21 or miscounting the between-boys gives wrong totals, so the answer is 25.

93. In a row, X is 6th from the left and Y is 10th from the right. After they exchange positions, X becomes 15th from the left. How many persons were originally between X and Y?
(a) 7 (b) 9 (c) 8 (d) 10
Answer: (c)
Solution: After exchange, X sits at Y's seat, so Y was originally 15th from the left while X was 6th from the left. Persons between = 15 − 6 − 1 = 8. Subtracting directly gives 9, which counts Y's seat, so the answer is 8.

95. In a class, the number of students above Anil in rank equals the number below him. Anil is 17th from the top. How many students are in the class?
(a) 32 (b) 35 (c) 34 (d) 33
Answer: (d)
Solution: Students above Anil = 16, so students below him = 16 as well. Total = 16 + 16 + 1 = 33. Doubling 17 to get 34 is the trap, since the 17th rank includes Anil himself, so the answer is 33.

99. In a row of 42 students, Ajay is 16th from the left and Vijay is 17th from the right. Ramesh sits exactly midway between Ajay and Vijay. What is Ramesh's position from the left?
(a) 22nd (b) 21st (c) 23rd (d) 24th
Answer: (b)
Solution: Vijay from left = 42 − 17 + 1 = 26. Midway between 16th and 26th = (16 + 26)/2 = 21st from left. Rounding up to 22 is the trap, since the midpoint is exactly 21, so the answer is 21st.
