# 📊 PYQ Analysis — Order & Ranking (Advanced Level)

## 1. How Exams Ask Questions from This Chapter

Order & Ranking is a **permanent fixture** of the reasoning section in SSC CGL, IBPS PO/Clerk, RRB NTPC, and State PCS prelims. The questions are never about memorised facts — they test whether you can convert verbal statements into inequalities and count positions without double-counting. Across exams, the question styles repeat in these recognisable forms:

**Sub-topics that appear again and again:**

1. **Single-row position questions** — "Ravi is 12th from the left and 18th from the right; how many students are in the row?" (Formula: Total = Left + Right − 1)
2. **Two-person interchange questions** — A and B swap positions; find the new rank or total strength.
3. **Between-two-persons counting** — "How many persons sit between X and Y?" The trap is whether endpoints are included.
4. **Comparison chains** — "A is taller than B but shorter than C; D is taller than C…" — building an inequality chain and finding the tallest/shortest or the one in the middle.
5. **Rank from both ends after removal/addition** — some persons leave or join the row.
6. **Overlapping/ambiguous statements** — deliberately incomplete data where the answer is "cannot be determined" (Banking exams love this).
7. **Marks/age/height-based ranking** — same logic, different costume.

**Exam-wise flavour (qualitative):**

- **SSC CGL / CHSL / NTPC:** Direct formula-based questions dominate — single row, both-ends rank, interchange. Usually solvable in 15–30 seconds.
- **IBPS PO / Clerk, SBI PO:** Longer comparison chains, "cannot be determined" options, and ranking embedded inside puzzle-style data. These take 45+ seconds.
- **State PCS prelims:** Statement-based questions — "Which of the following statements is sufficient?" — testing logical completeness rather than arithmetic.

## 2. The Examiner's Traps (Psychological Hooks)

- **The −1 trap:** Forgetting to subtract 1 in Total = L + R − 1, because the person is counted from both sides. This is the single most common error.
- **"Between" ambiguity:** "5 persons between A and B" — candidates add or subtract wrongly when converting to rank difference (|rank A − rank B| = 6, not 5).
- **Interchange confusion:** After swapping, the *person's* rank changes but the *position number* stays — candidates mix up whose new rank is being asked.
- **Near-correct statements:** In comparison chains, one statement like "E is not the shortest" is designed to look sufficient when it is not.
- **Negative wording:** "Who is NOT taller than C?" — candidates answer the tallest instead.
- **Mid-position illusion:** "Exactly in the middle" is only possible with an odd total; examiners give an even total and watch candidates force an answer.
- **Unspecified order:** When two persons' ranks from opposite ends are given along with the count between them, but their left–right order is not stated, two different totals are possible — the honest answer is "cannot be determined."

## 3. Top 10 High-Yield PYQ-Style Questions with Solutions

**Q1.** In a row of students, Rahul is 15th from the left end and 12th from the right end. How many students are there in the row?
(a) 26 (b) 27 (c) 25 (d) 28
**Answer: (a)**
**Solution:** Total = 15 + 12 − 1 = 26. **Trap:** Option (b) 27 is the raw sum 15 + 12 — placed there for those who forget the −1.
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q2.** In a class, Sita ranks 8th from the top and 35th from the bottom. How many students are in the class?
(a) 42 (b) 43 (c) 41 (d) 44
**Answer: (a)**
**Solution:** Total = 8 + 35 − 1 = 42. Same both-ends formula; the numbers change, the structure doesn't.
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q3.** In a row of 40 boys, A is 10th from the left. What is his position from the right?
(a) 30 (b) 31 (c) 29 (d) 32
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** Position from right = 40 − 10 + 1 = 31. **Trap:** Option (a) 30 = 40 − 10, the classic missing +1.
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q4.** In a queue, P is 14th from the front and Q is 20th from the back. They interchange their positions, and now P is 25th from the front. How many persons are in the queue?
(a) 44 (b) 45 (c) 38 (d) 39
**Answer: (a)**
**Solution:** After the interchange, P stands at Q's original position. So Q's original position is 25th from the front — and we are told it is also 20th from the back. Total = 25 + 20 − 1 = 44. **Trap:** Using P's original 14 instead of the new 25 gives 33 — the examiner banks on you not tracking *whose* position moved. The position number stays fixed; only the person standing there changes.
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q5.** A is taller than B. C is taller than A. D is taller than C but shorter than E. Who is the tallest?
(a) C (b) D (c) E (d) A
**Answer: (c)**
**Solution:** Chain: B < A < C < D < E. E is tallest. **Trap:** Option (b) D tempts those who stop reading at "D is taller than C."
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q6.** In a row of girls facing north, M is 9th from the left and N is 12th from the right. If there are 5 girls between M and N, what is the total number of girls in the row?
(a) 26 (b) 25 (c) 27 (d) Cannot be determined
**Answer: (d)**
**Solution:** The order of M and N is not stated. If M is to the **left** of N: total = 9 + 5 + 12 = 26. If N is to the **left** of M: total = 9 + 12 − 5 − 2 = 14 (the 5 girls between and the 2 endpoints are counted twice). Both arrangements satisfy the given data, so the total cannot be determined. **Trap:** Assuming M is left of N without being told — Banking exams plant exactly this ambiguity and reward option (d).
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q7.** Ramesh is 7 ranks ahead of Suresh in a class of 39 students. If Suresh is 17th from the last, what is Ramesh's rank from the start?
(a) 14th (b) 15th (c) 16th (d) 17th
**Answer: (c)**
**Solution:** Suresh from start = 39 − 17 + 1 = 23rd. Ramesh is 7 ahead = 23 − 7 = 16th from start. **Trap:** Adding 7 (getting 30th) instead of subtracting — "ahead" means *towards the front*, i.e., a smaller rank number.
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q8.** Five friends P, Q, R, S, T scored different marks. P scored more than Q but less than R. S scored more than T but less than Q. Who scored the least?
(a) Q (b) S (c) T (d) P
**Answer: (c)**
**Solution:** Chain: T < S < Q < P < R. Least = T. **Trap:** Option (b) S — candidates who build only half the chain pick S.
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q9.** In a row, X is 16th from either end. How many persons are in the row?
(a) 31 (b) 32 (c) 30 (d) 33
**Answer: (a)**
**Solution:** Total = 16 + 16 − 1 = 31. Note the total is odd — X is exactly in the middle. **Trap:** 32 (forgetting −1) is the planted distractor.
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q10.** In a class, Kiran is 11th from the top and 20th from the bottom among the boys. If there are 12 girls in the class, what is the total strength of the class?
(a) 30 (b) 42 (c) 41 (d) 43
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** Boys = 11 + 20 − 1 = 30. Total = 30 + 12 = 42. **Trap:** Option (a) 30 — forgetting the girls; the extra sentence is pure misdirection for hasty readers.
**Source:** PYQ-style

## 4. Skip-or-Solve Verdicts (15-sec / 45-sec Rule)

- **Q1, Q2, Q3, Q9 (single-row formula type):** ✅ **Solve in 15 seconds.** One formula, one subtraction. Never skip these — they are the cheapest marks in the section.
- **Q4 (interchange type):** ⚠️ **45-second zone.** Solve only if you have practised the "position stays, person moves" model; otherwise mark and return. In a Banking prelim with tight sectional timing, this is a legitimate skip on first pass.
- **Q6 (ambiguous-order type):** ⚠️ **Read twice before answering.** If the left–right order is not given, check whether both arrangements work — if they do, "cannot be determined" is the answer, and spotting that takes under 30 seconds once you know the pattern.
- **Q7 (rank-ahead conversion):** ⚠️ **45-second zone.** Two conversions (last→start, then ±7) double the error chance. Attempt in the second pass if your first pass left time.
- **Q5, Q8 (comparison chains):** ✅ Solve, but write the chain on paper — 20–30 seconds. Do not attempt mentally; chain errors are silent.
- **Any question with "cannot be determined" as an option (Banking pattern):** ⚠️ Read twice before solving; if data feels incomplete after one read, it usually is — answer accordingly or skip.

**Golden rule for this chapter:** If the question needs only Total = L + R − 1 or its rearrangement, it is a 15-second gift — take it. If it needs tracking two people through an interchange or a 5-link inequality chain, budget 45 seconds and decide after one read.
