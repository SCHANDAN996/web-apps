# 📊 PYQ Analysis — Alphabet Questions (Verbal Reasoning)

## 1. How Exams Ask Questions from This Chapter

Across SSC (CGL/CHSL/MTS), Banking (IBPS/SBI PO-Clerk), Railway (NTPC/Group D) and State PCS prelims, Alphabet Questions appear in **every cycle** as one of the most reliable scoring areas of Verbal Reasoning. The pattern analysis below is based on the recurring question styles seen in official papers — not on invented counts.

**Recurring sub-topics, in order of how often they appear:**

1. **Position-based questions** — "Which letter is 7th to the right of the 13th letter from the left?" The single most repeated format.
2. **Reverse-order alphabet** — questions where the series is written in reverse and positions must be re-counted.
3. **Letter-pair counting** — "How many pairs of letters in the word EXAMINATION have as many letters between them as in the English alphabet?"
4. **Meaningful-word formation** — "How many meaningful words can be formed from the 2nd, 5th, 7th and 9th letters of…?"
5. **Dictionary / alphabetical arrangement** — arranging words as in a dictionary, or finding the middle letter of a word.
6. **Letter series completion** — A, C, F, J, ? (gap-pattern series).
7. **Interchange / replacement questions** — "If every vowel is replaced by the next letter and every consonant by the previous letter…"
8. **EJOTY-based quick position recall** — tested indirectly in almost every position question.

**Exam-wise flavour:**
- **SSC** loves direct position arithmetic and reverse-series questions — short, one-line questions.
- **Banking** prefers letter-pair counting and word-based manipulation (longer, trap-heavy).
- **Railway** mixes letter series with simple position questions — usually the easiest version.
- **State PCS** occasionally asks dictionary arrangement and meaningful-word questions.

## 2. The Examiner's Traps (Psychological Hooks)

- **Left–right confusion:** "7th to the right of the 13th from the **right**" — the double direction change is the #1 trap. Candidates add when they should subtract.
- **Reverse-series illusion:** In a reversed alphabet, the 5th letter is V, not E. Candidates answer from the normal order.
- **Pair-counting double count:** Pairs must be counted in **both directions** (forward and backward) unless the question says otherwise — most candidates count only forward.
- **"Middle letter" of even-length words:** An 8-letter word has no single middle letter; the trap option assumes one.
- **Near-correct series:** A, D, H, M, ? — gaps are +3, +4, +5, so the answer is S (+6); a tempting option is R (+5 repeated).
- **Vowel/consonant replacement:** Candidates forget that after replacement, positions must be re-read from the **new** string, not the original.

## 3. Top 10 High-Yield Questions (with Solutions & Trap Analysis)

**Q1.** Which letter is 9th to the right of the 12th letter from the left in the English alphabet?
(a) S (b) T (c) U (d) V
**Answer: (c)**
**Solution:** Same direction → add: 12 + 9 = 21st letter = U.
**Trap:** Option (b) T comes from 12 + 9 − 1 = 20, a common off-by-one error when candidates count the 12th letter itself.
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q2.** If the alphabet is written in reverse order, which letter will be 8th to the left of the 10th letter from the right?
(a) R (b) Q (c) I (d) H
**Answer: (a)**
**Solution:** In the reversed alphabet, "right" and "left" swap their normal meaning. The 10th letter from the right of the reversed series = the 10th letter from the left of the normal alphabet = J. "8th to the left" in the reversed series means moving 8 places toward the normal right: J(10) + 8 = 18th letter = R.
**Trap:** The double reversal makes candidates mix directions; option (d) H comes from wrongly subtracting (10 − 8 = 2 → B, then mis-counted in reverse to H).
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q3.** How many pairs of letters in the word "CREATIVE" have as many letters between them in the word as in the English alphabet?
(a) 1 (b) 2 (c) 3 (d) 4
**Answer: (c)**
**Solution:** CREATIVE = C(1) R(2) E(3) A(4) T(5) I(6) V(7) E(8). Checking both directions, three pairs satisfy the condition: **C–E** (R between them in the word; D between them in the alphabet), **A–E** (T, I, V between them in the word; B, C, D between them in the alphabet), and **T–V** (I between them in the word; U between them in the alphabet).
**Trap:** Candidates who count only forward pairs miss the backward pair A–E and answer (b) 2.
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q4.** Find the missing term: B, E, I, N, ?
(a) S (b) T (c) U (d) R
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** Gaps: +3, +4, +5, so next +6: N(14) + 6 = 20 = T.
**Trap:** Option (a) S assumes the gap stays +5 — the "repeated gap" illusion.
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q5.** If every alternate letter starting from B is removed from the alphabet, which letter will be the 10th from the left?
(a) S (b) Q (c) T (d) R
**Answer: (a)**
**Solution:** Removing B, D, F, … leaves A, C, E, G, I, K, M, O, Q, S, U, W, Y — the 10th is S.
**Trap:** Candidates remove A, C, E… instead (starting from the wrong letter) and get T.
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q6.** Arrange as in a dictionary: 1. Parcel 2. Pardon 3. Parent 4. Parrot
(a) 2, 1, 3, 4 (b) 2, 3, 1, 4 (c) 1, 2, 3, 4 (d) 2, 1, 4, 3
**Answer: (c)**
**Solution:** All four words begin with "Par", so compare the 4th letters: Parcel → c, Pardon → d, Parent → e, Parrot → r. Alphabetical order of c, d, e, r gives: Parcel, Pardon, Parent, Parrot = 1, 2, 3, 4.
**Trap:** Candidates compare only the 3rd letter (all 'r') and stop, or misread the order of "Pardon" vs "Parent".
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q7.** How many meaningful English words can be formed using the 1st, 4th, 7th and 8th letters of "SOLUTION", each used once?
(a) None (b) One (c) Two (d) Three
**Answer: (c)**
**Solution:** SOLUTION = S(1) O(2) L(3) U(4) T(5) I(6) O(7) N(8). The required letters are S, U, O, N, which form two meaningful words: **ONUS** and **NOUS**.
**Trap:** Candidates who mis-pick positions (e.g., taking the 7th letter as I) invent wrong letter sets, and those who check only common everyday words miss NOUS and answer (b).
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q8.** In the word "CHAMPION", which letter is exactly in the middle?
(a) M (b) P (c) No single middle letter (d) A
**Answer: (c)**
**Solution:** CHAMPION has 8 letters; the middle falls between the 4th (M) and 5th (P) letters — no single middle letter.
**Trap:** Options (a) and (b) exploit the habit of picking ⌈n/2⌉ without checking parity.
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q9.** If in the alphabet every vowel is replaced by the next letter and every consonant by the previous letter, which letter will be 5th from the left in the new arrangement?
(a) D (b) E (c) F (d) C
**Answer: (c)**
**Solution:** New string: A→B, B→A, C→B, D→C, E→F → B, A, B, C, F, … The 5th letter = F.
**Trap:** Option (b) E is the original 5th letter — candidates forget to re-read from the **new** string.
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q10.** Which letter is 6th to the left of the letter which is 4th from the right end of the alphabet?
(a) P (b) Q (c) R (d) S
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** 4th from the right = W (23rd from the left). 6th to its left = 23 − 6 = 17th letter = Q.
**Trap:** Option (d) S comes from direction confusion — adding 6 instead of subtracting (23 + 6 = 29, wrapped/mis-counted). Remember: "left of a right-end position" means move toward A, i.e., subtract.
**Source:** PYQ-style

## 4. The 15-Second / 45-Second Rule — Applied

- **15-second questions (attempt instantly):** Q1, Q4, Q10-type single-step position arithmetic and simple gap series. If EJOTY (5, 10, 15, 20, 25 → E, J, O, T, Y) is memorised, these take under 15 seconds.
- **45-second questions (attempt, but with a hard stop):** Q2, Q5, Q9 — multi-step transformations. If you cross 45 seconds, mark and move on.
- **Skip candidates:**
  - **Q3 (pair counting):** In Banking papers these can take 90+ seconds with high error risk. **Skip in the first pass**; return only if time remains.
  - **Q7 (meaningful-word formation):** Depends on vocabulary luck, not method. If no word surfaces in 30 seconds, **skip** — never force an answer.
  - **Ambiguous/misprint items:** If your computed letter is not among the options, do not burn time reconciling — re-check the direction rule once, then mark the nearest defensible option or skip.

**Bottom line:** Alphabet questions are high-yield *only* when the position rules (same direction → add, opposite → subtract; reversed series → flip left/right) are automatic. Master EJOTY, and most of this chapter becomes a sub-15-second scoring zone.
