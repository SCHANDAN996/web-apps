# 🪄 Short Tricks & Mnemonics — Classification

---

**Box 1 — The "ODD SOCK" Rule**
> 🧦 **O-D-D = One Differs, Duh!**
> In every classification question, exactly ONE item refuses to follow the family rule. Your job is not to find what three share — it's to catch the one sock that doesn't match the pair. Ask: *"Which one would be embarrassed at this family reunion?"*

---

**Box 2 — The "3 vs 1" Chant**
> 🥁 *"Three must agree, one must flee!"*
> Before marking an answer, verify that the OTHER THREE genuinely share a property. If you can only explain why your answer is different but can't explain why the other three are alike — your answer is probably wrong.

---

**Box 3 — "CAKE" for Word Classification**
> 🍰 **C-A-K-E** checklist for word groups:
> - **C**ategory (same class? fruits, rivers, planets?)
> - **A**ttribute (same property? colour, size, gender?)
> - **K**ind of relationship (synonym? part-whole? tool-work?)
> - **E**xception (which one breaks it?)
> Bake the CAKE before you pick!

---

**Box 4 — The "POSSE" Trick for Parts of Speech**
> 🤠 **POSSE** = *Part Of Speech Separates Everything*
> Three nouns and one verb? Three adjectives and one noun? The odd one often hides in grammar, not meaning. When meanings look similar, check the POSSE!

---

**Box 5 — "VOWEL VILLAIN" for Letter Groups**
> 🔤 When letter clusters confuse you, interrogate the vowels first:
> *"How many vowels? Which vowels? Vowel positions?"*
> The villain (odd one) is often caught by vowel count alone — e.g., three groups with 2 vowels, one with 1.

---

**Box 6 — The "ALPHABET LADDER" Trick**
> 🪜 For letter-pair classification, climb the ladder:
> Write positions (A=1 … Z=26) and check the **gap** between letters.
> *"Same gap = same family. Broken ladder = odd one out."*
> e.g., CE (gap 2), FH (gap 2), KM (gap 2), QT (gap 3) ← **QT breaks the ladder!** Always compute, never guess!

---

**Box 7 — "SQUARE-CUBE-PRIME" Number Trio**
> 🔢 **SCP Mantra:** *"Is it a Square? a Cube? a Prime?"*
> Most number-classification questions in SSC, Railway and Bank exams are built on squares, cubes or primes. Check in this exact order: **Square → Cube → Prime → Divisibility → Digit-sum.** Five doors, knock in order!

---

**Box 8 — The "DIGIT SUM Detective"**
> 🕵️ When numbers look random, add their digits!
> 36 (3+6=9), 54 (5+4=9), 72 (7+2=9), 48 (4+8=12) ← **caught!**
> *"When in doubt, sum it out."*

---

**Box 9 — "ZOO vs JUNGLE" Rule**
> 🦁 Three items belong to a **narrow** category, one to a **wider** one (or vice versa).
> Apple–Mango–Banana–Fruit? "Fruit" is the category itself — the odd one!
> *"Don't pick the item that IS the family name."* Examiners love this trap.

---

**Box 10 — The "REVERSO" Trick**
> 🔄 Check if three pairs are reverses of each other or follow mirror logic:
> 12–21, 34–43, 56–65, 78–86 ← last one breaks the mirror!
> *"Flip it before you skip it."*

---

**Box 11 — "PAIR PRODUCT" Rule for Number Pairs**
> ✖️ For pairs like (4, 16), (5, 25), (6, 36), (7, 48):
> Check: Is the second = first²? First × 2? First + something?
> Three pairs follow ONE formula; the fourth pretends to. *"One formula rules three; the fourth is a fraud."*

---

**Box 12 — The "GEOGRAPHY GAG" Memory Hook**
> 🗺️ For GK-based classification (rivers, capitals, countries):
> *"Continent first, country next, city last — the odd one lives at a different address."*
> Three Indian rivers + one non-Indian? Three capitals + one non-capital? Check the "address level."

---

**Box 13 — "SYNONYM SIBLINGS, ANTONYM ORPHAN"**
> 👨‍👩‍👧 Three words are synonyms (happy–glad–joyful) and one is an antonym or unrelated (sad).
> *"If three words can swap seats in a sentence, the fourth is the orphan."*
> Warning: examiners use NEAR-synonyms as bait — check exact meaning!

---

**Box 14 — The "15-SECOND SCAN" Rhythm**
> ⏱️ Tap this mental rhythm:
> **5 sec** — read all four options fully.
> **5 sec** — test the most obvious property (category).
> **5 sec** — test the second property (structure/number pattern).
> No pattern after 15 seconds? → Skip protocol (below).

---

## 🚫 SKIP STRATEGY — When to Walk Away

**Tip 1 — The 15-Second Rule:**
If no clear pattern emerges within 15 seconds of focused scanning, mark the question and move on. Classification questions are designed to be solved in 20–30 seconds; a question eating 60+ seconds is stealing time from three easier ones. Return only if time remains.

**Tip 2 — The "Two-Answer Trap" Exit:**
If you can justify TWO different options as the odd one (e.g., odd by category AND another odd by structure), the question is using a deeper rule you haven't spotted. Don't gamble — skip it. A 50-50 guess with negative marking is a losing trade.

**Tip 3 — The "Unfamiliar Territory" Skip:**
If the classification depends on factual GK you don't know (e.g., obscure rivers, scientific instruments, foreign capitals), don't burn time re-reading — re-reading won't create knowledge you don't have. Skip instantly, bank the time, and invest it in pattern-based questions where logic alone wins.
