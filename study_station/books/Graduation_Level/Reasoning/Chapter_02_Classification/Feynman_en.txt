# 🧒 The Feynman Way — Classification (Odd One Out)

## The Hardest Idea, Explained Like You're 12

Imagine your mom sends you to the market with a basket. She says: *"Beta, bring back only fruits."* You come home with a mango, an apple, a banana, a guava — and one potato that rolled in by mistake.

Mom looks in the basket and instantly pulls out the potato. How? Not by tasting anything. She just asked one silent question: **"Which one doesn't belong to the family?"**

That is ALL of Classification. Four items belong to one family. One is the potato. Find the potato.

Sounds easy? Here's where the exam becomes a villain. The potato starts *wearing a fruit costume*.

## The Story of the Disguised Potato

One day the potato got clever. It painted itself red and sat between the mango and the apple. You almost missed it.

That's exactly what SSC and Banking exams do. Look at this:

**Rose, Lotus, Marigold, Lily**

"All flowers!" you shout, and freeze. But wait — the examiner's trick is deeper. Rose, Marigold, and Lily grow on **land plants**. The Lotus grows **in water**. The family isn't "flowers" — it's "land flowers." The lotus was the disguised potato.

Now the real punchline — the hardest concept in this whole chapter:

> **There is often MORE THAN ONE correct family. Your job is to find the family the EXAMINER intended — the one where exactly three items fit and exactly one doesn't.**

Example: **2, 3, 5, 9**

- 9 is the only non-prime → answer could be 9.
- 2 is the only even number → answer could be 2!

Two possible answers?! This is where students panic. The examiner resolves it by making one grouping *stronger* (3, 5, 9 are all odd — three members fit "odd", only 2 breaks it; while "prime" gives 2, 3, 5 fitting and 9 breaking). Both look valid, so the examiner will design the options so only ONE of these appears. **Your rule: check which odd-one-out is actually listed in the options, and prefer the grouping based on a stronger, more defining property (odd/even beats prime/non-prime in most exam keys).**

## The Unforgettable Trick: **"FAMILY PHOTO"**

Before answering, take a mental family photo and ask, in this order:

1. **C**ategory — Are they all the same type? (all animals? all numbers?)
2. **P**roperty — What do three share that one lacks? (odd/even, vowel position, land/water, herbivore/carnivore)
3. **D**egree — Is one a different level? (city vs. capital, planet vs. satellite)

Say it as: **"Check the Property, not the Costume."** The potato in red paint is still a potato. The lotus among flowers is still the water-dweller.

For letter/number pairs, add one more weapon: **write the alphabet positions** (A=1, B=2 … Z=26) above the letters. Differences of +2, +3, squares, and reversals jump out instantly when you see numbers instead of letters.
