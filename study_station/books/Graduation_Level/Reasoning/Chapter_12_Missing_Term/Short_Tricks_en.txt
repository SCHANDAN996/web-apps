# Short Tricks & Mnemonics — Missing Term

**Box 1 — "D-R-P-M-A: Doctor Rarely Prescribes Mango Ale"**
> Differences → Ratios → Powers → Mixed → Alternate. Check a series in exactly this order.

**Box 2 — "Walk, Jump, Grow, Mix, Split"**
> Walk = equal or growing steps, Jump = same multiplier, Grow = squares/cubes, Mix = × then ±, Split = two series in one.

**Box 3 — "EJOTY" (sounds like "a joy-tee")**
> E = 5, J = 10, O = 15, T = 20, Y = 25. Count from the nearest anchor: R = T − 2 = 18.

**Box 4 — "Opposites make 27"**
> A–Z, B–Y, C–X … M–N. If two letters travel in opposite directions, check whether they are opposites.

**Box 5 — "Fast growth = multiply"**
> If the last term is more than double the one before it, stop subtracting and start dividing.

**Box 6 — "Near a square? Add or take one away"**
> 3, 8, 15, 24 are squares minus one (4 − 1, 9 − 1 …). 2, 5, 10, 17 are squares plus one.

**Box 7 — "Cubes wear a ±1 mask"**
> 0, 7, 26, 63 (n³ − 1) and 2, 9, 28, 65 (n³ + 1). Know cubes 1–12 by heart.

**Box 8 — "Zig-zag? Split it"**
> If the series goes up, down, up, down, it is almost always two series sitting together.

**Box 9 — "Two rules? Ask the options"**
> When two rules fit, only one answer is usually printed. Check options before long calculations.

**Box 10 — "Grid: rows first"**
> In a 3×3 grid, test a full row; if it fails, test columns. Never use a rule that fits only one row.

**Box 11 — "Wrong term = odd one out of the rule"**
> Build the rule from the friendly terms, then the term that breaks it is your answer.

**Box 12 — "Alphanumeric = two questions in one"**
> Cover the numbers and solve the letters; then cover the letters and solve the numbers.

## Skip Strategy

1. **15 seconds:** if differences and ratios show nothing, try powers and mixed once.
2. **45 seconds:** if still nothing, mark the question and move on — return in the second round.
3. **Too-long chains:** if the next term needs three levels of differences, check whether only one option even fits the first step before calculating.
