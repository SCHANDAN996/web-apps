Card 1
Front: What is the very first thing to check in any number series?
Back: The differences between neighbouring terms. Most series are solved at this step.

Card 2
Front: The first differences look random. What next?
Back: Take the second differences (the differences of the differences). 2, 5, 11, 20 → 3, 6, 9 → the next gap is 12.

Card 3
Front: How do you recognise a ratio (multiplication) series quickly?
Back: The numbers grow very fast. Divide each term by the previous one: 5, 15, 45 → ×3 each time.

Card 4
Front: What does the mnemonic "D-R-P-M-A" stand for?
Back: Differences → Ratios → Powers → Mixed operations → Alternate terms. Check in this order.

Card 5
Front: What hidden pattern is in 4, 9, 25, 49, 121?
Back: Squares of prime numbers: 2², 3², 5², 7², 11². Next is 13² = 169.

Card 6
Front: 0, 7, 26, 63, 124 — which family?
Back: Cubes minus one (n³ − 1). Next is 6³ − 1 = 215.

Card 7
Front: The terms grow fast but the ratio is not constant. What should you try?
Back: A mixed rule such as ×2 + 1 or ×3 − 1. Example: 3, 7, 15, 31 → ×2 + 1 → 63.

Card 8
Front: How do you crack 2, 10, 4, 20, 8, 40, ?
Back: Split it into two series: odd places 2, 4, 8 (×2) and even places 10, 20, 40. The 7th term is 16.

Card 9
Front: What does the mnemonic "EJOTY" help you remember?
Back: Letter positions E = 5, J = 10, O = 15, T = 20, Y = 25 — count from the nearest anchor.

Card 10
Front: What is special about the pairs A–Z, B–Y, C–X?
Back: They are opposite letters: their positions add up to 27. Series like AZ, BY, CX → DW use this.

Card 11
Front: How do you solve an alphanumeric series like A1, C3, E5, ?
Back: Solve the letters and the numbers separately: letters skip one (A, C, E, G), numbers are odd (1, 3, 5, 7) → G7.

Card 12
Front: In a 3×3 number grid, where do you look for the rule?
Back: Rows first, then columns. The same rule must work for every complete row or column.

Card 13
Front: How do you find the wrong term in a series?
Back: Find the rule from the terms that clearly agree, then test each term. In 3, 5, 9, 17, 31, 65 the rule ×2 − 1 shows 31 should be 33.

Card 14
Front: Examiner's trap: 1, 2, 4, ? — why can this be dangerous?
Back: Two rules fit: ×2 gives 8 and +1, +2, +3 gives 7. Check which one appears in the options before answering.

Card 15
Front: Examiner's trap: why do students often pick a letter one place away from the answer?
Back: They count A as 0 instead of 1. The off-by-one letter is placed in the options on purpose.

Card 16
Front: When should you skip a series question?
Back: If no rule is clear after about 45 seconds of checking D-R-P-M-A, mark it and return later.

Card 17
Front: After Z, what comes next in a forward letter series?
Back: A again — positions wrap around (26 + 1 → 1).

Card 18
Front: Why must a rule fit every given term?
Back: Because a rule that fits only the first three terms is often a trap; the real rule explains the whole series.
