# Feynman Technique — Missing Term

## The Hardest Idea: "Which rule is the real one?"

The hardest part of a missing-term question is not calculating — it is choosing **which rule** the examiner had in mind. Many series can be explained in two ways, and only one of them leads to an answer in the options.

## A Story: Riya and the Staircase

Riya climbs the stairs of her school building. On the first floor she counts 3 steps, on the next landing 7, then 15, then 31. Her friend Aman says, "Easy — the gaps are 4, 8, 16, so they double. Next gap is 32."

Riya laughs. "Look at it another way: every landing has **double the steps of the last one, plus one**. 3 × 2 + 1 = 7, 7 × 2 + 1 = 15, 15 × 2 + 1 = 31."

Both are right for the numbers they have seen — and both give 63 for the next landing! That is the happy case. But sometimes two rules give **different** answers. Then the examiner has hidden only one of them in the options. That is why Riya always looks at the options before she finishes calculating.

## The Unforgettable Trick: "Walk, Jump, Grow, Mix, Split"

- **Walk** — differences (equal or growing steps)
- **Jump** — ratios (the same multiplier)
- **Grow** — powers (squares and cubes, sometimes ±1)
- **Mix** — multiply and add together
- **Split** — two series hidden in one

Say it like a game: *walk, jump, grow, mix, split.* Check in this order and you will rarely need more than 40 seconds.

## Why It Works

A series is a story told by one rule. If your rule explains **every** term (not just the first three), it is almost certainly the examiner's rule. If it fails even once, throw it away — however clever it looked.
