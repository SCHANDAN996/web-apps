# 🪄 Short Tricks & Mnemonics — Direction Sense (Coded/Distance)

---

**Box 1 — The NESW Clock**
> "**N**ever **E**at **S**oggy **W**afers" — North → East → South → West, always in **clockwise** order.
If you can recite the wafers, you can never mix up which way "right turn" goes.

---

**Box 2 — Right Turn = +90° Clockwise**
> "**R**ight is **R**ound the **C**lock."
Right turn → move one step along N-E-S-W. Left turn → jump one step **backwards** (N-W-S-E).
Trick: hold your right hand up — your thumb points the way a right turn faces.

---

**Box 3 — The Shadow Twins**
> "**Morning shadow runs West, evening shadow rests East.**"
Sun rises in East → shadow falls West (morning). Sun sets in West → shadow falls East (evening).
Memory hook: *"Shadow is shy — it hides on the opposite side of the Sun."*

---

**Box 4 — Pythagoras, the Rescue Trio**
> "**3-4-5 and 5-12-13 — the only two families the examiner invites.**"
If a question gives two legs of a right triangle, check these pairs first. 6-8-10 and 9-12-15 are just 3-4-5 wearing a multiplier.

---

**Box 5 — Shortest Distance = Crow Flight**
> "**The crow never takes turns.**"
"Shortest distance between start and end" = straight line = √(x² + y²). Never add the path lengths — that's the trap option sitting in the choices.

---

**Box 6 — Coded Directions: Decode First, Walk Later**
> "**Crack the code on paper before your feet move.**"
If 'A means North, B means South…', write the full decode table first (A→N, B→S…), then translate the whole instruction string in one go. Decoding mid-walk is how East becomes West.

---

**Box 7 — Turn Reversal Trick**
> "**Opposite turn, opposite world: Left ↔ Right when you face backwards.**"
If a man facing South turns "left", he goes East — not West. Chant: *"Facing South? Flip the mouth (of the turn)."*

---

**Box 8 — Net Displacement: Cancel the Opposites**
> "**North eats South, East eats West.**"
Before drawing, cancel opposite movements: 7 km North + 4 km South = 3 km North. Fewer lines on the diagram = fewer mistakes.

---

**Box 9 — The Paper Compass Ritual**
> "**Draw the cross before you read line two.**"
The moment you see a direction question, draw + (N up, E right) in the margin. Students who skip the cross donate 1 mark to the examiner.

---

**Box 10 — "Towards" vs "From" Trap**
> "**Walking TOWARDS the sun ≠ shadow BEHIND you — check who's chasing whom.**"
Morning: walking towards sun = facing East, shadow behind (West). Evening: walking towards sun = facing West, shadow behind (East). Say it: *"Face the sun, shadow shuns."*

---

**Box 11 — U-Turn = Double Right**
> "**About-turn is just two right turns holding hands.**"
Facing North, "reverse direction" = South = right + right (or left + left). Useful when coded questions say "he turned around".

---

**Box 12 — Degree-to-Direction Converter**
> "**45° = diagonal, 90° = cardinal, 180° = go home backwards.**"
45° right from North = North-East. 135° right from North = South-East. Convert degrees to compass points immediately — don't carry numbers in your head.

---

**Box 13 — Two-Person Meeting Problems**
> "**Two walkers, one map — draw BOTH on the SAME compass.**"
Never draw two separate diagrams. Same origin, same scale, then measure the gap between the two endpoints (usually Pythagoras again).

---

**Box 14 — The 15-Second Sniff Test**
> "**If after 15 seconds you can't place the starting direction, the question is a time-trap in disguise.**"
Mark it, move on, return with fresh eyes — direction questions feel obvious on the second look.

---

## ⏭️ Skip Strategy — When to Skip a Direction Question

1. **The 15-Second Rule:** If the coded-direction table (e.g., "P means turn left, Q means 135°…") isn't fully decoded within 15 seconds, **skip and return later**. Coded chains punish rushed decoding with 100% wrong answers.

2. **Multi-person + shadow + coded combo:** If a single question stacks 3+ conditions (two people, a shadow clue, AND coded turns), it costs 90+ seconds. Attempt it only after finishing all single-condition questions — the mark is the same, the time is triple.

3. **Missing-anchor questions:** If the question never fixes an absolute direction (no sun, no "facing North", no reference object) and asks for an absolute direction as the answer — re-read once; if still anchorless, **skip** — it's either a relative-direction question in disguise or misprinted, and guessing costs negative marks.
