# 🧒 The Feynman Way: Direction Sense Explained Like You're 12

## The Story of Ravi and the Treasure Map

Imagine this. It's a Sunday afternoon, and your friend Ravi calls you with exciting news: "I found a treasure map! It says — *Start at the old banyan tree. Walk 3 steps North, turn right and walk 4 steps, then turn left and walk 5 steps. Dig there!*"

You both run to the banyan tree. Ravi starts walking… and ends up digging in the wrong spot. Why? Because when he turned "right," he forgot **which way he was facing**. He turned right from where he *started*, not from where he was *standing*.

That, my friend, is the entire secret of Direction Sense. The whole chapter — every coded question, every distance puzzle — is just Ravi's mistake wearing different clothes.

## The One Big Idea: Directions Are Like a Clock Glued to the Ground

Here's how a 12-year-old should picture it:

- Draw a big **plus sign (+)** on the ground. Up = North, Right = East, Down = South, Left = West. This never changes. North is always North, no matter which way you face.
- **You** are a little arrow standing on that plus sign. When you "turn right," you don't move the map — you just rotate the arrow 90° clockwise.
- Facing North and turn right? Now you face **East**. Facing East and turn right? Now **South**. Facing South, turn right? **West**. It's a merry-go-round: N → E → S → W → N.

**The golden sentence:** *"Right turn = move one step clockwise on N-E-S-W. Left turn = one step anti-clockwise."*

## The Hardest Part Made Easy: Coded Directions

Now the examiners play a trick. They say: *"In a code, 'A @ B' means A is 5 m North of B."* Suddenly students panic. Don't! It's just Ravi's map written in shorthand.

Think of it like a secret language between friends:

1. **Decode first, walk later.** Before solving, translate every symbol into plain words on your rough sheet: `@ = North 5m`, `# = South 5m`, etc.
2. **Plot point by point.** Start with the first point as your banyan tree. Place each new point relative to the previous one — exactly like Ravi's steps.
3. **Answer from the picture, not from memory.** Once your mini-map is drawn, the question ("In which direction is P from R?") answers itself.

## The Unforgettable Trick: "The Shadow Never Lies" 🌅

Here's a trick you'll never forget. Morning sun rises in the **East**, so your **shadow falls West**. Evening sun sets in the **West**, so your **shadow falls East**.

So if a question says, *"One morning, Ashok's shadow falls exactly on Bina"* — instantly you know Bina is **West** of Ashok. No calculation needed. The shadow is a free compass the examiner hands you!

And for distance questions, remember the **Pythagoras shortcut**: if someone walks 3 km one way and 4 km at a right angle, the straight-line distance home is **5 km** (the famous 3-4-5 triangle). Also memorize 6-8-10 and 5-12-13 — examiners love them.

## The Three Traps (So You Don't Become Ravi)

- **Trap 1:** Turning from the *starting* direction instead of the *current* facing direction. Always ask: "Which way is my arrow pointing NOW?"
- **Trap 2:** "Direction of A **from** B" means stand at B and look at A — the opposite of what many students answer.
- **Trap 3:** In coded questions, applying the code to the wrong pair of points. Decode the full chain before plotting.
