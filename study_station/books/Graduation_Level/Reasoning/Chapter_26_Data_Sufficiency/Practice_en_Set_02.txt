Questions 26–50: Medium

26. Is the integer x divisible by 6?
Statement I: x is divisible by 12.
Statement II: x is divisible by 9.
(a) Statement I alone is sufficient, but Statement II alone is not sufficient (b) Statement II alone is sufficient, but Statement I alone is not sufficient (c) Either Statement I alone or Statement II alone is sufficient (d) Both statements together are sufficient, but neither alone is sufficient
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is assuming divisibility by 9 says something about divisibility by 6 — it does not, since 9 supplies no factor 2 (x = 9 fails, x = 18 passes, so Statement II is inconclusive). Statement I: every multiple of 12 = 6 × 2 is a multiple of 6, so the answer is definitely yes. Statement I alone is sufficient, so the answer is that Statement I alone is sufficient.
Source: PYQ-style

27. What is the value of the two-digit number?
Statement I: The sum of its digits is 9.
Statement II: The number exceeds the number obtained by reversing its digits by 27.
(a) Statement I alone is sufficient, but Statement II alone is not sufficient (b) Statement II alone is sufficient, but Statement I alone is not sufficient (c) Both statements together are sufficient, but neither alone is sufficient (d) Either Statement I alone or Statement II alone is sufficient
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is thinking Statement II alone fixes the number — a digit difference of 3 gives many pairs (41, 52, 63, 74, 85, 96), and Statement I alone gives 18, 27, 36, …, 90. Combining: digits sum to 9 and differ by 3, so the digits are 6 and 3, and since the number exceeds its reverse it must be 63. Both statements together are needed, so the answer is 63.

28. Among P, Q, R, S and T, who is the heaviest?
Statement I: P is heavier than Q and R but lighter than S.
Statement II: T is lighter than Q.
(a) Statement I alone is sufficient, but Statement II alone is not sufficient (b) Statement II alone is sufficient, but Statement I alone is not sufficient (c) Either Statement I alone or Statement II alone is sufficient (d) Both statements together are sufficient, but neither alone is sufficient
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is stopping at Statement I — it fixes S above P, Q and R, but T is never compared with anyone, so T could still be the heaviest. Statement II alone compares only T and Q. Together: S > P > Q > T and S > P > R, so S is above all four others. Both statements together are needed, so the answer is S.

29. Is triangle ABC a right-angled triangle?
Statement I: The sides are in the ratio 3 : 4 : 5.
Statement II: One angle of the triangle is 90°.
(a) Statement I alone is sufficient, but Statement II alone is not sufficient (b) Statement II alone is sufficient, but Statement I alone is not sufficient (c) Both statements together are sufficient, but neither alone is sufficient (d) Either Statement I alone or Statement II alone is sufficient
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is assuming both statements must be combined — each one independently settles the question. Statement I: 3² + 4² = 5², so by the converse of Pythagoras the triangle is right-angled; Statement II directly states a 90° angle. Each alone answers yes, so the answer is that either statement alone is sufficient.

30. What is the code for "sky" in a certain code language?
Statement I: In that language, "blue sky high" is coded as "ta ra ma" and "sky is blue" is coded as "ra na ta".
Statement II: In that language, "high sky limit" is coded as "ma ra ka".
(a) Statement II alone is sufficient, but Statement I alone is not sufficient (b) Both statements together are sufficient, but neither alone is sufficient (c) Either Statement I alone or Statement II alone is sufficient (d) Statement I alone is sufficient, but Statement II alone is not sufficient
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is declaring Statement I sufficient — its two sentences share two words ("sky", "blue") and two codes ("ta", "ra"), so "sky" cannot be separated from "blue". Statement II alone has only one sentence. Combining: "sky" and "high" are common between Statement II and the first sentence of Statement I with codes "ma" and "ra", and since "high" pairs with "ma", "sky" must be "ra". Both statements together are needed, so the answer is "ra".

31. On which day of the week was Rahul born?
Statement I: Rahul was born exactly 100 days after his sister, who was born on a Monday.
Statement II: Rahul was born in the year 2000.
(a) Both statements together are sufficient, but neither alone is sufficient (b) Statement II alone is sufficient, but Statement I alone is not sufficient (c) Either Statement I alone or Statement II alone is sufficient (d) Statement I alone is sufficient, but Statement II alone is not sufficient
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is Statement II — a birth year without a date can never give a weekday. Statement I: 100 ÷ 7 leaves remainder 2, so Rahul was born 2 weekdays after Monday, i.e., Wednesday. Statement I alone is sufficient, so the answer is Wednesday.

Statement II: The profit is ₹100.

Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is Statement II — a profit of ₹100 without the cost price gives no percentage (₹100 on ₹200 is 50%, on ₹1000 only 10%). Statement I gives profit ₹100 on cost ₹500, i.e., 100/500 × 100. Statement I alone is sufficient, so the answer is 20%.

35. Five friends A, B, C, D and E sit in a row facing north. Who sits in the middle?
Statement I: A sits at one end, and B sits second to the right of A.
Statement II: C sits between B and D, and E sits at the right end.
(a) Both statements together are sufficient, but neither alone is sufficient (b) Statement II alone is sufficient, but Statement I alone is not sufficient (c) Statement I alone is sufficient, but Statement II alone is not sufficient (d) Either Statement I alone or Statement II alone is sufficient
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is assuming Statement I fixes the row — it places only A (left end) and B (second from left), leaving C, D, E free, so the middle seat is undecided; Statement II alone also allows several arrangements. Combining: A, B fixed on the left, E at the right end, and C between B and D forces the order A, B, C, D, E. Both statements together are needed, so the answer is C.

36. Is the positive integer n a prime number?
Statement I: n is not divisible by any integer from 2 to 10.
Statement II: n < 121.
(a) Statement I alone is sufficient, but Statement II alone is not sufficient (b) Statement II alone is sufficient, but Statement I alone is not sufficient (c) Either Statement I alone or Statement II alone is sufficient (d) Both statements together are sufficient, but neither alone is sufficient
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is thinking Statement I alone proves primality — composites like 11 × 11 = 121 and 11 × 13 = 143 escape all divisors from 2 to 10. Statement II alone says nothing about primality. Together: any composite below 121 must have a factor at most √121 = 11, and since n has no factor from 2 to 10 and 11 × 11 is not below 121, n must be prime. Both statements together are needed, so the answer is yes, n is prime.

37. What is the speed of the train in km/h?
Statement I: The train crosses a pole in 12 seconds.
Statement II: The train is 180 metres long.
(a) Either Statement I alone or Statement II alone is sufficient (b) Statement II alone is sufficient, but Statement I alone is not sufficient (c) Both statements together are sufficient, but neither alone is sufficient (d) Statement I alone is sufficient, but Statement II alone is not sufficient
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is thinking the 12-second pole crossing gives speed — time without length is useless, and Statement II gives length without time. Together: speed = 180 ÷ 12 = 15 m/s = 15 × 18/5 km/h. Both statements together are needed, so the answer is 54 km/h.

50. What is the angle between the hour and minute hands of a clock at 3:20?
Statement I: The hour hand moves 0.5° per minute and the minute hand moves 6° per minute.
Statement II: At 3:00 the angle between the hands is 90°.
(a) Statement I alone is sufficient, but Statement II alone is not sufficient (b) Statement II alone is sufficient, but Statement I alone is not sufficient (c) Either Statement I alone or Statement II alone is sufficient (d) Both statements together are sufficient, but neither alone is sufficient
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is Statement II — the 3:00 angle cannot be extrapolated to 3:20 without the hands' speeds. Statement I gives both speeds: at 3:20 the minute hand is at 120° and the hour hand at 90° + 20 × 0.5° = 100°. Statement I alone is sufficient, so the answer is 20°.
