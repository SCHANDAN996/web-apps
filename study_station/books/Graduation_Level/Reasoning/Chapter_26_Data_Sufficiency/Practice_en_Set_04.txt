Questions 76–100: Medium

76. What is the value of the two-digit number?
Statement I: The sum of its digits is 9.
Statement II: The number obtained by reversing the digits exceeds the original number by 27.
(a) Statement I alone is sufficient, but Statement II alone is not sufficient (b) Each statement alone is sufficient (c) Statement II alone is sufficient, but Statement I alone is not sufficient (d) Both statements together are sufficient, but neither alone is sufficient
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is assuming Statement II alone fixes the number; pairs like 14/41, 25/52, 36/63 all differ by 27 on reversal, so II alone fails, and I alone gives 18, 27, 36, …, 90. Together: x + y = 9 and 9(y − x) = 27 give y − x = 3, so x = 3, y = 6, so the answer is that both statements together are sufficient and the number is 36.

77. Is x an even integer?
Statement I: x² is divisible by 4.
Statement II: 3x is an even integer.
(a) Statement I alone is sufficient, but Statement II alone is not sufficient (b) Both statements together are sufficient, but neither alone is sufficient (c) Each statement alone is sufficient (d) Statement II alone is sufficient, but Statement I alone is not sufficient
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is Statement II: 3x being an even integer does not force x to be an even integer, since x = 4/3 gives 3x = 4; Statement I also fails since x = 2√2 satisfies it. Together: 3x an even integer means x = 2m/3, and x² divisible by 4 forces m to be a multiple of 3, making x an even integer, so the answer is that both statements together are sufficient.

78. Among P, Q, R, S and T, who is the heaviest?
Statement I: Q is heavier than R and S but lighter than P.
Statement II: T is heavier than P.
(a) Statement I alone is sufficient, but Statement II alone is not sufficient (b) Both statements together are sufficient, but neither alone is sufficient (c) Statement II alone is sufficient, but Statement I alone is not sufficient (d) Each statement alone is sufficient
Answer: (b)
Solution: Statement I gives P > Q > R, S but says nothing about T, so it fails alone; Statement II gives only T > P with no link to the others, so it fails alone. Combining gives T > P > Q > R and S, so the answer is that both statements together are sufficient and T is the heaviest.

79. What is the code for "sky" in a certain code language?
Statement I: In that language, "blue sky high" is coded as "ta na ma" and "sky is blue" is coded as "na ta pa".
Statement II: In that language, "sky falls down" is coded as "na lo ki" and "falls down fast" is coded as "lo ki ra".
(a) Statement II alone is sufficient, but Statement I alone is not sufficient (b) Statement I alone is sufficient, but Statement II alone is not sufficient (c) Both statements together are sufficient, but neither alone is sufficient (d) Each statement alone is sufficient
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is Statement I: its two sentences share the words "blue" and "sky" and the codes "ta" and "na", so "sky" could be ta or na — I alone fails. Statement II: the two sentences share "falls" and "down" and the codes "lo" and "ki", leaving "sky" = na, so the answer is that Statement II alone is sufficient and the code is na.

82. Is triangle ABC a right-angled triangle?
Statement I: The sides are in the ratio 3 : 4 : 5.
Statement II: The sum of two of its angles equals the third angle.
(a) Each statement alone is sufficient (b) Statement I alone is sufficient, but Statement II alone is not sufficient (c) Both statements together are sufficient, but neither alone is sufficient (d) Statement II alone is sufficient, but Statement I alone is not sufficient
Answer: (a)
Solution: Statement I: 3² + 4² = 5², so by the converse of Pythagoras the triangle is right-angled — sufficient. Statement II: if A + B = C and A + B + C = 180°, then C = 90° — also sufficient, so the answer is that each statement alone is sufficient.

83. What is the speed of the train (in km/h)?
Statement I: The train crosses a pole in 12 seconds.

(a) Statement I alone is sufficient, but Statement II alone is not sufficient (b) Statement II alone is sufficient, but Statement I alone is not sufficient (c) Both statements together are sufficient, but neither alone is sufficient (d) Each statement alone is sufficient
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is thinking that crossing a pole in 12 seconds gives speed; without the train's length no speed can be found, so I alone fails, and II gives length with no time, so it fails alone. 84. In a row of 30 students, what is the position of Mohan from the left?

(a) Each statement alone is sufficient (b) Statement II alone is sufficient, but Statement I alone is not sufficient (c) Both statements together are sufficient, but neither alone is sufficient (d) Statement I alone is sufficient, but Statement II alone is not sufficient
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is overlooking that the total of 30 is given in the question itself, so Statement II also works: 30 − 11 = 19th from the left. Statement I gives 30 − 12 + 1 = 19th from the left, so the answer is that each statement alone is sufficient and Mohan is 19th from the left.
