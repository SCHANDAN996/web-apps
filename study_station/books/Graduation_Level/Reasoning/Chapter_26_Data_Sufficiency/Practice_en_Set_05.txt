101. Is the integer x divisible by 6?
Statement 1: x is divisible by 3.
Statement 2: x is divisible by 4.
(a) Statement 1 alone is sufficient, but Statement 2 alone is not (b) Both statements together are sufficient, but neither alone is sufficient (c) Statement 2 alone is sufficient, but Statement 1 alone is not (d) Each statement alone is sufficient
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is assuming divisibility by 3 alone settles it — but 9 is divisible by 3 and not by 6, and Statement 2 alone also fails since 8 is divisible by 4 but not by 6. Together, x is divisible by both 3 and 4, hence by their LCM 12, and every multiple of 12 is divisible by 6, so both statements together are sufficient.

102. What is the value of the two-digit number?
Statement 1: The sum of its digits is 9.
Statement 2: The number obtained by reversing the digits exceeds the original number by 27.
(a) Both statements together are sufficient, but neither alone is sufficient (b) Statement 2 alone is sufficient, but Statement 1 alone is not (c) Statement 1 alone is sufficient, but Statement 2 alone is not (d) Statements 1 and 2 together are not sufficient
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is picking Statement 2 alone, thinking one equation fixes the number — but a digit difference of 3 allows 14, 25, 36, 47, 58, 69, and Statement 1 alone allows 18, 27, 36, 45 and more. Together, only 36 satisfies both conditions, since 3 + 6 = 9 and 63 − 36 = 27, so both statements together are sufficient.
Source: PYQ-style

103. Is triangle ABC a right-angled triangle?
Statement 1: The sides are in the ratio 3 : 4 : 5.
Statement 2: The area of the triangle is 6 square units.
(a) Statement 2 alone is sufficient, but Statement 1 alone is not (b) Each statement alone is sufficient (c) Both statements together are sufficient, but neither alone is sufficient (d) Statement 1 alone is sufficient, but Statement 2 alone is not
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is Statement 2: an area of 6 can belong to many triangles, right-angled or not, so it proves nothing about the angles. Statement 1 fixes the shape, and since 3² + 4² = 5², the converse of the Pythagoras theorem makes the triangle right-angled, so Statement 1 alone is sufficient.

(a) Statement 1 alone is sufficient, but Statement 2 alone is not (b) Both statements together are sufficient, but neither alone is sufficient (c) Statement 2 alone is sufficient, but Statement 1 alone is not (d) Statements 1 and 2 together are not sufficient
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is answering with Statement 1 alone and ignoring D entirely — Statement 1 gives B < A < C but says nothing about D, and Statement 2 alone says nothing about A and B. Together, C is taller than A, B and D, so C is the tallest and both statements together are sufficient.

106. Is y positive?

Statement 2: x − y is positive.
(a) Both statements together are sufficient, but neither alone is sufficient (b) Statement 2 alone is sufficient, but Statement 1 alone is not (c) Statements 1 and 2 together are not sufficient (d) Statement 1 alone is sufficient, but Statement 2 alone is not

Solution: The trap is adding the two statements to get 2x > 0 and concluding something about y — that only shows x is positive. With x = 5 and y = −3 both statements hold yet y is negative, while x = 5 and y = 3 also satisfies both with y positive, so the statements together are not sufficient.

109. Is the integer n odd?

Statement 2: 3n is odd.
(a) Both statements together are sufficient, but neither alone is sufficient (b) Statement 2 alone is sufficient, but Statement 1 alone is not (c) Each statement alone is sufficient (d) Statement 1 alone is sufficient, but Statement 2 alone is not
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is thinking a square can hide parity — it cannot, since n² is odd only when n is odd, so Statement 1 suffices. Likewise 3n is odd only when n is odd because 3 is odd, so Statement 2 also suffices alone, meaning each statement alone is sufficient.

111. What is the present age of Ravi?
Statement 1: Ravi is twice as old as his son.

(a) Statement 1 alone is sufficient, but Statement 2 alone is not (b) Statement 2 alone is sufficient, but Statement 1 alone is not (c) Each statement alone is sufficient (d) Both statements together are sufficient, but neither alone is sufficient
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is treating "twice as old" as a fixed age — each statement alone is one equation with two unknowns, so infinitely many age pairs fit. Together, R = 2S and R − 5 = 3(S − 5) give S = 10 and R = 20, so both statements together are sufficient.

114. What is the profit percentage earned by selling an article?

(a) Each statement alone is sufficient (b) Statement 1 alone is sufficient, but Statement 2 alone is not (c) Statement 2 alone is sufficient, but Statement 1 alone is not (d) Both statements together are sufficient, but neither alone is sufficient
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is computing profit from the selling price alone by assuming some standard cost — profit percent needs both cost price and selling price, so each statement alone is useless. Together, profit % = (600 − 500)/500 × 100 = 20%, so both statements together are sufficient.

117. What is the value of p + q?
Statement 1: p − q = 6.

(a) Each statement alone is sufficient (b) Statement 2 alone is sufficient, but Statement 1 alone is not (c) Both statements together are sufficient, but neither alone is sufficient (d) Statement 1 alone is sufficient, but Statement 2 alone is not
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is assuming Statement 2 alone fixes the sum — it factors as (p − q)(p + q) = 72, but without p − q the sum is indeterminate, and Statement 1 alone leaves p + q free. Together, 6(p + q) = 72 gives p + q = 12, so both statements together are sufficient.

125. Six persons P, Q, R, S, T and U are of different weights. Who is the lightest?
Statement 1: P is heavier than Q and R; S is heavier than P; T is heavier than S.
Statement 2: U is heavier than T.
(a) Statements 1 and 2 together are not sufficient (b) Statement 2 alone is sufficient, but Statement 1 alone is not (c) Both statements together are sufficient, but neither alone is sufficient (d) Statement 1 alone is sufficient, but Statement 2 alone is not
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is Statement 1: it builds the chain T > S > P > Q with P > R, but never compares Q and R — either could be the lightest. Statement 2 only adds U at the heavy end and changes nothing about Q versus R, so even together the lightest person is undetermined and the statements together are not sufficient.
Source: PYQ-style
