Questions 51–75: Medium

51. What is the value of the two-digit number?
Statement I: The sum of its digits is 9.
Statement II: The number obtained by reversing the digits exceeds the original number by 27.
(a) Statement I alone is sufficient (b) Statement II alone is sufficient (c) Both statements together are not sufficient (d) Both statements together are sufficient
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is to pick Statement II alone, but y − x = 3 has many solutions (14, 25, 36, …), and Statement I alone also gives many numbers (18, 27, 36, …). Combining x + y = 9 and y − x = 3 gives x = 3, y = 6, so the number is 36. Hence both statements together are sufficient, so the answer is both together.
Source: PYQ-style (SSC CGL pattern)

52. Is x an even integer?
Statement I: 3x is an even integer.
Statement II: x/2 is an integer.
(a) Statement II alone is sufficient (b) Either statement alone is sufficient (c) Statement I alone is sufficient (d) Neither statement alone nor together is sufficient
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is Statement I: 3x being even forces x to be even only if x is an integer, but x = 2/3 gives 3x = 2 (even) while x is not even, so Statement I is not sufficient. Statement II says x/2 is an integer, so x = 2k, which is definitely an even integer. Hence Statement II alone is sufficient, so the answer is Statement II alone.
Source: PYQ-style (Banking exam pattern)

53. Among A, B, C, D and E, who is the heaviest?
Statement I: B is heavier than A and C but lighter than D.
Statement II: E is lighter than D but heavier than B.
(a) Both statements together are sufficient (b) Statement II alone is sufficient (c) Statement I alone is sufficient (d) Both statements together are not sufficient
Answer: (a)
Solution: Statement I alone gives D > B > A, C but says nothing about E; Statement II alone gives D > E > B but nothing about A and C, so neither alone works. Together: D > E > B > A and C, so D is the heaviest. Hence both statements together are sufficient, so the answer is both together.
Source: PYQ-style (IBPS PO pattern)

54. What is the code for "sky" in the given code language?
Statement I: In that language, "blue sky high" is coded as "ta na ma" and "sky is blue" is coded as "na ta ra".
Statement II: In that language, "sky falls down" is coded as "na pi qi".
(a) Both statements together are sufficient (b) Statement II alone is sufficient (c) Statement I alone is sufficient (d) Both statements together are not sufficient
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is to assume Statement I alone fixes the code, but its two phrases share two words ("blue", "sky") and two codes ("ta", "na"), so "sky" could be either — not sufficient alone, and Statement II alone has no anchor at all. Together, the only word common to Statement II's phrase and Statement I's phrases is "sky", and the only common code is "na", so "sky" = "na". Hence both statements together are sufficient, so the answer is both together.
Source: PYQ-style (SBI PO pattern)

55. What is the average of five consecutive even numbers?
Statement I: The smallest number is 12.
Statement II: The largest number is 20.
(a) Statement I alone is sufficient (b) Either statement alone is sufficient (c) Statement II alone is sufficient (d) Neither statement alone nor together is sufficient
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is to pick "both together", but five consecutive even numbers are fully fixed once any one of them is known. Statement I gives 12, 14, 16, 18, 20 (average 16); Statement II gives the same set from the top (average 16). Hence either statement alone is sufficient, so the answer is either alone.

56. Is the integer n divisible by 6?
Statement I: n is divisible by 2.
Statement II: n is divisible by 3.
(a) Both statements together are sufficient (b) Statement II alone is sufficient (c) Both statements together are not sufficient (d) Statement I alone is sufficient
Answer: (a)
Solution: Statement I alone fails since n = 4 is divisible by 2 but not by 6; Statement II alone fails since n = 9 is divisible by 3 but not by 6. Together, n is divisible by both 2 and 3, and since 2 and 3 are coprime, n is divisible by 6. Hence both statements together are sufficient, so the answer is both together.
Source: PYQ-style (Banking exam pattern)

57. In which direction is point P with respect to point Q?
Statement I: R is to the north of Q, and P is to the east of R.
Statement II: S is to the south of P, and Q is to the west of S.
(a) Statement I alone is sufficient (b) Either statement alone is sufficient (c) Statement II alone is sufficient (d) Both statements together are not sufficient
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is to assume only one statement works. Statement I: R is north of Q and P is east of R, so P is north-east of Q — sufficient. Statement II: P is north of S and Q is west of S, so from Q, P lies to the north-east — also sufficient. Hence either statement alone is sufficient, so the answer is either alone.
Source: PYQ-style (RRB NTPC pattern)

58. What is the profit percentage earned by selling an article?
Statement I: The article is sold for ₹720 after a discount of 10% on the marked price.
Statement II: The cost price of the article is ₹600.
(a) Both statements together are not sufficient (b) Statement II alone is sufficient (c) Statement I alone is sufficient (d) Both statements together are sufficient
Answer: (d)
Solution: Statement I alone gives the selling price ₹720 but no cost price; Statement II alone gives only the cost price, so neither alone works. Together, profit = 720 − 600 = ₹120, so profit % = 120/600 × 100 = 20%. Hence both statements together are sufficient, so the answer is both together.

60. What is the rate of simple interest per annum?
Statement I: A sum of ₹5,000 amounts to ₹6,200 in 4 years.
Statement II: The same sum earns ₹1,200 as interest in 4 years.
(a) Statement I alone is sufficient (b) Neither statement alone nor together is sufficient (c) Either statement alone is sufficient (d) Statement II alone is sufficient
Answer: (c)
Solution: Statement I: interest = 6200 − 5000 = ₹1,200 over 4 years on ₹5,000, so rate = 1200/(5000×4) × 100 = 6% — sufficient. Statement II directly gives interest ₹1,200 on the same ₹5,000 for 4 years, again giving 6% — sufficient. Hence either statement alone is sufficient, so the answer is either alone.
Source: PYQ-style (Banking exam pattern)

61. Five friends P, Q, R, S and T sit in a row facing north. Who sits in the middle?
Statement I: P sits at the left end, and Q sits second to the right of P.
Statement II: R sits to the immediate left of T, and S sits at the right end.
(a) Both statements together are sufficient (b) Statement II alone is sufficient (c) Statement I alone is sufficient (d) Both statements together are not sufficient
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is to combine both statements, but Statement I alone already answers it: P at position 1 and Q second to his right means Q sits at position 3, which is exactly the middle of five seats. Statement II alone gives the adjacent pair R–T and S at the right end, but the middle seat could be Q, R or T, so it is not sufficient. Hence Statement I alone is sufficient, so the answer is Statement I alone.
Source: PYQ-style (IBPS PO pattern)

62. Is triangle ABC a right-angled triangle?
Statement I: The sides are in the ratio 3 : 4 : 5.
Statement II: The sum of two angles is 90°.
(a) Either statement alone is sufficient (b) Statement II alone is sufficient (c) Statement I alone is sufficient (d) Neither statement alone nor together is sufficient
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is to think angle information is weaker than side information. Statement I: 3² + 4² = 5², so by the converse of Pythagoras the triangle is right-angled — sufficient. Statement II: if two angles sum to 90°, the third is 180° − 90° = 90° — sufficient. Hence either statement alone is sufficient, so the answer is either alone.
Source: PYQ-style (SSC CGL pattern)

63. What is the two-digit number?
Statement I: The digit at the unit's place is twice the digit at the ten's place.
Statement II: The sum of the digits is 9.
(a) Statement I alone is sufficient (b) Statement II alone is sufficient (c) Both statements together are sufficient (d) Both statements together are not sufficient
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is to pick Statement I alone, but it allows 12, 24, 36 and 48 — four possibilities; Statement II alone allows 18, 27, 36, 45, … — also not sufficient. Together, only 36 satisfies both conditions (3 + 6 = 9 and 6 = 2×3). Hence both statements together are sufficient, so the answer is both together.

67. Is x > y?
Statement I: x² > y².
Statement II: x and y are both positive, and x² > y².
(a) Both statements together are not sufficient (b) Statement II alone is sufficient (c) Both statements together are sufficient (d) Statement I alone is sufficient
Answer: (b)
Solution: Statement I is the classic trap: x = −3, y = 2 gives x² = 9 > 4 = y² but x < y, so it is not sufficient. Statement II adds that both are positive, and for positive numbers x² > y² implies x > y — sufficient. Hence Statement II alone is sufficient, so the answer is Statement II alone.
Source: PYQ-style (IBPS PO pattern)

Statement II: The number of children is between 40 and 50.

Answer: (d)
Solution: Statement I alone fits many numbers (11, 20, 31, 44, …); Statement II alone gives only a range, so neither alone works. Together: between 40 and 50, only 44 works since 44 + 5 = 49 = 7². Hence both statements together are sufficient, so the answer is both together.

69. What is the area of the rectangle?

73. What is the value of x + y?
Statement I: x² − y² = 45 and x − y = 5.
Statement II: x = 7.
(a) Both statements together are not sufficient (b) Both statements together are sufficient (c) Statement I alone is sufficient (d) Statement II alone is sufficient
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is to combine both statements, but Statement I alone already answers it: x² − y² = (x − y)(x + y) = 45 with x − y = 5 gives x + y = 45/5 = 9. Statement II alone gives only x and nothing about y, so it is not sufficient. Hence Statement I alone is sufficient, so the answer is Statement I alone.
