🧒 FEYNMAN TECHNIQUE — Sentence Arrangement Explained Like You're 12

Imagine your friend gives you a jigsaw puzzle, but instead of a picture, the pieces are sentences. Your job: arrange them so they tell a story that makes sense. That's all Sentence Arrangement (Para Jumbles) is — a story puzzle.

🧩 The Daily-Life Example

Suppose I give you these four lines about making tea:

P. Then she poured the tea into two cups.
Q. Meera boiled water in a kettle.
R. Finally, they enjoyed the tea with biscuits.
S. After that, she added tea leaves and sugar.

Read them aloud. Which one starts the story? Only Q has no "then", "after that", or "finally" — it introduces Meera and the first action. So Q is the opener. "Then" (P) must come after something, "after that" (S) too, and "finally" (R) is clearly the end. Answer: Q → S → P → R.

You just solved a para jumble using ONE skill: hunting for connector words.

🔑 The Unforgettable Trick: "The Story Chain"

Every jumbled paragraph is a chain. Each sentence holds hands with the next one through a LINK:

- Pronoun Link: "He", "She", "It", "They" → must point back to a person/thing named earlier. A sentence starting with "He" can NEVER open the paragraph.
- Connector Link: "But", "However", "Therefore", "Moreover" → these are middle sentences, never openers.
- Time Link: "First… Then… Finally…" or dates in order.
- Name Link: Full name first ("Ravi Kumar"), short name later ("Kumar" / "he").

🧠 Memory Hook: "No Baby Starts With a Nickname!"
Just like you wouldn't call a stranger by a pet name, a paragraph never starts with "He", "This", "But", or "Also". The opener always introduces the full name/topic.

🎯 The 3-Step Exam Method

1. Find the OPENER — the sentence with no pronoun, no connector, that introduces the topic.
2. Make PAIRS — lock two sentences together using pronoun/connector links (even if you can't solve the whole order, pairs eliminate options).
3. Check the OPTIONS — never arrange all sentences in your head first. Use the options; eliminate any option that breaks your confirmed pair.

⚠️ The Trap to Avoid

Don't read the sentences in the given order (P, Q, R, S) repeatedly hoping the meaning will "click". It won't — that's the examiner's time-wasting trap. Go straight to hunting the opener and one strong pair.

📝 Blurting Sheet Instruction

Close this book. Take a blank sheet and in 60 seconds blurt out everything you remember: the 4 types of links, the "No Baby Starts With a Nickname" rule, and the 3-step method. Whatever you forgot — that's what you revise tonight.
