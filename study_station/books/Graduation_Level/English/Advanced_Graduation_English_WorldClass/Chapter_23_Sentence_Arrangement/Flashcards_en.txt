Card 1
Front: What is the first step in solving a Para Jumble (Sentence Arrangement) question?
Back: Find the opening sentence — it is usually independent, introduces the topic, and has no pronoun (he, it, this, they) or connector (but, however, so) referring backward.

Card 2
Front: Which sentences can NEVER open a paragraph?
Back: Sentences starting with pronouns (He, It, They), connectors (But, However, Therefore, Moreover), or phrases like "This shows…", "Such cases…" — they all depend on an earlier sentence.

Card 3
Front: What is a "Mandatory Pair" in sentence arrangement?
Back: Two sentences that must come together because of a logical link — e.g., a general statement followed by its example, a question followed by its answer, or a noun followed by its pronoun.

Card 4
Front: 🧠 Mnemonic: How do you remember the 5 clues for ordering sentences?
Back: "P-T-C-E-C" → Pronoun reference, Time sequence, Connectors, Examples follow general statements, Conclusion comes last. Remember: "Please Try Connecting Every Clue."

Card 5
Front: A sentence begins with "This theory…". What does it tell you?
Back: "This theory" is a demonstrative reference — the sentence defining or naming the theory must come immediately before it. Use it to lock a mandatory pair.

Card 6
Front: ⚠️ Examiner's Trap: Two sentences both look like perfect openers. How do you decide?
Back: Check which one is more general and which introduces background vs. the main subject. The trap: examiners place a sentence that sounds introductory but actually continues an idea (e.g., starts with "Moreover" or contains an unstated reference). Read both with the sentence that would follow them.

Card 7
Front: What role do time markers play in sentence arrangement?
Back: Words like "first… then… later… finally", or dates/years, force a chronological order. Arrange events from earliest to latest unless the passage is a flashback.

Card 8
Front: In a 5-sentence jumble, what is the fastest solving strategy?
Back: Don't arrange all 5 at once. Find the opener, lock 1–2 mandatory pairs, then eliminate options. Often just the opener + one pair eliminates 3 of the 4 options.

Card 9
Front: What is the difference between Para Jumbles and Sentence Rearrangement (P-Q-R-S) questions?
Back: Para Jumbles give 4–6 whole sentences to reorder into a paragraph. P-Q-R-S splits ONE sentence into parts (P, Q, R, S) between fixed first (S1) and last (S6) segments — you arrange the fragments grammatically.

Card 10
Front: In P-Q-R-S questions, what is the first thing to check?
Back: The fixed opening segment (S1). Read its last words — the correct next fragment must continue the grammar (e.g., if S1 ends with "not only", the next part must begin with "but also").

Card 11
Front: 🧠 Mnemonic: How do you remember the structure of a well-ordered paragraph?
Back: "G-S-E-C" → General statement → Specific details → Examples/Explanation → Conclusion. Remember: "Good Students Explain Clearly."

Card 12
Front: ⚠️ Examiner's Trap: A sentence starting with "But this was not the case…" appears in the jumble. What mistake do students make?
Back: They place it too early or treat "But" as a paragraph opener. Trap type: Sound-Right. "But" needs a contrasting positive statement immediately before it — find that pair first, then place the pair in the paragraph.

Card 13
Front: Which connector signals that a result/effect sentence follows?
Back: "Therefore", "Hence", "Consequently", "As a result", "Thus" — the cause must come before these. Similarly, "Because/Since" sentences usually follow the statement they explain.

Card 14
Front: A jumble contains a full name ("Ramesh Sharma") in one sentence and "He" in another. What is the rule?
Back: The full noun/name always comes first; the pronoun sentence comes after. The pronoun sentence can never open the paragraph.

Card 15
Front: What is the "Option Elimination" method and when should you use it?
Back: Instead of building the full sequence, check the options: find which option starts with your identified opener, then test the next link. Use it always in exams — it converts a 45-second question into a 15–20 second one.

Card 16
Front: Under the 15/45-second rule, how are sentence arrangement questions classified?
Back: Mostly Inference-based (45 sec) — they need logical linking, not fact recall. Exception: P-Q-R-S with a fixed grammatical clue (like "not only… but also") can be Fact-based (15 sec). If no link appears in 45 seconds, mark and skip.

Card 17
Front: What is an "acronym rule" in paragraph ordering?
Back: The full form ("The Reserve Bank of India") must appear before the abbreviation ("The RBI"). Any sentence using only the abbreviation follows the one with the full form.

Card 18
Front: How do you identify the closing sentence of a paragraph?
Back: It summarizes, concludes, or gives a result/final remark — often marked by "Thus", "In conclusion", "Ultimately", or a general takeaway. It never introduces a new idea.

Card 19
Front: A jumble has a sentence with "For instance…". Where does it go?
Back: Immediately after the general statement it illustrates. Example sentences are the strongest mandatory-pair clue — they can never open a paragraph and never precede the idea they exemplify.

Card 20
Front: What should you do after arranging all sentences, before marking the answer?
Back: Read the full sequence once in order. Check: Does every pronoun have a noun before it? Does every connector have its matching idea? Does the paragraph flow general → specific → conclusion? If any link breaks, re-check that pair.
