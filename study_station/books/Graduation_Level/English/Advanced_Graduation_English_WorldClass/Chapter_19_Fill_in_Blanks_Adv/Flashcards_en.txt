Card 1
Front: The committee ______ divided in their opinion. (is/are) — which is correct and why?
Back: "are" — when a collective noun shows members acting individually (divided opinion), use a plural verb. Use "is" only when the group acts as one unit.

Card 2
Front: What is the Intervening Phrase trap in fill-in-the-blanks?
Back: A phrase between subject and verb ("The quality of the apples ______ good") tempts you to match the verb with the nearest noun. Always find the real subject ("quality") — the verb agrees with it, not the middle noun.

Card 3
Front: Mnemonic: How to remember that "news, physics, economics, mathematics, measles" take singular verbs?
Back: Remember "My NEWS is PEMM" — Physics, Economics, Mathematics, Measles and similar words ending in -ics/-s look plural but are singular: "The news is good." (Note: "sports" is normally plural — "Sports are popular.")

Card 4
Front: "One of my friends ______ going abroad." Fill the blank and state the rule.
Back: "is" — after "one of / each of / either of + plural noun," the verb is singular because the true subject is "one," not "friends."

Card 5
Front: Examiner's Trap: "Neither of the two boys ______ done his homework." Why is "have" wrong?
Back: "Neither of" is singular, so the verb must be "has." The plural noun "boys" right before the blank is the decoy — a classic Sound-Right trap.

Card 6
Front: What is the 15/45-second rule for cloze and fill-in-the-blank questions?
Back: Fact-based blanks (fixed prepositions, articles, simple agreement) should be solved in ~15 seconds. Inference-based blanks (tone, connector, vocabulary-in-context) deserve up to 45 seconds — read the sentence before and after.

Card 7
Front: Fill in: "He is junior ______ me." Why not "than"?
Back: "to" — Latin comparatives (junior, senior, superior, inferior, prior, prefer) take "to," never "than." Mnemonic: "Latin loves TO."

Card 8
Front: What does the Greek root "bene" mean? Give three derived words.
Back: "Good/well" — benefit, benevolent, beneficial. In a blank, a positive context clue (praise, kindness) signals a "bene-" word.

Card 9
Front: Fill in: "Scarcely had he entered the room ______ the phone rang." (when/than/then/that)
Back: "when" — "scarcely/hardly" pair with "when"; "no sooner" pairs with "than." Examiners swap these to trap you.

Card 10
Front: Mnemonic: How to remember the paired conjunctions?
Back: "NEITHER-NOR, EITHER-OR, NOT ONLY-BUT ALSO, NO SOONER-THAN, HARDLY-WHEN" — chant it as "N-E-N-N-H": Never Exchange Neatly Nested Hooks. Wrong pairing is a top exam trap.

Card 11
Front: What is the "Sound-Right" trap?
Back: An option that sounds natural in spoken Hindi-influenced English but is grammatically wrong — e.g., "He is more smarter" (double comparative) or "return back." Trust the rule, not your ear.

Card 12
Front: Fill in: "The principal, along with the teachers, ______ present." (was/were)
Back: "was" — phrases like "along with, as well as, together with, in addition to" do NOT change the subject. The verb agrees with "principal" alone.

Card 13
Front: What does the Latin root "mal" mean, and how does it help in blanks?
Back: "Bad/evil" — malicious, malfunction, malnutrition. If the sentence has negative tone words (harm, failure, disease), a "mal-" word fits the blank.

Card 14
Front: Examiner's Trap: "Each of the students have submitted the assignment." Spot the error.
Back: "have" should be "has." "Each of + plural noun" always takes a singular verb. The plural "students" is placed just before the verb to mislead you.

Card 15
Front: Fill in: "I look forward ______ meeting you." Why "meeting" and not "to meet"?
Back: Here "to" is a preposition (part of the phrasal verb "look forward to"), so it takes a gerund (-ing), not an infinitive. Same with "object to," "be used to," "accustomed to."

Card 16
Front: How do you choose between "fewer" and "less" in a blank?
Back: "Fewer" for countable nouns (fewer students), "less" for uncountable nouns (less water). Quick test: can you count it one-by-one? Then use "fewer."

Card 17
Front: What is the Detective Approach for inference-based blanks?
Back: Treat the sentence as a crime scene: (1) find tone clues (but, however, despite = contrast; because, so = cause), (2) predict the blank's meaning before seeing options, (3) eliminate options that clash with the tone.

Card 18
Front: Fill in: "Despite ______ rich, he lives simply." (being/he is/of being/his)
Back: "being" — "despite/in spite of" is followed by a noun or gerund, never a full clause. "Despite he is rich" is a classic Hindi-speaker error.

Card 19
Front: What does the root "chrono" mean? Use it to guess "chronological" and "anachronism."
Back: "Time" — chronological = arranged in time order; anachronism = something out of its proper time. Root knowledge lets you eliminate 2–3 wrong options instantly.

Card 20
Front: When should you SKIP a fill-in-the-blank question in the exam?
Back: Skip when (1) two options both seem correct after 45 seconds, (2) the blank depends on an idiom you've never seen, or (3) it's a hard vocabulary blank with no context clue. Mark it, move on, return only if time remains — never gamble blindly.
