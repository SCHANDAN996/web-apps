🧒 Feynman Technique: The "Subject-Verb Agreement" Trap

Imagine you have to explain Subject-Verb Agreement to a 10-year-old. If you can do it simply, you truly understand it. If you get stuck, that is exactly the gap you need to fix.

Step 1 — Explain it simply:
"Every sentence has a subject (who/what the sentence is about) and a verb (the action). The verb must match the subject: singular subject → singular verb, plural subject → plural verb."

Step 2 — The hardest concept: hidden modifiers

The trap: when a phrase comes between the subject and the verb, the verb must agree with the subject, not with the phrase in between.

Example: "The quality of the mangoes was good."
- Wrong instinct: "mangoes" is plural, so use "were" ❌
- Correct: the real subject is "quality" (singular), so "was" ✔
- "of the mangoes" is just extra information sitting between the subject and the verb.

Step 3 — Teach it with a trick:
"Cover the words between the subject and the verb with your finger. Now check: does the verb match the subject?"

Try it:
- "The box of chocolates is on the table." (subject = box, not chocolates)
- "A group of students was waiting outside." (subject = group)
- "The leader, along with his followers, has arrived." (subject = leader; "along with..." does not change it)

Step 4 — Find your gap:
If you picked the verb by looking at the nearest noun instead of the real subject, that is your weak point. Go back, re-read the rule, and explain it aloud again until a child could follow you.

Exam tip: SSC and Bank exams love this trap — they place a plural noun right before the verb to lure you. Always ask: "Who or what is the sentence really about?"
