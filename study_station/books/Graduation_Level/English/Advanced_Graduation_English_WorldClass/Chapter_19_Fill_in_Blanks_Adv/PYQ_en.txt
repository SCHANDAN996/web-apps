# PYQ Pattern Analysis — Fill in the Blanks (English)

## How Exams Test This Chapter

Fill in the Blanks is one of the most regularly asked question types in the English sections of SSC CGL, CHSL, IBPS PO/Clerk, and State PCS prelims. A sentence is given with one or two blanks, and you must choose the word or phrase that fits grammatically and logically. The questions look simple, but examiners design the options so that two or three of them look "almost right."

## Recurring Sub-Topics

Based on the pattern of questions asked across these exams, the blanks most often test:

1. **Vocabulary in context** — choosing the word whose meaning suits the sentence (e.g., *abstain / refrain / restrain*). This is the most common type.
2. **Prepositions** — fixed preposition usage after verbs, adjectives, and nouns (*depend on, insist on, afraid of, good at*).
3. **Articles** — *a / an / the* or no article, especially before uncountable nouns and fixed phrases.
4. **Tenses and verb forms** — choosing the correct tense or the correct form after modals, infinitives, and gerunds.
5. **Conjunctions and connectors** — *although / despite / because / unless / until*; the trap is mixing up *despite* (followed by a noun) with *although* (followed by a clause).
6. **Subject–verb agreement** — the blank hides the verb far from its real subject.
7. **Phrasal verbs and idioms** — *put off, give in, look after*; meaning must match the sentence.
8. **Confusable word pairs** — *principal / principle, advice / advise, stationary / stationery, accept / except*.

## Typical Examiner Traps

- **Near-correct options:** All four options are real English words, but only one fits both the grammar and the meaning. Read the whole sentence, not just the words around the blank.
- **Collocation trap:** A word may be grammatically correct but not the natural partner of the surrounding words (we say *make a decision*, not *do a decision*).
- **Double-blank confusion:** In two-blank questions, one option may have the first word right and the second wrong. Check **both** words before answering.
- **Negative and conditional clues:** Words like *hardly, unless, despite, although* reverse or restrict the meaning of the sentence. Missing them leads to the opposite answer.
- **Similar-sounding distractors:** Options like *affect / effect* or *elicit / illicit* test whether you know the exact meaning, not just the sound.

## How to Attempt (Strategy)

1. Read the full sentence once for meaning before looking at the options.
2. Predict your own word for the blank, then match it with the closest option.
3. Check grammar signals: article before the blank, preposition after it, singular/plural subject.
4. Eliminate options that break grammar first, then choose on meaning.
5. For double blanks, verify both words together — never decide on the first word alone.
6. Time advice: aim for about 20–30 seconds per question; if stuck, mark and move on.

## What Toppers Do Differently

- They maintain a personal list of confusable word pairs and phrasal verbs and revise it weekly.
- They read the sentence aloud (mentally) to catch what "sounds wrong" — a skill built by regular reading.
- They never guess between two options without re-reading the sentence for a clue word.
