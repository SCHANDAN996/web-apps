# 📋 Important Grammar Rules & Tips — Fill in the Blanks (Advanced)

## (a) 12 High-Yield Rules

| # | Rule | Example | Examiner's Trap |
|---|------|---------|-----------------|
| 1 | **Subject–Verb Agreement**: Ignore intervening phrases; the verb agrees with the real subject. | The quality of the apples **was** good. | "The quality of the apples **were**…" — 'apples' is a decoy; subject is 'quality'. |
| 2 | **Parallel Structure**: Items joined by and/or/but must be in the same grammatical form. | She likes reading, swimming, and **painting**. | "She likes reading, swimming, and **to paint**" breaks parallelism. |
| 3 | **Tense Consistency**: Keep the time frame uniform unless a time marker demands a shift. | When he **arrived**, the train had left. | Mixing past perfect and simple past wrongly: "When he arrived, the train **has** left." |
| 4 | **Preposition Collocation**: Certain verbs/adjectives take fixed prepositions. | He is good **at** mathematics; she insisted **on** going. | "Good **in** mathematics", "insisted **for**" — sound-right traps for Hindi speakers. |
| 5 | **Article Logic**: 'A/an' for first mention or general; 'the' for specific or second mention. | I saw **a** dog. **The** dog was black. | Blank before a unique noun (the Sun, the Ganga) filled with 'a'. |
| 6 | **Conditional Sentences**: If + past simple → would + V1; If + past perfect → would have + V3. | If he **had studied**, he would have passed. | "If he **would have** studied…" — 'would' never sits in the if-clause. |
| 7 | **Non-finite Verbs**: Gerund after prepositions and certain verbs (enjoy, avoid); infinitive after want, decide. | He avoided **meeting** her. | "He avoided **to meet** her" — a classic sound-right error. |
| 8 | **Comparatives/Superlatives**: 'Than' follows comparatives; double comparatives are wrong. | She is **taller than** her sister. | "More taller", "most unique" — absolute adjectives (unique, perfect) take no comparison. |
| 9 | **Pronoun Reference**: A pronoun must agree with its antecedent in number and gender. | Everyone should do **his or her** duty. | "Everyone should do **their** duty" — accepted in speech, tested as an error in exams. |
| 10 | **Conjunction Pairs**: Correlatives come in fixed pairs — either…or, neither…nor, not only…but also, hardly…when, no sooner…than. | **No sooner** had he left **than** it rained. | "No sooner…**when**" — pairing trap; 'than' follows 'no sooner'. |
| 11 | **Word Form (Morphology)**: Choose noun/verb/adjective/adverb by the blank's position in the sentence. | His decision was **final**. (adjective after 'was') | "His decision was **finally**" — right root, wrong form. |
| 12 | **Contextual Vocabulary**: The word must fit the tone and meaning of the whole sentence, not just the blank. | The evidence was too **flimsy** to convict him. | Options with similar meanings (weak/fragile/flimsy/feeble) — only one collocates with 'evidence'. |

## (b) Time Management — The 15/45-Second Rule

| Type | What it looks like | Time | Strategy |
|------|-------------------|------|----------|
| **Fact-based (15 sec)** | Preposition collocation, article, fixed conjunction pair, standard subject–verb agreement, common idiom | 15 seconds | Read the blank ± 3 words, recall the rule, mark, move on. If not known in 15 sec, flag and skip — these cannot be "figured out". |
| **Inference-based (45 sec)** | Vocabulary-in-context, tone-dependent blanks, connector choice (however/therefore/although), cloze passages where one blank depends on another | 45 seconds | Read the full sentence (or the whole cloze paragraph), predict your own word before looking at options, then match. Eliminate by tone first, then by grammar. |

**Rule of thumb:** If the answer lives inside the sentence structure → 15 sec. If the answer lives in the meaning of the passage → 45 sec. Never spend 45 seconds on a fact-based blank; never rush an inference blank in 15.

## (c) Morphology — Root, Affix, Example

| Root / Affix | Meaning | Example Word | Use in a Blank |
|--------------|---------|--------------|----------------|
| **bene-** (Latin) | good, well | benevolent, benefit | A ___ ruler cares for the people → *benevolent* |
| **mal-** (Latin) | bad | malicious, malfunction | His ___ intent was obvious → *malicious* |
| **dict-** (Latin) | say, speak | predict, contradict | No one dared to ___ the minister → *contradict* |
| **spect-** (Latin) | look, see | inspect, perspective | The officer came to ___ the site → *inspect* |
| **port-** (Latin) | carry | transport, export | Goods are ___ed by sea → *transported/exported* |
| **-phobia** (Greek) | fear | claustrophobia, xenophobia | Fear of foreigners → *xenophobia* |
| **anti-** (Greek) | against | antidote, antisocial | A substance that counters poison → *antidote* |
| **-logy** (Greek) | study of | biology, geology | Study of the Earth's structure → *geology* |
| **un- / dis- / mis-** (prefixes) | not / opposite / wrongly | unhappy, disagree, mislead | To guide wrongly → *mislead* |
| **-tion / -ment / -ness** (suffixes) | make a noun | decision, agreement, kindness | After 'the' or 'his', a verb form is wrong — pick the noun form. |

**Morphology shortcut for blanks:** Look at the word *before* the blank. After 'a/an/the/his/their' → noun. After 'is/was/seems' + describing → adjective. Modifying a verb → adverb (-ly). After 'to' → base verb (V1).
