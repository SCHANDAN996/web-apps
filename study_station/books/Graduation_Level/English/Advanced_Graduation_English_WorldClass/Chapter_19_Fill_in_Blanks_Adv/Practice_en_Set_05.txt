Fill in the Blanks (Advanced) — Practice Set 5
Questions 101–125 | Medium: 15 | Hard: 10 | Examiner-Trap Questions: 107, 118

101. The committee's decision was ______ with the long-term interests of the company.
(a) consistency (b) consisted (c) consisting (d) consistent
Answer: (d)
Solution: The blank needs an adjective after "was"; "consistent with" is the correct collocation meaning "in agreement with". "Consisted" is a verb form and breaks the structure.
Source: PYQ-style

102. Hardly ______ the station when the train left.
(a) did I reach (b) I had reached (c) I reached (d) had I reached
Answer: (d)
Solution: After "Hardly" at the start of a sentence, inversion is required, and the past perfect is used: "Hardly had I reached... when...". "Did I reach" wrongly pairs with "when" instead of "than"-type structures.
Source: NCERT-based grammar

103. The minister's speech was so ______ that even his opponents applauded.
(a) provocative (b) ambiguous (c) conciliatory (d) monotonous
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Even his opponents applauded" signals a positive, peace-making tone; "conciliatory" means intended to placate or reconcile. "Provocative" would anger opponents, contradicting the clue.
Source: PYQ-style

104. Neither the manager nor the employees ______ aware of the new policy.
(a) was (b) has been (c) were (d) is
Answer: (c)
Solution: With "neither...nor", the verb agrees with the nearer subject, "employees" (plural), so "were". "Was" is the classic trap of agreeing with the first subject.
Source: Standard grammar rule

105. The scientist's theory, though initially ridiculed, was eventually ______ by the academic community.
(a) refuted (b) vindicated (c) undermined (d) neglected
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Though initially ridiculed" sets up a contrast; "vindicated" (proven right) completes the reversal. "Refuted" repeats the ridicule instead of contrasting it.
Source: PYQ-style

106. He ______ his resignation before the inquiry could begin.
(a) tendered (b) rendered (c) surrendered (d) submitted up
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Tender one's resignation" is the standard formal collocation. "Render" means to provide a service or verdict, not resignation.
Source: PYQ-style

107. The number of students absent from class ______ increasing every semester.
(a) are (b) were (c) is (d) have been
Answer: (c)
Solution: Examiner's trap: "The number of" takes a singular verb ("is"), while "A number of" takes a plural verb. The plural noun "students" in between is a decoy.
Source: Standard grammar rule

108. The two brothers divided the property ______ themselves.
(a) among (b) between (c) within (d) amongst
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Between" is used for two persons; "among/amongst" is for more than two. The word "two" is the decisive clue.
Source: Standard grammar rule

109. The novel's protagonist is a ______ figure who defies easy moral classification.
(a) complex (b) transparent (c) one-dimensional (d) superficial
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Defies easy moral classification" demands a word meaning layered or intricate — "complex". "One-dimensional" and "superficial" mean the opposite.
Source: PYQ-style

110. ______ the rain, the match continued uninterrupted.
(a) Despite (b) In spite (c) Despite of (d) Although
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Despite" is followed directly by a noun; "despite of" is wrong, and "in spite" needs "of". "Although" requires a clause, not a noun.
Source: Standard grammar rule

111. The court ______ the verdict until further evidence could be examined.
(a) preserved (b) deserved (c) conserved (d) reserved
Answer: (d)
Solution: "Reserve judgment/verdict" is the legal collocation meaning to postpone a decision. "Preserve" and "conserve" relate to keeping things safe, not postponing rulings.
Source: PYQ-style

112. She is one of those writers who ______ never compromised on quality.
(a) have (b) is (c) has (d) was
Answer: (a)
Solution: In "one of those who...", the relative pronoun "who" refers to the plural "writers", so the verb is plural: "have". "Has" is the tempting trap agreeing with "one".
Source: Standard grammar rule

113. The new policy is likely to ______ small businesses more than large corporations.
(a) affect (b) defect (c) infect (d) effect
Answer: (a)
Solution: The blank needs a verb meaning "to influence" — "affect". "Effect" as a verb means "to bring about", which does not fit the meaning here.
Source: PYQ-style

114. The ancient manuscript was so ______ that only a handful of scholars could decipher it.
(a) obscure (b) lucid (c) legible (d) explicit
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Only a handful could decipher it" implies the text was unclear or difficult — "obscure". "Legible", "lucid" and "explicit" all suggest clarity, the opposite.
Source: PYQ-style

115. By the time we reach the venue, the ceremony ______.
(a) will start (b) has started (c) would start (d) will have started
Answer: (d)
Solution: "By the time" with a future reference requires the future perfect: "will have started". The simple future ignores the completion implied by "by the time".
Source: Standard grammar rule

116. The committee ______ the project's failure to poor planning and inadequate funding.
(a) attribute (b) attributable (c) attributed (d) attributing
Answer: (c)
Solution: The sentence needs a finite past-tense verb with subject "the committee" — "attributed X to Y" means "considered X to be caused by Y". "Attributable" is an adjective and cannot serve as the verb here.
Source: PYQ-style

117. The government has decided to ______ the outdated law and enact a comprehensive new act in its place.
(a) replenish (b) repeal (c) displace (d) conceal
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Repeal" means to officially revoke a law, which fits the context of replacing an old act with a new one. "Replenish" means to refill and "displace" means to force out of position — neither applies to laws.
Source: PYQ-style

118. Each of the candidates ______ given ten minutes to present their case.
(a) were (b) was (c) have been (d) are
Answer: (b)
Solution: Examiner's trap: "Each of" always takes a singular verb — "was". The plural "candidates" and the later "their" are deliberate decoys; "their" as a singular pronoun is acceptable, but the verb stays singular.
Source: Standard grammar rule

119. His argument was so ______ that it convinced even the most sceptical members of the jury.
(a) fallacious (b) cogent (c) tenuous (d) specious
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Convinced even the most sceptical" demands a word meaning forcefully convincing — "cogent". "Specious" (seemingly true but false) and "fallacious" are negative traps.
Source: PYQ-style

120. The company ______ its employees a bonus if profits exceed the target this year.
(a) would give (b) will give (c) gives (d) gave
Answer: (b)
Solution: In a real conditional about the future ("if profits exceed"), the main clause takes "will give". "Would give" belongs to unreal conditionals.
Source: Standard grammar rule

121. The diplomat handled the delicate negotiations with remarkable ______.
(a) clumsiness (b) apathy (c) rigidity (d) finesse
Answer: (d)
Solution: "Delicate negotiations" handled "remarkably" well calls for "finesse" (skilful subtlety). The other options describe failure, contradicting "remarkable".
Source: PYQ-style

122. Scarcely had she finished her speech ______ the audience erupted in applause.
(a) then (b) than (c) when (d) that
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Scarcely/Hardly... when" and "No sooner... than" are fixed pairs. Pairing "scarcely" with "than" is a classic examiner trap.
Source: Standard grammar rule

123. The report highlighted the ______ effects of climate change on coastal communities.
(a) ephemeral (b) beneficial (c) negligible (d) deleterious
Answer: (d)
Solution: Climate change's effects on coastal communities are harmful — "deleterious" means damaging. "Ephemeral" (short-lived) understates the harm, and "beneficial" reverses it.
Source: PYQ-style

124. I would rather you ______ the truth from the beginning.
(a) tell (b) had told (c) told (d) have told
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Would rather + subject" is followed by the past simple for present/future preference: "I would rather you told". "Had told" would refer to regret about the past, which "from the beginning" alone does not establish.
Source: Standard grammar rule

125. The CEO's ______ leadership style alienated employees who valued collaboration.
(a) autocratic (b) participatory (c) laissez-faire (d) democratic
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Alienated employees who valued collaboration" points to a dictatorial style — "autocratic". "Democratic" and "participatory" involve collaboration, so they contradict the clue.
Source: PYQ-style
