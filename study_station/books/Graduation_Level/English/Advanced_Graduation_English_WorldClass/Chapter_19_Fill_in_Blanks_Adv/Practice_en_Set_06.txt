Fill in the Blanks (Advanced) — Practice Set 6
Questions 126–150 | Medium: 126–135 | Hard: 136–150

126. The committee's report was so ______ that even seasoned officials struggled to grasp its implications.
(a) lucid (b) candid (c) abstruse (d) terse
Answer: (c)
Solution: The clause "struggled to grasp its implications" demands a word meaning "difficult to understand", which is "abstruse". "Lucid" means clear — the exact opposite; "candid" means frank; "terse" means brief.
Source: PYQ-style

127. Hardly ______ the station when the train pulled out.
(a) we had reached (b) did we reach (c) we reached (d) had we reached
Answer: (d)
Solution: After "Hardly" at the start of a sentence, inversion is required, and the past perfect is used: "Hardly had we reached". "Did we reach" wrongly pairs the simple past with "hardly... when".
Source: NCERT Class 10 English Grammar

128. The minister's speech was full of ______ promises that nobody took seriously.
(a) tangible (b) credible (c) hollow (d) concrete
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Nobody took seriously" signals the promises lacked substance — "hollow" fits. "Tangible", "credible" and "concrete" all suggest reliability, which contradicts the second half.
Source: PYQ-style

129. Neither the manager nor the employees ______ aware of the new policy.
(a) was (b) were (c) is (d) has been
Answer: (b)
Solution: With "neither... nor", the verb agrees with the nearer subject — "employees" (plural) — so "were". "Was" is the classic trap for those who match the verb with "manager".
Source: NCERT Class 9 English Grammar

130. His argument was so ______ that it convinced even his fiercest critics.
(a) cogent (b) specious (c) feeble (d) ambiguous
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Convinced even his fiercest critics" requires a positive word meaning "forcefully convincing" — "cogent". "Specious" (plausible but false) is the attractive trap; a specious argument would not genuinely convince critics.
Source: PYQ-style

131. The two brothers divided the property ______ themselves.
(a) between (b) among (c) within (d) amongst
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Between" is used for two persons; "among" and "amongst" are used for more than two. Since there are two brothers, "between" is correct.
Source: NCERT Class 8 English Grammar

132. The scientist's findings were ______ with the data collected over two decades.
(a) inconsistent (b) contrary (c) irrelevant (d) congruous
Answer: (d)
Solution: The sentence needs a word meaning "in harmony with" — "congruous". "Inconsistent" and "contrary" would need a contrast marker like "although", which is absent.
Source: PYQ-style

133. ______ the rain, the match continued uninterrupted.
(a) Despite (b) In spite (c) Despite of (d) Although
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Despite" takes no "of" and is followed by a noun phrase ("the rain"). "Despite of" is a common error; "in spite" needs "of"; "although" requires a clause with a verb.
Source: NCERT Class 10 English Grammar

134. The new law seeks to ______ the loopholes that allowed tax evasion.
(a) plug (b) exploit (c) widen (d) ignore
Answer: (a)
Solution: A law against tax evasion would close the loopholes — "plug" means to block or close. "Widen" and "exploit" would help evasion, contradicting the purpose.
Source: PYQ-style

135. She is one of those writers who ______ readers across generations.
(a) inspires (b) has inspired (c) inspire (d) is inspiring
Answer: (c)
Solution: The relative pronoun "who" refers to "writers" (plural), not "one", so the verb is plural — "inspire". Choosing "inspires" by matching "one" is the classic examiner's trap.
Source: NCERT Class 9 English Grammar

136. The CEO's ______ remarks at the press conference sparked a controversy that the company struggled to contain.
(a) guarded (b) diplomatic (c) measured (d) intemperate
Answer: (d)
Solution: "Sparked a controversy" demands a negative word — "intemperate" (lacking restraint). "Measured", "diplomatic" and "guarded" all imply careful speech, which would not cause controversy.
Source: PYQ-style

137. No sooner had the verdict been announced ______ the crowd erupted in protest.
(a) when (b) than (c) then (d) that
Answer: (b)
Solution: The fixed correlative pair is "No sooner... than". "When" pairs with "hardly" and "scarcely" — mixing the two pairs is a frequent trap.
Source: NCERT Class 10 English Grammar

138. The historian argued that the empire's collapse was not sudden but the result of decades of ______ decay.
(a) precipitous (b) overt (c) abrupt (d) insidious
Answer: (d)
Solution: "Not sudden but... decades of" calls for a word meaning "gradual and hidden" — "insidious". "Precipitous" and "abrupt" mean sudden, contradicting the sentence; "overt" means open, the opposite of hidden.
Source: PYQ-style

139. The report, along with the annexures, ______ submitted to the ministry yesterday.
(a) have been (b) was (c) were (d) are
Answer: (b)
Solution: With "along with", the verb agrees with the first subject — "the report" (singular) — and "yesterday" fixes the past tense: "was". "Were" is the trap for those who add the two nouns together.
Source: NCERT Class 9 English Grammar

140. His ______ memory allowed him to recall entire passages after a single reading.
(a) erratic (b) fading (c) prodigious (d) selective
Answer: (c)
Solution: Recalling entire passages after one reading shows an extraordinary memory — "prodigious". "Erratic", "fading" and "selective" all suggest weakness, contradicting the context.
Source: PYQ-style

141. The defendant claimed that he ______ the documents without reading them.
(a) has signed (b) had signed (c) signs (d) is signing
Answer: (b)
Solution: The signing happened before the claiming (past tense "claimed"), so the earlier action takes the past perfect — "had signed". "Has signed" wrongly mixes the present perfect with a past reporting verb.
Source: NCERT Class 10 English Grammar

142. The novel's charm lies in its ______ portrayal of village life, free from sentimentality.
(a) unvarnished (b) idealised (c) exaggerated (d) maudlin
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Free from sentimentality" points to a plain, honest depiction — "unvarnished". "Maudlin" means excessively sentimental, the exact opposite; "idealised" also contradicts the clue.
Source: PYQ-style

143. ______ the best efforts of the rescue team, the trapped miners could not be saved.
(a) For all (b) Despite of (c) Owing to (d) Because
Answer: (a)
Solution: "For all" means "in spite of" and fits a noun phrase. "Despite of" is ungrammatical; "owing to" would invert the logic (efforts causing failure); "because" needs "of" and also inverts the logic.
Source: PYQ-style

144. The principal prohibited the students ______ the laboratories without permission.
(a) to enter (b) enter (c) entering (d) from entering
Answer: (d)
Solution: "Prohibit" takes the fixed pattern "prohibit somebody from + -ing" — "from entering". "To enter" is the trap; that infinitive pattern belongs to verbs like "order" or "allow", not "prohibit".
Source: NCERT Class 9 English Grammar

145. The diplomat's ______ handling of the crisis prevented a potential conflict between the two nations.
(a) negligent (b) clumsy (c) adroit (d) belated
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Prevented a potential conflict" shows skilful handling — "adroit". "Clumsy" and "negligent" would cause, not prevent, a crisis; "belated" means late, which does not fit.
Source: PYQ-style

146. Each of the candidates ______ required to submit two photographs with the form.
(a) are (b) is (c) were (d) have been
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Each of" takes a singular verb — "is". "Are" is the trap for those who agree the verb with the plural noun "candidates" nearest to it.
Source: NCERT Class 8 English Grammar

147. The critic dismissed the film as a ______ imitation of the classic original.
(a) peerless (b) inspired (c) slavish (d) novel
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Dismissed" signals criticism, and imitation without originality is "slavish". "Peerless" and "inspired" are praises, contradicting "dismissed"; "novel" means new, also contradictory.
Source: PYQ-style

148. Scarcely ______ the lecture hall when the fire alarm rang.
(a) the professor entered (b) had the professor entered (c) the professor had entered (d) did the professor enter
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Scarcely" at the beginning requires inversion with the past perfect: "had the professor entered". The uninverted order and the simple past with "did" are both ungrammatical here.
Source: NCERT Class 10 English Grammar

149. The government's ______ approach to reform — bold in rhetoric but timid in action — drew criticism from all quarters.
(a) sweeping (b) resolute (c) consistent (d) pusillanimous
Answer: (d)
Solution: The dash explains the blank: "timid in action" — "pusillanimous" means lacking courage. "Resolute" and "sweeping" contradict "timid"; "consistent" clashes with the bold-timid contrast.
Source: PYQ-style

150. The ancient manuscript was so ______ that only a handful of scholars in the world could decipher it.
(a) recondite (b) lucid (c) hackneyed (d) legible
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Only a handful of scholars could decipher it" means the manuscript dealt with obscure, little-known matter — "recondite". "Legible" and "lucid" mean readable or clear, the opposite; "hackneyed" means overused and trite, which says nothing about difficulty.
Source: PYQ-style
