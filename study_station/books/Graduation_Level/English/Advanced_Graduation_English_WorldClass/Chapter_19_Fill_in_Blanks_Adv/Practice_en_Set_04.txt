76. The committee, along with its advisors, ______ submitted the final report to the ministry.
(a) have (b) has (c) were (d) are
Answer: (b)
Solution: When two subjects are joined by "along with", the verb agrees with the first subject ("committee", singular), so "has" is correct. "Have" tempts because "advisors" is plural — a classic intervening-phrase trap.
Source: PYQ-style

77. Hardly ______ the station when the train left.
(a) I had reached (b) did I reach (c) I reached (d) had I reached
Answer: (d)
Solution: After "Hardly" at the start of a sentence, inversion is required: "Hardly had I reached". The past perfect is used because reaching happened before the train left.
Source: NCERT-based grammar

78. The manager insisted that the report ______ completed by Friday.
(a) be (b) was (c) is (d) will be
Answer: (a)
Solution: After verbs of demand like "insist", the subjunctive mood is used: "that the report be completed". "Was" sounds natural in speech but is grammatically wrong here — a sound-right trap.
Source: PYQ-style

79. Neither the players nor the coach ______ satisfied with the umpire's decision.
(a) was (b) were (c) have been (d) are
Answer: (a)
Solution: With "neither...nor", the verb agrees with the nearer subject ("coach", singular), so "was". "Were" attracts because "players" is plural and comes first.
Source: PYQ-style

80. She is one of those writers who ______ always praised by critics.
(a) are (b) was (c) is (d) has been
Answer: (a)
Solution: In "one of those who...", the relative pronoun "who" refers to the plural "writers", so the verb is plural: "are". The examiner's trap is "is", which looks right because of "one of".
Source: PYQ-style

81. The data collected from the survey ______ that most voters favour the new policy.
(a) shows (b) show (c) is showing (d) has shown
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Data" is the plural of "datum" and in formal exam English takes a plural verb: "show". "Shows" is a sound-right trap since "data" is often treated as singular in casual usage.
Source: PYQ-style

82. ______ the heavy rain, the match continued without interruption.
(a) Because of (b) Despite (c) Due to (d) Owing to
Answer: (b)
Solution: The sentence shows contrast — rain yet the match continued — so "Despite" (in spite of) fits. "Because of", "Due to" and "Owing to" all express cause, which reverses the meaning.
Source: PYQ-style

83. He speaks English fluently, ______?
(a) isn't he (b) does he (c) is he (d) doesn't he
Answer: (d)
Solution: The statement is positive with the main verb "speaks" (present simple), so the tag is negative with "does": "doesn't he". "Isn't he" wrongly copies the auxiliary "is".
Source: NCERT-based grammar

84. The number of students absent from class ______ increasing every month.
(a) were (b) is (c) are (d) have been
Answer: (b)
Solution: "The number of" takes a singular verb: "is". Contrast with "a number of", which takes a plural verb — a favourite examiner's trap.
Source: PYQ-style

85. By the time we reached the theatre, the film ______.
(a) started (b) has started (c) had started (d) was starting
Answer: (c)
Solution: Two past actions: the earlier one (film starting) takes the past perfect "had started"; the later one takes the simple past "reached".
Source: NCERT-based grammar

86. The principal ordered that the gates ______ locked at nine.
(a) be (b) were (c) are (d) should been
Answer: (a)
Solution: After "ordered that", the subjunctive "be" is used: "that the gates be locked". "Were" is the sound-right trap, and "should been" is grammatically impossible.
Source: PYQ-style

87. Each of the boys ______ given a certificate at the ceremony.
(a) was (b) were (c) have been (d) are
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Each of" is always followed by a plural noun but a singular verb: "was". "Were" tempts because "boys" is plural — an intervening-phrase trap.
Source: PYQ-style

88. I would rather you ______ the truth yesterday.
(a) had told (b) have told (c) tell (d) told
Answer: (a)
Solution: With "would rather + subject", the simple past expresses a present or future wish, but the word "yesterday" points to a past action, so the past perfect "had told" is required. "Told" is the trap — it would be correct only without the past time reference.
Source: PYQ-style

89. The scenery of Kashmir ______ very beautiful.
(a) are (b) were (c) is (d) have been
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Scenery" is an uncountable noun and always takes a singular verb: "is". It has no plural form "sceneries".
Source: NCERT-based grammar

90. No sooner had she finished her speech ______ the audience started clapping.
(a) when (b) then (c) than (d) that
Answer: (c)
Solution: The fixed correlative pair is "No sooner...than". "When" pairs with "Hardly/Scarcely" — a classic pairing trap.
Source: PYQ-style

91. The government has ______ a committee to look into the matter.
(a) set aside (b) set off (c) set up (d) set out
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Set up" means to establish or constitute, which fits "a committee". "Set off" means to begin a journey, "set out" means to start a task, "set aside" means to reserve.
Source: PYQ-style

92. He is senior ______ me by three years.
(a) than (b) from (c) to (d) over
Answer: (c)
Solution: Latin comparatives like senior, junior, superior, inferior and prefer take "to", not "than". "Than" is the most common sound-right trap here.
Source: PYQ-style

93. The accused was ______ of all charges by the court.
(a) punished (b) convicted (c) suspected (d) acquitted
Answer: (d)
Solution: "Acquitted of" means declared not guilty, and it collocates with "of". "Convicted of" would mean the opposite — declared guilty — which does not fit the sense of release in the sentence.
Source: PYQ-style

94. Unless you ______ hard, you will not clear the examination.
(a) work (b) had worked (c) will work (d) worked
Answer: (a)
Solution: In conditional clauses with "unless", the present simple expresses the future: "Unless you work hard". "Will work" is never used in the if/unless clause.
Source: NCERT-based grammar

95. Many a soldier ______ laid down his life for the country.
(a) were (b) have (c) are (d) has
Answer: (d)
Solution: "Many a" is followed by a singular noun and a singular verb: "has". "Have" tempts because the meaning is plural — a sound-right trap.
Source: PYQ-style

96. The two brothers divided the property ______ themselves.
(a) among (b) between (c) within (d) amongst
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Between" is used for two persons; "among/amongst" for more than two. Since there are two brothers, "between" is correct.
Source: NCERT-based grammar

97. Scarcely had he stepped out of the house ______ it began to rain.
(a) than (b) that (c) then (d) when
Answer: (d)
Solution: "Scarcely/Hardly" pairs with "when"; "No sooner" pairs with "than". Mixing the pairs is the examiner's favourite trap.
Source: PYQ-style

98. The minister's speech had a profound ______ on the young audience.
(a) affect (b) effect (c) infect (d) defect
Answer: (b)
Solution: A noun is needed after the article "a": "effect" (noun, meaning influence). "Affect" is a verb — the most frequently confused pair in exams.
Source: PYQ-style

99. Bread and butter ______ his daily breakfast.
(a) are (b) were (c) have been (d) is
Answer: (d)
Solution: When two nouns joined by "and" denote a single combined idea or dish, the verb is singular: "is". "Are" is the trap because two nouns appear joined by "and".
Source: PYQ-style

100. It is high time we ______ serious about climate change.
(a) get (b) will get (c) got (d) have got
Answer: (c)
Solution: "It is high time" is followed by the simple past to express that something is overdue: "we got serious". The present tense "get" is the sound-right trap.
Source: PYQ-style
