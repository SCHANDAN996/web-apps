Fill in the Blanks (Advanced) — Practice Set 2
Questions 26–50 | Easy: 26–40 | Medium: 41–50

26. The committee ______ divided in its opinion on the new policy.
(a) have been (b) were (c) are (d) was
Answer: (d)
Solution: When a collective noun like "committee" is divided in opinion, it usually takes a plural verb, but here the sentence stresses the body as a whole being split on one issue; standard exam usage treats "divided in opinion" with a plural verb only when members act separately. Here the committee as a unit is described, so "was" is correct. "Were" tempts because of "divided," but the subject is singular in form.
Source: PYQ-style

27. Neither of the two boys ______ done his homework.
(a) have (b) are (c) has (d) were
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Neither of" takes a singular verb and singular pronoun ("his"). "Have" is the sound-right trap because "boys" sits just before the verb.
Source: NCERT-based grammar

28. She has been working in this office ______ 2015.
(a) for (b) by (c) from (d) since
Answer: (d)
Solution: "Since" is used with a point of time (2015); "for" is used with a period of time (for nine years). "From" does not pair with the present perfect continuous here.
Source: NCERT Class 9 English

29. The price of petrol ______ risen sharply this year.
(a) has (b) have (c) are (d) were
Answer: (a)
Solution: The subject is "price" (singular), not "petrol." The intervening phrase "of petrol" is the examiner's trap pushing you toward "have."
Source: PYQ-style

30. He is one of the best players who ______ ever played for the country.
(a) has (b) have (c) is (d) was
Answer: (b)
Solution: In "one of the + plural noun + who/that," the relative pronoun refers to the plural noun ("players"), so the verb is plural: "have." "Has" is the classic trap.
Source: PYQ-style

31. I ______ this book twice, so I can lend it to you.
(a) read (b) am reading (c) had read (d) have read
Answer: (d)
Solution: A completed action with present relevance ("so I can lend it") takes the present perfect: "have read." Simple past "read" would need a definite past time.
Source: NCERT Class 10 English

32. The teacher, along with his students, ______ gone to the museum.
(a) have (b) has (c) are (d) were
Answer: (b)
Solution: With "along with," the verb agrees with the first subject ("the teacher"), which is singular. "Have" is the intervening-phrase trap.
Source: PYQ-style

33. You should abide ______ the rules of the institution.
(a) with (b) to (c) by (d) on
Answer: (c)
Solution: The correct preposition with "abide" is "by" — abide by the rules. Other prepositions do not collocate with "abide."
Source: Standard usage (Oxford)

34. Hardly had he reached the station ______ the train left.
(a) then (b) when (c) than (d) that
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Hardly/Scarcely had … when" is the fixed correlative pair. "Than" pairs with "no sooner," not with "hardly" — a favourite examiner trap.
Source: PYQ-style

35. No sooner did she enter the room ______ the phone rang.
(a) when (b) then (c) that (d) than
Answer: (d)
Solution: "No sooner … than" is the correct correlative. "When" is the trap because it pairs with "hardly/scarcely," not with "no sooner."
Source: PYQ-style

36. Each of the students ______ given a certificate.
(a) were (b) have (c) was (d) are
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Each of" takes a singular verb: "was." "Were" is the sound-right trap created by the plural "students" before the verb.
Source: NCERT-based grammar

37. He is senior ______ me by two years.
(a) than (b) to (c) from (d) of
Answer: (b)
Solution: Latin comparatives — senior, junior, superior, inferior, prior — take "to," not "than." "Than" is the most common Hindi-speaker error here.
Source: Standard usage (Oxford)

38. The Ganges ______ one of the longest rivers in India.
(a) are (b) were (c) is (d) have been
Answer: (c)
Solution: Names of rivers, though plural-looking, are singular: "The Ganges is." "Are" is the sound-right trap.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Geography

39. Bread and butter ______ his daily breakfast.
(a) is (b) were (c) are (d) have been
Answer: (a)
Solution: When two nouns joined by "and" denote one combined dish or idea, the verb is singular: "is." "Are" tempts because two nouns appear.
Source: PYQ-style

40. I prefer tea ______ coffee.
(a) than (b) over (c) to (d) from
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Prefer" takes "to": prefer tea to coffee. "Than" is wrong because "prefer" is a Latin comparative in sense.
Source: Standard usage (Oxford)

41. The number of accidents on this highway ______ increasing every year.
(a) is (b) are (c) were (d) have been
Answer: (a)
Solution: "The number of" takes a singular verb; "a number of" takes a plural verb. Examiners love this contrast — "are" is the trap.
Source: PYQ-style

42. Had I known about the meeting earlier, I ______ attended it.
(a) had (b) will have (c) would (d) would have
Answer: (d)
Solution: This is an inverted third conditional ("Had I known" = "If I had known"), which takes "would have + past participle" in the main clause.
Source: NCERT Class 11 English

43. Unless you work hard, you ______ succeed.
(a) will (b) shall (c) would (d) will not
Answer: (d)
Solution: "Unless" already means "if … not," so the main clause needs a negative idea: "you will not succeed." Using "will" alone reverses the meaning — a classic over-thinking trap.
Source: PYQ-style

44. The manager made the workers ______ overtime without extra pay.
(a) work (b) to work (c) working (d) worked
Answer: (a)
Solution: After causative "make" in the active voice, the bare infinitive follows: "made the workers work." "To work" appears only in the passive ("were made to work").
Source: Standard usage (Cambridge)

45. Not only the students but also the teacher ______ present at the function.
(a) was (b) were (c) are (d) have been
Answer: (a)
Solution: With "not only … but also," the verb agrees with the nearer subject ("the teacher"), which is singular: "was." "Were" is the proximity trap in reverse.
Source: PYQ-style

46. He denied ______ the money from the drawer.
(a) taking (b) to take (c) taken (d) take
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Deny" is followed by a gerund, not an infinitive: "denied taking." Verbs like deny, avoid, enjoy, suggest take the -ing form.
Source: Standard usage (Cambridge)

47. By the time we reached the theatre, the film ______.
(a) started (b) has started (c) had started (d) starts
Answer: (c)
Solution: "By the time + past tense" signals that the earlier action must be in the past perfect: "had started." Simple past "started" breaks the sequence of tenses.
Source: NCERT Class 10 English

48. Many a soldier ______ laid down his life for the country.
(a) has (b) have (c) are (d) were
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Many a + singular noun" takes a singular verb and singular pronoun: "has … his." "Have" is the sound-right trap because the phrase means "many."
Source: PYQ-style

49. The scenery of Kashmir ______ very beautiful.
(a) are (b) is (c) were (d) have been
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Scenery" is an uncountable noun and always takes a singular verb: "is." It has no plural form — "sceneries" is wrong.
Source: Standard usage (Oxford)

50. He ______ TV when the lights went off.
(a) watched (b) was watching (c) had watched (d) watches
Answer: (b)
Solution: An ongoing past action interrupted by another past event takes the past continuous: "was watching … when the lights went off." Simple past "watched" loses the ongoing sense.
Source: NCERT Class 9 English
