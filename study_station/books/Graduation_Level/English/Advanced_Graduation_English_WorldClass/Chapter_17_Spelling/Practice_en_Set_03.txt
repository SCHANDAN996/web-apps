51. Choose the correctly spelt word.
(a) Recieve (b) Riceive (c) Receive (d) Receeve
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Receive" follows the rule "i before e except after c" — after 'c' we write 'ei'. "Recieve" reverses the letters and is the most common trap.
Source: NCERT Class 10 English

52. Choose the correctly spelt word.
(a) Occured (b) Ocurred (c) Occurred (d) Occurrd
Answer: (c)
Solution: Verbs ending in a single consonant after a stressed vowel double the consonant: occur → occurred. Both 'c' and 'r' are doubled.
Source: NCERT Class 10 English

53. Choose the correctly spelt word.
(a) Beginning (b) Begining (c) Beginninng (d) Biginning
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Beginning" doubles the 'n' because the stress falls on the final syllable of "begin". "Begining" with one 'n' is wrong.
Source: NCERT Class 9 English

54. Choose the correctly spelt word.
(a) Enviroment (b) Invironment (c) Envirnment (d) Environment
Answer: (d)
Solution: "Environment" contains an 'n' before 'm' (environ + ment). Dropping that 'n' is a frequent exam error.
Source: NCERT Class 10 English

55. Choose the correctly spelt word.
(a) Seperate (b) Separat (c) Saparate (d) Separate
Answer: (d)
Solution: "Separate" has 'a' in the middle — remember "there is A RAT in sepARATe". "Seperate" is the classic trap spelling.
Source: NCERT Class 9 English

56. Choose the correctly spelt word.
(a) Definately (b) Definitly (c) Definitely (d) Defenitely
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Definitely" is built from "finite" — de + finite + ly, so it keeps the 'i' sounds. "Definately" with 'a' is wrong.
Source: NCERT Class 10 English

57. Choose the correctly spelt word.
(a) Grammer (b) Grammmer (c) Gramar (d) Grammar
Answer: (d)
Solution: "Grammar" ends in '-ar', not '-er'. Mnemonic: "grammar has an A at the end, like an A grade".
Source: NCERT Class 9 English

58. Choose the correctly spelt word.
(a) Wensday (b) Wednesday (c) Wednsday (d) Wednessday
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Wednesday" keeps the silent 'd' after 'n' (from Woden's day). The 'd' is not pronounced but must be written.
Source: NCERT Class 8 English

59. Choose the correctly spelt word.
(a) Friend (b) Freind (c) Frend (d) Friand
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Friend" follows "i before e" (no preceding 'c'). Mnemonic: "a friend stays with you till the END — fri-END".
Source: NCERT Class 8 English

60. Choose the correctly spelt word.
(a) Necesary (b) Necessary (c) Neccessary (d) Neccesary
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Necessary" has one 'c' and double 's'. Mnemonic: "a shirt has one Collar and two Sleeves — one C, two S".
Source: NCERT Class 9 English

61. Choose the correctly spelt word.
(a) Accomodation (b) Accommodation (c) Acommodation (d) Accomadation
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Accommodation" doubles both 'c' and 'm' — remember "two Cots and two Mattresses in the accommodation". All other options drop one double letter.
Source: NCERT Class 10 English

62. Choose the correctly spelt word.
(a) Embarassment (b) Embarasment (c) Embarrassment (d) Emberrassment
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Embarrassment" doubles both 'r' and 's'. Mnemonic: "so embarrassed your face goes Really Red and you Stutter and Stammer — 2 R's, 2 S's".
Source: NCERT Class 10 English

63. Choose the correctly spelt word.
(a) Millennium (b) Millenium (c) Milenium (d) Milleniumm
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Millennium" doubles both 'l' and 'n' (from Latin mille = thousand, annus = year — the double 'n' comes from "annus"). "Millenium" with one 'n' is the trap.
Source: Standard English usage (Oxford spelling)

64. Choose the correctly spelt word.
(a) Questionaire (b) Questionnair (c) Questionnaire (d) Questionairre
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Questionnaire" is a French borrowing that keeps the double 'n' and the final '-aire'. Dropping one 'n' is the common error.
Source: Standard English usage (Oxford spelling)

65. Choose the correctly spelt word.
(a) Conscientious (b) Conscientous (c) Consientious (d) Conscentious
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Conscientious" comes from "conscience" + '-ious'; it keeps the 'sci' cluster and the 'i' before 'ous'. "Conscientous" drops that 'i'.
Source: Standard English usage (Oxford spelling)

66. Choose the correctly spelt word.
(a) Pronunciation (b) Pronounciation (c) Pronunciaton (d) Pronunsiation
Answer: (a)
Solution: Examiner's trap: the verb is "pronounce" but the noun is "pronunciation" — the 'ou' becomes 'u'. "Pronounciation" wrongly copies the verb's spelling.
Source: Standard English usage (Oxford spelling)

67. Choose the correctly spelt word.
(a) Maintainance (b) Maintenance (c) Maintenence (d) Mentainance
Answer: (b)
Solution: Examiner's trap: the verb is "maintain" but the noun is "maintenance" — 'ai' changes to 'e' in the second syllable. "Maintainance" wrongly keeps the verb form.
Source: Standard English usage (Oxford spelling)

68. Choose the correctly spelt word.
(a) Perseverance (b) Perseverence (c) Persaverance (d) Persevarance
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Perseverance" ends in '-ance' (from Latin perseverantia). Words from Latin '-antia' take '-ance'; "perseverence" with '-ence' is wrong.
Source: Standard English usage (Oxford spelling)

69. Choose the correctly spelt word.
(a) Existance (b) Existence (c) Existense (d) Exsistence
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Existence" ends in '-ence' (from Latin existentia). A quick check: the related adjective is "existent", which signals '-ence'.
Source: Standard English usage (Oxford spelling)

70. Choose the correctly spelt word.
(a) Ocassion (b) Occassion (c) Occasion (d) Occasionn
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Occasion" doubles the 'c' but keeps a single 's'. "Occassion" doubles both, which is the most frequent wrong version.
Source: Standard English usage (Oxford spelling)

71. Choose the correctly spelt word.
(a) Liaison (b) Liason (c) Liaision (d) Liasonn
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Liaison" is a French borrowing with two 'i's — one before and one after the 'a'. "Liason" drops the first 'i' and is wrong.
Source: Standard English usage (Oxford spelling)

72. Choose the correctly spelt word.
(a) Beaurocracy (b) Bureaucracy (c) Burocracy (d) Bureacracy
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Bureaucracy" keeps the French spelling of "bureau" (b-u-r-e-a-u) plus '-cracy'. "Beaurocracy" scrambles the vowel order.
Source: Standard English usage (Oxford spelling)

73. Choose the correctly spelt word.
(a) Entreprenur (b) Entreprenure (c) Entrapreneur (d) Entrepreneur
Answer: (d)
Solution: "Entrepreneur" is a French borrowing: entre + preneur. It ends in '-eur', not '-ur', and has 'e' (not 'a') in the middle syllable.
Source: Standard English usage (Oxford spelling)

74. Choose the correctly spelt word.
(a) Supercede (b) Supersade (c) Superseed (d) Supersede
Answer: (d)
Solution: "Supersede" is the only common English word ending in '-sede' (from Latin sedere = to sit). Words like "precede" and "concede" end in '-cede', which makes "supercede" the trap.
Source: Standard English usage (Oxford spelling)

75. Choose the correctly spelt word.
(a) Indispensible (b) Indispansable (c) Indespensable (d) Indispensable
Answer: (d)
Solution: "Indispensable" ends in '-able' because it comes from the verb "dispense" plus the prefix 'in-' and suffix '-able' (able to be dispensed with). The '-ible' version is the trap.
Source: Standard English usage (Oxford spelling)
