# 🪄 Short Tricks + Skip Strategy — Spelling Check

---

## 🧠 Mnemonic Boxes (Memorise These!)

**Box 1 — "I before E"**
> **"I before E, except after C — or when sounding like 'A' as in 'neighbour' and 'weigh'."**
> ✅ believe, receive, deceive, weigh
> ⚠️ Exceptions: weird, height, seize, foreign, leisure

**Box 2 — Full → -ful (One L Only)**
> **"When FULL joins a word, it loses one L."**
> ✅ beautiful, helpful, grateful, wonderful
> ❌ beautifull, gratefull

**Box 3 — The Big Three Doublers**
> **"Big, hot, run — short word, short vowel, one syllable → double the last letter."**
> ✅ big → bigger | hot → hottest | run → running | swim → swimming

**Box 4 — Silent E Rule**
> **"Silent E runs away when a vowel comes to play."**
> ✅ make → making | hope → hoping | write → writing
> BUT keeps E before consonants: hope → hopeful, care → careful

**Box 5 — "A RAT in Separate"**
> **"There's A RAT in sep-A-RAT-e."**
> ✅ separate ❌ seperate

**Box 6 — "Necessary: One Collar, Two Sleeves"**
> **"A shirt has one Collar (C) and two Sleeves (SS)."**
> ✅ necessary ❌ neccessary / necesary

**Box 7 — "Accommodate: Two Cars, Two Motorbikes"**
> **"The hotel accommodates 2 Cars (CC) and 2 Motorbikes (MM)."**
> ✅ accommodate ❌ accomodate / acommodate

**Box 8 — "Stationery vs Stationary"**
> **"StationEry = Envelopes (paper) | StationAry = cAr parked (not moving)."**

**Box 9 — "Principal vs Principle"**
> **"The princiPAL is your PAL (person) | A principLE is a ruLE."**

**Box 10 — "-ceed vs -cede"**
> **"Only 3 words end in -CEED: succeed, proceed, exceed.**
> **Only ONE ends in -SEDE: supersede.**
> **All others take -CEDE: precede, recede, concede, intercede."**

**Box 11 — "Embarrass: Really Red, Super Shy"**
> **"When embarrassed, you go Really Red (RR) and feel Super Shy (SS)."**
> ✅ embarrass ❌ embarass / emmbarass

**Box 12 — "Occurrence: 2 Cops, 2 Robbers"**
> **"An occurrence has 2 C's (cops) chasing 2 R's (robbers)."**
> ✅ occurrence, occurred, occurring

**Box 13 — "Definite: Finite Inside"**
> **"DeFINITE has FINITE in it — no A anywhere."**
> ✅ definite, definitely ❌ definately

**Box 14 — "Privilege: No D"**
> **"It's a PRIVILEGE to have no D."**
> ✅ privilege ❌ priviledge

**Box 15 — "Questionnaire: Double N"**
> **"A questionnaire asks so many questions it needs two N's."**
> ✅ questionnaire ❌ questionaire

## ⚡ Speed Tricks for the Exam Hall

**Trick 1 — The Syllable Clap Method**
Break the word into syllables mentally: *oc-cur-rence*, *ac-com-mo-date*. Doubled letters almost always sit at syllable joints. If you can't hear where it doubles, suspect the middle of the word.

**Trick 2 — The Family Test**
Can't spell "definite"? Think of its family: *define, definition, finite*. The root spelling never changes. Same trick: *sign → signature* (the G is silent in "sign" but alive in "signature").

**Trick 3 — The Vowel-Check for -able / -ible**
> Root word is complete on its own → **-able** (comfort → comfortable, read → readable)
> Root is NOT a complete word → **-ible** (visible, possible, terrible)
> ⚠️ Exceptions exist (accessible, flexible), but this works for most cases.

**Trick 4 — -ent vs -ant / -ence vs -ance**
> If the related noun ends in **-ation**, use **-ant/-ance**: tolerate → tolerant, tolerance.
> If the verb ends in **-ere/-er**, the adjective usually takes **-ent** and the noun **-ence**: differ → different, difference; prefer → preferent forms give preference.
> When in doubt, check the noun form you already know — the vowel stays consistent within the word family.

**Trick 5 — British vs American Alert**
SSC/Bank exams follow **British spelling**: colour, honour, centre, programme, travelled (double L). If two options differ only this way, pick the British form.

## ⏭️ Skip Strategy (When to Leave the Question)

**Skip Tip 1 — The "Two Look-Alikes" Trap**
If two options differ by only one letter (e.g., *occurence / occurrence*) and you genuinely cannot recall the rule, **don't burn 45 seconds guessing between them**. Mark it, move on, return only if time remains. A blind 50-50 guess with negative marking is a bad trade.

**Skip Tip 2 — The Unfamiliar Word**
If all four options are words you've never seen (rare in exams like CHSL/MTS, more common in CGL Tier-2 or IBPS), skip immediately. Spelling cannot be "worked out" for unknown words — it's pure recall. Save those 30 seconds for a grammar question you *can* reason through.

**Skip Tip 3 — Don't Over-Correct**
If your first instinct says a word is correct, and you start "seeing" errors after staring for 20 seconds, **trust the first instinct**. Over-thinking flips more right answers to wrong than any other trap in spelling questions. First read → decide → move.

## 🎯 15/45-Second Rule for Spelling

| Type | Time | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Fact-based (15 sec)** — common words, one rule applies | Scan & click | separate, necessary, -ful words, i-before-e |
| **Inference-based (45 sec)** — unfamiliar words, syllable analysis needed | Apply Family Test / Syllable Clap, else skip | questionnaire, reconnaissance, bureaucracy |

> **Golden Rule:** Spelling questions are the fastest marks in the paper — 15 seconds each. If you're crossing 30 seconds, you're in Skip territory.
