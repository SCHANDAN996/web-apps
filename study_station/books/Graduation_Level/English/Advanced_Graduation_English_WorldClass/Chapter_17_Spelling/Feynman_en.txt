🧒 Feynman Technique — Explaining Spelling Check to a 12-Year-Old

Imagine English words are like LEGO blocks. Each word is built from small pieces — a root (the main block), a prefix (a block stuck at the front), and a suffix (a block stuck at the back). If you know the pieces, you can build — and spell — almost any word, even one you have never seen before.

Take the word "unbelievable". Break it: un- (not) + believe + -able (can be). So it means "cannot be believed". Now here is the magic: once you know "believe", you can also spell believer, believing, belief — because the pieces follow fixed rules, like LEGO studs that only fit one way.

🎯 The Hardest Concept Made Simple: "i before e, except after c"

This is the rule that tricks most students. Think of it like a queue at a water tap:

- Normally, **i** stands before **e**: believe, achieve, relief, field.
- But when **c** comes just before them, **c** is the "big brother" who pushes **e** forward: receive, deceive, perceive, ceiling.
- Exception twist: when the sound is a long "ay" (like in "say"), **e** comes first: neighbour, weigh, vein.

So the full rhyme is: **"i before e, except after c, or when sounding like 'ay' as in neighbour and weigh."**

🧠 A Daily-Life Example

Suppose your friend writes "recieve" in a WhatsApp message. Ask yourself: is there a **c** before the pair? Yes! So big brother **c** pushes **e** forward → **receive**. One question, one second, correct spelling. That is exactly what you do in the exam.

🔑 The Unforgettable Trick: "Silent Letters Are Shy Guests"

Some letters come to the party but never speak — the **k** in knife, the **b** in doubt, the **w** in write, the **h** in honest. Trick: imagine these letters are shy guests hiding behind the door. They are IN the room (the spelling), but you never hear them. When in doubt, ask: "Is there a shy guest hiding here?" — doubt (b), subtle (b), psychology (p), rhythm (h).

Another one for double letters — learn each word's own pair of twins:

- **accommodate** = double **c**, double **m** ("two Cots and two Mattresses for every guest").
- **committee** = double **m**, double **t**, double **e** ("Many Members Talk Endlessly").
- **occasion** = double **c**, single **s** (its cousin "occurrence" is the opposite: double **r**, single **c** at the start — oc-cur-rence).
- **embarrass** = double **r**, double **s** ("Really Sad" to be embarrassed!).

⚠️ Why Students Lose Marks Here

In the exam, three options look almost right and one is correct — or three are correct and one is wrong. The examiner changes just ONE letter: "seperate" instead of "separate", "occurence" instead of "occurrence". Your eyes slide past it because your brain auto-corrects, just like WhatsApp. The fix: read each option letter by letter, not word by word. Slow eyes, fast marks.
