Card 1
Front: Which is correct — "accomodate" or "accommodate"?
Back: Accommodate. Remember: it has double C AND double M — "two Cars and two Motorbikes can be accommodated in the garage."

Card 2
Front: Mnemonic — How do you remember the spelling of "necessary"?
Back: "One Collar, two Sleeves" — one C, double S: neCeSSary.

Card 3
Front: Which is correct — "occurence", "occurance", or "occurrence"?
Back: Occurrence. Double C, double R, and -ence ending. Trap: examiners often offer "occurence" (single R) as the tempting wrong option.

Card 4
Front: What is the rule for adding -ing to words like "run", "sit", "begin"?
Back: Single vowel + single consonant after a stressed syllable → double the consonant: running, sitting, beginning. But "open → opening" (stress on first syllable, no doubling).

Card 5
Front: Examiner's Trap — "The committee has submitted its report." Is "committee" spelled correctly?
Back: Yes — committee has double M, double T, double E. Trap options usually drop one letter: "commitee" or "comittee". Count the doubles: M-M, T-T, E-E.

Card 6
Front: Which is correct — "definately" or "definitely"?
Back: Definitely. It comes from "finite" — de-FINITE-ly. If you can spell "finite", you can never write "definately".

Card 7
Front: What happens to the silent 'e' when adding a vowel suffix? (e.g., hope + ing)
Back: Drop the silent 'e' before a vowel suffix: hope → hoping, make → making. Keep it before a consonant suffix: hope → hopeful, sure → surely.

Card 8
Front: Mnemonic — How do you remember "embarrass"?
Back: "I go Really Red when my face turns Scarlet Scarlet" — double R, double S: embaRRaSS.

Card 9
Front: Which is correct — "seperate" or "separate"?
Back: Separate. "There's A RAT in sep-A-RAT-e." The middle vowel is A, not E.

Card 10
Front: Examiner's Trap — "liason", "liaision", or "liaison"?
Back: Liaison. It has two I's flanking the A: l-i-A-i-s-o-n. Examiners exploit its French origin by offering "liason" (missing the second I).

Card 11
Front: What is the 'i before e' rule and its main exceptions?
Back: "I before E, except after C" — believe, chief, but receive, ceiling. Exceptions: weird, seize, height, their, foreign.

Card 12
Front: Which is correct — "maintainance" or "maintenance"?
Back: Maintenance. Trap: the verb is "maintain" but the noun drops the second 'i' — main-te-nance, not main-tai-nance.

Card 13
Front: How do you spell the adjective from "beauty" and the noun from "true"?
Back: Beautiful (y → i before -ful, and -ful has one L); truly (drop the silent 'e' — an exception to the keep-e rule).

Card 14
Front: Which is correct — "questionaire", "questionnare", or "questionnaire"?
Back: Questionnaire. Double N plus the French -aire ending: question + n + aire.

Card 15
Front: What is the plural spelling rule for words ending in consonant + y? Give two examples.
Back: Change y → i and add -es: baby → babies, city → cities. But vowel + y just adds -s: boy → boys, key → keys.

Card 16
Front: Which is correct — "existance" or "existence"?
Back: Existence. Think of the root "exist" + -ence. Words ending in -ent take -ence: existent → existence, persistent → persistence.

Card 17
Front: Mnemonic — How do you remember "rhythm"?
Back: "Rhythm Helps Your Two Hips Move" — R-H-Y-T-H-M, six letters, no vowel except Y.

Card 18
Front: Examiner's Trap — "The government has launched a new scheme." Which spelling of the pair is right: "goverment" or "government"?
Back: Government — the N before -ment is silent in speech but must be written. Sound-right trap: we pronounce it "gover-ment", so examiners test the missing N.

Card 19
Front: Which is correct — "millenium" or "millennium"?
Back: Millennium. Double L AND double N — from Latin mille (thousand) + annus (year); the double N comes from "annual".

Card 20
Front: Which is correct — "publically" or "publicly"?
Back: Publicly. Exception to the -ally rule: adjectives ending in -ic usually take -ally (basically, tragically), but public → publicly.
