# 📋 Important Rules & Tips — Spelling Check

## (a) 12 High-Yield Rules

| # | Rule | Example | Examiner's Trap |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **"i before e, except after c"** — but only when the sound is /ee/. | bel**ie**ve, rec**ei**ve, but: w**ei**gh, n**ei**ghbour (sound /ay/) | "receive" vs "recieve"; also exceptions: seize, weird, height |
| 2 | Silent **'e' is dropped** before a vowel suffix, kept before a consonant suffix. | hope → hoping; hope → hopeful | "hopeing" ❌; but "noticeable" keeps 'e' (soft c/g) |
| 3 | **Double the final consonant** when a one-syllable word (or stressed final syllable) ends CVC before -ing/-ed. | run → running; begin → beginning | "begining" ❌; but "open → opening" (stress on first syllable) |
| 4 | **-ful has only one 'l'** (but full has two). | beautiful, grateful, successful | "beautifull" ❌; "gratefull" ❌ |
| 5 | Words ending in **-ceed / -cede / -sede**: only **supersede** takes -sede; only exceed, proceed, succeed take -ceed. | exceed, proceed, succeed, supersede | "supercede" ❌ — the single most-tested trap |
| 6 | **-able vs -ible**: full root word + -able (comfortable); Latin root + -ible (visible, possible). | acceptable, responsible | "responsable" ❌; "visable" ❌ |
| 7 | **-ance/-ence** pairs must be memorised; no shortcut rule. | appearance, existence, occurrence | "existance" ❌; "occurence" ❌ (also needs double r) |
| 8 | **Double letters in common exam words**: accommodate (cc, mm), committee (mm, tt, ee), embarrass (rr, ss), occurrence (cc, rr). | accommodate, embarrass | "accomodate" ❌ — the classic SSC favourite |
| 9 | **-ary vs -ery**: most words take -ary; -ery is a smaller set (stationery = paper). | stationery (paper) vs stationary (still) | "stationary shop" ❌ when meaning paper goods |
| 10 | **-ise vs -ize**: both correct in British English; exams accept standard British spelling — be consistent. | organise/organize | "recognice" ❌ — never -ice in verbs |
| 11 | **-eous / -ious / -uous**: adjective endings from Latin; memorise the common set. | courageous, religious, continuous | "couragous" ❌ (keeps the 'e' for soft g) |
| 12 | **Silent letters**: b (doubt, subtle), g (foreign, sign), k (knight), p (psychology, receipt), w (answer, sword). | doubt, psychology, receipt | "receit" ❌; "sychology" ❌ |

## (b) Time Management — 15/45-Second Rule

| Type | What it looks like | Time | Strategy |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Fact-based (15 sec)** | "Choose the correctly spelt word" — 4 similar-looking options; "Find the misspelt word" among common words | 15 sec | Trust your first visual read. Sound the word mentally; check double letters and -able/-ible. If two options look right, pick the one matching a rule above and move on. |
| **Inference-based (45 sec)** | Confusing pairs in context (stationery/stationary, principal/principle); rarely-seen words (questionnaire, millennium, bureaucracy); sentence-embedded spelling errors | 45 sec | Read the full sentence for context clues. Break the word into root + affix. Eliminate options violating Rules 1–12, then decide. |

**Golden advice:** Never spend more than 45 seconds on any spelling question. Mark, skip, return if time permits.

## (c) Morphology — Root + Affix Analysis

Breaking a word into parts is the fastest way to verify its spelling.

| Root (origin) | Meaning | Affix | Derived Word | Spelling Checkpoint |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **scrib/script** (Latin) | write | -tion, -scribe | description, prescribe | "discription" ❌ — root is *scrib*, not *scib* |
| **cede/ceed** (Latin) | go, yield | pro-, suc-, super- | proceed, succeed, supersede | Rule 5 applies |
| **mit/miss** (Latin) | send | com-, ad-, per- | committee, admit, permission | committee = com + mit + ee → double m, double t |
| **port** (Latin) | carry | sup-, trans- | support, transport | "suport" ❌ |
| **spect** (Latin) | look | in-, re-, pro- | inspect, respect, prospect | "prospet" ❌ |
| **graph** (Greek) | write | auto-, photo- | autograph, photograph | "photgraph" ❌ |
| **phon** (Greek) | sound | tele-, sym- | telephone, symphony | "sympony" ❌ |
| **chron** (Greek) | time | syn-, -ology | synchronize, chronology | "sychronize" ❌ |

**Morphology habit:** When in doubt, strip the word to its root. If the root is spelt correctly in a simpler word you know (describe → description), the longer word follows the same spelling logic.
