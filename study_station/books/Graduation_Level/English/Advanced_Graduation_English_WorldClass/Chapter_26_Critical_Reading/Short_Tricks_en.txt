# 🪄 Short Tricks + Skip Strategy — Critical Reading

## Mnemonic Boxes

**📦 TRICK 1 — "F.I.R.S.T." for Reading a Passage**
- **F**irst & last lines of each paragraph (thesis lives here)
- **I**dentify the tone (positive / negative / neutral)
- **R**ead the question BEFORE the options
- **S**can for keywords, don't re-read everything
- **T**one + Title = answer to 2 free questions

**📦 TRICK 2 — "D.E.T.E.C.T." — The Detective Approach for Inference**
- **D**on't assume — use only passage evidence
- **E**liminate extreme words (always, never, completely)
- **T**race the clue line in the passage
- **E**xamine what is implied, not stated
- **C**heck: "Would the author agree?"
- **T**ick the mildest supported option

**📦 TRICK 3 — "Extreme = Exit"**
Options containing *always, never, all, none, only, completely, impossible* are wrong 9 times out of 10 in inference questions. Mild words (*may, might, suggests, tends to, often*) survive.

**📦 TRICK 4 — "TONE MAP" — 3-Zone Tone Classifier**
- 🟢 **Positive**: optimistic, appreciative, laudatory, hopeful
- 🟡 **Neutral**: analytical, objective, descriptive, factual
- 🔴 **Negative**: critical, sceptical, pessimistic, sarcastic
First classify the zone, then pick the exact word. Half the options die instantly.

**📦 TRICK 5 — "Sarcasm Smells Sweet"**
If the author uses *praise words* in a *problem paragraph* ("What a brilliant way to waste taxpayer money!"), the tone is **sarcastic/critical**, not appreciative. Sweet words + bitter context = sarcasm.

**📦 TRICK 6 — "Title = Telescope, not Microscope"**
The correct title covers the WHOLE passage. Reject options that:
- name only one paragraph (too narrow), or
- go beyond the passage's scope (too broad).

**📦 TRICK 7 — "E.R.A.S.E." Wrong Options**
- **E**xtreme words
- **R**everse of the passage (opposite meaning)
- **A**dded information (true in real life, absent in passage)
- **S**cope error (too broad / too narrow)
- **E**motion the author never showed

**📦 TRICK 8 — "True ≠ Correct"**
A factually true statement is still WRONG if the passage doesn't say it. The passage is the only universe. Outside knowledge is a trap, not a tool.

**📦 TRICK 9 — "Keyword Boomerang"**
For fact-based questions: pick the unusual word in the question (a name, date, term), throw it back into the passage like a boomerang, read 2 lines above and below where it lands. Answer found — 15 seconds.

**📦 TRICK 10 — "But is Boss"**
After *but, however, yet, although, nevertheless* — the author's REAL opinion begins. Whatever comes before the "but" is a concession the examiner uses to build wrong options.

**📦 TRICK 11 — "V.I.N.E." for Vocabulary-in-Context**
- **V**iew the sentence, not the dictionary
- **I**nsert each option into the blank
- **N**ote which one keeps the sentence's logic
- **E**liminate the most common dictionary meaning if the sentence feels odd — passages love secondary meanings

**📦 TRICK 12 — "Author's Shoes Test"**
Before locking an inference answer, ask: "If I were the author, would I nod at this sentence?" If the author would frown or shrug — reject it.

**📦 TRICK 13 — "P.A.S.S." the Main Idea Question**
- **P**urpose (why did the author write this?)
- **A**rgument (what is being claimed?)
- **S**cope (whole passage, not one para)
- **S**tance (for / against / neutral)

**📦 TRICK 14 — "Half-Right = Fully Wrong"**
Examiner's favourite trap: an option where the first half matches the passage and the second half quietly adds a false claim. Read every option to the LAST word before marking.

**📦 TRICK 15 — "Two-Option Tie? Go Mild."**
Stuck between two options in an inference/tone question? Choose the one with the softer, more qualified language. Passages reward nuance; examiners punish absolutes.

---

## ⏭️ Skip Strategy

**🚫 SKIP 1 — The 90-Second Wall**
If an inference question has consumed 90 seconds and you're still between two options, mark your milder choice, flag it, and MOVE. One stubborn question costs you three easy ones. Return only if time remains.

**🚫 SKIP 2 — Vocabulary-in-Context with Unknown Words**
If you don't know the word AND the context gives no clue, don't burn 60 seconds guessing letter by letter. Eliminate options you know are wrong, take a 2-second call, move on. Vocab questions are binary — you know it or you don't; staring doesn't help.

**🚫 SKIP 3 — The "Double-Negative" Question Last**
Questions like *"Which of the following is NOT true, EXCEPT…"* demand re-verifying every option against the passage. Do them AFTER all direct fact questions in that passage are done — by then you've already re-read the key lines and the answer comes faster.

**✅ Golden Order of Attack:** Fact questions (15 sec each) → Vocab-in-context → Tone/Title → Inference (45 sec each) → Double-negative/EXCEPT questions.
