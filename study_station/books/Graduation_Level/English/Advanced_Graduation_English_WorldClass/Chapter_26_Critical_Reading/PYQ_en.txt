# 📊 PYQ Analysis — Critical Reading

## How Exams Test This Chapter (Pattern Analysis)

Critical Reading questions appear in every major exam — SSC CGL, IBPS PO/Clerk, RRB NTPC, and State PCS prelims — almost always as a passage followed by 3–5 questions. The pattern is remarkably stable:

- **Direct/Fact questions** ("According to the passage…", "Which of the following is stated…") — these reward fast scanning. The answer is a paraphrase of one line in the passage.
- **Inference questions** ("It can be inferred…", "The author implies…") — these reward the Detective Approach: the answer must be *supported* by the passage but is never *stated* in it.
- **Tone/Attitude questions** ("The tone of the passage is…", "The author's attitude towards X is…") — these reward tone mapping: track adjectives and verbs, not opinions you bring from outside.
- **Title/Central Idea questions** — the correct title covers the *whole* passage; trap options cover only one paragraph.
- **Vocabulary-in-context questions** — the word's meaning *as used in the passage*, not its most common dictionary meaning.

**Examiner's favourite traps (in words, not numbers):**

1. **The Almost-Right Statement** — an option that copies the passage's words but flips one detail (changes "some" to "all", "may" to "will", cause to effect).
2. **The Outside-Knowledge Lure** — an option that is true in real life but *not* in the passage. If the passage doesn't say it, it cannot be the answer.
3. **The Extreme Word Trap** — options containing *always, never, completely, only* are usually wrong in inference and tone questions.
4. **The Half-Passage Title** — a title that fits paragraph 1 perfectly but ignores the rest.
5. **The Tone Overreach** — the passage is "cautiously optimistic", but the option says "enthusiastic" or "alarmist". Examiners push the tone one notch too far.

**Exam distribution (qualitative):** SSC CGL and CHSL lean towards fact + vocabulary-in-context questions with short passages. IBPS PO/Clerk and SBI PO lean heavily on inference, tone, and assumption questions with longer, abstract passages (economy, technology, ethics). RRB NTPC keeps passages short and factual. State PCS prelims mix fact and inference with statement-based options.

## Top 10 High-Yield Questions (PYQ-style) with Solutions

**Passage (for Q1–Q3):**
"Artificial intelligence systems now screen job applications in many large firms. Proponents argue that algorithms remove human prejudice from hiring. Yet algorithms learn from historical data — and history is rarely neutral. When a company has historically hired more men than women for technical roles, an algorithm trained on that record may quietly learn to prefer male candidates, encoding yesterday's bias into tomorrow's decisions. The solution is not to abandon these tools but to audit them regularly, much as we audit financial accounts."

**Q1.** According to the passage, why might an AI hiring tool prefer male candidates?
(a) Because it is programmed to discriminate against women
(b) Because it learns patterns from historically biased hiring data
(c) Because men perform better in technical roles
(d) Because companies instruct it to favour men

**Answer:** (b)
**Solution:** The passage says the algorithm "learns from historical data" and may "quietly learn to prefer male candidates" when past hiring favoured men. (a) and (d) add intent the passage never mentions; (c) is an outside-knowledge lure.
**Trap type:** Outside-Knowledge Lure
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q2.** It can be inferred from the passage that the author believes AI hiring tools should be
(a) banned until they are perfect
(b) trusted more than human recruiters
(c) used, but checked periodically for bias
(d) replaced by traditional interviews

**Answer:** (c)
**Solution:** The last line says the solution is "not to abandon these tools but to audit them regularly" — use plus periodic checking. (a) and (d) contradict "not to abandon"; (b) is never claimed.
**Trap type:** Almost-Right Statement
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q3.** The author's tone towards AI in hiring is best described as
(a) celebratory (b) dismissive (c) cautiously balanced (d) alarmist

**Answer:** (c)
**Solution:** The author acknowledges the promise ("remove human prejudice"), the risk ("encoding yesterday's bias"), and a middle path ("audit them regularly") — balanced, with caution. (a) and (d) are tone overreach; (b) ignores the praise.
**Trap type:** Tone Overreach
**Source:** PYQ-style

---

**Q4.** In the sentence "The committee *deferred* the decision until the audit report arrived", the word "deferred" most nearly means
(a) cancelled (b) postponed (c) challenged (d) accelerated

**Answer:** (b)
**Solution:** "Until the report arrived" signals the decision was delayed, not cancelled — so *deferred* = postponed. (a) is the classic near-miss; (d) is the opposite.
**Trap type:** Almost-Right Statement (vocabulary-in-context)
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q5.** A passage discusses how electric vehicles reduce tailpipe emissions but notes that battery production and coal-based electricity still carry environmental costs, concluding that EVs are "a step forward, not the destination." The best title is:
(a) "Electric Vehicles: The Complete Solution to Pollution"
(b) "Why Electric Vehicles Will Fail"
(c) "Electric Vehicles: Progress with Caveats"
(d) "The History of the Automobile"

**Answer:** (c)
**Solution:** The passage balances benefit and limitation — "a step forward, not the destination" — so (c) covers the whole passage. (a) and (b) each capture only one side; (d) is far too broad.
**Trap type:** Half-Passage Title
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q6.** "Unlike its predecessor, the new policy is *sparing* in its promises, committing funds only to projects with proven outcomes." The word "sparing" implies the policy is
(a) generous (b) restrained (c) confusing (d) expensive

**Answer:** (b)
**Solution:** "Committing funds only to projects with proven outcomes" shows restraint. *Sparing* = economical, restrained. (a) is the antonym trap.
**Trap type:** Sound-Right (familiar word, unfamiliar sense)
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q7.** Passage gist: "Remote work cut commuting time and office costs, yet surveys show junior employees report weaker mentorship and slower skill growth." Which statement would the author most likely agree with?
(a) Remote work should be abolished
(b) Remote work has both benefits and hidden costs
(c) Senior employees suffer most from remote work
(d) Commuting is the biggest problem in modern jobs

**Answer:** (b)
**Solution:** The passage explicitly balances a benefit (time, cost) against a cost (mentorship). (a) is extreme; (c) inverts the passage (juniors suffer); (d) is an outside-knowledge lure.
**Trap type:** Extreme Word Trap
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q8.** "The minister's speech was *unequivocal*: the reforms would proceed regardless of opposition." "Unequivocal" means
(a) ambiguous (b) clear and unwavering (c) lengthy (d) persuasive

**Answer:** (b)
**Solution:** The colon explains the word — "regardless of opposition" signals firmness and clarity. *Unequivocal* = leaving no doubt. (a) is the antonym trap; (d) is a sound-right distractor.
**Trap type:** Sound-Right
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q9.** A passage on groundwater states: "Punjab's water table falls by roughly half a metre each year in several districts, driven by paddy cultivation that free electricity makes cheap to irrigate." It can be inferred that
(a) paddy is Punjab's only crop
(b) free electricity contributes indirectly to groundwater depletion
(c) the water table falls equally in all districts
(d) banning paddy will instantly solve the crisis

**Answer:** (b)
**Solution:** The passage links free electricity → cheap irrigation → over-extraction, so the inference is the *indirect* contribution. (a) and (c) use extreme words ("only", "all") the passage avoids; (d) overreaches ("instantly").
**Trap type:** Extreme Word Trap
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q10.** "The report's findings were *tentative*, based on a sample of just two hundred households." The author uses "tentative" to suggest the findings are
(a) conclusive (b) fabricated (c) provisional (d) irrelevant

**Answer:** (c)
**Solution:** A small sample signals the findings are provisional, not final. (a) is the antonym; (b) and (d) are harsher than the passage warrants — a tone overreach.
**Trap type:** Tone Overreach
**Source:** PYQ-style

## Applying the 15/45-Second Rule

**Q4 (15-second Fact/Vocabulary question):** The clue "until the audit report arrived" sits in the same sentence as the target word. Do not re-read the passage; match the clue to "postponed", mark (b), move on. Target: **15 seconds**.

**Q2 (45-second Inference question):** Budget the time: (1) 10 sec — locate the author's recommendation (last line). (2) 15 sec — eliminate (a) and (d), which contradict "not to abandon". (3) 15 sec — eliminate (b), which is never claimed. (4) 5 sec — confirm (c) paraphrases "audit them regularly". Target: **45 seconds**. Never spend 45 seconds on a fact question, and never rush an inference question in 15 — that mismatch is where marks are lost.
