Practice Set 2 — Critical Reading (Questions 26–50)
Easy: 26–40 | Medium: 41–50

26. Read the sentence: "The committee has submitted its report." The word "its" refers to:
(a) The members (b) The committee (c) The report (d) The government
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Its" is a singular possessive pronoun, and a collective noun like "committee" acting as one unit takes a singular pronoun. "The report" is tempting because it is nearest, but a pronoun refers to its subject, not the nearest noun. So the answer is the committee.
Source: NCERT Class 10 English Grammar

27. In a passage, the author's "tone" means:
(a) The speed of writing (b) The number of facts given (c) The length of the passage (d) The author's attitude toward the subject
Answer: (d)
Solution: Tone is the writer's attitude — critical, optimistic, neutral, sarcastic — conveyed through word choice. Speed, length, and fact-count are structural features, not tone. So the answer is the author's attitude toward the subject.
Source: PYQ-style

28. Choose the word closest in meaning to "candid":
(a) Secretive (b) Frank (c) Rude (d) Clever
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Candid" comes from Latin candidus (white, pure) and means open and honest. "Rude" is a trap because frankness can feel rude, but rudeness is not the meaning. So the answer is frank.
Source: NCERT Class 9 English

29. A fact-based question in a comprehension passage is one that:
(a) Asks what the author implies (b) Asks for the title (c) Asks what is directly stated in the passage (d) Asks for the author's opinion
Answer: (c)
Solution: Fact-based (explicit) questions are answered by locating a line in the passage; implied meanings, titles, and opinions need inference. So the answer is what is directly stated in the passage.
Source: PYQ-style

30. Choose the correctly spelt word:
(a) Occurrence (b) Occurence (c) Ocurrence (d) Occurrance
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Occurrence" doubles both the c and the r (occur + ence). Single-r spellings are the common trap. So the answer is occurrence.
Source: NCERT Class 10 English

31. "The data shows that rainfall has declined." Here "data" is treated as:
(a) Singular (b) Plural (c) Uncountable with plural verb (d) A collective noun
Answer: (a)
Solution: In modern usage, and in most exam keys, "data" with "shows" is treated as a singular mass noun. Though "data" is historically the plural of "datum", contemporary standard English accepts the singular verb. So the answer is singular.
Source: PYQ-style

32. An inference question usually contains the phrase:
(a) "The author suggests that…" (b) "According to the first line…" (c) "As stated in the passage…" (d) "The passage says…"
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Suggests", "implies", and "can be inferred" signal inference questions; "according to" and "as stated" signal direct fact questions. So the answer is "The author suggests that…".
Source: PYQ-style

33. Choose the word opposite in meaning to "transparent":
(a) Clear (b) Opaque (c) Fragile (d) Obvious
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Transparent" means allowing light through or open; its antonym is "opaque". "Obvious" is a synonym-trap, not an antonym. So the answer is opaque.
Source: NCERT Class 9 English

34. In the Detective Approach to inference, the first step is to:
(a) Find textual clues that support the conclusion (b) Guess the answer from general knowledge (c) Read the last paragraph only (d) Pick the longest option
Answer: (a)
Solution: Inference must be anchored in evidence from the passage, like a detective using clues; outside knowledge and option length are classic traps. So the answer is finding textual clues that support the conclusion.
Source: PYQ-style

35. Spot the error: "Neither of the two boys (P) / have done (Q) / his homework. (R)"
(a) Part P (b) No error (c) Part R (d) Part Q
Answer: (d)
Solution: "Neither of" takes a singular verb, so Part Q should read "has done". The plural-sounding "boys" nearby is the intervening-phrase trap. So the error is in Part Q.
Source: NCERT Class 10 English Grammar

36. The main purpose of a "summary table" while revising a passage is to:
(a) Compress key ideas for quick recall (b) Memorise every sentence (c) Count the paragraphs (d) Translate the passage
Answer: (a)
Solution: A summary table condenses main ideas, tone, and structure so revision is fast; memorising sentences or translating is neither required nor efficient. So the answer is compressing key ideas for quick recall.
Source: PYQ-style

37. Choose the correct passive form: "She wrote a letter."
(a) A letter is written by her. (b) A letter had written by her. (c) A letter has been written by her. (d) A letter was written by her.
Answer: (d)
Solution: Simple past active becomes simple past passive: was/were + past participle. "Is written" wrongly shifts the tense to present. So the answer is "A letter was written by her."
Source: NCERT Class 10 English Grammar

38. A passage that "presents both sides of an argument without favouring either" has a tone that is:
(a) Biased (b) Objective (c) Sarcastic (d) Emotional
Answer: (b)
Solution: Balanced presentation of both sides signals an objective or neutral tone. "Biased" is the tempting opposite trap. So the answer is objective.
Source: PYQ-style

39. Choose the correctly punctuated indirect speech: He said, "I am tired."
(a) He said that he is tired. (b) He said that he had tired. (c) He said that he was tired. (d) He said that he tired.
Answer: (c)
Solution: In reported speech with a past reporting verb, present tense shifts to past: "am" becomes "was". Keeping "is" is the common backshift trap. So the answer is "He said that he was tired."
Source: NCERT Class 10 English Grammar

40. The root "spect" (as in inspect, spectator) means:
(a) To hear (b) To look (c) To speak (d) To write
Answer: (b)
Solution: Latin spectare means "to look" — inspect (look into), spectator (one who looks), respect (look back at). So the answer is to look.
Source: NCERT Class 9 English

41. Read the line: "While the policy promises growth, its benefits remain confined to urban centres." The author's attitude toward the policy is best described as:
(a) Fully supportive (b) Completely dismissive (c) Cautiously critical (d) Indifferent
Answer: (c)
Solution: The author acknowledges the promise of growth but points out a limitation — partial praise plus criticism equals cautiously critical. "Completely dismissive" is the over-statement trap; the author does not reject the policy outright. So the answer is cautiously critical.
Source: PYQ-style

42. Passage line: "Renewable energy capacity doubled in the decade, yet coal still supplied over half of the nation's power." It can be inferred that:
(a) Renewable growth alone has not eliminated dependence on coal (b) Coal has been completely replaced (c) Renewable energy failed to grow (d) The nation uses no renewable energy
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Doubled" shows growth, but "yet coal still supplied over half" shows continued dependence — the inference combines both clues. "Coal has been completely replaced" contradicts the line and is the extreme-option trap. So the answer is that renewable growth alone has not eliminated dependence on coal.
Source: PYQ-style

43. Spot the error: "The quality of the mangoes (P) / in the basket (Q) / were not good. (R)"
(a) Part P (b) Part Q (c) Part R (d) No error
Answer: (c)
Solution: The subject is "quality" (singular), not "mangoes", so Part R should read "was not good". The plural noun "mangoes" sitting just before the verb is the intervening-phrase trap. So the error is in Part R.
Source: NCERT Class 10 English Grammar

44. In a passage about artificial intelligence, the author writes: "AI is a powerful servant but a dangerous master." The figure of speech used is:
(a) Simile (b) Alliteration (c) Hyperbole (d) Metaphor
Answer: (d)
Solution: AI is directly called a "servant" and a "master" without "like" or "as", which makes it a metaphor. "Simile" is the trap because the idea is comparative, but a simile needs an explicit comparison word. So the answer is metaphor.
Source: NCERT Class 10 English

45. Passage line: "The government claims the scheme reached every village; however, independent surveys tell a different story." The phrase "tell a different story" implies that the surveys:
(a) Contradict the government's claim (b) Confirm the government's claim (c) Are about storytelling (d) Are unrelated to the scheme
Answer: (a)
Solution: "However" signals contrast, so the surveys must disagree with the official claim. "Confirm" is the trap for readers who skip the contrast marker. So the answer is that the surveys contradict the government's claim.
Source: PYQ-style

46. Choose the option that best captures the central idea of this line: "Technology has made communication instant, but conversations shallower."
(a) Technology only harms communication (b) Conversations were never deep (c) Instant communication has come at the cost of depth (d) Technology should be banned
Answer: (c)
Solution: The line balances a gain (instant) against a loss (shallower); the central idea must hold both. "Technology only harms communication" is the one-sided extreme trap. So the answer is that instant communication has come at the cost of depth.
Source: PYQ-style

47. Spot the error: "One of my friend (P) / who lives in Delhi (Q) / has won a prize. (R)"
(a) Part Q (b) Part P (c) Part R (d) No error
Answer: (b)
Solution: "One of" must be followed by a plural noun, so Part P should read "one of my friends". The singular verb "has" in Part R is actually correct, which tempts candidates to mark it. So the error is in Part P.
Source: NCERT Class 10 English Grammar

48. A question asking "Which of the following would the author most likely agree with?" is a:
(a) Fact-based question, solvable in about 15 seconds (b) Vocabulary question (c) Inference-based question, needing about 45 seconds (d) Grammar question
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Most likely agree with" requires judging the author's unstated view from the whole passage, which is inference work and deserves the 45-second budget, not the 15-second fact budget. So the answer is an inference-based question needing about 45 seconds.
Source: PYQ-style

49. Passage line: "The new tax, though unpopular at first, gradually won acceptance as its benefits became visible." The word "though" here functions to:
(a) Add a similar idea (b) Give an example (c) State a cause (d) Introduce a concession or contrast
Answer: (d)
Solution: "Though" concedes a point (unpopular) before the main clause reverses it (won acceptance). Reading it as a cause is the connector-confusion trap. So the answer is introducing a concession or contrast.
Source: NCERT Class 10 English

50. Read the lines: "City planners celebrated the new flyover. Residents below it, however, spoke of dust, noise, and lost sunlight." The author juxtaposes these two views primarily to:
(a) Praise the city planners (b) Describe the weather in the city (c) Prove flyovers are useless (d) Show that development is experienced differently by different groups
Answer: (d)
Solution: The deliberate contrast between celebration and complaint shows that the same project affects groups differently. "Prove flyovers are useless" is the extreme-option trap; the author reports residents' experience, not a verdict on all flyovers. So the answer is that development is experienced differently by different groups.
Source: PYQ-style
