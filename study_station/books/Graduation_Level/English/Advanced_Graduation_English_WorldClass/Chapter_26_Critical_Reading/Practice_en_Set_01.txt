1. What is the primary purpose of critical reading?
(a) To evaluate, analyse and question the text rather than accept it passively (b) To read as fast as possible (c) To memorise every fact in the passage (d) To find grammatical errors in the text
Answer: (a)
Solution: Speed and memorisation are tempting because exams reward both, but critical reading means judging the author's claims, evidence and tone. So the answer is to evaluate, analyse and question the text.
Source: NCERT Class 11 English (Reading Skills)

2. The "main idea" of a passage is best described as:
(a) The first sentence of the passage (b) The central point the author wants to convey (c) The most interesting detail in the passage (d) The title of the passage
Answer: (b)
Solution: The first sentence often looks like the main idea, but it may only be a hook or example; the main idea is the central point the whole passage supports. So the answer is the central point the author wants to convey.
Source: PYQ-style

3. An inference question asks you to:
(a) Conclude something suggested but not directly stated (b) Copy a line directly from the passage (c) Give your personal opinion on the topic (d) Summarise the whole passage in one word
Answer: (a)
Solution: Copying a line is a fact-based task, not inference; inference means reading between the lines using evidence in the text. So the answer is to conclude something suggested but not directly stated.
Source: PYQ-style

4. The "tone" of a passage refers to:
(a) The length of the sentences (b) The author's attitude towards the subject (c) The number of paragraphs (d) The difficulty of the vocabulary
Answer: (b)
Solution: Sentence length affects style, not tone; tone is the author's attitude — optimistic, critical, neutral, sarcastic, etc. So the answer is the author's attitude towards the subject.
Source: NCERT Class 11 English (Reading Skills)

5. Which of the following is a Fact-based question that should take about 15 seconds?
(a) "What can be inferred about the author's view of AI?" (b) "According to the passage, in which year was the policy launched?" (c) "What is the tone of the second paragraph?" (d) "Which title best suits the passage?"
Answer: (b)
Solution: Inference, tone and title questions need whole-passage thinking (45 seconds); a directly stated year is a locate-and-pick fact question. So the answer is the question asking for the year the policy was launched.
Source: PYQ-style

6. Read the sentence: "The data clearly show that renewable energy outpaced coal last year." The word "clearly" signals:
(a) A sarcastic tone (b) The author's doubt about the data (c) The author's certainty about the claim (d) A neutral report with no stance
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Doubt" is tempting because data can be questioned, but intensifiers like "clearly" strengthen a claim and show the author's confidence. So the answer is the author's certainty about the claim.
Source: PYQ-style

7. In the Detective Approach to inference, the "clues" are:
(a) Your prior knowledge of the topic (b) The dictionary meanings of difficult words (c) The opinions of other readers (d) Specific words and details in the passage
Answer: (d)
Solution: Prior knowledge is a trap — inference must be grounded in the text, not outside knowledge; the clues are the words and details the author gives. So the answer is specific words and details in the passage.
Source: PYQ-style

8. A passage states: "Electric vehicles are gaining ground, yet charging infrastructure lags behind." The word "yet" indicates:
(a) An addition of similar ideas (b) A conclusion (c) A cause-and-effect relationship (d) A contrast between two ideas
Answer: (d)
Solution: "Addition" is tempting because both clauses discuss EVs, but "yet" is a contrast marker showing growth versus lagging infrastructure. So the answer is a contrast between two ideas.
Source: NCERT Class 10 English

9. Which word best describes a tone that is "objective and unbiased"?
(a) Pessimistic (b) Sarcastic (c) Passionate (d) Neutral
Answer: (d)
Solution: "Passionate" may sound positive, but it shows strong emotion, not objectivity; an unbiased report has a neutral tone. So the answer is Neutral.
Source: PYQ-style

10. The best title for a passage should:
(a) Mention every detail in the passage (b) Repeat the first sentence exactly (c) Be the longest possible phrase (d) Capture the central idea without being too broad or too narrow
Answer: (d)
Solution: A title mentioning every detail is tempting but becomes a summary, not a title; a good title fits the scope of the whole passage. So the answer is one that captures the central idea without being too broad or too narrow.
Source: PYQ-style

11. Spot the error: "The committee (P) / have decided (Q) / to postpone the meeting (R) / until next Monday. (S)"
(a) Part P (b) Part R (c) Part Q (d) No error
Answer: (c)
Solution: Part P looks suspicious because "committee" can take plural verbs in British usage, but in exam English a collective noun acting as one body takes a singular verb; Part Q should read "has decided". So the answer is Part Q.
Source: NCERT Class 10 English (Grammar)

12. "The author mentions the 2023 heatwave primarily in order to:" — this question type tests:
(a) The purpose or function of a detail (b) Vocabulary in context (c) Spelling rules (d) Grammar usage
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Vocabulary" is tempting because a specific detail is named, but "primarily in order to" asks why the detail is used — its rhetorical function. So the answer is the purpose or function of a detail.
Source: PYQ-style

13. In a passage, "mitigate" is used in: "New dams were built to mitigate the risk of floods." The closest meaning is:
(a) Increase (b) Reduce (c) Ignore (d) Measure
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Measure" is tempting because risk is often measured, but dams are built to lessen flood risk; "mitigate" means to reduce or lessen. So the answer is Reduce.
Source: NCERT Class 11 English

14. Which of these is an example of an assumption?
(a) A fact stated in the first line of the passage (b) A quotation from an expert (c) An unstated belief the author's argument depends on (d) A statistic cited by the author
Answer: (c)
Solution: Facts and statistics are stated, so they cannot be assumptions; an assumption is the hidden bridge the argument needs to work. So the answer is an unstated belief the author's argument depends on.
Source: PYQ-style

15. A passage says: "Critics argue that the policy, though well-intentioned, may hurt small farmers." The author's tone towards the policy is:
(a) Cautiously critical (b) Fully supportive (c) Completely dismissive (d) Humorous
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Completely dismissive" is tempting because critics are mentioned, but "well-intentioned" softens the criticism, showing balance. So the answer is cautiously critical.
Source: PYQ-style

16. Which signal word usually introduces a conclusion?
(a) However (b) Therefore (c) For example (d) Although
Answer: (b)
Solution: "However" is tempting because it is a common connector, but it signals contrast, not conclusion; "therefore" signals a result or conclusion. So the answer is Therefore.
Source: NCERT Class 10 English

17. Spot the error: "Neither of the two candidates (P) / have submitted (Q) / their documents (R) / on time. (S)"
(a) Part P (b) Part R (c) Part Q (d) No error
Answer: (c)
Solution: Part R looks wrong because "their" follows "neither", but modern usage accepts it; the real error is in Part Q — "neither" is singular and needs "has submitted". So the answer is Part Q.
Source: NCERT Class 10 English (Grammar)

18. When a question says "According to the passage…", you should:
(a) Answer from your general knowledge (b) Answer based on the most recent news (c) Answer strictly from what the passage states (d) Answer based on the longest option
Answer: (c)
Solution: General knowledge is the classic trap — even a true fact is wrong if the passage does not support it. So the answer is to answer strictly from what the passage states.
Source: PYQ-style

19. The root "cred" (as in credible, incredible) means:
(a) To believe (b) To hear (c) To write (d) To see
Answer: (a)
Solution: "To hear" is tempting because of words like "audible", but that is the root "audi"; "cred" means to believe, so "credible" means believable. So the answer is To believe.
Source: NCERT Class 11 English (Vocabulary)

20. A passage's first paragraph usually helps the reader by:
(a) Introducing the topic and often the main idea (b) Providing all the evidence (c) Listing all the references (d) Contradicting the conclusion
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Providing all the evidence" is tempting, but evidence is spread through body paragraphs; the opening introduces the topic and often the thesis. So the answer is introducing the topic and often the main idea.
Source: PYQ-style

21. Read the lines: "While AI promises efficiency, its unchecked growth raises questions about jobs, privacy and bias." The author's attitude towards AI is best described as:
(a) Unconditionally enthusiastic (b) Strongly hostile (c) Balanced — acknowledging benefits and risks (d) Indifferent
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Strongly hostile" is tempting because risks are listed, but the opening clause concedes AI's promise of efficiency, showing a two-sided view. So the answer is balanced — acknowledging benefits and risks.
Source: PYQ-style

22. Read the lines: "Despite record rainfall this year, the reservoir levels remain below average." What can be inferred?
(a) Rainfall data for this year is incorrect (b) Record rainfall guarantees full reservoirs (c) Reservoirs are always below average (d) Factors such as high consumption or poor storage may offset the rainfall
Answer: (d)
Solution: Claiming the data is incorrect is tempting, but the passage gives no reason to doubt it; the gap between rainfall and reservoir levels suggests other factors at play. So the answer is that factors such as high consumption or poor storage may offset the rainfall.
Source: PYQ-style

23. Read the lines: "The new tax regime, hailed by industry leaders, has left small traders anxious about compliance costs." The word "hailed" suggests industry leaders:
(a) Criticised the regime (b) Feared the regime (c) Ignored the regime (d) Praised the regime
Answer: (d)
Solution: "Criticised" is tempting because the sentence later mentions anxiety, but that anxiety belongs to small traders; "hailed" means praised or welcomed. So the answer is praised the regime.
Source: PYQ-style

24. Read the lines: "The report stops short of blaming the ministry, though it notes 'systemic delays' at every stage." The phrase "stops short of" means:
(a) Avoids doing something it comes close to doing (b) Fully supports (c) Strongly condemns (d) Praises indirectly
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Strongly condemns" is tempting because "systemic delays" sounds like blame, but "stops short of" means it refrains from going that far. So the answer is avoids doing something it comes close to doing.
Source: PYQ-style

25. Read the lines: "Online education has widened access, but the digital divide means its benefits remain unevenly shared." The main idea is:
(a) Online education is a complete failure (b) Online education expands access but does not benefit all equally (c) Online education helps everyone equally (d) The digital divide has been eliminated
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Complete failure" is tempting because a drawback is mentioned, but the sentence credits online education with widening access; the point is the mix of benefit and inequality. So the answer is that online education expands access but does not benefit all equally.
Source: PYQ-style
