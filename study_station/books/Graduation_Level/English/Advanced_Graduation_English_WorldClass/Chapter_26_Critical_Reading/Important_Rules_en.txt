# 📋 Important Rules & Tips — Critical Reading

## (a) 12 High-Yield Rules

| # | Rule | Example | Examiner's Trap |
|---|------|---------|-----------------|
| 1 | **Passage is supreme** — answer only from the passage, never from outside knowledge | If the passage says "AI may reduce some jobs," choose that even if you personally believe AI creates jobs | Options that are true in real life but NOT stated in the passage |
| 2 | **Read the questions first** (not options) — know what to hunt for | Glance at 5 questions in 20 seconds, then read the passage with targets in mind | Reading all 20 options first wastes time and plants wrong ideas |
| 3 | **Main idea = what the whole passage does**, not what one paragraph says | A passage mentioning "climate" in every paragraph but arguing "policy failure" has main idea = policy failure | Choosing the broadest or the narrowest option; correct answer sits in the middle |
| 4 | **Tone is the author's attitude**, not the topic | "The policy, though well-intentioned, collapsed within a year" → tone is *critical*, not *informative* | Confusing a factual/sad topic with a negative tone; topic ≠ tone |
| 5 | **Inference = must be true**, not *could* be true | Passage: "Rainfall fell 30%." Valid inference: "The region received less rain than before." Invalid: "Farmers committed suicide." | Options with extreme leaps — attractive but unsupported |
| 6 | **Beware extreme words** — *always, never, all, none, completely* — usually wrong in inference/tone questions | "The author completely rejects technology" is wrong if the passage only criticises *misuse* of technology | Extreme options feel decisive; examiners plant them as bait |
| 7 | **"According to the passage" = Fact question** — locate, don't think | Find the exact line; the answer is a paraphrase of that line | Over-thinking a fact question and picking an inferred option |
| 8 | **"It can be inferred / suggests" = Inference question** — think one step, not five | Passage: "He checked the lock twice." Inference: he was cautious — not "he was a thief" | Multi-step reasoning chains that go beyond the text |
| 9 | **Vocabulary-in-context: ignore the dictionary meaning you know first** | "The *grave* situation" — here *grave* = serious, not a burial place | Picking the most common meaning instead of the contextual one |
| 10 | **EXCEPT / NOT questions: verify all four options against the passage** | Three options will be directly stated; the odd one out is the answer | Marking the first true-looking option and forgetting the question is negative |
| 11 | **Transition words reveal structure** — *however, moreover, therefore, although* | "The scheme succeeded in cities. *However*, villages saw no change." → author's real point is after *however* | Treating both sides equally when a contrast word signals the author's actual stance |
| 12 | **First and last sentences of paragraphs carry the skeleton** | Skim first lines to map the passage in 30 seconds before deep reading | Getting lost in middle-paragraph examples and missing the argument's direction |

## (b) Time Management — The 15/45-Second Rule

| Question Type | Signal Words | Time | Strategy |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Fact-based (15 sec)** | "According to the passage…", "The author mentions…", "Which of the following is stated…", vocabulary-in-context | ⏱️ 15 seconds | Locate the line → match the paraphrase → mark. Do NOT re-read the whole passage. |
| **Inference-based (45 sec)** | "It can be inferred…", "The author suggests…", "The tone of the passage is…", "The primary purpose is…" | ⏱️ 45 seconds | Re-read the relevant paragraph → eliminate extreme/unsupported options → choose the mildest defensible option. |

**Golden split:** In a 5-question set, typically 2 are Fact (30 sec total) and 3 are Inference (~2 min). Budget **6–7 minutes per passage** including reading time. If an inference question crosses 60 seconds, mark your best elimination and move on.

## (c) Morphology — Roots & Affixes for Critical Reading

| Root / Affix | Meaning | Example Words | In a Passage |
|---|---|---|---|
| **cred** (Latin) | believe | credible, incredible, credulous, discredit | "The report was hardly *credible*." |
| **dict** (Latin) | say | predict, contradict, verdict, dictate | "Critics *contradict* the minister's claim." |
| **spect** (Latin) | look | inspect, perspective, retrospect, spectator | "In *retrospect*, the policy was flawed." |
| **path** (Greek) | feeling | empathy, apathy, sympathy, antipathy | "Public *apathy* doomed the campaign." |
| **chron** (Greek) | time | chronic, chronological, anachronism | "The *chronic* shortage persisted." |
| **anti-** (prefix) | against | antidote, antagonist, antithesis | "His view is the *antithesis* of hers." |
| **-ous** (suffix) | full of | dubious, ambiguous, cautious | "The data remains *dubious*." |
| **-ism** (suffix) | belief/system | scepticism, optimism, pragmatism | "Her *pragmatism* won support." |

**Usage tip:** When an unknown word appears in a tone or vocabulary question, break it into root + affix, then test the resulting meaning against the sentence. *Ambiguous* = *ambi-* (both) + *-ous* → "open to both interpretations" — exactly the kind of word examiners love in tone questions.
