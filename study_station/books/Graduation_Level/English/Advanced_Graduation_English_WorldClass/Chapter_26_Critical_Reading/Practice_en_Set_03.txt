51. What is the primary purpose of critical reading?
(a) To evaluate, analyse and question the author's claims (b) To memorise the passage word for word (c) To read as fast as possible (d) To agree with everything the author writes
Answer: (a)
Solution: Memorising or speed-reading are surface skills, and blind agreement is the opposite of critical thinking. Critical reading means actively evaluating arguments, evidence and tone, so the answer is to evaluate, analyse and question the author's claims.
Source: PYQ-style

52. In a reading comprehension passage, the "main idea" question asks for:
(a) A minor detail mentioned in one line (b) The central point the whole passage conveys (c) The author's biography (d) The most difficult word in the passage
Answer: (b)
Solution: A minor detail tempts students who pick the first fact they remember, but main idea questions test the overall message. The central point of the whole passage is what is asked, so the answer is the central point the whole passage conveys.

53. An inference question differs from a fact question because an inference:
(a) Must be logically derived from stated information (b) Is directly stated in the passage (c) Is always the author's opinion (d) Requires outside expert knowledge
Answer: (a)
Solution: Directly stated information belongs to fact questions, and outside knowledge is a trap — inferences must rest only on the passage. An inference is logically derived from what is stated, so the answer is that it must be logically derived from stated information.
Source: PYQ-style

54. The "tone" of a passage refers to:
(a) The author's attitude toward the subject (b) The length of the sentences (c) The number of paragraphs (d) The font used in printing
Answer: (a)
Solution: Sentence length and paragraphs are structural features, not tone. Tone is the author's attitude — critical, optimistic, sarcastic, neutral — toward the subject, so the answer is the author's attitude toward the subject.

55. Which word best describes a tone that is "objective"?
(a) Emotional and biased (b) Neutral and fact-based (c) Angry and accusing (d) Humorous and playful
Answer: (b)
Solution: Emotional, angry or humorous writing shows personal feeling, which is the opposite of objectivity. An objective tone is neutral and fact-based, so the answer is neutral and fact-based.

56. When a question says "according to the passage", you should:
(a) Answer strictly from what the passage states or implies (b) Use your general knowledge of the topic (c) Choose the most interesting option (d) Pick the longest option
Answer: (a)
Solution: General knowledge is the classic trap — the exam tests comprehension, not what you already know. The phrase "according to the passage" restricts you to the text, so the answer is to answer strictly from what the passage states or implies.

57. A "sarcastic" tone means the author:
(a) Praises the subject sincerely (b) Says the opposite of what is meant, to mock (c) Remains completely neutral (d) Gives only statistics
Answer: (b)
Solution: Sincere praise is the trap option because sarcasm often uses positive words; the meaning is mocking, not genuine. Sarcasm says the opposite of what is meant to mock, so the answer is that it says the opposite of what is meant, to mock.

58. The Detective Approach to inference means:
(a) Guessing the answer quickly (b) Reading only the first line (c) Skipping the passage entirely (d) Gathering clues from the text before concluding
Answer: (d)
Solution: Guessing and skipping are traps that ignore evidence. Like a detective, a critical reader collects textual clues and then concludes, so the answer is gathering clues from the text before concluding.

(a) Finding fault with everything (b) Analytical and evaluative (c) Fast and careless (d) Negative and hostile
