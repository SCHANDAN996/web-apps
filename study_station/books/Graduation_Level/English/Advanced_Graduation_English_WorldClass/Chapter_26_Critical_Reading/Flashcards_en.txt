Card 1
Front: What is the "Detective Approach" in Critical Reading?
Back: Treat every inference question like a detective treats a case — conclusions must be supported by evidence (clues) actually present in the passage, never by outside knowledge or assumptions. If the passage doesn't say it, you can't infer it.

Card 2
Front: What is the difference between a "Fact-based" and an "Inference-based" question?
Back: Fact-based: the answer is directly stated in the passage (15-second rule). Inference-based: the answer is implied and must be deduced from clues (45-second rule).

Card 3
Front: 🧠 Mnemonic: What does "TAPS" stand for when analysing a passage?
Back: Tone, Audience, Purpose, Structure — the four things you should identify in the first 30 seconds of reading any passage.

Card 4
Front: ⚠️ Examiner's Trap: An option says "The author strongly condemns artificial intelligence." The passage only lists AI's risks and benefits neutrally. Why is this option wrong?
Back: It is an "extreme tone" trap. A neutral/analytical passage cannot support an extreme word like "strongly condemns." Match the option's intensity to the author's actual tone.

Card 5
Front: What does the Greek root "chronos" mean, and give two derived words.
Back: Time. Derived words: chronological (arranged in time order), chronic (long-lasting).

Card 6
Front: What is "tone mapping" in reading comprehension?
Back: Labelling the author's attitude paragraph by paragraph (e.g., optimistic → cautious → critical) so that tone and attitude questions can be answered from your map, not from memory.

Card 7
Front: What does the Latin root "cred" mean, and give two derived words.
Back: Believe. Derived words: credible (believable), incredible (unbelievable); also credit, creed.

Card 8
Front: ⚠️ Examiner's Trap: An option is a true statement about the real world, but the passage never mentions it. Can it be the answer to an inference question?
Back: No. This is the "true but outside the passage" trap. In Critical Reading, truth in the real world is irrelevant — only what the passage supports counts.

Card 9
Front: What does the Greek root "pathos" mean, and give two derived words.
Back: Feeling/suffering. Derived words: sympathy (feeling with), apathy (lack of feeling); also pathetic, empathy.

Card 10
Front: 🧠 Mnemonic: What does "FIRE" remind you to check before finalising an inference answer?
Back: Facts in passage → Implied, not stated → Reasonable (not extreme) → Evidence line exists. If any letter fails, eliminate the option.

Card 11
Front: What is the 15/45-second rule?
Back: Spend about 15 seconds on fact-based questions (directly stated answers) and up to 45 seconds on inference/tone questions (which require deduction). Never give a fact question 45 seconds — that signals over-thinking.

Card 12
Front: What does the Latin root "dict" mean, and give two derived words.
Back: Say/speak. Derived words: predict (say before), contradict (speak against); also dictionary, verdict.

Card 13
Front: In a "main idea" question, why are options that are true of only one paragraph wrong?
Back: They are "too narrow" traps. The main idea must cover the whole passage, not just a supporting detail or a single paragraph's point.

Card 14
Front: What does the Greek root "graph" mean, and give two derived words.
Back: Write. Derived words: biography (writing about a life), autograph (self-written); also paragraph, geography.

Card 15
Front: What is the "Sound-Right" trap in comprehension options?
Back: An option that uses impressive vocabulary or echoes the passage's exact words but subtly distorts the meaning. It sounds correct because it feels familiar — always verify against the passage line by line.

Card 16
Front: What does the Latin root "port" mean, and give two derived words.
Back: Carry. Derived words: transport (carry across), export (carry out); also portable, import.

Card 17
Front: How do you handle a "EXCEPT/NOT" question in Critical Reading?
Back: Convert it: find the three options that ARE supported by the passage and eliminate them; the leftover option is the answer. Read the question stem twice — negative wording is itself a classic trap.

Card 18
Front: What does the Greek root "logos" mean, and give two derived words.
Back: Word/study/reason. Derived words: logic (reasoning), biology (study of life); also dialogue, psychology.

Card 19
Front: ⚠️ Examiner's Trap: The passage says "Some economists believe inflation will ease." An option says "Economists believe inflation will ease." Why is it wrong?
Back: It deletes the qualifier "some," turning a limited claim into a universal one. Watch for qualifier traps: some/all, may/will, often/always.

Card 20
Front: What is the "Blurting Sheet" technique for revising Critical Reading?
Back: After studying, close the book and write everything you remember — roots, rules, traps, mnemonics — on a blank sheet. Compare with your notes; the gaps are what you must review after 2 days.
