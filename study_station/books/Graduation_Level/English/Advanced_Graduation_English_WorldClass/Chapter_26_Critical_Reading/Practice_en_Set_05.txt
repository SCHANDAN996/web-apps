101. Read the passage: "While automation has displaced workers in manufacturing, it has simultaneously created new roles in data analysis, machine maintenance, and software design. The net effect on employment, therefore, remains contested among economists." What is the author's tone?
(a) Alarmist (b) Optimistic (c) Neutral and analytical (d) Sarcastic
Answer: (c)
Solution: The tempting wrong choice is "optimistic" because the passage mentions new jobs, but the author presents both displacement and creation and calls the net effect "contested," which signals balance, not cheerfulness. Words like "while," "simultaneously," and "contested" show an even-handed weighing of evidence, so the answer is neutral and analytical.
Source: PYQ-style (SSC CGL pattern)

102. Read the passage: "Critics argue that artificial intelligence will erode human creativity. Yet history suggests otherwise: the camera did not kill painting; it liberated it, pushing artists toward impressionism and abstraction." What is the author's main argument?
(a) New technology tends to transform rather than eliminate human creativity (b) AI will definitely destroy human creativity (c) Cameras were more important than paintings (d) Impressionism was caused by artificial intelligence
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is option two, which restates the critics' view, not the author's — a classic "attributed opinion" trap. The author counters the critics with the camera analogy, arguing technology redirects creativity into new forms, so the answer is that new technology tends to transform rather than eliminate human creativity.
