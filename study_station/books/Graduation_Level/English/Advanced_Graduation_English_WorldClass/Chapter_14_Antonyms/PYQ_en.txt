# 📊 PYQ Analysis — Antonyms (Vocabulary)

## How Exams Test Antonyms

Antonyms are a **permanent fixture** in the English section of SSC (CGL, CHSL, MTS, GD), Banking (IBPS, SBI), Railways (NTPC, Group D) and State exams. They appear in two formats:

1. **Direct format:** "Choose the word opposite in meaning to the given word." (Most common in SSC/Bank prelims.)
2. **Embedded format:** The word appears inside a sentence, and you must pick the option opposite to its contextual meaning. (Common in CGL Tier-II, IBPS PO mains.)

### Recurring Sub-Topics (Pattern Analysis)

Based on the consistent pattern of these exams, antonym questions cluster around these word families:

- **Character/behaviour words:** generous–stingy, humble–arrogant, brave–cowardly
- **Abstract quality words:** transparent–opaque, genuine–spurious, lucid–obscure
- **Action words:** expand–contract, accelerate–decelerate, conceal–reveal
- **Quantity/degree words:** abundant–scarce, meagre–plentiful, trivial–significant
- **Emotion/attitude words:** apathy–concern, hostility–friendliness, zeal–indifference

### The Examiner's Favourite Traps

1. **Synonym-as-distractor:** One option is a *synonym* of the given word, placed to catch students who misread "opposite" as "similar." (E.g., word: *frugal*; distractor: *thrifty* — a synonym, not the answer.)
2. **Near-antonym vs exact antonym:** Two options look opposite, but only one is the *true* opposite in degree. (E.g., opposite of *ancient* is *modern*, not *old*.)
3. **Part-of-speech mismatch:** The correct antonym must match the word class. If the given word is an adjective, a noun option is a trap even if the meaning seems opposite.
4. **Context flip (embedded format):** In a sentence, the word may carry a secondary meaning; the antonym must oppose *that* meaning, not the primary dictionary one.

## Top 10 High-Yield PYQ-Style Questions with Solutions

**Q1.** Choose the word opposite in meaning to: **TRANSPARENT**
(a) Clear (b) Opaque (c) Fragile (d) Thin
**Answer:** (b)
**Solution:** Transparent = allowing light through. Its exact opposite is *opaque*. (a) is a synonym — the classic synonym-distractor trap.
**Trap type:** Synonym-as-distractor | **Source:** PYQ-style

**Q2.** Choose the antonym of: **GREGARIOUS**
(a) Sociable (b) Reclusive (c) Talkative (d) Cheerful
**Answer:** (b)
**Solution:** Gregarious = fond of company. Opposite = *reclusive* (avoiding company). (a) and (c) are near-synonyms planted as traps.
**Trap type:** Synonym-as-distractor | **Source:** PYQ-style

**Q3.** Choose the antonym of: **OBSOLETE**
(a) Ancient (b) Outdated (c) Contemporary (d) Useless
**Answer:** (c)
**Solution:** Obsolete = no longer in use. The true opposite is *contemporary/modern*. (b) is a synonym planted as a distractor; (a) is a related word (old things are often obsolete, but "ancient" is not a synonym) and (d) describes a consequence, not the meaning.
**Trap type:** Synonym-as-distractor | **Source:** PYQ-style

**Q4.** Choose the antonym of: **AMELIORATE**
(a) Improve (b) Worsen (c) Delay (d) Complicate
**Answer:** (b)
**Solution:** Ameliorate = to make better. Direct opposite = *worsen* (aggravate). (a) is a synonym; (c) and (d) are related actions but not opposites.
**Trap type:** Over-thinking (students pick "complicate" assuming it's opposite) | **Source:** PYQ-style

**Q5.** Choose the antonym of: **CANDID**
(a) Frank (b) Honest (c) Evasive (d) Rude
**Answer:** (c)
**Solution:** Candid = truthful and straightforward. Opposite = *evasive* (dodging, not direct). (a) and (b) are synonyms.
**Trap type:** Synonym-as-distractor | **Source:** PYQ-style

**Q6.** Choose the antonym of: **EPHEMERAL**
(a) Brief (b) Permanent (c) Beautiful (d) Fragile
**Answer:** (b)
**Solution:** Ephemeral = short-lived. Opposite = *permanent/everlasting*. (a) is a synonym — a very frequently repeated trap pair in exams.
**Trap type:** Synonym-as-distractor | **Source:** PYQ-style

**Q7.** Choose the antonym of: **AUGMENT**
(a) Increase (b) Diminish (c) Support (d) Decorate
**Answer:** (b)
**Solution:** Augment = to increase. Opposite = *diminish/reduce*. Root clue: *aug-* (grow) vs *min-* (less, as in minus/minimum).
**Trap type:** Root-based elimination | **Source:** PYQ-style

**Q8.** Choose the antonym of: **BENEVOLENT**
(a) Kind (b) Generous (c) Malevolent (d) Strict
**Answer:** (c)
**Solution:** Benevolent = wishing good (bene = good). Opposite = *malevolent* (male = bad). Root knowledge solves this in seconds. (d) is a near-trap — strict people can still be benevolent.
**Trap type:** Over-thinking | **Source:** PYQ-style

**Q9.** Choose the antonym of: **SCARCE**
(a) Rare (b) Plentiful (c) Costly (d) Precious
**Answer:** (b)
**Solution:** Scarce = in short supply. Opposite = *plentiful/abundant*. (a) is a synonym; (c) and (d) are associations, not opposites.
**Trap type:** Association trap | **Source:** PYQ-style

**Q10.** Choose the antonym of: **ACQUIT**
(a) Free (b) Convict (c) Blame (d) Punish
**Answer:** (b)
**Solution:** Acquit = to declare not guilty (legal term). Its precise legal opposite is *convict* (declare guilty). (c) and (d) are related but not the formal opposite — a classic over-thinking trap.
**Trap type:** Over-thinking | **Source:** PYQ-style

## Applying the 15/45-Second Rule

**Q6 (Ephemeral) — 15-second question:** This is pure recall. You either know ephemeral = short-lived or you don't. Scan options, spot the synonym trap (brief), mark *permanent*, move on. Do NOT spend more than 15 seconds — extra time won't improve recall.

**Q10 (Acquit) — 45-second question:** This needs inference. Three options (free, blame, punish) are all semantically related to acquit. You must reason: acquit is a *legal verdict*; its formal opposite must also be a *legal verdict* → convict. This reasoning justifies 45 seconds. If still unsure after 45 seconds, mark the best legal-term match and move on.

**Rule of thumb:** Single common word with clear options → 15 sec. Legal/technical/embedded-context words with multiple "related" options → 45 sec.

---

## Key Takeaways

- Antonym questions reward **root knowledge** (bene/male, aug/min) — learn roots, not just word lists.
- The most repeated trap is the **synonym distractor** — always re-read the question word "OPPOSITE" before answering.
- Match the **part of speech** before matching the meaning.
- In embedded (sentence) format, find the word's *contextual* meaning first, then negate it.
