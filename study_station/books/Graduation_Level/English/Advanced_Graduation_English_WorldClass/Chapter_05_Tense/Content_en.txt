## 1. What Is Tense, Really?

Tense is the **time-stamp** a verb carries. English has **three times** (Present, Past, Future) and **four aspects** (Indefinite/Simple, Continuous, Perfect, Perfect Continuous) — giving **12 tense forms**.

The aspect tells you *how* the action relates to time:
- **Simple** → a fact or habit: *She writes.*
- **Continuous** → in progress at that moment: *She is writing.*
- **Perfect** → completed, with relevance to a reference point: *She has written.*
- **Perfect Continuous** → duration up to a reference point: *She has been writing for two hours.*

## 2. The Structure Engine

Every tense = **subject + auxiliary + main verb form**. Master the auxiliaries and you master the system:

| Aspect | Present | Past | Future |
|---|---|---|---|
| Simple | V1 / V1+s | V2 | will + V1 |
| Continuous | is/am/are + V1+ing | was/were + V1+ing | will be + V1+ing |
| Perfect | has/have + V3 | had + V3 | will have + V3 |
| Perfect Continuous | has/have been + V1+ing | had been + V1+ing | will have been + V1+ing |

**Key insight:** The *first auxiliary* carries the tense. "She **had been** waiting" — *had* is past, *been + ing* is perfect continuous. Learn to read the first auxiliary like a timestamp.

## 3. The 12 Golden Rules 🏆

1. **Since + point of time, for + period** — with perfect/perfect continuous: *since Monday, since 2010* vs *for two years*.
2. **Present Perfect ≠ definite past time.** Never say *"I have met him yesterday."* Definite past time (yesterday, last week, in 2019) demands **Simple Past**: *I met him yesterday.*
3. **"It is time / wish / if only" + Simple Past** (for present unreal): *It is time you **went** home.*
4. **Stative verbs refuse the continuous form**: know, believe, love, hate, own, seem, understand, belong. ❌ *I am knowing him.* ✅ *I know him.*
5. **Two past actions — earlier one takes Past Perfect**: *The train **had left** before I **reached** the station.* (After "before/after" the order is fixed by the conjunction itself.)
6. **Future in the past / reported speech**: *He said he **would** come.*
7. **Time clauses (when, as soon as, until, before, after) take Simple Present, not future**: *I will call you when he **arrives**.* ❌ *when he will arrive.*
8. **"By + future time" → Future Perfect**: *By 2030, she **will have completed** the project.*
9. **Universal truths stay in Simple Present**, even in reported speech: *The teacher said that the earth **revolves** around the sun.*
10. **"Hardly/Scarcely…when" and "No sooner…than"** take Past Perfect first: *No sooner **had** she **entered** than the phone rang.*
11. **Present Perfect with "this is the first/second time"**: *This is the first time I **have seen** the Taj.*
12. **Past Continuous + Simple Past = interrupted action**: *While I **was walking**, it **started** raining.*

🧠 **Active Recall:** Which rule bans "will" after "when"? Which verbs can't take -ing? Say the rule numbers aloud.

## ⚠️ Examiner's Trap Box

**Trap 1 — The "since" with continuous trap:** *"He is working here since 2015."* Sounds natural in Hindi-influenced English, but "since 2015" forces **has been working**. Examiners plant this in error-spotting again and again.

**Trap 2 — The "yesterday + have" trap:** *"I have submitted the form yesterday."* The word *yesterday* kills the Present Perfect. Answer: **submitted**.

**Trap 3 — The stative-verb trap:** *"She is owning a car."* *Own* is stative → **She owns a car.** Watch for know, believe, belong, consist, prefer.

## 🔁 Linguistic Bridge — Hindi से English

| हिंदी में हम सोचते हैं | गलत English | सही English |
|---|---|---|
| "मैं 2015 से यहाँ काम कर रहा हूँ" | I am working here since 2015. | I **have been working** here since 2015. |
| "कल मैं उससे मिला था" (कल = past) | I have met him yesterday. | I **met** him yesterday. |
| "मैं उसे जानता हूँ" | I am knowing him. | I **know** him. |
| "जब वह आएगा, मैं बताऊँगा" | When he **will come**, I will tell him. | When he **comes**, I will tell him. |
| "ट्रेन जा चुकी थी जब मैं पहुँचा" | The train left before I had reached. | The train **had left** before I **reached**. |

**Why this happens:** Hindi uses one continuous form ("रहा हूँ") for both "am doing" and "have been doing." English splits them — duration + since/for always demands the **perfect** family.

## 🧩 Mnemonic — The Stative Verb Shield

**"BELKO"** — verbs that refuse -ing:
**B**elieve, **E**motion verbs (love, hate, prefer), **L**iking/mental verbs (know, understand, seem), **K**eep/possession (own, belong, have=possess), **O**pinion & senses (think=opine, see, hear, smell when involuntary).

*(Note: "think" = opinion is stative; "think" = deliberate process can be continuous: "I am thinking about the plan.")*

## 📊 Summary Table — Signal Words Cheat Sheet

| Tense | Signal Words |
|---|---|
| Simple Present | daily, always, every day, usually, universal truths |
| Present Continuous | now, at present, currently, look!, listen! |
| Present Perfect | just, already, yet, ever, never, recently, so far |
| Present Perfect Continuous | since, for (with ongoing action) |
| Simple Past | yesterday, ago, last week, in 2010 |
| Past Continuous | while, at that moment, all day yesterday |
| Past Perfect | before, after, by the time, no sooner…than, hardly…when |
| Future Perfect | by 2030, by next Monday, by the end of |
| Future Perfect Continuous | for + duration + by a future point |
