# 📊 PYQ Analysis — Tense

## How Exams Test Tense (Pattern Analysis)

Across SSC (CGL, CHSL, MTS, GD), Banking (IBPS PO/Clerk, SBI), Railways (RRB NTPC, Group D) and State PCS prelims, Tense is one of the most dependable sources of grammar questions. It appears in **four recurring formats**:

1. **Spot the Error** — a sentence is split into parts; one part carries a tense violation (e.g., a past perfect missing where two past actions are sequenced).
2. **Fill in the Blank (Verb Form)** — choose the correct tense of the verb in brackets.
3. **Sentence Improvement** — the underlined verb phrase must be replaced with the correct tense.
4. **Cloze Test** — a short passage where 2–3 blanks test tense consistency within a narrative.

**Sub-topics that recur most often:**

- **Sequence of tenses** — Past Perfect vs Simple Past when two past actions occur ("The train *had left* before we reached").
- **Present Perfect vs Simple Past** — the "since/for" and "already/yet/just" signal words; using Present Perfect with a definite past time ("*I have seen* him yesterday" ❌) is the single most repeated trap.
- **Future time clauses** — after *when, if, unless, as soon as, till*, the Present Simple replaces the Future ("When he *comes*…", not "will come").
- **Present Perfect Continuous vs Present Perfect** — duration of an ongoing action vs completed result.
- **Conditional / Unreal past** — "If I *were*…", "I wish I *had known*…", "It is time you *went* home."
- **Stative verbs** — *know, believe, own, love, understand* do not take the continuous form.
- **Since vs For** — point of time vs period of time.

**The examiner's favourite traps (in words, not numbers):**

- **Near-correct statements** — options differing only in *have/has/had* or *-ing* form; the wrong one "sounds right" in spoken English.
- **Time-marker confusion** — planting "yesterday/last year" in the sentence while offering a Present Perfect option.
- **Double-past confusion** — two past actions where candidates pick Simple Past for both, ignoring the earlier action's Past Perfect.
- **Negative framing** — "Which of the following is NOT grammatically correct?" — candidates forget the negative and pick the correct sentence.
- **Sound-alike structures** — "I am working here since 2019" (a direct translation from Hindi) offered as a tempting option.

## Top 10 High-Yield Questions (PYQ-style) with Solutions

**Q1.** The train ______ before we reached the station.
(a) left (b) had left (c) has left (d) was leaving
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** Two past actions; the earlier one takes Past Perfect. "Reached" is the later action (Simple Past), so the train's departure — earlier — is "had left."
**Trap type:** Double-past confusion (Over-thinking makes candidates pick (a) because "left" sounds natural).
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q2.** I ______ him yesterday at the market.
(a) have seen (b) had seen (c) saw (d) see
**Answer: (c)**
**Solution:** A definite past time marker ("yesterday") forbids the Present Perfect. Simple Past "saw" is correct.
**Trap type:** Sound-Right — "have seen" feels polished but is wrong with a finished time.
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q3.** She has been working in this bank ______ 2015.
(a) for (b) since (c) from (d) by
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** "Since" + point of time (2015); "for" + period (for nine years). The Perfect Continuous tense confirms duration up to now.
**Trap type:** Intervening detail — the long verb phrase distracts from the small preposition.
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q4.** When he ______, we shall start the meeting.
(a) will come (b) comes (c) came (d) has come
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** In future time clauses after *when/if/as soon as/unless*, use Present Simple, not "will." The main clause carries the future ("shall start").
**Trap type:** Sound-Right — "will come" matches Hindi intuition ("जब वह आएगा").
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q5.** Spot the error: (a) I am knowing / (b) the answer / (c) to this question. / (d) No error
**Answer: (a)**
**Solution:** "Know" is a stative verb; it has no continuous form. Correct: "I know the answer."
**Trap type:** Sound-Right — continuous forms feel natural to Hindi speakers ("मैं जान रहा हूँ").
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q6.** If I ______ a bird, I would fly to you.
(a) am (b) was (c) were (d) had been
**Answer: (c)**
**Solution:** Hypothetical/unreal present condition takes "were" for all persons (subjunctive). "Was" appears in informal speech but exams want "were."
**Trap type:** Sound-Right — "was" is common in speech.
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q7.** By the time the police arrived, the thief ______.
(a) escapes (b) has escaped (c) had escaped (d) is escaping
**Answer: (c)**
**Solution:** "By the time + Simple Past" signals that the earlier action needs Past Perfect — the escape happened before the arrival.
**Trap type:** Double-past confusion.
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q8.** It is high time you ______ your studies seriously.
(a) take (b) took (c) have taken (d) will take
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** "It is (high) time + subject" is followed by Simple Past (unreal present): "you took." This fixed structure is frequently tested.
**Trap type:** Over-thinking — candidates reject "took" because the meaning is present/future.
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q9.** Which sentence is NOT grammatically correct?
(a) He has lived here for ten years.
(b) He has lived here since 2014.
(c) He has lived here last year.
(d) He has been living here for a decade.
**Answer: (c)**
**Solution:** "Last year" is a finished time; it cannot pair with Present Perfect. Correct version: "He lived here last year."
**Trap type:** Negative framing — many candidates mark (a) or (b) because they forget the "NOT."
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q10.** Hardly ______ the station when the train left.
(a) I had reached (b) had I reached (c) I reached (d) did I reach
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** "Hardly/Scarcely… when" demands Past Perfect with inversion: "Hardly had I reached… when the train left."
**Trap type:** Over-thinking — the inversion looks odd, so candidates pick the "normal-looking" (a).
**Source:** PYQ-style

## Applying the 15/45-Second Rule

**15-second question (Fact-based) — Q3 above:**
The moment you see a Perfect/Perfect Continuous tense plus a blank before a year, the rule "since + point, for + period" fires instantly. No re-reading needed: locate the time expression → classify it (point or period) → mark. Target: 15 seconds.

**45-second question (Inference-based) — Q9 above:**
Negative-framing questions require checking **all four** options against the tense rules, not just spotting one error. Read the stem twice to lock in the "NOT," then test each option's time marker against its tense. Budget the full 45 seconds — rushing here is exactly what the examiner wants.

**Strategy summary:** Single-blank verb-form questions with a visible time marker = 15 seconds. Spot-the-error across sentence parts, negative stems, and sequence-of-tense questions with two actions = 45 seconds. If a tense question has no time marker and no second action, look for stative verbs or fixed structures ("It is time…", "Hardly… when") before spending more than 45 seconds — mark and move on.
