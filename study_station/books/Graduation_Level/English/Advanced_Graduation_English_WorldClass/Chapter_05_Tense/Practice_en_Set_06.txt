126. By the time the results are declared, the candidates ______ for over three months.
(a) will wait (b) will have been waiting (c) will be waiting (d) have waited
Answer: (b)
Solution: "By the time" with a duration ("for over three months") and a future reference point demands the future perfect continuous, showing an action continuing up to that point. The future continuous would only show an action in progress, not its duration up to a deadline.
Source: PYQ-style

127. Hardly ______ the station when the train left.
(a) I had reached (b) had I reached (c) I reached (d) did I reach
Answer: (b)
Solution: After "Hardly", inversion is required, and the earlier action takes the past perfect: "Hardly had I reached... when the train left." The option with "did I reach" wrongly uses the simple past for the earlier of two past actions.
Source: PYQ-style

128. Choose the correctly framed sentence.
(a) I am knowing him since childhood. (b) I had known him since childhood. (c) I have known him since childhood. (d) I know him since childhood.
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Know" is a stative verb and cannot take the continuous form; with "since childhood" the present perfect is correct. The continuous-looking option tempts learners who over-apply the "since + continuous" rule.
Source: NCERT-based grammar

129. Spot the error: Part P: No sooner did she enter the room / Part Q: when the lights / Part R: went off. / Part S: No error
(a) Part Q (b) Part P (c) Part R (d) No error
Answer: (a)
Solution: "No sooner" pairs with "than", not "when", so Part Q should read "than the lights". "When" belongs with "Hardly/Scarcely", which is the trap here.
Source: PYQ-style

130. The committee ______ its report by next Friday.
(a) will have submitted (b) has submitted (c) submits (d) will submit
Answer: (a)
Solution: "By next Friday" marks a deadline in the future, so the future perfect shows completion before that point. The simple future only says the act will happen, not that it will be finished by the deadline.
Source: PYQ-style

131. He ______ in this city for ten years before he moved to Delhi.
(a) has been living (b) has lived (c) lived (d) had lived
Answer: (d)
Solution: Two past events: living in the city happened before moving, so the earlier action takes the past perfect. The present perfect options are wrong because the stay ended when he moved; it does not continue to the present.

132. Spot the error: Part P: I have been / Part Q: knowing him / Part R: for many years. / Part S: No error
(a) Part P (b) Part Q (c) Part R (d) No error
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Know" is a stative verb and is not used in continuous tenses, so Part Q should be "known him", making the sentence present perfect. "For many years" in Part R is perfectly fine with the perfect tense.

133. By 2030, scientists ______ a cure for this disease.
(a) will have discovered (b) will discover (c) will be discovering (d) discover
Answer: (a)
Solution: "By 2030" indicates completion before a future point, so the future perfect is required. The simple future ignores the deadline sense of "by".

134. Choose the correct sentence.
(a) I wish I was a bird. (b) I wish I am a bird. (c) I wish I were a bird. (d) I wish I had been a bird.
Answer: (c)
Solution: Wishes about an unreal present take the subjunctive "were" for all persons, not "was". The past perfect option would express regret about the past, not a present wish.

135. When I reached the theatre, the film ______.
(a) has already started (b) already started (c) had already started (d) starts already
Answer: (c)
Solution: The film started before the reaching, so the earlier of two past actions takes the past perfect. The simple past would suggest the two events happened together.

136. Spot the error: Part P: Scarcely he had / Part Q: stepped out of the house / Part R: when it began to rain. / Part S: No error
(a) Part Q (b) Part P (c) Part R (d) No error
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Scarcely" at the start of a clause needs inversion: "Scarcely had he stepped out...". Part P lacks the inversion; note that "scarcely... when" in Part R is the correct pairing.

137. It is high time we ______ the truth.
(a) accept (b) will accept (c) have accepted (d) accepted
Answer: (d)
Solution: "It is high time" is followed by the simple past to express that something is overdue, even though the meaning is present. The present tense option wrongly treats it as a real present action.

138. She ______ here tomorrow at this hour.
(a) will work (b) will have worked (c) works (d) will be working
Answer: (d)
Solution: "At this hour tomorrow" points to an action in progress at a specific future moment, so the future continuous is correct. The future perfect would need a deadline marker like "by".

139. Choose the correctly framed sentence.
(a) He works here since 2015. (b) He is working here since 2015. (c) He was working here since 2015. (d) He has been working here since 2015.
Answer: (d)
Solution: An action that began in the past and continues till now, with "since 2015", takes the present perfect continuous. The present continuous cannot combine with "since".

140. Spot the error: Part P: Unless you do not / Part Q: work hard, / Part R: you will fail. / Part S: No error
(a) No error (b) Part Q (c) Part R (d) Part P
Answer: (d)
Solution: "Unless" already carries a negative meaning, so "do not" makes a double negative; Part P should be "Unless you work hard". Learners wrongly add "not" thinking a condition needs it.

141. If he ______ harder, he would have cleared the examination.
(a) worked (b) would work (c) has worked (d) had worked
Answer: (d)
Solution: This is a third conditional about an unreal past: "if + past perfect, would have + past participle". The simple past option would fit a second conditional, whose main clause would be "would clear", not "would have cleared".
Source: PYQ-style

142. The train ______ before we reached the platform.
(a) left (b) has left (c) had left (d) was leaving
Answer: (c)
Solution: The departure happened before the arrival, so the earlier action takes the past perfect. The simple past would place both events at the same time.

143. Choose the correct sentence.
(a) I saw him yesterday. (b) I had seen him yesterday. (c) I have seen him yesterday. (d) I see him yesterday.
Answer: (a)
Solution: A definite past time marker like "yesterday" cannot be used with the present perfect; the simple past is correct. The present perfect option is the classic trap.

144. Spot the error: Part P: He denied / Part Q: that he had not / Part R: stolen the money. / Part S: No error

Answer: (b)
Solution: "Denied" already means "said he did not", so "had not" in Part Q creates a double negative; it should be "that he had". The verb "denied" in Part P is correctly used.

146. He talked as if he ______ a ghost.
(a) had seen (b) has seen (c) saw (d) see
Answer: (a)
Solution: "As if" with an unreal past comparison takes the past perfect: he talked as if he had seen a ghost, though he had not. The simple past would only suit an unreal present comparison.

147. Choose the correctly framed sentence.

Solution: "No sooner" requires inversion plus the past perfect, and it pairs with "than", not "when". The option with "when" mixes the "hardly... when" pattern into the wrong structure.

148. Spot the error: Part P: I am living / Part Q: in this house / Part R: since 2010. / Part S: No error
(a) Part P (b) Part Q (c) Part R (d) No error
Answer: (a)
Solution: With "since 2010" the action continues up to the present, so Part P should be "I have been living" (present perfect continuous). The present continuous cannot express duration from a past starting point.

149. If water ______ to 100 degrees Celsius, it boils.
(a) is heated (b) was heated (c) had been heated (d) will be heated
Answer: (a)
Solution: A universal truth or scientific fact uses the zero conditional: simple present in both clauses. The future form wrongly suggests a one-time prediction rather than a law of nature.
Source: NCERT-based grammar

150. Choose the correct sentence.
(a) He said that the earth had revolved around the sun. (b) He said that the earth revolved around the sun. (c) He said that the earth revolves around the sun. (d) He said that the earth is revolving around the sun.
Answer: (c)
Solution: Universal truths keep the simple present in reported speech even when the reporting verb is in the past. Backshifting to "revolved" is the trap, since that rule does not apply to permanent facts.
