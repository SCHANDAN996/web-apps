101. By the time the train reached the station, the passengers ______ for over two hours.
(a) waited (b) wait (c) have been waiting (d) had been waiting
Answer: (d)
Solution: "By the time + past simple" signals an action continuing up to a past point, so past perfect continuous is required. "Waited" would only show a completed act, not duration.
Source: NCERT Class 10 English Grammar

102. Choose the correctly framed sentence.
(a) I have known him since childhood. (b) I am knowing him since childhood. (c) I know him since childhood. (d) I had known him since childhood.
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Know" is a stative verb and cannot take the continuous form; with "since" the present perfect is used. "Since childhood" demands a perfect tense, not simple present.

103. Spot the error: Part P: Scarcely he had / Part Q: stepped out of the house / Part R: when it began to rain. / Part S: No error
(a) No error (b) Part Q (c) Part R (d) Part P
Answer: (d)
Solution: Part P is wrong. After "scarcely" at the start, inversion is needed: "Scarcely had he stepped out...". The examiner's trap is that the sentence sounds acceptable in speech.

104. She ______ her project before the deadline was announced.
(a) submitted (b) has submitted (c) had submitted (d) was submitting
Answer: (c)
Solution: Two past actions — the earlier one (submission) takes past perfect, the later one (announcement) takes simple past. Simple past for both would blur the sequence.

105. This is the first time I ______ such a magnificent palace.
(a) saw (b) have seen (c) had seen (d) see
Answer: (b)
Solution: "This is the first time..." is always followed by the present perfect. With "It was the first time...", the past perfect would be used instead.

106. He ______ in this city for ten years before he moved to Delhi.
(a) has lived (b) had lived (c) has been living (d) is living
Answer: (b)
Solution: The living happened entirely before a past event (moving), so past perfect is correct. Present perfect would wrongly connect the action to the present.

107. Choose the correct sentence.
(a) I wish I were a bird. (b) I wish I was a bird. (c) I wish I am a bird. (d) I wish I had been a bird.
Answer: (a)
Solution: Wishes contrary to present fact take the subjunctive "were" for all persons. "Was" is a common sound-right trap in informal speech but is marked wrong in exams.

108. By 2030, scientists ______ a cure for this disease.
(a) will discover (b) discover (c) will be discovering (d) will have discovered
Answer: (d)
Solution: "By + future time" requires the future perfect, showing completion before that point. Simple future only places the action in the future without the completion idea.

109. Spot the error: Part P: No sooner did the bell ring / Part Q: than the students / Part R: had rushed out of the classroom. / Part S: No error
(a) Part P (b) Part Q (c) No error (d) Part R
Answer: (d)
Solution: Part R is wrong. In "No sooner... than" constructions, the second clause takes simple past: "the students rushed out". The past perfect belongs only to the first action.

110. When I reached the theatre, the film ______.
(a) had already started (b) already started (c) has already started (d) starts
Answer: (a)
Solution: The starting happened before the past action of reaching, so past perfect is needed. "Already started" without "had" breaks the sequence of tenses.

111. Hardly ______ the station when the train left.
(a) had I reached (b) I had reached (c) I reached (d) did I reach
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Hardly" at the beginning demands inversion with past perfect: "Hardly had I reached...". Option with "did I reach" wrongly mixes simple past into the first clause.

112. He said that he ______ the letter the previous day.
(a) posted (b) has posted (c) had posted (d) posts
Answer: (c)
Solution: In reported speech, simple past shifts to past perfect, and "yesterday" becomes "the previous day". Keeping simple past ignores the backshift rule.

113. Choose the correctly framed sentence.
(a) I have been working here since 2015. (b) I am working here since 2015. (c) I was working here since 2015. (d) I worked here since 2015.
Answer: (a)
Solution: An action begun in the past and continuing now takes present perfect continuous with "since". Present continuous with "since" is a classic Hindi-speaker error.

114. Unless he ______ hard, he will not pass the examination.
(a) will work (b) works (c) worked (d) had worked
Answer: (b)
Solution: In conditional clauses introduced by "unless", the present simple expresses the future; "will" is not used in the if/unless clause. This is a frequent examiner trap.

116. The doctor ______ the patient before the nurse brought the reports.
(a) examined (b) examines (c) has examined (d) had examined

(a) will live (b) will be living (c) will have been living (d) have lived

(a) have met (b) had met (c) met (d) meet
Answer: (c)
Solution: A definite past time marker ("yesterday") forbids the present perfect; simple past is correct. Using "have met" with "yesterday" is a classic trap.

(a) Part P (b) No error (c) Part R (d) Part Q
Answer: (d)
Solution: Part Q is wrong. "Each of" takes a singular verb: "has done". The plural noun "boys" nearby is an intervening-phrase trap meant to mislead.

121. If he ______ the truth, he would have told us.
(a) had known (b) knew (c) knows (d) has known
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Would have told" marks a third conditional, so the if-clause needs past perfect: "had known". "Knew" would pair with "would tell", not "would have told".

122. The committee ______ its report by tomorrow evening.
(a) will submit (b) will have submitted (c) submits (d) has submitted
Answer: (b)
Solution: "By tomorrow evening" indicates completion before a future deadline, requiring future perfect. Simple future ignores the deadline sense of "by".

123. Choose the correctly framed sentence.
(a) No sooner she had entered the room than the phone rang. (b) No sooner she entered the room when the phone rang. (c) No sooner had she entered the room than the phone rang. (d) No sooner had she entered the room when the phone rang.
Answer: (c)
Solution: "No sooner" requires inversion with past perfect and pairs with "than", never "when". Options mixing "when" or skipping inversion are double traps.

124. While she ______ dinner, the lights went out.
(a) cooked (b) was cooking (c) had cooked (d) cooks
Answer: (b)
Solution: "While" introduces a longer background action interrupted by a shorter one, so past continuous is correct. Simple past would not show the ongoing background.

125. Spot the error: Part P: I have been / Part Q: waiting for you / Part R: since two hours. / Part S: No error
(a) Part R (b) Part Q (c) Part P (d) No error
Answer: (a)
Solution: Part R is wrong. "Since" is used with a point of time (since 5 o'clock); a period of time takes "for": "for two hours". This since/for confusion is a recurring examiner trap.
