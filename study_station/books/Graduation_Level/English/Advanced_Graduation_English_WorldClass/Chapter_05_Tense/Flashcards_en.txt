Card 1
Front: How many tenses does English have, and how are they structured?
Back: 3 main tenses — Present, Past, Future — each with 4 aspects: Indefinite (Simple), Continuous, Perfect, Perfect Continuous. Total = 12 forms.

Card 2
Front: What is the structure of Present Perfect Continuous?
Back: Subject + has/have + been + V1+ing + since/for + time. Example: She has been studying since morning.

Card 3
Front: When do we use the Simple Present tense even for future events?
Back: For fixed timetables, schedules, and official programs. Example: The train leaves at 6 a.m. tomorrow.

Card 4
Front: 🧠 MNEMONIC: How to remember the time markers of Present Perfect?
Back: "JAY-SEEN-EVER-YET" — Just, Already, Yet, Since, Ever, Never, Recently, So far. These words typically signal Present Perfect (has/have + V3). Caution: with a definite past time (yesterday, ago, last year), use Simple Past even if such a word appears — e.g., "I saw him recently" is fine, but "I have seen him yesterday" is wrong.

Card 5
Front: ⚠️ EXAMINER'S TRAP: "I have seen him yesterday." — Right or wrong?
Back: WRONG. With definite past time (yesterday, last year, ago, in 2019), use Simple Past, never Present Perfect. Correct: I saw him yesterday.

Card 6
Front: Which tense is used in conditional sentences after "if" for an unreal present/future condition?
Back: Simple Past in the if-clause + would + V1 in the main clause. Example: If I were a bird, I would fly. (Note: "were" for all subjects.)

Card 7
Front: What is the difference between "since" and "for"?
Back: Since = point of time (since Monday, since 2010, since 5 o'clock). For = period of time (for two days, for five years).

Card 8
Front: Which tense follows expressions like "It is time", "wish", "as if / as though" (for present unreal)?
Back: Simple Past (with "were" for all persons). Example: It is time you went home. / He talks as if he were a king.

Card 9
Front: 🧠 MNEMONIC: How to remember verbs never used in Continuous form?
Back: Think "STATIVE = STATE, not action" — verbs of the mind, feelings, and possession: know, believe, understand, love, hate, want, see, hear, own, belong. They take no -ing form. (I know him, NOT I am knowing him.)

Card 10
Front: ⚠️ EXAMINER'S TRAP: "He is working here since 2015." — Spot the error.
Back: "Since 2015" demands Perfect Continuous, not Present Continuous. Correct: He has been working here since 2015.

Card 11
Front: When do we use Past Perfect (had + V3)?
Back: When two actions happened in the past — the earlier action takes Past Perfect, the later one Simple Past. Example: The train had left before I reached the station.

Card 12
Front: In "Hardly/Scarcely... when" and "No sooner... than" sentences, which tense is used?
Back: Past Perfect with hardly/scarcely/no sooner; Simple Past after when/than. Example: No sooner had she entered than the phone rang.

Card 13
Front: Which tense is used for universal truths, proverbs, and habitual facts?
Back: Simple Present — even inside reported speech. Example: The teacher said that the earth revolves around the sun.

Card 14
Front: What is the structure of Future Perfect?
Back: Subject + will/shall + have + V3. Used for an action completed before a future point. Example: By 2026, he will have completed his degree.

Card 15
Front: When is Present Continuous used for the future?
Back: For fixed personal arrangements with a future time reference. Example: I am meeting the manager tomorrow.

Card 16
Front: Which tense is used in time clauses beginning with when, before, after, as soon as, until (future reference)?
Back: Simple Present — never "will" in the time clause. Example: I will call you when he arrives. (NOT when he will arrive.)

Card 17
Front: What is the difference between "I have lived here for ten years" and "I lived here for ten years"?
Back: Present Perfect = still living here (action continues to present). Simple Past = no longer living here (action finished).

Card 18
Front: Which tense expresses an action that started in the past and is still continuing, with emphasis on duration?
Back: Present Perfect Continuous. Example: They have been playing for two hours.

Card 19
Front: ⚠️ EXAMINER'S TRAP: "I am having a car." — Right or wrong?
Back: WRONG. "Have" meaning possession is stative → no continuous form. Correct: I have a car. (But "I am having lunch" is correct — there "have" means eating, an action.)

Card 20
Front: In reported speech, what happens to the tense when the reporting verb is in the past?
Back: Tense shifts one step back: Present Simple → Past Simple; Present Perfect → Past Perfect; will → would. Exception: universal truths and habitual facts stay unchanged.
