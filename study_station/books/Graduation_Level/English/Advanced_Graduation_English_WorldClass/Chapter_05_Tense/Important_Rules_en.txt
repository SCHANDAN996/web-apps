# 📋 Important Grammar Rules & Tips — Tense

## (a) 12 High-Yield Rules

| # | Rule | Example | Examiner's Trap |
|---|------|---------|-----------------|
| 1 | **Universal truths & habitual facts** take Simple Present, even after a past-tense reporting verb. | The teacher said that the earth **revolves** around the sun. | "The teacher said that the earth **revolved**…" — sounds consistent, but wrong for permanent truths. |
| 2 | **Since + point of time, For + period of time** — with Present Perfect / Present Perfect Continuous. | He has been working here **since** 2015 / **for** nine years. | "Since five years" is wrong; "since" never takes a duration. |
| 3 | **Past Perfect for the earlier of two past actions**; the later action stays in Simple Past (often joined by before/after/when). | The train **had left** before I **reached** the station. | Using Past Perfect for *both* actions, or for the later one, reverses the timeline. |
| 4 | **No future tense in time/conditional clauses** — after if, when, unless, until, before, as soon as, use Simple Present for future meaning. | I will call you when he **arrives**. (NOT "when he will arrive") | "If it **will rain**, I will stay home" — the double "will" trap. |
| 5 | **"It is time / It is high time" + Simple Past** (unreal present). | It is high time you **started** working seriously. | "It is high time you **start**…" — the past form here does not mean past time. |
| 6 | **Wish / If only + Past** (present regret), **+ Past Perfect** (past regret). | I wish I **were** a bird. / I wish I **had studied** harder. | "I wish I **was**" in formal exam English — "were" is the accepted subjunctive. |
| 7 | **By + future time → Future Perfect**; **by + past time → Past Perfect**. | She **will have finished** the project by Friday. / He **had finished** by 5 pm yesterday. | Confusing "by tomorrow" with Future Continuous ("will be finishing") changes the meaning. |
| 8 | **Present Perfect is never used with a definite past time** (yesterday, ago, last year, in 2019). | I **saw** him yesterday. (NOT "I have seen him yesterday.") | "I have met him last week" — the most frequently tested tense error. |
| 9 | **Hardly/Scarcely…when and No sooner…than** take Past Perfect in the first clause. | **No sooner had** she entered **than** the phone rang. | "No sooner **did she enter** when…" — double trap: wrong tense + wrong connector. |
| 10 | **Present Continuous with "always/constantly/forever"** expresses irritation or criticism, not routine. | He **is always complaining** about something. | Students mark it wrong because "always" usually signals Simple Present — context decides. |
| 11 | **Verbs of perception, possession, mental states** (know, believe, own, love, see, hear, understand) are normally **not used in continuous forms**. | I **know** the answer. (NOT "I am knowing.") | "I am understanding the lesson" — a classic Hindi-influenced error tested as a "sound-right" trap. |
| 12 | **Future in the past**: after past reporting verbs, will→would, shall→should, can→could, may→might. | He said he **would help** me. | "He said he **will help** me" — acceptable only if the event is still pending; exams prefer "would". |

## (b) Time Management — The 15/45-Second Rule

| Type | What it looks like | Time | Strategy |
|------|-------------------|------|----------|
| **Fact-based (15 sec)** | Spot-the-error on Rules 1–12; fill-in-the-blank with a clear time marker (since, ago, by, yesterday); sentence improvement with an obvious tense clash. | ≤ 15 sec | Match the time marker → recall the rule → lock the answer. Do **not** re-read the whole sentence twice. |
| **Inference-based (45 sec)** | Cloze passages where tense must stay consistent across 4–5 blanks; questions where two options are grammatically possible and context (sequence of events, reported speech, tone) decides; "which sentence is correct?" with four full sentences. | ≤ 45 sec | Read the surrounding sentences, fix the timeline (past/present/future), then eliminate options that break the timeline. If still stuck at 45 sec, mark and move on. |

**Quick classification test:** If the answer depends on a single rule or time word → 15 sec. If it depends on the flow of the passage or the relationship between two clauses → 45 sec.

## (c) Morphology — Roots, Affixes & Verb Forms

| Root / Affix | Meaning | Example (tense-relevant) |
|--------------|---------|--------------------------|
| **tempor- (Latin)** | time | temporal, temporary, contemporary — words that signal *time frames* in passages |
| **chron- (Greek)** | time | chronic, chronology, synchronize — "chronological order" questions test tense sequencing |
| **-ed (suffix)** | marks Simple Past / Past Participle of regular verbs | work → worked → worked |
| **-ing (suffix)** | marks continuous/progressive aspect | is working, was working, will be working |
| **-en / -t (irregular participle endings)** | Past Participle marker | written, broken, kept, built — used after has/have/had |
| **-s / -es (suffix)** | Simple Present, 3rd person singular | he works, she watches — the most commonly dropped ending in error-spotting |
| **will / would (modal)** | future / future-in-the-past | will go → would go (reported speech) |
| **have / has / had (auxiliary)** | perfect aspect marker | has gone (present perfect), had gone (past perfect) |
| **be + -ing (auxiliary)** | continuous aspect marker | am/is/are/was/were + V-ing |

**Memory hook:** Every English tense is built from only three bricks — **BE** (continuous), **HAVE** (perfect), and the **base verb** (simple). Master which brick appears in which tense, and no tense question can surprise you.
