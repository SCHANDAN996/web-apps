Questions 1–20: Easy | Questions 21–25: Medium

1. Identify the adverb in the sentence: "She sings beautifully."
(a) She (b) sings (c) beautifully (d) None of these
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Beautifully" modifies the verb "sings" and tells how she sings, so it is an adverb of manner. "Sings" is the verb itself, not the adverb.
Source: NCERT Class 8 English Grammar

2. Choose the correctly spelled adverb formed from "true".
(a) truely (b) truly (c) trueley (d) truily
Answer: (b)
Solution: "True" drops the silent "e" before adding "-ly", giving "truly". "Truely" is a common spelling trap.
Source: NCERT Class 9 English Grammar

3. "He arrived late for the meeting." Here "late" is:
(a) an adjective (b) a noun (c) an adverb (d) a conjunction
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Late" modifies the verb "arrived" and tells when he arrived, so it is an adverb of time. Note that "late" has the same form as adjective and adverb.
Source: NCERT Class 8 English Grammar

4. Select the sentence with the adverb in the correct position.
(a) She speaks English fluently. (b) She speaks fluently English. (c) She fluently speaks English fluently. (d) Fluently she speaks English fluently.
Answer: (a)
Solution: An adverb of manner normally comes after the object, not between the verb and its object. "Speaks fluently English" wrongly splits the verb and object.
Source: Wren & Martin, High School English Grammar

5. "The train is very fast." The word "very" is an adverb of:
(a) manner (b) degree (c) time (d) place
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Very" modifies the adjective "fast" and tells to what extent, so it is an adverb of degree. Adverbs of degree answer "how much/to what extent".
Source: NCERT Class 8 English Grammar

6. Choose the correct sentence.
(a) He drives fastly. (b) He drives fasterly. (c) He drives fasting. (d) He drives fast.
Answer: (d)
Solution: "Fast" is both an adjective and an adverb; "fastly" does not exist in standard English. This is a classic examiner trap with words like fast, hard, late.
Source: Wren & Martin, High School English Grammar

7. "I have almost finished my work." Here "almost" modifies:
(a) I (b) have (c) work (d) finished
Answer: (d)
Solution: "Almost" is an adverb of degree modifying the verb "finished", telling how near completion the work is. It does not modify the noun "work".
Source: NCERT Class 9 English Grammar

8. Spot the error: "He runs (P) / more faster (Q) / than his brother. (R) / No error (S)"
(a) Part Q (b) Part P (c) Part R (d) No error
Answer: (a)
Solution: Part Q has a double comparative. "Faster" is already comparative, so "more" must be removed. Examiners often pair "more/most" with "-er/-est" forms to trap candidates.
Source: PYQ-style

9. "She always reaches office on time." "Always" is an adverb of:
(a) degree (b) place (c) manner (d) frequency
Answer: (d)
Solution: "Always" tells how often the action happens, so it is an adverb of frequency. Other examples: often, seldom, never, usually.
Source: NCERT Class 8 English Grammar

10. Choose the correct adverb to fill the blank: "The soldiers fought ______."
(a) brave (b) braver (c) bravery (d) bravely
Answer: (d)
Solution: The verb "fought" needs an adverb of manner, so "bravely" is correct. "Brave" is an adjective and "bravery" is a noun.
Source: NCERT Class 8 English Grammar

11. "He is too weak to walk." Here "too" is an adverb of:
(a) manner (b) degree (c) time (d) place
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Too" modifies the adjective "weak" and shows excess, so it is an adverb of degree. "Too...to" expresses a negative result.
Source: Wren & Martin, High School English Grammar

12. Select the correctly placed adverb: "______ he comes here."
(a) Seldomer (b) Seldomly (c) Seldomness (d) Seldom
Answer: (d)
Solution: "Seldom" is already an adverb of frequency; there is no word "seldomly". Sentence-initial "seldom" is grammatically fine.
Source: NCERT Class 9 English Grammar

13. "The children played happily in the park." The adverb "happily" answers which question?
(a) How? (b) Where? (c) When? (d) How often?
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Happily" is an adverb of manner and answers "How did the children play?" "In the park" answers "where", not the adverb asked about.
Source: NCERT Class 8 English Grammar

14. Choose the correct sentence.
(a) He works hardly to earn money. (b) He works hard to earn money. (c) He works hardily to earn money. (d) He works hardened to earn money.
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Hard" itself is the adverb meaning "with effort"; "hardly" means "almost not", which reverses the meaning. This is a favourite examiner trap.
Source: Wren & Martin, High School English Grammar

15. "I could hardly hear anything in the noise." "Hardly" here means:
(a) almost not (b) with great effort (c) very loudly (d) completely
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Hardly" is a negative adverb of degree meaning "almost not". It must not be confused with the adverb "hard".
Source: NCERT Class 9 English Grammar

16. "She is quite intelligent." The word "quite" modifies:
(a) She (b) is (c) intelligent (d) none of these
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Quite" is an adverb of degree modifying the adjective "intelligent". Adverbs can modify verbs, adjectives, and other adverbs.
Source: NCERT Class 8 English Grammar

17. Choose the correct sentence.
(a) He speaks English very good. (b) He speaks English very best. (c) He speaks English very well. (d) He speaks English very nice.
Answer: (c)
Solution: The verb "speaks" needs the adverb "well", not the adjective "good". "Very" correctly modifies the adverb "well".
Source: NCERT Class 8 English Grammar

18. "They went outside to play." "Outside" is an adverb of:
(a) place (b) time (c) manner (d) frequency
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Outside" tells where they went, so it is an adverb of place. Other examples: here, there, everywhere, up.
Source: NCERT Class 8 English Grammar

19. Spot the error: "She is (P) / enough tall (Q) / to reach the shelf. (R) / No error (S)"
(a) Part P (b) Part R (c) Part Q (d) No error
Answer: (c)
Solution: Part Q is wrong because "enough" comes after the adjective it modifies: "tall enough". Placing "enough" before the adjective is a common trap.
Source: PYQ-style

20. "He will come tomorrow." "Tomorrow" is an adverb of:
(a) time (b) manner (c) place (d) degree
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Tomorrow" tells when he will come, so it is an adverb of time. Other examples: yesterday, now, soon, lately.
Source: NCERT Class 8 English Grammar

21. Choose the correct sentence.
(a) I have seen him yesterday. (b) I saw him yesterday. (c) I had seen him yesterday. (d) I am seeing him yesterday.
Answer: (b)
Solution: Adverbs of definite past time like "yesterday" take the simple past tense, not the present perfect. "Have seen" with "yesterday" is a classic tense-adverb trap.
Source: Wren & Martin, High School English Grammar

22. "No sooner had she left ______ it started raining."
(a) when (b) then (c) than (d) that
Answer: (c)
Solution: "No sooner" is always paired with "than". "Hardly/Scarcely" take "when", but "no sooner" takes "than" — a frequently tested pair.
Source: Wren & Martin, High School English Grammar

23. Choose the correct sentence.
(a) He is wiser than his friend. (b) He is more wiser than his friend. (c) He is most wiser than his friend. (d) He is much wiser then his friend.
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Wiser" is already comparative, so adding "more" is a double comparative error. Also note "than", not "then", is used in comparisons.
Source: NCERT Class 9 English Grammar

24. "She sings ______ than her sister."
(a) sweet (b) more sweetly (c) sweetly (d) most sweetly
Answer: (b)
Solution: With "than", the comparative form of the adverb is needed. "Sweetly" forms its comparative with "more", giving "more sweetly".
Source: NCERT Class 9 English Grammar

25. Choose the correct sentence.
(a) He only told me the truth. (b) He told only me only the truth. (c) Only he told me only the truth only. (d) He told me only the truth.
Answer: (d)
Solution: The meaning intended is that he told nothing but the truth, so "only" must sit next to "the truth". Misplaced "only" changes meaning and is a standard exam trap.
Source: Wren & Martin, High School English Grammar
