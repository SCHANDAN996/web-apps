26. Choose the correctly spelt adverb.
(a) Truely (b) Trully (c) Truley (d) Truly
Answer: (d)
Solution: The adverb of "true" drops the final "e" before adding "-ly", giving "truly". "Truely" is a very common spelling trap.
Source: NCERT Class 9 English Grammar

27. Identify the adverb in the sentence: "She sings beautifully."
(a) beautifully (b) sings (c) She (d) None of these
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Beautifully" modifies the verb "sings" and tells how she sings, so it is an adverb of manner.
Source: NCERT Class 8 English Grammar

28. Choose the correct sentence.
(a) He drives fastly. (b) He drives fasting. (c) He drives fast. (d) He drives fasterly.
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Fast" is both an adjective and an adverb; there is no word "fastly" in standard English.
Source: NCERT Class 8 English Grammar

29. "He always reaches office on time." The word "always" is an adverb of:
(a) manner (b) frequency (c) place (d) degree
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Always" tells how often the action happens, so it is an adverb of frequency.
Source: NCERT Class 8 English Grammar

30. Choose the correct option: "The movie was ______ interesting."
(a) really (b) real (c) reality (d) realise
Answer: (a)
Solution: An adverb is needed to modify the adjective "interesting"; "really" is the adverb form of "real".

31. Identify the adverb of place: "Come here and sit beside me."
(a) Come (b) here (c) sit (d) beside
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Here" answers the question "where?" and modifies the verb "come", so it is an adverb of place. "Beside" is a preposition here.
Source: NCERT Class 8 English Grammar

32. Choose the correct sentence.
(a) She speaks English fluent. (b) She speaks English fluently. (c) She speaks English fluency. (d) She speaks English fluenter.
Answer: (b)
Solution: The verb "speaks" needs an adverb; "fluently" is the adverb of "fluent".
Source: NCERT Class 9 English Grammar

33. "I have ______ finished my homework." Choose the correct adverb.
(a) yet (b) since (c) already (d) ever
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Already" is used in affirmative present perfect sentences to show completion earlier than expected. "Yet" is used in negatives and questions.
Source: NCERT Class 10 English Grammar

34. Spot the error: "He behaved (P) / very cowardly (Q) / in the meeting (R) / yesterday. (S)"
(a) Part P (b) No error (c) Part R (d) Part Q
Answer: (d)
Solution: Part Q is wrong. "Cowardly" is an adjective, not an adverb; the correct phrase is "in a cowardly manner". This is a classic examiner trap because words ending in "-ly" look like adverbs.
Source: PYQ-style

35. Choose the correct sentence.
(a) He works hardly to support his family. (b) He works hard to support his family. (c) He works hardful to support his family. (d) He works hardness to support his family.
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Hard" is the adverb meaning "with effort"; "hardly" means "almost not", which changes the meaning completely.
Source: NCERT Class 9 English Grammar

36. "The train arrived late." Here "late" is:
(a) an adjective (b) a conjunction (c) an adverb (d) a preposition
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Late" modifies the verb "arrived" and tells when, so it is an adverb of time. "Late" works as both adjective and adverb.
Source: NCERT Class 8 English Grammar

37. Choose the correct option: "She is ______ tired to walk any further."
(a) very (b) too (c) much (d) so
Answer: (b)
Solution: The structure "too + adjective + to" expresses excess with a negative result; "very" cannot be used in this pattern.
Source: NCERT Class 10 English Grammar

38. Identify the type of adverb: "He is extremely intelligent."
(a) adverb of time (b) adverb of place (c) adverb of degree (d) adverb of frequency
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Extremely" tells to what extent he is intelligent, modifying the adjective "intelligent", so it is an adverb of degree.
Source: NCERT Class 8 English Grammar

39. Choose the correct sentence.
(a) I am very much sorry. (b) I am so much sorryly. (c) I am much very sorry. (d) I am very sorry.
Answer: (d)
Solution: "Very" modifies the adjective "sorry" directly; "very much" is used with verbs, not before adjectives in this pattern.
Source: NCERT Class 9 English Grammar

40. "He looked at me angrily." The adverb "angrily" modifies:
(a) me (b) He (c) at (d) looked
Answer: (d)
Solution: "Angrily" describes the manner of the verb "looked", telling how he looked.
Source: NCERT Class 8 English Grammar

41. Choose the correct sentence.
(a) He is enough tall to reach the shelf. (b) He is too enough tall to reach the shelf. (c) He is tall enough to reach the shelf. (d) He is enoughly tall to reach the shelf.
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Enough" as an adverb comes after the adjective it modifies: "tall enough". Placing it before the adjective is a frequent error.
Source: NCERT Class 10 English Grammar

42. Spot the error: "She only told (P) / me the truth (Q) / when I (R) / insisted. (S)" — meaning she told nothing except the truth.
(a) No error (b) Part Q (c) Part R (d) Part P
Answer: (d)
Solution: Part P is wrong for the intended meaning. "Only" should be placed next to the word it limits: "She told me only the truth". Misplaced "only" changes meaning — a favourite examiner trap.
Source: PYQ-style

43. Choose the correct option: "______ did he enter the room than the phone rang."
(a) As soon (b) Hardly (c) Scarcely (d) No sooner
Answer: (d)
Solution: "No sooner... than" is the correct correlative pair. "Hardly" and "scarcely" pair with "when", not "than".
Source: NCERT Class 10 English Grammar

44. Choose the correct sentence.
(a) He hardly goes there, doesn't he? (b) He hardly goes there, isn't he? (c) He hardly goes there, does he? (d) He hardly goes there, won't he?
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Hardly" is negative in meaning, so the question tag must be positive: "does he?". Using a negative tag is a common trap.
Source: NCERT Class 10 English Grammar

45. Choose the correct option: "The patient is ______ better today."
(a) very (b) much (c) too (d) so
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Much" is used before comparative adjectives ("much better"); "very" modifies positive degree, not comparatives.
Source: NCERT Class 10 English Grammar

46. Choose the correct sentence.
(a) He explained the problem clearly to me. (b) He explained clearly to me the problem yesterday evening slowly. (c) He explained me clearly the problem. (d) He explained the problem me clearly.
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Explain" takes "explain something to somebody", and the adverb "clearly" sits naturally after the object. Option (a) follows correct word order.
Source: NCERT Class 10 English Grammar

47. Spot the error: "He ran (P) / quick enough (Q) / to catch (R) / the bus. (S)"
(a) Part Q (b) Part P (c) Part R (d) No error
Answer: (a)
Solution: Part Q is wrong. The verb "ran" needs the adverb "quickly", not the adjective "quick": "He ran quickly enough to catch the bus."
Source: PYQ-style

48. Choose the correct option: "I ______ go to the gym — maybe twice a year."
(a) seldom (b) often (c) always (d) usually
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Twice a year" indicates rarity, so "seldom" (rarely) fits. The other options suggest frequent action.
Source: NCERT Class 9 English Grammar

49. Choose the correct sentence.
(a) He is much wiser than his brother. (b) He is more wiser than his brother. (c) He is very wiser than his brother. (d) He is too wiser than his brother.
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Much" correctly intensifies the comparative "wiser". "More wiser" is a double comparative, and "very/too" cannot modify comparatives.
Source: NCERT Class 10 English Grammar

50. Choose the correct option: "______ the bell rang, the students rushed out."
(a) As soon as (b) No sooner (c) Hardly when (d) Scarcely than
Answer: (a)
Solution: "As soon as" correctly introduces the time clause without inversion. "No sooner" needs "than" and inversion; "hardly" pairs with "when" but also needs inversion ("Hardly had the bell rung...").
Source: NCERT Class 10 English Grammar
