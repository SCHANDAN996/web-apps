🧒 FEYNMAN TECHNIQUE — Explain It Like I'm 12

**The Hardest Concept: Adverb Position & "Enough" — Where Does the Adverb Go?**

Imagine you are telling your friend about a cricket match. You say:

❌ "Virat plays well cricket."
✅ "Virat plays cricket well."

Why did the first one sound wrong? Because in English, the adverb is like a polite guest at a dinner party — it knows exactly where to sit, and it never pushes between the verb and its object.

**The Dinner Party Rule 🍽️**

Think of a sentence as a dinner table:
- The **Verb** is the host ("plays").
- The **Object** is the guest of honour ("cricket").
- The **Adverb** is the polite guest ("well").

The polite guest NEVER sits between the host and the guest of honour. It waits at the end of the table.

❌ "She speaks fluently English." (Adverb crashed between verb and object!)
✅ "She speaks English fluently."

**Now the Trickiest Guest: "Enough" 🎯**

"Enough" is a shape-shifter. Its seat changes depending on WHO it is describing:

- With an **adjective/adverb**, it sits AFTER: "He is tall **enough**." / "She ran fast **enough**."
- With a **noun**, it sits BEFORE: "We have **enough** money."

**Unforgettable Trick:** 🧠 **"Enough is a tail for describing words, a hat for naming words."**
- Describing word (adjective/adverb) → enough is the TAIL (comes after): "good enough", "quickly enough"
- Naming word (noun) → enough is the HAT (comes before): "enough time", "enough water"

**One More Daily-Life Example: "Only" — The Meaning-Changer**

Watch how moving ONE adverb changes the whole story:
- "**Only** I lent him ₹100 yesterday." (Nobody else lent him money.)
- "I **only** lent him ₹100 yesterday." (I lent it — I didn't gift it.)
- "I lent him ₹100 **only** yesterday." (It happened just yesterday.)

Same words, different seats, different meanings. That's why examiners love "only" — one wrong seat and the sentence is wrong.
