51. Choose the correctly spelt adverb.
(a) truly (b) truely (c) trully (d) trueley
Answer: (a)
Solution: Adjectives ending in "-ue" drop the final "e" before adding "-ly": true → truly. The other spellings wrongly retain or double letters.
Source: NCERT Class 9 English Grammar

52. Identify the adverb in the sentence: "She sings beautifully."
(a) She (b) beautifully (c) sings (d) None of these
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Beautifully" modifies the verb "sings" and tells how she sings, so it is an adverb of manner.
Source: NCERT Class 8 English Grammar

53. Choose the correct sentence.
(a) He runs fastly. (b) He runs fast. (c) He runs fasting. (d) He runs fasterly.
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Fast" is both an adjective and an adverb; "fastly" does not exist in standard English. Parts with "-ly" added to fast are wrong.
Source: NCERT Class 8 English Grammar

54. "The train arrived late." Here "late" is —
(a) an adverb (b) an adjective (c) a noun (d) a conjunction
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Late" modifies the verb "arrived" and tells when, so it is an adverb of time. "Late" works as both adjective and adverb without change of form.
Source: NCERT Class 9 English Grammar

55. Select the adverb of frequency.
(a) quickly (b) always (c) there (d) very
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Always" tells how often an action happens, so it is an adverb of frequency. "Quickly" is manner, "there" is place, "very" is degree.
Source: NCERT Class 8 English Grammar

56. Choose the correct position of the adverb: "She ______ comes late to class."
(a) seldomly (b) seldom (c) seldomness (d) seld
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Seldom" is the correct adverb of frequency and sits before the main verb. "Seldomly" is not a standard word.
Source: NCERT Class 9 English Grammar

57. "He is very tall." The word "very" modifies —
(a) he (b) is (c) none of these (d) tall
Answer: (d)
Solution: "Very" is an adverb of degree modifying the adjective "tall". Adverbs can modify adjectives as well as verbs.
Source: NCERT Class 8 English Grammar

58. Choose the sentence with an adverb of place.
(a) She spoke softly. (b) He rarely eats out. (c) Come here at once. (d) They almost won.
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Here" answers the question "where?", so it is an adverb of place. "Softly" is manner, "rarely" frequency, "almost" degree.
Source: NCERT Class 8 English Grammar

59. Identify the adverb of time.
(a) upward (b) honestly (c) quite (d) yesterday
Answer: (d)
Solution: "Yesterday" tells when an action happened, so it is an adverb of time. "Honestly" is manner and "quite" is degree.
Source: NCERT Class 8 English Grammar

60. Choose the correct sentence.
(a) He did the work well. (b) He did the work good. (c) He did the work goodly. (d) He did the work best.
Answer: (a)
Solution: The verb "did" needs an adverb, and the adverb form of "good" is "well". "Good" is an adjective and cannot modify a verb.
Source: NCERT Class 9 English Grammar

61. Spot the error: Part P: He is enough tall / Part Q: to reach / Part R: the top shelf.
(a) No error (b) Part Q (c) Part R (d) Part P
Answer: (d)
Solution: "Enough" must come after the adjective it modifies: "tall enough", not "enough tall". Part P is therefore wrong.
Source: PYQ-style

62. Choose the correct sentence.
(a) He only earns five hundred rupees a day. (b) Only he earns five hundred rupees a day only. (c) He earns only five hundred rupees a day. (d) He earns five hundred rupees a day only earns.
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Only" should be placed immediately before the word it limits — here "five hundred rupees". Misplaced "only" changes the meaning, a favourite examiner trap.
Source: PYQ-style

63. Spot the error: Part P: She sings / Part Q: more sweeter / Part R: than her sister.
(a) Part P (b) Part R (c) Part Q (d) No error
Answer: (c)
Solution: Double comparison is wrong. Use either "sweeter" or "more sweet", not "more sweeter". Part Q contains the error.
Source: PYQ-style

64. "He works hard." and "He hardly works." The pair shows that —
(a) hard is the adverb of effort; hardly means scarcely (b) hardly means with great effort (c) hard and hardly mean the same (d) hardly is an adjective
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Hard" as an adverb means "with effort", while "hardly" means "scarcely/almost not". Confusing the two is a classic trap.
Source: NCERT Class 10 English Grammar

65. Choose the correct sentence.
(a) I have seen him yesterday. (b) I seen him yesterday. (c) I had saw him yesterday. (d) I saw him yesterday.
Answer: (d)
Solution: Adverbs of definite past time like "yesterday" take the simple past, not the present perfect. Hence "I saw him yesterday."
Source: NCERT Class 10 English Grammar

66. Spot the error: Part P: The teacher asked / Part Q: that why / Part R: he was late.
(a) Part P (b) No error (c) Part R (d) Part Q
Answer: (d)
Solution: In indirect speech "that" and "why" cannot be used together; only "why" is needed. Part Q is faulty.
Source: PYQ-style

67. Choose the correct adverb order: "She sang ______."
(a) beautifully yesterday at the concert (b) beautifully at the concert yesterday (c) at the concert beautifully yesterday (d) yesterday beautifully at the concert
Answer: (b)
Solution: The standard order is Manner → Place → Time (MPT): "beautifully" (manner), "at the concert" (place), "yesterday" (time).
Source: NCERT Class 10 English Grammar

68. "He is too weak to walk." Here "too" expresses —
(a) sufficiency (b) addition (c) excess leading to a negative result (d) frequency
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Too" means "more than is desirable", giving a negative sense — so weak that he cannot walk. "Enough" would express sufficiency.
Source: NCERT Class 9 English Grammar

69. Choose the correct sentence.
(a) He is clever enough to solve it. (b) He is enough clever to solve it. (c) He enough is clever to solve it. (d) Enough he is clever to solve it.
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Enough" follows the adjective it modifies: "clever enough". Placing it before the adjective is a common examiner trap.
Source: PYQ-style

70. Spot the error: Part P: No sooner did he arrive / Part Q: when / Part R: the meeting started.
(a) Part P (b) Part Q (c) Part R (d) No error
Answer: (b)
Solution: "No sooner" is paired with "than", not "when". "When" follows "hardly/scarcely". Part Q must be corrected to "than".
Source: PYQ-style

71. Choose the correct sentence.
(a) He speaks English fluent. (b) He speaks English fluentlyly. (c) He speaks English fluently. (d) He speaks English fluenter.
Answer: (c)
Solution: The verb "speaks" needs an adverb of manner; "fluent" is an adjective, so "fluently" is correct.
Source: NCERT Class 9 English Grammar

72. "The movie was rather boring." Here "rather" is an adverb of —
(a) manner (b) time (c) degree (d) place
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Rather" modifies the adjective "boring" and shows to what extent, so it is an adverb of degree.
Source: NCERT Class 9 English Grammar

73. Choose the correct sentence.
(a) She has almost finished her work. (b) She almost has finished her work. (c) Almost she has finished her work. (d) She has finished almost her work.
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Almost" should come immediately before the word it modifies — here "finished". Misplacing it distorts the meaning.
Source: PYQ-style

74. Spot the error: Part P: He behaved / Part Q: very cowardly / Part R: in the crisis.
(a) Part P (b) No error (c) Part R (d) Part Q
Answer: (d)
Solution: "Cowardly" is an adjective, not an adverb; the correct adverbial phrase is "in a cowardly manner". Words like friendly, costly, lively, cowardly end in "-ly" but are adjectives — a classic trap.
Source: PYQ-style

75. Choose the correct sentence.
(a) He has been working late lately. (b) He came lately to the office. (c) He late came lately. (d) Lately he late comes.
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Late" means after the expected time; "lately" means recently. The sentence correctly uses both with their distinct meanings.
Source: NCERT Class 10 English Grammar
