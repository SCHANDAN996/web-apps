Card 1
Front: What is an adverb?
Back: A word that modifies a verb, an adjective, another adverb, or a whole sentence. It answers how, when, where, how often, and to what extent. Example: She sings beautifully.

Card 2
Front: What are the five main types of adverbs?
Back: Manner (slowly), Time (yesterday), Place (here), Frequency (often), Degree (very). Mnemonic: "My Tall Plant Feels Dry" — Manner, Time, Place, Frequency, Degree.

Card 3
Front: Where should an adverb of frequency be placed in a sentence?
Back: Before the main verb but after the verb 'to be'. Example: He always comes late. / She is always punctual.

Card 4
Front: ⚠️ Examiner's Trap: "He plays good." — Is this correct?
Back: No. 'Good' is an adjective; it cannot modify the verb 'plays'. Correct: He plays well. Trap type: Sound-Right — 'good' sounds natural in speech but is grammatically wrong.

Card 5
Front: Which adverbs have the same form as their adjectives?
Back: Fast, hard, early, late, daily, long, straight. Example: He works hard (not 'hardly'). 'Hardly' means 'almost not'.

Card 6
Front: What is the difference between 'hard' and 'hardly'?
Back: 'Hard' = with great effort (He works hard). 'Hardly' = almost not (He hardly works). Same trap applies to late/lately, near/nearly, high/highly.

Card 7
Front: What is the correct order when multiple adverbs appear together?
Back: Manner → Place → Time (MPT). Example: She sang beautifully (M) at the concert (P) yesterday (T). Mnemonic: "My Pet Tiger."

Card 8
Front: ⚠️ Examiner's Trap: "He is enough tall to reach the shelf." — Spot the error.
Back: 'Enough' comes AFTER the adjective it modifies. Correct: He is tall enough to reach the shelf. But with nouns it comes before: enough money.

Card 9
Front: Where is 'only' placed, and why does it matter?
Back: 'Only' must sit immediately before the word it limits — position changes meaning. "Only he lent me ₹500" (no one else did) vs "He lent me only ₹500" (not more than ₹500).

Card 10
Front: Can two negative words be used in one clause in standard English?
Back: No. Double negatives are wrong: "He did not go nowhere" ❌ → "He did not go anywhere" ✔. Also avoid 'not' with hardly/scarcely/seldom: "I can hardly believe it" (not "can't hardly").

Card 11
Front: What is a conjunctive adverb? Give three examples.
Back: An adverb that connects two independent clauses, preceded by a semicolon. Examples: however, therefore, moreover, consequently. Example: He was ill; therefore, he stayed home.

Card 12
Front: What is a sentence adverb?
Back: An adverb that comments on the whole sentence, usually at the start with a comma: Fortunately, Clearly, Obviously, Unfortunately. Example: Fortunately, nobody was hurt.

Card 13
Front: How do you form the comparative and superlative of adverbs like 'quickly'?
Back: With more/most: quickly → more quickly → most quickly. Short adverbs take -er/-est: fast → faster → fastest; hard → harder → hardest.

Card 14
Front: ⚠️ Examiner's Trap: "She sings more sweeter than her sister." — What is wrong?
Back: Double comparative — 'more' + '-er' together is wrong. Correct: She sings sweeter (or more sweetly) than her sister. Trap type: Over-thinking — students add 'more' to sound emphatic.

Card 15
Front: What is the difference between 'too' and 'very'?
Back: 'Very' = to a high degree (neutral/positive): The tea is very hot. 'Too' = more than desirable (negative, often with 'to'): The tea is too hot to drink.

Card 16
Front: When do we use 'already' vs 'yet'?
Back: 'Already' — affirmative sentences, usually mid-position: I have already finished. 'Yet' — negatives and questions, at the end: Have you finished yet? / I haven't finished yet.

Card 17
Front: Mnemonic: How do you remember adverbs of definite vs indefinite frequency?
Back: Definite (daily, weekly, twice) go at the END: "I go there twice." Indefinite (always, often, seldom) go MID-sentence: "I often go there." Trick: "Definite Ends, Indefinite Inside."

Card 18
Front: What is wrong with "He returned back home"?
Back: 'Back' is redundant — 'return' already means 'go back'. Correct: He returned home. Same trap: repeat again, revert back, recall back.

Card 19
Front: Which verbs are followed by adjectives, not adverbs?
Back: Linking verbs of sense/state: look, feel, smell, taste, sound, seem, appear. Example: The soup tastes good (not 'well'). But: She tasted the soup carefully (action verb → adverb).

Card 20
Front: Mnemonic: How do you remember that 'seldom, rarely, hardly, scarcely' are already negative?
Back: Trick: "SHRS = Silent Haters of 'not'" — Seldom, Hardly, Rarely, Scarcely never take 'not'. "He seldom comes" ✔, "He does not seldom come" ❌.
