# 📋 Important Grammar Rules & Tips — Adverb

## (a) 12 High-Yield Rules

| # | Rule | Example | Examiner's Trap |
|---|------|---------|-----------------|
| 1 | An adverb modifies a verb, adjective, or another adverb — never a noun. | She sings **beautifully**. (NOT "beautiful sings") | "He is a real good player" ❌ → "really good" ✔ (adjective needs adverb) |
| 2 | Linking verbs (be, seem, look, feel, taste, smell, sound) take an **adjective**, not an adverb. | The soup tastes **good**. | "She looks beautifully" ❌ — looks = linking verb here → "beautiful" ✔ |
| 3 | Adverbs of manner usually end in **-ly**, but *fast, hard, late, early, daily* have the same form as adjectives. | He works **hard**. | "He works hardly" ❌ — *hardly* = almost not! |
| 4 | **Hardly / Scarcely** take *when*; **No sooner** takes *than*. | No sooner had she left **than** it rained. | "No sooner… when" ❌ — the classic pairing trap. |
| 5 | **Enough** comes *after* the adjective/adverb it modifies, but *before* a noun. | tall **enough**; **enough** money | "enough tall" ❌ — word-order trap. |
| 6 | **Too** = more than desirable (negative sense); **very** = high degree (neutral). | It is **too** hot to go out. | "I am too glad to see you" ❌ → "very glad" ✔ |
| 7 | Adverbs of frequency (always, often, never) go **before** the main verb but **after** 'be'. | He **always** comes late. / She is **always** late. | "He comes always late" ❌ — position trap. |
| 8 | **Only** must sit next to the word it limits — position changes meaning. | **Only** Ravi passed. / Ravi passed **only** one test. | Exams move "only" and ask which sentence is correct. |
| 9 | **Else** takes *but*; **other/other than** takes *than*. | It is nothing else **but** pride. | "nothing else than" ❌ — fixed-pair trap. |
| 10 | **Much** modifies comparatives & past participles; **very** modifies positives & present participles. | **much** better; **very** good; **much** surprised | "very better" ❌ — the most repeated error-spotting item. |
| 11 | **Since** (point of time) vs **ago** (past, with simple past) vs **before** — never mix. | He left two years **ago**. | "He has left two years ago" ❌ — ago kills present perfect. |
| 12 | Two negatives make a positive — avoid *not… hardly/scarcely/seldom/never* together. | I can hardly believe it. | "He cannot hardly walk" ❌ — double-negative trap. |

## (b) Time Management — The 15/45-Second Rule

**⚡ Fact-based questions (target: 15 seconds)**
- Spot-the-error items testing Rules 3, 4, 9, 10, 12 (hard/hardly, no sooner…than, else…but, much/very, double negative). These are fixed pairs — see the keyword, mark the answer, move on.
- Fill-in-the-blank with *enough / too / very / ago / since* — one keyword decides it.
- Adjective-vs-adverb choice after a linking verb (Rule 2).

**🔍 Inference-based questions (target: 45 seconds)**
- Sentence-improvement items where **only** / adverb position changes meaning (Rule 8) — read the whole sentence twice.
- Questions mixing tense with adverbs of time (Rule 11) — check the verb form first, then the adverb.
- "Which sentence is grammatically correct?" items with four near-identical options — eliminate by one rule at a time; never guess on sound.

**Rule of thumb:** If the question hinges on one fixed pair (no sooner…than), answer in 15 sec. If it hinges on meaning or position, invest 45 sec — these are the marks most candidates lose.

## (c) Morphology — How Adverbs Are Built

| Root / Base | Affix | Adverb Form | Example Sentence |
|---|---|---|---|
| quick (adj.) | -ly | quickly | She replied quickly. |
| happy (adj., ends in -y) | -y → -i + -ly | happily | They lived happily. |
| true (adj., ends in -e) | -e dropped + -ly | truly | I am truly sorry. |
| full (adj., ends in -ll) | -ly (one 'l' drops) | fully | The hall was fully booked. |
| economic (adj., ends in -ic) | -ally | economically | The plan is economically sound. |
| day (noun) | -ly | daily | The paper is published daily. |
| home (noun) | -ward(s) | homeward(s) | He walked homewards. |
| time (noun) | -wise | timewise | Timewise, we are on schedule. |

**Memory key:** *-ly* is the default; watch the three spelling shifts — **y→i** (happy→happily), **drop -e** (true→truly), **-ic→-ically** (economic→economically). And remember the rebels that never take -ly: **fast, hard, late, early, straight**.
