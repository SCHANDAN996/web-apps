76. Choose the correctly framed sentence.
(a) He speaks very fluently English. (b) He speaks English very fluently. (c) He very speaks English fluently. (d) He speaks English fluently very.
Answer: (b)
Solution: Adverbs of manner usually follow the verb and its object: "speaks English fluently"; the intensifier "very" precedes the adverb. Placing the adverb between verb and object breaks standard word order.
Source: NCERT Class 10 English Grammar

77. Spot the error: Part P: She sings / Part Q: more sweeter / Part R: than her sister. / Part S: No error
(a) Part P (b) No error (c) Part R (d) Part Q
Answer: (d)
Solution: Part Q is wrong. "Sweeter" is already comparative, so "more" is redundant; since "sings" needs an adverb, use "more sweetly". Double comparatives are a classic trap.
Source: PYQ-style

78. Choose the correct sentence.
(a) He goes there hardly. (b) He goes hardly there. (c) Hardly he goes there daily. (d) He hardly goes there.
Answer: (d)
Solution: "Hardly" (meaning "almost not") comes before the main verb. Putting it after the verb or at the end misplaces it and distorts the meaning.
Source: NCERT Class 9 English Grammar

79. Spot the error: Part P: The train arrived / Part Q: enough late / Part R: to miss the connection. / Part S: No error
(a) Part Q (b) Part P (c) Part R (d) No error
Answer: (a)
Solution: Part Q is wrong. "Enough" as an adverb comes after the word it modifies: "late enough". This traps Hindi speakers because in Hindi the equivalent word comes first.
Source: PYQ-style

80. Choose the correctly placed adverb: "She ______ visits her grandmother every Sunday."
(a) is always (b) always is (c) always (d) visits always
Answer: (c)
Solution: Adverbs of frequency come before the main verb: "She always visits." They follow the verb "be", but "visits" is a main verb here.
Source: NCERT Class 8 English Grammar

81. Spot the error: Part P: He is / Part Q: enough tall / Part R: to reach the shelf. / Part S: No error
(a) Part P (b) Part Q (c) Part R (d) No error
Answer: (b)
Solution: Part Q is wrong. "Enough" follows the adjective it modifies: "tall enough". The misplaced "enough" is one of the most repeated traps in these exams.
Source: PYQ-style

82. Choose the correct sentence.
(a) I am seeing that film yesterday. (b) I have seen that film yesterday. (c) I had seen that film yesterday. (d) I saw that film yesterday.
Answer: (d)
Solution: Definite past-time adverbs like "yesterday" require the simple past, not the present perfect. "Have seen" cannot combine with "yesterday".
Source: NCERT Class 10 English Grammar

83. Choose the correct word: "The patient is ______ better today."
(a) very (b) much (c) too (d) so
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Much" modifies comparative adjectives ("much better"). "Very" modifies only the positive degree, so "very better" is wrong — a frequent trap.
Source: PYQ-style

84. Spot the error: Part P: Scarcely he had / Part Q: entered the room / Part R: when the phone rang. / Part S: No error
(a) Part P (b) Part Q (c) Part R (d) No error
Answer: (a)
Solution: Part P is wrong. When "scarcely" begins a sentence, inversion is required: "Scarcely had he entered..." The subject-auxiliary order must flip.
Source: PYQ-style

85. Choose the correct sentence.
(a) No sooner did she arrive than it rained. (b) No sooner she arrived than it rained. (c) No sooner did she arrive when it rained. (d) No sooner she had arrived than it rained.
Answer: (a)
Solution: "No sooner" takes inversion ("did she arrive") and pairs with "than", never "when". "Hardly/Scarcely" pair with "when"; mixing them is a classic trap.
Source: PYQ-style

86. Choose the correct word: "He works ______ to support his family."
(a) hardly (b) harder enough (c) hard (d) most hardly
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Hard" is both adjective and adverb meaning "with effort". "Hardly" means "almost not", so it would reverse the meaning — a sound-alike trap.
Source: NCERT Class 9 English Grammar

87. Spot the error: Part P: She only told / Part Q: me the truth / Part R: yesterday evening. / Part S: No error
(a) No error (b) Part Q (c) Part R (d) Part P
Answer: (d)
Solution: Part P is wrong. "Only" should stand immediately before the word it limits — here "the truth": "She told me only the truth." Placed before "told", it wrongly suggests telling was the only thing she did.
Source: PYQ-style

88. Choose the correct sentence.
(a) He is much good to pass. (b) He is too good to not pass. (c) He is so good to pass. (d) He is too good not to pass.
Answer: (d)
Solution: "Too...to" with a negative infinitive ("not to pass") expresses that his goodness guarantees passing. The split infinitive "to not pass" is treated as faulty in exam grammar.
Source: PYQ-style

89. Choose the correct word: "The movie was ______ interesting that I watched it twice."
(a) very (b) so (c) too (d) much
Answer: (b)
Solution: The correlative pattern is "so...that" for result clauses. "Too...that" is ungrammatical; "too" pairs with "to".
Source: NCERT Class 10 English Grammar

90. Spot the error: Part P: He behaved / Part Q: very cowardly / Part R: during the crisis. / Part S: No error
(a) Part P (b) Part Q (c) Part R (d) No error
Answer: (b)
Solution: Part Q is wrong. "Cowardly" is an adjective, not an adverb; the adverbial phrase is "in a cowardly manner". Words like "friendly, costly, cowardly" end in -ly but are adjectives — a favourite trap.
Source: PYQ-style

91. Choose the correct sentence.
(a) She sings most beautiful. (b) She sings beautiful. (c) She sings beautifully. (d) She beautiful sings.
Answer: (c)
Solution: The verb "sings" needs an adverb of manner, "beautifully". Using the adjective "beautiful" after an action verb is a common error among Hindi speakers.
Source: NCERT Class 8 English Grammar

92. Choose the correct word: "I ______ know him; we met only once."
(a) bare (b) nearly (c) barely (d) quite
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Barely" means "only just / almost not", fitting "we met only once". "Bare" is an adjective meaning uncovered; "nearly" and "quite" contradict the context.
Source: PYQ-style

93. Spot the error: Part P: Never I have seen / Part Q: such a magnificent / Part R: sunset before. / Part S: No error
(a) Part P (b) Part Q (c) Part R (d) No error
Answer: (a)
Solution: Part P is wrong. Negative adverbs at the start force inversion: "Never have I seen". Keeping normal order after "Never" is a frequent exam error.
Source: PYQ-style

94. Choose the correct sentence.
(a) He came lately to the office. (b) He has been coming late to the office lately. (c) He late comes to office lately. (d) He is lately come to office.
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Late" means after the expected time; "lately" means recently. The correct sentence uses both properly: "coming late... lately". Confusing the pair is a classic trap.
Source: PYQ-style

95. Choose the correct word: "The two brothers love each other ______."
(a) deeply (b) deep (c) deepest (d) deepening
Answer: (a)
Solution: With abstract or emotional senses, the -ly adverb is used: "love deeply". "Deep" as an adverb suits literal physical depth ("dig deep").
Source: NCERT Class 10 English Grammar

96. Spot the error: Part P: He is / Part Q: very much / Part R: sorry for his mistake. / Part S: No error
(a) Part Q (b) No error (c) Part R (d) Part P
Answer: (a)
Solution: Part Q is wrong. "Very much" modifies verbs, not adjectives like "sorry"; use "very sorry". "Very much sorry" is a common Indian-English error tested often.
Source: PYQ-style

97. Choose the correct sentence.
(a) Seldom he visits his village. (b) He seldom does visits his village. (c) Seldom does he visit his village. (d) Seldom he does visit his village.
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Seldom" at the start requires inversion with the auxiliary: "Seldom does he visit". After inversion the main verb returns to base form ("visit", not "visits").
Source: PYQ-style

98. Choose the correct word: "The examination was ______ difficult; most students failed."
(a) fairly (b) little (c) quite enough (d) rather
Answer: (d)
Solution: "Rather" is used with unfavourable ideas ("rather difficult"), while "fairly" suits favourable ones ("fairly good"). The failing majority signals a negative judgement.
Source: PYQ-style

99. Spot the error: Part P: He drove / Part Q: fastly through / Part R: the crowded market. / Part S: No error
(a) Part P (b) Part R (c) Part Q (d) No error
Answer: (c)
Solution: Part Q is wrong. "Fast" is both adjective and adverb; "fastly" does not exist in standard English. Use "drove fast".
Source: NCERT Class 9 English Grammar

100. Choose the correct sentence.
(a) Not only does he sing but he also dances. (b) Not only he does sing but also dances. (c) Not only he sings but also dances. (d) He not only sings but also he dances well.
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Not only" at the start requires inversion ("does he sing"), and the correlative pair is "not only... but also". The other options either skip inversion or break parallel structure.
Source: PYQ-style
