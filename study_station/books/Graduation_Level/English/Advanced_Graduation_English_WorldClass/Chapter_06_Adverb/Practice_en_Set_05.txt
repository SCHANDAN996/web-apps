101. Choose the correctly framed sentence.
(a) He speaks English very fluently. (b) He speaks very fluently English. (c) He very speaks fluently English. (d) He fluently very speaks English.
Answer: (a)
Solution: When manner adverbs modify a verb with an object, the adverb goes after the object: "speaks English very fluently". Placing the adverb between verb and object is wrong.
Source: NCERT Class 10 English Grammar

102. Spot the error: She (P) / sings enough well (Q) / to win the competition. (R) / No error (S)
(a) Part P (b) No error (c) Part R (d) Part Q
Answer: (d)
Solution: "Enough" as an adverb comes after the adjective or adverb it modifies: "well enough", not "enough well". Part Q is wrong.

103. Choose the correct sentence.
(a) He too is good to pass. (b) He is too good to not pass. (c) He is good too to pass. (d) He is too good not to pass.
Answer: (d)
Solution: The structure "too + adjective + not to + verb" means "so good that he cannot fail". The split "to not pass" is non-standard in exams.
Source: PYQ-style

104. Spot the error: I (P) / have been working here (Q) / since five years. (R) / No error (S)
(a) Part R (b) Part Q (c) Part P (d) No error
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Since" is used with a point of time; a period of time takes "for": "for five years". Part R is wrong.
Source: NCERT Class 9 English Grammar

105. Choose the word that correctly completes: The patient is ______ better today.
(a) very (b) much (c) too (d) enough
Answer: (b)
Solution: Comparative degree ("better") is modified by "much", not "very". "Very" modifies the positive degree only.

106. Choose the correct sentence.
(a) He only lent me ten rupees. (b) Only he lent me ten rupees. (c) He lent me only ten rupees. (d) He lent only me ten rupees.
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Only" must be placed next to the word it limits. Here it limits "ten rupees", so it goes immediately before it. Other placements change the meaning.

107. Spot the error: No sooner (P) / she had entered the room (Q) / than the lights went off. (R) / No error (S)
(a) Part P (b) Part Q (c) Part R (d) No error
Answer: (b)
Solution: "No sooner" requires inversion: "No sooner had she entered". Part Q lacks the inversion.

108. Choose the correct option: He works ______ hard ______ fail.
(a) too … to (b) so … that (c) very … to (d) much … that
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Too … to" expresses an excessive degree with a negative result: "too hard to fail" is grammatically framed; the intended exam structure is too + adverb + to + verb.

109. Choose the correctly spelt and correctly used adverb.
(a) He behaved cowardly. (b) He behaved in a cowardly manner. (c) He behaved cowardlily. (d) He behaved coward.
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Cowardly" is an adjective, not an adverb; there is no adverb "cowardlily". The correct adverbial form is "in a cowardly manner".

110. Spot the error: She (P) / is comparatively (Q) / more happier today. (R) / No error (S)
(a) Part P (b) Part Q (c) No error (d) Part R
Answer: (d)
Solution: Double comparative is wrong: "happier" already shows comparison, so "more" must be dropped. Part R is wrong.

111. Choose the correct sentence.
(a) Hardly he had slept when the phone rang. (b) Hardly he slept when the phone rang. (c) Hardly had he slept when the phone rang. (d) Hardly had he slept than the phone rang.
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Hardly" at the start needs inversion (had he) and pairs with "when", not "than". Only option (c) satisfies both.

112. Choose the correct option: This is ______ the best book I have read.
(a) too far (b) so far (c) far too (d) by far
Answer: (d)
Solution: "By far" is used with superlatives to mean "by a great margin". "So far" means "until now" and does not fit.

113. Spot the error: He (P) / seldom or ever (Q) / visits his uncle. (R) / No error (S)
(a) Part P (b) Part R (c) Part Q (d) No error
Answer: (c)
Solution: The correct idiom is "seldom if ever" or "seldom or never". "Seldom or ever" is wrong. Part Q is wrong.

114. Choose the correct sentence.
(a) I am very obliged to you. (b) I am much obliged to you. (c) I am too obliged to you. (d) I am enough obliged to you.
Answer: (b)
Solution: Past participles used predicatively (obliged, pleased, surprised) take "much", not "very". Hence "much obliged".

115. Choose the correct option: The train arrived ______ late, so we missed the meeting.
(a) very much (b) too much (c) much too (d) so much
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Much too" modifies an adjective/adverb (much too late); "too much" modifies a noun or verb. Here "late" is an adverb, so "much too" fits.
Source: PYQ-style

116. Spot the error: Scarcely (P) / had he finished his speech (Q) / when the audience started clapping. (R) / No error (S)
(a) Part P (b) Part Q (c) No error (d) Part R
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Scarcely had he finished … when …" follows correct inversion and correct pairing with "when". The sentence is error-free — a trap for those who expect "than".

117. Choose the correct sentence.
(a) He is tall enough to reach the shelf. (b) He is enough tall to reach the shelf. (c) He is too tall enough to reach the shelf. (d) He is so tall enough to reach the shelf.
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Enough" follows the adjective it modifies: "tall enough". Placing it before the adjective is a classic examiner trap.

118. Choose the correct option: ______ did she realise her mistake.
(a) Later only (b) Only later (c) Only she later (d) She only later
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Only later" at the beginning causes inversion: "Only later did she realise". The other options break the inversion rule.

119. Spot the error: He (P) / plays cricket (Q) / more faster than his brother. (R) / No error (S)
(a) Part R (b) Part Q (c) Part P (d) No error
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Faster" is already comparative; adding "more" makes a double comparative. Part R should read "faster than".

120. Choose the correct sentence.
(a) He denied flatly the charge. (b) He denied the charge flatly always. (c) He flatly denied the charge. (d) Flatly he denied always the charge.
Answer: (c)
Solution: The adverb "flatly" (meaning completely) is correctly placed before the verb it modifies: "flatly denied". Note "flatly" is not the adverb of "flat" in the literal sense.

121. Choose the correct option: The two brothers love ______ deeply.
(a) mutually (b) one another (c) themselves (d) each other
Answer: (d)
Solution: Traditionally "each other" is used for two persons and "one another" for more than two. Here there are two brothers, so "each other" is correct.

122. Spot the error: Never before (P) / I have seen (Q) / such a beautiful sunset. (R) / No error (S)
(a) Part Q (b) Part P (c) Part R (d) No error
Answer: (a)
Solution: A negative adverbial at the start requires inversion: "Never before have I seen". Part Q is wrong.

123. Choose the correct sentence.
(a) He is very sorry for his mistake. (b) He is very much sorry for his mistake. (c) He is much very sorry for his mistake. (d) He is too much sorry for his mistake.
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Sorry" is an adjective in the positive degree, so it takes "very", not "much" or "very much". "Very much" modifies verbs.

124. Choose the correct option: ______ had the meeting started ______ the power failed.

Answer: (d)
Solution: "Scarcely/Hardly" pair with "when"; "No sooner" pairs with "than". Only option (d) gives a correct pair.

125. Spot the error: He (P) / told me that (Q) / he will come yesterday. (R) / No error (S)
(a) Part P (b) Part R (c) Part Q (d) No error
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Yesterday" demands a past time reference: "he had come the day before" or "he came yesterday". "Will come" clashes with the past adverb. Part R is wrong.
