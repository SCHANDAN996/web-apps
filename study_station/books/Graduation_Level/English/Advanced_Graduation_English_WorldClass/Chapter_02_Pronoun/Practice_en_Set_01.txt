Questions 1–20: Easy | Questions 21–25: Medium

1. Choose the correct pronoun: "Neither Ravi nor his friends brought ___ books."
(a) their (b) his (c) its (d) our
Answer: (a)
Solution: With "neither...nor", the pronoun agrees with the nearer subject ("friends" — plural), so "their" is correct. "His" would agree with the farther subject, which is wrong.
Source: NCERT Class 9 English Grammar

2. "Each of the boys has done ___ homework."
(a) their (b) our (c) them (d) his
Answer: (d)
Solution: "Each of" takes a singular pronoun, so "his" is correct. "Their" is a common trap because "boys" is plural, but the head word is "each".
Source: NCERT Class 10 English Grammar

3. Choose the correct sentence:
(a) Me and my friend went to the market. (b) My friend and me went to the market. (c) My friend and I went to the market. (d) I and my friend went to the market.
Answer: (c)
Solution: As subject, use the nominative case "I", and polite order puts the other person first. "Me" is objective case and cannot be a subject.
Source: NCERT Class 8 English Grammar

4. "It is ___ who called you yesterday."
(a) me (b) myself (c) mine (d) I
Answer: (d)
Solution: After the verb "to be" (is), the nominative case "I" is used in formal grammar. "Me" is common in speech but is the examiner's favourite trap.
Source: NCERT Class 10 English Grammar

5. "The jury ___ divided in ___ opinion."
(a) is, its (b) are, their (c) is, their (d) are, its
Answer: (b)
Solution: When a collective noun's members act separately (divided opinion), it takes a plural verb and plural pronoun. "Is/its" would suit only a unanimous jury.
Source: PYQ-style

6. "One should do ___ duty honestly."
(a) one's (b) his (c) their (d) our
Answer: (a)
Solution: The pronoun for "one" is "one's" in formal English. "His" is widely used but examiners prefer strict agreement with "one".
Source: NCERT Class 10 English Grammar

7. Choose the correct reflexive pronoun: "She hurt ___ while cooking."
(a) her (b) hers (c) herself (d) she
Answer: (c)
Solution: When the subject and object are the same person, use the reflexive pronoun "herself". "Her" would mean she hurt someone else.
Source: NCERT Class 8 English Grammar

8. "___ of the two sisters is taller?"
(a) Who (b) Which (c) Whom (d) Whose
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Which" is used for selection from a definite, limited group (two sisters). "Who" is used for indefinite reference.
Source: NCERT Class 9 English Grammar

9. "This is the man ___ house was burgled."
(a) who (b) whom (c) which (d) whose
Answer: (d)
Solution: "Whose" shows possession (the man's house). "Who" is a subject and "whom" an object; neither shows ownership.
Source: NCERT Class 9 English Grammar

10. "Between you and ___, this plan will fail."
(a) I (b) mine (c) me (d) myself
Answer: (c)
Solution: After a preposition ("between"), the objective case "me" is required. "Between you and I" is a classic sound-right trap.
Source: PYQ-style

11. "Everybody should respect ___ parents."
(a) their (b) one's (c) his (d) our
Answer: (c)
Solution: In traditional exam grammar, "everybody" is singular and takes "his". "Their" is the common modern usage but is marked wrong in strict agreement questions.
Source: NCERT Class 10 English Grammar

12. "He is one of the best players ___ have come from this academy."
(a) which (b) who (c) whom (d) whose
Answer: (b)
Solution: The relative pronoun refers to "players" (persons, subject of "have come"), so "who" is correct. "Which" is for things.
Source: PYQ-style

13. Choose the correct sentence:
(a) Avail yourself of the opportunity. (b) Avail the opportunity. (c) Avail you of the opportunity. (d) Avail himself of the opportunity.
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Avail" must be followed by a reflexive pronoun + "of". Omitting the reflexive is a frequent examiner trap.
Source: PYQ-style

14. "The two brothers love ___ deeply."
(a) each other (b) one another (c) themselves (d) one other
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Each other" is used for two persons; "one another" for more than two. Since there are two brothers, (a) is correct.
Source: NCERT Class 9 English Grammar

15. "___ book is this on the desk?"
(a) Who (b) Whose (c) Whom (d) Which
Answer: (b)
Solution: The question asks about possession of the book, so "whose" is correct. "Who" and "whom" cannot modify a noun directly.
Source: NCERT Class 8 English Grammar

16. "None of the milk ___ spilt; ___ is all in the jug."
(a) was, it (b) were, they (c) was, they (d) were, it
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Milk" is uncountable, so "none of the milk" is singular — "was" and "it". The plural-looking "none" is the trap.
Source: PYQ-style

17. "Let you and ___ decide the matter."
(a) I (b) mine (c) me (d) myself
Answer: (c)
Solution: After "let" (a verb taking an object), the objective case "me" is used. "Let you and I" sounds elegant but is grammatically wrong.
Source: NCERT Class 10 English Grammar

18. "He absented ___ from the class."
(a) himself (b) his (c) him (d) he
Answer: (a)
Solution: Verbs like "absent", "pride", "avail" take a reflexive pronoun. "Him" would refer to a different person.
Source: PYQ-style

19. "It was ___ who broke the window."
(a) him (b) he (c) his (d) himself
Answer: (b)
Solution: In the cleft structure "It was... who", formal grammar requires the nominative "he". "Him" is the sound-right trap examiners plant.
Source: NCERT Class 10 English Grammar

20. "My car is costlier than ___."
(a) you (b) your (c) yours (d) yourself
Answer: (c)
Solution: The comparison is between cars, so a possessive pronoun "yours" (= your car) is needed. "Your" cannot stand alone without a noun.
Source: NCERT Class 9 English Grammar

21. "The committee ___ submitted ___ report unanimously."
(a) have, their (b) has, their (c) have, its (d) has, its
Answer: (d)
Solution: "Unanimously" shows the committee acted as one body, so singular "has" and "its" are correct. "Their" would imply divided members.
Source: PYQ-style

22. "He and ___ were present at the meeting, but the manager praised only ___."
(a) me, me (b) me, I (c) I, I (d) I, me
Answer: (d)
Solution: First blank is part of the subject ("He and I were present") — nominative; second blank is the object of "praised" — objective "me". Case depends on each blank's own role.
Source: NCERT Class 10 English Grammar

23. "Neither of the two applicants has submitted ___ documents yet."
(a) his (b) their (c) them (d) its
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Neither of" is singular and takes "his" in strict exam grammar. "Their" is the attractive trap because "applicants" is plural.
Source: PYQ-style

24. "This is the same watch ___ I lost last week."
(a) which (b) that (c) as (d) what
Answer: (b)
Solution: After "the same", use "that" when the very same thing is meant ("the same watch that I lost"). "As" follows "the same" only when similarity, not identity, is meant.
Source: NCERT Class 10 English Grammar

25. "There is no rule ___ has some exception."
(a) but (b) that (c) which (d) as
Answer: (a)
Solution: After a negative ("no rule"), "but" works as a negative relative pronoun meaning "that...not" — "no rule but has some exception" = every rule has an exception. "That" would reverse the meaning.
Source: PYQ-style
