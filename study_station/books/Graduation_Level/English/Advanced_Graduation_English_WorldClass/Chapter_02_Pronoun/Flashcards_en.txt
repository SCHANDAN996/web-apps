Card 1
Front: What is a pronoun?
Back: A word used in place of a noun to avoid repetition — e.g., he, she, it, they, who, which.

Card 2
Front: Which pronoun is correct: "Between you and ___ (I/me), this plan will fail"?
Back: me. Prepositions (between, for, with) take the objective case. Trap: "between you and I" sounds polite but is wrong.

Card 3
Front: Mnemonic — How to remember the order of personal pronouns in a sentence (good vs bad sense)?
Back: "231 for good, 123 for bad." Good deeds: You, he, I (2nd-3rd-1st). Confessing a fault: I, you, he (1st-2nd-3rd). E.g., "You, he and I will go" but "I, you and he are guilty."

Card 4
Front: "Each of the boys has/have done his/their work" — choose correctly.
Back: has … his. "Each of + plural noun" takes a singular verb and singular pronoun. Trap: the plural noun "boys" pulls you toward "have/their."

Card 5
Front: When do we use "one's" instead of "his/her"?
Back: When the subject is the indefinite pronoun "one": "One should do one's duty." Never mix: "One should do his duty" is wrong in formal grammar.

Card 6
Front: "It is I who am/is responsible" — which verb?
Back: am. The verb after a relative pronoun agrees with its antecedent ("I"), so "who am responsible."

Card 7
Front: Difference between "who" and "whom"?
Back: "Who" = subject (he did it); "whom" = object (I saw him). Quick test: answer with he → who; with him → whom.

Card 8
Front: "The committee has/have taken its/their decision" — which is correct?
Back: has … its. A collective noun acting as one unit takes singular verb and pronoun. Use plural (have/their) only when members act separately: "The committee are divided in their opinions."

Card 9
Front: Examiner's Trap: "He is one of the best players who has/have come to India."
Back: have. The relative pronoun "who" refers to "players" (plural), not "one." Classic trap — the verb agrees with the plural antecedent.

Card 10
Front: When do we use "the same … as" vs "the same … that"?
Back: "as" for similarity (My book is the same as yours); "that" for identity (This is the same book that I lost).

Card 11
Front: "None of the students ___ (was/were) present" — which is acceptable?
Back: Both are accepted in modern usage, but exams prefer singular when "none" = "not one": was. With uncountable nouns, always singular: "None of the milk was spilt."

Card 12
Front: Mnemonic — How to remember that "each other" is for two and "one another" for more than two?
Back: "Two share each other; a crowd shares one another." Count the words: "each other" = 2 words for 2 people; "one another" = for a group.

Card 13
Front: Why is "Myself went to the market" wrong?
Back: Reflexive pronouns (myself, himself) cannot be the subject of a sentence. Correct: "I went to the market." Reflexives only reflect back on an existing subject.

Card 14
Front: "He as well as his brothers ___ (is/are) guilty" — which verb and why?
Back: is. Phrases like "as well as, along with, together with" are intervening phrases — the verb agrees with the first subject ("He") only.

Card 15
Front: What case follows "than" and "as" in comparisons: "She is taller than ___ (I/me)"?
Back: Formal grammar: I (nominative), because the full clause is "than I am." "Than me" is common in speech but exams prefer "than I."

Card 16
Front: Examiner's Trap: "Everyone should bring their/his book" — what do exams expect?
Back: his (or his/her). "Everyone, everybody, someone, anybody" are singular; traditional exam grammar demands a singular pronoun. "Their" is accepted in modern usage but flagged in many competitive exams.

Card 17
Front: Difference between "its" and "it's"?
Back: its = possessive pronoun (The dog wagged its tail); it's = contraction of "it is." Remember: apostrophe means a letter is missing, never possession.

Card 18
Front: "Neither of the two boys has/have done his/their homework" — choose.
Back: has … his. "Neither of / either of + plural noun" takes singular verb and singular pronoun.

Card 19
Front: When is "which" used for persons?
Back: When selecting one from a definite group: "Which of the two brothers is taller?" Also after superlatives/only: "He is the best player which…" — no, use "that" after superlatives: "the best player that I know."

Card 20
Front: "Availing oneself of" — why is "He availed the opportunity" incomplete?
Back: "Avail" must take a reflexive pronoun: "He availed himself of the opportunity." Same family: pride oneself on, absent oneself from, enjoy oneself.
