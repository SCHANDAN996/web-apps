# Pronoun — Practice Set 6 (Questions 126–150)
# Difficulty: 0 Easy, 10 Medium, 15 Hard | 2 Examiner-Trap Questions included

126. Each of the students who attended the seminar was asked to submit ______ feedback form by Friday.
(a) their (b) its (c) his or her (d) them
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Each of the students" is singular, so the pronoun must be singular — "his or her". "Their" is increasingly heard in speech, but in traditional exam grammar "each" takes a singular pronoun; "its" is wrong because students are persons.
Source: PYQ-style

127. Spot the error: Neither the manager (P)/ nor the employees (Q)/ was satisfied with his performance. (R)/ No error (S)
(a) Part P (b) Part Q (c) No error (d) Part R
Answer: (d)
Solution: With "neither…nor", the verb and pronoun agree with the nearer subject — "employees" (plural). Part R should read "were satisfied with their performance"; both "was" and "his" wrongly follow the distant subject "manager".
Source: PYQ-style

128. It was ______ who first pointed out the discrepancy in the accounts.
(a) her (b) she (c) herself (d) hers
Answer: (b)
Solution: After "It was", the pronoun that acts as subject of the following clause takes the nominative case — "she" (she first pointed out). "Her" is objective and is the classic Sound-Right trap here.
Source: NCERT Class 9 English Grammar

129. The committee has submitted ______ report, and ______ recommendations will be implemented soon.
(a) their, their (b) its, their (c) its, its (d) their, its
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Has submitted" shows the committee is acting as a single unit, so the pronoun must stay consistently singular — "its…its". Mixing "its" with "their" in the same sentence is a consistency error examiners love to test.
Source: PYQ-style

130. Spot the error: One should do his duty (P)/ sincerely if one wants (Q)/ to succeed in life. (R)/ No error (S)
(a) Part P (b) Part Q (c) Part R (d) No error
Answer: (a)
Solution: When the sentence begins with "one", all following pronouns must be "one/one's" — not "his". Part P should read "One should do one's duty". Mixing "one" with "his" is a classic trap.
Source: NCERT Class 10 English Grammar

131. Between you and ______, this plan is unlikely to succeed.
(a) I (b) mine (c) myself (d) me
Answer: (d)
Solution: "Between" is a preposition, so it must be followed by the objective case — "me". "Between you and I" is a famous Sound-Right trap: it sounds polished but is grammatically wrong.
Source: PYQ-style

132. The two brothers love ______ deeply and have always supported ______.
(a) one another, each other (b) each other, one another (c) one another, one another (d) each other, each other
Answer: (d)
Solution: "Each other" is used for two persons; "one another" for more than two. Since there are two brothers, "each other" is correct in both blanks. This distinction is frequently tested.
Source: NCERT Class 9 English Grammar

133. Spot the error: Avail the opportunity (P)/ without wasting (Q)/ any more time. (R)/ No error (S)
(a) Part Q (b) Part P (c) Part R (d) No error
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Avail" must be followed by a reflexive pronoun and "of" — "Avail yourself of the opportunity". Part P is missing both. Verbs like avail, pride, absent, and enjoy (when used reflexively) demand this structure.
Source: PYQ-style

134. Whom did you say ______ was responsible for the delay?
(a) who (b) whom (c) whose (d) which
Answer: (a)
Solution: Ignore the parenthetical "did you say" — the real structure is "______ was responsible", which needs a subject, so "who" is correct. "Whom" is the trap: it looks right because it follows "say", but the pronoun is the subject of "was".
Source: PYQ-style

135. The climate of Shimla is cooler than ______ of Delhi.
(a) this (b) it (c) those (d) that
Answer: (d)
Solution: When comparing two things of the same kind, we use "that of" (singular) or "those of" (plural) to avoid repeating the noun — "that of Delhi" means the climate of Delhi. "It" would wrongly refer to the same climate.
Source: NCERT Class 10 English Grammar

136. Spot the error: Myself and Rohan (P)/ went to the library (Q)/ to prepare for the examination. (R)/ No error (S)
(a) Part R (b) Part Q (c) Part P (d) No error
Answer: (c)
Solution: A reflexive pronoun like "myself" can never be the subject of a sentence. Part P should read "Rohan and I". Also, politeness demands the first person comes last.
Source: PYQ-style

137. It is I who ______ responsible for the error in the report.
(a) am (b) are (c) is (d) was
Answer: (a)
Solution: In "It is I who…", the verb after "who" agrees with the antecedent "I", so "am" is correct. The relative pronoun takes its number and person from its antecedent, not from "it".
Source: NCERT Class 10 English Grammar

138. The jury ______ divided in ______ opinion on the verdict.
(a) was, its (b) were, their (c) was, their (d) were, its
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Divided in opinion" shows the jury members are acting individually, so the collective noun takes a plural verb and plural pronoun — "were…their". When a collective noun acts as one body, it takes "was…its".
Source: PYQ-style

139. Spot the error: He is one of the best players (P)/ that has ever played (Q)/ for the national team. (R)/ No error (S)
(a) Part Q (b) Part R (c) No error (d) Part P
Answer: (a)
Solution: After "one of the + plural noun", the relative pronoun refers to the plural noun ("players"), so the verb must be plural — "that have ever played". Part Q wrongly uses the singular "has", agreeing with "one" instead of "players". This is a top examiner trap.
Source: PYQ-style

140. ______ of the two sisters is a doctor, and ______ is an engineer.
(a) Either, other (b) Each, another (c) One, the other (d) One, another
Answer: (c)
Solution: For exactly two persons, the pair is "one…the other". "Another" is used only when more than two are involved. "Either" would need "or", and "each" does not pair with "another" here.
Source: NCERT Class 9 English Grammar

141. He and ______ were selected for the final round of the competition.
(a) me (b) I (c) myself (d) mine
Answer: (b)
Solution: The pronoun is part of the subject of "were selected", so the nominative "I" is required — "He and I were selected". Test by dropping "He and": "I was selected" sounds right; "me was selected" does not.
Source: PYQ-style

142. Spot the error: Every one of the boys (P)/ have submitted (Q)/ his assignment on time. (R)/ No error (S)
(a) Part Q (b) Part P (c) Part R (d) No error
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Every one of the boys" is singular, so the verb must be "has submitted" — Part Q has the error. The pronoun "his" in Part R is correctly singular. The intervening phrase "of the boys" is the Intervening Phrase trap designed to pull you toward a plural verb.
Source: PYQ-style

143. The teacher praised the boy ______ answer was the most accurate.
(a) who (b) whom (c) whose (d) which
Answer: (c)
Solution: The blank shows possession — the answer belonging to the boy — so the possessive relative pronoun "whose" is correct. "Who" would need to be a subject; "whom" an object; "which" is not used for persons.
Source: NCERT Class 9 English Grammar

144. Spot the error: Being a rainy day, (P)/ I decided to stay (Q)/ indoors and read a book. (R)/ No error (S)
(a) Part P (b) Part R (c) Part Q (d) No error
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Being a rainy day" is a dangling modifier — "I" am not a rainy day. Part P should read "It being a rainy day", where the dummy pronoun "it" refers to the weather. Weather, time, and distance take the pronoun "it".
Source: PYQ-style

145. None of the information ______ accurate, and none of the reports ______ verified.
(a) were, was (b) was, was (c) was, were (d) were, were
Answer: (c)
Solution: "None" agrees with the noun that follows: "information" is uncountable (singular, so "was"), while "reports" is countable plural (so "were"). This double agreement in one sentence is a hard-level favourite.
Source: PYQ-style

146. Spot the error: The principal, along with the teachers, (P)/ have expressed their concern (Q)/ about the declining attendance. (R)/ No error (S)
(a) Part P (b) Part Q (c) Part R (d) No error
Answer: (b)
Solution: With "along with", the verb and pronoun agree with the first subject — "the principal" (singular). Part Q should read "has expressed his concern". The phrase "along with the teachers" is the Intervening Phrase trap.
Source: PYQ-style

147. Let you and ______ decide the matter between ourselves.
(a) I (b) me (c) mine (d) us
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Let" is a verb here taking an object, so the objective case "me" is required — "Let you and me decide". The reflexive "ourselves" later confirms the object structure. "Let you and I" is the Sound-Right trap.
Source: PYQ-style

148. Spot the error: He enjoyed during (P)/ the winter vacation (Q)/ at his grandmother's village. (R)/ No error (S)
(a) Part P (b) Part Q (c) Part R (d) No error
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Enjoy" used without an object must take a reflexive pronoun — "He enjoyed himself during the winter vacation". Part P is faulty. Enjoy, avail, pride, absent, and acquit demand reflexive pronouns when no object follows.
Source: PYQ-style

149. The Ganges is one of the holiest rivers that ______ through northern India.
(a) flows (b) is flowing (c) has flowed (d) flow
Answer: (d)
Solution: The relative pronoun "that" refers to "rivers" (plural), not "one", so the verb must be plural — "flow". This is the same "one of the + plural noun + who/that + plural verb" pattern tested in error-spotting form elsewhere in this set.
Source: NCERT Class 10 English Grammar

150. Spot the error: It was him (P)/ who broke (Q)/ the window last night. (R)/ No error (S)
(a) Part R (b) Part Q (c) No error (d) Part P
Answer: (d)
Solution: In the cleft structure "It was ___ who…", the pronoun is the subject of the following verb ("who broke"), so the nominative is required — "It was he who broke the window". Part P wrongly uses the objective "him", a classic Sound-Right trap because "him" feels natural in speech.
Source: PYQ-style
