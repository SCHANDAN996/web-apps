# PRONOUN — The Word That Stands In

## 🎯 Why This Chapter Can Make or Break Your Score

Every English paper — SSC CGL, IBPS PO, State PCS — hides pronoun errors inside the most innocent-looking sentences. "Each of the boys have done his work" looks fine to the ear, but the verb *have* is wrong — it must be *has*, because *each* is singular. Pronouns are the examiner's favourite weapon because they test three things at once: agreement, case, and reference. Master this chapter and you convert "guessed" questions into certain marks.

**Learning Objectives** — By the end of this chapter you will be able to:
1. Identify all 8 types of pronouns and their functions.
2. Apply the 12 Golden Rules of pronoun agreement, case, and order.
3. Spot the examiner's classic traps: intervening phrases, sound-right errors, and ambiguous reference.
4. Solve fact-based pronoun questions in 15 seconds and inference-based ones in 45 seconds.

---

## 1. What Is a Pronoun?

A **pronoun** is a word used **in place of a noun** (Latin: *pro* = for + *nomen* = name). Without pronouns, language becomes clumsy:

> ❌ *Ravi went to Ravi's office because Ravi had forgotten Ravi's files.*
> ✅ *Ravi went to his office because he had forgotten his files.*

The noun a pronoun replaces is called its **antecedent**. The single most tested idea in this chapter: **a pronoun must agree with its antecedent in number, gender, and person.**

🧠 **Active Recall:** In the sentence "The committee has submitted its report," what is the antecedent of *its*? Why is *its* singular?

## 2. The Eight Types of Pronouns

| Type | Words | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Personal | I, we, you, he, she, it, they | *She* is honest. |
| Possessive | mine, ours, yours, his, hers, theirs | The pen is *mine*. |
| Reflexive | myself, himself, themselves | He hurt *himself*. |
| Emphatic | myself, himself (for emphasis) | I *myself* saw it. |
| Demonstrative | this, that, these, those | *Those* are ripe. |
| Interrogative | who, whom, whose, which, what | *Whom* did you meet? |
| Relative | who, whom, whose, which, that | The man *who* came… |
| Indefinite | each, everyone, somebody, none, few | *Everyone* was present. |

**Reflexive vs Emphatic:** In "He hurt himself," removing *himself* breaks the sentence → reflexive. In "He himself did it," removing *himself* leaves a complete sentence → emphatic. Examiners love this distinction.

🧠 **Active Recall:** Classify: (a) *Who* is at the door? (b) The girl *who* won the prize. (c) *Each* of the students.

## 3. Case — The Heart of Pronoun Questions

Pronouns change form by **case**:

| Case | Forms | Use |
|---|---|---|
| Nominative (subject) | I, we, he, she, they, who | Doer of the action |
| Objective (object) | me, us, him, her, them, whom | Receiver of action / after prepositions |
| Possessive | my, our, his, her, their, whose | Ownership |

**Key tests:**
- After a **preposition**, use the objective case: *between you and **me*** (never "between you and I").
- After **than/as** in comparisons, mentally complete the clause: *He is taller than **I** (am).* — not "than me" in formal exam English.
- **Who** (subject) vs **Whom** (object): answer the question with *he* → who; with *him* → whom. "___ did you invite?" → "I invited *him*" → **whom**.

🧠 **Active Recall:** Fill in: "Let you and ___ (I/me) decide this." Why?

## 4. The 12 Golden Rules of Pronouns ⭐

1. **Each, every, everyone, everybody, someone, anyone, nobody, each one** → singular pronoun (*his/her*, not *their* in formal usage). *Everybody should do **his** duty.*
2. **Two or more singular nouns joined by *and*** → plural pronoun. *Ravi and Mohan have done **their** work.*
3. **Two singular nouns joined by *or / either…or / neither…nor*** → singular pronoun. *Ravi or Mohan has lost **his** book.*
4. **When one noun is singular and the other plural (with or/nor)** → pronoun agrees with the **nearer** noun. *Neither the teacher nor the students have done **their** part.*
5. **Collective noun as one unit** → singular; **as divided members** → plural. *The jury has given **its** verdict.* / *The jury are divided in **their** opinions.*
6. **Each of / one of / either of / neither of + plural noun** → **singular** verb and pronoun. *Each of the boys has done **his** work.*
7. **Order of personal pronouns:** 2nd person → 3rd → 1st (You, he and I) for good actions; 1st person **first** for confessions/mistakes (I and Ravi broke the window). Mnemonic: **"Good: 2-3-1; Bad: 1-2-3."**
8. **A pronoun must refer clearly to one antecedent** — avoid ambiguity. ❌ *Ravi told Mohan that he had failed.* (Who failed?) ✅ Rewrite clearly.
9. **'It'** is used for animals, babies, lifeless things, and as a dummy subject (*It is raining*). Never use *he/she* for a country unless personified — *India is proud of **her** soldiers* (personification allowed).
10. **Same + that / same + as:** Use *that* when the very same person or thing is meant (identity): *He is the same man **that** came yesterday.* Use *as* when only similarity is meant: *This is the same kind of pen **as** mine.* Similarly, *such + as*: *His problem is **such as** cannot be solved.*
11. **Relative pronoun 'who' agrees with its antecedent:** *It is I who **am** responsible.* / *It is you who **are** responsible.*
12. **Possessive adjective vs possessive pronoun:** *This is **my** book* (adjective, before noun); *This book is **mine*** (pronoun, stands alone). Never write *"mine book."*

## ⚠️ Examiner's Trap Box

**Trap 1 — The Intervening Phrase:** *"The captain, along with his men, was praised for **his** bravery."* The phrase "along with his men" is a decoy — the subject is *captain* (singular). Examiners insert *along with, as well as, together with, in addition to* to make you choose a plural pronoun.

**Trap 2 — Sound-Right Errors:** *"Between you and I"* sounds polished but is wrong — prepositions demand the objective case: *between you and **me***. Similarly *"Whom is coming?"* sounds formal but is wrong — *who* is the subject.

**Trap 3 — Ambiguous Reference:** *"When the manager met the clerk, he was angry."* Both readings are possible → the sentence is faulty. In "spot the error" questions, ambiguity itself is the error.

## 🔁 Linguistic Bridge — Hindi vs English

| Hindi instinct | ❌ Wrong English | ✅ Correct English |
|---|---|---|
| "मैं और रवि गए" (I first, as in Hindi politeness) | I and Ravi went | **Ravi and I** went (2-3-1 order) |
| "मेरी किताब" (possessive before noun) | This is mine book | This is **my** book |
| "हर कोई अपना काम करे" (their feels natural) | Everyone should do their duty | Everyone should do **his** duty (formal exam usage) |
| "तुम्हारे और मेरे बीच" | between you and I | between you and **me** |
| "यह मैं ही था" | It was me (in formal grammar) | It was **I** |

## 🧠 Mnemonic — "PEN-CARE"

**P**reposition → objective case | **E**ach/Every → singular | **N**earer noun wins (or/nor) | **C**ollective → singular if united | **A**nd joins → plural | **R**eference must be clear | **E**mphatic vs Reflexive — remove and test.

## 📋 Summary Table

| Concept | Rule | Quick Example |
|---|---|---|
| Agreement | Pronoun matches antecedent in number/gender/person | Each boy → his |
| Case after preposition | Objective | between you and me |
| Or/Nor | Nearer noun decides | Neither he nor they… their |
| Collective noun | United = singular; divided = plural | jury… its / their |
| Order | Good: 2-3-1; Bad: 1-2-3 | You, he and I |
| Same + that/as | Identity → that; similarity → as | the same man that came |
| Who/Whom | he-test / him-test | Whom did you call? |
| Ambiguity | One pronoun, one clear antecedent | Rewrite unclear sentences |
