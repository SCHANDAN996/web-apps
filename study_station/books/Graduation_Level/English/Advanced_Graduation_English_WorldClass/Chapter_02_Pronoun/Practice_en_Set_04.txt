Questions 76–95: Easy | Questions 96–100: Medium

76. Neither of the two brothers has submitted ______ assignment on time.
(a) their (b) his (c) them (d) theirs
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Neither of" takes a singular verb and a singular pronoun, so "his" is correct. "Their" is a common trap because two brothers are mentioned, but the subject "neither" is singular.
Source: PYQ-style

77. Each of the students in the class was asked to bring ______ own textbook.
(a) their (b) his or her (c) them (d) its
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Each" is singular, so the pronoun must be singular — "his or her". "Their" is widely used in speech but is marked wrong in traditional exam grammar.
Source: PYQ-style

78. Spot the error: The committee (P) / have submitted (Q) / its final report (R) / to the chairman. (S)
(a) No error (b) Part P (c) Part R (d) Part Q
Answer: (d)
Solution: "Committee" acting as one body takes a singular verb, so Part Q should read "has submitted". The singular pronoun "its" in Part R confirms the committee is treated as a unit.
Source: PYQ-style

79. It was ______ who solved the puzzle before anyone else.
(a) herself (b) her (c) hers (d) she
Answer: (d)
Solution: After the verb "to be" (was), the nominative case is required in formal grammar: "It was she". "Her" sounds natural in speech but is the objective case — a classic sound-right trap.
Source: PYQ-style

80. Between you and ______, this plan will never work.
(a) mine (b) I (c) me (d) myself
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Between" is a preposition and must be followed by the objective case "me". "Between you and I" is a famous hypercorrection trap.
Source: PYQ-style

81. One should do ______ duty honestly.
(a) his (b) one's (c) their (d) our
Answer: (b)
Solution: When "one" is the subject, the pronoun referring back to it must also be "one's" in formal English. Mixing "one" with "his" or "their" is a frequent exam error.
Source: PYQ-style

82. The manager, along with his assistants, ______ responsible for the delay.
(a) are (b) have been (c) were (d) is
Answer: (d)
Solution: With "along with", the verb agrees with the first subject "the manager" (singular), so "is" is correct. The intervening phrase "his assistants" is the examiner's trap.
Source: PYQ-style

83. Spot the error: Everyone of the boys (P) / who were present (Q) / praised his performance (R) / in the match. (S)
(a) Part P (b) Part Q (c) Part R (d) No error
Answer: (a)
Solution: Part P should be "Every one of the boys" — when "of" follows, "every one" must be written as two words; "everyone" (one word) cannot take "of". Part Q is fine because "who" refers to the plural "boys".
Source: PYQ-style

84. ______ of the two sisters is the elder?
(a) Which (b) Who (c) Whom (d) Whose
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Which" is used when choosing from a definite, limited group (two sisters). "Who" is used for open-ended identity questions.
Source: NCERT-based grammar

85. The dog wagged ______ tail when it saw its master.
(a) it's (b) his (c) their (d) its
Answer: (d)
Solution: The possessive of "it" is "its" without an apostrophe; "it's" means "it is". This apostrophe confusion is one of the most repeated exam traps.
Source: PYQ-style

86. He is one of those players who ______ always punctual.
(a) are (b) was (c) is (d) has been
Answer: (a)
Solution: In "one of those who…", the relative pronoun "who" refers to the plural "players", so the verb is plural "are". Choosing "is" by focusing on "one" is the trap.
Source: PYQ-style

87. My house is bigger than ______.
(a) yours (b) your (c) you're (d) yourself
Answer: (a)
Solution: A possessive pronoun standing alone (without a noun after it) must be "yours". "Your" needs a noun; "you're" means "you are".
Source: NCERT-based grammar

88. Spot the error: Neither the teacher (P) / nor the students (Q) / was present (R) / in the hall. (S)
(a) Part Q (b) Part R (c) Part P (d) No error
Answer: (b)
Solution: With "neither…nor", the verb agrees with the nearer subject "students" (plural), so Part R should be "were present". Agreement with the first subject is the trap.
Source: PYQ-style

89. The two brothers love ______ deeply.
(a) each other (b) one another (c) themselves (d) one other
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Each other" is used for two persons; "one another" for more than two. Since there are two brothers, "each other" is correct.
Source: NCERT-based grammar

90. It is I who ______ responsible for this decision.
(a) is (b) are (c) am (d) was
Answer: (c)
Solution: The verb after a relative pronoun agrees with its antecedent. Here "who" refers to "I", so the verb is "am". Choosing "is" by matching "It" is the trap.
Source: PYQ-style

91. None of the information ______ verified yet.
(a) has been (b) were (c) have been (d) are
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Information" is uncountable and singular, so "none of the information has been" is correct. "None" here means "not any of it", taking a singular verb.
Source: PYQ-style

92. She herself completed the project. Here "herself" is used as:
(a) a reflexive pronoun (b) a relative pronoun (c) an emphatic pronoun (d) a demonstrative pronoun
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Herself" here emphasises "she" and can be removed without breaking the sentence, so it is emphatic. A reflexive pronoun is essential to the meaning, as in "She hurt herself".
Source: NCERT-based grammar

93. Spot the error: Avail (P) / the opportunity (Q) / before it slips (R) / from your hands. (S)
(a) No error (b) Part Q (c) Part P (d) Part R
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Avail" must be used reflexively: "Avail yourself of the opportunity". Part P is wrong because the reflexive pronoun and "of" are missing.
Source: PYQ-style

94. ______ book is this lying on the desk?
(a) Who (b) Whom (c) Whose (d) Which
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Whose" shows possession — the book belongs to someone. "Who" is a subject pronoun and "whom" an object pronoun; neither shows ownership.
Source: NCERT-based grammar

95. The jury ______ divided in ______ opinion.
(a) are, their (b) are, its (c) is, their (d) is, its
Answer: (a)
Solution: When a collective noun's members act separately (divided opinion), it takes a plural verb and plural pronoun: "are… their". Treating it as singular here is the trap.
Source: PYQ-style

96. He and ______ were selected for the final round.
(a) me (b) myself (c) mine (d) I
Answer: (d)
Solution: The pronoun is part of the subject, so the nominative "I" is needed: "He and I were selected". Test by removing "He and" — "I was selected" is correct, "me was selected" is not.
Source: PYQ-style

97. Spot the error: The principal with his staff (P) / have decided (Q) / to introduce (R) / a new timetable. (S)
(a) Part P (b) No error (c) Part R (d) Part Q
Answer: (d)
Solution: With "with", the verb agrees with the first subject "the principal" (singular), so Part Q should be "has decided". The intervening phrase "his staff" misleads candidates.
Source: PYQ-style

98. This is the man ______ wallet was stolen yesterday.
(a) who (b) whose (c) whom (d) which
Answer: (b)
Solution: The blank shows possession (the man's wallet), so the relative pronoun "whose" is required. "Who" and "whom" cannot express possession.
Source: NCERT-based grammar

99. Spot the error: Each of the girls (P) / have done (Q) / her homework (R) / neatly. (S)
(a) Part P (b) No error (c) Part Q (d) Part R
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Each of" takes a singular verb, so Part Q should be "has done". The plural noun "girls" immediately before the verb is the examiner's trap; the pronoun "her" in Part R is correctly singular.
Source: PYQ-style

100. Let you and ______ decide the matter peacefully.
(a) I (b) me (c) mine (d) myself
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Let" is a verb whose object must be in the objective case: "Let you and me decide". "Let you and I" is a sound-right hypercorrection trap frequently tested.
Source: PYQ-style
