101. The manager, along with his assistants, ______ going to attend the conference tomorrow.
(a) are (b) have been (c) were (d) is
Answer: (d)
Solution: When two subjects are joined by "along with", the verb agrees with the first subject ("the manager", singular), so "is" is correct. "Are" is tempting because of the nearby plural "assistants" — a classic intervening-phrase trap.
Source: PYQ-style

102. Each of the boys ______ done his homework before the teacher arrived.
(a) had (b) has (c) have (d) were
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Each of" takes a singular verb, and the earlier action (before the teacher arrived) needs past perfect, so "had" is correct. "Have" fails both agreement and tense.
Source: PYQ-style

103. One should do ______ duty honestly.
(a) one's (b) his (c) their (d) our
Answer: (a)
Solution: The pronoun referring back to "one" must be "one's", not "his" or "their". Using "his" after "one" is a very common examiner trap.
Source: NCERT-based grammar rule

104. Neither of the two brothers ______ willing to compromise.
(a) is (b) were (c) are (d) have been
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Neither of" is singular and takes a singular verb, so "is" is correct. The plural noun "brothers" next to the verb is the distractor.
Source: PYQ-style

105. It is ______ who are responsible for this delay.
(a) their (b) them (c) they (d) theirs
Answer: (c)
Solution: After "It is", the nominative case is used, and the verb "are" confirms a plural subject pronoun — "they". "Them" is objective case and cannot be followed by "who are".
Source: PYQ-style

106. The committee ______ divided in their opinion on the new policy.
(a) is (b) are (c) was (d) has been
Answer: (b)
Solution: When a collective noun's members act separately (shown by "divided in their opinion"), a plural verb is used — "are". "Is" would be right only if the committee acted as one body.
Source: PYQ-style

107. Between you and ______, this plan will never work.
(a) I (b) myself (c) mine (d) me
Answer: (d)
Solution: Prepositions like "between" take the objective case, so "me" is correct. "Between you and I" is a famous sound-right trap that examiners love.
Source: PYQ-style

108. Every student and every teacher ______ present at the function.
(a) were (b) are (c) was (d) have been
Answer: (c)
Solution: When two singular nouns are each preceded by "every", the verb is singular — "was". The two nouns together look plural, which is the trap.
Source: PYQ-style

109. He is one of those players who ______ always punctual.
(a) is (b) was (c) are (d) has been
Answer: (c)
Solution: In "one of those who…", the relative pronoun "who" refers to the plural "players", so the verb is plural — "are". Choosing "is" by linking "who" to "one" is the classic error.
Source: PYQ-style

110. My house is bigger than ______.
(a) your (b) yours (c) you're (d) yourself
Answer: (b)
Solution: The comparison is between houses, so a possessive pronoun "yours" (= your house) is needed. "Your" is an adjective and cannot stand alone.
Source: PYQ-style

111. Spot the error: Part P: Neither the teacher / Part Q: nor the students / Part R: was present in the hall. / Part S: No error
(a) Part P (b) Part R (c) Part Q (d) No error
Answer: (b)
Solution: With "neither…nor", the verb agrees with the nearer subject ("students", plural), so Part R should be "were present". The singular "teacher" misleads many candidates.
Source: PYQ-style

112. ______ of the information given in the report is reliable.
(a) Many (b) Much (c) Few (d) Several
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Information" is uncountable, so "much" is correct. "Many", "few" and "several" are used only with countable nouns.
Source: NCERT-based grammar rule

113. The two sisters love ______ very much.
(a) each other (b) one another (c) themselves (d) mutually
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Each other" is used for two persons; "one another" for more than two. Since there are two sisters, "each other" is correct.
Source: PYQ-style

114. He ______ is to blame for the accident, not his brother.
(a) him (b) his (c) himself (d) he
Answer: (c)
Solution: The emphatic reflexive pronoun "himself" emphasises "he". "Him" cannot stand as subject, and "his" is possessive.
Source: PYQ-style

115. Spot the error: Part P: Avail the opportunity / Part Q: if you want / Part R: to succeed in life. / Part S: No error
(a) Part P (b) Part Q (c) Part R (d) No error
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Avail" must be followed by a reflexive pronoun: "Avail yourself of the opportunity". Part P misses both the reflexive pronoun and the preposition "of" — a frequent trap.
Source: PYQ-style

116. None of the milk ______ spilt on the floor.
(a) were (b) have been (c) are (d) was
Answer: (d)
Solution: "None of" with an uncountable noun ("milk") takes a singular verb — "was". With plural countable nouns, "none of" may take a plural verb.
Source: PYQ-style

117. Let you and ______ decide the matter peacefully.
(a) I (b) mine (c) me (d) myself
Answer: (c)
Solution: After "let", the objective case is used — "me". "Let you and I" sounds elegant but is grammatically wrong; examiners exploit this sound-right trap.
Source: PYQ-style

118. Spot the error: Part P: The Ganges is / Part Q: one of the longest river / Part R: in India. / Part S: No error
(a) Part P (b) Part Q (c) Part R (d) No error
Answer: (b)
Solution: After "one of the", a plural noun is required: "one of the longest rivers". Part Q uses the singular "river", which is the error.
Source: PYQ-style

119. Whom do you think ______ the prize yesterday?
(a) won (b) win (c) winning (d) wins
Answer: (a)
Solution: Remove the parenthetical "do you think"; the sentence becomes "Who won the prize yesterday?" — past tense "won" is needed. "Whom" here is a distractor; the real subject is understood as "who".
Source: PYQ-style

120. The principal and secretary ______ arrived at the venue.
(a) have (b) were (c) has (d) are
Answer: (c)
Solution: "The principal and secretary" (one article) refers to one person holding two posts, so the singular verb "has" is correct. Two articles would make it plural — a subtle examiner trap.
Source: PYQ-style

121. Spot the error: Part P: He is one of the best / Part Q: player that has / Part R: ever played for the country. / Part S: No error
(a) Part P (b) Part Q (c) Part R (d) No error
Answer: (b)
Solution: "One of the best players" (plural noun) is required, and the relative clause then takes a plural verb: "players that have". Part Q has both errors compressed into it.
Source: PYQ-style

122. There isn't ______ difference between the two proposals.
(a) many (b) several (c) few (d) much
Answer: (d)
Solution: "Difference" here is treated as uncountable (degree of difference), so "much" fits. "Many", "few" and "several" need countable plurals.
Source: PYQ-style

123. Hardly had he reached the station when the train left; he blamed ______ for the delay.
(a) himself (b) him (c) his (d) he
Answer: (a)
Solution: The subject and object are the same person, so the reflexive pronoun "himself" is required. "Him" would mean he blamed some other man.
Source: PYQ-style

124. Spot the error: Part P: Each man and each woman / Part Q: have the right / Part R: to vote in a democracy. / Part S: No error
(a) Part P (b) No error (c) Part R (d) Part Q
Answer: (d)
Solution: Subjects joined by "and" but each preceded by "each" take a singular verb: "has the right". Part Q wrongly uses the plural "have" — a standard agreement trap.
Source: PYQ-style

125. It was I who ______ responsible for organising the event.
(a) were (b) have been (c) am (d) was
Answer: (d)
Solution: In the cleft sentence "It was I who…", the verb in the relative clause agrees with "I" and matches the past frame "It was", giving "was". "Am" would clash with the past tense of the main clause.
Source: PYQ-style
