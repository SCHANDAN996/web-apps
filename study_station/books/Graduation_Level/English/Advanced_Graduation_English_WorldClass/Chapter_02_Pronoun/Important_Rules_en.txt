# 📋 Important Grammar Rules & Tips — Pronoun

## (a) 12 High-Yield Rules

| # | Rule | Example | Examiner's Trap |
|---|------|---------|-----------------|
| 1 | A pronoun must agree with its antecedent in **number, person and gender**. | Every student must bring **his/her** book. | "Everyone should bring **their** book" — sounds natural, but in formal exam grammar *everyone* is singular. |
| 2 | Pronouns joined by **and** take a plural pronoun; joined by **or / either…or / neither…nor**, the pronoun agrees with the **nearer** subject. | Neither the teacher nor the students did **their** duty. | Trap: using *his* because "teacher" comes first — the nearer subject (*students*) decides. |
| 3 | **Each, every, everyone, everybody, someone, anyone, nobody, either, neither** are singular → singular pronoun. | Each of the girls has done **her** work. | Trap: "Each of the boys have done **their** work" — *each* is always singular. |
| 4 | After **let**, use the objective case. | Let **him and me** go. | Trap: "Let he and I go" — *let* is a verb; what follows is its object. |
| 5 | After **than / as** in comparisons, use the nominative case (the verb is understood). | He is taller than **I** (am). | Trap: "than me" is common in speech but marked wrong in error-spotting. |
| 6 | After the verb **to be** (is, was, were), use the nominative case. | It is **I** who called. | Trap: "It is me" — colloquially fine, grammatically tested as wrong. |
| 7 | **One**, if used as subject, must be followed by **one/one's/oneself** throughout — never he/his. | One should do **one's** duty. | Trap: "One should do **his** duty" — mixing pronoun sets. |
| 8 | **Who** = subject; **whom** = object; **whose** = possession. | **Whom** did you invite? | Trap: "Who did you give the book to?" — the object of the preposition needs *whom*. |
| 9 | **Which** is used for things and for selection among a defined group; **who** for persons. | **Which** of the two brothers is taller? | Trap: using *who* when a choice from a limited set is meant. |
| 10 | **The same…that** (not *the same…which/who*). | This is the same book **that** I lost. | Trap: "the same man **who** came yesterday" — use *that* after *the same*. |
| 11 | **Each other** = two persons; **one another** = more than two. | The two brothers love **each other**; the five friends help **one another**. | Trap: swapping them in error-spotting questions. |
| 12 | Reflexive pronouns (**myself, himself**) cannot replace subject pronouns; use them only for emphasis or when subject = object. | I **myself** did it. / He hurt **himself**. | Trap: "**Myself** and Ravi went there" — *myself* can never be a subject. |

---

## (b) Time Management — The 15/45-Second Rule

**⚡ Fact-Based Questions — target 15 seconds (rule recall, no thinking needed):**
- Case after *let / than / to be* (Rules 4, 5, 6)
- *Each/every/everyone* → singular pronoun (Rule 3)
- *One…one's* consistency (Rule 7)
- *Each other* vs *one another* (Rule 11)
- Reflexive pronoun misuse (Rule 12)

👉 These are pattern-recognition questions. If you have memorised the 12 rules, the answer is visible in one reading. Do not re-read the sentence twice.

**🔍 Inference-Based Questions — allow up to 45 seconds (analysis required):**
- Pronoun–antecedent agreement with **intervening phrases** ("Each of the students has done **his** work" — the phrase *of the students* must not pull you to *their*)
- **Or / neither…nor** proximity agreement (Rule 2) — you must identify the nearer subject
- *Who/whom* inside long clauses — strip the sentence to subject–verb–object first
- Error-spotting where **two pronouns** appear and only one is wrong

👉 Strategy: underline the antecedent, ignore the phrase in between, then match. If still unsure after 45 seconds, mark and move on.

## (c) Morphology — Roots & Affixes Behind Pronoun Vocabulary

| Root / Affix | Meaning | Example Words |
|---|---|---|
| **pro-** (Latin) | "for, in place of" | **pro**noun (a word used *in place of* a noun), propose, proceed |
| **noun / nomen** (Latin *nomen*) | "name" | noun, nominal, nominate, nomenclature |
| **ante-** (Latin) | "before" | **ante**cedent (the word that comes *before* the pronoun), anterior, anticipate |
| **re-** (Latin) | "back, again" | **re**flexive (the action bends *back* to the subject), refer, return |
| **-self / -selves** (Old English) | one's own person | myself, himself, themselves |
| **inter-** (Latin) | "between, among" | **inter**rogative (asking *between* people), interrupt, intervene |
| **-ive** (Latin suffix) | "having the quality of" | relat**ive**, demonstrat**ive**, interrogat**ive**, possess**ive** |
| **monstr-** (Latin *monstrare*) | "to show, point out" | de**monstr**ative pronoun (this, that — words that *point out*), demonstrate, monster (originally "a warning sign") |

**Memory hook:** *Pro-noun* = "for-noun" — a pronoun is literally a word that stands **for** a noun. *Ante-cedent* = "going before" — the noun that goes before it. Learn the roots and the grammar terms explain themselves.
