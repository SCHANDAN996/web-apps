# 📊 PYQ Analysis — Pronoun (Pattern-Based, English)

## How Exams Test Pronouns

Across SSC (CGL, CHSL, MTS, GD), Banking (IBPS PO/Clerk, SBI), Railways (NTPC, Group D) and State exams, pronoun questions appear in four recurring formats:

1. **Spot the Error** — one part of the sentence has a pronoun error (case, number, agreement, or reference).
2. **Fill in the Blank** — choose the correct pronoun form.
3. **Sentence Improvement** — replace the underlined pronoun phrase.
4. **Cloze / Para-jumble** — pronoun reference decides the logical flow of sentences.

## Sub-topics That Repeat Most Often

- **Case confusion**: between you and *I/me*; *who/whom*; *we/us* before nouns.
- **Agreement with antecedent**: everyone/somebody/each → singular pronoun; collective nouns.
- **Intervening phrases**: "The captain, along with his men, ___ (he/they)…" — the phrase between subject and pronoun is the trap.
- **Relative pronouns**: *who/which/that/whose* — especially "that" after superlatives and "the same".
- **Reflexive pronouns**: *myself/himself* used wrongly as subjects ("Myself and Ravi went…").
- **Reciprocal pronouns**: *each other* (two) vs *one another* (more than two).
- **Pronoun after "than/as"**: "He is taller than I (am)" — formal case.
- **One/One's**: "One should do one's duty" (not *his*).

## Examiner's Favourite Traps (in words, not numbers)

- **Intervening Phrase Trap**: extra words between subject and pronoun ("The manager, as well as the clerks, has done *his* work") — students match the pronoun to the nearest noun (clerks) instead of the real subject (manager).
- **Sound-Right Trap**: "Between you and I" *sounds* polite and correct, but after a preposition the object case (*me*) is required.
- **Near-Name Confusion**: *its/it's*, *their/there/they're*, *whose/who's* — spelling-based traps in cloze tests.
- **Over-thinking Trap**: a simple, correct sentence is "improved" into a wrong one because the candidate assumes there must be an error.
- **Collective Noun Trap**: "The jury has given *its* verdict" (united = singular) vs "The jury are divided in *their* opinion" (divided = plural).

## Top 10 High-Yield Questions (with Solutions)

**Q1.** Each of the students has submitted ___ assignment.
(a) their (b) his or her (c) them (d) theirs
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** "Each" is singular, so the pronoun must be singular — *his or her*. "Their" (a) is the Sound-Right trap; it is common in speech but tested as an error in formal exam English.
**Trap type:** Sound-Right | **Source:** PYQ-style (agreement of pronoun with indefinite antecedent)

**Q2.** Between you and ___, this plan will not work.
(a) I (b) me (c) mine (d) myself
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** "Between" is a preposition; a preposition is always followed by the object case — *me*. "Between you and I" is the classic Sound-Right trap.
**Trap type:** Sound-Right | **Source:** NCERT Class 9 English (Grammar)

**Q3.** The captain, along with his team members, was praised for ___ performance.
(a) their (b) his (c) its (d) them
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** With "along with / as well as / together with", the pronoun agrees with the **first** subject (*captain* → his). The intervening phrase "along with his team members" is only a distraction.
**Trap type:** Intervening Phrase | **Source:** PYQ-style

**Q4.** One should do ___ duty honestly.
(a) his (b) one's (c) their (d) her
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** The pronoun for "one" is "one's" throughout the sentence. Mixing "one… his" is a frequent exam error.
**Trap type:** Sound-Right | **Source:** NCERT Class 10 English (Grammar)

**Q5.** This is the best book ___ I have ever read.
(a) which (b) that (c) who (d) what
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** After a superlative ("the best"), the relative pronoun "that" is preferred, not "which".
**Trap type:** Over-thinking (both *which* and *that* look possible) | **Source:** PYQ-style

**Q6.** The two brothers love ___ deeply.
(a) one another (b) each other (c) themselves (d) one other
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** "Each other" is used for two persons; "one another" for more than two. Here there are two brothers.
**Trap type:** Near-Name Confusion | **Source:** NCERT Class 9 English (Grammar)

**Q7.** ___ and Ravi went to the library.
(a) Myself (b) I (c) Me (d) Mine
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** A reflexive pronoun (*myself*) can never be the subject of a sentence. The subject case "I" is required.
**Trap type:** Sound-Right | **Source:** PYQ-style

**Q8.** The committee has submitted ___ report to the chairman.
(a) their (b) its (c) his (d) them
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** The committee acted as one united body, so the collective noun takes a singular pronoun — *its*. If the members were divided, "their" would be correct.
**Trap type:** Collective Noun | **Source:** PYQ-style

**Q9.** He is taller than ___.
(a) me (b) I (c) mine (d) myself
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** In formal grammar, "than" is a conjunction here: "than I (am)". The subject case *I* is the exam answer; "than me" is the Sound-Right trap.
**Trap type:** Sound-Right | **Source:** NCERT Class 10 English (Grammar)

**Q10.** It was ___ who called you last night.
(a) me (b) I (c) mine (d) myself
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** After "It is/was", the pronoun takes the subject case in formal English: "It was I who called." Note the verb "who called" also agrees with "I".
**Trap type:** Over-thinking | **Source:** PYQ-style

## Applying the 15/45-Second Rule

**15-second question (Fact-based) — Q2:** "Between you and ___" — the moment you see a preposition, the object case rule fires instantly. No reading of context needed. Target: under 15 seconds.

**45-second question (Inference-based) — Q8:** Here you must first judge whether the committee acted *united* or *divided*. Read the whole sentence, look for words like "divided", "differ", "unanimous", then decide *its* vs *their*. This needs thought — allow up to 45 seconds, and if the sentence gives no clue, default to singular (the safer exam convention).

**Rule of thumb:** Case rules (who/whom, I/me, preposition + pronoun) = 15 sec. Agreement and reference questions (collective nouns, antecedent clarity, cloze pronouns) = 45 sec.
