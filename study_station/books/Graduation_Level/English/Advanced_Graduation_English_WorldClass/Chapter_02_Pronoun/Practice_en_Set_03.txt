51. Choose the correct sentence.
(a) Me and my friend went to the market. (b) My friend and me went to the market. (c) My friend and I went to the market. (d) I and my friend went to the market.
Answer: (c)
Solution: When a pronoun is part of the subject, use the subjective case (I), and polite order puts the other person first. "Me" (objective case) cannot be a subject, and "I and my friend" violates the polite order.
Source: NCERT Class 9 English Grammar

52. Each of the boys ______ done his homework.
(a) has (b) have (c) are (d) were
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Each of + plural noun" takes a singular verb and singular pronoun reference. Hence "has done his homework" is correct.

53. One should do ______ duty honestly.
(a) one's (b) his (c) their (d) our
Answer: (a)
Solution: The pronoun that refers back to "one" must be "one's", not "his" or "their". Consistency of pronoun is the rule being tested.
Source: NCERT Class 10 English Grammar

54. This is the boy ______ won the first prize.
(a) which (b) who (c) whom (d) whose
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Who" is the relative pronoun used as the subject for persons. "Which" is for things, "whom" is objective case, and "whose" shows possession.
Source: NCERT Class 9 English Grammar

55. Nobody but Ram ______ present in the class.
(a) were (b) are (c) have been (d) was
Answer: (d)
Solution: With "but" (meaning 'except'), the verb agrees with the first subject "Nobody", which is singular. Hence "was".
Source: PYQ-style

56. The committee ______ divided in their opinions.
(a) were (b) was (c) is (d) has been
Answer: (a)
Solution: When a collective noun's members act individually (divided in opinions), it takes a plural verb and plural pronoun ("their"). Hence "were".
Source: NCERT Class 10 English Grammar

57. Between you and ______, this plan will fail.
(a) I (b) mine (c) myself (d) me
Answer: (d)
Solution: After a preposition ("between"), the objective case "me" is required. "Between you and I" is a classic sound-right trap.
Source: PYQ-style

58. ______ of the two sisters is married.
(a) Each (b) Both (c) All (d) Every
Answer: (a)
Solution: For two persons with a singular verb ("is"), "each" is correct. "Both" and "all" take plural verbs; "every" is not used with "of the two".
Source: NCERT Class 9 English Grammar

59. He is one of the best players that ______ ever played for India.
(a) has (b) have (c) is (d) was
Answer: (b)
Solution: In "one of the + plural noun + that/who", the relative pronoun refers to the plural noun ("players"), so the verb is plural: "have".
Source: PYQ-style

60. I, who ______ your friend, will help you.
(a) is (b) am (c) are (d) was
Answer: (b)
Solution: A relative pronoun's verb agrees with its antecedent. Here the antecedent is "I", so the verb is "am".
Source: NCERT Class 10 English Grammar

61. It is I who ______ responsible for this mistake.
(a) is (b) are (c) am (d) were
Answer: (c)
Solution: The relative pronoun "who" refers to "I", so the verb must agree with "I" — "am". The "sound-right" pull towards "is" (agreeing with "It") is the examiner's trap.
Source: PYQ-style

62. Neither of the two boys ______ brought his book.
(a) have (b) has (c) are (d) were
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Neither of" is singular and takes a singular verb and singular pronoun ("his"). Hence "has".
Source: NCERT Class 9 English Grammar

63. The teacher, as well as the students, ______ gone on the tour.
(a) has (b) have (c) are (d) were
Answer: (a)
Solution: With "as well as", the verb agrees with the first subject ("the teacher" — singular). The intervening phrase "as well as the students" is a trap to make you choose "have".
Source: PYQ-style

64. Everyone should respect ______ parents.
(a) their (b) our (c) one's (d) his
Answer: (d)
Solution: In traditional exam grammar, "everyone/everybody" is singular and takes "his" (or "his or her"). "Their" is increasingly accepted in modern usage, but standard exam keys prefer "his".
Source: NCERT Class 10 English Grammar

65. Avail ______ of this opportunity at once.
(a) you (b) your (c) yours (d) yourself
Answer: (d)
Solution: "Avail oneself of" is a fixed reflexive construction. The reflexive pronoun "yourself" is compulsory here.

66. He and ______ were present at the meeting.
(a) I (b) me (c) myself (d) mine
Answer: (a)
Solution: The pronoun is part of the subject, so the subjective case "I" is needed. Test by removing "He and": "I was present" sounds right, "me was present" does not.
Source: NCERT Class 9 English Grammar

67. The jury ______ given its verdict unanimously.
(a) have (b) were (c) are (d) has
Answer: (d)
Solution: When a collective noun acts as one unit ("unanimously", "its verdict"), it takes a singular verb. Hence "has".
Source: NCERT Class 10 English Grammar

68. Whom do you think ______ this letter?
(a) has wrote (b) written (c) wrote (d) write
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Do you think" is a parenthetical insertion; ignore it. The real subject is "who(m)" acting as subject of "wrote" — the past tense "wrote" is correct. The trap is the misleading "whom" at the start.
Source: PYQ-style

69. Each student and each teacher ______ given a certificate.
(a) were (b) was (c) have (d) are
Answer: (b)
Solution: When two singular nouns joined by "and" are each preceded by "each/every", the verb is singular. Hence "was".
Source: NCERT Class 10 English Grammar

70. It was ______ who called you last night.
(a) me (b) mine (c) I (d) myself
Answer: (c)
Solution: After the verb "to be" (was), the complement takes the subjective case in formal exam grammar: "It was I". "It was me" is informal speech, not the exam answer.
Source: PYQ-style

71. None of the information ______ correct.
(a) are (b) were (c) is (d) have been
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Information" is uncountable, so "none of the information" takes a singular verb: "is". With countable plurals, "none of" may take either, but uncountables are always singular.
Source: NCERT Class 10 English Grammar

72. She is one of those women who ______ always ready to help others.
(a) is (b) has been (c) was (d) are
Answer: (d)
Solution: "Who" refers to the plural "women" (those women who are ready), so the verb is plural: "are". Do not let "one of" pull you to "is" — that is the trap.
Source: PYQ-style

73. Let you and ______ decide the matter ourselves.
(a) me (b) I (c) mine (d) my
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Let" is a verb here taking an object, so the objective case "me" is required. The reflexive "ourselves" at the end confirms "you and me" as the object.
Source: NCERT Class 9 English Grammar

74. The principal and secretary ______ arrived.
(a) have (b) are (c) has (d) were
Answer: (c)
Solution: "The principal and secretary" (one article) means one person holding both posts, so the verb is singular: "has". "The principal and the secretary" would need "have" — the missing second "the" is the examiner's trap.
Source: PYQ-style

75. Many a student ______ failed in this examination.
(a) have (b) has (c) are (d) were
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Many a + singular noun" is grammatically singular and takes a singular verb: "has". Despite the plural meaning, the form is singular — a favourite exam trap.
Source: NCERT Class 10 English Grammar
