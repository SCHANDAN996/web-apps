26. Choose the correct pronoun: "Neither Ravi nor his friends brought ___ books."
(a) their (b) his (c) its (d) our
Answer: (a)
Solution: When two subjects are joined by "neither...nor", the pronoun agrees with the nearer subject ("friends" — plural), so "their" is correct. "His" wrongly agrees with the farther subject.
Source: NCERT Class 10 English Grammar

27. Choose the correct option: "Each of the boys has done ___ homework."
(a) them (b) their (c) his (d) theirs
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Each of" takes a singular pronoun, so "his" is correct. "Their" is a common trap because "boys" sounds plural, but the subject is "each".
Source: NCERT Class 9 English Grammar

28. Fill in the blank: "It was ___ who solved the puzzle."
(a) me (b) mine (c) myself (d) I
Answer: (d)
Solution: After the verb "to be" (was), the nominative case "I" is used in formal English. "Me" is common in speech but considered incorrect in exams.
Source: NCERT Class 10 English Grammar

29. Choose the correct sentence:
(a) Myself and Ram went to the market.
(b) Ram and myself went to the market.
(c) Ram and I went to the market.
(d) I and Ram went to the market.
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Myself" cannot be used as a subject; the nominative "I" is needed, and politeness puts the other person first. (d) has the right case but wrong order.
Source: NCERT Class 9 English Grammar

30. Fill in the blank: "The jury ___ unanimous in ___ verdict."
(a) was, its (b) were, their (c) were, its (d) was, their
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Unanimous" shows the jury acted as one body, so the collective noun takes a singular verb and pronoun — "was, its". Plural forms ("were, their") are used only when members act separately, e.g. "divided in opinion".
Source: NCERT Class 10 English Grammar

31. Choose the correct pronoun: "One should do ___ duty honestly."
(a) his (b) their (c) our (d) one's
Answer: (d)
Solution: The pronoun for "one" must be "one's" throughout the sentence. "His" is a frequent trap but breaks the one/one's consistency rule.
Source: NCERT Class 10 English Grammar

32. Fill in the blank: "This is the boy ___ father is a doctor."
(a) who (b) whose (c) which (d) whom
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Whose" shows possession (the boy's father). "Who" and "whom" refer to persons but do not show possession.
Source: NCERT Class 9 English Grammar

33. Choose the correct option: "Between you and ___, this plan will fail."
(a) I (b) myself (c) mine (d) me
Answer: (d)
Solution: After a preposition ("between"), the objective case "me" is required. "Between you and I" is a classic sound-right trap.
Source: PYQ-style

34. Fill in the blank: "The dog wagged ___ tail."
(a) it's (b) its (c) their (d) his
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Its" is the possessive pronoun; "it's" means "it is". This spelling confusion is one of the most common examiner traps.
Source: NCERT Class 8 English Grammar

35. Choose the correct sentence:
(a) Every student should bring his book. (b) Every student should bring their book. (c) Every students should bring his book. (d) Every student should bring them book.
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Every" is followed by a singular noun and singular pronoun — "his". "Their" with "every student" is widespread in modern usage but marked wrong in traditional exam grammar.
Source: NCERT Class 10 English Grammar

36. Fill in the blank: "This is the best book ___ I have ever read."
(a) whose (b) who (c) whom (d) that
Answer: (d)
Solution: After a superlative ("the best"), the relative pronoun "that" is used, not "which" or "who". "Who" is wrong here because the antecedent "book" is a thing.
Source: NCERT Class 10 English Grammar

37. Choose the correct pronoun: "___ of the two sisters is a doctor?"
(a) Who (b) Whom (c) Whose (d) Which
Answer: (d)
Solution: "Which" is used when selecting from a limited, known group (the two sisters). "Who" is used for open-ended questions about identity.
Source: NCERT Class 9 English Grammar

38. Fill in the blank: "Let you and ___ try once more."
(a) I (b) myself (c) me (d) mine
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Let" is a verb that takes the objective case, so "me" is correct. "You and I" after "let" is a sound-right trap.
Source: PYQ-style

39. Choose the correct option: "The committee ___ submitted ___ report."
(a) have, their (b) has, their (c) has, its (d) have, its
Answer: (c)
Solution: Here the committee acts as a single unit (submitted one report), so singular "has" and "its" are correct. Plural forms would need evidence of divided action.
Source: NCERT Class 10 English Grammar

40. Fill in the blank: "He hurt ___ while climbing the tree."
(a) his (b) him (c) himself (d) hisself
Answer: (c)
Solution: When the subject and object are the same person, a reflexive pronoun ("himself") is used. "Hisself" is not a word in standard English.
Source: NCERT Class 8 English Grammar

41. Choose the correct sentence:
(a) The principal, along with the teachers, were present. (b) The principal, along with the teachers, was present. (c) The principal along with the teachers have been present. (d) The principal, along with the teachers, are present.
Answer: (b)
Solution: With "along with", the verb agrees with the first subject ("the principal" — singular). The intervening phrase "along with the teachers" is the examiner's trap.
Source: PYQ-style

42. Fill in the blank: "It is I who ___ responsible for this mistake."
(a) is (b) am (c) are (d) was
Answer: (b)
Solution: The verb after a relative pronoun agrees with its antecedent — here "I", so "am" is correct. "Is" wrongly agrees with "it".
Source: NCERT Class 10 English Grammar

43. Choose the correct option: "None of the milk ___ spilt."
(a) are (b) were (c) was (d) have been
Answer: (c)
Solution: "None of" + uncountable noun ("milk") takes a singular verb. The trap is treating "none" as always plural.
Source: PYQ-style

44. Choose the correct sentence:
(a) He is the same man who came yesterday. (b) He is the same man that came yesterday. (c) He is the same man which came yesterday. (d) He is the same man whom came yesterday.
Answer: (b)
Solution: After "the same", the relative pronoun "that" is used (not "who") when it introduces a defining clause. This is a standard exam rule.
Source: NCERT Class 10 English Grammar

45. Fill in the blank: "Each of the girls was given a prize for ___ performance."
(a) her (b) their (c) his (d) its
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Each of the girls" is singular and feminine, so "her" is correct. "Their" is the tempting but traditionally incorrect choice.
Source: PYQ-style

46. Choose the correct sentence:
(a) Who do you think wrote this letter? (b) Whom do you think wrote this letter? (c) Whose do you think wrote this letter? (d) Which do you think wrote this letter?
Answer: (a)
Solution: Remove the parenthetical "do you think" — the sentence needs a subject for "wrote", so nominative "who" is correct. "Whom" is the trap because it looks like the object of "think".
Source: PYQ-style

47. Choose the correct sentence:
(a) Avail the opportunity. (b) Avail yourself of the opportunity. (c) Avail you of the opportunity. (d) Avail himself of the opportunity.
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Avail" must be followed by a reflexive pronoun + "of" — "avail yourself of". Using "avail" without the reflexive is a classic error-spotting trap.
Source: PYQ-style

48. Fill in the blank: "The two brothers love ___ deeply."
(a) one other (b) one another (c) themselves (d) each other
Answer: (d)
Solution: "Each other" is used for two persons; "one another" for more than two. Since there are two brothers, "each other" is correct.
Source: NCERT Class 9 English Grammar

49. Choose the correct option: "There is no use ___ over spilt milk."
(a) to cry (b) of crying (c) crying (d) cry
Answer: (c)
Solution: After "there is no use", the gerund form "crying" is used. "To cry" (infinitive) is the common trap here.
Source: PYQ-style

50. Choose the correct sentence:
(a) He, who is my friend, I trust him.
(b) He is my friend, whom I trust.
(c) He is my friend, who I trust.
(d) He is my friend, which I trust.
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Whom" is correct as the object of "trust" (I trust him). "Who I trust" misuses the nominative case — a frequent examiner trap; (a) double-marks the object with "him".
Source: NCERT Class 10 English Grammar
