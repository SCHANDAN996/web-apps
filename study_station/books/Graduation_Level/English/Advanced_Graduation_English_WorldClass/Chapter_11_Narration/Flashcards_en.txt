Card 1
Front: What is the basic difference between Direct and Indirect Speech?
Back: Direct Speech quotes the speaker's exact words inside inverted commas ("..."); Indirect Speech reports the meaning in our own words without quotation marks, usually with a reporting verb like said/told.

Card 2
Front: What happens to the tense when the reporting verb is in the past tense?
Back: The reported speech shifts one step back: Present Simple → Past Simple, Present Continuous → Past Continuous, Present Perfect → Past Perfect, Past Simple → Past Perfect, will → would, can → could.

Card 3
Front: When does the tense NOT change even if the reporting verb is past?
Back: When the reported speech expresses a universal truth, habitual fact, or scientific fact. E.g., He said, "The sun rises in the east." → He said that the sun rises in the east.

Card 4
Front: 🧠 Mnemonic: How to remember pronoun changes in narration?
Back: "SON" — S: First person changes according to the Subject of the reporting verb; O: Second person changes according to the Object; N: Third person needs No change.

Card 5
Front: Convert: She said to me, "I will help you."
Back: She told me that she would help me. (said to → told; I → she [subject]; will → would; you → me [object])

Card 6
Front: ⚠️ Examiner's Trap: "He said that he was going to Delhi the next day." — Original sentence had "tomorrow". Is "the next day" correct?
Back: Yes. Time expressions change: now → then, today → that day, tomorrow → the next day, yesterday → the previous day, here → there, this → that, ago → before.

Card 7
Front: How are Yes/No questions converted into Indirect Speech?
Back: Use "if" or "whether" (no "that"), change the question into statement order (subject before verb), and drop the auxiliary do/does/did. E.g., He said to me, "Do you like tea?" → He asked me if/whether I liked tea.

Card 8
Front: How are Wh-questions converted into Indirect Speech?
Back: Keep the Wh-word as the connector (no "that"), and use statement word order. E.g., She said, "Where do you live?" → She asked me where I lived. (NOT "where did I live")

Card 9
Front: ⚠️ Examiner's Trap: He asked me where did I live. — Spot the error.
Back: In Indirect Speech, questions lose interrogative order. Correct: He asked me where I lived. The auxiliary "did" must be removed and the verb shifted back (live → lived).

Card 10
Front: How are commands and requests reported?
Back: Use ordered/commanded/requested/advised + object + to-infinitive for affirmative; "not to" for negative. E.g., The teacher said, "Open your books." → The teacher ordered the students to open their books. "Don't run." → He told me not to run.

Card 11
Front: How are exclamatory sentences converted?
Back: Use exclaimed with joy/sorrow/surprise + that; "What a/How" is replaced by "very/great". E.g., She said, "What a beautiful flower!" → She exclaimed with joy that it was a very beautiful flower.

Card 12
Front: 🧠 Mnemonic: How to remember time-word changes (now, today, tomorrow, yesterday)?
Back: "N-T-T-Y go far away": Now→Then, Today→That day, Tomorrow→The next day, Yesterday→The day before. Everything moves one step "away" in time, just like tense moves one step back.

Card 13
Front: Convert: He said, "I have finished my work."
Back: He said that he had finished his work. (Present Perfect → Past Perfect when the reporting verb is past.)

Card 14
Front: What reporting verbs replace "said" for suggestions?
Back: "Suggested" + gerund or "that...should". E.g., He said, "Let us go for a walk." → He suggested going for a walk. / He suggested that they should go for a walk.

Card 15
Front: ⚠️ Examiner's Trap: The captain said, "Hurrah! We have won the match." — Which conversion is wrong: (a) exclaimed with joy (b) exclaimed with sorrow?
Back: (b) is wrong. "Hurrah!" expresses joy, so the verb must be "exclaimed with joy". Examiners swap joy/sorrow to test attention. Correct: The captain exclaimed with joy that they had won the match.

Card 16
Front: Convert: Mother said to me, "Do not go out in the rain."
Back: Mother advised/told me not to go out in the rain. (Negative imperative → "not to" + verb; no "that", no tense shift of the infinitive.)

Card 17
Front: What happens to "must" in Indirect Speech?
Back: "Must" usually remains "must" or changes to "had to" (for obligation). E.g., He said, "I must go now." → He said that he must/had to go then.

Card 18
Front: Convert: She said, "Can you lend me your pen?"
Back: She asked me if/whether I could lend her my pen. (can → could; you → I [object]; me → her; my → my, matching the new subject "I".)

Card 19
Front: How are wishes and prayers reported?
Back: Use "wished" or "prayed" + that + subject + might/would. E.g., He said, "May God bless you!" → He prayed that God might bless me.

Card 20
Front: Convert: The boy said, "Alas! My brother is dead."
Back: The boy exclaimed with sorrow/grief that his brother was dead. ("Alas!" signals sorrow; is → was; my → his.)
