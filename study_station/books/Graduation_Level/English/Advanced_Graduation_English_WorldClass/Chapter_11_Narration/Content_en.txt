# Direct & Indirect Speech (Narration)

## 🎯 The Hook

Every day you do it without thinking: your friend says *"I will come tomorrow,"* and ten minutes later you tell your mother, *"He said he would come the next day."* You just changed tense, pronoun, and time-word — flawlessly, in Hindi. In English, examiners turn this exact everyday skill into one of the most scoring — and most trap-laden — areas of the paper. Master the **logic** (not 50 memorized tables), and Narration becomes free marks.

## 🎯 Learning Objectives

By the end of this chapter you will be able to:

1. Convert any Direct Speech sentence into Indirect Speech (and vice versa) across all sentence types — statements, questions, commands, exclamations, and wishes.
2. Apply tense backshift, pronoun change, and time/place word change **simultaneously** without missing any one of them.
3. Spot the examiner's favourite traps: universal truths, "said to" vs "told", question-word retention, and modals that never change.
4. Solve any Narration MCQ in under 30 seconds using the 3-Step Filter.

---

## 1. What Is Narration?

**Direct Speech** quotes the speaker's exact words inside inverted commas ("…").
**Indirect (Reported) Speech** conveys the same meaning in the reporter's words, without quotes.

- Direct: He said, **"I am tired."**
- Indirect: He said **that he was tired.**

Every conversion involves changes in up to **four places** — remember them as **R-P-T-W**:

| Element | What changes | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **R** – Reporting verb | said → told / asked / ordered / exclaimed | said to me → told me |
| **P** – Pronoun | adjusted to reporter's viewpoint | I → he/she; my → his/her |
| **T** – Tense | one step back (backshift) | am → was; will → would |
| **W** – Words of time/place | now → then, here → there, today → that day | tomorrow → the next day |

🧠 **Active Recall:** In "She said to me, 'I will help you today'", identify the four elements that must change. (Answer: said to me → told me; I → she; will → would; you → me; today → that day.)

---

## 2. The Tense Backshift Ladder

When the reporting verb is in the **past** (said, told, asked), the tense inside the quote moves **one step into the past**:

| Direct | Indirect |
|---|---|
| Present simple (go) | Past simple (went) |
| Present continuous (is going) | Past continuous (was going) |
| Present perfect (has gone) | Past perfect (had gone) |
| Past simple (went) | Past perfect (had gone) |
| Past perfect (had gone) | **No change** (already at the bottom) |
| will / shall | would |
| can | could |
| may | might |
| **would, could, might, ought to, should, used to** | **No change** |

**When backshift does NOT happen:**
- Reporting verb in present/future: He **says**, "I am busy." → He says that he **is** busy.
- **Universal truths / habitual facts**: The teacher said, "The earth revolves around the sun." → The teacher said that the earth **revolves** around the sun.

🧠 **Active Recall:** Convert: He said, "I had already eaten." (Answer: He said that he had already eaten — past perfect never shifts further.)

---

## 3. Pronouns: The S-O-N Rule

Pronouns inside the quote change according to **S-O-N**:

- **S** – First person (I, we, me, us, my, our) changes according to the **Subject** of the reporting verb.
- **O** – Second person (you, your) changes according to the **Object** of the reporting verb.
- **N** – Third person (he, she, it, they) — **No change**.

Example: **He** said to **me**, "I will give you my book."
→ He told me that **he** (S→he) would give **me** (O→me) **his** (S→his) book.

## 4. Time & Place Word Changes

| Direct | Indirect |
|---|---|
| now | then |
| today | that day |
| tonight | that night |
| yesterday | the day before / the previous day |
| tomorrow | the next day / the following day |
| ago | before |
| here | there |
| this / these | that / those |
| last night | the night before |
| next week | the following week |
| hither / hence | thither / thence (rare, archaic) |

🧠 **Active Recall:** Convert: She said, "I met him here yesterday." (Answer: She said that she had met him there the day before.)

---

## 5. Sentence-Type Playbook

**(a) Statements** — use *that* (optional), said → told (+object).
He said to me, "I know you." → He told me that he knew me.

**(b) Interrogatives** — reporting verb → *asked/enquired*; **no question mark**; sentence becomes a statement (subject before verb).
- Yes/No questions → use **if / whether**: He said to me, "Are you ill?" → He asked me **if** I **was** ill.
- Wh-questions → keep the wh-word: She said, "Where do you live?" → She asked where I **lived**.

**(c) Imperatives** — reporting verb → ordered / requested / advised / forbade; verb becomes **to + V1** (negative: *not to + V1*).
- He said to me, "Open the door." → He ordered me **to open** the door.
- She said, "Don't touch it." → She forbade me **to touch** it. / She told me **not to touch** it.
- "Let us…" → suggested/proposed + that…should: He said, "Let us play." → He suggested that they should play.

**(d) Exclamations** — exclaimed with joy/sorrow/surprise + that; "What a/How" → very/great.
He said, "What a beautiful day!" → He exclaimed with joy that it **was** a very beautiful day.

**(e) Wishes/Prayers** — wished/prayed + that; may → might.
She said, "May you succeed!" → She prayed that I **might** succeed.

🧠 **Active Recall:** Why does "He asked me where did I live" fail? (Answer: reported questions use statement order — *where I lived*, no auxiliary inversion, no question mark.)

---

## 6. 🧠 Mnemonic: **"R-P-T-W, Sir!"**

> **R**eporting verb → **P**ronoun (S-O-N) → **T**ense (one step back) → **W**ords of time/place.
> Check all four, in this order, before you mark any option. If an option fails even one — eliminate it.

---

## 7. ⚠️ Examiner's Trap Box

**Trap 1 — The Universal Truth Freeze.** "The teacher said, 'The sun rises in the east.'" Options will offer *rose* to lure you into backshift. Correct: **rises** — universal truths never change tense.

**Trap 2 — "Said to" without an object.** "He said to me" must become "He **told** me" — never "He told to me" and never "He said me". Options like "He told to me that…" are instant eliminations.

**Trap 3 — Question Inversion.** In reported questions the auxiliary returns to statement position: "She asked where **I was** going" — not "where was I going". This is the single most-tested trap in Narration.

## 8. 🔁 Linguistic Bridge (Hindi → English)

| We think in Hindi… | Wrong English | Correct English |
|---|---|---|
| उसने मुझसे कहा कि… | He told to me that… | He **told me** that… / He **said to me** that… |
| उसने पूछा कि तुम कहाँ जाते हो | He asked that where was I going | He asked **where I was going** |
| उसने कहा मत जाओ | He said to not go | He told me **not to go** |
| कल / आज बदलना भूल गए | He said he would come **tomorrow** | He said he would come **the next day** |
| उसने कहा कि वह बीमार है (past reporting) | He said that he **is** ill | He said that he **was** ill |

## 9. Summary Table

| Sentence type | Reporting verb | Connector | Verb form inside |
|---|---|---|---|
| Statement | said / told | that (optional) | backshifted |
| Yes/No question | asked | if / whether | statement order |
| Wh-question | asked | wh-word | statement order |
| Command/Request | ordered / requested / advised | — | to + V1 / not to + V1 |
| Exclamation | exclaimed with joy/sorrow | that | very/great + backshift |
| Wish/Prayer | wished / prayed | that | may → might |
