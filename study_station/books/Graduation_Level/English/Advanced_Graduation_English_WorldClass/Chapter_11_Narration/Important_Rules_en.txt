# 📋 Important Grammar Rules & Tips — Direct & Indirect Speech (Narration)

## (a) 12 High-Yield Rules

| # | Rule | Example | Examiner's Trap |
|---|------|---------|-----------------|
| 1 | **Backshift of tense:** When the reporting verb is in the past, shift the tense one step back (Present Simple → Past Simple, Present Continuous → Past Continuous, Present Perfect → Past Perfect, Past Simple → Past Perfect). | He said, "I **am** busy." → He said that he **was** busy. | No backshift when the reported speech is a **universal truth, proverb, or habitual fact**: He said, "The sun rises in the east." → He said that the sun **rises** in the east. |
| 2 | **Will → Would, Shall → Should, Can → Could, May → Might.** | She said, "I **will** help you." → She said that she **would** help me. | **Could/Would/Should/Might/Ought to** do NOT change further. Trap option: "woulded" / "might have to" — reject. |
| 3 | **Pronoun change — S-O-N rule:** 1st person follows the **S**ubject, 2nd person follows the **O**bject, 3rd person makes **N**o change. | He said to me, "**I** will help **you**." → He told me that **he** would help **me**. | When there is no object, "you" often becomes "me/him/her" by context — examiners give a random pronoun as the trap. |
| 4 | **Time & place words change:** now→then, today→that day, tomorrow→the next day, yesterday→the previous day, ago→before, here→there, this→that, these→those. | He said, "I came **here yesterday**." → He said that he had gone **there the previous day**. | "Last night" → "the previous night", NOT "the yesterday night". |
| 5 | **Statements:** use "that" as connector; "said to" → "told" (with object) or "said" (without object). | He said **to me**, "I am tired." → He **told me that** he was tired. | "Told" must have an object; "said that" must NOT have one. Trap: "He told that…" — wrong. |
| 6 | **Yes/No questions:** use **if/whether**; the sentence becomes assertive (subject before verb); no question mark; use "asked/enquired". | He said to me, "**Are you** ready?" → He asked me **if I was** ready. | Trap: keeping question order — "if was I ready" is wrong. |
| 7 | **Wh-questions:** keep the wh-word as connector; convert to assertive order. | She said, "**Where do** you live?" → She asked **where I lived**. | Trap: adding "that" after the wh-word — "where that I lived" is wrong. |
| 8 | **Commands/Requests:** use **to + V1** (order/advise/request) or **not to + V1** (negative). | He said, "**Open** the door." → He ordered me **to open** the door. He said, "**Don't** run." → He forbade me **to run** / ordered me **not to run**. | Trap: "to not open" vs "not to open" — standard form is **not to + V1**. |
| 9 | **"Let" sentences:** proposal → "proposed/suggested that + subject + should + V1"; permission → "requested to be allowed to + V1". | He said, "**Let us** go for a walk." → He **suggested that they should go** for a walk. | "Let me do it" (permission) ≠ "Let us do it" (suggestion) — examiners swap these. |
| 10 | **Exclamations:** use "exclaimed with joy/sorrow/surprise/disgust + that"; remove "what/how" and the exclamation mark; interjections (Alas!, Hurrah!) become the emotion word. | He said, "**Alas!** I am ruined." → He **exclaimed with sorrow that** he was ruined. | Trap: keeping "what a" in indirect — "exclaimed that what a beautiful…" is wrong. |
| 11 | **Wishes/Prayers:** use "wished/prayed + that + subject + might + V1". | She said, "**May** God bless you!" → She **prayed that** God **might bless** me. | "Good morning/Thank you/Congratulations" → greeted/thanked/congratulated — no "that" clause needed. |
| 12 | **Reporting verb in present/future → NO tense change** in reported speech; only pronouns adjust. | He **says**, "I **am** ill." → He says that he **is** ill. | Trap: applying backshift anyway — "He says that he was ill" is wrong. |

## (b) ⏱️ Time Management — The 15/45-Second Rule

| Type | What it looks like | Time | Strategy |
|------|-------------------|------|----------|
| **Fact-based (15 sec)** | Direct tense/pronoun conversion; "said to → told"; time-word changes (now→then); simple command with to + V1. | ≤ 15 sec | Apply the rule mechanically. Check the reporting verb first, then eliminate options with wrong tense. Do NOT re-read. |
| **Inference-based (45 sec)** | Mixed sentences (statement + question), "let" sentences, exclamations with hidden emotion, questions inside requests ("Will you please…?"), options differing only in pronoun or connector. | ≤ 45 sec | Identify sentence type → pick connector → fix pronoun by S-O-N → verify tense. If two options look right, the difference is usually the **pronoun or the time-word** — check those two only. |
| **Skip signal** | A sentence with 3+ clauses or an unfamiliar reporting verb (e.g., "besought", "remonstrated") where all 4 options look plausible. | Skip & return | Mark it. Narration questions are independent — never sacrifice two easy ones for one hard one. |

## (c) 🔤 Morphology — Roots & Affixes in Narration Vocabulary

| Word | Root / Affix | Meaning | Example in use |
|------|-------------|---------|----------------|
| Narration | L. *narrare* = to tell | act of reporting | Narration questions test reporting. |
| Report | L. *re-* (back) + *portare* (carry) | carry back (information) | The reporting verb carries the message back. |
| Assertive | L. *ad-* + *serere* (join) | stating firmly | Questions become assertive in indirect speech. |
| Interrogative | L. *inter-* (between) + *rogare* (ask) | asking | Interrogative sentences take if/whether. |
| Imperative | L. *imperare* = to command | command | Imperatives take to + V1. |
| Exclamation | L. *ex-* (out) + *clamare* (cry) | cry out | Exclaimed with joy/sorrow. |
| Pronoun | L. *pro-* (for) + *nomen* (name) | stands for a noun | Pronouns change by the S-O-N rule. |
| Conversion | L. *con-* + *vertere* (turn) | turning around | Direct-to-indirect is a conversion. |
| Suggest | L. *sub-* (under) + *gerere* (bring) | put forward | "Let us…" → suggested that… should… |
| Request | L. *re-* + *quaerere* (seek) | ask for | "Please help me" → requested to help. |

**Quick tip:** Words built on *dic/dict* (say) — *dictate, predict, verdict, contradict* — often appear as reporting verbs in advanced papers: "He **dictated** that the letter be typed."
