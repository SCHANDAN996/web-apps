Practice Set 6 — Direct-Indirect Speech (Questions 126–150)
Difficulty: 10 Medium, 15 Hard | Examiner-trap questions marked in solutions

126. He said, "I may go to Delhi tomorrow."
(a) He said that he may go to Delhi the next day. (b) He said that he might go to Delhi the next day. (c) He said that he might go to Delhi tomorrow. (d) He said that he may go to Delhi the following day.
Answer: (b)
Solution: In reported speech, "may" changes to "might" and "tomorrow" changes to "the next day". Option (c) wrongly retains "tomorrow".
Source: NCERT Class 10 English Grammar

127. The teacher said to the boys, "Do not make a noise."
(a) The teacher forbade the boys to make a noise. (b) The teacher ordered the boys to not make a noise. (c) The teacher told the boys do not make a noise. (d) The teacher asked the boys that they should not make a noise.
Answer: (a)
Solution: Negative imperatives are reported with "forbade + to-infinitive" or "ordered/asked + not to". "Forbade the boys to make a noise" is the correct structure; "to not make" (split infinitive) is considered wrong in exams.
Source: PYQ-style

128. She said to me, "Will you lend me your book?"
(a) She asked me if I will lend her my book. (b) She told me if I would lend her my book. (c) She asked me that would I lend her my book. (d) She asked me whether I would lend her my book.
Answer: (d)
Solution: Yes/No questions take "if/whether"; "will" becomes "would"; pronouns shift: you→I, me→her, your→my. "Asked", not "told", is used for questions.
Source: NCERT Class 9 English Grammar

129. He said, "Alas! I have lost my wallet."
(a) He exclaimed with joy that he had lost his wallet. (b) He said alas that he lost his wallet. (c) He exclaimed with sorrow that he had lost his wallet. (d) He cried that he has lost his wallet.
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Alas" expresses sorrow, so "exclaimed with sorrow" is used; the present perfect "have lost" becomes past perfect "had lost".
Source: PYQ-style

130. The captain said to the soldiers, "March forward and attack the enemy."
(a) The captain said the soldiers to march forward and attack the enemy. (b) The captain told to the soldiers to march forward and attack the enemy. (c) The captain requested the soldiers that march forward and attack the enemy. (d) The captain commanded the soldiers to march forward and to attack the enemy.
Answer: (d)
Solution: Military orders use "commanded + object + to-infinitive". "Said the soldiers" and "told to the soldiers" are ungrammatical.
Source: PYQ-style

131. She said, "What a beautiful garden this is!"
(a) She exclaimed that what a beautiful garden it was. (b) She said that it is a very beautiful garden. (c) She asked if it was a beautiful garden. (d) She exclaimed with joy that it was a very beautiful garden.
Answer: (d)
Solution: Exclamatory "What a...!" sentences are reported with "exclaimed with joy/surprise that..." and "very" replaces the exclamatory form; "is" becomes "was".
Source: NCERT Class 10 English Grammar

132. He said, "I wrote a letter yesterday."
(a) He said that he had written a letter the day before. (b) He said that he wrote a letter yesterday. (c) He said that he has written a letter the previous day. (d) He said that he was writing a letter the day before.
Answer: (a)
Solution: Simple past "wrote" becomes past perfect "had written"; "yesterday" becomes "the day before / the previous day".
Source: NCERT Class 9 English Grammar

133. My friend said to me, "Let us go for a walk."
(a) My friend told me that we should go for a walk. (b) My friend said let us go for a walk. (c) My friend suggested me to go for a walk. (d) My friend proposed to me that we should go for a walk.
Answer: (d)
Solution: "Let us" expressing a proposal is reported with "proposed/suggested that... should". Note: "suggested me to go" is wrong — "suggest" is never followed by object + to-infinitive.
Source: PYQ-style

134. The mother said to her son, "Do not go out in the rain."
(a) The mother advised her son not to go out in the rain. (b) The mother said her son do not go out in the rain. (c) The mother told her son that do not go out in the rain. (d) The mother forbade her son going out in the rain.
Answer: (a)
Solution: Negative imperative → "advised/ordered + object + not + to-infinitive". "Forbade" takes the to-infinitive, not the -ing form.
Source: NCERT Class 10 English Grammar

135. He said, "I have been working here since 2010."
(a) He said that he had been working there since 2010. (b) He said that he has been working there since 2010. (c) He said that he was working there since 2010. (d) He said that he had worked there since 2010.
Answer: (a)
Solution: Present perfect continuous changes to past perfect continuous ("had been working"); "here" becomes "there"; "since 2010" remains unchanged.
Source: PYQ-style

136. Spot the error in the reported sentence: He told me (P)/ that why I was (Q)/ late for the meeting. (R)/ No error (S)
(a) Part P (b) Part R (c) Part Q (d) No error
Answer: (c)
Solution: Part Q is wrong. In reported Wh-questions the statement order is used: "why I was late", but the conjunction "that" must be dropped before a Wh-word. Correct: "He told me why I was late for the meeting." Actually "asked" fits better than "told" for a question.
Source: PYQ-style

137. She said to him, "Did you see the match last night?"
(a) She asked him that did he see the match the previous night. (b) She asked him if he had seen the match the previous night. (c) She asked him if he saw the match last night. (d) She told him whether he had seen the match the previous night.
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Did you see" (simple past question) becomes "if/whether + had seen"; "last night" becomes "the previous night". Questions use "asked", not "told".
Source: NCERT Class 10 English Grammar

138. He said, "I shall finish the work by evening."
(a) He said that he shall finish the work by evening. (b) He said that he should finish the work by evening. (c) He said that he would finish the work by evening. (d) He said that he will finish the work by evening.
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Shall" of the future becomes "would" in reported speech. "Should" is used only when "shall" carries obligation or duty, which is not the case here.
Source: PYQ-style

139. The old man said, "May God bless you, my child!"
(a) The old man said that may God bless the child. (b) The old man prayed that God might bless the child. (c) The old man exclaimed that God bless the child. (d) The old man wished that God may bless the child.
Answer: (b)
Solution: Sentences beginning with "May..." expressing a wish or prayer are reported with "prayed/wished that... might". "May" changes to "might".
Source: NCERT Class 10 English Grammar

140. Spot the error: She asked me (P)/ that whether I had (Q)/ completed my assignment. (R)/ No error (S)
(a) Part P (b) Part Q (c) Part R (d) No error
Answer: (b)
Solution: Part Q is wrong. "That" and "whether" cannot be used together. Correct: "She asked me whether I had completed my assignment." Examiner's trap: doubling of conjunctions is a favourite error.
Source: PYQ-style

141. He said, "I must leave now."
(a) He said that he must leave then. (b) He said that he has to leave now. (c) He said that he had to leave then. (d) He said that he would leave now.
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Must" expressing necessity becomes "had to"; "now" becomes "then". ("Must" may remain unchanged when it expresses a permanent rule or prohibition, but here it is personal necessity.)
Source: NCERT Class 10 English Grammar

142. The traveller said, "How beautiful the valley is!"
(a) The traveller told that the valley was beautiful. (b) The traveller asked how beautiful the valley was. (c) The traveller said that how beautiful the valley is. (d) The traveller exclaimed with wonder that the valley was very beautiful.
Answer: (d)
Solution: "How + adjective" exclamations are reported with "exclaimed with wonder/joy that... very + adjective"; "is" becomes "was".
Source: PYQ-style

143. She said to me, "Why did you not attend the class yesterday?"
(a) She asked me why I did not attend the class yesterday. (b) She asked me why I had not attended the class the day before. (c) She asked me why had I not attended the class the day before. (d) She asked me that why I had not attended the class the previous day.
Answer: (b)
Solution: Wh-question: "did you not attend" → "why I had not attended" (statement order, past perfect); "yesterday" → "the day before". Option (c) wrongly keeps the inverted order "had I".
Source: NCERT Class 9 English Grammar

144. He said, "If I were a bird, I would fly to you."
(a) He said that if he was a bird he would fly to me. (b) He said that if he had been a bird he would fly to me. (c) He said that if he were a bird he would fly to me. (d) He said that if he were a bird he will fly to me.
Answer: (c)
Solution: Unreal/imaginary conditions ("If I were...") do not change tense in reported speech because they express no real time. Examiner's trap: changing "were" to "had been" is wrong.
Source: PYQ-style

145. The principal said, "The earth revolves round the sun."
(a) The principal said that the earth revolves round the sun. (b) The principal said that the earth revolved round the sun. (c) The principal said that the earth had revolved round the sun. (d) The principal said that the earth was revolving round the sun.
Answer: (a)
Solution: Universal truths and scientific facts remain in the present tense even in reported speech. Examiner's trap: blindly applying tense backshift to a universal truth.
Source: NCERT Class 10 English Grammar

146. He said to her, "Please help me in this matter."
(a) He said to her to please help him in that matter. (b) He told her that please help him in that matter. (c) He ordered her to help him in this matter. (d) He requested her to help him in that matter.
Answer: (d)
Solution: "Please" indicates a request → "requested + object + to-infinitive"; "me" becomes "him" and "this" becomes "that".
Source: PYQ-style

147. Spot the error: He exclaimed with joy (P)/ that what a glorious (Q)/ victory it was. (R)/ No error (S)
(a) Part Q (b) Part P (c) Part R (d) No error
Answer: (a)
Solution: Part Q is wrong. After "exclaimed with joy that", the exclamatory form must be converted to a statement: "that it was a very glorious victory". "What a" cannot follow "that".
Source: PYQ-style

148. She said, "I was cooking dinner when the guests arrived."
(a) She said that she was cooking dinner when the guests arrived. (b) She said that she had been cooking dinner when the guests had arrived. (c) She said that she had been cooking dinner when the guests arrived. (d) She said that she has been cooking dinner when the guests arrived.
Answer: (c)
Solution: Past continuous "was cooking" becomes past perfect continuous "had been cooking"; the simple past in the time clause ("arrived") normally stays unchanged.
Source: NCERT Class 10 English Grammar

149. The boy said to his father, "Can I go out to play, Papa?"
(a) The boy asked his father whether he could go out to play. (b) The boy asked his father if he can go out to play. (c) The boy told his father that could he go out to play. (d) The boy requested his father that he may go out to play.
Answer: (a)
Solution: Yes/No question → "asked + if/whether"; "can" becomes "could"; "I" becomes "he". Option (d) wrongly mixes "requested... that... may".
Source: PYQ-style

150. He said, "I used to swim in this river in my childhood."
(a) He said that he was used to swim in that river in his childhood. (b) He said that he used to swim in that river in his childhood. (c) He said that he had used to swim in that river in his childhood. (d) He said that he use to swim in that river in his childhood.
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Used to" (past habit) normally remains unchanged in reported speech; only "this" changes to "that". "Was used to swim" and "had used to swim" are ungrammatical.
Source: PYQ-style
