# 📊 PYQ Analysis — Direct & Indirect Speech (Narration)

## How Exams Test This Chapter

Narration is one of the most **reliable** chapters in SSC (CGL, CHSL, MTS, GD), Banking (IBPS, SBI), and Railway (NTPC, Group D) exams. Questions appear in two formats:

1. **Direct → Indirect conversion:** A sentence in Direct Speech is given; choose the correct reported form.
2. **Indirect → Direct conversion:** Less common, but appears in SSC CGL Tier-I and CHSL.

**What examiners repeatedly test (pattern analysis in words, not numbers):**

- **Tense backshift** — the single most tested point. Present Simple → Past Simple, Present Perfect → Past Perfect, will → would, can → could.
- **Pronoun change (S-O-N rule)** — First person follows the Subject, Second person follows the Object, Third person No change.
- **Time/place word changes** — now→then, today→that day, yesterday→the day before, tomorrow→the next day, here→there, this→that, ago→before.
- **Reporting verb by sentence type** — said/told (statements), asked/enquired (questions), ordered/requested/advised (imperatives), exclaimed (exclamations).
- **Question conversion traps** — removing the question mark, changing interrogative word order to statement order, using *if/whether* for yes-no questions.
- **Universal truths & habitual facts** — **no tense change** (the examiner's favourite trap).
- **Imperative sentences** — to-infinitive structure; *not to* for negative commands.

**Examiner's favourite traps (classified):**

| Trap Type | How It Works |
|---|---|
| **Sound-Right** | An option keeps the original tense because it "sounds natural" — wrong when the reporting verb is past. |
| **Intervening Phrase** | Extra clauses distract you from the actual pronoun/tense change needed. |
| **Over-thinking** | A universal truth or a still-true fact needs **no** tense change — students change it anyway. |
| **Near-Miss Options** | Two options differ only in *said/told* or *that/if* — test of reporting-verb precision. |

**Time strategy:** Narration questions are overwhelmingly **Fact-based (15-second rule)** — apply the rule, pick the answer. Only Indirect → Direct conversions and mixed-sentence items deserve the **45-second rule**.

## Top 10 High-Yield Questions (with Solutions & Trap Classification)

**Q1.** He said, "I am busy now."
(a) He said that he is busy now.
(b) He said that he was busy then.
(c) He said that he had been busy then.
(d) He said that he was busy now.

**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** Reporting verb is past → backshift *am* → *was*; *now* → *then*. Option (a) fails on both counts; (d) keeps "now" — a Sound-Right trap.
**Trap type:** Sound-Right | **15-second rule applies** — pure tense + time-word change.

**Q2.** The teacher said, "The earth revolves around the sun."
(a) The teacher said that the earth revolved around the sun.
(b) The teacher said that the earth had revolved around the sun.
(c) The teacher said that the earth revolves around the sun.
(d) The teacher said that the earth was revolving around the sun.

**Answer: (c)**
**Solution:** Universal truths do **not** change tense in indirect speech. Students who mechanically backshift pick (a) — the classic Over-thinking trap.
**Trap type:** Over-thinking | Source: NCERT Class 9 English (Grammar)

**Q3.** She said to me, "Do you like tea?"
(a) She asked me that did I like tea.
(b) She asked me if I liked tea.
(c) She asked me whether did I like tea.
(d) She told me if I liked tea.

**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** Yes-no question → *asked + if/whether*; question order becomes statement order (*I liked*, not *did I like*); *said to* → *asked*, never *told* with a question. (a) and (c) keep interrogative order — the most common error.
**Trap type:** Near-Miss Options | **15-second rule applies.**

**Q4.** He said to her, "Where do you live?"
(a) He asked her where did she live.
(b) He asked her where she lived.
(c) He asked her that where she lived.
(d) He enquired her where she lived.

**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** Wh-question → keep the wh-word as connector (no *that*, no *if*); statement order *she lived*; backshift *do live* → *lived*. (d) fails because *enquire* takes "of" a person — "enquired of her."
**Trap type:** Sound-Right (option a mirrors spoken English)

**Q5.** The captain said to the soldiers, "March forward."
(a) The captain said the soldiers to march forward.
(b) The captain ordered the soldiers to march forward.
(c) The captain requested the soldiers that march forward.
(d) The captain told the soldiers march forward.

**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** Imperative (command) → *ordered + object + to-infinitive*. *Said* cannot take "to + verb" with an object; *requested* is too weak for a military command.
**Trap type:** Near-Miss Options | Source: PYQ-style

**Q6.** My mother said to me, "Do not go out in the rain."
(a) My mother told me do not go out in the rain.
(b) My mother forbade me to go out in the rain.
(c) My mother advised me not to go out in the rain.
(d) My mother said me not to go out in the rain.

**Answer: (c)**
**Solution:** Negative imperative → *advised/ordered + not to + verb*. (b) is grammatically possible in form but *forbade* changes the tone from advice to prohibition — examiners expect the neutral *advised … not to*. (a) and (d) have broken structures.
**Trap type:** Over-thinking (b looks "stronger" but distorts meaning)

**Q7.** He said, "Alas! I have lost my wallet."
(a) He exclaimed with sorrow that he had lost his wallet.
(b) He said alas that he lost his wallet.
(c) He exclaimed with joy that he had lost his wallet.
(d) He cried that he has lost his wallet.

**Answer: (a)**
**Solution:** Exclamation of grief → *exclaimed with sorrow*; *have lost* → *had lost* (backshift); *I* → *he* (first person follows subject). (c) reverses the emotion — a deliberate trap.
**Trap type:** Sound-Right | Source: PYQ-style

**Q8.** She said, "I will help you tomorrow."
(a) She said that she will help me tomorrow.
(b) She said that she would help me the next day.
(c) She said that she would help me tomorrow.
(d) She said that she will help me the next day.

**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** Both changes are mandatory: *will* → *would* AND *tomorrow* → *the next day*. Options (c) and (d) each fix only one — the classic half-right trap.
**Trap type:** Near-Miss Options | **15-second rule applies** — check tense and time-word together.

**Q9.** He said to me, "I met your brother yesterday."
(a) He told me that he had met my brother the day before.
(b) He told me that he met my brother yesterday.
(c) He said me that he had met my brother the day before.
(d) He told me that he has met my brother the previous day.

**Answer: (a)**
**Solution:** *Said to* → *told*; Past Simple (*met*) → Past Perfect (*had met*); *yesterday* → *the day before*; *your* → *my* (second person follows the object "me"). (c) fails on "said me" — *said* never takes a person as direct object.
**Trap type:** Intervening Phrase (multiple simultaneous changes) | **45-second rule applies** — verify all four changes before answering.

**Q10.** The boy said, "Hurrah! We have won the match."
(a) The boy said hurrah that they had won the match.
(b) The boy exclaimed with joy that they had won the match.
(c) The boy exclaimed with sorrow that they had won the match.
(d) The boy told that they have won the match.

**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** *Hurrah* signals joy → *exclaimed with joy*; *we* → *they*; *have won* → *had won*. (c) contradicts the emotion; (a) keeps the interjection, which is never retained in reported speech.
**Trap type:** Sound-Right | Source: NCERT Class 10 English (Grammar)

## Key Takeaways from the Pattern

1. **Master the backshift table** — it alone solves the majority of narration questions.
2. **Universal truths, proverbs, and habitual facts never change tense** — the highest-frequency trap.
3. **In questions, kill the question order** — "asked if I liked," never "asked did I like."
4. **Check every option for *both* tense and time-word changes** — half-fixed options are planted deliberately.
5. **said vs. told vs. asked** — *told* needs an object, *said* doesn't, *asked* is for questions. This micro-distinction eliminates two options instantly in most sets.
