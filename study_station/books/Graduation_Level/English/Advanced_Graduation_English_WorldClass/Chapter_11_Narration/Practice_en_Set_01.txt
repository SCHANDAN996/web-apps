Questions 1–20: Easy | Questions 21–25: Medium

1. He said, "I am happy."
Choose the correct indirect speech.
(a) He said that he was happy. (b) He said that he is happy. (c) He said that I was happy. (d) He said that he had been happy.
Answer: (a)
Solution: In reported speech, the present simple "am" shifts to past simple "was", and "I" changes to "he" to match the subject. Option (b) wrongly keeps the present tense.
Source: NCERT Class 9 English Grammar

2. She said to me, "I will help you."
Choose the correct indirect speech.
(a) She told me that she will help me. (b) She told me that I would help her. (c) She said me that she would help you. (d) She told me that she would help me.
Answer: (d)
Solution: "Said to me" becomes "told me"; "will" changes to "would"; "I" becomes "she" and "you" becomes "me". Option (a) fails to backshift "will".

3. The teacher said, "The sun rises in the east."
Choose the correct indirect speech.
(a) The teacher said that the sun rose in the east. (b) The teacher said that the sun was rising in the east. (c) The teacher said that the sun had risen in the east. (d) The teacher said that the sun rises in the east.
Answer: (d)
Solution: Universal truths and habitual facts do not change tense in reported speech, so "rises" stays unchanged. Option (a) wrongly backshifts a universal truth — a classic examiner's trap.

4. He said, "I have finished my work."
Choose the correct indirect speech.
(a) He said that he has finished his work. (b) He said that he finished his work. (c) He said that he had finished his work. (d) He said that he has finished my work.
Answer: (c)
Solution: Present perfect "have finished" shifts to past perfect "had finished", and "my" becomes "his". Option (a) keeps the present perfect, which is incorrect after a past reporting verb.

Answer: (d)
Solution: Past continuous "was reading" shifts to past perfect continuous "had been reading". Option (a) is the trap — it looks right but fails to backshift the past continuous.

7. He said to her, "Do you like tea?"
Choose the correct indirect speech.
(a) He asked her if she liked tea. (b) He asked her that did she like tea. (c) He asked her if she likes tea. (d) He told her if she liked tea.
Answer: (a)
Solution: Yes/no questions are reported with "if/whether", the question form becomes a statement ("she liked"), and "asked" replaces "said to". Option (b) wrongly keeps the inverted question order.

8. She said to me, "Where do you live?"
Choose the correct indirect speech.
(a) She asked me where I lived. (b) She asked me where did I live. (c) She asked me where do I live. (d) She asked me where I live.
Answer: (a)
Solution: Wh-questions keep the wh-word but take statement order ("I lived") and backshift the tense. Options (b) and (c) wrongly keep the question inversion — a frequent Hindi-speaker error.

9. The captain said to the soldiers, "March forward."
Choose the correct indirect speech.
(a) The captain ordered the soldiers that march forward. (b) The captain ordered the soldiers to march forward. (c) The captain said the soldiers to march forward. (d) The captain requested the soldiers to march forward.
Answer: (b)
Solution: Commands are reported with "ordered/commanded + object + to-infinitive". Option (d) changes the meaning — an army captain orders, he does not request.
Source: NCERT Class 9 English Grammar

10. Mother said to me, "Do not go out in the rain."
Choose the correct indirect speech.
(a) Mother told me to not go out in the rain. (b) Mother said me not to go out in the rain. (c) Mother forbade me to go out in the rain. (d) Mother told me do not go out in the rain.
Answer: (c)
Solution: Negative commands use "told + object + not to" or "forbade + object + to". Option (a) misplaces "not" before "to" instead of after the object in standard exam usage; "forbade" is the cleanest form.

11. He said, "Alas! I have lost my wallet."
Choose the correct indirect speech.
(a) He exclaimed with joy that he had lost his wallet. (b) He exclaimed with sorrow that he had lost his wallet. (c) He said alas that he lost his wallet. (d) He cried that he has lost his wallet.
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Alas!" expresses grief, so it is reported with "exclaimed with sorrow"; "have lost" becomes "had lost". Option (a) reverses the emotion — a meaning trap.

17. She said, "What a beautiful flower it is!"
Choose the correct indirect speech.
(a) She exclaimed that it was a very beautiful flower. (b) She said what a beautiful flower it was. (c) She exclaimed that it is a very beautiful flower. (d) She asked if it was a beautiful flower.
Answer: (a)
Solution: Exclamations with "what a/how" are reported with "exclaimed that" and "very" replaces the exclamatory form; "is" becomes "was". Option (b) keeps the direct exclamation structure.

(a) He asked me why was I late. (b) He asked me why I am late. (c) He asked me why I was late. (d) He told me why I was late.
Answer: (c)
Solution: Wh-questions take statement order ("I was") and "are" backshifts to "was". Option (a) keeps the inverted order — the most common error tested in narration questions.

Choose the correct indirect speech.

Answer: (a)
Solution: "Could you…?" is a polite request in question form, reported with "asked + if + could"; "this" becomes "that". Option (c) misreads a request as a command — a meaning trap.

Choose the correct indirect speech.
(a) She said that she would finish that work that day. (b) She said that she must finish this work today. (c) She said that she had to finish that work today. (d) She said that she had to finish that work that day.
Answer: (d)
Solution: "Must" (obligation) becomes "had to", "this" becomes "that", and "today" becomes "that day". Option (c) is the trap — it changes the modal but leaves "today" unchanged.
