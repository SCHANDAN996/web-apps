🧒 FEYNMAN TECHNIQUE — Direct & Indirect Speech, Explained Like You're 12

Imagine your friend Ravi tells you something, and later you have to pass that message to your mother. That's ALL narration is. There are only two ways to pass a message:

**Way 1 — Direct Speech:** You repeat Ravi's exact words, word for word, inside quotation marks.
👉 Ravi said, "I am tired."

**Way 2 — Indirect (Reported) Speech:** You tell the message in YOUR OWN words, like a news reporter. No quotation marks.
👉 Ravi said that he was tired.

That's it. The whole chapter is just this one game: **"Repeat exactly" vs. "Report in your own words."**

---

🎯 The Hardest Part Made Easy: Why does "am" become "was"?

Think of it like a **time machine**. When Ravi spoke, it was the present for him. But when YOU report it later, that moment has already gone into the past. So every verb takes **one step back in time**:

- am / is → **was**
- are → **were**
- has / have → **had**
- will → **would**
- can → **could**

🪄 **The Unforgettable Trick — "PRESENT GOES TO PAST, PAST GOES TO HAD":**
Whenever the reporting verb is in the past (said, told), push every verb inside the quote **one step backward**. If it's already in the past, push it into **"had"** (past perfect).

- Direct: She said, "I **write** a letter." → Indirect: She said that she **wrote** a letter.
- Direct: She said, "I **wrote** a letter." → Indirect: She said that she **had written** a letter.

---

🏠 The Daily-Life Example: The "Here-There" Rule

Imagine Ravi is standing in the kitchen and says: "I will eat **this** mango **here today**."

You run to the next room and report it to your mother. Now YOU are not in the kitchen, and maybe it's the next day. So the pointing words change with your position:

- **this** → that
- **these** → those
- **here** → there
- **now** → then
- **today** → that day
- **tomorrow** → the next day
- **yesterday** → the previous day
- **ago** → before

👉 Reported: Ravi said that he would eat **that** mango **there that day**.

Memory hook: **"Near becomes Far"** — everything close (this, here, now, today) gets pushed far away (that, there, then, that day), because the reporter is standing at a distance from the original scene.

---

👤 The Person Puzzle: Who is "I"?

When Ravi says "**I** am tired," the word "I" means Ravi. But when YOU report it, "I" would mean YOU — which is wrong! So pronouns follow the **SON rule**:

- **S** — First person (I, we) changes according to the **Subject** of the reporting verb.
- **O** — Second person (you) changes according to the **Object** of the reporting verb.
- **N** — Third person (he, she, it, they) — **No change**.

Example: He said to me, "**I** will help **you**."
- "I" → follows Subject (He) → **he**
- "you" → follows Object (me) → **me**
👉 He told me that **he** would help **me**.

---

❓ Questions and Commands — The Two Special Shapes

**Questions:** Remove the question mark, remove the helping verb from the front, and add **if/whether** (for yes/no questions) or keep the **Wh-word**.
- He said to me, "**Are** you ready?" → He asked me **if** I **was** ready.
- She said to him, "**Where** do you live?" → She asked him **where** he **lived**.

**Commands/Requests:** Use **to + verb** (positive) or **not to + verb** (negative).
- The teacher said, "**Open** your books." → The teacher ordered the students **to open** their books.
- Mother said, "**Do not** go out." → Mother forbade me **to go** out. (or: told me not to go out)

---

⚠️ The One Trap That Fools Everyone

If the direct speech is a **universal truth, proverb, or habitual fact**, the tense does NOT change — even if the reporting verb is past!
- The teacher said, "The sun **rises** in the east." → The teacher said that the sun **rises** in the east. (NOT "rose" — the sun still rises!)
