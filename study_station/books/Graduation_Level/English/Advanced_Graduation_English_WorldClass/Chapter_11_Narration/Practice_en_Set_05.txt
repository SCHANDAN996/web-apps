Practice Set 5 — Direct-Indirect Speech (Questions 101–125)
Difficulty: Questions 101–115: Medium | Questions 116–125: Hard

101. He said, "I may go to Delhi tomorrow."
(a) He said that he may go to Delhi the next day. (b) He said that he might go to Delhi tomorrow. (c) He said that he might go to Delhi the next day. (d) He said that he may go to Delhi the day after.
Answer: (c)
Solution: "May" changes to "might" in reported speech, and "tomorrow" becomes "the next day". Option (b) wrongly keeps "tomorrow".
Source: NCERT Class 10 English Grammar

102. She said to me, "Do you like coffee?"
(a) She asked me if I liked coffee. (b) She asked me did I like coffee. (c) She asked me whether I like coffee. (d) She asked me that did I like coffee.
Answer: (a)
Solution: Yes/No questions take "if/whether" with statement word order and backshifted tense: "if I liked". Option (b) wrongly keeps the question order "did I like".
Source: PYQ-style

103. The teacher said, "The earth revolves around the sun."
(a) The teacher said that the earth revolved around the sun. (b) The teacher said that the earth had revolved around the sun. (c) The teacher said that the earth was revolving around the sun. (d) The teacher said that the earth revolves around the sun.
Answer: (d)
Solution: Universal truths and scientific facts do not change tense in reported speech, so "revolves" stays unchanged.
Source: NCERT Class 9 English Grammar

104. He said, "Alas! I have lost my wallet."
(a) He cried that he lost his wallet. (b) He said with sorrow that he has lost his wallet. (c) He exclaimed with joy that he had lost his wallet. (d) He exclaimed with sorrow that he had lost his wallet.
Answer: (d)
Solution: "Alas" signals grief, so use "exclaimed with sorrow"; the present perfect backshifts to past perfect "had lost".
Source: PYQ-style

105. She said to him, "Please help me with this bag."
(a) She said to him to help her with that bag. (b) She told him that help her with this bag. (c) She requested him to help her with that bag. (d) She requested him that he helps her with the bag.
Answer: (c)
Solution: Polite requests with "please" are reported with "requested + object + to-infinitive"; "this" becomes "that".
Source: NCERT Class 10 English Grammar

106. He said, "I was writing a letter."
(a) He said that he was writing a letter. (b) He said that he has been writing a letter. (c) He said that he had been writing a letter. (d) He said that he wrote a letter.
Answer: (c)
Solution: Past continuous backshifts to past perfect continuous: "was writing" becomes "had been writing".
Source: PYQ-style

107. Mother said to me, "Do not go out in the rain."
(a) Mother forbade me to go out in the rain. (b) Mother told me do not go out in the rain. (c) Mother said me not to go out in the rain. (d) Mother requested that I do not go out in the rain.
Answer: (a)
Solution: Negative imperatives are reported with "forbade + object + to-infinitive" or "told me not to go". Option (b) wrongly keeps the imperative form.
Source: NCERT Class 10 English Grammar

108. He said, "I have been waiting here since morning."
(a) He said that he has been waiting there since morning. (b) He said that he had been waiting there since morning. (c) He said that he was waiting there since morning. (d) He said that he had waited here since morning.
Answer: (b)
Solution: Present perfect continuous backshifts to past perfect continuous, and "here" becomes "there".
Source: PYQ-style

109. She said, "What a beautiful garden this is!"
(a) She exclaimed that it was a very beautiful garden. (b) She said what a beautiful garden that was. (c) She exclaimed with joy that it was a very beautiful garden. (d) She told that the garden is very beautiful.
Answer: (c)
Solution: Exclamatory sentences are reported with "exclaimed with joy/surprise" plus a statement; "what a beautiful" becomes "very beautiful" and tense backshifts.
Source: NCERT Class 9 English Grammar

110. He said to me, "Will you lend me your pen?"
(a) He asked me will I lend him my pen. (b) He asked me if I would lend him my pen. (c) He asked me whether I will lend him his pen. (d) He asked me that would I lend him my pen.
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Will" backshifts to "would", the question becomes a statement after "if", and pronouns adjust: "your pen" becomes "my pen" from the listener's side.
Source: PYQ-style

111. The captain said, "Hurrah! We have won the match."
(a) The captain exclaimed with sorrow that they had won the match. (b) The captain said hurrah that they won the match. (c) The captain exclaimed with joy that they had won the match. (d) The captain told that they have won the match.
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Hurrah" expresses joy; present perfect "have won" backshifts to past perfect "had won", and "we" becomes "they".
Source: NCERT Class 10 English Grammar

112. He said, "I can solve this problem easily."
(a) He said that he can solve that problem easily. (b) He said that he could solve this problem easily. (c) He said that he may solve that problem easily. (d) He said that he could solve that problem easily.
Answer: (d)
Solution: "Can" backshifts to "could" and "this" becomes "that". Option (b) is a trap that changes the modal but forgets the demonstrative.
Source: PYQ-style

113. She said to me, "Where did you buy this dress?"
(a) She asked me where I had bought that dress. (b) She asked me where did I buy that dress. (c) She asked me where I bought this dress. (d) She asked me that where I had bought that dress.
Answer: (a)
Solution: Wh-questions keep the wh-word, take statement order, and simple past backshifts to past perfect: "I had bought". Option (b) wrongly keeps inverted order.
Source: NCERT Class 10 English Grammar

114. He said, "Let us go for a walk."
(a) He said that they should go for a walk. (b) He said let them go for a walk. (c) He told to go for a walk. (d) He proposed that they should go for a walk.
Answer: (d)
Solution: "Let us" expressing a suggestion is reported with "proposed/suggested that they should go".
Source: PYQ-style

115. The old man said, "May you live long!"
(a) The old man said that may I live long. (b) The old man wished that I may live long. (c) The old man prayed that I might live long. (d) The old man exclaimed that I lived long.
Answer: (c)
Solution: "May" expressing a wish or blessing is reported with "prayed/wished that + might".
Source: NCERT Class 9 English Grammar

116. He said, "If I were a bird, I would fly to you."
(a) He said that if he were a bird, he would fly to me. (b) He said that if he had been a bird, he would fly to me. (c) He said that if he was a bird, he would have flown to me. (d) He said that if he were a bird, he will fly to me.
Answer: (a)
Solution: Unreal conditional sentences (subjunctive "were") do not backshift; "were" and "would" remain unchanged in reported speech.
Source: PYQ-style

117. She said, "I shall have finished the work by evening."
(a) She said that she shall have finished the work by evening. (b) She said that she would have finished the work by evening. (c) She said that she would finish the work by evening. (d) She said that she had finished the work by evening.
Answer: (b)
Solution: Future perfect "shall have finished" backshifts to "would have finished"; the perfect structure is retained.
Source: NCERT Class 10 English Grammar

118. Spot the error in the reported sentence: He told me (P) / that why I was (Q) / late for the meeting (R) / that morning. (S)
(a) Part P (b) Part Q (c) Part R (d) No error
Answer: (b)
Solution: Part Q is wrong. A reported wh-question cannot begin with "that"; it should be "He asked me why I was late". "Told" with a question word is also incorrect — "asked" is needed.
Source: PYQ-style

119. He said, "I used to swim in this river in my childhood."
(a) He said that he use to swim in that river in his childhood. (b) He said that he was used to swim in that river in his childhood. (c) He said that he had used to swimming in that river in his childhood. (d) He said that he used to swim in that river in his childhood.
Answer: (d)
Solution: "Used to" for past habits does not change in reported speech; only "this" becomes "that". Option (b) confuses it with "be used to + -ing".
Source: PYQ-style

120. The traveller said, "I had been walking for hours before I found shelter."
(a) The traveller said that he had been walking for hours before he had found shelter. (b) The traveller said that he has been walking for hours before he found shelter. (c) The traveller said that he was walking for hours before he had found shelter. (d) The traveller said that he had walked for hours before he found shelter.
Answer: (a)
Solution: Past perfect continuous and past perfect do not backshift further; both remain unchanged in reported speech.
Source: NCERT Class 10 English Grammar

121. He said to her, "I will help you if you ask me politely."
(a) He told her that he would help her if she would ask him politely. (b) He told her that he will help her if she asks him politely. (c) He said her that he would help her if she asked him politely. (d) He told her that he would help her if she asked him politely.
Answer: (d)
Solution: In a real conditional, "will" becomes "would" and "ask" becomes "asked". Option (a) is a classic trap — "would" is never used in the if-clause of a first conditional.
Source: PYQ-style

122. Spot the error: She exclaimed with joy (P) / that what a beautiful (Q) / sunset it was (R) / that evening. (S)
(a) Part P (b) Part Q (c) Part R (d) No error
Answer: (b)
Solution: Part Q is wrong. In reported exclamations, "what a beautiful" must be converted to "very beautiful": "that it was a very beautiful sunset". The exclamatory structure cannot be retained.
Source: NCERT Class 9 English Grammar

123. He said, "You needn't come tomorrow."
(a) He said that I didn't need to come tomorrow. (b) He said that I needn't come the next day. (c) He said that I needed not come the next day. (d) He said that I need not to come the next day.
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Needn't" usually remains unchanged in reported speech, while "tomorrow" becomes "the next day". Option (d) wrongly adds "to" after "need not".
Source: PYQ-style

124. The boy said, "I wish I were a millionaire."
(a) The boy said that he wished he were a millionaire. (b) The boy said that he wished he had been a millionaire. (c) The boy said that he wishes he was a millionaire. (d) The boy said that he wished he would be a millionaire.
Answer: (a)
Solution: After "wish", the subjunctive "were" expresses an unreal present desire and does not backshift; only the reporting verb and pronoun change.
Source: PYQ-style

125. She said to him, "Why didn't you attend the meeting yesterday?"
(a) She asked him why he had not attended the meeting the previous day. (b) She asked him why did he not attend the meeting the previous day. (c) She asked him why he did not attend the meeting yesterday. (d) She asked him that why he had not attended the meeting the day before.
Answer: (a)
Solution: The negative wh-question takes statement order, "didn't attend" backshifts to "had not attended", and "yesterday" becomes "the previous day". Option (b) keeps the inverted question order — a common trap.
Source: NCERT Class 10 English Grammar
