26. He said, "I am happy."
(a) He said that he is happy. (b) He said that he had been happy. (c) He said that I was happy. (d) He said that he was happy.
Answer: (d)
Solution: In indirect speech, the present simple "am" shifts to past simple "was", and "I" changes to "he" to match the subject of the reporting verb.

27. She said to me, "I will help you."
(a) She told me that she will help me. (b) She told me that she would help you. (c) She told me that she would help me. (d) She said me that she would help me.
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Will" changes to "would" in reported speech, and "said to me" becomes "told me". The pronoun "you" refers to the listener, so it becomes "me".

32. Mother said to me, "Do not go out in the rain."

Answer: (c)
Solution: Negative imperatives are reported with "forbade + to-infinitive" or "told/ordered + not to". "Forbade me to go" is the correct structure; "told me to not go" is faulty split-infinitive style here.

(c) The manager asked the clerk if he had not submitted the report that he has asked for the week before. Answer: (b)
Solution: A negative yes/no question is reported with "whether/if ... not"; "did submit" shifts to "had submitted", "asked" to "had asked", and "last week" to "the week before". The trap is keeping "did not submit" unchanged.
