51. He said, "I am happy."
(a) He said that he is happy. (b) He said that he had been happy. (c) He said that I was happy. (d) He said that he was happy.
Answer: (d)
Solution: Present simple "am" changes to past simple "was" in reported speech, and "I" changes to "he" to match the subject of the reporting verb.
Source: NCERT Class 9 English Grammar

52. She said to me, "I like your dress."
(a) She said me that she liked my dress. (b) She told me that she liked my dress. (c) She told me that she likes my dress. (d) She told to me that she liked my dress.
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Said to me" becomes "told me" (no "to" after told); "like" becomes "liked" and "your" becomes "my".

53. The teacher said, "The earth revolves around the sun."

Answer: (a)
Solution: Universal truths do not change tense in reported speech, so "revolves" stays unchanged.

57. Mother said to me, "Do not go out in the rain."

Answer: (a)
Solution: Negative imperatives are reported with "forbade + to-infinitive" or "told me not to go". "Forbade me to go" is the correct compact form.

61. He said to me, "Why did you come late?"
(a) He asked me why I came late. (b) He asked me why did I come late. (c) He asked me why I had come late. (d) He asked me that why I had come late.
Answer: (c)
Solution: Wh-questions use statement word order (no inversion), and past simple "did come" shifts to past perfect "had come". "That" is not used after "asked" with a wh-word.

Answer: (c)
Solution: "Shall I...?" expressing an offer is reported with "offered to". A plain "asked if he should" would only suit a genuine question about duty.

74. The old man said, "May you live long!"
(a) The old man said that may I live long. (b) The old man exclaimed that I might live long. (c) The old man prayed that I might live long. (d) The old man wished that I may live long.
Answer: (c)
Solution: Optative sentences expressing a blessing are reported with "prayed/wished that"; "may" becomes "might" and "you" changes to "I" to match the object.
Source: NCERT Class 10 English Grammar

75. He said to me, "I shall help you if you work hard."
(a) He told me that he should help me if I worked hard. (b) He told me that he would help me if I had worked hard. (c) He told me that he will help me if I work hard. (d) He told me that he would help me if I worked hard.
Answer: (d)
Solution: In a conditional sentence, "shall" in the main clause becomes "would" and the if-clause "work" becomes "worked" (past simple), not past perfect.
