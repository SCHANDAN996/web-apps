# 📋 Important Grammar Rules & Tips — Conjunction

## (a) 12 High-Yield Rules

| # | Rule | Example | Examiner's Trap |
|---|------|---------|-----------------|
| 1 | **Correlative pairs must match**: either…or, neither…nor, not only…but also, both…and, whether…or, hardly/scarcely…when, no sooner…than. | *Neither Ram nor Shyam came.* | "Neither…or" ❌ — the trap swaps the second half of the pair. |
| 2 | **Not only…but also** — the two joined elements must be grammatically parallel. | *He is not only a singer but also a dancer.* | "He not only sings but also he dances" ❌ — broken parallelism. |
| 3 | **No sooner…than / Hardly, Scarcely…when** — use past perfect in the first clause, simple past in the second; inversion after the negative adverb. | *No sooner had she entered than the phone rang.* | "No sooner…when" ❌ — mixing the two pairs is the classic trap. |
| 4 | **Until vs Unless**: until = time; unless = condition (= if…not). Never use "unless" with a negative. | *Wait until I return. / Unless you work hard, you will fail.* | "Unless you do not work hard…" ❌ — double negative reverses meaning. |
| 5 | **Since (reason) vs Because**: both show reason, but "since" at the start of a sentence can also mean time — check context. "Because" is never paired with "so". | *Because he was ill, he stayed home.* | "Because he was ill, so he stayed home" ❌ — two conjunctions for one link. |
| 6 | **Although/Though never take "but"**; yet may follow though for emphasis. | *Although he is rich, he is humble.* | "Although he is rich, but he is humble" ❌ — the most repeated exam error. |
| 7 | **That (conjunction) after verbs of saying/thinking**; do not use "that" after interrogative words in indirect speech. | *He said that he was tired. / He asked where I lived.* | "He asked that where I lived" ❌ — double connector. |
| 8 | **As…as (positive) / so…as (negative)** in comparisons. | *She is as tall as her brother. / He is not so tall as his brother.* | "not as tall than" ❌ — mixing comparison markers. |
| 9 | **Lest…should** — "lest" is always followed by "should" (or subjunctive), never by "will/would", and never with a negative. | *Walk fast lest you should miss the train.* | "lest you will miss" / "lest you should not miss" ❌ — both wrong. |
| 10 | **Until/Till vs By**: until = duration up to a point; by = deadline. | *The shop is open until 9 pm. / Submit the form by Friday.* | "Submit until Friday" ❌ when a deadline is meant. |
| 11 | **Provided (that) / In case / As long as** — conditional conjunctions; the clause after them stays in present tense for future meaning. | *I will come provided that you invite me.* | "provided you will invite me" ❌ — future tense in a conditional clause. |
| 12 | **While vs Whereas vs Whilst**: while = time or mild contrast; whereas = sharp contrast between two facts. | *He likes tea, whereas she likes coffee.* | Using "while" for strong logical contrast in formal options — examiners prefer "whereas". |

## (b) Time Management — The 15/45-Second Rule

**⚡ 15-Second Questions (Fact / Rule-Recall based)** — solve on sight:
- Spot-the-error with a broken correlative pair (*neither…or*, *no sooner…when*).
- Fill-in-the-blank where only one conjunction fits the pair (*hardly…___* → when).
- "Although…but" / "because…so" double-conjunction errors.
- Lest + should questions.
- Strategy: read only the two halves of the sentence, match the pair, mark, move on.

**🔍 45-Second Questions (Inference / Meaning-based)** — invest time:
- Sentence improvement where 2–3 options are grammatically possible and you must judge meaning (until vs unless vs in case).
- Choosing the conjunction that preserves the logical relation (contrast vs cause vs condition) in a long sentence.
- Para-jumble or cloze questions where the conjunction signals the link between ideas (however, therefore, moreover).
- Strategy: identify the logical relationship first (addition / contrast / cause / condition / time), then eliminate options that express the wrong relation.

## (c) Morphology — Roots & Affixes in Conjunction Vocabulary

| Root / Affix | Meaning | Derived Words (useful in RC & cloze) |
|---|---|---|
| **junct- / join-** (Latin *jungere*) | to join | conjunction, junction, juncture, adjunct, disjunction |
| **con- / co-** | together | connect, coordinate, cooperate, combine |
| **dis-** | apart, not | disconnect, disjoin, contrast (via *contra-*) |
| **sub-** | under | subordinate, subordinating conjunction |
| **ordin-** (Latin *ordo*) | order | coordinate, subordinate, ordinary |
| **contra- / counter-** | against | contrast, contrary, counterargument (words that signal "but"-type links) |
| **sequ-** (Latin *sequi*) | to follow | consequence, subsequent, sequence (words that signal "so/therefore"-type links) |
| **-ive / -or** (suffixes) | forms adjectives/nouns | connective, connector, coordinator |

**Memory hook:** *Conjunction = con (together) + junct (join)* — a word whose only job is to **join together**. Every subordinating conjunction makes one clause *sub-ordinate* (of lower order) to the main clause.
