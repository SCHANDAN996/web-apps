Card 1
Front: What is a conjunction?
Back: A word that joins words, phrases, or clauses — e.g., and, but, because, although.

Card 2
Front: Name the three types of conjunctions.
Back: Coordinating, Subordinating, and Correlative conjunctions.

Card 3
Front: 🧠 Mnemonic: How do you remember the 7 coordinating conjunctions?
Back: FANBOYS — For, And, Nor, But, Or, Yet, So.

Card 4
Front: Which conjunctions are used in the correlative pair with "either"?
Back: "Either…or". (Similarly: neither…nor, both…and, not only…but also, whether…or.)

Card 5
Front: ⚠️ Examiner's Trap: "Although he was tired, but he kept working." — Correct or wrong?
Back: Wrong. "Although" and "but" cannot be used together. Use either "Although he was tired, he kept working" or "He was tired, but he kept working."

Card 6
Front: What verb form follows "No sooner…than"?
Back: Past perfect in the first clause, simple past in the second: "No sooner had he arrived than the show began."

Card 7
Front: Which word pairs with "Hardly" and "Scarcely" — "than" or "when"?
Back: "When". Hardly/Scarcely…when; but No sooner…than.

Card 8
Front: ⚠️ Examiner's Trap: "Neither of the two boys have done his homework." — Spot the error.
Back: "Have" should be "has". "Neither of" takes a singular verb.

Card 9
Front: When does "that" act as a conjunction?
Back: When it introduces a noun clause: "He said that he was ill." (As a relative pronoun it replaces a noun; as a conjunction it merely joins.)

Card 10
Front: What is the rule for "not only…but also" regarding parallel structure?
Back: The same part of speech must follow both: "He is not only a singer but also a dancer" (noun…noun), not "He not only sings but also a dancer."

Card 11
Front: 🧠 Mnemonic: How do you remember common subordinating conjunctions?
Back: "AWHITE BUS" — After, While, If, Though, Although, Because, Until, Since (or use "Since Because Although Unless").

Card 12
Front: "Unless" means the same as which phrase?
Back: "If…not". So "Unless you work hard, you will fail" = "If you do not work hard, you will fail." Never write "unless you do not work hard."

Card 13
Front: ⚠️ Examiner's Trap: "Scarcely he had reached the station when the train left." — Error?
Back: Inversion missing. After "Scarcely/Hardly/No sooner" at the start, invert: "Scarcely had he reached the station when the train left."

Card 14
Front: Difference between "since" and "for" as conjunctions of reason?
Back: "Since" (= because) introduces a reason clause: "Since it was raining, we stayed in." "For" as a conjunction is formal and comes mid-sentence: "We stayed in, for it was raining."

Card 15
Front: Which conjunction shows contrast: "yet" or "so"?
Back: "Yet" shows contrast (He is rich, yet unhappy); "so" shows result (He was ill, so he stayed home).

Card 16
Front: "Both…and" — what must be true of the two joined elements?
Back: They must be grammatically parallel: "Both Ram and Shyam" (noun…noun), not "Both Ram and a teacher."

Card 17
Front: Can a sentence begin with "Because" or "Although"?
Back: Yes — if it is a dependent clause followed by a main clause: "Because it rained, the match was cancelled." A dependent clause alone is a fragment.

Card 18
Front: "Lest" is followed by which modal/auxiliary?
Back: "Should" (or the subjunctive): "Work hard lest you should fail." Never use "will" after lest.

Card 19
Front: ⚠️ Examiner's Trap: "The reason is because he was late." — Correct?
Back: Wrong. Use "The reason is that he was late." "Reason…because" is redundant.

Card 20
Front: What is the difference between "until" and "unless"?
Back: "Until" relates to time (Wait until I come); "unless" relates to condition (I won't go unless you invite me).
