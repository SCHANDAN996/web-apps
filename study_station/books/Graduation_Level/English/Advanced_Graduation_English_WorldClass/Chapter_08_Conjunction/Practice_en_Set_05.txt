101. Choose the correct sentence:
(a) Scarcely he had reached the station when the train left. (b) Scarcely had he reached the station than the train left. (c) Scarcely he reached the station than the train left. (d) Scarcely had he reached the station when the train left.
Answer: (d)
Solution: "Scarcely" is a negative adverb, so inversion is required: "Scarcely had he reached…". It pairs with "when", not "than" ("than" pairs with "no sooner"). Option (a) lacks inversion; options (c) and (b) wrongly use "than".
Source: NCERT Class 10 English Grammar

102. Spot the error: Part P: No sooner did the bell ring / Part Q: when the students / Part R: rushed out of the classroom. / Part S: No error
(a) Part P (b) No error (c) Part R (d) Part Q
Answer: (d)
Solution: "No sooner" is always followed by "than", never "when". Part Q should read "than the students". This is a classic examiner trap because "when" sounds natural after time expressions.

103. Choose the correctly framed sentence:
(a) He not only is a good singer but also a good dancer. (b) He is not only a good singer but a good dancer also. (c) Not only he is a good singer but also a good dancer. (d) He is not only a good singer but also a good dancer.
Answer: (d)
Solution: Correlative conjunctions "not only…but also" must be placed immediately before the parallel elements they join — here the two noun phrases "a good singer" and "a good dancer". Options (c) and (a) misplace "not only".

104. Fill in the blank: _______ he is rich, he is not generous.
(a) Though (b) Because (c) Since (d) As
Answer: (a)
Solution: The two clauses show contrast (wealth vs. lack of generosity), so a concessive conjunction "Though" is needed. "Because/Since/As" express reason, which would make the sentence illogical.

105. Choose the correct sentence:
(a) Unless you do not work hard, you will fail.
(b) Unless you work hard, you will fail.
(c) Until you work hard, you will fail.
(d) Unless you work hard, you will not fail.
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Unless" itself means "if…not", so adding "not" (option a) creates a double negative. "Until" refers to time, not condition. Option (d) reverses the logic.

106. Spot the error: Part P: Neither the teacher / Part Q: nor the students / Part R: was present in the hall. / Part S: No error
(a) Part P (b) Part Q (c) Part R (d) No error
Answer: (c)
Solution: With "neither…nor", the verb agrees with the nearer subject — "students" (plural) — so it should be "were present". This proximity rule is a favourite examiner trap.

107. Fill in the blank: He worked hard _______ he might pass the examination.
(a) although (b) because (c) lest (d) so that
Answer: (d)
Solution: The clause expresses purpose, and "so that + might" correctly conveys purpose in the past. "Lest" expresses negative purpose (fear) and takes "should", which does not fit the meaning here.

108. Choose the correct sentence:
(a) He is senior to me by two years. (b) He is senior than me by two years. (c) He is more senior than me by two years. (d) He is senior from me by two years.
Answer: (a)
Solution: Latin comparatives like senior, junior, superior, inferior, prior and prefer take "to", not "than". They are already comparative, so "more senior" is also wrong.

109. Spot the error: Part P: Although it was raining heavily, / Part Q: but the match / Part R: continued as scheduled. / Part S: No error
(a) Part P (b) Part Q (c) Part R (d) No error
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Although" and "but" cannot be used together in the same sentence — one concessive marker is enough. Delete "but" from Part Q. This is a classic Hindi-speaker trap ("यद्यपि…फिर भी").

110. Fill in the blank: _______ the rain stopped, the children went out to play.
(a) Until (b) Lest (c) Unless (d) As soon as
Answer: (d)
Solution: The sentence shows one event immediately following another, so "As soon as" is correct. "Until" would need a negative main clause ("did not go out until…"), and "lest/unless" do not fit the meaning.

111. Choose the correct sentence:
(a) He is both intelligent as well as hardworking. (b) He is both intelligent or hardworking. (c) He is both intelligent and hardworking. (d) He is both intelligent but hardworking.
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Both" pairs only with "and". "As well as" cannot follow "both"; "or" and "but" break the correlative pair entirely.

112. Spot the error: Part P: Hardly had she finished her speech / Part Q: than the audience / Part R: started applauding loudly. / Part S: No error
(a) Part P (b) Part Q (c) Part R (d) No error
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Hardly" pairs with "when", not "than". Part Q should read "when the audience". Remember: hardly/scarcely → when; no sooner → than.

113. Fill in the blank: Take an umbrella _______ it should rain.
(a) lest (b) until (c) unless (d) because
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Lest…should" expresses a precaution against a feared event — exactly the meaning here. "Unless" means "if not" and would reverse the sense; "because" gives a reason, not a precaution.

114. Choose the correct sentence:
(a) Either you or your brother have broken the window. (b) Either you and your brother have broken the window. (c) Either you nor your brother has broken the window. (d) Either you or your brother has broken the window.
Answer: (d)
Solution: "Either…or" is the correct pair, and the verb agrees with the nearer subject "your brother" (singular) — hence "has". Option (c) wrongly mixes "either" with "nor".

115. Spot the error: Part P: Being a rainy day, / Part Q: I decided / Part R: to stay at home. / Part S: No error
(a) Part P (b) Part Q (c) Part R (d) No error
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Being a rainy day" is a dangling modifier — the day is not deciding anything. It should be "It being a rainy day" or "As it was a rainy day". Examiners love this trap because the sentence sounds smooth.

116. Fill in the blank: _______ she was tired, she continued working on the project.
(a) Since (b) Even though (c) Because (d) Therefore
Answer: (b)
Solution: The clauses contrast (tiredness vs. continuing to work), so a concessive "Even though" is required. "Since/Because" give reason; "therefore" is a conjunctive adverb showing result, not a subordinating conjunction.

117. Choose the correct sentence:
(a) He not only visited Delhi but also Agra. (b) Not only he visited Delhi but also Agra. (c) He visited not only Delhi but also Agra. (d) He visited Delhi not only but also Agra.
Answer: (c)
Solution: The parallel elements are the two places "Delhi" and "Agra", so "not only…but also" must sit directly before them, after the common verb "visited". Option (a) wrongly splits the verb phrase.

118. Spot the error: Part P: The reason why he failed / Part Q: is because he did not / Part R: prepare for the interview. / Part S: No error
(a) Part P (b) Part Q (c) Part R (d) No error
Answer: (b)
Solution: "The reason…is because" is redundant; use "The reason…is that". Part Q should read "is that he did not". This redundancy trap appears frequently in error-spotting questions.

119. Fill in the blank: He speaks English _______ fluently _______ his brother does.
(a) more … than (b) so … as (c) as … as (d) as … than
Answer: (c)
Solution: A positive comparison of equality uses "as…as". "So…as" is used in negatives ("not so fluent as"). "As…than" mixes two structures and is wrong.

120. Choose the correct sentence:
(a) Until you apologise, I will not forgive you. (b) Until you do not apologise, I will not forgive you. (c) Unless you apologise, I will forgive you. (d) Until you apologise, I will forgive you.
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Until" already carries a negative sense with a negative main clause, so "do not" in option (b) is a double negative. Option (c) reverses the logic; option (d) removes the needed "not" from the main clause.

Answer: (c)
Solution: "Neither of" takes a singular verb — "has submitted". The plural noun "boys" right before the verb is an intervening-phrase trap designed to pull you toward "have".

122. Fill in the blank: _______ had the meeting begun _______ the power went off.
(a) No sooner … when (b) Scarcely … when (c) Hardly … than (d) No sooner … scarcely
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Scarcely…when" is the correct correlative pair with inversion. "No sooner" takes "than", and "hardly" takes "when" — option (c) mismatches the pair.

123. Choose the correct sentence:
(a) He is such a weak that he cannot walk. (b) He is too weak that he cannot walk. (c) He is so weak that he cannot walk. (d) He is very weak that he cannot walk.
Answer: (c)
Solution: "So + adjective + that" expresses result. "Such" needs a noun phrase ("such a weak man that…"), and "too" pairs with "to", not "that".

124. Spot the error: Part P: In spite of being ill, / Part Q: but he attended / Part R: the office regularly. / Part S: No error
(a) Part Q (b) Part P (c) Part R (d) No error
Answer: (a)
Solution: "In spite of" already expresses concession, so "but" in Part Q is superfluous and must be deleted. Hindi speakers often double the connector ("के बावजूद…फिर भी"), which is exactly what the examiner tests.

125. Fill in the blank: _______ you hurry, you will miss the last bus.
(a) Unless (b) Provided (c) If (d) As
Answer: (a)
Solution: The sentence means "if you do not hurry, you will miss the bus", so "Unless" (= if…not) is correct. "If" and "Provided" would make the meaning illogical with the negative result that follows.
