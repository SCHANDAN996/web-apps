51. Choose the correct conjunction: "He is poor ______ honest."
(a) and (b) or (c) but (d) so
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Poor" and "honest" show contrast, so the adversative conjunction "but" is correct. "And" joins similar ideas, which does not fit here.
Source: NCERT Class 9 English Grammar

52. Fill in the blank: "Work hard ______ you will fail."
(a) and (b) or (c) but (d) because
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Or" is used to express a warning or alternative result — if you do not work hard, failure follows. "And" would wrongly suggest both actions happen together.
Source: NCERT Class 10 English Grammar

53. Choose the correct pair: "______ he is rich, he is not happy."
(a) Because (b) Since (c) Though (d) Unless
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Though" introduces a concession — wealth contrasts with unhappiness. "Because" would wrongly give a reason instead of a contrast.
Source: NCERT Class 9 English Grammar

54. Fill in the blank: "I waited ______ the train arrived."
(a) unless (b) until (c) since (d) lest
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Until" shows time up to a point — the waiting ended when the train arrived. "Unless" expresses a condition, which does not fit the time sense here.
Source: NCERT Class 10 English Grammar

55. Choose the correct conjunction: "She is ______ intelligent ______ hardworking."
(a) either … or (b) neither … nor (c) both … and (d) not only … but also
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Both … and" joins two positive qualities equally. "Either … or" and "neither … nor" express choice or negation, not addition of two virtues.
Source: NCERT Class 9 English Grammar

56. Fill in the blank: "He ran fast ______ he might catch the bus."
(a) so that (b) because (c) although (d) until
Answer: (a)
Solution: "So that" expresses purpose — catching the bus was the aim of running fast. "Because" gives a reason, not a purpose.
Source: NCERT Class 10 English Grammar

57. Choose the correct conjunction: "______ it was raining, we went out."
(a) Because (b) So (c) Unless (d) Although
Answer: (d)
Solution: "Although" shows concession — going out happened despite the rain. "Because" would reverse the logic of the sentence.
Source: NCERT Class 9 English Grammar

58. Fill in the blank: "You will not pass ______ you work hard."
(a) if (b) unless (c) until (d) since
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Unless" means "if not" — passing depends on working hard. "If" alone would need a negative main clause to make sense here.
Source: NCERT Class 10 English Grammar

59. Choose the correct conjunction: "He was tired, ______ he kept working."
(a) yet (b) because (c) since (d) as
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Yet" is a coordinating conjunction showing contrast between tiredness and continuing to work. "Because" and "since" give reasons, which reverses the meaning.
Source: NCERT Class 9 English Grammar

60. Fill in the blank: "______ the sun set, the farmers returned home."
(a) Whether (b) Unless (c) Lest (d) When
Answer: (d)
Solution: "When" marks the time at which the farmers returned. "Unless" and "lest" express condition and negative purpose, which do not fit a simple time relation.
Source: NCERT Class 10 English Grammar

61. Spot the error: "No sooner did he enter the room (P) / when the lights (Q) / went off. (R) / No error (S)"
(a) Part Q (b) Part P (c) Part R (d) No error
Answer: (a)
Solution: "No sooner" is always paired with "than", not "when". Part Q should read "than the lights". This is a classic correlative-pair trap.
Source: PYQ-style

62. Choose the correctly framed sentence:
(a) Scarcely had he slept then the phone rang. (b) Scarcely had he slept than the phone rang. (c) Scarcely he had slept when the phone rang. (d) Scarcely had he slept when the phone rang.
Answer: (d)
Solution: "Scarcely/Hardly" take "when", and the helping verb comes before the subject after these negative adverbs. "Than" pairs only with "no sooner".
Source: PYQ-style

63. Spot the error: "He is not only (P) / a good singer (Q) / but also a good dancer. (R) / No error (S)"
(a) Part P (b) Part Q (c) Part R (d) No error
Answer: (d)
Solution: The sentence is correct: "not only" and "but also" are placed before parallel items (a good singer / a good dancer). Balanced placement is the key test for this pair.
Source: NCERT Class 10 English Grammar

64. Choose the correct sentence:
(a) He not only plays cricket but also football. (b) Not only he plays cricket but also football. (c) He plays not only cricket but also football. (d) He plays cricket not only but also football.
Answer: (c)
Solution: Correlatives must stand immediately before the words they join — here the two games, cricket and football. Placing "not only" before "plays" wrongly suggests a second action is coming.
Source: PYQ-style

65. Fill in the blank: "Walk carefully ______ you should fall."
(a) so that (b) unless (c) lest (d) until
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Lest" means "for fear that" and is followed by "should" — never by "not". The sentence warns against falling, so negative purpose fits.
Source: NCERT Class 10 English Grammar

66. Spot the error: "Lest he should not (P) / miss the train, (Q) / he left early. (R) / No error (S)"
(a) Part Q (b) Part P (c) Part R (d) No error
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Lest" already carries a negative meaning, so "not" after it is wrong — it creates a double negative. Part P should read "Lest he should".
Source: PYQ-style

67. Choose the correct conjunction: "______ he apologized, she refused to forgive him."
(a) Even if (b) Provided that (c) Unless (d) Even though
Answer: (d)
Solution: "Even though" introduces a real fact (he did apologize) that contrasts with the result. "Even if" introduces a hypothetical condition, which does not match the past fact here.
Source: PYQ-style

68. Fill in the blank: "You can stay here ______ you keep quiet."
(a) as long as (b) as soon as (c) as well as (d) as though
Answer: (a)
Solution: "As long as" sets a condition — staying is permitted only while quietness continues. "As soon as" expresses time, and "as well as" means "in addition to".
Source: NCERT Class 10 English Grammar

69. Spot the error: "Neither the teacher (P) / nor the students (Q) / was present in the hall. (R) / No error (S)"
(a) Part R (b) Part Q (c) Part P (d) No error
Answer: (a)
Solution: With "neither … nor", the verb agrees with the nearer subject — "students" is plural, so "were" is needed. Part R wrongly uses the singular "was".
Source: PYQ-style

70. Choose the correct sentence:
(a) Either Ram or his brothers is responsible. (b) Either Ram or his brothers are responsible. (c) Either Ram or his brothers was responsible. (d) Either Ram or his brothers being responsible.
Answer: (b)
Solution: With "either … or", the verb agrees with the subject closer to it — "brothers" is plural, so "are" is correct. Proximity rule is the standard examiner trap here.
Source: PYQ-style

71. Fill in the blank: "He spoke ______ he were a great scholar."
(a) because (b) as if (c) so that (d) unless
Answer: (b)
Solution: "As if" introduces an unreal comparison, and it takes the past subjunctive "were" even with "he". The speaker is not actually a scholar, so the hypothetical form fits.
Source: NCERT Class 10 English Grammar

72. Spot the error: "The reason why he failed (P) / is because he did not (Q) / study regularly. (R) / No error (S)"
(a) Part P (b) No error (c) Part R (d) Part Q
Answer: (d)
Solution: "The reason … is because" is redundant; standard English uses "the reason … is that". Part Q should read "is that he did not".
Source: PYQ-style

73. Choose the correct conjunction: "______ being rich, he leads a simple life."
(a) Despite (b) Although (c) Though (d) Since
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Despite" is a preposition followed by a noun or -ing form ("being rich"). "Although" and "though" are conjunctions and must be followed by a full clause with a finite verb.
Source: PYQ-style

74. Fill in the blank: "Hardly had she reached the station ______ the train left."
(a) then (b) than (c) when (d) that
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Hardly" and "scarcely" pair with "when"; only "no sooner" pairs with "than". Examiners swap these pairs to trap candidates.
Source: PYQ-style

75. Choose the correctly punctuated and conjoined sentence:
(a) He was ill; therefore, he stayed at home. (b) He was ill therefore he stayed at home. (c) He was ill, therefore he stayed at home. (d) He was ill therefore, he stayed at home.
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Therefore" is a conjunctive adverb, not a coordinating conjunction, so it needs a semicolon before it and a comma after it when joining two clauses. A comma alone creates a comma splice.
Source: PYQ-style
