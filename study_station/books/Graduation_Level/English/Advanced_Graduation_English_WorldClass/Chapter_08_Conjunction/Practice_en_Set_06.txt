Questions 126–145: Easy | Questions 146–150: Moderate

126. Hardly had he stepped out of the house ______ it began to rain.
(a) than (b) before (c) when (d) that
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Hardly/Scarcely... when" is the fixed correlative pair. "Than" pairs only with "No sooner", which is the classic trap here.
Source: PYQ-style

127. No sooner did the bell ring ______ the students rushed out.
(a) when (b) then (c) than (d) that
Answer: (c)
Solution: "No sooner... than" is the correct pair. "When" goes with hardly/scarcely — examiners swap these two deliberately.
Source: PYQ-style

128. He is not only a good singer ______ a fine dancer.
(a) and also (b) but yet (c) as well as (d) but also
Answer: (d)
Solution: "Not only... but also" is the fixed correlative. "And also" after "not only" is a frequent examiner's trap.
Source: PYQ-style

129. ______ he is rich, he is not generous.
(a) Because (b) Since (c) Although (d) Unless
Answer: (c)
Solution: The two clauses show contrast, so a concessive conjunction "Although" is needed. Because/Since show reason; unless shows condition.
Source: NCERT-based grammar

130. Spot the error: Part P: Unless you do not work hard / Part Q: you will not / Part R: pass the examination. / Part S: No error
(a) No error (b) Part Q (c) Part R (d) Part P
Answer: (d)
Solution: "Unless" is itself negative, so "do not" must be removed — "Unless you work hard". Double negative after unless is a classic trap, so Part P is faulty.
Source: PYQ-style

131. Walk fast ______ you will miss the train.
(a) and (b) so (c) or (d) but
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Or" here means "otherwise" — a warning. "And" would wrongly suggest both actions happen together.
Source: NCERT-based grammar

132. He worked hard ______ he might win the scholarship.
(a) because (b) so that (c) lest (d) although
Answer: (b)
Solution: Purpose is expressed by "so that" + might. "Lest" means "for fear that" (negative purpose) and takes "should", so it does not fit.
Source: PYQ-style

133. Spot the error: Part P: He is senior than me / Part Q: and more experienced / Part R: than anyone else here. / Part S: No error
(a) Part Q (b) Part P (c) Part R (d) No error
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Senior" is a Latin comparative and takes "to", not "than" — "senior to me". Hence Part P contains the error; this comparative trap recurs in exams.
Source: PYQ-style

134. ______ she was tired, she continued working.
(a) Even (b) Despite (c) In spite of (d) Though
Answer: (d)
Solution: A clause (subject + verb) needs a conjunction — "Though". "Despite" and "In spite of" are prepositions and must be followed by a noun or gerund, a favourite trap.
Source: PYQ-style

135. He is ______ intelligent ______ hardworking.
(a) neither... nor (b) either... or (c) not only... but also (d) both... as well as
Answer: (c)
Solution: Both qualities are praised in one person, so "not only... but also" fits. "Both... as well as" is redundant and unidiomatic.
Source: NCERT-based grammar

136. Spot the error: Part P: Neither of the two boys / Part Q: have done / Part R: his homework. / Part S: No error
(a) Part Q (b) Part R (c) Part P (d) No error
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Neither of" takes a singular verb — "has done" — so Part Q is wrong. The plural noun "boys" nearby is an intervening-phrase trap.
Source: PYQ-style

137. ______ you apologise, I will not forgive you.
(a) Unless (b) Until (c) If (d) Till
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Unless" (= if... not) fits the negative main clause. "Until/Till" refer to time, not condition.
Source: NCERT-based grammar

138. He fled ______ he should be caught.
(a) lest (b) so that (c) because (d) although
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Lest" (= for fear that) expresses negative purpose and is followed by "should". Note that "lest" never takes "not" — a common trap.
Source: PYQ-style

139. Spot the error: Part P: Scarcely he had reached the station / Part Q: when the train / Part R: steamed off. / Part S: No error
(a) Part P (b) Part Q (c) Part R (d) No error
Answer: (a)
Solution: When "Scarcely" begins the sentence, inversion is required — "Scarcely had he reached". The word order in Part P is wrong.
Source: PYQ-style

140. ______ the rain stopped, the match resumed.
(a) Unless (b) No sooner (c) Hardly (d) As soon as
Answer: (d)
Solution: "As soon as" introduces a time clause without inversion. "No sooner/Hardly" need "than/when" and inversion, so they cannot stand here.
Source: NCERT-based grammar

141. You must either confess the truth ______ face the punishment.
(a) and (b) or (c) but (d) nor
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Either... or" is the fixed correlative pair. "Nor" pairs only with "neither", which is the tempting trap here.
Source: PYQ-style

142. Spot the error: Part P: Both Ramesh / Part Q: as well as his brother / Part R: were present at the meeting. / Part S: No error
(a) Part Q (b) Part P (c) Part R (d) No error
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Both" pairs with "and", never with "as well as" — "Both Ramesh and his brother". Part Q has this redundant pairing, a standard trap.
Source: PYQ-style

143. ______ hard he may try, he cannot lift that box.
(a) Whatever (b) Whenever (c) However (d) Wherever
Answer: (c)
Solution: "However + adverb" (however hard) means "no matter how". "Whatever" modifies nouns, not adverbs.
Source: PYQ-style

144. Take an umbrella ______ it rains.
(a) until (b) unless (c) because (d) in case
Answer: (d)
Solution: "In case" expresses precaution against a possible future event. "Because" would assert that it is already raining.
Source: NCERT-based grammar

145. Spot the error: Part P: He not only lost his wallet / Part Q: but also / Part R: his mobile phone. / Part S: No error
(a) Part P (b) Part Q (c) Part R (d) No error
Answer: (a)
Solution: Correlatives must be placed just before the parallel items — "He lost not only his wallet but also his mobile phone". The misplaced "not only" in Part P breaks parallelism.
Source: PYQ-style

146. ______ being punished, the boy repeated the mischief.
(a) Despite of (b) In spite (c) Despite of the (d) In spite of
Answer: (d)
Solution: "In spite of" is correct; "Despite" never takes "of". Before a gerund ("being punished") a preposition, not "although", is needed.
Source: PYQ-style

147. The reason he failed is ______ he never attended classes.
(a) that (b) because (c) since (d) due to
Answer: (a)
Solution: After "The reason is...", the complement clause takes "that", not "because" — "the reason is because" is redundant and a well-known trap.
Source: PYQ-style

148. Spot the error: Part P: Until the sun does not set, / Part Q: the farmers / Part R: keep working in the fields. / Part S: No error
(a) Part Q (b) Part P (c) Part R (d) No error
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Until" is negative in sense here and should not be followed by "not" — "Until the sun sets". Part P carries the double negative, a recurring trap with until/unless.
Source: PYQ-style

149. ______ you work, ______ you will earn.
(a) Harder... more (b) The harder... the more (c) The hard... the much (d) More hard... more much
Answer: (b)
Solution: Double comparatives take "the + comparative... the + comparative" — "The harder... the more". Dropping "the" is the trap.
Source: PYQ-style

150. Spot the error: Part P: He came late / Part Q: because of he was stuck / Part R: in a traffic jam. / Part S: No error
(a) Part P (b) Part Q (c) Part R (d) No error
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Because of" is a preposition and cannot introduce a clause with subject and verb; use the conjunction "because" — "because he was stuck". Part Q is faulty.
Source: PYQ-style
