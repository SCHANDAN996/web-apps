# 📊 PYQ Analysis — Conjunction

## How Exams Test Conjunctions

Across SSC (CGL, CHSL, MTS, GD), Banking (IBPS, SBI), Railway (NTPC, Group D) and State PCS prelims, conjunctions appear in **four recurring formats**:

1. **Spot the Error** — a wrong pair of correlative conjunctions (`neither…or`, `not only…but`), or a wrong connector after a fixed word (`no sooner…when` instead of `no sooner…than`).
2. **Fill in the Blank** — choosing between `although/though/but`, `because/since/as`, `unless/until`, `if/whether`.
3. **Sentence Improvement** — replacing an underlined connector with the correct one.
4. **Cloze Test / Para-jumble** — connectors as logical signposts (contrast, cause, result, addition).

## Sub-topics That Repeat Most Often

- **Correlative pairs**: either…or, neither…nor, not only…but also, both…and, whether…or, no sooner…than, hardly/scarcely…when, such…that, so…that, the more…the more.
- **Contrast connectors**: although/though/even though vs. but/yet — examiners love the double-connector error (`Although he is rich, but he is miserly.` ❌).
- **Reason connectors**: because/since/as/because of/due to — especially the *because of + noun* vs. *because + clause* distinction.
- **Condition connectors**: unless, provided that, in case, as long as — `unless` (= if…not) is a favourite trap with negative verbs.
- **Time connectors**: until/till, by the time, as soon as, no sooner…than (inversion trap).
- **Purpose/result**: so that, in order that, lest…should, so…that vs. such…that.

## Examiner's Trap Types (with classification key)

| Trap Code | Trap Type | What it looks like |
|---|---|---|
| **IP** | Intervening Phrase | Extra words between the pair hide the error: `Neither the players nor the captain *who was injured* were…` |
| **SR** | Sound-Right | A wrong pair that "sounds fine" in spoken English: `neither…or`, `no sooner…when` |
| **OT** | Over-thinking | A correct but unusual structure (inversion after `no sooner`) that students wrongly "correct" |
| **CN** | Collective/Double Connector | Two conjunctions doing one job: `Although…but`, `Since…therefore`, `Because…so` |

## Top 10 High-Yield Questions (with solutions)

**Q1.** Neither the manager nor the employees (a)/ was present (b)/ at the meeting. (c)/ No error (d)
**Answer:** (b)
**Solution:** With *neither…nor*, the verb agrees with the **nearer subject** (*employees* — plural), so it should be **were present**. Trap: **IP** — the pair is split across the sentence, hiding the proximity rule.
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q2.** No sooner did she enter the room (a)/ when the lights (b)/ went off. (c)/ No error (d)
**Answer:** (b)
**Solution:** *No sooner* pairs with **than**, never *when*. Correct: *No sooner did she enter the room **than** the lights went off.* Trap: **SR** — "no sooner…when" sounds natural in speech.
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q3.** Although he worked hard, (a)/ but he could not (b)/ clear the examination. (c)/ No error (d)
**Answer:** (b)
**Solution:** *Although* and *but* cannot appear in the same sentence — one contrast connector is enough. Delete *but*. Trap: **CN** — Hindi's "हालांकि…लेकिन" pattern pushes students to accept both.
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q4.** He is not only intelligent (a)/ but also hardworking. (b)/ No error (c)/ — choose the part with error, if any.
**Answer:** (c) No error
**Solution:** *Not only…but also* is correctly placed before parallel adjectives (*intelligent / hardworking*). Trap: **OT** — students hunt for an error that doesn't exist; check parallelism before changing anything.
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q5.** Hardly had he reached the station (a)/ than the train (b)/ left the platform. (c)/ No error (d)
**Answer:** (b)
**Solution:** *Hardly/Scarcely* pairs with **when**; *No sooner* pairs with **than**. Correct: *Hardly had he reached the station **when** the train left.* Trap: **SR** — the two pairs are constantly swapped in exams.
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q6.** Unless you do not work hard, (a)/ you will not (b)/ succeed in life. (c)/ No error (d)
**Answer:** (a)
**Solution:** *Unless* already means *if…not*, so a second negative creates a double negative. Correct: *Unless you work hard…* Trap: **SR** — "unless you do not" feels emphatic but is wrong.
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q7.** Since he was ill, (a)/ therefore he did not (b)/ attend the office. (c)/ No error (d)
**Answer:** (b)
**Solution:** *Since* (reason) and *therefore* (result) cannot both connect the same clauses. Delete *therefore*. Trap: **CN** — mirrors the Hindi "चूँकि…इसलिए" structure.
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q8.** He is such a clever boy (a)/ that everyone (b)/ likes him. (c)/ No error (d)
**Answer:** (d) No error
**Solution:** *Such + (a/an) + adjective + noun … that* is the correct result pattern. Trap: **OT** — students wrongly replace *such* with *so*; *so* would need *so clever a boy*.
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q9.** I don't know (a)/ that whether he (b)/ will come tomorrow. (c)/ No error (d)
**Answer:** (b)
**Solution:** *Whether* is itself a connector; adding *that* before it is redundant. Correct: *I don't know **whether** he will come.* Trap: **CN** — double connector again.
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q10.** Scarcely had the bell rung (a)/ when the students (b)/ rushed out of the class. (c)/ No error (d)
**Answer:** (d) No error
**Solution:** *Scarcely…when* with inversion (*had the bell rung*) is perfectly correct. Trap: **OT** — the inverted order tempts students to "fix" it into *the bell had rung*, which would destroy the structure.
**Source:** PYQ-style

## Applying the 15/45-Second Rule

**Q3 above — 15-second (Fact-based):** The moment you see *Although* at the start and *but* after the comma, mark the error. This is a memorised rule — no analysis needed. Target: **15 seconds**.

**Q1 above — 45-second (Inference-based):** The verb is far from *neither…nor*, and an intervening plural/singular mix (*manager / employees*) forces you to (1) locate both subjects, (2) identify the nearer one, (3) apply proximity agreement. Budget the full **45 seconds** — rushing here is exactly what the examiner wants.

## Key Takeaways

- Master the **fixed pairs** first — they carry the highest repeat value: *no sooner…than, hardly/scarcely…when, neither…nor, not only…but also, such…that, lest…should*.
- One clause, one connector: kill the **Although…but / Since…therefore / Because…so** habit.
- *Unless, until, lest* already carry a negative sense — never add *not* with them.
- When a sentence looks "too correct" (inversion after *no sooner/hardly*), suspect the **Over-thinking** trap before editing.
