# 🧒 Feynman Technique — Conjunctions Explained Like You're 12

## The Hardest Concept: Correlative Conjunctions & Parallel Structure

Imagine you are carrying **two shopping bags**, one in each hand. If one bag has 5 kg of potatoes and the other has a single chocolate wrapper, what happens? You tilt, you stumble, you look silly. Your body demands **balance**.

A sentence with correlative conjunctions — *either…or, neither…nor, not only…but also, both…and* — works exactly like your two arms. Whatever you put after the first word, you must put the **same kind of thing** after the second word. Noun with noun. Verb with verb. Phrase with phrase. Otherwise the sentence "tilts" — and the examiner is standing there waiting to catch you falling.

**The tilting sentence:**
❌ *He not only plays cricket but also football.*

Read it aloud. After "not only" comes a **verb** (plays), but after "but also" comes a **noun** (football). Unbalanced bags!

**The balanced sentence:**
✅ *He plays not only cricket but also football.* (noun + noun — balanced!)

## The Unforgettable Trick: "The Mirror Test" 🪞

Cover the sentence with your finger and read only what comes **immediately after each half** of the pair:

- *either* **a teacher** … *or* **a doctor** → noun | noun ✅
- *neither* **singing** … *nor* **dancing** → -ing | -ing ✅
- *not only* **rich** … *but also* **kind** → adjective | adjective ✅

If the two sides don't look like mirror images, the sentence is wrong. That's it. That's the whole rule.

## One More Hard Nut: "Neither…nor" and the Verb

When two subjects are joined by *neither…nor* or *either…or*, the verb is a loyal friend — it agrees with the subject standing **nearest** to it:

✅ *Neither the teacher nor the **students were** present.*
✅ *Neither the students nor the **teacher was** present.*

Think of it as a queue at a ticket window — the person **closest to the window** (the verb) gets served first.

## ❓ Test Yourself

Which sentence is correct?

(a) She is not only intelligent but also works hard.
(b) She not only is intelligent but also hardworking.
(c) She is not only intelligent but also hardworking.
(d) Not only she is intelligent but also hardworking.

*(Answer: (c) — adjective "intelligent" mirrors adjective "hardworking". In (a), an adjective is paired with a whole verb phrase — the bags tilt!)*
