1. Choose the correct conjunction: He is poor ______ honest.
(a) and (b) or (c) but (d) so
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Poor" and "honest" show contrast, so the adversative conjunction "but" is correct. "And" would join similar ideas, which does not fit the contrast here.
Source: NCERT Class 9 English Grammar

2. Fill in the blank: She was tired, ______ she kept working.
(a) yet (b) because (c) unless (d) since
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Yet" is a coordinating conjunction showing contrast between being tired and continuing to work. "Because" and "since" show reason, which reverses the logic.

3. Choose the correct pair: ______ he was ill, he attended the meeting.
(a) Because (b) Although (c) Unless (d) Until
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Although" introduces a concession — illness did not stop him. "Because" would wrongly suggest illness caused attendance.

4. Fill in the blank: Work hard ______ you will fail.
(a) and (b) or (c) lest (d) so
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Or" here means "otherwise", warning of the consequence of not working hard. "Lest" needs "should" and a negative sense, which does not fit.

5. Choose the correct conjunction: I will wait here ______ you return.
(a) unless (b) until (c) since (d) because
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Until" shows time up to the point of return. "Unless" expresses a condition, not time, so it is wrong here.

6. Fill in the blank: ______ it was raining, we went out.
(a) Though (b) Because (c) If (d) Since
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Though" introduces a contrast: rain did not stop the outing. "Because" and "since" would reverse the cause-effect logic.

7. Choose the correct option: He is ______ intelligent ______ hardworking.
(a) neither … nor (b) either … or (c) not only … but also (d) both … or
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Not only … but also" correctly joins two positive qualities. "Both … or" is not a valid correlative pair; the correct pair is "both … and".

8. Fill in the blank: ______ you work hard, you cannot succeed.
(a) Unless (b) If (c) Because (d) Although
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Unless" means "if … not" — without hard work, success is impossible. "If" alone would make the sentence illogical.

9. Choose the correct conjunction: Take an umbrella ______ it rains.
(a) until (b) unless (c) in case (d) though
Answer: (c)
Solution: "In case" expresses precaution against a possible event. "Unless" would wrongly imply the umbrella is taken only if it does not rain.

10. Fill in the blank: He is ______ a singer ______ a dancer.
(a) either … nor (b) neither … or (c) neither … nor (d) either … but
Answer: (c)
Solution: The correct correlative pair is "neither … nor". "Neither … or" and "either … nor" are common examiner traps mixing the pairs.

11. Choose the correct conjunction: She speaks English ______ fluently ______ her mother tongue.
(a) as … as (b) so … that (c) too … to (d) such … that
Answer: (a)
Solution: "As … as" expresses equality of comparison. "So … that" needs a result clause, which is absent here.

12. Fill in the blank: He ran fast ______ he might catch the train.
(a) so that (b) because (c) although (d) unless
Answer: (a)
Solution: "So that" expresses purpose — the reason for running fast. "Because" would state a cause, not a purpose.

13. Choose the correct option: ______ you apologize, I will not forgive you.
(a) If (b) Since (c) Until (d) Unless
Answer: (d)
Solution: "Unless" means "if you do not apologize", which fits the negative main clause. "Until" refers to time, not condition.

14. Fill in the blank: He is ______ weak ______ he cannot walk.
(a) too … that (b) so … that (c) as … as (d) such … as
Answer: (b)
Solution: "So … that" links degree with result: weakness to the point of being unable to walk. "Too … that" is not a valid pair; "too" takes "to".

15. Choose the correct conjunction: I know ______ he is honest.
(a) what (b) which (c) that (d) who
Answer: (c)
Solution: "That" introduces a noun clause as the object of "know". "Which" and "who" are relative pronouns needing an antecedent.

16. Fill in the blank: ______ he is rich, he is not happy.
(a) Although (b) Since (c) Because (d) If
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Although" shows concession — wealth does not bring happiness here. "Because" and "since" would wrongly make wealth the cause of unhappiness.

17. Choose the correct option: No sooner had he arrived ______ it started raining.
(a) than (b) then (c) when (d) that
Answer: (a)
Solution: The fixed correlative is "No sooner … than". "When" pairs with "Hardly/Scarcely", not with "No sooner" — a classic examiner trap.
Source: PYQ-style

18. Fill in the blank: Hardly had she reached the station ______ the train left.
(a) than (b) then (c) when (d) that
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Hardly … when" is the correct pair. "Than" pairs with "No sooner"; mixing the two is a frequent trap.

19. Choose the correct conjunction: He is senior ______ me.
(a) than (b) then (c) from (d) to
Answer: (d)
Solution: Latin comparatives like "senior, junior, superior, inferior" take "to", not "than". This is a very common examiner trap.

20. Fill in the blank: Walk carefully ______ you should fall.
(a) unless (b) lest (c) until (d) because
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Lest" means "for fear that" and is followed by "should". Note that "lest" is never followed by a negative — the negative sense is built into it.
Source: PYQ-style

21. Choose the correctly joined sentence: He was punished. He was fined.
(a) He was punished but fined. (b) He was either punished or fined. (c) He was neither punished nor fined. (d) He was not only punished but also fined.
Answer: (d)
Solution: Both penalties happened, so "not only … but also" correctly adds the two ideas. "Either … or" implies only one happened, and "but" wrongly suggests contrast.
Source: PYQ-style

22. Spot the error: Part P: Scarcely had he / Part Q: entered the room / Part R: than the phone rang. / Part S: No error
(a) Part P (b) Part Q (c) No error (d) Part R
Answer: (d)
Solution: Part R is wrong: "Scarcely" pairs with "when", not "than". "Than" is used only with "No sooner" — a classic trap.
Source: PYQ-style

23. Choose the correct option: ______ you sow, so shall you reap.
(a) Like (b) As (c) So (d) Such
Answer: (b)
Solution: "As … so" is the correlative pair showing proportion: the harvest matches the sowing. "Like" is a preposition and cannot introduce a clause here.
Source: NCERT Class 10 English Grammar

24. Spot the error: Part P: Unless you do not / Part Q: work hard, / Part R: you will fail. / Part S: No error
(a) No error (b) Part Q (c) Part R (d) Part P
Answer: (d)
Solution: Part P is wrong: "unless" already carries a negative meaning, so "do not" creates a double negative. It should be "Unless you work hard".

25. Choose the correct conjunction: The more you read, ______ you learn.
(a) the most (b) more (c) most (d) the more
Answer: (d)
Solution: "The more … the more" is the double comparative structure showing parallel increase. Dropping "the" breaks the pattern.
