# 📊 PYQ Analysis — Word Roots & Morphology

## How Exams Test This Chapter

Questions on word roots and morphology appear across SSC CGL, CHSL, IBPS PO/Clerk, and State PCS prelims — usually inside the **Vocabulary** section (one-word substitution, synonyms/antonyms, cloze test, and "meaning of the underlined word" in comprehension). Examiners rarely ask "What does the root 'bene' mean?" directly. Instead, they test whether you can **decode an unfamiliar word** using its root, prefix, or suffix.

### Recurring Sub-Topics (Pattern Analysis)

- **Root-based meaning inference**: A tough word is given; the correct option matches its root meaning (e.g., *loquacious* → *loqu* = speak → talkative).
- **Prefix traps**: Words that *look* negative but aren't, or prefixes with multiple meanings (*in-* = "not" in *inactive* but "into" in *inject*).
- **Suffix-based word-class identification**: Spotting noun/verb/adjective forms (*-tion*, *-ify*, *-ous*) — useful in error-spotting and fill-in-the-blanks.
- **Confusable pairs from similar roots**: *affect/effect*, *emigrant/immigrant*, *elicit/illicit* — examiners exploit sound-alike roots.
- **One-word substitutions built on roots**: *misanthrope* (*mis* = hate + *anthrop* = man), *philanthropist*, *polyglot*.

### Examiner's Favourite Traps (in words, not numbers)

1. **Sound-Right Trap**: The option that *sounds* like the word is wrong. *Pulchritude* sounds ugly but means beauty.
2. **Partial-Root Trap**: One option matches only the prefix, ignoring the root. *Circumvent* — "circle" (from *circum*) is tempting, but the word means "to bypass/evade" (*ven* = come).
3. **False-Friend Trap**: Words resembling Hindi/English lookalikes. *Restive* ≠ restful; it means restless.
4. **Over-thinking Trap**: The simple root meaning IS the answer; students reject it as "too easy."

## Top 10 High-Yield PYQ-Style Questions with Solutions

**Q1.** Choose the word closest in meaning to **LOQUACIOUS**.
(a) Silent (b) Talkative (c) Angry (d) Wise
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** Root *loqu/locut* = speak (as in *eloquent*, *soliloquy*). Loquacious = one who speaks a lot. (a) is the antonym trap.
**Trap type:** Sound-Right (students link it to "location").
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q2.** **MALEDICTION** means:
(a) A blessing (b) A curse (c) A prediction (d) A medicine
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** *Mal* = bad + *dict* = say → "to speak bad of" = curse. Contrast *benediction* (*bene* = good) = blessing — option (a) is the mirror trap.
**Trap type:** Partial-Root (picking "prediction" from *dict* alone).
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q3.** One-word substitution: "One who hates mankind."
(a) Philanthropist (b) Misanthrope (c) Anthropologist (d) Optimist
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** *Mis* = hate + *anthrop* = mankind. (a) is the exact opposite (*phil* = love) — the classic antonym-pair trap.
**Trap type:** Over-thinking / antonym confusion.
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q4.** Choose the correct meaning of **CIRCUMVENT**.
(a) To surround (b) To travel in a circle (c) To evade or bypass (d) To measure
**Answer: (c)**
**Solution:** *Circum* = around + *vent* = come → "to come around" → to get around a rule/problem, i.e., evade. (a) and (b) exploit only the prefix.
**Trap type:** Partial-Root.
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q5.** **PULCHRITUDE** means:
(a) Ugliness (b) Beauty (c) Strength (d) Dirtiness
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** From Latin *pulcher* = beautiful. The harsh sound misleads most candidates into (a) or (d).
**Trap type:** Sound-Right.
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q6.** The word **ANTIBIOTIC** is built from:
(a) anti (against) + bio (life) (b) ante (before) + bio (life) (c) anti (against) + bion (nerve) (d) ant (insect) + bio (life)
**Answer: (a)**
**Solution:** *Anti-* = against, *bio* = life → a substance acting against (harmful) life forms. (b) confuses *anti-* with *ante-* (before), a frequent prefix trap.
**Trap type:** Prefix confusion (anti vs ante).
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q7.** Choose the word closest in meaning to **OMNISCIENT**.
(a) All-powerful (b) All-knowing (c) Ever-present (d) All-merciful
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** *Omni* = all + *sci* = know (as in *science*, *conscious*). (a) is *omnipotent* (*potent* = power); (c) is *omnipresent* — examiners place all three "omni-" words together.
**Trap type:** Similar-root confusion.
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q8.** **ELICIT** means:
(a) Illegal (b) To draw out (a response) (c) To select (d) To erase
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** *E-* (out) + *licere* (draw) → to draw out information. (a) is *illicit* — a sound-alike trap word.
**Trap type:** Sound-Right (elicit/illicit).
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q9.** One-word substitution: "A person who speaks many languages."
(a) Polyglot (b) Bilingual (c) Linguist (d) Orator
**Answer: (a)**
**Solution:** *Poly* = many + *glot* = tongue/language. (c) is tempting — a linguist *studies* language but need not *speak* many; precision is the trap.
**Trap type:** Over-thinking / near-miss option.
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q10.** **RETROGRADE** means:
(a) Moving forward (b) Moving backward / declining (c) Highly graded (d) Old-fashioned clothing
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** *Retro* = backward + *grad* = step (as in *gradual*, *progress*). (a) is the antonym trap (*pro-* = forward).
**Trap type:** Partial-Root (picking "graded" from spelling).
**Source:** PYQ-style

## Applying the 15/45-Second Rule

**Q3 (Misanthrope) — 15-second question (Fact-based):**
You either know *mis* = hate + *anthrop* = man or you don't. Decode, match, mark (b), move on. Do NOT re-read the options twice — that converts a 15-second gift into a 60-second loss.

**Q7 (Omniscient) — 45-second question (Inference-based):**
Three options share the prefix *omni-*, so root-knowledge alone isn't enough; you must separate *sci* (know), *potent* (power), and *present* (presence). Budget the full 45 seconds: eliminate (a) and (c) by recalling *omnipotent/omnipresent*, then confirm (b). If the three "omni-" words blur after 45 seconds, mark for review and skip — guessing between three lookalikes is negative-marking territory.

---

## Key Takeaways from the Pattern

- Root questions are **fast-scoring**: most are 15-second fact recalls if your root bank is strong.
- Examiners cluster **antonym pairs** (bene/mal, phil/mis, pro/retro) in the same option set — always check whether the question wants the positive or the negative sense.
- **Sound-alike pairs** (elicit/illicit, emigrant/immigrant) appear repeatedly — learn them as pairs, never singly.
- When three options share a prefix, the question is testing the **root**, not the prefix — slow down to 45 seconds.
