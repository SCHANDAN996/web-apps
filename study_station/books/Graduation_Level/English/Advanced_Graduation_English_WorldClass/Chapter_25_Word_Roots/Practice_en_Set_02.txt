26. The root "bene" means:
(a) bad (b) before (c) against (d) good
Answer: (d)
Solution: "Mal" means bad and "anti" means against, which tempt students who confuse bene- with its opposite mal-. "Bene" means good, as in benefit and benevolent, so the answer is good.
Source: Standard Greek/Latin root lists

27. The root "graph" means:
(a) to see (b) to write (c) to carry (d) to say
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Graph" means to write, as in biography and autograph, so the answer is to write.
Source: Standard Greek/Latin root lists

28. The prefix "pre-" in "predict" means:
(a) before (b) against (c) after (d) again
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Post-" means after and "re-" means again, which students mix up with pre-. "Pre-" means before; predict literally means "to say before", so the answer is before.
Source: Standard Greek/Latin root lists

29. The root "port" means:
(a) to break (b) to see (c) to carry (d) to love
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Spect/vid" means to see and "fract" means to break, which tempt because they appear in similar word lists. "Port" means to carry, as in transport (carry across) and portable (able to be carried), so the answer is to carry.
Source: Standard Greek/Latin root lists

30. "Antonym" most nearly means a word that is:
(a) a newly coined word (b) similar in meaning (c) a name of a person (d) opposite in meaning
Answer: (d)
Solution: Synonym (similar meaning) is the classic trap because it differs by only one prefix. "Ant-" (against) + "onym" (name/word) gives opposite in meaning, so the answer is opposite in meaning.
Source: Standard Greek/Latin root lists

31. The root "chron" relates to:
(a) colour (b) light (c) sound (d) time
Answer: (d)
Solution: "Chrom" (colour) is the trap because it looks almost identical to "chron". "Chron" means time, as in chronology and chronic, so the answer is time.
Source: Standard Greek/Latin root lists

32. The suffix "-ology" means:
(a) study of (b) fear of (c) lover of (d) hater of
Answer: (a)
Solution: "-phobia" (fear) and "-phile" (lover) are tempting because they often appear beside -ology in lists. "-Ology" means study of, as in biology and geology, so the answer is study of.
Source: Standard Greek/Latin root lists

33. Which word means "a person who loves mankind"?
(a) Philanthropist (b) Misanthrope (c) Anthropologist (d) Polyglot
Answer: (a)
Solution: Misanthrope is the trap because it also contains "anthrop" (mankind), but "mis" means hatred. "Phil" (love) + "anthrop" (mankind) gives philanthropist, so the answer is Philanthropist.
Source: Standard Greek/Latin root lists

34. The root "dict" means:
(a) to say (b) to write (c) to lead (d) to do
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Scrib/script" (to write) tempts because dictionary work involves writing. "Dict" means to say, as in dictate and verdict, so the answer is to say.
Source: Standard Greek/Latin root lists

35. "Autobiography" is a book written:
(a) by a person about their own life (b) about ancient history (c) about cars (d) by a ghost writer
Answer: (a)
Solution: The "auto" in automobile (self-moving) tempts students toward cars. "Auto" (self) + "bio" (life) + "graph" (write) means a self-written life story, so the answer is by a person about their own life.
Source: Standard Greek/Latin root lists

36. The prefix "inter-" in "international" means:
(a) within (b) between (c) above (d) below
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Intra-" (within) is the classic trap because inter- and intra- differ by one letter. "Inter-" means between, so international means between nations, and the answer is between.
Source: Standard Greek/Latin root lists

37. The root "vid/vis" means:
(a) to hear (b) to run (c) to feel (d) to see
Answer: (d)
Solution: "Aud" (to hear) tempts because audio-visual pairs the two senses. "Vid/vis" means to see, as in video, vision and visible, so the answer is to see.
Source: Standard Greek/Latin root lists

38. "Monotheism" is the belief in:
(a) many gods (b) one god (c) nature as god (d) no god
Answer: (b)
Solution: Polytheism (many gods) is the trap because both words end in -theism. "Mono" means one, so monotheism is belief in one god, and the answer is one god.
Source: Standard Greek/Latin root lists

39. The suffix "-cide" in "homicide" means:
(a) loving (b) killing (c) ruling (d) fearing
Answer: (b)
Solution: "-cracy" (ruling) tempts because homicide and democracy are both common exam words. "-Cide" means killing, as in homicide (killing a human) and insecticide, so the answer is killing.
Source: Standard Greek/Latin root lists

40. The root "geo" means:
(a) earth (b) water (c) star (d) air
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Astro" (star) tempts because geography and astronomy are often studied together. "Geo" means earth, as in geography and geology, so the answer is 1950.

41. A "bibliophile" is a person who loves:
(a) books (b) money (c) food (d) travel
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Biblio" looks like "Bible" to some students, who then guess religion, but the root covers all books. "Biblio" (book) + "phile" (lover) means a lover of books, so the answer is books.
Source: Standard Greek/Latin root lists

42. If "mal" means bad, "malevolent" most nearly means:
(a) wishing good (b) feeling nothing (c) wishing harm (d) acting bravely
Answer: (c)
Solution: Benevolent (wishing good) is the trap because the two words differ only in their first root. "Mal" (bad) + "vol" (wish) means wishing harm, so the answer is wishing harm.
Source: Standard Greek/Latin root lists

43. The word "circumnavigate" literally means to:
(a) sail under (b) sail around (c) sail against (d) sail before
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Circum" is often confused with "sub" (under) because both describe position. "Circum" (around) + "nav" (ship/sail) means to sail around, so the answer is sail around.
Source: Standard Greek/Latin root lists

44. A "polyglot" is a person who:
(a) speaks one language (b) studies ancient scripts (c) speaks many languages (d) writes dictionaries
Answer: (c)
Solution: Monoglot (one language) tempts because the words look alike. "Poly" (many) + "glot" (tongue/language) means one who speaks many languages, so the answer is speaks many languages.
Source: Standard Greek/Latin root lists

45. "Postmortem" literally means:
(a) before death (b) fear of death (c) against death (d) after death
Answer: (d)
Solution: "Mort" (death) is clear, but students who mix up pre- and post- pick before death. "Post" means after, so postmortem is an examination after death, and the answer is after death.
Source: Standard Greek/Latin root lists

46. In "incredible", the prefix "in-" means:
(a) very (b) inside (c) not (d) again
Answer: (c)
Solution: "In-" sometimes means inside (as in include), which is the trap here. In incredible, "in-" is negative: not + credible (believable) means not believable, so the answer is not.
Source: Standard Greek/Latin root lists

47. Which word best fits: "The doctor was praised for her ______ approach; she treated rich and poor patients with equal care." (root: aequ = equal)
(a) equivocal (b) equestrian (c) equilateral (d) equitable
Answer: (d)
Solution: Equivocal (ambiguous) is the trap because it shares the "equi" spelling but means doubtful, not fair. Equitable means fair and equal in treatment, which fits the sentence, so the answer is equitable.
Source: Standard Greek/Latin root lists

48. "Retrospect" most nearly means:
(a) looking forward (b) looking back (c) looking inside (d) looking carefully
Answer: (b)
Solution: Prospect (looking forward) is the trap because it shares the root "spect". "Retro" (back) + "spect" (look) means looking back, so the answer is looking back.
Source: Standard Greek/Latin root lists

49. A "magnanimous" person is:
(a) small-minded (b) physically strong (c) generous in spirit (d) highly magnetic
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Magn" (great) misleads some into physically strong, and magnetism into highly magnetic. "Magn" (great) + "anim" (mind/spirit) means great-spirited, i.e. generous, so the answer is generous in spirit.
Source: Standard Greek/Latin root lists

50. The word "contemporary" describes something:
(a) beyond time (b) belonging to the past (c) belonging to the same time (d) against time
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Temp" (time) is clear, but "con/com" is misread as against by some students. "Con" (together) + "tempor" (time) means belonging to the same time, so the answer is belonging to the same time.
Source: Standard Greek/Latin root lists
