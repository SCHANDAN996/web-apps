1. The root "bene" means:
(a) bad (b) before (c) against (d) good
Answer: (d)
Solution: "Bene" is a Latin root meaning "good" or "well," as in benefit, benevolent, and benediction. The tempting wrong choice "bad" is actually the root "mal" (malicious, malnutrition), which examiners often pair with "bene" to confuse students. So the answer is good.
Source: Standard Greek & Latin Roots (Vocabulary reference)

2. The root "dict" means:
(a) write (b) hear (c) see (d) speak
Answer: (d)
Solution: "Dict" comes from the Latin "dicere," meaning "to say or speak," as in dictate, predict, and verdict. Students often confuse it with "scrib/script" (write), but dictation is spoken words being recorded. So the answer is speak.
Source: Standard Greek & Latin Roots (Vocabulary reference)

3. The root "port" means:
(a) carry (b) throw (c) break (d) love
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Port" is from Latin "portare," meaning "to carry," as in transport (carry across), export (carry out), and portable (able to be carried). "Throw" is the root "ject" (eject, project). So the answer is carry.
Source: Standard Greek & Latin Roots (Vocabulary reference)

4. The root "spect" means:
(a) hear (b) touch (c) look (d) move
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Spect" comes from Latin "specere," meaning "to look," as in inspect (look into), spectator (one who looks on), and prospect (look forward). "Hear" is the root "aud" (audible, audience). So the answer is look.
Source: Standard Greek & Latin Roots (Vocabulary reference)

5. The root "aud" means:
(a) see (b) speak (c) write (d) hear
Answer: (d)
Solution: "Aud" is from Latin "audire," meaning "to hear," as in audible, auditorium, and audition. The trap is confusing it with "vid/vis" (see), since audio-visual media involves both, but "aud" strictly means hear. So the answer is hear.
Source: Standard Greek & Latin Roots (Vocabulary reference)

6. The root "chron" means:
(a) colour (b) time (c) earth (d) water
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Chron" is a Greek root meaning "time," as in chronology, chronic (lasting over time), and synchronize (same time). "Colour" is the root "chrom" (chromatic, monochrome) — a classic examiner trap because chron and chrom differ by only one letter. So the answer is time.
Source: Standard Greek & Latin Roots (Vocabulary reference)

7. The root "geo" means:
(a) star (b) light (c) life (d) earth
Answer: (d)
Solution: "Geo" is Greek for "earth," as in geography (writing about the earth), geology (study of the earth), and geometry (originally earth-measurement). "Star" is "aster/astr" (astronomy, disaster). So the answer is earth.

8. The root "bio" means:
(a) death (b) book (c) life (d) people
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Bio" is Greek for "life," as in biology, biography (writing about a life), and antibiotic (against life of microbes). "Death" is "mort" (mortal, mortuary). So the answer is life.

9. The root "mort" means:
(a) life (b) peace (c) war (d) death
Answer: (d)
Solution: "Mort" is Latin for "death," as in mortal (subject to death), immortal (not subject to death), and mortify (originally "to make dead"). The trap is "bio" (life), its direct opposite, which examiners place side by side. So the answer is death.
Source: Standard Greek & Latin Roots (Vocabulary reference)

10. The root "graph" means:
(a) write (b) speak (c) run (d) cut
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Graph" is Greek for "write," as in autograph (self-writing), paragraph, and photograph (writing with light). "Speak" is "dict" or "log/loqu." So the answer is write.

11. The root "tele" means:
(a) near (b) far (c) small (d) many
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Tele" is Greek for "far" or "distant," as in telephone (sound from far), television (seeing from far), and telescope. "Near" is expressed by roots like "proxim" (proximity). So the answer is far.

12. The root "micro" means:
(a) small (b) large (c) middle (d) equal
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Micro" is Greek for "small," as in microscope, microbe, and microphone (small sound made large). "Large" is "mega" or "macro" (megaphone, macroeconomics) — a frequent pairing trap. So the answer is small.

13. The root "phil" means:
(a) hate (b) rule (c) fear (d) love
Answer: (d)
Solution: "Phil" is Greek for "love," as in philosophy (love of wisdom), philanthropy (love of mankind), and bibliophile (book lover). "Hate" is "mis" (misogyny, misanthrope). So the answer is love.

14. The root "phob" means:
(a) fear (b) love (c) sound (d) light
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Phob" is Greek for "fear," as in phobia, claustrophobia (fear of closed spaces), and xenophobia (fear of foreigners). The trap is "phil" (love), its opposite, which shares the same "ph" spelling and confuses many candidates. So the answer is fear.

(a) believe (b) know (c) run (d) lead
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Cred" is Latin for "believe," as in credible (believable), incredible (unbelievable), and credit (trust). "Know" is "sci" or "gn" (science, cognition). So the answer is believe.

16. The root "ject" means:
(a) carry (b) bend (c) throw (d) join
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Ject" is Latin for "throw," as in eject (throw out), project (throw forward), and reject (throw back). "Carry" is "port" (transport, import) — examiners love swapping these two. So the answer is throw.

Solution: "Rupt" is Latin for "break" or "burst," as in rupture, interrupt (break between), and erupt (break out). "Build" is "struct" (construct, structure). So the answer is break.

19. The root "scrib/script" means:
(a) read (b) count (c) write (d) draw
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Scrib/script" is Latin for "write," as in describe, manuscript (hand-written), and prescription (written before). "Read" is "leg/lect" (legible, lecture). So the answer is write.

20. The root "anthrop" means:
(a) god (b) man/human (c) animal (d) star
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Anthrop" is Greek for "man" or "human," as in anthropology (study of humans), philanthropy (love of humans), and misanthrope (hater of humans). "God" is "theo" (theology, atheist). So the answer is man/human.

21. The word "benevolent" most nearly means:
(a) harmful (b) kindly and charitable (c) wealthy (d) talkative
Answer: (b)
Solution: Break it down: "bene" (good) + "vol" (wish/will) = wishing good, hence kindly and charitable. The trap "wealthy" tempts because benefactors are often rich, but the root says nothing about money. So the answer is kindly and charitable.
Source: Standard Greek & Latin Roots (Vocabulary reference)

22. The word "malevolent" most nearly means:
(a) wishing harm to others (b) wishing good to others (c) unable to speak (d) fond of travel
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Mal" (bad) + "vol" (wish) = wishing bad, i.e., wishing harm. The examiner's trap is pairing it with "benevolent" (wishing good) — the two differ only in their first root, so read the prefix carefully. So the answer is wishing harm to others.
Source: Standard Greek & Latin Roots (Vocabulary reference)

23. The word "chronological" refers to arrangement by:
(a) size (b) time (c) importance (d) colour
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Chron" (time) + "log" (study/order) = arranged in order of time. The trap "colour" comes from confusing "chron" with "chrom" (colour, as in chrome) — a one-letter difference examiners exploit. So the answer is time.
Source: Standard Greek & Latin Roots (Vocabulary reference)

24. A "portable" device is one that can be:
(a) thrown easily (b) seen easily (c) carried easily (d) heard easily
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Port" (carry) + "able" (capable of) = able to be carried. The trap "thrown easily" misuses "ject" (throw) in place of "port" (carry), the two most commonly swapped roots in exams. So the answer is carried easily.
Source: Standard Greek & Latin Roots (Vocabulary reference)

25. The word "auditory" relates to the sense of:
(a) sight (b) smell (c) hearing (d) taste
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Aud" (hear) + "-itory" (relating to) = relating to hearing, as in auditory nerves. The trap "sight" belongs to "visual" (vid/vis = see); examiners pair auditory and visual because both describe senses. So the answer is hearing.
Source: Standard Greek & Latin Roots (Vocabulary reference)
