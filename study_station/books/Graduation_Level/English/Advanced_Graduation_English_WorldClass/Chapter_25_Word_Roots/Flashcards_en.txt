Card 1
Front: What does the Greek root "chron" mean? Give two derived words.
Back: Time. Examples: chronology (order of events in time), synchronize (to happen at the same time).

Card 2
Front: What does the Latin root "dict" mean, and how does it appear in "predict" and "contradict"?
Back: "Dict" = say/speak. Predict = say before (foretell); contradict = speak against.

Card 3
Front: Mnemonic: How do you remember that "bene" means good and "male" means bad?
Back: "BENEfits are good; MALicious people are bad." Benevolent = wishing good; malevolent = wishing harm.

Card 4
Front: What does the root "spect" mean? Name three words built on it.
Back: To look/see. Inspect (look into), respect (look again/regard), spectator (one who looks/watches).

Card 5
Front: Examiner's Trap: Does "inflammable" mean "not flammable"?
Back: No! "Inflammable" means easily set on fire — same as "flammable." The prefix "in-" here means "into," not "not." Don't let the prefix fool you.

Card 6
Front: What does the root "port" mean? Use it to decode "transport" and "export."
Back: To carry. Transport = carry across; export = carry out (of a country).

Card 7
Front: What is the difference between a prefix and a suffix?
Back: A prefix is added before the root and usually changes meaning (un-, pre-, anti-); a suffix is added after the root and often changes the word class (-tion makes a noun, -ful makes an adjective).

Card 8
Front: Decode "antibiotic" using roots: anti + bio + tic.
Back: Anti = against, bio = life, -tic = adjective suffix → "against life (of bacteria)" — a substance that kills living microbes.

Card 9
Front: What does the root "cred" mean? Why does "incredible" mean "unbelievable"?
Back: "Cred" = believe. Incredible = in (not) + cred (believe) + ible (able) → not able to be believed.

Card 10
Front: Mnemonic: How do you remember "omni" = all?
Back: "OMNIpresent God is everywhere, OMNIscient knows everything, OMNIvorous eats everything — OMNI = ALL."

Card 11
Front: Examiner's Trap: Is "uninterested" the same as "disinterested"?
Back: No. Uninterested = bored/not interested; disinterested = impartial/unbiased. Exams love swapping these two.

Card 12
Front: What does the root "path" mean, and how does it differ in "sympathy" and "apathy"?
Back: "Path" = feeling/suffering. Sympathy = feeling with someone; apathy = a (without) + pathy (feeling) → no feeling, indifference.

Card 13
Front: Break down "retrospect" into its morphological parts.
Back: Retro (backward) + spect (look) → to look back at past events.

Card 14
Front: What does the suffix "-phobia" mean? Give two examples.
Back: Fear. Claustrophobia (fear of closed spaces), xenophobia (fear of foreigners/strangers).

Card 15
Front: What does the root "vid/vis" mean? Decode "invisible" and "evidence."
Back: To see. Invisible = not able to be seen; evidence = that which is seen/shown (proof).

Card 16
Front: What does the root "morph" itself mean? What is "morphology"?
Back: "Morph" = form/shape. Morphology = the study of the forms/structure of words (roots + affixes).

Card 17
Front: Decode "circumlocution" using roots.
Back: Circum (around) + loqu/locut (speak) + -tion (noun suffix) → speaking around the point instead of directly.

Card 18
Front: What does the root "ject" mean? Decode "eject," "reject," and "project."
Back: To throw. Eject = throw out; reject = throw back; project = throw forward.

Card 19
Front: Examiner's Trap: The word "bemused" — does it mean "amused"?
Back: No. Bemused = confused or puzzled, not entertained. A classic sound-alike trap in exams.

Card 20
Front: Strategy: You meet an unknown word in the exam. What three-step root attack do you apply?
Back: (1) Strip the prefix and suffix to find the root. (2) Recall the root's meaning and reassemble the word. (3) Check the guessed meaning against the sentence context before answering.
