# 🧒 Feynman Technique — Word Roots & Morphology

## The Hardest Concept, Explained to a 12-Year-Old

**The concept:** How can you know the meaning of a word you have *never seen before*?

Imagine words are like **LEGO blocks**. Nobody memorises every possible LEGO model — that would be impossible. Instead, you learn the **blocks**: the red piece, the wheel piece, the door piece. Once you know the blocks, you can look at any new model and say, "Oh, that's a car with a door on top!"

English words work exactly the same way. There are only three kinds of blocks:

1. **Root** — the main block that carries the core meaning. Example: **port** = *carry*.
2. **Prefix** — a block stuck at the **front** that changes direction or feeling. Example: **ex-** = *out*.
3. **Suffix** — a block stuck at the **end** that tells you the word's *job* (noun, verb, adjective). Example: **-able** = *can be done*.

Now watch the magic. Take a word you have never seen: **exportable**.

- **ex-** = out
- **port** = carry
- **-able** = can be done

Put the blocks together: *"can be carried out"* → **something that can be sent out of the country**. You just decoded a brand-new word without a dictionary!

## A Daily-Life Example

Think of your **thali** at dinner. The **roti** is the root — the main part. The **achar (pickle)** on the side is the prefix — a small thing added before you eat that changes the whole taste. The **papad on top** is the suffix — it finishes the plate. Change the pickle, and the same roti tastes completely different. Change the prefix, and the same root means something completely different:

- **im**port = carry **in**
- **ex**port = carry **out**
- **trans**port = carry **across**
- **sup**port = carry from **below** (hold up)

One root, four different "tastes" — just by swapping the prefix.

## The Unforgettable Trick: "The Word Detective" 🕵️

Whenever you meet an unknown word in the exam, don't panic — **interrogate it** like a detective asking three questions:

1. **"Who is at the front?"** (prefix → direction/negation: un-, dis-, pre-, anti-)
2. **"Who is in the middle?"** (root → core meaning: dict = say, spect = look, rupt = break)
3. **"Who is at the end?"** (suffix → word's job: -tion = noun, -ive = adjective, -ify = verb)

Example under exam pressure: **"The verdict was unpredictable."**

- un- (not) + pre (before) + dict (say) + -able (can be) → *"cannot be said before"* → **cannot be foreseen**. Solved in 10 seconds, no dictionary, no guessing.

## Why This Beats Memorisation

Your brain forgets lists but remembers **stories and patterns**. Memorising 5,000 words is carrying 5,000 loose bricks. Learning 50 roots is carrying 50 LEGO sets that build those same 5,000 words. One root like **spect** (look) unlocks: *inspect, respect, spectator, spectacle, prospect, retrospect, introspect* — seven exam words for the price of one.
