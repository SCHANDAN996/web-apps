51. The root "bene" means:
(a) bad (b) good (c) against (d) before
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Mal" means bad and "anti" means against, which tempts students who confuse bene- with its opposite male-. "Bene" is Latin for good, as in benefit and benevolent, so the answer is good.
Source: Standard Latin root lists (NCERT-level vocabulary)

52. The root "chron" relates to:
(a) colour (b) sound (c) time (d) earth
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Chrom" (colour) looks almost identical to "chron" and is the classic trap. "Chron" is Greek for time, as in chronology and chronic, so the answer is time.
Source: Standard Greek root lists

53. In the word "transport", the prefix "trans-" means:
(a) under (b) across (c) behind (d) together
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Sub" means under and "con" means together, but neither fits carrying something from one place to another. "Trans-" means across, as in transfer and translate, so the answer is across.
Source: Standard Latin prefix lists

54. The root "dict" means:
(a) speak (b) write (c) read (d) hear
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Scrib/script" means write and "aud" means hear, which students mix up with dict. "Dict" is Latin for speak or say, as in predict and dictionary, so the answer is speak.
Source: Standard Latin root lists

55. The suffix "-ology" means:
(a) study of (b) fear of (c) lover of (d) hater of
Answer: (a)
Solution: "-Phobia" means fear and "-phile" means lover, both common look-alike suffixes. "-Ology" means study of, as in biology and geology, so the answer is study of.

56. The root "port" means:
(a) throw (b) see (c) break (d) carry

57. The prefix "anti-" means:
(a) before (b) within (c) after (d) against
Answer: (d)
Solution: "Ante-" means before and looks nearly identical to "anti-", the examiner's favourite trap. "Anti-" means against, as in antibody and antisocial, so the answer is against.

61. "Circumlocution" most nearly means:
(a) speaking directly (b) speaking loudly (c) talking in a roundabout way (d) writing in circles
Answer: (c)
Solution: Students who know "loqui" means speak often pick speaking loudly, but the prefix is the key. "Circum" means around and "loqui" means speak, so circumlocution is talking around the point, and the answer is talking in a roundabout way.
Source: Standard Latin root lists

62. The word "bilingual" contains the root "lingua", which means:
(a) speech sound (b) grammar (c) two (d) tongue or language
Answer: (d)
Solution: "Bi" means two, and students wrongly assign the whole meaning to the prefix. "Lingua" is Latin for tongue or language, as in linguist, so the answer is tongue or language.

63. "Autobiography" breaks down as auto + bio + graphy. The root "graph" means:

Answer: (c)
Solution: "Bio" means life and "auto" means self, so students who rush pick those. "Graph" is Greek for write, as in paragraph and telegraph, so the answer is write.

64. A "misanthrope" is a person who:
(a) loves mankind (b) hates mankind (c) fears crowds (d) studies humans
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Philanthrope" (lover of mankind) is the tempting opposite examiners pair with this word. "Mis" means hate and "anthropos" means mankind, so the answer is hates mankind.

65. The prefix in "postpone" means:
(a) before (b) against (c) after (d) away
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Pre" means before, and postpone/prepone confusion is a classic trap. "Post" is Latin for after, as in postscript and postwar, so the answer is after.
Source: Standard Latin prefix lists

66. "Credulous" comes from the root "cred" (believe). A credulous person:
(a) refuses to believe anything (b) is trustworthy (c) believes things too easily (d) spreads rumours
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Credible" (believable, trustworthy) tempts students who ignore the suffix. "-Ous" means full of, so credulous means full of belief, i.e. believing too easily, and the answer is believes things too easily.
Source: Standard Latin root lists

67. The root "mort" means death. Which word means "a place where dead bodies are kept"?
(a) Mortar (b) Mortuary (c) Mortgage (d) Immortal
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Immortal" (deathless) tempts because it shares the root but is an adjective, not a place. A mortuary is the place for the dead, so the answer is Mortuary.
Source: Standard Latin root lists

68. "Retrospect" contains "retro" (backward) and "spect" (look). It means:
(a) looking back at the past (b) looking forward (c) looking carefully (d) looking inside
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Prospect" (looking forward) is the paired trap word. "Retro" means backward, so retrospect is looking back, and the answer is looking back at the past.
Source: Standard Latin root lists

70. "Egregious" originally comes from "e-" (out of) + "grex" (flock). Its modern meaning is:
(a) outstandingly bad (b) highly sociable (c) part of a group (d) quietly obedient
Answer: (a)
Solution: Students who know "gregarious" (sociable, from grex = flock) pick highly sociable, the examiner's trap. Egregious means standing out from the flock in a bad way, so the answer is outstandingly bad.

73. The root "path" means feeling or suffering. "Apathy" therefore means:

Solution: "Sympathy" (shared feeling) tempts because both words contain path. The prefix "a-" means without, so apathy is without feeling, and the answer is lack of feeling.

74. "Pseudonym" contains "pseudo" (false) and "onym" (name). It means:

Solution: "Anonymous" (nameless) confuses students into picking a famous name. "Pseudo" means false, so a pseudonym is a false name, and the answer is a false or assumed name.
