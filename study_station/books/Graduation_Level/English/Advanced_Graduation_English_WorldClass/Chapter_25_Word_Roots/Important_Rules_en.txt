# 📋 Important Rules & Tips — Word Roots & Morphology

## (a) 12 High-Yield Rules

| # | Rule | Example | Examiner's Trap |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | A **root** carries the core meaning; it cannot be broken down further. | *spect* = look → inspect, spectator, prospect | Don't confuse root with prefix: "in-" in *inspect* is a prefix, not the root. |
| 2 | A **prefix** comes before the root and modifies meaning (often negation/direction). | *un-* + happy → unhappy | "in-" changes to *im-/il-/ir-* before certain letters (impossible, illegal, irregular) — spelling traps. |
| 3 | A **suffix** comes after the root and usually changes the **word class** (noun/verb/adjective). | teach + *-er* → teacher (verb → noun) | "-ic" vs "-ical" (historic ≠ historical) — meaning shifts, not just grammar. |
| 4 | Greek root **bio** = life; **geo** = earth; **chrono** = time. | biology, geography, chronological | *Biography* (life-writing) vs *bibliography* (book-writing) — look-alike trap. |
| 5 | Latin root **dict** = say; **port** = carry; **scrib/scrip** = write. | predict, transport, describe | *Predict* = say before; *contradict* = say against — one prefix flips meaning entirely. |
| 6 | Negative prefixes: *a-/an-* (without), *anti-* (against), *counter-* (opposing). | amoral, antibody, counterattack | *Amoral* (without morals) ≠ *immoral* (against morals) — classic exam trap. |
| 7 | Number roots: *mono-* (1), *bi-/di-* (2), *tri-* (3), *poly-/multi-* (many). | monopoly, bilingual, triangle, polyglot | *Biannual* (twice a year) vs *biennial* (once in two years) — frequently tested. |
| 8 | Roots of feeling/judgment: *path* (feeling), *cred* (believe), *jud* (judge). | sympathy, credible, judicial | *Credible* (believable) vs *credulous* (too ready to believe) — near-twins. |
| 9 | One root can spawn a whole word family — learn families, not single words. | *ject* = throw → reject, inject, project, eject, trajectory | Don't assume every "ject" word is negative: *project* is neutral. |
| 10 | **Context beats memorisation**: confirm a guessed meaning against the sentence. | "His *laconic* reply ended the debate" → laconic = brief | A familiar-looking word may be used in a rare sense (*grave* = serious, not burial place). |
| 11 | Some words only *look* like they share a root — false friends exist. | *Restive* ≠ restful (restive = restless) | Sound-right trap: *disinterested* (impartial) ≠ *uninterested* (bored). |
| 12 | Word class changes pronunciation/stress: noun PRO-duce vs verb pro-DUCE. | REcord (noun) / reCORD (verb) | In error-spotting, check whether the suffix matches the required word class. |

## (b) Time Management — The 15/45-Second Rule

| Type | What it looks like | Time | Strategy |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Fact-based (15 sec)** | Direct root/affix meaning: "The root 'port' means…", one-word substitution from a known family, antonym/synonym of a common derived word | ≤ 15 sec | Recall instantly. If the root is known, answer and move on — never re-read. |
| **Application (30 sec)** | Meaning of an unfamiliar word built from known parts (e.g., *circumspect* = circum + spect) | ~30 sec | Split into prefix + root + suffix, assemble meaning, match with options. |
| **Inference-based (45 sec)** | Word-in-context questions in a passage, tone questions, words with rare senses | ≤ 45 sec | Read the sentence before and after; eliminate options that fit the word but not the context. If still stuck after 45 sec, mark and skip. |

**Rule of thumb:** Never spend 45 seconds on a 15-second recall question — either you know the root or you don't. Save the time for context-inference questions where thinking actually pays.

## (c) Morphology — Root, Affix, Example

| Component | Type | Meaning | Example Words |
|---|---|---|---|
| *spect* (Latin) | Root | look, see | inspect, spectator, retrospect, perspective |
| *dict* (Latin) | Root | say, speak | predict, verdict, contradict, dictator |
| *port* (Latin) | Root | carry | transport, export, portable, portfolio |
| *scrib/scrip* (Latin) | Root | write | describe, manuscript, inscription, prescribe |
| *bio* (Greek) | Root | life | biology, biography, antibiotic, symbiosis |
| *chrono* (Greek) | Root | time | chronology, chronic, synchronize, anachronism |
| *path* (Greek) | Root | feeling, suffering | sympathy, empathy, apathy, pathology |
| *cred* (Latin) | Root | believe | credible, credit, incredible, credulous |
| *pre-* | Prefix | before | predict, preview, precaution |
| *re-* | Prefix | again, back | rewrite, return, retrospect |
| *anti-* | Prefix | against | antibiotic, antisocial, antidote |
| *circum-* | Prefix | around | circumspect, circumference, circumvent |
| *-logy* | Suffix | study of | biology, geology, psychology |
| *-able/-ible* | Suffix | capable of being | portable, credible, visible |
| *-tion/-sion* | Suffix | act/state (makes noun) | description, inspection, tension |
| *-ist* | Suffix | one who does | biologist, typist, novelist |

**Memory hook:** *Prefix fixes the direction, Root carries the meaning, Suffix decides the job (word class).*
