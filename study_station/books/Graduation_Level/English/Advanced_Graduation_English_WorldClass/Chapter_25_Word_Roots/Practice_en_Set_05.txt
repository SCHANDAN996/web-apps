101. The word "antebellum" is built from the Latin roots "ante" (before) and "bellum" (war). What does it mean?
(a) Relating to the period after a war (b) Relating to the period before a war (c) Opposed to all wars (d) A war fought between two nations
Answer: (b)
Solution: The tempting wrong choice confuses "ante" (before) with "anti" (against) or "post" (after). "Ante" means before, as in "antecedent" and "anterior," so antebellum means existing before a war, especially the American Civil War. The answer is "relating to the period before a war."
Source: NCERT Class 11 English (Vocabulary – Latin roots)

102. Choose the word that correctly completes the sentence: "The doctor warned that the disease was ______, spreading rapidly through the population."
(a) epidemic (b) endemic (c) pandemic (d) academic
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Endemic" is the tempting trap because it also relates to disease, but it means confined to a particular region and does not imply rapid spread; "pandemic" means spread across countries or continents, which is too broad here. "Epi" (upon) + "demos" (people) gives "epidemic" — spreading among the people. The answer is "epidemic."
Source: PYQ-style (Greek root: demos)

103. Identify the word whose root meaning is INCORRECTLY matched.
(a) Telephone — tele (sound) (b) Chronology — chronos (time) (c) Biology — bios (life) (d) Geography — geo (earth)
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is that "telephone" contains "phone," which means sound, so students assume "tele" also means sound. In fact "tele" means "far/distant" (television, telescope), while "phone" means sound. The incorrectly matched pair is "telephone — tele (sound)."
Source: NCERT Class 10 English (Word roots)

104. "The manager's ______ remarks offended everyone in the meeting." Choose the word derived from "mal" (bad).
(a) beneficial (b) benevolent (c) malediction (d) magnificent
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Benevolent" and "beneficial" come from "bene" (good), the opposite of what the sentence needs, and "magnificent" comes from "magnus" (great). "Mal" (bad) + "dicere" (to speak) gives "malediction" — evil speech, a curse or abusive remark. The answer is "malediction."
Source: PYQ-style (Latin root: mal)

105. Spot the error: "The unprecedented (P) / scale of the (Q) / disaster left the (R) / government completely helplessly. (S)"
(a) Part S (b) Part Q (c) Part P (d) Part R
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is to hunt for an error in the long word "unprecedented," which is correctly formed. The real error is in Part S: "helplessly" is an adverb, but the sentence needs the adjective "helpless" after the verb "left" as an object complement. The error is in Part S.
Source: PYQ-style (Morphology: adjective vs adverb)

106. Which of the following words is formed with a PREFIX meaning "against"?
(a) Predict (b) Transport (c) Contradict (d) Export
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Predict" is tempting because "pre" looks oppositional, but it means "before"; "trans" means across and "ex" means out. "Contra" means against, so "contradict" literally means "to speak against." The answer is "contradict."
Source: NCERT Class 11 English (Prefixes)

107. The root "cred" (believe) appears in all of the following EXCEPT:
(a) Credulous (b) Credential (c) Incredible (d) Decrepit
Answer: (d)
Solution: "Decrepit" is the trap because it sounds similar to "credit" words, but it comes from "crepare" (to crack/creak), meaning worn out. Incredible (not believable), credulous (too ready to believe), and credential (proof of trust) all contain "cred" (believe). The answer is "decrepit."
Source: PYQ-style (Latin root: cred)

108. Choose the correct meaning of "circumlocution" (circum = around, loqui = to speak).
(a) Speaking clearly and directly (b) Talking in a roundabout way (c) Speaking many languages (d) Repeating one's own words
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Speaking many languages" is tempting because "loqui" relates to speech and "circum" suggests many directions, but the literal sense is "speaking around" the point — an indirect, wordy way of talking. The answer is "talking in a roundabout way."
Source: NCERT Class 11 English (Latin roots)

109. "The new policy will ______ the old regulations." Choose the correctly spelt word meaning "set aside/replace," built from "super" (over) + "sedere" (sit).
(a) superceed (b) supersed (c) supercede (d) supersede
Answer: (d)
Solution: "Supercede" is the classic examiner's trap — it is the most common spelling error in English because of analogy with "precede" and "recede." The correct spelling is "supersede," from Latin "supersedere" (to sit above/refrain from). The answer is "supersede."
Source: PYQ-style (Spelling trap: supersede)

110. Spot the error: "The phenomenon (P) / of climate change (Q) / have affected (R) / every coastal region. (S)"
(a) Part P (b) Part Q (c) Part R (d) No error
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is the intervening phrase "of climate change," which pushes the plural-sounding "change" next to the verb and lures students toward "have." The true subject is the singular "phenomenon," so the verb must be "has affected." The error is in Part R.
Source: PYQ-style (Intervening phrase trap)

111. The suffix "-ology" means "the study of." Which word does NOT follow this pattern?
(a) Zoology (b) Apology (c) Geology (d) Criminology
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Apology" is the trap: it ends in "-logy" but comes from Greek "apologia" (a speech in defence), not "the study of" anything. Zoology (study of animals), geology (study of earth), and criminology (study of crime) all follow the "-ology = study of" pattern. The answer is "apology."
Source: NCERT Class 11 English (Suffixes)

112. Choose the word derived from "frangere" (to break) that means "easily broken."
(a) suffrage (b) refractory (c) infraction (d) fragile
Answer: (d)
Solution: "Infraction" is tempting because it also comes from "frangere," but it means a violation of a rule (breaking a law), not "easily broken"; "refractory" means stubborn, and "suffrage" (the right to vote) comes from "suffragium," not "frangere." "Fragile" directly means easily broken. The answer is "fragile."
Source: NCERT Class 11 English (Latin root: frangere)

113. Which pair of words shares the SAME root?
(a) Audience and audible (b) Audit and author (c) Audible and visual (d) See and vision
Answer: (a)
Solution: "See and vision" is the trap because both relate to sight, but "see" is a Germanic word while "vision" comes from Latin "videre" — different roots. "Audience" and "audible" both come directly from Latin "audire" (to hear): people who hear, and able to be heard. The answer is "audience and audible."
Source: NCERT Class 10 English (Latin root: audire)

114. The word "benevolent" means:
(a) Wishing harm to others (b) Speaking well of the dead (c) Wishing good to others (d) Having great power
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Speaking well of the dead" is the trap — that describes "eulogy" (eu = good, logos = speech), which students confuse with "bene." "Bene" (good/well) + "volens" (wishing) gives benevolent — kindly, wishing good. The answer is "wishing good to others."
Source: NCERT Class 11 English (Latin roots: bene, vol)

115. Spot the error: "Neither of the two (P) / candidates were (Q) / found eligible (R) / for the scholarship. (S)"
(a) Part P (b) Part R (c) Part Q (d) No error
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is the plural word "candidates" sitting right before the verb, which makes "were" sound correct (the sound-right trap). "Neither of" takes a singular verb, so it should be "was." The error is in Part Q.
Source: PYQ-style (Sound-right trap)

116. "The scientist's theory was ______ by years of experimental data." Choose the word from "robur" (strength/oak).
(a) corrugated (b) corrupted (c) incorporated (d) corroborated
Answer: (d)
Solution: "Corrupted" is the tempting look-alike (cor + rumpere, to break), but it means spoiled — the opposite of supported. "Corroborate" (com + robur = to strengthen together) means to confirm with evidence. The answer is "corroborated."
Source: PYQ-style (Latin root: robur)

117. Which word means "a government by the people"?
(a) Autocracy (b) Democracy (c) Theocracy (d) Bureaucracy
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Autocracy" is the trap for students who mix up "auto" (self) with "demos" (people). Democracy = demos (people) + kratos (rule); autocracy = rule by one self; bureaucracy = rule by officials; theocracy = rule by religious authority. The answer is "democracy."
Source: NCERT Class 10 English (Greek roots: demos, kratos)

118. The prefix "omni-" means "all." Which word is WRONGLY defined?
(a) Omniscient — all-knowing (b) Omnipresent — present in the past (c) Omnivorous — eating everything (d) Omnipotent — all-powerful
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is that "omnipresent" sounds like it could relate to time, but "present" here means "being in a place," so omnipresent means present everywhere, not "present in the past." The other three definitions are correct. The wrongly defined word is "omnipresent — present in the past."
Source: PYQ-style (Latin prefix: omni)

119. "The two countries signed an ______ to end hostilities." Choose the word built from "arma" (weapons) + "sistere" (to stand still).
(a) armistice (b) animosity (c) antipathy (d) annuity
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Animosity" and "antipathy" are tempting because they relate to conflict, but they mean hatred — the opposite of ending hostilities. "Armistice" literally means "a standing still of arms" — a formal agreement to stop fighting. The answer is "armistice."
Source: PYQ-style (Latin roots: arma, sistere)

120. Spot the error: "The committee (P) / have submitted (Q) / its report (R) / to the ministry. (S)"
(a) Part Q (b) No error (c) Part R (d) Part P
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is treating "committee" as plural because it contains many people (the collective noun trap). When the committee acts as a single body — confirmed by the singular pronoun "its" in Part R — it takes a singular verb: "has submitted." The error is in Part Q.
Source: PYQ-style (Collective noun trap)

121. The word "retrospect" is built from "retro" (backward) + "spect" (look). Its closest meaning is:
(a) Showing respect to elders (b) Looking back at past events (c) Examining something closely (d) Looking forward with hope
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Showing respect" is the trap because "respect" shares the "spect" root, but "re" in respect means "again" (to look again/esteem), while "retro" means backward. Retrospect = looking back at the past. The answer is "looking back at past events."
Source: NCERT Class 11 English (Latin root: spect)

122. Which sentence uses the morphologically correct word?
(a) The judge gave an impartial verdict. (b) The judge gave a nonpartial verdict. (c) The judge gave an unpartial verdict. (d) The judge gave an impart verdict.
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Unpartial" is the trap — students over-apply the common negative prefix "un-," but the standard negative of "partial" is formed with "im-" (im + partial = impartial), just as possible becomes impossible. The correct sentence is "The judge gave an impartial verdict."
Source: PYQ-style (Negative prefixes: im-/un-)

123. "The manager tried to ______ the angry workers with promises of a bonus." Choose the word from "placere" (to please).
(a) placid (b) implacable (c) placate (d) complacent
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Placid" is the trap — it shares the root but is an adjective meaning calm, and the sentence needs a verb; "implacable" means cannot be pacified, and "complacent" means self-satisfied. "Placate" means to calm or pacify someone. The answer is "placate."
Source: PYQ-style (Latin root: plac)

124. The root "mort" (death) is found in all of the following EXCEPT:
(a) Mortal (b) Mortgage (c) Morbid (d) Moral
Answer: (d)
Solution: "Morbid" is the trap because it relates to disease and gloom, but it does come from "morbus/mors," connected with death and decay. "Moral" comes from Latin "mos/moris" (custom, character) and has nothing to do with death. The answer is "moral."
Source: NCERT Class 11 English (Latin root: mort)

125. Read the inference: "Although the medicine was described as a panacea, it cured only one type of infection." What does "panacea" (pan = all, akos = cure) imply the writer believes?
(a) The medicine was cheap and available (b) The medicine was a complete success (c) The medicine needs more testing (d) The medicine was falsely claimed to cure everything
Answer: (d)
Solution: "Complete success" is the trap — students see "cured" and ignore the contrast word "although," which signals the writer's critical tone. A panacea is a supposed cure-all; saying it cured only one infection implies the claim was exaggerated or false. The answer is "the medicine was falsely claimed to cure everything."
Source: PYQ-style (Greek root: pan; tone/inference)
