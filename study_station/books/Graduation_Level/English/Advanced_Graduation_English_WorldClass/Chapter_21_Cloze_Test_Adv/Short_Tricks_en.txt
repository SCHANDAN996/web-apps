# 🪄 Short Tricks + Skip Strategy — Advanced Cloze Test

---

## Trick 1 — The "Read Twice, Fill Once" Rule

> 📦 **BOX: 2R1F**
> **R**ead the whole passage once (no filling) → **R**ead again blank-by-blank → **F**ill only on the second pass.
> Most cloze errors happen because students fill blank 1 before knowing the passage's tone. The answer to blank 3 often hides in the last line.

---

## Trick 2 — The Tone Thermometer 🌡️

> 📦 **BOX: P-N-N**
> Label the passage **P**ositive, **N**egative, or **N**eutral in 10 seconds.
> Then eliminate options that clash with the tone.
> Passage criticising a policy? Delete "commendable, laudable, praiseworthy" instantly — half the options die in 5 seconds.

---

## Trick 3 — The Collocation Magnet 🧲

> 📦 **BOX: "Words have best friends"**
> Some words only sit next to specific partners:
> - **make** a decision (never *do* a decision)
> - **do** homework (never *make* homework)
> - **heavy** rain (never *strong* rain)
> - **commit** a crime (never *do* a crime)
> - **take** a risk | **pay** attention | **catch** a cold
>
> When two options are grammatically correct, the **collocation** decides. Ask: "Which word is this blank's best friend?"

---

## Trick 4 — The Blank-Type Scanner 🔍

> 📦 **BOX: G-V-C (Grammar – Vocabulary – Connector)**
> Before looking at options, classify the blank:
> - **G** = Grammar blank (tense, article, preposition, subject-verb) → apply rules, 15 sec.
> - **V** = Vocabulary blank (word meaning/tone) → use context + roots, 30–45 sec.
> - **C** = Connector blank (however, therefore, moreover) → check if the sentence *continues* or *contrasts* the previous idea, 15 sec.
>
> Knowing the blank type tells you which tool to pull out.

---

## Trick 5 — The Connector Compass 🧭

> 📦 **BOX: "Same road or U-turn?"**
> - **Same road (continuation):** and, moreover, furthermore, therefore, consequently, similarly, for instance
> - **U-turn (contrast):** but, however, although, nevertheless, yet, despite, whereas, on the contrary
>
> Read the sentence before and after the blank. If idea 2 *opposes* idea 1 → U-turn connector. If it *adds* → same-road connector. This solves every connector blank in under 15 seconds.

---

## Trick 6 — The Preposition Glue Trick

> 📦 **BOX: "Verbs marry fixed prepositions"**
> - depend **on** | insist **on** | rely **on**
> - consist **of** | accuse **of** | dream **of**
> - succeed **in** | persist **in** | result **in**
> - belong **to** | object **to** | adhere **to**
> - differ **from** | refrain **from** | abstain **from**
> - deal **with** | cope **with** | comply **with**
>
> If the blank is a preposition, look LEFT at the verb/adjective — it has already chosen its partner.

---

## Trick 7 — The Subject-Verb Distance Trap 🎯

> 📦 **BOX: "Ignore the middlemen"**
> "The quality of the products **is/are** good."
> Cross out everything between the subject and the verb: *The quality ~~of the products~~ **is** good.*
> Examiners insert long intervening phrases (of…, along with…, as well as…) to make you match the verb with the wrong noun. **Bracket the phrase, then decide.**

---

## Trick 8 — The Article 3-Second Check

> 📦 **BOX: A / AN / THE**
> - First mention, singular, countable → **a/an** (sound decides: *an hour*, *a university*)
> - Second mention or specific/unique → **the** (*the sun*, *the Ganga*, *the book I bought*)
> - General plural/uncountable → **no article** (*___ honesty is the best policy*)
>
> Nearly every article blank in exams falls into one of these three buckets.

---

## Trick 9 — The Tense Timeline ⏳

> 📦 **BOX: "One passage, one clock"**
> Find the tense of the first sentence — that is the passage's "clock."
> - Story in past → blanks take past forms (*was, had, went*)
> - General facts/analysis → present forms (*is, has, shows*)
> Only switch tenses if a time marker (*yesterday, by 2030, since then*) forces a jump. No marker = stay on the main clock.

---

## Trick 10 — The Root Decoder 🔑

> 📦 **BOX: "Break the word, steal the meaning"**
> Unknown word in options? Split it:
> - **bene-** (good) + **volent** (wishing) = kind-hearted
> - **mal-** (bad) + **volent** (wishing) = wishing harm → malevolent
> - **omni-** (all) + **potent** (power) = all-powerful
> - **pre-** (before) + **dict** (say) = say before → foretell
>
> Even a partial decode eliminates 2 wrong options.

---

## Trick 11 — The Pronoun Mirror 🪞

> 📦 **BOX: "A pronoun must point backwards"**
> For blanks needing *it/they/this/these*, ask: "What exactly does it point to?"
> - Singular noun before → it/this
> - Plural noun before → they/these
> - No clear noun → the option is a trap; re-read the previous sentence.

---

## Trick 12 — The "Sound-Right" Alarm 🚨

> 📦 **BOX: "If it sounds right because of Hindi, it's probably wrong"**
> Hindi speakers' classic traps:
> - ❌ "discuss about" → ✅ "discuss"
> - ❌ "return back" → ✅ "return"
> - ❌ "comprise of" → ✅ "comprise / is composed of"
> - ❌ "cope up with" → ✅ "cope with"
>
> When an option feels comfortable because it mirrors Hindi word order — double-check it. Examiners plant these deliberately.

---

## Trick 13 — The Elimination Ladder 🪜

> 📦 **BOX: 4 → 2 → 1**
> Never hunt for the right answer directly. Climb down:
> 1. Delete options that break **grammar** (wrong form/tense).
> 2. Delete options that break **tone** (positive word in a critical passage).
> 3. Between the last two, pick the one with the correct **collocation**.
>
> Even with zero vocabulary knowledge, this ladder gets you to 50–50 every time.

---

## Trick 14 — The Master Mnemonic: **"T-G-C-P"** 🧠

> 📦 **BOX: "The Great Cloze Plan"**
> **T**one → **G**rammar → **C**ollocation → **P**osition (connector logic)
> Run every blank through these four filters in this order. If an option survives all four, it IS the answer. Chant it: *"Tone, Grammar, Collocation, Position — The Great Cloze Plan!"*

---

## ⏭️ Skip Strategy

> 📦 **SKIP TIP 1 — The 45-Second Ceiling**
> If a vocabulary blank has 4 unknown words and the context gives no clue after 45 seconds, mark it and move on. One stubborn blank costs less than three easy blanks you never reached. Return only if time remains.

> 📦 **SKIP TIP 2 — Solve Grammar Blanks First**
> Within a cloze passage, blanks are independent. Do all **G-type** (grammar/preposition/article) blanks first — they are rule-based and take 15 seconds each. Bank these marks, then spend remaining time on V-type vocabulary blanks.

> 📦 **SKIP TIP 3 — Never Skip Connector Blanks**
> Connector blanks (however/therefore/although) look hard but are the fastest marks in the passage — the "Same road or U-turn?" test solves them in 10–15 seconds with zero vocabulary needed. Skipping them is leaving free marks on the table.

> 📦 **SKIP TIP 4 — The No-Negative-Guess Rule**
> If you can eliminate **two** options, always guess — the maths favours you even with negative marking. If all four options look equally alien, skip. Guess from 50–50, never from 0–100.
