Card 1
Front: What is the very first step of the "Detective Approach" to solving an Advanced Cloze Test?
Back: Read the entire passage once WITHOUT looking at the options, to grasp the theme, tone (positive/negative/neutral), and direction of the argument. Options are traps until you know the story.

Card 2
Front: The passage says, "The new policy, though well-intentioned, ______ failed to help farmers." Which connector type fits the blank?
Back: A result/consequence word like "ultimately" or "eventually". "Though" signals concession, so the main clause must show the outcome despite good intentions.

Card 3
Front: What is the difference between a "grammar blank" and a "vocabulary blank" in a cloze test?
Back: A grammar blank tests structure (tense, article, preposition, conjunction, subject–verb agreement). A vocabulary blank tests meaning (the right word for the context, collocation, or tone). Identify the type first — it tells you where to look for the clue.

Card 4
Front: Mnemonic — how do you remember the four checks for every blank?
Back: "G-T-C-F": Grammar (does it fit the structure?), Tone (positive/negative/neutral match?), Collocation (does it partner naturally with neighbouring words?), Flow (does the sentence logic continue smoothly?). Check all four before locking an answer.

Card 5
Front: Why should you read the sentence BEFORE and AFTER the blank, not just the blank's own sentence?
Back: Cloze passages are connected text. Pronouns (he, it, they), time references, and contrast words (however, therefore) often get their meaning from neighbouring sentences. The clue is frequently outside the blank's sentence.

Card 6
Front: Examiner trap — two options are both grammatically correct. How do you choose?
Back: Match the tone and collocation, not just grammar. Example: "He ______ the offer" — both "rejected" and "declined" are grammatical, but "declined an offer" is the natural collocation in formal passages. Grammar alone never settles a cloze blank.

Card 7
Front: What does the connector "however" tell you about the blank that follows it?
Back: A contrast is coming. The idea after "however" must oppose or qualify the idea before it. If the earlier sentence is positive, the blank usually needs a negative or limiting word — and vice versa.

Card 8
Front: The blank is followed by "______ of the players was injured." Which option type is correct: "Each" or "All"?
Back: "Each" — because the verb is singular ("was"). "All of the players were injured" would need a plural verb. Subject–verb agreement after the blank is a free clue; always check the verb.

Card 9
Front: What is a "collocation" and why does it matter in cloze tests?
Back: A collocation is a natural word partnership: "make a decision" (not "do a decision"), "heavy rain" (not "strong rain"), "pay attention". Examiners place a correct-meaning but wrong-partner word as a distractor.

Card 10
Front: Examiner trap — the option "affect" vs "effect" appears at a blank. How do you decide quickly?
Back: Check the grammar slot: a verb position takes "affect" (The news affected him); a noun position takes "effect" (The effect was visible). If an article (a/an/the) sits before the blank, it must be the noun — "effect".

Card 11
Front: Should you solve cloze blanks strictly in order (1, 2, 3…)?
Back: No. Solve the easy, confident blanks first — they lock in the passage's meaning and tone, which then reveals clues for the harder blanks. Treat the passage as one puzzle, not ten separate questions.

Card 12
Front: Mnemonic — how do you classify connector words quickly?
Back: "A-C-R-E": Addition (and, moreover, also), Contrast (but, however, although), Result (so, therefore, hence), Example/Explanation (for instance, because, since). Name the relationship first, then pick the connector family.

Card 13
Front: The passage tone is critical of a government scheme, but one option is a praising word that fits grammatically. What do you do?
Back: Reject it. Tone consistency beats grammar. A passage that criticises throughout will not suddenly praise in one blank unless a contrast connector (but, yet) signals the shift. No signal, no shift.

Card 14
Front: What should you do when a blank seems to have no clue in its own sentence?
Back: Look for: (1) a pronoun and find its noun earlier, (2) a repeated idea or synonym elsewhere in the passage, (3) the paragraph's opening sentence, which usually states the main idea the blank must support.

Card 15
Front: Why is "eliminate the impossible" better than "find the perfect" in cloze tests?
Back: Usually 2 of the 4 options are clearly wrong (wrong tense, wrong tone, wrong collocation). Eliminating them doubles your odds and focuses your thinking on the real contest between the remaining two.

Card 16
Front: A blank needs an article: "______ honest man". Which article and why?
Back: "An" — article choice depends on SOUND, not spelling. "Honest" begins with a vowel sound (the h is silent), so it takes "an". Examiners use words like honest, hour, heir, university, European to test this.

Card 17
Front: After filling all blanks, what is the final step before moving on?
Back: Re-read the complete passage with your chosen words inserted. If any sentence breaks the flow, tone, or logic, revisit that blank. A correct set of answers reads like one smooth, original paragraph.

Card 18
Front: The blank is between two clauses: "He worked hard ______ he could pass the exam." Which connector family fits?
Back: Purpose — "so that" (or "in order that"). The second clause states the aim of the first. Don't confuse with result ("so" alone = result; "so that" = purpose), a classic examiner trap.

Card 19
Front: How much time should you spend on one stubborn blank in the exam?
Back: Very little — mark your best elimination-based guess and move on. Return only if time remains. One blank is worth the same as every other question; losing 90 seconds on it costs you easier marks elsewhere.

Card 20
Front: What single habit improves cloze scores the most over time?
Back: Daily reading of editorials and quality articles. Cloze tests reward an ear for natural English — collocations, connectors, and tone — which grows from reading, not from memorising word lists alone.
