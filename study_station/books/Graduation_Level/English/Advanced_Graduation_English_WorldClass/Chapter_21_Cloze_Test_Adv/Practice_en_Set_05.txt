Advanced Cloze Test — Practice Set 5 (Questions 101–125)
Difficulty: 15 Medium, 10 Hard | Examiner-trap questions: 108, 119

Directions (Q. 101–105): In the following passage, there are five blanks. Choose the most appropriate word for each blank.

Passage: Artificial intelligence is no longer a distant promise; it has begun to (101)______ nearly every sector of the economy. Yet its benefits are not evenly (102)______. While large firms can afford vast computing power, smaller enterprises often struggle to (103)______ pace. Policymakers therefore face a delicate task: encouraging innovation without (104)______ the social fabric. The choices made today will (105)______ the labour market for decades.

101. Choose the word for blank 101.
(a) perplex (b) persecute (c) perish (d) permeate
Answer: (d)
Solution: "Permeate" means to spread through every part of something, which fits "nearly every sector". "Persecute" (harass), "perish" (die) and "perplex" (confuse) do not fit the context of AI spreading across the economy.
Source: PYQ-style

102. Choose the word for blank 102.
(a) disguised (b) disturbed (c) distributed (d) dissolved
Answer: (c)
Solution: The contrast in the next sentence (large firms vs smaller enterprises) shows the issue is unequal sharing of benefits, so "distributed" is correct. "Disturbed" and "disguised" are sound-alike traps with wrong meanings.
Source: PYQ-style

103. Choose the word for blank 103.
(a) make (b) hold (c) take (d) keep
Answer: (d)
Solution: The fixed collocation is "keep pace" (with), meaning to progress at the same speed. "Hold pace", "take pace" and "make pace" are not standard English collocations.
Source: PYQ-style

104. Choose the word for blank 104.
(a) eroding (b) tearing up (c) rending (d) tearing
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Eroding the social fabric" is the standard collocation meaning gradually weakening society. "Tearing" is too violent and literal; the context is gradual harm, so "eroding" fits the measured tone.
Source: PYQ-style

105. Choose the word for blank 105.
(a) shape (b) shake (c) shatter (d) shrink
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Shape the labour market" means to influence its future form, matching "for decades". "Shatter" is too destructive and "shrink" is unsupported by the passage.
Source: PYQ-style

Directions (Q. 106–110): In the following passage, there are five blanks. Choose the most appropriate word for each blank.

Passage: Climate change is often described as a future threat, but for coastal communities it is a (106)______ reality. Rising seas have already (107)______ farmland in several delta regions, forcing families to migrate inland. Scientists warn that without rapid (108)______ of greenhouse-gas emissions, such displacement will (109)______. Adaptation funds, though growing, remain (110)______ compared to the scale of the problem.

106. Choose the word for blank 106.
(a) gifted (b) present (c) presented (d) prescient
Answer: (b)
Solution: The sentence contrasts "future threat" with a "present reality" — a reality existing now. "Gifted" and "presented" are traps playing on the word "present"; "prescient" means foreseeing, which does not fit.
Source: PYQ-style

107. Choose the word for blank 107.
(a) inaugurated (b) inundated (c) insulated (d) invigorated
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Inundated" means flooded, which matches "rising seas" and "farmland". The other options are positive or unrelated words chosen as sound-alike distractors.
Source: PYQ-style

108. Choose the word for blank 108. [Examiner's Trap]
(a) reduction (b) deduction (c) induction (d) seduction
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Reduction of emissions" is the correct collocation. This is a Sound-Right trap: "deduction" (subtraction in tax/logic), "induction" (initiation) and "seduction" look similar but are meaningless here. Read for meaning, not shape.
Source: PYQ-style

109. Choose the word for blank 109.
(a) alleviate (b) evaporate (c) escalate (d) stagnate
Answer: (c)
Solution: The warning is that displacement will worsen, so "escalate" (increase rapidly) fits. "Alleviate" means to relieve — the opposite — and is a classic antonym trap.
Source: PYQ-style

110. Choose the word for blank 110.
(a) inordinate (b) inadequate (c) inevitable (d) infallible
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Though growing, remain inadequate" — the concessive "though" demands a negative assessment, and "compared to the scale" confirms funds are insufficient. "Inordinate" (excessive) reverses the logic.
Source: PYQ-style

Directions (Q. 111–115): In the following passage, there are five blanks. Choose the most appropriate word for each blank.

Passage: The central bank's decision to hold interest rates steady was (111)______ by most economists, who had expected a cut. The governor argued that inflation, though (112)______, remained above the target band. Critics, however, (113)______ that high borrowing costs were stifling small businesses. The debate (114)______ a deeper question: should monetary policy prioritise price stability even at the (115)______ of growth?

111. Choose the word for blank 111.
(a) applauded (b) anticipated (c) criticised (d) ignored
Answer: (c)
Solution: The clause "who had expected a cut" shows economists were disappointed, so "criticised" fits. "Applauded" is the antonym trap; "anticipated" cannot take "by most economists" in this sense.
Source: PYQ-style

112. Choose the word for blank 112.
(a) rigid (b) rising (c) rampant (d) receding
Answer: (d)
Solution: "Though" signals concession: inflation is falling ("receding") yet still above target. "Rising" and "rampant" destroy the concessive logic of the sentence.
Source: PYQ-style

113. Choose the word for blank 113.
(a) consented (b) contended (c) contented (d) contained
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Contended" means argued or asserted, fitting a debate. "Consented" (agreed) and "contented" (satisfied) are look-alike traps with opposite or unrelated meanings.
Source: PYQ-style

114. Choose the word for blank 114.
(a) rouses (b) rises (c) raises (d) arises
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Raise a question" is the correct transitive collocation — the debate raises a question. "Rises" is intransitive and cannot take an object; this is a classic raise/rise grammar trap.
Source: PYQ-style

115. Choose the word for blank 115.
(a) expanse (b) expense (c) extent (d) expense of
Answer: (b)
Solution: The idiom is "at the expense of" (sacrificing something). "Expanse" (wide area) and "extent" (degree) are sound-alike traps; the blank already precedes "of", so the single word "expense" completes the idiom.
Source: PYQ-style

Directions (Q. 116–120): In the following passage, there are five blanks. Choose the most appropriate word for each blank.

Passage: India's digital payments revolution has been (116)______ by any standard. In less than a decade, QR codes have (117)______ cash in millions of small shops. Yet this success carries a hidden (118)______: those without smartphones or connectivity risk being (119)______ from the formal economy. Bridging this digital divide is not merely a technical challenge but a (120)______ imperative.

116. Choose the word for blank 116.
(a) remarkable (b) reluctant (c) redundant (d) remorseful
Answer: (a)
Solution: "By any standard" and the success described in the next lines demand a strongly positive word — "remarkable". The other options are negative words used as meaning traps.
Source: PYQ-style

117. Choose the word for blank 117.
(a) displaced (b) misplaced (c) replaced fully (d) displayed
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Displaced" means pushed aside, which fits QR codes taking over from cash. "Misplaced" (lost) and "displayed" (shown) are sound-alike traps; "replaced fully" is redundant and unidiomatic here.
Source: PYQ-style

118. Choose the word for blank 118.
(a) price (b) cost (c) expense (d) charge
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Hidden cost" is the standard collocation for an unseen negative consequence. "Price" and "charge" relate to payment transactions, not figurative consequences.
Source: PYQ-style

119. Choose the word for blank 119. [Examiner's Trap]
(a) secluded (b) included (c) concluded (d) excluded
Answer: (d)
Solution: The sentence describes a risk to those without smartphones — they risk being shut out, so "excluded" is correct. This is an antonym trap: "included" is the exact opposite, and "concluded"/"secluded" are look-alikes. Check the negative logic of "risk".
Source: PYQ-style

120. Choose the word for blank 120.
(a) mutual (b) morale (c) mortal (d) moral
Answer: (d)
Solution: "Moral imperative" is the fixed phrase meaning an ethical duty. "Morale" (spirit/confidence) is the classic spelling trap; "mortal" (deadly) and "mutual" (shared) do not fit.
Source: PYQ-style

Directions (Q. 121–125): In the following passage, there are five blanks. Choose the most appropriate word for each blank.

Passage: The historian's craft rests on a fragile (121)______ between evidence and imagination. Documents tell us what happened, but rarely why; to (122)______ motive, the historian must read between the lines. This is where (123)______ creeps in: two scholars examining the same archive may reach opposite conclusions. The honest response is not to abandon interpretation but to make one's assumptions (124)______. History, after all, is less a catalogue of facts than a continuing (125)______ with the past.

121. Choose the word for blank 121.
(a) balance (b) imbalance (c) alliance (d) reliance
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Balance between evidence and imagination" fits the idea of two elements held in fragile equilibrium. "Imbalance" reverses the meaning; "alliance" and "reliance" do not collocate with "between".
Source: PYQ-style

122. Choose the word for blank 122.
(a) impose (b) imply (c) infer (d) impart
Answer: (c)
Solution: The historian draws conclusions from evidence — "infer" (to deduce). "Imply" is what the documents do, not the reader; this infer/imply confusion is a favourite examiner trap.
Source: PYQ-style

123. Choose the word for blank 123.
(a) certainty (b) objectivity (c) subjectivity (d) apathy
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Two scholars may reach opposite conclusions" shows personal judgement entering — "subjectivity". "Objectivity" and "certainty" are antonym traps that contradict the example given.
Source: PYQ-style

124. Choose the word for blank 124.
(a) elusive (b) implicit (c) illicit (d) explicit
Answer: (d)
Solution: The honest response is to state assumptions openly — "explicit" (clearly expressed). "Implicit" (unstated) is the exact opposite and a classic trap; "illicit" (illegal) is a sound-alike distractor.
Source: PYQ-style

125. Choose the word for blank 125.
(a) dialogue (b) monologue (c) epilogue (d) catalogue
Answer: (a)
Solution: "A continuing dialogue with the past" is the well-known metaphor for history as an ongoing exchange. "Monologue" is one-sided, contradicting "with"; "catalogue" is already used earlier in the sentence as a rejected idea.
Source: PYQ-style
