Questions 51–60: Easy | Questions 61–75: Medium

51. The government has decided to ______ the old policy with a new one.
(a) replacing (b) replace (c) replaces (d) replaced
Answer: (b)
Solution: After "has decided to", the bare infinitive (base form) is required, so "replace" fits. The other forms break the infinitive structure.
Source: NCERT Class 10 English Grammar

52. She has been working in this bank ______ 2015.
(a) for (b) since (c) from (d) by
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Since" is used with a point of time (2015); "for" is used with a duration such as "for nine years". "From" and "by" do not fit the present perfect continuous here.
Source: NCERT Class 9 English Grammar

53. The children were afraid ______ the dark.
(a) of (b) from (c) with (d) by
Answer: (a)
Solution: The fixed preposition with "afraid" is "of" — afraid of something. The other prepositions do not collocate with "afraid".
Source: NCERT Class 8 English Grammar

54. Neither of the two boys ______ done his homework.
(a) have (b) has (c) are (d) were
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Neither of" takes a singular verb, so "has" is correct. "Have" is a common trap because the plural word "boys" sits just before the verb.
Source: PYQ-style

55. He is ______ honest man.
(a) a (b) the (c) no article (d) an
Answer: (d)
Solution: "Honest" begins with a vowel sound because the h is silent, so "an" is used. The choice of article depends on sound, not spelling.
Source: NCERT Class 8 English Grammar

56. The train ______ before we reached the station.
(a) had left (b) left (c) has left (d) was leaving
Answer: (a)
Solution: When two past actions occur, the earlier one takes the past perfect — the train "had left" before we "reached". Simple past "left" would wrongly suggest the actions were simultaneous.
Source: NCERT Class 10 English Grammar

57. You should abide ______ the rules of the institution.
(a) to (b) with (c) by (d) on
Answer: (c)
Solution: The fixed phrase is "abide by", meaning to follow or obey. No other preposition collocates with "abide" in this sense.
Source: PYQ-style

58. The teacher asked the students ______ noise.
(a) not to make (b) to not make (c) not make (d) do not make
Answer: (a)
Solution: The correct pattern is "asked + object + not to + verb". Splitting the infinitive as "to not make" is considered incorrect in standard exam English.
Source: NCERT Class 10 English Grammar

59. One of my friends ______ gone to Delhi.
(a) have (b) has (c) are (d) were
Answer: (b)
Solution: The subject is "One", which is singular, so "has" is correct. "Friends" is only part of the intervening phrase and does not control the verb.
Source: PYQ-style

60. He is junior ______ me by two years.
(a) than (b) to (c) from (d) with
Answer: (b)
Solution: Latin comparatives like junior, senior, superior and inferior take "to", not "than". "Than" is the classic trap here.
Source: NCERT Class 10 English Grammar

61. The committee ______ unanimously approved the new proposal.
(a) have (b) were (c) are (d) has
Answer: (d)
Solution: When a collective noun acts as a single body — here it approved unanimously — it takes a singular verb, so "has". A plural verb would fit only if the members were divided.
Source: PYQ-style

62. Hardly ______ the station when the train left.
(a) had I reached (b) I had reached (c) I reached (d) did I reach
Answer: (a)
Solution: After "Hardly", inversion is required: "Hardly had I reached... when...". The earlier action takes the past perfect with inverted order.
Source: PYQ-style

63. The principal, along with his staff, ______ present at the function.
(a) were (b) are (c) was (d) have been
Answer: (c)
Solution: With "along with", the verb agrees with the first subject, "the principal", which is singular. The intervening phrase "his staff" is the trap that tempts "were".
Source: PYQ-style

64. No sooner did she enter the room ______ the phone rang.
(a) than (b) then (c) when (d) that
Answer: (a)
Solution: "No sooner" is always paired with "than"; "hardly" and "scarcely" pair with "when". Mixing the two pairs is a favourite examiner trap.
Source: PYQ-style

65. The number of road accidents ______ increasing every year.
(a) have been (b) are (c) is (d) were
Answer: (c)
Solution: "The number of" takes a singular verb; "a number of" takes a plural verb. Here "is" agrees with "number", not "accidents".
Source: PYQ-style

66. He denied ______ the money from the drawer.
(a) to take (b) taking (c) take (d) taken
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Deny" is followed by a gerund (verb-ing), not an infinitive, so "taking" is correct. Verbs like deny, avoid, enjoy and suggest all take the gerund.
Source: NCERT Class 10 English Grammar

67. Unless you work hard, you ______ succeed.
(a) can (b) will (c) would (d) will not
Answer: (d)
Solution: "Unless" means "if not", so the sentence says that without hard work success will not come. "Will" alone would reverse the logic of the condition.
Source: PYQ-style

68. The scenery of Kashmir ______ very beautiful.
(a) is (b) are (c) were (d) have been
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Scenery" is an uncountable noun and always takes a singular verb. It has no plural form, so "are" is wrong despite the plural feel of the idea.
Source: PYQ-style

69. I look forward ______ you at the conference.
(a) meeting (b) to meet (c) for meeting (d) to meeting
Answer: (d)
Solution: In "look forward to", the word "to" is a preposition, not part of an infinitive, so it takes the gerund "meeting". This is a classic trap.
Source: PYQ-style

70. Each of the students ______ given a certificate.
(a) were (b) are (c) was (d) have been
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Each of" takes a singular verb, so "was". The plural noun "students" in the intervening phrase does not control the verb.
Source: NCERT Class 9 English Grammar

71. He is one of the best players who ______ ever played for the country.
(a) has (b) was (c) is (d) have
Answer: (d)
Solution: In "one of the + plural noun + who", the relative pronoun refers to the plural noun "players", so the verb is plural — "have". Linking "who" to "one" and choosing "has" is the trap.
Source: PYQ-style

72. The manager made the workers ______ overtime without extra pay.
(a) work (b) to work (c) working (d) worked
Answer: (a)
Solution: Causative "make" is followed by the bare infinitive: "made the workers work". "To work" would be correct only in the passive — "the workers were made to work".
Source: PYQ-style

73. Bread and butter ______ his daily breakfast.
(a) are (b) were (c) have been (d) is
Answer: (d)
Solution: When two nouns joined by "and" denote a single combined idea or dish, the verb is singular. Bread and butter is treated as one meal, so "is" is correct.
Source: NCERT Class 10 English Grammar

74. Spot the part of the sentence that contains an error: Part P — "One of my friend", Part Q — "has gone", Part R — "to Delhi", Part S — "yesterday evening."
(a) Part R (b) Part Q (c) Part P (d) No error
Answer: (c)
Solution: Part P is wrong: after "one of" a plural noun is required, so it should be "one of my friends". The verb "has" in Part Q is correctly singular because the subject is "One".
Source: PYQ-style

75. Choose the correctly spelt word.
(a) Occurence (b) Ocurrence (c) Occurrence (d) Occurrance
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Occurrence" doubles both the c and the r. The other spellings drop one of the double letters, which is a common spelling trap in exams.
Source: NCERT Class 10 English
