# PYQ Analysis

## Pattern Analysis

Cloze tests and fill-in-the-blank grammar questions appear regularly in SSC CGL, IBPS, and other exams. Here's how the examiner thinks.

### How the paper is set

- **Fact-based (15-second questions)**: One-word blanks testing prepositions, collocations, connectors, tense, and subject-verb agreement. If you know the rule, you answer instantly.
- **Inference-based (45-second questions)**: Tone, author's attitude, author's purpose, implied meaning. These need careful reading of the passage.

### Top 10 High-Yield Questions

**Q1.** The committee ______ divided on this issue.
(a) is (b) are (c) were (d) have
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Committee" is a collective noun treated as a single unit here, so it takes a singular verb. A 15-second question.
Source: PYQ-style

**Q2.** The number of students ______ increasing.
(a) is (b) are (c) were (d) have
Answer: (a)
Solution: "The number of + plural noun" takes a singular verb — the subject is "number," not "students." Don't confuse it with "A number of students are…", which is plural.
Source: PYQ-style

**Q3.** She is one of the few leaders who ______ respected by everyone.
(a) is (b) are (c) was (d) has
Answer: (b)
Solution: The relative pronoun "who" refers to "leaders" (plural), so the verb is plural: "one of the few leaders who are respected." A classic trap — the verb agrees with the plural noun after "one of the…", not with "one."
Source: PYQ-style

**Q4.** The data ______ clear.
(a) is (b) are (c) were (d) have
Answer: (a)
Solution: In modern usage, especially in exams, "data" is commonly treated as singular: "The data is clear." A 15-second question.
Source: PYQ-style

**Q5.** The news ______ good.
(a) is (b) are (c) were (d) have
Answer: (a)
Solution: "News" looks plural but is an uncountable noun and always takes a singular verb. Same family: physics, mathematics, economics (as subjects).
Source: PYQ-style

**Q6.** The police ______ arrived.
(a) has (b) is (c) was (d) have
Answer: (d)
Solution: "Police" is a plural noun with no singular form in this sense; it always takes a plural verb: "The police have arrived." (For one person, say "a policeman has arrived.")
Source: PYQ-style

**Q7.** The furniture in the room ______ new.
(a) is (b) are (c) were (d) have
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Furniture" is uncountable and takes a singular verb. Same family: luggage, baggage, information, advice. A 15-second question.
Source: PYQ-style

**Q8.** Bread and butter ______ his daily breakfast.
(a) is (b) are (c) were (d) have
Answer: (a)
Solution: When two nouns joined by "and" denote a single combined idea or dish ("bread and butter" as one meal), the verb is singular.
Source: PYQ-style

**Q9.** Each of the boys ______ given a prize.
(a) was (b) were (c) have (d) are
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Each of + plural noun" takes a singular verb — the true subject is "each." The intervening phrase "of the boys" is a trap.
Source: PYQ-style

**Q10.** Neither of the two answers ______ correct.
(a) are (b) were (c) is (d) have
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Neither of" takes a singular verb in formal exam usage: "Neither of the two answers is correct."
Source: PYQ-style

### Trap Types the Examiner Uses

1. **Intervening phrase trap**: A plural noun sits between the subject and the verb ("Each of the boys…", "The number of students…") to pull you toward the wrong verb.
2. **Looks-plural-but-singular**: news, physics, furniture, information, data.
3. **Looks-singular-but-plural**: police, cattle, people, poultry.
4. **Collective noun confusion**: committee, team, jury — singular when acting as one unit, plural when members act individually.
5. **"One of the + plural noun + who/that"**: the verb after the relative pronoun is plural ("one of the few leaders who are…").
6. **Near-correct options**: Options like "is/are/were/have" differ by only one feature — read the subject twice before marking.

### The 15-Second vs 45-Second Rule

- If a blank tests a fixed rule (agreement, preposition, collocation), answer in about 15 seconds and move on — don't re-read.
- If a question asks about tone, purpose, or implied meaning, budget about 45 seconds and re-read the relevant lines of the passage.
- Never spend 45 seconds on a rule-based blank; if the rule doesn't come to mind, mark it and return later.
