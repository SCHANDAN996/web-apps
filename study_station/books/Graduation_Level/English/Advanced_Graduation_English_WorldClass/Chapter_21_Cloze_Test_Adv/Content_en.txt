# Advanced Cloze Test — Content

## 🎯 The Hook

Imagine a paragraph with five holes punched into it — and your job is to fill each hole with the *one* word that fits grammar, meaning, and tone simultaneously. That is the Cloze Test: the single question type that tests your **grammar, vocabulary, collocation, and reading sense all at once**. In SSC CGL, IBPS PO, and State PCS prelims, a cloze passage of 5–10 blanks can be finished in 4–5 minutes — if you have a method. Without a method, it eats 10 minutes and still bleeds marks. This chapter gives you the method.

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this chapter you will be able to:

1. Read a cloze passage **twice with two different purposes** (gist first, grammar second).
2. Classify every blank into one of **5 blank types** and apply the matching strategy.
3. Eliminate wrong options using **collocation, connector logic, and tone consistency**.
4. Decide in seconds whether a blank is a 15-second (fact/grammar) or 45-second (inference/tone) question.
5. Avoid the examiner's three favourite traps.

---

## 1. What Exactly Is a Cloze Test?

A cloze test is a passage (usually 100–200 words) from which words have been deliberately removed. Each blank carries 4 options. The word "cloze" comes from **"closure"** — the human brain's tendency to complete an incomplete pattern. The examiner is literally testing how well your brain closes gaps in English.

At the advanced level (SSC CGL Tier-II, IBPS PO mains, PCS), the blanks are rarely single grammar points. They are **layered**: the correct word must satisfy grammar *and* fit the logic of the sentence *and* match the tone of the whole passage.

> **Key insight:** A cloze test is not five separate questions. It is **one passage wearing five masks**. Read it as one story, not five fragments.

---

## 2. The Two-Pass Method (Your Core Strategy)

**Pass 1 — The Gist Pass (30–40 seconds, no options):**
Read the entire passage *without looking at the options*. Ask yourself three questions:
- What is the **topic**? (e.g., inflation, AI, a historical event)
- What is the **tone**? (critical, optimistic, neutral/analytical, cautionary)
- What is the **direction**? (Is the author building an argument, narrating, or comparing?)

**Pass 2 — The Fill Pass:**
Now go blank by blank with options. For each blank, first decide its **type** (see Section 3), then apply the matching tool.

Why two passes? Because roughly half the blanks in an advanced cloze can only be solved using **context from other sentences** — sometimes from a sentence *after* the blank. If you fill blanks in isolation, you walk straight into the examiner's trap.

## 3. The Five Blank Types

Every cloze blank you will ever see belongs to one of these five types. Identify the type first — the strategy follows automatically.

| # | Blank Type | What It Tests | Your Tool |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Grammar Blank** | Tense, subject–verb agreement, article, preposition | Grammar rules |
| 2 | **Vocabulary Blank** | Meaning of the word in context | Read the sentence's logic; eliminate by meaning |
| 3 | **Collocation Blank** | Natural word partnership (*make a decision*, not *do a decision*) | Fixed phrases bank |
| 4 | **Connector Blank** | Logical link: contrast/addition/cause/result | Map the logic between the two clauses |
| 5 | **Tone/Inference Blank** | Author's attitude; word must match passage mood | Gist Pass tone map |

**Example of each (mini-passage):**

> "The new policy, (1)____ was announced last week, aims to reduce emissions. (2)____, critics argue that it lacks enforcement mechanisms. The government has (3)____ a committee to monitor progress — a step that many consider (4)____, given the scale of the crisis. Unless funding is increased, the policy may remain a (5)____ promise."

- (1) *which* — Grammar blank (relative pronoun for non-defining clause).
- (2) *However* — Connector blank (contrast with previous sentence).
- (3) *set up* — Collocation blank (*set up a committee*).
- (4) *inadequate* — Tone blank (critics' negative view).
- (5) *hollow* — Vocabulary blank (*hollow promise* = empty promise; also a collocation).

## 4. Connector Logic — The Highest-Yield Skill

Connector blanks appear in almost every advanced cloze. Master this table and you secure those marks permanently:

| Logic | Connectors | Signal in the sentence |
|---|---|---|
| **Contrast** | however, yet, nevertheless, although, whereas, despite | Two clauses pull in opposite directions |
| **Addition** | moreover, furthermore, also, besides, in addition | Second clause extends the same idea |
| **Cause** | because, since, as, owing to | One clause explains *why* |
| **Result** | therefore, thus, hence, consequently, so | One clause shows the *outcome* |
| **Condition** | if, unless, provided that, in case | One clause sets a requirement |
| **Time/Sequence** | subsequently, meanwhile, initially, eventually | Events in order |

**The Direction Test:** Cover the connector. Read the clause before the blank and the clause after it. Do they agree (→ addition/result) or fight (→ contrast)? This one question solves most connector blanks in under 15 seconds.

### 🧠 Active Recall #3

## 5. Collocations — The Silent Mark-Decider

Advanced cloze tests love collocations because rote grammar cannot save you here. Build a bank of high-frequency partnerships:

- **Verb + Noun:** *take* a risk, *make* an effort, *draw* a conclusion, *raise* a question, *meet* a demand, *exercise* caution, *render* services, *commit* a crime
- **Adjective + Noun:** *heavy* rain (not *strong* rain), *strong* tea (not *powerful* tea), *stiff* competition, *keen* interest, *grave* concern, *sweeping* changes
- **Preposition-bound:** *depend on*, *insist on*, *consist of*, *succeed in*, *abstain from*, *devoid of*, *conducive to*, *averse to*

**Mnemonic — "RATS Make Dogs Chase":** **R**aise a question, **T**ake a risk, **S**weeping changes, **M**ake an effort, **D**raw a conclusion, **C**ommit a crime. Six of the most-tested collocations in one silly sentence.

## 6. Tone Consistency — The Advanced Filter

In mains-level cloze tests, two options are often *grammatically* correct. The tie-breaker is **tone**. If the passage is critical of a policy, a blank describing the policy cannot take a positive word.

**Tone ladder (learn to place the author's voice):**
Neutral/Informative → Analytical → Cautionary → Critical → Alarmist
Neutral/Informative → Optimistic → Laudatory

**Quick tone markers:** words like *allegedly, so-called, merely* signal criticism; *remarkably, commendably* signal praise; *reportedly, according to* signal neutrality.

## ⚠️ Examiner's Trap Box

**Trap 1 — The Sound-Right Trap:** An option that *sounds* natural in spoken Hindi-English but is wrong in standard English. E.g., "discuss about the issue" — *discuss* never takes *about*. Similarly: *comprise of* (wrong), *order for* (wrong), *revert back* (redundant).

**Trap 2 — The Local-Fit Trap:** A word that fits its own sentence perfectly but contradicts the passage's overall tone or the next sentence. This is why Pass 1 (gist) is non-negotiable. The examiner places the contradicting clue *after* the blank on purpose.

**Trap 3 — The Twin-Option Trap:** Two options with nearly identical meaning (e.g., *although* / *though*; *because* / *since*). When twins appear, the differentiator is usually **grammar/position**, not meaning — e.g., *although* can begin a sentence more naturally; *despite* must be followed by a noun/gerund, never a full clause.

## 🔁 Linguistic Bridge — Hindi से English

| हिंदी में हम सोचते हैं | गलत English | सही English |
|---|---|---|
| "वह मुझसे बड़ा है" | He is more elder than me. | He is elder to me. (*elder* takes *to*, not *than*) |
| "मैंने उससे शादी की" | I married with her. | I married her. (no preposition after *marry*) |
| "उसने मेरा साथ दिया" | He gave me company/support... (loose) | He stood by me. / He supported me. |
| "मुझे ठंड लग रही है" | I am feeling cold air. | I am feeling cold. |
| "उसने मुझे सुझाव दिया" | He gave me an advice. | He gave me a suggestion. / He gave me advice. (*advice* is uncountable — never *an advice*) |
| "यह मेरे अनुकूल नहीं है" | It is not conducive to me. | It does not suit me. / It is not suitable for me. (*conducive to* is used for conditions favouring a result, not for a person) |

In cloze tests, these Hindi-driven instincts are exactly what the Sound-Right Trap exploits. When an option "feels" right, double-check it against standard usage.

## 📋 Summary Table

| Element | Rule of Thumb |
|---|---|
| Reading | Two passes: gist (tone/topic) → fill (blank by blank) |
| Blank types | Grammar, Vocabulary, Collocation, Connector, Tone — identify first |
| Connectors | Direction Test: do the clauses agree or fight? |
| Collocation | Fixed partnerships; cannot be derived from grammar |
| Tone | When two options are grammatical, tone decides |
| Time | Grammar/connector blanks ≈ 15 sec; tone/inference blanks ≈ 45 sec |
| Traps | Sound-Right, Local-Fit, Twin-Option |
