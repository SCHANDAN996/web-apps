1. The idiom "to bite the dust" means:
(a) to fail or be defeated (b) to eat quickly (c) to clean the floor (d) to work very hard
Answer: (a)
Solution: "To bite the dust" means to fail, be defeated, or come to an end. The literal image of eating dust misleads many students into choosing option (b).
Source: PYQ-style

2. "A blessing in disguise" refers to:
(a) a hidden enemy (b) a religious ceremony (c) a costly gift (d) a good thing that seemed bad at first
Answer: (d)
Solution: A blessing in disguise is something that appears unfortunate but turns out to be beneficial. Option (a) confuses "disguise" with deception by a person.
Source: PYQ-style

3. To "let the cat out of the bag" means:
(a) to free a pet (b) to reveal a secret (c) to start a quarrel (d) to waste money
Answer: (b)
Solution: The idiom means to disclose a secret, usually by mistake. The literal animal image in option (a) is a common trap.

4. "Once in a blue moon" means:
(a) very rarely (b) every night (c) during a festival (d) once a month

Solution: "To throw cold water on" something means to discourage it or dampen enthusiasm for it. Option (a) is the opposite and traps those guessing from "water" as life-giving.
