# 🧒 Feynman Technique — Idioms & Phrases

## The Hardest Concept, Explained to a 12-Year-Old

**The hardest thing about idioms is this: the words lie to you.**

Imagine your friend says, *"I spilled the beans."* You look at the floor. There are no beans. No tin, no mess, nothing. Your friend is not talking about food at all — they mean they **revealed a secret**.

That is an idiom: a group of words whose real meaning is **different from the dictionary meaning of each word**. You cannot solve an idiom like a maths problem by adding up the words. "Kick the bucket" has nothing to do with kicking or buckets — it means *to die*.

### The Daily-Life Example

Think of emojis. When someone sends you 😂, you don't think "a yellow face with tears." You instantly know it means *"that's hilarious."* The symbol means more than its parts.

Idioms are the **emojis of language**. Native speakers pack a whole feeling or situation into 3–4 words:

- *"It's raining cats and dogs"* = raining very heavily (no animals falling from the sky!)
- *"Break a leg"* = good luck (said before a performance — saying "good luck" directly is considered unlucky in theatre)
- *"A piece of cake"* = something very easy

### Why Exams Love to Trap You

The examiner knows you will try to translate word-by-word. So the wrong options are always the **literal meanings**. If the idiom is *"to burn the midnight oil"*, one option will say something about lamps or burning — and that option is **always wrong**. The answer is *"to study/work late into the night."*

### The Unforgettable Trick: 🎬 The Movie Trick

Never memorise an idiom as "words = meaning." Instead, **make a 3-second movie in your head**:

- *"To let the cat out of the bag"* → Picture a shopkeeper in old times secretly swapping a cat for a pig in a sack… and the cat jumps out. Secret exposed! Now you'll never forget it means **to reveal a secret**.
- *"To sit on the fence"* → Picture yourself literally sitting on a fence between two fields, unable to jump to either side = **unable to decide / staying neutral**.

One silly picture beats ten readings. The sillier the movie, the stronger the memory.

### The Second Trick: 🔍 The Literal-Option Eliminator

In the exam, when you see an idiom question:
1. Read the idiom.
2. Find the option that translates it **word-for-word** (beans, buckets, cats, oil…).
3. **Cross it out immediately** — it's the trap.
4. Choose from the remaining options using your mental movie.
