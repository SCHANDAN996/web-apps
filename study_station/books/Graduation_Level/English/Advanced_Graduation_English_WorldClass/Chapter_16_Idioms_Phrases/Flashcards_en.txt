Card 1
Front: What does "to bite the dust" mean?
Back: To fail, be defeated, or come to an end. Example: "His plan to cheat in the exam bit the dust when the invigilator caught him."

Card 2
Front: What does "a blessing in disguise" mean?
Back: Something that seems bad at first but turns out to be good. Example: "Losing that job was a blessing in disguise — he found a better one."

Card 3
Front: What does "to burn the midnight oil" mean?
Back: To study or work late into the night. Example: "She burned the midnight oil before her SSC CGL exam."

Card 4
Front: What does "once in a blue moon" mean?
Back: Very rarely. Example: "He visits his village once in a blue moon."

Card 5
Front: What does "to let the cat out of the bag" mean?
Back: To reveal a secret, usually by mistake. Example: "Ravi let the cat out of the bag about the surprise party."

Card 6
Front: MNEMONIC — How do you remember "to hit the nail on the head"?
Back: Picture a hammer striking a nail exactly on its head — it means to say or do exactly the right thing. "Her analysis of the passage hit the nail on the head."

Card 7
Front: What does "to sit on the fence" mean?
Back: To avoid taking sides in a dispute; to remain neutral. Example: "You cannot sit on the fence forever — choose a side."

Card 8
Front: EXAMINER'S TRAP — Does "to eat humble pie" mean to eat a tasty dessert?
Back: No! It means to admit you were wrong and apologise humbly. The literal meaning is the trap. Example: "After his prediction failed, he had to eat humble pie."

Card 9
Front: What does "to add insult to injury" mean?
Back: To make a bad situation worse. Example: "He lost his wallet, and to add insult to injury, he missed the last train."

Card 10
Front: What does "a red herring" mean?
Back: Something that misleads or distracts from the real issue. Example: "The extra detail in the passage was a red herring — the answer lay elsewhere."

Card 11
Front: What does "to beat about/around the bush" mean?
Back: To talk indirectly and avoid the main point. Example: "Stop beating about the bush and tell me your marks."

Card 12
Front: What does "to break the ice" mean?
Back: To start a conversation and ease initial awkwardness. Example: "The teacher broke the ice with a joke on the first day."

Card 13
Front: MNEMONIC — How do you remember "to spill the beans"?
Back: Imagine a jar of secret beans spilling open — secrets tumble out! It means to reveal secret information. Synonym partner: "let the cat out of the bag."

Card 14
Front: What does "to be on cloud nine" mean?
Back: To be extremely happy. Example: "She was on cloud nine after clearing the IBPS PO exam."

Card 15
Front: EXAMINER'S TRAP — Does "to pull someone's leg" mean to physically drag them?
Back: No! It means to tease or joke with someone. Literal readings of idioms are the most common trap in SSC and Bank exams.

Card 16
Front: What does "to cost an arm and a leg" mean?
Back: To be very expensive. Example: "The new phone costs an arm and a leg."

Card 17
Front: What does "to kill two birds with one stone" mean?
Back: To achieve two goals with a single action. Example: "By reading the newspaper daily, he killed two birds with one stone — current affairs and vocabulary."

Card 18
Front: What does "the ball is in your court" mean?
Back: It is your turn to take action or make a decision. Example: "I have given you all the notes; now the ball is in your court."

Card 19
Front: What does "to take something with a grain of salt" mean?
Back: To not fully believe something; to be sceptical. Example: "Take rumours about exam dates with a grain of salt until the official notice arrives."

Card 20
Front: What does "to face the music" mean?
Back: To accept the unpleasant consequences of one's actions. Example: "He skipped classes all year; now he must face the music in the results."
