51. The idiom "to bite the dust" means:
(a) to suffer defeat or failure (b) to eat in a hurry (c) to clean the floor (d) to work very hard
Answer: (a)
Solution: "To bite the dust" means to be defeated, fail, or come to an end. It has nothing to do with literally eating dust.
Source: PYQ-style

52. "A blessing in disguise" refers to:
(a) a hidden curse (b) a religious ceremony (c) a good thing that seemed bad at first (d) a person in costume
Answer: (c)
Solution: The idiom describes something that appears unfortunate but turns out to be beneficial. "Disguise" means a hidden appearance.
Source: PYQ-style

53. "To let the cat out of the bag" means:
(a) to free a pet (b) to create chaos (c) to reveal a secret (d) to buy something expensive
Answer: (c)
Solution: The idiom means to disclose a secret, usually by mistake. The image of a cat escaping a bag suggests something hidden becoming known.
Source: PYQ-style

54. "Once in a blue moon" means:
(a) every night (b) very rarely (c) during a festival (d) in difficult times
Answer: (b)
Solution: A blue moon is a rare astronomical event, so the idiom means something that happens very infrequently.
Source: PYQ-style

55. "To burn the midnight oil" means:
(a) to work or study late into the night (b) to celebrate all night (c) to set something on fire (d) to waste electricity
Answer: (a)
Solution: The idiom comes from the days of oil lamps; working late meant burning lamp oil past midnight. It means to study or work late at night.
Source: PYQ-style

56. "At the drop of a hat" means:
(a) at a formal event (b) after long planning (c) with great difficulty (d) instantly, without hesitation
Answer: (d)
Solution: The idiom means immediately and without any delay or persuasion, as if a mere signal (a hat dropping) is enough to act.
Source: PYQ-style

57. "To hit the nail on the head" means:
(a) to say exactly the right thing (b) to do carpentry work (c) to hurt someone (d) to miss the point
Answer: (a)
Solution: Striking a nail squarely on its head is precise; the idiom means to describe a situation or answer a question with perfect accuracy.
Source: PYQ-style

58. "A piece of cake" means:
(a) a dessert (b) a difficult task (c) a small share (d) something very easy
Answer: (d)
Solution: The idiom describes a task that is very easy to do. It is one of the most frequently asked idioms in SSC and Banking exams.
Source: PYQ-style

59. "To sit on the fence" means:
(a) to relax outdoors (b) to guard property (c) to avoid taking sides in a dispute (d) to take a firm decision
Answer: (c)
Solution: A person sitting on a fence belongs to neither side; the idiom means to remain neutral or undecided between two options.
Source: PYQ-style

60. "To break the ice" means:
(a) to destroy something (b) to end a friendship (c) to cool a drink (d) to start a conversation and ease tension
Answer: (d)
Solution: The idiom means to initiate social interaction and make people feel comfortable, especially among strangers.
Source: PYQ-style

61. He was dismissed from service because he was caught ________ with office funds.
(a) cooking the books (b) reading between the lines (c) wearing his heart on his sleeve (d) beating about the bush
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Cooking the books" means falsifying financial records, which fits the context of misuse of office funds. The other idioms are unrelated to fraud.
Source: PYQ-style

62. The manager told the team to stop ________ and come straight to the point.
(a) hitting the sack (b) beating about the bush (c) cutting corners (d) pulling their legs
Answer: (b)
Solution: "To beat about the bush" means to talk indirectly and avoid the main topic, which contrasts with "come straight to the point" in the sentence.
Source: PYQ-style

63. "To be at sixes and sevens" means:
(a) to count carefully (b) to be perfectly organised (c) to gamble heavily (d) to be in a state of confusion
Answer: (d)
Solution: The idiom means to be in disorder or confusion. Examiner's trap: the numbers tempt students to pick an option related to counting or gambling.
Source: PYQ-style

64. Despite the initial losses, the company is now ________ and earning huge profits.
(a) in the red (b) in the same boat (c) on cloud nine (d) out of the woods
Answer: (d)
Solution: "Out of the woods" means free from danger or difficulty, matching the recovery from losses. "In the red" means in debt, the opposite of the context.
Source: PYQ-style

65. "To have an axe to grind" means:
(a) to have a selfish motive behind an action (b) to work as a carpenter (c) to sharpen one's skills (d) to seek revenge violently
Answer: (a)
Solution: The idiom means having a private, selfish reason for doing or saying something. It does not imply physical violence.
Source: PYQ-style

66. The new evidence ________ and the accused was released immediately.
(a) spilled the beans (b) turned the tables (c) broke the ice (d) missed the boat
Answer: (b)
Solution: "To turn the tables" means to reverse a situation completely, which fits the sudden release of the accused. "Spilled the beans" means revealed a secret, which does not fit here.

67. "A red herring" is:
(a) a rare fish dish (b) something that misleads or distracts from the real issue (c) a clue that solves a mystery (d) an urgent warning signal
Answer: (b)
Solution: A red herring is a deliberate distraction from the main question, common in debates and detective fiction. Examiner's trap: students often choose the "clue" option because of the detective association.

68. She knew the project would fail, but she decided to ________ and support the team anyway.
(a) face the music (b) go with the flow (c) blow her own trumpet (d) read the riot act

Solution: "To go with the flow" means to accept a situation and follow others without resistance, which matches supporting the team despite doubts.

69. "To take something with a grain of salt" means:
(a) to accept it completely (b) to preserve it carefully (c) to add flavour to it (d) to doubt its truth and not believe it fully
Answer: (d)
Solution: The idiom means to view a statement with scepticism. The literal image of salt misleads many students toward food-related options.
Source: PYQ-style

70. The two brothers were ________ over their father's property for years.
(a) on the cards (b) in high spirits (c) at loggerheads (d) under a cloud

71. "To wash one's dirty linen in public" means:
(a) to do household chores openly (b) to confess a crime in court (c) to discuss private quarrels or scandals in front of others (d) to clean one's reputation
Answer: (c)
Solution: The idiom means to expose private, embarrassing matters publicly. Examiner's trap: the literal "washing" image pushes students toward the household-chores option.

72. After the scandal, the minister's reputation was ________ and he had to resign.
(a) in full swing (b) in tatters (c) at stake (d) on the horizon
Answer: (b)
Solution: "In tatters" means completely ruined or destroyed, which suits a destroyed reputation. "At stake" means at risk, but the sentence says he had to resign, so the damage was already done.
Source: PYQ-style

73. "To pour oil on troubled waters" means:
(a) to calm a tense or angry situation (b) to pollute the environment (c) to waste resources (d) to worsen a conflict
Answer: (a)
Solution: The idiom means to soothe a quarrel or reduce tension, the opposite of worsening a conflict. The calming effect of oil on waves is the origin.
Source: PYQ-style

74. The soldiers fought ________ and refused to surrender even when surrounded.
(a) tooth and nail (b) hand in glove (c) bag and baggage (d) kith and kin
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Tooth and nail" means with great ferocity and full effort, fitting soldiers refusing to surrender. "Hand in glove" means in close cooperation, which does not fit.
Source: PYQ-style

75. "To rest on one's laurels" means:
(a) to work harder after success (b) to receive an award (c) to be satisfied with past achievements and stop trying (d) to retire gracefully
Answer: (c)
Solution: The idiom warns against complacency — relying on past glory instead of making fresh efforts. Option describing harder work is the exact opposite and a common trap.
