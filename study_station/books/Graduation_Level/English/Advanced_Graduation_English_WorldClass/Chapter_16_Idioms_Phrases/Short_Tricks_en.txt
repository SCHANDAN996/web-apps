# 🪄 Short Tricks + Skip Strategy — Idioms & Phrases

## Trick 1: The "Literal = Wrong" Alarm

> 📦 **BOX 1**
> **If an option translates the idiom word-for-word, it is almost always WRONG.**
> "Kick the bucket" — any option mentioning a bucket or kicking = trap. Idioms are *figurative*; examiners always plant a literal option to catch hurried readers.

---

## Trick 2: The Picture-Story Method

> 📦 **BOX 2**
> Convert every idiom into a silly mental movie. The weirder the image, the longer it stays.
> - **"To let the cat out of the bag"** → Imagine a cat jumping out of a bag at a surprise party, ruining the secret. = *to reveal a secret*.
> - **"To burn the midnight oil"** → Picture yourself studying at 2 AM with an oil lamp. = *to work/study late into the night*.

---

## Trick 3: Mnemonic — "APPLE" for the 5 Most-Asked Idioms

> 📦 **BOX 3**
> **A-P-P-L-E** covers five idioms that appear again and again in SSC/Bank exams:
> - **A** — **A** red herring = a misleading distraction
> - **P** — **P**ull up one's socks = to make a greater effort
> - **P** — **P**our oil on troubled waters = to calm a dispute
> - **L** — **L**ion's share = the largest part
> - **E** — **E**lephant in the room = an obvious problem nobody discusses

---

## Trick 4: Mnemonic — "BIRD" Family

> 📦 **BOX 4**
> Bird idioms fly into exams every year:
> - **B** — **B**irds of a feather (flock together) = similar people stay together
> - **I** — (kill two birds with one stone) — **I**diom of double achievement = two results from one action
> - **R** — ea**R**ly bird catches the worm = the one who acts first succeeds
> - **D** — a **D**ead duck = a person/plan certain to fail

---

## Trick 5: Mnemonic — "HAND" Body-Part Cluster

> 📦 **BOX 5**
> - **H** — **H**and in glove = in close (often secret) cooperation
> - **A** — **A**t one's fingertips = readily available
> - **N** — bite off more tha**N** you can chew = take on more than you can manage
> - **D** — wash your han**D**s of something = refuse further responsibility

---

## Trick 6: The Colour Code

> 📦 **BOX 6**
> Colours carry fixed meanings in English idioms:
> - **RED** = anger/danger/debt → *see red* (become angry), *in the red* (in debt)
> - **BLUE** = sadness/rarity → *once in a blue moon* (very rarely), *feeling blue* (sad)
> - **GREEN** = envy/permission → *green with envy*, *get the green signal*
> - **BLACK** = disgrace → *black sheep* (disgraceful member of a family/group)
> - **WHITE** = purity/peace → *white lie* (harmless lie), *show the white flag* (surrender)

---

## Trick 7: The "Opposite Pair" Hook

> 📦 **BOX 7**
> Learn idioms in opposite pairs — if the exam asks one, the other is often a distractor:
> - **By hook or by crook** (by any means) ↔ **Fair and square** (honestly)
> - **At sixes and sevens** (in confusion) ↔ **Apple-pie order** (perfectly arranged)
> - **A bed of roses** (easy situation) ↔ **A bed of thorns** (full of difficulties)

---

## Trick 8: The "One-Word Swap" Test

> 📦 **BOX 8**
> Every idiom has a one-word substitute. Memorise the swap, and elimination becomes instant:
> - **To hit the nail on the head** = *exact*
> - **To beat about the bush** = *evade*
> - **A blessing in disguise** = *hidden benefit*
> - **To face the music** = *accept punishment*
> If an option contains your swap-word, that's your answer.

---

## Trick 9: Mnemonic — "COLD" for Exam-Day Idioms

> 📦 **BOX 9**
> - **C** — **C**old shoulder = to deliberately ignore
> - **O** — **O**nce in a blue moon = very rarely
> - **L** — **L**ast straw = the final problem that causes collapse
> - **D** — **D**ead ringer = an exact duplicate

---

## Trick 10: The "Origin = Meaning" Link

> 📦 **BOX 10**
> Knowing the origin fixes the meaning permanently:
> - **To bite the dust** — from soldiers falling face-down in battle = *to fail or be defeated*.
> - **To break the ice** — ships breaking ice to open a route = *to start a conversation/reduce awkwardness*.
> - **To sit on the fence** — a person literally sitting on a fence belongs to neither side = *to remain neutral*.

---

## Trick 11: The "Sound-Alike" Warning

> 📦 **BOX 11**
> Examiners swap one word of a famous idiom to create a fake option:
> - Correct: **"to rain cats and dogs"** — Trap: "to rain cats and *dogs heavily*" is fine, but "to rain *dogs and cats*" or "cats and *wolves*" = wrong.
> - Correct: **"a bolt from the blue"** — Trap: "a bolt from the *sky*" = wrong.
> **Rule:** Idioms are frozen phrases — even one changed word makes them incorrect.

---

## Trick 12: The "Context Clue" 10-Second Scan

> 📦 **BOX 12**
> When the idiom is new to you, scan the sentence for emotion words:
> - Sentence has *"unfortunately / failed / ruined"* → idiom meaning is **negative**.
> - Sentence has *"luckily / succeeded / saved"* → idiom meaning is **positive**.
> This alone eliminates 2 of 4 options in most questions.

---

## Trick 13: Mnemonic — "FISH" for Water Idioms

> 📦 **BOX 13**
> - **F** — **F**ish out of water = uncomfortable in a new situation
> - **I** — (neither fish nor fowl) — **I**ndeterminate = belonging to no clear category
> - **S** — **S**wim against the tide = oppose the majority
> - **H** — **H**ook, line and sinker = completely (believed completely)

## ⏭️ Skip Strategy

> 📦 **SKIP TIP 1 — The Unknown-Idiom Rule**
> If you have never seen the idiom AND the context gives no emotion clue, **mark and skip** — do not gamble. Idiom questions are pure recall; blind guessing between 4 unknown meanings gives only 25% odds, and negative marking eats your score. Return only if time remains.

> 📦 **SKIP TIP 2 — The 20-Second Cap**
> An idiom question should take **maximum 20 seconds**: you either know it (10 sec) or you don't (skip). Never spend 60 seconds "reasoning out" an idiom — idioms are not logical; "kick the bucket" cannot be derived by thinking. Save that minute for comprehension or error-spotting, where reasoning actually works.

> 📦 **SKIP TIP 3 — Eliminate, Then Decide**
> Before skipping, always do one quick pass: (a) delete the literal-meaning option, (b) delete any option with a changed word from the original idiom. If 2 options remain and one "feels" familiar from your practice sets, attempt it — familiarity from revision is a valid signal. If nothing feels familiar, skip without guilt.
