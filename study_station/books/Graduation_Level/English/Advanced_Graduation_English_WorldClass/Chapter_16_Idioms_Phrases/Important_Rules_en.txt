# 📋 Important Rules & Tips — Idioms & Phrases

## (a) 12 High-Yield Rules

| # | Rule | Example | Examiner's Trap |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | An idiom's meaning is fixed — never interpret it word by word. | "Kick the bucket" = to die. | Trap: choosing the literal meaning of the idiom. |
| 2 | Learn the **core image** behind the idiom, not just the meaning. | "Break the ice" = to start a conversation (image of breaking ice to open a path). | Trap: two options both look plausible if you only memorised a vague meaning. |
| 3 | Group idioms by **theme** — memory, anger, courage, time, money, death. | "Burn the midnight oil" (hard work) belongs with "toil day and night". | Trap: mixing up two idioms from the same theme. |
| 4 | Check the **fixed wording** — one wrong word makes the idiom wrong. | "By hook or by crook" — not "by hook and crook". | Trap: a slightly altered idiom presented as correct. |
| 5 | Watch the **article** — many idioms carry a/the; changing it breaks the phrase. | "In a nutshell" is correct; "in nutshell" is not. | Trap: option with a missing or extra word. |
| 6 | Match the **register** — some idioms are informal, some neutral. | "Kick the bucket" is informal; "pass away" is neutral. | Trap: an informal idiom in a formal sentence. |
| 7 | Use the **subject correctly** — some idioms need a person, not a thing. | "He is a dark horse" (a person of hidden talent). | Trap: applying a person-idiom to an object. |
| 8 | Idioms of **emotion** are the most frequently tested group. | "To be on cloud nine" = extremely happy. | Trap: confusing similar emotion idioms. |
| 9 | Idioms of **time/frequency** are a close second. | "Once in a blue moon" = very rarely. | Trap: mixing up frequency shades. |
| 10 | Idioms of **body parts** form a large, high-yield group. | "To have a bee in one's bonnet" = an obsession. | Trap: body-part idiom given a literal meaning. |
| 11 | Idioms of **animals** are repeatedly asked. | "To let the cat out of the bag" = to reveal a secret. | Trap: choosing the literal animal meaning. |
| 12 | Idioms of **colours** are a steady source of questions. | "To see red" = to become very angry. | Trap: colour idiom taken literally. |

## (b) Time Management — 15/45-Second Rule

| Type | Time | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| **Fact-based recall** — you know the idiom or you don't. | 15 sec | Read the idiom, pick the meaning, move on. |
| **Fact-based recall** — you don't know the idiom. | 15 sec | Eliminate the literal meaning, guess the most plausible figurative one, move on. |
| **Application** — idiom in a sentence, choose the correct idiom. | 45 sec | Read the full sentence, identify the intended meaning, then match. |
| **Trap-type** — two options look equally right. | 45 sec | Check the register, check the exact wording, then decide. |

## (c) Morphology — Root, Affix, Example

| Root/Affix | Derived Words | Idiom/Phrase |
|---|---|---|
| **manu-** (hand) | manual, manuscript, manufacture | "To have the upper hand" = to have control. |
| **cord-** (heart) | cordial, cordiality, discord | "A change of heart" = a change of opinion. |
| **cap-** (take) | capture, capable, caption | "To take with a grain of salt" = to doubt. |
| **luc-** (light) | lucid, lucidly, translucent | "To see the light" = to understand. |
| **mort-** (death) | mortal, mortality, mortuary | "To be at death's door" = to be near death. |
| **aqua-** (water) | aquatic, aquarium, aqueduct | "To be in hot water" = to be in trouble. |
| **pyro-** (fire) | pyre, pyrotechnic, pyromania | "To add fuel to the fire" = to make things worse. |
| **phon-** (sound) | phonetic, phonograph, symphony | "To strike a false note" = to say something wrong. |

## 🔁 Linguistic Bridge

| In Hindi we say… | In English we say… |
|---|---|
| "मुझे नींद नहीं आ रही" (I am not getting sleep) | I can't sleep. |
| "उसने मुझे बहुत परेशान किया" (He troubled me a lot) | He got on my nerves. |
| "यह किताब मेरे लिए बहुत मुश्किल है" (This book is very difficult for me) | This book is Greek to me. |
| Idiom-to-idiom: "मेरे सामने पहाड़ जैसा काम है" (a mountain of work before me) | I have my work cut out. |

## ⚠️ Examiner's Trap

- **Trap 1 — The Literal Trap:** Choosing the word-for-word meaning of the idiom.
- **Trap 2 — The Similar-Idiom Trap:** Two options with nearly the same meaning — check the register and the exact wording.
- **Trap 3 — The Over-thinking Trap:** Reading a simple idiom as a complex one. If you know the idiom, pick the direct meaning and move on.
