Card 1
Front: What is a precis?
Back: A concise summary of a passage that retains all essential ideas in roughly one-third of the original length, written in your own words.

Card 2
Front: What is the ideal length of a precis?
Back: About one-third of the original passage's word count, unless the question specifies otherwise.

Card 3
Front: Should a precis be written in first person or third person?
Back: Third person, using reported (indirect) speech — even if the original passage uses "I" or "we".

Card 4
Front: What are the three essential qualities of a good precis?
Back: Conciseness (brevity), Clarity (easy to understand), and Completeness (all main ideas retained).

Card 5
Front: What is the first step in writing a precis?
Back: Read the passage carefully (twice if needed) to grasp the central theme before underlining key points.

Card 6
Front: Mnemonic: What does "TIPS" stand for in precis writing?
Back: Title, Identify main points, Paraphrase, Shorten to one-third — the four core steps of precis writing.

Card 7
Front: Examiner's Trap: Can you add your own opinion or examples to a precis?
Back: No. A precis must contain only the author's ideas. Adding personal views, new examples, or outside information is a serious error.

Card 8
Front: Should you copy sentences directly from the passage into your precis?
Back: No. Use your own words (paraphrase). Lifting whole sentences shows lack of understanding and loses marks.

Card 9
Front: What should you do with examples, illustrations, and repetitions in the original passage?
Back: Omit them. Examples, figures of speech, quotations, and repeated ideas are dropped; only the core argument is kept.

Card 10
Front: Is a title necessary for a precis?
Back: Yes, usually. Give a short, suitable title (3–6 words) that captures the central theme of the passage.

Card 11
Front: Examiner's Trap: A precis is just a collection of shortened sentences from the passage — true or false?
Back: False. A precis must be a connected, coherent paragraph with logical flow, not chopped-up fragments of the original.

Card 12
Front: What tense and voice should generally be used in a precis?
Back: Past tense with indirect speech is the safe convention, though universal truths may stay in the present tense.

Card 13
Front: Mnemonic: What does "CORE" remind you to check in a finished precis?
Back: Complete (all main ideas), Own words, Reduced to one-third, Error-free grammar.

Card 14
Front: How many paragraphs should a precis normally have?
Back: Usually one paragraph, since a precis is short. Very long passages may allow two, but never fragment it like the original.

Card 15
Front: What is the difference between a precis and a paraphrase?
Back: A paraphrase restates the passage in different words at similar length; a precis compresses it to about one-third while keeping only essentials.

Card 16
Front: What should you do with direct quotations and dialogues in the passage?
Back: Convert them into indirect (reported) speech and compress them into a single statement of the idea.

Card 17
Front: Why should you count words in a precis exam question?
Back: To verify you are near the one-third limit. Writing too long (or too short) costs marks, so a quick word count is a final check.

Card 18
Front: What is a "rough draft" strategy in precis writing?
Back: First jot down the main points, draft the precis freely, then revise and compress it into the final neat version within the word limit.

Card 19
Front: Which details must never be cut from a precis?
Back: The central theme, the author's main arguments, and the conclusion — these form the skeleton that must survive every compression.

Card 20
Front: Common error: Is it acceptable to write a precis in bullet points?
Back: No. A precis must be written in continuous, connected prose — full sentences in paragraph form, never bullets or numbered lists.
