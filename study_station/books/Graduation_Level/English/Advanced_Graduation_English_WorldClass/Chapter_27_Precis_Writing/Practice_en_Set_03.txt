51. What is the primary purpose of a precis?
(a) To expand a passage with examples (b) To criticise the ideas in a passage (c) To translate a passage into simpler words (d) To condense a passage while retaining its essential meaning
Answer: (d)
Solution: Expanding or criticising a passage defeats the very idea of precis writing, which tempts many beginners. A precis compresses the original to about one-third of its length while keeping the core meaning intact, so the answer is "To condense a passage while retaining its essential meaning".
Source: NCERT Class 11 English (Writing Skills)

52. The ideal length of a precis is approximately what fraction of the original passage?
(a) One-half (b) One-fourth (c) Three-fourths (d) One-third
Answer: (d)
Solution: One-half is too long and one-fourth is usually too short to carry the full sense; these are common guesses. The standard convention taught for examinations is one-third of the original length, so the answer is "One-third".

53. Which of the following should NEVER appear in a precis?
(a) The main idea of the passage (b) A suitable title (c) The essential arguments of the author (d) The writer's own opinions and comments
Answer: (d)
Solution: The main idea and essential arguments must be retained, so those options mislead. A precis is an objective summary; the writer's personal opinions, additions, or criticisms have no place in it, so the answer is "The writer's own opinions and comments".

54. A precis should be written in which person?
(a) First person (b) Third person (c) Second person (d) Any person the writer prefers
Answer: (b)
Solution: First person feels natural because we summarise "what I read", which is the trap. Precis convention requires third-person, indirect narration even if the original uses "I" or "we", so the answer is "Third person".

55. Which of these is the correct first step in writing a precis?
(a) Writing the title immediately (b) Counting the words of the passage (c) Copying the first sentence of the passage (d) Reading the passage carefully to grasp its central theme
Answer: (d)
Solution: Counting words or drafting a title before understanding the passage is a mechanical trap. Everything in a precis flows from an accurate grasp of the central theme, which comes only from careful reading, so the answer is "Reading the passage carefully to grasp its central theme".

Solution: "To explain" sounds plausible because a precis clarifies, but that is the tempting wrong choice. "Precis" derives from the French/Latin root meaning "cut short" (compare "precise"), so the answer is "to cut short".

Solution: Omitting the conclusion or argument destroys the precis, which is why those options attract careless readers. Illustrations, examples, quotations and repetitions are exactly the material a precis drops, so the answer is "Illustrations, examples and repetitions".

62. Read the sentence from a passage: "The government, despite facing severe criticism from the opposition, the media, and even its own allies, firmly refused to withdraw the bill." Which is the best precis version?

Solution: Option (a) keeps the meaning but retains the list of critics, which is dispensable detail — the trap for those who fear cutting. 63. Which of the following best states the difference between a summary and a precis as used in competitive exams?
(a) A summary is always shorter than a precis (b) A precis follows a fixed proportion (about one-third) and strict objectivity, while a summary is more flexible (c) A summary must have a title, a precis must not (d) There is no difference at all
Answer: (b)
Solution: "No difference" is the sound-right trap; in casual usage the words overlap, but exam conventions distinguish them. A precis demands a fixed proportion, third person, a title and strict fidelity, whereas a summary is looser, so the answer is "A precis follows a fixed proportion (about one-third) and strict objectivity, while a summary is more flexible".

64. [Examiner's Trap] A passage argues: "Technology has undoubtedly improved communication, yet it has quietly eroded the depth of our relationships." A candidate's precis reads: "Technology has destroyed human relationships completely." What is the chief fault?
(a) It is too short (b) It uses the wrong tense (c) It distorts the author's meaning by exaggerating the claim (d) It lacks a title
Answer: (c)
Solution: Length and tense are not the issue here, though examiners plant them as distractors. "Eroded the depth" is a qualified claim; "destroyed completely" is an absolute distortion — the classic overstatement trap, so the answer is "It distorts the author's meaning by exaggerating the claim".

65. While condensing, the sentence "He worked hard day and night, toiling ceaselessly without rest" is best reduced to:
(a) He worked ceaselessly. (b) He worked hard day and night and toiled ceaselessly without rest. (c) He rested neither day nor night. (d) He toiled without working hard.

Solution: Option (b) merely joins the redundancies with "and" — it looks edited but saves nothing, which is the trap. The original repeats one idea three ways; "He worked ceaselessly" captures it in three words, so the answer is "He worked ceaselessly".

71. [Examiner's Trap] A passage says: "Few reforms succeed without public support." A precis states: "All reforms succeed with public support." The error is:
(a) Wrong tense usage (b) Exceeding the word limit (c) Reversing a qualified negative statement into an absolute positive one (d) Use of first person

Solution: Tense and word limit are planted distractors; the sentence is short and in acceptable tense. 72. Which order of steps is most efficient for writing a precis in an exam?
