1. What is a precis?
(a) A detailed expansion of a passage (b) A concise summary of a passage in one's own words (c) A word-for-word copy of a passage (d) A critical review of a passage
Answer: (b)
Solution: A precis is a brief, clear restatement of the main ideas of a passage, written in the writer's own words. It is not an expansion, a copy, or a review, so the answer is a concise summary of a passage in one's own words.
Source: NCERT Class 11 English (Writing Skills)

2. The word "precis" comes from which language?
(a) Greek (b) Latin (c) French (d) German
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Precis" is borrowed from French, where it means "cut short" or "condensed"; the French word itself traces back to Latin "praecisus". Since the immediate source is French, the answer is French.
Source: Standard English usage references

3. Ideally, a precis should be about what fraction of the original passage?
(a) One-half (b) One-third (c) Two-thirds (d) Three-fourths
Answer: (b)
Solution: The standard convention is that a precis should be roughly one-third the length of the original passage. Half or more would be too long to count as condensation, so the answer is one-third.
Source: NCERT Class 11 English (Writing Skills)

4. Which of the following must a good precis always have?
(a) Quotations from the passage (b) The writer's personal opinion (c) A suitable title (d) Examples copied from the passage
Answer: (c)
Solution: A precis must carry a short, suitable title that captures the central idea. Personal opinions and copied quotations or examples are not allowed, so the answer is a suitable title.
Source: NCERT Class 11 English (Writing Skills)

5. In a precis, the writer should use:
(a) Direct speech (b) Only bullet points (c) The exact sentences of the passage (d) His or her own words in indirect speech
Answer: (d)
Solution: A precis is written in the candidate's own words, and direct speech is converted into indirect (reported) speech. Copying sentences defeats the purpose, so the answer is his or her own words in indirect speech.
Source: NCERT Class 11 English (Writing Skills)

6. Which of these should be avoided in a precis?
(a) The main idea of the passage (b) The writer's own comments and criticisms (c) A logical order of ideas (d) Simple, clear language
Answer: (b)
Solution: A precis must reflect only the original author's ideas; adding personal comments or criticism is a classic error. Main ideas, logical order, and clear language are all required, so the answer is the writer's own comments and criticisms.
Source: NCERT Class 11 English (Writing Skills)

7. The first step in writing a precis is to:
(a) Read the passage carefully to grasp the main idea (b) Count the words (c) Write the title (d) Start writing the final draft
Answer: (a)
Solution: Careful reading to understand the central theme always comes first; title, drafting, and word count follow later. So the answer is read the passage carefully to grasp the main idea.
Source: NCERT Class 11 English (Writing Skills)

8. Which person and voice is normally preferred in a precis?
(a) First person, active voice (b) Third person, indirect speech (c) Second person, passive voice (d) First person, direct speech
Answer: (b)
Solution: Even if the passage uses "I" or "we", the precis is written in the third person with indirect speech. First or second person makes it personal, which is wrong, so the answer is third person, indirect speech.
Source: NCERT Class 11 English (Writing Skills)

9. Which of the following can usually be dropped while making a precis?
(a) Illustrations, examples, and repetitions (b) The central theme (c) The author's main argument (d) The conclusion of the passage
Answer: (a)
Solution: Examples, illustrations, quotations, and repetitions are supporting details and can be removed. The theme, argument, and conclusion must be retained, so the answer is illustrations, examples, and repetitions.

10. A precis should be written in:
(a) One connected paragraph of continuous prose (b) Note form with abbreviations (c) A dialogue format (d) Verse form
Answer: (a)
Solution: A precis is continuous, coherent prose — not notes, dialogue, or verse. So the answer is one connected paragraph of continuous prose.

11. What is the main purpose of precis writing in competitive exams?
(a) To test handwriting speed (b) To test memory of the passage (c) To test comprehension and the ability to express ideas concisely (d) To test vocabulary of difficult words
Answer: (c)
Solution: Precis writing checks whether a candidate has understood the passage and can express its essence briefly and clearly. It is not a memory or handwriting test, so the answer is to test comprehension and the ability to express ideas concisely.

12. Which of these is a quality of a good precis?
(a) It contains all the examples of the original (b) It is clear, brief, and complete in itself (c) It is longer than the original (d) It uses ornamental language
Answer: (b)
Solution: The three pillars of a good precis are clarity, brevity, and completeness — it must make sense on its own. Length, examples, and ornamentation are faults, so the answer is it is clear, brief, and complete in itself.

13. The title of a precis should be:
(a) A long sentence summarising everything (b) Written as a question always (c) Copied from the first line of the passage (d) Short and suggestive of the central idea
Answer: (d)
Solution: A title is a few words that point to the central theme; it need not be a full sentence or a copied line. So the answer is short and suggestive of the central idea.
Source: NCERT Class 11 English (Writing Skills)

14. While writing a precis, figurative language and metaphors should be:
(a) Replaced by plain, direct language (b) Retained exactly as they are (c) Expanded with more detail (d) Converted into quotations
Answer: (a)
Solution: Precis demands plain, direct expression; metaphors and figures of speech are translated into simple literal language. So the answer is replaced by plain, direct language.

15. If a passage contains a dialogue, the precis writer should:
(a) Convert it into indirect narrative form (b) Keep the dialogue as it is (c) Omit the idea of the dialogue entirely (d) Write it in brackets
Answer: (a)
Solution: Dialogues are reported in indirect narrative form, preserving the idea but not the exact words. Omitting the idea loses content, so the answer is convert it into indirect narrative form.
Source: NCERT Class 11 English (Writing Skills)

16. Which of the following best describes the tone of a precis?
(a) Emotional and personal (b) Humorous (c) Objective and impersonal (d) Critical of the author
Answer: (c)
Solution: A precis is an objective, impersonal restatement; emotion, humour, or criticism distort the original. So the answer is objective and impersonal.

17. How many times should one ideally read the passage before drafting a precis?
(a) Never — start writing immediately (b) Exactly ten times (c) At least twice — once for gist, once for details (d) Only the first paragraph
Answer: (c)
Solution: The standard method is a first reading for the general gist and a second reading to note key points. So the answer is at least twice — once for gist, once for details.

18. Which of these is NOT a step in precis writing?
(a) Reading the passage carefully (b) Adding new ideas to improve the passage (c) Underlining or noting key points (d) Writing a rough draft and revising it
Answer: (b)
Solution: Adding new ideas is strictly forbidden — the precis must contain only what the original says. Reading, noting key points, and revising a draft are genuine steps, so the answer is adding new ideas to improve the passage.

19. The length of the title of a precis is usually:
(a) One full paragraph (b) The whole first sentence (c) Exactly fifteen words (d) A word or a short phrase
Answer: (d)
Solution: A title is normally a single word or a brief phrase capturing the theme. So the answer is a word or a short phrase.

20. A precis differs from paraphrasing because a precis:
(a) Is longer than the original (b) Uses the author's exact words (c) Shortens the passage while paraphrasing restates it at similar length (d) Is written in verse
Answer: (c)
Solution: Paraphrasing restates the text in different words at roughly the same length; a precis compresses it to about one-third. So the answer is shortens the passage while paraphrasing restates it at similar length.
Source: NCERT Class 11 English (Writing Skills)

21. A passage of 300 words is given for precis. The precis should ideally be about:
(a) 100 words (b) 150 words (c) 300 words (d) 30 words
Answer: (a)
Solution: Applying the one-third rule, 300 ÷ 3 = 100 words. 150 words would be half — too long — and 30 words would lose essential content, so the answer is 100 words.
Source: NCERT Class 11 English (Writing Skills)

22. Read the sentence from a passage: "I believe that hard work, and hard work alone, is the key to success," said the leader. Which is the correct precis form?
(a) The leader said, "Hard work is the key to success." (b) Hard work, says the leader, and hard work alone! (c) I believe hard work is the key to success. (d) The leader believed that hard work alone was the key to success.
Answer: (d)
Solution: Examiner's trap: keeping the quotation marks or the first person "I" looks faithful but breaks the rule that a precis uses third-person indirect speech. The repetition "and hard work alone" is condensed but its meaning is kept, so the answer is the leader believed that hard work alone was the key to success.
Source: PYQ-style

23. A passage's main idea is: "Excessive use of mobile phones is harming the social skills of teenagers." Which is the best title?
(a) Mobile Phones (b) Why I Hate Mobile Phones (c) Teenagers and Technology: A Complete History of Mobile Phones in India (d) The Harmful Effects of Mobile Phones on Teenagers' Social Skills
Answer: (d)
Solution: Examiner's trap: "Mobile Phones" is too broad and "Why I Hate Mobile Phones" injects personal opinion — both common wrong choices. The title must be short yet capture the central idea exactly, so the answer is the harmful effects of mobile phones on teenagers' social skills.
Source: PYQ-style

24. While condensing, a precis writer finds two sentences in the passage expressing the same idea in different words. The writer should:
(a) Keep the idea once, in his or her own words (b) Keep both sentences to be safe (c) Delete the idea completely (d) Quote both sentences in inverted commas
Answer: (a)
Solution: Repetition is exactly what condensation removes; the idea is expressed once, concisely. Keeping both wastes words and deleting loses content, so the answer is keep the idea once, in his or her own words.
Source: NCERT Class 11 English (Writing Skills)

25. Which order of steps is correct for writing a precis?
(a) Write title → read passage → draft → count words (b) Draft → note key points → read passage → title (c) Count words → draft → read passage → revise (d) Read passage → note key points → draft → revise → give title
Answer: (d)
Solution: The logical sequence is: read carefully, note the key points, write a rough draft, revise and check length, and finally give a title. All other orders put writing before understanding, so the answer is read passage → note key points → draft → revise → give title.
Source: NCERT Class 11 English (Writing Skills)
