26. What is the primary purpose of a precis?
(a) To compress a passage while retaining its essential meaning (b) To expand a passage with examples (c) To criticise the author's viewpoint (d) To translate the passage into simpler words
Answer: (a)
Solution: A precis is a condensed restatement of a passage. The tempting option about simplifying words describes paraphrasing, not precis; a precis must shorten the text while keeping the core idea intact, so the answer is to compress a passage while retaining its essential meaning.
Source: NCERT Class 11 English (Writing Skills)

27. The ideal length of a precis is usually about what fraction of the original passage?
(a) One-third (b) One-tenth (c) One-half (d) Three-fourths
Answer: (a)
Solution: The standard convention taught for examinations is roughly one-third of the original length. One-half is too long to count as compression, and one-tenth would lose essential content, so the answer is one-third.
Source: Standard precis-writing convention (competitive exam pattern)

28. A precis should always be written in:
(a) Direct speech (b) The exact sentences of the passage (c) Bullet points (d) The writer's own words
Answer: (d)
Solution: Copying sentences from the passage defeats the purpose and is penalised; the tempting option of exact sentences shows no compression skill. A precis must be an original restatement, so the answer is the writer's own words.
Source: NCERT Class 11 English (Writing Skills)

29. Which of the following should be avoided in a precis?
(a) The main idea of the passage (b) Personal opinions of the precis writer (c) A suitable title (d) Logical order of ideas
Answer: (b)
Solution: A precis is an objective summary; the writer's own views, however relevant, distort the author's meaning. Title, main idea and logical order are all required, so the answer is personal opinions of the precis writer.
Source: Standard precis-writing rules

30. The title of a precis should be:
(a) As long as possible (b) Short and suggestive of the central theme (c) A full sentence describing every detail (d) Written in the form of a question always
Answer: (b)
Solution: A title captures the gist in a few words; a long descriptive sentence is a summary, not a title. Brevity with relevance is the rule, so the answer is short and suggestive of the central theme.
Source: Standard precis-writing rules

31. Which person and voice should a precis normally use?
(a) First person, direct speech (b) Second person, imperative (c) Third person, indirect (reported) speech (d) Any person the writer prefers
Answer: (c)
Solution: Even if the original uses "I" or "we", the precis reports the ideas objectively in third person and indirect speech. First person makes it look like the writer's own claim, so the answer is third person, indirect speech.
Source: Standard precis-writing rules

32. Which of these is the first step in writing a precis?
(a) Reading the passage carefully to grasp the main idea (b) Writing the final draft immediately (c) Counting the words of the passage (d) Choosing a decorative title
Answer: (a)
Solution: Word-counting and titling come later; without understanding the central idea, compression is impossible. Careful reading is the foundation step, so the answer is reading the passage carefully to grasp the main idea.
Source: NCERT Class 11 English (Writing Skills)

33. Examples, illustrations and repetitions in the original passage should be:
(a) Retained fully in the precis (b) Expanded with new examples (c) Omitted or reduced drastically in the precis (d) Converted into quotations
Answer: (c)
Solution: Examples merely support the main argument; keeping them makes the precis as long as the original. They are the first candidates for deletion, so the answer is omitted or reduced drastically in the precis.
Source: Standard precis-writing rules

34. The word "precis" comes from a word meaning:
(a) To enlarge (b) To cut short (c) To decorate (d) To argue
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Precis" derives from the French/Latin root meaning "cut short" (compare "precise"). It has nothing to do with enlargement or ornament, so the answer is to cut short.
Source: Etymology — French précis, Latin praecisus

35. A good precis must be:
(a) Coherent and readable as a complete whole (b) A collection of the longest sentences (c) A list of disconnected points (d) Written in note form
Answer: (a)
Solution: Note form and disconnected points are drafts, not a finished precis; examiners expect a smooth, connected paragraph. The precis must read like a miniature essay, so the answer is coherent and readable as a complete whole.
Source: Standard precis-writing rules

36. If a passage is 300 words long, the precis should ideally be about:
(a) 100 words (b) 150 words (c) 250 words (d) 30 words
Answer: (a)
Solution: Applying the one-third rule, 300 ÷ 3 = 100 words. 250 words shows almost no compression and 30 words loses substance, so the answer is 100 words.
Source: Standard precis-writing convention

37. Which of the following is a correct feature of precis writing?
(a) Adding statistical data not in the passage (b) Faithful representation of the author's ideas only (c) Using quotations from famous people (d) Inserting the writer's criticism
Answer: (b)
Solution: Anything not in the passage — new data, quotations, criticism — violates fidelity, the core virtue of a precis. Only the author's ideas may appear, so the answer is faithful representation of the author's ideas only.
Source: Standard precis-writing rules

38. Figurative language and ornamental phrases in the original should be:
(a) Preserved exactly for beauty (b) Expanded into longer images (c) Replaced by plain, direct language (d) Turned into the title
Answer: (c)
Solution: Ornament consumes words without adding meaning; a precis values clarity over beauty. Plain restatement is the rule, so the answer is replaced by plain, direct language.
Source: Standard precis-writing rules

39. How many paragraphs should a short precis of a single-theme passage normally have?
(a) As many as the original always (b) Five (c) Ten (d) One
Answer: (d)
Solution: A short single-theme precis is conventionally one compact paragraph; mechanically copying the original's paragraph count wastes words. Unity demands a single paragraph, so the answer is one.
Source: Standard precis-writing rules

40. Which skill is MOST directly tested by precis writing in exams?
(a) Comprehension and concise expression (b) Memorisation of facts (c) Speed of handwriting (d) Knowledge of grammar rules only
Answer: (a)
Solution: Precis writing tests whether the candidate has understood the passage and can express its gist economically; handwriting and rote memory are irrelevant. The twin skills are understanding plus brevity, so the answer is comprehension and concise expression.
Source: PYQ-style (SSC/State exam descriptive pattern)

41. Read the instruction: "Summarise the following passage in about one-third of its length and give it a suitable title." A candidate writes the summary but forgets the title. The candidate will:
(a) Get full marks since the title is optional (b) Be asked to write the passage again (c) Be rewarded for saving words (d) Lose marks because the title is a required component
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is assuming the title is decorative; when the question explicitly demands a title, omitting it is an incomplete answer and costs marks. Following every part of the instruction is essential, so the answer is lose marks because the title is a required component.
Source: PYQ-style (examiner's trap — instruction compliance)

42. A passage argues: "Technology saves time, but people feel busier than ever because they fill saved time with more tasks." The best title is:
(a) The History of Technology (b) Machines and Factories (c) Why People Are Lazy (d) The Paradox of Saved Time
Answer: (d)
Solution: The passage's core is the contradiction between time saved and busyness felt; the other options either miss the paradox or introduce ideas absent from the passage. The title must mirror the central tension, so the answer is The Paradox of Saved Time.
Source: PYQ-style (title selection)

43. In compressing the sentence "He returned back to his native village in order to meet his old, aged mother," which version is best for a precis?
(a) He returned back to his village to meet his old, aged mother (b) He went back to his native village in order to meet his aged mother (c) He returned to his village to meet his aged mother (d) He returned back to his native village in order to meet his mother
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Returned back", "native village" and "old, aged" are redundancies; the tempting options each retain at least one. The best version strips all redundancy while keeping the meaning, so the answer is "He returned to his village to meet his aged mother."
Source: PYQ-style (redundancy elimination)

44. A passage contains the sentence: "I believe," said the leader, "that discipline is the soul of success." In the precis this should appear as:
(a) "I believe that discipline is the soul of success," said the leader (b) Discipline, success, leader — key words (c) The leader believes discipline is success (d) The leader said that discipline was the soul of success
Answer: (d)
Solution: Direct speech must be converted to indirect speech in a precis, with tense shifted back; keeping the quotation or chopping it into keywords breaks the rules. The reported form preserves meaning objectively, so the answer is "The leader said that discipline was the soul of success."
Source: Standard precis-writing rules (speech conversion)

45. Which sentence should be deleted FIRST when compressing a paragraph?
(a) The topic sentence (b) A sentence stating the author's main argument (c) The concluding sentence (d) A sentence giving an illustrative anecdote
Answer: (d)
Solution: Topic, argument and concluding sentences carry the skeleton of meaning; an anecdote only illustrates what is already stated. Illustrations are the safest first cut, so the answer is a sentence giving an illustrative anecdote.
Source: Standard precis-writing rules

46. A candidate's precis is grammatically perfect but copies 60% of its sentences verbatim from the passage. The examiner will most likely:
(a) Award full marks for accuracy (b) Penalise it because a precis must be in the candidate's own words (c) Ignore the copying if grammar is correct (d) Award bonus marks for faithfulness
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is thinking accuracy of language compensates for copying; verbatim lifting shows no summarising skill and is heavily penalised even when grammar is flawless. Original wording is non-negotiable, so the answer is penalise it because a precis must be in the candidate's own words.
Source: PYQ-style (examiner's trap — verbatim copying)

47. The best order of steps in precis writing is:
(a) Write final draft → read passage → count words → give title (b) Give title → copy key sentences → submit (c) Read passage → identify main ideas → draft in own words → revise and check length → give title (d) Count words → write title → read passage
Answer: (c)
Solution: Every other sequence puts a later step before understanding the passage, which makes compression impossible. Understanding must precede drafting, and revision precedes titling, so the answer is read passage → identify main ideas → draft in own words → revise and check length → give title.
Source: NCERT Class 11 English (Writing Skills)

48. A passage discusses both the benefits and the drawbacks of social media but concludes that mindful use is the key. A precis that mentions only the benefits is:
(a) Acceptable because benefits come first (b) Better because it is shorter (c) Faulty because it distorts the author's balanced view (d) Correct because conclusions do not matter
Answer: (c)
Solution: Selective reporting of one side misrepresents the author; the trap is assuming brevity justifies omission of the balance and conclusion. A precis must preserve the overall stance, so the answer is faulty because it distorts the author's balanced view.
Source: PYQ-style (fidelity to author's viewpoint)

49. Which technique best helps reduce word count without losing meaning?
(a) Replacing clauses with phrases and phrases with single words (b) Removing all verbs (c) Deleting every third sentence mechanically (d) Using abbreviations and symbols
Answer: (a)
Solution: Mechanical deletion destroys logic, and abbreviations are not acceptable in formal precis; the systematic method is grammatical compression — clause to phrase to word. This preserves meaning while cutting words, so the answer is replacing clauses with phrases and phrases with single words.
Source: Standard precis-writing technique

50. "Despite the fact that he was tired, he continued to work." The most compressed correct version is:
(a) Despite the fact of being tired, he continued to work (b) Although tired, he continued working (c) He was tired but despite that fact he continued to work on (d) In spite of the fact that tiredness was there, he worked
Answer: (b)
Solution: The tempting options keep wordy frames like "the fact that" or add filler; "although tired" compresses the whole concessive clause into two words without losing meaning. Maximum economy with full sense is the goal, so the answer is "Although tired, he continued working."
Source: PYQ-style (sentence compression)
