126. What is the ideal length of a precis compared to the original passage?
(a) About one-half of the original (b) About one-third of the original (c) About one-tenth of the original (d) Exactly equal to the original
Answer: (b)
Solution: Many candidates assume half the length is acceptable, but that retains too much detail and defeats the purpose of compression. The standard convention in competitive exams is that a precis should be roughly one-third of the original passage, so the answer is about one-third of the original.

127. Which of the following must a precis always carry?
(a) The writer's personal opinion on the topic (b) Statistical data from the passage (c) Direct quotations from the passage (d) A suitable title reflecting the central idea
Answer: (d)
Solution: Personal opinions and fresh quotations are traps — a precis must never add the writer's views, and quotations are usually paraphrased away. A title capturing the central idea is an essential requirement, so the answer is a suitable title reflecting the central idea.

128. While writing a precis, the passage should be written in:
(a) First person, exactly as the author wrote it (b) Second person, to address the reader (c) Any person, depending on mood (d) Third person, indirect speech
Answer: (d)
Solution: Keeping the first person is a common trap; even if the original uses "I", the precis writer reports the ideas objectively. Precis is conventionally written in third person and indirect (reported) speech, so the answer is third person, indirect speech.

129. Which of these should be eliminated first when compressing a passage?
(a) Illustrations, examples and repetitions (b) The main argument (c) The author's conclusion (d) The central theme
Answer: (a)
Solution: Deleting the conclusion or theme is a trap — those are the skeleton of the passage. Examples, illustrations, anecdotes and repetitions are flesh that can be cut without harming the skeleton, so the answer is illustrations, examples and repetitions.

Answer: (c)
Solution: Retaining the quotation marks is the tempting error — a precis must convert direct speech into indirect speech. The present tense "says" also breaks the reported-speech convention, so the answer is "The author declared that he would never surrender."

131. The word "precis" is derived from a word meaning:

Solution: Many guess "translate" because precis involves rewording, but the word comes from French "précis", from Latin "praecidere" — to cut short. The core idea is cutting, so the answer is to shorten by cutting.
