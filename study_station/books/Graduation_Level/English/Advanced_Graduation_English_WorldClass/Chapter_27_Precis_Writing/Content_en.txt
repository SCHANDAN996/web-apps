# Precis Writing — The Art of Saying More with Less

## 🎯 The Hook

Imagine you are a government officer. A 10-page report lands on your desk, and your senior says: *"Give me the gist in one page — by 4 PM."* That single skill — compressing a long passage into a short, faithful summary without losing its soul — is **Precis Writing**. It is not just an exam question in SSC CGL, IBPS, and State PCS descriptive papers; it is a life skill of the professional world. The word *precis* (pronounced "pray-see") comes from French, meaning "cut short" — and that is exactly what you will learn to do: **cut the fat, keep the muscle.**

## 🎓 Learning Objectives

By the end of this chapter, you will be able to:

1. Define a precis and state the standard **one-third length rule**.
2. Apply the **5-step method** (Read → Identify → Draft → Trim → Title) to any passage.
3. Distinguish between what to **keep** (main ideas, arguments, conclusions) and what to **delete** (examples, repetitions, figures of speech, quotations).
4. Convert direct speech to **indirect speech** and first person to **third person** while summarising.
5. Give a suitable **title** to a passage.
6. Avoid the classic examiner traps that cost marks in descriptive evaluation.

---

## 📌 What Exactly is a Precis?

A **precis** is a concise, clear, and faithful restatement of a passage in your own words, roughly **one-third the length** of the original. It is *not* a paraphrase (which can be as long as the original), *not* a critical analysis (your opinions are banned), and *not* a collection of copied sentences stitched together.

**The four pillars of a good precis:**

| Pillar | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Conciseness** | About 1/3 of the original length — no padding |
| **Clarity** | Simple, direct English; no jargon added |
| **Completeness** | All main ideas present; nothing essential dropped |
| **Coherence** | Ideas flow logically, connected by linkers |

A useful formula to remember: **A precis = the author's thoughts ÷ your words × ⅓ length.**

*(Answers: 1. One-third. 2. Conciseness, Clarity, Completeness, Coherence. 3. A paraphrase restates everything and may be as long as the original; a precis compresses to the essentials.)*

## 🛠️ The 5-Step Method (Your Assembly Line)

**Step 1 — Read twice.** First reading: get the general theme. Second reading: underline topic sentences and the author's conclusion. Ask: *"What is the ONE thing the author wants me to believe?"*

**Step 2 — Identify the skeleton.** Every passage has a skeleton (main ideas) and flesh (examples, anecdotes, statistics, repetitions). List the main points in the order they appear. Never reorder the author's logic.

**Step 3 — Draft in your own words.** Write in **third person**, **past tense** (usually), and **indirect speech**. Replace long phrases with single words: *"at this point in time"* → *"now"*; *"in spite of the fact that"* → *"although"*.

**Step 4 — Trim to one-third.** Count the original words, divide by 3, and cut ruthlessly. Delete: examples, illustrations, quotations, proverbs, repetitions, and ornamental adjectives.

**Step 5 — Give a title.** The title is the passage in 3–6 words. It must capture the **central theme**, not a side detail. Bad titles are the #1 reason examiners doubt whether you understood the passage at all.

## ✂️ What to Keep vs. What to Kill

| KEEP ✅ | KILL ❌ |
|---|---|
| Main arguments & thesis | Examples, stories, anecdotes |
| Author's conclusion | Quotations & proverbs |
| Cause-effect relationships | Repetitions & restatements |
| Essential facts | Statistics & figures (unless central) |
| Logical sequence | Figurative language, similes, metaphors |
| Author's tone (neutral reporting) | Your own opinions & comments |

**Golden conversion rule:** Direct speech → Indirect speech.
- Original: *The leader said, "We shall fight on the beaches."*
- Precis: *The leader declared their resolve to resist everywhere.*

## 🧩 Mnemonic: **"TRIM"** your passage

> **T** — **T**itle last, theme first
> **R** — **R**ead twice before writing once
> **I** — **I**ndirect speech, third person
> **M** — **M**ain ideas only; murder the examples

Whenever you sit down to write a precis, whisper **TRIM** — because that is literally what you are doing to the passage.

---

## ⚠️ Examiner's Trap

> **Trap 1 — The Copy-Paste Trap:** Lifting whole sentences from the passage. Examiners spot this instantly; a precis must be in *your* words. Copying signals you didn't understand — you merely transcribed.
>
> **Trap 2 — The Opinion Trap:** Adding your own views ("In my opinion the author is right…"). A precis reports the *author's* mind, not yours. One personal comment can cost you heavily in descriptive marking.
>
> **Trap 3 — The Length Trap:** Writing half the passage "to be safe." Exceeding one-third shows poor compression skill; falling far below it shows you dropped main ideas. Count words. Respect the third.

## 🔁 Linguistic Bridge

| In Hindi-influenced English we write… | Correct precis English |
|---|---|
| "The author is telling that hard work is necessary." | "The author states that hard work is essential." |
| "He said that he will not give up." (tense mismatch) | "He said that he would not give up." |
| "According to me, the passage means…" | *(Delete — no personal opinion in a precis)* |
| "The writer is giving many examples like for instance…" | *(Delete — examples are cut, not reported)* |
| "In this paragraph it is written about…" | "The passage discusses…" |

## 📊 Summary Table

| Element | Rule |
|---|---|
| Length | ≈ ⅓ of the original |
| Person | Third person |
| Tense | Usually past (reporting) |
| Speech | Indirect only |
| Language | Your own words, simple & formal |
| Title | 3–6 words, captures the central theme |
| Opinion | Strictly forbidden |
| Examples/Quotes | Strictly deleted |
| Order of ideas | Same as the original |
