# 📋 Important Rules & Tips — Precis Writing

## (a) 12 High-Yield Rules

| # | Rule | Example | Examiner's Trap |
|---|------|---------|-----------------|
| 1 | **One-third length rule**: A precis should be roughly 1/3 of the original passage's word count. | 300-word passage → ~100-word precis | Writing a summary of 60–70% length "to be safe" — examiners deduct marks for exceeding the limit. |
| 2 | **Use your own words**: Never lift full sentences from the passage. | Original: "The rapid proliferation of digital devices has transformed pedagogy." → Precis: "Digital devices have changed how we teach." | Copying key sentences verbatim and joining them — this is extraction, not precis, and scores near zero. |
| 3 | **Third person & past/indirect narration**: Write in third person; convert direct speech to indirect. | "I believe," said the minister, "that reform is urgent." → The minister believed that reform was urgent. | Keeping "I" or quoted dialogue because the passage uses it. |
| 4 | **Give a suitable title**: 3–6 words capturing the central idea, not a side detail. | Passage on how discipline beats talent → Title: "Discipline: The True Key to Success" | Choosing a title from an attractive but minor example in the passage. |
| 5 | **One paragraph, one flow**: A precis is a single connected paragraph (unless the passage is very long); ideas must link logically. | Use connectors: "therefore," "however," "consequently." | Choppy, telegram-style sentences that read like notes. |
| 6 | **No personal opinion**: Add nothing — no comments, no examples of your own, no criticism. | Passage criticises social media → you report the criticism, you don't endorse or refute it. | Inserting "In my view…" or "This is true because…" — instant mark loss. |
| 7 | **Retain the author's tone**: If the passage is ironic, cautious, or hopeful, the precis must reflect it. | Author is sceptical of AI hype → precis says "the author questions whether AI can deliver its promises." | Flattening a critical passage into a neutral or praising one. |
| 8 | **Drop examples, illustrations, repetitions**: Keep the argument; delete anecdotes, statistics, and restatements. | Three examples of hardworking leaders → "History shows that perseverance brings success." | Keeping one "important-looking" example — examples are the first thing to cut. |
| 9 | **Preserve the order of ideas**: Follow the passage's sequence; don't rearrange arguments. | Cause → effect in the passage stays cause → effect in the precis. | Starting with the conclusion because it "sounds stronger." |
| 10 | **Figures of speech → plain language**: Convert metaphors and poetic lines into direct statements. | "He burned the midnight oil" → "He worked late into the night." | Carrying a beautiful metaphor into the precis to show style — precis rewards clarity, not ornament. |
| 11 | **Every sentence must carry the main idea**: Test each sentence — if the central argument survives without it, delete it. | Cut: dates, names of minor people, parenthetical remarks. | Keeping a striking statistic (e.g., "70% of youth…") that adds flavour but not meaning. |
| 12 | **Draft → count → revise**: Write a rough draft, count words, trim to the limit, then write the fair copy. | Draft 130 words → trim redundancies → final 100 words. | Writing the precis directly in the answer sheet with no word count — overshooting the limit is the most common technical error. |

## (b) Time Management — The 15/45-Second Rule

Precis questions in descriptive papers (SSC CGL Tier-III style, Bank PO descriptive, State PCS) are not MCQs, but the same discipline applies when you **read and plan**:

| Task Type | Nature | Time Rule |
|---|---|---|
| **Fact-based (15 sec per decision)** | Identifying the topic sentence, spotting examples/repetitions to delete, counting words, checking third person | Decide in ~15 seconds each — these are mechanical checks. Don't re-read a whole paragraph to confirm one example. |
| **Inference-based (45 sec per decision)** | Deciding the central theme, choosing the title, judging the author's tone, compressing a complex argument into one sentence | Allow ~45 seconds each — these need thought. Rushing the title or tone costs more marks than a slow read. |

**Practical split for a 30-minute precis task:**
- First reading (gist only): 4 min — *15-sec mode*
- Second reading (underline main ideas, strike out examples): 6 min — *45-sec mode*
- Rough draft: 10 min
- Word count + trim + fair copy: 10 min

**Skip rule:** If a sentence in the passage confuses you after 45 seconds, mark it and move on — it is usually a supporting detail, and supporting details get deleted anyway.

## (c) Morphology — Roots & Affixes for Precis Vocabulary

Precis passages are dense with formal vocabulary. Knowing roots lets you compress long phrases into single precise words.

| Root / Affix | Meaning | Derived Words | Compression Use |
|---|---|---|---|
| **brev** (L) | short | brief, brevity, abbreviate | "said in very few words" → "stated briefly" |
| **dict** (L) | say, speak | dictate, predict, verdict, contradict | "spoke against" → "contradicted" |
| **spect** (L) | look, see | inspect, perspective, retrospect | "looking back at events" → "in retrospect" |
| **port** (L) | carry | transport, export, portable, support | "carries the idea forward" → "supports the argument" |
| **scrib/script** (L) | write | describe, manuscript, inscription | "written account of" → "description of" |
| **-ise / -ize** (suffix) | to make | summarise, modernise, centralise | "make a summary of" → "summarise" |
| **con-** (prefix) | together | condense, consolidate, conclude | "bring together into a shorter form" → "condense" |
| **re-** (prefix) | again | reiterate, revise, restate | "say again" → "reiterate" (note: repetition is what a precis *deletes*) |

**Memory hook:** *"A precis writer is a **con-dict-brev** machine — he brings ideas **together** (con-), says them (**dict**), and keeps them **short** (brev)."*
