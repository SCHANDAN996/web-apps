# 📊 PYQ Analysis — Precis Writing

## How Exams Test This Chapter (Pattern Analysis)

Precis writing appears in **descriptive papers** (SSC CGL Tier-III/Descriptive, IBPS PO Mains Descriptive, State PCS Mains, UPSC CAPF) and, indirectly, in **objective papers** as comprehension-based questions testing the same skills: identifying the main idea, distinguishing essential from non-essential, and condensing without distortion.

Across SSC, Banking, and State exams, questions drawn from this chapter cluster into these recurring patterns:

**1. Title Selection (most frequent in objective form)**
A short passage is given; you must choose the most suitable title. The examiner's trap: one option is too narrow (covers only one paragraph), one is too broad (goes beyond the passage), and one is a catchy but irrelevant phrase. The correct title captures the **central idea only**.

**2. Central Idea / Main Theme Identification**
"What is the passage mainly about?" — the same skill as writing the precis's opening sentence. Trap: options that are *true according to the passage* but are supporting details, not the main point.

**3. "Which of the following is NOT essential to the passage?"**
Directly tests the precis skill of separating the skeleton from the flesh — examples, illustrations, repetitions, and figures of speech are what a precis drops.

**4. Statement-Based Questions (Statement I, II, III)**
Common at the Graduation level: "Which statements would find a place in a precis of this passage?" Trap: a statement that is factually correct but adds information *not in the passage* — a precis must never import outside knowledge.

**5. Tone and Attitude Questions**
Since a precis must preserve the author's tone, exams ask: "The author's tone is — (a) sarcastic (b) optimistic (c) neutral (d) alarmist." Trap: confusing the *topic's* emotional charge with the *author's* attitude.

**6. Descriptive-Paper Precis Tasks**
Typical instructions: "Write a precis of the following passage in about one-third of its length and give it a suitable title." Marks are lost chiefly for: exceeding the word limit, copying whole sentences, adding personal opinion, and omitting the title.

## Examiner's Traps — Classified

| Trap Type | How It Appears | Defence |
|---|---|---|
| **Sound-Right** | An option that *feels* like a good title because it uses words from the passage | Check: does it cover the WHOLE passage or just one line? |
| **Over-thinking** | Choosing an inference-heavy option when the question asks for the *stated* main idea | A precis reports, it does not interpret |
| **Intervening Detail** | A vivid example/statistic in the passage distracts you into making it the theme | Ask: "If I delete this, does the argument survive?" If yes, it's decoration |
| **Outside Knowledge** | An option that is true in real life but absent from the passage | The passage is the only universe |

## High-Yield PYQ-Style Questions with Solutions

**Q1.** A passage discusses how cities are expanding, farmland is shrinking, and then focuses for three paragraphs on one farmer in Vidarbha who switched to organic farming. The best title is:
(a) The Story of a Vidarbha Farmer
(b) Urbanisation and Its Impact on Agriculture
(c) Organic Farming in India
(d) The Death of Indian Agriculture
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** The farmer's story is an *illustration* of the larger trend — in a precis it would be deleted. (a) is the Intervening Detail trap; (c) is too narrow; (d) is an emotional exaggeration the author never states (Sound-Right trap).
**Source: PYQ-style (SSC CGL Descriptive pattern)**

**Q2.** In a precis, which of the following should be retained?
(a) A striking metaphor used by the author
(b) A statistical example proving the author's claim
(c) The author's central argument
(d) A quotation from a famous leader
**Answer: (c)**
**Solution:** Metaphors, statistics, and quotations are *supporting devices* — the first casualties of compression. Only the argument itself is indispensable.
**Source: PYQ-style**

**Q3.** "Which statement would NOT appear in a precis of the passage?" — The passage argues that remote work increases productivity, citing a 2023 survey of IT firms and quoting a CEO. The correct choice is the option that:
(a) restates the main claim in fewer words
(b) mentions the CEO's name and company
(c) summarises the survey's conclusion
(d) states the author's recommendation
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** Names, designations, and attributions are ornamental; the *substance* of the quote survives, the citation does not. This is the classic "drop the example, keep the point" rule.
**Source: PYQ-style (IBPS PO Mains pattern)**

**Q4.** The prescribed length of a precis is generally:
(a) one-half of the original
(b) one-third of the original
(c) one-fourth of the original
(d) exactly 100 words
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** The standard convention is one-third, unless the question specifies otherwise. Writing half the passage is the most common reason for losing marks in descriptive papers.
**Source: PYQ-style**

**Q5.** A precis must be written:
(a) in the first person if the passage uses "I"
(b) in the past tense always
(c) in the third person and reported/indirect speech
(d) using the author's exact sentences
**Answer: (c)**
**Solution:** Direct speech and first person are converted to indirect narration; copying sentences is penalised as it shows no compression skill.
**Source: PYQ-style**

**Q6.** The author's tone in a passage that lists the dangers of plastic but ends with workable solutions is best described as:
(a) alarmist (b) pessimistic (c) cautiously optimistic (d) indifferent
**Answer: (c)**
**Solution:** Over-thinking trap: the dangers tempt you toward (a)/(b), but the concluding solutions reveal the author's real stance. A precis must end on the same note the author does.
**Source: PYQ-style**

**Q7.** Which is the correct first step in precis writing?
(a) Counting the words of the passage
(b) Writing a rough draft immediately
(c) Reading the passage twice to grasp the central idea
(d) Giving the passage a title
**Answer: (c)**
**Solution:** Comprehension precedes compression. The title comes *last*, after the precis reveals what the passage truly says.
**Source: PYQ-style**

**Q8.** A passage of 450 words is given. Your precis should ideally contain:
(a) about 450 words (b) about 300 words (c) about 150 words (d) about 50 words
**Answer: (c)**
**Solution:** One-third of 450 = 150. (d) is over-compression — it would force you to drop essential arguments, not just illustrations.
**Source: PYQ-style**

**Q9.** Which of these is a valid reason to reject a sentence while writing a precis?
(a) It is grammatically complex
(b) It repeats an idea already expressed
(c) It contains an unfamiliar word
(d) It is the longest sentence in the passage
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** Redundancy is the first thing a precis eliminates. Length or difficulty of a sentence is irrelevant; only its *necessity to the argument* matters.
**Source: PYQ-style**

**Q10.** In statement-based precis questions, a statement that is factually true but not mentioned in the passage should be:
(a) included, because it strengthens the precis
(b) included if it is commonly known
(c) excluded, because a precis contains only the passage's ideas
(d) included if space permits
**Answer: (c)**
**Solution:** The Outside Knowledge trap. A precis is a faithful miniature of the original — nothing may be added, however true.
**Source: PYQ-style**

## Applying the 15/45-Second Rule

**Fact-based (15 seconds):** Q4, Q8, Q9 — these test a single memorised convention (one-third rule, what to delete). Recall, don't deliberate. If you find yourself re-reading the options twice, you are over-thinking a recall question.

**Inference-based (45 seconds):** Q1, Q6 — title and tone questions demand a full mental precis of the passage. Budget your time here: read the passage once for gist (20 sec), eliminate the narrow/broad/emotional options (15 sec), confirm the survivor covers every paragraph (10 sec). Never spend 45 seconds on Q4-type questions — that time belongs to Q1 and Q6.
