76. The primary purpose of a precis is to:
(a) Add the writer's own opinions to the passage (b) Translate the passage into simpler vocabulary (c) Condense a passage to about one-third of its length while retaining its essential meaning (d) Criticise the arguments presented in the passage
Answer: (c)
Solution: The tempting wrong option suggests adding opinions, but a precis must never include the writer's personal views — that is the most common examiner trap. A precis is a faithful miniature of the original, reduced to roughly one-third of its length, so the answer is condensing the passage while retaining its essential meaning.

77. Which of the following should NEVER appear in a precis?
(a) The central idea of the passage (b) The main arguments of the original author (c) The writer's personal comment on the topic (d) A suitable title
Answer: (c)
Solution: Central ideas, main arguments and a title are all legitimate parts of a precis. The examiner's trap is the option that looks harmless — a personal comment — but objectivity is the soul of precis writing; your own views destroy its fidelity, so the answer is the writer's personal comment on the topic.

78. The ideal length of a precis, as conventionally prescribed in competitive examinations, is:
(a) One-third of the original passage (b) One-fourth of the original passage (c) One-half of the original passage (d) Equal to the original passage
Answer: (a)
Solution: One-half is too long and one-fourth is the distractor that sounds precise but is not the convention. The standard rule taught for SSC and banking examinations is one-third of the original length, so the answer is one-third of the original passage.

79. A good title for a precis should be:
(a) A complete sentence summarising the whole passage (b) The first line of the passage copied exactly (c) A short phrase capturing the central theme (d) A question addressed to the reader
Answer: (c)
Solution: Copying the first line is the tempting trap — the opening line often introduces, not summarises. A title must be brief, usually a phrase of a few words that captures the theme, so the answer is a short phrase capturing the central theme.

80. While writing a precis, examples, illustrations and repetitions in the original passage should be:
(a) Retained in full to preserve clarity (b) Expanded with fresh examples (c) Omitted, as they are supporting details, not the core idea (d) Converted into quotations
Answer: (c)
Solution: Retaining examples in full is the trap — it inflates the precis beyond the one-third limit. Examples merely support the argument; the core idea survives without them, so the answer is that they should be omitted as supporting details.

81. A precis should normally be written in:
(a) The first person, to show involvement (b) The second person, to address the reader (c) Any person, depending on the writer's mood (d) The third person and indirect (reported) speech
Answer: (d)
Solution: First person feels natural but is the classic trap — it injects the precis-writer into the text. The convention demands third person and reported speech even when the original uses "I", so the answer is the third person and indirect speech.

82. If the original passage contains a direct quotation, the precis writer should:
(a) Convert it into indirect speech or omit it (b) Reproduce it word for word within inverted commas (c) Underline it for emphasis (d) Expand it with an explanation
Answer: (a)
Solution: Reproducing the quotation verbatim is the tempting error — quotations waste precious words in a precis. The rule is to render the idea in indirect speech or drop it if it is merely decorative, so the answer is convert it into indirect speech or omit it.

83. The first step in writing a precis is:
(a) Reading the passage carefully, more than once, to grasp its central idea (b) Writing the final draft immediately (c) Counting the words of the passage (d) Choosing a title
Answer: (a)
Solution: Counting words first is the mechanical trap — arithmetic before understanding produces a distorted precis. Comprehension of the central idea through careful repeated reading must precede everything, so the answer is reading the passage carefully to grasp its central idea.

84. Which quality is LEAST essential in a good precis?
(a) Clarity (b) Ornate and decorative language (c) Coherence (d) Conciseness
Answer: (b)
Solution: Clarity, coherence and conciseness are the three pillars of precis writing. Ornate language is the trap — it sounds impressive but violates economy and objectivity, so the answer is ornate and decorative language.

85. In a precis, the tone of the original passage should be:
(a) Reversed to show critical thinking (b) Preserved as far as possible (c) Made humorous to engage the examiner (d) Ignored completely
Answer: (b)
Solution: Reversing the tone is the trap that masquerades as "critical thinking" — a precis is a summary, not a review. Fidelity to the author's tone is part of fidelity to meaning, so the answer is preserved as far as possible.

86. A passage of 300 words is given. The precis should ideally contain about:
(a) 300 words (b) 150 words (c) 100 words (d) 30 words
Answer: (c)
Solution: 150 words is the tempting wrong choice — it is one-half, not one-third. Applying the one-third rule, 300 ÷ 3 = 100, so the answer is 100 words.

87. Which of the following best describes "coherence" in a precis?
(a) Logical connection of ideas so the precis reads as a smooth, unified whole (b) Using difficult vocabulary throughout (c) Writing each sentence as a separate paragraph (d) Repeating the main idea in every sentence
Answer: (a)
Solution: Repeating the main idea is the trap — repetition destroys conciseness, not coherence. Coherence means the sentences flow logically and the precis stands alone as a complete piece, so the answer is logical connection of ideas into a unified whole.

88. The precis writer should use:
(a) The exact sentences of the passage joined together (b) His or her own words as far as possible (c) Only technical jargon (d) Phrases copied from the passage's conclusion
Answer: (b)
Solution: Stringing together the original sentences is the commonest trap — it produces a patchwork, not a precis, and examiners penalise it. Original wording demonstrates comprehension, so the answer is his or her own words as far as possible.

89. Identify the part containing an error: "A precis must contains (P) / all the essential ideas (Q) / of the original passage (R) / in brief form. (S)"
(a) No error (b) Part Q (c) Part R (d) Part P
Answer: (d)
Solution: Part Q looks suspicious because of "all the essential ideas", but it is grammatically fine — that is the sound-right trap. The real error is in Part P: after the modal "must", the bare infinitive "contain" is required, not "contains", so the answer is Part P.

90. When a passage presents arguments both for and against an idea, the precis should:
(a) Present both sides proportionally, as in the original (b) Present only the side the precis writer agrees with (c) Ignore both sides and state only facts (d) Add a third, balancing viewpoint
Answer: (a)
Solution: Choosing one side is the trap — it feels decisive but betrays the original's balance. A precis mirrors the author's structure and emphasis, so the answer is present both sides proportionally, as in the original.

(a) "To argue" (b) "To praise" (c) "To cut short" or "condensed" (d) "To narrate"

93. A precis differs from a paraphrase mainly because a precis:

Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is assuming the two terms are interchangeable — examiners love this confusion. A paraphrase restates fully in different words; a precis compresses, so the answer is that a precis shortens the passage while a paraphrase restates it in roughly equal length.

94. Which sentence is most appropriate for inclusion in a precis of a passage on climate change?
(a) "The author argues that rising global temperatures demand urgent collective action." (b) "In my opinion, governments are doing nothing about climate change." (c) "Climate change is a hoax, as everyone knows." (d) "Last year I visited a glacier and saw it melting."
Answer: (a)
Solution: The first-person options are the trap — personal opinion and anecdote have no place in a precis. The correct sentence reports the author's argument objectively in the third person, so the answer is "The author argues that rising global temperatures demand urgent collective action."

95. Identify the part containing an error: "Neither the examples (P) / nor the illustrations (Q) / was included (R) / in the final precis. (S)"
(a) Part P (b) Part R (c) Part Q (d) No error
Answer: (b)
Solution: Part P tempts students who think "neither" alone controls the verb — that is the proximity-rule trap. With "neither…nor", the verb agrees with the nearer subject, "illustrations" (plural), so "were included" is needed; the error is in Part R, so the answer is Part R.

96. The final step in precis writing is:
(a) Adding a personal conclusion (b) Revising the draft, checking length, grammar and fidelity to the original (c) Memorising the original passage (d) Writing the passage again in full
Answer: (b)
Solution: Adding a personal conclusion is the trap — it violates objectivity at the last moment. Revision against the original for length, accuracy and language completes the process, so the answer is revising the draft and checking length, grammar and fidelity.
