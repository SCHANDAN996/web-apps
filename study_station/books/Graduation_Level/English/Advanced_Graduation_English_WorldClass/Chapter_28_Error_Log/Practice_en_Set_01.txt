1. What is the primary purpose of maintaining an Error Log during exam preparation?
(a) To record, classify and review every mistake so it is never repeated (b) To record only the questions you answered correctly (c) To copy the entire question paper for future reading (d) To count how many hours you studied each day
Answer: (a)
Solution: An Error Log exists to capture mistakes, classify their cause, and force periodic review so the same error is not repeated in the real exam. Recording only correct answers defeats its purpose, so the answer is to record, classify and review every mistake.
Source: PYQ-style

2. Which of the following is the MOST essential column in an Error Log?
(a) The reason why you made the mistake (b) The colour of the pen used (c) The name of the book the question came from (d) The time of day you attempted the question
Answer: (a)
Solution: Knowing why you erred — concept gap, silly mistake, or trap — is what converts a log into learning. Pen colour and timing are irrelevant details, so the answer is the reason why you made the mistake.

3. In an Error Log, a "silly mistake" is best defined as an error caused by:
(a) Not knowing the underlying concept at all (b) Carelessness despite knowing the concept well (c) The question being wrongly framed (d) Running out of time in the exam
Answer: (b)
Solution: A silly mistake happens when the concept is known but haste or inattention causes the error; this distinction matters because the fix is mindfulness, not re-study. Unknown concepts belong to the concept-gap category, so the answer is carelessness despite knowing the concept well.

4. How often should a serious aspirant review the Error Log?
(a) Only once, just before the exam (b) Never, once it is written (c) Only when a new mistake is added (d) On a fixed schedule, such as every 2 days and weekly
Answer: (d)
Solution: Spaced review — after 2 days, then weekly — moves errors into long-term memory; a single pre-exam reading is too late to change habits. Writing without reviewing makes the log useless, so the answer is on a fixed schedule, such as every 2 days and weekly.

5. Which category should a mistake fall into if you picked a nearly-correct option because the examiner hid an intervening phrase between subject and verb?
(a) Examiner's trap (b) Concept gap (c) Time pressure (d) Guesswork
Answer: (a)
Solution: When the question is deliberately designed to mislead — like an intervening phrase breaking subject-verb agreement — the error is classified as an examiner's trap, not a concept gap, because you likely knew the rule. So the answer is examiner's trap.
