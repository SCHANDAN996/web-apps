Card 1
Front: What is an Error Log in English exam preparation?
Back: A personal record of every mistake you make in mocks and practice sets — the question, your wrong answer, the correct answer, the rule violated, and the trap type. It converts random errors into a fixable pattern.

Card 2
Front: What are the 5 mandatory columns of an Error Log entry?
Back: (1) Question/Topic, (2) My wrong answer, (3) Correct answer, (4) Rule violated, (5) Trap type (e.g., Intervening Phrase, Sound-Right). Without all five, the log is just a list, not a learning tool.

Card 3
Front: Why is writing the RULE violated more important than writing the correct answer?
Back: The correct answer fixes one question; the rule fixes the entire family of similar questions. Exams repeat rules, not exact questions.

Card 4
Front: What is the "Trap Type" column and name the four main trap types.
Back: It labels HOW the examiner fooled you. The four types: Intervening Phrase, Collective Noun confusion, Sound-Right (sounds correct but is wrong), and Over-thinking (changing a right answer to a wrong one).

Card 5
Front: 🧠 Mnemonic: What does "E-R-R-O-R" stand for as a logging routine?
Back: Examine the mistake → Record the rule → Review after 2 days → Omit repeated errors → Re-test yourself weekly. If you skip Review and Re-test, the log is wasted effort.

Card 6
Front: How soon after a mock test should you log your errors, and why?
Back: Within 24 hours, ideally the same day. Memory of WHY you chose the wrong option fades fast; logging late records the mistake but loses the reasoning — and the reasoning is what needs fixing.

Card 7
Front: ⚠️ Examiner's Trap: What is the "Sound-Right" trap?
Back: An option that sounds natural to a Hindi-English bilingual ear but is grammatically wrong — e.g., "He is more smarter than me." Your ear is trained by wrong everyday usage, so never trust sound alone; check the rule.

Card 8
Front: ⚠️ Examiner's Trap: What is the "Over-thinking" trap and how does the Error Log cure it?
Back: You pick the right answer, then doubt yourself and switch to a wrong one. If your log shows many "right → wrong" changes, the cure is: change an answer only if you can name the rule that demands it.

Card 9
Front: What is the "Review after 2 days" rule in error-log maintenance?
Back: Re-attempt every logged question after 2 days without looking at the solution. If you get it right, mark it ✓; if wrong again, mark it ★ (star errors get weekly revision). Spaced repetition beats one-time correction.

Card 10
Front: What is a "starred error" (★) and what does a growing star count tell you?
Back: An error repeated even after review. A cluster of stars in one topic (say, subject-verb agreement) signals a conceptual gap — stop doing new mocks and re-study that chapter first.

Card 11
Front: How do you convert an Error Log entry into a Flashcard?
Back: Front: the question stem or the rule in question form ("One of my friends ___ coming."). Back: correct answer + the rule + the trap type. This turns passive records into active-recall material.

Card 12
Front: 🧠 Mnemonic: What does "LOG" remind you about each entry?
Back: L — Locate the exact word/phrase where the error hides; O — Own the mistake (write YOUR wrong reasoning, not just the right answer); G — Generalise it into a rule you can reuse.

Card 13
Front: What is the difference between a "knowledge error" and a "process error" in the log?
Back: Knowledge error: you didn't know the rule (fix = study the topic). Process error: you knew the rule but misapplied it under time pressure (fix = timed drills + the 15/45-second rule). The log must tag which one it is.

Card 14
Front: How does the 15/45-second rule connect to your Error Log?
Back: Tag each logged question as Fact-based (15 sec: vocab, spelling, direct grammar) or Inference-based (45 sec: tone, comprehension, sentence arrangement). If you spent 45 seconds on a Fact question and still erred, it's a knowledge gap, not a speed issue.

Card 15
Front: Why should you also log questions you got RIGHT by guessing?
Back: A lucky guess is a hidden error. If you can't explain why the answer is correct, log it as "guessed-right" — in the exam, luck won't repeat, but the rule will.

Card 16
Front: What is the weekly Error Log ritual before attempting a new mock?
Back: Spend 20–30 minutes re-reading only the ★ starred entries and the trap-type column. Then take the mock. This primes your brain to spot the exact traps you personally fall for.

Card 17
Front: What does it mean when your Error Log starts "shrinking" over weeks?
Back: Fewer new entries per mock means your repeated-error rate is falling — the log is working. If entries stay constant in number and topic, you are recording errors but not reviewing them.

Card 18
Front: Give an example of a complete Error Log entry (all 5 columns).
Back: Topic: Subject-Verb Agreement | My answer: "The quality of apples were good" | Correct: "The quality of apples was good" | Rule: Verb agrees with the subject (quality), not the intervening phrase's noun | Trap: Intervening Phrase.

Card 19
Front: Why should the Error Log be handwritten or self-typed rather than copied from a solution PDF?
Back: Writing in your own words forces processing — the brain remembers what it generates (generation effect). Copy-pasting creates an illusion of preparation with zero retention.

Card 20
Front: When can you finally delete or retire an Error Log entry?
Back: When you have answered that question correctly in three consecutive spaced reviews (2 days → 1 week → 3 weeks). Retired entries prove mastery; keep them in a "mastered" section for a final pre-exam confidence scan.
