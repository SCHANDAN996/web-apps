# 🪄 Short Tricks + Skip Strategy — Error Log Template

## Mnemonics & Memory Tricks

**Box 1 — The "LOG" Formula**
> **L** – **L**ocate the exact error (underline the wrong word, not the whole sentence)
> **O** – **O**wn the rule (write the grammar rule in your own words)
> **G** – **G**enerate a new example (make one fresh sentence using the rule correctly)

**Box 2 — The "RED" Review Cycle**
> **R** – **R**eview after 2 days
> **E** – **E**xamine again after 7 days
> **D** – **D**rill once more after 30 days
> *If you solve it correctly all three times, retire the card from your log.*

**Box 3 — The "4W" Error Entry**
> Every log entry must answer: **W**hat was wrong? **W**hy was it wrong? **W**hat is the rule? **W**hen did I last repeat this error?

**Box 4 — "DATE" Your Mistake**
> **D** – **D**ate of the error
> **A** – **A**rea (topic: Tense / SVA / Preposition / Vocab…)
> **T** – **T**rap type (Intervening Phrase, Sound-Right, Collective Noun, Over-thinking)
> **E** – **E**xample corrected

**Box 5 — The "3-Strike" Rule**
> Same error 3 times = it is not a mistake, it is a **habit**. Move it to a separate "Habit Page" and revise that page daily until the exam.

**Box 6 — "SPOT" for Spotting-Error Questions**
> **S** – **S**ubject–Verb agreement first
> **P** – **P**arallel structure next
> **O** – **O**rder of words / modifiers
> **T** – **T**ense consistency last
> *Check in this fixed order — fastest to slowest to verify.*

**Box 7 — The "One-Line Rule" Trick**
> Never copy a full grammar chapter into your log. Compress every rule into **one line** you could say aloud in 5 seconds. If it needs two lines, split it into two entries.

**Box 8 — "TAG" Your Traps**
> **T** – **T**ype of trap
> **A** – **A**nswer you chose (wrong)
> **G** – **G**enuine answer + why the wrong one *felt* right
> *The last line is the gold — examiners sell the "feels right" option.*

**Box 9 — The 80/20 Log Filter**
> 80% of your repeated errors come from 20% of topics. Every Sunday, tally your "Area" column — the top 2 areas get next week's practice priority.

**Box 10 — "PAIR" for Vocab Errors**
> **P** – **P**air the word with a synonym
> **A** – **A**ntonym beside it
> **I** – **I**mage or Hindi cue
> **R** – **R**oot (Greek/Latin) it comes from

**Box 11 — The "Mirror Sentence" Trick**
> For every corrected sentence, write its **mirror** — the same structure with new content. Error: "One of my friend…" → Correction: "One of my friends…" → Mirror: "One of my teachers is…" If the mirror is right, the rule is fixed.

**Box 12 — Colour-Code Memory Hook**
> 🔴 Red = repeated error (3+ times) | 🟡 Yellow = trap-type error | 🟢 Green = careless slip. Revise Red daily, Yellow weekly, Green only before mocks.

## Skip Strategy

**Tip 1 — Skip the "Sound-Right" Trap in the First Pass.**
If an error-spotting sentence "sounds correct" on first reading, do not waste 60 seconds hunting. Mark it, move on, and return with fresh eyes — the ear deceives, the rule checklist (SPOT) does not. Log every such question later; these are your highest-value entries.

**Tip 2 — Skip Over-thinking on Fact-based Questions.**
If you know the rule instantly (e.g., "each/every + singular verb"), answer in 15 seconds and never revisit. Re-reading a known answer invites doubt, and doubt invites wrong changes. In your log, record *changed-right-to-wrong* answers separately — that pattern is cured by skipping the second guess, not by more study.

**Tip 3 — Skip Logging Careless Slips After Two Mocks.**
Your log is for *concept* gaps and *trap* patterns, not for every misread word. If a slip happens once and the rule is already known, note it as Green and move on. A bloated log is never revised; a lean log is revised daily. Quality of entries beats quantity — 50 sharp entries outperform 500 vague ones.
