26. What is the primary purpose of maintaining an Error Log during exam preparation?
(a) To record every question you attempt (b) To copy the entire question paper (c) To record and analyse your mistakes so they are not repeated (d) To note down only the questions you got right
Answer: (c)
Solution: Many students wrongly think the log is a record of everything attempted, but logging everything wastes time and hides the real signal. An Error Log exists to capture mistakes, diagnose why they happened, and prevent repetition, so the answer is to record and analyse your mistakes so they are not repeated.
Source: PYQ-style

27. Which of the following is the most essential column in an Error Log?
(a) The colour of the pen used (b) The date of the exam notification (c) The reason why the error occurred (d) The name of the book the question came from
Answer: (c)
Solution: Tempting options like the book's name feel useful, but they do not change future performance. Knowing why you erred — concept gap, misreading, or haste — is what drives correction, so the answer is the reason why the error occurred.

28. How often should you ideally review your Error Log?
(a) Only once, right before the exam (b) On a fixed schedule, such as every 2–3 days and weekly (c) Never, it is only for writing (d) Only when you score full marks
Answer: (b)
Solution: Reviewing only before the exam is tempting but useless because spaced repetition is what fixes errors in memory. A fixed review cycle of every 2–3 days plus a weekly sweep works best, so the answer is on a fixed schedule, such as every 2–3 days and weekly.
