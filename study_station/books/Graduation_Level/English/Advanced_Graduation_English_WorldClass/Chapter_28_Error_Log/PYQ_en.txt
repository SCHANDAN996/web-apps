# 📊 PYQ Analysis — Error Log Template

## How Exams Test This Skill

No exam asks "What is an error log?" directly. Instead, the error log is a *meta-skill* — it is tested through the very questions you should be logging. Across SSC CGL, IBPS PO/Clerk, and State PCS prelims, the English section repeatedly draws from a small, stable pool of error types. Aspirants who maintain an error log notice that the same traps return in new disguises; those who don't, keep losing marks to the same trap year after year.

**Pattern analysis (in words, not numbers):**

- **Subject–Verb Agreement** with intervening phrases ("The quality of the apples *was*…") is the single most recycled trap. Examiners insert a long phrase between subject and verb to make your ear choose the wrong verb.
- **Collective nouns** (team, jury, committee, audience) appear regularly — singular when acting as one unit, plural when members act individually.
- **Sound-Right traps**: sentences that *sound* correct to a Hindi-speaker's ear but violate a rule ("He is more superior to me", "One of my friend…").
- **Over-thinking traps**: grammatically correct sentences that look suspicious, pushing well-prepared students to "fix" what isn't broken (No Error questions).
- **Tense consistency** in conditional and reported-speech frames, and **parallelism** in lists, round out the recurring set.

**Examiner's favourite disguises:**

1. *Intervening Phrase* — separate subject from verb with "of + plural noun".
2. *Collective Noun* — force a singular/plural decision.
3. *Sound-Right* — exploit spoken-English habits.
4. *Over-thinking* — reward the student who trusts the rule, punish the one who second-guesses.

**Why the error log matters here:** every question below is the kind you should enter into your log with four fields — *Question | My wrong answer & why I chose it | Correct rule | Trap type*. The trap-type column is where the marks are: once you see that 7 of your last 10 errors were "Sound-Right", you know exactly what to drill.

## Top 10 High-Yield Questions (with Solutions & Trap Classification)

**Q1.** The quality of the mangoes (a)/ were not good (b)/ this season. (c)/ No error (d)
**Answer:** (b)
**Solution:** Subject is "quality" (singular), not "mangoes". The phrase "of the mangoes" only modifies it. Correct: "was not good".
**Trap type:** Intervening Phrase
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q2.** One of my friends (a)/ are coming (b)/ to the party tonight. (c)/ No error (d)
**Answer:** (b)
**Solution:** "One of + plural noun" takes a singular verb — the subject is "One". Correct: "is coming".
**Trap type:** Intervening Phrase / Sound-Right
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q3.** The jury (a)/ has delivered (b)/ its verdict unanimously. (c)/ No error (d)
**Answer:** (d)
**Solution:** "Unanimously" signals the jury acted as one unit, so singular "has" and "its" are correct. This sentence is error-free — the trap is changing it.
**Trap type:** Over-thinking (Collective Noun)
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q4.** He is more superior (a)/ to his brother (b)/ in studies. (c)/ No error (d)
**Answer:** (a)
**Solution:** "Superior" is already comparative in meaning; it never takes "more" and is followed by "to", not "than". Correct: "He is superior to…".
**Trap type:** Sound-Right
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q5.** Neither of the two boys (a)/ have done (b)/ his homework. (c)/ No error (d)
**Answer:** (b)
**Solution:** "Neither of" takes a singular verb. Correct: "has done".
**Trap type:** Intervening Phrase
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q6.** Scarcely he had reached (a)/ the station (b)/ when the train left. (c)/ No error (d)
**Answer:** (a)
**Solution:** Sentences beginning with "Scarcely/Hardly" need inversion: "Scarcely *had he* reached".
**Trap type:** Sound-Right (Hindi word-order habit)
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q7.** The committee (a)/ were divided (b)/ in their opinion on the bill. (c)/ No error (d)
**Answer:** (d)
**Solution:** "Divided in their opinion" shows members acting individually, so plural "were" + "their" is correct. Error-free sentence.
**Trap type:** Over-thinking (Collective Noun)
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q8.** Each of the students (a)/ have submitted (b)/ the assignment on time. (c)/ No error (d)
**Answer:** (b)
**Solution:** "Each of + plural noun" takes a singular verb: "has submitted".
**Trap type:** Intervening Phrase
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q9.** I prefer (a)/ tea more than (b)/ coffee in the morning. (c)/ No error (d)
**Answer:** (b)
**Solution:** "Prefer" already contains comparison; use "to", not "more than". Correct: "I prefer tea to coffee".
**Trap type:** Sound-Right
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q10.** Bread and butter (a)/ are (b)/ his daily breakfast. (c)/ No error (d)
**Answer:** (b)
**Solution:** "Bread and butter" is one combined dish, so it takes a singular verb: "is his daily breakfast". (Two separate items would take "are".)
**Trap type:** Sound-Right / Over-thinking
**Source:** PYQ-style

## Applying the 15/45-Second Rule

**Q4 (15-second, Fact-based):** The moment you see "more superior", a logged rule fires: *superior/inferior/senior/junior/prefer → no "more", always "to"*. No re-reading, no hesitation — spot, mark, move. This is exactly the kind of entry your error log converts from a 45-second struggle into a 15-second reflex.

**Q3 (45-second, Inference-based):** Collective-noun questions demand a meaning check, not a reflex. Ask: *is the jury acting as one body or as individuals?* The word "unanimously" answers it — one body, singular verb, no error. Budget the full 45 seconds here; rushing an Over-thinking trap is how prepared students donate marks.

**Log takeaway:** after every mock, file each error under one of the four trap types above. Review the log after 2 days, then weekly — the pattern you discover *is* your personalised syllabus.
