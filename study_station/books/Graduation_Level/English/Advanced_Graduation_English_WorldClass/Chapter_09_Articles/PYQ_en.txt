# 📊 PYQ Analysis — Articles

## How Exams Test Articles (Pattern Analysis)

Articles look small, but examiners (SSC CGL, CHSL, IBPS PO/Clerk, RRB NTPC, State PCS prelims) use them to test *precision*. Across recent papers, questions on articles appear in four recurring formats:

1. **Fill in the blank** — one article missing in a sentence (the most common format).
2. **Error spotting** — the article error is hidden inside a long sentence with an intervening phrase.
3. **Sentence improvement** — the underlined article must be replaced or deleted.
4. **Cloze test** — 1–2 blanks in a passage are article-based.

**Sub-topics that repeat most often:**

- **Zero article traps** — before uncountable/abstract nouns used generally (*honesty is the best policy*, not *the honesty*).
- **"The" with superlatives, ordinals, and unique things** — *the best, the first, the sun*.
- **A/An by sound, not spelling** — *an hour, a university, an MBA, a one-rupee note*.
- **"The" before musical instruments** but not before games — *play the flute* vs *play chess*.
- **Fixed phrases with no article** — *go to school/church/bed* (purpose) vs *go to the school* (building).
- **"The" with rivers, oceans, mountain ranges, holy books, newspapers** — *the Ganga, the Himalayas, the Ramayana*.
- **Same noun, different meaning with/without "the"** — *few/a few, little/a little* style distinctions extended to articles.

**Examiner's favourite traps (in words, not numbers):**

- **Sound-Right trap:** an option that "sounds" correct when read aloud quickly but violates a rule (*an European country*).
- **Intervening-Phrase trap:** the noun is pushed far from the blank so you forget whether it is specific or general.
- **Over-thinking trap:** a simple rule (a/an by sound) dressed in a long, intimidating sentence.
- **Hindi-transfer trap:** Hindi has no articles, so candidates either drop "the" where English needs it or insert it where Hindi logic suggests emphasis.

## Top 10 High-Yield Questions (with Solutions & Trap Classification)

**Q1.** He is ___ honest man and ___ university student.
(a) a, a (b) an, a (c) an, an (d) a, an
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** "Honest" begins with a vowel *sound* (h is silent) → **an**. "University" begins with a consonant sound /juː/ → **a**. Article choice depends on pronunciation, not spelling.
**Trap type:** Sound-Right.
**Source:** PYQ-style (SSC pattern)

**Q2.** ___ Ganga is one of the longest rivers in India.
(a) A (b) An (c) The (d) No article
**Answer: (c)**
**Solution:** Names of rivers, seas, oceans and mountain ranges always take **the** — *the Ganga, the Himalayas, the Indian Ocean*.
**Trap type:** Over-thinking (candidates hesitate because a single proper noun usually takes no article).
**Source:** NCERT Class 7 English (grammar) / PYQ-style

**Q3.** She plays ___ guitar very well, but she plays ___ chess even better.
(a) the, the (b) the, no article (c) no article, the (d) a, no article
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** Musical instruments take **the** (*play the guitar*); games and sports take **no article** (*play chess, play cricket*).
**Trap type:** Sound-Right — "play the chess" feels parallel and tempts many.
**Source:** PYQ-style (Bank exam pattern)

**Q4.** ___ honesty of the clerk was never questioned.
(a) A (b) An (c) The (d) No article
**Answer: (c)**
**Solution:** Abstract nouns take no article when used generally (*Honesty is the best policy*), but here "honesty" is **particularised** by "of the clerk" → **the**.
**Trap type:** Intervening Phrase — the qualifying phrase hides after the noun.
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q5.** He goes to ___ school by bus every day. (He is a student.)
(a) a (b) an (c) the (d) no article
**Answer: (d)**
**Solution:** When school/church/hospital/bed is used for its **primary purpose**, no article is used. "Go to the school" would mean visiting the building for some other reason.
**Trap type:** Hindi-transfer — "वह स्कूल जाता है" gives no clue, so candidates insert "the" mechanically.
**Source:** NCERT Class 9 English (grammar) / PYQ-style

**Q6.** I had ___ European friend in college who knew ___ Sanskrit.
(a) a, no article (b) an, the (c) a, the (d) an, no article
**Answer: (a)**
**Solution:** "European" starts with /j/ sound → **a**. Names of languages take **no article** (*know Sanskrit*); "the Sanskrit" is wrong unless qualified (*the Sanskrit of Kalidasa*).
**Trap type:** Sound-Right (spelling suggests "an European").
**Source:** PYQ-style (SSC CHSL pattern)

**Q7.** ___ richer a person becomes, ___ more worried he often is.
(a) A, a (b) The, the (c) No article, no article (d) The, a
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** In the double-comparative structure "the + comparative … the + comparative", **the** is fixed on both sides.
**Trap type:** Over-thinking — candidates treat "the" here as pointing to a noun; it is a structural "the".
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q8.** Spot the error: "Gold (a)/ of this ring (b)/ is not pure. (c)/ No error (d)"
**Answer: (a)**
**Solution:** Material nouns take no article when used generally (*Gold is expensive*), but here "gold" is **particularised** by the phrase "of this ring", so it must be **The gold**. Part (a) should read "The gold".
**Trap type:** Intervening Phrase — the of-phrase that forces "the" sits away from the noun.
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q9.** ___ Ramayana is one of the two great epics of India.
(a) A (b) An (c) The (d) No article
**Answer: (c)**
**Solution:** Names of holy/epic books, newspapers and famous buildings take **the** — *the Ramayana, the Times of India, the Taj Mahal*.
**Trap type:** Over-thinking (proper noun → no article is the wrong default here).
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q10.** He is ___ best player in the team and ___ captain as well.
(a) the, the (b) the, no article (c) a, the (d) the, a
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** Superlatives take **the** (*the best*). But when a post/position (captain, president, monitor) follows "be/become/appoint" as a unique role, the article is dropped — *He is captain of the team*.
**Trap type:** Sound-Right — "the captain as well" feels natural and is the most-chosen wrong option.
**Source:** PYQ-style (SSC CGL pattern)

## Applying the 15/45-Second Rule

**15-second question (Fact-based) — Q2 above:**
"___ Ganga is…" → Rule recalled instantly: rivers take *the*. No sentence analysis needed. **Target: under 15 seconds.** If you spend more, you are over-thinking a direct rule.

**45-second question (Inference-based) — Q4 above:**
"___ honesty of the clerk…" → You must (1) notice the abstract noun, (2) check whether it is general or particularised, (3) spot the qualifying phrase "of the clerk", (4) then choose *the*. This needs two reasoning steps — **allow up to 45 seconds**, and never rush it into the "no article for abstract nouns" reflex.

**Strategy summary:** If the blank sits directly before a familiar category word (river, language, game, instrument, superlative) → 15-second rule question. If the noun is followed by an *of*-phrase, a relative clause, or the sentence contrasts general vs specific meaning → 45-second inference question.
