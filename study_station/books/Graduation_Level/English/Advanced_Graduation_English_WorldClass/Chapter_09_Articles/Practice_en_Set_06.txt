126. Choose the correct sentence.
(a) The honesty is the best policy. (b) An honesty is a best policy. (c) Honesty is the best policy. (d) The honesty is best policy.
Answer: (c)
Solution: Abstract nouns used in a general sense take no article. "Honesty" here means the quality in general, so no article is needed. The first option wrongly adds "the" before an abstract noun used generally.
Source: NCERT Class 10 English Grammar

127. Choose the correct sentence.
(a) He is an university student. (b) He is a university student. (c) He is the university student. (d) He is university student.
Answer: (b)
Solution: "University" begins with a consonant sound /juː/, so it takes "a", not "an". The spelling starts with a vowel letter but the sound decides the article — a classic examiner trap.
Source: PYQ-style

128. Spot the error: Part P: The more you read, / Part Q: the more knowledge / Part R: you will acquire the wisdom. / Part S: No error
(a) Part P (b) Part Q (c) No error (d) Part R
Answer: (d)
Solution: Part R is faulty. In the double comparative structure "the more… the more…", the second clause should be parallel: "the more wisdom you will acquire". The extra "the" before "wisdom" breaks the structure.

129. Choose the correct sentence.
(a) She plays an guitar very well. (b) She plays the guitar very well. (c) She plays a guitar very well. (d) She plays guitar very well.
Answer: (b)
Solution: Musical instruments take the definite article: "play the guitar, the piano, the flute". The second option drops the required article.

130. Choose the correct sentence.
(a) The gold of this ring is pure. (b) Gold of this ring is pure. (c) A gold of this ring is pure. (d) An gold of this ring is pure.
Answer: (a)
Solution: When a material noun is particularised (the gold of THIS ring), it takes "the". Used generally, "gold" takes no article — the context here makes it specific.

131. Spot the error: Part P: He is / Part Q: one of the best player / Part R: in the team. / Part S: No error
(a) Part Q (b) Part R (c) Part P (d) No error
Answer: (a)
Solution: Part Q should read "one of the best players". After "one of the + superlative", the noun must be plural. This is a frequent trap where the singular "player" sounds right but is wrong.

132. Choose the correct sentence.
(a) I had a lunch at 2 p.m. (b) I had the lunch at 2 p.m. (c) I had lunch at 2 p.m. (d) I had an lunch at 2 p.m.
Answer: (c)
Solution: Names of meals (breakfast, lunch, dinner) take no article when used in the ordinary sense. An article appears only when the meal is particularised: "The lunch we had yesterday was heavy."
Source: NCERT Class 10 English Grammar

133. Choose the correct sentence.
(a) The Mount Everest is the highest peak. (b) An Mount Everest is the highest peak. (c) A Mount Everest is the highest peak. (d) Mount Everest is the highest peak.
Answer: (d)
Solution: Individual mountain peaks take no article (Mount Everest, Mount K2), but mountain RANGES take "the" (the Himalayas, the Alps). Examiners exploit this contrast.
Source: NCERT Class 7 Geography

134. Choose the correct sentence.
(a) He goes to the school to study. (b) He goes to an school to study. (c) He goes to a school to study. (d) He goes to school to study.
Answer: (d)
Solution: Institutions used for their primary purpose take no article: go to school (to study), go to prison (as a prisoner), go to church (to pray). "The school" would mean visiting the building for another purpose.
Source: NCERT Class 9 English Grammar

135. Spot the error: Part P: The Ganges / Part Q: is one of the / Part R: holiest river in India. / Part S: No error
(a) Part R (b) Part Q (c) No error (d) Part P
Answer: (a)
Solution: Part R should be "holiest rivers". After "one of the + superlative", a plural noun is required. "The Ganges" in Part P is correct because river names take "the".

136. Choose the correct sentence.
(a) He is a best singer in the choir. (b) He is the best singer in the choir. (c) He is an best singer in the choir. (d) He is best singer in the choir.
Answer: (b)
Solution: Superlative adjectives always take "the": the best, the tallest, the most honest. The first option wrongly uses "a" before a superlative.

137. Choose the correct sentence.
(a) The poor deserves our help. (b) The poor deserve our help. (c) Poor deserve our help. (d) A poor deserves our help.
Answer: (b)
Solution: "The + adjective" denotes a whole class (the poor = poor people) and takes a plural verb. The first option has the right article but a singular verb — a double trap.
Source: PYQ-style

138. Choose the correct sentence.
(a) I saw an European tourist at the station. (b) I saw a European tourist at the station. (c) I saw the European tourist at a station. (d) I saw European tourist at the station.
Answer: (b)
Solution: "European" begins with the consonant sound /jʊə/, so it takes "a". Like "university" and "one-rupee", the sound — not the letter — decides. A classic sound-based trap.
Source: PYQ-style

139. Spot the error: Part P: This is / Part Q: the most unique / Part R: painting in the gallery. / Part S: No error
(a) Part P (b) Part Q (c) Part R (d) No error
Answer: (b)
Solution: Part Q is wrong. "Unique" is an absolute adjective — something is either unique or not — so it cannot take comparative or superlative degrees. Say "a unique painting" or "the most unusual painting".
Source: PYQ-style

140. Choose the correct sentence.
(a) He returned home by bus. (b) He returned home by a bus. (c) He returned home by an bus. (d) He returned home by the bus.
Answer: (a)
Solution: With "by + means of transport", no article is used: by bus, by train, by air. But with other prepositions an article appears: "in a bus", "on the train".
Source: NCERT Class 9 English Grammar

141. Choose the correct sentence.
(a) Man is mortal. (b) The man is mortal. (c) A man is mortal. (d) An man is mortal.
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Man" meaning the whole human race takes no article. "The man" would refer to a specific male person. Examiners test this distinction repeatedly.
Source: NCERT Class 10 English Grammar

142. Choose the correct sentence.
(a) She is an heir to a large fortune. (b) She is a heir to a large fortune. (c) She is the heir to large fortune. (d) She is heir to a large fortune.
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Heir" has a silent 'h' and begins with a vowel sound /eə/, so it takes "an". Words like honest, hour, honour behave the same way — a favourite sound-based trap.
Source: PYQ-style

143. Spot the error: Part P: The teacher as well as / Part Q: the students were / Part R: present in the hall. / Part S: No error
(a) Part P (b) No error (c) Part Q (d) Part R
Answer: (c)
Solution: Part Q should have "was". With "as well as", the verb agrees with the FIRST subject ("the teacher", singular). The intervening phrase "as well as the students" is an examiner trap designed to pull you toward a plural verb.
Source: PYQ-style

144. Choose the correct sentence.
(a) A Hindi is sweet language. (b) The Hindi is a sweet language. (c) Hindi is a sweet language. (d) An Hindi is a sweet language.
Answer: (c)
Solution: Names of languages take no article: Hindi, English, French. But "the Hindi language" is correct when the word "language" follows — the article then belongs to "language".
Source: NCERT Class 9 English Grammar

145. Choose the correct sentence.
(a) He is same man who helped me. (b) He is a same man who helped me. (c) He is the same man who helped me. (d) He is an same man who helped me.
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Same" is always preceded by "the": the same man, the same day. It never takes "a" or "an" because it expresses identity, not one-among-many.
Source: NCERT Class 8 English Grammar

146. Choose the correct sentence.
(a) The United States is a powerful nation. (b) The United States are a powerful nation. (c) United States is a powerful nation. (d) An United States is a powerful nation.
Answer: (a)
Solution: Plural-looking country names (the United States, the Netherlands, the Philippines) take "the" and a SINGULAR verb. The first option traps you with the plural form into choosing a plural verb.
Source: PYQ-style

147. Spot the error: Part P: He gave me / Part Q: an useful advice / Part R: about my career. / Part S: No error
(a) Part P (b) Part R (c) Part Q (d) No error
Answer: (c)
Solution: Part Q has two errors packed together: "useful" begins with a consonant sound /juː/ so it needs "a", and "advice" is uncountable so it takes no article at all. Correct form: "gave me useful advice" or "a useful piece of advice".
Source: PYQ-style

148. Choose the correct sentence.
(a) An rice of this field is of good quality. (b) Rice of this field is of good quality. (c) A rice of this field is of good quality. (d) The rice of this field is of good quality.
Answer: (d)
Solution: Material nouns take no article in general use ("Rice is grown here"), but when particularised by an of-phrase ("the rice of this field"), they take "the".
Source: NCERT Class 10 English Grammar

149. Choose the correct sentence.
(a) He goes to prison to meet his brother. (b) He goes to an prison to meet his brother. (c) He goes to a prison to meet his brother. (d) He goes to the prison to meet his brother.
Answer: (d)
Solution: Visiting a prison for a purpose OTHER than its primary one (meeting someone, not serving a sentence) requires "the". "Goes to prison" would mean he is a prisoner. This primary-purpose contrast is a hard-level favourite.
Source: PYQ-style

150. Spot the error: Part P: Neither of the two boys / Part Q: have brought / Part R: the book. / Part S: No error
(a) No error (b) Part P (c) Part R (d) Part Q
Answer: (d)
Solution: Part Q should have "has". "Neither of" takes a singular verb even though a plural noun ("boys") follows — the intervening plural noun is the trap. "The book" in Part R is correct as it refers to a specific book.
