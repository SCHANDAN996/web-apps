Card 1
Front: When do we use "a" vs "an"?
Back: "A" before consonant SOUND, "an" before vowel SOUND. Sound matters, not spelling: "a university" (yoo-sound), "an hour" (silent h).

Card 2
Front: Why is it "an MBA" but "a BA"?
Back: "MBA" starts with the sound "em" (vowel sound) → an. "BA" starts with "bee" (consonant sound) → a.

Card 3
Front: 🧠 MNEMONIC — Which things take "the" because they are unique? (Trick: "SUN-MOON-EARTH Rule")
Back: Unique things in the universe take "the": the sun, the moon, the earth, the sky, the equator. If there's only ONE of it, give it "the".

Card 4
Front: ⚠️ EXAMINER'S TRAP — "He is ___ honest man." Which article, and why do students get it wrong?
Back: "an" — because "h" is silent in "honest", so the word begins with a vowel sound. Trap: students see the letter "h" and write "a honest".

Card 5
Front: Which article is used before superlative degrees?
Back: "The" — the best, the tallest, the most beautiful. Example: She is the smartest girl in the class.

Card 6
Front: When do we use NO article (zero article) before nouns?
Back: Before uncountable/abstract nouns and plural nouns used in a general sense: "Honesty is the best policy", "Dogs are faithful animals", "I like tea."

Card 7
Front: Which rivers, oceans, and mountain ranges take "the"?
Back: All of them: the Ganga, the Indian Ocean, the Himalayas. But single mountain peaks take NO article: Mount Everest, not "the Mount Everest".

Card 8
Front: ⚠️ EXAMINER'S TRAP — "___ gold of this ring is pure." Is an article needed?
Back: Yes — "The gold of this ring is pure." When a general noun is made SPECIFIC by a phrase/clause after it, use "the". Trap: students omit the article thinking gold is uncountable.

Card 9
Front: What article goes before names of countries?
Back: Normally none: India, Japan, France. But "the" with unions/republics/kingdoms: the USA, the UK, the UAE, the Republic of Ireland.

Card 10
Front: 🧠 MNEMONIC — Remember "the" with musical instruments. (Trick: "Play THE Piano")
Back: With musical instruments after "play", always use "the": play the guitar, play the flute. But with games/sports, NO article: play cricket, play chess.

Card 11
Front: When does a proper noun take "the"?
Back: When used as a common noun for comparison: "Kalidas is the Shakespeare of India." Also with famous buildings/newspapers/holy books: the Taj Mahal, the Hindu, the Ramayana.

Card 12
Front: "He goes to school" vs "He goes to the school" — what's the difference?
Back: No article = primary purpose (he goes there to study). "The" = visiting the building for another purpose (e.g., a parent goes to the school to meet the teacher). Same rule: church, hospital, prison, bed.

Card 13
Front: Which article before ordinal numbers (first, second, third)?
Back: "The" — the first boy, the second chapter. Example: February is the second month of the year.

Card 14
Front: "___ little money he had was stolen." Fill in the blank and explain.
Back: "The little" — "the little" means "all that there was" (some, though small). "Little" = almost none (negative); "a little" = some (positive); "the little" = whatever little existed.

Card 15
Front: What is the rule for articles with "few / a few / the few"?
Back: Few = almost none (negative); a few = some (positive); the few = all of the small number. "The few friends he had helped him."

Card 16
Front: When do we repeat the article before two nouns?
Back: Repeat when two DIFFERENT persons/things are meant: "The poet and the philosopher are dead" (two people). One article = same person: "The poet and philosopher is dead" (one person who is both).

Card 17
Front: Which article before languages and subjects of study?
Back: No article: "She speaks English", "He studies physics." But "the" when referring to the language as a body: "The English language is widely spoken."

Card 18
Front: ⚠️ EXAMINER'S TRAP — "What ___ beautiful weather!" Which article fits?
Back: NO article — "weather" is uncountable, so "a" is wrong. Trap: students write "What a beautiful weather!" Similarly: "What good news!" (not "a good news").

Card 19
Front: Which article is used with meals (breakfast, lunch, dinner)?
Back: Normally none: "We had lunch at noon." But "the" when specific: "The dinner we had yesterday was delicious"; "a" when describing: "We had a heavy breakfast."

Card 20
Front: "He is ___ European." Which article and why?
Back: "A" — "European" begins with a "yoo" sound (consonant sound), so "a European", like "a union", "a one-rupee note", "a useful book".
