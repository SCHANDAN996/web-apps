# 🧒 Feynman Technique — Articles Explained to a 12-Year-Old

## The Hardest Concept: "The" vs "A/An" vs No Article

Imagine you have a magic torch 🔦. When you shine it on something, everyone in the room can see **exactly which thing** you mean.

- **"A/An"** = the torch is OFF. You're talking about *any* one thing, not a specific one.
  👉 "I saw **a** dog." (Which dog? Nobody knows. Just some dog.)

- **"The"** = the torch is ON. Both the speaker and listener know *exactly which* thing.
  👉 "I saw **the** dog." (The one from your street. That specific dog.)

- **No article (zero article)** = you're talking about things *in general*, like a whole category.
  👉 "**Dogs** are loyal." (All dogs, everywhere, in general.)

### The Daily-Life Test 🏠

You're at home and say: *"Mummy, where is **the** milk?"*
Why "the"? Because there's only ONE milk in your house that both of you know about — the torch is ON.

But at the shop you say: *"I want to buy **a** pen."*
Why "a"? Any pen will do — you haven't chosen a specific one yet. Torch OFF.

Then: *"**Pens** are cheaper here."* — pens in general, so NO article.

### The Unforgettable Trick: **"First A, Then THE"** 🎯

When you mention something for the **first time**, use **a/an** (it's new, unknown).
When you mention it **again**, use **the** (now everyone knows it).

> "I bought **a** shirt yesterday. **The** shirt is blue."

First time → **a**. Second time → **the**. That's it. This one trick helps with a large share of article questions in exams — examiners love testing whether you can spot a "second mention."

### Why "An" before some consonant-letters? 🔊

Articles follow the **SOUND**, not the spelling:
- "**an** hour" — 'h' is silent, so it starts with a vowel *sound* /aʊə/
- "**a** university" — sounds like "yoo-niversity", a consonant *sound* /j/
- "**an** M.P." — pronounced "em-pee", vowel sound /e/

**Trick: Close your eyes and SAY the word. Your ear decides the article, not your eye.**
