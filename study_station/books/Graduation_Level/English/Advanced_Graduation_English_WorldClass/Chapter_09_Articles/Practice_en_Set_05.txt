101. Choose the correct sentence:
(a) He is a European who speaks French fluently. (b) He is an European who speaks the French fluently. (c) He is an European who speaks French fluently. (d) He is a European who speaks the French fluently.
Answer: (a)
Solution: "European" begins with a consonant sound /j/ (yoo), so it takes "a", not "an". Language names like French take no article unless followed by "language". The trap is choosing "an" by looking at the letter E instead of the sound.
Source: PYQ-style

102. Spot the error: The honesty (P) / is the best policy (Q) / in every walk of life. (R) / No error (S)
(a) Part R (b) Part Q (c) Part P (d) No error
Answer: (c)
Solution: Abstract nouns like honesty, truth and beauty take no article when used in a general sense. Part P should read simply "Honesty". This is a classic examiner trap where the proverb misleads students into keeping "The".
Source: PYQ-style

103. Choose the correctly framed sentence:
(a) The more you practise, the more fluent you become. (b) More you practise, more fluent you become. (c) The more you practise, more fluent you become. (d) More you practise, the more fluent you become.
Answer: (a)
Solution: In the double comparative structure "the + comparative … the + comparative", "the" is mandatory before both comparatives. Dropping either one is a frequent exam error.

104. Fill in the blank: Mount Everest is ______ highest peak in the world.
(a) a (b) an (c) no article (d) the
Answer: (d)
Solution: Superlative adjectives always take "the". "Highest" is a superlative, so "the highest peak" is correct.
Source: NCERT Class 9 English

105. Spot the error: He goes to the school (P) / every day (Q) / to study mathematics. (R) / No error (S)
(a) Part Q (b) Part P (c) Part R (d) No error
Answer: (b)
Solution: When a place is visited for its primary purpose (school for studying, hospital for treatment, prison for punishment), no article is used. Since he goes to study, it should be "goes to school". "The school" would mean he visits the building for some other purpose.
Source: PYQ-style

106. Choose the correct option: ______ Ganga is one of ______ longest rivers in India.
(a) A, the (b) The, the (c) The, a (d) No article, the
Answer: (b)
Solution: Names of rivers always take "the" (the Ganga), and superlatives take "the" (the longest). Both blanks need "the".

107. Fill in the blank: He is ______ M.P. from ______ Uttar Pradesh.
(a) an, no article (b) a, the (c) an, the (d) a, no article
Answer: (a)
Solution: "M.P." is pronounced "em-pee", beginning with a vowel sound, so it takes "an". State names like Uttar Pradesh take no article. The trap is choosing "a" by looking at the consonant letter M instead of its sound.

108. Choose the correct sentence:
(a) The gold of this ring is pure. (b) Gold of this ring is pure. (c) A gold of this ring is pure. (d) An gold of this ring is pure.
Answer: (a)
Solution: When a material noun is particularised (the gold of this ring, not gold in general), it takes "the". Material nouns used generally take no article, but here the reference is specific.

109. Spot the error: The rich (P) / should help (Q) / poors of the society. (R) / No error (S)
(a) Part R (b) Part Q (c) Part P (d) No error
Answer: (a)
Solution: "The + adjective" denotes a whole class (the rich, the poor). "Poor" used this way is already plural in sense and never takes "-s". Part R should be "the poor of society". This is a favourite examiner trap.

110. Fill in the blank: ______ honesty of the clerk was never questioned.
(a) A (b) An (c) No article (d) The
Answer: (d)
Solution: Although abstract nouns normally take no article, when they are particularised by an "of-phrase" (the honesty of the clerk), they take "the".

111. Choose the correct sentence:
(a) I had a lunch at an hotel near the station. (b) I had lunch at a hotel near the station. (c) I had the lunch at a hotel near the station. (d) I had lunch at an hotel near the station.
Answer: (b)
Solution: Names of meals (breakfast, lunch, dinner) take no article when used generally. "Hotel" is pronounced with an aspirated h, so it takes "a", not "an".

112. Fill in the blanks: ______ United States and ______ United Kingdom are permanent members of the Security Council.
(a) The, a (b) A, the (c) The, the (d) No article, no article
Answer: (c)
Solution: Countries whose names are plural or contain words like "States", "Kingdom" or "Republic" take "the": the United States, the United Kingdom, the Czech Republic.

113. Spot the error: He plays (P) / the cricket (Q) / every Sunday morning. (R) / No error (S)
(a) Part P (b) Part R (c) Part Q (d) No error
Answer: (c)
Solution: Names of games and sports take no article: play cricket, play football. Note the contrast with musical instruments, which take "the": play the flute. Examiners exploit this contrast.

114. Choose the correct sentence:
(a) She plays flute in the evening. (b) She plays a flute in the evening. (c) She plays the flute in the evening. (d) She plays an flute in the evening.
Answer: (c)
Solution: Musical instruments take "the" after "play": play the flute, play the guitar. Games take no article — the two rules are often tested together.

115. Fill in the blank: ______ man who called yesterday is my uncle.
(a) A (b) The (c) An (d) No article
Answer: (b)
Solution: When a noun is made definite by a following phrase or clause ("who called yesterday"), it takes "the". This is the rule of second mention / particularisation.
Source: NCERT Class 9 English

116. Choose the correct sentence:
(a) The breakfast we had at hotel was the delicious. (b) Breakfast we had at hotel was delicious. (c) A breakfast we had at the hotel was delicious. (d) The breakfast we had at the hotel was delicious.
Answer: (d)
Solution: Meals normally take no article, but when particularised ("the breakfast we had at the hotel"), they take "the". "Hotel" is a countable singular noun needing "the" here; "delicious" is an adjective and takes no article.
Source: PYQ-style

117. Spot the error: Kalidas is (P) / a Shakespeare (Q) / of India. (R) / No error (S)
(a) Part P (b) Part R (c) Part Q (d) No error
Answer: (c)
Solution: When a proper noun is used as a common noun to compare two persons, it takes "the": "Kalidas is the Shakespeare of India." Using "a" weakens the comparison and is the standard trap here.

118. Fill in the blanks: ______ iron is ______ useful metal.
(a) No article, a (b) The, a (c) The, the (d) No article, an
Answer: (a)
Solution: Material nouns used in a general sense take no article ("Iron is…"). "Useful" begins with a consonant sound /j/ (yoo), so it takes "a", not "an" — a sound-based trap.

119. Choose the correct sentence:
(a) He is best player in the team. (b) He is a best player in the team. (c) He is an best player in the team. (d) He is the best player in the team.
Answer: (d)
Solution: Superlatives require "the": the best, the tallest, the most honest. Omitting "the" before a superlative is among the most common errors tested.

120. Spot the error: The more (P) / he earns, the more (Q) / he wants the money. (R) / No error (S)
(a) Part P (b) Part Q (c) Part R (d) No error
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Money" here is used in a general sense, so it takes no article: "he wants money". "The money" would refer to some specific sum already mentioned. The double-comparative frame in Parts P and Q is correct and is included to distract.

121. Fill in the blank: ______ Pacific Ocean is larger than ______ Atlantic Ocean.
(a) No article, the (b) A, the (c) The, a (d) The, the
Answer: (d)
Solution: Names of oceans, seas, rivers, gulfs and mountain ranges take "the": the Pacific, the Atlantic, the Himalayas, the Persian Gulf.

122. Choose the correct sentence:
(a) What a beautiful scenery it is! (b) What beautiful scenery it is! (c) What the beautiful scenery it is! (d) What an beautiful scenery it is!
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Scenery" is uncountable, so it cannot take "a" or "an". In exclamations with uncountable or plural nouns, "what" is used without any article. This is a classic examiner trap built on the "What a…!" pattern.
Source: PYQ-style

123. Fill in the blanks: He goes to ______ church on Sundays, but yesterday he went to ______ church to attend a wedding.
(a) no article, the (b) the, the (c) the, no article (d) no article, no article
Answer: (a)
Solution: First blank: going to church for its primary purpose (prayer) takes no article. Second blank: going for a different purpose (a wedding) makes the building a specific place, so "the" is required. Both halves of the rule are tested in one sentence.
Source: PYQ-style

124. Spot the error: One should (P) / always speak (Q) / truth in life. (R) / No error (S)
(a) Part P (b) Part Q (c) No error (d) Part R
Answer: (d)
Solution: Certain abstract nouns are idiomatically used with "the" in fixed expressions: speak the truth, tell a lie. Part R should read "the truth". The general "no article before abstract nouns" rule misleads students here — a deliberate trap.
Source: PYQ-style

125. Choose the correct sentence:
(a) The Delhi is the capital of the India. (b) Delhi is the capital of India. (c) The Delhi is capital of India. (d) Delhi is capital of the India.
Answer: (b)
Solution: Names of cities and most countries take no article (Delhi, India). But "capital" here is a specific, unique position, so it takes "the": "the capital of India". Adding "the" to city or country names is the trap.
