76. Choose the correctly spelt word.
(a) Maintainance (b) Maintenance (c) Maintenence (d) Maintennance
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Maintainance" tempts because the verb is "maintain", but the noun drops the second "i" and takes "-enance": main + tenance. The correct spelling is Maintenance.

77. Select the word closest in meaning to METICULOUS.
(a) Careless (b) Superficial (c) Hasty (d) Thorough
Answer: (d)
Solution: "Superficial" tempts as it relates to detail, but it means shallow — the opposite. Meticulous means showing great attention to detail, so the answer is Thorough.

78. Select the word opposite in meaning to FRUGAL.
(a) Thrifty (b) Economical (c) Extravagant (d) Prudent
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Thrifty" and "prudent" tempt because they look like opposites, but both are near-synonyms of frugal. The antonym is Extravagant.

79. Spot the error: Part P — One of my friend / Part Q — has gone / Part R — to Delhi yesterday. / Part S — No error
(a) No error (b) Part Q (c) Part R (d) Part P
Answer: (d)
Solution: Part R tempts because "has gone... yesterday" is also wrong, but the first error is in Part P: "one of" must be followed by a plural noun — "one of my friends". So the error lies in Part P.

80. Choose the correct passive form: "The manager signed the cheque."
(a) The cheque was being signed by the manager. (b) The cheque is signed by the manager. (c) The cheque had signed by the manager. (d) The cheque was signed by the manager.
Answer: (d)
Solution: "Was being signed" tempts as it uses "was", but that is past continuous passive; the active sentence is simple past, needing "was + V3". The correct passive is "The cheque was signed by the manager."
Source: PYQ-style

81. Select the correctly punctuated indirect speech: He said, "I am tired."
(a) He said that he was tired. (b) He said that he is tired. (c) He said that he had tired. (d) He said that he has been tired.
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Is tired" tempts when the reporting verb feels current, but "said" is past, so backshift applies: am → was. The correct indirect speech is "He said that he was tired."
Source: PYQ-style

82. Fill in the blank: Neither the teacher nor the students ______ present.
(a) were (b) is (c) was (d) has been
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Was" tempts because "teacher" comes first, but with neither...nor the verb agrees with the nearer subject, "students" (plural). So the answer is were.

Solution: "To celebrate all night" tempts because of "midnight", but the idiom refers to labour, not festivity. It means to work or study late into the night.

85. Fill in the blank: He has been working here ______ 2015.

91. Select the correctly framed question.
(a) Why you are late? (b) Why late you are? (c) Why are you late? (d) Why you late are?
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Why you are late?" tempts Hindi speakers because Hindi keeps the statement order ("tum late kyun ho?"), but English questions need inversion: auxiliary before subject. The correct form is "Why are you late?"

97. Choose the option that best expresses the meaning: "The new policy, albeit well-intentioned, failed to curb inflation."

Solution: The first option tempts because "well-intentioned" sounds positive, but "albeit" means "although" and signals contrast with failure. The correct meaning is: although well-intentioned, the policy did not reduce inflation.

Solution: "Did I reach" tempts because inversion is needed, but "hardly...when" pairs with past perfect, not simple past. The correct form is had I reached.

99. In the sentence "The data were analysed carefully," the verb "were" is:
(a) Incorrect, because data is always singular (b) Incorrect, because analysed needs "was" (c) Correct, because data is the plural of datum (d) Correct, because data is a collective noun
Answer: (c)
Solution: The first option tempts because modern usage often treats "data" as singular, but in formal exam grammar "data" is the plural of "datum" and takes a plural verb. The verb is correct because data is the plural of datum.
Source: PYQ-style

100. Choose the sentence with correct subject-verb agreement.
(a) The quality of the mangoes was poor. (b) The quality of the mangoes were poor. (c) The quality of the mangoes have been poor. (d) The quality of the mangoes are poor.
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Were poor" tempts because "mangoes" (plural) sits just before the verb — the classic intervening-phrase trap — but the subject is "quality" (singular). The correct sentence is "The quality of the mangoes was poor."
