1. What is the core purpose of a Revision Tracker?
(a) To record how many hours you studied each day (b) To list all the books you plan to buy (c) To schedule and monitor spaced reviews of learned topics (d) To count the total pages of a syllabus
Answer: (c)
Solution: Many students pick the study-hours option because trackers feel like diaries, but hours logged say nothing about retention. A Revision Tracker exists to plan repeated reviews at increasing intervals so memory stays strong, so the answer is to schedule and monitor spaced reviews of learned topics.
Source: PYQ-style

2. In spaced repetition, the gap between two successive reviews should generally:
(a) Increase each time (b) Stay exactly the same (c) Decrease each time (d) Be chosen randomly
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is choosing equal gaps, which feels organised but wastes effort on material you already know. As memory strengthens, intervals grow — for example 1 day, then 3 days, then 7 days — so the answer is that the gap should increase each time.
Source: PYQ-style

3. Active Recall means:
(a) Re-reading the chapter many times (b) Highlighting important lines in the book (c) Testing yourself by retrieving information from memory (d) Listening to a lecture twice
Answer: (c)
Solution: Re-reading and highlighting are tempting because they feel productive, but they create only familiarity, not memory. Active Recall is the deliberate act of pulling information out of your brain, so the answer is testing yourself by retrieving information from memory.
Source: PYQ-style

4. The "Forgetting Curve" was described by which psychologist?
(a) Sigmund Freud (b) Ivan Pavlov (c) Hermann Ebbinghaus (d) B. F. Skinner
Answer: (c)
Solution: Freud is the tempting wrong choice because he is the most famous name in psychology, but he worked on the unconscious mind, not memory decay. Hermann Ebbinghaus experimentally plotted how quickly we forget new information, so the answer is Hermann Ebbinghaus.
Source: PYQ-style

5. According to the Forgetting Curve, without revision we forget most new information within:
(a) A few minutes (b) A few days (c) A few months (d) A few years
Answer: (b)
Solution: Choosing "a few months" is the over-optimistic trap; memory decays far faster than learners expect. Ebbinghaus showed the steepest loss happens in the first days after learning, so the answer is a few days.
Source: PYQ-style

6. Which of these is the best first revision interval after learning a topic?
(a) Never revise (b) After 6 months (c) After 1 year (d) After 24 hours
Answer: (d)
Solution: Waiting months is the trap that lets the forgetting curve wipe out most of what you learned. The first review must come quickly, ideally within a day, to reset the curve, so the answer is after 24 hours.
Source: PYQ-style

7. A "Blurting Sheet" is used to:
(a) Copy notes neatly from the textbook (b) Write down everything you remember about a topic from memory (c) Make a timetable for the week (d) List your exam centre details
Answer: (b)
Solution: Neat note-copying is the tempting option because it looks studious, but it is passive. A Blurting Sheet means closing the book and dumping everything you can recall, then checking gaps, so the answer is writing down everything you remember about a topic from memory.

8. The Feynman Technique tests your understanding by asking you to:
(a) Memorise definitions word for word (b) Solve only difficult questions (c) Explain the concept in simple language as if teaching a child (d) Read the topic five times
Answer: (c)
Solution: Word-for-word memorisation is the trap — it hides gaps in understanding behind fancy language. Feynman's idea is that if you cannot explain something simply, you do not truly know it, so the answer is explaining the concept in simple language as if teaching a child.

9. Which colour coding is commonly suggested in a Revision Tracker for a topic you keep forgetting?
(a) Red (b) Green (c) Blue (d) White
Answer: (a)
Solution: Green is the tempting wrong choice, but green signals mastery, not weakness. Red flags the topics that need urgent re-review, so the answer is red.

10. Re-reading a chapter for the fifth time without testing yourself is an example of:
(a) Passive revision (b) Spaced Repetition (c) Active Recall (d) Interleaving
Answer: (a)
Solution: Students often label any contact with the book as "revision", which is the trap here. Without retrieval, re-reading is passive and gives an illusion of competence, so the answer is passive revision.

11. "Interleaving" in revision means:
(a) Mixing different topics or subjects within a study session (b) Studying one subject for the whole month (c) Skipping difficult chapters (d) Studying only at night
Answer: (a)
Solution: Blocking — one subject for weeks — feels comfortable and is the common trap. Interleaving mixes topics, forcing the brain to discriminate between problem types, so the answer is mixing different topics or subjects within a study session.
Source: PYQ-style

12. The 15/45-second rule in exams suggests spending about 15 seconds on:
(a) Inference-based questions (b) Fact-based recall questions (c) Reading comprehension passages (d) Essay questions
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is applying the short time to inference questions, which actually need more thought. Direct fact-recall items should be answered almost instantly, so the answer is fact-based recall questions.
Source: PYQ-style

13. In the 15/45-second rule, inference-based questions deserve about:
(a) 45 seconds (b) 15 seconds (c) 5 seconds (d) 10 minutes
Answer: (a)
Solution: Choosing 15 seconds is the trap of treating all questions alike. Inference questions require weighing options, so they earn the longer slot, and the answer is 45 seconds.

14. Which of the following is the best sign that a topic can be marked "mastered" in your tracker?
(a) You have read it three times (b) You can recall and apply it correctly in a test without seeing notes (c) You have highlighted the whole chapter (d) You attended a class on it
Answer: (b)
Solution: Reading three times is the classic illusion-of-competence trap. True mastery is demonstrated by correct retrieval and application under test conditions, so the answer is that you can recall and apply it correctly in a test without seeing notes.

15. Flashcards work mainly because they force:
(a) Speed reading (b) Group discussion (c) Neat handwriting (d) Retrieval practice
Answer: (d)
Solution: Speed reading is the tempting distractor since flashcards are quick, but speed is not the mechanism. Each card makes you pull an answer from memory, so the answer is retrieval practice.

16. A good Revision Tracker entry for each topic should include:
(a) The name of the book's author (b) The price of the textbook (c) The date of the next planned review (d) The number of pages in the book
Answer: (c)
Solution: Book details are the irrelevant trap options; they never drive memory. A tracker must tell you when to review next, so the answer is the date of the next planned review.

17. Which statement best describes the "testing effect"?
(a) Tests only measure learning, they never strengthen it (b) Taking tests on material strengthens memory more than re-studying it (c) Tests should be avoided until the final exam (d) Testing works only for mathematics
Answer: (b)
Solution: The belief that tests merely measure is the trap that makes students avoid self-testing. Research shows retrieval during tests itself strengthens memory, so the answer is that taking tests on material strengthens memory more than re-studying it.
Source: PYQ-style

18. If you get a question wrong in a practice set, the best tracker action is to:
(a) Ignore it and move on (b) Change your study material immediately (c) Delete the topic from the syllabus (d) Mark the topic for an earlier re-review
Answer: (d)
Solution: Ignoring errors is the most common trap; a wrong answer is precise data about a weak spot. The correct response is to pull that topic's next review closer, so the answer is to mark the topic for an earlier re-review.
Source: PYQ-style

19. Which of these is a common "examiner's trap" style in English error-spotting questions?
(a) An intervening phrase between subject and verb (b) Very short sentences (c) Simple present tense only (d) Questions with no options
Answer: (a)
Solution: Short simple sentences are the trap option — they rarely hide errors. Examiners insert phrases like "along with his friends" to make you match the verb to the wrong noun, so the answer is an intervening phrase between subject and verb.
Source: PYQ-style

20. The best time to revise a topic you learned this morning is:
(a) Tonight or tomorrow (b) After the exam (c) After one year (d) Only if you feel like it
Answer: (a)
Solution: Waiting for motivation is the trap; motivation fades faster than memory. The first review should come within about a day, so the answer is tonight or tomorrow.

21. Ravi revised a grammar chapter on 1 March. Using a 1-3-7 day spaced schedule, on which date should his third review fall?
(a) 2 March (b) 4 March (c) 15 March (d) 8 March
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is picking 4 March, which is only the second review (day 3). Reviews fall on 2 March (day 1), 4 March (day 3) and 8 March (day 7), so the third review is on 8 March.

22. Meena scores 90% in re-reading-based self-study but only 55% when she tests herself with closed books. What does this gap mainly indicate?
(a) She should stop testing herself (b) Testing is harmful for her (c) Re-reading gave her an illusion of competence (d) Her syllabus is complete
Answer: (c)
Solution: Stopping self-testing is the dangerous trap — it protects the feeling of knowing while hiding the weakness. The gap shows familiarity was mistaken for mastery, so the answer is that re-reading gave her an illusion of competence.

23. A tracker shows Topic A reviewed 5 times (all correct) and Topic B reviewed once (wrong). With limited time, the smarter choice is to:
(a) Review Topic A again because it is your favourite (b) Start a brand-new topic instead (c) Review neither, since both were already studied (d) Review Topic B because it is weak and under-reviewed
Answer: (d)
Solution: Reviewing the strong favourite topic is the comfort trap — it feels good but adds little score. Time goes where errors and low review counts are, so the answer is to review Topic B because it is weak and under-reviewed.

24. In an exam, you face a direct vocabulary question you know instantly and a tough inference question. Under the 15/45-second rule you should:
(a) Spend 45 seconds on the vocabulary question first (b) Answer the vocabulary question in about 15 seconds and reserve about 45 seconds for the inference question (c) Skip both questions (d) Spend equal time on every question in the paper
Answer: (b)
Solution: Equal time for all questions is the fairness trap that wastes seconds on easy items and starves hard ones. Match time to question type, so the answer is to answer the vocabulary question in about 15 seconds and reserve about 45 seconds for the inference question.
Source: PYQ-style

25. Which revision plan best applies both spaced repetition and active recall?
(a) Read the whole book daily from cover to cover (b) Only watch video lectures repeatedly (c) Study everything once, then rest until the exam (d) Review each topic at growing intervals by testing yourself with questions each time
Answer: (d)
Solution: Daily cover-to-cover reading is the overwork trap — it is passive and impossible to sustain. Combining widening gaps with self-testing uses both proven techniques, so the answer is to review each topic at growing intervals by testing yourself with questions each time.
