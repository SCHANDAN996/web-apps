# 🪄 Short Tricks + Skip Strategy — Revision Tracker

## Mnemonic Boxes

**Box 1 — The "3R" Revision Cycle**
> **R**ead → **R**ecall → **R**e-test
> Never revise by re-reading alone. Read once, close the book, recall aloud, then re-test with MCQs. Re-reading creates the "illusion of competence."

**Box 2 — "SPACE" Your Revision (Spaced Repetition)**
> **S**ame day → **P**lus 1 day → **A**fter 3 days → **C**heck after 7 days → **E**nd-of-month review
> Memory decays fastest in the first 24 hours. The 1-3-7-30 schedule beats 5 hours of same-day cramming.

**Box 3 — "FADE" the Forgetting Curve**
> **F**irst review within 24 hrs → **A**ctive recall only → **D**ifficult topics get extra slots → **E**rror log reviewed weekly

**Box 4 — "LOG" Your Errors**
> **L**ist the mistake → **O**wn the rule behind it → **G**roup similar errors
> An error log entry without the *rule* is useless. Write: "Wrong: subject-verb agreement — intervening phrase trap," not just "Q.42 wrong."

**Box 5 — "MAP" Before You Start**
> **M**ark weak chapters → **A**llocate time by weakness, not preference → **P**lan weekly targets
> Students revise what they already know because it feels good. The tracker exists to force you toward weak areas.

**Box 6 — "TICK" the Tracker Columns**
> **T**opic → **I**nitial score → **C**urrent score → **K**eep or drop from rotation
> A topic with 3 consecutive 90%+ scores leaves the weekly rotation; it moves to monthly maintenance.

**Box 7 — "PEEL" for Practice Set Review**
> **P**ick wrong questions → **E**xplain why you erred → **E**xtract the rule → **L**ink to a flashcard
> Every wrong answer must produce one flashcard. No flashcard = the mistake will repeat.

**Box 8 — "HALT" Before a Bad Session**
> **H**ungry / **A**ngry / **L**ow-sleep / **T**ired → don't do new topics
> In a HALT state, only do light recall (flashcards). New learning in a bad state encodes poorly.

**Box 9 — "80/20" Rule of Revision**
> 80% of your errors come from 20% of sub-topics. Find your personal 20% from the error log and give it 80% of revision time.

**Box 10 — "2-DAY" Alarm**
> If a topic hasn't been touched in 2 days after first learning, its retention is already below half. The tracker column "Last Revised" is your alarm — never let it cross 7 days for weak topics.

**Box 11 — "MIX" It Up (Interleaving)**
> **M**ultiple topics per session → **I**nterleave grammar + vocab + RC → **X**-check across sets
> Blocked practice (50 questions of one type) feels smooth but transfers poorly. Mixed practice feels harder and sticks longer.

**Box 12 — "TEST > REST"**
> One self-test beats three re-reads. When in doubt between revising notes and attempting a set, always attempt the set.

**Box 13 — "RED FLAG" Topics**
> **R**epeatedly wrong → **E**rror log has 3+ entries → **D**o daily until fixed
> A topic with 3+ error-log entries becomes a Red Flag topic: 10 minutes daily, no exceptions, until two clean sets.

## 🚫 Skip Strategy

**Tip 1 — Skip the "Comfort Loop."**
If your tracker shows a topic at 90%+ across three sessions, *skip it* in the weekly plan. Revising mastered topics is the most common hidden time-waste; it feels productive but adds zero marks.

**Tip 2 — Skip Low-Yield Perfectionism.**
If a rare grammar rule (e.g., archaic subjunctive forms) has cost you at most one mark historically and consumes hours, mark it "Skip — revisit in final week only." Depth in high-yield areas beats coverage of exotic ones.

**Tip 3 — Skip a Session, Not a Cycle.**
Missed a day? Don't restart the whole schedule or double the next day. Skip the missed slot entirely and continue the cycle — doubling up causes shallow revision of everything and burnout by day three.
