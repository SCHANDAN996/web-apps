126. Choose the correctly spelt word.
(a) Occurence (b) Occurrence (c) Ocurrence (d) Occurrance
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Occur" doubles the "r" before "-ence" because the stress falls on the final syllable; the tempting single-r spellings break this doubling rule. The correct spelling is Occurrence, so the answer is Occurrence.

127. Select the word closest in meaning to "EPHEMERAL".
(a) Strong (b) Eternal (c) Short-lived (d) Bright
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Eternal" is the antonym and is the classic trap; "ephemeral" (from Greek ephēmeros, lasting a day) means lasting a very short time. The correct synonym is Short-lived, so the answer is Short-lived.

128. Choose the correctly punctuated sentence.
(a) Its a beautiful day, isn't it? (b) Its' a beautiful day, isn't it? (c) It's a beautiful day isn't it? (d) It's a beautiful day, isn't it?
Answer: (d)
Solution: "Its" without the apostrophe is possessive and is the most common trap; the contraction of "it is" needs an apostrophe, and the tag question needs a comma before it. The correct sentence is "It's a beautiful day, isn't it?", so the answer is It's a beautiful day, isn't it?

129. Spot the error: Part P: Neither of the two boys / Part Q: have done / Part R: his homework. / Part S: No error
(a) Part R (b) Part P (c) Part Q (d) No error
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Neither of" takes a singular verb, so "have" is wrong; the plural-sounding "boys" nearby is the intervening-phrase trap. Part Q should read "has done", so the answer is Part Q.

130. Choose the correct passive form: "Someone has stolen my wallet."
(a) My wallet has been stolen. (b) My wallet is stolen. (c) My wallet was being stolen. (d) My wallet had stolen.
Answer: (a)
Solution: The present perfect "has stolen" becomes "has been stolen" in the passive; the simple "is stolen" loses the perfect aspect and is the tempting wrong choice. The correct passive is "My wallet has been stolen", so the answer is My wallet has been stolen.

133. Fill in the blank: Hardly ______ the station when the train left.
(a) I had reached (b) I reached (c) had I reached (d) did I reached
Answer: (c)
Solution: After "Hardly" at the start, inversion with the past perfect is required; "did I reached" wrongly mixes "did" with a past participle and is the trap. The correct form is "had I reached", so the answer is had I reached.

134. Choose the correct indirect speech: He said, "I may go tomorrow."
(a) He said that he may go the next day. (b) He said that he may go tomorrow. (c) He said that he might go tomorrow. (d) He said that he might go the next day.
Answer: (d)
Solution: In reported speech "may" shifts to "might" and "tomorrow" to "the next day"; keeping either unchanged is the trap. The correct version is "He said that he might go the next day", so the answer is He said that he might go the next day.

Answer: (a)
Solution: "Linguist" studies language scientifically and "bilingual" means only two languages — both are traps; "polyglot" (poly = many, glōtta = tongue) means a speaker of many languages. The correct word is Polyglot, so the answer is Polyglot.

149. Fill in the blank: Both the principal ______ the teachers were present in the hall.
(a) and (b) as well as (c) along with (d) or
Answer: (a)
Solution: "As well as" and "along with" would make the verb agree with "principal" alone and need "was", so they are the traps; the plural "were" requires a compound subject joined by "and". The correct connector is "and", so the answer is and.

150. Inference question: "Despite record harvests, farmer incomes stagnated; the granaries were full, but the markets were empty of buyers." It can be inferred that:
(a) Farmers stopped growing crops (b) Granaries were poorly built (c) The government banned crop sales (d) Good harvests alone do not guarantee farmer prosperity
Answer: (d)
Solution: Options about banning sales and stopping crops add facts not in the text — the over-thinking trap; the passage contrasts full granaries with stagnant incomes. The supported inference is that good harvests alone do not guarantee farmer prosperity, so the answer is Good harvests alone do not guarantee farmer prosperity.
