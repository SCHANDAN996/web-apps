# 📋 Important Rules & Tips — Revision Tracker

## (a) 12 High-Yield Rules of Effective Revision

| # | Rule | Example | Examiner's Trap |
|---|------|---------|-----------------|
| 1 | **Revise within 24 hours** of first learning — memory decays fastest on day one. | Learn 20 idioms today → re-test them tomorrow morning. | Trap: "I'll revise everything at the end." By then, most of it is gone and re-learning costs double the time. |
| 2 | **Use spaced intervals: 1 day → 3 days → 7 days → 21 days** (counted from the day of learning). | Vocabulary learned on 1st Jan is revised on 2nd, 4th, 8th and 22nd Jan. | Trap: Revising the same page 5 times in one sitting feels productive but builds only short-term memory. |
| 3 | **Active Recall beats re-reading.** Close the book, then retrieve. | After reading grammar rules, write them from memory, then check. | Trap: Highlighting and re-reading create an "illusion of competence" — recognition is not recall. |
| 4 | **Maintain an Error Log** — every wrong answer gets recorded with the reason. | "Chose (b) in Q.14 — confused 'affect' (verb) with 'effect' (noun)." | Trap: Only counting scores. A mock score without error analysis repeats the same mistakes. |
| 5 | **Revise errors more often than fresh topics.** | Error-log entries revised every 3 days; new content weekly. | Trap: Chasing new material while old errors stay un-fixed — exams punish repeated weaknesses. |
| 6 | **Test in mixed format**, not chapter-wise only. | One set mixing grammar + vocab + comprehension. | Trap: Chapter-wise practice tells you the topic in advance; real exams don't. |
| 7 | **One source, many revisions** — depth beats breadth. | One grammar book revised 4 times > four books read once. | Trap: Collecting PDFs and books. Unrevised material is zero marks. |
| 8 | **Track revision, not just syllabus completion.** | Tracker columns: Topic | 1st Rev | 2nd Rev | 3rd Rev | Accuracy %. | Trap: "Syllabus finished" ≠ "Exam ready." Finishing once without revision retains very little. |
| 9 | **Use the Blurting Sheet:** blank page, write all you remember, then compare. | After revising tenses, blurt all 12 tense structures in 5 minutes. | Trap: Checking notes *before* blurting — this converts recall back into recognition. |
| 10 | **Revise weakest topics first** when energy is highest. | Start the session with your worst area (e.g., para jumbles), not your favourite. | Trap: Starting with strong topics for motivation — weak areas never get prime attention. |
| 11 | **Set a fixed revision slot daily** — same time, same place. | 30 minutes every morning before new study. | Trap: "I'll revise when I get time." Unscheduled revision never happens. |
| 12 | **In the final week, revise only your notes and error log** — nothing new. | Last 7 days: flashcards + error log + formula/rules sheet. | Trap: Starting a new topic in the last week — it shakes confidence and gets zero revision cycles. |

## (b) Time Management — The 15/45-Second Rule

Not all questions deserve equal time. Classify **before** you solve:

| Type | Nature | Time Budget | Examples | Strategy |
|------|--------|-------------|----------|----------|
| **Fact-based (15 sec)** | Direct recall — you either know it or you don't. | ≤ 15 seconds | Synonyms, antonyms, one-word substitution, idioms, spelling, direct grammar rule | Answer instantly if known; if unknown after 15 sec, **mark and skip** — thinking longer won't create knowledge you don't have. |
| **Inference-based (45 sec)** | Requires application, elimination, or reading. | ≤ 45 seconds | Error spotting with intervening phrases, para jumbles, cloze test, RC inference, sentence improvement | Read carefully, eliminate two options fast, decide between the remaining two. If stuck beyond 45 sec, mark the best guess and move on. |

**Golden sequencing rule:** In any section, do all 15-second questions first, then return to 45-second questions. This banks easy marks before time pressure builds.

## (c) Morphology — Root, Affix, Example

Word-parts make revision faster: learn one root, unlock many words.

| Root / Affix | Meaning | Derived Words | Revision Tip |
|--------------|---------|---------------|--------------|
| **spect** (Latin) | to look | inspect, respect, spectator, retrospect | Group all "spect" words on one flashcard — revise the family, not individuals. |
| **port** (Latin) | to carry | transport, export, portable, portfolio | Add new words to the same root card as you meet them. |
| **dict** (Latin) | to say | predict, dictate, verdict, contradict | "Pre-" (before) + dict = say before → predict. Decode, don't memorise. |
| **chron** (Greek) | time | chronic, chronology, synchronize, anachronism | Link to a story: "a chronicle is a record across time." |
| **-phobia** (Greek suffix) | fear of | claustrophobia, xenophobia, hydrophobia | Suffix cards are high-yield: one suffix explains dozens of exam words. |
| **re-** (Latin prefix) | again | revise, review, recall, retain | The root of this very chapter: **re-vise = to look again** (re- + vis, "see"). |

**Morphology rule for revision:** When you forget a word in the exam, break it into prefix + root + suffix and reconstruct the meaning — this is your built-in dictionary.
