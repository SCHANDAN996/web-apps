# 📊 PYQ Analysis — Revision Tracker

## How Exams Test This Chapter

"Revision Tracker" is a meta-skill chapter: no exam asks "What is spaced repetition?" directly. But the *skills this chapter trains* — error-spotting under time pressure, vocabulary recall, tone/inference judgement, and trap recognition — are exactly what SSC CGL, IBPS PO, and State PCS prelims measure. The analysis below is a **pattern analysis in words**, based on the recurring question types across these exams.

### Where Revision-Tracker Skills Show Up in Exams

- **Error Spotting / Sentence Improvement:** Examiners repeatedly test subject-verb agreement with intervening phrases, collective nouns, and "sound-right" informal constructions. These are the questions a revision tracker catches best — they are rule-based, so a logged error almost never repeats.
- **Vocabulary (Synonyms, Antonyms, One-word, Idioms):** Pure recall questions. Aspirants who revise word lists on a spaced schedule (Day 1 → Day 3 → Day 7) retain far more than those who re-read once. Root-based words (bene-, mal-, -phobia, chron-) recur across exams with different derived words.
- **Cloze Test & Reading Comprehension:** Tone and inference questions punish over-thinking. The detective approach — answer only from the passage, never from outside knowledge — is the single most revised skill here.
- **Para-jumbles:** Tested through logical connectors and pronoun references; improves sharply when aspirants log *why* a sequence failed, not just the right order.

### The Examiner's Four Trap Types (used throughout this chapter)

| Trap Type | What it looks like | Defence |
|---|---|---|
| **Intervening Phrase** | "The quality of apples *were* good" — a plural noun sits between subject and verb | Strike out the phrase between commas/of-phrase; match verb to the real subject |
| **Collective Noun** | "The jury *has* delivered its verdict" vs "The jury *are* divided" | Singular when acting as one unit; plural when members act separately |
| **Sound-Right** | "He is more smarter than me" — sounds fine in speech, wrong in grammar | Never trust the ear; check the rule (no double comparatives) |
| **Over-thinking** | An RC inference question where the simplest text-supported answer is correct | Detective approach: the answer must be provable from the passage alone |

## Top 10 High-Yield PYQ-Style Questions with Solutions

**Q1.** The quality of the mangoes in the market (a) / were not good (b) / this season. (c) / No error (d)
**Answer:** (b)
**Solution:** Subject is "quality" (singular); "of the mangoes in the market" is an intervening phrase. Correct: "was not good."
**Trap type:** Intervening Phrase
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q2.** The committee have submitted its report to the chairman.
(a) have submitted (b) its report (c) to the chairman (d) No error
**Answer:** (a)
**Solution:** "Committee" acting as one body takes a singular verb: "has submitted." Plural verb only when members disagree.
**Trap type:** Collective Noun
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q3.** Choose the word closest in meaning to **BENEVOLENT**.
(a) cruel (b) kind (c) wealthy (d) strict
**Answer:** (b)
**Solution:** Root *bene-* = good/well (benefit, benefactor). Benevolent = kindly. "Wealthy" is the sound-right distractor — benefactors are often rich, but the word means kind.
**Trap type:** Sound-Right
**Source:** NCERT-level vocabulary; PYQ-style

**Q4.** He is more superior to his colleague in experience.
(a) more superior (b) to his colleague (c) in experience (d) No error
**Answer:** (a)
**Solution:** "Superior" is already comparative in Latin origin; never use "more" with it. Also note: superior takes "to," not "than."
**Trap type:** Sound-Right
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q5.** One of my friends (a) / are going (b) / to Delhi tomorrow. (c) / No error (d)
**Answer:** (b)
**Solution:** "One of + plural noun" takes a singular verb — the subject is "one," not "friends." Correct: "is going."
**Trap type:** Intervening Phrase
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q6.** RC Inference: *The passage states that renewable energy capacity grew rapidly, yet coal still supplied most of the grid's power.* What can be inferred?
(a) Coal will be eliminated within five years
(b) Growth in renewables has not yet displaced coal's dominant share
(c) Renewable energy is a failure
(d) The government opposes renewable energy
**Answer:** (b)
**Solution:** Only (b) is provable from the sentence. (a) invents a timeline, (c) overstates, (d) introduces outside claims. The least dramatic, text-supported option wins.
**Trap type:** Over-thinking
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q7.** Neither of the two boys (a) / have done (b) / his homework. (c) / No error (d)
**Answer:** (b)
**Solution:** "Neither" is singular: "has done." The nearby plural "boys" is the decoy.
**Trap type:** Intervening Phrase
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q8.** Choose the correctly spelt word.
(a) Occurence (b) Occurrence (c) Ocurrence (d) Occurrance
**Answer:** (b)
**Solution:** Occur → occurring → occurrence: double 'c' and double 'r'. A classic recall item — exactly the kind a revision tracker logs once and never misses again.
**Trap type:** Sound-Right
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q9.** The audience was clapping loudly after the performance.
(a) The audience was (b) clapping loudly (c) after the performance (d) No error
**Answer:** (d)
**Solution:** "Audience" acting as a single body takes singular "was" — correct as given. The trap is *changing* a right answer because collective nouns feel plural.
**Trap type:** Collective Noun / Over-thinking
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q10.** Idiom: "To beat about the bush"
(a) To destroy a garden (b) To talk indirectly, avoiding the main point (c) To search thoroughly (d) To argue loudly
**Answer:** (b)
**Solution:** The idiom means speaking around a topic without coming to the point. Literal-meaning options like (a) are standard distractors.
**Trap type:** Sound-Right
**Source:** PYQ-style

## Applying the 15/45-Second Rule

**Q3 (BENEVOLENT) — 15-second question.** This is pure recall: either the root *bene-* = good is in your memory or it isn't. Strategy: commit within 15 seconds, flag if unsure, never re-read the options twice. Revision trackers exist precisely to convert such questions into instant recalls.

**Q6 (RC inference) — 45-second question.** Inference demands the detective approach: re-read the cited lines, eliminate options that add outside information (a, d) or exaggerate (c), then verify (b) against the text. Budget the full 45 seconds — rushing inference questions is the costliest time error in the exam.

**Revision-tracker takeaway:** Log every missed question with its trap type (Intervening Phrase / Collective Noun / Sound-Right / Over-thinking). After two revision cycles, your error log will show that most of your mistakes come from just one or two trap families — and those are the rules to re-drill, not the whole syllabus.
