Practice Set 2 — Error Spotting (Advanced)
Questions 26–50 | Easy: 26–40 | Medium: 41–50

26. Spot the error: (P) One of my friend / (Q) has gone / (R) to Delhi yesterday. / (S) No error
(a) Part P (b) Part Q (c) Part R (d) No error
Answer: (a)
Solution: "One of" must be followed by a plural noun — "one of my friends". Part P is wrong. Also note "has gone... yesterday" is tense-inconsistent, but the first error is in Part P.
Source: NCERT Class 9 English Grammar

27. Spot the error: (P) The Ganges / (Q) are / (R) a holy river. / (S) No error
(a) Part Q (b) Part P (c) Part R (d) No error
Answer: (a)
Solution: Names of rivers take a singular verb — "The Ganges is a holy river." Part Q is wrong.
Source: NCERT Class 10 English Grammar

28. Spot the error: (P) He is more wiser / (Q) than / (R) his brother. / (S) No error
(a) No error (b) Part Q (c) Part R (d) Part P
Answer: (d)
Solution: Double comparative is wrong. "Wiser" is already comparative; say "He is wiser than his brother." Part P is wrong.
Source: NCERT Class 9 English Grammar

29. Spot the error: (P) I have been living / (Q) in this city / (R) since five years. / (S) No error
(a) Part P (b) Part Q (c) Part R (d) No error
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Since" is used with a point of time; a period of time takes "for" — "for five years". Part R is wrong.

31. Spot the error: (P) She is / (Q) senior than / (R) me by two years. / (S) No error
(a) Part P (b) Part R (c) Part Q (d) No error
Answer: (c)
Solution: Latin comparatives (senior, junior, superior, inferior, prior) take "to", not "than" — "senior to me". Part Q is wrong.

41. Spot the error: (P) The captain, along with his team members, / (Q) were / (R) present at the ceremony. / (S) No error
(a) Part P (b) Part Q (c) Part R (d) No error
Answer: (b)
Solution: With "along with", the verb agrees with the first subject ("the captain"), which is singular — "was present". The intervening phrase is the examiner's trap. Part Q is wrong.
Source: PYQ-style (Intervening Phrase trap)

42. Spot the error: (P) Scarcely he had reached the station / (Q) when / (R) the train left. / (S) No error
(a) No error (b) Part Q (c) Part R (d) Part P
Answer: (d)
Solution: After a negative adverb like "scarcely" at the start, inversion is needed — "Scarcely had he reached the station when the train left." Part P is wrong.
Source: NCERT Class 11 English Grammar

43. Spot the error: (P) The committee / (Q) have divided / (R) on this issue. / (S) No error
(a) Part Q (b) Part P (c) Part R (d) No error
Answer: (a)
Solution: When a collective noun's members act separately, use "are divided"; "have divided" with "on this issue" is unidiomatic — "The committee are divided on this issue." Part Q is wrong.
Source: PYQ-style (Collective Noun trap)

50. Spot the error: (P) The principal and secretary / (Q) have / (R) arrived for the meeting. / (S) No error
(a) Part P (b) No error (c) Part R (d) Part Q
Answer: (d)
Solution: One article before two titles means one person holding both posts, so the verb is singular — "has arrived". If two people were meant, it would be "The principal and the secretary". Part Q is wrong.
Source: PYQ-style (Over-thinking trap)
