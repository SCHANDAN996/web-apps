1. One of my friends (P) / are going (Q) / to Delhi tomorrow. (R) / No error (S)
(a) Part P (b) No error (c) Part R (d) Part Q
Answer: (d)
Solution: After "one of + plural noun", the verb must be singular. Part Q should read "is going". Part P is correct because "one of my friends" is the right structure.

2. The Ganges (P) / is one of the longest (Q) / river in India. (R) / No error (S)
(a) Part P (b) Part Q (c) Part R (d) No error
Answer: (c)
Solution: After "one of the + superlative", the noun must be plural: "rivers in India". Part R has the error.

3. He is senior (P) / than me (Q) / by two years. (R) / No error (S)
(a) Part P (b) Part Q (c) Part R (d) No error
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Senior" is a Latin comparative and takes "to", not "than". Part Q should be "to me". This is a classic examiner trap — "than" sounds natural but is wrong.

4. Each of the boys (P) / have done (Q) / his homework. (R) / No error (S)
(a) Part Q (b) Part P (c) Part R (d) No error
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Each of" takes a singular verb, so Part Q should be "has done". The plural noun "boys" between subject and verb is an intervening-phrase trap.

5. I have been living (P) / in this city (Q) / since five years. (R) / No error (S)
(a) Part R (b) Part Q (c) Part P (d) No error
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Since" is used with a point of time; a period of time takes "for". Part R should be "for five years".

6. Neither the teacher (P) / nor the students (Q) / was present in the hall. (R) / No error (S)
(a) Part R (b) Part Q (c) Part P (d) No error
Answer: (a)
Solution: With "neither…nor", the verb agrees with the nearer subject. "Students" is plural, so Part R needs "were present".

7. She is more wiser (P) / than her (Q) / younger sister. (R) / No error (S)
(a) Part R (b) Part Q (c) Part P (d) No error
Answer: (c)
Solution: Double comparatives are wrong. "Wiser" is already comparative, so Part P should be just "wiser" (drop "more").

(a) Part Q (b) Part P (c) Part R (d) No error
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Scenery" is an uncountable noun and has no plural form. Part P should be "The scenery".

Answer: (b)

Answer: (b)

(a) Part Q (b) Part P (c) Part R (d) No error
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Police" is always plural, so Part Q should be "have arrested". Also, with "yesterday" the simple past ("arrested") is preferred, but the clear grammatical error is the singular verb.

13. Unless you do not work hard, (P) / you will not (Q) / pass the examination. (R) / No error (S)
(a) No error (b) Part Q (c) Part R (d) Part P
Answer: (d)
Solution: "Unless" is itself negative, so "do not" must not follow it. Part P should be "Unless you work hard". This double-negative trap is a favourite of examiners.

Solution: "The number of" takes a singular verb ("is increasing"); "a number of" would take a plural verb. The phrase "of students" is an intervening-phrase trap.

Answer: (d)
Solution: In "one of those + plural noun + who", the relative pronoun refers to the plural noun, so the verb must be plural: "have been promoted". Part R is wrong — a subtle trap because "one" tempts a singular verb.

Answer: (d)
Solution: When a negative adverb like "scarcely" begins a clause, inversion is required: "Scarcely had he finished…". Part P has the error.
