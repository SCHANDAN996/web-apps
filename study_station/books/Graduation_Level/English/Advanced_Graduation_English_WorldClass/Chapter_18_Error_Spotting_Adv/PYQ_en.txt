# 📊 PYQ Analysis — Error Spotting (Advanced)

## 1. How Exams Test This Chapter (Pattern Analysis)

Error spotting is a permanent fixture of SSC CGL (Tier-I & Tier-II), SSC CHSL, IBPS PO/Clerk, SBI PO, and State PCS prelims. The questions are almost never about rare grammar — examiners recycle the **same 10–12 rule families** and dress them in new sentences.

**What repeats, year after year:**

- **Subject–Verb Agreement with intervening phrases** — "The quality of the apples *were*…" The phrase between subject and verb is the examiner's favourite hiding place.
- **Collective nouns & indefinite pronouns** — *each, every, everyone, neither, either, none* taking singular verbs; collective nouns like *committee, team, jury* normally taking singular verbs in exam usage.
- **Sound-Right traps** — sentences that *sound* correct to a Hindi speaker's ear but break a rule: "He is more superior to me", "She insisted to go", "Despite of the rain".
- **Parallelism** — lists where one item breaks the pattern: "He likes reading, swimming, and *to jog*."
- **Tense sequence** — "When I reached the station, the train *left*" (should be *had left*).
- **Misplaced/dangling modifiers** — "Walking down the road, the building collapsed" (the building wasn't walking).
- **Article & preposition precision** — "discuss about", "comprise of", "order for food".
- **Over-thinking traps** — a perfectly correct sentence that *looks* wrong because it uses an unusual but valid structure (e.g., subjunctive: "I suggest that he *be* on time").

**Examiner's four favourite trap types (used throughout this chapter):**

| Trap Type | What it looks like |
|---|---|
| **Intervening Phrase** | Extra words between subject and verb to hide disagreement |
| **Collective Noun / Indefinite Pronoun** | *Each/everyone/team/jury* with wrong verb number |
| **Sound-Right** | Feels correct by ear (often Hindi-influenced), breaks a rule |
| **Over-thinking** | Correct sentence that looks suspicious; the answer is "No error" |

**Exam distribution (qualitative):** SSC CGL/CHSL lean heavily on agreement, articles, and prepositions in short sentences. IBPS/SBI PO prefer longer sentences with parallelism, modifiers, and tense sequence — and frequently include "No error" as a genuine option. State exams mirror SSC patterns at a slightly easier level.

## 2. Top 10 High-Yield Questions (with Solutions & Trap Classification)

**Q1.** One of my friends **(a)/ are going (b)/ to Delhi tomorrow. (c)/ No error (d)**
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** Subject is "One" (singular), not "friends". The phrase "of my friends" is an intervening phrase. Correct: *is going*.
**Trap type:** Intervening Phrase
**Source:** PYQ-style (SSC pattern)

**Q2.** The committee **(a)/ have submitted (b)/ its report on time. (c)/ No error (d)**
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** In exam usage, a collective noun acting as one unit takes a **singular** verb — and the singular pronoun "its" later in the sentence confirms the committee is acting as a single body. Correct: *has submitted*. The plural-looking sense of "committee" is the decoy.
**Trap type:** Collective Noun
**Source:** PYQ-style (Bank pattern)

**Q3.** He is senior **(a)/ than me by (b)/ two years. (c)/ No error (d)**
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** Latin comparatives (*senior, junior, superior, inferior, prior*) take **to**, not *than*. Correct: *senior to me*. "Senior than" sounds fine to many ears — that's the trap.
**Trap type:** Sound-Right
**Source:** PYQ-style (SSC pattern)

**Q4.** Neither of the two boys **(a)/ have done (b)/ his homework. (c)/ No error (d)**
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** *Neither of* takes a singular verb: *has done*. The plural noun "boys" right before the verb is the decoy.
**Trap type:** Intervening Phrase
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q5.** Walking through the park, **(a)/ a snake suddenly (b)/ appeared before me. (c)/ No error (d)**
**Answer: (a)**
**Solution:** Dangling modifier — the snake wasn't walking through the park. Correct: *While I was walking through the park, a snake appeared…*
**Trap type:** Sound-Right (the sentence "flows" well but logic fails)
**Source:** PYQ-style (Bank pattern)

**Q6.** I suggest that **(a)/ he is (b)/ more careful in future. (c)/ No error (d)**
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** After verbs of suggestion/demand (*suggest, recommend, insist, demand*), the subjunctive uses the base form: *he be more careful* (or restructure: *he should be*).
**Trap type:** Over-thinking-adjacent (unfamiliar rule)
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q7.** She insisted **(a)/ to go (b)/ with me. (c)/ No error (d)**
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** *Insist* takes **on + gerund**: *insisted on going*. "Insisted to go" is a classic Hindi-influenced construction (उसने जाने पर ज़ोर दिया).
**Trap type:** Sound-Right
**Source:** PYQ-style (SSC pattern)

**Q8.** The Ganges is **(a)/ one of the longest (b)/ river in India. (c)/ No error (d)**
**Answer: (c)**
**Solution:** "One of the + superlative + **plural noun**": *one of the longest rivers*. The singular "river" is the error.
**Trap type:** Intervening Phrase (the superlative phrase hides the noun-number rule)
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q9.** Hardly had he **(a)/ reached the station (b)/ than the train left. (c)/ No error (d)**
**Answer: (c)**
**Solution:** *Hardly/Scarcely* pairs with **when**, not *than*. (*No sooner* pairs with *than*.) Correct: *when the train left*.
**Trap type:** Sound-Right (hardly…than is a very common spoken error)
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q10.** Each of the students **(a)/ were asked (b)/ to submit the assignment. (c)/ No error (d)**
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** *Each of* + plural noun takes a **singular** verb: *was asked*. The nearby plural "students" is the decoy.
**Trap type:** Intervening Phrase / Collective-Indefinite
**Source:** PYQ-style

## 3. Applying the 15/45-Second Rule

**Q1 above — a 15-second (Fact-based) question.**
Scan: subject = "One" → singular → "are" is wrong. You don't need to read the sentence twice. Rule recall, mark, move. **Target: 15 seconds.**

**Q5 above — a 45-second (Inference-based) question.**
Nothing looks grammatically broken at first glance — verb tenses agree, articles are fine. You must ask: *who is doing the walking?* The subject after the comma is "a snake" — logic fails. This needs a meaning-check, not a rule-check. **Target: up to 45 seconds; if the modifier logic doesn't click, flag it and return later.**

**Quick classifier for the exam hall:**
- Error in verb form, article, preposition, or number → **Fact-based → 15 sec.**
- Sentence "looks fine" but may have a modifier, parallelism, or logic flaw → **Inference-based → 45 sec.**
- "No error" is a real option in bank exams — if two full passes find nothing, choose it confidently instead of inventing an error (Over-thinking trap).
