101. Identify the part of the sentence that contains an error.
Part P: Neither of the two candidates
Part Q: have submitted
Part R: the required documents
Part S: before the deadline.
(a) Part P (b) Part S (c) Part R (d) Part Q
Answer: (d)
Solution: "Neither of" takes a singular verb, so Part Q should read "has submitted". The plural noun "candidates" sitting just before the verb misleads the ear — that is the trap.

102. Identify the part of the sentence that contains an error.
Part P: The committee, along with its advisors,
Part Q: were unanimous
Part R: in rejecting
Part S: the proposal.
(a) Part P (b) Part Q (c) Part S (d) Part R
Answer: (b)
Solution: With "along with", the verb agrees with the first subject "the committee" (singular), so Part Q should be "was unanimous". The intervening plural phrase is the examiner's trap.

103. Choose the correctly spelt word.
(a) Occurrence (b) Ocurrence (c) Occurrance (d) Occurence
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Occurrence" doubles both the c and the r. The other forms each drop one of the doubled letters.

104. Identify the part of the sentence that contains an error.
Part P: Scarcely he had entered
Part Q: the examination hall
Part R: when the bell rang
Part S: and the papers were distributed.
(a) Part Q (b) No error (c) Part R (d) Part P
Answer: (d)
Solution: When "scarcely" begins a clause, inversion is required: "Scarcely had he entered". Part P keeps normal word order, which is wrong.

105. Identify the part of the sentence that contains an error.
Part P: One of my friends
Part Q: are going
Part R: to Delhi
Part S: tomorrow morning.
(a) Part P (b) No error (c) Part Q (d) Part R
Answer: (c)
Solution: "One of + plural noun" takes a singular verb: "is going". The plural noun "friends" just before the verb is the sound-right trap.

106. Choose the sentence with correct subject-verb agreement.
(a) The quality of the mangoes have not been good. (b) The quality of the mangoes are not good. (c) The quality of the mangoes was not good. (d) The quality of the mangoes were not good.
Answer: (c)
Solution: The subject is "quality" (singular); "of the mangoes" is only a prepositional phrase. Hence the singular verb "was" is correct.
Source: PYQ-style

107. Identify the part of the sentence that contains an error.
Part P: He is one of the best players
Part Q: that has ever played
Part R: for the national team
Part S: in this decade.
(a) Part Q (b) Part P (c) Part R (d) No error
Answer: (a)
Solution: In "one of the best players that…", the relative pronoun "that" refers to "players" (plural), so it should be "that have ever played". A very frequently tested trap.
Source: PYQ-style

108. Choose the option that best expresses the meaning of the idiom "to burn the midnight oil".
(a) To waste electricity at night (b) To work or study late into the night (c) To celebrate all night (d) To light lamps during a festival
Answer: (b)
Solution: The idiom means working or studying till late at night, from the days of oil lamps. The literal options are distractors.

109. Identify the part of the sentence that contains an error.
Part P: No sooner did the minister arrive
Part Q: at the venue
Part R: then the crowd
Part S: started cheering loudly.
(a) Part R (b) Part Q (c) No error (d) Part P
Answer: (a)
Solution: "No sooner" is paired with "than", not "then", so Part R should read "than the crowd". The then/than confusion is a classic examiner trap.

110. Choose the grammatically correct sentence.
(a) Each of the boys has done their homework. (b) Each of the boys have done his homework. (c) Each of the boys has done his homework. (d) Each of the boys have done their homework.
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Each of" takes a singular verb ("has") and a singular pronoun ("his"). Options mixing a plural verb or plural pronoun are wrong.

111. Identify the part of the sentence that contains an error.
Part P: The Ganges is
Part Q: one of the longest

112. Choose the word nearest in meaning to "CANDID".
(a) Deceptive (b) Timid (c) Frank (d) Arrogant
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Candid" means truthful and straightforward, i.e., frank. "Deceptive" is nearly its opposite.

113. Identify the part of the sentence that contains an error.
Part P: Being a rainy day,
Part Q: I decided
Part R: to stay at home
Part S: and read a book.
(a) Part R (b) Part Q (c) No error (d) Part P
Answer: (d)
Solution: This is a dangling modifier — "being a rainy day" cannot describe "I". It should be "It being a rainy day, I decided…". A favourite hard-level trap of examiners.
Source: PYQ-style

114. Choose the option that is the antonym of "OBSOLETE".
(a) Modern (b) Outdated (c) Useless (d) Ancient
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Obsolete" means no longer in use; its opposite is "modern" or "current". "Ancient" and "outdated" are synonyms, not antonyms.
Source: Standard vocabulary lists

115. Identify the part of the sentence that contains an error.
Part P: The teacher asked the students
Part Q: that why they
Part R: had not completed
Part S: their assignments on time.
(a) Part P (b) Part R (c) No error (d) Part Q
Answer: (d)
Solution: In reported speech with a wh-word, "that" is not used: "asked the students why they had not…". Part Q wrongly inserts "that".
Source: PYQ-style

116. Choose the sentence with the correct use of the article.
(a) He is a university professor. (b) He is the university professor which I met yesterday. (c) He is an university professor. (d) He is an European university professor.
Answer: (a)
Solution: "University" begins with a consonant sound /juː/, so it takes "a", not "an". The same sound rule makes "an European" wrong, and "which" cannot refer to a person.
Source: PYQ-style

117. Identify the part of the sentence that contains an error.
Part P: Hardly had she
Part Q: finished her speech
Part R: than the audience
Part S: began to applaud.
(a) Part R (b) Part Q (c) Part P (d) No error
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Hardly" pairs with "when", not "than": "Hardly had she finished… when the audience began…". The "than" sounds plausible after an inverted structure, which is the trap.
Source: PYQ-style

118. Choose the correctly framed passive voice of: "The manager will sign the documents."
(a) The documents will be signed by the manager. (b) The documents will be sign by the manager. (c) The documents will signed by the manager. (d) The documents will being signed by the manager.
Answer: (a)
Solution: Future passive = will + be + past participle, i.e., "will be signed". The other options break the verb pattern.

120. Choose the word opposite in meaning to "TRANSPARENT".
(a) Fragile (b) Opaque (c) Clear (d) Obvious
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Transparent" means allowing light through, i.e., clear; its antonym is "opaque". "Clear" and "obvious" are near-synonyms, not opposites.

121. Identify the part of the sentence that contains an error.
Part P: Walking through the park,
Part Q: the flowers
Part R: looked beautiful
Part S: in the morning light.
(a) No error (b) Part Q (c) Part R (d) Part P
Answer: (d)
Solution: Dangling modifier: the flowers were not walking through the park, so Part P has no proper subject to attach to. It should be "Walking through the park, I found the flowers beautiful…". The sentence sounds smooth, which is exactly the trap.
Source: PYQ-style

122. Choose the sentence with the correct tense.
(a) I see him yesterday at the market. (b) I have seen him yesterday at the market. (c) I saw him yesterday at the market. (d) I had seen him yesterday at the market.
Answer: (c)
Solution: With a definite past time marker like "yesterday", the simple past is used; the present perfect is wrong with finished time.
Source: PYQ-style

Part P: Neither the manager
Part Q: nor the employees
Part R: was aware

(a) Part Q (b) Part R (c) Part P (d) No error

124. Choose the option that best substitutes the phrase: "A person who speaks many languages."
(a) Bilingual (b) Polyglot (c) Linguist (d) Orator
Answer: (b)
Solution: A "polyglot" speaks many languages. "Bilingual" means two languages; a "linguist" studies language; an "orator" is a skilled public speaker.
Source: Standard one-word substitution lists

125. Identify the part of the sentence that contains an error.
Part P: The number of students
Part Q: absent from the class
Part R: were quite large
Part S: on Monday.
(a) Part P (b) Part Q (c) No error (d) Part R
Answer: (d)
Solution: "The number of" takes a singular verb: "was quite large". The plural noun "students" nearby tempts candidates toward a plural verb — that is the trap. (Note: "a number of" would take a plural verb.)
