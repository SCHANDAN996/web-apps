# 🪄 Short Tricks + Skip Strategy — Error Spotting (Advanced)

## Memory Boxes (Mnemonics)

**Box 1 — FANBOYS (Coordinating Conjunctions)**
For, And, Nor, But, Or, Yet, So — these join two *equal* clauses. If you see a comma + one of these, check that both sides are complete sentences.
Trap: "He came late, and because he missed the bus." ❌ (unequal clauses)

**Box 2 — SVA: "Phrases Don't Matter"**
Between subject and verb, examiners insert long phrases. Strike them out mentally:
"The quality of the mangoes **was** good." ✅ (subject = quality, not mangoes)
Trick: *Cover the middle, match the ends.*

**Box 3 — "Each/Every/Neither/Either = SINGULAR"**
Each boy, every student, neither of them, either of the two → always takes a singular verb.
"Neither of the answers **is** correct." ✅

**Box 4 — "One of the + Plural Noun + Singular Verb"**
"One of my **friends has** come." ✅
But with a relative clause: "One of the boys who **were** absent…" (verb agrees with "boys" after "who").

**Box 5 — "No sooner…than / Hardly…when / Scarcely…when"**
Fixed pairs — never mix them!
"No sooner had he left **than** it rained." ✅
"Hardly had he left **when** it rained." ✅
Trap: "No sooner…when" ❌

**Box 6 — "The + Adjective = Plural Verb"**
The rich, the poor, the blind, the unemployed → plural.
"The rich **are** not always happy." ✅

**Box 7 — "News, Physics, Mathematics, Ethics = Singular"**
Subjects ending in -s that are singular: "The news **is** good." ✅
But: scissors, trousers, spectacles, premises → plural.

**Box 8 — "Prefer/Senior/Junior/Superior + TO (not than)"**
Latin comparatives take "to": "He is senior **to** me." ✅
Trap: "senior than" ❌

**Box 9 — "Since (point of time) vs For (period)"**
since Monday / since 2010 / since childhood
for two days / for ten years / for a long time
Trick: **Since = Start point, For = Full stretch.**

**Box 10 — "If I were…" (Subjunctive)**
Imaginary/impossible conditions → "were" for all persons.
"If I **were** a bird, I would fly." ✅

**Box 11 — "Hardly/Scarcely/Seldom = Negative already"**
Never add "not" with them: "He hardly works." ✅ (not "He does not hardly work" ❌)

**Box 12 — "Cattle, People, Police, Gentry = Plural"**
"The police **have** arrested him." ✅ — no "polices" ever.

**Box 13 — "It is high time + Past Tense"**
"It is high time we **went** home." ✅ (past form, present meaning)

**Box 14 — "Lest…should"**
"He ran fast lest he **should** miss the train." ✅ — never "will" after lest.

**Box 15 — "A number of = Plural | The number of = Singular"**
"**A** number of students **were** absent." ✅
"**The** number of students **is** rising." ✅

## ⏭️ Skip Strategy

**Tip 1 — Skip the "Sounds Right" Trap (save 30 seconds):**
If a sentence "sounds correct" because of spoken-English habit (e.g., "What you are doing?"), it is probably wrong. Don't waste time re-reading it aloud — apply the rule (inversion after question words) and move on. *Sound is not grammar.*

**Tip 2 — Skip Over-thinking on Fact-based Questions (15-second rule):**
Subject-verb agreement, fixed prepositions, and article errors are Fact-based. Spot the rule, mark the answer, don't hunt for a second error. Reserve 45 seconds only for Inference-based items (tense logic across clauses, pronoun reference, parallelism in long sentences).

**Tip 3 — Skip and Return on Multi-clause Sentences:**
If a sentence has 3+ clauses and you can't locate the error in 20 seconds, mark it for review and move ahead. Long sentences often hide errors in the *last* clause (tense shift or pronoun mismatch) — come back with fresh eyes and read the sentence **backwards, clause by clause**.
