Error Spotting (Advanced) — Practice Set 3
Questions 51–60: Easy | Questions 61–75: Medium

51. Spot the error: One of my friend (P) / has gone (Q) / to Delhi yesterday. (R)
(a) Part P (b) Part Q (c) Part R (d) No error
Answer: (a)
Solution: Part P is wrong. After "one of", we always use a plural noun: "one of my friends". The verb stays singular because the subject is "one".
Source: NCERT Class 9 English Grammar

52. Spot the error: He as well as his brothers (P) / were present (Q) / at the meeting. (R)
(a) Part P (b) Part Q (c) Part R (d) No error
Answer: (b)
Solution: Part Q is wrong. With "as well as", the verb agrees with the first subject "He", so it should be "was present". This is a classic intervening-phrase trap.
Source: PYQ-style

53. Spot the error: She is more cleverer (P) / than (Q) / her sister. (R)
(a) Part P (b) Part Q (c) Part R (d) No error
Answer: (a)
Solution: Part P is wrong. "Cleverer" is already comparative; using "more" with it makes a double comparative. Say "cleverer than" or "more clever than".
Source: NCERT Class 8 English Grammar

54. Spot the error: I have been living (P) / in this city (Q) / since five years. (R)
(a) Part P (b) Part Q (c) Part R (d) No error
Answer: (c)
Solution: Part R is wrong. "Since" is used with a point of time (since 2019); "for" is used with a period of time: "for five years".
Source: NCERT Class 9 English Grammar

55. Spot the error: The news (P) / are (Q) / very shocking. (R)
(a) Part R (b) Part P (c) Part Q (d) No error
Answer: (c)
Solution: Part Q is wrong. "News" looks plural but is singular, so the verb must be "is". Similar words: physics, mathematics, politics.
Source: NCERT Class 8 English Grammar

56. Spot the error: Neither of the two boys (P) / have done (Q) / his homework. (R)
(a) Part R (b) Part P (c) Part Q (d) No error
Answer: (c)
Solution: Part Q is wrong. "Neither of" takes a singular verb: "has done". The plural noun "boys" between them is the examiner's trap.
Source: PYQ-style

57. Spot the error: He is senior (P) / than me (Q) / by two years. (R)
(a) Part P (b) No error (c) Part R (d) Part Q
Answer: (d)
Solution: Part Q is wrong. Latin comparatives like senior, junior, superior, inferior take "to", not "than": "senior to me".
Source: NCERT Class 10 English Grammar

58. Spot the error: Each of the students (P) / have submitted (Q) / the assignment. (R)
(a) No error (b) Part P (c) Part R (d) Part Q
Answer: (d)
Solution: Part Q is wrong. "Each of" takes a singular verb: "has submitted". Ignore the plural noun "students" — it is an intervening phrase.
Source: PYQ-style

59. Spot the error: I prefer tea (P) / more than (Q) / coffee. (R)
(a) Part Q (b) Part P (c) Part R (d) No error
Answer: (a)
Solution: Part Q is wrong. "Prefer" already contains the idea of comparison, so use "to": "I prefer tea to coffee." Never "more than" with prefer.
Source: NCERT Class 10 English Grammar

60. Spot the error: The police (P) / has arrested (Q) / the thief. (R)
(a) Part P (b) Part Q (c) Part R (d) No error
Answer: (b)
Solution: Part Q is wrong. "Police" is always plural: "The police have arrested the thief." Similar plural-only nouns: cattle, people, clergy.
Source: NCERT Class 9 English Grammar

61. Spot the error: Scarcely he had reached (P) / the station (Q) / when the train left. (R)
(a) Part R (b) Part Q (c) No error (d) Part P
Answer: (d)
Solution: Part P is wrong. When a negative adverb like "scarcely" begins a clause, inversion is required: "Scarcely had he reached". The examiner banks on the "sounds right" trap.
Source: PYQ-style

62. Spot the error: The teacher, along with the students, (P) / have gone (Q) / on an excursion. (R)
(a) Part Q (b) Part P (c) Part R (d) No error
Answer: (a)
Solution: Part Q is wrong. With "along with", the verb agrees with the first subject "The teacher", so use "has gone". The plural phrase in the middle is the trap.
Source: PYQ-style

63. Spot the error: No sooner did she enter the room (P) / when (Q) / the lights went off. (R)
(a) Part P (b) Part R (c) Part Q (d) No error
Answer: (c)
Solution: Part Q is wrong. "No sooner" is always paired with "than", never "when": "No sooner did she enter... than the lights went off."
Source: NCERT Class 10 English Grammar

64. Spot the error: He denied (P) / to help (Q) / his friend in need. (R)
(a) No error (b) Part P (c) Part R (d) Part Q
Answer: (d)
Solution: Part Q is wrong. "Deny" is followed by a gerund, not an infinitive: "denied helping his friend". Verbs like avoid, enjoy, mind, suggest also take gerunds.
Source: NCERT Class 10 English Grammar

65. Spot the error: The Ganges (P) / is one of the longest river (Q) / in India. (R)
(a) Part P (b) Part Q (c) Part R (d) No error
Answer: (b)
Solution: Part Q is wrong. After "one of the", the noun must be plural: "one of the longest rivers". The verb remains singular because the subject is "The Ganges".
Source: PYQ-style

66. Spot the error: Unless you do not work hard, (P) / you will not (Q) / pass the examination. (R)
(a) Part P (b) Part Q (c) Part R (d) No error
Answer: (a)
Solution: Part P is wrong — it carries a double negative. "Unless" itself means "if not", so "do not" must be removed: "Unless you work hard". This is a favourite sound-right trap.
Source: PYQ-style

67. Spot the error: The furnitures in this office (P) / are (Q) / very expensive. (R)
(a) Part Q (b) Part P (c) Part R (d) No error
Answer: (b)
Solution: Part P is wrong. "Furniture" is an uncountable noun and has no plural form: "The furniture in this office is very expensive." The verb then becomes singular too.
Source: NCERT Class 9 English Grammar

68. Spot the error: Hardly had he slept (P) / than (Q) / the phone rang. (R)
(a) Part P (b) No error (c) Part Q (d) Part R
Answer: (c)
Solution: Part Q is wrong. "Hardly" pairs with "when", not "than": "Hardly had he slept when the phone rang." Remember: hardly/scarcely → when; no sooner → than.
Source: NCERT Class 10 English Grammar

69. Spot the error: Bread and butter (P) / are (Q) / his daily breakfast. (R)
(a) Part P (b) No error (c) Part R (d) Part Q
Answer: (d)
Solution: Part Q is wrong. "Bread and butter" is treated as one combined dish, so it takes a singular verb: "is his daily breakfast". Two nouns joined by "and" forming one idea take a singular verb.
Source: PYQ-style

70. Spot the error: He is one of the best players (P) / that has ever played (Q) / for the country. (R)
(a) Part P (b) Part Q (c) Part R (d) No error
Answer: (b)
Solution: Part Q is wrong. In "one of the best players that...", the relative pronoun "that" refers to the plural "players", so the verb must be plural: "have ever played". A classic over-thinking trap.
Source: PYQ-style

71. Spot the error: I look forward (P) / to meet (Q) / you at the conference. (R)
(a) Part Q (b) Part R (c) Part P (d) No error
Answer: (a)
Solution: Part Q is wrong. In "look forward to", "to" is a preposition, not part of an infinitive, so a gerund follows: "to meeting you". This is one of the most repeated examiner traps.
Source: PYQ-style

72. Spot the error: The number of accidents (P) / are increasing (Q) / day by day. (R)
(a) Part P (b) Part R (c) Part Q (d) No error
Answer: (c)
Solution: Part Q is wrong. "The number of" takes a singular verb: "is increasing". Contrast: "A number of accidents are..." takes a plural verb.
Source: NCERT Class 10 English Grammar

73. Spot the error: He suggested (P) / me to take (Q) / a day off. (R)
(a) Part P (b) Part Q (c) Part R (d) No error
Answer: (b)
Solution: Part Q is wrong. "Suggest" is never followed by a person plus infinitive. Say "He suggested that I (should) take a day off" or "He suggested taking a day off."
Source: NCERT Class 10 English Grammar

74. Spot the error: Many a soldier (P) / have laid down (Q) / their lives for the nation. (R)
(a) Part Q (b) Part R (c) Part P (d) No error
Answer: (a)
Solution: Part Q is wrong. "Many a" is followed by a singular noun and a singular verb: "Many a soldier has laid down his life". The plural meaning is the trap; the grammar is singular.
Source: PYQ-style

75. Spot the error: Despite of the heavy rain, (P) / the match (Q) / continued as scheduled. (R)
(a) No error (b) Part Q (c) Part R (d) Part P
Answer: (d)
Solution: Part P is wrong. "Despite" never takes "of": "Despite the heavy rain". Use "of" only with "in spite of". Mixing the two is a common sound-right trap.
Source: NCERT Class 10 English Grammar
