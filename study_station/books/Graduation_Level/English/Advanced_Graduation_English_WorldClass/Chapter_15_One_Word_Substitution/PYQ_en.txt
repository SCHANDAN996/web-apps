# 📊 PYQ Analysis — One-word Substitution

## How Exams Test This Chapter

One-word substitution questions are a permanent fixture in SSC, Banking and Railway exams. Examiners test three things: (1) knowledge of Greek/Latin roots, (2) understanding of similar-sounding words, (3) traps based on confusing word pairs. Most questions are direct recall (15-second rule applies), but some require careful reading of the sentence context (45-second rule applies).

## Pattern Analysis

**Frequently tested sub-topics:**
- Words for phobias (fear of...), manias (madness for...), -cide (killing of...), -phile (lover of...)
- Words for government types (autocracy, bureaucracy, democracy, plutocracy)
- Words for people (misanthrope, misogynist, philanthropist, misogamist)
- Words for collections (anthology, bouquet, constellation, fleet)
- Words for speech (soliloquy, monologue, monosyllable)
- Words for writing (autobiography, biography, calligraphy)

**Common examiner traps:**
- Similar-sounding words (e.g., "epitaph" vs "epithet")
- Negative sentences ("Which of the following is NOT...?")
- Chronological confusion (e.g., "prehistoric" vs "historic")
- Near-correct options (e.g., "omniscient" vs "omnipotent")

## Top 10 High-Yield Questions

**Q1.** A person who speaks two languages.
(a) Bilingual (b) Polyglot (c) Multilingual (d) Biped
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Bi-" means two, "lingua" means tongue/language. "Polyglot" means one who knows many languages, not two. "Biped" means a two-footed animal — a sound-alike trap.
Source: NCERT Class 10 English

**Q2.** A person who loves books.
(a) Bibliophile (b) Bibliomaniac (c) Bibliographer (d) Bibliophobe
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Biblio-" means book, "-phile" means lover. "Bibliomaniac" is one with a madness for collecting books, not simple love. "Bibliophobe" is one who fears or hates books — the opposite trap.
Source: PYQ-style

**Q3.** A person who eats only vegetables/plants.
(a) Herbivore (b) Carnivore (c) Omnivore (d) Insectivore
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Herba-" means plants, "-vore" means eater. "Carnivore" means flesh-eater and "omnivore" means one who eats everything — both are near-correct traps.
Source: NCERT Class 10 English

**Q4.** A person who loves mankind.
(a) Misanthrope (b) Philanthropist (c) Misogynist (d) Misogamist
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Phil-" means love, "anthropos" means man/mankind. "Misanthrope" means hater of mankind — the exact opposite trap. "Misogynist" hates women; "misogamist" hates marriage.
Source: PYQ-style

**Q5.** A person who hates mankind.
(a) Philanthropist (b) Misanthrope (c) Philanthropy (d) Anthropologist
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Miso-" means hate, "anthropos" means mankind. "Philanthropist" is the opposite (lover of mankind). "Anthropologist" studies mankind — a root-based trap.
Source: PYQ-style

**Q6.** A person who loves wisdom.
(a) Philosopher (b) Philanthropist (c) Sophomore (d) Sophist
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Philo-" means love, "sophia" means wisdom — hence philosopher. "Philanthropist" loves mankind, not wisdom. "Sophist" is one who argues cleverly but deceptively — a root-based trap.
Source: PYQ-style

**Q7.** A person who loves and enjoys good food and drink; a connoisseur of food.
(a) Gourmet (b) Glutton (c) Herbivore (d) Ascetic
Answer: (a)
Solution: A "gourmet" (from French) is a connoisseur of fine food and drink. "Glutton" is one who eats excessively — quantity, not quality. "Ascetic" is one who shuns pleasures — the opposite trap.
Source: PYQ-style

**Q8.** A person who believes that pleasure is the chief good in life.
(a) Hedonist (b) Ascetic (c) Stoic (d) Sadist
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Hedone" (Greek) means pleasure; a hedonist pursues pleasure as life's goal. "Ascetic" and "stoic" both reject pleasure — opposite traps. "Sadist" enjoys others' pain, not pleasure in general.
Source: PYQ-style

**Q9.** A collection of poems or literary pieces by various authors in one book.
(a) Anthology (b) Bibliography (c) Biography (d) Glossary
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Anthos" (Greek) means flower — an anthology is a "bouquet" of selected writings. "Bibliography" is a list of books, not a collection of texts. "Glossary" is a list of word meanings.
Source: PYQ-style

**Q10.** The scientific study of birds.
(a) Ornithology (b) Entomology (c) Zoology (d) Anthropology
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Ornis" (Greek) means bird — ornithology is the study of birds. "Entomology" is the study of insects — the most common confusion trap. "Zoology" is the study of animals in general.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Science

## Trap Classification

| Trap Type | Example Question | Trap (Distractor) |
|---|---|---|
| Opposite-meaning root | "A person who hates mankind" | Philanthropist (means lover, not hater) |
| Similar-sounding word | "A person who speaks two languages" | Biped (sounds like "bi-" word but means two-footed animal) |
| Near-correct option | "A person who loves books" | Bibliomaniac (madness for books, not love) |
| Root-based confusion | "The study of birds" | Entomology (study of insects, often confused) |

## 15/45-Second Rule Application

**15-second rule (direct recall):** Q1, Q3, Q4, Q5, Q9, Q10 — standard definitions memorised from root lists; answer instantly if known, else mark and move on.

**45-second rule (careful reading):** Q2, Q6, Q7, Q8 — options contain near-correct or opposite-meaning words; read the question twice and eliminate distractors by breaking each option into its roots before answering.
