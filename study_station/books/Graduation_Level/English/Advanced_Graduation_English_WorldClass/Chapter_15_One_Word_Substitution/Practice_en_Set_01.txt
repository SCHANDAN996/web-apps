Questions 1–20: Easy | Questions 21–25: Medium

1. A person who speaks many languages
(a) Polyglot (b) Bilingual (c) Linguist (d) Orator
Answer: (a)
Solution: A polyglot is one who knows or uses several languages. A linguist studies language scientifically but need not speak many.
Source: PYQ-style

2. One who loves mankind
(a) Philanthropist (b) Misanthrope (c) Optimist (d) Humanitarian aid
Answer: (a)
Solution: Philanthropist comes from Greek philos (love) + anthropos (man). A misanthrope is the opposite — one who hates mankind.
Source: NCERT-based vocabulary

3. A place where dead bodies are kept for identification
(a) Cemetery (b) Mortuary (c) Sanatorium (d) Monastery
Answer: (b)
Solution: A mortuary (morgue) keeps dead bodies for identification or post-mortem. A cemetery is where bodies are buried, not kept for identification.
Source: PYQ-style

4. One who cannot read or write
(a) Literate (b) Scholar (c) Illiterate (d) Novice
Answer: (c)
Solution: Illiterate means unable to read or write. Do not confuse with "innumerate", which means unable to work with numbers.
Source: PYQ-style

5. A government by the people
(a) Autocracy (b) Monarchy (c) Bureaucracy (d) Democracy
Answer: (d)
Solution: Democracy = demos (people) + kratos (rule). Autocracy is rule by one person with absolute power.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Civics

6. One who looks at the bright side of things
(a) Pessimist (b) Optimist (c) Idealist (d) Realist
Answer: (b)
Solution: An optimist expects favourable outcomes; a pessimist expects the worst. This pair is frequently tested together.
Source: PYQ-style

7. A place where birds are kept
(a) Apiary (b) Stable (c) Aquarium (d) Aviary
Answer: (d)
Solution: Aviary (Latin avis = bird) is for birds. Apiary is for bees — a classic examiner trap pairing the two.
Source: PYQ-style

8. Murder of one's own brother
(a) Homicide (b) Patricide (c) Fratricide (d) Regicide
Answer: (c)
Solution: Fratricide = frater (brother) + cide (killing). Patricide is killing one's father; regicide is killing a king.
Source: PYQ-style

9. One who eats human flesh
(a) Cannibal (b) Carnivore (c) Herbivore (d) Omnivore
Answer: (a)
Solution: A cannibal eats human flesh. A carnivore eats the flesh of animals — the examiner's favourite confusion.
Source: PYQ-style

10. A list of books available in a library
(a) Glossary (b) Catalogue (c) Index (d) Anthology
Answer: (b)
Solution: A catalogue is a complete list of items (books in a library). An anthology is a collection of poems or writings in one book.
Source: PYQ-style

11. One who does not believe in the existence of God
(a) Agnostic (b) Ascetic (c) Atheist (d) Apostate
Answer: (c)
Solution: An atheist denies God's existence; an agnostic says nothing can be known about God. This distinction is a repeated trap.
Source: PYQ-style

12. That which cannot be corrected
(a) Incredible (b) Inevitable (c) Infallible (d) Incorrigible
Answer: (d)
Solution: Incorrigible means incapable of being corrected or reformed. Inevitable means that which cannot be avoided — a near-miss trap.
Source: PYQ-style

13. A person who lives at the same time as another
(a) Colleague (b) Contemporary (c) Companion (d) Comrade
Answer: (b)
Solution: Contemporary (con = together, tempus = time) means living in the same period. Colleague refers to working together, not time.
Source: PYQ-style

14. One who pretends to be what he is not
(a) Diplomat (b) Imposter syndrome (c) Sycophant (d) Hypocrite
Answer: (d)
Solution: A hypocrite pretends to have virtues or beliefs he does not possess. A sycophant flatters the powerful for gain.
Source: PYQ-style

15. A place where weapons and ammunition are stored
(a) Hangar (b) Granary (c) Arsenal (d) Depot
Answer: (c)
Solution: An arsenal stores arms and ammunition. A granary stores grain; a hangar houses aircraft.
Source: PYQ-style

16. One who is all-powerful
(a) Omniscient (b) Omnipotent (c) Omnipresent (d) Sovereign
Answer: (b)
Solution: Omnipotent = omni (all) + potens (powerful). Omniscient means all-knowing; omnipresent means present everywhere — the classic triple trap.
Source: PYQ-style

17. The scientific study of birds
(a) Entomology (b) Zoology (c) Ornithology (d) Anthropology
Answer: (c)
Solution: Ornithology (Greek ornis = bird) is the study of birds. Entomology is the study of insects — a frequent confusion.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Science

18. A word or law no longer in use
(a) Extinct (b) Ancient (c) Archaic law (d) Obsolete
Answer: (d)
Solution: Obsolete means no longer in use or out of date. Extinct applies to species that have died out completely, not words or laws.
Source: PYQ-style

19. One who walks in sleep
(a) Somnambulist (b) Insomniac (c) Somniloquist (d) Dreamer
Answer: (a)
Solution: Somnambulist = somnus (sleep) + ambulare (walk). A somniloquist talks in sleep — the examiner's paired trap.
Source: PYQ-style

20. A speech delivered without preparation
(a) Monologue (b) Extempore (c) Soliloquy (d) Rhetoric
Answer: (b)
Solution: Extempore means spoken without prior preparation. A soliloquy is speaking one's thoughts aloud when alone.
Source: PYQ-style

21. One who is indifferent to pleasure or pain
(a) Sadist (b) Ascetic (c) Masochist (d) Stoic
Answer: (d)
Solution: A stoic remains unmoved by pleasure or pain. An ascetic renounces worldly pleasures deliberately — a subtle but tested difference.
Source: PYQ-style

22. A person who collects stamps
(a) Philatelist (b) Numismatist (c) Cartographer (d) Archaeologist
Answer: (a)
Solution: A philatelist collects stamps. A numismatist collects coins — the two are routinely swapped in options to trap candidates.
Source: PYQ-style

23. That which can be easily broken
(a) Fragile (b) Flexible (c) Ductile (d) Brittle glass
Answer: (a)
Solution: Fragile means easily broken or damaged. Flexible means easily bent without breaking — the opposite quality.
Source: PYQ-style

24. One who knows everything
(a) Omnipotent (b) Omnivorous (c) Omniscient (d) Veteran
Answer: (c)
Solution: Omniscient = omni (all) + scire (to know). Omnivorous means eating all kinds of food — a sound-alike trap.
Source: PYQ-style

25. A place where leather is tanned
(a) Tannery (b) Brewery (c) Mint (d) Kiln
Answer: (a)
Solution: A tannery is where hides are converted into leather. A mint is where coins are made; a brewery makes beer.
Source: PYQ-style
