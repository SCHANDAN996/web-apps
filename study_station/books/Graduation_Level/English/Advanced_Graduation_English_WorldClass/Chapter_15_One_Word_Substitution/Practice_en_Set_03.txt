51. A person who speaks many languages
(a) Multilingual speaker (b) Bilingual (c) Linguist (d) Polyglot
Answer: (d)
Solution: "Polyglot" (Greek: poly = many, glotta = tongue) is the standard one-word substitute. "Linguist" is a trap — it means one who studies language scientifically, not necessarily a speaker of many languages.
Source: PYQ-style

52. A place where dead bodies are kept for identification
(a) Cemetery (b) Mortuary (c) Crematorium (d) Mausoleum
Answer: (b)
Solution: A mortuary is where bodies are kept before burial/identification. A cemetery is a burial ground; a crematorium is for cremation; a mausoleum is a grand tomb.
Source: NCERT-based vocabulary

53. One who does not believe in the existence of God
(a) Atheist (b) Agnostic (c) Ascetic (d) Apostate
Answer: (a)
Solution: An atheist denies God's existence. An agnostic (trap option) believes God's existence is unknowable — a classic examiner confusion pair.
Source: PYQ-style

54. A government run by the people, for the people
(a) Democracy (b) Autocracy (c) Bureaucracy (d) Oligarchy
Answer: (a)
Solution: Democracy = rule by the people (Greek: demos = people, kratos = rule). Autocracy is rule by one; oligarchy by a few; bureaucracy by officials.
Source: PYQ-style

55. A list of books available in a library
(a) Anthology (b) Glossary (c) Index (d) Catalogue
Answer: (d)
Solution: A catalogue is a systematic list of items/books. A glossary explains difficult words; an index lists topics in a book; an anthology is a collection of poems/stories.
Source: PYQ-style

56. One who can use both hands equally well
(a) Ambivalent (b) Ambiguous (c) Ambidextrous (d) Amphibian
Answer: (c)
Solution: Ambidextrous (Latin: ambi = both, dexter = right hand). Ambivalent means having mixed feelings — a frequent sound-alike trap.
Source: PYQ-style

57. Murder of one's own brother
(a) Fratricide (b) Homicide (c) Patricide (d) Regicide
Answer: (a)
Solution: Fratricide (Latin: frater = brother). Patricide = killing one's father; regicide = killing a king; homicide = killing a human generally.
Source: PYQ-style

58. A place where bees are kept
(a) Apiary (b) Aviary (c) Aquarium (d) Stable
Answer: (a)
Solution: Apiary (Latin: apis = bee). Aviary is for birds — the most common trap pair in exams. Aquarium is for fish; stable for horses.
Source: PYQ-style

59. One who loves mankind
(a) Misanthropist (b) Philanthropist (c) Anthropologist (d) Humanitarian worker
Answer: (b)
Solution: Philanthropist (Greek: philos = loving, anthropos = man). Misanthropist is the exact opposite — hates mankind. Examiners love this pair.
Source: PYQ-style

60. A speech delivered without preparation
(a) Soliloquy (b) Extempore (c) Monologue (d) Rhetoric
Answer: (b)
Solution: Extempore means spoken without prior preparation. A soliloquy is talking to oneself on stage; a monologue is a long speech by one person.
Source: PYQ-style

61. That which cannot be corrected
(a) Incorrigible (b) Invincible (c) Inevitable (d) Indelible
Answer: (a)
Solution: Incorrigible = incapable of being corrected or reformed. Invincible = cannot be defeated; inevitable = cannot be avoided; indelible = cannot be erased. All four begin with "in-" — a deliberate examiner trap.
Source: PYQ-style

62. A person who lives at the same time as another
(a) Comrade (b) Colleague (c) Companion (d) Contemporary
Answer: (d)
Solution: Contemporary (Latin: con = together, tempus = time) means living in the same period. Colleague/companion/comrade imply working or friendship relations, not time.
Source: PYQ-style

63. A place where weapons and ammunition are stored
(a) Granary (b) Arsenal (c) Hangar (d) Depot
Answer: (b)
Solution: Arsenal = store of arms and ammunition. Granary stores grain; hangar houses aircraft; depot is a general storage place.
Source: PYQ-style

64. One who pretends to be what he is not
(a) Sycophant (b) Imposter syndrome (c) Hypocrite (d) Sceptic
Answer: (c)
Solution: A hypocrite feigns virtue or beliefs he does not hold. A sycophant flatters for gain; a sceptic doubts everything.
Source: PYQ-style

65. The study of ancient things, especially through material remains
(a) Geology (b) Anthropology (c) Archaeology (d) Palaeography
Answer: (c)
Solution: Archaeology studies past human life via artefacts and remains. Palaeography is the study of ancient handwriting — a close trap. Anthropology studies humans broadly; geology studies the earth.
Source: PYQ-style

66. A word or phrase no longer in current use
(a) Archaic (b) Obsolete currency (c) Antique (d) Ancient
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Archaic" describes words/expressions fallen out of everyday use. "Obsolete" also fits things, but for words "archaic" is the precise exam answer; "antique" refers to old objects of value.
Source: PYQ-style

67. One who eats human flesh
(a) Cannibal (b) Carnivore (c) Herbivore (d) Omnivore
Answer: (a)
Solution: A cannibal eats human flesh. Carnivore = flesh-eater generally; herbivore = plant-eater; omnivore = eats both.
Source: PYQ-style

68. A place where leather is tanned
(a) Mint (b) Brewery (c) Tannery (d) Kiln
Answer: (c)
Solution: A tannery processes animal hides into leather. Brewery = beer; mint = coins; kiln = bricks/pottery baking.
Source: PYQ-style

69. That which can be easily broken
(a) Flexible (b) Ductile (c) Fragile glass only (d) Brittle
Answer: (d)
Solution: Brittle = hard but easily broken (like glass or biscuits). Flexible bends; ductile can be drawn into wires. Option three is wrong because brittleness is not limited to glass.
Source: PYQ-style

70. A person who collects stamps
(a) Archivist (b) Numismatist (c) Cartographer (d) Philatelist
Answer: (d)
Solution: Philatelist collects stamps (Greek: philos + ateles = tax-free stamp). Numismatist collects coins — the standard paired trap.
Source: PYQ-style

71. A song sung at a funeral or for the dead
(a) Sonnet (b) Ode (c) Elegy (d) Ballad
Answer: (c)
Solution: An elegy is a mournful poem/song for the dead. An ode praises; a sonnet is a 14-line poem; a ballad tells a story.
Source: PYQ-style

72. One who looks at the bright side of things
(a) Pessimist (b) Optimist (c) Idealist dreamer (d) Realist
Answer: (b)
Solution: An optimist expects the best; a pessimist expects the worst. This pair is among the most frequently asked antonym-trap substitutions.
Source: PYQ-style

73. A place where coins are made
(a) Treasury (b) Bank (c) Mint (d) Exchange
Answer: (c)
Solution: Coins are manufactured at a mint. A treasury stores government funds; a bank deals in money but does not manufacture coins.
Source: PYQ-style

74. An animal that lives both on land and in water
(a) Aquatic animal (b) Reptile (c) Mammal (d) Amphibian
Answer: (d)
Solution: Amphibian (Greek: amphi = both, bios = life) — e.g., frog. "Aquatic animal" lives only in water, so it fails the "both" condition.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Science

75. A handwriting that cannot be read
(a) Ineligible (b) Illegible (c) Invisible (d) Unreadable print
Answer: (b)
Solution: Illegible = impossible to read (usually handwriting). Ineligible = not qualified — a sound-alike trap examiners use often. Invisible = cannot be seen.
Source: PYQ-style
