Card 1
Front: A person who speaks two languages?
Back: Bilingual. (Root: bi- = two, lingua = tongue/language.)

Card 2
Front: One who looks at the bright side of things?
Back: Optimist. (Opposite: Pessimist — one who looks at the dark side.)

Card 3
Front: A government by the people, of the people, for the people?
Back: Democracy. (Root: demos = people, kratos = rule/power.)

Card 4
Front: MNEMONIC — How to remember "Misanthrope" vs "Philanthrope"?
Back: "MIS = Miss/Hate, PHIL = Love." Misanthrope = one who hates mankind; Philanthrope = one who loves mankind. Think: "Miss Anthrope avoids people; Phil Anthrope fills people's lives."

Card 5
Front: A word or law no longer in use?
Back: Obsolete.

Card 6
Front: EXAMINER'S TRAP — "One who can use both hands equally well" — Ambidextrous or Ambivalent?
Back: Ambidextrous. Ambivalent means having mixed/contradictory feelings about something. Examiners pair them because both start with "ambi-" (both). Hands = dexterous; feelings = ambivalent.

Card 7
Front: Murder of one's own brother?
Back: Fratricide. (Root: frater = brother, -cide = killing. Compare: homicide = killing of a human, patricide = killing of one's father.)

Card 8
Front: A person who eats human flesh?
Back: Cannibal. (Not "carnivore" — a carnivore eats the flesh of animals generally.)

Card 9
Front: A list of books available in a library?
Back: Catalogue.

Card 10
Front: One who does not believe in the existence of God?
Back: Atheist. (Contrast: Agnostic — one who believes God's existence is unknown/unknowable; Theist — believer.)

Card 11
Front: MNEMONIC — How to remember "Egotist" vs "Altruist"?
Back: "Ego = I, Altru = All others." Egotist = one who always talks about oneself; Altruist = one who lives for the welfare of others. Trick: "Ego sits alone; Altru shares the throne."

Card 12
Front: A speech delivered without preparation?
Back: Extempore (also: Impromptu).

Card 13
Front: EXAMINER'S TRAP — "That which cannot be avoided" — Inevitable or Incredible?
Back: Inevitable. Incredible means "hard to believe." Examiners exploit the similar sound. Trick: "In-evitable = cannot evade (escape)."

Card 14
Front: A place where dead bodies are kept for identification?
Back: Mortuary. (Not "cemetery" — that is a burial ground.)

Card 15
Front: One who knows everything?
Back: Omniscient. (Root: omni = all, sciens = knowing. Family: omnipotent = all-powerful, omnipresent = present everywhere.)

Card 16
Front: A person who collects stamps?
Back: Philatelist. (Root: phil = love, ateleia = exemption from tax — stamps showed tax was prepaid.)

Card 17
Front: Writing on a wall, building, or monument?
Back: Inscription. (On a grave specifically: Epitaph.)

Card 18
Front: A person who lives at the same time as another?
Back: Contemporary. (Root: con- = together, tempus = time.)

Card 19
Front: That which can be easily carried?
Back: Portable. (Root: portare = to carry. Family: transport, export, import.)

Card 20
Front: A person appointed by two parties to settle a dispute?
Back: Arbitrator. (A mediator only facilitates; an arbitrator gives a binding decision — a classic exam distinction.)
