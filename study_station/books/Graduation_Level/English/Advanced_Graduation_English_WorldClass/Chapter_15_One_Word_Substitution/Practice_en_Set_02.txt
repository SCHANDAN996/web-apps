Questions 26–40: Easy | Questions 41–50: Medium

26. A person who speaks many languages
(a) Linguist (b) Bilingual (c) Polyglot (d) Orator
Answer: (c)
Solution: A polyglot is one who knows or uses many languages. A linguist studies language scientifically but need not speak many.
Source: PYQ-style

27. One who loves mankind
(a) Misanthrope (b) Philanthropist (c) Optimist (d) Humanitarian aid worker
Answer: (b)
Solution: Philanthropist comes from Greek philos (love) + anthropos (mankind). A misanthrope is the opposite — one who hates mankind.
Source: PYQ-style

28. The scientific study of birds
(a) Ornithology (b) Entomology (c) Zoology (d) Anthropology
Answer: (a)
Solution: Ornithology (Greek ornis = bird) is the study of birds. Entomology is the study of insects — a classic examiner's confusion pair.
Source: NCERT-based vocabulary

29. A government by the people
(a) Autocracy (b) Democracy (c) Oligarchy (d) Bureaucracy
Answer: (b)
Solution: Democracy = demos (people) + kratos (rule). Autocracy is rule by one person; oligarchy is rule by a few.
Source: PYQ-style

30. One who cannot read or write
(a) Literate (b) Scholar (c) Illiterate (d) Novice
Answer: (c)
Solution: Illiterate means unable to read or write. A novice is simply a beginner at any activity, not necessarily uneducated.
Source: PYQ-style

31. A place where dead bodies are kept for identification
(a) Cemetery (b) Monastery (c) Sanatorium (d) Mortuary
Answer: (d)
Solution: A mortuary (or morgue) keeps dead bodies for identification or post-mortem. A cemetery is where bodies are buried.
Source: PYQ-style

32. One who eats human flesh
(a) Herbivore (b) Carnivore (c) Cannibal (d) Omnivore
Answer: (c)
Solution: A cannibal eats human flesh. A carnivore eats the flesh of animals in general — the examiner's favourite near-miss option.
Source: PYQ-style

33. A list of books available in a library
(a) Glossary (b) Catalogue (c) Index (d) Bibliography
Answer: (b)
Solution: A catalogue is the complete list of items (books) in a library. A bibliography lists books referred to by an author.
Source: PYQ-style

34. One who looks at the bright side of things
(a) Pessimist (b) Realist (c) Idealist (d) Optimist
Answer: (d)
Solution: An optimist expects favourable outcomes; a pessimist expects the worst. An idealist pursues high ideals, which is a different idea.
Source: PYQ-style

35. Murder of one's own brother
(a) Fratricide (b) Homicide (c) Patricide (d) Regicide
Answer: (a)
Solution: Fratricide = frater (brother) + caedere (to kill). Patricide is killing one's father; regicide is killing a king.
Source: PYQ-style

36. A person who lives at the same time as another
(a) Companion (b) Colleague (c) Contemporary (d) Comrade
Answer: (c)
Solution: Contemporary = con (together) + tempus (time). A colleague is a co-worker, not necessarily of the same era.
Source: PYQ-style

37. That which cannot be believed
(a) Inevitable (b) Incredible (c) Invincible (d) Invisible
Answer: (b)
Solution: Incredible means beyond belief. Inevitable = cannot be avoided; invincible = cannot be defeated; invisible = cannot be seen. The examiner banks on these similar-looking words.
Source: PYQ-style

38. One who pretends to be what he is not
(a) Sycophant (b) Imposter syndrome (c) Hypocrite (d) Diplomat
Answer: (c)
Solution: A hypocrite feigns virtue or beliefs he does not hold. A sycophant flatters powerful people for gain — a different fault.
Source: PYQ-style

39. A place where coins are made
(a) Factory (b) Bank (c) Treasury (d) Mint
Answer: (d)
Solution: Coins are manufactured at a mint. A treasury stores government funds; a bank deals in money transactions.
Source: PYQ-style

40. One who does not believe in the existence of God
(a) Agnostic (b) Apostate (c) Ascetic (d) Atheist
Answer: (d)
Solution: An atheist denies God's existence; an agnostic says God's existence cannot be known. Examiners love this atheist–agnostic trap.
Source: PYQ-style

41. A speech delivered without preparation
(a) Extempore (b) Monologue (c) Soliloquy (d) Rhetoric
Answer: (a)
Solution: Extempore means spoken without prior preparation. A soliloquy is speaking one's thoughts aloud, usually in drama.
Source: PYQ-style

42. One who is indifferent to pleasure or pain
(a) Sadist (b) Cynic (c) Masochist (d) Stoic
Answer: (d)
Solution: A stoic remains unmoved by pleasure or pain. A cynic distrusts human motives; a sadist enjoys others' pain.
Source: PYQ-style

43. The practice of having only one wife or husband at a time
(a) Monogamy (b) Polygamy (c) Bigamy (d) Celibacy
Answer: (a)
Solution: Monogamy = mono (one) + gamos (marriage). Bigamy is having two spouses; polygamy is having many.
Source: PYQ-style

44. A word or law no longer in use
(a) Obsolete (b) Obscure (c) Ancient (d) Archaic script
Answer: (a)
Solution: Obsolete means out of date and no longer used. Obscure means unclear or little known — a near-sounding distractor.
Source: PYQ-style

45. One who walks in sleep
(a) Insomniac (b) Somniloquist (c) Somnambulist (d) Dreamer
Answer: (c)
Solution: Somnambulist = somnus (sleep) + ambulare (to walk). A somniloquist talks in sleep — the examiner's twin trap.
Source: PYQ-style

46. That which can be easily broken
(a) Flexible (b) Fragile (c) Ductile (d) Brittle glassware
Answer: (b)
Solution: Fragile means easily broken or damaged. Flexible means easily bent without breaking — the opposite quality.
Source: PYQ-style

47. A person who collects stamps
(a) Numismatist (b) Philatelist (c) Cartographer (d) Archaeologist
Answer: (b)
Solution: A philatelist collects and studies stamps. A numismatist collects coins — the standard confusion pair in exams.
Source: PYQ-style

48. An animal that lives both on land and in water
(a) Amphibian (b) Reptile (c) Aquatic (d) Mammal
Answer: (a)
Solution: Amphibian = amphi (both) + bios (life), e.g., a frog. Aquatic animals live only in water.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Science

49. A place where weapons and ammunition are stored
(a) Arsenal (b) Granary (c) Hangar (d) Apiary
Answer: (a)
Solution: An arsenal stores arms and ammunition. A granary stores grain; a hangar houses aircraft; an apiary keeps bees.
Source: PYQ-style

50. One who knows everything
(a) Omnipotent (b) Omnivorous (c) Omnipresent (d) Omniscient
Answer: (d)
Solution: Omniscient = omni (all) + scire (to know). Omnipotent = all-powerful; omnipresent = present everywhere. This trio is the examiner's classic trap — match the suffix to the meaning.
Source: PYQ-style
