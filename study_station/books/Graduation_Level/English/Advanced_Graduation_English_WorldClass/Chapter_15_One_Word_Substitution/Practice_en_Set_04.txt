76. A person who speaks many languages
(a) Linguist (b) Bilingual (c) Polyglot (d) Multilateral
Answer: (c)
Solution: A polyglot knows or uses several languages. A linguist studies language scientifically but need not speak many; bilingual means only two languages.
Source: NCERT-based vocabulary / PYQ-style

77. The scientific study of birds
(a) Ornithology (b) Entomology (c) Ichthyology (d) Herpetology
Answer: (a)
Solution: Ornithology comes from Greek 'ornis' (bird). Entomology is the study of insects — the classic trap pair in exams.
Source: PYQ-style

78. A government by the people, of the people, for the people
(a) Autocracy (b) Oligarchy (c) Bureaucracy (d) Democracy
Answer: (d)
Solution: Democracy = rule by the people (Greek 'demos' = people, 'kratos' = rule). Oligarchy is rule by a few; autocracy is rule by one.
Source: PYQ-style

79. One who does not believe in the existence of God
(a) Agnostic (b) Atheist (c) Ascetic (d) Apostate
Answer: (b)
Solution: An atheist denies God's existence. Examiner's trap: an agnostic only holds that God's existence is unknowable — do not confuse the two.
Source: PYQ-style

80. The murder of one's own brother
(a) Homicide (b) Regicide (c) Fratricide (d) Patricide
Answer: (c)
Solution: Fratricide (Latin 'frater' = brother). Regicide is killing a king; patricide is killing one's father; homicide is killing any human.

81. A list of books available in a library
(a) Glossary (b) Catalogue (c) Index (d) Anthology
Answer: (b)
Solution: A catalogue is a systematic list of items (books in a library). An anthology is a collection of poems or writings; an index locates topics within one book.

82. One who looks at the bright side of things
(a) Optimist (b) Pessimist (c) Idealist (d) Philanthropist
Answer: (a)
Solution: An optimist expects favourable outcomes; a pessimist expects the worst. An idealist pursues high ideals, which is not the same as habitual hopefulness.
Source: PYQ-style

83. A place where dead bodies are kept for identification
(a) Cemetery (b) Crematorium (c) Mortuary (d) Mausoleum
Answer: (c)
Solution: A mortuary (morgue) stores bodies pending identification or autopsy. A cemetery is a burial ground; a mausoleum is a grand tomb.
Source: PYQ-style

84. That which cannot be read
(a) Inedible (b) Invisible (c) Illicit (d) Illegible
Answer: (d)
Solution: Illegible = impossible to read (bad handwriting). Illicit means unlawful — a frequent sound-alike trap; inedible means unfit to eat.
Source: PYQ-style

85. A person who lives at the same time as another
(a) Companion (b) Colleague (c) Contemporary (d) Comrade
Answer: (c)
Solution: Contemporary (Latin 'con-' = together, 'tempus' = time) means living in the same period. Colleague and companion imply a working or personal relationship, not merely shared time.
Source: PYQ-style

86. The practice of having only one wife or husband at a time
(a) Polygamy (b) Celibacy (c) Bigamy (d) Monogamy
Answer: (d)
Solution: Monogamy (Greek 'monos' = one, 'gamos' = marriage). Bigamy is the crime of two simultaneous marriages; celibacy is remaining unmarried.
Source: PYQ-style

87. A speech delivered without any previous preparation
(a) Extempore (b) Soliloquy (c) Rhetoric (d) Monologue
Answer: (a)
Solution: Extempore means spoken without preparation. A soliloquy is speaking one's thoughts aloud alone — prepared or not, it is a drama device, not impromptu speech.
Source: PYQ-style

88. One who is indifferent to pleasure and pain
(a) Sadist (b) Stoic (c) Cynic (d) Epicure
Answer: (b)
Solution: A stoic endures pleasure and pain without showing feeling. A cynic distrusts human motives; an epicure delights in fine food — the opposite pole.
Source: PYQ-style

89. The study of ancient things, especially through material remains
(a) Geology (b) Palaeography (c) Anthropology (d) Archaeology
Answer: (d)
Solution: Archaeology studies past human life through artefacts and ruins. Palaeography is the study of ancient handwriting — a close distractor; anthropology studies humans broadly.
Source: PYQ-style

90. A word or law no longer in use
(a) Obsolete (b) Obscure (c) Absolute (d) Obstinate
Answer: (a)
Solution: Obsolete means out of date or no longer used. Obscure means unclear or little known — the word may still be in use, so it is the trap option.
Source: PYQ-style

91. One who eats human flesh
(a) Herbivore (b) Carnivore (c) Omnivore (d) Cannibal
Answer: (d)
Solution: A cannibal eats human flesh. A carnivore eats the flesh of animals generally — examiners exploit this near-miss; omnivore eats both plants and flesh.
Source: PYQ-style

92. A place where weapons and ammunition are stored
(a) Arsenal (b) Granary (c) Hangar (d) Apiary
Answer: (a)
Solution: An arsenal (or armoury) stores arms. A granary stores grain, a hangar houses aircraft, and an apiary keeps bees.
Source: PYQ-style

93. That which cannot be corrected or improved
(a) Inevitable (b) Incorrigible (c) Incredible (d) Infallible
Answer: (b)
Solution: Incorrigible = incapable of being corrected (a habitually bad person). Infallible means incapable of error — the opposite sense and a common trap.
Source: PYQ-style

94. A person who collects stamps
(a) Numismatist (b) Philatelist (c) Cartographer (d) Lexicographer
Answer: (b)
Solution: A philatelist collects and studies stamps. A numismatist collects coins — the standard confusing pair; a lexicographer compiles dictionaries.
Source: PYQ-style

95. The act of killing one's own mother
(a) Matricide (b) Patricide (c) Uxoricide (d) Infanticide
Answer: (a)
Solution: Matricide (Latin 'mater' = mother). Uxoricide is killing one's wife; infanticide is killing an infant. Remember: 'mater' → matricide, 'pater' → patricide.
Source: PYQ-style

96. A statement that contradicts itself yet may express a truth
(a) Oxymoron (b) Antithesis (c) Paradox (d) Irony
Answer: (c)
Solution: A paradox is a self-contradictory statement that may still be true ("less is more"). Examiner's trap: an oxymoron is only a two-word contradictory phrase like "deafening silence", not a full statement.
Source: PYQ-style

97. One who pretends to be what he is not, especially in matters of virtue or religion
(a) Sycophant (b) Impostor (c) Hypocrite (d) Charlatan
Answer: (c)
Solution: A hypocrite feigns virtue or beliefs he does not hold. A charlatan falsely claims skill or knowledge (e.g., fake doctor); an impostor assumes a false identity — the distinction examiners test.
Source: PYQ-style

98. A word having the same or nearly the same meaning as another word, but which can never fully replace it in every context
(a) Antonym (b) Synonym (c) Homonym (d) Pseudonym
Answer: (b)
Solution: A synonym shares meaning with another word, though usage contexts may differ. A homonym merely shares spelling or sound; a pseudonym is a fictitious name.
Source: PYQ-style

99. The belief that events are decided by fate and cannot be changed by human effort
(a) Fatalism (b) Determinism (c) Nihilism (d) Pragmatism
Answer: (a)
Solution: Fatalism accepts all events as fixed by fate. Determinism is the broader philosophical doctrine that all events have prior causes — examiners use it as the near-correct trap; nihilism rejects all meaning.
Source: PYQ-style

100. One who can use both hands with equal skill
(a) Ambivalent (b) Amphibian (c) Ambiguous (d) Ambidextrous
Answer: (d)
Solution: Ambidextrous (Latin 'ambi-' = both, 'dexter' = right hand) means equally skilled with both hands. Trap: ambivalent means having mixed feelings, and ambiguous means having two meanings — all share 'ambi-' but differ sharply.
Source: PYQ-style
