126. A person who speaks many languages
(a) Polyglot (b) Bilingual (c) Linguist (d) Multilingual speaker
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Polyglot" (Greek poly = many, glotta = tongue) is the precise one-word substitute. "Linguist" is a student of language science, not necessarily a speaker of many languages — a classic trap.
Source: PYQ-style

127. A government by the wealthy
(a) Plutocracy (b) Aristocracy (c) Oligarchy (d) Bureaucracy
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Plutocracy" (Greek ploutos = wealth) means rule by the rich. "Aristocracy" is rule by nobility, and "oligarchy" is rule by a small group — examiners pair these to confuse.
Source: PYQ-style

128. One who believes that the existence of God can neither be proved nor disproved
(a) Sceptic (b) Agnostic (c) Atheist (d) Cynic
Answer: (b)
Solution: An "agnostic" holds that God's existence is unknowable, neither affirming nor denying it. An "atheist" denies God's existence outright, and a "sceptic" doubts knowledge claims generally — a subtle but frequently tested distinction.
Source: PYQ-style

129. The scientific study of birds
(a) Entomology (b) Ornithology (c) Herpetology (d) Ichthyology
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Ornithology" (Greek ornis = bird) is the study of birds. "Entomology" is insects, "herpetology" reptiles, "ichthyology" fish.

130. A speech delivered without preparation
(a) Rhetoric (b) Soliloquy (c) Monologue (d) Extempore

Solution: "Extempore" means spoken without prior preparation. A "soliloquy" is speaking one's thoughts aloud alone — a near-miss distractor.

131. Murder of one's own brother
(a) Homicide (b) Fratricide (c) Patricide (d) Regicide
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Fratricide" (Latin frater = brother) is killing one's brother. "Patricide" is killing one's father, "regicide" killing a king. The -cide family is a favourite examiner cluster.

Answer: (a)

133. A place where dead bodies are kept for identification
(a) Cemetery (b) Mausoleum (c) Mortuary (d) Crematorium
Answer: (c)
Solution: A "mortuary" (or morgue) is where bodies are kept before burial or identification. A "cemetery" is a burial ground, a "mausoleum" a grand tomb.

134. One who does not believe in the existence of God
(a) Agnostic (b) Ascetic (c) Atheist (d) Apostate
Answer: (c)
Solution: An "atheist" denies God's existence. An "ascetic" practises severe self-discipline; an "apostate" abandons his religion — both are tempting distractors.

135. A word or phrase no longer in current use
(a) Archaic (b) Obsolete coinage (c) Neologism (d) Colloquialism
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Archaic" describes words that have fallen out of everyday use. A "neologism" is the opposite — a newly coined word — the classic antonym trap.

136. The study of ancient things through material remains
(a) Archaeology (b) Palaeontology (c) Anthropology (d) Ethnology
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Archaeology" studies past human life through artefacts and ruins. "Palaeontology" studies fossils — examiners exploit this overlap.

137. One who looks at the bright side of things

138. A list of books available in a library
(a) Catalogue (b) Index (c) Glossary (d) Bibliography
Answer: (a)
Solution: A "catalogue" lists items in a collection such as a library. A "bibliography" lists books referred to in a work — a frequent confusion pair.
