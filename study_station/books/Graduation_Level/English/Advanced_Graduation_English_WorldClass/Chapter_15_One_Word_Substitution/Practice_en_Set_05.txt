101. A person who speaks many languages
(a) Polyglot (b) Bilingual (c) Linguist (d) Multilateral
Answer: (a)
Solution: A polyglot is one who knows or uses several languages (Greek poly = many, glotta = tongue). A linguist studies language scientifically but need not speak many; bilingual means only two languages.

102. A government by the wealthy
(a) Aristocracy (b) Plutocracy (c) Oligarchy (d) Bureaucracy
Answer: (b)
Solution: Plutocracy = rule by the rich (Greek ploutos = wealth). Aristocracy is rule by nobility; oligarchy is rule by a few; bureaucracy is rule by officials.

103. That which cannot be corrected
(a) Invincible (b) Indelible (c) Inevitable (d) Incorrigible
Answer: (d)
Solution: Incorrigible means incapable of being corrected or reformed. Invincible = cannot be defeated; inevitable = cannot be avoided; indelible = cannot be erased.

104. A person who pretends to have more knowledge or skill than he really has
(a) Hypocrite (b) Charlatan (c) Impostor (d) Sycophant
Answer: (b)
Solution: A charlatan is a fake expert or quack. A hypocrite pretends to have virtues; an impostor assumes a false identity; a sycophant flatters for gain.

105. The scientific study of birds
(a) Entomology (b) Zoology (c) Ornithology (d) Anthropology
Answer: (c)
Solution: Ornithology is the study of birds (Greek ornis = bird). Entomology is the study of insects — a classic examiner trap pairing the two.

106. A place where dead bodies are kept for identification
(a) Cemetery (b) Mausoleum (c) Mortuary (d) Crematorium
Answer: (c)
Solution: A mortuary (morgue) keeps dead bodies for identification or autopsy. A cemetery is a burial ground; a mausoleum is a stately tomb; a crematorium is for cremation.

107. One who does not believe in the existence of God
(a) Agnostic (b) Atheist (c) Ascetic (d) Apostate
Answer: (b)
Solution: An atheist denies God's existence. Examiner's trap: an agnostic merely holds that God's existence is unknowable — do not confuse the two.

108. A speech delivered without preparation
(a) Rhetoric (b) Soliloquy (c) Monologue (d) Extempore
Answer: (d)
Solution: Extempore means spoken without prior preparation. A soliloquy is speaking one's thoughts aloud alone; rhetoric is the art of persuasive speaking.

109. Murder of one's own brother
(a) Homicide (b) Regicide (c) Patricide (d) Fratricide
Answer: (d)
Solution: Fratricide = killing one's brother (Latin frater = brother). Patricide = killing one's father; regicide = killing a king; homicide = killing a human being generally.

110. A person who lives on the earnings of others
(a) Parasite (b) Patriot (c) Pauper (d) Pessimist
Answer: (a)
Solution: A parasite lives off others without contributing. A pauper is simply very poor — a trap option since poverty does not imply living off others.

111. That which can be easily broken
(a) Flexible (b) Fertile (c) Futile (d) Fragile
Answer: (d)
Solution: Fragile means easily broken (Latin frangere = to break). Flexible bends without breaking; futile means useless; fertile means productive.

112. A list of books available in a library
(a) Glossary (b) Index (c) Catalogue (d) Anthology
Answer: (c)
Solution: A catalogue is a systematic list of items such as library books. A glossary explains difficult words; an index locates topics within a book; an anthology is a collection of poems or writings.

113. One who is indifferent to pleasure or pain
(a) Stoic (b) Sadist (c) Cynic (d) Stooge
Answer: (a)
Solution: A stoic remains unmoved by pleasure or pain. A cynic distrusts human motives; a sadist enjoys others' pain; a stooge is a subordinate used by another.

114. The practice of having only one wife or husband at a time
(a) Polygamy (b) Celibacy (c) Bigamy (d) Monogamy
Answer: (d)
Solution: Monogamy = one spouse (mono = one, gamos = marriage). Bigamy = two at once (a crime); polygamy = many; celibacy = remaining unmarried.

115. A place where weapons and ammunition are stored
(a) Hangar (b) Granary (c) Arsenal (d) Apiary

116. A person who can use both hands equally well
(a) Ambidextrous (b) Ambivalent (c) Ambiguous (d) Amphibian
Answer: (a)
Solution: Ambidextrous = skilled with both hands (Latin dexter = right/skilled). Examiner's trap: ambivalent means having mixed feelings, and ambiguous means having double meaning — all share the prefix ambi (both).

117. An animal that lives both on land and in water
(a) Aquatic (b) Amphibian (c) Terrestrial (d) Arboreal
Answer: (b)
Solution: Amphibian = living both on land and in water (amphi = both, bios = life). Arboreal animals live in trees; terrestrial ones live on land.

118. A word or phrase that reads the same backwards as forwards
(a) Anagram (b) Acronym (c) Palindrome (d) Homonym
Answer: (c)
Solution: A palindrome reads the same both ways (e.g., "madam"). An anagram rearranges letters into a new word; an acronym forms a word from initial letters.

119. One who loves mankind
(a) Philanthropist (b) Misanthropist (c) Anthropologist (d) Philatelist
Answer: (a)
Solution: A philanthropist loves humanity (philos = love, anthropos = man). A misanthropist hates mankind; a philatelist collects stamps — a trap based on the shared "phil" prefix.

120. The act of killing one's own mother
(a) Infanticide (b) Patricide (c) Uxoricide (d) Matricide
Answer: (d)
Solution: Matricide = killing one's mother (Latin mater = mother). Uxoricide = killing one's wife; infanticide = killing an infant; patricide = killing one's father.

121. A person who speaks on behalf of others, especially a government or organisation
(a) Arbitrator (b) Mediator (c) Spokesman (d) Orator
Answer: (c)
Solution: A spokesman officially voices the views of an organisation. A mediator settles disputes; an arbitrator gives binding decisions; an orator is simply a skilled public speaker.

122. That which cannot be believed
(a) Incredulous (b) Incredible (c) Inaudible (d) Invisible
Answer: (b)
Solution: Incredible = cannot be believed (said of a thing or story). Examiner's trap: incredulous means unwilling to believe (said of a person) — the two are frequently interchanged in exams.

123. A place where leather is tanned
(a) Brewery (b) Tannery (c) Bakery (d) Mint
Answer: (b)
Solution: A tannery is where hides are converted into leather. A brewery makes beer; a mint makes coins — a common "place of work" trap set.

124. One who has a long experience of a particular field
(a) Veteran (b) Novice (c) Amateur (d) Prodigy
Answer: (a)
Solution: A veteran is a person with long experience. A novice is a beginner; an amateur works for love not money; a prodigy has exceptional early talent.

125. The study of ancient things, especially through material remains
(a) Archaeology (b) Astrology (c) Geology (d) Genealogy
Answer: (a)
Solution: Archaeology studies past human life through artefacts and remains. Genealogy traces family descent; geology studies the earth's physical structure; astrology is not a science of ancient things.
