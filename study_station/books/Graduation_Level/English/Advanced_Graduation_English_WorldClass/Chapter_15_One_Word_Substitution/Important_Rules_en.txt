# IMPORTANT RULES & TIPS
## 12 High-Yield Rules

| Rule | Example | Examiner's Trap |
|---|---|---|
| 1. The substitution must be a single word — if the answer needs two words, it is wrong for this question type | "A person who speaks many languages" = **polyglot** (not "many-languaged person") | Options may include a correct-sounding phrase instead of one word |
| 2. Learn words through their roots (Greek/Latin), not by rote | *auto* = self → autograph, autobiography, autocracy | Two options sharing a root but with different meanings (e.g., biography vs. bibliography) |
| 3. Distinguish "-phile" (lover of) from "-phobe" (fearer of) | Bibliophile = lover of books; Bibliophobe = one who fears/hates books | Options pair a -phile word with its -phobe opposite to catch careless readers |
| 4. "-cide" means killing; check what is being killed | Homicide = killing of a human; Fratricide = killing of one's brother | Regicide (killing of a king) is often confused with homicide |
| 5. "-cracy" = rule/government; identify who rules | Democracy = rule by the people; Autocracy = rule by one person | Plutocracy (rule by the rich) vs. Aristocracy (rule by nobles) is a frequent confusion pair |
| 6. "Pan-" means all; "Omni-" also means all — note the fixed combinations | Panacea = a remedy for all diseases; Omnipotent = all-powerful | Panacea is wrongly matched with "a place of peace" in distractors |
| 7. Words for "one who eats": check the food type | Carnivore = flesh-eater; Herbivore = plant-eater; Omnivore = eats both | Cannibal (eats own species) is confused with carnivore |
| 8. Study-place words are frequently asked | Apiary = place where bees are kept; Aviary = place where birds are kept | Apiary vs. Aviary differ by one letter — a classic trap |
| 9. Fear words end in "-phobia"; learn the common ones | Claustrophobia = fear of closed spaces; Hydrophobia = fear of water | Acrophobia (fear of heights) vs. Agoraphobia (fear of open/public spaces) |
| 10. Government/people words: match the group to the word | Bureaucracy = rule by officials; Gerontocracy = rule by the old | Mobocracy (rule by a mob) is often confused with democracy |
| 11. "Posthumous" applies after death — to a child born, an award given, or a book published after the father's/author's death | "A posthumous award was given to the soldier" | Used wrongly for events before death in distractor sentences |
| 12. Read the definition twice — one changed word changes the answer | "One who looks at the bright side" = optimist; "…dark side" = pessimist | Optimist/pessimist and introvert/extrovert are swapped in options |

## Quick Revision Tips
- Revise root words daily: 10 roots a day covers most exam vocabulary in a month.
- Make pairs of confusing words (apiary/aviary, -phile/-phobe) and test yourself on the difference, not just the meaning.
- In the exam, underline the key noun in the definition ("place", "person", "fear", "killing") before looking at the options.
- If two options look similar, the answer usually hinges on the root meaning — recall the root, don't guess by sound.
