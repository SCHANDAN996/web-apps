# 📋 Important Rules & Tips — Advanced Reading Comprehension

## (a) 12 High-Yield Rules

| # | Rule | Example | Examiner's Trap |
|---|------|---------|-----------------|
| 1 | **Read the questions before the passage** (except the title/tone question) — your brain then hunts for answers while reading. | Scan Q1–Q5 in 20 seconds, then read the passage. | Reading the passage twice "to be safe" wastes 3–4 minutes; one focused read is enough. |
| 2 | **Fact-based answers must be traceable to a line.** If you cannot point to the sentence, you are guessing. | "According to the passage, solar capacity grew by…" → find the exact line. | Options that *sound* true in real life but are NOT in the passage are the classic trap. |
| 3 | **Inference = passage + one small logical step, never outside knowledge.** | Passage: "The policy failed despite generous funding." → Inference: money alone doesn't guarantee success. | Over-inference: stretching one step into three ("therefore corruption must be the cause"). |
| 4 | **Tone is judged by word choice, not topic.** A passage on climate can be optimistic, alarmist, or neutral-analytical. | Words like "promising, encouraging" → optimistic; "reckless, doomed" → critical. | Choosing "pessimistic" just because the topic is a problem (war, pollution). |
| 5 | **Extreme words in options are usually wrong** for tone/main-idea questions: *always, never, completely, entirely, all*. | "The author completely rejects technology" — almost never correct. | Mild options ("sceptical", "cautious") beat extreme ones ("hostile", "furious"). |
| 6 | **Main idea = what the WHOLE passage does, not one paragraph.** Check: does the option cover paragraph 1 AND the last paragraph? | A passage may discuss AI in para 1 but its main idea is "regulation of AI". | Paragraph-level options dressed up as main-idea options. |
| 7 | **Vocabulary-in-context: ignore the dictionary meaning you know first.** Re-read the sentence and substitute each option. | "The minister gave a *guarded* reply" → cautious, not "protected". | The most common meaning of the word is planted as the wrong option. |
| 8 | **EXCEPT/NOT questions: verify all four options against the passage; three will be present.** | "All of the following are mentioned EXCEPT…" | Marking the first true statement you find — you need the one that is ABSENT. |
| 9 | **Connector words signal the author's direction:** *however, yet, but* = turn; *moreover, also* = continuation; *thus, hence* = conclusion. | "The scheme was popular. **However**, enrolment fell." → author's real point is the fall. | Quoting the part before "however" as the author's view. |
| 10 | **Title question = main idea in 4–8 words.** Reject titles that are too broad, too narrow, or one-sided. | Passage on pros+cons of remote work → "Remote Work: Promise and Pitfalls", not "The Death of Offices". | Catchy, dramatic titles that match only the last line. |
| 11 | **Author's purpose ≠ author's opinion.** Purpose options use verbs: *to analyse, to criticise, to advocate, to describe*. | A balanced passage → "to analyse", not "to advocate". | Confusing "the author describes X" with "the author supports X". |
| 12 | **Never leave an RC question blank in exams without negative marking; in negative-marking exams, attempt only when you can eliminate 2 options.** | Eliminate (a) and (d) confidently → attempt; 50-50 → attempt only if your net score strategy allows. | Blind guessing on all 5 questions of a hard passage. |

## (b) Time Management — The 15/45-Second Rule

**Total budget:** roughly 8–10 minutes for a 5-question passage (≈ 3–4 min reading + per-question time below).

### ⏱️ 15-Second Questions — Fact-Based (answer is IN the text)
| Question Type | Signal Words | Strategy |
|---|---|---|
| Direct fact / detail | "According to the passage…", "The author mentions…" | Locate the keyword, match the line, mark. |
| Vocabulary-in-context (easy) | "The word 'X' as used in the passage means…" | Substitute options in the sentence. |
| EXCEPT/NOT (fact type) | "All are mentioned EXCEPT…" | Tick off the three present in the text. |
| Specific-data question | "What percentage / which year / which country…" | Scan for numbers/names only. |

### ⏱️ 45-Second Questions — Inference-Based (answer is BETWEEN the lines)
| Question Type | Signal Words | Strategy |
|---|---|---|
| Inference / implication | "It can be inferred…", "The author implies…" | Passage + one logical step; reject outside knowledge. |
| Tone / attitude | "The author's tone is…" | Map adjective choices; avoid extreme options. |
| Main idea / title | "The central theme…", "A suitable title…" | Check coverage of first AND last paragraphs. |
| Author's purpose | "The primary purpose is to…" | Match the verb (analyse/criticise/advocate) to the passage's balance. |
| Assumption / strengthen-weaken | "The argument assumes…" | Find the hidden link between evidence and conclusion. |

**Rule of thumb:** if you cross 60 seconds on any question, mark your best elimination and move on — return only if time remains.

## (c) Morphology — Root, Affix, Example

| Root / Affix | Origin & Meaning | Example Words (as seen in RC passages) |
|---|---|---|
| **cred** | Latin — believe | credible, incredible, credentials, credulous |
| **dict** | Latin — say, speak | predict, contradict, verdict, dictum |
| **spect** | Latin — look, see | perspective, introspect, circumspect, prospect |
| **port** | Latin — carry | transport, export, portable, support |
| **rupt** | Latin — break | disrupt, erupt, bankrupt, interrupt |
| **chron** | Greek — time | chronic, chronological, anachronism |
| **path** | Greek — feeling, suffering | empathy, apathy, antipathy, pathetic |
| **anthrop** | Greek — human | anthropology, philanthropy, misanthrope |
| **pre-** | Prefix — before | precedent, precaution, premature |
| **post-** | Prefix — after | postpone, postscript, posterior |
| **inter-** | Prefix — between | intervene, interpret, intersperse |
| **mis-** | Prefix — wrong(ly) | misinterpret, misnomer, misgiving |
| **-ous / -ious** | Suffix — full of (adjective) | cautious, ambitious, dubious |
| **-tion / -sion** | Suffix — act/state (noun) | regulation, expansion, persuasion |
| **-ise / -ize** | Suffix — to make (verb) | modernise, scrutinise, stabilise |

**Usage tip:** In vocabulary-in-context questions, break the word into root + affix, guess the meaning family, then confirm by substituting into the sentence — never rely on the root alone.
