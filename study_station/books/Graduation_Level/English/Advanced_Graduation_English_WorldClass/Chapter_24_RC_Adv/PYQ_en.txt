# 📊 PYQ Analysis — Advanced Reading Comprehension

## How Exams Test This Chapter (Pattern Analysis)

Reading Comprehension is the single heaviest block in the English section of SSC CGL, IBPS PO/Clerk, SBI PO, and State PCS prelims. Across these exams, one or two passages appear in almost every paper, typically carrying 5–10 questions per passage. The pattern that repeats year after year:

- **Direct fact questions** ("According to the passage…", "Which of the following is true?") — these reward fast scanning, not deep reading.
- **Inference questions** ("It can be inferred that…", "The author implies…") — the highest-scoring trap zone; examiners insert options that are *true in real life* but *not supported by the passage*.
- **Tone/attitude questions** ("The author's tone is…") — options pair a near-correct word with an extreme one (e.g., "critical" vs. "condemnatory").
- **Vocabulary-in-context** ("The word 'X' as used in the passage means…") — the dictionary meaning is often the trap; the *contextual* meaning is the answer.
- **Title/main idea questions** — traps are options that are too narrow (one paragraph only) or too broad (beyond the passage's scope).

**Examiner's favourite traps, in words:**
1. **Sound-Right trap** — an option that sounds scholarly and plausible but distorts one detail from the passage.
2. **Over-thinking trap** — the simplest, most literal answer is correct, but aspirants reject it as "too easy."
3. **Extreme-word trap** — options containing *always, never, completely, only* are usually wrong unless the passage itself is absolute.
4. **Half-true trap** — first half of the option matches the passage, second half quietly contradicts it.
5. **Out-of-scope trap** — a factually correct statement about the topic that the passage never mentions.

## Top 10 High-Yield PYQ-Style Questions with Solutions

**Q1.** (Inference) *Passage excerpt:* "While renewable energy capacity has tripled in a decade, grid storage has not kept pace, leaving surplus power unused on windy nights."
It can be inferred that the author believes:
(a) Renewable energy is a failure
(b) Storage technology is the bottleneck in renewable growth
(c) Wind energy should be abandoned
(d) Grids produce surplus power deliberately

**Answer:** (b)
**Solution:** The author contrasts growth in capacity with lagging storage — the implied concern is the storage bottleneck. (a) and (c) are extreme-word traps; (d) reverses the logic.
**Trap type:** Extreme-word | **Time rule:** 45 sec (inference)
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q2.** (Fact) According to the passage, surplus power goes unused because:
(a) Demand falls at night
(b) Storage capacity is insufficient
(c) Wind turbines shut down
(d) Renewable capacity tripled

**Answer:** (b)
**Solution:** The passage states directly that "grid storage has not kept pace." This is a pure fact question — scan for "unused" and match. (d) is true but answers the wrong question.
**Trap type:** Half-true | **Time rule:** 15 sec (fact)
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q3.** (Tone) *Passage excerpt:* "The policy, though well-intentioned, has produced outcomes its architects neither predicted nor desired."
The author's tone is best described as:
(a) Condemnatory
(b) Cautiously critical
(c) Celebratory
(d) Indifferent

**Answer:** (b)
**Solution:** "Well-intentioned" softens the criticism; "neither predicted nor desired" delivers it. That balance = cautiously critical. (a) is the extreme-word trap; (c) and (d) contradict the excerpt.
**Trap type:** Extreme-word | **Time rule:** 45 sec
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q4.** (Vocabulary-in-context) *Passage excerpt:* "The committee's report was **trenchant**, cutting through months of evasive testimony."
As used here, "trenchant" most nearly means:
(a) Lengthy
(b) Sharply effective and incisive
(c) Delayed
(d) Confusing

**Answer:** (b)
**Solution:** The clue "cutting through" signals incisiveness. The dictionary-adjacent trap is picking a word related to "trench" (digging, length); context decides.
**Trap type:** Sound-Right | **Time rule:** 15 sec
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q5.** (Main idea) *Passage summary:* A passage discusses how AI improves medical diagnosis, then notes risks of biased training data, and ends urging regulated adoption.
The best title is:
(a) "AI Will Replace Doctors"
(b) "The Dangers of Artificial Intelligence"
(c) "AI in Medicine: Promise Tempered by Caution"
(d) "How Training Data Works"

**Answer:** (c)
**Solution:** The title must cover promise + risk + recommendation. (a) and (b) are one-sided (too narrow); (d) covers only a detail.
**Trap type:** Too narrow / too broad | **Time rule:** 45 sec
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q6.** (Inference) *Passage excerpt:* "Urban flooding in the city is less an act of nature than a ledger of neglected drains and paved-over wetlands."
The author suggests urban flooding is primarily:
(a) An unavoidable natural disaster
(b) A result of human mismanagement
(c) Caused by excessive rainfall alone
(d) A problem with no solution

**Answer:** (b)
**Solution:** "Less an act of nature than…" is a contrast marker pointing blame at human neglect. (a) is the exact reverse — the classic inversion trap.
**Trap type:** Sound-Right (inversion) | **Time rule:** 45 sec
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q7.** (Fact — negative question) *Passage excerpt:* "The scheme offers subsidies, training, and credit access to small farmers."
According to the passage, the scheme does NOT provide:
(a) Subsidies
(b) Training
(c) Credit access
(d) Crop insurance

**Answer:** (d)
**Solution:** Negative questions ("NOT", "EXCEPT") cause more errors than any other fact type. Tick off what IS mentioned; the leftover is the answer. (a)–(c) appear verbatim.
**Trap type:** Negative-wording | **Time rule:** 15 sec
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q8.** (Inference) *Passage excerpt:* "Voters rewarded the incumbent despite rising prices — a reminder that economic pain alone rarely decides elections."
It can be inferred that:
(a) Rising prices help incumbents
(b) Electoral outcomes depend on factors beyond the economy
(c) Voters ignore price rises
(d) The incumbent controlled prices

**Answer:** (b)
**Solution:** "Economic pain *alone* rarely decides" implies other factors matter — that is (b), stated moderately. (a) and (c) overstate; (d) is out-of-scope.
**Trap type:** Over-thinking (rejecting the moderate option) | **Time rule:** 45 sec
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q9.** (Tone) *Passage excerpt:* "One marvels at the committee's ability to spend two years producing a report that says, in essence, nothing."
The tone is:
(a) Laudatory
(b) Sarcastic
(c) Neutral
(d) Apologetic

**Answer:** (b)
**Solution:** "One marvels" is mock-praise — the hallmark of sarcasm. (a) is the surface-reading trap; sarcasm means the opposite of the literal words.
**Trap type:** Sound-Right (literal reading) | **Time rule:** 45 sec
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q10.** (Fact) *Passage excerpt:* "Unlike earlier missions, Chandrayaan-3 achieved a soft landing near the lunar south polar region in 2023."
According to the passage, what distinguished Chandrayaan-3 from earlier missions?
(a) It was launched in 2023
(b) It reached the Moon
(c) Its soft landing near the south polar region
(d) It carried more instruments

**Answer:** (c)
**Solution:** The contrast phrase "Unlike earlier missions" flags the distinguishing feature directly. (a) is a true detail but not the distinction — a half-true trap.
**Trap type:** Half-true | **Time rule:** 15 sec
**Source:** PYQ-style

## 15/45-Second Rule Applied (Worked Examples)

**Q7 above — 15-second execution:** See "NOT" → underline it mentally → scan the sentence listing subsidies, training, credit → the unlisted item (crop insurance) is the answer. No re-reading of the whole passage. **Target: 15 seconds.**

**Q3 above — 45-second execution:** Identify tone question → locate emotional words ("well-intentioned", "neither predicted nor desired") → weigh praise vs. criticism → eliminate extremes (condemnatory, celebratory) → choose the balanced option. **Target: 45 seconds.**

**Rule of thumb:** If the answer is *locatable in one line* of the passage, spend ≤15 seconds. If the answer must be *built from the author's attitude or implication*, allow up to 45 seconds — and never more, or you steal time from the next passage.
