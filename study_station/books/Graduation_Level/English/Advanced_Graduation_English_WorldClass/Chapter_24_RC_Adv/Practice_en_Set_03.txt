51. The primary purpose of "skimming" a passage is to:
(a) Memorise every detail (b) Get the general idea quickly (c) Find grammatical errors (d) Translate the passage
Answer: (b)
Solution: Skimming means reading quickly for the gist, not details. Memorising every detail (option a) describes intensive reading, the opposite of skimming, so the answer is getting the general idea quickly.
Source: PYQ-style

52. "Scanning" a passage is best used when you need to:
(a) Understand the author's tone (b) Locate a specific fact or keyword (c) Summarise the whole passage (d) Guess the title
Answer: (b)
Solution: Scanning is searching for a particular piece of information like a date or name. Tone and title questions need whole-passage reading, so the answer is locating a specific fact or keyword.
Source: PYQ-style

53. The "main idea" of a passage is usually found in:
(a) The last example only (b) The first or last sentence of the passage (c) The footnotes (d) The longest sentence
Answer: (b)
Solution: Writers typically state the central idea in the opening or concluding lines. The longest sentence may carry only supporting detail, so the answer is the first or last sentence of the passage.
Source: PYQ-style

54. An "inference" question asks you to:
(a) Copy a line from the passage (b) Find a synonym (c) Conclude something suggested but not directly stated (d) Count the paragraphs
Answer: (c)
Solution: Inference means reading between the lines — a conclusion implied by the text. Copying a line is a fact-based task, not inference, so the answer is concluding something suggested but not directly stated.
Source: PYQ-style

55. The "tone" of a passage refers to:
(a) The font used (b) The author's attitude towards the subject (c) The number of words (d) The exam's difficulty
Answer: (b)
Solution: Tone is the writer's attitude — critical, optimistic, neutral, sarcastic, etc. It has nothing to do with length or format, so the answer is the author's attitude towards the subject.
Source: PYQ-style

56. If a passage says "Although the policy reduced costs, it hurt small traders," the author's tone is:
(a) Balanced / mixed (b) Fully supportive (c) Completely negative (d) Humorous
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Although" signals the author presents both a benefit and a drawback — a balanced view. Fully supportive ignores the criticism, so the answer is balanced / mixed.
Source: PYQ-style

57. A fact-based question in RC can usually be answered in about:
(a) 15 seconds (b) 5 minutes (c) Only after reading the passage twice (d) Never
Answer: (a)
Solution: Fact questions need only locating a stated detail — the 15-second rule applies. Inference questions take longer, so the answer is 15 seconds.
Source: PYQ-style

58. The best strategy before reading an RC passage is to:
(a) Read the passage backwards (b) Read the questions first to know what to look for (c) Skip the passage entirely (d) Read only the title
Answer: (b)
Solution: Reading questions first primes your eyes for relevant details while reading. Skipping the passage makes answers guesswork, so the answer is reading the questions first.
Source: PYQ-style

59. In the sentence "The data shows a sharp rise," the word "data" is treated as:
(a) An adjective (b) Plural here (c) A verb (d) Singular here
Answer: (d)
Solution: The verb "shows" is singular, so "data" is treated as singular in this sentence. Modern usage accepts data as singular, so the answer is singular here.
Source: PYQ-style

60. A synonym question in RC asks for a word that:
(a) Means the opposite (b) Rhymes with the word (c) Has a similar meaning in the given context (d) Is longer than the word
Answer: (c)
Solution: Synonym means similar meaning, and context decides which meaning fits. Opposite meaning describes an antonym question, so the answer is a similar meaning in the given context.
Source: PYQ-style

61. Read: "Artificial intelligence can diagnose diseases from scans faster than most radiologists, yet hospitals remain cautious, fearing accountability when algorithms err." The author's attitude towards AI in medicine is:
(a) Cautiously optimistic (b) Enthusiastic without reservation (c) Hostile (d) Indifferent
Answer: (a)
Solution: The author praises AI's speed but notes hospitals' caution — a mixed, careful stance. Enthusiastic without reservation ignores the worry about accountability, so the answer is cautiously optimistic.

62. Read: "Despite record harvests, farmer incomes stagnated, as middlemen captured most of the profit." It can be inferred that:
(a) The supply chain structure hurts farmers (b) Harvests were poor (c) Middlemen are farmers (d) Prices rose for farmers
Answer: (a)
Solution: The passage implies the marketing structure, not production, is the problem. Poor harvests contradict "record harvests," so the answer is the supply chain structure hurts farmers.
Source: PYQ-style

63. Read: "The new metro line, though expensive, cut travel time by half and reduced road congestion." The word "though" signals:
(a) Addition (b) Contrast / concession (c) Cause (d) Time
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Though" concedes a drawback (expense) before stating benefits — a contrast marker. It does not show cause, so the answer is contrast / concession.
Source: PYQ-style

64. Read: "Renewable energy is now cheaper than coal in many regions; however, storage remains the Achilles' heel of the sector." "Achilles' heel" means:
(a) Greatest strength (b) Fatal weakness (c) Main source (d) New invention
Answer: (b)
Solution: The idiom means a vulnerable weak point — here, storage. Greatest strength is the tempting opposite, so the answer is fatal weakness.
Source: NCERT Class 10 English (idiom usage)

65. Read: "The committee's report was exhaustive, covering every clause in detail." "Exhaustive" here means:
(a) Tiring (b) Confusing (c) Brief (d) Thorough and complete
Answer: (d)
Solution: In context, "covering every clause" shows thoroughness, not tiredness. Tiring is the trap meaning of "exhausting," so the answer is thorough and complete.

66. Read: "Unlike his predecessors, the new governor spoke plainly, avoiding jargon." It can be inferred that his predecessors:
(a) Also spoke plainly (b) Were silent (c) Used technical or unclear language (d) Avoided meetings
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Unlike" sets up a contrast: the new governor avoids jargon, so the earlier ones used it. Also speaking plainly destroys the contrast, so the answer is used technical or unclear language.
Source: PYQ-style

67. Read: "The study found that students who slept eight hours scored significantly higher." The passage supports which conclusion?
(a) Sleep has no effect on scores (b) All students sleep eight hours (c) Adequate sleep is linked to better performance (d) Scores depend only on luck
Answer: (c)
Solution: The finding directly links more sleep with higher scores. No effect contradicts the study, so the answer is adequate sleep is linked to better performance.

68. Read: "Critics hailed the novel as a masterpiece, but readers found it tedious." The author's tone towards the novel is:
(a) Neutral reporting of two views (b) One-sided praise (c) Sarcastic attack (d) Personal admiration

Solution: The author merely reports critics' praise and readers' boredom without taking sides. One-sided praise ignores the readers' view, so the answer is neutral reporting of two views.

69. Read: "The glacier has retreated two kilometres in a decade — a silent testimony to a warming planet." The phrase "silent testimony" suggests the glacier:
(a) Speaks loudly (b) Is noisy (c) Provides evidence without words (d) Is growing

70. Read: "Not only did the scheme fail to create jobs, it also drained the treasury." The author's view of the scheme is:
(a) Mildly positive (b) Neutral (c) Strongly critical (d) Confused

(a) A burden (b) An asset (c) A school (d) A museum
