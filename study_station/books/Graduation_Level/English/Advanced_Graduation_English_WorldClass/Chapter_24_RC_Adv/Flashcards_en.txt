Card 1
Front: What are the three steps of the Detective Approach to Reading Comprehension?
Back: (1) Skim the passage for structure and tone, (2) Read the question stem and locate the relevant lines, (3) Eliminate options that go beyond or against the passage. Never answer from outside knowledge.

Card 2
Front: What is the difference between a Fact-based and an Inference-based question?
Back: Fact-based: the answer is directly stated in the passage (15-second rule). Inference-based: the answer must be logically deduced from stated information (45-second rule).

Card 3
Front: Mnemonic — What does "TAPS" stand for when identifying the author's tone?
Back: T = Topic, A = Attitude (positive/negative/neutral), P = Purpose (inform, persuade, criticise), S = Style (analytical, narrative, satirical). Check all four before choosing a tone option.

Card 4
Front: Examiner's Trap — An option is factually true in the real world but not mentioned in the passage. Should you select it?
Back: No. This is the "Outside Knowledge Trap." In RC, the passage is the only universe of truth. If it isn't stated or implied in the passage, reject it.

Card 5
Front: What does the Greek root "chron" mean? Give two derived words.
Back: "Time." Derived words: chronology (order of events in time), chronic (long-lasting, e.g., chronic disease).

Card 6
Front: What does the Latin root "dict" mean? Give two derived words.
Back: "Say/speak." Derived words: predict (say before), contradict (speak against).

Card 7
Front: In a "main idea" question, what is wrong with an option that is true but covers only one paragraph?
Back: It is too narrow — a classic trap. The main idea must cover the entire passage, not just a supporting detail.

Card 8
Front: Mnemonic — How do you remember the difference between "imply" and "infer"?
Back: "The writer IMPLIES, the reader INFERS." Speaker/writer gives the hint; listener/reader draws the conclusion.

Card 9
Front: What is "tone mapping" in Advanced RC?
Back: Tracking the author's attitude paragraph by paragraph (e.g., optimistic → cautious → critical). Tone shifts are signalled by words like "however," "yet," "admittedly," and are favourite exam targets.

Card 10
Front: Examiner's Trap — An option uses extreme words like "always," "never," "completely," "all." What should you suspect?
Back: It is likely wrong. Passages rarely make absolute claims; correct answers usually use moderate language ("often," "may," "tends to"). Verify against the passage before accepting any extreme option.

Card 11
Front: What does the Greek root "bio" mean? Give two derived words.
Back: "Life." Derived words: biology (study of life), biodegradable (capable of being broken down by living organisms).

Card 12
Front: What is the correct strategy when a question asks "The author would most likely agree with…"?
Back: Find the author's stated opinions and tone, then pick the option that is a small, safe logical step from them. Reject options that exaggerate the author's position.

Card 13
Front: What does the Latin root "port" mean? Give two derived words.
Back: "Carry." Derived words: transport (carry across), export (carry out of a country).

Card 14
Front: What is the 15/45-second rule in RC?
Back: Spend about 15 seconds on direct factual/detail questions (answer is in the text) and up to 45 seconds on inference, tone, and main-idea questions (answer must be deduced).

Card 15
Front: What does the Greek root "geo" mean? Give two derived words.
Back: "Earth." Derived words: geography (describing the earth), geology (study of the earth).

Card 16
Front: In a "title of the passage" question, what two qualities must the correct title have?
Back: It must (1) capture the central theme of the whole passage and (2) match the author's tone. Reject titles that are too broad, too narrow, or emotionally mismatched.

Card 17
Front: What does the Latin root "vid/vis" mean? Give two derived words.
Back: "See." Derived words: visible (able to be seen), revise (look at again).

Card 18
Front: What should you do when two options both look correct in an inference question?
Back: Choose the one that stays closest to the passage's exact wording and scope. The trap option usually over-generalises or adds an idea the author never supported.

Card 19
Front: What is the "Sound-Right Trap" in RC options?
Back: An option that uses impressive vocabulary or echoes passage words but distorts the meaning. It sounds scholarly, so students pick it without verifying against the text. Always match meaning, not words.

Card 20
Front: What does the Latin root "scrib/script" mean? Give two derived words.
Back: "Write." Derived words: describe (write about), manuscript (something written by hand).
