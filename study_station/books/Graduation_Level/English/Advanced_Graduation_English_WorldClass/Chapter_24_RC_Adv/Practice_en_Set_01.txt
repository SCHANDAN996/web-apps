1. What is the FIRST step of the Detective Approach to Reading Comprehension?
(a) Read the passage twice carefully (b) Read the questions before the passage (c) Memorize all difficult words (d) Write a summary of the passage
Answer: (b)
Solution: Reading the passage twice wastes time, and memorizing words is not a strategy. Glancing at the questions first tells you what to hunt for, so the answer is "Read the questions before the passage."
Source: PYQ-style

2. A fact-based question in RC can usually be answered in about:
(a) 5 minutes (b) 45 seconds (c) 2 minutes (d) 15 seconds
Answer: (d)
Solution: Fact-based questions only require locating a line in the passage, which is quick; inference questions need the longer 45-second window. The answer is 15 seconds.
Source: PYQ-style

3. An inference-based question should be given about:
(a) 5 seconds (b) 15 seconds (c) 45 seconds (d) 3 minutes
Answer: (c)
Solution: Inference questions demand that you combine clues and judge the author's intent, which needs more time than a simple fact lookup. The answer is 45 seconds.
Source: PYQ-style

4. The tone of a passage that lists only facts and figures without any opinion is:
(a) Sarcastic (b) Optimistic (c) Objective (d) Critical
Answer: (c)
Solution: Sarcastic and critical tones carry judgment, and optimistic carries hope; a purely factual presentation is neutral. The answer is Objective.
Source: PYQ-style

5. The Greek root "chron" means:
(a) Sound (b) Time (c) Life (d) Earth
Answer: (b)
Solution: Words like chronology and chronic both deal with time, not sound or life. The answer is Time.
Source: Standard Etymology Reference

6. The Latin root "bene" means:
(a) Good / well (b) Bad (c) Against (d) Far
Answer: (a)
Solution: Benefit and benevolent both carry the sense of good; "bad" is the root mal. The answer is Good / well.
Source: Standard Etymology Reference

7. The word "biodiversity" contains the root "bio", which means:
(a) Life (b) Water (c) Earth (d) Light
Answer: (a)
Solution: Biology and biography both relate to life, confirming the root's meaning. The answer is Life.
Source: Standard Etymology Reference

8. In tone mapping, words like "alarming", "bleak" and "grim" signal a tone that is:
(a) Humorous (b) Indifferent (c) Celebratory (d) Pessimistic
Answer: (d)
Solution: Such words express worry and hopelessness, the opposite of celebratory or humorous. The answer is Pessimistic.
Source: PYQ-style

9. The main purpose of skimming a passage is to:
(a) Learn every word (b) Check grammar errors (c) Get the central idea quickly (d) Count the paragraphs
Answer: (c)
Solution: Skimming is rapid reading for gist, not for vocabulary or grammar. The answer is "Get the central idea quickly."
Source: PYQ-style

10. Scanning is best used when you need to:
(a) Understand the author's childhood (b) Locate a specific fact, name or number (c) Judge the author's tone (d) Write an essay
Answer: (b)
Solution: Scanning means running your eyes over the text to find one targeted detail. The answer is "Locate a specific fact, name or number."
Source: PYQ-style

11. The Latin root "dict" (as in predict, dictate) means:
(a) Write (b) Say / speak (c) See (d) Carry
Answer: (b)
Solution: Predict means to say beforehand, and dictate means to speak for someone to write down. The answer is Say / speak.
Source: Standard Etymology Reference

12. The Greek root "geo" means:
(a) Earth (b) Star (c) Water (d) Air
Answer: (a)
Solution: Geography and geology both study the earth. The answer is Earth.
Source: Standard Etymology Reference

13. If an author writes "The so-called green policy has, unsurprisingly, enriched only the contractors", the tone is best described as:
(a) Admiring (b) Apologetic (c) Neutral (d) Sarcastic
Answer: (d)
Solution: "So-called" and "unsurprisingly" mock the policy rather than praise it, ruling out admiring or neutral. The answer is Sarcastic.
Source: PYQ-style

14. A "title question" in RC actually tests whether you have understood the:
(a) Central idea of the whole passage (b) First sentence only (c) Author's name (d) Difficult vocabulary
Answer: (a)
Solution: A good title captures the main theme, not one line or one word. The answer is "Central idea of the whole passage."
Source: PYQ-style

15. The root "port" (as in transport, export) means:
(a) Throw (b) Break (c) Carry (d) Hold
Answer: (c)
Solution: Transport means to carry across, and export means to carry out. The answer is Carry.
Source: Standard Etymology Reference

16. Which of these is a common examiner's trap in RC options?
(a) An option that is true in real life but not stated in the passage (b) An option that restates the passage exactly (c) An option that is too short (d) An option written in simple words
Answer: (a)
Solution: Examiners plant options that are factually true in the world but unsupported by the passage; only passage-based answers count. The answer is "An option that is true in real life but not stated in the passage."
Source: PYQ-style

17. The word "malnutrition" contains the root "mal", which means:
(a) Good (b) Small (c) Many (d) Bad / faulty
Answer: (d)
Solution: Malfunction and malicious also carry a negative sense, the opposite of bene. The answer is Bad / faulty.
Source: Standard Etymology Reference

18. When a question asks "What can be inferred from the passage?", you should choose the option that:
(a) Is directly copied from one line (b) Uses the most difficult words (c) Is the most interesting (d) Must be true based on the passage's clues
Answer: (d)
Solution: A directly copied line is a fact answer, not an inference; inference means a conclusion the clues force upon you. The answer is "Must be true based on the passage's clues."
Source: PYQ-style

19. The root "spect" (as in inspect, spectator) means:
(a) Hear (b) Move (c) Touch (d) Look / see
Answer: (d)
Solution: Inspect means to look into, and a spectator is one who watches. The answer is Look / see.
Source: Standard Etymology Reference

20. The best strategy when two options both look correct is to:
(a) Pick the longer one (b) Pick the first one (c) Re-read the relevant lines and choose the one fully supported by the passage (d) Leave the question always
Answer: (c)
Solution: Length and position are not evidence; only the passage decides between close options. The answer is "Re-read the relevant lines and choose the one fully supported by the passage."
Source: PYQ-style

Passage for Questions 21–25: "Artificial intelligence now screens millions of job applications before any human sees them. Proponents argue this removes human prejudice; critics counter that algorithms trained on historical data merely automate yesterday's biases. The truth, as usual, lies in the audit: systems that are regularly tested for fairness perform measurably better than those left unexamined."

21. The central idea of the passage is that:
(a) AI should be banned from hiring (b) AI in hiring needs regular fairness audits to work well (c) Human recruiters are always biased (d) Historical data is useless
Answer: (b)
Solution: The passage neither demands a ban nor condemns all humans; its final line stresses auditing as the key. The answer is "AI in hiring needs regular fairness audits to work well."
Source: PYQ-style

22. The author's tone towards AI in hiring is:
(a) Cautiously balanced (b) Completely dismissive (c) Blindly enthusiastic (d) Sarcastic
Answer: (a)
Solution: The author presents both proponents and critics and ends with a practical condition, showing balance rather than dismissal or blind praise. The answer is Cautiously balanced.
Source: PYQ-style

23. It can be inferred from the passage that algorithms trained on historical data:
(a) Are always fair (b) Cannot be improved (c) May repeat the biases present in past hiring (d) Are cheaper than humans
Answer: (c)
Solution: The tempting wrong choice is "cannot be improved", but the passage says audited systems improve, so improvement is possible; the critics' point is that old data carries old biases. The answer is "May repeat the biases present in past hiring."
Source: PYQ-style

24. According to the passage, systems that perform measurably better are those that:
(a) Are left unexamined (b) Are regularly tested for fairness (c) Use the oldest data (d) Replace all humans
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Left unexamined" is the trap — the passage says exactly the opposite, that unexamined systems perform worse. The answer is "Are regularly tested for fairness."
Source: PYQ-style

25. The word "proponents" in the passage most nearly means:
(a) Supporters (b) Opponents (c) Auditors (d) Applicants
Answer: (a)
Solution: Opponents is the direct opposite and a classic trap; proponents are those who argue in favour. The answer is Supporters.
Source: PYQ-style
