# 🧒 Feynman Technique — Advanced Reading Comprehension

## The Hardest Concept: Inference Questions ("What can be inferred from the passage?")

Most students lose marks not on what the passage *says*, but on what it *means*. Let's fix that forever.

---

### Explain It Like I'm 12

Imagine you come home and see:
- Your mother's slippers are at the door.
- The kitchen light is on.
- You smell fresh pakoras.

Nobody told you anything. But you *know*: **Mom is home and she's making pakoras.**

Congratulations — you just made an **inference**. You took **clues (facts)** + **your logic** and reached a conclusion that was never spoken aloud.

A Reading Comprehension passage works exactly the same way:

> **Inference = Fact in the passage + Common sense = Hidden answer**

The writer never says the answer directly. The writer *leaves slippers at the door* — small clues — and you, the detective, must connect them.

---

### The Detective Approach 🕵️

Think of every inference question as a small crime scene:

1. **Fact (the clue):** What does the passage literally say? Underline it.
2. **Logic (your magnifying glass):** What must be true if this fact is true?
3. **Conclusion (the arrest):** Pick the option that is *one small step* away from the fact — never a giant leap.

**The golden rule:** An inference must be *provable from the passage*. If you need outside knowledge, assumptions, or "maybe in real life…" — it's a trap, not an inference.

---

### A Concrete Example

**Passage line:** *"Despite record investments in renewable energy, the city's air quality index worsened for the third consecutive year."*

**Question:** What can be inferred?

- ❌ (a) Renewable energy is useless. → **Giant leap!** Too extreme ("useless").
- ❌ (b) The government is corrupt. → **Outside knowledge!** Nothing in the passage says this.
- ✅ (c) Investment in renewables alone has not yet improved the city's air quality. → **One small step** from the fact. Provable. Correct.
- ❌ (d) Air quality will improve next year. → **Prediction**, not inference. The passage gives no such clue.

See? The correct answer is always the *boring, careful, provable* one — not the dramatic one.

---

### The Unforgettable Trick: **"SLIPPERS Test"** 🩴

Before choosing an inference answer, ask — **S.L.I.P.P.E.R.S**:

| Letter | Ask yourself |
|---|---|
| **S** | **S**upported by a line in the passage? |
| **L** | **L**ogical — one small step, not a leap? |
| **I** | **I**nside the passage (no outside knowledge)? |
| **P** | **P**rovable — could you point to the clue? |
| **P** | **P**recise — not too broad, not too narrow? |
| **E** | **E**xtreme words avoided? (always, never, completely, useless) |
| **R** | **R**ealistic tone matching the author? |
| **S** | **S**tays in the passage's scope and time? |

If an option fails even one letter — **drop the slipper, drop the option.**

---

### Why Students Get Trapped

The examiner knows your brain loves drama. So the wrong options are always:
- **Too extreme** ("completely failed", "always", "never")
- **Too emotional** (angrier or happier than the author)
- **Too outside** (true in real life, but not in the passage)

The correct inference is like Mom's slippers — quiet, ordinary, and *definitely there*.

### ❓ Your Turn (Answer Before Sleeping)

*Passage line:* "The new metro line, though expensive, reduced average commute time by 40 minutes within six months of opening."

**What can be inferred?**
(a) Metros are the best transport system in the world.
(b) The metro's high cost was a waste of public money.
(c) The metro produced a measurable benefit despite its cost.
(d) All cities should immediately build metro lines.

*(Hint: Run each option through the SLIPPERS test. Two options fail on "Extreme", one fails on "Outside knowledge".)*

**Answer: (c)** — one small, provable step from the fact. (a) and (d) are extreme leaps; (b) is an opinion the passage never supports.
