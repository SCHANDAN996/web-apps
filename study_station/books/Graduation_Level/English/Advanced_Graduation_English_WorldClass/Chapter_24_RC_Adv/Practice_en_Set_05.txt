Advanced Reading Comprehension — Practice Set 5 (Questions 101–125)

Passage 1 (Questions 101–105): Artificial intelligence systems now screen millions of job applications every year. Proponents argue that algorithms remove human prejudice from hiring. Yet researchers have found that when historical hiring data reflects past discrimination, the algorithm learns and amplifies it. The machine, far from being neutral, becomes a mirror of the biases embedded in its training data. Some companies now audit their algorithms regularly, but regulation lags far behind deployment.

101. The central idea of the passage is that
(a) AI can reproduce and even magnify human bias in hiring (b) AI has made hiring completely fair (c) companies should stop using AI in recruitment (d) regulation of AI has kept pace with its use
Answer: (a)
Solution: The passage concedes AI's promise but stresses that biased training data makes algorithms amplify discrimination. Option (b) contradicts the passage; (c) and (d) overstate or distort the text. The answer is that AI can reproduce and even magnify human bias.
Source: PYQ-style

102. The phrase "a mirror of the biases" suggests that algorithms
(a) correct the errors of human recruiters (b) are transparent and easy to inspect (c) reflect the prejudices present in their training data (d) create entirely new forms of bias
Answer: (c)
Solution: A mirror reflects what is before it; the metaphor means the algorithm reflects existing bias, not that it invents new bias (d) or fixes humans (a). The answer is that algorithms reflect the prejudices in their training data.
Source: PYQ-style

103. The author's tone is best described as
(a) cautiously critical (b) dismissive (c) celebratory (d) indifferent
Answer: (a)
Solution: The author acknowledges AI's claimed benefits but warns of bias and weak regulation — criticism with balance, not celebration (c) or outright dismissal (b). The answer is cautiously critical.
Source: PYQ-style

104. It can be inferred that "regulation lags far behind deployment" means
(a) rules governing AI arrive much later than the technology itself (b) laws are made before technology is used (c) companies deploy AI only after audits (d) regulators have banned AI hiring tools
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Lags behind" means comes later; the sentence implies AI is widely used before adequate rules exist. Options (b) and (d) reverse or invent facts. The answer is that rules arrive much later than the technology.
Source: PYQ-style

105. According to the passage, some companies respond to algorithmic bias by
(a) auditing their algorithms regularly (b) abandoning AI entirely (c) hiding their training data (d) lobbying against regulation
Answer: (a)
Solution: The last sentence states directly that some companies audit their algorithms regularly. The other options are not mentioned. The answer is auditing their algorithms regularly.

Passage 2 (Questions 106–110): The world's oceans absorb over ninety percent of the excess heat trapped by greenhouse gases. This has shielded land dwellers from even more extreme warming, but at a cost: marine heatwaves have doubled in frequency since the 1980s, bleaching coral reefs and shifting fish populations toward the poles. Coastal communities that depend on fishing face declining catches, while warmer waters fuel more intense tropical storms.

106. The passage suggests that oceans have
(a) prevented all effects of global warming (b) stopped marine heatwaves from occurring (c) temporarily protected land areas by absorbing heat (d) reduced the intensity of tropical storms
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Shielded" implies protection, but "at a cost" shows it is not total or free — ruling out (a). Options (b) and (d) contradict the passage. The answer is that oceans temporarily protected land areas by absorbing heat.
Source: PYQ-style

107. Marine heatwaves since the 1980s have
(a) remained stable (b) doubled in frequency (c) slightly decreased (d) tripled in frequency
Answer: (b)
Solution: This is a direct fact question; the passage says heatwaves "have doubled in frequency." Option (d) is the tempting exaggeration trap. The answer is doubled in frequency.
Source: PYQ-style

108. [Examiner's Trap] Which of the following is NOT mentioned as a consequence of ocean warming?
(a) Coral bleaching (b) Migration of fish toward the poles (c) Rising sea levels flooding coastal cities (d) More intense tropical storms
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is that rising sea levels is a famous, true effect of warming — but this passage never mentions it; the question asks what is NOT mentioned. Options (a), (b) and (d) all appear in the text. The answer is rising sea levels flooding coastal cities.
Source: PYQ-style

109. The word "bleaching" in the passage most nearly means
(a) cleaning with chemicals (b) dyeing artificially (c) losing colour due to stress (d) warming gradually
Answer: (c)
Solution: In context, coral bleaching means corals losing colour when stressed by heat; the literal chemical sense (a) is the trap. The answer is losing colour due to stress.

110. The author's primary purpose is to
(a) praise the oceans for absorbing heat (b) argue that greenhouse gases are harmless (c) describe fishing techniques of coastal communities (d) explain the hidden costs of ocean heat absorption
Answer: (d)
Solution: The passage's structure — benefit, then "at a cost" — shows the purpose is to reveal hidden costs. Options (a) and (b) misread the tone; (c) is a minor detail. The answer is to explain the hidden costs of ocean heat absorption.

111. Choose the word closest in meaning to AMPLIFY (as used in Passage 1):
(a) intensify (b) diminish (c) complicate (d) conceal
Answer: (a)
Solution: Amplify means to make stronger or greater; "intensify" matches. "Complicate" (c) is the tempting near-miss but means to make harder to understand, not stronger. The answer is intensify.
Source: PYQ-style

112. Choose the word OPPOSITE in meaning to NEUTRAL:
(a) impartial (b) moderate (c) passive (d) biased
Answer: (d)
Solution: Neutral means unbiased; its opposite is biased. Option (a) is a synonym — the classic trap in antonym questions. The answer is biased.
Source: PYQ-style

113. In the sentence "The committee were divided in their opinions," the subject-verb usage is
(a) incorrect; it must always be "was" (b) correct; "were" agrees with "opinions" (c) incorrect; "opinions" should be singular (d) correct; collective noun takes plural verb when members act separately
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is option (b): the verb agrees with "committee," not "opinions." A collective noun takes a plural verb when members act individually, as here with divided opinions. The answer is that it is correct because the members act separately.

114. Spot the error: "Neither of the two candidates (P) / have submitted (Q) / their documents (R) / on time. (S)"
(a) Part P (b) No error (c) Part R (d) Part Q
Answer: (d)
Solution: "Neither of" takes a singular verb, so Part Q should read "has submitted." Part R is fine because "their" is now accepted with neither. The error is in Part Q.

115. Choose the correctly spelt word:
(a) Occurence (b) Occurrence (c) Ocurrence (d) Occurrance
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Occurrence" doubles both the c and the r. The other options each drop or misplace one double letter. The answer is Occurrence.

116. The idiom "to read between the lines" means
(a) to read very slowly (b) to understand the hidden or implied meaning (c) to skip lines while reading (d) to memorise a text
Answer: (b)
Solution: The idiom refers to grasping what is implied but not stated — the core inference skill. Options (a) and (c) take the idiom literally, which is the trap. The answer is to understand the hidden or implied meaning.

Passage 3 (Questions 117–121): Economists once assumed that people make rational choices to maximise their own benefit. Behavioural economics has overturned this view, showing that decisions are shaped by framing, habit and emotion. For instance, shoppers buy more of a product labelled "90% fat-free" than one labelled "contains 10% fat," though the two are identical. Such findings have led governments to design "nudges" — small changes in how choices are presented — to encourage saving, vaccination and energy conservation without restricting freedom.

117. The example of "90% fat-free" versus "10% fat" illustrates that
(a) shoppers cannot read labels (b) framing the same fact differently changes decisions (c) fat-free products are healthier (d) governments control what people buy
Answer: (b)
Solution: The identical products show that presentation, not content, drives choice — the framing effect. Option (a) insults shoppers and misses the point; (d) distorts "nudges." The answer is that framing the same fact differently changes decisions.
Source: PYQ-style

118. A "nudge," as described in the passage, is
(a) a law forcing people to save money (b) a financial reward for vaccination (c) a small change in how choices are presented (d) a ban on unhealthy products
Answer: (c)
Solution: The passage defines nudges as "small changes in how choices are presented" that preserve freedom — ruling out force (a) and bans (d). The answer is a small change in how choices are presented.
Source: PYQ-style

119. [Examiner's Trap] According to the passage, behavioural economics claims that people
(a) always act against their own interest (b) are influenced by framing, habit and emotion (c) never make rational choices (d) maximise benefit in every decision
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap options (a) and (c) use absolute words — "always" and "never" — which overstate the passage; it says decisions are "shaped by" these factors, not wholly irrational. Option (d) is the old view that was overturned. The answer is that people are influenced by framing, habit and emotion.
Source: PYQ-style

120. The author's attitude toward behavioural economics is
(a) hostile (b) neutral and informative (c) mocking (d) fearful
Answer: (b)
Solution: The author reports findings and applications without judgement — an informative, neutral tone. There is no hostility (a) or mockery (c). The answer is neutral and informative.

121. It can be inferred that nudges are considered ethically acceptable because they
(a) do not restrict people's freedom of choice (b) are invisible to the public (c) are designed by private companies (d) always produce the desired result
Answer: (a)
Solution: The passage ends with "without restricting freedom," implying this is what makes nudges acceptable. Options (b), (c) and (d) are not supported. The answer is that they do not restrict freedom of choice.

122. Choose the sentence with correct parallel structure:
(a) She likes reading, swimming, and to cycle. (b) She likes reading, to swim, and cycling. (c) She likes to read, swimming, and cycling. (d) She likes reading, swimming, and cycling.
Answer: (d)
Solution: Parallelism requires all items in the same form; only option (d) uses three gerunds consistently. The others mix gerunds with infinitives. The answer is "She likes reading, swimming, and cycling."
Source: PYQ-style

123. Spot the error: "One of my friends (P) / have gone (Q) / to Delhi (R) / yesterday. (S)"
(a) Part P (b) Part R (c) Part Q (d) No error
Answer: (c)
Solution: "One of + plural noun" takes a singular verb, so Part Q should be "has gone." The trap is thinking "friends" controls the verb; the true subject is "One." The error is in Part Q.
Source: PYQ-style

124. Choose the word closest in meaning to CANDID:
(a) frank (b) evasive (c) clever (d) timid
Answer: (a)
Solution: Candid means open and truthful — frank. "Evasive" (b) is its opposite and the common confusion trap. The answer is frank.
Source: PYQ-style

125. In an inference question, the safest answer is the one that
(a) uses strong absolute words like "always" and "never" (b) is directly copied word-for-word from the passage (c) introduces new information not in the passage (d) follows logically from the passage without overstating it
Answer: (d)
Solution: Absolute wording (a) is the classic overstatement trap; word-for-word copying (b) signals a fact, not an inference; new information (c) is unsupported. The answer is the option that follows logically without overstating the passage.
