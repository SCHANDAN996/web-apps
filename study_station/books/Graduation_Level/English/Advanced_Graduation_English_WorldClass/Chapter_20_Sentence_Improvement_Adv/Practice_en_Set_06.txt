126. Choose the option that best improves the underlined part: "Scarcely he had entered the room when the phone rang."
(a) No improvement (b) Scarcely he entered (c) Scarcely had he entered (d) Scarcely did he entered
Answer: (c)
Solution: After negative adverbs like "scarcely" at the start of a sentence, inversion is required: "Scarcely had he entered". The past perfect is correct because the entry happened before the phone rang.
Source: PYQ-style

127. Choose the option that best improves the underlined part: "The manager, along with his assistants, have left for the conference."
(a) has left (b) were leaving (c) have been leaving (d) No improvement
Answer: (a)
Solution: With "along with", the verb agrees with the first subject, "the manager" (singular), so "has left" is correct. The intervening phrase "along with his assistants" is the examiner's trap.
Source: PYQ-style

128. Choose the option that best improves the underlined part: "He is one of the best players that has ever played for the country."
(a) who has ever played (b) No improvement (c) which has ever played (d) that have ever played
Answer: (d)
Solution: In "one of the + plural noun + relative clause", the verb in the relative clause agrees with the plural noun "players", so "have" is correct. This is a classic sound-right trap.
Source: PYQ-style

129. Choose the option that best improves the underlined part: "No sooner did the bell ring when the students rushed out."
(a) then the students rushed out (b) than the students rushed out (c) as the students rushed out (d) No improvement
Answer: (b)
Solution: "No sooner" is always paired with "than", never "when". "Scarcely" and "Hardly" take "when", but "no sooner" takes "than".
Source: PYQ-style

130. Choose the option that best improves the underlined part: "The committee were unanimous in their decision to postpone the event."
(a) was unanimous in its decision (b) were unanimous in its decision (c) was unanimous in their decision (d) No improvement
Answer: (a)
Solution: When a collective noun acts as a single unit (unanimous), it takes a singular verb and singular pronoun: "was unanimous in its decision". Consistency of number is the trap here.

131. Choose the option that best improves the underlined part: "Neither of the two boys have submitted his assignment."
(a) have submitted their assignments (b) No improvement (c) has submitted their assignment (d) has submitted his assignment
Answer: (d)
Solution: "Neither of" takes a singular verb and singular pronoun: "has submitted his". The plural "boys" before the verb is an intervening-phrase trap.

132. Choose the option that best improves the underlined part: "The teacher asked the students that why they were late."
(a) why they were late (b) why were they late (c) that why they were late (d) that why were they late
Answer: (a)
Solution: In indirect speech, "that" is not used before a wh-word, and the clause takes statement order: "why they were late", not question order.

133. Choose the option that best improves the underlined part: "Hardly had she finished her speech than the audience started applauding."
(a) as the audience started applauding (b) then the audience started applauding (c) when the audience started applauding (d) No improvement
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Hardly" and "scarcely" are paired with "when"; only "no sooner" pairs with "than". The given sentence mixes the two correlative pairs.

(a) were given a certificate (b) have been given certificates (c) has been given a certificate (d) No improvement
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Each of" is followed by a plural noun but a singular verb: "has been given". The plural noun "students" is the intervening-phrase trap.

135. Choose the option that best improves the underlined part: "I look forward to meet you at the seminar next week."
(a) to have met you (b) for meeting you (c) to meeting you (d) No improvement
Answer: (c)
Solution: In "look forward to", "to" is a preposition, not part of an infinitive, so it takes a gerund: "to meeting you". This is a very common sound-right trap.
Source: PYQ-style

136. Choose the option that best improves the underlined part: "The number of accidents on this highway are increasing every year."
(a) are increased every year (b) have increased every year (c) No improvement (d) is increasing every year
Answer: (d)
Solution: "The number of" takes a singular verb; "a number of" takes a plural verb. Here "the number" is the subject, so "is increasing" is correct.
Source: PYQ-style

137. Choose the option that best improves the underlined part: "He denied to help me in my difficulty."
(a) denied helping me (b) denied for helping me (c) denied to helping me (d) No improvement
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Deny" is followed by a gerund or a that-clause, never an infinitive, so "denied helping me" is correct. The infinitive after "deny" is the error; verbs like "refuse" take the infinitive, but "deny" does not.
Source: PYQ-style

138. Choose the option that best improves the underlined part: "Unless you do not work hard, you will not succeed."
(a) Until you do not work hard (b) Unless you work hard (c) Unless you worked hard (d) No improvement

Solution: When "not only" begins a clause, inversion is required: "Not only was he late, but he also forgot…". The normal word order is the error.

(a) is very expensive (b) were very expensive (c) have been very expensive (d) No improvement
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Furniture" is an uncountable noun and always takes a singular verb: "is very expensive". Words like furniture, luggage, information, and advice are classic traps.

150. Choose the option that best improves the underlined part: "He told me that he will come to the party the next day."
(a) he will come to the party the next day (b) No improvement (c) he would come to the party tomorrow (d) he would come to the party the next day
Answer: (d)
Solution: In reported speech with a past reporting verb ("told"), "will" backshifts to "would", and "the next day" correctly replaces "tomorrow": "he would come to the party the next day".
