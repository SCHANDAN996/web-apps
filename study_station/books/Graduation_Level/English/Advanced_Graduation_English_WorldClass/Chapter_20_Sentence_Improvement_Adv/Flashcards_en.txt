Card 1
Front: "One of my friends have gone to Delhi." — Spot the error.
Back: "have" → "has". With "one of + plural noun", the verb agrees with "one" (singular). Correct: "One of my friends has gone to Delhi."

Card 2
Front: What verb form follows "It is high time…"?
Back: Past simple (V2), even though the meaning is present/future. "It is high time we left." (Not "leave".)

Card 3
Front: "The Ganges is one of the longest river in India." — Correct it.
Back: "river" → "rivers". After "one of the + superlative", the noun must be plural: "one of the longest rivers".

Card 4
Front: 🧠 MNEMONIC: How to remember verbs followed only by gerunds (V-ing)?
Back: "MRS PADEQ": Mind, Resent, Suggest, Practise, Avoid, Deny, Enjoy, Quit. All take -ing: "I enjoy swimming," never "enjoy to swim."

Card 5
Front: "Neither of the two boys have done his homework." — Is this correct?
Back: No. "Neither of" takes a singular verb: "Neither of the two boys has done his homework."

Card 6
Front: ⚠️ EXAMINER'S TRAP: "The teacher, as well as the students, were present." — Right or wrong?
Back: Wrong. With "as well as / along with / together with", the verb agrees with the FIRST subject only: "The teacher… was present." The intervening phrase is a decoy.

Card 7
Front: When do we use "fewer" and when "less"?
Back: "Fewer" for countable nouns (fewer students); "less" for uncountable nouns (less water). Trap: "less people" is wrong → "fewer people".

Card 8
Front: "He is more wiser than his brother." — Spot the error.
Back: Double comparative. Use either "wiser" or "more wise" — never both. Correct: "He is wiser than his brother."

Card 9
Front: What is the correct order of adjectives before a noun?
Back: OSASCOMP: Opinion, Size, Age, Shape, Colour, Origin, Material, Purpose. "A beautiful small old round black Italian wooden dining table."

Card 10
Front: "I, you and he will go." — Is the pronoun order correct?
Back: For pleasant actions, order is 2-3-1: "You, he and I will go." For confessions/blame, use 1-2-3: "I, you and he are guilty."

Card 11
Front: "No sooner did he enter the room than the phone rang." — Why "than" and not "when"?
Back: "No sooner" always pairs with "than"; "Hardly/Scarcely" pair with "when/before". Mixing them is a classic trap.

Card 12
Front: 🧠 MNEMONIC: Which conjunctions keep the verb agreeing with the NEARER subject?
Back: "NEON": Neither…nor, Either…or, Or, Not only…but also. "Neither the boys nor the teacher was late." (teacher = nearer → singular)

Card 13
Front: "He denied to help me." — Correct it.
Back: "denied to help" → "denied helping" (or "refused to help"). "Deny" takes a gerund; "refuse" takes an infinitive.

Card 14
Front: ⚠️ EXAMINER'S TRAP: "The cattle is grazing in the field." — Right or wrong?
Back: Wrong. "Cattle" is always plural: "The cattle are grazing." Same family: people, police, poultry — all take plural verbs.

Card 15
Front: When is "the" used before a superlative, and when can it be dropped?
Back: "The" is required before superlatives used attributively: "the best player". It can drop in predicative use after possessives or in comparisons of the same person: "He is (the) tallest in the class."

Card 16
Front: "Unless you do not work hard, you will fail." — Spot the error.
Back: Double negative. "Unless" already means "if not": "Unless you work hard, you will fail."

Card 17
Front: What is the difference between "economic" and "economical"?
Back: "Economic" = related to the economy (economic policy). "Economical" = money-saving (an economical car). Sound-alike adjectives are a favourite trap.

Card 18
Front: "He is working here since 2015." — Correct it.
Back: "is working" → "has been working". With "since/for" showing duration up to now, use present perfect (continuous): "He has been working here since 2015."

Card 19
Front: Which words are followed by "to" as a preposition (taking V-ing), not an infinitive?
Back: "Be used to, look forward to, object to, be accustomed to, be addicted to." "I look forward to meeting you" — not "to meet".

Card 20
Front: "The furniture in this room are very old." — Spot the error.
Back: "Furniture" is uncountable → singular verb: "The furniture… is very old." Same family: luggage, information, advice, scenery.
