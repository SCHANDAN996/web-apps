Questions 1–20: Easy | Questions 21–25: Medium

1. Choose the correct improvement: "He is more cleverer than his brother."
(a) more clever (b) most clever (c) cleverer (d) No improvement
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Cleverer" is already comparative; using "more" with it is a double comparative error. "More clever" is grammatically possible but the standard exam answer is the simple comparative "cleverer".
Source: PYQ-style

2. Choose the correct improvement: "She insisted to go with me."
(a) No improvement (b) in going (c) for going (d) on going
Answer: (d)
Solution: "Insist" is followed by the preposition "on" plus a gerund: "insisted on going". The infinitive "to go" after "insist" is a classic fixed-preposition trap.
Source: PYQ-style

3. Choose the correct improvement: "I have been living in Delhi since five years."
(a) from five years (b) for five years (c) since five years ago (d) No improvement
Answer: (b)
Solution: "For" is used with a period of time (five years); "since" is used with a point of time (since 2019). This is the most frequently tested since/for distinction.
Source: NCERT-based grammar

4. Choose the correct improvement: "Neither of the two boys have done his homework."
(a) has done his (b) have done their (c) has done their (d) No improvement
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Neither of" takes a singular verb and singular pronoun: "has done his". The plural noun "boys" before the verb is an intervening-phrase trap.
Source: PYQ-style

5. Choose the correct improvement: "He denied to help me."
(a) refused to help (b) denied for helping (c) denied helping (d) No improvement
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Deny" is followed by a gerund, not an infinitive: "denied helping". "Refused to help" changes the verb rather than improving the given one, so the gerund form is the direct improvement.
Source: PYQ-style

6. Choose the correct improvement: "Unless you do not work hard, you will fail."
(a) Until you do not work hard (b) Unless you work hard (c) If you do not work hard not (d) No improvement
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Unless" already carries a negative meaning, so "do not" must be removed: "Unless you work hard". Double negative with "unless" is a favourite examiner trap.
Source: PYQ-style

7. Choose the correct improvement: "The teacher asked that why he was late."
(a) asked why he was late (b) asked that why was he late (c) asked why was he late (d) No improvement
Answer: (a)
Solution: In indirect speech no "that" is used before a wh-word, and the order stays statement order: "asked why he was late". Option with "was he" wrongly keeps question order.
Source: NCERT-based grammar

8. Choose the correct improvement: "He is one of the best player in the team."
(a) one of best player (b) one of the best players (c) one of the best playing (d) No improvement
Answer: (b)
Solution: After "one of the + superlative", the noun must be plural: "one of the best players". The singular "player" is the error.
Source: PYQ-style

9. Choose the correct improvement: "I am looking forward to meet you."
(a) for meeting you (b) to meeting you (c) to met you (d) No improvement
Answer: (b)
Solution: In "look forward to", "to" is a preposition, so it takes a gerund: "to meeting you". The infinitive "to meet" is the classic sound-right trap.
Source: PYQ-style

10. Choose the correct improvement: "Scarcely he had reached the station when the train left."
(a) Scarcely had he reached (b) Scarcely he reached (c) He had scarcely reached when (d) No improvement
Answer: (a)
Solution: When a negative adverb like "scarcely" begins a sentence, inversion is required: "Scarcely had he reached… when…". Note "when" pairs with scarcely, not "than".
Source: PYQ-style

11. Choose the correct improvement: "No sooner did she enter the room when the phone rang."
(a) than the phone rang (b) then the phone rang (c) as the phone rang (d) No improvement
Answer: (a)
Solution: "No sooner" is always paired with "than", never "when". "Scarcely/hardly" take "when" — examiners swap these to confuse.
Source: PYQ-style

12. Choose the correct improvement: "He is working here since 2015."
(a) No improvement (b) was working here since 2015 (c) is working here from 2015 (d) has been working here since 2015
Answer: (d)
Solution: An action starting in the past and continuing now needs present perfect continuous with "since": "has been working". Simple present with "since" is a common Hindi-speaker error.
Source: NCERT-based grammar

13. Choose the correct improvement: "The furniture in this room are very old."
(a) is very old (b) were very old (c) have been very old (d) No improvement
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Furniture" is an uncountable noun and always takes a singular verb: "is very old". Other such nouns: luggage, information, advice, news.
Source: PYQ-style

14. Choose the correct improvement: "He availed of the opportunity."
(a) availed for (b) availed to (c) availed himself of (d) No improvement
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Avail" used in this sense needs a reflexive pronoun: "availed himself of the opportunity". Without the reflexive, the sentence is incomplete — a frequently tested point.
Source: PYQ-style

15. Choose the correct improvement: "I prefer tea more than coffee."
(a) prefer tea over coffee (b) prefer tea than coffee (c) prefer tea to coffee (d) No improvement
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Prefer" is followed by "to", not "than" or "more than": "prefer tea to coffee". Words like senior, junior, superior, prefer take "to".
Source: PYQ-style

16. Choose the correct improvement: "Each of the students have submitted the assignment."
(a) are submitting (b) have been submitting (c) has submitted (d) No improvement
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Each of" takes a singular verb: "has submitted". The plural "students" in between is an intervening-phrase trap meant to pull you toward "have".
Source: PYQ-style

17. Choose the correct improvement: "He told me that he will come tomorrow."
(a) he will come the next day (b) he would come the next day (c) he would come tomorrow (d) No improvement
Answer: (b)
Solution: In reported speech with a past reporting verb, "will" becomes "would" and "tomorrow" becomes "the next day". Both changes are needed.
Source: NCERT-based grammar

18. Choose the correct improvement: "The police has arrested the thief."
(a) No improvement (b) had arrested (c) is arresting (d) have arrested
Answer: (d)
Solution: "Police" is a plural noun and takes a plural verb: "have arrested". Similar plural-only nouns: cattle, people, clergy.
Source: PYQ-style

19. Choose the correct improvement: "She is good in mathematics."
(a) good at mathematics (b) good with mathematics (c) good for mathematics (d) No improvement
Answer: (a)
Solution: The correct fixed preposition is "good at" for skills and subjects. "Good in" is a direct translation from Hindi ("अच्छी है गणित में") — a linguistic-bridge error.
Source: PYQ-style

20. Choose the correct improvement: "Hardly had he slept than the alarm rang."
(a) then the alarm rang (b) when the alarm rang (c) as the alarm rang (d) No improvement
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Hardly" and "scarcely" pair with "when"; only "no sooner" pairs with "than". Examiners deliberately mix these pairs.
Source: PYQ-style

21. Choose the correct improvement: "The Ganges is one of the longest rivers that has its origin in the Himalayas."
(a) No improvement (b) which has its origin (c) that have its origin (d) that have their origin
Answer: (d)
Solution: The relative pronoun "that" refers to "rivers" (plural), so the verb must be "have" and the pronoun "their". The singular "one" nearby is the intervening-phrase trap.
Source: PYQ-style

22. Choose the correct improvement: "Being a rainy day, I stayed at home."
(a) It being a rainy day (b) Being raining (c) As being a rainy day (d) No improvement
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Being a rainy day, I stayed…" wrongly suggests "I" was a rainy day — a dangling modifier. "It being a rainy day" gives the participle its proper subject.
Source: PYQ-style

23. Choose the correct improvement: "He is more superior to his colleague in experience."
(a) No improvement (b) more superior than (c) superior than (d) superior to
Answer: (d)
Solution: "Superior" is already comparative in meaning; it takes "to" and never "more" or "than". Double-comparative plus wrong preposition is a combined trap.
Source: PYQ-style

24. Choose the correct improvement: "I, along with my friends, were present at the meeting."
(a) No improvement (b) are present (c) have been present (d) was present
Answer: (d)
Solution: With "along with", the verb agrees with the first subject "I", so "was present" is correct. The nearer plural "friends" is the classic proximity trap.
Source: PYQ-style

25. Choose the correct improvement: "No sooner had the match started when it began to rain."
(a) as it began to rain (b) then it began to rain (c) than it began to rain (d) No improvement
Answer: (c)
Solution: "No sooner… than" is the fixed correlative pair; "when" belongs with "hardly/scarcely". This pairing swap is one of the most repeated examiner traps.
Source: PYQ-style
