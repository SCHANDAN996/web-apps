101. No sooner had the meeting begun when the fire alarm rang.
(a) than the fire alarm rang (b) then the fire alarm rang (c) as the fire alarm rang (d) No improvement
Answer: (a)
Solution: "No sooner" is always paired with "than", never "when". The correct correlative pair is "No sooner... than".
Source: PYQ-style

102. The manager, along with his assistants, have left for the conference.
(a) were leaving for the conference (b) has left for the conference (c) have been left for the conference (d) No improvement
Answer: (b)
Solution: When two subjects are joined by "along with", the verb agrees with the first subject ("the manager", singular), so "has left" is correct. The intervening phrase is the examiner's trap.
Source: PYQ-style

103. Hardly had she entered the room than the lights went off.
(a) when the lights went off (b) then the lights went off (c) as the lights went off (d) No improvement
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Hardly" and "Scarcely" take "when", while "No sooner" takes "than". Mixing the two pairs is a classic trap.
Source: NCERT-based grammar

104. He is one of the best players that has ever represented the country.
(a) No improvement (b) who has ever represented the country (c) that is ever representing the country (d) that have ever represented the country
Answer: (d)
Solution: In "one of the + plural noun + relative clause", the verb in the relative clause agrees with the plural noun ("players"), so "have" is correct. This is a favourite examiner trap.
Source: PYQ-style

105. Unless you do not work hard, you will not succeed.
(a) Until you do not work hard (b) Unless you work hard (c) If you do not work hard not (d) No improvement
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Unless" already carries a negative meaning, so "not" must not be used with it. Double negative makes the sentence wrong.
Source: NCERT-based grammar

106. The Ganges is one of the longest river in India.
(a) one of longest river in India (b) one of the longest rivers in India (c) the longest rivers in India (d) No improvement
Answer: (b)
Solution: After "one of the + superlative", the noun must be plural: "one of the longest rivers".
Source: PYQ-style

107. I look forward to meet you at the seminar next week.
(a) to met you at the seminar (b) to meeting you at the seminar (c) for meeting you at the seminar (d) No improvement
Answer: (b)
Solution: In "look forward to", "to" is a preposition, not part of an infinitive, so it takes a gerund: "to meeting".
Source: PYQ-style

108. Neither of the two boys have done his homework.
(a) No improvement (b) have done their homework (c) has done their homework (d) has done his homework
Answer: (d)
Solution: "Neither of" takes a singular verb and singular pronoun: "has done his homework".
Source: NCERT-based grammar

109. The teacher asked the students that why they were late.
(a) asked the students why were they late (b) asked the students that why were they late (c) asked the students why they were late (d) No improvement
Answer: (c)
Solution: In indirect questions, "that" is not used with a wh-word, and the word order stays affirmative: "why they were late".
Source: PYQ-style

110. He denied to help me in my difficulty.
(a) No improvement (b) denied helping me not (c) denied for helping me (d) refused to help me
Answer: (d)
Solution: "Deny" is followed by a gerund, not an infinitive; since "denied to help" is wrong, "refused to help" correctly conveys the meaning.
Source: PYQ-style

111. Scarcely he had stepped out of the house when it began to rain.
(a) No improvement (b) Scarcely he has stepped out of the house (c) Hardly he had stepped out of the house (d) Scarcely had he stepped out of the house
Answer: (d)
Solution: When a negative adverb like "Scarcely" begins a sentence, inversion is required: "Scarcely had he stepped out".
Source: NCERT-based grammar

112. Each of the students in the class have submitted the assignment.
(a) have been submitting the assignment (b) has submitted the assignment (c) are submitting the assignment (d) No improvement
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Each of" is followed by a plural noun but a singular verb: "has submitted".
Source: PYQ-style

113. The principal as well as the teachers were present at the function.
(a) was present at the function (b) were presented at the function (c) have been present at the function (d) No improvement
Answer: (a)
Solution: With "as well as", the verb agrees with the first subject ("the principal", singular), so "was present". The phrase between subject and verb is the trap.
Source: PYQ-style

114. He is more wiser than his brother.
(a) most wiser than his brother (b) more wise than his brother (c) wiser than his brother (d) No improvement
Answer: (c)
Solution: Double comparatives are wrong; "wiser" is already comparative, so "more" must be dropped.
Source: NCERT-based grammar

115. No sooner did the bell ring when the students rushed out.
(a) then the students rushed out (b) than the students rushed out (c) as the students rushed out (d) No improvement
Answer: (b)
Solution: "No sooner... than" is the fixed correlative pair; "when" belongs with "hardly/scarcely". This pairing confusion is a repeated examiner trap.
Source: PYQ-style

116. Being a rainy day, I stayed at home.
(a) No improvement (b) Being a rainy day, staying at home (c) As being a rainy day, I stayed at home (d) It being a rainy day, I stayed at home
Answer: (d)
Solution: The original has a dangling participle — the day is not "I". "It being a rainy day" correctly attaches the reason.
Source: PYQ-style

117. He insisted to go with me to the station.
(a) insisted in going with me (b) insisted for going with me (c) insisted on going with me (d) No improvement
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Insist" is followed by the preposition "on" plus a gerund: "insisted on going".
Source: NCERT-based grammar

118. The number of accidents on this road are increasing every year.
(a) is increasing every year (b) were increasing every year (c) have increased every year (d) No improvement
Answer: (a)
Solution: "The number of" takes a singular verb, while "a number of" takes a plural verb. Examiners exploit this contrast.
Source: PYQ-style

119. I have been living in this city since five years.
(a) since five years ago (b) from five years (c) for five years (d) No improvement
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Since" is used with a point of time; "for" is used with a period of time like "five years".
Source: NCERT-based grammar

120. Walking through the park, the flowers looked beautiful.
(a) The flowers looked beautiful walking through the park (b) Walking through the park, the flowers were looking beautiful (c) Walking through the park, I found the flowers beautiful (d) No improvement
Answer: (c)
Solution: The original is a dangling modifier — flowers cannot walk. The subject doing the walking ("I") must follow the participial phrase.
Source: PYQ-style

121. He is senior than me by two years.
(a) No improvement (b) senior from me by two years (c) more senior than me by two years (d) senior to me by two years
Answer: (d)
Solution: Latin comparatives like senior, junior, superior, inferior and prior take "to", not "than".
Source: NCERT-based grammar

122. One should do his duty honestly.
(a) One should do one's duty honestly (b) One should do their duty honestly (c) One should do her duty honestly (d) No improvement
Answer: (a)
Solution: The pronoun for "one" must remain "one/one's" throughout the sentence; mixing it with "his" is a common trap.
Source: PYQ-style

123. The committee has taken their decision unanimously.
(a) has taken its decision unanimously (b) have taken their decision unanimously (c) has taken his decision unanimously (d) No improvement
Answer: (a)
Solution: When a collective noun acts as a single unit (indicated by "unanimously"), it takes a singular verb and singular pronoun "its".
Source: PYQ-style

124. He told me that he will come to the party the next day.
(a) he shall come to the party the next day (b) he will be coming to the party the next day (c) he would come to the party the next day (d) No improvement
Answer: (c)
Solution: When the reporting verb is in the past tense, "will" changes to "would" in reported speech.
Source: NCERT-based grammar

125. Bread and butter are my favourite breakfast.
(a) is my favourite breakfast (b) were my favourite breakfast (c) have been my favourite breakfast (d) No improvement
Answer: (a)
Solution: When two nouns joined by "and" denote a single dish or idea, the verb is singular: "Bread and butter is".
Source: PYQ-style
