# Sentence Improvement (Advanced)

## The Hook: One Word Can Cost You the Job

In SSC CGL, IBPS PO, and State PCS prelims, Sentence Improvement questions look deceptively friendly: a sentence with an underlined part, three alternatives, and "No improvement." Most aspirants answer by *ear* — "this sounds right." That is exactly what the examiner is counting on. At the advanced level, the wrong option is deliberately designed to *sound* more natural than the correct one. This chapter trains you to answer by **rule**, not by ear — so that a question others solve in 60 doubtful seconds, you solve in 15 confident ones.

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this chapter you will be able to:

1. Identify the grammatical concept being tested behind any underlined phrase (tense, subject-verb agreement, parallelism, modifiers, idioms, conditionals, subjunctive).
2. Apply the 12 Golden Rules to eliminate wrong options systematically.
3. Distinguish Fact-based questions (15-second rule) from Inference-based ones (45-second rule).
4. Recognise the four classic examiner traps: Intervening Phrase, Collective Noun, Sound-Right, and Over-thinking.
5. Decide when "No improvement" is genuinely correct — and when it is bait.

---

## Part 1: What Sentence Improvement Really Tests

A Sentence Improvement question is never testing "which sentence is prettiest." It tests one specific rule hidden inside the underlined portion. Your first job is **diagnosis**: read the whole sentence, ignore the underlining for a moment, and ask — *what is grammatically wrong here, if anything?* Only then look at the options.

The advanced twist: sometimes the original sentence is already correct, and the "improved" options introduce errors. Roughly speaking, "No improvement" is correct often enough that you must check it seriously — but rarely enough that choosing it by default is a losing strategy.

🧠 **Active Recall:** Before looking at options in a Sentence Improvement question, what should you do first? (Answer: diagnose the error in the original sentence yourself.)

## Part 2: The Core Battlegrounds

### 2.1 Tense and Time Consistency

Advanced questions love mixing time frames. The rule: the tense of a subordinate clause must be consistent with the main clause unless logic demands otherwise.

- ❌ *He said that he **will** come tomorrow.* → ✅ *He said that he **would** come the next day.*
- ❌ *I **have seen** him yesterday.* → ✅ *I **saw** him yesterday.* (A definite past time marker like *yesterday* forbids the present perfect.)

### 2.2 Subject–Verb Agreement with Intervening Phrases

The examiner inserts a long phrase between subject and verb to make you match the verb to the wrong noun.

- ❌ *The quality of the mangoes **were** not good.* → ✅ *The quality of the mangoes **was** not good.* (Subject = *quality*, singular.)

### 2.3 Parallelism

Items in a list or comparison must share the same grammatical form.

- ❌ *She likes reading, swimming, and **to dance**.* → ✅ *She likes reading, swimming, and **dancing**.*

🧠 **Active Recall:** In "The captain, along with his teammates, ___ arrived," which verb — *has* or *have*? (Answer: *has* — "along with" phrases do not change the subject.)

### 2.4 Misplaced and Dangling Modifiers

A modifying phrase must sit next to the thing it modifies.

- ❌ *Walking down the road, a snake bit him.* (Was the snake walking?) → ✅ *Walking down the road, he was bitten by a snake.*

### 2.5 Conditionals and the Subjunctive

- Type 2 (unreal present): *If I **were** a bird, I would fly.* — *were*, not *was*, for all persons.
- Type 3 (unreal past): *If he **had worked** harder, he **would have passed**.*
- After *wish, if only, as if, it is time*: use the past form for present unreal — *I wish I **were** taller.* / *It is time you **went** home.*

### 2.6 Fixed Idiomatic Structures

These cannot be reasoned out — they must be memorised:

- *No sooner **had** he left **than** the phone rang.*
- *Hardly/Scarcely **had** she arrived **when** it rained.*
- *The **sooner**, the **better**.*
- *He **insisted on** going.* / *She is **good at** chess.* / *He **abstained from** voting.*

🧠 **Active Recall:** Which word pairs with "No sooner" — *when* or *than*? (Answer: *than*. *Hardly/Scarcely* take *when*.)

## 🧷 Mnemonic: "**TAPS-MIC**" — the seven things to check, in order

**T**ense → **A**greement → **P**arallelism → **S**ubjunctive/conditionals → **M**odifiers → **I**dioms → **C**omparisons.

Run the underlined part through TAPS-MIC like a checklist. The moment one letter fails, you have your diagnosis.

---

## ⚠️ Examiner's Trap Box

**Trap 1 — The Sound-Right Trap:** The wrong option is phrased the way people actually speak. *"He is more smarter than me"* sounds conversational; the correct form is *"smarter than I"* (double comparatives like *more smarter* are always wrong). If an option sounds like everyday speech, be suspicious.

**Trap 2 — The Intervening Phrase Trap:** *"One of my friends **have**…"* — the plural word *friends* sits right before the verb to pull your eye. The subject is *One*, so the verb must be *has*. Always strip the sentence to subject + verb before deciding.

**Trap 3 — The Over-thinking Trap:** The original sentence is perfectly correct, but it is short and plain, so candidates assume "it must be wrong — the exam can't be that easy" and pick a flowery, incorrect alternative. When TAPS-MIC finds no error, choose **No improvement** with confidence.

## 🔁 Linguistic Bridge: Hindi से English

| हिंदी में हम सोचते हैं | गलत English | सही English |
|---|---|---|
| "मैंने कल उसे देखा है" (है लगा दिया) | I have seen him yesterday. | I saw him yesterday. |
| "वह मुझसे बड़ा है" (from लगा दिया) | He is elder **from** me. | He is elder **to** me. |
| "मैं तीन साल से यहाँ हूँ" (since + अवधि) | I am here **since** three years. | I have been here **for** three years. |
| "उसने मुझसे शादी की" (with लगा दिया) | She married **with** him. | She married him. (no preposition) |
| "मेरे एक दोस्त ने…" | One of my friend… | One of my friend**s**… |

Rule of thumb: Hindi's postpositions and its flexible perfect tense do not map one-to-one onto English. Translate the *meaning*, never the *structure*.

## Part 3: The 12 Golden Rules (Preview — full table in the Rules section)

1. Definite past time (yesterday, ago, in 2019) → simple past, never present perfect.
2. *Since* + point of time; *for* + period of time — with perfect/perfect-continuous.
3. Intervening phrases (*along with, as well as, together with, in addition to*) do not change the subject's number.
4. *One of / Each of / Every one of* + plural noun + **singular** verb.
5. Uncountable nouns (*advice, information, furniture, luggage, news, scenery*) take singular verbs and never take *a/an* or a plural -s.
6. Parallel structures in lists and comparisons must match in form.
7. Double comparatives/double superlatives are wrong (*more better* ❌).
8. *Elder/eldest* take *to*, not *than*; *senior/junior/superior/inferior/prefer* also take *to*.
9. Unreal present wishes and conditions use *were* for all persons.
10. *No sooner…than*, *Hardly/Scarcely…when* — with past perfect in the first clause and inversion.
11. A participial phrase must modify the subject of the main clause (no dangling modifiers).
12. *It is time / It is high time* + past tense for a present meaning.

## Summary Table

| Concept | Wrong | Right | Quick Test |
|---|---|---|---|
| Past time marker | I have met him yesterday. | I met him yesterday. | See "yesterday/ago"? → simple past |
| Intervening phrase | The box of apples are heavy. | The box of apples is heavy. | Cover the phrase; check subject |
| One of… | One of the boys have come. | One of the boys has come. | Subject = One |
| Parallelism | He likes to read and swimming. | He likes reading and swimming. | Match the -ing/to-forms |
| Subjunctive | If I was you… | If I were you… | Unreal → were |
| Idiom | No sooner he had left when… | No sooner had he left than… | than/when pairing |
| Modifier | Flying over the hills, the houses looked tiny. | Flying over the hills, we saw the tiny houses. | Who is flying? |
| Comparison | He is senior than me. | He is senior to me. | senior/junior → to |
