# 📊 PYQ Analysis — Sentence Improvement (Advanced)

## How Exams Test This Chapter

Sentence Improvement is one of the most dependable areas of the English section across SSC CGL, CHSL, IBPS PO/Clerk, SBI PO, and State PCS prelims. The pattern is remarkably stable: a sentence with an underlined (or bold) part, three replacement options, and one "No improvement required" option. What changes from exam to exam is *which* grammar concept hides inside the underlined part.

**What examiners repeatedly ask (pattern analysis in words, not invented numbers):**

- **Tense consistency inside complex sentences** — the most persistent favourite. A past-tense main clause with a wrongly matched subordinate clause ("He said that he *will* come") appears in some form in nearly every cycle of SSC and Banking exams.
- **Subject–verb agreement with intervening phrases** — "The quality of the apples *were* good." Examiners love inserting long prepositional phrases between subject and verb.
- **Conditional sentences** — mixed conditionals and the classic "If I was/were" trap.
- **Parallel structure** — lists where one item breaks the grammatical pattern ("He likes reading, swimming, and *to jog*").
- **Misplaced and dangling modifiers** — "Walking down the road, the building collapsed" style items, common in CGL Tier-II and IBPS PO.
- **Idiomatic prepositions and phrasal verbs** — "discuss about," "comprise of," "cope up with."
- **Subjunctive and formal constructions** — "It is high time he *went*," "I would rather you *stayed*."
- **Comparatives and superlatives** — "more better," "senior/junior/prefer + to (not than)."

**Examiner's favourite traps (the four big families):**

1. **Intervening Phrase Trap** — a long phrase between subject and verb makes you match the verb to the wrong noun.
2. **Sound-Right Trap** — an option that "sounds natural" in spoken Indian English ("He is more smarter") but is grammatically wrong.
3. **Collective Noun / Indefinite Pronoun Trap** — "each of the boys have," "the committee were divided" — testing whether you know when singular vs. plural applies.
4. **Over-thinking Trap** — the original sentence is already correct, and "No improvement required" is the answer. Examiners know candidates rarely pick it.

**Weightage pattern (qualitative):** In SSC CGL Tier-I and CHSL, sentence improvement typically forms a small but fixed cluster of the English section; in Banking exams (IBPS/SBI PO), it often appears merged with "phrase replacement" and "error spotting" formats, and the difficulty leans toward modifiers, parallelism, and subjunctive forms rather than simple tense.

## Top 10 High-Yield PYQ-Style Questions with Solutions

**Q1.** The manager, along with his team members, **were present** at the meeting.
(a) were presenting
(b) was present
(c) have been present
(d) No improvement required

**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** With "along with," the verb agrees with the first subject ("the manager" — singular), so "was present." The intervening phrase "along with his team members" is the classic distractor.
**Trap type:** Intervening Phrase
**Source:** PYQ-style (SSC CGL pattern)

**Q2.** If I **was** the Prime Minister, I would abolish poverty.
(a) am
(b) were
(c) had been
(d) No improvement required

**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** Hypothetical/unreal conditions use the subjunctive "were" for all persons: "If I were…"
**Trap type:** Sound-Right ("was" sounds natural in speech)
**Source:** PYQ-style (SSC CHSL pattern)

**Q3.** He is **more superior** to his colleague in experience.
(a) much superior
(b) superior
(c) most superior
(d) No improvement required

**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** "Superior" is already comparative in meaning (Latin comparatives: superior, inferior, senior, junior, prior, posterior) and takes "to," never "more" or "than."
**Trap type:** Sound-Right
**Source:** PYQ-style (Banking exam pattern)

**Q4.** **Walking through the park, the flowers** looked beautiful.
(a) Walking through the park, I found the flowers
(b) While walking through the park, the flowers
(c) The flowers, walking through the park,
(d) No improvement required

**Answer: (a)**
**Solution:** Dangling modifier — "walking" must logically attach to a person, not "the flowers." Option (a) supplies the correct subject "I."
**Trap type:** Over-thinking (candidates often pick (b), which keeps the same error)
**Source:** PYQ-style (IBPS PO pattern)

**Q5.** Each of the students **have submitted** the assignment.
(a) have been submitting
(b) has submitted
(c) had submitted
(d) No improvement required

**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** "Each of + plural noun" takes a singular verb: "has submitted."
**Trap type:** Collective Noun / Indefinite Pronoun
**Source:** PYQ-style (SSC CGL pattern)

**Q6.** It is high time we **start** taking climate change seriously.
(a) started
(b) will start
(c) had started
(d) No improvement required

**Answer: (a)**
**Solution:** "It is high time + subject" is followed by the simple past (subjunctive usage) to express urgency about the present: "we started."
**Trap type:** Sound-Right ("start" feels logical because the meaning is present/future)
**Source:** PYQ-style (SSC CGL Tier-II pattern)

**Q7.** The teacher asked the students that why **they were not completing** their homework on time.
(a) why were they not completing
(b) that why they did not complete
(c) why they had not completed
(d) No improvement required

**Answer: (c)**
**Solution:** In reported questions, "that" must be dropped and statement word order (subject + verb) used. Option (c) removes "that" and keeps statement order; the past perfect "had not completed" correctly backshifts the original past continuous in reported speech. Option (a) wrongly keeps question order ("were they"), and (b) keeps the faulty "that why."
**Trap type:** Over-thinking
**Source:** PYQ-style (SSC pattern — reported speech)

**Q8.** He **denied to help** me in the hour of need.
(a) refused to help
(b) denied helping
(c) denied for helping
(d) No improvement required

**Answer: (a)**
**Solution:** "Deny" takes a gerund (deny doing) or a that-clause, not an infinitive; "refuse to help" is the idiomatic correction. (b) "denied helping" reverses the meaning (it would mean he admitted nothing about helping).
**Trap type:** Sound-Right
**Source:** PYQ-style (Banking pattern)

**Q9.** Neither of the two boys **have done** his work.
(a) have been doing
(b) has done
(c) had done
(d) No improvement required

**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** "Neither of" takes a singular verb in formal English: "has done."
**Trap type:** Collective Noun / Indefinite Pronoun
**Source:** PYQ-style (SSC CHSL pattern)

**Q10.** Scarcely he had **entered the room when** the phone rang.
(a) he had entered the room than
(b) had he entered the room when
(c) he entered the room when
(d) No improvement required

**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** When "scarcely/hardly" begins a sentence, inversion is required: "Scarcely had he entered…" Also, "scarcely/hardly" pairs with "when" (never "than").
**Trap type:** Sound-Right + Over-thinking
**Source:** PYQ-style (SSC CGL Tier-II pattern)

## Applying the 15/45-Second Rule

**Q5 (Each of the students have submitted…) — 15-second question.**
This is Fact-based: a single memorised rule ("each of → singular verb") solves it. Trained candidates should lock the answer in under 15 seconds without reading all options fully. If you spend more than 20 seconds here, you are losing time you will need elsewhere.

**Q4 (Walking through the park, the flowers…) — 45-second question.**
This is Inference-based: you must identify the dangling modifier, then check *each* option to confirm which one actually fixes the logic (option (b) is a deliberate near-miss that keeps the error). Budget the full 45 seconds: read the stem, name the error type, then eliminate options one by one. Never rush modifier questions — they are designed to punish fast readers.

**Rule of thumb:** Agreement, tense, and fixed-idiom items = 15 seconds. Modifiers, parallelism, subjunctive, and "No improvement required" items = 45 seconds, because you must verify all four options before committing.
