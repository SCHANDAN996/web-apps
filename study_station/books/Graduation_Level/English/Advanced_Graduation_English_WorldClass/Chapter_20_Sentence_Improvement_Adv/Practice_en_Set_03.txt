51. Choose the correct improvement: "He is working here since 2015."
(a) He is working here from 2015. (b) No improvement (c) He has been working here since 2015. (d) He was working here since 2015.
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Since 2015" with an action continuing up to the present requires the present perfect continuous tense, so "has been working" is correct. The simple present cannot be used with "since" for a continuing action.

52. Choose the correct improvement: "One of my friend lives in Delhi."
(a) One of my friends live in Delhi. (b) One of my friend live in Delhi. (c) One of my friends lives in Delhi. (d) No improvement
Answer: (c)
Solution: After "one of", the noun must be plural ("friends") but the verb stays singular ("lives") because the subject is "one". This is a classic examiner trap testing both parts together.
Source: NCERT Class 9 English Grammar

53. Choose the correct improvement: "She is more taller than her sister."
(a) She is taller than her sister. (b) No improvement (c) She is most taller than her sister. (d) She is much more taller than her sister.
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Taller" is already comparative; adding "more" creates a double comparative, which is wrong. Use either "taller" or "more tall" (the latter is not standard here).
Source: PYQ-style

54. Choose the correct improvement: "I and Ravi went to the market."
(a) No improvement (b) Me and Ravi went to the market. (c) Ravi and me went to the market. (d) Ravi and I went to the market.
Answer: (d)
Solution: As subjects, the pronoun must be "I", and polite usage places the other person first: "Ravi and I". "Me" is an object pronoun and cannot be a subject.
Source: NCERT Class 8 English Grammar

55. Choose the correct improvement: "I am having two brothers."
(a) I am having two brother. (b) No improvement (c) I have two brothers. (d) I have been having two brothers.
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Have" meaning "possess" is a stative verb and is not used in the continuous form, so "I have two brothers" is correct. The continuous form is allowed only when "have" means eating or experiencing, as in "I am having lunch".
Source: NCERT Class 9 English Grammar

56. Choose the correct improvement: "The news are very shocking."
(a) The news were very shocking. (b) The news is very shocking. (c) The news have been very shocking. (d) No improvement
Answer: (b)
Solution: "News" looks plural but is an uncountable noun and always takes a singular verb. This is a frequent sound-right trap because of the final "s".
Source: NCERT Class 10 English Grammar

57. Choose the correct improvement: "Unless you do not work hard, you will fail."
(a) Unless you work hard, you will fail. (b) Until you do not work hard, you will fail. (c) Unless you will not work hard, you will fail. (d) No improvement
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Unless" already carries a negative meaning ("if not"), so adding "do not" makes a double negative. Remove "not" after "unless".

58. Choose the correct improvement: "He is senior than me by two years."
(a) He is senior to me by two years. (b) He is senior from me by two years. (c) He is more senior than me by two years. (d) No improvement
Answer: (a)
Solution: Latin comparatives like senior, junior, superior, inferior and prefer take "to", not "than". They also never take "more".
Source: NCERT Class 10 English Grammar

59. Choose the correct improvement: "I have seen him yesterday."
(a) I have seen him the day before. (b) I saw him yesterday. (c) I had seen him yesterday. (d) No improvement
Answer: (b)
Solution: With a definite past time marker like "yesterday", use the simple past, not the present perfect. The present perfect is used only with indefinite time.

60. Choose the correct improvement: "Each of the boys have done his work."
(a) Each of the boys have done their work. (b) No improvement (c) Each of the boys has done their works. (d) Each of the boys has done his work.
Answer: (d)
Solution: "Each of" takes a plural noun but a singular verb and singular pronoun: "has done his work". The plural "boys" in between is an intervening-phrase trap.

61. Choose the correct improvement: "The teacher, along with the students, were present in the hall."
(a) No improvement (b) The teacher, along with the students, have been present in the hall. (c) The teacher, along with the students, are present in the hall. (d) The teacher, along with the students, was present in the hall.
Answer: (d)
Solution: With "along with", the verb agrees with the first subject, "the teacher", which is singular. The phrase after it is an intervening phrase and must be ignored — a classic examiner trap.

62. Choose the correct improvement: "No sooner did he reach the station when the train left."
(a) No sooner did he reach the station than the train left. (b) No sooner did he reach the station then the train left. (c) No sooner he reached the station than the train left. (d) No improvement
Answer: (a)
Solution: "No sooner" is always paired with "than", never "when" or "then". The inversion "did he reach" is already correct.

63. Choose the correct improvement: "He is one of the best players that has ever played for India."
(a) No improvement (b) He is one of the best player that has ever played for India. (c) He is one of the best players that have ever played for India. (d) He is one of the best players who has ever played for India.
Answer: (c)
Solution: In "one of the + plural noun + relative clause", the verb in the relative clause agrees with the plural noun ("players"), so "have" is correct. The singular "one" misleads many candidates.

64. Choose the correct improvement: "Scarcely he had entered the room when the phone rang."
(a) Scarcely he had entered the room than the phone rang. (b) No improvement (c) Scarcely did he entered the room when the phone rang. (d) Scarcely had he entered the room when the phone rang.
Answer: (d)
Solution: When a negative adverb like "scarcely" begins a sentence, inversion is required: "Scarcely had he entered". "Scarcely" pairs with "when", which is already correct.
Source: PYQ-style

65. Choose the correct improvement: "The committee has taken their decision unanimously."
(a) No improvement (b) The committee has taken its decision unanimously. (c) The committee has took its decision unanimously. (d) The committee have taken their decision unanimously.
Answer: (b)
Solution: When a collective noun acts as a single unit (shown by "unanimously"), it takes singular pronouns: "its". "Their" would suggest divided members, contradicting "unanimously" — a collective-noun trap.
Source: PYQ-style

66. Choose the correct improvement: "He insisted to go with me."
(a) He insisted in going with me. (b) He insisted for going with me. (c) He insisted on going with me. (d) No improvement
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Insist" is followed by the preposition "on" plus a gerund: "insisted on going". It never takes a bare infinitive.

67. Choose the correct improvement: "Neither of the two boys have passed the examination."
(a) Neither of the two boy has passed the examination. (b) Neither of the two boys has passed the examination. (c) Neither of the two boys have been passed the examination. (d) No improvement
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Neither of" takes a plural noun but a singular verb: "has passed". The plural "boys" sitting next to the verb is an intervening-phrase trap.

68. Choose the correct improvement: "I look forward to meet you soon."
(a) I look forward to meeting with you soon. (b) I look forward to meeting you soon. (c) I look forward for meeting you soon. (d) No improvement
Answer: (b)
Solution: In "look forward to", the word "to" is a preposition, not part of an infinitive, so it must be followed by a gerund: "meeting". This is a very common sound-right trap.

69. Choose the correct improvement: "The Ganges is one of the longest river in India."
(a) The Ganges is one of the longest rivers in India. (b) The Ganges is one of the most longest rivers in India. (c) The Ganges is one of longest river in India. (d) No improvement
Answer: (a)
Solution: After "one of the + superlative", the noun must be plural: "longest rivers". The superlative "longest" never takes "most".
Source: NCERT Class 9 English Grammar

70. Choose the correct improvement: "Hardly had I reached home than it started raining."
(a) Hardly I had reached home when it started raining. (b) Hardly had I reached home when it started raining. (c) Hardly had I reached home then it started raining. (d) No improvement
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Hardly" pairs with "when", not "than" ("than" belongs with "no sooner"). The inversion "had I reached" is already correct.

71. Choose the correct improvement: "He as well as his brothers were invited to the party."
(a) He as well as his brothers was invited to the party. (b) He as well as his brothers have been invited to the party. (c) He as well as his brothers are invited to the party. (d) No improvement
Answer: (a)
Solution: With "as well as", the verb agrees with the first subject, "He", so the singular "was" is needed. The nearby plural "brothers" is the intervening-phrase trap the examiner sets.
Source: PYQ-style

72. Choose the correct improvement: "Being a rainy day, I stayed at home."
(a) Being a rainy day, staying at home was done by me. (b) No improvement (c) As being a rainy day, I stayed at home. (d) It being a rainy day, I stayed at home.
Answer: (d)
Solution: "Being a rainy day" cannot describe "I" — a dangling modifier. Adding the dummy subject "It" fixes the logic: "It being a rainy day, I stayed at home."
Source: PYQ-style

73. Choose the correct improvement: "The principal and secretary have arrived for the meeting."
(a) The principal and secretary has arrived for the meeting. (b) The principal and the secretary has arrived for the meeting. (c) The principal and secretary is arriving for the meeting. (d) No improvement
Answer: (a)
Solution: One article before two joined nouns means one person holding both posts, so the verb must be singular: "has arrived". Repeating "the" would mean two people — an article trap.
Source: PYQ-style

74. Choose the correct improvement: "He told me that he will come the next day."
(a) No improvement (b) He told me that he will be coming the next day. (c) He told me that he shall come the next day. (d) He told me that he would come the next day.
Answer: (d)
Solution: In reported speech with a past reporting verb ("told"), "will" backshifts to "would". "Shall" is wrong for a third-person subject here.
Source: NCERT Class 10 English Grammar

75. Choose the correct improvement: "Many a student have failed in this examination."
(a) Many a students have failed in this examination. (b) Many a student have been failed in this examination. (c) Many a student has failed in this examination. (d) No improvement
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Many a" is followed by a singular noun and a singular verb: "Many a student has failed". The plural meaning is a sound-right trap; the grammar is strictly singular.
