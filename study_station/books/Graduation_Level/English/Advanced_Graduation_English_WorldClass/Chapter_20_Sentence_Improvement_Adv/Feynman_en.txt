# 🧒 Feynman Technique — Sentence Improvement (Advanced)

## The Hardest Concept, Explained to a 12-Year-Old: **The Intervening Phrase Trap**

Imagine you are at a birthday party. Your best friend Rohan is standing right next to you. Between you and Rohan, ten other kids squeeze in — your cousins, neighbours, the magician's assistant, everyone. Now someone asks: *"Who is cutting the cake?"*

The answer is still **Rohan** — one boy. The ten kids in between don't change that. But if you just look at the crowd, your eyes get confused and you might say "the kids are cutting the cake."

**That is exactly what examiners do to you in Sentence Improvement questions.**

Look at this sentence:

> *The quality of the mangoes **were** not good.*

Your ear hears "mangoes were" — and it *sounds* right. That's the trap! But cover the middle part with your finger:

> *The quality ………… **were** not good.* ❌

Now it's obvious: **quality** is the real subject, and quality is singular. The correct sentence is:

> *The quality of the mangoes **was** not good.* ✅

The phrase *"of the mangoes"* is just the crowd of kids standing between you and Rohan. It decorates the sentence but never controls the verb.

---

## 🎯 The Unforgettable Trick: **"Cover the Middle"**

Whenever a sentence feels long and confusing:

1. **Find the verb** (was/were, is/are, has/have).
2. **Ask "who or what is doing it?"**
3. **Cover everything between the subject and the verb with your finger.**
4. Read the shortened sentence aloud. Your ear will now catch the error instantly.

**Practice it:**
- *The box of chocolates (is/are) on the table.* → Cover "of chocolates" → *The box **is** on the table.* ✅
- *The players, along with the coach, (was/were) felicitated.* → Cover the middle → *The players **were** felicitated.* ✅

**Memory hook:** *"Phrases in between are just noise — the subject alone makes the choice."*

---

## Why This Matters at the Advanced Level

At SSC CGL / IBPS PO level, examiners stretch the intervening phrase longer and longer, hoping you'll match the verb with the nearest noun instead of the true subject. Watch this one:

> *The committee, comprising senior officers from five departments and headed by the retired judge, **has** submitted its report.* ✅

The real subject is **committee** — singular — so the verb is **has** and the pronoun is **its**. The trap is that your eye lands on *officers* (plural) or *judge* (singular) right before the verb, tempting you to write *have* or to lose track of the subject entirely. Cover the middle — *"The committee … has submitted its report"* — and the answer is instant. The "Cover the Middle" trick defeats every version of this trap in under 15 seconds.
