76. Choose the option that best improves the underlined part: "The committee have decided to postpone the meeting until next week."
(a) have decided to postponed (b) No improvement (c) have deciding to postpone (d) has decided to postpone
Answer: (d)
Solution: "Committee" is a collective noun treated as singular when it acts as one body, so it takes "has". The infinitive "to postpone" is already correct.

77. Choose the option that best improves the underlined part: "Scarcely he had entered the room when the phone rang."
(a) No improvement (b) Scarcely had he entered (c) Scarcely he entered (d) Scarcely he has entered
Answer: (b)
Solution: Negative adverbs like "scarcely" at the start of a sentence require inversion: auxiliary before subject — "Scarcely had he entered".

79. Choose the option that best improves the underlined part: "He is one of the best players that has ever played for the country."
(a) which has ever played (b) that have ever played (c) who has ever played (d) No improvement
Answer: (b)
Solution: In "one of the + plural noun + that/who", the relative pronoun refers to the plural noun ("players"), so the verb must be plural: "have played". This is a classic examiner trap.

80. Choose the option that best improves the underlined part: "The teacher, along with his students, were present at the seminar."
(a) were presented (b) No improvement (c) was present (d) have been present
Answer: (c)
Solution: With "along with", the verb agrees with the first subject ("the teacher"), which is singular: "was present". The intervening phrase is a trap.

83. Choose the option that best improves the underlined part: "He denied to help me in my difficulty."

Solution: "Deny" cannot be followed by an infinitive; the natural correction expressing the intended meaning is "refused to help", since "refuse" takes the infinitive. The other options use wrong prepositions after "denied".

95. Choose the option that best improves the underlined part: "Not only he was late but also he did not apologise."
(a) Not only was he late but also he did not apologised (b) Not only he was late but also he did not apologised (c) He was not only late but also did not apologise (d) No improvement
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Not only... but also" must join parallel elements; placing it after the subject keeps the structure parallel: "not only late but also did not apologise". The other options break the verb form with "apologised" after "did not".

96. Choose the option that best improves the underlined part: "The number of accidents on this road are increasing every year."

Answer: (a)
Solution: "The number of" takes a singular verb; "a number of" takes a plural verb. Hence "is increasing". This sound-right trap fools many candidates.
