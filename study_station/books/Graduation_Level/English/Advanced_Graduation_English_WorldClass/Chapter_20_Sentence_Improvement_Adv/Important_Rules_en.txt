# Important Rules — English Grammar for Government Exams

These 12 rules cover the grammar points that SSC, Railway, Bank and State exams test most often. Learn each rule with its example and the trap examiners set.

---

## Rule 1: Subject–Verb Agreement

The verb agrees with the **subject**, not with the word nearest to it.

- ✅ The quality of the mangoes **was** good. (subject = quality, singular)
- ❌ The quality of the mangoes were good.

**Trap:** Phrases like *along with, as well as, together with, in addition to* do NOT make the subject plural.
- The captain, along with his players, **was** present.

---

## Rule 2: Two Subjects Joined by "and"

Two subjects joined by *and* take a **plural** verb — unless they refer to one person/thing.

- Rice and curry **are** ready.
- The poet and philosopher **is** dead. (one person — no second "the")
- The poet and the philosopher **are** dead. (two persons)

---

## Rule 3: Each, Every, Everyone, Everybody, Someone, Nobody

These words are always **singular**.

- Each of the boys **has** a pen.
- Everyone **is** present.

**Trap:** "Each of / Every one of + plural noun" still takes a singular verb.

---

## Rule 4: Neither…nor / Either…or / Not only…but also

The verb agrees with the subject **nearest** to it.

- Neither the teacher nor the students **were** present.
- Neither the students nor the teacher **was** present.

---

## Rule 5: Uncountable Nouns

*News, information, furniture, luggage, baggage, advice, scenery, machinery, stationery, crockery, poetry, work* are **uncountable** — no plural form, singular verb, no "a/an".

- ✅ He gave me some **advice**. / The news **is** good.
- ❌ He gave me an advice. / The furnitures are new.

---

## Rule 6: Nouns That Look Plural but Are Singular

*Mathematics, physics, economics, politics, ethics, innings, gallows, mumps, measles* take a **singular** verb.

- Mathematics **is** a difficult subject.
- The first innings **was** very exciting.

---

## Rule 7: Nouns Always Plural

*Scissors, trousers, spectacles, goggles, tongs, pliers, premises, wages, goods, thanks, proceeds, outskirts, alms* take a **plural** verb.

- The scissors **are** on the table.
- His wages **were** paid weekly.

---

## Rule 8: Collective Nouns

*Team, committee, jury, army, audience, family, government* — **singular** when acting as one unit, **plural** when members act separately.

- The jury **has** given its verdict. (one decision)
- The jury **are** divided in their opinion. (members disagree)

---

## Rule 9: One of the + Plural Noun + Singular Verb

- One of my friends **has** gone to Delhi.
- One of the boys **was** absent.

**Trap:** In "One of the boys who **were** absent…", the verb after *who* agrees with *boys* (plural), because *who* refers to the boys, not to "one".

---

## Rule 10: Tense in Conditional Sentences

- **If + present simple, … will + V1** → If it rains, I will stay home.
- **If + past simple, … would + V1** → If I were a bird, I would fly. (use *were*, not *was*, for all persons in wishes/unreal conditions)
- **If + past perfect, … would have + V3** → If he had worked hard, he would have passed.

**Trap:** Never use *will/would* in the if-clause itself: ❌ If it will rain…

---

## Rule 11: Parallel Structure

Words joined by *and, or, but, not only…but also* must be in the **same grammatical form**.

- ✅ She likes reading, writing and painting.
- ❌ She likes reading, writing and to paint.

---

## Rule 12: Articles — A / An / The

- **A** before consonant *sound*: a university, a European, a one-rupee note.
- **An** before vowel *sound*: an hour, an honest man, an MLA, an heir.
- **The** for unique things (the sun, the Ganga), superlatives (the best), and things already mentioned.

**Trap:** Sound matters, not spelling — "an hour" (h is silent), "a university" (yoo- sound).

---

## How to Use These Rules in the Exam

- **15/45 second rule:** If a grammar question matches a rule you know cold, answer in ~15 seconds. If it needs analysis, give it up to 45 seconds, then mark and move on.
- Examiners' favourite traps: a long phrase between subject and verb (Rule 1), *each of/every one of* (Rule 3), uncountable nouns made plural (Rule 5), and *were* vs *was* in conditionals (Rule 10).
- In error-spotting questions, check these 12 rules in order — most errors come from Rules 1, 3, 5, 9 and 10.

Source: Standard high-school English grammar (NCERT level) and PYQ-style pattern analysis.
