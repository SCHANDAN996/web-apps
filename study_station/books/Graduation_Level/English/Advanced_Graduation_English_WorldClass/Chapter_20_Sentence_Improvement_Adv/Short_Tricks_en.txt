# Short Tricks — Subject-Verb Agreement

Quick rules and memory aids for spotting the correct verb in exam questions (SSC, Bank, Railway, State exams).

---

## Trick 1 — Find the real subject, ignore the middle

Words between the subject and the verb do **not** change the verb.

- The quality **of the apples** *was* good. (subject = quality, singular)
- The players, **as well as the captain**, *were* present. (subject = players, plural)

**Exam trap:** Phrases like *along with, as well as, together with, in addition to, besides, accompanied by* — the verb agrees only with the first subject.

---

## Trick 2 — "Each / Every / Everyone / Everybody / Someone / Nobody" → Singular

- Each of the boys *has* a pen.
- Everybody *knows* the answer.

**Memory hook:** "Each one is one" → singular verb.

---

## Trick 3 — "Either…or / Neither…nor" → verb agrees with the **nearer** subject

- Neither the teacher nor the students *were* there.
- Neither the students nor the teacher *was* there.

**Memory hook:** The verb listens to whoever stands closest.

---

## Trick 4 — "Many a / Many an" → Singular (despite the meaning)

- Many a soldier *has* died for the country.

**Trap:** Meaning is plural, grammar is singular.

---

## Trick 5 — "A number of" vs "The number of"

- **A number of** students *were* absent. (= many → plural)
- **The number of** students *was* fifty. (= the figure → singular)

**Memory hook:** "A number = many; The number = one figure."

---

## Trick 6 — Uncountable-looking traps

- **News, physics, mathematics, politics, measles, billiards** → singular.
  - The news *is* good.
- **Scissors, trousers, spectacles, goods, wages, premises** → plural.
  - The scissors *are* on the table.

---

## Trick 7 — Collective nouns

- Team, committee, jury, family, government → **singular** when acting as one unit; **plural** when members act separately.
  - The jury *has* given its verdict. (one decision)
  - The jury *are* divided in their opinion. (members disagree)

---

## Trick 8 — "One of the + plural noun" → Singular verb

- One of my friends *has* gone to Delhi.

**Trap:** The plural noun ("friends") tempts a plural verb — the subject is "one".

---

## Trick 9 — Relative pronoun (who/which/that) — verb agrees with its **antecedent**

- It is I who *am* responsible.
- It is you who *are* responsible.
- He is one of those boys who *have* passed. (who → boys)

---

## Trick 10 — Amounts, distances, time as one unit → Singular

- Five hundred rupees *is* a big amount for him.
- Ten kilometres *is* a long walk.
- Two years *is* a long time.

---

## Trick 11 — "There" sentences — verb agrees with the subject **after** it

- There *is* a book on the table.
- There *are* three books on the table.

---

## Trick 12 — "None" and "Some/Most/Half of"

- With uncountable noun → singular: Some of the milk *was* spilt.
- With plural noun → plural: Some of the boys *were* absent.
- None of the information *is* correct. / None of the students *were* present.

---

## 15-Second Exam Routine

1. Underline the subject — skip everything between commas/prepositional phrases.
2. Check for trigger words: *each, every, either/neither, many a, a number of, one of*.
3. Match the verb to the real subject.
4. If two subjects are joined by *and* → plural, unless they name one thing (e.g., "Bread and butter *is* my breakfast").
