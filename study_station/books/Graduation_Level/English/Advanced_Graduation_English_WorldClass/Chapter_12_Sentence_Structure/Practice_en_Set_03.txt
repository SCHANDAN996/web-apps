51. Choose the grammatically correct sentence.
(a) She don't like coffee. (b) She doesn't likes coffee. (c) She not like coffee. (d) She doesn't like coffee.
Answer: (d)
Solution: With a third-person singular subject, the auxiliary is "doesn't" and the main verb stays in base form: "doesn't like". Option (a) uses the wrong auxiliary and option (b) wrongly adds -s after "doesn't".
Source: NCERT Class 9 English Grammar

52. Identify the correctly framed question.
(a) Where are you going? (b) Where you are going? (c) Where going you are? (d) You are going where?
Answer: (a)
Solution: In wh-questions the auxiliary comes before the subject: "Where are you going?" Option (b) copies Hindi word order ("तुम कहाँ जा रहे हो?") and drops the inversion.
Source: PYQ-style

53. Choose the correct sentence.
(a) The news are shocking. (b) The news have shocked. (c) The news were shocking. (d) The news is shocking.
Answer: (d)
Solution: "News" looks plural but is an uncountable noun and takes a singular verb: "The news is shocking."
Source: NCERT Class 10 English Grammar

54. Spot the error: "One of my friend (P) / has invited me (Q) / to his birthday party. (R) / No error (S)"
(a) Part R (b) Part Q (c) Part P (d) No error
Answer: (c)
Solution: After "one of", the noun must be plural: "one of my friends". Part Q is correct because the verb agrees with "one" (singular).

55. Choose the correct sentence.
(a) I am agree with you. (b) I agree with you. (c) I am agreeing with you always. (d) I agreed with you always.
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Agree" is a stative verb; it does not need "am" and is not normally used in the continuous form. "I agree with you" is correct.
Source: NCERT Class 9 English Grammar

56. Choose the correct sentence.
(a) He is more taller than his brother. (b) He is taller than his brother. (c) He is most taller than his brother. (d) He is tall than his brother.
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Taller" is already comparative; adding "more" (option a) makes a double comparative, which is wrong.
Source: NCERT Class 8 English Grammar

57. Choose the correct sentence.
(a) Neither of the answers is correct. (b) Neither of the answers are correct. (c) Neither of the answers were correct. (d) Neither of the answers have been correct.
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Neither" is singular, so the verb must be "is" even though the plural noun "answers" sits next to the verb. (Examiner's trap: intervening phrase.)

58. Choose the correct sentence.
(a) She is good at mathematics. (b) She is good in mathematics. (c) She is good on mathematics. (d) She is good with mathematics.
Answer: (a)
Solution: The fixed preposition with "good" for skill is "at": "good at mathematics".

59. Spot the error: "The teacher, as well as the students, (P) / were present (Q) / in the assembly. (R) / No error (S)"
(a) Part P (b) Part R (c) Part Q (d) No error
Answer: (c)
Solution: With "as well as", the verb agrees with the first subject ("the teacher", singular), so it should be "was present". The plural noun just before the verb is the trap.

60. Choose the correct sentence.
(a) I have seen him yesterday. (b) I saw him yesterday. (c) I had seen him yesterday. (d) I see him yesterday.
Answer: (b)
Solution: A definite past time marker like "yesterday" takes the simple past, not the present perfect: "I saw him yesterday."

61. Choose the correct sentence.
(a) The quality of the mangoes were poor. (b) The quality of the mangoes are poor. (c) The quality of the mangoes was poor. (d) The quality of the mangoes have been poor.
Answer: (c)
Solution: The subject is "quality" (singular), not "mangoes"; the phrase "of the mangoes" only intervenes. Hence "was". (Examiner's trap: intervening phrase.)

62. Spot the error: "Each of the boys (P) / have submitted (Q) / his assignment on time. (R) / No error (S)"
(a) Part P (b) Part R (c) Part Q (d) No error
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Each" is singular, so the verb must be "has submitted". The plural "boys" next to the verb misleads many candidates.

63. Choose the correct sentence.
(a) Hardly I had reached the station when the train left. (b) Hardly I reached the station when the train had left. (c) Hardly had I reached the station when the train left. (d) Hardly had I reached the station than the train left.
Answer: (c)
Solution: When "hardly" begins a sentence, inversion is required ("Hardly had I..."), and it pairs with "when", not "than".
Source: NCERT Class 11 English Grammar

64. Choose the correct sentence.
(a) No sooner did she enter the room when the phone rang. (b) No sooner did she enter the room than the phone rang. (c) No sooner she entered the room than the phone rang. (d) No sooner had she entered the room when the phone rang.
Answer: (b)
Solution: "No sooner" takes inversion with "did/had" and is always followed by "than", never "when".

65. Choose the correct sentence.
(a) The committee have taken its decision. (b) The committee has taken its decision. (c) The committee has taken their decision yesterday itself. (d) The committee are taking its decision now.
Answer: (b)
Solution: When a collective noun acts as one unit, it takes a singular verb and singular pronoun: "has taken its decision".

66. Spot the error: "Scarcely he had stepped out (P) / of the house (Q) / when it began to rain. (R) / No error (S)"

Answer: (b)
Solution: A negative adverb at the start needs inversion: "Scarcely had he stepped out". Part P wrongly keeps the normal order.

67. Choose the correct sentence.

Solution: With "as well as", the verb agrees with the first subject "He" (singular): "was invited". The nearby plural "friends" is the trap.

68. Choose the correct sentence.
(a) Unless you do not work hard, you will fail. (b) Unless you work hard, you will fail. (c) Unless you will not work hard, you fail. (d) Unless you worked hard, you will fail.
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Unless" already carries a negative meaning, so adding "not" (option a) makes a double negative. The correct form is "Unless you work hard".

69. Spot the error: "The number of students (P) / attending the seminar (Q) / are increasing every year. (R) / No error (S)"
(a) Part P (b) Part R (c) Part Q (d) No error
Answer: (b)
Solution: "The number of" takes a singular verb: "is increasing". ("A number of" would take a plural verb — a classic examiner contrast.)

70. Choose the correct sentence.
(a) Bread and butter is my favourite breakfast. (b) Bread and butter are my favourite breakfast. (c) Bread and butter were my favourite breakfast. (d) Bread and butter have been my favourite breakfast.
Answer: (a)
Solution: When two nouns joined by "and" denote one single dish/idea, the verb is singular: "Bread and butter is...".

71. Choose the correct sentence.
(a) Not only he passed the exam but also topped it. (b) Not only passed he the exam but also topped it. (c) Not only he did pass the exam but also topped it. (d) Not only did he pass the exam but also topped it.
Answer: (d)
Solution: When "not only" begins the clause, inversion follows: "Not only did he pass...". Option (a) keeps normal order and is wrong.

Answer: (c)

73. Choose the correct sentence.

Answer: (d)
Solution: After "It is high time", the past simple is used to express urgency about the present: "we started".

(a) Part Q (b) Part P (c) Part R (d) No error
Answer: (a)
Solution: With "neither...nor", the verb agrees with the nearer subject ("employees", plural): "were willing". Proximity rule applies.
