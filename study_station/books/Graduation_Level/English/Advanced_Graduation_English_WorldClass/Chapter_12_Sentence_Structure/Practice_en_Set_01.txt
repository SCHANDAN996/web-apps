Questions 1–20: Easy | Questions 21–25: Medium

1. A complete sentence must contain at least one:
(a) adjective (b) preposition (c) independent clause (d) conjunction
Answer: (c)
Solution: A sentence needs at least one independent clause — a subject plus a finite verb that expresses a complete thought. Adjectives, prepositions, and conjunctions alone cannot form a sentence.
Source: NCERT Class 9 English Grammar

2. Identify the subject in the sentence: "The tall boy in the red shirt won the race."
(a) shirt (b) boy (c) race (d) red
Answer: (b)
Solution: The subject is "boy"; "the tall" and "in the red shirt" are modifiers. The verb "won" agrees with "boy", not with the nearer noun "shirt".
Source: NCERT Class 10 English Grammar

3. Which of the following is a simple sentence?
(a) She sings and she dances. (b) Although tired, he kept working. (c) I know that he is honest. (d) The sun set behind the hills.
Answer: (d)
Solution: A simple sentence has one independent clause with one finite verb. "The sun set behind the hills" has a single subject-verb pair; the others contain two clauses.
Source: NCERT Class 9 English Grammar

4. A compound sentence is joined by:
(a) a subordinating conjunction (b) a coordinating conjunction (c) a relative pronoun (d) a preposition
Answer: (b)
Solution: Compound sentences join two independent clauses with coordinating conjunctions — for, and, nor, but, or, yet, so (FANBOYS). Subordinating conjunctions create complex sentences.
Source: NCERT Class 10 English Grammar

5. Which sentence is complex?
(a) He ran fast, but he missed the bus. (b) Open the window. (c) Because it rained, the match was cancelled. (d) She is a doctor and a writer.
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Because it rained" is a dependent clause joined to the independent clause "the match was cancelled" — that makes it complex. Option (a) is compound; (b) and (d) are simple.
Source: NCERT Class 10 English Grammar

6. Choose the correct sentence:
(a) What are you doing? (b) What you are doing? (c) What doing you are? (d) What doing are you?
Answer: (a)
Solution: In direct questions the auxiliary verb comes before the subject: "What are you doing?" Hindi word order ("आप क्या कर रहे हैं?") tempts learners into "What you are doing?", which is wrong.
Source: PYQ-style

7. Identify the predicate in: "My elder brother works in a bank."
(a) My elder brother (b) My elder (c) in a bank (d) works in a bank
Answer: (d)
Solution: The predicate is everything said about the subject — the verb and its complements. Here the subject is "My elder brother" and the predicate is "works in a bank".
Source: NCERT Class 9 English Grammar

8. Which of these is a phrase, not a clause?
(a) she sings well (b) in the morning (c) he came home (d) they were late
Answer: (b)
Solution: A phrase has no subject-verb pair. "In the morning" is a prepositional phrase; the other three each contain a subject and a finite verb, so they are clauses.
Source: NCERT Class 9 English Grammar

9. An imperative sentence:
(a) gives a command or request (b) expresses strong emotion (c) asks a question (d) states a fact
Answer: (a)
Solution: Imperative sentences order, request, or advise — "Sit down", "Please help me". The subject "you" is usually understood, not stated.
Source: NCERT Class 8 English Grammar

10. Choose the correctly punctuated compound sentence:
(a) I was tired, so I went to bed early. (b) I was tired so, I went to bed early. (c) I was tired so I went, to bed early. (d) I was, tired so I went to bed early.
Answer: (a)
Solution: When two independent clauses are joined by a coordinating conjunction, a comma comes before the conjunction: "tired, so". The other placements break the clauses incorrectly.
Source: NCERT Class 10 English Grammar

11. Spot the error: "One of my friend (P) / has gone (Q) / to Delhi. (R)"
(a) Part R (b) Part Q (c) Part P (d) No error
Answer: (c)
Solution: Part P is wrong — "one of" is always followed by a plural noun: "one of my friends". The verb stays singular ("has") because the real subject is "one". This is a classic examiner trap.
Source: PYQ-style

12. Which sentence is declarative?
(a) Where are you going? (b) What a beautiful day! (c) The train leaves at six. (d) Close the door.
Answer: (c)
Solution: A declarative sentence makes a statement and ends with a full stop. Option (a) is interrogative, (b) exclamatory, and (d) imperative.
Source: NCERT Class 8 English Grammar

13. The clause "which I bought yesterday" in "The book which I bought yesterday is lost" is:
(a) a noun clause (b) a main clause (c) an adverb clause (d) an adjective (relative) clause
Answer: (d)
Solution: It begins with the relative pronoun "which" and describes the noun "book", so it is an adjective (relative) clause. It cannot stand alone as a sentence.
Source: NCERT Class 10 English Grammar

14. Choose the correct transformation: "He is too weak to walk." (Remove "too")
(a) He is very weak to walk. (b) He is so weak that he cannot walk. (c) He is weak so he walks. (d) He is too weak that he cannot walk.
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Too … to" converts to "so … that … cannot". Option (d) wrongly mixes both patterns; (a) and (c) change the meaning.
Source: NCERT Class 10 English Grammar

15. Spot the error: "The quality of the apples (P) / were (Q) / not good. (R)"
(a) Part P (b) Part Q (c) Part R (d) No error
Answer: (b)
Solution: Part Q is wrong — the subject is "quality" (singular), not "apples", so the verb must be "was". The intervening phrase "of the apples" is the examiner's trap.
Source: PYQ-style

16. Which of the following is an exclamatory sentence?
(a) How clever you are! (b) Are you clever? (c) You are clever. (d) Be clever.
Answer: (a)
Solution: Exclamatory sentences express sudden emotion and end with an exclamation mark, often beginning with "How" or "What". The others are interrogative, declarative, and imperative.
Source: NCERT Class 8 English Grammar

17. "Unless you work hard, you will fail." The underlined clause "Unless you work hard" is:
(a) an adverb clause of condition (b) a noun clause (c) an adjective clause (d) a principal clause
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Unless" introduces a condition, and the clause modifies the verb "will fail", so it is an adverb clause of condition. "You will fail" is the principal clause.
Source: NCERT Class 10 English Grammar

18. Choose the correct sentence:
(a) He is more taller than his brother. (b) He is taller than his brother. (c) He is most taller than his brother. (d) He is much more taller than his brother.
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Taller" is already comparative; adding "more" or "most" makes a double comparative, which is wrong. "Much taller" would be fine, but "much more taller" is not.
Source: NCERT Class 9 English Grammar

19. Identify the type: "Although she was ill, she attended the meeting, and she presented the report."
(a) compound-complex (b) compound (c) complex (d) simple
Answer: (a)
Solution: It has two independent clauses ("she attended…", "she presented…") joined by "and", plus a dependent clause ("Although she was ill") — the definition of a compound-complex sentence.
Source: NCERT Class 12 English Grammar

20. Choose the correct question tag: "She hardly ever comes late, ___?"
(a) doesn't she (b) didn't she (c) isn't she (d) does she
Answer: (d)
Solution: "Hardly" is negative in meaning, so the statement counts as negative and the tag must be positive: "does she". Learners who treat "hardly" as positive pick the wrong tag — a favourite trap.
Source: PYQ-style

21. Spot the error: "Neither of the two boys (P) / have done (Q) / his homework. (R)"
(a) Part P (b) Part R (c) Part Q (d) No error
Answer: (c)
Solution: Part Q is wrong — "neither of" takes a singular verb: "has done". The plural noun "boys" in the intervening phrase misleads many candidates.
Source: PYQ-style

22. Choose the correctly framed indirect question:
(a) Do you know where the station is? (b) Do you know where is the station? (c) Do you know where the station? (d) Do you know that where is the station?
Answer: (a)
Solution: Inside an indirect question, statement word order is used — subject before verb: "where the station is". Option (b) keeps direct-question inversion, a very common Hindi-speaker error.
Source: PYQ-style

23. "No sooner did he enter the room ___ the phone rang."
(a) when (b) then (c) than (d) that
Answer: (c)
Solution: "No sooner" always pairs with "than", never "when" or "then". Note also the inversion "did he enter" after the negative opener "no sooner".
Source: NCERT Class 12 English Grammar

24. Identify the sentence with correct subject-verb agreement:
(a) The news are shocking. (b) Mathematics are difficult for him. (c) Bread and butter are his daily breakfast. (d) The committee has taken its decision.
Answer: (d)
Solution: "News" and "Mathematics" are singular despite the -s ending, so (a) and (b) need "is". "Bread and butter" as one combined dish takes a singular verb. The collective noun "committee" acting as one body correctly takes "has".
Source: NCERT Class 10 English Grammar

25. Choose the best combination of: "He was tired. He continued working."
(a) Being tired, but he continued working. (b) Despite tired, he continued working. (c) He was tired, therefore he continued working. (d) Although he was tired, he continued working.
Answer: (d)
Solution: The relationship is contrast, correctly shown by "although". Option (a) wrongly uses both a participle and "but"; (c) reverses the logic; (b) needs a noun after "despite" ("despite being tired" or "despite his tiredness").
Source: PYQ-style
