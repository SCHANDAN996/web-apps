# Sentence Structure — Practice Set 2 (Questions 26–50)
# Difficulty: 15 Easy (recall) + 10 Medium (application)

26. Which of the following is a simple sentence?
(a) Walking down the road, I met an old friend. (b) She sings, and he dances. (c) Although it rained, we went out. (d) He is poor, but he is honest.
Answer: (a)
Solution: A simple sentence has one independent clause. "Walking down the road" is a participial phrase, not a clause, so the sentence has only one finite verb ("met"). The others have two clauses joined by conjunctions, making them compound or complex.
Source: NCERT Class 9 English Grammar

27. Identify the type of sentence: "Do your work quietly."
(a) Imperative (b) Interrogative (c) Exclamatory (d) Assertive
Answer: (a)
Solution: The sentence gives a command with the implied subject "you", so it is imperative. It does not ask a question or express strong emotion.
Source: NCERT Class 8 English Grammar

28. Which sentence is a complex sentence?
(a) He worked hard and passed the exam. (b) He worked hard; he passed the exam. (c) Because he worked hard, he passed the exam. (d) Working hard, he passed the exam.
Answer: (c)
Solution: A complex sentence has one independent clause and at least one dependent clause. "Because he worked hard" is a dependent clause introduced by a subordinating conjunction.
Source: NCERT Class 9 English Grammar

29. Choose the correctly framed interrogative sentence.
(a) Where are you going? (b) Where you are going? (c) Where going you are? (d) You are going where?
Answer: (a)
Solution: In a wh-question, the auxiliary verb comes before the subject: "Where + are + you + going". Option (b) is a classic Hindi-speaker error ("Aap kahan ja rahe hain?" word order).
Source: PYQ-style

30. "What a beautiful flower it is!" is an example of a/an ______ sentence.
(a) assertive (b) imperative (c) interrogative (d) exclamatory
Answer: (d)
Solution: The sentence expresses strong admiration and ends with an exclamation mark, so it is exclamatory.
Source: NCERT Class 8 English Grammar

31. Which of the following is a compound sentence?
(a) The bell rang, and the students left. (b) When the bell rang, the students left. (c) The bell having rung, the students left. (d) The students left after the bell.
Answer: (a)
Solution: A compound sentence joins two independent clauses with a coordinating conjunction (FANBOYS). Here "and" joins "The bell rang" and "the students left".
Source: NCERT Class 9 English Grammar

32. Identify the subject in: "Into the room walked the tired old man."
(a) the room (b) into (c) walked (d) the tired old man
Answer: (d)
Solution: This is an inverted sentence; the verb "walked" precedes the subject. Ask "who walked?" — the tired old man. "The room" is part of a prepositional phrase and cannot be the subject. (Examiner's trap: inverted word order.)
Source: PYQ-style

33. Which sentence has a transitive verb?
(a) The baby slept peacefully. (b) He runs fast. (c) She wrote a letter. (d) The sun rises in the east.
Answer: (c)
Solution: A transitive verb takes a direct object. "Wrote" takes the object "a letter". The other verbs have no object, so they are intransitive.
Source: NCERT Class 8 English Grammar

34. Choose the correct transformation: "He is too weak to walk." (Remove 'too')
(a) He is too weak that he cannot walk. (b) He is very weak to walk. (c) He is weak so he walks. (d) He is so weak that he cannot walk.
Answer: (d)
Solution: "Too...to" converts to "so...that + cannot/can't". The structure is: so + adjective + that + subject + cannot + verb.
Source: PYQ-style

35. Spot the part containing the error: "One of my friend (P) / has gone (Q) / to Delhi yesterday. (R)"
(a) Part R (b) Part Q (c) Part P (d) No error
Answer: (c)
Solution: Part P is wrong: after "one of" we use a plural noun — "one of my friends". The verb stays singular ("has gone"), which is the examiner's trap that makes Part Q look suspicious.
Source: PYQ-style

36. Which of the following is a declarative (assertive) sentence?
(a) Please close the door. (b) How clever you are! (c) Did you finish your homework? (d) The earth revolves around the sun.
Answer: (d)
Solution: A declarative sentence makes a statement and ends with a full stop. Option (d) states a fact.
Source: NCERT Class 8 English Grammar

37. Identify the clause type in bold: "I know **that he is honest**."
(a) Noun clause (b) Adverb clause (c) Adjective clause (d) Principal clause
Answer: (a)
Solution: The clause acts as the object of the verb "know" — answering "know what?" — so it functions as a noun, making it a noun clause.
Source: NCERT Class 10 English Grammar

38. Choose the correct passive form of: "She teaches us grammar."
(a) Grammar was taught by her. (b) Grammar is taught to us by her. (c) We are teach grammar by her. (d) Grammar has been taught by her.
Answer: (b)
Solution: Present simple active becomes "is/am/are + V3" in passive. "Teaches" → "is taught". The tense must not change, which rules out the past and perfect options.
Source: NCERT Class 9 English Grammar

39. Which sentence is grammatically correct?
(a) He as well as his brothers were present. (b) He as well as his brothers have been present. (c) He as well as his brothers are present. (d) He as well as his brothers was present.
Answer: (d)
Solution: With "as well as", the verb agrees with the first subject ("He"), so a singular verb "was" is correct. (Examiner's trap: the plural noun "brothers" next to the verb tempts a plural verb.)
Source: PYQ-style

40. Identify the type of sentence: "Unless you work hard, you will fail."
(a) Simple (b) Complex (c) Compound (d) Compound-complex
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Unless you work hard" is a dependent adverb clause of condition joined to the independent clause "you will fail" — one main clause plus one subordinate clause makes it complex.
Source: NCERT Class 10 English Grammar

41. Choose the correct indirect speech: He said, "I am busy today."
(a) He said that he is busy today. (b) He said that he was busy that day. (c) He said that he had been busy that day. (d) He said that he was busy today.
Answer: (b)
Solution: In reported speech, present simple shifts to past simple ("am" → "was") and "today" becomes "that day". Both changes must happen together.
Source: NCERT Class 9 English Grammar

42. Spot the part containing the error: "Neither of the two boys (P) / have done (Q) / his homework. (R)"
(a) Part P (b) Part Q (c) Part R (d) No error
Answer: (b)
Solution: Part Q is wrong: "neither of" takes a singular verb — "has done". The plural noun "boys" beside the verb is the examiner's trap.
Source: PYQ-style

43. Which sentence contains an adjective clause?
(a) He left because he was ill. (b) The boy who won the prize is my cousin. (c) I believe that he is innocent. (d) She sings sweetly.
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Who won the prize" describes the noun "boy" and begins with the relative pronoun "who", so it is an adjective (relative) clause.
Source: NCERT Class 10 English Grammar

44. Choose the correct question tag: "Let us go for a walk, ______?"
(a) shan't we (b) will we (c) shall we (d) do we
Answer: (c)
Solution: Sentences beginning with "Let us" take the tag "shall we". This is a fixed rule frequently tested.
Source: PYQ-style

45. Identify the object in: "The teacher gave the students a difficult test."
(a) the students and a difficult test (b) gave (c) The teacher (d) difficult
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Gave" is ditransitive — it takes two objects: "the students" (indirect object) and "a difficult test" (direct object). Both together complete the verb.
Source: NCERT Class 9 English Grammar

46. Which is the correct combination of: "He was tired. He continued working."
(a) Although he was tired, he continued working. (b) Being tired, but he continued working. (c) He was tired, therefore he continued working. (d) Unless he was tired, he continued working.
Answer: (a)
Solution: The two ideas show contrast, so the subordinating conjunction "although" correctly joins them into a complex sentence. Option (b) wrongly uses both a participle and "but".
Source: PYQ-style

47. Spot the part containing the error: "The Ganges (P) / are (Q) / one of the holiest rivers of India. (R)"
(a) Part P (b) Part R (c) Part Q (d) No error
Answer: (c)
Solution: Part Q is wrong: names of rivers like "The Ganges" are singular and take "is". The plural-looking form of the name is the examiner's trap.
Source: PYQ-style

48. Choose the correct sentence structure for a compound-complex sentence.
(a) She smiled and waved. (b) Because he was late, he missed the train. (c) Running fast, he caught the bus. (d) When the rain stopped, we went out, and we played football.
Answer: (d)
Solution: A compound-complex sentence has at least two independent clauses ("we went out" + "we played football") joined by a coordinator, plus at least one dependent clause ("When the rain stopped").
Source: NCERT Class 10 English Grammar

49. Which transformation of "He is the best player in the team" into comparative degree is correct?
(a) He is better than all player in the team. (b) He is better than any other player in the team. (c) He is more better than any player in the team. (d) He is best than any other player in the team.
Answer: (b)
Solution: Superlative → comparative uses "better than any other + singular noun". "More better" is a double comparative error, and "any other" is essential to exclude the subject himself.
Source: PYQ-style

50. Identify the finite verb in: "Hoping to win the match, the captain practised hard every day."
(a) Hoping (b) to win (c) practised (d) hard
Answer: (c)
Solution: A finite verb shows tense and agrees with a subject — here "practised" (past tense). "Hoping" is a present participle and "to win" is an infinitive; both are non-finite.
Source: NCERT Class 10 English Grammar
