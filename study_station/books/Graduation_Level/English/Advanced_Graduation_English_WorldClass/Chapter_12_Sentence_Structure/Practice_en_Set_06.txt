126. Identify the part of the sentence containing an error: "Neither of the two candidates (P) / have submitted (Q) / their documents on time. (R)"
(a) Part Q (b) Part P (c) Part R (d) No error
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Neither of" takes a singular verb, so Part Q should read "has submitted". The plural "have" is a classic sound-right trap because "candidates" sits next to the verb.
Source: PYQ-style

127. Choose the correctly structured sentence.
(a) Scarcely he had entered the room when the phone rang. (b) Scarcely had he entered the room when the phone rang. (c) Scarcely he entered the room when had the phone rung. (d) Scarcely had he entered the room than the phone rang.
Answer: (b)
Solution: After a negative adverb like "scarcely" at the start, inversion is required: "had he entered". "Scarcely" pairs with "when", not "than", so the last option fails on the correlative.
Source: PYQ-style

128. Identify the error: "The committee have (P) / unanimously decided (Q) / to accept the proposal. (R)"
(a) No error (b) Part Q (c) Part R (d) Part P
Answer: (d)
Solution: When a collective noun acts as one body — signalled by "unanimously" — it takes a singular verb: "The committee has decided". The plural "have" would be right only if the members were divided.
Source: PYQ-style

129. Choose the sentence with correct subject-verb agreement.
(a) The quality of the mangoes were not good. (b) The quality of the mangoes have not been good. (c) The quality of the mangoes was not good. (d) The quality of the mangoes are not good.
Answer: (c)
Solution: The subject is "quality" (singular); "of the mangoes" is an intervening phrase that must not control the verb. Hence "was".
Source: PYQ-style

130. Identify the error: "No sooner did the bell ring (P) / then the students (Q) / rushed out of the class. (R)"
(a) Part Q (b) Part R (c) Part P (d) No error
Answer: (a)
Solution: "No sooner" is always paired with "than", not "then". Part Q should read "than the students".

131. Choose the correctly framed conditional sentence.
(a) If I would have known, I would have helped you. (b) If I had known, I would have helped you. (c) If I knew, I would have helped you. (d) If I have known, I would help you.
Answer: (b)
Solution: Third conditional for an unreal past: "If + past perfect, would have + V3". "Would have" never appears in the if-clause.

Solution: Sentence-initial "hardly" demands inversion ("had I reached") and pairs with "when".

(a) Part Q (b) No error (c) Part R (d) Part P
Answer: (a)
Solution: "One of + plural noun" takes a singular verb: "is going". The plural "friends" misleads the ear — a sound-right trap.

135. Choose the correctly structured sentence.
(a) He not only plays cricket but also football. (b) He plays cricket not only but also football. (c) Not only he plays cricket but also football. (d) He plays not only cricket but also football.
Answer: (d)
Solution: Correlatives "not only... but also" must be placed just before the parallel items they join — here the two nouns "cricket" and "football".

Answer: (b)
Solution: With "as well as", the verb agrees with the first subject, "the teacher" (singular): "was present". The nearer plural noun is a trap.

137. Choose the sentence with correct parallelism.
(a) She likes reading, swimming, and to dance. (b) She likes to read, swimming, and dancing. (c) She likes reading, swimming, and dancing. (d) She likes reading, to swim, and dancing.
Answer: (c)
Solution: Items in a series must share the same grammatical form — all gerunds here. Mixing gerunds and infinitives breaks parallelism.

139. Choose the correctly structured sentence.
(a) Unless you do not work hard, you will fail. (b) Until you work hard, you will fail. (c) Unless you will work hard, you fail. (d) Unless you work hard, you will fail.
Answer: (d)
Solution: "Unless" is itself negative, so adding "not" creates a double negative. Also, the clause after "unless" uses the simple present, not "will".

140. Identify the error: "It is high time (P) / we start (Q) / preparing for the examination. (R)"
(a) Part P (b) Part Q (c) Part R (d) No error
Answer: (b)
Solution: "It is high time" is followed by the simple past to express urgency: "we started". The present tense is a common sound-right error.

141. Choose the sentence with the correct tense sequence.

Answer: (d)
Solution: Universal truths keep the simple present even in reported speech, regardless of the past reporting verb.

143. Choose the correctly structured sentence.
(a) It being a rainy day, I stayed at home. (b) Being a rainy day, I stayed at home. (c) Being a rainy day, staying at home was done by me. (d) A rainy day being there, I stayed at home.
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Being a rainy day" cannot logically modify "I" (dangling modifier). The absolute construction "It being a rainy day" supplies the correct subject.

Answer: (d)
Solution: The relative pronoun "that" refers to "players" (plural), so the verb must be "have ever played". The singular "one" nearby is the trap.

145. Choose the sentence with correct modifier placement.
(a) Walking down the road, a snake bit him. (b) He was bitten by a snake walking down the road. (c) Walking down the road, he was bitten by a snake. (d) A snake, walking down the road, bit him.
Answer: (c)
Solution: The participial phrase must modify the person walking — "he". In the first option the snake appears to be walking (dangling modifier); the second option is ambiguous.
Source: PYQ-style

146. Choose the sentence with the correct order of personal pronouns.
(a) He, you and I will share the prize. (b) I, you and he will share the prize. (c) You, I and he will share the prize. (d) You, he and I will share the prize.
Answer: (d)
Solution: For pleasant or neutral ideas the order is 2nd, 3rd, 1st person: "You, he and I". The order 1st, 2nd, 3rd is reserved for confessing faults, which is not the case here.
Source: PYQ-style

147. Choose the correctly structured sentence.
(a) The more you read, the more knowledge you gain. (b) The more you read, more knowledge you gain. (c) More you read, the more knowledge you gain. (d) The more you read, the most knowledge you gain.
Answer: (a)
Solution: The double comparative structure is "the + comparative... the + comparative". Dropping "the" or using the superlative "most" breaks the pattern.
Source: PYQ-style
