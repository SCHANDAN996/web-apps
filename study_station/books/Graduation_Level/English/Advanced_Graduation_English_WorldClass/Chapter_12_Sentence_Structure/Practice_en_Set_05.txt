101. Identify the correctly structured sentence.
(a) Hardly he had reached the station when the train left. (b) Hardly he reached the station when had the train left. (c) Hardly had he reached the station when the train left. (d) Hardly had reached he the station when the train left.
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Hardly" is a negative adverb; when it begins a sentence, inversion is required: Hardly + had + subject + past participle. The other options either skip inversion or misplace the subject.
Source: PYQ-style

102. Spot the error: Part P: Neither of the two boys / Part Q: have done / Part R: his homework / Part S: on time.
(a) Part P (b) Part Q (c) Part R (d) No error
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Neither of" takes a singular verb, so Part Q should read "has done". The plural-looking phrase "two boys" is the examiner's trap — the true subject is "neither".

103. Choose the sentence with correct subject-verb agreement.
(a) The quality of the mangoes were not good. (b) The quality of the mangoes was not good. (c) The quality of the mangoes have not been good. (d) The quality of the mangoes are not good.
Answer: (b)
Solution: The subject is "quality" (singular); "of the mangoes" is only a prepositional phrase. Hence the singular verb "was" is correct.

105. Choose the correctly framed conditional sentence.
(a) If I would have known, I would have helped you. (b) If I have known, I would help you. (c) If I knew, I would have helped you. (d) If I had known, I would have helped you.
Answer: (d)
Solution: Third conditional: If + past perfect, subject + would have + past participle. "Would have" never appears in the if-clause.

Answer: (c)
Solution: "Not only... but also" must join parallel elements placed immediately after each part. Option (c) keeps the structure balanced.

108. Choose the correct transformation: "He is too weak to walk." (Remove 'too')
(a) He is weak enough that he cannot walk. (b) He is very weak so he cannot walk. (c) He is so weak to walk. (d) He is so weak that he cannot walk.
Answer: (d)
Solution: "Too... to" converts to "so... that + cannot". The negative sense must be preserved in the that-clause.

109. Identify the correctly punctuated and structured complex sentence.
(a) Although it was raining, we went out. (b) Although it was raining, but we went out. (c) Although it was raining, yet we went out but slowly. (d) Although it was raining, however we went out.
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Although" and "but" cannot be used together in standard English; one subordinator is enough. Option (a) is the clean structure.

114. Choose the correctly structured sentence beginning with a negative expression.
(a) No sooner the bell rang than the students rushed out. (b) No sooner had the bell rung than the students rushed out. (c) No sooner the bell had rung when the students rushed out. (d) No sooner had the bell rang when the students rushed out.
Answer: (b)
Solution: "No sooner" requires inversion (had + subject + past participle) and pairs with "than", never "when". Only option (b) satisfies both rules.

115. Identify the sentence with a dangling modifier.
(a) He was walking down the road when a snake bit him. (b) Walking down the road, he saw a snake. (c) Walking down the road, a snake bit him. (d) While he walked down the road, a snake appeared.
Answer: (c)
Solution: In option (c) the modifier "Walking down the road" illogically attaches to "a snake" — the snake was not walking. That is a dangling modifier.

118. Choose the correct transformation into a simple sentence: "Although he is rich, he is not happy."

125. Identify the sentence with correct word order in an embedded question.
(a) Do you know where is the station? (b) Do you know where is it the station? (c) Do you know where the station is it? (d) Do you know where the station is?
Answer: (d)
Solution: In an embedded (indirect) question, statement order is used: "where the station is", not the inverted question order. Hindi-speakers often err here ("What you are doing?").
