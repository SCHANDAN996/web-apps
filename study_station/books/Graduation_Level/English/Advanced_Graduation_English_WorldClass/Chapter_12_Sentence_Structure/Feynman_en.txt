# 🧒 Feynman Technique — Sentence Structure Explained to a 12-Year-Old

## The Hardest Concept: "Subject–Verb Agreement with Intervening Phrases"

Imagine you are the captain of a football team. Your name is **Ravi**. You are standing with your whole team — 10 players — waiting for the referee's whistle.

Now, someone asks: *"Who is playing today?"*

The correct answer is: **"Ravi is playing."** — NOT "Ravi are playing."

But here's the trick the examiner plays. He hides Ravi behind his team:

> ❌ "Ravi, **along with his ten teammates**, are playing today."
> ✅ "Ravi, **along with his ten teammates**, is playing today."

See what happened? The moment you see "ten teammates" sitting right next to the verb, your brain screams *"So many people! Use ARE!"* But the real subject is still only **Ravi**. The teammates are just standing *between* Ravi and the verb — like fans standing between you and the TV. They block your view, but they don't change who's actually playing.

**The Unforgettable Trick — "The Curtain Rule":**

Everything between two commas (or after phrases like *along with, as well as, together with, in addition to, besides*) is a **curtain**. Close the curtain. Read the sentence without it:

> "Ravi, ~~along with his ten teammates~~, **is** playing today." ✔

If the sentence still sounds complete and correct behind the curtain, you've found the real subject and the real verb.

**Try it yourself:**

> "The quality of these mangoes **(is/are)** excellent."

Close the curtain on "of these mangoes" → "The quality **is** excellent." ✔
(*Quality* is singular — one quality. The mangoes are just fans blocking your view!)
