# 📊 PYQ Analysis — Sentence Structure

## How Exams Test This Chapter

Sentence Structure is the **backbone** of the English section in SSC CGL, CHSL, IBPS PO/Clerk, and State PCS prelims. Examiners rarely ask "What is a clause?" directly — instead, they hide structural errors inside long, decorated sentences. Based on the recurring pattern of recent papers, questions from this chapter appear in these forms:

- **Spot the Error** — the most frequent format. A sentence is split into parts (a)/(b)/(c)/(d); one part breaks a structural rule (subject-verb agreement across an intervening phrase, faulty parallelism, dangling modifier, wrong connector).
- **Sentence Improvement** — an underlined segment must be replaced; three options are grammatically wrong but "sound right."
- **Fill in the Blanks (connector-based)** — choosing between *although / despite / unless / hardly…when*.
- **Sentence Arrangement (PQRS)** — tests whether you can track clause logic and pronoun references.
- **Direct/Indirect & Active/Passive** — structurally, these are transformation questions: they test whether you understand how a sentence is built.

**Sub-topics that repeat most often:** subject-verb agreement with intervening phrases, parallelism in lists, correlative conjunctions (not only…but also), dangling/misplaced modifiers, and run-on/comma-splice errors.

**The examiner's favourite traps (in words, not numbers):**

1. **Intervening Phrase Trap** — a long phrase between subject and verb ("The quality of the mangoes *were*…") makes you match the verb to the nearest noun instead of the real subject.
2. **Sound-Right Trap** — an option that sounds natural in spoken English ("Neither of the boys *have*…") but is formally wrong.
3. **Parallelism Trap** — a list where the third item quietly changes form ("He likes reading, swimming, and *to jog*").
4. **Over-thinking Trap** — a perfectly correct sentence part that *feels* wrong, pushing you to "fix" a correct option.
5. **Connector Confusion** — *despite* followed by a clause ("Despite he was ill…") instead of a noun phrase.

## Top 10 High-Yield Questions (PYQ-style) with Solutions

**Q1.** The quality of the apples (a) / in the basket (b) / were not good. (c) / No error (d)
**Answer:** (c)
**Solution:** The subject is *quality* (singular), not *apples*. "In the basket" and "of the apples" are intervening phrases. Correct: *was not good*.
**Trap type:** Intervening Phrase
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q2.** Neither of the two candidates (a) / have submitted (b) / his documents yet. (c) / No error (d)
**Answer:** (b)
**Solution:** *Neither of* takes a singular verb: *has submitted*. Spoken English makes "have" sound acceptable — that is the trap.
**Trap type:** Sound-Right
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q3.** The teacher asked the students (a) / to submit their assignments on time, (b) / to attend classes regularly, (c) / and that they should revise daily. (d)
**Answer:** (d)
**Solution:** The list must be parallel: three infinitive phrases. Correct: *and to revise daily*. The clause "that they should…" breaks the pattern.
**Trap type:** Parallelism
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q4.** Despite of heavy rain, (a) / the match continued (b) / without interruption. (c) / No error (d)
**Answer:** (a)
**Solution:** *Despite* never takes *of* (that is *in spite of*). Correct: *Despite heavy rain* or *In spite of heavy rain*.
**Trap type:** Connector Confusion
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q5.** Walking down the road, (a) / a snake suddenly appeared (b) / in front of him. (c) / No error (d)
**Answer:** (a)/(b) — the error is the dangling modifier: the sentence says the *snake* was walking down the road.
**Solution:** The introductory participle must modify the subject of the main clause. Correct: *Walking down the road, he suddenly saw a snake.*
**Trap type:** Dangling Modifier
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q6.** He is not only (a) / a good singer (b) / but also plays the guitar well. (c) / No error (d)
**Answer:** (a)–(b) region — faulty correlative placement.
**Solution:** *Not only…but also* must join parallel elements. Correct: *He is not only a good singer but also a good guitarist* — or restructure so both parts carry the same grammatical form.
**Trap type:** Parallelism (correlative conjunctions)
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q7.** Each of the boys (a) / who participated in the race (b) / were given a certificate. (c) / No error (d)
**Answer:** (c)
**Solution:** *Each* is singular; the relative clause "who participated…" is an intervening distractor. Correct: *was given a certificate*.
**Trap type:** Intervening Phrase
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q8.** Hardly he had reached the station (a) / when the train (b) / started moving. (c) / No error (d)
**Answer:** (a)
**Solution:** After *Hardly/Scarcely/No sooner* at the start, inversion is required: *Hardly **had he** reached…*. Also note the fixed pairs: hardly/scarcely…**when**, no sooner…**than**.
**Trap type:** Sound-Right (word order)
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q9.** The committee (a) / has submitted its report (b) / to the ministry yesterday. (c) / No error (d)
**Answer:** (c)
**Solution:** A definite past time marker (*yesterday*) forces simple past: *submitted its report yesterday*. Present perfect cannot live with a finished time word.
**Trap type:** Over-thinking (tense-structure hybrid)
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q10.** Being a rainy day, (a) / I stayed at home (b) / and read a book. (c) / No error (d)
**Answer:** (a)
**Solution:** "Being a rainy day" dangles — *I* am not a rainy day. Correct: *It being a rainy day, I stayed at home* (absolute construction) or *As it was a rainy day…*.
**Trap type:** Dangling Modifier
**Source:** PYQ-style

## Applying the 15/45-Second Rule

**Q2 (Neither of the two candidates…) — 15-second question.**
This is **fact-based**: the rule "neither of + plural noun + singular verb" is memorised. Do not re-read the sentence three times. Spot *Neither of* → expect singular verb → mark (b) → move on. Budget: 15 seconds.

**Q5 (Walking down the road, a snake…) — 45-second question.**
This is **structure-based inference**: you must ask *who* is walking. Check the subject of the main clause (*a snake*) against the introductory phrase. If they don't match, it's a dangling modifier — then you must also verify the corrected version keeps the meaning. Budget: up to 45 seconds; if the modifier logic still feels unclear after that, mark for review and skip.

**Strategy summary:** Agreement and fixed-connector questions are 15-second recalls. Modifier, parallelism, and sentence-arrangement questions earn their 45 seconds — spend them checking *logic*, not re-reading.
