# 🪄 Short Tricks + Skip Strategy — Sentence Structure

## Memory Boxes (Mnemonics & One-Line Tricks)

**Box 1 — FANBOYS (Coordinating Conjunctions)**
> **F**or, **A**nd, **N**or, **B**ut, **O**r, **Y**et, **S**o
> Join two independent clauses. Comma before them when joining full sentences.
> ✅ "He was tired, **so** he slept."

**Box 2 — SWABI (Subordinating Conjunctions that start dependent clauses)**
> **S**ince, **W**hen, **A**fter, **B**ecause, **I**f
> A SWABI clause cannot stand alone. "Because he was late." = fragment ❌

**Box 3 — The "Comma Flip" Rule**
> Dependent clause first → comma. Dependent clause last → no comma.
> ✅ "**When it rains,** the match stops." / "The match stops **when it rains**."

**Box 4 — AAAWWUBBIS (Front-loaders needing a comma)**
> **A**fter, **A**lthough, **A**s, **W**hen, **W**hile, **U**ntil, **B**ecause, **B**efore, **I**f, **S**ince
> If a sentence *starts* with one of these, a comma almost always follows the first clause.

**Box 5 — THAMO (Conjunctive Adverbs / Semicolon Joiners)**
> **T**herefore, **H**owever, **A**lso, **M**eanwhile, **O**therwise
> Pattern: `; thamo,` → "He studied hard; **however,** he failed."
> Never join two full sentences with just a comma + THAMO (that's a comma splice).

**Box 6 — The "Period Test" for Fragments**
> Cover everything except the suspected clause. Can it stand alone with a capital letter and full stop? If it leaves you asking "then what?", it's a fragment.

**Box 7 — Run-on Killer: "3 Doors"**
> Two independent clauses can meet through only 3 doors:
> 1. Full stop (.) 2. Semicolon (;) 3. Comma + FANBOYS
> Anything else = run-on.

**Box 8 — "There/Here" Inversion Trick**
> In "There **are** three boys," the subject comes *after* the verb. Cross out "There/Here" and find the real subject to fix agreement.

**Box 9 — Intervening Phrase Scissors ✂️**
> "The **box** of chocolates ~~of chocolates~~ **is** on the table."
> Cut everything between subject and verb; check agreement with the naked subject.

**Box 10 — Question Structure: "Wh + Auxiliary + Subject + Verb"**
> ❌ "What you are doing?" ✅ "What **are you** doing?"
> In embedded questions, flip back: "Tell me what **you are** doing." (no inversion)

**Box 11 — Parallelism: "Same Dress Code"**
> Items in a list must wear the same grammatical dress.
> ❌ "She likes reading, swimming, and **to dance**."
> ✅ "She likes reading, swimming, and dancing."

**Box 12 — Modifier Hug Rule**
> A modifier must hug the word it describes.
> ❌ "Walking down the road, the tree was seen." (Was the tree walking?)
> ✅ "Walking down the road, **I** saw the tree."

**Box 13 — "Neither…nor / Either…or" Proximity Rule**
> Verb agrees with the subject **nearest** to it.
> "Neither the teacher nor the **students are** here."

**Box 14 — Sentence-Type Counter: "1-2-1+"**
> Simple = 1 independent clause | Compound = 2+ independent | Complex = 1 independent + 1+ dependent | Compound-Complex = 2+ independent + 1+ dependent.
> Count the clauses first — the type reveals itself.

**Box 15 — Active vs Passive: "By-Zombie Test"**
> If you can add "by zombies" after the verb and it still makes sense, it's passive.
> "The bill was passed (by zombies)." → Passive. Convert to active unless the doer is unknown/irrelevant.

## ⏭️ Skip Strategy (Exam Hall)

**Tip 1 — Skip the "Sentence Rearrangement Monster" on first pass.**
Para-jumbles with 5–6 sentences eat 2–3 minutes. Mark them, finish all error-spotting and fill-in-the-blank questions first (15-second fact-based wins), then return. A jumble solved under time pressure is often a jumble solved wrong.

**Tip 2 — Skip when two options differ only in punctuation.**
If options (b) and (c) are identical except for a comma vs semicolon, and you can't recall the exact rule, don't gamble immediately — flag it. Punctuation-only distinctions are classic over-thinking traps; revisit with a fresh eye and apply the "3 Doors" rule.

**Tip 3 — Never skip "Find the error" questions with an intervening phrase.**
These look long but are 15-second gifts: apply the Scissors trick (Box 9), strip the phrase, and the agreement error jumps out. Long sentence ≠ hard question. Skip the *long reading*, not the *long sentence*.
