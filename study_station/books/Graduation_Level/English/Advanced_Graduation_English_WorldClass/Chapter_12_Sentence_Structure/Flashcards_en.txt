Card 1
Front: What are the four basic sentence types by structure?
Back: Simple (one independent clause), Compound (two+ independent clauses joined by FANBOYS/semicolon), Complex (one independent + one+ dependent clause), Compound-Complex (two+ independent + one+ dependent clause).

Card 2
Front: What does FANBOYS stand for? (Mnemonic)
Back: The 7 coordinating conjunctions: For, And, Nor, But, Or, Yet, So. They join two independent clauses — always with a comma before them.

Card 3
Front: Identify the error: "The list of items are on the desk."
Back: "are" → "is". The subject is "list" (singular), not "items". Trap type: Intervening Phrase — words between subject and verb distract you.

Card 4
Front: What is a dependent (subordinate) clause?
Back: A group of words with a subject and verb that cannot stand alone; it begins with a subordinating conjunction (because, although, since, when, if) or a relative pronoun (who, which, that).

Card 5
Front: "Although he worked hard, but he failed." — Correct or incorrect?
Back: Incorrect. English never pairs "although" with "but". Use either "Although he worked hard, he failed" or "He worked hard, but he failed." (Classic Hindi-speaker error: "यद्यपि… परंतु".)

Card 6
Front: What is a run-on sentence and how do you fix it?
Back: Two independent clauses joined without punctuation or a conjunction. Fix with: (1) a full stop, (2) semicolon, (3) comma + FANBOYS, or (4) make one clause dependent.

Card 7
Front: What is a comma splice?
Back: Joining two independent clauses with only a comma: "He came late, he missed the bus." Fix it with a semicolon, a conjunction, or a full stop.

Card 8
Front: Mnemonic for common subordinating conjunctions?
Back: "AAAWWUBBIS": After, Although, As, When, While, Until, Because, Before, If, Since. If the clause comes first, put a comma after it ("AAAWWUBBIS + comma").

Card 9
Front: Identify the error: "Neither of the two boys have done his homework."
Back: "have" → "has". "Neither of" takes a singular verb. Trap type: Sound-Right — plural noun "boys" sits next to the verb and misleads the ear.

Card 10
Front: What is a sentence fragment?
Back: An incomplete sentence missing a subject, a finite verb, or a complete thought: "Running down the street." Fix by attaching it to an independent clause or adding the missing element.

Card 11
Front: What is the difference between a phrase and a clause?
Back: A clause has a subject + finite verb; a phrase does not. "In the morning" = phrase; "when the sun rose" = clause.

Card 12
Front: "The committee have taken its decision." — Correct or incorrect?
Back: Incorrect (in standard exam usage). A collective noun acting as one unit takes a singular verb: "The committee has taken its decision." Use plural only when members act individually.

Card 13
Front: What are the four sentence types by function?
Back: Declarative (statement), Interrogative (question), Imperative (command/request), Exclamatory (strong emotion). Mnemonic: "D-I-I-E — Declare, Inquire, Instruct, Exclaim."

Card 14
Front: "Being a rainy day, I stayed at home." — What is wrong?
Back: Dangling modifier. "Being a rainy day" has no logical subject — I am not a rainy day. Correct: "It being a rainy day, I stayed at home."

Card 15
Front: In the sentence "She gave him a book," identify subject, verb, indirect object, direct object.
Back: Subject: She; Verb: gave; Indirect object: him (receives); Direct object: a book (what is given). Pattern: S + V + IO + DO.

Card 16
Front: What is an inverted sentence, and give one example.
Back: The verb comes before the subject, often after negative adverbs or in questions: "Never have I seen such courage." / "Hardly had he arrived when it rained."

Card 17
Front: Examiner's Trap: "One of my friends are coming tonight." — Spot the error.
Back: "are" → "is". After "one of + plural noun", the verb agrees with "one" (singular). Trap type: Intervening Phrase — the plural noun before the verb is bait.

Card 18
Front: When do you use a semicolon in sentence structure?
Back: (1) Between two related independent clauses without a conjunction: "He studied hard; he topped the exam." (2) Before conjunctive adverbs (however, therefore, moreover) joining two clauses: "He studied hard; therefore, he topped."

Card 19
Front: What is a compound-complex sentence? Give an example.
Back: At least two independent clauses + at least one dependent clause. Example: "Although it was raining, we went out, and we enjoyed the walk."

Card 20
Front: Quick test — classify: "When the bell rang, the students left the class, and the teacher locked the room."
Back: Compound-Complex: dependent clause "When the bell rang" + two independent clauses joined by "and".
