# 🧒 Feynman Technique — Synonyms Explained to a 12-Year-Old

## The Hardest Concept: "Two words can mean almost the same thing — but the examiner wants the CLOSEST one, not just a similar one."

Imagine you have a box of crayons. You ask your friend for the **red** crayon. Your friend gives you **orange**. Is orange a colour? Yes. Is it close to red? Sort of. But is it red? **No!**

Synonym questions work exactly like this. The examiner gives you a word — say **"happy"** — and four options:

- (a) glad
- (b) tall
- (c) angry
- (d) sleepy

"Tall" and "sleepy" are easy to throw out — they are not even in the same family. "Angry" is the opposite — a trap for students who read too fast. **"Glad"** is the red crayon: it means the same as happy.

Now here is where it gets tricky at the advanced level. The examiner stops giving you "tall" and "sleepy." Instead, ALL four options look like shades of red:

**Word: FRUGAL**
- (a) miserly — hates spending, even on needs (too negative)
- (b) economical — careful with money, avoids waste ✅
- (c) poor — has no money (a condition, not a habit)
- (d) generous — the opposite

All four are about money! But only **"economical"** matches the *shade* of frugal — careful spending without the negative sting of "miserly." The exam is not testing whether you know a similar word; it is testing whether you know the **exact shade of meaning**.

## The Unforgettable Trick: "The Twin Test" 👯

Ask yourself: *"If I swap the two words in a sentence, would anyone notice?"*

- "She is a **frugal** person." → "She is an **economical** person." — Nobody blinks. ✅ Twins!
- "She is a **frugal** person." → "She is a **miserly** person." — People notice. The sentence turned insulting. ❌ Not twins.

If the swap changes the *feeling* of the sentence, it is not a true synonym — it is a trap wearing a synonym's clothes.

## Why Roots Are Your Superpower 🦸

You cannot memorise every English word. But you can memorise about 50 roots and unlock thousands of words. Think of roots as LEGO blocks:

- **BENE** = good → benefit, benevolent, benediction
- **MAL** = bad → malicious, malnutrition, malevolent

See **"malevolent"** in the exam and never learned it? Break the LEGO: MAL (bad) + VOL (wish) = "wishing bad" → synonym: **spiteful**. You solved a word you never memorised. That is the superpower.

## 📝 Blurting Sheet Instruction

Close this book. Take a blank page. In 90 seconds, blurt out everything you remember:
1. The crayon-box idea (closest shade, not just similar).
2. The Twin Test (swap the words in a sentence).
3. Three roots and two words from each.

Now open the book and check what you missed. Whatever you forgot — that is exactly what you revise tonight.

**Question for you:** Is "begin" a true twin of "commence"? Try the swap: "The meeting will commence at 10." / "The meeting will begin at 10." — Same meaning, but which one sounds more formal? That feeling-difference is called *register* — and spotting it is what separates a good score from a great one.
