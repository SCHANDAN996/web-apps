# 📊 PYQ Analysis — Synonyms

## How Exams Test Synonyms

Synonym questions appear in almost every major exam — SSC CGL, CHSL, IBPS PO/Clerk, RRB NTPC, and State PCS prelims. The pattern is remarkably stable: a single word is given (sometimes in a sentence, sometimes standalone), and you must pick the word **closest in meaning** from four options.

**What examiners repeatedly test:**

- **High-frequency GRE/bank-exam vocabulary**: words like *abate, candid, frugal, lucid, obsolete, pragmatic, tenacious, verbose* keep returning across years and exams.
- **Words with deceptive look-alikes**: options that *sound* similar to the target word but mean something different (e.g., *elicit* vs. *illicit*).
- **Context-dependent synonyms**: the word is embedded in a sentence, and only one option fits *that* usage (e.g., *grave* = serious, not burial place).
- **Degree traps**: an option that is related in meaning but too strong or too weak (e.g., *annoyed* vs. *furious* for *irritated*).
- **Positive/negative connotation traps**: options with the right field of meaning but the wrong emotional colour (e.g., *frugal* is positive; *stingy* is negative).

**Examiner's favourite traps (in words, not numbers):**

1. **Sound-Right Trap** — an option that resembles the word phonetically (*appraise* vs. *apprise*).
2. **Partial-Meaning Trap** — an option correct for *one* meaning of a polysemous word, but not the one used in the sentence.
3. **Over-thinking Trap** — a rare, fancy option that tempts strong students away from the simple correct answer.
4. **Antonym-as-Option Trap** — one option is the exact opposite, planted for students who misread the question as "antonym."

## Top 10 High-Yield Questions (with Solutions)

**Q1.** Choose the word closest in meaning to **ABATE**.
(a) increase (b) diminish (c) provoke (d) settle
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** *Abate* means to reduce in intensity — "the storm abated." (a) is the antonym trap; (c) and (d) are unrelated distractors.
**Trap type:** Antonym-as-Option | **Source:** PYQ-style

**Q2.** Choose the word closest in meaning to **CANDID**.
(a) secretive (b) clever (c) frank (d) rude
**Answer: (c)**
**Solution:** *Candid* = truthful and straightforward. (d) *rude* is the connotation trap — candour can feel rude, but the words are not synonyms. (a) is the antonym.
**Trap type:** Connotation | **Source:** PYQ-style

**Q3.** Choose the word closest in meaning to **FRUGAL**.
(a) wasteful (b) stingy (c) economical (d) poor
**Answer: (c)**
**Solution:** *Frugal* = careful with money (positive). (b) *stingy* is the degree/connotation trap — same field, negative shade. (d) confuses cause with characteristic.
**Trap type:** Connotation | **Source:** PYQ-style

**Q4.** Choose the word closest in meaning to **OBSOLETE**.
(a) ancient (b) outdated (c) broken (d) rare
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** *Obsolete* = no longer in use because superseded. (a) *ancient* is the over-thinking trap — old things may still be used; obsolete things are *disused*, not merely old.
**Trap type:** Over-thinking | **Source:** PYQ-style

**Q5.** Choose the word closest in meaning to **ELICIT**.
(a) illegal (b) draw out (c) suppress (d) permit
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** *Elicit* = to draw out a response ("elicit information"). (a) is the classic sound-right trap — *illicit* (illegal) is a different word entirely.
**Trap type:** Sound-Right | **Source:** PYQ-style

**Q6.** In the sentence "She gave a **grave** reply," choose the closest meaning of **grave**.
(a) burial place (b) serious (c) angry (d) brief
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** Here *grave* is an adjective meaning serious/solemn. (a) is the partial-meaning trap — correct for the noun, wrong for this context.
**Trap type:** Partial-Meaning | **Source:** PYQ-style

**Q7.** Choose the word closest in meaning to **TENACIOUS**.
(a) stubborn (b) persistent (c) aggressive (d) talented
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** *Tenacious* = holding firmly, not giving up (positive/neutral). (a) *stubborn* is the connotation trap — same field, negative shade.
**Trap type:** Connotation | **Source:** PYQ-style

**Q8.** Choose the word closest in meaning to **LUCID**.
(a) bright (b) clear (c) liquid (d) lucky
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** *Lucid* = easy to understand ("a lucid explanation"). (a) is the partial-meaning trap (lucid can mean shining in literary use); (c) and (d) are sound-right distractors.
**Trap type:** Sound-Right | **Source:** PYQ-style

**Q9.** Choose the word closest in meaning to **PRAGMATIC**.
(a) idealistic (b) practical (c) dogmatic (d) optimistic
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** *Pragmatic* = dealing with things sensibly and realistically. (a) is the antonym trap; (c) is a sound-right distractor.
**Trap type:** Antonym-as-Option | **Source:** PYQ-style

**Q10.** Choose the word closest in meaning to **VERBOSE**.
(a) wordy (b) silent (c) truthful (d) violent
**Answer: (a)**
**Solution:** *Verbose* = using more words than needed. (c) is the sound-right trap (*veracity* = truthfulness); (b) is the antonym.
**Trap type:** Sound-Right | **Source:** PYQ-style

## Applying the 15/45-Second Rule

**Q1 (ABATE) — 15-second question.** This is pure recall: you either know *abate* = diminish or you don't. Strategy: read the word, scan options, mark (b), move on. Do **not** re-read the options twice — that converts a 15-second win into a 60-second loss.

**Q6 (GRAVE in a sentence) — 45-second question.** This is context-based inference. Strategy: (1) identify the word's role — here it's an adjective before a noun, so the noun meaning (burial place) is impossible; (2) substitute each remaining option into the sentence — "a serious reply" reads naturally, "an angry reply" changes the tone, "a brief reply" is unrelated; (3) mark (b). Spending the full 45 seconds here is correct — this question *rewards* careful reading.

**Rule of thumb:** Standalone word → 15 seconds (fact-based). Word inside a sentence → 45 seconds (inference-based). If a 15-second word is unknown after 10 seconds, eliminate obvious antonyms, guess between the survivors, and skip — never leave it blank if there is no negative marking, and never burn 2 minutes on it if there is.
