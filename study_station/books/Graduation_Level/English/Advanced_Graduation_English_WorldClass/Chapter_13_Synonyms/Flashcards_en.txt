Card 1
Front: What is a synonym?
Back: A word that has the same or nearly the same meaning as another word. Example: "begin" and "commence".

Card 2
Front: Give three synonyms of "abundant".
Back: Plentiful, copious, ample.

Card 3
Front: What is the synonym of "ephemeral"?
Back: Short-lived / transient / fleeting. (Root: Greek "ephēmeros" = lasting a day.)

Card 4
Front: Mnemonic: How do you remember "loquacious" = talkative?
Back: Think of "LOQUA = LOG (people) who keep talking". Root "loqui" (Latin) = to speak — also in eloquent, soliloquy, colloquial.

Card 5
Front: Synonym of "candid".
Back: Frank, honest, straightforward. (Trap: it does NOT mean "candidate-like" or "secretive".)

Card 6
Front: Examiner's Trap: Is "enormity" a synonym of "enormousness" (huge size)?
Back: No. "Enormity" means great wickedness or a serious crime (the enormity of the offence). Examiners use it to trap students who guess by word appearance.

Card 7
Front: What does the Latin root "bene" mean? Give two derived words.
Back: "Bene" = good/well. Words: benevolent (kind), beneficial (advantageous), benediction (blessing).

Card 8
Front: Synonym of "obstinate".
Back: Stubborn, headstrong, adamant.

Card 9
Front: What does the Latin root "mal" mean? Give two derived words.
Back: "Mal" = bad/evil. Words: malevolent (ill-willed), malicious (spiteful), malnutrition.

Card 10
Front: Mnemonic: How do you remember "gregarious" = sociable?
Back: "GREGArious = GREet + GATHER" — a person who loves to gather and greet people. Root "grex/gregis" (Latin) = flock — also in congregate, segregate.

Card 11
Front: Synonym of "meticulous".
Back: Careful, precise, painstaking, thorough.

Card 12
Front: Examiner's Trap: "Restive" looks like "restful". Is it a synonym of "calm"?
Back: No. "Restive" means restless, impatient, difficult to control — nearly the opposite of restful. A classic sound-right trap.

Card 13
Front: What does the Greek root "chrono" mean? Give two derived words.
Back: "Chrono" = time. Words: chronology (order of events), chronic (long-lasting), synchronize.

Card 14
Front: Synonym of "pragmatic".
Back: Practical, realistic, sensible (opposite: idealistic).

Card 15
Front: What does the Latin root "dict" mean? Give two derived words.
Back: "Dict" = say/speak. Words: predict (say before), contradict (speak against), verdict.

Card 16
Front: Synonym of "lucid".
Back: Clear, transparent, easy to understand. (Root "lux/luc" = light.)

Card 17
Front: What is the difference between "synonym" and "cognate"?
Back: A synonym shares meaning within a language; cognates are words in different languages from a common origin (e.g., English "mother", Hindi "mātā"). Exams test synonyms, not cognates.

Card 18
Front: Synonym of "ubiquitous".
Back: Omnipresent, found everywhere, pervasive.

Card 19
Front: What does the Greek root "phil" mean? Give two derived words.
Back: "Phil" = love. Words: philanthropy (love of mankind), bibliophile (book lover), philosophy (love of wisdom).

Card 20
Front: Quick strategy: If you don't know a word in a synonym question, what should you do first?
Back: Break it into root + prefix/suffix, then eliminate options whose meaning clearly clashes with the root. If still unsure after 45 seconds, skip and return — don't guess blindly.
