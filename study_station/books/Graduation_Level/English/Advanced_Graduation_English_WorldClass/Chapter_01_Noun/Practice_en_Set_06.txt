126. Choose the correct sentence.
(a) The sceneries of Kashmir are very beautiful. (b) The sceneries of Kashmir is very beautiful. (c) The scenery of Kashmir is very beautiful. (d) The scenery of Kashmir are very beautiful.
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Scenery" is an uncountable noun; it has no plural form and takes a singular verb. Options with "sceneries" or "are" are wrong.
Source: NCERT Class 9 English Grammar

127. Spot the error: Part P: The furniture in this office / Part Q: are very costly / Part R: and needs replacement. / Part S: No error
(a) Part Q (b) Part R (c) Part P (d) No error
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Furniture" is uncountable and always takes a singular verb, so Part Q should read "is very costly". Part R correctly uses "needs".

128. Choose the correctly spelt plural form.
(a) Phenomenons (b) Phenomena (c) Phenomenas (d) Phenomenae
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Phenomenon" is a Greek-origin noun whose plural is "phenomena". English plurals like "phenomenons" are incorrect in formal usage.

(a) credential (b) credentials (c) credentialed (d) credencing

130. Spot the error: Part P: One of my friend / Part Q: has gone / Part R: to Delhi yesterday. / Part S: No error
(a) Part R (b) Part P (c) Part Q (d) No error
Answer: (b)
Solution: After "one of", a plural noun is required: "one of my friends". This is a classic examiner trap — the verb stays singular ("has") because the real subject is "one".

Answer: (c)

132. Choose the correct sentence.
(a) She gave me an useful information. (b) She gave me a useful piece of information. (c) She gave me many informations. (d) She gave me an information.
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Information" is uncountable, so it cannot take "an" or a plural -s; we count it as "a piece of information". Also note "useful" begins with a consonant sound (yoo-), so it would take "a", not "an".

Solution: Here "mathematics" means calculations, treated as singular in standard exam usage; Part Q should be "is so complicated". Subjects like mathematics, physics, news take singular verbs.

(a) Criterions (b) Criterias (c) Criterion (d) Criteria
Answer: (d)

135. Choose the correct possessive form.
(a) The children's toys are scattered. (b) The childrens' toys are scattered. (c) The childrens toys are scattered. (d) The children toys' are scattered.
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Children" is already plural without -s, so the possessive is formed by adding 's: "children's". Plurals ending in -s take only an apostrophe (boys'), but irregular plurals take 's.
Source: NCERT Class 8 English Grammar

136. Spot the error: Part P: I need some informations / Part Q: about the admission process / Part R: before I apply. / Part S: No error
(a) Part P (b) Part Q (c) Part R (d) No error
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Information" is uncountable and has no plural form; Part P should read "some information". Similar nouns: advice, news, luggage, homework.

137. A ______ of wolves attacked the village livestock.
(a) herd (b) flock (c) swarm (d) pack
Answer: (d)
Solution: The correct collective noun for wolves is "pack". Herd = cattle, flock = sheep/birds, swarm = bees/insects. Examiners swap these to trap you.

139. Spot the error: Part P: The Ganges is / Part Q: one of the longest river / Part R: in India. / Part S: No error

140. Which sentence uses the noun "work" correctly?
(a) I have many works to finish today. (b) I have works to finishing today. (c) I have a lot of work to finish today. (d) I have a work to finish today.
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Work" (meaning tasks/job) is uncountable; it cannot take "a" or a plural -s. "A lot of work" is correct. ("Works" is correct only for artistic creations, e.g., the works of Shakespeare.)
Source: NCERT Class 9 English Grammar

141. The ______ of the building has weakened over the years.
(a) founding (b) found (c) foundation (d) founded
Answer: (c)
Solution: The blank needs a noun after "The". "Foundation" is the noun; "found" is a verb/adjective, "founded" a verb form, "founding" a gerund that does not fit the meaning.
Source: PYQ-style

142. Spot the error: Part P: Each of the boys / Part Q: have submitted / Part R: his assignment on time. / Part S: No error
(a) Part P (b) No error (c) Part R (d) Part Q
Answer: (d)
Solution: "Each of" takes a singular verb: "has submitted". The plural noun "boys" between the subject and verb is an intervening-phrase trap; the pronoun "his" in Part R is correctly singular.

143. Choose the correct sentence.

144. She is ______ honest woman.
(a) a (b) the (c) no article needed (d) an
Answer: (d)
Solution: "Honest" begins with a vowel sound (the h is silent), so "an" is used. Article choice depends on sound, not spelling — a favourite examiner trap.

145. Spot the error: Part P: The teacher, along with his students, / Part Q: were present / Part R: at the function. / Part S: No error
(a) Part Q (b) Part P (c) Part R (d) No error
Answer: (a)
Solution: With "along with", the verb agrees with the first subject ("the teacher"), so it should be "was present". Phrases like along with, as well as, together with do not make the subject plural.

146. The plural of "analysis" is:

147. Choose the correct sentence.
(a) He gave me many advices. (b) He gave me a piece of advice. (c) He gave me an advice. (d) He gave me advices.
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Advice" is uncountable; to count it we say "a piece of advice" or "some advice". "Advices" and "an advice" are wrong.

148. Spot the error: Part P: Neither the manager nor the employees / Part Q: was aware / Part R: of the new policy. / Part S: No error
(a) Part P (b) Part R (c) Part Q (d) No error
Answer: (c)
Solution: With "neither…nor", the verb agrees with the nearer subject ("employees", plural), so it should be "were aware". This proximity rule is a classic trap.

149. Which of the following is an abstract noun?
(a) Table (b) Teacher (c) Honesty (d) Delhi
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Honesty" names a quality that cannot be seen or touched — an abstract noun. Teacher is common, Delhi is proper, table is concrete.
Source: NCERT Class 8 English Grammar

150. Spot the error: Part P: The number of students / Part Q: in the class / Part R: are increasing every year. / Part S: No error
(a) Part P (b) Part R (c) Part Q (d) No error
Answer: (b)
Solution: "The number of" takes a singular verb: "is increasing". Contrast: "A number of students are…" (plural). Examiners deliberately pair these two to confuse candidates.
