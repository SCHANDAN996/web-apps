# 📋 Important Grammar Rules & Tips — Noun

## (a) 12 High-Yield Rules

| # | Rule | Example | Examiner's Trap |
|---|------|---------|-----------------|
| 1 | Uncountable nouns (advice, information, furniture, luggage, news, scenery, machinery, stationery, crockery, poetry, bread, hair) take **no plural form** and **no 'a/an'**; they take a **singular verb**. | The furniture in this room **is** new. | "He gave me many advices" ❌ — trap: adding -s to look plural. |
| 2 | Nouns that **look plural but are singular**: news, physics, mathematics, economics, politics, athletics, measles, mumps, billiards, innings. | Mathematics **is** a difficult subject. | "The news are good" ❌ — the -s ending tricks the eye. |
| 3 | Nouns that **exist only in plural form** (take plural verb): scissors, trousers, spectacles, goggles, tongs, pliers, wages, premises, belongings, earnings, outskirts, alms, thanks. | His trousers **are** torn. | "A scissors is on the table" ❌ — use "a pair of scissors". |
| 4 | **Collective nouns** (team, jury, committee, family, audience, government) take a **singular verb** when acting as one unit, **plural verb** when members act individually. | The jury **has** given its verdict. / The jury **are** divided in their opinion. | Trap: always choosing singular; check whether members act together or separately. |
| 5 | Some nouns have the **same form in singular and plural**: sheep, deer, fish, aircraft, species, series, dozen (after a number), score. | Two **sheep are** grazing. | "Two sheeps" ❌ — trap: forcing a regular plural. |
| 6 | **Compound nouns** form plurals on the **main word**: sons-in-law, passers-by, commanders-in-chief, men-of-war, step-mothers. | My two **sons-in-law** are doctors. | "Son-in-laws" ❌ — pluralising the wrong word. |
| 7 | **Possessive case** is used mainly with **living things** and time/distance expressions; for non-living things use **'of'**. | Ram's book; a week's holiday; **the leg of the table** (not "the table's leg"). | "The room's window" ❌ — trap: applying 's to objects. |
| 8 | **Double possessive is wrong**: don't use two apostrophes or 's + of together for the same noun. | the wife of my brother / my brother's wife | "My brother's wife's…" chains and "the book of Mohan's" ❌ are classic error-spotting items. |
| 9 | **Apostrophe rules**: plural nouns ending in -s take only an apostrophe (boys' school); singular nouns take 's (boss's car); joint possession — apostrophe on the **last** name (Ram and Shyam's house = one house); separate possession — apostrophe on **each** (Ram's and Shyam's houses). | **Girls'** hostel; **Ram and Sita's** shop (shared). | "Ram's and Sita's shop" for a shared shop ❌ — trap: joint vs separate ownership. |
| 10 | **'A number of' + plural verb**, but **'The number of' + singular verb**. | **A number of** students **were** absent. / **The number of** students **is** fifty. | Trap: both phrases look identical; "the number" refers to one figure. |
| 11 | Nouns like **cattle, people, police, poultry, gentry, peasantry, clergy** are **plural in meaning** — no 's' added, always plural verb. | The police **have** arrested the thief. | "The police has come" ❌ / "cattles" ❌ — double trap. |
| 12 | **Material and abstract nouns** are not used with 'a/an' and not pluralised — unless they mean a **specific type or instance**. | Gold **is** costly. / He gave me **a paper** (= newspaper). | "He gave me an advice" ❌; but "a kindness" (an act of kindness) is correct — trap: over-applying the rule. |

## (b) Time Management — 15/45-Second Rule

| Type | What it looks like | Time | Strategy |
|------|-------------------|------|----------|
| **Fact-based (15 sec)** | Direct rule recall: plural forms, countable/uncountable, apostrophe position, singular-looking plurals (news, physics) | ≤ 15 sec | Spot the keyword (news, furniture, police, a number of) → apply the memorised rule → mark and move. Never re-read. |
| **Inference-based (45 sec)** | Collective noun verb agreement (unit vs members), joint vs separate possession, abstract noun used concretely, error hidden inside a long intervening phrase | ≤ 45 sec | Strip the sentence to subject + verb, ignore intervening phrases ("The quality of the apples **was**…"), then decide. If still unsure after 45 sec, mark for review and skip. |

**Rule of thumb:** If the answer depends on one memorised rule → 15 seconds. If it depends on analysing the sentence structure → 45 seconds. Anything beyond 45 seconds is a trap question — skip and return.

## (c) Morphology — Roots & Affixes that Build Nouns

| Root / Affix | Meaning | Noun Formed | Example Sentence |
|--------------|---------|-------------|------------------|
| **-tion / -sion** (suffix) | act or state of | decide → deci**sion**; educate → educa**tion** | His decision was final. |
| **-ness** (suffix) | state or quality | kind → kind**ness**; dark → dark**ness** | Her kindness is known to all. |
| **-ity / -ty** (suffix) | quality or state | honest → honest**y**; cruel → cruel**ty** | Honesty is the best policy. |
| **-ment** (suffix) | result or action | govern → govern**ment**; judge → judge**ment** | The government announced a scheme. |
| **-er / -or / -ar** (suffix) | one who does | teach → teach**er**; act → act**or**; beg → begg**ar** | The actor received an award. |
| **-ist** (suffix) | person who practises | art → art**ist**; science → scient**ist** | The scientist won a prize. |
| **-dom** (suffix) | state or realm | free → free**dom**; king → king**dom** | Freedom is our birthright. |
| **-ship** (suffix) | state, skill, office | friend → friend**ship**; leader → leader**ship** | Their friendship is strong. |
| **-age** (suffix) | action, result, collection | marry → marri**age**; post → post**age** | Their marriage lasted fifty years. |
| **-al** (suffix) | act of | arrive → arriv**al**; refuse → refus**al** | His arrival was unexpected. |
| **un- / in- / dis-** (prefix) | negation | un**happiness**; in**justice**; dis**honesty** | Injustice anywhere is a threat everywhere. |

**Exam tip:** In error-spotting questions, check whether the noun formed by a suffix is **countable or uncountable** — e.g., *information* (-tion) is uncountable, but *decision* (-tion) is countable ("a decision"). The suffix does not decide countability; usage does.
