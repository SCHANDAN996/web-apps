## 1. What Is a Noun?

A **noun** is a word used as the name of a person, place, animal, thing, quality, action, or idea.

- Person: *teacher, Ashoka, doctor*
- Place: *Delhi, river, market*
- Thing: *book, pen, honesty* (yes — a quality is also a "thing" we name)
- Idea/Action: *freedom, arrival, growth*

**The five kinds of nouns:**

| Kind | Meaning | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| Proper Noun | Special name of one particular person/place | *Ganga, Mumbai, Kalidas* |
| Common Noun | General name of a class | *river, city, poet* |
| Collective Noun | Name of a group seen as one unit | *army, jury, flock, committee* |
| Material Noun | Name of matter/substance | *gold, water, cotton, rice* |
| Abstract Noun | Name of quality, state, or action | *honesty, childhood, bravery* |

🧠 **Active Recall:** Without looking back — which kind of noun is *team*? Which kind is *kindness*? Say the five kinds aloud in order.

## 2. Number — Where Most Errors Live

Nouns have two numbers: **singular** and **plural**. The exam does not test *boy–boys*; it tests the exceptions.

**Regular plurals:** add *-s* or *-es* (boy–boys, box–boxes, church–churches).

**Key irregular plurals:**

| Singular | Plural |
|---|---|
| child | children |
| man / woman | men / women |
| foot / tooth / goose | feet / teeth / geese |
| mouse / louse | mice / lice |
| ox | oxen |
| datum / medium | data / media |
| criterion / phenomenon | criteria / phenomena |
| analysis / crisis / thesis | analyses / crises / theses |
| formula | formulae / formulas |

**Nouns that look plural but are singular (take a singular verb):**
*news, physics, mathematics, economics, politics, ethics, innings, mumps, measles, billiards, gallows, summons.*

> The news **is** good. Mathematics **is** difficult.

**Nouns always plural (take a plural verb; never use *a/an* before them):**
*scissors, trousers, spectacles, goggles, pliers, tongs, bellows, wages, premises, thanks, proceeds, riches, alms, cattle, people, police, poultry, gentry, clergy, peasantry.*

> The cattle **are** grazing. His trousers **are** new.

**Nouns with the same form in singular and plural:**
*sheep, deer, fish, swine, aircraft, series, species, dozen, score, hundred* (when preceded by a numeral: *two hundred rupees*, but *hundreds of people*).

**Uncountable nouns — never plural, never with *a/an*, take singular verb:**
*information, furniture, luggage, baggage, advice, news, work, homework, knowledge, equipment, bread, scenery, poetry, machinery, stationery, crockery.*

> He gave me **some advice** (not *an advice*). The furniture **is** new.

🧠 **Active Recall:** Is *information* countable? What is the plural of *crisis*? Does *news* take *is* or *are*?

## 3. Gender

Four genders: **Masculine** (king), **Feminine** (queen), **Common** (teacher, student, friend — either sex), **Neuter** (table, book — no sex).

Common masculine–feminine pairs tested in exams:

| Masculine | Feminine |
|---|---|
| bachelor | spinster |
| bull | cow |
| horse | mare |
| fox | vixen |
| nephew | niece |
| wizard | witch |
| host | hostess |
| poet | poetess |
| landlord | landlady |
| monk | nun |

**Convention:** Objects of great strength or beauty are sometimes personified — *the Sun, Death, War* as masculine; *the Moon, Nature, Mercy, a ship, a country* as feminine (*India loves her children*).

## 4. Case — The Apostrophe Rules

**Possessive case** shows ownership.

- Singular noun: add **'s** → *the boy's book*, *Ramesh's pen*.
- Plural ending in *-s*: add only **'** → *the boys' school*, *the girls' hostel*.
- Plural not ending in *-s*: add **'s** → *children's toys*, *men's shoes*.
- Names ending in *-s*: *Keats' poems* or *Keats's poems* (both accepted).
- Joint possession: apostrophe on the **last** name → *Ram and Shyam's house* (one house). Separate possession: apostrophe on **each** → *Ram's and Shyam's books* (different books).

**Rule:** The apostrophe is normally used with **living things** and **personified/time/space expressions** (*a day's work, a stone's throw, at arm's length, for heaven's sake*). For lifeless things, prefer **of**: *the leg of the table* (not *the table's leg*).

🧠 **Active Recall:** One house owned by two brothers — where does the apostrophe go? How do you write "the toys of the children" with an apostrophe?

## 5. Collective Nouns — Singular or Plural?

A collective noun takes a **singular verb** when the group acts as one unit, and a **plural verb** when members act separately.

> The jury **has** given its verdict. (one decision)
> The jury **are** divided in their opinion. (members disagree)

> The team **is** playing well. / The team **are** changing their jerseys.

---

## 6. Nouns Used as Different Parts of Speech

Some words change meaning with number:

| Singular meaning | Plural meaning |
|---|---|
| air (atmosphere) | airs (pretentious manners) |
| good (benefit) | goods (merchandise) |
| iron (metal) | irons (fetters) |
| force | forces (army) |
| custom (habit) | customs (import duty) |
| manner (way) | manners (behaviour) |
| pain | pains (efforts — *take pains*) |
| quarter (one-fourth) | quarters (lodgings) |
| work (task) | works (factory / writings) |

🧠 **Active Recall:** What does *goods* mean? What is the difference between *manner* and *manners*?

## 🧩 Mnemonic — "SIT-PC"

For nouns that are **always plural**, remember **SIT-PC**:
**S**cissors, **I**nstruments of cutting/gripping (*pliers, tongs, shears*), **T**rousers — **P**olice, **C**attle.
If it cuts or grips, you wear it on the legs, or it is a group of living beings → plural verb, no *a/an*.

## ⚠️ EXAMINER'S TRAP BOX

**Trap 1 — The Sound-Right Trap:** *"He gave me many advices."* Sounds fine to a Hindi speaker (*उसने मुझे बहुत सलाहें दीं*), but *advice* is uncountable → **much advice / some advice / a piece of advice**.

**Trap 2 — The Intervening Phrase Trap:** *"The quality of the apples **were** good."* The subject is *quality* (singular), not *apples* → **was**. Examiners insert a plural noun just before the verb to fool your ear.

**Trap 3 — The False Plural Trap:** *"The news are shocking."* *News* ends in *-s* but is singular → **is**. Same family: *physics, mathematics, politics, athletics*.

## 🏆 12 GOLDEN RULES OF NOUNS

1. Uncountable nouns (*advice, information, furniture, luggage, work, knowledge*) — no plural, no *a/an*, singular verb.
2. *News, physics, mathematics, politics, measles, innings* — singular despite the *-s*.
3. *Scissors, trousers, spectacles, cattle, police, people* — always plural.
4. *Sheep, deer, fish, series, species, aircraft* — same form both numbers.
5. *Hundred, thousand, dozen, score* — no *-s* after a numeral (*five hundred*), but *-s* with *of* (*hundreds of*).
6. Collective noun as one unit → singular verb; members acting separately → plural verb.
7. Apostrophe for living beings; **of**-phrase for lifeless things (*the roof of the house*).
8. Joint possession → apostrophe on the last name only.
9. *One of the / each of the / many a* + plural noun, but **singular verb** (*One of the boys was absent*).
10. *A number of* + plural verb; *The number of* + singular verb.
11. Words like *air/airs, good/goods, custom/customs* change meaning with number — check the sentence.
12. After *many, few, several, these, those* → plural countable noun; after *much, little* → uncountable noun.

## 🔁 LINGUISTIC BRIDGE — Hindi vs. English

| Hindi speaker says ❌ | Correct English ✅ | Why |
|---|---|---|
| He gave me an advice. | He gave me **some advice / a piece of advice**. | *Advice* is uncountable; Hindi "एक सलाह" misleads. |
| I have many works to do. | I have **a lot of work** to do. | *Work* (task) is uncountable; *works* = factory/writings. |
| The news are good. | The news **is** good. | *News* is singular. |
| My all luggages were stolen. | **All my luggage** was stolen. | *Luggage* has no plural. |
| The police has arrested him. | The police **have** arrested him. | *Police* is always plural. |
| He is a man of few words but many knowledges. | …but great **knowledge**. | *Knowledge* is uncountable. |
