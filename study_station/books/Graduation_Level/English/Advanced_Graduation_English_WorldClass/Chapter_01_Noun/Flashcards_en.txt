Card 1
Front: What is a noun? Name its five main types.
Back: A noun is a word that names a person, place, thing, quality, or idea. Types: Proper, Common, Collective, Material, Abstract.

Card 2
Front: Which nouns are always singular and take a singular verb? (Mnemonic)
Back: Uncountable nouns — remember "RAIL-BAG": Rice, Advice, Information, Luggage, Bread, Accommodation, Furniture (F), News. They never take 'a/an' or plural '-s'.

Card 3
Front: "The news are shocking." — Correct or incorrect? Why?
Back: Incorrect. 'News' looks plural but is uncountable and singular in use: "The news is shocking." Same with physics, mathematics, politics, measles.

Card 4
Front: EXAMINER'S TRAP: "One of my friends have come." — Spot the error.
Back: Error is 'have'. After "one of + plural noun", the verb agrees with 'one' (singular): "One of my friends has come." The plural noun in between is a decoy.

Card 5
Front: When does a collective noun take a plural verb?
Back: When the members of the group act individually, not as one unit. "The jury are divided in their opinion" (divided) vs "The jury has given its verdict" (united).

Card 6
Front: Which nouns have the same form in singular and plural?
Back: Sheep, deer, fish, aircraft, species, series, headquarters. "One sheep is grazing; ten sheep are grazing."

Card 7
Front: Which nouns are always plural and take a plural verb?
Back: Scissors, trousers, spectacles, goggles, tongs, wages, premises, thanks, earnings, belongings. Use "a pair of scissors is…" for singular sense.

Card 8
Front: "He gave me many advices." — Correct or incorrect?
Back: Incorrect. 'Advice' is uncountable; it has no plural. Say "He gave me much advice" or "many pieces of advice."

Card 9
Front: What is the rule for possessive of plural nouns ending in -s?
Back: Add only an apostrophe, not another 's': boys' school, girls' hostel. But irregular plurals take 's: children's toys, men's shoes.

Card 10
Front: Whose book is it in "Ravi and Mohan's book" vs "Ravi's and Mohan's books"?
Back: "Ravi and Mohan's book" = one book owned jointly. "Ravi's and Mohan's books" = separate books, one belonging to each.

Card 11
Front: EXAMINER'S TRAP: "The furniture in this room are new." — Spot the error.
Back: 'Furniture' is uncountable and singular: "The furniture in this room is new." Examiners pair it with a plural-looking context to lure you.

Card 12
Front: Which nouns are used only in the singular even though they end in -s?
Back: News, physics, mathematics, economics (subject), politics (subject), measles, mumps, billiards, athletics.

Card 13
Front: "A number of students ___ absent" vs "The number of students ___ increasing." Fill in the verbs.
Back: "A number of students are absent" (a number of = many, plural verb). "The number of students is increasing" (the number = the figure, singular verb).

Card 14
Front: Mnemonic: How to remember nouns that take singular verbs despite plural form?
Back: "M-N-P Games": Mathematics, News, Physics + games/diseases (billiards, measles) — all take singular verbs. "Looks plural, acts singular."

Card 15
Front: What is an abstract noun? Give three examples formed from adjectives.
Back: A noun naming a quality, state, or idea that cannot be touched. Kind → kindness, honest → honesty, brave → bravery.

Card 16
Front: "The cattle is grazing in the field." — Correct or incorrect?
Back: Incorrect. 'Cattle' is a plural noun (no singular form in this sense): "The cattle are grazing." Same with people, police, poultry, gentry.

Card 17
Front: When do we use 'fewer' and when 'less'?
Back: 'Fewer' with countable nouns (fewer students), 'less' with uncountable nouns (less water). Trap: "less people" is wrong — say "fewer people."

Card 18
Front: What is the correct plural of 'brother-in-law', 'passer-by', 'commander-in-chief'?
Back: The main word takes the plural: brothers-in-law, passers-by, commanders-in-chief.

Card 19
Front: "Mathematics are a difficult subject." — Spot and correct the error.
Back: 'Mathematics' (the subject) takes a singular verb: "Mathematics is a difficult subject." (Note: when it means 'calculations', it can be plural: "His mathematics are weak.")

Card 20
Front: Which is correct — "two dozens eggs" or "two dozen eggs"?
Back: "Two dozen eggs." After a definite number, dozen/score/hundred/thousand stay singular: two hundred rupees, three score years. But indefinite: "dozens of eggs."
