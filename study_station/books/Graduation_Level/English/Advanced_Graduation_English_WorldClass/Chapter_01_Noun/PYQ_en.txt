# 📊 PYQ Analysis — Noun (Pattern-Based)

## How Exams Test Nouns

Across SSC (CGL, CHSL, MTS, GD), Banking (IBPS PO/Clerk, SBI), Railways (NTPC, Group D) and State PCS prelims, Noun questions appear in three recurring formats:

1. **Error Spotting** — the most frequent. The noun error hides inside a long sentence, usually in subject–verb agreement or wrong plural form.
2. **Fill in the Blanks** — choosing the correct noun form (singular/plural, countable/uncountable).
3. **Sentence Improvement** — replacing an underlined noun phrase with the grammatically correct option.

## Sub-topics That Repeat Most Often (in words, not numbers)

- **Uncountable nouns wrongly pluralised**: *informations, furnitures, advices, luggages, sceneries, poetries* — the single most repeated trap.
- **Nouns same in singular and plural**: *sheep, deer, aircraft, series, species, means, headquarters*.
- **Nouns always plural**: *scissors, trousers, spectacles, earnings, premises, outskirts, alms, thanks*.
- **Nouns plural in form but singular in use**: *news, physics, mathematics, measles, billiards, innings*.
- **Collective noun agreement**: team/jury/committee/family — singular verb when acting as one unit, plural when members act separately.
- **Possessive case errors**: *children's toys* (not *childrens'*), *somebody else's*, apostrophe with time expressions (*a day's work*).
- **Compound noun plurals**: *brothers-in-law, passers-by, commanders-in-chief* (plural on the main word).
- **Number + noun as adjective**: *a five-year plan* (not *five-years plan*), *a ten-rupee note*.

## Examiner's Trap Types

| Trap Type | What it looks like |
|---|---|
| **Intervening Phrase** | "The quality of the apples *were* good." — the verb must agree with *quality*, not *apples*. |
| **Collective Noun** | "The jury *has* given its verdict" vs "The jury *are* divided" — both can be right; context decides. |
| **Sound-Right** | "He gave me many advices" *sounds* fine to Hindi speakers ("उसने मुझे बहुत सलाहें दीं") but is wrong. |
| **Over-thinking** | A simple, correct sentence like "The news is good" is doubted because *news* ends in -s. |

## Top 10 High-Yield Questions (PYQ-style) with Solutions

**Q1.** One of my friend / (a) is going / (b) to Delhi tomorrow. / (c) No error (d)
**Answer:** (a) — should be "One of my **friends**"
**Solution:** After "one of", the noun must be plural (one out of many), though the verb stays singular.
**Trap type:** Sound-Right | **Source:** PYQ-style

**Q2.** He gave me many informations about the case.
(a) many information (b) much information (c) many informations (d) much informations
**Answer:** (b)
**Solution:** *Information* is uncountable — no plural form, and it takes *much*, not *many*.
**Trap type:** Sound-Right | **Source:** PYQ-style

**Q3.** The furniture in this room / (a) are / (b) very old. / (c) No error (d)
**Answer:** (b) — should be "**is**"
**Solution:** *Furniture* is uncountable and always takes a singular verb.
**Trap type:** Sound-Right | **Source:** PYQ-style

**Q4.** The committee has taken / (a) its decision / (b) unanimously. / (c) No error (d)
**Answer:** (d) No error
**Solution:** The committee acts as one body ("unanimously"), so singular *has/its* is correct. Don't change it — this is the over-thinking trap.
**Trap type:** Over-thinking | **Source:** PYQ-style

**Q5.** She bought two dozens eggs from the market.
(a) two dozen eggs (b) two dozens of eggs (c) two dozen of eggs (d) No improvement
**Answer:** (a)
**Solution:** After a definite number, *dozen/score/hundred* stay singular: *two dozen eggs*.
**Trap type:** Sound-Right | **Source:** PYQ-style

**Q6.** The sceneries of Kashmir / (a) are / (b) very beautiful. / (c) No error (d)
**Answer:** (a) — should be "The **scenery** of Kashmir"
**Solution:** *Scenery* is uncountable; it has no plural form. The verb *are* then correctly refers to beauty of the scenery — wait, with *scenery* the verb becomes *is*: so the full correction is "The scenery of Kashmir is very beautiful."
**Trap type:** Sound-Right | **Source:** PYQ-style

**Q7.** Mathematics / (a) are / (b) a difficult subject. / (c) No error (d)
**Answer:** (b) — should be "**is**"
**Solution:** Subjects ending in -ics (mathematics, physics, economics) take a singular verb when meaning the subject of study.
**Trap type:** Over-thinking (the -s ending misleads) | **Source:** PYQ-style

**Q8.** All the *managements* were present at the meeting.
(a) management (b) manager (c) managers (d) No improvement
**Answer:** (a)
**Solution:** *Management* as a collective body is singular/uncountable here; "all the management was present" is the intended form.
**Trap type:** Collective Noun | **Source:** PYQ-style

**Q9.** He is one of the best player / (a) in the team / (b) this season. / (c) No error (d)
**Answer:** (a) — should be "best **players**"
**Solution:** "One of the + superlative + plural noun" is a fixed structure.
**Trap type:** Sound-Right | **Source:** PYQ-style

**Q10.** The cattle / (a) is / (b) grazing in the field. / (c) No error (d)
**Answer:** (b) — should be "**are**"
**Solution:** *Cattle* is always plural (like *people, police, poultry*). There is no singular "cattle".
**Trap type:** Sound-Right | **Source:** PYQ-style

## Applying the 15/45-Second Rule

**15-second question (Fact-based):** Q7 — "Mathematics are a difficult subject." You know the rule: -ics subjects take singular verbs. Spot, mark, move on. No re-reading needed.

**45-second question (Inference-based):** Q4 — "The committee has taken its decision unanimously." Here you must *reason*: is the committee acting as one unit or as divided members? The word *unanimously* signals unity → singular is correct → "No error". Spending 45 seconds here is an investment, not a waste; guessing in 15 seconds would likely make you "correct" a correct sentence.

**Strategy summary:** If the noun error is a fixed rule (uncountable, -ics, dozen, one of + plural), answer in 15 seconds. If it involves collective nouns, intervening phrases, or a sentence that "sounds wrong but might be right", give it the full 45 seconds.
