26. Which of the following is a proper noun?
(a) city (b) river (c) Ganga (d) mountain
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Ganga" names a specific river, so it is a proper noun and is capitalised. "City", "river" and "mountain" are common nouns.
Source: NCERT Class 6 English Grammar

27. Identify the abstract noun in the sentence: "Honesty is the best policy."
(a) Honesty (b) best (c) policy (d) is
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Honesty" is a quality that cannot be seen or touched, so it is an abstract noun. "Policy" here is a common noun.
Source: NCERT Class 7 English Grammar

28. The plural of "child" is:
(a) children (b) childes (c) childs (d) childrens
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Child" has an irregular plural — "children". "Childrens" is doubly wrong because "children" is already plural.
Source: NCERT Class 6 English Grammar

29. Which word is a collective noun?
(a) soldiers (b) fight (c) brave (d) army
Answer: (d)
Solution: "Army" denotes a group of soldiers taken as one unit — a collective noun. "Soldiers" is an ordinary plural common noun.
Source: NCERT Class 7 English Grammar

30. The plural of "knife" is:
(a) knifes (b) knivs (c) knives (d) knife
Answer: (c)
Solution: Nouns ending in -fe often change to -ves: knife → knives, wife → wives, life → lives.
Source: NCERT Class 6 English Grammar

31. Which of the following is an uncountable noun?
(a) book (b) chair (c) water (d) pen
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Water" is a material noun and cannot be counted; we say "some water", not "a water" or "two waters".
Source: NCERT Class 7 English Grammar

32. Identify the material noun: "The ring is made of gold."
(a) ring (b) gold (c) made (d) the
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Gold" is a substance/material, hence a material noun. "Ring" is a common noun (a countable thing).
Source: NCERT Class 7 English Grammar

33. The plural of "ox" is:
(a) oxes (b) oxen (c) ox (d) oxies
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Ox" has an old irregular plural "oxen", like child → children.
Source: NCERT Class 6 English Grammar

34. Which sentence uses a noun as the subject?
(a) She runs fast. (b) Delhi is a big city. (c) He gave her quickly. (d) They sang sweetly.
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Delhi" (a proper noun) is the subject of the verb "is". In (a), (c) and (d) the subjects are pronouns (She, He, They).
Source: NCERT Class 7 English Grammar

35. The plural of "tooth" is:
(a) tooths (b) tooth (c) toothes (d) teeth
Answer: (d)
Solution: "Tooth → teeth" is an irregular vowel-change plural, like foot → feet, goose → geese.
Source: NCERT Class 6 English Grammar

36. "Kindness" is what kind of noun?
(a) Abstract noun (b) Common noun (c) Proper noun (d) Collective noun
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Kindness" is a quality we can feel but not touch — an abstract noun formed from the adjective "kind".
Source: NCERT Class 7 English Grammar

37. Which of these nouns is always plural in form?
(a) news (b) scissors (c) mathematics (d) physics
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Scissors" (like trousers, spectacles) exists only in plural form and takes a plural verb. "News", "mathematics" and "physics" look plural but take singular verbs.
Source: NCERT Class 8 English Grammar

38. The plural of "phenomenon" is:
(a) phenomenons (b) phenomenon (c) phenomenas (d) phenomena
Answer: (d)
Solution: "Phenomenon" is a Greek word; its plural is "phenomena". Similarly: criterion → criteria.
Source: NCERT Class 9 English Grammar

39. In "A flock of sheep was grazing," the collective noun is:
(a) sheep (b) grazing (c) a (d) flock
Answer: (d)
Solution: "Flock" is the collective noun for a group of sheep. "Sheep" is a common noun (same in singular and plural).
Source: NCERT Class 7 English Grammar

40. Which is a countable noun?
(a) milk (b) furniture (c) information (d) apple
Answer: (d)
Solution: "Apple" can be counted — one apple, two apples. Milk, furniture and information are uncountable and have no plural form.
Source: NCERT Class 7 English Grammar

41. Choose the correct sentence:
(a) He gave me many advices. (b) He gave me many advice. (c) He gave me much advice. (d) He gave me an advice.
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Advice" is uncountable, so it takes "much", never "many", "an" or a plural -s. The countable equivalent is "a piece of advice".
Source: PYQ-style

42. Choose the correctly spelt plural:
(a) mosquitos (b) mosquitoes (c) mosquittoes (d) mosquitos'
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Mosquito" takes -es in the plural: mosquitoes. (Some -o nouns take only -s, e.g., photos, pianos — these must be memorised.)
Source: NCERT Class 8 English Grammar

43. "The furniture in this room ___ very old."
(a) are (b) is (c) were (d) have been
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Furniture" is an uncountable noun and always takes a singular verb, even though it refers to many items. (Examiner's trap: "are" sounds right because we picture many chairs and tables.)
Source: PYQ-style

44. Which sentence is correct?
(a) The news is shocking. (b) The news were shocking. (c) The news are shocking. (d) The news have shocked.
Answer: (a)
Solution: "News" ends in -s but is singular and takes a singular verb. This is a classic examiner's trap — the -s ending tempts students to choose a plural verb.
Source: PYQ-style

45. "One of my friends ___ coming to the party."
(a) are (b) were (c) is (d) have
Answer: (c)
Solution: The subject is "One" (singular), not "friends"; the phrase "of my friends" only intervenes. So the verb is singular — "is". (Trap type: Intervening Phrase.)
Source: PYQ-style

46. The plural of "analysis" is:
(a) analysises (b) analyses (c) analysies (d) analysis
Answer: (b)
Solution: Greek-origin nouns ending in -is change to -es: analysis → analyses, crisis → crises, thesis → theses.
Source: NCERT Class 9 English Grammar

47. Choose the correct sentence:
(a) I need some informations. (b) I need an information. (c) I need some information. (d) I need many informations.
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Information" is uncountable — no plural, no "a/an". Say "some information" or "a piece of information".
Source: PYQ-style

48. "The committee ___ divided in their opinion."
(a) was (b) is (c) has (d) does
Answer: (a)
Solution: When a collective noun's members act separately (divided opinion), it takes a plural verb — and "their" confirms plural sense. If the committee acted as one unit, we would say "The committee has decided."
Source: NCERT Class 9 English Grammar

49. Which pair of singular–plural is correct?
(a) datum – datums (b) medium – mediums (for mass communication) (c) index – indexs (d) formula – formulae (scientific use)
Answer: (d)
Solution: In scientific contexts "formula → formulae" is correct. "Datum → data", and for mass communication "medium → media"; "indexs" is wrong (indexes/indices).
Source: NCERT Class 9 English Grammar

50. "Neither the teacher nor the students ___ present."
(a) were (b) is (c) was (d) has been
Answer: (a)
Solution: With "neither…nor", the verb agrees with the nearer subject — "students" (plural) — so "were". (Examiner's trap: choosing "was" by agreeing with "teacher".)
Source: PYQ-style
