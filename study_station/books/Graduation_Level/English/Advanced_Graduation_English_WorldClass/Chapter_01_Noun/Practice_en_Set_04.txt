76. The number of students absent from class ___ increasing every month.
(a) are (b) is (c) were (d) have been
Answer: (b)
Solution: "The number of" takes a singular verb, so "is" is correct. "A number of" would take a plural verb — examiners love this contrast.
Source: NCERT Class 10 English Grammar

77. He gave me many useful ___ for the interview.
(a) advices (b) advise (c) pieces of advices (d) advice
Answer: (d)
Solution: "Advice" is uncountable and has no plural form. Use "pieces of advice" if counting is needed; "advise" is the verb.
Source: PYQ-style

78. The ___ of the building were damaged in the fire.
(a) roofs (b) roof (c) rooves (d) roofes
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Roof" forms its plural regularly as "roofs" (not "rooves"). Words like chief, proof, belief also take only -s.
Source: NCERT Class 9 English Grammar

79. Neither the manager nor the employees ___ aware of the new policy.
(a) was (b) is (c) were (d) has been
Answer: (c)
Solution: With "neither…nor", the verb agrees with the nearer subject ("employees" — plural), so "were". The trap is agreeing with "manager".
Source: PYQ-style

80. She bought two ___ of bread from the bakery.
(a) loafs (b) loaves (c) loafes (d) loaf
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Loaf" changes -f to -ves in the plural: "loaves". Like wife–wives, knife–knives.
Source: NCERT Class 8 English Grammar

81. The committee ___ divided in their opinions on the issue.
(a) were (b) was (c) is (d) has been
Answer: (a)
Solution: When a collective noun's members act individually (divided opinions), use a plural verb — "were". If acting as one unit, it would be "was".
Source: PYQ-style

82. I need some ___ about the admission procedure.
(a) informations (b) pieces of informations (c) information (d) inform
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Information" is uncountable; it never takes -s. Say "some information" or "a piece of information".
Source: NCERT Class 10 English Grammar

83. The ___ of the two brothers is remarkable.
(a) similarity (b) similar (c) similarly (d) similars
Answer: (a)
Solution: The sentence needs a noun as subject after "The"; "similarity" is the noun form. "Similar" is an adjective, "similarly" an adverb.
Source: PYQ-style

84. Mathematics ___ a difficult subject for many students.
(a) are (b) were (c) is (d) have been
Answer: (c)
Solution: Subjects ending in -ics (Mathematics, Physics, Economics) take a singular verb when meaning the subject of study.
Source: NCERT Class 10 English Grammar

85. The police ___ arrested the thief.
(a) has (b) is (c) have (d) was
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Police" is a plural noun and always takes a plural verb — "have". There is no singular "police"; use "policeman" for one person.
Source: PYQ-style

86. He has a large collection of ancient ___.
(a) coin (b) coin's (c) coines (d) coins
Answer: (d)
Solution: "A collection of" is followed by a plural countable noun — "coins". The apostrophe form (coin's) shows possession, not plurality.
Source: NCERT Class 8 English Grammar

87. The furniture in this room ___ very old.
(a) are (b) were (c) is (d) have been
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Furniture" is uncountable and always singular — "is". Never say "furnitures"; use "pieces of furniture" to count.
Source: PYQ-style

88. One of my friends ___ going to Delhi tomorrow.
(a) is (b) are (c) were (d) have been
Answer: (a)
Solution: The real subject is "One" (singular), not "friends". The intervening phrase "of my friends" is the examiner's trap.
Source: PYQ-style

89. The scissors ___ on the table.
(a) are (b) was (c) is (d) has been
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Scissors" is a plural-only noun (like trousers, spectacles) and takes a plural verb — "are". Use "a pair of scissors is" for singular.
Source: NCERT Class 9 English Grammar

90. His ___ in the company has grown rapidly.
(a) influence (b) influent (c) influentially (d) influences
Answer: (a)
Solution: The possessive "His" must be followed by a noun — "influence". "Influentially" is an adverb; "influent" is not standard here.
Source: PYQ-style

91. Cattle ___ grazing in the field.
(a) is (b) was (c) are (d) has been
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Cattle" is a plural collective noun and always takes a plural verb — "are". It has no singular form.
Source: NCERT Class 9 English Grammar

92. The news ___ really shocking this morning.
(a) were (b) was (c) are (d) have been
Answer: (b)
Solution: Despite ending in -s, "news" is uncountable and singular — "was". A classic sound-right trap.
Source: PYQ-style

93. She showed great ___ during the crisis.
(a) courageous (b) courage (c) courageously (d) courages
Answer: (b)
Solution: After the adjective "great", a noun is required — "courage" (uncountable, no plural). "Courageous" is an adjective.
Source: NCERT Class 10 English Grammar

94. Each of the boys ___ a bicycle.
(a) have (b) were having (c) are having (d) has
Answer: (d)
Solution: "Each of" takes a singular verb — "has". The plural noun "boys" in the intervening phrase is the trap.
Source: PYQ-style

95. The ___ of the accident were taken to hospital.
(a) witnesses (b) witness (c) witness's (d) witnessing
Answer: (a)
Solution: The plural verb "were" demands a plural subject — "witnesses". Nouns ending in -ss form plurals with -es.
Source: NCERT Class 8 English Grammar

96. The poet and philosopher ___ passed away.
(a) have (b) has (c) were (d) are
Answer: (b)
Solution: One article ("The") before two nouns joined by "and" means one person holding both roles — singular verb "has". "The poet and the philosopher" would mean two people. This is a classic examiner's trap.
Source: PYQ-style

97. Many a student ___ failed to understand this rule.
(a) have (b) has (c) are (d) were
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Many a" is followed by a singular noun and singular verb — "has", despite the plural meaning. A favourite trap in SSC exams.
Source: PYQ-style

98. The gentry of this town ___ not interested in politics.
(a) is (b) was (c) has been (d) are
Answer: (d)
Solution: "Gentry" is a plural collective noun (like cattle, peasantry) and takes a plural verb — "are".
Source: PYQ-style

99. Two-thirds of the work ___ completed.
(a) have been (b) were (c) are (d) has been
Answer: (d)
Solution: With fractions, the verb agrees with the noun after "of". "Work" is uncountable/singular, so "has been". If it were "two-thirds of the workers", the verb would be plural.
Source: PYQ-style

100. The Arabian Nights ___ a collection of famous stories.
(a) are (b) were (c) have been (d) is
Answer: (d)
Solution: Titles of books, though plural in form, take a singular verb — "is". The plural-looking title is the sound-right trap.
Source: PYQ-style
