Card 1
Front: What is the first job when you meet a para jumble?
Back: Find the opener — the sentence that introduces the topic and does not lean on any earlier sentence.

Card 2
Front: Why can a sentence starting with "However" never be the opener?
Back: "However" contrasts with something already said, so there must be a sentence before it.

Card 3
Front: "A committee was formed." / "The committee submitted its report." — which comes first?
Back: The "a" sentence. "A" introduces something new; "the" refers back to it.

Card 4
Front: What does the mnemonic OPCO stand for?
Back: Opener → Pairs → Check the options → Order the rest and read once.

Card 5
Front: What does the trick "Who is leaning?" mean?
Back: Ask which earlier sentence each pronoun, "this/these" or connector depends on. The sentence that leans on no one opens the paragraph.

Card 6
Front: Where does an example ("For example, …") usually go?
Back: Right after the general statement it illustrates.

Card 7
Front: "These measures reduced pollution." What must the previous sentence contain?
Back: More than one measure (a plural idea) that "these" can point back to.

Card 8
Front: Which words usually mark the conclusion?
Back: Thus, therefore, hence, in short, finally — a sentence that sums up rather than adds a new detail.

Card 9
Front: How do the options save time?
Back: Once you are sure of one pair (say S must come before Q), strike out every option where Q appears before S.

Card 10
Front: In a question with S1 and S6 fixed, what should you check first?
Back: Which middle sentence follows naturally from the end of S1, and which leads into the start of S6.

Card 11
Front: How do you find the odd sentence out?
Back: Find the common theme of most sentences; the one on a different topic or with a different tone is the odd one.

Card 12
Front: Cause or effect — which comes first in a paragraph?
Back: Usually the cause, then the effect with "as a result", "so", or "consequently".

Card 13
Front: Examiner's trap: a sentence sounds general but begins with "This". Can it open the paragraph?
Back: No. "This" points back to an earlier idea, so it cannot be the first sentence.

Card 14
Front: Examiner's trap: "it" seems to fit two sentences. How do you decide?
Back: Check number and meaning — "it" needs a singular thing that makes sense in that sentence.

Card 15
Front: What is the usual flow of an expository paragraph?
Back: General idea → explanation → example → conclusion.

Card 16
Front: How much time should a 4-sentence jumble take?
Back: About 45 seconds; if no pair is clear by then, skip and return later.

Card 17
Front: Does "also" ever start a paragraph?
Back: No — "also" adds to a point already made.

Card 18
Front: What is the safest final step?
Back: Read the chosen order once from start to end; it should flow like natural speech.
