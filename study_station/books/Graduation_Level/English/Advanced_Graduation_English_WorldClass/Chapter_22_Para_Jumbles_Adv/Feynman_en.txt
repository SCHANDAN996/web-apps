# Feynman Technique — Advanced Para Jumbles

## The Hardest Idea: "Which sentence is pointing back?"

The hardest part of a para jumble is noticing that some sentences cannot stand on their own. They lean on an earlier sentence, like a person leaning on a wall. If you remove the wall, they fall.

## A Story: Aarav's Recipe Cards

Aarav's grandmother wrote her famous kheer recipe on four cards, and the cards got mixed up:

- "Then add the soaked rice and stir."
- "Boil a litre of milk in a heavy pan."
- "Finally, add sugar and cardamom."
- "Let it simmer until it thickens."

Aarav laughed: "The cards are telling me the order!" The card that starts with "Boil" needs nothing before it — it is the start. "Then" leans on something that came before. "Let it simmer" needs rice and milk already in the pan. "Finally" is obviously last.

So the order is: boil milk → then add rice → let it simmer → finally sugar and cardamom.

## The Unforgettable Trick: "Who is leaning?"

For every sentence, ask: **"Who is it leaning on?"**
- "He", "she", "it", "they" lean on a name.
- "This", "these", "such" lean on an idea.
- "However", "therefore", "also", "then" lean on a previous sentence.

The sentence that leans on **no one** is your opener. The one everyone leans towards in the end is your conclusion.
