# PYQ Analysis — Advanced Para Jumbles

## 1. How Often It Appears

Para jumbles (sentence rearrangement) are a regular part of the English section in SSC CGL/CHSL, banking (IBPS/SBI PO and Clerk), CDS, CAPF and management entrance tests. Most papers carry a few questions of this type; bank PO papers often set a block based on one passage. Exact yearly counts vary from paper to paper, so this analysis describes the pattern rather than quoting numbers.

## 2. Sub-topic Weightage (qualitative)

- **Four-part arrangement (P, Q, R, S)** — the most common type in SSC papers.
- **Fixed first and last sentence (S1, S6)** — common in SSC and CDS.
- **Find the first / last sentence after rearrangement** — common in banking papers.
- **Odd sentence out** — management entrance tests and some bank papers.
- **Sentence-part arrangement (within one sentence)** — SSC CHSL and MTS level.

## 3. Exam-wise Tendencies

- **SSC:** short, everyday topics; strong pronoun and connector clues.
- **Banking:** longer passages on economy, banking and technology; questions ask the position of one sentence.
- **CDS/CAPF:** S1–S6 format with general-awareness topics.
- **Management tests:** abstract passages, odd-one-out, no options to lean on.

## 4. Ten High-Yield Questions (PYQ-style, with solutions)

**1.** P: She later became the first Indian woman to win an Olympic medal in boxing. Q: Mary Kom grew up in a farming family in Manipur. R: Her early training began against her father's wishes. S: Despite this, she won her first world title in 2002.
Answer: **QRSP** — Q introduces her by name; R explains how she started; S begins with "Despite this", pointing back to R; P mentions "later" and the Olympic medal.
Hook: P sounds like a strong opener but begins with "She".

**2.** P: As a result, many farmers shifted to drip irrigation. Q: Groundwater levels in the region fell sharply. R: The state then offered subsidies for drip systems. S: Years of flood irrigation had wasted water.
Answer: **SQRP** — cause (S) → effect (Q) → government response (R) → result (P).
Hook: "As a result" tempts students to put P after Q.

**3.** Find the first sentence: P: These apps track daily steps. Q: Fitness apps have become very popular. R: Some also remind users to drink water. S: However, they cannot replace a doctor's advice.
Answer: **Q** — the only sentence with no pointing word.

**4.** S1: The Indian Railways is one of the largest railway networks in the world. P: It carries millions of passengers every day. Q: It also moves huge amounts of freight. R: This makes it a lifeline of the economy. S: Modernisation is therefore a national priority. S6: Several high-speed corridors are planned for the coming years.
Answer: **PQRS** — "also" follows P; "This" sums up P and Q; "therefore" draws the conclusion that leads into S6.

**5.** Odd one out: (A) Bees pollinate many crops. (B) Without bees, fruit and vegetable yields would fall. (C) The honey bee dance shows the direction of flowers. (D) Pesticides are harming bee populations. (E) Cricket is the most watched sport in India.
Answer: **E** — every other sentence is about bees.

**6–10** follow the same five patterns in the practice sets: pronoun pairs, cause → effect, opener test, fixed ends, and odd-one-out.

## 5. Skip Guidance (15-second / 45-second rule)

- Question 5 (odd one out) should take 15 seconds once the theme is clear — if not, skip.
- A six-sentence arrangement where no pair is clear in 45 seconds should be skipped in the first round.

## 6. Examiner's Traps Seen Again and Again

1. The false opener beginning with "This" or "Such".
2. A pronoun that seems to fit two sentences.
3. An example dressed up as the conclusion.
