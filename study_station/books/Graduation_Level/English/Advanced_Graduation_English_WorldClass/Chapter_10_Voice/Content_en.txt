# Active & Passive Voice — Mastering the Switch

## 🎯 The Hook

Many candidates lose easy marks in the English section not because of difficult questions, but because of one small slip in Voice — writing *"The documents have been submitted by me yesterday"* or choosing an option that drops the word *being*. Voice is not just a grammar topic — it is the difference between sounding like a textbook and sounding like the language itself. And in SSC CGL, IBPS PO, and State PCS papers, Voice questions are among the most **predictable, rule-bound, and scoreable** items in the entire English section. Master the rules once, and these marks are yours forever.

## 🎯 Learning Objectives

By the end of this chapter, you will be able to:

1. Convert any active sentence into passive (and back) across **all tenses that have passive forms** — the eight tenses (present/past/future simple, continuous, and perfect, plus future perfect), modals, infinitives, and imperatives.
2. Instantly identify sentences that **cannot** be passivised (intransitive verbs, stative verbs).
3. Spot the examiner's favourite traps: preposition retention, "by whom" questions, double-passive errors, and quasi-passives.
4. Answer Voice questions in under 20 seconds using the **Object-First Test**.

---

## 1. What Is Voice?

**Voice** tells you whether the subject of the sentence *does* the action or *receives* it.

- **Active Voice:** The subject performs the action.
  → *The committee **approved** the proposal.* (Committee = doer)
- **Passive Voice:** The subject receives the action.
  → *The proposal **was approved** (by the committee).* (Proposal = receiver)

The magic formula of every passive sentence in English:

> **Object of active → becomes Subject + appropriate form of "be" + V3 (past participle) (+ by + doer)**

The verb **"be"** carries the tense; the main verb is frozen forever in its **third form (V3)**. This single insight solves most Voice questions.

🧠 **Active Recall:** In the sentence *"The letter was written by her,"* which word carries the tense, and which word is frozen in V3? *(Answer: "was" carries past tense; "written" is V3.)*

---

## 2. The Conversion Engine — Tense by Tense

Only the **"be"** verb changes with tense. Memorise this table — it is the heart of the chapter.

| Tense | Active | Passive ("be" form) |
|---|---|---|
| Present Simple | writes | **is/am/are** written |
| Present Continuous | is writing | is **being** written |
| Present Perfect | has written | has **been** written |
| Past Simple | wrote | **was/were** written |
| Past Continuous | was writing | was **being** written |
| Past Perfect | had written | had **been** written |
| Future Simple | will write | will **be** written |
| Future Perfect | will have written | will have **been** written |
| Modals | can/must/should write | can/must/should **be** written |
| Modal Perfect | should have written | should have **been** written |

⚠️ **No passive exists** for the Perfect Continuous tenses (has been writing) or Future Continuous (will be writing) in standard exam English. If an option offers *"is being been written"* — reject it instantly.

🧠 **Active Recall:** Convert: *"She has completed the project."* → *(The project has been completed by her.)* Which three words form the passive verb? *(has + been + completed)*

---

## 3. Sentences That Refuse to Become Passive

**Rule:** Only **transitive verbs** (verbs that take an object) can be passivised.

- ❌ *He sleeps early.* → No object → no passive possible.
- ❌ *The sun rises in the east.* → Intransitive → no passive.
- **Stative verbs** (know, believe, love, own, resemble, contain) are rarely passivised in the normal way — but note exam favourites:
  - *Everyone knows him.* → *He is known to everyone.* (✅ "known **to**", not "known by")

**Preposition Retention Trap:** When a verb has a fixed preposition, the preposition **survives** the conversion:
- *They laughed at the beggar.* → *The beggar was laughed **at**.*
- *She looks after the children.* → *The children are looked **after**.*

🧠 **Active Recall:** Why can't *"The baby cried loudly"* be passivised? *(Cried is intransitive — no object to promote to subject.)*

---

## 4. Special Structures the Examiner Loves

**(a) Imperatives:** Use *Let + object + be + V3*.
- *Open the door.* → *Let the door be opened.*
- Negative: *Do not touch it.* → *Let it not be touched.*

**(b) Infinitives:** *to + be + V3*
- *I want someone to help me.* → *I want to be helped.*

**(c) Questions with "Who":**
- *Who taught you?* → *By whom were you taught?* (formal) / *Who were you taught by?*

**(d) Double objects:** Either object can become the subject.
- *She gave me a book.* → *I was given a book.* / *A book was given to me.*

**(e) Reporting verbs:** *People say that he is honest.*
→ *It is said that he is honest.* / *He is said to be honest.*

---

## 🧩 Mnemonic: "**B-B-V3**" — *"Be Brave, Use V3"*

Every passive = **B**e (in the right tense) + **V3**. If an option lacks "be" or lacks V3, eliminate it in 3 seconds.

---

## ⚠️ Examiner's Trap Box

1. **The Missing "Being":** *"The work is done"* vs *"The work is being done"* — continuous passive MUST have *being*. Options that drop it are wrong.
2. **The Dropped Preposition:** *"He was laughed"* ❌ → *"He was laughed at"* ✅. Examiners delete the preposition to tempt you.
3. **The Over-Passive:** *"The accident was happened"* ❌ — *happen* is intransitive; it can never be passive. Sound-right traps like this appear frequently in exams.

## 🔁 Linguistic Bridge — Hindi vs English

| Hindi speakers often say | Correct English |
|---|---|
| "The work has been done by me yesterday." (mixing perfect with a past time word) | "The work **was done** by me yesterday." |
| "He is knowing the answer." | "He **knows** the answer." (stative verb — no continuous) |
| "By whom this letter was written?" | "**By whom was** this letter written?" (invert in questions) |
| "It is said that he is a thief by people." | "**It is said that** he is a thief." (drop the unnecessary agent) |

In Hindi, *"मुझसे यह नहीं किया जा सकता"* translates naturally to the passive *"This cannot be done by me"* — but English prefers the active *"I cannot do this."* When in doubt in sentence-improvement questions, prefer the cleaner active unless the doer is unknown or unimportant.

## 📊 Summary Table

| Situation | Passive Formula | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Normal transitive verb | be + V3 | The cake was eaten. |
| Continuous tense | be + being + V3 | The cake is being eaten. |
| Perfect tense | have + been + V3 | The cake has been eaten. |
| Modal | modal + be + V3 | The cake must be eaten. |
| Imperative | Let + obj + be + V3 | Let the cake be eaten. |
| Verb + preposition | keep the preposition | He was laughed at. |
| Intransitive verb | ❌ No passive | — |
