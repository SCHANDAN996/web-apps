Card 1
Front: What is the basic structure of a passive voice sentence?
Back: Object + be-verb (as per tense) + V3 (past participle) + by + Subject. Example: "A letter was written by Ram."

Card 2
Front: In Active → Passive conversion, what happens to the subject and object?
Back: They swap places: the object of the active sentence becomes the subject of the passive sentence, and the doer (subject) moves after "by" (or is dropped if unimportant).

Card 3
Front: Which verb form is ALWAYS used in passive voice, regardless of tense?
Back: The past participle (V3). Only the helping verb "be" changes with tense; V3 never changes.

Card 4
Front: Mnemonic: How do you remember the passive formula?
Back: "BE + V3 = Passive Key" — but only when it expresses an action done by someone (agent stated or implied), e.g., "The window was broken by the boys." If BE + V3 merely describes a state ("The window is broken" = it is in a broken condition), it is stative/adjectival, not a true passive.

Card 5
Front: Convert: "She writes a letter." (Present Simple → Passive)
Back: "A letter is written by her." (is/am/are + V3)

Card 6
Front: Convert: "He was painting the wall." (Past Continuous → Passive)
Back: "The wall was being painted by him." (was/were + being + V3)

Card 7
Front: Convert: "They have completed the project." (Present Perfect → Passive)
Back: "The project has been completed by them." (has/have + been + V3)

Card 8
Front: How is a modal sentence made passive? Example: "You must obey the rules."
Back: Modal + be + V3 → "The rules must be obeyed by you."

Card 9
Front: Examiner's Trap: "The house is being built." — Why is "is being built" correct and not "is being build"?
Back: After "being," only V3 (built) is used. Examiners tempt you with V1/V2 after "being" — remember: being + V3 always.

Card 10
Front: Which sentences CANNOT be changed into passive voice?
Back: Sentences with intransitive verbs (verbs with no object), e.g., "He sleeps," "She arrived." No object = no passive.

Card 11
Front: Convert the imperative: "Open the door."
Back: "Let the door be opened." (Let + object + be + V3)

Card 12
Front: Convert the question: "Why did he beat the boy?"
Back: "Why was the boy beaten by him?" (Wh-word + was/were + subject + V3)

Card 13
Front: Examiner's Trap: "By whom was this book written?" vs "By who was this book written?" — which is correct and why?
Back: "By whom" is correct. After a preposition (by), the objective case "whom" is required. Examiners swap who/whom to trap you.

Card 14
Front: Convert: "People speak English worldwide." (When the doer is vague/general)
Back: "English is spoken worldwide." — Drop vague subjects like people, someone, they, everybody in the passive.

Card 15
Front: Mnemonic: How do you remember which tenses use "being" in passive?
Back: "Continuous BEINGS" — Only continuous tenses take "being": is/am/are being, was/were being. Perfect tenses take "been."

Card 16
Front: Convert: "The teacher gave me a book." (Two objects — which becomes the subject?)
Back: Either object can lead: "A book was given to me by the teacher." OR "I was given a book by the teacher." (The indirect object "me" is usually preferred in exams.)

Card 17
Front: Convert: "Someone has stolen my watch."
Back: "My watch has been stolen." (Drop "someone" — the doer is unknown/unimportant.)

Card 18
Front: What is the passive of "I know him"? (Preposition trap)
Back: "He is known to me." — The verb "know" takes "to" (not "by") before the agent in passive. But note: like and love take the normal "by" — "He is liked by all," "She is loved by everyone."

Card 19
Front: Which prepositions replace "by" with certain verbs in passive?
Back: known to, surprised at, amazed at, annoyed with, contained in, cut with, made of — the verb decides the preposition, not habit.

Card 20
Front: Convert: "Let him finish the work." (Imperative with object)
Back: "Let the work be finished by him." (Let + object + be + V3 + by + agent)
