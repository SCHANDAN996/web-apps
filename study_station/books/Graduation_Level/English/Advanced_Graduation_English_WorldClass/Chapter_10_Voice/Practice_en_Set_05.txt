Practice Set 5 — Active-Passive Voice (Questions 101–125) | Difficulty: 15 Medium, 10 Hard

101. Choose the correct passive form: "The manager will sign the documents tomorrow."
(a) The documents will signed by the manager tomorrow. (b) The documents will be sign by the manager tomorrow. (c) The documents will be signed by the manager tomorrow. (d) The documents will being signed by the manager tomorrow.
Answer: (c)
Solution: Future simple passive = will + be + V3 (signed). "Will signed" and "will be sign" break the be + past participle structure.
Source: PYQ-style

102. Choose the correct passive form: "Someone has stolen my wallet."
(a) My wallet has been stolen. (b) My wallet is been stolen. (c) My wallet has stolen by someone. (d) My wallet had been steal.
Answer: (a)
Solution: Present perfect passive = has/have + been + V3. When the doer is unknown or unimportant ("someone"), the agent is dropped.
Source: PYQ-style

103. Choose the correct active form: "The novel was written by her in just six months."
(a) She was written the novel in just six months. (b) She wrote the novel in just six months. (c) She has written the novel by six months. (d) She had wrote the novel in just six months.
Answer: (b)
Solution: Passive "was written" = past simple, so active is "wrote". The agent "her" becomes the subject "she".
Source: PYQ-style

104. Choose the correct passive form: "They are repairing the bridge."
(a) The bridge is repaired by them. (b) The bridge has being repaired. (c) The bridge is been repaired. (d) The bridge is being repaired.
Answer: (d)
Solution: Present continuous passive = is/am/are + being + V3. "Is been" is a classic wrong form examiners plant.
Source: PYQ-style

105. Choose the correct passive form: "You must obey the rules."
(a) The rules must obeyed by you. (b) The rules must being obeyed. (c) The rules must been obeyed. (d) The rules must be obeyed.
Answer: (d)
Solution: Modal passive = modal + be + V3. "Must obeyed" and "must been" both violate this fixed pattern.
Source: PYQ-style

106. Choose the correct passive form: "The teacher gave the students a difficult test."
(a) The students were gave a difficult test by the teacher. (b) A difficult test was given to the students by the teacher. (c) A difficult test is given the students by the teacher. (d) The students have been given a difficult test by the teacher.
Answer: (b)
Solution: With two objects, either can become the subject, but the tense must stay past simple: "was given". The option with "were gave" wrongly mixes an auxiliary with the base form.
Source: PYQ-style

107. Choose the correct passive form: "People speak English all over the world."
(a) English is spoken all over the world. (b) English is speak all over the world. (c) English spoken all over the world. (d) English has spoken all over the world.
Answer: (a)
Solution: Present simple passive = is + V3 (spoken). Vague agents like "people" are dropped in the passive.
Source: PYQ-style

108. Choose the correct passive form: "One should keep one's promises."
(a) One's promises should be keep. (b) One's promises should be kept. (c) Promises should been kept by one. (d) One's promises should kept.
Answer: (b)
Solution: Trap: the pronoun "one" tempts students to retain it as agent. Modal passive = should + be + kept (V3 of keep); "should be keep" uses the base form, which is wrong.
Source: PYQ-style

109. Choose the correct passive form: "The committee was discussing the proposal at that time."
(a) The proposal was being discussed by the committee at that time. (b) The proposal was discussed being by the committee at that time. (c) The proposal had being discussed by the committee at that time. (d) The proposal is being discussed by the committee at that time.
Answer: (a)
Solution: Past continuous passive = was/were + being + V3. The time marker "at that time" confirms past, ruling out the present form.
Source: PYQ-style

110. Choose the correct passive form: "They will have completed the project by March."
(a) The project would have been complete by March. (b) The project will have been completed by March. (c) The project will have completed by March. (d) The project will been completed by March.
Answer: (b)
Solution: Future perfect passive = will + have + been + V3. Dropping "been" is the most common error here.
Source: PYQ-style

111. Choose the correct active form: "The thief was caught by the police last night."
(a) The police were caught the thief last night. (b) The police caught the thief last night. (c) The police has caught the thief last night. (d) The police had been caught the thief last night.
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Was caught" = past simple passive, so active = "caught". "Last night" forbids the present perfect ("has caught").
Source: PYQ-style

112. Choose the correct passive form: "Did she invite you to the party?"
(a) Were you invited to the party by her? (b) Was you invited to the party by her? (c) Did you invited to the party by her? (d) Were you invite to the party by her?
Answer: (a)
Solution: Past simple interrogative passive = Was/Were + subject + V3. "You" takes "were", and the participle is "invited".
Source: PYQ-style

113. Choose the correct passive form: "The noise frightened the child."
(a) The child was frighten by the noise. (b) The child is frightened by the noise. (c) The child had frightened by the noise. (d) The child was frightened by the noise.
Answer: (d)
Solution: Past simple passive = was + V3 (frightened). "Frighten" is the base form, not the participle.
Source: PYQ-style

114. Choose the correct passive form: "They made him captain of the team."
(a) He is made captain of the team. (b) He was made the captain by them of the team. (c) He was made captain of the team. (d) He was being make captain of the team.
Answer: (c)
Solution: Verbs of appointing (make, elect, appoint) drop the article before the complement in both voices: "made him captain" becomes "was made captain".
Source: PYQ-style

115. Choose the correct passive form: "I know him."
(a) He is known with me. (b) He is known by me. (c) He was known to me. (d) He is known to me.
Answer: (d)
Solution: Trap verb: "know" takes the preposition "to" in the passive, not "by". The tense stays present simple.
Source: PYQ-style

116. Choose the correct passive form: "His behaviour annoyed me."
(a) I am annoyed with his behaviour. (b) I was annoyed by his behaviour to me. (c) I was annoyed with his behaviour. (d) I had been annoyed with his behaviour.
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Annoyed" takes "with" (for things) in the passive. Past simple is retained: "was annoyed".
Source: PYQ-style

117. Choose the correct passive form: "Open the window."
(a) Let the window opened. (b) The window is open. (c) Let the window be opened. (d) Let the window being opened.
Answer: (c)
Solution: Imperative passive = Let + object + be + V3. "Let the window opened" omits the essential "be".
Source: PYQ-style

118. Choose the correct passive form: "Who taught you French?"
(a) Whom were you taught French by him? (b) By who was you taught French? (c) By whom were you taught French? (d) By whom you were taught French?
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Who" becomes "By whom" in the passive; inversion follows: "were you taught". The option with "was you" has a subject-verb agreement error.
Source: PYQ-style

119. Choose the correct passive form: "The doctor is examining the patient."
(a) The patient is examined by the doctor being. (b) The patient is being examined by the doctor. (c) The patient has been examined by the doctor. (d) The patient is been examined by the doctor.
Answer: (b)
Solution: Trap: "is been examined" looks plausible but is grammatically impossible — continuous passive needs "being", perfect passive needs "been", and the original is continuous.
Source: PYQ-style

120. Choose the correct passive form: "She likes people admiring her."
(a) She likes being admired. (b) She likes to being admired. (c) She likes been admired. (d) She is liked being admired.
Answer: (a)
Solution: After "like", the gerund passive is "being + V3". "Been" cannot follow "likes" here.
Source: PYQ-style

121. Choose the correct passive form: "I remember my father taking me to the fair."
(a) I remember been taken to the fair by my father. (b) I remember to be taken to the fair by my father. (c) I am remembered being taken to the fair. (d) I remember being taken to the fair by my father.
Answer: (d)
Solution: Gerund passive after "remember" = being + V3. The meaning is of a past memory, so "being taken" fits; "to be taken" would suggest a future arrangement.
Source: PYQ-style

122. Choose the correctly converted sentence: "It is said that he is a spy."
(a) He is said to be a spy. (b) He is said being a spy. (c) He was said to be a spy. (d) He is say to be a spy.
Answer: (a)
Solution: "It is said that…" converts to subject + is said + to-infinitive. The tense stays present, and the infinitive is "to be".
Source: PYQ-style

123. Choose the correct passive form: "The fire has destroyed many houses."
(a) Many houses had destroyed by the fire. (b) Many houses has been destroyed by the fire. (c) Many houses have been destroyed by the fire. (d) Many houses have being destroyed by the fire.
Answer: (c)
Solution: Present perfect passive = have/has + been + V3. The plural subject "houses" takes "have"; "has been" fails agreement.
Source: PYQ-style

124. Choose the correct passive form: "Don't insult the poor."
(a) Let the poor not be insulted. (b) The poor are not insulted by anyone. (c) Let the poor not insulted. (d) The poor should not insulted.
Answer: (a)
Solution: Negative imperative passive = Let + object + not + be + V3: "Let the poor not be insulted." The other options either drop "be" or change the sentence into a plain statement rather than a command.
Source: PYQ-style

125. Choose the correct active form: "We were taught grammar by a strict teacher."
(a) A strict teacher had been teaching us grammar. (b) A strict teacher was taught us grammar. (c) A strict teacher has taught us grammar. (d) A strict teacher taught us grammar.
Answer: (d)
Solution: "Were taught" = past simple passive, so the active is past simple "taught". The other options shift the tense or misuse the passive auxiliary.
Source: PYQ-style
