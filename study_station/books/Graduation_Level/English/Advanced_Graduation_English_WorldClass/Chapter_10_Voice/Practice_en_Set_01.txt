Active-Passive Voice — Practice Set 1 (Questions 1–25)
Difficulty: Questions 1–20 Easy | Questions 21–25 Medium

1. Choose the correct passive form: "She writes a letter."
(a) A letter is being written by her. (b) A letter was written by her. (c) A letter is written by her. (d) A letter has been written by her.
Answer: (c)
Solution: The active sentence is in simple present tense, so the passive uses "is/are + V3". "Writes" becomes "is written". The past forms in the other options change the tense, which is not allowed.
Source: NCERT Class 9 English Grammar

2. Choose the correct passive form: "He killed the snake."
(a) The snake is killed by him. (b) The snake has been killed by him. (c) The snake was killed by him. (d) The snake had been killed by him.
Answer: (c)
Solution: Simple past "killed" becomes "was/were + V3" in passive. "The snake" is singular, so "was killed" is correct. The other options shift the tense.
Source: NCERT Class 10 English Grammar

3. Choose the correct passive form: "They are playing football."
(a) Football is played by them. (b) Football was being played by them. (c) Football has been played by them. (d) Football is being played by them.
Answer: (d)
Solution: Present continuous "are playing" becomes "is/are being + V3". "Football" is singular, so "is being played" is correct.
Source: NCERT Class 9 English Grammar

4. Choose the correct passive form: "The teacher has praised the boy."
(a) The boy has praised by the teacher. (b) The boy was praised by the teacher. (c) The boy had been praised by the teacher. (d) The boy has been praised by the teacher.
Answer: (d)
Solution: Present perfect "has praised" becomes "has/have been + V3". Option (a) drops "been", which is a classic examiner's trap — perfect passive always needs "been".
Source: PYQ-style

5. Choose the correct passive form: "Someone stole my watch."
(a) My watch is stolen. (b) My watch was stolen. (c) My watch has stolen. (d) My watch had stolen.
Answer: (b)
Solution: Simple past "stole" becomes "was + V3". When the doer is vague (someone), the "by" phrase is dropped. Options (c) and (d) are active forms, not passive.
Source: NCERT Class 10 English Grammar

6. Choose the correct passive form: "Open the door."
(a) The door should opened. (b) The door is opened. (c) The door was opened. (d) Let the door be opened.
Answer: (d)
Solution: Imperative sentences become passive with "Let + object + be + V3". Option (a) is grammatically broken (should + opened is wrong).
Source: PYQ-style

7. Choose the correct passive form: "She will finish the work."
(a) The work will finished by her. (b) The work will being finished by her. (c) The work will be finished by her. (d) The work would be finished by her.
Answer: (c)
Solution: Simple future "will finish" becomes "will be + V3". After "will" we need the base verb "be", so options (a) and (b) are wrong.
Source: NCERT Class 9 English Grammar

8. Choose the correct passive form: "The mother loves her child."
(a) Her child is being loved by the mother. (b) Her child was loved by the mother. (c) Her child is loved by the mother. (d) Her child has been loved by the mother.
Answer: (c)
Solution: Simple present "loves" becomes "is + V3". Verbs of emotion like love, like, hate are not used in continuous passive, so option (a) is wrong.
Source: NCERT Class 10 English Grammar

9. Choose the correct passive form: "He was writing a letter."
(a) A letter was being written by him. (b) A letter was written by him. (c) A letter is being written by him. (d) A letter had been written by him.
Answer: (a)
Solution: Past continuous "was writing" becomes "was/were being + V3". The tense must stay past, so option (c) is wrong.
Source: NCERT Class 9 English Grammar

10. Choose the correct passive form: "People speak English all over the world."
(a) English is speaking all over the world. (b) English was spoken all over the world. (c) English is spoken all over the world. (d) English has spoken all over the world.
Answer: (c)
Solution: Simple present "speak" becomes "is + V3" (spoken). With a general doer like "people", the "by" phrase is omitted. Options (a) and (d) are not passive structures.
Source: PYQ-style

11. Choose the correct passive form: "The police arrested the thief."
(a) The thief is arrested by the police. (b) The thief was arrested by the police. (c) The thief has arrested by the police. (d) The thief was being arrested by the police.
Answer: (b)
Solution: Simple past "arrested" becomes "was + V3". Option (c) misses "been" and option (d) wrongly changes simple past into past continuous.
Source: NCERT Class 10 English Grammar

12. Choose the correct passive form: "She can solve this problem."
(a) This problem can solved by her. (b) This problem could solved by her. (c) This problem can being solved by her. (d) This problem can be solved by her.
Answer: (d)
Solution: Modal + verb becomes "modal + be + V3". After "can" the base form "be" is required, so options (a), (c) and (b) are wrong.
Source: NCERT Class 9 English Grammar

13. Choose the correct passive form: "They had completed the project before the deadline."
(a) The project had completed before the deadline. (b) The project has been completed before the deadline. (c) The project was completed before the deadline. (d) The project had been completed before the deadline.
Answer: (d)
Solution: Past perfect "had completed" becomes "had been + V3". Option (a) is active, and options (b) and (c) change the tense.
Source: PYQ-style

14. Choose the correct passive form: "Do not insult the poor."
(a) The poor are not insulted. (b) Let the poor not be insulted. (c) The poor should not insulted. (d) Let not the poor insulted.
Answer: (b)
Solution: Negative imperatives become "Let + object + not + be + V3". Option (d) drops "be", and option (c) is grammatically broken.
Source: PYQ-style

15. Choose the correct passive form: "The chef is cooking the food."
(a) The food is being cooked by the chef. (b) The food is cooked by the chef. (c) The food was being cooked by the chef. (d) The food has been cooked by the chef.
Answer: (a)
Solution: Present continuous "is cooking" becomes "is being + V3". The tense must remain present continuous, so option (c) is wrong.
Source: NCERT Class 9 English Grammar

16. Choose the correct passive form: "Who taught you English?"
(a) By whom you were taught English? (b) By whom was you taught English? (c) Who was taught you English? (d) By whom were you taught English?
Answer: (d)
Solution: "Who" becomes "By whom" in passive questions, and the helping verb comes before the subject: "were you taught". Option (b) has wrong agreement (was-you), and option (a) lacks inversion.
Source: PYQ-style

17. Choose the correct passive form: "She gave me a book."
(a) I was given a book by her. (b) I am given a book by her. (c) A book is given to me by her. (d) I had been given a book by her.
Answer: (a)
Solution: With two objects, either can become the subject; the indirect object "me" becomes "I was given". Simple past "gave" needs "was + V3", so option (b) is wrong.
Source: NCERT Class 10 English Grammar

18. Choose the correct passive form: "We should respect our elders."
(a) Our elders should respected by us. (b) Our elders should be respected by us. (c) Our elders should being respected by us. (d) Our elders should been respected by us.
Answer: (b)
Solution: Modal passive = modal + be + V3. Only option (b) has the correct "should be respected"; the others misuse the verb form after the modal.
Source: NCERT Class 9 English Grammar

19. Choose the correct passive form: "The storm damaged the crops."
(a) The crops were damaged by the storm. (b) The crops are damaged by the storm. (c) The crops have damaged by the storm. (d) The crops had damaged by the storm.
Answer: (a)
Solution: Simple past "damaged" becomes "were + V3" (crops is plural). Options (c) and (d) are active structures, not passive.
Source: NCERT Class 10 English Grammar

20. Choose the correct passive form: "Please help me."
(a) I am helped by you. (b) You are requested to help me. (c) Let me helped. (d) I was helped please.
Answer: (b)
Solution: Imperatives beginning with "please" are politely converted as "You are requested to + V1". Option (c) drops "be", and option (d) changes the tense.
Source: PYQ-style

21. Choose the correct passive form: "The manager will sign the documents tomorrow."
(a) The documents would be signed by the manager tomorrow. (b) The documents will signed by the manager tomorrow. (c) The documents will be signed by the manager tomorrow. (d) The documents will being signed by the manager tomorrow.
Answer: (c)
Solution: Simple future becomes "will be + V3". Option (b) drops "be" — a frequent examiner's trap — and option (a) changes "will" to "would".
Source: PYQ-style

22. Choose the correct passive form: "One should keep one's promises."
(a) Promises should kept. (b) Promises should be kept. (c) Promises should be keep. (d) Promises are should be kept.
Answer: (b)
Solution: With "one" as subject, the passive drops the doer: "should be + V3 (kept)". Option (a) drops "be" and option (c) uses the base verb after "be".
Source: PYQ-style

23. Choose the correct active form: "The novel was written by Premchand."
(a) Premchand wrote the novel. (b) Premchand writes the novel. (c) Premchand has written the novel. (d) Premchand was writing the novel.
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Was written" is simple past passive, so the active is simple past "wrote". The other options change the tense.
Source: NCERT Class 10 English Grammar

24. Choose the correct passive form: "The children were flying kites."
(a) Kites were being flown by the children. (b) Kites were flown by the children. (c) Kites are being flown by the children. (d) Kites had been flown by the children.
Answer: (a)
Solution: Past continuous "were flying" becomes "were being + V3 (flown)". Option (b) changes it to simple past and option (c) to present.
Source: NCERT Class 9 English Grammar

25. Choose the correct passive form: "Did he invite you to the party?"
(a) Were you invited by him to the party? (b) Was you invited by him to the party? (c) Did you invited by him to the party? (d) Were you invite by him to the party?
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Did + V1" (simple past question) becomes "Was/Were + subject + V3". "You" takes "were", so option (b) fails agreement, and option (d) uses the base form instead of the past participle.
Source: PYQ-style
