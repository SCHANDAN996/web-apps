126. Choose the correct passive form: "They made him sign the document."
(a) He was made sign the document. (b) He was made to sign the document. (c) He had been made sign the document. (d) He is made to sign the document.
Answer: (b)
Solution: Verbs of compulsion like "make" drop "to" in the active voice but require "to + V1" in the passive: "made him sign" becomes "was made to sign". The first option is the classic trap — it copies the active structure.
Source: PYQ-style

127. Choose the correct passive form: "People speak English all over the world."
(a) English is being spoken all over the world. (b) English is spoken all over the world. (c) English has spoken all over the world. (d) English was spoken all over the world.
Answer: (b)
Solution: Simple present active becomes "is/am/are + V3" in passive. The habitual fact needs simple present passive, not continuous or past.
Source: NCERT Class 10 English Grammar

128. Spot the error: The letter (P) / was wrote (Q) / by my grandfather (R) / before independence. (S)
(a) Part Q (b) Part P (c) Part R (d) No error
Answer: (a)
Solution: Part Q is wrong. The passive needs the past participle "written", not the past tense "wrote": "was written". This is a Sound-Right trap because "wrote" sounds natural after "was".

129. Choose the correct passive form: "Someone has stolen my wallet."
(a) My wallet had been stolen. (b) My wallet has been stolen. (c) My wallet is stolen by someone. (d) My wallet was being stolen.
Answer: (b)
Solution: Present perfect active becomes "has/have been + V3". The vague agent "someone" is dropped in the passive.

132. Choose the correct passive form: "Who taught you French?"
(a) By whom were you taught French? (b) By whom was you taught French? (c) Who was taught you French? (d) By whom you were taught French?
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Who" as agent becomes "by whom", and the interrogative passive keeps inversion: "were you taught". The last option loses the inversion; the second has wrong agreement.

133. Spot the error: The new bridge (P) / has been built (Q) / since two years (R) / by the municipal corporation. (S)
(a) No error (b) Part Q (c) Part P (d) Part R
Answer: (d)
Solution: Part R is wrong. "Since" takes a point of time ("since 2022"); a duration needs "for": "for two years". The since-for confusion is a standard examiner trap.

134. Choose the correct passive form: "The teacher gave me a book."
(a) A book was given to me by the teacher. (b) A book was given me by the teacher. (c) I was given a book to the teacher. (d) Me was given a book by the teacher.
Answer: (a)
Solution: With two objects, either can become the subject, but the retained indirect object needs "to": "a book was given to me". The third option reverses the giver and receiver — an over-thinking trap.

Solution: Past continuous passive = "was/were being + V3". The first option changes the aspect to simple past, losing the ongoing sense.

141. Choose the correct passive form: "The noise frightened the child."
(a) The child was frightened with the noise. (b) The child is frightened by the noise. (c) The child had frightened by the noise. (d) The child was frightened by the noise.
Answer: (d)
Solution: Simple past passive = "was/were + V3", and "frightened" takes "by" for the agent. The tense is fixed by the active sentence, so the present-tense option fails.

(a) Why the offer was refused by him? (b) Why was the offer refused by him? (c) Why was the offer refuse by him? (d) Why did the offer refused by him?
Answer: (b)

150. Choose the correct active form: "The students are being taught grammar by the new teacher."
(a) The new teacher was teaching grammar to the students. (b) The new teacher teaches grammar to the students. (c) The new teacher has been teaching grammar to the students. (d) The new teacher is teaching grammar to the students.
Answer: (d)
Solution: "Are being taught" is present continuous passive, so the active must be present continuous: "is teaching". The other options each shift the tense — a reverse-conversion trap where the passive is given and the active must be matched exactly.
