# 📊 PYQ Analysis — Active & Passive Voice

## How Exams Test This Chapter (Pattern Analysis)

Voice is one of the most reliable scoring areas in SSC CGL, CHSL, MTS, IBPS PO/Clerk, and State PCS prelims. Questions appear in two formats: **(a) direct conversion** — "Choose the correct passive form of the given sentence" — and **(b) error spotting / sentence improvement** where the voice is wrong or awkward. Based on the recurring pattern across these exams, the following sub-topics dominate:

**Most frequently tested areas (in order of importance):**

1. **Tense-wise conversion** — especially Present Perfect, Past Perfect, and Future forms ("has been done", "had been done", "will be done"). Examiners love the Perfect tenses because students forget the "been".
2. **Modals in passive** — "can be solved", "should be obeyed", "must be completed". The trap: students write "can been" or drop "be".
3. **Interrogative sentences** — converting "Why did he beat the boy?" into passive. The word order confuses most candidates.
4. **Imperative sentences** — "Let the door be opened" / "You are requested to…". A classic SSC favourite.
5. **Preposition retention** — verbs like *look at, laugh at, listen to, agree to* keep their preposition in passive: "He was laughed at." Dropping the preposition is the single most common error.
6. **Double-object verbs** — "She gave me a book" → both "A book was given to me" and "I was given a book" are correct; exams test whether you recognise both.
7. **Sentences that CANNOT be passivised** — intransitive verbs (go, come, sleep, die, run) and sentences with no object. The "Sound-Right" trap lives here.
8. **By-agent omission** — when the doer is unknown or obvious (someone, people, they), the passive drops "by + agent". Exams test whether you wrongly retain "by them".

**The examiner's favourite traps:**

- **Sound-Right Trap:** An option that sounds natural but breaks a rule — e.g., "The match was won by them" offered as passive of "They won the match" is fine, but "The letter is being wrote" sounds plausible to a hurried reader and is wrong (wrote → written).
- **Over-thinking Trap:** A simple Present Indefinite conversion dressed in a long sentence. Students waste 60 seconds hunting for a hidden complication that doesn't exist.
- **Intervening Phrase Trap:** Extra clauses between subject and verb ("The letter, along with the documents, was sent…") make students misidentify the object.
- **Tense-Shift Trap:** Options that correctly change the structure but silently change the tense ("is being done" vs "has been done").

## Top 10 High-Yield Questions (with Solutions)

**Q1.** Choose the correct passive form: "She has written a letter."
(a) A letter has written by her.
(b) A letter has been written by her.
(c) A letter was been written by her.
(d) A letter is been written by her.

**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** Present Perfect passive = has/have + been + V3. "Has been written" is the only correct form. (a) drops "been"; (c) and (d) mix tenses illegally.
**Trap type:** Tense-Shift / Sound-Right (option (a) sounds close but is grammatically broken).
**Source:** PYQ-style

---

**Q2.** Choose the correct passive form: "Open the door."
(a) Let the door open.
(b) The door should opened.
(c) Let the door be opened.
(d) The door is open by you.

**Answer: (c)**
**Solution:** Imperative sentences take "Let + object + be + V3". (a) misses "be" and the past participle; (b) has "should opened" (modal + V3 is impossible); (d) is not a passive of a command.
**Trap type:** Sound-Right — (a) is the most-chosen wrong answer because it "feels" short and natural.
**Source:** PYQ-style

---

**Q3.** Choose the correct passive form: "Why did he beat the boy?"
(a) Why was the boy beaten by him?
(b) Why the boy was beaten by him?
(c) Why was the boy beat by him?
(d) Why had the boy been beaten by him?

**Answer: (a)**
**Solution:** In interrogative passives, the helping verb comes before the subject: "Why + was + object + V3…". (b) keeps statement order; (c) uses V2 "beat" instead of V3 "beaten"; (d) wrongly upgrades the tense to Past Perfect.
**Trap type:** Over-thinking — (d) tempts students who assume a harder-looking option must be right.
**Source:** PYQ-style

---

**Q4.** Choose the correct passive form: "People speak English all over the world."
(a) English is spoken all over the world.
(b) English is spoken by people all over the world.
(c) English was spoken all over the world.
(d) English is being spoken by people all over the world.

**Answer: (a)**
**Solution:** When the doer is a vague word like *people, someone, they*, the "by-agent" is dropped in passive. (b) unnecessarily retains "by people" — grammatically possible but not the standard exam answer; (c) and (d) change the tense.
**Trap type:** Sound-Right — (b) feels complete, but exams expect agent omission.
**Source:** PYQ-style

---

**Q5.** Choose the correct passive form: "The teacher gave me a prize."
(a) A prize was given me by the teacher.
(b) I was given a prize by the teacher.
(c) A prize is given to me by the teacher.
(d) Me was given a prize by the teacher.

**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** Double-object verbs allow two passives: "A prize was given **to** me…" or "I was given a prize…". (a) misses the preposition "to"; (c) changes tense; (d) uses the object pronoun "me" as subject.
**Trap type:** Intervening choice confusion — both conversions exist, but only one is correctly formed among the options.
**Source:** PYQ-style

---

**Q6.** Choose the correct passive form: "They are building a new bridge."
(a) A new bridge is built by them.
(b) A new bridge is being built by them.
(c) A new bridge has been built by them.
(d) A new bridge is being build by them.

**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** Present Continuous passive = is/am/are + being + V3. (d) uses "build" (V1) instead of "built" (V3) — the classic trap; (a) and (c) shift the tense.
**Trap type:** Sound-Right — (d) is the most-picked wrong option.
**Source:** PYQ-style

---

**Q7.** Choose the correct passive form: "You can solve this problem."
(a) This problem can solved by you.
(b) This problem can be solve by you.
(c) This problem can be solved by you.
(d) This problem could be solved by you.

**Answer: (c)**
**Solution:** Modal passive = modal + be + V3. (a) drops "be"; (b) uses V1 after "be"; (d) changes "can" to "could", altering the meaning.
**Trap type:** Sound-Right — (b) fools students who remember "be" but forget the participle.
**Source:** PYQ-style

---

**Q8.** Choose the correct passive form: "Someone stole my watch yesterday."
(a) My watch was stolen yesterday.
(b) My watch was stolen by someone yesterday.
(c) My watch is stolen yesterday.
(d) My watch had been stolen yesterday.

**Answer: (a)**
**Solution:** Past Indefinite passive = was/were + V3; vague agent "someone" is dropped. (b) retains the unnecessary agent; (c) clashes with "yesterday"; (d) wrongly uses Past Perfect for a single past event.
**Trap type:** Over-thinking — (d) attracts students who associate "stolen" with "had been".
**Source:** PYQ-style

---

**Q9.** Choose the correct passive form: "The boys were laughing at the beggar."
(a) The beggar was being laughed by the boys.
(b) The beggar was laughed at by the boys.
(c) The beggar was being laughed at by the boys.
(d) The beggar is being laughed at by the boys.

**Answer: (c)**
**Solution:** Past Continuous passive = was/were + being + V3, and the preposition "at" must stay attached to "laughed". (a) and (b) each drop one element — "being" or the correct structure; (d) changes the tense.
**Trap type:** Preposition-drop — the highest-frequency trap in voice questions.
**Source:** PYQ-style

---

**Q10.** Which sentence CANNOT be changed into passive voice?
(a) He plays football every evening.
(b) She sings a sweet song.
(c) The baby sleeps peacefully.
(d) They elected him president.

**Answer: (c)**
**Solution:** "Sleeps" is an intransitive verb — it takes no object, and passive requires an object to promote to subject position. (a), (b), (d) all have objects (football, a song, him) and convert normally.
**Trap type:** Sound-Right — "peacefully" looks like it could be an object to a careless reader.
**Source:** PYQ-style

## ⏱️ Applying the 15/45-Second Rule

**Fact-based (15 seconds):** Direct tense conversions with a clear object and no extra clauses — like **Q1** and **Q7** above. Method: identify the tense (2 sec) → recall the passive formula (3 sec) → match the option and eliminate those missing "been/be" or using the wrong participle (10 sec). Do NOT re-read the sentence; the formula does the work.

**Inference-based (45 seconds):** Questions with interrogatives, double objects, vague agents, or "cannot be passivised" formats — like **Q3** and **Q10**. Method: first check whether a passive is even possible (is there an object? is the verb transitive?) → then check agent retention/omission → then verify word order and prepositions → only then match options. Spending 45 seconds here is an investment, not a loss — these are the questions where 70% of candidates err.

**Skip signal:** If an option set has two answers that both look grammatically valid (e.g., both double-object passives), mark the question, move on, and return — never burn 90 seconds on a 1-mark voice question.
