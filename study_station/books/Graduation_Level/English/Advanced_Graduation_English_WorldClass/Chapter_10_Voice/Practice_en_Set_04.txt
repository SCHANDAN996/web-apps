76. Choose the correct passive form: "The manager will sign the documents tomorrow."
(a) The documents will signed by the manager tomorrow. (b) The documents would be signed by the manager tomorrow. (c) The documents will be sign by the manager tomorrow. (d) The documents will be signed by the manager tomorrow.
Answer: (d)
Solution: Future simple passive = will + be + V3. "Sign" becomes "signed", so "will be signed" is correct. The first option drops "be" and the third uses the base form after "be", both wrong.
Source: NCERT Class 10 English Grammar

77. Choose the correct passive form: "Someone has stolen my wallet."
(a) My wallet has stolen by someone. (b) My wallet is been stolen. (c) My wallet has been stolen. (d) My wallet had been steal.
Answer: (c)
Solution: Present perfect passive = has/have + been + V3. When the doer ("someone") is unknown or unimportant, it is dropped. "Is been" is never a valid structure.

78. Choose the correct active form: "The novel was written by her in just three months."
(a) She was written the novel in just three months. (b) She has wrote the novel in just three months. (c) She wrote the novel in just three months. (d) She had wrote the novel in just three months.
Answer: (c)
Solution: Passive "was written" is past simple, so the active must be past simple "wrote". The last two options wrongly pair "has/had" with "wrote" instead of "written".

79. Choose the correct passive form: "They are repairing the bridge."
(a) The bridge is being repaired by them. (b) The bridge has being repaired by them. (c) The bridge is being repair by them. (d) The bridge is repaired by them.
Answer: (a)
Solution: Present continuous passive = is/am/are + being + V3. The first option changes the tense to simple present, and "has being" is not a valid structure.

80. Choose the correct passive form: "You must obey the rules."
(a) The rules must be obeyed. (b) The rules should must be obeyed. (c) The rules must been obeyed. (d) The rules must obeyed by you.
Answer: (a)
Solution: Modal passive = modal + be + V3. "Must be obeyed" is correct; the agent "by you" is usually dropped with rules and orders. Two modals can never come together.

81. Choose the correct passive form: "The teacher praised the boy for his honesty."
(a) The boy was being praised by the teacher for his honesty. (b) The boy is praised by the teacher for his honesty. (c) The boy has been praised by the teacher for his honesty. (d) The boy was praised by the teacher for his honesty.
Answer: (d)
Solution: "Praised" is past simple, so passive = was/were + V3. The last option is past continuous passive, which would need "was praising" in the active.

82. Choose the correct passive form: "Open the window."
(a) The window should opened. (b) Let the window be opened. (c) Let the window is opened. (d) The window is being open.
Answer: (b)
Solution: Imperative sentences take the passive "Let + object + be + V3". The last option uses the adjective "open" instead of the past participle "opened".

(a) English has spoken all over the world. (b) English is spoke all over the world. (c) English is spoken all over the world. (d) English was spoken all over the world.
Answer: (c)
Solution: Simple present passive = is/am/are + V3. The past participle of "speak" is "spoken", not "spoke". The vague agent "people" is dropped.

84. Choose the correct active form: "By whom was this letter written?"
(a) Who was written this letter? (b) Whom wrote this letter? (c) Who wrote this letter? (d) Who did wrote this letter?
Answer: (c)
Solution: "By whom" in the passive becomes "Who" (subject) in the active, with past simple "wrote". After "did", the base form "write" is required, so the last option is wrong.

85. Choose the correct passive form: "The police have arrested the thief."
(a) The thief has been arrested by the police. (b) The thief has arrested by the police. (c) The thief have been arrested by the police. (d) The thief was been arrested by the police.
Answer: (a)

87. Choose the correct passive form: "The company offered him a good salary."
(a) He is offered a good salary by the company. (b) He was offered a good salary by the company. (c) A good salary is offered him by the company. (d) He has been offered a good salary by the company.
Answer: (b)
Solution: With two objects, the indirect object "him" becomes the subject: "He was offered a good salary". "Offered" is past simple, so "was offered", not "is offered".

88. Choose the correct passive form: "Do not insult the poor."
(a) The poor are not insulted. (b) The poor should not insulted. (c) Let not the poor be insulted. (d) The poor were not being insulted.
Answer: (c)
Solution: Negative imperatives take the passive "Let not + object + be + V3". The second option lacks "be" after the modal, and the first changes the sentence into a plain statement rather than a command.

(a) Millions of fans were watching the match. (b) Millions of fans are watching the match. (c) Millions of fans have watched the match. (d) Millions of fans watch the match.
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Is being watched" is present continuous passive, so the active is present continuous "are watching". The first option is past continuous, a tense-shift trap.

90. Choose the correct passive form: "Someone stole my car last night."
(a) My car was stolen last night. (b) My car is stolen last night. (c) My car had stolen last night. (d) My car was being stolen last night.
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Stole" with "last night" is past simple, so passive = was + V3 = "was stolen". The unknown agent "someone" is dropped. The last option wrongly suggests an ongoing action.

91. Choose the correct passive form: "They elected him president."
(a) President was elected him. (b) He is elected president by them. (c) He has been elected president by them. (d) He was elected president.
Answer: (d)
Solution: Verbs like elect, appoint, make take an object complement; in the passive the complement stays: "He was elected president". "Elected" is past simple, so "was elected".

96. Choose the correct passive form: "The storm damaged many houses in the village."
(a) Many houses in the village had damaged by the storm. (b) Many houses in the village were damaged by the storm. (c) Many houses in the village are damaged by the storm. (d) Many houses in the village was damaged by the storm.
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Houses" is plural, so "were damaged", not "was damaged" — a subject-verb agreement trap. "Damaged" is past simple, so "are damaged" is wrong.

97. Choose the correct passive form: "They will have been building the bridge for two years by then."
(a) The bridge has been built by them for two years by then. (b) The bridge will have been being built by them for two years by then. (c) The bridge will be built by them for two years by then. (d) This sentence cannot be changed into the passive naturally.
Answer: (d)
Solution: Future perfect continuous and present perfect continuous have no standard passive form in English; examiners test whether you recognise this. The last two options change the tense and meaning.

98. Choose the correct passive form: "His behaviour annoyed me."
(a) I am annoyed at his behaviour. (b) I was annoyed at his behaviour. (c) I was annoyed with his behaviour. (d) I had been annoyed at his behaviour.
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Annoyed" is past simple, so "was annoyed". With "annoyed", a thing takes the preposition "at" (a person takes "with"); "behaviour" is a thing, so "at" is correct — a preposition trap.

99. Choose the correct passive form: "The mother looks after the children."
(a) The children are looked after by the mother. (b) The children are looked by the mother. (c) The children are being looked after by the mother. (d) The children were looked after by the mother.
Answer: (a)
Solution: Prepositional phrasal verbs keep their preposition in the passive: "looked after", not just "looked". "Looks after" is simple present, so "are looked after"; the third option wrongly makes it continuous.

100. Choose the correct passive form: "They told us that the flight had been delayed."
(a) We were been told that the flight had been delayed. (b) We are told that the flight had been delayed. (c) We were told that the flight had been delayed. (d) We had told that the flight had been delayed.
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Told" is past simple, so the first clause becomes "We were told"; the reported clause "had been delayed" (already past perfect passive) stays unchanged. The third option doubles the auxiliary "were been", which is impossible.
