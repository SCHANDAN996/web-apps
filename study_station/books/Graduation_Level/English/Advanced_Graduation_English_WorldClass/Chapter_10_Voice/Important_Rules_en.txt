# 📋 Important Grammar Rules & Tips — Active & Passive Voice

## (a) 12 High-Yield Rules

| # | Rule | Example | Examiner's Trap |
|---|------|---------|-----------------|
| 1 | Only **transitive verbs** (verbs with an object) can be passivized. Intransitive verbs (sleep, go, die, happen) have no passive. | Active: She wrote a letter. → Passive: A letter was written by her. ❌ "He was slept." | Trap: "The accident was happened." — *happen* is intransitive; correct: "The accident happened." |
| 2 | Passive structure = **Object + be (tense-matched) + V3 (+ by + subject)**. The tense lives in "be", never in V3. | He is writing a letter → A letter **is being written** by him. | Trap: "A letter is being wrote." — V3 of write is *written*, not *wrote*. |
| 3 | **Continuous tenses**: only Present & Past Continuous have standard passive forms (is/are being + V3; was/were being + V3). Future/Perfect Continuous passives are avoided in exams. | They are building a bridge → A bridge is being built. | Trap: "The work has been being done." — grammatically possible but exam-answer is "The work has been done." |
| 4 | **Perfect tenses**: has/have/had + been + V3. | She has finished the work → The work has been finished by her. | Trap: "The work has being finished." — after *has*, use *been*, not *being*. |
| 5 | **Modals**: modal + be + V3. Modal perfect: modal + have + been + V3. | You must obey the rules → The rules must be obeyed. / He should have helped her → She should have been helped. | Trap: "The rules must been obeyed." — *be* is missing. |
| 6 | **Two objects** (direct + indirect): either object can become the passive subject; the indirect-object passive is preferred in exams. | He gave me a book → I was given a book by him. / A book was given to me by him. | Trap: "A book was given me." — when the direct object becomes subject, keep the preposition *to*. |
| 7 | **Prepositional/phrasal verbs**: the preposition stays glued to the verb in passive. | They laughed at him → He was laughed **at**. / She looks after the child → The child is looked **after**. | Trap: "He was laughed." — dropping *at* changes the meaning; preposition must be retained. |
| 8 | **Imperative sentences**: Let + object + be + V3 (advice/order); "You are requested/ordered to + V1" for polite forms. | Open the door → Let the door be opened. / Kindly help me → You are requested to help me. | Trap: "Let the door be open." — after *be*, use V3 (*opened*), not adjective form. |
| 9 | **Infinitive passive**: to + be + V3. | I want someone to help me → I want to be helped. / He is to deliver the speech → The speech is to be delivered. | Trap: "I want to be help." — *to be* demands V3. |
| 10 | **"By" agent is dropped** when the doer is unknown, obvious, or unimportant (someone, people, they, police). | Someone stole my watch → My watch was stolen. (not "by someone") | Trap: Over-adding "by them/by people" — exam keys prefer the agent dropped. |
| 11 | **Verbs of perception/causative** (see, hear, make, help): bare infinitive in active → **to + V1** in passive. | I saw him cross the road → He was seen **to cross** the road. / She made me cry → I was made **to cry**. | Trap: "He was seen cross the road." — passive restores the hidden *to*. |
| 12 | **Quasi-passive / stative verbs** (know, like, love, surprise, contain, consist of) take fixed prepositions (to/with/of), not always "by". | The news surprised me → I was surprised **at/by** the news. / I know him → He is known **to** me. | Trap: "He is known by me." — *know* takes *to*: "He is known to me." |

## (b) Time Management — The 15/45-Second Rule

| Type | What it looks like | Time | Strategy |
|------|-------------------|------|----------|
| **Fact-based (15 sec)** | Direct tense conversion; modal passive; "let" imperative; single-clause sentences. | ≤ 15 sec | Match tense → fix "be" form → check V3 → done. Do NOT re-read. |
| **Fact-based (15 sec)** | Spot-the-error where the error is a missing *be/been/being* or wrong V3. | ≤ 15 sec | Scan for "being/been + verb" pairs; check preposition retention. |
| **Inference-based (45 sec)** | Sentences with two objects, phrasal verbs, perception verbs, or agent-drop decisions. | ≤ 45 sec | Identify verb type first (transitive? prepositional? causative?), then apply the specific rule. |
| **Inference-based (45 sec)** | "Choose the correctly passive sentence" with 4 near-identical options differing only in *be* form or preposition. | ≤ 45 sec | Eliminate by structure, not by "sound". Sound-right is the trap. |

**Golden discipline:** If a voice question crosses 45 seconds, mark it and move on — voice questions are scoring, not sinking.

## (c) Morphology — Roots, Affixes & the Passive Connection

The passive is built on the **past participle (V3)**. Knowing how participles are formed from Latin/Greek roots helps you spot wrong V3 forms instantly.

| Root | Meaning | Verb | V3 (Participle) | Passive Example |
|------|---------|------|-----------------|-----------------|
| **scrib/script** (L.) | write | describe | described | The scene was described vividly. |
| **duc/duct** (L.) | lead | conduct | conducted | The exam was conducted fairly. |
| **port** (L.) | carry | transport | transported | Goods are transported by rail. |
| **mit/miss** (L.) | send | transmit | transmitted | The signal was transmitted at dawn. |
| **ject** (L.) | throw | reject | rejected | His plea was rejected. |
| **vis/vid** (L.) | see | revise | revised | The rules have been revised. |
| **fac/fact** (L.) | make/do | manufacture | manufactured | These cars are manufactured in India. |

**Affix alert:** The suffix **-able/-ible** creates a "hidden passive" meaning — *readable* = "can be read", *edible* = "can be eaten". Exams test this as: "This book is readable" ≈ "This book can be read."
