1. Choose the correct sentence.
(a) She is taller than her sister. (b) She is more taller than her sister. (c) She is most taller than her sister. (d) She is much more taller than her sister.
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Taller" is already a comparative form, so adding "more" or "most" makes it a double comparative, which is wrong. Only "taller than" is correct.
Source: NCERT Class 9 English Grammar

2. Fill in the blank: This is the _______ book I have ever read.
(a) good (b) better (c) best (d) most good
Answer: (c)
Solution: The phrase "I have ever read" shows comparison among all books, so the superlative "best" is needed. "Good" is positive and "better" is comparative.
Source: NCERT Class 8 English Grammar

3. Choose the correctly spelt adjective.
(a) beautifull (b) beautiful (c) beutiful (d) beautifuller
Answer: (b)
Solution: The correct spelling is "beautiful" with a single "l" at the end. The other options are common spelling errors.
Source: NCERT Class 7 English

4. Fill in the blank: There is _______ milk in the jug.
(a) much (b) few (c) many (d) several
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Milk" is an uncountable noun, so it takes "much". "Many", "few" and "several" are used only with countable nouns.
Source: NCERT Class 8 English Grammar

5. Choose the correct sentence.
(a) I have fewer money than you. (b) I have lesser money than you. (c) I have less money than you. (d) I have least money than you.
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Money" is uncountable, so "less" is correct; "fewer" is for countable nouns. "Lesser" is not used for quantity comparisons in this way.
Source: NCERT Class 9 English Grammar

6. Fill in the blank: Mount Everest is the _______ peak in the world.
(a) highest (b) higher (c) high (d) most high
Answer: (a)
Solution: Comparison among all peaks of the world requires the superlative "highest". "Most high" is not a standard superlative form.
Source: NCERT Class 8 English Grammar

7. Choose the correct order of adjectives: She bought a _______ dress.
(a) beautiful red silk (b) red beautiful silk (c) silk red beautiful (d) red silk beautiful
Answer: (a)
Solution: The standard order is opinion (beautiful) → colour (red) → material (silk). Any other order sounds unnatural in English.
Source: NCERT Class 10 English Grammar

8. Fill in the blank: He is _______ honest man.
(a) a (b) an (c) the (d) no article
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Honest" begins with a vowel sound (the "h" is silent), so "an" is used before it. The sound, not the letter, decides the article.
Source: NCERT Class 8 English Grammar

9. Choose the correct sentence.
(a) The gold is a precious metal. (b) An gold is a precious metal. (c) A gold is a precious metal. (d) Gold is a precious metal.
Answer: (d)
Solution: Material nouns like "gold" used in a general sense take no article. "The gold" would refer to some specific gold.
Source: NCERT Class 9 English Grammar

10. Fill in the blank: _______ students passed the exam; only two failed.
(a) A little (b) Little (c) Much (d) Most
Answer: (d)
Solution: The sense is that nearly everyone passed, so "most" fits. "Little" and "a little" are used with uncountable nouns, not "students".
Source: NCERT Class 9 English Grammar

11. Choose the correct sentence.
(a) He is senior than me. (b) He is senior of me. (c) He is senior from me. (d) He is senior to me.
Answer: (d)
Solution: Latin comparatives like senior, junior, superior, inferior and prior take "to", not "than". This is a fixed rule.
Source: NCERT Class 10 English Grammar

12. Fill in the blank: This road is _______ than that one.
(a) narrow (b) more narrow (c) narrowest (d) narrower
Answer: (d)
Solution: Comparison between two roads needs the comparative "narrower". "More narrow" is a double comparative and is wrong.
Source: NCERT Class 8 English Grammar

13. Choose the correct sentence.
(a) I saw a two leggeds dog. (b) I saw a two-leg dog. (c) I saw a two-legs dog. (d) I saw a two-legged dog.
Answer: (d)
Solution: In compound adjectives with numbers, the noun stays singular and is hyphenated: "a two-legged dog". Plural forms inside the compound are wrong.
Source: NCERT Class 10 English Grammar

14. Fill in the blank: _______ information you gave me was useful.
(a) Many (b) Few (c) Several (d) The
Answer: (d)
Solution: "Information" is uncountable, so "many", "few" and "several" cannot be used. "The" refers to the specific information given.
Source: NCERT Class 9 English Grammar

15. Choose the correct sentence.
(a) He gave me a lot of advice. (b) He gave me much advices. (c) He gave me many advices. (d) He gave me an advice.
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Advice" is uncountable and has no plural "advices" in this sense, nor can it take "an". "A lot of advice" is correct.
Source: NCERT Class 9 English Grammar

16. Fill in the blank: Of the two brothers, Ravi is the _______.
(a) tall (b) taller (c) tallest (d) most tall
Answer: (b)
Solution: When comparing two persons with "of the two", English uses the comparative with "the": "the taller". The superlative is used for three or more.
Source: NCERT Class 10 English Grammar

17. Choose the correct sentence.
(a) The riches should help the poors. (b) The rich should help the poor. (c) Rich should help poor. (d) The rich should help the poors.
Answer: (b)
Solution: "The + adjective" (the rich, the poor) denotes a whole class of people and is plural in sense; the adjective itself never takes "-s".
Source: NCERT Class 10 English Grammar

18. Fill in the blank: She is the _______ girl in the class.
(a) intelligent (b) more intelligent (c) most intelligent (d) intelligenter
Answer: (c)
Solution: Comparison within the whole class needs the superlative. "Intelligent" is a long adjective, so it forms the superlative with "most", not "-er/-est".
Source: NCERT Class 8 English Grammar

19. Choose the correct sentence.
(a) Delhi is bigger than any city in India. (b) Delhi is bigger than all city in India. (c) Delhi is bigger than any other city in India. (d) Delhi is biggest than any other city in India.
Answer: (c)
Solution: When a thing is compared with others of the same group, "any other" is needed to exclude itself; otherwise Delhi would be compared with Delhi. This is a classic examiner's trap.
Source: PYQ-style

20. Fill in the blank: He is _______ stronger of the two wrestlers.
(a) a (b) the (c) an (d) no article
Answer: (b)
Solution: With "of the two", the comparative adjective takes "the" before it: "the stronger of the two". This is a standard pattern.
Source: NCERT Class 10 English Grammar

21. Spot the error: Part P: No sooner had I / Part Q: finished the more difficult sum / Part R: than the bell rang. / Part S: No error
(a) Part P (b) Part Q (c) No error (d) Part R
Answer: (c)
Solution: The sentence is correct. "The more difficult sum" is a valid comparative form for a long adjective, and "no sooner... than" is the correct correlative pair.

22. Choose the correct sentence.
(a) He is the best of the two players. (b) He is better of two players. (c) He is the better of the two players. (d) He is the most better of the two players.
Answer: (c)
Solution: For two persons or things, the comparative "better" with "the" is used, not the superlative "best". Examiners trap students by mixing "of the two" with superlatives.
Source: PYQ-style

23. Fill in the blank: The _______ news made everyone sad.
(a) latest (b) late (c) latter (d) last
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Latest" means most recent, which fits the context of news. "Late" means delayed, "latter" refers to the second of two things, and "last" means final — none fits here.
Source: NCERT Class 10 English Grammar

24. Choose the correct sentence.
(a) My elder brother is six years older than me. (b) My elder brother is six years elder to me. (c) My elder brother is six years elder than me. (d) My older brother is six years elder to me.
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Elder" shows family relationship and is never followed by "than" or used with exact age gaps; for stating the age difference, "older than" is required. This is a favourite examiner's trap.
Source: PYQ-style

25. Fill in the blank: _______ knowledge is a dangerous thing.
(a) Little (b) A little (c) The little (d) Much
Answer: (b)
Solution: "A little" means "some, though not much" — the proverb says a small amount of knowledge is dangerous. "Little" would mean almost none, which reverses the meaning.
Source: NCERT Class 10 English Grammar
