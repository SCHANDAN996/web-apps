# 🧒 Feynman Technique — Adjective

## The Hardest Concept, Explained to a 12-Year-Old

**The concept that trips up even advanced students: "Order of Adjectives" — why we say *"a beautiful small old round black Italian wooden table"* and never *"an Italian wooden black round old small beautiful table".***

Imagine you are describing your new bicycle to a friend on the phone. You cannot show it — only words. Now watch what your brain does automatically:

You say: *"I bought a **beautiful, brand-new, red, racing** bicycle."*

You would NEVER say: *"I bought a racing, red, brand-new, beautiful bicycle."* It sounds like a robot glitching. Why? Because English has a **secret queue** — adjectives stand in a fixed line, like students in a school assembly line arranged by height. Nobody teaches you the queue; native speakers just "feel" it. But in exams, YOU need to know the queue by name.

### The Queue (Memorize This Order)

**O-S-A-S-C-O-M-P** → **"OSASCOMP"**

| Position | Type | Example word |
|---|---|---|
| 1. **O**pinion | what you *feel* | beautiful, ugly, lovely |
| 2. **S**ize | how big | small, huge, tiny |
| 3. **A**ge | how old | old, new, ancient |
| 4. **S**hape | what form | round, square, flat |
| 5. **C**olour | what colour | red, black, golden |
| 6. **O**rigin | where from | Indian, Italian, Japanese |
| 7. **M**aterial | made of what | wooden, silk, gold |
| 8. **P**urpose | used for what | racing, walking, dining |

So: *"a **beautiful** (opinion) **small** (size) **old** (age) **round** (shape) **black** (colour) **Italian** (origin) **wooden** (material) **dining** (purpose) table."*

### The Unforgettable Trick 🧠

**"Old Students Always Study Chemistry On Monday Periods."**

Old → Opinion, Students → Size, Always → Age, Study → Shape, Chemistry → Colour, On → Origin, Monday → Material, Periods → Purpose.

**Daily-life anchor:** Think of the phrase *"a lovely little old rectangular black French silver walking stick."* Lovely (opinion) comes first, French (origin) comes near the noun, and walking (purpose) hugs the noun tightest. **Purpose always stands closest to the noun** — because it almost *becomes* the noun (a "walking stick", a "dining table", a "racing bicycle"). Words like *masala* in "masala dosa" are not purpose adjectives at all — they are nouns acting as names of a variety, so they sit right next to the noun like a compound name.

### Why Exams Trap You Here

The examiner gives you four options where ALL the adjectives are the same — only the **order changes**. Three options "sound okay" if you read fast. Only one follows OSASCOMP. If you know the queue, it's a 15-second question. If you go by "sound", you'll pick the trap.

**Quick test:** Which is correct?
- (a) a leather black new bag
- (b) a new black leather bag ✅
- (c) a black new leather bag
- (d) a leather new black bag

New (age) → black (colour) → leather (material). Answer: **(b)**.
