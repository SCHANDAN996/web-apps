# 🪄 Short Tricks + Skip Strategy — Adjective

## Mnemonic Boxes

**📦 Box 1 — Order of Adjectives: "OSASCOMP"**
**O**pinion → **S**ize → **A**ge → **S**hape → **C**olour → **O**rigin → **M**aterial → **P**urpose
*Example: "a beautiful (O) small (S) old (A) round (S) black (C) Indian (O) wooden (M) walking (P) stick."*
Trick: **"Old Students Always Study Carefully On Monday Periods."**

**📦 Box 2 — Adjectives that take "to" (not "than"): "SIP-J"**
**S**enior, **J**unior, **I**nferior, **S**uperior, **P**rior, **P**osterior → all followed by **to**.
*Example: "He is senior **to** me" (❌ senior than me).*
Trick: **"Senior-Junior Inferior-Superior Prefer 'to'"** — Latin comparatives never marry "than".

**📦 Box 3 — No comparative/superlative degree: "UPPER-C"**
**U**nique, **P**erfect, **U**niversal, **E**xcellent, **R**ound, **E**ternal, **C**omplete (also: empty, full, supreme, chief, extreme).
*❌ "most unique" → ✅ "unique".* Absolute adjectives have no degrees.

**📦 Box 4 — "The + Adjective" = whole class (plural verb)**
**The rich, the poor, the blind, the deaf, the old, the young, the unemployed** → plural meaning, plural verb.
*Example: "The rich **are** not always happy."*
Trick: **"THE makes an adjective a TEAM."**

**📦 Box 5 — Adjective vs Adverb after verbs: "Sense verbs stay adjective"**
Verbs of **sense/state** — look, feel, smell, taste, sound, seem, appear, become — take an **adjective**, not adverb.
*Example: "The soup tastes **good**" (❌ well). "She looks **happy**."*
Trick: **"Senses describe, they don't act."**

**📦 Box 6 — "Enough" position trick**
**Enough** comes **after** an adjective but **before** a noun.
*Example: "He is tall **enough**" / "He has **enough** money."*
Trick: **"Adjective pushes enough back; noun pulls it forward."**

**📦 Box 7 — "Little / A little / The little"**
**Little** = almost none (negative) | **A little** = some (positive) | **The little** = all that exists.
*Example: "There is little hope" (hopeless) vs "a little hope" (some hope).*
Same family: **few / a few / the few** (for countable nouns).
Trick: **"No 'a' = No hope; 'a' = At least some."**

**📦 Box 8 — "Elder vs Older"**
**Elder** → only for family members, never with "than" (use "to").
**Older** → general comparison, takes "than".
*Example: "He is my elder brother" / "He is older **than** me."*
Trick: **"Elder stays in the family; Older goes everywhere."**

**📦 Box 9 — "Farther vs Further"**
**Farther** = physical distance | **Further** = additional/more.
*Example: "Delhi is farther than Noida" / "For further details, call us."*
Trick: **"FARther has FAR in it — distance."**

**📦 Box 10 — "Later vs Latter / Latest vs Last"**
**Later/Latest** = time | **Latter/Last** = position/order.
*Example: "Of the two, the **latter** is better" / "the **latest** news" / "the **last** chapter."*
Trick: **"Latter = Letter order; Later = Late (time)."**

**📦 Box 11 — "Each vs Every"**
**Each** → two or more, individual focus | **Every** → three or more, group focus.
*Example: "Each hand has five fingers" (two hands) / "Every student passed."*
Trick: **"Each = 2+, Every = 3+."**

**📦 Box 12 — Double comparative ban**
Never use **more/most** with -er/-est forms.
*❌ "more better", "most fastest" → ✅ "better", "fastest".*
Trick: **"One degree at a time — no double-decker comparison."**

**📦 Box 13 — "Many a" takes singular**
**Many a + singular noun + singular verb.**
*Example: "Many a soldier **has** died for the country."*
Trick: **"Many a = one at a time."**

**📦 Box 14 — Adjectives after indefinite pronouns**
Adjective comes **after** something, anything, nothing, somebody, anyone.
*Example: "something **interesting**" (❌ interesting something), "nothing **new**".*
Trick: **"Indefinite pronouns wear adjectives backwards."**

**📦 Box 15 — "First two" vs "Two first"**
Ordinal (first, second, last) comes **before** cardinal (one, two, three).
*Example: "the **first two** chapters" (❌ the two first chapters).*
Trick: **"Rank before count."**

## ⏭️ Skip Strategy

**Tip 1 — Skip "Sound-Right" traps in 15 seconds.**
If an option "sounds natural" (e.g., "more superior", "most unique", "senior than"), it is almost certainly the trap. These are Fact-based recall questions — apply Box 2/3/12 instantly and move on. Don't re-read the sentence hoping it will "feel" different.

**Tip 2 — Skip long-sentence order-of-adjectives questions strategically.**
If a question asks you to arrange 5–6 adjectives and you can't recall OSASCOMP under pressure, **mark it and skip** — it is a 45-second question. Guessing randomly here wastes time; return after finishing all 15-second degree/comparison questions, which are faster and surer marks.

**Tip 3 — Skip when two options differ only by "the".**
Questions like "___ rich should help ___ poor" test "the + adjective". If you are unsure between two near-identical options, don't burn 60 seconds — recall Box 4 (class = "the + adjective", plural verb), pick, and move. If the verb in the sentence is plural, the answer must have "the"; use the verb as your 5-second clue.
