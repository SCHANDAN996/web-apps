Card 1
Front: What is an adjective?
Back: A word that describes or modifies a noun or pronoun — it tells us what kind, how many, how much, or which one. Example: "a brave soldier," "five books," "this pen."

Card 2
Front: Where does an adjective usually sit in a sentence? Name both positions.
Back: (1) Attributive — before the noun: "a tall building." (2) Predicative — after a linking verb: "The building is tall."

Card 3
Front: What is the correct order of adjectives before a noun? Give the mnemonic.
Back: OSASCOMP — Opinion, Size, Age, Shape, Colour, Origin, Material, Purpose. Example: "a beautiful small old round black Italian wooden dining table."

Card 4
Front: MNEMONIC — How do you remember the adjective order?
Back: "OSASCOMP" — remember it as "Oh Sir, A Smart Cat Owns My Pen" (Opinion, Size, Age, Shape, Colour, Origin, Material, Purpose).

Card 5
Front: Which adjectives are used only predicatively (after the verb, never before the noun)?
Back: Adjectives beginning with "a-": afraid, asleep, awake, alive, alone, ashamed. Say "The baby is asleep," not "an asleep baby."

Card 6
Front: What is the difference between "elder" and "older"?
Back: "Elder" is used only for family members and never with "than" (my elder brother). "Older" is general and takes "than" (He is older than me).

Card 7
Front: EXAMINER'S TRAP — "He is more superior to me." What is wrong?
Back: Superior, inferior, senior, junior, prior, posterior, and prefer already contain comparison. They take "to," never "than," and never "more/most." Correct: "He is superior to me."

Card 8
Front: When do we use "the + adjective"? Give an example.
Back: To refer to a whole class of people (plural meaning): the rich, the poor, the blind, the unemployed. "The rich should help the poor." These take plural verbs.

Card 9
Front: What is the difference between "farther" and "further"?
Back: "Farther" = physical distance (Delhi is farther than Agra). "Further" = additional/more (further information, further discussion).

Card 10
Front: "Each," "every," "either," "neither" — what kind of adjectives are these and what verb do they take?
Back: Distributive adjectives. They are followed by a singular noun and take a singular verb: "Each student has a book."

Card 11
Front: EXAMINER'S TRAP — "I have fewer money." Correct it and state the rule.
Back: Correct: "I have less money." "Fewer" is for countable nouns (fewer books); "less" is for uncountable nouns (less water, less money).

Card 12
Front: What is the difference between "later" and "latter"?
Back: "Later" = after some time (I will come later). "Latter" = the second of two things mentioned (Of tea and coffee, I prefer the latter). Opposite of "latter" is "former."

Card 13
Front: MNEMONIC — How do you remember which Latin comparatives take "to" instead of "than"?
Back: "SISJPP" — Superior, Inferior, Senior, Junior, Prior, Posterior (plus Prefer). Trick: "Senior Inspectors Skip Junior's Poor Performance" — all take "to."

Card 14
Front: When do we use the comparative vs. the superlative degree?
Back: Comparative (-er / more) for comparing TWO persons or things; superlative (-est / most) for comparing THREE or more. "Ram is taller than Shyam." "Ram is the tallest boy in the class."

Card 15
Front: "Little," "a little," "the little" — what does each mean?
Back: Little = almost none (negative sense): "There is little hope." A little = some (positive): "There is a little hope." The little = all that exists: "He spent the little money he had."

Card 16
Front: Can we say "very unique" or "most perfect"? Why or why not?
Back: No. Absolute adjectives — unique, perfect, universal, complete, empty, dead, supreme — have no degrees of comparison. Something is either unique or not.

Card 17
Front: What are demonstrative adjectives? How do they differ from demonstrative pronouns?
Back: This, that, these, those. As adjectives they come before a noun ("this book"); as pronouns they stand alone ("This is mine").

Card 18
Front: "No sooner had he come than it started raining." Which word pairs with "no sooner" — "than" or "when"?
Back: "Than." No sooner...than; Hardly/Scarcely...when. Examiners swap these to trap you.

Card 19
Front: What is an adjective phrase? Give an example.
Back: A group of words doing the work of an adjective: "a man of courage" (= courageous man); "a girl with blue eyes."

Card 20
Front: In "The injured were taken to hospital," what is "the injured" and what verb form does it take?
Back: "The + adjective" used as a plural noun meaning "injured people" — it takes a plural verb ("were," not "was").
