# 📋 Important Grammar Rules & Tips — Adjective

## (a) 12 High-Yield Rules

| # | Rule | Example | Examiner's Trap |
|---|------|---------|-----------------|
| 1 | Adjectives of quantity (**much, little**) are used with **uncountable** nouns; adjectives of number (**many, few, several**) with **countable** nouns. **Some** and **any** work with both. | There is **little** milk left. / There are **few** students absent. / I have **some** money and **some** friends. | "Much people were present" ❌ → "Many people were present" ✅ |
| 2 | **Comparative** for two things, **superlative** for more than two. | Ravi is the **taller** of the two brothers. / He is the **tallest** boy in the class. | "She is the best of the two sisters" ❌ → "the better of the two" ✅ |
| 3 | Adjectives ending in **-ior** (senior, junior, superior, inferior, prior, posterior) take **to**, not *than*. | He is senior **to** me by two years. | "He is senior than me" ❌ — the most repeated trap in SSC/Bank exams. |
| 4 | **Prefer, preferable** also take **to**, not *than*. | I prefer tea **to** coffee. | "I prefer tea more than coffee" ❌ (double error: *more* + *than*). |
| 5 | Some adjectives are used only **predicatively** (after the verb), never before a noun: afraid, asleep, awake, alive, alone, alike. (⚠️ *Ill* is normally predicative, but appears before a noun in fixed phrases like *ill health*, *ill will*.) | The baby is **asleep**. | "an asleep baby" ❌ → "a sleeping baby" ✅ |
| 6 | **The + adjective** = the whole class of people (plural verb). | **The rich** should help **the poor**. | "The rich is always selfish" ❌ → "The rich are…" ✅ |
| 7 | Order of adjectives before a noun: **Opinion–Size–Age–Shape–Colour–Origin–Material–Purpose** (OSASCOMP). | a beautiful **small old round black Indian wooden** table | Exams test 2–3 adjectives: "a wooden old box" ❌ → "an old wooden box" ✅ |
| 8 | **No comparative/superlative** for absolute adjectives: unique, perfect, universal, complete, extreme, chief, empty, round (in strict usage). | This is a **unique** opportunity. | "the most unique design" ❌ → "a unique design" ✅ |
| 9 | **Farther** = physical distance; **further** = additional/more. | Delhi is **farther** than I thought. / For **further** details, call me. | "No farther discussion" ❌ → "No further discussion" ✅ |
| 10 | **Elder/eldest** for members of the same family (never with *than*); **older/oldest** for general age. | My **elder** brother is **older** than me. | "He is elder than me" ❌ → "He is elder to me" / "older than me" ✅ |
| 11 | **Later/latter/latest/last**: later = time, latter = the second of two, latest = most recent, last = final. | Of the two plans, I prefer the **latter**. / the **latest** news | "the later of the two options" ❌ → "the latter" ✅ |
| 12 | After **one of the**, use superlative + plural noun; but the verb stays singular. | One of the **best players** of the team **is** injured. | "One of the best player are…" ❌ — double trap: noun plural, verb singular. |

## (b) Time Management — The 15/45-Second Rule

| Type | What it looks like | Time | Strategy |
|------|-------------------|------|----------|
| **Fact-based (15 sec)** | Direct rule recall: senior/junior + *to*; much vs many; elder vs older; order of adjectives; the + adjective | 15 seconds | See the keyword (senior, much, one of the…) → apply the rule instantly → mark and move. Never re-read. |
| **Inference-based (45 sec)** | Error-spotting with long intervening phrases; sentence improvement where two options look right; questions mixing adjective + subject-verb agreement | 45 seconds | Strip the sentence to subject + verb + adjective. Check: (1) Is the adjective form right? (2) Is the comparison logical (like-with-like)? (3) Is there a hidden double comparative? |

**Rule of thumb:** If the answer depends on one memorised rule → 15 sec. If you must parse the whole sentence or compare two near-correct options → 45 sec. Anything beyond 45 sec → mark for review and skip.

## (c) Morphology — Roots, Affixes, Adjective Formation

| Root / Affix | Meaning | Adjective formed | Example word in use |
|--------------|---------|------------------|---------------------|
| **-ful** (suffix) | full of | beauty → beautiful | a beautiful painting |
| **-less** (suffix) | without | hope → hopeless | a hopeless situation |
| **-ous / -ious** | having the quality of | danger → dangerous | a dangerous road |
| **-ive** | tending to | act → active | an active member |
| **-able / -ible** | capable of being | read → readable | a readable novel |
| **-al / -ial** | relating to | nation → national | national policy |
| **-ic** | relating to | hero → heroic | a heroic deed |
| **-ish** | somewhat / like | child → childish | childish behaviour |
| **-ly** (with nouns!) | having the quality of | friend → friendly | a friendly neighbour (⚠️ *friendly* is an adjective, not an adverb) |
| **-y** | characterised by | rain → rainy | a rainy day |
| **bene-** (Latin root) | good | benevolent | a benevolent donor |
| **mal-** (Latin root) | bad | malicious | malicious intent |
| **magn-** (Latin root) | great | magnificent | a magnificent palace |
| **omni-** (Latin root) | all | omnipotent | an omnipotent ruler |
| **multi-** (Latin root) | many | multilingual | a multilingual country |

**Exam tip:** Words like *friendly, costly, lonely, lively, cowardly* end in **-ly** but are **adjectives**, not adverbs — a favourite error-spotting trap: "She spoke to me very friendly" ❌ → "in a friendly manner" ✅.
