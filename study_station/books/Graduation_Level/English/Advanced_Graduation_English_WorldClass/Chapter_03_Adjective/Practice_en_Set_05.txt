101. Choose the correctly framed sentence:
(a) He is more senior than me by two years.
(b) He is senior to me by two years.
(c) He is senior than me by two years.
(d) He is more senior to me by two years.
Answer: (b)
Solution: Latin comparatives like senior, junior, superior, inferior, prior and posterior take "to", not "than". (a) and (c) wrongly use "than"; (d) adds an unnecessary "more".
Source: NCERT Class 10 English Grammar

102. Identify the error in: "She is the most unique singer in our colony."
(a) She is (b) the most unique (c) singer in (d) our colony
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Unique" is an absolute adjective and has no degrees of comparison; "most unique" is wrong. Say "a unique singer" or "a most unusual singer".
Source: PYQ-style

103. Choose the correct sentence:
(a) I prefer tea to coffee.
(b) I prefer tea more than coffee.
(c) I prefer tea than coffee.
(d) I prefer tea over than coffee.
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Prefer" itself carries the comparative sense, so it takes "to", never "than" or "more than". (b) and (c) are classic examiner traps.
Source: NCERT Class 9 English Grammar

104. Fill in the blank: "Of the two brothers, Ramesh is the ______."
(a) tallest (b) most tall (c) taller (d) more tall
Answer: (c)
Solution: When comparing two persons with "of the two", the comparative degree is used with "the" — "the taller". Superlative (tallest) needs three or more.
Source: PYQ-style

105. Choose the correctly ordered adjectives: "She bought a ______ vase."
(a) beautiful old blue Chinese
(b) old blue beautiful Chinese
(c) Chinese beautiful blue old
(d) blue Chinese old beautiful
Answer: (a)
Solution: Standard order is Opinion–Age–Colour–Origin (OSASCOMP). "Beautiful" (opinion) → "old" (age) → "blue" (colour) → "Chinese" (origin) — only (a) follows it.
Source: NCERT Class 8 English Grammar

106. Identify the error: "No sooner did he reach the station than the train had left."
(a) than the train had left (b) the station (c) No error (d) No sooner did he reach
Answer: (a)
Solution: With "No sooner did he reach" (simple past), the later action takes the simple past: "the train left". Past perfect "had left" is correct only with "No sooner had he reached... than the train left" — mixing the two patterns is the error.
Source: PYQ-style

107. Choose the correct sentence:
(a) He is the better of the two players. (b) He is the most better of the two players. (c) He is better of the two players. (d) He is the best of the two players.
Answer: (a)
Solution: For two persons/things, use the comparative with "the": "the better of the two". Superlative "best" requires three or more; "most better" is a double comparative error.
Source: NCERT Class 10 English Grammar

108. Fill in the blank: "This is ______ than any other novel I have read."
(a) interesting (b) most interesting (c) more interesting (d) the most interesting
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Than any other + singular noun" demands the comparative degree: "more interesting than any other novel". The superlative would need "of all".
Source: PYQ-style

109. Choose the correct sentence:
(a) Gold is precious metal. (b) The gold is a precious metal. (c) The gold is precious metal. (d) Gold is a precious metal.
Answer: (d)
Solution: Material nouns like "gold" take no article when used generally, but "metal" is a countable singular noun needing "a". Hence "Gold is a precious metal."
Source: NCERT Class 9 English Grammar

110. Identify the error: "He is comparatively more happier now than before."
(a) more happier (b) now than before (c) He is comparatively (d) No error
Answer: (a)
Solution: "More happier" is a double comparative — use either "happier" or "more happy", never both. "Comparatively happier" alone would be acceptable.
Source: PYQ-style

111. Choose the correct sentence:
(a) A little knowledge is a dangerous thing. (b) Few knowledge is a dangerous thing. (c) The little knowledge is a dangerous thing. (d) Little knowledge is a dangerous thing.
Answer: (a)
Solution: "A little" means "some, though not much" — the intended sense of the proverb. "Little" (almost none) reverses the meaning; "few" cannot modify uncountable "knowledge".
Source: NCERT Class 10 English Grammar

112. Fill in the blank: "There isn't ______ milk left in the fridge."
(a) many (b) few (c) much (d) a few
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Milk" is uncountable, so it takes "much". "Many/few/a few" go with countable plurals — a frequent trap in error-spotting questions.
Source: NCERT Class 8 English Grammar

113. Choose the correct sentence:
(a) He is strong enough to lift the box. (b) He is enough strong to lift the box. (c) He is too strong enough to lift the box. (d) Enough he is strong to lift the box.
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Enough" as an adverb comes after the adjective it modifies: "strong enough". Before a noun it comes first ("enough money"), but after an adjective — a classic position trap.
Source: PYQ-style

114. Identify the error: "The scenery of Kashmir are very beautiful."
(a) very beautiful (b) The scenery of Kashmir (c) are (d) No error
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Scenery" is an uncountable noun and always takes a singular verb: "is very beautiful". Words like scenery, furniture, information, luggage never take plural verbs.
Source: NCERT Class 9 English Grammar

115. Choose the correct sentence:
(a) He is wiser than any boy in the class.
(b) He is wiser than any other boy in the class.
(c) He is wiser than all boy in the class.
(d) He is the wisest than any boy in the class.
Answer: (b)
Solution: Since "he" belongs to the class, "any other boy" is needed to exclude him from the comparison. (a) illogically compares him with himself — a favourite examiner trap.
Source: PYQ-style

116. Identify the error: "The population of India is greater than China."
(a) is greater (b) The population of India (c) than China (d) No error
Answer: (c)
Solution: The comparison must be between two populations, not between population and country: "greater than that of China". Comparing unlike things is a subtle hard-level trap.
Source: PYQ-style

117. Choose the correct sentence:
(a) No sooner had she entered the room when the phone rang.
(b) Hardly had she entered the room than the phone rang.
(c) Scarcely had she entered the room when the phone rang.
(d) No sooner she had entered the room than the phone rang.
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Scarcely/Hardly" pair with "when"; "No sooner" pairs with "than". Only (c) matches the correct pair with correct inversion ("had she entered").
Source: NCERT Class 10 English Grammar

118. Identify the error: "He is one of the best player in the team."
(a) He is one of (b) the best player (c) in the team (d) No error
Answer: (b)
Solution: "One of the + superlative + plural noun" — it must be "one of the best players". The singular "player" after "one of the" is a very common hard-level error.
Source: PYQ-style

119. Choose the correct sentence:
(a) The thief was caught by the police and beaten black and blue.
(b) The thief was caught by the police and was beaten black and blue.
(c) The thief caught by police and was beaten black and blue.
(d) The thief was caught by the police and being beaten black and blue.
Answer: (a)
Solution: With the same subject and auxiliary, the second "was" is redundant; parallel structure needs "caught... and beaten". (d) breaks parallelism with "being beaten".
Source: PYQ-style

120. Identify the error: "My house is more spacious than my friend."
(a) My house is (b) more spacious (c) than my friend (d) No error
Answer: (c)
Solution: A house must be compared with a house, not with a person: "than my friend's (house)". This faulty-comparison trap appears repeatedly in CGL/CHSL error questions.
Source: PYQ-style

121. Choose the correct sentence:
(a) He is the cleverest of all other students.
(b) He is cleverest than all students.
(c) He is the more clever of all students.
(d) He is the cleverest of all students.
Answer: (d)
Solution: With the superlative, "other" is unnecessary and wrong — "of all students" already includes everyone. (b) mixes superlative with "than"; (c) uses comparative for a group.
Source: NCERT Class 10 English Grammar

122. Identify the error: "Neither of the two boys have done his homework."
(a) Neither of the two boys (b) his homework (c) No error (d) have done
Answer: (d)
Solution: "Neither of" takes a singular verb: "has done". The plural noun "boys" sitting next to the verb is an intervening-phrase trap designed to pull you toward "have".
Source: PYQ-style

123. Choose the correct sentence:
(a) The Ganga is one of the longest river in India.
(b) The Ganga is one of the longest rivers in India.
(c) The Ganga is one of longest rivers in India.
(d) The Ganga is one of the longer rivers in India.
Answer: (b)
Solution: "One of the + superlative + plural noun": "one of the longest rivers". (a) uses singular "river"; (c) drops "the"; (d) uses comparative where superlative is required.
Source: NCERT Class 9 English Grammar

124. Identify the error: "He is too weak that he cannot lift this bag."
(a) He is (b) too weak (c) lift this bag (d) that he cannot
Answer: (d)
Solution: The correct pattern is "too + adjective + to + verb": "too weak to lift this bag". "Too... that" is not standard English, so the faulty connector lies in part (d) — a sound-right trap that feels natural in Hindi-influenced speech.
Source: PYQ-style

125. Choose the correct sentence:
(a) The principal, along with the teachers, are present at the meeting. (b) The principal, along with the teachers, were present at the meeting. (c) The principal along with the teachers have been present at the meeting. (d) The principal, along with the teachers, was present at the meeting.
Answer: (d)
Solution: With "along with", the verb agrees with the first subject ("the principal" — singular), so "was present". The intervening phrase "along with the teachers" is the examiner's trap.
Source: PYQ-style
