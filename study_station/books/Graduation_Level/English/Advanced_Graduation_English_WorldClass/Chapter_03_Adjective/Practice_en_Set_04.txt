76. Choose the correctly spelt adjective:
(a) benefical (b) beneficial (c) benificial (d) benneficial
Answer: (b)
Solution: 'Beneficial' is the correct spelling — benefit + -ial. 'Benefical' misses the 'i', and 'benificial' wrongly uses 'i' instead of 'e'.
Source: PYQ-style

77. Choose the correct adjective for the sentence: "She is the ___ of the two sisters."
(a) tallest (b) taller (c) tall (d) most tall
Answer: (b)
Solution: When selecting one out of two persons/things, 'the + comparative' is used — "the taller of the two". The superlative (tallest) is for three or more.
Source: NCERT Class 9 English Grammar

78. Choose the part with the error: "He is more superior (a)/ to me (b)/ in rank. (c)/ No error (d)"
Answer: (a)
Solution: 'Superior' is a Latin comparative, so 'more' is not used with it. Correct: "He is superior to me in rank." Similarly, inferior, senior, junior, prior, posterior do not take 'more/most' or 'than'.
Source: PYQ-style

79. Choose the correct sentence:
(a) The gold is a precious metal. (b) Gold is a precious metal. (c) A gold is a precious metal. (d) An gold is a precious metal.
Answer: (b)
Solution: As a material noun, 'gold' takes no article. 'The gold' would be correct only if some specific gold were being referred to.
Source: NCERT Class 10 English Grammar

80. "The ___ always helps the ___." — Fill in the blanks:
(a) rich, poors (b) riches, poor (c) rich, poor (d) richer, poorer
Answer: (c)
Solution: 'The + adjective' denotes a whole class: the rich (the wealthy class), the poor (the poor class). These take a plural verb and have no plural form (poors).
Source: NCERT Class 9 English Grammar

81. Choose the part with the error: "I have (a)/ a five-rupees note (b)/ in my pocket. (c)/ No error (d)"
Answer: (b)
Solution: A hyphenated compound adjective formed with a number + noun is always singular: "a five-rupee note", "a ten-year-old boy". Here 'rupees' is wrong.
Source: PYQ-style

82. Choose the sentence with the correct order of adjectives:
(a) a beautiful old Italian painting (b) an Italian beautiful old painting (c) an old beautiful Italian painting (d) a painting beautiful old Italian
Answer: (a)
Solution: The order of adjectives is: Opinion → Size → Age → Shape → Colour → Origin → Material → Purpose (OSASCOMP). 'Beautiful' (opinion) → 'old' (age) → 'Italian' (origin) is the correct order.
Source: NCERT Class 10 English Grammar

83. "He is ___ honest man." — Fill in the blank:
(a) a (b) an (c) the (d) no article
Answer: (b)
Solution: In 'honest' the 'h' is silent and the pronunciation begins with a vowel sound, so 'an' is used. Follow the sound, not the letter.
Source: NCERT Class 8 English Grammar

84. Choose the part with the error: "No sooner did he arrive (a)/ than the meeting (b)/ started. (c)/ No error (d)"
Answer: (d)
Solution: The sentence is completely correct. 'No sooner...than' is the correct pair; 'hardly/scarcely' takes 'when' — this is the examiner's favourite trap.
Source: PYQ-style

85. "This is the ___ book I have ever read." — Fill in the blank:
(a) most interesting (b) more interesting (c) interesting (d) most interested
Answer: (a)
Solution: 'I have ever read' shows a comparison among many books, so the superlative 'most interesting' is needed. 'Interested' (a feeling) is wrong in meaning here.
Source: NCERT Class 9 English Grammar

86. Choose the correct sentence:
(a) He is enough strong to lift it. (b) He is strong enough to lift it. (c) He strong is enough to lift it. (d) Enough he is strong to lift it.
Answer: (b)
Solution: 'Enough' comes after an adjective (strong enough) but before a noun (enough money). This positional rule is asked repeatedly in exams.
Source: NCERT Class 10 English Grammar

87. Choose the part with the error: "My house is (a)/ farther than (b)/ yours. (c)/ No error (d)"
Answer: (d)
Solution: The sentence is correct — 'farther' is right for distance. 'Further' is used in the sense of additional/more (further information). Since distance is compared here, 'farther' is fine.
Source: PYQ-style

88. "The ___ news made everyone sad." — Fill in the blank:
(a) late (b) latest (c) later (d) latter
Answer: (b)
Solution: 'Latest' = most recent/fresh (last in time). 'Late' = delayed; 'later' = afterwards; 'latter' = the second of two. By meaning, 'latest' is correct.
Source: NCERT Class 9 English Grammar

89. Choose the correct sentence:
(a) He is my elder brother. (b) He is elder than me. (c) He is my older brother than me. (d) He is elder to me than my sister.
Answer: (a)
Solution: 'Elder' is used attributively for family members' age and does not take 'than' ('elder to' is correct). Option (a) 'elder brother' is completely correct.
Source: PYQ-style

90. "___ information you gave me was useful." — Fill in the blank:
(a) An (b) Many (c) The (d) Few
Answer: (c)
Solution: 'Information' is an uncountable noun — it cannot take 'an/many/few'. Here specific information (that you gave) is meant, so 'the' is correct.
Source: NCERT Class 10 English Grammar

91. Choose the part with the error: "He gave me (a)/ many useful advices (b)/ on the matter. (c)/ No error (d)"
Answer: (b)
Solution: 'Advice' is uncountable; it has no plural 'advices'. Correct: "much useful advice" or "many useful suggestions".
Source: PYQ-style

92. "Of all the students, Ravi is ___." — Fill in the blank:
(a) the most intelligent (b) more intelligent (c) intelligent (d) most intelligentest
Answer: (a)
Solution: 'Of all' demands the superlative, and 'the' is essential before the superlative. There is no word like 'intelligentest' — multi-syllable adjectives take 'most'.
Source: NCERT Class 9 English Grammar

93. Choose the correct sentence:
(a) I prefer tea more than coffee. (b) I prefer tea over coffee than. (c) I prefer tea to coffee. (d) I prefer tea from coffee.
Answer: (c)
Solution: 'Prefer' itself carries a comparative meaning, so it takes 'to', not 'than' or 'more than'. "Prefer tea to coffee" is the correct form.
Source: PYQ-style

94. "There is ___ milk in the jug; go and buy some." — Fill in the blank:
(a) little (b) a little (c) the little (d) few
Answer: (a)
Solution: 'Little' = practically nothing (negative sense) — that is why one must go and buy. 'A little' = some (sufficient). 'Few' is for countable nouns.
Source: NCERT Class 9 English Grammar

95. Choose the part with the error: "The two first (a)/ chapters of this book (b)/ are very easy. (c)/ No error (d)"
Answer: (a)
Solution: The ordinal (first, second) comes before the cardinal (two, three). Correct: "The first two chapters".
Source: PYQ-style

96. Choose the part with the error: "He is the best (a)/ of the two brothers (b)/ in the family. (c)/ No error (d)"
Answer: (a)
Solution: ⚠️ Examiner's trap: seeing 'of the two' and using the superlative is a mistake. When two are compared, it should be 'the better of the two'. 'Best' is for three or more.
Source: PYQ-style

97. Choose the correct sentence:
(a) The population of India is greater than China. (b) The population of India is greater than that of China. (c) The population of India is more greater than that of China. (d) The population of India is greatest than China.
Answer: (b)
Solution: Comparison must be between like things — population with population, so 'that of China' is essential. 'More greater' is a double comparative, which is wrong.
Source: PYQ-style

98. Choose the part with the error: "No sooner had she (a)/ finished her speech (b)/ when the audience started clapping. (c)/ No error (d)"
Answer: (c)
Solution: ⚠️ Examiner's trap: 'No sooner' takes 'than', not 'when'. 'When' pairs only with 'hardly/scarcely'. Correct: "No sooner had she finished... than the audience started clapping."
Source: PYQ-style

99. "He is ___ university professor and ___ European by birth." — Fill in the blanks:
(a) a, a (b) an, an (c) a, an (d) an, a
Answer: (a)
Solution: Both 'university' (yu-sound) and 'European' (yu-sound) begin with the consonant sound /j/, so both take 'a'. Remember the sound rule, not the letter.
Source: NCERT Class 10 English Grammar

100. Choose the correct sentence:
(a) He is comparatively more better today. (b) He is much more better today. (c) He is much better today. (d) He is most better today.
Answer: (c)
Solution: 'Better' is itself comparative; it does not take 'more/most' (double comparative/superlative is wrong). To intensify a comparative, 'much/far' may be used — "much better" is correct.
Source: PYQ-style
