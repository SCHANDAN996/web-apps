# 📊 PYQ Analysis — Adjective

## How Exams Test Adjectives (Pattern Analysis)

Across SSC (CGL, CHSL, MTS, GD), Banking (IBPS, SBI), and Railways (NTPC, Group D), adjectives appear in **four recurring formats**:

1. **Spot the Error** — the most frequent format. The error hides in degree of comparison, adjective-adverb confusion, or wrong order of adjectives.
2. **Fill in the Blanks** — choosing between *fewer/less*, *elder/older*, *many/much*, *later/latter*.
3. **Sentence Improvement** — replacing a wrong comparative/superlative form.
4. **Cloze Test / Vocabulary** — picking the adjective that fits the tone of the passage.

**Sub-topics that repeat most often:**
- Comparative vs. Superlative degree (double comparatives, "than any other" constructions)
- Adjectives used as nouns (*the rich, the poor, the blind*)
- Adjective vs. Adverb confusion (*He sings good* ❌)
- Order of adjectives (Opinion–Size–Age–Shape–Colour–Origin–Material–Purpose)
- *Fewer/Less, Many/Much, Elder/Older, Later/Latter, Farther/Further*
- Adjectives followed by fixed prepositions (*afraid of, fond of, superior to*)

**Examiner's favourite traps:**
- **Sound-Right Trap:** "He is more superior to me" — sounds fine, but *superior* is already comparative; "more" is wrong.
- **Intervening Phrase Trap:** a long phrase between the subject and the adjective distracts you from agreement.
- **Near-Miss Pair Trap:** *economic/economical, historic/historical, childish/childlike* — similar words, different meanings.
- **Over-thinking Trap:** a simple *fewer/less* question dressed in a long sentence to make you doubt the obvious rule.

## Top 10 High-Yield Questions (with Solutions)

**Q1.** He is more superior to his brother in intelligence.
(a) He is more superior (b) to his brother (c) in intelligence (d) No error
**Answer: (a)**
**Solution:** *Superior* is a Latin comparative adjective; it never takes "more" and is followed by "to", not "than". Correct: "He is superior to his brother…"
**Trap type:** Sound-Right | **Source:** PYQ-style

**Q2.** The Ganga is one of the longest river in India.
(a) The Ganga is (b) one of the longest (c) river in India (d) No error
**Answer: (c)**
**Solution:** After "one of the + superlative", the noun must be plural: "one of the longest **rivers**".
**Trap type:** Sound-Right | **Source:** PYQ-style

**Q3.** Choose the correct sentence:
(a) There were less students today. (b) There were fewer students today.
(c) There were lesser students today. (d) There were fewest students today.
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** *Students* is countable → *fewer*. *Less* is for uncountable nouns; *lesser* means "smaller in importance".
**Trap type:** Near-Miss Pair | **Source:** PYQ-style

**Q4.** My elder brother is senior than me by five years.
(a) My elder brother (b) is senior (c) than me by five years (d) No error
**Answer: (c)**
**Solution:** *Senior* takes "to", not "than": "senior **to** me". Latin comparatives (senior, junior, superior, inferior, prior, posterior) all take "to".
**Trap type:** Sound-Right | **Source:** PYQ-style

**Q5.** She is the most beautiful of the two sisters.
(a) She is the most beautiful (b) of the two sisters (c) No error (d) —
**Answer: (a)**
**Solution:** Comparing only **two** persons requires the comparative degree: "the **more** beautiful of the two sisters".
**Trap type:** Over-thinking | **Source:** PYQ-style

**Q6.** He felt badly about losing the match.
(a) He felt (b) badly about (c) losing the match (d) No error
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** *Feel* here is a linking verb describing his state, so it takes an **adjective**: "felt **bad**". (*Badly* would describe how he performed an action.)
**Trap type:** Sound-Right | **Source:** PYQ-style

**Q7.** Choose the correctly ordered phrase:
(a) a wooden old big table (b) a big old wooden table
(c) an old big wooden table (d) a wooden big old table
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** Order of adjectives: Size → Age → Material (OSASCOMP). "Big (size) + old (age) + wooden (material)".
**Trap type:** Over-thinking | **Source:** PYQ-style

**Q8.** The rich should help the poors.
(a) The rich should (b) help (c) the poors (d) No error
**Answer: (c)**
**Solution:** Adjectives used as nouns (*the rich, the poor*) are already plural in meaning; never add -s. Correct: "the poor".
**Trap type:** Sound-Right | **Source:** PYQ-style

**Q9.** Of all the players, Rohan played ______.
(a) good (b) better (c) the best (d) more well
**Answer: (c)**
**Solution:** "Of all" signals a superlative; *well* → *best*. "Good" is an adjective and cannot modify the verb *played*.
**Trap type:** Near-Miss Pair | **Source:** PYQ-style

**Q10.** This is the most unique painting in the gallery.
(a) This is the most unique (b) painting (c) in the gallery (d) No error
**Answer: (a)**
**Solution:** *Unique* is an absolute adjective — something is either unique or not; it cannot take "most". Correct: "a unique painting".
**Trap type:** Sound-Right | **Source:** PYQ-style

## Applying the 15/45-Second Rule

**15-second (Fact-based) — Q3 (fewer/less):** The moment you see a countable plural noun (*students*), the rule fires instantly: *fewer*. No need to read the sentence twice. Target: under 15 seconds.

**45-second (Inference-based) — Q6 (felt badly):** This one needs thought. Ask: is *feel* an action verb or a linking verb here? Since it describes his emotional **state**, it links to an adjective → *bad*. If the sentence were "He played badly", the adverb would be correct. This verb-type check justifies 45 seconds.

**Exam strategy:** Degree-of-comparison and fewer/less questions are almost always 15-second questions — bank them fast and spend the saved time on linking-verb and order-of-adjective questions, which reward careful reading.
