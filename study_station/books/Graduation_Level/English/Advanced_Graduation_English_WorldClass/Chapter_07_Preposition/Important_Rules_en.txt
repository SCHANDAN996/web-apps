# 📋 Important Grammar Rules & Tips — Preposition

## (a) 12 High-Yield Rules

| # | Rule | Example | Examiner's Trap |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **No preposition after "discuss, order, request, reach, enter, marry, accompany, attack, resemble"** (transitive verbs) | He entered **the room**. / We discussed **the plan**. | ❌ "entered into the room", "discussed about" — the most repeated trap in SSC/Bank exams. |
| 2 | **"Different from", not "different than/to"** | Your answer is different **from** mine. | ❌ "different than" is common in speech; exams want "from". |
| 3 | **Time: at (point) / on (day/date) / in (month, year, part of day)** | at 5 p.m., on Monday, on 15 August, in July, in 2024, in the morning | ❌ "in Monday", "at the morning". Trap: "on Monday morning" (specific day → on). |
| 4 | **Place: at (point) / in (enclosed/large area) / on (surface)** | at the station, in Delhi, on the table | ❌ "in the bus stop" → "at the bus stop". |
| 5 | **Since (point of time) vs For (period of time)** | since 2010, since Monday; for ten years, for a week | ❌ "since ten years" — classic error-spotting item. |
| 6 | **By (agent/deadline) vs With (instrument)** | killed by the robber **with** a knife; finish it **by** 5 p.m. | ❌ "killed with the robber". Passive voice hides the agent — check carefully. |
| 7 | **Between (two) vs Among (more than two)** | between Ram and Shyam; among the students | ❌ "between the three brothers" → "among". |
| 8 | **Beside (next to) vs Besides (in addition to)** | Sit beside me. / Besides English, she knows French. | One extra "s" changes the meaning — favourite fill-in-the-blank trap. |
| 9 | **In (within, at the end of time) vs Within (before the limit)** | He will return **in** a week (after a week). / He will return **within** a week (before a week ends). | Exams test the subtle difference in sentence improvement. |
| 10 | **Adjective + fixed preposition: afraid of, fond of, good at, angry with (person)/at (thing), interested in, similar to, superior to, junior to** | She is good **at** maths. He is junior **to** me. | ❌ "junior than me" — Latin comparatives (junior, senior, superior, inferior, prefer) take **to**, never "than". |
| 11 | **Verb + fixed preposition: depend on, insist on, rely on, consist of, look after, look into, agree with (person)/to (proposal), apologize to (person) for (thing)** | The committee will look **into** the matter. | ❌ "consist in", "discuss about". Phrasal-verb swaps (look into/look after/look over) are high-yield. |
| 12 | **Preposition is retained when a question or relative clause moves it** | Who are you talking **to**? / This is the house I lived **in**. | ❌ Dropping the preposition in error-spotting: "the house I lived" is wrong. |

## (b) Time Management — The 15/45-Second Rule

**Fact-based questions (target: 15 seconds)** — answer from memory, no re-reading:
- Fixed preposition collocations (depend on, afraid of, junior to)
- Since/for, at/on/in, between/among, beside/besides
- "No preposition" verbs (discuss, enter, reach, order)
- One-word fill-in-the-blank with a standard phrase

**Inference-based questions (target: 45 seconds)** — slow down, parse the sentence:
- Error-spotting where the preposition error hides inside a long intervening phrase
- Sentence improvement with two grammatically "possible" options — check meaning (in vs within, by vs with)
- Phrasal-verb questions where context decides (look into = investigate, look after = care for)
- Questions where the preposition sits at the end of a question/relative clause

**Rule of thumb:** if the blank follows a verb/adjective directly → 15 sec. If you must read the whole sentence twice to find the subject or the agent → 45 sec.

## (c) Morphology — Roots & Affixes Behind Preposition Vocabulary

| Root / Affix | Meaning | Derived Words |
|---|---|---|
| **pre-** (Latin) | before | preposition, prefix, predict, precede |
| **post-** (Latin) | after | postpone, postscript, post-mortem |
| **inter-** (Latin) | between | international, intervene, interval |
| **sub-** (Latin) | under | submarine, subscribe, subordinate |
| **super- / sur-** (Latin) | over, above | supervise, surface, surpass |
| **trans-** (Latin) | across | transport, transfer, translate |
| **ad-** (Latin, → a-/ac-/af-) | to, towards | advance, accompany, affect |
| **ex- / e-** (Latin) | out of | exit, exclude, emit |
| **circum-** (Latin) | around | circumference, circumstance |
| **-position** (Latin *ponere/positum*) | to place | position, deposit, composition |

**Why this matters:** the word *preposition* itself = *pre-* (before) + *position* (placed) — "a word placed before" a noun. Most English prepositions and phrasal-verb particles carry these root meanings, so knowing the roots lets you guess unfamiliar phrasal verbs (e.g., *circumvent* = go around = avoid).
