Questions 76–95: Easy | Questions 96–100: Medium

76. He is very fond ________ playing chess in the evening.
(a) of (b) in (c) at (d) for
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Fond of" is the fixed adjective-preposition combination, followed by the -ing form. "Fond in" and "fond for" do not exist in standard English.
Source: NCERT Class 8 English

77. He has been suffering ________ fever since Monday.
(a) with (b) from (c) by (d) of
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Suffer from" is the fixed verb-preposition combination used for illnesses. "Suffer with" and "suffer of" are incorrect in standard English.
Source: NCERT Class 10 English

78. The manager congratulated me ________ my success.
(a) for (b) about (c) at (d) on
Answer: (d)
Solution: "Congratulate someone on something" is the fixed pattern. "Congratulate for" is a common Hindi-influenced error.
Source: PYQ-style

79. She is senior ________ me by two years.
(a) than (b) from (c) over (d) to
Answer: (d)
Solution: Latin comparatives like senior, junior, superior, inferior and prefer take "to", not "than". This is a classic examiner trap.
Source: PYQ-style

80. The train arrived exactly ________ time.
(a) in (b) at (c) on (d) by
Answer: (c)
Solution: "On time" means punctually, at the scheduled moment. "In time" means early enough for something, which changes the meaning.
Source: NCERT Class 9 English

81. He was accused ________ stealing the documents.
(a) of (b) for (c) with (d) on
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Accused of" is the fixed combination for charges or blame. "Accused for" is a frequent error among Hindi speakers.
Source: PYQ-style

82. Spot the error: The teacher (P) / asked the students (Q) / to discuss about (R) / the chapter in groups. (S)
(a) Part P (b) Part Q (c) No error (d) Part R
Answer: (d)
Solution: "Discuss" is a transitive verb and never takes "about"; we discuss something directly. Part R should read "to discuss".
Source: PYQ-style

83. The two brothers divided the property ________ themselves.
(a) between (b) among (c) within (d) into
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Between" is used when dividing something between two persons; "among" is used for more than two. Since only two brothers are involved, "between themselves" is correct.
Source: NCERT Class 9 English

84. I am looking forward ________ meeting you next week.
(a) for (b) on (c) at (d) to
Answer: (d)
Solution: In "look forward to", the word "to" is a preposition, so it is followed by the -ing form "meeting". Learners often wrongly treat it as an infinitive.
Source: PYQ-style

85. He jumped ________ the river to save the child.
(a) in (b) on (c) into (d) upon
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Into" shows movement from outside to inside, which fits jumping to enter the river. "In" only shows position, not motion.
Source: NCERT Class 8 English

86. The flight was delayed ________ bad weather.
(a) because (b) as (c) since (d) due to
Answer: (d)
Solution: A preposition is needed before the noun phrase "bad weather"; "due to" fills that role. "Because", "since" and "as" are conjunctions needing a clause.
Source: PYQ-style

87. She insisted ________ paying the bill herself.
(a) in (b) at (c) for (d) on
Answer: (d)
Solution: "Insist on" is the fixed verb-preposition pair, followed by the -ing form. No other option collocates with "insist".
Source: PYQ-style

88. Spot the error: Despite of (P) / heavy rain, (Q) / the match continued (R) / till evening. (S)
(a) Part R (b) Part Q (c) Part P (d) No error
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Despite" never takes "of"; either say "despite heavy rain" or "in spite of heavy rain". Part P is faulty — a favourite examiner trap.
Source: PYQ-style

89. The soldiers fought ________ the last man.
(a) till (b) to (c) until (d) upto
Answer: (b)
Solution: "To the last man" is the idiomatic expression meaning until everyone. "Till" and "until" take time expressions, not "the last man".
Source: PYQ-style

90. He is devoid ________ any sense of responsibility.
(a) from (b) with (c) of (d) in
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Devoid of" is the fixed adjective-preposition combination meaning "lacking". The other prepositions do not collocate with "devoid".
Source: PYQ-style

91. The child hid ________ the bed when he heard the noise.
(a) under (b) below (c) beneath of (d) down
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Under" is the natural preposition for position directly below something like a bed. "Below" suggests a lower level generally, and "beneath of" is ungrammatical.
Source: NCERT Class 8 English

92. We must abide ________ the rules of the institution.
(a) by (b) with (c) to (d) on
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Abide by" is the fixed phrasal-prepositional verb meaning "to obey or follow". This combination is frequently tested.
Source: PYQ-style

93. The manager dealt ________ the complaint very firmly.
(a) in (b) with (c) about (d) over
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Deal with" means to handle or tackle a matter. "Deal in" means to trade in goods, which does not fit the context.
Source: PYQ-style

94. He has been living in Delhi ________ 2015.
(a) since (b) from (c) for (d) by
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Since" is used with a point of time (a year, date, day); "for" is used with a period of time. "2015" is a point of time.
Source: NCERT Class 9 English

95. Spot the error: He entered (P) / into the room (Q) / without knocking (R) / at the door. (S)
(a) Part P (b) Part Q (c) Part R (d) No error
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Enter" is never followed by "into" when it means to go inside a place; we simply "enter the room". Part Q is the error.
Source: PYQ-style

96. The poem was written ________ a famous poet of the era.
(a) with (b) from (c) by (d) through
Answer: (c)
Solution: In the passive sense of authorship, the agent is introduced by "by". "With" would indicate an instrument, not the doer.
Source: NCERT Class 10 English

97. She is anxious ________ her son's safety.
(a) about (b) for (c) of (d) on
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Anxious about" expresses worry concerning something. "Anxious for" means eager to obtain something, which changes the meaning.
Source: PYQ-style

98. The new policy will come ________ effect from April.
(a) in (b) to (c) into (d) with
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Come into effect" is the fixed idiom meaning to become operative. "Come in effect" is a common but incorrect variant.
Source: PYQ-style

99. Hardly had he stepped out of the house ________ it began to rain.
(a) than (b) when (c) then (d) that
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Hardly... when" is the correct correlative pair. "Than" pairs with "no sooner", and mixing the two is a classic examiner trap.
Source: PYQ-style

100. Choose the correctly framed sentence:
(a) He is capable to handle any crisis. (b) He is capable of handling any crisis. (c) He is capable for handling any crisis. (d) He is capable in handling any crisis.
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Capable of" is the fixed adjective-preposition combination, and a preposition must be followed by the -ing form "handling". The other options use wrong prepositions or an infinitive.
Source: PYQ-style
