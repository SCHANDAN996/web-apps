Questions 101–120: Easy | Questions 121–125: Medium

101. The members of the committee were divided _______ themselves on the funding issue.
(a) into (b) between (c) within (d) among
Answer: (d)
Solution: "Divided among themselves" is the idiomatic reflexive use for a group of more than two people. "Between themselves" is reserved for exactly two parties, and a committee is treated as a larger group here.
Source: PYQ-style

102. He is junior _______ me by two years.
(a) to (b) from (c) than (d) by
Answer: (a)
Solution: Latin comparatives like junior, senior, superior, inferior, prior and posterior take "to", never "than". This is a classic examiner trap because "than" sounds natural after a comparative idea.
Source: NCERT-based grammar rule

103. Spot the error: Part P: He insisted / Part Q: to go / Part R: with me / Part S: to the station.
(a) Part P (b) Part Q (c) Part R (d) No error
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Insist" is followed by "on" + gerund: "insisted on going". Part Q wrongly uses the infinitive "to go". Verbs like insist, persist, succeed and concentrate take a preposition + -ing, not the infinitive.
Source: PYQ-style

104. She has been suffering _______ fever since Monday.
(a) with (b) by (c) from (d) of
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Suffer from" is used for diseases and ailments; "suffer" with other prepositions is wrong here. "Die of" a disease but "suffer from" it — examiners love pairing these two.
Source: PYQ-style

105. The train arrived exactly _______ time.
(a) in (b) on (c) at (d) by
Answer: (b)
Solution: "On time" means punctually, at the scheduled moment; "in time" means early enough for a purpose. Since the train arrived exactly as scheduled, "on time" fits. This pair is a frequent trap.
Source: PYQ-style

106. He was accused _______ theft by the police.
(a) for (b) of (c) with (d) about
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Accused of" is the fixed preposition. Compare: "charged with" a crime, "blamed for" something, "convicted of" an offence. Examiners swap these to test collocation memory.
Source: PYQ-style

107. Spot the error: Part P: Despite of / Part Q: his wealth, / Part R: he lives / Part S: very simply.
(a) Part Q (b) Part P (c) Part R (d) No error
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Despite" never takes "of" — either say "despite his wealth" or "in spite of his wealth". Part P combines both, a very common error among Hindi speakers translating "के बावजूद".
Source: PYQ-style

108. The two brothers quarrelled _______ themselves over the property.
(a) among (b) amongst (c) within (d) between
Answer: (d)
Solution: "Between" is used when exactly two persons are involved, even with a reflexive pronoun: "the two brothers quarrelled between themselves". "Among/amongst" is for more than two, so it cannot fit "two brothers".
Source: PYQ-style

109. I am looking forward _______ meeting you soon.
(a) to (b) for (c) at (d) towards
Answer: (a)
Solution: In "look forward to", "to" is a preposition, so it must be followed by a gerund (meeting), not an infinitive. This is a top examiner trap because "to" usually signals an infinitive.
Source: PYQ-style

110. He is devoid _______ any sense of responsibility.
(a) from (b) in (c) of (d) with
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Devoid of" means completely lacking. Similar adjectives taking "of": bereft of, conscious of, guilty of, fond of, proud of.
Source: PYQ-style

111. The manager dealt _______ the complaint very firmly.
(a) on (b) over (c) about (d) with
Answer: (d)
Solution: "Deal with" means to handle or tackle a matter. "Deal in" means to trade in goods (he deals in spices). The two meanings are frequently confused in exams.
Source: PYQ-style

112. Spot the error: Part P: Since I was ill, / Part Q: I could not / Part R: attend to / Part S: the meeting yesterday.
(a) No error (b) Part Q (c) Part P (d) Part R
Answer: (d)
Solution: "Attend" meaning "be present at" takes no preposition: "attend the meeting". "Attend to" means to take care of something or someone. Part R wrongly inserts "to".
Source: PYQ-style

113. She congratulated me _______ my success in the examination.
(a) on (b) for (c) at (d) about
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Congratulate someone on something" is the fixed pattern. "For" is the tempting wrong choice because Hindi uses "के लिए" — a classic linguistic-bridge trap.
Source: PYQ-style

114. The old man died _______ hunger during the famine.
(a) of (b) with (c) by (d) from
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Die of" is used for disease, hunger, thirst and old age; "die from" for injuries or external causes; "die by" for the means; "die in" an accident or war. Hunger takes "of".
Source: PYQ-style

115. He has been living in Delhi _______ 2015.
(a) from (b) for (c) since (d) by
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Since" is used with a point of time (2015, Monday, childhood); "for" with a period of time (ten years). 2015 is a point, so "since" is correct — a perennial exam favourite.
Source: NCERT Class 9 English

116. Spot the error: Part P: He is / Part Q: capable to handle / Part R: any situation / Part S: calmly.
(a) Part P (b) Part R (c) No error (d) Part Q
Answer: (d)
Solution: "Capable" takes "of" + gerund: "capable of handling". The infinitive after "capable" is wrong, though "able to handle" would be correct. Examiners exploit this able/capable contrast.
Source: PYQ-style

117. The soldiers fought _______ the last man.
(a) until (b) till (c) to (d) upto
Answer: (c)
Solution: "To the last man" is the idiomatic phrase meaning until everyone was involved or had fallen. "Till/until" refer to time, not extent; "upto" is not standard spelling (it is "up to").
Source: PYQ-style

118. I differ _______ you on this point.
(a) from (b) with (c) to (d) than
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Differ with" a person means to disagree; "differ from" means to be unlike (this cloth differs from that one). Since the sentence is about disagreement with a person, "with" is correct.
Source: PYQ-style

119. He was absorbed _______ his studies when I entered.
(a) into (b) in (c) with (d) at
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Absorbed in" means fully engrossed. Similar pairs: engaged in, interested in, involved in, immersed in — all take "in" for deep involvement.
Source: PYQ-style

120. Spot the error: Part P: One should / Part Q: abide with / Part R: the rules / Part S: of the institution.
(a) Part Q (b) No error (c) Part R (d) Part P
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Abide by" means to comply with rules or decisions. "Abide with" is archaic and means to stay with someone. Part Q uses the wrong preposition.
Source: PYQ-style

121. The doctor advised him to abstain _______ smoking.
(a) of (b) off (c) from (d) away
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Abstain from" is the fixed collocation meaning to refrain deliberately. Similar verbs with "from": refrain from, desist from, escape from, prohibit from.
Source: PYQ-style

122. She is anxious _______ her son's safety.
(a) for (b) on (c) about (d) of
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Anxious about" expresses worry concerning something; "anxious for" expresses eager desire (anxious for a change). Worry about safety takes "about" — the two shades are a subtle hard-level trap.
Source: PYQ-style

123. The building was _______ fire when we reached.
(a) on (b) in (c) at (d) with
Answer: (a)
Solution: "On fire" is the idiom meaning burning. "In fire" is a literal translation of Hindi "आग में" and is wrong here. Idiomatic prepositional phrases must be memorised as wholes.
Source: PYQ-style

124. He jumped _______ the conclusion that she was guilty.
(a) to (b) at (c) into (d) upon
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Jump to a conclusion" is the correct idiom meaning to decide hastily. "Jump at" means to accept eagerly (jump at an offer). Examiners test such near-identical idioms at the hard level.
Source: PYQ-style

125. Spot the error: Part P: Being a rainy day, / Part Q: I decided / Part R: to stay / Part S: at home.
(a) No error (b) Part Q (c) Part R (d) Part P
Answer: (d)
Solution: Part P is a dangling modifier: "Being a rainy day" illogically describes "I". It should be "It being a rainy day, I decided…". Hard-level error questions often hide the mistake in the opening phrase rather than in a preposition.
Source: PYQ-style
