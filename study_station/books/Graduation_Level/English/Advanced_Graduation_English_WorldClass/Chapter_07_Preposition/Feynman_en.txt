🧒 FEYNMAN TECHNIQUE — Prepositions Explained Like You're 12

The Hardest Concept: Why "married TO" but "angry WITH"? Why "good AT" but "interested IN"?

Imagine prepositions are like the tiny screws in a mobile phone. Nobody notices them, but if you put the wrong screw in the wrong hole, the whole phone falls apart. English has thousands of fixed "screw-and-hole" pairs — verb + preposition, adjective + preposition — and there is NO logic you can reason out. You cannot guess "married with" just because it sounds fine in your head. The pair is fixed by habit, not by grammar rules.

So how do you master something that has no logic? You stop trying to find logic — and start treating each pair like a friendship.

🏠 The Daily-Life Example: The House of Friends

Picture a big house. Every room has a nameplate, and inside each room lives a word with its best friend (its preposition). They NEVER change partners:

- Room 1: "Married" lives with "TO" → married TO Rahul (never "married with")
- Room 2: "Angry" lives with "WITH" (for people) and "AT" (for things) → angry WITH me, angry AT the delay
- Room 3: "Good" lives with "AT" → good AT cricket
- Room 4: "Interested" lives with "IN" → interested IN music
- Room 5: "Depend" lives with "ON" → depend ON parents
- Room 6: "Different" lives with "FROM" → different FROM mine (examiners LOVE to trick you with "different than")

Your job is not to understand WHY they are friends. Your job is to visit the house so often that you can walk through it blindfolded.

🔑 The Unforgettable Trick: "The Glue Test"

Whenever you see a verb or adjective in a sentence, ask: "What is glued to it?" Say the pair aloud three times like a chant:

"Depend ON, depend ON, depend ON."
"Accused OF, accused OF, accused OF."
"Prevent FROM, prevent FROM, prevent FROM."

The ear remembers what the brain cannot reason. Toppers don't "solve" preposition questions — they HEAR the error, because the wrong pair sounds like a broken song. "He was accused with theft" should hurt your ears. If it doesn't hurt yet, chant more pairs.

⏱️ Why this matters in the exam: A preposition question is a 15-second question ONLY if the pair is already glued in your memory. If you start "thinking" in the exam hall, you have already lost — because there is nothing to think about. Memory, not logic.

📝 Blurting Sheet Instruction

Close this book. Take a blank page. In 3 minutes, blurt out every fixed pair you can remember — verb + preposition and adjective + preposition. Don't organize, don't think, just dump. Now open the book and tick what you got right. Every pair you missed goes on tomorrow's list. Repeat after 2 days, then after 7 days. The pairs that survive three rounds are yours forever.
