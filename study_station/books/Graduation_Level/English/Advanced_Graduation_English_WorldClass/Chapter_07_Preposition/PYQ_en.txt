# 📊 PYQ Analysis — Preposition (Pattern Study of the Last Decade)

## How Exams Test Prepositions

Across SSC (CGL, CHSL, GD, MTS), IBPS (PO/Clerk), RRB (NTPC, Group D) and State PCS prelims, prepositions appear in **four recurring formats**:

1. **Fill in the blank** — one preposition missing in a sentence (the most common format).
2. **Spot the error** — a wrong or extra preposition hidden in one part of the sentence.
3. **Idiom/Phrase-based** — fixed prepositional phrases like *abide by, cope with, consist of*.
4. **Sentence improvement** — replace the underlined prepositional phrase with the correct one.

## Which Sub-Topics Repeat Most Often

- **Fixed prepositions after verbs/adjectives/nouns** — *depend on, insist on, afraid of, good at, angry with (a person), angry at (a thing), different from, married to, junior/senior to, prefer X to Y*. This is the single most fertile area; examiners know students translate from Hindi and write *"different than"* or *"married with"*.
- **Time prepositions** — *in / on / at* (in 2024, on Monday, at 5 p.m.), *since vs for*, *by vs until*.
- **Place prepositions** — *in / on / at*, *between vs among*, *beside vs besides*, *above vs over*.
- **Movement prepositions** — *into vs in*, *through vs across*, *towards*.
- **Phrasal/prepositional verbs** — *look after, put off, give in, call off* — often tested as meaning-based MCQs.
- **Omission traps** — sentences where Indian English adds an extra preposition: *"discuss about", "comprise of", "enter into the room", "reach to Delhi"*.

## The Examiner's Favourite Traps (in words, not numbers)

- **Near-correct sound trap**: options like *"discuss about"* sound natural to Hindi speakers ("के बारे में चर्चा करना") but are wrong — *discuss* takes no preposition.
- **Literal-translation trap**: *"angry on"*, *"married with"*, *"different than"* — all born from Hindi sentence patterns.
- **Intervening-phrase trap**: a long phrase between the verb and the blank distracts you from the fixed combination, e.g., *"He insisted, despite our repeated requests, ___ going alone."* (answer: *on*).
- **Over-thinking trap**: the simplest option (*at / in / on*) is correct, but a fancy option (*within / unto / amidst*) tempts advanced students.
- **Since/For confusion**: *since* + point of time (Monday, 2010, childhood); *for* + period (two years, a long time).

## Top 10 High-Yield Questions (PYQ-style) with Solutions

**Q1.** He has been working in this bank ___ 2015.
(a) for (b) since (c) from (d) by
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** 2015 is a *point of time*, so *since* is correct. *For* needs a period (for nine years). *From* would need *from 2015 to now*.
**Trap type:** Sound-Right (Hindi "से" suggests *from*).
**Source:** PYQ-style (concept: NCERT Class 9–10 Grammar)

**Q2.** The committee discussed ___ the proposal for two hours.
(a) about (b) on (c) over (d) — (no preposition)
**Answer: (d)**
**Solution:** *Discuss* is a transitive verb and takes a direct object — no preposition. *Discuss about* is the classic Indian-English error.
**Trap type:** Sound-Right / literal translation.
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q3.** She is junior ___ me by two years.
(a) than (b) from (c) to (d) with
**Answer: (c)**
**Solution:** Latin comparatives — *junior, senior, superior, inferior, prior, posterior* — take *to*, never *than*.
**Trap type:** Over-thinking (*than* looks right because of "comparison").
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q4.** I prefer tea ___ coffee.
(a) than (b) over (c) to (d) from
**Answer: (c)**
**Solution:** *Prefer X to Y* is the fixed pattern. *Prefer tea over coffee* is acceptable in modern informal usage, but in exam grammar *to* is the expected answer.
**Trap type:** Sound-Right.
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q5.** The train arrived exactly ___ 6 o'clock.
(a) in (b) on (c) at (d) by
**Answer: (c)**
**Solution:** *At* + exact clock time; *on* + day/date; *in* + month/year/season. This is a pure 15-second recall question.
**Trap type:** None — direct fact.
**Source:** NCERT Class 8 Grammar

**Q6.** He was married ___ his childhood friend last year.
(a) with (b) to (c) by (d) from
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** *Married to* is the fixed preposition. *Married with* is a Hindi-translation error ("से शादी की").
**Trap type:** Literal translation.
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q7.** The manager, along with his entire team of senior executives, insisted ___ revising the schedule.
(a) upon (b) on (c) at (d) for
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** The long intervening phrase hides the fixed combination *insist on*. Both *insist on* and *insist upon* exist, but when only one is offered with *on*, choose it; here (b) is the standard exam answer.
**Trap type:** Intervening Phrase.
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q8.** He jumped ___ the river to save the child.
(a) in (b) into (c) inside (d) within
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** Movement from outside to inside demands *into*. *In* shows position (he is *in* the river), not motion.
**Trap type:** Sound-Right.
**Source:** NCERT Class 9 Grammar

**Q9.** The property was divided equally ___ the four brothers.
(a) between (b) among (c) in (d) within
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** *Between* for two, *among* for more than two. (Note: *between* is acceptable with more than two when individual one-to-one relations are stressed, but the standard exam rule gives *among* here.)
**Trap type:** Over-thinking.
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q10.** Besides ___ English, she speaks French and German.
(a) speaking (b) speak (c) to speak (d) spoke
**Answer: (a)**
**Solution:** *Besides* is a preposition here (= in addition to), and a preposition is followed by a gerund (*-ing* form). Don't confuse *besides* (in addition to) with *beside* (next to).
**Trap type:** Sound-Right / word-confusion.
**Source:** PYQ-style

## Applying the 15/45-Second Rule

**15-second question (Fact-based): Q5** — *at 6 o'clock* is a memorised rule. Read, recall, mark, move on. Never spend more than 15 seconds on *in/on/at* time-place questions; if the rule doesn't click instantly, flag it and return.

**45-second question (Inference-based): Q7** — the intervening phrase *"along with his entire team of senior executives"* must be mentally bracketed out before you can see *insisted ___ revising*. Strip the sentence to its skeleton, identify the fixed verb-preposition pair, then answer. Budget the full 45 seconds here — rushing this type causes the most errors.

---

## Key Takeaways

- Master the **fixed preposition list** (depend on, insist on, afraid of, good at, different from, married to, junior to, prefer to) — it answers the majority of exam questions.
- Learn the **no-preposition verbs**: discuss, enter, reach, comprise, accompany, attack, resemble, order (food).
- Treat *in/on/at, since/for, between/among, beside/besides, in/into* as automatic 15-second recalls.
- In error-spotting, first strip intervening phrases, then check the verb–preposition bond.
