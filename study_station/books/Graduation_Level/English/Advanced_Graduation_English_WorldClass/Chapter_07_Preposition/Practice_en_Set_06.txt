126. The committee was divided ______ its opinion on the new policy.
(a) over (b) on (c) in (d) into
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Divided in opinion" is the standard idiom when people hold different views. "Divided into" refers to splitting into parts, which tempts many candidates here.

127. He has been suffering ______ fever since Monday.
(a) from (b) with (c) by (d) of
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Suffer from" a disease is the fixed collocation. "Suffer with" is used for sharing someone's pain, not illness.

128. The manager was angry ______ the clerk's careless behaviour.
(a) with (b) on (c) at (d) upon
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Angry with" a person but "angry at" a thing, action, or behaviour. Here the object is behaviour, so "at" is correct — a classic examiner trap.

129. Spot the error: Part P: He is junior / Part Q: than me by / Part R: two years in service. / Part S: No error
(a) Part Q (b) Part P (c) Part R (d) No error
Answer: (a)
Solution: Part Q is wrong. Latin comparatives like junior, senior, superior, inferior take "to", not "than": "junior to me". The sound-right trap makes "than" feel natural.

130. She was married ______ a rich merchant last year.
(a) with (b) by (c) from (d) to
Answer: (d)
Solution: "Married to" is correct in active voice. "Married with" is a common Hindi-speaker error carried over from "usse shaadi hui".

131. The train arrived exactly ______ time despite the fog.
(a) on (b) in (c) at (d) by
Answer: (a)
Solution: "On time" means punctually at the scheduled moment; "in time" means early enough for a purpose. The sentence stresses exact punctuality, so "on" fits.

132. He is devoid ______ any sense of responsibility.
(a) of (b) with (c) from (d) in
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Devoid of" is a fixed phrase meaning completely lacking. No other preposition collocates with "devoid".

133. Spot the error: Part P: Despite of / Part Q: his wealth, he lives / Part R: a very simple life. / Part S: No error
(a) Part P (b) Part Q (c) Part R (d) No error
Answer: (a)
Solution: Part P is wrong. "Despite" never takes "of"; either say "despite his wealth" or "in spite of his wealth". This is one of the most repeated traps in SSC and bank exams.
Source: PYQ-style

134. The two brothers divided the property ______ themselves.
(a) among (b) between (c) in (d) within
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Between" is used for two persons; "among" for more than two. Since there are two brothers, "between" is correct. Note "between themselves", not "among themselves".
Source: NCERT-based grammar

135. I have known him ______ ten years.
(a) since (b) by (c) from (d) for
Answer: (d)
Solution: "For" is used with a period of time (ten years); "since" with a point of time (2014, Monday). The trap is confusing duration with starting point.
Source: PYQ-style

136. The child was afraid ______ the dark.
(a) from (b) of (c) with (d) by
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Afraid of" is the fixed adjective-preposition pair. Hindi speakers often write "afraid from" under the influence of "darna kisi se".
Source: PYQ-style

137. He insisted ______ going with me to the station.
(a) on (b) in (c) at (d) for
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Insist on" + gerund is the correct pattern. "Insist in" is a frequent error; the verb governs "on" before a noun or gerund.

138. Spot the error: Part P: Being a rainy day, / Part Q: I decided / Part R: to stay at home. / Part S: No error
(a) Part R (b) Part Q (c) Part P (d) No error
Answer: (c)
Solution: Part P has a dangling modifier — "being a rainy day" cannot describe "I". Correct: "It being a rainy day, I decided to stay at home." Exams test this as a prepositional-phrase trap.
Source: PYQ-style

139. The soldiers fought ______ the last man.
(a) till (b) until (c) to (d) upto
Answer: (c)
Solution: "To the last man" is the idiomatic phrase meaning until everyone was involved or fallen. "Till" needs a time reference, not a person-limit.
Source: Advanced grammar reference

140. She is eligible ______ this post as she holds a degree.
(a) to (b) for (c) of (d) in
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Eligible for" a post or position is the fixed collocation. "Eligible to" is used before a verb (eligible to apply), which lures candidates here.
Source: PYQ-style

141. The old man died ______ hunger during the famine.
(a) from (b) by (c) of (d) with
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Die of" is used for disease, hunger, thirst, grief; "die from" for external causes like injuries; "die by" for violence or suicide. Hunger takes "of".
Source: PYQ-style

142. Spot the error: Part P: He ordered / Part Q: for a cup of tea / Part R: and a plate of pakoras. / Part S: No error
(a) Part P (b) Part Q (c) Part R (d) No error
Answer: (b)
Solution: Part Q is wrong. "Order" takes a direct object without "for" when you request something be brought: "ordered a cup of tea". "Order for" is used only in special senses like placing an order for goods.
Source: PYQ-style

143. The teacher was pleased ______ my progress in mathematics.
(a) on (b) with (c) at (d) by
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Pleased with" a person or thing is standard. "Pleased at" can precede news or an event, but progress as an object takes "with".
Source: NCERT-based grammar

144. He jumped ______ the river to save the drowning boy.
(a) into (b) in (c) upon (d) on
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Into" shows movement from outside to inside; "in" shows position. Jumping involves motion, so "into" is correct — a subtle medium-hard distinction.
Source: PYQ-style

145. The principal congratulated me ______ my success.
(a) for (b) about (c) at (d) on
Answer: (d)
Solution: "Congratulate someone on something" is the fixed pattern. "For" is the common distractor because we say "thanks for", but congratulate governs "on".
Source: PYQ-style

146. Spot the error: Part P: One should / Part Q: abide with the rules / Part R: of the institution. / Part S: No error
(a) Part P (b) Part R (c) Part Q (d) No error
Answer: (c)
Solution: Part Q is wrong. "Abide by" rules is the correct phrasal verb; "abide with" is archaic and wrong here. Examiners love swapping "by" and "with" in phrasal verbs.
Source: PYQ-style

147. The flight was delayed ______ bad weather.
(a) since (b) due to (c) because (d) owing to
Answer: (d)
Solution: "Owing to" correctly introduces an adverbial phrase explaining the delay. "Due to" should follow a linking verb as an adjective (the delay was due to fog), so its use here is debated and examiners prefer "owing to" after an active verb clause.
Source: Advanced grammar reference

148. He is second ______ none in his knowledge of Sanskrit.
(a) than (b) to (c) from (d) after
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Second to none" is an idiom meaning the very best. The comparative-looking "second" tempts candidates toward "than", which is the trap.
Source: PYQ-style

149. Spot the error: Part P: The Ganges is / Part Q: one of the longest rivers / Part R: in India which flows into the Bay of Bengal. / Part S: No error
(a) Part P (b) Part Q (c) No error (d) Part R
Answer: (d)
Solution: Part R is faulty in agreement logic: "one of the longest rivers" requires the relative clause to refer to "rivers" (plural), so it should be "that flow", or restructure the sentence. Preposition "in" is fine; the trap hides the real error after a correct preposition to test over-thinking.
Source: PYQ-style

150. Hardly had he stepped out of the house ______ it began to rain.
(a) than (b) that (c) then (d) when
Answer: (d)
Solution: "Hardly/Scarcely... when" and "No sooner... than" are fixed correlative pairs. Pairing "hardly" with "than" is the classic examiner trap tested almost every year.
Source: PYQ-style
