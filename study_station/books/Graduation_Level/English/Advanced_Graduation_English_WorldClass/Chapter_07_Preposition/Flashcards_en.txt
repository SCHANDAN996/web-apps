Card 1
Front: Which preposition is used with "married"?
Back: "Married to" — never "married with". (She is married to a doctor.)

Card 2
Front: "Different" is followed by which preposition in standard exam English?
Back: "Different from" — not "different than/with". (My book is different from yours.)

Card 3
Front: 🧠 Mnemonic: How to remember prepositions of time — AT, ON, IN?
Back: "AT a point, ON a day, IN a period" — AT 5 o'clock (exact point), ON Monday (day/date), IN July/2024/morning (longer period). Think of a zoom lens: AT = zoomed in, IN = zoomed out.

Card 4
Front: ⚠️ Examiner's Trap: "He is senior ___ me." Which preposition?
Back: "to" — senior, junior, superior, inferior, prefer all take "to", never "than". (He is senior to me.)

Card 5
Front: Which preposition follows "abide", "comply", and "dispense"?
Back: Abide by, comply with, dispense with. (We must abide by the rules.)

Card 6
Front: "Since" vs "For" — what is the difference?
Back: "Since" + point of time (since Monday, since 2010); "For" + period of time (for two years, for a week).

Card 7
Front: ⚠️ Examiner's Trap: "He entered ___ the room." Is a preposition needed?
Back: No preposition. "Enter" is directly followed by the object: "He entered the room." Same with "resemble", "accompany", "attack", "reach" — no preposition after them.

Card 8
Front: Which preposition is used with "angry" — with a person vs a thing?
Back: Angry with a person; angry at/about a thing. (Angry with him; angry at the delay.)

Card 9
Front: 🧠 Mnemonic: How to remember verbs that take NO preposition?
Back: "R-E-A-C-H" — Resemble, Enter, Accompany, Comprise, attack/reacH. These verbs hug their object directly — no preposition in between.

Card 10
Front: "Beside" vs "Besides" — what is the difference?
Back: Beside = next to (Sit beside me). Besides = in addition to (Besides Hindi, she knows French.)

Card 11
Front: Which preposition follows "afraid", "fond", "proud", and "aware"?
Back: All take "of": afraid of, fond of, proud of, aware of.

Card 12
Front: "Between" vs "Among" — when do you use each?
Back: Between = two persons/things (or one-to-one relations); Among = more than two. (Between Ram and Shyam; among the students.)

Card 13
Front: ⚠️ Examiner's Trap: "He is good ___ English." Which preposition?
Back: "at" — good at, bad at, clever at (skill). But "good for" = beneficial (Milk is good for health). Don't confuse the two.

Card 14
Front: Which preposition follows "insist", "persist", and "succeed"?
Back: Insist on, persist in, succeed in. (He insisted on going; she succeeded in life.)

Card 15
Front: "In" vs "Into" — what is the difference?
Back: In = position/rest (He is in the room); Into = motion/entry (He went into the room).

Card 16
Front: Which preposition is used with "die" for disease vs cause?
Back: Die of a disease (died of cholera); die from an injury/cause (died from wounds); die for a cause/person (died for the country).

Card 17
Front: "By" vs "With" for instruments and agents — what's the rule?
Back: By = agent/doer (killed by the robber); With = instrument/tool (killed with a knife).

Card 18
Front: Which preposition follows "agree" — with a person, on a point, to a proposal?
Back: Agree with a person; agree on/upon a point; agree to a proposal/plan.

Card 19
Front: ⚠️ Examiner's Trap: "Despite ___ his wealth, he is unhappy." Should "of" be added?
Back: No. "Despite" never takes "of" — but "in spite of" always does. (Despite his wealth = In spite of his wealth.)

Card 20
Front: Which preposition follows "listen", "look", "wait", and "ask"?
Back: Listen to, look at, wait for, ask for. (Listen to the teacher; wait for me.)
