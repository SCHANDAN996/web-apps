Practice Set 4 — Placement Test (Questions 76–100)
Difficulty: 20 Medium, 5 Hard | Examiner-trap questions: 82, 95

76. Choose the correctly spelt word.
(a) Occurence (b) Ocurrence (c) Occurrence (d) Occurrance
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Occurrence" doubles both the c and the r — a classic double-letter trap. The tempting option drops one r, which is the most common spelling error in exams. So the answer is Occurrence.
Source: PYQ-style

77. Select the word closest in meaning to "EPHEMERAL".
(a) Eternal (b) Fragile (c) Strong (d) Short-lived
Answer: (d)
Solution: "Eternal" is the antonym and the favourite trap. Ephemeral (Greek ephēmeros — lasting a day) means lasting a very short time. So the answer is Short-lived.
Source: NCERT-based vocabulary

78. Choose the correct passive form: "Someone has stolen my wallet."
(a) My wallet was been stolen. (b) My wallet is stolen. (c) My wallet has been stolen. (d) My wallet had stolen.
Answer: (c)
Solution: Present perfect active becomes "has/have been + V3" in passive; "is stolen" wrongly shifts the tense to simple present. So the answer is "My wallet has been stolen."
Source: PYQ-style

79. Spot the error: Part P: Neither of the two boys / Part Q: have done / Part R: their homework. / Part S: No error
(a) Part P (b) Part Q (c) Part R (d) No error
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Neither of" takes a singular verb, so "have" in Part Q must be "has"; the plural "boys" nearby is an intervening-phrase trap. So the error is in Part Q.
Source: PYQ-style

80. Choose the correct indirect speech: He said, "I may go to Delhi tomorrow."
(a) He said that he may go to Delhi tomorrow. (b) He said that he might go to Delhi the next day. (c) He said that he might went to Delhi the next day. (d) He said that he will go to Delhi the next day.
Answer: (b)
Solution: In reported speech "may" backshifts to "might" and "tomorrow" becomes "the next day"; keeping "may" and "tomorrow" is the tempting error. So the answer is "He said that he might go to Delhi the next day."
Source: PYQ-style

81. Select the correctly punctuated sentence.
(a) Its a beautiful day, isn't it? (b) Its' a beautiful day isn't it? (c) It's a beautiful day, isn't it? (d) It's a beautiful day isnt it?
Answer: (c)
Solution: "It's" (it is) needs the apostrophe, and the tag question needs a comma and apostrophe; "Its" without an apostrophe is the classic trap. So the answer is "It's a beautiful day, isn't it?"
Source: PYQ-style

82. Spot the error: Part P: The quality of the mangoes / Part Q: were not good / Part R: in the market yesterday. / Part S: No error
(a) Part P (b) Part R (c) Part Q (d) No error
Answer: (c)
Solution: EXAMINER'S TRAP (Intervening Phrase): the subject is "quality" (singular), not "mangoes", so "were" must be "was". The plural noun right before the verb lures most candidates. So the error is in Part Q.
Source: PYQ-style

83. Choose the word opposite in meaning to "TRANSPARENT".
(a) Clear (b) Opaque (c) Fragile (d) Obvious
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Clear" and "obvious" are near-synonyms and act as distractors. Opaque means not allowing light through — the true antonym. So the answer is Opaque.
Source: NCERT-based vocabulary

84. Fill in the blank: The committee ______ divided in their opinion on the new policy.
(a) was (b) has been (c) is (d) were
Answer: (d)
Solution: When a collective noun's members act individually (signalled by "their"), a plural verb is used; "was" is the trap for those who apply the collective-noun rule blindly. So the answer is "were".
Source: PYQ-style

85. Choose the correct meaning of the idiom "to burn the midnight oil".
(a) To work or study late into the night (b) To waste electricity (c) To stay awake worrying (d) To celebrate all night
Answer: (a)
Solution: The literal image of burning oil tempts candidates toward the electricity option. The idiom means working or studying late at night. So the answer is "To work or study late into the night."
Source: PYQ-style

86. Select the correctly spelt word.
(a) Questionnaire (b) Questionaire (c) Questionnair (d) Qustionnaire
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Questionnaire" keeps the double n from French; dropping one n is the most frequent misspelling tested. So the answer is Questionnaire.
Source: PYQ-style

87. Choose the correct sentence.
(a) He is senior to me. (b) He is more senior than me. (c) He is senior than me. (d) He is more senior to me.
Answer: (a)
Solution: Latin comparatives (senior, junior, superior, inferior) take "to", never "than" — "senior than" is the sound-right trap. So the answer is "He is senior to me."
Source: PYQ-style

88. Fill in the blank: Hardly ______ the station when the train left.
(a) I had reached (b) I reached (c) had I reached (d) did I reach
Answer: (c)
Solution: After "Hardly/Scarcely" at the start, inversion with past perfect is required: "Hardly had I reached… when…". The uninverted order is the tempting error. So the answer is "had I reached".
Source: PYQ-style

89. Choose the one-word substitute: "A person who speaks many languages."
(a) Polyglot (b) Bilingual (c) Linguist (d) Orator
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Linguist" (a student of language) and "bilingual" (two languages) are close traps. Polyglot (Greek poly = many, glōtta = tongue) means a speaker of many languages. So the answer is Polyglot.
Source: NCERT-based vocabulary

90. Spot the error: Part P: One of my friend / Part Q: has invited me / Part R: to his wedding. / Part S: No error
(a) No error (b) Part Q (c) Part R (d) Part P
Answer: (d)
Solution: "One of" must be followed by a plural noun — "friends", not "friend"; the singular verb "has" in Part Q is actually correct, which is the trap. So the error is in Part P.
Source: PYQ-style

91. Choose the correct active form: "The novel was written by her in a month."
(a) She had written the novel in a month. (b) She has written the novel in a month. (c) She was writing the novel in a month. (d) She wrote the novel in a month.
Answer: (d)
Solution: Simple past passive converts to simple past active; the perfect and continuous forms change the tense. So the answer is "She wrote the novel in a month."
Source: PYQ-style

92. Select the word closest in meaning to "CANDID".
(a) Secretive (b) Frank (c) Rude (d) Clever
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Rude" tempts because frankness can feel rude, but candid simply means truthful and open, without the negative tone. So the answer is Frank.
Source: NCERT-based vocabulary

93. Fill in the blank: Each of the students ______ given a certificate.
(a) were (b) are (c) have been (d) was
Answer: (d)
Solution: "Each of" takes a singular verb; the plural "students" before the verb is the intervening-phrase trap. So the answer is "was".
Source: PYQ-style

94. Choose the correct meaning of the idiom "a red herring".
(a) A misleading distraction (b) A rare achievement (c) An expensive mistake (d) A lucky escape
Answer: (a)
Solution: The literal fish image misleads; a red herring is something that diverts attention from the real issue. So the answer is "A misleading distraction."
Source: PYQ-style

95. Spot the error: Part P: The teacher, as well as the students, / Part Q: were present / Part R: at the seminar. / Part S: No error
(a) Part Q (b) Part P (c) Part R (d) No error
Answer: (a)
Solution: EXAMINER'S TRAP (Intervening Phrase): with "as well as", the verb agrees with the first subject — "the teacher" (singular) — so "were" must be "was". The nearby plural "students" is the bait. So the error is in Part Q.
Source: PYQ-style

96. Read the inference: "Despite record harvests, farmer incomes fell as wholesale prices collapsed." What can be inferred?
(a) Farmers grew less food (b) The government banned exports (c) Higher output does not guarantee higher income (d) Consumers stopped buying food
Answer: (c)
Solution: The other options add facts not in the sentence — the over-reading trap. The passage directly implies that output and income can move in opposite directions. So the answer is "Higher output does not guarantee higher income."
Source: PYQ-style

97. Choose the correctly spelt word.
(a) Millennium (b) Millenium (c) Milennium (d) Milenium
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Millennium" doubles both l and n (Latin mille + annus); dropping one n is the standard trap. So the answer is Millennium.
Source: PYQ-style

98. Fill in the blank: The data ______ that the policy has failed. (HARD)
(a) shows (b) show (c) is showing (d) has shown
Answer: (b)
Solution: In formal academic English "data" is the plural of "datum" and takes a plural verb; "shows" is the sound-right trap based on everyday usage. So the answer is "show".
Source: PYQ-style

99. Choose the sentence with correct article usage. (HARD)
(a) He is an European artist. (b) He is a European artist. (c) He is the European artist. (d) He is European artist.
Answer: (b)
Solution: "European" begins with a consonant sound (/j/), so it takes "a", not "an" — the spelling-based choice is the trap. So the answer is "He is a European artist."
Source: PYQ-style

100. Read the tone: "The report claims the scheme transformed rural life; the empty warehouses tell a different story." The author's tone is: (HARD)
(a) Admiring (b) Celebratory (c) Neutral (d) Sceptical
Answer: (d)
Solution: "Admiring" and "celebratory" misread the first clause alone; the contrast ("empty warehouses tell a different story") signals doubt about the claim. So the answer is Sceptical.
Source: PYQ-style
