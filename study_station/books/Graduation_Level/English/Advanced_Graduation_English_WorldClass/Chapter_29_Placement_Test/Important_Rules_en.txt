# 📋 Important Grammar Rules & Tips — Placement Test

## (a) 12 High-Yield Rules

| # | Rule | Example | Examiner's Trap |
|---|------|---------|-----------------|
| 1 | **Subject-Verb Agreement:** The verb agrees with the subject, not with the noun in an intervening phrase. | The quality of the mangoes **was** good. | "The quality of the mangoes **were**…" — 'mangoes' is a decoy; the subject is 'quality'. |
| 2 | **Collective Nouns** (team, jury, committee, audience) take a singular verb when acting as one unit; plural when members act individually. | The jury **has** given its verdict. / The jury **are** divided in opinion. | Test-makers pair a collective noun with a plural verb even when the group acts as one. |
| 3 | **Each / Every / Either / Neither / Many a** take a singular verb. | Each of the boys **has** a pen. | "Each of the boys **have**…" — 'boys' lures you into a plural verb. |
| 4 | **'One of the + plural noun'** takes a singular verb; in "one of those who…", the relative pronoun 'who' refers to the plural noun. | One of my friends **is** a doctor. / He is one of those who **work** hard. | Confusing the two patterns is the most repeated trap. |
| 5 | **Uncountable nouns** (news, information, furniture, advice, luggage, scenery, bread) are always singular and take no 's'. | The news **is** good. / He gave me some **advice**. | "advices", "furnitures", "informations" — all wrong. |
| 6 | **Scissors, trousers, spectacles, wages, premises** are plural; but "a pair of scissors" is singular. | These scissors **are** sharp. / This pair of scissors **is** sharp. | "The scissors **is**…" vs "The pair of scissors **are**…" — both reversed. |
| 7 | **Tense with 'since/for':** Use present perfect (continuous) with since/for, not simple present. | I **have been living** here since 2015. | "I **am living** here since 2015" — a classic Hindi-speaker error. |
| 8 | **No sooner…than / Hardly…when / Scarcely…when** — never mix the pairs. | No sooner did he enter **than** it rained. | "No sooner…**when**" is the planted error. |
| 9 | **Comparative degree:** 'Senior, junior, superior, inferior, prior, prefer' take **to**, not 'than'. | He is senior **to** me. | "senior **than** me" — sounds right, is wrong. |
| 10 | **'The + adjective'** (the rich, the poor, the blind) is plural in meaning. | The rich **are** not always happy. | "The rich **is**…" — singular verb trap. |
| 11 | **Conditional sentences:** "If + past simple → would + V1"; never use 'would' in the if-clause. | If I **were** a bird, I would fly. | "If I **would be** a bird…" — double 'would' error. |
| 12 | **Prepositions after verbs:** 'discuss' takes no 'about'; 'enter' takes no 'into' (for places); 'comprise' takes no 'of'; 'order' takes no 'for' (order food, not order for food). | We **discussed** the matter. / She **entered** the room. | "discussed **about**", "entered **into** the room" — the most frequently tested preposition traps. |

## (b) Time Management — The 15/45-Second Rule

| Type | Question Kinds | Time Budget | Strategy |
|------|----------------|-------------|----------|
| **Fact-based (15 sec)** | Synonym/Antonym, One-word substitution, Idioms, Spelling, Fill-in-the-blank (single word), Spot-the-error (rule-based) | 15 seconds | You either know it or you don't. Answer instantly; never re-read. If unknown in 15 sec, mark and move on. |
| **Inference-based (45 sec)** | Reading comprehension (tone, inference, title), Para-jumbles, Sentence improvement (long), Cloze test (contextual blanks) | 45 seconds | These reward careful reading. Invest time here — accuracy is achievable, unlike pure recall items. |

**Golden sequencing:** Attempt all 15-sec questions first (builds score + confidence), then return to 45-sec questions with the remaining time. Never spend 45 seconds on a synonym you don't know — no amount of staring will retrieve it.

## (c) Morphology — Root, Affix, Example

| Root / Affix | Meaning | Example Words |
|--------------|---------|---------------|
| **bene-** (Latin) | good, well | benefit, benevolent, beneficial |
| **mal-** (Latin) | bad, evil | malicious, malfunction, malnutrition |
| **dict-** (Latin) | say, speak | predict, dictionary, verdict |
| **spect-** (Latin) | see, look | inspect, spectator, perspective |
| **port-** (Latin) | carry | transport, portable, export |
| **cred-** (Latin) | believe | credible, incredible, credit |
| **-phobia** (Greek) | fear | claustrophobia, hydrophobia |
| **-logy** (Greek) | study of | biology, geology, psychology |
| **pre-** (prefix) | before | preview, precaution, prefix |
| **-less** (suffix) | without | hopeless, fearless, careless |

**Tip:** In the exam, break an unknown word into root + affix. "Malfunction" = mal (bad) + function = working badly. This unlocks meaning without a dictionary — the fastest 15-second tool for vocabulary questions.
