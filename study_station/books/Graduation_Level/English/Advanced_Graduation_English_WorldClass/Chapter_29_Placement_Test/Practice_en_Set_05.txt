Practice Set 5 (Questions 101–125) — 15 Medium, 10 Hard

101. Choose the correctly spelt word.
(a) Occurence (b) Ocurrence (c) Occurrence (d) Occurrance
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Occur" doubles the r before -ence, and the word keeps both c's: oc-cur-rence. The tempting option drops one r, a classic sound-right trap, so the answer is Occurrence.
Source: PYQ-style

102. Select the word closest in meaning to "CANDID".
(a) Secretive (b) Rude (c) Timid (d) Frank
Answer: (d)
Solution: Candid means truthful and straightforward; "rude" is a tempting distractor because frankness can feel rude, but rudeness is not the meaning. The correct synonym is Frank.
Source: PYQ-style

103. Select the word opposite in meaning to "OBSOLETE".
(a) Ancient (b) Outdated (c) Useless (d) Modern
Answer: (d)
Solution: Obsolete means no longer in use; "ancient" and "outdated" are near-synonyms planted as traps. The antonym is Modern.
Source: PYQ-style

104. Choose the correctly punctuated sentence.
(a) It's a beautiful day, isn't it? (b) Its a beautiful day, isn't it? (c) Its' a beautiful day, isn't it? (d) It's a beautiful day isn't it?
Answer: (a)
Solution: "It's" is the contraction of "it is" and needs the apostrophe; the tag question also needs a comma and question mark. The first option confuses possessive "its" with the contraction, so the answer is "It's a beautiful day, isn't it?"
Source: PYQ-style

105. Spot the error: Part P: Neither of the two boys / Part Q: have done / Part R: his homework. / Part S: No error
(a) Part P (b) Part Q (c) Part R (d) No error
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Neither" is singular and takes a singular verb, so "have" must be "has"; the plural noun "boys" in the intervening phrase tempts you toward the plural verb. The error lies in Part Q.
Source: NCERT Class 10 English Grammar

106. Choose the correct passive form: "Someone has stolen my wallet."
(a) My wallet had been stolen. (b) My wallet was stolen. (c) My wallet is stolen. (d) My wallet has been stolen.
Answer: (d)
Solution: Present perfect active ("has stolen") becomes present perfect passive ("has been stolen"); the past-tense option breaks tense consistency. The correct form is "My wallet has been stolen."
Source: NCERT Class 10 English Grammar

107. Select the correctly framed indirect speech: He said, "I may go to Delhi tomorrow."
(a) He said that he may go to Delhi the next day. (b) He said that he may went to Delhi the next day. (c) He said that he might go to Delhi tomorrow. (d) He said that he might go to Delhi the next day.
Answer: (d)
Solution: In reported speech "may" shifts to "might" and "tomorrow" to "the next day"; keeping "tomorrow" is the common trap. The correct version is "He said that he might go to Delhi the next day."
Source: NCERT Class 10 English Grammar

108. Choose the correct sentence.
(a) The Ganges is one of the longest rivers in India. (b) The Ganges is one of the longest river in India. (c) Ganges is one of the longest rivers in India. (d) The Ganges is one of longest rivers in India.
Answer: (a)
Solution: "One of the" must be followed by a plural noun ("rivers"), and river names take "the"; dropping the article or the plural are the planted errors. The correct sentence is "The Ganges is one of the longest rivers in India."
Source: NCERT Class 9 English Grammar

109. Select the meaning of the idiom "to burn the midnight oil".
(a) To waste resources (b) To work or study late into the night (c) To stay awake worrying (d) To celebrate all night
Answer: (b)
Solution: The idiom refers to working or studying late by lamplight; "staying awake worrying" is a tempting literal misreading. The correct meaning is to work or study late into the night.
Source: PYQ-style

110. Choose the correct preposition: He has been suffering ___ fever since Monday.
(a) with (b) by (c) from (d) of
Answer: (c)
Solution: The fixed collocation is "suffer from" a disease; "suffering with" is a sound-right trap used in speech but wrong in standard usage. The correct preposition is from.
Source: NCERT Class 10 English Grammar

111. Spot the error: Part P: The quality of the mangoes / Part Q: were not good / Part R: in the market yesterday. / Part S: No error
(a) Part Q (b) Part P (c) Part R (d) No error
Answer: (a)
Solution: The subject is "quality" (singular), not "mangoes"; the intervening plural noun lures you into the plural verb "were" — a classic intervening-phrase trap. The verb should be "was", so the error is in Part Q.
Source: PYQ-style

112. Choose the correct option to fill the blank: Hardly had he reached the station ___ the train left.
(a) when (b) than (c) then (d) that
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Hardly/Scarcely" pairs with "when", while "No sooner" pairs with "than"; mixing the two is the standard examiner trap. The correct word is when.
Source: NCERT Class 10 English Grammar

113. Select the one-word substitute: "A person who speaks many languages."
(a) Bilingual (b) Polyglot (c) Linguist (d) Orator
Answer: (b)
Solution: A linguist studies language but need not speak many; "polyglot" (Greek poly = many, glotta = tongue) precisely means a speaker of many languages. The answer is Polyglot.
Source: PYQ-style

114. Choose the correct sentence.
(a) Each of the students have submitted the form. (b) Each students has submitted the form. (c) Each of the student have submitted the form. (d) Each of the students has submitted the form.
Answer: (d)
Solution: "Each of" takes a plural noun but a singular verb; the plural "students" next to the verb tempts "have". The correct sentence is "Each of the students has submitted the form."
Source: NCERT Class 9 English Grammar

115. Select the word closest in meaning to "EPHEMERAL".
(a) Eternal (b) Short-lived (c) Strong (d) Fragile
Answer: (b)
Solution: Ephemeral means lasting a very short time; "fragile" is a trap because short-lived things may seem delicate, but fragility is not the meaning. The correct synonym is Short-lived.
Source: PYQ-style

116. Choose the correct tense: By next June, she ___ her degree.
(a) will complete (b) has completed (c) will have completed (d) completes
Answer: (c)
Solution: "By + future time" signals the future perfect, showing completion before that point; the simple future ignores the deadline nuance. The correct form is will have completed.
Source: NCERT Class 10 English Grammar

117. Spot the error: Part P: Scarcely he had / Part Q: entered the room / Part R: when the phone rang. / Part S: No error
(a) Part P (b) Part Q (c) Part R (d) No error
Answer: (a)
Solution: When a negative adverb like "scarcely" begins a clause, inversion is required: "Scarcely had he entered". The normal word order in Part P is the error, so the answer is Part P.
Source: PYQ-style

118. Choose the correct article: He is ___ honest man.
(a) a (b) no article (c) the (d) an
Answer: (d)
Solution: "Honest" begins with a silent h, so the first sound is a vowel and takes "an"; choosing "a" by spelling rather than sound is the trap. The correct article is an.
Source: NCERT Class 8 English Grammar

119. Select the correctly spelt word.
(a) Questionnaire (b) Questionaire (c) Questionnair (d) Questionairee
Answer: (a)
Solution: The word has double n and ends -aire: questionnaire. The single-n version is the most common misspelling trap, so the answer is Questionnaire.
Source: PYQ-style

120. Choose the correct option: The committee ___ divided in their opinion.
(a) are (b) is (c) was (d) has been
Answer: (a)
Solution: When a collective noun's members act individually (signalled by "their"), a plural verb is used; treating "committee" as automatically singular is the collective-noun trap. The correct verb is are.
Source: NCERT Class 10 English Grammar

121. Select the word opposite in meaning to "TRANSPARENT".
(a) Clear (b) Fragile (c) Opaque (d) Obvious
Answer: (c)
Solution: Transparent means allowing light through or easily understood; "clear" and "obvious" are synonyms planted as traps. The antonym is Opaque.
Source: PYQ-style

122. Choose the correct conditional: If he ___ harder, he would have passed.
(a) worked (b) would work (c) had worked (d) has worked
Answer: (c)
Solution: A third conditional (past unreal) needs "had + past participle" in the if-clause; the simple past is the sound-right trap. The correct form is had worked.
Source: NCERT Class 10 English Grammar

123. Spot the error: Part P: One of my friend / Part Q: is coming / Part R: to the party tonight. / Part S: No error
(a) Part R (b) Part Q (c) Part P (d) No error
Answer: (c)
Solution: "One of" must be followed by a plural noun — "one of my friends"; the singular verb "is" is correct, which tempts over-thinkers to mark Part Q. The error is in Part P.
Source: PYQ-style

124. Choose the correct meaning of "to take with a grain of salt".
(a) To accept completely (b) To doubt the truth of something (c) To add flavour (d) To insult someone politely
Answer: (b)
Solution: The idiom means to view something with scepticism; "to accept completely" is the opposite trap. The correct meaning is to doubt the truth of something.
Source: PYQ-style

125. Choose the correctly framed sentence.
(a) Despite of the rain, the match continued. (b) Despite the rain, the match continued. (c) Despite the rain, but the match continued. (d) Despite of rain, but the match continued.
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Despite" never takes "of" (that is "in spite of"), and it cannot be paired with "but"; both errors are combined in the distractors. The correct sentence is "Despite the rain, the match continued."
Source: NCERT Class 10 English Grammar
