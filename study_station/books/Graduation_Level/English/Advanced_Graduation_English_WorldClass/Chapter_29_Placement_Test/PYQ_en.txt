# 📊 PYQ Analysis — Placement Test

## How Exams Test This Chapter

Placement tests and government-exam English papers (SSC CGL, IBPS PO, campus recruitment tests by TCS/Infosys/Accenture-style patterns) do not test "Placement Test" as a topic — they test the **skills a placement test measures**: grammar accuracy, vocabulary in context, reading comprehension, and verbal reasoning. The pattern analysis below tells you *how* examiners frame these questions and where they hide their traps.

### What Examiners Repeatedly Ask

- **Spot the Error** — the single most repeated format. The error is almost always in subject-verb agreement, tense sequence, or preposition choice, and it is almost always hidden *far away* from the word it depends on (an intervening phrase between subject and verb).
- **Sentence Improvement** — the underlined part looks acceptable to the ear but breaks a formal rule. The "No improvement" option is the classic bait: it is correct only about one time in five, yet hesitant students pick it constantly.
- **Fill in the Blanks (vocabulary)** — two options are near-synonyms; only one collocates correctly with the surrounding words ("*make* a decision", not "*do* a decision").
- **Reading Comprehension** — modern passages (AI, climate policy, gig economy, digital banking). Direct questions are free marks; inference questions carry the traps, because one option is always *true in real life* but *not stated in the passage*.
- **Para-jumbles** — examiners exploit pronoun chains: a sentence beginning with "This…" or "He…" can never open the paragraph.

### The Examiner's Four Favourite Traps (in words, not numbers)

1. **Intervening Phrase Trap** — "The quality of the mangoes *were* good." The ear hears "mangoes were"; the rule says "quality was."
2. **Sound-Right Trap** — "He is more senior than me" sounds fine in speech; formal English demands "senior to me."
3. **Over-thinking Trap** — a simple, correct sentence is presented under "Spot the Error," and students invent an error that does not exist. "No error" is a legitimate answer.
4. **True-but-Not-Stated Trap (RC)** — an inference option that matches your general knowledge but not the passage. The passage is the only universe that matters.

## Top 10 High-Yield PYQ-Style Questions with Solutions

**Q1.** The number of students absent from the test (a) / were higher than (b) / the teacher expected. (c) / No error (d)
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** "The number of…" takes a singular verb → "was higher." "A number of" would take "were." Trap type: **Intervening Phrase** ("of students absent from the test" hides the singular subject "number").
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q2.** Each of the boys in the team (a) / have submitted (b) / his fitness certificate. (c) / No error (d)
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** "Each of + plural noun" still takes a singular verb → "has submitted." Trap type: **Intervening Phrase**.
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q3.** Choose the correct improvement: *He denied to help me in the interview.*
(a) denied helping me (b) refused to help me (c) denied for helping me (d) No improvement
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** "Deny" takes a gerund (*denied helping*), but that changes the intended meaning of refusal; "refused to help" is the natural, correct construction. "No improvement" is the bait. Trap type: **Sound-Right**.
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q4.** The manager, along with his assistants, (a) / were present (b) / at the placement drive. (c) / No error (d)
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** With "along with / as well as / together with," the verb agrees with the first subject only → "was present." Trap type: **Intervening Phrase**.
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q5.** Fill in the blank: The company decided to ______ its recruitment process to reduce bias.
(a) overhaul (b) overflow (c) overlook (d) overtake
**Answer: (a)**
**Solution:** "Overhaul" = thoroughly revise a system — the only word that collocates with "process." The other three are sound-alike distractors. Trap type: **Sound-Right** (all begin with "over-").
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q6.** Spot the error: Scarcely he had entered (a) / the examination hall (b) / when the bell rang. (c) / No error (d)
**Answer: (a)**
**Solution:** After negative adverbs (scarcely, hardly, no sooner) at the start of a clause, inversion is required → "Scarcely had he entered." Trap type: **Sound-Right** (the spoken order feels natural).
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q7.** Choose the correctly spelt word:
(a) Occurence (b) Occurrence (c) Ocurrence (d) Occurrance
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** Double 'c' and double 'r' — "occurrence." Spelling traps target doubled consonants. Trap type: **Sound-Right**.
**Source:** NCERT-based word list / PYQ-style

**Q8.** (RC — inference) Passage gist: "Automation has not reduced total employment in the logistics sector; it has shifted workers from manual sorting to system supervision." — What can be inferred?
(a) Automation always creates more jobs than it destroys
(b) In the logistics sector, automation changed the *nature* of jobs rather than their total number
(c) Manual workers are no longer needed anywhere
(d) Supervisors earn more than sorters
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** (b) restates exactly what the passage implies. (a) and (c) overreach with "always/anywhere"; (d) is plausible in real life but unstated — the classic **True-but-Not-Stated** trap.
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q9.** Arrange into a coherent paragraph:
P. This shift has forced companies to redesign training programmes.
Q. Artificial intelligence is transforming entry-level jobs.
R. Routine tasks are now handled by software.
S. Employees therefore need analytical rather than clerical skills.
(a) Q R P S (b) Q R S P (c) R Q P S (d) P Q R S
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** Q introduces the topic (only Q can open — P starts with "This," R and S continue ideas). R explains Q, S draws the consequence ("therefore"), P closes with the organisational response ("This shift" points back to S). Trap type: **Over-thinking** — students often force P first because it sounds important.
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q10.** Spot the error: Neither the recruiter nor the candidates (a) / was satisfied (b) / with the test results. (c) / No error (d)
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** With "neither…nor," the verb agrees with the *nearer* subject — "candidates" is plural → "were satisfied." Trap type: **Intervening Phrase / proximity rule**.
**Source:** PYQ-style

## Applying the 15/45-Second Rule

**15-second question — Q7 (spelling):** This is pure recall. You either know "occurrence" or you don't. Look, decide, move. Spending 60 seconds staring at four spellings does not improve accuracy; it only steals time from comprehension.

**45-second question — Q8 (RC inference):** This demands the Detective Approach: (1) read the question stem first, (2) locate the relevant sentence in the passage, (3) eliminate the two overreaching options ("always," "anywhere"), (4) reject the true-but-unstated option (d), (5) confirm (b) against the passage wording. Budget the full 45 seconds — inference questions reward patience, not speed.

**Rule of thumb:** Fact-based questions (spelling, synonym, single-rule grammar, direct RC detail) → 15 seconds, trust your first answer. Inference-based questions (RC tone/inference, para-jumbles, complex error-spotting with multiple clauses) → 45 seconds, verify by elimination.
