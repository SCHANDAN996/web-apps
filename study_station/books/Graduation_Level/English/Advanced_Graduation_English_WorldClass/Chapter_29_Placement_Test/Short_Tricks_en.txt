# 🪄 Short Tricks + Skip Strategy — Placement Test

Quick-recall tools for the exam hall. Revise these the night before the test — each box is designed to be recalled in under 5 seconds.

---

## Mnemonic Boxes

**BOX 1 — FANBOYS (Coordinating Conjunctions)**
> **F**or, **A**nd, **N**or, **B**ut, **O**r, **Y**et, **S**o
> Join two independent clauses. Comma before FANBOYS when joining full sentences.

**BOX 2 — SVA Pair Check (Subject–Verb Agreement)**
> "Find the REAL subject — kill the phrase in between."
> *The quality of the apples **was** good.* (subject = quality, not apples)
> Trap words between subject and verb: *of, along with, as well as, together with, in addition to* — they never change the verb.

**BOX 3 — Each/Every/Neither/Either = SINGULAR**
> "**EENE** is alone" — **E**ach, **E**very, **N**either, **E**ither → always take a singular verb.
> *Neither of the boys **is** ready.* (not *are*)

**BOX 4 — Uncountable Nouns Never Take 's' or 'a'**
> "**FAIIL-B**" — **F**urniture, **A**dvice, **I**nformation, **I**nfrastructure, **L**uggage, **B**read
> Wrong: *an advice, many informations* → Right: *a piece of advice, much information*

**BOX 5 — Preposition Glue (Fixed Pairs)**
> "**DALI-G**" — **D**epend **on**, **A**ccused **of**, **L**isten **to**, **I**nsist **on**, **G**ood **at**
> Placement tests love swapping these: *depends in* ❌ → *depends on* ✅

**BOX 6 — Tense Signal Words**
> **Since/For** → Perfect tense (*has been working since 2020*)
> **Yesterday/Last/Ago** → Simple Past (*went*, never *has gone*)
> **By tomorrow/By 2026** → Future Perfect (*will have finished*)

**BOX 7 — Comparatives Need 'Than', Superlatives Need 'The'**
> *She is taller **than** me.* / *She is **the** tallest in the class.*
> Double comparative trap: *more better* ❌ → *better* ✅

**BOX 8 — Active → Passive Formula**
> "Object becomes king, verb becomes **be + V3**, doer hides behind 'by'."
> *He wrote a letter* → *A letter **was written** by him.*
> Only transitive verbs (verbs with objects) can go passive.

**BOX 9 — Reported Speech: One Step Back**
> Present → Past | Past → Past Perfect | will → would | can → could
> "I **am** tired," he said → He said that he **was** tired.
> Universal truths never step back: *The sun rises in the east* stays as is.

**BOX 10 — Article Quick Test**
> **A** = consonant sound, **AN** = vowel **sound** (not letter!)
> *an hour, an MBA, an honest man* but *a university, a one-rupee coin, a European*
> **THE** = specific/known/unique (*the sun, the Ganga, the book I bought*)

**BOX 11 — Root Power for Vocabulary**
> **BENE** = good (benefit, benevolent) | **MAL** = bad (malicious, malfunction)
> **CHRON** = time (chronology, chronic) | **PHOB** = fear (claustrophobia)
> Unknown word in the test? Break it into root + prefix + suffix before guessing.

**BOX 12 — RC Detective Rule**
> "The answer is IN the passage, not in your head."
> For inference questions: pick the option that is **one small step** from the text, never a giant leap. Extreme words (*always, never, completely*) are usually wrong.

**BOX 13 — Tone Words Ladder**
> Positive: optimistic, appreciative, laudatory
> Neutral: objective, analytical, factual
> Negative: critical, cynical, pessimistic, sarcastic
> First classify the passage as + / 0 / −, then eliminate half the options instantly.

**BOX 14 — Error-Spotting Scan Order**
> Check in this order: **(1) Verb tense → (2) Subject-verb agreement → (3) Preposition → (4) Article → (5) Parallel structure.**
> Most placement-test errors hide in the first three.

**BOX 15 — Sentence Arrangement (Para Jumbles)**
> Find the **opener** first: it has no pronoun without a noun, no connector (*but, however, so*), and introduces the topic.
> Then chain **pronoun → noun** pairs (*…Ravi… he…*) and **idea → example** pairs.

## ⏭️ Skip Strategy

**Tip 1 — The 15/45 Split**
Fact-based questions (synonym, antonym, one-word substitution, spelling, fill-in-the-blank with a single grammar rule) deserve **15 seconds**. If the answer doesn't click in 15 seconds, mark it and move on — these are recall questions; staring longer rarely helps. Inference-based questions (RC inference, tone, para jumbles, complex error-spotting) deserve **45 seconds** because they need elimination, not recall.

**Tip 2 — Skip the "Two-Option Trap" Gracefully**
When you're stuck between two options in an error-spotting or vocabulary question, do NOT burn 2 minutes. Apply one test (read the sentence aloud mentally / break the word into roots), pick, flag, and move. Return only if time remains. A skipped-then-revisited question costs less than a rushed block of five easy questions you never reached.

**Tip 3 — RC Last, But Never Blind**
If the section is mixed, attempt vocabulary and grammar first (fast marks), then RC. But never guess RC answers without reading — even a 60-second skim of the passage plus reading the questions lets you answer the 2–3 direct ("according to the passage") questions, which are free marks. Skip only the heavy inference questions if the clock is under 2 minutes.
