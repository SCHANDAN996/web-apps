Practice Set 3 — Placement Test (Questions 51–75)
Easy: Questions 51–60 | Medium: Questions 61–75

51. Choose the correctly spelt word.
(a) Receive (b) Recieve (c) Riceive (d) Receeve
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Recieve" is the classic trap because we hear the "ee" sound and forget the rule. Remember: "i before e, except after c" — after C it is "ei". So the answer is Receive.
Source: NCERT Class 10 English

52. Choose the correct synonym of "Abundant".
(a) Scarce (b) Meagre (c) Rare (d) Plentiful
Answer: (d)
Solution: "Scarce" and "Rare" are antonyms and tempt students who read the question in a hurry. "Abundant" means existing in large quantity, so the answer is Plentiful.
Source: PYQ-style

53. Fill in the blank: She has been working here ______ 2015.
(a) for (b) from (c) by (d) since
Answer: (d)
Solution: "For" tempts because it goes with duration, but 2015 is a point of time, not a duration. With a point of time we use "since". So the answer is since.
Source: NCERT Class 9 English

54. Choose the correct antonym of "Transparent".
(a) Clear (b) Obvious (c) Opaque (d) Fragile
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Clear" and "Obvious" are near-synonyms and trap careless readers. The opposite of transparent is opaque. So the answer is Opaque.
Source: PYQ-style

55. Spot the error: Part P: One of my friend / Part Q: has gone / Part R: to Delhi yesterday.
(a) Part P (b) Part Q (c) Part R (d) No error
Answer: (a)
Solution: Students often check the verb and miss the noun. After "one of", the noun must be plural — "one of my friends". So the error is in Part P.
Source: NCERT Class 10 English

56. Fill in the blank: The teacher asked the students ______ the chapter.
(a) read (b) reading (c) to read (d) reads
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Read" tempts because "asked" feels like an imperative, but "ask" takes the pattern ask + object + to-infinitive. So the answer is to read.
Source: NCERT Class 9 English

57. Choose the one-word substitute for: "A person who speaks many languages."
(a) Polyglot (b) Bilingual (c) Linguist (d) Orator
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Linguist" is the trap — a linguist studies language scientifically and need not speak many languages. "Polyglot" (poly = many, glot = tongue) is exact. So the answer is Polyglot.
Source: PYQ-style

58. Fill in the blank: Neither of the two boys ______ present.
(a) were (b) are (c) have been (d) was
Answer: (d)
Solution: "Were" tempts because "boys" is plural and sits next to the verb, but the subject is "neither", which is singular. So the answer is was.
Source: NCERT Class 10 English

59. Choose the correctly punctuated indirect speech: He said, "I am tired."
(a) He said that he is tired. (b) He said that he was tired. (c) He said that he had tired. (d) He said that he tired.
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Is" tempts students who forget backshifting. With reporting verb in past, present simple shifts to past simple. So the answer is "He said that he was tired."
Source: NCERT Class 10 English

60. Choose the correct meaning of the idiom "To bite the dust".
(a) To suffer defeat (b) To eat in a hurry (c) To clean the floor (d) To work very hard
Answer: (a)
Solution: "To work very hard" tempts because dust suggests labour, but the idiom means to fail or be defeated. So the answer is To suffer defeat.
Source: PYQ-style

61. Spot the error: Part P: The Ganges / Part Q: is / Part R: one of the longest river in India.
(a) Part P (b) Part Q (c) Part R (d) No error
Answer: (c)
Solution: The verb "is" looks fine, so students pick Part Q — that is the trap. After "one of the", the noun must be plural: "longest rivers". So the error is in Part R.
Source: NCERT Class 10 English

62. Fill in the blank: Hardly had he reached the station ______ the train left.
(a) than (b) when (c) then (d) that
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Than" is the classic trap because "no sooner" pairs with "than". But "hardly" and "scarcely" pair with "when". So the answer is when.
Source: PYQ-style

63. Choose the correct passive form: "Someone has stolen my watch."
(a) My watch has stolen. (b) My watch is stolen by someone. (c) My watch was being stolen. (d) My watch has been stolen.
Answer: (d)
Solution: "Was being stolen" tempts because it is also passive, but it changes the tense. Present perfect active becomes present perfect passive: has been stolen. So the answer is "My watch has been stolen."
Source: NCERT Class 10 English

64. Choose the correct synonym of "Candid".
(a) Cunning (b) Frank (c) Timid (d) Rude
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Rude" tempts because candid remarks can hurt, but candid means honest and open, not impolite. So the answer is Frank.
Source: PYQ-style

65. Fill in the blank: He is junior ______ me by two years.
(a) to (b) from (c) than (d) with
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Than" is the biggest trap — Latin comparatives (junior, senior, superior, inferior, prefer) take "to", not "than". So the answer is to.
Source: NCERT Class 10 English

66. Spot the error: Part P: Scarcely he had / Part Q: entered the room / Part R: when the phone rang.
(a) Part P (b) Part Q (c) Part R (d) No error
Answer: (a)
Solution: The conjunction "when" is correct, which misleads students toward Part R. When a negative adverb begins a sentence, inversion is required: "Scarcely had he entered". So the error is in Part P.
Source: PYQ-style

67. Choose the one-word substitute for: "That which cannot be avoided."
(a) Invincible (b) Inevitable (c) Incredible (d) Infallible
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Invincible" tempts because it also starts with "in-" and sounds strong, but it means "cannot be defeated". "Cannot be avoided" is inevitable. So the answer is Inevitable.
Source: PYQ-style

68. Fill in the blank: I look forward to ______ you soon.
(a) meet (b) meeting (c) met (d) have met
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Meet" is the trap because "to" usually signals an infinitive. But in "look forward to", "to" is a preposition and takes a gerund. So the answer is meeting.
Source: NCERT Class 10 English

69. Choose the correct antonym of "Obsolete".
(a) Ancient (b) Outdated (c) Useless (d) Modern
Answer: (d)
Solution: "Ancient" and "Outdated" are synonyms and trap fast readers. The opposite of obsolete is modern or current. So the answer is Modern.
Source: PYQ-style

70. Choose the correctly spelt word.
(a) Occurence (b) Ocurrence (c) Occurrence (d) Occurrance
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Occurence" tempts because the verb "occur" has one r, but the noun doubles both c and r: oc-cur-rence. So the answer is Occurrence.
Source: NCERT Class 10 English

71. Fill in the blank: The committee ______ divided in their opinion.
(a) was (b) has been (c) is (d) were
Answer: (d)
Solution: "Was" is the trap because committee is a collective noun and usually singular. But when members act individually ("divided in their opinion"), the verb is plural. So the answer is were.
Source: PYQ-style

72. Choose the correct indirect speech: She said to me, "Do you like tea?"
(a) She asked me that did I like tea. (b) She asked me whether did I like tea. (c) She asked me if I liked tea. (d) She told me if I like tea.
Answer: (c)
Solution: "That did I like tea" tempts because it keeps the question form, but indirect questions use statement order and "if/whether" without inversion. So the answer is "She asked me if I liked tea."
Source: NCERT Class 10 English

73. Choose the correct meaning of the idiom "To burn the midnight oil".
(a) To study or work late into the night (b) To waste electricity (c) To celebrate all night (d) To destroy evidence
Answer: (a)
Solution: "To celebrate all night" tempts because of "midnight", but the idiom refers to working or studying late by lamplight. So the answer is To study or work late into the night.
Source: PYQ-style

74. Spot the error: Part P: No sooner did the bell ring / Part Q: when / Part R: the students rushed out.
(a) Part P (b) Part R (c) Part Q (d) No error
Answer: (c)
Solution: Part P looks unusual because of inversion, so students wrongly mark it — that is the trap; inversion after "no sooner" is correct. The real error is the conjunction: "no sooner" pairs with "than", not "when". So the error is in Part Q.
Source: PYQ-style

75. Fill in the blank: Unless you ______ hard, you will not succeed.
(a) will work (b) work (c) worked (d) would work
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Will work" is the trap because the main clause has "will". In conditional clauses with unless/if, we use present simple, not future. So the answer is work.
Source: NCERT Class 10 English
