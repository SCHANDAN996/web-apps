# 📖 VERB — The Engine of Every Sentence

## The Hook

Strip a sentence of everything — its nouns, adjectives, adverbs — and one thing refuses to die: the **verb**. "Birds fly." "Time passes." "He is." A sentence can survive without a subject in commands ("*Go!*"), but it can never survive without a verb. That is why examiners at SSC CGL, IBPS PO and State PCS level attack the verb more than any other part of speech — subject-verb agreement, tense consistency, and verb form errors together form the backbone of the Error Spotting and Sentence Improvement sections.

Master the verb, and you master the sentence. Master the sentence, and you master the paper.

---

## 🎯 Learning Objectives

By the end of this chapter you will be able to:

1. Identify every type of verb — main, auxiliary, modal, finite, non-finite — in any sentence.
2. Apply the **12 Golden Rules** of subject-verb agreement, including the traps examiners love most.
3. Choose the correct verb form after special structures (causatives, subjunctives, "would rather", etc.).
4. Spot verb errors in under 15 seconds using the **Subject Hunt Method**.
5. Avoid the classic Hindi-to-English translation errors that cost marks.

## 1. What Exactly Is a Verb?

A **verb** is a word that expresses an **action** (run, write, decide), an **occurrence** (happen, become), or a **state of being** (is, seem, remain).

Every complete sentence needs a verb, and every verb needs to answer two questions:

- **Who** is doing/being it? → decides the *number* (singular/plural)
- **When** is it happening? → decides the *tense*

> *The committee **has** submitted its report.*
> (Who? committee — singular → "has"; When? completed now → present perfect)

## 2. The Verb Family — Know Your Players

### (a) Main Verbs vs Auxiliary Verbs

| Type | Job | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Main (Lexical) verb** | Carries the real meaning | eat, go, analyse, decide |
| **Primary auxiliary** | Builds tense/voice/questions | be, have, do |
| **Modal auxiliary** | Shows mood — ability, duty, possibility | can, could, may, might, shall, should, will, would, must, ought to, need, dare, used to |

**Key exam fact:** Modals are always followed by the **bare infinitive** (V1, without "to").

> ✅ He must **go**. ❌ He must **to go**. ❌ He must **goes**.

**Semi-modals** — *need* and *dare* — behave as modals only in negatives and questions:

> ✅ He **need not** come. (modal — no -s, bare infinitive)
> ✅ He **needs to** come. (main verb — takes -s and "to")

### (b) Finite vs Non-Finite Verbs

- **Finite verbs** change with subject and tense: *She **writes**. They **write**.*
- **Non-finite verbs** never change:
  - **Infinitive** — to + V1: *to err is human*
  - **Gerund** — V1+ing used as a noun: *Swimming is healthy.*
  - **Participle** — present (writing) / past (written): *a written letter*

🧠 **Active Recall:** In the sentence *"Walking to office daily, he has decided to buy a car,"* identify: (1) the participle, (2) the finite verb, (3) the infinitive. *(Answer: Walking — participle; has decided — finite; to buy — infinitive.)*

### (c) Transitive vs Intransitive

- **Transitive** verbs need an object: *She wrote **a letter**.* (Wrote *what*?)
- **Intransitive** verbs need none: *The baby **slept**.*

⚠️ This distinction decides **voice**: only transitive verbs can be made passive. *"He was slept"* is impossible.

## 3. The 12 Golden Rules of Subject-Verb Agreement ⭐

This is the single most-tested verb area. Memorise these twelve.

| # | Rule | Example |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Two subjects joined by **and** → plural verb | *Ravi and his friend **are** here.* |
| 2 | Subjects joined by **or / either…or / neither…nor / not only…but also** → verb agrees with the **nearer** subject | *Neither the teacher nor the students **were** present.* |
| 3 | **Intervening phrases** (with, along with, together with, as well as, in addition to, accompanied by) do NOT change the subject | *The captain, along with his men, **was** praised.* |
| 4 | **Each, every, everyone, everybody, someone, anyone, nobody, either, neither** → singular verb | *Each of the boys **has** a pen.* |
| 5 | **Many a / many an** → singular noun + singular verb | *Many a soldier **has** died for the country.* |
| 6 | Collective nouns (team, jury, committee, family) → **singular** when acting as one unit; **plural** when members act separately | *The jury **has** given its verdict. / The jury **are** divided in their opinion.* |
| 7 | **News, physics, mathematics, politics, measles** — plural-looking but singular | *The news **is** shocking.* |
| 8 | **Scissors, trousers, spectacles, wages, premises** → plural verb (but "a pair of scissors **is**") | *These trousers **are** new.* |
| 9 | **A number of** → plural; **The number of** → singular | *A number of students **were** absent. The number of students **is** fifty.* |
| 10 | Uncountable nouns (information, furniture, luggage, advice, bread) → always singular | *The furniture in this room **is** old.* |
| 11 | **One of + plural noun + singular verb** | *One of my friends **has** gone abroad.* |
| 12 | Relative pronoun verb agrees with its **antecedent** | *It is I who **am** responsible. It is you who **are** responsible.* |

🧠 **Active Recall:** Without looking, complete: "Neither the players nor the coach ___ (was/were) happy." "The number of accidents ___ (is/are) rising." *(Answer: was — nearer subject "coach"; is — "the number of".)*

### 🧠 Mnemonic — **"NEARER WINS"**
For *or / either…or / neither…nor / not only…but also*, remember: **the verb flirts with the NEARER subject.** Whoever stands closest to the verb controls it.

## 4. Special Verb Structures Examiners Love

### (a) The Subjunctive — "Were" for Everyone
After *if, wish, as if, as though* expressing something **unreal or hypothetical**, use **were** for all persons:

> ✅ If I **were** a bird, I would fly. ❌ If I **was** a bird…
> ✅ He talks as if he **were** a king.

After *It is time / It is high time*, use the **past tense** (V2):

> ✅ It is high time you **started** working.

### (b) Causative Verbs — have / get / make
> *I **had** my car **repaired**.* (have + object + V3)
> *She **made** him **cry**.* (make + object + V1 — but passive: *He was made **to cry**.*)

### (c) Verbs Followed by Gerund vs Infinitive — The Killer List

| Gerund only (V+ing) | Infinitive only (to + V1) |
|---|---|
| enjoy, avoid, mind, suggest, finish, admit, deny, consider, practise, keep, postpone, risk | want, wish, hope, decide, promise, agree, refuse, expect, afford, learn, manage, offer |

> ✅ I enjoy **reading**. ❌ I enjoy **to read**.
> ✅ He promised **to come**. ❌ He promised **coming**.

⚠️ Some verbs take both but **change meaning**:
- *stop smoking* = quit the habit | *stop to smoke* = pause in order to smoke
- *remember locking* = recall a past act | *remember to lock* = don't forget to do it

🧠 **Active Recall:** Correct these: (1) "I suggest to go early." (2) "She avoids to meet him." *(Answer: suggest going; avoids meeting.)*

## ⚠️ EXAMINER'S TRAP BOX

**Trap 1 — The Intervening Phrase Ambush.**
*The quality of the apples **were** good.* ❌
Examiners stuff ten words between subject and verb so you match the verb with the nearest noun ("apples"). The real subject is **quality** → *The quality of the apples **was** good.* ✅ **Defence: hunt the subject first, ignore everything between commas.**

**Trap 2 — The Sound-Right Error.**
*"One of my friend **is** a doctor."* ❌ — sounds fine in fast reading, but "one of" demands a **plural noun**: *One of my **friends is** a doctor.* ✅ Two errors often hide in one phrase: plural noun + singular verb.

**Trap 3 — The Modal + Perfect Confusion.**
*"He must **went** yesterday."* ❌ Modals take V1: *must **go***. But for a past deduction: *must **have gone*** (modal + have + V3). Mixing the two patterns is a favourite IBPS/SSC trap.

## 🔁 LINGUISTIC BRIDGE — Hindi से English

| हम हिंदी से सोचकर कहते हैं ❌ | Correct English ✅ | Why |
|---|---|---|
| "What you are doing?" | "What **are you** doing?" | Questions need inversion: auxiliary before subject |
| "He is having a car." | "He **has** a car." | Stative verbs (have, know, believe, love) don't take -ing |
| "I am knowing the answer." | "I **know** the answer." | Same rule — "know" is a state, not an action |
| "He told that…" | "He **said** that… / He told **me** that…" | "Tell" needs an object; "say" doesn't |
| "I and Ravi went." | "**Ravi and I** went." | English politeness: first person comes last |
| "He is more senior than me." | "He is senior **to** me." | senior/junior/superior/inferior/prefer take "to", not "than" |

## 5. Stative vs Dynamic Verbs — The Silent Mark-Killer

**Stative verbs** describe states, not actions, and are **never** used in continuous tenses:

- **Mind:** know, believe, understand, think (=opinion), remember, mean
- **Emotion:** love, hate, like, want, wish, prefer
- **Senses:** see, hear, smell, taste, feel (involuntary)
- **Possession:** have, own, belong, possess
- **Others:** seem, appear (=seem), consist, contain, cost, weigh, measure

> ❌ I am believing you. ✅ I believe you.
> ❌ This book is belonging to me. ✅ This book belongs to me.

⚠️ **Nuance trap:** Some verbs are stative in one meaning, dynamic in another:
- *I **think** he is right* (opinion — stative) vs *I **am thinking** about the problem* (mental activity — dynamic)
- *She **has** a car* (possession) vs *She **is having** lunch* (eating)

## 6. Verb + Preposition Combinations (Fixed Pairs)

These are pure memory items — examiners test them directly:

| Verb + Preposition | Example |
|---|---|
| agree **with** (person) / **to** (proposal) | I agree with you; he agreed to the plan |
| consist **of** / insist **on** | The team consists of eleven players |
| depend **on** / rely **on** | Success depends on effort |
| apologize **to** (person) **for** (thing) | He apologized to me for the delay |
| look **after** / look **into** / look **for** | She looks after her parents; police looked into the case |
| differ **from** / differ **with** | My answer differs from yours |
| prevent **from** / prohibit **from** | He was prevented from leaving |
