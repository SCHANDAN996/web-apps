Card 1
Front: What is a finite verb?
Back: A verb that changes with tense, number, and person of the subject — it is the main verb of a clause. Example: "She writes" / "They write."

Card 2
Front: What is a non-finite verb? Name its three types.
Back: A verb that does not change with the subject and cannot stand alone as the main verb. Types: infinitive (to go), gerund (going), participle (gone/going).

Card 3
Front: "One of my friends ___ (is/are) coming." Which is correct and why?
Back: "is" — the real subject is "One," not "friends." The phrase "of my friends" only intervenes. (Examiner's Trap: Intervening Phrase)

Card 4
Front: Mnemonic: How to remember verbs that take a gerund (not infinitive) after them?
Back: "MAD FISH P": Mind, Avoid, Delay, Finish, Imagine, Suggest, Keep (continue), Practise. Example: "She suggested going," not "suggested to go."

Card 5
Front: "The committee ___ (has/have) taken its decision." Which verb?
Back: "has" — a collective noun acting as one unit takes a singular verb. Use plural only when members act individually: "The committee have divided opinions."

Card 6
Front: What is the subjunctive mood? Give one example.
Back: It expresses a wish, demand, or hypothetical situation, using the base form of the verb. Example: "I suggest that he be on time." / "If I were you…"

Card 7
Front: "Neither the teacher nor the students ___ (was/were) present." Rule?
Back: "were" — with neither…nor / either…or, the verb agrees with the subject nearer to it (proximity rule).

Card 8
Front: Examiner's Trap: "The news ___ (is/are) shocking." Why do students get this wrong?
Back: "is" — "news" ends in -s but is singular (an uncountable noun). Same with physics, mathematics, measles, billiards. Trap type: Sound-Right.

Card 9
Front: What is the difference between transitive and intransitive verbs?
Back: Transitive verbs need an object ("She bought a car"); intransitive verbs do not ("He slept"). Some verbs can be both: "He runs a shop" / "He runs fast."

Card 10
Front: Mnemonic: Which verbs are followed by a bare infinitive (without "to")?
Back: "Let, Make, Help, Bid + sense verbs (see, hear, feel, watch)": "I made him go," "Let her speak," "I saw him cross the road." (But in passive: "He was made to go.")

Card 11
Front: "Scissors ___ (is/are) on the table." Correct form and rule?
Back: "are" — instruments/tools with two parts (scissors, pliers, trousers, spectacles) are plural. But "a pair of scissors is…" is singular.

Card 12
Front: What is a gerund? How is it different from a present participle?
Back: Both end in -ing, but a gerund acts as a noun ("Swimming is fun" — subject), while a participle acts as an adjective or part of a tense ("a swimming champion" / "She is swimming").

Card 13
Front: "Each of the boys ___ (has/have) a pen." Rule?
Back: "has" — each, every, everyone, everybody, either, neither (as subjects) always take a singular verb.

Card 14
Front: Examiner's Trap: "I am knowing the answer." What is wrong?
Back: Stative verbs (know, believe, love, understand, own, seem) are not used in continuous tenses. Correct: "I know the answer." Trap type: Sound-Right.

Card 15
Front: What are modal auxiliaries? List the main ones.
Back: Helping verbs expressing ability, permission, obligation, possibility: can/could, may/might, shall/should, will/would, must, ought to, need, dare, used to.

Card 16
Front: "Hardly had I reached the station ___ the train left." Which connector: when/than/then?
Back: "when" — Hardly/Scarcely…when; No sooner…than. Never mix them: "No sooner had I reached than the train left."

Card 17
Front: What is a causative verb? Give the structure.
Back: Verbs like have/get/make showing that someone arranges for an action to be done by another: "I had my car repaired" (have + object + past participle); "I got him to repair it."

Card 18
Front: "Bread and butter ___ (is/are) my breakfast." Singular or plural?
Back: "is" — when two nouns joined by "and" express one single idea/dish, the verb is singular. But "Bread and butter are sold here" (two separate items) is plural.

Card 19
Front: What is the rule for "It is high time"?
Back: "It is high time" is followed by the simple past to express urgency about the present: "It is high time we left" (not "we leave").

Card 20
Front: In "If I had studied, I would have passed," which conditional is this and what does it show?
Back: Third conditional — an unreal past situation. Structure: If + past perfect, … would have + past participle. Common trap: "If I would have studied" is wrong.
