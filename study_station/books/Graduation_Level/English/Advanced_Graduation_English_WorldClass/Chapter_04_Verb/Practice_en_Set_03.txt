51. Choose the correct form of the verb: She ______ to school every day.
(a) goes (b) go (c) going (d) gone
Answer: (a)
Solution: With a third-person singular subject (she) in the simple present tense, the verb takes -s/-es. "Goes" is correct. "Go" would be correct only with I/you/we/they or plural subjects.
Source: NCERT Class 9 English Grammar

52. Identify the verb in the sentence: "The children played happily in the park."
(a) children (b) played (c) happily (d) park
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Played" is the action word, hence the verb. "Children" and "park" are nouns; "happily" is an adverb modifying the verb.
Source: NCERT Class 8 English Grammar

53. Fill in the blank: They ______ finished their homework before the teacher arrived.
(a) has (b) have (c) having (d) had
Answer: (d)
Solution: Two past actions are given; the earlier action (finishing homework) takes the past perfect "had finished". "Have/has" would wrongly suggest present perfect.
Source: NCERT Class 10 English Grammar

54. Choose the correct sentence:
(a) He don't like coffee.
(b) He doesn't likes coffee.
(c) He doesn't like coffee.
(d) He not like coffee.
Answer: (c)
Solution: After "doesn't", the main verb stays in its base form (like), because "does" already carries the third-person marker. (a) misuses "don't" with "he"; (b) doubles the -s.
Source: NCERT Class 9 English Grammar

55. Select the correct passive form: "The chef cooked the meal."
(a) The meal is cooked by the chef.
(b) The meal was cooked by the chef.
(c) The meal has cooked by the chef.
(d) The meal cooked by the chef.
Answer: (b)
Solution: Active is in simple past, so passive = was/were + past participle → "was cooked". (a) is present tense; (c) and (d) are grammatically broken.
Source: NCERT Class 10 English Grammar

56. Fill in the blank: I ______ my keys. Can you help me find them?
(a) lose (b) lost (c) have lost (d) had lost
Answer: (c)
Solution: A past action with a present result (the keys are still missing) takes the present perfect "have lost". Simple past "lost" would cut the link to the present.
Source: NCERT Class 10 English Grammar

57. Choose the correct modal: You ______ wear a helmet while riding a bike. (obligation/law)
(a) might (b) would (c) could (d) must
Answer: (d)
Solution: "Must" expresses strong obligation or legal necessity. "Might/could" express possibility; "would" expresses habit or willingness — none convey compulsion.
Source: NCERT Class 9 English Grammar

58. Identify the type of verb underlined: "She is a doctor."
(a) Action verb (b) Linking verb (c) Helping verb only (d) Modal verb
Answer: (b)
Solution: Here "is" links the subject "she" to the complement "a doctor" — it shows a state, not an action, so it is a linking (copular) verb.
Source: NCERT Class 8 English Grammar

59. Fill in the blank: Look! The baby ______ .
(a) cries (b) has cried (c) cried (d) is crying
Answer: (d)
Solution: "Look!" signals an action happening right now, so the present continuous "is crying" is required. Simple present "cries" would describe a habit, not the current scene.
Source: NCERT Class 9 English Grammar

60. Choose the correct past tense of "bring":
(a) brang (b) brung (c) brought (d) bringed
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Bring" is an irregular verb: bring–brought–brought. "Brang", "brung" and "bringed" are common sound-right errors with no standard existence.
Source: NCERT Class 8 English Grammar

61. Choose the correct option: Neither of the two boys ______ done his homework.
(a) have (b) were (c) are (d) has
Answer: (d)
Solution: "Neither of" takes a singular verb, so "has" is correct. [Examiner's Trap — Sound-Right]: "boys" sits right before the verb and tempts you toward "have", but the true subject is "neither" (singular).
Source: PYQ-style

62. Fill in the blank: By the time we reached the station, the train ______ .
(a) left (b) had left (c) has left (d) was leaving
Answer: (b)
Solution: The train's departure happened before our arrival (both past), so the earlier action takes the past perfect "had left". Simple past "left" would wrongly suggest both actions were simultaneous.
Source: NCERT Class 10 English Grammar

63. Choose the correct sentence:
(a) The news are very shocking. (b) The news is very shocking. (c) The news were very shocking. (d) The news have been very shocking.
Answer: (b)
Solution: "News" is an uncountable noun ending in -s but is always singular, so it takes "is". [Examiner's Trap — Sound-Right]: the -s ending fools students into choosing plural verbs.
Source: PYQ-style

64. Fill in the blank: He ______ in this company since 2015.
(a) has been working (b) is working (c) works (d) worked
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Since 2015" marks an action that began in the past and continues now — the present perfect continuous "has been working" fits. Simple present "works" ignores the duration.
Source: NCERT Class 10 English Grammar

65. Choose the correct form: Each of the students ______ given a certificate.
(a) was (b) were (c) have (d) are
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Each of + plural noun" takes a singular verb — "was". The plural noun "students" in the intervening phrase is the classic trap; the subject is "each".
Source: PYQ-style

66. Select the correctly framed question:
(a) Why you are late?
(b) Why are you late?
(c) Why you late are?
(d) Why are late you?
Answer: (b)
Solution: In wh-questions the auxiliary comes before the subject: Why + are + you + late. (a) copies Hindi word order ("तुम late क्यों हो?") and drops the inversion.
Source: NCERT Class 9 English Grammar

67. Fill in the blank: If I ______ a bird, I would fly to you.
(a) am (b) was (c) were (d) will be
Answer: (c)
Solution: This is an unreal (hypothetical) condition, which uses the subjunctive "were" for all persons. "Was" is common in speech but is marked wrong in exams.
Source: NCERT Class 10 English Grammar

68. Choose the correct sentence:
(a) One of my friend lives in Delhi.
(b) One of my friends lives in Delhi.
(c) One of my friends live in Delhi.
(d) One of my friend live in Delhi.
Answer: (b)
Solution: The pattern is "one of + plural noun + singular verb": friends (plural) but lives (singular), because the subject is "one". (c) falls for the proximity trap with "friends".
Source: PYQ-style

69. Fill in the blank: The teacher, along with her students, ______ gone on a trip.
(a) have (b) are (c) has (d) were
Answer: (c)
Solution: With "along with", the verb agrees with the first subject ("the teacher" — singular), so "has". The phrase "her students" is an intervening distractor, not part of the subject.
Source: PYQ-style

70. Choose the correct passive: "Someone has stolen my wallet."
(a) My wallet has been stolen.
(b) My wallet is stolen by someone.
(c) My wallet was being stolen.
(d) My wallet had stolen.
Answer: (a)
Solution: Present perfect active → present perfect passive: has/have + been + past participle → "has been stolen". (b) shifts tense; (d) is not a passive structure at all.
Source: NCERT Class 10 English Grammar

71. Fill in the blank: Hardly ______ the station when the train left.
(a) I had reached (b) had I reached (c) I reached (d) did I reached
Answer: (b)
Solution: After negative adverbs like "hardly/scarcely" at the start, inversion is compulsory: "had I reached". Also, "hardly...when" pairs with the past perfect. (d) wrongly adds -ed after "did".
Source: PYQ-style

72. Choose the correct option: The committee ______ divided in their opinion on the issue.
(a) were (b) was (c) is (d) has been
Answer: (a)
Solution: When a collective noun's members act separately (divided in opinion), it takes a plural verb — "were". "Is/was" would be correct only if the committee acted as one united body.
Source: PYQ-style

73. Fill in the blank: I wish I ______ the offer earlier.
(a) accept (b) accepted (c) have accepted (d) had accepted
Answer: (d)
Solution: "I wish" + past perfect expresses regret about a past action that did not happen. Simple past "accepted" after "wish" expresses a present wish, not past regret.
Source: NCERT Class 10 English Grammar

74. Choose the correct sentence:
(a) He as well as his brothers is going. (b) He as well as his brothers are going. (c) He as well as his brothers were going. (d) He as well as his brothers have gone.
Answer: (a)
Solution: With "as well as", the verb agrees with the first subject ("He" — singular, present context) → "is going". The brothers are an intervening phrase and do not control agreement.
Source: PYQ-style

75. Fill in the blank: No sooner had she finished her speech ______ the audience started clapping.
(a) when (b) then (c) than (d) that
Answer: (c)
Solution: The fixed correlative pair is "no sooner...than". [Examiner's Trap]: "hardly/scarcely" pair with "when", so examiners mix the two — "no sooner...when" (a) is the classic wrong combination.
Source: PYQ-style
