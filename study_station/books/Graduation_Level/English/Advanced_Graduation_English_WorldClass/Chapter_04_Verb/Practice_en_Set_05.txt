Questions 101–115: Medium | Questions 116–125: Hard

101. Neither the manager nor the employees _____ aware of the new policy.
(a) was (b) were (c) is (d) has been
Answer: (b)
Solution: With "neither…nor", the verb agrees with the nearer subject ("employees" — plural), so "were". Option (a) tempts those who agree with "manager".
Source: PYQ-style

102. The committee _____ divided in their opinion on the issue.
(a) was (b) has (c) is (d) were
Answer: (d)
Solution: When a collective noun's members act individually (divided opinion), use a plural verb — "were". "Was" would fit only if the committee acted as one unit.
Source: PYQ-style

103. By the time we reached the station, the train _____.
(a) had left (b) has left (c) left (d) was leaving
Answer: (a)
Solution: Two past actions — the earlier one takes past perfect ("had left"), the later one simple past ("reached").
Source: NCERT Class 10 English

104. He _____ here since 2015.
(a) is living (b) lived (c) was living (d) has been living
Answer: (d)
Solution: "Since + point of time" with an action continuing till now requires present perfect continuous — "has been living".
Source: PYQ-style

105. One of my friends _____ gone to Delhi.
(a) have (b) are (c) has (d) were
Answer: (c)
Solution: The real subject is "One" (singular), not "friends" — so "has". The intervening phrase "of my friends" is the examiner's trap.
Source: PYQ-style

106. I wish I _____ a millionaire.
(a) am (b) were (c) was (d) would be
Answer: (b)
Solution: After "I wish" for an unreal present condition, use "were" for all persons (subjunctive). "Was" sounds right in speech but is not the standard exam answer.
Source: PYQ-style

107. Hardly _____ the station when the train left.
(a) I had reached (b) had I reached (c) I reached (d) did I reach
Answer: (b)
Solution: Sentences beginning with "Hardly/Scarcely" need inversion + past perfect: "had I reached". Trap: (d) uses simple past inversion, which fits "No sooner… did" patterns wrongly here.
Source: PYQ-style

108. The quality of the mangoes _____ not good.
(a) were (b) was (c) are (d) have been
Answer: (b)
Solution: Subject is "quality" (singular); "of the mangoes" is only a modifier. Hence "was".
Source: PYQ-style

109. She suggested that he _____ a doctor.
(a) consults (b) consult (c) consulted (d) will consult
Answer: (b)
Solution: After "suggest that", the subjunctive uses the base form of the verb — "consult" (no -s). This is a classic sound-right trap for (a).
Source: PYQ-style

110. Many a student _____ failed in this examination.
(a) has (b) have (c) are (d) were
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Many a + singular noun" takes a singular verb — "has", despite the plural meaning.
Source: PYQ-style

111. If he _____ harder, he would have passed.
(a) had worked (b) worked (c) would work (d) has worked
Answer: (a)
Solution: Third conditional: If + past perfect, … would have + V3. So "had worked".
Source: NCERT Class 10 English

112. The news _____ really shocking.
(a) were (b) are (c) was (d) have been
Answer: (c)
Solution: "News" looks plural but is uncountable/singular — "was". Trap: (a) attracts those misled by the final -s.
Source: PYQ-style

113. He denied _____ the money.
(a) to steal (b) stealing (c) steal (d) having steal
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Deny" is followed by a gerund (V-ing), not an infinitive — "stealing".
Source: PYQ-style

114. Each of the boys _____ given a prize.
(a) were (b) have been (c) was (d) are
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Each of + plural noun" takes a singular verb — "was".
Source: PYQ-style

115. I _____ my homework before my father returned.
(a) finished (b) finish (c) have finished (d) had finished
Answer: (d)
Solution: Earlier of two past actions takes past perfect — "had finished" before "returned".
Source: NCERT Class 10 English

116. It is high time we _____ the truth.
(a) accept (b) accepted (c) should accept (d) have accepted
Answer: (b)
Solution: "It is high time" is followed by simple past (subjunctive) — "accepted", though the meaning is present/future. Trap: (a) feels natural but is wrong.
Source: PYQ-style

117. The principal, along with the teachers, _____ present at the meeting.
(a) was (b) were (c) have been (d) are
Answer: (a)
Solution: With "along with", the verb agrees with the first subject ("the principal" — singular). The intervening phrase is the examiner's trap.
Source: PYQ-style

118. No sooner had she entered the room _____ the phone rang.
(a) when (b) than (c) then (d) that
Answer: (b)
Solution: "No sooner" pairs with "than"; "Hardly/Scarcely" pair with "when". Mixing them is a classic trap.
Source: PYQ-style

119. He is one of those students who _____ always punctual.
(a) is (b) was (c) are (d) has been
Answer: (c)
Solution: The relative pronoun "who" refers to "students" (plural), so the verb is "are". Trap: agreeing with "one" gives (a), which is wrong here.
Source: PYQ-style

120. The Ganges _____ one of the longest rivers of India.
(a) are (b) have been (c) were (d) is
Answer: (d)
Solution: Names of rivers, though plural-looking, take a singular verb — "is".
Source: NCERT Class 9 English

121. I would rather you _____ home now.
(a) went (b) go (c) will go (d) gone
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Would rather + subject" is followed by simple past for present/future preference — "went".
Source: PYQ-style

122. Bread and butter _____ his daily breakfast.
(a) are (b) were (c) is (d) have been
Answer: (c)
Solution: When two nouns together denote one combined dish/idea, the verb is singular — "is". Trap: (a) treats them as two separate items.
Source: PYQ-style

123. Unless you _____ hard, you will not succeed.
(a) will work (b) worked (c) work (d) had worked
Answer: (c)
Solution: In conditional clauses with "unless/if", use simple present for future meaning — "work"; "will" is not used in the if-clause.
Source: NCERT Class 10 English

124. The poet and philosopher _____ dead.
(a) are (b) were (c) have been (d) is
Answer: (d)
Solution: Single article "The" before two nouns means one person (the poet-philosopher), so singular "is". Two articles would make it plural.
Source: PYQ-style

125. Scarcely had he stepped out of the house _____ it began to rain.
(a) when (b) than (c) then (d) that
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Scarcely/Hardly" take "when"; "No sooner" takes "than". Hence "when".
Source: PYQ-style
