# 📋 Important Grammar Rules & Tips — Verb

## (a) 12 High-Yield Rules

| # | Rule | Example | Examiner's Trap |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Subject-Verb Agreement:** The verb agrees with the subject, not with words in an intervening phrase. | The quality of the mangoes **was** good. | "The quality of the mangoes **were**…" — 'mangoes' sits next to the verb to lure you; the real subject is 'quality' (singular). |
| 2 | **Two subjects joined by 'and'** take a plural verb — unless they refer to one person/thing. | Rice and curry **is** my favourite dish. / The poet and philosopher **is** dead. (one person) | "The poet and the philosopher is dead" — the second 'the' means two persons, so use **are**. |
| 3 | **Each, every, everyone, everybody, someone, nobody, either, neither** take a singular verb. | Each of the boys **has** a pen. | "Each of the boys **have**…" — 'boys' is the decoy; 'each' is the subject. |
| 4 | **Either…or / Neither…nor / Not only…but also:** the verb agrees with the **nearer** subject. | Neither the teacher nor the students **were** present. | "Neither the students nor the teacher **were**…" — here 'teacher' is nearer, so use **was**. |
| 5 | **Collective nouns** (team, jury, committee, family) take a singular verb when the group acts as one; plural when members act separately. | The jury **has** given its verdict. / The jury **are** divided in their opinion. | Examiners test the divided-group case: "The committee **is** divided on this issue" is wrong — use **are**. |
| 6 | **Uncountable nouns** (news, information, furniture, luggage, advice, scenery, machinery) are always singular. | The news **is** good. / No information **was** available. | "These news **are**…" — 'news' looks plural but is singular; never add -s to 'information/furniture'. |
| 7 | **'A number of' = plural; 'The number of' = singular.** | A number of students **were** absent. / The number of students **was** fifty. | This pair is swapped in options deliberately — check which article (a/the) is used. |
| 8 | **Tense consistency in conditionals:** If + past simple → would + V1 (Type 2); If + past perfect → would have + V3 (Type 3). | If he **worked** hard, he **would pass**. / If he **had worked** hard, he **would have passed**. | "If he **would have worked**…" — 'would have' never goes in the if-clause. |
| 9 | **Universal truths, facts and habitual actions** take the simple present, even after a past-tense reporting verb. | The teacher said that the earth **revolves** around the sun. | "…the earth **revolved**…" — sounds right because of 'said', but truths stay in present tense. |
| 10 | **Since vs For:** 'since' + point of time; 'for' + period — both usually with perfect/perfect-continuous tense. | He has been working here **since** 2015 / **for** nine years. | "He **is** working here since 2015" — present simple/continuous cannot pair with 'since/for'; use perfect continuous. |
| 11 | **Verbs followed by gerund (V-ing), not infinitive:** enjoy, avoid, mind, suggest, finish, admit, deny, consider, practise. | He **enjoys swimming**. / She **avoided meeting** him. | "He enjoys **to swim**" — a classic Sound-Right trap for Hindi speakers. |
| 12 | **Causative & special structures:** make/have/get + object + V1 (bare infinitive); but 'get' often takes V3 for passive sense. | I **made him go**. / I **got my car repaired**. | "I made him **to go**" — after 'make' in active voice, drop the 'to'. (Passive: He was made **to go**.) |

## (b) Time Management — The 15/45-Second Rule

| Type | What it looks like | Time | Strategy |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Fact-based (15 sec)** | Direct subject-verb agreement, tense identification, gerund vs infinitive, since/for, fixed structures (make/get/have). One rule, one answer. | ≤ 15 sec | Spot the subject → recall the rule → mark. Do NOT re-read the sentence twice. If you know the rule, the answer is instant; if not, guessing costs the same as thinking. |
| **Inference-based (45 sec)** | Complex error-spotting with intervening phrases, conditional sentences, reported speech with tense shifts, sentences where two rules collide (e.g., collective noun + proximity). | ≤ 45 sec | Strip the sentence: remove prepositional phrases and clauses between subject and verb → check agreement → check tense logic → then check the remaining parts. If still unresolved at 45 sec, mark the most rule-backed option and move on. |

**Golden split:** In a 25-question verb set, roughly 15 questions are Fact-based and 10 are Inference-based. Bank the 15-second questions first to buy time for the rest.

## (c) Morphology — Roots, Affixes & Verb Formation

| Root / Affix | Meaning | Verb formed | Example in use |
|---|---|---|---|
| **-ize / -ise** (suffix) | to make or become | modernize, realize, organize | The city was **modernized** rapidly. |
| **-ify** (suffix) | to make | simplify, clarify, justify | Please **clarify** your statement. |
| **en- / -en** (prefix/suffix) | to cause to be | enable, strengthen, widen | Exercise **strengthens** the body. |
| **-ate** (suffix) | to act upon | activate, create, regulate | The law **regulates** trade. |
| **dict** (Latin: say) | say/speak | predict, dictate, contradict | No one can **predict** the result. |
| **port** (Latin: carry) | carry | transport, export, support | India **exports** spices. |
| **ject** (Latin: throw) | throw | reject, project, inject | They **rejected** the proposal. |
| **scrib/script** (Latin: write) | write | describe, prescribe, inscribe | The doctor **prescribed** rest. |

**Tip:** Recognizing the suffix tells you the word is a verb even in an unfamiliar sentence — useful in error-spotting when the examiner swaps a noun for a verb form ("The government plans to **regulation** trade" ❌ → **regulate** ✔).
