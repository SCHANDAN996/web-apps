# 📊 PYQ Analysis — Verb (Pattern Analysis)

## How Exams Test Verbs

Across SSC (CGL, CHSL, MTS, GD), Banking (IBPS PO/Clerk, SBI), Railways (RRB NTPC, Group D) and State exams, verbs are the single most-tested grammar area. Questions appear in four formats:

1. **Spot the Error** — a sentence split into parts; one part has a verb error.
2. **Fill in the Blanks** — choose the correct verb form/tense.
3. **Sentence Improvement** — replace the underlined verb phrase.
4. **Cloze Test** — a passage where several blanks test tense consistency.

## Recurring Sub-Topics (in order of how often examiners return to them)

- **Subject–Verb Agreement** — especially with intervening phrases ("The quality of the mangoes *was* good"), indefinite pronouns (each, everyone, neither), and collective nouns.
- **Tense Consistency** — mixing past and present in one sentence; sequence of tenses in reported/complex sentences.
- **Conditional Sentences** — "If I *were*…", "If he *had worked*…, he *would have passed*."
- **Non-finite Verbs** — gerund vs infinitive ("I enjoy *swimming*", "He wants *to go*").
- **Active–Passive logic** — spotting where passive is wrongly formed ("The work has *been completed*").
- **Modal + base form** — "He must *go*" (never "must goes").
- **Subjunctive & fixed structures** — "It is time he *went* home", "I wish I *were* a bird."

## The Examiner's Four Traps (Trap Types)

| Trap Type | What it looks like | Defence |
|---|---|---|
| **Intervening Phrase** | "The list of selected candidates *have* been released." | Strip the phrase between subject and verb; match verb to the real subject ("list … has"). |
| **Collective Noun** | "The jury *has* delivered its verdict" vs "The jury *are* divided." | One body acting together → singular; members acting separately → plural. |
| **Sound-Right** | "Neither of the boys *have* come." (sounds fine in speech) | Neither/either/each/everyone = always singular. Trust the rule, not the ear. |
| **Over-thinking** | A simple present-tense blank where students pick a fancy perfect form. | If time reference is a daily habit/fact, simple present wins. Don't invent complexity. |

## Top 10 High-Yield Question Patterns (with Solutions)

**Q1.** The quality of the apples (a) / were not good (b) / so we returned them. (c) / No error (d)
**Answer:** (b)
**Solution:** Subject is "quality" (singular), not "apples". Correct: "was not good". **Trap: Intervening Phrase.**
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q2.** Neither of the two brothers ______ passed the exam.
(a) have (b) has (c) are having (d) were
**Answer:** (b)
**Solution:** "Neither" is singular → "has". **Trap: Sound-Right.**
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q3.** If I ______ a bird, I would fly to you.
(a) am (b) was (c) were (d) had been
**Answer:** (c)
**Solution:** Imaginary condition → subjunctive "were" for all persons. **Trap: Sound-Right ("was" sounds natural).**
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q4.** He denied (a) / to help me (b) / in my difficulty. (c) / No error (d)
**Answer:** (b)
**Solution:** "Deny" takes a gerund: "denied helping me". **Trap: Over-thinking — students hunt for a tense error and miss the gerund rule.**
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q5.** The committee ______ divided on this issue.
(a) is (b) are (c) was (d) has been
**Answer:** (b)
**Solution:** "Divided" shows members acting separately → plural verb "are". **Trap: Collective Noun.**
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q6.** Each of the students (a) / have submitted (b) / the assignment on time. (c) / No error (d)
**Answer:** (b)
**Solution:** "Each of + plural noun" still takes a singular verb: "has submitted". **Trap: Intervening Phrase.**
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q7.** I ______ here since 2015.
(a) am living (b) have been living (c) was living (d) live
**Answer:** (b)
**Solution:** "Since + point of time" → present perfect continuous. **Trap: Over-thinking; "since" is the fixed signal word.**
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q8.** It is time you ______ home.
(a) go (b) went (c) have gone (d) will go
**Answer:** (b)
**Solution:** "It is time + subject" takes past simple (unreal present). **Trap: Sound-Right.**
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q9.** The news ______ really shocking last night.
(a) were (b) are (c) was (d) have been
**Answer:** (c)
**Solution:** "News" is uncountable/singular despite the -s; "last night" → past: "was". **Trap: Sound-Right (plural-looking noun).**
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q10.** Hardly had he reached the station (a) / than the train left. (b) / No error (c)
**Answer:** (b)
**Solution:** "Hardly/Scarcely" pairs with "when", not "than". **Trap: Sound-Right (than follows comparatives, so the ear accepts it).**
**Source:** PYQ-style

## Applying the 15/45-Second Rule

**15-second question (Fact-based):** Q7 above. The moment you see "since 2015", the rule fires automatically — present perfect continuous. No reading of tone, no options comparison needed. Target: 15 seconds.

**45-second question (Inference-based):** Q5 above. "The committee ______ divided" needs you to judge whether the collective acts as one body or as separate members — that is meaning-based, not rule-recall. Read the whole sentence, decide singular vs plural from context, then check tense. Budget: up to 45 seconds. If still unsure, mark and move on — never let one collective-noun question eat two minutes.

## Key Takeaway

Verb questions reward **rule recall under time pressure**. Master the signal words (since, for, hardly, it is time, each of, neither), train your eye to strip intervening phrases, and classify every practice question by trap type — that classification habit is what separates 90-percentile scorers from the rest.
