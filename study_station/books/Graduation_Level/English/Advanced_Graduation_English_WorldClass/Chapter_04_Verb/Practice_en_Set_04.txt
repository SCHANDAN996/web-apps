Questions 76–100: Medium (76–95) | Hard (96–100)

76. Neither the manager nor the employees ______ aware of the new policy.
(a) was (b) is (c) were (d) has been
Answer: (c)
Solution: With "neither…nor", the verb agrees with the nearer subject ("employees" — plural), so "were". Option (a) is the trap for those who agree with "manager".
Source: PYQ-style

77. The quality of the products ______ deteriorated since last year.
(a) have (b) are (c) has (d) were
Answer: (c)
Solution: The subject is "quality" (singular); "of the products" is an intervening phrase. Hence "has". Trap: agreeing with "products".
Source: PYQ-style

78. Hardly ______ the station when the train left.
(a) I had reached (b) I reached (c) had I reached (d) did I reach
Answer: (c)
Solution: After "Hardly", inversion is required and the past perfect is used: "Hardly had I reached… when…".
Source: NCERT-based grammar

79. Each of the boys ______ given a certificate.
(a) were (b) was (c) are (d) have been
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Each of + plural noun" takes a singular verb — "was". Trap: agreeing with "boys".
Source: PYQ-style

80. I wish I ______ a millionaire.
(a) am (b) was (c) were (d) will be
Answer: (c)
Solution: Wishes contrary to present fact take the subjunctive "were" for all persons.
Source: Standard grammar (Wren & Martin)

81. The committee ______ divided in their opinion on this issue.
(a) is (b) was (c) are (d) has been
Answer: (c)
Solution: When a collective noun's members act individually (divided opinion), a plural verb is used — "are". "Their" confirms plurality.
Source: PYQ-style

82. By next December, she ______ her doctorate.
(a) will complete (b) will have completed (c) has completed (d) completes
Answer: (b)
Solution: "By + future time" requires the future perfect: "will have completed".
Source: Standard grammar

83. One of my friends ______ going to London next month.
(a) are (b) is (c) were (d) have been
Answer: (b)
Solution: "One of + plural noun" takes a singular verb — "is". Trap: agreeing with "friends".
Source: PYQ-style

84. No sooner had she finished her speech ______ the audience started clapping.
(a) when (b) then (c) that (d) than
Answer: (d)
Solution: "No sooner" pairs with "than"; "Hardly/Scarcely" pair with "when". A classic pairing trap.
Source: PYQ-style

85. The news ______ really shocking this morning.
(a) were (b) are (c) have been (d) was
Answer: (d)
Solution: "News" is an uncountable noun and always takes a singular verb, despite ending in -s.
Source: NCERT-based grammar

86. He denied ______ the money from the drawer.
(a) to take (b) take (c) taking (d) taken
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Deny" is followed by a gerund (verb-ing), not an infinitive — "denied taking".
Source: Standard grammar

87. Mathematics ______ a difficult subject for many students.
(a) are (b) is (c) have been (d) were
Answer: (b)
Solution: Names of subjects ending in -ics (Mathematics, Physics, Economics) take a singular verb when meaning the subject of study.
Source: NCERT-based grammar

88. Unless you ______ hard, you will not succeed.
(a) work (b) will work (c) worked (d) had worked
Answer: (a)
Solution: In conditional/time clauses referring to the future, use the simple present, not "will" — "Unless you work…".
Source: PYQ-style

89. The teacher, along with his students, ______ gone on a picnic.
(a) have (b) has (c) were (d) are
Answer: (b)
Solution: With "along with", the verb agrees with the first subject ("the teacher" — singular), so "has". Trap: agreeing with "students".
Source: PYQ-style

90. I would rather you ______ home now.
(a) go (b) have gone (c) will go (d) went
Answer: (d)
Solution: "Would rather + subject" is followed by the simple past for a present/future preference — "you went".
Source: Standard grammar

91. Many a soldier ______ laid down his life for the country.
(a) has (b) have (c) are (d) were
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Many a + singular noun" takes a singular verb — "has". Trap: the plural sense tempts "have".
Source: PYQ-style

92. It is high time we ______ our mistakes seriously.
(a) took (b) take (c) will take (d) have taken
Answer: (a)
Solution: "It is high time" is followed by the simple past (subjunctive sense) — "we took".
Source: Standard grammar

93. Bread and butter ______ his daily breakfast.
(a) are (b) were (c) have been (d) is
Answer: (d)
Solution: When two nouns joined by "and" denote one combined dish/idea, the verb is singular — "is".
Source: PYQ-style

94. He is one of those players who ______ always punctual.
(a) are (b) was (c) is (d) has been
Answer: (a)
Solution: In "one of those who…", the relative pronoun "who" refers to the plural "players", so the verb is plural — "are". A classic examiner trap.
Source: PYQ-style

95. The furniture in this room ______ very old.
(a) is (b) were (c) are (d) have been
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Furniture" is uncountable and always takes a singular verb — "is".
Source: NCERT-based grammar

96. Scarcely had he stepped out of the house ______ it began to rain.
(a) than (b) when (c) then (d) that
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Scarcely/Hardly" pair with "when"; only "No sooner" pairs with "than". Trap: mixing the two pairs.
Source: PYQ-style

97. The number of road accidents ______ increasing every year.
(a) is (b) were (c) have been (d) are
Answer: (a)
Solution: "The number of" takes a singular verb; "a number of" takes a plural verb. Hence "is". A high-frequency trap.
Source: PYQ-style

98. Had I known about the meeting earlier, I ______ attended it.
(a) will have (b) had (c) would have (d) would
Answer: (c)
Solution: Third conditional with inversion: "Had I known…, I would have attended…". The main clause needs "would have + V3".
Source: Standard grammar

99. Not only the students but also the teacher ______ surprised by the result.
(a) was (b) are (c) were (d) have been
Answer: (a)
Solution: With "not only…but also", the verb agrees with the nearer subject ("the teacher" — singular), so "was". Trap: agreeing with "students".
Source: PYQ-style

100. Ten thousand rupees ______ a large sum for a villager in those days.
(a) were (b) are (c) have been (d) was
Answer: (d)
Solution: A sum of money regarded as a single amount takes a singular verb — "was". Trap: the plural-looking "rupees".
Source: PYQ-style
