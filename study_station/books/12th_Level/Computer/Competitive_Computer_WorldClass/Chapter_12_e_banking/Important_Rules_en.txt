1. RBI Mandates Two-Factor Authentication (2FA) for All Online (Card-Not-Present) Card Transactions  
   Example: An online purchase requires both card details (first factor) and an OTP sent to the registered mobile (second factor).  
   Exception: Contactless card payments (card-present) up to ₹5,000 per transaction are exempt from PIN entry as per RBI’s 2021 circular; this is a PIN exemption, not a 2FA exemption.

2. NEFT Settles in Half-Hourly Batches; RTGS Processes Transactions Individually in Real Time  
   Example: A NEFT transfer initiated at 10:15 AM settles in the 10:30 AM batch; an RTGS transfer of ₹10 lakh at the same time credits the beneficiary instantly.  
   Exception: NEFT is available 24×7 since December 2019; RTGS is also 24×7 since December 2020, but both require the beneficiary bank to be a participant.

3. IMPS and UPI Enable 24×7 Instant Fund Transfers Including Holidays  
   Example: Sending ₹2,000 via UPI at 2:00 AM on a Sunday credits the receiver immediately.  
   Exception: IMPS has a per-transaction limit of ₹5 lakh; UPI limits vary by bank and may be lower for new users (e.g., ₹5,000 for first 24 hours).

4. Digital Signatures Under the IT Act, 2000 Are Legally Equivalent to Handwritten Signatures  
   Example: A digitally signed loan agreement submitted via a bank’s portal is admissible in court.  
   Exception: The digital signature certificate must be issued by a licensed Certifying Authority (CA) and not be revoked or expired.

5. KYC Compliance Is Mandatory Before Activating Any E-Banking Service  
   Example: A new savings account holder must submit Aadhaar/PAN and complete video KYC before receiving internet banking credentials.  
   Exception: Small accounts (BSBDA) can be opened with simplified KYC but have transaction limits (₹1 lakh annual credit, ₹10,000 monthly withdrawal).

6. OTP Is Valid for a Single Transaction and Expires Within Minutes  
   Example: An OTP received for a fund transfer cannot be reused for a second transaction and typically expires in 3–5 minutes.  
   Exception: Some banks allow a longer window (up to 10 minutes) for specific services like beneficiary addition.

7. Banks Must Encrypt Data in Transit Using TLS 1.2 or Higher and Encrypt Sensitive Data at Rest  
   Example: The bank’s website shows “https://” with a valid certificate; database fields storing passwords use salted hashing (e.g., bcrypt).  
   Exception: Legacy internal systems may still use older protocols during migration, but RBI mandates phase-out timelines.

8. CERT-In Directs All Entities to Report Cyber Security Incidents Within 6 Hours of Detection  
   Example: A bank detecting a ransomware attack on its ATM switch must inform CERT-In within 6 hours.  
   Exception: The 6-hour rule applies to all cyber security incidents as per CERT-In’s April 2022 direction; sectoral guidelines may prescribe additional reporting requirements.

9. Customer Liability for Unauthorized Electronic Transactions Is Zero If Reported Within 3 Working Days  
   Example: A customer spots a fraudulent UPI debit on Day 1 and informs the bank; the bank credits the amount within 10 working days.  
   Exception: Liability is ₹5,000 for Basic Savings Bank Deposit Accounts (BSBDA) and ₹10,000 for other accounts if reported after 3 days but within 7 days; beyond 7 days, liability is determined by the bank’s board-approved policy.

10. Virtual Payment Address (VPA) in UPI Masks the Actual Bank Account Number and IFSC  
    Example: Sending money to “user@upi” does not reveal the receiver’s account number or branch.  
    Exception: The VPA is linked to a specific bank account; if the user changes the linked account, the VPA maps to the new account.

11. Auto-Debit Mandates (e.g., for SIPs, Subscriptions) Require Explicit Customer Consent and a Pre-Debit Notification at Least 24 Hours Before  
    Example: A mutual fund SIP mandate via UPI Autopay sends an SMS/email 24 hours before the debit date.  
    Exception: Mandates registered before RBI’s 2021 framework (e.g., ECS) follow older rules but must be migrated to the new framework.

12. Tokenization Replaces Actual Card Details with a Unique Token; Banks and Merchants Cannot Store Full Card Data (CVV, PIN) Post-Tokenization  
    Example: A saved card on an e-commerce site shows only the last four digits; the token is used for subsequent payments.  
    Exception: Card-on-file tokenization applies to domestic online transactions; international transactions may follow different standards.

13. Phishing (Email), Vishing (Voice Call), and Smishing (SMS) Are the Most Common Social Engineering Attacks Targeting Banking Credentials  
    Example: A caller claiming to be from “bank security” asks for OTP to “block a fraudulent transaction” — the OTP is actually used to authorize a transfer.  
    Exception: Banks never ask for OTP, PIN, or password via call, email, or SMS; any such request is fraudulent.

14. Mobile Banking Apps Must Implement App Sandboxing, Root/Jailbreak Detection, and Certificate Pinning  
    Example: The app refuses to launch on a rooted Android device and shows a security warning.  
    Exception: Some banks allow limited functionality (e.g., balance check) on compromised devices but block transactions.

15. RBI’s Integrated Ombudsman Scheme (2021) Provides a Single-Window Redressal for Digital Banking Grievances  
    Example: A customer whose UPI complaint is unresolved by the bank for 30 days can file a complaint online at cms.rbi.org.in.  
    Exception: The scheme covers deficiency in service; disputes requiring legal adjudication (e.g., property title) are excluded.
