Card 1
Front: What is an input device?
Back: A hardware component used to send data or instructions to a computer (e.g., keyboard, mouse, scanner).

Card 2
Front: What is an output device?
Back: A hardware component that receives data from a computer and presents it to the user (e.g., monitor, printer, speaker).

Card 3
Front: Name two devices that function as both input and output.
Back: Touchscreen, modem, network interface card (NIC), headset with microphone.

Card 4
Front: What is a port in computer hardware?
Back: A physical interface (socket) on a computer where external devices are connected via cables.

Card 5
Front: Which port is commonly used for connecting a keyboard or mouse on modern PCs?
Back: USB (Universal Serial Bus) port. Legacy PS/2 ports (purple for keyboard, green for mouse) are now rare.

Card 6
Front: What does USB stand for?
Back: Universal Serial Bus.

Card 7
Front: List three common USB connector types.
Back: USB Type-A, USB Type-B, USB Type-C (also Micro-USB, Mini-USB).

Card 8
Front: What is the maximum data transfer rate of USB 3.0 (also called USB 3.1 Gen 1)?
Back: 5 Gbps (SuperSpeed).

Card 9
Front: Which port is used for connecting a monitor using a digital video signal?
Back: HDMI (High-Definition Multimedia Interface), DisplayPort, or DVI.

Card 10
Front: What is the purpose of an Ethernet (RJ-45) port?
Back: To connect a computer to a wired local area network (LAN) using twisted-pair cable.

Card 11
Front: Which port is traditionally used for a serial communication device (e.g., old modem, barcode scanner)?
Back: DB-9 (DE-9) male connector, often called a COM port or RS-232 port.

Card 12
Front: What is the function of a parallel port (DB-25 female)?
Back: Historically used for printers; transfers 8 bits simultaneously. Largely replaced by USB.

Card 13
Front: Which port connects speakers, headphones, or a microphone on a PC?
Back: 3.5 mm audio jacks — green (line-out/speakers), pink (mic-in), blue (line-in).

Card 14
Front: What is the difference between HDMI and DisplayPort?
Back: Both carry digital video/audio. DisplayPort supports higher refresh rates and daisy-chaining; HDMI is common on TVs and consumer electronics.

Card 15
Front: What does VGA stand for, and what type of signal does it carry?
Back: Video Graphics Array; carries analog video signal only (no audio).

Card 16
Front: Which port is used for high-speed external storage and video on modern laptops?
Back: USB Type-C with Thunderbolt 3/4 or USB4 support (up to 40 Gbps).

Card 17
Front: What is a KVM switch?
Back: Keyboard, Video, Mouse switch — allows one set of keyboard, monitor, and mouse to control multiple computers.

Card 18
Front: Common mistake: Confusing USB versions with connector shapes. Clarify.
Back: USB version (2.0, 3.0, 3.1, 4) defines speed/power; connector shape (Type-A, Type-C) defines physical form. A Type-C port can run at USB 2.0 speeds.
