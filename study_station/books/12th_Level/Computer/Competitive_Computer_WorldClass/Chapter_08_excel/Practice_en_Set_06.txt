126. What is the default file extension for an Excel workbook in versions 2007 and later?
(a) .xls (b) .xlsx (c) .xlsm (d) .xlsb
Answer: (b)
Solution: The legacy .xls format was used up to Excel 2003, while .xlsm is for macro-enabled workbooks and .xlsb is binary; the standard default for modern Excel is the XML-based .xlsx format. Therefore, the correct answer is .xlsx.

128. In a formula, what does the $ symbol before a column letter and row number (e.g., $A$1) indicate?
(a) Mixed reference (b) Absolute reference (c) Relative reference (d) Circular reference
Answer: (b)
Solution: A mixed reference locks only the row or only the column (e.g., A$1 or $A1), while a relative reference has no $ signs; $A$1 locks both, making it an absolute reference. Thus, the answer is Absolute reference.
Source: Microsoft Learn

138. What is the result of the formula =2+3*4 in Excel?
(a) 24 (b) 20 (c) 11 (d) 14
Answer: (d)
Solution: Following operator precedence, multiplication is performed before addition: 3*4 = 12, then 2+12 = 14; 20 would result from left-to-right evaluation without precedence. So the answer is 14.

142. In Excel, a circular reference occurs when:
(a) Two cells refer to each other (b) A formula refers to its own cell directly or indirectly (c) A formula refers to a deleted cell (d) A formula contains a syntax error
Answer: (b)
Solution: Two cells referring to each other is a specific case of circular reference, a deleted cell shows #REF!, and a syntax error prevents entry; the general definition is a formula that depends on its own result. 146. A formula in cell D10 is =B$5+$C5. If this formula is copied to cell E12, what will the formula become?
(a) =C$5+$D5 (b) =C$5+$D7 (c) =C$5+$C7 (d) =B$5+$C7
Answer: (c)
Solution: B$5 has a relative column (B→C) and absolute row (5 stays 5); $C5 has an absolute column (C stays C) and relative row (5→7 because copied down 2 rows). The trap is thinking the $C5 column changes or the row stays 5. Therefore, the formula becomes =C$5+$C7.

149. What does the formula =SUMPRODUCT((A1:A5>10)*(B1:B5)) calculate?
(a) Product of sums of A1:A5 and B1:B5 (b) Sum of B1:B5 where corresponding A1:A5 > 10 (c) Count of cells in A1:A5 > 10 multiplied by sum of B1:B5 (d) Sum of products of A1:A5 and B1:B5 for all rows
Answer: (b)
Solution: The Boolean array (A1:A5>10) acts as a 1/0 filter; multiplying by B1:B5 zeros out rows where the condition is false, then SUMPRODUCT sums the result. The trap is thinking it multiplies sums or counts. Therefore, it calculates Sum of B1:B5 where corresponding A1:A5 > 10.
