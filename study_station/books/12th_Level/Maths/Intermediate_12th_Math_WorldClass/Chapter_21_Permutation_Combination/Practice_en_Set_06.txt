126. In how many ways can 5 boys and 5 girls be seated in a row so that no two girls sit together?
(a) 6! × 5! (b) 5! × 5! (c) 10! (d) 2 × 5! × 5!
Answer: (a)
Solution: The tempting wrong option 2 × 5! × 5! treats this like an alternating arrangement, but that forces a strict boy-girl pattern, which is not required. First arrange the 5 boys in 5! ways, creating 6 gaps (including both ends); place the 5 girls in these gaps in ⁶P₅ = 6! ways. Total = 5! × 6!, so the answer is 6! × 5!.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Mathematics

127. How many words can be formed from the letters of the word "MISSISSIPPI"?
(a) 11! (b) 39600 (c) 34650 (d) 69300
Answer: (c)
Solution: The tempting wrong option 11! ignores the repeated letters. MISSISSIPPI has 11 letters with I appearing 4 times, S appearing 4 times, and P appearing 2 times, so the count is 11!/(4! × 4! × 2!) = 34650, so the answer is 34650.

128. In how many ways can 10 identical balls be distributed among 4 distinct boxes if each box must get at least one ball?
(a) 120 (b) 286 (c) 210 (d) 84
Answer: (d)
Solution: The tempting wrong option 286 = ¹³C₃ counts distributions where boxes may stay empty. With at least one ball per box, give one ball to each box first, then distribute the remaining 6 identical balls freely: ⁹C₃ = 84, so the answer is 84.

129. How many diagonals does a convex polygon of 12 sides have?
(a) 48 (b) 66 (c) 60 (d) 54
Answer: (d)
Solution: The tempting wrong option 66 = ¹²C₂ counts all line segments joining pairs of vertices, including the 12 sides. Diagonals = ¹²C₂ − 12 = 66 − 12 = 54, so the answer is 54.

130. A committee of 5 is to be formed from 6 men and 4 women so that the committee contains at least 2 women. In how many ways can this be done?
(a) 186 (b) 180 (c) 336 (d) 246
Answer: (a)
Solution: The tempting wrong option 336 = ⁴C₂ × ⁸C₃ double-counts committees having 3 or 4 women. The correct case-by-case count is ⁴C₂·⁶C₃ + ⁴C₃·⁶C₂ + ⁴C₄·⁶C₁ = 120 + 60 + 6 = 186, so the answer is 186.
Source: PYQ-style

131. How many 4-digit numbers greater than 5000 can be formed using the digits 0, 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6 without repetition?
(a) 480 (b) 360 (c) 240 (d) 720
Answer: (c)
Solution: The tempting wrong option 720 = 6 × 5 × 4 × 3 wrongly allows the thousands digit to be 1, 2, 3 or 4, which produces numbers below 5000. The thousands digit must be 5 or 6 (2 choices), and the remaining three places are filled from the remaining 6 digits in ⁶P₃ = 120 ways: 2 × 120 = 240, so the answer is 240.

132. In how many ways can 8 people sit around a round table if 2 particular persons must not sit together?
(a) 4320 (b) 3600 (c) 5040 (d) 720
Answer: (b)
Solution: The tempting wrong option 5040 = 7! counts all circular arrangements without the restriction. Total circular arrangements = 7! = 5040; arrangements with the two together = 2 × 6! = 1440; required = 5040 − 1440 = 3600, so the answer is 3600.

133. How many words can be formed using all the letters of "ARRANGE" so that the two R's are never together?
(a) 1080 (b) 1260 (c) 900 (d) 840
Answer: (c)
Solution: The tempting wrong option 1260 counts all arrangements ignoring the restriction. Total arrangements = 7!/(2!·2!) = 1260 (A and R each repeat twice); arrangements with both R's together = 6!/2! = 360; required = 1260 − 360 = 900, so the answer is 900.

135. How many numbers between 100 and 1000 have all distinct digits?
(a) 900 (b) 720 (c) 504 (d) 648
Answer: (d)
Solution: The tempting wrong option 900 = 9 × 10 × 10 allows digits to repeat. The hundreds digit has 9 choices (1–9), the tens digit has 9 choices (0 plus the 8 unused non-zero digits), and the units digit has 8 choices: 9 × 9 × 8 = 648, so the answer is 648.

138. How many ways are there to arrange 6 different books on a shelf so that two particular books are always together?
(a) 120 (b) 240 (c) 720 (d) 360
Answer: (b)
Solution: The tempting wrong option 720 = 6! ignores the togetherness condition. Treat the two books as one unit: 5 units arrange in 5! = 120 ways, and the pair swaps internally in 2 ways: 120 × 2 = 240, so the answer is 240.

139. In how many ways can 12 people be divided into three groups of 4 each?
(a) 11550 (b) 34650 (c) 17325 (d) 5775
Answer: (d)
Solution: The tempting wrong option 34650 = 12!/(4!·4!·4!) treats the three groups as labelled, but identical-sized unlabelled groups need division by 3!. So the count is 12!/(4!·4!·4!·3!) = 5775, so the answer is 5775.

140. How many 5-letter words (meaningful or not) can be formed from the letters of "EQUATION" if each word must contain exactly 3 vowels and 2 consonants?
(a) 7200 (b) 1440 (c) 3600 (d) 2880
Answer: (c)
Solution: The tempting wrong option 1440 undercounts by skipping the arrangement step. EQUATION has 5 vowels and 3 consonants; choose 3 vowels in ⁵C₃ = 10 ways and 2 consonants in ³C₂ = 3 ways, then arrange the 5 letters in 5! = 120 ways: 10 × 3 × 120 = 3600, so the answer is 3600.

(a) 5040 (b) 360 (c) 720 (d) 2520
Answer: (b)
Solution: The tempting wrong option 720 = 6! treats the necklace like a round table, but a necklace can be flipped, merging clockwise and anticlockwise arrangements. Necklace arrangements = (7−1)!/2 = 720/2 = 360, so the answer is 360.

147. How many ways can 6 men and 6 women be seated alternately in a row?

150. In how many ways can 10 examination papers be arranged so that the best and the worst papers never come together?
(a) 8 × 9! (b) 9 × 9! (c) 10! − 9! (d) 8 × 8!
Answer: (a)
Solution: The tempting wrong option 10! − 9! subtracts only one ordering of the pair instead of both. Total arrangements = 10!; arrangements with the two together = 2 × 9!; required = 10! − 2 × 9! = 9!(10 − 2) = 8 × 9!, so the answer is 8 × 9!.
