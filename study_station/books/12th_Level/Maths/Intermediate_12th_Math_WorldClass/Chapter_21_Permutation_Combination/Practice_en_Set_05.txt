101. Find the value of 5P3.
(a) 20 (b) 60 (c) 120 (d) 15
Answer: (b)
Solution: The tempting wrong option 120 is 5!, which counts arrangements of all 5 objects, not 3 out of 5. Using nPr = n!/(n−r)!, we get 5P3 = 5!/2! = 5 × 4 × 3 = 60, so the answer is 60.

102. In how many ways can 2 students be chosen from 6 students?
(a) 15 (b) 30 (c) 12 (d) 36
Answer: (a)
Solution: The tempting wrong option 30 comes from using permutation 6P2, but selection does not involve order. Using 6C2 = 6!/(2! × 4!) = (6 × 5)/2 = 15, so the answer is 15.

103. How many different arrangements can be made using all the letters of the word APPLE?
(a) 120 (b) 24 (c) 30 (d) 60
Answer: (d)
Solution: The tempting wrong option 120 is 5!, which ignores that the letter P repeats twice. Dividing by 2! for the repeated P: 5!/2! = 120/2 = 60, so the answer is 60.

104. A committee of 3 members is to be formed from 8 persons. In how many ways can this be done?
(a) 336 (b) 112 (c) 56 (d) 24
Answer: (c)
Solution: The tempting wrong option 336 is 8P3, which wrongly treats committee posts as distinct. Since order does not matter, 8C3 = (8 × 7 × 6)/(3 × 2 × 1) = 56, so the answer is 56.

105. In how many ways can 6 persons be seated around a round table?

Solution: The tempting wrong option 720 is 6!, which is for a straight row; in a circle, rotations of the same arrangement are identical. Circular arrangements = (6−1)! = 5! = 120, so the answer is 120.

111. At a party, 10 people shake hands with each other exactly once. How many handshakes take place?
(a) 45 (b) 90 (c) 100 (d) 20
Answer: (a)
Solution: The tempting wrong option 90 counts each handshake twice (A with B and B with A). Each handshake is a selection of 2 from 10: 10C2 = 45, so the answer is 45.

Solution: The tempting wrong option 60 results from multiplying wrong combination values or forgetting one selection step. Choose 2 women from 4: 4C2 = 6; choose 2 men from 6: 6C2 = 15; total = 6 × 15 = 90, so the answer is 90.

120. In how many ways can 5 men and 4 women be seated in a row so that no two women sit together?
(a) 2880 (b) 14400 (c) 720 (d) 43200
Answer: (d)
Solution: The tempting wrong option 2880 forces a strict alternating pattern, but women may also sit in the two end gaps. Arrange the 5 men first: 5! = 120 ways; this creates 6 gaps, and placing 4 women in different gaps gives 6P4 = 360 ways, giving 120 × 360 = 43200, so the answer is 43200.

122. A committee of 5 is to be formed from 7 men and 5 women such that at least 3 women are included. In how many ways can this be done?
(a) 252 (b) 210 (c) 246 (d) 231
Answer: (c)
Solution: The tempting wrong option 210 counts only the case of exactly 3 women and stops there. Add all valid cases: exactly 3 women: 5C3 × 7C2 = 10 × 21 = 210; exactly 4 women: 5C4 × 7C1 = 5 × 7 = 35; exactly 5 women: 5C5 = 1. Total = 210 + 35 + 1 = 246, so the answer is 246.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Mathematics

123. How many 4-digit even numbers can be formed using the digits 0, 1, 2, 3, 4, 5 without repetition?
(a) 300 (b) 180 (c) 156 (d) 96
Answer: (c)
Solution: The tempting wrong option 180 assumes all even endings behave alike and ignores the zero restriction at the thousands place. Split by units digit: if units is 0, thousands has 5 choices, then 4 × 3 for the middle: 5 × 4 × 3 = 60. If units is 2 or 4 (2 choices), thousands has 4 choices (non-zero, non-used), then 4 × 3 for the middle: 2 × 4 × 4 × 3 = 96. Total = 60 + 96 = 156, so the answer is 156.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Mathematics

124. Six persons are seated around a round table. In how many arrangements are two particular persons NOT sitting together?
(a) 72 (b) 48 (c) 96 (d) 120
Answer: (a)
Solution: The tempting wrong option 48 is actually the count of arrangements where the two ARE together, which is the opposite of what is asked. Total circular arrangements = 5! = 120; together arrangements = 2 × 4! = 48; not together = 120 − 48 = 72, so the answer is 72.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Mathematics

125. There are 12 points in a plane, out of which 5 are collinear. How many triangles can be formed using these points as vertices?
(a) 210 (b) 220 (c) 215 (d) 200
Answer: (a)
Solution: The tempting wrong option 220 is 12C3, which counts all triples including the collinear ones that cannot form triangles. Subtract the degenerate triples from the 5 collinear points: 12C3 − 5C3 = 220 − 10 = 210, so the answer is 210.
