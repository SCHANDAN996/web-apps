Card 1
Front: What is the Fundamental Principle of Counting (multiplication rule)?
Back: If one task can be done in m ways and another in n ways, both together can be done in m × n ways.

Card 2
Front: When do we ADD the number of ways instead of multiplying?
Back: When the tasks are alternatives (either/or) — the addition rule: m + n ways.

Card 3
Front: What is n! (n factorial)?
Back: n! = n × (n−1) × (n−2) × … × 2 × 1. Also, 0! = 1.

Card 4
Front: What does ⁿPᵣ count, and what is its formula?
Back: Arrangements of r objects out of n distinct objects (order matters): ⁿPᵣ = n! / (n−r)!.

Card 5
Front: What does ⁿCᵣ count, and what is its formula?
Back: Selections of r objects out of n distinct objects (order doesn't matter): ⁿCᵣ = n! / [r!(n−r)!].

Card 6
Front: What is the mnemonic trick to remember Permutation vs Combination?
Back: "P = Position matters, C = Committee (only chosen)." Permutation arranges; Combination just selects.

Card 7
Front: What is the relation between ⁿPᵣ and ⁿCᵣ?
Back: ⁿPᵣ = ⁿCᵣ × r! — every selection of r objects can be arranged in r! ways.

Card 8
Front: What is the value of ⁿC₀ and ⁿCₙ?
Back: Both equal 1 — there is exactly one way to choose nothing or everything.

Card 9
Front: What is the symmetry property of combinations?
Back: ⁿCᵣ = ⁿCₙ₋ᵣ. Choosing r to keep is the same as choosing n−r to leave out.

Card 10
Front: How many ways can n distinct objects be arranged in a circle?
Back: (n−1)! — fix one object to remove rotational duplicates.

Card 11
Front: What is the trick to remember circular arrangement?
Back: "Circle mein ek ko baitha do" — in a circle, seat one person fixed, arrange the rest: (n−1)!.

Card 12
Front: How many ways can n distinct beads be arranged in a necklace (or garland)?
Back: (n−1)! / 2 — a necklace can be flipped, so clockwise and anticlockwise count as the same.

Card 13
Front: How do you arrange n objects where p are identical of one kind, q of another?
Back: n! / (p! × q! × …) — divide by the factorials of the repeated groups.

Card 14
Front: How many arrangements does the word "BANANA" have?
Back: 6! / (3! × 2!) = 60 — A repeats 3 times, N repeats 2 times.

Card 15
Front: What is the trick for "vowels together" or "certain people always together" problems?
Back: Tie them into one bundle: treat the group as a single unit, arrange units, then multiply by arrangements inside the bundle.

Card 16
Front: How do you solve "no two vowels together" problems?
Back: Gap method: arrange consonants first, then place vowels in the gaps between/around them.

Card 17
Front: What is ⁿCᵣ + ⁿCᵣ₋₁ equal to?
Back: ⁿ⁺¹Cᵣ — Pascal's rule, the building block of Pascal's triangle.

Card 18
Front: How many ways can a committee of r people be formed from n people if one particular person must be included?
Back: ⁿ⁻¹Cᵣ₋₁ — fix that person, choose the remaining r−1 from n−1.

Card 19
Front: How many diagonals does a polygon with n sides have?
Back: ⁿC₂ − n = n(n−3)/2 — every pair of vertices gives a line; subtract the n sides.

Card 20
Front: In how many ways can r identical objects be distributed among n distinct boxes (boxes may be empty)?
Back: ⁿ⁺ʳ⁻¹Cᵣ — the stars and bars formula.
