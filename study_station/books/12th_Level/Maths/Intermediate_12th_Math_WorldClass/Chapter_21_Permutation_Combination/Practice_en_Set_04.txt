Questions 76–100: Medium

76. In how many ways can the letters of the word "APPLE" be arranged?
(a) 120 (b) 60 (c) 30 (d) 24
Answer: (b)
Solution: The tempting wrong option 120 ignores the repeated P and uses plain 5!. The word APPLE has 5 letters with P repeated twice, so arrangements = 5!/2! = 120/2, so the answer is 60.

77. How many 3-digit numbers can be formed using the digits 1, 2, 3, 4, 5 if repetition of digits is allowed?
(a) 60 (b) 125 (c) 100 (d) 25
Answer: (b)
Solution: The tempting wrong option 60 comes from 5 × 4 × 3, which wrongly assumes repetition is not allowed. Each of the 3 places can be filled in 5 ways since repetition is allowed, giving 5 × 5 × 5, so the answer is 125.

78. In how many ways can 5 boys and 3 girls be seated in a row so that all the girls sit together?
(a) 5760 (b) 720 (c) 4320 (d) 40320
Answer: (c)
Solution: The tempting wrong option 720 forgets the internal arrangement of the girls inside their block. Treat the 3 girls as one block, giving 6 units arranged in 6! = 720 ways, and the girls arrange within the block in 3! = 6 ways, so total = 720 × 6, so the answer is 4320.

79. How many diagonals does a polygon with 10 sides have?
(a) 45 (b) 35 (c) 40 (d) 30
Answer: (b)
Solution: The tempting wrong option 45 is C(10,2), which counts all segments joining vertices including the 10 sides. Diagonals = n(n−3)/2 = 10 × 7/2, so the answer is 35.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Mathematics

80. A committee of 4 is to be formed from 6 men and 4 women such that it contains at least one woman. In how many ways can this be done?
(a) 150 (b) 195 (c) 180 (d) 210
Answer: (b)
Solution: The tempting wrong option 210 ignores the "at least one woman" condition entirely. Total committees = C(10,4) = 210 and all-men committees = C(6,4) = 15, so required = 210 − 15, so the answer is 195.

81. In how many ways can the letters of the word "MISSISSIPPI" be arranged?
(a) 3465 (b) 39916800 (c) 69300 (d) 34650
Answer: (d)
Solution: The tempting wrong option 39916800 is plain 11!, ignoring all repeated letters. The word has 11 letters with I ×4, S ×4, P ×2, so arrangements = 11!/(4! × 4! × 2!) = 39916800/1152, so the answer is 34650.

82. How many numbers greater than 3000 can be formed from the digits 1, 2, 3, 4, 5 without repetition, using 4 digits?
(a) 72 (b) 96 (c) 48 (d) 120
Answer: (a)
Solution: The tempting wrong option 120 is 5 × 4 × 3 × 2, ignoring the "greater than 3000" restriction. The thousands digit must be 3, 4 or 5 (3 choices), and the remaining places fill in 4 × 3 × 2 = 24 ways, so total = 3 × 24, so the answer is 72.

83. In how many ways can 6 different books be distributed among 3 students so that each student gets at least one book?
(a) 216 (b) 540 (c) 360 (d) 729
Answer: (b)
Solution: The tempting wrong option 729 ignores the "each gets at least one" condition. By inclusion-exclusion: 3^6 − C(3,1)·2^6 + C(3,2)·1^6 = 729 − 192 + 3, so the answer is 540.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Mathematics

84. From a group of 7 men and 6 women, a committee of 5 is to be formed with exactly 3 men. In how many ways can this be done?
(a) 525 (b) 350 (c) 700 (d) 210
Answer: (a)
Solution: The tempting wrong option 350 pairs C(7,3) with C(6,3) by mistake, which would make a 6-member committee. Choose 3 men from 7 in C(7,3) = 35 ways and 2 women from 6 in C(6,2) = 15 ways, so total = 35 × 15, so the answer is 525.

85. In how many ways can 8 people be seated around a circular table if two particular persons must sit together?
(a) 1440 (b) 5040 (c) 720 (d) 10080
Answer: (a)
Solution: The tempting wrong option 5040 is 7!, which wrongly applies linear arrangement to a circle. Treat the pair as one unit, giving 7 units around a circle in (7−1)! = 720 ways, and the pair swaps in 2 ways, so total = 720 × 2, so the answer is 1440.

86. How many words can be formed from the letters of "DAUGHTER" so that the vowels always come together?
(a) 4320 (b) 40320 (c) 2160 (d) 8640
Answer: (a)
Solution: The tempting wrong option 40320 is 8!, ignoring the togetherness condition. Vowels A, U, E form one block with the 5 consonants, giving 6 units in 6! = 720 ways, and the vowels arrange internally in 3! = 6 ways, so total = 720 × 6, so the answer is 4320.

87. A box contains 5 red and 4 blue balls. In how many ways can 3 balls be drawn such that at least one is blue?
(a) 84 (b) 64 (c) 74 (d) 80
Answer: (c)
Solution: The tempting wrong option 84 ignores the "at least one blue" condition. Total draws = C(9,3) = 84 and all-red draws = C(5,3) = 10, so required = 84 − 10, so the answer is 74.

88. How many 4-letter words (with or without meaning) can be formed from the letters of "LOGARITHMS" using each letter at most once?
(a) 5040 (b) 10000 (c) 210 (d) 720
Answer: (a)
Solution: The tempting wrong option 210 is C(10,4), which ignores the order of letters in a word. LOGARITHMS has 10 distinct letters, so arrangements = P(10,4) = 10 × 9 × 8 × 7, so the answer is 5040.

89. In how many ways can 5 prizes be given to 4 boys if each boy is eligible for all the prizes?
(a) 20 (b) 625 (c) 1024 (d) 120
Answer: (c)
Solution: The tempting wrong option 625 is 5^4, which reverses the base and exponent — prizes are assigned to boys, so the number of boys is the base. Each prize can go to any of the 4 boys, giving 4^5, so the answer is 1024.

90. How many straight lines can be drawn through 10 points, no three of which are collinear?
(a) 100 (b) 45 (c) 90 (d) 55
Answer: (b)
Solution: The tempting wrong option 90 doubles the count by treating each pair as ordered, but a line through two points is the same regardless of order. Each line needs 2 points, so lines = C(10,2), so the answer is 45.

91. In how many ways can the letters of "BANANA" be arranged?
(a) 120 (b) 720 (c) 60 (d) 360
Answer: (c)
Solution: The tempting wrong option 720 is plain 6!, ignoring the repeated A's and N's. BANANA has 6 letters with A ×3 and N ×2, so arrangements = 6!/(3! × 2!) = 720/12, so the answer is 60.

92. A cricket team of 11 is to be selected from 15 players, and one particular player must be included. In how many ways can the team be chosen?
(a) 455 (b) 1365 (c) 364 (d) 1001
Answer: (d)
Solution: The tempting wrong option 1365 is C(15,11), which ignores the compulsory inclusion. Since one player is fixed, choose the remaining 10 from the other 14: C(14,10) = C(14,4), so the answer is 1001.

(a) 36 (b) 60 (c) 24 (d) 48
Answer: (c)
Solution: The tempting wrong option 60 is 5 × 4 × 3, ignoring the evenness condition. The units digit must be 2 or 4 (2 choices), and the other two places fill from the leftover 4 digits in 4 × 3 = 12 ways, so total = 2 × 12, so the answer is 24.

94. In how many ways can 4 boys and 4 girls be seated in a row so that boys and girls alternate?
(a) 576 (b) 2880 (c) 40320 (d) 1152
Answer: (d)
Solution: The tempting wrong option 576 counts only one starting pattern. The row can start with a boy or a girl (2 patterns), with boys in 4! = 24 ways and girls in 4! = 24 ways, so total = 2 × 24 × 24, so the answer is 1152.

95. How many triangles can be formed from 12 points in a plane, of which 5 are collinear?
(a) 220 (b) 200 (c) 210 (d) 110
Answer: (c)
Solution: The tempting wrong option 220 ignores that collinear points cannot form a triangle. Total triples = C(12,3) = 220 and degenerate triples from the 5 collinear points = C(5,3) = 10, so required = 220 − 10, so the answer is 210.

96. In how many ways can 10 identical balls be distributed among 3 distinct boxes if each box must get at least one ball?
(a) 45 (b) 66 (c) 72 (d) 36
Answer: (d)
Solution: The tempting wrong option 66 is C(12,2), which allows empty boxes and ignores the "at least one" condition. By stars and bars with each box non-empty, ways = C(10−1, 3−1) = C(9,2), so the answer is 36.

97. In how many ways can the letters of the word "BETTER" be arranged so that the two T's are never together?
(a) 180 (b) 90 (c) 60 (d) 120
Answer: (d)
Solution: The tempting wrong option 180 is the total arrangements 6!/(2! × 2!), which ignores the "never together" condition. Arrangements with the two T's together (treat TT as one block, E repeated twice) = 5!/2! = 60, so required = 180 − 60, so the answer is 120.

98. A student must answer 8 out of 12 questions, including exactly 4 of the first 6 questions. In how many ways can he choose?
(a) 225 (b) 450 (c) 495 (d) 300
Answer: (a)
Solution: The tempting wrong option 495 is C(12,8), which ignores the condition that exactly 4 must come from the first 6. Choose 4 of the first 6 in C(6,4) = 15 ways and the remaining 4 from the last 6 in C(6,4) = 15 ways, so total = 15 × 15, so the answer is 225.

99. In how many ways can 7 different beads be strung into a necklace?
(a) 2520 (b) 720 (c) 5040 (d) 360
Answer: (d)
Solution: The tempting wrong option 720 forgets that a necklace read clockwise and anticlockwise is the same. Circular arrangements of 7 beads = (7−1)! = 720, and dividing by 2 for flipping, so the answer is 360.

100. How many 5-digit numbers divisible by 4 can be formed using the digits 0, 1, 2, 3, 4, 5 without repetition?
(a) 144 (b) 120 (c) 96 (d) 156
Answer: (a)
Solution: The tempting wrong option 120 wrongly assumes all endings behave alike, ignoring that endings containing 0 free up the first digit. Valid two-digit endings with distinct digits are 04, 12, 20, 24, 32, 40, 52; endings with 0 (04, 20, 40) give 4 × 3 × 2 = 24 each and endings without 0 (12, 24, 32, 52) give 3 × 3 × 2 = 18 each, so total = 3 × 24 + 4 × 18 = 72 + 72, so the answer is 144.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Mathematics
