1. In how many ways can 5 different books be arranged on a shelf?
(a) 120 (b) 25 (c) 60 (d) 720
Answer: (a)
Solution: Arranging 5 distinct books is a permutation of 5 objects taken all at once, which equals 5! = 5 × 4 × 3 × 2 × 1 = 120. The tempting option 720 is 6!, which would apply only if there were 6 books, so the answer is 120.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Mathematics

2. How many 3-digit numbers can be formed using the digits 1, 2, 3, 4, 5 without repetition?
(a) 15 (b) 125 (c) 60 (d) 30
Answer: (c)
Solution: This is a permutation of 5 digits taken 3 at a time: 5P3 = 5 × 4 × 3 = 60. The option 125 comes from wrongly allowing repetition (5 × 5 × 5), but repetition is not allowed here, so the answer is 60.

3. The value of 6! is:
(a) 720 (b) 120 (c) 5040 (d) 360
Answer: (a)
Solution: 6! = 6 × 5 × 4 × 3 × 2 × 1 = 720. The option 120 is 5!, a common slip when students stop multiplying one step early, so the answer is 720.

4. In how many ways can a committee of 3 members be chosen from 8 persons?
(a) 24 (b) 56 (c) 336 (d) 512
Answer: (b)
Solution: Choosing a committee is a combination since order does not matter: 8C3 = (8 × 7 × 6)/(3 × 2 × 1) = 56. The option 336 is 8P3, which wrongly counts arrangements instead of selections, so the answer is 56.

5. The value of 7C2 is:
(a) 21 (b) 42 (c) 14 (d) 49
Answer: (a)
Solution: 7C2 = (7 × 6)/(2 × 1) = 21. The option 42 is 7P2, which counts ordered pairs and ignores that selection is unordered, so the answer is 21.

10. The value of 9C0 is:
(a) 0 (b) 9 (c) 1 (d) 81
Answer: (c)
Solution: By definition, nC0 = 1 for any n, since there is exactly one way to choose nothing. The option 0 is a common misconception, but the empty selection counts as one way, so the answer is 1.

Answer: (d)
Solution: With repetition allowed, each of the two places can be filled in 4 ways: 4 × 4 = 16. The option 12 is 4P2, which applies only when repetition is not allowed, so the answer is 16.

(a) 5 (b) 6 (c) 7 (d) 8
Answer: (b)
Solution: nC2 = n(n−1)/2 = 15 gives n(n−1) = 30, so n = 6 since 6 × 5 = 30. Testing the option 5 gives only 10, which is too small, so the answer is 6.

17. How many diagonals does a hexagon have?
(a) 6 (b) 12 (c) 9 (d) 15
Answer: (c)
Solution: Number of diagonals = nC2 − n = 6C2 − 6 = 15 − 6 = 9, since sides are not diagonals. The option 15 forgets to subtract the 6 sides, so the answer is 9.

(a) 20 (b) 10 (c) 15 (d) 5

Solution: APPLE has 5 letters with P repeated twice, so arrangements = 5!/2! = 120/2 = 60. The option 120 ignores the repeated P and overcounts, so the answer is 60.

22. In how many ways can 6 people be seated around a round table?
(a) 720 (b) 120 (c) 360 (d) 60
Answer: (b)
Solution: Circular arrangements of n people = (n−1)! = 5! = 120, since rotations of the same arrangement are identical. The option 720 is 6!, which wrongly treats the table like a straight row, so the answer is 120.

23. A committee of 4 is to be formed from 5 men and 4 women such that it contains exactly 2 men and 2 women. In how many ways can this be done?
(a) 60 (b) 120 (c) 30 (d) 126
Answer: (a)
Solution: Choose 2 men in 5C2 = 10 ways and 2 women in 4C2 = 6 ways; total = 10 × 6 = 60. The option 120 comes from wrongly adding extra arrangements of the chosen members, so the answer is 60.

25. In how many ways can the letters of the word BALLOON be arranged?
(a) 1260 (b) 5040 (c) 2520 (d) 630
Answer: (a)
Solution: BALLOON has 7 letters with L repeated twice and O repeated twice, so arrangements = 7!/(2! × 2!) = 5040/4 = 1260. The option 5040 ignores both repetitions, and 2520 accounts for only one of them, so the answer is 1260.
