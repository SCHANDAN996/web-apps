51. In how many ways can 6 different books be arranged on a shelf?
(a) 120 (b) 720 (c) 360 (d) 5040
Answer: (b)
Solution: The tempting wrong option 120 is 5!, which students pick when they mistakenly count only 5 books. Arranging 6 distinct books means 6! = 6 × 5 × 4 × 3 × 2 × 1 = 720, so the answer is 720.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Mathematics

52. How many 3-digit numbers can be formed using the digits 1, 2, 3, 4, 5 without repetition?
(a) 60 (b) 125 (c) 30 (d) 20
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap option 125 comes from allowing repetition (5 × 5 × 5), but repetition is not allowed. The number of arrangements is ⁵P₃ = 5 × 4 × 3 = 60, so the answer is 60.

53. In how many ways can the letters of the word "APPLE" be arranged?
(a) 120 (b) 24 (c) 30 (d) 60
Answer: (d)
Solution: The tempting wrong option 120 is 5!, ignoring that the letter P repeats twice. Total arrangements = 5!/2! = 120/2 = 60, so the answer is 60.

54. How many ways are there to select 3 students from a group of 10 for a committee?
(a) 240 (b) 720 (c) 30 (d) 120
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap option 720 is ¹⁰P₃, which counts order, but a committee has no order. Selections = ¹⁰C₃ = (10 × 9 × 8)/(3 × 2 × 1) = 120, so the answer is 120.

55. In how many ways can 5 boys and 3 girls be seated in a row so that all girls sit together?
(a) 720 (b) 4320 (c) 5760 (d) 40320
Answer: (b)
Solution: The tempting wrong option 40320 is 8!, ignoring the together condition. Treat the 3 girls as one block: 6 units arrange in 6! = 720 ways, and girls arrange within the block in 3! = 6 ways, giving 720 × 6 = 4320, so the answer is 4320.

56. How many diagonals does a polygon with 10 sides have?
(a) 45 (b) 90 (c) 35 (d) 40
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap option 45 is ¹⁰C₂, which counts all line segments including the 10 sides. Diagonals = ¹⁰C₂ − 10 = 45 − 10 = 35, so the answer is 35.

60. In how many ways can 7 people be seated around a circular table?
(a) 5040 (b) 120 (c) 360 (d) 720
Answer: (d)
Solution: The tempting wrong option 5040 is 7!, which treats the arrangement as linear. In a circle, rotations are identical, so arrangements = (7 − 1)! = 6! = 720, so the answer is 720.

61. How many numbers between 100 and 1000 have all distinct digits?
(a) 900 (b) 729 (c) 648 (d) 504
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap option 900 counts all 3-digit numbers, ignoring the distinct-digit condition. Hundreds digit: 9 choices (1–9), tens digit: 9 choices (0 plus remaining), units digit: 8 choices, giving 9 × 9 × 8 = 648, so the answer is 648.

62. In how many ways can a team of 11 players be chosen from 15 players?
(a) 3003 (b) 2730 (c) 1365 (d) 455
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap option 3003 is ¹⁵C₁₀ or ¹⁵C₅, choosing the wrong group size. Ways = ¹⁵C₁₁ = ¹⁵C₄ = (15 × 14 × 13 × 12)/(4 × 3 × 2 × 1) = 1365, so the answer is 1365.

68. How many 5-digit numbers divisible by 2 can be formed using digits 0, 1, 2, 3, 4 without repetition?
(a) 96 (b) 60 (c) 48 (d) 120
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap option 96 counts all valid 5-digit numbers, ignoring divisibility by 2. Case 1: units digit 0 gives 4! = 24 numbers. Case 2: units digit 2 or 4 gives 2 × 3 × 3 × 2 × 1 = 36 numbers. Total = 24 + 36 = 60, so the answer is 60.

71. In how many ways can 8 people sit in a row if two particular persons must not sit together?
(a) 30240 (b) 40320 (c) 10080 (d) 20160
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap option 10080 is only the together case (2 × 7!), subtracted from nothing. Total arrangements = 8! = 40320, together arrangements = 2 × 7! = 10080, so required = 40320 − 10080 = 30240, so the answer is 30240.

74. In how many ways can 6 men and 6 women sit around a round table so that no two women sit together?
(a) 14400 (b) 518400 (c) 86400 (d) 720
Answer: (c)
Solution: The tempting wrong option 518400 is 6! × 6!, which wrongly uses linear arrangement for the men. Seat 6 men in a circle in (6 − 1)! = 120 ways, creating 6 gaps; place 6 women in the gaps in 6! = 720 ways. Total = 120 × 720 = 86400, so the answer is 86400.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Mathematics

75. How many different signals can be made by hoisting 5 flags of different colours, using any number of them at a time?
(a) 325 (b) 120 (c) 300 (d) 720
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap option 120 is 5!, counting only signals using all 5 flags. Signals using 1, 2, 3, 4 or 5 flags = ⁵P₁ + ⁵P₂ + ⁵P₃ + ⁵P₄ + ⁵P₅ = 5 + 20 + 60 + 120 + 120 = 325, so the answer is 325.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Mathematics
