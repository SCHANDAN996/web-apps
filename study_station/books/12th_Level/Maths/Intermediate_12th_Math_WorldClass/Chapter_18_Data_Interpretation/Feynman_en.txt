# 🧒 Data Interpretation — Explained Like You're 12

## The Hardest Concept: Percentage Change & Comparison

Most students don't fail DI because of big calculations. They fail because of one sneaky idea: **"percentage of WHAT?"** Let me tell you a story.

---

## 🍕 The Pizza Shop Story

Imagine two pizza shops on your street.

- **Shop A** sold 10 pizzas last month. This month it sold 20 pizzas.
- **Shop B** sold 200 pizzas last month. This month it sold 220 pizzas.

Now I ask you: **Which shop grew more?**

Most kids shout: "Shop B! It sold 20 more pizzas, Shop A only sold 10 more!"

But wait. Look again:

- Shop A: 10 → 20. It **doubled**! That's a **100% increase**.
- Shop B: 200 → 220. That's only a **10% increase**.

Shop A grew **10 times faster** than Shop B — even though its numbers look tiny!

**This is the whole secret of Data Interpretation:** the exam rarely asks just "what is the number?" It usually asks "what is the number *compared to* something?" A big number can hide a small change, and a small number can hide a huge change.

---

## 🪜 The Staircase Trick (Never Forget This!)

Think of percentage change like climbing stairs:

> **"Change ÷ Start × 100 — always divide by where you STARTED, not where you ENDED."**

- You climbed from step 10 to step 20 → you climbed 10 steps **out of your starting 10** → 100%.
- You climbed from step 200 to step 220 → you climbed 20 steps **out of your starting 200** → 10%.

The trap in every exam: they make you divide by the wrong number. Remember the chant:

> 🗣️ **"New minus Old, over Old, times 100 — Old is gold!"**

---

## 📊 Why This Matters in Every DI Chart

Whether it's a bar graph of company profits, a pie chart of family expenses, or a table of cricket scores — the examiner is always testing the same three things:

1. **Can you read the right number?** (Careful reading)
2. **Can you compare it correctly?** (Percentage of WHAT?)
3. **Can you do it fast?** (Approximation — 498 is basically 500!)

Master the pizza-shop idea, and a large share of DI questions start looking like the same question wearing different clothes.

---

## ❓ Now You Try!

Ravi's pocket money went from ₹50 to ₹75. His sister's went from ₹200 to ₹240.

**Whose pocket money increased by a bigger percentage?**

*(Hint: Don't look at the rupees. Climb the staircase — divide by where each one STARTED!)*
