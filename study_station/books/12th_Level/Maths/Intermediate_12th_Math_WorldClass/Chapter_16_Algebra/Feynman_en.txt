# The Feynman Explanation: Algebra

## The Hardest Idea in Algebra — "Letters Doing Math"

Ask any student what scares them about Algebra, and the answer is almost always the same: *"Why are there LETTERS in my MATH?"* Numbers were fine. 5 + 3 = 8. Clean. Done. Then suddenly a teacher writes **x + 3 = 8** and the brain freezes. A letter? In arithmetic? That is the single hardest concept in this whole chapter — and today we kill that fear forever.

## The Story of the Locked Lunchbox

Imagine you are 12 years old. Your friend Rohan brings a locked lunchbox to school. He grins and says:

> "Whatever number of laddoos is inside this box, if you add 3 more, the total becomes 8."

You cannot see inside. You cannot open it. But you *can* think.

So you ask yourself: "Some mystery number... plus 3... equals 8. What could it be?" You try 4 — no, 4 + 3 = 7. You try 6 — no, that's 9. You try 5 — **5 + 3 = 8. Got it!** The box has 5 laddoos.

Congratulations. You just solved **x + 3 = 8**. That is ALL algebra is.

**The letter x is not math. It is a locked lunchbox.** It is a placeholder — a name tag stuck on a number that is hiding from you. Your entire job in algebra is one thing: *find the number hiding behind the letter.*

## Why Do We Even Need Letters?

Fair question. Why not just write "the mystery number"? Because mathematicians are lazy (in a good way). Writing "mystery number" a hundred times is exhausting. Writing **x** takes half a second. That's it. There is no deeper magic. x, y, a, b — they are just nicknames for numbers we haven't found yet.

And when a problem says **2x + 3 = 11**, read it like a sentence in the lunchbox story:

> "Double the laddoos in the box, add 3 more, and you get 11."

Now work backwards, like unwrapping a gift. The last thing that happened was "+3", so undo it first: 11 − 3 = 8. Before that, the number was doubled, so undo that: 8 ÷ 2 = 4. The box had **4 laddoos**. Check: 2 × 4 + 3 = 11. ✔

## The Trick You Will Never Forget: **"UNDO in REVERSE"** 🎁

Think of an equation as a gift that was wrapped in layers. To reach the gift (x), you **unwrap the layers in reverse order** — the last thing done to x is the first thing you undo.

- x was **doubled**, then **3 was added** → first **subtract 3**, then **divide by 2**.
- Whatever you do to one side of "=", do to the other side. The "=" sign is a balance scale — keep both pans equal, always.

Memory hook: **"Last In, First Out — like removing your shoes before your socks would be silly!"** You put socks on first, shoes on last. When undressing, shoes come off first. Same with equations: undo the last operation first.

## One More Thing — Letters Can Be Anything

Sometimes the letter isn't hiding one fixed number. In **y = 2x**, y changes whenever x changes — like the price of samosas: 1 samosa = ₹10, 2 samosas = ₹20. The letter is now a *machine*: feed it x, it gives you y. But the core idea never changes — **letters stand for numbers**. Always. Nothing more.
