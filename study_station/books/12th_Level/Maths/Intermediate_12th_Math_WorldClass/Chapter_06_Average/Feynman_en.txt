# The Hardest Idea in Average — Explained Like You're 12

## The Story of the Cricket Team and the "Fair Share"

Imagine you and 4 friends — that's 5 of you — go to a birthday party. The host brings out **20 samosas** and says, "Share them equally!"

You don't even need to think hard. 20 ÷ 5 = **4 samosas each**.

That number — 4 — is the **average**. That's it. That's the whole secret.

**Average is just "fair share."** If everything were divided perfectly equally, how much would each person get? That's the average.

## But Here's Where It Gets Tricky (The Hardest Part!)

Now imagine this: your friend Monty eats **8 samosas** (he's greedy!). That means someone else must get *fewer* than 4 — because the total is still only 20.

This is the idea that confuses everyone in exams:

> **If one value goes UP by some amount, the others must go DOWN by the same total amount — because the total never changes unless you add or remove something.**

The average is like the **water level in a tank**. If you scoop water from one corner and dump it in another, the level stays the same. The level only rises if you *pour in new water*.

## A Real Exam-Style Moment

Suppose the average age of 10 players is 20 years. Total age = 10 × 20 = **200 years**.

Now a new player joins, and the average becomes 21. New total = 11 × 21 = **231 years**.

So the new player's age = 231 − 200 = **31 years**.

See what we did? We never touched "average" formulas. We just asked: *what's the total before, what's the total after, and where did the extra come from?*

## The Trick You'll Never Forget 🎯

> **"Average is a LIAR — always ask for the TOTAL!"**

Whenever a problem says "average," your brain should immediately shout: **"Give me the total!"**

- Average × Number of items = **Total**
- New person joins? → **New total − Old total = New person's value**
- Someone leaves? → **Old total − New total = Leaver's value**
- Average goes up by 2 for 10 people? → Total went up by **2 × 10 = 20**

Remember it like this: **A × N = T** — "ANT" 🐜
The little ant carries the total on its back. Average and Number are its two legs. If you know any two, the ant gives you the third!

## Why This Works Everywhere

Replacement problems, wrong-value problems, "average increases when teacher walks in" problems — they ALL melt down to one question:

**"What happened to the total?"**

Master the total, and the average can never trick you.

## Now You Try! 🤔

The average weight of 8 friends is 50 kg. One friend of 60 kg leaves the group. Without using any formula — just thinking about the *total* — can you find the new average of the remaining 7 friends?

*(Hint: What was the old total? How much weight walked out the door? What's left, and among how many?)*
