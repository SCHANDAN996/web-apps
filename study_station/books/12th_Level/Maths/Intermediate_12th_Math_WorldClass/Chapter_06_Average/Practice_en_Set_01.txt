1. Find the average of the first 10 natural numbers.
(a) 5 (b) 5.5 (c) 6 (d) 4.5
Answer: (b)
Solution: The sum of the first 10 natural numbers is 10 × 11 ÷ 2 = 55. Dividing by 10 gives 5.5. Choosing 5 is a trap for those who take the middle term of 1 to 9 instead of 1 to 10, so the answer is 5.5.

2. The average of 4, 8, 12, 16 and 20 is:
(a) 10 (b) 12 (c) 11 (d) 14
Answer: (b)
Solution: The numbers are equally spaced, so the average equals the middle term, which is 12. Adding all and dividing by 5 also gives 60 ÷ 5 = 12. Picking 10 comes from wrongly dividing by 6, so the answer is 12.
Source: NCERT Class 7 Mathematics

3. The average of 5 numbers is 20. What is their sum?
(a) 4 (b) 25 (c) 80 (d) 100
Answer: (d)
Solution: Sum = average × number of terms = 20 × 5 = 100. Dividing 20 by 5 gives 4, which is the reverse operation trap, so the answer is 100.
Source: NCERT Class 7 Mathematics

4. Find the average of the first 5 even numbers.
(a) 6 (b) 5 (c) 7 (d) 8
Answer: (a)
Solution: The first 5 even numbers are 2, 4, 6, 8, 10, whose sum is 30 and average is 30 ÷ 5 = 6. A quick rule: average of first n even numbers is n + 1, so the answer is 6.
Source: NCERT Class 7 Mathematics

5. Find the average of the first 5 odd numbers.
(a) 4 (b) 5 (c) 6 (d) 7
Answer: (b)
Solution: The first 5 odd numbers are 1, 3, 5, 7, 9, whose sum is 25 and average is 25 ÷ 5 = 5. The average of the first n odd numbers is always n, so the answer is 5.

6. The average of 10, 20 and 30 is:
(a) 15 (b) 20 (c) 25 (d) 60
Answer: (b)
Solution: Sum = 10 + 20 + 30 = 60, and 60 ÷ 3 = 20. Choosing 60 is the trap of forgetting to divide the sum by the count, so the answer is 20.

7. The average of two numbers is 15. If one number is 10, find the other.
(a) 20 (b) 15 (c) 25 (d) 30
Answer: (a)
Solution: Sum of the two numbers = 15 × 2 = 30, so the other number = 30 − 10 = 20. Taking 15 as the other number is the trap of confusing average with the unknown, so the answer is 20.

8. The average of 6 numbers is 8. If one number, 12, is removed, the average of the remaining numbers is:
(a) 6.8 (b) 7 (c) 7.5 (d) 7.2
Answer: (d)
Solution: Total sum = 6 × 8 = 48. After removing 12, the sum is 36 over 5 numbers, giving 36 ÷ 5 = 7.2. Dividing 36 by 6 gives 6, which wrongly keeps the old count, so the answer is 7.2.

10. The average of 8 observations is 12. The total of all observations is:
(a) 20 (b) 84 (c) 96 (d) 108

12. Find the average of the first 20 natural numbers.
(a) 10 (b) 11 (c) 10.5 (d) 11.5
Answer: (c)
Solution: Sum = 20 × 21 ÷ 2 = 210, and average = 210 ÷ 20 = 10.5. For the first n natural numbers, the average is (n + 1) ÷ 2 = 21 ÷ 2 = 10.5, so the answer is 10.5.

13. The average of 3, 7, 9 and x is 7. Find x.
(a) 7 (b) 8 (c) 10 (d) 9
Answer: (d)
Solution: Total = 7 × 4 = 28. The known numbers add to 3 + 7 + 9 = 19, so x = 28 − 19 = 9. Taking 7 as x is the trap of assuming the missing value equals the average, so the answer is 9.

14. The average of 5 consecutive numbers is 21. The smallest number is:

Answer: (a)
Solution: For 5 consecutive numbers, the average is the middle (third) number, so the numbers are 19, 20, 21, 22, 23. The smallest is 19. Choosing 21 is the trap of treating the average itself as the smallest, so the answer is 19.

15. The average of 7 consecutive numbers is 33. The largest number is:
(a) 35 (b) 38 (c) 37 (d) 36
Answer: (d)
Solution: The average of 7 consecutive numbers is the fourth (middle) number, 33. The largest is 3 steps ahead: 33 + 3 = 36. Adding 7 to 33 gives 40, a wrong-step trap, so the answer is 36.

16. The average of 12 numbers is 15. The average of the first 6 of these numbers is 10. The average of the last 6 numbers is:
(a) 15 (b) 18 (c) 20 (d) 25
Answer: (c)
Solution: Total sum = 12 × 15 = 180. Sum of first 6 = 6 × 10 = 60, so sum of last 6 = 120 and their average = 120 ÷ 6 = 20. Taking 15 is the trap of assuming both halves share the overall average, so the answer is 20.
Source: PYQ-style

17. The average weight of 10 students is 40 kg. If the teacher's weight is included, the average becomes 42 kg. The teacher's weight is:
(a) 52 kg (b) 58 kg (c) 62 kg (d) 72 kg
Answer: (c)
Solution: Students' total = 10 × 40 = 400 kg. New total = 11 × 42 = 462 kg, so teacher's weight = 462 − 400 = 62 kg. Choosing 42 is the trap of thinking the teacher weighs the new average, so the answer is 62 kg.
Source: PYQ-style

18. The average of 5 numbers is 27. If one number is excluded, the average becomes 25. The excluded number is:
(a) 30 (b) 32 (c) 40 (d) 35
Answer: (d)
Solution: Original sum = 5 × 27 = 135. New sum = 4 × 25 = 100, so the excluded number = 135 − 100 = 35. Taking the difference of averages, 2, is meaningless here, so the answer is 35.

19. The average of 11 results is 50. If the average of the first 6 results is 49 and that of the last 6 is 52, the sixth result is:
(a) 46 (b) 48 (c) 50 (d) 56
Answer: (d)
Solution: Sum of first 6 = 294 and sum of last 6 = 312, totalling 606, but the sixth result is counted twice. Total of all 11 = 550, so the sixth result = 606 − 550 = 56. Forgetting the double count gives 606 − 550 wrongly handled, so the answer is 56.

20. The average of 20 numbers is zero. At most how many of them can be greater than zero?
(a) 0 (b) 1 (c) 19 (d) 10
Answer: (c)
Solution: If the average is zero, the total sum is zero, so positive values must be balanced by negative ones. Hence at least one number must be negative (or zero), meaning at most 19 can be positive. Choosing 20 is the trap of ignoring the need for balance, so the answer is 19.

22. The average age of a class of 30 students is 14 years. If the teacher's age is included, the average increases by 1 year. The teacher's age is:
(a) 31 years (b) 40 years (c) 45 years (d) 50 years
Answer: (c)
Solution: Students' total age = 30 × 14 = 420. New average = 15 over 31 people, giving a total of 465. Teacher's age = 465 − 420 = 45 years. A shortcut: teacher = old average + (new count × increase) = 14 + 31 = 45, so the answer is 45 years.
Source: PYQ-style

23. The average of 8 numbers is 20. The average of the first two is 15.5 and that of the next three is 21.33 (approximately). If the sixth number is 4 less than the seventh and 7 less than the eighth, the eighth number is:
(a) 25 (b) 22 (c) 18 (d) 26
Answer: (a)
Solution: Total = 160. First two sum to 31, next three sum to 64, leaving 65 for the last three. Let the sixth be x; then x + (x + 4) + (x + 7) = 65, so 3x = 54 and x = 18. The eighth number = 18 + 7 = 25. Reporting 18 is the trap of answering the sixth number instead of the eighth, so the answer is 25.
Source: PYQ-style

24. A batsman makes a score of 87 runs in the 17th innings and thus increases his average by 3. His average after the 17th innings is:
(a) 36 (b) 39 (c) 42 (d) 45
Answer: (b)
Solution: Let the new average be x. Then 17x = 16(x − 3) + 87, giving 17x = 16x − 48 + 87, so x = 39. Choosing 36 is the trap of reporting the old average instead of the new one, so the answer is 39.
Source: PYQ-style

25. The average weight of A, B and C is 45 kg. If the average weight of A and B is 40 kg and that of B and C is 43 kg, then the weight of B is:
(a) 31 kg (b) 32 kg (c) 35 kg (d) 38 kg
Answer: (a)
Solution: A + B + C = 135, A + B = 80, and B + C = 86. Adding the pairs: A + 2B + C = 166, so B = 166 − 135 = 31 kg. Choosing 35 comes from wrongly halving the difference, so the answer is 31 kg.
Source: PYQ-style
