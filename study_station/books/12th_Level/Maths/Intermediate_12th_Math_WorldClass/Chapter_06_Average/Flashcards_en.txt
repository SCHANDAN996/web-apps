Card 1
Front: What is the basic formula for average?
Back: Average = Sum of all observations ÷ Number of observations.

Card 2
Front: If the average of n numbers is A, what is their total sum?
Back: Sum = n × A. (Average × count gives the total — the most-used trick in exams.)

Card 3
Front: What is the average of the first n natural numbers?
Back: (n + 1)/2.

Card 4
Front: What is the average of the first n even numbers?
Back: n + 1.

Card 5
Front: What is the average of the first n odd numbers?
Back: n.

Card 6
Front: What is the average of consecutive numbers (an arithmetic sequence)?
Back: (First term + Last term) ÷ 2 — the middle value itself.

Card 7
Front: If every number in a group is increased by k, what happens to the average?
Back: The average also increases by exactly k. (Same for decrease, ×k, or ÷k.)

Card 8
Front: A person joins a group and the average rises. Was the newcomer above or below the old average?
Back: Above the old average. A new member pulls the average toward their own value.

Card 9
Front: What is the mnemonic trick to remember how a new member changes the average?
Back: "Heavy friend lifts the boat" — anyone above average pulls the average up; anyone below pulls it down.

Card 10
Front: Quick trick: average of 11, 13, 15, 17, 19 — without adding?
Back: The middle term, 15. For an odd count of equally spaced numbers, average = middle term.

Card 11
Front: What is the "assumed average" (deviation) method?
Back: Pick a round number near the values, average only the deviations, then add it back. Saves time on big numbers.

Card 12
Front: What is the mnemonic for the assumed-average method?
Back: "Anchor and Adjust" — fix an anchor number, adjust by the average of the gaps.

Card 13
Front: If one number is replaced by a number x greater, how does the total sum change?
Back: The sum increases by x, so the average rises by x ÷ n.

Card 14
Front: Average of two groups: 10 students avg 60, 20 students avg 70. Combined average?
Back: (10×60 + 20×70) ÷ 30 = 2000 ÷ 30 = 66.67. Weighted average — never just average the averages.

Card 15
Front: Why can't you simply average two group averages to get the combined average?
Back: Because groups may have different sizes; the bigger group pulls the result more (weighted average).

Card 16
Front: A batsman's average rises after a big innings. How do you find the runs he scored?
Back: New total − old total: (new avg × new innings) − (old avg × old innings).

Card 17
Front: What is the average speed for equal distances covered at speeds a and b?
Back: Harmonic mean: 2ab ÷ (a + b) — not the simple average.

Card 18
Front: What is the average speed when equal *times* are travelled at speeds a and b?
Back: Simple average: (a + b) ÷ 2.

Card 19
Front: What is the mnemonic to choose between the two average-speed formulas?
Back: "Distance Doubles, Time Adds" — equal distance → 2ab/(a+b); equal time → (a+b)/2.

Card 20
Front: The average of 5 numbers is 20. If one number is removed, the average becomes 18. Which number was removed?
Back: 5×20 − 4×18 = 100 − 72 = 28.
