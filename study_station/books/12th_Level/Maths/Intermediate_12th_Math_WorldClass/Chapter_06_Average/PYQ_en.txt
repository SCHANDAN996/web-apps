# 📊 PYQ Analysis — Average (Intermediate Level)

## How Exams Test This Chapter

Average is one of the most reliable scoring areas in SSC CGL, IBPS PO, RRB NTPC and CAT. Examiners rarely ask a plain "find the average" question at this level — they wrap the concept inside a story. The recurring patterns are:

- **Replacement problems** — a person leaves a group, a new one joins, and the average rises or falls. This is the single most repeated pattern.
- **Weighted average** — two groups (boys/girls, two classes, two purchases) with different averages are merged.
- **Consecutive numbers** — average of consecutive even/odd/natural numbers, where the average equals the middle term.
- **Cricket/runs problems** — a batsman's average changes after one innings; find runs scored.
- **Age problems in disguise** — average age of a family now, after some years, or when a baby is born.
- **"One number was misread" problems** — a wrong value was included; correct the average.
- **Speed problems** — average speed for equal distances (harmonic mean trap).

## The Examiner's Traps (Psychological Hooks)

1. **The "average of averages" trap** — adding two group averages and dividing by 2. This works only when both groups are equal in size. Examiners deliberately give unequal groups.
2. **The arithmetic-mean speed trap** — for equal distances, average speed is NOT (x+y)/2; it is 2xy/(x+y). The wrong option is always present.
3. **The sign confusion trap** — "average increases by 2 when a person is replaced" — students forget whether to add or subtract (n × change).
4. **The middle-term illusion** — for consecutive numbers the average is the middle term only when the count is odd; for even counts it is the mean of the two middle terms.
5. **The "included/excluded" wording trap** — "average of 10 results is 20; one result 25 is excluded" — students subtract wrongly or forget the count drops to 9.
6. **The negative-change trap** — when a baby is born or a younger person joins, the total can behave counter-intuitively; read whether the question asks for the new member's age or the new average.

## Top 10 High-Yield Questions (with Solutions)

**Q1.** The average of 11 numbers is 50. If the average of the first six is 49 and that of the last six is 52, find the sixth number.
(a) 56 (b) 46 (c) 50 (d) 60
**Answer:** (a)
**Solution:** Sum of all 11 = 550. First six = 294, last six = 312. The sixth number is counted twice: 294 + 312 − 550 = 56.
**Hook:** Overlapping groups — students forget the middle number is double-counted.
**Source:** PYQ-style (SSC pattern)

**Q2.** The average age of 30 students is 12 years. When the teacher's age is included, the average becomes 13 years. Find the teacher's age.
(a) 39 (b) 43 (c) 31 (d) 42
**Answer:** (b)
**Solution:** New total = 31 × 13 = 403; old total = 30 × 12 = 360. Teacher = 403 − 360 = 43. Shortcut: 12 + 31 × 1 = 43.
**Hook:** The new member carries the old average PLUS the total increase (n × rise).
**Source:** PYQ-style (SSC/NTPC pattern)

**Q3.** A batsman has an average of 40 runs after 20 innings. How many runs must he score in the 21st innings to raise his average to 41?
(a) 61 (b) 41 (c) 60 (d) 51
**Answer:** (a)
**Solution:** Required = 21 × 41 − 20 × 40 = 861 − 800 = 61. Shortcut: 41 + 20 × 1 = 61.
**Hook:** Students answer 41, forgetting he must also "gift" 1 run to each of the previous 20 innings.
**Source:** PYQ-style (cricket pattern)

**Q4.** The average weight of 8 persons increases by 2.5 kg when a new person replaces one weighing 65 kg. Find the new person's weight.
(a) 85 kg (b) 76 kg (c) 80 kg (d) 90 kg
**Answer:** (a)
**Solution:** Increase in total = 8 × 2.5 = 20 kg. New person = 65 + 20 = 85 kg.
**Hook:** Sign trap — average *increases*, so the newcomer is *heavier*; many subtract.
**Source:** PYQ-style (replacement pattern)

**Q5.** The average of five consecutive even numbers is 24. Find the largest number.
(a) 28 (b) 26 (c) 30 (d) 32
**Answer:** (a)
**Solution:** Average = middle (3rd) term = 24. Numbers: 20, 22, 24, 26, 28. Largest = 28.
**Hook:** Students compute 24 + 5 or 24 + 4 instead of 24 + 2×2.
**Source:** PYQ-style (consecutive numbers)

**Q6.** The average of 20 numbers is 15. Later it was found that one number, 36, was wrongly read as 26. Find the correct average.
(a) 15.5 (b) 16 (c) 14.5 (d) 15
**Answer:** (a)
**Solution:** Correction = +10 spread over 20 numbers = +0.5. Correct average = 15.5.
**Hook:** Students add 10 directly to the average instead of dividing by the count.
**Source:** PYQ-style (misread pattern)

**Q7.** The average score of boys in a class is 70 and that of girls is 73. The average of the whole class is 71.8. Find the ratio of boys to girls.
(a) 2 : 3 (b) 3 : 2 (c) 4 : 5 (d) 3 : 5
**Answer:** (a)
**Solution:** Alligation: boys : girls = (73 − 71.8) : (71.8 − 70) = 1.2 : 1.8 = 2 : 3.
**Hook:** The ratio is inverted — the group with the *lower* average gets the *farther* difference.
**Source:** PYQ-style (weighted average / alligation)

**Q8.** A man travels 60 km at 30 km/h and returns the same distance at 20 km/h. Find his average speed for the whole journey.
(a) 25 km/h (b) 24 km/h (c) 26 km/h (d) 22 km/h
**Answer:** (b)
**Solution:** Equal distances ⇒ average speed = 2xy/(x+y) = (2×30×20)/50 = 24 km/h.
**Hook:** The classic trap — option (a) 25 is the arithmetic mean, placed deliberately.
**Source:** PYQ-style (average speed)

**Q9.** Three years ago, the average age of a family of 5 members was 17 years. A baby having been born, the average age of the family is the same today. Find the age of the baby.
(a) 1 year (b) 2 years (c) 3 years (d) 1.5 years
**Answer:** (b)
**Solution:** Three years ago total = 85. Today (without baby) = 85 + 15 = 100, average of 5 = 20. But average stays 17 with 6 members ⇒ total = 102. Baby = 102 − 100 = 2 years.
**Hook:** Two time-shifts at once — students forget to age all 5 members by 3 years.
**Source:** PYQ-style (age + new member)

**Q10.** The average of 7 consecutive numbers is 20. If the next two numbers are also included, the average becomes:
(a) 21 (b) 22 (c) 20.5 (d) 21.5
**Answer:** (a)
**Solution:** Numbers are 17–23 (average 20). Adding 24 and 25 gives 17–25, nine consecutive numbers; average = middle term = 21.
**Hook:** Students average 20 with 24.5 (the mean of the two new numbers) incorrectly instead of re-finding the middle term.
**Source:** PYQ-style (consecutive extension)

## Key Takeaways for the Exam Hall

- Master the **deviation method**: new member's value = old average ± (new count × change). It solves Q2, Q3, Q4-type questions in under 20 seconds.
- For any "two groups merged" question, go straight to **alligation** — never average the averages.
- For equal-distance speed, write 2xy/(x+y) before even reading the options.
- Read the last line twice: "excluded", "included", "replaced", "misread" — the whole question turns on that one word.
