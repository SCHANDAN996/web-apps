# 🧒 Feynman Explanation: Profit & Loss — The Trickiest Idea, Made Childishly Simple

## The Hardest Concept: "Profit % is always on COST PRICE, not Selling Price"

Ask any student why they lose marks in Profit & Loss, and the answer is almost always the same: they calculated the percentage on the wrong number. Let's fix that forever — with a story.

---

## 🍋 The Story of Ravi's Lemonade Stand

Ravi is 12 years old. One summer, he decides to sell lemonade.

He spends **₹100** on lemons, sugar, and cups. This ₹100 is his **Cost Price (CP)** — the money that went *out* of his pocket.

He sells all the lemonade for **₹130**. This is his **Selling Price (SP)** — the money that came *into* his pocket.

His profit = ₹130 − ₹100 = **₹30**. Easy.

Now comes the tricky part. Ravi's friend Meena asks: *"What percent profit did you make?"*

Ravi thinks: "₹30 out of ₹130… that's about 23%!"

**Wrong!** And here is why —

## 🔑 The Golden Question: "Compared to WHAT?"

A percentage always answers: *"Compared to what?"*

When we say "profit percent," we are asking: **"For every rupee I INVESTED, how much did I gain?"**

Ravi invested ₹100 (his cost). He gained ₹30. So:

$$\text{Profit \%} = \frac{30}{100} \times 100 = 30\%$$

The **Cost Price** is the *seed* you planted. The profit is the *fruit* that grew. You always measure the fruit against the seed — never against the whole tree!

## 🪄 The Trick You'll Never Forget: **"Dad's Wallet Rule"**

Think of it like this:

> **CP is DAD's wallet. SP is YOUR wallet.**
>
> When someone asks "how much did you earn?", your dad asks: *"Compared to the money I GAVE you?"* — not compared to what you have now.

So: **Profit % and Loss % are ALWAYS measured on CP (Dad's wallet).** No exceptions. Ever.

**Memory hook:** *"Percentage ki Maa hai CP"* — the mother of every percentage in this chapter is the Cost Price.

## ⚠️ The One Exception Trap (Marked Price)

There is ONE place where the percentage is NOT on CP: **Discount**. Discount is always on the **Marked Price (MP)** — the price tag on the item.

So remember the family:
- **Profit/Loss %** → on **CP** (Dad's wallet)
- **Discount %** → on **MP** (the price tag)

## 🧠 Quick Check

A shopkeeper buys a pen for ₹40 and sells it for ₹50. His profit % is:

(a) 20%  (b) 25%  (c) 10%  (d) 50%

*Think before you answer — whose wallet are you comparing against?*

**Answer: (b) 25%** — Profit = ₹10, and 10/40 × 100 = 25%. (Option (a) is the trap: 10/50 = 20% uses the selling price — that's YOUR wallet, not Dad's!)
