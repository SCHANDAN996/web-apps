Card 1
Front: What is the single most important base for calculating profit % and loss %?
Back: Always the Cost Price (CP). Profit % = (Profit/CP) × 100; Loss % = (Loss/CP) × 100.

Card 2
Front: If SP = ₹600 and profit is 20%, what is the CP?
Back: CP = 600 × 100/120 = ₹500. Rule: divide SP by (100 + profit%) and multiply by 100.

Card 3
Front: A shopkeeper sells at a 20% loss. If SP = ₹480, find CP.
Back: CP = 480 × 100/80 = ₹600. Rule: divide SP by (100 − loss%) and multiply by 100.

Card 4
Front: What is the mnemonic to remember that % is always on CP?
Back: "CP is the Boss" — Cost Price Boss, Percentage usi par. Profit%, Loss%, everything bows to CP.

Card 5
Front: Two items sold at the same SP, one at x% profit and one at x% loss — overall result?
Back: Always a loss = x²/100 %. E.g., x = 10 → 1% overall loss. Mnemonic: "Same price, opposite signs — loss always shines."

Card 6
Front: What is the relation between MP, Discount and SP?
Back: SP = MP × (100 − Discount%)/100. Discount is always calculated on Marked Price, never on CP.

Card 7
Front: A trader marks goods 40% above CP and gives 10% discount. Profit %?
Back: Profit = 40 − 10 − (40×10)/100 = 26%. Use the successive formula: a + b + ab/100 (discount is negative).

Card 8
Front: What is the mnemonic for the successive change formula?
Back: "A-B-AB" — a + b + ab/100. Add the two, then add their product over 100. Discount enters with a minus sign.

Card 9
Front: Selling price is 4/5 of CP. What is the loss %?
Back: Loss = 1/5 of CP = 20%. When SP = (a/b) × CP with a < b, loss% = (b−a)/b × 100.

Card 10
Front: A dishonest dealer uses 900 g instead of 1 kg. What is his profit %?
Back: Profit % = (Error/True weight sold) × 100 = 100/900 × 100 = 11.11%. Mnemonic: "Chori ka maal — error upon what customer gets."

Card 11
Front: If the CP of x articles equals the SP of y articles (x > y), what is profit %?
Back: Profit % = (x − y)/y × 100. E.g., CP of 20 = SP of 16 → profit = 4/16 = 25%.

Card 12
Front: What is the difference between Marked Price and Selling Price?
Back: MP is the price tag printed on the item; SP is what the customer actually pays after discount. SP = MP − Discount.

Card 13
Front: A man buys at 10% discount and sells at the marked price. Profit %?
Back: Profit % = 10/90 × 100 = 11.11%. Rule: profit% = d/(100−d) × 100 when buying at d% discount and selling at MP.

Card 14
Front: To gain 25% after allowing a 20% discount, how much above CP should the MP be?
Back: MP = CP × 125/80 = 1.5625 × CP, i.e., 56.25% above CP. Mnemonic: "Ulta chalo" — reverse the two multipliers.

Card 15
Front: What is the mnemonic for the false-weight profit formula?
Back: "Ghatiya tol, upar error" — Profit% = Error/(True weight − Error) × 100. The error sits on top, what the customer actually receives sits below.

Card 16
Front: If profit% equals loss% numerically on two different items, can the overall deal ever be a profit?
Back: No. With equal SPs it is always a loss of x²/100%. Profit is impossible in this setup.

Card 17
Front: CP : SP = 5 : 6. What is the profit %?
Back: Profit = 1 part on CP of 5 parts → 1/5 × 100 = 20%.

Card 18
Front: A sells to B at 20% profit, B sells to C at 25% profit. C pays ₹900. What did A pay?
Back: A's CP = 900 × 100/125 × 100/120 = ₹600. Chain rule: divide by each (100 + gain%) step in reverse.

Card 19
Front: What is "break-even" in profit & loss terms?
Back: The point where SP = CP — no profit, no loss. Profit% = 0.

Card 20
Front: Selling an article at ₹X gives a% profit; at ₹Y gives b% profit (X > Y). How to find CP quickly?
Back: CP = (bX − aY)/(b − a). Or use: difference in SP ÷ difference in % = value of 1% of CP.
