1. A shopkeeper buys a pen for ₹50 and sells it for ₹65. What is his profit?
(a) ₹15 (b) ₹10 (c) ₹20 (d) ₹25
Answer: (a)
Solution: Profit = Selling Price − Cost Price = 65 − 50 = ₹15. The tempting wrong option ₹20 comes from misreading or wrongly adding the prices, so the answer is ₹15.
Source: NCERT Class 7 Mathematics

2. An article is bought for ₹200 and sold for ₹180. What is the loss?
(a) ₹10 (b) ₹15 (c) ₹30 (d) ₹20
Answer: (d)
Solution: Loss = Cost Price − Selling Price = 200 − 180 = ₹20. The trap ₹30 comes from a careless subtraction, so the answer is ₹20.

3. Cost price of a book is ₹100 and it is sold at a profit of 20%. What is the selling price?
(a) ₹110 (b) ₹115 (c) ₹120 (d) ₹125
Answer: (c)
Solution: Profit = 20% of 100 = ₹20, so SP = 100 + 20 = ₹120. The option ₹110 comes from taking 10% instead of 20%, so the answer is ₹120.

4. An article is sold for ₹90 at a loss of 10%. What is the cost price?
(a) ₹99 (b) ₹110 (c) ₹100 (d) ₹81
Answer: (c)
Solution: SP = 90% of CP, so CP = 90 × 100/90 = ₹100. The trap ₹99 comes from adding 10% of 90 to 90, which is wrong because the loss is on CP, not SP, so the answer is ₹100.

21. A shopkeeper buys an article for ₹600 and sells it at a profit of 20% after giving a discount of 10% on the marked price. The marked price is:
(a) ₹800 (b) ₹750 (c) ₹720 (d) ₹780
Answer: (a)

(a) ₹800 (b) ₹880 (c) ₹792 (d) ₹850
Answer: (b)
Solution: CP = 720 × 100/90 = ₹800. For 10% gain, SP = 800 × 110/100 = ₹880. The trap ₹792 comes from adding 10% of 720 to 720, ignoring that the base must be CP, so the answer is ₹880.

23. A trader marks his goods 40% above cost price and gives a discount of 10%. His profit percentage is:
(a) 30% (b) 28% (c) 24% (d) 26%
Answer: (d)
Solution: Let CP = 100, then MP = 140 and SP = 140 × 90/100 = 126. Profit = 26%. The trap 30% comes from simply subtracting 10 from 40, which ignores that discount applies to the marked price, so the answer is 26%.
Source: PYQ-style

24. A man buys two articles for ₹1200 each. He sells one at a profit of 20% and the other at a loss of 20%. His overall result is:
(a) No profit, no loss (b) Profit of ₹96 (c) Loss of ₹96 (d) Profit of ₹48
Answer: (a)
Solution: SP of first article = 1200 × 120/100 = ₹1440; SP of second = 1200 × 80/100 = ₹960. Total SP = 1440 + 960 = ₹2400, which equals total CP of ₹2400. The trap "loss of ₹96" assumes a loss like the equal-SP case, but here the cost prices are equal, so the answer is no profit, no loss.
Source: PYQ-style
