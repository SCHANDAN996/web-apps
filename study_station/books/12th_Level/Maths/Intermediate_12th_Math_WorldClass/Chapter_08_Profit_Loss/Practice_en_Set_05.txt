101. A shopkeeper buys an article for ₹480 and sells it for ₹600. What is his profit percentage?
(a) 20% (b) 15% (c) 30% (d) 25%
Answer: (d)
Solution: The tempting wrong option 20% comes from dividing the profit ₹120 by the selling price ₹600 instead of the cost price. Profit% is always calculated on cost price: (120/480) × 100 = 25%, so the answer is 25%.
Source: NCERT Class 8 Mathematics (Comparing Quantities)

102. A trader sells a watch at ₹810 and incurs a loss of 10%. What was the cost price?
(a) ₹880 (b) ₹891 (c) ₹900 (d) ₹729
Answer: (c)
Solution: The tempting wrong option ₹891 comes from adding 10% of ₹810 to ₹810, but loss% is on cost price, not selling price. SP = 90% of CP, so CP = 810 × 100/90 = ₹900, so the answer is ₹900.
Source: NCERT Class 8 Mathematics (Comparing Quantities)

103. By selling a chair for ₹720, a man gains 20%. At what price should he sell it to gain 30%?
(a) ₹800 (b) ₹760 (c) ₹780 (d) ₹792
Answer: (c)
Solution: The tempting wrong option ₹792 comes from adding 10% of ₹720 to ₹720, treating the selling price as the base. First find CP = 720 × 100/120 = ₹600. New SP for 30% gain = 600 × 1.30 = ₹780, so the answer is ₹780.
Source: NCERT Class 8 Mathematics (Comparing Quantities)

104. An article is marked 40% above cost price and a discount of 10% is given. What is the profit percentage?
(a) 26% (b) 30% (c) 24% (d) 28%
Answer: (a)
Solution: The tempting wrong option 30% comes from simply subtracting 40 − 10, ignoring that discount applies to the marked price. Let CP = 100, MP = 140, SP = 140 × 0.9 = 126. Profit = 26%, so the answer is 26%.

105. A dishonest dealer sells goods at cost price but uses a weight of 900 g instead of 1 kg. His profit percentage is:
(a) 10% (b) 11.11% (c) 9.09% (d) 12.5%
Answer: (b)
Solution: The tempting wrong option 10% comes from computing 100/1000, but profit% is on the actual cost (900 g worth). Profit = 100 g on 900 g cost = (100/900) × 100 = 11.11%, so the answer is 11.11%.

106. The cost price of 20 articles equals the selling price of 16 articles. What is the profit percentage?
(a) 20% (b) 25% (c) 30% (d) 16%
Answer: (b)
Solution: The tempting wrong option 20% comes from computing 4/20, but profit is measured on cost. Let CP of each = ₹1; CP of 20 = ₹20 = SP of 16, so SP of each = ₹1.25. Profit per article = ₹0.25 on ₹1 = 25%, so the answer is 25%.

107. A man buys two articles for ₹1500 each. He sells one at 20% profit and the other at 20% loss. His overall result is:
(a) No profit, no loss (b) 4% profit (c) 4% loss (d) 2% loss
Answer: (a)
Solution: The tempting wrong option 4% loss applies the rule for equal selling prices, but here the cost prices are equal. Total CP = ₹3000; SP = 1500 × 1.2 + 1500 × 0.8 = 1800 + 1200 = ₹3000, so the answer is no profit, no loss.
Source: NCERT Class 8 Mathematics (Comparing Quantities)

108. A man sells two articles for ₹990 each, gaining 10% on one and losing 10% on the other. His overall result is:
(a) No profit, no loss (b) 1% loss (c) 1% profit (d) 2% loss
Answer: (b)
Solution: The tempting wrong option "no profit, no loss" ignores that the cost prices differ. When two items are sold at the same SP with equal gain% and loss%, there is always a loss of x²/100 = 100/100 = 1%, so the answer is 1% loss.
Source: PYQ-style

109. A shopkeeper allows a discount of 20% and still earns a profit of 20%. The marked price is what percent above the cost price?
(a) 40% (b) 44% (c) 48% (d) 50%
Answer: (d)
Solution: The tempting wrong option 40% comes from adding 20 + 20, ignoring the different bases. Let CP = 100, SP = 120. Since SP = 80% of MP, MP = 120 × 100/80 = 150. MP is 50% above CP, so the answer is 50%.

110. Successive discounts of 20% and 10% are equivalent to a single discount of:
(a) 28% (b) 30% (c) 25% (d) 32%
Answer: (a)
Solution: The tempting wrong option 30% comes from simply adding the two discounts. Net SP = 100 × 0.8 × 0.9 = 72, so equivalent discount = 100 − 72 = 28%, so the answer is 28%.

112. An article sold for ₹460 earns the same profit as the loss incurred when it is sold for ₹340. The cost price is:
(a) ₹390 (b) ₹400 (c) ₹410 (d) ₹420
Answer: (b)
Solution: The tempting wrong option ₹410 comes from averaging incorrectly with the profit added. When profit at one SP equals loss at another, CP is the average of the two selling prices: (460 + 340)/2 = ₹400, so the answer is ₹400.

113. A trader marks his goods 25% above cost price. What maximum discount can he offer so as not to incur a loss?
(a) 20% (b) 25% (c) 15% (d) 22.5%
Answer: (a)
Solution: The tempting wrong option 25% assumes discount equals the markup, but discount is on the marked price. Let CP = 100, MP = 125. To break even, SP = 100, so discount = 25 on 125 = 20%, so the answer is 20%.

116. The selling price of an article is 5/4 of its cost price. The profit percentage is:
(a) 20% (b) 25% (c) 30% (d) 15%
Answer: (b)
Solution: The tempting wrong option 20% comes from reading 1/4 as 20% instead of 25%. Profit = SP − CP = (5/4 − 1) = 1/4 of CP = 25%, so the answer is 25%.

117. A man bought an article and sold it at a gain of 15%. Had he bought it at 10% less and sold it for ₹7 less, he would have gained 20%. The original cost price is:
(a) ₹80 (b) ₹140 (c) ₹120 (d) ₹100
Answer: (d)
Solution: The tempting wrong option ₹140 comes from a sign error while setting up the two selling prices. Let CP = x. First SP = 1.15x. New CP = 0.9x, new SP = 0.9x × 1.2 = 1.08x. Given 1.15x − 1.08x = 7, so 0.07x = 7 and x = ₹100, so the answer is ₹100.
Source: PYQ-style

121. A trader sells an article at a loss of 8%. Had he sold it for ₹72 more, he would have gained 10%. To gain 25%, he should sell it for:
(a) ₹480 (b) ₹450 (c) ₹500 (d) ₹520
Answer: (c)
Solution: The tempting wrong option ₹480 comes from using 20% instead of 25% on the computed cost. The ₹72 difference equals 8% + 10% = 18% of CP, so CP = 72 × 100/18 = ₹400. SP for 25% gain = 400 × 1.25 = ₹500, so the answer is ₹500.
Source: PYQ-style

122. A shopkeeper cheats by 10% while buying (takes 1100 g for the price of 1000 g) and also cheats by 10% while selling (gives 900 g for the price of 1000 g). His overall profit percentage is:
(a) 20% (b) 21% (c) 23.1% (d) 22.22%
Answer: (d)
Solution: The tempting wrong option 20% comes from adding the two cheats, ignoring compounding. He pays for 900 g worth but sells goods worth 1100 g in value: profit = (1100 − 900)/900 × 100 = 200/9 ≈ 22.22%, so the answer is 22.22%.
Source: PYQ-style

123. A man sells an article at 5% profit. If he had bought it at 5% less and sold it for ₹1 less, he would have gained 10%. The cost price is:
(a) ₹200 (b) ₹100 (c) ₹150 (d) ₹250
Answer: (a)
Solution: The tempting wrong option ₹100 comes from a sign error in the equation. Let CP = x. First SP = 1.05x. New CP = 0.95x, new SP = 0.95x × 1.10 = 1.045x. Given 1.05x − 1.045x = 1, so 0.005x = 1 and x = ₹200, so the answer is ₹200.
Source: PYQ-style

125. A trader marks his goods so that after allowing a discount of 25% he gains 20%. If the cost price of an article is ₹560, its marked price is:
(a) ₹900 (b) ₹840 (c) ₹896 (d) ₹875
Answer: (c)
Solution: The tempting wrong option ₹840 comes from simply computing 560 × 1.5, mixing up the bases. SP = 560 × 1.2 = ₹672. Since SP = 75% of MP, MP = 672 × 100/75 = ₹896, so the answer is ₹896.
