51. A shopkeeper buys an article for ₹800 and sells it for ₹920. What is his profit percentage?
(a) 15% (b) 12% (c) 18% (d) 20%
Answer: (a)
Solution: The tempting wrong option 12% comes from wrongly taking profit on the selling price (120/920 ≈ 13%) or misreading the profit. Profit = 920 − 800 = ₹120. Profit % = (120/800) × 100 = 15%, so the answer is 15%.
Source: NCERT Class 8 Mathematics

52. An article is sold for ₹1,440 at a loss of 10%. What was its cost price?
(a) ₹1,550 (b) ₹1,584 (c) ₹1,620 (d) ₹1,600
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is to add 10% of 1,440 to it, giving 1,584, but loss is always calculated on the cost price, not the selling price. SP = 90% of CP, so CP = 1,440 × 100/90 = ₹1,600, so the answer is ₹1,600.
Source: NCERT Class 8 Mathematics

53. A trader marks his goods 40% above cost price and gives a discount of 10%. What is his profit percentage?
(a) 30% (b) 24% (c) 26% (d) 28%
Answer: (c)
Solution: The tempting wrong answer 30% comes from simply subtracting 40 − 10, which ignores that discount applies to the marked price, not the cost price. Let CP = 100; MP = 140; SP = 140 × 0.9 = 126. Profit = 26%, so the answer is 26%.

54. By selling a chair for ₹540, a man loses 10%. At what price should he sell it to gain 10%?
(a) ₹660 (b) ₹600 (c) ₹640 (d) ₹680
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is to compute 540 + 20% of 540 = 648, but the gain must be on the cost price. CP = 540 × 100/90 = ₹600. Required SP = 600 × 110/100 = ₹660, so the answer is ₹660.

55. The cost price of 20 articles equals the selling price of 16 articles. What is the profit percentage?
(a) 20% (b) 16% (c) 25% (d) 30%
Answer: (c)
Solution: The tempting wrong answer 20% comes from taking 4/20, but profit must be measured on the cost of the 16 articles sold. Let CP of each = ₹1; CP of 16 = 16, SP of 16 = 20. Profit % = (4/16) × 100 = 25%, so the answer is 25%.

57. A man buys two articles for ₹1,500 each. He sells one at a profit of 20% and the other at a loss of 20%. What is his overall result?
(a) No profit, no loss (b) Loss of ₹60 (c) Profit of ₹120 (d) Loss of ₹120
Answer: (a)
Solution: The tempting trap is to think there must be some loss because "equal percent profit and loss always means loss" — but that rule applies when the selling prices are equal, not the cost prices. Here CP of each is ₹1,500: profit = ₹300 on one, loss = ₹300 on the other; total SP = 1,800 + 1,200 = ₹3,000 = total CP, so the answer is no profit, no loss.
Source: PYQ-style

58. A man sells two articles for ₹990 each. On one he gains 10% and on the other he loses 10%. His overall result is:
(a) No profit, no loss (b) Loss of 1% (c) Loss of 2% (d) Profit of 1%
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is "no profit, no loss", but with equal selling prices the cost prices differ, so a net loss always occurs. CP1 = 990 × 100/110 = 900; CP2 = 990 × 100/90 = 1,100; total CP = 2,000, total SP = 1,980. Loss = 20/2,000 = 1%, so the answer is loss of 1%.
Source: PYQ-style

59. A shopkeeper allows a discount of 20% and still earns a profit of 20%. The marked price is what percent above the cost price?
(a) 50% (b) 44% (c) 40% (d) 60%
Answer: (a)
Solution: The tempting wrong answer 40% comes from adding 20 + 20, ignoring the different bases. Let CP = 100; SP = 120; MP × 0.8 = 120, so MP = 150. MP is 50% above CP, so the answer is 50%.
Source: PYQ-style

60. If the selling price of an article is doubled, the profit triples. What is the original profit percentage?
(a) 100% (b) 25% (c) 75% (d) 50%
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is to assume profit doubles when SP doubles, but the cost stays fixed. Let CP = C and SP = S; profit = S − C. Doubling SP: 2S − C = 3(S − C), so 2S − C = 3S − 3C, giving S = 2C. Profit = 2C − C = C, i.e. 100%, so the answer is 100%.
Source: PYQ-style

61. A retailer buys 40 pens for the price of 36 pens and sells them at ₹5 per pen. What is his profit percentage?
(a) 10% (b) 11.11% (c) 12% (d) 9%
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is to compare the selling price with the price of 36 pens directly instead of finding the cost per pen. Cost of 40 pens = price of 36 pens = 36 × 5 = ₹180, so CP per pen = 180/40 = ₹4.5. Profit % = (0.5/4.5) × 100 = 11.11%, so the answer is 11.11%.
Source: PYQ-style

62. An article is sold at a profit of 25%. If it had been sold for ₹60 more, the profit would have been 40%. What is the cost price?
(a) ₹300 (b) ₹450 (c) ₹350 (d) ₹400
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is to divide 60 by 40% or 25% separately instead of by their difference. The extra ₹60 corresponds to 40% − 25% = 15% of CP. CP = 60 × 100/15 = ₹400, so the answer is ₹400.

63. A trader sells an article at a loss of 8%. If he had sold it for ₹72 more, he would have gained 10%. The cost price is:
(a) ₹360 (b) ₹450 (c) ₹400 (d) ₹500
Answer: (c)
Solution: The tempting error is to use only 10% or only 8% as the divisor. The swing from −8% to +10% is 18% of CP, equal to ₹72. CP = 72 × 100/18 = ₹400, so the answer is ₹400.

64. A shopkeeper cheats by 10% while buying (takes 1,100 g for the price of 1,000 g) and by 10% while selling (gives 900 g for the price of 1,000 g). His total profit percentage is:
(a) 20% (b) 25% (c) 21% (d) 22.22%
Answer: (d)
Solution: The tempting wrong answer 20% comes from adding the two cheats, but they compound. For ₹1,000 he gets 1,100 g of goods; he sells 900 g for ₹1,000, so 1,100 g fetches 1,100 × 1,000/900 = ₹1,222.22. Profit % = 22.22%, so the answer is 22.22%.
Source: PYQ-style

65. The marked price of a watch is ₹1,600. After two successive discounts of 10% and 20%, the selling price is:
(a) ₹1,120 (b) ₹1,152 (c) ₹1,168 (d) ₹1,200
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is to add the discounts to 30% and compute 1,600 × 0.7 = 1,120, but successive discounts apply one after another. SP = 1,600 × 0.9 × 0.8 = ₹1,152, so the answer is ₹1,152.

66. A single discount equivalent to two successive discounts of 20% and 25% is:
(a) 45% (b) 38% (c) 42% (d) 40%
Answer: (d)
Solution: The tempting wrong answer 45% comes from simply adding the two discounts. Equivalent discount = 20 + 25 − (20 × 25)/100 = 45 − 5 = 40%, so the answer is 40%.

67. A man bought a cycle for ₹2,500 and spent ₹300 on repairs. He sold it for ₹3,080. His profit percentage is:
(a) 8% (b) 12% (c) 10% (d) 15%
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is to compute profit on ₹2,500 alone, ignoring repair cost which adds to the effective cost price. Total CP = 2,500 + 300 = ₹2,800. Profit = 3,080 − 2,800 = 280. Profit % = (280/2,800) × 100 = 10%, so the answer is 10%.

74. A man sells an article at 5% above cost price. If he had bought it at 5% less and sold it for ₹2 less, he would have gained 10%. The original cost price is:
(a) ₹200 (b) ₹300 (c) ₹400 (d) ₹500
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is to set the new selling price equal to the old, but it is ₹2 less. Let CP = 100x. Original SP = 105x. New CP = 95x; new SP = 95x × 1.1 = 104.5x. Difference: 105x − 104.5x = 0.5x = 2, so x = 4. CP = 100 × 4 = ₹400, so the answer is ₹400.
Source: PYQ-style

75. A trader mixes 26 kg of rice at ₹20 per kg with 30 kg of rice at ₹36 per kg and sells the mixture at ₹30 per kg. His profit percentage is:
(a) 6% (b) 5% (c) 4.76% (d) 3.5%
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is to average the two prices as ₹28 per kg, ignoring that more rice was bought at ₹36. Total CP = 26 × 20 + 30 × 36 = ₹1,600; total SP = 56 × 30 = ₹1,680. Profit % = (80/1,600) × 100 = 5%, so the answer is 5%.
Source: PYQ-style
