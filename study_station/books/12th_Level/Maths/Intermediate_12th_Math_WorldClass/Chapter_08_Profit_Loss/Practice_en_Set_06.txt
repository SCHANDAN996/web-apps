126. A shopkeeper marks his goods 40% above the cost price and allows a discount of 10% on the marked price. What is his profit percentage?
(a) 30% (b) 28% (c) 24% (d) 26%
Answer: (d)
Solution: The tempting answer is 40 − 10 = 30%, but discount applies to the marked price, not the cost price. Let CP = ₹100, so MP = ₹140 and SP = 140 × 0.90 = ₹126. Profit = 126 − 100 = ₹26 on ₹100, so the answer is 26%.

127. A single discount equivalent to two successive discounts of 20% and 25% is:
(a) 40% (b) 42.5% (c) 45% (d) 35%
Answer: (a)
Solution: Simply adding 20 + 25 = 45% is the trap, because the second discount acts on the already reduced price. On ₹100: after 20% off, price = ₹80; after 25% off, price = 80 × 0.75 = ₹60. Total discount = 100 − 60 = ₹40, so the answer is 40%.

128. Selling an article for ₹X causes a loss of 25%, while selling it for ₹Y gives a profit of 25%. The ratio X : Y is:
(a) 3 : 5 (b) 4 : 5 (c) 2 : 3 (d) 1 : 2
Answer: (a)
Solution: Do not assume the ratio is 1 : 2 just because the percentages look symmetric. X = 75% of CP and Y = 125% of CP, so X : Y = 75 : 125 = 3 : 5. Hence the answer is 3 : 5.

129. An article is sold at a profit of 25%. Had it been bought at 20% less and sold for ₹10.50 less, the profit would have been 30%. The original cost price is:
(a) ₹40 (b) ₹50 (c) ₹45 (d) ₹60
Answer: (b)
Solution: A common slip is applying 30% to the original CP instead of the reduced CP. Let CP = C; SP = 1.25C. New CP = 0.8C, new SP = 1.25C − 10.50, and 1.25C − 10.50 = 1.30 × 0.8C = 1.04C. So 0.21C = 10.50, giving C = ₹50. Check: SP = 62.50; new CP = 40, new SP = 52, profit = 12/40 = 30%. The answer is ₹50.

130. A dishonest dealer claims to sell rice at cost price but uses a 900 g weight in place of 1 kg. His actual profit percentage is:
(a) 11.11% (b) 10% (c) 12.5% (d) 9.09%
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is computing 100/1000 = 10%, but profit % is always on the cost actually incurred. He gives 900 g worth of goods and receives the price of 1000 g, so profit = 100/900 × 100 = 11.11%. The answer is 11.11%.

131. A trader marks his goods 25% above cost price, gives a 10% discount, and also cheats by giving only 900 g instead of 1 kg. His overall profit percentage is:
(a) 12.5% (b) 25% (c) 20% (d) 15%
Answer: (b)
Solution: Ignoring the weight fraud and computing only 1.25 × 0.90 − 1 = 12.5% is the trap. For goods costing ₹0.90 (900 g), he charges 1.25 × 0.90 = ₹1.125. Profit = 1.125 − 0.90 = 0.225 on 0.90, i.e. 0.225/0.90 × 100 = 25%. The answer is 25%.

132. A sells an article to B at 20% profit, and B sells it to C at 25% profit. If C pays ₹600, the cost price for A was:
(a) ₹450 (b) ₹400 (c) ₹480 (d) ₹500
Answer: (b)
Solution: Subtracting 45% from 600 is wrong because the profits compound. A's CP × 1.20 × 1.25 = 600, so A's CP = 600/1.5 = ₹400. The answer is ₹400.

133. A man buys two articles for the same price each. He sells one at a profit of 20% and the other at a loss of 20%. Overall he:
(a) neither gains nor loses (b) loses 4% (c) gains 2% (d) gains 4%
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is assuming a 4% loss as in the equal-SP case; here the cost prices are equal, not the selling prices. Let each CP = ₹100; total CP = ₹200, total SP = 120 + 80 = ₹200. There is neither gain nor loss, so the answer is neither gains nor loses.

134. When the price of sugar falls by 20%, a family can buy 5 kg more sugar for ₹600. The reduced price of sugar per kg is:
(a) ₹20 (b) ₹25 (c) ₹24 (d) ₹30
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is dividing 600 by 5 to get ₹120 or taking 20% of the wrong base. The saving due to the price fall is 20% of ₹600 = ₹120, and this saving buys 5 extra kg, so the reduced price = 120/5 = ₹24 per kg. The answer is ₹24.

135. A man sells an article at a loss of 5%. Had he sold it for ₹27 more, he would have gained 4%. The cost price is:
(a) ₹270 (b) ₹320 (c) ₹300 (d) ₹350
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is treating ₹27 as 5% or 4% of CP; it actually bridges the gap from −5% to +4%, i.e. 9% of CP. So 9% of CP = 27, giving CP = 27 × 100/9 = ₹300. The answer is ₹300.

136. A trader sells two articles at ₹990 each. On one he gains 10% and on the other he loses 10%. His overall result is:
(a) no profit, no loss (b) loss of ₹20 (c) profit of ₹20 (d) loss of ₹10
Answer: (b)
Solution: "No profit, no loss" is the classic trap; equal gain and loss percentages on equal selling prices always give a net loss. CP of first = 990/1.1 = ₹900, CP of second = 990/0.9 = ₹1100; total CP = ₹2000, total SP = ₹1980. Loss = ₹20, so the answer is loss of ₹20.

137. A merchant's profit is 20% of the selling price. His profit as a percentage of the cost price is:
(a) 20% (b) 25% (c) 16.67% (d) 30%
Answer: (b)
Solution: Quoting 20% again is the trap because the base changes from SP to CP. If SP = ₹100, profit = ₹20 and CP = ₹80. Profit on CP = 20/80 × 100 = 25%. The answer is 25%.

138. A shopkeeper wants to earn a 25% profit after allowing a 20% discount on the marked price. By what percentage above the cost price should he mark his goods?

Solution: Adding 25 + 20 = 45% is the trap. MP × 0.80 = CP × 1.25, so MP/CP = 1.25/0.80 = 1.5625. The markup is 56.25% above CP, so the answer is 56.25%.

140. The cost price of 16 articles equals the selling price of 12 articles. The profit percentage is:
(a) 33⅓% (b) 25% (c) 30% (d) 40%

Solution: Taking 4/16 = 25% is the trap; profit must be measured on the cost side. Profit on the deal = 16 − 12 = 4 articles' worth against a cost of 12, so profit % = 4/12 × 100 = 33⅓%. The answer is 33⅓%.

141. A grocer mixes rice costing ₹40 per kg with rice costing ₹60 per kg and sells the mixture at ₹62.50 per kg, gaining 25%. The mixing ratio (cheaper : dearer) is:
(a) 2 : 1 (b) 1 : 2 (c) 3 : 2 (d) 1 : 1

Solution: Using the selling price ₹62.50 directly in alligation is the trap; first find the mixture's cost price. CP of mixture = 62.50/1.25 = ₹50. By alligation, ratio = (60 − 50) : (50 − 40) = 10 : 10 = 1 : 1. The answer is 1 : 1.

147. A trader finds that if he doubles the selling price of an article, his profit triples. His original profit percentage is:
(a) 50% (b) 100% (c) 75% (d) 200%
Answer: (b)
Solution: Let CP = C and original profit = P, so SP = C + P. Doubling SP triples profit: 2(C + P) = C + 3P, which gives C = P. Profit % = P/C × 100 = 100%. The answer is 100%.

148. Selling an article at 80% of its marked price yields a profit of 12%. If the article is sold at the full marked price, the profit percentage will be:
