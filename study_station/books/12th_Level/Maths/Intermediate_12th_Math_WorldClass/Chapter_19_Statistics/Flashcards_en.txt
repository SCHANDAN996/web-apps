Card 1
Front: What is the mean of the first n natural numbers?
Back: (n + 1)/2. Example: mean of 1 to 10 = 11/2 = 5.5.

Card 2
Front: What is the mean of the first n even natural numbers?
Back: n + 1. Example: first 5 even numbers (2,4,6,8,10) → mean = 6.

Card 3
Front: What is the mean of the first n odd natural numbers?
Back: n. Example: first 7 odd numbers → mean = 7.

Card 4
Front: If every value in a dataset is increased by k, what happens to the mean, median and mode?
Back: All three increase by k. (They shift with the data.)

Card 5
Front: If every value in a dataset is multiplied by k, what happens to the mean and standard deviation?
Back: Both get multiplied by k. (Variance gets multiplied by k².)

Card 6
Front: Which measure of central tendency is NOT affected by extreme values (outliers)?
Back: Median — it depends only on the middle position, not on actual values.

Card 7
Front: What is the empirical relationship between Mean, Median and Mode?
Back: Mode = 3 Median − 2 Mean.

Card 8
Front: Mnemonic trick: how do you remember "Mode = 3 Median − 2 Mean"?
Back: "Mo-3-Me-2-M" — Mode takes 3 Medians and pays back 2 Means. Picture Mode as a shopkeeper: takes 3, returns 2.

Card 9
Front: For a moderately skewed distribution, which lies between Mean and Mode?
Back: Median always lies between Mean and Mode.

Card 10
Front: What is the median of n observations when n is odd?
Back: The value of the ((n+1)/2)th observation after arranging in order.

Card 11
Front: What is the median of n observations when n is even?
Back: The average of the (n/2)th and (n/2 + 1)th observations after arranging in order.

Card 12
Front: What is the formula for the median of grouped (continuous) data?
Back: Median = l + [(N/2 − cf)/f] × h, where l = lower limit of median class, cf = cumulative frequency before it, f = its frequency, h = class width.

Card 13
Front: What is the formula for the mode of grouped data?
Back: Mode = l + [(f₁ − f₀)/(2f₁ − f₀ − f₂)] × h, where f₁ = frequency of modal class, f₀ and f₂ = frequencies of the classes before and after it.

Card 14
Front: What is the range of a dataset?
Back: Range = Maximum value − Minimum value. It is the simplest measure of dispersion.

Card 15
Front: What is the relationship between variance and standard deviation?
Back: Standard deviation = √Variance. Variance is the mean of squared deviations from the mean.

Card 16
Front: What is the coefficient of variation (CV) and when is it used?
Back: CV = (Standard deviation / Mean) × 100%. Used to compare consistency of two datasets with different means or units — lower CV means more consistent.

Card 17
Front: Mnemonic trick: how do you remember which average is affected by outliers?
Back: "Mean is MEAN to outliers" — the Mean gets dragged by extreme values, while Median and Mode stay calm in the middle.

Card 18
Front: If the sum of squares of deviations is taken from the mean versus any other number, which is smaller?
Back: The sum of squared deviations is minimum when taken from the mean.

Card 19
Front: What is the combined mean of two groups with means x̄₁, x̄₂ and sizes n₁, n₂?
Back: Combined mean = (n₁x̄₁ + n₂x̄₂)/(n₁ + n₂).

Card 20
Front: In a frequency distribution, what does the "modal class" mean?
Back: The class interval with the highest frequency — the mode of grouped data is computed from this class.
