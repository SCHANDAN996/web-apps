76. The mean of 20 observations is 15. If each observation is multiplied by 3, the new mean is:
(a) 45 (b) 30 (c) 60 (d) 15
Answer: (a)
Solution: The tempting wrong option is 15, chosen by those who forget that scaling affects the mean. When every observation is multiplied by a constant k, the mean is also multiplied by k. New mean = 15 × 3 = 45, so the answer is 45.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Mathematics (Statistics)

77. The median of the data 12, 5, 9, 18, 7, 15, 3 is:
(a) 7 (b) 12 (c) 9 (d) 15
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is picking 12 by taking the middle value without arranging the data. First arrange in ascending order: 3, 5, 7, 9, 12, 15, 18. With 7 observations, the median is the 4th value, so the answer is 9.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Mathematics (Statistics)

78. The mode of the data 4, 6, 8, 6, 4, 6, 9, 6, 4 is:
(a) 6 (b) 4 (c) 8 (d) 9
Answer: (a)
Solution: The tempting wrong option is 4, since it appears 3 times and looks frequent. But 6 appears 4 times, which is the highest frequency. The mode is the most frequent value, so the answer is 6.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Mathematics (Statistics)

79. If the mean of x, x+2, x+4, x+6, x+8 is 13, then the value of x is:
(a) 7 (b) 9 (c) 11 (d) 13
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is assuming the mean equals x itself, giving 13. Sum of observations = 5x + 20, so mean = (5x + 20)/5 = x + 4 = 13, giving x = 9, so the answer is 9.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Mathematics (Statistics)

80. The mean of the first 10 natural numbers is:
(a) 5 (b) 10 (c) 6 (d) 5.5
Answer: (d)
Solution: The tempting wrong option is 5, taken as the middle of 1 to 10. Sum of first 10 natural numbers = 10 × 11/2 = 55, so mean = 55/10 = 5.5, so the answer is 5.5.
Source: NCERT Class 8 Mathematics (Data Handling)

81. For a moderately skewed distribution, if mean = 26 and mode = 20, the median is:
(a) 22 (b) 23 (c) 24 (d) 25
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is averaging mean and mode directly, giving 23. The empirical relation is Mode = 3 Median − 2 Mean, so 20 = 3 Median − 52, giving Median = 72/3 = 24, so the answer is 24.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Mathematics (Statistics)

82. The median of first 15 odd natural numbers is:
(a) 13 (b) 15 (c) 17 (d) 19
Answer: (b)
Solution: The tempting wrong option is 13, from miscounting the middle term. The first 15 odd numbers are 1, 3, 5, …, 29; the median is the 8th term = 2 × 8 − 1 = 15, so the answer is 15.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Mathematics (Statistics)

83. If the mean of 5 observations is 20 and the mean of another 10 observations is 30, the combined mean is:
(a) 28 (b) 25 (c) 27.5 (d) 26.67
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is taking the simple average of 20 and 30, giving 25. Combined mean = (5 × 20 + 10 × 30)/15 = 400/15 ≈ 26.67, so the answer is 26.67.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Mathematics (Statistics)

84. The range of the data 25, 18, 42, 33, 10, 27 is:
(a) 22 (b) 28 (c) 32 (d) 42
Answer: (c)
Solution: The tempting wrong option is 42, which is just the maximum value. Range = Maximum − Minimum = 42 − 10 = 32, so the answer is 32.

85. The mean deviation about the mean of the data 4, 7, 8, 9, 12 is:
(a) 3.2 (b) 2.8 (c) 3.0 (d) 2.0
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is forgetting absolute values, which makes the deviations cancel to zero. Mean = 40/5 = 8; absolute deviations are 4, 1, 0, 1, 4 with sum 10; mean deviation = 10/5 = 2.0, so the answer is 2.0.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Mathematics (Statistics)

86. The variance of the data 2, 4, 6, 8, 10 is:
(a) 8 (b) 6 (c) 10 (d) 12
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is computing standard deviation and stopping, or mis-squaring. Mean = 6; squared deviations are 16, 4, 0, 4, 16; sum = 40; variance = 40/5 = 8, so the answer is 8.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Mathematics (Statistics)

87. The standard deviation of the data 5, 5, 5, 5, 5 is:
(a) 1 (b) 0 (c) 5 (d) 25
Answer: (b)
Solution: The tempting wrong option is 5, confusing the value with its spread. Since all observations are equal, there is no dispersion; standard deviation = 0, so the answer is 0.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Mathematics (Statistics)

88. If the variance of a data set is 16, its standard deviation is:
(a) 2 (b) 8 (c) 256 (d) 4
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is squaring 16 to get 256 instead of taking the root. Standard deviation = √variance = √16 = 4, so the answer is 4.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Mathematics (Statistics)

89. The coefficient of variation of a data set with mean 50 and standard deviation 10 is:
(a) 5% (b) 10% (c) 20% (d) 25%
Answer: (c)
Solution: The tempting wrong option is 10%, from just quoting the standard deviation. Coefficient of variation = (standard deviation/mean) × 100 = (10/50) × 100 = 20%, so the answer is 20%.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Mathematics (Statistics)

90. In a frequency distribution, the class mark of the class 20–30 is:
(a) 25 (b) 30 (c) 20 (d) 50
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is taking the upper limit 30. Class mark = (lower limit + upper limit)/2 = (20 + 30)/2 = 25, so the answer is 25.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Mathematics (Statistics)

91. The sum of deviations of observations from their mean is always:
(a) Positive (b) Negative (c) Zero (d) One
Answer: (c)
Solution: The tempting wrong option is "Positive", since deviations look like distances. By the defining property of the mean, positive and negative deviations cancel exactly, so the sum is always zero, so the answer is Zero.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Mathematics (Statistics)

92. If each observation of a data set is increased by 5, the standard deviation:
(a) Decreases by 5 (b) Remains unchanged (c) Increases by 5 (d) Becomes 5 times
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is thinking the spread grows by 5 like the mean does. Standard deviation measures dispersion, which is unaffected by adding a constant to every value, so the answer is Remains unchanged.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Mathematics (Statistics)

93. The mode of the distribution can be found graphically using:
(a) Pie chart (b) Ogive (c) Bar graph (d) Histogram
Answer: (d)
Solution: The tempting wrong option is Ogive, which actually locates the median. The mode is located from a histogram by drawing diagonals in the modal class rectangle, so the answer is Histogram.

94. The median of a grouped frequency distribution is found graphically using:
(a) Histogram (b) Frequency polygon (c) Ogive (d) Pie chart
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is choosing Histogram, which gives the mode. The median is read from the ogive (cumulative frequency curve) at the N/2 point, so the answer is Ogive.

95. The mean of the squares of the first 5 natural numbers is:
(a) 9 (b) 12 (c) 10 (d) 11

96. For the data 3, 5, 7, 9, 11, the mean deviation about the median is:
(a) 2.4 (b) 2.0 (c) 2.8 (d) 3.0
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is mis-adding the absolute deviations or dividing by the wrong count. Median = 7; absolute deviations are 4, 2, 0, 2, 4; sum = 12; mean deviation = 12/5 = 2.4, so the answer is 2.4.

97. If the mean of a distribution is 40 and the sum of all observations is 800, the number of observations is:
(a) 15 (b) 20 (c) 25 (d) 30
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is multiplying 800 and 40 instead of dividing. Number of observations = Sum/Mean = 800/40 = 20, so the answer is 20.

98. The empirical relationship among mean, median and mode is:
(a) Mode = 3 Median − 2 Mean (b) Mode = 2 Median − 3 Mean (c) Median = 3 Mode − 2 Mean (d) Mean = 3 Median − 2 Mode
Answer: (a)
Solution: The tempting wrong option is Mode = 2 Median − 3 Mean, which swaps the coefficients. The verified empirical relation for moderately skewed data is Mode = 3 Median − 2 Mean, so the answer is Mode = 3 Median − 2 Mean.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Mathematics (Statistics)

99. The standard deviation of the first 5 natural numbers is:
(a) √2 (b) 2 (c) √8 (d) 3
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is reporting the variance 2 as the standard deviation. Mean = 3; squared deviations are 4, 1, 0, 1, 4; variance = 10/5 = 2; standard deviation = √2, so the answer is √2.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Mathematics (Statistics)

100. In a class of 40 students, the mean weight of boys is 50 kg and of girls is 40 kg. If there are 25 boys, the mean weight of the whole class is:
(a) 45 kg (b) 46.25 kg (c) 47.5 kg (d) 48 kg
Answer: (b)
Solution: The tempting wrong option is 45 kg, the simple average of 50 and 40, which ignores unequal group sizes. Girls = 15; total weight = 25 × 50 + 15 × 40 = 1250 + 600 = 1850; mean = 1850/40 = 46.25 kg, so the answer is 46.25 kg.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Mathematics (Statistics)
