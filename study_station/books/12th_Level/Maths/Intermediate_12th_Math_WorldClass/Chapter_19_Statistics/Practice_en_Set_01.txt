1. The mean of the data 4, 8, 12, 16, 20 is:
(a) 10 (b) 12 (c) 14 (d) 16
Answer: (b)
Solution: Many students pick 10 by averaging only the first and last terms incorrectly as (4+20)/3. Mean = (4+8+12+16+20)/5 = 60/5 = 12, so the answer is 12.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Maths, Statistics

2. The median of the data 3, 7, 5, 9, 11 is:
(a) 5 (b) 7 (c) 9 (d) 6
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is taking the middle value 5 without arranging the data. Arranged in order: 3, 5, 7, 9, 11; the middle (3rd) value is 7, so the answer is 7.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Maths, Statistics

3. The mode of the data 2, 3, 4, 3, 5, 3, 6 is:
(a) 3 (b) 4 (c) 6 (d) 2
Answer: (a)
Solution: Students often pick 4 thinking it is the largest repeated-looking value, but 3 occurs three times, more than any other value. The most frequent value is 3, so the answer is 3.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Maths, Statistics

4. The range of the data 12, 25, 8, 30, 17 is:
(a) 18 (b) 25 (c) 22 (d) 30
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is choosing 30, the maximum value itself, instead of the difference. Range = maximum − minimum = 30 − 8 = 22, so the answer is 22.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Maths, Statistics

5. The mean of the first 10 natural numbers is:
(a) 10 (b) 5 (c) 6 (d) 5.5
Answer: (d)
Solution: Many pick 5 by halving 10 directly. Sum of first 10 natural numbers = 10×11/2 = 55; mean = 55/10 = 5.5, so the answer is 5.5.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Maths, Statistics

6. If the mean of 5 observations is 20, the sum of the observations is:
(a) 50 (b) 25 (c) 4 (d) 100
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is dividing 20 by 5 to get 4, reversing the formula. Sum = mean × number of observations = 20 × 5 = 100, so the answer is 100.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Maths, Statistics

7. The median of an even number of observations 2, 4, 6, 8 is:
(a) 4 (b) 5 (c) 6 (d) 7
Answer: (b)
Solution: Students often pick 4 or 6, one of the two middle values, instead of their average. Median = (4+6)/2 = 5, so the answer is 5.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Maths, Statistics

8. The class mark of the class interval 10–20 is:
(a) 10 (b) 20 (c) 15 (d) 30
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is taking the upper limit 20. Class mark = (lower limit + upper limit)/2 = (10+20)/2 = 15, so the answer is 15.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Maths, Statistics

9. The mode of the data 5, 5, 7, 7, 9, 5, 8 is:
(a) 6 (b) 7 (c) 9 (d) 5
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is picking 7 because it repeats, but 7 appears only twice while 5 appears three times. The most frequent value is 5, so the answer is 5.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Maths, Statistics

10. The mean of the data 10, 20, 30, 40, 50 is:
(a) 25 (b) 30 (c) 35 (d) 150
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is choosing 150, which is the sum, not the mean. Mean = 150/5 = 30, so the answer is 30.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Maths, Statistics

11. In a frequency distribution, the class with the highest frequency is called the:
(a) median class (b) mean class (c) modal class (d) range class
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is choosing median class, which is found from cumulative frequency, not highest frequency. The class with maximum frequency is the modal class, so the answer is modal class.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Maths, Statistics

12. The empirical relationship between mean, median and mode is:
(a) Mode = 3 Median − 2 Mean (b) Mode = 2 Median − 3 Mean (c) Mode = 3 Mean − 2 Median (d) Mode = Mean + Median
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is the option Mode = 3 Mean − 2 Median, which swaps mean and median. The correct empirical formula is Mode = 3 Median − 2 Mean, so the answer is Mode = 3 Median − 2 Mean.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Maths, Statistics

13. The median of the data 15, 10, 20, 25, 5 is:
(a) 20 (b) 10 (c) 15 (d) 25
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is taking 20, the middle value of the unarranged list. Arranged: 5, 10, 15, 20, 25; middle value is 15, so the answer is 15.

14. If each observation of a data is increased by 5, the mean:
(a) remains the same (b) increases by 5 (c) decreases by 5 (d) is multiplied by 5
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is thinking the mean stays the same because the data only shifts. Adding a constant to every value adds the same constant to the mean, so the answer is increases by 5.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Maths, Statistics

15. The sum of deviations of observations from their mean is always:
(a) zero (b) 1 (c) negative (d) positive
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is choosing positive, since deviations look like distances. Positive and negative deviations cancel exactly, so the answer is zero.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Maths, Statistics

19. A graph drawn using the mid-points of the tops of bars of a histogram is called a:
(a) bar graph (b) pie chart (c) frequency polygon (d) ogive
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is choosing ogive, which is a cumulative frequency curve. Joining mid-points of bar tops gives a frequency polygon, so the answer is frequency polygon.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Maths, Statistics

20. The mean of 6, 8, 10, 12, 14 is:
(a) 10 (b) 9 (c) 8 (d) 12
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is picking 8 or 12 by guessing near the ends. Mean = (6+8+10+12+14)/5 = 50/5 = 10, so the answer is 10.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Maths, Statistics

21. The mean of 20 observations is 15. If one observation 25 was wrongly read as 35, the correct mean is:
(a) 14 (b) 15.5 (c) 15 (d) 14.5
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is leaving the mean at 15 or subtracting 10 from it directly. Wrong sum = 20×15 = 300; correct sum = 300 − 35 + 25 = 290; correct mean = 290/20 = 14.5, so the answer is 14.5.
Source: PYQ-style

22. The median of the data 12, 5, 9, 18, 7, 15 is:
(a) 9 (b) 11 (c) 12 (d) 10.5
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is averaging 9 and 12 without arranging, or picking 12. Arranged: 5, 7, 9, 12, 15, 18; median = (9+12)/2 = 10.5, so the answer is 10.5.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Maths, Statistics

23. If the mean of x, x+2, x+4, x+6, x+8 is 13, then x equals:
(a) 9 (b) 7 (c) 8 (d) 11
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is setting x = 13 directly, forgetting the added constants. Mean = (5x + 20)/5 = x + 4 = 13, giving x = 9, so the answer is 9.
Source: PYQ-style

24. For a moderately skewed distribution, if mean = 26 and mode = 32, the median is:
(a) 28 (b) 29 (c) 30 (d) 27
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is averaging mean and mode to get 29. Using Mode = 3 Median − 2 Mean: 32 = 3 Median − 52, so 3 Median = 84 and Median = 28, so the answer is 28.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Maths, Statistics

25. The average of 11 numbers is 50. The average of the first 6 is 49 and that of the last 6 is 52. The 6th number is:
(a) 56 (b) 52 (c) 50 (d) 48
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is taking 50, the overall average, as the middle number. Total = 11×50 = 550; first 6 sum = 294; last 6 sum = 312; 6th number = 294 + 312 − 550 = 56, so the answer is 56.
Source: PYQ-style
