26. The mean of 10 observations is 15. If one observation, 25, is removed, the mean of the remaining observations is:
(a) 14.2 (b) 13.9 (c) 13.5 (d) 14.5
Answer: (b)
Solution: The tempting wrong option 14.5 comes from wrongly subtracting 25 from the mean instead of from the total. Total of 10 observations = 10 × 15 = 150. After removing 25, sum = 125 and count = 9, so new mean = 125/9 ≈ 13.89 ≈ 13.9, so the answer is 13.9.

27. The median of the data 12, 18, 7, 25, 15, 10, 21 is:
(a) 18 (b) 12 (c) 15 (d) 14
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is to take the middle value 25 without arranging the data in order. Arranging in ascending order: 7, 10, 12, 15, 18, 21, 25. With 7 observations, the median is the 4th value, so the answer is 15.

29. If the mean of x, x+2, x+4, x+6, x+8 is 11, then the value of x is:

31. The median of the first 12 natural numbers is:

32. If the mode of a data set is 24 and the mean is 20, then using the empirical relation, the median is approximately:
(a) 21 (b) 22 (c) 22.67 (d) 21.33
Answer: (d)
Solution: The tempting wrong option 22 comes from simply averaging 20 and 24, which ignores the empirical weights. The empirical relation is Mode = 3 Median − 2 Mean, so 24 = 3 Median − 40, giving Median = 64/3 ≈ 21.33, so the answer is 21.33.
