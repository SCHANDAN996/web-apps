126. The mean of 5 observations is 20. If one observation, 40, is removed, the mean of the remaining observations is:
(a) 15 (b) 18 (c) 20 (d) 25
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is assuming the mean stays 20 after removing a value — that only happens if the removed value equals the mean. Total of 5 observations = 5 × 20 = 100. After removing 40, remaining total = 60 over 4 observations, so mean = 60/4 = 15. So the answer is 15.

127. The mean of 10 numbers is 12.5. The mean of the first 6 numbers is 15 and the mean of the last 3 numbers is 10. The 7th number is:
(a) 5 (b) 7.5 (c) 10 (d) 12.5
Answer: (a)
Solution: Many students average 15 and 10 to guess the 7th number, but the 7th number must be found by subtraction. Total of 10 numbers = 125; first 6 total = 90; last 3 total = 30. So the 7th number = 125 − 90 − 30 = 5. So the answer is 5.

130. If the mode of the data 16, 15, 17, 16, 15, x, 19, 17, 14 is 15, then the value of x is:
(a) 15 (b) 14 (c) 16 (d) 17
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is assuming x can be any value; but for 15 to be the unique mode it must occur most often. Currently 15, 16 and 17 each occur twice. Setting x = 15 makes 15 occur three times, more than any other value. So the answer is 15.

131. For a moderately skewed distribution, if the mean is 26 and the mode is 20, then the median is:
(a) 22 (b) 23 (c) 24 (d) 25
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is taking the simple average of mean and mode, which gives 23, but the empirical relation is Mode = 3 Median − 2 Mean. So 20 = 3 Median − 52, giving 3 Median = 72 and Median = 24. So the answer is 24.

132. The mean of the squares of the first n natural numbers is:

Solution: The trap is choosing n(n+1)/2, which is the mean of the first n natural numbers, not of their squares. Sum of squares = n(n+1)(2n+1)/6; dividing by n gives (n+1)(2n+1)/6. So the answer is (n+1)(2n+1)/6.

133. The variance of the first 10 natural numbers is:
(a) 9.25 (b) 8.25 (c) 10.5 (d) 7.25
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is using n²/12 = 100/12 ≈ 8.33, but the correct formula is (n² − 1)/12. Variance = (100 − 1)/12 = 99/12 = 8.25. So the answer is 8.25.

Solution: The trap is thinking adding 10 to each value increases the spread by 10, but standard deviation measures spread, which is unchanged by adding a constant. Each value is just shifted by 10, so the standard deviation remains σ. So the answer is σ.

137. The coefficient of variation of a data set with mean 50 and standard deviation 8 is:

Solution: The trap is matching the larger CV with the larger mean, but CV = SD/Mean × 100 means a larger CV implies a smaller mean for equal SD. Mean₁ = 5×100/25 = 20; Mean₂ = 5×100/20 = 25. So the answer is 20 and 25.
