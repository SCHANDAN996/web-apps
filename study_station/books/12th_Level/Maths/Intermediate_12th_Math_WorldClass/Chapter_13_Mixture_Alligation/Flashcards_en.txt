Card 1
Front: How do you find the mean price of a mixture?
Back: Mean = Σ(quantity × price) ÷ total quantity — a weighted average.

Card 2
Front: What is the alligation rule for the mixing ratio?
Back: Cheaper : Dearer = (dearer − mean) : (mean − cheaper).

Card 3
Front: What does "Swap the distances" mean?
Back: Find each price's distance from the mean, then give each item the other one's distance as its share.

Card 4
Front: ₹200 and ₹300 tea are mixed to cost ₹240. What is the ratio?
Back: (300 − 240) : (240 − 200) = 60 : 40 = 3 : 2.

Card 5
Front: When can you take a simple average of two prices?
Back: Only when equal quantities are mixed.

Card 6
Front: What is the replacement formula?
Back: Quantity left = V × (1 − x/V)ⁿ.

Card 7
Front: 80 L milk, 20 L replaced by water twice — milk left?
Back: 80 × (3/4)² = 45 litres.

Card 8
Front: How do you mix two solutions of different strength?
Back: Concentration = (V₁ × p₁ + V₂ × p₂) ÷ (V₁ + V₂).

Card 9
Front: Milk : water is 4 : 1 in 50 litres. 10 litres of water is added. New ratio?
Back: 40 : 20 = 2 : 1.

Card 10
Front: What does the mnemonic "Cross and Subtract, Far goes Near" remind you?
Back: Draw the alligation cross and subtract; each item takes the distance of the far price.

Card 11
Front: The mean price is closer to the cheaper item. What does that tell you?
Back: More of the cheaper item is used.

Card 12
Front: Examiner's trap: the ratio option is reversed. How do you avoid it?
Back: The cheaper item's share is (dearer − mean); check which item is closer to the mean.

Card 13
Front: Examiner's trap: subtracting 20 litres twice from 80 litres of milk gives 40. Why is it wrong?
Back: The second removal takes out a mixture, not pure milk, so only 15 litres of milk leave the second time.

Card 14
Front: Can alligation solve average marks or average price questions?
Back: Yes — it works for any weighted arithmetic mean of two groups, such as marks or prices. It does not apply to averages like speed over equal distances, which use the harmonic mean.

Card 15
Front: When should you skip a mixture question?
Back: When three or more items are mixed and the ratio is not clear in 45 seconds.
