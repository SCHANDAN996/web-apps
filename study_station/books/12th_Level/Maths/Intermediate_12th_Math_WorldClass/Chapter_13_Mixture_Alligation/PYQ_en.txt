# PYQ Analysis — Mixture and Alligation

## 1. How Often It Appears

Mixture and alligation questions appear regularly in SSC CGL/CHSL, banking (IBPS/SBI PO and Clerk) and railway papers. Most papers carry one or two questions; banking papers also use alligation inside profit and data-interpretation questions. Exact yearly counts vary, so this analysis describes the pattern rather than quoting numbers.

## 2. Sub-topic Weightage (qualitative)

- **Mixing ratio by alligation** — the most common type.
- **Mean price / concentration** — common.
- **Replacement (milk–water)** — common in banking.
- **Adding one component to change a ratio** — common in SSC.
- **Alligation applied to profit, speed or marks** — moderate.

## 3. Ten High-Yield Questions (PYQ-style, with solutions)

1. Tea at ₹200 and ₹300 per kg mixed to cost ₹240 → **3 : 2**.
2. 3 kg at ₹40 and 2 kg at ₹50 → mean **₹44**. Hook: ₹45 (simple average).
3. 80 L milk, 20 L replaced twice → **45 L**. Hook: 40 L.
4. 20 L of 10% + 30 L of 20% solution → **16%**. Hook: 15%.
5. Milk : water 4 : 1 in 50 L, add 10 L water → **2 : 1**.
6. Rice at ₹30 and ₹45 to get ₹40 → **1 : 2**. Hook: 2 : 1.
7. 60 L milk, 6 L replaced 3 times → 60 × (9/10)³ = **43.74 L**.
8. A shopkeeper sells part at 10% profit and the rest at 20% profit, overall 14% → ratio **3 : 2**.
9. 40 L of a 3 : 1 mixture; how much water to make it 1 : 1? → milk 30, water 10 → add **20 L**.
10. Two alloys with gold 60% and 80% mixed to get 75% → **1 : 3**.

## 4. Skip Guidance (15-second / 45-second rule)

- Questions 1, 2 and 6 should take under 15 seconds.
- Question 7 type (three replacements with odd fractions): skip in the first round if slow.

## 5. Examiner's Traps Seen Again and Again

1. Reversed ratio in the options.
2. Linear subtraction in replacement questions.
3. Simple average instead of weighted average.
