# Feynman Technique — Mixture and Alligation

## The Hardest Idea: "Why does the far price decide the near share?"

## A Story: The See-Saw

Riya (light) and her uncle (heavy) sit on a see-saw. To balance it, the uncle must sit **closer** to the middle and Riya **farther** away. The middle point is the mean price; the two prices are the two people.

In alligation, the quantity of each item behaves like distance on the see-saw — but swapped. The price that is **closer** to the mean needs **more** quantity, because it pulls the average less strongly per kilogram.

Chai: ₹200 is 40 away from ₹240; ₹300 is 60 away. So the ₹200 tea gets 60 parts and the ₹300 tea gets 40 parts → 3 : 2. **Each side takes the other side's distance.**

## The Unforgettable Trick: "Swap the distances"

Write the two distances from the mean, then swap them. That is the mixing ratio.
