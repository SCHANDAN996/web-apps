# Feynman Technique: Mixture and Alligation

Imagine a see-saw in a children's playground. A lighter child sits on one end, and a heavier child sits on the other. Where must the fulcrum be placed so that the board balances horizontally?
It must be placed closer to the heavier child! The ratio of their distances from the fulcrum is inversely proportional to their weights.

**Alligation is simply the physics of a see-saw applied to prices, concentrations, and averages!**

### 1. The Alligation Cross: Balancing Prices
Suppose you have cheap rice worth Rs. 20/kg and premium basmati rice worth Rs. 50/kg. You want to create a blend that sells for Rs. 30/kg.
- How far is Rs. 30 from the cheap rice? $|30 - 20| = 10$.
- How far is Rs. 30 from the premium rice? $|50 - 30| = 20$.

Because Rs. 30 is much closer to Rs. 20 than to Rs. 50, your mixture must contain much more of the cheap rice!
In fact, the ratio of cheap rice to premium rice is exactly the inverse of those distances:
$$\frac{\text{Cheap Rice}}{\text{Premium Rice}} = \frac{20}{10} = \frac{2}{1}$$
You mix 2 kilograms of cheap rice for every 1 kilogram of premium rice. That is all the "Alligation Cross" does!

### 2. The Dilution Mystery: Why doesn't it go to zero?
Suppose you have a bucket with 10 liters of pure red paint.
You take out 1 liter of red paint and pour in 1 liter of clear water. Now 90% of the bucket is paint.
Now you stir it thoroughly, take out another 1 liter of the mixture, and pour in 1 liter of water.
Did you take out 1 full liter of red paint this time?
NO! Because the liquid was already diluted, that 1-liter scoop contained only 0.9 liters of red paint and 0.1 liters of water!
So each time you scoop, you remove a fraction of the *remaining* red paint:
- After 1st scoop: $10 \times (1 - 1/10) = 9 \text{ liters}$
- After 2nd scoop: $9 \times (1 - 1/10) = 8.1 \text{ liters}$
- After $n$ scoops: $10 \times (0.9)^n \text{ liters}$

This explains the famous formula: $\text{Remaining} = x \left(1 - \frac{y}{x}\right)^n$. It is repeated percentage reduction!

### 3. Alligation is Everywhere
Whenever you have two groups with different averages being combined into a single grand group, Alligation will tell you the ratio of the group sizes:
- Men's average score vs Women's average score $\implies$ Ratio of number of Men to Women.
- Car speed vs Train speed $\implies$ Ratio of time spent in Car vs Train.
- Part of money at 5% interest vs part at 10% interest $\implies$ Ratio of the two investment amounts.

See the world as mixtures and see-saws, and alligation problems will solve themselves in seconds!
