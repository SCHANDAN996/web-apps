# Important Formulas & Approaches — Mixture and Alligation

## 1. Key Formulas

| Formula | Use | Example |
|---------|-----|---------|
| Mean = Σ(q × c) ÷ Σq | Average price of a mixture | 3 kg at ₹40 + 2 kg at ₹50 → ₹44 |
| Cheaper : Dearer = (d − m) : (m − c) | Mixing ratio | ₹200, ₹300, mean ₹240 → 3 : 2 |
| Left = V (1 − x/V)ⁿ | Repeated replacement | 80 L, 20 L twice → 45 L |
| Concentration = Σ(V × %) ÷ ΣV | Mixing two solutions | 20 L of 10% + 30 L of 20% → 16% |
| New ratio after adding | Find each part, add, simplify | 40 : 10 + 10 water → 2 : 1 |
| Same rule for speeds, marks, profits | Any two-group average | 10% profit on part, 20% on rest |

## 2. Time Management

| Rule | What to do |
|------|------------|
| 15-second rule | Direct alligation and mean-price questions |
| 45-second rule | Three-item mixtures or replacement with odd fractions — mark and return if slow |
| Option check | The reversed ratio is a common wrong option |

## 3. Logical Shortcuts

| Shortcut | Meaning |
|----------|---------|
| Mean is nearer to the larger share | If the mean is closer to ₹200, more of the ₹200 item is used |
| Equal quantities → simple average | Only then can you average the prices directly |
| Replacement factor (1 − x/V) | Multiply once for each repetition |
