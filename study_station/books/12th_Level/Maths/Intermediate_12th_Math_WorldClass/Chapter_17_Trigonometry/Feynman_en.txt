# Trigonometry, Explained Like You're 12

## The Hardest Concept: Why does sin²θ + cos²θ = 1 ALWAYS work?

Most students memorize this identity like a phone number. But nobody tells them *why* it's true. Let's fix that — with a story about a ladder.

## 🪜 The Ladder Story

Imagine you have a ladder that is exactly **1 metre long**. You lean it against a wall.

- The bottom of the ladder slides along the **ground** — that distance is **cos θ**.
- The top of the ladder touches the **wall** at some height — that height is **sin θ**.
- The ladder itself is the slant — its length is **1**.

Now here's the magic: the ground, the wall, and the ladder form a **right-angled triangle**. Always. No matter how you tilt the ladder.

And what does our old friend Pythagoras say about right triangles?

**(ground)² + (wall height)² = (ladder)²**

Substitute our names:

**(cos θ)² + (sin θ)² = (1)² = 1**

That's it. That's the whole identity. It's not a formula to memorize — it's just **Pythagoras wearing a fancy dress**. 🎭

## Try it yourself

- Ladder almost flat on the ground (θ tiny): ground ≈ 1, height ≈ 0 → 1² + 0² = 1 ✔
- Ladder at 45°: ground = height = 1/√2 → (1/√2)² + (1/√2)² = ½ + ½ = 1 ✔
- Ladder almost straight up (θ ≈ 90°): ground ≈ 0, height ≈ 1 → 0² + 1² = 1 ✔

Tilt it any way you like. The ladder never grows or shrinks, so the sum never changes. **The ladder is the boss, and its length is always 1.**

## 🎯 The Trick You'll Never Forget

> **"The Ladder Never Lies."**
>
> Whenever you see sin²θ + cos²θ, picture the 1-metre ladder against the wall. Ground² + Wall² = Ladder² = 1. Every other identity in trigonometry is just this ladder story told in different words:
> - Divide by cos²θ → 1 + tan²θ = sec²θ
> - Divide by sin²θ → 1 + cot²θ = cosec²θ

One ladder. Three identities. Zero memorization.
