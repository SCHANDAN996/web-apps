101. If one root of the equation x² − 6x + k = 0 is 2, then the value of k is
(a) 6 (b) 12 (c) 10 (d) 8
Answer: (d)
Solution: The tempting wrong option 12 comes from mis-solving after substitution. Substituting x = 2: 4 − 12 + k = 0 gives k = 8. Check: the roots are 2 and 4, whose product is 8, so the answer is 8.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Mathematics

102. The discriminant of the quadratic equation 3x² − 4x + 2 = 0 is
(a) −8 (b) 8 (c) −4 (d) 40
Answer: (a)
Solution: The tempting wrong option 40 comes from computing b² + 4ac instead of b² − 4ac. Here D = (−4)² − 4(3)(2) = 16 − 24 = −8, so the answer is −8.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Mathematics

103. If the roots of x² − px + 36 = 0 are equal, then the positive value of p is
(a) 6 (b) 9 (c) 12 (d) 18
Answer: (c)
Solution: The tempting wrong option 6 comes from taking the square root of 36 directly. For equal roots, D = 0, so p² − 144 = 0, giving p = ±12; the positive value is 12, so the answer is 12.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Mathematics

104. The sum of the roots of 2x² − 7x + 3 = 0 is
(a) −3/2 (b) 7/2 (c) 3/2 (d) −7/2
Answer: (b)
Solution: The tempting wrong option −7/2 comes from forgetting the sign rule: sum of roots = −b/a. Here sum = −(−7)/2 = 7/2, so the answer is 7/2.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Mathematics

105. The product of the roots of 5x² + 3x − 2 = 0 is
(a) −2/5 (b) 2/5 (c) 3/5 (d) −3/5
Answer: (a)
Solution: The tempting wrong option 2/5 ignores the negative sign of the constant term. Product of roots = c/a = −2/5, so the answer is −2/5.

106. The roots of x² − 5x + 6 = 0 are
(a) 1 and 6 (b) −2 and −3 (c) 5 and 1 (d) 2 and 3
Answer: (d)
Solution: The tempting wrong option −2 and −3 gives the correct product 6 but the wrong sum −5. We need two numbers with sum 5 and product 6, which are 2 and 3, so the answer is 2 and 3.

107. The 10th term of the AP 3, 7, 11, 15, … is
(a) 39 (b) 37 (c) 41 (d) 43
Answer: (a)
Solution: The tempting wrong option 43 comes from using n = 10 as the multiplier of d instead of n − 1 = 9. Here a = 3, d = 4, so a₁₀ = 3 + 9 × 4 = 39, so the answer is 39.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Mathematics

108. The sum of the first 20 terms of the AP 1, 4, 7, 10, … is
(a) 560 (b) 580 (c) 590 (d) 600
Answer: (c)
Solution: The tempting wrong option 600 comes from rounding the last term to 60. Here a = 1, d = 3, a₂₀ = 1 + 19 × 3 = 58, so S₂₀ = 20/2 × (1 + 58) = 10 × 59 = 590, so the answer is 590.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Mathematics

109. Which term of the AP 5, 9, 13, 17, … is 81?
(a) 21st (b) 19th (c) 18th (d) 20th
Answer: (d)
Solution: The tempting wrong option 21st comes from dividing 81 by 4 and rounding. Solve 5 + (n − 1) × 4 = 81, so (n − 1) × 4 = 76, n − 1 = 19, n = 20, so the answer is 20th.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Mathematics

110. The 5th term of the GP 2, 6, 18, … is
(a) 54 (b) 108 (c) 162 (d) 486
Answer: (c)
Solution: The tempting wrong option 486 is the 6th term, obtained by multiplying one extra time by 3. Here a = 2, r = 3, so a₅ = 2 × 3⁴ = 2 × 81 = 162, so the answer is 162.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Mathematics

111. The sum of the first 5 terms of the GP 3, 6, 12, … is
(a) 90 (b) 99 (c) 96 (d) 93
Answer: (d)
Solution: The tempting wrong option 96 comes from taking the 6th term 96 as the sum. Using Sₙ = a(rⁿ − 1)/(r − 1): S₅ = 3(2⁵ − 1)/(2 − 1) = 3 × 31 = 93, so the answer is 93.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Mathematics

112. If the sum and product of the roots of a quadratic equation are 7 and 12 respectively, the equation is
(a) x² + 7x + 12 = 0 (b) x² − 7x − 12 = 0 (c) x² + 7x − 12 = 0 (d) x² − 7x + 12 = 0
Answer: (d)
Solution: The tempting wrong option x² + 7x + 12 = 0 has roots −3 and −4, whose sum is −7, not 7. The standard form is x² − (sum)x + (product) = 0, giving x² − 7x + 12 = 0, so the answer is x² − 7x + 12 = 0.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Mathematics

113. The nature of the roots of x² + 4x + 5 = 0 is
(a) real and distinct (b) not real (c) real and equal (d) rational
Answer: (b)
Solution: The tempting wrong option "real and distinct" comes from miscomputing the discriminant as positive. Here D = 16 − 20 = −4 < 0, so the roots are not real, so the answer is not real.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Mathematics

114. If α and β are roots of x² − 3x + 2 = 0, then α² + β² equals
(a) 7 (b) 5 (c) 9 (d) 13
Answer: (b)
Solution: The tempting wrong option 9 comes from squaring the sum 3 to get 9 without subtracting 2αβ. Use α² + β² = (α + β)² − 2αβ = 9 − 4 = 5, so the answer is 5.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Mathematics

115. The sum of the first 15 natural numbers is
(a) 105 (b) 120 (c) 110 (d) 136
Answer: (b)
Solution: The tempting wrong option 105 is the sum of the first 14 natural numbers. Using n(n + 1)/2 = 15 × 16/2 = 120, so the answer is 120.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Mathematics

116. The common difference of the AP whose nth term is 4n + 1 is
(a) 4 (b) 3 (c) 5 (d) 1
Answer: (a)
Solution: The tempting wrong option 1 comes from reading the constant term instead of the coefficient of n. The common difference equals the coefficient of n, which is 4 (the terms are 5, 9, 13, …), so the answer is 4.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Mathematics

117. The roots of x² + 2x − 15 = 0 are
(a) 3 and 5 (b) 3 and −5 (c) −3 and 5 (d) −3 and −5
Answer: (b)
Solution: The tempting wrong option −3 and 5 gives the correct product −15 but the wrong sum +2. We need sum −2 and product −15, so the roots are 3 and −5, so the answer is 3 and −5.

118. The 8th term of the GP 5, 10, 20, … is
(a) 320 (b) 1280 (c) 640 (d) 2560
Answer: (c)
Solution: The tempting wrong option 1280 is the 9th term, from one extra multiplication by 2. Here a₈ = 5 × 2⁷ = 5 × 128 = 640, so the answer is 640.

119. If the roots of x² − kx + 16 = 0 are equal, then k equals
(a) 4 (b) ±8 (c) 16 (d) 8
Answer: (b)
Solution: The tempting wrong option 8 misses the negative value, which also satisfies the condition. For equal roots, k² − 64 = 0, so k = ±8, so the answer is ±8.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Mathematics

120. The sum of the infinite GP 1, 1/2, 1/4, … is
(a) 2 (b) 5/2 (c) 3/2 (d) 4
Answer: (a)
Solution: The tempting wrong option 3/2 comes from adding only the first three terms. Using S = a/(1 − r) = 1/(1 − 1/2) = 2, so the answer is 2.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Mathematics

121. If α and β are the roots of 2x² − 5x + 1 = 0, then the value of 1/α + 1/β is
(a) 5 (b) −5 (c) 5/2 (d) 2/5
Answer: (a)
Solution: The tempting wrong option 5/2 comes from stopping at the sum of the roots without dividing by the product. Here 1/α + 1/β = (α + β)/(αβ) = (5/2)/(1/2) = 5, so the answer is 5.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Mathematics

122. The sum of all two-digit numbers divisible by 9 is
(a) 540 (b) 495 (c) 630 (d) 585
Answer: (d)
Solution: The tempting wrong option 540 comes from taking only 9 terms instead of 10. The numbers are 18, 27, …, 99, so n = (99 − 18)/9 + 1 = 10 and S = 10/2 × (18 + 99) = 5 × 117 = 585, so the answer is 585.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Mathematics

123. If the equation x² − (k + 2)x + 2k = 0 has one root equal to 2, then the other root is
(a) 2k (b) k + 2 (c) k (d) k − 2
Answer: (c)
Solution: The tempting wrong option 2k confuses the product of roots with a root itself. Product of roots = 2k, and one root is 2, so the other root = 2k/2 = k, so the answer is k.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Mathematics

124. Three numbers in AP have sum 24 and product 440. The numbers are
(a) 5, 8, 11 (b) 4, 8, 12 (c) 6, 8, 10 (d) 3, 8, 13
Answer: (a)
Solution: The tempting wrong option 6, 8, 10 has the correct sum 24 but product 480, not 440. Let the numbers be 8 − d, 8, 8 + d; then 8(64 − d²) = 440 gives 64 − d² = 55, d² = 9, d = 3, so the numbers are 5, 8, 11, so the answer is 5, 8, 11.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Mathematics

125. The value of √(12 + √(12 + √(12 + … ∞))) is
(a) 3 (b) 12 (c) 4 (d) 6
Answer: (c)
Solution: The tempting wrong option 3 comes from solving x² = 12 instead of x² = 12 + x. Let x = √(12 + x); then x² − x − 12 = 0, so (x − 4)(x + 3) = 0, and since x > 0, x = 4, so the answer is 4.
Source: PYQ-style
