51. If the roots of the equation x² − 8x + k = 0 differ by 4, then the value of k is
(a) 16 (b) 10 (c) 8 (d) 12
Answer: (d)
Solution: Let the roots be α and α + 4. Sum of roots = 2α + 4 = 8, so α = 2 and the roots are 2 and 6. The tempting wrong value 16 comes from assuming equal roots, but here the difference is 4, so k = 2 × 6 = 12 and the answer is 12.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Mathematics

52. The sum of the first 20 terms of the AP 3, 7, 11, 15, … is
(a) 780 (b) 820 (c) 840 (d) 800
Answer: (b)
Solution: Here a = 3, d = 4, n = 20. S₂₀ = (20/2)[2(3) + 19(4)] = 10 × (6 + 76) = 10 × 82 = 820. The trap 800 tempts those who drop the 2a term and compute 10 × 80, so the answer is 820.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Mathematics

53. For what value of p does the equation 3x² + px + 12 = 0 have equal roots?
(a) ±6 (b) ±24 (c) ±9 (d) ±12
Answer: (d)
Solution: For equal roots, discriminant = 0: p² − 4(3)(12) = 0, so p² = 144 and p = ±12. The trap ±24 comes from doubling 12 or mishandling the square root of 144, so the answer is ±12.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Mathematics

54. The 10th term of the GP 2, 6, 18, 54, … is
(a) 59049 (b) 13122 (c) 19683 (d) 39366
Answer: (d)
Solution: Here a = 2, r = 3. T₁₀ = ar⁹ = 2 × 3⁹ = 2 × 19683 = 39366. The tempting wrong option 19683 is 3⁹ alone, forgetting to multiply by the first term 2, so the answer is 39366.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Mathematics

55. If α and β are the roots of 2x² − 5x + 3 = 0, then the value of α² + β² is
(a) 7/4 (b) 25/4 (c) 13/4 (d) 19/4
Answer: (c)
Solution: α + β = 5/2 and αβ = 3/2. Then α² + β² = (α + β)² − 2αβ = 25/4 − 3 = 13/4. The trap 25/4 is just (α + β)² without subtracting 2αβ, so the answer is 13/4.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Mathematics

56. How many terms of the AP 1, 3, 5, 7, … must be taken so that their sum is 400?
(a) 18 (b) 25 (c) 20 (d) 22
Answer: (c)
Solution: This is the AP of odd numbers with a = 1, d = 2, so Sₙ = (n/2)[2 + (n−1)2] = n². Setting n² = 400 gives n = 20. The trap 25 comes from miscomputing √400 or confusing the sum with 625, so the answer is 20.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Mathematics

57. The discriminant of the equation 4x² − 12x + 9 = 0 is
(a) 36 (b) −36 (c) 0 (d) 144
Answer: (c)
Solution: D = b² − 4ac = (−12)² − 4(4)(9) = 144 − 144 = 0, so the roots are real and equal (x = 3/2). The tempting 144 is b² alone, forgetting to subtract 4ac, so the answer is 0.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Mathematics

58. The sum of the infinite GP 1, 1/3, 1/9, 1/27, … is
(a) 4/3 (b) 5/3 (c) 2 (d) 3/2
Answer: (d)
Solution: Here a = 1, r = 1/3, and |r| < 1, so S∞ = a/(1 − r) = 1/(1 − 1/3) = 3/2. The trap 2 comes from wrongly using r = 1/2 in the formula, so the answer is 3/2.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Mathematics

59. If one root of the equation x² − 6x + k = 0 is twice the other, then k equals
(a) 6 (b) 8 (c) 12 (d) 9
Answer: (b)
Solution: Let the roots be α and 2α. Sum: 3α = 6, so α = 2 and the roots are 2 and 4. The tempting 12 comes from taking roots 2 and 6 by misreading "twice" as a difference of 4, so k = 2 × 4 = 8 and the answer is 8.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Mathematics

60. The 15th term of the AP whose 5th term is 19 and 9th term is 35 is
(a) 59 (b) 55 (c) 63 (d) 51
Answer: (a)
Solution: a + 4d = 19 and a + 8d = 35. Subtracting: 4d = 16, so d = 4 and a = 3. The trap 55 comes from using 13d instead of 14d, an off-by-one in the term formula, so T₁₅ = 3 + 56 = 59 and the answer is 59.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Mathematics

61. The quadratic equation whose roots are 3 + √2 and 3 − √2 is
(a) x² − 6x + 7 = 0 (b) x² + 6x + 7 = 0 (c) x² − 6x + 11 = 0 (d) x² − 6x − 7 = 0
Answer: (a)
Solution: Sum of roots = 6, product = 9 − 2 = 7, so the equation is x² − 6x + 7 = 0. The trap x² − 6x + 11 = 0 comes from adding 9 + 2 instead of subtracting in the product, so the answer is x² − 6x + 7 = 0.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Mathematics

62. The sum of the series 5 + 10 + 15 + … up to 20 terms is
(a) 1050 (b) 1000 (c) 1100 (d) 950
Answer: (a)
Solution: a = 5, d = 5, n = 20. S₂₀ = (20/2)[2(5) + 19(5)] = 10 × 105 = 1050. The tempting 1000 comes from miscounting 1 + 2 + … + 20 as 200 instead of 210, so the answer is 1050.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Mathematics

63. If the roots of x² + bx + 36 = 0 are equal, then b equals
(a) ±18 (b) ±6 (c) ±9 (d) ±12
Answer: (d)
Solution: Equal roots need D = 0: b² − 4(1)(36) = 0, so b² = 144 and b = ±12. The trap ±6 is √36, forgetting that the discriminant uses 4ac = 144, so the answer is ±12.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Mathematics

64. The third term of a GP is 12 and the sixth term is 96. The first term is
(a) 3 (b) 2 (c) 4 (d) 6
Answer: (a)
Solution: ar² = 12 and ar⁵ = 96. Dividing: r³ = 8, so r = 2 and a = 12/4 = 3. The trap 2 comes from confusing r with a since r = 2, so the answer is 3.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Mathematics

65. The value of k for which x² + kx + 64 = 0 has real and equal roots is
(a) ±32 (b) ±8 (c) ±16 (d) ±12
Answer: (c)
Solution: D = k² − 4(64) = 0, so k² = 256 and k = ±16. The tempting ±8 is √64, ignoring the factor 4 in 4ac, so the answer is ±16.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Mathematics

66. The sum of the first n terms of an AP is 3n² + 2n. Its common difference is
(a) 3 (b) 6 (c) 5 (d) 2
Answer: (b)
Solution: Tₙ = Sₙ − Sₙ₋₁ = (3n² + 2n) − (3(n−1)² + 2(n−1)) = 6n − 1, so T₁ = 5, T₂ = 11 and d = 6. The trap 3 comes from reading the coefficient of n² directly; the common difference is twice that coefficient, so the answer is 6.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Mathematics

67. If α and β are roots of x² − 7x + 12 = 0, the equation whose roots are 1/α and 1/β is
(a) 12x² − 7x + 1 = 0 (b) x² − 7x + 12 = 0 (c) 12x² + 7x + 1 = 0 (d) x² + 7x + 12 = 0
Answer: (a)
Solution: New sum = (α + β)/(αβ) = 7/12, new product = 1/(αβ) = 1/12, giving x² − (7/12)x + 1/12 = 0, i.e. 12x² − 7x + 1 = 0. The trap is keeping the original equation unchanged; reciprocating roots reverses the coefficients, so the answer is 12x² − 7x + 1 = 0.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Mathematics

68. The 8th term of the series 1, 4, 9, 16, 25, … is
(a) 64 (b) 56 (c) 49 (d) 72
Answer: (a)
Solution: The series is of perfect squares: Tₙ = n², so T₈ = 64. The trap 49 is T₇, from an off-by-one count, so the answer is 64.

69. For the equation 2x² − 4x + m = 0 to have real roots, the value of m must satisfy
(a) m ≤ 4 (b) m ≥ 2 (c) m ≤ 2 (d) m ≥ −2
Answer: (c)
Solution: Real roots need D ≥ 0: 16 − 8m ≥ 0, so m ≤ 2. The trap m ≥ 2 comes from forgetting to reverse the inequality when dividing by −8, so the answer is m ≤ 2.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Mathematics

70. The sum of all two-digit numbers divisible by 6 is
(a) 900 (b) 780 (c) 840 (d) 810
Answer: (d)
Solution: The numbers are 12, 18, …, 96 with n = (96 − 12)/6 + 1 = 15 terms. Sum = (15/2)(12 + 96) = 15 × 54 = 810. The trap 780 comes from taking n = 14 by miscounting the terms, so the answer is 810.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Mathematics

71. If the sum of the roots of x² − (k + 2)x + 2k = 0 is 6, then the product of the roots is
(a) 10 (b) 6 (c) 8 (d) 4
Answer: (c)
Solution: Sum of roots = k + 2 = 6, so k = 4 and product = 2k = 8. The trap 6 is confusing the product with the given sum, so the answer is 8.

72. The sum of the series 2 + 6 + 18 + … up to 6 terms is
(a) 486 (b) 728 (c) 720 (d) 1458
Answer: (b)
Solution: GP with a = 2, r = 3, n = 6. S₆ = a(rⁿ − 1)/(r − 1) = 2(729 − 1)/2 = 728. The trap 1458 is 2 × 3⁶, forgetting to subtract 1 in the numerator, so the answer is 728.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Mathematics

73. The roots of x² − 5x + 6 = 0 are in the ratio
(a) 1 : 6 (b) 2 : 3 (c) 3 : 5 (d) 1 : 2
Answer: (b)
Solution: Factoring: (x − 2)(x − 3) = 0, so the roots are 2 and 3, giving the ratio 2 : 3. The trap 1 : 6 comes from reading the constant term 6 as a root, so the answer is 2 : 3.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Mathematics

74. If the 7th term of an AP is 32 and the 12th term is 52, then the 20th term is
(a) 84 (b) 80 (c) 88 (d) 76
Answer: (a)
Solution: a + 6d = 32 and a + 11d = 52. Subtracting: 5d = 20, so d = 4 and a = 8. The trap 80 comes from using 18d instead of 19d, so T₂₀ = 8 + 76 = 84 and the answer is 84.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Mathematics

75. The number of real roots of the equation x² + 4x + 9 = 0 is
(a) 2 (b) 0 (c) 1 (d) infinite
Answer: (b)
Solution: D = 16 − 36 = −20 < 0, so the equation has no real roots. The trap 2 comes from assuming every quadratic has two real roots; a negative discriminant means none, so the answer is 0.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Mathematics
