# 🧒 Feynman Explanation — Fractions & Decimals

## The Hardest Concept: Why does dividing by a fraction make the answer BIGGER?

Ask any student: "What is 6 ÷ ½?" and most will shout "3!" — because dividing feels like making things smaller. But the real answer is **12**. Bigger! Let's fix this forever with a story.

---

## 🍕 The Pizza Party Story

Imagine you have **6 whole pizzas** and your friends are coming over.

**Round 1:** You cut each pizza into 2 pieces (each friend gets ½ pizza).
How many friends can you feed?

- Pizza 1 → 2 friends
- Pizza 2 → 2 friends
- … 6 pizzas × 2 halves = **12 friends!**

So **6 ÷ ½ = 12**. You didn't make the pizza smaller — you made the *pieces* smaller, so you got *more* pieces!

**Round 2:** Now each friend gets only ¼ pizza. How many friends?
6 pizzas × 4 quarters = **24 friends!** So **6 ÷ ¼ = 24**.

See the pattern? The smaller the piece, the MORE pieces you get. Division by a fraction is really asking: *"How many of these little pieces fit inside my number?"*

---

## 🔑 The Never-Forget Trick: "FLIP & MULTIPLY — The Door Trick"

Think of the division sign **÷** as a **door**. When a fraction walks through the door, it gets scared and **flips upside down** (reciprocal!), and the door itself turns into a **×** sign.

$$6 \div \frac{1}{2} \;\longrightarrow\; 6 \times \frac{2}{1} = 12$$

**Memory line:** *"Don't divide — just FLIP the second guy and multiply!"*

Same magic works for decimals, because decimals are just fractions in disguise:
- 0.5 = ½, 0.25 = ¼, 0.2 = ⅕
- So **6 ÷ 0.5 = 6 ÷ ½ = 6 × 2 = 12** ✅

---

## 🎯 One More "Aha!" — Recurring Decimals

Why does 1 ÷ 3 = 0.333… forever? Try sharing **1 chocolate bar equally among 3 friends** using only tenths, hundredths, thousandths… You give 0.3 each, a little is left. Break the leftover into hundredths, give 0.03 each, still a little left… **it never ends!** That's why ⅓ can never be written as a clean decimal — the chocolate never divides perfectly.

**Trick:** Fractions whose denominator (after simplifying) has only **2s and 5s** → decimal ends (terminates). Any other factor (3, 7, 11…) → decimal repeats forever. Why 2 and 5? Because 10 = 2 × 5, and our whole decimal system runs on 10!
