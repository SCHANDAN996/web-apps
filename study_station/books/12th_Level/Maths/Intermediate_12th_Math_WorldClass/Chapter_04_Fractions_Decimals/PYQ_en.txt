# 📊 PYQ Analysis — Fractions & Decimals

## How Exams Test This Chapter

Fractions and decimals appear in **every** major exam — SSC CGL, IBPS PO, RRB NTPC, CAT — but rarely as "solve this fraction" questions. Examiners hide them inside **simplification, comparison, and word problems**. Based on the pattern of questions asked over the years, here is what actually gets tested:

**The most repeated question types:**

1. **Simplification with BODMAS** — long expressions mixing fractions, decimals, brackets and "of". This is the single most common format. The trap is always the same: students multiply before resolving "of", or divide before handling brackets.

2. **Comparing/arranging fractions** — "Which is the largest/smallest?" or "Arrange in ascending order." The trap: fractions that look obviously ordered (like 3/4, 4/5, 5/6) but require actual comparison.

3. **Fraction of a quantity word problems** — "A man spends 2/5 of his salary on rent, 1/3 of the remainder on food..." The trap: applying the second fraction to the *original* amount instead of the *remainder*.

4. **Decimal operations** — multiplication/division by 0.1, 0.01, or finding values like 0.04 × 0.5. The trap: misplacing the decimal point by one place.

5. **Recurring decimals to fractions** — converting 0.363636... or 2.1̄6 into p/q form. The trap: forgetting that the number of 9s in the denominator equals the number of repeating digits.

6. **Missing number in fraction equations** — "If 3/4 of a number is 36, find 5/6 of it." The trap: finding the number but forgetting the final step.

7. **Continued/remainder problems** — "After giving away 1/4, then 1/3 of the remainder, what fraction is left?" The trap: adding the fractions instead of multiplying the remainders.

**The examiner's favourite psychological traps:**

- **Near-correct options**: If the answer is 7/12, options will include 5/12 (arithmetic slip) and 12/7 (inversion error).
- **The "of" trap**: "1/2 of 1/3 ÷ 1/4" — students who read "of" as an afterthought get it wrong.
- **Remainder confusion**: "1/3 of the remainder" silently changes the base — the most successful trap in this chapter.
- **Decimal place bait**: 0.2 × 0.3 = 0.6 is offered as an option to catch students who forget to count decimal places.

## Top 10 High-Yield Questions (with Solutions)

**Q1.** Simplify: 3/4 of 2/3 of 4/5 of 120

(a) 48  (b) 36  (c) 60  (d) 72

**Answer: (a)**
**Solution:** "Of" means multiply: (3/4) × (2/3) × (4/5) × 120. Cancel step by step: 3/4 × 2/3 = 1/2; 1/2 × 4/5 = 2/5; 2/5 × 120 = 48.
**Trap:** Students who compute 3/4 × 120 = 90 first, then lose track of the chain, often land on 60 or 72.

---

**Q2.** Which of the following fractions is the largest?

(a) 5/6  (b) 7/8  (c) 11/12  (d) 13/15

**Answer: (c)**
**Solution:** Three of the fractions — 5/6, 7/8, 11/12 — are of the form (n−1)/n = 1 − 1/n, where a larger denominator means a larger fraction; so among these, 11/12 is the largest. But 13/15 does **not** follow that pattern, so compare it directly: 11/12 = 0.9167 and 13/15 = 0.8667. Hence 11/12 is the largest.
**Trap:** The "bigger denominator = bigger fraction" shortcut fails here because 13/15 breaks the consecutive pattern — students who apply the shortcut blindly pick (d).

---

**Q3.** A man spends 2/5 of his income on rent and 1/4 of the remainder on food. What fraction of his income is left?

(a) 9/20  (b) 7/20  (c) 3/10  (d) 2/5

**Answer: (a)**
**Solution:** After rent, remainder = 1 − 2/5 = 3/5. Food = 1/4 of remainder = 1/4 × 3/5 = 3/20. Left = 3/5 − 3/20 = 12/20 − 3/20 = 9/20.
**Trap:** The classic remainder trap — taking 1/4 of the *original* income gives 1 − 2/5 − 1/4 = 7/20, which is option (b). Examiners deliberately include it.

---

**Q4.** The value of 0.2 × 0.3 × 0.04 is:

(a) 0.24  (b) 0.024  (c) 0.0024  (d) 0.00024

**Answer: (c)**
**Solution:** Multiply as integers: 2 × 3 × 4 = 24. Total decimal places = 1 + 1 + 2 = 4. So the answer is 0.0024.
**Trap:** Option (b) 0.024 catches students who count only 3 decimal places; option (a) catches those who ignore decimals entirely.

---

**Q5.** Express 0.363636... as a fraction:

(a) 4/11  (b) 36/99  (c) 36/100  (d) 3/8

**Answer: (a)**
**Solution:** Two repeating digits → denominator 99: 0.3636... = 36/99 = 4/11 (dividing by 9).
**Trap:** Option (b) 36/99 is mathematically equal but not in lowest terms — exams that ask for "simplest form" make (b) wrong. Always reduce.

---

**Q6.** If 3/5 of a number is 45, then 4/9 of the same number is:

(a) 75  (b) 100/3  (c) 33.33  (d) 27

**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** Number = 45 × 5/3 = 75. Then 4/9 of 75 = 300/9 = 100/3.
**Trap:** Option (a) 75 is the *number itself* — students who stop one step early pick it. This "incomplete answer" trap is extremely common.

---

**Q7.** Simplify: 2.5 × 4.8 ÷ 0.6

(a) 2  (b) 20  (c) 12  (d) 200

**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** 2.5 × 4.8 = 12. Then 12 ÷ 0.6 = 120 ÷ 6 = 20.
**Trap:** Dividing by 0.6 *increases* the value — students expecting a smaller answer second-guess themselves and pick 2 or 12.

---

**Q8.** A boy gave 1/4 of his money to his sister and 1/3 of the remainder to his brother. If he has ₹300 left, how much did he start with?

(a) ₹500  (b) ₹600  (c) ₹450  (d) ₹720

**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** Work backwards with remainders: after sister, 3/4 remains; after brother, 2/3 of that remains. Final fraction = 3/4 × 2/3 = 1/2. So 1/2 of money = ₹300 → original = ₹600.
**Trap:** Adding the fractions (1/4 + 1/3 = 7/12 given away, 5/12 left → ₹720) produces option (d). Multiplying remainders, not adding fractions, is the key.

---

**Q9.** The sum of 1/2, 1/6, 1/12, 1/20 is:

(a) 4/5  (b) 5/6  (c) 3/4  (d) 1

**Answer: (a)**
**Solution:** Rewrite each term: 1/2 = 1 − 1/2; 1/6 = 1/2 − 1/3; 1/12 = 1/3 − 1/4; 1/20 = 1/4 − 1/5. The series telescopes: 1 − 1/5 = 4/5.
**Trap:** Students who add mechanically with LCM 60 can still get 4/5, but under time pressure make arithmetic slips and land on 5/6. The telescoping pattern (1/n(n+1)) is a repeated exam favourite.

---

**Q10.** Which of the following is equal to 3.125?

(a) 25/8  (b) 25/4  (c) 13/4  (d) 31/8

**Answer: (a)**
**Solution:** 3.125 = 3 + 0.125 = 3 + 1/8 = 25/8. (Remember: 0.125 = 1/8, 0.25 = 1/4, 0.375 = 3/8.)
**Trap:** Option (d) 31/8 = 3.875 catches students who misread 0.125 as 0.875. Knowing standard decimal-fraction pairs by heart eliminates this trap.

---

## Key Takeaways for Exam Day

- **Master "of" chains and remainder problems** — together they dominate this chapter across SSC, Banking and Railways.
- **Memorise standard pairs**: 0.125 = 1/8, 0.375 = 3/8, 0.625 = 5/8, 0.875 = 7/8, 0.1666... = 1/6, 0.0833... = 1/12.
- **In remainder problems, multiply the fractions remaining** — never add the fractions given away.
- **Check whether the question asks for the number or a fraction of the number** — the "incomplete answer" trap is the cheapest mark-loser.
- **Recurring decimals**: repeating digits → that many 9s; mixed (like 0.166...) → 9s for repeating, 0s for non-repeating digits.
