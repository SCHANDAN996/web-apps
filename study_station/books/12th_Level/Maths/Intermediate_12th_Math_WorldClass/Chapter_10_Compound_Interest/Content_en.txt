# Compound Interest (चक्रवृद्धि ब्याज)

## Learning Goals

By the end of this chapter you will be able to:

1. Find the amount and compound interest for any number of years.
2. Handle half-yearly and quarterly compounding.
3. Find the difference between compound and simple interest in seconds.
4. Find the rate or time when the amount is given.
5. Apply the same formula to population growth and depreciation.

## The Hook: Interest on Interest

Put ₹10,000 in a bank at 10% a year. With **simple interest** you get ₹1,000 every year — ₹2,000 in two years. With **compound interest**, the first year's ₹1,000 is added to your money, so in the second year you earn 10% of ₹11,000 = ₹1,100. Total: ₹2,100. That extra ₹100 is **interest on interest** — and over many years it grows like a snowball.

## Why Students Struggle

- They use the simple-interest formula out of habit.
- They forget to halve the rate and double the time for half-yearly compounding.
- They do long multiplications instead of using shortcuts for 2 and 3 years.

🧠 **Active Recall:** In the example above, why is the second year's interest ₹1,100 and not ₹1,000?

## Chunk 1: The Main Formula

**Amount A = P (1 + R/100)ⁿ** and **CI = A − P**

where P = principal, R = rate per cent per year, n = number of years.

Example: P = ₹8,000, R = 5%, n = 2 years.
A = 8000 × (21/20)² = 8000 × 441/400 = ₹8,820. CI = ₹820.

## Chunk 2: Compounding More Than Once a Year

| Compounding | Rate per period | Number of periods |
|-------------|-----------------|-------------------|
| Yearly | R | n |
| Half-yearly | R/2 | 2n |
| Quarterly | R/4 | 4n |

Example: ₹10,000 for 1 year at 20% compounded half-yearly → 10% for 2 periods → A = 10000 × (1.1)² = ₹12,100, CI = ₹2,100 (not ₹2,000).

🧠 **Active Recall:** For quarterly compounding at 12% for 1 year, what are the rate per period and the number of periods?

## Chunk 3: CI − SI Shortcuts

- **2 years:** CI − SI = P (R/100)²
- **3 years:** CI − SI = P (R/100)² (3 + R/100)

Example: P = ₹5,000, R = 10%, 2 years → 5000 × (1/10)² = ₹50.

## Chunk 4: Finding Rate or Time

If A/P is a perfect square for 2 years, take the square root: ₹6,250 becomes ₹7,840 in 2 years → (1 + R/100)² = 7840/6250 = 784/625 → 1 + R/100 = 28/25 → R = 12%.

A sum that **doubles** in n years at compound interest becomes **4 times** in 2n years and **8 times** in 3n years.

## Chunk 5: Growth and Depreciation

- Population after n years = P (1 + R/100)ⁿ
- Value of a machine after n years = P (1 − R/100)ⁿ

**Mnemonic — "PRN-A":** Principal, Rate, n years → Amount. Growth adds R, depreciation subtracts R.

## Summary Table

| Situation | Formula |
|-----------|---------|
| Amount | P (1 + R/100)ⁿ |
| Compound interest | A − P |
| Half-yearly | rate R/2, periods 2n |
| CI − SI, 2 years | P (R/100)² |
| CI − SI, 3 years | P (R/100)² (3 + R/100) |
| Depreciation | P (1 − R/100)ⁿ |

## ⚠️ Caution: Examiner's Trap

1. **Simple interest in disguise:** the option equal to simple interest is always present. Compound interest is larger after the first year.
2. **Half-yearly confusion:** using the yearly rate for half-yearly compounding gives a smaller answer that is placed in the options.
3. **Amount vs interest:** the question asks for interest, but the amount appears as an option — subtract the principal.
