# Important Formulas & Approaches — Compound Interest

## 1. Key Formulas

| Formula | Use | Example |
|---------|-----|---------|
| A = P (1 + R/100)ⁿ | Amount after n years | ₹8,000 at 5% for 2 years → ₹8,820 |
| CI = A − P | Compound interest | ₹8,820 − ₹8,000 = ₹820 |
| Half-yearly: R/2 per period, 2n periods | Compounding twice a year | 20% for 1 year → 10% × 2 |
| Quarterly: R/4 per period, 4n periods | Compounding four times a year | 12% for 1 year → 3% × 4 |
| CI − SI (2 years) = P (R/100)² | Quick difference | ₹5,000, 10% → ₹50 |
| CI − SI (3 years) = P (R/100)² (3 + R/100) | Quick difference | ₹8,000, 5% → ₹61 |
| Growth: P (1 + R/100)ⁿ | Population, bacteria | 50,000 at 10% for 2 years → 60,500 |
| Depreciation: P (1 − R/100)ⁿ | Value of machines, cars | ₹40,000 at 10% for 2 years → ₹32,400 |
| Different rates each year: P (1 + R₁/100)(1 + R₂/100) | Changing rate | 10% then 20% → × 1.1 × 1.2 |
| Doubling in n years → 4 times in 2n, 8 times in 3n | Time questions | Doubles in 5 years → 4 times in 10 |

## 2. Time Management

| Rule | What to do |
|------|------------|
| 15-second rule | For CI − SI and 2-year amounts, use the shortcut formulas directly |
| 45-second rule | Rate/time questions with large numbers: if the ratio A/P is not a neat square or cube, mark and return |
| Option check | Eliminate the simple-interest and full-amount options first |
