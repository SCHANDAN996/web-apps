Card 1
Front: What is the formula for the amount under compound interest?
Back: A = P (1 + R/100)ⁿ, and CI = A − P.

Card 2
Front: Why is compound interest larger than simple interest after the first year?
Back: Each year's interest is added to the principal, so later years earn interest on interest.

Card 3
Front: How do you handle half-yearly compounding?
Back: Use half the rate (R/2) and double the number of periods (2n).

Card 4
Front: What is CI − SI for 2 years?
Back: P (R/100)². For ₹5,000 at 10%, it is ₹50.

Card 5
Front: What is CI − SI for 3 years?
Back: P (R/100)² (3 + R/100).

Card 6
Front: What does the mnemonic "PRN-A" stand for?
Back: Principal, Rate and n years give the Amount: A = P (1 + R/100)ⁿ.

Card 7
Front: How do you find the value of a machine after depreciation?
Back: Value = P (1 − R/100)ⁿ.

Card 8
Front: A sum doubles in 5 years at compound interest. When will it be 4 times?
Back: In 10 years — doubling twice.

Card 9
Front: What is (1.1)² and (1.1)³?
Back: 1.21 and 1.331 — worth remembering for 10% questions.

Card 10
Front: How do you find the rate if ₹6,250 becomes ₹7,840 in 2 years?
Back: (1 + R/100)² = 7840/6250 = 784/625, so 1 + R/100 = 28/25 and R = 12%.

Card 11
Front: Rates of 10% and 20% in two successive years — what is the amount factor?
Back: 1.1 × 1.2 = 1.32.

Card 12
Front: Examiner's trap: one option equals the simple interest. How do you avoid it?
Back: Remember CI is always greater than SI after the first year (for the same P, R and n).

Card 13
Front: Examiner's trap: the question asks for interest, but one option is the amount. What should you do?
Back: Subtract the principal — CI = A − P.

Card 14
Front: Quarterly compounding at 8% for 1 year — rate and periods?
Back: 2% per quarter for 4 quarters.

Card 15
Front: What is (1.2)²?
Back: 1.44 — so ₹10,000 at 20% for 2 years becomes ₹14,400.

Card 16
Front: When should you skip a CI question?
Back: When finding rate or time needs roots of awkward numbers and 45 seconds have passed — mark it and return.
