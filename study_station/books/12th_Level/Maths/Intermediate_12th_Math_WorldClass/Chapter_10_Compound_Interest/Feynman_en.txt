# Feynman Technique — Compound Interest

## The Hardest Idea: "Why does the money grow faster every year?"

## A Story: The Mango Tree Savings

Aman's grandfather gives him 100 mangoes and a deal: "Every year, I will add 10% more mangoes to whatever you have."

- Year 1: 10% of 100 = 10 new mangoes → 110.
- Year 2: 10% of **110** = 11 new mangoes → 121.
- Year 3: 10% of **121** = 12.1 → about 133.

Aman's friend Riya gets a different deal: "10 mangoes every year, fixed." After 3 years she has 130. Aman has 133 — and the gap keeps growing, because Aman's new mangoes also earn mangoes.

Riya's deal is **simple interest**. Aman's deal is **compound interest**: the reward is added to the pile, and next year's reward is worked out on the bigger pile.

## The Unforgettable Trick: "The snowball rolls on itself"

A snowball rolling downhill picks up snow on the snow it already picked up. Compound interest is the same: **interest on principal plus interest on interest.** That is why the formula multiplies by (1 + R/100) again and again instead of adding the same amount.
