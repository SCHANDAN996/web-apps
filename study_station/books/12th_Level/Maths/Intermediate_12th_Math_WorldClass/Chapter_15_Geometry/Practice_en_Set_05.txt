101. In a triangle ABC, the bisector of angle A meets BC at D. If AB = 8 cm, AC = 6 cm and BC = 7 cm, then BD is
(a) 3 cm (b) 4 cm (c) 3.5 cm (d) 4.5 cm
Answer: (b)
Solution: Many students split BC equally and pick 3.5 cm, but the angle bisector divides the opposite side in the ratio of the adjacent sides, not equally. By the Angle Bisector Theorem, BD : DC = AB : AC = 8 : 6 = 4 : 3, so BD = 7 × 4/7 = 4 cm. The answer is 4 cm.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Mathematics (Triangles)

102. Two circles of radii 5 cm and 3 cm touch each other externally. The length of their direct common tangent is
(a) 2√15 cm (b) 8 cm (c) 4√3 cm (d) √34 cm
Answer: (a)
Solution: A common slip is to use the distance between centres, 8 cm, as the tangent length, but the tangent is shorter than the line joining centres. Direct common tangent = √(d² − (r₁ − r₂)²) = √(64 − 4) = √60 = 2√15 cm. The answer is 2√15 cm.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Mathematics (Circles)

103. The angles of a triangle are in the ratio 2 : 3 : 4. The largest angle of the triangle is
(a) 60° (b) 100° (c) 90° (d) 80°
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is assuming the largest ratio part equals 90° without computing the value of one part. Sum of ratio parts = 9, and the angles sum to 180°, so each part = 20° and the largest angle = 4 × 20° = 80°. The answer is 80°.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Mathematics (Triangles)

104. In a circle of radius 10 cm, a chord of length 16 cm is drawn. The distance of the chord from the centre is
(a) 4 cm (b) 8 cm (c) 5 cm (d) 6 cm
Answer: (d)
Solution: The tempting error is to use the full chord 16 in the Pythagoras step, but the perpendicular from the centre bisects the chord, so we use half of it. Distance = √(10² − 8²) = √(100 − 64) = √36 = 6 cm. The answer is 6 cm.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Mathematics (Circles)

105. The diagonals of a rhombus are 24 cm and 10 cm. Its side is
(a) 13 cm (b) 17 cm (c) 12 cm (d) 26 cm
Answer: (a)
Solution: A frequent mistake is to use the full diagonals in Pythagoras, giving 26 cm, but the diagonals of a rhombus bisect each other at right angles, so we use halves. Side = √(12² + 5²) = √(144 + 25) = √169 = 13 cm. The answer is 13 cm.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Mathematics (Quadrilaterals)

106. The exterior angle of a regular polygon is 40°. The number of sides of the polygon is
(a) 8 (b) 9 (c) 10 (d) 12
Answer: (b)
Solution: Some students divide 360° by the interior angle instead of the exterior angle; here the exterior angle is directly given as 40°. Number of sides = 360° ÷ 40° = 9. The answer is 9.
Source: NCERT Class 8 Mathematics (Understanding Quadrilaterals)

107. In triangle ABC, angle A = 60°, angle B = 70°. The side opposite the largest angle is
(a) AB (b) cannot be determined (c) BC (d) AC
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is to assume the side opposite angle A is largest because it is named first; we must first find all three angles. Angle C = 180° − 60° − 70° = 50°, so the largest angle is B = 70°, and the side opposite angle B is AC. The answer is AC.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Mathematics (Triangles)

108. The area of an equilateral triangle is 16√3 cm². Its perimeter is
(a) 24 cm (b) 12 cm (c) 36 cm (d) 48 cm
Answer: (a)
Solution: A common error is to forget the √3/4 factor and solve a² = 16√3 directly. Using (√3/4)a² = 16√3 gives a² = 64, so a = 8 cm and perimeter = 3 × 8 = 24 cm. The answer is 24 cm.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Mathematics (Heron's Formula / Areas)

109. Two parallel chords of a circle are on the same side of the centre. The radius is 13 cm and the chords are 24 cm and 10 cm long. The distance between the chords is
(a) 7 cm (b) 12 cm (c) 17 cm (d) 5 cm
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is to add the two distances (giving 17 cm), which is correct only when chords are on opposite sides of the centre; here they are on the same side, so we subtract. Distances from centre: √(169 − 144) = 5 cm and √(169 − 25) = 12 cm; distance between chords = 12 − 5 = 7 cm. The answer is 7 cm.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Mathematics (Circles)

110. In a right-angled triangle, the hypotenuse is 25 cm and one leg is 7 cm. The other leg is
(a) 20 cm (b) 18 cm (c) 26 cm (d) 24 cm
Answer: (d)
Solution: Students sometimes add the squares instead of subtracting, getting √674, but the hypotenuse is the largest side, so we subtract. Other leg = √(25² − 7²) = √(625 − 49) = √576 = 24 cm. The answer is 24 cm.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Mathematics (Triangles)

111. The angle subtended by an arc at the centre of a circle is 100°. The angle subtended by the same arc at any point on the remaining part of the circle is
(a) 100° (b) 80° (c) 50° (d) 200°
Answer: (c)
Solution: The tempting wrong choice is 100°, assuming the angles are equal, but the angle at the centre is double the angle at the circumference standing on the same arc. Required angle = 100° ÷ 2 = 50°. The answer is 50°.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Mathematics (Circles)

112. The ratio of the areas of two similar triangles is 9 : 16. The ratio of their corresponding sides is
(a) 9 : 16 (b) 81 : 256 (c) 3 : 4 (d) 4 : 3
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is to keep the ratio as 9 : 16, but areas of similar triangles are in the ratio of the squares of corresponding sides. So the sides ratio = √9 : √16 = 3 : 4. The answer is 3 : 4.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Mathematics (Triangles)

113. A tangent is drawn to a circle of radius 6 cm from a point 10 cm away from the centre. The length of the tangent is
(a) 4 cm (b) √136 cm (c) 8 cm (d) 16 cm
Answer: (c)
Solution: A common slip is to add the squares (giving √136), but the radius is perpendicular to the tangent, forming a right triangle where 10 cm is the hypotenuse. Tangent = √(10² − 6²) = √(100 − 36) = √64 = 8 cm. The answer is 8 cm.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Mathematics (Circles)

114. In triangle PQR, PQ = QR and angle Q = 80°. Then angle P is
(a) 50° (b) 80° (c) 40° (d) 60°
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is to think angle P equals angle Q, but equal sides PQ and QR mean the angles opposite them, namely angle R and angle P, are equal. Angle P + angle R = 180° − 80° = 100°, so each is 50°. The answer is 50°.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Mathematics (Triangles)

115. The sum of interior angles of a polygon is 1260°. The number of sides is
(a) 8 (b) 9 (c) 7 (d) 10
Answer: (b)
Solution: Students often divide 1260° by 180° and report 7, forgetting the formula has (n − 2), not n. Solving (n − 2) × 180° = 1260° gives n − 2 = 7, so n = 9. The answer is 9.
Source: NCERT Class 8 Mathematics (Understanding Quadrilaterals)

116. ABCD is a cyclic quadrilateral. If angle A = 75°, then angle C is
(a) 75° (b) 105° (c) 115° (d) 95°
Answer: (b)
Solution: The tempting answer is 75°, assuming opposite angles are equal as in a parallelogram, but in a cyclic quadrilateral opposite angles are supplementary. Angle C = 180° − 75° = 105°. The answer is 105°.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Mathematics (Circles)

117. The medians of a triangle meet at point G. If a median AD is 18 cm long, then AG is
(a) 6 cm (b) 9 cm (c) 15 cm (d) 12 cm
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is to divide the median into two equal halves (9 cm), but the centroid divides each median in the ratio 2 : 1, with the longer part towards the vertex. AG = (2/3) × 18 = 12 cm. The answer is 12 cm.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Mathematics (Coordinate Geometry / Triangles)

118. In a triangle, the sides are 6 cm, 8 cm and 10 cm. The triangle is
(a) acute-angled (b) right-angled (c) obtuse-angled (d) equilateral
Answer: (b)
Solution: Some students call it acute because all sides look ordinary, but we must compare the square of the longest side with the sum of squares of the other two. Since 10² = 100 = 6² + 8² = 36 + 64, the triangle is right-angled. The answer is right-angled.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Mathematics (Triangles)

119. The length of the diagonal of a square is 8√2 cm. Its area is
(a) 64 cm² (b) 128 cm² (c) 32 cm² (d) 16 cm²
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is to square the diagonal directly (getting 128), but the area of a square equals half the square of its diagonal. Area = (8√2)² / 2 = 128/2 = 64 cm². The answer is 64 cm².
Source: NCERT Class 9 Mathematics (Areas)

120. From an external point, two tangents PA and PB are drawn to a circle. If angle APB = 60°, then angle AOB, where O is the centre, is
(a) 60° (b) 90° (c) 150° (d) 120°
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is to take angle AOB equal to angle APB, but the radius is perpendicular to the tangent, so angles OAP and OBP are each 90°. In quadrilateral OAPB, angle AOB = 360° − 90° − 90° − 60° = 120°. The answer is 120°.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Mathematics (Circles)

121. In triangle ABC, D and E are points on AB and AC such that DE is parallel to BC. If AD = 2 cm, DB = 4 cm and AE = 3 cm, then EC is
(a) 5 cm (b) 4.5 cm (c) 6 cm (d) 8 cm
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is to use AD : AB instead of AD : DB; the Basic Proportionality Theorem uses the two segments of the same side. AD/DB = AE/EC gives 2/4 = 3/EC, so EC = 6 cm. The answer is 6 cm.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Mathematics (Triangles)

122. Two circles of radii 13 cm each intersect such that their common chord is 24 cm long. The distance between their centres is
(a) 26 cm (b) 10 cm (c) 12 cm (d) 5 cm
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is to report the distance from one centre to the chord (5 cm) as the answer, but the line of centres bisects the common chord, and each centre is 5 cm from the chord on opposite sides. Distance from one centre to chord = √(13² − 12²) = √25 = 5 cm, so distance between centres = 2 × 5 = 10 cm. The answer is 10 cm.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Mathematics (Circles)

123. In a triangle ABC, the internal bisectors of angles B and C meet at I. If angle A = 70°, then angle BIC is
(a) 125° (b) 110° (c) 135° (d) 145°
Answer: (a)
Solution: A common error is to compute (180° − 70°)/2 = 55° and stop, but that gives angle IBC + angle ICB, not angle BIC. Using the result angle BIC = 90° + A/2 = 90° + 35° = 125°. The answer is 125°.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Mathematics (Triangles)

124. A ladder 25 m long leans against a wall. The foot of the ladder is 7 m from the wall. If the foot slides 8 m further away from the wall, the top of the ladder slides down by
(a) 5 m (b) 8 m (c) 4 m (d) 6 m
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is to assume the top slides the same 8 m as the foot, but the ladder length stays fixed, so we must compute both heights. Initial height = √(625 − 49) = 24 m; new foot distance = 15 m, new height = √(625 − 225) = 20 m; slide = 24 − 20 = 4 m. The answer is 4 m.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Mathematics (Triangles)

125. In a circle, chord AB and chord CD intersect at point P inside the circle. If AP = 4 cm, PB = 6 cm and CP = 3 cm, then PD is
(a) 9 cm (b) 4.5 cm (c) 8 cm (d) 6 cm
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is to set up a ratio like AP/PB = CP/PD, but for chords intersecting inside a circle the products of the segments are equal: AP × PB = CP × PD. So 4 × 6 = 3 × PD, giving PD = 8 cm. The answer is 8 cm.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Mathematics (Circles)
