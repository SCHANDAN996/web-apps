51. In a triangle ABC, the angles are in the ratio 2 : 3 : 4. What is the measure of the largest angle?
(a) 60° (b) 100° (c) 80° (d) 120°
Answer: (c)
Solution: The tempting wrong choice 120° comes from wrongly taking the ratio parts as degrees directly scaled by 30. Sum of angles of a triangle is 180°, and total parts = 2 + 3 + 4 = 9, so each part = 20°. Largest angle = 4 × 20° = 80°, so the answer is 80°.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Mathematics

52. Two parallel lines are cut by a transversal. If one of the interior angles on the same side of the transversal is 65°, the other interior angle on the same side is:
(a) 65° (b) 115° (c) 125° (d) 25°
Answer: (b)
Solution: Many students pick 65° thinking of corresponding angles, but interior angles on the same side of a transversal are supplementary, not equal. So the required angle = 180° − 65° = 115°, so the answer is 115°.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Mathematics

53. In a circle of radius 10 cm, a chord of length 16 cm is drawn. The distance of the chord from the centre is:
(a) 4 cm (b) 8 cm (c) 5 cm (d) 6 cm
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap answer 8 cm comes from halving the chord and forgetting the radius. The perpendicular from the centre bisects the chord, giving a right triangle with hypotenuse 10 cm and one leg 8 cm. Distance = √(10² − 8²) = √36 = 6 cm, so the answer is 6 cm.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Mathematics

54. The angle subtended by an arc at the centre of a circle is 100°. The angle subtended by the same arc at any point on the remaining part of the circle is:
(a) 100° (b) 200° (c) 50° (d) 25°
Answer: (c)
Solution: Choosing 100° is the common trap of assuming the angles are equal. The angle at the centre is double the angle at the circumference standing on the same arc, so the required angle = 100°/2 = 50°, so the answer is 50°.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Mathematics

55. In triangle ABC, AB = AC and angle A = 40°. Then angle B equals:
(a) 100° (b) 40° (c) 80° (d) 70°
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is 40°, assuming the base angles equal the vertex angle. Since AB = AC, the angles opposite them are equal: angle C = angle B. So 2 × angle B + 40° = 180°, giving angle B = 70°, so the answer is 70°.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Mathematics

56. The diagonals of a rhombus are 24 cm and 10 cm. Its side length is:
(a) 26 cm (b) 17 cm (c) 12 cm (d) 13 cm
Answer: (d)
Solution: Picking 26 cm comes from adding the half-diagonals instead of using Pythagoras. Diagonals of a rhombus bisect each other at right angles, so side = √(12² + 5²) = √169 = 13 cm, so the answer is 13 cm.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Mathematics

57. The sum of the interior angles of a polygon is 1260°. The number of sides of the polygon is:
(a) 8 (b) 7 (c) 10 (d) 9
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap answer 10 comes from dividing 1260 by 180 and then adding 2 unnecessarily. Using (n − 2) × 180° = 1260°, we get n − 2 = 7, so n = 9, so the answer is 9.
Source: NCERT Class 8 Mathematics

58. Each exterior angle of a regular polygon is 24°. The number of its sides is:
(a) 18 (b) 12 (c) 15 (d) 20
Answer: (c)
Solution: Choosing 12 comes from confusing exterior with interior angle division. The sum of exterior angles of any polygon is 360°, so number of sides = 360/24 = 15, so the answer is 15.
Source: NCERT Class 8 Mathematics

59. In a cyclic quadrilateral ABCD, angle A = 75°. Then angle C equals:
(a) 75° (b) 105° (c) 115° (d) 95°
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is 75°, wrongly applying "opposite angles are equal" which holds for parallelograms, not cyclic quadrilaterals. Opposite angles of a cyclic quadrilateral are supplementary, so angle C = 180° − 75° = 105°, so the answer is 105°.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Mathematics

60. Two circles of radii 5 cm and 3 cm touch each other externally. The distance between their centres is:
(a) 15 cm (b) 2 cm (c) 8 cm (d) 4 cm
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap answer 2 cm is the difference of radii, which applies when circles touch internally. For external touching, distance between centres = sum of radii = 5 + 3 = 8 cm, so the answer is 8 cm.

61. The length of a tangent drawn from a point 13 cm away from the centre of a circle of radius 5 cm is:
(a) 12 cm (b) 18 cm (c) 10 cm (d) 8 cm
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap 8 cm comes from subtracting 13 − 5 directly. The tangent is perpendicular to the radius at the point of contact, so tangent length = √(13² − 5²) = √144 = 12 cm, so the answer is 12 cm.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Mathematics

62. In triangle PQR, angle P = 50° and angle Q = 60°. The side opposite the largest angle is:
(a) PQ (b) PR (c) QR (d) Cannot be determined
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is picking QR because it is listed first among the "opposite-looking" sides. Angle R = 180° − 50° − 60° = 70° is the largest angle, and in a triangle the greatest side lies opposite the greatest angle; the side opposite angle R is PQ, so the answer is PQ.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Mathematics

63. The angles of a quadrilateral are in the ratio 1 : 2 : 3 : 4. The smallest angle is:
(a) 36° (b) 72° (c) 90° (d) 30°
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap 30° comes from dividing 360 by 12 instead of by the sum of ratio parts. Total parts = 1 + 2 + 3 + 4 = 10, and the angle sum of a quadrilateral is 360°, so each part = 36°. Smallest angle = 1 × 36° = 36°, so the answer is 36°.
Source: NCERT Class 8 Mathematics

64. In a right-angled triangle, the hypotenuse is 25 cm and one leg is 7 cm. The other leg is:
(a) 18 cm (b) 24 cm (c) 20 cm (d) 32 cm
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap 18 cm comes from subtracting 25 − 7. By Pythagoras, other leg = √(25² − 7²) = √(625 − 49) = √576 = 24 cm, so the answer is 24 cm.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Mathematics

65. The area of an equilateral triangle with side 6 cm is:
(a) 18 cm² (b) 12√3 cm² (c) 9√3 cm² (d) 36√3 cm²
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap 18 cm² comes from using half base times side instead of the proper height. Area = (√3/4) × side² = (√3/4) × 36 = 9√3 cm², so the answer is 9√3 cm².
Source: NCERT Class 9 Mathematics

66. In triangle ABC, D and E are midpoints of AB and AC respectively. If BC = 12 cm, then DE equals:
(a) 6 cm (b) 4 cm (c) 8 cm (d) 3 cm
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap 4 cm comes from dividing by 3, confusing the midpoint theorem with centroid division. By the midpoint theorem, the segment joining midpoints of two sides is half the third side: DE = 12/2 = 6 cm, so the answer is 6 cm.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Mathematics

67. The centroid of a triangle divides each median in the ratio:
(a) 1 : 2 (b) 2 : 1 (c) 1 : 1 (d) 3 : 1
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap 1 : 2 reverses the ratio. The centroid divides each median in the ratio 2 : 1, with the longer part (2) being from the vertex to the centroid, so the answer is 2 : 1.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Mathematics

68. Two triangles are similar with corresponding sides in the ratio 3 : 5. The ratio of their areas is:
(a) 3 : 5 (b) 5 : 3 (c) 27 : 125 (d) 9 : 25
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap 3 : 5 keeps the linear ratio, and 27 : 125 wrongly cubes it. The ratio of areas of similar triangles equals the square of the ratio of corresponding sides: 3² : 5² = 9 : 25, so the answer is 9 : 25.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Mathematics

69. In a parallelogram, one angle is 70°. The adjacent angle is:
(a) 110° (b) 120° (c) 70° (d) 90°
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap 70° applies "opposite angles are equal" to adjacent angles. Adjacent angles of a parallelogram are supplementary, so the adjacent angle = 180° − 70° = 110°, so the answer is 110°.
Source: NCERT Class 8 Mathematics

70. The number of diagonals in a hexagon is:
(a) 6 (b) 15 (c) 12 (d) 9
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap 12 comes from multiplying 6 by 2. Number of diagonals = n(n − 3)/2 = 6 × 3/2 = 9, so the answer is 9.
Source: NCERT Class 8 Mathematics

71. An angle is 30° more than its complement. The angle is:
(a) 30° (b) 60° (c) 45° (d) 75°
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap 30° is just the difference stated in the question. Let the angle be x; then x + (x − 30°) = 90°, giving 2x = 120°, so x = 60°, so the answer is 60°.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Mathematics

72. An angle is twice its supplement. The angle is:
(a) 90° (b) 120° (c) 60° (d) 150°
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap 60° is the supplement, not the angle itself. Let the angle be x; then x = 2(180° − x), so 3x = 360° and x = 120°, so the answer is 120°.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Mathematics

73. In triangle ABC, the bisectors of angles B and C meet at I. If angle A = 70°, then angle BIC equals:
(a) 125° (b) 145° (c) 135° (d) 110°
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap 110° comes from computing 180° − 70° directly. The standard result is angle BIC = 90° + angle A/2 = 90° + 35° = 125°, so the answer is 125°.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Mathematics

74. The perimeter of a semicircle of radius 7 cm is (take π = 22/7):
(a) 22 cm (b) 44 cm (c) 36 cm (d) 29 cm
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap 22 cm counts only the curved part πr and forgets the diameter. Perimeter of a semicircle = πr + 2r = 22 + 14 = 36 cm, so the answer is 36 cm.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Mathematics

75. In a circle, two equal chords are 8 cm from the centre. If the radius is 17 cm, the length of each chord is:
(a) 30 cm (b) 25 cm (c) 15 cm (d) 20 cm
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap 15 cm is only half the chord, obtained from the right triangle but not doubled. Half-chord = √(17² − 8²) = √225 = 15 cm, so full chord = 2 × 15 = 30 cm, so the answer is 30 cm.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Mathematics
