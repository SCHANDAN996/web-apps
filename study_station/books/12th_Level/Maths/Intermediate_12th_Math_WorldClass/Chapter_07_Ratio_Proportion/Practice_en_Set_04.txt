76. If A : B = 3 : 4 and B : C = 5 : 6, then A : C is equal to
(a) 3 : 6 (b) 15 : 24 (c) 5 : 8 (d) 1 : 2
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is writing 3 : 6 directly, ignoring that B has different values (4 and 5) in the two ratios. Make B common: A : B = 15 : 20 and B : C = 20 : 24, so A : C = 15 : 24 = 5 : 8. So the answer is 5 : 8.
Source: NCERT Class 8 Mathematics

77. Two numbers are in the ratio 7 : 11. If 7 is added to each, the ratio becomes 2 : 3. The smaller number is
(a) 77 (b) 42 (c) 35 (d) 49
Answer: (d)
Solution: Many students take the numbers as 7 and 11 directly, which fails the second condition. Let the numbers be 7x and 11x. Then (7x + 7)/(11x + 7) = 2/3, giving 21x + 21 = 22x + 14, so x = 7. The smaller number is 7 × 7 = 49. So the answer is 49.
Source: PYQ-style

78. The ratio of incomes of A and B is 5 : 4 and the ratio of their expenditures is 3 : 2. If each saves ₹1600, the income of A is
(a) ₹3600 (b) ₹4000 (c) ₹3200 (d) ₹5000
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is assuming savings are in ratio 2 : 2, i.e., equal differences without equations. Let incomes be 5x, 4x and expenditures 3y, 2y. Then 5x − 3y = 1600 and 4x − 2y = 1600. Solving, x = 800, so A's income = 5 × 800 = ₹4000. So the answer is ₹4000.
Source: PYQ-style

79. ₹7800 is divided among A, B and C in the ratio 1/2 : 1/3 : 1/4. The share of B is
(a) ₹2000 (b) ₹3600 (c) ₹2400 (d) ₹1800
Answer: (c)
Solution: The tempting error is treating 1/2 : 1/3 : 1/4 as 2 : 3 : 4 by inverting wrongly. Multiply by LCM 12: the ratio becomes 6 : 4 : 3. B's share = 7800 × 4/13 = ₹2400. So the answer is ₹2400.
Source: NCERT Class 8 Mathematics

80. If x : y = 2 : 3, then (5x + 2y) : (3x + 4y) is equal to
(a) 4 : 5 (b) 7 : 9 (c) 16 : 18 (d) 8 : 9
Answer: (d)
Solution: A common slip is substituting x = 2, y = 3 but miscomputing the denominator as 3(2) + 4(3) = 16 instead of 18. Numerator = 10 + 6 = 16, denominator = 6 + 12 = 18, so the ratio is 16 : 18 = 8 : 9. So the answer is 8 : 9.
Source: NCERT Class 8 Mathematics

81. The third proportional to 12 and 18 is
(a) 27 (b) 24 (c) 36 (d) 30
Answer: (a)
Solution: Students often confuse third proportional with mean proportional and compute √(12 × 18), which is not an integer here. Third proportional x satisfies 12 : 18 = 18 : x, so x = 18 × 18/12 = 27. So the answer is 27.

82. The mean proportional between 0.08 and 0.18 is
(a) 0.14 (b) 0.13 (c) 0.16 (d) 0.12
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is taking the average (0.13) instead of the geometric mean. Mean proportional = √(0.08 × 0.18) = √0.0144 = 0.12. So the answer is 0.12.
Source: NCERT Class 8 Mathematics

83. If a : b = 2 : 3, b : c = 4 : 5 and c : d = 6 : 7, then a : d is
(a) 12 : 35 (b) 4 : 7 (c) 16 : 35 (d) 8 : 15
Answer: (c)
Solution: The tempting shortcut of just multiplying first and last terms (2 × 6 : 3 × 7 = 12 : 21) is wrong because middle ratios must be chained. a/d = (2/3)(4/5)(6/7) = 48/105 = 16/35. So the answer is 16 : 35.
Source: PYQ-style

84. A bag contains ₹1, 50 paise and 25 paise coins in the ratio 3 : 4 : 12, amounting to ₹800. The number of 50 paise coins is
(a) 300 (b) 400 (c) 600 (d) 800
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is dividing ₹800 in ratio 3 : 4 : 12 directly, ignoring coin values. Let the coins be 3k, 4k, 12k; their value is 3k(1) + 4k(0.5) + 12k(0.25) = 3k + 2k + 3k = 8k = 800, so k = 100. Number of 50 paise coins = 4 × 100 = 400. So the answer is 400.
Source: PYQ-style

85. The ratio of boys to girls in a school is 4 : 5. When 100 girls leave, the ratio becomes 6 : 7. The number of boys is
(a) 1200 (b) 1000 (c) 1500 (d) 800
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is changing both terms of the ratio; only girls decrease. Let boys = 4x, girls = 5x. Then 4x/(5x − 100) = 6/7, giving 28x = 30x − 600, so x = 300. Boys = 4 × 300 = 1200. So the answer is 1200.
Source: PYQ-style

86. If (3a + 5b) : (3a − 5b) = 5 : 1, then a : b is
(a) 5 : 3 (b) 3 : 5 (c) 2 : 1 (d) 5 : 2
Answer: (d)
Solution: The common trap is treating 3a + 5b = 5 and 3a − 5b = 1 as absolute values instead of cross-multiplying. From 3a + 5b = 5(3a − 5b) = 15a − 25b, we get 30b = 12a, so a/b = 30/12 = 5/2. So the answer is 5 : 2.
Source: PYQ-style

87. ₹1020 is divided among A, B, C such that A gets 2/3 of what B gets and B gets 1/4 of what C gets. The share of C is
(a) ₹440 (b) ₹720 (c) ₹550 (d) ₹660
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is writing A : B : C = 2 : 3 : 4 directly. A : B = 2 : 3 and B : C = 1 : 4 = 3 : 12, so A : B : C = 2 : 3 : 12, giving 17 parts. C's share = 1020 × 12/17 = ₹720. So the answer is ₹720.
Source: PYQ-style

88. The ratio of the ages of a father and son is 7 : 2. After 10 years the ratio will be 9 : 4. The present age of the father is
(a) 42 years (b) 28 years (c) 49 years (d) 35 years
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is adding 10 to only one age. Let ages be 7x and 2x. Then (7x + 10)/(2x + 10) = 9/4, giving 28x + 40 = 18x + 90, so x = 5. Father's age = 7 × 5 = 35 years. So the answer is 35 years.
Source: PYQ-style

89. If a/b = c/d = e/f = 2/3, then (2a + 3c + 4e) : (2b + 3d + 4f) is
(a) 3 : 2 (b) 2 : 3 (c) 4 : 9 (d) 9 : 4
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is squaring the ratio because three terms are involved. By the equal-ratios property, each of a, c, e is 2/3 of b, d, f, so any same-weighted combination keeps the ratio 2 : 3. So the answer is 2 : 3.
Source: NCERT Class 8 Mathematics

90. A mixture contains milk and water in the ratio 5 : 1. On adding 5 litres of water, the ratio becomes 5 : 2. The quantity of milk in the mixture is
(a) 25 litres (b) 20 litres (c) 30 litres (d) 16 litres
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is letting milk also change; only water increases. Let milk = 5x, water = x. Then 5x/(x + 5) = 5/2, giving 10x = 5x + 25, so x = 5. Milk = 5 × 5 = 25 litres. So the answer is 25 litres.
Source: PYQ-style

91. The fourth proportional to 5, 8 and 15 is
(a) 24 (b) 20 (c) 18 (d) 30
Answer: (a)
Solution: Students often compute the third proportional to 8 and 15 instead. Fourth proportional x satisfies 5 : 8 = 15 : x, so x = 8 × 15/5 = 24. So the answer is 24.

92. If x/2 = y/3 = z/5 and x + y + z = 100, then the value of y is
(a) 30 (b) 20 (c) 50 (d) 40
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is dividing 100 equally or in ratio 2 : 3 : 5 but picking the wrong term. Let x = 2k, y = 3k, z = 5k; then 10k = 100, k = 10, so y = 30. So the answer is 30.

93. Two numbers are in the ratio 3 : 5. If 9 is subtracted from each, the new ratio is 12 : 23. The larger number is
(a) 55 (b) 45 (c) 60 (d) 50
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is adding 9 instead of subtracting, which gives a negative or inconsistent x. Let numbers be 3x and 5x. Then (3x − 9)/(5x − 9) = 12/23, giving 69x − 207 = 60x − 108, so 9x = 99, x = 11. Larger number = 5 × 11 = 55. So the answer is 55.
Source: PYQ-style

94. The ratio of two numbers is 4 : 7 and their HCF is 6. Their LCM is
(a) 126 (b) 168 (c) 84 (d) 196
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is taking LCM = 4 × 7 = 28, forgetting the HCF multiplier. Numbers are 24 and 42; LCM = 4 × 7 × 6 = 168. So the answer is 168.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Mathematics

95. ₹3360 is divided among A, B and C such that A's share : B's share = 3 : 4 and B's share : C's share = 6 : 7. The share of A is
(a) ₹1080 (b) ₹960 (c) ₹864 (d) ₹1260
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is making B common without adjustment, writing 3 : 4 : 7. Convert: A : B = 9 : 12 and B : C = 12 : 14, so A : B : C = 9 : 12 : 14, giving 35 parts. A's share = 3360 × 9/35 = ₹864. So the answer is ₹864.
Source: PYQ-style

96. If A : B = 5 : 7 and B : C = 6 : 11, then A : B : C is
(a) 15 : 21 : 22 (b) 5 : 7 : 11 (c) 30 : 42 : 66 (d) 30 : 42 : 77
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is writing 5 : 7 : 11, ignoring that B is 7 in one ratio and 6 in the other. LCM of 7 and 6 is 42: A : B = 30 : 42 and B : C = 42 : 77, so A : B : C = 30 : 42 : 77. So the answer is 30 : 42 : 77.
Source: NCERT Class 8 Mathematics

97. In a 60-litre mixture, the ratio of milk to water is 2 : 1. How much water must be added to make the ratio 1 : 2?
(a) 60 litres (b) 40 litres (c) 30 litres (d) 45 litres
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is changing milk too, or solving 40/(20 + w) = 1/2 as 2/1. Milk = 40 litres, water = 20 litres. Need 40/(20 + w) = 1/2, so 20 + w = 80, w = 60 litres. So the answer is 60 litres.
Source: PYQ-style

98. The salaries of A, B and C are in the ratio 2 : 3 : 5. If increments of 15%, 10% and 20% are given respectively, the new ratio is
(a) 3 : 5 : 7 (b) 23 : 33 : 50 (c) 23 : 33 : 60 (d) 46 : 66 : 120
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is adding percentages to ratio terms directly (17 : 13 : 25 style errors). New salaries: 2 × 1.15 = 2.3, 3 × 1.10 = 3.3, 5 × 1.20 = 6.0, giving 23 : 33 : 60. So the answer is 23 : 33 : 60.
Source: PYQ-style

99. If (a + b) : (b + c) : (c + a) = 6 : 7 : 8 and a + b + c = 14, then the value of c is
(a) 5 (b) 7 (c) 6 (d) 8
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is equating a + b = 6, b + c = 7, c + a = 8 directly to the sum 14 without scaling. Sum of the three pair-sums = 2(a + b + c) = 28, and 6 + 7 + 8 = 21 parts = 28, so 1 part = 4/3. Then a + b = 6 × 4/3 = 8, so c = 14 − 8 = 6. So the answer is 6.
Source: PYQ-style

100. A sum of money is divided among P, Q and R such that for every ₹2 that P gets, Q gets ₹3, and for every ₹4 that Q gets, R gets ₹5. If R gets ₹350 more than P, the total sum is
(a) ₹1600 (b) ₹1750 (c) ₹1500 (d) ₹1450
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is chaining P : Q = 2 : 3 and Q : R = 4 : 5 as 2 : 3 : 5. Make Q common: P : Q = 8 : 12 and Q : R = 12 : 15, so P : Q : R = 8 : 12 : 15. R − P = 7 parts = ₹350, so 1 part = ₹50 and the total = 35 × 50 = ₹1750. So the answer is ₹1750.
Source: PYQ-style
