1. The ratio of 25 to 75 in its simplest form is
(a) 1 : 3 (b) 1 : 2 (c) 2 : 3 (d) 3 : 1
Answer: (a)
Solution: Dividing both terms by their HCF 25 gives 1 : 3. The tempting option 2 : 3 comes from a wrong division of the terms, so the answer is 1 : 3.
Source: NCERT Class 6 Mathematics

2. If a : b = 3 : 4, then the value of 2a : 3b is
(a) 2 : 3 (b) 3 : 4 (c) 1 : 2 (d) 4 : 3
Answer: (c)
Solution: Let a = 3k and b = 4k. Then 2a : 3b = 6k : 12k = 1 : 2. The trap 2 : 3 comes from just writing the multipliers as a ratio, so the answer is 1 : 2.
Source: NCERT Class 6 Mathematics

3. Divide ₹600 between A and B in the ratio 2 : 3. A gets
(a) ₹240 (b) ₹200 (c) ₹300 (d) ₹360
Answer: (a)
Solution: Total parts = 5, so A gets (2/5) × 600 = ₹240. The tempting ₹300 is half the amount, which would be correct only for a 1 : 1 split, so the answer is ₹240.
Source: NCERT Class 6 Mathematics

4. Which of the following is equivalent to the ratio 4 : 5?
(a) 8 : 15 (b) 20 : 24 (c) 16 : 25 (d) 12 : 15
Answer: (d)
Solution: Multiplying both terms of 4 : 5 by 3 gives 12 : 15. The trap 16 : 25 comes from squaring the terms, which changes the ratio, so the answer is 12 : 15.
Source: NCERT Class 6 Mathematics

5. If 3 : 5 = x : 20, then x equals
(a) 10 (b) 15 (c) 12 (d) 18
Answer: (c)
Solution: x = (3 × 20)/5 = 12. The tempting 15 comes from subtracting 20 − 5, which ignores proportionality, so the answer is 12.
Source: NCERT Class 6 Mathematics

6. The ratio of boys to girls in a class is 5 : 4. If there are 25 boys, the number of girls is
(a) 20 (b) 16 (c) 18 (d) 24
Answer: (a)
Solution: Each part = 25/5 = 5, so girls = 4 × 5 = 20. The trap 24 comes from using 6 as the multiplier, so the answer is 20.
Source: NCERT Class 6 Mathematics

7. The third proportional to 4 and 8 is
(a) 12 (b) 20 (c) 16 (d) 32
Answer: (c)
Solution: Third proportional x satisfies 4 : 8 = 8 : x, so x = 8²/4 = 16. The tempting 12 comes from simply adding 8 + 4, so the answer is 16.
Source: NCERT Class 7 Mathematics

8. The mean proportional between 4 and 25 is
(a) 20 (b) 12 (c) 10 (d) 14.5
Answer: (c)
Solution: Mean proportional = √(4 × 25) = √100 = 10. The trap 14.5 is the arithmetic mean, not the geometric mean, so the answer is 10.
Source: NCERT Class 7 Mathematics

9. If a : b = 2 : 3 and b : c = 3 : 5, then a : c is
(a) 2 : 3 (b) 3 : 5 (c) 5 : 2 (d) 2 : 5
Answer: (d)
Solution: a : c = (a/b) × (b/c) = (2/3) × (3/5) = 2/5, so a : c = 2 : 5. The tempting 3 : 5 just copies the second ratio, so the answer is 2 : 5.
Source: NCERT Class 7 Mathematics

10. The ratio 1.5 : 2.5 in simplest form is
(a) 2 : 3 (b) 3 : 5 (c) 5 : 3 (d) 15 : 25
Answer: (b)
Solution: Multiply both by 10 to get 15 : 25, then divide by 5 to get 3 : 5. The trap 15 : 25 is not in simplest form, so the answer is 3 : 5.
Source: NCERT Class 6 Mathematics

11. Two numbers are in the ratio 7 : 9. If the smaller number is 35, the larger number is
(a) 45 (b) 42 (c) 49 (d) 40
Answer: (a)
Solution: Each part = 35/7 = 5, so the larger number = 9 × 5 = 45. The tempting 40 comes from adding 5 to 35 instead of scaling, so the answer is 45.
Source: NCERT Class 6 Mathematics

12. If 5 pens cost ₹75, the cost of 8 pens at the same rate is
(a) ₹100 (b) ₹130 (c) ₹110 (d) ₹120
Answer: (d)
Solution: Cost of one pen = 75/5 = ₹15, so 8 pens cost 8 × 15 = ₹120. The trap ₹110 comes from a wrong unit rate, so the answer is ₹120.
Source: NCERT Class 7 Mathematics

13. The ratio of 500 grams to 2 kilograms is
(a) 1 : 2 (b) 1 : 4 (c) 2 : 5 (d) 5 : 2
Answer: (b)
Solution: Convert to the same unit: 500 g : 2000 g = 1 : 4. The tempting 5 : 2 comes from comparing 500 with 2 without converting units, so the answer is 1 : 4.
Source: NCERT Class 6 Mathematics

14. If a : b = 5 : 7, then (3a + 2b) : (3a − 2b) equals
(a) 15 : 14 (b) 29 : 2 (c) 29 : 1 (d) 1 : 29
Answer: (c)
Solution: Let a = 5k, b = 7k. Then 3a + 2b = 29k and 3a − 2b = k, giving 29 : 1. The trap 15 : 14 uses only 3a and 2b separately, so the answer is 29 : 1.
Source: NCERT Class 7 Mathematics

15. The ages of Ram and Shyam are in the ratio 3 : 4. If Shyam is 16 years old, Ram's age is
(a) 10 years (b) 12 years (c) 14 years (d) 15 years
Answer: (b)
Solution: Each part = 16/4 = 4, so Ram's age = 3 × 4 = 12 years. The tempting 10 comes from using 2.5 as the multiplier, so the answer is 12 years.
Source: NCERT Class 6 Mathematics

16. Which ratio is the greatest among the following?
(a) 5 : 6 (b) 3 : 4 (c) 4 : 5 (d) 2 : 3
Answer: (a)
Solution: As decimals: 0.667, 0.75, 0.8, 0.833. The largest is 5 : 6. The trap 2 : 3 looks simple but is the smallest, so the answer is 5 : 6.

17. If x : y = 4 : 9, then x² : y² equals
(a) 16 : 81 (b) 4 : 9 (c) 2 : 3 (d) 8 : 27
Answer: (a)
Solution: Squaring both terms: 4² : 9² = 16 : 81. The tempting 8 : 27 comes from cubing, which applies to x³ : y³, so the answer is 16 : 81.

18. Divide ₹840 among three people in the ratio 2 : 3 : 7. The largest share is
(a) ₹140 (b) ₹210 (c) ₹420 (d) ₹490
Answer: (d)
Solution: Total parts = 12, so the largest share = (7/12) × 840 = ₹490. The trap ₹420 is half the amount, which is not the 7-part share, so the answer is ₹490.

19. The inverse ratio of 5 : 8 is
(a) 25 : 64 (b) 5 : 8 (c) 8 : 5 (d) 10 : 16
Answer: (c)
Solution: The inverse (reciprocal) ratio of 5 : 8 is 8 : 5. The tempting 10 : 16 is an equivalent ratio, not the inverse, so the answer is 8 : 5.

20. If 2x = 3y, then x : y equals
(a) 2 : 3 (b) 4 : 9 (c) 9 : 4 (d) 3 : 2
Answer: (d)
Solution: From 2x = 3y, x/y = 3/2, so x : y = 3 : 2. The trap 2 : 3 comes from reading the coefficients in the wrong order, so the answer is 3 : 2.
Source: NCERT Class 7 Mathematics

21. Two numbers are in the ratio 3 : 5. If 9 is added to each, the ratio becomes 2 : 3. The smaller number is
(a) 27 (b) 18 (c) 36 (d) 45
Answer: (a)
Solution: Let the numbers be 3k and 5k. Then (3k + 9)/(5k + 9) = 2/3, so 9k + 27 = 10k + 18, giving k = 9 and the smaller number = 3 × 9 = 27. The trap 18 comes from solving with k = 6 by an arithmetic slip in cross-multiplication, so the answer is 27.
Source: PYQ-style

22. The ratio of incomes of A and B is 5 : 4 and the ratio of their expenditures is 3 : 2. If each saves ₹1600, A's income is
(a) ₹3200 (b) ₹4000 (c) ₹4800 (d) ₹5600
Answer: (b)
Solution: Let incomes be 5k and 4k, expenditures 3m and 2m. Then 5k − 3m = 1600 and 4k − 2m = 1600. From the second, m = 2k − 800; substituting gives 5k − 6k + 2400 = 1600, so k = 800 and A's income = 5 × 800 = ₹4000. The trap ₹3200 is B's income, so the answer is ₹4000.
Source: PYQ-style

23. The ratio of three numbers is 2 : 3 : 4 and the sum of their squares is 725. The largest number is
(a) 15 (b) 20 (c) 10 (d) 25
Answer: (b)
Solution: Let the numbers be 2k, 3k, 4k. Then 4k² + 9k² + 16k² = 29k² = 725, so k² = 25 and k = 5. The largest number = 4 × 5 = 20. The tempting 25 comes from taking k² itself as the answer, so the answer is 20.
Source: PYQ-style

24. A bag contains 25-paise, 50-paise and 1-rupee coins in the ratio 8 : 4 : 1. If the total value is ₹50, the number of 25-paise coins is
(a) 40 (b) 60 (c) 100 (d) 80
Answer: (d)
Solution: Let the coins be 8k, 4k and k. Total value = 0.25(8k) + 0.5(4k) + 1(k) = 2k + 2k + k = 5k = 50, so k = 10. Number of 25-paise coins = 8 × 10 = 80. The trap 40 is the count of 50-paise coins, so the answer is 80.
Source: PYQ-style

25. If a : b = 2 : 3, b : c = 4 : 5 and c : d = 6 : 7, then a : d is
(a) 2 : 7 (b) 16 : 35 (c) 8 : 15 (d) 4 : 7
Answer: (b)
Solution: a : d = (2/3) × (4/5) × (6/7) = 48/105 = 16/35, so a : d = 16 : 35. The trap 2 : 7 just takes the first and last numbers, ignoring the chain, so the answer is 16 : 35.
Source: PYQ-style
