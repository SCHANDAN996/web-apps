• T = Trailing Zeroes: Hunt only prime factor 5 in factorials (twos are everywhere).

Trick 3: The 4-Power Guillotine (Unit Digit Cyclicity)
==================================================================
Whenever you see a huge power on {2, 3, 7, 8}, drop the guillotine on all but the last two digits of the exponent:
• Divide last two digits by 4.
• Remainder 1 -> Power 1
• Remainder 2 -> Power 2
• Remainder 3 -> Power 3
• Remainder 0 -> Power 4 (The guillotine resets at 4, never at 0!)

Trick 5: The Fermat Speedboat (Fermat's Little Theorem)
==================================================================
Picture a speedboat with a Prime engine "p".
Whenever the passenger "a" has fuel power exactly equal to (p - 1):
a^(p - 1) modulo p = 1.
The speedboat always crosses the finish line with exactly 1 liter of fuel left.

Trick 6: Wilson's Prime Shield (Wilson's Theorem)
==================================================================
Picture a gladiator named Wilson defending a prime number p.
His shield has a factorial (p - 1)! engraved on it.
When struck by divisor p, the shield always reflects back (p - 1) or -1.
Example: 16! mod 17 = 16.

Trick 8: The Factor Birthday Party (Total Number of Factors)
==================================================================

p brings (a + 1) choices: {p^0, p^1, ..., p^a}.
q brings (b + 1) choices.
Total handshakes at the party = (a + 1)(b + 1)(c + 1).

Trick 10: The Binomial Elevator (Remainders of (a + 1)^n and (a - 1)^n)
==================================================================
An elevator has capacity 'a'.
• Case (a + 1)^n / a: Every passenger is 1 above capacity. The excess 1 rides to the top. Remainder = 1 always.
• Case (a - 1)^n / a: Every passenger is 1 short (-1). 
  - Even number of trips: (-1)^even = +1 (remainder 1).
  - Odd number of trips: (-1)^odd = -1 (remainder a - 1).

Trick 11: The Odd Couple Love Story (a^n + b^n Divisibility)
==================================================================
Picture a romantic couple 'a' and 'b'.
They can only hold hands when the exponent 'n' is ODD!
When n is odd, (a^n + b^n) melts smoothly into (a + b).
If n is even, (a^n + b^n) refuses to factor over real numbers.

Trick 12: The Divisible Breakup (a^n - b^n Divisibility)
==================================================================
Unlike the sum, (a^n - b^n) ALWAYS separates from (a - b) for ALL natural powers n.
And if n is EVEN, it splits generously into BOTH (a - b) and (a + b)!
Mnemonic: "Minus is generous; even powers double the love!"
