# 🪄 Short Tricks — Number System (Advanced)

---

┌─────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ **TRICK 1 — Divisibility Squad: "2-3-5 Family Photo"** │
└─────────────────────────────────────────────┘

Imagine a family photo where everyone poses differently:
- **2** checks only the LAST digit (even?) — the lazy one, looks at feet only.
- **3 & 9** add up ALL digits — the accountants, they sum everything.
- **5** checks last digit (0 or 5) — the picky eater.
- **4** checks last TWO digits — the twins, always together.
- **8** checks last THREE digits — triplets!
- **11** alternates + − + − — the seesaw kid.

**Memory line:** *"Lazy 2, Accountant 3-9, Picky 5, Twins 4, Triplets 8, Seesaw 11."*

┌─────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ **TRICK 2 — Remainder Theorem: "The Lazy Postman"** │
└─────────────────────────────────────────────┘

To find remainder of a huge power like 7^100 ÷ 5, the Lazy Postman never delivers the full parcel. He only carries the **remainder of the base**: 7 → 2 (mod 5). Then he watches the remainders **cycle**: 2, 4, 3, 1, 2, 4, 3, 1…

**Rule:** *"Shrink the base, find the cycle, jump to the power's position."*
Cycle length of 2 mod 5 is 4 → 100 ÷ 4 = 0 remainder → answer = 1.

┌─────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ **TRICK 3 — Unit Digit: "The 4-Seat Merry-Go-Round"** │
└─────────────────────────────────────────────┘

Every unit digit rides a merry-go-round with at most **4 seats** (cycle ≤ 4):
- 2: 2-4-8-6 | 3: 3-9-7-1 | 7: 7-9-3-1 | 8: 8-4-2-6
- 0, 1, 5, 6 never move (they're glued to their seat!)

**Trick:** Divide the power by 4. Remainder tells the seat. Remainder 0 = seat 4.
**Memory line:** *"0,1,5,6 are couch potatoes — they never change!"*

┌─────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ **TRICK 4 — Number of Factors: "Add-One Party"** │
└─────────────────────────────────────────────┘

If N = a^p × b^q × c^r, then number of factors = **(p+1)(q+1)(r+1)**.

**Story:** Every prime exponent gets invited to a party, but each must bring **+1 guest** (the power 0 option — "I might not show up at all!"). Multiply all the (guest+1)s.

**Memory hook:** *"Every exponent brings a plus-one!"*

---

┌─────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ **TRICK 5 — Sum of Factors: "The ATM Formula"** │
└─────────────────────────────────────────────┘

Sum of factors of N = a^p × b^q is:
**(a^(p+1) − 1)/(a − 1) × (b^(q+1) − 1)/(b − 1)**

**Story:** An ATM dispenses every power of each prime (a⁰, a¹, … a^p). The geometric-series formula is the ATM's receipt. **"Power up one, minus one, over prime minus one."**

┌─────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ **TRICK 6 — HCF × LCM: "The Marriage Law"** │
└─────────────────────────────────────────────┘

**HCF × LCM = Product of the two numbers.**

**Story:** HCF is what the couple SHARES, LCM is EVERYTHING they own together. Multiply shared × everything = both their complete assets = a × b.
⚠️ **Warning:** This marriage law works ONLY for TWO numbers — three numbers break the marriage!

┌─────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ **TRICK 7 — LCM of Fractions: "Topsy-Turvy"** │
└─────────────────────────────────────────────┘

- **LCM of fractions = LCM of numerators / HCF of denominators**
- **HCF of fractions = HCF of numerators / LCM of denominators**

**Memory line:** *"LCM stands tall (big on top), HCF lies flat (big at bottom)."*
LCM wants to be BIG → big operation (LCM) on top. HCF wants to be SMALL → big operation (LCM) at bottom.

┌─────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ **TRICK 8 — Remainder Same for All: "Common Sweater"** │
└─────────────────────────────────────────────┘

"Smallest number which when divided by a, b, c leaves remainder r in each case":
**Answer = LCM(a, b, c) + r**

**Story:** The number wears the LCM as a perfectly-fitting sweater, then carries remainder **r in its pocket**. Same pocket money for every divisor!

If remainders DIFFER but (divisor − remainder) is constant = k:
**Answer = LCM − k** — *"Everyone is short by the same amount k, so the number itself is short by k."*

┌─────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ **TRICK 9 — Trailing Zeroes: "Count the 5s, Ignore the 2s"** │
└─────────────────────────────────────────────┘

Zeroes at the end of n! = ⌊n/5⌋ + ⌊n/25⌋ + ⌊n/125⌋ + …

**Story:** Every zero needs a couple: one 2 × one 5. But 2s are everywhere (like pigeons), 5s are rare (like peacocks). **The rare bird decides the count!** So just count 5s — and remember 25, 125 hide EXTRA 5s inside.

**Memory line:** *"Peacocks are rare — count only the peacocks (5s)!"*

┌─────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ **TRICK 10 — Divisibility by 7: "The Double-Chop"** │
└─────────────────────────────────────────────┘

Chop off the last digit, **DOUBLE** it, and **SUBTRACT** from the rest. Repeat till small. If the result is divisible by 7, so was the original.

Example: 343 → 34 − (3×2) = 28 → divisible by 7 ✓

**Memory line:** *"For 7, chop the tail, double it, and bite it off!"*
(For 13, same chop but ADD 4× the digit; for 19, ADD 2× — *"7 bites, 13 feeds four, 19 feeds two."*)

┌─────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ **TRICK 11 — Perfect Square Detective: "The Unit-Digit Bouncer"** │
└─────────────────────────────────────────────┘

A perfect square's unit digit can ONLY be **0, 1, 4, 5, 6, 9**.
Never 2, 3, 7, 8.

**Mnemonic:** *"Squares enter the club only with ID 0-1-4-5-6-9. The bouncer kicks out 2, 3, 7, 8."*
Instant elimination in MCQs: any option ending in 2, 3, 7, 8 can never be a perfect square!

Bonus: a perfect square has an **ODD** number of factors (one factor pairs with itself — *"the square root dances solo!"*).

┌─────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ **TRICK 12 — a^n − b^n vs a^n + b^n: "Minus is Friendly, Plus is Picky"** │
└─────────────────────────────────────────────┘

- **a^n − b^n** is ALWAYS divisible by (a − b). If n is even, also by (a + b).
- **a^n + b^n** is divisible by (a + b) ONLY when n is ODD.

**Memory line:** *"Minus welcomes everyone; Plus opens the door only for ODD guests."*
Classic exam trap: 5^even + 3^even is NOT divisible by 8 via this rule — check before applying!

┌─────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ **TRICK 13 — Digit Sum & Casting Out Nines: "The 9-Vacuum"** │
└─────────────────────────────────────────────┘

A number and the sum of its digits leave the **same remainder when divided by 9**.

**Story:** 9 is a vacuum cleaner — suck out all 9s (and pairs summing to 9) from the digit sum; whatever dust remains is the remainder.
Example: 7854 → 7+8+5+4 = 24 → 2+4 = 6. So 7854 ÷ 9 leaves remainder 6.

**Use:** Lightning-fast checking of multiplication/addition answers in exams.

---

┌─────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ **TRICK 14 — Successive Division / Base Conversion: "Read the Remainders Upside-Down"** │
└─────────────────────────────────────────────┘

To convert decimal to base b: keep dividing by b, note remainders, then **read them BOTTOM-UP**.

**Story:** The remainders fall into a well. The LAST one falls deepest but is the MOST important (highest place value) — so you rescue-read from the bottom up.

**Memory line:** *"Last remainder, first citizen!"*

┌─────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ **TRICK 15 — Fermat's Little Theorem: "The Prime Elevator"** │
└─────────────────────────────────────────────┘

If p is prime and a is not a multiple of p:
**a^(p−1) ≡ 1 (mod p)**

**Story:** In a prime-numbered elevator with p floors, any number a, when pressed (p−1) times, always lands back on floor **1**.
Example: remainder of 2^100 ÷ 101 → since 101 is prime, 2^100 ≡ 1. Done in 5 seconds!

**Memory line:** *"Prime p, power p−1, answer 1 — the elevator always returns home."*
