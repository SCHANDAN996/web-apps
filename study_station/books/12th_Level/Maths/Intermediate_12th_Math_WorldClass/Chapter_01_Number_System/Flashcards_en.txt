Card 1
Front: What is the rule for testing divisibility by a composite number N?
Back: Factor N into two co-prime numbers a and b (where gcd(a, b) = 1). The number must be simultaneously divisible by both a and b.

Card 2
Front: Why can't we test divisibility by 24 using factors 4 and 6?
Back: Because 4 and 6 are not co-prime (gcd(4, 6) = 2 != 1). Instead, use the co-prime pair 3 and 8.

Card 3
Front: How do you find the unit digit of an exponential expression a^b?
Back: Divide power b by 4. If remainder r > 0, unit digit is that of a^r; if remainder is 0, use a^4.

Card 4
Front: Which digits have a cyclicity of 1 for unit digits?
Back: {0, 1, 5, 6} always retain their own unit digit regardless of power.

Card 5
Front: What is the unit digit cyclicity of 4 and 9?
Back: Cyclicity 2. 4^odd = 4, 4^even = 6; 9^odd = 9, 9^even = 1.

Card 6
Front: State Fermat's Little Theorem.
Back: If p is a prime and gcd(a, p) = 1, then a^(p-1) mod p = 1.

Card 7
Front: What is the remainder when (p - 1)! is divided by prime p (Wilson's Theorem)?
Back: Remainder is p - 1 (or -1 mod p).

Card 8
Front: What is the formula for the total number of factors of N = p^a * q^b * r^c?
Back: Total factors T(N) = (a + 1)(b + 1)(c + 1).

Card 9
Front: How do you calculate the number of odd factors of N = 2^a * 3^b * 5^c?
Back: Discard all powers of 2. Number of odd factors = (b + 1)(c + 1).

Card 10
Front: What is the formula for the sum of all factors of N = p^a * q^b?
Back: [(p^(a+1) - 1) / (p - 1)] * [(q^(b+1) - 1) / (q - 1)].

Card 11
Front: What is the product of all factors of a composite number N?
Back: P(N) = N^(T(N) / 2), where T(N) is the total number of factors.

Card 12
Front: What determines the number of trailing zeroes in n!?
Back: The exponent of prime factor 5 in n!, calculated via Legendre's formula: floor(n/5) + floor(n/25) + floor(n/125) + ...

Card 13
Front: What is the remainder when (a + 1)^n is divided by a?
Back: Remainder is always 1 for all natural numbers n.

Card 14
Front: What is the remainder when (a - 1)^n is divided by a?
Back: Remainder is 1 if n is even, and a - 1 (or -1) if n is odd.

Card 15
Front: What algebraic factor always divides a^n + b^n when n is an odd positive integer?
Back: (a + b) always divides a^n + b^n when n is odd.

Card 16
Front: What does the mnemonic acronym P.O.E.T. stand for in Number System?
Back: P = Periodicity (cyclicity 4), O = Osculator (prime divisor rule), E = Euler/Fermat theorem (a^(p-1) = 1), T = Trailing zeroes (hunt factor 5).

Card 17
Front: What memory trick helps you remember which prime factor determines trailing zeroes?
Back: "Factory Twos are Free, Hunt the Rare Five" — twos are abundant in every even step, so only fives limit the count of 10s.

Card 18
Front: How many trailing zeroes are in 50!?
Back: floor(50/5) + floor(50/25) = 10 + 2 = 12 trailing zeroes.
