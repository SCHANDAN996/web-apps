26. What is the remainder when 7^84 is divided by 342?
(a) 1 (b) 7 (c) 0 (d) 49
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is trying to compute powers of 7 directly, which is impossible by hand. Note 7^3 = 343 = 342 + 1, so 7^3 ≡ 1 (mod 342), and 7^84 = (7^3)^28 ≡ 1 (mod 342), so the answer is 1.
Source: PYQ-style

27. The number 3^12 − 1 is divisible by which of the following?
(a) 11 (b) 89 (c) 73 (d) 97
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is testing 11 first; 3^5 = 243 ≡ 1 (mod 11) gives 3^12 ≡ 3^2 = 9 (mod 11), so 3^12 − 1 ≡ 8 (mod 11) and 11 fails. Factor: 3^12 − 1 = (3^6 − 1)(3^6 + 1) = 728 × 730, and 730 = 2 × 5 × 73, so the answer is 73.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Mathematics (Number Systems)

28. How many zeros are there at the end of 100!?
(a) 22 (b) 25 (c) 20 (d) 24
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is counting only multiples of 5, which gives 20 and misses the extra 5s contributed by 25, 50, 75 and 100. Number of zeros = ⌊100/5⌋ + ⌊100/25⌋ = 20 + 4 = 24, so the answer is 24.
Source: PYQ-style

29. What is the unit digit of 1! + 2! + 3! + … + 49!?
(a) 3 (b) 7 (c) 1 (d) 5
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is trying to sum all factorials; from 5! onward every factorial ends in 0, so only 1! to 4! matter. Unit digits: 1 + 2 + 6 + 4 = 13, so the answer is 3.
Source: PYQ-style

30. Find the remainder when 2^1000 is divided by 7.
(a) 1 (b) 2 (c) 6 (d) 4
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is assuming the cycle of powers of 2 mod 7 has length 6; actually 2^3 = 8 ≡ 1 (mod 7), so the cycle length is 3. Since 1000 = 3 × 333 + 1, 2^1000 ≡ 2 (mod 7), so the answer is 2.
Source: PYQ-style

31. A number when divided by 5 leaves remainder 3, and when divided by 7 leaves remainder 4. What is the smallest such positive number?
(a) 53 (b) 23 (c) 18 (d) 33
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is adding the remainders or multiplying the divisors blindly. Numbers leaving remainder 3 on division by 5 are 3, 8, 13, 18, 23…; 18 ÷ 7 leaves remainder 4, so the answer is 18.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Mathematics (Real Numbers)

32. The HCF of two numbers is 13 and their LCM is 1976. If one number is 104, find the other.
(a) 221 (b) 247 (c) 169 (d) 312
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is dividing the LCM by the given number directly, ignoring the HCF. Product of numbers = HCF × LCM = 13 × 1976 = 25688; other number = 25688 ÷ 104 = 247, so the answer is 247.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Mathematics (Real Numbers)

33. What is the largest number that divides 398, 436 and 542 leaving remainders 7, 11 and 15 respectively?
(a) 34 (b) 17 (c) 51 (d) 23
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is taking the HCF of the original numbers instead of subtracting the remainders first. Required number = HCF of 391, 425 and 527 = 17, so the answer is 17.
Source: PYQ-style

34. How many factors does 360 have?
(a) 20 (b) 24 (c) 22 (d) 28
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is counting factors by listing, which is slow and error-prone. 360 = 2^3 × 3^2 × 5^1, so number of factors = (3+1)(2+1)(1+1) = 24, so the answer is 24.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Mathematics (Real Numbers)

35. The sum of the first 50 natural numbers is:
(a) 1275 (b) 1225 (c) 2550 (d) 1250
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is using n(n+1) instead of n(n+1)/2, which gives the doubled value 2550. Sum = 50 × 51 / 2 = 1275, so the answer is 1275.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Mathematics (Arithmetic Progressions)

36. What is the unit digit of (173)^45 × (152)^77 × (777)^999?
(a) 8 (b) 6 (c) 2 (d) 4
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is computing full powers; only unit digits matter. Unit digit of 3^45 is 3 (cycle 3,9,7,1; 45 mod 4 = 1), of 2^77 is 2 (77 mod 4 = 1), of 7^999 is 3 (999 mod 4 = 3). Product: 3 × 2 × 3 = 18, so the answer is 8.
Source: PYQ-style

37. If the number 517*324 is completely divisible by 3, the smallest whole number in place of * is:
(a) 0 (b) 1 (c) 2 (d) 3
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is applying the divisibility rule of 9 instead of 3. Sum of digits = 5+1+7+*+3+2+4 = 22 + *; the smallest digit making this divisible by 3 is 2 (giving 24), so the answer is 2.
Source: NCERT Class 8 Mathematics (Playing with Numbers)

38. The difference between a two-digit number and the number obtained by interchanging its digits is 36. What is the difference of the two digits?
(a) 3 (b) 6 (c) 4 (d) 9
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is assuming the difference of the numbers equals the difference of the digits. If the digits are x and y, (10x + y) − (10y + x) = 9(x − y) = 36, so x − y = 4, so the answer is 4.
Source: NCERT Class 8 Mathematics (Linear Equations)

39. Which of the following is a prime number?
(a) 247 (b) 229 (c) 323 (d) 221
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is assuming all such numbers are prime or testing only very small primes. 221 = 13 × 17, 323 = 17 × 19 and 247 = 13 × 19, but 229 has no prime factor up to √229 ≈ 15.1, so the answer is 229.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Mathematics (Real Numbers)

40. The LCM of two numbers is 495 and their HCF is 5. If the sum of the numbers is 100, find their difference.
(a) 10 (b) 45 (c) 70 (d) 90
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is taking the numbers as 495 and 5 directly. Let the numbers be 5a and 5b with HCF(a,b) = 1; then 5ab = 495 gives ab = 99 and 5(a+b) = 100 gives a + b = 20, so a = 11, b = 9. The numbers are 55 and 45, so the answer is 10.
Source: PYQ-style

41. What is the remainder when (17^23 + 19^23) is divided by 36?
(a) 24 (b) 12 (c) 18 (d) 0
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is expanding the powers individually. Since 23 is odd, a^n + b^n is divisible by a + b; here 17 + 19 = 36, so the sum is divisible by 36 and the answer is 0.
Source: PYQ-style

42. How many numbers between 100 and 300 are divisible by both 7 and 11?
(a) 3 (b) 4 (c) 2 (d) 5
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is counting multiples of 7 and 11 separately and adding. Numbers divisible by both are multiples of 77; between 100 and 300 these are only 154 and 231 (77 × 1 = 77 is too small, 77 × 4 = 308 is too big), so the answer is 2.
Source: PYQ-style

43. The sum of all two-digit numbers divisible by 6 is:
(a) 720 (b) 780 (c) 810 (d) 750
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is taking the first term as 6 instead of 12. Two-digit multiples of 6 run from 12 to 96: number of terms = (96 − 12)/6 + 1 = 15; sum = 15 × (12 + 96)/2 = 810, so the answer is 810.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Mathematics (Arithmetic Progressions)

44. If x = 2 + √3, then the value of x + 1/x is:
(a) 4 (b) 2 (c) 2√3 (d) 3
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is rationalising incorrectly or squaring first. 1/x = 1/(2 + √3) = 2 − √3, so x + 1/x = (2 + √3) + (2 − √3) = 4, so the answer is 4.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Mathematics (Number Systems)

45. A number divided by 56 gives remainder 29. What will be the remainder when the same number is divided by 8?
(a) 3 (b) 1 (c) 7 (d) 5
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is assuming the remainder stays 29. Since 8 divides 56, the remainder on division by 8 equals the remainder when 29 is divided by 8, which is 5, so the answer is 5.
Source: PYQ-style

46. The product of two consecutive even numbers is 4224. The smaller number is:
(a) 62 (b) 68 (c) 66 (d) 64
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is estimating √4224 ≈ 65 and picking 66 without checking. Let the numbers be n and n+2: n² + 2n − 4224 = 0 gives n = (−2 + √16900)/2 = 64. Check: 64 × 66 = 4224, so the answer is 64.
Source: PYQ-style

47. What is the greatest 4-digit number which when divided by 12, 15, 20 and 27 leaves remainder 5 in each case?
(a) 9720 (b) 9185 (c) 9945 (d) 9725
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is subtracting 5 from the greatest 4-digit multiple of the LCM instead of adding. LCM of 12, 15, 20, 27 = 540; greatest 4-digit multiple of 540 is 9720; required number = 9720 + 5 = 9725, so the answer is 9725.
Source: PYQ-style

48. The number 2^32 + 1 is divisible by:
(a) 641 (b) 3 (c) 5 (d) 7
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is testing only small primes; 2^32 + 1 = 4294967297 is odd, its digit sum 59 rules out 3, it does not end in 0 or 5, and division by 7 fails. Euler showed this Fermat number equals 641 × 6700417, so the answer is 641.
Source: PYQ-style

49. If the sum of the digits of a number is subtracted from the number, the result is always divisible by:
(a) 5 (b) 8 (c) 11 (d) 9
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is guessing 11 by analogy with the alternating digit rule. Since 10 ≡ 1 (mod 9), every number equals (sum of its digits) + 9k, so number − (sum of digits) = 9k, always divisible by 9, so the answer is 9.
Source: NCERT Class 8 Mathematics (Playing with Numbers)

50. The least number which when divided by 4, 6, 8 and 9 leaves remainder 2 in each case, but is divisible by 13, is:
(a) 722 (b) 650 (c) 434 (d) 578
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is taking LCM + 2 = 74 and stopping, but 74 is not divisible by 13. The number must be of the form 72k + 2 (LCM of 4, 6, 8, 9 is 72); checking 74, 146, 218, 290, 362, 434, 506, 578, 650 — the first divisible by 13 is 650 = 13 × 50, so the answer is 650.
Source: PYQ-style
