51. The remainder when 7^84 is divided by 342 is
(a) 0 (b) 49 (c) 1 (d) 341
Answer: (c)
Solution: The tempting wrong choice is 49, which comes from stopping at 7^2 = 49 and forgetting the cube. Note 7^3 = 343 ≡ 1 (mod 342), and 84 = 3 × 28, so 7^84 = (7^3)^28 ≡ 1^28 = 1 (mod 342), so the answer is 1.
Source: PYQ-style

52. The unit digit of 3^2019 + 7^2019 is
(a) 4 (b) 0 (c) 6 (d) 8
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is 6, obtained by adding the unit digits 7 and 9 wrongly as 16 → 6 without checking the cycle. Unit digit of 3^n cycles 3, 9, 7, 1; 2019 mod 4 = 3, so unit digit is 7. Unit digit of 7^n cycles 7, 9, 3, 1; 2019 mod 4 = 3, so unit digit is 3. Sum = 7 + 3 = 10, so the answer is 0.
Source: PYQ-style

53. How many zeros are there at the end of 125!?
(a) 30 (b) 25 (c) 32 (d) 31
Answer: (d)
Solution: The tempting wrong answer is 25, which counts only multiples of 5. Count factors of 5: ⌊125/5⌋ + ⌊125/25⌋ + ⌊125/125⌋ = 25 + 5 + 1 = 31, so the answer is 31.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Mathematics (concept extended)

54. The remainder when 2^1000 is divided by 7 is
(a) 2 (b) 1 (c) 4 (d) 6
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is 1, from assuming the exponent divides evenly by the cycle length. Powers of 2 mod 7 cycle as 2, 4, 1 with period 3; 1000 mod 3 = 1, so 2^1000 ≡ 2 (mod 7), so the answer is 2.
Source: PYQ-style

55. If the number 4567x32 is divisible by 8, where x is a digit, then the sum of all possible values of x is
(a) 10 (b) 18 (c) 25 (d) 20
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is 10, from testing only a few digits and missing that every even digit works. Divisibility by 8 depends only on the last three digits x32; checking x = 0 to 9, the values 032, 232, 432, 632 and 832 are all divisible by 8, so x ∈ {0, 2, 4, 6, 8}. Sum = 0 + 2 + 4 + 6 + 8 = 20, so the answer is 20.
Source: PYQ-style

56. The HCF of two numbers is 13 and their LCM is 780. If one number is 65, the other number is
(a) 156 (b) 104 (c) 52 (d) 195
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is 52, from dividing LCM by 13 and stopping. Product of numbers = HCF × LCM = 13 × 780 = 10140; other number = 10140 ÷ 65 = 156. Check: HCF(65, 156) = 13, so the answer is 156.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Mathematics

57. What is the least number which when divided by 12, 15, 20 and 27 leaves a remainder of 7 in each case?
(a) 1087 (b) 527 (c) 547 (d) 540
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is 540, which is just the LCM with no remainder added. LCM of 12, 15, 20, 27 = 540; required number = 540 + 7 = 547, so the answer is 547.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Mathematics

58. The remainder when 17^200 is divided by 18 is
(a) 2 (b) 17 (c) 0 (d) 1
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is 17, from writing 17 ≡ −1 and forgetting the exponent is even. 17 ≡ −1 (mod 18), so 17^200 ≡ (−1)^200 = 1 (mod 18), so the answer is 1.
Source: PYQ-style

59. A number when divided by 221 leaves a remainder of 64. The remainder when the same number is divided by 13 is
(a) 0 (b) 1 (c) 12 (d) 11
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is 0, from assuming 64 is divisible by 13. Number = 221k + 64; since 221 = 13 × 17, remainder mod 13 = 64 mod 13 = 64 − 52 = 12, so the answer is 12.
Source: PYQ-style

60. The sum of the first 50 odd natural numbers is
(a) 2500 (b) 2450 (c) 2550 (d) 2601
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is 2550, which is the sum of the first 50 natural numbers. Sum of first n odd numbers = n² = 50² = 2500, so the answer is 2500.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Mathematics

61. If x = 2^3 × 3^2 × 5 and y = 2^2 × 3^3 × 7, then HCF × LCM of x and y equals
(a) xy (b) x + y (c) x²y² (d) 2xy
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is 2xy, from doubling the product formula. For any two positive integers, HCF × LCM = product of the numbers = xy, so the answer is xy.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Mathematics

62. The digit in the unit place of (257)^33 − (257)^32 is
(a) 0 (b) 2 (c) 4 (d) 6
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is 0, from assuming the two terms cancel. Unit digit of 257^n is the unit digit of 7^n: 33 mod 4 = 1 gives 7, and 32 mod 4 = 0 gives 1. Difference = 7 − 1 = 6, so the answer is 6.
Source: PYQ-style

63. The smallest number that must be added to 803642 so that the sum is divisible by 11 is
(a) 1 (b) 7 (c) 4 (d) 9
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is 4, which is the remainder itself rather than what must be added. Alternating sum of digits: (2 + 6 + 0) − (4 + 3 + 8) = 8 − 15 = −7 ≡ 4 (mod 11), so 803642 leaves remainder 4 on division by 11 (check: 11 × 73058 = 803638). Hence we must add 11 − 4 = 7, so the answer is 7.
Source: PYQ-style

64. How many numbers between 100 and 300 are divisible by both 7 and 11?
(a) 2 (b) 3 (c) 5 (d) 4
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is 3, from counting 77 or 308 which lie outside the range. Numbers divisible by both 7 and 11 are multiples of 77; between 100 and 300 these are 77 × 2 = 154 and 77 × 3 = 231 only, since 77 × 4 = 308 exceeds 300, so the answer is 2.
Source: PYQ-style

65. The remainder when (67^67 + 67) is divided by 68 is
(a) 67 (b) 1 (c) 0 (d) 66
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is 0, from assuming both terms vanish mod 68. 67 ≡ −1 (mod 68), so 67^67 ≡ −1 and 67 ≡ −1; sum ≡ −2 ≡ 66 (mod 68), so the answer is 66.
Source: PYQ-style

66. The remainder when 5^99 is divided by 8 is
(a) 1 (b) 5 (c) 3 (d) 7
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is 1, from noting 5^2 = 25 ≡ 1 (mod 8) and forgetting that 99 is odd. 5^99 = 5^98 × 5 = (5^2)^49 × 5 ≡ 1^49 × 5 = 5 (mod 8), so the answer is 5.
Source: PYQ-style

67. The largest 4-digit number exactly divisible by 12, 15, 18 and 27 is
(a) 9540 (b) 9960 (c) 9720 (d) 9990
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is 9990, which is divisible by 15 and 27 but not by 12 and 18 together. LCM of 12, 15, 18, 27 = 540; largest 4-digit multiple = 9999 − (9999 mod 540) = 9999 − 279 = 9720, so the answer is 9720.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Mathematics

68. The value of 1 + 2 + 3 + … + 100 − (2 + 4 + 6 + … + 100) is
(a) 2500 (b) 2550 (c) 5050 (d) 5000
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is 2550, which is the sum of the even terms alone. Total sum = 5050; even sum = 2(1 + 2 + … + 50) = 2550; difference = 5050 − 2550 = 2500, which equals the sum of the first 50 odd numbers, so the answer is 2500.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Mathematics

69. When a number is divided by 5, the remainder is 3. What is the remainder when the square of the number is divided by 5?
(a) 4 (b) 2 (c) 1 (d) 3
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is 3, from assuming the remainder stays the same after squaring. Number ≡ 3 (mod 5), so square ≡ 9 ≡ 4 (mod 5), so the answer is 4.
Source: PYQ-style

70. The number of prime factors of 6^10 × 7^17 × 55^5 is
(a) 52 (b) 42 (c) 37 (d) 47
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is 37, from ignoring the factors contributed by 55^5. Prime factorisation: 6^10 = 2^10 × 3^10; 55^5 = 5^5 × 11^5. Total prime factors counted with multiplicity = 10 + 10 + 17 + 5 + 5 = 47, so the answer is 47.
Source: PYQ-style

71. The difference of two numbers is 1365. On dividing the larger by the smaller, the quotient is 6 and remainder is 15. The smaller number is
(a) 360 (b) 270 (c) 295 (d) 240
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is 240, from dividing 1365 by 6 directly without subtracting the remainder. Larger = 6 × smaller + 15, so difference = 5 × smaller + 15 = 1365, giving smaller = (1365 − 15)/5 = 270, so the answer is 270.
Source: PYQ-style

72. The unit digit of 1! + 2! + 3! + … + 100! is
(a) 1 (b) 3 (c) 5 (d) 7
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is 5, from adding unit digits of 1! to 4! wrongly. From 5! onward every factorial ends in 0, so only 1! + 2! + 3! + 4! = 1 + 2 + 6 + 24 = 33 matters; unit digit is 3, so the answer is 3.
Source: PYQ-style

73. If n is a natural number, then 6^n − 5^n always ends with the digit
(a) 5 (b) 3 (c) 1 (d) 9
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is 5, from looking only at the unit digit of 5^n. 6^n always ends in 6 and 5^n always ends in 5, so their difference ends in 6 − 5 = 1, so the answer is 1.
Source: PYQ-style

74. The greatest number which divides 1657 and 2037 leaving remainders 6 and 5 respectively is
(a) 123 (b) 127 (c) 235 (d) 305
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is 123, from taking HCF of the original numbers instead of the reduced ones. Required number = HCF of (1657 − 6) = 1651 and (2037 − 5) = 2032; HCF(1651, 2032) = 127 (1651 = 13 × 127, 2032 = 16 × 127), so the answer is 127.
Source: PYQ-style

75. The sum of all two-digit numbers divisible by 7 is
(a) 945 (b) 1071 (c) 728 (d) 735
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is 945, from starting the series at 7 instead of 14. Two-digit multiples of 7 are 14, 21, …, 98: number of terms = (98 − 14)/7 + 1 = 13; sum = (13/2) × (14 + 98) = (13/2) × 112 = 13 × 56 = 728, so the answer is 728.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Mathematics (concept extended)
