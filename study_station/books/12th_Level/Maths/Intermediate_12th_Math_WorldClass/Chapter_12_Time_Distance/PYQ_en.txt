# 📊 PYQ Analysis — Time & Distance (English)

## How Exams Test This Chapter

Time & Distance is one of the most reliable scoring areas in SSC CGL, IBPS PO, RRB NTPC and similar exams. Across these exams, the questions cluster around a small set of repeatable patterns. Master the pattern, and the "new" question in your paper is just an old friend wearing a different shirt.

### The Recurring Sub-Topics (in words, not numbers)

- **Basic speed–time–distance conversion** — especially km/h ↔ m/s. This appears in almost every paper, often hidden inside a train question.
- **Trains crossing poles, platforms and other trains** — the single most repeated family. The trap is always "which length(s) to add."
- **Relative speed** — same direction vs opposite direction, two objects moving together.
- **Average speed for a round trip** — the harmonic-mean trap (2xy/(x+y)), not the simple average.
- **Boats and streams** — upstream/downstream speed, finding still-water speed or stream speed.
- **Races and circular tracks** — head starts, dead heats, meeting points on a circular path.
- **"Late/early" problems** — a man walking at different speeds reaches late or early by given minutes; find the distance.
- **Chase problems** — police and thief, two trains starting at different times.

### The Examiner's Favourite Traps

1. **Unit trap:** Speed given in km/h, length in metres, answer expected in seconds. If you skip the ×5/18 conversion, a wrong option is waiting for you — it was designed from your mistake.
2. **Length-addition trap:** A train crossing a *platform* covers train length + platform length; crossing a *pole* covers only its own length. Options include both wrong versions.
3. **Average-speed trap:** For equal distances at speeds x and y, the average is 2xy/(x+y), never (x+y)/2. The simple average is always one of the options.
4. **Relative-speed direction trap:** Opposite directions → add speeds; same direction → subtract. Questions phrase this indirectly ("overtakes", "meets").
5. **Time-format trap:** "Reaches 10 minutes late at 3 km/h and 10 minutes early at 4 km/h" — students forget to convert minutes to hours before applying the formula.
6. **Stream-direction trap:** "Speed in still water" and "speed of stream" are mixed up in the options; downstream = sum, upstream = difference.

## Top 10 High-Yield Questions (with Solutions)

**Q1.** A train 240 m long crosses a pole in 12 seconds. What is the speed of the train in km/h?
(a) 60 km/h (b) 72 km/h (c) 80 km/h (d) 90 km/h

**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** Speed = 240/12 = 20 m/s = 20 × 18/5 = 72 km/h.
**Trap:** Option (a) 60 km/h comes from a faulty m/s → km/h conversion (using ×3 instead of ×3.6). Crossing a *pole* means only the train's own length counts.
**Source:** PYQ-style

---

**Q2.** A train 150 m long crosses a platform 250 m long in 20 seconds. Find the speed of the train.
(a) 15 m/s (b) 20 m/s (c) 25 m/s (d) 30 m/s

**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** Total distance = 150 + 250 = 400 m. Speed = 400/20 = 20 m/s.
**Trap:** Using only the train's length gives 150/20 = 7.5 m/s; using only the platform's length gives 250/20 = 12.5 m/s. Always add both lengths when crossing a platform (train length + platform length).
**Source:** PYQ-style

---

**Q3.** Two trains of lengths 120 m and 180 m run towards each other on parallel tracks at 40 km/h and 50 km/h. In how much time will they cross each other?
(a) 10 s (b) 12 s (c) 15 s (d) 18 s

**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** Relative speed (opposite direction) = 40 + 50 = 90 km/h = 90 × 5/18 = 25 m/s. Total length = 120 + 180 = 300 m. Time = 300/25 = 12 s.
**Trap:** Subtracting the speeds (same-direction logic) gives 10 km/h and a wildly wrong answer. "Towards each other" = add speeds.
**Source:** PYQ-style

---

**Q4.** A man travels from A to B at 40 km/h and returns at 60 km/h. Find his average speed for the whole journey.
(a) 48 km/h (b) 50 km/h (c) 52 km/h (d) 45 km/h

**Answer: (a)**
**Solution:** Equal distances, so average speed = 2xy/(x+y) = (2 × 40 × 60)/(40 + 60) = 4800/100 = 48 km/h.
**Trap:** Option (b) 50 km/h is the simple average (40+60)/2 — the most-planted wrong answer in this chapter. Average speed for equal distances is always the harmonic mean, which is *less* than the arithmetic mean.
**Source:** PYQ-style

---

**Q5.** A boat goes 30 km downstream in 2 hours and the same distance upstream in 3 hours. Find the speed of the boat in still water.
(a) 10 km/h (b) 12.5 km/h (c) 15 km/h (d) 7.5 km/h

**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** Downstream speed = 30/2 = 15 km/h; upstream speed = 30/3 = 10 km/h. Speed in still water = (15 + 10)/2 = 12.5 km/h.
**Trap:** Option (c) 15 km/h is the downstream speed itself; option (d) 7.5 km/h is a muddled half of downstream. Still-water speed = average of downstream and upstream speeds; stream speed = half their difference = 2.5 km/h.
**Source:** PYQ-style

---

**Q6.** Walking at 3/4 of his usual speed, a man reaches his office 20 minutes late. Find his usual time to reach the office.
(a) 40 min (b) 60 min (c) 80 min (d) 100 min

**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** Speed becomes 3/4, so time becomes 4/3 of usual. Extra time = (4/3 − 1) = 1/3 of usual time = 20 min. Usual time = 60 min.
**Trap:** Students compute 3/4 × 20 or 4/3 × 20 directly. Remember: the *extra* time is the difference (1/3 of usual), not the new time itself.
**Source:** PYQ-style

---

**Q7.** A man covers a certain distance at 4 km/h and reaches 10 minutes late; at 5 km/h he reaches 5 minutes early. Find the distance.
(a) 3 km (b) 4 km (c) 5 km (d) 6 km

**Answer: (c)**
**Solution:** Distance = (S₁ × S₂)/(S₂ − S₁) × (total time gap in hours) = (4 × 5)/(5 − 4) × (15/60) = 20 × 1/4 = 5 km.
**Trap:** The time gap is 10 + 5 = 15 minutes (late + early add up), and it must be converted to hours. Using 15 minutes directly gives 300 km — absurd, but a sign the units were skipped.
**Source:** PYQ-style

---

**Q8.** A thief runs at 8 km/h. A policeman chases him at 10 km/h. If the thief is initially 200 m ahead, how long will the policeman take to catch him?
(a) 4 min (b) 5 min (c) 6 min (d) 8 min

**Answer: (c)**
**Solution:** Relative speed (same direction) = 10 − 8 = 2 km/h. Time = 0.2 km / 2 km/h = 0.1 h = 6 minutes.
**Trap:** Adding the speeds (18 km/h) gives ~40 seconds — a tempting but wrong option. Chases are same-direction: subtract. Also convert 200 m to 0.2 km.
**Source:** PYQ-style

---

**Q9.** In a 1000 m race, A beats B by 100 m. In a 2000 m race, by how many metres will A beat B (speeds constant)?
(a) 100 m (b) 150 m (c) 200 m (d) 250 m

**Answer: (c)**
**Solution:** When A runs 1000 m, B runs 900 m, so B's speed is 9/10 of A's. When A runs 2000 m, B runs 2000 × 9/10 = 1800 m. A beats B by 200 m.
**Trap:** Option (a) 100 m assumes the margin stays fixed. Margins scale with race length because the speed *ratio* is constant, not the gap.
**Source:** PYQ-style

---

**Q10.** Two persons start from the same point on a circular track of length 600 m, running in opposite directions at 4 m/s and 6 m/s. After how long will they meet for the first time?
(a) 50 s (b) 60 s (c) 100 s (d) 150 s

**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** Opposite directions → relative speed = 4 + 6 = 10 m/s. First meeting = one full lap together = 600/10 = 60 s.
**Trap:** Option (d) 150 s comes from subtracting speeds (6 − 4 = 2 m/s) — that formula is for the *same* direction. On a circular track, opposite direction = add, same direction = subtract.
**Source:** PYQ-style

## Quick Revision Pointers

- Convert units **before** anything else: km/h × 5/18 = m/s.
- Train + platform: add lengths. Train + pole: train length only.
- Opposite direction: add speeds. Same direction: subtract.
- Equal-distance average speed: 2xy/(x+y) — always less than (x+y)/2.
- Late/early problems: add the two time gaps, convert to hours.
- Boats: still-water speed = (downstream + upstream)/2; stream = (downstream − upstream)/2.
