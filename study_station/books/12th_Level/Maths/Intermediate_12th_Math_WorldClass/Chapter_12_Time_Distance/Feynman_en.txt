# Time & Distance — Explained Like You're 12

## The Hardest Concept: Relative Speed (When Two Things Move Together)

Most students can handle "one train goes from A to B." But the moment the question says *"two trains are coming towards each other"* — panic! Let's fix that forever.

## The Playground Story

Imagine you and your friend Rohan are standing at opposite ends of a 100-metre playground.

**Case 1: You run TOWARDS each other.**
You run at 5 m/s, Rohan runs at 5 m/s. Every second, the gap between you shrinks by 5 + 5 = **10 metres**. So you meet in 100 ÷ 10 = **10 seconds**.

**Case 2: Rohan runs AWAY and you chase him.**
Same speeds — 5 m/s each. But wait… every second you gain 5 metres, and Rohan runs away 5 metres. You gain **nothing**! You will *never* catch him. The gap stays 100 metres forever.

**Case 3: You're faster — you run 7 m/s, Rohan runs 5 m/s.**
Now every second you close the gap by only 7 − 5 = **2 metres**. So you catch him in 100 ÷ 2 = **50 seconds**.

## The Big Secret

When two things move, don't think about them separately. **Combine their speeds into ONE speed:**

- **Coming towards each other (opposite directions)?** → **ADD** the speeds. They help each other close the gap.
- **Same direction (a chase)?** → **SUBTRACT** the speeds. The one in front "steals" some of your speed.

That combined speed is called **relative speed**. Then it's just a normal problem: Time = Distance ÷ Relative Speed.

## The Trick You'll Never Forget: 🚗💨 "Horn vs. Chase"

> **HORNS = ADD** 📢📢
> When two vehicles face each other, they honk — *beep beep!* — because they're about to crash. Facing = danger = ADD the speeds.
>
> **CHASE = SUBTRACT** 🏃
> In a chase, the runner in front "eats" your speed. Same direction = SUBTRACT.

Just ask yourself: *"Is this a horn situation or a chase situation?"* — and you'll never mix up add/subtract again.

## Bonus: Why a Train Crossing a Pole vs. a Platform Is Different

A train crossing a **pole** only needs to cover **its own length** (a pole has no length). A train crossing a **platform** must cover **train length + platform length** — the front enters, but the train hasn't "crossed" until its tail leaves!

Think of it like a caterpillar crossing a rug: it's not fully across until its tail is off the rug.
