# Comprehensive Study Material: Time, Speed and Distance

## 1. Fundamentals of Motion
The study of Time, Speed, and Distance (TSD) forms one of the most critical pillars of quantitative aptitude in competitive examinations. At its core lies the simple physical relationship between the space traversed and the time taken:
$$\text{Distance} = \text{Speed} \times \text{Time} \quad (D = S \times T)$$

### Units and Conversions
Speed expresses the rate of change of position. The two universal units are:
1. **Kilometers per hour (km/h):** Used predominantly in road transportation and railways.
2. **Meters per second (m/s):** The standard SI unit, widely used in physics and short-distance track races.

**Derivation of the Conversion Factor:**
$$1 \text{ km/h} = \frac{1 \text{ km}}{1 \text{ hour}} = \frac{1000 \text{ meters}}{3600 \text{ seconds}} = \frac{10}{36} \text{ m/s} = \frac{5}{18} \text{ m/s}$$
Conversely:
$$1 \text{ m/s} = \frac{18}{5} \text{ km/h} = 3.6 \text{ km/h}$$

## 2. Average Speed
Average speed measures the overall rate of motion over an entire journey comprising varying speeds and distances.
$$\text{Average Speed} = \frac{\text{Total Distance Covered}}{\text{Total Time Taken}}$$

### Special Case A: Two Equal Distances
If a person travels a distance $d$ at speed $x$ and returns the same distance $d$ at speed $y$:
$$\text{Total Distance} = 2d$$
$$\text{Total Time} = \frac{d}{x} + \frac{d}{y} = \frac{d(x + y)}{xy}$$
$$\text{Average Speed} = \frac{2d}{\frac{d(x + y)}{xy}} = \frac{2xy}{x + y}$$
*Note: The average speed is the Harmonic Mean of the two individual speeds.*

### Special Case B: Three Equal Distances
If three equal segments of distance are traversed at speeds $x$, $y$, and $z$:
$$\text{Average Speed} = \frac{3xyz}{xy + yz + zx}$$

### Special Case C: Equal Time Intervals
If a body travels for equal durations $t$ at speed $x$ and then at speed $y$:
$$\text{Average Speed} = \frac{xt + yt}{2t} = \frac{x + y}{2}$$
*(Arithmetic Mean)*

## 3. The Proportionality Framework
When dealing with two distinct journeys or scenarios, the three fundamental variables yield three powerful proportionalities:

1. **Distance is Constant ($D_1 = D_2$):**
   $$S_1 \times T_1 = S_2 \times T_2 \implies \frac{S_1}{S_2} = \frac{T_2}{T_1}$$
   Speed and Time are inversely proportional. If speed increases by $25\%$ ($\frac{5}{4}$ of normal), time taken decreases to $\frac{4}{5}$ of normal (a $20\%$ reduction).

2. **Time is Constant ($T_1 = T_2$):**
   $$\frac{D_1}{S_1} = \frac{D_2}{S_2} \implies \frac{D_1}{D_2} = \frac{S_1}{S_2}$$
   Distance is directly proportional to speed. In a given time, a faster traveler covers proportionately more ground.

3. **Speed is Constant ($S_1 = S_2$):**
   $$\frac{D_1}{T_1} = \frac{D_2}{T_2} \implies \frac{D_1}{D_2} = \frac{T_1}{T_2}$$
   Distance is directly proportional to time.

---

## 4. Relative Speed
Relative speed is the speed of one moving object as observed from another moving object.

1. **Objects moving in Opposite Directions:**
   Since the distance between them closes (or expands) at a rate equal to the sum of their individual speeds:
   $$\text{Relative Speed} = S_1 + S_2$$

2. **Objects moving in the Same Direction:**
   The faster object gains on the slower object at a rate equal to the difference between their speeds:
   $$\text{Relative Speed} = |S_1 - S_2|$$

---

## 5. Problems on Trains
A train is an extended physical body possessing significant length $L$.
- **Passing a Stationary Point Object (pole, tree, telegraph post, stationary man):**
  $$\text{Distance} = L_{\text{train}}$$
  $$\text{Time} = \frac{L_{\text{train}}}{S_{\text{train}}}$$
- **Passing an Extended Stationary Object (platform, bridge, tunnel):**
  $$\text{Distance} = L_{\text{train}} + L_{\text{platform}}$$
  $$\text{Time} = \frac{L_{\text{train}} + L_{\text{platform}}}{S_{\text{train}}}$$
- **Two Trains Crossing Each Other:**
  $$\text{Total Distance} = L_1 + L_2$$
  - Opposite Direction: $\text{Time} = \frac{L_1 + L_2}{S_1 + S_2}$
  - Same Direction: $\text{Time} = \frac{L_1 + L_2}{|S_1 - S_2|}$

---

## 6. Boats and Streams
When water in a river flows at a uniform speed $v$, it assists a boat traveling along the current and opposes a boat traveling against the current.
- **Downstream Speed ($D$):** Speed with the current $= u + v$
- **Upstream Speed ($U$):** Speed against the current $= u - v$
- Solving simultaneously:
  $$u = \frac{D + U}{2} \quad (\text{Speed of boat in still water})$$
  $$v = \frac{D - U}{2} \quad (\text{Speed of stream / current})$$

---

## 7. Circular Tracks and Races
- **Linear Races:**
  - "A gives B a head start of $x$ meters": In a race of $D$ meters, A runs $D$ meters while B runs $(D - x)$ meters.
  - "A beats B by $t$ seconds": A and B run the same distance, but B takes $t$ seconds longer than A.
- **Circular Tracks:**
  - Length of track $= L$.
  - First meeting anywhere: $\text{Time} = \frac{L}{S_{\text{rel}}}$.
  - First meeting at the starting point: $\text{LCM}\left(\frac{L}{S_1}, \frac{L}{S_2}\right)$.
