# Time & Distance

## Chapter Outline & Learning Goals

By the end of this chapter, you will be able to:

1. Convert between km/h and m/s without a calculator.
2. Solve problems on trains crossing poles, platforms, and other trains.
3. Handle boats & streams (upstream/downstream) questions.
4. Crack relative speed, average speed, and circular track problems.
5. Spot the traps examiners set in SSC CGL, IBPS PO, and RRB NTPC papers.

---

## The Hook: The Train That "Shrank"

Imagine you're standing on a platform. A 240-metre-long train zooms past you in 12 seconds. Simple, right?

Now the same train crosses a 360-metre-long platform. How long does it take?

Most students instantly say: "It took 12 seconds for 240 metres, so 360 metres should take 18 seconds."

**Wrong.** The correct answer is **30 seconds**.

Why? Because when a train crosses *you* (a point), it only needs to cover its **own length**. But when it crosses a *platform*, it must cover **its own length PLUS the platform's length** — 240 + 360 = 600 metres. At 20 m/s, that's 30 seconds.

This one idea — *"what distance is actually being covered?"* — is the heart of almost every Time & Distance question in competitive exams. Master it, and this chapter becomes free marks.

## Let's Be Honest First

If Time & Distance questions make you nervous, you're not alone. Students usually struggle for three reasons:

- **Unit confusion** — mixing km/h with metres and seconds.
- **Relative speed** — not knowing when to add and when to subtract speeds.
- **Hidden distances** — forgetting that a train must "drag its own body" across a platform.

We'll fix all three, one small chunk at a time.

---

## Chunk 1: The One Formula That Rules Them All

$$\text{Speed} = \frac{\text{Distance}}{\text{Time}}$$

From this single formula come two more:

- **Distance = Speed × Time**
- **Time = Distance ÷ Speed**

Everything in this chapter — trains, boats, races, circular tracks — is just this formula wearing different costumes.

**The Golden Triangle trick:** Draw a triangle with **D** on top, **S** and **T** at the bottom. Cover the one you want to find:
- Cover D → S × T
- Cover S → D ÷ T
- Cover T → D ÷ S

🧠 **Active Recall:** Without looking back — if a car covers 150 km in 3 hours, what is its speed? And how long would it take to cover 200 km at that speed? *(Answer: 50 km/h; 4 hours)*

---

## Chunk 2: Unit Conversion — The 5/18 Magic Number

Examiners love giving speed in km/h and distance in metres. You **must** convert.

$$1 \text{ km/h} = \frac{1000 \text{ m}}{3600 \text{ s}} = \frac{5}{18} \text{ m/s}$$

So:

- **km/h → m/s: multiply by 5/18**
- **m/s → km/h: multiply by 18/5**

**Example:** 72 km/h = 72 × 5/18 = **20 m/s**. (This is why the train in our hook moved 240 m in 12 s.)

**Mnemonic 🎭:** *"Small guy, big appetite."* m/s is the **small** unit, so to reach it you multiply by the **small** fraction 5/18. km/h is the **big** unit, so you multiply by the **big** fraction 18/5. Small wants small, big wants big.

🧠 **Active Recall:** Convert 90 km/h to m/s, and 15 m/s to km/h. *(Answer: 25 m/s; 54 km/h)*

## Chunk 3: Average Speed — The Trap Everyone Falls Into

**Question:** A man goes from A to B at 40 km/h and returns at 60 km/h. What is his average speed for the whole journey?

If you said 50 km/h — you just walked into the examiner's favourite trap. The average of 40 and 60 is 50, but average speed is **NOT** the average of speeds, because he spends *more time* travelling at the slower speed.

**Average Speed = Total Distance ÷ Total Time**

For equal distances at speeds *x* and *y*:

$$\text{Average Speed} = \frac{2xy}{x+y}$$

Here: (2 × 40 × 60) / (40 + 60) = 4800/100 = **48 km/h**.

For three equal-distance speeds x, y, z:

$$\text{Average Speed} = \frac{3xyz}{xy + yz + zx}$$

🧠 **Active Recall:** A cyclist covers a certain distance at 30 km/h and returns the same distance at 20 km/h. Average speed? *(Answer: 2×30×20/50 = 24 km/h — not 25!)*

## Chunk 4: Relative Speed — When Two Things Move

This is the concept that separates toppers from the rest. The rule is beautifully simple:

- **Opposite directions (towards or away from each other): ADD the speeds.**
- **Same direction (one chasing the other): SUBTRACT the speeds.**

**Example 1 (opposite):** Two trains, 60 km/h and 40 km/h, approach each other. Relative speed = 100 km/h. They close the gap fast.

**Example 2 (same direction):** A police jeep at 80 km/h chases a thief at 60 km/h, with 10 km between them. Relative speed = 20 km/h. Time to catch = 10/20 = **0.5 hour = 30 minutes**.

**Memory hook 🎭:** *"Enemies add, friends subtract."* Moving opposite (like enemies facing off) → speeds ADD. Moving the same way (like friends walking together) → the faster one only gains by the DIFFERENCE.

🧠 **Active Recall:** Two runners on a straight road run in the same direction at 12 km/h and 9 km/h. If the faster one starts 6 km behind, when does he catch up? *(Answer: relative speed 3 km/h, time = 2 hours)*

## Chunk 5: Trains — The "Drag Your Body" Rule

Remember the hook? Here's the complete rulebook:

| Situation | Distance to cover |
|---|---|
| Train crosses a pole / man / signal (a point) | Length of train (L) |
| Train crosses a platform / bridge / tunnel | L + platform length |
| Train crosses another moving train (opposite) | L₁ + L₂, speeds ADD |
| Train crosses another moving train (same direction) | L₁ + L₂, speeds SUBTRACT |
| Train crosses a man sitting in another train | Only L₁ (the crossing train's length) |

**Example:** Train A (150 m, 54 km/h) meets Train B (200 m, 36 km/h) coming from the opposite direction. Time to cross each other?

- Relative speed = 54 + 36 = 90 km/h = 25 m/s
- Distance = 150 + 200 = 350 m
- Time = 350/25 = **14 seconds**

🧠 **Active Recall:** A 180 m train crosses a pole in 9 seconds. What is its speed in km/h? *(Answer: 20 m/s = 72 km/h)*

## Chunk 6: Boats & Streams — The River Helps or Fights

- **Speed of boat in still water = B**, **Speed of stream = S**
- **Downstream (with the current): B + S** — the river helps you.
- **Upstream (against the current): B − S** — the river fights you.

And the reverse formulas examiners love:

$$B = \frac{\text{Downstream} + \text{Upstream}}{2}, \qquad S = \frac{\text{Downstream} - \text{Upstream}}{2}$$

**Example:** A boat goes 30 km downstream in 2 hours and returns upstream in 6 hours.

- Downstream speed = 15 km/h, Upstream speed = 5 km/h
- Boat speed B = (15+5)/2 = **10 km/h**
- Stream speed S = (15−5)/2 = **5 km/h**

🧠 **Active Recall:** A boat's speed in still water is 12 km/h and the stream flows at 3 km/h. How long to go 45 km downstream? *(Answer: 45/15 = 3 hours)*

## Chunk 7: Classic Exam Models

**1. The "late/early" model:** Walking at 3/4 of his usual speed, a man is 20 minutes late. Usual time?

- Speed becomes 3/4 → time becomes 4/3. Extra time = 1/3 of usual = 20 min → **Usual time = 60 minutes.**
- Shortcut: Usual time = (late time × new speed fraction) ÷ (1 − fraction) = (20 × 3/4) ÷ (1/4) = 60 min.

**2. Circular tracks:** Two people run around a circular track. They meet (anywhere on the track) every:
- Same direction: L ÷ (difference of speeds)
- Opposite direction: L ÷ (sum of speeds)
where L = track length. They meet **at the starting point** after LCM of their individual lap times.

**3. Stoppages:** A train travels at 60 km/h without stoppage and 45 km/h with stoppages. Stoppage time per hour = (60−45)/60 × 60 = **15 minutes per hour**.

🧠 **Active Recall:** A bus covers a route at 50 km/h without stops and 40 km/h with stops. How many minutes per hour does it halt? *(Answer: (10/50)×60 = 12 minutes)*
