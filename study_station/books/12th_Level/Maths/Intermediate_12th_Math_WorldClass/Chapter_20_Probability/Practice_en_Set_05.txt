101. A die is thrown once. What is the probability of getting a prime number?
(a) 2/3 (b) 1/3 (c) 1/6 (d) 1/2
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is counting 1 as prime, which gives 4/6 = 2/3. Prime numbers on a die are 2, 3, 5 — three outcomes out of six, so the answer is 1/2.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Mathematics

102. Two coins are tossed simultaneously. What is the probability of getting exactly one head?
(a) 1/2 (b) 3/4 (c) 1/4 (d) 1/3
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is treating HT and TH as one case, giving 1/3. The sample space is HH, HT, TH, TT, and exactly one head occurs in HT and TH, so the answer is 1/2.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Mathematics

103. A card is drawn from a well-shuffled deck of 52 cards. What is the probability of drawing a red king?
(a) 2/13 (b) 1/13 (c) 1/52 (d) 1/26
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is taking all four kings, giving 4/52 = 1/13. Only the king of hearts and king of diamonds are red, so 2/52, and the answer is 1/26.

Solution: The trap is adding 4 kings and 13 spades to get 17/52, double-counting the king of spades. The correct count is 4 + 13 − 1 = 16 cards, so 16/52 and the answer is 4/13.

Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is counting only the single-head cases, giving 3/8. Use the complement: P(no head) = P(TTT) = 1/8, so P(at least one head) = 1 − 1/8, and the answer is 7/8.

120. A bag contains 6 red and 4 black balls. Two balls are drawn one after another without replacement. What is the probability that both are red?
(a) 2/5 (b) 3/10 (c) 9/25 (d) 1/3
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is treating the draws as independent, giving (6/10)² = 9/25. Without replacement, P = (6/10) × (5/9) = 30/90, so the answer is 1/3.

121. A bag contains 5 red and 3 blue balls. Two balls are drawn at random without replacement. What is the probability that one is red and one is blue?
(a) 15/28 (b) 15/56 (c) 5/14 (d) 3/8
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is counting only one order (red then blue), giving 15/56. Both orders count: (5/8)(3/7) + (3/8)(5/7) = 30/56, so the answer is 15/28.

Solution: The trap is multiplying both truth probabilities, giving 0.42. Contradiction means A true and B false, or A false and B true: (0.6)(0.3) + (0.4)(0.7) = 0.18 + 0.28, so the answer is 0.46.

(a) 1/169 (b) 1/221 (c) 4/663 (d) 1/26
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is using replacement logic, giving (4/52)² = 1/169. Without replacement, P = (4/52) × (3/51) = 12/2652, which simplifies to the answer 1/221.

125. A problem is given to three students whose chances of solving it are 1/2, 1/3 and 1/4 respectively. What is the probability that the problem is solved?
(a) 3/4 (b) 1/4 (c) 2/3 (d) 5/6
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is adding the probabilities, giving 13/12 which exceeds 1. Use the complement: P(none solves) = (1/2)(2/3)(3/4) = 1/4, so P(solved) = 1 − 1/4, and the answer is 3/4.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Mathematics
