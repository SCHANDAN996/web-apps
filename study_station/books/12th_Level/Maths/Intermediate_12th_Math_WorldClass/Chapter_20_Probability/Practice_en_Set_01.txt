1. A fair coin is tossed once. What is the probability of getting a head?
(a) 1/4 (b) 1/3 (c) 1/2 (d) 1
Answer: (c)
Solution: A coin has two equally likely outcomes — head and tail. The tempting wrong choice 1/4 wrongly assumes four outcomes. Favourable outcome is 1 out of 2, so the answer is 1/2.

2. A die is thrown once. What is the probability of getting the number 4?
(a) 1/2 (b) 1/3 (c) 1/6 (d) 1/4
Answer: (c)
Solution: A die has 6 equally likely faces, and only one face shows 4. The wrong option 1/4 confuses the required number with the count of outcomes. Favourable = 1, total = 6, so the answer is 1/6.

6. What is the probability of an impossible event?

Answer: (b)
Solution: An impossible event has no favourable outcomes, so its probability is 0. The trap 1 is the probability of a sure event, the exact opposite. Hence the answer is 0.

(a) 0.3 (b) 0.6 (c) 1 (d) 0.7
Answer: (d)
Solution: P(not E) = 1 − P(E) = 1 − 0.3 = 0.7. The trap 0.3 simply repeats the given probability instead of subtracting from 1. Hence the answer is 0.7.

22. A bag contains 4 red, 3 green and 5 blue balls. Two balls are drawn at random without replacement. What is the probability that both are red?

Answer: (d)
Solution: Total ways to draw 2 balls from 12 = C(12,2) = 66; ways to draw 2 red from 4 = C(4,2) = 6. The trap 2/11 uses 12/66, doubling the count by treating order as relevant. Probability = 6/66, so the answer is 1/11.
