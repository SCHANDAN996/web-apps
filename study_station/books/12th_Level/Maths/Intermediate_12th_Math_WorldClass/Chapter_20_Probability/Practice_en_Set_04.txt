76. A bag contains 5 red and 3 blue balls. Two balls are drawn at random without replacement. What is the probability that both are red?
(a) 5/8 (b) 25/64 (c) 10/28 (d) 5/14
Answer: (d)
Solution: The tempting option 25/64 comes from multiplying 5/8 by 5/8, which wrongly assumes replacement. Without replacement, P = (5/8) × (4/7) = 20/56 = 5/14, so the answer is 5/14.

77. Two dice are thrown together. What is the probability of getting a sum of 9?
(a) 1/6 (b) 1/9 (c) 1/12 (d) 5/36
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is counting only the pairs 4 and 5 in both orders and forgetting the pairs 3 and 6. Favourable outcomes are (3,6), (4,5), (5,4), (6,3), i.e. 4 out of 36, so the answer is 1/9.

78. A card is drawn from a well-shuffled pack of 52 cards. What is the probability that it is either a king or a heart?
(a) 4/13 (b) 16/52 (c) 17/52 (d) 15/52
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is adding 13 hearts and 4 kings to get 17/52, which double-counts the king of hearts. Correct count: 13 + 4 − 1 = 16, giving 16/52 = 4/13, so the answer is 4/13.

79. The probability that A solves a problem is 1/2 and that B solves it is 1/3. If both try independently, what is the probability that the problem is solved?
(a) 1/6 (b) 2/3 (c) 5/6 (d) 1/3
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is adding 1/2 + 1/3 = 5/6, which double-counts the case where both solve it. P(solved) = 1 − P(neither) = 1 − (1/2)(2/3) = 1 − 1/3 = 2/3, so the answer is 2/3.

Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is counting only the all-heads case. Favourable outcomes: HHT, HTH, THH, HHH, i.e. 4 out of 8, so the answer is 1/2.

Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is using (4/10)(6/10) = 6/25, which assumes replacement. Without replacement, P = (4/10)(6/9) = 24/90 = 4/15, so the answer is 4/15.

85. The letters of the word "RANDOM" are arranged at random. What is the probability that the vowels come together?
(a) 1/3 (b) 2/3 (c) 1/6 (d) 5/6
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is treating the vowel block as one letter and forgetting the internal arrangement of A and O. Treat the vowel pair as one unit: 5! × 2! = 240 arrangements out of 6! = 720, so the answer is 1/3.

86. A speaks the truth in 75% of cases and B in 80% of cases. What is the probability that they contradict each other on the same statement?
(a) 3/10 (b) 1/4 (c) 13/20 (d) 7/20
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is adding both truth probabilities. Contradiction means one is truthful and the other lies: (3/4)(1/5) + (1/4)(4/5) = 3/20 + 4/20 = 7/20, so the answer is 7/20.

87. A box contains 10 bulbs, of which 4 are defective. Two bulbs are drawn at random without replacement. What is the probability that at least one is defective?
(a) 7/15 (b) 1/3 (c) 2/3 (d) 8/15
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is computing only the exactly-one-defective case. Use complement: P(none defective) = (6/10)(5/9) = 1/3, so P(at least one) = 1 − 1/3 = 2/3, so the answer is 2/3.

89. Out of 9 persons, a committee of 4 is chosen at random. What is the probability that two particular persons are both included?
(a) 4/9 (b) 1/6 (c) 1/3 (d) 2/9
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is dividing 2 by 9 directly. Fix the two persons, then choose 2 more from the remaining 7: C(7,2) = 21 out of C(9,4) = 126, giving 21/126 = 1/6, so the answer is 1/6.

91. The probability of rain tomorrow is 0.4. What is the probability that it does not rain on two consecutive independent days?
(a) 0.48 (b) 0.16 (c) 0.6 (d) 0.36
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is squaring 0.4 to get 0.16, which is the probability of rain on both days. P(no rain) = 0.6 each day, so P = 0.6 × 0.6 = 0.36, so the answer is 0.36.

93. Two numbers are selected at random from 1 to 10 without replacement. What is the probability that their sum is even?
(a) 1/2 (b) 2/5 (c) 5/9 (d) 4/9
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is assuming 1/2 by symmetry, which fails because the numbers are drawn without replacement. Sum is even when both are even or both odd: [C(5,2) + C(5,2)]/C(10,2) = 20/45 = 4/9, so the answer is 4/9.

95. A and B toss a coin alternately; the first to get a head wins. If A starts, what is the probability that A wins?
(a) 1/2 (b) 2/3 (c) 3/4 (d) 5/8
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is assuming equal chances since the coin is fair. A wins on toss 1, 3, 5, and so on: P = 1/2 + (1/2)³ + (1/2)⁵ + … = (1/2)/(1 − 1/4) = 2/3, so the answer is 2/3.

Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is writing 3/2, which is a ratio, not a probability. Odds 3 : 2 in favour mean P = 3/(3 + 2) = 3/5, so the answer is 3/5.

100. A machine produces items of which 5% are defective. If 2 items are chosen at random independently, what is the probability that exactly one is defective?
(a) 0.095 (b) 0.0025 (c) 0.1 (d) 0.9025
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is computing 2 × 0.05 = 0.1, which ignores that the other item must be non-defective. P = 2 × (0.05)(0.95) = 0.095, so the answer is 0.095.
