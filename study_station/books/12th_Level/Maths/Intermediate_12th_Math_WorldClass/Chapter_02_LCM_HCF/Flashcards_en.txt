Card 1
Front: What is the HCF of two numbers, in one line?
Back: The largest number that divides both numbers exactly (also called GCD).

Card 2
Front: What is the LCM of two numbers, in one line?
Back: The smallest number that is exactly divisible by both numbers.

Card 3
Front: What is the golden product rule connecting LCM and HCF?
Back: For two numbers: LCM × HCF = Product of the two numbers. (Works only for TWO numbers!)

Card 4
Front: Mnemonic trick — how do you remember which of LCM/HCF is bigger?
Back: "LCM is Large, HCF is Humble." LCM ≥ numbers; HCF ≤ numbers.

Card 5
Front: What is the HCF of two co-prime numbers?
Back: 1. (Co-prime = no common factor other than 1, e.g., 8 and 15.)

Card 6
Front: What is the LCM of two co-prime numbers?
Back: Their product. E.g., LCM(8, 15) = 120.

Card 7
Front: In prime factorisation, how do you find HCF?
Back: Take each COMMON prime factor with its LOWEST power. HCF(2³·3², 2²·3⁵) = 2²·3².

Card 8
Front: In prime factorisation, how do you find LCM?
Back: Take EVERY prime factor present with its HIGHEST power. LCM(2³·3², 2²·3⁵) = 2³·3⁵.

Card 9
Front: Mnemonic trick — how to remember lowest vs highest powers for HCF/LCM?
Back: "HCF is Humble — takes the Lowest; LCM is Lavish — takes the Largest."

Card 10
Front: How do you find the HCF of fractions?
Back: HCF of fractions = HCF of numerators ÷ LCM of denominators.

Card 11
Front: How do you find the LCM of fractions?
Back: LCM of fractions = LCM of numerators ÷ HCF of denominators.

Card 12
Front: "Greatest number that divides a, b, c leaving the same remainder r" — what's the formula?
Back: HCF of (a−r, b−r, c−r).

Card 13
Front: "Greatest number that divides a, b, c leaving remainders p, q, s" — what's the formula?
Back: HCF of (a−p, b−q, c−s).

Card 14
Front: "Smallest number which when divided by a, b, c leaves the same remainder r" — formula?
Back: LCM(a, b, c) + r.

Card 15
Front: "Smallest number which when divided by a, b, c leaves remainders p, q, s" — what's the trick?
Back: If a−p = b−q = c−s = k (common difference), answer = LCM(a, b, c) − k.

Card 16
Front: Bells toll at intervals of a, b, c seconds — when do they ring together again?
Back: After LCM(a, b, c) seconds. (Classic "together again" = LCM keyword.)

Card 17
Front: Exam keyword trick — which word signals HCF and which signals LCM?
Back: "Greatest/Largest/Maximum size" → HCF. "Smallest/Least/Together again/Minimum" → LCM.

Card 18
Front: If HCF of two numbers is h, what form do the numbers take?
Back: ha and hb, where a and b are co-prime. Then LCM = h·a·b.

Card 19
Front: What is the LCM of 12, 18 and 24?
Back: 72. (12 = 2²·3, 18 = 2·3², 24 = 2³·3 → LCM = 2³·3² = 72.)

Card 20
Front: Can LCM × HCF = product work for three numbers?
Back: No! The product rule holds only for two numbers — a favourite exam trap.
