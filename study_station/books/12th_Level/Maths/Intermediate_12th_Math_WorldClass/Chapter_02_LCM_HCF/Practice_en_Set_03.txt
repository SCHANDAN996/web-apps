51. The LCM of 24, 36 and 40 is
(a) 720 (b) 240 (c) 360 (d) 180
Answer: (c)
Solution: The tempting option 720 comes from wrongly multiplying the numbers together. Prime factorise: 24 = 2³ × 3, 36 = 2² × 3², 40 = 2³ × 5. LCM = 2³ × 3² × 5 = 8 × 9 × 5 = 360, so the answer is 360.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Mathematics

52. The HCF of 96 and 404 is
(a) 16 (b) 8 (c) 12 (d) 4
Answer: (d)
Solution: Many students pick 8 because 96 is divisible by 8 — but 404 ÷ 8 = 50.5, so 8 does not divide 404. By Euclid's method: 404 = 96 × 4 + 20; 96 = 20 × 4 + 16; 20 = 16 × 1 + 4; 16 = 4 × 4 + 0. HCF = 4, so the answer is 4.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Mathematics

53. The product of two numbers is 2028 and their HCF is 13. The number of such pairs is
(a) 2 (b) 4 (c) 3 (d) 1
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is assuming only one pair exists. Let the numbers be 13a and 13b with a, b coprime. Then 169ab = 2028, so ab = 12. Coprime factor pairs of 12 are (1, 12) and (3, 4) — note (2, 6) is not coprime. Hence there are 2 pairs, so the answer is 2.
Source: PYQ-style

54. The LCM of two numbers is 495 and their HCF is 5. If one number is 45, the other number is
(a) 50 (b) 55 (c) 60 (d) 99
Answer: (b)
Solution: A common error is dividing 495 by 45 to get 11 and reporting that. Use the rule: product of numbers = LCM × HCF. Other number = (495 × 5) ÷ 45 = 2475 ÷ 45 = 55, so the answer is 55.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Mathematics

55. The smallest number which when divided by 12, 15, 20 and 54 leaves a remainder of 4 in each case is
(a) 536 (b) 540 (c) 548 (d) 544
Answer: (d)
Solution: The tempting answer 540 is just the LCM, forgetting the remainder. LCM of 12, 15, 20, 54 = 2² × 3³ × 5 = 540. Required number = 540 + 4 = 544, so the answer is 544.
Source: PYQ-style

56. The greatest number that divides 1657 and 2037 leaving remainders 6 and 5 respectively is
(a) 123 (b) 235 (c) 305 (d) 127
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is taking the HCF of 1657 and 2037 directly. Subtract the remainders first: 1657 − 6 = 1651 and 2037 − 5 = 2032. HCF of 1651 and 2032: 2032 − 1651 = 381; 1651 = 381 × 4 + 127; 381 = 127 × 3. HCF = 127, so the answer is 127.
Source: PYQ-style

57. Three bells ring at intervals of 12, 15 and 20 minutes. If they ring together at 10 a.m., they will next ring together at
(a) 1 p.m. (b) 11 a.m. (c) 10:30 a.m. (d) 12 noon
Answer: (b)
Solution: Students often multiply 12 × 15 × 20 = 3600 minutes, which is wrong because the numbers share factors. LCM of 12, 15, 20 = 60 minutes = 1 hour. Next simultaneous ring = 10 a.m. + 1 hour = 11 a.m., so the answer is 11 a.m.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Mathematics

58. The HCF and LCM of two numbers are 21 and 4641 respectively. If one number is 147, the other is
(a) 693 (b) 663 (c) 441 (d) 567
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is dividing 4641 by 147 and mishandling the non-integer result. Other number = (HCF × LCM) ÷ given number = (21 × 4641) ÷ 147 = 97461 ÷ 147 = 663, so the answer is 663.
Source: PYQ-style

59. The least number which is a perfect square and is divisible by each of 16, 20 and 24 is
(a) 3600 (b) 1600 (c) 14400 (d) 2400
Answer: (a)
Solution: The tempting option 2400 is a multiple of the LCM but is not a perfect square. LCM of 16, 20, 24 = 2⁴ × 3 × 5 = 240. To make a perfect square, every prime power must be even: multiply by 3 × 5 = 15, giving 240 × 15 = 3600 = 60², so the answer is 3600.
Source: PYQ-style

60. The ratio of two numbers is 3 : 4 and their HCF is 4. Their LCM is
(a) 12 (b) 16 (c) 24 (d) 48
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is taking the LCM of 3 and 4, i.e. 12, and stopping there. The numbers are 3 × 4 = 12 and 4 × 4 = 16. LCM of 12 and 16 = 48, so the answer is 48.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Mathematics

61. The greatest number of four digits which is divisible by 15, 25, 40 and 75 is
(a) 9800 (b) 9000 (c) 9600 (d) 9750
Answer: (c)
Solution: Many pick 9750 assuming it divides evenly, but 9750 ÷ 40 is not an integer. LCM of 15, 25, 40, 75 = 2³ × 3 × 5² = 600. Largest 4-digit number = 9999; 9999 ÷ 600 = 16 remainder 399, so required number = 9999 − 399 = 9600, so the answer is 9600.
Source: PYQ-style

62. The smallest number which when increased by 5 is exactly divisible by 8, 11 and 24 is
(a) 259 (b) 264 (c) 269 (d) 523
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is reporting the LCM itself, 264, forgetting the condition "increased by 5". LCM of 8, 11, 24 = 264. Required number = 264 − 5 = 259, so the answer is 259.
Source: PYQ-style

63. The HCF of 2923 and 3239 is
(a) 47 (b) 79 (c) 73 (d) 37
Answer: (b)
Solution: Guessing small primes like 37 is the common trap. Euclid: 3239 − 2923 = 316; 2923 = 316 × 9 + 79; 316 = 79 × 4 + 0. HCF = 79, so the answer is 79.
Source: PYQ-style

64. The LCM of 2/3, 4/9 and 5/6 is
(a) 10/3 (b) 20/9 (c) 20/3 (d) 40/9
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is applying the integer LCM rule to numerators only and writing 20/9 or similar. LCM of fractions = LCM of numerators ÷ HCF of denominators = LCM(2, 4, 5) ÷ HCF(3, 9, 6) = 20 ÷ 3 = 20/3, so the answer is 20/3.
Source: PYQ-style

65. The HCF of 3/4, 9/16 and 21/8 is
(a) 1/16 (b) 3/8 (c) 3/16 (d) 9/16
Answer: (c)
Solution: The tempting error is mixing up the formula and writing 3/8. HCF of fractions = HCF of numerators ÷ LCM of denominators = HCF(3, 9, 21) ÷ LCM(4, 16, 8) = 3 ÷ 16 = 3/16, so the answer is 3/16.
Source: PYQ-style

66. Six bells toll at intervals of 2, 4, 6, 8, 10 and 12 seconds. In 30 minutes, how many times do they toll together (including the start)?
(a) 16 (b) 15 (c) 31 (d) 30
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is dividing 1800 by 120 and answering 15, forgetting the toll at time zero. LCM of 2, 4, 6, 8, 10, 12 = 120 seconds. In 1800 seconds, number of simultaneous tolls = 1800 ÷ 120 + 1 = 15 + 1 = 16, so the answer is 16.
Source: PYQ-style

67. The sum of two numbers is 216 and their HCF is 27. The number of such pairs is
(a) 3 (b) 2 (c) 4 (d) 1
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is counting all pairs summing to 8, including non-coprime ones like (2, 6) and (4, 4). Let the numbers be 27a and 27b with a, b coprime; then 27(a + b) = 216, so a + b = 8. Coprime pairs are only (1, 7) and (3, 5), giving the number pairs (27, 189) and (81, 135). Hence there are 2 pairs, so the answer is 2.
Source: PYQ-style

68. The least multiple of 7 which leaves a remainder of 4 when divided by 6, 9, 15 and 18 is
(a) 94 (b) 364 (c) 184 (d) 274
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is taking LCM + 4 = 90 + 4 = 94, which is not a multiple of 7. LCM of 6, 9, 15, 18 = 90. Numbers of the form 90k + 4 must be divisible by 7: 90k + 4 ≡ 6k + 4 ≡ 0 (mod 7), so k ≡ 4 (mod 7). Smallest k = 4 gives 90 × 4 + 4 = 364 = 7 × 52, so the answer is 364.
Source: PYQ-style

69. The greatest length of a scale that can measure exactly 3 m 60 cm, 4 m 80 cm and 6 m is
(a) 60 cm (b) 40 cm (c) 120 cm (d) 90 cm
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is taking the HCF of 3, 4, 6 without converting everything to centimetres. Lengths are 360 cm, 480 cm, 600 cm. HCF of 360, 480, 600 = 120 cm, so the answer is 120 cm.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Mathematics

70. The LCM of 5, 8, 12 and 20 will not be a multiple of
(a) 9 (b) 5 (c) 8 (d) 3
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is checking the options only superficially against the LCM. LCM = 2³ × 3 × 5 = 120. 120 is divisible by 3, 8 and 5, but 120 ÷ 9 is not an integer since 120 has only one factor of 3. So the LCM is not a multiple of 9, so the answer is 9.
Source: PYQ-style

71. Two numbers are in the ratio 5 : 6 and their LCM is 480. Their HCF is
(a) 20 (b) 12 (c) 8 (d) 16
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is mishandling the ratio by taking the LCM of 5 and 6 as the HCF. Numbers are 5k and 6k; since 5 and 6 are coprime, LCM = 30k = 480, so k = 16. HCF = k = 16, so the answer is 16.
Source: PYQ-style

72. The smallest number which when divided by 5, 6, 7 and 8 leaves remainders 3, 4, 5 and 6 respectively is
(a) 840 (b) 837 (c) 838 (d) 843
Answer: (c)
Solution: The tempting answer 840 is the plain LCM, which leaves remainder 0. Since each divisor exceeds its remainder by exactly 2, the required number = LCM − 2 = 840 − 2 = 838. Verify: 838 = 5 × 167 + 3 = 6 × 139 + 4 = 7 × 119 + 5 = 8 × 104 + 6, so the answer is 838.
Source: PYQ-style

73. The HCF of two numbers is 11 and their LCM is 7700. If one number is 275, the other number is
(a) 318 (b) 279 (c) 283 (d) 308
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is computing 7700 ÷ 275 = 28 and reporting it. Other number = (HCF × LCM) ÷ given number = (11 × 7700) ÷ 275 = 84700 ÷ 275 = 308, so the answer is 308.
Source: PYQ-style

74. A rectangular courtyard 4.55 m long and 5.25 m broad is to be paved with square tiles of the same size. The minimum number of tiles required is
(a) 195 (b) 273 (c) 91 (d) 1001
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is using the LCM instead of the HCF for the tile size. Convert to centimetres: 455 cm and 525 cm. Largest square tile side = HCF(455, 525) = 35 cm. Tiles along length = 455 ÷ 35 = 13, along breadth = 525 ÷ 35 = 15, total = 13 × 15 = 195, so the answer is 195.
Source: PYQ-style

75. The product of the HCF and LCM of 18 and 24 is equal to
(a) 432 (b) 72 (c) 216 (d) 144
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is reporting the LCM 72 alone instead of the required product. HCF(18, 24) = 6, LCM(18, 24) = 72, and HCF × LCM = product of the numbers = 18 × 24 = 432, so the answer is 432.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Mathematics
