101. Find the LCM of 12, 18 and 24.
(a) 72 (b) 36 (c) 108 (d) 144
Answer: (a)
Solution: Prime factors: 12 = 2² × 3, 18 = 2 × 3², 24 = 2³ × 3. Taking the highest powers, LCM = 2³ × 3² = 8 × 9 = 72. The option 36 tempts because it is a common multiple of 12 and 18, but 36 is not divisible by 24, so the answer is 72.

102. Find the HCF of 36 and 84.
(a) 12 (b) 6 (c) 18 (d) 24
Answer: (a)
Solution: 36 = 2² × 3² and 84 = 2² × 3 × 7. Common factors with lowest powers: 2² × 3 = 12. The option 6 tempts because 6 divides both numbers, but it is not the greatest common divisor, so the answer is 12.

103. The product of two numbers is 216 and their HCF is 6. Find their LCM.
(a) 36 (b) 42 (c) 48 (d) 72
Answer: (a)
Solution: Product of two numbers = HCF × LCM, so LCM = 216 ÷ 6 = 36. The option 72 tempts if one multiplies 216 by 6 and then halves wrongly, but the rule is direct division, so the answer is 36.

104. Find the LCM of 2/3, 3/4 and 4/5.
(a) 24 (b) 1/60 (c) 12 (d) 2/15
Answer: (c)
Solution: LCM of fractions = LCM of numerators ÷ HCF of denominators = LCM(2, 3, 4) ÷ HCF(3, 4, 5) = 12 ÷ 1 = 12. The option 1/60 tempts because it is the HCF formula applied by mistake, so the answer is 12.

106. Two numbers are in the ratio 3 : 4 and their HCF is 5. Find their LCM.
(a) 30 (b) 45 (c) 90 (d) 60
Answer: (d)
Solution: The numbers are 3 × 5 = 15 and 4 × 5 = 20. LCM = (15 × 20) ÷ 5 = 60. The option 30 tempts if one forgets that the ratio parts 3 and 4 are coprime and halves the product wrongly, so the answer is 60.

107. Three bells toll at intervals of 12, 15 and 20 seconds. After how many seconds will they toll together?

Solution: They toll together at the LCM of 12, 15 and 20. LCM = 2² × 3 × 5 = 60 seconds. The option 120 tempts as it is also a common multiple, but the first simultaneous toll is at the least common multiple, so the answer is 60.

Answer: (c)

112. The sum of two numbers is 45 and their HCF is 5. If their LCM is 100, find the numbers.
(a) 15 and 30 (b) 20 and 25 (c) 10 and 35 (d) 5 and 40
Answer: (b)
Solution: Let the numbers be 5x and 5y with x, y coprime. Then 5(x + y) = 45, so x + y = 9, and LCM = 5xy = 100, so xy = 20. Coprime pair with sum 9 and product 20 is 4 and 5, giving 20 and 25. The pair 15 and 30 tempts as its sum is 45, but its HCF is 15, not 5, so the answer is 20 and 25.

121. Find the smallest number which when divided by 5, 6, 7 and 8 leaves remainder 3 in each case, but is exactly divisible by 9.
(a) 843 (b) 3363 (c) 2523 (d) 1683
Answer: (d)
Solution: LCM of 5, 6, 7, 8 = 840, so the number is of the form 840k + 3. For divisibility by 9: 840k + 3 ≡ 3k + 3 (mod 9), which is 0 when k = 2, giving 840 × 2 + 3 = 1683. The option 843 tempts as the smallest number with remainder 3, but 843 ÷ 9 leaves remainder 6, so the answer is 1683.

124. Six bells toll at intervals of 2, 4, 6, 8, 10 and 12 seconds. If they toll together now, how many times will they toll together in the next 30 minutes?
(a) 16 (b) 15 (c) 18 (d) 30
Answer: (a)
Solution: LCM of 2, 4, 6, 8, 10, 12 = 2³ × 3 × 5 = 120 seconds = 2 minutes. In 30 minutes they toll together at 2, 4, …, 30 minutes, i.e. 15 times, plus the toll at the start (now) counted in the span gives 30 ÷ 2 + 1 = 16 times. The option 15 tempts if one forgets to count the initial simultaneous toll, so the answer is 16.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Mathematics (Real Numbers)

125. The product of three numbers is 8100, their HCF is 3 and their LCM is 900. Find the sum of the three numbers.
(a) 96 (b) 90 (c) 102 (d) 108
Answer: (a)
Solution: Let the numbers be 3a, 3b, 3c. Then 27abc = 8100, so abc = 300, and LCM = 3 × lcm(a, b, c) = 900, so lcm(a, b, c) = 300. Since abc = lcm(a, b, c) = 300, the numbers a, b, c are pairwise coprime; 300 = 4 × 3 × 25, so a, b, c = 3, 4, 25. The numbers are 9, 12 and 75, sum = 96. The option 90 tempts from a wrong split like 5, 6, 10 which are not pairwise coprime, so the answer is 96.
