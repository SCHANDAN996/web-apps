26. Find the LCM of 12, 18 and 24.
(a) 36 (b) 48 (c) 144 (d) 72
Answer: (d)
Solution: The tempting option 144 comes from multiplying numbers blindly, but LCM is not a product. Prime factors: 12 = 2² × 3, 18 = 2 × 3², 24 = 2³ × 3. Take the highest powers: 2³ × 3² = 8 × 9 = 72, so the answer is 72.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Mathematics, Real Numbers

27. Find the HCF of 84 and 126.
(a) 14 (b) 42 (c) 21 (d) 63
Answer: (b)
Solution: Option 21 looks right because it divides both numbers, but it is not the greatest common divisor. Prime factors: 84 = 2² × 3 × 7, 126 = 2 × 3² × 7. Common factors with lowest powers: 2 × 3 × 7 = 42, so the answer is 42.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Mathematics, Real Numbers

28. The product of two numbers is 2160 and their HCF is 12. Find their LCM.
(a) 120 (b) 150 (c) 240 (d) 180
Answer: (d)
Solution: A common trap is dividing the product by the LCM instead of the HCF. Use the rule: Product of two numbers = HCF × LCM. So LCM = 2160 ÷ 12 = 180, so the answer is 180.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Mathematics, Real Numbers

29. The LCM of two numbers is 495 and their HCF is 5. If one number is 45, find the other.
(a) 50 (b) 55 (c) 60 (d) 65
Answer: (b)
Solution: Many students multiply 495 by 5 and then divide wrongly; the correct relation is Product = HCF × LCM. Other number = (5 × 495) ÷ 45 = 2475 ÷ 45 = 55, so the answer is 55.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Mathematics, Real Numbers

30. Find the least number which when divided by 15, 20 and 36 leaves a remainder of 8 in each case.
(a) 188 (b) 180 (c) 368 (d) 728
Answer: (a)
Solution: Option 180 is the trap — it is only the LCM and leaves remainder 0, not 8. LCM of 15, 20, 36 = 180. Required number = 180 + 8 = 188, so the answer is 188.
Source: PYQ-style

31. Find the greatest number which divides 245 and 1029 leaving remainders 5 and 9 respectively.
(a) 60 (b) 20 (c) 40 (d) 30
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is to take the HCF of 245 and 1029 directly, ignoring the remainders. Subtract remainders first: 245 − 5 = 240 and 1029 − 9 = 1020. HCF of 240 and 1020 = 60, so the answer is 60.
Source: PYQ-style

32. Three bells ring at intervals of 12, 15 and 20 minutes. If they ring together at 9 a.m., when will they next ring together?
(a) 9:30 a.m. (b) 10:30 a.m. (c) 10:00 a.m. (d) 11:00 a.m.
Answer: (c)
Solution: Option 9:30 a.m. is tempting if one takes the HCF instead of the LCM. LCM of 12, 15, 20 = 60 minutes = 1 hour. Next ringing together = 9 a.m. + 1 hour = 10:00 a.m., so the answer is 10:00 a.m.
Source: PYQ-style

33. Find the LCM of 2/3, 4/5 and 6/7.
(a) 12 (b) 12/35 (c) 2/35 (d) 24/35
Answer: (a)
Solution: Option 12/35 is the trap — that is the HCF of fractions, not the LCM. LCM of fractions = LCM of numerators ÷ HCF of denominators = LCM(2, 4, 6) ÷ HCF(3, 5, 7) = 12 ÷ 1 = 12, so the answer is 12.
Source: PYQ-style

34. Find the HCF of 3/4, 9/16 and 15/32.
(a) 45/32 (b) 3/16 (c) 3/32 (d) 9/32
Answer: (c)
Solution: Option 45/32 is the trap — it comes from taking the LCM of numerators, which is used for LCM of fractions, not HCF. HCF of fractions = HCF of numerators ÷ LCM of denominators = HCF(3, 9, 15) ÷ LCM(4, 16, 32) = 3 ÷ 32, so the answer is 3/32.
Source: PYQ-style

35. The HCF and LCM of two numbers are 13 and 1989 respectively. If one number is 117, find the other.
(a) 169 (b) 221 (c) 234 (d) 273
Answer: (b)
Solution: A common slip is dividing 1989 by 117 directly, forgetting the HCF factor. Other number = (HCF × LCM) ÷ one number = (13 × 1989) ÷ 117 = 25857 ÷ 117 = 221, so the answer is 221.
Source: PYQ-style

36. Find the smallest number which when divided by 8, 12 and 15 leaves no remainder.
(a) 60 (b) 120 (c) 90 (d) 240
Answer: (b)
Solution: Option 60 is tempting but 60 is not divisible by 8. LCM of 8, 12, 15: 8 = 2³, 12 = 2² × 3, 15 = 3 × 5, so LCM = 2³ × 3 × 5 = 120, so the answer is 120.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Mathematics, Real Numbers

37. Find the greatest number of 4 digits which is exactly divisible by 12, 15, 18 and 27.
(a) 9720 (b) 9828 (c) 9900 (d) 9990
Answer: (a)
Solution: Option 9990 is the trap — it is close to 9999 but not divisible by all four numbers. LCM of 12, 15, 18, 27 = 540. Largest 4-digit number = 9999; 9999 ÷ 540 leaves remainder 279, so required number = 9999 − 279 = 9720, so the answer is 9720.
Source: PYQ-style

38. Find the smallest number of 5 digits which is exactly divisible by 16, 24, 36 and 54.
(a) 10800 (b) 10368 (c) 10476 (d) 10296
Answer: (b)
Solution: Option 10800 is tempting as a round number, but 10800 ÷ 16 is not exact. LCM of 16, 24, 36, 54 = 432. Smallest 5-digit number = 10000; 10000 ÷ 432 leaves remainder 64, so required number = 10000 + (432 − 64) = 10368, so the answer is 10368.
Source: PYQ-style

39. The ratio of two numbers is 3 : 4 and their HCF is 4. Find their LCM.
(a) 12 (b) 24 (c) 36 (d) 48
Answer: (d)
Solution: Option 12 is the trap — it is just 3 × 4, ignoring the HCF. Numbers are 3 × 4 = 12 and 4 × 4 = 16. LCM of 12 and 16 = 48, so the answer is 48.
Source: PYQ-style

40. Two numbers are in the ratio 5 : 6 and their LCM is 120. Find their HCF.
(a) 5 (b) 2 (c) 4 (d) 6
Answer: (c)
Solution: Option 6 is tempting because it appears in the ratio, but the ratio parts are not the actual numbers. Let numbers be 5x and 6x; their LCM = 30x = 120, so x = 4. HCF = x = 4, so the answer is 4.
Source: PYQ-style

41. Find the least number which when divided by 5, 6, 7 and 8 leaves remainder 3 in each case, but is divisible by 9.
(a) 843 (b) 2523 (c) 1683 (d) 3363
Answer: (c)
Solution: Option 843 is the trap — it is LCM + 3 = 840 + 3, but 843 is not divisible by 9. LCM of 5, 6, 7, 8 = 840. Numbers of the form 840k + 3: for k = 2, 1683 ÷ 9 = 187 exactly, so the answer is 1683.
Source: PYQ-style

42. The sum of two numbers is 216 and their HCF is 27. How many such pairs of numbers are possible (order of the two numbers does not matter)?
(a) 4 (b) 3 (c) 6 (d) 2
Answer: (d)
Solution: Option 4 is the trap — it counts ordered pairs like 27, 189 and 189, 27 as different. Let numbers be 27a and 27b with a + b = 8 and a, b coprime. Coprime pairs with a < b: only 1 + 7 and 3 + 5, since 2 + 6 and 4 + 4 are not coprime, so the answer is 2.
Source: PYQ-style

43. Find the greatest number that will divide 43, 91 and 183 so as to leave the same remainder in each case.
(a) 13 (b) 4 (c) 9 (d) 7
Answer: (b)
Solution: Option 7 is tempting since 91 = 7 × 13, but 7 does not leave equal remainders. Required number = HCF of differences: 91 − 43 = 48, 183 − 91 = 92, 183 − 43 = 140. HCF of 48, 92, 140 = 4, so the answer is 4.
Source: PYQ-style

44. The LCM of two numbers is 48 and the numbers are in the ratio 2 : 3. Find the sum of the numbers.
(a) 28 (b) 32 (c) 40 (d) 64
Answer: (c)
Solution: Option 64 is the trap — it comes from taking the numbers as 32 and 48 by misreading the ratio. Let numbers be 2x and 3x; LCM = 6x = 48, so x = 8. Numbers are 16 and 24; sum = 40, so the answer is 40.
Source: PYQ-style

45. Find the HCF of 96, 144 and 192.
(a) 48 (b) 24 (c) 36 (d) 12
Answer: (a)
Solution: Option 24 is tempting because it divides all three numbers, but it is not the greatest. Prime factors: 96 = 2⁵ × 3, 144 = 2⁴ × 3², 192 = 2⁶ × 3. Common lowest powers: 2⁴ × 3 = 48, so the answer is 48.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Mathematics, Real Numbers

46. A rectangular courtyard 3.78 m long and 5.25 m wide is to be paved with square tiles of the same size. Find the least number of such tiles.
(a) 450 (b) 90 (c) 180 (d) 225
Answer: (a)
Solution: Option 90 is the trap — it comes from a division slip after finding the tile size. Convert to cm: 378 and 525; HCF = 21 cm, the largest tile side. Tiles along length = 378 ÷ 21 = 18, along width = 525 ÷ 21 = 25, so total tiles = 18 × 25 = 450, so the answer is 450.
Source: PYQ-style

47. Find the least number which when diminished by 7 is divisible by 12, 16, 18 and 21.
(a) 1001 (b) 1008 (c) 1022 (d) 1015
Answer: (d)
Solution: Option 1008 is the trap — it is exactly the LCM, but the number must exceed the LCM by 7. LCM of 12, 16, 18, 21 = 1008. Required number = 1008 + 7 = 1015, so the answer is 1015.
Source: PYQ-style

48. The HCF of two numbers is 11 and their LCM is 7700. If one number is 275, find the other.
(a) 279 (b) 283 (c) 308 (d) 318
Answer: (c)
Solution: A common error is dividing 7700 by 275 and stopping there, which gives 28 — forgetting the HCF factor. Other number = (11 × 7700) ÷ 275 = 84700 ÷ 275 = 308, so the answer is 308.
Source: PYQ-style

49. Six bells toll at intervals of 2, 4, 6, 8, 10 and 12 seconds. In 30 minutes, how many times do they toll together (including the start)?
(a) 4 (b) 10 (c) 15 (d) 16
Answer: (d)
Solution: Option 15 is the trap — it forgets to count the tolling at time zero. LCM of 2, 4, 6, 8, 10, 12 = 120 seconds = 2 minutes. In 30 minutes they toll together 30 ÷ 2 = 15 times after the start, plus the initial toll = 16, so the answer is 16.
Source: PYQ-style

50. Find the greatest number which on dividing 1657 and 2037 leaves remainders 6 and 5 respectively.
(a) 127 (b) 123 (c) 235 (d) 305
Answer: (a)
Solution: Option 123 is tempting as it nearly divides 1657 − 6 = 1651, but it fails for the other number. Subtract remainders: 1657 − 6 = 1651 and 2037 − 5 = 2032. HCF of 1651 and 2032: 2032 − 1651 = 381; 1651 = 4 × 381 + 127; 381 = 3 × 127, so HCF = 127, so the answer is 127.
Source: PYQ-style
