1. Find the HCF of 12 and 18.
(a) 6 (b) 3 (c) 2 (d) 9
Answer: (a)
Solution: The tempting option 3 is only a common factor, not the greatest one. Factors of 12 are 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 12 and of 18 are 1, 2, 3, 6, 9, 18; the greatest common factor is 6, so the answer is 6.
Source: NCERT Class 6 Mathematics

2. Find the LCM of 4 and 6.
(a) 12 (b) 24 (c) 8 (d) 6
Answer: (a)
Solution: The tempting option 24 is the product of the two numbers, but LCM is the smallest common multiple, not the product. Multiples of 4 are 4, 8, 12, 16… and of 6 are 6, 12, 18…; the smallest common multiple is 12, so the answer is 12.
Source: NCERT Class 6 Mathematics

3. The HCF of two co-prime numbers is always:
(a) 0 (b) their product (c) 1 (d) their sum
Answer: (c)
Solution: The tempting option "their product" is actually the LCM of co-prime numbers, not the HCF. Co-prime numbers share no common factor except 1, so the answer is 1.
Source: NCERT Class 6 Mathematics

4. Find the LCM of 5 and 7.
(a) 12 (b) 35 (c) 1 (d) 70
Answer: (b)
Solution: The tempting option 70 is double the required value; since 5 and 7 are co-prime, their LCM equals their product, which is 5 × 7 = 35, so the answer is 35.
Source: NCERT Class 6 Mathematics

5. Find the HCF of 24 and 36.
(a) 6 (b) 12 (c) 8 (d) 18
Answer: (b)
Solution: The tempting option 6 is a common factor but not the greatest. 24 = 2³ × 3 and 36 = 2² × 3²; taking the lowest powers of common primes gives 2² × 3 = 12, so the answer is 12.
Source: NCERT Class 6 Mathematics

6. Find the LCM of 3, 4 and 5.
(a) 30 (b) 12 (c) 60 (d) 120
Answer: (c)
Solution: The tempting option 30 misses the factor 4. Since 3, 4, 5 are pairwise co-prime except sharing nothing common, LCM = 3 × 4 × 5 = 60, so the answer is 60.
Source: NCERT Class 6 Mathematics

7. The product of two numbers is 216 and their HCF is 6. Their LCM is:
(a) 36 (b) 72 (c) 18 (d) 216
Answer: (a)
Solution: The tempting option 216 is just the product itself, ignoring the formula. Using LCM × HCF = product of the numbers, LCM = 216 ÷ 6 = 36, so the answer is 36.

8. Find the HCF of 15 and 25.

Answer: (c)
Solution: The tempting option 15 is a factor of 15 but does not divide 25. 15 = 3 × 5 and 25 = 5 × 5; the only common prime is 5, so the answer is 5.

12. The HCF of 9 and 27 is:

13. If one number is a multiple of the other, their LCM is:
(a) the larger number (b) the smaller number (c) their product (d) their HCF
Answer: (a)
Solution: The tempting option "their product" is larger than needed. When one number divides the other, every multiple of the larger is already a multiple of the smaller, so the answer is the larger number.

16. The LCM of two numbers is 60 and their HCF is 5. If one number is 20, the other is:
(a) 10 (b) 15 (c) 12 (d) 30
Answer: (b)
Solution: The tempting option 12 divides 60 but fails the product check. Using product = LCM × HCF = 60 × 5 = 300, the other number = 300 ÷ 20 = 15, so the answer is 15.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Mathematics

17. Find the HCF of 100 and 75.
(a) 5 (b) 25 (c) 10 (d) 50
Answer: (b)
Solution: The tempting option 10 does not even divide 75. 100 = 2² × 5² and 75 = 3 × 5²; the common part is 5² = 25, so the answer is 25.

19. The smallest number which is exactly divisible by 6, 8 and 10 is:
(a) 240 (b) 480 (c) 60 (d) 120
Answer: (d)
Solution: The tempting option 240 is a common multiple but not the smallest. 6 = 2 × 3, 8 = 2³, 10 = 2 × 5; LCM = 2³ × 3 × 5 = 120, so the answer is 120.

21. Find the HCF of 72, 108 and 144.

Answer: (d)
Solution: The tempting option 12 is a common factor but not the greatest. 72 = 2³ × 3², 108 = 2² × 3³, 144 = 2⁴ × 3²; taking lowest powers of common primes gives 2² × 3² = 36, so the answer is 36.

22. The LCM of two numbers is 120 and their HCF is 4. If one number is 24, find the other.
(a) 20 (b) 16 (c) 30 (d) 40
Answer: (a)
Solution: The tempting option 30 gives LCM 120 with 24 but its HCF with 24 is 6, not 4. Product = 120 × 4 = 480, so the other number = 480 ÷ 24 = 20, and indeed HCF of 24 and 20 is 4, so the answer is 20.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Mathematics

23. Find the least number which when divided by 12, 15 and 20 leaves a remainder of 5 in each case.
(a) 60 (b) 65 (c) 125 (d) 305
Answer: (b)
Solution: The tempting option 60 is the LCM itself, which leaves remainder 0, not 5. LCM of 12, 15, 20 = 60; adding the common remainder gives 60 + 5 = 65, so the answer is 65.

24. The greatest number that divides 165 and 275 leaving no remainder is:
(a) 11 (b) 25 (c) 5 (d) 55
Answer: (d)
Solution: The tempting option 11 divides 165 but 275 ÷ 11 = 25 exactly too — however it is not the greatest. 165 = 3 × 5 × 11 and 275 = 5² × 11; HCF = 5 × 11 = 55, so the answer is 55.
Source: NCERT Class 6 Mathematics

25. Three bells ring at intervals of 4, 6 and 8 minutes. If they ring together at 9 a.m., when will they next ring together?
(a) 10:00 a.m. (b) 9:48 a.m. (c) 9:12 a.m. (d) 9:24 a.m.
Answer: (d)
Solution: The tempting option 9:48 a.m. comes from multiplying 4 × 6 × 8 ÷ 4 wrongly; the correct step is the LCM. LCM of 4, 6, 8 = 24 minutes, so they ring together at 9:24 a.m., so the answer is 9:24 a.m.
Source: PYQ-style (RRB NTPC pattern)
