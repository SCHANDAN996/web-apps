🪄 SHORT TRICKS — PERCENTAGE
(10–15 Unforgettable Memory Tricks)

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
🧠 TRICK 1 — The "Double Zero Killer" (Keyword Method)
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
To find x% of y: kill two zeros, multiply the rest.

Example: 40% of 250 → kill the two zeros of % and the number:
4 × 25 = 100. Done!

Memory hook: "Percent means PER-CENT — per HUNDRED. Two zeros must DIE before you multiply."

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
🧠 TRICK 2 — "SWAP IT!" (The Commutativity Trick)
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
x% of y = y% of x. Always!

Hard: 8% of 25 → Swap it → 25% of 8 = 2. Instant!

Memory hook: "When the numbers look ugly, SWAP the percent sign like swapping seats in a train — the journey (answer) stays the same."

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
🧠 TRICK 3 — The Fraction Family Photo (Loci Method)
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
Imagine a family photo, biggest to smallest:

1/2 = 50% (Dad — half the house)
1/3 = 33.33% (Mom)
1/4 = 25% (Big brother)
1/5 = 20% (Sister)
1/6 = 16.66% (You)
1/8 = 12.5% (Little brother)
1/10 = 10% (The baby)
1/12 = 8.33% (The pet dog 🐕)

Memory hook: "Bigger the denominator, smaller the family member's share of the pizza."

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
🧠 TRICK 4 — "10% is Just a Decimal Dance"
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
10% of anything = move the decimal one step LEFT.

10% of 470 = 47 | 10% of 83 = 8.3 | 10% of 6 = 0.6

Then build everything from it:
5% = half of 10% | 20% = double 10% | 15% = 10% + 5%

Example: 15% of 240 → 24 + 12 = 36.

Memory hook: "Ten percent trips on the stairs and falls one place to the left."

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
🧠 TRICK 5 — The "RIP" Acronym for % Change
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
% Change = (Change ÷ Original) × 100

R — Result difference (new − old)
I — Initial value (divide by THIS, always the original!)
P — Percent (multiply by 100)

Memory hook: "The wrong answer RIPs in peace when you divide by the new value instead of the original."

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
🧠 TRICK 6 — The "Multiplier Ladder" (Successive Change)
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
For successive changes of a% and b%:

Net % = a + b + (ab/100)

Memory hook: "A and B climb a ladder: first A climbs, then B climbs, then they shake hands (a×b) on the 100th rung."

Example: +20% then +10% → 20 + 10 + 2 = 32% (NOT 30%!)

⚠️ Sign rule: increase = +, decrease = −.
+20% then −10% → 20 − 10 − 2 = 8% increase.

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
🧠 TRICK 7 — "The Price-Pocket Seesaw" (Consumption Rule)
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
If price rises by r%, cut consumption by r/(100+r) × 100% to keep spending same.
If price falls by r%, you may consume r/(100−r) × 100% more.

Memory hook: "Price goes UP the seesaw, consumption must come DOWN — but by a SMALLER fraction, because the denominator grew fatter (100+r)."

Example: Price ↑ 25% → cut consumption by 25/125 = 20%.

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
🧠 TRICK 8 — "The Exam Marks Elevator"
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
Pass mark problems: (Marks scored) + (Shortfall) = Pass mark.

Example: A scores 30% and fails by 15 marks; B scores 40% and gets 35 marks more than pass mark.
Difference: 10% = 15 + 35 = 50 marks → 100% = 500.

Memory hook: "One student is 15 floors BELOW the pass floor, the other is 35 floors ABOVE it. Total gap = 50 floors = 10%."

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
🧠 TRICK 9 — "The 1% Detective" (Unitary Method)
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
Never find 100% directly. First find 1%, then multiply.

Example: 13% of a number = 52 → 1% = 4 → 100% = 400.

Memory hook: "A detective never solves the whole case at once — he finds ONE clue (1%) and the whole story opens up."

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
🧠 TRICK 10 — "X-Y Reversal" (Population & Value Change)
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
If A is x% more than B, then B is x/(100+x) × 100% less than A.
If A is x% less than B, then B is x/(100−x) × 100% more than A.

Memory hook: "Going up is a short ladder, coming down is a long slide — the percentages are NEVER equal in reverse."

Example: A is 25% more than B → B is 25/125 = 20% less than A. (Not 25%!)

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
🧠 TRICK 11 — "The Compound Growth Doubling Clock"
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
Population/value growing at r% per year for n years:
Final = Initial × (1 + r/100)ⁿ

Memory hook: "Every year the value puts on a (1 + r/100) jacket. After n years, it's wearing n jackets — multiply, don't add!"

Example: 10,000 growing 10% for 2 years = 10,000 × 1.1 × 1.1 = 12,100.

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
🧠 TRICK 12 — "VOTE: Victory Or Total Elimination" (Election Trick)
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
In a 2-candidate election, if winner gets x% of votes:
Winner's votes − Loser's votes = (2x − 100)% of total = Margin.

Memory hook: "Winner takes x%, loser takes (100−x)%. The MARGIN is the gap: x − (100−x) = 2x − 100."

Example: Winner gets 62% → margin = 24% of total votes. If total = 5,000 → margin = 1,200 votes.

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
🧠 TRICK 13 — "The Fraction-to-Percent ATM PIN"
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
Memorize this PIN code for life:

1/3 = 33.33% | 1/6 = 16.66% | 1/9 = 11.11% | 1/11 = 9.09%
1/7 = 14.28% | 1/8 = 12.5% | 1/12 = 8.33% | 1/15 = 6.66%

Memory hook: "The 3-6-9 family repeats (33.33, 16.66, 11.11) — like an ATM PIN you can never forget: thirds, sixths, ninths all stutter!"

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
🧠 TRICK 14 — "Base-100 Boss Rule" (Comparing Numbers)
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
To compare A% of X with B% of Y, convert both to actual values — never compare percentages directly.

Memory hook: "Percentages are masks; the BASE is the face. 50% of 200 (100) beats 90% of 100 (90) — always unmask before judging!"

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
🧠 TRICK 15 — "The Net-Zero Illusion" (Increase then Decrease)
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
A number increased by x% then decreased by x% is NEVER back to original — it always LOSES x²/100 %.

Example: +10% then −10% → net = 10 − 10 − 1 = −1% loss.

Memory hook: "What goes up by the stairs comes down by the same stairs — but lands one step LOWER. The square (x²/100) is the missing step."

⚠️ Exam trap: Options always include "No change" — it's the trap answer!
