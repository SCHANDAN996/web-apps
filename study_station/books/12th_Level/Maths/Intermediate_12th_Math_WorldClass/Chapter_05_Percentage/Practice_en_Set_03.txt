51. A number is increased by 20% and then decreased by 20%. The net change in the number is:
(a) No change (b) 2% decrease (c) 4% increase (d) 4% decrease
Answer: (d)
Solution: The tempting answer is "no change", but the 20% decrease applies to the increased value, not the original. Net change = 20 − 20 − (20×20)/100 = −4%, so the answer is 4% decrease.
Source: NCERT Class 8 Mathematics (Comparing Quantities)

52. If the price of sugar rises by 25%, by what percentage should consumption be reduced so that expenditure remains the same?
(a) 25% (b) 20% (c) 15% (d) 30%
Answer: (b)
Solution: Many students pick 25%, assuming the same percentage reverses the rise, but the reduction is on the new higher price. Required reduction = 25/125 × 100 = 20%, so the answer is 20%.

53. In an election, a candidate who got 40% of the votes lost by 200 votes. The total number of votes polled was:
(a) 800 (b) 1200 (c) 1000 (d) 500
Answer: (c)
Solution: The loser got 40%, so the winner got 60%; the margin is 20% of total votes, not 40% as some assume. 20% of total = 200, so total = 1000, so the answer is 1000.

54. A's salary is 25% more than B's salary. By what percentage is B's salary less than A's?
(a) 25% (b) 20% (c) 22.5% (d) 33.33%
Answer: (b)
Solution: Choosing 25% is the trap, because the base changes from B to A. If B = 100, A = 125; B is less by 25/125 × 100 = 20%, so the answer is 20%.

55. The population of a town increases by 10% every year. If the present population is 12100, what was it 2 years ago?
(a) 10000 (b) 11000 (c) 9800 (d) 10500
Answer: (a)
Solution: Subtracting 10% twice to get 11000 is wrong because the base differs each year. Population 2 years ago = 12100/(1.1)² = 12100/1.21 = 10000, so the answer is 10000.
Source: NCERT Class 8 Mathematics (Comparing Quantities)

56. A student scored 30% marks and failed by 15 marks. Another student scored 40% marks and got 35 marks more than the pass marks. The maximum marks are:
(a) 450 (b) 500 (c) 400 (d) 600
Answer: (b)
Solution: The gap between the two scores is 10% of maximum, which equals 15 + 35 = 50 marks; taking only 15 or only 35 is the trap. 10% of maximum = 50, so maximum = 500, so the answer is 500.

59. A man spends 75% of his income. His income increases by 20% and his expenditure increases by 10%. His savings increase by:
(a) 40% (b) 50% (c) 25% (d) 30%
Answer: (b)
Solution: Let income = 100, expenditure = 75, savings = 25. New income = 120, new expenditure = 82.5, new savings = 37.5. Increase = 12.5/25 × 100 = 50%; assuming savings rise by 20 − 10 = 10% is the trap, so the answer is 50%.

60. In a class, 60% of the students are boys. If 20% of the boys and 30% of the girls pass an exam, what percentage of the class failed?
(a) 76% (b) 24% (c) 70% (d) 72%
Answer: (a)
Solution: Pass percentage = 60%×20% + 40%×30% = 12% + 12% = 24%. Failed = 100 − 24 = 76%; reporting the pass figure 24% instead of the failed figure is the trap, so the answer is 76%.

61. The value of a machine depreciates by 10% annually. If its present value is ₹81000, its value 2 years hence will be:
(a) ₹72900 (b) ₹70000 (c) ₹64800 (d) ₹65610
Answer: (d)
Solution: Subtracting 20% of 81000 (giving 64800) is the trap, since depreciation compounds. Value = 81000 × 0.9 × 0.9 = 81000 × 0.81 = 65610, so the answer is ₹65610.
Source: NCERT Class 8 Mathematics (Comparing Quantities)

62. If x% of y is equal to y% of 100, then x equals:
(a) 10000/y (b) y (c) 100/y (d) 100
Answer: (d)
Solution: x% of y = xy/100 and y% of 100 = y. Equating: xy/100 = y, which gives x = 100; the trap is assuming x must depend on y, so the answer is 100.
Source: NCERT Class 8 Mathematics (Comparing Quantities)

63. A number is first increased by 10% and then the result is increased by 20%. The overall percentage increase is:
(a) 32% (b) 35% (c) 28% (d) 30%
Answer: (a)
Solution: Adding 10 + 20 = 30% is the trap because the second increase acts on the new base. Net = 10 + 20 + (10×20)/100 = 32%, so the answer is 32%.

64. In an examination, 35% of students failed in Hindi, 45% failed in English and 20% failed in both. The percentage of students who passed in both subjects is:
(a) 45% (b) 60% (c) 40% (d) 50%
Answer: (c)
Solution: Failed in at least one = 35 + 45 − 20 = 60%; forgetting to subtract the overlap 20% is the trap. Passed in both = 100 − 60 = 40%, so the answer is 40%.

66. A village has 5000 people. 52% are males and the rest females. If 30% of males and 20% of females are literate, the total number of literate people is:
(a) 1300 (b) 1200 (c) 1260 (d) 1350
Answer: (c)
Solution: Males = 2600, females = 2400. Literate = 30% of 2600 + 20% of 2400 = 780 + 480 = 1260; applying one literacy rate to the whole population is the trap, so the answer is 1260.

67. If 15% of A is equal to 25% of B, then 40% of A is what percent of B?
(a) 66.67% (b) 60% (c) 75% (d) 50%
Answer: (a)
Solution: 15A = 25B gives A = 5B/3. Then 40% of A = (2/5)(5B/3) = 2B/3 ≈ 66.67% of B; assuming the ratio is 15:25 directly is the trap, so the answer is 66.67%.

74. If the numerator of a fraction is increased by 20% and the denominator is decreased by 20%, the fraction becomes 3/2. The original fraction is:
(a) 3/4 (b) 5/4 (c) 1 (d) 4/5
Answer: (c)
Solution: New fraction = (1.2N)/(0.8D) = 1.5 × (N/D) = 3/2, so N/D = 1; ignoring the base changes on both parts is the trap, so the answer is 1.

75. A man gave 30% of his money to his wife, 40% of the remaining to his son, and was left with ₹8400. How much money did he have initially?
(a) ₹20000 (b) ₹18000 (c) ₹24000 (d) ₹15000
Answer: (a)
Solution: After giving 30%, 70% remains; after giving 40% of that, 60% of 70% = 42% remains. 42% of total = 8400, so total = 20000; computing 40% of the original instead of the remainder is the trap, so the answer is ₹20000.
