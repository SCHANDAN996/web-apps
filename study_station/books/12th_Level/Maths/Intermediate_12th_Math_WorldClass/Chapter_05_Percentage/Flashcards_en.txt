Card 1
Front: What does "per cent" literally mean?
Back: "Per hundred" (from Latin per centum). So 45% simply means 45 out of every 100.

Card 2
Front: How do you convert a percentage to a fraction in one step?
Back: Divide by 100. Example: 35% = 35/100 = 7/20.

Card 3
Front: How do you convert a fraction to a percentage in one step?
Back: Multiply by 100. Example: 3/5 = (3/5) × 100 = 60%.

Card 4
Front: What is the trick to remember common fraction–percentage pairs like 1/3, 1/6, 1/8?
Back: "Family of 100": 1/2 = 50%, 1/3 = 33.33%, 1/4 = 25%, 1/5 = 20%, 1/6 = 16.66%, 1/8 = 12.5%, 1/10 = 10%. Just remember the "100 ÷ denominator" trick — 100/8 = 12.5.

Card 5
Front: A number is increased by 20% and then decreased by 20%. Is it back to the original?
Back: No! Net change = +20 − 20 − (20×20)/100 = −4%. The number ends up 4% lower.

Card 6
Front: What is the successive percentage change formula?
Back: Net % change = a + b + (ab/100), where a and b are the two successive changes (use negative sign for decrease).

Card 7
Front: If the price of an item rises by 25%, by what % must consumption fall to keep expenditure the same?
Back: Reduction = r/(100+r) × 100 = 25/125 × 100 = 20%.

Card 8
Front: If A's salary is 25% more than B's, by what % is B's salary less than A's?
Back: 25/125 × 100 = 20%. Trick: "more by r% → less by r/(100+r) × 100 %".

Card 9
Front: What is the mnemonic for the "more/less" salary trick?
Back: "More over MORE, Less over LESS": If A is r% MORE than B, then B is r/(100+r) × 100 % less than A. If A is r% LESS than B, then B is r/(100−r) × 100 % more than A. The denominator takes the same word as the first statement.

Card 10
Front: What is the population after 2 years if it grows at 10% per year from 10,000?
Back: 10,000 × (1.1)² = 10,000 × 1.21 = 12,100. Use the compound formula P(1 + r/100)ⁿ.

Card 11
Front: How do you quickly find 15% of any number without a calculator?
Back: 15% = 10% + 5%. Find 10% (shift decimal one place left), then add half of it. Example: 15% of 240 = 24 + 12 = 36.

Card 12
Front: What is the fastest way to compute x% of y?
Back: Remember x% of y = y% of x. So 8% of 25 = 25% of 8 = 2. Swap to whichever is easier!

Card 13
Front: In an exam, a student needs 40% to pass. He gets 180 marks and fails by 20 marks. What are the maximum marks?
Back: Pass marks = 180 + 20 = 200 = 40% of total. Total = 200 × 100/40 = 500.

Card 14
Front: What is percentage point vs percentage change — why do examiners trap you here?
Back: If interest rises from 4% to 6%, that's a rise of 2 percentage POINTS but a 50% percentage CHANGE. Mixing them is a classic trap.

Card 15
Front: If a rectangle's length increases 10% and breadth decreases 10%, what happens to its area?
Back: Net change = 10 − 10 − (10×10)/100 = −1%. Area decreases by 1%.

Card 16
Front: What is the mnemonic to remember that percentage problems are just "base × rate"?
Back: "BRB — Base, Rate, Bring it together": Part = Base × Rate. Every percentage question is just finding one of these three when two are given.

Card 17
Front: A number increased by x% gives 240. How do you find the original number?
Back: Original = 240 × 100/(100 + x). Always divide the final value by (100 ± x)/100.

Card 18
Front: If 30% of a number is 90, what is 70% of that number?
Back: Number = 90 × 100/30 = 300. So 70% = 210. Shortcut: 70% = (90/30) × 70 = 210.

Card 19
Front: In a mixture, what does "20% alcohol" actually tell you?
Back: 20 litres of alcohol per 100 litres of total mixture — percentage is always parts per hundred of the WHOLE.

Card 20
Front: Why does a 50% loss need a 100% gain to recover?
Back: Losing 50% halves the value (100 → 50). To return from 50 to 100 you must double it — a 100% gain. Recovery % = r/(100−r) × 100.
