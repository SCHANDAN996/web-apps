The Feynman Technique: Intuitive Percentage Mastery

Think of percentage not as a test score out of 100, but as a zoom lens that rescales reality.

1. The Invariant Product Tug-of-War (AB Rule):
Imagine a rectangle of Area = Length * Width. If you stretch the length by 25% (making it 5/4 of original), the width MUST shrink to 4/5 of original to keep the same area. Shrinking to 4/5 means dropping by 1/5, which is 20%! Notice: an increase of 1/4 requires a decrease of 1/(4+1) = 1/5. If price jumps by 1/3 (33.3%), consumption must drop by 1/(3+1) = 1/4 (25%). The top number stays the same; the bottom number just adds the top!

2. The Symmetrical Shift Fallacy (+20% then -20% is NOT zero):
If you have $100 and gain 20%, you now have $120. If you then lose 20%, you are losing 20% of ONE HUNDRED TWENTY, which is $24! You end up with $96. You lost $4! Why? Because the base of the decrease was bigger than the base of the increase. You always lose x^2 / 100 percent.

3. The Voter List Pipeline:
Think of an election as water flowing through filters:
Total Enrolled Voters -> Filter 1 (Deduct Non-Voters) -> Polled Votes -> Filter 2 (Deduct Invalid Votes) -> Valid Votes. Only the valid votes are split between Winner and Loser. Always identify which pool of votes each percentage refers to!
