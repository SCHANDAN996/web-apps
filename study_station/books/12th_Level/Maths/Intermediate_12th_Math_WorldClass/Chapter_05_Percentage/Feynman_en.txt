# The Pizza Shop Mystery — Understanding Percentage Like a 12-Year-Old

## The Hardest Concept: "Percentage OF" vs "Percentage CHANGE"

## The Story

Ravi's father runs a small pizza shop. One evening Ravi looked at the accounts book and got confused.

"Papa," he said, "yesterday you sold pizzas worth ₹800. Today you sold pizzas worth ₹1,000. The newspaper boy said your sales went up by 25%. But when I calculated 25% of ₹1,000, I got ₹250, and ₹1,000 − ₹250 = ₹750, not ₹800. Something is wrong!"

His father smiled. "Nothing is wrong, beta. You mixed up the two different meanings of percentage. Let me show you."

He drew two boxes on a paper:

**Box 1 — "Percentage OF":** 25% **of** ₹1,000 means take 25 parts out of every 100 parts of ₹1,000.
25% of 1,000 = (25/100) × 1,000 = ₹250.

**Box 2 — "Percentage CHANGE":** Sales **rose by** 25% means compare the *change* with the *starting* value.
Change = 1,000 − 800 = ₹200. Starting value = ₹800.
Percentage change = (200/800) × 100 = 25%.

"See?" said his father. "In Box 1, the base is ₹1,000. In Box 2, the base is ₹800 — the value *before* the change. Same word 'percentage', different base. That is the whole mystery."

Ravi thought for a moment. "So when the question says 'find 20% of 450', the base is 450. But when it says 'a price increased from 450 to 540, find the percentage increase', the base is the *old* value, 450?"

"Exactly," said his father. "Always ask one question first: **percentage of WHAT?** Find the base, and the problem is half solved."

## The Trick

Before solving any percentage question, write down:

1. **What is the base?** (the number after "of", or the *original/old* value in a change question)
2. **Which type is it?**
   - "x% of y" → answer = (x/100) × y
   - "x% change from old to new" → answer = (change/old) × 100, where change = new − old

**Quick check:** If a value rises from 80 to 100, the increase is (20/80) × 100 = 25%. But if it falls back from 100 to 80, the decrease is (20/100) × 100 = 20%. Same ₹20 change, different percentages — because the base changed. This is why a 25% rise followed by a 25% fall does **not** bring you back to the start.

## Try It Yourself

1. Find 15% of 240.
   → (15/100) × 240 = **36**

2. A shirt's price rose from ₹500 to ₹575. Find the percentage increase.
   → Change = 75, base = 500 → (75/500) × 100 = **15%**

3. A number decreased from 200 to 170. Find the percentage decrease.
   → Change = 30, base = 200 → (30/200) × 100 = **15%**

4. Population of a village was 2,000 last year and is 2,200 this year. Find the percentage increase.
   → (200/2,000) × 100 = **10%**

## The One-Line Lesson

**Percentage is always "of" something — find the base first, and the rest is just multiply and divide.**
