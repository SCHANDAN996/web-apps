## 1. The Real Villain: Doing Operations in the Wrong Order

Let's start with a trap that has fooled lakhs of aspirants:

**What is 6 + 4 × 5?**

If you said 50, you added first — and fell into the classic trap. The correct answer is **26**, because multiplication comes *before* addition: 6 + (4 × 5) = 6 + 20 = 26.

Maths has a strict queue, like a railway reservation counter. Some operations are VIPs and go first. Break the queue, and your answer breaks too.

### BODMAS — The VIP Queue of Maths

| Letter | Stands for | Priority |
|---|---|---|
| **B** | Brackets ( ) , { } , [ ] | 1st — always |
| **O** | Orders (powers, roots, "of") | 2nd |
| **D** | Division | 3rd (left to right) |
| **M** | Multiplication | 3rd (left to right) |
| **A** | Addition | 4th (left to right) |
| **S** | Subtraction | 4th (left to right) |

**Important:** Division and Multiplication have *equal* rank — solve whichever comes first from the **left**. Same for Addition and Subtraction.

**Example:** 24 ÷ 4 × 3 = ?
- Wrong: 24 ÷ 12 = 2 (multiplied first — queue broken!)
- Right: (24 ÷ 4) × 3 = 6 × 3 = **18**

### The Memory Trick 🎭

Remember BODMAS as: **"Big Oxen Drink Milk And Sleep"** — a giant ox who drinks milk and immediately falls asleep. Absurd? Yes. Unforgettable? Also yes. (Brackets, Orders, Division, Multiplication, Addition, Subtraction.)

🧠 **Active Recall:** Without looking up — what does the 'O' in BODMAS stand for? And in 100 ÷ 10 × 5, which operation do you do first? (Answer: Orders; division, because it comes first from the left.)

## 2. Brackets Have Their Own Mini-Queue

Inside BODMAS, brackets themselves follow an order:

1. **Bar / Vinculum** (a line over numbers, e.g., $\overline{5-3}$) — first
2. **Small brackets ( )**
3. **Curly brackets { }**
4. **Square brackets [ ]**

**Example:** 20 − [12 − {8 − (6 − $\overline{4-2}$)}]
- Bar first: $\overline{4-2}$ = 2
- Small bracket: 6 − 2 = 4
- Curly: 8 − 4 = 4
- Square: 12 − 4 = 8
- Final: 20 − 8 = **12**

Work from the **innermost bracket outward**, like peeling an onion layer by layer.

🧠 **Active Recall:** Which bracket is solved before ( )? (Answer: the bar/vinculum.)

## 3. "Of" — The Small Word That Causes Big Losses

In simplification, **"of" means multiplication**, but it must be solved *before* division and multiplication signs.

**Example:** 36 ÷ 6 of 2 + 5 = ?
- "Of" first: 6 of 2 = 12
- Then: 36 ÷ 12 + 5 = 3 + 5 = **8**
- Trap answer: 36 ÷ 6 = 6, then 6 × 2 = 12, +5 = 17 ❌

Examiners *love* this trap. Now you love it too — because it's a free mark.

## 4. Fractions, Decimals, Percentages — The Three Disguises of One Number

Half the battle in simplification is converting quickly between these three forms.

**Must-memorise conversions:**

| Fraction | Decimal | Percentage |
|---|---|---|
| 1/2 | 0.5 | 50% |
| 1/3 | 0.333… | 33.33% |
| 1/4 | 0.25 | 25% |
| 1/5 | 0.2 | 20% |
| 1/6 | 0.1666… | 16.66% |
| 1/8 | 0.125 | 12.5% |
| 3/4 | 0.75 | 75% |
| 2/5 | 0.4 | 40% |

**Why this matters:** "37.5% of 160" looks scary. But 37.5% = 3/8, so the question is just (3/8) × 160 = **60**. Ten seconds, done.

**Adding fractions:** use the LCM of denominators.
$$\frac{2}{3} + \frac{3}{4} = \frac{8 + 9}{12} = \frac{17}{12} = 1\frac{5}{12}$$

🧠 **Active Recall:** What is 12.5% as a fraction? What is 62.5% of 80? (Answer: 1/8; 62.5% = 5/8, so 5/8 × 80 = 50.)

## 5. Surds and Indices — Powers Without Panic

**Laws of indices (your toolkit):**

| Law | Formula | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Product | $a^m \times a^n = a^{m+n}$ | $2^3 \times 2^2 = 2^5 = 32$ |
| Quotient | $a^m \div a^n = a^{m-n}$ | $3^5 \div 3^2 = 3^3 = 27$ |
| Power of power | $(a^m)^n = a^{mn}$ | $(2^2)^3 = 2^6 = 64$ |
| Zero power | $a^0 = 1$ | $99^0 = 1$ |
| Negative power | $a^{-n} = \frac{1}{a^n}$ | $2^{-3} = \frac{1}{8}$ |
| Fractional power | $a^{1/n} = \sqrt[n]{a}$ | $27^{1/3} = 3$ |

**Simplifying surds:** break the number inside the root into a perfect square × remainder.
$$\sqrt{72} = \sqrt{36 \times 2} = 6\sqrt{2}$$

**Rationalising the denominator:** multiply top and bottom by the root.
$$\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}} = \frac{1 \times \sqrt{2}}{\sqrt{2} \times \sqrt{2}} = \frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}$$

🧠 **Active Recall:** What is $5^0$? What is $\sqrt{200}$ in simplest surd form? (Answer: 1; $\sqrt{100 \times 2} = 10\sqrt{2}$.)

## 6. Squares, Cubes, and Roots — Your Mental Calculator

Memorise these cold. They appear *everywhere*.

**Squares (1–30, the essentials):** 11² = 121, 12² = 144, 13² = 169, 14² = 196, 15² = 225, 16² = 256, 17² = 289, 18² = 324, 19² = 361, 20² = 400, 25² = 625, 30² = 900.

**Cubes (1–15):** 2³ = 8, 3³ = 27, 4³ = 64, 5³ = 125, 6³ = 216, 7³ = 343, 8³ = 512, 9³ = 729, 10³ = 1000, 11³ = 1331, 12³ = 1728, 13³ = 2197, 14³ = 2744, 15³ = 3375.

**Quick square trick (numbers ending in 5):** For 65² — multiply the first digit by (itself + 1): 6 × 7 = 42, then attach 25 → **4225**. Works for any number ending in 5: 85² = 8×9 then 25 = **7225**.

🧠 **Active Recall:** Use the trick — what is 75²? (Answer: 7 × 8 = 56, attach 25 → 5625.)

## 7. Approximation — When the Exam *Wants* You to Estimate

Bank exams (IBPS, SBI PO) love questions like:

**≈ What is 49.98 × 20.03 ÷ 9.9?**

Don't calculate exactly! Round: 50 × 20 ÷ 10 = **100**. Check the options — the closest one wins.

**Rules of approximation:**
- Round to the nearest "friendly" number (whole tens, easy squares).
- √63.8 ≈ √64 = 8. (24.9)² ≈ 25² = 625.
- Approximation is valid **only when options are far apart**. If options are close (like 100, 102, 104), calculate properly.

## 8. The Classic Exam Patterns

**Pattern 1 — Mixed BODMAS:**
$$48 \div 8 \times 3 + 12 - 5 = 6 \times 3 + 12 - 5 = 18 + 12 - 5 = \mathbf{25}$$

**Pattern 2 — Find the missing number:**
$$\frac{?}{15} = \frac{24}{45} \implies ? = \frac{24 \times 15}{45} = \mathbf{8}$$

**Pattern 3 — Decimal operations:**
$$0.4 \times 0.04 \times 0.004 = 0.000064$$
(Count total decimal places: 1 + 2 + 3 = 6 places.)

**Pattern 4 — Continued fractions:** solve from the **bottom up**.
