# Simplification — Explained Like You're 12

## The Hardest Concept: Why BODMAS Actually Works

Imagine this. It's your birthday, and your mom hands you a note:

**"Your gift money = 10 + 5 × 2 rupees."**

Your little cousin shouts, "That's 10 + 5 = 15, then 15 × 2 = **30 rupees!**"

But your mom smiles and says, "No beta, it's 5 × 2 = 10 first, then 10 + 10 = **20 rupees.**"

Who is right? And more importantly — **why**?

## The Cricket Team Story 🏏

Think of maths operations as players in a cricket team, waiting to bat.

- **Brackets** are the captain. Captain always bats FIRST. No argument.
- **Orders** (powers, roots, like 5² or √16) are the vice-captain. Bats second.
- **Division and Multiplication** are the star openers. They bat together, third.
- **Addition and Subtraction** are the tail-enders. They bat last.

Now here's the secret nobody tells you: **BODMAS is not a law of nature. It's an agreement.**

Long ago, mathematicians around the world realised that if everyone solved 10 + 5 × 2 their own way, one person would get 30 and another 20 — and bridges, banks, and exam papers would all collapse into chaos. So they made a **queue rule**: strong operations (×, ÷) go before weak ones (+, −). Multiplication is just "repeated addition" — it's addition on steroids — so naturally it gets priority.

That's it. BODMAS is just a queue at a ticket counter. The stronger player doesn't wait behind the weaker one.

## One More Trap: The "Of" Monster 👹

In exams, you'll see things like: **24 ÷ 4 of 2**

"Of" (जैसे "4 का 2 गुना") is a hidden multiplication that jumps the queue — it binds tighter than ÷. So:

- 4 of 2 = 4 × 2 = 8
- Then 24 ÷ 8 = **3** ✅

If you blindly went left to right (24 ÷ 4 = 6, then 6 × 2 = 12), the "Of" monster just ate your marks. ❌

## The Trick You'll Never Forget 🪄

**"Captain BODMAS never shares his batting order."**

Say it like a movie title. Picture a strict captain in sunglasses holding a bat labelled **B-O-D-M-A-S**:

> **B**rackets → **O**f/Orders → **D**ivision & **M**ultiplication (left to right) → **A**ddition & **S**ubtraction (left to right)

And remember the golden rule inside each level: **D and M are twins** — whoever comes first from the left goes first. Same for A and S. They don't fight; they just take turns left to right.

## Why This Wins You Exams

In SSC, Banking, and Railway exams, simplification questions are pure speed games. The examiner's favourite trap? Writing the question so that the *wrong* order gives a tempting answer sitting right there in the options. If you respect the queue — captain first, tail-enders last — the trap can never catch you.
