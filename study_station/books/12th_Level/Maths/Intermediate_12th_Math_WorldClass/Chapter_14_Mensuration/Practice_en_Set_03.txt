Questions 51–75: Medium

51. The area of a circle is 616 cm². Its circumference is (take π = 22/7):
(a) 176 cm (b) 88 cm (c) 44 cm (d) 132 cm
Answer: (b)
Solution: πr² = 616 gives r² = 616 × 7/22 = 196, so r = 14 cm. The trap is stopping at r = 14 and picking a diameter-related value. Circumference = 2πr = 2 × (22/7) × 14 = 88, so the answer is 88 cm.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Mathematics

52. A rectangular park is 60 m long and 40 m wide. A path 2 m wide runs inside it along the boundary. The area of the path is:
(a) 368 m² (b) 400 m² (c) 416 m² (d) 384 m²
Answer: (d)
Solution: Inner rectangle = (60 − 4) × (40 − 4) = 56 × 36 = 2016 m². Path area = 2400 − 2016 = 384 m². The trap is subtracting only 2 m from each side (58 × 38 = 2204, giving 196), forgetting the path is on both sides, so the answer is 384 m².
Source: NCERT Class 8 Mathematics

53. The diagonal of a square is 12√2 cm. Its area is:
(a) 288 cm² (b) 144 cm² (c) 72 cm² (d) 96 cm²
Answer: (b)
Solution: Area = d²/2 = (12√2)²/2 = 288/2 = 144 cm². The trap is using d² directly and answering 288, forgetting the division by 2, so the answer is 144 cm².
Source: NCERT Class 10 Mathematics

54. The radius of a cylinder is 7 cm and height is 10 cm. Its total surface area is (π = 22/7):
(a) 748 cm² (b) 440 cm² (c) 616 cm² (d) 528 cm²
Answer: (a)
Solution: TSA = 2πr(h + r) = 2 × (22/7) × 7 × 17 = 748 cm². The trap is computing only the curved surface 2πrh = 440 and forgetting the two circular ends, so the answer is 748 cm².
Source: NCERT Class 9 Mathematics

55. The sides of a triangle are 13 cm, 14 cm and 15 cm. Its area is:
(a) 84 cm² (b) 96 cm² (c) 72 cm² (d) 90 cm²
Answer: (a)
Solution: s = (13 + 14 + 15)/2 = 21. Area = √[21 × 8 × 7 × 6] = √7056 = 84 cm². The trap is an arithmetic slip in s (taking s = 20 gives √[20×7×6×5] = √4200, not a clean value), so the answer is 84 cm².
Source: NCERT Class 9 Mathematics

56. A wheel of radius 35 cm makes 100 revolutions. The distance covered is (π = 22/7):
(a) 220 m (b) 110 m (c) 440 m (d) 22 m
Answer: (a)
Solution: Circumference = 2 × (22/7) × 35 = 220 cm. Distance = 220 × 100 = 22000 cm = 220 m. The trap is forgetting to convert cm to m and answering 22000 or misreading as 22, so the answer is 220 m.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Mathematics

57. The volume of a cube is 512 cm³. Its total surface area is:
(a) 256 cm² (b) 512 cm² (c) 384 cm² (d) 320 cm²
Answer: (c)
Solution: a³ = 512 gives a = 8 cm. TSA = 6a² = 6 × 64 = 384 cm². The trap is using 4a² (lateral area) = 256, forgetting all six faces, so the answer is 384 cm².
Source: NCERT Class 8 Mathematics

58. The area of a trapezium is 180 cm² and its height is 12 cm. If one parallel side is 18 cm, the other is:
(a) 10 cm (b) 15 cm (c) 12 cm (d) 14 cm
Answer: (c)
Solution: 180 = ½ × 12 × (18 + x) gives 18 + x = 30, so x = 12 cm. The trap is forgetting the ½ and solving 180 = 12 × (18 + x), which gives a negative value and signals the error, so the answer is 12 cm.
Source: NCERT Class 8 Mathematics

59. A cone has radius 7 cm and slant height 25 cm. Its curved surface area is (π = 22/7):
(a) 275 cm² (b) 704 cm² (c) 1100 cm² (d) 550 cm²
Answer: (d)
Solution: CSA = πrl = (22/7) × 7 × 25 = 550 cm². The trap is using the vertical height instead of slant height, or adding the base area to get 704 (total surface area), so the answer is 550 cm².
Source: NCERT Class 9 Mathematics

60. The perimeter of a semicircle of radius 7 cm is (π = 22/7):
(a) 44 cm (b) 22 cm (c) 36 cm (d) 29 cm
Answer: (c)
Solution: Perimeter = πr + 2r = 22 + 14 = 36 cm. The trap is taking only the curved part πr = 22 cm and forgetting the diameter, so the answer is 36 cm.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Mathematics

61. A rectangular field has area 5400 m² and length 90 m. The cost of fencing its boundary at ₹25 per metre is:
(a) ₹9000 (b) ₹6000 (c) ₹7500 (d) ₹6750
Answer: (c)
Solution: Breadth = 5400/90 = 60 m. Perimeter = 2(90 + 60) = 300 m. Cost = 300 × 25 = ₹7500. The trap is fencing only length + breadth (150 m → ₹3750) instead of the full boundary, so the answer is ₹7500.
Source: NCERT Class 8 Mathematics

62. The ratio of the radii of two circles is 3 : 4. The ratio of their areas is:
(a) 16 : 9 (b) 3 : 4 (c) 6 : 8 (d) 9 : 16
Answer: (d)
Solution: Areas are in ratio r₁² : r₂² = 9 : 16. The trap is keeping the linear ratio 3 : 4 for areas, forgetting that area scales with the square of the radius, so the answer is 9 : 16.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Mathematics

63. A cylinder and a cone have the same base radius and same height. The ratio of their volumes is:
(a) 2 : 1 (b) 1 : 3 (c) 3 : 1 (d) 1 : 1
Answer: (c)
Solution: Cylinder volume = πr²h; cone volume = (1/3)πr²h. Ratio = 3 : 1. The trap is inverting the ratio to 1 : 3 by putting the cone first, so the answer is 3 : 1.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Mathematics

64. The area of a rhombus is 240 cm² and one diagonal is 16 cm. The other diagonal is:
(a) 15 cm (b) 30 cm (c) 24 cm (d) 20 cm
Answer: (b)
Solution: Area = ½ × d₁ × d₂, so 240 = ½ × 16 × d₂, giving d₂ = 30 cm. The trap is omitting the ½ and computing d₂ = 240/16 = 15, so the answer is 30 cm.
Source: NCERT Class 8 Mathematics

65. A room is 8 m long, 6 m wide and 4 m high. The area of its four walls is:
(a) 112 m² (b) 96 m² (c) 160 m² (d) 208 m²
Answer: (a)
Solution: Area of four walls = 2h(l + b) = 2 × 4 × (8 + 6) = 112 m². The trap is adding floor and ceiling (total surface 208 m²) when only walls are asked, so the answer is 112 m².
Source: NCERT Class 8 Mathematics

66. The circumference of a circle is 44 cm. Its area is (π = 22/7):
(a) 77 cm² (b) 308 cm² (c) 616 cm² (d) 154 cm²
Answer: (d)
Solution: 2πr = 44 gives r = 7 cm. Area = πr² = (22/7) × 49 = 154 cm². The trap is doubling the area (308) by confusing diameter with radius midway, so the answer is 154 cm².
Source: NCERT Class 10 Mathematics

67. A solid metallic sphere of radius 6 cm is melted and recast into a cylinder of radius 4 cm. The height of the cylinder is:
(a) 24 cm (b) 12 cm (c) 18 cm (d) 9 cm
Answer: (c)
Solution: Sphere volume = (4/3)π × 216 = 288π. Cylinder: π × 16 × h = 288π gives h = 18 cm. The trap is using surface areas instead of volumes, so the answer is 18 cm.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Mathematics

68. The length of a rectangle is increased by 20% and breadth decreased by 20%. The net change in area is:
(a) 2% decrease (b) 4% increase (c) no change (d) 4% decrease
Answer: (d)
Solution: New area = 1.2 × 0.8 = 0.96 of original, i.e. 4% decrease. The trap is assuming the changes cancel out (no change), ignoring the multiplicative effect, so the answer is 4% decrease.
Source: NCERT Class 8 Mathematics

69. A sector of a circle of radius 14 cm has central angle 90°. Its area is (π = 22/7):
(a) 154 cm² (b) 616 cm² (c) 308 cm² (d) 77 cm²
Answer: (a)
Solution: Sector area = (90/360) × πr² = ¼ × (22/7) × 196 = 154 cm². The trap is taking the full circle area 616 and forgetting the quarter fraction, so the answer is 154 cm².
Source: NCERT Class 10 Mathematics

70. The diagonal of a cube is 6√3 cm. Its volume is:
(a) 108 cm³ (b) 216 cm³ (c) 72 cm³ (d) 144 cm³
Answer: (b)
Solution: Diagonal = a√3 = 6√3 gives a = 6 cm. Volume = a³ = 216 cm³. The trap is using a√2 (square diagonal) giving a = 6√3/√2 and a messy volume, so the answer is 216 cm³.
Source: NCERT Class 8 Mathematics

71. A circular garden of radius 21 m has a 3.5 m wide path around it. The area of the path is (π = 22/7):
(a) 462 m² (b) 500.5 m² (c) 539 m² (d) 385 m²
Answer: (b)
Solution: Outer radius = 24.5 m. Path area = π(24.5² − 21²) = (22/7)(600.25 − 441) = (22/7) × 159.25 = 500.5 m². The trap is computing π(R − r)² = π × 12.25 = 38.5, confusing the ring area formula, so the answer is 500.5 m².
Source: NCERT Class 10 Mathematics

72. The volume of a cone is 1570 cm³ and its height is 15 cm. Its base radius is (π = 3.14):
(a) 10 cm (b) 7 cm (c) 14 cm (d) 5 cm
Answer: (a)
Solution: (1/3) × 3.14 × r² × 15 = 1570 gives 15.7 r² = 1570, so r² = 100 and r = 10 cm. The trap is omitting the 1/3 factor, which gives r² = 100/3 and a non-clean value, so the answer is 10 cm.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Mathematics

73. The perimeter of a rectangle is 64 cm and its length is 20 cm. Its area is:
(a) 320 cm² (b) 240 cm² (c) 200 cm² (d) 280 cm²
Answer: (b)
Solution: 2(l + b) = 64 gives l + b = 32, so b = 12 cm. Area = 20 × 12 = 240 cm². The trap is taking b = 64 − 20 = 44 (forgetting the factor 2), giving an absurd area, so the answer is 240 cm².
Source: NCERT Class 8 Mathematics

74. A hemisphere of radius 7 cm has total surface area (π = 22/7):
(a) 154 cm² (b) 308 cm² (c) 616 cm² (d) 462 cm²
Answer: (d)
Solution: TSA of hemisphere = 3πr² = 3 × (22/7) × 49 = 462 cm². The trap is using 2πr² = 308 (curved surface only) and forgetting the flat circular base, so the answer is 462 cm².
Source: NCERT Class 9 Mathematics

75. The area of an equilateral triangle is 16√3 cm². Its perimeter is:
(a) 24 cm (b) 16 cm (c) 32 cm (d) 12 cm
Answer: (a)
Solution: (√3/4)a² = 16√3 gives a² = 64, so a = 8 cm. Perimeter = 3 × 8 = 24 cm. The trap is stopping at a = 8 and reporting the side instead of the perimeter, so the answer is 24 cm.
