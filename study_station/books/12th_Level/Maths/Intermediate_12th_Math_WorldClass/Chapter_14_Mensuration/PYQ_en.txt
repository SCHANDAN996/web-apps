# PYQ Pattern Analysis

This chapter is usually tested through direct, fact-based questions. In most exams, the examiner checks whether the student can identify the correct term, match related facts, and avoid confusing similar names or concepts.

## Question patterns commonly seen

- **Direct identification:** A definition, feature, or description is given, and the correct term must be selected.
- **Matching-type questions:** Related persons, places, events, terms, or classifications must be matched correctly.
- **Statement-based questions:** Two or more statements are given, and the student must identify the correct statement or combination.
- **Chronology questions:** Events, developments, or arrangements must be placed in the correct order.
- **Exception questions:** Words such as **NOT**, **EXCEPT**, or **incorrect** are used to reverse the usual direction of the question.
- **Close-option questions:** Options contain similar names, dates, places, or terms, so careful reading is essential.

## Common traps used by examiners

- Replacing one word in an otherwise correct statement.
- Interchanging two similar terms, names, places, or dates.
- Presenting a partially correct statement as completely correct.
- Using negative wording such as “Which of the following is **not** correct?”
- Keeping all options familiar so that the question appears easier than it is.
- Changing the sequence in chronology-based questions.

## How to attempt these questions

1. Read the complete question before looking at the options.
2. Underline or mentally note words such as **not**, **except**, **correct**, and **incorrect**.
3. Recall the basic definition or fact first; then compare it with every option.
4. Eliminate options containing absolute or exaggerated claims unless the fact clearly supports them.
5. In matching questions, first match the pairs you know confidently.
6. In statement-based questions, judge each statement independently before selecting the combination.
7. Do not choose an option merely because it looks familiar; verify the exact wording.

## Preparation strategy

- Revise the chapter’s key terms, classifications, important names, and distinguishing features.
- Prepare short comparison notes for concepts that are commonly confused.
- Practise statement-based and negative-worded questions separately.
- After every practice set, note whether the mistake was caused by weak recall, careless reading, or confusion between similar options.
- Revisit flashcards and the mind map before attempting another set.
