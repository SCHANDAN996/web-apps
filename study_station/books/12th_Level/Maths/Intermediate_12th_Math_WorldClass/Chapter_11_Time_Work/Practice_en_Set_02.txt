26. A can do a piece of work in 12 days and B can do it in 18 days. If they work together, in how many days will they complete the work?
(a) 7.2 days (b) 8 days (c) 6.5 days (d) 9 days
Answer: (a)
Solution: The tempting wrong option is 8 days, obtained by wrongly averaging 12 and 18 and halving. Correct method: A's 1 day work = 1/12, B's = 1/18. Together = 1/12 + 1/18 = 3/36 + 2/36 = 5/36, so time = 36/5 = 7.2 days, so the answer is 7.2 days.
Source: NCERT Class 8 Mathematics (direct and inverse proportions)

27. A and B together can finish a work in 10 days. A alone can finish it in 15 days. In how many days can B alone finish the work?
(a) 25 days (b) 30 days (c) 35 days (d) 20 days
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is 25 days, found by simply adding 10 and 15. B's 1 day work = 1/10 − 1/15 = 3/30 − 2/30 = 1/30, so B alone takes 30 days, so the answer is 30 days.
Source: PYQ-style

28. A can do a work in 20 days. He works for 5 days and then B finishes the remaining work in 15 days. In how many days can B alone do the whole work?
(a) 24 days (b) 18 days (c) 20 days (d) 30 days
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is 15 days, assuming B's finishing time equals his full time. In 5 days A completes 5/20 = 1/4 of the work, leaving 3/4. B does 3/4 of the work in 15 days, so full work takes 15 × 4/3 = 20 days, so the answer is 20 days.
Source: PYQ-style

29. 6 men can complete a work in 12 days. How many men are needed to complete the same work in 8 days?
(a) 9 (b) 8 (c) 10 (d) 12
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is 8, confusing days with men. Total work = 6 × 12 = 72 man-days. Men needed for 8 days = 72/8 = 9, so the answer is 9.
Source: NCERT Class 8 Mathematics (inverse proportion)

30. A is twice as efficient as B. Together they finish a work in 14 days. In how many days can A alone finish it?
(a) 21 days (b) 18 days (c) 24 days (d) 28 days
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is 28 days, doubling 14 without considering combined efficiency. Let B's rate be x, then A's rate is 2x; together 3x = 1/14, so x = 1/42 and A's rate = 2/42 = 1/21. A alone takes 21 days, so the answer is 21 days.
Source: PYQ-style

31. A and B can do a work in 12 days, B and C in 15 days, and C and A in 20 days. In how many days will A, B and C together complete the work?
(a) 8 days (b) 12 days (c) 10 days (d) 15 days
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is 12 days, taking the smallest pair time. Adding: 2(A+B+C) = 1/12 + 1/15 + 1/20 = 5/60 + 4/60 + 3/60 = 12/60 = 1/5. So A+B+C = 1/10 per day; together they take 10 days, so the answer is 10 days.
Source: PYQ-style

32. A can do a work in 10 days and B in 15 days. They work together for 4 days, then A leaves. In how many more days will B finish the remaining work?
(a) 3 days (b) 4 days (c) 6 days (d) 5 days
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is 6 days, forgetting A also worked during the 4 days. In 4 days together they complete 4 × (1/10 + 1/15) = 4 × 1/6 = 2/3. Remaining = 1/3, which B does in (1/3) ÷ (1/15) = 5 days, so the answer is 5 days.
Source: PYQ-style

33. 12 women can do a work in 20 days. After working for 8 days, 4 women leave. In how many total days will the work be completed?
(a) 28 days (b) 26 days (c) 30 days (d) 24 days
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is 28 days, wrongly scaling the full 20 days. Total work = 12 × 20 = 240 woman-days. In 8 days, 96 woman-days are done; remaining = 144 woman-days. With 8 women it takes 144/8 = 18 more days, so total = 8 + 18 = 26 days, so the answer is 26 days.
Source: PYQ-style

34. A can build a wall in 12 days and B can destroy it in 18 days. If they work on alternate days starting with A, in how many days will the wall be completely built?
(a) 70 days (b) 72 days (c) 71 days (d) 69 days
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is 72 days, assuming a whole number of 2-day cycles finishes the job. Net work in 2 days = 1/12 − 1/18 = 1/36, so after 35 cycles (70 days) the work done is 35/36. On day 71, A adds 1/12 = 3/36, taking the total past 1, so the wall is completed on day 71, so the answer is 71 days.
Source: PYQ-style

35. A is 50% more efficient than B. If B alone can do a work in 30 days, in how many days can A alone do it?
(a) 22 days (b) 18 days (c) 15 days (d) 20 days
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is 15 days, halving 30 as if A were twice as fast. 50% more efficient means A's rate = 1.5 × B's rate, so A's time = 30/1.5 = 20 days, so the answer is 20 days.
Source: PYQ-style

36. A and B together can do a work in 8 days and B alone can do it in 12 days. They work together for 3 days, after which A leaves. In how many more days will B finish the remaining work alone?
(a) 8 days (b) 6 days (c) 7.5 days (d) 9 days
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is 9 days, dividing the full work by B's rate instead of the remaining work. In 3 days together they complete 3/8 of the work, leaving 5/8. B alone needs (5/8) ÷ (1/12) = 60/8 = 7.5 days, so the answer is 7.5 days.
Source: PYQ-style

37. A alone can do a work in 25 days and B alone in 20 days. They work together for 5 days and then A leaves. In how many days will B complete the remaining work?
(a) 9 days (b) 11 days (c) 10 days (d) 12 days
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is 10 days, from using the wrong remaining fraction. Combined rate = 1/25 + 1/20 = 4/100 + 5/100 = 9/100. In 5 days: 45/100 = 9/20 done; remaining = 11/20. B needs (11/20) ÷ (1/20) = 11 days, so the answer is 11 days.
Source: PYQ-style

38. 4 men and 6 women can complete a work in 8 days, while 3 men and 7 women can complete it in 10 days. In how many days will 10 women complete it?
(a) 35 days (b) 50 days (c) 45 days (d) 40 days
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is 35 days, from a wrong man-woman ratio. Let a man's daily work be m and a woman's be w. Then 8(4m + 6w) = 10(3m + 7w), giving 32m + 48w = 30m + 70w, so 2m = 22w and m = 11w. Total work = 10(33w + 7w) = 400w; ten women do 10w per day, needing 400/10 = 40 days, so the answer is 40 days.
Source: PYQ-style

39. A can do a work in 9 days. B is 50% more efficient than A. In how many days will B do the same work?
(a) 4.5 days (b) 7 days (c) 13.5 days (d) 6 days
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is 4.5 days, halving 9 as if B were twice as efficient. B's rate = 1.5 × A's rate, so B's time = 9/1.5 = 6 days, so the answer is 6 days.
Source: PYQ-style

40. A, B and C can do a work in 10, 12 and 15 days respectively. They start together, but A leaves after 2 days and B leaves 3 days before completion. In how many days is the work finished?
(a) 7 days (b) 9 days (c) 8 days (d) 6 days
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is 6 days, ignoring that C works alone at the end. Let total time be T days. A works 2 days, B works (T − 3) days, C works T days: 2/10 + (T−3)/12 + T/15 = 1. Multiplying by 60: 12 + 5(T−3) + 4T = 60, so 9T = 63 and T = 7 days, so the answer is 7 days.
Source: PYQ-style

41. A pipe can fill a tank in 6 hours and another pipe can empty it in 9 hours. If both are opened together, in how many hours will the tank be filled?
(a) 15 hours (b) 20 hours (c) 12 hours (d) 18 hours
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is 15 hours, adding the two times. Net rate = 1/6 − 1/9 = 3/18 − 2/18 = 1/18, so the tank fills in 18 hours, so the answer is 18 hours.
Source: NCERT Class 8 Mathematics

42. Two pipes A and B can fill a tank in 12 and 16 hours respectively. A third pipe C can empty the full tank in 6 hours. If all three are opened together, the tank will:
(a) fill in 96 hours (b) never fill (c) fill in 24 hours (d) fill in 48 hours
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is 48 hours, from combining the rates with wrong signs. Net rate = 1/12 + 1/16 − 1/6 = 4/48 + 3/48 − 8/48 = −1/48, which is negative, meaning water level only falls, so the tank never fills, so the answer is never fill.
Source: PYQ-style

43. A can do a work in 15 days and B in 20 days. They work on alternate days, starting with A. In how many days will the work be completed?
(a) 17¼ days (b) 17 days (c) 17½ days (d) 18 days
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is 17½ days, assuming the leftover needs half a day. Work in 2 days = 1/15 + 1/20 = 7/60, so in 16 days (8 cycles) 56/60 is done, leaving 4/60 = 1/15. On day 17, A works and does exactly 1/15 in one full day, finishing the work, so the answer is 17 days.
Source: PYQ-style

44. A works twice as fast as B. If B can complete a work in 24 days, in how many days can they complete it working together?
(a) 8 days (b) 10 days (c) 12 days (d) 6 days
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is 12 days, halving B's time. A takes 24/2 = 12 days. Combined rate = 1/12 + 1/24 = 3/24 = 1/8, so together they take 8 days, so the answer is 8 days.
Source: NCERT Class 8 Mathematics (inverse proportion)

45. 15 men can complete a work in 16 days. If 5 more men join after 6 days, in how many total days will the work be finished?
(a) 12 days (b) 13 days (c) 14 days (d) 13.5 days
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is 12 days, applying the increased workforce to the whole job. Total work = 15 × 16 = 240 man-days. In 6 days, 15 men do 90 man-days; remaining = 150 man-days. With 20 men: 150/20 = 7.5 more days, so total = 6 + 7.5 = 13.5 days, so the answer is 13.5 days.
Source: PYQ-style

46. A can do 1/3 of a work in 5 days and B can do 1/2 of the same work in 9 days. Working together, in how many days will they finish the work?
(a) 8 2/11 days (b) 7 1/2 days (c) 9 days (d) 8 days
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is 9 days, averaging the partial times directly. A's full time = 5 × 3 = 15 days; B's full time = 9 × 2 = 18 days. Combined rate = 1/15 + 1/18 = 6/90 + 5/90 = 11/90, so time = 90/11 = 8 2/11 days, so the answer is 8 2/11 days.
Source: PYQ-style

47. A and B can do a work in 18 and 24 days respectively. They work together for 6 days and then A leaves. B finishes the rest alone. The total time taken is:
(a) 16 days (b) 18 days (c) 14 days (d) 20 days
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is 14 days, from miscomputing the remaining fraction. Combined rate = 1/18 + 1/24 = 4/72 + 3/72 = 7/72. In 6 days: 42/72 = 7/12 done; remaining = 5/12. B needs (5/12) ÷ (1/24) = 10 days, so total = 6 + 10 = 16 days, so the answer is 16 days.
Source: PYQ-style

48. A is 60% more efficient than B. Together they can finish a work in 10 days. In how many days can B alone finish it?
(a) 24 days (b) 30 days (c) 26 days (d) 28 days
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is 16 days, wrongly adding 60% of 10. Let B's rate be x; A's rate = 1.6x. Together 2.6x = 1/10, so x = 1/26. B alone takes 26 days, so the answer is 26 days.

49. 8 men or 12 women can do a work in 25 days. In how many days can 6 men and 11 women do the same work?
(a) 20 days (b) 18 days (c) 15 days (d) 12 days
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is 20 days, from averaging the two group times. 8 men = 12 women, so 1 man = 1.5 women and 6 men = 9 women. Thus 6 men + 11 women = 20 women. Work = 12 × 25 = 300 woman-days; 300/20 = 15 days, so the answer is 15 days.
Source: PYQ-style

50. A can complete a work in 12 days working 8 hours a day. B can complete the same work in 8 days working 10 hours a day. If both work together 8 hours a day, in how many days will they finish?
(a) 4 4/5 days (b) 5 5/11 days (c) 6 days (d) 5 days
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is 6 days, ignoring the different daily hours. A's total hours = 96; B's = 80. Hourly rates: 1/96 and 1/80. In 8 hours a day together: 8 × (1/96 + 1/80) = 8 × (5/480 + 6/480) = 88/480 = 11/60 per day. Days needed = 60/11 = 5 5/11 days, so the answer is 5 5/11 days.
Source: PYQ-style
