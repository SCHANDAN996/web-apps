101. A can complete a piece of work in 12 days and B can complete the same work in 18 days. In how many days will they complete the work together?
(a) 7 days (b) 7.2 days (c) 8 days (d) 6.5 days
Answer: (b)
Solution: The tempting shortcut is averaging 12 and 18 to get 15, which is wrong because combined rates add, not times. Take total work = LCM(12, 18) = 36 units. A does 3 units/day and B does 2 units/day, so together they do 5 units/day. Time = 36/5 = 7.2, so the answer is 7.2 days.
Source: NCERT Class 8 Maths (direct and inverse proportion), PYQ-style

102. A can finish a work in 10 days and B in 15 days. They work together for 4 days, after which A leaves. In how many more days will B finish the remaining work?
(a) 4 days (b) 5 days (c) 6 days (d) 3 days
Answer: (b)
Solution: A common trap is computing the remaining work and then dividing by the combined rate, but A has left, so only B works. Total work = 30 units; A = 3 units/day, B = 2 units/day. In 4 days together they finish 4 × 5 = 20 units, leaving 10 units. B alone needs 10/2 = 5, so the answer is 5 days.
Source: PYQ-style

103. A is twice as efficient as B. Working together they finish a job in 14 days. In how many days can A alone finish the job?
(a) 28 days (b) 24 days (c) 18 days (d) 21 days
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is to double 14 and get 28, but that ignores B's contribution entirely. Let B's rate be 1 unit/day, so A's rate is 2 units/day; together 3 units/day. Total work = 14 × 3 = 42 units. A alone needs 42/2 = 21, so the answer is 21 days.
Source: PYQ-style

104. 10 men can complete a work in 12 days. In how many days can 15 men complete the same work?
(a) 8 days (b) 9 days (c) 18 days (d) 10 days
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is direct proportion (more men, more days), but men and days are inversely proportional. Total work = 10 × 12 = 120 man-days. With 15 men, days = 120/15 = 8, so the answer is 8 days.
Source: NCERT Class 8 Maths (inverse proportion)

105. A can do a work in 20 days and B in 30 days. They work on alternate days, starting with A. In how many days will the work be completed?
(a) 24 days (b) 25 days (c) 23 days (d) 26 days
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is taking 60/5 = 12 cycles and answering 24 without checking — here it actually works, but only because the work ends exactly on B's day. Total work = 60 units; A = 3 units/day, B = 2 units/day. Each 2-day pair completes 5 units, so 11 pairs (22 days) complete 55 units. On day 23 A adds 3 (total 58), on day 24 B adds 2 (total 60), so the answer is 24 days.
Source: PYQ-style

106. Pipe A can fill a tank in 6 hours while pipe B can empty it in 12 hours. If both are opened together, in how many hours will the tank be filled?
(a) 8 hours (b) 10 hours (c) 12 hours (d) Never filled
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is subtracting times (12 − 6 = 6 hours), which is meaningless; rates must be subtracted. Take capacity = 12 units; A fills 2 units/hour, B empties 1 unit/hour, net gain = 1 unit/hour. Time = 12/1 = 12, so the answer is 12 hours.
Source: PYQ-style

107. A and B together can complete a work in 8 days. A alone can complete it in 12 days. In how many days can B alone complete it?
(a) 20 days (b) 16 days (c) 24 days (d) 18 days
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is 12 − 8 = 4 days, but B's rate is found by subtracting rates, not times. B's daily work = 1/8 − 1/12 = 3/24 − 2/24 = 1/24. So B alone needs 24, so the answer is 24 days.
Source: PYQ-style

108. A can do a work in 15 days and B in 20 days. With the help of C, they finish the work in 6 days. In how many days can C alone do the work?
(a) 20 days (b) 30 days (c) 15 days (d) 24 days
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is assuming C's share equals the leftover time of 6 days, but C's rate must be isolated. Combined rate of all three = 1/6; A + B = 1/15 + 1/20 = 7/60. C's rate = 1/6 − 7/60 = 10/60 − 7/60 = 3/60 = 1/20. So C alone needs 20, so the answer is 20 days.
Source: PYQ-style

109. The efficiency ratio of A and B is 3 : 2. If A alone can finish a work in 10 days, in how many days can B alone finish it?
(a) 12 days (b) 20 days (c) 18 days (d) 15 days
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is inverting the ratio and answering 10 × 2/3, which is not a whole number and signals a flipped ratio. Higher efficiency means less time, so B takes longer: time ratio is inverse, A : B = 2 : 3. B's time = 10 × 3/2 = 15, so the answer is 15 days.
Source: PYQ-style

110. A man works twice as fast as a boy. 5 men and 5 boys together finish a work in 6 days. In how many days will 3 men and 9 boys finish the same work?
(a) 8 days (b) 5 days (c) 6 days (d) 7 days
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is treating men and boys as equal workers. Let a boy's rate be 1 unit/day, so a man's rate is 2 units/day. First team: 5(2) + 5(1) = 15 units/day, so total work = 15 × 6 = 90 units. Second team: 3(2) + 9(1) = 15 units/day, giving 90/15 = 6, so the answer is 6 days.
Source: PYQ-style

111. Pipe A can fill a tank in 10 hours and pipe B in 15 hours. If both pipes are opened together, in how much time will the tank be full?
(a) 5 hours (b) 8 hours (c) 6 hours (d) 12.5 hours
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is averaging 10 and 15 to get 12.5, but two filling pipes together are always faster than either alone. Capacity = 30 units; A = 3 units/hour, B = 2 units/hour, together 5 units/hour. Time = 30/5 = 6, so the answer is 6 hours.
Source: NCERT Class 8 Maths, PYQ-style

112. A can complete a work in 24 days. He works alone for 6 days, then B joins him and together they finish the remaining work in 6 more days. In how many days can B alone complete the whole work?
(a) 12 days (b) 16 days (c) 18 days (d) 24 days
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is assuming B worked only 6 days so B needs 6 days alone, ignoring that A also worked during those 6 days. A worked 12 days total, completing 12/24 = 1/2 of the work. B did the other half in 6 days, so B alone needs 6 × 2 = 12, so the answer is 12 days.
Source: PYQ-style

113. A and B together can do a work in 12 days, B and C in 15 days, and C and A in 20 days. In how many days will A, B and C together complete the work?
(a) 8 days (b) 12 days (c) 10 days (d) 15 days
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is adding the three pair-times and dividing by 3, which has no mathematical basis. Adding the pair rates: 2(A + B + C) = 1/12 + 1/15 + 1/20 = 5/60 + 4/60 + 3/60 = 12/60 = 1/5. So the combined rate of all three is 1/10, and the answer is 10 days.
Source: PYQ-style

114. A can do a work in 16 days and B in 24 days. They work together for 4 days, after which A leaves. In how many more days will B complete the remaining work?
(a) 12 days (b) 14 days (c) 16 days (d) 10 days
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is dividing the remaining work by the combined rate even though A has left. Total work = 48 units; A = 3 units/day, B = 2 units/day. In 4 days together they finish 20 units, leaving 28 units. B alone needs 28/2 = 14, so the answer is 14 days.

115. 12 men working 6 hours a day can build a wall in 10 days. In how many days will 8 men working 9 hours a day build the same wall?
(a) 12 days (b) 15 days (c) 8 days (d) 10 days
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is adjusting only for the number of men and forgetting the hours per day. Total work = 12 × 6 × 10 = 720 man-hours. New daily output = 8 × 9 = 72 man-hours, so days = 720/72 = 10, so the answer is 10 days.

116. A is 60% more efficient than B. If B alone can finish a work in 24 days, in how many days can A alone finish it?
(a) 18 days (b) 15 days (c) 16 days (d) 20 days
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is reducing 24 by 60% to get 9.6, but efficiency and time are inversely related, not directly. Efficiency ratio A : B = 160 : 100 = 8 : 5, so time ratio A : B = 5 : 8. A's time = 24 × 5/8 = 15, so the answer is 15 days.

117. Tap A fills a tank in 3 hours, tap B in 4 hours, and tap C can empty the full tank in 6 hours. If all three are opened together, in how many hours will the tank be filled?
(a) 2 hours (b) 2.4 hours (c) 3 hours (d) 2.5 hours
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is adding all three rates as if C also fills, which would give a faster but wrong time. Net rate = 1/3 + 1/4 − 1/6 = 4/12 + 3/12 − 2/12 = 5/12 of the tank per hour. Time = 12/5 = 2.4, so the answer is 2.4 hours.
Source: PYQ-style

118. A can do a work in 9 days and B in 12 days. They work on alternate days, starting with B. In how many days will the work be completed?
(a) 10⅓ days (b) 10 days (c) 10½ days (d) 11 days
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is stopping at 5 full cycles (10 days) and ignoring the leftover unit of work. Total work = 36 units; A = 4 units/day, B = 3 units/day. Each 2-day pair completes 7 units, so 5 pairs (10 days) complete 35 units. On day 11 it is B's turn; B needs 1 more unit at 3 units/day, taking 1/3 day, so the answer is 10⅓ days.

119. 20 men undertook to finish a work in 25 days. After 15 days, only half the work was done. How many extra men must be employed to finish the work on time?
(a) 15 (b) 20 (c) 5 (d) 10
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is assuming the original pace was correct and answering 0, but the crew is behind schedule. 20 men × 15 days = 300 man-days completed half the work, so the whole work needs 600 man-days. Remaining 300 man-days must be done in 10 days, requiring 30 men. Extra men = 30 − 20 = 10, so the answer is 10.
Source: PYQ-style

120. A can do a work in 20 days and B in 30 days. They start together, but A leaves 5 days before the work is completed. In how many days is the work finished?
(a) 12 days (b) 18 days (c) 15 days (d) 16 days
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is computing the combined time (12 days) and adding 5, which double-counts the days A actually worked. Let total time be T days: B works all T days, A works (T − 5) days. Then T/30 + (T − 5)/20 = 1, giving 2T + 3T − 15 = 60, so 5T = 75 and T = 15, so the answer is 15 days.
Source: PYQ-style

121. A, B and C can individually complete a work in 10, 12 and 15 days respectively. A works alone for 2 days and then leaves; B and C together complete the remaining work. What is the total time taken to finish the work?
(a) 7 days (b) 6⅔ days (c) 8 days (d) 7⅓ days
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is making all three work together from the start, which ignores that A leaves after 2 days. In 2 days A completes 2/10 = 1/5 of the work, leaving 4/5. B and C together work at 1/12 + 1/15 = 9/60 = 3/20 per day, needing (4/5)/(3/20) = 16/3 days. Total = 2 + 16/3 = 22/3 = 7⅓, so the answer is 7⅓ days.
Source: PYQ-style

122. Two pipes can fill a tank in 20 minutes and 30 minutes respectively, while a waste pipe can empty the full tank in 15 minutes. If all three are opened together, in how much time will the tank be filled?
(a) 45 minutes (b) 40 minutes (c) 50 minutes (d) 60 minutes
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is thinking the tank never fills because the waste pipe is the fastest, but the two filling pipes combined outpace it. Take capacity = 60 units: filling rates are 3 and 2 units/minute, waste is 4 units/minute. Net rate = 3 + 2 − 4 = 1 unit/minute. Time = 60/1 = 60, so the answer is 60 minutes.
Source: PYQ-style

123. A is thrice as efficient as B and therefore finishes a work 60 days earlier than B. In how many days can they finish the work together?
(a) 22.5 days (b) 25 days (c) 20 days (d) 30 days
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is taking A's time as 60 days, but 60 days is the difference, not A's time. Efficiency ratio 3 : 1 means time ratio 1 : 3; let A take x days and B take 3x days. Then 3x − x = 60, so x = 30: A takes 30 days, B takes 90 days. Combined rate = 1/30 + 1/90 = 4/90, giving 90/4 = 22.5, so the answer is 22.5 days.
Source: PYQ-style

124. 2 men and 3 women can complete a work in 10 days, while 3 men and 2 women can complete it in 8 days. In how many days can 1 man and 1 woman together complete the same work?
(a) 20 days (b) 200/9 days (c) 25 days (d) 18 days
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is averaging the two given times, but the team compositions differ, so rates must be solved algebraically. Equating total work: 10(2m + 3w) = 8(3m + 2w), giving 20m + 30w = 24m + 16w, so m = 3.5w. Total work = 10(7w + 3w) = 100w units. One man and one woman work at 4.5w per day, needing 100/4.5 = 200/9, so the answer is 200/9 days.
Source: PYQ-style

125. A alone would take 8 hours more than the time taken by A and B working together, while B alone would take 4.5 hours more than the time taken by both together. In how many hours will A and B together complete the work?
(a) 6 hours (b) 5 hours (c) 7 hours (d) 8 hours
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is averaging 8 and 4.5, but the standard result is that the combined time is the geometric mean of the two excess times. Combined time = √(8 × 4.5) = √36 = 6. Verification: A takes 14 hours, B takes 10.5 hours; combined rate = 1/14 + 1/10.5 = 3/42 + 4/42 = 7/42 = 1/6, so the answer is 6 hours.
Source: PYQ-style
