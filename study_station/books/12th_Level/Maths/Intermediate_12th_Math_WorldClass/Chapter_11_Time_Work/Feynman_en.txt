# Time & Work — Explained Like You're 12

## The Hardest Concept: "Work Done Together"

Most students can handle "A does a job in 10 days." But the moment the question says *"A and B work **together**"* — brains freeze. Why? Because you can't add days! If A takes 10 days and B takes 15 days, together they do NOT take 25 days. That would be silly — two people should finish *faster*, not slower!

So what's the secret? **Never count days. Count the daily appetite for work.**

## 🍕 The Pizza Story

Imagine you and your friend Ravi order a giant pizza with 30 slices.

- You are a fast eater: you eat **3 slices per minute**.
- Ravi is slower: he eats **2 slices per minute**.

Question: If you both attack the pizza together, how long until it's gone?

Easy, right? Together you eat **3 + 2 = 5 slices per minute**. So 30 slices ÷ 5 = **6 minutes**. Done!

Now here is the magic: **every Time & Work problem is secretly a pizza problem.**

- The "job" = the pizza (the total work).
- "A does the job in 10 days" just means A eats 1/10 of the pizza each day.
- "B does it in 15 days" means B eats 1/15 of the pizza each day.
- Together: 1/10 + 1/15 = 3/30 + 2/30 = **5/30 = 1/6 of the job per day** → finished in **6 days**.

Notice something? The pizza had 30 slices — and 30 is the LCM of 10 and 15. That's no accident!

## 🪄 The Trick You'll Never Forget: "The LCM Pizza"

**Step 1:** Total work = LCM of the days. (Make the pizza that big.)
**Step 2:** Each person's 1-day work = LCM ÷ their days. (Slices eaten per day.)
**Step 3:** Add or subtract appetites depending on who helps and who leaves.
**Step 4:** Days = Total slices ÷ combined appetite.

Say it like a chant: **"LCM is the pizza, days are the eaters, divide and conquer!"**

Why does this beat fractions? Because 1/10 + 1/15 makes your brain juggle denominators, but "3 slices + 2 slices" is something a 10-year-old does while licking chutney off their fingers. Same math, zero fear.

## One More Twist — The "Lazy Worker" (Negative Work)

What if a third friend, Monty, doesn't eat pizza but *hides* 1 slice per minute? Then the net speed is 3 + 2 − 1 = 4 slices/minute. That's exactly how "a leak empties the tank" or "C destroys the wall" problems work — pipes and cisterns are just pizza with a food thief!
