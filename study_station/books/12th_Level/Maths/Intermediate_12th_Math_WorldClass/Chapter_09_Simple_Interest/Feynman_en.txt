# Simple Interest — Explained Like You're 12

## The Story of Ravi and the Mango Tree 🥭

Imagine you have a mango tree. Every year, it gives you exactly **10 mangoes**. Not 11, not 9 — exactly 10, every single year, forever.

Now here's the question: after 5 years, how many mangoes did you get?

Easy, right? 10 × 5 = **50 mangoes**.

**That is Simple Interest.** That's literally the whole idea.

## Wait, Where's the Money?

Now replace the mango tree with money:

- The **tree** = your **Principal (P)** — the money you deposit or lend. Say ₹1,000.
- The **mangoes each year** = the **Interest** — the "thank you" money the bank pays you. Say 10% per year = ₹100 every year.
- The **years** = **Time (T)**.
- The **10%** = **Rate (R)**.

So if you put ₹1,000 in a bank at 10% per year for 5 years:

- Every year the bank gives you ₹100.
- After 5 years: 5 × ₹100 = **₹500 interest**.
- You get back your ₹1,000 + ₹500 = **₹1,500 total** (this total is called the **Amount**).

## The One Formula That Rules Them All

$$SI = \frac{P \times R \times T}{100}$$

Why divide by 100? Because "rate" is a *percentage* — and percent literally means "per hundred." So 10% = 10/100. That's it. No magic.

## The Hardest Part (and Why It's Actually Easy)

Students get confused here: **why does the interest stay the same every year?**

Because in Simple Interest, the bank only pays you on your **original** money — the Principal. The interest you earned last year just sits in your pocket; it does NOT join the tree. The tree never grows. 10 mangoes. Every year. Forever.

(There's another system called *Compound Interest* where the mangoes DO grow new trees — but that's a different chapter!)

## 🎯 The Trick You'll Never Forget: **"PeRT over 100"**

Think of a **perky little dog named PeRT** jumping **over a fence that's 100 cm tall**:

- **P** = Principal
- **R** = Rate
- **T** = Time
- All three multiply together and jump **over 100**

**SI = P × R × T / 100** — PeRT over 100! 🐕

And remember the mango tree rule: **"Same tree, same mangoes, every year."** If the interest is the same every year → it's Simple Interest.

## Quick Test of the Trick

₹2,000 at 5% for 3 years?
PeRT over 100 = (2000 × 5 × 3) / 100 = 30,000 / 100 = **₹300**. Done in 10 seconds.

## 🤔 Now You Try

Your friend lends ₹5,000 to a shopkeeper at 8% per year (simple interest) for 4 years. Using PeRT over 100 — how much interest does your friend earn, and what total amount does the shopkeeper pay back?

*(Solve it before peeking at the Practice Sets — if you got ₹1,600 interest and ₹6,600 total, the mango tree has officially taught you Simple Interest!)*
