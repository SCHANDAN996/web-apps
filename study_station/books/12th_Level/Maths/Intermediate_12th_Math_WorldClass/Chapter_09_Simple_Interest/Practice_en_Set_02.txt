26. A sum of ₹8,400 is lent at simple interest at 9% per annum for 4 years. Find the simple interest.
(a) ₹2,916 (b) ₹3,240 (c) ₹2,808 (d) ₹3,024
Answer: (d)
Solution: SI = P × R × T / 100 = 8400 × 9 × 4 / 100 = 3024. The tempting wrong value 2,916 comes from using 8,100 as principal instead of 8,400, so the answer is ₹3,024.
Source: NCERT Class 8 Mathematics

27. At what rate percent per annum will ₹6,500 earn a simple interest of ₹1,560 in 3 years?
(a) 6% (b) 7% (c) 8% (d) 9%
Answer: (c)
Solution: R = SI × 100 / (P × T) = 1560 × 100 / (6500 × 3) = 8. The trap value 6% comes from dividing by 4 years instead of 3, so the answer is 8%.
Source: NCERT Class 8 Mathematics

28. In how many years will ₹4,800 amount to ₹6,720 at 10% per annum simple interest?
(a) 5 years (b) 4 years (c) 3 years (d) 6 years
Answer: (b)
Solution: SI = 6720 − 4800 = 1920. T = SI × 100 / (P × R) = 1920 × 100 / (4800 × 10) = 4. The trap value 5 years comes from wrongly using the amount 6,720 as principal, so the answer is 4 years.
Source: NCERT Class 8 Mathematics

29. A sum becomes ₹10,000 in 4 years and ₹11,500 in 7 years at simple interest. Find the rate of interest.
(a) 5% (b) 6.25% (c) 7.5% (d) 5.5%
Answer: (b)
Solution: Interest for 3 years = 11500 − 10000 = 1500, so interest for 4 years = 2000 and principal = 10000 − 2000 = 8000. R = 2000 × 100 / (8000 × 4) = 6.25. The trap value 5% comes from using 10,000 as principal, so the answer is 6.25%.
Source: PYQ-style

30. A sum of money doubles itself in 8 years at simple interest. In how many years will it become four times itself?
(a) 24 years (b) 20 years (c) 16 years (d) 32 years
Answer: (a)
Solution: Doubling in 8 years means interest equal to the principal is earned in 8 years. To become four times, interest must be 3 times the principal, needing 3 × 8 = 24 years. The trap value 16 years comes from simply doubling the time, forgetting the interest stays simple, so the answer is 24 years.
Source: PYQ-style

31. ₹12,000 is invested partly at 8% and partly at 12% per annum simple interest. The total interest after 2 years is ₹2,400. Find the sum invested at 8%.
(a) ₹4,000 (b) ₹5,000 (c) ₹6,000 (d) ₹8,000
Answer: (c)
Solution: Average rate = 2400 × 100 / (12000 × 2) = 10%. By alligation, the parts at 8% and 12% are equal, so the sum at 8% = 6,000. The trap value 4,000 comes from a wrong alligation ratio, so the answer is ₹6,000.
Source: PYQ-style

32. The simple interest on a sum for 6 years at 5% per annum is ₹450. Find the sum.
(a) ₹2,000 (b) ₹1,800 (c) ₹1,500 (d) ₹2,250
Answer: (c)
Solution: P = SI × 100 / (R × T) = 450 × 100 / (5 × 6) = 45000 / 30 = 1500. The trap value 1,800 comes from dividing by 25 instead of 30, so the answer is ₹1,500.

33. A man borrowed ₹15,000 at 12% per annum simple interest. How much should he pay at the end of 4 years to clear the debt completely?

Answer: (d)

35. A sum of ₹9,600 amounts to ₹12,000 in 5 years at simple interest. If the rate is increased by 2%, what will be the amount after 5 years?
(a) ₹12,960 (b) ₹13,200 (c) ₹13,440 (d) ₹12,480
Answer: (a)
Solution: Original SI = 2400, so R = 2400 × 100 / (9600 × 5) = 5%. New rate = 7%, new SI = 9600 × 7 × 5 / 100 = 3360, amount = 9600 + 3360 = 12960. The trap value 12,480 adds only the extra 2% interest to the old amount incorrectly, so the answer is ₹12,960.
Source: PYQ-style

36. The simple interest on a certain sum at 8% per annum for 3 years is half the simple interest on ₹6,000 at 10% for 2 years. Find the first sum.
(a) ₹3,500 (b) ₹2,000 (c) ₹3,000 (d) ₹2,500
Answer: (d)
Solution: SI on the second sum = 6000 × 10 × 2 / 100 = 1200; half of it = 600. First sum = 600 × 100 / (8 × 3) = 2500. The trap value 3,000 comes from forgetting the "half" condition, so the answer is ₹2,500.
Source: PYQ-style

37. A sum becomes 5/4 of itself in 4 years at simple interest. Find the rate percent per annum.
(a) 5% (b) 6% (c) 6.25% (d) 8%
Answer: (c)
Solution: Interest = P/4 in 4 years. R = (P/4) × 100 / (P × 4) = 100/16 = 6.25. The trap value 5% comes from dividing 25 by 5 instead of 4, so the answer is 6.25%.

38. A sum of ₹7,500 is lent for 3 years at simple interest. If the rate were 2% higher, the interest would be ₹450 more. Find the interest at the original rate of 6% for 3 years.
(a) ₹1,050 (b) ₹1,200 (c) ₹1,500 (d) ₹1,350
Answer: (d)
Solution: Extra interest = 7500 × 2 × 3 / 100 = 450, which confirms the data. Interest at the original rate = 7500 × 6 × 3 / 100 = 1350. The trap value 1,500 comes from using the higher rate of 8% instead of 6%, so the answer is ₹1,350.
Source: PYQ-style

39. A person invests ₹5,000 at 6% and ₹7,000 at 8% per annum simple interest. What is the overall rate of interest per annum on the total investment?
(a) 7.17% (b) 7.25% (c) 7% (d) 7.5%
Answer: (a)
Solution: Total interest in 1 year = 5000 × 6/100 + 7000 × 8/100 = 300 + 560 = 860. Overall rate = 860 × 100 / 12000 ≈ 7.17%. The trap value 7% is the plain average of 6 and 8, ignoring the unequal sums, so the answer is 7.17%.
Source: PYQ-style

40. The simple interest on a sum for 5 years is two-fifths of the sum. Find the rate percent per annum.
(a) 8% (b) 10% (c) 7% (d) 6%
Answer: (a)
Solution: SI = 2P/5 = P × R × 5 / 100, so R = (2/5) × 100 / 5 = 8. The trap value 10% comes from treating 2/5 as 40% earned over 4 years, so the answer is 8%.
Source: NCERT Class 8 Mathematics

41. A sum of ₹10,000 is lent in two parts such that the interest on the first part at 5% for 3 years equals the interest on the second part at 10% for 2 years. Find the first part.
(a) ₹4,000 (b) ₹5,000 (c) ₹6,000 (d) ₹5,714
Answer: (d)
Solution: Let the parts be x and 10000 − x. Then x × 5 × 3 = (10000 − x) × 10 × 2, so 15x = 200000 − 20x, giving 35x = 200000 and x ≈ 5714. The trap value 5,000 assumes equal parts, so the answer is ₹5,714.
Source: PYQ-style

42. In what time will the simple interest on ₹8,000 at 6% per annum be equal to the simple interest on ₹6,000 at 8% per annum for 4 years?
(a) 3 years (b) 4 years (c) 5 years (d) 6 years
Answer: (b)
Solution: SI on the second sum = 6000 × 8 × 4 / 100 = 1920. Time = 1920 × 100 / (8000 × 6) = 4. The trap value 6 years comes from swapping the two principals, so the answer is 4 years.
Source: PYQ-style

43. A sum of money amounts to ₹9,200 in 3 years and ₹10,400 in 5 years at simple interest. Find the sum.
(a) ₹8,000 (b) ₹7,400 (c) ₹7,600 (d) ₹7,000
Answer: (b)
Solution: Interest for 2 years = 10400 − 9200 = 1200, so yearly interest = 600 and interest for 3 years = 1800. Principal = 9200 − 1800 = 7400. The trap value 8,000 comes from subtracting only 2 years' interest from 9,200, so the answer is ₹7,400.
Source: PYQ-style

44. A man lends ₹4,000 at 10% per annum simple interest for 2 years and ₹6,000 at 5% per annum for 4 years. Find the total interest earned.

Answer: (d)
Solution: First SI = 4000 × 10 × 2 / 100 = 800. Second SI = 6000 × 5 × 4 / 100 = 1200. Total = 2000. The trap value 2,400 comes from using 10% for both sums, so the answer is ₹2,000.

46. A sum triples itself in 12 years at simple interest. Find the rate percent per annum.
(a) 16.67% (b) 12.5% (c) 20% (d) 15%
Answer: (a)
Solution: Interest = 2P in 12 years. R = 2P × 100 / (P × 12) = 200/12 ≈ 16.67%. The trap value 20% comes from treating "triples" as interest of 3P, so the answer is 16.67%.

47. The difference between the simple interests received from two banks on ₹5,000 for 2 years is ₹100. Find the difference between their rates of interest.
(a) 0.5% (b) 1% (c) 1.5% (d) 2%
Answer: (b)
Solution: Difference in SI = P × T × (R1 − R2) / 100, so 100 = 5000 × 2 × d / 100, giving d = 1. The trap value 2% comes from forgetting the time factor of 2 years, so the answer is 1%.
Source: PYQ-style

48. A sum was invested at simple interest at a certain rate for 2 years. Had it been invested at 3% higher rate, it would have fetched ₹720 more. Find the sum.
(a) ₹12,000 (b) ₹15,000 (c) ₹14,000 (d) ₹10,000
Answer: (a)
Solution: Extra interest = P × 3 × 2 / 100 = 720, so P = 720 × 100 / 6 = 12000. The trap value 10,000 comes from dividing by 7.2 instead of 6, so the answer is ₹12,000.

49. A person borrows ₹8,000 at 5% per annum simple interest and lends it at 8% per annum simple interest. What is his gain after 3 years?
(a) ₹600 (b) ₹720 (c) ₹840 (d) ₹960
Answer: (b)
Solution: Gain per year = 8000 × (8 − 5)/100 = 240. Gain in 3 years = 720. The trap value 960 comes from using the 8% rate alone instead of the difference of rates, so the answer is ₹720.
Source: PYQ-style

Answer: (a)
Solution: T = SI × 100 / (P × R) = 1500 × 100 / (12500 × 4) = 3. The trap value 2.5 years comes from using 12,000 as principal, so the answer is 3 years.
