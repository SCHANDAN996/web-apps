76. A sum of ₹8,000 is lent at simple interest for 3 years. If the interest earned is ₹1,440, what is the annual rate of interest?
(a) 5% (b) 6% (c) 7% (d) 8%
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is dividing 1440 by 8000 directly, which gives 18% — that is the total interest over 3 years, not the yearly rate. Use SI = PRT/100: 1440 = (8000 × R × 3)/100, so R = 144000/24000 = 6, so the answer is 6%.
Source: NCERT Class 8 Mathematics

77. At what rate percent per annum will a sum of ₹6,250 amount to ₹7,750 in 4 years at simple interest?
(a) 4% (b) 5% (c) 6% (d) 8%
Answer: (c)
Solution: Interest = 7750 − 6250 = ₹1,500. The tempting error is computing 1500/6250 = 24% and picking a wrong option; that 24% is over 4 years. R = (1500 × 100)/(6250 × 4) = 150000/25000 = 6, so the answer is 6%.
Source: PYQ-style

78. A sum becomes ₹11,500 in 5 years and ₹13,000 in 8 years at simple interest. Find the sum.
(a) ₹10,000 (b) ₹8,000 (c) ₹9,500 (d) ₹9,000
Answer: (d)
Solution: Interest for 3 extra years = 13000 − 11500 = ₹1,500, so yearly interest = ₹500. Interest for 5 years = ₹2,500. The trap is treating ₹11,500 itself as the principal. Principal = 11500 − 2500 = 9,000, so the answer is ₹9,000.
Source: PYQ-style

79. In how many years will a sum double itself at 12.5% per annum simple interest?
(a) 6 years (b) 7 years (c) 8 years (d) 10 years
Answer: (c)
Solution: To double, interest must equal the principal: P = PRT/100, so T = 100/R = 100/12.5 = 8. The trap is computing 100/12.5 incorrectly as 12.5 years or halving it to 4. So the answer is 8 years.
Source: NCERT Class 8 Mathematics

80. A sum of money triples itself in 16 years at simple interest. In how many years will it become 5 times itself?
(a) 28 years (b) 32 years (c) 24 years (d) 40 years
Answer: (b)
Solution: Tripling means interest = 2P in 16 years, so interest of P takes 8 years. To become 5 times, interest must be 4P, needing 4 × 8 = 32 years. The trap is scaling 16 × 5/3 ≈ 26.7, which wrongly assumes the amount grows linearly instead of the interest. So the answer is 32 years.
Source: PYQ-style

81. The simple interest on a sum for 6 years at 8% per annum is ₹2,880. Find the sum.
(a) ₹5,000 (b) ₹5,500 (c) ₹6,500 (d) ₹6,000
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is computing 2880/48 = 60 and misreading it as the principal in hundreds. P = (SI × 100)/(R × T) = (2880 × 100)/(8 × 6) = 288000/48 = 6,000, so the answer is ₹6,000.
Source: NCERT Class 8 Mathematics

82. A man lends ₹10,000 in two parts such that the first part at 6% for 4 years gives the same interest as the second part at 8% for 3 years. Find the first part.
(a) ₹5,000 (b) ₹4,000 (c) ₹6,000 (d) ₹7,000
Answer: (a)
Solution: Let the first part be x. Then x × 6 × 4 = (10000 − x) × 8 × 3, so 24x = 240000 − 24x, giving 48x = 240000 and x = 5,000. The trap is splitting the money in the ratio 6:8 instead of using the time-weighted rates 24:24. So the answer is ₹5,000.
Source: PYQ-style

83. The simple interest on a certain sum at 6% per annum for 4 years is ₹960 more than the interest on the same sum at 4% per annum for 4 years. Find the sum.
(a) ₹10,000 (b) ₹11,000 (c) ₹15,000 (d) ₹12,000
Answer: (d)
Solution: First interest = P × 6 × 4/100 = 0.24P; second = P × 4 × 4/100 = 0.16P. Difference: 0.08P = 960, so P = 12,000. The trap is using the rate difference 2% for only 1 year, which gives ₹48,000. So the answer is ₹12,000.
Source: PYQ-style

84. A sum of ₹12,000 is invested at simple interest at 5% per annum for the first 2 years, and then the rate is increased to 8% per annum. Find the interest earned in the last 3 years.
(a) ₹2,400 (b) ₹2,880 (c) ₹2,640 (d) ₹3,000
Answer: (b)
Solution: Interest for the last 3 years = 12000 × 8 × 3/100 = ₹2,880. The trap is averaging the rate to 6.5% and applying it to all 5 years, which mixes the two periods. So the answer is ₹2,880.

85. What sum will amount to ₹9,300 in 3 years at 8% per annum simple interest?
(a) ₹7,500 (b) ₹7,000 (c) ₹7,200 (d) ₹7,800
Answer: (a)
Solution: Amount = P(1 + RT/100) = P × 1.24, so P = 9300/1.24 = 7,500. The trap is subtracting 24% of 9300, which gives ₹7,068 — the interest is 24% of the principal, not of the amount. So the answer is ₹7,500.
Source: NCERT Class 8 Mathematics

86. The difference between the simple interest received from two banks on ₹5,000 for 2 years is ₹250. Find the difference between their rates of interest.
(a) 1.5% (b) 2.5% (c) 3% (d) 2%
Answer: (b)
Solution: Difference in interest = P × T × (R1 − R2)/100, so 250 = 5000 × 2 × d/100, giving d = 25000/10000 = 2.5. The trap is dividing 250 by 5000 to get 5% and forgetting the 2-year factor. So the answer is 2.5%.
Source: PYQ-style

87. A sum was put at simple interest at a certain rate for 3 years. Had it been put at 2% higher rate, it would have fetched ₹900 more. Find the sum.
(a) ₹15,000 (b) ₹12,000 (c) ₹18,000 (d) ₹20,000
Answer: (a)
Solution: Extra interest = P × 2 × 3/100 = 900, so P = 900 × 100/6 = 15,000. The trap is using T = 1 year, which gives ₹45,000, or dividing 900 by 2 and stopping. So the answer is ₹15,000.
Source: PYQ-style

88. A person borrows ₹5,000 at 12% per annum simple interest. At the end of 4 years he repays ₹2,400 and agrees to pay the balance immediately after. How much is the balance (interest calculated on the original principal throughout)?
(a) ₹5,000 (b) ₹4,800 (c) ₹5,200 (d) ₹4,600
Answer: (a)
Solution: Total interest for 4 years = 5000 × 12 × 4/100 = ₹2,400, so total amount = ₹7,400. Balance = 7400 − 2400 = ₹5,000. The trap is forgetting the interest and subtracting 2400 from 5000 to get ₹2,600. So the answer is ₹5,000.
Source: PYQ-style

89. The simple interest on a sum is 4/9 of the principal and the number of years is equal to the rate percent per annum. Find the rate.
(a) 5% (b) 7½% (c) 6% (d) 6⅔%
Answer: (d)
Solution: SI/P = RT/100 = R²/100 = 4/9, so R² = 400/9 and R = 20/3 = 6⅔. The trap is taking R = 4/9 × 100 ≈ 44% or setting R = T = 4/9. So the answer is 6⅔%.
Source: PYQ-style

90. A sum of ₹15,500 is lent partly at 8% and partly at 10% simple interest. The total interest after 3 years is ₹4,200. Find the part lent at 8%.
(a) ₹9,000 (b) ₹8,000 (c) ₹8,500 (d) ₹7,500
Answer: (d)
Solution: Let the 8% part be x. Then (x × 8 × 3 + (15500 − x) × 10 × 3)/100 = 4200, so 24x + 465000 − 30x = 420000, giving 6x = 45000 and x = 7,500. The trap is reporting the 10% part ₹8,000 instead of the 8% part asked. So the answer is ₹7,500.
Source: PYQ-style

91. At what rate percent per annum will the simple interest on ₹2,500 be ₹500 in 2 years 6 months?

Answer: (c)
Solution: T = 2.5 years. R = (500 × 100)/(2500 × 2.5) = 50000/6250 = 8. The trap is using T = 2 years, which gives 10%, and forgetting the extra 6 months. So the answer is 8%.

92. A sum amounts to ₹11,800 in 3 years and ₹13,000 in 5 years at simple interest. Find the sum and the rate.

Answer: (b)
Solution: Interest for 2 extra years = 13000 − 11800 = ₹1,200, so yearly interest = ₹600. Principal = 11800 − 3 × 600 = ₹10,000, and R = 600 × 100/10000 = 6%. The trap is treating ₹11,800 as the principal, which gives a wrong rate. So the answer is ₹10,000, 6%.

93. A man invests ₹4,000 at 5% and ₹3,500 at 4% simple interest. In how many years will the total interest from both investments be ₹1,020?
(a) 3 years (b) 2.5 years (c) 4 years (d) 2 years
Answer: (a)
Solution: Yearly interest = 4000 × 5/100 + 3500 × 4/100 = 200 + 140 = ₹340. Time = 1020/340 = 3 years. The trap is averaging the principals or rates first and then solving, which distorts the yearly interest. So the answer is 3 years.

94. The simple interest on a sum for 5 years is two-fifths of the sum. The rate percent per annum is:
(a) 6% (b) 7% (c) 10% (d) 8%
Answer: (d)
Solution: SI/P = 2/5 = RT/100 = 5R/100, so R = (2/5) × 100/5 = 8. The trap is computing (2/5) × 100 = 40% and then forgetting to divide by the 5 years. So the answer is 8%.

100. The rate of simple interest for the first 2 years is 6%, for the next 3 years is 8%, and beyond 5 years is 10%. If a sum earns ₹3,360 as interest in 7 years, find the sum.
(a) ₹6,500 (b) ₹5,000 (c) ₹6,000 (d) ₹7,500
Answer: (c)
Solution: Effective interest over 7 years = P/100 × (6 × 2 + 8 × 3 + 10 × 2) = P/100 × (12 + 24 + 20) = 56P/100. So 56P/100 = 3360, giving P = 336000/56 = 6,000. The trap is averaging the three rates to 8% for 7 years, which gives 56% only by coincidence here but fails in general — always weight each rate by its own years. So the answer is ₹6,000.
Source: PYQ-style
