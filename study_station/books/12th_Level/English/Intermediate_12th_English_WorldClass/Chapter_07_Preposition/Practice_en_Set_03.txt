Questions 51–60: Easy | Questions 61–75: Medium

51. The train arrived ______ time.
(a) in (b) by (c) at (d) on
Answer: (d)
Solution: "On time" is the fixed expression meaning punctually. "In time" means early enough for something, which changes the meaning.
Source: NCERT Class 9 English Grammar

52. She has been living in Delhi ______ 2015.
(a) for (b) from (c) since (d) at
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Since" is used with a point of time (2015). "For" is used with a period of time (for ten years).
Source: NCERT Class 10 English Grammar

53. The cat is hiding ______ the table.
(a) under (b) over (c) on (d) above
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Under" shows position below something and touching or covered by it. "Below" would also fit, but among the options only "under" gives the correct sense.
Source: NCERT Class 8 English Grammar

54. He is fond ______ playing cricket.
(a) in (b) at (c) of (d) with
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Fond of" is the correct fixed preposition phrase. Learners often wrongly write "fond in" by translating from Hindi.
Source: PYQ-style

55. We go to school ______ bus every day.
(a) on (b) by (c) in (d) with
Answer: (b)
Solution: "By" is used with means of transport when there is no article: by bus, by train, by car. With an article we say "on the bus".
Source: NCERT Class 8 English Grammar

56. The meeting will be held ______ Monday.
(a) on (b) at (c) in (d) by
Answer: (a)
Solution: "On" is used with days and dates. "In" is for months and years; "at" is for exact times.
Source: NCERT Class 9 English Grammar

57. Please listen ______ what the teacher says.
(a) at (b) to (c) for (d) with
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Listen" always takes "to" before its object. Hindi speakers often drop the preposition because Hindi uses "सुनो" directly.
Source: PYQ-style

58. The book belongs ______ me.
(a) with (b) to (c) for (d) of
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Belong to" is the fixed phrase showing possession. Note that "belong" is never used in the continuous form.
Source: NCERT Class 10 English Grammar

59. He jumped ______ the river to save the child.
(a) in (b) into (c) on (d) at
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Into" shows movement from outside to inside. "In" shows position, not movement, so it is wrong after "jumped".
Source: NCERT Class 9 English Grammar

60. My birthday falls ______ August.
(a) on (b) at (c) by (d) in
Answer: (d)
Solution: "In" is used with months, years and seasons. "On" is used only with specific days or dates.
Source: NCERT Class 8 English Grammar

61. He has been suffering ______ fever since Monday.
(a) with (b) of (c) by (d) from
Answer: (d)
Solution: "Suffer from" is used for diseases and problems. "Suffer with" is a common error made by translating "से पीड़ित" loosely.
Source: PYQ-style

62. The two brothers divided the property ______ themselves.
(a) between (b) among (c) in (d) within
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Between" is used for two persons or things; "among" is used for more than two. Here there are only two brothers.
Source: NCERT Class 10 English Grammar

63. I am looking forward ______ meeting you soon.
(a) for (b) at (c) to (d) towards
Answer: (c)
Solution: In "look forward to", the word "to" is a preposition, so it is followed by a gerund (meeting), not an infinitive. This is a classic examiner trap.
Source: PYQ-style

64. Spot the error: Part P: He is senior / Part Q: than me / Part R: by two years. / Part S: No error
(a) Part P (b) Part R (c) Part Q (d) No error
Answer: (c)
Solution: Part Q is wrong. "Senior" is a Latin comparative and takes "to", not "than": "senior to me". The same rule applies to junior, superior, inferior and prior.
Source: PYQ-style

65. She congratulated me ______ my success.
(a) for (b) at (c) on (d) about
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Congratulate someone on something" is the fixed construction. "For" is a tempting but incorrect option.
Source: NCERT Class 10 English Grammar

66. The child was afraid ______ the dark.
(a) of (b) with (c) from (d) by
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Afraid of" is the correct phrase. Similarly: fond of, proud of, jealous of, tired of — all take "of".
Source: NCERT Class 9 English Grammar

67. He insisted ______ going with me.
(a) in (b) upon for (c) at (d) on
Answer: (d)
Solution: "Insist on" is the fixed phrase and is followed by a gerund. Note that "insist upon" is also correct, but the option here is distorted, so "on" is the answer.
Source: PYQ-style

68. Spot the error: Part P: Despite of / Part Q: the heavy rain, / Part R: the match continued. / Part S: No error
(a) Part Q (b) Part P (c) Part R (d) No error
Answer: (b)
Solution: Part P is wrong. "Despite" never takes "of"; the correct forms are "despite the rain" or "in spite of the rain". This is a favourite examiner trap.
Source: PYQ-style

69. The police are looking ______ the case carefully.
(a) into (b) after (c) over (d) at
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Look into" means to investigate. "Look after" means to take care of, and "look over" means to examine quickly — neither fits the context of a police case.
Source: NCERT Class 10 English Grammar

70. He died ______ cancer last year.
(a) of (b) from (c) by (d) with
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Die of" is used for diseases and hunger; "die from" is used for injuries or wounds; "die by" is used for means like the sword. Cancer is a disease, so "of" is correct.
Source: PYQ-style

71. You must abide ______ the rules of the institution.
(a) with (b) to (c) on (d) by
Answer: (d)
Solution: "Abide by" means to follow or obey rules. The other prepositions do not combine with "abide" in standard English.
Source: NCERT Class 10 English Grammar

72. The manager was angry ______ his clerk for coming late.
(a) upon (b) with (c) at (d) on
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Angry with" is used for a person; "angry at" is used for a thing or action. The clerk is a person, so "with" is correct.
Source: PYQ-style

73. I prefer tea ______ coffee.
(a) to (b) over (c) than (d) from
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Prefer" takes "to", not "than", when comparing two nouns: "I prefer tea to coffee." Using "than" is a very common error tested in exams.
Source: PYQ-style

74. He is ______ honest man and ______ university student.
(a) a, an (b) an, an (c) the, a (d) an, a
Answer: (d)
Solution: "Honest" begins with a vowel sound (h is silent), so it takes "an"; "university" begins with a consonant sound (yu), so it takes "a". Articles depend on sound, not spelling.
Source: NCERT Class 9 English Grammar

75. Spot the error: Part P: One of my friend / Part Q: has gone / Part R: to Mumbai yesterday. / Part S: No error
(a) Part R (b) Part Q (c) Part P (d) No error
Answer: (c)
Solution: Part P is wrong. After "one of", the noun must be plural: "one of my friends". The verb stays singular because the subject is "one".
Source: PYQ-style
