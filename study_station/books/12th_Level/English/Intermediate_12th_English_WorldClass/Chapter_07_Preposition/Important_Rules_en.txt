# 📋 Important Grammar Rules & Tips — Preposition

## (a) 12 High-Yield Rules

| # | Rule | Example | Examiner's Trap |
|---|------|---------|-----------------|
| 1 | **Since** = point of time; **For** = period of time | He has lived here **since** 2015 / **for** nine years. | "Since ten years" ❌ — period of time always takes *for*. |
| 2 | **In** = months/years/seasons; **On** = days/dates; **At** = exact time | **in** July, **on** Monday, **at** 5 o'clock | "On 2024" ❌ — years take *in*. |
| 3 | No preposition before **today, tomorrow, yesterday, last night, next week** | I met him **yesterday**. (not "on yesterday") | "On tomorrow" ❌ — a classic Sound-Right trap. |
| 4 | **Between** = two; **Among** = more than two | Divide it **between** Ram and Shyam; share it **among** the students. | "Between the three brothers" ❌ (unless comparing pairs). |
| 5 | **Beside** = next to; **Besides** = in addition to | Sit **beside** me. / **Besides** English, he knows French. | Examiners swap these two in error-spotting. |
| 6 | **In** = inside; **Into** = movement towards inside | He is **in** the room. / He went **into** the room. | "He jumped in the well" ❌ — movement needs *into*. |
| 7 | **By** = agent (passive); **With** = instrument | The snake was killed **by** the boy **with** a stick. | "Killed with the boy" ❌ — reverses agent and tool. |
| 8 | **On foot**, not "by foot"; but **by bus/car/train** | He goes to school **on foot** but returns **by bus**. | "By walk" ❌ — very common Hindi-speaker error. |
| 9 | **Different from**, not "different than/to" | My book is different **from** yours. | "Different than" is American informal — rejected in SSC/Bank exams. |
| 10 | **Married to, junior to, senior to, prefer...to** (Latin comparatives take *to*, not *than*) | He is senior **to** me. / I prefer tea **to** coffee. | "Senior than me" ❌ — top trap in error-spotting. |
| 11 | **Die of** (disease/hunger), **die from** (injury/wound), **die in** (accident/war) | He died **of** cholera / **from** his wounds / **in** an accident. | "Died with hunger" ❌ — cause of death takes *of*. |
| 12 | **At** = small place/specific point; **In** = large area | He lives **at** Rampur **in** Uttar Pradesh. | "He arrived in the station" ❌ — *arrive at* a station, *arrive in* a city. |

## (b) Time Management — The 15/45-Second Rule

| Type | What it looks like | Time | Strategy |
|------|-------------------|------|----------|
| **Fact-based (15 sec)** | Fill-in-the-blank with fixed prepositions (since/for, in/on/at, married to, die of) | 15 sec | Recall the rule instantly. If the rule doesn't fire in 15 seconds, mark and move on — do NOT re-read the sentence hoping it "sounds right". |
| **Inference-based (45 sec)** | Error-spotting with intervening phrases, sentence improvement where two options look close, prepositions after phrasal verbs in long sentences | 45 sec | Strip the sentence to subject + verb + object, then test the preposition against the rule. Check the trap type: Intervening Phrase, Sound-Right, or Over-thinking. |

**Rule of thumb:** A preposition question is 15-second if it tests ONE fixed rule; it is 45-second if you must first find WHERE the error hides in a long sentence.

## (c) Morphology — Roots & Affixes in Preposition Vocabulary

| Root/Affix | Meaning | Example Words | Preposition Link |
|------------|---------|---------------|------------------|
| **pre-** (Latin) | before | prefix, predict, precede | "Pre-position" itself = placed BEFORE the noun |
| **pos/pon** (Latin) | to place | position, postpone, deposit | A preposition "places" a noun in relation to other words |
| **inter-** (Latin) | between | international, intervene | Mirrors the preposition *between/among* |
| **trans-** (Latin) | across | transport, transfer | Mirrors *across, through* |
| **sub-** (Latin) | under | submarine, subway | Mirrors *under, below, beneath* |
| **super-** (Latin) | above/over | supervise, superior | Mirrors *over, above* |
| **-tion** (suffix) | act/state of | relation, position, location | Turns verbs into nouns that prepositions often follow |

**Memory hook:** *Pre + position* = "placed before" — a preposition normally comes **before** its object (noun/pronoun). One exception to remember: in questions and relative clauses, standard English allows a preposition to be left at the end ("What are you looking **at**?", "the book I told you **about**") — this is correct usage, not an error, so don't "correct" it in error-spotting questions.
