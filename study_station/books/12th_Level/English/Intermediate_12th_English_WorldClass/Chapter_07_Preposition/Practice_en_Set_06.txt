126. He has been suffering ___ fever since Monday.
(a) with (b) of (c) by (d) from
Answer: (d)
Solution: "Suffer from" is the fixed preposition for diseases. "Suffer with" is used for a person (suffer with someone), not an illness.

127. She is junior ___ me by two years.
(a) than (b) with (c) from (d) to
Answer: (d)
Solution: Latin comparatives like junior, senior, superior, inferior, prior and prefer take "to", never "than". This is a classic examiner trap.

128. The train arrived exactly ___ time.
(a) on (b) at (c) in (d) by
Answer: (a)
Solution: "On time" means punctually, at the scheduled moment. "In time" means early enough for something. The trap is confusing the two.
Source: NCERT Class 10 English Grammar

129. He was accused ___ theft by the police.
(a) for (b) with (c) on (d) of
Answer: (d)
Solution: "Accuse of" is the fixed combination. Compare: "blame for", "charge with" — similar verbs take different prepositions, which examiners exploit.

130. Spot the error: Part P: He is good / Part Q: in mathematics / Part R: but weak in English. / Part S: No error
(a) Part P (b) Part R (c) Part Q (d) No error
Answer: (c)
Solution: Part Q is wrong. The correct idiom is "good at" a subject, not "good in". "Weak in" is correct, so Part R is fine.

131. I have been living in Delhi ___ 2015.
(a) from (b) since (c) for (d) by
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Since" is used with a point of time (2015, Monday, childhood); "for" is used with a period (ten years). 2015 is a point of time.

132. The children were warned not to play ___ fire.
(a) with (b) on (c) by (d) at
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Play with fire" is the idiomatic expression meaning to take a dangerous risk. Other prepositions do not form the idiom.

133. Spot the error: Part P: Despite of / Part Q: the heavy rain, / Part R: the match continued. / Part S: No error
(a) Part P (b) Part Q (c) Part R (d) No error
Answer: (a)
Solution: Part P is wrong. "Despite" never takes "of"; either write "despite the rain" or "in spite of the rain". A very common trap.
Source: PYQ-style

134. He jumped ___ the river to save the drowning boy.
(a) in (b) into (c) on (d) upon
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Into" shows movement from outside to inside; "in" shows position. Jumping involves motion, so "into" is correct.

135. The manager agreed ___ my proposal after a long discussion.
(a) with (b) on (c) upon (d) to
Answer: (d)
Solution: "Agree to" a proposal/plan, "agree with" a person or opinion, "agree on" a point after discussion. Here the object is a proposal.
Source: PYQ-style

136. Spot the error: Part P: He ordered / Part Q: for a cup of tea / Part R: and a sandwich. / Part S: No error
(a) Part P (b) Part Q (c) Part R (d) No error
Answer: (b)
Solution: Part Q is wrong. "Order" as a verb takes a direct object without "for": "He ordered a cup of tea". "Order for" is used only when ordering on someone's behalf.
Source: PYQ-style

137. She is married ___ a doctor.
(a) to (b) with (c) by (d) from
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Married to" is the fixed preposition. Hindi speakers often say "married with" under the influence of "से शादी" — a classic linguistic trap.
Source: PYQ-style

138. The two brothers divided the property ___ themselves.
(a) among (b) between (c) within (d) amongst
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Between" is used for two persons; "among/amongst" for more than two. Two brothers, so "between".
Source: NCERT Class 9 English Grammar

139. Spot the error: Part P: He entered / Part Q: into the room / Part R: without knocking. / Part S: No error
(a) Part P (b) No error (c) Part R (d) Part Q
Answer: (d)
Solution: Part Q is wrong. "Enter" is never followed by "into" when it means to go inside: "He entered the room". "Enter into" is used only for agreements or discussions.
Source: PYQ-style

140. He is fond ___ reading detective novels.
(a) of (b) for (c) in (d) about
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Fond of" is the fixed adjective-preposition combination. No other option forms a correct phrase.
Source: NCERT Class 8 English Grammar

141. The meeting will be held ___ 10 a.m. ___ Monday.
(a) at, on (b) in, on (c) at, in (d) on, at
Answer: (a)
Solution: "At" is used for exact clock time; "on" for days and dates. So "at 10 a.m. on Monday".
Source: NCERT Class 9 English Grammar

142. Spot the error: Part P: He discussed / Part Q: about the problem / Part R: with his friends. / Part S: No error
(a) Part P (b) No error (c) Part R (d) Part Q
Answer: (d)
Solution: Part Q is wrong. "Discuss" takes a direct object without "about": "He discussed the problem". This is one of the most frequent examiner traps.
Source: PYQ-style

143. He died ___ cancer after a long illness.
(a) from (b) by (c) of (d) with
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Die of" is used for disease, hunger and old age; "die from" for injuries or wounds; "die by" for violence or suicide. Cancer is a disease.
Source: PYQ-style

144. The teacher was angry ___ the boy ___ his misbehaviour.
(a) with, on (b) at, for (c) with, for (d) upon, at
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Angry with" a person and "angry at/about" a thing; the reason is expressed with "for". So "angry with the boy for his misbehaviour".
Source: PYQ-style

145. Spot the error: Part P: He is / Part Q: capable to do / Part R: this difficult work. / Part S: No error
(a) Part P (b) Part Q (c) Part R (d) No error
Answer: (b)
Solution: Part Q is wrong. "Capable" is followed by "of" plus a gerund: "capable of doing". "Able" takes "to do", but "capable" does not.
Source: PYQ-style

146. We should abide ___ the rules of the institution.
(a) with (b) to (c) by (d) on
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Abide by" is the fixed phrasal combination meaning to obey or follow rules, laws or decisions.
Source: NCERT Class 10 English Grammar

147. Spot the error: Part P: He reached / Part Q: at the station / Part R: before the train left. / Part S: No error
(a) Part P (b) Part Q (c) Part R (d) No error
Answer: (b)
Solution: Part Q is wrong. "Reach" is never followed by a preposition: "He reached the station". "Arrive at" would be correct, but "reach at" is not.
Source: PYQ-style

148. He has been waiting for her ___ two hours.
(a) since (b) from (c) for (d) at
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Two hours" is a period of time, so "for" is correct. "Since" needs a point of time such as "since 5 o'clock".
Source: NCERT Class 9 English Grammar

149. Spot the error: Part P: He is / Part Q: more superior / Part R: to his colleague in experience. / Part S: No error
(a) Part P (b) Part R (c) Part Q (d) No error
Answer: (c)
Solution: Part Q is wrong. "Superior" is already comparative in sense, so "more" is redundant. Write "superior to his colleague". Examiners love this double-comparative trap.
Source: PYQ-style

150. He insisted ___ going with me to the market.
(a) on (b) for (c) in (d) at
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Insist on" plus a gerund is the fixed combination. No other preposition follows "insist" in standard English.
Source: PYQ-style
