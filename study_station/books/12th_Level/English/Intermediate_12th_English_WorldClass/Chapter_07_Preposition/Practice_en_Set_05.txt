101. He has been suffering ______ fever since Monday.
(a) with (b) from (c) by (d) of
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Suffer with" tempts learners because "with" often shows accompaniment, but the fixed verb-preposition pair is "suffer from" a disease. "Suffer of" and "suffer by" are not standard English. The correct preposition is "from", so the answer is from.
Source: NCERT Class 9 English Grammar

102. She is junior ______ me by two years.
(a) than (b) to (c) from (d) with
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is "than", which feels natural because the sentence compares two people. But Latin comparatives like junior, senior, superior, inferior and prefer take "to", never "than". The correct preposition is "to", so the answer is to.

103. The train arrived exactly ______ time.
(a) in (b) at (c) by (d) on
Answer: (d)
Solution: "In time" means before the deadline, which is a tempting confusion. "On time" means punctually, at the scheduled moment, which fits "exactly". "At time" and "by time" are wrong here. The correct phrase is "on time", so the answer is on.

104. He was accused ______ theft by the police.
(a) of (b) for (c) with (d) on
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Accused for" sounds plausible because "for" follows many verbs of reason, but the fixed pattern is "accuse somebody of something". "Accused with" and "accused on" are not used. The correct preposition is "of", so the answer is of.

105. Spot the error: Part P: He is / Part Q: capable to handle / Part R: any difficult situation / Part S: No error
(a) Part P (b) Part R (c) Part Q (d) No error
Answer: (c)
Solution: Part Q tempts readers because "to handle" follows the adjective smoothly, but "capable" always takes "of + gerund": "capable of handling". Parts P and R are correct. The error lies in Part Q, so the answer is Part Q.

106. I have been living in Delhi ______ 2015.
(a) since (b) from (c) for (d) by
Answer: (a)
Solution: "For" is the trap because the present perfect continuous often pairs with "for", but "for" is used with a period of time (for ten years), while "since" is used with a point of time (since 2015). "From" needs "to/till" to complete the sense. The correct preposition is "since", so the answer is since.

107. The manager agreed ______ my proposal after a long discussion.
(a) with (b) to (c) on (d) upon
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Agree with" is the trap, but it is used with a person or an opinion (agree with you), not with a proposal or plan. One agrees "to" a proposal, request or suggestion. "Agree on" is used when two sides reach mutual terms. The correct preposition is "to", so the answer is to.

109. Spot the error: Part P: Despite of / Part Q: the heavy rain, / Part R: the match continued. / Part S: No error
(a) Part P (b) Part Q (c) Part R (d) No error
Answer: (a)
Solution: Part P is the trap because "in spite of" is correct and learners extend the "of" to "despite". "Despite" never takes "of"; say either "despite the rain" or "in spite of the rain". Parts Q and R are fine. The error lies in Part P, so the answer is Part P.

110. The children were warned ______ the danger of swimming in the flooded river.
(a) against (b) about (c) of (d) from
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Warned against" tempts because "warn against doing something" is a real pattern, but here the noun "the danger" follows, and the structure is "warn somebody of the danger". "Warned from" is incorrect. The correct preposition is "of", so the answer is of.

112. He died ______ cancer after a long illness.
(a) from (b) with (c) by (d) of
Answer: (d)
Solution: "Died from" is the trap; modern usage allows "die from" for external causes like injuries, but for diseases the traditional exam answer is "die of". "Die by" is used for means like the sword, and "die with" is wrong here. The correct preposition is "of", so the answer is of.

113. The two brothers divided the property ______ themselves.
(a) among (b) in (c) between (d) within
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Among" tempts because it appears with plural groups, but "between" is used for two persons, and "among" for more than two. Here there are only two brothers. The correct preposition is "between", so the answer is between.

Answer: (d)
Solution: Part Q tempts because many verbs take an infinitive, but "insist" takes "on + gerund": "insisted on going". Parts P and R are correct. The error lies in Part Q, so the answer is Part Q.

Answer: (d)
Solution: "In" is the trap because it shows position, but the sentence shows movement from outside to inside, which requires "into". "Inside" and "within" do not express the motion here. The correct preposition is "into", so the answer is into.

124. Spot the error: Part P: Being a rainy day, / Part Q: I decided / Part R: to stay at home. / Part S: No error
(a) Part P (b) Part Q (c) Part R (d) No error
Answer: (a)
Solution: Part P is the trap because it sounds natural in Hindi-influenced English, but the dangling phrase "Being a rainy day" wrongly attaches to "I" — a person cannot be a rainy day. It should read "It being a rainy day, I decided…". Parts Q and R are correct. The error lies in Part P, so the answer is Part P.

Solution: "Consists from" tempts learners who confuse it with "is made from", but the fixed verb-preposition pair is "consist of". Note that "comprise" takes no preposition at all, which is the related trap. The correct preposition is "of", so the answer is of.
