Questions 26–40: Easy | Questions 41–50: Medium

26. She has been living in Delhi ______ 2015.
(a) since (b) for (c) from (d) at
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Since" is used with a point of time (2015, Monday, January). "For" is used with a period of time, such as "for five years", so it does not fit here.
Source: NCERT Class 9 English Grammar

27. The train arrived ______ time.
(a) in (b) on (c) by (d) at
Answer: (b)
Solution: "On time" means punctually, at the scheduled time. "In time" means before a deadline, which changes the meaning.
Source: PYQ-style

28. He is fond ______ playing cricket.
(a) at (b) with (c) in (d) of
Answer: (d)
Solution: "Fond" is always followed by the preposition "of". Such fixed preposition pairs must be memorised.
Source: NCERT Class 8 English Grammar

29. The cat jumped ______ the table.
(a) over (b) on (c) above (d) across
Answer: (b)
Solution: "On" shows contact with a surface, which fits a cat landing on a table. "Above" means higher than something without touching it, so it is wrong here.
Source: NCERT Class 7 English Grammar

30. We should not laugh ______ others.
(a) at (b) on (c) upon (d) over
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Laugh at" means to mock someone. "Laugh on" and "laugh over" are not standard usages in this sense.
Source: PYQ-style

31. My birthday falls ______ 15th August.
(a) in (b) at (c) on (d) by
Answer: (c)
Solution: "On" is used with specific dates and days. "In" goes with months and years, and "at" with exact times, so both are wrong here.
Source: NCERT Class 8 English Grammar

32. He died ______ cancer.
(a) with (b) of (c) from (d) by
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Die of" is used for diseases. "Die from" is used for injuries or external causes, so it does not fit an illness like cancer.
Source: PYQ-style

33. The children are afraid ______ the dark.
(a) of (b) with (c) from (d) by
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Afraid" takes the fixed preposition "of". This pair is frequently tested in exams.
Source: NCERT Class 8 English Grammar

34. She sat ______ her mother and her aunt.
(a) among (b) amidst (c) in (d) between
Answer: (d)
Solution: "Between" is used for two persons or things; "among" is used for more than two. Here there are exactly two people, so "between" is correct.
Source: NCERT Class 9 English Grammar

35. I have been waiting here ______ two hours.
(a) for (b) since (c) from (d) at
Answer: (a)
Solution: "For" is used with a duration or period of time, such as two hours. "Since" needs a starting point, not a duration, so it is the tempting wrong choice.
Source: NCERT Class 9 English Grammar

36. The shop is ______ the corner of the street.
(a) in (b) on (c) by (d) at
Answer: (d)
Solution: "At" is used for a specific point or spot, such as "at the corner" or "at the bus stop". "In the corner" refers to the inside corner of a room, not a street.
Source: PYQ-style

37. He is junior ______ me by two years.
(a) than (b) with (c) to (d) from
Answer: (c)
Solution: Words of Latin origin like junior, senior, superior, inferior and prefer take "to", not "than". Choosing "than" is the classic examiner trap.
Source: PYQ-style

38. The bridge was built ______ the river.
(a) over (b) above (c) on (d) upon
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Over" suggests spanning or covering from one side to the other, which suits a bridge. "Above" only means at a higher level, without the sense of spanning.
Source: NCERT Class 8 English Grammar

39. Please listen ______ what the teacher says.
(a) to (b) for (c) at (d) on
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Listen" is always followed by "to" when an object is mentioned. Without an object the preposition is dropped, as in "Listen carefully."
Source: NCERT Class 7 English Grammar

40. He apologised ______ his rude behaviour.
(a) about (b) at (c) for (d) on
Answer: (c)
Solution: The pattern is "apologise for" something and "apologise to" a person. Here the object is the behaviour, so "for" fits.
Source: PYQ-style

41. Spot the error: Part P: He has been suffering / Part Q: from fever / Part R: since last Monday. / Part S: No error
(a) Part P (b) Part R (c) No error (d) Part Q
Answer: (c)
Solution: The sentence is correct. "Has been suffering" matches the duration shown by "since last Monday", and "suffer from" is the right fixed pair for an illness, so no part has an error.
Source: PYQ-style

42. The manager was angry ______ his clerk for coming late.
(a) on (b) with (c) upon (d) at
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Angry with" is used for a person; "angry at" or "angry about" is used for a thing or situation. The clerk is a person, so "with" is correct.
Source: NCERT Class 9 English Grammar

43. Spot the error: Part P: Despite of / Part Q: heavy rain, / Part R: the match continued. / Part S: No error
(a) No error (b) Part P (c) Part R (d) Part Q
Answer: (b)
Solution: Part P is wrong. "Despite" never takes "of"; write either "despite heavy rain" or "in spite of heavy rain". This is a favourite examiner trap.
Source: PYQ-style

44. He has been absent ______ class since Friday.
(a) in (b) of (c) at (d) from
Answer: (d)
Solution: "Absent from" is the fixed preposition pair. "Absent in" and "absent of" are incorrect usages.
Source: NCERT Class 8 English Grammar

45. The two brothers divided the property ______ themselves.
(a) within (b) among (c) between (d) amongst
Answer: (c)
Solution: Although "themselves" sounds plural, the division is between two brothers, so "between" is correct. "Among" and "amongst" are used for more than two.
Source: PYQ-style

46. She is married ______ a doctor.
(a) with (b) from (c) by (d) to
Answer: (d)
Solution: "Married to" is the correct fixed preposition. "Married with" is a common error among Hindi speakers because Hindi uses "se".
Source: PYQ-style

47. I prefer tea ______ coffee.
(a) over (b) to (c) than (d) from
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Prefer" takes "to", not "than", when comparing two nouns. "Prefer tea over coffee" is informal; in exams "to" is the expected answer.
Source: PYQ-style

48. The police are looking ______ the case carefully.
(a) for (b) at (c) after (d) into
Answer: (d)
Solution: "Look into" means to investigate, which fits the context. "Look after" means to take care of and "look for" means to search, so neither fits.
Source: NCERT Class 9 English Grammar

49. He jumped ______ the well to save the child.
(a) above (b) in (c) into (d) upon
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Into" shows movement from outside to inside, which matches the action of jumping. "In" shows position, not motion, so it is wrong here.
Source: NCERT Class 8 English Grammar

50. Spot the error: Part P: One of my friend / Part Q: has gone / Part R: to Mumbai. / Part S: No error
(a) Part P (b) Part Q (c) Part R (d) No error
Answer: (a)
Solution: Part P is wrong. After "one of", a plural noun is required: "one of my friends". The verb stays singular because the subject is "one", so Part Q is correct.
Source: PYQ-style
