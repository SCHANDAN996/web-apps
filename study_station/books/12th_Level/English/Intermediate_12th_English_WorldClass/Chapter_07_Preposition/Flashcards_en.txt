Card 1
Front: What is a preposition?
Back: A word placed before a noun or pronoun to show its relationship with another word in the sentence (e.g., on, in, at, by, with).

Card 2
Front: Which preposition is used for exact clock time — in, on, or at?
Back: At. Example: The train leaves at 6 o'clock. (At = exact point of time.)

Card 3
Front: Fill in: He has been living in Delhi ______ 2015.
Back: Since. "Since" is used with a point of time (2015, Monday, January); "for" is used with a period of time (two years, a long time).

Card 4
Front: MNEMONIC: How to remember the time prepositions in–on–at?
Back: "Big to Small: IN–ON–AT" — IN for big time (years, months, seasons), ON for days and dates, AT for exact clock time. Think of zooming in: year → day → hour.

Card 5
Front: EXAMINER'S TRAP: "He is senior than me." — Correct or wrong?
Back: Wrong. Senior, junior, superior, inferior, prefer take "to", not "than". Correct: He is senior to me.

Card 6
Front: Which preposition follows "married", "angry (with a person)", and "good (at a skill)"?
Back: Married to, angry with, good at. Example: She is married to a doctor. / He is angry with me. / She is good at maths.

Card 7
Front: Fill in: The cat jumped ______ the wall. (above / over)
Back: Over. "Over" shows movement across or above something; "above" shows a static higher position.

Card 8
Front: What is the difference between "between" and "among"?
Back: "Between" is used for two persons/things (or clear one-to-one relations); "among" is used for more than two. Example: Between Ram and Shyam; among the students.

Card 9
Front: Fill in: He died ______ cancer, but he died ______ overwork.
Back: Died of cancer (disease), died from overwork (cause). Die of = disease/hunger; die from = injury/cause; die in = accident/war.

Card 10
Front: EXAMINER'S TRAP: "Despite of the rain, we went out." — Correct or wrong?
Back: Wrong. "Despite" never takes "of". Correct: Despite the rain, we went out. (But: In spite of the rain — "in spite of" needs "of".)

Card 11
Front: MNEMONIC: How to remember prepositions that never take "of"?
Back: "D-C-O": Despite, Comprise, Order (as a verb) — never followed by "of". Say: "The DCO officer needs no 'of'."

Card 12
Front: Fill in: I have been waiting here ______ two hours.
Back: For. "For" + period of time (two hours, five days); "since" + point of time (5 o'clock, Monday).

Card 13
Front: Which preposition: "He is fond ______ music" and "He is afraid ______ dogs"?
Back: Fond of, afraid of. Both are adjective + "of" combinations frequently tested in exams.

Card 14
Front: What is the difference between "in" and "into"?
Back: "In" shows position (rest): He is in the room. "Into" shows movement from outside to inside: He went into the room.

Card 15
Front: Fill in: The bridge is ______ the river. (on / over)
Back: Over. "Over" means directly above, often covering or crossing; "on" means touching the surface.

Card 16
Front: Which preposition follows these verbs: listen, look, wait, depend?
Back: Listen to, look at, wait for, depend on. Example: Listen to the teacher. / We depend on our parents.

Card 17
Front: Fill in: The book is different ______ yours.
Back: From. "Different from" is the standard form; "different than" is considered incorrect in exams.

Card 18
Front: EXAMINER'S TRAP: "He entered into the room." — Correct or wrong?
Back: Wrong. "Enter" (meaning go into) is not followed by "into". Correct: He entered the room. (Similarly: reach the station, not "reach to the station".)

Card 19
Front: Fill in: We travelled ______ bus but went there ______ foot.
Back: By bus, on foot. "By" + vehicle (by car, by train), but "on foot" is a fixed exception.

Card 20
Front: Which preposition: "He was accused ______ theft" and "He was acquitted ______ all charges"?
Back: Accused of, acquitted of. Both take "of" — a frequent pair in error-spotting questions.
