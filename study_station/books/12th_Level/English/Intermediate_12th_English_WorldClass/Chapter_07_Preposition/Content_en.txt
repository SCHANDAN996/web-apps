# Preposition — The Small Words That Decide Big Marks

Imagine this: you write *"I have been waiting since two hours"* in your exam. It sounds perfectly fine to a Hindi speaker's ear — "मैं दो घंटे से इंतज़ार कर रहा हूँ।" But the examiner marks it wrong. The correct sentence is *"I have been waiting **for** two hours."* One tiny word — *since* vs *for* — and one mark gone.

That is the power of prepositions. They are the smallest words in English grammar, yet they appear in almost every error-spotting, fill-in-the-blank, and sentence-improvement question in SSC CHSL, RRB NTPC, and similar exams. Master them, and you convert your weakest area into guaranteed marks.

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this chapter, you will be able to:

1. Identify the correct preposition of **time, place, direction, and agency** in any sentence.
2. Apply the **12 Golden Rules** that cover the vast majority of exam questions.
3. Spot the examiner's favourite traps — especially the *since/for*, *in/at/on*, and *between/among* confusions.
4. Correct the typical errors Hindi speakers make because of direct translation.

---

## 1. What Is a Preposition?

A **preposition** is a word placed **before** a noun or pronoun to show its **relationship** with another word in the sentence.

- The book is **on** the table. (relationship between *book* and *table*)
- She came **after** lunch. (relationship between *came* and *lunch*)

Think of a preposition as a **bridge** — it connects two ideas and tells you *how* they are connected: in time, in place, in direction, or in manner.

> 🧠 **Active Recall:** Without looking back — what two things does a preposition connect? Give one example of your own.

---

## 2. Prepositions of Time — *at, on, in, since, for*

This is the most-tested family. Learn it as a **pyramid**:

| Preposition | Used for | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **at** | exact point of time | at 5 o'clock, at noon, at midnight, at dawn |
| **on** | days and dates | on Monday, on 15th August, on Diwali |
| **in** | longer periods | in January, in 2024, in the morning, in winter |

**Mnemonic 🎯 — "A Point, A Day, A Period":**
**AT** a point (5 pm), **ON** a day (Monday), **IN** a period (July / 2024 / the evening).

### Since vs For — the examiner's favourite trap

- **Since** → point of time (when it *started*): since Monday, since 2010, since childhood.
- **For** → period/duration of time (how *long*): for two hours, for five years, for a long time.

- ✅ He has lived here **since** 2015.
- ✅ He has lived here **for** nine years.

> 🧠 **Active Recall:** Fill in the blanks — (a) I have known her ___ March. (b) I have known her ___ six months. *(Answers: since, for)*

---

## 3. Prepositions of Place — *at, on, in*

| Preposition | Used for | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **at** | a specific point | at the bus stop, at the door, at the station |
| **on** | a surface | on the table, on the wall, on the first floor |
| **in** | an enclosed area | in the room, in Delhi, in the box |

- **In** a city/country (in India, in Mumbai) but **at** a specific address (at 12, MG Road).
- **On** the left/right, **on** the river bank, **on** the map.

### Between vs Among

- **Between** → two persons/things: The secret is **between** you and me.
- **Among** → more than two: Distribute the sweets **among** the children.

> 🧠 **Active Recall:** Which is correct — "Divide the property between the three brothers" or "among the three brothers"? *(Answer: among — more than two.)*

---

## 4. Prepositions of Direction & Movement

- **To** → destination: She went **to** school.
- **Into** → movement inside: He jumped **into** the river. (Compare: He is **in** the river — already inside, no movement.)
- **Towards** → in the direction of: The dog ran **towards** me.
- **Across** → from one side to the other: They swam **across** the Ganga.
- **Through** → passing within: The train passed **through** the tunnel.

> 🧠 **Active Recall:** What is the difference between *in* and *into*? *(In = position inside; into = movement from outside to inside.)*

---

## 5. Other High-Yield Prepositions

- **By / With** (agency vs instrument): The letter was written **by** Ram **with** a pen. (*by* = doer, *with* = tool)
- **Beside / Besides**: Sit **beside** me (next to). **Besides** English, she knows French (in addition to).
- **Above / Over**: *above* = higher than (not touching); *over* = directly higher, often covering or touching.
- **Below / Under**: *below* = lower than; *under* = directly lower, often covered.
- **Ago / Before**: He came two days **ago** (from now). He had come two days **before** (from a past point).

---

## ⚠️ Examiner's Trap Box

**Trap 1 — "Since two hours":** Hindi "दो घंटे से" tempts you to write *since*. Wrong. Duration takes **for**: *for two hours*.

**Trap 2 — "Married with":** Correct form is **married to**, not *married with*. Similarly: *angry with* a person, *angry at* a thing; *good at*, *interested in*, *afraid of*, *depend on*.

**Trap 3 — "Discuss about":** *Discuss* never takes *about*. ✅ We discussed the plan. ❌ We discussed about the plan. Same with *comprise* (no *of*), *enter* (no *into* a room), *reach* (no *to* a place).

## 🔁 Linguistic Bridge — Hindi से English

| Hindi speakers often say ❌ | Correct English ✅ |
|---|---|
| He is angry **on** me. | He is angry **with** me. |
| I reached **to** Delhi. | I reached Delhi. |
| She is suffering **from** fever since Monday. *(mixed)* | She has been suffering from fever **since** Monday. |
| We discussed **about** the matter. | We discussed the matter. |
| He jumped **in** the well. | He jumped **into** the well. |
| Open page **number** ten **of** the book. | Open the book **at** page ten. |

**Why does this happen?** Hindi uses postpositions (के, में, से, पर) that come *after* the noun, and one Hindi word often maps to several English prepositions. "से" alone can be *from, since, by, with* — so direct translation fails. Learn prepositions as **fixed partnerships** with verbs and adjectives, not as translations.

---

## 6. The 12 Golden Rules of Prepositions

1. **At** = exact time; **on** = day/date; **in** = month/year/season/part of day.
2. **Since** = point of time; **for** = period of time.
3. **In** = rest inside; **into** = movement inside.
4. **Between** = two; **among** = more than two.
5. **By** = doer/agent; **with** = instrument.
6. **Beside** = next to; **besides** = in addition to.
7. Verbs like *discuss, enter, reach, comprise, order, request* take **no preposition** before their object.
8. **Married to, angry with (person), good at, interested in, afraid of, depend on, consist of, listen to** — fixed pairs; never translate from Hindi.
9. **Ago** counts back from now; **before** counts back from a past moment.
10. **On foot** (not *by foot*), but **by bus/train/car** (no article).
11. **At night** but **in the morning/afternoon/evening**.
12. A preposition is followed by a noun/pronoun in the **objective case**: *between you and **me*** (never *between you and I*).
