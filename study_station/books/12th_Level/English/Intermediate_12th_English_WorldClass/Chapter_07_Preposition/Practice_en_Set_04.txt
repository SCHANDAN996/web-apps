76. She has been suffering ___ fever since Monday.
(a) with (b) of (c) by (d) from
Answer: (d)
Solution: "Suffer from" is the fixed preposition for illness or disease. "Suffer with" is a common trap but incorrect in standard usage.

77. He is junior ___ me by two years.
(a) than (b) from (c) with (d) to
Answer: (d)
Solution: Latin comparatives like junior, senior, superior, inferior and prefer take "to", not "than". "Than" is the examiner's favourite trap here.

78. The train arrived exactly ___ time.
(a) in (b) at (c) by (d) on
Answer: (d)
Solution: "On time" means punctually, at the scheduled moment. "In time" means before a deadline, which changes the meaning.

79. He was accused ___ stealing the money.

81. I have been living in Delhi ___ 2015.
(a) from (b) since (c) for (d) by
Answer: (b)

82. She is good ___ mathematics but weak ___ English.

83. The child hid ___ the bed when he heard the noise.

84. He died ___ cancer last year.

Answer: (a)

86. We should abide ___ the rules of the institution.

87. He jumped ___ the river to save the drowning boy.

91. He has been waiting here ___ two hours.
(a) since (b) from (c) for (d) at
Answer: (c)
Solution: "For" is used with a duration (two hours); "since" needs a starting point (2 o'clock). Duration versus point of time is the key distinction.

92. The two brothers divided the property ___ themselves.

Answer: (b)
Solution: "Between" is used for two persons; "among" for more than two. The phrase "two brothers" fixes the answer.

93. He is fond ___ playing chess.

Answer: (b)

99. Hardly had he stepped out of the house ___ it began to rain.
(a) than (b) then (c) when (d) that
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Hardly/Scarcely … when" is the correct correlative pair. "No sooner … than" is the parallel structure; mixing them is the examiner's trap.

100. Choose the correctly framed sentence:
(a) He entered into the room quietly.
(b) He entered the room quietly.
(c) He entered in the room quietly.
(d) He entered at the room quietly.
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Enter" takes no preposition before a place: "entered the room". "Entered into" is used only for agreements or discussions, which makes the first option a trap.
