# 📊 PYQ Analysis — Adjective

## How Exams Test Adjectives (Pattern Analysis)

Across SSC (CGL, CHSL, MTS, GD), Banking (IBPS, SBI), and Railway (NTPC, Group D) exams, adjectives appear in **four recurring formats**:

1. **Spot the Error** — the most common format. The error hides in degree of comparison ("more superior"), wrong adjective form ("He is more taller"), or adjective–adverb confusion ("She sings beautiful").
2. **Fill in the Blank** — choose the correct degree (positive / comparative / superlative) or the correct adjective from confusing pairs (fewer/less, elder/older, many/much).
3. **Sentence Improvement** — replace the underlined part with the grammatically correct adjective usage.
4. **One-word / Arrangement** — order of adjectives (opinion → size → age → shape → colour → origin → material → purpose) before a noun.

**Sub-topics that repeat most often:**
- Degrees of comparison and their traps (superior/inferior take *to*, not *than*)
- Confusing pairs: fewer/less, many/much, elder/older, later/latter, little/a little/the little
- Adjective vs. adverb after verbs of sense (look, feel, smell, taste, sound)
- Order of adjectives before a noun
- "The + adjective" as a noun (the rich, the poor)

**Examiner's favourite traps:**
- *Near-correct statements*: "more superior" sounds fine in speech but is wrong.
- *Sound-right errors*: "I feel badly" — after *feel* (state), we need the adjective *bad*.
- *Intervening phrase*: a long phrase between subject and adjective distracts from agreement.
- *Over-thinking*: a simple rule question dressed in a long, formal sentence.

## Top 10 High-Yield Questions (with Solutions)

**Q1.** He is more superior to his brother in intelligence.
(a) He is more superior (b) to his brother (c) in intelligence (d) No error
**Answer: (a)**
**Solution:** *Superior* is a Latin comparative adjective; it never takes *more/most* and is followed by *to*, not *than*. Correct: "He is superior to his brother…"
**Trap type:** Sound-Right | **Source:** PYQ-style

**Q2.** She sings very ______.
(a) beautiful (b) beautifully (c) beauty (d) beautify
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** The verb *sings* (an action) needs an adverb, not an adjective. *Beautifully* modifies *sings*.
**Trap type:** Sound-Right | **Source:** PYQ-style

**Q3.** There were ______ people at the rally than we expected.
(a) less (b) fewer (c) lesser (d) few
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** *People* is countable → *fewer*. *Less* is for uncountable nouns (less water).
**Trap type:** Confusing pair | **Source:** PYQ-style

**Q4.** My ______ brother is a doctor in Delhi.
(a) older (b) elder (c) eldest (d) old
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** For family members used attributively (before the noun), use *elder/eldest*. *Older* is used with *than* or for things.
**Trap type:** Confusing pair | **Source:** PYQ-style

**Q5.** This is the ______ building in the whole city.
(a) tall (b) taller (c) tallest (d) most tall
**Answer: (c)**
**Solution:** "In the whole city" signals comparison among many → superlative *tallest*. *Most tall* is never correct.
**Trap type:** Over-thinking | **Source:** PYQ-style

**Q6.** The food smells ______.
(a) well (b) good (c) nicely (d) sweetly
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** Verbs of sense (smell, taste, look, feel, sound) describing a *state* take an adjective, not an adverb. "Smells good" describes the food, not the act of smelling.
**Trap type:** Sound-Right | **Source:** PYQ-style

**Q7.** He bought a ______ watch from Switzerland.
(a) Swiss beautiful gold (b) beautiful gold Swiss (c) beautiful Swiss gold (d) gold beautiful Swiss
**Answer: (c)**
**Solution:** Adjective order: opinion (beautiful) → origin (Swiss) → material (gold). Remember **OSASCOMP**.
**Trap type:** Over-thinking | **Source:** PYQ-style

**Q8.** ______ should help the poor.
(a) Rich (b) The rich (c) A rich (d) Riches
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** "The + adjective" denotes a whole class of people: *the rich* = rich people. It takes a plural verb.
**Trap type:** Intervening Phrase (class noun confusion) | **Source:** PYQ-style

**Q9.** Of the two sisters, Riya is the ______.
(a) tallest (b) taller (c) tall (d) most tall
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** Between exactly two, use the comparative with *the*: "the taller of the two." Superlative needs three or more.
**Trap type:** Over-thinking | **Source:** PYQ-style

**Q10.** He gave me ______ information, so I could not decide.
(a) a little (b) little (c) the little (d) few
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** *Little* = almost none (negative sense), which matches "could not decide." *A little* = some (positive). *Information* is uncountable, so *few* is impossible.
**Trap type:** Confusing pair | **Source:** PYQ-style

## Applying the 15/45-Second Rule

**Q2 (15-second, Fact-based):** See a verb of action + blank → ask "adjective or adverb?" Action verb → adverb. *Beautifully*. No reading of the full sentence needed beyond the verb. Target: under 15 seconds.

**Q9 (45-second, Inference-based):** The trap is the phrase "Of the two sisters." Many students reflexively pick the superlative *tallest*. You must pause, count the items being compared (two), recall the special rule (comparative + *the* for two), then answer. Budget up to 45 seconds — this question *rewards* careful reading.

**Exam strategy:** Degree questions with "than" or "of the two" → slow down (45 sec). Simple adjective/adverb choice after a verb → trust the rule, move fast (15 sec).
