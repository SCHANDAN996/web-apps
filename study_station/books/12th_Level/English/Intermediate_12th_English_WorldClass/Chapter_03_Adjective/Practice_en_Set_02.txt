26. Choose the correct sentence.
(a) She is more taller than her sister. (b) She is much more taller than her sister. (c) She is most taller than her sister. (d) She is taller than her sister.
Answer: (d)
Solution: "More taller" is a double comparative, a classic examiner trap — never use "more/most" with an -er/-est form. Only one comparative marker is allowed, so the answer is "She is taller than her sister."
Source: NCERT Class 8 English Grammar

27. Identify the adjective in the sentence: "The hungry boy ate quickly."
(a) hungry (b) boy (c) ate (d) quickly
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Quickly" tempts because it describes the verb, but a word describing a verb is an adverb, not an adjective. "Hungry" describes the noun "boy", so the answer is "hungry".
Source: NCERT Class 7 English Grammar

28. Fill in the blank: This is the ______ book I have ever read.
(a) best (b) better (c) good (d) most good
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Better" tempts because it is a comparative, but "ever read" compares the book with all books, needing a superlative. "Most good" is wrong English; the superlative of good is "best", so the answer is "best".
Source: NCERT Class 8 English Grammar

29. Choose the correct order of adjectives: "She bought a ______ dress."
(a) beautiful red silk (b) red beautiful silk (c) silk red beautiful (d) red silk beautiful
Answer: (a)
Solution: Adjective order is Opinion–Size–Age–Shape–Colour–Origin–Material (OSASCOMP). "Beautiful" (opinion) comes before "red" (colour) and "silk" (material), so the answer is "beautiful red silk".
Source: Wren & Martin, High School English Grammar

30. Fill in the blank: There is ______ water in the jug.
(a) many (b) few (c) several (d) much
Answer: (d)
Solution: "Many" tempts students who ignore countability, but water is uncountable. "Much" is used with uncountable nouns, so the answer is "much".
Source: NCERT Class 6 English Grammar

31. Choose the correct sentence.
(a) He is senior to me. (b) He is senior than me. (c) He is senior from me. (d) He is senior of me.
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Senior than" is the most common trap — Latin comparatives (senior, junior, superior, inferior, prior, prefer) take "to", never "than". So the answer is "He is senior to me."
Source: Wren & Martin, High School English Grammar

32. Identify the type of adjective in: "Which book do you want?"
(a) Adjective of quality (b) Interrogative adjective (c) Demonstrative adjective (d) Possessive adjective
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Which" here is not a pronoun because it comes before the noun "book" and asks a question about it. A question-word used before a noun is an interrogative adjective, so the answer is "Interrogative adjective".
Source: NCERT Class 7 English Grammar

33. Fill in the blank: Gold is ______ than silver.
(a) precious (b) much precious (c) most precious (d) more precious
Answer: (d)
Solution: "Much precious" tempts because "much" intensifies, but "much" cannot form a comparative by itself. Long adjectives form comparatives with "more", so the answer is "more precious".
Source: NCERT Class 8 English Grammar

34. Choose the correct sentence.
(a) I have fewer money than you. (b) I have least money than you. (c) I have lesser money than you. (d) I have less money than you.
Answer: (d)
Solution: "Fewer" is the trap — it is used only with countable nouns, and money is uncountable. "Less" is used with uncountable nouns, so the answer is "I have less money than you."
Source: Wren & Martin, High School English Grammar

35. Identify the adjective of quantity in: "He drank some milk."
(a) some (b) drank (c) milk (d) He
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Milk" tempts as the thing measured, but it is a noun, not an adjective. "Some" shows indefinite quantity of the noun, so the answer is "some".
Source: NCERT Class 6 English Grammar

36. Fill in the blank: Mount Everest is the ______ peak in the world.
(a) high (b) higher (c) most high (d) highest
Answer: (d)
Solution: "Higher" tempts because two things seem compared, but Everest is compared with all peaks in the world, needing a superlative. The superlative of high is "highest", so the answer is "highest".
Source: NCERT Class 7 English Grammar

37. Choose the correct sentence.
(a) Rich should help poor. (b) The rich should help the poors. (c) The rich should help the poor. (d) The riches should help the poor.
Answer: (c)
Solution: "The poors" is the trap — adjectives used as nouns with "the" (the rich, the poor) are already plural in meaning and never take -s. So the answer is "The rich should help the poor."
Source: Wren & Martin, High School English Grammar

38. Fill in the blank: ______ boy in the class passed the test. (every/each sense of 'all individually')
(a) Many (b) Every (c) Much (d) Several
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Many" and "several" need plural nouns, but "boy" here is singular. "Every" is a distributive adjective used with a singular noun, so the answer is "Every".
Source: NCERT Class 7 English Grammar

39. Identify the demonstrative adjective in: "Those mangoes are sweet."
(a) mangoes (b) Those (c) are (d) sweet
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Sweet" tempts as a describing word, but it is an adjective of quality, not a pointer. "Those" points out which mangoes, so the answer is "Those".
Source: NCERT Class 6 English Grammar

40. Choose the correct sentence.
(a) Delhi is bigger than any city in India. (b) Delhi is bigger than all city in India. (c) Delhi is bigger than any other city in India. (d) Delhi is bigger than every cities in India.
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Any city" is the trap — without "other", Delhi would be compared with itself, which is illogical. When comparing a member with its own group, use "any other", so the answer is "Delhi is bigger than any other city in India."
Source: Wren & Martin, High School English Grammar

41. Spot the error: Part P: "No sooner had I" / Part Q: "seen the darker cloud" / Part R: "than it started raining." / Part S: No error
(a) Part P (b) Part Q (c) Part R (d) No error
Answer: (b)
Solution: Part Q tempts students who think "darker" fits the mood, but here only one cloud is described with no comparison, so the positive degree "dark" is required. The error is in Part Q.

42. Choose the correctly framed comparison.
(a) The population of India is greater than China. (b) The population of India is greater than those of China. (c) The population of India is greater than that of China. (d) The population of India is greater than the China.
Answer: (c)
Solution: Option without "that of" is the classic trap — it wrongly compares population with China itself. "That of" stands for "the population of", making the comparison logical, so the answer is "The population of India is greater than that of China."
Source: PYQ-style

43. Fill in the blank: Of the two brothers, Ravi is the ______.
(a) taller (b) tall (c) tallest (d) most tall
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Tallest" tempts because of "the", but when exactly two are compared, English uses "the + comparative", not the superlative. So the answer is "taller".
Source: Wren & Martin, High School English Grammar

44. Spot the error: Part P: "She is the most" / Part Q: "unique painting" / Part R: "in the exhibition." / Part S: No error
(a) Part R (b) Part Q (c) Part P (d) No error
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Unique" means one of a kind and has no degrees, so "most unique" is wrong — this is an absolute-adjective trap. The error lies in Part P.

45. Choose the correct sentence.
(a) He is the best of the two players. (b) He is the better of the two players. (c) He is the good of the two players. (d) He is the most good of the two players.
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Best" is the trap — superlatives need three or more items, and here only two players are compared. With two, use the comparative, so the answer is "He is the better of the two players."
Source: NCERT Class 8 English Grammar

46. Fill in the blank: This is ______ worse than I expected.
(a) very (b) more (c) much (d) most
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Very" tempts as an intensifier, but "very" modifies positive degree; comparatives like "worse" are intensified by "much" or "far". So the answer is "much".
Source: Wren & Martin, High School English Grammar

47. Spot the error: Part P: "My house is" / Part Q: "more farther" / Part R: "than yours." / Part S: No error
(a) Part Q (b) Part P (c) Part R (d) No error
Answer: (a)
Solution: "More farther" is a double comparative — "farther" already carries the comparative sense, so "more" must be dropped. The error is in Part Q.
Source: PYQ-style

48. Choose the correct sentence.
(a) I prefer tea more than coffee. (b) I prefer tea than coffee. (c) I prefer tea over than coffee. (d) I prefer tea to coffee.
Answer: (d)
Solution: "Prefer... than" is the trap — "prefer" is a Latin comparative and always takes "to", and adding "more" makes it a double comparative. So the answer is "I prefer tea to coffee."
Source: Wren & Martin, High School English Grammar

49. Fill in the blank: The ______ news made everyone sad.
(a) late (b) latter (c) latest (d) last
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Late" tempts as an adjective of time, but the sentence needs "most recent", which is "latest". "Latter" means the second of two and "last" means final, so the answer is "latest".
Source: NCERT Class 9 English Grammar

50. Spot the error: Part P: "He gave me" / Part Q: "a five-rupees" / Part R: "note yesterday." / Part S: No error
(a) Part P (b) Part Q (c) Part R (d) No error
Answer: (b)
Solution: In a hyphenated compound adjective like "five-rupee", the noun stays singular — "five-rupees" is the trap. The error is in Part Q.
