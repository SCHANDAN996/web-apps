# 📋 Important Grammar Rules & Tips — Adjective

## (a) 12 High-Yield Rules

| # | Rule | Example | Examiner's Trap |
|---|------|---------|-----------------|
| 1 | Adjectives of quantity (much, little, whole) go with **uncountable** nouns; adjectives of number (many, few) go with **countable** nouns. | He has **much** money. / He has **many** books. | "He gave me many advices." ❌ → "much advice" ✔ (advice is uncountable) |
| 2 | **Some** is used in affirmative sentences; **any** in negative and interrogative sentences. | I have **some** friends. / I don't have **any** friends. | "I have any money." ❌ → "some money" ✔ |
| 3 | **Few** = almost none (negative sense); **a few** = some (positive sense); **the few** = not many, but all that there are. | **Few** students passed. / **A few** students passed. / **The few** friends he had helped him. | "He has few friends, so he is never lonely." ❌ — logic demands "a few" |
| 4 | **Little** = almost nothing; **a little** = some; **the little** = not much, but all that there is. | There is **little** hope. / There is **a little** milk left. | "There is little water, so give him some." ❌ → "a little water" ✔ |
| 5 | Comparative degree is used for **two** persons/things; superlative for **more than two**. | She is the **taller** of the two sisters. / She is the **tallest** of the three. | "He is the best of the two brothers." ❌ → "the better of the two" ✔ |
| 6 | Adjectives ending in **-ior** (senior, junior, superior, inferior, prior, posterior) take **to**, not "than". | He is senior **to** me. | "He is senior than me." ❌ — the most repeated trap in SSC/Bank exams |
| 7 | Words like **unique, perfect, universal, extreme, complete, empty, full** have no comparative/superlative degree. | This is the most **unique** design. ❌ → This design is **unique**. ✔ | "Most unique / more perfect" is grammatically wrong — examiners love it |
| 8 | When two adjectives describe the same noun, both must be in the **same degree**. | She is the **wisest and most beautiful** girl in the class. | "She is the wisest and more beautiful girl." ❌ |
| 9 | Order of adjectives before a noun: **Opinion–Size–Age–Shape–Colour–Origin–Material–Purpose** (OSASCOMP). | A **beautiful small old round black Italian leather** bag. | "A leather black Italian bag" ❌ — wrong order |
| 10 | **The + adjective** denotes a whole class (plural verb follows). | **The rich** should help **the poor**. | "The rich is always happy." ❌ → "The rich **are**…" ✔ |
| 11 | Adjectives like **asleep, afraid, alive, alike, alone** are used only **predicatively** (after the verb), never before the noun. | The baby is **asleep**. | "An asleep baby" ❌ → "a sleeping baby" ✔ |
| 12 | **Enough** comes **after** the adjective but **before** the noun. | He is **brave enough**. / He has **enough money**. | "He is enough brave." ❌ — word-order trap |

## (b) Time Management — The 15/45-Second Rule

| Type | What it looks like | Time | Strategy |
|------|-------------------|------|----------|
| **Fact-based (15 sec)** | Direct rule recall: senior/junior + to, few/a few, much/many, order of adjectives, degrees of comparison | 15 seconds | Spot the keyword (than, to, two, of the…) → apply the rule → mark and move. Never re-read. |
| **Inference-based (45 sec)** | Sentence improvement with 2–3 errors, cloze-test adjective choice, "which option best describes…", tone/attitude words in RC | 45 seconds | Read the full sentence twice, eliminate options that break a rule first, then choose on meaning and tone. |

**Rule of thumb:** If the answer depends on a single memorised rule → 15 sec. If it depends on the meaning of the whole sentence → 45 sec. If you cross 60 seconds, mark for review and skip.

## (c) Morphology — Roots, Affixes & Adjective Formation

| Root / Affix | Meaning | Adjective formed | Example |
|--------------|---------|------------------|---------|
| **-ful** | full of | hopeful, beautiful, careful | a hopeful candidate |
| **-less** | without | hopeless, careless, fearless | a fearless soldier |
| **-ous / -ious** | having the quality of | dangerous, ambitious, curious | a dangerous road |
| **-able / -ible** | capable of being | readable, visible, comfortable | a visible change |
| **-ive** | tending to | active, attractive, creative | an active member |
| **-al / -ial** | relating to | national, cultural, social | a national issue |
| **-ic** | relating to | historic, scientific, heroic | a heroic deed |
| **-ly** (added to nouns) | having the quality of | friendly, costly, cowardly | a friendly neighbour |
| **-en** | made of / become | wooden, golden, woollen | a wooden chair |
| **-ish** | somewhat / like | foolish, reddish, childish | a foolish mistake |
| **un- / in- / dis-** (prefix) | not / opposite | unhappy, incorrect, dishonest | an incorrect answer |
| **-y** | full of / like | rainy, dusty, noisy | a noisy classroom |

**Exam tip:** "Friendly, costly, cowardly, lonely" end in **-ly** but are **adjectives**, not adverbs — a classic trap. ("He behaved cowardly." ❌ → "in a cowardly manner" ✔)
