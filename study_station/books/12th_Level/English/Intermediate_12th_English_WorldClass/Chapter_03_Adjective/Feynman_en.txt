🧒 FEYNMAN TECHNIQUE — Adjectives Explained Like You're 12

**The Hardest Concept: Order of Adjectives (Why "a beautiful small old round red Italian wooden table" is correct but "a wooden red Italian table" sounds wrong)**

Imagine you are building a burger. 🍔

You cannot put the bun in the middle and the patty on top — the burger falls apart. There is a fixed order: bottom bun → patty → cheese → salad → top bun.

Adjectives work exactly like a burger. When you use many adjectives before one noun, English has a **fixed order**, and if you break it, the sentence "falls apart" — it sounds wrong to every English speaker, and examiners love testing this.

**The Burger Order of Adjectives (OSASCOMP):**

1. **O**pinion — beautiful, ugly, nice (what you *feel*)
2. **S**ize — big, small, tiny
3. **A**ge — old, new, ancient
4. **S**hape — round, square, flat
5. **C**olour — red, black, golden
6. **O**rigin — Indian, Chinese, American
7. **M**aterial — wooden, plastic, gold
8. **P**urpose — walking (stick), sleeping (bag)

**Daily-life example:** You buy a phone cover. You say: *"a beautiful small new red plastic cover"* — opinion → size → age → colour → material. Perfect burger! 🍔

Now try: *"a plastic red new cover"* — the cheese is under the bun. Wrong!

**The Unforgettable Trick — "OSASCOMP = Oh! Sam Ate Six Chapatis On Monday, Please!"**

- **O**h = Opinion
- **S**am = Size
- **A**te = Age
- **S**ix = Shape
- **C**hapatis = Colour
- **O**n = Origin
- **M**onday = Material
- **P**lease = Purpose

**One more secret:** In exams you rarely get all 8. Usually 2–3 adjectives appear. Just check: *Opinion always comes first, Material/Purpose always comes last (stuck to the noun).* Example: "a lovely **silk** saree" ✓ (opinion + material), never "a silk lovely saree" ✗.

---

**⚠️ The Second Trap a 12-Year-Old Must Know: Adjective vs. Adverb**

Your friend says: *"She sings beautiful."* Sounds okay in Hindi-English ("वह सुंदर गाती है"), but English asks: **are you describing the girl (noun) or the singing (verb)?**

- Describing a **noun** → adjective: *She is **beautiful**.*
- Describing a **verb** → adverb: *She sings **beautifully**.*

**Trick:** Ask "कैसी/कैसा?" (how is the thing?) → adjective. Ask "कैसे?" (how is the action done?) → adverb.
