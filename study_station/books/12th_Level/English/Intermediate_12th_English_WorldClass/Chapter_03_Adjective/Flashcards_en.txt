Card 1
Front: What is an adjective?
Back: A word that describes or modifies a noun or pronoun — it tells us about quality, quantity, number, or possession. Example: "She is a brave girl." (brave describes girl)

Card 2
Front: What is the correct order of adjectives before a noun? (Mnemonic)
Back: OSASCOMP — Opinion, Size, Age, Shape, Colour, Origin, Material, Purpose. Example: "a beautiful small old round red Italian wooden dining table." Remember: "Oh Sir, All Students Can Obtain Maximum Points."

Card 3
Front: Which is correct — "He is more superior to me" or "He is superior to me"?
Back: "He is superior to me." Superior, inferior, senior, junior, prior, and prefer already contain comparison, so "more/most" is never used with them. Also, they take "to," not "than."

Card 4
Front: Examiner's Trap: "I have a five-years-old son." — Spot the error.
Back: It should be "a five-year-old son." When a compound adjective comes before a noun, the unit word stays singular (year, not years) and is joined by hyphens.

Card 5
Front: What is the difference between "elder" and "older"?
Back: "Elder" is used only for family members and takes "to" (He is elder to me). "Older" is used for people and things in general and takes "than" (This building is older than that one).

Card 6
Front: When do we use "fewer" and when "less"?
Back: "Fewer" for countable nouns (fewer students), "less" for uncountable nouns (less water). Trap: "less people" is wrong — say "fewer people."

Card 7
Front: What are the three degrees of comparison of "good"?
Back: Good (positive) – Better (comparative) – Best (superlative). Similarly: bad–worse–worst; many/much–more–most; little–less–least.

Card 8
Front: Examiner's Trap: "She is the most unique girl in the class." — Spot the error.
Back: "Unique" is an absolute adjective — it cannot be compared. Drop "most": "She is a unique girl." Other absolute adjectives: perfect, complete, universal, supreme, extreme, dead, empty.

Card 9
Front: What is the difference between "later" and "latter"?
Back: "Later" refers to time (I will come later). "Latter" refers to the second of two things mentioned (Of tea and coffee, I prefer the latter).

Card 10
Front: Mnemonic: How to remember adjectives that take "to" instead of "than"?
Back: SISJPP — Superior, Inferior, Senior, Junior, Prior, Prefer. Trick: "SISter Jumps Perfectly Past" — all take "to": "He is senior to me," never "senior than me."

Card 11
Front: Which is correct — "the gold ring" or "the golden ring"?
Back: Both exist but differ: "gold ring" = made of gold (material); "golden" = colour or figurative (golden hair, golden opportunity). In exams, material nouns used as adjectives stay in noun form: a gold watch, a stone wall.

Card 12
Front: What is a possessive adjective? Give examples.
Back: Words showing ownership placed before a noun: my, our, your, his, her, its, their. Example: "This is my book." Trap: "its" (possessive) has no apostrophe; "it's" means "it is."

Card 13
Front: When do we use "each" and when "every"?
Back: "Each" for two or more, stressing individuals (Each of the two boys…). "Every" for more than two, stressing the group as a whole (Every student in the class…). Both take a singular verb.

Card 14
Front: What is the rule for using "the" with superlative degree?
Back: The superlative always takes "the" before it: "the tallest boy," "the most beautiful painting." Trap: "He is best player" is wrong — say "He is the best player."

Card 15
Front: What is the difference between "some" and "any"?
Back: "Some" is used in affirmative sentences (I have some money); "any" in negatives and questions (I don't have any money / Do you have any money?). Exception: "some" in offers/requests — "Would you like some tea?"

Card 16
Front: Examiner's Trap: "No sooner had he come than she left." vs "He is taller than me." — When is "than" used with adjectives?
Back: "Than" follows the comparative degree (taller than, better than). But superior/inferior/senior/junior/prefer take "to." Also, "no sooner" pairs with "than" — a fixed correlative, not an adjective rule.

Card 17
Front: What are demonstrative adjectives?
Back: This, that, these, those — when placed before a noun: "This pen is mine." If used alone ("This is mine"), they are demonstrative pronouns, not adjectives.

Card 18
Front: Which is correct — "He is comparatively better" or "He is better"?
Back: "He is better." Double comparatives are wrong: never use "more better," "most best," or "comparatively better." One comparative marker is enough.

Card 19
Front: What is the difference between "farther" and "further"?
Back: "Farther" = physical distance (Delhi is farther than Agra). "Further" = additional/more (further information, further discussion).

Card 20
Front: Mnemonic: How to remember absolute (non-gradable) adjectives?
Back: PUCES — Perfect, Unique, Complete, Empty, Supreme. Trick: "PUCES never compare" — never say "most perfect" or "more unique"; these qualities are total, so no degree is possible.
