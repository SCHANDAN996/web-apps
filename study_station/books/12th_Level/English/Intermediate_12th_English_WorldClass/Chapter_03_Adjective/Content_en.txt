# ADJECTIVE — The Word That Paints Pictures 🎨

## Why This Chapter Is Important for Exams

Imagine two sentences: *"The dog barked."* and *"The ferocious, black dog barked loudly at the stranger."* The second sentence creates a movie in your mind. That magic comes from **adjectives** — and examiners at SSC CHSL, RRB NTPC, and Banking exams know that most students confuse adjectives with adverbs, misplace them in comparisons, and fall for "sound-right" traps. Master this chapter and you convert their traps into your marks.

## 🎯 Learning Objectives

By the end of this chapter you will be able to:
1. Identify all types of adjectives in any sentence.
2. Apply the correct order of adjectives (OSASCOMP).
3. Use degrees of comparison without errors.
4. Spot the classic adjective-vs-adverb traps examiners love.
5. Solve error-spotting and fill-in-the-blank questions quickly and accurately.

## 1. What Is an Adjective?

An **adjective** is a word that describes or modifies a **noun or pronoun**. It answers: *What kind? How many? Which one? How much?*

- She is a **brave** girl. (What kind?)
- He has **five** pens. (How many?)
- **This** book is mine. (Which one?)

🧠 **Active Recall:** In the sentence *"The tall man bought three ripe mangoes,"* find all three adjectives before reading further. *(Answer: tall, three, ripe)*

---

## 2. Types of Adjectives

| Type | Function | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Quality (Descriptive)** | What kind? | honest, beautiful, large |
| **Quantity** | How much? (uncountable) | some, much, little, whole |
| **Number (Numeral)** | How many? / order | five, first, many, few |
| **Demonstrative** | Points out | this, that, these, those |
| **Distributive** | Each one separately | each, every, either, neither |
| **Interrogative** | Asks about noun | what, which, whose |
| **Possessive** | Shows ownership | my, your, his, her, their |
| **Proper** | From proper nouns | Indian, French, Shakespearean |
| **Emphasizing** | Adds emphasis | own, very (the very man) |

⚠️ **Note:** *Each, every, either, neither* are followed by a **singular noun and singular verb**: *Each boy **has** a pen.*

🧠 **Active Recall:** What type is "every" in *"Every student passed"*? *(Distributive)* What type is "whose" in *"Whose bag is this?"* *(Interrogative)*

## 3. Order of Adjectives — OSASCOMP

When several adjectives come before one noun, they follow a fixed order:

**O**pinion – **S**ize – **A**ge – **S**hape – **C**olour – **O**rigin – **M**aterial – **P**urpose + Noun

- a **beautiful small old round black Indian wooden** table ✅
- ~~a wooden black Indian table~~ ❌ (wrong order)

🧠 **Mnemonic:** **"OSASCOMP — Oh Sir, A Silly Cat Ordered My Pizza!"**

## 4. Degrees of Comparison

| Degree | Use | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Positive** | Describes one | Ram is **tall**. |
| **Comparative** | Compares two (+ *than*) | Ram is **taller than** Shyam. |
| **Superlative** | Compares 3+ (*the* + est/most) | Ram is **the tallest** boy in class. |

**Key rules:**
- Comparative is used for **two**; superlative for **more than two**.
- Use **"the"** before superlative: *the best, the most honest*.
- Some adjectives take **"to"**, not "than": *senior to, junior to, superior to, inferior to, prefer…to*. → *He is senior **to** me* (never "senior than me").
- Latin comparatives have no superlative degree and no "than".
- Double comparatives are wrong: ~~more better~~ ❌ → *better* ✅

🧠 **Active Recall:** Correct this: *"She is more superior to her colleagues."* *(Answer: She is superior to her colleagues.)*

## 5. Adjective vs. Adverb — The Examiner's Favourite Trap

- Adjectives modify **nouns**; adverbs modify **verbs, adjectives, other adverbs**.
- After **linking verbs** (be, seem, look, feel, smell, taste, sound), use an **adjective**, not an adverb:
  - *The soup tastes **good**.* ✅ (not "well")
  - *She feels **bad** about it.* ✅
- But: *She sings **well**.* ✅ (sings = action verb → adverb)

---

## ⚠️ EXAMINER'S TRAP BOX — 12 Golden Rules

1. **Senior/junior/superior/inferior/prefer → "to"**, never "than".
2. After linking verbs (look, feel, taste, smell, seem) → **adjective**: *He looks happy*, not "happily".
3. **No double comparative**: ~~more faster~~ ❌.
4. **"The + adjective" = a class of people**: *the rich, the poor, the blind* (plural verb: *The poor **are** suffering*).
5. **"Few" vs "a few" vs "the few"**: few = almost none (negative); a few = some (positive); the few = all that exist.
6. **"Little / a little / the little"** — same logic for uncountable nouns.
7. **Each/every/either/neither + singular verb.**
8. Adjectives like *unique, perfect, universal, extreme* have **no degrees** — ~~most unique~~ ❌.
9. **"One of the + superlative + plural noun"**: *One of the best **players**.*
10. Comparative of **two things only**: *Of the two sisters, she is the **taller**.* (comparative with "the" when choosing between two!)
11. **"Enough"** comes **after** the adjective: *tall enough*, but **before** a noun: *enough money*.
12. Order matters: ~~a red big ball~~ ❌ → *a big red ball* ✅ (OSASCOMP).

## 🔁 Linguistic Bridge — Hindi vs English

| Hindi speaker says ❌ | Correct English ✅ | Why |
|---|---|---|
| "He is more taller than me." | He is taller than me. | No double comparative |
| "She is senior than me." | She is senior **to** me. | Latin comparatives take "to" |
| "The soup is tasting well." | The soup tastes good. | Linking verb + adjective |
| "I have less friends." | I have **fewer** friends. | fewer = countable; less = uncountable |
| "He is my most unique friend." | He is a unique friend. | "Unique" has no degrees |
