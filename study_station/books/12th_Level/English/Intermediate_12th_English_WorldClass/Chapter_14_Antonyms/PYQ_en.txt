# 📊 PYQ Analysis — Antonyms

## How Exams Test Antonyms

Antonyms are one of the most **predictable** vocabulary question types across SSC (CGL, CHSL, MTS, GD), RRB (NTPC, Group D), IBPS, and State exams. Instead of chasing year-wise counts, understand the **pattern** — because the pattern repeats even when the words change.

### What the pattern looks like

- **Format:** A word is given in CAPITALS or bold; you must choose the option *most nearly opposite* in meaning.
- **Where they appear:** SSC CGL/CHSL Tier-I vocabulary block, RRB NTPC General Awareness/English sections, IBPS PO/Clerk English Language section, State PCS prelims.
- **Difficulty mix:** Roughly half the words are everyday (abundant, ancient, expand); the rest are mid-level (obscure, fragile, reluctant) with an occasional tough one (obstreperous, ephemeral).

### Sub-topics that recur most often

1. **Prefix-based opposites** — words whose antonym is formed by *un-, in-, im-, dis-, mis-* (happy → unhappy, possible → impossible). Examiners love these because students confuse *which* prefix fits.
2. **Positive vs. negative trait words** — generous/stingy, brave/cowardly, humble/arrogant.
3. **Size/quantity words** — abundant/scarce, enormous/tiny, maximum/minimum.
4. **Time words** — ancient/modern, permanent/temporary, former/latter.
5. **Emotion/attitude words** — hostile/friendly, reluctant/willing, gloomy/cheerful.

### The Examiner's Four Traps

| Trap | How it works | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Synonym-in-disguise** | One option is a *synonym* of the given word, planted to catch students who misread "opposite" | Word: HOSTILE → trap option "unfriendly" (synonym), correct "friendly" |
| **Near-opposite vs. exact opposite** | Two options feel negative; only one is the true antonym | Word: ANCIENT → "old" (synonym trap), "medieval" (near trap), correct "modern" |
| **Sound-right confusion** | Options that *sound* related to the word but aren't opposites | Word: EXPAND → "expend" (sound-alike trap), correct "contract" |
| **Over-thinking** | The obvious simple antonym IS correct; students reject it as "too easy" | Word: BEGIN → correct "end" (students pick "pause" or "delay") |

## Top 10 High-Yield PYQ-Style Questions

**Q1.** Choose the word most nearly OPPOSITE in meaning: **ABUNDANT**
(a) Plentiful (b) Scarce (c) Ample (d) Copious
**Answer:** (b)
**Solution:** (a), (c), (d) are all synonyms of abundant — a classic *Synonym-in-disguise* trap where three options point the wrong way. The opposite of "plenty" is "scarce."
**Trap type:** Synonym-in-disguise | **Source:** PYQ-style (SSC pattern)

**Q2.** Choose the word most nearly OPPOSITE in meaning: **ANCIENT**
(a) Old (b) Antique (c) Modern (d) Medieval
**Answer:** (c)
**Solution:** "Old" and "antique" are synonyms; "medieval" is a near-trap (still in the past). Only "modern" is the true opposite.
**Trap type:** Near-opposite vs. exact opposite | **Source:** NCERT Class 9–10 vocabulary

**Q3.** Choose the word most nearly OPPOSITE in meaning: **EXPAND**
(a) Expend (b) Extend (c) Contract (d) Enlarge
**Answer:** (c)
**Solution:** "Expend" is a sound-alike trap meaning "to spend." "Extend" and "enlarge" are synonyms. "Contract" (to shrink) is the true opposite.
**Trap type:** Sound-right confusion | **Source:** PYQ-style (SSC CGL pattern)

**Q4.** Choose the word most nearly OPPOSITE in meaning: **HUMBLE**
(a) Modest (b) Arrogant (c) Meek (d) Shy
**Answer:** (b)
**Solution:** "Modest" and "meek" are synonyms of humble. "Shy" is a near-trap (a related but different trait). The opposite of humility is arrogance.
**Trap type:** Synonym-in-disguise | **Source:** PYQ-style (RRB NTPC pattern)

**Q5.** Choose the word most nearly OPPOSITE in meaning: **PERMANENT**
(a) Stable (b) Lasting (c) Temporary (d) Durable
**Answer:** (c)
**Solution:** Three synonym traps (stable, lasting, durable) surround the single opposite. Time words are a favourite exam sub-topic.
**Trap type:** Synonym-in-disguise | **Source:** PYQ-style (IBPS pattern)

**Q6.** Choose the word most nearly OPPOSITE in meaning: **RELUCTANT**
(a) Unwilling (b) Hesitant (c) Willing (d) Resistant
**Answer:** (c)
**Solution:** "Unwilling" and "hesitant" are synonyms; "resistant" is a near-trap. The clean opposite is "willing."
**Trap type:** Near-opposite vs. exact opposite | **Source:** PYQ-style (SSC CHSL pattern)

**Q7.** Choose the word most nearly OPPOSITE in meaning: **TRANSPARENT**
(a) Clear (b) Opaque (c) Obvious (d) Visible
**Answer:** (b)
**Solution:** "Clear," "obvious," and "visible" all echo the meaning of transparent. "Opaque" (not allowing light through) is the precise opposite — a favourite in science-flavoured vocabulary questions.
**Trap type:** Synonym-in-disguise | **Source:** NCERT Class 10 vocabulary

**Q8.** Choose the word most nearly OPPOSITE in meaning: **GUILTY**
(a) Criminal (b) Innocent (c) Accused (d) Suspect
**Answer:** (b)
**Solution:** "Criminal," "accused," and "suspect" all belong to the same legal field — a *field-association* trap. Only "innocent" is the antonym.
**Trap type:** Sound-right / field association | **Source:** PYQ-style (State PCS pattern)

**Q9.** Choose the word most nearly OPPOSITE in meaning: **VICTORY**
(a) Triumph (b) Defeat (c) Success (d) Conquest
**Answer:** (b)
**Solution:** A classic over-thinking question: three synonyms (triumph, success, conquest) and one obvious opposite. Don't reject "defeat" for being too simple.
**Trap type:** Over-thinking | **Source:** PYQ-style (SSC GD pattern)

**Q10.** Choose the word most nearly OPPOSITE in meaning: **ARTIFICIAL**
(a) Synthetic (b) Fake (c) Natural (d) Man-made
**Answer:** (c)
**Solution:** "Synthetic," "fake," and "man-made" are all synonyms. The opposite of man-made is "natural."
**Trap type:** Synonym-in-disguise | **Source:** PYQ-style (SSC CGL pattern)

## Applying the 15/45-Second Rule

**15-second question (Fact-based):** Q9 (VICTORY → Defeat). You know the word, you spot three synonyms instantly, and the opposite is a basic word. Read, eliminate, mark — **15 seconds, no deliberation.**

**45-second question (Inference-based):** Q3 (EXPAND → Contract). Here "expend" tempts you by sound, and "contract" has multiple meanings (a legal agreement vs. to shrink). You must pause, recall the *verb* sense of contract, and confirm it means "to become smaller." Budget the full **45 seconds** — rushing here costs a mark.

**Rule of thumb:** If all four options are familiar everyday words → 15 seconds. If any option is a sound-alike, a multi-meaning word, or an unfamiliar term → give it 45 seconds and verify the *exact* opposite, not just a negative-sounding word.
