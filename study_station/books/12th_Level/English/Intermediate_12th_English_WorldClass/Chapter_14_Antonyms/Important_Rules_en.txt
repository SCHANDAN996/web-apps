# 📋 Important Rules & Tips — Antonyms

## (a) 12 High-Yield Rules

| # | Rule | Example | Examiner's Trap |
|---|------|---------|-----------------|
| 1 | An antonym must be of the **same part of speech** as the given word. A noun's opposite is a noun, an adjective's opposite is an adjective. | "Bravery" (noun) → Cowardice, NOT "cowardly" (adjective) | Options mix parts of speech: "cowardly" looks right but is grammatically the wrong form. |
| 2 | **Prefix-based opposites** (un-, in-, im-, il-, ir-, dis-, non-) are the most tested pattern. Learn which prefix pairs with which root. | Possible → Impossible (not "unpossible"); Literate → Illiterate | "Unlegal" or "inregular" type wrong combinations appear as distractors. |
| 3 | **Match the intensity/degree.** The antonym must be equally strong, not milder or stronger. | "Furious" → Calm (not merely "unhappy"); "Tiny" → Huge | "Furious → Sad" is a trap — sadness is not the opposite of anger. |
| 4 | **Beware of near-synonyms planted as options.** In antonym questions, one option is often a synonym of the given word. | Word: "Abundant" — options include "Plentiful" (synonym trap); correct: "Scarce" | Under time pressure, students pick the synonym because it "matches" the word. |
| 5 | **Context fixes meaning.** A polysemous word's antonym depends on the sense used in the exam. | "Light" → Dark (brightness) OR Heavy (weight) | Both "Dark" and "Heavy" may appear; choose by the sense the question implies. |
| 6 | **Positive ↔ Negative connotation** pairs are frequently tested with abstract nouns. | Optimism → Pessimism; Virtue → Vice | Options like "realism" sit between the two and tempt over-thinkers. |
| 7 | **Gradable vs absolute adjectives:** absolute words (dead, perfect, unique) have strict opposites; gradable ones have a scale. | Dead → Alive (absolute); Hot → Cold (gradable) | "Unique → Common" is accepted; "Unique → Ordinary" also appears — pick the most standard opposite. |
| 8 | **Verb antonyms** often reverse the action, not just the quality. | Expand → Contract; Advance → Retreat; Accept → Reject | "Advance → Stop" is a trap — stopping is not reversing. |
| 9 | **Foreign-root words** (Greek/Latin) usually have learned opposites, not everyday ones. | Transparent → Opaque; Ancient → Modern; Flexible → Rigid | Everyday guesses like "unclear" for "transparent" are planted as traps. |
| 10 | **Eliminate before you select.** Remove synonyms and unrelated words first; the antonym usually survives among the last two. | Word: "Hostile" — remove "aggressive" (synonym) and "violent" (related); "Friendly" survives as the antonym. | Hasty elimination removes the correct answer — check direction (opposite, not same) twice. |
| 11 | **"Sound-alike" confusion words** are classic traps — the option resembles the given word in sound/spelling. | "Flaunt" (show off) vs "Flout" (disobey); antonym of "Flout" → Obey | Students answer for the wrong word entirely. Read the stem word letter by letter. |
| 12 | **When two options look correct, choose the most direct, standard opposite** — the one a dictionary lists first. | "Generous" → Stingy (standard) beats "selfish" (related but indirect) | Over-thinking leads students to the "clever" option; examiners reward the direct one. |

## (b) ⏱️ Time Management — The 15/45-Second Rule

| Type | What it looks like | Time | Strategy |
|------|-------------------|------|----------|
| **Fact-based (15 sec)** | Common one-word antonyms: direct vocabulary recall. E.g., "Antonym of VICTORY", "Antonym of EXPAND" | ≤ 15 seconds | Know it → mark it → move on. If the opposite doesn't come in 15 seconds, eliminate synonyms, take your best guess, and flag it. Never cross 30 seconds here. |
| **Inference-based (45 sec)** | Unfamiliar or tricky words where you must decode via roots/prefixes, match intensity, or choose between two close options. E.g., "Antonym of OPAQUE", "Antonym of LETHARGIC" | Up to 45 seconds | Break the word into root + affix → guess meaning → flip it → match with options. Use elimination of synonyms and wrong parts of speech. |

**Golden rule:** Antonyms are the fastest-scoring vocabulary questions. Bank the 15-second ones first; spend saved time only on root-decoding questions.

## (c) 🔬 Morphology — Root + Affix = Antonym Power

| Root / Affix | Meaning | Word | Antonym |
|---|---|---|---|
| **bene-** (Latin) | good | Benefit, Benevolent | Malevolent, Harm |
| **mal-** (Latin) | bad | Malice, Malfunction | Benevolence (opposite of malice); Proper working (opposite of malfunction) |
| **chron-** (Greek) | time | Chronic (long-lasting) | Acute (short, sudden) |
| **phil-** (Greek) | love | Philanthropy | Misanthropy |
| **anthrop-** (Greek) | human | Philanthropist | Misanthropist |
| **cred-** (Latin) | believe | Credible | Incredible |
| **dict-** (Latin) | say | Benediction (a blessing, good saying) | Malediction (a curse, bad saying) |
| **port-** (Latin) | carry | Export (carry out) | Import (carry in) |
| **un- / in- / im- / il- / ir-** | not | Happy → Unhappy; Possible → Impossible; Legal → Illegal; Regular → Irregular | (removing the prefix gives the antonym) |
| **dis-** | not, opposite of | Agree → Disagree; Honest → Dishonest | — |
| **-ful / -less** | full of / without | Hopeful ↔ Hopeless; Careful ↔ Careless | — |
| **anti-** | against | Antibody, Antisocial | Pro- (in favour) |

**Memory hook:** *"Bene is good, Mal is bad — Benevolent uncle, Malicious lad."*
