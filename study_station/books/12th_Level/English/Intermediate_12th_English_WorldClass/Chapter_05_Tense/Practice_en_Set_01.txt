Questions 1–20: Easy | Questions 21–25: Medium

1. She ______ to school every day.
(a) go (b) goes (c) going (d) gone
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Every day" signals a habitual action, so use Simple Present. With third person singular "she", the verb takes -s: "goes".
Source: NCERT Class 9 English Grammar

2. They ______ football in the park yesterday.
(a) play (b) played (c) plays (d) will play
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Yesterday" indicates Simple Past, so the past form "played" is correct.
Source: NCERT Class 9 English Grammar

3. I ______ my homework now.
(a) am doing (b) did (c) do (d) have done
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Now" shows an action in progress, so Present Continuous "am doing" is needed.
Source: NCERT Class 9 English Grammar

4. He ______ Delhi last year.
(a) visited (b) visits (c) has visited (d) is visiting
Answer: (a)
Solution: A finished time expression "last year" takes Simple Past — "visited". Present Perfect is wrong with a definite past time.

24. Spot the error: Part P: I have seen / Part Q: him / Part R: yesterday / Part S: in the market.
(a) Part R (b) Part Q (c) Part P (d) No error
Answer: (c)
Solution: Part P is wrong. With a definite past time like "yesterday", Simple Past must be used: "I saw him yesterday". This is a classic examiner trap — Present Perfect never goes with finished time words.
Source: PYQ-style

25. Spot the error: Part P: She is / Part Q: working here / Part R: since 2018 / Part S: without any leave.
(a) Part Q (b) Part P (c) Part R (d) No error
Answer: (b)
Solution: Part P is wrong. "Since 2018" demands Present Perfect (Continuous): "She has been working here since 2018". Examiners often pair "since/for" with a continuous tense to trap candidates.
