## 1. What Is a Tense, Really?

A tense is the **form of the verb** that tells us **when** an action happens and **whether it is complete or ongoing**. English has **3 times** (Present, Past, Future) and **4 aspects** (Indefinite/Simple, Continuous, Perfect, Perfect Continuous) — giving **12 tense forms** in total.

Think of it as a camera:
- **Simple** = a snapshot (the action as a fact).
- **Continuous** = a video playing (action in progress).
- **Perfect** = a finished video with the result in hand.
- **Perfect Continuous** = a video that has been playing for a long time.

## 2. The 12 Tense Forms at a Glance

| Time | Simple | Continuous | Perfect | Perfect Continuous |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Present** | He writes | He is writing | He has written | He has been writing |
| **Past** | He wrote | He was writing | He had written | He had been writing |
| **Future** | He will write | He will be writing | He will have written | He will have been writing |

**Signal words are your best friends:**
- *yesterday, ago, last year* → Past Simple
- *since, for, already, just, yet* → Perfect tenses
- *now, at this moment, look!, listen!* → Present Continuous
- *tomorrow, next week* → Future forms

## 3. The 12 Golden Rules of Tense ⭐

1. **Universal truths stay in Present Simple** — even inside a past sentence. *The teacher said that the earth **revolves** around the sun.* (NOT "revolved")
2. **"Since" + point of time, "for" + period of time** — both take Perfect/Perfect Continuous. *He has been working here **since** 2019 / **for** five years.*
3. **Past Simple never sits with "since/for"** — *❌ I lived here since 2015. ✅ I have lived here since 2015.*
4. **Two past actions: earlier action = Past Perfect, later = Past Simple** (joined by *before/after/when*). *The train **had left** before I **reached** the station.*
5. **"Hardly/Scarcely…when" and "No sooner…than"** take Past Perfect first. *No sooner **had** she **entered** than the phone rang.*
6. **Stative verbs avoid Continuous** — know, believe, love, hate, own, seem, understand. *❌ I am knowing him. ✅ I know him.*
7. **Present Perfect never takes a definite past time** — *❌ I have met him yesterday. ✅ I met him yesterday.*
8. **"It is time / wish / if only" + Past Simple** (for present unreal). *It is time you **went** home.*
9. **Future is not used in time/condition clauses** — use Present Simple after *when, if, unless, until, as soon as*. *When he **comes**, we will start.* (NOT "will come")
10. **"By + future time" → Future Perfect.** *She **will have finished** the work by 6 pm.*
11. **Past Continuous + Past Simple** = an ongoing action interrupted by a shorter one. *While I **was walking**, it **started** raining.*
12. **"This is the first/second time…" → Present Perfect.** *This is the first time I **have seen** the Taj Mahal.*

## ⚠️ Examiner's Trap Box

**Trap 1 — The "since/for" bait:** The sentence looks fine in Past Simple, but "since 2010" silently demands Perfect. *❌ He works here since 2010. ✅ He has worked here since 2010.*

**Trap 2 — The universal-truth switch:** Examiners hide a fact inside a reported sentence to tempt you into past tense. *❌ He said that the sun rose in the east. ✅ He said that the sun rises in the east.* A universal truth stays in Present Simple even after a past reporting verb.

**Trap 3 — The stative-verb disguise:** "I am understanding the lesson" *sounds* modern and natural — it is still wrong in standard exam English. *✅ I understand the lesson.*

## 🔁 Linguistic Bridge — Hindi से English

| Hindi में हम सोचते हैं | गलत English | सही English |
|---|---|---|
| मैं उसे जानता हूँ (जान रहा हूँ) | I am knowing him | I know him |
| मैं 2015 से यहाँ रहता हूँ | I live here since 2015 | I have lived here since 2015 |
| वह कल स्कूल गया है | He has gone to school yesterday | He went to school yesterday |
| जब वह आएगा, हम खाएँगे | When he will come, we will eat | When he comes, we will eat |
| मैंने अभी-अभी खाना खाया | I just ate my meal (in exam context) | I have just eaten my meal |

**Why does this happen?** Hindi uses one verb form where English splits time and aspect into two. Train yourself to ask: *"Is the action finished? Is the time definite?"* before choosing the tense.

## 🧩 Mnemonic — Remember the 4 Aspects

**"S-C-P-PC: Students Can Pass Perfectly Confident"**
- **S**imple → fact
- **C**ontinuous → in progress
- **P**erfect → completed
- **P**erfect **C**ontinuous → long duration, still on
