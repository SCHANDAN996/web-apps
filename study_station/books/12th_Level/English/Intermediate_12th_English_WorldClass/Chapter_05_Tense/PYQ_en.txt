# 📊 PYQ Analysis — Tense (Pattern Analysis of the Last 10 Years)

## How Exams Test Tense

Across SSC (CGL, CHSL, MTS, GD), RRB NTPC, IBPS and State exams, tense questions appear in **four recurring formats**:

1. **Spot the Error** — one part of the sentence has a tense mistake (most common).
2. **Fill in the Blank** — choose the correct verb form.
3. **Sentence Improvement** — replace the underlined verb phrase.
4. **Cloze Test** — a passage where 2–3 blanks test tense consistency.

## Which Sub-topics Repeat Most Often (in words, not numbers)

- **Since vs. For with Perfect/Perfect Continuous** — the single most repeated trap. "I am living here since 2015" ❌ appears in some form almost every year.
- **Past Perfect in two-past-action sentences** — "The train had left before I reached."
- **Time clauses (when, as soon as, until, before) take Present Simple, not Future** — "I will call you when I will reach" ❌.
- **Universal truths stay in Present Simple** even after past reporting verbs — "The teacher said that the earth revolves around the sun."
- **Conditional sentences (If-clauses)** — Type 2 ("If I were…") and Type 3 ("If I had known…") confusion.
- **Past Continuous vs. Simple Past** — "While I was walking, I met him" (not "while I walked, I was meeting").
- **Present Perfect vs. Simple Past with finished-time words** — "I have seen him yesterday" ❌.

## Examiner's Trap Types Used in Tense Questions

| Trap Type | What it looks like |
|---|---|
| **Sound-Right** | "I am knowing him for years" — sounds natural in Hindi-English, but stative verbs reject continuous form. |
| **Intervening Phrase** | A long time phrase between the verb and its real time marker hides the tense clash: "He *has been working* in this office, which is near the station, *since* 2018." (Correct — but swap "since" for "ago" and many miss it.) |
| **Over-thinking** | A perfectly correct Present Perfect sentence is "improved" into Past Perfect by candidates who assume the harder option is right. |
| **Sequence Confusion** | Two past events given without "before/after" — candidate must supply Past Perfect for the earlier action. |

## Top 10 High-Yield Questions (with Solutions)

**Q1.** I ______ in this city since 2010.
(a) am living (b) have been living (c) was living (d) had lived
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** "Since + point of time" demands Present Perfect (Continuous) for an action continuing till now. (a) is the classic Sound-Right trap.
**Trap type:** Sound-Right | **Source:** PYQ-style

**Q2.** The train ______ before we reached the station.
(a) left (b) has left (c) had left (d) was leaving
**Answer: (c)**
**Solution:** Two past actions — the earlier one (train leaving) takes Past Perfect; the later one takes Simple Past.
**Trap type:** Sequence Confusion | **Source:** PYQ-style

**Q3.** I will inform you as soon as he ______.
(a) will arrive (b) arrives (c) arrived (d) has arrived
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** After time conjunctions (as soon as, when, until, before), use Present Simple for future meaning — never "will" in the time clause.
**Trap type:** Sound-Right | **Source:** PYQ-style

**Q4.** She said that the sun ______ in the east.
(a) rose (b) rises (c) had risen (d) was rising
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** Universal truths remain in Present Simple even inside past-tense reported speech.
**Trap type:** Over-thinking (candidates pick (a) to "match" *said*) | **Source:** NCERT Class 9 English Grammar

**Q5.** Spot the error: "I have seen (a)/ that movie (b)/ last Friday. (c)/ No error (d)"
**Answer: (c)**
**Solution:** A finished time expression ("last Friday") forces Simple Past: "I saw that movie last Friday." Present Perfect cannot sit with definite past time.
**Trap type:** Sound-Right | **Source:** PYQ-style

**Q6.** If I ______ a bird, I would fly to you.
(a) am (b) was (c) were (d) had been
**Answer: (c)**
**Solution:** Type-2 conditional (imaginary present) uses Past Simple; with "be", **were** is used for all persons.
**Trap type:** Sound-Right ("was" tempts Hindi speakers) | **Source:** PYQ-style

**Q7.** While I ______ down the road, I met an old friend.
(a) walked (b) was walking (c) had walked (d) am walking
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** A longer background action interrupted by a shorter one: Past Continuous + Simple Past.
**Trap type:** Sequence Confusion | **Source:** PYQ-style

**Q8.** He ______ his homework before his father returned.
(a) finished (b) has finished (c) had finished (d) finishes
**Answer: (c)**
**Solution:** "Before" signals the earlier of two past actions → Past Perfect.
**Trap type:** Sequence Confusion | **Source:** PYQ-style

**Q9.** Spot the error: "I am knowing (a)/ the answer (b)/ to this question. (c)/ No error (d)"
**Answer: (a)**
**Solution:** "Know" is a stative verb — it never takes the continuous form. Correct: "I know the answer."
**Trap type:** Sound-Right | **Source:** NCERT Class 10 English Grammar

**Q10.** By next December, we ______ this project.
(a) will complete (b) will have completed (c) have completed (d) had completed
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** "By + future time" = Future Perfect — action completed before a future deadline.
**Trap type:** Over-thinking | **Source:** PYQ-style

## Applying the 15/45-Second Rule

**15-second question (Fact-based):** Q1 above. The moment you see **"since 2010"**, the rule fires automatically — Present Perfect Continuous. No need to read options twice. Target: under 15 seconds.

**45-second question (Inference-based):** Q5 above. Here you must (1) locate the time marker "last Friday", (2) recall that Present Perfect forbids definite past time, (3) check the other parts for decoy errors. Budget up to 45 seconds — and if the time marker doesn't reveal itself in one scan, mark and move on.

## Key Takeaway

Tense questions are won by **time-marker spotting**, not by translating the sentence into Hindi. Train yourself to underline *since, for, ago, before, after, when, by, already, yesterday* the instant you read the sentence — the marker chooses the tense for you.
