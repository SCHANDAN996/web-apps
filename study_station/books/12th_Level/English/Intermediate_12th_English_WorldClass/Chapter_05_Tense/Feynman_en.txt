🧒 FEYNMAN TECHNIQUE — TENSE EXPLAINED TO A 12-YEAR-OLD

The Hardest Concept: Present Perfect vs. Simple Past

Imagine your friend asks you: "Did you eat lunch?"

Now, here's the secret most students never learn: English cares about one thing — **is the time finished or still open?**

Think of time like a door. 🚪

- **Simple Past = the door is CLOSED.** The time is over, finished, dead. "I ate lunch **at 1 o'clock**." One o'clock is gone. That door is shut forever. So we say: *I **ate** lunch at 1 o'clock.*

- **Present Perfect = the door is still OPEN.** The time period is still alive. "I **have eaten** lunch **today**." Today is not over yet — you're still living inside "today." The door is open. So we say: *I **have eaten** lunch today.*

That's it. That's the whole rule. Ask yourself one question: **"Is the time word finished or still running?"**

- yesterday, last year, in 2019, two days ago → finished → CLOSED door → Simple Past (V2)
- today, this week, since Monday, ever, never, just, already, yet → still running or no fixed time → OPEN door → Present Perfect (have/has + V3)

🎯 The Unforgettable Trick: **"Ago is a NO for Have."**
If you see "ago" (two days ago, long ago), the door is closed — never use "have/has" with it.
❌ I have met him two days ago.
✅ I met him two days ago.

One more daily-life picture: Your mother asks, "**Have you done** your homework?" — she uses Present Perfect because there's no fixed finished time; she just wants to know the result NOW. But if she says, "When **did** you finish it?" — she's pointing at a closed moment in the past, so Simple Past.

🧠 Quick Check (answer in your head):
1. I ___ (see) that movie yesterday.
2. I ___ (see) that movie three times!
(Answers: 1. saw — "yesterday" = closed door. 2. have seen — no fixed time, the count can still grow = open door.)
