101. By the time the train reached the station, the passengers ______ for over two hours.
(a) waited (b) are waiting (c) have been waiting (d) had been waiting
Answer: (d)
Solution: "By the time + past simple" signals an action continuing up to a past point, so past perfect continuous is needed. "Waited" ignores the duration phrase "for over two hours".
Source: NCERT Class 10 English Grammar

102. She ______ in this city since 2015, and she still loves it.
(a) has been living (b) lived (c) was living (d) had lived
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Since 2015" with an action continuing to the present takes present perfect continuous. Simple past "lived" would mean she no longer lives there.
Source: NCERT Class 9 English Grammar

103. Choose the correctly framed sentence.
(a) I am knowing the answer. (b) I have been knowing the answer. (c) I was knowing the answer. (d) I know the answer.
Answer: (d)
Solution: "Know" is a stative verb and is not used in continuous forms. Stative verbs of perception, possession and emotion stay in simple tenses.

104. Hardly ______ the station when the train left.
(a) I had reached (b) had I reached (c) I reached (d) did I reach
Answer: (b)
Solution: After "Hardly", inversion is required and the earlier action takes past perfect: "Hardly had I reached". Option (d) wrongly uses simple past with inversion.

105. Spot the error: Part P: He has been working / Part Q: in this factory / Part R: since five years. / Part S: No error
(a) Part P (b) Part Q (c) No error (d) Part R
Answer: (d)
Solution: Part R is wrong: "since" is used with a point of time, "for" with a period. It should be "for five years". This is a classic examiner trap.

106. When I met her last week, she told me that she ______ a new job.
(a) has got (b) will get (c) gets (d) had got
Answer: (d)
Solution: In reported speech with a past reporting verb, the earlier action shifts to past perfect. "Has got" breaks the sequence of tenses.

107. By 2030, scientists ______ a cure for this disease.
(a) will discover (b) discover (c) will be discovering (d) will have discovered
Answer: (d)
Solution: "By + future time" indicates completion before that point, so future perfect is correct. Simple future only says it will happen sometime, not by then.

108. Spot the error: Part P: I am living / Part Q: in Delhi / Part R: since 2018. / Part S: No error
(a) Part R (b) Part Q (c) Part P (d) No error
Answer: (c)
Solution: Part P is wrong: with "since 2018" the verb must be present perfect continuous — "I have been living". Present continuous cannot express duration from a past point.

109. The teacher said that the earth ______ round the sun.
(a) revolved (b) had revolved (c) revolves (d) was revolving
Answer: (c)
Solution: Universal truths remain in simple present even in reported speech. "Revolved" wrongly applies the backshift rule to a permanent fact — a favourite examiner trap.
Source: NCERT Class 10 English Grammar

110. He ______ his homework before his father returned.
(a) had finished (b) finished (c) has finished (d) was finishing
Answer: (a)
Solution: Of two past actions, the earlier one takes past perfect. "Finished" would suggest both happened together.

111. This time tomorrow, we ______ over the Atlantic.
(a) will fly (b) will have flown (c) will be flying (d) fly
Answer: (c)
Solution: "This time tomorrow" points to an action in progress at a future moment, so future continuous is needed. Future perfect would mean the flight is already over by then.

112. Spot the error: Part P: No sooner did she enter the room / Part Q: when the lights / Part R: went off. / Part S: No error
(a) Part P (b) Part R (c) Part Q (d) No error
Answer: (c)
Solution: Part Q is wrong: "No sooner" pairs with "than", not "when". "When" pairs with "Hardly/Scarcely". This pairing trap is very common in exams.

113. I ______ this book twice, so I can lend it to you.
(a) read (b) have read (c) had read (d) am reading
Answer: (b)
Solution: A completed action at an unspecified time with present relevance takes present perfect. Simple past would need a definite past time.

114. Choose the sentence with the correct tense.
(a) He is working here since 2010. (b) He has been working here since 2010. (c) He works here since 2010. (d) He was working here since 2010.
Answer: (b)
Solution: Duration from a past point continuing now requires present perfect continuous. All other options mismatch "since 2010".

115. By the end of this month, they ______ the construction of the bridge.
(a) will have completed (b) will complete (c) will be completing (d) have completed
Answer: (a)
Solution: "By the end of this month" marks a deadline, so future perfect shows completion before it. Simple future does not guarantee completion by the deadline.

116. Spot the error: Part P: When I reached the theatre, / Part Q: the film already started / Part R: and I missed the beginning. / Part S: No error
(a) Part Q (b) Part P (c) Part R (d) No error
Answer: (a)
Solution: Part Q is wrong: the film started before the reaching, so it needs past perfect — "the film had already started". Two past actions must be sequenced correctly.

117. She ______ English for five years before she moved to London.
(a) studied (b) had been studying (c) has studied (d) is studying
Answer: (b)
Solution: An action continuing up to a past event ("moved") takes past perfect continuous. Simple past would not convey the five-year duration.

118. Choose the correctly framed sentence.
(a) I am having a car. (b) I have a car. (c) I was having a car. (d) I am have a car.
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Have" meaning possession is stative and avoids continuous forms. "I am having a car" is a typical Hindi-speaker error.

119. He said that he ______ the letter the previous day.
(a) had posted (b) posted (c) has posted (d) posts
Answer: (a)
Solution: "The previous day" in reported speech pushes the action one step back into past perfect. Simple past would clash with the reporting verb "said".
Source: NCERT Class 10 English Grammar

120. Spot the error: Part P: I have seen / Part Q: that movie / Part R: last night. / Part S: No error
(a) Part Q (b) Part P (c) Part R (d) No error
Answer: (b)
Solution: Part P is wrong: present perfect cannot be used with a definite past time like "last night". It should be "I saw that movie last night" — a very frequent trap.

121. Unless it ______ raining, the match will continue.
(a) had stopped (b) will stop (c) stopped (d) stops
Answer: (d)
Solution: In conditional clauses with "unless/if/when", simple present replaces future tense. "Will stop" is the classic over-eager future trap.

122. The doctor ______ the patient before the nurse brought the reports.
(a) examined (b) has examined (c) had examined (d) examines
Answer: (c)
Solution: The examination happened before the reports arrived; the earlier of two past actions takes past perfect. Simple past would reverse the sequence.

123. Choose the sentence with correct tense usage.
(a) I wish I know the truth. (b) I wish I have known the truth. (c) I wish I knew the truth. (d) I wish I will know the truth.
Answer: (c)
Solution: After "I wish", an unreal present desire takes simple past ("knew"). Present tense after "wish" is a common error.

124. Spot the error: Part P: It is high time / Part Q: you start / Part R: preparing for the exam. / Part S: No error
(a) Part Q (b) Part P (c) Part R (d) No error
Answer: (a)
Solution: Part Q is wrong: after "It is high time", the verb takes simple past — "you started". Present tense here is a subtle examiner trap.

125. By next June, she ______ here for ten years.
(a) will have been working (b) will work (c) will be working (d) has worked
Answer: (a)
Solution: A future deadline plus a duration ("for ten years") calls for future perfect continuous. Future perfect simple would fit a completed event, not an ongoing activity.
