# 📋 Important Grammar Rules & Tips — Tense

## (a) 12 High-Yield Rules

| # | Rule | Example | Examiner's Trap |
|---|------|---------|-----------------|
| 1 | **Universal truths & habitual facts** take Simple Present, even after past-tense reporting verbs. | The teacher said that the earth **revolves** around the sun. | "The teacher said that the earth revolved…" — sounds consistent, but truth never changes tense. |
| 2 | **Since + point of time, For + period of time** — with Present Perfect / Perfect Continuous. | He has been working here **since** 2015 / **for** nine years. | "Since five years" ❌ — since never takes a duration. |
| 3 | **Present Perfect** is never used with a definite past time (yesterday, ago, last year, in 2019). | I **have finished** the work. / I **finished** the work yesterday. | "I have met him yesterday" ❌ — yesterday forces Simple Past. |
| 4 | In **"It is time / It is high time"** clauses, the verb takes Simple Past (though meaning is present). | It is high time you **went** home. | "It is high time you go home" ❌ — the past form here is idiomatic, not past in meaning. |
| 5 | **By + future time** → Future Perfect; **by + past time** → Past Perfect. | She will **have completed** it by Monday. / He **had left** by 5 pm. | "She will complete it by Monday" loses the deadline sense the examiner tests. |
| 6 | Two past actions: the **earlier** action takes Past Perfect, the later one Simple Past (often joined by before/after/when). | The train **had left** before I **reached** the station. | Both verbs in Simple Past destroys the sequence — the classic trap. |
| 7 | **Hardly/Scarcely…when** and **No sooner…than** take Past Perfect in the first clause. | No sooner **had** she **entered** than the phone rang. | "No sooner she entered than…" ❌ — inversion + had is compulsory. |
| 8 | **If-clause (conditional):** If + Simple Present → will + V1; If + Simple Past → would + V1; If + Past Perfect → would have + V3. | If it **rains**, we **will stay** home. / If I **were** rich, I **would help** you. | "If it will rain, we will stay" ❌ — will never enters the if-clause. |
| 9 | Verbs of perception/state — **know, believe, love, hate, own, seem, understand** — are not used in continuous forms. | I **know** the answer. (not *am knowing*) | "I am understanding the lesson" ❌ — stative verbs resist -ing. |
| 10 | **Wish / If only / Would that** for present regret take Past form; for past regret take Past Perfect. | I wish I **were** a bird. / I wish I **had studied** harder. | "I wish I was a bird" is informal speech; exams prefer *were*. |
| 11 | With **"as if / as though"**, use Past (or Past Perfect) for unreal comparison. | He talks as if he **were** a king. | "He talks as if he is a king" ❌ — the comparison is unreal, so shift the tense back. |
| 12 | Time clauses with **when, before, after, until, as soon as** take Simple Present for future reference — never "will". | Call me when you **reach** Delhi. | "Call me when you will reach" ❌ — the most repeated trap in SSC/Bank papers. |

## (b) Time Management — The 15/45-Second Rule

**⚡ Fact-based questions — target 15 seconds:**
- Spot-the-error items testing Rules 2, 3, 4, 9, 12 (since/for, definite past time, stative verbs, will-in-time-clause). These are pattern-recognition: see the trigger word (*since, yesterday, when, wish*) → apply the rule → mark.
- Fill-in-the-blank with clear time markers (*by 2026, before, no sooner*).

**🔍 Inference-based questions — allow up to 45 seconds:**
- Sentences with **two actions** where you must decide the sequence (Past Perfect vs Simple Past) — read the whole sentence twice.
- Conditionals mixing types, and "as if / it is high time" items where the *meaning* (real vs unreal) must be judged, not just the form.
- Improvement-of-sentence questions where two options look grammatically possible — check which preserves the intended time relationship.

**Rule of thumb:** if a trigger word solves it, don't re-read; if meaning decides it, don't rush.

## (c) Morphology — Roots & Affixes for Tense-Related Vocabulary

| Root / Affix | Meaning | Example Words |
|---|---|---|
| **chron** (Greek) | time | chronic, chronology, synchronize, anachronism |
| **tempor** (Latin) | time | temporary, contemporary, tempo, extempore |
| **ann / enn** (Latin) | year | annual, anniversary, perennial, biennial |
| **dur** (Latin) | last, hard | duration, durable, endure, during |
| **pre-** (prefix) | before | predict, precede, prevent |
| **post-** (prefix) | after | postpone, postscript, post-mortem |
| **re-** (prefix) | again | recur, repeat, renew |
| **-ed / -ing / -en** (suffixes) | tense/participle markers | played, playing, broken, eaten |

**Exam tip:** Words like *anachronism* (something out of its proper time) and *contemporary* (of the same time) appear in cloze tests and comprehension alongside tense questions — knowing the root lets you guess meaning even when the sentence is complex.
