Card 1
Front: What is the first step before choosing a word for a blank in an advanced fill-in-the-blank question?
Back: Read the whole sentence and identify the tone (positive/negative/neutral) and the connector (but, although, because, therefore). The connector tells you whether the blank needs a similar or opposite idea.

Card 2
Front: "Despite his wealth, he lived a ______ life." Which type of word fits — positive or negative?
Back: A word showing simplicity/modesty (e.g., "frugal", "austere"). "Despite" signals contrast, so the blank must oppose "wealth".

Card 3
Front: What is a "collocation" and why does it matter in fill-in-the-blanks?
Back: A collocation is a natural word pairing (e.g., "make a decision", not "do a decision"; "heavy rain", not "strong rain"). Examiner traps often use grammatically correct but unnatural pairings.

Card 4
Front: Mnemonic: How do you remember the difference between "affect" and "effect"?
Back: RAVEN — Remember: Affect = Verb, Effect = Noun. ("The medicine affected him" / "The effect was instant.")

Card 5
Front: Examiner Trap: "The committee ______ divided in their opinion." Is "is" or "are" correct?
Back: "Are" — when a collective noun's members act individually (divided opinion), use a plural verb. If the group acts as one unit, use singular ("The committee has decided").

Card 6
Front: What does the root "bene" mean, and give two derived words.
Back: "Bene" = good/well. Derived words: benefit, benevolent, beneficial.

Card 7
Front: What does the root "mal" mean, and give two derived words.
Back: "Mal" = bad/evil. Derived words: malicious, malnutrition, malfunction.

Card 8
Front: "Neither of the two boys ______ done his homework." Fill the blank: has or have?
Back: "Has" — "neither of" takes a singular verb. Trap: the plural noun "boys" right before the verb tempts you to pick "have".

Card 9
Front: What is the 15/45-second rule for fill-in-the-blank questions?
Back: Fact-based questions (fixed prepositions, collocations, simple grammar) should take ~15 seconds. Inference-based questions (tone, context, vocabulary-in-context) may take up to 45 seconds. Skip and return if a question exceeds its budget.

Card 10
Front: Which preposition follows these: "abstain ___", "accused ___", "comply ___"?
Back: Abstain from, accused of, comply with. Fixed prepositions are pure recall — answer in 15 seconds.

Card 11
Front: Mnemonic: How do you remember commonly confused pairs like "principal/principle"?
Back: "The principal is your PAL" (person = head of school); "principle" ends in -le like "rule" (a rule/standard).

Card 12
Front: Examiner Trap: "One of my friends ______ going to Delhi." Why is "is" correct and not "are"?
Back: The subject is "One" (singular); "of my friends" is only an intervening phrase. Examiners place a plural noun just before the verb to lure you into "are".

Card 13
Front: What does the root "dict" mean, and give two derived words.
Back: "Dict" = say/speak. Derived words: predict, dictate, verdict, contradict.

Card 14
Front: In a two-blank question, what is the fastest solving strategy?
Back: Solve one blank at a time and eliminate options. Usually the second blank is easier — fit it first, then check whether the first word of the surviving options also fits.

Card 15
Front: "The manager, along with his assistants, ______ arrived." Fill: has or have?
Back: "Has" — with "along with / as well as / together with", the verb agrees with the first subject ("the manager").

Card 16
Front: What does the root "port" mean, and give two derived words.
Back: "Port" = carry. Derived words: transport, export, portable, support.

Card 17
Front: How do you detect the required tone of a blank when the sentence has no connector?
Back: Look at the "emotion words" around the blank (e.g., "unfortunately", "remarkably", "sadly"). They reveal whether the missing word should be positive or negative.

Card 18
Front: "He is one of those students who ______ always punctual." Fill: is or are?
Back: "Are" — in "one of those + plural noun + who/that", the relative pronoun refers to the plural noun ("students"), so the verb is plural. Classic over-thinking trap.

Card 19
Front: What does the root "cred" mean, and give two derived words.
Back: "Cred" = believe. Derived words: credible, incredible, credit, credulous.

Card 20
Front: When should you skip a fill-in-the-blank question in the exam?
Back: Skip when (1) all four options are unfamiliar words, (2) you've spent over 45 seconds without eliminating even two options, or (3) two options seem equally correct — mark it, move on, and return only if time remains.
