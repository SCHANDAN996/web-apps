Practice Set 6 — Fill in the Blanks (Advanced)
Questions 126–150 | Medium: 126–135 | Hard: 136–150

126. The committee's report, ______ with statistics and case studies, was tabled in Parliament yesterday.
(a) complete (b) deplete (c) replete (d) delete
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Replete with" is a fixed phrase meaning "full of", which fits a report full of statistics. "Complete with" is tempting but means "having as an additional feature", not "full of", so the answer is replete.
Source: PYQ-style

127. Hardly ______ the station when the train left.
(a) I had reached (b) had I reached (c) I reached (d) did I reach
Answer: (b)
Solution: After "Hardly" at the start of a clause, inversion is required, and the past perfect is used for the earlier action. "Did I reach" is tempting because of inversion, but the tense must be past perfect, so the answer is had I reached.
Source: PYQ-style

128. The manager was ______ to criticism; he welcomed every suggestion from his team.
(a) open (b) immune (c) averse (d) indifferent
Answer: (a)
Solution: The second clause says he welcomed suggestions, so the blank needs a positive word. "Open to criticism" fits; "averse" and "indifferent" contradict the context, so the answer is open.
Source: PYQ-style

129. Neither the students nor the teacher ______ aware of the change in schedule.
(a) were (b) have been (c) are (d) was
Answer: (d)
Solution: With "neither…nor", the verb agrees with the nearer subject, "the teacher" (singular). "Were" is tempting because "students" appears first, but proximity rules the agreement, so the answer is was.
Source: PYQ-style

130. His argument was so ______ that even his opponents were convinced.
(a) fallacious (b) specious (c) tenuous (d) cogent
Answer: (d)
Solution: Opponents being convinced shows the argument was convincing; "cogent" means logical and persuasive. "Specious" is the classic trap — it means seemingly convincing but actually false, so the answer is cogent.
Source: PYQ-style

131. The company decided to ______ its losses by selling off the unprofitable units.
(a) aggravate (b) foment (c) incur (d) recoup
Answer: (d)
Solution: Selling units to recover money matches "recoup", meaning to regain losses. "Incur" is tempting but means to suffer fresh losses, the opposite direction, so the answer is recoup.
Source: PYQ-style

132. ______ the rain, the match continued uninterrupted.
(a) Despite of (b) In spite (c) Despite (d) Although
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Despite" takes a noun directly without "of"; "in spite" needs "of", and "although" needs a clause. "Despite of" is a common Hindi-speaker error, so the answer is Despite.
Source: PYQ-style

133. The two brothers divided the property ______ themselves.
(a) among (b) between (c) within (d) amongst
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Between" is used for two persons; "among/amongst" is for more than two. Since there are two brothers, the answer is between.
Source: PYQ-style

134. She has been working in this organisation ______ 2015.
(a) since (b) for (c) from (d) by
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Since" is used with a point of time (2015); "for" is used with a duration. "From" is tempting in Hindi-influenced English but is wrong with the present perfect continuous here, so the answer is since.
Source: PYQ-style

135. The judge ______ the accused of all charges due to lack of evidence.
(a) convicted (b) indicted (c) blamed (d) acquitted
Answer: (d)
Solution: Lack of evidence leads to freeing the accused; "acquit of" is the correct collocation. "Convicted" and "indicted" mean the opposite, so the answer is acquitted.
Source: PYQ-style

136. The politician's speech was full of ______ promises that he had no intention of keeping.
(a) hollow (b) sincere (c) profound (d) candid
Answer: (a)
Solution: Promises one does not intend to keep are empty; "hollow" fits. "Candid" is tempting as a speech adjective but means frank and honest, which contradicts the context, so the answer is hollow.
Source: PYQ-style

137. Scarcely had she stepped out of the house ______ it began to pour.
(a) than (b) that (c) then (d) when
Answer: (d)
Solution: "Scarcely/Hardly" pairs with "when"; "No sooner" pairs with "than". "Than" is the classic trap here because learners mix the two correlative pairs, so the answer is when.
Source: PYQ-style

138. The new policy is likely to ______ inflation rather than control it.
(a) alleviate (b) exacerbate (c) mitigate (d) ameliorate
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Rather than control it" signals contrast, so the blank needs a word meaning "worsen"; "exacerbate" fits. The other three options all mean "to lessen", so the answer is exacerbate.
Source: PYQ-style

139. Each of the boys ______ been given a certificate.
(a) have (b) are (c) has (d) were
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Each of" takes a singular verb. "Have" is tempting because "boys" is plural and nearest, but the true subject is "each", so the answer is has.
Source: PYQ-style

140. His ______ remarks at the funeral offended many of the mourners.
(a) sympathetic (b) tactful (c) insensitive (d) soothing
Answer: (c)
Solution: Remarks that offend mourners must be lacking in sensitivity; "insensitive" fits. The other options are positive and contradict "offended", so the answer is insensitive.
Source: PYQ-style

141. The evidence was so ______ that the jury took only an hour to reach a verdict.
(a) compelling (b) ambiguous (c) dubious (d) flimsy
Answer: (a)
Solution: A quick verdict shows the evidence was powerful and convincing; "compelling" fits. "Flimsy" is tempting but would delay or prevent a verdict, so the answer is compelling.
Source: PYQ-style

142. I would rather you ______ the truth yesterday.
(a) tell (b) had told (c) told (d) have told
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Would rather" with a past-time reference (yesterday) takes the past perfect to express an unreal past wish. "Told" is tempting but refers to present/future preference, so the answer is had told.
Source: PYQ-style

143. The minister tried to ______ the scandal, but the media exposed it within days.
(a) hush up (b) divulge (c) bring out (d) lay bare
Answer: (a)
Solution: "But the media exposed it" shows the minister wanted to conceal it; "hush up" means to suppress information. The other options mean "to reveal", so the answer is hush up.
Source: PYQ-style

144. The ancient manuscript was so ______ that only a few scholars could decipher it.
(a) legible (b) lucid (c) obscure (d) explicit
Answer: (c)
Solution: If only a few scholars could decipher it, the manuscript was unclear; "obscure" fits. "Legible" and "lucid" are tempting positives but contradict the context, so the answer is obscure.
Source: PYQ-style

145. No sooner had the bell rung ______ the students rushed out.
(a) when (b) then (c) than (d) that
Answer: (c)
Solution: "No sooner" always pairs with "than". "When" is the trap because it pairs with "hardly/scarcely", so the answer is than.
Source: PYQ-style

146. The scientist's theory, though initially ______, was eventually accepted by the entire fraternity.
(a) derided (b) acclaimed (c) embraced (d) lauded
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Though…eventually accepted" demands a contrast, so initially the theory was mocked; "derided" fits. The other options mean "praised", which destroys the contrast, so the answer is derided.
Source: PYQ-style

147. The wages of sin ______ death, says the old proverb.
(a) are (b) is (c) were (d) have been
Answer: (b)
Solution: In this proverb "wages" means the reward/result and takes a singular verb — a famous examiner trap. "Are" tempts because "wages" looks plural, so the answer is is.
Source: PYQ-style

148. The soldiers fought to the last man with ______ courage.
(a) exemplary (b) cowardly (c) timid (d) feeble
Answer: (a)
Solution: Fighting to the last man shows admirable courage; "exemplary" fits. The other options are negative and contradict the context, so the answer is exemplary.
Source: PYQ-style

149. The data ______ that unemployment has risen sharply this quarter.
(a) shows (b) show (c) is showing (d) has shown
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Data" is the plural of "datum" and in formal exam English takes a plural verb. "Shows" is tempting because "data" sounds singular in everyday speech, so the answer is show.
Source: PYQ-style

150. His argument rested on a ______ premise, so the entire conclusion collapsed.
(a) sound (b) valid (c) tenable (d) fallacious
Answer: (d)
Solution: If the conclusion collapsed, the premise must have been false; "fallacious" means based on error. "Sound", "valid" and "tenable" all mean strong, which contradicts the result, so the answer is fallacious.
Source: PYQ-style
