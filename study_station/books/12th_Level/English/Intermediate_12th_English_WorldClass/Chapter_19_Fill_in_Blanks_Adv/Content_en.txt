# Fill in the Blanks (Advanced)

## The Hook: One Word Can Change Everything

Imagine losing a Bank PO seat by a fraction of a mark — and the question that cost you was a single blank: *"The committee ___ divided in their opinion."* You wrote *"was"* — the examiner wanted *"were"*. One word. One rule. One seat.

Fill in the Blanks (FIB) is not a vocabulary test. It is a **logic test wearing a vocabulary costume**. Every blank is a crime scene, and the sentence around it leaves clues — grammar clues, tone clues, and collocation clues. This chapter teaches you to read those clues like a detective.

---

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this chapter, you will be able to:

1. Identify the **three clue types** (grammar, tone, collocation) that solve the vast majority of blanks.
2. Apply the **12 Golden Rules** of subject-verb agreement, tense, and prepositions to advanced blanks.
3. Eliminate wrong options using the **Detective Approach** instead of guessing by "sound."
4. Handle **double-blank** and **phrase-blank** questions common in SSC CGL, IBPS PO, and RRB NTPC.
5. Decide in 5 seconds whether a question is a 15-second fact question or a 45-second inference question.

---

## Part 1: The Three Clues — How Every Blank Is Solved

### Clue 1: Grammar (The Skeleton)

Before you even look at the options, ask: *What does the grammar of this sentence demand?*

- **Number:** Is the subject singular or plural? *"Neither of the boys ___ present."* → singular verb (*was*), because "neither of" takes a singular verb.
- **Tense:** What time frame does the sentence set? *"By 2020, she ___ her degree."* → past perfect (*had completed*), because "by + past time" demands it.
- **Article/Preposition:** *"He is good ___ mathematics."* → *at* (fixed preposition).

Grammar clues are **fact-based** — they have one correct answer and can be solved in 15 seconds.

### Clue 2: Tone (The Mood)

Read the sentence for its emotional direction. Is it positive, negative, or neutral?

*"Despite the setback, the team remained ___."*
The word *"despite"* signals **contrast** — the setback is negative, so the blank must be positive: *optimistic, resilient, undeterred*. Any negative word (hopeless, shattered) is eliminated instantly, even if it is grammatically perfect.

**Signal words for contrast:** despite, although, however, yet, nevertheless, but.
**Signal words for continuation:** moreover, furthermore, and, also, similarly.

### Clue 3: Collocation (The Partnership)

Some words simply "live together" in English. Native speakers never say *"do a mistake"* — they say *"make a mistake."* These fixed partnerships are called **collocations**, and examiners love them.

| Verb | Correct Partner | Wrong Partner |
|---|---|---|
| make | a decision, a mistake, an effort | ~~do a mistake~~ |
| do | homework, business, harm | ~~make homework~~ |
| take | a risk, a break, responsibility | ~~make a risk~~ |
| pay | attention, a compliment, a visit | ~~give attention~~ |

🧠 **Active Recall #1:** Without looking back — (a) What are the three clue types? (b) In *"Although he was tired, he ___ working,"* which signal word tells you the tone? (c) Which is correct: *do a mistake* or *make a mistake*?

## Part 2: The 12 Golden Rules for Advanced Blanks

These twelve rules cover the grammar behind most advanced FIB questions. Master them and grammar-clue questions become free marks.

1. **Intervening Phrase Rule:** Words between subject and verb do not change the verb. *"The quality of the mangoes **was** good."* (Subject = *quality*, not *mangoes*.)
2. **Each / Every / Neither / Either** → singular verb. *"Each of the students **has** a book."*
3. **Collective Nouns** (team, committee, jury) → singular when acting as one unit, plural when members act separately. *"The jury **has** given its verdict"* vs. *"The jury **were** divided in their opinion."*
4. **"By + past time"** → past perfect. *"By 2015, he **had left** the city."*
5. **"Since + point of time / For + period"** with perfect tenses. *"She has lived here **since** 2010 / **for** ten years."*
6. **Parallel Structure:** Items in a list must match in form. *"He likes reading, swimming, and **cycling**"* (not *to cycle*).
7. **Comparatives:** *"than"* demands a comparative adjective. *"She is taller **than** her sister."*
8. **Fixed Prepositions:** *afraid **of**, good **at**, depend **on**, consist **of**, insist **on**, different **from***.
9. **"One of the + plural noun + singular verb."** *"One of my friends **is** a doctor."*
10. **Uncountable nouns** (information, furniture, advice, news) → always singular, never take *a/an*. *"The news **is** good"* (never *an information*).
11. **Conditional sentences:** *"If + past simple … would + V1."* *"If he **worked** hard, he **would pass**."*
12. **Subjunctive after wish/if only:** *"I wish I **were** a bird"* (not *was*).

🧠 **Active Recall #2:** Cover the rules. (a) *"The bouquet of roses ___ (is/are) beautiful."* (b) *"One of the players ___ (was/were) injured."* (c) Why is *"an information"* wrong?

## Part 3: The Detective Approach — Solving Without Knowing Every Word

What if all four options are words you half-know? Use the **Detective Approach**:

**Step 1 — Read the whole sentence twice.** Never judge a blank from the words immediately around it alone.

**Step 2 — Predict your own word.** Before looking at options, say a simple word in your head (even a Hindi word). This anchors the meaning.

**Step 3 — Match, don't guess.** Find the option closest to your prediction. If your prediction was "increase" and an option is *escalate*, that is your answer.

**Step 4 — Plug in and re-read.** Read the full sentence with your chosen word. Does it flow? Does the tone survive?

*Example:* "The new policy is expected to ___ inflation."
Predict: "reduce/control." Options: (a) aggravate (b) curb (c) ignite (d) neglect. Match: *curb* = control. ✔

### Mnemonic: **"G-T-C" — Grammar, Tone, Collocation**
Remember: **"Good Toppers Check"** — Grammar first, Tone second, Collocation third. In that order, every time.

## Part 4: Double Blanks — Two Locks, One Key

Advanced exams (SSC CGL, IBPS PO) give sentences with **two blanks** and paired options. The trick: **solve the easier blank first.**

*"The minister's speech was so ___ that the audience was completely ___."*
(a) boring … interested (b) inspiring … moved (c) lengthy … excited (d) dull … attentive

The connector *"so … that"* demands **cause and matching effect**. Only (b) has a positive cause with a matching positive effect. You never needed to check every word — just the logical pairing.

**Rule of thumb:** In double blanks, both words must agree with each other in tone. A positive first blank with a negative second blank is almost always wrong unless a contrast signal (but, yet) sits between them.

🧠 **Active Recall #3:** (a) In double blanks, which blank do you solve first? (b) What must the two words agree on, unless a contrast signal appears?

## Part 5: Word Power — Greek & Latin Roots for FIB

Examiners recycle words built from the same roots. Learn the root, and you unlock a family of words.

| Root | Meaning | Derived Words |
|---|---|---|
| **bene** (Latin) | good | benefit, benevolent, beneficial, benediction |
| **mal** (Latin) | bad | malice, malfunction, malnutrition, malevolent |
| **dict** (Latin) | say/speak | predict, dictate, verdict, contradict |
| **port** (Latin) | carry | transport, export, portable, support |
| **cred** (Latin) | believe | credible, credit, incredible, credulous |
| **spect** (Latin) | look | inspect, respect, spectator, perspective |
| **rupt** (Latin) | break | interrupt, erupt, corrupt, bankrupt |
| **chrono** (Greek) | time | chronology, chronic, synchronize, anachronism |

### Roots in Context — Read This Passage

*When the economist began to **predict** a **malfunction** in the banking system, few believed her — her warnings seemed hardly **credible**. But when a major bank went **bankrupt**, the public began to **inspect** her earlier reports with new **respect**. Her **benevolent** aim had always been to protect small depositors, and a **chronological** review of her articles showed she had been right for years. Soon, the government agreed to **transport** emergency funds to **support** the affected branches.*

Every bold word comes from the table above. Notice how the sentence context itself defines the word — this is exactly how FIB clues work in the exam.

## ⚠️ Examiner's Trap Box

**Trap 1 — The Intervening Phrase:** *"The rise in prices of essential commodities ___ (has/have) worried everyone."* The plural word *commodities* sits next to the blank to seduce you. The real subject is *rise* → **has**.

**Trap 2 — The Sound-Right Trap:** *"He is senior ___ me."* *Than* sounds right to Hindi speakers ("मुझसे"), but *senior/junior/superior/inferior/prefer* take **to**, never *than*.

**Trap 3 — The Over-Thinking Trap:** Your first grammar-based answer is usually right. Students change *"Neither of them was"* to *"were"* because *them* "feels plural." Trust the rule, not the feeling.

## 🔁 Linguistic Bridge: Hindi vs. English

| We think in Hindi | Wrong English | Correct English |
|---|---|---|
| "वह मुझसे सीनियर है" | He is senior **than** me. | He is senior **to** me. |
| "मुझे एक सलाह दो" | Give me **an advice**. | Give me **some advice / a piece of advice**. |
| "उसने गलती की" | He **did** a mistake. | He **made** a mistake. |
| "मैं यहाँ 2010 से रह रहा हूँ" | I am living here since 2010. | I **have been living** here since 2010. |
| "उसके पास अच्छा ज्ञान है" | He has a good **knowledge**. | He has good knowledge. (no *a*) |

## Summary Table

| Situation | Clue to Use | Time |
|---|---|---|
| Subject-verb, tense, preposition blanks | Grammar (12 Golden Rules) | 15 sec |
| Words like despite, however, moreover in sentence | Tone (contrast/continuation) | 15–30 sec |
| make/do/take/pay type options | Collocation | 15 sec |
| Unknown vocabulary, long sentence | Detective Approach (predict → match) | 45 sec |
| Double blanks | Solve easier blank; check tone agreement | 45 sec |
