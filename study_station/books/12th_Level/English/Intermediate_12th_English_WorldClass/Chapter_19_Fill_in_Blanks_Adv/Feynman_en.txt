# 🧒 Feynman Technique — Fill in the Blanks (Advanced)

## The Hardest Concept, Explained to a 12-Year-Old

The hardest thing in advanced fill-in-the-blanks is not grammar — it is **choosing between two words that both "sound right"** (e.g., *affect/effect*, *economic/economical*, *sensible/sensitive*). Here is how to explain it to a child:

> Imagine you are packing a school bag in the dark. You grab two boxes that look identical — one has your lunch, one has your crayons. Both *feel* right in your hand. The only way to know which is correct is to **look at the label**, not the box.
>
> A sentence works the same way. The blank is the empty slot in your bag. The two confusing options are the identical boxes. The **label** is the *rest of the sentence* — the words around the blank. Never choose a word because it "sounds smart." Choose it because the label (the context) demands it.

**Example:** "The medicine had an immediate ______ on the patient."
- (a) affect (b) effect

Tell the 12-year-old: *affect* is an **A**ction (verb — something you *do*), *effect* is an **E**nd-result (noun — a *thing*). Here the sentence needs a *thing* ("had an immediate ___"), so the label says **effect**. ✅

## The Unforgettable Trick: **"Read the Label, Not the Box"** 🏷️

Before picking any option, ask three label-questions:
1. **What job does the blank do?** (noun / verb / adjective / adverb — check the words around it: "an ___" → noun; "he ___ quickly" → verb)
2. **What is the sentence's mood?** (positive/negative, formal/casual — "despite the loss, she remained ___" needs a *positive* word)
3. **Is there a partner word?** (prepositions and collocations: *depend **on***, *accused **of***, *good **at*** — the partner is fixed, like a lock and key)

If an option fails even one label-question, throw the box away — no matter how good it sounds.

## Daily-Life Anchor

Think of a **puzzle piece**. You never force a piece in because it is pretty. You check its *shape* (grammar), its *picture* (meaning), and its *edges* (collocation). Advanced blanks are solved exactly like that — three checks, then commit.
