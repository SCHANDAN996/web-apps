76. The jury ______ given its verdict unanimously.
(a) has (b) have (c) are (d) were
Answer: (a)
Solution: The tempting option is "have" because a jury has many members, but when a collective noun acts as one united body — here the verdict is unanimous — it takes a singular verb. So the answer is "has".
Source: NCERT Class 10 English Grammar

77. She has been working here ______ 2015.
(a) from (b) since (c) for (d) by
Answer: (b)
Solution: "For" tempts many learners, but it is used with a duration (for ten years), not a point of time. "Since" is used with a definite starting point like 2015, so the answer is "since".
Source: NCERT Class 9 English Grammar

78. Neither the manager nor the employees ______ satisfied with the decision.
(a) were (b) is (c) was (d) has been
Answer: (a)
Solution: With "neither…nor", the verb agrees with the nearer subject. Here the nearer subject is "employees" (plural), so the verb must be plural. "Was" is the trap because it agrees with "manager", so the answer is "were".
Source: PYQ-style

79. He is one of the best players who ______ ever played for the country.
(a) is (b) has (c) have (d) was
Answer: (c)
Solution: In "one of the + plural noun + who/that", the relative pronoun refers to the plural noun ("players"), so the verb is plural. "Has" is the trap set by the word "one", so the answer is "have".
Source: PYQ-style

80. The teacher asked the students why they ______ late the previous day.
(a) had been (b) were (c) have been (d) are
Answer: (a)
Solution: In reported speech with a past reporting verb, an action completed before another past action takes the past perfect. "Were" is tempting but shows simple past, not the earlier action, so the answer is "had been".
Source: NCERT Class 10 English Grammar

81. Hardly ______ the station when the train left.
(a) I had reached (b) I reached (c) had I reached (d) did I reach
Answer: (c)
Solution: After "hardly" at the beginning of a sentence, inversion is required and the earlier action takes the past perfect. "I had reached" is the trap because it has the right tense but no inversion, so the answer is "had I reached".
Source: PYQ-style

82. The principal, along with the teachers, ______ attending the meeting.
(a) are (b) have been (c) were (d) is
Answer: (d)
Solution: Phrases like "along with", "as well as" and "together with" do not change the subject. The real subject is "the principal" (singular). "Are" is the trap created by the intervening plural noun "teachers", so the answer is "is".
Source: PYQ-style

83. He is ______ honest man and ______ European citizen.
(a) an, an (b) a, an (c) a, a (d) an, a
Answer: (d)
Solution: Articles depend on sound, not spelling. "Honest" begins with a vowel sound (h is silent), so it takes "an"; "European" begins with a "yoo" consonant sound, so it takes "a". The answer is "an, a".
Source: NCERT Class 9 English Grammar

84. I look forward ______ you at the conference.
(a) to meet (b) to meeting (c) for meeting (d) meeting
Answer: (b)
Solution: In "look forward to", the word "to" is a preposition, not part of an infinitive, so it takes a gerund. "To meet" is the trap because learners treat "to" as an infinitive marker, so the answer is "to meeting".
Source: PYQ-style

85. The number of accidents on this road ______ increasing every year.
(a) were (b) are (c) is (d) have been
Answer: (c)
Solution: "The number of" takes a singular verb, while "a number of" takes a plural verb. "Are" is the trap set by the plural noun "accidents" next to the blank, so the answer is "is".
Source: PYQ-style

86. Unless you ______ hard, you will not pass the examination.
(a) would work (b) will work (c) worked (d) work
Answer: (d)
Solution: In conditional sentences with "unless", the future tense is not used in the condition clause; the simple present expresses the future. "Will work" is the trap, so the answer is "work".
Source: NCERT Class 10 English Grammar

87. Each of the boys ______ given a certificate at the function.
(a) were (b) are (c) was (d) have been
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Each of" is always followed by a plural noun but a singular verb. "Were" is the trap created by the plural noun "boys", so the answer is "was".
Source: NCERT Class 9 English Grammar

88. He denied ______ the money from the drawer.
(a) take (b) to take (c) taken (d) taking
Answer: (d)
Solution: "Deny" is always followed by a gerund, never an infinitive. "To take" is the trap because many verbs take infinitives, so the answer is "taking".
Source: PYQ-style

89. The Ganges ______ one of the longest rivers in India.
(a) are (b) is (c) were (d) have been
Answer: (b)
Solution: Names of rivers, countries and books that look plural are singular. "Are" is the trap because "Ganges" ends in "s", so the answer is "is".
Source: NCERT Class 9 English Grammar

90. I would rather you ______ home now.
(a) go (b) went (c) will go (d) going
Answer: (b)
Solution: After "would rather" followed by a subject, the past tense is used to express a present preference. "Go" is the trap because "would rather" without a subject takes the bare infinitive, so the answer is "went".
Source: PYQ-style

91. Bread and butter ______ his daily breakfast.
(a) is (b) were (c) are (d) have been
Answer: (a)
Solution: When two nouns joined by "and" together express one single idea or dish, the verb is singular. "Are" is the trap because two nouns are joined by "and", so the answer is "is".
Source: PYQ-style

92. She is senior ______ me by two years.
(a) than (b) to (c) from (d) over
Answer: (b)
Solution: Latin comparatives like senior, junior, superior, inferior and prior take "to", not "than". "Than" is the trap because it follows most comparatives, so the answer is "to".
Source: PYQ-style

93. No sooner had he entered the room ______ the phone rang.
(a) when (b) than (c) that (d) then
Answer: (b)
Solution: "No sooner" is always paired with "than", while "hardly" and "scarcely" take "when". "When" is the trap because of confusion with "hardly…when", so the answer is "than".
Source: PYQ-style

94. The furniture in this room ______ very old.
(a) are (b) were (c) have been (d) is
Answer: (d)
Solution: "Furniture" is an uncountable noun and always takes a singular verb; it has no plural form. "Are" is the trap for learners who think of many pieces of furniture, so the answer is "is".
Source: NCERT Class 9 English Grammar

95. He prevented me ______ going there alone.
(a) from (b) to (c) for (d) against
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Prevent" is followed by the preposition "from" plus a gerund. "To" is the trap because many verbs take "to", so the answer is "from".
Source: PYQ-style

96. Scarcely ______ his speech when the audience started shouting slogans.
(a) had he finished (b) he had finished (c) he finished (d) did he finish
Answer: (a)
Solution: When "scarcely" begins a sentence, inversion is compulsory and the earlier action takes the past perfect. "He had finished" is the trap because the tense is right but the inversion is missing, so the answer is "had he finished".
Source: PYQ-style

97. It is high time we ______ serious about our careers.
(a) get (b) getting (c) got (d) will get
Answer: (c)
Solution: After "it is high time", the past tense is used to express a present necessity. "Get" is the trap because the meaning refers to the present, so the answer is "got".
Source: PYQ-style

98. Many a student ______ failed in this examination.
(a) were (b) have (c) are (d) has
Answer: (d)
Solution: "Many a" is followed by a singular noun and a singular verb even though the meaning is plural. "Have" is the trap because the sense is "many students", so the answer is "has".
Source: PYQ-style

99. Not only the students but also the teacher ______ absent yesterday.
(a) are (b) were (c) was (d) have been
Answer: (c)
Solution: With "not only…but also", the verb agrees with the subject nearer to it, which here is "the teacher" (singular), and "yesterday" demands the past tense. "Were" is the trap set by the plural "students", so the answer is "was".
Source: PYQ-style

100. Had I known about the meeting earlier, I ______ attended it.
(a) would have (b) would (c) had (d) will have
Answer: (a)
Solution: This is an inverted third conditional: "Had I known" means "If I had known", so the result clause takes "would have" plus the past participle. "Had" is the trap because it matches the tense of the condition clause, so the answer is "would have".
Source: PYQ-style
