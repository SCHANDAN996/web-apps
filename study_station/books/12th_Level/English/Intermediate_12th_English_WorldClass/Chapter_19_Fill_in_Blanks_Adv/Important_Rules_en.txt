# 📋 Important Grammar Rules & Tips — Fill in the Blanks (Advanced)

## (a) 12 High-Yield Rules

| # | Rule | Example | Examiner's Trap |
|---|------|---------|-----------------|
| 1 | **Subject–Verb Agreement**: Ignore intervening phrases (with, along with, as well as, together with). The verb agrees with the real subject. | The captain, along with his men, ___ rescued. → **was** | "men" sits next to the blank, tempting you to pick "were". |
| 2 | **Each / Every / Either / Neither** take a singular verb. | Each of the boys ___ present. → **was** | Plural noun "boys" before the verb is a decoy. |
| 3 | **Collective nouns** (team, jury, committee) are singular when acting as one unit, plural when members act separately. | The jury ___ given its verdict. → **has** | "Jury ___ divided" needs "are" — context decides, not habit. |
| 4 | **Parallel Structure**: Items joined by and/or/but must be in the same grammatical form. | She likes reading, writing and ___. → **painting** | Options mix "to paint / painted / painting" — match the series. |
| 5 | **Preposition pairs are fixed**: depend **on**, insist **on**, consist **of**, abide **by**, differ **from**, good **at**. | He insisted ___ going alone. → **on** | Hindi logic ("पर जोर दिया") pushes you toward "in". |
| 6 | **No sooner…than / Hardly…when / Scarcely…when** — never mix the pairs. | No sooner had she left ___ the phone rang. → **than** | "No sooner…when" sounds right but is always wrong. |
| 7 | **The + comparative…, the + comparative** for proportional change. | ___ higher you go, the cooler it gets. → **The** | Options offer "A/An/More" — only "The" fits the double-comparative frame. |
| 8 | **Since (point of time) vs For (duration)** with perfect tenses. | He has lived here ___ 2015. → **since** | "Since two years" is a classic Hindi-speaker error. |
| 9 | **Conditional consistency**: If + past simple → would + V1 (Type 2). | If I ___ a bird, I would fly. → **were** | "Was" sounds natural in speech; formal English wants "were". |
| 10 | **Collocation beats translation**: verbs pair with fixed nouns — *commit* a crime, *take* a decision, *pay* attention, *catch* a cold. | The accused ___ the crime in 2019. → **committed** | "Did/made a crime" mirrors Hindi ("अपराध किया") — reject it. |
| 11 | **Adjective vs Adverb**: Verbs of sense (look, feel, smell, taste, sound) take adjectives when describing the subject. | The soup tastes ___. → **good** | "Well" is the sound-right trap; "tastes well" would mean the soup has a good tongue! |
| 12 | **One of the + plural noun + singular verb.** | One of my friends ___ won. → **has** | "Friends" next to the blank lures you to "have". |

## (b) Time Management — The 15/45-Second Rule

| Type | What it looks like | Time | Strategy |
|------|-------------------|------|----------|
| **Fact-based (15 sec)** | Preposition pairs, fixed collocations, subject–verb agreement, since/for, no sooner…than | ⏱️ 15 seconds | Spot the keyword → recall the rule → mark. Never re-read twice. |
| **Inference-based (45 sec)** | Tone/context blanks, connector choice (however/therefore), vocabulary-in-context, phrasal verbs with multiple meanings | ⏱️ 45 seconds | Read the full sentence, predict your own word *before* seeing options, then match. |

**Golden habit:** Scan the paper once and sort blanks into the two types above. Finish the fact-based ones first in one sweep, bank the time, and spend it on inference questions.

## (c) Morphology — Root, Affix, Example

| Root / Affix | Meaning | Example word | In a blank |
|--------------|---------|--------------|------------|
| **bene-** (Latin) | good | benevolent, benefit | A ___ ruler cares for all. → benevolent |
| **mal-** (Latin) | bad | malicious, malfunction | His ___ intent was obvious. → malicious |
| **dict-** (Latin) | say | predict, contradict | No one can ___ the future. → predict |
| **port-** (Latin) | carry | transport, export | Goods are ___ by sea. → transported |
| **-phobia** (Greek) | fear | claustrophobia | Fear of closed spaces is ___. → claustrophobia |
| **pre-** (prefix) | before | preview, precaution | Take ___ against fire. → precautions |
| **-less** (suffix) | without | hopeless, careless | A ___ driver causes accidents. → careless |
| **re-** (prefix) | again | rebuild, rewrite | The town was ___ after the flood. → rebuilt |

**Why this matters in blanks:** When all four options are unfamiliar words, break each into root + affix. The sentence's tone (positive/negative) usually eliminates two options instantly — *mal-* words can never fill a praise-blank.
