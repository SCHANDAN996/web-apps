# Direct & Indirect Speech (Narration)

## 🎯 The Hook

Imagine this: your friend says, *"I will meet you tomorrow."* An hour later, you tell your mother: *"He said that he would meet me the next day."* Without realising it, you just performed one of the most tested skills in SSC, Banking and Railway exams — **Narration change**. Examiners across these exams regularly include questions from this topic, and most students lose those marks not because the rule is hard, but because they forget **one small word** — a tense, a pronoun, or a time expression. This chapter makes sure that never happens to you.

## 🎓 Learning Objectives

By the end of this chapter you will be able to:

1. Convert any Direct Speech sentence into Indirect Speech (and back) quickly and confidently.
2. Apply the tense-backshift, pronoun-change and time-word-change rules automatically.
3. Handle all five sentence types: statements, questions, commands, exclamations and optatives.
4. Spot the examiner's favourite traps — "said to" vs "told", universal truths, and reporting verbs in the present tense.

---

## 1. What Are Direct and Indirect Speech?

**Direct Speech** repeats the speaker's exact words, placed inside inverted commas ("...").

> Rohan said, **"I am tired."**

**Indirect (Reported) Speech** conveys the same meaning in the reporter's own words — no inverted commas, and the sentence structure changes.

> Rohan said **that he was tired.**

Every Direct Speech sentence has two parts:

- **Reporting Verb** — the part outside the quotes (*Rohan said*).
- **Reported Speech** — the words inside the quotes (*I am tired*).

🧠 **Active Recall:** In the sentence *She said to me, "I know your brother,"* which part is the reporting verb and which is the reported speech? (Answer: *She said to me* = reporting verb; *I know your brother* = reported speech.)

---

## 2. The Three Pillars of Conversion

Whenever the reporting verb is in the **past tense** (said, told, asked), three things change inside the reported speech:

### Pillar 1 — Tense Backshift (one step into the past)

| Direct | Indirect |
|---|---|
| am / is / was | was / had been |
| are / were | were / had been |
| has / have | had |
| had | had (no change) |
| will / shall | would / should |
| can | could |
| may | might |
| V1 / do / does | V2 (past simple) |
| V2 / did | had + V3 (past perfect) |
| is/are + V-ing | was/were + V-ing |
| has/have + V3 | had + V3 |

> He said, "I **write** a letter." → He said that he **wrote** a letter.
> She said, "I **have finished** the work." → She said that she **had finished** the work.

### Pillar 2 — Pronoun Change (the S-O-N rule)

- **S** — First person (I, we, me, us, my, our) changes according to the **Subject** of the reporting verb.
- **O** — Second person (you, your) changes according to the **Object** of the reporting verb.
- **N** — Third person (he, she, it, they) — **No change**.

> He said to me, "**I** will help **you**." → He told me that **he** would help **me**.
> (I → he, because subject is *He*; you → me, because object is *me*.)

### Pillar 3 — Time & Place Word Change

| Direct | Indirect |
|---|---|
| now | then |
| today | that day |
| tonight | that night |
| yesterday | the day before / the previous day |
| tomorrow | the next day / the following day |
| last night/week | the night/week before |
| next week/year | the following week/year |
| ago | before |
| here | there |
| this | that |
| these | those |
| thus | so |

🧠 **Active Recall:** Convert: *She said, "I bought this book yesterday."* (Answer: She said that she had bought that book the day before.)

## 3. The Golden Connector Rules

The word that joins the reporting verb to the reported speech depends on the **type of sentence**:

| Sentence Type | Connector | Reporting Verb |
|---|---|---|
| Statement | that | said / told |
| Yes-No question | if / whether | asked / enquired |
| Wh-question | the Wh-word itself | asked |
| Command / Request | to / not to | ordered, requested, advised |
| Exclamation | that | exclaimed with joy/sorrow |
| Wish / Prayer (optative) | that | wished / prayed |

⚠️ **Note:** *Said to* becomes **told** (with an object) or remains **said** (without an object). *Told* must always have an object: *He told me...* ✔ | *He told that...* ✘

---

## 4. Sentence Type by Sentence Type

### (a) Statements
> He said to me, "I am busy." → He **told** me **that** he **was** busy.

### (b) Questions
**Yes-No type** (begins with is/are/was/do/did/has/will/can...): use *if/whether*, and change the question into statement order (subject before verb).
> She said to him, "**Are you** happy?" → She asked him **if he was** happy.

**Wh-type**: keep the Wh-word as the connector; again use statement order.
> He said to me, "Where **do you** live?" → He asked me where **I lived**.

⚠️ Remove *do/does/did* and adjust the main verb: *did you go* → *I had gone*.

### (c) Commands & Requests
Use **to + V1** for positive commands, **not to + V1** for negative ones.
> The teacher said, "**Open** your books." → The teacher **ordered** (them) **to open** their books.
> Mother said to me, "**Do not go** out." → Mother **forbade** me **to go** out. / Mother told me **not to go** out.

Polite words matter: *please* → **requested**; *let us* (suggestion) → **suggested/proposed + that... should**.

### (d) Exclamations
> He said, "**Hurrah!** We have won." → He **exclaimed with joy that** they had won.
> She said, "**Alas!** My brother is dead." → She **exclaimed with sorrow that** her brother was dead.
> He said, "**What a** beautiful flower!" → He exclaimed that it **was a very** beautiful flower.

### (e) Optatives (wishes & prayers)
> She said, "**May God bless you!**" → She **prayed that** God **might bless** me.

🧠 **Active Recall:** Which connector is used for a Wh-question? For a negative command? (Answer: the Wh-word itself; *not to + V1*.)

---

## 5. When the Tense Does NOT Change

This is where toppers score and others slip. The tense stays the **same** when:

1. The reported speech expresses a **universal truth, scientific fact, proverb or habitual fact**:
   > The teacher said, "The sun **rises** in the east." → The teacher said that the sun **rises** in the east.
2. The reporting verb is in the **present or future tense** (says, will say):
   > He **says**, "I am ill." → He says that he **is** ill.
3. The reported speech states a **historical fact** or an **unreal past wish** (would to God, if only):
   > He said, "I **wish** I **were** a king." → He said that he wished he **were** a king.

---

## 🧠 Mnemonic: "**S-O-N, T-T-T**"

> **S**ubject decides 1st person, **O**bject decides 2nd person, **N**o change for 3rd person — and remember the **T**rio of changes: **T**ense, **T**ime-words, **T**hat-connector.

---

## ⚠️ Examiner's Trap Box

**Trap 1 — "Said to" vs "Told":** Options often include *"He told that he was tired"* — grammatically wrong because *told* needs an object. Correct: *He said that...* or *He told me that...*

**Trap 2 — Universal Truth Backshift:** The examiner writes *"The teacher said that the earth revolved around the sun"* — tempting, but wrong. Universal truths never backshift: *the earth revolves*.

**Trap 3 — Question Word Order:** *"She asked where did he live"* is wrong. In indirect speech, questions become statements: *She asked where he lived.*

## 🔁 Linguistic Bridge: Hindi vs English

| In Hindi we think... | Wrong English | Correct English |
|---|---|---|
| उसने कहा कि वह थका है (present kept) | He said that he **is** tired. | He said that he **was** tired. |
| उसने मुझसे पूछा कि मैं कहाँ रहता हूँ | He asked me where **did I live**. | He asked me where **I lived**. |
| उसने कहा मुझे (told without object) | He **told that** he was busy. | He **told me that** / He **said that** he was busy. |
| आज → today (unchanged) | He said he would come **today**. | He said he would come **that day**. |

Hindi keeps the original tense and time words; English demands backshift and time-word change. Train your ear to switch automatically.

## 📋 Summary Table

| Element | Direct → Indirect |
|---|---|
| Reporting verb (past) | said → said/told; said to → told/asked/ordered |
| Connector (statement) | that |
| Connector (Yes-No Q) | if / whether |
| Connector (Wh-Q) | Wh-word |
| Connector (command) | to / not to |
| Tense | one step back (except universal truths) |
| Pronouns | S-O-N rule |
| now/today/tomorrow | then/that day/the next day |
| Question order | becomes statement order |
