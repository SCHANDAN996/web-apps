# 📋 Important Grammar Rules & Tips — Direct–Indirect Speech (Narration)

## (a) 12 High-Yield Rules

| # | Rule | Example | Examiner's Trap |
|---|------|---------|-----------------|
| 1 | **Backshift of tense:** When the reporting verb is in the past, the tense inside the quote moves one step back (Present Simple → Past Simple, Present Continuous → Past Continuous, Present Perfect → Past Perfect, Past Simple → Past Perfect). | He said, "I **am** busy." → He said that he **was** busy. | No backshift for **universal truths / habitual facts**: He said, "The sun rises in the east." → He said that the sun **rises** (not rose) in the east. |
| 2 | **Will → Would, Shall → Should, Can → Could, May → Might.** | She said, "I **will** help you." → She said that she **would** help me. | "Would/Could/Should/Might/Ought to" in direct speech **do not change** further. Don't write "woulded" or shift "should" again. |
| 3 | **Pronouns change by the S-O-N rule:** 1st person follows the **S**ubject of the reporting verb, 2nd person follows the **O**bject, 3rd person **N**o change. | He said to me, "**I** will teach **you**." → He told me that **he** would teach **me**. | Students change the 3rd person too — "he/she/it/they" inside the quote usually stays the same. |
| 4 | **Time & place words shift:** now → then, today → that day, tomorrow → the next day, yesterday → the previous day, ago → before, here → there, this → that, these → those. | She said, "I came **here yesterday**." → She said that she had gone **there the previous day**. | "Last night" → "the previous night / the night before"; "next week" → "the following week". Mixing "that day" with "yesterday" is a classic wrong option. |
| 5 | **Statements:** use **said (that) / told + object (that)**; remove inverted commas; no question mark. | He said to me, "You are late." → He **told** me **that** I was late. | "Told" **must** take an object (told me/him); "said" must NOT take a direct personal object (❌ said me). |
| 6 | **Yes/No questions:** use **asked + if/whether**; change the question into statement order (subject before verb); drop do/does/did. | He said to me, "**Do you** play chess?" → He asked me **if I played** chess. | Keeping question order ("if did you play") or keeping "do/does" is the trap. No question mark at the end. |
| 7 | **Wh-questions:** keep the wh-word as the connector (no if/whether); statement order; drop do/does/did. | She said to him, "**Where do you live?**" → She asked him **where he lived**. | ❌ "where did he live" — the helping verb disappears and the main verb takes the past form (lived). |
| 8 | **Commands/Requests:** use **ordered / commanded / requested / advised / forbade + object + to / not to + V1**. | The teacher said, "**Open** your books." → The teacher **ordered the students to open** their books. | Negative command: "Don't go" → **not to go** (❌ "to not go" is marked wrong in most keys). "Forbade" already carries the negative — don't add "not" with it. |
| 9 | **Exclamations:** use **exclaimed with joy/sorrow/surprise/wonder + that**; convert the exclamation into a statement; "What a/How" → "very/great". | He said, "**What a** beautiful day!" → He exclaimed with joy that it **was a very** beautiful day. | "Hurrah!" → exclaimed with joy; "Alas!" → exclaimed with sorrow/grief. Don't keep "What a…!" in indirect speech. |
| 10 | **Wishes/Prayers:** use **wished/prayed + that + subject + might/could**. | She said, "**May** God bless you!" → She **prayed that** God **might** bless me. | "Goodbye/Good morning" → bade/wished (He **bade** me goodbye). "May" of wish → might, not "would". |
| 11 | **"Let's" suggestions:** use **suggested/proposed + that + subject + should + V1** (or "suggested going"). | He said, "**Let us** go for a walk." → He **suggested that they should go** for a walk. | "Let me do it" (permission) → **requested to be allowed to do it** — not "suggested". Context decides. |
| 12 | **Reporting verb in present/future → NO tense change** inside the quote. | He **says**, "I am tired." → He says that he **is** tired. | Only the reporting verb's tense matters. If it is "says / is saying / will say", don't backshift — examiners mix this with Rule 1 to confuse you. |

## (b) Time Management — The 15/45-Second Rule

| Type | What it looks like | Time | Strategy |
|------|-------------------|------|----------|
| **Fact-based (15 sec)** | Direct tense shift, pronoun change, time-word change, said/told choice, if/whether connector | ⏱️ 15 sec | Spot the reporting verb's tense → apply backshift mechanically → eliminate options with wrong pronoun/connector. Never re-read the full sentence twice. |
| **Inference-based (45 sec)** | Mixed sentences (command + question), "Let's" vs "Let me", universal-truth exceptions, exclamations with emotion words, choosing between advised/requested/ordered/forbade | ⏱️ 45 sec | First decode the **speaker's intention** (order? request? wish? suggestion?), then pick the reporting verb, then check the structure (to-V1 / that+should / if / wh-word). |

**Quick classification trick:** If the sentence is a plain statement or simple question → 15 sec. If it carries **emotion, intention, or an exception** (universal truth, let's, alas/hurrah, don't) → 45 sec.

## (c) Morphology — Word Building Around "Speech & Saying"

| Root / Affix | Meaning | Examples in this chapter |
|--------------|---------|--------------------------|
| **dict** (Latin: *dicere* = say) | say, speak | dictation, dictate, predict, verdict, contradict |
| **narr** (Latin: *narrare* = tell) | tell, relate | narration, narrator, narrative |
| **port** (Latin: *portare* = carry) | carry | report (carry back news), reporter, transport |
| **log / loqu** (Greek/Latin: speak) | speech, word | dialogue, monologue, eloquent, colloquial |
| **claim / clam** (Latin: *clamare* = shout) | cry out | exclaim, exclamation, proclaim, clamour |
| **voc / vok** (Latin: *vocare* = call) | call, voice | invoke, revoke, advocate, vocabulary |
| **re-** (prefix) | back, again | report, reply, repeat, retell |
| **ex-** (prefix) | out | exclaim (cry out), express, exclamation |
| **inter-** (prefix) | between | indirect, interrupt, interrogative |
| **-tion / -sion** (suffix) | act/state of | narration, exclamation, question, assertion |
| **-ive** (suffix) | having the quality of | interrogative, narrative, assertive, imperative |
| **-or / -er** (suffix) | one who does | narrator, reporter, speaker, listener |

**Memory hook:** *DICT says, NARR tells, PORT carries, LOQU speaks, CLAM shouts, VOC calls* — six roots cover almost every "speech" word the exam can throw at you.
