101. He said to me, "Do you know where the station is?"
(a) He asked me did I know where the station is. (b) He asked me if I knew where the station was. (c) He asked me that if I knew where the station was. (d) He asked me whether I know where the station was.
Answer: (b)
Solution: The tempting wrong option keeps the question word order "did I know", but reported speech needs statement order "I knew". A yes/no question takes "if/whether", and "know" backshifts to "knew", "is" to "was", so the answer is "He asked me if I knew where the station was."

102. She said, "I was cooking dinner when the lights went out."
(a) She said that she was cooking dinner when the lights had gone out. (b) She said that she had been cooking dinner when the lights went out. (c) She said that she had cooked dinner when the lights went out. (d) She said that she is cooking dinner when the lights went out.
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is keeping "was cooking" unchanged; past continuous in direct speech backshifts to past perfect continuous "had been cooking" in reported speech. "Went" (past simple) stays as "went" because it is already in a time clause, so the answer is "She said that she had been cooking dinner when the lights went out."
