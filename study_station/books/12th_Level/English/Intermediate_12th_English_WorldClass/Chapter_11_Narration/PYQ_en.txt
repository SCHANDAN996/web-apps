# 📊 PYQ Analysis — Direct & Indirect Speech (Narration)

## How Exams Test This Chapter (Pattern Analysis)

Narration is one of the most **reliable** grammar topics across SSC (CGL, CHSL, MTS, GD), Banking (IBPS, SBI), and Railway (NTPC, Group D) exams. Almost every Tier-1 paper carries questions from this chapter, usually as a sentence in Direct Speech followed by four Indirect Speech options (or vice versa). The questions are **rule-based, not memory-based** — if you know the transformation rules, these are among the fastest marks in the paper.

**What examiners repeatedly test:**

1. **Tense backshift** — Present Simple → Past Simple, Present Perfect → Past Perfect, will → would, can → could. This is the single most tested rule.
2. **Pronoun changes** — the S-O-N rule (Subject–Object–No change for 1st/2nd/3rd person). Trap options keep the original pronoun unchanged.
3. **Time/place word changes** — now → then, today → that day, tomorrow → the next day, here → there, ago → before. Examiners love hiding one wrong time-word in an otherwise correct option.
4. **Question transformations** — Wh-questions keep the Wh-word; Yes/No questions take *if/whether*; the question mark disappears and the sentence becomes assertive (subject before verb). The most common trap: an option that keeps the inverted question order ("he asked where was I going" ❌).
5. **Imperatives** — ordered/requested/advised + object + to-infinitive; negative commands use *not to*. Trap: options using "that" clauses instead of the infinitive.
6. **Exclamations & wishes** — exclaimed with joy/sorrow/surprise + that; "What a/How" removed.
7. **Universal truths & habitual facts** — **no tense change** ("The sun rises in the east" stays in present). This is a classic trap: three options change the tense, one keeps it — the unchanged one is correct.
8. **Reporting verb in present/future** — when the reporting verb is "says" or "will say", the tense does **not** change. Examiners exploit over-thinking here.

**Examiner's favourite traps (in words):**

- *Almost-right tense shift*: three options shift the tense correctly but one shifts it one step too far (Past Simple → Past Perfect when not needed).
- *Pronoun confusion*: "I" in Direct Speech referring to the speaker must become he/she according to the reporting subject — options that keep "I" are wrong.
- *Statement-order trap in questions*: reported questions must read like statements ("he asked me where I lived"), not questions.
- *Over-thinking trap*: universal truths and present reporting verbs need **no change** — students who "apply the rule blindly" lose these marks.

**Difficulty reality:** Most Narration questions are **Fact/Rule-based (15-second questions)** — match the tense shift and pronoun, eliminate, move on. Only a few (mixed sentences, optatives, "let" sentences) deserve 45 seconds.

## Top 10 High-Yield PYQ-Style Questions with Solutions

**Q1.** He said, "I am busy now."
(a) He said that he is busy now.
(b) He said that he was busy then.
(c) He said that he had been busy then.
(d) He said that he was busy now.
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** Reporting verb "said" is past → "am" backshifts to "was", and "now" → "then". (a) and (d) fail on tense/time word; (c) over-shifts to past perfect.
**Trap type:** Over-thinking (c) + Sound-Right (d).
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q2.** She said to me, "Do you like tea?"
(a) She asked me did I like tea.
(b) She asked me if I liked tea.
(c) She asked me whether did I like tea.
(d) She asked me if I like tea.
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** Yes/No question → *if/whether* + **statement order** (subject before verb) + backshift "like" → "liked". (a) and (c) keep question order; (d) misses the tense shift.
**Trap type:** Statement-order trap.
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q3.** The teacher said, "The earth revolves around the sun."
(a) The teacher said that the earth revolved around the sun.
(b) The teacher said that the earth had revolved around the sun.
(c) The teacher said that the earth revolves around the sun.
(d) The teacher said that the earth was revolving around the sun.
**Answer: (c)**
**Solution:** Universal truths do **not** change tense even after a past reporting verb. Every other option applies an unnecessary backshift.
**Trap type:** Over-thinking — the "apply the rule blindly" trap.
**Source:** NCERT Class 9 English (Beehive) grammar

**Q4.** He said to her, "Please help me."
(a) He said to her to help him.
(b) He requested her to help him.
(c) He told her that help him.
(d) He requested her that she helps him.
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** Polite imperative ("Please…") → *requested + object + to-infinitive*; "me" → "him" (object of reporting verb is "her", speaker is "he"). (a) keeps "said to", (c) and (d) misuse "that".
**Trap type:** Sound-Right (d looks formal but is ungrammatical).
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q5.** Mother said to me, "Do not go out in the rain."
(a) Mother told me do not go out in the rain.
(b) Mother forbade me to not go out in the rain.
(c) Mother ordered me not to go out in the rain.
(d) Mother said that I should not go out in the rain.
**Answer: (c)**
**Solution:** Negative command → *ordered/told + object + not to + verb*. (a) keeps direct form; (b) double negative ("forbade… not"); (d) changes the structure unnecessarily.
**Trap type:** Double-negative trap (b).
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q6.** He said, "Alas! I have lost my wallet."
(a) He said with sorrow that he lost his wallet.
(b) He exclaimed with sorrow that he had lost his wallet.
(c) He cried that he has lost his wallet.
(d) He exclaimed with joy that he had lost his wallet.
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** "Alas!" → *exclaimed with sorrow*; Present Perfect "have lost" → Past Perfect "had lost". (d) has the wrong emotion; (a) and (c) miss the tense shift.
**Trap type:** Emotion-word trap (joy vs sorrow).
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q7.** She says, "I know the answer."
(a) She says that she knew the answer.
(b) She says that she knows the answer.
(c) She says that she had known the answer.
(d) She said that she knows the answer.
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** Reporting verb in **present tense ("says") → no tense change** in the reported speech. Only "I" → "she" (SON rule). (d) wrongly changes the reporting verb itself.
**Trap type:** Over-thinking — students backshift out of habit.
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q8.** He said to me, "Where did you go yesterday?"
(a) He asked me where did I go yesterday.
(b) He asked me where I had gone the previous day.
(c) He asked me where I went yesterday.
(d) He asked me that where I had gone the previous day.
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** Wh-question keeps the Wh-word (no "that" — so (d) is out); statement order required (so (a) is out); "did go" (Past Simple) → "had gone"; "yesterday" → "the previous day". (c) misses both changes.
**Trap type:** Time-word + tense double trap.
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q9.** The captain said to the soldiers, "March forward."
(a) The captain said the soldiers march forward.
(b) The captain ordered the soldiers to march forward.
(c) The captain requested the soldiers that march forward.
(d) The captain told the soldiers marching forward.
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** A command to subordinates → *ordered + object + to-infinitive*. (a) drops the infinitive structure; (c) and (d) are ungrammatical.
**Trap type:** Sound-Right (d resembles continuous tense but is wrong).
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q10.** He said, "Let me go home."
(a) He said that let him go home.
(b) He requested to be allowed to go home.
(c) He told that he should go home.
(d) He wished to let go home.
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** "Let me…" expressing a desire/permission → *requested to be allowed to + verb*. (a) keeps "let" inside the that-clause — a classic error; (c) and (d) distort the meaning.
**Trap type:** "Let"-sentence trap — the most feared narration variant.
**Source:** PYQ-style

## Applying the 15/45-Second Rule

**15-second question — Q1 above:** See "said" (past) → scan options for "was… then". Only (b) has both. Eliminate and move. **Target: 15 seconds.** Never re-read the sentence twice.

**45-second question — Q8 above:** This needs three checks: (1) Wh-word retained without "that", (2) statement order, (3) tense + time-word shift. Budget the full 45 seconds, verify each option against all three checks, then commit. Spending 45 seconds here is an investment — this question type defeats guesswork.

**Strategy summary:** If the Direct Speech is a plain statement with one tense → 15 seconds. If it is a question, imperative with "let", or a mixed/universal-truth sentence → 45 seconds with a checklist.
