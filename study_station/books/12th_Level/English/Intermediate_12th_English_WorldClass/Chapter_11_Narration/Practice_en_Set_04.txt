76. He said to me, "I will help you tomorrow."
(a) He told me that he would help me the next day. (b) He told me that he will help me tomorrow. (c) He told me that he would help me tomorrow. (d) He said me that he would help me the next day.
Answer: (a)
Solution: The tempting wrong option keeps "tomorrow" unchanged, but in reported speech "tomorrow" becomes "the next day" and "will" becomes "would". Also "said to me" becomes "told me", so the answer is "He told me that he would help me the next day."
Source: NCERT Class 9 English Grammar

77. She said, "I have finished my work."
(a) She said that she has finished her work. (b) She said that she finished her work. (c) She said that she had finished her work. (d) She said that she has been finished her work.
Answer: (c)
Solution: Present perfect in direct speech changes to past perfect in indirect speech, so "have finished" becomes "had finished". The tempting wrong option keeps the present perfect, which breaks the backshift rule, so the answer is "She said that she had finished her work."

81. The captain said to the soldiers, "March forward."
(a) The captain ordered the soldiers that march forward. (b) The captain ordered the soldiers to march forward. (c) The captain said the soldiers to march forward. (d) The captain requested the soldiers to march forward.
Answer: (b)
Solution: Commands are reported with "ordered/commanded + object + to-infinitive". A captain gives orders, not requests, and "said" cannot take an object plus infinitive here, so the answer is "The captain ordered the soldiers to march forward."
