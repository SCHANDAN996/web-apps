Card 1
Front: What is the first step in converting Direct Speech to Indirect Speech?
Back: Remove the inverted commas (" ") and add the conjunction "that" (for statements). Then change the reporting verb, pronouns, tense, and time/place expressions.

Card 2
Front: If the reporting verb is in the present or future tense, what happens to the tense of the reported speech?
Back: Nothing — the tense of the reported speech stays unchanged. Only pronouns and time/place words may change. (e.g., He says, "I am busy." → He says that he is busy.)

Card 3
Front: Change: She said, "I will go tomorrow."
Back: She said that she would go the next day. (will → would; tomorrow → the next day)

Card 4
Front: 🧠 MNEMONIC — How do you remember the tense backshift ladder?
Back: "PPP steps back one": Present Simple → Past Simple, Present Perfect → Past Perfect, Past Simple → Past Perfect. Think: "Every tense takes ONE step back in time."

Card 5
Front: What happens to "this", "these", "here", "now" in Indirect Speech?
Back: this → that, these → those, here → there, now → then.

Card 6
Front: How is a Yes/No question reported? Change: He said to me, "Are you ill?"
Back: Use "if" or "whether" (no "that"), and the sentence becomes assertive (subject before verb). → He asked me if/whether I was ill.

Card 7
Front: ⚠️ EXAMINER'S TRAP — He asked me that where was I going. What is wrong?
Back: Two errors: (1) Wh-questions take no conjunction — drop "that"; (2) reported questions use statement order, not question order. Correct: He asked me where I was going.

Card 8
Front: How are Wh-questions reported? Change: She said, "Why are you late?"
Back: Keep the Wh-word as the connector; change question order to statement order. → She asked why I was late.

Card 9
Front: How do you report commands and requests? Change: The teacher said, "Open your books."
Back: Use told/ordered/requested + object + to-infinitive. → The teacher ordered the students to open their books. Negative: "Don't go" → told me not to go.

Card 10
Front: 🧠 MNEMONIC — How do you remember pronoun changes (SON rule)?
Back: S-O-N: First person changes according to the Subject of the reporting verb, Second person according to the Object, Third person needs No change. ("I" follows Subject, "you" follows Object, "he/she/it" stays.)

Card 11
Front: Change: He said, "Alas! I am ruined."
Back: He exclaimed with sorrow that he was ruined. (Exclamations: use "exclaimed with joy/sorrow/surprise" + that; remove the interjection and "!")

Card 12
Front: Which sentences do NOT change tense even when the reporting verb is past?
Back: Universal truths, proverbs, and habitual facts. (e.g., The teacher said, "The earth revolves around the sun." → The teacher said that the earth revolves around the sun.)

Card 13
Front: ⚠️ EXAMINER'S TRAP — She said that she is happy. (Reporting verb "said" is past.) Is this always wrong?
Back: Yes, unless it expresses a universal truth or a fact still true. Normally: She said that she was happy. Examiners test whether you blindly keep the present tense.

Card 14
Front: Change: He said, "I wrote a letter yesterday."
Back: He said that he had written a letter the day before / the previous day. (Past Simple → Past Perfect; yesterday → the day before.)

Card 15
Front: How do "said" and "said to" change in Indirect Speech?
Back: "said" stays "said"; "said to + object" becomes "told + object" (statements) or "asked" (questions). Remember: "told" always needs an object — never "told that" without one.

Card 16
Front: Change: Mother said to me, "Do not go out in the rain."
Back: Mother told me not to go out in the rain. (Negative imperative → "not to + verb"; no "that".)

Card 17
Front: Change: He said, "Let us go for a walk."
Back: He suggested that they should go for a walk. / He proposed going for a walk. ("Let us" → suggested/proposed + should.)

Card 18
Front: How are wishes and prayers reported? Change: She said, "May God bless you!"
Back: Use "wished" or "prayed" + that + subject + might. → She prayed that God might bless me.

Card 19
Front: Change: The captain said, "Hurrah! We have won the match."
Back: The captain exclaimed with joy that they had won the match. (Present Perfect → Past Perfect; "we" follows the subject "the captain" → they.)

Card 20
Front: What happens to modals in Indirect Speech?
Back: can → could, may → might, shall → should, will → would. But could, would, should, might, ought to, and must (as a rule) remain unchanged.
