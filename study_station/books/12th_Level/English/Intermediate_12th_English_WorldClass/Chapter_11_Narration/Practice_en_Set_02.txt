26. He said, "I am happy today."
(a) He said that he was happy that day. (b) He said that he is happy today. (c) He said that he had been happy that day. (d) He said that he was happy today.
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap in option (d) is keeping "today" unchanged; time words must shift in indirect speech. Present simple "am" becomes past simple "was", and "today" becomes "that day", so the answer is "He said that he was happy that day."

27. She said to me, "Do you like tea?"
(a) She asked me did I like tea. (b) She asked me if I liked tea. (c) She asked me that did I like tea. (d) She told me if I liked tea.
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap in option (a) is keeping the question form "did I like"; indirect questions use statement order. Yes/no questions take "if/whether", "said to" becomes "asked", and "like" becomes "liked", so the answer is "She asked me if I liked tea."

Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap in option (c) is applying the tense-shift rule blindly; universal truths never change tense. Since this is a scientific fact, "revolves" stays unchanged, so the answer is "The teacher said that the earth revolves around the sun."
