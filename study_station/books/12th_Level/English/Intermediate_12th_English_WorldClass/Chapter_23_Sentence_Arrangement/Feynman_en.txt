# 🧒 Feynman Technique — Sentence Arrangement

## The Hardest Concept, Explained to a 12-Year-Old

Imagine your friend gives you a jigsaw puzzle — but instead of a picture, the pieces are **sentences**. Your job: put them in order so they tell a story. Sounds hard? Here's the secret: **you never need to read all the pieces carefully.** You only need to find **two things**:

1. **The piece with a straight edge** — the sentence that can ONLY come first.
2. **Two pieces that click together** — a pair that must stay glued.

That's the whole game. Let's see how.

---

## 🔑 Trick 1: Find the "Straight Edge" (The Opening Sentence)

The first sentence of a paragraph is like the first sentence you say when you meet someone: **"Hi, I'm Rohan."** You would never start with *"He also likes cricket."* — because who is "He"?!

So the opening sentence:
- **Introduces a name or topic** (a noun, not a pronoun).
- **Never starts with** "But", "However", "Therefore", "Also", "He/She/It/They", "This", "That" — these words point backwards, so they can't open.

👉 **Unforgettable trick:** *"A paragraph never starts with a pointing finger."* Pronouns and connectors are pointing fingers — they point at something said earlier.

---

## 🔗 Trick 2: Find the "Glued Pair" (Mandatory Pairs)

Some sentences are like best friends — they always walk together. Look for these glue words:

| Glue | Example |
|---|---|
| **Pronoun → Noun** | "Rohan bought a dog." → "**It** was very playful." (It = dog) |
| **Article a → the** | "I saw **a** movie." → "**The** movie was boring." |
| **Connectors** | "He was tired. **Therefore**, he slept early." |
| **Time order** | First → Then → Finally |
| **Question → Answer** | A question sentence is followed by its answer. |

Once you find ONE glued pair, check the options — usually **only one option keeps that pair together**, and boom, you have your answer without arranging everything!

---

## 🎯 The 3-Step Exam Formula

1. **ELIMINATE the opener** — cross out options whose first sentence starts with a pronoun/connector.
2. **HUNT one glued pair** — pronoun-noun, a→the, or connector.
3. **MATCH with options** — pick the only option that respects both.

You solved a 4-sentence puzzle by reading like a detective, not like a student. 🕵️
