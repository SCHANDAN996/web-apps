# 📋 Important Rules & Tips — Sentence Arrangement (Para Jumbles)

## (a) 12 High-Yield Rules

| # | Rule | Example | Examiner's Trap |
|---|------|---------|-----------------|
| 1 | **Find the independent opener first.** The opening sentence introduces a topic/person and depends on no other sentence. It never starts with a pronoun (he, it, they), a connector (but, so, however), or "this/these/such". | "Ravi was a farmer in a small village." can open; "He worked hard every day." cannot. | A sentence that *sounds* like an opener but begins with "This led to…" — "this" needs an earlier reference. |
| 2 | **Pronoun → Noun chain.** A sentence with he/she/it/they/this must come *after* the sentence naming that noun. | "The Ganga is India's longest river. It flows through five states." | Two sentences both contain pronouns — trace each pronoun back to its noun to fix the order. |
| 3 | **Article rule: 'a/an' before 'the'.** First mention uses *a/an*; later mentions use *the*. | "He bought *a* book. *The* book was about space." | Options that place "the + noun" before "a + noun" are almost always wrong. |
| 4 | **Chronology wins.** When sentences describe events, arrange by time: past → present → future, or step 1 → step 2 → result. | Sowing → watering → harvesting. | A "result" sentence (therefore, thus, finally) planted early to confuse you. |
| 5 | **General → Specific.** A paragraph usually opens broad, then narrows to details, examples, or data. | "Pollution is a global problem." → "In Delhi, air quality fell sharply in 2023." | A very specific example placed as the opener in the options. |
| 6 | **Mandatory pairs stick together.** Some pairs are inseparable: question→answer, problem→solution, cause→effect, statement→example (for instance, such as). | "Why do leaves turn yellow? The reason is loss of chlorophyll." | Options that split a question from its answer with an unrelated sentence between them. |
| 7 | **Connectors show direction.** *But/however/yet* = contrast with previous line; *so/therefore/hence* = result; *moreover/furthermore/also* = addition. | "He was rich. *However*, he was not happy." | "However" used where no contrast exists in the previous sentence — a classic sound-right trap. |
| 8 | **Idea–Example order.** "For example / For instance / Such as" sentences always follow the general idea they illustrate. | "Exercise keeps us fit. For example, walking daily improves the heart." | The example sentence offered as the opening line. |
| 9 | **Closing sentence signals.** Look for conclusion words: finally, in conclusion, thus, overall, in short — or a sentence that summarises all others. | "Thus, discipline is the key to success." | A summary sentence placed in the middle; check whether anything after it still needs concluding. |
| 10 | **Eliminate by links, not by reading all 24 orders.** Find one fixed pair (e.g., P→Q), then cancel every option that breaks it. | If "a boy… the boy" is a fixed pair, delete all options separating them. | Over-thinking: reading every option fully wastes 2 minutes. One strong link usually kills 2–3 options. |
| 11 | **Full name before short name.** A person/organisation is introduced fully first, then referred to briefly. | "Dr. A.P.J. Abdul Kalam was born in Rameswaram. Kalam loved reading." | Options using the surname/nickname before the full introduction. |
| 12 | **Read the final sequence once.** After choosing, read your arranged paragraph silently — it must flow like a story with no jump or repetition. | If any sentence feels "sudden", recheck its link. | Sound-right trap: an option that reads smoothly for 3 sentences but breaks logic in the 4th. |

## (b) Time Management — 15/45-Second Rule

| Type | What it looks like | Time | Strategy |
|------|-------------------|------|----------|
| **Fact-based / Link-spotting (15 sec)** | Clear pronoun–noun chain, obvious opener, or a fixed pair (question–answer, a→the). | 15 sec | Spot the one strong link, eliminate options, mark, move on. |
| **Inference-based / Flow-building (45 sec)** | 5–6 sentences with no pronouns, abstract topic (economy, environment), or two options differing by only one swap. | 45 sec | Build the chain: opener → pairs → closer; read the final order once before marking. |
| **Skip signal** | After 45 sec you still have 2 plausible orders. | Skip | Mark the more logical one, flag it, and return only if time remains. Never spend 90+ seconds on one jumble. |

## (c) Morphology — Word Parts that Signal Sentence Roles

| Root / Affix | Meaning | Example word | How it helps in arrangement |
|--------------|---------|--------------|------------------------------|
| **chron** (Greek) | time | chronological, chronic | Words like "chronological" hint the paragraph follows time order. |
| **sequ/secu** (Latin) | follow | sequence, subsequent, consequence | "Subsequent" and "consequence" sentences can never open a paragraph. |
| **con-** (Latin) | together | connect, conclusion | "Conclusion" words mark the closing sentence. |
| **re-** (Latin) | again/back | refer, return, repeat | "Referring to this…" signals a backward link — find what "this" points to. |
| **trans-** (Latin) | across | transition, transfer | Transition sentences bridge two ideas — they sit in the middle, never at the ends. |
| **-tion / -ment** (suffix) | act/result | arrangement, development | Result-nouns ("as a result of this development") point to a preceding cause sentence. |
