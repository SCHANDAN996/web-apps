Card 1
Front: What is the first step in solving a Para Jumble (Sentence Arrangement) question?
Back: Don't read the sentences in given order. First scan all sentences and find the OPENING sentence — one that is independent, introduces the topic, and has no pronoun/connector referring backward.

Card 2
Front: Which sentences can NEVER be the opening sentence of a paragraph?
Back: Sentences starting with: But, However, So, Therefore, Also, Moreover, Yet, And, Thus, or containing pronouns like He/It/They/This/These without an antecedent.

Card 3
Front: What is a "Mandatory Pair" in sentence arrangement?
Back: Two sentences that MUST come together because of a logical link — e.g., a noun in one sentence followed by its pronoun in the next, or a general statement followed by a specific example.

Card 4
Front: 🧠 MNEMONIC — What does "TAP" stand for when hunting the opening sentence?
Back: T – Topic introduction, A – Article/Full name used (not pronoun), P – No backward Pointer (no this/these/however). A sentence passing all three is your opener.

Card 5
Front: ⚠️ EXAMINER'S TRAP — A sentence begins with "This theory..." Can it open the paragraph?
Back: NO. "This" is a demonstrative pointer needing an earlier mention of the theory. Examiners place such sentences first in the jumbled order to trick you into picking them as openers.

Card 6
Front: If a sentence says "He was awarded the Nobel Prize in 1921," what must the previous sentence contain?
Back: The full name of the person (e.g., "Albert Einstein..."). Pronoun sentences always FOLLOW the sentence containing the noun they replace.

Card 7
Front: What is the "Chronology Clue" in sentence arrangement?
Back: Time words — first, then, in 1947, later, finally, decades after — fix the order. Arrange events from earliest to latest unless the passage signals a flashback.

Card 8
Front: What does the connector "However" tell you about sentence order?
Back: It signals CONTRAST. The sentence before it must state an idea that "However" contradicts. Find the opposing pair and lock them together.

Card 9
Front: ⚠️ EXAMINER'S TRAP — Two options both start with the same opening sentence. What should you do?
Back: Don't re-read everything. Compare only where the options DIFFER (usually the 2nd or last sentence) and test that single link. This saves 30+ seconds.

Card 10
Front: What is the "General → Specific" rule?
Back: A paragraph usually moves from a general statement to specific details, examples, or data. The general sentence comes first; the example ("For instance...") follows it.

Card 11
Front: 🧠 MNEMONIC — What does "C-P-C" stand for in checking connectors?
Back: Cause – Problem – Consequence... actually remember it as: Connector needs Partner, Partner needs Context. Every connector (so, but, because) must have its logical partner sentence BEFORE it.

Card 12
Front: In a 5-sentence jumble, which is faster — arranging all 5 freely or using options?
Back: Using options. Identify just the opening sentence and one mandatory pair; this usually eliminates 3 of the 4 options immediately.

Card 13
Front: What is the "Article Clue" for sequencing?
Back: "A/An" introduces a noun for the FIRST time; "The" refers to it AFTERWARD. So the sentence with "a scientist" comes before the sentence with "the scientist."

Card 14
Front: A sentence ends with a question. Where does it usually go?
Back: Just before the sentence that answers it, or near the end as a transition to the conclusion. Question → Answer is a strong mandatory pair.

Card 15
Front: What is the closing sentence of a paragraph usually like?
Back: It contains conclusion words (thus, hence, in conclusion, ultimately), summarises the idea, or states a final result/future implication. It never introduces a brand-new topic.

Card 16
Front: ⚠️ EXAMINER'S TRAP — A sentence sounds perfect as an opener because it is short and simple. Is that enough?
Back: NO. Short ≠ opening. Check for hidden backward references (it, this, such, these). Examiners make trap openers short and smooth-sounding on purpose.

Card 17
Front: What is the "Pronoun Chain" technique?
Back: Link sentences by tracking pronouns: Noun (full name) → he/she/it → this/that. The chain reveals the exact sequence of 3+ sentences at once.

Card 18
Front: Under the 15/45-second rule, where does a Para Jumble fall?
Back: 45-second (Inference-based) category. If no opening sentence or pair emerges in ~45 seconds, mark it and move on — return only if time remains.

Card 19
Front: What should you do when two sentences both seem to follow sentence A logically?
Back: Read A + candidate aloud (mentally) and check the connector/pronoun fit. The correct one continues the SAME idea; the wrong one jumps topic or repeats information.

Card 20
Front: What is the "Acronym Rule" in sentence arrangement?
Back: The full form comes first, the acronym later. "The Reserve Bank of India announced..." must precede "The RBI then decided..." — a quick, reliable ordering clue.
