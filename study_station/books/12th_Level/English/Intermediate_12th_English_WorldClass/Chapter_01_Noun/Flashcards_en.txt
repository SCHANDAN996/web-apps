Card 1
Front: What is a noun?
Back: A word that names a person, place, thing, animal, idea, or quality. Examples: teacher, Delhi, honesty, courage.

Card 2
Front: Name the five main types of nouns.
Back: Proper Noun, Common Noun, Collective Noun, Material Noun, Abstract Noun.

Card 3
Front: Mnemonic — How to remember the 5 types of nouns?
Back: "P-C-C-M-A" → "Please Carry Cold Milk Always" = Proper, Common, Collective, Material, Abstract.

Card 4
Front: Is "gold" a countable or uncountable noun? Can we say "a gold"?
Back: Uncountable (Material Noun). We say "gold is expensive," never "a gold" — unless it means "a gold medal/ring."

Card 5
Front: Examiner's Trap — "The news are shocking." Correct or wrong?
Back: Wrong. "News" looks plural but is singular: "The news is shocking." Same with physics, mathematics, politics.

Card 6
Front: What is the plural of "child," "ox," and "mouse"?
Back: Children, oxen, mice — irregular plurals that don't take -s/-es.

Card 7
Front: Which nouns have the same form in singular and plural?
Back: Sheep, deer, fish, aircraft, series, species. "One sheep, two sheep."

Card 8
Front: "Scissors, trousers, spectacles" — singular or plural verb?
Back: Always plural: "The scissors are on the table." Use "a pair of scissors is…" for singular sense.

Card 9
Front: Examiner's Trap — "He gave me many advices." Spot the error.
Back: "Advice" is uncountable — no plural form. Correct: "He gave me much advice / many pieces of advice." (Note: "advise" is the verb.)

Card 10
Front: What is a collective noun? Give three examples.
Back: A noun naming a group as one unit: team, jury, committee, flock, army.

Card 11
Front: When does a collective noun take a plural verb?
Back: When members act individually/divided: "The jury are divided in their opinion." United as one body → singular verb.

Card 12
Front: Mnemonic — Which nouns are always uncountable?
Back: "LIFE-RAP" → Luggage, Information, Furniture, Equipment, Rice, Advice, Poetry. No -s, no "a/an" before them.

Card 13
Front: What is an abstract noun? How is it formed?
Back: A noun for a quality, feeling, or idea that can't be touched. Formed from adjectives/verbs: kind → kindness, brave → bravery, choose → choice.

Card 14
Front: "The furniture in the room are new." Correct it.
Back: "The furniture in the room is new." Furniture is uncountable and always takes a singular verb.

Card 15
Front: Possessive case — where does the apostrophe go in "the books of the girls"?
Back: The girls' books (plural ending in -s takes apostrophe after s). For irregular plurals: children's books, men's shoes.

Card 16
Front: Which is correct — "one of my friend" or "one of my friends"?
Back: "One of my friends." After "one of," the noun must be plural (but the verb stays singular: "One of my friends is a doctor.").

Card 17
Front: What is the difference between "a number of" and "the number of"?
Back: "A number of students are absent" (plural verb = many). "The number of students is rising" (singular verb = the figure itself).

Card 18
Front: Examiner's Trap — "Mathematics are difficult for me." Why is this wrong?
Back: Subjects ending in -ics (mathematics, physics, economics) take a singular verb: "Mathematics is difficult." Trap: the -s ending looks plural.

Card 19
Front: What are compound nouns and how do they form plurals?
Back: Two+ words acting as one noun: mother-in-law, passer-by. Plural goes on the main word: mothers-in-law, passers-by.

Card 20
Front: "Peoples" — when is this word actually correct?
Back: Only when meaning "nations/ethnic groups": "the peoples of Asia." For ordinary plural, it's always "people."
