🧒 FEYNMAN TECHNIQUE — Explain It Like I'm 12

The Hardest Concept: "Uncountable Nouns" (Why can't we say "an information" or "two advices"?)

Imagine you have a box of chocolates and a bucket of water.

🍫 Chocolates: You can count them — 1 chocolate, 2 chocolates, 10 chocolates. Easy! These are COUNTABLE nouns — pen, book, chair, boy.

💧 Water: Can you say "one water, two waters"? No! Water is like a big blob — you can't count it, you can only measure it (a glass of water, a litre of water). These are UNCOUNTABLE nouns.

Now here's the trick that trips everyone up: some English words look like chocolates but behave like water!

- Information = water 💧 (You can't say "an information." You say "a piece of information.")
- Advice = water 💧 (Not "advices" — say "pieces of advice.")
- Furniture = water 💧 (Not "furnitures" — say "pieces of furniture.")
- News = water 💧 (It ends in 's' but it's singular — "The news IS good.")

🎯 The Unforgettable Trick — "The Water Test":
Before adding "a/an" or making a plural, ask yourself: "Can I put this in a bucket and count it, or is it a blob?"
- Blob → no "a/an", no plural, use "some / a piece of / much".
- Countable pieces → "a/an", plural with -s, use "many / a few".

🧠 Memory Hook — "FAIN-Scum":
The most-tested uncountable nouns hide in this word:
F – Furniture
A – Advice
I – Information
N – News
+ Scenery, Luggage, Bread, Work, Knowledge, Homework, Equipment

If it's in the FAIN-Scum gang, never give it "a/an" or "-s"!

One more daily-life picture: Think of your mother's kitchen. She counts "3 onions" (countable) but she never counts "3 rices" — she says "3 bowls of rice." English works exactly like that kitchen!
