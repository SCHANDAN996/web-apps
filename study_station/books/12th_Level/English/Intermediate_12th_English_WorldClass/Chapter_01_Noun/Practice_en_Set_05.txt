101. Choose the correct sentence.
(a) The scenery of Kashmir are beautiful. (b) The sceneries of Kashmir are beautiful. (c) The scenery of Kashmir is beautiful. (d) The sceneries of Kashmir is beautiful.
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Scenery" is an uncountable noun; it has no plural form and takes a singular verb. Hence "The scenery of Kashmir is beautiful" is correct.
Source: NCERT Class 9 English Grammar

102. Spot the error: Part P: The furnitures in this office / Part Q: are very costly / Part R: and need repair. / Part S: No error
(a) Part P (b) Part Q (c) Part R (d) No error
Answer: (a)
Solution: Part P is wrong. "Furniture" is uncountable and has no plural; write "The furniture in this office". The plural verb in Part Q follows from this root error.

103. Choose the correctly spelt plural form.
(a) Phenomenons (b) Phenomenae (c) Phenomenas (d) Phenomena
Answer: (d)
Solution: "Phenomenon" is a Greek-origin noun whose plural is "phenomena". Similar pairs: criterion–criteria, medium–media.

104. Choose the correct sentence.
(a) He gave me a piece of advice. (b) He gave me an advice. (c) He gave me advices. (d) He gave me many advices.
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Advice" is uncountable; it cannot take "many", "an", or a plural. We count it with "a piece of advice".

105. Spot the error: Part P: The police has / Part Q: arrested the thief / Part R: red-handed last night. / Part S: No error
(a) Part P (b) Part Q (c) Part R (d) No error
Answer: (a)
Solution: Part P is wrong. "Police" is a plural noun and always takes a plural verb: "The police have arrested…". This is a classic examiner trap because "police" sounds singular.

106. Choose the correct possessive form.
(a) The children's toys (b) The children toys' (c) The childrens toys (d) The childrens' toys
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Children" is already plural but does not end in -s, so the possessive is formed by adding 's: "children's toys".

113. Choose the correct sentence.
(a) He is a man of few words. (b) He is a man of few word. (c) He is a man of little words. (d) He is man of few words.
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Words" is countable here, so "few" (not "little") is correct, and the article "a" is needed before "man". "A man of few words" is the standard idiom.

114. Choose the correct possessive form for two owners of one shop.
(a) Ram's and Shyam's shop (b) Ram and Shyam's shop (c) Rams' and Shyams' shop (d) Ram and Shyams shop
Answer: (b)
Solution: When two people jointly own one thing, the apostrophe goes only with the second name: "Ram and Shyam's shop". "Ram's and Shyam's shops" would mean separate shops.

Solution: One article before two nouns joined by "and" means one person with two roles, so a singular verb is used. "The poet and the philosopher are dead" would mean two persons.
