51. Which of the following is a proper noun?
(a) Ganga (b) river (c) city (d) mountain
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Ganga" names a specific river, so it is a proper noun. City, river and mountain are common nouns because they name a general class.
Source: NCERT Class 6 English Grammar

52. Identify the abstract noun in the sentence: "Honesty is the best policy."
(a) the (b) best (c) policy (d) honesty
Answer: (d)
Solution: "Honesty" is a quality that cannot be seen or touched, so it is an abstract noun. "Policy" here is a common noun, and "best" is an adjective.
Source: NCERT Class 7 English Grammar

53. The plural of "child" is:
(a) childs (b) children (c) childes (d) childrens
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Child" has an irregular plural — "children". "Childrens" is a trap; the word is already plural and never takes another -s.
Source: NCERT Class 6 English Grammar

54. Which of these is a collective noun?
(a) army (b) soldier (c) gun (d) war
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Army" stands for a group of soldiers, so it is a collective noun. Soldier, gun and war name individual things or ideas.
Source: NCERT Class 7 English Grammar

55. The plural of "knife" is:
(a) knives (b) knifes (c) knive (d) knifes'
Answer: (a)
Solution: Nouns ending in -fe usually change to -ves in the plural: knife → knives, wife → wives. "Knifes" is a common examiner trap.
Source: NCERT Class 6 English Grammar

56. Identify the material noun: "This ring is made of gold."
(a) ring (b) gold (c) made (d) this
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Gold" is a substance or raw material, so it is a material noun. "Ring" is a common noun (a finished object).
Source: NCERT Class 7 English Grammar

57. Which word is an uncountable noun?
(a) book (b) water (c) chair (d) pen
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Water" cannot be counted as separate units, so it is uncountable. Book, chair and pen are countable nouns.
Source: NCERT Class 8 English Grammar

58. The plural of "tooth" is:
(a) tooths (b) toothes (c) teeth (d) teethes
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Tooth" has an irregular plural "teeth" (like foot → feet). Adding -s to it is wrong.
Source: NCERT Class 6 English Grammar

59. In "A flock of birds was flying," the noun "flock" is:
(a) abstract noun (b) proper noun (c) material noun (d) collective noun
Answer: (d)
Solution: "Flock" names a group of birds, so it is a collective noun. It is not abstract because the group can be seen.
Source: NCERT Class 7 English Grammar

60. Which sentence uses the noun correctly?
(a) She gave me many advices. (b) She gave me much advice. (c) She gave me many advice. (d) She gave me an advice.
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Advice" is uncountable, so it takes "much", never "many" or "an". "Advices" exists only in the formal sense of official communications, not everyday help.
Source: NCERT Class 8 English Grammar

61. Choose the correct sentence:
(a) The furniture in this room is new. (b) The furniture in this room are new. (c) The furnitures in this room are new. (d) The furnitures in this room is new.
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Furniture" is an uncountable noun and always takes a singular verb. It has no plural form "furnitures" — a favourite examiner trap.
Source: PYQ-style

62. Choose the correct sentence:
(a) The news are shocking. (b) The news were shocking. (c) The newses are shocking. (d) The news is shocking.
Answer: (d)
Solution: "News" looks plural because of the final -s but is singular in meaning and takes a singular verb. This sound-right trap appears often in exams.
Source: PYQ-style

63. Spot the error: Part P: "The sceneries of" / Part Q: "Kashmir are" / Part R: "very beautiful."
(a) No error (b) Part Q (c) Part R (d) Part P
Answer: (d)
Solution: "Scenery" is uncountable and has no plural "sceneries". Part P should read "The scenery of". Parts Q and R are correct once the noun is singular.
Source: PYQ-style

64. Choose the correct possessive form: "The house belongs to Ravi and Sohan."
(a) Ravi's and Sohan's house (b) Ravis' and Sohans' house (c) Ravi and Sohan's house (d) Ravi and Sohans house
Answer: (c)
Solution: When two people jointly own one thing, the apostrophe goes only with the second name: "Ravi and Sohan's house". Option (a) would mean two separate houses.
Source: NCERT Class 8 English Grammar

65. Choose the correct sentence:
(a) Mathematics is a difficult subject. (b) Mathematics are a difficult subject. (c) Mathematic is a difficult subject. (d) Mathematicses are difficult.
Answer: (a)
Solution: Names of subjects ending in -ics (mathematics, physics, economics) take a singular verb when they mean one subject of study.
Source: PYQ-style

66. Spot the error: Part P: "He gave me" / Part Q: "many informations" / Part R: "about the case."
(a) Part P (b) Part R (c) Part Q (d) No error
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Information" is uncountable and never takes a plural -s or "many". Part Q should be "much information" or "a lot of information".
Source: PYQ-style

67. Choose the correct sentence:
(a) The police has arrested the thief. (b) The police have arrested the thief. (c) The polices have arrested the thief. (d) The police is arrest the thief.
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Police" is a plural noun with no singular form in this sense, so it takes "have". "Polices" is wrong — a classic examiner trap.
Source: PYQ-style

68. Which sentence uses "cattle" correctly?
(a) The cattle is grazing in the field. (b) The cattles are grazing in the field. (c) The cattle are grazing in the field. (d) A cattle is grazing in the field.
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Cattle" is always plural and takes a plural verb. It has no plural "cattles" and cannot be used with "a".
Source: PYQ-style

69. Choose the correct sentence:
(a) I need an scissors. (b) I need a scissor. (c) I need scissors pair. (d) I need a pair of scissors.
Answer: (d)
Solution: "Scissors" is a plural-only noun; we count it with "a pair of". "A scissor" and "an scissors" are both wrong.
Source: NCERT Class 8 English Grammar

70. Spot the error: Part P: "One of my friend" / Part Q: "has gone" / Part R: "to Delhi."
(a) Part Q (b) Part P (c) Part R (d) No error
Answer: (b)
Solution: After "one of", the noun must be plural: "one of my friends". The verb stays singular ("has"), so Part Q is correct — the trap is in Part P.
Source: PYQ-style

71. Choose the correct sentence:
(a) The committee has taken their decision. (b) The committee have taken its decision. (c) The committee has taken its decision. (d) The committee are taken its decision.
Answer: (c)
Solution: When a collective noun acts as one united body, it takes a singular verb and singular pronoun: "has taken its decision". "Have" would suit only if members acted separately.
Source: PYQ-style

72. Choose the correct sentence:
(a) He is a man of a few word. (b) He is a man of few word. (c) He is a man of few words. (d) He is man of few words.
Answer: (c)
Solution: In the idiom "a man of few words", "words" must be plural and the article "a" is required before "man". Option (d) drops the article, and (b) drops the plural.
Source: PYQ-style

73. Spot the error: Part P: "The wages of sin" / Part Q: "is" / Part R: "death."
(a) Part P (b) Part Q (c) Part R (d) No error
Answer: (d)
Solution: "Wages" meaning a reward or result (not salary) can take a singular verb, as in this well-known biblical phrase. The sentence is correct as it stands — an over-thinking trap.
Source: PYQ-style

74. Choose the correct sentence:
(a) She has bought two dozen eggs. (b) She has bought two dozens eggs. (c) She has bought two dozen of eggs. (d) She has bought two dozens of egg.
Answer: (a)
Solution: When "dozen" follows a definite number, it stays singular and takes no "of": "two dozen eggs". "Dozens of" is used only without a number.
Source: PYQ-style

75. Choose the correct sentence:
(a) My brothers-in-law live in Agra. (b) My brother-in-laws live in Agra. (c) My brother-in-law live in Agra. (d) My brothers-in-laws lives in Agra.
Answer: (a)
Solution: In compound nouns, the plural -s attaches to the main word: "brothers-in-law" (like passers-by, commanders-in-chief). "Brother-in-laws" is a common trap.
Source: PYQ-style
