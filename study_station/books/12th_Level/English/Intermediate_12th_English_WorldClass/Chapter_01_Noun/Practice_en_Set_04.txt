76. Choose the correct sentence.
(a) The sceneries of Kashmir are very beautiful. (b) The scenery of Kashmir is very beautiful. (c) The scenery of Kashmir are very beautiful. (d) The sceneries of Kashmir is very beautiful.
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Scenery" is an uncountable noun; it has no plural form and takes a singular verb. Hence "scenery … is" is correct.
Source: NCERT Class 9 English Grammar

77. Spot the error: Part P: The furnitures in this office / Part Q: is made of teak wood / Part R: and was imported from Kerala. / Part S: No error
(a) Part P (b) Part Q (c) Part R (d) No error
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Furniture" is uncountable and has no plural form; Part P should read "The furniture in this office". The singular verbs in Parts Q and R are correct.
Source: PYQ-style

78. Choose the correctly spelt plural form.
(a) Phenomenons (b) Phenomena (c) Phenomenas (d) Phenomenae
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Phenomenon" is a Greek-origin noun whose plural is "phenomena". "Phenomenons" is a common examiner trap.
Source: NCERT Class 10 English Grammar

79. The committee ______ divided in their opinion on the new policy.
(a) has been (b) is (c) were (d) was
Answer: (c)
Solution: When the members of a collective noun act separately, shown here by "divided" and the plural pronoun "their", it takes a plural verb: "were". The singular "is" would need a united body with "its".
Source: PYQ-style

80. Choose the correct sentence.
(a) He gave me many advices on the matter. (b) He gave me much advices on the matter. (c) He gave me much advice on the matter. (d) He gave me many advice on the matter.
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Advice" is uncountable, so it takes "much", not "many", and never a plural form. Examiner trap: "advices" looks natural but is wrong in standard English.
Source: NCERT Class 9 English Grammar

81. Spot the error: Part P: One of my friend / Part Q: has gone / Part R: to Delhi yesterday. / Part S: No error
(a) Part Q (b) Part P (c) Part R (d) No error
Answer: (b)
Solution: After "one of", the noun must be plural: "one of my friends". The singular verb "has" in Part Q is correct, which is the trap — the plural noun belongs to the phrase, not the subject.
Source: PYQ-style

82. The plural of "crisis" is:
(a) crisis (b) crisises (c) crisii (d) crises
Answer: (d)
Solution: Greek-origin nouns ending in "-is" form the plural with "-es": crisis → crises, analysis → analyses.
Source: NCERT Class 10 English Grammar

83. Choose the correct possessive form.
(a) The childrens' toys are scattered. (b) The children's toys are scattered. (c) The childrens toys are scattered. (d) The children toys' are scattered.
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Children" is already plural without "-s", so the possessive is formed by adding 's: "children's". "Childrens'" is a classic trap.
Source: NCERT Class 8 English Grammar

84. Mathematics ______ a difficult subject for many students.
(a) is (b) were (c) are (d) have been
Answer: (a)
Solution: Names of subjects ending in "-ics" (mathematics, physics, economics) are singular when they denote a subject of study. Trap: the "-s" ending misleads students into choosing a plural verb.
Source: PYQ-style

85. Spot the error: Part P: The police has / Part Q: arrested the thief / Part R: who stole the jewels. / Part S: No error
(a) Part P (b) Part Q (c) Part R (d) No error
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Police" is a plural noun and always takes a plural verb: "The police have arrested…". "Has" in Part P is the error.
Source: PYQ-style

86. Choose the correct sentence.
(a) I need some informations about the course. (b) I need an information about the course. (c) I need many informations about the course. (d) I need some information about the course.
Answer: (d)
Solution: "Information" is uncountable: no plural, no indefinite article. Use "some information" or "a piece of information".
Source: NCERT Class 9 English Grammar

87. A ______ of wolves was seen near the forest edge.
(a) herd (b) swarm (c) flock (d) pack
Answer: (d)
Solution: The correct collective noun for wolves is "pack". Herd (cattle), flock (sheep/birds), swarm (bees) are distractors.
Source: NCERT Class 8 English Grammar

88. Spot the error: Part P: The poet and philosopher / Part Q: have delivered / Part R: his lecture today. / Part S: No error
(a) Part P (b) No error (c) Part R (d) Part Q
Answer: (d)
Solution: "The poet and philosopher" with one article refers to one person, so the verb must be singular: "has delivered". Two articles ("the poet and the philosopher") would need a plural verb — a favourite examiner trap.
Source: PYQ-style

89. The plural of "criterion" is:
(a) criteria (b) criterias (c) criterions (d) criterium
Answer: (a)
Solution: Greek-origin "criterion" becomes "criteria" in the plural. Note that "criteria" is plural; using it as singular is a common error.
Source: NCERT Class 10 English Grammar

90. Choose the correct sentence.
(a) The news is really shocking. (b) The news are really shocking. (c) The news were really shocking. (d) The news have been really shocking.
Answer: (a)
Solution: "News" looks plural but is uncountable and singular: "The news is…". This sound-right trap appears frequently in exams.
Source: PYQ-style

91. Spot the error: Part P: He is one of the best player / Part Q: in the team / Part R: and scores consistently. / Part S: No error
(a) No error (b) Part Q (c) Part R (d) Part P
Answer: (d)
Solution: "One of the" must be followed by a plural noun: "one of the best players". The singular verb later ("scores") is correct because the subject is "He".
Source: PYQ-style

92. Which sentence uses the noun correctly?
(a) She has bought two dozens eggs. (b) She has bought two dozens of eggs. (c) She has bought two dozen eggs. (d) She has bought two dozen of eggs.
Answer: (c)
Solution: When "dozen" is preceded by a numeral, it stays singular and takes no "of": "two dozen eggs". "Dozens of" is correct only without a numeral.
Source: NCERT Class 9 English Grammar

93. The cattle ______ grazing in the field.
(a) has been (b) was (c) is (d) are
Answer: (d)
Solution: "Cattle" is a plural noun with no singular form in this sense; it always takes a plural verb.
Source: PYQ-style

94. Spot the error: Part P: My brother-in-laws / Part Q: live in Mumbai / Part R: with their families. / Part S: No error
(a) Part Q (b) Part P (c) Part R (d) No error
Answer: (b)
Solution: In compound nouns, the plural is added to the main word: "brothers-in-law", not "brother-in-laws".
Source: NCERT Class 10 English Grammar

95. Choose the correct sentence.
(a) The wages of sin is death. (b) The wages of sin are death. (c) The wage of sin are death. (d) The wages of sin have death.
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Wages" meaning a reward or result (not salary) takes a singular verb in this fixed expression: "The wages of sin is death." When "wages" means payment, it takes a plural verb — a subtle examiner trap.
Source: PYQ-style

96. Which of the following is an abstract noun?
(a) Crowd (b) Teacher (c) Honesty (d) Mumbai
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Honesty" names a quality that cannot be touched — an abstract noun. Teacher is common, Mumbai is proper, crowd is collective.
Source: NCERT Class 8 English Grammar

97. Spot the error: Part P: The Ganges is / Part Q: one of the longest river / Part R: in India. / Part S: No error
(a) Part P (b) No error (c) Part Q (d) Part R
Answer: (c)
Solution: After "one of the longest", the noun must be plural: "one of the longest rivers". The singular "river" in Part Q is the error.

98. Choose the correct sentence.
(a) All the machinery was imported from Germany. (b) All the machineries were imported from Germany. (c) All the machineries was imported from Germany. (d) All the machinery were imported from Germany.
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Machinery" is uncountable: no plural form, singular verb. Hence "machinery was".
Source: NCERT Class 9 English Grammar

99. Spot the error: Part P: Neither the teacher nor the students / Part Q: was present / Part R: in the auditorium. / Part S: No error
(a) Part P (b) Part Q (c) Part R (d) No error
Answer: (b)
Solution: With "neither…nor", the verb agrees with the nearer subject. "Students" is plural, so it should be "were present". The singular "teacher" near the start is the trap.
Source: PYQ-style

100. Choose the sentence with the correct noun usage.
(a) The jury has given their verdicts unanimously. (b) The jury have given its verdict unanimously. (c) The jury has given its verdict unanimously. (d) The jury have gave its verdict unanimously.
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Unanimously" shows the jury acted as one body, so the collective noun takes a singular verb and singular pronoun: "has given its verdict". Division would require "have … their".
Source: PYQ-style
