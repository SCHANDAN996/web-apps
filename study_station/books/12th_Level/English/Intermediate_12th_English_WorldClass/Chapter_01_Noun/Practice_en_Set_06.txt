126. Spot the error: The sceneries (P) / of Kashmir (Q) / are very beautiful. (R)
(a) Part P (b) Part Q (c) Part R (d) No error
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Scenery" is an uncountable noun and has no plural form. Part P should read "The scenery". Words like scenery, furniture, luggage, information, and advice are never pluralised.

127. Choose the correct sentence:
(a) He gave me many advices on the matter. (b) He gave me many advice on the matter. (c) He gave me much advice on the matter. (d) He gave me much advices on the matter.
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Advice" is uncountable, so it takes "much", not "many", and never takes a plural form. The countable equivalent is "a piece of advice".

128. Spot the error: One of my friend (P) / has gone (Q) / to Delhi yesterday. (R)
(a) Part Q (b) Part P (c) Part R (d) No error
Answer: (b)
Solution: After "one of", a plural noun is required: "one of my friends". This is a classic examiner trap — the verb stays singular ("has") because the subject is "one", but the noun after "of" must be plural.

131. Choose the correct possessive form:
(a) The childrens' toys are scattered. (b) The children toys' are scattered. (c) The childrens toys are scattered. (d) The children's toys are scattered.
Answer: (d)
Solution: "Children" is already plural without an "s", so the possessive is formed by adding apostrophe + s: "children's". For plurals ending in s, only an apostrophe is added (girls' school).

Answer: (c)

133. Choose the sentence with the correct use of a collective noun:
(a) The jury has given its verdict after long deliberation. (b) The jury have given its verdict after long deliberation. (c) The jury has given their verdict after long deliberation. (d) The jury are given its verdict after long deliberation.
Answer: (a)
Solution: When a collective noun acts as one unit, it takes a singular verb and singular pronoun: "The jury has given its verdict". If members act individually, use plural: "The jury are divided in their opinions".

Answer: (b)

138. Spot the error: He is (P) / a man of few word (Q) / and great deeds. (R)

Answer: (d)
Solution: The idiom is "a man of few words" — "words" must be plural here. Note the difference: "word" (a single unit of language) versus "words" (speech, talk), as in "keep your word" (promise) but "have words with" (quarrel).

Solution: "Police" is a plural noun with no singular form in this sense and always takes a plural verb: "The police have arrested the thief". To refer to one person, say "a policeman/police officer".

140. Spot the error: The teacher as well as the students (P) / were present (Q) / in the hall. (R)
(a) Part P (b) No error (c) Part R (d) Part Q
Answer: (d)
Solution: When two subjects are joined by "as well as", the verb agrees with the first subject. "The teacher" is singular, so use "was present". The intervening phrase "as well as the students" is a classic trap.

145. Choose the correct sentence:

Answer: (a)
Solution: In a compound adjective before a noun, the noun stays singular and is hyphenated: "a five-rupee note", "a ten-year-old boy", "a three-day match". The plural appears only when there is no following noun: "The boy is ten years old."

146. Spot the error: The principal and secretary (P) / have (Q) / arrived. (R)

Answer: (d)
Solution: A single article "the" before "principal and secretary" means one person holding both posts, so the verb must be singular: "has arrived". If two different people were meant, it would be "the principal and the secretary have arrived". This article trap is a favourite of examiners.

147. Choose the correct sentence:
(a) Bread and butter are my favourite breakfast. (b) Bread and butter were my favourite breakfast. (c) Bread and butter is my favourite breakfast. (d) Bread and butter have been my favourite breakfast.
Answer: (c)
Solution: When two nouns joined by "and" together express one idea or one dish, the verb is singular: "Bread and butter is my favourite breakfast". Compare: "Rice and curry are on the table" (two separate items).

148. Spot the error: Each of the boys (P) / have done (Q) / his homework. (R)
(a) Part P (b) Part Q (c) Part R (d) No error
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Each of" is followed by a plural noun but a singular verb: "Each of the boys has done his homework". The plural noun between "each" and the verb is the intervening-phrase trap.

149. Choose the correct sentence:
(a) The majority of students was in favour of the trip. (b) The majority of students has in favour of the trip. (c) The majority of students is in favour of the trip. (d) The majority of students were in favour of the trip.
Answer: (d)
Solution: "The majority of + plural noun" takes a plural verb because the sense is plural: "The majority of students were in favour". But "The majority was small" treats majority itself as a unit. Examiners test both uses.

150. Spot the error: Neither the teacher nor the students (P) / was (Q) / present in the auditorium. (R)
(a) Part P (b) No error (c) Part R (d) Part Q
Answer: (d)
Solution: With "neither...nor", the verb agrees with the nearer subject. Here "students" (plural) is nearer, so use "were present". This proximity rule is a standard hard-level trap.
