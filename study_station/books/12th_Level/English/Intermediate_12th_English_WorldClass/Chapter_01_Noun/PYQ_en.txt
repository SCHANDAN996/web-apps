# 📊 PYQ Analysis — Noun (Previous Year Question Pattern)

## How Exams Test Nouns

Nouns appear in almost every English section of SSC, Banking, Railway, and State exams — rarely as direct "identify the noun" questions, but hidden inside **Error Spotting, Fill in the Blanks, and Sentence Improvement**. The examiner's favourite trick is to test whether you can spot a **singular–plural mismatch** or a **wrong possessive form** buried inside a long sentence.

### Where Noun Questions Hide

| Question Type | How Nouns Are Tested | Difficulty |
|---|---|---|
| Error Spotting | Uncountable noun made plural ("informations"), wrong verb agreement with collective nouns | Medium–Hard |
| Fill in the Blanks | Choosing between "advice/advise", "practice/practise", countable vs uncountable | Easy–Medium |
| Sentence Improvement | Possessive case errors, "one of the + plural noun" | Medium |
| Direct Grammar | Identify type of noun (abstract, collective, material) | Easy |

### Topics That Repeat Most Often

- **Uncountable nouns** wrongly pluralised: *information, furniture, luggage, advice, news, scenery, machinery, stationery, poetry, crockery, bread, hair (as a mass)*.
- **Nouns same in singular and plural**: *sheep, deer, fish, aircraft, species, series, dozen (after numbers)*.
- **Nouns always plural**: *scissors, trousers, spectacles, goods, earnings, premises, surroundings, alms, thanks*.
- **Nouns plural in form but singular in use**: *news, physics, mathematics, measles, mumps, billiards, innings*.
- **Collective noun agreement**: team/jury/committee — singular when acting as one body, plural when members act separately.
- **Possessive case**: apostrophe with living things vs "of" with non-living things; *else's*, *somebody else's*.
- **"One of the + plural noun + singular verb"** — a classic trap.

### Examiner's Trap Types (with names used below)

1. **Intervening Phrase** — extra words between subject and verb hide the real subject ("The quality of the apples *were*…").
2. **Sound-Right Trap** — the wrong form "sounds" natural in speech ("The news *are* good").
3. **Collective Noun Confusion** — jury/team agreement.
4. **Over-thinking** — the simple rule applies; the student invents an exception.

## Top 10 High-Yield PYQ-Style Questions with Solutions

**Q1.** One of my friend / (a) is going / (b) to Delhi tomorrow. / (c) No error (d)
**Answer:** (a)
**Solution:** After "one of the / one of my", the noun must be **plural**: "one of my **friends**". The verb stays singular ("is") because the subject is "one". **Trap type:** Sound-Right Trap — "one of my friend" sounds fine in casual speech.
**Source:** PYQ-style (SSC pattern)

**Q2.** He gave me many valuable **informations** about the case. — Spot the error.
(a) He gave me (b) many valuable informations (c) about the case (d) No error
**Answer:** (b)
**Solution:** "Information" is **uncountable** — it never takes a plural form or "many". Correct: "much valuable information" or "many pieces of information". **Trap type:** Sound-Right Trap.
**Source:** NCERT Class 9 English (grammar concept)

**Q3.** The jury ______ divided in their opinion.
(a) is (b) are (c) was (d) has
**Answer:** (b)
**Solution:** "Their" shows the members are acting **individually**, so the collective noun takes a plural verb: "are". If the jury acted as one body, we would say "The jury **is** unanimous in **its** verdict." **Trap type:** Collective Noun Confusion.
**Source:** PYQ-style (Bank exam pattern)

**Q4.** The **sceneries** of Kashmir are very beautiful. — Spot the error.
(a) The sceneries (b) of Kashmir (c) are very beautiful (d) No error
**Answer:** (a)
**Solution:** "Scenery" is uncountable. Correct: "The **scenery** of Kashmir **is** very beautiful." Note the verb also changes to singular. **Trap type:** Sound-Right Trap.
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q5.** Mathematics ______ a difficult subject for many students.
(a) are (b) were (c) is (d) have been
**Answer:** (c)
**Solution:** Subjects ending in "-ics" (mathematics, physics, economics) are **singular** when they mean the subject of study. **Trap type:** Over-thinking — the "-s" ending fools students into choosing a plural verb.
**Source:** NCERT Class 10 English

**Q6.** I need ______ to cut this paper.
(a) a scissor (b) scissors (c) a pair of scissor (d) scissoring
**Answer:** (b)
**Solution:** "Scissors" is always plural and has no singular form "scissor", so (a) and (c) are wrong. With the article "a", the correct phrasing would be "a **pair of** scissors"; without it, simply "scissors" is correct. **Trap type:** Over-thinking.
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q7.** The **furniture** in this room ______ very old.
(a) are (b) were (c) is (d) have been
**Answer:** (c)
**Solution:** "Furniture" is uncountable and always takes a **singular** verb, even though it refers to many items. **Trap type:** Sound-Right Trap.
**Source:** PYQ-style (SSC CGL pattern)

**Q8.** This is **Ramesh's and Suresh's** house. — Spot the error when the house belongs to both jointly.
(a) This is (b) Ramesh's and Suresh's (c) house (d) No error
**Answer:** (b)
**Solution:** For **joint ownership**, the apostrophe goes only with the last name: "Ramesh **and Suresh's** house". "Ramesh's and Suresh's houses" would mean two separate houses. **Trap type:** Over-thinking.
**Source:** NCERT grammar concept

**Q9.** The **cattles** are grazing in the field. — Spot the error.
(a) The cattles (b) are grazing (c) in the field (d) No error
**Answer:** (a)
**Solution:** "Cattle" is already plural in meaning and has no plural form "cattles". Correct: "The **cattle** are grazing." Similar words: *people, police, poultry, gentry*. **Trap type:** Sound-Right Trap.
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q10.** He is one of the best **player** in the team. — Spot the error.
(a) He is one of the best (b) player (c) in the team (d) No error
**Answer:** (b)
**Solution:** "One of the + superlative + **plural noun**": "one of the best **players**". **Trap type:** Sound-Right Trap — the most repeated noun trap across SSC and Banking exams.
**Source:** PYQ-style

## Applying the 15/45-Second Rule

**15-Second (Fact/Rule-based):** Q5 and Q7. The moment you see "Mathematics" or "furniture" as the subject, the rule fires instantly — singular verb. No reading of the full sentence is needed beyond identifying the subject. Target: under 15 seconds.

**45-Second (Inference-based):** Q3 and Q8. For Q3 you must check the pronoun ("their") to decide whether the jury acts as one unit or as individuals — that requires reading the whole sentence and reasoning. For Q8 you must infer whether ownership is joint or separate from the singular word "house". These deserve up to 45 seconds.

---

## Key Takeaways from the Pattern

- Master the **uncountable noun list** — it answers the largest share of noun errors.
- Always locate the **real subject** before choosing a verb; ignore intervening phrases.
- For collective nouns, check the **pronoun** (its/their) — it reveals the intended meaning.
- "One of the + plural noun" and possessive-case rules are small but appear again and again — treat them as guaranteed marks.
