Questions 1–20: Easy | Questions 21–25: Medium

1. Which of the following is a proper noun?
(a) Ganga (b) city (c) river (d) mountain
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Ganga" names one specific river, so it is a proper noun and begins with a capital letter. City, river and mountain are common nouns because they refer to any member of a class.
Source: NCERT Class 6 English Grammar

2. Which of the following is an abstract noun?
(a) teacher (b) table (c) crowd (d) honesty
Answer: (d)
Solution: "Honesty" is a quality that cannot be seen or touched, so it is an abstract noun. Teacher and table are concrete nouns, and crowd is a collective noun.
Source: NCERT Class 7 English Grammar

3. Choose the collective noun from the following.
(a) book (b) happiness (c) team (d) water
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Team" stands for a group of players taken as one unit, so it is a collective noun. Book is a common noun, happiness is abstract, and water is a material noun.
Source: NCERT Class 6 English Grammar

4. Which of the following is a material noun?
(a) beauty (b) army (c) gold (d) child
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Gold" is a substance or raw material, so it is a material noun. Beauty is abstract, army is collective, and child is a common noun.
Source: NCERT Class 7 English Grammar

5. Identify the countable noun.
(a) chair (b) rice (c) milk (d) information
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Chair" can be counted — one chair, two chairs — so it is countable. Milk, rice and information are uncountable and have no plural form in ordinary use.
Source: NCERT Class 8 English Grammar

6. What is the plural of "child"?
(a) children (b) childes (c) childrens (d) childs
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Child" has an irregular plural, "children". "Childrens" is a common error because children is already plural and never takes another -s.
Source: NCERT Class 6 English Grammar

7. What is the plural of "knife"?
(a) knifes (b) knives (c) knivs (d) knife
Answer: (b)
Solution: Nouns ending in -fe usually change to -ves in the plural: knife → knives, wife → wives. "Knifes" is a frequent examiner trap.
Source: NCERT Class 7 English Grammar

8. What is the plural of "ox"?
(a) oxen (b) oxes (c) ox (d) oxies
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Ox" has the old English irregular plural "oxen". Adding -es to it is wrong.
Source: NCERT Class 6 English Grammar

9. What is the plural of "analysis"?
(a) analysises (b) analysies (c) analysis (d) analyses
Answer: (d)
Solution: Greek-origin nouns ending in -is change to -es: analysis → analyses, crisis → crises. "Analysises" is a trap option that sounds plausible but is wrong.
Source: Wren & Martin, High School English Grammar

10. Which sentence uses the noun correctly?
(a) She gave me many advices. (b) She gave me many advice. (c) She gave me much advice. (d) She gave me an advice.
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Advice" is uncountable, so it takes "much", never "many", a plural -s, or the article "an". This is one of the most repeated traps in SSC and Banking exams.
Source: Wren & Martin, High School English Grammar

11. Choose the correctly spelt plural.
(a) tomatos (b) tomato (c) tomatoes (d) tomaties
Answer: (c)
Solution: Nouns ending in -o preceded by a consonant usually add -es: tomato → tomatoes, potato → potatoes. A few like "photo" and "piano" take only -s.
Source: NCERT Class 7 English Grammar

12. "The furniture in the room ___ new." Choose the correct verb.
(a) are (b) is (c) were (d) have been
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Furniture" is an uncountable noun and always takes a singular verb, even though it refers to many items. It has no plural form "furnitures".
Source: Wren & Martin, High School English Grammar

13. Which of the following nouns is always plural in form?
(a) physics (b) news (c) mathematics (d) scissors
Answer: (d)
Solution: "Scissors" names a tool with two joined parts and is always plural — "the scissors are on the table". News, mathematics and physics look plural but take singular verbs.
Source: Wren & Martin, High School English Grammar

14. "The news ___ very shocking." Choose the correct verb.
(a) are (b) have been (c) were (d) is
Answer: (d)
Solution: "News" ends in -s but is singular and uncountable, so it takes "is". Examiners exploit the -s ending to make "are" look correct.
Source: Wren & Martin, High School English Grammar

15. Identify the abstract noun in the sentence: "Her courage saved the child."
(a) her (b) child (c) saved (d) courage
Answer: (d)
Solution: "Courage" is a quality that cannot be perceived by the senses, so it is abstract. Child is a common (concrete) noun, her is a pronoun, and saved is a verb.
Source: NCERT Class 7 English Grammar

16. What is the feminine form of "actor" commonly used today?
(a) actress (b) actorine (c) actoress (d) actrix
Answer: (a)
Solution: The traditional feminine form is "actress", though modern usage often prefers "actor" for all genders. The other forms do not exist in standard English.
Source: NCERT Class 6 English Grammar

17. Choose the correct possessive form for "the school of many boys".
(a) the boys school (b) the boy's school (c) the boys' school (d) the boys's school
Answer: (c)
Solution: For a plural noun already ending in -s, the apostrophe goes after the s: boys' school. "Boy's school" would mean the school of only one boy, and "boys's" wrongly adds a second s after a plural ending in s.
Source: Wren & Martin, High School English Grammar

18. Which of the following is an uncountable noun?
(a) pen (b) luggage (c) apple (d) student
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Luggage" is uncountable — we say "some luggage", never "luggages" or "a luggage". Pen, apple and student are ordinary countable nouns.
Source: Wren & Martin, High School English Grammar

19. "A ___ of sheep was grazing in the field." Choose the correct collective noun.
(a) flock (b) swarm (c) bunch (d) pack
Answer: (a)
Solution: Sheep move in a "flock". A swarm is of bees, a bunch is of keys or grapes, and a pack is of wolves or dogs.
Source: NCERT Class 6 English Grammar

20. What is the plural of "criterion"?
(a) criterions (b) criteria (c) criterias (d) criterion
Answer: (b)
Solution: Greek nouns ending in -on change to -a: criterion → criteria, phenomenon → phenomena. "Criterias" is a double-plural trap.
Source: Wren & Martin, High School English Grammar

21. Spot the error: "One of my friend (P) / has gone (Q) / to Delhi. (R) / No error (S)"
(a) Part P (b) Part Q (c) Part R (d) No error
Answer: (a)
Solution: After "one of", the noun must be plural — "one of my friends". Part P is wrong. This is a classic examiner trap because the verb stays singular ("has"), which makes the singular noun look acceptable.
Source: PYQ-style

22. Choose the correctly framed sentence.
(a) The sceneries of Kashmir are beautiful. (b) A scenery of Kashmir is beautiful. (c) The scenery of Kashmir are beautiful. (d) The scenery of Kashmir is beautiful.
Answer: (d)
Solution: "Scenery" is uncountable and singular, so it takes "is" and never a plural -s or the article "a". Options with "sceneries" or "are" are traps.
Source: Wren & Martin, High School English Grammar

23. Spot the error: "The police (P) / has arrested (Q) / the thief. (R) / No error (S)"
(a) Part R (b) Part P (c) Part Q (d) No error
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Police" is a plural noun and always takes a plural verb — "the police have arrested". Part Q is wrong. The trap is that police looks singular like "army" or "team".
Source: PYQ-style

24. "He gave me ___ useful piece of information." Choose the correct article.
(a) an (b) a (c) the (d) no article
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Useful" begins with a consonant sound /juː/, so it takes "a", not "an". "Information" is uncountable, so we count it with "a piece of".
Source: NCERT Class 8 English Grammar

25. Spot the error: "Mathematics (P) / are (Q) / a difficult subject. (R) / No error (S)"
(a) Part P (b) Part Q (c) Part R (d) No error
Answer: (b)
Solution: Names of subjects ending in -ics (mathematics, physics, economics) take a singular verb — "Mathematics is". Part Q is wrong; the -s ending is the examiner's trap.
Source: PYQ-style
