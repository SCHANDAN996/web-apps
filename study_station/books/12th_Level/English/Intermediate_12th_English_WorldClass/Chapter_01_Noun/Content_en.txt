# NOUN — The Name of Everything

## 🎯 Why This Chapter Decides Your Score

Look around you. Everything you can see, touch, feel, or even imagine has a **name** — *pen, Delhi, honesty, team, gold*. In English grammar, every one of these names is a **Noun**. That is why the Noun is the very first brick of English: if you cannot identify the noun in a sentence, you cannot fix its verb, its pronoun, or its article — and **subject–verb agreement, pronoun errors, and article questions** (the backbone of SSC CHSL, RRB NTPC and CGL Tier-1 grammar sections) all collapse.

Examiners know this. So they rarely ask "What is a noun?" directly. Instead, they hide noun errors inside sentences: *"The **scenery** of Kashmir **are** beautiful"* — and wait for you to slip. This chapter trains you to never slip.

---

## 🎯 Learning Objectives

By the end of this chapter you will be able to:

1. Identify all **five types of nouns** in any sentence.
2. Apply the **12 Golden Rules** of nouns to spot errors instantly.
3. Handle tricky cases: collective nouns, uncountable nouns, nouns same in singular and plural, and nouns that look plural but are singular.
4. Convert nouns correctly across **number, gender, and case**.
5. Solve error-spotting and fill-in-the-blank questions within the 15/45-second rule.

---

## 1. What is a Noun?

A **Noun** is a word used as the **name of a person, place, animal, thing, quality, action, or idea**.

- Person → *teacher, doctor, Ravi*
- Place → *Agra, market, school*
- Animal → *tiger, cow*
- Thing → *table, book, phone*
- Quality/Idea/Feeling → *honesty, courage, happiness*

**Quick test:** If a word can answer *"Who?"* or *"What?"* in a sentence, it is usually a noun.
*Ravi bought a car.* → Who? **Ravi**. What? **car**. Both are nouns.

## 2. The Five Types of Nouns

### (a) Proper Noun — the SPECIAL name
Names one particular person, place, or thing. **Always begins with a capital letter.**
*India, Mahatma Gandhi, the Ganga, Monday, January.*

### (b) Common Noun — the GENERAL name
Names any member of a class. *boy, city, river, book.*
(*boy* = any boy; *Ravi* = one particular boy → proper.)

### (c) Collective Noun — the GROUP name
Names a group or collection treated as one unit.
*army, team, jury, committee, flock, bunch, crowd, fleet, family.*

### (d) Material Noun — the SUBSTANCE name
Names matter or raw material from which things are made.
*gold, water, rice, iron, milk, cotton, wood.*

### (e) Abstract Noun — the INVISIBLE name
Names a quality, state, action, or idea you **cannot touch or see**.
*honesty, kindness, childhood, freedom, anger, knowledge.*

**How abstract nouns are formed:**
| From | Word | Abstract Noun |
|---|---|---|
| Adjective | kind | kindness |
| Adjective | honest | honesty |
| Verb | choose | choice |
| Verb | live | life |
| Noun | child | childhood |

### 🧠 Active Recall
Classify: *jury, silver, bravery, Mumbai, girl*. Which type is each? Which one can you not touch?

## 3. Countable vs Uncountable — The Battlefield of Exams

This is where most marks are won or lost.

**Countable nouns** can be counted and have plural forms: *book/books, boy/boys, chair/chairs.*

**Uncountable nouns** cannot be counted. They have **no plural form** and take a **singular verb**. The exam's favourite uncountable nouns:

> **Furniture, luggage, baggage, information, advice, news, knowledge, work, homework, scenery, poetry, machinery, crockery, stationery, bread, hair (all the hair on the head), evidence, progress, traffic, money, cash.**

❌ *He gave me many advices.* ✅ *He gave me much advice / many pieces of advice.*
❌ *The sceneries of Shimla are lovely.* ✅ *The scenery of Shimla is lovely.*

**To count them, use a unit word:** *a piece of advice, an item of news, a loaf of bread, a sheet of paper, a bar of soap.*

### 🧠 Active Recall
Why is "informations" wrong? How would you say "three breads" correctly? Is "news" singular or plural?

## 4. Number — Singular and Plural Traps

**Regular plurals:** add *-s* (boy→boys), *-es* after s, sh, ch, x, o (bus→buses, brush→brushes, watch→watches, box→boxes, mango→mangoes), change *-y* to *-ies* after a consonant (city→cities, but boy→boys), and *-f/-fe* often becomes *-ves* (knife→knives, wife→wives, leaf→leaves — but *chief→chiefs, proof→proofs*).

**Irregular plurals to memorise:**
| Singular | Plural |
|---|---|
| man | men |
| woman | women |
| child | children |
| tooth | teeth |
| foot | feet |
| mouse | mice |
| ox | oxen |
| criterion | criteria |
| phenomenon | phenomena |
| analysis | analyses |
| datum | data |
| medium | media |

**Nouns SAME in singular and plural** (no change): *sheep, deer, fish, aircraft, series, species, dozen (after a number), score (after a number), offspring.*
✅ *Two dozen eggs.* ❌ *Two dozens eggs.* (But: *dozens of eggs* is correct.)

**Nouns that LOOK plural but are SINGULAR:** *news, physics, mathematics, economics, politics, ethics, measles, mumps, billiards, athletics, innings.*
✅ *Mathematics is difficult.* ✅ *The news is good.*

**Nouns ALWAYS plural (take plural verb, no singular form):** *scissors, trousers, pants, spectacles, goggles, pliers, tongs, wages, premises, belongings, earnings, surroundings, goods, cattle, people, police, poultry, gentry, clergy.*
✅ *The police have arrested him.* ❌ *The police has…*
✅ *Cattle are grazing.* ❌ *Cattles are grazing.*

**Compound noun plurals** — pluralise the MAIN word: *brother-in-law → brothers-in-law, commander-in-chief → commanders-in-chief, passer-by → passers-by.*

## 5. Gender — Four Types

1. **Masculine** — male: *king, boy, lion*
2. **Feminine** — female: *queen, girl, lioness*
3. **Common** — either: *teacher, student, friend, doctor*
4. **Neuter** — non-living: *table, book, stone*

**High-yield gender pairs:** *actor–actress, host–hostess, waiter–waitress, prince–princess, hero–heroine, bachelor–spinster, monk–nun, wizard–witch, landlord–landlady, nephew–niece, bull–cow, horse–mare, dog–bitch, fox–vixen, gander–goose, drake–duck, stag–hind, cock–hen.*

## 6. Case — The Possessive (Apostrophe) Rules

**Case** shows the noun's relation to other words. The tested one is the **Possessive Case**:

- Singular noun → add **'s**: *Ravi's book, the girl's dress.*
- Plural ending in -s → add only **'**: *boys' school, girls' hostel.*
- Plural NOT ending in -s → add **'s**: *children's park, men's room.*
- **Joint possession** → apostrophe on the LAST name: *Ravi and Mohan's shop* (one shared shop).
- **Separate possession** → apostrophe on EACH: *Ravi's and Mohan's shops* (two shops).
- **Double possessive is wrong:** ❌ *Ravi's brother's wife's sister* — rewrite as *the sister of the wife of Ravi's brother*.

⚠️ **Apostrophe is NOT used with lifeless things** (use *of*):
❌ *the table's leg* ✅ *the leg of the table*
❌ *India's population* is accepted in modern usage, but in exams prefer *the population of India*.
**Exception:** time, distance, and personified nouns take 's — *a day's work, a stone's throw, nature's beauty, fortune's favourite.*

## ⚠️ EXAMINER'S TRAP BOX

**Trap 1 — The Intervening Phrase:** *"The quality of the apples **was/were** good."* The subject is *quality* (singular), not *apples*. Examiners insert a plural noun right before the verb to pull your eye. ✅ **was**.

**Trap 2 — Sound-Right Errors:** *"He gave me many **advices**"* sounds fine to Hindi ears ("उसने मुझे बहुत सलाहें दीं") but *advice* is uncountable. If it "sounds right," check it twice.

**Trap 3 — Collective Noun Confusion:** *"The jury **has/have** given its verdict."* When the group acts as ONE unit → singular (*has*). When members act SEPARATELY → plural (*The jury are divided in their opinion*). Examiners swap these deliberately.

## 🏆 THE 12 GOLDEN RULES OF NOUNS

1. Uncountable nouns (*advice, information, furniture, news, scenery, luggage, work, knowledge*) have **no plural** and take a **singular verb**.
2. *News, physics, mathematics, measles, billiards, innings* look plural but are **singular**.
3. *Scissors, trousers, spectacles, wages, belongings, premises* are **always plural**.
4. *Police, cattle, people, poultry, gentry, clergy* are always **plural**; never add -s to *cattle/people*.
5. *Sheep, deer, fish, series, species, aircraft* have the **same form** in singular and plural.
6. After a definite number, *dozen, score, hundred, thousand* stay **singular**: *two hundred rupees*; but *hundreds of people*.
7. A **collective noun** takes a singular verb when the group acts as one, plural when members act individually.
8. Use **'s** for living things; use **of** for lifeless things (*the roof of the house*).
9. In compound nouns, pluralise the **main word**: *mothers-in-law, passers-by*.
10. *Hair* (all hair on the head) is singular: *Her hair is black.* *Hairs* = a few individual strands.
11. *Work* = job/task (uncountable); *works* = writings/factory (*the works of Shakespeare, a steel works*). Same with *wood/woods, manner/manners, custom/customs, quarter/quarters* — meaning changes with number.
12. Two nouns joined by *and* meaning ONE thing take a **singular verb**: *Rice and curry is my favourite dish. The poet and philosopher is dead.* (one person) vs *The poet and the philosopher are dead.* (two persons — note the repeated *the*).

## 🔁 LINGUISTIC BRIDGE — Hindi से English

| हिंदी में सोच | ❌ Hindi-speaker's English | ✅ Correct English |
|---|---|---|
| उसने मुझे बहुत सलाहें दीं | He gave me many advices. | He gave me a lot of advice. |
| पुलिस ने उसे पकड़ लिया | The police has caught him. | The police have caught him. |
| उसके बाल काले हैं | Her hairs are black. | Her hair is black. |
| मेरे पास दो सौ रुपये हैं | I have two hundreds rupees. | I have two hundred rupees. |
| समाचार अच्छा है | The news are good. | The news is good. |
| मेज़ का पैर | the table's leg | the leg of the table |

**Why does this happen?** Hindi happily pluralises *सलाहें, बाल, समाचार* in speech, and Hindi has no apostrophe. Train yourself: before writing an English plural, ask — *"Can I count it one-two-three in English?"*

## 🧠 MNEMONIC — "F-L-I-N-S-K" (फ्लिन्स्क)

To remember the five most-tested **uncountable nouns**, remember the spy **FLINSK**:

> **F**urniture – **L**uggage – **I**nformation – **N**ews – **S**cenery – **K**nowledge
> *"FLINSK the spy carries no plurals — his luggage, news and knowledge are all singular!"*
