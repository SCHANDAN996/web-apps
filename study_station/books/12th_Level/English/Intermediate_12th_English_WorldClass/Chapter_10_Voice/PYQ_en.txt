# 📊 PYQ Analysis — Active & Passive Voice

## How Exams Test This Chapter (Pattern Analysis)

Voice questions are a permanent fixture in SSC (CGL, CHSL, MTS, GD), Banking (IBPS, SBI), and Railway (NTPC, Group D) exams. They appear in two formats:

1. **Direct conversion** — "Choose the correct passive form of the given sentence."
2. **Embedded in Error Spotting / Sentence Improvement** — where the voice itself is wrong (e.g., "The letter has wrote" instead of "has been written").

**What repeats most often:**
- Present/Past/Future **Perfect** tenses in passive (has been + V3, had been + V3) — the single most tested area.
- **Modals** in passive (can be + V3, should be + V3, must be + V3).
- **Interrogative** sentences in passive (Who/Why/When + helping verb + subject + V3).
- **Imperative** sentences ("Let + object + be + V3").
- **Preposition retention** — verbs like *laughed at, listened to, looked after* must keep their preposition in passive.
- Sentences that **cannot** be passivized (intransitive verbs: *go, come, sleep, die*).

**The examiner's favourite traps:**
- **Tense drift:** the passive option changes the tense slightly (e.g., "is written" vs "was written"). Read the original tense twice.
- **Missing preposition:** options drop the preposition of a phrasal verb ("He was laughed" instead of "He was laughed at").
- **Wrong V3 form:** options use V2 instead of V3 ("was wrote" instead of "was written").
- **Over-thinking:** a simple active sentence is offered with a needlessly complex passive ("is being been written"). The simplest grammatically correct option usually wins.

## Top 10 High-Yield Question Types (with Solutions)

**Q1. Active: "She writes a letter." Choose the correct passive.**
(a) A letter is written by her. (b) A letter was written by her. (c) A letter is wrote by her. (d) A letter has written by her.
**Answer: (a)**
**Solution:** Simple Present → is/am/are + V3. "Writes" → "is written". (b) changes tense; (c) uses V2 "wrote" instead of V3 "written"; (d) drops "been".
**Trap type:** Wrong V3 form.
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q2. Active: "The teacher has praised the boy."**
(a) The boy has praised by the teacher. (b) The boy has been praised by the teacher. (c) The boy had been praised by the teacher. (d) The boy is praised by the teacher.
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** Present Perfect passive = has/have + **been** + V3. (a) omits "been"; (c) shifts to Past Perfect; (d) shifts to Simple Present.
**Trap type:** Tense drift.
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q3. Active: "They laughed at the beggar."**
(a) The beggar was laughed by them. (b) The beggar was laughed at by them. (c) The beggar is laughed at by them. (d) The beggar had laughed at by them.
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** Phrasal verb "laughed at" keeps its preposition in passive. Simple Past → was/were + V3.
**Trap type:** Missing preposition.
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q4. Active: "Who taught you English?"**
(a) By whom was you taught English? (b) By whom were you taught English? (c) Who was taught you English? (d) By whom you were taught English?
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** "Who" (subject) → "By whom"; "you" takes "were"; question order = helping verb before subject. (a) has wrong agreement; (c) destroys structure; (d) lacks inversion.
**Trap type:** Sound-right (option (a) sounds plausible but violates agreement).
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q5. Active: "Open the door."**
(a) The door is opened. (b) Let the door be opened. (c) The door should opened. (d) The door was opened.
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** Imperative sentences → "Let + object + be + V3". Other options change the sentence from a command to a statement.
**Trap type:** Over-thinking.
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q6. Active: "You can solve this problem."**
(a) This problem can solved by you. (b) This problem can be solved by you. (c) This problem could be solve by you. (d) This problem can be solve by you.
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** Modal passive = modal + **be** + V3. (a) drops "be"; (c) changes modal and uses V1; (d) uses V1 "solve" instead of V3 "solved".
**Trap type:** Wrong verb form after modal.
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q7. Active: "Someone stole my watch yesterday."**
(a) My watch was stolen yesterday. (b) My watch is stolen yesterday. (c) My watch had stolen yesterday. (d) My watch has been stolen yesterday.
**Answer: (a)**
**Solution:** When the doer is vague (someone/people/they), drop "by + agent". "Yesterday" fixes Simple Past → "was stolen". (d) is wrong because Present Perfect cannot take a definite past time like "yesterday".
**Trap type:** Tense drift + unnecessary agent.
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q8. Active: "The mother is looking after the child."**
(a) The child is looked after by the mother. (b) The child is being looked after by the mother. (c) The child is being looked by the mother. (d) The child has been looked after by the mother.
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** Present Continuous passive = is/am/are + **being** + V3; preposition "after" retained. (a) drops "being"; (c) drops the preposition; (d) changes tense.
**Trap type:** Missing preposition + tense drift.
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q9. Active: "People speak English all over the world."**
(a) English is spoken all over the world. (b) English is spoke all over the world. (c) English was spoken all over the world. (d) English is being spoken by people all over the world.
**Answer: (a)**
**Solution:** Simple Present → is + V3 ("spoken"). Vague agent "people" is dropped. (b) uses V2 "spoke"; (c) changes tense; (d) adds an unnecessary agent and changes the structure.
**Trap type:** Over-thinking (option (d) looks elaborate but is inferior).
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q10. Active: "He will have finished the work by evening."**
(a) The work will have been finished by him by evening. (b) The work will be finished by him by evening. (c) The work would have been finished by him. (d) The work will have finished by him.
**Answer: (a)**
**Solution:** Future Perfect passive = will have + **been** + V3. (b) is Future Simple; (c) changes modal; (d) drops "been".
**Trap type:** Tense drift.
**Source:** PYQ-style

## Applying the 15/45-Second Rule

**15-second question (Fact-based): Q1** — "She writes a letter." The moment you see Simple Present, your formula (is/am/are + V3) eliminates (b), (c), (d) instantly. No reading of all options needed beyond checking the V3 form. Target: under 15 seconds.

**45-second question (Careful-check): Q7** — "Someone stole my watch yesterday." Here you must (1) identify the vague agent and drop it, (2) lock the tense using the time marker "yesterday", and (3) reject the tempting Present Perfect option (d). Three checks = up to 45 seconds. Time markers like *yesterday, since, for, by evening* always push a question into the 45-second category.

**Rule of thumb:** Single-clause, no time marker, standard tense → 15 seconds. Time markers, vague agents, interrogatives, or phrasal verbs → 45 seconds.

---

## Key Takeaways from the Pattern

- Master the **Perfect tense passives** first — they dominate.
- Never drop the **preposition** of a phrasal verb in passive.
- "By whom" questions and "Let…be" imperatives appear regularly — memorise their fixed frames.
- When two options look right, pick the one that **preserves the original tense exactly** — tense drift is the examiner's favourite trap.
