# 📋 Important Grammar Rules & Tips — Active & Passive Voice

## (a) 12 High-Yield Rules

| # | Rule | Example | Examiner's Trap |
|---|------|---------|-----------------|
| 1 | Only **transitive verbs** (verbs with an object) can be made passive. Intransitive verbs (go, sleep, die, arrive) have **no passive form**. | Active: She sings a song. → Passive: A song is sung by her. | Trap: "The accident was happened." ❌ (happen is intransitive) → "The accident happened." ✅ |
| 2 | The **object** of the active sentence becomes the **subject** of the passive; the active subject becomes the agent with **by** (often dropped if unimportant). | Ram killed the snake. → The snake was killed by Ram. | Trap: Keeping the object as object — "The snake was killed Ram." ❌ |
| 3 | Passive structure = **be (is/am/are/was/were/been/being) + V3 (past participle)**. The "be" verb matches the **new subject** and the **tense** of the active verb. | He writes letters. → Letters are written by him. | Trap: Using V2 instead of V3 — "Letters are wrote." ❌ |
| 4 | **Present Continuous** passive: is/am/are + being + V3. | She is cooking food. → Food is being cooked by her. | Trap: "Food is cooked by her" (changes tense) ❌ |
| 5 | **Present Perfect** passive: has/have + been + V3. | They have sold the house. → The house has been sold by them. | Trap: "The house has being sold." ❌ (been, not being) |
| 6 | **Past Perfect** passive: had + been + V3. | He had finished the work. → The work had been finished by him. | Trap: "The work had being finished." ❌ |
| 7 | **Modals** passive: modal + be + V3 (can, may, must, should, will, shall). | You must obey the rules. → The rules must be obeyed. | Trap: "The rules must been obeyed." ❌ |
| 8 | **Modal perfect** passive: modal + have + been + V3. | Someone may have stolen it. → It may have been stolen. | Trap: "It may have being stolen." ❌ |
| 9 | **Imperative sentences**: "Let + object + be + V3." For negative orders: "Let + object + not + be + V3." | Open the door. → Let the door be opened. | Trap: "Let the door be open." ❌ (V3 needed: opened) |
| 10 | **Preposition attached to the verb must stay with it** in passive. | She looks after the children. → The children are looked after by her. | Trap: Dropping the preposition — "The children are looked by her." ❌ |
| 11 | **Double-object verbs** (give, send, teach, offer) allow two passives; the **person as subject** is preferred in exams. | He gave me a book. → I was given a book by him. / A book was given to me by him. | Trap: "A book was given me." ❌ (missing "to") |
| 12 | **Quasi-passive / stative verbs** (know, contain, resemble, lack, suit) and verbs like "let" behave specially: "It is known to me" (not "by me"); "let" → "allowed to". | He knows me. → I am known to him. | Trap: "I am known by him." ❌ |

## (b) Time Management — The 15/45-Second Rule

**Fact-based questions (15 seconds — direct rule application):**
- Simple tense conversion (Present/Past Simple, Future Simple)
- Modal + be + V3 conversions
- Identifying the correct passive form of a given active sentence
- Choosing the correct V3 form in a passive sentence
- Imperative → "Let + be + V3" conversions

**Inference-based questions (45 seconds — need careful checking):**
- Questions mixing tense + preposition traps (looked after, cut off)
- Double-object verbs where both passives look plausible
- Quasi-passive verbs (known to, contained in) where "by" tempts you
- Sentences where the agent must be dropped or changed (somebody, people, they)
- Spot-the-error questions where the passive is hidden inside a long sentence

**Strategy:** If the sentence has one verb and one clear object → 15-second rule recall. If it has modals + perfect forms, attached prepositions, or two objects → slow down to 45 seconds and check each element.

## (c) Morphology — Root, Affix, Example

| Root/Affix | Meaning | Example in Voice Context |
|---|---|---|
| **act** (Latin: agere) | to do, drive | **act**ive = the doer performs the action |
| **pass** (Latin: pati) | to suffer, undergo | **pass**ive = the subject receives the action |
| **trans-** (prefix) | across | **trans**itive verb = action crosses over to an object |
| **in-** (prefix) | not | **in**transitive verb = action does not cross to an object |
| **-ive** (suffix) | having the quality of | act**ive**, pass**ive** |
| **agent** (Latin: agens) | one who acts | the "by-agent" in passive sentences |
| **particip-** (Latin: particeps) | sharing, taking part | past **participle** (V3) — the heart of every passive |
| **object** (Latin: ob + jacere) | thrown before | the receiver of action; becomes passive subject |

**Memory hook:** *Trans*itive = action **travels across** to an object → can go passive. *In*transitive = action **stays inside** the subject → no passive possible.
