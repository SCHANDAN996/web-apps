# Active & Passive Voice — The Power Switch of English Grammar

## 🎯 Why This Chapter Matters

Imagine two headlines:

- **"Police arrested the thief."**
- **"The thief was arrested."**

Same event. Same facts. Yet the *focus* shifts completely — in the first, the police matter; in the second, the thief matters. That single shift of focus is called **Voice (वाच्य)**, and examiners at SSC CHSL, CGL Tier-1, RRB NTPC and IBPS love it because it tests three skills at once: tense knowledge, verb forms, and sentence logic.

Voice questions appear regularly — as direct conversion ("Change the voice"), as error spotting ("was been killed" ❌), and as fill-in-the-blanks. Master this chapter once, and these become your fastest, safest marks.

---

## 📌 Learning Objectives

By the end of this chapter you will be able to:

1. Identify whether a sentence is Active or Passive in under 5 seconds.
2. Convert any tense from Active to Passive (and back) using a fixed formula.
3. Handle special cases — modals, imperatives, questions, infinitives, and two-object sentences.
4. Spot the examiner's favourite traps (by-agent confusion, missing "been", wrong V3).
5. Know **when NOT to convert** — sentences with intransitive verbs.

## 1. What Is Voice?

**Voice** tells us whether the **subject performs** the action or **receives** it.

| Voice | Structure | Example | Focus |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Active** | Subject + Verb + Object | *Ravi wrote a letter.* | Doer (Ravi) |
| **Passive** | Object + be + V3 (+ by + Subject) | *A letter was written by Ravi.* | Receiver (letter) |

The golden transformation:

> **Object of Active → Subject of Passive**
> **Main verb → be + V3 (past participle)**
> **Subject of Active → "by + agent" (often dropped)**

🧠 **Active Recall:** In "The cake was eaten by the children," who performs the action? What is the grammatical subject? *(Answer: children perform it; "the cake" is the subject.)*

---

## 2. The Universal Passive Formula

Memorise this one line and most of the chapter is done:

> **PASSIVE = Object + (be in the tense of the active verb) + V3 + (by + agent)**

The verb **"be"** is a shape-shifter — it copies the tense of the original active verb:

| Tense | Active | Passive |
|---|---|---|
| Present Simple | She **writes** letters. | Letters **are written** by her. |
| Present Continuous | She **is writing** letters. | Letters **are being written** by her. |
| Present Perfect | She **has written** letters. | Letters **have been written** by her. |
| Past Simple | She **wrote** letters. | Letters **were written** by her. |
| Past Continuous | She **was writing** letters. | Letters **were being written** by her. |
| Past Perfect | She **had written** letters. | Letters **had been written** by her. |
| Future Simple | She **will write** letters. | Letters **will be written** by her. |
| Modals | She **can write** letters. | Letters **can be written** by her. |

⚠️ **Notice:** Perfect Continuous tenses (has been writing, had been writing) and Future Continuous (will be writing) are **NOT converted** into passive in standard exam English.

🧠 **Active Recall:** Convert — "The teacher has praised the boy." *(Answer: The boy has been praised by the teacher.)*

---

## 3. The Three Musketeers of Passive: BE + BEING + BEEN

- **BE (is/am/are/was/were)** → Simple tenses
- **BEING** → Continuous tenses
- **BEEN** → Perfect tenses

🎵 **Mnemonic — "BIN" rule:** **B**e = Simple, **I** (being) = Continuous, **N** (been) = Perfect. *"Simple BE, Continuous BEING, Perfect BEEN."*

🧠 **Active Recall:** Which helper word signals a perfect tense in passive? *(Answer: been.)*

---

## 4. Special Cases the Examiner Loves

**(a) Two objects (direct + indirect):** Either object can become the passive subject.
- Active: *He gave **me** a **book**.*
- Passive 1: *I was given a book by him.* ✅ (preferred)
- Passive 2: *A book was given to me by him.* ✅

**(b) Imperative sentences:** Use **"Let + object + be + V3."**
- *Open the door.* → *Let the door be opened.*
- With advice/orders: *You are ordered/advised/requested to…*

**(c) Questions:** Keep the question structure.
- *Did she cook food?* → *Was food cooked by her?*
- *Why did he beat you?* → *Why were you beaten by him?*

**(d) Infinitive:** *to + V1* → *to + be + V3*
- *I want someone to help me.* → *I want to be helped.*

**(e) Preposition-verbs:** The preposition stays glued to the verb.
- *They laughed at the beggar.* → *The beggar was laughed at by them.* (never "was laughed by")

**(f) "By" is dropped** when the agent is unknown, obvious, or unimportant:
- *Someone stole my watch.* → *My watch was stolen.* (not "by someone")

🧠 **Active Recall:** Convert — "Shut the window." *(Answer: Let the window be shut.)*

---

## 5. When Conversion Is IMPOSSIBLE

**Intransitive verbs** (verbs with no object — go, come, sleep, run, die, arrive, cry) cannot become passive. No object = nothing to promote to subject.

- *He sleeps.* → ❌ No passive possible.
- *The baby cried.* → ❌ No passive possible.

This is a classic "which sentence CANNOT be changed into passive?" question.

---

## ⚠️ EXAMINER'S TRAP BOX

> **Trap 1 — The Missing "been":** "The work has *being* done" ❌ / "The work has *been* done" ✅. Perfect passive always needs **been**, never *being*.
>
> **Trap 2 — "By" everywhere:** "The match was won *by them*" is fine, but "My purse has been stolen *by someone*" is wrong — drop the vague agent.
>
> **Trap 3 — V2 instead of V3:** "The letter was *wrote*" ❌ → "was *written*" ✅. Passive always takes the **third form (V3)** — watch irregular verbs: wrote→written, broke→broken, chose→chosen.

## 🔁 LINGUISTIC BRIDGE — Hindi vs English

| Hindi speaker says ❌ | Correct English ✅ | Why |
|---|---|---|
| "The thief has been *catch* by police." | The thief has been **caught** by the police. | Passive needs V3, not V1. |
| "The work is *being did*." | The work is **being done**. | After being/been, always V3. |
| "By me a letter was written." (हिंदी क्रम: मेरे द्वारा पत्र लिखा गया) | A letter was written **by me**. | English keeps Subject–Verb–Agent order. |
| "He was *laughed* by everyone." | He was **laughed at** by everyone. | Preposition must stay with the verb. |

## 📊 Summary Table — One-Look Revision

| Item | Rule |
|---|---|
| Core formula | Object + be(tense) + V3 + by agent |
| Simple tenses | is/am/are/was/were + V3 |
| Continuous | is/am/are/was/were + **being** + V3 |
| Perfect | has/have/had + **been** + V3 |
| Modals | modal + **be** + V3 |
| Imperative | Let + object + be + V3 |
| No passive | Intransitive verbs; Perfect Continuous; Future Continuous |
| Agent dropped | Unknown / obvious / unimportant doer |
