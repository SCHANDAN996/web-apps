126. The word "antebellum" most nearly means:
(a) Against war (b) After the war (c) Fierce in war (d) Before the war
Answer: (d)
Solution: The tempting option "against war" confuses "ante-" (before) with "anti-" (against) — a classic examiner trap. "Bellum" is Latin for war (cf. bellicose, rebellion), and "ante-" means before (cf. antecedent, anterior), so the answer is "before the war."
Source: PYQ-style

127. Choose the word that correctly completes the sentence: "The scientist's theory was so ______ that no one could disprove it for decades."
(a) Irrefutable (b) Refulgent (c) Mutable (d) Refutable
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Refutable" means able to be disproved, the exact opposite of what the sentence needs. "Irrefutable" = in- (not) + refutable (able to be disproved), so the answer is "irrefutable."
Source: PYQ-style

128. Identify the root and meaning in "circumlocution":
(a) Circum = with; locut = light (b) Circum = circle; loc = place (c) Circum = against; loqui = law (d) Circum = around; loqui = to speak
Answer: (d)
Solution: The option "circle/place" mixes the root "loc" (place, as in location) with "loqui" (to speak, as in eloquent, loquacious). Circumlocution is "speaking around" a point instead of directly, so the answer is "circum = around; loqui = to speak."
Source: PYQ-style

129. Spot the error: "The doctor (P)/ advised the patient (Q)/ to take complete rest (R)/ since two days. (S)"
(a) No error (b) Part R (c) Part P (d) Part Q
Answer: (b)
Solution: Many candidates accept Part R because "since" and "for" are often confused in everyday speech, but "since" is used with a point of time (since Monday) while a duration like "two days" requires "for". The error lies in Part R, so the answer is "Part R."
Source: PYQ-style

130. "Misanthrope" is to "philanthropist" as:
(a) Doctor is to patient (b) War is to peace (c) Enemy is to friend (d) Hater of mankind is to lover of mankind
Answer: (d)
Solution: "Enemy/friend" is a generic antonym pair and ignores the shared root "anthropos" (mankind). "Mis-" = hatred, "phil-" = love; both words are built on the same root, so the answer is "hater of mankind is to lover of mankind."
Source: PYQ-style

131. Which word means "a government ruled by the people"?
(a) Autocracy (b) Bureaucracy (c) Democracy (d) Theocracy
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Autocracy" (auto = self, one ruler) is tempting because it is the most commonly tested word, but the question asks about rule by the people. "Demos" = people, "cracy" = rule, so the answer is "democracy."

132. The prefix in "postmortem" and "postpone" means:
(a) Against (b) Before (c) After (d) Together
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Before" is the trap — it is the meaning of "pre-", not "post-". "Post" = after: postmortem (after death), postpone (put off to after), so the answer is "after."

135. Spot the error: "Being an omnivorous animal, (P)/ the bear eats (Q)/ both plants and (R)/ flesh of other animals. (S)"
(a) Part P (b) No error (c) Part R (d) Part Q
Answer: (b)
Solution: Candidates often flag Part P thinking "omnivorous" is misused, but "omni" (all) + "vorare" (to devour) correctly describes an animal eating both plants and flesh. The sentence is correct, so the answer is "No error."

136. Which of the following words does NOT share the root "port" (to carry)?
(a) Portfolio (b) Portion (c) Transport (d) Export
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Portfolio" is tempting because it looks unrelated, but it originally meant a case for carrying (port) loose sheets (folio). "Portion" comes from "portio" (a share/part), unrelated to carrying, so the answer is "portion."

137. "Equanimity" (equi = equal, animus = mind) means:

Answer: (b)
Solution: "Equal strength" is the trap — it translates "equi" correctly but ignores "animus" (mind/spirit, cf. unanimous, animated). Equanimity is an even, balanced mind under stress, so the answer is "calmness of mind."

139. The suffix "-ology" in "biology, geology, psychology" means:
(a) Fear of (b) Study of (c) Love of (d) Rule of
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Love of" is the trap — that is the meaning of "-phile/-philia" (bibliophile). "Logos" gives "-ology" = the study or science of something, so the answer is "study of."

140. "His ______ memory allowed him to recall every detail of the meeting." (root: ten/tain = to hold)
(a) Repentant (b) Reticent (c) Retentive (d) Resilient
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Reticent" (silent, reserved) is the classic trap because it looks and sounds similar to "retentive". "Retentive" = re- + tenere (to hold), i.e., holding information well, so the answer is "retentive."

141. Spot the error: "The audience was (P)/ requested to maintain (Q)/ decorum during the (R)/ valedictory function. (S)"

Answer: (a)
Solution: Many candidates hunt for a vocabulary error in Part R, but "valedictory" (vale = farewell + dicere = to say) is correctly used, and "audience" as a collective noun acting as one body rightly takes the singular verb "was". The sentence is grammatically sound, so the answer is "No error."

Answer: (d)

148. Spot the error: "The committee (P)/ has submitted (Q)/ its unanimous verdict (R)/ to the chairman yesterday. (S)"
(a) Part Q (b) No error (c) Part P (d) Part R
Answer: (a)
Solution: Candidates often flag Part R thinking "unanimous" (unus = one, animus = mind) is wrongly used, but it is correct. The real error is in Part Q: with the time marker "yesterday", the present perfect "has submitted" must become the simple past "submitted", so the answer is "Part Q."
Source: PYQ-style

149. Which pair of words shares the root "ject" (to throw)?
(a) Join, junction (b) Jovial, jubilant (c) Judge, judicial (d) Project, eject
Answer: (d)
Solution: "Join, junction" is tempting because both are genuine root-relatives, but their root is "jungere" (to join), not "ject". "Project" = throw forward, "eject" = throw out, so the answer is "project, eject."
Source: PYQ-style

150. Read the line and infer: "The politician's speech was pure circumlocution — after twenty minutes, the audience still had no answer." The word "circumlocution" here implies the speech was:
(a) Indirect and evasive (b) Loud and aggressive (c) Brief and clear (d) Full of factual errors
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Full of factual errors" is the trap — the passage never mentions falsehoods, only the absence of an answer. "Circum" (around) + "loqui" (speak) = talking around the point, i.e., indirect and evasive, so the answer is "indirect and evasive."
Source: PYQ-style
