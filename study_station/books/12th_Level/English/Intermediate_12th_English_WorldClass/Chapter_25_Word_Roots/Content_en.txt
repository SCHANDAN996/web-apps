# Word Roots & Morphology — The Secret Code of English Vocabulary

## 🔓 The Hook: One Root = Fifty Words

Imagine you are in the exam hall. A question asks the meaning of **"benevolent"** — a word you have never seen before. Most students panic. But you smile, because you know one small secret: **bene = good** (as in *benefit*), and **vol = wish** (as in *voluntary*). Good + wish = "wishing good for others" = kind, generous. Answer locked in 10 seconds — without memorising a single dictionary page.

That is the power of **Word Roots**. English has borrowed lakhs of words from **Greek and Latin**. Learn just 50–60 common roots, and you can decode **thousands** of words — including words you have never seen. For SSC CHSL, CGL, RRB NTPC and Banking exams, where vocabulary questions (synonyms, antonyms, one-word substitution, cloze test) carry heavy marks, roots are your biggest shortcut.

## 🧱 What is Morphology? (The Anatomy of a Word)

**Morphology** is the study of how words are built. Every complex English word is like a Lego model — made of small blocks:

| Block | Name | Job | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Root** | The core | Carries the main meaning | *dict* = say |
| **Prefix** | Added before | Changes direction/meaning | *pre-* = before |
| **Suffix** | Added after | Changes word class (noun/verb/adj.) | *-ion* = makes a noun |

**Example:** **PRE + DICT + ION** = "the act of saying before" = *prediction*.

One more: **UN + BELIEVE + ABLE** = *unbelievable* = "not able to be believed."

🧠 **Active Recall:** Break the word **"transport"** into its parts. (*trans-* = across, *port* = carry → "carry across".) Now guess: **export**, **import**, **portable**.

## 🏛️ The 10 Golden Roots (Memorise These First)

These 10 roots appear again and again in SSC, Railway and Bank exams:

| Root | Meaning | Derived Words |
|---|---|---|
| **dict** (Latin) | say, speak | predict, dictate, verdict, dictionary, contradict |
| **port** (Latin) | carry | transport, export, import, portable, support |
| **spect** (Latin) | see, look | inspect, respect, spectator, prospect, spectacle |
| **scrib/script** (Latin) | write | describe, manuscript, prescription, inscription |
| **cred** (Latin) | believe | credible, incredible, credit, credulous, creed |
| **bene** (Latin) | good, well | benefit, benevolent, beneficial, benediction |
| **mal** (Latin) | bad, evil | malfunction, malicious, malnutrition, malaria |
| **chrono** (Greek) | time | chronology, chronic, synchronize, anachronism |
| **bio** (Greek) | life | biology, biography, antibiotic, biodegradable |
| **geo** (Greek) | earth | geography, geology, geometry, geothermal |

**Memory trick:** Roots travel in **families of opposites** — *bene* (good) vs *mal* (bad); *pre-* (before) vs *post-* (after); *micro* (small) vs *mega* (large). Learn them in pairs and you double your score with half the effort.

🧠 **Active Recall:** Without looking back — what do **cred**, **mal**, and **chrono** mean? Write one word from each root.

## 🧩 Mnemonic: "The PORT-able DICT-ionary"

> **"A CREDible BIO-grapher will PORT (carry) his DICT-ionary across the GEO (earth) through CHRONO (time)."**

Read this silly sentence twice. It locks **six roots** — *cred, bio, port, dict, geo, chrono* — into one picture: a writer carrying a dictionary across the earth through time. Silly images stick; plain lists don't.

## 📖 Roots in Action: A Short Reading Passage

*Read this passage. Notice how the bold words all come from your root table.*

> When the **biographer** began to **describe** the life of Dr. Rao, she found his old **manuscript** in a broken box. The papers were **credible** evidence of his **benevolent** work — he had carried (**port**) free medicines to remote villages for decades. His **chronicle** of rural life, written in clear **geographical** detail, earned him the **respect** of every **spectator** at the award ceremony. Critics called his achievements **incredible**, but the **verdict** of history was clear: one man's good will had **transported** hope across an entire region.

**Now test yourself:** *benevolent, credible, verdict, spectator, chronicle* — can you state each meaning from its root alone? This is exactly how you will handle unknown words in the exam's cloze test and comprehension passages: **don't panic, dissect**.

🧠 **Active Recall:** In the passage, "incredible" was used positively ("amazing"). What is its literal root meaning? (*in-* = not + *cred* = believe → "not believable".)

## ⚠️ Examiner's Trap Box

**Trap 1 — Look-alike roots:** *"Mortal"* and *"mortal combat"* make students link **mort** (death) with **moral** (character). **Mortify** = to humiliate (literally "to make dead" with shame), not "to make moral." Similar trap pair: **stationary** (still) vs **stationery** (pens, paper).

**Trap 2 — The prefix flip:** *in-* usually means "not" (*inactive*), but in **inflammable** it means "into" — inflammable actually means *easily catches fire*, the same as flammable! Examiners love this.

**Trap 3 — False friends:** **"Credulous"** (believes too easily — a fault) vs **"credible"** (believable — a quality). Same root, opposite exam answers. Read the sentence before choosing.

## 🔁 Linguistic Bridge: Hindi से English

| हिंदी में सोच | ❌ Common Error | ✅ Correct English |
|---|---|---|
| "उसने मुझसे कहा" | He dictated me. | He **said to me** / He **told me**. (*Dictate* = to order or read aloud for writing.) |
| "यह शब्द मूल से बना है" | This word is made from root. | This word is **derived from** a root. |
| "मैं शब्द का अर्थ अनुमान लगा सकता हूँ" | I can guess the word's meaning **from seeing**. | I can **infer** the meaning **from the root/context**. |
| "परीक्षा में शब्दावली आती है" | Vocabulary comes in exam. | Vocabulary **is asked / appears** in the exam. |

**Rule of thumb:** English verbs like *derive, infer, predict* need their fixed prepositions (*from, from, —*). Never translate Hindi word-order directly.

## 📊 Summary Table: Decode Any Word in 3 Steps

| Step | Action | Example: "contradiction" |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Spot the **prefix** | *contra-* = against |
| 2 | Spot the **root** | *dict* = say |
| 3 | Spot the **suffix** | *-ion* = noun → "the act of saying against" = **contradiction** |

**Golden Formula:** *Prefix (direction) + Root (meaning) + Suffix (word class) = Full meaning.*
