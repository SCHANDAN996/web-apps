101. The root "CHRON" relates to which of the following?
(a) Color (b) Writing (c) Sound (d) Time
Answer: (d)
Solution: "CHRON" comes from the Greek "chronos" meaning time, as in chronology, chronic, and synchronize. Color is "chrom" (chromosome, monochrome), a tempting confusion because the two roots look alike, so the answer is Time.
Source: NCERT Class 10 English (Vocabulary)

102. Which word means "a government ruled by the people"?
(a) Autocracy (b) Theocracy (c) Bureaucracy (d) Democracy
Answer: (d)
Solution: "Demos" means people and "cracy" means rule, so democracy is rule by the people. Autocracy (self-rule by one) is the tempting wrong choice because it is also a common "-cracy" word, so the answer is Democracy.
Source: Standard Greek root lists (Vocabulary)

103. The prefix "ANTI-" in "antibiotic" means:
(a) Against (b) Before (c) After (d) Together
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Anti-" means against, so an antibiotic works against bacteria. "Ante-" (before) is the trap because it looks nearly identical, so the answer is Against.

104. Which of the following words contains a root meaning "to see"?
(a) Audible (b) Portable (c) Spectacle (d) Credible
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Spect" (Latin spectare) means to see, as in spectacle, inspect, and spectator. "Audible" (hear) is the tempting distractor since it is also a sense-related root, so the answer is Spectacle.

105. The word "benevolent" most nearly means:
(a) Kind and generous (b) Harmful (c) Neutral (d) Wealthy
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Bene" means good/well and "vol" means wish, so benevolent means wishing good — kind. "Wealthy" tempts because benefactors are often rich, but the root meaning is goodwill, so the answer is Kind and generous.
Source: Standard Latin root lists (Vocabulary)

106. In the word "unbelievable", the root is:
(a) believe (b) un (c) able (d) unbelieve
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Un-" is a prefix and "-able" is a suffix; the core word carrying meaning is "believe". Choosing "unbelieve" is the trap because it joins a prefix to the root, which is not a standalone word, so the answer is believe.
Source: NCERT Class 9 English (Morphology)

107. The root "DICT" (as in predict, verdict) means:
(a) To say or speak (b) To write (c) To judge (d) To lead
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Dict" comes from Latin "dicere", to say — predict (say before), verdict (truly said). "To judge" tempts because verdicts are judicial, but the root itself means to say, so the answer is To say or speak.
Source: Standard Latin root lists (Vocabulary)

108. Which word is formed with a suffix that means "one who"?
(a) Quickly (b) Teacher (c) Happiness (d) Beautiful
Answer: (b)
Solution: "-er" in teacher means "one who teaches". "Quickly" tempts because "-ly" is also a common suffix, but it forms adverbs, not agent nouns, so the answer is Teacher.

109. "Malnutrition" literally means:
(a) Excess nutrition (b) No nutrition (c) Bad or poor nutrition (d) Balanced nutrition
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Mal-" means bad (malfunction, malpractice), so malnutrition is bad/poor nutrition. "No nutrition" is the trap because malnutrition is often confused with starvation, so the answer is Bad or poor nutrition.
Source: Standard Latin prefix lists (Vocabulary)

110. The root "PORT" appears in all of the following EXCEPT:
(a) Transport (b) Portion (c) Portfolio (d) Import
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Port" (carry) is in transport, import, and portfolio (carry leaves/papers). "Portion" comes from Latin "portio" (share/part), a classic look-alike trap, so the answer is Portion.

111. A "polyglot" is a person who:
(a) Has many spouses (b) Studies rocks (c) Speaks many languages (d) Draws maps
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Poly" means many and "glot" (glossa) means tongue/language. "Has many spouses" tempts because polygamy also starts with "poly", but the second root differs, so the answer is Speaks many languages.

112. The prefix "OMNI-" in "omniscient" means:
(a) All (b) None (c) One (d) Few
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Omni-" means all — omniscient (all-knowing), omnipresent (present everywhere). "One" tempts because "omni" sounds like "uni", but "uni-" is the root for one, so the answer is All.
Source: Standard Latin prefix lists (Vocabulary)

113. Which pair of words shares the same root?
(a) Biology / Biography (b) All three pairs share a root (c) Microscope / Telescope (d) Telephone / Telegraph
Answer: (b)
Solution: Biology and biography share "bio" (life); telephone and telegraph share "tele" (far); microscope and telescope share "scope" (look). Each pair shares one root even though the other root differs, so the answer is All three pairs share a root.
Source: Standard Greek root lists (Vocabulary)

114. The word "retrospect" is built from roots meaning:
(a) Look forward (b) Look again (c) Look around (d) Look back
Answer: (d)
Solution: "Retro" means backward and "spect" means look, so retrospect means to look back. "Look again" tempts because "re-" usually means again, but here the prefix is "retro-", so the answer is Look back.
Source: Standard Latin root lists (Vocabulary)

115. In "disagreement", how many morphemes (meaningful units) are there?
(a) One (b) Two (c) Four (d) Three
Answer: (d)
Solution: The word breaks into dis- + agree + -ment: a prefix, a root, and a suffix — three morphemes. Counting syllables (four) is the trap, since morphemes are units of meaning, not sound, so the answer is Three.
Source: NCERT Class 9 English (Morphology)

116. "Amicable" and "amiable" both come from a root meaning:
(a) Friend/love (b) War (c) Mind (d) Strength
Answer: (a)
Solution: Both come from Latin "amicus" (friend) / "amare" (to love). "Mind" tempts because "ami" resembles "animus" in some forms, but these words are about friendliness, so the answer is Friend/love.
Source: Standard Latin root lists (Vocabulary)

117. Which word means "fear of water"?
(a) Dehydration (b) Aquaculture (c) Hydraulics (d) Hydrophobia
Answer: (d)
Solution: "Hydro" (water) + "phobia" (fear) = fear of water. "Dehydration" tempts because it also contains "hydr", but it means removal of water, not fear, so the answer is Hydrophobia.
Source: Standard Greek root lists (Vocabulary)

118. The suffix "-ology" means:
(a) The study of (b) The fear of (c) One who does (d) Full of
Answer: (a)
Solution: "-ology" (Greek logos) means the study of, as in biology, geology, psychology. "One who does" is the trap because "-ologist" (not "-ology") names the person, so the answer is The study of.
Source: Standard Greek suffix lists (Vocabulary)

119. "Credulous" and "incredible" share a root meaning:
(a) To create (b) To believe (c) To grow (d) To blame
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Cred" (Latin credere) means to believe — credulous (too ready to believe), incredible (not believable). "To create" tempts because "cre" also begins "create", but that is a different root, so the answer is To believe.
Source: Standard Latin root lists (Vocabulary)

120. Spot the part containing an error: Part P: "The geography" / Part Q: "of these two regions" / Part R: "are completely different." / Part S: No error
(a) Part P (b) Part R (c) Part Q (d) No error
Answer: (b)
Solution: Part R is wrong because the subject is "geography" (singular), so it should be "is completely different"; the intervening phrase "of these two regions" tricks the ear into a plural verb. Part Q is only a prepositional phrase and carries no error, so the answer is Part R.
Source: PYQ-style (Subject-Verb Agreement with root word "geo")

121. The word "autobiography" contains roots meaning:
(a) Self + earth + write (b) Self + book + read (c) One + life + speak (d) Self + life + write
Answer: (d)
Solution: "Auto" (self) + "bio" (life) + "graph" (write) = writing about one's own life. "Self + book + read" tempts because biographies are books, but "graph" means write, not read, so the answer is Self + life + write.
Source: Standard Greek root lists (Vocabulary)

122. Which sentence uses a word with the root "JECT" (to throw)?
(a) The committee rejected the proposal. (b) The doctor injected the medicine. (c) All three sentences do (d) The player was ejected from the game.
Answer: (c)
Solution: Reject (throw back), inject (throw in), and eject (throw out) all contain "ject". Each option alone is correct, which is why "All three" is the complete answer, so the answer is All three sentences do.
Source: Standard Latin root lists (Vocabulary)

123. "Pseudonym" literally means:
(a) True name (b) False name (c) Old name (d) Holy name
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Pseudo" (false) + "onym" (name) = a false name, like a pen name. "Old name" tempts because "pseudo" is sometimes confused with "paleo" (old), so the answer is False name.
Source: Standard Greek root lists (Vocabulary)

124. Spot the part containing an error: Part P: "She is one of the most" / Part Q: "intelligent student" / Part R: "in the entire class." / Part S: No error
(a) Part P (b) Part R (c) Part Q (d) No error
Answer: (c)
Solution: Part Q should be "intelligent students" because "one of the" is always followed by a plural noun; the singular "student" sounds right because "she" is singular, which is the classic sound-right trap. Parts P and R are grammatically fine, so the answer is Part Q.
Source: PYQ-style (One of the + plural noun)

125. The roots "MORT" and "NECRO" both relate to:
(a) Birth (b) Sleep (c) Death (d) Dreams
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Mort" (mortal, mortuary) is Latin and "necro" (necropolis, necrology) is Greek, both meaning death. "Sleep" tempts because "somnus" (insomnia) is often studied alongside, but it is a separate root, so the answer is Death.
Source: Standard Greek/Latin root lists (Vocabulary)
