51. The root "bene" means:
(a) good (b) bad (c) against (d) before
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Mal" means bad and "anti" means against, which are tempting opposites, but "bene" is Latin for good, as in benefit and benevolent. So the answer is good.
Source: Standard Greek-Latin root lists

52. The root "dict" (as in predict, dictionary) means:
(a) to write (b) to speak/say (c) to see (d) to hear
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Scrib/script" means to write, which confuses learners because a dictionary involves writing, but "dict" is Latin for say or speak. So the answer is to speak/say.
Source: Standard Greek-Latin root lists

53. The root "port" (as in transport, export) means:
(a) to carry (b) to send (c) to break (d) to build
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Mit/miss" means to send, which appears in similar words like transmit, but "port" is Latin for carry — a porter carries luggage. So the answer is to carry.
Source: Standard Greek-Latin root lists

54. The root "chron" means:
(a) colour (b) earth (c) time (d) water
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Chrom" (as in chrome, chromosome) means colour and looks almost identical, but "chron" is Greek for time, as in chronology and chronic. So the answer is time.
Source: Standard Greek-Latin root lists

55. The root "audi" means:
(a) to see (b) to touch (c) to hear (d) to feel
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Vid/vis" means to see, and video is often confused with audio, but "audi" is Latin for hear, as in audience and audible. So the answer is to hear.
Source: Standard Greek-Latin root lists

56. The prefix "un-" in "unhappy" means:
(a) again (b) before (c) not (d) together
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Re-" means again, which is a common prefix learners mix up, but "un-" simply negates the word — unhappy means not happy. So the answer is not.
Source: NCERT Class 9 English (word formation)

57. The root "geo" (as in geography, geology) means:
(a) star (b) life (c) air (d) earth
Answer: (d)
Solution: "Bio" means life and "astro" means star, both common science roots, but "geo" is Greek for earth — geography is writing about the earth. So the answer is earth.
Source: Standard Greek-Latin root lists

58. The suffix "-ful" in "hopeful" means:
(a) without (b) state of (c) one who (d) full of
Answer: (d)
Solution: "-less" means without, which is the direct opposite and a frequent trap, but "-ful" means full of — hopeful means full of hope. So the answer is full of.
Source: NCERT Class 9 English (word formation)

59. The root "bio" (as in biology, biography) means:
(a) life (b) death (c) book (d) two
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Bi" meaning two (as in bicycle) looks similar and confuses many students, but "bio" is Greek for life — biology is the study of life. So the answer is life.
Source: Standard Greek-Latin root lists

60. The prefix "pre-" in "preview" means:
(a) after (b) before (c) against (d) again
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Post-" means after, the natural opposite trap, but "pre-" is Latin for before — a preview is a view before the actual event. So the answer is before.
Source: NCERT Class 9 English (word formation)

61. "Benevolent" most nearly means:
(a) wealthy (b) famous (c) powerful (d) kind and generous
Answer: (d)
Solution: Wealthy is tempting because benevolent people often donate money, but the word itself comes from "bene" (good) + "vol" (wish) — wishing good for others. So the answer is kind and generous.
Source: Standard Greek-Latin root lists

62. "Chronological order" means arrangement according to:
(a) time (b) importance (c) size (d) alphabet
Answer: (a)
Solution: Importance is tempting because lists often rank things, but "chron" means time, so chronological order follows the sequence of events in time. So the answer is time.
Source: Standard Greek-Latin root lists

63. A "portable" device is one that can be:
(a) repaired easily (b) used anywhere free (c) carried easily (d) connected to a port
Answer: (c)
Solution: Connected to a port is the modern trap — computer ports mislead students — but "port" means carry, so portable means able to be carried. So the answer is carried easily.
Source: Standard Greek-Latin root lists

64. "Malevolent" is the opposite of "benevolent" because the root "mal" means:
(a) small (b) false (c) many (d) bad/evil
Answer: (d)
Solution: False is tempting because "mal-" appears in words like malfunction which feel like errors, but "mal" strictly means bad or evil — malevolent means wishing harm. So the answer is bad/evil.
Source: Standard Greek-Latin root lists

65. An "auditorium" is a place where people:
(a) watch films only (b) gather to hear (c) display art (d) hold elections
Answer: (b)
Solution: Watch films only is tempting from modern usage, but "audi" means hear — an auditorium is fundamentally a place for an audience to hear. So the answer is gather to hear.
Source: Standard Greek-Latin root lists

66. The word "contradict" means to:
(a) speak against (b) write clearly (c) agree silently (d) repeat loudly
Answer: (a)
Solution: Repeat loudly is tempting because "dict" relates to speaking, but "contra" means against and "dict" means say — to contradict is to say the opposite. So the answer is speak against.
Source: Standard Greek-Latin root lists

67. "Geology" is the study of:
(a) maps (b) the earth's physical structure (c) animals (d) weather
Answer: (b)
Solution: Maps is tempting because of confusion with geography, but "geo" (earth) + "logy" (study) makes geology the study of the earth's rocks and structure. So the answer is the earth's physical structure.
Source: Standard Greek-Latin root lists

68. In the word "transportation", the prefix "trans-" means:
(a) under (b) behind (c) across (d) within
Answer: (c)
Solution: Within is tempting because transport happens inside a system, but "trans-" means across — transport carries things across places, as in translate and transfer. So the answer is across.
Source: Standard Greek-Latin root lists

69. A "biography" is a written account of someone's:
(a) dreams (b) achievements only (c) travels (d) life
Answer: (d)
Solution: Achievements only is tempting because biographies highlight successes, but "bio" (life) + "graph" (write) means a written account of a life. So the answer is life.
Source: Standard Greek-Latin root lists

70. The word "inaudible" means:
(a) too loud (b) not able to be heard (c) not able to be seen (d) unclear in meaning
Answer: (b)
Solution: Not able to be seen is the classic trap — students confuse "audi" with "vid" — but "in-" (not) + "audi" (hear) + "-ible" (able) means not able to be heard. So the answer is not able to be heard.
Source: Standard Greek-Latin root lists

71. "Predict" literally means to:
(a) say before (b) write before (c) think before (d) act before
Answer: (a)
Solution: Think before is tempting because prediction involves thinking, but "pre-" (before) + "dict" (say) literally means to say beforehand. So the answer is say before.
Source: Standard Greek-Latin root lists

72. Which word contains a root meaning "earth"?
(a) biology (b) astronomy (c) geometry (d) chronicle
Answer: (c)
Solution: Astronomy is tempting because it deals with the planet, but its root "astro" means star; geometry contains "geo" (earth) — it originally meant measuring the earth. So the answer is geometry.
Source: Standard Greek-Latin root lists

73. The suffix "-logy" in "biology" and "geology" means:
(a) fear of (b) study of (c) love of (d) one who
Answer: (b)
Solution: Love of is tempting because of confusion with "-phile" (as in bibliophile), but "-logy" from Greek "logos" means study or science of. So the answer is study of.
Source: Standard Greek-Latin root lists

74. "Export" and "import" differ because the prefixes mean:
(a) out and in (b) in and out (c) up and down (d) before and after
Answer: (a)
Solution: In and out reverses the real meanings and is the most common student error; "ex-" means out and "im-/in-" means in — export carries goods out, import carries them in. So the answer is out and in.
Source: Standard Greek-Latin root lists

75. A "verdict" is literally a:
(a) written judgement (b) public speech (c) final argument (d) true saying
Answer: (d)
Solution: Written judgement is tempting because verdicts are recorded in court, but "ver" (true) + "dict" (say) literally means a true saying — the jury's declaration of truth. So the answer is true saying.
Source: Standard Greek-Latin root lists
