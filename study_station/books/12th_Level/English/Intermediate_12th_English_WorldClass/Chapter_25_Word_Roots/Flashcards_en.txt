Card 1
Front: What does the root "spect" mean? Name two words built from it.
Back: "Spect" means "to look/see" (Latin: specere). Examples: inspect (look into), spectator (one who looks/watches), retrospect (look back).

Card 2
Front: What does the root "dict" mean? Give two derived words.
Back: "Dict" means "to say/speak" (Latin: dicere). Examples: predict (say before), contradict (say against), verdict (a saying of truth).

Card 3
Front: What does the root "port" mean? Give two derived words.
Back: "Port" means "to carry" (Latin: portare). Examples: transport (carry across), export (carry out), portable (able to be carried).

Card 4
Front: What does the root "cred" mean? Give two derived words.
Back: "Cred" means "to believe" (Latin: credere). Examples: credible (believable), incredible (not believable), credit (belief/trust in payment).

Card 5
Front: What does the root "bio" mean? Give two derived words.
Back: "Bio" means "life" (Greek: bios). Examples: biology (study of life), biography (writing about a life), antibiotic (against life of microbes).

Card 6
Front: What does the root "chron" mean? Give two derived words.
Back: "Chron" means "time" (Greek: chronos). Examples: chronology (order of time), synchronize (happen at the same time), chronic (lasting over time).

Card 7
Front: What does the root "phon" mean? Give two derived words.
Back: "Phon" means "sound" (Greek: phone). Examples: telephone (sound from afar), symphony (sounds together), phonetics (study of sounds).

Card 8
Front: What does the root "scrib/script" mean? Give two derived words.
Back: It means "to write" (Latin: scribere). Examples: describe (write about), manuscript (written by hand), inscription (written on something).

Card 9
Front: What does the root "vid/vis" mean? Give two derived words.
Back: It means "to see" (Latin: videre). Examples: video (I see), visible (able to be seen), revise (see again).

Card 10
Front: What does the root "mort" mean? Give two derived words.
Back: "Mort" means "death" (Latin: mors). Examples: mortal (subject to death), immortal (not subject to death), mortuary (place for the dead).

Card 11
Front: MNEMONIC — How do you remember that "bene" = good and "mal" = bad?
Back: Think: "BENEfit is good; MALaria is bad." So benefit, benevolent = good; malnutrition, malicious, malfunction = bad.

Card 12
Front: MNEMONIC — How do you remember the root "aqua"?
Back: Think of an "aquarium" — a tank full of water. Aqua = water. Hence aquatic (living in water), aqueduct (channel carrying water).

Card 13
Front: EXAMINER'S TRAP — Does the prefix "in-" always mean "not"?
Back: No. In "inactive" it means "not", but in "inflammable" it means "into/able to catch fire" — inflammable actually means easily set on fire, NOT fireproof. Examiners exploit this confusion.

Card 14
Front: EXAMINER'S TRAP — "Valuable" and "invaluable" — are they opposites?
Back: No. "Invaluable" means so valuable that its value cannot be measured — it is a stronger positive, not a negative. The opposite of valuable is "valueless/worthless".

Card 15
Front: What is the difference between a prefix and a suffix? Give one example of each.
Back: A prefix is added before the root and usually changes meaning (un- + happy = unhappy). A suffix is added after the root and often changes the word class (happy + -ness = happiness, adjective → noun).

Card 16
Front: What does the root "geo" mean? Give two derived words.
Back: "Geo" means "earth" (Greek: ge). Examples: geography (writing about the earth), geology (study of the earth), geothermal (earth's heat).

Card 17
Front: What does the root "anthrop" mean? Give two derived words.
Back: "Anthrop" means "human/mankind" (Greek: anthropos). Examples: anthropology (study of humans), philanthropy (love of mankind), misanthrope (hater of mankind).

Card 18
Front: Break the word "unbelievable" into its morphological parts.
Back: un- (prefix: not) + believe (root: to trust) + -able (suffix: capable of). Meaning: not capable of being believed. Root "cred" family shares the same idea of belief.

Card 19
Front: What does the root "therm" mean? Give two derived words.
Back: "Therm" means "heat" (Greek: therme). Examples: thermometer (heat measurer), thermal (related to heat), hypothermia (dangerously low body heat).

Card 20
Front: If "micro" = small and "scope" = to look, what is a "microscope"? Use the same logic for "telescope".
Back: Microscope = an instrument to look at small things. Telescope = an instrument to look at things far away (tele = far). Root-combining lets you decode new words without a dictionary.
