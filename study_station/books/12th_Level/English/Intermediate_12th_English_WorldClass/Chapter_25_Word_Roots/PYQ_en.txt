# 📊 PYQ Analysis — Word Roots & Morphology

## How Exams Test This Chapter

Across SSC (CGL, CHSL, GD), Banking (IBPS, SBI PO/Clerk), and Railways (NTPC, Group D), roots and morphology appear **indirectly but constantly** — rarely as "What does the root 'bene' mean?" and mostly as:

- **Synonym/Antonym questions** where the answer can be cracked from the root (e.g., *benevolent* → *bene* = good).
- **One-word substitution** built on Greek/Latin roots (*autobiography* = *auto* + *bio* + *graph*).
- **Fill in the blanks** where two options differ only by a prefix (*disinterested* vs *uninterested*).
- **Spelling questions** where knowing the root fixes the spelling (*necessary* — one 'c', two 's' — from *necesse*).
- **Reading comprehension vocabulary** — "The word 'unprecedented' in the passage means…"

**Most repeated sub-topics:** prefixes of negation (un-, dis-, in-/im-/il-/ir-), number roots (mono-, bi-, tri-, poly-), "good/bad" roots (bene/mal, eu/dys), and word-formation (noun → adjective suffixes like -ous, -ive, -al).

**Examiner's favourite traps:**
1. **Sound-Right trap** — options that "sound" correct to a Hindi speaker's ear (*unrespectful* instead of *disrespectful*).
2. **Near-miss prefix trap** — *disinterested* (impartial) vs *uninterested* (bored); *immoral* vs *amoral*.
3. **False-friend trap** — *inflammable* means "easily catches fire", NOT "not flammable".
4. **Over-thinking trap** — the simplest root-based meaning is usually right; students reject it looking for something fancier.

## Top 10 High-Yield Questions (with Solutions)

**Q1.** Choose the word that means "a person who speaks many languages":
(a) Bilingual (b) Polyglot (c) Linguist (d) Orator
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** *Poly* = many, *glot* = tongue/language → polyglot = one who knows many languages. A linguist *studies* language but may speak only one. **Trap type:** Sound-Right (students pick "linguist" because it contains "lingua").
**Source:** PYQ-style (one-word substitution)

**Q2.** The prefix in "impossible" changes from *in-* to *im-* because:
(a) It is a spelling error that became standard
(b) *in-* becomes *im-* before b, m, p for ease of pronunciation
(c) *im-* is the Greek form of *in-*
(d) There is no rule; each word is memorised separately
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** The negative prefix *in-* assimilates: *im-* before b/m/p (impossible, imbalance, immature), *il-* before l (illegal), *ir-* before r (irregular). **Trap type:** Over-thinking — option (d) tempts students who memorise word lists.
**Source:** PYQ-style (morphology rule)

**Q3.** "Malnutrition" means:
(a) No nutrition (b) Excess nutrition (c) Faulty/bad nutrition (d) Balanced nutrition
**Answer: (c)**
**Solution:** *Mal-* = bad/faulty (malpractice, malfunction, malice). It does not mean "no" — that would be *a-*. **Trap type:** Near-miss prefix (mal- vs a-/un-).
**Source:** PYQ-style (root meaning)

**Q4.** Choose the correctly spelt word:
(a) Occurence (b) Occurrence (c) Ocurrence (d) Occurrance
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** *Occur* (ob- + currere = run towards) doubles the 'r' before -ence: occur**r**ence. **Trap type:** Sound-Right spelling.
**Source:** PYQ-style (spelling)

**Q5.** In the sentence "The judge must remain disinterested," *disinterested* means:
(a) Bored (b) Impartial (c) Unqualified (d) Distracted
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** *Disinterested* = having no personal stake → impartial. *Uninterested* = bored. Exams love this pair. **Trap type:** Near-miss prefix — the classic dis-/un- confusion.
**Source:** PYQ-style (vocabulary in context)

**Q6.** "Autobiography" is formed from:
(a) auto + bio + graphy (b) auto + biograph (c) aut + obio + graphy (d) auto + biblio + graphy
**Answer: (a)**
**Solution:** *Auto* (self) + *bio* (life) + *graphy* (writing) = writing about one's own life. *Biblio* = book (bibliography) — a deliberate distractor. **Trap type:** False-friend root (bio vs biblio).
**Source:** PYQ-style (word formation)

**Q7.** Choose the antonym of "benevolent":
(a) Kind (b) Malevolent (c) Generous (d) Benign
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** *Bene* = good, *mal* = bad; *vol* = wish. Benevolent = wishing good; malevolent = wishing harm. (a) and (c) are synonyms — trap options. **Trap type:** Sound-Right (students who don't know the root pick "benign", which is actually a near-synonym).
**Source:** PYQ-style (antonym via roots)

**Q8.** Which word means "a government by the people"?
(a) Autocracy (b) Democracy (c) Bureaucracy (d) Theocracy
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** *Demos* = people, *cracy* = rule. Auto = self, bureau = officials, theo = God. One root (*-cracy*) unlocks all four options — this is why roots are high-yield. **Trap type:** Over-thinking.
**Source:** PYQ-style (one-word substitution)

**Q9.** "Inflammable material must be kept away from fire." From the sentence, *inflammable* means:
(a) Cannot catch fire (b) Easily catches fire (c) Fire-resistant (d) Already burnt
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** Here *in-* is NOT the negative prefix — it comes from *inflame* (to set on fire). Flammable and inflammable mean the SAME thing. **Trap type:** False-friend prefix — the single most dangerous morphology trap in exams.
**Source:** PYQ-style (contextual vocabulary)

**Q10.** The suffix "-ology" in "biology, geology, psychology" means:
(a) Fear of (b) Study of (c) Love of (d) Hatred of
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** *-logy/-ology* = study/science of. Fear = *-phobia*, love = *-philia*. Exams rotate these three suffixes constantly. **Trap type:** Near-miss suffix.
**Source:** PYQ-style (suffix meaning)

## Applying the 15/45-Second Rule

**Fact-based (15 sec) — Q8 above:** You see "-cracy" and four root-words. If you know *demos* = people, the answer is instant. No sentence to parse, no tone to judge. Read → recall root → mark → move. **Never spend more than 15 seconds on a pure root-recall question** — either you know the root or you don't; staring won't help.

**Inference-based (45 sec) — Q5 above:** Here the word sits inside a sentence about a judge. You must (1) read the full sentence, (2) recall both *disinterested* and *uninterested*, (3) match "judge" with "impartial", (4) eliminate "bored" as the trap. This deserves the full 45 seconds — rushing it is exactly how the examiner catches you.

**Rule of thumb:** Root/suffix recall, spelling, one-word substitution → 15 sec. Word-in-context, dis/un-type pairs, RC vocabulary → 45 sec.

## Trap-Type Summary (memorise before the exam)

| Trap type | What it looks like | Defence |
|---|---|---|
| Sound-Right | *unrespectful, occurence* | Trust the root rule, not your ear |
| Near-miss prefix/suffix | dis-/un-, mal-/a-, -phobia/-philia | Learn confusing pairs as a set |
| False-friend | inflammable, invaluable (= very valuable) | Make a personal "danger words" list |
| Over-thinking | rejecting the obvious root meaning | Simplest root-based answer is usually right |

**Source:** Pattern analysis based on standard SSC/Banking/Railway question formats; individual questions are PYQ-style reconstructions, not verbatim paper quotes.
