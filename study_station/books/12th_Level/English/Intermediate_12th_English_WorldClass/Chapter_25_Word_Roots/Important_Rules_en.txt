# 📋 Important Rules & Tips — Word Roots & Morphology

## (a) 12 High-Yield Rules

| # | Rule | Example | Examiner's Trap |
|---|------|---------|-----------------|
| 1 | A root carries the core meaning; prefixes and suffixes only modify it. | **port** = carry → transport, export, portable | Options built on a *similar-looking* root (port vs. part) — read the root, not the look. |
| 2 | Prefix changes **meaning/direction**, never the word class by itself. | un- + happy = unhappy (still adjective) | "Prefix changes noun to verb" — a sound-right but false statement. |
| 3 | Suffix usually decides the **part of speech**. | -tion → noun (dictate → dictation); -ous → adjective (danger → dangerous) | Question asks "which is an adjective?" — trap option is a noun form (danger). |
| 4 | One root can spawn words of opposite meaning via prefixes. | **ject** = throw → inject (throw in) vs. eject (throw out) | Exam pairs antonyms as options; don't assume same root = same meaning. |
| 5 | Greek roots dominate science/medicine; Latin roots dominate law/government. | Greek: bio (life), geo (earth); Latin: jus (law), dict (say) | "Tele-" is Greek, not Latin — origin-swapping is a classic trap. |
| 6 | "a-/an-" before a root = "not/without". | amoral (without morals), anonymous (without name) | Confusing a- (not) with ad- (towards) — opposite forces. |
| 7 | "bene-" (good) vs. "mal-" (bad) — the most tested opposite pair. | benevolent ↔ malevolent; benefit ↔ malice | Options swap bene/mal deliberately; check the first three letters. |
| 8 | Numbers hide in roots: uni-1, bi-2, tri-3, quad-4, penta-5, dec-10, cent-100. | decade = 10 years; century = 100 years | "Bicentenary = 200 years" — trap option says 100. |
| 9 | "-cide" = killing; "-phobia" = fear; "-logy" = study. | homicide, claustrophobia, biology | "Insecticide kills insects" — trap option says "attracts insects". |
| 10 | Some "prefixes" are part of the root and cannot be split. | "receive" is not re- + ceive in modern meaning | Over-analysis trap: don't break every word mechanically. |
| 11 | Same suffix, different function: -er = doer (teacher) OR comparison (taller). | singer (noun) vs. faster (comparative) | Question: "function of -er in 'faster'?" — trap answer: "one who does". |
| 12 | Context beats memory: if a derived word looks odd, re-read the sentence. | "The **dictator**'s **verdict** was **predictable**. | Sound-right trap: a familiar word used in the wrong sense (e.g., "portable evidence". |

## (b) Time Management — 15/45-Second Rule

| Type | What it looks like | Time | Strategy |
|------|-------------------|------|----------|
| **Fact-based (15 sec)** | "The root 'chron' means —", "Which word means 'fear of heights'?", "Pick the correct one-word substitution from a known root." | 15 sec | Recall the root → match → mark. If not known in 15 sec, flag and move on. |
| **Inference-based (45 sec)** | "In the sentence, 'malfunction' most nearly means —", "Which word best fits the blank?", "The tone of the word 'benevolent' in the passage is —" | 45 sec | Break word → root + affix → test each option against the sentence context. |

**Rule of thumb:** If the question gives you a word and asks its meaning directly → 15 sec. If it gives a sentence/passage and asks meaning *in context* → 45 sec.
