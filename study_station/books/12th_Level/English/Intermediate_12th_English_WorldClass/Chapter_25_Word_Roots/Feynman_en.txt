# 🧒 Feynman Technique — Word Roots & Morphology

## The Hardest Concept, Explained to a 12-Year-Old

Imagine you have a box of **LEGO blocks**. You can build a car, a house, or a spaceship — but you never buy a new toy. You just **rearrange the same blocks**.

English words work exactly like LEGO.

- The **ROOT** is the biggest block — it carries the main meaning.
- The **PREFIX** is a small block you snap on the **front** — it changes the direction of meaning.
- The **SUFFIX** is a small block you snap on the **back** — it changes the word's job (noun, verb, adjective).

### Let's play with one root: **PORT = carry** (from Latin *portare*)

| Blocks snapped together | Word | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| ex (out) + port | **export** | carry OUT (send goods out of the country) |
| im (in) + port | **import** | carry IN (bring goods into the country) |
| trans (across) + port | **transport** | carry ACROSS |
| port + able (can be) | **portable** | CAN BE carried |
| sup (under) + port | **support** | carry from UNDER (hold up) |

See the magic? You learned **one root** and unlocked **five words**. Learn a handful of common roots, and you can guess the meaning of **many** exam words — even words you have never seen before!

### The Unforgettable Trick: "The Word Detective" 🕵️

When you meet a strange word in the exam, don't panic. **Cut it into pieces like a pizza.**

Example: **"The document was illegible."**

Cut it: **il + leg + ible**
- **il-** = not (like *im*possible)
- **leg** = read (like *leg*end — something meant to be read)
- **-ible** = able to be

So: **il-leg-ible = NOT able to be READ** = handwriting you cannot read. Solved in seconds, without a dictionary!

### Why this beats memorizing word lists

Memorizing 500 words one-by-one is like memorizing 500 phone numbers. But learning roots is like learning the **area codes** — one code tells you the whole region. In SSC, Banking, and Railway exams, vocabulary questions often use words built from common Greek and Latin roots. The detective who knows the roots wins; the parrot who memorized lists forgets.
