# 🪄 Short Tricks + Skip Strategy — Error Spotting (Advanced)

## Memory Boxes (Mnemonics)

**Box 1 — FANBOYS (Coordinating Conjunctions)**
> **F**or, **A**nd, **N**or, **B**ut, **O**r, **Y**et, **S**o
> These 7 join two independent clauses. If a comma + FANBOYS joins two full sentences, check both sides are complete.

**Box 2 — SVA: "Phrases Don't Matter"**
> Between subject and verb, examiners insert long phrases: *"The quality of the mangoes **was** good."*
> Trick: **Cover the middle, match the ends.** Ignore "of the mangoes" — subject is "quality" (singular).

**Box 3 — EACH/EVERY/EITHER/NEITHER = Singular**
> Mnemonic: **"E-E-E-N walks alone"** — Each, Every, Either, Neither always take a singular verb.
> *Neither of the boys **has** (not have) come.*

**Box 4 — Collective Noun Test: "One Body or Many Minds?"**
> Team/jury/committee acting **together** → singular; acting **separately** → plural.
> *The jury **has** given its verdict.* / *The jury **are** divided in their opinion.*

**Box 5 — "One of the + Plural Noun + Singular Verb"**
> Mnemonic: **"One of many, verb for one."**
> *One of my friends **has** (not have) gone.* The noun after "one of the" is plural, but the verb stays singular.

**Box 6 — Scarcely/Hardly/No sooner Pairs**
> **"Scarcely–When, Hardly–When, No sooner–Than"**
> Mnemonic: *"No sooner THAN — the rest take WHEN."*
> *No sooner did he arrive **than** it rained.* (Never "when" with "no sooner".)

**Box 7 — Inversion after Negative Starters**
> **"Negative start? Flip like a question!"**
> Seldom, Rarely, Never, Hardly, Scarcely, Not only at the start → auxiliary before subject.
> *Seldom **does he** go out.* (Not "Seldom he goes".)

**Box 8 — Lest + Should**
> **"Lest fears SHOULD"** — after *lest*, always use *should* (or bare verb), never "will/would".
> *Walk fast lest you **should** miss the train.*

**Box 9 — Prefer/Senior/Junior/Superior take "to", not "than"**
> Mnemonic: **"P-S-J-S love TO, hate THAN"**
> *He is senior **to** me.* / *I prefer tea **to** coffee.*

**Box 10 — Since vs For**
> **"Since = point (Since Monday), For = period (For two days)."**
> Mnemonic: **"Since is a dot, For is a line."**

**Box 11 — Adjective after Verb of Sensation**
> Look, feel, seem, taste, smell, sound + **adjective** (not adverb).
> *The soup tastes **good**.* (Not "well".) Mnemonic: **"Senses describe, not modify."**

**Box 12 — The + Adjective = Plural Verb**
> *The rich **are** not always happy.* (The rich = rich people, plural.)
> Mnemonic: **"The + adjective = a whole class of people."**

**Box 13 — Sound-Right Trap: "It's vs Its"**
> **It's = it is** (apostrophe = missing letter). **Its = possession.**
> Test: expand to "it is" — if the sentence breaks, use *its*.

**Box 14 — Neither…nor / Either…or: Verb Follows the Nearest Subject**
> Mnemonic: **"Proximity Rule — the verb listens to its neighbour."**
> *Neither the teacher nor the students **were** present.*

**Box 15 — "One of the + Noun + who/that/which" → Plural Verb**
> *He is one of those boys who **have** passed.* (Who = boys, plural.)
> But: *He is the **only** one of the boys who **has** passed.* ("Only" flips it back to singular!)

## ⏭️ Skip Strategy

**Tip 1 — The 15-Second Scan:** Read the sentence once. If the error is a visible SVA, tense, or article slip (Fact-based), answer in 15 seconds and move on. Never re-read a solved question — re-reading breeds doubt, and doubt breeds wrong changes.

**Tip 2 — Skip the "Over-thinking" Trap:** If a sentence *sounds* correct and you cannot name a specific broken rule after 45 seconds, mark "No error" and move ahead. Examiners plant long, complex-looking sentences that are actually correct to waste your time. Come back only if time remains.

**Tip 3 — Eliminate Before You Commit:** In 4-part error questions (a/b/c/d), check each segment against one rule at a time — subject-verb, tense, preposition, article. If three segments pass cleanly, the fourth is your answer even if you can't articulate why. Never leave an error-spotting question unattempted if your exam has no negative marking; if it does, attempt only when you can eliminate at least two options.
