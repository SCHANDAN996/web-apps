# 📊 PYQ Analysis — Error Spotting (Advanced)

## 1. How Exams Test This Chapter (Pattern Analysis)

Error Spotting is one of the most dependable areas of the English section across SSC (CGL, CHSL, MTS, GD), Banking (IBPS PO/Clerk, SBI), Railways (RRB NTPC, Group D) and State PCS prelims. Instead of inventing numbers, here is what repeated official papers consistently show:

- **SSC CGL / CHSL:** Error spotting appears in almost every Tier-I paper, usually as "find the part of the sentence that contains an error" (four-part split sentences). Tier-II raises the difficulty with longer sentences and intervening phrases.
- **IBPS / SBI:** Bank exams prefer "improved sentence" and "identify the erroneous part" formats, often mixing grammar with usage (wrong preposition, wrong tense sequence).
- **RRB NTPC / Group D:** Shorter, fact-based sentences — mostly subject–verb agreement, articles, and basic tense errors.
- **State exams:** Frequently borrow SSC-style formats; collective nouns and pronoun agreement are favourites.

**Sub-topics that recur most often (in words, not numbers):**
1. Subject–verb agreement with intervening phrases ("The quality of apples *was*…")
2. Collective nouns and indefinite pronouns (each, every, everyone, neither)
3. Tense sequence in conditional and reported sentences
4. Parallel structure in lists
5. Misplaced/dangling modifiers
6. Wrong prepositions and article usage
7. "Sound-right" errors — phrases that feel correct in spoken Hindi-influenced English but are wrong in standard English

## 2. Examiner's Trap Types — Classification Key

Throughout this section, each question is tagged with one of these trap types:

| Trap Type | What it means |
|---|---|
| **Intervening Phrase** | Extra words between subject and verb hide the real subject |
| **Collective Noun** | Team/jury/committee/family — singular or plural confusion |
| **Sound-Right** | Spoken-English habit that is grammatically wrong |
| **Over-thinking** | Sentence is actually correct; the trap is changing a right answer |

## 3. Top 10 High-Yield Questions (PYQ-style) with Solutions

**Q1.** One of my friends (a) / are going (b) / to Delhi tomorrow. (c) / No error (d)
**Answer:** (b)
**Solution:** The real subject is "One" (singular), not "friends". The phrase "of my friends" only intervenes. Correct: "One of my friends **is** going…"
**Trap type:** Intervening Phrase
**Source:** PYQ-style (SSC pattern)

**Q2.** The jury (a) / has given (b) / its verdict unanimously. (c) / No error (d)
**Answer:** (d)
**Solution:** "Jury" acting as one unanimous body takes a singular verb and singular pronoun ("its"). The sentence is correct — the trap is assuming "jury" must be plural.
**Trap type:** Over-thinking + Collective Noun
**Source:** PYQ-style (Banking pattern)

**Q3.** Neither of the two boys (a) / have done (b) / his homework. (c) / No error (d)
**Answer:** (b)
**Solution:** "Neither" is singular, so the verb must be "has done". "Of the two boys" is an intervening phrase.
**Trap type:** Intervening Phrase
**Source:** PYQ-style (SSC pattern)

**Q4.** What you are doing (a) / in the library (b) / at this hour? (c) / No error (d)
**Answer:** (a)
**Solution:** In a direct question the auxiliary must come before the subject: "**What are you doing**…?" Hindi word order ("तुम क्या कर रहे हो?") misleads speakers here.
**Trap type:** Sound-Right
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q5.** The principal, along with the teachers, (a) / were present (b) / at the meeting. (c) / No error (d)
**Answer:** (b)
**Solution:** With "along with", the verb agrees with the first subject ("The principal" — singular). Correct: "**was** present".
**Trap type:** Intervening Phrase
**Source:** PYQ-style (SSC CGL pattern)

**Q6.** He is one of the best players (a) / that has ever played (b) / for the country. (c) / No error (d)
**Answer:** (b)
**Solution:** After "one of the + plural noun", the relative pronoun "that" refers to the plural noun "players", so the verb is plural: "that **have** ever played". Compare: "He is the only one of the players who **has**…" (singular, because of "only one").
**Trap type:** Intervening Phrase (classic advanced trap)
**Source:** PYQ-style (Banking pattern)

**Q7.** Scarcely he had reached the station (a) / when the train (b) / left the platform. (c) / No error (d)
**Answer:** (a)
**Solution:** When a negative adverb (scarcely, hardly, no sooner) begins a sentence, inversion is required: "**Scarcely had he reached** the station when…"
**Trap type:** Sound-Right
**Source:** PYQ-style (SSC pattern)

**Q8.** The committee (a) / were divided (b) / in their opinion on the new policy. (c) / No error (d)
**Answer:** (d)
**Solution:** When a collective noun's members act separately ("divided in their opinion"), plural verb and plural pronoun are correct. The sentence is right — the trap is forcing a singular verb.
**Trap type:** Over-thinking + Collective Noun
**Source:** PYQ-style (Banking pattern)

**Q9.** Each of the students (a) / have submitted (b) / their assignments on time. (c) / No error (d)
**Answer:** (b)
**Solution:** "Each of + plural noun" takes a singular verb: "**has** submitted". (In formal exam English, "his or her assignment" is preferred over "their", but the verb error is the tested point.)
**Trap type:** Intervening Phrase
**Source:** PYQ-style (SSC CHSL pattern)

**Q10.** No sooner did she enter the room (a) / when the lights (b) / went off. (c) / No error (d)
**Answer:** (b)
**Solution:** "No sooner" pairs with "**than**", not "when". ("Scarcely/Hardly" pair with "when".) Correct: "No sooner did she enter the room **than** the lights went off."
**Trap type:** Sound-Right (correlative confusion)
**Source:** PYQ-style (SSC/Banking pattern)

## 4. Applying the 15/45-Second Rule

**15-second question (Fact-based) — Q9 above:**
The moment you see "Each of the students…", your rule reflex fires: *each/every/neither/either + of → singular verb*. Scan the verb, spot "have", mark (b). No need to read the sentence twice. Target: 15 seconds.

**45-second question (Inference-based) — Q6 above:**
"One of the best players that has/have…" requires you to decide what "that" refers to — "one" or "players". You must recall the special rule (relative pronoun after "one of the + plural noun" agrees with the plural noun), check for the word "only" (which would flip the answer), and then decide. This deserves your full 45 seconds — rushing here is exactly what the examiner wants.

**Rule of thumb:** If the error is a single word-level rule (agreement marker, article, fixed preposition pair), it is a 15-second question. If you must identify *which* noun a pronoun/verb refers to, or judge whether a sentence is actually correct, give it 45 seconds.

## 5. What the Last Decade's Papers Teach (Qualitative Summary)

- **Intervening-phrase traps have grown longer.** Modern papers insert "along with…", "as well as…", "together with…", "accompanied by…" between subject and verb. Train your eye to delete these phrases mentally.
- **"No error" is a real option.** Roughly speaking, examiners include genuinely correct sentences to punish over-thinking. If every part passes your rule checks, choose "No error" confidently.
- **Inversion and correlative pairs** (no sooner…than, hardly…when, not only…but also) appear regularly in SSC and Banking papers — memorise the pairs as fixed units.
- **Collective noun questions test context**, not a blind rule: unanimous/single action → singular; divided/individual action → plural.
- **Hindi-influenced "sound-right" errors** (question-word order, "He is having a car", "I and Ram went") are evergreen in Railway and State exams.
