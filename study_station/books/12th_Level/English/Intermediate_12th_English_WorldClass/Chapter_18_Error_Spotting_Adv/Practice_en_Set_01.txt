1. Spot the error in the sentence: "One of my friend (P) / has gone (Q) / to Delhi yesterday. (R)"
(a) Part R (b) Part Q (c) Part P (d) No error
Answer: (c)
Solution: The tempting choice is Part R because "has gone" with "yesterday" looks wrong, and indeed tense mixing is a trap here, but the clearest structural error is in Part P: after "one of" we always use a plural noun, so it must be "one of my friends". The error lies in Part P.

2. Spot the error: "He is more wiser (P) / than his (Q) / younger brother. (R)"
(a) Part R (b) Part Q (c) Part P (d) No error
Answer: (c)
Solution: Part Q tempts students who think "than his" needs an object pronoun, but "his younger brother" is fine. The real error is the double comparative: "wiser" is already comparative, so "more" must be removed. The error lies in Part P.

Answer: (b)
Solution: Part P tempts because "news" ends in -s and looks plural, but "news" is an uncountable singular noun and takes a singular verb. Replace "are" with "is". The error lies in Part Q.

Answer: (d)
Solution: Part R looks suspicious to some, but "in deep water" is a correct prepositional phrase. The error is that "know" must be followed by "how to" before an infinitive: "know how to swim". The error lies in Part Q.

Answer: (d)
Solution: Part Q tempts because "I stayed" looks too simple, but it is correct. The error is a dangling modifier: "being a rainy day" cannot describe "I". Rewrite as "It being a rainy day, I stayed at home". The error lies in Part P.
