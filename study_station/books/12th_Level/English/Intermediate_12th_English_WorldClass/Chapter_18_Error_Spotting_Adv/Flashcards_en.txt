Card 1
Front: What is the "Intervening Phrase" trap in error spotting?
Back: When words come between the subject and verb (e.g., "The quality of apples *were* good"), the verb must agree with the real subject ("quality"), not the nearest noun. Correct: "The quality of apples *was* good."

Card 2
Front: "One of my friends *have* gone to Delhi." — Spot the error.
Back: "have" → "has". After "one of the + plural noun", the verb is singular because the real subject is "one".

Card 3
Front: Which verb form follows "Neither of the two boys"?
Back: A singular verb: "Neither of the two boys *was* present." (In formal exam English, "neither of" takes a singular verb.)

Card 4
Front: Mnemonic: How do you remember verbs that are always followed by a gerund (V-ing)?
Back: "MAD FISH PEG" — Mind, Avoid, Deny, Finish, Imagine, Suggest, Keep, Practise, Enjoy, Give up. Example: "He suggested *going* there" (not "to go").

Card 5
Front: "The sceneries of Kashmir are beautiful." — Spot the error.
Back: "sceneries" → "scenery". Scenery is an uncountable noun and has no plural form. Other such nouns: furniture, luggage, information, advice, news.

Card 6
Front: Examiner's Trap: "He is more wiser than his brother." — Why is this wrong?
Back: Double comparative. "Wiser" is already comparative, so "more" is redundant. Correct: "He is wiser than his brother." Trap type: Sound-Right (it sounds natural in speech).

Card 7
Front: What is the rule for "hardly/scarcely... when" and "no sooner... than"?
Back: "Hardly/Scarcely" pairs with "when"; "No sooner" pairs with "than". Also, when these begin a sentence, use inversion: "No sooner did he arrive than it rained."

Card 8
Front: What is the correct order of personal pronouns in a sentence?
Back: For pleasant/neutral contexts: 2nd–3rd–1st person — "You, he and I will go." For unpleasant/confession contexts: 1st–2nd–3rd person — "I, you and he are guilty." Key exam point: in polite listing, "I" comes last when the context is good.

Card 9
Front: Mnemonic: How do you remember adjectives that take "to" instead of "than"?
Back: "SIP JAP" — Superior, Inferior, Prior, Junior, Anterior, Posterior. All Latin comparatives take "to": "He is senior *to* me" (not "senior than me").

Card 10
Front: "Each of the students have submitted the form." — Spot the error.
Back: "have" → "has". "Each of + plural noun" takes a singular verb.

Card 11
Front: Examiner's Trap: "The police has arrested the thief." — Why is this wrong?
Back: "Police" is a plural noun and always takes a plural verb: "The police *have* arrested the thief." Trap type: Collective Noun confusion. (But "The police force has..." is singular.)

Card 12
Front: When do we use "fewer" and when "less"?
Back: "Fewer" for countable nouns (fewer students); "less" for uncountable nouns (less water). Exam trap: "less people" is wrong — use "fewer people".

Card 13
Front: "He is working here since 2015." — Spot the error.
Back: "is working" → "has been working". With "since/for" showing time continuing up to now, use the present perfect (continuous) tense.

Card 14
Front: What is the difference between "between" and "among"?
Back: "Between" is used for two persons/things (or one-to-one relations); "among" for more than two. "Distribute the sweets among the children" / "between the two brothers".

Card 15
Front: "Unless you do not work hard, you will fail." — Spot the error.
Back: Remove "not". "Unless" already means "if not", so a second negative is wrong: "Unless you work hard, you will fail."

Card 16
Front: Which preposition follows these: "abide by", "accused of", "afraid of", "angry with"?
Back: abide **by** a rule; accused **of** a crime; afraid **of** something; angry **with** a person (angry **at** a thing). Fixed prepositions are a favourite exam trap.

Card 17
Front: "The Ganges is one of the longest river in India." — Spot the error.
Back: "river" → "rivers". After "one of the + superlative", the noun must be plural: "one of the longest rivers".

Card 18
Front: What is the "Sound-Right" trap and how do you beat it?
Back: Some wrong sentences sound correct because we hear them in daily speech (e.g., "What you are doing?"). Beat it by checking the grammar rule, not your ear. Correct: "What are you doing?" — in questions, the helping verb comes before the subject.

Card 19
Front: "Bread and butter ___ my favourite breakfast." — is/are?
Back: "is". When two nouns joined by "and" express one single idea/dish, the verb is singular. But "Bread and butter *are* sold here" (two separate items) takes a plural verb.

Card 20
Front: Quick rule: adjective or adverb after "look, feel, smell, taste, sound"?
Back: Use an adjective when describing the subject's state: "The cake tastes *good*" (not "well"). Use an adverb only when the verb shows an action: "She looked *carefully* at the paper."
