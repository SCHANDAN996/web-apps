# 📋 Important Grammar Rules & Tips — Error Spotting (Advanced)

## (a) 12 High-Yield Rules

| # | Rule | Example | Examiner's Trap |
|---|------|---------|-----------------|
| 1 | **Intervening phrases don't change the subject.** Verb agrees with the real subject, not the nearest noun. | The quality of the mangoes **was** good. (not *were*) | "The captain, along with his team, **were**…" ❌ — "along with" is a trap; use *was*. |
| 2 | **Each / Every / Either / Neither + singular verb.** | Neither of the two boys **has** come. | "Each of the students **have**…" ❌ — *of the students* lures you into plural. |
| 3 | **Collective nouns** take singular verb when acting as one unit; plural when members act separately. | The jury **has** given its verdict. / The jury **are** divided in opinion. | "The committee **have** decided unanimously" ❌ — unanimous = one unit. |
| 4 | **One of the + plural noun + singular verb.** | One of my friends **is** a doctor. | "One of the boy **is**…" ❌ — noun must be plural (*boys*), verb singular. |
| 5 | **Relative pronoun verb agrees with its antecedent.** | It is I who **am** responsible. / He is one of those who **work** hard. | "One of the best players that **has**…" ❌ — antecedent is *players*, so *have*. |
| 6 | **No sooner…than / Hardly…when / Scarcely…when** — with past perfect + inversion. | No sooner **had** he entered **than** it rained. | "No sooner he entered **when**…" ❌ — wrong pair and no inversion. |
| 7 | **Unless / Until / If / When (future time clauses)** — no *will* in the condition clause. | If it rains, I **will** stay home. | "Unless you **will not** work hard…" ❌ — double negative + *will* both wrong. |
| 8 | **Adjectives after verbs of sensation** (look, feel, seem, taste, smell, sound). | She looks **beautiful**. The soup tastes **good**. | "She looks **beautifully**" ❌ — adverb after linking verb. |
| 9 | **Comparative for two, superlative for three+.** | He is the **taller** of the two brothers. | "He is the **best** of the two" ❌ — use *better*. |
| 10 | **Parallel structure** — items in a list must be in the same grammatical form. | He likes reading, swimming, and **cycling**. | "He likes reading, swimming, and **to cycle**" ❌ — mixed forms. |
| 11 | **Gerund after prepositions and certain verbs** (enjoy, avoid, mind, suggest, look forward to). | I look forward to **meeting** you. | "I look forward to **meet** you" ❌ — here *to* is a preposition, not infinitive. |
| 12 | **Redundant pairs are wrong**: return back, repeat again, enter into (a room), revert back, reason…because. | Please **return** my book. (not *return back*) | "The reason is **because**…" ❌ — use *that*: "The reason is **that**…" |

## (b) Time Management — The 15/45-Second Rule

**Fact-based questions → 15 seconds.** These test a single memorised rule. Spot the keyword, apply the rule, mark, move on.

- Subject–verb agreement with clear markers (*each, every, one of, neither*)
- Fixed pairs (*no sooner…than, hardly…when*)
- Redundancy (*return back, repeat again*)
- Gerund after prepositions (*look forward to + V-ing*)
- Wrong article or preposition in a fixed phrase

**Inference-based questions → 45 seconds.** These need you to read the whole sentence, weigh meaning, and eliminate.

- Intervening-phrase traps (find the *real* subject first)
- Collective noun singular/plural (decide: one unit or separate members?)
- Tense consistency across a long sentence
- Parallelism in a 3–4 item list
- "Sound-right" errors — sentences that feel correct in spoken English but break a formal rule

**Practical tip:** If a sentence looks error-free after 45 seconds, re-check the verb–subject pair and the prepositions — 80% of hidden errors live there. Never spend more than 60 seconds on one question; mark and revisit.

## (c) Morphology — Root, Affix, Example

Knowing word parts helps you spot wrong word *forms* (a common advanced trap: noun used where adjective is needed).

| Root / Affix | Meaning | Example Words | Error Trap |
|---|---|---|---|
| **-spect-** (Latin) | look | inspect, respect, spectator | "He gave a **respectfully** speech" ❌ → *respectful* (full of respect) vs *respectable* (deserving respect) |
| **-dict-** (Latin) | say | predict, contradict, verdict | "His **prediction** was wrongly" ❌ → *wrong* (adjective after *was*) |
| **-port-** (Latin) | carry | transport, export, portable | "This machine is **portability**" ❌ → *portable* (adjective needed) |
| **-cred-** (Latin) | believe | credible, incredible, credit | "His story is **credulous**" ❌ → *credible* (believable); *credulous* = a person who believes too easily |
| **-ly** (suffix) | makes adverbs — but *friendly, costly, lonely, lively* are **adjectives** | a friendly behaviour | "He behaved **friendly**" ❌ → *in a friendly manner* |
| **un- / in- / dis-** (prefix) | not | unable, incorrect, disagree | "He is **uninterested** in the case" (judge) ❌ → *disinterested* (impartial); *uninterested* = bored |
| **-ous** (suffix) | full of (adjective) | dangerous, famous | "The situation is **dangerously**" ❌ → *dangerous* |
| **-tion / -ment** (suffix) | makes nouns | decision, movement | "The **decide** was final" ❌ → *decision* |

**Golden check:** After a linking verb (*is, seems, looks, became*) ask — "noun/pronoun or adjective?" Never an adverb. Before a noun ask — "adjective?" After a verb of action ask — "adverb?" This three-question scan kills most word-form errors in under 15 seconds.
