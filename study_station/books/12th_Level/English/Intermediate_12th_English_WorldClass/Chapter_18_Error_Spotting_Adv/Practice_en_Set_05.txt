101. Spot the error: The number of students (P) / absent from class (Q) / are increasing day by day. (R)
(a) Part P (b) Part Q (c) No error (d) Part R
Answer: (d)
Solution: "The number of" always takes a singular verb, so "are" must be "is". The tempting trap is to agree the verb with the plural noun "students" sitting just before it, but the true subject is "number". The error is in Part R.

102. Spot the error: Neither the manager (P) / nor the employees (Q) / was present at the meeting. (R)
(a) Part R (b) Part Q (c) Part P (d) No error
Answer: (a)
Solution: With "neither…nor", the verb agrees with the nearer subject, which is "employees" (plural), so "was" should be "were". Many candidates match the verb with "manager" and wrongly mark Part P. The error is in Part R.

103. Spot the error: One of my friend (P) / who lives in Delhi (Q) / has sent me a gift. (R)
(a) Part P (b) Part Q (c) Part R (d) No error
Answer: (a)
Solution: After "one of", a plural noun is required, so "friend" must be "friends". The trap is that the verb "has" is correctly singular, which makes the sentence sound right and hides the real error. The error is in Part P.

104. Spot the error: Scarcely he had reached (P) / the station when (Q) / the train left. (R)
(a) Part P (b) Part Q (c) Part R (d) No error
Answer: (a)
Solution: When a negative adverb like "scarcely" begins a clause, inversion is required: "Scarcely had he reached". Candidates often accept the normal word order because it sounds natural in Hindi-influenced speech. The error is in Part P.

106. Spot the error: He is more wiser (P) / than his brother (Q) / in handling money. (R)

Answer: (c)
Solution: Double comparatives are wrong; "wiser" is already comparative, so "more" must be removed. The trap is that "more" is correct before longer adjectives like "beautiful", so it feels acceptable here. The error is in Part P.
