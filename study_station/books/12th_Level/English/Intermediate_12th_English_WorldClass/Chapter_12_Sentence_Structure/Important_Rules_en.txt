# 📋 Important Grammar Rules & Tips — Sentence Structure

## (a) 12 High-Yield Rules

| # | Rule | Example | Examiner's Trap |
|---|------|---------|-----------------|
| 1 | Every sentence needs a **subject + finite verb**; a group of words without both is a fragment. | ❌ *Running down the street.* → ✅ *He was running down the street.* | Options that look complete but lack a finite verb: *"Being a holiday."* — "being" is a participle, not a finite verb, so this is a fragment, not a sentence. |
| 2 | **Subject-Verb Agreement**: the verb agrees with the subject, not with the nearest noun. | *The quality of the mangoes **was** good.* | **Intervening phrase trap**: "The box of chocolates *were*…" — "box" is the subject, so use *was*. |
| 3 | **Two subjects joined by 'and'** take a plural verb; joined by 'or/nor', the verb agrees with the **nearer** subject. | *Either the teacher or the students **are** wrong.* | "Neither of the boys *have*…" — "neither of" takes a singular verb: *has*. |
| 4 | **Collective nouns** (team, jury, committee) take a singular verb when acting as one unit, plural when members act separately. | *The jury **has** given its verdict.* / *The jury **are** divided in opinion.* | Examiner mixes both ideas in one sentence to confuse you. |
| 5 | **Inversion after negative adverbs**: Never, Hardly, Scarcely, No sooner, Not only → auxiliary comes before the subject. | *No sooner **had** he entered **than** it rained.* | "No sooner he had entered" — wrong order. Also: *No sooner…than*, *Hardly/Scarcely…when* — never mix the pairs. |
| 6 | **Question order**: In direct questions, auxiliary comes before the subject; in **indirect questions**, normal statement order returns. | *Do you know where he **lives**?* (not "where does he live") | **Sound-right trap**: "What you are doing?" sounds fine to Hindi ears but is wrong → *What are you doing?* |
| 7 | **There is/are**: the verb agrees with the noun that follows 'there'. | *There **is** a book and two pens on the table.* (proximity rule accepted) | "There *is* two reasons…" — should be *are* when the first noun is plural. |
| 8 | **Each, Every, Everyone, Everybody, Someone, Nobody** → always singular verb and singular pronoun. | *Each of the students **has** a book.* | "Everyone should bring *their* books" — traditionally tested as *his/her*; watch for singular-verb errors first. |
| 9 | **Many a / Many an** + singular noun + singular verb (though the meaning is plural). | *Many a soldier **has** died for the country.* | Looks plural, so candidates pick *have* — classic trap. |
| 10 | **Relative pronoun agreement**: the verb after who/which/that agrees with its **antecedent**. | *It is I who **am** responsible.* / *He is one of those who **work** hard.* | "One of the boys who *was*…" — here 'who' refers to 'boys', so use *were*. |
| 11 | **Clause types**: a complex sentence has one main clause + subordinate clause(s) joined by because, although, when, if, that. | *Although it rained, we went out.* | Two main clauses joined only by a comma = **comma splice** ❌: *It rained, we stayed home.* → use a conjunction or semicolon. |
| 12 | **Parallel structure**: items in a list or joined by and/or/but must be in the same grammatical form. | *She likes reading, writing, and swimming.* | "He came, saw, and was conquering" — breaks parallelism; correct: *conquered*. |

## (b) Time Management — The 15 / 45-Second Rule

| Type | What it looks like | Time | Strategy |
|------|-------------------|------|----------|
| **Fact-based (15 sec)** | Spot the fragment; choose the correct verb after "each/every/many a"; fix "What you are doing?"; pick the right pair (no sooner…than); identify sentence type (simple/compound/complex). | 15 sec | Apply the rule directly. If you know the rule, answer instantly and move on — do NOT re-read. |
| **Inference-based (45 sec)** | Sentence rearrangement (PQRS); choosing the best-combined sentence; identifying the error in a long sentence with an intervening phrase; parallel-structure errors hidden in lists. | 45 sec | Read once fully, mark the subject and verb, check agreement and parallelism, then eliminate. If still unsure after 45 sec, mark and skip. |

**Rule of thumb:** If the question tests ONE rule on a short sentence → 15 seconds. If it tests sentence order, combination, or a long multi-clause sentence → 45 seconds maximum.

## (c) Morphology — Roots & Affixes for Sentence-Structure Vocabulary

| Root / Affix | Meaning | Example Word | Use in a Sentence |
|--------------|---------|--------------|-------------------|
| **struct** (Latin) | build | structure, construct, reconstruction | *The structure of the sentence is faulty.* |
| **junct** (Latin) | join | conjunction, junction, adjunct | *A conjunction joins two clauses.* |
| **frag** (Latin) | break | fragment, fragile, fragmentary | *A fragment is a broken sentence.* |
| **sub-** (prefix) | under, below | subordinate, subclause, subject | *A subordinate clause depends on the main clause.* |
| **co-/con-** (prefix) | together | combine, connect, coordinate | *Coordinate clauses are joined by 'and' or 'but'.* |
| **-tion / -sion** (suffix) | act or state of | completion, inversion, combination | *Inversion changes the normal word order.* |
| **agree** (Latin *gratus*) | pleasing, in harmony | agreement, disagree | *The verb must be in agreement with the subject.* |
| **ord/ordin** (Latin) | order | order, ordinary, subordinate | *Word order decides whether a sentence is correct.* |

**Memory tip:** *STRUCT + JUNCT* = "build + join" — sentence structure is simply **building** words and **joining** clauses correctly.
