51. Which of the following is a complete sentence?
(a) Running through the park in the morning (b) After the match ended yesterday (c) Because the rain was heavy (d) The children played in the garden
Answer: (d)
Solution: Options like "Running through the park" and "Because the rain was heavy" are tempting because they contain verbs, but they are fragments — a phrase and a dependent clause cannot stand alone. A complete sentence needs a subject and a finite verb expressing a full thought, so the answer is "The children played in the garden."
Source: NCERT Class 9 English Grammar

52. Identify the subject in the sentence: "The tall boy in the blue shirt won the race."
(a) The tall boy in the blue shirt (b) The blue shirt (c) The race (d) Won
Answer: (a)
Solution: "The race" tempts students because it is a noun near the verb, but it is the object, not the doer. The subject is the complete noun phrase performing the action "won", so the answer is "The tall boy in the blue shirt."
Source: NCERT Class 8 English Grammar

53. Which sentence is a simple sentence?
(a) She sings, and she dances (b) Although he was tired, he worked (c) The sun rises in the east (d) When the bell rang, the students left
Answer: (c)
Solution: "She sings, and she dances" looks short and easy, which tempts students, but it has two independent clauses joined by "and", making it compound. A simple sentence has only one subject-verb pair, so the answer is "The sun rises in the east."

54. A compound sentence is formed by joining two independent clauses with:
(a) a subordinating conjunction (b) a coordinating conjunction (c) a relative pronoun (d) a preposition
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Subordinating conjunction" is the tempting wrong choice because it also joins clauses, but it creates a complex sentence, not a compound one. Coordinating conjunctions (FANBOYS: for, and, nor, but, or, yet, so) join equals, so the answer is "a coordinating conjunction."

Answer: (a)
Solution: "What a beautiful day it is" tempts students because it states something, but it expresses strong emotion, making it exclamatory. A declarative sentence simply states a fact, so the answer is "The train leaves at six o'clock."

57. Which sentence is in the correct SVO (Subject–Verb–Object) order?
(a) Quickly the boy his homework finished (b) His homework the boy quickly finished (c) The boy finished his homework quickly (d) Finished the boy his homework quickly
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Quickly the boy his homework finished" tempts Hindi speakers because Hindi allows flexible word order, but English demands Subject–Verb–Object. Only one option follows that order naturally, so the answer is "The boy finished his homework quickly."

(a) ends with an exclamation mark (b) asks a question (c) gives a command (d) expresses a wish
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Gives a command" tempts students because commands can sound forceful like questions, but commands are imperative sentences. Interrogative means question-asking, so the answer is "asks a question."

Answer: (d)
Solution: "The baby cried loudly" tempts students because it is short, but it has a subject and a finite verb, making it a clause. A phrase is a group of words without a subject-verb pair, so the answer is "in the middle of the night."

61. Spot the error: Part P: "One of my friends" / Part Q: "have gone" / Part R: "to Delhi yesterday."
(a) Part Q (b) Part P (c) Part R (d) No error
Answer: (a)
Solution: Part P tempts students because "friends" is plural and sits right before the verb, but the real subject is "One", which is singular — this is the classic Intervening Phrase trap. The verb must be "has gone", so the error is in Part Q.

62. Choose the correctly structured complex sentence:
(a) Because he was ill, he stayed at home (b) He was ill, so he stayed at home (c) He was ill and stayed at home (d) Being ill, staying at home
Answer: (a)
Solution: "He was ill, so he stayed at home" tempts students because it shows cause and effect, but "so" is a coordinating conjunction, making it compound, not complex. A complex sentence needs one independent and one dependent clause, so the answer is "Because he was ill, he stayed at home."

Answer: (b)
Solution: "The boy who stood first is my cousin" tempts students because it has a "who" clause, but that clause describes a noun, making it an adjective (relative) clause. "That she was tired" acts as the object of "told", so the answer is "She told me that she was tired."

65. Choose the correct transformation: "He is too weak to walk." (into a complex sentence)
(a) He is weak, so he cannot walk (b) He is weak and he cannot walk (c) Being weak, he cannot walk (d) He is so weak that he cannot walk
Answer: (d)
Solution: "He is weak, so he cannot walk" tempts students because it keeps the meaning, but "so" makes it compound, not complex. "So…that…cannot" correctly converts "too…to" into a complex structure, so the answer is "He is so weak that he cannot walk."

67. Which sentence is a compound-complex sentence?
(a) She sings beautifully (b) He came late because he missed the bus (c) Although it rained, we went out, and we enjoyed the trip (d) The sun set, and the birds returned home
Answer: (c)
Solution: "The sun set, and the birds returned home" tempts students because it is long, but it has only two independent clauses, making it merely compound. A compound-complex sentence needs at least two independent clauses plus one dependent clause, so the answer is "Although it rained, we went out, and we enjoyed the trip."

68. Choose the correctly punctuated and structured sentence:
(a) I like tea, however, I prefer coffee today (b) I like tea; however, I prefer coffee today (c) I like tea however I prefer coffee today (d) I like tea, however I prefer coffee, today
Answer: (b)
Solution: "I like tea, however, I prefer coffee today" tempts students because commas around "however" look natural, but joining two independent clauses with only a comma is a comma splice. A semicolon before the conjunctive adverb is required, so the answer is "I like tea; however, I prefer coffee today."

71. Which sentence uses a gerund correctly as the subject?
(a) Swim is good for health (b) Swimming is good for health (c) To swimming is good for health (d) Swam is good for health
Answer: (b)
Solution: "To swimming is good for health" tempts students because "to" often precedes verbs, but "to + -ing" is not a valid infinitive or gerund form. A gerund is the -ing form used as a noun, so the answer is "Swimming is good for health."

72. Choose the correct indirect question structure:
(a) Do you know where is the station (b) Do you know where is station the (c) Do you know where the station (d) Do you know where the station is
Answer: (d)
Solution: "Do you know where is the station" is the classic Hindi-speaker trap ("क्या तुम जानते हो स्टेशन कहाँ है?") because it keeps the direct-question inversion inside an indirect question. Indirect questions use statement order (subject before verb), so the answer is "Do you know where the station is."
Source: NCERT Class 10 English Grammar
