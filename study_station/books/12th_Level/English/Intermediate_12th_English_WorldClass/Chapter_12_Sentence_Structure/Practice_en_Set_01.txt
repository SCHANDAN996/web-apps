1. Which of the following is a complete sentence?
(a) The children played in the garden (b) Running through the park in the morning (c) Because the rain stopped suddenly (d) Under the old wooden bridge
Answer: (a)
Solution: A complete sentence needs a subject and a finite verb expressing a full thought. Options like "Running through the park" and "Because the rain stopped" are fragments — the tempting trap is the long phrase that feels like a sentence but lacks a finite verb. Only "The children played in the garden" has a subject (the children) and a finite verb (played), so the answer is "The children played in the garden".
Source: NCERT Class 9 English Grammar

2. Identify the subject in the sentence: "The tall boy in the red shirt won the race."
(a) The tall boy in the red shirt (b) The race (c) Won (d) Red shirt
Answer: (a)
Solution: The subject is who or what performs the action. The trap is picking "the race" because it sits near the verb, but the race is the object. The doer of "won" is the whole noun phrase "The tall boy in the red shirt", so the answer is "The tall boy in the red shirt".

3. Which sentence is a simple sentence?
(a) She sings, and he dances (b) Although it rained, we went out (c) When he arrived, the meeting started (d) The sun rises in the east
Answer: (d)
Solution: A simple sentence has one independent clause with one subject-verb pair. The trap is the sentence joined by "and", which looks short but is compound. "The sun rises in the east" has a single clause, so the answer is "The sun rises in the east".

4. A compound sentence is joined by:
(a) A subordinating conjunction (b) A relative pronoun (c) A coordinating conjunction (d) A preposition
Answer: (c)
Solution: Compound sentences join two independent clauses with coordinating conjunctions — remember FANBOYS (for, and, nor, but, or, yet, so). The trap is "subordinating conjunction", which creates complex sentences instead. So the answer is "A coordinating conjunction".

6. Which of the following is a complex sentence?
(a) Because she was ill, she stayed home (b) He ran fast and caught the bus (c) The dog barked loudly (d) Sit down and open your books
Answer: (a)
Solution: A complex sentence has one independent clause plus at least one dependent clause introduced by a subordinating conjunction like "because". The trap is the sentence with "and", which is compound, not complex. "Because she was ill, she stayed home" fits the definition, so the answer is "Because she was ill, she stayed home".

7. Spot the error: "One of my friend (P) / is coming (Q) / to the party tonight. (R) / No error (S)"
(a) No error (b) Part Q (c) Part R (d) Part P
Answer: (d)
Solution: Examiner's trap: "one of" must be followed by a plural noun. The verb "is" in Part Q sounds correct and tempts students to mark it, but the real error is in Part P — it should be "one of my friends". So the answer is Part P.

8. Which sentence is in passive voice?
(a) The teacher praised the student (b) The teacher will praise the student (c) The teacher is praising the student (d) The student was praised by the teacher
Answer: (d)
Solution: In passive voice, the object of the action becomes the subject and the verb takes "be + past participle" form. The trap is the continuous form "is praising", which has "is" but is still active. "The student was praised by the teacher" is passive, so the answer is "The student was praised by the teacher".

Answer: (b)
Solution: Sentences expressing strong emotion and ending with an exclamation mark are exclamatory. The trap is "interrogative" because the sentence begins with "What", but it asks no question. So the answer is "Exclamatory".

17. Which sentence is compound-complex?
(a) Although he was tired, he worked, and he finished the project (b) She smiled and waved (c) He works hard because he wants success (d) The bell rang loudly
Answer: (a)
Solution: A compound-complex sentence has at least two independent clauses plus one dependent clause. The trap is the sentence with "because", which is only complex since it has just one independent clause. The sentence with "although" plus "and" joining two independent clauses fits, so the answer is "Although he was tired, he worked, and he finished the project".

24. Which of the following has the correct subject-verb agreement?

Answer: (b)
Solution: "Neither of" takes a singular verb, and "news" is singular despite ending in s. The traps: "each of the boys have" sounds right because "boys" is plural, but "each" is singular; "news were" fails because news is uncountable singular. So the answer is "Neither of the answers is correct".

Solution: The clause "that he is honest" acts as the object of the verb "know", so it functions as a noun. The trap is "adjective clause" because the clause begins with "that", but it describes no noun — it names what is known. So the answer is "Noun clause".
