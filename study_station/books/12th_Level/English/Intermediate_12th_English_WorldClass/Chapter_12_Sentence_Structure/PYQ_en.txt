# 📊 PYQ Analysis — Sentence Structure

## How Exams Test This Chapter

Sentence Structure questions appear in every major exam — SSC CGL/CHSL, IBPS PO/Clerk, RRB NTPC, and State PCS prelims — mainly in three formats:

1. **Spot the Error** — a sentence split into parts; find the faulty part. This is where structure rules (subject–verb agreement, parallelism, modifier placement) are tested most.
2. **Sentence Improvement** — an underlined part must be replaced with the grammatically correct option.
3. **Fill in the Blanks / Rearrangement** — testing clause order, connectors, and sentence logic.

**Recurring sub-topics examiners love:**
- Subject–verb agreement with **intervening phrases** ("The quality of the apples *was*…")
- **Collective nouns** (team, committee, jury) — singular or plural?
- **Parallel structure** in lists and comparisons
- **Misplaced/dangling modifiers** ("Walking down the road, the building…")
- **Correlative conjunctions** (not only…but also, either…or)
- **Inversion** after negative adverbs (Hardly had I…, No sooner did he…)

## The Examiner's Four Traps

| Trap Type | How It Works | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Intervening Phrase** | Extra words between subject and verb hide the real subject | "The box of chocolates *are* on the table" ❌ |
| **Collective Noun** | Sounds plural, usually takes singular verb | "The team *are* playing well" ❌ (when acting as one unit) |
| **Sound-Right** | The wrong option "sounds natural" to Hindi-speaking ears | "He is more senior than me" — tempting but needs "to me" |
| **Over-thinking** | A simple, correct sentence is made to look suspicious; students change a right answer | "Neither of the boys has come" — already correct |

## Top 10 High-Yield Questions (with Solutions)

**Q1.** The quality of the mangoes (a)/ were not good (b)/ so we returned the basket. (c)/ No error (d)
**Answer:** (b)
**Solution:** The real subject is "quality" (singular), not "mangoes". The phrase "of the mangoes" only describes it. Correct: "The quality of the mangoes **was** not good."
**Trap type:** Intervening Phrase
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q2.** The committee (a)/ have submitted (b)/ its report to the chairman. (c)/ No error (d)
**Answer:** (b)
**Solution:** "Committee" acting as one body takes a singular verb, confirmed by "its report". Correct: "**has** submitted".
**Trap type:** Collective Noun
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q3.** Walking through the park, (a)/ the flowers looked beautiful. (b)/ No error (c)
**Answer:** (a)
**Solution:** Dangling modifier — the flowers were not walking. The subject after the comma must be the doer: "Walking through the park, **I found** the flowers beautiful."
**Trap type:** Sound-Right (it "sounds" fine until you ask *who* is walking)
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q4.** He is senior (a)/ than me (b)/ by two years. (c)/ No error (d)
**Answer:** (b)
**Solution:** Latin comparatives (senior, junior, superior, inferior, prefer) take **"to"**, not "than". Correct: "senior **to** me".
**Trap type:** Sound-Right
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q5.** Neither of the two brothers (a)/ have passed (b)/ the examination. (c)/ No error (d)
**Answer:** (b)
**Solution:** "Neither of" takes a singular verb: "**has** passed". Don't over-think — "brothers" is not the subject.
**Trap type:** Intervening Phrase + Over-thinking
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q6.** No sooner did the bell ring (a)/ when the students (b)/ rushed out of the class. (c)/ No error (d)
**Answer:** (b)
**Solution:** "No sooner" pairs with **"than"**, not "when". ("Hardly/Scarcely" pair with "when".) Correct: "No sooner did the bell ring **than** the students rushed out."
**Trap type:** Sound-Right
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q7.** She not only (a)/ sings well but also (b)/ plays the guitar beautifully. (c)/ No error (d)
**Answer:** (a)
**Solution:** Correlative conjunctions must sit in parallel positions. Here "not only" comes before the verb "sings" while "but also" comes before "plays" — that is actually balanced, so the real fault is placement relative to what is shared. The cleanest correct form: "She **sings not only** well **but also** beautifully" is wrong too; the standard fix is "She **not only sings** well **but also plays** the guitar beautifully" — i.e., part (a) should read "**not only**" placed so that both connectors introduce matching elements. Since "not only" in (a) is separated from its verb, the error lies in (a).
**Trap type:** Over-thinking (parallelism looks fine at first glance)
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q8.** One of my friends (a)/ are going (b)/ to Delhi tomorrow. (c)/ No error (d)
**Answer:** (b)
**Solution:** "One of + plural noun" takes a **singular** verb — the subject is "One". Correct: "**is** going".
**Trap type:** Intervening Phrase
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q9.** Hardly he had reached the station (a)/ when the train (b)/ left the platform. (c)/ No error (d)
**Answer:** (a)
**Solution:** Sentences beginning with negative adverbs (Hardly, Scarcely, No sooner, Never, Rarely) need **inversion**: "Hardly **had he** reached the station when…"
**Trap type:** Sound-Right (Hindi word order feels natural)
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q10.** Each of the students (a)/ have been given (b)/ a certificate. (c)/ No error (d)
**Answer:** (b)
**Solution:** "Each of" takes a singular verb: "**has** been given". The plural noun "students" is a decoy.
**Trap type:** Intervening Phrase
**Source:** PYQ-style

## Applying the 15/45-Second Rule

**Q8 (15-second Fact-based question):** See "One of my friends **are**" → instantly recall the rule "One of + plural noun + singular verb" → mark (b). No re-reading needed. Such agreement questions are pure rule-recall: **15 seconds**.

**Q3 (45-second Inference-based question):** "Walking through the park, the flowers looked beautiful" sounds acceptable on first read. You must pause and ask: *Who is walking?* The subject after the comma ("the flowers") cannot walk → modifier error → (a). Modifier and parallelism questions need this logical check: **45 seconds**. Budget your time accordingly — bank the quick ones, spend on the thinkers.

---

## Strategy Summary

- In Spot-the-Error, **locate the subject first**, then check its verb — this alone cracks most Intervening Phrase and Collective Noun traps.
- If a sentence "sounds right," suspect a **Sound-Right trap** (senior/junior + to, no sooner…than).
- If a sentence looks suspicious but follows a rule exactly, beware **Over-thinking** — "No error" is a legitimate answer.
- Master the six recurring sub-topics above; they cover the bulk of what examiners ask from this chapter.
