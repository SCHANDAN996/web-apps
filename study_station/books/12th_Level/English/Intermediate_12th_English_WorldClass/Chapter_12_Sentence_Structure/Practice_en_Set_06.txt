# Sentence Structure — Practice Set 6 (Questions 126–150)

126. Identify the type of sentence: "Although the rain had stopped, the streets remained flooded."
(a) Simple sentence (b) Compound sentence (c) Complex sentence (d) Compound-complex sentence
Answer: (c)
Solution: The tempting wrong choice is compound sentence, but a compound sentence joins two independent clauses with a coordinating conjunction like "and" or "but". Here "Although the rain had stopped" is a dependent (subordinate) clause joined to one independent clause, which makes it a complex sentence, so the answer is complex sentence.
Source: NCERT Class 10 English Grammar

127. Choose the correctly punctuated and structured sentence.
(a) A holiday being, the office was closed. (b) Being a holiday, the office was closed. (c) Being a holiday the office was closed. (d) It being a holiday, the office was closed.
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is the dangling modifier in "Being a holiday, the office was closed" — it wrongly suggests the office is a holiday. The absolute construction "It being a holiday" gives the participle its own subject and is grammatically sound, so the answer is "It being a holiday, the office was closed."

128. Spot the error: "P: Hardly had he reached the station / Q: then the train / R: started moving. / S: No error"
(a) Part P (b) Part Q (c) Part R (d) No error
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is that "then" sounds natural in Hindi-influenced English ("jaise hi... waise hi"). The correct correlative pair is "Hardly/Scarcely... when", so Part Q must read "when the train". The error lies in Part Q, so the answer is Part Q.
Source: PYQ-style

129. Which sentence contains a compound-complex structure?
(a) She sings and dances well. (b) He was ill, so he stayed home. (c) He stayed home because he was ill. (d) Because he was ill, he stayed home, and his sister called the doctor.
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is "He was ill, so he stayed home", which is only compound (two independent clauses, no subordinate clause). A compound-complex sentence needs at least two independent clauses plus one dependent clause: "Because he was ill" (dependent) + "he stayed home" and "his sister called the doctor" (two independent clauses joined by "and"), so the answer is "Because he was ill, he stayed home, and his sister called the doctor."
Source: NCERT Class 10 English Grammar

130. Choose the correct transformation of "He is too weak to walk" without changing the meaning.
(a) He is so weak that he cannot walk. (b) He is very weak and cannot walk. (c) He is weak enough not to walk. (d) He is so weak to walk.
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is "He is very weak and cannot walk", which changes the degree of meaning conveyed by "too...to". The standard conversion of "too + adjective + to" is "so + adjective + that... cannot", so the answer is "He is so weak that he cannot walk."
Source: PYQ-style

131. Spot the error: "P: No sooner did the bell ring / Q: when the students / R: rushed out of the class. / S: No error"
(a) Part P (b) No error (c) Part R (d) Part Q
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is pairing "No sooner" with "when" because "Hardly... when" is correct; students mix the two pairs. "No sooner" always takes "than", so Part Q should read "than the students". The error is in Part Q, so the answer is Part Q.
Source: PYQ-style

132. Identify the subject in the sentence: "Down the street marched the band."
(a) street (b) Down the street (c) band (d) marched
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is "street", which sits right before the verb and looks like the subject in this inverted sentence. Ask "who or what marched?" — the band marched, so the answer is band.
Source: NCERT Class 9 English Grammar

133. Choose the sentence with correct parallel structure.
(a) She likes reading, swimming, and to dance. (b) She likes reading, swimming, and dancing. (c) She likes to read, swimming, and dancing. (d) She likes reading, to swim, and dancing.
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is "She likes reading, swimming, and to dance", which feels acceptable in speech but breaks parallelism by mixing a gerund with an infinitive. All items in a series must share the same grammatical form — three gerunds here — so the answer is "She likes reading, swimming, and dancing."

134. Spot the error: "P: The teacher, as well as the students, / Q: were present / R: at the meeting. / S: No error"
(a) Part Q (b) No error (c) Part R (d) Part P
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is the intervening phrase "as well as the students", which tempts a plural verb. With "as well as", the verb agrees with the first subject, "the teacher" (singular), so Part Q must be "was present". The error is in Part Q, so the answer is Part Q.

135. Which of the following is a fragment, not a complete sentence?
(a) The boy ran down the hill. (b) Running down the hill. (c) Down the hill ran the boy. (d) The boy, running fast, fell.
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is "Down the hill ran the boy", which looks odd but is a complete inverted sentence with subject and verb. "Running down the hill" has no finite verb and no subject performing a complete action, so the answer is "Running down the hill."
Source: NCERT Class 9 English Grammar

136. Choose the correct combination of the sentences: "He worked hard. He wanted to pass the exam."
(a) He worked hard for passing the exam. (b) He worked hard and he passed the exam. (c) He worked hard so that he might pass the exam. (d) Working hard, the exam was passed by him.
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is "He worked hard and he passed the exam", which adds a result never stated in the original. The purpose relationship is best expressed by "so that... might", so the answer is "He worked hard so that he might pass the exam."

137. Spot the error: "P: Neither of the two boys / Q: have done / R: his homework. / S: No error"
(a) Part Q (b) Part R (c) Part P (d) No error
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is "two boys", which pulls the mind toward a plural verb. "Neither of" is singular and takes a singular verb, so Part Q should be "has done". The error is in Part Q, so the answer is Part Q.

138. Identify the clause type in bold: "What he said surprised everyone."
(a) Adjective clause (b) Noun clause (c) Principal clause (d) Adverb clause
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is principal clause, because the words form a complete idea inside the sentence. But "What he said" functions as the subject of the verb "surprised" — a clause doing a noun's job is a noun clause, so the answer is noun clause.
Source: NCERT Class 10 English Grammar

139. Choose the correctly structured sentence.
(a) Unless you work hard, you will fail. (b) Unless you work hard, you will not fail. (c) Unless you do not work hard, you will fail. (d) Until you work hard, you will fail.
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is "Unless you do not work hard", a double negative common in Hindi-influenced English ("jab tak mehnat nahi karoge"). "Unless" already means "if not", so no extra "not" is needed, and the answer is "Unless you work hard, you will fail."
Source: PYQ-style

140. Spot the error: "P: Scarcely he had stepped out of the house / Q: when it began / R: to rain heavily. / S: No error"
(a) Part R (b) Part Q (c) Part P (d) No error
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is Part Q, because "scarcely... when" is the correct pair and looks suspicious. The real error is word order: when a negative adverb like "Scarcely" begins a sentence, inversion is required — "Scarcely had he stepped out". The error is in Part P, so the answer is Part P.
Source: PYQ-style

141. Which sentence uses the correlative conjunction correctly?
(a) Neither you come in time, or leave the job. (b) Either you come in time, nor leave the job. (c) Either you come in time, or leave the job. (d) Both you come in time, or leave the job.
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is "Either... nor", which mixes two pairs because "neither... nor" is so familiar. The fixed pairs are either...or, neither...nor, both...and, so the answer is "Either you come in time, or leave the job."
Source: PYQ-style

142. Identify the sentence pattern of: "The teacher gave the students homework."
(a) Subject + Verb + Object (b) Subject + Verb + Object + Complement (c) Subject + Verb + Complement (d) Subject + Verb + Indirect Object + Direct Object
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is Subject + Verb + Object, which ignores that there are two objects. "The students" receives the action indirectly and "homework" is what was given directly, so the answer is Subject + Verb + Indirect Object + Direct Object.

143. Spot the error: "P: One of my friends / Q: are going / R: to Delhi tomorrow. / S: No error"
(a) Part Q (b) Part P (c) Part R (d) No error
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is "friends", the nearest noun, which tempts the plural verb "are". The true subject is "One", which is singular, so Part Q must read "is going". The error is in Part Q, so the answer is Part Q.

144. Choose the correct transformation of "As soon as he saw the police, he ran away" using "No sooner".
(a) No sooner he saw the police than he ran away. (b) No sooner did he see the police than he ran away. (c) No sooner did he see the police when he ran away. (d) No sooner he had seen the police then he ran away.
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is "No sooner did he see the police when he ran away", which gets the inversion right but pairs "No sooner" with "when". The correct structure is "No sooner + did + subject + verb... than", so the answer is "No sooner did he see the police than he ran away."
Source: PYQ-style

145. Which sentence contains a dangling modifier?
(a) Walking to school, I met an old friend. (b) While walking to school, I got wet in the rain. (c) Walking to school, the rain started. (d) I was walking to school when it rained.
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is "Walking to school, I met an old friend", which is correct because "I" is doing the walking. In "Walking to school, the rain started", the rain appears to be walking to school — the modifier has no proper subject, so the answer is "Walking to school, the rain started."
Source: PYQ-style

146. Identify the type of the underlined clause: "This is the house where I was born."
(a) Noun clause (b) Adverb clause of place (c) Principal clause (d) Adjective clause
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is adverb clause of place, because "where" usually signals place. Here "where I was born" describes the noun "house", and a clause that qualifies a noun is an adjective (relative) clause, so the answer is adjective clause.
Source: NCERT Class 10 English Grammar

147. Spot the error: "P: The Ganges is / Q: one of the longest river / R: in India. / S: No error"
(a) Part P (b) Part Q (c) Part R (d) No error
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is that "one of the longest river" sounds acceptable in casual speech. The rule is "one of the + superlative + plural noun", so Part Q must read "one of the longest rivers". The error is in Part Q, so the answer is Part Q.

148. Choose the sentence with the correct use of an absolute phrase.
(a) The weather being fine, we went for a picnic. (b) The weather being fine, a picnic was gone by us. (c) Being fine weather, we went picnic. (d) The weather fine, a picnic we went.
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is "The weather being fine, a picnic was gone by us", which keeps the absolute phrase but distorts the main clause into an unnatural passive. An absolute phrase ("noun + participle") must attach to a clear, active main clause, so the answer is "The weather being fine, we went for a picnic."
Source: PYQ-style

149. Which option best combines: "The boy is my cousin. He won the first prize."?
(a) The boy who won the first prize is my cousin. (b) The boy is my cousin and he won the first prize which is good. (c) My cousin is a boy and won prize. (d) The boy, he is my cousin, won the first prize.
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is "The boy, he is my cousin, won the first prize", which repeats the subject ("the boy... he"), a common Hindi-speaker error. A relative clause with "who" joins the ideas smoothly into one grammatical sentence, so the answer is "The boy who won the first prize is my cousin."
Source: PYQ-style

150. Spot the error: "P: Not only he cheated his friends / Q: but also / R: his own family. / S: No error"
(a) Part R (b) Part Q (c) No error (d) Part P
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is Part R, which looks incomplete but is a legitimate elliptical construction after "but also". The real error is inversion: when "Not only" begins a clause, the auxiliary must precede the subject — "Not only did he cheat his friends". The error is in Part P, so the answer is Part P.
Source: PYQ-style
