Card 1
Front: What are the four basic sentence types by structure?
Back: Simple (one independent clause), Compound (two independent clauses joined by FANBOYS/semicolon), Complex (one independent + one dependent clause), Compound-Complex (two independent + at least one dependent clause).

Card 2
Front: What does FANBOYS stand for? (Mnemonic)
Back: The seven coordinating conjunctions: For, And, Nor, But, Or, Yet, So. Use them to join two independent clauses in a compound sentence.

Card 3
Front: Identify the structure: "Although it rained, we played cricket."
Back: Complex sentence. "Although it rained" is a dependent (subordinate) clause; "we played cricket" is the independent clause.

Card 4
Front: Examiner's Trap: "The quality of the mangoes were good." — Spot the error.
Back: "were" should be "was". The subject is "quality" (singular), not "mangoes". This is the Intervening Phrase trap — the verb agrees with the real subject, not the nearest noun.

Card 5
Front: What is a clause? How is it different from a phrase?
Back: A clause has a subject and a finite verb ("she sings"). A phrase is a group of words without a subject-verb pair ("in the morning"). Every sentence needs at least one independent clause.

Card 6
Front: What is a run-on sentence? Give an example.
Back: Two independent clauses joined without proper punctuation or conjunction. Wrong: "He was tired he went to sleep." Correct: "He was tired, so he went to sleep."

Card 7
Front: What is a comma splice and how do you fix it?
Back: Joining two independent clauses with only a comma: "She came, she sat down." Fix it with a conjunction (and), a semicolon, or by splitting into two sentences.

Card 8
Front: Mnemonic: How do you remember common subordinating conjunctions?
Back: "AAAWWUBBIS" — After, Although, As, When, While, Until, Because, Before, If, Since. These begin dependent clauses in complex sentences.

Card 9
Front: Examiner's Trap: "Neither of the two boys have done his homework." — Correct or wrong?
Back: Wrong. "Neither" is singular, so use "has": "Neither of the two boys has done his homework." Sound-Right trap — the plural noun "boys" misleads the ear.

Card 10
Front: What is the difference between an independent and a dependent clause?
Back: An independent clause expresses a complete thought and can stand alone ("The sun set"). A dependent clause begins with a subordinating conjunction or relative pronoun and cannot stand alone ("because the sun set").

Card 11
Front: Convert to a compound sentence: "He worked hard. He passed the exam."
Back: "He worked hard, so he passed the exam." (Two independent clauses joined by the coordinating conjunction "so" with a comma.)

Card 12
Front: What is a sentence fragment?
Back: An incomplete sentence missing a subject, a finite verb, or a complete thought. Fragment: "Running down the street." Complete: "The boy was running down the street."

Card 13
Front: In the sentence "The boy who won the race is my friend," identify the clauses.
Back: Independent clause: "The boy is my friend." Dependent (relative) clause: "who won the race" — it modifies "boy" and cannot stand alone.

Card 14
Front: What is the basic word order of an English sentence?
Back: Subject + Verb + Object (SVO). Hindi uses SOV ("Main kitab padhta hoon"), but English needs "I read a book" — never "I a book read."

Card 15
Front: Examiner's Trap: "What you are doing?" — Why is this wrong?
Back: In interrogative sentences the auxiliary comes before the subject: "What are you doing?" Hindi-speakers often keep statement order in questions — a classic Linguistic Bridge error.

Card 16
Front: How do you form a compound-complex sentence? Give an example.
Back: Combine at least two independent clauses with at least one dependent clause: "Although it was late, he finished the work, and he submitted the report."

Card 17
Front: Which punctuation marks can join two independent clauses without a conjunction?
Back: A semicolon (;) or a colon (:) when the second clause explains the first. Example: "She was ill; she stayed home." A comma alone is never enough (comma splice).

Card 18
Front: Mnemonic: How do you check subject-verb agreement quickly?
Back: "Strip the sentence" trick — remove all intervening phrases (of..., along with..., as well as...) and match the verb to the bare subject. "The box of chocolates is (not are) on the table."

Card 19
Front: What happens to the comma when a dependent clause comes first in a complex sentence?
Back: Use a comma after the opening dependent clause: "Because it rained, the match was cancelled." When the dependent clause comes second, no comma: "The match was cancelled because it rained."

Card 20
Front: "Each of the students have submitted the assignment." — Spot and fix the error.
Back: "have" should be "has". "Each" is always singular, regardless of the plural noun after "of". Trap type: Intervening Phrase / Sound-Right.
