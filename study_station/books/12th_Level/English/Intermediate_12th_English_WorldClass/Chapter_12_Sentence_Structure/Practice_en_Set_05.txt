101. Choose the grammatically correct sentence.
(a) Neither of the two boys have done his homework. (b) Neither of the two boys have done their homework. (c) Neither of the two boys has done his homework. (d) Neither of the two boys has did his homework.
Answer: (c)
Solution: The tempting option uses "have" because "boys" sounds plural, but "neither of" takes a singular verb. "Did" after "has" is also wrong since "has" needs a past participle. So the answer is "Neither of the two boys has done his homework."

102. Spot the error: Part P: The quality of the mangoes / Part Q: were not good / Part R: so the customers / Part S: refused to buy them.
(a) Part P (b) Part Q (c) Part R (d) No error
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is "mangoes" sitting just before the verb, which lures you toward "were". The real subject is "quality" (singular), so the verb must be "was". The error is in Part Q.

103. Choose the correctly framed question.
(a) Why you are late today? (b) Why late you are today? (c) Why are you late today? (d) Why you late are today?
Answer: (c)
Solution: Hindi speakers often keep statement order in questions ("Why you are late?"), which is the trap. In English wh-questions, the auxiliary comes before the subject: "Why are you…". So the answer is "Why are you late today?"

109. Choose the correctly punctuated and structured sentence.
(a) Being a rainy day, I stayed at home. (b) It being a rainy day, I stayed at home. (c) Being a rainy day, staying at home was done by me. (d) A rainy day being it, home I stayed at.
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Being a rainy day, I stayed…" is a dangling modifier — it suggests I am the rainy day, which is the trap. Adding the subject "It" fixes the absolute phrase. So the answer is "It being a rainy day, I stayed at home."

125. Choose the correct transformation: "Open the door," said the teacher to the boy.
(a) The teacher requested the boy open the door. (b) The teacher said the boy open the door. (c) The teacher told to the boy to open the door. (d) The teacher ordered the boy to open the door.
Answer: (d)
Solution: Imperatives in reported speech use "ordered/requested + object + to-infinitive". "Told to the boy" wrongly inserts "to" after "told" — a classic trap. So the answer is "The teacher ordered the boy to open the door."
