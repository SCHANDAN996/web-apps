# 🪄 Short Tricks + Skip Strategy — Sentence Structure

## Memory Boxes (Mnemonics)

**Box 1 — FANBOYS (Coordinating Conjunctions)**
```
F — For
A — And
N — Nor
B — But
O — Or
Y — Yet
S — So
```
Trick: *"FANBOYS join equals — two full sentences, never a boss and a servant."*
Use it to spot run-ons: if a FANBOYS word joins two independent clauses, a comma must come before it.

---

**Box 2 — SVA: "The Verb Marries the Subject, Not the Neighbour"**
```
The bouquet of roses IS fresh.   (subject = bouquet, not roses)
```
Trick: Cross out everything between the subject and the verb (of-phrases, along with, as well as, together with). What remains must agree.

---

**Box 3 — "Each, Every, Either, Neither, One" → Singular**
```
Mnemonic: "E-E-E-N-O walk ALONE."
Each boy has. | Neither answer is. | One of the girls was.
```
Trap: "One of the boys **have**" ❌ → **has** ✔

---

**Box 4 — Interrupter Test for Commas**
```
Read the sentence aloud and drop the middle part.
"My brother, who lives in Pune, is a doctor."
Drop "who lives in Pune" → sentence still complete → commas correct.
```

---

**Box 5 — Fragment Finder: "Can It Stand Alone?"**
```
Ask the sentence: "Do you have a subject AND a verb AND a complete thought?"
"Because he was late." → No complete thought → FRAGMENT.
Fix: attach it — "Because he was late, he missed the bus."
```

---

**Box 6 — Modifier Rule: "Keep the Describer Next to the Described"**
```
❌ Walking down the road, the tree was seen.
✔ Walking down the road, I saw the tree.
```
Trick: After an opening "-ing" phrase, the very next noun must be the doer of the action.

---

**Box 7 — Parallel Structure: "Same Dress Code"**
```
❌ She likes reading, swimming, and to dance.
✔ She likes reading, swimming, and dancing.
```
Trick: In a list, all items must wear the same grammatical dress (all -ing, all to-verb, or all nouns).

---

**Box 8 — There/Here Inversion Trick**
```
"There ARE three books on the table."
```
Trick: Ignore "There/Here" — find the real subject AFTER the verb and match the verb to it.

---

**Box 9 — Collective Noun Quick Check**
```
Team, jury, committee, family, audience
Acting as ONE unit → singular: "The team has won."
Acting separately → plural: "The jury are divided in their opinions."
```
Trick: *"United it stands (singular), divided it falls (plural)."*

---

**Box 10 — Question Formation: "Helping Verb Jumps First"**
```
❌ What you are doing?
✔ What are you doing?
```
Trick: In Wh-questions, the helping verb (is/are/do/did/have) jumps BEFORE the subject. In indirect questions it does NOT: "Tell me what you **are doing**." ✔

---

**Box 11 — Double Negative Alarm**
```
❌ He didn't do nothing.
✔ He didn't do anything. / He did nothing.
```
Trick: Two negatives in standard English = one positive. If you see "not + nothing/nowhere/nobody," an error is hiding there.

---

**Box 12 — Clause Counting for Sentence Type**
```
1 independent clause                    → Simple
2+ independent (FANBOYS/semicolon)      → Compound
1 independent + 1+ dependent            → Complex
2+ independent + 1+ dependent           → Compound-Complex
```
Trick: *"Count the bosses (independent) and the servants (dependent)."*

---

**Box 13 — Semicolon Rule: "Full Sentences on Both Sides"**
```
✔ He studied hard; he passed.
❌ He studied hard; because he wanted to pass.
```
Trick: A semicolon is a weak full stop — both sides must be complete sentences.

---

**Box 14 — "Sound-Right" Trap Guard**
```
❌ The news are good.   ✔ The news is good.
❌ Mathematics are hard. ✔ Mathematics is hard.
```
Trick: Words ending in -s that are actually singular: news, physics, mathematics, economics, measles. *"Looks plural, acts singular."*

---

## ⏭️ Skip Strategy (Exam Hall)

**Tip 1 — Skip the "Over-thinking" Question.**
If a sentence-correction question has very long options and you have read it twice without spotting the error, mark it and move on. Sentence-structure questions are mostly Fact-based (15-second rule): the error is either visible in one read (agreement, fragment, modifier) or it is not worth 90 seconds. Return only if time remains.

**Tip 2 — Never Skip "Find the Sentence Type" Questions.**
Clause-counting questions (simple/compound/complex) are pure formula questions — Box 12 solves them in under 15 seconds. These are guaranteed marks; attempt them first.

**Tip 3 — Skip Rearrangement (PQRS) Only When the First Link Is Invisible.**
If you cannot find the opening sentence or one fixed pair within 30 seconds, the whole chain will eat 2+ minutes. Park it. But if you spot even one mandatory pair (e.g., a pronoun that must follow its noun), solve it — one pair usually unlocks the entire sequence.
