🧒 Feynman Technique — Sentence Structure Explained Like You're 12

The Hardest Concept: Why "What you are doing?" is Wrong

Imagine English sentences are like a train. Every train has an engine (the Subject) and it must come first, followed by the coaches (the Verb and everything else). This is the golden track of English:

Subject → Verb → Object/Rest (S → V → O)

Now, when you ask a question, English does something strange — it pulls a small "helper engine" (do/does/did/is/are/was/have/can/will) out of the train and parks it BEFORE the main engine:

Helper → Subject → Main Verb → Rest

So: "You are doing what?" becomes "What are you doing?"
The helper "are" jumps in front of "you". The main verb "doing" stays behind.

Here's where most students crash the train: they move the question word (What) to the front but forget to move the helper. So they say "What you are doing?" — the question word moved, but the helper didn't. Half a job = a broken sentence.

A Daily-Life Example

Think of a school assembly line. Normally: Class Teacher (subject) stands first, students (verb/rest) follow. But on Inspection Day (question day), the Inspector (question word) comes first, AND the Monitor (helper verb) must step out and stand right behind the Inspector. If the Monitor stays in the line, the arrangement is wrong.

Normal day: The teacher is teaching. → She is singing.
Inspection day: What is she singing? (Inspector "What" + Monitor "is" stepped out)

The Unforgettable Trick: "Q-H-S-V"

Remember the question formula as QHSV — "Questions Have Special Vehicles":

Q = Question word (What/Where/Why/When/How)
H = Helper verb (is/are/do/does/did/can/will/have)
S = Subject
V = Main verb

What (Q) + are (H) + you (S) + doing (V)? ✅
What (Q) + you (S) + are + doing? ❌ — the Special Vehicle never arrived!

One more twist: if there is NO question word (yes/no questions), the Helper itself leads the train:
"Are you coming?" — Helper first, then Subject, then Verb.

And the sneaky exception: when the question word IS the subject, no helper jumps out:
"Who called you?" ✅ (not "Who did call you?") — because "Who" is already the engine.
