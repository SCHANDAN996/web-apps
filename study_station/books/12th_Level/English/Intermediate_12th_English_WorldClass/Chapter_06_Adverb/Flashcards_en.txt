Card 1
Front: What is an adverb?
Back: A word that modifies a verb, an adjective, or another adverb. It answers how, when, where, how often, or to what extent. Example: She sings beautifully.

Card 2
Front: How are most adverbs of manner formed?
Back: By adding -ly to an adjective: quick → quickly, careful → carefully. (Adjectives ending in -y change y to i: easy → easily.)

Card 3
Front: Which adjectives do NOT take -ly to become adverbs?
Back: Friendly, lively, lonely, lovely, cowardly — these are adjectives only. Say "in a friendly manner," not "friendlily."

Card 4
Front: Mnemonic — What does "MPT" stand for in adverb order?
Back: Manner → Place → Time. Correct: "She sang beautifully at the concert yesterday." (How → Where → When)

Card 5
Front: Where does an adverb of frequency go in a sentence?
Back: Before the main verb but after the verb 'to be': "He always comes late." / "He is always late."

Card 6
Front: Examiner's Trap — "He runs fastly." Correct or wrong?
Back: Wrong. 'Fast' is both an adjective and an adverb — there is no word 'fastly'. Correct: "He runs fast."

Card 7
Front: What is the difference between 'hard' and 'hardly'?
Back: 'Hard' = with effort (He works hard). 'Hardly' = almost not (He hardly works = he barely works). Meanings are opposite!

Card 8
Front: What is the difference between 'late' and 'lately'?
Back: 'Late' = after the expected time (He came late). 'Lately' = recently (I haven't seen him lately).

Card 9
Front: Which adverbs have the same form as their adjectives?
Back: Fast, hard, early, late, daily, long, straight, enough. Example: "an early train" (adj) / "He came early" (adv).

Card 10
Front: Mnemonic — How to remember what an adverb modifies?
Back: "VAA" — Verb, Adjective, Adverb. An adverb can modify all three, but NEVER a noun.

Card 11
Front: Examiner's Trap — "She is very beautiful." vs "She is too beautiful." Which shows excess?
Back: 'Too' shows excess (more than wanted, often negative): "The tea is too hot to drink." 'Very' just intensifies: "The tea is very hot." Don't swap them.

Card 12
Front: Where should 'only' be placed, and why does it matter?
Back: Just before the word it modifies — position changes meaning. "Only he lent me money" (no one else did) vs "He lent me only money" (nothing else).

Card 13
Front: What are conjunctive adverbs? Give three examples.
Back: Adverbs that connect two independent clauses: however, therefore, moreover, consequently, nevertheless. Example: "He was ill; therefore, he stayed home."

Card 14
Front: What is the difference between 'ago' and 'before'?
Back: 'Ago' = back from now, with simple past (I met him two days ago). 'Before' = earlier than a past point, often with past perfect (I had met him two days before).

Card 15
Front: Can an adverb modify a noun? What modifies a noun?
Back: No. Only adjectives modify nouns. Trap: "She is a beautifully dancer" ❌ → "She is a beautiful dancer" ✓ (but "She dances beautifully").

Card 16
Front: What are interrogative adverbs?
Back: Adverbs used to ask questions: how, when, where, why. Example: "Where do you live?"

Card 17
Front: What is wrong with "He is enough tall to reach the shelf"?
Back: 'Enough' comes AFTER the adjective/adverb it modifies: "He is tall enough to reach the shelf." (But before a noun: "enough money.")

Card 18
Front: What are relative adverbs? Give examples.
Back: Adverbs that join clauses like relative pronouns: where, when, why. Example: "This is the house where I was born."

Card 19
Front: Examiner's Trap — "I am much happy." Correct or wrong?
Back: Wrong. Use 'very' with positive degree and 'much' with comparative degree: "I am very happy." / "I am much happier now."

Card 20
Front: What is the correct order when multiple adverbs of time appear together?
Back: Smaller time unit before larger: "He was born at 5 a.m. on Monday in June 2000." (time → day → month → year)
