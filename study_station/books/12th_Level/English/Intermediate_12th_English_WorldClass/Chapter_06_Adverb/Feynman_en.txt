# 🧒 Feynman Technique — Adverbs, Explained Like You're 12

## The Hardest Concept: Adjective vs. Adverb (and Where to Put Them)

Imagine words in a sentence are people at a party.

- A **noun** is a person (Ravi, the dog, the teacher).
- A **verb** is what that person is *doing* (runs, sings, eats).
- An **adjective** is a sticker you paste **on the person** — it describes *them*: "Ravi is **tall**." "The dog is **brown**."
- An **adverb** is a sticker you paste **on the action** — it describes *how, when, where, or how often* the action happens: "Ravi runs **fast**." "She sings **beautifully**." "He **always** eats here."

So here's the one-question test that helps with most adjective–adverb questions in exams:

> **Ask: "Is this word describing a person/thing, or describing an action?"**
> - Person/thing → adjective.
> - Action → adverb.

## A Daily-Life Example

Your mother says: **"Drive safe!"** (Everyone says this in India.)

But wait — "drive" is an *action*. The word must describe *how* to drive, not what kind of driver you are. So grammatically correct English is:

> ✅ **"Drive safely!"**

The action "drive" needs an adverb (**safely**), not an adjective (**safe**).

Same logic:
- ❌ "He did the work **quick**." → ✅ "He did the work **quickly**."
- ❌ "She sings **beautiful**." → ✅ "She sings **beautifully**."

## The Sneaky Exception: Linking Verbs

Now the twist that examiners love. Some verbs don't show action — they just *connect* the subject to a description. These are **linking verbs**: *is, am, are, was, were, seem, look, feel, taste, smell, sound, appear, become.*

After a linking verb, the word describes the **subject**, not an action — so use an **adjective**:

- ✅ "The soup **tastes** good." (The soup *is* good — adjective.)
- ✅ "She **looks** happy." (She *is* happy — adjective.)
- ✅ "I **feel** bad about it." (Not "badly" — you're describing your state, not how well you feel things.)

**The Unforgettable Trick — the "IS" Swap:**

> Replace the verb with **"is/are"**. If the sentence still makes sense, use an **adjective**. If it sounds nonsense, use an **adverb**.

- "She looks happy" → "She **is** happy" ✔ makes sense → adjective ✔
- "She runs quickly" → "She **is** quickly" ✘ nonsense → adverb ✔

## One More Trap: Words That Are Both

Some words work as **both** adjective and adverb — *fast, hard, early, late, daily, enough*:

- "He is a **fast** runner." (adjective — describes the runner)
- "He runs **fast**." (adverb — describes the running)

⚠️ **"Hardly" is NOT the adverb of "hard"!**
- "He works **hard**." = He works a lot.
- "He **hardly** works." = He almost doesn't work at all.

Examiners often set this trap. Don't fall in.
