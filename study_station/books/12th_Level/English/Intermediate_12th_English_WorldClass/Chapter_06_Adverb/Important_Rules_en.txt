# 📋 Important Grammar Rules & Tips — Adverb

## (a) 12 High-Yield Rules

| # | Rule | Example | Examiner's Trap |
|---|------|---------|-----------------|
| 1 | An adverb modifies a verb, adjective, or another adverb — never a noun. | She sings **beautifully**. | "She is a beautifully singer" ❌ (noun needs adjective: *beautiful singer*) |
| 2 | Linking verbs (be, seem, look, feel, taste, smell) take an **adjective**, not an adverb. | The soup tastes **good**. | "The soup tastes well" ❌ — but "She sings well" ✔ (action verb) |
| 3 | Adverbs of manner usually end in **-ly**, but *fast, hard, late, early, daily* are both adjective and adverb. | He works **hard**. | "He works hardly" ❌ — *hardly* = almost not |
| 4 | **Hardly / Scarcely** take *when*; **No sooner** takes *than*. | Hardly had I reached **when** it rained. | "No sooner had he come when…" ❌ (use *than*) |
| 5 | **Enough** comes after the adjective/adverb it modifies, but before a noun. | He is tall **enough**. / enough money | "He is enough tall" ❌ |
| 6 | **Too** = more than needed (negative sense); **very** = to a high degree (neutral). | It is **too** hot to go out. | "I am too glad to see you" ❌ (use *very* — glad is positive) |
| 7 | Adverbs of frequency (always, often, never) come **before** the main verb but **after** 'be'. | He **always** comes late. / He is **always** late. | "He comes always late" ❌ |
| 8 | **Only** must sit next to the word it modifies — position changes meaning. | **Only** he lent me ₹100. / He lent me **only** ₹100. | Exams test meaning-shift by moving *only* |
| 9 | **Else** takes *but*; **other/other than** used for comparison with 'no/none'. | It is nothing **else but** pride. | "nothing else than" ❌ |
| 10 | **Much** modifies comparatives & past participles; **very** modifies positive degree & present participles. | **much** better; **very** good; **much** surprised | "very better" ❌ |
| 11 | **So** is followed by *that* (result); **such** + noun phrase + *that*. | He was **so** tired **that** he slept. | "He was so tired and slept" — weak; exam wants *so…that* |
| 12 | Two negatives make a positive — avoid double negatives with hardly/scarcely/seldom. | He **hardly** knows anything. | "He doesn't hardly know" ❌ |

## (b) Time Management — 15/45-Second Rule

| Type | What it looks like | Time |
|------|-------------------|------|
| **Fact-based (15 sec)** | Spot-the-error on a fixed rule: hardly…when, enough position, too/very, much/very, double negative. If the rule is memorised, answer instantly. | 15 sec |
| **Fact-based (15 sec)** | Fill-in-the-blank choosing adjective vs adverb after a linking verb (tastes good/well). | 15 sec |
| **Inference-based (45 sec)** | Sentence improvement where *only/even* position changes meaning — read the whole sentence twice and check intended meaning. | 45 sec |
| **Inference-based (45 sec)** | Complex error questions mixing adverb rule with tense/agreement — eliminate options one by one; don't rush. | 45 sec |

**Strategy:** If a question is a pure rule recall, never spend more than 15 seconds. Bank that time for position-of-adverb and multi-error questions.

## (c) Morphology — Roots & Affixes for Adverbs

| Root/Affix | Meaning | Example Words |
|------------|---------|---------------|
| **-ly** (suffix) | manner → forms adverb from adjective | quick → quickly, brave → bravely |
| **-ward(s)** (suffix) | direction | forward, backward, homeward |
| **-wise** (suffix) | in the manner/respect of | clockwise, likewise, otherwise |
| **a-** (prefix) | in/on/at (forms adverbs) | aloud, ahead, asleep, apart |
| **be-** (prefix) | about/around | before, behind, besides |
| **dict** (Latin, *dicere*) | say | predict, dictate, verdict |
| **spect** (Latin, *specere*) | look | inspect, respect, prospect |
| **port** (Latin, *portare*) | carry | transport, export, portable |
| **scrib/script** (Latin) | write | describe, manuscript, inscription |
| **vid/vis** (Latin, *videre*) | see | visible, revise, evident |

**Tip:** Not every word ending in *-ly* is an adverb — *friendly, costly, lonely, lively, cowardly* are **adjectives**. This is a favourite examiner trap: "She spoke friendly" ❌ → "She spoke **in a friendly manner**" ✔.
