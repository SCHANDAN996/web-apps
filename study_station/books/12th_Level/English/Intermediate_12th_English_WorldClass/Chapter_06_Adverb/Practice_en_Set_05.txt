101. Choose the correctly spelt adverb.
(a) Truely (b) Truly (c) Truley (d) Trully
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Truely" tempts because we keep the "e" of "true", but adjectives ending in "-ue" drop the final "e" before "-ly" (true → truly, due → duly). The correct spelling is truly, so the answer is truly.
Source: NCERT Class 9 English Grammar

102. Identify the adverb of frequency in the sentence: "She rarely visits her old school now."
(a) now (b) visits (c) rarely (d) old
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Now" is tempting because it is also an adverb, but it shows time, not frequency. "Rarely" tells how often the action happens, which is the definition of an adverb of frequency, so the answer is rarely.
Source: NCERT Class 8 English Grammar

103. Spot the error: He works (P) / very hardly (Q) / to support (R) / his family. (S)
(a) Part P (b) No error (c) Part Q (d) Part R
Answer: (c)
Solution: Part Q tempts because "hardly" looks like the adverb of "hard", but "hardly" means "almost not", which reverses the meaning; the adverb meaning "with great effort" is "hard" itself. The error is in Part Q, so the answer is Part Q.

104. Choose the correct sentence.
(a) He speaks English well enough. (b) He speaks English enough well. (c) He enough speaks English well. (d) He speaks enough English well.
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Enough well" tempts because "enough" comes before nouns, but with adjectives and adverbs "enough" is placed after the word it modifies (well enough, fast enough). The correct order is "well enough", so the answer is "He speaks English well enough."
Source: NCERT Class 10 English Grammar

105. "The train arrived late." Here "late" is —
(a) an adverb (b) a conjunction (c) an adjective (d) a preposition
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Adjective" tempts because "late" often describes nouns (a late train), but here it modifies the verb "arrived" and tells when, so it functions as an adverb. The answer is an adverb.
Source: NCERT Class 8 English Grammar

106. Choose the correctly framed sentence.
(a) I met only her yesterday. (b) I met her only yesterday. (c) Only I met her yesterday. (d) I only met her yesterday.
Answer: (b)
Solution: "I only met her" tempts because it is common in speech, but "only" must sit next to the word it limits; here the meaning is "yesterday, not earlier", so "only" must precede "yesterday". The precise sentence is "I met her only yesterday", so the answer is that option.

107. Spot the error: She sings (P) / more sweeter (Q) / than her (R) / sister does. (S)
(a) Part P (b) No error (c) Part R (d) Part Q
Answer: (d)
Solution: Part Q tempts because "more" feels natural before comparatives, but "sweeter" is already comparative; adding "more" makes a double comparative, and the adverb form should be "more sweetly" or simply "sweeter". The error lies in Part Q, so the answer is Part Q.
Source: PYQ-style

108. Choose the correct option: "He is ______ tired to walk any further."
(a) too (b) very (c) much (d) so
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Very" tempts because it intensifies adjectives, but the structure "______ + adjective + to-infinitive" with a negative result requires "too" (too tired to walk); "very" would make the sentence illogical. The answer is too.
Source: NCERT Class 9 English Grammar

109. "The movie was ______ interesting that we watched it twice."
(a) much (b) very (c) too (d) so
Answer: (d)
Solution: "Very" tempts as a general intensifier, but the correlative pattern here is "so + adjective + that-clause" showing result, and "too...that" is not standard. The correct word is so, so the answer is so.
Source: NCERT Class 10 English Grammar

110. Spot the error: He behaved (P) / very cowardly (Q) / in front of (R) / the robbers. (S)
(a) No error (b) Part P (c) Part R (d) Part Q
Answer: (d)
Solution: Part Q tempts because "cowardly" ends in "-ly" and looks like an adverb, but "cowardly" is an adjective; English uses "in a cowardly manner" instead. The error is in Part Q, so the answer is Part Q.

111. Choose the sentence where "fast" is used as an adverb.
(a) The fast colour did not fade. (b) She kept a fast on Monday. (c) He is a fast runner. (d) He runs fast.
Answer: (d)
Solution: "He is a fast runner" tempts, but there "fast" describes the noun "runner", making it an adjective. In "He runs fast", "fast" modifies the verb "runs" and is an adverb, so the answer is "He runs fast."

112. "I ______ go to the gym on Sundays — maybe twice a month."
(a) always (b) seldom (c) usually (d) never
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Usually" tempts because the person does go sometimes, but "twice a month" indicates low frequency, which matches "seldom" (rarely); "always" and "never" contradict the clue. The answer is seldom.

113. Spot the error: No sooner (P) / she had entered (Q) / the room than (R) / the phone rang. (S)
(a) Part P (b) No error (c) Part Q (d) Part R
Answer: (c)
Solution: Part Q tempts because "she had entered" is correct in ordinary order, but after "No sooner" the sentence needs inversion: "No sooner had she entered". The error is in Part Q, so the answer is Part Q.

114. Choose the correct adverb to complete: "The two brothers love each other ______."
(a) depth (b) deep (c) deepen (d) deeply
Answer: (d)
Solution: "Deep" tempts because it can be an adverb (dig deep), but for emotions and abstract feelings the adverb is "deeply" (love deeply, regret deeply). The answer is deeply.

115. "He is ______ intelligent to solve this puzzle."
(a) too (b) very (c) enough (d) so
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Too" tempts because it is a familiar intensifier, but "too intelligent to solve" would mean he cannot solve it, which reverses the intended meaning; "very" and "so" do not fit the "to-infinitive" structure. "Intelligent enough to solve" correctly expresses ability, so the answer is enough.
Source: PYQ-style

116. Spot the error: The patient (P) / is much (Q) / better today (R) / than yesterday. (S)
(a) Part P (b) No error (c) Part R (d) Part Q
Answer: (b)
Solution: Part Q tempts because learners think "much" cannot go with comparatives, but "much" correctly intensifies comparative adjectives and adverbs (much better, much faster). The sentence is fully correct, so the answer is No error.

117. Choose the correct position of the adverb "yet".
(a) Have you finished your work yet? (b) Have yet you finished your work? (c) You have finished your work yet? (d) Yet have finished you your work?
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Have yet you finished" tempts by placing "yet" mid-verb, but "yet" in interrogative and negative sentences normally comes at the end. The natural sentence is "Have you finished your work yet?", so the answer is that option.

118. "She spoke ______ at the meeting; everyone was impressed."
(a) eloquent (b) eloquence (c) eloquently (d) eloquentness
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Eloquent" tempts because it describes the speaker, but the blank must modify the verb "spoke", which requires an adverb. The adverb form is "eloquently", so the answer is eloquently.

(a) Part P (b) Part Q (c) Part R (d) No error
Answer: (b)
Solution: Part R tempts because the adverb placement looks suspicious, but "certainly come" is fine; the real error is tense — after the past reporting verb "told", "will" must become "would". The error is in Part Q, so the answer is Part Q.

120. "He worked for ______ ten hours without a break."
(a) mostly (b) near (c) most (d) almost
Answer: (d)
Solution: "Most" tempts because it sounds close in meaning, but "most ten hours" is ungrammatical; "near" would need "nearly", and "mostly" means "mainly". The word meaning "very close to" a number is "almost", so the answer is almost.
Source: PYQ-style

121. "Hardly ______ the station when the train left."
(a) had I reached (b) I had reached (c) I reached (d) did I reach
Answer: (a)
Solution: "I had reached" tempts because the past perfect is right, but when a negative adverb like "Hardly" begins a sentence, inversion of subject and auxiliary is compulsory: "Hardly had I reached". The answer is "had I reached".

122. Spot the error: Scarcely he had (P) / stepped out of (Q) / the house when (R) / it began to rain. (S)

Answer: (a)
Solution: Part R tempts because "when" with "scarcely" looks doubtful, but "scarcely...when" is the correct pair; the real error is the missing inversion after the initial negative adverb — it should be "Scarcely had he stepped out". The error is in Part P, so the answer is Part P.

123. "The committee members were ______ divided on the issue."
(a) sharp (b) sharpen (c) sharply (d) sharpness
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Sharp" tempts because it can be an adverb of time or direction (turn sharp), but modifying the adjective "divided" needs the degree adverb "sharply" (sharply divided = strongly split). The answer is sharply.

Answer: (a)
Solution: "Enough rich" tempts because "enough" precedes nouns (enough money), but with adjectives "enough" follows them: "rich enough". The correct sentence is "He is rich enough to buy a car", so the answer is that option.

125. Spot the error: He not only (P) / plays cricket (Q) / but also (R) / football. (S)
(a) Part Q (b) Part P (c) Part R (d) No error
Answer: (b)
Solution: Part R tempts because "but also" seems wrongly placed, but the real fault is parallelism: "not only" must come immediately before the parallel element — "He plays not only cricket but also football." The error is in Part P, so the answer is Part P.
