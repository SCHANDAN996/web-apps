# Adverb

## 1. Hook

Imagine you are a car. You have a powerful engine, but you don't know how to drive. You need a driver. In the same way, a verb in a sentence often needs a helper that tells us *how*, *when*, or *where* the action happens. That helper is the **adverb**.

In this chapter, you will learn what adverbs are, their types, and how to use them correctly in exams.

## 2. What is an Adverb?

An adverb is a word that modifies a **verb**, an **adjective**, or another **adverb**. It tells us *how*, *when*, *where*, *how often*, and *to what extent* something happens.

Examples:
- She sings **beautifully**. (modifies the verb *sings* — how?)
- The movie was **very** interesting. (modifies the adjective *interesting* — to what extent?)
- He ran **quite** fast. (modifies the adverb *fast* — to what extent?)

## 3. Types of Adverbs

Adverbs can be of many types. The main types are:

1. **Adverb of Manner** — tells *how* an action happens.
   - Examples: slowly, quickly, carefully, well
   - He drives **carefully**.

2. **Adverb of Place** — tells *where* an action happens.
   - Examples: here, there, everywhere, outside, up
   - Come **here**.

3. **Adverb of Time** — tells *when* an action happens.
   - Examples: now, then, yesterday, today, soon, already
   - I will call you **tomorrow**.

4. **Adverb of Frequency** — tells *how often* an action happens.
   - Examples: always, often, sometimes, rarely, never, usually
   - She **always** reaches on time.

5. **Adverb of Degree (or Quantity)** — tells *to what extent* or *how much*.
   - Examples: very, too, quite, almost, fully, enough
   - The tea is **too** hot.

6. **Adverb of Reason (or Cause)** — tells *why* something happens.
   - Examples: therefore, hence, consequently, so
   - He was ill; **therefore**, he stayed at home.

7. **Interrogative Adverb** — used to ask questions.
   - Examples: when, where, why, how
   - **When** did you arrive?

8. **Relative Adverb** — joins a clause to the main sentence.
   - Examples: when, where, why
   - This is the house **where** I was born.

9. **Sentence Adverb** — modifies the whole sentence and shows the speaker's attitude.
   - Examples: certainly, obviously, fortunately, surely
   - **Fortunately**, no one was hurt.

## 4. Position of Adverbs

- Adverbs of manner usually come **after the verb or its object**: She spoke **politely**.
- Adverbs of frequency usually come **before the main verb** but **after the verb 'to be'**: He **often** visits us. / She is **always** cheerful.
- Adverbs of time and place often come **at the end** of the sentence: We met **yesterday** at the **station**.

## 5. Comparison of Adverbs

Like adjectives, some adverbs have three degrees:

| Positive | Comparative | Superlative |
|---|---|---|
| fast | faster | fastest |
| hard | harder | hardest |
| early | earlier | earliest |
| well | better | best |
| badly | worse | worst |
| much | more | most |

- Ram runs **fast**. Shyam runs **faster** than Ram. Mohan runs the **fastest** of all.

## 6. Common Errors to Avoid

- ❌ He did the work **good**. → ✅ He did the work **well**. (*Good* is an adjective; *well* is the adverb.)
- ❌ She sings **beautiful**. → ✅ She sings **beautifully**.
- ❌ He is **very** taller than me. → ✅ He is **much** taller than me. (*Very* is not used before a comparative.)
- ❌ I **seldom go** there never. → ✅ I **seldom go** there. (Double negative is wrong.)

## 7. Quick Revision Points

- An adverb modifies a verb, an adjective, or another adverb.
- Main types: manner, place, time, frequency, degree, reason, interrogative, relative, sentence adverb.
- Many adverbs of manner end in **-ly**, but not all (fast, hard, well).
- Some words can be both adjective and adverb: *fast*, *hard*, *early*, *daily*.
- Use *well* (adverb) with verbs; use *good* (adjective) with nouns.
