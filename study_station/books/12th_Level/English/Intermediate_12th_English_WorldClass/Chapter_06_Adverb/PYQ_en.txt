# 📊 PYQ Analysis — Adverb

## How Exams Test Adverbs (Pattern Analysis)

Across SSC (CGL, CHSL, MTS, GD), Banking (IBPS, SBI), and Railway (RRB NTPC, Group D) exams, adverb questions appear in four recurring formats:

1. **Spot the Error** — an adjective is wrongly used where an adverb is needed ("He drives *careful*"), or the adverb is misplaced.
2. **Fill in the Blank** — choose between confusing pairs: hard/hardly, late/lately, fast/fastly, too/very, much/very.
3. **Sentence Improvement** — replace the underlined part with the correct adverb form or position.
4. **Cloze Test** — adverbs of degree and frequency inside a passage.

**Sub-topics that repeat most often:**
- Adjective vs. Adverb confusion (He sings *beautiful/beautifully*)
- Position of adverbs of frequency (always, often, never) — before the main verb, after "be"
- "Enough" placement — after adjective/adverb, before noun
- Hard vs. Hardly, Late vs. Lately, Near vs. Nearly
- "Too" vs. "Very" and "Much" vs. "Very"
- Adverbs with the same form as adjectives (fast, hard, early, daily)
- Only / Even / Almost — meaning changes with position

---

## 🎯 Examiner's Trap Types (with classification)

| Trap Type | What the examiner does | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Sound-Right** | Uses an adjective that "sounds natural" after a verb | "She sings beautiful." ❌ |
| **Intervening Phrase** | Puts words between verb and adverb so you lose track | "The boy, along with his friends, plays the game very good." ❌ |
| **Look-Alike Pair** | Tests hard/hardly, late/lately where both look correct | "He works hardly." (means: barely works!) |
| **Over-thinking** | A simple, correct sentence is made to look suspicious | "She always arrives on time." — already correct! |
| **Position Trap** | "Only" placed wrongly changes the meaning | "Only he lent me ₹500" vs "He lent me only ₹500" |

## 🏆 Top 10 High-Yield Questions (PYQ-style) with Solutions

**Q1.** He drives his car very careful on busy roads.
(a) very careful on (b) his car very carefully on (c) very carefully his car on (d) No improvement
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** "Drives" is a verb; it needs the adverb *carefully*, not the adjective *careful*. Trap type: **Sound-Right**.
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q2.** She is enough tall to reach the shelf.
(a) enough tall (b) tall enough (c) too tall (d) No improvement
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** "Enough" comes **after** an adjective/adverb but **before** a noun: *tall enough*, but *enough money*. Trap type: **Position Trap**.
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q3.** He works hardly to support his family.
(a) works hardly (b) works hard (c) working hard (d) No improvement
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** *Hardly* = almost not. To mean "with great effort," use *hard*. Trap type: **Look-Alike Pair** — the deadliest adverb trap in SSC/Bank exams.
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q4.** I have been waiting for you since two hours.
(a) since two hours (b) for two hours (c) from two hours (d) No improvement
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** Period of time → *for*; point of time → *since*. Often appears alongside adverb-of-time questions. Trap type: **Sound-Right**.
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q5.** The train arrived lately because of the fog.
(a) arrived lately (b) arrived late (c) arriving late (d) No improvement
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** *Lately* = recently; *late* = after the expected time. Trap type: **Look-Alike Pair**.
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q6.** He only told me the truth, nothing else.
(a) He only told me (b) He told me only (c) Only he told me (d) No improvement
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** The phrase "nothing else" shows that *only* must modify "the truth" — he told me **only the truth**. So "only" must move next to the word it limits: "He told me only the truth." Option (b) places "only" after "me," directly before "the truth," which is the intended correction. In (a) and the original, "only" wrongly modifies "told" (suggesting he did nothing but tell). Trap type: **Position Trap**.
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q7.** She sings more sweeter than her sister.
(a) more sweeter (b) sweeter (c) most sweet (d) No improvement
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** Double comparative is wrong. "Sweeter" alone is correct. Trap type: **Sound-Right**.
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q8.** The weather is too pleasant today for a picnic.
(a) too pleasant (b) very pleasant (c) much pleasant (d) No improvement
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** *Too* carries a negative sense (more than desirable). For a positive remark, use *very*. Trap type: **Sound-Right** — Indian English speakers say "too good" constantly; exams don't accept it.
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q9.** He is very much sorry for his mistake.
(a) very much sorry (b) very sorry (c) much very sorry (d) No improvement
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** *Very* modifies adjectives (very sorry); *much* modifies comparatives and past participles (much better, much surprised). Trap type: **Sound-Right**.
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q10.** My grandfather goes for a walk daily in the morning.
(a) goes for a walk daily (b) daily goes for a walk (c) goes daily for a walk (d) No improvement
**Answer: (d)**
**Solution:** The sentence is already correct — "daily" is both an adjective and an adverb, and its end position is natural. Trap type: **Over-thinking** — the examiner wants you to "fix" a correct sentence.
**Source:** PYQ-style

## ⏱️ Applying the 15/45-Second Rule

**15-second question (Fact-based):** Q3 — "He works hardly to support his family."
The moment you see *hardly* after "works," recall the rule: hardly = barely. No analysis needed. Spot, recall, mark — under 15 seconds.

**45-second question (Inference-based):** Q6 — the "only" question.
Here you must (1) read the whole sentence, (2) decide the intended meaning, (3) check where "only" sits, (4) compare all options. This deserves your full 45 seconds. Never rush a position-of-adverb question; never linger on a hard/hardly question.

---

## 📌 Key Takeaways from the Pattern

- **Hard/Hardly and Late/Lately** appear in some form almost every year across SSC and Bank exams — memorise them cold.
- **"No improvement needed"** is a genuine answer roughly once per paper — don't let over-thinking steal that mark.
- Position questions (only, even, enough, almost) reward students who read the *meaning*, not just the grammar.
- When two options both "sound right," the trap is almost always a **Look-Alike Pair** — check the exact meaning of each word.
