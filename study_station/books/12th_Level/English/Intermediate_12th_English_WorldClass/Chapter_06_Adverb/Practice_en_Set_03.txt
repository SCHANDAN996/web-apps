51. Choose the correct sentence.
(a) She sings beauty. (b) She sings beautiful. (c) She sings beautifuller. (d) She sings beautifully.
Answer: (d)
Solution: A verb like "sings" is modified by an adverb, not an adjective, so "beautiful" is the trap here. The adverb form "beautifully" correctly tells how she sings, so the answer is "She sings beautifully."
Source: NCERT Class 8 English Grammar

52. Identify the adverb in the sentence: "He ran quickly to catch the bus."
(a) quickly (b) catch (c) bus (d) ran
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Ran" is the verb, and the word telling how he ran is the adverb. "Quickly" modifies the verb, so the answer is "quickly."
Source: NCERT Class 7 English Grammar

53. Choose the correctly spelt adverb.
(a) truely (b) truly (c) trewly (d) trully
Answer: (b)
Solution: "True" drops the silent "e" before adding "-ly", giving "truly"; "truely" is a very common spelling trap. The correct spelling is "truly."
Source: NCERT Class 8 English Grammar

54. "The train arrived late." Here "late" is —
(a) a conjunction (b) an adjective (c) a noun (d) an adverb
Answer: (d)
Solution: "Late" here tells when the train arrived, modifying the verb "arrived", so it works as an adverb. In "a late train" it would be an adjective, but here the answer is "an adverb."
Source: NCERT Class 8 English Grammar

55. Choose the correct sentence.
(a) He works hardly to earn money. (b) He works harderly to earn money. (c) He works hardful to earn money. (d) He works hard to earn money.
Answer: (d)
Solution: "Hardly" means "almost not", which reverses the meaning — a classic examiner trap. "Hard" itself is the adverb of manner here, so the answer is "He works hard to earn money."

56. Identify the adverb of frequency: "She always speaks the truth."
(a) She (b) always (c) speaks (d) truth
Answer: (b)
Solution: An adverb of frequency tells how often an action happens. "Always" tells how often she speaks the truth, so the answer is "always."

57. Choose the correct sentence.
(a) I am very much tired. (b) I am very tired. (c) I am much very tired. (d) I am tiring very.
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Very much" is used with verbs, not before adjectives like "tired"; "very" correctly intensifies the adjective. The answer is "I am very tired."

58. "The children played ______ in the park." Choose the correct adverb.
(a) happily (b) happy (c) happiness (d) happier
Answer: (a)
Solution: The verb "played" needs an adverb of manner; "happy" is an adjective and "happiness" is a noun. The adverb "happily" is correct, so the answer is "happily."

59. Choose the correct sentence.
(a) He is tall enough to reach the shelf. (b) He is too enough tall to reach the shelf. (c) He is enough tall to reach the shelf. (d) He enough is tall to reach the shelf.
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Enough" comes after the adjective it qualifies, not before it, so "enough tall" is wrong. The correct order is "tall enough", so the answer is "He is tall enough to reach the shelf."

(a) here (b) Come (c) sit (d) beside
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Here" tells where the action of coming happens, so it is an adverb of place; "beside" is a preposition before "me". The answer is "here."

61. Choose the correct sentence.
(a) He drives fastly on the highway. (b) He drives fasterly on the highway. (c) He drives fast on the highway. (d) He drives fasting on the highway.
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Fast" is both an adjective and an adverb; "fastly" does not exist in standard English — a frequent trap. The correct sentence is "He drives fast on the highway."

62. Spot the error: Part P: "She spoke" / Part Q: "too polite" / Part R: "to offend anyone." / Part S: No error
(a) Part P (b) Part R (c) Part Q (d) No error
Answer: (c)
Solution: The structure "too … to" gives a negative sense ("so polite that she could not offend"), which is illogical here; the verb "spoke" needs the adverb "politely". The error lies in Part Q.

63. Choose the correct sentence.
(a) I have seen that movie yesterday. (b) I see that movie yesterday. (c) I saw that movie yesterday. (d) I had saw that movie yesterday.
Answer: (c)
Solution: With a definite past-time adverb like "yesterday", the present perfect "have seen" is wrong; the simple past is required. The answer is "I saw that movie yesterday."
Source: NCERT Class 9 English Grammar

64. "There is ______ water in the pot to make tea." Choose the correct word.
(a) much too (b) too little (c) very little enough (d) too much
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Too much" would mean an excess, which contradicts the idea of insufficiency implied by "to make tea"; "much too" goes before adjectives, not nouns. "Too little" correctly means insufficient quantity, so the answer is "too little."
Source: PYQ-style

65. Choose the sentence in which the adverb "only" is correctly placed to mean that she told the truth and nothing else.
(a) She told me only the truth. (b) Only she told me the truth. (c) She told only me the truth. (d) She only told me the truth yesterday only.
Answer: (a)
Solution: The meaning changes with the position of "only"; placing it before "me" would mean no one else was told, and placing it before "she" would mean no one else told it. To limit what was told, "only" must come just before "the truth", so the answer is "She told me only the truth."
Source: PYQ-style

66. Spot the error: Part P: "He behaved" / Part Q: "very cowardly" / Part R: "in the battlefield." / Part S: No error
(a) Part Q (b) No error (c) Part R (d) Part P
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Cowardly" is an adjective, not an adverb, so it cannot modify the verb "behaved"; the correct phrase is "in a cowardly manner". The error lies in Part Q.

67. Choose the correct sentence.
(a) He is more wiser than his brother. (b) He is much wiser than his brother. (c) He is much more wiser than his brother. (d) He is most wiser than his brother.
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Wiser" is already comparative, so adding "more" makes a double comparative — a common trap; "much" can correctly intensify a comparative. The answer is "He is much wiser than his brother."

68. "I ______ go to the gym on Sundays, but today is Monday." Choose the adverb of frequency that fits.
(a) always (b) never (c) seldom (d) rarely
Answer: (a)
Solution: The contrast "but today is Monday" implies going on Sundays is a fixed habit, so a high-frequency adverb is needed; "seldom", "never" and "rarely" contradict the habit. The answer is "always."

69. Choose the correct sentence.
(a) She speaks English fluent than her brother. (b) She speaks English most fluently than her brother. (c) She speaks English fluentlier than her brother. (d) She speaks English more fluently than her brother.
Answer: (d)
Solution: The verb "speaks" needs an adverb, so the adjective "fluent" is wrong; the comparative of the adverb "fluently" is "more fluently", and "most" with "than" is a superlative-comparative mix-up. The answer is "She speaks English more fluently than her brother."

(a) No error (b) Part Q (c) Part P (d) Part R
Answer: (d)
Solution: "No sooner" is always paired with "than", not "when" — a favourite examiner trap with correlative adverbs. The error lies in Part R.

71. Choose the correct sentence.
(a) He hardly knows anything about the deal, isn't it? (b) He hardly knows anything about the deal, does he? (c) He hardly knows anything about the deal, is he? (d) He hardly knows anything about the deal, doesn't he?
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Hardly" is negative in meaning, so the question tag must be positive; "doesn't he" would make a double negative. The answer is "He hardly knows anything about the deal, does he?"

72. "The movie was ______ interesting that we watched it twice." Choose the correct word.
(a) too (b) very (c) much (d) so
Answer: (d)
Solution: The pattern "so + adjective + that" expresses result; "too … that" is incorrect because "too" pairs with "to". The answer is "so."

75. Choose the correct sentence.
(a) Scarcely he had stepped out when it began to rain. (b) Scarcely he had stepped out than it began to rain. (c) Scarcely had he stepped out when it began to rain. (d) Scarcely had he stepped out than it began to rain.
Answer: (c)
Solution: When a negative adverb like "scarcely" begins a sentence, inversion (had he) is required, and "scarcely" pairs with "when", not "than". The answer is "Scarcely had he stepped out when it began to rain."
