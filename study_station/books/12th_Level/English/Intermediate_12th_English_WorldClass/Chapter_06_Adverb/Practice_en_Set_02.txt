26. Choose the correct sentence.
(a) She sings beauty. (b) She sings beautifull. (c) She sings beautiful. (d) She sings beautifully.
Answer: (d)
Solution: A verb like "sings" must be modified by an adverb, not an adjective. "Beautiful" is an adjective and cannot describe how she sings, so that option is the trap. The adverb form is "beautifully", so the answer is "She sings beautifully."
Source: NCERT Class 8 English Grammar

27. Identify the adverb in the sentence: "He ran quickly to catch the bus."
(a) ran (b) catch (c) quickly (d) bus
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Ran" and "catch" are verbs, and "bus" is a noun, so none of them can be the adverb. "Quickly" tells us how he ran, so the answer is "quickly."
Source: NCERT Class 7 English Grammar

28. The word "very" in "She is very kind" is an adverb of —
(a) manner (b) place (c) time (d) degree
Answer: (d)
Solution: "Very" does not tell how, when, or where; it tells to what extent she is kind. Adverbs that answer "how much/to what extent" are adverbs of degree, so the answer is "degree."
Source: NCERT Class 8 English Grammar

29. Choose the correctly spelled adverb.
(a) truely (b) truly (c) truily (d) trully
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is keeping the silent "e" of "true" — "truely" is a common misspelling. When "true" becomes an adverb, the "e" is dropped, so the answer is "truly."
Source: Standard English spelling rules

30. "He always reaches office on time." The adverb "always" shows —
(a) place (b) frequency (c) manner (d) degree
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Always" answers the question "how often?" — it tells how frequently he reaches on time. Adverbs answering "how often" are adverbs of frequency, so the answer is "frequency."
Source: NCERT Class 8 English Grammar

31. Choose the correct sentence.
(a) He drives fastly. (b) He drives fasting. (c) He drives fast. (d) He drives fasterly.
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is adding "-ly" to "fast" — but "fast" is both an adjective and an adverb, and "fastly" does not exist in standard English. The adverb form is simply "fast", so the answer is "He drives fast."
Source: Examiner's trap — same-form adverbs

32. "The children played ______ in the park."
(a) happily (b) happy (c) happiness (d) happier
Answer: (a)
Solution: The verb "played" needs an adverb of manner. "Happy" is an adjective and "happiness" is a noun, so both are wrong here. The adverb is "happily", so the answer is "happily."
Source: NCERT Class 7 English Grammar

33. Identify the adverb of place: "Come here at once."
(a) Come (b) here (c) at (d) once
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Once" tells when (time), which is the tempting wrong choice. "Here" tells where to come, answering the question "where?", so the answer is "here."
Source: NCERT Class 8 English Grammar

34. "She spoke ______ to the old man." (polite)
(a) politeness (b) polite (c) politely (d) politer
Answer: (c)
Solution: The verb "spoke" must be modified by an adverb. "Polite" is an adjective and "politeness" is a noun. The adverb formed by adding "-ly" is "politely", so the answer is "politely."
Source: NCERT Class 8 English Grammar

35. "I have ______ finished my homework." (just now)
(a) yet (b) ever (c) already (d) since
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Yet" is used in negatives and questions, not in positive statements, so it is the trap here. "Already" fits a positive sentence meaning "just now/by now", so the answer is "already."
Source: NCERT Class 9 English Grammar

36. Choose the sentence with the adverb in the correct position.
(a) Fluently she English speaks. (b) She fluently speaks English fluently. (c) She speaks English fluently. (d) She speaks fluently English fluently.
Answer: (c)
Solution: An adverb of manner normally comes after the verb or after the object, not between the verb and its object. Splitting "speaks" and "English" is the common error. The natural order is verb + object + adverb, so the answer is "She speaks English fluently."
Source: Examiner's trap — adverb position

37. "Hardly had he reached the station ______ the train left."
(a) than (b) that (c) then (d) when
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is "than", which pairs with "no sooner", not with "hardly". "Hardly/Scarcely" always takes "when", so the answer is "when."
Source: Examiner's trap — correlative adverbs

38. "He is ______ tall to reach the shelf."
(a) much (b) very (c) so (d) too
Answer: (d)
Solution: "Very" only intensifies; it cannot express the negative sense of "more than needed". "Too...to" gives the meaning "so tall that he cannot reach", so the answer is "too."
Source: NCERT Class 9 English Grammar

39. Identify the adverb of time: "We will leave tomorrow."
(a) tomorrow (b) We (c) leave (d) will
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Will" is an auxiliary verb and "leave" is the main verb. "Tomorrow" answers the question "when?", so the answer is "tomorrow."
Source: NCERT Class 7 English Grammar

40. "The movie was ______ interesting that I watched it twice."
(a) so (b) very (c) too (d) much
Answer: (a)
Solution: The structure "so + adjective + that" expresses result. "Too...that" is not standard English, which is the tempting error. The correct pattern here is "so...that", so the answer is "so."
Source: NCERT Class 9 English Grammar

41. Choose the correct sentence.
(a) He works hardly to earn money. (b) He works hard to earn money. (c) He works harder to earn money daily basis. (d) He works hardily to earn money.
Answer: (b)
Solution: The classic trap: "hardly" means "almost not", so it reverses the meaning. The adverb meaning "with great effort" is "hard" (no -ly), so the answer is "He works hard to earn money."
Source: Examiner's trap — hard vs hardly

42. "She ______ visits her grandmother." (once a week)
(a) weekly (b) never (c) rarely (d) seldom
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Seldom", "rarely", and "never" all suggest almost no visits, which contradicts "once a week". "Weekly" matches the given frequency, so the answer is "weekly."
Source: NCERT Class 8 English Grammar

43. Spot the error: Part P: "He behaved" / Part Q: "very cowardly" / Part R: "in the meeting." / Part S: "No error"
(a) Part R (b) Part P (c) Part Q (d) No error
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is assuming every word ending in "-ly" is an adverb. "Cowardly" is only an adjective, so it cannot modify the verb "behaved"; the correct phrase is "in a very cowardly manner". The error lies in Part Q, so the answer is "Part Q."
Source: Examiner's trap — adjectives ending in -ly

44. "______ did he enter the room than the phone rang."
(a) No sooner (b) Hardly (c) Scarcely (d) As soon
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Hardly" and "scarcely" pair with "when", not "than", so they fail here. "No sooner...than" is the correct correlative pair, so the answer is "No sooner."
Source: NCERT Class 10 English Grammar

45. "The train arrived ______ late." (a small amount)
(a) little (b) a little (c) few (d) the little
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Little" means "almost none" and gives a negative sense, which is the trap. "A little" means "a small amount", fitting the clue, so the answer is "a little."
Source: NCERT Class 9 English Grammar

46. Choose the sentence in which "only" is correctly placed.
(a) He earns only five hundred rupees a day. (b) He only earns five hundred rupees a day. (c) Only he earns five hundred rupees a day only. (d) He earns five hundred rupees a only day.
Answer: (a)
Solution: The position of "only" changes meaning; placing it before "earns" wrongly suggests earning is all he does. "Only" must sit just before the word it limits — "five hundred rupees" — so the answer is "He earns only five hundred rupees a day."
Source: Examiner's trap — position of "only"

47. "She is intelligent ______ to solve this puzzle."
(a) so (b) too (c) very (d) enough
Answer: (d)
Solution: "Too" before the adjective would reverse the meaning, and "so" or "very" cannot sit after the adjective in this structure. "Enough" placed after the adjective means "sufficiently intelligent", so the answer is "enough."
Source: NCERT Class 9 English Grammar

48. Identify the sentence where "enough" is used correctly.
(a) He enough is strong to lift the box. (b) He is strong enough to lift the box. (c) He is enough strong to lift the box. (d) Enough he is strong to lift the box.
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is placing "enough" before the adjective, as we do with "very". "Enough" always comes after the adjective or adverb it modifies, so the answer is "He is strong enough to lift the box."
Source: NCERT Class 9 English Grammar

49. "I ______ go to the gym — maybe twice a year."
(a) rarely (b) often (c) always (d) usually
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Always", "often", and "usually" all suggest frequent visits, contradicting "twice a year". "Rarely" matches the very low frequency, so the answer is "rarely."
Source: NCERT Class 8 English Grammar

50. Choose the correct comparative sentence.
(a) She sings sweetly more than her sister. (b) She sings sweetlier than her sister. (c) She sings more sweet than her sister. (d) She sings more sweetly than her sister.
Answer: (d)
Solution: "Sweetlier" is not a word — adverbs ending in -ly form the comparative with "more", which is the trap. "More sweetly" is the correct comparative adverb form, so the answer is "She sings more sweetly than her sister."
Source: NCERT Class 9 English Grammar
