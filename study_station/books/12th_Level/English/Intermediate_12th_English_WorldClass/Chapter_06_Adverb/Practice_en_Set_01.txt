1. Identify the adverb in the sentence: "She sings beautifully."
(a) She (b) beautifully (c) sings (d) No adverb
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Sings" is the verb, and the word that tells how she sings is "beautifully", which modifies the verb. "She" is a pronoun, not an adverb, so the answer is beautifully.
Source: NCERT Class 8 English Grammar

2. Choose the correct adverb: "He drives very ___."
(a) careful (b) carefulness (c) care (d) carefully
Answer: (d)
Solution: A verb like "drives" needs an adverb to describe it, not an adjective like "careful". The adverb form is made by adding -ly, so the answer is carefully.
Source: NCERT Class 8 English Grammar

3. "The train arrived late." Here "late" is:
(a) an adverb (b) an adjective (c) a noun (d) a conjunction
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Late" tells when the train arrived, so it modifies the verb "arrived" and works as an adverb. Do not assume every word ending without -ly is an adjective, so the answer is an adverb.
Source: NCERT Class 9 English Grammar

4. Which sentence uses the adverb correctly?
(a) She is a beautifully dancer. (b) She dances beautifully. (c) She beautifully is dancer. (d) Beautifully she is a dancer girl.
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Beautifully" is an adverb and must modify a verb, not a noun like "dancer", which needs the adjective "beautiful". Only the second sentence places it with the verb, so the answer is "She dances beautifully."
Source: NCERT Class 8 English Grammar

5. Identify the adverb of time: "I will meet you tomorrow."
(a) will (b) meet (c) tomorrow (d) you
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Tomorrow" answers the question "when?", so it is an adverb of time. "Meet" is a verb and "will" is an auxiliary, so the answer is tomorrow.
Source: NCERT Class 8 English Grammar

6. "He always speaks the truth." The adverb "always" is an adverb of:
(a) manner (b) place (c) frequency (d) degree
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Always" tells how often the action happens, which is the job of an adverb of frequency. It does not describe how, where, or to what extent, so the answer is frequency.
Source: NCERT Class 9 English Grammar

7. Choose the correct sentence:
(a) He runs fast. (b) He runs fastly. (c) He runs fasterly. (d) He runs fasting.
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Fast" is both an adjective and an adverb; "fastly" does not exist in English, which is a common trap. The verb "runs" is correctly modified by "fast", so the answer is "He runs fast."

8. "The children played outside." Here "outside" is an adverb of:
(a) time (b) manner (c) place (d) degree
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Outside" answers the question "where?", so it is an adverb of place. It gives no information about time or manner, so the answer is place.

9. Identify the adverb of degree: "The soup is too hot."
(a) soup (b) is (c) hot (d) too
Answer: (d)
Solution: "Too" modifies the adjective "hot" and tells to what extent, making it an adverb of degree. "Hot" itself is an adjective, so the answer is too.

10. Choose the correctly spelt adverb:
(a) truly (b) truely (c) trully (d) truily
Answer: (a)
Solution: When "true" becomes an adverb, the final "e" is dropped before adding -ly, giving "truly". "Truely" looks right but is a spelling trap, so the answer is truly.

11. "She hardly works." This sentence means:
(a) She works very hard. (b) She almost does not work. (c) She works with difficulty. (d) She works regularly.
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Hardly" is a negative adverb meaning "almost not"; it does not mean "working hard", which is the classic trap. The sentence says she barely works, so the answer is "She almost does not work."

12. Choose the correct adverb: "The exam was ___ easy; everyone passed."
(a) surprisingly (b) surprised (c) surprising (d) surprise
Answer: (a)
Solution: The adjective "easy" needs an adverb of degree before it, and "surprisingly" is the adverb form. "Surprising" is an adjective and cannot modify another adjective here, so the answer is surprisingly.

13. "He spoke politely to the teacher." The adverb "politely" is an adverb of:
(a) place (b) time (c) manner (d) frequency
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Politely" tells how he spoke, answering the question "in what manner?". It does not say when or where, so the answer is manner.

14. Which word is NOT an adverb?
(a) quickly (b) honest (c) yesterday (d) very
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Honest" is an adjective that describes a noun; it never modifies a verb. "Quickly", "yesterday" and "very" all function as adverbs, so the answer is honest.

15. "I have ___ finished my homework." Choose the correct adverb of time.
(a) quick (b) slow (c) already (d) beautiful
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Already" is an adverb of time that fits the present perfect tense "have finished". "Quick", "slow" and "beautiful" are adjectives and cannot modify a verb, so the answer is already.

16. Choose the correct sentence:
(a) She sings very sweetly. (b) She sings very sweet. (c) She very sings sweetly. (d) Sweetly she very sings.
Answer: (a)
Solution: The verb "sings" needs the adverb "sweetly", and the degree adverb "very" correctly comes before it. "Sweet" is an adjective and cannot modify a verb, so the answer is "She sings very sweetly."

17. "He is tall enough to reach the shelf." Here "enough" is:
(a) an adjective (b) a pronoun (c) an adverb (d) a preposition
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Enough" here modifies the adjective "tall" and comes after it, working as an adverb of degree. Before a noun it would be an adjective, but that is not the case here, so the answer is an adverb.

18. Identify the adverb in: "They searched everywhere for the lost key."
(a) searched (b) everywhere (c) lost (d) key
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Everywhere" tells where they searched, so it is an adverb of place. "Lost" is an adjective describing "key", so the answer is everywhere.

19. Choose the correct adverb: "Please speak ___; the baby is sleeping."
(a) soft (b) softly (c) softness (d) soften
Answer: (b)
Solution: The verb "speak" must be modified by an adverb, and "softly" is the adverb form of "soft". "Soft" is an adjective and "softness" is a noun, so the answer is softly.

20. "She visits her grandmother once a week." "Once a week" shows:
(a) adverb of manner (b) adverb of place (c) adverb of degree (d) adverb of frequency
Answer: (d)
Solution: "Once a week" tells how often she visits, which is frequency. It does not describe manner, place or degree, so the answer is adverb of frequency.

21. Spot the error: "He behaved (P) / very rudely (Q) / with his friends (R) / yesterday evening. (S)"
(a) Part P (b) Part Q (c) No error (d) Part R
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is Part Q, but "rudely" is a correct adverb modifying "behaved". The real error is in Part R: with people we say "behaved rudely towards/to his friends", not "with", so the answer is Part R.

22. Choose the correctly framed sentence:
(a) He only earns five hundred rupees a day. (b) He earns five hundred rupees only a day. (c) Only he earns five hundred rupees a day only. (d) He earns only five hundred rupees a day.
Answer: (d)
Solution: "Only" must sit just before the word it limits; placing it before "earns" wrongly suggests earning is the only thing he does. The amount "five hundred rupees" is what is limited, so the answer is "He earns only five hundred rupees a day."
Source: PYQ-style

23. "The movie was ___ interesting that I watched it twice."
(a) very (b) too (c) much (d) so
Answer: (d)
Solution: The structure "so + adjective + that" expresses result; "too...that" is wrong because "too" pairs with "to", which is the tempting trap. "Very" cannot take a "that" clause, so the answer is so.
Source: NCERT Class 9 English Grammar

24. Choose the correct sentence:
(a) He is strong enough to lift the box. (b) He is enough strong to lift the box. (c) He is strongly enough to lift the box. (d) Enough he is strong to lift the box.
Answer: (a)
Solution: As an adverb of degree, "enough" comes after the adjective it modifies, not before it — the common trap is placing it before "strong". The adjective "strong" (not "strongly") is needed after "is", so the answer is "He is strong enough to lift the box."
Source: PYQ-style

25. "She hardly ever comes late." The sentence is:
(a) correct — "hardly ever" means rarely (b) incorrect — double negative (c) incorrect — use "hardly never" (d) incorrect — "ever" must be removed
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is thinking "hardly ever" is a double negative; in fact it is a standard, correct expression meaning "very rarely". "Hardly never" would be the real double negative, so the answer is "correct — 'hardly ever' means rarely".
Source: NCERT Class 9 English Grammar
