101. The new manager promised to turn over a new leaf after the team's poor performance last quarter.
(a) To change one's behaviour for the better (b) To start reading a new book (c) To change jobs frequently (d) To blame others for past mistakes
Answer: (a)
Solution: "To turn over a new leaf" means to change one's behaviour or habits for the better and make a fresh start. The tempting wrong choice about reading a book plays on the literal word "leaf" (a page), which is the classic literal-meaning trap. The idiom has nothing to do with books, so the answer is to change one's behaviour for the better.

Solution: "To sweep something under the carpet" means to hide or ignore a problem or wrongdoing instead of dealing with it. The cleaning option is the literal trap; the idiom is about concealment, not cleanliness. Hence the answer is to hide something embarrassing or wrong.
