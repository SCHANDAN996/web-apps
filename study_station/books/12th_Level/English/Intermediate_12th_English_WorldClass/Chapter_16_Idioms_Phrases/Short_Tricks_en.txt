# 🪄 SHORT TRICKS + SKIP STRATEGY — Idioms & Phrases

---

## TRICK 1 — The "Literal = Wrong" Alarm 🚨

> **BOX:** If an option translates the idiom **word-for-word**, it is almost always WRONG.
> "Kick the bucket" has nothing to do with a bucket. The literal option is the examiner's bait — eliminate it first.

---

## TRICK 2 — The Body-Part Cluster 🖐️

> **BOX:** Group idioms by body part — one memory hook, many idioms.
> - **Hand:** wash one's hands of (abandon), live hand to mouth (barely survive), bite the hand that feeds you (harm your benefactor)
> - **Heart:** wear one's heart on one's sleeve (show feelings openly), learn by heart (memorise)
> - **Tooth:** armed to the teeth (fully armed), by the skin of one's teeth (narrowly)
> - **Heel:** cool one's heels (be kept waiting)

---

## TRICK 3 — The Colour Cluster 🎨

> **BOX:** Colour idioms = fixed meanings, never literal.
> - **Red:** red tape (official delay), in the red (in debt)
> - **Blue:** out of the blue (suddenly), feel blue (sad), once in a blue moon (very rarely), a bolt from the blue (a sudden shock)
> - **Black:** black sheep (disgraceful member), in black and white (in writing)
> - **Green:** green with envy (jealous), greenhorn (inexperienced person)

---

## TRICK 4 — The Animal Cluster 🐘

> **BOX:** Animal idioms follow the animal's nature.
> - **White elephant** — costly but useless possession (elephants were expensive to keep!)
> - **A lion's share** — the largest part
> - **To bell the cat** — do a dangerous task
> - **A wild goose chase** — a useless search
> - **Kill the goose that lays the golden eggs** — destroy a source of income

---

## TRICK 5 — The Weather Cluster ⛈️

> **BOX:** Weather idioms describe situations, not weather.
> - **Rain cats and dogs** — rain heavily
> - **Save for a rainy day** — save for hard times
> - **A storm in a teacup** — a big fuss over a small matter
> - **Under the weather** — feeling ill

---

## TRICK 6 — The "Death & Danger" Cluster ⚔️

> **BOX:** War/death idioms = endings and risks.
> - **Kick the bucket** — to die
> - **At daggers drawn** — bitter enemies
> - **Burn one's boats/bridges** — cut off all means of retreat
> - **A dead letter** — a law/rule no longer enforced
> - **Die in harness** — die while still working

---

## TRICK 7 — The Money Cluster 💰

> **BOX:** Money idioms = wealth or poverty, nothing else.
> - **Born with a silver spoon** — born rich
> - **Live from hand to mouth** — very poor
> - **Make both ends meet** — manage within one's income
> - **In the red** — in debt (pair it with Trick 3's colour cluster)

---

## TRICK 8 — The "Apple of One's Eye" Origin Hook 🍎

> **BOX:** Link idioms to a mini-story — stories stick, lists don't.
> Old belief: the pupil of the eye was a solid "apple" — the most precious part of the body. So **"apple of one's eye" = most beloved person**. One story = permanent memory.

---

## TRICK 9 — The Opposite-Pair Trick ↔️

> **BOX:** Learn idioms in opposite pairs — the exam often swaps them.
> - **By fits and starts** (irregularly) ↔ **by leaps and bounds** (rapidly)
> - **At sixes and sevens** (in confusion) ↔ **in apple-pie order** (perfectly arranged)
> - **To add fuel to fire** (worsen) ↔ **to pour oil on troubled waters** (calm)

---

## TRICK 10 — The "One Word = One Idiom" Match 🔑

> **BOX:** Some idioms are just fancy one-word substitutes. Match them instantly.
> - **A man of straw** = a person of no substance
> - **A man of letters** = a scholar
> - **A man of parts** = a talented person
> - **A man of the world** = an experienced person
> Trap: examiners swap "straw" and "letters" — read the middle word carefully!

---

## TRICK 11 — The Preposition Lock 🔒

> **BOX:** In phrasal idioms, the preposition is fixed. Test yourself on the preposition, not the verb.
> - **Look down on / look down upon** (despise) — both forms are correct and standard
> - **Put up with** (tolerate), **put off** (postpone), **put out** (extinguish)
> - **Call off** (cancel), **call on** (visit), **call for** (demand)
> One verb + four prepositions = four different meanings. Learn them as a grid.

---

## TRICK 12 — The 3-Second Elimination Ladder 🪜

> **BOX:** In the exam, eliminate in this order:
> 1. Literal meaning option → ❌
> 2. Option with the opposite tone (positive idiom, negative option) → ❌
> 3. Option that is too extreme ("always/never") → ❌
> 4. What remains is your answer — even if you never saw the idiom before.

---

## TRICK 13 — The Hindi-Anchor Trick 🌉

> **BOX:** Anchor English idioms to Hindi equivalents you already know.
> - **To shed crocodile tears** = मगरमच्छ के आँसू
> - **A bolt from the blue** = आसमान से बिजली गिरना
> - **To add fuel to fire** = आग में घी डालना
> - **To burn the midnight oil** = रात-रात भर जागकर पढ़ना
> Your Hindi knowledge is a free memory bank — use it!

---

## TRICK 14 — The "Confusing Twins" Flash Rule 👯

> **BOX:** These pairs are swapped in exams every year — memorise the difference NOW.
> - **At large** (free, not caught) ≠ **by and large** (on the whole)
> - **In the long run** (eventually) ≠ **on the run** (fleeing)
> - **Hard of hearing** (partially deaf) ≠ **hard-hearted** (cruel)

---

## TRICK 15 — The Revision Rhythm 🔁

> **BOX:** Idioms fade fast. Follow the 1-3-7 rule:
> Revise today's idioms after **1 day**, again after **3 days**, again after **7 days**. Three touches = permanent memory. One reading = forgotten by exam day.

---

# ⏭️ SKIP STRATEGY

## Skip Tip 1 — The "Never-Seen-It" Rule
If you have **never seen the idiom** and cannot decode it from context clusters (Tricks 2–7), do NOT guess blindly. Apply the Elimination Ladder (Trick 12); if you still can't cut it to 2 options, **skip and return**. A wild guess costs negative marks; a skipped question costs nothing.

## Skip Tip 2 — The 15-Second Cap
Idiom questions are **fact-based, not inference-based** — you either know the meaning or you don't. Give each idiom question a hard cap of **15 seconds**. If the answer doesn't surface in 15 seconds, mark it and move on. Save your 45-second slots for comprehension and inference questions where thinking actually helps.

## Skip Tip 3 — Don't Over-Think Familiar Idioms
The opposite trap: you **know** the idiom, but a clever option makes you doubt yourself ("Could 'break the ice' mean to destroy something?"). **First instinct wins** for idioms you have revised. Over-thinking converts sure marks into wrong answers. Answer, and move.
