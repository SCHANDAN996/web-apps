# Idioms & Phrases — Practice Set 3 (Questions 51–75)
# Difficulty: Q 51–60 Easy | Q 61–75 Medium | 2 Examiner-Trap Questions marked ⚠️

51. The idiom "to break the ice" means:
(a) To destroy something valuable (b) To initiate a conversation or social interaction (c) To end a friendship (d) To cool down an argument
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Break the ice" refers to easing initial social awkwardness and starting a conversation, not destroying anything. The literal ice image tempts learners toward destruction meanings, so the answer is to initiate a conversation.
Source: NCERT Class 10 English

52. "A blessing in disguise" refers to:
(a) A hidden enemy (b) A good thing that seemed bad at first (c) A gift wrapped beautifully (d) A religious ceremony
Answer: (b)
Solution: The idiom describes a misfortune that later turns out to be beneficial. The word "disguise" tempts the "hidden enemy" reading, but the idiom is about outcomes, not people, so the answer is a good thing that seemed bad at first.
Source: PYQ-style

53. "To hit the nail on the head" means:
(a) To hurt someone physically (b) To work as a carpenter (c) To describe a situation exactly right (d) To miss an opportunity
Answer: (c)
Solution: Striking a nail squarely on its head is precise work; the idiom borrows that precision for speech — saying exactly the right thing. The answer is to describe a situation exactly right.
Source: NCERT Class 9 English

54. The phrase "once in a blue moon" means:
(a) Very frequently (b) Very rarely (c) At night time (d) During a festival
Answer: (b)
Solution: A "blue moon" (a second full moon in one calendar month) is uncommon, so the idiom means something happens very rarely. The answer is very rarely.
Source: NCERT Class 8 English

55. "To let the cat out of the bag" means:
(a) To reveal a secret (b) To free an animal (c) To create chaos (d) To buy a pet
Answer: (a)
Solution: The idiom comes from old market frauds where a cat was secretly substituted for a piglet in a bag; letting it out exposed the trick. Hence it means to reveal a secret.
Source: PYQ-style

56. "To burn the midnight oil" means:
(a) To waste resources (b) To light lamps at night (c) To work or study late into the night (d) To stay awake worrying
Answer: (c)
Solution: Before electricity, working late meant burning lamp oil; the idiom now means studying or working late into the night. The answer is to work or study late into the night.
Source: NCERT Class 10 English

57. The idiom "a piece of cake" means:
(a) A delicious dessert (b) A small share of something (c) Something very easy to do (d) A birthday celebration
Answer: (c)
Solution: "A piece of cake" describes a task requiring almost no effort. The food image tempts literal readings, but the idiomatic meaning is something very easy to do.
Source: NCERT Class 8 English

58. "To sit on the fence" means:
(a) To relax outdoors (b) To watch others work (c) To guard one's property (d) To avoid taking sides in a dispute
Answer: (d)
Solution: A person sitting on a fence has not come down on either side; the idiom means remaining neutral or undecided. The answer is to avoid taking sides in a dispute.
Source: PYQ-style

59. "To cost an arm and a leg" means:
(a) To require hard labour (b) To cause physical injury (c) To be worthless (d) To be very expensive
Answer: (d)
Solution: Losing an arm and a leg would be a terrible price; the idiom uses that exaggeration to mean something costs a great deal of money. The answer is to be very expensive.
Source: NCERT Class 10 English

60. The phrase "to kill two birds with one stone" means:
(a) To be cruel to animals (b) To hunt efficiently (c) To waste effort (d) To achieve two aims with a single action
Answer: (d)
Solution: One stone bringing down two birds is maximum efficiency; the idiom means accomplishing two goals with one effort. The answer is to achieve two aims with a single action.
Source: NCERT Class 9 English

61. ⚠️ Examiner-Trap: "To wash one's dirty linen in public" means:
(a) To discuss private or embarrassing matters publicly (b) To do laundry openly (c) To clean one's reputation (d) To insult someone in a meeting
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is the "insult someone" option — the idiom is about exposing one's OWN private affairs or scandals in public, not attacking others. "Clean one's reputation" reverses the meaning. The answer is to discuss private or embarrassing matters publicly.
Source: PYQ-style

62. "To have a bee in one's bonnet" means:
(a) To be obsessed with an idea (b) To be stung by an insect (c) To wear a strange hat (d) To be very busy
Answer: (a)
Solution: A bee buzzing inside a bonnet would be impossible to ignore; the idiom means being preoccupied or obsessed with a fixed idea. "Very busy" is the tempting near-miss, but the idiom is about fixation, not busyness. The answer is to be obsessed with an idea.
Source: PYQ-style

63. "To read between the lines" means:
(a) To skip parts of a text (b) To criticise a writer (c) To read very quickly (d) To find the hidden or implied meaning
Answer: (d)
Solution: The idiom means understanding what is suggested but not directly stated — the meaning "between" the written words. The answer is to find the hidden or implied meaning.
Source: NCERT Class 10 English

64. "A red herring" is:
(a) A type of fish dish (b) A misleading distraction from the real issue (c) An important clue (d) A warning signal
Answer: (b)
Solution: A smoked (red) herring's strong smell was used to throw hunting dogs off a trail; the idiom means a deliberate distraction. "An important clue" is the exact opposite and the classic trap. The answer is a misleading distraction from the real issue.
Source: PYQ-style

65. "To take something with a grain of salt" means:
(a) To accept something completely (b) To doubt whether something is entirely true (c) To add flavour to food (d) To preserve information
Answer: (b)
Solution: The idiom advises scepticism — not believing something fully without verification. "Accept completely" is the opposite trap. The answer is to doubt whether something is entirely true.
Source: PYQ-style

66. "To be on cloud nine" means:
(a) To be extremely happy (b) To be confused (c) To be in danger (d) To be very tall
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Cloud nine" denotes a state of blissful happiness. The height image tempts "confused" or "in danger" readings, but the idiom is purely about joy. The answer is to be extremely happy.
Source: NCERT Class 9 English

67. "To bite off more than one can chew" means:
(a) To attempt more than one can manage (b) To eat too quickly (c) To be greedy with food (d) To speak too much
Answer: (a)
Solution: Taking a mouthful too big to chew is the image; the idiom means taking on a task beyond one's capacity. The answer is to attempt more than one can manage.
Source: NCERT Class 10 English

68. ⚠️ Examiner-Trap: "To turn over a new leaf" means:
(a) To start reading a new book (b) To plant a tree (c) To change one's behaviour for the better (d) To forget the past completely
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is "forget the past completely" — the idiom is about reforming conduct, not erasing memory. Here "leaf" means a page, not a plant leaf, so "plant a tree" is a literal trap. The answer is to change one's behaviour for the better.
Source: PYQ-style

69. "To pour oil on troubled waters" means:
(a) To calm a quarrel or tense situation (b) To pollute the environment (c) To waste money (d) To make a problem worse
Answer: (a)
Solution: Oil literally calms rough seas; the idiom means soothing a dispute. "Make a problem worse" is the opposite trap (that is "adding fuel to fire"). The answer is to calm a quarrel or tense situation.
Source: PYQ-style

70. "A wild goose chase" is:
(a) A useless or hopeless search (b) An exciting adventure (c) A hunting expedition (d) A fast journey
Answer: (a)
Solution: Chasing a wild goose can never succeed; the idiom means a futile pursuit. "Exciting adventure" is tempting because chases sound thrilling, but the idiom stresses futility. The answer is a useless or hopeless search.
Source: NCERT Class 10 English

71. "To have cold feet" means:
(a) To suffer from poor circulation (b) To feel unwelcome (c) To suddenly become too nervous to do something planned (d) To walk barefoot
Answer: (c)
Solution: The idiom describes last-minute nervousness that makes someone hesitate or withdraw from a planned action, like a wedding or exam. The answer is to suddenly become too nervous to do something planned.
Source: PYQ-style

72. "To burn one's boats" means:
(a) To destroy enemy ships (b) To waste one's savings (c) To celebrate a victory (d) To make retreat impossible; commit fully
Answer: (d)
Solution: Ancient generals burned their boats so soldiers could not retreat; the idiom means committing to a course of action with no way back. The answer is to make retreat impossible and commit fully.
Source: PYQ-style

73. "To be at sixes and sevens" means:
(a) To gamble with dice (b) To be perfectly organised (c) To be in a state of confusion or disorder (d) To count incorrectly
Answer: (c)
Solution: The idiom describes a muddled, disorganised state of mind or affairs. "Perfectly organised" is the opposite trap. The answer is to be in a state of confusion or disorder.
Source: PYQ-style

74. "To grease someone's palm" means:
(a) To shake hands warmly (b) To bribe someone (c) To help someone in need (d) To apply lotion
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Greasing the palm" is a euphemism for slipping money into someone's hand to get favours — bribery. "Help someone" is the polite-sounding trap. The answer is to bribe someone.
Source: PYQ-style

75. "To rest on one's laurels" means:
(a) To sleep after hard work (b) To retire from service (c) To receive an award (d) To be so satisfied with past success that one stops trying
Answer: (d)
Solution: Laurel wreaths crowned victors in ancient Greece; "resting" on them means relying on old achievements instead of making new efforts. "Receive an award" is the tempting trap, but the idiom carries a warning against complacency. The answer is to be so satisfied with past success that one stops trying.
Source: NCERT Class 10 English
