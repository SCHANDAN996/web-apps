# 📋 Important Rules & Tips — Idioms & Phrases

## (a) 12 High-Yield Rules

| # | Rule | Example | Examiner's Trap |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | An idiom's meaning is **figurative**, never literal. Translate the idea, not the words. | "To kick the bucket" = to die (not to hit a bucket) | Options often include the literal meaning as a distractor — reject it instantly. |
| 2 | Idioms are **fixed expressions** — you cannot change words, tense markers, or word order freely. | "By hook or by crook" ✅; "By crook or by hook" ❌ | Exams test near-correct versions with one word swapped ("at the drop of a cap"). |
| 3 | Learn idioms with their **prepositions** — a wrong preposition kills the idiom. | "To give in" (surrender) vs "To give up" (quit) | "Give in" and "give up" are both offered; context decides. |
| 4 | One idiom can have **multiple meanings**; pick the one that fits the sentence. | "To break down" = to cry / to stop working / to collapse | The first meaning you memorised may not fit the given sentence. |
| 5 | **Phrasal verbs** (verb + particle) are tested like idioms — meaning changes completely with the particle. | look after (care for), look into (investigate), look up to (respect) | Same verb, different particles in all four options. |
| 6 | Idioms of **colour, animals, body parts, and numbers** are the most repeated families. | once in a blue moon, to let the cat out of the bag, to wash one's hands of | Similar-sounding idioms ("black sheep" vs "dark horse") are paired as options. |
| 7 | Match the idiom's **register/tone** to the sentence — formal vs informal. | "To pass away" (formal) vs "To kick the bucket" (informal) | A grammatically right but tonally wrong idiom is a wrong answer. |
| 8 | In "choose the correct meaning" questions, **eliminate** literal and opposite options first. | "To burn the midnight oil" → eliminate "to waste time" | Two options are usually opposites; the answer lies between the remaining two. |
| 9 | In sentence-based questions, the **context clue** (a nearby word) reveals the meaning. | "He was *penniless*, living from hand to mouth" → "penniless" confirms poverty | Ignoring the clue word and answering from memory alone. |
| 10 | **Foreign words/phrases** (Latin/French) used in English are a separate scoring area. | viz. (namely), de facto (in fact), en route (on the way) | Confusing "e.g." (for example) with "i.e." (that is). |
| 11 | **One-word substitutes** often pair with idioms — learn them together. | "A man of few words" = laconic/reticent | Options mix an idiom's meaning with a one-word substitute's meaning. |
| 12 | Never guess an idiom from **one familiar word** inside it. | "A red herring" = a distraction (nothing to do with fish) | The familiar word ("red", "herring") is planted to mislead. |

## (b) Time Management — The 15/45-Second Rule

| Type | What it looks like | Time | Strategy |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Fact-based (15 sec)** | "What is the meaning of the idiom 'to bite the dust'?" — direct recall, no sentence context | 15 sec | Know it → mark it → move on. Don't re-read options twice. If unknown in 15 sec, mark for review and skip. |
| **Inference-based (45 sec)** | Idiom used inside a sentence; you must use context clues, tone, and elimination | 45 sec | Read the full sentence → underline the clue word → eliminate literal + opposite options → verify the survivor by re-reading. |

**Rule of thumb:** Direct-meaning questions are 15-second questions. Sentence-embedded, "most appropriate meaning", and tone/register questions are 45-second questions. Never spend 45 seconds on a recall question — that time belongs to inference questions.

## (c) Morphology — Roots & Affixes Behind Idiomatic Vocabulary

| Root/Affix | Meaning | Derived Words (seen in idioms & options) |
|---|---|---|
| **dict** (Latin, dicere) | say, speak | dictate, predict, verdict, contradict |
| **port** (Latin, portare) | carry | transport, export, portable, support |
| **cred** (Latin, credere) | believe | credible, incredible, credit, credulous |
| **spect** (Latin, specere) | look, see | inspect, respect, spectator, perspective |
| **manu** (Latin, manus) | hand | manual, manufacture, manuscript, manipulate |
| **aqua** (Latin) | water | aquatic, aquarium, aqueduct |
| **pre-** (prefix) | before | predict, precaution, premature |
| **re-** (prefix) | again, back | rebuild, return, revive |
| **-less** (suffix) | without | penniless, hopeless, restless |
| **-ology** (suffix) | study of | biology, psychology, geology |

**Why this matters for idioms:** Many idiom options contain formal words built on these roots. Knowing that *cred* = believe instantly tells you "incredible" ≈ "unbelievable" — helping you eliminate wrong options even when the idiom itself is new.
