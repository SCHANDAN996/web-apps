# 🧒 Feynman Technique — Idioms & Phrases Explained to a 12-Year-Old

## The Hardest Concept: "Why can't I just translate the words?"

Imagine your friend says: **"It's raining cats and dogs."**

You look out of the window. No cats. No dogs falling from the sky. Just... rain. Lots of rain.

Now imagine you translate it word by word into Hindi: *"बिल्लियाँ और कुत्ते बरस रहे हैं।"* Your grandmother would laugh at you! Because in Hindi we say *"मूसलाधार बारिश हो रही है।"*

**That is the whole secret of idioms:**

> An idiom is a group of words whose meaning is a **locked box**. You cannot open it word by word. The whole phrase together means ONE thing — and that meaning has nothing to do with the individual words.

## A Daily-Life Example

Think of a **pizza**. 🍕

- Flour + tomato + cheese + onion = these are the *ingredients* (the words).
- But when someone says "pizza", you don't think of flour and tomatoes separately. You think of ONE thing — a pizza!

An idiom works exactly like that:
- **"Kick" + "the" + "bucket"** = three ordinary words.
- **"Kick the bucket"** = ONE meaning: *to die*. (No bucket is kicked!)

So the rule is simple: **Never break an idiom into pieces. Swallow it whole, like a pizza slice.**

## The Unforgettable Trick: 🎬 "The Movie Trick"

For every idiom, don't memorise the meaning — **make a 3-second movie in your head.**

| Idiom | Your mental movie | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| To burn the midnight oil | You studying at 2 AM with a lamp | To work/study late into the night |
| A blessing in disguise | A gift wrapped in ugly paper, gold inside | A hidden good thing |
| To let the cat out of the bag | A cat jumping out of a bag, everyone shocked | To reveal a secret |
| Once in a blue moon | A rare blue-coloured moon in the sky | Very rarely |
| To hit the nail on the head | A hammer hitting a nail perfectly | To say exactly the right thing |

The sillier the movie, the stronger the memory. Your brain forgets definitions; it never forgets a funny picture.

## Why Exams Trick You (and how to beat them)

The examiner knows you will try to translate. So he puts a **word-by-word meaning** as one of the options — and it is always wrong!

**Example:** *"To break the ice"*
- (a) To melt frozen water ← **TRAP!** (word-by-word meaning)
- (b) To start a conversation / remove awkwardness ← **CORRECT**
- (c) To destroy something
- (d) To feel cold

**Your shield:** If an option looks like the literal dictionary meaning of the words, **eliminate it first.** The correct answer is always the *figurative* (hidden) meaning.

## One More Thing: Idioms vs. Phrases

- **Idiom** = meaning is hidden (kick the bucket = die).
- **Phrase** = meaning is visible in the words (in the morning, at the door).

If you can guess the meaning from the words, it's a phrase. If the words seem crazy, it's an idiom. Easy!
