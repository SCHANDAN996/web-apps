1. The idiom "to bite the dust" means:
(a) to eat in a hurry (b) to work very hard (c) to clean the floor (d) to fail or be defeated
Answer: (d)
Solution: The tempting wrong choice is "to work very hard" because biting dust sounds like exhausting labour, but the idiom actually describes falling to the ground in defeat or failure. It has nothing to do with eating or cleaning, so the answer is to fail or be defeated.
Source: NCERT Class 9 English (Beehive)

2. "A blessing in disguise" refers to:
(a) a hidden enemy (b) a gift wrapped in paper (c) a religious ceremony (d) a good thing that seemed bad at first
Answer: (d)
Solution: Many students pick "a hidden enemy" because "disguise" suggests something concealed and suspicious, but the idiom means a misfortune that turns out to be beneficial. The disguise hides a blessing, not a threat, so the answer is a good thing that seemed bad at first.
Source: PYQ-style

3. The phrase "once in a blue moon" means:
(a) every night (b) during a festival (c) very rarely (d) in the evening
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Every night" tempts students who connect the moon with daily nightfall, but a blue moon is an uncommon event, so the idiom means something that happens very rarely. Frequency, not time of day, is the idea, so the answer is very rarely.
Source: NCERT Class 8 English (Honeydew)

4. "To let the cat out of the bag" means:
(a) to free a pet (b) to reveal a secret (c) to buy a cat (d) to create confusion
Answer: (b)
Solution: The literal image of freeing a pet misleads many, but the idiom comes from an old market trick where a cat was secretly sold in place of a pig; opening the bag exposed the trick. Hence it means to reveal a secret, so the answer is to reveal a secret.
Source: PYQ-style

5. The idiom "to hit the nail on the head" means:
(a) to say exactly the right thing (b) to hurt someone (c) to do carpentry (d) to miss the target
Answer: (a)
Solution: "To miss the target" is the trap because hitting sounds aggressive and uncertain, but striking a nail squarely on its head is precise and accurate. The idiom therefore means describing a situation perfectly, so the answer is to say exactly the right thing.
Source: NCERT Class 10 English (First Flight)

6. "An apple of one's eye" means:
(a) a source of trouble (b) a round fruit (c) something very precious (d) an eye disease
Answer: (c)
Solution: Students sometimes choose "a source of trouble" thinking of the biblical apple, but this idiom refers to the pupil of the eye, once believed to be apple-shaped and extremely delicate. It means someone dearly loved, so the answer is something very precious.
Source: PYQ-style

7. "To burn the midnight oil" means:
(a) to work or study late into the night (b) to waste fuel (c) to light lamps at dusk (d) to sleep early
Answer: (a)
Solution: "To waste fuel" is tempting because burning oil sounds wasteful, but the idiom praises effort — working by lamplight late into the night. It describes hard study or labour, so the answer is to work or study late into the night.

8. The idiom "a red herring" means:
(a) a tasty dish (b) a lucky sign (c) an important clue (d) something that misleads from the real issue
Answer: (d)
Solution: "An important clue" is the trap because herrings appear in detective stories, but a red herring is a false clue that distracts investigators. It diverts attention from the main point, so the answer is something that misleads from the real issue.

9. "To break the ice" means:
(a) to end a friendship (b) to destroy something (c) to cool a drink (d) to start a conversation
Answer: (d)
Solution: "To end a friendship" tempts because "break" sounds negative, but the idiom pictures breaking frozen ice so a ship can move — similarly, it means overcoming initial awkwardness to begin talking. So the answer is to start a conversation.

10. The phrase "at the drop of a hat" means:
(a) after long thinking (b) instantly, without hesitation (c) when angry (d) by accident
Answer: (b)
Solution: "By accident" misleads because dropping a hat seems unintentional, but historically a dropped hat was the signal to start a race or fight immediately. The idiom means acting instantly, so the answer is instantly, without hesitation.

(a) to avoid taking sides (b) to relax outdoors (c) to guard property (d) to take a firm decision
Answer: (a)
Solution: "To take a firm decision" is the trap because sitting sounds settled and decided, but a person on a fence belongs to neither side. The idiom means staying neutral, so the answer is to avoid taking sides.

12. The idiom "to cost an arm and a leg" means:
(a) to be very cheap (b) to cause injury (c) to be very expensive (d) to require physical effort
Answer: (c)
Solution: "To cause injury" tempts because of the body parts mentioned, but the idiom is about price, not pain — something so costly it feels like paying with limbs. So the answer is to be very expensive.

13. "To kill two birds with one stone" means:
(a) to be cruel to animals (b) to waste resources (c) to achieve two aims with one action (d) to fail at both tasks

(a) travelling abroad (b) standing in the rain (c) enjoying good weather (d) feeling slightly ill
Answer: (d)

(a) an exciting adventure (b) a hunting trip (c) a useless search (d) a fast journey
Answer: (c)

21. Choose the correct meaning of the idiom in the sentence: "The manager's vague reply only added fuel to the fire, and the workers grew angrier."
(a) calmed the situation (b) made the situation worse (c) solved the problem (d) confused everyone
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Confused everyone" is tempting because a vague reply does confuse, but the idiom itself means intensifying an already bad situation, as fuel intensifies fire. The workers grew angrier, confirming worsening, so the answer is made the situation worse.
Source: PYQ-style

22. In the sentence "He knew the answer off the top of his head," the idiom means:
(a) after checking his notes (b) from memory, without checking (c) by guessing wildly (d) with great difficulty
Answer: (b)
Solution: "By guessing wildly" is the trap because "off the top" sounds careless, but the idiom means recalling instantly from memory without consulting anything. It implies confident knowledge, so the answer is from memory, without checking.
Source: NCERT Class 10 English (First Flight)

23. Choose the correct meaning: "After the scandal, the minister decided to lie low for a few months."
(a) to hide or avoid attention (b) to rest in bed (c) to resign from office (d) to speak to the media
Answer: (a)
Solution: "To rest in bed" misleads through the word "lie," but the idiom means keeping out of public notice until trouble passes. The context of a scandal confirms concealment, so the answer is to hide or avoid attention.

24. In "The new policy turned the tide in favour of small farmers," the idiom "turned the tide" means:
(a) reversed the situation (b) caused a flood (c) delayed the decision (d) divided the people
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Delayed the decision" tempts because tides move slowly, but the idiom means reversing the direction of events, just as a turning tide reverses the sea's flow. The policy changed fortunes for farmers, so the answer is reversed the situation.
Source: PYQ-style

25. Choose the correct meaning of the idiom in: "She passed the exam with flying colours."
(a) with great success (b) by cheating (c) with average marks (d) after many attempts
Answer: (a)
Solution: "After many attempts" is the trap because students associate passing with struggle, but "flying colours" comes from ships returning victorious with flags flying high. It means passing brilliantly, so the answer is with great success.
