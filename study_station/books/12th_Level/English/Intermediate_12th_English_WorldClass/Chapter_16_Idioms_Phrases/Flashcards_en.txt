Card 1
Front: What is an idiom? How is it different from a phrase?
Back: An idiom is a group of words whose meaning cannot be guessed from the individual words (e.g., "kick the bucket" = to die). A phrase is a group of words that functions as a unit but lacks a subject and a finite verb; an idiom is a special phrase with a fixed, figurative meaning.

Card 2
Front: What does "to burn the midnight oil" mean?
Back: To study or work late into the night. Trap: it has nothing to do with oil or lamps literally — it means hard work at night.

Card 3
Front: Meaning of "a blessing in disguise"?
Back: Something that seems bad at first but turns out to be good. Example: Losing that job was a blessing in disguise — he found a better one.

Card 4
Front: 🧠 MNEMONIC: How to remember "once in a blue moon"?
Back: A blue moon is extremely rare in the sky — so the idiom means "very rarely." Picture a rare blue moon = rare event. "He visits us once in a blue moon."

Card 5
Front: ⚠️ EXAMINER'S TRAP: "To bite the dust" — does it mean to eat dirt?
Back: No. It means to fail, be defeated, or die. Examiners offer the literal meaning ("to eat dust") as a wrong option. Always choose the figurative meaning.

Card 6
Front: Meaning of "to let the cat out of the bag"?
Back: To reveal a secret, usually by mistake. Example: She let the cat out of the bag about the surprise party.

Card 7
Front: What does "to hit the nail on the head" mean?
Back: To say or do exactly the right thing; to describe a situation perfectly.

Card 8
Front: Meaning of "to sit on the fence"?
Back: To remain neutral; to avoid taking sides in a dispute or argument.

Card 9
Front: 🧠 MNEMONIC: Remember "to add insult to injury"?
Back: Imagine falling down (injury) and then someone laughing at you (insult) — two bad things together. Meaning: to make a bad situation worse.

Card 10
Front: ⚠️ EXAMINER'S TRAP: "A red herring" — is it about fish?
Back: No. It means a misleading clue or distraction that diverts attention from the real issue. Common in RC and idiom questions; the literal "fish" option is the trap.

Card 11
Front: Meaning of "to break the ice"?
Back: To start a conversation or remove initial awkwardness between strangers. Example: He told a joke to break the ice.

Card 12
Front: What does "to cost an arm and a leg" mean?
Back: To be extremely expensive. Example: That phone costs an arm and a leg.

Card 13
Front: Meaning of "to kill two birds with one stone"?
Back: To achieve two goals with a single action. Example: By cycling to work, he kills two birds with one stone — saves money and stays fit.

Card 14
Front: What does "under the weather" mean?
Back: Feeling slightly ill or unwell. Example: She's a bit under the weather today, so she stayed home.

Card 15
Front: Meaning of "to beat about the bush"?
Back: To talk around a topic without coming to the main point. (British: "beat about the bush"; American: "beat around the bush" — both are accepted.)

Card 16
Front: What does "a piece of cake" mean?
Back: Something very easy to do. Example: The SSC mock test was a piece of cake after all that practice.

Card 17
Front: Meaning of "to face the music"?
Back: To accept the unpleasant consequences of one's actions. Example: After the scam was exposed, the manager had to face the music.

Card 18
Front: What does "by hook or by crook" mean?
Back: By any means possible, fair or unfair. Example: He was determined to pass the exam by hook or by crook.

Card 19
Front: Meaning of "to rain cats and dogs"?
Back: To rain very heavily. Trap: never interpret it literally — it simply means torrential rain.

Card 20
Front: What does "to be on cloud nine" mean?
Back: To be extremely happy. Example: She was on cloud nine after clearing the exam.
