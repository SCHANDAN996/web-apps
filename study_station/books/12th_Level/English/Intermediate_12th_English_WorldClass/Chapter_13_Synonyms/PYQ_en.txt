# 📊 PYQ Analysis — Synonyms

## How Exams Test Synonyms

Synonyms are among the most **predictable** question types in SSC (CGL, CHSL, MTS, GD), Banking (IBPS, SBI), and Railway (NTPC, Group D) exams. Instead of inventing numbers, here is what repeated analysis of actual papers shows — in words:

**Pattern 1 — Direct word replacement (most common).**
A single word is given (sometimes in a sentence, sometimes standalone) and you must pick the closest meaning. SSC CGL and CHSL favour **medium-frequency formal words**: *abandon, candid, fragile, obsolete, reluctant, vigilant*. Banking exams lean toward **economy/report words**: *surge, slump, curb, boost, scrutiny*.

**Pattern 2 — Synonym in a sentence context.**
The word appears inside a sentence, and the context quietly changes which option fits. Example: "The manager gave a *curt* reply." Here *curt* = brief/rude, not just "short". Exams use this to punish dictionary-only learners.

**Pattern 3 — Confusable near-synonyms.**
Options are four words that all *feel* similar, but only one matches the exact shade of meaning: e.g., for *begin*, options like *commence, initiate, inaugurate, embark* — the examiner wants the closest general equivalent (*commence*), not the fanciest word.

**Pattern 4 — Negative framing.**
"Which of the following is NOT a synonym of X?" — students misread and pick a true synonym. Always circle the NOT.

**Pattern 5 — Hindi-medium trap words.**
Words whose English meaning differs from what Hindi speakers assume: *pretend* (नाटक करना, not "intend"), *demand* (माँगना, stronger than "ask"), *sympathetic* (दयालु/सहानुभूतिपूर्ण, not just "friendly").

## Examiner's Favourite Sub-Topics

- **Abstract/formal verbs:** abandon, abolish, accomplish, endure, persevere
- **Adjectives of character:** candid, timid, arrogant, humble, obstinate
- **Words of change:** transform, modify, alter, amend, rectify
- **Words of emotion:** elated, gloomy, furious, serene, anxious
- **Formal/report vocabulary:** adequate, subsequent, inevitable, feasible, lucrative

## Trap-Type Classification

| Trap Type | What It Looks Like | Defence |
|---|---|---|
| **Sound-Right** | Option sounds similar to the question word but means something else (*affect/effect*, *elicit/illicit*) | Never match by sound; match by meaning |
| **Intervening Context** | Sentence context changes the shade of meaning | Read the full sentence, not just the word |
| **Over-thinking** | You reject the obvious correct answer because it seems "too easy" | First instinct on direct synonyms is usually right |
| **Near-Synonym Crowd** | All four options are loosely related | Rank options by *closeness*, not by fanciness |

## Top 10 High-Yield PYQ-Style Questions with Solutions

**Q1. Choose the synonym of ABANDON.**
(a) keep (b) forsake (c) cherish (d) maintain
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** *Abandon* = to give up completely = *forsake*. (a), (c), (d) are antonyms — classic **Sound-Right** trap where students pick a "positive" word.
**Source:** PYQ-style (SSC pattern)

**Q2. Choose the synonym of CANDID.**
(a) secretive (b) frank (c) clever (d) rude
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** *Candid* = truthful and straightforward = *frank*. (d) *rude* is the **Over-thinking** trap — candid speech can *seem* rude, but the word itself means honest.
**Source:** PYQ-style (SSC CGL pattern)

**Q3. Choose the synonym of OBSOLETE.**
(a) outdated (b) modern (c) useful (d) rare
**Answer: (a)**
**Solution:** *Obsolete* = no longer in use = *outdated*. (d) *rare* is the trap — obsolete things are often rare, but rare ≠ out of use.
**Source:** PYQ-style (Banking pattern)

**Q4. Choose the synonym of RELUCTANT.**
(a) eager (b) unwilling (c) ready (d) careless
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** *Reluctant* = hesitant, not wanting to = *unwilling*. (d) *careless* is a **Sound-Right** distractor with no meaning link.
**Source:** PYQ-style (SSC CHSL pattern)

**Q5. Choose the synonym of VIGILANT.**
(a) careless (b) watchful (c) violent (d) sleepy
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** *Vigilant* = alert, keeping careful watch = *watchful*. (c) *violent* is the classic **Sound-Right** trap — similar sound, opposite family of meaning.
**Source:** PYQ-style (Railway NTPC pattern)

**Q6. "The new policy will CURB inflation." Choose the synonym of CURB.**
(a) increase (b) control (c) ignore (d) announce
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** *Curb* = restrain/check = *control*. **Intervening Context** trap: in a different sentence *curb* can mean the edge of a pavement — context decides.
**Source:** PYQ-style (Banking/economy pattern)

**Q7. Choose the synonym of ELATED.**
(a) depressed (b) overjoyed (c) angry (d) tired
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** *Elated* = extremely happy = *overjoyed*. (a) is its antonym — placed first to catch hasty readers.
**Source:** PYQ-style (SSC pattern)

**Q8. Choose the synonym of FEASIBLE.**
(a) impossible (b) practicable (c) expensive (d) imaginary
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** *Feasible* = possible to do = *practicable*. (c) *expensive* is an **Over-thinking** trap — feasibility is about possibility, not cost.
**Source:** PYQ-style (SSC CGL pattern)

**Q9. Choose the synonym of OBSTINATE.**
(a) flexible (b) stubborn (c) obedient (d) polite
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** *Obstinate* = refusing to change one's mind = *stubborn*. The other three are positive traits — a **Near-Synonym Crowd** of character words.
**Source:** PYQ-style (SSC MTS pattern)

**Q10. Which is NOT a synonym of BRIEF?**
(a) short (b) concise (c) lengthy (d) terse
**Answer: (c)**
**Solution:** *Brief* = short; *concise* and *terse* are true synonyms. *Lengthy* is the opposite — the **Negative Framing** trap catches students who skip the word NOT.
**Source:** PYQ-style (all exams)

## Applying the 15/45-Second Rule

**15-second question (Fact-based):** Q5 (*vigilant*). You either know the word or you don't. See it → recall *watchful* → mark (b) → move on. Spending 60 seconds here steals time from Reading Comprehension.

**45-second question (Inference-based):** Q6 (*curb* in a sentence). Here you must (1) read the full sentence, (2) test each option by substituting it — "The new policy will *control* inflation" ✓ makes sense; "will *announce* inflation" ✗ nonsense. Substitution takes time — budget it.

**Rule of thumb:** Standalone word → 15 sec. Word inside a sentence or NOT-framed question → 45 sec. If a 15-second word is unknown after 20 seconds, mark a guess and skip — never let one vocabulary word eat two questions' time.
