🧒 FEYNMAN TECHNIQUE — Synonyms Explained Like You're 12

🎯 The Hardest Concept: "Two words can mean almost the same thing — but the examiner wants the CLOSEST one, not just any similar one."

Imagine you have a box of crayons. You ask your friend for the "red" crayon. Your friend gives you a crayon that is orange-red. Is it red? Sort of. But there was also a pure red crayon in the box! A good friend picks the pure red one — the CLOSEST match.

Synonyms work exactly like this. In the exam, all four options may look "similar" to the word given. But only ONE is the pure red crayon — the closest in meaning. The other three are orange-red, pink, and brown — near, but not the answer.

📦 A Daily-Life Example

Word: ABANDON
Options: (a) leave (b) destroy (c) hate (d) forget

Think of a sailor leaving a sinking ship. He "abandons" the ship — he LEAVES it. He doesn't destroy it, hate it, or forget it. So the answer is (a) leave. The other options are "orange-red crayons" — they feel related to a sinking ship, but they don't mean abandon.

🧠 The Unforgettable Trick: "The Twin Test"

Ask yourself: "If I swap the given word with this option in a sentence, does the sentence still mean the SAME thing?"

- "He abandoned the project." → "He left the project." ✅ Same meaning!
- "He destroyed the project." ❌ Different meaning!

If the sentence survives the swap, you've found the twin. If it breaks, throw that crayon back.

⚠️ One More Thing a 12-Year-Old Should Know

Words have FLAVOURS (positive / negative / neutral). "Famous" and "notorious" both mean "well-known" — but famous is a good flavour, notorious is a bad one. The examiner loves this trap! Always match the flavour, not just the meaning.

❓ Question for You

If "FRAGILE" is the word and the options are (a) delicate (b) weak (c) soft (d) thin — which one is the pure red crayon? Apply the Twin Test: "The fragile glass broke." → "The ___ glass broke." Which option keeps the sentence's meaning alive? (Answer: (a) delicate — the closest twin.)
