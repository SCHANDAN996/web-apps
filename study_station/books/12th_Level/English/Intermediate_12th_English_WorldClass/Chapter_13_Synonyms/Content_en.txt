# Synonyms — Mastering Words That Mean the Same

## 🎯 Why This Chapter Matters

Imagine this: the exam hall is silent, the clock is ticking, and a question flashes on your screen — *"Choose the synonym of ABANDON."* Four options stare back: *forsake, cherish, maintain, adopt.* If you know the word, this is a **15-second gift of a mark**. If you don't, it becomes a 2-minute gamble. Synonym questions appear regularly in SSC CHSL, CGL, RRB NTPC and Banking exams, and they are among the fastest questions to solve — but only for students who prepare them *systematically*, not by memorising random word lists.

This chapter teaches you that system.

## 📌 Learning Objectives

By the end of this chapter, you will be able to:

1. Understand what synonyms are — and what they are **not**.
2. Use **Greek and Latin roots** to decode unfamiliar words in the exam.
3. Eliminate wrong options using context, tone and word-family logic.
4. Recognise the examiner's favourite traps (similar-sounding words, partial synonyms, antonym-in-disguise).
5. Answer fact-based synonym questions in under 15 seconds.

## 1. What Exactly Is a Synonym?

A **synonym** is a word that has the **same or nearly the same meaning** as another word.

- **Happy** → joyful, glad, delighted, cheerful
- **Begin** → commence, start, initiate
- **Brave** → courageous, valiant, bold

⚠️ **Critical point:** Synonyms are *nearly* the same, not perfectly identical. "Happy" and "ecstatic" both express joy, but *ecstatic* is far more intense. Exam questions test the **closest** meaning, so always pick the option nearest in both meaning **and** intensity.

### Degrees of Intensity — The Hidden Layer

Words on the same "meaning ladder" differ in strength:

| Mild | Medium | Strong |
|---|---|---|
| happy | delighted | ecstatic |
| big | large | gigantic |
| angry | furious | enraged |
| wet | soaked | drenched |

If the question word is *furious* (strong), the answer is *enraged* — not *annoyed* (mild). **Match the intensity.**

🧠 **Active Recall:** Without looking back — (1) Define a synonym in one line. (2) Arrange in increasing intensity: *drenched, wet, soaked*. (3) Why can't "annoyed" be the synonym of "furious"?

## 2. The Root-Word Method: Your Superpower

You cannot memorise every English word. But you **can** memorise about 50 roots and unlock thousands of words. Most tough exam words are built from Greek and Latin roots.

### High-Yield Roots Table

| Root | Meaning | Derived Words |
|---|---|---|
| **bene** (Latin) | good, well | benefit, benevolent, beneficial, benediction |
| **mal** (Latin) | bad, evil | malicious, malnutrition, malfunction, malevolent |
| **dict** (Latin) | say, speak | predict, dictate, verdict, contradict |
| **port** (Latin) | carry | transport, export, portable, import |
| **spect** (Latin) | see, look | inspect, spectator, respect, perspective |
| **cred** (Latin) | believe | credible, incredible, credit, credulous |
| **chron** (Greek) | time | chronic, chronology, synchronize, anachronism |
| **phil** (Greek) | love | philanthropy, philosophy, bibliophile |
| **phob** (Greek) | fear | phobia, claustrophobia, xenophobia |
| **omni** (Latin) | all | omnipotent, omniscient, omnivorous |

**Example in action:** The question asks for the synonym of **BENEVOLENT**. You don't know the word — but you know *bene* = good. Options: *kind, cruel, selfish, rude*. Only *kind* relates to "good." **Answer locked in 15 seconds.**

🧠 **Active Recall:** (1) What does *mal* mean? Give two words from it. (2) If *spect* = see, what might *inspect* mean? (3) Decode: *omniscient* = omni + sci (know) = ?

## 3. Roots in Context — Read and Feel the Words

Read this short passage. Notice how the root-words behave inside real sentences:

> In an era of **chronic** water shortages, one **benevolent** engineer transformed her village. She designed a **portable** filter that any family could carry to the river. Critics had called her claims **incredible**, but a single **inspection** of the device silenced them. Her **philanthropy** did not stop there — she predicted, with almost **omniscient** accuracy, which villages would face drought next, and her **verdict** was always trusted. Those who once viewed technology with **phobia** now became its loudest supporters.

**Notice:** *chronic* (long-lasting, from time), *benevolent* (kind, from good), *portable* (carry-able), *incredible* (not believable), *inspection* (looking into), *philanthropy* (love of humanity), *omniscient* (all-knowing), *verdict* (a speaking of truth), *phobia* (fear). Nine root-words, one story — this is how memory sticks.

🧠 **Active Recall:** Close the passage. Write from memory the synonym of: *benevolent, chronic, verdict, portable*.

## 4. The 4-Step Exam Method

When a synonym question appears, follow this order:

1. **Read the word.** Do you know it? If yes → answer in 15 seconds.
2. **Break it into root/prefix/suffix.** *Mal-function* = bad + working.
3. **Check intensity and tone.** Is the word positive or negative? Mild or strong? Eliminate options with the wrong tone.
4. **Eliminate and guess smartly.** Even removing 2 wrong options improves your odds from 1-in-4 to 1-in-2.

### 🧠 Mnemonic: **"RITE"**
**R**oot → **I**ntensity → **T**one → **E**liminate.
*"Answer it RITE, or lose the mark!"*

## ⚠️ Examiner's Trap Box

**Trap 1 — The Sound-Alike:** Options that *sound* like the question word but mean something different. *Synonym of ABSTAIN?* Option: *obtain* ❌ (sounds similar). Correct: *refrain* ✔️.

**Trap 2 — The Antonym-in-Disguise:** One option is often the *opposite* meaning, placed to catch students who misread the question as "antonym." *Synonym of CANDID (frank)?* Option: *secretive* ❌ — that's the antonym. Correct: *frank/honest* ✔️.

**Trap 3 — The Partial Synonym:** A word related in meaning but wrong in context or intensity. *Synonym of OBSOLETE?* Option: *old* ❌ (too mild — obsolete means *no longer in use*). Correct: *outdated/discarded* ✔️.

## 🔁 Linguistic Bridge — Hindi से English

Hindi speakers often carry Hindi word-order and usage into English. Watch these:

| ❌ What Hindi speakers say | ✅ Correct English | Why |
|---|---|---|
| "What you are doing?" | "What **are you** doing?" | In questions, the helping verb comes before the subject. |
| "He is my **cousin brother**." | "He is my **cousin**." | English has no "cousin brother/sister" — *cousin* covers both. |
| "I am having a car." | "I **have** a car." | Possession uses simple present, not continuous. |
| "She told **me that why** I came late." | "She asked me **why I had come** late." | Reported speech changes word order and tense. |

**Why this matters for synonyms:** Many students pick wrong options because they translate the Hindi meaning too literally. *"Mehngai"* feels like "expensive-ness," but the precise English word is **inflation**. Learn words as English words, not as translations.
