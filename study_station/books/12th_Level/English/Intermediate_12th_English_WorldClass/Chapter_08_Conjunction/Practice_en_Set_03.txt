51. Choose the correct conjunction: "He is poor ______ honest."
(a) and (b) or (c) but (d) so
Answer: (c)
Solution: "And" is tempting because two qualities are listed, but the two ideas contrast (poor vs honest), so a contrastive conjunction is needed. "But" shows contrast, so the answer is but.
Source: NCERT Class 9 English Grammar

52. Choose the correct conjunction: "Work hard ______ you will fail."
(a) and (b) but (c) or (d) because
Answer: (c)
Solution: "And" is tempting as it joins two clauses, but the sentence gives a warning with an alternative result. "Or" means "otherwise", so the answer is or.
Source: NCERT Class 8 English Grammar

53. Choose the correct conjunction: "She was tired, ______ she kept working."
(a) because (b) so (c) yet (d) unless
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Because" is tempting, but tiredness is not the reason she kept working; the second clause contrasts with the first. "Yet" expresses contrast, so the answer is yet.

54. Choose the correct conjunction: "I could not come ______ I was ill."

61. Spot the error: "No sooner did he (P) / enter the room (Q) / when the phone rang. (R) / No error (S)"
(a) Part P (b) Part Q (c) No error (d) Part R
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is that "when" sounds natural after "enter the room", but "no sooner" is always paired with "than", never "when". Part R has the error, so the answer is Part R.

62. Spot the error: "Hardly had she (P) / reached the station (Q) / than the train left. (R) / No error (S)"
(a) Part P (b) Part Q (c) No error (d) Part R
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is confusing "hardly" with "no sooner"; "hardly/scarcely" take "when", while "no sooner" takes "than". Part R has the error, so the answer is Part R.

63. Choose the correct sentence:
(a) He is both a singer as well as a dancer. (b) He is both a singer and a dancer. (c) He is both a singer or a dancer. (d) He is both a singer but also a dancer.
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is "both … as well as", which sounds grand but is wrong; "both" pairs only with "and". The correct pairing is both … and, so the answer is "He is both a singer and a dancer."

64. Choose the correct sentence:
(a) Neither he nor I am going. (b) Neither he or I am going. (c) Neither he nor I is going. (d) Neither he or I is going.
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is "neither … or", which sounds acceptable in speech but is wrong; "neither" pairs with "nor", and the verb agrees with the nearer subject "I" (am). The correct form is "Neither he nor I am going", so the answer is that sentence.

Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is that the sentence sounds fine in speech, but "not only" must come just before the parallel element; it should be "He plays not only cricket but also football". Part P has the misplaced conjunction, so the answer is Part P.

Answer: (b)
Solution: "Lest" is tempting, but "lest" is followed by "should" and expresses fear of something, not a precaution for a possibility. "In case" suits a precaution, so the answer is in case.

70. Choose the correct sentence:

Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is "than" after "scarcely"; "scarcely/hardly" take "when", and inversion (had he) is required. The correct sentence is "Scarcely had he slept when the alarm rang", so the answer is that sentence.

Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is making the verb agree with the nearer plural-looking idea or with "boys"; with "either … or" the verb agrees with the subject nearer to it, here "girl" (was). The correct sentence is "Either the boys or the girl was present", so the answer is that sentence.

74. Choose the correct conjunction: "He is weak ______ he cannot walk."
(a) such … that (b) as … as (c) too … to (d) so … that
Answer: (d)
Solution: "Too … to" is tempting, but it is used without a following clause ("too weak to walk"); here a full clause follows, needing "so … that". The correct pair is so … that, so the answer is so … that.
Source: NCERT Class 9 English Grammar

75. Spot the error: "The reason why he failed (P) / was because (Q) / he did not study. (R) / No error (S)"
(a) Part P (b) No error (c) Part R (d) Part Q
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is that "the reason … was because" is extremely common in speech, but it is redundant; after "the reason", use "that", not "because". Part Q has the error, so the answer is Part Q.
