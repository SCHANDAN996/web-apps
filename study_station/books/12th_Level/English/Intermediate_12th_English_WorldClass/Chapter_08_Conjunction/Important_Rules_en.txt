# 📋 Important Grammar Rules & Tips — Conjunction

## (a) 12 High-Yield Rules

| # | Rule | Example | Examiner's Trap |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Correlative pairs are fixed** — neither…nor, either…or, not only…but also, both…and, no sooner…than, hardly/scarcely…when. Never mix them. | Neither Ram **nor** Shyam came. | "Neither…or" ❌ — the most frequently tested trap. |
| 2 | **Verb agrees with the nearer subject** in either…or / neither…nor / not only…but also. | Neither the teacher nor the students **were** present. | Students see "teacher" first and write "was". Check the subject closest to the verb. |
| 3 | **No sooner…than / Hardly…when** use past perfect + inversion. | No sooner **had** she entered **than** it rained. | "No sooner…when" ❌ and "Hardly…than" ❌ are classic wrong pairings. |
| 4 | **Though/Although never takes "but"**; use "yet" if needed. | Although he is rich, **yet** he is honest. | "Although…but" ❌ — a direct Hindi-habit error. |
| 5 | **Because / Since / As never combine with "so/therefore"** in the same clause. | Because he was ill, he stayed home. | "Because…so" ❌ (from Hindi "क्योंकि…इसलिए"). |
| 6 | **Until/Unless are negative in meaning** — do not add "not" after them. | Wait here **until** I return. | "Until you do not come" ❌ — double negative trap. |
| 7 | **Lest…should** — "lest" is followed by "should" (or bare verb), never by "not". | Work hard **lest** you **should** fail. | "Lest you should not fail" ❌ — "lest" already means "so that…not". |
| 8 | **Both…and joins equals** — same part of speech on both sides (parallel structure). | He is both **intelligent** and **hardworking**. | "Both intelligent and a hard worker" ❌ — adjective must match adjective. |
| 9 | **As well as / along with / together with** do NOT make the subject plural; verb follows the first subject. | The captain, **as well as** the players, **was** tired. | Students treat it like "and" and write "were". |
| 10 | **That vs. Whether** — use "whether" (not "that") when doubt or a choice exists; "if" only for conditions/indirect yes-no questions. | I doubt **whether** he will come. | "I doubt that he will come" changes the meaning — examiners test this nuance. |
| 11 | **The reason…is that**, never "the reason is because". | The reason he failed is **that** he didn't study. | "The reason is because" ❌ — redundancy trap. |
| 12 | **Rather than / other than / no other than** — keep the fixed preposition; "rather than" takes a bare infinitive. | I would die **rather than beg**. | "Rather than to beg" ❌ — no "to" after rather than. |

## (b) Time Management — The 15/45-Second Rule

**⏱️ 15-Second Questions (Fact/Rule-based — answer instantly, don't re-read):**
- Fixed correlative pairs (neither…nor, no sooner…than) — spot the wrong partner.
- Fill-in-the-blank with although/because/until/unless — apply Rules 4–7 directly.
- "As well as" subject-verb agreement — check the first subject only.
- Error spotting where the error is a double negative or "although…but".

**⏱️ 45-Second Questions (Inference/Application — worth the time):**
- Sentence improvement where two options look grammatically possible — check meaning and parallel structure.
- Complex error spotting with an intervening phrase between the correlative pair and the verb.
- "That vs. whether vs. if" questions — decide if the sentence expresses doubt, condition, or fact.
- Rearrangement (PQRS) items where conjunctions are the linking clues — trace each connector before ordering.

**Strategy:** If a conjunction question has no fixed-pair signal after 20 seconds, mark it and move on — return in the second pass.

## (c) Morphology — Roots & Affixes Behind Conjunction Vocabulary

| Root / Affix | Meaning | Derived Words |
|---|---|---|
| **junct- / join-** (Latin *jungere*) | to join | conjunction, junction, juncture, adjoin, disjoin |
| **con- / co-** | together, with | connect, combine, cooperate, correlate |
| **dis-** | apart, not | disconnect, disjoin, contrast (via contra-) |
| **sub-** | under | subordinate, subordinating conjunction |
| **ordin-** (Latin *ordo*) | order, rank | coordinate, subordinate, ordinary |
| **relat-** (Latin *referre*) | carry back, refer | correlative, relative, relate |
| **-ive** (suffix) | having the nature of | connective, correlative, coordinative |
| **-tion / -ion** (suffix) | act or state of | conjunction, connection, coordination |

**Memory hook:** *Conjunction* = **con** (together) + **junct** (join) + **-ion** (the act) → "the act of joining together" — exactly what a conjunction does to words, phrases, and clauses.
