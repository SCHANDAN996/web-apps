# 📊 PYQ Analysis — Conjunction

## How Exams Test Conjunctions (Pattern Analysis)

Across SSC (CGL, CHSL, MTS, GD), RRB (NTPC, Group D), Banking (IBPS, SBI) and State exams, conjunctions appear in **four recurring formats**:

1. **Fill in the blank** — choose the correct connector (*although / because / unless / until*). This is the most common format.
2. **Spot the error** — a wrong or doubled conjunction hides in the sentence (*Although… but*, *Since… therefore*, *Scarcely… than*).
3. **Sentence improvement** — replace the underlined connector with a better one.
4. **Sentence rearrangement / cloze test** — connectors act as signposts to fix the logical order of sentences.

**Sub-topics that repeat most often:**
- Correlative pairs: *either…or, neither…nor, not only…but also, both…and, no sooner…than, hardly/scarcely…when*
- *Unless / until / provided that / in case* — condition connectors
- *Although / though / yet / however / nevertheless* — contrast connectors
- *Because / since / as / so that / in order that* — reason and purpose
- *That* after verbs of saying/thinking; *whether* vs *if* in indirect speech

**Examiner's favourite traps (in words, not numbers):**
- **Double conjunction trap:** *Although he is rich, but he is not happy.* — Hindi speakers love this because Hindi allows "यद्यपि… फिर भी". English allows only one.
- **Pair-swap trap:** *No sooner did he arrive when it rained.* — *No sooner* always pairs with *than*, never *when*.
- **Sound-right trap:** *Neither of the two boys have come.* — sounds fine, but *neither* takes a singular verb.
- **Over-thinking trap:** a perfectly correct *that* clause is "improved" into a wrong one by candidates who assume every underlined part must be wrong.
- **Negative-meaning trap:** *Unless you do not work hard, you will fail.* — *unless* already means "if not"; adding *not* reverses the meaning.

## Top 10 High-Yield Questions (PYQ-Style) with Solutions

**Q1.** Although he worked hard, ______ he could not pass the exam.
(a) but (b) yet (c) still (d) No word needed
**Answer:** (d)
**Solution:** *Although* and *but/yet* cannot sit in the same sentence. One contrast connector is enough.
**Trap type:** Sound-Right (Hindi "यद्यपि… फिर भी" influence)
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q2.** No sooner did the bell ring ______ the students rushed out.
(a) when (b) then (c) than (d) that
**Answer:** (c)
**Solution:** Fixed pair: *No sooner… than*. (*Hardly/Scarcely* pair with *when*.)
**Trap type:** Pair-swap
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q3.** ______ you work hard, you will not succeed.
(a) If (b) Unless (c) Until (d) Till
**Answer:** (b)
**Solution:** *Unless* = "if… not". The sentence needs a negative condition.
**Trap type:** Meaning confusion (unless vs until)
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q4.** He is not only a good singer ______ a fine dancer.
(a) and also (b) but also (c) as well as (d) also
**Answer:** (b)
**Solution:** Correlative pair: *not only… but also*. Both halves must be grammatically parallel.
**Trap type:** Pair-swap
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q5.** Wait here ______ I come back.
(a) unless (b) until (c) since (d) because
**Answer:** (b)
**Solution:** *Until* marks time (up to the point when); *unless* marks condition. Time is meant here.
**Trap type:** Unless/until confusion
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q6.** Scarcely had she entered the room ______ the phone rang.
(a) than (b) when (c) then (d) that
**Answer:** (b)
**Solution:** *Scarcely/Hardly… when*; *No sooner… than*. Do not mix the two pairs.
**Trap type:** Pair-swap
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q7.** ______ it was raining heavily, the match continued.
(a) Because (b) Since (c) Though (d) So
**Answer:** (c)
**Solution:** The two clauses show contrast, not reason — so a concessive connector (*Though*) is needed.
**Trap type:** Reason vs contrast confusion
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q8.** He asked me ______ I had finished the work.
(a) that (b) if (c) because (d) so that
**Answer:** (b)
**Solution:** An indirect yes/no question takes *if/whether*, not *that*.
**Trap type:** That/if confusion in reported speech
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q9.** Neither Ravi nor his friends ______ present at the meeting.
(a) was (b) were (c) is (d) has been
**Answer:** (b)
**Solution:** With *neither…nor*, the verb agrees with the **nearer** subject (*friends* → plural).
**Trap type:** Over-thinking / proximity agreement
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q10.** He spoke softly ______ nobody could hear him.
(a) so that (b) in order that (c) such that (d) because
**Answer:** (a)
**Solution:** Result/purpose with a negative outcome is expressed by *so that*. *Such that* needs "such + noun" earlier in the sentence.
**Trap type:** Sound-Right
**Source:** PYQ-style

## Applying the 15/45-Second Rule

**15-second question (Fact-based):** Q2 above. The moment you see *No sooner*, your hand should move to *than* — it is a memorised fixed pair. Do not read all options twice. **Target: under 15 seconds.**

**45-second question (Inference-based):** Q7 above. Here you must read **both clauses**, decide the logical relationship (contrast, not reason), and only then pick the connector. Meaning-based blanks deserve the full 45 seconds — rushing them is how *Because* gets wrongly chosen.

**Rule of thumb:** Fixed pairs and single-word rules → 15 sec. Blanks where the logic of the whole sentence decides the connector → 45 sec.

---

## One-Line Revision of the Pattern

Examiners rarely invent new conjunction questions — they recycle the same five traps: doubled conjunctions, swapped pairs, unless/until confusion, that/if confusion, and proximity agreement after *neither…nor*. Master these five, and most conjunction questions in any exam become 15-second gifts.
