Card 1
Front: What is a conjunction?
Back: A word that joins words, phrases, or clauses — e.g., and, but, because, although.

Card 2
Front: Name the three types of conjunctions.
Back: Coordinating, Subordinating, and Correlative conjunctions.

Card 3
Front: What does the mnemonic FANBOYS stand for?
Back: For, And, Nor, But, Or, Yet, So — the seven coordinating conjunctions.

Card 4
Front: Which pairs are correlative conjunctions?
Back: Either…or, Neither…nor, Not only…but also, Both…and, Whether…or, No sooner…than, Hardly/Scarcely…when.

Card 5
Front: Mnemonic: How to remember that "no sooner" pairs with "than"?
Back: "No sooner THAN, Hardly WHEN" — sooner rhymes with nothing here, so remember: sooner→than, hardly/scarcely→when.

Card 6
Front: Examiner's Trap: "No sooner had he entered the room when the phone rang." — Correct or wrong?
Back: Wrong. "No sooner" takes "than": No sooner had he entered the room than the phone rang.

Card 7
Front: What is the rule for subject-verb agreement with "either…or" / "neither…nor"?
Back: The verb agrees with the subject nearer to it: "Neither the teacher nor the students were present."

Card 8
Front: Examiner's Trap: "Although he was tired, but he kept working." — Correct or wrong?
Back: Wrong. "Although" and "but" cannot be used together. Use either "Although he was tired, he kept working" or "He was tired, but he kept working."

Card 9
Front: What is the difference between "because" and "because of"?
Back: "Because" is followed by a clause (subject + verb); "because of" is followed by a noun/noun phrase: "He left because he was ill" / "He left because of illness."

Card 10
Front: When do we use "since" vs "for" (as conjunctions of time/reason)?
Back: "Since" = from a point of time / because; "For" = a period of time / because (formal). As conjunctions of reason, "since" often means "because": "Since it was raining, we stayed home."

Card 11
Front: What is the correct pair: "Scarcely had she arrived ___ it started raining"?
Back: "when" — Scarcely/Hardly…when. (Trap: students wrongly write "than".)

Card 12
Front: Mnemonic: How to remember subordinating conjunctions?
Back: "A WHITE BUS": Although/After, When/While/Where, If, Though/That, How, Even though/Before, Because, Until/Unless, Since/So that.

Card 13
Front: "Unless" vs "Until" — what is the difference?
Back: "Unless" = if…not (condition): "Unless you work hard, you will fail." "Until" = up to the time: "Wait until I come."

Card 14
Front: Examiner's Trap: "He is senior than me." — Correct or wrong?
Back: Wrong. "Senior" takes "to", not "than": "He is senior to me." (Same for junior, superior, inferior, prefer.)

Card 15
Front: What happens to the verb after "not only…but also"?
Back: The verb agrees with the subject after "but also": "Not only the students but also the teacher was present."

Card 16
Front: Can a sentence begin with "Because" or "Although"?
Back: Yes — if it is a dependent clause followed by a main clause: "Because it rained, the match was cancelled." But a dependent clause alone is a fragment.

Card 17
Front: What is the difference between "though" and "although"?
Back: Both mean the same; "although" is slightly more formal and usually begins a clause, while "though" can also come at the end: "He came, though."

Card 18
Front: "Lest" is followed by which helping verb?
Back: "Should": "Work hard lest you should fail." (Trap: never use "lest…should not" — lest already carries a negative sense.)

Card 19
Front: Which conjunction is used after "such" to show result?
Back: "that": "It was such a hot day that we stayed indoors." (Compare: "so hot that…")

Card 20
Front: What is wrong with: "Being a holiday, the office was closed"?
Back: This is a dangling modifier, not a conjunction error — but in conjunction questions, watch for: "As it was a holiday, the office was closed" — the conjunction "as" correctly links the reason clause.
