126. Choose the correct sentence:
(a) Neither intelligent he is nor hardworking. (b) He is neither intelligent nor hardworking. (c) He neither is intelligent nor hardworking. (d) Neither he is intelligent nor hardworking.
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Neither…nor" must be placed immediately before the parallel words being joined. In the other options "neither" sits before the verb or subject, breaking parallelism. The correlative pair here joins the adjectives "intelligent" and "hardworking", so the answer is "He is neither intelligent nor hardworking."
Source: NCERT Class 9 English Grammar

127. Hardly had he reached the station ______ the train left.
(a) than (b) when (c) that (d) then
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Than" tempts students because of "no sooner…than", but "hardly" and "scarcely" always pair with "when", never "than". The correct correlative is "hardly…when", so the answer is "when".

128. He is ______ honest ______ hardworking.
(a) either / or (b) both / as well (c) not only / but also (d) neither / nor
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Both…as well" is wrong because "both" pairs with "and", not "as well". The sentence praises two qualities together, and the standard correlative for this is "not only…but also", so the answer is "not only / but also".
Source: NCERT Class 10 English Grammar

129. Choose the correctly punctuated and conjoined sentence:
(a) He was tired, so he went to bed early. (b) He was tired so, he went to bed early. (c) He was tired, so, he went to bed early. (d) He was tired so he went, to bed early.
Answer: (a)
Solution: A comma comes before the coordinating conjunction "so" joining two independent clauses, never after it. Only one option places the comma correctly, so the answer is "He was tired, so he went to bed early."

130. ______ he is rich, he is not happy.
(a) Although (b) Because (c) Since (d) Unless
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Because" and "since" show cause, but the sentence shows contrast between wealth and unhappiness. The contrast conjunction needed is "although", so the answer is "Although".

131. She worked hard ______ she might pass the examination.
(a) so that (b) lest (c) although (d) because
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Lest" is tempting but it expresses a negative purpose ("for fear that") and takes "should", which does not fit "might pass". Purpose with "might" is expressed by "so that", so the answer is "so that".

132. Spot the error: Part P: No sooner did the bell ring / Part Q: when the students / Part R: rushed out of the class. / Part S: No error
(a) Part R (b) Part P (c) Part Q (d) No error
Answer: (c)
Solution: "When" sounds natural after "no sooner" to many ears, but the fixed correlative pair is "no sooner…than". Part Q must read "than the students", so the error is in Part Q.

133. You must apologize ______ you will be punished.
(a) and (b) so (c) or (d) but
Answer: (c)
Solution: "And" and "so" suggest addition or result, but the sentence gives a warning with two alternatives: apologize or face punishment. The conjunction of alternative warning is "or", so the answer is "or".

134. Choose the correct sentence:
(a) Neither of the two boys has done their homework. (b) Neither of the two boys have done his homework. (c) Neither of the two boys has done his homework. (d) Neither of the two boys have done their homework.
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Neither of" takes a singular verb, so "have" is wrong; and exam grammar requires the singular pronoun "his" to agree with "neither", not "their". The fully consistent sentence is "Neither of the two boys has done his homework", so the answer is that option.

135. Choose the correct sentence:
(a) Unless you do not work hard, you will fail. (b) Until you work hard, you will fail. (c) Unless and until you work hard, you will fail. (d) Unless you work hard, you will fail.
Answer: (d)
Solution: "Unless" itself is negative, so adding "not" makes a double negative; "until" refers to time, not condition; "unless and until" together is redundant. The clean conditional is "Unless you work hard, you will fail", so the answer is that option.
Source: NCERT Class 10 English Grammar

136. Spot the error: Part P: Scarcely he had stepped out of the house / Part Q: when it began / Part R: to rain heavily. / Part S: No error
(a) Part Q (b) Part R (c) Part P (d) No error
Answer: (c)
Solution: Part Q's "when" is correct because "scarcely" pairs with "when", so students who hunt for a "than" error are misled. The real error is in Part P: when a negative adverb like "scarcely" begins a clause, inversion is required — "Scarcely had he stepped out". Part P lacks inversion, so the error is in Part P.

137. ______ he had finished his work, he went out to play.
(a) Since (b) While (c) Until (d) After
Answer: (d)
Solution: "While" and "until" do not fit a completed earlier action; the past perfect "had finished" signals that one action was completed before the other, which "after" expresses precisely. The best answer is "After".
Source: NCERT Class 9 English Grammar

138. Choose the correct sentence:
(a) He not only lost his wallet but also his keys. (b) Not only he lost his wallet but also his keys. (c) He lost his wallet not only but also his keys. (d) He lost not only his wallet but also his keys.
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is placing "not only" before the verb, which breaks parallelism — the pair must join the two objects "his wallet" and "his keys". Only one option keeps the correlatives before parallel items, so the answer is "He lost not only his wallet but also his keys."

139. Spot the error: Part P: Both Ramesh / Part Q: as well as Suresh / Part R: were present at the meeting. / Part S: No error
(a) Part P (b) No error (c) Part R (d) Part Q
Answer: (d)
Solution: "Both" pairs only with "and", never with "as well as" — this is a classic sound-right trap because "both…as well as" is common in speech. Part Q should read "and Suresh", so the error is in Part Q.

140. He walked carefully ______ he should fall.
(a) lest (b) although (c) because (d) so that
Answer: (a)
Solution: "So that" expresses positive purpose, but falling is something to be avoided. "Lest" means "for fear that" and correctly takes "should", so the answer is "lest".

141. Choose the correct sentence:
(a) He is more cleverer than his brother. (b) He is cleverer than his brother. (c) He is most cleverer than his brother. (d) He is most clever than his brother.
Answer: (b)
Solution: "More cleverer" is a double comparative — a tempting error because "more" feels emphatic. "Cleverer" alone is the comparative, so the answer is "He is cleverer than his brother."

142. Spot the error: Part P: He denied / Part Q: that he had not / Part R: stolen the money. / Part S: No error
(a) Part P (b) Part Q (c) Part R (d) No error
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Denied" already carries a negative meaning, so "had not stolen" creates a double negative that reverses the sense — a trap because it sounds natural in Hindi-influenced speech. Part Q should read "that he had", so the error is in Part Q.

143. ______ being rich, he is humble.
(a) Although (b) Despite (c) Since (d) Because
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Although" is tempting but it must be followed by a clause with a finite verb ("Although he is rich"), not a gerund phrase. Before "being rich" the correct word is "despite", so the answer is "Despite".

144. Spot the error: Part P: The reason why he failed / Part Q: is because / Part R: he did not study. / Part S: No error
(a) Part Q (b) No error (c) Part R (d) Part P
Answer: (a)
Solution: "The reason…is because" is redundant — a very common sound-right trap; the correct form is "the reason…is that". Part Q should read "is that", so the error is in Part Q.

145. He will not pass ______ he works very hard.
(a) if (b) until (c) provided (d) unless
Answer: (d)
Solution: "If" alone would need "not" ("if he does not work hard"); "until" refers to time. "Unless" means "if…not" and fits the negative main clause perfectly, so the answer is "unless".

146. Choose the correct sentence:

Answer: (b)
Solution: "Supposing" already means "if", so combining them is redundant — a frequent examiner trap. The clean form drops "if", so the answer is "Supposing he comes, what will you do?"

147. Spot the error: Part P: He not only / Part Q: plays cricket / Part R: but also football. / Part S: No error
(a) Part Q (b) Part R (c) No error (d) Part P
Answer: (d)
Solution: The correlatives must precede parallel elements: here "but also" introduces the noun "football", so "not only" must also stand before a parallel noun — "He plays not only cricket but also football". The misplacement begins in Part P, so the error is in Part P.

148. ______ the rain stopped, the match resumed.
(a) So soon as (b) As soon that (c) As soon as (d) No sooner
Answer: (c)
Solution: "No sooner" requires inversion and "than" ("No sooner had the rain stopped than…"), which this sentence lacks; "so soon as" is used only in negatives. The correct time conjunction is "as soon as", so the answer is "As soon as".
Source: NCERT Class 10 English Grammar

149. Choose the correct sentence:
(a) He came late because he missed the bus. (b) He came late because of he missed the bus. (c) He came late due to he missed the bus. (d) He came late because of missing the bus reason.
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Because of" and "due to" are prepositions and must be followed by a noun or noun phrase, not a full clause with a finite verb — a classic trap. A clause needs the conjunction "because", so the answer is "He came late because he missed the bus."
Source: PYQ-style

150. Choose the correct sentence:
(a) No sooner had she entered the room than the phone rang. (b) No sooner had she entered the room when the phone rang. (c) No sooner she had entered the room than the phone rang. (d) No sooner she entered the room than the phone rang.
Answer: (a)
Solution: "No sooner" at the start of a clause demands inversion ("had she entered") and pairs with "than", not "when" — both traps appear in the wrong options. The only sentence with both inversion and "than" is "No sooner had she entered the room than the phone rang", so the answer is that option.
Source: PYQ-style
