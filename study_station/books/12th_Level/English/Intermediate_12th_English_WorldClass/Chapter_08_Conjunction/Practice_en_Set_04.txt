76. Choose the correct conjunction: "He worked hard ______ he could pass the examination."
(a) although (b) because (c) so that (d) unless
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Because" is tempting since hard work and passing seem like cause and effect, but the clause "he could pass" expresses purpose, not reason. Purpose is shown by "so that", usually with can, could, may or might, so the answer is "so that".
Source: NCERT Class 9 English Grammar

77. Choose the correctly framed sentence:
(a) Scarcely he had reached the station when the train left. (b) Scarcely had he reached the station when the train left. (c) Scarcely he had reached the station than the train left. (d) Scarcely had he reached the station than the train left.
Answer: (b)
Solution: The option with "than" is the trap: "scarcely" pairs with "when", never with "than" — "than" belongs with "no sooner". Also, a negative adverb at the start demands inversion, "had he", not "he had". Only one sentence has both inversion and "when", so the answer is "Scarcely had he reached the station when the train left."
Source: PYQ-style

78. Spot the error: Part P: No sooner did the bell ring / Part Q: when the students / Part R: rushed out of the classroom. / Part S: No error
(a) Part Q (b) Part P (c) Part R (d) No error
Answer: (a)
Solution: Part P looks suspicious because of the inversion, but "No sooner did the bell ring" is perfectly correct. The real error hides in Part Q: "no sooner" must be followed by "than", not "when", so the error is in Part Q.

79. Choose the correct conjunction: "______ he is rich, he is not happy."
(a) Because (b) Though (c) Unless (d) Since
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Because" and "since" are tempting because they introduce reasons, but wealth is not a reason for unhappiness here — the sentence contrasts two ideas. Contrast needs a concessive conjunction, and "unless" sets a condition, which does not fit, so the answer is "Though".
Source: NCERT Class 8 English Grammar

80. Choose the correct conjunction: "You will fail ______ you work hard."
(a) provided that (b) unless (c) until (d) if
Answer: (b)
Solution: "If" is the trap — "You will fail if you work hard" reverses the intended meaning. The sentence means "if you do not work hard, you will fail", and "unless" means exactly "if...not", so the answer is "unless".

81. Spot the error: Part P: Neither the teacher / Part Q: nor the students / Part R: was present in the hall. / Part S: No error
(a) Part Q (b) Part R (c) Part P (d) No error
Answer: (b)
Solution: Part P tempts students who think "neither" is wrong, but the pair "neither...nor" is correct. With "neither...nor", the verb agrees with the nearer subject — here "students", which is plural — so it should be "were present", not "was present", and the error is in Part R.

82. Choose the correctly framed sentence:
(a) He is both intelligent as well as hardworking. (b) He is both intelligent but hardworking. (c) He is both intelligent or hardworking. (d) He is both intelligent and hardworking.
Answer: (d)
Solution: The option with "as well as" is the classic trap: "both...as well as" sounds grand but is wrong — "both" pairs only with "and". "Or" and "but" break the correlative pair, so the answer is "He is both intelligent and hardworking."
Source: NCERT Class 9 English Grammar

83. Choose the correct conjunction: "Walk fast ______ you will miss the bus."
(a) and (b) so (c) but (d) or
Answer: (d)
Solution: "And" is tempting because two clauses seem to join with "and", but the second clause warns of a consequence of not obeying the first. In "Do X or Y will happen" structures, "or" means "otherwise", so the answer is "or".

84. Spot the error: Part P: Although he was tired, / Part Q: but he continued / Part R: working till midnight. / Part S: No error
(a) Part R (b) Part P (c) Part Q (d) No error
Answer: (c)
Solution: Part P looks fine and it is — "although" is correctly used. The trap is that Hindi allows "yadyapi...phir bhi", so "although...but" feels natural, but English forbids two conjunctions for one join. Delete "but", so the error is in Part Q.

85. Choose the correct conjunction: "She is ______ intelligent ______ hardworking."
(a) neither...or (b) either...and (c) not only...but also (d) both...or
Answer: (c)
Solution: The other options are broken correlative pairs: "neither" takes "nor", "either" takes "or", and "both" takes "and". Only "not only...but also" is a valid pair and fits the praising tone, so the answer is "not only...but also".

86. Choose the correctly framed sentence:
(a) Hardly he had slept when the phone rang. (b) Hardly had he slept then the phone rang. (c) Hardly had he slept than the phone rang. (d) Hardly had he slept when the phone rang.
Answer: (d)
Solution: The option with "than" is the trap — "than" belongs with "no sooner", not with "hardly". The option without inversion fails because a sentence starting with "hardly" needs "had he", and "then" is not a conjunction here. Correct form is inversion plus "when", so the answer is "Hardly had he slept when the phone rang."

87. Choose the correct conjunction: "He was punished ______ he was guilty."
(a) unless (b) because (c) although (d) lest
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Although" is tempting because punishment sentences often show contrast, but here guilt is the reason for punishment — a straight cause-effect link. "Unless" sets a condition and "lest" expresses fear, both illogical here, so the answer is "because".
Source: NCERT Class 8 English Grammar

88. Choose the correctly framed sentence:
(a) He plays not only cricket but also football. (b) He not only plays cricket but also football. (c) He plays cricket not only but also football. (d) Not only he plays cricket but also football.
Answer: (a)
Solution: The first option is the trap: "not only" before "plays" promises two verbs, but the sentence offers two games, so the correlative pair is misplaced. "Not only...but also" must stand immediately before the parallel items — here "cricket" and "football" — so the answer is "He plays not only cricket but also football."
Source: PYQ-style

89. Choose the correct conjunction: "Take an umbrella ______ it should rain."
(a) lest (b) unless (c) until (d) since
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Unless" is tempting because the sentence is precautionary, but "unless" means "if not" and would reverse the sense. Precaution against a feared event uses "lest", followed by "should" or the bare verb, so the answer is "lest".
Source: PYQ-style

90. Choose the correctly framed sentence:
(a) Either you or your brother has broken the vase. (b) Either you nor your brother has broken the vase. (c) Either you or your brother have broken the vase. (d) Either you and your brother have broken the vase.
Answer: (a)
Solution: The option with "have" is the trap: with "either...or" the verb agrees with the nearer subject — "your brother", which is singular — so "has", not "have". The remaining options break the pair, since "either" takes "or", so the answer is "Either you or your brother has broken the vase."

91. Choose the correct conjunction: "______ it was raining heavily, the match continued."
(a) Unless (b) Because (c) If (d) Though
Answer: (d)
Solution: "Because" is the trap — rain is not the reason the match continued; the sentence contrasts expectation with reality. Concession, something surprising, needs "though" or "although", so the answer is "Though".
Source: NCERT Class 9 English Grammar

92. Spot the error: Part P: Since he has no money, / Part Q: therefore he cannot / Part R: buy the book. / Part S: No error
(a) Part Q (b) Part P (c) Part R (d) No error
Answer: (a)
Solution: Part P is correct — "since" rightly shows reason. The trap is Hindi's "kyunki...isliye" habit: English never uses "since" or "because" together with "therefore" or "so" in the same sentence. Drop "therefore", so the error is in Part Q.

93. Choose the correct conjunction: "He talks ______ he were a judge."
(a) as if (b) even if (c) so that (d) in case
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Even if" is tempting because both phrases start similarly, but the sentence describes an unreal comparison — he is not a judge. Unreal supposition uses "as if" or "as though", with "were" for all persons, so the answer is "as if".

94. Choose the correctly framed sentence:
(a) Neither did he come nor did he call. (b) Neither he came nor he called. (c) Neither he came or he called. (d) Neither did he come or did he call.
Answer: (a)
Solution: The option "Neither he came nor he called" is the trap: when "neither" begins a clause, inversion is required — "neither did he come". The options with "or" break the pair, since "neither" takes "nor", so the answer is "Neither did he come nor did he call."

95. Choose the correct conjunction: "I will wait here ______ you return."
(a) because (b) since (c) until (d) unless
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Unless" is tempting because the sentence is about the future, but "unless" means "if not" and would make the sentence illogical. Waiting up to a point of time needs "until" or "till", so the answer is "until".

96. Spot the error: Part P: The reason why he failed / Part Q: is because he did not / Part R: study regularly. / Part S: No error
(a) Part P (b) Part Q (c) Part R (d) No error
Answer: (b)
Solution: Part P sounds heavy but is acceptable. The real error is the doubled connector: "the reason...is because" is redundant — standard English says "the reason...is that". Replace "because" with "that", so the error is in Part Q.

97. Choose the correct conjunction: "______ you work hard, you cannot succeed."
(a) If (b) Provided that (c) Unless (d) As long as
Answer: (c)
Solution: "If" is the trap — "If you work hard, you cannot succeed" states the opposite of the intended meaning. The sentence needs a negative condition, "if you do not work hard", which is exactly what "unless" means, so the answer is "Unless".

98. Choose the correctly framed sentence:
(a) He is senior than me by two years. (b) He is more senior than me by two years. (c) He is senior from me by two years. (d) He is senior to me by two years.
Answer: (d)
Solution: The option with "than" is the trap: "than" feels natural after a comparative idea, but Latin comparatives — senior, junior, superior, inferior, prefer — take "to", not "than". The option with "more" wrongly doubles the comparative, so the answer is "He is senior to me by two years."
Source: PYQ-style

99. Choose the correct conjunction: "He is ______ weak ______ he cannot walk a mile."
(a) such...that (b) very...that (c) too...to (d) so...that
Answer: (d)
Solution: "Too...to" is tempting, but it is followed by a verb — "too weak to walk" — not a full clause with "he cannot". Before an adjective plus a result clause, the pattern is "so + adjective + that", so the answer is "so...that".
Source: NCERT Class 10 English Grammar

(a) Part P (b) Part R (c) Part Q (d) No error
Answer: (c)
Solution: Part P is the decoy — the inversion "had she finished" is exactly right after "no sooner". The genuine error is the connector: "no sooner" pairs only with "than", never "when" — "when" belongs with "scarcely" and "hardly", so the error is in Part Q.
