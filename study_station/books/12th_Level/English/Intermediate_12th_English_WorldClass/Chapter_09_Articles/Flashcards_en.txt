Card 1
Front: When do we use "a" and when "an"?
Back: "A" before a consonant SOUND, "an" before a vowel SOUND — sound matters, not the letter. A university, an hour, an M.P., a one-rupee note.

Card 2
Front: Why "an hour" but "a hotel"?
Back: "Hour" has a silent 'h', so it begins with a vowel sound → an hour. "Hotel" has a pronounced 'h' → a hotel.

Card 3
Front: 🧠 Mnemonic: Which things always take "the"? (Think: SUN, RIVERS, OCEANS)
Back: "The" is used with unique things and famous natural features: the sun, the moon, the Ganga, the Himalayas, the Indian Ocean, the equator.

Card 4
Front: ⚠️ Examiner's Trap: "He is ___ honest man." — a or an?
Back: "An" — because 'h' in honest is silent; the word starts with a vowel sound. Examiners test sound vs. letter confusion.

Card 5
Front: When is NO article used before a noun?
Back: Before plural/uncountable nouns used in a general sense: "Dogs are faithful", "Water is essential". Also before proper nouns, meals, languages, and most games: "I play cricket", "She speaks Hindi".

Card 6
Front: "The + adjective" — what does it mean? Example?
Back: It denotes a whole class of people: the rich = rich people, the poor, the blind, the unemployed. The verb that follows is plural: "The rich are not always happy."

Card 7
Front: Which article comes before superlatives and ordinals?
Back: "The": the best student, the first boy, the most honest officer. (But "a" before comparative in selection: "He is a better player of the two" is wrong — use "the better of the two".)

Card 8
Front: ⚠️ Examiner's Trap: "___ Ganga is a holy river." — which article?
Back: "The" — names of rivers, seas, oceans, mountain ranges, and holy books always take "the": the Ganga, the Himalayas, the Ramayana.

Card 9
Front: Do countries take "the"? Give examples.
Back: Most do not: India, Japan, France. But "the" is used with republics, unions, and plural names: the USA, the UK, the UAE, the Netherlands, the Republic of Ireland.

Card 10
Front: 🧠 Mnemonic: Remember zero-article places with "SCHOOL-BED-CHURCH-PRISON-HOSPITAL"
Back: When these places are used for their PRIMARY purpose, no article: go to school (to study), go to bed (to sleep), go to church (to pray), go to prison (as a prisoner). Use "the" for any other purpose: "I went to the school to meet the teacher."

Card 11
Front: "A" vs "the" — first mention vs second mention rule?
Back: First mention (indefinite, any one) → a/an: "I saw a dog." Second mention (now specific) → the: "The dog was black."

Card 12
Front: Which article before uncountable nouns like water, gold, honesty?
Back: Normally none: "Gold is costly." But "the" when made specific: "The gold of this ring is pure."

Card 13
Front: "Few" vs "a few" vs "the few" — meaning difference?
Back: Few = almost none (negative sense); A few = some (positive sense); The few = not many, but all of them: "The few friends he had stood by him."

Card 14
Front: "Little" vs "a little" vs "the little"?
Back: Little = almost nothing; A little = some (positive); The little = not much, but all of it: "He spent the little money he had."

Card 15
Front: Which article before names of newspapers, ships, and famous buildings?
Back: "The": the Times of India, the Titanic, the Taj Mahal, the Red Fort.

Card 16
Front: ⚠️ Examiner's Trap: "He goes to ___ school by bus." vs "The principal entered ___ school."
Back: First: no article (going to study — primary purpose). Second: "the" (a specific building being entered). Purpose decides the article.

Card 17
Front: Article with musical instruments and inventions?
Back: "The": She plays the flute; the telephone was invented by Graham Bell.

Card 18
Front: When does a proper noun take "the"?
Back: When used as a common noun for comparison: "Kalidas is the Shakespeare of India." Also with plural family names: the Sharmas.

Card 19
Front: Article before "same" and "only"?
Back: Always "the": the same book, the only solution.

Card 20
Front: "What kind of ___ man is he?" — is an article needed?
Back: Yes — "a": "What kind of a man is he?" Similarly: "many a student", "such a boy" — singular countable nouns after these expressions take "a/an".
