# 📊 PYQ Analysis — Articles

## How Exams Test Articles (Pattern Analysis)

Articles look small, but examiners (SSC CGL/CHSL, RRB NTPC, IBPS, State exams) use them to test *precision*. Across recent years, questions on articles appear in three formats:

1. **Fill in the blank** — "He is ___ honest man." (a/an/the/zero article)
2. **Spot the error** — one part of the sentence has a wrong/missing/extra article.
3. **Sentence improvement** — replace the underlined article phrase.

### Sub-topics that recur most often

- **a vs. an by sound, not spelling** — *an hour, a university, an MBA, a one-rupee note*. This is the single most repeated trap.
- **The with superlatives, ordinals, and unique things** — *the best, the first, the sun, the equator*.
- **Zero article** — before uncountable/abstract nouns used generally (*Honesty is the best policy*), before meals, games, languages, and proper nouns.
- **The with rivers, oceans, mountain ranges, holy books, newspapers** — *the Ganga, the Himalayas, the Ramayana, the Hindu*.
- **The before adjectives used as nouns (a class of people)** — *the poor, the rich, the blind*.
- **Fixed phrases** — *in a hurry, at a loss, a number of, the number of*.

### Examiner's favourite traps (in words, not numbers)

- **Sound-Right trap**: options like "an university" *feel* wrong to the ear of a Hindi speaker but "an hour" feels wrong too — both are decided by *pronunciation*, not the first letter.
- **Intervening-phrase trap**: a long phrase between the article and the noun hides the real noun — "She is ___ European artist of great repute." (Answer: *a*, because "European" begins with a /y/ sound.)
- **Over-thinking trap**: a simple zero-article case ("___ gold is expensive") dressed up so students insert "the" unnecessarily.
- **Near-identical pair trap**: "a number of students *were*" vs. "the number of students *was*" — article choice changes the verb.

## Top 10 High-Yield PYQ-Style Questions (with solutions)

**Q1.** He is ___ honest officer whom everyone respects.
(a) a (b) an (c) the (d) no article
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** "Honest" begins with a vowel *sound* (/ɒnɪst/ — h is silent), so use **an**.
**Trap type:** Sound-Right. *Source: PYQ-style (SSC pattern)*

**Q2.** ___ Ganga is one of the holiest rivers of India.
(a) A (b) An (c) The (d) No article
**Answer: (c)**
**Solution:** Names of rivers always take **the** — the Ganga, the Yamuna, the Thames.
**Trap type:** Over-thinking (students hesitate because proper nouns usually take no article; rivers are the exception). *Source: NCERT Class 7 English / PYQ-style*

**Q3.** She wants to become ___ university professor.
(a) a (b) an (c) the (d) no article
**Answer: (a)**
**Solution:** "University" starts with a /juː/ (consonant "y") sound, so **a**, not an.
**Trap type:** Sound-Right. *Source: PYQ-style*

**Q4.** ___ rich should help the poor.
(a) A (b) An (c) The (d) No article
**Answer: (c)**
**Solution:** "The + adjective" denotes a whole class of people: *the rich, the poor, the elderly*.
**Trap type:** Over-thinking. *Source: PYQ-style (Bank exam pattern)*

**Q5.** I had ___ European friend in college who knew five languages.
(a) a (b) an (c) the (d) no article
**Answer: (a)**
**Solution:** "European" is pronounced /jʊərə…/ — initial "y" sound → **a**. The intervening adjective "European" is what the article actually attaches to.
**Trap type:** Intervening Phrase + Sound-Right. *Source: PYQ-style*

**Q6.** ___ honesty is the best policy.
(a) A (b) An (c) The (d) No article
**Answer: (d)**
**Solution:** Abstract nouns used in a general sense take **no article**. (Compare: "*The* honesty of this clerk is remarkable" — specific, so "the".)
**Trap type:** Over-thinking. *Source: NCERT Class 9 English / PYQ-style*

**Q7.** He goes to ___ school by bus every day. (as a student)
(a) a (b) an (c) the (d) no article
**Answer: (d)**
**Solution:** Institutions used for their primary purpose (school, college, hospital, prison, church) take **no article**: *go to school, go to hospital*. But "I went to *the* school to meet the principal" — specific building → the.
**Trap type:** Sound-Right (students insert "the" because it "sounds complete"). *Source: PYQ-style*

**Q8.** This is ___ best book I have ever read.
(a) a (b) an (c) the (d) no article
**Answer: (c)**
**Solution:** Superlative adjectives always take **the**: the best, the tallest, the most beautiful.
**Trap type:** Over-thinking. *Source: PYQ-style*

**Q9.** ___ number of students absent today is surprising.
(a) A (b) An (c) The (d) No article
**Answer: (c)**
**Solution:** "**The** number of" = the figure itself (singular verb: *is*). "**A** number of" = many (plural verb: *are*). Here the verb is "is", so **the**.
**Trap type:** Near-identical pair. *Source: PYQ-style (SSC CGL pattern)*

**Q10.** Mount Everest is ___ highest peak in the world.
(a) a (b) an (c) the (d) no article
**Answer: (c)**
**Solution:** Superlative "highest" requires **the**. Note: "Mount Everest" itself takes no article (single peaks don't), but mountain *ranges* do: *the Himalayas*.
**Trap type:** Intervening Phrase (the proper noun distracts from the superlative rule). *Source: NCERT Class 8 Geography / PYQ-style*

## Applying the 15/45-Second Rule

**15-second (Fact-based) — Q8:** "___ best book" → superlative → **the**. No context needed; a memorised rule fires instantly. Mark and move in under 15 seconds.

**45-second (Inference-based) — Q9:** You must (1) read the whole sentence, (2) notice the verb "is" (singular), (3) recall that "the number of" is singular while "a number of" is plural, (4) match and answer. Budget up to 45 seconds — this question *rewards* careful reading, and rushing it is exactly what the examiner wants.

**Strategy takeaway:** In the exam, solve all a/an-by-sound and the-with-superlative questions first (15-sec facts), then return to zero-article and "a number of / the number of" items (45-sec inference).
