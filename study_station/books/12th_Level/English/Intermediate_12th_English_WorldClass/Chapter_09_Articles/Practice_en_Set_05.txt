101. Choose the correct sentence:
(a) He is an university student. (b) He is the university student. (c) He is a university student. (d) He is university student.
Answer: (c)
Solution: The tempting choice "an university" sounds right because the word starts with a vowel letter, but "university" begins with a consonant sound /juː/, so it takes "a", not "an". The rule is sound-based, not letter-based, so the answer is "He is a university student."
Source: NCERT Class 9 English Grammar

102. Fill in the blank: "___ honesty is the best policy."
(a) An (b) The (c) A (d) No article
Answer: (d)
Solution: Abstract nouns like honesty, truth, and kindness used in a general sense take no article. Learners often add "the" by analogy with "the truth" in specific contexts, but here honesty is general, so the answer is "No article."

103. Fill in the blank: "She plays ___ guitar very well."
(a) the (b) an (c) a (d) no article
Answer: (a)
Solution: Musical instruments take "the" when we talk about playing them. The trap is "no article", which is correct for games like cricket or chess, but instruments follow a different rule, so the answer is "the".

104. Spot the error: "The gold (P) / is a precious metal (Q) / found in many countries. (R) / No error (S)"
(a) Part P (b) Part Q (c) Part R (d) No error
Answer: (a)
Solution: Material nouns like gold, water, and iron used in a general sense take no article, so Part P is wrong; it should be just "Gold". The tempting thought that "the gold" is fine because gold is specific is the trap — generality removes the article, so the error is in Part P.

Answer: (c)
Solution: Names of rivers, seas, oceans, and mountain ranges always take "the". Some learners drop the article thinking proper nouns need none, but geographical names of this type are an exception, so the answer is "The".

110. Choose the correct sentence:
(a) Mount Everest is the highest peak in the world. (b) The Mount Everest is the highest peak in the world. (c) Mount Everest is highest peak in the world. (d) A Mount Everest is the highest peak in the world.
Answer: (a)
Solution: Individual mountain peaks take no article, but superlatives take "the" — hence "Mount Everest" without "the" and "the highest peak" with it. 115. Fill in the blank: "What ___ beautiful weather we are having!"

Answer: (a)
Solution: "Weather" is an uncountable noun and cannot take "a" or "an" even in exclamations. The trap "a beautiful weather" copies the pattern of "What a beautiful day!", but day is countable and weather is not, so the answer is "no article".

Solution: In the double comparative structure "the more … the more …", "the" is an adverb, not an article, and the sentence is fully correct. The trap is to suspect Part P because two "the"s look unusual, but the structure is standard, so the answer is "No error".

121. Fill in the blank: "___ iron is a useful metal, but ___ iron of this region is impure."
(a) No article, the (b) The, the (c) A, the (d) The, no article
Answer: (a)
Solution: A material noun used generally takes no article, but when particularised by a phrase like "of this region" it takes "the". The trap "The, the" puts the article in both places, ignoring the general first use, so the answer is "No article, the".

125. Fill in the blank: "___ higher you climb, ___ colder it becomes."
(a) A, a (b) The, the (c) No article, no article (d) An, the
Answer: (b)
Solution: In parallel comparative clauses — "the higher … the colder …" — "the" is used before both comparatives as an adverb. The trap "No article, no article" treats these as ordinary comparatives, but the correlative structure demands "the" in both places, so the answer is "The, the".
