# 📋 Important Rules & Tips — Para Jumbles

## (a) 12 High-Yield Rules

| # | Rule | Example | Examiner's Trap |
|---|------|---------|-----------------|
| 1 | **Find the independent opening sentence first** — it introduces the topic with no pronoun, no connector, no reference backwards. | "The monsoon is the lifeline of Indian agriculture." (can stand alone) | A sentence starting with "However…", "This…", "It…" can almost never open the paragraph. |
| 2 | **Pronouns must have an antecedent** — "he/she/it/they/this" must point to a noun already mentioned. | "Ramesh joined the bank. **He** worked there for 20 years." | Two sentences may both contain pronouns; only the one whose pronoun is resolved can come earlier. |
| 3 | **Article rule: 'a/an' before 'the'** — a noun introduced with "a/an" is later referred to with "the". | "**A** farmer bought a cow. **The** cow gave rich milk." | Options often reverse this order to look plausible. |
| 4 | **Chronology rule** — events follow time order (past → present → future; cause → effect). | "He was born in 1947… He graduated in 1968… He retired in 2005." | Dates scattered across sentences are a deliberate ordering trap. |
| 5 | **Connector pairs** — "Although… yet", "Not only… but also", "On one hand… on the other". | "Not only did he win gold, **but he also** broke the record." | The second half of the pair can never precede the first half. |
| 6 | **General → Specific** — the paragraph moves from a broad statement to details, examples, data. | "Pollution kills millions. Delhi's air, for instance, …" | A specific example placed before the general claim is a classic wrong sequence. |
| 7 | **Idea → Example** — "For example / For instance / Such as" always follow the idea they illustrate. | "Exercise keeps the mind sharp. **For example**, chess players…" | Example sentences look like openers — they are not. |
| 8 | **Concluding signals come last** — "Thus, Therefore, Hence, In conclusion, Finally" mark the end. | "**Thus**, discipline is the true key to success." | "Finally" can also mean "at last" mid-story — check context, not just the word. |
| 9 | **Mandatory pairs (linking)** — find 2 sentences that must stick together; eliminate options that break the pair. | Q: "What causes inflation?" → A: "Demand exceeding supply causes it." | In option-elimination, one solid pair often kills 2 of 4 options instantly. |
| 10 | **Same-subject clustering** — sentences about the same sub-topic stay together before the topic shifts. | Three sentences on "causes of flood" precede two on "solutions". | Examiners insert one "solutions" sentence among "causes" to break your cluster. |
| 11 | **Full name → short name** — a person/organisation is introduced fully, then referred to briefly. | "**Dr. A.P.J. Abdul Kalam** was born in Rameswaram. **Kalam** later…" | The short-name sentence appearing first is a sound-right trap. |
| 12 | **Tone & flow check** — read your final sequence once; the paragraph must read smoothly with one consistent tone. | A humorous sentence inside a serious argument signals misplacement. | An option can be grammatically fine yet logically jarring — always do the final read-through. |

## (b) Time Management — The 15/45-Second Rule

| Type | What it looks like | Time | Strategy |
|------|-------------------|------|----------|
| **Fact-based / Mechanical (15 sec)** | Clear opener + obvious pronoun/article links; 4-sentence jumbles; questions asking "which is the FIRST/LAST sentence?" | ~15 sec | Spot the opener or one mandatory pair, match with options, mark and move. |
| **Inference-based / Logical (45 sec)** | 5–6 sentence jumbles; abstract topics (philosophy, economy); "arrange to form a coherent paragraph" with no obvious opener; tone-based ordering | ~45 sec | Build 2 mandatory pairs → eliminate options → read the surviving sequence once. |

**Rule of thumb:** If after 45 seconds no pair clicks, mark the option containing your strongest pair and move on — never cross 90 seconds on one jumble.

## (c) Morphology — Roots & Affixes Useful in Para Jumbles (and Vocabulary)

| Root / Affix | Meaning | Example Words |
|--------------|---------|---------------|
| **sequ** (Latin) | follow | sequence, sequel, subsequent |
| **ord** (Latin) | order | order, ordinal, coordinate |
| **junct** (Latin) | join | junction, conjunction, adjunct |
| **co-/con-** (prefix) | together | coherent, connect, combine |
| **re-** (prefix) | again / back | refer, rearrange, return |
| **trans-** (prefix) | across | transition, transfer, transform |
| **-tion / -sion** (suffix) | act/state of | arrangement, connection, conclusion |
| **-ive** (suffix) | having the quality of | cohesive, conclusive, narrative |

**Why this matters:** Words like *coherent, sequence, transition, conjunction* are the metalanguage of Para Jumble questions and options — knowing the roots makes instructions and answer choices instantly clear.
