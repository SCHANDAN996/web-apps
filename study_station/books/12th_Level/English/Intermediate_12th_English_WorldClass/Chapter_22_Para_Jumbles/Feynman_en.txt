# 🧒 Feynman Technique — Para Jumbles Explained to a 12-Year-Old

## The Hardest Concept: "How do I find the FIRST sentence when all four look equally good?"

Imagine your friend hands you a torn comic book. The pages are scattered on the floor. You have never read this comic before. How do you know which page comes first?

Easy! You look for the page that **introduces the hero**. The first page never starts with "Then he ran away…" or "Because of this, she was happy…" — because *who* ran away? *Who* is "she"? The first page always says something like: **"Ravi was a boy who loved kites."** Full name. Full introduction. No mystery words.

A Para Jumble is exactly that torn comic book. The examiner has cut one paragraph into 4–6 sentences, shuffled them, and asked you to tape them back together.

## The Two Detective Rules

**Rule 1 — The Introduction Rule (find the opener):**
The first sentence never contains:
- a pronoun without a name before it (*he, she, it, they, this, these*)
- a connector that points backwards (*however, therefore, moreover, but, so*)
- a conclusion word (*thus, finally, in conclusion*)

The first sentence usually has a **full noun** (a person's full name, a topic's full name) and makes a **general statement**.

**Rule 2 — The Chain Rule (link the rest):**
Once you have the opener, every next sentence must "hold hands" with the previous one. Look for:
- **Pronoun chains:** "Ravi loved kites" → "**He** flew them every evening." (He = Ravi ✔)
- **Word echoes:** "…bought a **telescope**" → "The **telescope** cost him…"
- **Time order:** first → then → finally
- **Idea order:** general statement → specific example → result

## The Unforgettable Trick: "Name First, Pronoun Later" 🪢

Think of a wedding. The host announces: **"Please welcome Mr. Sharma!"** — full name first. Only *after* that can anyone say "**He** looks great today." Nobody walks into a wedding shouting "He looks great!" before the groom is introduced. Everyone would ask: *He who?*

> **Memory hook: "No HE without a NAME."**
> If a sentence starts with *he/she/it/they/this* and no name has appeared yet, it can NEVER be the opening sentence.

## Try It Right Now (30 seconds)

(A) He won the race easily.
(B) Mohan practised running every morning for a year.

Which comes first? **B**, because "Mohan" is the name, and "He" in A needs Mohan to exist first. B → A. Done. You just solved a Para Jumble.

## ⚠️ The Trap Even Toppers Fall Into

Two sentences may *both* look like openers. When stuck, don't read the sentences again and again — instead, **find one locked pair** (like "telescope → the telescope") and check which option contains that pair. Options are your biggest clue: eliminate, don't meditate!
