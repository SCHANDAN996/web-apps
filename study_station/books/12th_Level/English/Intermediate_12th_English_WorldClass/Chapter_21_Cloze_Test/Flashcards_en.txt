Card 1
Front: What is a Cloze Test?
Back: A reading passage with several words removed (blanks). You must choose the most suitable word for each blank from the given options, using context, grammar, and vocabulary.

Card 2
Front: What is the FIRST step before filling any blank in a Cloze Test?
Back: Read the entire passage once WITHOUT filling anything, to grasp the theme, tone, and context — most blanks depend on the overall meaning, not just one sentence.

Card 3
Front: Mnemonic: What does "CATS" stand for in Cloze Test strategy?
Back: **C**ontext (read the whole passage) → **A**nswer elimination (rule out wrong options) → **T**ense/grammar check → **S**ense re-read (read the sentence again with your chosen word).

Card 4
Front: Why should you identify the tone of the passage first?
Back: Tone (positive, negative, neutral, formal) eliminates options that clash with it. E.g., in a passage praising someone, a negative word like "failed" is unlikely to fit a blank about their achievements.

Card 5
Front: What grammar checks should you apply to every blank?
Back: (1) Part of speech needed (noun/verb/adjective/adverb), (2) tense and subject-verb agreement, (3) singular/plural, (4) article (a/an/the) before the blank.

Card 6
Front: If a blank comes right after "an", what does it tell you?
Back: The missing word must begin with a vowel sound. Eliminate all options starting with a consonant sound.

Card 7
Front: What are "connector clues" in a Cloze Test?
Back: Linking words near the blank — but, however, therefore, because, although — that signal contrast, cause, or result, and tell you whether the missing word should be positive or negative in meaning.

Card 8
Front: The sentence before a blank says "Despite his hard work, he ___ the exam." Which type of word fits?
Back: A negative-outcome word (e.g., "failed"), because "despite" signals contrast between effort and result.

Card 9
Front: What is a collocation and why does it matter in Cloze Tests?
Back: A collocation is a natural word pairing (e.g., "make a decision", not "do a decision"; "heavy rain", not "strong rain"). Exams often test whether you know the correct pairing.

Card 10
Front: What should you do when two options both seem correct?
Back: Re-read the sentences before AND after the blank. The correct option usually fits the wider context and collocates naturally; the trap option fits only the immediate sentence.

Card 11
Front: Should you fill blanks strictly in order (1, 2, 3…)?
Back: Not necessarily. Fill the easy blanks first — each filled blank makes the passage clearer and gives more context for the harder ones.

Card 12
Front: What is the most common trap in Cloze Test options?
Back: Options that are grammatically correct but wrong in meaning/context, or near-synonyms where only one collocates correctly (e.g., "say" vs "tell").

Card 13
Front: How do prepositions help solve Cloze blanks?
Back: Many blanks depend on fixed preposition usage: "depend on", "interested in", "good at", "consist of". Check the word before/after the blank for the required preposition.

Card 14
Front: If the passage is in past tense, what should you check in verb blanks?
Back: The verb form must stay consistent with the past-tense narrative (e.g., "went", "was working"), unless the sentence reports a general truth.

Card 15
Front: What is the final step after filling all blanks?
Back: Re-read the complete passage with all your chosen words inserted. It should read smoothly, logically, and grammatically — if any sentence feels odd, recheck that blank.

Card 16
Front: Which exams commonly include Cloze Tests?
Back: SSC CGL, SSC CHSL, IBPS PO/Clerk, SBI PO, RRB NTPC and other banking exams — usually 5–10 blanks in one passage.

Card 17
Front: What is the difference between a vocabulary blank and a grammar blank?
Back: Vocabulary blanks test word meaning/collocation (all options are the same part of speech); grammar blanks test tense, agreement, articles, or prepositions (options are forms of related words).

Card 18
Front: Time strategy: how long should a 10-blank Cloze Test take in an exam?
Back: Roughly 6–8 minutes: 1 minute for the first full read, 4–5 minutes for the blanks, 1 minute for the final re-read. Don't get stuck — mark and move on.
