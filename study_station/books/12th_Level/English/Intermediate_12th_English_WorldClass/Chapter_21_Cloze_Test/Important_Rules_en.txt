# 📋 Important Grammar Rules & Tips — Cloze Test

## (a) 12 High-Yield Rules

| # | Rule | Example | Examiner's Trap |
|---|------|---------|-----------------|
| 1 | Read the whole passage first; the blank's answer must fit the **overall theme**, not just the sentence. | A passage on climate change: "The planet is getting ___" → *warmer*, not *colder*. | An option that is grammatically correct but contradicts the passage's tone. |
| 2 | **Subject–Verb Agreement**: the verb agrees with the real subject, not the nearest noun. | "The quality of the products ___ good." → *is* (subject = quality). | Intervening phrase ("of the products") pushes a plural noun next to the blank. |
| 3 | **Collocation check**: some words simply "live together" in English. | *make* a decision (not *do* a decision); *heavy* rain (not *strong* rain). | Two options with the same meaning — only one collocates. |
| 4 | **Preposition is fixed by the word before/after it.** | depend *on*, consist *of*, interested *in*, good *at*. | Options like on/in/at/for where all look possible without checking the verb. |
| 5 | **Connector logic**: identify the relation — contrast, cause, addition, result. | "He was tired; ___, he kept working." → *nevertheless/however*. | "Because" and "although" swapped — read the logic, not the sound. |
| 6 | **Tense consistency**: the blank's tense must match the timeline of the passage. | Past narrative → "He ___ (enter) the room." → *entered*. | One present-tense option planted in an all-past paragraph. |
| 7 | **Article rules**: *a/an* for first mention, *the* for specific/second mention. | "I saw ___ dog. ___ dog was black." → *a, The*. | "The" offered where the noun is being introduced for the first time. |
| 8 | **Pronoun reference**: the pronoun must match its noun in number and gender. | "The team won ___ match." → *its* (team = singular unit). | "Their" sounds natural in speech but is wrong for a singular collective noun. |
| 9 | **Parallel structure**: items in a list take the same grammatical form. | "She likes reading, writing and ___." → *swimming* (not *to swim*). | One option breaks the -ing or infinitive pattern of the list. |
| 10 | **Word form (part of speech)**: decide if the blank needs a noun, verb, adjective or adverb. | "He spoke ___." → *softly* (adverb modifies verb). | Options give the same root in four forms: soft / softly / softness / soften. |
| 11 | **Positive vs negative word**: match the sentiment of the surrounding sentence. | "Despite the crisis, the economy remained ___." → *stable/resilient*. | A negative word (collapsed, weak) that fits grammar but reverses the meaning. |
| 12 | **Eliminate before you select**: cross out 2 clearly wrong options, then compare the remaining two on collocation + tone. | Between *say* and *tell*: "He ___ me a story." → *told* (tell + object). | Choosing the first option that "sounds right" without checking the rest. |

## (b) Time Management — The 15/45-Second Rule

| Type | What it looks like | Time | Strategy |
|------|-------------------|------|----------|
| **Fact-based (15 sec)** | Prepositions, articles, subject–verb agreement, tense, word form, fixed collocations. | 15 seconds | Apply the rule directly. If you know the rule, answer and move on — do not re-read. |
| **Inference-based (45 sec)** | Connectors (however/therefore), tone words, theme-based vocabulary, pronoun reference across sentences. | 45 seconds | Read one sentence before and one after the blank; identify the logic (contrast? cause? result?), then answer. |

**Practical split for a 10-blank cloze test:** roughly 6 blanks are fact-based (6 × 15 sec = 90 sec) and 4 are inference-based (4 × 45 sec = 180 sec) → about **5.5–6 minutes total** (4.5 minutes for the blanks plus 1–1.5 minutes for the initial read-through of the passage).

**Golden sequence:** Read the full passage once without touching the options → attempt fact-based blanks first → return to inference blanks → never leave a blank empty (where there is no negative marking, an educated guess is always better than a skipped blank — check your exam's marking scheme).

## (c) Morphology — Root, Affix, Example

Knowing roots lets you guess the meaning of unknown words inside the passage and the options.

| Root / Affix | Meaning | Example Words |
|--------------|---------|---------------|
| **bene-** (Latin) | good, well | benefit, benevolent, beneficial |
| **mal-** (Latin) | bad | malfunction, malicious, malnutrition |
| **dict-** (Latin) | say, speak | predict, dictionary, contradict |
| **port-** (Latin) | carry | transport, export, portable |
| **spect-** (Latin) | see, look | inspect, respect, spectator |
| **chron-** (Greek) | time | chronic, chronology, synchronize |
| **bio-** (Greek) | life | biology, biography, antibiotic |
| **geo-** (Greek) | earth | geography, geology, geothermal |
| **-phobia** (Greek) | fear | claustrophobia, hydrophobia |
| **pre-** (prefix) | before | predict, preview, precaution |
| **re-** (prefix) | again | rebuild, revise, return |
| **un- / in- / dis-** (prefix) | not, opposite | unhappy, incorrect, disagree |
| **-ful** (suffix) | full of | hopeful, careful, powerful |
| **-less** (suffix) | without | hopeless, careless, powerless |
| **-tion / -sion** (suffix) | makes a noun | decide → decision, educate → education |
| **-ly** (suffix) | makes an adverb | quick → quickly, careful → carefully |

**How to use this in the exam:** if a blank offers *benevolent, malicious, chronic, portable* and the sentence says "The old man was known for his kind and generous nature" — the root *bene-* (good) instantly points to *benevolent*.
