# 📊 PYQ Analysis — Cloze Test

## How Exams Test This Chapter (Pattern Analysis)

Cloze Test is a **permanent fixture** in SSC CGL, SSC CHSL, IBPS PO/Clerk, SBI PO, and Railway (RRB NTPC) exams. A typical passage carries **5–10 blanks**, each blank worth 1 mark. The pattern across exams is remarkably stable:

- **Grammar-based blanks (most frequent):** articles (a/an/the), prepositions (in/on/at/for), subject-verb agreement, tense consistency, conjunctions (although/because/however).
- **Vocabulary-based blanks:** choosing the word that fits the *context and tone* — e.g., "The economy showed signs of ___" (recovery / discovery / coverage).
- **Connector blanks:** transition words that test whether you understood the passage's logic (therefore, nevertheless, moreover).
- **Collocation blanks:** fixed pairs like "take place", "heavy rain", "make a decision" — a favourite of SSC.

**Recurring sub-topics:** passages on environment/climate, economy, science & technology, social issues, and motivational/anecdotal stories. Banking exams (IBPS/SBI) lean towards economy and abstract topics; SSC leans towards narrative and general-knowledge passages.

**Examiner's favourite traps:**
1. **Sound-Right Trap:** an option that "sounds correct" in isolation but breaks tense or logic in the passage.
2. **Near-Synonym Confusion:** two options with similar meanings (e.g., "affect" vs "effect", "rise" vs "raise") where only one fits grammatically.
3. **Connector Reversal:** the passage needs a contrast word (however) but a continuation word (moreover) is offered first.
4. **Over-thinking:** the simplest, most common word is correct, but students pick the "fancy" option.

## Top 10 High-Yield Questions (with Solutions)

**Q1.** The government has launched several schemes to ___ rural employment.
(a) boast (b) boost (c) boot (d) booth
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** "Boost" means to increase — fits "employment". "Boast" (to brag) is the sound-right trap; "boot/booth" are spelling traps.
**Trap type:** Sound-Right | **Source:** PYQ-style

**Q2.** She has been working in this bank ___ 2015.
(a) from (b) since (c) for (d) by
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** "Since" is used with a point of time (2015); "for" is used with a duration (for 9 years). "From" does not pair with present perfect continuous here.
**Trap type:** Sound-Right | **Source:** NCERT Class 9 English (Grammar)

**Q3.** The passage: "The new policy was unpopular. ___, the government decided to withdraw it."
(a) Moreover (b) Nevertheless (c) Consequently (d) Otherwise
**Answer: (c)**
**Solution:** The second sentence is a *result* of the first, so a cause-effect connector (Consequently) is needed. "Nevertheless" (contrast) is the connector-reversal trap.
**Trap type:** Connector Reversal | **Source:** PYQ-style

**Q4.** The rise in prices has badly ___ the common man.
(a) effected (b) affected (c) infected (d) defected
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** "Affect" is the verb (to influence); "effect" is usually a noun. Here a verb is needed after "has".
**Trap type:** Near-Synonym Confusion | **Source:** PYQ-style

**Q5.** He is ___ honest man and can be trusted.
(a) a (b) an (c) the (d) no article
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** "Honest" begins with a vowel *sound* (the 'h' is silent), so "an" is used. Students who go by spelling pick "a" — a classic trap.
**Trap type:** Sound-Right | **Source:** NCERT Class 8 English (Grammar)

**Q6.** The committee ___ its report yesterday.
(a) submit (b) submitted (c) has submitted (d) had submitted
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** A specific past time marker like "yesterday" requires the simple past tense — "submitted". "Has submitted" (present perfect) can never be used with a definite past time; "had submitted" needs an earlier past reference point.
**Trap type:** Tense Consistency | **Source:** NCERT Class 10 English (Grammar)

**Q7.** "The scientist's discovery was a major ___ in cancer research."
(a) breakdown (b) breakthrough (c) breakout (d) breakup
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** "Breakthrough" = a significant advance. The other compound words are real words with wrong meanings — a vocabulary trap.
**Trap type:** Near-Synonym Confusion | **Source:** PYQ-style

**Q8.** Neither the manager nor the employees ___ present at the meeting.
(a) was (b) were (c) is (d) has been
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** With "neither…nor", the verb agrees with the *nearer* subject ("employees" — plural), so "were". The distant singular "manager" is the intervening-phrase trap.
**Trap type:** Intervening Phrase | **Source:** NCERT Class 10 English (Grammar)

**Q9.** "Despite repeated warnings, he continued to drive ___."
(a) recklessly (b) reckless (c) recklessness (d) reckon
**Answer: (a)**
**Solution:** The verb "drive" needs an adverb to modify it — "recklessly". "Reckless" (adjective) is the sound-right trap.
**Trap type:** Sound-Right | **Source:** PYQ-style

**Q10.** "The two brothers divided the property ___ themselves."
(a) between (b) among (c) within (d) into
**Answer: (a)**
**Solution:** "Between" is used for two persons; "among" for more than two. "Themselves" with two brothers confirms "between".
**Trap type:** Over-thinking | **Source:** NCERT Class 9 English (Grammar)

## Applying the 15/45-Second Rule

**Q5 (article blank) — 15-second question:** This is fact/rule-based. The moment you see "___ honest man", the silent-'h' rule fires instantly. Do not re-read the whole passage. **Target: 15 seconds.**

**Q3 (connector blank) — 45-second question:** This is inference-based. You must read the sentence *before* and *after* the blank, identify the logical relationship (cause → effect), and only then choose. Rushing here leads to the connector-reversal trap. **Target: up to 45 seconds.**

**Rule of thumb:** Grammar blanks (articles, prepositions, agreement, tense) = 15 sec. Logic/tone blanks (connectors, vocabulary-in-context, inference) = 45 sec.
