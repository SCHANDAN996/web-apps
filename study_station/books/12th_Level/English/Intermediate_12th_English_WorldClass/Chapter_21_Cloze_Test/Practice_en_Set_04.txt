76. Passage: "The government has launched a new scheme to ___ (76) ___ clean drinking water to every household by 2026." Choose the correct word for blank 76.
(a) provided (b) provides (c) providing (d) provide
Answer: (d)
Solution: After the infinitive marker "to", the base form of the verb is required. "Provides" and "provided" are finite forms and "providing" is a participle, so all three break the infinitive structure. The base verb fits, so the answer is "provide".

77. Passage: "Although the task was difficult, she completed it ___ (77) ___ any help." Choose the correct word.
(a) with (b) without (c) by (d) from
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Although" signals contrast: the task was hard, yet she finished it — so the blank must show absence of help. "With" destroys the contrast and is the tempting trap; "by" and "from" do not collocate with "help" here. The contrast demands "without", so the answer is "without".

78. Passage: "The new policy is expected to bring ___ (78) ___ significant changes in the education sector." Choose the correct word.
(a) up (b) about (c) on (d) over
Answer: (b)
Solution: The phrasal verb "bring about" means "to cause". "Bring up" means to raise a topic or a child, "bring on" means to cause illness, and "bring over" means to carry something to a place — none fit "changes". The correct phrasal verb is "bring about", so the answer is "about".

79. Passage: "He is one of the most ___ (79) ___ scientists of our time." Choose the correct word.
(a) eloquent (b) imminent (c) immanent (d) eminent
Answer: (d)
Solution: ⚠️ Examiner's trap: "eminent" (famous, distinguished) and "imminent" (about to happen) look and sound alike. A scientist can be distinguished, not "about to happen". "Immanent" means inherent and "eloquent" means fluent in speech. The word meaning "distinguished" is "eminent", so the answer is "eminent".

80. Passage: "The company decided to ___ (80) ___ its operations to smaller towns." Choose the correct word.
(a) expend (b) expanse (c) expect (d) expand
Answer: (d)
Solution: The sentence needs a verb meaning "to enlarge". "Expanse" is a noun, "expect" means to anticipate, and "expend" means to spend — all traps based on similar spelling. The verb meaning "to grow larger" is "expand", so the answer is "expand".

81. Passage: "___ (81) ___ the heavy rain, the match continued." Choose the correct word.
(a) Despite (b) Because (c) Although (d) Since
Answer: (a)
Solution: The blank is followed by a noun phrase ("the heavy rain"), not a clause, so conjunctions like "although", "because" and "since" — which need a subject and verb — are grammatically impossible here. The preposition showing contrast is "despite", so the answer is "Despite".

82. Passage: "The teacher asked the students to ___ (82) ___ the chapter before the test." Choose the correct word.
(a) revive (b) reverse (c) revise (d) reveal
Answer: (c)
Solution: Before a test, students re-study a chapter. "Reverse" means to turn backward, "revive" means to bring back to life, and "reveal" means to disclose — none fit the study context. The word meaning "to study again" is "revise", so the answer is "revise".

83. Passage: "Hard work is the key ___ (83) ___ success." Choose the correct word.
(a) of (b) for (c) in (d) to
Answer: (d)
Solution: ⚠️ Examiner's trap: "key of" sounds acceptable to many Hindi speakers ("safalta ki kunji"), but in standard English the fixed collocation is "key to success", like "answer to", "solution to" and "introduction to". The correct preposition is "to", so the answer is "to".

84. Passage: "The minister gave a long speech, but ___ (84) ___ of what he said was new." Choose the correct word.
(a) many (b) little (c) few (d) much
Answer: (b)
Solution: "What he said" refers to uncountable content, so countable quantifiers "many" and "few" are ruled out. The contrast word "but" demands a negative sense, and "much" is positive. The negative uncountable quantifier is "little", so the answer is "little".

85. Passage: "The bridge was built ___ (85) ___ the river to connect the two villages." Choose the correct word.
(a) across (b) along (c) between (d) through
Answer: (a)
Solution: A bridge spans from one side of a river to the other. "Along" means parallel to it, "through" suggests passing inside something, and "between" needs two reference points in a different structure. The preposition meaning "from one side to the other" is "across", so the answer is "across".

86. Passage: "She has been working in this bank ___ (86) ___ 2015." Choose the correct word.
(a) for (b) since (c) from (d) by
Answer: (b)
Solution: "2015" is a point of time, and the present perfect continuous "has been working" pairs a point of time with "since". "For" takes a duration (for nine years), which is the tempting trap. The correct word for a point of time is "since", so the answer is "since".

87. Passage: "The report was ___ (87) ___ by a committee of experts." Choose the correct word.
(a) prepared (b) preparation (c) preparatory (d) preparing
Answer: (a)
Solution: The passive structure "was ___ by" requires the past participle (V3). "Preparation" is a noun, "preparatory" is an adjective, and "preparing" would make an active continuous form that clashes with "by a committee". The past participle is "prepared", so the answer is "prepared".

88. Passage: "We must ___ (88) ___ our natural resources for future generations." Choose the correct word.
(a) waste (b) consume (c) conserve (d) destroy
Answer: (c)
Solution: The phrase "for future generations" shows the resources must be protected, not used up. "Waste", "consume" and "destroy" all contradict that purpose. The word meaning "to protect and use carefully" is "conserve", so the answer is "conserve".

89. Passage: "Neither the manager nor the employees ___ (89) ___ aware of the change." Choose the correct word.
(a) was (b) has been (c) is (d) were
Answer: (d)
Solution: With "neither…nor", the verb agrees with the nearer subject, which is the plural "employees". "Was" is the trap for those who match the verb with "manager". The nearer subject is plural, so the answer is "were".

90. Passage: "The old building was ___ (90) ___ down to make way for a shopping mall." Choose the correct word.
(a) pulled (b) pushed (c) put (d) broken
Answer: (a)
Solution: The phrasal verb "pull down" means "to demolish". "Push down" means to press, "put down" means to suppress or place, and "break down" means to stop functioning. The phrasal verb meaning "demolish" is "pulled down", so the answer is "pulled".

91. Passage: "His argument does not ___ (91) ___ any sense." Choose the correct word.
(a) create (b) do (c) make (d) give
Answer: (c)
Solution: The fixed collocation is "make sense". "Do", "create" and "give" do not collocate with "sense" in this idiomatic meaning, though "give" tempts Hindi speakers ("koi matlab nahi deta"). The correct collocation is "make sense", so the answer is "make".

92. Passage: "The children were ___ (92) ___ excited to see the circus." Choose the correct word.
(a) quiet (b) quit (c) quite (d) quilt
Answer: (c)
Solution: ⚠️ Examiner's trap: "quiet" (silent) and "quite" (fairly/very) are look-alikes. The blank needs an intensifier before the adjective "excited", and "quiet" would reverse the meaning. "Quit" is a verb and "quilt" is a noun. The intensifier is "quite", so the answer is "quite".

93. Passage: "The flight was delayed ___ (93) ___ bad weather." Choose the correct word.
(a) because (b) due to (c) since (d) owing
Answer: (b)
Solution: The blank is followed by the noun phrase "bad weather", so clause-taking conjunctions "because" and "since" are ungrammatical here, and "owing" is incomplete without "to". The prepositional phrase meaning "caused by" is "due to", so the answer is "due to".

94. Passage: "She is ___ (94) ___ than her elder sister." Choose the correct word.
(a) more taller (b) tallest (c) taller (d) most tall
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Than" demands the comparative degree, ruling out the superlatives "tallest" and "most tall". "More taller" is a double comparative — a classic trap — since "taller" already carries the comparative force. The correct comparative is "taller", so the answer is "taller".

(a) was (b) were (c) are (d) have been
Answer: (a)
Solution: With "along with", the verb agrees with the first subject, "the principal", which is singular. "Were" is the trap for those who match the verb with the nearer plural "teachers". The true subject is singular, so the answer is "was".

96. Passage: "He could not attend the meeting as he was ___ (96) ___ with fever." Choose the correct word.
(a) suffering (b) filled (c) caught (d) down
Answer: (d)
Solution: The idiom "down with fever" means ill with fever. "Suffering" would need "from", "caught" needs "a fever" as an object, and "filled" is meaningless here. The idiomatic word is "down", so the answer is "down".

97. Passage: "The data collected by the survey ___ (97) ___ a sharp rise in unemployment." Choose the correct word.
(a) shows (b) show (c) showing (d) shown
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Data" in formal exam English is treated as plural, but here the trap is reversed: the real subject test is tense and number with "the survey" as a distractor. Actually "data" is plural, so "show" tempts — but standard exam usage accepts "data" as a singular mass noun in modern usage; the passage's singular sense of the collected dataset takes a singular verb. The intended answer is "shows", so the answer is "shows".

99. Passage: "The novel was so ___ (99) ___ that I finished it in one sitting." Choose the correct word.

Answer: (b)
Solution: Finishing a book in one sitting shows it held the reader's attention. "Boring", "lengthy" and "predictable" would all discourage reading, contradicting the result clause. The word meaning "highly engaging" is "captivating", so the answer is "captivating".

100. Passage: "The two brothers divided the property ___ (100) ___ themselves." Choose the correct word.
(a) between (b) among (c) within (d) into
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Between" is used for two persons, and "among" for more than two — the classic trap here, since "themselves" tempts many students toward "among". With exactly two brothers, the correct preposition is "between", so the answer is "between".
