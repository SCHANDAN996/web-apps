# Cloze Test — Fill the Gaps, Win the Marks

## 🎯 The Hook

Imagine a sentence with a hole in it: *"The government has ______ a new scheme for farmers."* Four options stare at you — *launched, lunched, lounged, loaned*. Three are traps. One is the answer. That, in a nutshell, is the **Cloze Test** — the single most scoring "mini-passage" in SSC CHSL, CGL, IBPS and Railway exams. A passage of 5–10 blanks appears, and every blank is a full-mark question. Master this one format and you master vocabulary, grammar and comprehension **at the same time**.

## 🎓 Learning Objectives

By the end of this chapter you will be able to:

1. Read a cloze passage once and predict the *type* of word each blank needs (noun, verb, connector, preposition).
2. Eliminate wrong options using grammar, collocation and tone — not guesswork.
3. Distinguish **grammar blanks** (15-second) from **context/vocabulary blanks** (45-second).
4. Avoid the three classic traps examiners plant in every cloze test.

---

## 1️⃣ What Exactly Is a Cloze Test?

The word *cloze* comes from **"closure"** — a psychology term for the human brain's ability to complete an incomplete pattern. The examiner deletes words from a paragraph; your brain must restore them. There are two formats:

- **Passage-based cloze** — 5 to 10 blanks inside one paragraph (SSC CGL, CHSL, IBPS PO/Clerk).
- **Single-sentence fillers** — one or two blanks in isolated sentences (Railway, SSC MTS, State exams).

The skill tested is never "do you know this word?" alone. It is: *does this word fit the grammar, the meaning AND the tone of the passage?*

## 2️⃣ The Three-Layer Check (The Heart of the Method)

Every blank must pass **three filters**. If an option fails even one, reject it.

| Layer | Question to Ask | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Grammar** | Does the part of speech fit? Tense? Number? | "He has ______" → needs a past participle (*taken*, not *took*) |
| **2. Meaning (Collocation)** | Do these words naturally go together in English? | We *make* a decision, never *do* a decision |
| **3. Tone/Context** | Is the passage positive, negative, neutral? Formal or informal? | A passage praising a scheme won't take *"failed"* |

**Worked example:** *"The new policy will ______ small businesses to grow."*
(a) prevent (b) enable (c) forbid (d) block
- Grammar: all four are base verbs after *will* — Layer 1 passes for all.
- Meaning: *enable somebody to grow* is the natural collocation.
- Tone: "will grow" is positive → only **(b) enable** survives. ✅

> 🧠 **Active Recall:** Name the three layers, in order. Which layer usually eliminates the most options in a vocabulary-based blank?

## 3️⃣ Reading Strategy: Two Passes, Never One

**Pass 1 — Read the whole passage WITHOUT looking at options (20–30 seconds).** Understand the story: Who? What happened? Is the tone happy, critical, neutral? Often you can *predict* the missing word in your own words before seeing the choices.

**Pass 2 — Attack each blank.** Compare your prediction with the options. The option closest to your prediction is usually right. This protects you from the "sound-right" trap — options that feel correct in isolation but betray the passage.

**Golden sequence for each blank:**
1. Look at the words **immediately before and after** the blank (local clue).
2. Check the **connector logic** — *although, because, however, therefore* flip or continue the idea.
3. Only then scan the options.

> 🧠 **Active Recall:** Why should you read the passage before looking at the options?

## 4️⃣ The Connector Compass 🧭

Connectors tell you the *direction* of the sentence. Memorise this mnemonic:

> **"BATS fly in the Same direction, BUT Owls turn Around"**
> - **B**ecause, **A**nd, **T**herefore, **S**o → idea continues (same direction)
> - **But, However, Although, Yet, Despite** → idea reverses (opposite direction)

If the sentence says *"Although the team lost, the crowd was ______"*, the reversal signal *although* demands a positive word — *cheerful*, not *disappointed*.

## 5️⃣ Vocabulary Power: Roots That Rule Cloze Tests

Cloze options are often built from the same Greek/Latin roots. Learn the root, unlock ten words.

| Root | Meaning | Derived Words |
|---|---|---|
| **bene** (Latin) | good | benefit, benevolent, beneficial, benediction |
| **mal** (Latin) | bad | malicious, malnutrition, malfunction, malpractice |
| **dict** (Latin) | say | predict, dictate, verdict, contradict |
| **port** (Latin) | carry | transport, export, portable, support |
| **cred** (Latin) | believe | credible, credit, incredible, credentials |
| **spect** (Latin) | look | inspect, respect, prospect, spectator |
| **rupt** (Latin) | break | interrupt, erupt, bankrupt, disrupt |

**See them in context (mini-passage):**

> "The minister's speech was full of **benevolent** promises, but critics called his claims **incredible**. They **predicted** that the scheme would **disrupt** the market, while supporters argued it would **benefit** small traders and **transport** goods more cheaply. An official was asked to **inspect** the first centres and verify the **credentials** of the agencies involved."

Eight root-words, one paragraph — this is exactly how cloze passages feel in the exam.

> 🧠 **Active Recall:** Cover the table. What do *bene, mal, dict, cred* mean? Give one derived word for each.

## ⚠️ Examiner's Trap Box

1. **The Sound-Right Trap:** An option that is grammatically fine alone but contradicts the passage's tone. *"The economy improved; however, inflation ______."* — *"fell"* sounds nice, but *however* demands a negative turn → **"rose"**.
2. **The Collocation Twin:** Two options with similar meanings, only one of which pairs with the surrounding word. We say *"heavy rain"*, not *"strong rain"*; *"take a risk"*, not *"make a risk"*.
3. **The Tense Echo:** The passage runs in past tense; one option is present tense and "feels modern". Match the passage's tense unless a time marker (*now, today, by 2025*) forces a change.

## 🔁 Linguistic Bridge — Hindi से English

| हम हिंदी से सोचकर कहते हैं ❌ | Correct English ✅ |
|---|---|
| "He is more superior than me." | "He is **superior to** me." (superior/inferior/senior take *to*, never *than*) |
| "I am agree with you." | "I **agree** with you." (agree is a verb, no *am*) |
| "He did a mistake." | "He **made** a mistake." |
| "She is good in cricket." | "She is good **at** cricket." |
| "The news are good." | "The news **is** good." (news is singular) |

Cloze blanks love exactly these spots — a preposition after *superior*, or *made/did* before *mistake*.

| Blank needs vocabulary/tone | Read full sentence, check tone + collocation | ~45 sec |

| Totally stuck | Mark, skip, return after finishing other blanks | 0 sec now |
