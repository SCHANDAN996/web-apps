🧒 Feynman Technique — Cloze Test Explained to a 12-Year-Old

The Hardest Concept: "How do I know which word fits the blank when all four options look correct?"

Imagine you are doing a jigsaw puzzle. You have a missing piece, and four pieces in your hand. All four are blue. All four look almost the same. So how do you pick the right one?

You don't stare at the pieces. You look at the HOLE. You look at the picture around the hole — the colours, the lines, the shapes touching the empty space. The piece that matches the surroundings is the answer.

A Cloze Test is exactly this. The blank is the hole. The sentence before and after the blank is the picture around it. Most students make one big mistake: they read only the blank and try all four options one by one, like forcing puzzle pieces into the hole. That is slow and confusing.

The smart way — the Detective Way:

Step 1: Read the WHOLE passage once, ignoring the blanks. Just understand the story. Is it happy or sad? About the past or future? Positive or negative?

Step 2: For each blank, read one sentence BEFORE and one sentence AFTER. Ask: "What is the writer talking about here?"

Step 3: Before looking at the options, GUESS your own word for the blank. Say it in your head — even a Hindi word is fine!

Step 4: Now look at the four options. Pick the one closest to YOUR guess.

Why does Step 3 work like magic? Because the examiner designs wrong options to trap people who have no guess. If you already know "the answer should be something like 'increase'", then options like "decrease", "decreased", "decreasing" cannot fool you.

A Daily-Life Example:

Your mother says: "Beta, please ___ the milk from the fridge."
You don't need options! Your brain already says "bring". If someone gave you options — (a) throw (b) bring (c) burn (d) sell — you would laugh at the wrong ones. That is exactly how a Cloze Test should feel. The passage always gives you enough clues; you just have to listen to it the way you listen to your mother.

The Unforgettable Trick — "PEHLE SOCHO, PHIR DEKHO" (Guess First, Options Later):

🧠 Remember it as GPS:
- G — Guess your own word first
- P — Positive or Negative? Match the tone of the passage
- S — Surroundings — check the sentence before and after

If an option passes G, P, and S — mark it and move on. Never re-read a solved blank five times; that is over-thinking, and over-thinking changes right answers into wrong ones.

One More Thing — Grammar is your second detective:

Sometimes the clue is not the story but the grammar. If the blank comes after "has", only a V3 form (gone, taken, eaten) can fit — so "go", "goes", "going" are out in 5 seconds. If the blank comes before a noun, you probably need an adjective, not an adverb. Grammar deletes 2-3 wrong options instantly, and then the story picks the winner.

❓ Question for you:

A passage says: "Although the team worked hard, the results were quite ___." Before reading further, say your own guess out loud. Now choose: (a) disappointing (b) wonderful (c) celebrated (d) exciting. Which one did you pick — and which word in the sentence ("Although") told you the answer?
