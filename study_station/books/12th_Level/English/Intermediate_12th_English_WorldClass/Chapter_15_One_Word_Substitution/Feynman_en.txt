# 🧒 Feynman Technique — One-word Substitution

## The Hardest Concept, Explained to a 12-Year-Old

Imagine your school bag. Inside it you keep a pencil box, a lunch box, a water bottle, and a notebook. Now suppose someone asks you, "What are you carrying?" You don't say, "I am carrying a small container in which I keep pencils, pens, an eraser and a sharpener." You simply say — **"a pencil box."**

One word. Whole meaning. Done.

That is exactly what **One-word Substitution** is. English loves shortcuts. Instead of saying *"a person who speaks many languages,"* English says **polyglot**. Instead of *"a place where dead bodies are kept for identification,"* English says **mortuary**. The exam simply checks: do you know the shortcut word?

## Why Do Students Find It Hard?

Because they try to **memorise 500 separate words** like 500 separate phone numbers. Impossible! But here's the secret: most of these words are built from a small set of **Greek and Latin building blocks** — like Lego pieces.

- **phile** = love → *biblio****phile*** (book-lover), ***philo****sopher* (lover of wisdom)
- **phobia** = fear → *claustro****phobia*** (fear of closed spaces)
- **cide** = kill → *homi****cide*** (killing a man), *insecti****cide*** (killing insects)
- **cracy** = rule → *demo****cracy*** (rule by the people), *auto****cracy*** (rule by one)

So instead of 500 phone numbers, you learn about **30 Lego pieces** — and suddenly you can *guess* words you have never seen before!

## A Daily-Life Example

Your friend Rahul is afraid of water. You could say: "Rahul has an extreme fear of water." Or one word: Rahul has **hydrophobia** (*hydro* = water + *phobia* = fear). Now guess: fear of heights? *Acro* (height) + *phobia* = **acrophobia**. You just created an exam answer yourself!

## The Unforgettable Trick 🎯

**"Break it, don't cram it."**
Whenever you see a long one-word substitution, break it into parts and ask: *Which Lego pieces are hiding inside?*
- **Somn**ambulist → *somn* = sleep (remember "insomnia" = can't sleep) + *ambul* = walk (remember "ambulance" moves) → **one who walks in sleep**. You'll never forget it.

## ⚠️ The Trap to Avoid

Examiners give options that *sound* similar: **epidemic / endemic / pandemic**. Don't panic — break them:
- *epi-* = upon → disease spreading **upon** many people in a region = **epidemic**
- *en-* = within → disease regularly found **within** a particular area = **endemic**
- *pan-* = all → disease spread over **all** countries = **pandemic**

## 📝 Blurting Sheet Instruction

Close this book. Take a blank page. In **3 minutes**, write down every root you remember (phile, phobia, cide, cracy, somn, ambul, hydro, pan…) and **one word made from each**. Then open the book and check what you missed. Whatever you missed — that's your real weak point, not the whole chapter.

**Now answer this without looking back:** *A person who loves mankind and works for humanity is called a ______?* (Hint: *phil* = love + *anthropos* = mankind.)
