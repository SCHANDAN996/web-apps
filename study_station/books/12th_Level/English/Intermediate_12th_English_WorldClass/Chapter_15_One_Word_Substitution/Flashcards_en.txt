Card 1
Front: One who loves mankind?
Back: Philanthropist (Greek: philos = love, anthropos = man). Mnemonic: "Phil loves Anthony and all people."

Card 2
Front: One who hates mankind?
Back: Misanthropist (Greek: misein = to hate). Trap: Don't confuse with "misogynist" — one who hates women.

Card 3
Front: A government by the people?
Back: Democracy (Greek: demos = people, kratos = rule/power).

Card 4
Front: One who can use both hands equally well?
Back: Ambidextrous (Latin: ambi = both, dexter = right hand). Mnemonic: "Ambi = both, as in ambiguous (both meanings possible)."

Card 5
Front: A word or law no longer in use?
Back: Obsolete. Trap: "Outdated" is close, but the standard one-word answer in exams is "obsolete".

Card 6
Front: One who speaks two languages?
Back: Bilingual (Latin: bi = two, lingua = tongue/language). One who speaks many languages = Polyglot.

Card 7
Front: Murder of one's own brother?
Back: Fratricide (Latin: frater = brother, cide = killing). Compare: homicide (man), patricide (father), matricide (mother), regicide (king).

Card 8
Front: One who believes in God?
Back: Theist. Opposite: Atheist — one who does not believe in God. Trap: "Agnostic" = one who is unsure whether God exists — examiners swap these two.

Card 9
Front: A list of books available in a library?
Back: Catalogue.

Card 10
Front: One who eats human flesh?
Back: Cannibal. One who eats everything = Omnivorous; only plants = Herbivorous; only flesh = Carnivorous.

Card 11
Front: That which cannot be read?
Back: Illegible (Latin: in = not, legere = to read). Trap: "Inaudible" = cannot be heard; "invisible" = cannot be seen. Match the root to the sense.

Card 12
Front: A place where dead bodies are kept for identification?
Back: Mortuary. Trap: "Morgue" is also correct, but "mortuary" is the standard exam answer; "cemetery" = burial ground.

Card 13
Front: One who looks at the bright side of things?
Back: Optimist. Opposite: Pessimist — one who looks at the dark side. Mnemonic: "O for Optimist = O for Opportunity."

Card 14
Front: Government by the rich?
Back: Plutocracy (Greek: ploutos = wealth). Compare: aristocracy (nobles), bureaucracy (officials), autocracy (one ruler).

Card 15
Front: A speech delivered without preparation?
Back: Extempore (also: Impromptu). Trap: "Maiden speech" = first speech, not unprepared speech — a classic examiner swap.

Card 16
Front: One who lives in solitude / lives alone for religious reasons?
Back: Hermit (also: Recluse). Mnemonic: "Hermit = He Remains In Total isolation."

Card 17
Front: That which cannot be avoided?
Back: Inevitable. Related: incorrigible (cannot be corrected), invincible (cannot be defeated), indelible (cannot be erased).

Card 18
Front: The scientific study of birds?
Back: Ornithology (Greek: ornis = bird, logos = study). Trap: "Entomology" = study of insects; "Etymology" = study of word origins — examiners love this pair.

Card 19
Front: One who pretends to be what he is not?
Back: Hypocrite. Related: impostor (pretends to be someone else), sycophant (flatters for gain).

Card 20
Front: A person who collects stamps?
Back: Philatelist (Greek: philos = love, ateleia = exemption from tax — stamps showed postage was prepaid). Mnemonic: "Phil loves little stamps."
