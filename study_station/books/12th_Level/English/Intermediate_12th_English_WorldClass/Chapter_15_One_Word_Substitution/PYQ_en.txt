# 📊 PYQ Analysis — One-word Substitution

## How Exams Test This Chapter

One-word substitution is one of the most **reliable, high-frequency** vocabulary areas across SSC (CGL, CHSL, MTS, GD), Banking (IBPS, SBI), Railways (RRB NTPC, Group D), and State exams. The pattern is remarkably stable:

- **Direct recall questions dominate.** The examiner gives a phrase ("A person who speaks many languages") and asks for the single word ("Polyglot"). These are pure memory questions — you either know the word or you don't.
- **Root-based questions are rising.** Instead of a plain definition, the question tests whether you can decode a word from its Greek/Latin parts (e.g., "bio" + "graphy").
- **Confusing-pair traps are the examiner's favourite weapon.** Options are built from words that *sound similar* or *mean almost the same*: Homicide vs. Fratricide vs. Regicide; Optimist vs. Pessimist; Autobiography vs. Biography.
- **Negative/awkward phrasing traps.** "One who does NOT believe in God" — students who skim miss the "not" and pick "Theist" instead of "Atheist".
- **Context-sentence format (growing trend).** A sentence with a blank: "A person who eats human flesh is called a ______." This tests the same words but in sentence form.

**Sub-topics that appear again and again:** words for "killing" (-cide family), "fear" (-phobia family), "government/rule" (-cracy family), "people types" (philanthropist, misanthrope, optimist), "places" (apiary, aviary, orphanage), and "writing/speech" (biography, soliloquy, extempore).

---

## The Examiner's Four Trap Types

| Trap Type | What It Looks Like | Defence |
|---|---|---|
| **Sound-Right** | Options differ by one letter/syllable: *Cannibal / Carnivore / Herbivore* | Read the definition word-by-word, not the options first |
| **Intervening Phrase** | Long definition hides the key word: "A person, who in spite of being trained, does not believe in the existence of God" | Underline the core: "does not believe in God" → Atheist |
| **Near-Synonym Confusion** | *Epitaph vs. Epilogue vs. Epigram* — all start with "epi-" | Anchor each word to its root meaning |
| **Over-thinking** | Simple definition, but you reject the obvious answer | If the word matches the definition exactly, mark it and move on |

## Top 10 High-Yield Questions (with Solutions)

**Q1.** A person who speaks many languages.
(a) Bilingual (b) Polyglot (c) Multilingual speaker (d) Linguist
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** "Poly" = many, "glot" = tongue/language → Polyglot. Trap: (d) Linguist is a *student of language*, not necessarily a speaker of many. **Trap type: Near-Synonym Confusion.**

**Q2.** The killing of one's brother.
(a) Homicide (b) Fratricide (c) Regicide (d) Patricide
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** "Frater" (Latin) = brother + "cide" = killing. Homicide = killing a human (general), Regicide = killing a king, Patricide = killing one's father. **Trap type: Sound-Right (the -cide family).**

**Q3.** One who does not believe in the existence of God.
(a) Theist (b) Atheist (c) Agnostic (d) Ascetic
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** "A-" = without, "theos" = God. Trap: (c) Agnostic = one who believes God's existence is *unknowable* — a classic near-miss. **Trap type: Near-Synonym Confusion.**

**Q4.** A place where bees are kept.
(a) Aviary (b) Apiary (c) Aquarium (d) Granary
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** "Apis" (Latin) = bee → Apiary. Aviary = birds, Aquarium = fish, Granary = grain. **Trap type: Sound-Right (Aviary vs. Apiary is the most repeated confusion).**

**Q5.** A speech delivered without preparation.
(a) Soliloquy (b) Extempore (c) Monologue (d) Rhetoric
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** Extempore = spoken without prior preparation. Soliloquy = talking to oneself (alone). **Trap type: Near-Synonym Confusion.**

**Q6.** The life story of a person written by himself.
(a) Biography (b) Autobiography (c) Bibliography (d) Memoir
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** "Auto" = self + "bio" = life + "graphy" = writing. Trap: (d) Memoir is a *partial* personal account, not the full life story. **Trap type: Over-thinking — the root decode gives the answer in seconds.**

**Q7.** Government by the people.
(a) Autocracy (b) Democracy (c) Bureaucracy (d) Theocracy
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** "Demos" = people + "kratos" = rule. Autocracy = rule by one, Bureaucracy = rule by officials, Theocracy = rule by religious law. **Trap type: Sound-Right (-cracy family).**

**Q8.** One who loves mankind.
(a) Misanthrope (b) Philanthropist (c) Optimist (d) Philosopher
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** "Phil" = love + "anthropos" = mankind. Its exact opposite, Misanthrope (option a), is deliberately placed first to catch skimmers. **Trap type: Intervening Phrase / opposite-pair trap.**

**Q9.** A list of books available in a library.
(a) Catalogue (b) Glossary (c) Index (d) Anthology
**Answer: (a)**
**Solution:** Catalogue = complete list of items/books. Glossary = list of difficult words with meanings; Anthology = collection of poems/stories. **Trap type: Near-Synonym Confusion.**

**Q10.** An animal that lives both on land and in water.
(a) Arboreal (b) Amphibian (c) Aquatic (d) Terrestrial
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** "Amphi" = both + "bios" = life. Arboreal = living in trees. **Trap type: Sound-Right.**

## Applying the 15/45-Second Rule

**15-second question (Fact-based) — Q2 (Fratricide):** The definition is short and maps directly onto one memorised word. Decode the root ("frater" = brother), match, mark, move. Do not re-read the options. Target: **15 seconds.**

**45-second question (Inference-based) — Q3 (Atheist vs. Agnostic):** Here two options are genuinely close. Spend the extra time: (1) underline "does not believe", (2) recall that Agnostic = "cannot be known", not "does not believe", (3) eliminate, then mark. Target: **up to 45 seconds** — this is where accuracy is won.

**Rule of thumb:** If the definition is one line and you know the word → 15 seconds. If two options feel "almost right" → invest 45 seconds in elimination; never guess between near-synonyms in a hurry.

---

## Preparation Takeaway

Master the **five word-families** (-cide, -phobia, -cracy, -graphy, -ist people words) and the **top confusing pairs** (Apiary/Aviary, Atheist/Agnostic, Biography/Autobiography, Optimist/Pessimist, Cannibal/Carnivore). These cover the bulk of what examiners recycle year after year.
