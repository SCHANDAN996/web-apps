# 📋 Important Rules & Tips — One-word Substitution

## (a) 12 High-Yield Rules

| # | Rule | Example | Examiner's Trap |
|---|------|---------|-----------------|
| 1 | Learn the **root**, not just the word — one root unlocks 5–10 substitutions. | *Anthropos* = man → anthropology, philanthropy, misanthrope | Options often pair two words from the same root with opposite prefixes (philanthropist vs misanthropist). Check the prefix! |
| 2 | **-cide** always means "killing" — check *what* is killed. | Homicide (man), fratricide (brother), regicide (king), infanticide (infant) | "Killing of one's mother" = matricide, NOT patricide (father). |
| 3 | **-phobia** = irrational fear; **-phile/-philia** = love. Don't mix them. | Claustrophobia (fear of closed spaces) vs Bibliophile (lover of books) | "Fear of water" = hydrophobia; "love of wisdom" = philosophy. Exams swap these deliberately. |
| 4 | **-cracy** = rule/government by. Identify *who* rules. | Democracy (people), autocracy (one person), bureaucracy (officials), theocracy (religious leaders) | "Rule by the rich" = plutocracy, NOT aristocracy (rule by nobility). |
| 5 | **-logy** = study of; **-grapher/-graphy** = writing/recording. | Biology (study of life) vs Biography (written life story) | "One who writes for another" = ghostwriter; "study of handwriting" = graphology. |
| 6 | **Ambi-** = both; **Omni-** = all. | Ambidextrous (using both hands), omnipotent (all-powerful), omniscient (all-knowing) | Omnipresent (present everywhere) vs omniscient (knowing everything) — classic confusion pair. |
| 7 | **-ist** = a person; **-ism** = a belief/doctrine. | Egoist (person) vs Egoism (doctrine); Optimist vs Optimism | If the question says "a person who…", the answer must end in -ist/-er/-or, not -ism. |
| 8 | **Post-** = after; **Ante-/Pre-** = before. | Postmortem (examination after death), antenatal (before birth) | "Postscript" (P.S.) = written after; "preamble" = before the main text. |
| 9 | **-arium / -ary / -ory** endings often denote a *place*. | Apiary (bees), aviary (birds), aquarium (fish), granary (grain) | Apiary (bees) vs aviary (birds) — one letter apart, both frequent in exams. |
| 10 | **Killing of self** = suicide; **killing of a human** = homicide; mercy killing = euthanasia. | Euthanasia = painless killing to end suffering | Euthanasia is NOT "murder" — the element of mercy/consent is the key. |
| 11 | **-gamy** = marriage. Count the number. | Monogamy (one), bigamy (two), polygamy (many) | Bigamy (two spouses, a crime) vs polygamy (many) — exams test the exact number. |
| 12 | A substitution must be **one word** and match the *exact* shade of meaning. | "That which cannot be avoided" = inevitable (not "unavoidable-looking" phrases) | Two options may both be single words — pick the one matching the precise meaning (e.g., "cannot be corrected" = incorrigible, not irreparable, when about a person's habits). |

## (b) Time Management — 15/45-Second Rule

| Type | What it looks like | Time | Strategy |
|------|-------------------|------|----------|
| **Fact-based (15 sec)** | Direct recall: "One who loves books" → Bibliophile | 15 sec | Answer instantly if you know it. If the root is familiar, decode it in 10 sec. Never re-read the options twice. |
| **Inference-based (45 sec)** | Long definition with a twist: "A person who pretends to have more knowledge than he actually has" → Charlatan | 45 sec | Break the definition into parts (person? act? place? belief?), match each part with the root, then eliminate. |
| **Skip signal** | All four options are unfamiliar words with no recognisable root | — | Mark and move on. Return only if time remains. One unknown OWS is never worth 2 minutes. |

**Rule of thumb:** If you can spot a known root (cide, phobia, cracy, logy, gamy) in the options, the question is 15-second. If the definition is abstract (human behaviour, attitudes, pretence), budget 45 seconds.

## (c) Morphology — Root + Affix = Answer

| Root / Affix | Meaning | Derived One-word Substitutions |
|--------------|---------|-------------------------------|
| *Anthropos* (Greek) | man, mankind | Anthropology, philanthropy, misanthrope |
| *Cide* (Latin: caedere) | killing | Homicide, suicide, regicide, genocide |
| *Phobos* (Greek) | fear | Hydrophobia, claustrophobia, xenophobia |
| *Philos* (Greek) | love | Philanthropy, bibliophile, philosophy |
| *Kratos* (Greek) | rule, power | Democracy, autocracy, plutocracy |
| *Logos* (Greek) | study, word | Biology, zoology, etymology |
| *Gamos* (Greek) | marriage | Monogamy, bigamy, polygamy |
| *Omni* (Latin) | all | Omnipotent, omniscient, omnipresent |
| *Ambi* (Latin) | both | Ambidextrous, ambiguous, ambivalent |
| *Mort* (Latin) | death | Postmortem, immortal, mortuary |
| *-ist / -ism* (suffix) | person / doctrine | Optimist–optimism, egoist–egoism |
| *-arium / -ary* (suffix) | place for | Apiary, aviary, granary, library |

**Decoding formula:** Definition → split into (WHO/WHAT + ACTION) → find root for each part → combine. Example: "fear (phobia) of foreigners (xeno)" → **Xenophobia**.
