🧒 FEYNMAN TECHNIQUE — Sentence Improvement (Advanced)

Explain it like you're 12: "Why does the sentence break?"

Imagine a sentence is a bicycle. It has two wheels that MUST match in size:

- Front wheel = SUBJECT (who is doing it)
- Back wheel = VERB (what is being done)

If the front wheel is small (one person — singular) and the back wheel is big (plural verb), the bicycle flips. This subject–verb mismatch is the single most common error tested in sentence improvement questions — the examiner just hides the wheels from you!

🎯 The Hardest Concept: The Intervening Phrase Trap

Look at this sentence:

"The box of chocolates ARE on the table."

Sounds right? Your ear heard "chocolates" (plural) just before the verb, so "are" feels natural. But the real subject is "box" (singular). The phrase "of chocolates" is just a passenger sitting between the two wheels — it doesn't pedal the bicycle!

Correct: "The box of chocolates IS on the table."

🍫 Daily-Life Example

Think of a school assembly line:
"The line of students moves slowly."

Who is moving? Not "students" as the subject of the sentence — the LINE is moving. The students are just standing inside the line. Same trick:

- "A bunch of keys WAS found." (bunch = subject, not keys)
- "The quality of mangoes IS good." (quality = subject, not mangoes)

🔑 The Unforgettable Trick: "COVER THE MIDDLE"

Whenever you see a long sentence, COVER everything between the subject and the verb with your finger:

"The captain, along with his teammates, [cover this] ___ playing well."

Now read: "The captain ___ playing well." → Obviously "IS playing"! The phrase "along with his teammates" is a trap passenger. Phrases like along with, as well as, together with, in addition to, accompanied by NEVER change the subject.

🧠 Memory Hook: "AS-WELL-AS = AS GOOD AS ABSENT"

If "as well as" appears in a sentence, pretend those words are invisible. The verb only obeys the FIRST subject.

"Rohan as well as his friends WAS invited." ✓ (Rohan = singular)

⚡ One More Hard Nut: "One of the + plural noun + WHO/WHICH/THAT + plural verb"

"One of the boys who HAVE passed is my friend."

Confusing? Ask: WHO passed? → the BOYS (all of them). So "have" is correct. But the main verb "is" belongs to "One". Two different verbs, two different masters!
