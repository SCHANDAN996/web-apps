Card 1
Front: What is the first step in solving any Sentence Improvement question?
Back: Read the whole sentence first, identify the underlined part, and check what grammar rule it tests (tense, subject-verb agreement, parallelism, idiom, etc.) — never jump to options before diagnosing the error.

Card 2
Front: "One of my friends ___ going abroad." — is or are?
Back: is. After "one of + plural noun," the verb agrees with "one" (singular), not the plural noun. Trap: the plural noun right before the verb misleads you.

Card 3
Front: What is the rule for "Neither… nor" with two different subjects?
Back: The verb agrees with the subject closer to it (proximity rule). "Neither the teacher nor the students were present." / "Neither the students nor the teacher was present."

Card 4
Front: MNEMONIC — How do you remember verbs that always take a gerund (V-ing), not an infinitive?
Back: "MAD FISH P": Mind, Avoid, Deny, Finish, Imagine, Suggest, Hate, Practise. Note: "hate" usually takes a gerund for general habits ("I hate swimming"); it can also take an infinitive for a specific occasion ("I hate to disturb you"). The others in the list take only the gerund: "I enjoy swimming," NOT "I enjoy to swim."

Card 5
Front: EXAMINER'S TRAP — "The quality of the apples were good." What's wrong?
Back: The subject is "quality" (singular), not "apples." Correct: "The quality of the apples was good." This is the Intervening Phrase trap — a plural noun between subject and verb.

Card 6
Front: When do we use "fewer" and when "less"?
Back: "Fewer" for countable nouns (fewer students, fewer books); "less" for uncountable nouns (less water, less time). Trap: "less people" is wrong — say "fewer people."

Card 7
Front: What is parallelism in sentence improvement?
Back: Items in a list or comparison must be in the same grammatical form. Wrong: "She likes reading, swimming, and to dance." Right: "She likes reading, swimming, and dancing."

Card 8
Front: "He is senior ___ me." — than or to?
Back: to. The Latin comparative adjectives — senior, junior, superior, inferior, prior — take "to," never "than." The verb "prefer" also takes "to": "I prefer tea to coffee."

Card 9
Front: MNEMONIC — How to remember the words that take "to" instead of "than"?
Back: "SJ SIP": Senior, Junior, Superior, Inferior, Prior — all comparative adjectives taking "to." Remember separately that the verb "prefer" also takes "to" ("prefer X to Y"), though it is not a comparative adjective.

Card 10
Front: EXAMINER'S TRAP — "No sooner did he arrive when the show started." Correct it.
Back: "No sooner" pairs with "than," not "when." Correct: "No sooner did he arrive than the show started." Similarly, "Hardly/Scarcely" pair with "when."

Card 11
Front: Which tense follows "It is high time"?
Back: Past simple (subjunctive sense): "It is high time we left." NOT "It is high time we leave."

Card 12
Front: "Each of the boys ___ a book." — has or have?
Back: has. "Each of," "every one of," "either of," "neither of" always take a singular verb.

Card 13
Front: When does a collective noun take a plural verb?
Back: When the members act individually/divided: "The jury were divided in their opinion." When acting as one unit, singular: "The jury has given its verdict."

Card 14
Front: What is wrong with "He is more taller than his brother"?
Back: Double comparative. Use either "taller" or "more tall" (never both). Correct: "He is taller than his brother." Same rule: no "most best," "more better."

Card 15
Front: "Unless you work hard, you will not pass." — why is "Unless you do not work hard" wrong?
Back: "Unless" already means "if not" — adding another "not" creates a double negative. Never use "not" with unless.

Card 16
Front: Which is correct: "I, you and he" or "You, he and I"?
Back: For positive/confession-neutral statements: 2nd person → 3rd → 1st ("You, he and I will go"). For admitting faults: 1st → 2nd → 3rd ("I, you and he are guilty").

Card 17
Front: What does "Sound-Right trap" mean in sentence improvement?
Back: An option that sounds natural in spoken/Hindi-influenced English but is grammatically wrong — e.g., "What you are doing?" instead of "What are you doing?" Trust the rule, not your ear.

Card 18
Front: "The news ___ shocking." — is or are?
Back: is. News, physics, mathematics, politics, measles look plural but take singular verbs.

Card 19
Front: When should you choose "No improvement required"?
Back: Only after checking the underlined part against all grammar rules and confirming every other option introduces an error or changes the meaning. Don't pick it just because the sentence "sounds fine."

Card 20
Front: What is the 15/45-second rule for sentence improvement?
Back: Fact-based questions (fixed rules: subject-verb agreement, idioms, prepositions) should take ~15 seconds. Inference-based ones (best-fit meaning, tone, complex restructuring) may take up to 45 seconds. If stuck beyond that, mark and move on.
