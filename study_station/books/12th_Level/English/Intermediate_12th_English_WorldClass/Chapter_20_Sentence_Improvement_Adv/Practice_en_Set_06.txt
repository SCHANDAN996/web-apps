126. Choose the best improvement for the underlined part: "No sooner did the bell ring when the students rushed out."
(a) then the students rushed out (b) as the students rushed out (c) No improvement (d) than the students rushed out
Answer: (d)
Solution: "No sooner" is always paired with "than", never "when" — the tempting option "then" is wrong because "then" is an adverb of time, not a correlative conjunction. The correct pair is "No sooner…than", so the answer is "than the students rushed out".

127. Choose the best improvement for the underlined part: "He is one of the best players that has ever played for India."
(a) who has ever played (b) that have ever played (c) which has ever played (d) No improvement
Answer: (b)
Solution: In "one of the + plural noun + relative clause", the verb in the relative clause agrees with the plural noun ("players"), so it must be "have". "Has" sounds right because of the nearby "one", which is the classic trap. The correct form is "that have ever played".

Answer: (d)
Solution: "Unless" already carries a negative meaning ("if…not"), so adding "not" creates a double negative. "Until" refers to time, not condition, so it is wrong here. The correct form is "Unless you work hard".

Answer: (a)
Solution: "Prefer" is a Latin comparative and takes "to", not "than" or "more than". "Over" is informal and not accepted in standard exam English. The correct form is "tea to coffee".
