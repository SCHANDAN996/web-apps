101. Choose the best replacement for the underlined part: The manager, as well as his assistants, **were present** at the meeting.
(a) were presents (b) No improvement (c) have been present (d) was present
Answer: (d)
Solution: The tempting trap is to keep the plural verb because "assistants" stands right before it, but with "as well as" the verb agrees with the first subject, "the manager", which is singular. Hence "was present" is correct, so the answer is was present.
Source: PYQ-style

102. Choose the best replacement: Scarcely **he had entered** the room when the lights went off.
(a) had he entered (b) No improvement (c) he entered (d) he has entered
Answer: (a)
Solution: After negative adverbs like "scarcely", "hardly" and "no sooner" at the start of a clause, inversion is required: auxiliary before subject. "Had he entered" gives the correct inverted structure, so the answer is had he entered.
Source: PYQ-style

103. Choose the best replacement: One of my friends **are going** to Delhi tomorrow.
(a) were going (b) is going (c) have gone (d) No improvement
Answer: (b)
Solution: "One of + plural noun" always takes a singular verb because the real subject is "one". The plural "friends" misleads many candidates into choosing a plural verb. "Is going" is correct, so the answer is is going.
Source: NCERT-based grammar rule

104. Choose the best replacement: He **denied to help** me in my difficulty.
(a) denied helping (b) denied for helping (c) refused to help (d) No improvement
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Deny" is followed by a gerund (denied helping), not an infinitive, but the intended meaning here is refusal, which naturally takes "to help". "Refused to help" is the idiomatic and correct choice, so the answer is refused to help.
Source: PYQ-style

105. Choose the best replacement: Unless you **do not work** hard, you will not pass.
(a) will not work (b) work (c) did not work (d) No improvement
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Unless" already carries a negative meaning, so adding "not" creates a double negative — a classic examiner trap. The correct clause is "Unless you work hard", so the answer is work.
Source: PYQ-style

106. Choose the best replacement: The teacher asked the students that why **they were late**.
(a) why were they late (b) that why they are late (c) why they were late (d) No improvement
Answer: (c)
Solution: In indirect speech, "that" and the wh-word are not used together, and the clause takes statement order, not question order. "Why they were late" satisfies both rules, so the answer is why they were late.
Source: PYQ-style

107. Choose the best replacement: I **am living** in this city since 2015.
(a) have been living (b) was living (c) had been living (d) No improvement
Answer: (a)
Solution: An action that began in the past and continues up to the present, marked by "since 2015", needs the present perfect continuous. "Have been living" expresses this correctly, so the answer is have been living.
Source: NCERT Class 10 English grammar

108. Choose the best replacement: No sooner did the bell ring **when** the students rushed out.
(a) then (b) No improvement (c) as (d) than
Answer: (d)
Solution: The fixed correlative pair is "no sooner … than", while "scarcely/hardly" pair with "when". Mixing the two is a frequent trap. "Than" is correct, so the answer is than.
Source: PYQ-style

109. Choose the best replacement: He is **more wiser** than his brother.
(a) most wiser (b) more wise (c) much wiser (d) No improvement
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Wiser" is already comparative, so adding "more" makes a double comparative. "Much" can intensify a comparative, giving "much wiser", so the answer is much wiser.
Source: PYQ-style

110. Choose the best replacement: The Ganges **are** one of the longest rivers of India.
(a) is (b) were (c) have been (d) No improvement
Answer: (a)
Solution: Names of rivers, though plural in form, take a singular verb when treated as one entity. "The Ganges is" is standard usage, so the answer is is.
Source: NCERT-based grammar rule

111. Choose the best replacement: Hardly **I had reached** the station when the train left.
(a) had I reached (b) I have reached (c) I reached (d) No improvement
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Hardly" at the beginning of a clause demands inversion, just like "scarcely" and "no sooner". The correct form is "had I reached", so the answer is had I reached.
Source: PYQ-style

112. Choose the best replacement: She insisted **to go** with me to the market.
(a) in going (b) for going (c) on going (d) No improvement
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Insist" is followed by the preposition "on" plus a gerund, not by an infinitive. "On going" is the correct pattern, so the answer is on going.
Source: PYQ-style

113. Choose the best replacement: Each of the boys **have done** his homework.
(a) have did (b) has done (c) had did (d) No improvement
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Each of" takes a singular verb even though a plural noun follows. The plural "boys" is the decoy here. "Has done" is correct, so the answer is has done.
Source: NCERT-based grammar rule

114. Choose the best replacement: He **told that** he would come the next day.
(a) said me that (b) told to me that (c) No improvement (d) told me that
Answer: (d)
Solution: "Tell" requires an indirect object (tell somebody), so "told that" without an object is wrong; "said me" is equally wrong because "say" never takes a person as its direct object. "Told me that" supplies the required object, so the answer is told me that.
Source: PYQ-style

115. Choose the best replacement: The principal with his staff members **were** present in the hall.
(a) were being (b) No improvement (c) have been (d) was
Answer: (d)
Solution: With phrases like "with", "along with" and "together with", the verb agrees with the first subject, "the principal", which is singular. The nearby plural "members" is the trap. "Was" is correct, so the answer is was.
Source: PYQ-style

116. Choose the best replacement: I look forward **to meet** you at the conference.
(a) No improvement (b) for meeting (c) to meeting (d) to be meeting
Answer: (c)
Solution: In "look forward to", the word "to" is a preposition, not part of an infinitive, so it must be followed by a gerund. "To meeting" is correct, so the answer is to meeting.
Source: PYQ-style

117. Choose the best replacement: **Being a rainy day**, I stayed at home.
(a) No improvement (b) Being it a rainy day (c) On being a rainy day (d) It being a rainy day
Answer: (d)
Solution: "Being a rainy day" is a dangling modifier because the subject "I" is not a rainy day. The standard correction is to give the phrase its own subject with the absolute construction "It being a rainy day", so the answer is It being a rainy day.
Source: PYQ-style

118. Choose the best replacement: He is one of the best players that **has** ever played for the country.
(a) is (b) had (c) have (d) No improvement
Answer: (c)
Solution: In "one of the + plural noun + relative clause", the relative pronoun refers to the plural noun "players", so the verb must be plural. The singular "one" misleads candidates. "Have" is correct, so the answer is have.
Source: PYQ-style

119. Choose the best replacement: The police **has arrested** the thief.
(a) is arresting (b) have arrested (c) had arrested (d) No improvement
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Police" is a plural noun in English and always takes a plural verb; there is no singular "police" for a force. "Have arrested" is correct, so the answer is have arrested.
Source: NCERT-based grammar rule

120. Choose the best replacement: Neither of the two brothers **have passed** the examination.
(a) has passed (b) No improvement (c) had been passing (d) have been passing
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Neither of" takes a singular verb because it means "not the one and not the other". The plural "brothers" is the decoy. "Has passed" is correct, so the answer is has passed.
Source: PYQ-style

121. Choose the best replacement: He **suggested me to go** to a doctor.
(a) suggested to me going (b) suggested me going (c) No improvement (d) suggested that I should go
Answer: (d)
Solution: "Suggest" is never followed by an object plus infinitive; the correct patterns are "suggest + gerund" or "suggest that + subject + should + verb". "Suggested that I should go" follows the correct pattern, so the answer is suggested that I should go.
Source: PYQ-style

122. Choose the best replacement: The furniture in this room **are** very old.
(a) is (b) were (c) have been (d) No improvement
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Furniture" is an uncountable noun and always takes a singular verb, despite referring to many items — a sound-right trap for Hindi speakers. "Is" is correct, so the answer is is.
Source: NCERT-based grammar rule

123. Choose the best replacement: It is high time we **start** taking climate change seriously.
(a) started (b) have started (c) will start (d) No improvement
Answer: (a)
Solution: After "It is high time", English uses the simple past to express a present necessity — an unreal past. The present tense "start" sounds logical but is ungrammatical here. "Started" is correct, so the answer is started.
Source: PYQ-style

124. Choose the best replacement: Walking through the park, **the flowers looked beautiful**.
(a) the flowers were looking beautiful (b) I saw the flowers looking beautiful (c) the beauty of the flowers was visible (d) No improvement
Answer: (b)
Solution: The opening participial phrase must modify the person walking, but "the flowers" cannot walk — a dangling modifier. Only the option beginning with "I" gives the phrase a proper subject, so the answer is I saw the flowers looking beautiful.
Source: PYQ-style

125. Choose the best replacement: He **is working** in this factory for the last ten years.
(a) was working (b) has been working (c) had been working (d) No improvement
Answer: (b)
Solution: "For the last ten years" marks an action continuing from the past into the present, which requires the present perfect continuous, not the simple present continuous. "Has been working" is correct, so the answer is has been working.
Source: PYQ-style
