# Sentence Improvement (Advanced)

## Why This Chapter Decides Your Score

In almost every SSC, Banking and State exam, 3–5 questions ask you to replace an underlined part of a sentence with a better option. These are not "vocabulary" questions — they test whether you can spot a hidden grammar fault in under 30 seconds. The cruel part? Three of the four options usually *sound* acceptable when read aloud. The examiner is not testing your ear; he is testing your knowledge of rules. This chapter gives you those rules — and the traps built around them.

**Learning Objectives**
By the end of this chapter you will be able to:
1. Identify the exact grammar rule being tested in any improvement question.
2. Eliminate two wrong options within seconds using structural clues.
3. Avoid the "sounds right" trap that fools most candidates.
4. Apply the 12 Golden Rules to subject-verb agreement, tense, modifiers, parallelism and idiomatic usage.

---

## Part 1: How Sentence Improvement Questions Work

A sentence is given with one part underlined (or in bold). You must choose the option that improves it. If the sentence is already correct, you choose "No improvement". The golden principle: **an improvement must fix an error — it must never merely rephrase a correct sentence.**

Read the original sentence first and ask: *Is anything actually wrong here?* Many students waste time "improving" a sentence that was already perfect. If the underlined part obeys all rules, "No improvement" is the answer — and examiners use this option far more often than students expect.

## Part 2: The 12 Golden Rules

**Rule 1 — Subject-Verb Agreement with Intervening Phrases.**
The verb agrees with the real subject, not the noun sitting next to it.
*The quality of the apples **was** good.* (Not "were" — the subject is "quality".)

**Rule 2 — Collective Nouns.**
Team, committee, jury, family, audience take a singular verb when acting as one unit, plural when members act separately.
*The jury **has** given its verdict.* / *The jury **are** divided in their opinion.*

**Rule 3 — Each, Every, Either, Neither, Everyone.**
These are always singular.
*Each of the boys **has** a pen.* / *Neither of the answers **is** correct.*

**Rule 4 — "One of the + plural noun + singular verb".**
*One of my friends **is** a doctor.* But watch the relative clause: *He is one of those boys who **are** honest.* (Here "who" refers to "boys".)

**Rule 5 — Tense Consistency.**
A sentence should not shift tense without reason.
Wrong: *He went to the market and buys vegetables.* → *He went to the market and **bought** vegetables.*

**Rule 6 — Since vs For.**
"Since" + point of time (Monday, 2010, childhood); "for" + period of time (two years, a long time).
*I have lived here **since** 2015.* / *I have lived here **for** ten years.*

**Rule 7 — Present Perfect with "since/for" — never simple past.**
Wrong: *I am living here since 2018.* / *I lived here since 2018.*
Right: *I **have been living** here since 2018.*

**Rule 8 — Parallelism.**
Items in a list must share the same grammatical form.
Wrong: *She likes reading, swimming, and to dance.*
Right: *She likes reading, swimming, and dancing.*

**Rule 9 — Misplaced and Dangling Modifiers.**
A modifier must sit next to what it describes.
Wrong: *Walking down the road, a snake bit him.* (Was the snake walking?)
Right: *Walking down the road, **he** was bitten by a snake.*

**Rule 10 — Comparisons must be logical and parallel.**
Wrong: *The population of India is greater than China.* (Population vs China!)
Right: *The population of India is greater than **that of** China.*

**Rule 11 — Idiomatic prepositions are fixed.**
Different **from** (not "than"), prefer X **to** Y (not "than"), superior **to**, junior **to**, abide **by**, insist **on**, capable **of**, good **at**.

**Rule 12 — No double comparatives / double negatives.**
Wrong: *more better*, *most fastest*, *He does not know nothing.*
Right: *better*, *fastest*, *He does not know anything.*

🧠 **Active Recall:** Without looking back, state the rule for (a) "one of the + plural noun", (b) "since vs for", (c) comparisons between two things belonging to different owners.

## Part 3: The Elimination Method

When you cannot spot the error immediately, work backwards from the options:

1. **Find what changes across the options.** If three options use "was" and one uses "were", the question is testing subject-verb agreement. The options themselves reveal the rule being tested.
2. **Apply the rule mechanically.** Locate the true subject; check the tense timeline; check the preposition.
3. **Read the full sentence with your chosen option.** Only after the rule check — never before.

This method converts a "feeling" question into a mechanical checklist. Toppers do not have better ears; they have better checklists.

**Mnemonic — "S-T-P-M-C" (Sentences Test Patient Minds Carefully):**
**S**ubject-verb agreement → **T**ense → **P**reposition/Parallelism → **M**odifier → **C**omparison. Run these five checks in order on every question.

🧠 **Active Recall:** What do the five letters of S-T-P-M-C stand for? Why do we check the options before applying rules?

## Part 4: Advanced Traps — Where Toppers Slip

**Trap A: The Intervening Phrase.**
*The rise in the prices of essential commodities **has** (not have) worried the government.*
The examiner inserts a long phrase between subject and verb so your ear picks the nearest noun ("commodities").

**Trap B: The Sound-Right Option.**
*He is more cleverer than his brother.* Many students "feel" this is fine because they hear it in speech. The rule (no double comparative) says otherwise. **Trust the rule, not the ear.**

**Trap C: Over-thinking a Correct Sentence.**
When the original is correct, nervous candidates pick a fancier option. Remember: longer or more formal ≠ better. Improvement requires an actual error.

> ⚠️ **EXAMINER'S TRAP BOX**
> 1. **Intervening Phrase Trap:** "The captain, along with his teammates, ___ (was/were) felicitated." → *was* (subject = captain; "along with..." doesn't make it plural).
> 2. **Sound-Right Trap:** "Despite of the rain..." sounds natural to many Hindi speakers, but correct English is "Despite the rain" or "In spite of the rain".
> 3. **No-Improvement Trap:** A perfectly correct sentence is dressed up with three attractive alternatives. If no rule is broken, choose "No improvement" confidently.

## Part 5: Fixed Expressions You Must Memorise

Some improvements are pure idiom — no logic will derive them:

| Wrong | Right |
|---|---|
| different than | different **from** |
| prefer tea than coffee | prefer tea **to** coffee |
| superior than | superior **to** |
| despite of | despite / in spite of |
| discuss about | discuss |
| comprise of | comprise / be composed of |
| return back | return |
| repeat again | repeat |
| enter into the room | enter the room |
| cope up with | cope with |

🧠 **Active Recall:** Correct these: (a) He entered into the room. (b) She is senior than me. (c) We discussed about the plan.

## 🔁 Linguistic Bridge — Hindi से English

| हम हिंदी सोच में कहते हैं | गलत English | सही English |
|---|---|---|
| "मैं यहाँ 2018 से रह रहा हूँ" | I am living here since 2018. | I **have been living** here since 2018. |
| "उसने मुझसे शादी की" | He married **with** me. | He married me. (no preposition) |
| "मैंने उसे समझाया" | I explained **him** the matter. | I explained the matter **to him**. |
| "वह मुझसे बड़ा है" | He is elder **than** me. / bigger than me | He is elder **to** me. / older than me |
| "मेरे पास कोई पैसा नहीं है" | I don't have **no** money. | I don't have **any** money. |
| "कल मैं दिल्ली जा रहा हूँ (निश्चित योजना)" | I will go to Delhi tomorrow. | I **am going** to Delhi tomorrow. |

## Summary Table

| Checkpoint | Quick Test | Classic Example |
|---|---|---|
| Subject-Verb | Find the real subject | The quality of apples **was** good |
| Tense | One timeline per sentence | went … **bought** |
| Since/For | Point vs period | since 2015 / for 5 years |
| Parallelism | Same form in lists | reading, swimming, **dancing** |
| Modifier | Who is doing the action? | Walking…, **he** saw a snake |
| Comparison | Compare like with like | greater than **that of** China |
| Idiom | Memorise, don't derive | different **from**, prefer **to** |
| No improvement | Is anything actually wrong? | If not — leave it alone |
