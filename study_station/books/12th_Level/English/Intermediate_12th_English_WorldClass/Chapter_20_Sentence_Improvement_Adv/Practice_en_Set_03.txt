51. Choose the correct improvement: "He is more wiser than his brother."
(a) wiser (b) more wise (c) most wise (d) No improvement
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Wiser" is already a comparative adjective, so adding "more" creates a double comparative — the tempting trap. The correct form is simply "wiser", so the answer is (a).
Source: NCERT Class 9 English Grammar

52. Choose the correct improvement: "She insisted to go with me."
(a) in going (b) on going (c) for going (d) No improvement
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Insist" is always followed by the preposition "on" plus a gerund, not the infinitive "to go". Hence "on going" is correct, so the answer is (b).

53. Choose the correct improvement: "Unless you do not work hard, you will fail."
(a) No improvement (b) Until you do not work hard (c) If you do not work hard not (d) Unless you work hard
Answer: (d)
Solution: "Unless" itself carries a negative meaning, so using "not" with it makes a double negative — a classic examiner trap. The correct phrase is "Unless you work hard", so the answer is (d).

54. Choose the correct improvement: "I have been living in Delhi since five years."
(a) from five years (b) No improvement (c) since five years ago (d) for five years
Answer: (d)
Solution: "Since" is used with a point of time (since 2019), while a period of time takes "for". "Five years" is a period, so the answer is (d).

55. Choose the correct improvement: "He denied to help me."
(a) refused to help (b) denied for helping (c) denied helping (d) No improvement
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Deny" is followed by a gerund, not an infinitive; "denied to help" is a common Hindi-speaker error. "Refused to help" changes the meaning, so the answer is (c).

56. Choose the correct improvement: "The teacher asked that why he was late."
(a) asked that why was he late (b) asked why he was late (c) asked why was he late (d) No improvement
Answer: (b)
Solution: In indirect speech "asked" is not followed by "that" before a wh-word, and the clause uses statement order (he was), not question order (was he). The correct form is "asked why he was late", so the answer is (b).

57. Choose the correct improvement: "No sooner did he enter the room when the phone rang."
(a) then the phone rang (b) No improvement (c) as the phone rang (d) than the phone rang
Answer: (d)
Solution: "No sooner" is always paired with "than", never "when" — a favourite sound-right trap. The correct pairing is "than the phone rang", so the answer is (d).

59. Choose the correct improvement: "I prefer tea more than coffee."
(a) tea to coffee (b) tea than coffee (c) tea over coffee (d) No improvement
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Prefer" already contains the idea of comparison, so "more than" is redundant; the fixed pattern is "prefer X to Y". The correct phrase is "tea to coffee", so the answer is (a).

60. Choose the correct improvement: "The furnitures in this room are new."
(a) The furniture in this room are (b) The furnitures in this room is (c) The furniture in this room is (d) No improvement
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Furniture" is an uncountable noun — it has no plural form and takes a singular verb. The correct sentence is "The furniture in this room is", so the answer is (c).

61. Choose the correct improvement: "Hardly had he reached the station than the train left."
(a) as the train left (b) then the train left (c) when the train left (d) No improvement
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Hardly" and "scarcely" pair with "when", while "no sooner" pairs with "than" — examiners swap these to trap you. The correct phrase is "when the train left", so the answer is (c).

62. Choose the correct improvement: "The number of students in the class are increasing."
(a) is increasing (b) were increasing (c) have increased (d) No improvement
Answer: (a)
Solution: "The number of" takes a singular verb, while "a number of" takes a plural verb — a classic intervening-phrase trap. The correct verb is "is increasing", so the answer is (a).

(a) were presented (b) No improvement (c) are present (d) was present
Answer: (d)
Solution: With "as well as", the verb agrees with the first subject ("He"), not with the nearer noun "friends" — the proximity trap. The correct verb is "was present", so the answer is (d).

67. Choose the correct improvement: "Scarcely he had stepped out when it began to rain."

Answer: (d)
Solution: When a negative adverb like "scarcely" begins a sentence, inversion is required: auxiliary before subject, i.e. "had he stepped". The correct form is "Scarcely had he stepped out", so the answer is (d).

(a) has been working here since 2015 (b) was working here since 2015 (c) is working here from 2015 (d) No improvement
Answer: (a)
Solution: An action that began in the past and continues up to now takes the present perfect continuous with "since/for", not the simple present. The correct form is "has been working here since 2015", so the answer is (a).

(a) had arrested (b) have arrested (c) is arresting (d) No improvement

Solution: "Police" is a plural noun in English and always takes a plural verb — treating it as singular is a common trap. The correct verb is "have arrested", so the answer is (b).

74. Choose the correct improvement: "He is enough tall to reach the shelf."
(a) enough taller (b) tall enough (c) too enough tall (d) No improvement
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Enough" comes before a noun but after an adjective or adverb; "enough tall" reverses this rule. The correct order is "tall enough", so the answer is (b).

Answer: (a)
Solution: When two nouns joined by "and" denote a single dish or idea, the verb is singular — the plural-looking subject is the trap. The correct verb is "is my favourite breakfast", so the answer is (a).
