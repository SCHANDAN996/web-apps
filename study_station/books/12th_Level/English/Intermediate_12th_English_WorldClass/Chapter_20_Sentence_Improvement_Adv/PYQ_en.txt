# 📊 PYQ Analysis — Sentence Improvement (Advanced)

## How Exams Test This Chapter

Sentence Improvement is one of the most dependable scoring areas in SSC CGL, CHSL, MTS, IBPS PO/Clerk, and RRB NTPC. The pattern is stable: a sentence with an underlined (or bold) part, three alternatives, and "No improvement" as the fourth. The tested concepts repeat in cycles — examiners rotate the same grammar fault lines with fresh sentences.

## Recurring Sub-Topics (Pattern Analysis)

Based on the recurring style of questions across SSC and Banking exams, these sub-topics appear again and again:

1. **Tense consistency** — mixing past and present in one sentence; sequence of tenses after "said that", "since", "hardly…when".
2. **Subject–Verb Agreement with traps** — intervening phrases ("The quality of apples *was*…"), collective nouns, "each/everyone/neither of".
3. **Conditional sentences** — "If I were…", "Had he come…", wrong use of "would" in the if-clause.
4. **Parallel structure** — "He likes reading, swimming, and *to dance*" → must be "dancing".
5. **Misplaced/dangling modifiers** — "Walking down the road, the building collapsed" (who was walking?).
6. **Wrong prepositions & phrasal verbs** — "discuss about", "comprise of", "cope up with".
7. **Redundancy** — "return back", "repeat again", "free gift", "consensus of opinion".
8. **Comparatives/superlatives** — "more better", "senior than" (should be "senior to").
9. **Articles & determiners** — "the gold", "a honest man".
10. **Idiomatic replacement** — "by hook or by crook", "to burn the midnight oil" used incorrectly.

## Examiner's Trap Types (Classification Used Below)

- **Intervening Phrase Trap** — extra words between subject and verb hide the real subject.
- **Sound-Right Trap** — the wrong option "sounds natural" to Hindi-influenced ears ("He is more smarter").
- **Over-thinking Trap** — the original sentence is already correct; the answer is "No improvement".
- **Parallelism Trap** — one item in a list breaks the grammatical pattern.
- **Tense-Shift Trap** — the sentence quietly changes timeline mid-way.

## Top 10 High-Yield PYQ-Style Questions with Solutions

**Q1.** The quality of the mangoes *were* not good.
(a) was not good (b) are not good (c) have been not good (d) No improvement
**Answer: (a)**
**Solution:** The subject is "quality" (singular), not "mangoes". The phrase "of the mangoes" only describes it. Hence "was".
**Trap type:** Intervening Phrase Trap.
**Source:** PYQ-style (SSC CGL pattern)

**Q2.** Hardly had he reached the station *than* the train left.
(a) when the train left (b) then the train left (c) the train had left (d) No improvement
**Answer: (a)**
**Solution:** "Hardly/Scarcely" pairs with "when"; "No sooner" pairs with "than". A classic fixed-correlative test.
**Trap type:** Sound-Right Trap ("than" feels natural after a comparative-like word).
**Source:** PYQ-style (SSC CHSL pattern)

**Q3.** If I was the Prime Minister, I would abolish poverty.
(a) If I were (b) If I am (c) If I had been (d) No improvement
**Answer: (a)**
**Solution:** Hypothetical/unreal conditions take "were" for all persons: "If I were…".
**Trap type:** Sound-Right Trap ("was" sounds fine in speech).
**Source:** PYQ-style (Banking exam pattern)

**Q4.** He is senior *than* me by two years.
(a) senior to me (b) senior from me (c) more senior than me (d) No improvement
**Answer: (a)**
**Solution:** Latin comparatives (senior, junior, superior, inferior, prior) take "to", never "than".
**Trap type:** Sound-Right Trap.
**Source:** PYQ-style (SSC CGL pattern)

**Q5.** She returned back the book yesterday.
(a) returned the book (b) returned back the book again (c) was returned the book (d) No improvement
**Answer: (a)**
**Solution:** "Return" already means "give back" — "back" is redundant. Same family: repeat again, recall back.
**Trap type:** Over-thinking Trap in reverse — students hunt for a tense error and miss the redundancy.
**Source:** PYQ-style (SSC MTS pattern)

**Q6.** Walking through the park, the flowers looked beautiful.
(a) the flowers looked beautiful to me as I walked through the park
(b) I saw the flowers looking beautiful
(c) the flowers were looking beautiful
(d) No improvement
**Answer: (a)**
**Solution:** Dangling modifier — "walking" must attach to a person, not "the flowers". Option (a) supplies the walker.
**Trap type:** Over-thinking Trap — students fix the tense instead of the modifier.
**Source:** PYQ-style (IBPS pattern)

**Q7.** Each of the boys *have* done his homework.
(a) has done (b) have been doing (c) are doing (d) No improvement
**Answer: (a)**
**Solution:** "Each of + plural noun" takes a singular verb. The real subject is "Each".
**Trap type:** Intervening Phrase Trap.
**Source:** PYQ-style (RRB NTPC pattern)

**Q8.** He enjoys to swim in the river.
(a) enjoys swimming (b) enjoys for swimming (c) enjoys swim (d) No improvement
**Answer: (a)**
**Solution:** "Enjoy" is followed by a gerund (V-ing), not an infinitive. Same family: avoid, mind, suggest, finish.
**Trap type:** Sound-Right Trap (Hindi: "tairna pasand karta hai" tempts "to swim").
**Source:** PYQ-style (SSC CHSL pattern)

**Q9.** The teacher asked the students that why they were late.
(a) asked the students why they were late
(b) asked the students that why were they late
(c) asked to the students why they were late
(d) No improvement
**Answer: (a)**
**Solution:** In indirect questions, drop "that" and use statement order (why they were, not why were they).
**Trap type:** Sound-Right Trap (direct-question word order feels natural).
**Source:** PYQ-style (SSC CGL pattern)

**Q10.** Unless you do not work hard, you will fail.
(a) Unless you work hard (b) Until you do not work hard (c) If you do not work hard not (d) No improvement
**Answer: (a)**
**Solution:** "Unless" is already negative (= if…not); adding "not" makes a double negative.
**Trap type:** Sound-Right Trap (double negative is common in Hindi speech: "जब तक नहीं…").
**Source:** PYQ-style (Banking pattern)

## Applying the 15/45-Second Rule

**15-second question (Fact-based):** Q2 above. The moment you see "Hardly had…", the rule "Hardly → when" fires instantly. No need to read all options deeply — spot the correlative pair, mark, move on. Target: 15 seconds.

**45-second question (Inference-based):** Q6 above. Dangling-modifier questions need you to (1) identify the introductory phrase, (2) ask "who is doing this action?", (3) check which option supplies the correct doer without creating a new error. This needs careful reading of every option — budget 40–45 seconds.

**Strategy note:** In the exam, do all 15-second rule-based questions first (tense pairs, fixed prepositions, gerund/infinitive lists). Mark modifier and "No improvement" questions for the second pass — "No improvement" is correct often enough that you must verify the original sentence is truly error-free before changing it.
