Passage 1 (Questions 26–30):
Honeybees communicate the location of food sources through a remarkable behaviour called the "waggle dance." When a forager bee discovers flowers rich in nectar, it returns to the hive and performs a figure-eight movement. The angle of the dance indicates the direction of the food relative to the sun, while the duration of the waggle tells other bees how far away the food is. Scientists consider this one of the most sophisticated forms of animal communication known.

26. What is the "waggle dance" used for?
(a) Attracting a mate (b) Warning about predators (c) Communicating the location of food (d) Cooling the hive
Answer: (c)
Solution: The passage states directly that honeybees "communicate the location of food sources" through the waggle dance. Mating and predator warnings are not mentioned anywhere, so the answer is communicating the location of food.
Source: PYQ-style

27. What does the angle of the dance indicate?
(a) The distance of the food (b) The direction of the food relative to the sun (c) The quality of the nectar (d) The number of flowers available
Answer: (b)
Solution: Distance is shown by the duration of the waggle, not the angle — that is the tempting confusion. The passage clearly says the angle indicates direction relative to the sun, so the answer is the direction of the food relative to the sun.
Source: PYQ-style

28. What does the duration of the waggle tell other bees?
(a) Who found the food (b) How sweet the nectar is (c) When the food was found (d) How far away the food is
Answer: (d)
Solution: The passage states that "the duration of the waggle tells other bees how far away the food is." Sweetness, time, and identity of the finder are never mentioned, so the answer is how far away the food is.
Source: PYQ-style

29. The waggle dance is performed in which shape?
(a) A circle (b) A straight line (c) A figure-eight (d) A spiral
Answer: (c)
Solution: The passage says the bee "performs a figure-eight movement." The other shapes are not mentioned, so the answer is a figure-eight.
Source: PYQ-style

30. According to scientists, the waggle dance is —
(a) one of the most sophisticated forms of animal communication (b) a common behaviour among all insects (c) a recently learned behaviour (d) a sign of distress in the hive
Answer: (a)
Solution: The last sentence calls it "one of the most sophisticated forms of animal communication known." The passage never says all insects do it, so the answer is one of the most sophisticated forms of animal communication.
Source: PYQ-style

Passage 2 (Questions 31–35):
The city of Jaipur, founded in 1727 by Maharaja Sawai Jai Singh II, is known as the "Pink City." In 1876, the city was painted pink to welcome the Prince of Wales, as pink was considered the colour of hospitality. Today, Jaipur is part of the Golden Triangle tourist circuit along with Delhi and Agra, and its old city was declared a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 2019.

31. Jaipur was founded in —
(a) 1876 (b) 1727 (c) 2019 (d) 1756
Answer: (b)
Solution: 1876 is the year the city was painted pink, which is the trap for careless readers. The founding year given is 1727, so the answer is 1727.
Source: PYQ-style

32. Who founded Jaipur?
(a) The Prince of Wales (b) Lord Curzon (c) Akbar (d) Maharaja Sawai Jai Singh II
Answer: (d)
Solution: The passage names Maharaja Sawai Jai Singh II as the founder. The Prince of Wales was only a guest in 1876, so the answer is Maharaja Sawai Jai Singh II.
Source: PYQ-style

33. Why was Jaipur painted pink in 1876?
(a) To celebrate a festival (b) To hide damage from a war (c) To welcome the Prince of Wales (d) To mark its founding anniversary
Answer: (c)
Solution: The passage says the city was painted pink "to welcome the Prince of Wales." No festival or war is mentioned, so the answer is to welcome the Prince of Wales.
Source: PYQ-style

34. The Golden Triangle tourist circuit includes Delhi, Agra and —
(a) Jaipur (b) Jodhpur (c) Ajmer (d) Udaipur
Answer: (a)
Solution: The passage lists Jaipur with Delhi and Agra in the Golden Triangle. The other cities are not mentioned, so the answer is Jaipur.
Source: PYQ-style

35. Jaipur's old city became a UNESCO World Heritage Site in —
(a) 2019 (b) 1876 (c) 1727 (d) 2001
Answer: (a)
Solution: The three dates in the passage are a classic trap: 1727 is the founding and 1876 is the painting. The UNESCO declaration year is 2019, so the answer is 2019.

Passage 3 (Questions 36–40):
Ravi had never been good at saving money. Every month, his salary disappeared within two weeks. One day, his grandmother gave him a simple advice: "Before you spend anything, put aside ten rupees out of every hundred." Ravi followed her advice faithfully. Within a year, he was surprised to find that he had saved enough to buy a bicycle he had wanted for years.

36. What was Ravi's problem?
(a) He earned too little (b) He could not save money (c) He spent money on bicycles (d) He disliked his grandmother
Answer: (b)
Solution: The first two sentences say his salary "disappeared within two weeks," meaning he could not save. His income level is never discussed, so the answer is he could not save money.
Source: PYQ-style

37. Who advised Ravi to save?
(a) His father (b) His grandmother (c) His friend (d) His bank manager
Answer: (b)
Solution: The passage says "his grandmother gave him a simple advice." No other adviser is mentioned, so the answer is his grandmother.
Source: PYQ-style

38. What was the grandmother's advice?
(a) Stop buying expensive things (b) Work extra hours (c) Open a bank account (d) Save ten rupees out of every hundred before spending
Answer: (d)
Solution: Her exact advice was to put aside ten rupees out of every hundred before spending anything. A bank account is never mentioned, so the answer is save ten rupees out of every hundred before spending.
Source: PYQ-style

39. How long did it take Ravi to save enough for the bicycle?
(a) One year (b) Six months (c) Two weeks (d) One month
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Two weeks" is the trap — that is how fast his salary used to disappear, not the saving period. The passage says "within a year" he had saved enough, so the answer is one year.
Source: PYQ-style

40. What did Ravi buy with his savings?
(a) A motorcycle (b) A mobile phone (c) A gift for his grandmother (d) A bicycle
Answer: (d)
Solution: The last sentence says he saved enough to buy "a bicycle he had wanted for years." A motorcycle is a near-word trap, so the answer is a bicycle.
Source: PYQ-style

Passage 4 (Questions 41–45):
While many people fear artificial intelligence, few understand that AI systems are only as good as the data they are trained on. If the training data contains biases — for example, if a hiring algorithm is trained mostly on resumes of male candidates — the system will reproduce and even amplify those biases. Thus, the real challenge is not the technology itself, but the human choices behind it.

41. According to the passage, AI systems depend mainly on —
(a) the speed of computers (b) the cost of development (c) the data they are trained on (d) government regulations
Answer: (c)
Solution: The first sentence says AI systems "are only as good as the data they are trained on." Speed and cost are not discussed, so the answer is the data they are trained on.
Source: PYQ-style

42. What happens if training data contains biases?
(a) The system reproduces and amplifies them (b) The system ignores them (c) The system corrects them automatically (d) The system stops working
Answer: (a)
Solution: The passage warns that a biased system "will reproduce and even amplify those biases." Automatic correction is the hopeful but wrong option, so the answer is the system reproduces and amplifies them.
Source: PYQ-style

43. The example in the passage concerns —
(a) a medical diagnosis tool (b) a language translation app (c) a self-driving car (d) a hiring algorithm trained mostly on male candidates' resumes
Answer: (d)
Solution: The only example given is a hiring algorithm trained mostly on resumes of male candidates. The other technologies are not mentioned, so the answer is a hiring algorithm trained mostly on male candidates' resumes.
Source: PYQ-style

44. The author's main argument is that —
(a) the real challenge is the human choices behind AI, not the technology itself (b) people should stop fearing technology entirely (c) AI should be banned (d) AI is always biased and useless
Answer: (a)
Solution: "AI is always biased and useless" is an overstatement trap — the author never says AI is useless. The final sentence states the real challenge is human choices, so the answer is the real challenge is the human choices behind AI, not the technology itself.
Source: PYQ-style

45. The tone of the passage is best described as —
(a) alarmist (b) humorous (c) analytical and cautionary (d) celebratory
Answer: (c)

Passage 5 (Questions 46–50):
Mangrove forests, found along tropical coastlines, act as natural barriers against storms and tsunamis. Their dense root systems slow down waves and reduce the impact on coastal villages. Studies have shown that areas protected by mangroves suffer significantly less damage during cyclones than areas where mangroves have been cleared for shrimp farming or construction. Despite this, mangrove cover continues to shrink in many countries.

46. Where are mangrove forests found?
(a) In deserts (b) Along tropical coastlines (c) On mountain tops (d) In deep oceans
Answer: (b)
Solution: The opening sentence places mangroves "along tropical coastlines." Deep oceans are impossible for rooted trees, so the answer is along tropical coastlines.
Source: PYQ-style

47. How do mangroves protect coastal villages?
(a) Their dense roots slow down waves and reduce storm impact (b) By absorbing salt from seawater (c) By attracting rain clouds (d) By providing building material
Answer: (a)
Solution: The passage says their "dense root systems slow down waves and reduce the impact on coastal villages." Rain attraction is not mentioned, so the answer is their dense roots slow down waves and reduce storm impact.
Source: PYQ-style

48. Areas where mangroves have been cleared suffer —
(a) less damage during cyclones (b) the same damage as protected areas (c) more damage during cyclones (d) no cyclones at all
Answer: (c)
Solution: The comparison in the passage says protected areas suffer "significantly less damage," which means cleared areas suffer more. The reversed option is the trap, so the answer is more damage during cyclones.
Source: PYQ-style

49. Mangroves are being cleared for —
(a) building schools and hospitals (b) shrimp farming and construction (c) planting coconut trees (d) making national parks
Answer: (b)
Solution: The passage names "shrimp farming or construction" as the reasons for clearing. National parks would protect mangroves, not clear them, so the answer is shrimp farming and construction.
Source: PYQ-style

50. The word "shrink" in the last sentence is closest in meaning to —
(a) expand (b) shift location (c) disappear completely (d) decrease
Answer: (d)
Solution: "Disappear completely" is the overstatement trap — shrinking means becoming smaller, not vanishing. The context says mangrove cover "continues to shrink," i.e., reduce, so the answer is decrease.
Source: PYQ-style
