Card 1
Front: What is the first step of the Detective Approach to Reading Comprehension?
Back: Read the questions first (not the options), then scan the passage for evidence — like a detective who knows what clues to look for before entering the scene.

Card 2
Front: What is the 15/45-second rule in Reading Comprehension?
Back: Fact-based (direct) questions should be answered in about 15 seconds by locating the line in the passage; inference/tone questions may take up to 45 seconds because they require reasoning.

Card 3
Front: How do you identify a "fact-based" question?
Back: It asks for information directly stated in the passage — usually begins with "According to the passage…", "The author mentions…", or "Which of the following is true…".

Card 4
Front: What is an "inference" question?
Back: A question asking what the author implies or suggests but does not state directly. The answer must be supported by evidence in the passage, not by outside knowledge.

Card 5
Front: 🧠 Mnemonic: What does "SKIM-SCAN-SOLVE" stand for?
Back: SKIM the passage for the main idea (30–40 sec), SCAN for keywords from the question, SOLVE by matching evidence. Memory hook: "Skim the sea, Scan the shore, Solve the shore's treasure."

Card 6
Front: What is "tone" in a comprehension passage, and name four common tones.
Back: Tone is the author's attitude toward the subject. Four common tones: optimistic, critical, neutral/objective, sarcastic.

Card 7
Front: ⚠️ Examiner's Trap: In inference questions, why are "extreme" options usually wrong?
Back: Options with words like always, never, completely, all, none go beyond the passage. Passages are usually measured and balanced, so extreme statements are traps.

Card 8
Front: ⚠️ Examiner's Trap: An option is factually true in real life but not mentioned in the passage. Should you choose it?
Back: No. In Reading Comprehension, the passage is the only universe. An answer must be supported by the passage, even if you "know" something different.

Card 9
Front: What is the "main idea" question and where is the answer usually found?
Back: It asks for the central theme of the whole passage. The answer is usually in the first or last paragraph — check the opening and closing lines first.

Card 10
Front: What does the Greek root "chron" mean? Give two derived words.
Back: "Time." Derived words: chronology (order of events in time), chronic (lasting a long time).

Card 11
Front: What does the Latin root "port" mean? Give two derived words.
Back: "To carry." Derived words: transport (carry across), export (carry out of a country), import (carry in).

Card 12
Front: What does the Greek root "bio" mean? Give two derived words.
Back: "Life." Derived words: biology (study of life), biography (written account of a life), antibiotic (against living microbes).

Card 13
Front: 🧠 Mnemonic: How do you remember the difference between "affect" and "effect"?
Back: RAVEN: Remember Affect = Verb, Effect = Noun. "The effect (noun) of rain affected (verb) the match."

Card 14
Front: What is the strategy for vocabulary-in-context questions ("The word 'X' in line 5 means…")?
Back: Read the sentence before and after the word, replace the word with each option, and pick the one that keeps the sentence's meaning intact — don't rely on the word's most common dictionary meaning.

Card 15
Front: What should you do when a passage is on an unfamiliar topic (e.g., AI, economy)?
Back: Don't panic — no outside knowledge is needed. Every answer is inside the passage. Focus on structure: what problem is presented, what view the author takes, what conclusion is drawn.

Card 16
Front: What is the difference between "skimming" and "scanning"?
Back: Skimming = reading quickly for the general gist (main idea). Scanning = searching quickly for a specific detail (name, date, keyword) mentioned in a question.

Card 17
Front: ⚠️ Examiner's Trap: In "title of the passage" questions, what kind of option should you reject?
Back: Reject titles that are too narrow (cover only one paragraph) or too broad (cover more than the passage). The correct title captures the whole passage in a few words.

Card 18
Front: What does the Latin root "dict" mean? Give two derived words.
Back: "To say/speak." Derived words: dictate (speak for someone to write), predict (say beforehand), contradict (speak against).

Card 19
Front: In a negative question ("Which of the following is NOT true…"), what is the safe method?
Back: Convert it into three mini true/false checks: verify each option against the passage; the one statement contradicted by (or absent from) the passage is the answer. Underline "NOT/EXCEPT" so you don't forget.

Card 20
Front: What is the ideal order of attempting a comprehension set in the exam?
Back: (1) Read questions quickly, (2) skim the passage for structure and tone, (3) answer fact-based questions first (15 sec each), (4) then attempt inference/tone/title questions (45 sec each), (5) skip and return if any question exceeds 60 seconds.
