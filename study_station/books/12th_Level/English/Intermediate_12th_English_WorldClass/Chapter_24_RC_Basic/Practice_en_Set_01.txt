Reading Comprehension (Basic) — Practice Set 1
Questions 1–20: Easy | Questions 21–25: Medium

Passage 1 (Questions 1–5):
Ravi wakes up at six every morning. He drinks a glass of warm water and goes for a short walk in the park near his house. After the walk, he reads the newspaper for half an hour. He believes that a calm morning routine helps him stay focused at work all day.

1. What does Ravi do first after waking up?
(a) Reads the newspaper (b) Drinks warm water (c) Goes to work (d) Eats breakfast
Answer: (b)
Solution: The passage states Ravi wakes up and then drinks a glass of warm water before anything else. Reading the newspaper comes later, so the answer is drinks warm water.
Source: PYQ-style

2. Where does Ravi go for his walk?
(a) In the park near his house (b) On the terrace (c) Around his office (d) In the market
Answer: (a)
Solution: The passage clearly says he walks in the park near his house. The office and market are never mentioned, so the answer is in the park near his house.
Source: PYQ-style

3. How long does Ravi read the newspaper?
(a) One hour (b) Fifteen minutes (c) Half an hour (d) Two hours
Answer: (c)
Solution: The passage says he reads the newspaper for half an hour. One hour is a tempting misreading of "half an hour", so the answer is half an hour.
Source: PYQ-style

4. Why does Ravi follow a morning routine?
(a) To impress his neighbours (b) To save money (c) To stay focused at work (d) To wake up early
Answer: (c)
Solution: The last line says a calm morning routine helps him stay focused at work all day. Waking up early is part of the routine, not its purpose, so the answer is to stay focused at work.
Source: PYQ-style

5. What time does Ravi wake up?
(a) Five (b) Eight (c) Seven (d) Six
Answer: (d)
Solution: The first sentence says Ravi wakes up at six every morning, so the answer is six.
Source: PYQ-style

Passage 2 (Questions 6–10):
The honeybee is a small but hardworking insect. It flies from flower to flower collecting nectar, which it turns into honey. While collecting nectar, the bee also carries pollen from one flower to another. This process, called pollination, helps plants produce fruits and seeds. Without bees, many of the crops we eat would become scarce.

6. What does the honeybee collect from flowers?
(a) Nectar (b) Pollen only (c) Seeds (d) Fruits
Answer: (a)
Solution: The passage says the bee collects nectar and turns it into honey. Pollen is carried accidentally, not collected as food, so the answer is nectar.
Source: PYQ-style

7. What is pollination?
(a) Making honey (b) Carrying pollen from one flower to another (c) Building a hive (d) Eating fruits
Answer: (b)
Solution: The passage defines pollination as carrying pollen from one flower to another. Making honey is a separate activity, so the answer is carrying pollen from one flower to another.
Source: PYQ-style

8. How does pollination help plants?
(a) It gives them water (b) It helps them produce fruits and seeds (c) It protects them from insects (d) It makes them grow taller
Answer: (b)
Solution: The passage states pollination helps plants produce fruits and seeds, so the answer is it helps them produce fruits and seeds.
Source: PYQ-style

9. What would happen without bees, according to the passage?
(a) Flowers would stop blooming (b) Plants would grow faster (c) Honey would become cheaper (d) Many crops would become scarce
Answer: (d)
Solution: The last sentence says that without bees, many crops we eat would become scarce. Flowers stopping entirely is an exaggeration not stated, so the answer is many crops would become scarce.
Source: PYQ-style

10. The word "scarce" in the passage means:
(a) Available in small quantity (b) Costly (c) Plentiful (d) Harmful
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Scarce" means not enough or available in small quantity. Plentiful is its opposite and a common trap, so the answer is available in small quantity.
Source: PYQ-style

13. How did Meena make her baskets popular?

Solution: The passage says she displayed baskets at the weekly market and told people about their quality. Television is never mentioned, so the answer is displaying them at the weekly market and telling people about their quality.
Source: PYQ-style

14. How long did it take for Meena to hire helpers?
(a) One month (b) Six months (c) Within a year (d) Five years

15. Which quality of Meena does the passage mainly highlight?

Passage 4 (Questions 16–20):
Water covers about seventy percent of the Earth's surface, yet only a tiny part of it is fresh water that people can drink. Most of the Earth's water is salty ocean water. Fresh water is found in rivers, lakes, and underground. As the world's population grows, the demand for fresh water is rising, and scientists warn that we must use it wisely.

16. About how much of the Earth's surface is covered by water?
(a) Thirty percent (b) Seventy percent (c) Fifty percent (d) Ninety percent
Answer: (b)
Solution: The passage states water covers about seventy percent of the Earth's surface. Ninety percent is an exaggerated trap figure, so the answer is seventy percent.
Source: PYQ-style

17. Most of the Earth's water is:
(a) Fresh water (b) Underground water (c) Salty ocean water (d) River water
Answer: (c)
Solution: The passage says most of the Earth's water is salty ocean water. Fresh water is described as only a tiny part, so the answer is salty ocean water.
Source: PYQ-style

18. Where is fresh water found?
(a) In rivers, lakes, and underground (b) Only in oceans (c) Only in glaciers (d) In rain clouds only
Answer: (a)
Solution: The passage lists rivers, lakes, and underground as sources of fresh water. Oceans hold salty water, so the answer is in rivers, lakes, and underground.
Source: PYQ-style

19. Why is the demand for fresh water rising?
(a) Because the world's population is growing (b) Because oceans are drying (c) Because rivers are disappearing (d) Because rain has stopped
Answer: (a)
Solution: The passage links rising demand to the growing world population. Oceans drying is not mentioned, so the answer is because the world's population is growing.
Source: PYQ-style

20. What do scientists warn us to do?
(a) Drink only bottled water (b) Stop using water (c) Build more oceans (d) Use fresh water wisely
Answer: (d)
Solution: The last line says scientists warn that we must use fresh water wisely. Stopping water use entirely is impossible and not suggested, so the answer is use fresh water wisely.
Source: PYQ-style

Passage 5 (Questions 21–25):
Asha was afraid of speaking in English in front of her class. Her teacher advised her to practise a little every day instead of waiting to become perfect. Asha began reading one page of an English storybook aloud each evening and noting down new words. In three months, she could speak short sentences confidently. She realised that small, regular efforts matter more than waiting for the right moment.

21. What was Asha afraid of?
(a) Speaking in English in front of her class (b) Writing essays (c) Reading storybooks (d) Meeting her teacher
Answer: (a)
Solution: The first sentence says Asha was afraid of speaking in English in front of her class. Reading storybooks was her remedy, not her fear, so the answer is speaking in English in front of her class.
Source: PYQ-style

22. What did the teacher advise Asha?
(a) To wait until she became perfect (b) To change her school (c) To practise a little every day (d) To stop speaking in class
Answer: (c)
Solution: The teacher advised practising a little every day instead of waiting to become perfect. Waiting for perfection is exactly what the teacher warned against — a common trap — so the answer is to practise a little every day.
Source: PYQ-style

23. What did Asha do each evening?
(a) Watched English films (b) Memorised grammar rules (c) Wrote letters to friends (d) Read one page of a storybook aloud and noted new words
Answer: (d)
Solution: The passage says she read one page of an English storybook aloud each evening and noted down new words. Films and letters are not mentioned, so the answer is read one page of a storybook aloud and noted new words.
Source: PYQ-style

24. How long did it take Asha to speak short sentences confidently?
(a) Three weeks (b) Three months (c) One year (d) Six months
Answer: (b)
Solution: The passage states that in three months she could speak short sentences confidently. Three weeks is a look-alike trap, so the answer is three months.
Source: PYQ-style

25. What is the main lesson of the passage?
(a) Only talented people can learn English (b) Teachers should be strict (c) Small, regular efforts matter more than waiting for the right moment (d) Storybooks are useless for learning
Answer: (c)
Solution: The last sentence directly states the lesson: small, regular efforts matter more than waiting for the right moment. The other options contradict the passage, so the answer is small, regular efforts matter more than waiting for the right moment.
Source: PYQ-style
