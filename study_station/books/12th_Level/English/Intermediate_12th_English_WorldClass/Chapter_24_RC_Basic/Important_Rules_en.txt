# 📋 Important Rules & Tips — Reading Comprehension (Basic)

## (a) 12 High-Yield Rules

| # | Rule | Example | Examiner's Trap |
|---|------|---------|-----------------|
| 1 | Read the **questions first**, then the passage — your brain scans for answers like a searchlight. | Note keywords in Q1–Q5 before reading. | Reading the passage twice wastes 2–3 minutes. |
| 2 | The answer to a **fact-based** question is always **in the passage** — never use outside knowledge. | Passage says "The Ganga originates at Gangotri" — pick it even if a question tempts you with extra facts. | Options that are true in real life but NOT stated in the passage. |
| 3 | For **inference** questions, the answer is *suggested*, not stated — find the line that *implies* it. | "The author sighed at the policy" → author is *critical* of it. | Choosing an option that is directly copied (that's a fact, not an inference). |
| 4 | **Tone** is judged by adjectives and verbs, not by the topic. | "Alarmingly, glaciers are vanishing" → tone = *concerned/alarmed*. | Confusing a *serious* topic with a *negative* tone; a serious passage can be optimistic. |
| 5 | In **title** questions, the correct title covers the *whole* passage, not one paragraph. | Passage on AI in farming + healthcare + jobs → title: "AI's Growing Role", not "AI in Farming". | Options naming only one paragraph's idea (too narrow) or a vague grand idea (too broad). |
| 6 | **Synonym/Antonym in context**: replace the option in the sentence and re-read. | "The scheme was *lauded*" → try "praised" in the sentence. | Picking the dictionary meaning that doesn't fit the sentence's context. |
| 7 | Words like **always, never, all, none, completely** make options extreme — usually wrong in inference/tone questions. | "The author completely rejects technology" → almost always wrong. | Extreme options look confident and trap hurried readers. |
| 8 | **Negative questions** ("Which is NOT true / EXCEPT") — tick each option against the passage; three will be stated, one won't. | Mark T/F beside each option before answering. | Forgetting the "NOT" and picking the first true statement. |
| 9 | **Pronoun reference** ("it", "they", "this") — go back 1–2 sentences to find the nearest logical noun. | "The dam was raised, and *it* displaced villages" → *it* = the dam/raising of the dam. | Choosing the noun nearest in the sentence but illogical in meaning. |
| 10 | **Detective Approach** for inference: clue (line) + logic = conclusion. Never guess without a clue. | Clue: "Farmers checked phones before sowing" → inference: farmers used digital information. | Over-thinking: adding assumptions the passage never hints at. |
| 11 | The **main idea** usually sits in the first or last paragraph — check both before answering. | Opening line: "Climate change is reshaping India's monsoon" → main idea found. | Picking an interesting detail from the middle as the main idea. |
| 12 | If two options look correct, choose the one **closer to the passage's exact words/idea**. | Passage: "may reduce rainfall" → prefer "might lower rainfall" over "will end rainfall". | The "Sound-Right" trap: a fluent, impressive option that distorts the passage. |

## (b) Time Management — The 15/45-Second Rule

| Question Type | How to Recognise | Time Budget | Strategy |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Fact-based (15 sec)** | "According to the passage…", "Which of the following is stated…", direct synonym/antonym, pronoun reference | ⏱️ 15 seconds | Locate the keyword in the passage, match, mark. Do NOT re-read the whole passage. |
| **Vocabulary-in-context (15 sec)** | "The word 'X' in line 3 means…" | ⏱️ 15 seconds | Substitute each option into the sentence; pick what fits. |
| **Inference-based (45 sec)** | "It can be inferred…", "The author implies…", "What is the tone…", "The best title…" | ⏱️ 45 seconds | Find the clue line → apply Detective Approach → eliminate extreme/narrow options. |
| **Main idea / Tone (45 sec)** | "The passage is mainly about…", "The author's attitude is…" | ⏱️ 45 seconds | Re-read first + last paragraph; check adjectives and verbs for tone. |

**Golden split:** In a 5-question set, roughly 2–3 questions are 15-second type and 2–3 are 45-second type. If a fact question takes more than 30 seconds, mark it and move on — return later.

## (c) Morphology — Root, Affix, Example

| Root / Affix | Meaning | Example Word | Meaning in Passage Context |
|---|---|---|---|
| **bio** (Greek) | life | biodegradable | capable of being broken down by living organisms |
| **geo** (Greek) | earth | geothermal | heat from the earth |
| **chron** (Greek) | time | chronic | lasting for a long time |
| **dict** (Latin) | say/speak | predict | to say beforehand |
| **port** (Latin) | carry | transport | carry across |
| **spect** (Latin) | look | inspect | look into carefully |
| **pre-** (prefix) | before | precaution | care taken before |
| **re-** (prefix) | again | renewable | able to be made new again |
| **un- / in-** (prefix) | not | unsustainable | not able to be maintained |
| **-tion / -ment** (suffix) | act/state (noun) | pollution, development | noun forms of verbs |
| **-able / -ible** (suffix) | capable of | affordable | that can be afforded |
| **-less** (suffix) | without | powerless | without power |

**Tip:** In the exam, break an unknown word into *prefix + root + suffix*. "Unsustainable" = un- (not) + sustain (hold up) + -able (capable) → "not capable of being maintained" — you can answer the vocabulary question without a dictionary.
