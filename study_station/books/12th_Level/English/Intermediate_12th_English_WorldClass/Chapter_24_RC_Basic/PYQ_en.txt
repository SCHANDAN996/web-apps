# 📊 PYQ Analysis — Reading Comprehension (Basic)

## How Exams Test This Chapter (Pattern Analysis)

Reading Comprehension appears in **every** SSC, Railway, Banking and State exam — SSC CHSL, SSC CGL (Tier-I & II), RRB NTPC, IBPS PO/Clerk, and State PCS prelims. It is one of the highest-weightage areas of the English section, usually appearing as one or two passages followed by 5–10 questions.

**What examiners repeatedly ask:**

1. **Direct/Fact-based questions** — "According to the passage, what caused…?" The answer is written word-for-word in the passage. These are the easiest marks; locate the keyword and match.
2. **Vocabulary-in-context** — "The word 'X' as used in the passage means…" The trap: the dictionary meaning is given as an option, but the *contextual* meaning is different.
3. **Inference questions** — "It can be inferred that…", "The author implies…" The answer is NOT written directly; you must deduce it from clues.
4. **Tone/Attitude questions** — "The tone of the author is…" (optimistic, critical, neutral, sarcastic, cautionary).
5. **Title/Main idea questions** — "The most suitable title is…" The trap: an option that is true but covers only one paragraph, not the whole passage.
6. **True/Not True questions** — "Which of the following is NOT true according to the passage?" Negative wording is the biggest trap — students miss the "NOT".

**Examiner's favourite traps (in words):**

- **Sound-Right trap**: an option that sounds sensible in real life but is not supported by the passage. The passage is the only truth.
- **Half-True trap**: an option where the first half matches the passage but the second half adds something extra or wrong.
- **Extreme-word trap**: options with "always", "never", "completely", "all" are usually wrong unless the passage itself uses such absolute language.
- **Negative-wording trap**: "NOT true", "EXCEPT", "least likely" — misread the question stem and you pick the opposite answer.
- **Over-thinking trap**: students reject the direct, obvious answer because it "seems too easy" and pick a twisted option instead.

## Top 10 High-Yield PYQ-Style Questions with Solutions

**Passage (for Q1–Q5):**
*Artificial intelligence is transforming the way banks detect fraud. Traditional systems relied on fixed rules: a transaction above a certain amount, or from an unusual location, would trigger an alert. Such systems generated thousands of false alarms every day, exhausting investigation teams. Machine-learning models, by contrast, learn the spending pattern of each customer and flag only genuinely abnormal behaviour. However, these models are not flawless. If the data used to train them is biased, the model may unfairly flag transactions from certain regions or groups. Experts therefore caution that human oversight must remain central to fraud detection.*

**Q1.** According to the passage, traditional fraud-detection systems triggered alerts based on:
(a) customer spending patterns
(b) fixed rules
(c) machine-learning models
(d) biased training data

**Answer:** (b)
**Solution:** The passage states directly: "Traditional systems relied on fixed rules." This is a pure Fact-based question — 15-second rule applies.
**Trap type:** Sound-Right — option (a) describes the *new* system, not the traditional one.
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q2.** The word "flawless" as used in the passage is closest in meaning to:
(a) expensive
(b) without faults
(c) slow
(d) biased

**Answer:** (b)
**Solution:** "Flawless" = without flaws/faults. The sentence "these models are not flawless" is followed by an example of a fault (biased flagging), confirming the meaning.
**Trap type:** Sound-Right — (d) "biased" appears nearby in the passage and lures skimmers.
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q3.** It can be inferred from the passage that the author believes:
(a) AI should fully replace human investigators
(b) machine-learning models should be abandoned
(c) human supervision of AI systems is necessary
(d) false alarms are no longer a problem

**Answer:** (c)
**Solution:** "Human oversight must remain central" directly supports (c). Options (a) and (b) are extreme-word traps; (d) contradicts nothing stated but is not supported either.
**Trap type:** Extreme-word — (a) "fully replace" and (b) "abandoned" go beyond the author's view.
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q4.** The tone of the author is best described as:
(a) celebratory
(b) cautionary
(c) dismissive
(d) sarcastic

**Answer:** (b)
**Solution:** The author praises AI's benefits but warns about bias and insists on oversight — a balanced, cautionary tone. "Experts therefore caution…" is the tone marker.
**Trap type:** Over-thinking — students see praise in the first half and pick (a), ignoring the warning in the second half.
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q5.** Which of the following is NOT true according to the passage?
(a) Traditional systems produced many false alarms.
(b) Machine-learning models learn individual spending patterns.
(c) Biased training data can cause unfair flagging.
(d) Machine-learning models have completely eliminated fraud.

**Answer:** (d)
**Solution:** The passage never claims fraud is eliminated; it says models "are not flawless." Note the negative stem "NOT true" — underline it before reading options.
**Trap type:** Negative-wording + Extreme-word ("completely eliminated").
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Passage (for Q6–Q8):**
*Rising urban temperatures have made cities significantly warmer than the surrounding countryside — a phenomenon scientists call the "urban heat island" effect. Concrete and asphalt absorb heat during the day and release it slowly at night, while the loss of trees removes nature's own cooling system. Some cities have responded by painting roofs white and planting avenue trees, measures that are inexpensive yet remarkably effective.*

**Q6.** The "urban heat island" effect refers to:
(a) islands near cities becoming hotter
(b) cities being warmer than nearby rural areas
(c) heat waves occurring only in summer
(d) the cooling effect of trees in cities

**Answer:** (b)
**Solution:** Defined in the first sentence itself: "cities significantly warmer than the surrounding countryside." Fact-based — 15-second rule.
**Trap type:** Sound-Right — (a) misreads "island" literally.
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q7.** According to the passage, white roofs and avenue trees are described as:
(a) costly but necessary
(b) inexpensive yet effective
(c) experimental and risky
(d) traditional but outdated

**Answer:** (b)
**Solution:** The last sentence says "inexpensive yet remarkably effective" — a direct match. Fact-based — 15-second rule.
**Trap type:** Half-True — (a) "necessary" may be true in life, but "costly" contradicts the passage.
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q8.** The most suitable title for the passage is:
(a) The History of Urban Planning
(b) How Trees Grow in Cities
(c) Urban Heat Islands and Simple Solutions
(d) The Dangers of Concrete

**Answer:** (c)
**Solution:** The passage covers both the problem (heat islands) and the response (white roofs, trees). (b) and (d) cover only fragments; (a) is off-topic.
**Trap type:** Half-True — (d) is mentioned but is only one detail, not the main idea.
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q9.** (Standalone vocabulary-in-context, PYQ pattern) In a passage about the economy, the sentence reads: "The government took *austere* measures to reduce the deficit." The word "austere" here means:
(a) generous
(b) strict and economical
(c) corrupt
(d) delayed

**Answer:** (b)
**Solution:** "Austere measures" = strict, cost-cutting steps. Context clue: "to reduce the deficit" implies spending control.
**Trap type:** Sound-Right — (d) "delayed" fits grammatically but not the economic context.
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q10.** (Inference, PYQ pattern) A passage states: "Despite record harvests, farmer incomes remained stagnant, as middlemen captured most of the price paid by consumers." It can be inferred that:
(a) farmers produced too little food
(b) consumers paid low prices for food
(c) higher production alone does not guarantee higher farmer income
(d) middlemen are farmers' biggest employers

**Answer:** (c)
**Solution:** Record harvests + stagnant income = production alone isn't enough; the distribution chain matters. (a) and (b) contradict the passage; (d) is unsupported.
**Trap type:** Over-thinking — students hunt for a complex answer when (c) follows directly from the two given facts.
**Source:** PYQ-style

## Applying the 15/45-Second Rule (Worked Examples)

**Q1 (Fact-based → 15 seconds):** The question asks what traditional systems "relied on." Strategy: scan the passage for the keyword "traditional," read that one sentence, match "fixed rules" with option (b). No need to re-read the whole passage. Target time: **15 seconds**.

**Q3 (Inference-based → 45 seconds):** The question asks what the author "believes." Strategy: locate the opinion sentence ("Experts therefore caution that human oversight must remain central"), then eliminate the two extreme options (a) and (b), then check (d) for textual support — none found. Confirm (c). Target time: **45 seconds**.

**Rule of thumb:** If the question stem says "according to the passage" or quotes a word → 15-second fact hunt. If it says "inferred", "implies", "tone", "title" → 45-second detective work.
