Passage (Q. 101–105): Artificial intelligence is transforming healthcare faster than any previous technology. Algorithms can now detect diabetic retinopathy from eye scans with accuracy rivalling specialists, and predict heart failure days before symptoms appear. Yet doctors warn that an algorithm trained mostly on data from wealthy urban hospitals may fail rural patients whose symptoms, diets and medical histories differ. The machine is only as wise as the data it has seen.

101. According to the passage, AI algorithms can currently —
(a) perform surgery without human supervision (b) cure heart failure before it begins (c) replace rural doctors completely (d) detect diabetic retinopathy from eye scans
Answer: (d)
Solution: The passage directly states that algorithms "can now detect diabetic retinopathy from eye scans." Option (a) and (c) go beyond what is claimed, and (b) confuses prediction with cure — the text says AI predicts heart failure, not cures it. So the answer is (d).
Source: PYQ-style

102. The word "rivalling" in the passage is closest in meaning to —
(a) opposing (b) assisting (c) defeating (d) matching
Answer: (d)
Solution: "Rival" as a verb means to equal or be comparable to; here the algorithm's accuracy matches that of specialists. "Opposing" is the trap because rivalry sounds like conflict, but the context is about equal accuracy. So the answer is (d).
Source: PYQ-style

103. Why might an AI system fail rural patients, according to the passage?
(a) Rural patients refuse to use technology (b) The algorithms are too expensive for villages (c) Rural diseases cannot be detected by machines (d) It was trained mostly on data from wealthy urban hospitals
Answer: (d)
Solution: The passage explicitly says an algorithm "trained mostly on data from wealthy urban hospitals may fail rural patients." Options (a) and (c) are invented, and cost is never mentioned, so (b) is the tempting distractor. So the answer is (d).
Source: PYQ-style

104. The tone of the passage is best described as —
(a) cautiously optimistic (b) celebratory (c) hostile (d) indifferent
Answer: (a)
Solution: The writer praises AI's achievements ("transforming healthcare") but adds a warning ("Yet doctors warn…"). Pure celebration ignores the warning, and hostility ignores the praise — both are one-sided traps. So the answer is (a).
Source: PYQ-style

105. "The machine is only as wise as the data it has seen" implies that —
(a) machines are wiser than doctors (b) data is useless without machines (c) AI quality depends on the quality and range of its training data (d) machines can learn nothing from data
Answer: (c)
Solution: The sentence is a metaphor: limited or biased data produces limited or biased AI. Option (a) reverses the meaning, and (d) contradicts the whole passage. So the answer is (c).
Source: PYQ-style

Passage (Q. 106–110): For decades, the sparrow was the most familiar bird of Indian towns, nesting in the eaves of houses and feeding on grain scattered in courtyards. In the last thirty years, however, its cheerful chirping has faded from many cities. Ornithologists blame a combination of factors: modern glass-and-concrete buildings offer no cavities for nesting, unleaded petrol has reduced the insects sparrows feed their chicks, and mobile-phone radiation is suspected, though not proven, to disturb them. Where old neighbourhoods survive, sparrows often survive too.

106. The passage suggests that sparrows traditionally nested —
(a) in tree holes in forests (b) on mobile-phone towers (c) in the eaves of houses (d) inside grain shops
Answer: (c)
Solution: The first sentence says sparrows nested "in the eaves of houses." Forests and grain shops are plausible-sounding inventions; towers are mentioned only regarding radiation. So the answer is (c).
Source: PYQ-style

107. According to the passage, which factor is only suspected, not proven, to harm sparrows?
(a) Mobile-phone radiation (b) Reduction of insects (c) Loss of nesting cavities (d) Scarcity of grain
Answer: (a)
Solution: The text says radiation "is suspected, though not proven." The trap is to treat all listed causes as proven; the writer carefully separates the confirmed causes from the suspected one. So the answer is (a).
Source: PYQ-style

108. The word "cavities" in the passage means —
(a) large open fields (b) small holes or hollow spaces (c) types of buildings (d) kinds of insects
Answer: (b)
Solution: A cavity is a hollow space; modern buildings "offer no cavities for nesting," meaning no gaps where sparrows can build nests. So the answer is (b).

109. It can be inferred that the decline of sparrows is —
(a) linked to changes in urban design and environment (b) irreversible in all cities (c) entirely due to one single cause (d) caused mainly by forest destruction
Answer: (a)
Solution: The passage lists multiple urban factors — building style, petrol, possibly radiation — so a single cause (c) is wrong. "Irreversible" is too strong since sparrows survive in old neighbourhoods. So the answer is (a).

110. The last sentence of the passage conveys a feeling of —
(a) despair (b) anger (c) hope (d) confusion
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Where old neighbourhoods survive, sparrows often survive too" suggests the decline is not total and conservation is possible — a hopeful note. Despair would require the opposite claim. So the answer is (c).

(a) Occurrence (b) Occurence (c) Ocurrence (d) Occurrance

112. Choose the word nearest in meaning to FRAGILE.
(a) Sturdy (b) Rigid (c) Delicate (d) Massive
Answer: (c)

113. Choose the word opposite in meaning to TRANSPARENT.
(a) Clear (b) Opaque (c) Obvious (d) Fragile
Answer: (b)
Solution: Transparent means allowing light through; its opposite is opaque. "Clear" and "obvious" are near-synonyms and trap careless readers. So the answer is (b).

114. Spot the error: Part P: One of my friend / Part Q: has gone / Part R: to Delhi yesterday. / Part S: No error
(a) Part P (b) Part Q (c) Part R (d) No error
Answer: (a)
Solution: After "one of" we need a plural noun — "one of my friends." The trap is to hunt for a tense error in Part R, but "has gone… yesterday" is not the tested point here; the noun-number error in Part P is. So the answer is (a).
Source: PYQ-style

115. Choose the correctly punctuated indirect speech: He said to me, "I will help you."
(a) He told me that he will help me. (b) He told to me that he would help me. (c) He said me that he would help you. (d) He told me that he would help me.
Answer: (d)
Solution: In reported speech "will" shifts to "would," "said to me" becomes "told me," and "you" becomes "me." Option (a) keeps "will," (c) wrongly keeps "you," and (b) adds an incorrect "to" after told. So the answer is (d).
Source: PYQ-style

Passage (Q. 116–120): Economists often speak of the "paradox of thrift." If every household in a country suddenly decides to save more and spend less, each family feels prudent. But when millions cut spending together, shops sell less, factories slow down, workers lose jobs, and incomes fall — so the nation may end up saving less in total, not more. What is virtue in one person can become a collective misfortune. This is why governments sometimes encourage spending during a recession, even while advising citizens, in normal times, to save.

116. The "paradox of thrift" refers to the situation where —
(a) individual saving, when practised by everyone at once, can reduce total national saving (b) saving always makes a nation richer (c) governments waste money during recessions (d) spending is always better than saving
Answer: (a)
Solution: The paradox is precisely that universal saving shrinks incomes and hence total saving. Option (d) overstates the passage, which calls saving a virtue in normal times. So the answer is (a).
Source: PYQ-style

117. The word "prudent" in the passage means —
(a) careless (b) wise and cautious (c) wealthy (d) generous
Answer: (b)
Solution: Prudent means showing careful judgement; each family "feels prudent" when it saves. The trap is "wealthy," but saving makes one feel wise, not necessarily rich. So the answer is (b).
Source: PYQ-style

118. According to the passage, why do governments encourage spending during a recession?
(a) Because saving is always harmful (b) Because reduced collective spending deepens job losses and falling incomes (c) Because shops demand higher prices (d) Because citizens refuse to save
Answer: (b)
Solution: The chain in the passage is: less spending → fewer sales → slower factories → lost jobs → lower incomes. Encouraging spending breaks this chain. Option (a) is the trap; the passage calls saving a virtue in normal times. So the answer is (b).
Source: PYQ-style

119. The author's attitude towards saving can best be described as —
(a) entirely negative (b) neutral, with no opinion (c) mocking (d) balanced — a virtue individually and in normal times, harmful if universal during a slump
Answer: (d)
Solution: The author calls saving a "virtue" yet shows its collective danger — a balanced view. "Entirely negative" ignores the praise; "neutral" ignores the clear judgement expressed. So the answer is (d).
Source: PYQ-style

120. "What is virtue in one person can become a collective misfortune" is an example of —
(a) a criticism of government policy (b) a proverb about honesty (c) a fallacy of composition in reverse — what is good for the part may not be good for the whole (d) a definition of recession
Answer: (c)
Solution: The sentence captures the core logic: individual good does not automatically add up to collective good. It is not about honesty or a policy attack, so (b) and (a) are traps. So the answer is (c).

(a) Part P (b) Part R (c) Part Q (d) No error
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Neither of" takes a singular verb — "has done." The trap is the plural noun "boys" sitting just before the verb, which pulls the ear towards "have" (an intervening-phrase trap). So the answer is (c).

(a) To read very quickly (b) To memorise a text (c) To skip difficult passages (d) To understand the hidden or implied meaning

Solution: The idiom means grasping what is implied but not stated — exactly the inference skill comprehension tests. The literal-sounding options are traps. So the answer is (d).

124. A passage states: "The new dam brought electricity to fifty villages, though three hamlets were submerged and their families relocated." The author's tone is —
(a) purely celebratory (b) measured — acknowledging both benefit and cost (c) purely critical (d) sarcastic
Answer: (b)
Solution: The sentence records a gain and a loss side by side with "though," signalling a measured tone. Choosing only one side (celebratory or critical) is the classic one-sided-tone trap. So the answer is (b).
Source: PYQ-style

125. In an inference question, the safest answer is the one that —
(a) uses the strongest words like "always" and "never" (b) follows logically from the passage even if not stated in so many words (c) is directly copied word-for-word from the passage (d) introduces new information not in the passage
Answer: (b)
Solution: Inference means drawing a conclusion the passage supports without stating outright. Extreme words (a) are usually wrong, word-for-word copying (c) is a fact answer not an inference, and new information (d) is unsupported. So the answer is (b).
Source: PYQ-style
