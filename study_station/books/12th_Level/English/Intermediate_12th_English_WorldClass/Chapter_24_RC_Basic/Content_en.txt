# Reading Comprehension (Basic)

## 🎯 Why This Chapter Can Decide Your Result

In almost every government exam that tests English — SSC CHSL, SSC CGL Tier-1, IBPS Clerk/PO, and state-level exams — Reading Comprehension is the single biggest block of marks in the English section. One passage, five questions, and no grammar rule to memorise. Yet most students lose marks here not because they can't read English, but because they **read the passage like a story and answer like a guess**. This chapter teaches you to read like a detective: every answer must be *found* or *proved* from the passage, never imagined.

## 📌 Learning Objectives

By the end of this chapter you will be able to:

1. Read a passage strategically (skim → scan → verify) instead of reading it word by word.
2. Distinguish **fact-based** questions (answer is written in the passage) from **inference-based** questions (answer is hidden between the lines).
3. Identify the author's **tone** (critical, optimistic, neutral, sarcastic) from clue words.
4. Spot and reject the examiner's favourite traps: half-true options, extreme words, and out-of-passage knowledge.
5. Use Greek/Latin roots to unlock unfamiliar words inside the passage.

---

## 1. What Reading Comprehension Really Tests

A comprehension passage is not a test of your general knowledge. It is a test of **one skill only**: can you find and understand what *this author* wrote in *these 200–300 words*? The moment you bring in outside knowledge — "but I read somewhere that…" — you fall into the examiner's trap. The passage is the whole universe. If the passage says the sky is green, then for those five questions, the sky is green.

Passages in SSC and Banking exams are usually 150–300 words long and come from modern themes: technology and AI, environment and climate, the Indian economy, health, education, and social issues. The questions are of four standard types:

| Question Type | What It Asks | Where the Answer Lives |
|---|---|---|
| Fact / Detail | What did the passage say? | Directly in a line |
| Vocabulary | Synonym / antonym of a word | In the sentence around the word |
| Inference | What did the author imply? | Between the lines |
| Tone / Title / Main Idea | How does the author feel? What is the passage about? | The whole passage |

## 2. The Three-Pass Reading Strategy

Never read the passage once slowly and then panic at the questions. Read it **three times, each time with a different job**:

**Pass 1 — Skim (30–40 seconds).** Read the first and last sentence of every paragraph. Your only goal: *What is this passage about, and is the author for it or against it?* Do not stop at difficult words.

**Pass 2 — Read the questions (not the options yet).** Now you know what to hunt for. Questions become a map.

**Pass 3 — Scan and verify.** For each question, go back to the passage, locate the relevant line, and match it with the options. The correct option is almost always a **paraphrase** — the same idea in different words. An option that copies the passage word-for-word is often a trap.

> 🧠 **Mnemonic — "SOS":** **S**kim the passage → **O**bserve the questions → **S**can for the answer. When you feel lost in a passage, send an SOS!

## 3. Fact vs Inference — The Detective Approach 🕵️

This is the heart of Reading Comprehension.

A **fact question** asks what is *stated*. The answer sits in one line of the passage. Example: "According to the passage, when did the scheme begin?" — you locate the year, match, done. These are your **15-second questions**.

An **inference question** asks what is *suggested but not said*. The author never writes it directly; you must deduce it like a detective from clues. Example: the passage says, *"Despite record harvests, farmer incomes continued to fall."* The author never says "the policy failed" — but the word **"despite"** is a clue that the author finds the outcome disappointing and unexpected. These are your **45-second questions**, and they deserve the extra time.

**The Detective's Rule:** an inference must be *provable from the passage*. If you cannot point to the clue words that support it, it is not an inference — it is a guess. And beware **over-inference**: if the passage says "some students struggled," the option "all students failed" goes too far.

## 4. Tone Mapping — Reading the Author's Mind

Tone questions ("The tone of the passage is…") frighten students, but tone is just the author's **emotion**, and emotions leak through word choice. Build the habit of underlining emotionally loaded words while skimming:

| Tone | Clue Words You Will See |
|---|---|
| Optimistic / hopeful | promising, encouraging, remarkable, potential |
| Critical / concerned | alarming, unfortunately, neglect, despite, however |
| Neutral / factual | according to, data shows, the report states |
| Sarcastic / ironic | "of course," "naturally," praise that is clearly excessive |

**Quick method:** read the **last paragraph** carefully — authors reveal their truest feelings in the conclusion. Then ask: *If this author were speaking aloud, would the voice sound happy, worried, or just reporting?*

## 5. Word Power Inside Passages — Greek & Latin Roots

Vocabulary questions in RC ask for the meaning of a word *as used in the passage*. If you know roots, you can decode words you have never seen before.

| Root | Meaning | Derived Words |
|---|---|---|
| spect (Latin) | look, see | inspect, spectator, perspective, retrospect |
| dict (Latin) | say, speak | predict, dictate, verdict, contradict |
| port (Latin) | carry | transport, export, portable, support |
| cred (Latin) | believe | credible, incredible, credit, credulous |
| bio (Greek) | life | biology, biography, antibiotic, biodiversity |
| chron (Greek) | time | chronic, chronology, synchronize |
| geo (Greek) | earth | geography, geology, geothermal |
| scrib/script (Latin) | write | describe, manuscript, inscription |

**Now watch these roots come alive in a passage:**

> *When inspectors visited the coastal laboratory, the chief scientist gave a credible account of the team's work on marine biodiversity. "We cannot predict every consequence of climate change," she said, "but the chronic decline of fish populations is well documented in our records." Her verdict was clear: unless transport of industrial waste into the sea is controlled, the damage, visible in retrospect, will seem both predictable and preventable.*

Six of the eight roots above — *spect, cred, bio, dict, chron, port* — appear in this one short passage. In the exam, even if a question asks the synonym of *credible* (believable) or *chronic* (long-lasting), the roots do the work for you.

## ⚠️ Examiner's Trap Box

**Trap 1 — The Half-True Option.** The option starts with words straight from the passage, then quietly adds a wrong ending. *Passage:* "The scheme benefited small farmers in eastern states." *Trap option:* "The scheme benefited all farmers in eastern states." One word — *all* — makes it false.

**Trap 2 — Extreme Words.** Options containing *always, never, all, none, completely, only* are usually wrong, because passages rarely make absolute claims. Prefer options with *some, often, may, likely*.

**Trap 3 — The Outside-Knowledge Bait.** An option states something true in real life but **not mentioned in the passage**. It feels safe because it is true — but RC never rewards outside truth. If it isn't in the passage, it isn't the answer.

## 🔁 Linguistic Bridge — Hindi से English

| हम हिंदी से सोचकर कहते हैं ❌ | Correct English ✅ |
|---|---|
| "According to me, the answer is B." | "In my opinion, the answer is B." ("According to" is only for third parties: *according to the passage*.) |
| "Passage के अनुसार" → "From the passage it is saying…" | "According to the passage, …" |
| "What the author wants to say?" | "What does the author want to say?" (question word order) |
| "He is giving a speech since morning." | "He has been giving a speech since morning." |

**Golden habit for RC:** in Hindi we say "गद्यांश के अनुसार" — in English the fixed phrase is **"According to the passage, …"** — and that phrase is your licence: every answer must be able to begin with it.
