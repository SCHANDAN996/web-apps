Passage 1 (Questions 126–130): Artificial intelligence systems now screen job applications in many large companies. Supporters argue that algorithms remove human prejudice from hiring. Critics counter that algorithms learn from historical data, and if past hiring favoured certain groups, the machine simply automates the old bias at greater speed and scale. The question, therefore, is not whether machines are biased, but whether we audit them as rigorously as we claim to audit ourselves.

126. What is the central concern of the passage?
(a) Machines work faster than humans (b) AI may reproduce human bias hidden in past data (c) Companies should stop using computers (d) Historical data is always accurate
Answer: (b)
Solution: The tempting wrong choice is the speed point, which the passage mentions only as making bias worse, not as the main idea. The author's core worry is that algorithms trained on biased history automate that bias, so the answer is that AI may reproduce human bias hidden in past data.
Source: PYQ-style

127. The word "audit" in the passage is closest in meaning to:
(a) ignore (b) purchase (c) examine carefully (d) delay
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Audit" here means a rigorous check of the machines' decisions; "ignore" reverses the meaning. The answer is "examine carefully".
Source: PYQ-style

128. According to the passage, why do critics distrust AI hiring tools?
(a) They are too slow (b) They learn patterns from possibly biased historical data (c) They are expensive (d) They cannot read applications
Answer: (b)
Solution: Cost and speed are never criticised in the passage; the objection is specifically about training data. The answer is that they learn patterns from possibly biased historical data.

Passage 2 (Questions 131–135): Cities are often several degrees warmer than the countryside around them, a phenomenon called the urban heat island effect. Concrete and asphalt absorb sunlight by day and release heat by night, while the loss of trees removes nature's cooling system — shade and evaporation. Planting trees and painting roofs white are cheap remedies, yet they remain rare because their benefits arrive slowly, while election cycles are short.

131. The urban heat island effect refers to:
(a) cities being warmer than surrounding rural areas (b) islands becoming cities (c) heat waves on tropical islands (d) cooling of cities at night
Answer: (a)
Solution: The passage defines it in the first sentence; the tropical-island option misreads the metaphor. The answer is that cities are warmer than surrounding rural areas.
Source: PYQ-style

Solution: The passage never says spills are profitable overall; the point is that cleanup spending inflates GDP while the damage is never subtracted. The answer is that GDP can register destruction as economic gain.

137. "Well-being budgets" measure:
