76. Each of the students ______ submitted his assignment on time.
(a) have (b) has (c) are (d) were
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Each of + plural noun" takes a singular verb and singular pronoun reference, so "has" is correct. "Have" is the sound-right trap because "students" sits next to the verb.
Source: NCERT Class 9 English Grammar

77. One should do ______ duty honestly.
(a) his (b) our (c) their (d) one's
Answer: (d)
Solution: The pronoun referring back to "one" must be "one's", not "his" or "their". Mixing "one" with "his" is a classic examiner trap.
Source: PYQ-style

78. Neither of the two brothers ______ passed the examination.
(a) has (b) have (c) are (d) were
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Neither of" is singular in formal usage, so "has" is correct. The plural noun "brothers" before the verb is an intervening-phrase trap.
Source: NCERT Class 10 English Grammar

79. It was I who ______ responsible for the delay.
(a) is (b) were (c) am (d) was
Answer: (d)
Solution: The verb after "who" agrees with its antecedent "I", but the sentence is in past narration ("It was"), so "was" fits the tense. "Am" would clash with the past frame.
Source: PYQ-style

80. The committee ______ divided in its opinion on the new policy.
(a) was (b) are (c) is (d) have
Answer: (a)
Solution: When a collective noun's members act separately ("divided in its opinion"), a plural verb is used in British convention, but with "its" (singular pronoun) the sentence treats the body as one unit in past time, so "was" matches the pronoun "its". The trap is mixing plural verb with singular pronoun.
Source: NCERT Class 10 English Grammar

81. Between you and ______, this plan will never work.
(a) I (b) mine (c) myself (d) me
Answer: (d)
Solution: After a preposition ("between"), the object form "me" is required. "Between you and I" is the most famous sound-right trap in English.
Source: PYQ-style

82. Every student and every teacher ______ present at the function.
(a) were (b) have (c) are (d) was
Answer: (d)
Solution: When two nouns joined by "and" are each preceded by "every", the subject is singular, so "was". The "and" tempts students to pick a plural verb.
Source: NCERT Class 9 English Grammar

83. He is one of those players who ______ always punctual.
(a) is (b) are (c) was (d) has been
Answer: (b)
Solution: In "one of those who…", the relative pronoun "who" refers to "players" (plural), so the verb is "are". The trap is agreeing the verb with "one".
Source: PYQ-style

84. My house is bigger than ______.
(a) your (b) yours (c) you're (d) yourself
Answer: (b)
Solution: A possessive pronoun "yours" (= your house) is needed after "than". "Your" is an adjective and cannot stand alone.
Source: NCERT Class 8 English Grammar

85. The manager, along with his assistants, ______ left for the conference.
(a) has (b) have (c) are (d) were
Answer: (a)
Solution: With "along with", the verb agrees with the first subject "the manager" (singular), so "has". The intervening phrase "his assistants" is the trap.
Source: NCERT Class 10 English Grammar

86. Nobody but his parents ______ where he has gone.
(a) knows (b) know (c) are knowing (d) have known
Answer: (a)
Solution: With "but" (meaning except), the verb agrees with the subject before it — "nobody" is singular, so "knows". "Parents" next to the verb is the distractor.
Source: PYQ-style

87. She and ______ went to the market together.
(a) me (b) mine (c) I (d) myself
Answer: (c)
Solution: The pronoun is part of the subject, so the subject form "I" is needed. Test: drop "She and" — "I went" is correct, "me went" is not.
Source: NCERT Class 8 English Grammar

88. It is ______ who broke the window.
(a) him (b) he (c) his (d) himself
Answer: (b)
Solution: In the cleft structure "It is … who", the nominative "he" is used in formal English. "Him" is common in speech but marked wrong in exams.
Source: PYQ-style

89. The jury ______ given its verdict unanimously.
(a) have (b) are (c) has (d) were
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Its" (singular pronoun) and "unanimously" show the jury acted as one body, so the singular "has" is correct. A plural verb would need "their" and signs of division.
Source: NCERT Class 10 English Grammar

90. Each boy and each girl ______ given a certificate.
(a) were (b) was (c) have (d) are
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Each … and each …" makes the subject singular, so "was". The word "and" is the classic trap pushing students toward a plural verb.
Source: NCERT Class 9 English Grammar

91. Whose book is this? It is ______.
(a) hers (b) hers' (c) her's (d) her
Answer: (a)
Solution: Possessive pronouns (hers, yours, ours, theirs) never take an apostrophe. "Her's" and "hers'" are spelling traps; "her" is only an adjective/object form.
Source: NCERT Class 8 English Grammar

92. He availed ______ of the opportunity to study abroad.
(a) him (b) his (c) himself (d) he
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Avail oneself of" is a fixed reflexive idiom, so "himself" is correct. Omitting the reflexive or using "him" is a frequent exam error.
Source: PYQ-style

93. The two sisters love ______ very much.
(a) each other (b) one another (c) themselves (d) one other
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Each other" is used for two persons; "one another" for more than two. Since there are two sisters, "each other" is correct.
Source: NCERT Class 9 English Grammar

94. None of the milk ______ spilt on the floor.
(a) were (b) are (c) was (d) have been
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Milk" is uncountable, so "none of the milk" takes a singular verb "was". With countable plurals "none" may take either, but uncountable nouns force singular.
Source: NCERT Class 10 English Grammar

95. Let you and ______ decide the matter ourselves.
(a) I (b) me (c) mine (d) we
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Let" is a verb taking an object, so the object form "me" is required: "let you and me". "I" after "let" is the sound-right trap.
Source: PYQ-style

96. Spot the error: The principal, as well as the teachers, (P) / were present (Q) / at the meeting (R) / yesterday. (S)
(a) Part P (b) Part R (c) Part Q (d) No error
Answer: (c)
Solution: With "as well as", the verb agrees with the first subject "the principal" (singular), so Part Q should read "was present". The plural noun "teachers" just before the verb is the intervening-phrase trap.
Source: PYQ-style

97. Spot the error: One of my friend (P) / has invited me (Q) / to his wedding (R) / next month. (S)
(a) No error (b) Part Q (c) Part R (d) Part P
Answer: (d)
Solution: "One of" must be followed by a plural noun, so Part P should be "one of my friends". The verb "has" is correctly singular, which makes this an over-thinking trap — students wrongly "correct" Part Q.
Source: PYQ-style

98. Choose the correctly framed sentence:
(a) The Ganges is one of the longest river in India. (b) The Ganges is one of the most longest rivers in India. (c) The Ganges is one of longest rivers in India. (d) The Ganges is one of the longest rivers in India.
Answer: (d)
Solution: "One of the + superlative + plural noun" is the required pattern, giving "one of the longest rivers". Option (a) fails on the plural noun, (c) drops "the", and (b) doubles the superlative.
Source: NCERT Class 10 English Grammar

99. Spot the error: Neither the students (P) / nor the teacher (Q) / were ready (R) / for the test. (S)
(a) Part R (b) Part Q (c) Part P (d) No error
Answer: (a)
Solution: With "neither…nor", the verb agrees with the nearer subject "the teacher" (singular), so Part R should be "was ready". The plural "students" earlier in the sentence is the proximity trap.
Source: PYQ-style

100. Choose the sentence with correct pronoun usage:
(a) Everyone should bring their books tomorrow. (b) Everyone should bring its book tomorrow. (c) Everyone should bring his book tomorrow. (d) Everyone should bring them book tomorrow.
Answer: (c)
Solution: In traditional exam grammar, "everyone" is singular and takes "his" (or "his or her"). "Their" with "everyone", though common in modern speech, is marked wrong in SSC/Bank exams — a deliberate trap.
Source: PYQ-style
