# 📋 Important Grammar Rules & Tips — Pronoun

## (a) 12 High-Yield Rules

| # | Rule | Example | Examiner's Trap |
|---|------|---------|-----------------|
| 1 | A pronoun must agree with its antecedent in **number, person and gender**. | Every student must bring **his/her** book. | "Every student must bring **their** book" — sounds modern, but in exam grammar "every" takes a singular pronoun. |
| 2 | Pronouns joined by **and** → plural pronoun; joined by **or / either…or / neither…nor** → pronoun agrees with the **nearer** subject. | Either Ram or his brothers forgot **their** keys. | "Neither the teacher nor the student has done **their** work" ❌ → **his** work (nearer subject = student, singular). |
| 3 | After **than / as** (comparison), use the **nominative** case when a verb is understood. | She is taller than **I** (am). | "He loves you more than **me**" changes meaning — it means "more than he loves me". Trap: case changes the sense! |
| 4 | After **let, between, but (except), prepositions** → **objective** case. | Between you and **me**, he is wrong. | "Between you and **I**" ❌ — the most repeated trap in SSC/Bank exams. |
| 5 | **Each other** → two persons; **one another** → more than two. | Ram and Shyam help **each other**. The four friends help **one another**. | "The two brothers quarrel with **one another**" ❌ → **each other**. |
| 6 | **One**, if used as subject, must be followed by **one/one's** throughout — never he/his/they. | One should do **one's** duty. | "One should do **his** duty" ❌ in traditional exam grammar. |
| 7 | **Each, every, everyone, everybody, somebody, anybody, nobody** → singular pronoun. | Everybody brought **his** lunch. | "Everybody brought **their** lunch" — spoken English accepts it; exam key says **his**. |
| 8 | **The same** is followed by **that**, not who/which, when "same" means identical. | This is the same book **that** I lost. | "the same man **who** came" ❌ → **that** came. |
| 9 | **Who** = subject; **whom** = object; **whose** = possession. | **Whom** did you invite? | "Who did you give the book to?" — exam expects **Whom**. |
| 10 | A **relative pronoun** agrees with its antecedent, not with the word nearest to it. | It is I who **am** responsible. | "It is I who **is** responsible" ❌ — verb follows "I", not "who". |
| 11 | **Which** is used for things/animals and for **selection among a definite group**; **what** when there is no definite choice. | **Which** of the two pens is yours? | "What of these two do you want?" ❌ → **Which** (choice is limited to two). |
| 12 | After **than**, comparisons of possessives need the **possessive pronoun** (mine, yours, his) — don't compare a person with a thing. | Your house is bigger than **mine**. | "Your house is bigger than **me**" ❌ — compares house with a person. |

## (b) Time Management — The 15/45-Second Rule

**⏱️ 15-Second Questions (Fact-based — spot the rule, mark, move on):**
- Case errors after prepositions: *between you and I/me*, *let he/him go*.
- each other vs one another (just count: 2 or more than 2?).
- one…one's consistency.
- who/whom (ask: subject or object?).
- Singular pronoun after *each/every/everybody*.

**⏱️ 45-Second Questions (Inference-based — read the full sentence twice):**
- Pronoun–antecedent agreement hidden by an **intervening phrase**: *"The captain, along with his players, … their/his"* — find the real subject first.
- than/as case questions where **meaning changes** with case (more than I / more than me).
- Relative pronoun agreement: *"It is you who is/are…"* — trace back to the antecedent.
- Error-spotting items where the pronoun error sits in part (c) of a long sentence.

**Strategy:** In the exam, attempt all 15-second pronoun questions first; flag the agreement/meaning ones for the second pass.

## (c) Morphology — Roots & Affixes in Pronoun-Related Words

| Root / Affix | Meaning | Example Words |
|---|---|---|
| **pro-** (Latin) | for, in place of | pro-noun (a word used *in place of* a noun), pro-verb |
| **nom / nomin** (Latin *nomen*) | name | nom-inative case, nom-inee, de-nomination |
| **inter-** (Latin) | between | inter-rogative (asking *between* people), inter-vene |
| **-ive** (suffix) | having the nature of | relat-ive pronoun, demonstrat-ive, interrogat-ive |
| **-cede / -cedent** (Latin *cedere* = go) | go | ante-cedent (that which *goes before* the pronoun), pre-cede |
| **re-** (Latin) | back, again | re-flexive (bending *back* to the subject), re-fer |
| **-self** (Old English) | one's own person | my-self, them-selves (reflexive/emphatic pronouns) |
| **de-** (Latin) | down, away | de-monstrative (pointing *out*), de-clension |

**Memory hook:** *Antecedent* = **ante** (before) + **cedent** (going) → "the noun that goes before the pronoun." Learn the root, and half the grammar terminology decodes itself.
