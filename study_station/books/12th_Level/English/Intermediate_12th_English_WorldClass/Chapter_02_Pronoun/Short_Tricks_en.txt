# 🪄 Short Tricks + Skip Strategy — Pronoun

## Memory Boxes (Mnemonics)

**📦 Box 1 — "U ME HIM HER" (Objective Case after Preposition)**
After any preposition (to, with, between, for), always use the objective case.
✅ Between you and **me** (❌ between you and I)
Trick: *"Preposition ke baad I kabhi nahi — ME hi aayega!"*

**📦 Box 2 — "123 vs 231" (Order of Persons)**
- Good/positive sense → **2-3-1**: *You, he and I will go.*
- Bad/confession sense → **1-2-3**: *I, you and he are guilty.*
Trick: *"Achhe kaam mein peeche, bure kaam mein aage."*

**📦 Box 3 — "Each & Every = HE"**
Each, every, everyone, everybody, someone, anyone, nobody → **singular** pronoun + singular verb.
✅ Everyone brought **his** book.
Trick: *"E-words (each/every/everyone) are lonely — they take singular."*

**📦 Box 4 — "Than/As + I or ME?"**
Say the hidden verb aloud:
- *She is taller than I (am).* → subject comparison → **I**
- *He loves you more than (he loves) me.* → object comparison → **me**
Trick: *"Complete the sentence silently — the case reveals itself."*

**📦 Box 5 — "It is I, not It is me" (Formal English)**
After the verb **to be** (is/am/was/were), use the **subjective** case.
✅ It is **I** who called. (❌ It is me — acceptable only in speech, not in exams)

**📦 Box 6 — "Let + ME"**
After **let**, always objective case.
✅ Let you and **me** go. (❌ Let you and I go)

**📦 Box 7 — "One → One's"**
If the subject is **one**, the pronoun must be **one/one's/oneself** — never his/her.
✅ One should do **one's** duty.

**📦 Box 8 — "AVOID = Singular" (Either/Neither)**
**A**nyone, **V**eryone(everyone), **O**ne(each one), **I**ndividual, **D**ual(either/neither) → singular.
✅ Neither of the boys has done **his** work.

**📦 Box 9 — "Who vs Whom — the HIM test"**
Answer the question with **he** → use **who**; answer with **him** → use **whom**.
- ___ called? → **He** called → **Who**
- To ___ did you speak? → to **him** → **Whom**
Trick: *"HIM = WHOM (both end in M)."*

**📦 Box 10 — "Same + as, Such + as"**
Relative pronoun **as** follows *same* and *such*.
✅ This is the **same** book **as** I bought.

**📦 Box 11 — "But/Except = Objective"**
After *but* (meaning except) and *except*, use objective case.
✅ Everyone but **him** came.

**📦 Box 12 — "Possessive before Gerund"**
Before a verb-ing used as a noun, use possessive: **my, his, their** — not me/him/them.
✅ I object to **his** coming late. (❌ him coming)

**📦 Box 13 — "Reflexive needs a Mirror"**
A reflexive pronoun (myself, himself) must have its subject in the same sentence.
❌ Myself went to Delhi. ✅ **I** went to Delhi. / I **myself** went to Delhi.

**📦 Box 14 — "Both/All/Few = Plural"**
Both, all, few, many, several → plural pronoun.
✅ All students brought **their** books.

**📦 Box 15 — "Each other (2) vs One another (3+)"**
Two people → **each other**; more than two → **one another**.
✅ Ram and Shyam help **each other**. / The four friends help **one another**.

## ⏭️ Skip Strategy

**Tip 1 — Skip the "Sound-Right" trap in 10 seconds.**
If an option *sounds* natural in spoken English ("between you and I", "it is me", "him coming"), be suspicious. Spoken English and exam grammar often differ. When two options both "sound fine," pick the one that follows the **rule**, not the ear — and if you can't recall the rule in 15 seconds, mark and move on.

**Tip 2 — Long intervening phrase? Slash it, then decide.**
In sentences like *"The captain, along with his team members, ___ (has/have) arrived,"* mentally cross out everything between the commas. If the sentence still takes more than 45 seconds after slashing, skip it and return — these are designed to eat time.

**Tip 3 — Never fight a "who/whom" question beyond 30 seconds.**
Apply the HIM test once. If the sentence structure is too tangled (embedded clauses, passive voice), don't re-read it three times — flag it, finish the easy fact-based pronoun questions first, and come back with a fresh eye. One tough question is never worth three easy ones.
