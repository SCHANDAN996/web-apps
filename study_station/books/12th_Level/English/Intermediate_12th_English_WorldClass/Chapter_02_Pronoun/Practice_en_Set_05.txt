Questions 101–120: Easy | Questions 121–125: Moderate

101. Each of the students in the class has submitted ______ assignment on time.
(a) his or her (b) their (c) them (d) theirs
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Each of" takes a singular pronoun, so "his or her" is correct. "Their" is a common trap because it sounds natural in speech, but traditional exam grammar demands singular agreement after "each of".
Source: PYQ-style

102. The team ______ celebrating ______ victory in the tournament.
(a) are, its (b) is, its (c) is, their (d) are, their
Answer: (b)
Solution: Here the team acts as one united body, so the collective noun takes the singular "is" and the singular possessive "its". "Their" tempts candidates who think of the individual players, but unity in the sentence demands singular forms.
Source: PYQ-style

103. One should do ______ duty honestly.
(a) his (b) one's (c) their (d) our
Answer: (b)
Solution: The pronoun "one" must be followed by "one's", not "his" or "their". This is a classic trap because "his" was traditionally accepted, but modern exam keys prefer consistent "one... one's".
Source: NCERT-based grammar

104. It was ______ who called you last night.
(a) I (b) me (c) mine (d) myself
Answer: (a)
Solution: After the verb "to be" (was), the nominative case "I" is used in formal grammar. "Me" is the sound-right trap that spoken English makes tempting.
Source: PYQ-style

105. Neither of the two brothers has brought ______ books.
(a) their (b) his (c) them (d) theirs
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Neither of" is singular, so it takes "his". "Their" is the trap here because everyday speech often pairs "neither" with plural pronouns.
Source: PYQ-style

106. The teacher praised the boy ______ had won the first prize.
(a) which (b) who (c) whom (d) whose
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Who" is the relative pronoun for persons as the subject of the clause. "Which" is for things, and "whom" is used only in the objective case.
Source: NCERT Class 9 English

107. Spot the error: Part P: Avail the opportunity / Part Q: without any delay / Part R: if you want / Part S: to succeed in life.
(a) Part R (b) Part Q (c) No error (d) Part P
Answer: (d)
Solution: "Avail" is always used reflexively: "avail yourself of the opportunity". Part P lacks the reflexive pronoun and the preposition "of", which is a frequent examiner trap.
Source: PYQ-style

108. Between you and ______, this plan will never work.
(a) I (b) mine (c) me (d) myself
Answer: (c)
Solution: After the preposition "between", the objective case "me" is required. "Between you and I" is a famous sound-right trap tested repeatedly in exams.
Source: PYQ-style

109. The dog wagged ______ tail when it saw its master.
(a) it's (b) their (c) his (d) its
Answer: (d)
Solution: "Its" is the possessive pronoun; "it's" means "it is". Examiners love this trap because the apostrophe wrongly suggests possession.
Source: NCERT Class 8 English

110. He and ______ went to the market together.
(a) I (b) me (c) mine (d) himself
Answer: (a)
Solution: The pronoun is part of the subject, so the nominative "I" is correct. Test it by removing "He and" — "I went" is right, "me went" is wrong.
Source: PYQ-style

111. Spot the error: Part P: One of my friends / Part Q: are going / Part R: to Delhi / Part S: tomorrow morning.
(a) Part P (b) Part R (c) No error (d) Part Q
Answer: (d)
Solution: "One of my friends" is singular, so the verb must be "is going", not "are going". The plural word "friends" in the intervening phrase misleads many candidates.
Source: PYQ-style

112. This is the man ______ car was stolen yesterday.
(a) who (b) whom (c) whose (d) which
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Whose" shows possession — the car belongs to the man. "Who" and "whom" cannot express possession, and "which" is not used for persons.
Source: NCERT Class 10 English

113. None of the milk ______ spilt on the floor.
(a) were (b) are (c) was (d) have been
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Milk" is uncountable, so "none of the milk" takes a singular verb "was". With uncountable nouns after "none of", the verb is always singular.
Source: PYQ-style

114. The two sisters love ______ very much.
(a) each other (b) one another (c) themselves (d) one other
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Each other" is used for two persons; "one another" is used for more than two. Since there are two sisters, "each other" is correct — a classic reciprocal-pronoun trap.
Source: PYQ-style

115. He ______ completed the project without any help.
(a) itself (b) hisself (c) oneself (d) himself
Answer: (d)
Solution: The reflexive pronoun for "he" is "himself". "Hisself" does not exist in standard English, and "itself" refers to things or animals.
Source: NCERT Class 8 English

116. Spot the error: Part P: Every student and every teacher / Part Q: have attended / Part R: the meeting held / Part S: in the auditorium.
(a) No error (b) Part P (c) Part R (d) Part Q
Answer: (d)
Solution: When two singular nouns joined by "and" are each preceded by "every", the subject is singular, so "has attended" is required. The word "and" tricks candidates into choosing a plural verb.
Source: PYQ-style

117. ______ of the two answers is correct?
(a) What (b) Which (c) Whose (d) Whom
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Which" is used when choosing from a limited, definite set — here, two answers. "What" is used when the choice is open-ended or unlimited.
Source: PYQ-style

118. The jury ______ given ______ verdict unanimously.
(a) have, their (b) has, their (c) has, its (d) have, its
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Unanimously" shows the jury acted as one body, so the collective noun is singular: "has given its verdict". If members disagreed, plural forms would be used.
Source: PYQ-style

119. Let you and ______ decide the matter peacefully.
(a) me (b) I (c) mine (d) myself
Answer: (a)
Solution: After the verb "let", the objective case is needed: "let you and me". The sound-right trap "let you and I" is wrong because "let" takes an object, not a subject.
Source: PYQ-style

120. It is ______ who is responsible for this mistake.
(a) him (b) he (c) his (d) himself
Answer: (b)
Solution: In the cleft structure "It is... who", the nominative case "he" follows the verb "is". "Him" is the spoken-English trap that examiners frequently plant.
Source: PYQ-style

121. Spot the error: Part P: The Ganges is / Part Q: one of the longest rivers / Part R: that flows / Part S: through northern India.
(a) Part P (b) Part Q (c) Part R (d) No error
Answer: (c)
Solution: After "one of the + plural noun", the relative clause refers to "rivers" (plural), so it should be "that flow". The singular "flows" is a trap created by the nearby singular word "one".
Source: PYQ-style

122. ______ of the information given in the report is reliable.
(a) Many (b) Several (c) Much (d) Few
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Information" is uncountable, so "much" is correct. "Many", "few", and "several" are used only with countable plural nouns — a frequent countable/uncountable trap.
Source: NCERT Class 9 English

123. The manager, along with his assistants, ______ left for the conference.
(a) has (b) have (c) were (d) are
Answer: (a)
Solution: With "along with", the verb agrees with the first subject, "the manager" (singular), so "has" is correct. The intervening phrase "along with his assistants" is the classic trap.
Source: PYQ-style

124. There isn't ______ sugar left in the jar; buy some.
(a) several (b) many (c) few (d) much
Answer: (d)
Solution: "Sugar" is uncountable, so "much" is used in negative sentences. "Many", "few", and "several" apply only to countable nouns.
Source: NCERT Class 8 English

125. Spot the error: Part P: Neither the teacher / Part Q: nor the students / Part R: was present / Part S: in the classroom.
(a) Part R (b) Part Q (c) Part P (d) No error
Answer: (a)
Solution: With "neither... nor", the verb agrees with the nearer subject, "students" (plural), so "were present" is correct. Proximity agreement is one of the most tested pronoun-verb traps.
Source: PYQ-style
