51. Choose the correct pronoun: "Neither of the two boys has brought ___ books."
(a) their (b) his (c) them (d) they
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Neither of" takes a singular pronoun, so "his" is correct. "Their" is a common trap because two boys are mentioned, but "neither" means "not one of them" — singular.
Source: NCERT Class 9 English Grammar

52. Fill in the blank: "She is taller than ___."
(a) I (b) me (c) mine (d) myself
Answer: (a)
Solution: After "than", the subject form is used because the full clause is "than I am". "Me" is widely used in speech but is treated as incorrect in exams.
Source: PYQ-style

53. Choose the correct option: "Each of the students ___ given a certificate."
(a) were (b) have (c) are (d) was
Answer: (d)
Solution: "Each of" is always singular, so the singular verb "was" is correct. The plural noun "students" right before the verb is the examiner's trap.
Source: NCERT Class 10 English Grammar

54. Fill in the blank: "I, you and he ___ responsible for this mistake."
(a) is (b) are (c) am (d) was
Answer: (b)
Solution: When pronouns of different persons are joined, the verb is plural. For admitting faults, the order is first person first: "I, you and he".
Source: PYQ-style

55. Choose the correct pronoun: "The jury ___ divided in their opinion."
(a) is (b) was (c) were (d) has
Answer: (c)
Solution: When a collective noun like "jury" acts as separate individuals (shown by "divided" and "their"), it takes a plural verb. If it acted as one body, "was" would be correct.
Source: NCERT Class 10 English Grammar

56. Fill in the blank: "One should do ___ duty honestly."
(a) one's (b) his (c) their (d) our
Answer: (a)
Solution: The pronoun "one" must be followed by "one's", not "his" or "their". This is a classic examiner trap.
Source: PYQ-style

57. Choose the correct option: "It is ___ who called you yesterday."
(a) me (b) I (c) mine (d) myself
Answer: (b)
Solution: After "It is", the nominative (subject) form of the pronoun is used: "It is I". "Me" is common in speech but wrong in formal grammar.
Source: NCERT Class 9 English Grammar

58. Fill in the blank: "Between you and ___, this plan will fail."
(a) me (b) I (c) mine (d) myself
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Between" is a preposition, so it takes the object form "me". "Between you and I" is a famous sound-right trap.
Source: PYQ-style

59. Choose the correct pronoun: "Every student and every teacher ___ present in the hall."
(a) were (b) are (c) was (d) have
Answer: (c)
Solution: When two nouns are joined by "and" but each is preceded by "every", the subject is singular, so "was" is correct.
Source: NCERT Class 10 English Grammar

60. Fill in the blank: "He hurt ___ while climbing the tree."
(a) him (b) his (c) he (d) himself
Answer: (d)
Solution: When the subject and object are the same person, a reflexive pronoun is used — "himself".
Source: NCERT Class 8 English Grammar

61. Spot the error: "The teacher asked the students (P) / that which of them (Q) / had completed the homework. (R) / No error (S)"
(a) Part P (b) Part Q (c) Part R (d) No error
Answer: (b)
Solution: Part Q is wrong. "Asked" already introduces the question, so "that" is unnecessary; the correct form is "asked the students which of them had completed the homework".
Source: PYQ-style

62. Choose the correct option: "None of the information ___ correct."
(a) is (b) were (c) are (d) have been
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Information" is uncountable, so "none of the information" takes a singular verb. "None" with uncountable nouns is always singular.
Source: NCERT Class 10 English Grammar

63. Fill in the blank: "You, he and I ___ going to attend the seminar."
(a) is (b) am (c) are (d) was
Answer: (c)
Solution: For positive statements, the order of personal pronouns is second person, third person, first person (2-3-1): "You, he and I", with a plural verb.
Source: PYQ-style

64. Choose the correct pronoun: "The committee ___ submitted its report."
(a) have (b) has (c) are (d) were
Answer: (b)
Solution: Here the committee acts as a single body ("its report"), so the singular verb "has" is correct. The clue is the singular pronoun "its".
Source: NCERT Class 10 English Grammar

65. Spot the error: "Each of the girls (P) / have brought (Q) / her own lunch. (R) / No error (S)"
(a) Part P (b) Part R (c) Part Q (d) No error
Answer: (c)
Solution: Part Q is wrong. "Each of" takes a singular verb, so it should be "has brought". The plural "girls" in between is the intervening-phrase trap.
Source: PYQ-style

66. Fill in the blank: "Whose book is this? It is ___."
(a) her (b) she (c) hers (d) she's
Answer: (c)
Solution: A possessive pronoun that stands alone (without a noun after it) is "hers". "Her" needs a noun after it, e.g. "her book".
Source: NCERT Class 8 English Grammar

67. Choose the correct option: "One of my friends ___ gone to Delhi."
(a) have (b) are (c) has (d) were
Answer: (c)
Solution: "One of + plural noun" takes a singular verb because the real subject is "one". "Friends" is only part of the intervening phrase.
Source: NCERT Class 9 English Grammar

68. Fill in the blank: "He and ___ went to the market together."
(a) me (b) myself (c) mine (d) I
Answer: (d)
Solution: The pronoun is part of the subject of "went", so the subject form "I" is needed. Test by removing "He and" — "I went" is correct, "me went" is not.
Source: PYQ-style

69. Choose the correct pronoun: "Neither Ravi nor his brothers ___ present at the meeting."
(a) were (b) is (c) was (d) has been
Answer: (a)
Solution: With "neither...nor", the verb agrees with the nearer subject — "his brothers" (plural), so "were" is correct.
Source: NCERT Class 10 English Grammar

70. Spot the error: "Myself and Rohan (P) / went to (Q) / the library yesterday. (R) / No error (S)"
(a) Part P (b) Part Q (c) Part R (d) No error
Answer: (a)
Solution: Part P is wrong. A reflexive pronoun like "myself" cannot be the subject of a sentence; the correct form is "Rohan and I went to the library".
Source: PYQ-style

71. Fill in the blank: "The two sisters love ___ very much."
(a) one other (b) one another (c) themselves (d) each other
Answer: (d)
Solution: "Each other" is used for two persons; "one another" is used for more than two. Since there are two sisters, "each other" is correct.
Source: NCERT Class 9 English Grammar

72. Choose the correct option: "It was ___ who broke the window."
(a) he (b) him (c) his (d) himself
Answer: (a)
Solution: After "It was", the nominative form "he" is used because the pronoun is the subject of the relative clause "who broke the window".
Source: PYQ-style

73. Fill in the blank: "Many a student ___ failed in this examination."
(a) have (b) has (c) are (d) were
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Many a" is followed by a singular noun and a singular verb, even though the meaning is plural. This is a favourite examiner trap.
Source: NCERT Class 10 English Grammar

74. Choose the correct pronoun: "Let you and ___ solve this problem together."
(a) I (b) myself (c) mine (d) me
Answer: (d)
Solution: "Let" is a verb that takes an object, so the object form "me" is correct. "Let you and I" is a sound-right trap.
Source: PYQ-style

75. Spot the error: "Neither of the three brothers (P) / have passed (Q) / the entrance test. (R) / No error (S)"
(a) No error (b) Part Q (c) Part R (d) Part P
Answer: (d)
Solution: Part P is wrong. "Neither" is used for two; for three or more, "none" should be used — "None of the three brothers has passed". Part Q would then also need "has", but the primary error is in Part P.
Source: PYQ-style
