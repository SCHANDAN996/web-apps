126. Neither the manager nor the employees were satisfied with ______ performance in the annual review.
(a) his (b) our (c) their (d) its
Answer: (c)
Solution: With "neither…nor", the pronoun agrees with the nearer subject "employees" (plural), so "their" is correct. "His" would agree only with "manager".
Source: NCERT Class 10 English Grammar

127. Each of the boys has brought ______ own lunch box.
(a) their (b) his (c) its (d) our
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Each of" takes a singular pronoun; traditionally "his" is used in formal exam English. "Their" is the common trap because "boys" is plural, but the true subject is "each".
Source: PYQ-style

128. It was I who ______ responsible for the delay.
(a) am (b) is (c) were (d) was
Answer: (d)
Solution: The relative pronoun "who" refers to "I", but the sentence is in past time ("It was"), so "was" fits. The verb after "who" agrees with its antecedent and the tense of the main clause.
Source: NCERT Class 9 English Grammar

129. The committee has submitted ______ report to the chairman.
(a) their (b) his (c) our (d) its
Answer: (d)
Solution: "Committee" acting as a single unit (unanimous action — submitting one report) takes the singular pronoun "its". "Their" is used only when members act separately.
Source: PYQ-style

130. Between you and ______, this plan will never work.
(a) I (b) myself (c) me (d) mine
Answer: (c)
Solution: After the preposition "between", the objective case "me" is required. "Between you and I" is a classic Sound-Right trap — it sounds polite but is grammatically wrong.
Source: NCERT Class 10 English Grammar

131. One should do ______ duty honestly.
(a) his (b) their (c) one's (d) our
Answer: (c)
Solution: When "one" is the subject, the pronoun must be "one's" throughout the sentence. Mixing "one" with "his" or "their" is a frequent examiner trap.
Source: NCERT Class 9 English Grammar

132. He is one of those students who ______ always punctual.
(a) is (b) are (c) was (d) has been
Answer: (b)
Solution: In "one of those + plural noun + who", the relative pronoun "who" refers to the plural "students", so the verb is "are". The trap is agreeing with "one".
Source: PYQ-style

133. The two brothers love ______ very much.
(a) one another (b) themselves (c) each other (d) them
Answer: (c)
Solution: Traditionally "each other" is used for two persons and "one another" for more than two. Since there are two brothers, "each other" is correct.
Source: NCERT Class 8 English Grammar

134. Avail ______ of this opportunity before it is lost.
(a) yourself (b) you (c) your (d) yours
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Avail" is always followed by a reflexive pronoun: "avail yourself of". Using "you" or "your" after "avail" is a common error tested in exams.
Source: PYQ-style

135. Whom do you think ______ the prize yesterday?
(a) win (b) winning (c) won (d) wins
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Do you think" is a parenthetical insertion; the real question is "Who won the prize yesterday?" — past tense "won" is needed. The insertion is an Intervening Phrase trap.
Source: PYQ-style

136. Spot the error: The principal, along with the teachers, (P) / have decided that they (Q) / will attend the workshop (R) / held next Monday. (S)
(a) Part P (b) Part R (c) Part Q (d) No error
Answer: (c)
Solution: With "along with", the verb and pronoun agree with the first subject "principal" (singular). Part Q should read "has decided that he". The phrase "along with the teachers" is an Intervening Phrase trap.
Source: PYQ-style

137. Spot the error: Everyone of the students (P) / have submitted (Q) / their assignments (R) / before the deadline. (S)
(a) No error (b) Part P (c) Part R (d) Part Q
Answer: (d)
Solution: "Everyone" is singular, so Part Q should be "has submitted". Consequently Part R should be "his assignment", but the first error occurs in Part Q.
Source: NCERT Class 10 English Grammar

138. Spot the error: It is me (P) / who is to blame (Q) / for the loss (R) / of the documents. (S)
(a) No error (b) Part Q (c) Part R (d) Part P
Answer: (d)
Solution: After "It is", formal grammar requires the nominative case: "It is I". Also, since "who" refers to "I", Part Q should be "who am to blame", but the first error is in Part P. "It is me" is a Sound-Right trap.
Source: PYQ-style

139. Spot the error: The jury were divided (P) / in its opinion (Q) / regarding the verdict (R) / announced in court. (S)
(a) Part P (b) Part Q (c) Part R (d) No error
Answer: (b)
Solution: When "jury" is divided (members acting separately), the verb is plural ("were"), so the pronoun must also be plural: "in their opinion". Mixing plural verb with singular "its" is the error.
Source: NCERT Class 10 English Grammar

140. Spot the error: He and myself (P) / went to the library (Q) / to collect (R) / our books. (S)
(a) Part P (b) Part Q (c) Part R (d) No error
Answer: (a)
Solution: A reflexive pronoun cannot be the subject of a sentence. Part P should be "He and I". Using "myself" as a subject is a very common Sound-Right trap.
Source: PYQ-style

141. Choose the grammatically correct sentence:
(a) Whom does this pen belong to? (b) To who does this pen belong? (c) Whose does this pen belong to? (d) Who this pen belongs to?
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Who" here is the object of the preposition "to", so the objective form "whom" is correct: "Whom does this pen belong to?" Option with "to who" breaks the preposition rule, and "whose" wrongly shows possession.
Source: NCERT Class 10 English Grammar

142. Spot the error: Neither of the two candidates (P) / have submitted (Q) / their certificates (R) / to the office. (S)
(a) Part P (b) Part Q (c) Part R (d) No error
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Neither of" is singular, so Part Q should be "has submitted" and Part R "his certificate". The plural "candidates" nearby is an Intervening Phrase trap.
Source: PYQ-style

143. Choose the correct sentence:
(a) The climate of Pune is better than Mumbai. (b) The climate of Pune is better than it of Mumbai. (c) The climate of Pune is better than those of Mumbai. (d) The climate of Pune is better than that of Mumbai.
Answer: (d)
Solution: Comparison must be between two climates, not between climate and city. "That of" stands for "the climate of" (singular). "Those" would wrongly suggest plural.
Source: NCERT Class 10 English Grammar

144. Spot the error: Myself and my friend (P) / enjoyed ourselves (Q) / at the fair (R) / last Sunday. (S)
(a) Part P (b) Part Q (c) Part R (d) No error
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Myself" cannot begin a sentence as subject; Part P should be "My friend and I". Note the polite order — the other person comes first.
Source: PYQ-style

145. Choose the correct option: The manager wanted you and ______ to attend the meeting.
(a) me (b) I (c) myself (d) mine
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Wanted" is a verb; its object takes the objective case — "you and me". Test by removing "you and": "The manager wanted me" is clearly right, "wanted I" is wrong.
Source: NCERT Class 9 English Grammar

146. Spot the error: Each student and each teacher (P) / were asked to bring (Q) / their identity card (R) / for verification. (S)
(a) Part Q (b) No error (c) Part R (d) Part P
Answer: (a)
Solution: Subjects joined by "and" but each preceded by "each" take a singular verb: "was asked". Part R should also be "his identity card". This is a classic Over-thinking trap — two nouns look plural but the rule makes them singular.
Source: PYQ-style

147. Choose the correct sentence:
(a) He is the same man whom I saw yesterday. (b) He is the same man that I saw yesterday. (c) He is the same man who I saw yesterday. (d) He is the same man which I saw yesterday.
Answer: (b)
Solution: After "the same", the relative pronoun "that" is preferred when the clause describes an action done to the person ("the same…that"). "Same…as" is used for similarity of kind, "same…that" for identity.
Source: NCERT Class 10 English Grammar

148. Spot the error: There is no question (P) / of me returning (Q) / the money (R) / before Friday. (S)
(a) Part Q (b) Part P (c) Part R (d) No error
Answer: (a)
Solution: Before a gerund ("returning"), the possessive case is required: "of my returning". Using the objective "me" before a gerund is a frequent examiner trap.
Source: PYQ-style

149. Choose the correct option: ______ of the information you gave me was useful.
(a) Many (b) Much (c) Few (d) Several
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Information" is uncountable, so it takes "much" (singular verb "was" confirms it). "Many", "few" and "several" are used only with countable plurals.
Source: NCERT Class 8 English Grammar

150. Spot the error: Let you and I (P) / promise each other (Q) / to keep this secret (R) / forever. (S)
(a) Part R (b) Part Q (c) No error (d) Part P
Answer: (d)
Solution: After "let" (a verb), the objective case is needed: "Let you and me". "Let you and I" is a Sound-Right trap — it feels formal but breaks the case rule.
Source: PYQ-style
