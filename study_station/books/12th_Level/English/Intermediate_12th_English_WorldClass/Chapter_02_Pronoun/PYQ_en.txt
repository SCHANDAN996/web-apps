# 📊 PYQ Analysis — Pronoun

## How Exams Test Pronouns (Pattern Analysis)

Across SSC (CGL, CHSL, MTS, GD), RRB NTPC, IBPS and State exams, pronoun questions appear in three formats:

1. **Error Spotting** — one part of the sentence has a wrong pronoun (case, number, or agreement).
2. **Fill in the Blanks** — choose the correct pronoun form.
3. **Sentence Improvement** — replace the underlined pronoun.

**Most frequently tested sub-topics (in words, not numbers):**

- **Pronoun–antecedent agreement** — "Everyone… *their*?" vs "Everyone… *his/her*". This is the single most repeated trap.
- **Case after prepositions and verbs** — "between you and *I*" ❌ → "between you and *me*" ✅.
- **Intervening phrases** — "The captain, along with his men, *was/were*…" — the phrase between subject and verb misleads students into wrong agreement.
- **Each / Every / Either / Neither** take singular pronouns — examiners pair them with plural-sounding nouns.
- **Relative pronouns** — *who/whom/whose/which/that*; "the man *which* came" ❌.
- **Reflexive pronouns** — "He *himself* did it" vs wrong uses like "Myself went to Delhi" ❌.
- **One… one's / one** — "One should do *his* duty" ❌ → "One should do *one's* duty" ✅.
- **It vs This/That** for introducing a subject: "*It* is I who am responsible."

**Examiner's favourite traps (Trap Types):**

| Trap Type | What it looks like |
|---|---|
| **Intervening Phrase** | Extra words between subject and pronoun/verb hide the real subject |
| **Sound-Right** | "Between you and I" *sounds* polite and correct — it is wrong |
| **Over-thinking** | A simple rule question dressed in a long sentence; students change a correct option |
| **Collective Noun** | "The jury has given *its* verdict" (united) vs "The jury are divided in *their* opinion" (divided) |
| **Near-name confusion** | *its/it's*, *their/there/they're*, *whose/who's* |

## Top 10 High-Yield Questions (PYQ-style) with Solutions

**Q1.** Each of the students (a)/ have submitted (b)/ their assignment on time. (c)/ No error (d)
**Answer:** (b) and (c) — should be *has submitted* and *his/her assignment*.
**Solution:** "Each of + plural noun" takes a **singular** verb and singular pronoun. The plural noun "students" is the decoy.
**Trap Type:** Intervening Phrase
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q2.** Between you and I, (a)/ this plan (b)/ will never succeed. (c)/ No error (d)
**Answer:** (a) — should be *between you and me*.
**Solution:** After a preposition (*between*), use the **objective case** — *me*, not *I*. Politeness does not change grammar.
**Trap Type:** Sound-Right
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q3.** The manager, along with his assistants, (a)/ have left (b)/ for the conference. (c)/ No error (d)
**Answer:** (b) — should be *has left*.
**Solution:** With "along with / as well as / together with", the verb agrees with the **first** subject (*the manager* — singular).
**Trap Type:** Intervening Phrase
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q4.** One should always do (a)/ his duty (b)/ honestly. (c)/ No error (d)
**Answer:** (b) — should be *one's duty*.
**Solution:** The pronoun for **one** is **one/one's**, never *his/their*.
**Trap Type:** Sound-Right
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q5.** The jury (a)/ are divided (b)/ in its opinion. (c)/ No error (d)
**Answer:** (c) — should be *their opinion*.
**Solution:** When a collective noun acts as **separate individuals** ("are divided"), the pronoun is plural — *their*. If united ("has given"), use *its*.
**Trap Type:** Collective Noun
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q6.** It is me (a)/ who is responsible (b)/ for this delay. (c)/ No error (d)
**Answer:** (a) and (b) — should be *It is I who am responsible*.
**Solution:** After "It is", formal English uses the **subjective case** (*I*), and the verb agrees with *who* → *I* → *am*.
**Trap Type:** Over-thinking (students "correct" the wrong part)
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q7.** Neither of the two brothers (a)/ have returned (b)/ their books. (c)/ No error (d)
**Answer:** (b) and (c) — should be *has returned* and *his books*.
**Solution:** **Neither of** = singular. Verb *has*, pronoun *his*.
**Trap Type:** Intervening Phrase
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q8.** Myself and my friend (a)/ went to (b)/ the library yesterday. (c)/ No error (d)
**Answer:** (a) — should be *My friend and I*.
**Solution:** A reflexive pronoun (*myself*) can never be a subject. Also, mention yourself **last** out of courtesy.
**Trap Type:** Sound-Right
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q9.** This is the boy (a)/ which won (b)/ the first prize. (c)/ No error (d)
**Answer:** (b) — should be *who won*.
**Solution:** For persons, the relative pronoun is **who/that**, never *which*. *Which* is for things/animals.
**Trap Type:** Near-name confusion
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q10.** Everyone should bring (a)/ their own lunch (b)/ to the picnic. (c)/ No error (d)
**Answer:** (b) — in traditional exam grammar, should be *his/her own lunch*.
**Solution:** *Everyone, everybody, someone, anyone, nobody* are **singular** in exam English. (Modern usage accepts "their", but SSC/Bank keys follow the singular rule.)
**Trap Type:** Sound-Right
**Source:** PYQ-style

## ⏱️ Applying the 15/45-Second Rule

**15-Second (Fact-based) — Q9 above:**
See "the boy **which**" → rule fires instantly: persons take *who*. No need to read the whole sentence twice. Spot the relative pronoun, check its antecedent, mark, move on. **Target: 15 seconds.**

**45-Second (Inference-based) — Q5 above:**
"The jury are divided in its opinion" needs two steps: (1) Is the collective noun united or divided? "Are divided" → individuals acting separately. (2) Therefore the pronoun must be plural → *their*, not *its*. You must reason about meaning, not just recall a rule. **Target: 45 seconds — worth the time; do not rush these.**

---

## Key Takeaways

- **Agreement traps** (each/every/everyone + singular) dominate — master them first.
- When a sentence feels long, **delete the intervening phrase mentally** and check subject–pronoun agreement again.
- If an option "sounds right" because you hear it in daily speech (*between you and I*, *it is me*), treat it as a **suspect**, not an answer.
- Collective nouns: united → *its*; divided → *their*. Check the verb for the clue.
