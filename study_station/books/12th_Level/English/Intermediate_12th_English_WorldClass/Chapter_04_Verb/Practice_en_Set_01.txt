Verb — Practice Set 1 (Questions 1–25)
Questions 1–20: Easy | Questions 21–25: Medium

1. She ______ to school every day.
(a) go (b) goes (c) going (d) gone
Answer: (b)
Solution: With a singular subject "she" in simple present tense, the verb takes -s/-es, so "goes" is correct. "Go" would need a plural subject.
Source: NCERT Class 9 English Grammar

2. They ______ playing cricket in the park now.
(a) are (b) is (c) was (d) has
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Now" signals present continuous tense, and "they" is plural, so "are playing" is correct. "Is" is used only with singular subjects.
Source: NCERT Class 8 English Grammar

3. The train ______ before we reached the station.
(a) leaves (b) has left (c) left (d) had left
Answer: (d)
Solution: When two past actions occur, the earlier one takes past perfect ("had left") and the later one simple past ("reached"). "Left" alone would not show the sequence clearly.
Source: NCERT Class 10 English Grammar

4. Each of the boys ______ a book.
(a) has (b) were having (c) are having (d) have
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Each of" is always followed by a singular verb, so "has" is correct. "Have" is a trap because "boys" looks like the subject, but the real subject is "each".
Source: PYQ-style (examiner trap: intervening phrase)

5. I ______ my homework yesterday.
(a) do (b) done (c) doing (d) did
Answer: (d)
Solution: "Yesterday" indicates simple past tense, so "did" is correct. "Done" is a past participle and cannot stand alone as the main verb here.
Source: NCERT Class 8 English Grammar

6. Bread and butter ______ his daily breakfast.
(a) are (b) is (c) were (d) have been
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Bread and butter" is treated as one combined dish, so it takes a singular verb "is". "Are" is a trap for those who count two nouns joined by "and".
Source: PYQ-style (examiner trap: compound subject treated as one)

7. He ______ in this city since 2015.
(a) lives (b) has lived (c) is living (d) lived
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Since 2015" with an action continuing up to now requires present perfect tense, so "has lived" is correct. Simple present "lives" does not convey the duration from the past.
Source: NCERT Class 10 English Grammar

8. The teacher asked the students why they ______ late.
(a) be (b) are (c) is (d) were
Answer: (d)
Solution: In reported speech with a past reporting verb ("asked"), the verb shifts back, so "were" is correct. "Are" would break the sequence of tenses.
Source: NCERT Class 10 English Grammar

9. One of my friends ______ gone to Delhi.
(a) have (b) has (c) are (d) were
Answer: (b)
Solution: The subject is "one", which is singular, so "has" is correct. "Friends" is part of an intervening phrase and does not control the verb.
Source: PYQ-style

10. She ______ TV when I entered the room.
(a) watches (b) watched (c) was watching (d) has watched
Answer: (c)
Solution: An ongoing past action interrupted by another past action takes past continuous, so "was watching" is correct. "Watched" would suggest two sequential completed actions.
Source: NCERT Class 9 English Grammar

11. Neither Ram nor his brothers ______ present at the meeting.
(a) was (b) has been (c) is (d) were
Answer: (d)
Solution: With "neither…nor", the verb agrees with the nearer subject, which is "brothers" (plural), so "were" is correct. "Was" is a trap for those who match the verb with "Ram".
Source: PYQ-style

12. We ______ the project by next Monday.
(a) will have completed (b) are completing (c) complete (d) will complete
Answer: (a)
Solution: "By next Monday" indicates completion before a future point, so future perfect "will have completed" is correct. "Will complete" does not stress completion before the deadline.
Source: NCERT Class 10 English Grammar

13. The news ______ very shocking.
(a) were (b) is (c) are (d) have been
Answer: (b)
Solution: "News" is an uncountable noun and always takes a singular verb, so "is" is correct. "Are" is a common error because "news" ends in -s.
Source: NCERT Class 9 English Grammar

14. If it rains tomorrow, we ______ at home.
(a) will stay (b) would stay (c) stayed (d) stay
Answer: (a)
Solution: In a first conditional (real possibility), the if-clause takes simple present and the main clause takes "will + verb", so "will stay" is correct. "Would stay" belongs to the second conditional.
Source: NCERT Class 10 English Grammar

15. The Ganga ______ one of the longest rivers in India.
(a) are (b) were (c) is (d) have been
Answer: (c)
Solution: The subject "The Ganga" is singular, so "is" is correct. "Rivers" in the intervening phrase does not affect the verb.
Source: NCERT Class 8 English Grammar

16. He ______ English very well.
(a) speaking (b) speak (c) speaks (d) is speak
Answer: (c)
Solution: A habitual fact in simple present with a singular subject takes "speaks". "Is speak" is grammatically impossible because "is" must be followed by verb-ing.
Source: NCERT Class 8 English Grammar

17. Spot the error: Part P: The quality of the mangoes / Part Q: were not good / Part R: so we returned the basket. / Part S: No error
(a) Part P (b) No error (c) Part Q (d) Part R
Answer: (c)
Solution: The subject is "quality" (singular), not "mangoes", so Part Q should read "was not good". The plural noun in the intervening phrase misleads many candidates.
Source: PYQ-style (examiner trap: intervening phrase)

18. I ______ this movie twice already.
(a) have seen (b) see (c) saw (d) am seeing
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Already" with an experience up to the present requires present perfect, so "have seen" is correct. "Saw" would need a definite past time reference.
Source: NCERT Class 10 English Grammar

19. Mathematics ______ a difficult subject for many students.
(a) are (b) were (c) is (d) have been
Answer: (c)
Solution: Names of subjects ending in -ics (mathematics, physics) take a singular verb, so "is" is correct. "Are" is a trap due to the -s ending.
Source: NCERT Class 9 English Grammar

20. They ______ their lunch before the bell rang.
(a) had finished (b) finished (c) have finished (d) finish
Answer: (a)
Solution: The earlier of two past actions takes past perfect, so "had finished" is correct; "rang" is the later action. "Finished" alone would blur the sequence.
Source: NCERT Class 10 English Grammar

21. Spot the error: Part P: Neither of the two boys / Part Q: have done / Part R: his homework. / Part S: No error
(a) Part P (b) Part R (c) No error (d) Part Q
Answer: (d)
Solution: "Neither of" takes a singular verb, so Part Q should be "has done". The plural-looking phrase "two boys" is the examiner's trap.
Source: PYQ-style (examiner trap: neither of + singular verb)

22. By the time we reached the theatre, the film ______.
(a) had started (b) has started (c) started (d) starts
Answer: (a)
Solution: "By the time" with a past reference point requires past perfect for the earlier action, so "had started" is correct. "Started" would wrongly suggest both actions happened together.
Source: NCERT Class 10 English Grammar

23. Scarcely had he stepped out of the house ______ it began to rain.
(a) than (b) that (c) then (d) when
Answer: (d)
Solution: "Scarcely/Hardly…when" is the correct correlative pair, so "when" is correct. "Than" pairs with "no sooner", which is the classic confusion here.
Source: PYQ-style

24. The committee ______ divided in their opinion on the new policy.
(a) is (b) were (c) was (d) has been
Answer: (b)
Solution: When a collective noun's members act separately or disagree, it takes a plural verb; "divided in their opinion" shows the members differ, so "were" is correct. "Was" would fit only if the committee acted as one united body.
Source: PYQ-style (collective noun)

25. I wish I ______ a bird and could fly to you.
(a) am (b) was (c) were (d) will be
Answer: (c)
Solution: After "I wish" for an unreal present desire, the subjunctive "were" is used for all persons, so "were" is correct. "Was" is common in speech but considered incorrect in standard exam grammar.
Source: NCERT Class 10 English Grammar
