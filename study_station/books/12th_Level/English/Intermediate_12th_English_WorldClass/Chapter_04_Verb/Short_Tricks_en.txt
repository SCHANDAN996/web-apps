# 🪄 Short Tricks + Skip Strategy — Verb

## Mnemonic Boxes

**Box 1 — "AND = Plural" (The only joining word that adds subjects)**
> Two subjects joined by **and** → **plural verb**.
> "Ram **and** Shyam **are** friends." ✅
> Warning: the other FANBOYS words do **not** work this way —
> - **or / nor** → verb agrees with the **nearest** subject (see Box 3).
> - **but / yet / so / for** join clauses, not two subjects of one verb.
> Exception: if the two nouns name **one thing/person**, use a singular verb.
> "Rice and curry **is** my favourite meal." ✅ | "The poet and philosopher **is** dead." ✅ (one person)

**Box 2 — "EACH-EVERY-ONE" (Always Singular)**
> **Each, Every, Everyone, Everybody, Someone, Somebody, Anyone, Anybody, No one, Nobody** → singular verb.
> Trick: *"Each one is alone, so the verb stands alone (singular)."*
> "Everybody **is** present." ✅ (never "are")

**Box 3 — "NEITHER-NOR Rule: Verb loves the NEAR one"**
> With **either…or / neither…nor / not only…but also**, the verb agrees with the subject **closest** to it.
> "Neither the teacher nor the **students are** here." ✅
> "Neither the students nor the **teacher is** here." ✅

**Box 4 — "M-A-N-Y A" (Singular despite plural meaning)**
> **Many a / Many an** + singular noun + singular verb.
> "Many a soldier **has** died for the country." ✅

**Box 5 — "The 5 Senses Stay Adjective" (Linking Verbs)**
> **Look, Sound, Smell, Taste, Feel** → followed by an **adjective**, not an adverb.
> "The soup tastes **good**." ✅ (not "well")
> Trick: *"Senses describe, they don't act."*

**Box 6 — "NEWS is Singular" (Plural-looking singulars)**
> **News, Physics, Mathematics, Economics, Politics, Measles, Billiards** → singular verb.
> "The news **is** shocking." ✅
> Trick: *"Subjects and News end in 's' but think single."*

**Box 7 — "Scissors & Trousers Take a Pair" (Always plural)**
> **Scissors, trousers, spectacles, goggles, pliers, shoes** → plural verb.
> But "a **pair of** scissors **is**…" → singular (verb agrees with "pair").

**Box 8 — "I WISH I WERE" (Subjunctive)**
> Unreal wishes / conditions → **were** for all persons.
> "If I **were** a bird…" ✅ | "I wish I **were** rich." ✅
> Trick: *"In dreams, everyone is WERE."*

**Box 9 — "HAD + V3 = Earlier Past" (Past Perfect Signal)**
> Two past actions → the **earlier** one takes **had + V3**, the later one takes V2.
> "The train **had left** before I **reached** the station." ✅
> Trick: *"Before/After ke saath, pehle wala HAD pakadta hai."*

**Box 10 — "SINCE for Point, FOR for Period"**
> **Since** + point of time (Monday, 2010, childhood) | **For** + duration (two years, a long time).
> Both demand **perfect/perfect-continuous** tense.
> "He **has been working** here **since** 2015." ✅

**Box 11 — "One of the + Plural Noun + Singular Verb"**
> "One of the **boys has**…" ✅
> Trap: the noun is plural, but the verb follows **One**.
> Trick: *"ONE is the boss; the verb obeys ONE."*

**Box 12 — "Intervening Phrase? Delete it!"**
> Between subject and verb, if you see *with, along with, together with, as well as, in addition to* — **ignore that phrase**.
> "The captain, **along with his teammates**, **was** felicitated." ✅
> Trick: *"Along with = alone for the verb."*

**Box 13 — "There + Verb: Look AFTER the verb"**
> In "There is/are…" sentences, the real subject comes **after** the verb.
> "There **are** fifty students in the class." ✅

**Box 14 — "Hardly/Scarcely → WHEN; No sooner → THAN"**
> "Hardly **had** he entered **when** the phone rang." ✅
> "No sooner **had** she left **than** it rained." ✅
> Trick: *"No sooner THAN — they rhyme in exams, not in sound!"*

**Box 15 — "Causative: HAVE/GET + Object + V3"**
> "I **got** my watch **repaired**." ✅ (someone else did it)
> Trick: *"You don't do it — you GET it DONE."*

## ⏭️ Skip Strategy

**Tip 1 — Skip the "Over-thinking" trap question (for 30 seconds, not forever).**
If an error-spotting sentence looks perfectly correct and you've read it twice, mark it and move on. These are usually "No error" questions designed to make you invent a mistake. Return in the last 5 minutes with fresh eyes — the real error (often a tense mismatch or a hidden preposition) becomes visible.

**Tip 2 — Skip long intervening-phrase questions until you've crossed out the phrase.**
Never solve a subject-verb agreement question by reading the whole sentence aloud. First **strike out** everything between the subject and the verb ("along with…", "as well as…", "together with…"). If you can't identify the true subject within 15 seconds, flag it — it's a 45-second question, not a 15-second one.

**Tip 3 — Skip "Sound-Right" traps on the first pass.**
Questions testing *collective nouns* (The jury **is/are** divided) or *subjunctive mood* (If I **was/were**) sound correct in spoken Hindi-English habits, so your ear will mislead you. If your only basis is "it sounds right," skip and return — solve it by **rule**, not by ear. Rule-based solving takes 45 seconds; ear-based guessing loses the mark.
