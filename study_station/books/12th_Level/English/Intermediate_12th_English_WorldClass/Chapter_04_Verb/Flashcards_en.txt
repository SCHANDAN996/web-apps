Card 1
Front: What is a verb?
Back: A word that expresses an action, state, or occurrence — e.g., run, is, seem. Every complete sentence must have a verb.

Card 2
Front: What is the difference between a transitive and an intransitive verb?
Back: A transitive verb needs an object (She ate *an apple*). An intransitive verb does not take an object (He *sleeps*).

Card 3
Front: MNEMONIC — How do you remember the 8 forms of "be"?
Back: "I AM Wise, ARE You?" → am, is, are, was, were, be, being, been. These forms of "be" are the most common auxiliary verbs in English.

Card 4
Front: EXAMINER'S TRAP — "One of my friends ___ (is/are) coming." Which is correct?
Back: "is" — the subject is "One", not "friends". The phrase "of my friends" is an intervening phrase meant to mislead you.

Card 5
Front: What are the three principal parts of a verb? Give an example.
Back: Present (V1), Past (V2), Past Participle (V3) — e.g., go–went–gone; write–wrote–written.

Card 6
Front: What is a finite verb?
Back: A verb that changes with the subject and tense and can act as the main verb of a sentence — e.g., "She *writes* letters."

Card 7
Front: Name the three types of non-finite verbs.
Back: Infinitive (to + V1), Gerund (V1+ing as noun), Participle (present/past participle).

Card 8
Front: EXAMINER'S TRAP — "The news ___ (is/are) shocking." Which is correct?
Back: "is" — "news" looks plural but is an uncountable noun and always takes a singular verb. Same with physics, mathematics, politics.

Card 9
Front: After "did", which form of the verb is used?
Back: Always V1 (base form): "He did not *go*" — never "did not went". The past tense is already carried by "did".

Card 10
Front: What is a modal verb? Name five.
Back: A helping verb expressing ability, permission, possibility, or obligation — can, could, may, might, must (also shall, should, will, would, ought to).

Card 11
Front: MNEMONIC — Which verbs are followed by a gerund (V+ing), not an infinitive?
Back: "MICE PAGS" → Mind, Imagine, Consider, Enjoy, Postpone, Avoid, Give up, Suggest. E.g., "I enjoy *reading*," not "enjoy to read".

Card 12
Front: "Neither of the two boys ___ (has/have) done his homework." Which is correct?
Back: "has" — "neither of" takes a singular verb. Same rule for "either of", "each of", "every one of".

Card 13
Front: What is the difference between "lie" and "lay"?
Back: Lie (recline): lie–lay–lain, intransitive. Lay (put down): lay–laid–laid, transitive. "Lay the book on the table"; "I lay down yesterday."

Card 14
Front: Which verb form follows "had better", "would rather", and "let"?
Back: Bare infinitive (V1 without "to"): "You had better *study*." "Let him *go*."

Card 15
Front: What is a phrasal verb? Give two examples.
Back: A verb + preposition/adverb with a new meaning — e.g., "give up" (quit), "look after" (take care of). Meaning often cannot be guessed from the parts.

Card 16
Front: "Scissors ___ (is/are) on the table." Which is correct and why?
Back: "are" — pluralia tantum nouns (scissors, trousers, spectacles, goods) always take a plural verb. But "a pair of scissors *is*…"

Card 17
Front: What is the subjunctive form after "I wish" or "if" for unreal conditions?
Back: Use "were" for all persons: "If I *were* a bird…", "I wish I *were* taller." Not "was" in formal/exam English.

Card 18
Front: What is a causative verb structure?
Back: have/get + object + past participle (V3): "I *got* my hair *cut*." The subject arranges for someone else to do the action.

Card 19
Front: EXAMINER'S TRAP — "The teacher, as well as the students, ___ (was/were) present." Which is correct?
Back: "was" — with "as well as", "along with", "together with", the verb agrees with the FIRST subject only ("the teacher").

Card 20
Front: When do we use the present perfect vs. simple past?
Back: Present perfect (has/have + V3): action with present relevance, no finished time — "I *have lost* my keys." Simple past (V2): finished time — "I *lost* my keys yesterday." Never use present perfect with "yesterday/ago/last week".
