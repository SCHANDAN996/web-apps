101. Neither the manager nor the employees ______ aware of the new policy.
(a) was (b) were (c) is (d) has been
Answer: (b)
Solution: With "neither...nor", the verb agrees with the nearer subject. "Employees" is plural, so "were" is correct. "Was" tempts learners who match the verb with the first subject "manager".
Source: NCERT Class 10 English Grammar

102. The quality of the products ______ deteriorated since last year.
(a) have (b) were (c) are (d) has
Answer: (d)
Solution: The subject is "quality" (singular), not "products". The phrase "of the products" only modifies the subject, so "has" is correct. This is the classic intervening-phrase trap.
Source: PYQ-style

103. Each of the boys ______ given a certificate at the ceremony.
(a) were (b) are (c) have been (d) was
Answer: (d)
Solution: "Each of" always takes a singular verb, so "was" is correct. The plural noun "boys" after "of" does not control the verb.
Source: NCERT Class 9 English Grammar

104. By the time we reached the station, the train ______.
(a) left (b) has left (c) had left (d) was leaving
Answer: (c)
Solution: When two past actions occur, the earlier one takes the past perfect. The train left before we reached, so "had left" is correct.
Source: NCERT Class 10 English Grammar

105. Spot the error: Part P: One of my friend / Part Q: has gone / Part R: to Delhi yesterday.
(a) Part P (b) Part Q (c) Part R (d) No error
Answer: (a)
Solution: "One of" must be followed by a plural noun, so Part P should read "One of my friends". Part Q correctly uses the singular verb "has" because the real subject is "one".

106. I ______ this book since morning, and I am still reading it.
(a) have been reading (b) read (c) had read (d) am read
Answer: (a)
Solution: An action that began in the past and continues up to now, with "since morning", takes the present perfect continuous: "have been reading".
Source: NCERT Class 10 English Grammar

107. The committee ______ divided in their opinion on the issue.
(a) was (b) were (c) is (d) has been
Answer: (b)
Solution: When a collective noun's members act individually (shown by "their" and "divided"), a plural verb is used, so "were" is correct. "Was" would fit only if the committee acted as one body.
Source: PYQ-style

108. Hardly ______ the station when the train left.
(a) had I reached (b) I had reached (c) I reached (d) did I reach
Answer: (a)
Solution: After "hardly" at the start of a sentence, inversion is required with the past perfect: "Hardly had I reached... when...". "Did I reach" wrongly pairs inversion with the simple past.
Source: PYQ-style

109. He denied ______ the money from the drawer.
(a) to take (b) taken (c) take (d) taking
Answer: (d)
Solution: "Deny" is always followed by a gerund (verb-ing), never an infinitive, so "taking" is correct. "To take" is a common Hindi-speaker error.
Source: NCERT Class 10 English Grammar

110. Spot the error: Part P: The news / Part Q: broadcast last night / Part R: were quite disturbing.
(a) Part P (b) Part Q (c) Part R (d) No error
Answer: (c)
Solution: "News" is an uncountable noun and always takes a singular verb, so Part R should be "was quite disturbing". The plural-looking ending "-s" misleads many candidates.
Source: PYQ-style

111. If I ______ a bird, I would fly to you.
(a) were (b) was (c) am (d) had been
Answer: (a)
Solution: In hypothetical conditional sentences, "were" is used for all persons: "If I were...". "Was" is acceptable only in informal speech, not in exams.
Source: NCERT Class 10 English Grammar

112. The teacher, along with his students, ______ gone on a picnic.
(a) have (b) has (c) are (d) were
Answer: (b)
Solution: With "along with", the verb agrees with the first subject, "the teacher" (singular), so "has" is correct. The intervening phrase does not change the number.
Source: PYQ-style

113. She ______ her homework before her mother returned.
(a) finished (b) has finished (c) had finished (d) finishes
Answer: (c)
Solution: Finishing homework happened before the mother returned (both past), so the earlier action takes the past perfect: "had finished".
Source: NCERT Class 10 English Grammar

114. Spot the error: Part P: Bread and butter / Part Q: are / Part R: my favourite breakfast.
(a) Part Q (b) Part P (c) Part R (d) No error
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Bread and butter" is treated as one combined dish, so it takes a singular verb: Part Q should be "is". Two nouns joined by "and" are plural only when they name separate things.
Source: PYQ-style

115. I wish I ______ the offer earlier.
(a) accept (b) had accepted (c) accepted (d) have accepted
Answer: (b)
Solution: "I wish" about a past regret takes the past perfect: "had accepted". "Accepted" would express a wish about the present, which does not fit "earlier".
Source: NCERT Class 10 English Grammar

116. Not only the students but also the teacher ______ surprised by the result.
(a) was (b) were (c) have been (d) are
Answer: (a)
Solution: With "not only...but also", the verb agrees with the nearer subject, "the teacher" (singular), so "was" is correct.
Source: PYQ-style

117. He is one of those players who ______ hard for every match.
(a) practises (b) has practised (c) is practising (d) practise
Answer: (d)
Solution: In "one of those who...", the relative pronoun "who" refers to the plural "players", so the plural verb "practise" is correct. "Practises" wrongly agrees with "one".
Source: PYQ-style

118. Spot the error: Part P: Scarcely he had / Part Q: entered the room / Part R: when the phone rang.
(a) Part Q (b) Part P (c) Part R (d) No error
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Scarcely" at the beginning requires inversion: "Scarcely had he entered...". Part P has the wrong word order.
Source: PYQ-style

119. The number of road accidents ______ increasing every year.
(a) is (b) are (c) have been (d) were
Answer: (a)
Solution: "The number of" takes a singular verb, so "is" is correct. Contrast: "a number of" takes a plural verb — examiners love this pair.
Source: PYQ-style

120. You had better ______ your work before the boss arrives.
(a) to finish (b) finish (c) finishing (d) finished
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Had better" is followed by the bare infinitive (base verb): "finish". "To finish" is a frequent error because most modals-like phrases seem to need "to".
Source: NCERT Class 10 English Grammar

121. Spot the error: Part P: Many a soldier / Part Q: have laid down / Part R: his life for the country.
(a) Part P (b) Part R (c) Part Q (d) No error
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Many a" is followed by a singular noun and a singular verb, so Part Q should be "has laid down". The plural sense of the phrase tricks candidates into a plural verb.
Source: PYQ-style

122. Unless you ______ hard, you will not pass the examination.
(a) had worked (b) will work (c) worked (d) work
Answer: (d)
Solution: In conditional clauses with "unless", the present simple expresses the future; "will" is not used in the if/unless clause. So "work" is correct.
Source: NCERT Class 10 English Grammar

123. The Ganges ______ one of the longest rivers in India.
(a) are (b) have been (c) were (d) is
Answer: (d)
Solution: Names of rivers, though plural-looking, are singular and take a singular verb: "is". The same rule applies to book titles and country names.
Source: NCERT Class 9 English Grammar

124. Spot the error: Part P: He as well as his brothers / Part Q: were present / Part R: at the meeting.
(a) Part P (b) Part R (c) Part Q (d) No error
Answer: (c)
Solution: With "as well as", the verb agrees with the first subject, "He" (singular), so Part Q should be "was present". The plural noun in the middle phrase is the trap.
Source: PYQ-style

125. It is high time we ______ serious about our careers.
(a) get (b) will get (c) got (d) have got
Answer: (c)
Solution: "It is high time" is followed by the simple past to express a present necessity: "got". The present tense "get" is grammatically wrong after this phrase.
Source: PYQ-style
