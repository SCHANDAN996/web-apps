# 📊 PYQ Analysis — Verb

## How Exams Test "Verb" — Pattern Analysis

Verbs appear throughout the English section of SSC (CGL, CHSL, MTS, GD), Banking (IBPS, SBI), and Railway (NTPC, Group D) exams. Across these exams, verb-based questions appear in **four recurring formats**:

1. **Spot the Error** — a sentence split into parts (a)/(b)/(c)/(d); the verb error hides in one part.
2. **Fill in the Blank** — choose the correct verb form (tense, agreement, voice).
3. **Sentence Improvement** — replace the underlined verb phrase with the correct option.
4. **Cloze Test** — 5–10 blanks in a passage, several of them verb-based.

## Sub-topics That Repeat Often

- **Subject–Verb Agreement** — examiners love inserting a long phrase between subject and verb ("The quality of the mangoes *was* good").
- **Tense consistency** — mixing past and present in one sentence; sequence of tenses after "said that…".
- **Conditional sentences** — "If I were…", "If he had come…, he would have…".
- **Non-finite verbs** — gerund vs infinitive ("enjoy *going*", "want *to go*").
- **Active/Passive voice** — identifying the correct passive form.
- **Modal verbs** — should/would/must + base form; "needn't have" vs "didn't need to".
- **Phrasal verbs** — meaning-based fill-ups (put off, give up, look into).

## The Examiner's Four Favourite Traps

| Trap Type | How It Works | Example Pattern |
|---|---|---|
| **Intervening Phrase** | A long phrase between subject and verb makes you match the verb to the wrong noun | "The box of chocolates *is/are* on the table" → *is* (subject = box) |
| **Collective Noun Confusion** | Team, jury, committee, audience — singular or plural depending on unity vs division | "The jury *has* given its verdict" (united) vs "The jury *are* divided" |
| **Sound-Right Error** | The wrong form "sounds" natural in speech, so candidates skip it | "Each of the boys *have* a pen" → *has* |
| **Over-thinking** | The sentence is actually correct; candidates invent an error | Option (d) "No error" is sometimes the right answer — check it seriously |

## Top 10 High-Yield PYQ-Style Questions with Solutions

**Q1.** The quality of the apples (a)/ were not good (b)/ so we did not buy them. (c)/ No error (d)
**Answer:** (b)
**Solution:** The subject is "quality" (singular), not "apples". The phrase "of the apples" only describes the quality. Correct: *was not good*.
**Trap type:** Intervening Phrase
**Source:** PYQ-style (SSC pattern)

**Q2.** Each of the students in the class (a)/ have submitted (b)/ the assignment on time. (c)/ No error (d)
**Answer:** (b)
**Solution:** "Each of + plural noun" always takes a singular verb. Correct: *has submitted*.
**Trap type:** Sound-Right Error
**Source:** PYQ-style (Banking pattern)

**Q3.** If I ______ a bird, I would fly to you.
(a) am (b) was (c) were (d) had been
**Answer:** (c)
**Solution:** In imaginary (unreal) conditions, use "were" for all persons: "If I were…". This is the subjunctive mood.
**Trap type:** Sound-Right Error ("was" sounds acceptable in speech)
**Source:** PYQ-style (SSC pattern)

**Q4.** He said that he (a)/ will come (b)/ to the meeting the next day. (c)/ No error (d)
**Answer:** (b)
**Solution:** After a past-tense reporting verb ("said"), the reported clause shifts back: will → would. Correct: *would come*.
**Trap type:** Over-thinking (candidates often wrongly change "the next day")
**Source:** PYQ-style (Railway pattern)

**Q5.** The committee ______ divided on this issue.
(a) is (b) are (c) was (d) has been
**Answer:** (b)
**Solution:** "Divided" signals disagreement — members acting separately — so the collective noun takes a plural verb here. (If the committee acted as one body, "is" would be correct.)
**Trap type:** Collective Noun Confusion
**Source:** PYQ-style (SSC pattern)

**Q6.** I enjoy ______ in the rain.
(a) walk (b) to walk (c) walking (d) walked
**Answer:** (c)
**Solution:** "Enjoy" is always followed by a gerund (verb+ing), never an infinitive. Learn the gerund-verb list: enjoy, avoid, mind, suggest, finish, keep.
**Trap type:** Sound-Right Error
**Source:** NCERT Class 9–10 Grammar

**Q7.** Neither the teacher nor the students ______ present in the hall.
(a) was (b) were (c) is (d) has been
**Answer:** (b)
**Solution:** With "neither…nor", the verb agrees with the **nearer** subject — "students" (plural) → *were*.
**Trap type:** Intervening Phrase (verb pulled toward "teacher")
**Source:** PYQ-style (Banking pattern)

**Q8.** The letter ______ before I reached the post office.
(a) posted (b) had been posted (c) has been posted (d) was posting
**Answer:** (b)
**Solution:** Two past actions — the earlier one takes past perfect, and the letter receives the action (passive): *had been posted*.
**Trap type:** Over-thinking (candidates pick simple past "was posted" ignoring the earlier/later order)
**Source:** PYQ-style (SSC pattern)

**Q9.** One of my friends (a)/ are going (b)/ to Delhi tomorrow. (c)/ No error (d)
**Answer:** (b)
**Solution:** "One of + plural noun" takes a singular verb — the real subject is "one". Correct: *is going*.
**Trap type:** Intervening Phrase
**Source:** PYQ-style (Railway pattern)

**Q10.** You needn't ______ water; the plants have already been watered.
(a) bring (b) to bring (c) brought (d) bringing
**Answer:** (a)
**Solution:** "Needn't" is a modal here, and modals take the base form of the verb: *needn't bring*. (Compare: "You don't need **to bring** water" — with "don't need to", the infinitive is required.)
**Trap type:** Sound-Right Error
**Source:** PYQ-style (Banking pattern)

## Applying the 15/45-Second Rule

**15-second question (Fact-based):** Q6 above. If you have memorised that "enjoy + gerund", you read the sentence, spot "enjoy", and mark "walking" in under 15 seconds. No analysis needed — pure recall of the rule list.

**45-second question (Inference-based):** Q5 above. You must first decide whether the committee acts as one unit or as divided members. The word "divided" is the clue, but you must reason it out — this legitimately takes 40–45 seconds. Do not rush collective-noun questions; the trap is built for hasty readers.

## Key Takeaways from the Pattern

- Build a strong grip on **subject–verb agreement** — it underlies a large share of verb questions in these formats.
- Always **strip the sentence to subject + verb** before answering; ignore phrases in between.
- In error-spotting, check option (d) "No error" seriously — over-thinking costs easy marks.
- Memorise the short gerund/infinitive verb lists; they convert 45-second questions into 15-second ones.
