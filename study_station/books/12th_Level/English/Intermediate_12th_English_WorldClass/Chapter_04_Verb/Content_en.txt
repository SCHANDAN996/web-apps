# VERB — The Engine of Every Sentence

## 🚀 The Hook

Remove the verb from any sentence and watch it die. "Ravi ___ the ball." Nothing happens. No action, no state, no meaning. The verb is the only part of speech that can stand alone as a complete sentence — "Go!" "Stop!" "Run!" — and verbs are a heavily tested grammar topic in SSC, Banking and Railway exams. Master the verb, and you have mastered the skeleton of English.

## 🎯 Learning Objectives

By the end of this chapter you will be able to:

1. Identify main verbs, auxiliary verbs and their forms (V1–V5) instantly.
2. Apply Subject–Verb Agreement rules even when long phrases separate subject and verb.
3. Choose the correct tense using time markers and context.
4. Handle tricky cases: collective nouns, indefinite pronouns, "each/every", "neither…nor".
5. Spot the examiner's favourite traps in under 15 seconds.

---

## 1. What Exactly Is a Verb?

A **verb** is a word that expresses an **action** (run, write, think), an **occurrence** (happen, become) or a **state of being** (is, seem, remain).

Every grammatically complete sentence must contain a verb. Even "She is happy" has one — *is* — because verbs don't only "do"; they also "are".

**The Five Forms of a Verb (V1–V5):**

| Form | Name | Example (GO) | Example (WRITE) |
|---|---|---|---|
| V1 | Base / Present | go | write |
| V2 | Past | went | wrote |
| V3 | Past Participle | gone | written |
| V4 | Present Participle (-ing) | going | writing |
| V5 | Third Person Singular (-s/es) | goes | writes |

🧠 **Active Recall:** Without looking up, write V1–V5 of *eat, take, begin, teach*. Which form is used after "has/have/had"? (Answer: V3.)

## 2. Main Verbs vs Auxiliary Verbs

- **Main verb** — carries the real meaning: *She **sings** beautifully.*
- **Auxiliary (helping) verb** — supports the main verb to show tense, voice or mood.

**Three families of auxiliaries:**

1. **Be** — am, is, are, was, were, been, being → continuous tenses & passive voice.
2. **Have** — have, has, had → perfect tenses.
3. **Do** — do, does, did → questions, negatives, emphasis.

**Modal auxiliaries** — can, could, may, might, shall, should, will, would, must, ought to, need, dare, used to. Most modals are followed by **V1** (bare infinitive): *He must **go*** (never "must goes" / "must to go"). But **ought to** and **used to** are followed by the *to*-infinitive: *He ought to **go*** / *He used to **go** there.*

🧠 **Active Recall:** In "The letter has been written by her", identify all auxiliaries and the main verb. (Auxiliaries: has, been; main verb: written.)

---

## 3. Transitive and Intransitive Verbs

- **Transitive verb** — needs an object to complete its meaning: *She bought **a book**.* (Bought *what*? — a book.)
- **Intransitive verb** — complete without an object: *The baby **sleeps**.* (Sleeps *what*? — nothing; the question makes no sense.)

⚠️ Only **transitive** verbs can be changed into passive voice. "The baby was slept" is impossible.

Some verbs work both ways: *He **runs** a shop* (transitive) / *He **runs** fast* (intransitive).

---

## 4. Subject–Verb Agreement — The Heart of This Chapter

The golden principle: **the verb agrees with its subject in number and person.**

- Singular subject → singular verb (V5 in present): *Ravi **plays**.*
- Plural subject → plural verb (V1): *Ravi and Mohan **play**.*

The examiner's whole game is to **hide the real subject** from you. Train your eye to find the subject first, ignore everything in between, then choose the verb.

*The quality **of these mangoes** is excellent.* — Subject is "quality" (singular), not "mangoes". The phrase "of these mangoes" is a decoy.

🧠 **Active Recall:** Choose: "The leader, along with his followers, (was/were) arrested." (Answer: *was* — "along with" phrases don't change the subject.)

## 5. Tense — Time + Form

Tense = **when** the action happens + **how** it is viewed (simple, continuous, perfect, perfect continuous). That gives 12 tense forms, built from just three time zones:

| | Past | Present | Future |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Simple** | wrote (V2) | writes (V5) | will write |
| **Continuous** | was writing | is writing | will be writing |
| **Perfect** | had written | has written | will have written |
| **Perfect Continuous** | had been writing | has been writing | will have been writing |

**Time markers are your clues:**
- *since, for, already, yet, just, never* → Perfect tense
- *yesterday, ago, last year, in 2010* → Simple Past (never Present Perfect!)
- *now, at present, look!, listen!* → Present Continuous
- *tomorrow, next week, soon* → Future

⚠️ Classic error: *"I have seen him yesterday."* ❌ → *"I saw him yesterday."* ✔ (A finished past time marker kills the Present Perfect.)

🧠 **Active Recall:** Why is "I am knowing the answer" wrong? (Answer: *know* is a stative verb — see below.)

---

## 6. Stative Verbs — Verbs That Refuse "-ing"

Some verbs describe **states, not actions**, and normally do not take the continuous form:

- **Mind:** know, believe, understand, remember, forget, mean
- **Emotion:** love, hate, like, prefer, want, wish
- **Senses:** see, hear, smell, taste (when involuntary)
- **Possession:** have (own), own, belong, consist

*"I **know** the answer"* ✔ — *"I am knowing"* ❌

But note: *"I **am thinking** about the plan"* ✔ (thinking = mental activity in progress), while *"I **think** it is wrong"* ✔ (think = opinion).

---

## 🧠 Mnemonic — "SHAPE" for Stative Verbs

**S**enses – **H**aving (possession) – **A**ffection (love/hate) – **P**erceiving mind (know, believe) – **E**motions (want, wish)
👉 *"Stative verbs have SHAPE — they never run (no -ing)!"*

## ⚠️ EXAMINER'S TRAP — Read Before Any Exam

**Trap 1 — The Intervening Phrase:** *"One of my friends **have** gone to Delhi."* ❌ The subject is "One", not "friends". Correct: **has**. After *one of / each of / either of / neither of*, the noun is plural but the verb is **singular**.

**Trap 2 — The Sound-Right Trap:** *"The news **are** shocking."* ❌ *News* sounds plural but is singular: **is**. Same family: physics, mathematics, economics, innings, gallows.

**Trap 3 — The Proximity Confusion:** With *neither…nor / either…or / not only…but also*, the verb agrees with the **nearer** subject: *"Neither the teacher nor the students **were** present."* Don't let the first subject fool you.

## 🔁 LINGUISTIC BRIDGE — Hindi vs English

| We think in Hindi | Wrong English | Correct English |
|---|---|---|
| "मैं कल से बीमार हूँ" | I am ill since yesterday. | I **have been** ill since yesterday. |
| "वह क्या कर रहा है?" | What he is doing? / What you are doing? | What **is he doing**? / What **are you doing**? (question = auxiliary before subject) |
| "मुझे यह पता है" | I am knowing this. | I **know** this. |
| "उसने मुझसे कहा कि…" | He told that… | He **told me that**… (tell needs an object; *say* does not) |
| "मैंने उसे कल देखा था" | I had seen him yesterday. | I **saw** him yesterday. (Past Perfect needs two past actions) |

**Rule of thumb:** Hindi uses one verb form where English demands tense precision. Always ask: *Is the action finished? Is the time finished?*

## 📋 Summary Table — Verb at a Glance

| Concept | Core Rule | Quick Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb forms | V1–V5; V3 after have/has/had | has **gone** |
| Auxiliaries | Be / Have / Do / Modals + V1 (ought to / used to + to-infinitive) | must **go** |
| Transitive | Takes object; can be passive | She wrote **a letter** |
| Agreement | Verb agrees with subject, not decoy phrases | One of the boys **is**… |
| Tense | Match time markers to tense | yesterday → Simple Past |
| Stative verbs | No continuous form | I **know** (not am knowing) |
| Neither…nor | Verb agrees with nearer subject | …nor the students **were** |
