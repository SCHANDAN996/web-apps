Card 1
Front: Which Delhi Sultanate ruler was called a "mixture of opposites" by contemporary historians, and why?
Back: Muhammad bin Tughlaq. He was highly learned yet made disastrous decisions — shifting the capital to Daulatabad and introducing token currency — earning him the paradoxical reputation of a "wise fool."

Card 2
Front: Who was the only woman to sit on the throne of Delhi, and what ended her reign?
Back: Razia Sultan (1236–1240), daughter of Iltutmish. The Turkish nobles (Chahalgani) resented a woman ruler and her favouritism toward the Ethiopian officer Jamal-ud-din Yaqut; she was deposed and killed.

Card 3
Front: Which Sultan introduced the market control policy, and what everyday items did it regulate?
Back: Alauddin Khilji. His price-control system fixed rates for grains, cloth, horses, cattle and even slaves, enforced through separate markets (mandis) and harsh punishments for cheating.

Card 4
Front: Which traveller came to India during Muhammad bin Tughlaq's reign, and what was his book called?
Back: Ibn Battuta of Morocco. His account is the Rihla (Rehla). He served as a Qazi in Delhi. (Trap: Marco Polo came earlier, to South India in the Pandyan kingdom.)

Card 5
Front: What was the Iqta system, and who organised it systematically?
Back: Land revenue assignments given to officers (iqtadars) in lieu of salary. Iltutmish organised it systematically under the Delhi Sultanate.

Card 6
Front: Which Mughal emperor lost his empire and then regained the throne, ruling twice?
Back: Humayun. He was defeated by Sher Shah Suri and lost his empire in 1540, spent years in exile, and regained Delhi in 1555. (Trap: don't confuse with Shah Jahan, who was imprisoned by Aurangzeb but never re-enthroned.)

Card 7
Front: Which battle in 1526 is called the "First Battle of Panipat," and what new technology decided it?
Back: Babur defeated Ibrahim Lodi. Gunpowder artillery and the Tulghuma (flanking) tactic, combined with the Araba (cart) formation, decided the battle.

Card 8
Front: Which Mughal emperor abolished the Jizya tax, and who reimposed it?
Back: Akbar abolished Jizya in 1564; Aurangzeb reimposed it in 1679.

Card 9
Front: What was Akbar's Din-i-Ilahi, and how many followers did it actually attract?
Back: A syncretic ethical order started by Akbar in 1582, drawn from many religions. It had very few followers and died with him — it was never a mass religion.

Card 10
Front: Which Mughal emperor is called "Zinda Pir" (living saint), and why is the title ironic?
Back: Aurangzeb, for his austere personal life and sewing caps/copying the Quran. Ironic because his reign saw the harshest religious policies — temple destructions and Jizya reimposition.

Card 11
Front: Who built the Taj Mahal, and who was its chief architect traditionally credited?
Back: Shah Jahan, in memory of Mumtaz Mahal (completed c. 1653). Ustad Ahmad Lahauri is traditionally credited as chief architect.

Card 12
Front: Which Sikh Guru was executed by Jahangir, and which by Aurangzeb?
Back: Guru Arjan Dev was executed by Jahangir (1606); Guru Tegh Bahadur was executed by Aurangzeb (1675). (Trap: examiners swap the two Gurus and emperors.)

Card 13
Front: What mnemonic helps you remember the order of the first six Mughal emperors?
Back: "BHAJSA" — Babur, Humayun, Akbar, Jahangir, Shah Jahan, Aurangzeb. Remember: "BHAJSA rules the Mughal throne."

Card 14
Front: What quick trick recalls the three Battles of Panipat in order?
Back: "1526 Babur, 1556 Akbar (via Bairam), 1761 Abdali" — remember "26-56-61: Mughals win twice, Marathas lose once." Panipat I: Babur vs Lodi; II: Akbar vs Hemu; III: Ahmad Shah Abdali vs Marathas.

Card 15
Front: Examiner's trap: Babur wrote his memoirs Tuzuk-i-Baburi in which language — Persian or Turkish?
Back: Turkish (Chagatai Turkic), not Persian. It was later translated into Persian as Baburnama by Abdur Rahim Khan-i-Khanan. Examiners offer "Persian" as the tempting wrong option.

Card 16
Front: Examiner's trap: Who introduced the Dagh (horse branding) and Chehra (soldier description) system — Balban or Alauddin Khilji?
Back: Alauddin Khilji. Balban is associated with Sijda and Paibos (prostration) and the theory of kingship; examiners swap these two rulers' reforms.

Card 17
Front: Which Bhakti saints' verses are included in the Guru Granth Sahib? Name any two.
Back: Verses of Kabir, Guru Nanak, Ravidas, Namdev and others are included. Kabir and Ravidas are the most frequently asked.

Card 18
Front: Who founded the Vijayanagara Empire, and which river was its capital on?
Back: Harihara and Bukka (1336), guided by sage Vidyaranya. Capital Hampi lay on the banks of the Tungabhadra river.

Card 19
Front: Which foreign travellers visited Vijayanagara during Krishnadevaraya's era, and what did they praise?
Back: Domingo Paes and Fernao Nuniz (Portuguese). They praised the empire's wealth, bustling bazaars and the efficient administration of Krishnadevaraya (author of Amuktamalyada).

Card 20
Front: What was the Mansabdari system, and what did "zat" and "sawar" mean?
Back: Akbar's civil-military ranking system. Zat fixed the officer's personal rank and salary; Sawar fixed the number of cavalrymen he had to maintain.
