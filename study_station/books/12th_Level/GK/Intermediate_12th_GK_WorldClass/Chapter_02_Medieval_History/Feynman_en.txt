# 🧒 Feynman Technique — Medieval Indian History

## The Most Confusing Concept: Why Did the Delhi Sultanate Keep Changing Dynasties, but the Mughals Lasted So Long?

Students mix up the five dynasties of the Delhi Sultanate (Slave, Khilji, Tughlaq, Sayyid, Lodi) and then wonder how the Mughals — also foreign invaders — ruled for over 300 years. Let's make this crystal clear.

### Explain It Like I'm 12

Imagine two cricket teams renting the same stadium.

**Team 1 (Delhi Sultanate):** Five different captains in about 320 years (1206–1526). Each captain grabbed the captaincy by force, made his own rules, and the moment he died or weakened, a new captain fought his way in. There was no fixed "rule book" for who becomes captain next. Result? Constant fighting, short reigns, instability.

**Team 2 (Mughals):** One family, but with a smarter system. Babur's descendants didn't just conquer — they **made friends with the locals**. Akbar married into Rajput families, gave Hindus top jobs (like Todar Mal and Birbal), and removed the hated jizya tax. People felt the Mughals were *their* rulers, not outsiders.

**The simple truth:** The Sultanate ruled *over* India; the Mughals (at least till Aurangzeb) ruled *with* India.

### Daily-Life Example

Think of a new student who joins your school:
- If he bullies everyone and sits alone, he'll be kicked out of every group quickly (like the Sultanate dynasties replacing each other).
- If he makes friends, shares lunch, and joins the team, he stays for years (like Akbar's Mughals).

### Unforgettable Trick

**"MUGHAL = Mingle, U Grow, Hate And Leave"**
- **M**ingle with locals (Akbar's Rajput alliances) → empire **grows**
- **H**ate the locals (Aurangzeb's jizya, temple destruction) → empire **leaves** (collapses within 50 years of his death)

**Dynasty order trick for the Sultanate:** **"S-K-T-S-L"** → *"Some Kings Took Sultanate Lightly"*
**S**lave → **K**hilji → **T**ughlaq → **S**ayyid → **L**odi

(Examiner's trap: they swap Sayyid and Lodi, or place Tughlaq before Khilji. Remember: Khilji came with Alauddin's market reforms; Tughlaq came with Muhammad bin Tughlaq's "failed experiments" — token currency, capital shift to Daulatabad. The Sayyids came after Timur's invasion of 1398, and the Lodis were the last, defeated by Babur at Panipat in 1526.)
