126. Consider the following statements about the Delhi Sultanate:
1. The Iqta system was introduced in India by Iltutmish.
2. Balban transferred iqtas freely among nobles to reward loyalty.
3. Alauddin Khalji brought the iqtas of the Doab under direct crown control (khalisa).
Which of the statements given above is/are correct?
(a) 1 and 2 only (b) 1 and 3 only (c) 2 and 3 only (d) 1, 2 and 3
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is statement 2: Balban did the opposite — he strictly curtailed the power of iqtadars, refused to transfer iqtas as rewards, and even resumed many grants, because he distrusted the nobility. Statement 1 is correct (Iltutmish organised the iqta system), and statement 3 is correct (Alauddin converted much land, especially in the Doab, into khalisa or crown land), so the answer is 1 and 3 only.
Source: PYQ-style

127. Who among the following travellers visited India during the reign of Muhammad bin Tughlaq and wrote the account 'Rihla'?
(a) Marco Polo (b) Nicolo Conti (c) Al-Biruni (d) Ibn Battuta
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is Al-Biruni, who also wrote a famous account (Kitab-ul-Hind) but came with Mahmud of Ghazni in the 11th century, centuries earlier. Marco Polo visited South India in the 13th century and Conti in the 15th. Ibn Battuta, the Moroccan traveller, came in 1333 during Muhammad bin Tughlaq's reign and wrote the Rihla, so the answer is Ibn Battuta.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Themes in Indian History Part II

128. The 'Dagh' (branding of horses) and 'Chehra' (descriptive roll of soldiers) systems were introduced by:
(a) Muhammad bin Tughlaq (b) Balban (c) Alauddin Khalji (d) Firoz Shah Tughlaq
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is Balban, famous for military reorganisation, but his focus was on the army's discipline and the iqta-holding nobles, not branding. Alauddin Khalji introduced dagh and chehra to eliminate fake cavalrymen and support his standing army paid in cash, so the answer is Alauddin Khalji.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Themes in Indian History Part II

129. Arrange the following events of Muhammad bin Tughlaq's reign in correct chronological order:
1. Transfer of capital from Delhi to Daulatabad
2. Introduction of token currency
3. Qarachil expedition in the Kumaon hills
4. Increase of taxation in the Doab
(a) 4, 2, 1, 3 (b) 1, 2, 3, 4 (c) 2, 4, 1, 3 (d) 4, 1, 2, 3
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is the sequence 1-2-3-4, which sounds like a neat order but is wrong. The Doab taxation increase came earliest (c. 1326–27), followed by the capital transfer to Daulatabad (c. 1327), then token currency (c. 1329–30), and the Qarachil expedition (c. 1333), so the answer is 4, 1, 2, 3.
Source: PYQ-style

130. With reference to the Vijayanagara Empire, consider the following statements:
1. It was founded in 1336 by Harihara and Bukka of the Sangama dynasty.
2. The 'Amara-nayaka' system was a military-administrative arrangement derived from the iqta system.
3. Abdur Razzaq visited Vijayanagara during the reign of Krishnadeva Raya.
Which of the statements given above is/are correct?
(a) 1 and 3 only (b) 2 and 3 only (c) 1, 2 and 3 (d) 1 and 2 only
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is statement 3: Abdur Razzaq, the Persian ambassador, visited during the reign of Deva Raya II, not Krishnadeva Raya; the travellers of Krishnadeva Raya's time were the Portuguese Domingo Paes and Fernao Nuniz. Statements 1 and 2 are correct, so the answer is 1 and 2 only.

131. The Battle of Talikota (1565) was fought between the Vijayanagara Empire and:
(a) The Mughal forces of Akbar (b) The Bahmani Sultanate alone (c) A confederacy of the Deccan Sultanates (d) The Portuguese
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is the Bahmani Sultanate alone — by 1565 the Bahmani kingdom had already fragmented into five successor sultanates. Four of them (Bijapur, Ahmadnagar, Golconda and Bidar) formed a confederacy that defeated Rama Raya at Talikota, so the answer is a confederacy of the Deccan Sultanates.

132. Who among the following wrote 'Ain-i-Akbari'?
(a) Nizamuddin Ahmad (b) Abul Fazl (c) Faizi (d) Badauni
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is Badauni, another contemporary historian of Akbar's court, but he wrote Muntakhab-ut-Tawarikh, a critical account, not the Ain. Abul Fazl wrote the Akbarnama, of which the Ain-i-Akbari is the third volume, so the answer is Abul Fazl.

133. Consider the following statements about Akbar's religious policy:
1. He abolished the jizya tax in 1564.
2. The Ibadat Khana at Fatehpur Sikri was built in 1575.
3. The Mahzar (1579) declared Akbar the supreme interpreter of Islamic law.
Which of the statements given above is/are correct?
(a) 1 and 2 only (b) 1 and 3 only (c) 1, 2 and 3 (d) 2 and 3 only
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap lies in the dates, which examiners often swap: jizya was abolished in 1564 (the pilgrimage tax was abolished a year earlier, in 1563), the Ibadat Khana was built in 1575, and the Mahzar was issued in 1579. All three statements are correct, so the answer is 1, 2 and 3.
Source: PYQ-style

134. The Mansabdari system under Akbar had two ranks — 'zat' and 'sawar'. What did 'zat' indicate?
(a) The personal status and salary of the mansabdar (b) The number of horses a mansabdar maintained (c) The province assigned to the mansabdar (d) The number of infantry under the mansabdar
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is the first option, which describes the sawar rank — the number of cavalrymen (with horses) the mansabdar had to maintain. The zat rank fixed the officer's personal status, position in the hierarchy and pay, so the answer is the personal status and salary of the mansabdar.

135. Which Mughal emperor granted the English East India Company the 'Farman' of 1717, often called the 'Magna Carta' of the Company?
(a) Jahangir (b) Shah Jahan (c) Aurangzeb (d) Farrukhsiyar
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is Jahangir, under whom the English first got permission to trade (via Sir Thomas Roe in 1615), but the famous duty-free farman came much later. It was Farrukhsiyar who in 1717 granted the Company exemption from customs duties in Bengal in return for an annual payment of Rs 3,000, so the answer is Farrukhsiyar.
Source: PYQ-style

136. Consider the following pairs of Mughal emperors and their tombs' locations:
1. Humayun — Delhi
2. Akbar — Sikandra (Agra)
3. Aurangzeb — Aurangabad (Khuldabad)
Which of the pairs given above is/are correctly matched?
(a) 1 and 2 only (b) 2 and 3 only (c) 1, 2 and 3 (d) 1 and 3 only
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is Aurangzeb's tomb: unlike the grand Mughal mausoleums, he was buried in a simple open grave at Khuldabad near Aurangabad, which is still correctly matched here. Humayun's tomb is in Delhi and Akbar's at Sikandra, so the answer is 1, 2 and 3.

137. The 'Din-i-Ilahi' of Akbar:
(a) Was a syncretic ethical order with very few members, including Birbal (b) Was a new religion with a large body of followers and scriptures (c) Was imposed forcibly on all Mughal nobles (d) Survived and flourished after Akbar's death
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is the second option: Din-i-Ilahi had no scripture, no priesthood and no mass following — it never became a religion in the conventional sense. It was a small ethical-spiritual circle (some sources say only about 18–19 members, including the Hindu courtier Birbal), and it died with Akbar, so the answer is that it was a syncretic ethical order with very few members, including Birbal.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Themes in Indian History Part II

138. Arrange the following battles in correct chronological order:
1. First Battle of Panipat
2. Battle of Khanwa
3. Battle of Chausa
4. Battle of Ghaghra
(a) 2, 1, 4, 3 (b) 1, 2, 3, 4 (c) 1, 2, 4, 3 (d) 1, 3, 2, 4

Solution: The trap is placing Chausa before Ghaghra: Chausa (1539), where Sher Shah defeated Humayun, came a decade after Babur's death. Babur's sequence was Panipat (1526), Khanwa (1527) and Ghaghra (1529), so the answer is 1, 2, 4, 3.

139. With reference to Sher Shah Suri's administration, consider the following statements:
1. He introduced the silver 'Rupiya' and copper 'Dam'.
2. He built the Grand Trunk Road from Sonargaon to Peshawar.
3. He introduced the Dagh and Chehra systems for the first time in India.
Which of the statements given above is/are correct?
(a) 1, 2 and 3 (b) 1 and 2 only (c) 1 and 3 only (d) 2 and 3 only
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is statement 3: Sher Shah did use branding of horses, but dagh and chehra were first introduced by Alauddin Khalji, not Sher Shah — a classic similar-measure confusion. His currency reforms and the sarai-lined road from Sonargaon (Bengal) to the Indus region are well attested, so the answer is 1 and 2 only.

140. The 'Mahanubhava' sect of Maharashtra, whose followers rejected caste distinctions, was founded in the 13th century by:
(a) Namdev (b) Chakradhara (c) Tukaram (d) Eknath
Answer: (b)

144. The 'Zabt' or 'Dahsala' system of land revenue, associated with Raja Todar Mal, was introduced during the reign of:
(a) Akbar (b) Sher Shah Suri (c) Babur (d) Jahangir
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is Sher Shah Suri, whose land measurement and crop-rate (ray) system strongly influenced later Mughal revenue practice, but the formal Dahsala system — fixing revenue on ten-year average yields and prices — was finalised under Akbar in 1580–82 by Todar Mal, so the answer is Akbar.

146. Who was the first and only woman to sit on the throne of Delhi as Sultan?
(a) Razia Sultan (b) Nur Jahan (c) Chand Bibi (d) Jahanara Begum
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is Nur Jahan, who wielded real power as Jahangir's wife but was never a crowned ruler, and Chand Bibi, who was a regent of Ahmadnagar, not Delhi. Razia Sultan (reigned 1236–1240), daughter of Iltutmish, was the first and only woman Sultan of Delhi, so the answer is Razia Sultan.

147. Consider the following statements about Mughal painting:

3. Painting reached its zenith of refinement under Aurangzeb.

148. The 'Ashtapradhan' council of ministers was a feature of the administration of:
(a) The Vijayanagara Empire (b) The Bahmani kingdom (c) The Mughal Empire (d) Shivaji's Maratha state
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is the Mughal Empire, which had ministers like the Diwan and Mir Bakshi but no eight-member council called Ashtapradhan. Shivaji's council of eight ministers, headed by the Peshwa (Mukhya Pradhan), was the Ashtapradhan, so the answer is Shivaji's Maratha state.

149. Consider the following statements about the Mughal succession:
1. The Mughals followed the law of primogeniture strictly.
2. Aurangzeb imprisoned Shah Jahan at Agra Fort.
3. Dara Shikoh was defeated at the Battle of Samugarh (1658).
Which of the statements given above is/are correct?
(a) 1 and 2 only (b) 1, 2 and 3 (c) 1 and 3 only (d) 2 and 3 only
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is statement 1: the Mughals followed no fixed primogeniture; succession was decided by fratricidal war among the princes, as in 1657–58. Aurangzeb did confine Shah Jahan in Agra Fort until his death in 1666, and Dara Shikoh lost the decisive Battle of Samugarh in May 1658, so the answer is 2 and 3 only.

150. Which of the following correctly describes the 'Chauth' and 'Sardeshmukhi' levied by the Marathas?
(a) Chauth was 10% and Sardeshmukhi was 25% of the revenue (b) Chauth was 25% and Sardeshmukhi was an additional 10% of the revenue (c) Both were land taxes on Maratha home territory only (d) Both were customs duties on trade routes
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is the second option, which simply swaps the two figures — the most common examiner's trick on this topic. Chauth was one-fourth (25%) of the revenue of a territory claimed in return for protection, and Sardeshmukhi was an additional 10% claimed by virtue of the Maratha claim to be hereditary deshmukhs, so the answer is Chauth 25% and Sardeshmukhi an additional 10%.
Source: PYQ-style
