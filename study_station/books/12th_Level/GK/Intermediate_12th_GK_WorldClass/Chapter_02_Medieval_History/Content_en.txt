# Medieval Indian History

## 🌟 The Hook: A Kingdom Won in a Day, Lost in a Moment

In 1192, on the same battlefield of Tarain where he had been defeated and spared just one year earlier, Muhammad Ghori returned — and this time he did not forgive. Prithviraj Chauhan's defeat did not just end a king's reign; it opened the gates of North India to nearly 600 years of new dynasties, new languages, new architecture, and new ideas. One afternoon changed the course of Indian history. That is the power — and the drama — of the medieval period you are about to master.

## 🎯 Learning Objectives

By the end of this chapter, you will be able to:

1. Trace the major dynasties of the Delhi Sultanate (1206–1526) in correct order.
2. Explain the rise, expansion, and decline of the Mughal Empire (1526–1857).
3. Identify key administrative, architectural, and cultural contributions of medieval rulers.
4. Recognise the Bhakti and Sufi movements and their saints.
5. Spot the examiner's favourite traps — similar names, reversed chronology, and "almost correct" statements.

## Part 1: The Delhi Sultanate (1206–1526)

### 1.1 How It All Began

After Muhammad Ghori's death in 1206, his slave-general **Qutbuddin Aibak** declared himself Sultan and founded the **Mamluk (Slave) Dynasty**. This is why the early Sultanate is often called the "Slave Dynasty" — though Aibak himself died just four years later, in 1210, after falling from a horse while playing polo (chaugan).

The real architect of the Sultanate was **Iltutmish** (1211–1236), who:
- Shifted the capital from Lahore to **Delhi**.
- Introduced the silver **tanka** and copper **jital** coins.
- Completed the **Qutub Minar** (started by Aibak in honour of Sufi saint Qutbuddin Bakhtiyar Kaki).
- Organised the **Chalisa** — a corps of forty Turkish nobles.

His daughter **Razia Sultan** (1236–1240) became the **first and only woman ruler of the Delhi Sultanate**. Nobles resented a woman on the throne, and she was killed within four years.

🧠 **Active Recall:** Who completed the Qutub Minar? Which dynasty did Aibak found, and in which year?

### 1.2 The Khilji Dynasty (1290–1320)

**Alauddin Khilji** (1296–1316) was the most powerful Khilji ruler:
- First Sultan to attack **South India** (his general **Malik Kafur** led the southern campaigns).
- Introduced **market control** — fixed prices of goods in Delhi.
- Started the system of **branding horses (dagh)** and maintaining a descriptive roll of soldiers (chehra).
- Built the **Alai Darwaza** and the Siri fort in Delhi.

🧠 **Active Recall:** Which Sultan introduced price control? What was the dagh system?

### 1.3 The Tughlaq Dynasty (1320–1414)

- **Ghiyasuddin Tughlaq** founded the dynasty and built **Tughlaqabad**.
- **Muhammad bin Tughlaq** (1325–1351) is called a "mixture of opposites." His famous failures:
  - Shifting the capital from **Delhi to Daulatabad** (and back).
  - Introducing **token currency** of bronze/copper, which collapsed because people forged coins at home.
  - The traveller **Ibn Battuta** (from Morocco) visited India during his reign and wrote the *Rihla*.
- **Firoz Shah Tughlaq** (1351–1388) built canals, new towns (Firozabad, Hisar, Jaunpur), and brought two **Ashokan pillars** to Delhi.
- In **1398, Timur** invaded Delhi and devastated it — the Sultanate never fully recovered.

### 1.4 Sayyids (1414–1451) and Lodis (1451–1526)

- The **Lodis** were the first **Afghan** dynasty of Delhi.
- **Sikandar Lodi** founded **Agra** in 1504.
- **Ibrahim Lodi** was defeated and killed by **Babur** in the **First Battle of Panipat, 1526** — ending the Sultanate and beginning the Mughal era.

🧠 **Active Recall:** Who wrote the Rihla? Which battle in 1526 ended the Delhi Sultanate?

### 🧩 Mnemonic 1 — The Five Dynasties in Order
**"My King Took Sweet Laddoos"**
- **M**amluk (Slave) → **K**hilji → **T**ughlaq → **S**ayyid → **L**odi

## Part 2: The Mughal Empire (1526–1857)

### 2.1 Babur (1526–1530): The Founder

- A descendant of **Timur** (father's side) and **Genghis Khan** (mother's side).
- Won **Panipat (1526)** against Ibrahim Lodi using artillery and the **Tulughma** tactic.
- Defeated **Rana Sanga** at **Khanwa (1527)**.
- Wrote his memoirs, the **Tuzuk-i-Baburi (Baburnama)**, in **Turkish**.
- Died in 1530; buried in **Kabul**.

### 2.2 Humayun (1530–1540, 1555–1556)

- Defeated by **Sher Shah Suri** at **Chausa (1539)** and **Kannauj (1540)** — fled to Persia.
- Regained Delhi in 1555, but died in 1556 after **falling from the stairs of his library** (Sher Mandal, Purana Qila).
- His sister Gulbadan Begum wrote the **Humayunnama**.

### 2.3 The Sur Interlude: Sher Shah Suri (1540–1545)

Though he ruled only five years, Sher Shah's administration became the model for Akbar:
- Built the **Grand Trunk Road** (Sadak-e-Azam) from Sonargaon to Peshawar.
- Introduced the silver **Rupiya** — the ancestor of the modern rupee.
- Land revenue based on measurement; built sarais (rest houses) along the roads.
- His tomb at **Sasaram (Bihar)** is an architectural marvel.

🧠 **Active Recall:** Who introduced the Rupiya? Where is Sher Shah's tomb?

### 2.4 Akbar (1556–1605): The Great

- Crowned at **13**, under the regent **Bairam Khan**.
- **Second Battle of Panipat (1556)**: Bairam Khan's forces defeated **Hemu**.
- Abolished **jizya** (1564) and the pilgrimage tax.
- Founded the **Ibadat Khana** (Hall of Prayer, 1575) at Fatehpur Sikri for interfaith discussions.
- Proclaimed **Din-i-Ilahi** (1582) — a syncretic faith; it had very few followers and died with him.
- **Mansabdari system**: ranks (mansabs) with **zat** (personal rank) and **sawar** (cavalry rank).
- Revenue system: **Dahsala / Zabti**, designed with **Todar Mal**.
- The **Navaratnas** (nine gems) of his court included Birbal, Tansen, Todar Mal, Abul Fazl (wrote *Akbarnama* and *Ain-i-Akbari*), and Faizi.
- Built **Fatehpur Sikri**, Buland Darwaza (to celebrate the Gujarat victory), and began **Agra Fort**.

### 2.5 Jahangir (1605–1627)

- Famous for his **chain of justice** (zanjir-i-adl).
- Executed **Guru Arjan Dev**, the fifth Sikh Guru.
- His wife **Nur Jahan** wielded enormous influence; coins were issued in her name.
- English visitors: **Captain William Hawkins** and **Sir Thomas Roe** (ambassador of James I) came to his court.
- Wrote his memoirs, **Tuzuk-i-Jahangiri**, in Persian.

### 2.6 Shah Jahan (1628–1658): The Builder

- Built the **Taj Mahal** (Agra, in memory of Mumtaz Mahal), **Red Fort** and **Jama Masjid** (Delhi), and the **Peacock Throne**.
- His reign is called the **Golden Age of Mughal architecture**.
- War of succession among his sons; **Aurangzeb** imprisoned him in Agra Fort, where he died in 1666.

### 2.7 Aurangzeb (1658–1707)

- Took the title **Alamgir**; crowned twice.
- Reimposed **jizya** (1679).
- Executed **Guru Tegh Bahadur**, the ninth Sikh Guru (1675).
- Spent the last 25+ years fighting the **Marathas** in the Deccan — draining the empire.
- Died in 1707 at **Ahmednagar**; the Mughal Empire began rapid decline after him.

🧠 **Active Recall:** Which Mughal abolished jizya, and which reimposed it? Who built the Peacock Throne?

### 2.8 Later Mughals and the End

- Weak successors ("Later Mughals") could not stop invasions: **Nadir Shah** (1739) looted Delhi and took the **Peacock Throne and Kohinoor**; **Ahmad Shah Abdali** invaded repeatedly.
- **Bahadur Shah Zafar II**, the last Mughal, was exiled to **Rangoon** after the 1857 revolt. The Mughal era formally ended in **1857**.

### 🧩 Mnemonic 2 — The Six Great Mughals
**"BHAJSA"** — **B**abur, **H**umayun, **A**kbar, **J**ahangir, **S**hah Jahan, **A**urangzeb
(Remember: Sher Shah Suri sits *between* Humayun's two reigns — "Humayun's sandwich.")

## Part 3: The Marathas and Other Regional Powers

- **Shivaji** (1630–1680) founded the Maratha kingdom; crowned at **Raigad in 1674** with the title **Chhatrapati**.
- His council of ministers was the **Ashtapradhan** (eight ministers); the PM was the **Peshwa**.
- Taxes: **Chauth** (one-fourth of revenue) and **Sardeshmukhi** (additional one-tenth).
- Famous episode: killing of **Afzal Khan** (1659) and the daring escape from Agra (1666).
- After Shivaji, power shifted to the **Peshwas** (Pune). Maratha power was broken at the **Third Battle of Panipat (1761)** against Ahmad Shah Abdali.
- **Vijayanagara Empire** (founded 1336 by **Harihara and Bukka**, Sangama dynasty; capital **Hampi**) and the **Bahmani Kingdom** were the great southern powers of the early medieval period. **Krishnadevaraya** (Tuluva dynasty) was Vijayanagara's greatest ruler; he wrote *Amuktamalyada*.

🧠 **Active Recall:** What were Chauth and Sardeshmukhi? Who founded Vijayanagara, and when?

## Part 4: Bhakti and Sufi Movements

### 4.1 Bhakti Saints

| Saint | Region / Association | Key Point |
|---|---|---|
| Shankaracharya | South India | Advaita (non-dualism) |
| Ramanuja | South India | Vishishtadvaita |
| Kabir | Varanasi | Disciple of Ramananda; dohas in Bijak |
| Guru Nanak | Punjab | Founder of Sikhism (1469–1539) |
| Chaitanya Mahaprabhu | Bengal | Krishna-bhakti, kirtans |
| Mirabai | Rajasthan | Devotion to Krishna |
| Tulsidas | Varanasi | Ramcharitmanas (Awadhi) |
| Surdas | Braj region | Sursagar; Krishna bhakti |
| Namdev, Tukaram | Maharashtra | Varkari tradition |

### 4.2 Sufi Orders (Silsilas)

- **Chishti** order: Khwaja **Moinuddin Chishti** (Ajmer), **Nizamuddin Auliya** (Delhi), Baba Farid (Pakpattan).
- **Suhrawardi** order: Bahauddin Zakariya (Multan).
- Sufi khanqahs (hospices) welcomed all castes and creeds — a bridge between communities.

### 🧩 Mnemonic 3 — Bhakti Saints by Region
**"KaNaCha MiTuSu"** — **Ka**bir (UP), **Na**nak (Punjab), **Cha**itanya (Bengal), **Mi**rabai (Rajasthan), **Tu**lsidas (UP), **Su**rdas (Braj).

## Part 5: Art, Architecture, and Culture

- **Indo-Islamic architecture**: arches, domes, minarets blended with Indian styles.
- Sultanate landmarks: **Qutub Minar** (Delhi), Alai Darwaza, Quwwat-ul-Islam mosque.
- Mughal landmarks: Humayun's Tomb (Delhi — first garden tomb, built by **Haji Begum**), Taj Mahal, Red Fort, Fatehpur Sikri.
- **Charbagh** (four-part garden) style came with the Mughals.
- Language: **Persian** was the court language; **Urdu** developed in this era from the interaction of Persian with local dialects.
- Painting: Mughal miniatures flourished under Akbar (Hamzanama), Jahangir (naturalism — artists **Mansur**, Abul Hasan).

When you read news about any Mughal-era monument, connect it back to its builder — that double linkage makes both facts stick.

## ⚠️ Caution: Examiner's Trap

**Trap 1 — Chronological Confusion (कालानुक्रमिक भ्रम):** Questions love to swap the three Battles of Panipat (1526, 1556, 1761) or ask who fought whom. Fix: **1526 = Babur vs Lodi; 1556 = Akbar vs Hemu; 1761 = Abdali vs Marathas.** Notice the gaps: 30 years, then 205 years.

**Trap 2 — Almost-Correct Statement (लगभग सही कथन):** "Qutbuddin Aibak completed the Qutub Minar." Sounds right — but Aibak only **started** it; **Iltutmish completed** it. Similarly, "Babur wrote Baburnama in Persian" is wrong — it was **Turkish** (translated into Persian later by Abdur Rahim Khan-i-Khana).

**Trap 3 — Similar Names (समान नाम):** Sher **Shah** Suri vs **Shah** Jahan; **Ibrahim Lodi** vs **Ibrahim Adil Shah**; **Nadir Shah** (1739, took Peacock Throne) vs **Ahmad Shah Abdali** (1761, Panipat). Read every name twice before answering.

**Trap 4 — Negative Phrasing (नकारात्मक वाक्यांश):** "Which of the following was NOT built by Shah Jahan?" — one option will be Humayun's Tomb or Agra Fort (Akbar). Underline the NOT before you even look at the options.
