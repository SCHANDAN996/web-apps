101. Who founded the Delhi Sultanate in 1206 CE?
(a) Iltutmish (b) Alauddin Khalji (c) Balban (d) Qutbuddin Aibak
Answer: (d)
Solution: Iltutmish is a tempting option because he consolidated the Sultanate and is sometimes called its real founder, but the dynasty was formally founded in 1206 by Qutbuddin Aibak, a former slave of Muhammad Ghori, who started the Mamluk (Slave) dynasty. So the answer is Qutbuddin Aibak.

102. The Iqta system, under which land revenue was assigned to officers in lieu of salary, was institutionalised in Delhi primarily by:
(a) Qutbuddin Aibak (b) Iltutmish (c) Razia Sultan (d) Balban
Answer: (b)
Solution: Balban is tempting because he strengthened the nobility's discipline, but the Iqta system as a regular administrative institution was organised by Iltutmish, who also created the corps of Forty (Chalisa). So the answer is Iltutmish.

103. Who was the first and only woman ruler of the Delhi Sultanate?
(a) Chand Bibi (b) Razia Sultan (c) Nur Jahan (d) Rani Durgavati
Answer: (b)
Solution: Nur Jahan is a tempting trap because she wielded real power as Jahangir's wife, but she was never a crowned ruler. Razia Sultan, daughter of Iltutmish, ruled Delhi from 1236 to 1240. So the answer is Razia Sultan.

104. Alauddin Khalji's market control policy primarily aimed at:
(a) Promoting foreign trade (b) Collecting more customs duty (c) Encouraging guilds of merchants (d) Keeping prices low to maintain a large army cheaply
Answer: (d)
Solution: Promoting foreign trade sounds plausible but is wrong; Alauddin fixed prices of essential goods, set up granaries and appointed shahna-i-mandi so that soldiers could be paid low salaries while living costs stayed low. So the answer is keeping prices low to maintain a large army cheaply.

105. Muhammad bin Tughlaq shifted his capital from Delhi to:
(a) Devagiri (Daulatabad) (b) Lahore (c) Agra (d) Jaunpur
Answer: (a)
Solution: Agra is tempting because later rulers moved capitals there, but Muhammad bin Tughlaq ordered the shift to Devagiri in the Deccan, renaming it Daulatabad, around 1327, to control the south better. So the answer is Devagiri (Daulatabad).

106. The famous traveller Ibn Battuta, who visited India during Muhammad bin Tughlaq's reign, came from:
(a) Persia (b) Turkey (c) Morocco (d) Arabia
Answer: (c)
Solution: Persia is a tempting trap because many medieval travellers like Al-Biruni had Persian links, but Ibn Battuta was a Moroccan scholar who wrote the Rihla and was made qazi of Delhi. So the answer is Morocco.

107. Who among the following introduced the token currency of bronze and copper coins?
(a) Alauddin Khalji (b) Firoz Shah Tughlaq (c) Muhammad bin Tughlaq (d) Sikandar Lodi
Answer: (c)
Solution: Alauddin Khalji is tempting due to his economic experiments, but token currency (tanka of bronze/copper exchangeable for silver) was introduced by Muhammad bin Tughlaq around 1329–30, and it failed due to forgery. So the answer is Muhammad bin Tughlaq.

Answer: (b)

109. Timur invaded India in 1398 during the reign of:
(a) Firoz Shah Tughlaq (b) Nasiruddin Mahmud Shah Tughlaq (c) Bahlul Lodi (d) Khizr Khan
Answer: (b)
Solution: Firoz Shah Tughlaq is a chronological trap because he was the most famous Tughlaq, but he died in 1388. Timur's invasion of 1398 occurred when the weak Nasiruddin Mahmud Shah Tughlaq ruled Delhi. So the answer is Nasiruddin Mahmud Shah Tughlaq.

Answer: (a)
Solution: Turks is a tempting answer because most Sultanate dynasties were Turkish, but the Lodis (1451–1526), founded by Bahlul Lodi, were the first Afghan dynasty to rule Delhi. So the answer is Afghans.

111. The First Battle of Panipat (1526) was fought between Babur and:
(a) Rana Sanga (b) Medini Rai (c) Ibrahim Lodi (d) Daulat Khan Lodi
Answer: (c)
Solution: Rana Sanga is a chronological trap because Babur fought him at Khanwa in 1527, a year later. At Panipat in 1526, Babur defeated and killed Sultan Ibrahim Lodi, ending the Delhi Sultanate. So the answer is Ibrahim Lodi.

(a) Persian (b) Arabic (c) Urdu (d) Chagatai Turkish
Answer: (d)
Solution: Persian is a strong trap because most Mughal court literature was in Persian and the Baburnama was later translated into Persian, but Babur originally wrote his memoirs in Chagatai Turkish, his mother tongue. So the answer is Chagatai Turkish.

Answer: (d)
Solution: Tanka is tempting as the standard Sultanate coin, but Sher Shah Suri issued the silver Rupiya, which became the basis of the modern rupee; the copper dam also continued under him. So the answer is Rupiya.

(a) Sher Shah Suri (b) Alauddin Khalji (c) Akbar (d) Ashoka
Answer: (a)
Solution: Ashoka is a trap because the route existed in ancient times, but its medieval reconstruction with sarais, trees and wells was the work of Sher Shah Suri during his short reign (1540–1545). So the answer is Sher Shah Suri.

116. Akbar's land revenue system, based on measurement and average produce, was organised by:
(a) Birbal (b) Abul Fazl (c) Raja Todar Mal (d) Faizi
Answer: (c)
Solution: Abul Fazl is a trap because he wrote the Ain-i-Akbari describing the system, but the Dahsala (Zabti) revenue settlement was designed by the finance minister Raja Todar Mal. So the answer is Raja Todar Mal.

117. The Din-i-Ilahi, propounded by Akbar in 1582, was:
(a) A syncretic ethical code blending ideas from many faiths (b) A new religion with priests and scriptures (c) A sect of Islam (d) A branch of Sufism
Answer: (a)
Solution: Calling it a new religion with priests and scriptures is the classic trap; Din-i-Ilahi had no holy book, no priesthood and very few followers — it was an ethical-spiritual order drawing on several religions. So the answer is a syncretic ethical code blending ideas from many faiths.

121. Consider the following statements about the Battle of Talikota (1565):
1. It was fought between Vijayanagara and a confederacy of Deccan Sultanates.
2. Rama Raya, the de facto ruler of Vijayanagara, was killed.
3. It led to the immediate annexation of Vijayanagara by the Mughals.
Which of the above is/are correct?
(a) 1, 2 and 3 (b) 2 and 3 only (c) 1 and 3 only (d) 1 and 2 only
Answer: (d)
Solution: Statement 3 is the trap: the Mughals had no role at Talikota; the victors were the Deccan Sultanates (Bijapur, Golconda, Ahmadnagar, Bidar), and the city was sacked but the empire lingered under the Aravidu line. Statements 1 and 2 are correct. So the answer is 1 and 2 only.

122. Arrange the following Mughal emperors in correct chronological order of their accession:
