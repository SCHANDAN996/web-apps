Questions 51–75: Medium

51. Who was the founder of the Bahmani Kingdom?
(a) Muhammad Shah I (b) Alauddin Hasan Bahman Shah (c) Firuz Shah Bahmani (d) Ahmad Shah Wali
Answer: (b)
Solution: Alauddin Hasan Bahman Shah, also known as Hasan Gangu, founded the Bahmani Kingdom in 1347 after rebelling against Muhammad bin Tughlaq. Firuz Shah Bahmani was a later notable ruler, not the founder, so the answer is Alauddin Hasan Bahman Shah.
Source: NCERT Class 7 History (Our Pasts II)

52. The Vijayanagara Empire was founded in 1336 by which brothers?
(a) Deva Raya I and Deva Raya II (b) Harihara and Bukka (c) Krishnadeva Raya and Achyuta Raya (d) Narasimha and Virupaksha
Answer: (b)
Solution: Harihara and Bukka of the Sangama dynasty founded Vijayanagara in 1336 under the guidance of sage Vidyaranya. Krishnadeva Raya belonged to the later Tuluva dynasty, so the answer is Harihara and Bukka.
Source: NCERT Class 7 History (Our Pasts II)

53. Which Delhi Sultan introduced the token currency of bronze and copper?
(a) Alauddin Khalji (b) Muhammad bin Tughlaq (c) Firuz Shah Tughlaq (d) Balban
Answer: (b)
Solution: Muhammad bin Tughlaq introduced token currency of bronze and copper around 1329–30, which failed due to widespread counterfeiting. Alauddin Khalji is known for market reforms, not token currency, so the answer is Muhammad bin Tughlaq.
Source: NCERT Class 7 History (Our Pasts II)

54. The famous traveller Ibn Battuta visited India during the reign of which ruler?
(a) Muhammad bin Tughlaq (b) Ghiyasuddin Balban (c) Alauddin Khalji (d) Firuz Shah Tughlaq
Answer: (a)
Solution: The Moroccan traveller Ibn Battuta came to India in 1333 during Muhammad bin Tughlaq's reign and was appointed Qazi of Delhi. A common trap is to link him with Alauddin Khalji, but that period is associated with Amir Khusrau, so the answer is Muhammad bin Tughlaq.
Source: NCERT Class 7 History (Our Pasts II)

55. Who wrote the book 'Kitab-ul-Hind'?
(a) Abdur Razzaq (b) Ibn Battuta (c) Marco Polo (d) Al-Biruni
Answer: (d)
Solution: Al-Biruni, who came to India with Mahmud of Ghazni, wrote Kitab-ul-Hind (Tahqiq-i-Hind), a detailed account of Indian sciences and culture. Ibn Battuta wrote the Rihla, so the answer is Al-Biruni.
Source: NCERT Class 7 History (Our Pasts II)

56. The First Battle of Panipat (1526) was fought between Babur and which ruler?
(a) Ibrahim Lodi (b) Rana Sanga (c) Sikandar Lodi (d) Mahmud Lodi
Answer: (a)
Solution: Babur defeated and killed Ibrahim Lodi at the First Battle of Panipat in 1526, laying the foundation of the Mughal Empire. Rana Sanga was defeated a year later at Khanwa (1527), which is a frequent chronological trap, so the answer is Ibrahim Lodi.
Source: NCERT Class 7 History (Our Pasts II)

57. Which Mughal emperor built the city of Fatehpur Sikri?
(a) Akbar (b) Humayun (c) Babur (d) Jahangir
Answer: (a)
Solution: Akbar built Fatehpur Sikri near Agra in honour of Sheikh Salim Chishti and made it his capital from about 1571 to 1585. The Buland Darwaza there commemorates his Gujarat victory, so the answer is Akbar.
Source: NCERT Class 7 History (Our Pasts II)

58. The 'Din-i-Ilahi' was propounded by Akbar in which year?
(a) 1575 (b) 1582 (c) 1562 (d) 1590
Answer: (b)
Solution: Akbar propounded Din-i-Ilahi in 1582 as a syncretic ethical path drawing on many religions; it never became a mass movement. The Ibadat Khana discussions began earlier (1575), which is the tempting wrong date, so the answer is 1582.
Source: NCERT Class 7 History (Our Pasts II)

59. Who among the following was the finance minister (Diwan) of Akbar who introduced the Dahsala system?
(a) Birbal (b) Raja Todar Mal (c) Tansen (d) Abul Fazl
Answer: (b)
Solution: Raja Todar Mal, Akbar's revenue minister, introduced the Dahsala (Zabti) system based on ten-year average yields and prices. Abul Fazl was the court historian, not the revenue minister, so the answer is Raja Todar Mal.
Source: NCERT Class 7 History (Our Pasts II)

60. The Battle of Haldighati (1576) was fought between Akbar's forces and which Rajput ruler?
(a) Rana Sanga (b) Raja Man Singh (c) Rana Udai Singh (d) Maharana Pratap
Answer: (d)
Solution: Maharana Pratap of Mewar fought the Mughal army led by Man Singh and Asaf Khan at Haldighati in 1576. Rana Sanga fought Babur at Khanwa decades earlier, a classic similar-name trap, so the answer is Maharana Pratap.
Source: NCERT Class 7 History (Our Pasts II)

61. Which Sikh Guru was executed on the orders of Jahangir?
(a) Guru Nanak Dev (b) Guru Angad Dev (c) Guru Tegh Bahadur (d) Guru Arjan Dev
Answer: (d)
Solution: Guru Arjan Dev, the fifth Sikh Guru who compiled the Adi Granth, was executed in 1606 on Jahangir's orders. Guru Tegh Bahadur was executed under Aurangzeb, a frequent confusion, so the answer is Guru Arjan Dev.
Source: NCERT Class 7 History (Our Pasts II)

62. The Peacock Throne (Takht-i-Taus) was made for which Mughal emperor?
(a) Akbar (b) Jahangir (c) Aurangzeb (d) Shah Jahan
Answer: (d)
Solution: The Peacock Throne was commissioned by Shah Jahan and completed around 1635; Nadir Shah carried it to Persia in 1739. It is wrongly attributed to Akbar at times, so the answer is Shah Jahan.
Source: NCERT Class 7 History (Our Pasts II)

63. Who was the last ruler of the Lodi dynasty?
(a) Bahlul Lodi (b) Sikandar Lodi (c) Ibrahim Lodi (d) Daulat Khan Lodi
Answer: (c)
Solution: Ibrahim Lodi, son of Sikandar Lodi, was the last Lodi ruler and died at Panipat in 1526. Daulat Khan Lodi was the governor of Punjab who invited Babur, not a sultan, so the answer is Ibrahim Lodi.
Source: NCERT Class 7 History (Our Pasts II)

64. The 'Chahalgani' or Corps of Forty was created by which Delhi Sultan?
(a) Iltutmish (b) Qutbuddin Aibak (c) Balban (d) Alauddin Khalji
Answer: (a)
Solution: Iltutmish organised the Turkan-i-Chahalgani, a group of forty Turkish nobles, which later became a powerful and disruptive faction. Balban actually destroyed the Forty, which is the trap, so the answer is Iltutmish.
Source: NCERT Class 7 History (Our Pasts II)

65. Which Bhakti saint was a disciple of Ramananda and composed verses in Hindi?
(a) Chaitanya Mahaprabhu (b) Tukaram (c) Namdev (d) Kabir
Answer: (d)
Solution: Kabir, traditionally regarded as a disciple of Ramananda, composed dohas in simple Hindi criticising both Hindu and Muslim orthodoxy. Namdev and Tukaram belonged to the Maharashtra Varkari tradition, so the answer is Kabir.
Source: NCERT Class 7 History (Our Pasts II)

66. The Second Battle of Panipat (1556) was fought between the forces of Akbar and which commander?
(a) Hemu (b) Sher Shah Suri (c) Sikandar Shah Suri (d) Ibrahim Lodi
Answer: (a)
Solution: Hemu, the general of Adil Shah Suri, was defeated by Akbar's forces under Bairam Khan at the Second Battle of Panipat in 1556. Sher Shah died in 1545, so he could not have fought in 1556, and the answer is Hemu.
Source: NCERT Class 7 History (Our Pasts II)

67. Who introduced the 'Dagh' (branding of horses) and 'Chehra' (descriptive roll of soldiers) systems?
(a) Alauddin Khalji (b) Balban (c) Muhammad bin Tughlaq (d) Akbar
Answer: (a)
Solution: Alauddin Khalji introduced the Dagh and Chehra systems to maintain a large standing army and prevent fraud in the cavalry. Akbar later adopted similar practices through the Mansabdari system, which causes confusion, so the answer is Alauddin Khalji.
Source: NCERT Class 7 History (Our Pasts II)

68. The Moti Masjid inside the Red Fort of Delhi was built by which emperor?
(a) Shah Jahan (b) Jahangir (c) Aurangzeb (d) Bahadur Shah I
Answer: (c)
Solution: The Moti Masjid in Delhi's Red Fort was built by Aurangzeb around 1659–60 for his personal use. The Moti Masjid in Agra Fort was built by Shah Jahan, a classic same-name trap, so the answer is Aurangzeb.
Source: NCERT Class 7 History (Our Pasts II)

69. Which Mughal emperor granted the English East India Company the 'Farman' of 1717 through his officials?
(a) Aurangzeb (b) Bahadur Shah I (c) Farrukhsiyar (d) Muhammad Shah
Answer: (c)
Solution: Farrukhsiyar issued the famous farman of 1717 granting the English duty-free trade privileges in Bengal in return for an annual payment. It is often wrongly credited to Aurangzeb, so the answer is Farrukhsiyar.
Source: NCERT Class 7 History (Our Pasts II)

70. The 'Ain-i-Akbari' was written by which scholar?
(a) Faizi (b) Abul Fazl (c) Badauni (d) Nizamuddin Ahmad
Answer: (b)
Solution: Abul Fazl wrote the Ain-i-Akbari, the third volume of the Akbarnama, detailing Akbar's administration, revenue and culture. Badauni wrote Muntakhab-ut-Tawarikh, a critical account, so the answer is Abul Fazl.
Source: NCERT Class 7 History (Our Pasts II)

71. Who among the following shifted his capital from Delhi to Daulatabad?
(a) Muhammad bin Tughlaq (b) Alauddin Khalji (c) Firuz Shah Tughlaq (d) Sikandar Lodi
Answer: (a)
Solution: Muhammad bin Tughlaq ordered the transfer of the capital from Delhi to Daulatabad (Devagiri) around 1327 to control the Deccan better; the move caused great suffering and was reversed. Sikandar Lodi shifted his capital to Agra, a tempting confusion, so the answer is Muhammad bin Tughlaq.
Source: NCERT Class 7 History (Our Pasts II)

72. Krishnadeva Raya belonged to which dynasty of the Vijayanagara Empire?
(a) Sangama (b) Saluva (c) Tuluva (d) Aravidu
Answer: (c)
Solution: Krishnadeva Raya (reigned 1509–1529), the greatest Vijayanagara ruler, belonged to the Tuluva dynasty. The Sangama dynasty was the founding dynasty of Harihara and Bukka, which is the common trap, so the answer is Tuluva.
Source: NCERT Class 7 History (Our Pasts II)

73. Which Rajput kingdom's ruler, Rana Kumbha, built the Vijay Stambh (Tower of Victory) at Chittor?
(a) Marwar (b) Amber (c) Mewar (d) Bundi
Answer: (c)
Solution: Rana Kumbha of Mewar built the Vijay Stambh at Chittorgarh around 1448 to commemorate his victory over Mahmud Khalji of Malwa. Mewar, not Marwar, was the Sisodia kingdom of Chittor, so the answer is Mewar.
Source: NCERT Class 7 History (Our Pasts II)

74. The Mughal emperor who died after falling from the stairs of his library was:
(a) Babur (b) Akbar (c) Humayun (d) Jahangir
Answer: (c)
Solution: Humayun died in 1556 after falling from the steps of his library (Sher Mandal) at Purana Qila in Delhi. Babur died of illness in 1530, so the answer is Humayun.
Source: NCERT Class 7 History (Our Pasts II)

75. Which of the following statements about the Mansabdari system is correct?
(a) It was introduced by Babur in 1526 (b) It was abolished by Akbar in 1595 (c) Mansabdars were always paid only in cash (d) The rank (zat) fixed the mansabdar's status and salary
Answer: (d)
Solution: The Mansabdari system was organised by Akbar, not Babur, and mansabdars were often paid through jagirs (land revenue assignments), not only cash; it was never abolished by Akbar. The zat rank determined a mansabdar's personal status and pay, so the answer is that the rank (zat) fixed the mansabdar's status and salary.
Source: NCERT Class 7 History (Our Pasts II)
