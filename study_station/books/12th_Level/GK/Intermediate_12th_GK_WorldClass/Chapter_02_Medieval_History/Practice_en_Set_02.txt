26. Who was the founder of the Delhi Sultanate?
(a) Balban (b) Iltutmish (c) Qutb-ud-din Aibak (d) Alauddin Khilji
Answer: (c)
Solution: Iltutmish is a tempting choice because he consolidated the Sultanate and is sometimes called its real founder, but the actual founder in 1206 was Qutb-ud-din Aibak, a slave of Muhammad Ghori, who started the Mamluk (Slave) dynasty. So the answer is Qutb-ud-din Aibak.

27. The famous Qutub Minar was completed by which ruler?
(a) Iltutmish (b) Qutb-ud-din Aibak (c) Firoz Shah Tughlaq (d) Alauddin Khilji
Answer: (a)
Solution: Aibak only began the construction of the Qutub Minar, which is why he is the tempting wrong option; he died before completing it. Iltutmish completed the tower, and Firoz Shah Tughlaq later repaired its top storeys after lightning damage. So the answer is Iltutmish.

28. Who was the first and only woman ruler of the Delhi Sultanate?
(a) Nur Jahan (b) Rani Durgavati (c) Chand Bibi (d) Razia Sultan
Answer: (d)
Solution: Nur Jahan is a trap because she wielded great power, but she was the Mughal empress and wife of Jahangir, never a crowned Sultan. Razia Sultan, daughter of Iltutmish, ruled Delhi from 1236 to 1240. So the answer is Razia Sultan.

29. The 'Iqta' system of land revenue assignment was introduced in India by which Sultan?
(a) Iltutmish (b) Alauddin Khilji (c) Balban (d) Muhammad bin Tughlaq
Answer: (a)
Solution: Alauddin Khilji is tempting because of his famous revenue and market reforms, but the Iqta system — assigning land revenue to officers in lieu of salary — was institutionalised by Iltutmish. So the answer is Iltutmish.

30. Which Sultan introduced the token currency of bronze and copper coins?
(a) Alauddin Khilji (b) Muhammad bin Tughlaq (c) Firoz Shah Tughlaq (d) Sikandar Lodi
Answer: (b)
Solution: Firoz Shah Tughlaq is tempting because of his many economic measures like canals and new towns, but the failed experiment of token currency (around 1329–30), inspired by Chinese paper money, belongs to Muhammad bin Tughlaq. So the answer is Muhammad bin Tughlaq.

31. The market control policy (price regulation) of the Delhi Sultanate is associated with which ruler?
(a) Balban (b) Muhammad bin Tughlaq (c) Ibrahim Lodi (d) Alauddin Khilji
Answer: (d)
Solution: Muhammad bin Tughlaq is tempting since he too experimented with economic measures, but the strict price-control system with separate markets (mandis) and intelligence officers was the work of Alauddin Khilji, aimed at maintaining a large standing army cheaply. So the answer is Alauddin Khilji.

32. Who wrote the book 'Tarikh-i-Firoz Shahi'?
(a) Ziauddin Barani (b) Amir Khusrau (c) Minhaj-us-Siraj (d) Ibn Battuta
Answer: (a)
Solution: Amir Khusrau is tempting as the most famous writer of the Sultanate era, but his works include Tughlaq Nama and Khazain-ul-Futuh. Tarikh-i-Firoz Shahi, covering rulers from Balban to Firoz Shah Tughlaq, was written by Ziauddin Barani. So the answer is Ziauddin Barani.

33. The Moroccan traveller Ibn Battuta visited India during the reign of which Sultan?
(a) Muhammad bin Tughlaq (b) Firoz Shah Tughlaq (c) Alauddin Khilji (d) Balban
Answer: (a)
Solution: Firoz Shah Tughlaq is tempting because Ibn Battuta's account describes a prosperous Delhi, but Ibn Battuta arrived in 1333 and served as a qazi under Muhammad bin Tughlaq, whose court he describes in his book Rihla. So the answer is Muhammad bin Tughlaq.

34. The First Battle of Panipat (1526) was fought between Babur and which ruler?
(a) Rana Sanga (b) Mahmud Lodi (c) Sikandar Lodi (d) Ibrahim Lodi
Answer: (d)
Solution: Rana Sanga is the classic trap because Babur did fight him — but that was the Battle of Khanwa in 1527, not Panipat. At Panipat in 1526 Babur defeated and killed Ibrahim Lodi, ending the Delhi Sultanate. So the answer is Ibrahim Lodi.

35. Which Mughal emperor defeated Hemu in the Second Battle of Panipat (1556)?
(a) Babur (b) Humayun (c) Jahangir (d) Akbar
Answer: (d)
Solution: Humayun is tempting because he was Akbar's father and had died in 1556, but the battle was fought after his death by the 13-year-old Akbar under the regency of Bairam Khan, defeating Hemu, the general of Adil Shah Suri. So the answer is Akbar.

36. The Din-i-Ilahi, a syncretic religious path, was propounded by which emperor?
(a) Shah Jahan (b) Aurangzeb (c) Jahangir (d) Akbar
Answer: (d)
Solution: Aurangzeb is tempting in the opposite direction as the orthodox ruler, which confuses some candidates. Din-i-Ilahi (1582) was Akbar's attempt to blend elements of many religions; it had very few followers and died with him. So the answer is Akbar.

37. The 'Mansabdari system' of the Mughals was primarily a system related to what?
(a) Military and civil ranking of officials (b) Land revenue only (c) Religious taxation (d) Trade regulation
Answer: (a)
Solution: Land revenue is tempting because mansabdars were paid through jagirs (land assignments), but the Mansabdari system itself was a grading system fixing each officer's rank (zat and sawar), status, salary and military obligation. So the answer is military and civil ranking of officials.

39. The Peacock Throne (Takht-i-Taus) was taken away to Persia by which invader?

Answer: (c)
Solution: Krishnadeva Raya is tempting as the most famous Vijayanagara ruler, but he belonged to the Tuluva dynasty a century and a half later. The empire was founded in 1336 by the brothers Harihara and Bukka of the Sangama dynasty. So the answer is Harihara and Bukka.

41. Krishnadeva Raya, the greatest ruler of Vijayanagara, belonged to which dynasty?
(a) Sangama (b) Tuluva (c) Saluva (d) Aravidu
Answer: (b)
Solution: Sangama is tempting because it was the founding dynasty of Vijayanagara, but Krishnadeva Raya (reigned 1509–1529), author of Amuktamalyada, belonged to the third dynasty, the Tuluva dynasty. So the answer is Tuluva.

48. Which taxes did Shivaji collect from territories outside his kingdom?

Answer: (b)
