# Key Facts & Figures — Environment & Ecology (Basic)

## Part 1: High-Yield Facts

| Fact | Description | Examiner's Trap | Trap Type |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ecology term coined by | Ernst Haeckel (1866), German biologist | Confused with Charles Darwin or Carl Linnaeus | Similar names |
| Father of Ecology in India | Ramdeo Misra | Confused with Salim Ali (ornithology) or M.S. Swaminathan (Green Revolution) | Similar names |
| Ecosystem term coined by | A.G. Tansley (1935) | Confused with Haeckel (ecology) | Similar names |
| Basic unit of ecology | Organism; ecosystem is the functional unit of nature | "Cell" given as tempting wrong option | Nearly-correct statement |
| 10% energy law | Only ~10% energy transfers to next trophic level (Lindeman, 1942) | Options like 1%, 50%, 90% | Numerical confusion |
| Trophic level with maximum energy | Producers (green plants) | "Top carnivore" as trap | Reversal trap |
| Primary productivity | Rate of biomass production by producers via photosynthesis | Confused with standing crop (biomass present at a time) | Similar terms |
| Food chain vs food web | Chain = linear; web = interconnected chains | Asked which is more stable — food web | Nearly-correct statement |
| Ecological pyramid always upright | Pyramid of energy | Pyramid of numbers/biomass can be inverted (e.g., pond, parasitic chain) | Exception trap |
| Decomposers | Bacteria and fungi; break dead matter into inorganic nutrients | Earthworm listed as decomposer (it is a detritivore) | Category confusion |
| Biome | Largest ecological unit on land (e.g., tundra, taiga, desert) | Confused with biosphere or habitat | Similar terms |
| Biosphere term | Coined by Eduard Suess; popularised by Vernadsky | Haeckel given as trap | Similar names |
| Ecotone | Transition zone between two ecosystems; high species diversity (edge effect) | Called "low diversity zone" in wrong option | Reversal trap |
| Ecological niche | Functional role/address of a species in ecosystem | Confused with habitat (place only) | Similar terms |
| Pioneer species | First species in succession (lichens on bare rock) | Climax community given as trap | Sequence confusion |
| Climax community | Stable, final community of succession | Confused with seral community | Similar terms |
| Hydrosere vs Xerosere | Succession starting in water vs dry/bare rock | Order of stages asked in reverse | Sequence confusion |
| Oxygen-carbon dioxide balance | Photosynthesis maintains atmospheric O₂–CO₂ balance | Respiration given as answer trap | Nearly-correct statement |
| Ozone layer location | Stratosphere (absorbs UV-B/UV-C) | Troposphere given as trap (ground-level ozone is a pollutant) | Location confusion |
| Ozone depleting substances | CFCs, halons, methyl bromide | CO₂ given as trap (greenhouse gas, not ODS) | Category confusion |
| Montreal Protocol (1987) | Phase-out of ODS (CFCs) | Confused with Kyoto Protocol (GHG) or Paris Agreement | Treaty confusion |
| Kyoto Protocol (1997, in force 2005) | Reduction of greenhouse gas emissions | Confused with Montreal Protocol | Treaty confusion |
| Paris Agreement (2015) | Limit warming well below 2°C, pursue 1.5°C | "Mandatory cuts for all" as trap (it uses NDCs) | Nearly-correct statement |
| Greenhouse gases | CO₂, CH₄, N₂O, water vapour, CFCs, O₃ | O₂ or N₂ given as trap | Category confusion |
| Largest GHG contributor (by volume) | CO₂; but CH₄ has higher warming potential per molecule | "Most potent = most abundant" confusion | Category confusion |
| Acid rain pH | Below 5.6 (normal rain ~5.6) | pH 7 given as trap | Numerical confusion |
| Acid rain cause | SO₂ and NOx → sulphuric & nitric acid | CO₂ given as trap | Category confusion |
| Eutrophication | Nutrient (N, P) enrichment of water → algal bloom → oxygen depletion | Called "increase in dissolved oxygen" in trap | Reversal trap |
| BOD | Biochemical Oxygen Demand — higher BOD = more polluted water | "High BOD = clean water" trap | Reversal trap |
| Biomagnification | Increasing concentration of toxins (DDT, mercury) up the food chain | Maximum in producers given as trap (actually top carnivores) | Reversal trap |
| Minamata disease | Mercury poisoning (Japan) | Confused with Itai-itai (cadmium) or Blue baby syndrome (nitrate) | Disease–pollutant confusion |
| Itai-itai disease | Cadmium poisoning (Japan) | Confused with Minamata | Disease–pollutant confusion |
| Smog types | Classical (London, sulphurous, reducing) vs Photochemical (Los Angeles, oxidising, PAN) | Causes swapped in options | Reversal trap |
| PAN | Peroxyacetyl nitrate — component of photochemical smog | Called a greenhouse gas in trap | Category confusion |
| National parks vs wildlife sanctuaries | NP: stricter, no grazing; boundaries fixed by legislation | "Hunting allowed in sanctuaries" as trap (both prohibit hunting) | Nearly-correct statement |
| Biosphere reserve zones | Core, buffer, transition | "Tourism in core zone" as trap | Nearly-correct statement |
| First biosphere reserve of India | Nilgiri (1986) | Sundarbans or Nanda Devi given as trap | Similar names |
| Project Tiger launch | 1973 | Confused with Project Elephant (1992) | Year confusion |
| Project Elephant launch | 1992 | Confused with Project Tiger (1973) | Year confusion |
| IUCN Red List categories | Extinct, EW, CR, EN, VU, NT, LC | Order of threat categories asked | Sequence confusion |
| Red Data Book published by | IUCN (founded 1948, HQ Gland, Switzerland) | UNEP or WWF given as trap | Organisation confusion |
| CITES | Regulates international trade in endangered species (Washington, 1973) | Confused with CMS (Bonn Convention, migratory species) | Treaty confusion |
| Ramsar Convention (1971) | Wetlands of international importance | Confused with World Heritage Convention | Treaty confusion |
| Chipko Movement | 1973, Gaura Devi / Sunderlal Bahuguna, tree-hugging (Garhwal) | Medha Patkar (Narmada) given as trap | Person confusion |
| Environment Protection Act | 1986 (after Bhopal disaster; umbrella legislation) | 1972 (Water Act 1974, Air Act 1981) given as trap | Year confusion |
| World Environment Day | 5 June (UNEP, since 1973; Stockholm Conference 1972) | 22 April (Earth Day) or 16 September (Ozone Day) given as trap | Date confusion |
| Stockholm Conference | 1972 — first UN conference on human environment | Confused with Rio Earth Summit 1992 | Year confusion |
| Rio Earth Summit (1992) | Agenda 21, CBD, UNFCCC | Confused with Stockholm 1972 | Treaty confusion |
| In-situ vs Ex-situ conservation | In-situ: natural habitat (NP, sanctuary); Ex-situ: zoo, botanical garden, gene bank | Categories swapped in options | Reversal trap |
| Keystone species | Species with disproportionate effect on ecosystem (e.g., fig trees, sea otter) | Confused with flagship or umbrella species | Similar terms |
| Indicator species | Species signalling environmental quality (lichens → air pollution) | Lichen called "pollution-tolerant" in trap (it is pollution-sensitive) | Reversal trap |
