Questions 1–20: Easy | Questions 21–25: Medium

1. The term "ecology" was coined by:
(a) Ernst Haeckel (b) Charles Darwin (c) Gregor Mendel (d) Alexander von Humboldt
Answer: (a)
Solution: Many students pick Darwin because of his association with evolution and nature study, but he never coined the term. The word "ecology" (Oekologie) was coined by the German biologist Ernst Haeckel in 1866, so the answer is Ernst Haeckel.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Biology

2. The study of interactions between organisms and their environment is called:
(a) Geology (b) Ecology (c) Ethology (d) Taxonomy
Answer: (b)
Solution: Ethology is the study of animal behaviour, which is a tempting distractor, and taxonomy deals with classification of organisms. The study of interrelationships between living organisms and their environment is ecology, so the answer is ecology.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Biology

3. Which of the following is a biotic component of an ecosystem?
(a) Sunlight (b) Temperature (c) Soil minerals (d) Decomposers
Answer: (d)
Solution: Sunlight, temperature and soil minerals are all non-living, i.e. abiotic components, which is the common confusion here. Decomposers like bacteria and fungi are living organisms, so the answer is decomposers.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Biology

4. The basic functional unit of the environment is:
(a) Ecosystem (b) Community (c) Population (d) Biosphere
Answer: (a)
Solution: Population and community are only groups of organisms and do not include the non-living environment, so they cannot be the functional unit. The ecosystem, which includes both biotic and abiotic components interacting together, is the basic functional unit, so the answer is ecosystem.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Biology

5. In a food chain, green plants occupy the position of:
(a) Producers (b) Primary consumers (c) Secondary consumers (d) Decomposers
Answer: (a)
Solution: Consumers eat other organisms, so plants cannot be consumers of any level — that is the tempting error. Green plants prepare their own food by photosynthesis and are called producers, so the answer is producers.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science

6. The 10% law of energy transfer in a food chain was given by:
(a) Elton (b) Odum (c) Tansley (d) Lindeman
Answer: (d)
Solution: Tansley coined the term "ecosystem" and Elton gave the concept of ecological pyramids, so both are common traps. The 10% law of energy transfer between trophic levels was proposed by Raymond Lindeman in 1942, so the answer is Lindeman.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Biology

7. The term "ecosystem" was coined by:
(a) Ernst Haeckel (b) E.P. Odum (c) A.G. Tansley (d) Charles Elton
Answer: (c)
Solution: Haeckel coined "ecology", not "ecosystem" — a classic similar-name trap. The term "ecosystem" was coined by the British ecologist A.G. Tansley in 1935, so the answer is A.G. Tansley.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Biology

8. Which of the following is an example of a decomposer?
(a) Grass (b) Fungi (c) Deer (d) Tiger
Answer: (b)
Solution: Grass is a producer and deer and tiger are consumers, so none of them break down dead matter. Fungi, along with bacteria, decompose dead organic matter into simpler substances, so the answer is fungi.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science

9. The largest ecosystem of the world is:
(a) Forest ecosystem (b) Ocean ecosystem (c) Grassland ecosystem (d) Desert ecosystem
Answer: (b)
Solution: Forests look vast on land, which tempts many students, but oceans cover about 71% of the Earth's surface. Hence the ocean (marine) ecosystem is the largest ecosystem, so the answer is ocean ecosystem.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Geography

10. The pyramid of energy in any ecosystem is always:
(a) Inverted (b) Irregular (c) Spindle-shaped (d) Upright
Answer: (d)
Solution: Pyramids of number and biomass can be inverted in some ecosystems (e.g., a tree supporting many insects), which is the trap. Because energy decreases at every successive trophic level, the pyramid of energy is always upright, so the answer is upright.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Biology

11. Ozone layer is found in which layer of the atmosphere?
(a) Troposphere (b) Mesosphere (c) Stratosphere (d) Thermosphere
Answer: (c)
Solution: The troposphere is where weather occurs and is the lowest layer, so it is a common wrong guess. The ozone layer, which absorbs harmful ultraviolet radiation, lies in the stratosphere, so the answer is stratosphere.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Geography

12. Which gas is mainly responsible for the greenhouse effect?
(a) Oxygen (b) Nitrogen (c) Hydrogen (d) Carbon dioxide
Answer: (d)
Solution: Oxygen and nitrogen make up most of the atmosphere but do not trap heat, which misleads some students. Carbon dioxide is the principal greenhouse gas responsible for global warming, so the answer is carbon dioxide.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Chemistry

13. World Environment Day is celebrated on:
(a) 5 June (b) 22 April (c) 16 September (d) 11 July
Answer: (a)
Solution: 22 April is Earth Day and 16 September is World Ozone Day — these similar dates are the examiner's trap. World Environment Day, led by UNEP since 1973, is celebrated on 5 June, so the answer is 5 June.
Source: UNEP official website

14. The Chipko Movement is associated with:
(a) Protecting trees (b) Saving rivers (c) Wildlife conservation (d) Anti-mining protest
Answer: (a)
Solution: The movement is sometimes confused with later anti-mining or dam protests, which is the trap. The Chipko Movement (1973, Garhwal, Uttarakhand) involved villagers hugging trees to prevent their felling, so the answer is protecting trees.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Geography

15. Which of the following is a non-renewable resource?
(a) Solar energy (b) Wind energy (c) Coal (d) Forests
Answer: (c)
Solution: Forests can regrow and solar and wind energy are inexhaustible, so they are renewable. Coal takes millions of years to form and gets exhausted by use, so the answer is coal.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Geography

16. The biological process by which nitrogen gas is converted into ammonia in the soil is called:
(a) Nitrogen fixation (b) Denitrification (c) Nitrification (d) Ammonification
Answer: (a)
Solution: Nitrification converts ammonia into nitrates and denitrification releases nitrogen back to the atmosphere — these similar terms are the trap. Conversion of atmospheric nitrogen into ammonia by bacteria like Rhizobium is nitrogen fixation, so the answer is nitrogen fixation.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Biology

17. Which of the following is a primary consumer in a grassland ecosystem?
(a) Grass (b) Frog (c) Grasshopper (d) Snake
Answer: (c)
Solution: Frog and snake are higher-level consumers and grass is a producer, so they are distractors. The grasshopper feeds directly on plants, making it a herbivore or primary consumer, so the answer is grasshopper.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science

18. Biodiversity hotspots are characterised by:
(a) Only marine species (b) Low species richness (c) Absence of human activity (d) High endemism and high threat
Answer: (d)
Solution: Hotspots are not defined by absence of humans — in fact human pressure is the reason for their identification, which is the trap. A biodiversity hotspot must have high endemism and significant habitat loss/threat, so the answer is high endemism and high threat.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Biology

19. The full form of CFC, responsible for ozone depletion, is:
(a) Carbon Fluoro Chloride (b) Chloroform Carbon (c) Chlorofluorocarbon (d) Carbon Fluoride Compound
Answer: (c)
Solution: The options are designed to look alike, which is the trap; only one is the standard chemical name. CFC stands for chlorofluorocarbon, compounds of chlorine, fluorine and carbon used in refrigerants, so the answer is chlorofluorocarbon.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Chemistry

20. An example of an artificial ecosystem is:
(a) Pond (b) Forest (c) Ocean (d) Crop field
Answer: (d)
Solution: Pond, forest and ocean are natural ecosystems formed without human intervention. A crop field (agroecosystem) is created and maintained by humans, so the answer is crop field.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Biology

21. Consider the following statements about ecological pyramids:
1. The pyramid of numbers can be inverted in a tree ecosystem.
2. The pyramid of biomass can be inverted in a pond ecosystem.
3. The pyramid of energy can be inverted in a desert ecosystem.
Which of the statements given above is/are correct?
(a) 1, 2 and 3 (b) 2 and 3 only (c) 1 only (d) 1 and 2 only
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is statement 3, which sounds plausible but is false — the pyramid of energy can never be inverted in any ecosystem because energy always decreases at higher trophic levels. Statements 1 and 2 are correct: one tree supports many insects (inverted number pyramid), and in ponds the biomass of phytoplankton at a given moment can be less than that of zooplankton (inverted biomass pyramid), so the answer is 1 and 2 only.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Biology

22. Arrange the following atmospheric layers from the Earth's surface upward:
1. Stratosphere 2. Troposphere 3. Mesosphere 4. Thermosphere
(a) 2, 3, 1, 4 (b) 1, 2, 3, 4 (c) 2, 1, 3, 4 (d) 1, 3, 2, 4
Answer: (c)
Solution: The chronological/order trap here is swapping troposphere and stratosphere; remember the troposphere is the lowest layer where we live and weather occurs. The correct order from the surface upward is troposphere, stratosphere, mesosphere, thermosphere, so the answer is 2, 1, 3, 4.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Geography

23. Consider the following pairs:
1. Montreal Protocol — Ozone depleting substances
2. Kyoto Protocol — Greenhouse gas emissions
3. Rio Summit 1992 — Convention on Biological Diversity
Which of the pairs given above is/are correctly matched?
(a) 1 only (b) 1, 2 and 3 (c) 2 and 3 only (d) 1 and 2 only
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is confusing Montreal with Kyoto — Montreal (1987) deals with ozone-depleting substances like CFCs, while Kyoto (1997) deals with greenhouse gases; both are correctly matched here. The Rio Earth Summit 1992 also produced the Convention on Biological Diversity, so all three pairs are correct and the answer is 1, 2 and 3.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Biology

24. In an ecosystem, the energy flow is:
(a) Bidirectional (b) Unidirectional (c) Cyclic (d) Random
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is "cyclic" — nutrients cycle in an ecosystem, so students often assume energy also cycles. Energy flows in one direction only, from the sun to producers to consumers, and is lost as heat at each step, so the answer is unidirectional.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Biology

25. Consider the following statements about the carbon cycle:
1. Carbon is fixed by producers through photosynthesis.
2. Decomposers return carbon to the atmosphere through respiration.
3. The ocean plays no role in the carbon cycle.
Which of the statements given above is/are correct?
(a) 1 and 2 only (b) 1 only (c) 2 and 3 only (d) 1, 2 and 3
Answer: (a)
Solution: Statement 3 is the trap — oceans are in fact a major carbon reservoir, absorbing large amounts of carbon dioxide, so the statement is false. Producers fix carbon by photosynthesis and decomposers release it back through respiration, so statements 1 and 2 are correct and the answer is 1 and 2 only.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Biology
