Questions 26–50: Medium

26. Which of the following is the primary source of energy for almost all ecosystems on Earth?
(a) Geothermal heat (b) Chemical energy from rocks (c) Sunlight (d) Wind energy
Answer: (c)
Solution: Geothermal and chemical energy support only a few specialised ecosystems like deep-sea vents, which tempts some students to pick them, but they are exceptions. Nearly all ecosystems run on solar energy fixed by producers through photosynthesis, so the answer is Sunlight.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Biology

27. In an ecosystem, the organisms that break down dead organic matter into simpler inorganic substances are called:
(a) Decomposers (b) Primary consumers (c) Producers (d) Secondary consumers
Answer: (a)
Solution: Primary consumers eat producers and secondary consumers eat primary consumers, so neither fits the description of breaking down dead matter. Decomposers like bacteria and fungi convert dead organic material into inorganic nutrients, so the answer is Decomposers.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Biology

28. According to the 10 percent law of energy transfer, if producers store 10,000 units of energy, the energy available to secondary consumers will be approximately:
(a) 1,000 units (b) 10 units (c) 100 units (d) 1 unit
Answer: (c)
Solution: Many students stop after one transfer and pick 1,000 units, but that is the energy at the primary consumer level only. Applying the 10 percent rule twice gives 10,000 → 1,000 → 100, so the answer is 100 units.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Biology

29. Which of the following is an example of a grazing food chain?
(a) Dead leaves → bacteria → fungi (b) Humus → microbes → protozoa (c) Detritus → earthworm → bird (d) Grass → deer → tiger
Answer: (d)
Solution: Options involving dead leaves, detritus and humus describe detritus food chains, which is the common trap here. A grazing food chain starts from living green plants, so the answer is Grass → deer → tiger.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Biology

30. The term 'ecosystem' was coined by:
(a) A.G. Tansley (b) Charles Darwin (c) Ernst Haeckel (d) E.P. Odum
Answer: (a)
Solution: Ernst Haeckel is a tempting option because he coined the term 'ecology', not 'ecosystem' — a classic similar-names trap. The term ecosystem was proposed by the British ecologist A.G. Tansley in 1935, so the answer is A.G. Tansley.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Biology

31. Which biome is characterised by the highest biodiversity on land?
(a) Temperate grassland (b) Tropical rainforest (c) Tundra (d) Desert
Answer: (b)
Solution: Deserts and tundra have harsh climates with low species diversity, and temperate grasslands are moderate. Warm temperatures and high rainfall year-round make tropical rainforests the richest terrestrial biome, so the answer is Tropical rainforest.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Geography

32. The ozone layer is mainly found in which layer of the atmosphere?
(a) Stratosphere (b) Troposphere (c) Mesosphere (d) Thermosphere
Answer: (a)
Solution: The troposphere is tempting because it is where weather occurs and where we live, but ozone there is a pollutant, not a protective layer. The protective ozone layer lies in the stratosphere, roughly 15–35 km above the surface, so the answer is Stratosphere.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Geography

33. Which gas is the largest contributor to the natural greenhouse effect?
(a) Carbon dioxide (b) Methane (c) Water vapour (d) Nitrous oxide
Answer: (c)
Solution: Carbon dioxide is the tempting answer because it dominates discussions of human-caused global warming, but the question asks about the natural greenhouse effect. Water vapour contributes the most to the natural greenhouse effect, so the answer is Water vapour.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Geography

34. The Montreal Protocol (1987) deals with:
(a) Protection of the ozone layer (b) Control of greenhouse gas emissions (c) Conservation of biodiversity (d) Control of hazardous waste movement
Answer: (a)
Solution: Students often confuse it with the Kyoto Protocol, which deals with greenhouse gases — a chronological and thematic confusion trap. The Montreal Protocol phases out ozone-depleting substances like CFCs, so the answer is Protection of the ozone layer.
Source: Official UNEP Ozone Secretariat

35. Which of the following is a non-renewable natural resource?
(a) Solar energy (b) Forests (c) Wind energy (d) Coal
Answer: (d)
Solution: Forests are tempting because they can be destroyed, but they regenerate naturally and are renewable if managed sustainably. Coal takes millions of years to form and cannot be replenished on a human timescale, so the answer is Coal.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science

36. In a food chain, the trophic level with the maximum amount of available energy is:
(a) Top carnivores (b) Producers (c) Herbivores (d) Decomposers
Answer: (b)
Solution: Top carnivores seem powerful and are a tempting choice, but energy decreases at each successive trophic level. Producers capture solar energy directly and hold the largest energy pool, so the answer is Producers.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Biology

37. Which of the following pollutants is mainly responsible for acid rain?
(a) Sulphur dioxide (b) Carbon monoxide (c) Methane (d) Ozone
Answer: (a)
Solution: Carbon monoxide is a tempting pick as a well-known toxic gas, but it does not form acids in the atmosphere. Sulphur dioxide and nitrogen oxides react with water to form sulphuric and nitric acids, so the answer is Sulphur dioxide.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Chemistry

38. The biological oxygen demand (BOD) of a water body increases when:
(a) The water is very clean (b) Dissolved oxygen increases (c) Organic waste pollution increases (d) Temperature of water falls sharply
Answer: (c)
Solution: High dissolved oxygen indicates clean water and is the reverse of what the question describes — a tempting inversion trap. More organic waste means microbes consume more oxygen to decompose it, raising BOD, so the answer is Organic waste pollution increases.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Biology

39. Which of the following is an example of ex-situ conservation?
(a) National park (b) Wildlife sanctuary (c) Botanical garden (d) Biosphere reserve
Answer: (c)
Solution: National parks, sanctuaries and biosphere reserves all protect species in their natural habitats, which is in-situ conservation — the trap is grouping all protected areas together. Botanical gardens conserve plants outside their natural habitat, so the answer is Botanical garden.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Biology

40. The Earth Summit (Rio Summit) of 1992 was held in which country?
(a) Sweden (b) South Africa (c) Brazil (d) Japan
Answer: (c)
Solution: Sweden is tempting because the first UN environment conference (Stockholm, 1972) was held there — a chronological confusion trap. The 1992 Earth Summit took place in Rio de Janeiro, so the answer is Brazil.
Source: Official UN website

41. Which of the following is the correct sequence of ecological succession on bare rock?
(a) Forest → shrubs → herbs → mosses → lichens (b) Lichens → mosses → herbs → shrubs → forest (c) Mosses → lichens → shrubs → herbs → forest (d) Herbs → lichens → mosses → forest → shrubs
Answer: (b)
Solution: The option starting with mosses is tempting, but mosses cannot colonise bare rock first; lichens are the pioneer species that break down rock into soil. Succession then proceeds through mosses, herbs and shrubs to a climax forest, so the answer is Lichens → mosses → herbs → shrubs → forest.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Biology

42. The Red Data Book is maintained by:
(a) IUCN (b) UNEP (c) WWF (d) UNESCO
Answer: (a)
Solution: WWF is tempting because of its famous panda logo and wildlife work, but it is a conservation NGO, not the compiler of species threat status. The IUCN publishes the Red Data Book/Red List of threatened species, so the answer is IUCN.
Source: Official IUCN website

43. Which of the following is a secondary pollutant?
(a) Sulphur dioxide (b) Carbon monoxide (c) Particulate matter (d) Ground-level ozone
Answer: (d)
Solution: Sulphur dioxide, carbon monoxide and particulate matter are all emitted directly from sources, making them primary pollutants — the trap is assuming all well-known pollutants are secondary. Ground-level ozone forms in the atmosphere by reactions of nitrogen oxides and hydrocarbons in sunlight, so the answer is Ground-level ozone.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Chemistry

44. The term 'biodiversity hotspot' requires a region to have:
(a) High species richness only (b) High rainfall only (c) Large area only (d) High endemism and significant habitat loss
Answer: (d)
Solution: High species richness alone is tempting but incomplete — it ignores the threat criterion that defines a hotspot. A hotspot must have high endemism (at least 1,500 endemic plant species) and have lost at least 70 percent of its original habitat, so the answer is High endemism and significant habitat loss.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Biology

45. Which of the following ecosystems has the highest net primary productivity per unit area?
(a) Open ocean (b) Tundra (c) Desert (d) Tropical rainforest
Answer: (d)
Solution: The open ocean is tempting because of its vast total productivity, but per unit area it is low due to nutrient limitation — a classic total-versus-per-area trap. Tropical rainforests lead in productivity per unit area, so the answer is Tropical rainforest.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Biology

46. Eutrophication of a lake is primarily caused by an excess of:
(a) Heavy metals (b) Nitrates and phosphates (c) Pesticides (d) Suspended solids
Answer: (b)
Solution: Pesticides and heavy metals are harmful pollutants and tempting choices, but they cause toxicity, not nutrient-driven algal blooms. Excess nitrates and phosphates from fertilisers and sewage trigger algal growth and oxygen depletion, so the answer is Nitrates and phosphates.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Biology

47. In India, Project Tiger was launched in the year:
(a) 1973 (b) 1972 (c) 1975 (d) 1980
Answer: (a)
Solution: 1972 is tempting because the Wildlife Protection Act was passed that year — a chronological confusion trap between the Act and the project. Project Tiger was launched on 1 April 1973, so the answer is 1973.
Source: Official National Tiger Conservation Authority website

48. Which of the following statements about decomposers is correct?
(a) They occupy the first trophic level (b) They release nutrients back into the soil for reuse by producers (c) They convert inorganic matter into organic matter (d) They are absent in aquatic ecosystems
Answer: (b)
Solution: The statement about converting inorganic matter into organic matter is almost correct-sounding but describes producers, not decomposers — a nearly-correct statement trap. Decomposers mineralise dead matter and return nutrients to the soil, so the answer is They release nutrients back into the soil for reuse by producers.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Biology

49. The Chipko Movement (1973) is associated with the conservation of:
(a) Tigers (b) Wetlands (c) Rivers (d) Forests
Answer: (d)
Solution: The movement is sometimes loosely linked with general environmental causes, which tempts students toward rivers or wetlands. Villagers in the Garhwal Himalaya hugged trees to prevent felling, so the answer is Forests.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Biology

50. Which of the following pairs is correctly matched?
(a) Kyoto Protocol — Ozone depletion (b) Ramsar Convention — Wetlands (c) CITES — Climate change (d) Basel Convention — Biodiversity
Answer: (b)
Solution: The Kyoto Protocol is tempting but it addresses climate change, not ozone — a treaty-mixing trap; similarly CITES covers trade in endangered species and Basel covers hazardous waste. The Ramsar Convention (1971) is the treaty on wetlands of international importance, so the answer is Ramsar Convention — Wetlands.
Source: Official Ramsar Convention website
