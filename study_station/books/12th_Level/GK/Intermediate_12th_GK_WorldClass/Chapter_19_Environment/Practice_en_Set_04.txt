Questions 76–100: Medium

76. Which of the following is the primary source of energy in almost all ecosystems?
(a) Sunlight (b) Wind (c) Geothermal heat (d) Chemical bonds in soil
Answer: (a)
Solution: Wind and geothermal heat drive some processes but do not power food chains. Nearly all ecosystems begin with producers converting sunlight into chemical energy through photosynthesis, so the answer is Sunlight.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Biology

77. In an ecosystem, decomposers mainly include:
(a) Lions and tigers (b) Green algae and mosses (c) Bacteria and fungi (d) Grasshoppers and deer
Answer: (c)
Solution: Green algae and mosses are producers, and grasshoppers and deer are consumers — the tempting trap is to treat all small organisms as decomposers. Decomposers break down dead organic matter, a role performed chiefly by bacteria and fungi, so the answer is Bacteria and fungi.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Biology

78. The 10 percent law of energy transfer in a food chain was given by:
(a) Charles Darwin (b) E. P. Odum (c) Lindeman (d) Rachel Carson
Answer: (c)
Solution: E. P. Odum is a famous ecologist but is known for his textbook and ecosystem concept, not this law — a classic similar-names trap. The 10 percent law of energy transfer between trophic levels was proposed by Lindeman, so the answer is Lindeman.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Biology

79. Which of the following is a secondary consumer in a grassland food chain: grass → grasshopper → frog → snake?
(a) Frog (b) Grasshopper (c) Snake (d) Grass
Answer: (a)
Solution: The snake looks like the "second" meat-eater and tempts many, but counting trophic levels shows grass is the producer, grasshopper the primary consumer, and frog the secondary consumer. The snake is a tertiary consumer, so the answer is Frog.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Biology

80. An ecological pyramid of energy is:
(a) Spindle-shaped (b) Always inverted (c) Sometimes upright, sometimes inverted (d) Always upright
Answer: (d)
Solution: Pyramids of number and biomass can be inverted (e.g., a tree supporting many insects), which tempts students to extend this to energy. Since energy always decreases at higher trophic levels, the energy pyramid is always upright, so the answer is Always upright.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Biology

81. The term 'ecosystem' was coined by:
(a) Ernst Haeckel (b) A. G. Tansley (c) Charles Elton (d) E. P. Odum
Answer: (b)
Solution: Ernst Haeckel coined the word 'ecology', which is the near-identical trap here. The term 'ecosystem' was coined by the British ecologist A. G. Tansley in 1935, so the answer is A. G. Tansley.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Biology

82. Which gas is the largest contributor to the natural greenhouse effect?
(a) Carbon dioxide (b) Water vapour (c) Methane (d) Nitrous oxide
Answer: (b)
Solution: Carbon dioxide dominates exam discussions of global warming, which makes it the tempting wrong choice. In the natural greenhouse effect, water vapour contributes the most among greenhouse gases, so the answer is Water vapour.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Chemistry (Environmental Chemistry)

83. The Montreal Protocol (1987) deals with:
(a) Reduction of greenhouse gases (b) Control of hazardous waste movement (c) Conservation of biodiversity (d) Protection of the ozone layer
Answer: (d)
Solution: The Kyoto Protocol deals with greenhouse gases and the Basel Convention with hazardous waste — a frequent treaty-confusion trap. The Montreal Protocol phases out ozone-depleting substances like CFCs, so the answer is Protection of the ozone layer.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Chemistry (Environmental Chemistry)

84. The Kyoto Protocol is associated with:
(a) Ozone depletion (b) Climate change and greenhouse gas reduction (c) Wetland conservation (d) Trade in endangered species
Answer: (b)
Solution: Wetlands fall under the Ramsar Convention and endangered species trade under CITES, both common confusions. The Kyoto Protocol (1997) commits parties to reduce greenhouse gas emissions, so the answer is Climate change and greenhouse gas reduction.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Chemistry (Environmental Chemistry)

85. Which of the following is a biodegradable pollutant?
(a) DDT (b) Sewage (c) Plastic bags (d) Mercury compounds
Answer: (b)
Solution: DDT, plastics and mercury persist in the environment and biomagnify, so they are non-biodegradable despite being common pollutants. Sewage is broken down by microorganisms, so the answer is Sewage.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Biology

86. Biomagnification refers to:
(a) Increase in population of a species (b) Increase in biodiversity in an ecosystem (c) Increase in body size up the food chain (d) Increase in concentration of a toxic substance at successive trophic levels
Answer: (d)
Solution: The word 'magnification' tempts students toward body size or population growth. Biomagnification is the rising concentration of non-degradable toxins like DDT at higher trophic levels, so the answer is Increase in concentration of a toxic substance at successive trophic levels.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Biology

87. The Ramsar Convention is related to the conservation of:
(a) Mangroves (b) Coral reefs (c) Wetlands (d) Deserts
Answer: (c)
Solution: Mangroves and coral reefs are wetland-adjacent ecosystems, which creates the trap. The Ramsar Convention (1971, Iran) is the international treaty for the conservation and wise use of wetlands, so the answer is Wetlands.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Biology

88. Which of the following is an example of ex-situ conservation?
(a) National park (b) Wildlife sanctuary (c) Biosphere reserve (d) Botanical garden
Answer: (d)
Solution: National parks, sanctuaries and biosphere reserves all protect species in their natural habitat, i.e., in-situ. A botanical garden conserves plants outside their natural habitat, so the answer is Botanical garden.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Biology

89. The 'Red Data Book' is maintained by:
(a) UNEP (b) UNESCO (c) WWF (d) IUCN
Answer: (d)
Solution: WWF (panda logo) is strongly associated with endangered species in popular memory, making it the tempting wrong option. The Red Data Book listing threatened species is published by the IUCN, so the answer is IUCN.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Biology

90. Which of the following statements about the ozone layer is correct?
(a) It is found in the troposphere and absorbs infrared radiation (b) It is found in the mesosphere and absorbs visible light (c) It is found in the stratosphere and absorbs ultraviolet radiation (d) It is found in the stratosphere and absorbs infrared radiation
Answer: (c)
Solution: The almost-correct trap is the option pairing the right layer (stratosphere) with the wrong radiation (infrared). The ozone layer lies in the stratosphere and absorbs harmful ultraviolet radiation, so the answer is the statement placing it in the stratosphere absorbing ultraviolet radiation.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Chemistry (Environmental Chemistry)

91. BOD (Biochemical Oxygen Demand) of a water body increases when:
(a) Dissolved oxygen increases (b) Water temperature decreases sharply (c) Organic waste pollution increases (d) Fish population increases
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is to link high BOD with high dissolved oxygen — in fact the relationship is inverse. More organic waste means microbes consume more oxygen to decompose it, raising BOD, so the answer is Organic waste pollution increases.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Biology

92. Eutrophication of a lake is primarily caused by an excess of:
(a) Sodium and potassium (b) Calcium and magnesium (c) Nitrates and phosphates (d) Chlorides and sulphates
Answer: (c)
Solution: Calcium and magnesium cause water hardness, a tempting confusion. Nutrient enrichment by nitrates and phosphates (from fertilisers and detergents) triggers algal blooms and eutrophication, so the answer is Nitrates and phosphates.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Biology

93. Which of the following is a non-renewable natural resource?
(a) Solar energy (b) Forests (c) Wind energy (d) Coal
Answer: (d)
Solution: Forests are a trap — though destructible, they regenerate and are classed as renewable. Coal forms over millions of years and cannot be replenished on a human timescale, so the answer is Coal.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science (Management of Natural Resources)

94. The Chipko Movement (1973) is associated with:
(a) Sunderlal Bahuguna (b) Medha Patkar (c) Anil Agarwal (d) M. S. Swaminathan
Answer: (a)
Solution: Medha Patkar leads the Narmada Bachao Andolan and M. S. Swaminathan is linked to the Green Revolution — both similar-name traps. The Chipko Movement in Uttarakhand (then UP) is associated with Sunderlal Bahuguna, so the answer is Sunderlal Bahuguna.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science (Management of Natural Resources)

95. Which of the following is the correct sequence of ecological succession on bare rock?
(a) Mosses → lichens → shrubs → herbs → forest (b) Lichens → mosses → herbs → shrubs → forest (c) Herbs → lichens → mosses → shrubs → forest (d) Shrubs → herbs → mosses → lichens → forest
Answer: (b)
Solution: The chronological-confusion trap places mosses before lichens, but mosses cannot colonise bare rock first. Pioneer lichens weather the rock, followed by mosses, herbs, shrubs and finally a climax forest, so the answer is Lichens → mosses → herbs → shrubs → forest.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Biology

96. Project Tiger was launched in India in:
(a) 1973 (b) 1972 (c) 1975 (d) 1985
Answer: (a)
Solution: The Wildlife Protection Act was passed in 1972, which is the classic year-confusion trap. Project Tiger was launched on 1 April 1973, so the answer is 1973.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Biology / Government of India records

97. Which of the following is a greenhouse gas that is also used as a refrigerant and was phased out under the Montreal Protocol?
(a) Carbon monoxide (b) Chlorofluorocarbons (c) Sulphur dioxide (d) Ammonia
Answer: (b)
Solution: Sulphur dioxide causes acid rain and carbon monoxide is a toxic pollutant, but neither is a refrigerant. CFCs were widely used as refrigerants, damage ozone and act as greenhouse gases, so the answer is Chlorofluorocarbons.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Chemistry (Environmental Chemistry)

98. In a food chain, the maximum concentration of DDT would be found in:
(a) Fish-eating birds (b) Zooplankton (c) Small fish (d) Phytoplankton
Answer: (a)
Solution: Phytoplankton start the chain and tempt students who think the source holds most toxin. Due to biomagnification, the top consumer accumulates the highest DDT concentration, so the answer is Fish-eating birds.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Biology

99. Which of the following pairs is correctly matched?
(a) Basel Convention — Hazardous wastes (b) CITES — Ozone layer (c) Ramsar — Climate change (d) Stockholm Convention — Wetlands
Answer: (a)
Solution: Each wrong option swaps treaty subjects — CITES covers endangered species trade, Ramsar covers wetlands, and the Stockholm Convention covers persistent organic pollutants. Only the Basel Convention correctly matches hazardous wastes, so the answer is Basel Convention — Hazardous wastes.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Biology / UNEP

100. The Environment (Protection) Act of India was enacted in:
(a) 1986 (b) 1980 (c) 1974 (d) 1992
Answer: (a)
Solution: The Water Act (1974) is the tempting near-date trap. The umbrella Environment (Protection) Act was enacted in 1986, in the wake of the Bhopal gas tragedy, so the answer is 1986.
Source: Ministry of Environment, Forest and Climate Change, Government of India
