# Environment & Ecology (Basic)

## 🌍 The Hook: A Forest That Became a Movement

In 1730, in a small village called Khejarli in Rajasthan, a woman named **Amrita Devi Bishnoi** hugged a tree and refused to let the king's men cut it. She and 362 others gave their lives to protect the Khejri trees. Nearly 250 years later, in 1974, women in Uttarakhand repeated the same act — and the world came to know it as the **Chipko Movement**.

Why start here? Because environment questions in SSC, Railway and Banking exams are not just about definitions — they are about **who, what, where and when**. And this chapter gives you all four, in the simplest possible way.

## 1. What is Environment? What is Ecology?

**Environment** = everything around us — living (biotic) + non-living (abiotic). The word comes from the French *environner*, meaning "to surround."

**Ecology** = the scientific study of the **relationship** between organisms and their environment. The term was coined by German biologist **Ernst Haeckel in 1866** (from Greek *oikos* = house, *logos* = study).

> 🧠 **Active Recall:** Who coined the term "ecology" and in which year? What does *oikos* mean?

**Levels of organisation (small → big):**
Organism → Population → Community → Ecosystem → Biome → **Biosphere**

🎯 **Mnemonic 1 — "Old People Can Eat Big Burgers"**
**O**rganism → **P**opulation → **C**ommunity → **E**cosystem → **B**iome → **B**iosphere

## 2. The Ecosystem: Nature's Working Unit

An **ecosystem** is a self-sustaining unit where living things interact with non-living things. The term was coined by **A.G. Tansley in 1935**.

**Two components:**
- **Abiotic (non-living):** sunlight, water, air, soil, temperature.
- **Biotic (living):**
  - **Producers** — green plants (make food by photosynthesis).
  - **Consumers** — herbivores (primary), carnivores (secondary/tertiary).
  - **Decomposers** — bacteria and fungi (break down dead matter; called "nature's scavengers").

**Types of ecosystems:**
- **Natural:** forest, grassland, desert, pond, lake, ocean.
- **Artificial (man-made):** crop field, aquarium, garden.

> 🧠 **Active Recall:** Who coined "ecosystem"? Name the three biotic components. Is an aquarium a natural ecosystem?

## 3. Food Chain, Food Web & the 10% Law

**Food chain** = transfer of energy from one organism to another in a straight line.
Grass → Grasshopper → Frog → Snake → Eagle

Each step is a **trophic level**. Producers = 1st trophic level.

**The 10% Law (Lindeman, 1942):** Only about **10% of energy** passes from one trophic level to the next; 90% is lost as heat. This is why food chains rarely have more than 4–5 levels.

**Food web** = many interconnected food chains. It is more stable than a single chain.

**Ecological pyramids:**
- Pyramid of **numbers** — can be upright or inverted (e.g., one tree → many insects).
- Pyramid of **biomass** — upright on land, **inverted in oceans/ponds**.
- Pyramid of **energy** — **always upright** (never inverted). ⭐ Favourite exam fact!

🎯 **Mnemonic 2 — "Energy Never Lies Down"**
Energy pyramid is **always upright**; biomass can lie inverted in water.

> 🧠 **Active Recall:** Which ecological pyramid is never inverted? Who gave the 10% law?

## 4. Biomes and the Biosphere

A **biome** is a large region with a distinct climate and characteristic plants and animals.

**Major world biomes:** Tundra, Taiga (coniferous forest), Temperate forest, Tropical rainforest, Grassland (Savanna, Prairies, Steppes), Desert.

**Quick grassland memory:**
- **Savanna** — Africa | **Prairies** — North America | **Steppes** — Eurasia | **Pampas** — South America (Argentina) | **Velds** — South Africa | **Downs** — Australia

🎯 **Mnemonic 3 — "SaPa StePa VeDo"**
**Sa**vanna-Africa, **Pa**mpas-Argentina, **Ste**ppes-Eurasia, **Pa**iries-N.America, **Ve**lds-S.Africa, **Do**wns-Australia.

**Biosphere** = the narrow zone where land (lithosphere), water (hydrosphere) and air (atmosphere) meet and life exists.

- **Stockholm Conference, 1972** — first UN conference on environment; UNEP born.
- **Rio Earth Summit, 1992** — Agenda 21, biodiversity & climate conventions.
- **Kyoto Protocol, 1997** — emission cuts (came into force 2005).
- **Paris Agreement, 2015** — limit warming well below 2°C, pursue 1.5°C.

## 6. Pollution Basics

- **Air pollution:** CO (from incomplete combustion), SO₂ and NOx (cause **acid rain**), CFCs (damage the **ozone layer**).
- **Water pollution:** excess nutrients (nitrates, phosphates) cause **eutrophication** — algal bloom → oxygen depletion → fish die.
- **Biomagnification:** concentration of toxins (like DDT, mercury) increases at higher trophic levels. Minamata disease (Japan) = mercury poisoning.
- **Greenhouse gases:** CO₂, methane (CH₄), nitrous oxide, water vapour, CFCs. Methane is a more potent greenhouse gas than CO₂ per molecule.

> 🧠 **Active Recall:** What is eutrophication? Which disease is linked to mercury? Name two gases causing acid rain.

## ⚠️ Caution: Examiner's Trap

**Trap 1 — Chronological Confusion (कालानुक्रमिक भ्रम):**
"Chipko Movement started in 1983." ❌ — 1983 is **Appiko** (Karnataka); Chipko is **1973–74**. Examiners love swapping these twin movements and their years.

**Trap 2 — Nearly-True Statement (लगभग सही कथन):**
"The pyramid of biomass is always upright." ❌ — Sounds 90% true, but biomass pyramid is **inverted in aquatic ecosystems**. Only the **energy** pyramid is always upright.

**Trap 3 — Similar Names (समान नाम):**
"Ecosystem" was coined by **A.G. Tansley (1935)**, but "Ecology" by **Ernst Haeckel (1866)**. Options often swap the two scientists — read the term in the question twice.
