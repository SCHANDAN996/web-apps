# 📊 PYQ Analysis — Environment & Ecology (Basic)

## 1. How Exams Ask Questions from This Chapter

Environment & Ecology is a **consistently recurring** area across SSC (CGL, CHSL, MTS, GD), RRB (NTPC, Group D), Banking (IBPS, SBI) and State PCS prelims. The questions are almost always **direct, fact-based and NCERT-level** — the difficulty comes not from depth but from the examiner's traps: look-alike terms, reversed definitions and negative phrasing.

**Pattern in words (no invented counts):**

- **Most frequently asked sub-topics:** National Parks & Wildlife Sanctuaries (location/state matching), Biosphere Reserves, major environmental conventions and their years (Rio 1992, Kyoto 1997, Montreal 1987, Paris 2015), pollutants and their effects (CO, SO₂, CFCs, BOD), and basic ecology terms (food chain, trophic levels, 10% law, biome).
- **Moderately asked:** Environmental movements (Chipko, Silent Valley), important days (World Environment Day — 5 June), acts (Wildlife Protection Act 1972, Environment Protection Act 1986), and organisations (IUCN, UNEP, CITES).
- **Occasionally asked:** Deeper concepts like ecological succession, ecotone, edge effect, and biogeochemical cycles — these appear more in CGL/PCS than in GD/MTS.

**Exam-wise flavour:**

- **SSC CGL / CHSL:** Statement-based and match-the-following questions; loves conventions, years and "which is NOT correct" formats.
- **SSC MTS / GD, RRB Group D:** One-liner facts — "Where is X National Park?", "Which gas causes ozone depletion?"
- **Banking (IBPS/SBI):** Environment appears in GA sections mostly through days, reports, and convention names.
- **State PCS:** Adds state-specific national parks, state animals/birds, and local environmental movements.

## 2. The Examiner's Traps (जाल) — With Examples

### Trap 1: Almost Correct Statement
One word is changed in an otherwise true statement.
> "The Montreal Protocol deals with the protection of the ozone layer by phasing out **CO₂**." ❌
> (Correct: CFCs/ODS, not CO₂. CO₂ belongs to climate conventions.)

**Defence:** After reading a statement, re-check the *one technical word* — gas name, year, or place.

### Trap 2: Chronological Confusion
Options offer years that are close together: 1987 / 1992 / 1997 / 2015.
> Montreal Protocol — 1987; Rio Earth Summit — 1992; Kyoto Protocol — 1997; Paris Agreement — 2015.

**Defence:** Memorise as a chain: **"Montreal → Rio → Kyoto → Paris"** (87 → 92 → 97 → 15).

### Trap 3: Negative Phrasing
"Which of the following is **NOT** a greenhouse gas?" — students misread and pick a GHG.
> Answer: Oxygen (O₂). CO₂, CH₄, N₂O and water vapour are GHGs.

**Defence:** Underline NOT/EXCEPT in the question before looking at options.

### Trap 4: Look-Alike Names
- **National Park vs Wildlife Sanctuary vs Biosphere Reserve** — human activities are most restricted in National Parks; Biosphere Reserves are the largest units with three zones (core, buffer, transition).
- **Jim Corbett (Uttarakhand, 1936 — first NP of India)** vs **Hemis (Ladakh — largest NP)** — examiners swap "first" and "largest".

### Trap 5: Reversed Definition
> "BOD is the amount of oxygen **released** by bacteria while decomposing organic matter." ❌
> (BOD = oxygen **demanded/consumed**; higher BOD = more polluted water.)

## 3. Top 10 High-Yield Questions (with Solutions)

**Q1. Which was the first National Park established in India?**
(a) Kanha (b) Jim Corbett (c) Kaziranga (d) Gir
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** Jim Corbett National Park (Uttarakhand) was established in 1936 as Hailey National Park. Trap: Kaziranga and Gir are more famous for rhino/lion, but "first" belongs to Corbett.
**Source:** NCERT Class 8 Geography (Resources and Development)

**Q2. The Montreal Protocol (1987) is related to:**
(a) Climate change (b) Ozone layer protection (c) Biodiversity conservation (d) Hazardous waste
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** Montreal Protocol phases out ozone-depleting substances (CFCs, halons). Climate change = Kyoto/Paris; biodiversity = CBD (Rio 1992); hazardous waste = Basel Convention. Classic convention-swap trap.
**Source:** NCERT Class 11 Chemistry (Environmental Chemistry)

**Q3. Which of the following is NOT a greenhouse gas?**
(a) Carbon dioxide (b) Methane (c) Nitrous oxide (d) Oxygen
**Answer: (d)**
**Solution:** O₂ does not absorb infrared radiation. Negative-phrasing trap — read the question twice.
**Source:** NCERT Class 11 Chemistry

**Q4. The '10% law' of energy transfer in a food chain was given by:**
(a) Charles Elton (b) Lindeman (c) Odum (d) Tansley
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** Raymond Lindeman (1942) stated that only about 10% of energy passes to the next trophic level. Tansley coined "ecosystem"; Elton gave the ecological pyramid — a classic name-swap trap.
**Source:** NCERT Class 12 Biology (Ecosystem)

**Q5. Chipko Movement is associated with:**
(a) Medha Patkar (b) Sunderlal Bahuguna (c) Baba Amte (d) Vandana Shiva only
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** Chipko (1973, Garhwal, Uttarakhand) is linked with Sunderlal Bahuguna and Chandi Prasad Bhatt; Gaura Devi led the famous Reni incident. Medha Patkar = Narmada Bachao — movement-leader swap trap.
**Source:** NCERT Class 10 Geography / PYQ-style

**Q6. World Environment Day is celebrated on:**
(a) 22 April (b) 5 June (c) 16 September (d) 2 February
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** 5 June (since 1974, led by UNEP). Trap dates: 22 April = Earth Day; 16 September = Ozone Day; 2 February = World Wetlands Day.
**Source:** UNEP official / PYQ-style

**Q7. High BOD in a water body indicates:**
(a) Clean water (b) High organic pollution (c) High dissolved oxygen (d) Low bacterial activity
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** Biochemical Oxygen Demand rises when organic waste is high; bacteria consume dissolved oxygen, so DO falls. Options (a) and (c) are reversed-definition traps.
**Source:** NCERT Class 12 Biology (Environmental Issues)

**Q8. The Environment (Protection) Act was passed in India in:**
(a) 1972 (b) 1980 (c) 1986 (d) 1992
**Answer: (c)**
**Solution:** Passed in 1986 after the Bhopal gas tragedy (1984), under Article 253. Trap: 1972 is the Wildlife Protection Act and the Stockholm Conference year.
**Source:** India Code / PYQ-style

**Q9. Which is the largest Biosphere Reserve of India?**
(a) Nilgiri (b) Gulf of Mannar (c) Sundarbans (d) Nanda Devi
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** Gulf of Mannar (Tamil Nadu) is the largest; Nilgiri was the **first** (1986). "First vs largest" swap is the standard trap here.
**Source:** Ministry of Environment, Forest and Climate Change / PYQ-style

**Q10. CITES is related to:**
(a) Ozone depletion (b) International trade in endangered species (c) Wetland conservation (d) Climate finance
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** CITES (1973, Washington) regulates trade in wild flora and fauna. Wetlands = Ramsar Convention (1971) — the usual confusion pair.
**Source:** CITES official / PYQ-style

## 4. The 15/45-Second Rule Applied

**Rule:** If a question is a pure recall fact you have revised, answer within **15 seconds**. If it needs elimination or statement-checking, give it up to **45 seconds** — then mark and move on.

**Applied to Q6 (World Environment Day):** Pure date recall. If you know the day-pairs (5 June / 22 April / 16 September), this is a **15-second** question. Do not re-read the options — second-guessing wastes time and invites errors.

**Applied to Q3 (NOT a greenhouse gas):** Negative phrasing demands care. Spend up to **45 seconds**: underline "NOT", verify each option (CO₂ ✓ GHG, CH₄ ✓ GHG, N₂O ✓ GHG, O₂ ✗), then answer. Never rush negative questions into the 15-second bucket.

**Applied to Q9 (largest Biosphere Reserve):** If "first vs largest" is not crystal clear in memory, do not gamble in 15 seconds — take 45 seconds, recall "Nilgiri = first (1986), Gulf of Mannar = largest", and only then mark. If still unsure after 45 seconds, skip rather than guess (negative marking).

---

## 5. Revision Takeaways from PYQ Patterns

1. Master the **convention-year chain**: Ramsar 1971 → Stockholm 1972 → Montreal 1987 → Rio 1992 → Kyoto 1997 → Paris 2015.
2. Master the **"first vs largest" pairs**: Corbett (first NP) vs Hemis (largest NP); Nilgiri (first BR) vs Gulf of Mannar (largest BR).
3. Master the **day-date list**: 2 Feb, 22 April, 5 June, 16 September, 2 October (Swachh Bharat), 11 July (World Population Day).
4. For every statement question, check the **one technical word** — gas, year, place, or person.
5. Treat NOT/EXCEPT questions as 45-second questions, always.
