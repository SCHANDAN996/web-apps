126. Which of the following statements about the ozone layer is correct?
(a) It is found in the troposphere and absorbs infrared radiation (b) It is found in the mesosphere and reflects radio waves (c) It is found in the stratosphere and absorbs ultraviolet radiation (d) It is found in the thermosphere and absorbs X-rays
Answer: (c)
Solution: The tempting wrong option places ozone in the troposphere — ozone does exist there, but as a pollutant (bad ozone), not as the protective layer. The protective ozone layer lies in the stratosphere (roughly 15–35 km) and absorbs harmful UV-B and UV-C radiation, so the answer is the stratosphere–ultraviolet combination.

127. In an ecosystem, the 10% law of energy transfer was given by:
(a) Raymond Lindeman (b) E.P. Odum (c) Charles Elton (d) A.G. Tansley
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap here is similar names of ecologists: Tansley coined the term "ecosystem" and Elton gave the concept of ecological pyramids, so both are tempting. The 10% law — only about 10% of energy passes to the next trophic level — was proposed by Raymond Lindeman (1942), so the answer is Raymond Lindeman.

128. Which of the following is a correct sequence of ecological succession on bare rock (lithosere)?
(a) Lichens → Mosses → Herbs → Shrubs → Forest (b) Mosses → Lichens → Shrubs → Herbs → Forest (c) Herbs → Lichens → Mosses → Shrubs → Forest (d) Lichens → Herbs → Mosses → Forest → Shrubs
Answer: (a)
Solution: This is a chronological-confusion trap: mosses and herbs are often swapped in wrong options. On bare rock, pioneer species are crustose lichens, which weather the rock; mosses follow, then herbs, shrubs, and finally a climax forest, so the answer is Lichens → Mosses → Herbs → Shrubs → Forest.

129. The term "biodiversity hotspot" requires which two criteria to be met?
(a) Presence of 10 national parks and 5% endemism (b) At least 500 endemic plants and 50% habitat loss (c) At least 2,000 endemic animals and 30% habitat loss (d) At least 1,500 endemic vascular plants and 70% loss of original habitat
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is the almost-correct figures in the other options. Norman Myers' hotspot criteria are: at least 1,500 species of endemic vascular plants (0.5% of the world's total) and loss of at least 70% of the original primary vegetation, so the answer is 1,500 endemic plants with 70% habitat loss.

131. Biomagnification refers to:
(a) Increase in concentration of non-biodegradable toxins at successive trophic levels (b) Increase in population size at higher trophic levels (c) Increase in biomass of producers in a food chain (d) Increase in body size of organisms along a food chain

Solution: The tempting wrong options confuse biomagnification with biomass or body size. 132. Which of the following is the correct order of increasing biological oxygen demand (BOD) implication?
(a) Higher BOD → cleaner water (b) BOD is unrelated to pollution (c) Higher BOD → more polluted water (d) Lower BOD → more sewage in water
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is the reversed logic in the first and last options. BOD measures the oxygen microbes consume to decompose organic matter; more organic pollution means more microbial activity and hence higher BOD, so higher BOD indicates more polluted water.

135. In a food chain, the maximum energy is available at the level of:
(a) Primary consumers (b) Producers (c) Secondary consumers (d) Decomposers
Answer: (b)
Solution: The tempting wrong option is decomposers, since they act on all trophic levels — but they receive energy only after losses at each step. Energy decreases at every transfer (about 90% lost), so the maximum energy is with the producers, the first trophic level.

136. Which gas is primarily responsible for the formation of acid rain?
(a) Carbon monoxide (b) Methane (c) Sulphur dioxide (d) Ozone
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is carbon monoxide, a well-known pollutant, but it does not form acids in water. Sulphur dioxide (and nitrogen oxides) oxidise and react with water vapour to form sulphuric and nitric acids, causing acid rain (pH below 5.6), so the answer is sulphur dioxide.

137. The pyramid of energy in any ecosystem is:
(a) Always inverted (b) Spindle-shaped (c) Sometimes inverted (d) Always upright
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is confusing the energy pyramid with pyramids of number or biomass, which can be inverted (e.g., in a tree ecosystem or pond). Because energy is always lost at each transfer, the pyramid of energy can never be inverted — it is always upright, so the answer is always upright.

138. Which of the following statements about CFCs is correct?
(a) They are highly reactive and break down quickly in the lower atmosphere (b) They are stable in the lower atmosphere but release chlorine atoms in the stratosphere that destroy ozone (c) They directly absorb ultraviolet radiation (d) They are produced only by natural volcanic activity
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is the first option's claim of high reactivity — in fact CFCs are dangerously stable, which lets them survive to the stratosphere. There, UV light breaks them to release chlorine atoms, each of which can destroy thousands of ozone molecules, so the answer is stability below and chlorine release in the stratosphere.

139. Eutrophication of a water body leads to:
(a) Increase in dissolved oxygen (b) Depletion of dissolved oxygen due to algal bloom decay (c) Decrease in algal growth (d) Increase in fish population
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is assuming more nutrients and algae mean more oxygen and fish. Excess nutrients (nitrates, phosphates) trigger algal blooms; when the algae die, decomposing bacteria consume the dissolved oxygen, killing aquatic life, so the answer is depletion of dissolved oxygen due to algal bloom decay.

140. Which of the following is a secondary pollutant?
(a) Sulphur dioxide (b) Carbon monoxide (c) Particulate matter (d) PAN (peroxyacetyl nitrate)
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is that all four are air pollutants, but three are primary pollutants emitted directly from sources. Secondary pollutants form in the atmosphere by reactions among primary pollutants; PAN, a component of photochemical smog, forms from nitrogen oxides and hydrocarbons in sunlight, so the answer is PAN.

147. Which of the following statements about ecological pyramids is correct?
(a) The pyramid of biomass in a pond ecosystem can be inverted (b) The pyramid of numbers in a parasitic food chain is upright (c) The pyramid of biomass in a pond ecosystem is always upright (d) The pyramid of energy in a forest ecosystem is inverted
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is the word "always": in a pond, the biomass of small, fast-reproducing phytoplankton at any moment can be less than that of the zooplankton and fish they support, inverting the biomass pyramid. Energy pyramids are never inverted, so the answer is that the pond biomass pyramid can be inverted.

148. Photochemical smog is characterised by the presence of:
(a) Ozone, PAN and nitrogen oxides (b) Sulphur dioxide and smoke (c) Carbon dioxide and water vapour (d) Methane and ammonia
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is the first option, which describes classical (London-type) smog made of smoke and sulphur dioxide. Photochemical (Los Angeles-type) smog forms in sunlight from vehicle-emitted nitrogen oxides and hydrocarbons, producing ozone and PAN, so the answer is ozone, PAN and nitrogen oxides.

149. Which of the following is the correct decreasing order of contribution to global warming among greenhouse gases (by relative share)?
