101. Which of the following is the primary greenhouse gas responsible for the largest share of human-caused global warming?
(a) Methane (b) Ozone (c) Nitrous oxide (d) Carbon dioxide
Answer: (d)
Solution: Methane is more potent per molecule, which tempts many students, but carbon dioxide is emitted in the largest quantities and stays in the atmosphere for centuries, contributing the biggest share of human-caused warming, so the answer is Carbon dioxide.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Chemistry (Environmental Chemistry)

102. The term "ecosystem" was coined by:
(a) Charles Darwin (b) A.G. Tansley (c) E.P. Odum (d) Ernst Haeckel
Answer: (b)
Solution: Ernst Haeckel coined "ecology", not "ecosystem" — a classic similar-names trap. Arthur George Tansley coined the term "ecosystem" in 1935, so the answer is A.G. Tansley.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Biology (Ecosystem)

103. In a food chain, the maximum energy is available at the level of:
(a) Top carnivores (b) Producers (c) Primary consumers (d) Decomposers
Answer: (b)
Solution: Only about 10% of energy transfers to each next trophic level, so energy decreases as we move up; producers (green plants) hold the maximum energy, so the answer is Producers.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Biology (Ecosystem)

104. The 10% law of energy transfer in a food chain was given by:
(a) Tansley (b) Lindeman (c) Odum (d) Elton
Answer: (b)
Solution: Elton is associated with the ecological pyramid concept, which confuses students, but the 10% energy transfer law was proposed by Raymond Lindeman in 1942, so the answer is Lindeman.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Biology (Ecosystem)

105. Which of the following is a biodegradable pollutant?
(a) DDT (b) Plastic bags (c) Sewage (d) Mercury compounds
Answer: (c)
Solution: DDT, plastics and mercury compounds persist in the environment for long periods and are non-biodegradable; sewage is broken down by microorganisms, so the answer is Sewage.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Biology (Environmental Issues)

106. The ozone layer is found mainly in which layer of the atmosphere?
(a) Troposphere (b) Thermosphere (c) Mesosphere (d) Stratosphere
Answer: (d)
Solution: Weather phenomena occur in the troposphere, which tempts some students, but the ozone layer that absorbs harmful UV radiation lies mainly in the stratosphere, so the answer is Stratosphere.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Chemistry (Environmental Chemistry)

107. The Montreal Protocol (1987) is related to:
(a) Protection of the ozone layer (b) Control of greenhouse gases (c) Conservation of biodiversity (d) Control of hazardous waste
Answer: (a)
Solution: The Kyoto Protocol deals with greenhouse gases — a frequent confusion — but the Montreal Protocol aims at phasing out ozone-depleting substances like CFCs, so the answer is Protection of the ozone layer.

108. Which gas is mainly responsible for the depletion of the ozone layer?
(a) Chlorofluorocarbons (b) Carbon monoxide (c) Sulphur dioxide (d) Ammonia
Answer: (a)
Solution: Sulphur dioxide causes acid rain, not ozone depletion — a tempting mix-up. Chlorofluorocarbons release chlorine atoms in the stratosphere that destroy ozone, so the answer is Chlorofluorocarbons.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Chemistry (Environmental Chemistry)

109. The Kyoto Protocol was adopted in which year?
(a) 1992 (b) 2005 (c) 2002 (d) 1997
Answer: (d)
Solution: 2005 is the year the protocol came into force, not when it was adopted — a chronological trap. The Kyoto Protocol was adopted in 1997 and entered into force in 2005, so the answer is 1997.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Biology (Environmental Issues)

110. BOD (Biochemical Oxygen Demand) of water is a measure of:
(a) Dissolved minerals (b) Radioactive contamination (c) Organic pollution (d) Thermal pollution
Answer: (c)
Solution: A higher BOD means more organic matter is present for microbes to decompose, consuming dissolved oxygen; thus BOD indicates organic pollution of water, so the answer is Organic pollution.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Biology (Environmental Issues)

111. Which of the following is an example of ex-situ conservation?
(a) National park (b) Wildlife sanctuary (c) Biosphere reserve (d) Botanical garden
Answer: (d)
Solution: National parks, sanctuaries and biosphere reserves protect species in their natural habitat (in-situ); a botanical garden conserves plants outside their natural habitat, so the answer is Botanical garden.

113. Which of the following is the first national park established in India?
(a) Kanha National Park (b) Kaziranga National Park (c) Jim Corbett National Park (d) Gir National Park
Answer: (c)
Solution: Kaziranga is famous for rhinos and often mischosen, but India's first national park, established in 1936 as Hailey National Park, is Jim Corbett National Park in Uttarakhand, so the answer is Jim Corbett National Park.

114. Acid rain is mainly caused by oxides of:
(a) Carbon and hydrogen (b) Sodium and potassium (c) Chlorine and fluorine (d) Sulphur and nitrogen
Answer: (d)
Solution: Carbon dioxide forms only weak carbonic acid and is not the main cause of acid rain; sulphur dioxide and nitrogen oxides form strong acids in rainwater, so the answer is Sulphur and nitrogen.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Chemistry (Environmental Chemistry)

116. In an ecosystem, decomposers are mainly:
(a) Green plants and algae (b) Bacteria and fungi (c) Herbivores (d) Carnivores
Answer: (b)
Solution: Green plants are producers, not decomposers; bacteria and fungi break down dead organic matter and recycle nutrients, so the answer is Bacteria and fungi.

118. Which of the following is a secondary pollutant?
(a) Sulphur dioxide (b) Carbon monoxide (c) Ground-level ozone (d) Particulate matter
Answer: (c)
Solution: Sulphur dioxide, carbon monoxide and particulates are emitted directly (primary pollutants); ground-level ozone forms in the atmosphere by reactions of primary pollutants in sunlight, so the answer is Ground-level ozone.

120. Eutrophication of a water body is caused by an excess of:
(a) Nitrates and phosphates (b) Heavy metals (c) Pesticides (d) Oil spills
Answer: (a)
Solution: Pesticides cause toxicity, not algal blooms; excess nitrates and phosphates from fertilisers and sewage trigger algal growth that depletes oxygen, so the answer is Nitrates and phosphates.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Biology (Environmental Issues)

121. Consider the following statements about ecological pyramids:
Statement 1: The pyramid of energy is always upright.
Statement 2: The pyramid of biomass can be inverted in some aquatic ecosystems.
Statement 3: The pyramid of numbers is always upright.
Which of the above statements are correct?
(a) 1 and 3 only (b) 2 and 3 only (c) 1 and 2 only (d) 1, 2 and 3
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is Statement 3, which sounds plausible but is false — the pyramid of numbers can be inverted, e.g. one tree supporting many insects. Energy pyramids are always upright (energy is lost at each level), and biomass pyramids can be inverted in oceans where phytoplankton reproduce faster than they are consumed, so the answer is 1 and 2 only.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Biology (Ecosystem)

122. Match the following conventions with their subjects:
1. Stockholm Conference 1972 — P. Human environment
2. Rio Earth Summit 1992 — Q. Environment and development, Agenda 21
3. Paris Agreement 2015 — R. Climate change mitigation
(a) 1-R, 2-Q, 3-P (b) 1-Q, 2-P, 3-R (c) 1-P, 2-R, 3-Q (d) 1-P, 2-Q, 3-R
Answer: (d)
Solution: The chronological confusion trap swaps Stockholm and Rio. Stockholm (1972) was the first UN conference on the human environment; Rio (1992) produced Agenda 21 and the climate and biodiversity conventions; Paris (2015) set global climate targets, so the answer is 1-P, 2-Q, 3-R.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Biology (Environmental Issues)

123. Which of the following statements about biomagnification is correct?
(a) The concentration of toxic substances decreases at higher trophic levels (b) The concentration of non-biodegradable toxic substances increases at successive trophic levels (c) It occurs only in terrestrial food chains (d) It is caused only by radioactive substances
Answer: (b)
Solution: Option one states the exact opposite and tempts careless readers. Biomagnification is the progressive increase in concentration of persistent, non-biodegradable toxins like DDT and mercury at higher trophic levels, in both aquatic and terrestrial chains, so the answer is that the concentration of non-biodegradable toxic substances increases at successive trophic levels.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Biology (Environmental Issues)

124. Consider the following statements about biodiversity hotspots:
Statement 1: A region must have at least 1,500 endemic vascular plant species to qualify as a hotspot.
Statement 2: A region must have lost at least 70% of its original habitat to qualify as a hotspot.
Statement 3: The concept of biodiversity hotspots was given by Norman Myers.
Which of the above statements are correct?
(a) 1, 2 and 3 (b) 2 and 3 only (c) 1 and 3 only (d) 1 and 2 only
Answer: (a)
Solution: All three statements are correct: Norman Myers proposed the hotspot concept, and the two criteria are at least 1,500 endemic vascular plants and at least 70% loss of original habitat, so the answer is 1, 2 and 3.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Biology (Biodiversity and Conservation)

125. Which of the following pairs is incorrectly matched?
(a) Project Tiger — 1973 (b) Biological Diversity Act — 2010 (c) Wildlife Protection Act — 1972 (d) Project Elephant — 1992
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap lies in the near-correct year. Project Tiger began in 1973, Project Elephant in 1992, and the Wildlife Protection Act was passed in 1972 — all correct. The Biological Diversity Act was passed in 2002, not 2010, so the incorrectly matched pair is Biological Diversity Act — 2010.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Biology (Biodiversity and Conservation)
