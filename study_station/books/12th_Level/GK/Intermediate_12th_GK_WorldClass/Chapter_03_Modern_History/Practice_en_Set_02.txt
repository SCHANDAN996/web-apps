Questions 26–50: Medium

26. The Regulating Act of 1773 designated the Governor of Bengal as the Governor-General of Bengal. Who was the first person to hold this post?
(a) Warren Hastings (b) Lord Cornwallis (c) Lord Clive (d) Lord Wellesley
Answer: (a)
Solution: Robert Clive is the tempting wrong choice because he won Plassey and Buxar, but he was only Governor of Bengal, never Governor-General. Warren Hastings became the first Governor-General of Bengal in 1773 under the Regulating Act, so the answer is Warren Hastings.
Source: NCERT Class 8 History (Our Pasts III)

27. The Pitt's India Act of 1784 is considered significant mainly because it:
(a) ended the East India Company's trade monopoly (b) introduced the portfolio system in India (c) separated the judiciary from the executive (d) established a Board of Control to supervise the Company's political affairs
Answer: (d)
Solution: Ending the trade monopoly looks attractive but that happened through the Charter Act of 1813, not Pitt's Act. Pitt's India Act created a Board of Control for political matters while leaving commercial affairs to the Court of Directors, so the answer is that it established a Board of Control.
Source: NCERT Class 8 History (Our Pasts III)

28. Arrange the following events in correct chronological order: 1. Battle of Buxar 2. Battle of Plassey 3. Third Battle of Panipat 4. Battle of Wandiwash
(a) 4, 2, 3, 1 (b) 2, 3, 4, 1 (c) 2, 4, 3, 1 (d) 2, 4, 1, 3
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is placing Buxar before Panipat; Buxar (1764) actually came after the Third Battle of Panipat (1761). The correct sequence is Plassey (1757), Wandiwash (1760), Panipat (1761), Buxar (1764), so the answer is 2, 4, 3, 1.
Source: NCERT Class 8 History (Our Pasts III)

29. The Doctrine of Lapse, under which states without a natural male heir were annexed, is chiefly associated with:
(a) Lord Wellesley (b) Lord Dalhousie (c) Lord Hastings (d) Lord Canning
Answer: (b)
Solution: Lord Wellesley is the confusing option because he too expanded British territory, but his instrument was the Subsidiary Alliance, not annexation of heirless states. Satara (1848) and Jhansi (1853) were annexed under Dalhousie's Doctrine of Lapse, so the answer is Lord Dalhousie.
Source: NCERT Class 8 History (Our Pasts III)

30. Which of the following was the FIRST state to accept the Subsidiary Alliance of Lord Wellesley?
(a) Awadh (b) Tanjore (c) Mysore (d) Hyderabad
Answer: (d)
Solution: Awadh is tempting because it accepted the alliance in 1801 and is frequently asked, but the Nizam of Hyderabad accepted it earlier, in 1798, making Hyderabad the first, so the answer is Hyderabad.

31. The revolt of 1857 began at Meerut on 10 May 1857. The sepoys then marched to Delhi and proclaimed which Mughal ruler as their leader?
(a) Akbar II (b) Bahadur Shah Zafar (c) Shah Alam II (d) Aurangzeb
Answer: (b)
Solution: Shah Alam II is the trap because he was the Mughal emperor during Buxar, but he died in 1806. The sepoys declared the aged Bahadur Shah Zafar (Bahadur Shah II) as Emperor of India, so the answer is Bahadur Shah Zafar.
Source: NCERT Class 8 History (Our Pasts III)

32. Who among the following led the revolt of 1857 at Kanpur?
(a) Rani Lakshmibai (b) Nana Saheb (c) Kunwar Singh (d) Tantia Tope
Answer: (b)
Solution: Tantia Tope is the near-correct trap since he fought alongside Nana Saheb at Kanpur, but the leadership of the Kanpur revolt rested with Nana Saheb, the adopted son of Peshwa Baji Rao II, so the answer is Nana Saheb.

33. The Queen's Proclamation of 1858, transferring the government of India from the East India Company to the Crown, was read out by:
(a) Lord Lytton (b) Lord Dalhousie (c) Lord Canning (d) Lord Ripon
Answer: (c)
Solution: Lord Lytton is tempting because he held the grand Delhi Durbar of 1877, but the 1858 Proclamation was announced by Lord Canning, who also became the first Viceroy of India, so the answer is Lord Canning.

34. The Brahmo Samaj was founded in 1828 by Raja Rammohan Roy. Which of the following statements about it is NOT correct?
(a) It supported the practice of Sati (b) It was influenced by the monotheism of the Upanishads (c) It opposed idol worship and the caste system (d) It promoted the education of women
Answer: (a)
Solution: This is a negative-statement trap; options that sound reformist can make a careless reader pick a true statement. The Brahmo Samaj campaigned against Sati, and Rammohan Roy's efforts led to its abolition in 1829, so the statement that it supported Sati is NOT correct, and the answer is that it supported the practice of Sati.
Source: NCERT Class 8 History (Our Pasts III)

35. The Arya Samaj, founded by Swami Dayananda Saraswati in 1875, gave the call:
(a) "Swaraj is my birthright" (b) "Do or Die" (c) "Back to the Vedas" (d) "Jai Jawan Jai Kisan"
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Swaraj is my birthright" is the tempting option as it also belongs to the nationalist era, but it was Tilak's slogan. Dayananda's call was "Back to the Vedas", so the answer is "Back to the Vedas".

36. The Indian National Congress was founded in 1885. Its first session was held in Bombay under the presidentship of:
(a) A. O. Hume (b) W. C. Bonnerjee (c) Dadabhai Naoroji (d) Surendranath Banerjee
Answer: (b)
Solution: A. O. Hume is the classic trap because he founded the Congress, but a founder need not be the first president. Womesh Chandra Bonnerjee presided over the first session in December 1885, so the answer is W. C. Bonnerjee.

37. The 'Drain of Wealth' theory, explaining India's poverty under British rule, was propounded by:
(a) Gopal Krishna Gokhale (b) R. C. Dutt (c) M. G. Ranade (d) Dadabhai Naoroji
Answer: (d)
Solution: R. C. Dutt is a close distractor since he wrote the Economic History of India, but the systematic Drain of Wealth theory was developed by Dadabhai Naoroji in "Poverty and Un-British Rule in India", so the answer is Dadabhai Naoroji.
Source: NCERT Class 8 History (Our Pasts III)

38. The Partition of Bengal in 1905 was carried out by Lord Curzon. In which year was the partition annulled?
(a) 1911 (b) 1909 (c) 1916 (d) 1919
Answer: (a)
Solution: 1909 is tempting because of the Morley-Minto Reforms of that year, but the annulment of Bengal's partition was announced at the Delhi Durbar of 1911, when the capital also shifted to Delhi, so the answer is 1911.

39. The Swadeshi and Boycott movement was launched as a response to:
(a) the Jallianwala Bagh massacre (b) the Rowlatt Act (c) the Partition of Bengal (d) the Simon Commission
Answer: (c)
Solution: The Rowlatt Act is the chronological-confusion trap since it also triggered mass protest, but that led to the 1919 satyagraha. The Swadeshi and Boycott movement began in 1905 against Curzon's Partition of Bengal, so the answer is the Partition of Bengal.
Source: NCERT Class 8 History (Our Pasts III)

40. The Muslim League was founded in 1906 at:
(a) Lucknow (b) Dhaka (c) Karachi (d) Aligarh
Answer: (b)
Solution: Aligarh is tempting because of Sir Syed Ahmad Khan's movement there, but the All-India Muslim League was founded at Dhaka in December 1906 under the leadership of Aga Khan and Nawab Salimullah, so the answer is Dhaka.

41. The Lucknow Pact of 1916 is remembered for:
(a) the acceptance of the demand for Pakistan (b) the launch of the Non-Cooperation Movement (c) the merger of the Moderates and Extremists and a Congress-League agreement (d) the signing of the Gandhi-Irwin Pact
Answer: (c)
Solution: The Gandhi-Irwin Pact is a similar-name trap, but it was signed in 1931. At Lucknow in 1916 the two Congress wings reunited and the Congress and the Muslim League agreed on a joint scheme of reforms, so the answer is the merger of the Moderates and Extremists and a Congress-League agreement.

43. Who returned his knighthood in protest against the Jallianwala Bagh massacre?
(a) Rabindranath Tagore (b) Mahatma Gandhi (c) Motilal Nehru (d) C. R. Das

Solution: Gandhi is the tempting option because he led the protest movement, but he surrendered his Kaiser-i-Hind medal, not a knighthood. Rabindranath Tagore renounced his knighthood, so the answer is Rabindranath Tagore.

44. The Non-Cooperation Movement (1920–22) was withdrawn by Gandhi after which incident?
(a) Chauri Chaura (b) Jallianwala Bagh (c) Dandi March (d) Kakori conspiracy
Answer: (a)
Solution: Jallianwala Bagh is the trap because it prompted the movement's launch, not its end. After a mob burnt a police station at Chauri Chaura (February 1922), killing 22 policemen, Gandhi suspended the movement, so the answer is Chauri Chaura.
Source: NCERT Class 8 History (Our Pasts III)

45. The Simon Commission (1928) was boycotted in India primarily because:
(a) it proposed the partition of India (b) it was headed by a conservative British MP (c) it recommended the abolition of dyarchy (d) it had no Indian member
Answer: (d)
Solution: The abolition of dyarchy sounds plausible but dyarchy was actually ended by the Government of India Act 1935, not Simon's report. The all-white commission with no Indian member provoked the nationwide "Simon Go Back" protests, so the answer is that it had no Indian member.
Source: NCERT Class 8 History (Our Pasts III)

46. The Dandi March of 1930, which launched the Civil Disobedience Movement, covered the distance from Sabarmati Ashram to Dandi in how many days?
(a) 24 days (b) 15 days (c) 30 days (d) 40 days
Answer: (a)
Solution: The march began on 12 March 1930 and Gandhi broke the salt law on 6 April 1930, a journey of about 240 miles completed in 24 days, so the answer is 24 days.

47. The Government of India Act 1935 provided for:
(a) provincial autonomy and an all-India federation (b) complete independence for India (c) separate electorates for the first time (d) the partition of Bengal
Answer: (a)
Solution: Separate electorates are the trap, but they were first introduced by the Act of 1909, not 1935. The 1935 Act introduced provincial autonomy (1937) and proposed an all-India federation that never came into being, so the answer is provincial autonomy and an all-India federation.
Source: NCERT Class 8 History (Our Pasts III)

48. The Quit India Movement of 1942 was launched after the failure of:
(a) the Wavell Plan (b) the Cabinet Mission (c) the Cripps Mission (d) the Mountbatten Plan
Answer: (c)
Solution: The Cabinet Mission is the chronological trap, but it came in 1946, after Quit India. The failure of Stafford Cripps' proposals in March 1942 led directly to the August 1942 "Do or Die" movement, so the answer is the Cripps Mission.

49. The Indian National Army (Azad Hind Fauj) was reorganised and led by Subhas Chandra Bose, who gave the slogan:
(a) "Inquilab Zindabad" (b) "Jai Hind" (c) "Vande Mataram" (d) "Sarfaroshi ki Tamanna"
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Inquilab Zindabad" is tempting but it is associated with Bhagat Singh. Bose's greeting and slogan for the INA was "Jai Hind", along with "Dilli Chalo", so the answer is "Jai Hind".

50. Arrange the following in correct chronological order: 1. Cabinet Mission 2. Cripps Mission 3. Mountbatten Plan 4. Wavell Plan (Shimla Conference)
(a) 1, 2, 4, 3 (b) 2, 1, 4, 3 (c) 4, 2, 1, 3 (d) 2, 4, 1, 3
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is placing the Cabinet Mission before the Wavell Plan; the Shimla Conference (June 1945) preceded the Cabinet Mission (March 1946). The correct order is Cripps Mission (1942), Wavell Plan (1945), Cabinet Mission (1946), Mountbatten Plan (June 1947), so the answer is 2, 4, 1, 3.
