# Modern Indian History

## The Night a Nation Woke Up

On the night of 14 August 1947, as the clock struck twelve, Jawaharlal Nehru spoke words that still give goosebumps: *"At the stroke of the midnight hour, when the world sleeps, India will awake to life and freedom."* But that freedom was not a gift — it was the result of nearly **200 years of struggle**, from the first sparks of 1857 to the final push of 1947.

This chapter is the story of how a colonised nation fought back — with swords, with pens, with salt, and with sheer willpower. And here is why you must master it: **Modern Indian History is one of the most frequently asked areas in SSC, Railway, Banking and State PCS exams.** Almost every paper has questions on the freedom struggle, Governor-Generals, Congress sessions, and revolutionary movements.

## Part 1: The British Arrive — From Traders to Rulers

### The East India Company

The English East India Company was formed in **1600** by a royal charter from Queen Elizabeth I. It came to India to trade, not to rule. But two battles changed everything:

- **Battle of Plassey (1757):** Robert Clive defeated Siraj-ud-Daulah, the Nawab of Bengal. The real cause of victory was treachery — Mir Jafar, the Nawab's commander, betrayed him. This battle laid the **foundation of British rule in India**.
- **Battle of Buxar (1764):** The British defeated the combined forces of Mir Qasim (Bengal), Shuja-ud-Daulah (Awadh) and Mughal Emperor Shah Alam II. The **Treaty of Allahabad (1765)** gave the Company the **Diwani rights** (right to collect revenue) of Bengal, Bihar and Orissa.

> 🧠 **Active Recall:** Which battle gave the British the Diwani rights of Bengal? Who betrayed Siraj-ud-Daulah at Plassey?

### How the British Expanded

The Company used clever policies to swallow Indian states one by one:

| Policy | Governor-General | What it did |
|---|---|---|
| Subsidiary Alliance | Lord Wellesley (1798–1805) | Indian rulers had to keep British troops and pay for them; a British Resident stayed in their court |
| Doctrine of Lapse | Lord Dalhousie (1848–1856) | If a ruler died without a natural male heir, his state was annexed — Satara (1848), Jhansi (1853), Nagpur (1854) |
| Misgovernance annexation | Dalhousie | Awadh annexed in 1856 on grounds of "misrule" |

**Mnemonic 🪄 — "S-J-N-A" for Doctrine of Lapse:** **S**atara, **J**hansi, **N**agpur, **A**wadh (though Awadh was technically annexed for misrule, examiners love to mix these up!).

> 🧠 **Active Recall:** Who introduced the Subsidiary Alliance? Name two states annexed under the Doctrine of Lapse.

## Part 2: The Revolt of 1857 — The First War of Independence

### Causes

- **Political:** Doctrine of Lapse angered Indian rulers.
- **Economic:** Heavy land revenue, destruction of Indian handicrafts.
- **Social-Religious:** Fear of forced conversion to Christianity.
- **Military (Immediate cause):** The new **Enfield rifle cartridges** were greased with cow and pig fat — offensive to both Hindus and Muslims.

### The Spark and the Spread

- **29 March 1857:** Mangal Pandey attacked British officers at **Barrackpore**.
- **10 May 1857:** Revolt began at **Meerut**; soldiers marched to Delhi and declared **Bahadur Shah Zafar II** (the last Mughal emperor) their leader.

### Key Centres and Leaders

| Centre | Leader |
|---|---|
| Delhi | Bahadur Shah Zafar II (General Bakht Khan led the fighting) |
| Kanpur | Nana Saheb, with Tantia Tope |
| Lucknow | Begum Hazrat Mahal |
| Jhansi | Rani Lakshmibai |
| Bihar (Jagdishpur) | Kunwar Singh |

### Why It Failed and What Changed

The revolt failed due to limited spread, lack of central leadership, and superior British resources. But its consequences were huge:

- **Government of India Act 1858:** Rule transferred from the East India Company to the **British Crown**.
- **Queen Victoria's Proclamation (1858):** Promised non-interference in religion.
- The office of Governor-General became **Viceroy** — **Lord Canning** was the first Viceroy.

> 🧠 **Active Recall:** Where did the revolt begin, and on what date? Who was the first Viceroy of India?

## Part 3: The Birth of the Congress and the Freedom Movement

### Indian National Congress (1885)

- Founded by **A.O. Hume** (Allan Octavian Hume), a retired British civil servant.
- **First session: Bombay, 1885**, presided over by **W.C. Bonnerjee** (Womesh Chunder Bonnerjee), attended by 72 delegates.

### Moderates vs Extremists

- **Moderates (1885–1905):** Dadabhai Naoroji, Gopal Krishna Gokhale, Surendranath Banerjee. They believed in petitions and prayers. Dadabhai Naoroji gave the famous **"Drain of Wealth"** theory in his book *Poverty and Un-British Rule in India*.
- **Extremists:** **Lala Lajpat Rai, Bal Gangadhar Tilak, Bipin Chandra Pal** — the famous **"Lal-Bal-Pal"** trio. Tilak declared: *"Swaraj is my birthright and I shall have it."*

### Partition of Bengal (1905) and Swadeshi Movement

- **Lord Curzon** partitioned Bengal in **1905**, officially for "administrative convenience" — but Indians saw it as **divide and rule**.
- The **Swadeshi and Boycott Movement** erupted. **16 October 1905** was observed as Raksha Bandhan day (Rabindranath Tagore's idea).
- **Surat Split (1907):** Congress split into Moderates and Extremists.
- **Partition annulled in 1911**; capital shifted from Calcutta to **Delhi**.

**Mnemonic 🪄 — "Lal-Bal-Pal":** **Lal**a Lajpat Rai (Punjab), **Bal** Gangadhar Tilak (Maharashtra), Bipin Chandra **Pal** (Bengal).

### Muslim League and Revolutionary Phase

- **Muslim League founded: 1906, Dhaka** — by Aga Khan and Nawab Salimullah.
- **Lucknow Pact (1916):** Congress and Muslim League joined hands; Extremists re-entered Congress.
- **Home Rule Leagues (1916):** Tilak (Poona) and Annie Besant (Madras).
- Revolutionaries: **Khudiram Bose** (hanged 1908), **Chandrashekhar Azad**, **Bhagat Singh, Rajguru, Sukhdev** (hanged 23 March 1931 for the Saunders murder and Assembly bomb case). Bhagat Singh founded the **Hindustan Socialist Republican Association (HSRA, 1928)**.

> 🧠 **Active Recall:** Who founded the Muslim League and where? What was the Lucknow Pact?

## Part 4: The Gandhi Era (1915–1947)

### Gandhi's Arrival and Early Movements

Mahatma Gandhi returned from South Africa in **1915**. His early successes:

- **Champaran Satyagraha (1917, Bihar):** Against indigo planters (tinkathia system) — Gandhi's **first** satyagraha in India.
- **Ahmedabad Mill Strike (1918):** For mill workers' wages.
- **Kheda Satyagraha (1918, Gujarat):** For peasants unable to pay revenue due to crop failure.

### Rowlatt Act and Jallianwala Bagh (1919)

- **Rowlatt Act (1919):** Allowed detention without trial — called the "Black Act."
- **Jallianwala Bagh Massacre (13 April 1919, Amritsar):** General **Dyer** ordered firing on a peaceful gathering on Baisakhi day. Hundreds were killed. Rabindranath Tagore renounced his knighthood in protest.

### Non-Cooperation Movement (1920–22)

- Launched with the Khilafat issue; boycott of schools, courts, foreign cloth.
- **Chauri Chaura (5 February 1922, Gorakhpur, UP):** A mob burnt a police station, killing 22 policemen. Gandhi **withdrew the movement**.

### Civil Disobedience Movement (1930–34)

- **Dandi March (12 March – 6 April 1930):** Gandhi walked 240 miles (about 385 km) from **Sabarmati Ashram to Dandi** with 78 followers and broke the salt law.
- **Round Table Conferences (London):** First (1930) — Congress absent; **Second (1931)** — Gandhi attended but it failed; Third (1932) — Congress absent.
- **Communal Award (1932)** by Ramsay MacDonald gave separate electorates to the Depressed Classes; Gandhi's fast led to the **Poona Pact (1932)** with **B.R. Ambedkar** — reserved seats instead of separate electorates.

### Government of India Act 1935 and Provincial Elections

- Introduced **provincial autonomy** and proposed an All-India Federation (never implemented).
- **1937 elections:** Congress formed ministries in 8 of 11 provinces; resigned in 1939 over India being dragged into WWII without consultation.

### Quit India Movement (1942)

- **8 August 1942, Bombay (Gowalia Tank Maidan):** Gandhi gave the call **"Do or Die"** (Karo ya Maro).
- All top leaders were arrested; the movement became leaderless and was crushed.

### INA and the Final Phase

- **Subhas Chandra Bose** escaped from house arrest (1941), reached Germany, then Japan.
- He took command of the **Indian National Army (Azad Hind Fauj)** — originally formed by **Mohan Singh** and revived by **Rash Behari Bose**.
- **21 October 1943:** Bose formed the **Provisional Government of Azad Hind** in Singapore. His famous slogans: **"Give me blood, and I will give you freedom"** and **"Jai Hind."** INA's women's regiment was the **Rani Jhansi Regiment**, led by Captain Lakshmi (Sahgal).

### Towards Independence

- **Cabinet Mission (1946):** Proposed a Constituent Assembly; rejected Pakistan demand but proposed grouping of provinces.
- **Constituent Assembly first met: 9 December 1946**; Dr. Rajendra Prasad became its President.
- **Mountbatten Plan (3 June 1947)** → **Indian Independence Act 1947** → **15 August 1947: Independence and Partition.**

**Mnemonic 🪄 — Gandhi's movements in order: "C-A-K-N-C-Q" →** **C**hamparan (1917), **A**hmedabad (1918), **K**heda (1918), **N**on-Cooperation (1920), **C**ivil Disobedience (1930), **Q**uit India (1942). Remember: *"Chai Aur Khana — Nahi Chahiye, Quit!"*

> 🧠 **Active Recall:** On which date did the Dandi March begin and end? Who gave the slogan "Do or Die"? Who originally formed the INA?

## 🔗 Static-Dynamic Link

The **26 January** we celebrate as Republic Day was first chosen because the Congress observed **26 January 1930 as "Purna Swaraj Day"** after the Lahore Session of 1929. When the Constitution came into force, this historic date was deliberately picked. Similarly, the **salt tax** Gandhi protested against in 1930 was finally abolished only after independence — showing how one march at Dandi echoed for decades.

## ⚠️ Caution: Examiner's Trap

**Trap 1 — Chronological Confusion (कालानुक्रमिक भ्रम):**
*Question:* "Which movement came first — Civil Disobedience or Non-Cooperation?" Many students mix up 1920 and 1930. Remember: **Non-Cooperation (1920) → Civil Disobedience (1930) → Quit India (1942)** — a neat 10-year gap pattern. Also, Champaran (1917) came **before** Kheda and Ahmedabad (both 1918).

**Trap 2 — Nearly-True Statement (लगभग सही कथन):**
*"Bhagat Singh founded the Indian National Army."* Sounds plausible (both are revolutionary icons), but it is **false** — the INA was first formed by **Captain Mohan Singh** and later led by **Subhas Chandra Bose**. Bhagat Singh founded the **HSRA**. Examiners love swapping revolutionary names.

**Trap 3 — Similar Names/Years (समान नाम/वर्ष):**
- **First session of Congress (1885, Bombay)** vs **Surat Split (1907)** vs **Lucknow Pact (1916)** — all involve Congress and get shuffled in options.
- **Annie Besant (1917, first woman president)** vs **Sarojini Naidu (1925, first *Indian* woman president)** — the word "Indian" is the hidden trap. Read every word!
