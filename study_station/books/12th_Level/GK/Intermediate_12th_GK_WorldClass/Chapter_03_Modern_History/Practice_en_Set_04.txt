Questions 76–100: Medium

76. The Ilbert Bill controversy (1883) arose because the Bill proposed to:
(a) Allow Indian judges to try European offenders (b) Impose tax on European-owned factories (c) Allow Indians to join the Indian Civil Service (d) Give voting rights to Indian women
Answer: (a)
Solution: The tempting trap is the Civil Service option, since ICS reform was a live issue of that era, but the Ilbert Bill specifically sought to let Indian judges exercise jurisdiction over European British subjects, which provoked furious European opposition. The Bill was passed in a diluted form in 1884, so the answer is allowing Indian judges to try European offenders.
Source: NCERT Class 12 History (Themes in Indian History Part III)

77. Arrange the following events in correct chronological order: 1. Formation of the Muslim League 2. Partition of Bengal 3. Surat Split of Congress 4. Lucknow Pact
(a) 1, 2, 3, 4 (b) 2, 1, 3, 4 (c) 2, 1, 4, 3 (d) 1, 2, 4, 3
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is placing the Muslim League (1906) before the Partition of Bengal (1905); the Partition came first and the League was founded in December 1906 at Dacca. Surat Split followed in 1907 and the Lucknow Pact in 1916, so the answer is 2, 1, 3, 4.
Source: NCERT Class 12 History

78. The 'Drain of Wealth' theory was systematically propounded by:
(a) Gopal Krishna Gokhale (b) Dadabhai Naoroji (c) R. C. Dutt (d) Surendranath Banerjee
Answer: (b)
Solution: R. C. Dutt is the tempting wrong option because his 'Economic History of India' documented the same exploitation, but the systematic theory of drain was Naoroji's, presented in 'Poverty and Un-British Rule in India'. Hence the answer is Dadabhai Naoroji.
Source: NCERT Class 12 History

79. Which of the following statements about the Revolt of 1857 is correct?
(a) It began at Meerut on 10 May 1857 (b) It began at Barrackpore in March 1857 under Bahadur Shah II (c) The revolt was led in Kanpur by Tantia Tope alone (d) The revolt ended with the Treaty of Allahabad
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is the Barrackpore option: Mangal Pandey's outbreak at Barrackpore (29 March 1857) was a precursor, not the start of the general revolt, and Bahadur Shah II was not there. Kanpur was led by Nana Saheb with Tantia Tope, and the Treaty of Allahabad (1765) belongs to Buxar. The revolt began at Meerut on 10 May 1857, so the answer is that option.
Source: NCERT Class 12 History

80. The Hunter Commission (1882) was appointed to enquire into:
(a) The working of local self-government (b) Police administration (c) Primary and secondary education (d) The Vernacular Press Act
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is local self-government, because Ripon (who appointed the Hunter Commission) is famous for local bodies; but the Commission under W. W. Hunter examined education, especially the implementation of Wood's Despatch. So the answer is primary and secondary education.
Source: NCERT Class 12 History

81. Who among the following founded the Ramakrishna Mission?
(a) Ramakrishna Paramahamsa (b) Swami Vivekananda (c) Swami Dayananda Saraswati (d) Keshab Chandra Sen
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is Ramakrishna Paramahamsa himself, since the Mission bears his name; but it was founded by his disciple Swami Vivekananda in 1897 at Belur. Dayananda founded the Arya Samaj and Keshab Chandra Sen led the Brahmo Samaj of India, so the answer is Swami Vivekananda.
Source: NCERT Class 12 History

82. The Lucknow Pact of 1916 was an agreement between:
(a) Congress and the Muslim League (b) Congress and the British Government (c) Moderates and Extremists only (d) The Muslim League and the British Government
Answer: (a)
Solution: The tempting option is Moderates and Extremists, because the 1916 Lucknow session also saw their reunion; but the 'Pact' proper was the Congress–League scheme accepting separate electorates and joint constitutional demands. So the answer is Congress and the Muslim League.
Source: NCERT Class 12 History

83. Which Viceroy announced the partition of Bengal in 1905?
(a) Lord Hardinge (b) Lord Minto (c) Lord Curzon (d) Lord Lytton
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is Lord Minto, because the formal partition took effect in October 1905 and Minto succeeded Curzon in November 1905, causing confusion; the announcement (July 1905) and the scheme were Curzon's. Hardinge annulled the partition in 1911, so the answer is Lord Curzon.
Source: NCERT Class 12 History

84. The Ghadar Party was founded in 1913 at:
(a) Vancouver (b) London (c) San Francisco (d) Singapore
Answer: (c)
Solution: Vancouver is the trap because many Punjabi emigrants landed in Canada and the Komagata Maru episode is linked to it; but the Ghadar Party was founded by Lala Har Dayal and others at San Francisco, USA. So the answer is San Francisco.
Source: NCERT Class 12 History

85. Which of the following pairs is correctly matched?
(a) Brahmo Samaj – 1828 – Raja Rammohan Roy (b) Arya Samaj – 1875 – Swami Vivekananda (c) Aligarh Movement – Syed Ahmad Khan – Deoband (d) Satyashodhak Samaj – Jyotiba Phule – Bengal
Answer: (a)
Solution: The traps: Arya Samaj was founded by Dayananda Saraswati, not Vivekananda; Syed Ahmad Khan's centre was Aligarh, not Deoband; Phule's Satyashodhak Samaj was in Maharashtra, not Bengal. Rammohan Roy founded the Brahmo Samaj in 1828, so the answer is that pair.
Source: NCERT Class 12 History

86. The Swadeshi movement after 1905 saw the first major use of which technique on a large scale?
(a) Armed insurrection (b) Hunger strikes in prisons (c) Boycott of foreign goods and institutions (d) Formation of a parallel government
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is armed insurrection, associated with revolutionary terrorism of the same years; the Swadeshi movement's hallmark was boycott of foreign cloth, schools and courts, with promotion of indigenous enterprise. So the answer is boycott of foreign goods and institutions.
Source: NCERT Class 12 History

87. Who was the first Indian to become a member of the British House of Commons?
(a) M. K. Gandhi (b) Surendranath Banerjee (c) W. C. Bonnerjee (d) Dadabhai Naoroji
Answer: (d)
Solution: W. C. Bonnerjee is the trap as the first Congress president, but he never entered Parliament. Dadabhai Naoroji was elected to the House of Commons from Finsbury Central in 1892 as a Liberal, so the answer is Dadabhai Naoroji.

88. The Vernacular Press Act of 1878 was repealed by:
(a) Lord Dufferin (b) Lord Lytton (c) Lord Ripon (d) Lord Lansdowne
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is Lord Lytton, who enacted the Act in 1878; it was repealed by his successor Lord Ripon in 1882. So the answer is Lord Ripon.

89. Which of the following was NOT a cause of the Revolt of 1857?
(a) Doctrine of Lapse (b) Abolition of sati (c) Greased cartridge issue (d) Subsidiary Alliance system
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is the Subsidiary Alliance, which students may forget was Wellesley's earlier expansionist device that fuelled resentment against British paramountcy. The abolition of sati (1829) was a social reform, not a cause of the 1857 revolt, so the answer is abolition of sati.
Source: NCERT Class 12 History

90. The 'Two-Nation Theory' was first systematically articulated in the 1930s by:
(a) Muhammad Iqbal (b) Muhammad Ali Jinnah (c) Aga Khan (d) Liaquat Ali Khan
Answer: (a)
Solution: Jinnah is the tempting option since he championed the theory at Lahore in 1940, but the earliest systematic articulation was Iqbal's presidential address at the Allahabad session of the Muslim League in 1930. So the answer is Muhammad Iqbal.
Source: NCERT Class 12 History

91. The Chauri Chaura incident (1922) led Gandhi to:
(a) Launch the Civil Disobedience Movement (b) Sign the Gandhi-Irwin Pact (c) Withdraw the Non-Cooperation Movement (d) Start the Quit India Movement
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is Civil Disobedience, which students confuse because it too followed a Gandhi decision, but after the killing of 22 policemen at Chauri Chaura on 5 February 1922, Gandhi suspended the Non-Cooperation Movement. So the answer is withdrawal of the Non-Cooperation Movement.
Source: NCERT Class 12 History

92. Who presided over the Lahore session of the Congress (1929) where Purna Swaraj was declared?
(a) Motilal Nehru (b) Jawaharlal Nehru (c) Subhas Chandra Bose (d) Vallabhbhai Patel
Answer: (b)
Solution: Motilal Nehru is the trap, being Jawaharlal's father and a Congress president (1928), but the historic 1929 Lahore session was presided over by Jawaharlal Nehru. So the answer is Jawaharlal Nehru.

93. The Simon Commission was boycotted primarily because:
(a) It proposed partition of India (b) It had no Indian member (c) It recommended abolition of dyarchy (d) It was headed by a Conservative
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is abolition of dyarchy, which the Commission did eventually recommend, but that was not the reason for the boycott; Indians resented that an all-white commission would decide India's constitutional future. So the answer is that it had no Indian member.
Source: NCERT Class 12 History

94. Which of the following statements about the Government of India Act 1935 is correct?
(a) It introduced dyarchy in the provinces (b) It created Pakistan (c) It granted dominion status immediately (d) It introduced provincial autonomy
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is dyarchy in the provinces — dyarchy was introduced by the 1919 Act in provinces, while the 1935 Act abolished it and introduced provincial autonomy (with dyarchy proposed at the Centre, never implemented). So the answer is provincial autonomy.
Source: NCERT Class 12 History

95. The Azad Hind Fauj (INA) was first organised by:
(a) Mohan Singh (b) Subhas Chandra Bose (c) Rash Behari Bose (d) Shah Nawaz Khan
Answer: (a)
Solution: Subhas Chandra Bose is the classic trap because he led the INA to fame from 1943; but the INA was first organised in 1942 by Captain Mohan Singh from Indian prisoners of war, with Rash Behari Bose's Indian Independence League support. So the answer is Mohan Singh.
Source: NCERT Class 12 History

96. The Cabinet Mission (1946) proposed:
(a) Immediate partition of India (b) A separate electorate for all minorities (c) Complete independence for princely states (d) A three-tier federation with grouping of provinces
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is immediate partition — the Cabinet Mission explicitly rejected Pakistan and instead proposed a Union with Sections A, B and C grouping of provinces. So the answer is a three-tier federation with grouping of provinces.
Source: NCERT Class 12 History

97. Who gave the slogan 'Do or Die'?
(a) Subhas Chandra Bose (b) Bal Gangadhar Tilak (c) Bhagat Singh (d) Mahatma Gandhi
Answer: (d)
Solution: Bose is the trap because of his militant slogans like 'Give me blood, I will give you freedom'; 'Do or Die' (Karo ya Maro) was Gandhi's call at the launch of the Quit India Movement on 8 August 1942. So the answer is Mahatma Gandhi.
Source: NCERT Class 12 History

98. The Cripps Mission (1942) failed mainly because:
(a) It offered only dominion status after the war (b) It proposed immediate partition (c) The Muslim League rejected it outright (d) Congress demanded control over defence during the war
Answer: (d)
Solution: The tempting option is dominion status, which was indeed offered, but the immediate sticking point was Congress's demand for an effective national government with control of defence during the war, which the British refused. So the answer is Congress's demand for control over defence.
Source: NCERT Class 12 History

99. Arrange in chronological order: 1. Dandi March 2. Gandhi-Irwin Pact 3. Second Round Table Conference 4. Communal Award
(a) 1, 2, 3, 4 (b) 2, 1, 3, 4 (c) 1, 3, 2, 4 (d) 1, 2, 4, 3
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is swapping the Gandhi-Irwin Pact and the Second Round Table Conference; the Dandi March (March 1930) came first, then the Pact (March 1931), then Gandhi attended the Second RTC (late 1931), and the Communal Award came in August 1932. So the answer is 1, 2, 3, 4.
Source: NCERT Class 12 History

100. The Mountbatten Plan of 3 June 1947 provided for:
(a) A united India with a weak centre (b) Independence for princely states as sovereign nations (c) A confederation of three zones (d) Partition of India into two dominions
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is the united-India-with-weak-centre option, which describes the Cabinet Mission Plan, not the Mountbatten Plan; the 3 June Plan accepted partition into India and Pakistan as two dominions, with princely states required to accede to one. So the answer is partition of India into two dominions.
Source: NCERT Class 12 History
