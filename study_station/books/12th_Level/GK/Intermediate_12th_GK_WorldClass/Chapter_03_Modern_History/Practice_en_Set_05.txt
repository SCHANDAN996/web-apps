101. The Regulating Act of 1773 designated the Governor of Bengal as the Governor-General of Bengal. Who was the first person to hold this post?
(a) Warren Hastings (b) Lord Cornwallis (c) Lord Clive (d) Lord Wellesley
Answer: (a)
Solution: Robert Clive is the tempting wrong option because he won Plassey and laid the Company's foundation, but he was only Governor of Bengal, not Governor-General. Warren Hastings became the first Governor-General of Bengal in 1773 under the Regulating Act, so the answer is Warren Hastings.
Source: NCERT Class 8 History (Our Pasts III)

102. The Pitt's India Act of 1784 is significant because it:
(a) ended the East India Company's trade monopoly (b) established a Board of Control to supervise the Company's political affairs (c) abolished the office of Governor-General (d) introduced separate electorates for Indians
Answer: (b)
Solution: Ending the trade monopoly is the tempting wrong option, but that was done by the Charter Act of 1813, not Pitt's India Act. The 1784 Act created a Board of Control in London to oversee political matters, establishing dual control, so the answer is that it established a Board of Control.
Source: NCERT Class 8 History (Our Pasts III)

103. The Doctrine of Lapse, under which states without a natural male heir were annexed, is most associated with:
(a) Lord Hastings (b) Lord Canning (c) Lord Dalhousie (d) Lord Bentinck
Answer: (c)
Solution: Lord Canning is the tempting wrong option because he was Governor-General when the 1857 revolt broke out, partly due to annexations, but the policy itself was applied vigorously by Dalhousie (1848–56), under whom Satara, Jhansi and Nagpur were annexed, so the answer is Lord Dalhousie.
Source: NCERT Class 8 History (Our Pasts III)

104. Which of the following was the immediate cause of the Revolt of 1857?
(a) Annexation of Awadh (b) Subsidiary Alliance system (c) Abolition of sati (d) Introduction of greased cartridges in the Enfield rifle
Answer: (d)
Solution: The annexation of Awadh is the tempting wrong option, but it was a long-term political cause, not the immediate spark. The cartridges greased with cow and pig fat, which soldiers had to bite, triggered the mutiny at Meerut in May 1857, so the answer is the greased cartridges.
Source: NCERT Class 8 History (Our Pasts III)

105. Who among the following led the Revolt of 1857 at Kanpur?
(a) Rani Lakshmibai (b) Kunwar Singh (c) Nana Saheb (d) Begum Hazrat Mahal
Answer: (c)
Solution: Rani Lakshmibai is the tempting wrong option, but she led the revolt at Jhansi, not Kanpur. Nana Saheb, the adopted son of Peshwa Baji Rao II, led the revolt at Kanpur, so the answer is Nana Saheb.
Source: NCERT Class 8 History (Our Pasts III)

106. After the Revolt of 1857, the administration of India was transferred from the East India Company to the British Crown by the:
(a) Regulating Act, 1773 (b) Charter Act, 1853 (c) Government of India Act, 1858 (d) Indian Councils Act, 1861
Answer: (c)
Solution: The Charter Act of 1853 is the tempting wrong option because it was the last Charter Act, but the Company continued to rule after it. The Government of India Act, 1858 ended Company rule and made the Governor-General the Viceroy, so the answer is the Government of India Act, 1858.
Source: NCERT Class 8 History (Our Pasts III)

107. The Indian National Congress was founded in 1885. Its first session was held at:
(a) Calcutta (b) Lahore (c) Madras (d) Bombay
Answer: (d)
Solution: Calcutta is the tempting wrong option because it was the capital and hosted many early sessions, but the first session (December 1885), presided over by W.C. Bonnerjee, was held at Gokuldas Tejpal Sanskrit College, Bombay, so the answer is Bombay.
Source: NCERT Class 8 History (Our Pasts III)

108. The partition of Bengal in 1905 was carried out during the viceroyalty of:
(a) Lord Curzon (b) Lord Minto (c) Lord Hardinge (d) Lord Ripon
Answer: (a)
Solution: Lord Hardinge is the tempting wrong option, but he annulled the partition in 1911; he did not carry it out. Lord Curzon partitioned Bengal in October 1905, officially for administrative efficiency, sparking the Swadeshi movement, so the answer is Lord Curzon.
Source: NCERT Class 8 History (Our Pasts III)

109. The Muslim League was founded in 1906 at:
(a) Lucknow (b) Aligarh (c) Karachi (d) Dhaka
Answer: (d)
Solution: Aligarh is the tempting wrong option because it was the centre of Sir Syed Ahmad Khan's movement, but the All-India Muslim League was founded at Dhaka in December 1906 under Aga Khan and Nawab Salimullah, so the answer is Dhaka.
Source: NCERT Class 8 History (Our Pasts III)

110. The Swadeshi movement after the Bengal partition was formally proclaimed at a meeting held at:
(a) Belur Math (b) Shantiniketan (c) Town Hall, Calcutta (d) Serampore
Answer: (c)
Solution: Shantiniketan is the tempting wrong option because of its association with Tagore, who supported Swadeshi, but the formal proclamation of the Swadeshi movement was made at the Calcutta Town Hall meeting on 7 August 1905, so the answer is Town Hall, Calcutta.
Source: Spectrum, Modern India

111. The Surat Split of the Congress (1907) divided the party into:
(a) Nationalists and Loyalists (b) Swarajists and No-changers (c) Leavers and Stayers (d) Moderates and Extremists
Answer: (d)
Solution: Swarajists and No-changers is the tempting wrong option, but that split occurred in 1922–23 after the withdrawal of Non-Cooperation. At Surat in 1907 the Congress split into Moderates and Extremists (Lal-Bal-Pal group), so the answer is Moderates and Extremists.
Source: NCERT Class 8 History (Our Pasts III)

112. The Ghadar Party, founded in 1913, had its headquarters at:
(a) San Francisco (b) Vancouver (c) London (d) Berlin
Answer: (a)
Solution: Vancouver is the tempting wrong option because of the Komagata Maru incident, but the Ghadar Party, founded by Lala Har Dayal and Sohan Singh Bhakna, was headquartered at San Francisco, USA, so the answer is San Francisco.

113. The Lucknow Pact of 1916 was an agreement between:
(a) Congress and the British Government (b) Congress and the Muslim League (c) Moderates and Extremists only (d) Muslim League and the British Government
Answer: (b)
Solution: The reunion of Moderates and Extremists is the tempting wrong option because it also happened at Lucknow in 1916, but the Lucknow Pact specifically refers to the Congress–Muslim League agreement on joint constitutional demands, so the answer is Congress and the Muslim League.
Source: NCERT Class 8 History (Our Pasts III)

114. The Rowlatt Act (1919) was opposed because it:
(a) introduced separate electorates (b) allowed detention of suspects without trial (c) increased salt tax (d) partitioned Bengal
Answer: (b)
Solution: Separate electorates is the tempting wrong option, but that was the Morley-Minto Reforms of 1909. The Rowlatt Act authorised imprisonment without trial, provoking Gandhi's first all-India satyagraha, so the answer is detention without trial.
Source: NCERT Class 8 History (Our Pasts III)

115. The Jallianwala Bagh massacre (13 April 1919) took place in the city of:
(a) Amritsar (b) Lahore (c) Delhi (d) Lucknow
Answer: (a)
Solution: Lahore is the tempting wrong option because it was the centre of Punjab unrest in 1919, but General Dyer's firing on the unarmed crowd occurred at Jallianwala Bagh in Amritsar on Baisakhi day, so the answer is Amritsar.
Source: NCERT Class 8 History (Our Pasts III)

116. The Non-Cooperation Movement (1920–22) was withdrawn by Gandhi after the incident at:
(a) Jallianwala Bagh (b) Chauri Chaura (c) Dandi (d) Bardoli
Answer: (b)
Solution: Bardoli is the tempting wrong option because Gandhi later used Bardoli for a satyagraha in 1928, but the Non-Cooperation Movement was suspended in February 1922 after a mob burnt a police station at Chauri Chaura (UP), killing 22 policemen, so the answer is Chauri Chaura.
Source: NCERT Class 8 History (Our Pasts III)

117. The Simon Commission (1928) was boycotted in India because:
(a) it proposed the partition of India (b) it recommended abolition of dyarchy (c) it had no Indian member (d) it supported the princely states
Answer: (c)
Solution: Abolition of dyarchy is the tempting wrong option because the Commission's report did deal with dyarchy, but the boycott was because the all-white commission contained not a single Indian member, so the answer is that it had no Indian member.
Source: NCERT Class 8 History (Our Pasts III)

118. The Dandi March (1930) began from Sabarmati Ashram and ended at Dandi, covering approximately:
(a) 100 miles (b) 240 miles (c) 400 miles (d) 50 miles
Answer: (b)
Solution: 100 miles is the tempting wrong option as a round figure, but Gandhi and 78 followers marched about 240 miles (385 km) from 12 March to 6 April 1930 to break the salt law, so the answer is 240 miles.

119. The Government of India Act, 1935 provided for:
(a) complete independence for India (b) separate electorates for the first time (c) provincial autonomy and a federal structure (d) the creation of Pakistan
Answer: (c)
Solution: Separate electorates is the tempting wrong option, but they were first introduced in 1909, not 1935. The 1935 Act introduced provincial autonomy (1937 elections) and proposed an all-India federation, so the answer is provincial autonomy and a federal structure.
Source: NCERT Class 8 History (Our Pasts III)

120. The Quit India Movement (1942) was launched with Gandhi's slogan:
(a) "Swaraj is my birthright" (b) "Do or Die" (c) "Inquilab Zindabad" (d) "Jai Hind"
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Swaraj is my birthright" is the tempting wrong option, but that was Bal Gangadhar Tilak's slogan. At the Bombay session of August 1942 Gandhi gave the call "Do or Die" (Karo ya Maro), so the answer is "Do or Die".
Source: NCERT Class 8 History (Our Pasts III)

121. Consider the following events and arrange them in correct chronological order:
1. Formation of the Swaraj Party
2. Chauri Chaura incident
3. Dandi March
4. Poona Pact
(a) 1, 2, 4, 3 (b) 1, 2, 3, 4 (c) 2, 1, 4, 3 (d) 2, 1, 3, 4
Answer: (d)
Solution: The tempting wrong order places the Swaraj Party before Chauri Chaura, but the party (C.R. Das and Motilal Nehru, 1923) was formed after the movement's withdrawal following Chauri Chaura (February 1922). Dandi March was 1930 and the Poona Pact 1932, so the correct sequence is 2, 1, 3, 4.
Source: Spectrum, Modern India

122. Which of the following statements about the Cabinet Mission (1946) is correct?
(a) It proposed a three-tier federation with grouping of provinces (b) It accepted the demand for a separate Pakistan (c) It recommended immediate transfer of power to the Congress (d) It was headed by Lord Mountbatten
Answer: (a)
Solution: Lord Mountbatten is the tempting wrong option, but he came as Viceroy in 1947 with the June 3 Plan; the Cabinet Mission (Pethick-Lawrence, Cripps, Alexander) rejected Pakistan and proposed a Union with Section-wise grouping of provinces, so the answer is the three-tier federation with grouping.
Source: Spectrum, Modern India

123. Match the following leaders with the places they led during the 1857 Revolt:
1. Begum Hazrat Mahal — Lucknow
2. Kunwar Singh — Arrah (Bihar)
3. Tantia Tope — Kanpur/Kalpi
4. Mangal Pandey — Jhansi
Which of the above are correctly matched?
(a) 1, 2, 3 and 4 (b) 1 and 2 only (c) 2, 3 and 4 only (d) 1, 2 and 3 only
Answer: (d)
Solution: The tempting wrong option includes Mangal Pandey with Jhansi, but Mangal Pandey's action was at Barrackpore (March 1857), while Jhansi was led by Rani Lakshmibai. The first three pairs are correct, so the answer is 1, 2 and 3 only.
Source: NCERT Class 8 History (Our Pasts III)

124. Which of the following pairs is NOT correctly matched?
(a) Hunter Commission — Jallianwala Bagh inquiry (b) Nehru Report — 1928 (c) Cripps Mission — 1940 (d) Wavell Plan — Shimla Conference, 1945
Answer: (c)
Solution: The Hunter Commission pair is tempting to doubt, but it did inquire into the Jallianwala Bagh massacre. The Cripps Mission came in March 1942, not 1940 (1940 saw the August Offer), so the incorrectly matched pair is the Cripps Mission — 1940.

125. Consider the following statements about the Indian National Army (INA):
1. It was first organised by Mohan Singh.
2. Subhas Chandra Bose took command of it in 1943.
3. Its provisional government, Azad Hind, was proclaimed at Singapore.
Which of the statements given above are correct?
(a) 1, 2 and 3 (b) 2 and 3 only (c) 1 and 3 only (d) 1 and 2 only
Answer: (a)
Solution: The tempting wrong options drop one statement, but all three are correct: Captain Mohan Singh formed the first INA in 1942, Bose assumed leadership in 1943, and the Azad Hind Government was proclaimed in Singapore on 21 October 1943, so the answer is 1, 2 and 3.
Source: Spectrum, Modern India
