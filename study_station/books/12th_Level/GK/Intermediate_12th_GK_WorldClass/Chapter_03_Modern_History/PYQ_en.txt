# 📊 PYQ Analysis — Modern Indian History (Pattern & Trap Study)

**How to use this section:** We do not invent year-wise counts or percentages — no verified dataset exists for that. Instead, this section tells you *how* examiners actually ask questions from Modern Indian History: which sub-topics return again and again, which traps they set, and how to decide in seconds whether to attempt or skip.

---

## 1. Where the Questions Come From (Sub-topic Weightage in Words)

Across SSC (CGL/CHSL/GD), Banking (IBPS/SBI), Railways (NTPC/Group D) and State PCS prelims, Modern Indian History questions cluster around a small set of recurring zones. Roughly in order of how often they appear:

**Zone A — The Freedom Struggle, 1857–1947 (heaviest zone)**
- Revolt of 1857: causes, leaders by centre (Rani Lakshmibai–Jhansi, Begum Hazrat Mahal–Lucknow, Nana Saheb–Kanpur, Kunwar Singh–Bihar, Mangal Pandey–Barrackpore), why it failed, its nature.
- Indian National Congress: founding (1885, A.O. Hume, W.C. Bonnerjee as first president, Bombay session), Moderates vs Extremists, Surat Split (1907).
- Partition of Bengal (1905, Curzon) and Swadeshi Movement.
- Gandhi era: Champaran (1917), Kheda and Ahmedabad (1918), Rowlatt Act & Jallianwala Bagh (1919), Non-Cooperation (1920–22), Chauri Chaura (1922), Civil Disobedience & Dandi March (1930), Round Table Conferences, Quit India (1942).
- Revolutionary movement: Bhagat Singh, Chandrashekhar Azad, HSRA, Kakori (1925), Chittagong Armoury Raid (1930, Surya Sen).

**Zone B — Governor-Generals, Viceroys and their Acts**
- Matching events/reforms to the right office-holder: Cornwallis (Permanent Settlement 1793), Wellesley (Subsidiary Alliance), Dalhousie (Doctrine of Lapse, first railway 1853, telegraph), Canning (first Viceroy, 1858 Act), Lytton (Vernacular Press Act 1878, Arms Act), Ripon (Local Self-Government 1882, Ilbert Bill), Curzon (Bengal Partition 1905), Mountbatten (June 3 Plan, Independence).
- Key Acts: Regulating Act 1773, Pitt's India Act 1784, Charter Acts (1813, 1833, 1853), Government of India Acts 1858/1909/1919/1935, Indian Independence Act 1947.

**Zone C — Socio-Religious Reform Movements**
- Brahmo Samaj (Raja Rammohan Roy, 1828), Arya Samaj (Dayananda Saraswati, 1875), Ramakrishna Mission (Vivekananda, 1897), Aligarh Movement (Sir Syed Ahmed Khan), Satyashodhak Samaj (Jyotiba Phule, 1873), Theosophical Society (Blavatsky & Olcott, 1875; Annie Besant in India), Young Bengal (Derozio), Prarthana Samaj (Atmaram Pandurang, 1867).
- Sati abolition (1829, Bentinck + Rammohan Roy), Widow Remarriage Act 1856 (Ishwar Chandra Vidyasagar's campaign).

**Zone D — Press, Literature and Organisations**
- Newspapers and founders: Bengal Gazette (Hicky, 1780 — first newspaper in India), Sambad Kaumudi (Rammohan Roy), Kesari & Maratha (Tilak), Young India & Harijan (Gandhi), The Hindu, Amrita Bazar Patrika.
- Books: *Anandamath* (Bankim Chandra — "Vande Mataram"), *Poverty and Un-British Rule in India* (Dadabhai Naoroji — Drain Theory), *Gitanjali* (Tagore), *Hind Swaraj* (Gandhi, 1909), *Discovery of India* (Nehru).

**Zone E — Constitutional & Communal Milestones**
- Muslim League (1906, Dhaka), Hindu Mahasabha, Khilafat Movement, Simon Commission (1928), Nehru Report (1928), Lahore Session 1929 (Purna Swaraj), Communal Award (1932) & Poona Pact (1932), Cripps Mission (1942), Cabinet Mission (1946), Constituent Assembly (first meeting 9 Dec 1946), Mountbatten Plan, Radcliffe Line.

**Zone F — Pre-1857 British Expansion (lighter but regular)**
- Battles: Plassey (1757), Buxar (1764), Anglo-Mysore wars (Haidar Ali, Tipu Sultan), Anglo-Maratha wars, Anglo-Sikh wars (Ranjit Singh, annexation 1849).
- Land revenue systems: Permanent Settlement, Ryotwari (Munro, Madras/Bombay), Mahalwari (North-West).

## 2. Exam-wise Tendencies

- **SSC (CGL/CHSL/GD/MTS):** Direct factual one-liners — "Who founded X?", "In which year did Y happen?", matching leaders to 1857 centres, newspapers to founders. Speed matters; traps are usually *Almost Correct* options.
- **Railways (NTPC/Group D):** Very similar to SSC but even more factual; dates and "firsts" (first railway line, first newspaper, first Congress president) are favourites.
- **Banking (IBPS/SBI):** Fewer history questions overall; when they appear, they are static-GK style — Gandhi-era movements, important sessions, books and slogans.
- **State PCS prelims:** Slightly deeper — statement-based questions, chronology arrangement, Acts and their provisions, reform movements with regional flavour (e.g., Phule in Maharashtra PCS, reformers of the concerned state).

## 3. The Examiner's Traps (with Examples)

**Trap 1 — Almost Correct (लगभग-सही):** Three options are plainly wrong; the fourth is wrong by one small detail.
*Example:* "The Swadeshi Movement was launched after the Partition of Bengal in — (a) 1903 (b) 1904 (c) 1905 (d) 1907." All are years of real events; only 1905 is correct. The trap works if you remember "early 1900s" vaguely.

**Trap 2 — Chronological Confusion:** Events within 2–3 years of each other are shuffled.
*Example:* Champaran (1917) vs Kheda (1918) vs Ahmedabad mill strike (1918) vs Rowlatt Satyagraha (1919). Or: Non-Cooperation (1920) → Chauri Chaura (1922) → Swaraj Party (1923) → Simon Commission (1928). Examiners love "Arrange in chronological order" here.

**Trap 3 — Similar Names:** Lytton vs Ripon (both 1870s–80s Viceroys with famous press-related measures — Vernacular Press Act by Lytton, its repeal by Ripon); Wavell vs Mountbatten; Arya Samaj vs Brahmo Samaj vs Prarthana Samaj; Khudai Khidmatgar (Khan Abdul Ghaffar Khan) vs other organisations.

**Trap 4 — Negative Phrasing:** "Which of the following was NOT a cause of the Revolt of 1857?" / "Which movement did Gandhi NOT lead?" Under exam pressure, candidates misread NOT and pick the first true statement. Train yourself to circle the NOT before reading options.

**Trap 5 — Person–Work Mismatch:** Correct person, wrong achievement. "Dadabhai Naoroji is known for — (a) founding the Muslim League (b) the Drain of Wealth theory (c) the Aligarh Movement (d) the Poona Pact." Every option is a real historical item attached to the wrong man.

**Trap 6 — Session/Place Swap:** Congress sessions and their venues/presidents: Surat 1907 (split), Lucknow 1916 (Lucknow Pact, reunion of Moderates–Extremists), Lahore 1929 (Purna Swaraj, Nehru president), Karachi 1931 (resolution on Fundamental Rights, Vallabhbhai Patel president). Swapping 1929 and 1931 is a classic.

## 4. Top 10 High-Yield PYQ-Style Questions (with Trap Tags)

**Q1.** Who was the first President of the Indian National Congress?
(a) A.O. Hume (b) W.C. Bonnerjee (c) Dadabhai Naoroji (d) Surendranath Banerjee
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** W.C. Bonnerjee presided over the first session (Bombay, 1885). Trap: A.O. Hume *founded* the Congress but never presided over its first session — founder vs president is the classic swap. **Trap type: Almost Correct.**

**Q2.** The Dandi March (Salt Satyagraha) began in which year?
(a) 1928 (b) 1929 (c) 1930 (d) 1932
**Answer: (c)**
**Solution:** Gandhi began the march from Sabarmati Ashram on 12 March 1930, reaching Dandi on 6 April 1930. Trap: 1929 (Lahore Purna Swaraj session) and 1932 (Poona Pact) are neighbouring Gandhi-era years. **Trap type: Chronological Confusion.**

**Q3.** Which of the following was NOT associated with the Revolt of 1857?
(a) Rani Lakshmibai (b) Kunwar Singh (c) Begum Hazrat Mahal (d) Tipu Sultan
**Answer: (d)**
**Solution:** Tipu Sultan died in 1799 (Fourth Anglo-Mysore War), decades before 1857. The other three led the revolt at Jhansi, Bihar (Jagdishpur) and Lucknow respectively. **Trap type: Negative Phrasing + Chronological Confusion.**

**Q4.** The Vernacular Press Act of 1878 was passed during the viceroyalty of:
(a) Lord Ripon (b) Lord Lytton (c) Lord Canning (d) Lord Curzon
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** Lytton passed it to gag the Indian-language press; Ripon *repealed* it in 1882. The Lytton–Ripon pair is the examiner's favourite name-swap. **Trap type: Similar Names.**

**Q5.** Who gave the slogan "Swaraj is my birthright and I shall have it"?
(a) Bipin Chandra Pal (b) Bal Gangadhar Tilak (c) Lala Lajpat Rai (d) Aurobindo Ghosh
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** Tilak. All four are Extremist leaders (Lal-Bal-Pal + Aurobindo), so the options feel interchangeable — that is the trap. **Trap type: Almost Correct.**

**Q6.** The Poona Pact (1932) was an agreement between:
(a) Gandhi and Irwin (b) Gandhi and Ambedkar (c) Nehru and Jinnah (d) Congress and the Muslim League
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** Signed after Gandhi's fast against the Communal Award's separate electorates for Depressed Classes; it provided reserved seats instead. Trap: Gandhi–Irwin Pact (1931) is one year earlier and also a "Gandhi pact." **Trap type: Similar Names / Chronological Confusion.**

**Q7.** Arrange in chronological order: (i) Chauri Chaura (ii) Dandi March (iii) Jallianwala Bagh (iv) Quit India Movement
(a) iii, i, ii, iv (b) i, iii, ii, iv (c) iii, ii, i, iv (d) i, ii, iii, iv
**Answer: (a)**
**Solution:** Jallianwala Bagh (1919) → Chauri Chaura (1922) → Dandi March (1930) → Quit India (1942). **Trap type: Chronological Confusion.**

**Q8.** The first railway line in India was laid (1853) during the tenure of:
(a) Lord Bentinck (b) Lord Dalhousie (c) Lord Canning (d) Lord Hardinge
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** Bombay to Thane, 1853, under Dalhousie — who also gave the telegraph and the Doctrine of Lapse. **Trap type: Almost Correct (all are plausible Governor-Generals).**

**Q9.** "Vande Mataram" was taken from which novel?
(a) *Gitanjali* (b) *Anandamath* (c) *Hind Swaraj* (d) *Nil Darpan*
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** Bankim Chandra Chatterjee's *Anandamath* (1882), set against the Sanyasi Rebellion. *Nil Darpan* (Dinabandhu Mitra) is about indigo planters — a tempting distractor. **Trap type: Person–Work Mismatch.**

**Q10.** The Cabinet Mission came to India in:
(a) 1942 (b) 1945 (c) 1946 (d) 1947
**Answer: (c)**
**Solution:** Cabinet Mission (1946) proposed a federation and the Constituent Assembly framework; Cripps Mission was 1942, Mountbatten Plan 1947. Three missions in five years — examiners exploit exactly this crowding. **Trap type: Chronological Confusion.**

## 5. The 15/45-Second Rule Applied

**The rule:** If a question is a direct fact you have revised (a date, a founder, a slogan), answer within **15 seconds** and move on. If it needs reasoning (chronology arrangement, statement-based, negative phrasing), give it up to **45 seconds** — then mark and move. Never cross 45 seconds on a static GK question; the fact either is or isn't in your memory.

**Applied to Q1 (first Congress president):** This is a pure recall item. You either know Bonnerjee or you don't. Decide in 15 seconds. Do not spend time re-reading A.O. Hume's option — hesitation here only invites the founder/president trap.

**Applied to Q7 (chronology of four events):** This legitimately needs the full 45 seconds. Method: fix the two anchors you are surest of — Jallianwala Bagh (1919, earliest) and Quit India (1942, latest). That alone eliminates options (b) and (d). Then compare Chauri Chaura (1922) vs Dandi (1930): Chauri Chaura came first → (a). Anchoring first and eliminating is faster than recalling all four dates.

**Skip signal:** If a question asks about a person, session or Act you have genuinely never seen (e.g., an obscure reformer or a minor Charter Act provision), skip immediately at the 5-second mark. Static GK has no partial reasoning rescue — a blind guess with negative marking costs more than a skip.

## 6. One-Page Revision Cues from PYQ Patterns

- Memorise 1857 leaders **with their centres as pairs**, never as a bare list.
- Learn Gandhi-era events as a **dated chain**: 1915 (return) → 1917 Champaran → 1919 Jallianwala → 1920 NCO → 1922 Chauri Chaura → 1930 Dandi → 1932 Poona Pact → 1942 Quit India.
- Pair every Viceroy with **one signature event and one "undo"** (Lytton passed the Vernacular Press Act; Ripon repealed it).
- For every reform organisation, lock the **founder + year + place** triple.
- Practise reading "NOT/EXCEPT" questions by physically underlining the negative word first.
- For missions and pacts (Cripps 1942, Wavell Plan 1945, Cabinet Mission 1946, Mountbatten Plan 1947), revise them as one cluster — examiners always test them together.
