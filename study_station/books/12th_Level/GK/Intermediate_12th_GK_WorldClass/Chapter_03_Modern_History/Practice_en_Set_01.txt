1. The Battle of Plassey was fought in which year?
(a) 1772 (b) 1764 (c) 1757 (d) 1782
Answer: (c)
Solution: Many students confuse Plassey with the Battle of Buxar, which was fought in 1764 and is the tempting wrong option. Plassey, where Robert Clive defeated Siraj-ud-Daulah, took place on 23 June 1757, so the answer is 1757.
Source: NCERT Class 8 History (Our Pasts III)

2. Who was the first Governor-General of Bengal?
(a) Lord Cornwallis (b) Lord Wellesley (c) Warren Hastings (d) Lord William Bentinck
Answer: (c)
Solution: Lord William Bentinck is the trap — he was the first Governor-General of India (1833), not of Bengal. The Regulating Act of 1773 made Warren Hastings the first Governor-General of Bengal, so the answer is Warren Hastings.
Source: NCERT Class 8 History (Our Pasts III)

3. The Revolt of 1857 began at which place?
(a) Delhi (b) Kanpur (c) Meerut (d) Lucknow
Answer: (c)
Solution: Delhi is the tempting wrong answer because the rebels marched there and proclaimed Bahadur Shah Zafar their leader. The revolt actually began with the sepoy mutiny at Meerut on 10 May 1857, so the answer is Meerut.
Source: NCERT Class 8 History (Our Pasts III)

4. Who founded the Brahmo Samaj in 1828?
(a) Raja Rammohan Roy (b) Swami Vivekananda (c) Ishwar Chandra Vidyasagar (d) Swami Dayananda Saraswati
Answer: (a)
Solution: Swami Dayananda Saraswati is the trap — he founded the Arya Samaj in 1875, not the Brahmo Samaj. Raja Rammohan Roy founded the Brahmo Samaj in 1828, so the answer is Raja Rammohan Roy.
Source: NCERT Class 8 History (Our Pasts III)

5. The Indian National Congress was founded in which year?
(a) 1885 (b) 1857 (c) 1905 (d) 1915
Answer: (a)
Solution: 1905 is the tempting wrong option — that was the year of the Partition of Bengal and the Swadeshi Movement. The INC was founded in 1885, with its first session in Bombay under W.C. Bonnerjee, so the answer is 1885.

6. Who was the Viceroy of India during the Partition of Bengal in 1905?
(a) Lord Lytton (b) Lord Minto (c) Lord Hardinge (d) Lord Curzon
Answer: (d)
Solution: Lord Hardinge is the trap — he was Viceroy when the partition was annulled in 1911. The Partition of Bengal in 1905 was carried out by Lord Curzon, so the answer is Lord Curzon.

7. The Jallianwala Bagh massacre took place in which year?
(a) 1917 (b) 1918 (c) 1920 (d) 1919
Answer: (d)
Solution: 1920 is tempting because the Non-Cooperation Movement, launched partly in response to the massacre, began that year. The massacre itself occurred on 13 April 1919 in Amritsar, so the answer is 1919.

8. Who gave the slogan "Do or Die"?
(a) Subhas Chandra Bose (b) Mahatma Gandhi (c) Jawaharlal Nehru (d) Bhagat Singh
Answer: (b)
Solution: Subhas Chandra Bose is the trap — his famous slogans were "Give me blood, and I will give you freedom" and "Jai Hind". "Do or Die" was given by Mahatma Gandhi during the Quit India Movement of 1942, so the answer is Mahatma Gandhi.

9. The Dandi March (Salt Satyagraha) was started in which year?
(a) 1930 (b) 1928 (c) 1932 (d) 1935
Answer: (a)
Solution: 1932 is tempting because Gandhi was imprisoned and undertook a fast against the Communal Award that year. The Dandi March began on 12 March 1930 from Sabarmati Ashram, so the answer is 1930.

10. Who was the first President of the Indian National Congress?
(a) A.O. Hume (b) Dadabhai Naoroji (c) W.C. Bonnerjee (d) Surendranath Banerjee
Answer: (c)
Solution: A.O. Hume is the classic trap — he founded the Congress but was its General Secretary, not its first President. W.C. Bonnerjee presided over the first session at Bombay in 1885, so the answer is W.C. Bonnerjee.

11. The Swadeshi Movement was launched in response to which event?
(a) Jallianwala Bagh massacre (b) Simon Commission (c) Rowlatt Act (d) Partition of Bengal
Answer: (d)
Solution: The Rowlatt Act is tempting because it too sparked mass protest, but it led to the 1919 satyagraha, not Swadeshi. The Swadeshi Movement of 1905 was a direct response to the Partition of Bengal, so the answer is Partition of Bengal.

12. Who founded the Arya Samaj?
(a) Swami Dayananda Saraswati (b) Raja Rammohan Roy (c) Swami Vivekananda (d) Keshab Chandra Sen
Answer: (a)
Solution: Raja Rammohan Roy is the trap because he is associated with the similarly named Brahmo Samaj. Swami Dayananda Saraswati founded the Arya Samaj in 1875 with the call "Back to the Vedas", so the answer is Swami Dayananda Saraswati.

13. The Simon Commission was boycotted because —
(a) it proposed the partition of India (b) it had no Indian member (c) it recommended separate electorates (d) it imposed new taxes
Answer: (b)
Solution: Separate electorates is the tempting wrong option — that was the feature of the Morley-Minto Reforms of 1909. The Simon Commission (1928) was boycotted because it was an all-white commission with no Indian member, so the answer is "it had no Indian member".
Source: NCERT Class 8 History (Our Pasts III)

14. Who was known as the "Iron Man of India"?
(a) Jawaharlal Nehru (b) Sardar Vallabhbhai Patel (c) Bal Gangadhar Tilak (d) Lala Lajpat Rai
Answer: (b)
Solution: Lala Lajpat Rai is the trap due to his similar-sounding title "Punjab Kesari" (Lion of Punjab). Sardar Vallabhbhai Patel, who integrated the princely states, is called the Iron Man of India, so the answer is Sardar Vallabhbhai Patel.

(a) 1940 (b) 1945 (c) 1942 (d) 1947
Answer: (c)
Solution: 1940 is tempting because the August Offer and the Individual Satyagraha occurred that year. The Quit India Movement was launched on 8 August 1942 with the "Do or Die" call, so the answer is 1942.

16. Who established the Ramakrishna Mission?

Answer: (a)
Solution: Ramakrishna Paramahamsa is the trap — the Mission is named after him, but he did not found it. His disciple Swami Vivekananda established the Ramakrishna Mission in 1897, so the answer is Swami Vivekananda.

17. The Doctrine of Lapse was introduced by —
(a) Lord Canning (b) Lord Dalhousie (c) Lord Wellesley (d) Lord Hastings
Answer: (b)
Solution: Lord Wellesley is the tempting wrong option — he introduced the Subsidiary Alliance, another expansionist device. The Doctrine of Lapse, under which states without a natural heir were annexed, was applied by Lord Dalhousie, so the answer is Lord Dalhousie.

Answer: (a)
Solution: Tantia Tope is the trap — he was Nana Saheb's close associate and general at Kanpur, but not the leader. Nana Saheb led the revolt at Kanpur, so the answer is Nana Saheb.

19. The first session of the Indian National Congress was held at —
(a) Bombay (b) Calcutta (c) Madras (d) Lahore
Answer: (a)
Solution: Calcutta is tempting because the second session (1886) was held there and it was then the capital of British India. The first session in December 1885 was held at Bombay (Gokuldas Tejpal Sanskrit College), so the answer is Bombay.

21. Consider the following events: 1. Battle of Buxar 2. Regulating Act 3. Battle of Plassey 4. Pitt's India Act. Which is the correct chronological order?
(a) 1-2-3-4 (b) 1-3-2-4 (c) 3-2-1-4 (d) 3-1-2-4
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is placing Buxar before Plassey — Plassey (1757) came first, then Buxar (1764). The Regulating Act followed in 1773 and Pitt's India Act in 1784, so the answer is 3-1-2-4.
Source: NCERT Class 8 History (Our Pasts III)

22. Which of the following statements about the Government of India Act 1935 is correct?
(a) It introduced dyarchy in the provinces (b) It ended the East India Company's rule (c) It introduced separate electorates for Muslims (d) It provided for an All-India Federation and provincial autonomy
Answer: (d)
Solution: Dyarchy in the provinces is the trap — that was introduced by the Government of India Act 1919, and the 1935 Act actually abolished it. The 1935 Act provided for an All-India Federation and provincial autonomy, so the answer is "It provided for an All-India Federation and provincial autonomy".
Source: NCERT Class 12 History (Themes in Indian History III)

23. Match the leaders with the places of their role in the 1857 revolt: 1. Rani Lakshmibai — Jhansi 2. Kunwar Singh — Arrah (Bihar) 3. Begum Hazrat Mahal — Lucknow. Which of the above are correctly matched?
(a) 1 and 2 only (b) 1, 2 and 3 (c) 1 and 3 only (d) 2 and 3 only
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is assuming one pairing must be wrong, as examiners often swap Jhansi with Gwalior — but Rani Lakshmibai led at Jhansi and only later died near Gwalior. All three pairings are historically correct, so the answer is "1, 2 and 3".
Source: NCERT Class 8 History (Our Pasts III)

24. The Lucknow Pact of 1916 was an agreement between —
(a) the British and the Muslim League (b) the Moderates and the British (c) the Congress and the Khilafat Committee (d) the Congress and the Muslim League
Answer: (d)
Solution: The Congress–Khilafat Committee option is the trap — that cooperation came later, during the Non-Cooperation Movement (1920–22). The Lucknow Pact of 1916 was signed between the Congress and the Muslim League, and also reunited the Moderates and Extremists, so the answer is "the Congress and the Muslim League".
Source: NCERT Class 12 History (Themes in Indian History III)

25. Which of the following pairs is NOT correctly matched?
(a) Permanent Settlement — Lord Cornwallis (b) Ryotwari System — Thomas Munro (c) Subsidiary Alliance — Lord Dalhousie (d) Mahalwari System — Holt Mackenzie
Answer: (c)
Solution: The first three pairs are all correct, which tempts students to doubt one of them; the real error lies in the last pair. The Subsidiary Alliance was introduced by Lord Wellesley (1798), while Dalhousie is associated with the Doctrine of Lapse, so the answer is "Subsidiary Alliance — Lord Dalhousie".
Source: NCERT Class 8 History (Our Pasts III)
