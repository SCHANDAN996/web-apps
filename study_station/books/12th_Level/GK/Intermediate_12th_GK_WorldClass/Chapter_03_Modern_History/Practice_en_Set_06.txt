126. Consider the following statements about the Regulating Act of 1773:
1. It designated the Governor of Bengal as the Governor-General of Bengal.
2. It established a Supreme Court at Calcutta in 1774.
3. It subordinated the Governors of Bombay and Madras to the Governor-General in all matters without exception.
Which of the statements given above is/are correct?
(a) 1 and 2 only (b) 2 and 3 only (c) 1 and 3 only (d) 1, 2 and 3
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is statement 3, which looks plausible but is wrong: the presidencies were subordinated only in matters of war and peace, not in all matters without exception. Statements 1 and 2 are correct — Warren Hastings became the first Governor-General of Bengal and the Supreme Court at Calcutta was established in 1774, so the answer is 1 and 2 only.
Source: NCERT Class 8 History (Our Pasts III)

127. The Treaty of Bassein (1802), which converted the Peshwa into a subsidiary ally of the British, was signed between the East India Company and:
(a) Balaji Vishwanath (b) Baji Rao I (c) Baji Rao II (d) Madhavrao II
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is the similar name Baji Rao I, the famous expansionist Peshwa of the early 18th century; the treaty was signed by his namesake Baji Rao II, the last Peshwa, after his defeat at Poona by the Holkars. This treaty directly provoked the Second Anglo-Maratha War, so the answer is Baji Rao II.
Source: NCERT Class 8 History (Our Pasts III)

4. Foundation of the Theosophical Society in India (Adyar)
(a) 1, 2, 3, 4 (b) 1, 4, 2, 3 (c) 2, 1, 4, 3 (d) 1, 2, 4, 3

Solution: The trap is that the Raleigh Commission sounds like a police body, but it (1902) dealt with universities and led to the Indian Universities Act, 1904; police reforms were examined by the Fraser Commission (1902–03). (a) 1, 2 and 3 (b) 2 and 3 only (c) 1 and 2 only (d) 1 only

141. The Poona Pact (1932) was an agreement between Mahatma Gandhi and B.R. Ambedkar concerning:
(a) Separate electorates for Muslims (b) Partition of Bengal (c) Abolition of untouchability by law (d) Reserved seats for Depressed Classes within the general electorate
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is the separate-electorates option: the Communal Award had granted separate electorates to the Depressed Classes, which Gandhi opposed with a fast; the Poona Pact replaced this with reserved seats within joint electorates. It was not about Muslims or Bengal, so the answer is reserved seats for Depressed Classes within the general electorate.
