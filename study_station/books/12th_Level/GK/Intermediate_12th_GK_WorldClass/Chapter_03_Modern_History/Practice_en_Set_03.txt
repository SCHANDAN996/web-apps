51. The Regulating Act of 1773 designated the Governor of Bengal as the Governor-General of Bengal. Who was the first to hold this post?
(a) Lord Cornwallis (b) Lord Wellesley (c) Robert Clive (d) Warren Hastings
Answer: (d)
Solution: Robert Clive is the tempting wrong choice because he won Plassey and served as Governor of Bengal, but he never held the new title created by the Regulating Act. The Act of 1773 made Warren Hastings the first Governor-General of Bengal, so the answer is Warren Hastings.
Source: NCERT Class 8 History (Our Pasts III)

52. The Pitt's India Act of 1784 distinguished between the commercial and political functions of the East India Company by creating which body to control political affairs?
(a) Court of Directors (b) Board of Control (c) Council of India (d) India Office
Answer: (b)
Solution: The Court of Directors is the trap — it existed earlier and managed commercial affairs, not political control. Pitt's India Act created the Board of Control to supervise political matters, establishing dual control, so the answer is Board of Control.
Source: NCERT Class 8 History (Our Pasts III)

53. Which Governor-General introduced the Permanent Settlement in Bengal in 1793?
(a) Lord Cornwallis (b) Lord Wellesley (c) Warren Hastings (d) Lord Hastings
Answer: (a)
Solution: Lord Hastings is a near-name trap — he was Governor-General later (1813–1823) and is associated with the Ryotwari settlement's approval in Madras, not Bengal's Permanent Settlement. The Permanent Settlement of 1793 was introduced by Lord Cornwallis, so the answer is Lord Cornwallis.

54. The Ryotwari system of land revenue was first introduced by Thomas Munro in which region?
(a) Bengal (b) Punjab (c) Madras (d) Bombay
Answer: (c)
Solution: Bombay is the trap because the Ryotwari system was later extended there, but it was first tried in Madras. Thomas Munro introduced the Ryotwari system in the Madras Presidency around 1820, settling revenue directly with the cultivators, so the answer is Madras.

55. Arrange the following events in correct chronological order: 1. Battle of Buxar 2. Battle of Plassey 3. Third Battle of Panipat 4. First Anglo-Mysore War
(a) 3, 2, 1, 4 (b) 2, 3, 1, 4 (c) 2, 1, 3, 4 (d) 3, 1, 2, 4
Answer: (b)
Solution: The chronological trap is placing Panipat (1761) after Plassey — it actually occurred between Plassey (1757) and Buxar (1764). The correct order is Plassey 1757, Panipat 1761, Buxar 1764, First Anglo-Mysore War 1767–69, so the answer is 2, 3, 1, 4.

56. The Doctrine of Lapse, under which states without a natural male heir were annexed, is most associated with which Governor-General?
(a) Lord Wellesley (b) Lord Dalhousie (c) Lord Canning (d) Lord Bentinck
Answer: (b)
Solution: Lord Wellesley is the trap because he expanded British territory aggressively, but through the Subsidiary Alliance, not the Doctrine of Lapse. Satara, Jhansi and Nagpur were annexed under Dalhousie's Doctrine of Lapse, so the answer is Lord Dalhousie.

57. Who was the first Indian to qualify for the Indian Civil Service, in 1869?
(a) Surendranath Banerjee (b) Romesh Chandra Dutt (c) Satyendranath Tagore (d) Behramji Malabari
Answer: (c)
Solution: Surendranath Banerjee is the tempting wrong answer because he is famous for clearing the ICS exam and his later dismissal and political career, but he qualified in 1871, after Tagore. Satyendranath Tagore, elder brother of Rabindranath Tagore, was the first Indian to join the ICS in 1869, so the answer is Satyendranath Tagore.

58. The Vernacular Press Act of 1878, which curbed the freedom of Indian-language newspapers, was enacted during the viceroyalty of:
(a) Lord Lytton (b) Lord Ripon (c) Lord Curzon (d) Lord Mayo
Answer: (a)
Solution: Lord Ripon is the trap because press laws are strongly linked to him — but he repealed the Vernacular Press Act in 1882, not enacted it. The Act was passed under Lord Lytton in 1878, so the answer is Lord Lytton.

59. The Ilbert Bill controversy of 1883 occurred during the tenure of which Viceroy?
(a) Lord Lytton (b) Lord Lansdowne (c) Lord Dufferin (d) Lord Ripon
Answer: (d)
Solution: Lord Lytton is the trap since his tenure was full of controversies (Vernacular Press Act, Arms Act), but the Ilbert Bill — allowing Indian judges to try European offenders — came under Lord Ripon, whose liberal measures angered Europeans, so the answer is Lord Ripon.

60. The partition of Bengal in 1905 was officially justified by the British on the grounds of:
(a) Linguistic reorganization (b) Hindu-Muslim division (c) Economic development (d) Administrative efficiency
Answer: (d)
Solution: Hindu-Muslim division is the tempting trap — it was the real motive critics alleged and the practical effect, but Curzon's official justification was administrative efficiency for the large province. The stated reason was administrative convenience, so the answer is Administrative efficiency.

(a) Madras Marina (b) Bombay's Girgaum (c) Calcutta Town Hall (d) Lahore's Bradlaugh Hall

63. The Muslim League was founded in 1906 at:
(a) Lucknow (b) Dhaka (c) Karachi (d) Aligarh
Answer: (b)
Solution: Aligarh is the trap because it was the centre of Sir Syed Ahmad Khan's movement and League leadership, but the founding session was elsewhere. The All-India Muslim League was founded at Dhaka in December 1906 during the All India Muhammadan Educational Conference, so the answer is Dhaka.

Answer: (c)
Solution: Tilak and Besant are traps because their Home Rule Leagues dominated 1916 politics, but neither presided over the Lucknow session. Ambica Charan Mazumdar presided over the 1916 Lucknow session where the Pact was signed, so the answer is Ambica Charan Mazumdar.

65. The Jallianwala Bagh massacre of 13 April 1919 occurred on the day of which festival?
(a) Lohri (b) Holi (c) Diwali (d) Baisakhi
Answer: (d)
Solution: Holi is a plausible trap as a spring festival, but the massacre occurred during the harvest festival of Punjab. The crowd had gathered at Amritsar on Baisakhi, 13 April 1919, when General Dyer ordered firing, so the answer is Baisakhi.

66. The Hunter Commission was appointed to inquire into which event?
(a) Jallianwala Bagh massacre (b) Chauri Chaura incident (c) Kakori conspiracy (d) Partition of Bengal
Answer: (a)
Solution: Chauri Chaura is the trap because it too involved violence and an official response, but it led to Gandhi withdrawing Non-Cooperation, not the Hunter Commission. The Hunter (Disorders Inquiry) Committee of 1919 investigated the Jallianwala Bagh massacre, so the answer is Jallianwala Bagh massacre.

67. Gandhi withdrew the Non-Cooperation Movement in February 1922 after which incident?
(a) Chauri Chaura (b) Bardoli satyagraha (c) Moplah rebellion (d) Jallianwala Bagh
Answer: (a)
Solution: Bardoli is the trap — it was a later satyagraha (1928) led by Vallabhbhai Patel, not the cause of withdrawal. After a mob burnt a police station killing 22 policemen at Chauri Chaura on 5 February 1922, Gandhi suspended the movement, so the answer is Chauri Chaura.

(a) Tilak and Besant (b) Gandhi and Patel (c) Nehru and Bose (d) Motilal Nehru and C. R. Das

69. The Simon Commission (1928) was boycotted in India primarily because:
(a) It proposed partition of India (b) It supported separate electorates (c) It recommended abolition of dyarchy (d) It had no Indian member
Answer: (d)
Solution: Abolition of dyarchy is the trap — the Commission's eventual report did recommend ending dyarchy, but that was not the reason for the boycott. The all-white commission with no Indian member insulted Indian aspirations, sparking the "Simon Go Back" protests, so the answer is It had no Indian member.

70. Lala Lajpat Rai died following injuries sustained during a lathi charge while protesting against:
(a) The Rowlatt Act (b) The Simon Commission (c) The Salt Tax (d) The Communal Award
Answer: (b)
Solution: The Rowlatt Act is the trap because Lajpat Rai opposed repressive laws, but his fatal protest was a decade later. Leading the anti-Simon demonstration in Lahore in October 1928, he was injured in a lathi charge and died in November 1928, so the answer is The Simon Commission.

71. The Purna Swaraj resolution was adopted at which session of the Indian National Congress?
(a) Lahore 1929 (b) Tripuri 1939 (c) Karachi 1931 (d) Calcutta 1928
Answer: (a)
Solution: Karachi 1931 is the trap — it endorsed the Gandhi-Irwin Pact and passed the famous resolution on Fundamental Rights, but not Purna Swaraj. The Lahore session of December 1929, presided by Jawaharlal Nehru, declared Purna Swaraj as the goal, so the answer is Lahore 1929.

72. The Dandi March of 1930 began from Sabarmati Ashram on 12 March and ended on 6 April. Approximately how long was the march route?
(a) About 50 miles (b) About 240 miles (c) About 400 miles (d) About 100 miles
Answer: (b)
Solution: 100 miles is a tempting underestimate because the march is often described as a short coastal walk, but it covered far more. Gandhi and 78 followers walked roughly 240 miles (about 385 km) from Sabarmati to Dandi in 24 days, so the answer is About 240 miles.

73. The Communal Award of 1932, which granted separate electorates to the Depressed Classes, was announced by:
(a) Winston Churchill (b) Stanley Baldwin (c) Ramsay MacDonald (d) Clement Attlee
Answer: (c)
Solution: Attlee is the trap because he was Prime Minister during the 1947 transfer of power, but he had no role in 1932. British Prime Minister Ramsay MacDonald announced the Communal Award in August 1932, against which Gandhi fasted in Yerwada jail, so the answer is Ramsay MacDonald.

74. The Poona Pact of 1932 was an agreement between Gandhi and B. R. Ambedkar that provided for:
(a) Reserved seats for Depressed Classes within joint electorates (b) Separate electorates for Depressed Classes (c) Abolition of untouchability by law (d) Separate provinces for Depressed Classes
Answer: (a)
Solution: Separate electorates is the trap — that was the Communal Award's provision which Gandhi opposed with his fast; the Pact replaced it. The Poona Pact provided reserved seats for the Depressed Classes within joint electorates in provincial legislatures, so the answer is Reserved seats for Depressed Classes within joint electorates.
Source: NCERT Class 8 History (Our Pasts III)

75. The Government of India Act 1935 introduced dyarchy at which level?
(a) Provinces (b) Municipalities (c) Centre (d) Districts
Answer: (c)
Solution: Provinces is the classic trap — dyarchy at the provincial level was introduced earlier by the Government of India Act 1919, and the 1935 Act abolished it there by granting provincial autonomy. The 1935 Act proposed dyarchy at the Centre (which never came into operation), so the answer is Centre.
