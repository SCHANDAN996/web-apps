Card 1
Front: Which revolution in India is associated with milk production?
Back: The White Revolution (Operation Flood). India is the largest producer of milk in the world.

Card 2
Front: Which Indian state is the largest producer of wheat?
Back: Uttar Pradesh.

Card 3
Front: Which region is known as the "rice bowl of India"?
Back: Andhra Pradesh (the Krishna–Godavari delta region).

Card 4
Front: Which state is known as the "granary of India"?
Back: Punjab.

Card 5
Front: Which state is known as the "sugar bowl of India"?
Back: Uttar Pradesh. (Note: Brazil, not India, is the world's largest sugarcane producer.)

Card 6
Front: What is India's rank in world fish production?
Back: India is the second-largest producer of fish in the world (after China).

Card 7
Front: What are the three main cropping seasons in India?
Back: Kharif (monsoon season, sown June–July), Rabi (winter season, sown October–November), and Zaid (short summer season).

Card 8
Front: Name two major Kharif crops and two major Rabi crops.
Back: Kharif: rice (paddy), cotton, maize, sugarcane. Rabi: wheat, barley, gram, mustard.

Card 9
Front: Which crop requires high temperature, high rainfall (above 100 cm) and alluvial soil?
Back: Rice (paddy).

Card 10
Front: Which soil is considered best for cotton cultivation?
Back: Black soil (regur soil), found mainly in the Deccan Plateau (Maharashtra, Gujarat).

Card 11
Front: Which state is the largest producer of rice in India?
Back: West Bengal.

Card 12
Front: What is the Green Revolution?
Back: The sharp increase in food-grain production (especially wheat and rice) from the mid-1960s, using high-yielding variety (HYV) seeds, fertilisers and irrigation. M.S. Swaminathan is called the father of India's Green Revolution.

Card 13
Front: Which revolution is associated with food-grain production, and which with milk?
Back: Green Revolution — food grains; White Revolution — milk (Operation Flood, led by Verghese Kurien).

Card 14
Front: Which state is the largest producer of sugarcane in India?
Back: Uttar Pradesh.

Card 15
Front: Which state is the largest producer of cotton in India?
Back: Gujarat.

Card 16
Front: What is the minimum support price (MSP)?
Back: The price at which the government purchases crops from farmers to protect them from a sharp fall in market prices. MSP is announced by the Government of India on the recommendations of the CACP (Commission for Agricultural Costs and Prices).

Card 17
Front: Which crop is India the world's largest producer of — pulses, or wheat?
Back: Pulses. India is the largest producer of pulses in the world.

Card 18
Front: What type of farming is practised by most Indian farmers — subsistence or commercial?
Back: Subsistence farming — small landholdings, mainly to feed the farmer's own family.

Card 19
Front: What is "slash and burn" agriculture called in North-East India?
Back: Jhum cultivation (shifting cultivation).

Card 20
Front: Which two states lead in tea and coffee production in India?
Back: Assam is the largest tea-producing state; Karnataka is the largest coffee-producing state.
