Questions 26–50: Medium

26. Which of the following is NOT a function of the Reserve Bank of India?
(a) Issuing currency notes (b) Banker to the Government (c) Accepting fixed deposits from the general public (d) Controller of credit
Answer: (c)
Solution: The tempting wrong idea is that since RBI is "the central bank," it must do everything a bank does — but RBI does not deal directly with the public. Accepting deposits from the public is the job of commercial banks. RBI issues currency, acts as banker to the government, and controls credit, so the answer is accepting fixed deposits from the general public.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Introductory Macroeconomics

27. The first Five-Year Plan of India (1951–56) was based on which model?
(a) Mahalanobis Model (b) Solow Model (c) Lewis Model (d) Harrod–Domar Model
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is the Mahalanobis Model, which sounds more "Indian" but actually underpinned the Second Five-Year Plan (1956–61), focused on heavy industries. The First Plan, focused on agriculture, was based on the Harrod–Domar Model, so the answer is Harrod–Domar Model.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Indian Economic Development

28. Which sector is the largest contributor to India's Gross Value Added (GVA) in recent years?
(a) Services (b) Industry (c) Agriculture (d) Mining
Answer: (a)
Solution: Many students pick agriculture because it employs the most people — but employment share and output share are different things. The services sector (tertiary sector) contributes the largest share, over half of India's GVA, so the answer is Services.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Indian Economic Development

29. What does 'fiscal deficit' mean?
(a) Total expenditure minus total revenue receipts (b) Total expenditure minus total receipts excluding borrowings (c) Revenue expenditure minus revenue receipts (d) Total receipts minus total expenditure
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is option one, which describes the budgetary/total gap loosely but ignores that borrowings are receipts too. Fiscal deficit = total expenditure − total receipts excluding borrowings; it shows the government's total borrowing requirement, so the answer is total expenditure minus total receipts excluding borrowings.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Introductory Macroeconomics

30. The Goods and Services Tax (GST) in India was implemented from which date?
(a) 1 April 2016 (b) 1 July 2017 (c) 1 January 2018 (d) 8 November 2016
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is 8 November 2016, which is the demonetisation date — examiners love pairing these two events. The GST Constitutional Amendment was passed in 2016, but GST actually came into force on 1 July 2017, so the answer is 1 July 2017.
Source: PYQ-style

31. Which of the following is an example of a direct tax?
(a) Income tax (b) Customs duty (c) Goods and Services Tax (d) Excise duty
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is GST, which is constantly in the news and feels like a tax "directly" paid by consumers — but it is an indirect tax because its burden can be shifted. A direct tax is one whose burden cannot be shifted, like income tax, so the answer is Income tax.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Introductory Macroeconomics

32. NITI Aayog replaced which institution on 1 January 2015?
(a) National Development Council (b) Finance Commission (c) Planning Commission (d) Inter-State Council
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is the Finance Commission, which is a constitutional body that still exists and is often confused with the Planning Commission because of the similar name. The Planning Commission (established 1950) was replaced by NITI Aayog, so the answer is Planning Commission.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Indian Economic Development

33. Which Five-Year Plan is associated with the slogan 'Garibi Hatao'?
(a) Third Five-Year Plan (b) Fourth Five-Year Plan (c) Sixth Five-Year Plan (d) Fifth Five-Year Plan
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is the Fourth Plan, since poverty removal was a general theme of that era. The 'Garibi Hatao' slogan was given by Indira Gandhi during the 1971 elections and became central to the Fifth Five-Year Plan (1974–79), so the answer is Fifth Five-Year Plan.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Indian Economic Development

34. In India, the 'Repo Rate' is the rate at which:
(a) RBI borrows from the government (b) Commercial banks lend to RBI (c) RBI lends money to commercial banks for short term (d) Banks lend to their most creditworthy customers
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is option two, which describes the reverse repo rate — the names are deliberately confusing. Repo rate is the rate at which RBI lends short-term funds to commercial banks against securities, so the answer is RBI lends money to commercial banks for short term.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Introductory Macroeconomics

35. Which committee recommended the introduction of the Goods and Services Tax in India?
(a) Kelkar Committee (b) Chelliah Committee (c) Rangarajan Committee (d) Narasimham Committee
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is the Narasimham Committee, which is famous but dealt with banking sector reforms, not taxation. The Kelkar Committee (Task Force on Implementation of FRBM and indirect taxes) recommended a comprehensive GST, so the answer is Kelkar Committee.
Source: PYQ-style

36. What is the full form of 'GDP'?
(a) Gross Development Product (b) Gross Domestic Product (c) General Domestic Price (d) Gross Domestic Price
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is "Gross Development Product," which sounds plausible to students who link GDP with development. GDP stands for Gross Domestic Product — the market value of all final goods and services produced within a country in a year, so the answer is Gross Domestic Product.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Introductory Macroeconomics

37. Which of the following is counted in the calculation of GDP?
(a) Sale of second-hand goods (b) Services of a housewife at home (c) Transfer payments like old-age pension (d) Production of new machinery by a factory
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is the sale of second-hand goods — money changes hands, so it feels like economic output, but no new production occurs. GDP counts only current production of final goods and services; new machinery is fresh output, so the answer is production of new machinery by a factory.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Introductory Macroeconomics

38. The New Economic Policy of 1991 in India is associated with which three components?
(a) Privatisation, Nationalisation, Subsidisation (b) Nationalisation, Liberalisation, Globalisation (c) Liberalisation, Privatisation, Globalisation (d) Globalisation, Centralisation, Privatisation
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is option two, which sneaks in "Nationalisation" — the opposite of what 1991 stood for. The 1991 reforms under P.V. Narasimha Rao and Manmohan Singh rested on Liberalisation, Privatisation and Globalisation (LPG), so the answer is Liberalisation, Privatisation, Globalisation.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Indian Economic Development

39. Which institution releases the estimates of national income in India?
(a) Reserve Bank of India (b) Ministry of Finance (c) NITI Aayog (d) National Statistical Office (NSO)
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is RBI, because it publishes many economic statistics and reports. But official national income estimates are released by the National Statistical Office (formerly CSO, merged with NSSO in 2019) under the Ministry of Statistics and Programme Implementation, so the answer is National Statistical Office (NSO).
Source: NCERT Class 12 Introductory Macroeconomics

40. 'Disguised unemployment' is most commonly found in which sector of the Indian economy?
(a) Agriculture (b) Manufacturing (c) Banking (d) Information technology
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is manufacturing, since factory layoffs make news — but that is open unemployment, not disguised. Disguised unemployment, where more people work than needed and marginal productivity is near zero, is characteristic of Indian agriculture, so the answer is Agriculture.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Indian Economic Development

41. Which of the following is a 'capital receipt' for the Government of India?
(a) Income tax collection (b) GST collection (c) Disinvestment of a public sector company (d) Interest received on loans
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is income tax collection — it is a large receipt, so it feels "capital," but it is a recurring revenue receipt. Capital receipts either create liabilities (borrowings) or reduce assets; disinvestment reduces government assets, so the answer is disinvestment of a public sector company.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Introductory Macroeconomics

42. The 'Minimum Support Price' (MSP) in India is announced by:
(a) Reserve Bank of India (b) Ministry of Agriculture on the recommendation of CACP (c) Food Corporation of India independently (d) NITI Aayog
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is the Food Corporation of India, because FCI actually procures the crops — but procurement is not the same as price announcement. MSP is announced by the Government (Ministry of Agriculture) based on recommendations of the Commission for Agricultural Costs and Prices, so the answer is Ministry of Agriculture on the recommendation of CACP.
Source: PYQ-style

43. Which plan period is known as the 'Plan Holiday' in India?
(a) 1961–66 (b) 1966–69 (c) 1979–80 (d) 1990–92
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is 1990–92, a period of political instability when annual plans were also made — but the classic "Plan Holiday" refers to 1966–69, when three annual plans replaced the Five-Year Plan due to wars, drought and economic crisis, so the answer is 1966–69.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Indian Economic Development

44. Inflation measured by the Consumer Price Index (CPI) reflects price changes at which level?
(a) Retail level (b) Wholesale level (c) Producer level (d) Import level
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is wholesale level, which describes the Wholesale Price Index (WPI) — the two indices are frequently swapped in exams. CPI measures the change in prices paid by consumers at the retail level, so the answer is retail level.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Introductory Macroeconomics

45. Which of the following banks is considered the pioneer of the 'Lead Bank Scheme' in India?
(a) State Bank of India (b) Punjab National Bank (c) Bank of Baroda (d) The scheme assigned districts to banks; it was not pioneered by one bank
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is State Bank of India, since SBI leads in so many banking initiatives that students assume it pioneered this too. The Lead Bank Scheme (1969, on the Gadgil Study Group/Nariman Committee recommendations) allotted districts among all major banks for coordinated development, so the answer is that the scheme assigned districts to banks and was not pioneered by one bank.
Source: PYQ-style

46. 'Stagflation' refers to a situation of:
(a) High growth with high inflation (b) High growth with low inflation (c) Falling prices with rising employment (d) Stagnant growth with high inflation and unemployment
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is option one — high growth with inflation is a normal boom, not stagflation. Stagflation combines economic stagnation (slow growth, high unemployment) with high inflation, an unusual and difficult combination, so the answer is stagnant growth with high inflation and unemployment.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Introductory Macroeconomics

47. The headquarters of the World Bank is located at:
(a) Geneva (b) Washington D.C. (c) New York (d) Paris
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is New York, because the UN headquarters is there and students merge the two in memory. The World Bank (IBRD) is headquartered in Washington D.C., USA, so the answer is Washington D.C.
Source: PYQ-style

48. Which of the following is NOT a direct tax in India?
(a) Customs duty (b) Income tax (c) Wealth tax (when it was in force) (d) Corporation tax
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is wealth tax — since it was abolished in 2016, students think it "doesn't count," but while in force it was a direct tax. Customs duty is levied on goods and its burden is passed to consumers, making it indirect, so the answer is Customs duty.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Introductory Macroeconomics

49. The 'Green Revolution' in India primarily began in which decade?
(a) 1960s (b) 1950s (c) 1970s (d) 1980s
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is the 1970s, because the Green Revolution's effects became widely visible then. It actually began in the mid-1960s (1966–67) with the introduction of high-yielding variety (HYV) seeds of wheat and rice, so the answer is 1960s.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Indian Economic Development

50. 'Fiscal policy' in India is formulated by:
(a) Reserve Bank of India (b) NITI Aayog (c) Ministry of Finance, Government of India (d) Parliament's Public Accounts Committee
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is RBI, because students confuse fiscal policy with monetary policy — RBI handles only monetary policy. Fiscal policy (taxation and government spending) is formulated by the Ministry of Finance through the Union Budget, so the answer is Ministry of Finance, Government of India.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Introductory Macroeconomics
