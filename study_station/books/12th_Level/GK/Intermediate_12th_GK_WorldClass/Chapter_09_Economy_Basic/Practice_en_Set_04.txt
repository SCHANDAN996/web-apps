Questions 76–100: Medium

76. Which of the following is NOT a function of money?
(a) Creation of value (b) Measure of value (c) Store of value (d) Medium of exchange
Answer: (a)
Solution: Money facilitates exchange, measures value and stores value, but it does not itself create value — value is created by production of goods and services. The tempting trap is "store of value", which sounds unusual but is a genuine function of money. So the answer is Creation of value.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Macroeconomics

77. The Reserve Bank of India was nationalised in which year?
(a) 1935 (b) 1949 (c) 1947 (d) 1950
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is 1935, the year RBI was established as a private shareholders' bank — examiners deliberately pair establishment with nationalisation. RBI was nationalised on 1 January 1949. So the answer is 1949.
Source: RBI official website (rbi.org.in)

78. Which sector contributes the largest share to India's Gross Value Added (GVA)?
(a) Agriculture (b) Industry (c) Services (d) Mining
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is agriculture, which employs the largest share of workers but contributes far less to GVA than services. Services such as trade, finance, IT and transport contribute over half of India's GVA. So the answer is Services.
Source: Economic Survey, Ministry of Finance

79. Fiscal deficit is best described as:
(a) Total expenditure minus total revenue receipts (b) Total expenditure minus total receipts excluding borrowings (c) Revenue expenditure minus revenue receipts (d) Interest payments minus tax revenue
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is the first option, which ignores that borrowings are also receipts — counting them would make the deficit zero by definition. Fiscal deficit equals total expenditure minus total receipts excluding borrowings, which is the government's total borrowing requirement. So the answer is total expenditure minus total receipts excluding borrowings.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Macroeconomics

80. Which of the following is a direct tax?
(a) GST (b) Income tax (c) Customs duty (d) Excise duty
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is GST, which is a major tax but indirect — its burden is passed on to the consumer. Income tax is paid directly by the person on whom it is levied, making it a direct tax. So the answer is Income tax.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Indian Economic Development

81. The New Economic Policy of liberalisation, privatisation and globalisation was launched in India in:
(a) 2001 (b) 1985 (c) 1980 (d) 1991
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is 1985, when some early liberalisation steps began, but the comprehensive New Economic Policy came in July 1991 under P.V. Narasimha Rao with Manmohan Singh as Finance Minister, during a balance-of-payments crisis. So the answer is 1991.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Indian Economic Development

82. The Goods and Services Tax (GST) was implemented across India on:
(a) 1 July 2017 (b) 1 April 2016 (c) 1 January 2018 (d) 1 April 2010
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is 1 April 2010, the originally proposed rollout date that was missed — examiners exploit this. GST was actually launched at midnight on 1 July 2017 after the 101st Constitutional Amendment. So the answer is 1 July 2017.
Source: GST Council / CBIC official material

83. Which of the following is included in the calculation of GDP?
(a) Sale of second-hand goods (b) Services of a housewife (c) Production of goods for sale in the market (d) Transfer payments like pensions
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is transfer payments — pensions feel like income but no good or service is produced against them, so they are excluded. Second-hand sales and unpaid household work are also excluded; only current production of goods and services for the market counts. So the answer is production of goods for sale in the market.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Macroeconomics

84. The Minimum Support Price (MSP) in India is announced by:
(a) Commission for Agricultural Costs and Prices recommendation, approved by Cabinet (b) NITI Aayog (c) Food Corporation of India (d) Ministry of Commerce
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is FCI, which procures at MSP but does not fix it. MSP is recommended by the Commission for Agricultural Costs and Prices (CACP) and approved by the Cabinet Committee on Economic Affairs. So the answer is CACP recommendation approved by the Cabinet.
Source: Ministry of Agriculture (agricoop.gov.in)

85. Which Five-Year Plan is known as the 'Mahalanobis Plan' focusing on heavy industries?
(a) Second Five-Year Plan (b) First Five-Year Plan (c) Third Five-Year Plan (d) Fourth Five-Year Plan
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is the First Plan, which focused on agriculture using the Harrod-Domar model. The Second Plan (1956–61), based on P.C. Mahalanobis's model, prioritised heavy and basic industries like the steel plants at Bhilai, Durgapur and Rourkela. So the answer is the Second Five-Year Plan.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Indian Economic Development

86. 'Repo rate' is the rate at which:
(a) Government borrows from RBI (b) Commercial banks lend to RBI (c) Banks lend to the public (d) RBI lends to commercial banks for short term
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is the second option, which describes the reverse repo rate — the names are deliberately confusing. Repo rate is the rate at which banks borrow short-term funds from RBI against securities. So the answer is RBI lends to commercial banks for short term.
Source: RBI official website (rbi.org.in)

87. Which of the following is NOT a part of the organized sector of the Indian economy?
(a) Street vendors (b) Government schools (c) Registered factories (d) Banks
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is registered factories — "factory" sounds informal to some, but registered units belong to the organized sector. Street vendors work without formal registration, job security or benefits, placing them in the unorganized sector. So the answer is Street vendors.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Economics (Understanding Economic Development)

88. The term 'disguised unemployment' is most commonly associated with:
(a) Industrial sector (b) Service sector (c) Agricultural sector (d) IT sector
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is the industrial sector, where unemployment is usually visible as open joblessness. Disguised unemployment — where more people work than needed and removing some does not reduce output — is characteristic of Indian agriculture. So the answer is the Agricultural sector.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Indian Economic Development

89. NITI Aayog replaced which institution on 1 January 2015?
(a) Finance Commission (b) National Development Council (c) Planning Commission (d) Economic Advisory Council
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is the Finance Commission, a constitutional body that still exists and distributes tax revenues — it was never replaced. NITI Aayog replaced the Planning Commission, ending the era of Five-Year Plans. So the answer is the Planning Commission.
Source: NITI Aayog official website (niti.gov.in)

90. Which of the following is a qualitative (selective) credit control measure of RBI?
(a) Bank rate (b) Margin requirements (c) Cash Reserve Ratio (d) Open market operations
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is CRR — it is a famous RBI tool but a quantitative one, affecting the overall volume of credit. Margin requirements selectively control credit for specific purposes, making them qualitative. So the answer is Margin requirements.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Macroeconomics

91. The committee whose 2009 methodology became the widely used basis for estimating poverty in India was headed by:
(a) D.T. Lakdawala (b) Vijay Kelkar (c) Y.K. Alagh (d) Suresh Tendulkar
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is Lakdawala, whose committee gave an earlier widely used methodology — the chronological order confuses many. The Tendulkar Committee (2009) revised the poverty line methodology that was then adopted for official estimates. So the answer is Suresh Tendulkar.
Source: Planning Commission / NITI Aayog reports

92. Which of the following taxes was subsumed under GST?
(a) Income tax (b) Corporation tax (c) Value Added Tax (VAT) (d) Property tax
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is property tax — it is a tax, but levied by municipalities and not subsumed. GST subsumed indirect taxes like VAT, excise duty and service tax; direct taxes like income tax remain separate. So the answer is Value Added Tax (VAT).
Source: GST Council / CBIC official material

93. 'Green Revolution' in India is primarily associated with:
(a) Verghese Kurien (b) Sam Pitroda (c) M.S. Swaminathan (d) Baba Amte
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is Verghese Kurien — he led the White Revolution (Operation Flood, dairy), and examiners swap the two revolutions. M.S. Swaminathan is called the father of India's Green Revolution in food grains. So the answer is M.S. Swaminathan.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Indian Economic Development

94. Which of the following best describes 'stagflation'?
(a) High growth with low inflation (b) High growth with high inflation (c) Deflation with unemployment (d) Stagnant growth with high inflation and unemployment
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is the second option — high growth with inflation is common and is not stagflation. Stagflation is the unusual combination of stagnant output, high inflation and high unemployment together. So the answer is stagnant growth with high inflation and unemployment.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Macroeconomics

95. The headquarters of the World Bank is located at:
(a) Washington D.C. (b) New York (c) Geneva (d) London
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is New York, home of the UN headquarters — examiners exploit this similarity. Both the World Bank and the IMF are headquartered in Washington D.C. So the answer is Washington D.C.
Source: World Bank official website (worldbank.org)

96. Which of the following is an example of a capital receipt for the government?
(a) Income tax collection (b) Disinvestment of PSU shares (c) GST collection (d) Interest received on loans
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is interest received — it is a receipt but a revenue (non-debt, recurring) one. Disinvestment reduces government assets and is classified as a capital receipt, while tax and GST collections are revenue receipts. So the answer is Disinvestment of PSU shares.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Macroeconomics

97. The 'Twin Deficit' problem refers to:
(a) Fiscal deficit and revenue deficit (b) Fiscal deficit and current account deficit (c) Revenue deficit and trade deficit (d) Budget deficit and primary deficit
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is fiscal plus revenue deficit — both are budget concepts and sound like a natural pair, but the standard term 'twin deficits' links the government's fiscal deficit with the country's current account deficit. So the answer is fiscal deficit and current account deficit.
Source: Economic Survey, Ministry of Finance

98. Which bank is known as the 'Bankers' Bank' in India?
(a) State Bank of India (b) EXIM Bank (c) NABARD (d) Reserve Bank of India
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is SBI — being the largest bank, it feels like the answer, but size is not the criterion. RBI is called the Bankers' Bank because commercial banks keep deposits with it and borrow from it. So the answer is the Reserve Bank of India.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Macroeconomics

99. The first bank established in India was:
(a) Bank of Bombay (b) State Bank of India (c) Punjab National Bank (d) Bank of Hindustan
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is Punjab National Bank (1894), famous as the first bank started with Indian capital and management — but the Bank of Hindustan (1770, Calcutta) was the first bank established in India. So the answer is Bank of Hindustan.
Source: Banking history, RBI publications

100. 'Human Development Index' is published annually by:
(a) UNDP (b) IMF (c) World Bank (d) World Economic Forum
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is the World Bank, which publishes many development reports like the World Development Report and confuses candidates. The HDI — combining health, education and income — is published by the United Nations Development Programme. So the answer is UNDP.
Source: UNDP official website (hdr.undp.org)
