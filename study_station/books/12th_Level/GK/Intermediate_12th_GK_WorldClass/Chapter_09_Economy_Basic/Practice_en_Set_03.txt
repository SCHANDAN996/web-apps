51. Which of the following best describes a "mixed economy"?
(a) An economy where only the government owns all resources (b) An economy where both public and private sectors coexist (c) An economy based only on agriculture (d) An economy with no foreign trade
Answer: (b)
Solution: A tempting wrong choice is the one describing full government ownership — that is a socialist/command economy, not a mixed one. India follows a mixed economy where the public sector and private sector operate side by side, so the answer is an economy where both public and private sectors coexist.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Indian Economic Development

52. The National Income of India is estimated and published by which organisation?
(a) National Statistical Office (NSO), Ministry of Statistics and Programme Implementation (b) NITI Aayog (c) Reserve Bank of India (d) Ministry of Finance
Answer: (a)
Solution: Many students pick the Reserve Bank of India because it deals with money and data, but RBI handles monetary policy, not national income estimation. National income estimates are released by the National Statistical Office under the Ministry of Statistics and Programme Implementation, so the answer is the National Statistical Office (NSO).
Source: NCERT Class 12 Macroeconomics

53. Which sector of the Indian economy is also called the "service sector"?
(a) Primary sector (b) Secondary sector (c) Quaternary sector (d) Tertiary sector
Answer: (d)
Solution: The primary sector covers agriculture and mining, and the secondary sector covers manufacturing; students often confuse secondary with services. Services such as banking, transport, trade and education form the tertiary sector, so the answer is the tertiary sector.

54. GDP at market price minus net indirect taxes gives:
(a) Personal income (b) GNP at market price (c) NNP at factor cost (d) GDP at factor cost
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is choosing NNP at factor cost, which additionally requires subtracting depreciation. Moving from market price to factor cost only requires removing net indirect taxes (indirect taxes minus subsidies), so the answer is GDP at factor cost.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Macroeconomics

55. Which of the following is included in the calculation of Gross National Product (GNP) but not in GDP?
(a) Depreciation (b) Subsidies (c) Indirect taxes (d) Net factor income from abroad
Answer: (d)
Solution: Depreciation distinguishes gross from net, not GDP from GNP — that is the common trap. GNP equals GDP plus net factor income earned by residents from abroad, so the answer is net factor income from abroad.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Macroeconomics

56. The First Five-Year Plan of India was based on which model?
(a) Harrod-Domar model (b) Mahalanobis model (c) Lewis model (d) Solow model
Answer: (a)
Solution: The Mahalanobis model is the tempting wrong answer, but it guided the Second Five-Year Plan, not the First. The First Plan (1951–56) was based on the Harrod-Domar model with emphasis on agriculture, so the answer is the Harrod-Domar model.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Indian Economic Development

57. NITI Aayog replaced which institution on 1 January 2015?
(a) Finance Commission (b) National Development Council (c) Planning Commission (d) Inter-State Council
Answer: (c)
Solution: The Finance Commission is a constitutional body that still exists, so choosing it is a chronological confusion trap. The Planning Commission, set up in 1950, was replaced by NITI Aayog on 1 January 2015, so the answer is the Planning Commission.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Indian Economic Development

58. Which of the following is a direct tax?
(a) Goods and Services Tax (b) Customs duty (c) Income tax (d) Excise duty
Answer: (c)
Solution: GST, customs duty and excise duty are all indirect taxes because their burden can be shifted to others; students often wrongly treat excise as direct. Income tax is paid directly by the person on whom it is imposed, so the answer is income tax.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Indian Economic Development

59. The Goods and Services Tax (GST) was implemented in India from:
(a) 1 July 2017 (b) 1 April 2016 (c) 1 January 2018 (d) 1 April 2017
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is 1 April 2017, since the financial year begins then and the Constitutional Amendment was passed in 2016. GST actually came into force at midnight on 1 July 2017, so the answer is 1 July 2017.

60. Which of the following is NOT a function of the Reserve Bank of India?
(a) Issuing currency notes (b) Banker to the government (c) Controller of credit (d) Formulating the Union Budget
Answer: (d)
Solution: Students sometimes think RBI prepares the budget because it manages government debt, but that is the trap. The Union Budget is prepared by the Ministry of Finance, while RBI issues currency, acts as banker to the government and controls credit, so the answer is formulating the Union Budget.

61. The Reserve Bank of India was nationalised in the year:
(a) 1935 (b) 1949 (c) 1947 (d) 1950
Answer: (b)
Solution: The year 1935 is the classic trap — RBI was established in 1935 but as a privately owned institution. It was nationalised on 1 January 1949, so the answer is 1949.

62. Inflation caused by an increase in the cost of production, such as higher wages or raw material prices, is called:
(a) Demand-pull inflation (b) Cost-push inflation (c) Hyperinflation (d) Stagflation
Answer: (b)
Solution: Demand-pull inflation is the tempting wrong answer, but it arises when aggregate demand exceeds supply. When rising production costs push prices up, it is cost-push inflation, so the answer is cost-push inflation.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Macroeconomics

63. Which index is used by the Reserve Bank of India as the primary measure of inflation for monetary policy?
(a) Wholesale Price Index (WPI) (b) GDP deflator (c) Consumer Price Index (CPI) combined (d) Index of Industrial Production
Answer: (c)
Solution: WPI is the trap because it was historically used for inflation measurement. Since 2016, RBI's flexible inflation targeting framework uses the Consumer Price Index (combined) as the nominal anchor, so the answer is Consumer Price Index (CPI) combined.
Source: PYQ-style

64. The repo rate is the rate at which:
(a) RBI lends short-term funds to commercial banks (b) Commercial banks lend to each other (c) RBI borrows money from commercial banks (d) The government borrows from RBI
Answer: (a)
Solution: The reverse repo rate is the trap — it is the rate at which RBI borrows from (absorbs funds of) banks. The repo rate is the rate at which RBI lends short-term money to commercial banks against securities, so the answer is RBI lends short-term funds to commercial banks.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Macroeconomics

65. Which of the following is an example of a capital receipt for the government?
(a) Income tax collection (b) GST collection (c) Disinvestment of public sector shares (d) Interest received on loans
Answer: (c)
Solution: Interest received is the tempting wrong option, but it is a revenue receipt since it neither creates liability nor reduces assets. Disinvestment reduces government assets and is a capital receipt, so the answer is disinvestment of public sector shares.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Macroeconomics

66. Fiscal deficit is calculated as:
(a) Total expenditure minus total revenue receipts (b) Revenue expenditure minus revenue receipts (c) Total expenditure minus total receipts excluding borrowings (d) Total receipts minus total expenditure
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is subtracting only revenue receipts, which overstates the gap; that formula mixes concepts. Fiscal deficit equals total expenditure minus total receipts other than borrowings, so the answer is total expenditure minus total receipts excluding borrowings.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Macroeconomics

67. The New Economic Policy of 1991 in India is associated with which three components?
(a) Nationalisation, regulation, planning (b) Land reforms, green revolution, bank nationalisation (c) Demonetisation, digitisation, formalisation (d) Liberalisation, privatisation, globalisation
Answer: (d)
Solution: Nationalisation and regulation describe the pre-1991 approach, which is the trap for confused students. The 1991 reforms are summarised as LPG — liberalisation, privatisation and globalisation, so the answer is liberalisation, privatisation, globalisation.

68. Who is known as the father of the Green Revolution in India?
(a) Norman Borlaug (b) M. S. Swaminathan (c) Verghese Kurien (d) C. Subramaniam

69. The White Revolution in India is associated with:
(a) M. S. Swaminathan (b) Amul and Verghese Kurien (c) Hiralal Chaudhuri (d) Sam Pitroda
Answer: (b)
Solution: Hiralal Chaudhuri is linked with the Blue Revolution (fisheries), which is the trap here. The White Revolution (Operation Flood) in milk production is associated with Verghese Kurien and the Amul cooperative, so the answer is Amul and Verghese Kurien.

70. Which of the following banks was nationalised first, in 1955?
(a) Punjab National Bank (b) Bank of Baroda (c) State Bank of India (Imperial Bank converted) (d) Canara Bank
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is recalling the 1969 nationalisation of 14 major banks, which included Canara Bank and Bank of Baroda. In 1955 the Imperial Bank of India was converted into the State Bank of India, so the answer is State Bank of India (Imperial Bank converted).

71. The Poverty Line in India was estimated by the Tendulkar Committee using which approach?
(a) Calorie intake only (b) Landholding size of households (c) Per capita income of the country (d) Consumption expenditure including food, education, health and clothing
Answer: (d)
Solution: The calorie-intake-only approach is the trap — it was used by the earlier Lakdawala methodology. The Tendulkar Committee (report 2009) broadened the basket to include expenditure on food, education, health and clothing, so the answer is consumption expenditure including food, education, health and clothing.

72. Which of the following is a quantitative instrument of monetary policy?
(a) Open market operations (b) Selective credit control (c) Moral suasion (d) Margin requirements
Answer: (a)
Solution: Selective credit control and margin requirements are qualitative tools, which is the trap. Open market operations — buying and selling government securities — affect the overall quantity of money, so the answer is open market operations.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Macroeconomics

73. The term "disguised unemployment" is most commonly found in which sector in India?
(a) Agriculture (b) Manufacturing (c) Information technology (d) Banking
Answer: (a)
Solution: Manufacturing is a tempting answer because of factory layoffs, but that is open unemployment, not disguised. In Indian agriculture, more people work on family farms than needed, so marginal productivity of extra workers is near zero — this is disguised unemployment, so the answer is agriculture.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Economics

74. Which of the following statements about the Finance Commission is correct?
(a) It is a constitutional body constituted every five years under Article 280 (b) It is a statutory body set up by an Act of Parliament (c) It decides the monetary policy of the country (d) It prepares the Union Budget
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is calling it a statutory body — students confuse it with NITI Aayog, which is neither constitutional nor statutory. The Finance Commission is a constitutional body under Article 280, constituted every five years to recommend tax devolution, so the answer is that it is a constitutional body constituted every five years under Article 280.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Indian Constitution at Work

75. If the RBI wants to reduce inflation in the economy, it should:
(a) Decrease the repo rate and buy government securities (b) Increase the repo rate and sell government securities (c) Decrease the cash reserve ratio (d) Print more currency notes
Answer: (b)
Solution: Decreasing the repo rate is the trap — cheaper money increases lending and worsens inflation. To curb inflation, RBI raises the repo rate and sells securities through open market operations to absorb liquidity, so the answer is increase the repo rate and sell government securities.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Macroeconomics
