101. Which of the following is a direct tax?
(a) Goods and Services Tax (b) Income tax (c) Customs duty (d) Excise duty
Answer: (b)
Solution: The tempting wrong options are GST, customs and excise, but all three are indirect taxes because their burden can be shifted to consumers. A direct tax is paid by the person on whom it is imposed, so the answer is Income tax.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Economics (Indian Economic Development)

102. The Reserve Bank of India was nationalised in which year?
(a) 1935 (b) 1947 (c) 1949 (d) 1950
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is 1935, the year RBI was established (as a private shareholders' bank), not nationalised. Nationalisation happened on 1 January 1949, so the answer is 1949.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Economics / RBI official history

103. Which Five-Year Plan is known as the 'Gadgil Plan'?
(a) Second Five-Year Plan (b) Third Five-Year Plan (c) Fourth Five-Year Plan (d) Fifth Five-Year Plan
Answer: (b)
Solution: The tempting wrong option is the Second Plan, which is associated with the Mahalanobis model — a classic similar-names trap. The Third Plan (1961–66) is called the Gadgil Plan after D. R. Gadgil, so the answer is Third Five-Year Plan.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Economics (Indian Economic Development)

104. Which institution replaced the Planning Commission in 2015?
(a) National Development Council (b) Inter-State Council (c) Finance Commission (d) NITI Aayog
Answer: (d)
Solution: The National Development Council is tempting because it approved plans, but it was a separate body and was not the replacement. NITI Aayog was constituted on 1 January 2015, so the answer is NITI Aayog.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Economics / Government of India

105. The base year for the current GDP series of India (with 2011–12 prices) measures GDP at:
(a) Market prices (b) Factor cost only (c) Constant 2004–05 prices (d) Wholesale prices
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is factor cost, which was used in older series; since the 2011–12 revision, headline GDP is reported at market prices. So the answer is Market prices.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Macroeconomics / CSO (MoSPI)

106. Which of the following is NOT a function of the Reserve Bank of India?
(a) Issuing currency notes (b) Accepting deposits from the general public (c) Banker to the government (d) Controller of credit
Answer: (b)
Solution: The tempting wrong options are issuing notes and banker to government, which are genuine RBI functions. RBI does not deal directly with the public's deposits — that is commercial banks' work, so the answer is Accepting deposits from the general public.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Macroeconomics

107. The New Economic Policy of 1991 is associated with which trio of reforms?
(a) Liberalisation, Privatisation, Globalisation (b) Nationalisation, Regulation, Subsidy (c) Demonetisation, Digitisation, Deregulation (d) Planning, Protection, Promotion
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is the first option — nationalisation belongs to the pre-1991 era, not the reforms. The 1991 reforms are summarised as LPG, so the answer is Liberalisation, Privatisation, Globalisation.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Economics (Indian Economic Development)

108. Fiscal deficit is calculated as:
(a) Total receipts minus total expenditure (b) Revenue expenditure minus revenue receipts (c) Total expenditure minus total receipts excluding borrowings (d) Interest payments minus subsidies
Answer: (c)
Solution: The tempting wrong option is revenue expenditure minus revenue receipts, which defines revenue deficit, not fiscal deficit. Fiscal deficit equals total expenditure minus total receipts excluding borrowings, so the answer is the first option.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Macroeconomics

109. Which sector contributes the largest share to India's Gross Value Added?
(a) Agriculture (b) Industry (c) Mining (d) Services
Answer: (d)
Solution: Agriculture is tempting because it employs the most people, but employment share is not output share — a classic examiner's trap. Services contribute the largest share of GVA, so the answer is Services.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Economics (Indian Economic Development)

110. The Minimum Support Price (MSP) in India is announced on the recommendation of:
(a) NITI Aayog (b) Ministry of Finance (c) Food Corporation of India (d) Commission for Agricultural Costs and Prices
Answer: (d)
Solution: FCI is tempting because it procures foodgrains, but procurement is not price recommendation. MSP is recommended by the CACP, so the answer is Commission for Agricultural Costs and Prices.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Economics / Government of India

111. Which of the following is an example of a capital receipt for the government?
(a) Income tax collection (b) Disinvestment of a public sector company (c) GST collection (d) Interest received on loans
Answer: (b)
Solution: The tempting wrong options are tax collections, but they are revenue receipts since they neither create liability nor reduce assets. Disinvestment reduces government assets and is a capital receipt, so the answer is Disinvestment of a public sector company.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Macroeconomics

112. The 'Mahalanobis model' of the Second Five-Year Plan emphasised:
(a) Heavy and basic industries (b) Agriculture and irrigation (c) Export promotion (d) Poverty removal
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is agriculture, which was the First Plan's priority — a chronological confusion examiners love. The Second Plan (1956–61) focused on heavy and basic industries, so the answer is Heavy and basic industries.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Economics (Indian Economic Development)

113. Which committee recommended the introduction of GST in India in its early form?
(a) Rangarajan Committee (b) Sarkaria Commission (c) Narasimham Committee (d) Kelkar Task Force
Answer: (d)
Solution: The Narasimham Committee is tempting, but it dealt with banking sector reforms — a similar-names trap. The Kelkar Task Force (2004) recommended a nationwide GST, so the answer is Kelkar Task Force.
Source: PYQ-style

114. Inflation measured by the Consumer Price Index (CPI) is used by RBI for its monetary policy target of:
(a) 4% with a band of ±2% (b) 2% with a band of ±2% (c) 6% with a band of ±3% (d) 5% with a band of ±1%
Answer: (a)
Solution: The tempting wrong option is 2%, which resembles targets of some foreign central banks. India's flexible inflation target is 4% CPI with a tolerance band of ±2%, so the answer is 4% with a band of ±2%.
Source: RBI / Government of India (inflation targeting framework)

115. Which of the following banks was NOT nationalised in 1969?
(a) Punjab National Bank (b) State Bank of India (c) Bank of Baroda (d) Canara Bank
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is assuming all big banks were nationalised in 1969. SBI was nationalised earlier, in 1955 (from the Imperial Bank), so the answer is State Bank of India.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Economics / RBI

116. 'Green Revolution' in India primarily began in which decade?
(a) 1950s (b) 1970s (c) 1960s (d) 1980s
Answer: (c)
Solution: The 1970s is tempting because the revolution's effects peaked then, but its introduction with HYV seeds began in the mid-1960s. So the answer is 1960s.

117. Which of the following is included in the calculation of Gross National Product (GNP) but not in GDP?
(a) Net factor income from abroad (b) Government consumption (c) Depreciation (d) Indirect taxes
Answer: (a)
Solution: Depreciation is tempting, but it distinguishes Gross from Net, not National from Domestic. GNP = GDP plus net factor income from abroad, so the answer is Net factor income from abroad.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Macroeconomics

118. The FRBM Act, 2003 primarily deals with:
(a) Fiscal discipline and deficit targets (b) Foreign exchange management (c) Banking regulation (d) Monetary policy framework
Answer: (a)
Solution: Foreign exchange management is tempting because of the similar-sounding FEMA, but FEMA is a 1999 law — a similar-names trap. FRBM stands for Fiscal Responsibility and Budget Management, so the answer is Fiscal discipline and deficit targets.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Macroeconomics / Government of India

119. Which of the following is a qualitative (selective) credit control measure of RBI?
(a) Repo rate (b) Cash Reserve Ratio (c) Open market operations (d) Margin requirements
Answer: (d)
Solution: The tempting wrong options are repo rate and CRR, but both are quantitative measures affecting the overall volume of credit. Margin requirements selectively control credit for specific purposes, so the answer is Margin requirements.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Macroeconomics

120. The term 'disguised unemployment' is most commonly associated with which sector in India?
(a) Manufacturing (b) Information technology (c) Agriculture (d) Banking
Answer: (c)
Solution: Manufacturing is tempting because of factory layoffs, but disguised unemployment means more workers are engaged than actually needed — typical of Indian agriculture. So the answer is Agriculture.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Economics (Indian Economic Development)

121. Consider the following statements:
1. The first nationalisation of 14 major commercial banks took place in 1969.
2. The second round of bank nationalisation in 1980 covered 6 banks.
3. RBI itself was nationalised in 1955 along with the Imperial Bank.
Which of the above statements is/are correct?
(a) 2 and 3 only (b) 1 and 2 only (c) 1 and 3 only (d) 1, 2 and 3
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is statement 3, which sounds plausible but mixes dates: the Imperial Bank was nationalised as SBI in 1955, while RBI was nationalised in 1949. Statements 1 and 2 are correct, so the answer is 1 and 2 only.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Economics / RBI

122. Arrange the following events in correct chronological order:
1. Establishment of NITI Aayog
2. Nationalisation of 14 banks
3. Launch of the First Five-Year Plan
4. Introduction of GST
(a) 3-1-2-4 (b) 2-3-1-4 (c) 3-2-1-4 (d) 2-1-3-4
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is placing bank nationalisation (1969) before the First Plan (1951) — a chronological confusion. The correct order is 1951, 1969, 2015, 2017, so the answer is 3-2-1-4.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Economics

123. Consider the following statements about GDP and GNP:
1. If net factor income from abroad is positive, GNP exceeds GDP.
2. GDP measures production within the geographical boundary of a country.
3. GNP is always greater than GDP for India.
Which of the above statements is/are correct?
(a) 1, 2 and 3 (b) 2 and 3 only (c) 1 only (d) 1 and 2 only
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is statement 3, an almost-correct-looking claim; for India, net factor income from abroad is generally negative, so GNP is usually slightly less than GDP. Statements 1 and 2 are correct, so the answer is 1 and 2 only.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Macroeconomics

124. Match the following:
P. Repo rate — 1. RBI lends long-term funds to banks
Q. Reverse repo — 2. RBI borrows short-term funds from banks
R. Bank rate — 3. RBI lends short-term funds to banks
S. CRR — 4. Share of deposits banks keep with RBI
(a) P-2, Q-3, R-1, S-4 (b) P-3, Q-2, R-1, S-4 (c) P-3, Q-1, R-2, S-4 (d) P-1, Q-2, R-3, S-4
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is swapping repo and reverse repo — repo is RBI lending to banks, reverse repo is RBI absorbing funds from banks; also bank rate is for long-term lending. The correct matching is P-3, Q-2, R-1, S-4, so the answer is the first option.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Macroeconomics / RBI

125. Consider the following statements about the 1991 economic reforms:
1. They were triggered by a severe balance of payments crisis.
2. Industrial licensing was abolished for all industries without exception.
3. The rupee was devalued in July 1991.
Which of the above statements is/are correct?
(a) 2 and 3 only (b) 1 and 2 only (c) 1 and 3 only (d) 1, 2 and 3
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is statement 2, an almost-correct statement: licensing was abolished for most industries but retained for a few (such as those related to security, hazardous chemicals and environment). Statements 1 and 3 are correct, so the answer is 1 and 3 only.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Economics (Indian Economic Development)
