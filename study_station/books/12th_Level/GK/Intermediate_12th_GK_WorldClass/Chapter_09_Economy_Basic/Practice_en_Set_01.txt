1. Which sector of the Indian economy is also called the "primary sector"?
(a) Manufacturing sector (b) Transport sector (c) Banking sector (d) Agriculture and allied activities
Answer: (d)
Solution: The tempting wrong choice is manufacturing, but that belongs to the secondary sector. The primary sector includes agriculture, forestry, fishing and mining, which directly use natural resources, so the answer is agriculture and allied activities.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Economics

2. Which of the following activities falls under the secondary sector?
(a) Fishing (b) Cotton farming (c) Making cloth from cotton in a mill (d) Selling cloth in a shop
Answer: (c)
Solution: Fishing and farming are primary activities, and selling cloth is a tertiary (service) activity, which makes them attractive but wrong options. Converting raw cotton into cloth is manufacturing, so the answer is making cloth from cotton in a mill.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Economics

3. The tertiary sector of the economy is also known as the:
(a) Mining sector (b) Industrial sector (c) Agricultural sector (d) Service sector
Answer: (d)
Solution: Industrial and mining activities belong to the secondary and primary sectors respectively, so those options mislead. The tertiary sector provides services like banking, transport, education and health, so the answer is service sector.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Economics

4. GDP stands for:
(a) Gross Domestic Product (b) General Domestic Price (c) Gross Development Plan (d) General Development Product
Answer: (a)
Solution: The similar-sounding expansions in the other options are a classic examiner's trap using near-identical words. GDP is the money value of all final goods and services produced within a country in a year, so the answer is Gross Domestic Product.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Economics

5. In India, GDP data is officially released by which organisation?
(a) Reserve Bank of India (b) State Bank of India (c) NITI Aayog (d) National Statistical Office (NSO)
Answer: (d)
Solution: RBI and NITI Aayog are tempting because both deal with the economy, but RBI controls monetary policy and NITI Aayog is a policy think tank. National income estimates are released by the NSO under the Ministry of Statistics and Programme Implementation, so the answer is National Statistical Office (NSO).
Source: NCERT Class 11 Economics

6. Which sector contributes the largest share to India's GDP at present?
(a) Tertiary sector (b) Secondary sector (c) Primary sector (d) Mining sector
Answer: (a)
Solution: Many students pick the primary sector because most workers were historically in agriculture, but share in employment and share in GDP are different things. Services contribute more than half of India's GDP, so the answer is tertiary sector.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Economics

7. The Reserve Bank of India was established in the year:
(a) 1935 (b) 1947 (c) 1950 (d) 1969
Answer: (a)
Solution: 1947 and 1950 are traps linked to Independence and the Constitution, and 1969 is the year of bank nationalisation — a chronological confusion trap. RBI was set up on 1 April 1935 under the RBI Act, 1934, so the answer is 1935.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Economics

8. The headquarters of the Reserve Bank of India is located in:
(a) Mumbai (b) Kolkata (c) New Delhi (d) Chennai
Answer: (a)
Solution: New Delhi tempts students because most central institutions are there, and RBI was originally established in Kolkata in 1935 before moving. Its central office has been in Mumbai since 1937, so the answer is Mumbai.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Economics

9. How many banks were nationalised in India in 1969?
(a) 6 (b) 14 (c) 20 (d) 12
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap here is 20, which is the total after the second round of nationalisation in 1980 (14 + 6). In July 1969, fourteen major commercial banks were nationalised, so the answer is 14.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Economics

10. Which of the following is the central bank of India?
(a) State Bank of India (b) Punjab National Bank (c) Bank of India (d) Reserve Bank of India
Answer: (d)
Solution: SBI is tempting because it is the largest bank, but it is a commercial bank, not the central bank. The Reserve Bank of India issues currency and regulates all banks, so the answer is Reserve Bank of India.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Economics

11. The rate at which the RBI lends money to commercial banks for the short term is called:
(a) Reverse repo rate (b) Repo rate (c) Bank deposit rate (d) Cash reserve ratio
Answer: (b)
Solution: Reverse repo is the trap — it is the rate at which RBI borrows from banks, the opposite direction. The rate at which banks borrow from RBI is the repo rate, so the answer is repo rate.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Economics

12. Cash Reserve Ratio (CRR) is the portion of deposits that banks must keep with:
(a) The Government of India (b) The Reserve Bank of India (c) Their own head office vault only (d) The World Bank
Answer: (b)
Solution: Keeping cash in their own vault sounds plausible but describes day-to-day cash, not CRR. CRR is a fixed percentage of a bank's deposits maintained with the RBI, so the answer is the Reserve Bank of India.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Economics

13. Which tax was introduced in India on 1 July 2017 to replace multiple indirect taxes?
(a) Income tax (b) Wealth tax (c) Goods and Services Tax (GST) (d) Customs duty
Answer: (c)
Solution: Income tax and wealth tax are direct taxes, so they cannot replace indirect taxes — that is the trap. GST subsumed taxes like excise duty, service tax and VAT, so the answer is Goods and Services Tax (GST).
Source: NCERT Class 11 Economics

14. GST in India is an example of which type of tax?
(a) Indirect tax (b) Direct tax (c) Wealth tax (d) Poll tax
Answer: (a)
Solution: Direct tax is tempting because GST is collected by the government, but the burden of GST is passed on to the consumer through prices. Taxes on goods and services are indirect taxes, so the answer is indirect tax.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Economics

15. Which of the following is a direct tax in India?
(a) Income tax (b) Customs duty (c) GST (d) Excise duty
Answer: (a)
Solution: GST, customs duty and excise duty are all levied on goods or services and their burden can be shifted, making them indirect taxes. Income tax is paid directly by the person who earns the income, so the answer is income tax.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Economics

16. The Union Budget of India is presented in Parliament under which Article of the Constitution?
(a) Article 280 (b) Article 110 (c) Article 112 (d) Article 360
Answer: (c)
Solution: Article 110 is a trap because it deals with Money Bills, and Article 280 concerns the Finance Commission — similar-sounding constitutional topics. The Annual Financial Statement (Budget) is presented under Article 112, so the answer is Article 112.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Economics (Indian Constitution at Work)

17. The Finance Commission of India is constituted every:
(a) Three years (b) Five years (c) Four years (d) Six years
Answer: (b)
Solution: The other durations are plausible-sounding distractors with no constitutional basis. Under Article 280, the President constitutes the Finance Commission every fifth year, so the answer is five years.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Economics (Indian Constitution at Work)

18. Who was the first Finance Minister of independent India?
(a) Jawaharlal Nehru (b) C. D. Deshmukh (c) R. K. Shanmukham Chetty (d) Morarji Desai
Answer: (c)
Solution: Nehru is the common trap because he was the first Prime Minister and later held the finance portfolio briefly. The first Finance Minister, who presented the first budget in November 1947, was R. K. Shanmukham Chetty, so the answer is R. K. Shanmukham Chetty.
Source: PYQ-style

19. The New Economic Policy of liberalisation, privatisation and globalisation was launched in India in:
(a) 1991 (b) 1985 (c) 1980 (d) 2001
Answer: (a)
Solution: The other years are chronological-confusion traps with no reform significance. Facing a balance of payments crisis, India launched economic reforms in July 1991 under P. V. Narasimha Rao's government, so the answer is 1991.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Economics

20. Which institution replaced the Planning Commission of India in 2015?
(a) Finance Commission (b) National Development Council (c) NITI Aayog (d) RBI
Answer: (c)
Solution: The Finance Commission is a trap because it is a constitutional body that already existed and has a different role. The Planning Commission was replaced by NITI Aayog on 1 January 2015, so the answer is NITI Aayog.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Economics

21. Consider the following statements about the Planning Commission:
1. It was established in 1950 by an Act of Parliament.
2. It was chaired by the Prime Minister.
Which of the above is/are correct?
(a) Only 2 (b) Only 1 (c) Both 1 and 2 (d) Neither 1 nor 2
Answer: (a)
Solution: Statement 1 is the almost-correct trap: the Planning Commission was indeed set up in 1950, but by a Cabinet resolution, not by an Act of Parliament — it was a non-statutory body. The Prime Minister was its ex-officio chairman, so statement 2 is correct and the answer is Only 2.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Economics

22. Which of the following correctly arranges the sectors in the order primary, secondary, tertiary?
(a) Banking, farming, mining (b) Banking, textile mill, farming (c) Textile mill, banking, farming (d) Farming, textile mill, banking
Answer: (d)
Solution: The other sequences mix up the order and tempt students who memorise sector names without the logic of raw material → processing → service. Farming is primary, a textile mill is secondary, and banking is tertiary, so the answer is farming, textile mill, banking.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Economics

23. Disguised unemployment is most commonly found in India in:
(a) The IT sector (b) The agriculture sector (c) The banking sector (d) The railways
Answer: (b)
Solution: The organised sectors listed are traps because their staffing is formally fixed and visible. In Indian agriculture, more people work on family farms than needed, so removing some does not reduce output — this is disguised unemployment, so the answer is the agriculture sector.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Economics

24. Consider the following statements about the rupee:
1. The Reserve Bank of India issues all currency notes including the one-rupee note.
2. The one-rupee note is issued by the Ministry of Finance.
Which of the above is/are correct?
(a) Only 1 (b) Only 2 (c) Both 1 and 2 (d) Neither 1 nor 2
Answer: (b)
Solution: Statement 1 is the classic trap — RBI issues all notes except the one-rupee note and coins, so saying it issues "all" makes the statement false. The one-rupee note is issued by the Ministry of Finance and bears the Finance Secretary's signature, so the answer is Only 2.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Economics

25. The first Five-Year Plan of India was launched in which year?
(a) 1947 (b) 1950 (c) 1956 (d) 1951
Answer: (d)
Solution: 1950 is the trap because the Planning Commission was formed that year, and 1956 marks the Second Plan — a chronological confusion trap. The First Five-Year Plan ran from 1951 to 1956, so the answer is 1951.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Economics
