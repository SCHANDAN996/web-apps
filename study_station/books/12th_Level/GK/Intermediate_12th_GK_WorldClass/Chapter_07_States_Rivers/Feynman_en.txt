# 🧒 Feynman Technique: The Trickiest Concept in States & Rivers

## The Confusion: Why do some rivers flow EAST into the Bay of Bengal, while others flow WEST into the Arabian Sea — even though they start almost next to each other?

This is the single most confusing fact for exam aspirants. The Narmada and the Mahanadi both rise in central India, barely a few hundred kilometres apart. Yet one runs west to the Arabian Sea and the other runs east to the Bay of Bengal. Students mix this up in every exam — and examiners know it.

## Explain It Like I'm 12

Imagine your school building sits on a long, gently sloping roof. The ridge of the roof is what geographers call a **water divide** — the high ground that decides which way rain water rolls. India actually has more than one such ridge:

- The **Western Ghats** run north–south along the western coast. They are the main water divide for the peninsular rivers: rain falling on their eastern side feeds rivers that roll down toward the **Bay of Bengal**.
- The **central Indian highlands** — the Vindhya, Satpura and Maikal ranges — run east–west across the middle of the country and form the divide for central Indian rivers.

Now pour a bucket of water on that roof:

- Water falling on the **east side** of these divides rolls down toward the **Bay of Bengal** — that's the Mahanadi, Godavari, Krishna and Kaveri (the great east-flowing peninsular rivers).
- Water falling on the **west side** rolls down toward the **Arabian Sea** — that's the Narmada, Tapi, Sabarmati, Mahi.

(The Ganga and Brahmaputra are a separate story — they are **Himalayan rivers**, born in the northern mountains, and they too drain east into the Bay of Bengal.)

The river doesn't "choose" a direction. It simply follows the **slope of the land**. India's peninsular plateau tilts eastward, so most big peninsular rivers flow east. The Narmada and Tapi are special — they flow through **rift valleys** (cracks in the Earth's crust between the Vindhyas and Satpuras), and those cracks run east-to-west, so the water has no choice but to go west.

**One-line summary:** Rivers are lazy — they always take the downhill path, and India's peninsular roof mostly slopes east.

## The Daily-Life Example

Next time it rains, watch water on your street. It never flows "north" or "south" because of a compass — it flows toward the **drain**, because the drain is lower. Rivers are exactly the same. The Bay of Bengal side is the bigger "drain" for most of India, which is why most of India's river water empties into the Bay of Bengal, not the Arabian Sea.

## The Unforgettable Trick 🪄

**"Na-Ta goes West, the rest go East."**

- **Na** = Narmada, **Ta** = Tapi — the two famous west-flowing peninsular rivers.
- The other major peninsular rivers (Godavari, Krishna, Kaveri, Mahanadi) → East → Bay of Bengal. The Himalayan giants (Ganga, Brahmaputra) also end in the Bay of Bengal.

Add-on trick for rift valleys: **"Na-Ta crack the floor"** — Narmada and Tapi are the only big rivers flowing through rift (fault) valleys, which is *why* they go west.

And remember the exam trap: **Luni** (Rajasthan) also flows west but never reaches the sea — it dies in the Rann of Kutch. Examiners love asking "which river does NOT reach the sea?"

## Why Examiners Trap You Here

They will write: *"The Narmada flows eastward into the Bay of Bengal"* — sounds perfectly normal, but it's wrong. Or they'll swap Godavari (east) with Tapi (west). Another favourite: calling the Ganga a "peninsular river" — it is a Himalayan river. If you only memorised river names without understanding the **slope logic**, you'll fall for it. If you understand the roof-and-drain picture, you can *derive* the answer even if you forget the list.
