# States & Rivers

## 🌊 The River That Changed a Capital

In 1911, when the British decided to shift India's capital from Calcutta to Delhi, one silent factor weighed heavily in the decision — the **Yamuna**. A capital needs water, trade routes, and a lifeline. Delhi had all three because of its river. This is not just history; it is a pattern. Almost every major Indian city — Varanasi (Ganga), Kolkata (Hooghly), Ahmedabad (Sabarmati), Hyderabad (Musi), Guwahati (Brahmaputra) — sits on a riverbank. **If you understand rivers, you understand the map of India itself.**

And that is exactly why examiners love this chapter. SSC, Banking, Railway and State PCS exams regularly ask questions like: *Which river is called the 'Sorrow of Bihar'? Through which states does the Godavari flow? Which is the largest tributary of the Ganga?*

By the end of this chapter, you will be able to:

1. Classify Indian rivers into **Himalayan** and **Peninsular** systems and explain the difference.

4. Recall famous **dams, waterfalls and nicknames** linked to rivers.

## 1. Two Great Families of Indian Rivers

Indian rivers are divided into two families:

| Feature | Himalayan Rivers | Peninsular Rivers |
|---|---|---|
| Source | Glaciers (snow-fed) | Rain-fed (monsoon) |
| Flow | Perennial (flow all year) | Seasonal (shrink in summer) |
| Nature | Young, erosional, form gorges | Old, graded, form broad valleys |
| Examples | Ganga, Indus, Brahmaputra | Godavari, Krishna, Kaveri, Narmada |

**Simple memory trick:** Himalayan rivers are like salaried employees — income (water) all year. Peninsular rivers are like farmers — income depends on the monsoon.

🧠 **Active Recall:** Close the book. Name two differences between Himalayan and Peninsular rivers. Why does the Ganga never dry up but the Kaveri shrinks in April?

## 2. The Indus System (The Western Giant)

- **Origin:** Near **Lake Mansarovar** in Tibet, from a glacier near **Bokhar Chu** (Sengge Khabab / Lion's Mouth spring).
- **Course:** Flows through Ladakh (between Ladakh and Zanskar ranges), enters Pakistan, and empties into the **Arabian Sea** near Karachi.
- **Major tributaries:** **Jhelum, Chenab, Ravi, Beas, Sutlej** — the five rivers that give **Punjab** ("land of five rivers") its name.
- **Indus Water Treaty (1960):** Brokered by the World Bank between India and Pakistan. India got full rights over the **eastern rivers (Ravi, Beas, Sutlej)**; Pakistan over the **western rivers (Indus, Jhelum, Chenab)** — with limited use allowed to India.

**🧠 Mnemonic — "JCRBS" for the five rivers:** **J**helum, **C**henab, **R**avi, **B**eas, **S**utlej — remember *"Jammu CRaves Beautiful Sunsets."*

🧠 **Active Recall:** Which treaty divided the Indus waters, and in which year? Which three rivers went to India?

## 3. The Ganga System (The Heart of India)

- **Origin:** The **Bhagirathi** rises from the **Gangotri Glacier** at **Gaumukh** (Uttarakhand).
- **Birth of the Ganga:** At **Devprayag**, the **Alaknanda** meets the **Bhagirathi** — from here the river is called **Ganga**.
- **The Panch Prayag (five confluences)** of the Alaknanda: **Vishnuprayag** (Dhauliganga), **Nandaprayag** (Nandakini), **Karnaprayag** (Pindar), **Rudraprayag** (Mandakini), **Devprayag** (Bhagirathi).
- **Course:** Enters plains at **Haridwar**, flows through **Uttar Pradesh, Bihar, Jharkhand (border), West Bengal**, then enters Bangladesh where it is called the **Padma**. It joins the Brahmaputra (called **Jamuna** in Bangladesh) and finally the Meghna, emptying into the **Bay of Bengal** — forming the **Sundarbans**, the world's largest delta.
- **Major tributaries:**
  - **Left bank:** Ramganga, Gomti, Ghaghara, Gandak, Kosi, Mahananda
  - **Right bank:** Yamuna, Son, Damodar (via Hooghly)
- **The Yamuna** — the **largest tributary of the Ganga** — rises from the **Yamunotri Glacier** and joins the Ganga at **Prayagraj (Triveni Sangam)**, where the mythical **Saraswati** is also believed to meet.
- **Famous nicknames:**
  - **Kosi** — *"Sorrow of Bihar"* (frequent floods and course changes)
  - **Damodar** — *"Sorrow of Bengal"* (now tamed by the Damodar Valley Corporation)
  - **Hooghly** — the distributary on which **Kolkata** stands

**🧠 Mnemonic for left-bank tributaries — "RaGo GhaGaKoMa":** **Ra**mganga, **Go**mti, **Gha**ghara, **Ga**ndak, **Ko**si, **Ma**hananda.

🧠 **Active Recall:** Where does the Ganga officially begin (which confluence)? What is the Ganga called in Bangladesh? Why is the Kosi called the Sorrow of Bihar?

## 4. The Brahmaputra System (The Mighty One)

- **Origin:** **Chemayungdung Glacier** near Lake Mansarovar in Tibet, where it is called the **Tsangpo**.
- **Course:** Takes a dramatic **U-turn** around **Namcha Barwa** peak, enters India through **Arunachal Pradesh** (where it is called the **Dihang**, then Brahmaputra), flows through **Assam**, and enters Bangladesh as the **Jamuna**.
- **Special facts:**
  - It is a **braided river** — it splits into many channels.
  - **Majuli Island** (Assam) on the Brahmaputra is one of the world's largest **river islands**.
  - It carries a huge volume of water and silt — causing Assam's annual floods.
- **Tributaries:** Subansiri, Kameng, Manas, Teesta, Dibang, Lohit.

🧠 **Active Recall:** What is the Brahmaputra called in Tibet and in Bangladesh? On which river is Majuli island located?

## 5. The Peninsular Rivers — East Flowing

These flow into the **Bay of Bengal** and form **deltas**:

| River | Origin | States | Key Fact |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Godavari** | Trimbakeshwar, Nashik (Maharashtra) | Maharashtra, Telangana, Andhra Pradesh | Longest peninsular river — *"Dakshin Ganga"* (Ganga of the South) |
| **Krishna** | Mahabaleshwar (Maharashtra) | Maharashtra, Karnataka, Telangana, Andhra Pradesh | Tributaries: Tungabhadra, Bhima, Koyna |
| **Kaveri** | Talakaveri, Brahmagiri Hills (Karnataka) | Karnataka, Tamil Nadu | Historic water dispute between Karnataka & Tamil Nadu |
| **Mahanadi** | Sihawa, Chhattisgarh | Chhattisgarh, Odisha | Hirakud Dam — one of the longest dams in the world |
| **Pennar** | Nandi Hills (Karnataka) | Karnataka, Andhra Pradesh | — |

## 6. The Peninsular Rivers — West Flowing

Only two major rivers flow **west into the Arabian Sea**, and both flow through **rift valleys** (fault-created troughs) — so they form **estuaries, not deltas**:

- **Narmada:** Rises from **Amarkantak** (Madhya Pradesh); flows between the **Vindhya and Satpura** ranges through MP, Maharashtra and Gujarat. Famous for the **Marble Rocks at Bhedaghat (Jabalpur)** and the **Dhuandhar Falls**. The **Sardar Sarovar Dam** (Gujarat) is built on it.
- **Tapti (Tapi):** Rises near **Multai** (Betul, MP); flows through MP, Maharashtra and Gujarat; **Surat** stands on it.

**🧠 Mnemonic — "Na-Ta go West":** Only **Na**rmada and **Ta**pti (among majors) flow west. Remember: *"Nana and Tata went West."*

🧠 **Active Recall:** Why do the Narmada and Tapti form estuaries instead of deltas? Which river is called the Dakshin Ganga?

## 🔗 Static–Dynamic Link

River facts are static, but they keep returning to the news: **inter-state water disputes** (Kaveri, Krishna), **river interlinking proposals** (Ken–Betwa link project between MP and UP), and **Namami Gange** (the Ganga cleaning programme) all connect directly to this chapter. When a river appears in current affairs, revise its static facts the same day — that pairing is exactly how examiners frame questions.

## ⚠️ Caution: Examiner's Trap

**Trap 1 — "लगभग सही कथन" (Almost-True Statement):**
*"The Ganga originates from the Gangotri glacier."* Sounds perfect — but technically the **Bhagirathi** originates at Gangotri; the river is called **Ganga only from Devprayag**, where Alaknanda and Bhagirathi meet. Examiners exploit this one-word difference.

**Trap 2 — "समान नाम/वर्ष" (Similar Names Confusion):**
**Ghaghara vs Gandak**, **Bhagirathi vs Alaknanda**, **Yamuna (tributary of Ganga) vs Jamuna (Brahmaputra in Bangladesh)**. Options are deliberately placed side by side. Read each option fully before marking.

**Trap 3 — "नकारात्मक वाक्यांश" (Hidden Negative):**
*"Which of the following rivers does NOT flow into the Bay of Bengal?"* Students in a hurry mark Godavari (which does flow east). The correct answer is **Narmada**. Always circle the word NOT/EXCEPT in the question before answering.
