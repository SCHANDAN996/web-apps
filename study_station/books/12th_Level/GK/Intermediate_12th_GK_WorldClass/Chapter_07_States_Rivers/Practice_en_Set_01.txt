Questions 1–20: Easy | Questions 21–25: Medium

1. Which is the longest river in India?
(a) Ganga (b) Godavari (c) Brahmaputra (d) Yamuna
Answer: (a)
Solution: The Godavari is the longest river of peninsular India, which tempts many students, but the question asks about all of India. The Ganga, about 2,525 km long, is the longest river in India, so the answer is Ganga.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Geography

2. The Godavari river originates from which state?
(a) Maharashtra (b) Madhya Pradesh (c) Karnataka (d) Andhra Pradesh
Answer: (a)
Solution: Many students pick Andhra Pradesh because the Godavari is longest there, but origin is asked. The Godavari rises from Trimbakeshwar near Nashik in Maharashtra, so the answer is Maharashtra.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Geography (India Physical Environment)

3. Which river is known as the "Sorrow of Bihar"?
(a) Son (b) Gandak (c) Kosi (d) Bagmati
Answer: (c)
Solution: The Damodar is the "Sorrow of Bengal", which confuses students; do not mix the two titles. The Kosi, notorious for shifting its course and causing floods, is called the Sorrow of Bihar, so the answer is Kosi.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Geography (India Physical Environment)

4. The Brahmaputra river enters India through which state?
(a) Sikkim (b) Assam (c) Arunachal Pradesh (d) West Bengal
Answer: (c)
Solution: Assam is tempting because the Brahmaputra flows mainly through it, but the question asks where it enters India. It enters through Arunachal Pradesh (where it is called the Dihang), so the answer is Arunachal Pradesh.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Geography (India Physical Environment)

5. Which is the longest tributary of the Ganga?
(a) Yamuna (b) Kosi (c) Gandak (d) Ghaghara
Answer: (a)
Solution: The Ghaghara is tempting because it carries a huge volume of water, but by length the Yamuna (about 1,376 km) is the longest tributary of the Ganga, joining it at Prayagraj. So the answer is Yamuna.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Geography (India Physical Environment)

6. The Narmada river flows into which water body?
(a) Arabian Sea (b) Gulf of Mannar (c) Indian Ocean (d) Bay of Bengal
Answer: (a)
Solution: Most Indian rivers drain into the Bay of Bengal, so students often mark it by habit. The Narmada flows westward through a rift valley and empties into the Arabian Sea (Gulf of Khambhat), so the answer is Arabian Sea.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Geography

7. Which river flows through a rift valley?
(a) Ganga (b) Kaveri (c) Krishna (d) Narmada
Answer: (d)
Solution: The Krishna and Kaveri are peninsular rivers but flow over normal terrain, not rift valleys. The Narmada (along with the Tapi) flows through a rift valley between the Vindhya and Satpura ranges, so the answer is Narmada.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Geography (India Physical Environment)

8. The Chambal river is a tributary of which river?
(a) Ganga (b) Narmada (c) Yamuna (d) Betwa
Answer: (c)
Solution: Students often mark Ganga because the Chambal ultimately joins the Ganga system, but it first merges directly into the Yamuna, so the answer is Yamuna.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Geography (India Physical Environment)

9. Which state is called the "Land of Five Rivers"?
(a) Haryana (b) Rajasthan (c) Uttar Pradesh (d) Punjab
Answer: (d)
Solution: Haryana shares the same rivers regionally, which misleads some, but the name Punjab itself means "five waters" (the Sutlej, Beas, Ravi, Chenab and Jhelum), so the answer is Punjab.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Geography

10. The Kaveri river originates at Talakaveri in which state?
(a) Kerala (b) Karnataka (c) Tamil Nadu (d) Andhra Pradesh
Answer: (b)
Solution: Tamil Nadu is tempting because the Kaveri is its lifeline, but the question is about origin. Talakaveri lies in the Brahmagiri hills of Kodagu district in Karnataka, so the answer is Karnataka.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Geography (India Physical Environment)

11. Which river is also known as the "Dakshin Ganga" (Ganga of the South)?
(a) Krishna (b) Kaveri (c) Godavari (d) Mahanadi
Answer: (c)
Solution: The Kaveri is sacred like the Ganga, which confuses students, but the title Dakshin Ganga belongs to the Godavari because of its great length and large basin, so the answer is Godavari.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Geography

12. The Indus river originates near which lake/mansarovar region?
(a) Lake Baikal (b) Wular Lake (c) Dal Lake (d) Mansarovar (Tibet)
Answer: (d)
Solution: Dal and Wular lakes are in Kashmir where the Jhelum flows, a common mix-up. The Indus rises near Lake Mansarovar in Tibet from a spring at Sengge Khabab, so the answer is Mansarovar (Tibet).
Source: NCERT Class 11 Geography (India Physical Environment)

13. Which river forms the boundary between Uttar Pradesh and Madhya Pradesh for a long stretch?
(a) Chambal (b) Betwa (c) Ken (d) Son
Answer: (a)
Solution: The Betwa and Ken also flow in Bundelkhand, tempting students, but the Chambal forms a long natural boundary between Uttar Pradesh and Madhya Pradesh, so the answer is Chambal.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Geography (India Physical Environment)

14. The Mahanadi river flows mainly through which two states?
(a) Odisha and Chhattisgarh (b) Odisha and West Bengal (c) Jharkhand and Bihar (d) Chhattisgarh and Madhya Pradesh
Answer: (a)
Solution: West Bengal is tempting because of the Hooghly delta nearby, but that is a different river system. The Mahanadi rises in Chhattisgarh and flows through Odisha to the Bay of Bengal, so the answer is Odisha and Chhattisgarh.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Geography (India Physical Environment)

15. Which is the largest river island in the world, located in the Brahmaputra?
(a) Diu (b) Majuli (c) Srirangapatna (d) Sagar Island
Answer: (b)
Solution: Srirangapatna is a famous river island but on the Kaveri, which is the trap here. Majuli in Assam, on the Brahmaputra, is recognised as the world's largest river island, so the answer is Majuli.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Geography (India Physical Environment)

16. The Teesta river is a major river of which state?
(a) Manipur (b) Nagaland (c) Sikkim (d) Mizoram
Answer: (c)
Solution: Students often guess West Bengal because the Teesta reaches it, but the river originates in and is the main river of Sikkim, so the answer is Sikkim.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Geography (India Physical Environment)

17. Which river is known as the "Sorrow of Bengal" (before the Damodar Valley Project)?
(a) Hooghly (b) Ajay (c) Mayurakshi (d) Damodar
Answer: (d)
Solution: The Hooghly is Bengal's most famous river, which tempts students, but the devastating floods of the Damodar earned it the title Sorrow of Bengal, so the answer is Damodar.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Geography

18. The Krishna river originates from which place?
(a) Amarkantak (b) Talakaveri (c) Trimbakeshwar (d) Mahabaleshwar
Answer: (d)
Solution: Trimbakeshwar (Godavari) and Amarkantak (Narmada) are famous origins and confuse students. The Krishna rises near Mahabaleshwar in Maharashtra, so the answer is Mahabaleshwar.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Geography (India Physical Environment)

19. Which river does NOT flow into the Bay of Bengal?
(a) Godavari (b) Tapi (c) Mahanadi (d) Krishna
Answer: (b)
Solution: The Mahanadi, Godavari and Krishna all flow east into the Bay of Bengal. The Tapi (Tapti) flows west into the Arabian Sea, so the answer is Tapi.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Geography

20. The Sutlej river enters India through which pass/region?
(a) Banihal (b) Nathu La (c) Shipki La (d) Zoji La
Answer: (c)
Solution: Nathu La (Sikkim) and Zoji La (Ladakh) are famous passes and mislead students. The Sutlej enters India near Shipki La in Himachal Pradesh after cutting a gorge through the Himalayas, so the answer is Shipki La.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Geography (India Physical Environment)

21. Consider the following statements about the Narmada river:
1. It originates from the Amarkantak plateau.
2. It flows eastward into the Bay of Bengal.
3. It flows through a rift valley.
Which of the above statements is/are correct?
(a) 1 and 2 only (b) 1, 2 and 3 (c) 2 and 3 only (d) 1 and 3 only
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is statement 2, which sounds plausible because most big Indian rivers reach the Bay of Bengal, but the Narmada flows west into the Arabian Sea. Statements 1 and 3 are correct, so the answer is 1 and 3 only.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Geography (India Physical Environment)

22. Arrange the following peninsular rivers from north to south based on their location:
1. Mahanadi 2. Godavari 3. Krishna 4. Kaveri
(a) 1, 3, 2, 4 (b) 2, 1, 3, 4 (c) 1, 2, 3, 4 (d) 2, 1, 4, 3
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is swapping the Mahanadi and the Godavari; the Mahanadi (Odisha–Chhattisgarh) lies clearly north of the Godavari. North to south the order is Mahanadi, Godavari, Krishna, Kaveri, so the answer is 1, 2, 3, 4.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Geography (India Physical Environment)

23. Which of the following pairs is correctly matched?
(a) Betwa — tributary of Chambal (b) Son — tributary of Ganga (c) Gandak — tributary of Yamuna (d) Bhagirathi — tributary of Kosi
Answer: (b)
Solution: The tempting wrong pairs reverse real relationships: the Betwa joins the Yamuna (not the Chambal) and the Gandak joins the Ganga (not the Yamuna). The Son is a right-bank tributary of the Ganga, so the answer is Son — tributary of Ganga.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Geography (India Physical Environment)

24. Consider the following statements about the Brahmaputra:
1. It is known as the Tsangpo in Tibet.
2. In Bangladesh it is called the Padma.
3. It takes a U-turn near Namcha Barwa.
Which of the above statements is/are correct?
(a) 1 and 2 only (b) 1 and 3 only (c) 2 and 3 only (d) 1, 2 and 3
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is statement 2: in Bangladesh the Brahmaputra is called the Jamuna, while the Padma is the name of the Ganga there — a classic similar-names confusion. Statements 1 and 3 are correct, so the answer is 1 and 3 only.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Geography (India Physical Environment)

25. Which of the following rivers is a tributary of the Indus?
(a) Tapi (b) Jhelum (c) Mahanadi (d) Kaveri
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is picking a large peninsular river like the Kaveri out of habit, but the Tapi, Mahanadi and Kaveri all belong to separate peninsular systems. The Jhelum is one of the five rivers of the Punjab that join the Indus system, so the answer is Jhelum.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Geography (India Physical Environment)
