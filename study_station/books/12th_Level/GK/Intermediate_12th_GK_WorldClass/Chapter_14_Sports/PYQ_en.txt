# 📊 PYQ Analysis — Sports & Competitions

## 1. How Exams Ask Questions from This Chapter

Sports GK appears in almost every government exam — SSC (CGL, CHSL, MTS, GD), Banking (IBPS, SBI), Railway (NTPC, Group D), and State PCS prelims. The questions are almost always **one-liner factual MCQs**, but examiners earn their marks through traps, not through depth. Based on the recurring pattern of past papers, questions from this chapter cluster around these sub-topics:

**Most frequently asked sub-topics (in words, not numbers):**

1. **Trophies and associated sports** — the single most repeated area. Durand Cup, Santosh Trophy, Ranji Trophy, Dhyan Chand Trophy, Thomas Cup, Uber Cup, Davis Cup, Wimbledon trophies. Examiners love swapping the sport against the trophy.
2. **Number of players in a team** — hockey (11), football (11), cricket (11), volleyball (6), basketball (5), kabaddi (7), kho-kho (9 on field), water polo (7), rugby union (15). A classic "almost correct" trap zone.
3. **Olympics facts** — first modern Olympics (Athens 1896), Olympic rings and their colours, motto (*Citius, Altius, Fortius* — "Faster, Higher, Stronger"; "Communiter" added in 2021), India's first individual gold (Abhinav Bindra, Beijing 2008), first Olympic medal for India (Norman Pritchard, 1900, two silvers).
4. **National Sports Day and awards** — 29 August (Major Dhyan Chand's birthday), Major Dhyan Chand Khel Ratna Award, Arjuna Award, Dronacharya Award, Dhyan Chand Award for lifetime achievement.
5. **Stadiums and venues** — Eden Gardens (Kolkata), Wankhede (Mumbai), MCG (Melbourne), Lord's (London — "Home of Cricket").
6. **Terminology of sports** — "googly", "LBW" (cricket); "smash", "deuce" (badminton/tennis); "checkmate" (chess); "knockout" (boxing); "bull's eye" (shooting/archery).
7. **Measurements** — cricket pitch length (22 yards), football field dimensions, marathon distance (42.195 km), basketball hoop height (10 feet), chessboard squares (64).
8. **Firsts and records** — first Indian to win an individual Olympic gold, first Indian woman Olympic medallist (Karnam Malleswari, Sydney 2000, weightlifting bronze), Sachin Tendulkar's 100 international centuries, Viswanathan Anand as India's first Grandmaster (1988).
9. **Games and their country of origin / national games** — a favourite trap area because many "national game" claims are myths (see Trap Type 5 below).
10. **Cups in international sports** — FIFA World Cup (football), ICC Cricket World Cup, Hockey World Cup, Ryder Cup (golf).

**Exam-wise flavour:**

- **SSC / Railway:** direct one-liners — trophies, number of players, stadiums, National Sports Day.
- **Banking (IBPS/SBI):** sports awards, recent winners, terminology, and "which of the following is NOT correctly matched" questions.
- **State PCS:** adds state-level stadiums, state games, and regional sportspersons.
- **UPSC (rare in prelims):** only when sports intersect with polity/culture (e.g., Khelo India scheme, Fit India Movement).

## 2. The Examiner's Traps (जाल) — Know Them Before the Exam

**Trap 1 — Almost Correct Statement:**
"India won its first Olympic hockey gold in 1948." Sounds right — and it is. The trap version: "India won its first Olympic hockey gold in 1936." India actually won in **1928 (Amsterdam)**. Examiners shift the year by one Olympiad.

**Trap 2 — Chronological Confusion:**
Questions asking "which came first" — e.g., Arjuna Award (1961) vs Dronacharya Award (1985) vs Khel Ratna (1991–92, renamed Major Dhyan Chand Khel Ratna in 2021). Memorise the sequence, not isolated years.

**Trap 3 — Similar-Sounding Names:**
Thomas Cup vs **Uber Cup** (both badminton — men's vs women's world team championships). Durand Cup (football) vs Duleep Trophy (cricket). Beighton Cup (hockey) vs Durand Cup (football) — both are among India's oldest tournaments and are often mixed up. The examiner places the "sibling" trophy as an option.

**Trap 4 — Negative Phrasing:**
"Which of the following is **NOT** associated with hockey?" Students in a hurry miss the NOT and pick the first hockey-related option. Underline NOT/EXCEPT in the question before reading options.

**Trap 5 — The Myth Trap:**
"Hockey is the national game of India." **False.** The Ministry of Youth Affairs clarified (RTI reply, 2012) that **India has no officially declared national game**. This is one of the most repeated trap questions across exams.

**Trap 6 — Renaming Trap:**
The Rajiv Gandhi Khel Ratna was renamed **Major Dhyan Chand Khel Ratna Award** in August 2021. Old books still say Rajiv Gandhi Khel Ratna — examiners test whether you know the current name.

## 3. Top 10 High-Yield PYQ-Style Questions with Solutions

**Q1. The Uber Cup is associated with which sport?**
(a) Tennis (b) Badminton (c) Table Tennis (d) Hockey
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** Uber Cup is the women's world team championship in badminton; Thomas Cup is the men's equivalent.
**Trap type:** Similar-Sounding Names — Thomas Cup is usually placed as a distractor context; students who memorised "Thomas = badminton" get confused when Uber appears.
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q2. How many players of one team are on the field in a standard game of Kabaddi?**
(a) 6 (b) 7 (c) 9 (d) 11
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** A kabaddi team fields 7 players. Kho-kho fields 9 — the most common wrong pick.
**Trap type:** Almost Correct — 9 is the kho-kho number, planted to catch mixed-up memory.
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q3. National Sports Day in India is celebrated on 29 August to mark the birth anniversary of:**
(a) Milkha Singh (b) Dhyan Chand (c) P.T. Usha (d) Kapil Dev
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** Major Dhyan Chand, the hockey legend, was born on 29 August 1905. National Sports Awards are also presented on this day.
**Trap type:** Almost Correct — Milkha Singh is the emotional distractor.
**Source:** Official (myas.gov.in)

**Q4. Which of the following is NOT correctly matched?**
(a) Durand Cup — Football (b) Ranji Trophy — Cricket (c) Davis Cup — Tennis (d) Santosh Trophy — Hockey
**Answer: (d)**
**Solution:** Santosh Trophy is a **football** tournament (inter-state), not hockey. The other three are correct.
**Trap type:** Negative Phrasing + Almost Correct — Santosh Trophy "feels" like hockey to many aspirants.
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q5. Who was the first Indian to win an individual Olympic gold medal?**
(a) Rajyavardhan Singh Rathore (b) Abhinav Bindra (c) Leander Paes (d) Vijender Singh
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** Abhinav Bindra won gold in 10m Air Rifle shooting at Beijing 2008. Rathore (silver, Athens 2004) is the classic distractor.
**Trap type:** Almost Correct — Rathore was a shooting medallist too, just silver and earlier.
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q6. The term "Deuce" is used in which of the following sports?**
(a) Cricket (b) Tennis (c) Hockey (d) Boxing
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** Deuce is a 40–40 score in tennis (also used in badminton and table tennis contexts, but tennis is the standard exam answer).
**Trap type:** Terminology confusion — "googly" (cricket) is often swapped in options.
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q7. The first modern Olympic Games were held in:**
(a) Paris, 1900 (b) Athens, 1896 (c) London, 1908 (d) Rome, 1960
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** The modern Olympics began in Athens, Greece, in 1896, inspired by Pierre de Coubertin.
**Trap type:** Chronological Confusion — Paris 1900 is offered because Paris hosted the second Games.
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q8. Which of the following statements about India's "national game" is correct?**
(a) Hockey is the officially declared national game of India
(b) Cricket is the national game of India
(c) India has no officially declared national game
(d) Kabaddi was declared the national game in 1950
**Answer: (c)**
**Solution:** The Ministry of Youth Affairs & Sports clarified in 2012 that no game has been officially declared the national game of India.
**Trap type:** Myth Trap — option (a) is what most people "know"; it is wrong.
**Source:** Official (Ministry of Youth Affairs & Sports clarification)

**Q9. The Dronacharya Award is given for excellence in:**
(a) Playing performance (b) Sports coaching (c) Sports journalism (d) Lifetime achievement in administration
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** Dronacharya Award honours coaches; Arjuna Award and Khel Ratna honour players.
**Trap type:** Similar-Sounding Names — Dronacharya vs Arjuna (both from the Mahabharata, deliberately confusing).
**Source:** Official (myas.gov.in)

**Q10. The length of a cricket pitch (between the two sets of stumps) is:**
(a) 20 yards (b) 22 yards (c) 24 yards (d) 18 yards
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** The pitch is 22 yards (20.12 m) long — one "chain" in old measurement.
**Trap type:** Almost Correct — 20 yards is planted because it sounds plausible.
**Source:** PYQ-style

## 4. The 15/45-Second Rule Applied

**The rule:** If a question is a direct fact you know (trophy, number of players, date), answer within **15 seconds** and move on. If it requires elimination or has negative phrasing, give it up to **45 seconds** — never more.

**Example A — 15-second question (Q7 above):**
"First modern Olympics — Athens 1896." You either know it or you don't. Do not spend 45 seconds re-reading options; Paris 1900 will not become correct by staring at it. Mark and move.

**Example B — 45-second question (Q4 above):**
"Which is NOT correctly matched?" Here you must check **all four** pairs: Durand–Football ✓, Ranji–Cricket ✓, Davis–Tennis ✓, Santosh–Hockey ✗. This legitimately takes 30–45 seconds. Budget for it — but if after 45 seconds you are still unsure between two options, mark the one where a famous trophy is paired with an unusual sport (examiners' favourite mismatch) and move on.

**Practical takeaway:** Most questions in this chapter are direct one-line facts. Your preparation should therefore be **flashcard-speed recall**, not deep study. Save your 45-second budget for NOT/EXCEPT and match-the-following questions.
