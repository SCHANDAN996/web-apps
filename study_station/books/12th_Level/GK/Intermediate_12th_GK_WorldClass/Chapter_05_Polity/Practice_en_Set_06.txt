126. Which of the following statements about the amendment procedure under Article 368 is correct?
(a) A constitutional amendment bill must be ratified by half of the State Legislatures in all cases (b) A constitutional amendment bill requires prior recommendation of the President (c) A constitutional amendment bill can be introduced only in the Lok Sabha (d) A constitutional amendment bill can be introduced in either House of Parliament
Answer: (d)
Solution: The tempting wrong option is that the bill needs the President's prior recommendation — that rule applies to money bills under Article 117, not to amendment bills. Ratification by half the States is needed only for provisions listed in the proviso to Article 368, not in all cases, so the answer is that an amendment bill can be introduced in either House of Parliament.

127. Consider the following pairs of Schedules and their subjects:
1. Third Schedule — Forms of Oaths
2. Seventh Schedule — Division of powers
3. Tenth Schedule — Anti-defection provisions
Which of the pairs given above are correctly matched?
(a) 1 and 2 only (b) 2 and 3 only (c) 1, 2 and 3 (d) 1 and 3 only
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap here is the similar-sounding Schedules — the Eighth Schedule deals with languages while the Tenth deals with defection, and students often swap them. All three pairs given are correct: Third Schedule lists oaths, Seventh distributes legislative powers, and Tenth contains anti-defection provisions, so the answer is 1, 2 and 3.

128. The doctrine of 'Basic Structure' of the Constitution was propounded by the Supreme Court in which case?
(a) Golaknath v. State of Punjab (b) S.R. Bommai v. Union of India (c) Kesavananda Bharati v. State of Kerala (d) Minerva Mills v. Union of India
Answer: (c)
Solution: The chronological confusion trap: Golaknath (1967) held that Parliament cannot amend Fundamental Rights, but it did not propound the basic structure doctrine; Minerva Mills (1980) only applied and strengthened it. The doctrine was first propounded in Kesavananda Bharati (1973), so the answer is Kesavananda Bharati v. State of Kerala.

129. Which of the following is NOT a condition for the President's satisfaction to proclaim a Financial Emergency under Article 360?
(a) Occurrence of a natural calamity causing heavy expenditure (b) Threat to the financial stability of India (c) Threat to the financial stability of any part of India (d) Threat to the credit of India
Answer: (a)
Solution: The almost-correct trap: natural calamity sounds like a plausible ground, but Article 360 mentions only a threat to the financial stability or credit of India or of any part of its territory. A natural calamity is nowhere listed as a ground, so the answer is occurrence of a natural calamity causing heavy expenditure.

130. A Money Bill, after being passed by the Lok Sabha and transmitted to the Rajya Sabha, must be returned by the Rajya Sabha within how many days?
(a) 21 days (b) 7 days (c) 14 days (d) 30 days
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is the similar figure of 7 days, which students confuse with other constitutional time limits. Under Article 109, the Rajya Sabha must return a Money Bill with its recommendations within 14 days; if it does not, the bill is deemed passed by both Houses, so the answer is 14 days.

131. Which Article of the Constitution empowers the Supreme Court to issue writs for the enforcement of Fundamental Rights?
(a) Article 13 (b) Article 32 (c) Article 226 (d) Article 14
Answer: (b)
Solution: The similar-numbers trap: Article 226 also empowers writ issuance but by High Courts, not the Supreme Court, and it covers rights beyond Fundamental Rights too. Article 32, called the heart and soul of the Constitution by Dr. Ambedkar, empowers the Supreme Court, so the answer is Article 32.

132. The 73rd Constitutional Amendment Act added which Schedule to the Constitution?
(a) Twelfth Schedule (b) Tenth Schedule (c) Ninth Schedule (d) Eleventh Schedule
Answer: (d)
Solution: The chronological confusion trap: the Twelfth Schedule was added by the 74th Amendment (municipalities) in the same year, 1992, so students often mix them up. The 73rd Amendment on Panchayats added the Eleventh Schedule with 29 subjects, so the answer is the Eleventh Schedule.

133. Who among the following decides whether a bill is a Money Bill or not?
(a) The President of India (b) The Union Finance Minister (c) The Speaker of the Lok Sabha (d) The Chairman of the Rajya Sabha
Answer: (c)
Solution: The tempting wrong option is the President, since the President's recommendation is needed to introduce a Money Bill — but recommendation is different from certification. Under Article 110(3), the Speaker of the Lok Sabha has the final authority to decide whether a bill is a Money Bill, so the answer is the Speaker of the Lok Sabha.

134. Which of the following Fundamental Rights is available only to citizens of India and not to foreigners?
(a) Equality before law under Article 14 (b) Prohibition of discrimination under Article 15 (c) Protection against ex-post-facto laws under Article 20 (d) Protection of life and personal liberty under Article 21
Answer: (b)
Solution: The almost-correct trap: Articles 14, 20 and 21 are available to all persons including foreigners, which makes them look like the odd one out. Article 15 (along with Articles 16, 19, 29 and 30) is available only to citizens, so the answer is prohibition of discrimination under Article 15.

136. Under which Article can the Parliament create a new All-India Service?
(a) Article 309 (b) Article 310 (c) Article 311 (d) Article 312
Answer: (d)
Solution: The similar-numbers trap: Articles 309 to 311 deal with recruitment, tenure and dismissal of civil servants, which distracts students. Article 312 specifically empowers the Rajya Sabha, by a two-thirds majority resolution, to authorise Parliament to create a new All-India Service, so the answer is Article 312.

137. Which of the following statements about the Vice-President of India is correct?
(a) He can be removed only by impeachment like the President (b) He is elected by members of both Houses of Parliament including nominated members (c) He must be a member of the Rajya Sabha at the time of election (d) He is elected by an electoral college identical to that of the President
Answer: (b)
Solution: The almost-correct trap: the Vice-President's electoral college differs from the President's because State Legislatures do not participate, but it includes nominated members of Parliament, unlike the President's election. Removal is by a Rajya Sabha resolution agreed to by the Lok Sabha, not impeachment, so the answer is that he is elected by members of both Houses including nominated members.

138. The 'Doctrine of Pith and Substance' is applied by courts to resolve disputes relating to:
(a) Fundamental Rights violations (b) Legislative competence between Union and States (c) Presidential assent to bills (d) Disqualification of legislators
Answer: (b)
Solution: The tempting wrong option is Fundamental Rights, since doctrines like severability and eclipse relate to Article 13. Pith and substance is used when a law made by one legislature incidentally encroaches on another's list, to test its true nature and legislative competence, so the answer is legislative competence between Union and States.

Answer: (a)
Solution: The chronological confusion trap: the 42nd and 44th Amendments of the Emergency era are far more famous and attract guesses. The 61st Amendment Act of 1988 amended Article 326 to reduce the voting age for Lok Sabha and Assembly elections from 21 to 18 years, so the answer is the 61st Amendment Act.

142. The President's power to promulgate ordinances under Article 123 is subject to which limitation?
(a) The ordinance must be approved by the Supreme Court (b) The ordinance can be issued only on the advice of the Chief Justice of India (c) The ordinance ceases to operate six weeks after the reassembly of Parliament (d) The ordinance remains valid for one year without approval
Answer: (c)
Solution: The tempting wrong option is one-year validity, which confuses ordinances with the maximum gap between two sessions of Parliament (six months). An ordinance lapses six weeks after Parliament reassembles, with the outer limit being six months plus six weeks, so the answer is that it ceases six weeks after the reassembly of Parliament.

145. The total number of ministers, including the Prime Minister, in the Council of Ministers shall not exceed what percentage of the total strength of the Lok Sabha?
(a) 15 percent (b) 10 percent (c) 20 percent (d) 25 percent
Answer: (a)
Solution: The tempting wrong option is 10 percent, which students confuse with the anti-defection split provision that once required one-third (later deleted) — the figures get mixed up. The 91st Amendment Act, 2003 inserted Article 75(1A) capping the Council of Ministers at 15 percent of the Lok Sabha's strength, so the answer is 15 percent.

146. Which of the following statements about a National Emergency under Article 352 is correct?
(a) Its continuance beyond one month requires approval of both Houses within that period (b) Once approved, it needs no further parliamentary approval (c) It can be proclaimed only on the ground of war or external aggression (d) It automatically suspends all Fundamental Rights
Answer: (a)
Solution: The almost-correct trap: 'armed rebellion' was added by the 44th Amendment, so war or external aggression alone is incomplete; also only Articles 20 and 21 remain immune from suspension, not all rights. After the 44th Amendment, a proclamation must be approved by both Houses within one month, so the answer is that its continuance beyond one month requires approval of both Houses within that period.

147. The concept of 'Concurrent List' in the Indian Constitution was borrowed from which country?
(a) Australia (b) United States of America (c) United Kingdom (d) Canada
Answer: (a)
Solution: The similar-countries trap: Canada supplied federation with a strong centre and residuary powers with the Centre, which confuses students. The Concurrent List, along with the joint sitting provision, was borrowed from the Australian Constitution, so the answer is Australia.

149. Consider the following statements about the Attorney General of India:
1. He is appointed by the President under Article 76.
2. He has the right to speak in either House of Parliament without being a member.
3. He has the right to vote in Parliament.
Which of the statements given above is/are correct?
(a) 1 only (b) 2 and 3 only (c) 1, 2 and 3 (d) 1 and 2 only
Answer: (d)
Solution: The almost-correct trap: statement 3 looks plausible because the Attorney General can participate in parliamentary proceedings, but participation does not include voting. Under Article 88 he may speak and take part in proceedings of either House without a right to vote, so the answer is 1 and 2 only.

150. Which of the following pairs of constitutional features and their source countries is INCORRECTLY matched?
(a) Fundamental Duties — Japan (b) Concurrent List — Australia (c) Directive Principles of State Policy — Ireland (d) Emergency provisions — Germany
Answer: (a)
Solution: The almost-correct trap: Japan did contribute a feature to the Indian Constitution — the expression 'procedure established by law' — so Japan looks like a plausible source here. But Fundamental Duties were borrowed from the Constitution of the erstwhile USSR and inserted by the 42nd Amendment Act, 1976, while the other three pairs are correctly matched, so the answer is Fundamental Duties — Japan.
