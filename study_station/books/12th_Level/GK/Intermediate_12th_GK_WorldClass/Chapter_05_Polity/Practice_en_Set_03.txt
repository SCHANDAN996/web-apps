51. Which Article of the Indian Constitution deals with the impeachment of the President?
(a) Article 56 (b) Article 74 (c) Article 72 (d) Article 61
Answer: (d)
Solution: Article 56 deals with the term of office of the President, which tempts many students, but impeachment procedure is laid down in Article 61. Article 72 covers the President's pardoning power and Article 74 the Council of Ministers. So the answer is Article 61.

(a) Canada (b) Australia (c) Ireland (d) United Kingdom
Answer: (b)
Solution: Canada is a tempting wrong choice because the federal scheme and residuary powers came from Canada, but the Concurrent List was borrowed from Australia. Ireland gave us Directive Principles and the UK the parliamentary system. So the answer is Australia.

53. Which of the following is NOT a condition for a Money Bill under Article 110?
(a) Expenditure charged on the Contingency Fund (b) Custody of the Consolidated Fund of India (c) Imposition of taxes (d) Borrowing of money by the Government of India
Answer: (a)
Solution: Students often confuse the Consolidated Fund with the Contingency Fund; Article 110 mentions expenditure charged on the Consolidated Fund of India, not the Contingency Fund, making the Contingency Fund option the odd one out. Taxation, custody of the Consolidated Fund and government borrowing are all listed in Article 110. So the answer is expenditure charged on the Contingency Fund.

75. Which of the following statements about the Attorney General of India is correct?
(a) He is a member of the Union Cabinet (b) He has the right to vote in Parliament (c) He is appointed by the President under Article 76 and must be qualified to be a Supreme Court judge (d) He holds office for a fixed term of five years
Answer: (c)
Solution: The fixed five-year term is a tempting wrong statement because many constitutional posts have such terms, but the Attorney General holds office during the pleasure of the President with no fixed term. He is appointed under Article 76 and must possess the qualifications of a Supreme Court judge; he can speak in Parliament but cannot vote. So the answer is that he is appointed by the President under Article 76 and must be qualified to be a Supreme Court judge.
