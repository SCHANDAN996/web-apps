Questions 76–100: Medium

76. Which Article of the Indian Constitution deals with the amendment procedure?
(a) Article 356 (b) Article 368 (c) Article 360 (d) Article 370
Answer: (b)
Solution: Article 356 (President's Rule) and Article 360 (Financial Emergency) are tempting because all are high-numbered emergency-era Articles, but they deal with emergencies, not amendments. Article 368 in Part XX lays down the power and procedure of Parliament to amend the Constitution, so the answer is Article 368.

77. The concept of 'Directive Principles of State Policy' in the Indian Constitution was borrowed from the Constitution of which country?
(a) USA (b) Australia (c) Canada (d) Ireland
Answer: (d)
Solution: Canada is the tempting wrong option because India borrowed the federation with a strong centre from Canada, which confuses many aspirants. The Directive Principles of State Policy were borrowed from the Irish Constitution, which itself took them from Spain, so the answer is Ireland.

78. Which of the following is NOT a condition for the President's office under the Constitution?
(a) He should not hold any office of profit (b) He should not be a member of either House of Parliament (c) He must be a member of the Rajya Sabha before election (d) His emoluments cannot be diminished during his term
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is that options describing genuine constitutional conditions look so standard that the odd one hides among them. The Constitution only requires that the President not be a member of Parliament or a state legislature; there is no requirement of prior Rajya Sabha membership, so the answer is that he must be a member of the Rajya Sabha before election.

79. The 73rd Constitutional Amendment Act, 1992 added which Schedule to the Constitution?
(a) Eleventh Schedule (b) Tenth Schedule (c) Ninth Schedule (d) Twelfth Schedule
Answer: (a)
Solution: The Twelfth Schedule is the classic chronological-confusion trap because it was added by the very next amendment, the 74th Amendment for municipalities. The 73rd Amendment, dealing with Panchayati Raj, added the Eleventh Schedule with 29 subjects, so the answer is the Eleventh Schedule.

80. Who presides over a joint sitting of the two Houses of Parliament summoned under Article 108?
(a) The President (b) The Vice-President (c) The Chief Justice of India (d) The Speaker of the Lok Sabha
Answer: (d)
Solution: The Vice-President is the tempting wrong option because he is the ex-officio Chairman of the Rajya Sabha, and aspirants assume he presides over joint sittings too. Article 118(4) provides that the Speaker of the Lok Sabha presides over a joint sitting, so the answer is the Speaker of the Lok Sabha.

81. The Fundamental Duties were incorporated into the Constitution on the recommendation of which committee?
(a) Sarkaria Commission (b) Swaran Singh Committee (c) Shah Commission (d) Verma Committee
Answer: (b)
Solution: The Sarkaria Commission is a trap because it is a famous commission, but it dealt with Centre-State relations, not duties. The Swaran Singh Committee, set up during the Emergency, recommended Fundamental Duties, which were added by the 42nd Amendment Act, 1976, so the answer is the Swaran Singh Committee.

82. Under which Article can the Supreme Court issue writs for the enforcement of Fundamental Rights?
(a) Article 13 (b) Article 226 (c) Article 32 (d) Article 143
Answer: (c)
Solution: Article 226 is the near-correct trap because High Courts issue writs under it, and the two are frequently confused. Article 32 empowers the Supreme Court to issue writs and is itself a Fundamental Right, called the 'heart and soul' of the Constitution by Dr. Ambedkar, so the answer is Article 32.

83. Which of the following pairs is correctly matched?
(a) Habeas Corpus — to command a public official to perform his duty (b) Quo Warranto — to question the authority by which a person holds a public office (c) Mandamus — to produce a detained person before the court (d) Certiorari — to restrain a lower court from exceeding jurisdiction
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is that each wrong option swaps the meaning of one writ with another, and they look plausible if meanings are memorised loosely. Quo Warranto literally means 'by what authority' and questions the legality of a person's claim to a public office, so the answer is the Quo Warranto pair.

(a) 30 years (b) 25 years (c) 21 years (d) 35 years
Answer: (a)
Solution: 25 years is the tempting wrong option because it is the minimum age for the Lok Sabha, and the two Houses' age requirements are a classic confusion point. Article 84 prescribes 30 years for the Rajya Sabha, so the answer is 30 years.

85. The idea of a 'Constituent Assembly' for India was first put forward by:
(a) M. N. Roy (b) Jawaharlal Nehru (c) Mahatma Gandhi (d) B. R. Ambedkar
Answer: (a)
Solution: Jawaharlal Nehru is the trap because the Congress officially demanded a Constituent Assembly in 1935 and Nehru moved the Objectives Resolution. The idea was first put forward in 1934 by M. N. Roy, a pioneer of the communist movement in India, so the answer is M. N. Roy.

86. Which Schedule of the Constitution contains provisions regarding the disqualification of legislators on grounds of defection?
(a) Tenth Schedule (b) Ninth Schedule (c) Eighth Schedule (d) Seventh Schedule
Answer: (a)
Solution: The Ninth Schedule is the trap because both Ninth and Tenth Schedules were added by amendments and are often mixed up; the Ninth protects laws from judicial review. The Tenth Schedule, added by the 52nd Amendment Act, 1985, contains the anti-defection provisions, so the answer is the Tenth Schedule.

87. The President can declare a Financial Emergency under:
(a) Article 352 (b) Article 356 (c) Article 360 (d) Article 365
Answer: (c)
Solution: Article 356 is the tempting wrong option because it is the most frequently discussed emergency provision, but it deals with the failure of constitutional machinery in a state. Article 360 empowers the President to proclaim a Financial Emergency, which has never been declared in India, so the answer is Article 360.

88. Which of the following statements about the Attorney General of India is correct?
(a) He is a member of the Union Cabinet (b) He has the right to speak and take part in the proceedings of either House of Parliament without a right to vote (c) He has the right to vote in Parliament (d) He is appointed by the Chief Justice of India
Answer: (b)
Solution: The voting-right option is the trap because the Attorney General can participate in parliamentary proceedings, and aspirants assume participation includes voting. Under Article 88, he can speak and take part in proceedings of either House and its committees but has no right to vote, so the answer is the participation-without-vote statement.

89. The 'Objectives Resolution' was moved in the Constituent Assembly by Jawaharlal Nehru in:
(a) December 1946 (b) August 1947 (c) January 1947 (d) November 1949
Answer: (a)
Solution: January 1947 is the chronological trap because the Objectives Resolution was adopted on 22 January 1947, and adoption and moving dates are easily swapped. Nehru moved the resolution on 13 December 1946, so the answer is December 1946.

90. Which Article provides that the law declared by the Supreme Court shall be binding on all courts within India?
(a) Article 136 (b) Article 143 (c) Article 141 (d) Article 131
Answer: (c)
Solution: Article 143 is the trap because it also concerns the Supreme Court, but it deals with the President's power to seek advisory opinion. Article 141 declares that the law laid down by the Supreme Court is binding on all courts in India, so the answer is Article 141.

91. The Vice-President of India is elected by an electoral college consisting of:
(a) Elected members of both Houses of Parliament only (b) All members of both Houses of Parliament (c) Members of Parliament and State Legislative Assemblies (d) Elected members of Parliament and State Legislatures
Answer: (b)
Solution: The Presidential electoral college option is the trap because aspirants assume the Vice-President is elected the same way as the President, by elected members of Parliament and state assemblies. The Vice-President is elected by all members — elected and nominated — of both Houses of Parliament only, so the answer is all members of both Houses of Parliament.

92. Which Amendment Act reduced the voting age from 21 years to 18 years?
(a) 61st Amendment Act (b) 44th Amendment Act (c) 42nd Amendment Act (d) 73rd Amendment Act
Answer: (a)
Solution: The 42nd Amendment is the trap because it made so many famous changes that aspirants attribute most amendments to it. The 61st Constitutional Amendment Act, 1989 reduced the voting age for Lok Sabha and Assembly elections from 21 to 18 years, so the answer is the 61st Amendment Act.

93. The Comptroller and Auditor General of India is appointed by:
(a) The Prime Minister (b) The Chief Justice of India (c) The Parliament (d) The President
Answer: (d)
Solution: Parliament is the tempting wrong option because the CAG audits accounts and reports to Parliament, suggesting parliamentary appointment. Under Article 148, the CAG is appointed by the President by warrant under his hand and seal, so the answer is the President.

94. Which of the following is a money bill feature as per Article 110?
(a) A bill relating to the creation of a new state (b) A bill relating to the election of the President (c) A bill relating to the imposition, abolition or regulation of any tax (d) A bill relating to the amendment of the Preamble
Answer: (c)
Solution: The new-state option is the trap because creating states involves expenditure, and aspirants assume any bill with financial implications is a money bill. Article 110 defines a money bill narrowly, covering taxation, government borrowing, and the Consolidated Fund, so the answer is the taxation bill.

95. The total number of Fundamental Duties in the Constitution at present is:
(a) 11 (b) 10 (c) 12 (d) 9
Answer: (a)
Solution: 10 is the classic trap because the 42nd Amendment originally added ten Fundamental Duties, and most summaries mention only that figure. The 86th Amendment Act, 2002 added the eleventh duty, on parents providing education to children aged 6–14, so the answer is 11.

Answer: (d)
Solution: Dr. Rajendra Prasad is the trap because he presided over the Constituent Assembly, and the two chairmanships are frequently confused. Dr. B. R. Ambedkar chaired the Drafting Committee set up on 29 August 1947, so the answer is Dr. B. R. Ambedkar.

97. Under Article 74, the advice of the Council of Ministers to the President is:
(a) Binding, and the President can never ask for reconsideration (b) Not binding in any circumstance (c) Binding, but the President may ask the Council to reconsider it once (d) Binding only during a national emergency
Answer: (c)
Solution: The never-reconsider option is the trap because it states the position before the 44th Amendment, which many outdated notes still carry. The 42nd Amendment made the advice binding, and the 44th Amendment, 1978 allowed the President to require reconsideration once, after which the advice is binding, so the answer is binding with one reconsideration.

98. Which part of the Constitution deals with Panchayats?
(a) Part VIII (b) Part X (c) Part IX-A (d) Part IX
Answer: (d)
Solution: Part IX-A is the near-identical trap because it was inserted by the very next amendment and deals with the parallel subject of Municipalities. Part IX, covering Articles 243 to 243-O, was inserted by the 73rd Amendment and deals with Panchayats, so the answer is Part IX.

99. The power of judicial review in India is:
(a) Explicitly unlimited like in the USA (b) Available but narrower than in the USA because of the 'procedure established by law' clause (c) Not available to the Supreme Court (d) Available only during emergencies
Answer: (b)
Solution: The USA-style unlimited option is the trap because judicial review is often taught as an American borrowing, implying identical scope. Article 21 uses 'procedure established by law' rather than the American 'due process of law', making Indian judicial review narrower, so the answer is the narrower-scope statement.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Indian Constitution at Work

100. Which of the following statements about the Speaker of the Lok Sabha is correct?
(a) He vacates office as soon as the Lok Sabha is dissolved (b) He can vote on every motion in the House (c) He is appointed by the President (d) He continues in office until the newly elected Lok Sabha meets, even after dissolution
Answer: (d)
Solution: The vacates-on-dissolution option is the trap because it sounds logical that the Speaker's office ends with the House itself. The Speaker does not vacate office on dissolution and continues until immediately before the first meeting of the new Lok Sabha; he votes only in case of a tie, so the answer is the continuation statement.
