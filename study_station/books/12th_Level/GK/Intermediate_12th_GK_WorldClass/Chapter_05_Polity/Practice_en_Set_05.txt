101. Which Article of the Indian Constitution deals with the impeachment of the President?
(a) Article 61 (b) Article 56 (c) Article 72 (d) Article 74
Answer: (a)
Solution: Article 56 deals with the term of office of the President, which is a tempting confusion, but impeachment procedure is laid down in Article 61. Article 72 covers the President's pardoning power and Article 74 the Council of Ministers, so the answer is Article 61.

102. The idea of a Constituent Assembly for India was first put forward by
(a) M. N. Roy (b) Jawaharlal Nehru (c) Mahatma Gandhi (d) B. R. Ambedkar
Answer: (a)
Solution: Nehru and Gandhi are tempting because of their role in the freedom movement, but the demand for a Constituent Assembly was first raised by M. N. Roy in 1934. The Congress officially demanded it in 1935, so the answer is M. N. Roy.

103. Which Schedule of the Constitution contains provisions regarding the disqualification of legislators on grounds of defection?
(a) Tenth Schedule (b) Ninth Schedule (c) Eighth Schedule (d) Twelfth Schedule
Answer: (a)
Solution: The Ninth Schedule is tempting because it also protects laws from judicial review, but the anti-defection law was added by the 52nd Amendment of 1985 as the Tenth Schedule. The Eighth lists languages and the Twelfth covers municipalities, so the answer is the Tenth Schedule.

121. Consider the following statements about the Vice-President of India:
1. He is the ex-officio Chairman of the Rajya Sabha.
2. He is elected by members of both Houses of Parliament, including nominated members.
3. He can act as President for a maximum period of six months.
Which of the above is/are correct?
(a) 1 and 2 only (b) 1, 2 and 3 (c) 1 and 3 only (d) 2 and 3 only
Answer: (b)
Solution: Statement 3 is the trap — many assume the six-month limit applies only to a vacancy filled by election, but Article 65 read with Article 62 requires a new President within six months, capping the acting period; the electoral college for the Vice-President includes nominated members unlike the President's, so the answer is 1, 2 and 3.

122. Which of the following pairs is correctly matched?
(a) Habeas Corpus — to command a public official to perform duty (b) Quo Warranto — to question the legality of a claim to a public office (c) Mandamus — to produce a detained person before the court (d) Certiorari — to restrain a lower court from exceeding jurisdiction
Answer: (b)
Solution: Habeas Corpus is tempting in the first option, but it means producing the detained person, which is wrongly described there; Mandamus commands performance of duty and Prohibition, not Certiorari, restrains a lower court, so the answer is Quo Warranto — to question the legality of a claim to a public office.

123. Arrange the following in chronological order of their enactment:
1. 42nd Constitutional Amendment
2. 73rd Constitutional Amendment
3. 86th Constitutional Amendment
4. 52nd Constitutional Amendment
(a) 1, 2, 4, 3 (b) 4, 1, 2, 3 (c) 1, 4, 2, 3 (d) 4, 2, 1, 3
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is placing the 52nd Amendment before the 42nd because of its smaller number, but the 42nd came in 1976, the 52nd (anti-defection) in 1985, the 73rd (Panchayati Raj) in 1992 and the 86th (right to education) in 2002, so the answer is 1, 4, 2, 3.

124. Consider the following statements about a Governor:
1. He is appointed by the President and holds office during his pleasure.
2. The Constitution fixes his term at five years.
3. He must be a resident of the State to which he is appointed.
Which of the above is/are correct?
(a) 1 only (b) 2 and 3 only (c) 1 and 2 only (d) 1, 2 and 3
Answer: (c)
Solution: Statement 3 is the trap — it sounds like a natural qualification, but the Constitution deliberately does NOT require the Governor to be a resident of that State; the qualifications under Article 157 are citizenship and 35 years of age, so the answer is 1 and 2 only.

125. Which of the following is NOT a federal feature of the Indian Constitution?
(a) Division of powers between the Union and the States (b) Written Constitution (c) Independent judiciary (d) Single citizenship
Answer: (d)
Solution: Division of powers, a written Constitution and an independent judiciary are all classic federal features; single citizenship, borrowed from Britain, is a unitary feature of the Indian system, so the answer is Single citizenship.
