Questions 26–50: Medium

26. Which Article of the Indian Constitution deals with the amendment procedure?
(a) Article 368 (b) Article 360 (c) Article 370 (d) Article 356
Answer: (a)
Solution: Article 356 (President's Rule) and Article 360 (Financial Emergency) are tempting because all three are famous emergency-era Articles, but they deal with emergencies, not amendments. Article 368 in Part XX lays down the power and procedure of Parliament to amend the Constitution, so the answer is Article 368.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Indian Constitution at Work

27. The idea of the Preamble of the Indian Constitution was borrowed from the Constitution of which country?
(a) United Kingdom (b) Canada (c) United States of America (d) Ireland
Answer: (c)
Solution: Ireland is a trap because the Directive Principles were borrowed from it, and students often mix up the two borrowings. The Preamble as an introductory statement was inspired by the Constitution of the United States of America, so the answer is the United States of America.

28. Which of the following is NOT a Fundamental Right under the Indian Constitution?
(a) Right to Property (b) Right to Equality (c) Right to Freedom of Religion (d) Right to Constitutional Remedies
Answer: (a)
Solution: Right to Property is the classic trap — it was a Fundamental Right under Article 31 until the 44th Amendment Act, 1978 removed it and made it a legal right under Article 300A. The other three remain Fundamental Rights in Part III, so the answer is Right to Property.

29. The 42nd Constitutional Amendment Act, 1976 is often called the "Mini Constitution" because it —
(a) created the office of the Vice-President (b) lowered the voting age from 21 to 18 years (c) granted constitutional status to Panchayati Raj institutions (d) made extensive changes including adding words to the Preamble and adding Fundamental Duties
Answer: (d)
Solution: The options naming the voting age of 18 (61st Amendment) and Panchayati Raj (73rd Amendment) are chronological traps. The 42nd Amendment added "Socialist", "Secular" and "Integrity" to the Preamble and inserted Fundamental Duties (Article 51A), so the answer is the option describing these extensive changes.

30. Who presides over a joint sitting of the two Houses of Parliament summoned under Article 108?
(a) The President of India (b) The Vice-President of India (c) The Chief Justice of India (d) The Speaker of the Lok Sabha
Answer: (d)
Solution: The Vice-President is a tempting wrong choice because he chairs the Rajya Sabha, but a joint sitting is not a Rajya Sabha session. The President only summons the joint sitting; the Speaker of the Lok Sabha presides over it, so the answer is the Speaker of the Lok Sabha.

31. The Fundamental Duties were incorporated into the Constitution on the recommendation of which committee?
(a) Sarkaria Commission (b) Swaran Singh Committee (c) Balwant Rai Mehta Committee (d) Ashok Mehta Committee
Answer: (b)
Solution: The Mehta committees are traps relating to Panchayati Raj, and the Sarkaria Commission dealt with Centre–State relations. The Swaran Singh Committee (1976) recommended the inclusion of Fundamental Duties, added by the 42nd Amendment, so the answer is the Swaran Singh Committee.

32. A Money Bill can be introduced only in —
(a) the Lok Sabha (b) the Rajya Sabha (c) a joint sitting of both Houses (d) either House of Parliament
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Either House" is tempting because ordinary bills can be introduced in either House, but Article 109 restricts Money Bills to the Lok Sabha, and only on the President's recommendation. The Rajya Sabha can only make recommendations within 14 days, so the answer is the Lok Sabha.

33. Which Article empowers the Supreme Court to issue writs for the enforcement of Fundamental Rights?
(a) Article 143 (b) Article 226 (c) Article 136 (d) Article 32
Answer: (d)
Solution: Article 226 is the classic trap — it empowers High Courts, not the Supreme Court, to issue writs (and for other legal rights too). Dr. Ambedkar called Article 32 the "heart and soul" of the Constitution, so the answer is Article 32.

34. The concept of "Directive Principles of State Policy" in the Indian Constitution was borrowed from —
(a) the Constitution of Canada (b) the Constitution of Australia (c) the Constitution of Germany (d) the Constitution of Ireland
Answer: (d)
Solution: Canada is a trap because the federation with a strong Centre came from it, and Germany supplied the emergency provisions. The Directive Principles (Part IV, Articles 36–51) were borrowed from the Irish Constitution, so the answer is the Constitution of Ireland.

35. What is the minimum age prescribed for election as the President of India?
(a) 25 years (b) 30 years (c) 35 years (d) 40 years
Answer: (c)
Solution: 25 and 30 are traps — 25 is the minimum age for the Lok Sabha and State Assemblies, and 30 for the Rajya Sabha and Legislative Councils. Article 58 requires a presidential candidate to be at least 35 years of age, so the answer is 35 years.

36. The 73rd Constitutional Amendment Act, 1992 is related to —
(a) Municipalities (b) Cooperative Societies (c) Panchayati Raj Institutions (d) Anti-defection law
Answer: (c)
Solution: Municipalities is the near-miss trap — that subject belongs to the 74th Amendment passed in the same year, and examiners exploit the confusion. The 73rd Amendment gave constitutional status to Panchayati Raj Institutions and added the Eleventh Schedule, so the answer is Panchayati Raj Institutions.

37. Who is known as the "Father of the Indian Constitution"?
(a) Jawaharlal Nehru (b) Dr. Rajendra Prasad (c) Dr. B. R. Ambedkar (d) Sardar Vallabhbhai Patel
Answer: (c)
Solution: Dr. Rajendra Prasad is a similar-role trap — he was the President of the Constituent Assembly, not its drafting chief. Dr. B. R. Ambedkar chaired the Drafting Committee and piloted the Constitution, so the answer is Dr. B. R. Ambedkar.

38. Under which Article can the President impose President's Rule in a State?
(a) Article 360 (b) Article 352 (c) Article 356 (d) Article 365
Answer: (c)
Solution: Article 352 (National Emergency) and Article 360 (Financial Emergency) are number-confusion traps since all three sit in the emergency provisions. Article 356 deals with the failure of constitutional machinery in a State, leading to President's Rule, so the answer is Article 356.

39. The Constitution of India was adopted by the Constituent Assembly on —
(a) 26 November 1949 (b) 26 January 1949 (c) 15 August 1947 (d) 26 January 1950
Answer: (a)
Solution: 26 January 1950 is the most common trap — that is when the Constitution came into force, not when it was adopted. The Constituent Assembly adopted and enacted the Constitution on 26 November 1949, now celebrated as Constitution Day, so the answer is 26 November 1949.

40. Which Schedule of the Indian Constitution contains the list of recognised languages?
(a) Seventh Schedule (b) Eighth Schedule (c) Ninth Schedule (d) Tenth Schedule
Answer: (b)
Solution: The Tenth Schedule (anti-defection) and Seventh Schedule (three lists) are schedule-confusion traps. The Eighth Schedule lists the recognised languages — originally 14, now 22 after later amendments, so the answer is the Eighth Schedule.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Indian Constitution at Work

41. The power of judicial review in India is —
(a) exercised by the Supreme Court and High Courts to examine the constitutionality of laws (b) available only against executive actions, not legislation (c) explicitly borrowed from the United Kingdom (d) barred during a National Emergency
Answer: (a)
Solution: The UK option is a trap — judicial review comes from the USA, since the UK follows parliamentary sovereignty. In India the Supreme Court and High Courts review laws and executive actions against the Constitution, so the answer is the option describing review by the Supreme Court and High Courts.

42. How many Fundamental Duties are currently listed in Article 51A of the Constitution?
(a) 10 (b) 9 (c) 12 (d) 11
Answer: (d)
Solution: 10 is the tempting trap — the 42nd Amendment (1976) originally inserted ten Fundamental Duties. The 86th Amendment Act, 2002 added the eleventh duty (parent or guardian to provide education to children aged 6–14), so the answer is 11.

43. The Vice-President of India is elected by —
(a) an electoral college of elected members of the Lok Sabha only (b) an electoral college of elected members of both Houses of Parliament and elected members of State Legislative Assemblies (c) members of the Lok Sabha and State Legislative Assemblies (d) an electoral college consisting of members of both Houses of Parliament
Answer: (d)
Solution: The option including State Legislative Assemblies is the trap — that describes the President's electoral college, not the Vice-President's. Under Article 66, the Vice-President is elected by an electoral college consisting of members of both Houses of Parliament (elected and nominated), so the answer is the first option.

44. Which of the following pairs is correctly matched?
(a) Article 14 — Abolition of untouchability (b) Article 21 — Protection of life and personal liberty (c) Article 17 — Equality before law (d) Article 19 — Right against exploitation
Answer: (b)
Solution: The options scramble famous Articles: abolition of untouchability is Article 17, equality before law is Article 14, and Article 19 guarantees six freedoms — so the first and third options swap each other and the last mislabels Article 19. Article 21 protects life and personal liberty, so the answer is the second option.

45. The first session of the Constituent Assembly of India was held in —
(a) December 1946 (b) August 1947 (c) January 1947 (d) November 1949
Answer: (a)
Solution: August 1947 (Independence) and November 1949 (adoption of the Constitution) are chronological traps. The Constituent Assembly first met on 9 December 1946 in the Constitution Hall, New Delhi, with Dr. Sachchidananda Sinha as temporary chairman, so the answer is December 1946.

46. A Bill passed by Parliament becomes law only after —
(a) approval of the Supreme Court (b) assent of the President (c) ratification by half the States (d) publication in the Official Gazette
Answer: (b)
Solution: Ratification by half the States is a trap — that applies only to certain constitutional amendments under Article 368, not ordinary bills. Under Article 111, every bill passed by both Houses requires the President's assent to become an Act, so the answer is assent of the President.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Indian Constitution at Work

47. The "Basic Structure" doctrine of the Constitution was propounded by the Supreme Court in —
(a) Golaknath v. State of Punjab (b) Kesavananda Bharati v. State of Kerala (c) Minerva Mills v. Union of India (d) S. R. Bommai v. Union of India
Answer: (b)
Solution: Golaknath is the near-miss trap — it held that Parliament could not amend Fundamental Rights, but it did not propound the basic structure doctrine. The 13-judge bench in Kesavananda Bharati (1973) held that Parliament may amend the Constitution but not its basic structure, so the answer is Kesavananda Bharati v. State of Kerala.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Indian Constitution at Work

48. Which Article of the Constitution deals with the abolition of titles?
(a) Article 18 (b) Article 16 (c) Article 17 (d) Article 15
Answer: (a)
Solution: Article 17 is the adjacent-Article trap — it abolishes untouchability, not titles, and the two are frequently confused. Article 18 prohibits the State from conferring titles (except military and academic distinctions), so the answer is Article 18.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Indian Constitution at Work

49. The Speaker of the Lok Sabha can be removed from office by —
(a) a resolution of the Rajya Sabha passed by special majority (b) an order of the President on the advice of the Prime Minister (c) a resolution of the Lok Sabha passed by a majority of all its then members (d) impeachment by Parliament
Answer: (c)
Solution: Impeachment is a trap — that procedure applies to the President and judges, not the Speaker. Under Article 94, the Speaker is removed by a resolution of the Lok Sabha passed by a majority of all the then members (effective majority), after 14 days' notice, so the answer is the first option.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Indian Constitution at Work

50. The 86th Constitutional Amendment Act, 2002 is associated with —
(a) reservation in promotions for SCs and STs (b) making elementary education a Fundamental Right (c) constitutional status to cooperative societies (d) Goods and Services Tax
Answer: (b)
Solution: GST (101st Amendment, 2016) and cooperative societies (97th Amendment, 2011) are amendment-number traps. The 86th Amendment inserted Article 21A making free and compulsory education for children aged 6–14 a Fundamental Right, so the answer is making elementary education a Fundamental Right.
