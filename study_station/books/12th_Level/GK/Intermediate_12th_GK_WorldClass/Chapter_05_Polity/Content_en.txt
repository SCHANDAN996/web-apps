# Indian Polity — The Rulebook of the World's Largest Democracy

## 🪝 The Hook: A Book Written by 299 People

On 9 December 1946, in the Constitution Hall of New Delhi, the Constituent Assembly of India met for the first time. Outside, the country was heading towards Partition. Inside, a group of Indians was doing something no colony had ever done before — writing its own rulebook *before* fully tasting freedom.

It took them **2 years, 11 months and 18 days**. Dr. B.R. Ambedkar, chairman of the Drafting Committee, steered the Assembly through every clause and every debate. The result: the **longest written constitution of any sovereign nation** in the world.

Why does this matter to you? Because every year, SSC, Banking, Railway and State PCS exams ask dozens of questions from this one "rulebook" — and most students lose marks not on hard questions, but on **traps**: Article 352 vs 356, borrowed features mixed up, schedules counted wrong. This chapter will make you trap-proof.

---

## 🎯 Learning Objectives

By the end of this chapter, you will be able to:

1. Trace how the Constitution was made and what it borrowed from where.
2. Recall the Preamble, Fundamental Rights, DPSP and Fundamental Duties without confusion.
3. Distinguish clearly between the President, Prime Minister, Parliament, and Judiciary — who does what.
4. Handle "examiner's trap" questions: near-correct statements, reversed pairs, and hidden negatives.

## 1. Making of the Constitution

### The Constituent Assembly

- The idea of a Constituent Assembly was first put forward by **M.N. Roy in 1934**.
- The Assembly was formed under the **Cabinet Mission Plan (1946)**.
- **First meeting: 9 December 1946**; **Dr. Sachchidananda Sinha** was the temporary (interim) President.
- **Dr. Rajendra Prasad** was elected permanent President on 11 December 1946.
- **Dr. B.R. Ambedkar** chaired the **Drafting Committee** (set up on 29 August 1947).
- The Constitution was **adopted on 26 November 1949** (celebrated as Constitution Day / Samvidhan Divas) and **came into force on 26 January 1950** (chosen because of *Purna Swaraj Day*, 26 January 1930).

🧠 **Active Recall:** Who was the *first/temporary* President of the Constituent Assembly, and who was the *permanent* one? Why do exams love this pair? (Answer: Sinha vs Rajendra Prasad — a classic "similar names" trap.)

### Sources: The "Borrowed" Constitution

| Feature | Borrowed from |
|---|---|
| Parliamentary system, Rule of Law, Single Citizenship | Britain |
| Fundamental Rights, Judicial Review, Impeachment of President | USA |
| Directive Principles (DPSP) | Ireland |
| Emergency provisions | Germany (Weimar) |
| Federal system with strong Centre, Residuary powers with Centre | Canada |
| Concurrent List | Australia |
| Amendment procedure | South Africa |
| Republic, ideals of liberty-equality-fraternity | France |
| Five-Year Plans concept | USSR |

**Mnemonic 🧠:** *"B-U-I-G-C-A-S-F-R"* — **B**ritain gave Parliament, **U**SA gave Rights, **I**reland gave DPSP, **G**ermany gave Emergency, **C**anada gave Federation, **A**ustralia gave Concurrent List, **S**outh Africa gave Amendment, **F**rance gave Republic, **R**ussia (USSR) gave Planning.

## 2. The Preamble — The Identity Card

The Preamble declares India a **Sovereign, Socialist, Secular, Democratic Republic** and promises **Justice, Liberty, Equality, Fraternity**.

- The words **"Socialist" and "Secular"** were added by the **42nd Amendment, 1976** (the "Mini-Constitution").
- The Preamble is based on the **Objectives Resolution** moved by **Jawaharlal Nehru** (December 1946).
- In the **Kesavananda Bharati case (1973)**, the Supreme Court held the Preamble is a **part of the Constitution**.

**Mnemonic 🧠:** Order of Justice in the Preamble — **"S-E-P"**: **S**ocial, **E**conomic, **P**olitical. (Examiners shuffle this order — remember: society first, then economy, then politics!)

🧠 **Active Recall:** Which amendment added "Secular" to the Preamble, and in which year?

## 3. Fundamental Rights (Part III, Articles 12–35)

Originally **7** rights; now **6** (Right to Property was removed by the **44th Amendment, 1978** and made a legal right under Article 300A).

| Right | Articles |
|---|---|
| Right to Equality | 14–18 |
| Right to Freedom | 19–22 |
| Right against Exploitation | 23–24 |
| Right to Freedom of Religion | 25–28 |
| Cultural & Educational Rights | 29–30 |
| Right to Constitutional Remedies | 32 |

- **Article 32** — Ambedkar called it the **"heart and soul"** of the Constitution. It lets you move the Supreme Court directly (writs: *habeas corpus, mandamus, prohibition, certiorari, quo warranto*).
- **Article 21** (Right to Life) cannot be suspended even during Emergency (44th Amendment).
- **Article 14** = Equality before law (British concept); **Article 19** = six freedoms (speech, assembly, association, movement, residence, profession).

🧠 **Active Recall:** Which Article did Ambedkar call the "heart and soul"? Which right was deleted in 1978?

## 4. DPSP & Fundamental Duties

- **DPSP (Part IV, Articles 36–51):** borrowed from **Ireland**; **non-justiciable** (cannot be enforced by courts). Examples: Article 40 (village panchayats), Article 44 (Uniform Civil Code), Article 48 (prohibition of cow slaughter), Article 50 (separation of judiciary from executive).
- **Fundamental Duties (Article 51A, Part IVA):** added by the **42nd Amendment, 1976**, on the recommendation of the **Swaran Singh Committee**; inspired by the **USSR**. Originally 10 duties; the **11th** was added by the **86th Amendment, 2002** (parent/guardian to provide education to child aged 6–14).

**Trap alert:** DPSP = Ireland; Fundamental Duties = USSR. Exams swap these constantly.

## 5. The Union Executive

### President (Articles 52–62)
- **Nominal head**; real power lies with the Council of Ministers headed by the PM (Article 74).
- Elected by an **Electoral College** (elected MPs + elected MLAs of states and UTs of Delhi & Puducherry).
- Term: **5 years**; eligible for re-election; impeachment under **Article 61** for "violation of the Constitution."
- **Article 72:** pardoning power (including death sentences).

### Prime Minister & Council of Ministers
- PM is appointed by the President; must be a member of either House (or become one within **6 months**).
- **Article 75:** Ministers are **collectively responsible** to the **Lok Sabha**.

### Vice-President
- Ex-officio **Chairman of the Rajya Sabha**; acts as President for a maximum of **6 months** in case of vacancy.

---

## 6. Parliament (Articles 79–122)

- **Lok Sabha (House of the People):** maximum strength **552** (530 from states + 20 from Union Territories + 2 nominated Anglo-Indian members, as provided in Article 81); members directly elected; normal term **5 years**; minimum age **25**. (Note: the provision for nominating Anglo-Indian members was discontinued by the 104th Amendment, 2020.)
- **Rajya Sabha (Council of States):** maximum 250 (238 elected + 12 nominated by the President); **permanent body** — one-third members retire every **2 years**; term of a member **6 years**; minimum age **30**.
- **Money Bill (Article 110):** can be introduced **only in Lok Sabha**; Rajya Sabha can only suggest, and must return it within **14 days**. Speaker certifies whether a bill is a Money Bill.
- **Joint sitting (Article 108):** summoned by the **President**, presided over by the **Lok Sabha Speaker**.

🧠 **Active Recall:** Who presides over a joint sitting? (Not the Vice-President — a favourite trap!)

---

## 7. The Judiciary

- **Supreme Court (Article 124):** established 28 January 1950; originally 8 judges (1 Chief Justice + 7); strength is fixed by Parliament by law.
- Judges retire at **65** (Supreme Court) and **62** (High Courts).
- **Article 226:** High Courts can issue writs — note this power is **wider** than the Supreme Court's Article 32 (HCs can issue writs for ordinary legal rights too).
- **Judicial review** and the **Basic Structure doctrine** (Kesavananda Bharati, 1973) keep Parliament's amending power in check.

## 8. Emergency Provisions (Part XVIII)

| Emergency | Article | Ground |
|---|---|---|
| National Emergency | 352 | War, external aggression, armed rebellion |
| President's Rule (State Emergency) | 356 | Failure of constitutional machinery in a state |
| Financial Emergency | 360 | Threat to financial stability (never proclaimed so far) |

- "Armed rebellion" replaced "internal disturbance" via the **44th Amendment, 1978**.
- President's Rule needs parliamentary approval within **2 months**; maximum **3 years** with conditions.

## 10. Schedules — Count Carefully!

Originally **8 schedules**; now **12**:

- **9th** — added by 1st Amendment, 1951 (land reforms, protection from judicial review)
- **10th** — added by 52nd Amendment, 1985 (**Anti-Defection Law**)
- **11th** — added by 73rd Amendment, 1992 (**Panchayats**, 29 subjects)
- **12th** — added by 74th Amendment, 1992 (**Municipalities**, 18 subjects)

The **8th Schedule** lists **22 scheduled languages** (originally 14).

## ⚠️ Caution: Examiner's Trap

**Trap 1 — लगभग सही कथन (Near-Correct Statement):**
"The President is elected by all members of Parliament and State Assemblies."
❌ Only **elected** members vote — nominated members do NOT. One word changes everything.

**Trap 2 — कालानुक्रमिक भ्रम (Chronological Confusion):**
"The Constitution was adopted on 26 January 1950."
❌ Adopted **26 Nov 1949**, enforced **26 Jan 1950**. Exams swap these two dates relentlessly.

**Trap 3 — समान नाम/जोड़ी उलटना (Swapped Pairs):**
"DPSP were borrowed from the USSR and Fundamental Duties from Ireland."
❌ It's the **reverse**: DPSP → Ireland, Duties → USSR. Also watch: National Emergency (352) vs President's Rule (356) — digits get shuffled.
