1. The Constitution of India was adopted by the Constituent Assembly on:
(a) 26 November 1949 (b) 15 August 1947 (c) 26 January 1950 (d) 2 October 1950
Answer: (a)
Solution: Many students confuse the adoption date with the enforcement date. The Constitution was adopted on 26 November 1949 (now celebrated as Constitution Day), while it came into force on 26 January 1950. So the answer is 26 November 1949.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Indian Constitution at Work

2. Who was the Chairman of the Drafting Committee of the Constituent Assembly?
(a) Jawaharlal Nehru (b) Sardar Vallabhbhai Patel (c) Dr. B. R. Ambedkar (d) Dr. Rajendra Prasad
Answer: (c)
Solution: Dr. Rajendra Prasad was the President of the Constituent Assembly, a common confusion, but the Drafting Committee was chaired by Dr. B. R. Ambedkar. So the answer is Dr. B. R. Ambedkar.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Indian Constitution at Work

3. The idea of the Preamble of the Indian Constitution was borrowed from the Constitution of:
(a) United Kingdom (b) Ireland (c) United States of America (d) Canada
Answer: (c)
Solution: Ireland is a tempting wrong option because the Directive Principles were borrowed from Ireland, but the Preamble's idea came from the US Constitution. So the answer is the United States of America.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Indian Constitution at Work

4. How many Fundamental Rights are currently guaranteed by the Indian Constitution?
(a) Six (b) Seven (c) Five (d) Eight
Answer: (a)
Solution: Originally there were seven Fundamental Rights, but the Right to Property was removed by the 44th Amendment (1978), leaving six. So the answer is six.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Indian Constitution at Work

5. Which Article of the Indian Constitution abolishes untouchability?
(a) Article 14 (b) Article 17 (c) Article 15 (d) Article 19
Answer: (b)
Solution: Article 14 deals with equality before law and Article 15 with prohibition of discrimination, which often confuse students; Article 17 specifically abolishes untouchability. So the answer is Article 17.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Indian Constitution at Work

6. The Directive Principles of State Policy are contained in which Part of the Constitution?
(a) Part III (b) Part V (c) Part IV-A (d) Part IV
Answer: (d)
Solution: Part III contains Fundamental Rights and Part IV-A contains Fundamental Duties, both common confusions; Directive Principles are in Part IV (Articles 36–51). So the answer is Part IV.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Indian Constitution at Work

7. Fundamental Duties were added to the Constitution by which amendment?
(a) 52nd Amendment, 1985 (b) 42nd Amendment, 1976 (c) 44th Amendment, 1978 (d) 73rd Amendment, 1992
Answer: (b)
Solution: The 44th Amendment is a tempting option because it also followed the Emergency, but Fundamental Duties (Article 51A) were inserted by the 42nd Amendment, 1976, on the recommendation of the Swaran Singh Committee. So the answer is the 42nd Amendment, 1976.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Indian Constitution at Work

8. Who is known as the 'Father of the Indian Constitution'?
(a) Dr. B. R. Ambedkar (b) Mahatma Gandhi (c) Dr. Rajendra Prasad (d) B. N. Rau
Answer: (a)
Solution: B. N. Rau was the Constitutional Adviser, not the chief architect; Dr. B. R. Ambedkar, as Chairman of the Drafting Committee, is called the Father of the Indian Constitution. So the answer is Dr. B. R. Ambedkar.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Indian Constitution at Work

9. The minimum age to become the President of India is:
(a) 25 years (b) 30 years (c) 40 years (d) 35 years
Answer: (d)
Solution: 25 years is the minimum age for Lok Sabha and Vidhan Sabha membership, a frequent trap; the President, Vice-President and Governors must be at least 35 years old. So the answer is 35 years.

10. Who administers the oath of office to the President of India?
(a) The Chief Justice of India (b) The Prime Minister (c) The Vice-President (d) The Speaker of Lok Sabha
Answer: (a)
Solution: The Chief Justice of India administers the oath to the President; in the CJI's absence, the senior-most judge of the Supreme Court does so. So the answer is the Chief Justice of India.

11. The maximum strength of the Lok Sabha as fixed by the Constitution is:
(a) 545 (b) 552 (c) 550 (d) 560
Answer: (b)
Solution: 545 was the actual elected strength for many years, which misleads students; the constitutional maximum is 552 (530 from States, 20 from Union Territories, and earlier 2 Anglo-Indian nominees, a provision now abolished). So the answer is 552.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Indian Constitution at Work

12. Money Bills can be introduced only in:
(a) Lok Sabha (b) Rajya Sabha (c) Either House of Parliament (d) A joint sitting of both Houses
Answer: (a)
Solution: Under Article 109, a Money Bill can be introduced only in the Lok Sabha, and the Rajya Sabha can only make recommendations within 14 days. So the answer is Lok Sabha.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Indian Constitution at Work

13. Who decides whether a Bill is a Money Bill or not?
(a) The Speaker of the Lok Sabha (b) The Chairman of the Rajya Sabha (c) The President (d) The Finance Minister
Answer: (a)
Solution: Under Article 110, the Speaker of the Lok Sabha has the final authority to certify whether a Bill is a Money Bill, and this decision cannot be questioned. So the answer is the Speaker of the Lok Sabha.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Indian Constitution at Work

14. The judges of the Supreme Court of India retire at the age of:
(a) 60 years (b) 65 years (c) 62 years (d) 70 years
Answer: (b)
Solution: 62 years is the retirement age of High Court judges, a classic examiner trap; Supreme Court judges retire at 65 years. So the answer is 65 years.

15. Which Article empowers the President to promulgate ordinances when Parliament is not in session?
(a) Article 213 (b) Article 123 (c) Article 352 (d) Article 360
Answer: (b)
Solution: Article 213 is the tempting wrong option because it deals with the same ordinance power, but for State Governors; Article 123 covers the President's ordinance-making power. So the answer is Article 123.

16. The Panchayati Raj system was constitutionalised by which amendment?
(a) 73rd Amendment (b) 52nd Amendment (c) 42nd Amendment (d) 74th Amendment
Answer: (a)
Solution: The 74th Amendment is the close distractor, but it deals with Municipalities (urban local bodies); the 73rd Amendment (1992) gave constitutional status to Panchayati Raj institutions. So the answer is the 73rd Amendment.

17. The concept of Judicial Review in India was borrowed from the Constitution of:
(a) United States of America (b) South Africa (c) Australia (d) United Kingdom
Answer: (a)
Solution: The UK follows parliamentary sovereignty, so it is a wrong lure; India borrowed judicial review from the US Constitution. So the answer is the United States of America.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Indian Constitution at Work

18. Which of the following is NOT a Fundamental Right under the Indian Constitution?
(a) Right to Equality (b) Right to Freedom of Religion (c) Right to Property (d) Right against Exploitation
Answer: (c)
Solution: The Right to Property was a Fundamental Right under Article 31 until the 44th Amendment (1978) made it a legal right under Article 300A; the other three remain Fundamental Rights. So the answer is Right to Property.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Indian Constitution at Work

19. The Election Commission of India is a:
(a) Statutory body (b) Executive body (c) Constitutional body (d) Advisory body
Answer: (c)
Solution: The Election Commission is established directly by Article 324 of the Constitution, making it a constitutional body, not one created by an Act of Parliament. So the answer is constitutional body.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Indian Constitution at Work

20. The words 'Socialist' and 'Secular' were added to the Preamble by:
(a) 52nd Amendment, 1985 (b) 42nd Amendment, 1976 (c) 44th Amendment, 1978 (d) 61st Amendment, 1989
Answer: (b)
Solution: The 42nd Amendment (1976), often called the 'Mini Constitution', inserted 'Socialist', 'Secular' and 'Integrity' into the Preamble. So the answer is the 42nd Amendment, 1976.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Indian Constitution at Work

21. Consider the following statements about the Vice-President of India:
1. He is the ex-officio Chairman of the Rajya Sabha.
2. He is elected by members of both Houses of Parliament.
3. He can be removed by a resolution of the Lok Sabha alone.
Which of the statements given above is/are correct?
(a) 1, 2 and 3 (b) 2 and 3 only (c) 1 and 3 only (d) 1 and 2 only
Answer: (d)
Solution: Statement 3 is the trap: removal of the Vice-President requires a resolution of the Rajya Sabha passed by an effective majority and agreed to by the Lok Sabha — the Lok Sabha alone cannot remove him. Statements 1 and 2 are correct, so the answer is 1 and 2 only.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Indian Constitution at Work

22. Which of the following pairs of a constitutional feature and its source country is correctly matched?
(a) Judicial Review — United Kingdom (b) Concurrent List — Canada (c) Directive Principles of State Policy — Ireland (d) Fundamental Rights — Germany
Answer: (c)
Solution: The traps: the Concurrent List came from Australia, judicial review from the USA, and Fundamental Rights from the USA as well — not Germany. The Directive Principles of State Policy were borrowed from the Irish Constitution, so the answer is Directive Principles of State Policy — Ireland.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Indian Constitution at Work

23. Consider the following statements about a joint sitting of Parliament:
1. It is summoned by the President under Article 108.
2. It is presided over by the Chairman of the Rajya Sabha.
3. It cannot be held for a Money Bill.
Which of the statements given above is/are correct?
(a) 1 only (b) 1, 2 and 3 (c) 2 and 3 only (d) 1 and 3 only
Answer: (d)
Solution: Statement 2 is the trap: a joint sitting is presided over by the Speaker of the Lok Sabha, not the Chairman of the Rajya Sabha. Statements 1 and 3 are correct, so the answer is 1 and 3 only.

24. Arrange the following constitutional amendments in the correct chronological order:
1. 86th Amendment (Right to Education)
2. 42nd Amendment (Mini Constitution)
3. 73rd Amendment (Panchayati Raj)
4. 44th Amendment (Right to Property removed from Fundamental Rights)
(a) 2, 3, 4, 1 (b) 4, 2, 3, 1 (c) 2, 4, 3, 1 (d) 2, 4, 1, 3
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is swapping the 42nd and 44th Amendments: the 42nd came in 1976 and the 44th in 1978, followed by the 73rd in 1992 and the 86th in 2002. The correct sequence is 2, 4, 3, 1, so the answer is 2, 4, 3, 1.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Indian Constitution at Work

25. Consider the following statements about the Attorney General of India:
1. He is appointed by the President under Article 76.
2. He has the right to speak in either House of Parliament but cannot vote.
3. He must be qualified to be appointed a judge of a High Court.
Which of the statements given above is/are correct?
(a) 1 and 3 only (b) 2 and 3 only (c) 1, 2 and 3 (d) 1 and 2 only
Answer: (d)
Solution: Statement 3 is the trap: the Attorney General must be qualified to be appointed a judge of the Supreme Court, not merely a High Court. Statements 1 and 2 are correct, so the answer is 1 and 2 only.
