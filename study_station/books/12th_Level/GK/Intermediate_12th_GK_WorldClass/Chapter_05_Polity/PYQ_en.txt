# 📊 PYQ Analysis — Indian Polity (Intermediate Level)

## 1. How Exams Ask Questions from This Chapter

Indian Polity is one of the most dependable scoring areas across SSC (CGL, CHSL, MTS, GD), Banking (IBPS, SBI), Railway (NTPC, Group D) and State PCS prelims. The questions are almost always **direct and fact-based**, but the examiner earns his money through *options*, not through the question stem. The stem is simple; the trap sits in the choices.

**What examiners repeatedly ask:**

- **Articles and their content** — "Which Article deals with…?" is the single most repeated format. Articles 14–18, 21, 32, 74, 110, 112, 148, 280, 324, 352, 356, 360 and 368 appear again and again.
- **Borrowed features of the Constitution** — matching a feature (e.g., Directive Principles, Emergency provisions, Concurrent List) with the country it was borrowed from.
- **Schedules and Parts** — especially the 8th Schedule (languages), 10th Schedule (anti-defection), and which Part deals with what.
- **Constitutional Amendments** — 42nd (Mini-Constitution), 44th, 52nd, 61st, 73rd, 74th, 86th, 91st and 101st are the favourites.
- **Office-holders** — appointment, tenure, removal, and who administers the oath (President, Vice-President, Governor, CJI, CAG, CEC).
- **Parliamentary procedure** — Money Bill vs Ordinary Bill, joint sitting (Article 108), Question Hour, quorum, presiding officers.
- **Panchayati Raj & Municipalities** — 73rd/74th Amendments, three-tier structure, reservation of seats.
- **Fundamental Rights vs DPSP vs Fundamental Duties** — classification questions and "which of the following is NOT…" formats.

## 2. Sub-topic Weightage (in words, not numbers)

- **Heaviest:** Fundamental Rights, the President & Parliament, Constitutional Amendments, and borrowed features. A serious aspirant cannot skip these.
- **Moderate:** Judiciary (Supreme Court/High Courts), Panchayati Raj, Schedules, Constitutional bodies (Election Commission, CAG, Finance Commission, UPSC).
- **Light but regular:** Preamble, citizenship, Fundamental Duties, special provisions (questions on the old Article 370 have shifted to post-2019 J&K reorganisation facts), non-constitutional bodies (NITI Aayog, NHRC).

**Exam-wise flavour:**

- **SSC / Railway:** pure one-liner recall — Articles, amendments, "who appoints whom."
- **Banking:** fewer polity questions, but when they come they favour constitutional bodies, recent amendments and Money Bill / budget procedure.
- **State PCS:** adds state-level polity — Governor, State Legislature, State Public Service Commission, Panchayati Raj in that state.

## 3. The Examiner's Traps (जाल) — Know Them by Name

1. **Almost Correct Statement** — "The Vice-President is elected by members of both Houses of Parliament *and* State Legislative Assemblies." Everything is right except the last clause. Read every word.
2. **Chronological Confusion** — amendments and events shuffled: 42nd vs 44th Amendment effects, or the order of words added to the Preamble.
3. **Negative Phrasing** — "Which of the following is **NOT** a Fundamental Right?" Under time pressure, candidates pick the first true statement. Circle the NOT.
4. **Similar-Sounding Names** — Article 32 vs 226 (both writs, different courts); CAG vs CEC; 73rd vs 74th Amendment.
5. **Number Swap** — 5 years vs 6 years tenure; 250 vs 245 Rajya Sabha strength; 552 vs 550 Lok Sabha maximum.
6. **Borrowed-Feature Mix-up** — DPSP from *Ireland*, not Britain; Emergency from *Germany*, not the USA.
7. **"Only" Trap** — "Money Bill can be introduced *only* in the Lok Sabha" (true) vs "Constitution Amendment Bill can be introduced *only* in the Lok Sabha" (false — either House).

## 4. Top 10 High-Yield PYQ-Style Questions with Solutions

**Q1. The Directive Principles of State Policy in the Indian Constitution were borrowed from the Constitution of:**
(a) USA (b) Ireland (c) Britain (d) Canada
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** DPSPs (Part IV, Articles 36–51) were borrowed from the Irish Constitution. Trap: *Almost Correct* — Britain tempts because our parliamentary system is British, but DPSP is Irish.
**Source:** NCERT Class 11, Indian Constitution at Work

**Q2. Which Article of the Constitution empowers the Supreme Court to issue writs for the enforcement of Fundamental Rights?**
(a) Article 13 (b) Article 21 (c) Article 32 (d) Article 226
**Answer: (c)**
**Solution:** Article 32 empowers the Supreme Court; Article 226 empowers High Courts. Dr. Ambedkar called Article 32 the "heart and soul" of the Constitution. Trap: *Similar-Sounding Names* — 32 vs 226 is the classic confusion.
**Source:** NCERT Class 11, Indian Constitution at Work

**Q3. Which of the following is NOT a Fundamental Right under the Indian Constitution?**
(a) Right to Equality (b) Right to Property (c) Right to Freedom of Religion (d) Right against Exploitation
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** The Right to Property was removed from Fundamental Rights by the 44th Amendment (1978) and made a legal right under Article 300A. Trap: *Negative Phrasing* — the word NOT decides the answer.
**Source:** NCERT Class 11, Indian Constitution at Work

**Q4. The 73rd Constitutional Amendment Act is related to:**
(a) Municipalities (b) Panchayati Raj Institutions (c) Anti-defection (d) Voting age reduction
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** 73rd Amendment (1992) → Panchayati Raj (Part IX, 11th Schedule); 74th → Municipalities (Part IXA, 12th Schedule). Trap: *Similar-Sounding Names* — 73rd and 74th are deliberately placed together.
**Source:** NCERT Class 11, Local Governments

**Q5. A Money Bill can be introduced only in:**
(a) Rajya Sabha (b) Lok Sabha (c) Either House (d) A joint sitting
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** Under Article 110, a Money Bill can be introduced only in the Lok Sabha, on the President's recommendation. The Rajya Sabha can only suggest changes within 14 days, which the Lok Sabha may reject. Trap: *"Only" Trap* — do not generalise this rule to all bills.
**Source:** NCERT Class 11, Legislature

**Q6. Which Amendment Act is known as the 'Mini-Constitution'?**
(a) 24th (b) 42nd (c) 44th (d) 52nd
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** The 42nd Amendment (1976) made sweeping changes — added 'Socialist', 'Secular', 'Integrity' to the Preamble, added Fundamental Duties, and altered the President–Council of Ministers relationship. Trap: *Chronological Confusion* — the 44th (1978) *undid* several 42nd-Amendment provisions; examiners swap their effects.
**Source:** NCERT Class 11, Indian Constitution at Work

**Q7. The Comptroller and Auditor General of India is appointed by:**
(a) The Prime Minister (b) The Parliament (c) The President (d) The Chief Justice of India
**Answer: (c)**
**Solution:** The CAG (Article 148) is appointed by the President and holds office for 6 years or up to age 65, whichever is earlier. Trap: *Almost Correct* — "Parliament" tempts because the CAG's reports go to Parliament, but appointment is by the President.
**Source:** NCERT Class 11, Executive

**Q8. Which Schedule of the Constitution deals with the anti-defection law?**
(a) 7th (b) 8th (c) 9th (d) 10th
**Answer: (d)**
**Solution:** The 10th Schedule, added by the 52nd Amendment (1985), contains the anti-defection provisions. The 8th Schedule lists official languages; the 7th has the three lists. Trap: *Number Swap* — schedules 7, 8, 9, 10 are shuffled in options.
**Source:** NCERT Class 11, Indian Constitution at Work

**Q9. The maximum strength of the Lok Sabha envisaged by the Constitution is:**
(a) 545 (b) 550 (c) 552 (d) 560
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** Article 81 originally fixed the maximum at 552 (530 from States + 20 from UTs + 2 nominated Anglo-Indians). The 104th Amendment (2020) discontinued the Anglo-Indian nomination, so the constitutional maximum is now **550**. Trap: *Number Swap* — 552 was the pre-2020 figure and still appears in older books; 545 is the *actual* elected strength often seen in news, not the constitutional maximum.
**Source:** NCERT Class 11, Legislature; 104th Constitutional Amendment Act, 2020

**Q10. Who administers the oath of office to the President of India?**
(a) The Vice-President (b) The Prime Minister (c) The Chief Justice of India (d) The Speaker of Lok Sabha
**Answer: (c)**
**Solution:** The Chief Justice of India administers the oath to the President (Article 60); in the CJI's absence, the senior-most Supreme Court judge does so. Trap: *Almost Correct* — the Vice-President tempts because he/she succeeds the President, but does not swear him/her in.
**Source:** NCERT Class 11, Executive

## 5. The 15/45-Second Rule Applied

**The rule:** If a question is pure recall (an Article, an amendment number, an appointment), you should answer in **15 seconds** — you either know it or you don't. If it is a statement-based or NOT-type question, allow **45 seconds** to read every option fully. Never spend 45 seconds on a 15-second recall question, and never rush a 45-second statement question in 15 seconds.

**Applied to Q3 (Right to Property):** This is a *45-second* question. The word NOT means every option must be checked. A candidate who reads fast sees "Right to Equality — yes, that's a Fundamental Right" and marks it in 10 seconds — and loses the mark. Slow down; the trap is in the phrasing, not the facts.

**Applied to Q6 (Mini-Constitution):** This is a *15-second* question. "Mini-Constitution = 42nd Amendment" is a fixed association. If you know it, mark and move. If you don't, no amount of staring will help — flag it, guess only if negative marking permits, and bank the saved 30 seconds for a statement question later.

**Exam strategy takeaway:** Roughly two-thirds of polity questions in SSC/Railway are 15-second recall. Master the high-frequency Articles, amendments and schedules so thoroughly that these become free marks, and reserve your thinking time for the NOT-type and multi-statement questions where the examiner actually sets his traps.
