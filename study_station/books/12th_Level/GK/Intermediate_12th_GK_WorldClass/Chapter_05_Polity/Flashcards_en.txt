Card 1
Front: Who is called the "Father of the Indian Constitution"?
Back: Dr. B.R. Ambedkar, Chairman of the Drafting Committee of the Constituent Assembly.

Card 2
Front: From which country did India borrow the idea of Fundamental Rights?
Back: The United States of America (USA).

Card 3
Front: Which Article of the Constitution abolishes untouchability?
Back: Article 17. It is an absolute right — no law can permit untouchability in any form.

Card 4
Front: How many Fundamental Duties are there, and which Amendment added them?
Back: 11 Fundamental Duties. Originally 10 were added by the 42nd Amendment (1976); the 11th was added by the 86th Amendment (2002).

Card 5
Front: Which Article gives the President the power to declare a National Emergency?
Back: Article 352. It can be proclaimed on grounds of war, external aggression, or armed rebellion.

Card 6
Front: What is the minimum age to become the President of India?
Back: 35 years. The person must also be qualified for election as a member of the Lok Sabha.

Card 7
Front: Who presides over a joint sitting of the two Houses of Parliament?
Back: The Speaker of the Lok Sabha. The joint sitting is summoned by the President under Article 108, and the Speaker presides over it.

Card 8
Front: Which mnemonic helps you remember the 6 Fundamental Rights in Articles 14–32?
Back: "Equality, Freedom, Exploitation, Religion, Culture, Remedies" — remember "EFERCR" or the phrase "Every Free Indian Enjoys Rights & Culture with Remedies."

Card 9
Front: Trick question: Is the Vice-President a member of the Rajya Sabha?
Back: No. The Vice-President is the ex-officio Chairman of the Rajya Sabha but is NOT a member of it. Examiners often trap students here.

Card 10
Front: Which Amendment is known as the "Mini-Constitution"?
Back: The 42nd Constitutional Amendment, 1976. It added "Socialist, Secular, Integrity" to the Preamble and added Fundamental Duties.

Card 11
Front: Who has the power to pardon a death sentence — and under which Article?
Back: The President, under Article 72. The Governor has a similar but narrower power under Article 161 (cannot pardon death sentence, only commute).

Card 12
Front: Examiner's trap: Can the Governor pardon a death sentence?
Back: No. Under Article 161, the Governor can only suspend, remit, or commute a death sentence — full pardon power lies only with the President (Article 72).

Card 13
Front: Which Schedule of the Constitution contains the list of official languages?
Back: The Eighth Schedule. It originally had 14 languages; now it has 22.

Card 14
Front: What is the tenure of a Rajya Sabha member, and why is it called a "permanent body"?
Back: 6 years. It is permanent because it is never dissolved; one-third of its members retire every 2 years.

Card 15
Front: Which Article deals with the amendment procedure of the Constitution?
Back: Article 368 (Part XX). Some amendments need a simple majority, others a special majority, and some also need ratification by half the states.

Card 16
Front: Memory trick: How do you quickly recall the three words added to the Preamble by the 42nd Amendment?
Back: Remember "SSI" — Socialist, Secular, Integrity. These were the only words ever added to the Preamble.

Card 17
Front: Who was the first woman President of India?
Back: Pratibha Patil (2007–2012). She was the 12th President.

Card 18
Front: Under which Article can the Supreme Court issue writs for enforcement of Fundamental Rights?
Back: Article 32 — called the "heart and soul of the Constitution" by Dr. Ambedkar. High Courts issue writs under Article 226.

Card 19
Front: Examiner's trap: Are Directive Principles of State Policy enforceable by courts?
Back: No. DPSPs (Part IV, Articles 36–51) are non-justiciable — courts cannot enforce them, unlike Fundamental Rights.

Card 20
Front: Which Constitutional Amendment lowered the voting age from 21 to 18 years?
Back: The 61st Constitutional Amendment, 1989 (amending Article 326).
