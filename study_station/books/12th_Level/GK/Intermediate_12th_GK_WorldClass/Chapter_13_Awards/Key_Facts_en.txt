# Key Facts & Figures — Awards & Honours

## Part 1: High-Yield Facts

| Fact | Description | Examiner's Trap | Trap Type |
|---|---|---|---|
| Bharat Ratna | Highest civilian award of India; instituted 1954; first recipients: C. Rajagopalachari, S. Radhakrishnan, C.V. Raman | Confusing it with Padma Vibhushan (2nd highest) | Rank confusion |
| Bharat Ratna limit | Maximum 3 awards per year (normally) | Claiming a fixed quota of exactly 3 every year | Near-true statement |
| Padma Awards order | Padma Vibhushan > Padma Bhushan > Padma Shri | Swapping Bhushan and Shri order | Order confusion |
| Padma Awards announcement | Announced on Republic Day eve (25/26 January) | Confusing with Independence Day | Date confusion |
| Gandhi Peace Prize | Instituted 1995 (125th birth anniversary of Gandhi); for social/economic/political transformation through non-violence | Confusing with Indira Gandhi Peace Prize (1986) | Similar names |
| Nobel Prize founder | Alfred Nobel; prizes first awarded 1901; 6 categories (Peace added? No — Economics added 1968) | Claiming Economics was an original Nobel category | Near-true statement |
| Nobel Peace Prize venue | Awarded in Oslo, Norway; all others in Stockholm, Sweden | Claiming all Nobels are given in Stockholm | Venue trap |
| First Indian Nobel laureate | Rabindranath Tagore, Literature, 1913 (Gitanjali) | Confusing with C.V. Raman (Physics, 1930) | Chronology confusion |
| C.V. Raman | Nobel Physics 1930 for Raman Effect; National Science Day = 28 February | Confusing discovery date with award year | Date confusion |
| Mother Teresa | Nobel Peace Prize 1979; Bharat Ratna 1980 | Swapping the years | Chronology confusion |
| Kailash Satyarthi | Nobel Peace Prize 2014 (shared with Malala Yousafzai) | Claiming he won alone | Shared-award trap |
| Amartya Sen | Nobel Economics 1998; Bharat Ratna 1999 | Confusing with Abhijit Banerjee (Economics 2019) | Similar field confusion |
| Param Vir Chakra | Highest wartime gallantry award | Confusing with Ashoka Chakra (peacetime) | Rank confusion |
| Gallantry order (wartime) | Param Vir Chakra > Maha Vir Chakra > Vir Chakra | Swapping Maha Vir and Vir Chakra | Order confusion |
| Gallantry order (peacetime) | Ashoka Chakra > Kirti Chakra > Shaurya Chakra | Mixing wartime and peacetime lists | Category confusion |
| First Param Vir Chakra | Major Somnath Sharma, 1947 (posthumous, Battle of Badgam) | Confusing with other 1947-48 awardees | Name confusion |
| Jnanpith Award | Highest literary award in India; instituted 1961; first winner: G. Sankara Kurup (Malayalam, 1965) | Confusing with Sahitya Akademi Award | Similar names |
| Sahitya Akademi Award | Given in 24 languages (22 scheduled + English + Rajasthani) | Claiming only 22 languages | Number trap |
| Dada Saheb Phalke Award | Highest award in Indian cinema; instituted 1969; first recipient: Devika Rani | Confusing with National Film Award for Best Film | Similar names |
| Arjuna Award | For outstanding sportspersons; instituted 1961 | Confusing with Dronacharya (coaches) | Category confusion |
| Major Dhyan Chand Khel Ratna | India's highest sporting honour; renamed from Rajiv Gandhi Khel Ratna in 2021 | Using the old name in current questions | Name-change trap |
| Dronacharya Award | For coaches; instituted 1985 | Confusing with Dhyan Chand Award (lifetime achievement in sports) | Similar names |
| Bharat Ratna to foreigners | Khan Abdul Ghaffar Khan (1987), Nelson Mandela (1990) | Claiming no foreigner ever received it | Negative-statement trap |
| Posthumous Bharat Ratna | Lal Bahadur Shastri (1966) was the first posthumous recipient | Confusing with other leaders | Chronology confusion |
| Youngest Bharat Ratna | Sachin Tendulkar (2014, age 40); also first sportsperson | Confusing with C.N.R. Rao (same year, scientist) | Same-year trap |
| Grammy/Oscar Indians | A.R. Rahman — 2 Oscars (Slumdog Millionaire, 2009); Bhanu Athaiya — first Indian Oscar (Gandhi, 1982, costume design) | Confusing Bhanu Athaiya with Satyajit Ray (Honorary Oscar 1992) | Name confusion |
| Ramon Magsaysay Award | Asian award instituted 1957 (Philippines); Vinoba Bhave among early Indian winners (1958) | Calling it an Indian award | Origin trap |
| Booker Prize | For English fiction (UK); first Indian-origin winner: Salman Rushdie (1981, Midnight's Children) | Confusing with Arundhati Roy (1997, The God of Small Things) | Chronology confusion |
| Pulitzer Indian connection | Jhumpa Lahiri (2000, Interpreter of Maladies) | Confusing Booker and Pulitzer | Award confusion |
| Right to refuse awards | Awards cannot be used as titles/suffixes (Article 18); National awards are not "titles" per SC clarification | Claiming Bharat Ratna violates Article 18 | Constitutional trap |
