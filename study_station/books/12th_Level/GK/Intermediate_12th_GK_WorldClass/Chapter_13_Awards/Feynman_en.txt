# 🧒 Feynman Technique — Awards & Honours

## The Most Confusing Concept: "If Bharat Ratna is India's highest award, why can't Sachin Tendulkar write 'Bharat Ratna Sachin Tendulkar' on his visiting card?"

Students mix this up every single year. Let me explain it like you're 12.

### The Simple Explanation

Imagine your school gives you a **trophy** for coming first in class. The trophy is yours — you keep it at home, you show it to guests, you feel proud. But can you change your *name* to "First-Prize Rahul"? Can you write it on your exam answer sheet? Of course not! The trophy is a **thing you received**, not a **part of your name**.

National awards work exactly the same way:

- **Bharat Ratna, Padma Vibhushan, Padma Bhushan, Padma Shri** — these are like trophies. You *receive* them, but they are **not titles**. Article 18 of our Constitution abolishes titles, and the Supreme Court (1995) confirmed that these awards cannot be used as prefixes or suffixes with your name.
- But **"Dr.", "Major", "Justice"** — these are different. They come from your **education or job**, so they *can* be used with your name.

So: Sachin Tendulkar **received** the Bharat Ratna (2014), but his name stays just "Sachin Tendulkar."

### The Daily-Life Example

Think of a **medal vs. a uniform**:
- A soldier's **medal** (like Param Vir Chakra) = pinned on the chest on special days, then kept safely. → Like Bharat Ratna/Padma awards. Received, not a name.
- A soldier's **rank** (Captain, Colonel) = part of who he is at work every day. → Like "Dr." or "Prof." Can be used with the name.

**Award = medal. Title = rank. Medals don't become names.**

### The Unforgettable Trick 🪄

**"TROPHY, not TAG!"**
Say it aloud: *"Bharat Ratna is a TROPHY, not a TAG."*
A trophy sits on your shelf. A tag sticks to your name. The Constitution allows the trophy, bans the tag.

Bonus memory hook for the Padma order (highest to lowest):
**"VBBS — Very Big Beautiful Smile"**
- **V**ibhushan → **B**hushan → **S**hri (and Bharat Ratna sits above all three).
