# Awards & Honours

## 🎯 The Hook: A Medal That Changed a Nation's Mood

In 1952, a young hockey player named Balbir Singh Sr. stood on the Olympic podium in Helsinki as India won gold. But two years later, in 1954, something bigger happened — India decided that sporting glory alone wasn't enough. The country created its own way of saying "thank you" to extraordinary citizens: the **Bharat Ratna**, first awarded in 1954 to C. Rajagopalachari, Dr. S. Radhakrishnan and Dr. C.V. Raman. Since then, every Republic Day season, one question echoes in exam halls across India: *"Who got what, and when?"*

This chapter turns that confusion into confidence.

## 🏅 Part 1: India's Civilian Awards

India's civilian honours are announced every year on **Republic Day (26 January)**. They are given by the **President of India**.

### The Order of Precedence (Highest → Lowest)

| Rank | Award | Hindi Name |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Bharat Ratna | भारत रत्न |
| 2 | Padma Vibhushan | पद्म विभूषण |
| 3 | Padma Bhushan | पद्म भूषण |
| 4 | Padma Shri | पद्म श्री |

**Key facts to lock in:**

- **Bharat Ratna** — instituted in **1954**; highest civilian award; given for exceptional service in any field. Normally a maximum of **3 awards per year** — this limit was introduced by a **statute amendment in 1955**. (Note: in **1999**, four Bharat Ratnas were announced — an exception examiners sometimes use as a trap.)
- **Padma Awards** — also instituted in **1954**. Padma Vibhushan is for "exceptional and distinguished service," Padma Bhushan for "distinguished service of a high order," and Padma Shri for "distinguished service."
- The awards **cannot be used as titles** before or after names (Article 18(1) of the Constitution prohibits titles, and the Supreme Court clarified in 1995 that these awards are not "titles" but cannot be used as prefixes/suffixes).
- Bharat Ratna and Padma awards were **suspended twice** — from 1977 to 1980 and from 1992 to 1995.

🧠 **Active Recall:** Without looking, write the four civilian awards in order. Which year were they instituted? Who presents them?

### 🧠 Mnemonic 1: **"B-V-B-S" → "Big Victory Brings Smiles"**
**B**harat Ratna → **V**ibhushan → **B**hushan → **S**hri (highest to lowest).

## ⚔️ Part 2: Gallantry Awards (Bravery Awards)

These honour the armed forces and, in some cases, civilians. They are announced on **Republic Day** and **Independence Day**.

### Wartime Gallantry Awards (Highest → Lowest)

1. **Param Vir Chakra (PVC)** — highest wartime gallantry award
2. **Maha Vir Chakra (MVC)**
3. **Vir Chakra (VrC)**

### Peacetime Gallantry Awards (Highest → Lowest)

1. **Ashoka Chakra** — highest peacetime gallantry award
2. **Kirti Chakra**
3. **Shaurya Chakra**

**Key facts:**

- The **Param Vir Chakra** was designed by **Savitri Khanolkar** (born Eve Yvonne Maday de Maros), a Swiss-born woman who became Indian — a favourite exam fact!
- The **Ashoka Chakra** (peacetime) is the equivalent of the PVC in wartime.
- The first Param Vir Chakra was awarded to **Major Somnath Sharma** (posthumous), for the Battle of Badgam, 1947.

🧠 **Active Recall:** Which is higher — Kirti Chakra or Vir Chakra? (Careful: one is wartime, one is peacetime — they belong to different ladders!)

### 🧠 Mnemonic 2: **"PMV" and "AKS"**
- Wartime: **P**aram → **M**aha → **V**ir ("PMV — Prime Minister's Valour")
- Peacetime: **A**shoka → **K**irti → **S**haurya ("AKS — A King Salutes")

## 🌍 Part 3: Major International Awards

### Nobel Prize
- Established by the will of **Alfred Nobel** (Swedish inventor of dynamite); first awarded in **1901**.
- Six categories: **Physics, Chemistry, Medicine (Physiology), Literature, Peace, and Economic Sciences** (Economics added in 1968 by Sweden's central bank).
- Awarded on **10 December** every year (Nobel's death anniversary).
- The **Peace Prize** is awarded in **Oslo (Norway)**; all others in **Stockholm (Sweden)**.

**Famous Indian Nobel Laureates:**

| Laureate | Category | Year |
|---|---|---|
| Rabindranath Tagore | Literature | 1913 |
| C.V. Raman | Physics | 1930 |
| Hargobind Khorana | Medicine | 1968 |
| Mother Teresa | Peace | 1979 |
| Subrahmanyan Chandrasekhar | Physics | 1983 |
| Amartya Sen | Economics | 1998 |
| Venkatraman Ramakrishnan | Chemistry | 2009 |
| Kailash Satyarthi | Peace | 2014 |

- **Rabindranath Tagore (1913)** was the **first Asian** to win a Nobel Prize.
- **C.V. Raman** won for the **Raman Effect** (scattering of light).

### Other Big International Awards

- **Booker Prize** — for fiction written in English and published in the UK/Ireland. Indian winners include **Arundhati Roy** (1997, *The God of Small Things*) and **Kiran Desai** (2006, *The Inheritance of Loss*). The **International Booker** (for translated works) went to **Geetanjali Shree** (2022, *Tomb of Sand*, translated from Hindi by Daisy Rockwell) — the first Hindi novel to win it.
- **Pulitzer Prize** — American award for journalism, literature and music, established by **Joseph Pulitzer**; first awarded in **1917**.
- **Oscars (Academy Awards)** — first awarded in **1929**. **Bhanu Athaiya** was the first Indian Oscar winner (Best Costume Design, *Gandhi*, 1982). **Satyajit Ray** received an Honorary Oscar in 1992. In 2023, *Naatu Naatu* (from *RRR*) won Best Original Song, and *The Elephant Whisperers* won Best Documentary Short.
- **Grammy Awards** — for music, presented by the Recording Academy (USA).
- **Magsaysay Award (Ramon Magsaysay Award)** — often called "Asia's Nobel Prize," established in **1957** in memory of a Philippine president; awarded in Manila.

🧠 **Active Recall:** Nobel Peace is given in which city? Who was the first Indian Nobel laureate and in which category?

## 🏆 Part 4: Important Indian Awards by Field

### Sports Awards
- **Major Dhyan Chand Khel Ratna Award** — India's highest sporting honour (renamed from **Rajiv Gandhi Khel Ratna** in **2021**).
- **Arjuna Award** — instituted **1961**, for consistent outstanding performance.
- **Dronacharya Award** — for **coaches** (instituted 1985).
- **Dhyan Chand Award** — for lifetime contribution to sports.

### Literature
- **Jnanpith Award** — highest literary honour in India, instituted **1961**; first awarded in **1965** to **G. Sankara Kurup** (Malayalam).
- **Sahitya Akademi Award** — given in 24 languages (22 scheduled languages + English and Rajasthani).

### Cinema
- **Dadasaheb Phalke Award** — highest award in Indian cinema, instituted **1969**; first given to **Devika Rani** (1969).
- National Film Awards — presented by the Directorate of Film Festivals.

### Science
- **Shanti Swarup Bhatnagar Prize** — for science and technology, instituted **1958**, given by CSIR.

### Others
- **Gandhi Peace Prize** — instituted **1995** by the Government of India.
- **Indira Gandhi Prize** — for peace, disarmament and development (instituted 1986).
- **Bharatiya Jnanpith's Moortidevi Award** — for literature.

🧠 **Active Recall:** Match these: Khel Ratna, Dronacharya, Jnanpith, Dadasaheb Phalke — to their fields (player / coach / literature / cinema).

### 🧠 Mnemonic 3: **"Drona teaches, Arjuna plays"**
Dronacharya = **coach** (the teacher), Arjuna = **player** (the student-archer). The Mahabharata itself is your memory hook!

## 🔗 Static-Dynamic Link

Awards are static in structure but dynamic in winners. The **list of awards and their order never changes** — that's your static core. The **annual winners** (Bharat Ratna, Padma, Nobel, Khel Ratna) change every year and are covered in the app's **Current Affairs** section. Strategy: master the static framework here, then update only the "who won this year" layer from current affairs before your exam.

## ⚠️ Caution: Examiner's Trap

**Trap 1 — Chronological Confusion (कालानुक्रमिक भ्रम):**
*"The Arjuna Award was instituted in 1985."* — Sounds right, but **1985 is the Dronacharya Award**; Arjuna began in **1961**. Examiners love swapping institution years of similar awards.

**Trap 2 — Almost-True Statement (लगभग सही कथन):**
*"All Nobel Prizes are awarded in Stockholm, Sweden."* — 90% true, but the **Peace Prize is awarded in Oslo, Norway**. One small detail flips the answer.

**Trap 3 — Similar Names (समान नाम):**
**Padma Bhushan vs Padma Vibhushan**, **Kirti Chakra vs Vir Chakra**, **Jnanpith vs Sahitya Akademi**. Options are designed so that two look nearly identical. Read every option fully before marking.

**Trap 4 — Negative Phrasing (नकारात्मक वाक्यांश):**
*"Which of the following is NOT a peacetime gallantry award?"* — The word "NOT" hides in the question. Vir Chakra (wartime) is the correct answer, but in a hurry students pick Kirti Chakra.

**Trap 5 — The "Maximum 3" Rule (अधिकतम 3 का नियम):**
*"The Bharat Ratna can never be given to more than 3 people in a year."* — The 3-per-year limit is real (added by the 1955 statute amendment), but **1999 saw four recipients**. If an option says "never more than 3," be careful.

| परम वीर चक्र | Param Vir Chakra |
| अशोक चक्र | Ashoka Chakra |
| शौर्य चक्र | Shaurya Chakra |
| ज्ञानपीठ पुरस्कार | Jnanpith Award |
| दादा साहेब फाल्के पुरस्कार | Dadasaheb Phalke Award |
| खेल रत्न | Khel Ratna ("Sports Jewel") |

Note: Most Indian award names are **transliterated, not translated** — "Padma Shri" stays "Padma Shri" in English. Don't invent translations in the exam.
