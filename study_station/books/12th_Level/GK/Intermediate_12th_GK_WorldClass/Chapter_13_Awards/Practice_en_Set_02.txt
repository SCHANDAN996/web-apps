Questions 26–50: Medium

26. The Bharat Ratna, India's highest civilian award, was instituted in which year?
(a) 1947 (b) 1950 (c) 1954 (d) 1956
Answer: (c)
Solution: Many students pick 1950 because the Constitution came into force that year and several national honours are associated with it, but the Bharat Ratna was formally instituted on 2 January 1954, along with the Padma awards, so the answer is 1954.
Source: PYQ-style

27. Who among the following was one of the first recipients of the Bharat Ratna in 1954?
(a) Jawaharlal Nehru (b) Dr. B. R. Ambedkar (c) Sardar Vallabhbhai Patel (d) C. Rajagopalachari
Answer: (d)
Solution: Nehru is a tempting wrong choice because he was the first Prime Minister, but he received the Bharat Ratna only in 1955; in 1954 the award went to C. Rajagopalachari, Dr. S. Radhakrishnan and Dr. C. V. Raman, so the answer is C. Rajagopalachari.
Source: PYQ-style

28. Which of the following is the correct order of India's civilian awards from highest to lowest?
(a) Padma Shri, Padma Bhushan, Padma Vibhushan, Bharat Ratna (b) Bharat Ratna, Padma Bhushan, Padma Vibhushan, Padma Shri (c) Bharat Ratna, Padma Vibhushan, Padma Bhushan, Padma Shri (d) Padma Vibhushan, Bharat Ratna, Padma Bhushan, Padma Shri
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is swapping Padma Vibhushan and Padma Bhushan; Vibhushan is the second-highest and Bhushan the third-highest civilian award, so the correct descending order is Bharat Ratna, Padma Vibhushan, Padma Bhushan, Padma Shri.
Source: PYQ-style

29. The Nobel Prize is awarded in memory of Alfred Nobel, who belonged to which country?
(a) Switzerland (b) Norway (c) Denmark (d) Sweden
Answer: (d)
Solution: Norway is the classic trap because the Nobel Peace Prize is awarded in Oslo, Norway, but Alfred Nobel himself was a Swedish chemist and the inventor of dynamite, so the answer is Sweden.
Source: PYQ-style

30. Who was the first Indian to win the Nobel Prize?
(a) C. V. Raman (b) Mother Teresa (c) Rabindranath Tagore (d) Hargobind Khorana
Answer: (c)
Solution: C. V. Raman is a common wrong pick as the first Indian Nobel laureate in science, but the first Indian Nobel winner overall was Rabindranath Tagore, who received the Literature prize in 1913 for Gitanjali, so the answer is Rabindranath Tagore.
Source: PYQ-style

31. C. V. Raman won the Nobel Prize in Physics in 1930 for his work on:
(a) Photoelectric effect (b) Wireless telegraphy (c) Scattering of light (d) Nuclear fission
Answer: (c)
Solution: The photoelectric effect is a tempting distractor because it won Einstein the Nobel Prize, but Raman was honoured for the scattering of light, known as the Raman Effect, so the answer is scattering of light.
Source: PYQ-style

32. The Jnanpith Award is given for outstanding contribution in the field of:
(a) Literature (b) Science (c) Music (d) Social service
Answer: (a)
Solution: Music is a trap because the Sangeet Natak Akademi awards cover performing arts, but the Jnanpith Award, instituted in 1961, honours Indian literature, so the answer is literature.
Source: PYQ-style

33. Who was the first recipient of the Jnanpith Award?
(a) Kuvempu (b) G. Sankara Kurup (c) Ashapurna Devi (d) Sumitranandan Pant
Answer: (b)
Solution: Kuvempu is a trap as the first Kannada writer to win it, but the very first Jnanpith Award in 1965 went to Malayalam poet G. Sankara Kurup for Odakkuzhal, so the answer is G. Sankara Kurup.
Source: PYQ-style

34. The Dronacharya Award is given to:
(a) Outstanding sportspersons (b) Sports coaches (c) Sports journalists (d) University athletes
Answer: (b)
Solution: Outstanding sportspersons is the tempting wrong option because that describes the Arjuna Award and Khel Ratna; the Dronacharya Award, named after the guru of the Pandavas, honours coaches, so the answer is sports coaches.
Source: PYQ-style

35. The Major Dhyan Chand Khel Ratna Award is associated with which field?
(a) Cinema (b) Science (c) Literature (d) Sports
Answer: (d)
Solution: This award, earlier called the Rajiv Gandhi Khel Ratna and renamed in 2021 after hockey legend Dhyan Chand, is India's highest sporting honour, so the answer is sports.
Source: PYQ-style

36. The Dadasaheb Phalke Award is given for contribution to:
(a) Painting (b) Classical music (c) Theatre (d) Indian cinema
Answer: (d)
Solution: Theatre is a trap because Phalke worked in a performing art, but he is called the father of Indian cinema and the award named after him is the highest honour in Indian films, so the answer is Indian cinema.
Source: PYQ-style

37. Who was the first woman to receive the Bharat Ratna?
(a) Mother Teresa (b) Indira Gandhi (c) M. S. Subbulakshmi (d) Aruna Asaf Ali
Answer: (b)
Solution: Mother Teresa is a frequent wrong answer because she received it in 1980, but Indira Gandhi received the Bharat Ratna in 1971, earlier than Teresa, so the answer is Indira Gandhi.
Source: PYQ-style

38. The Arjuna Award was instituted in which year?
(a) 1961 (b) 1956 (c) 1985 (d) 1991
Answer: (a)
Solution: 1956 is a trap because the National Sports Awards framework began around then, but the Arjuna Award itself was instituted in 1961, so the answer is 1961.
Source: PYQ-style

39. Which award is given for gallantry during peacetime and is considered the peacetime equivalent of the Param Vir Chakra?
(a) Ashoka Chakra (b) Shaurya Chakra (c) Kirti Chakra (d) Vir Chakra
Answer: (a)
Solution: Shaurya Chakra and Kirti Chakra are also peacetime gallantry awards but rank lower; the highest peacetime gallantry award, equivalent to the Param Vir Chakra, is the Ashoka Chakra, so the answer is Ashoka Chakra.
Source: PYQ-style

40. The Booker Prize is awarded for excellence in:
(a) Scientific research (b) Journalism (c) Poetry (d) Fiction writing in English
Answer: (d)
Solution: Journalism is a distractor linked to the Pulitzer; the Booker Prize honours the best novel written in English and published in the UK or Ireland, so the answer is fiction writing in English.
Source: PYQ-style

41. Who was the first Indian citizen to win the Booker Prize?
(a) Arundhati Roy (b) Aravind Adiga (c) Kiran Desai (d) Salman Rushdie
Answer: (a)
Solution: Salman Rushdie is the classic trap because he won the Booker in 1981 for Midnight's Children, but he is a British citizen; the first Indian citizen to win was Arundhati Roy in 1997 for The God of Small Things, so the answer is Arundhati Roy.
Source: PYQ-style

42. The Pulitzer Prize is associated with which country?
(a) United Kingdom (b) France (c) United States of America (d) Sweden
Answer: (c)
Solution: The United Kingdom is a trap because of the Booker Prize, but the Pulitzer Prize, for journalism, literature and music, is an American award administered by Columbia University, so the answer is the United States of America.
Source: PYQ-style

43. The Gandhi Peace Prize, instituted by the Government of India, was first awarded in:
(a) 1990 (b) 2005 (c) 2000 (d) 1995
Answer: (d)
Solution: The prize was instituted in 1995 on the 125th birth anniversary of Mahatma Gandhi, and the first award went to Julius Nyerere of Tanzania, so the answer is 1995.
Source: PYQ-style

44. Which of the following National Sports Awards is given for lifetime contribution to sports and games after retirement?
(a) Dhyan Chand Award (b) Arjuna Award (c) Dronacharya Award (d) Tenzing Norgay Award
Answer: (a)
Solution: The Dronacharya Award is the trap because it also has a lifetime category, but that is for coaches; the Dhyan Chand Award honours sportspersons for lifetime achievement in sports after their playing career, so the answer is Dhyan Chand Award.
Source: PYQ-style

45. The Nobel Peace Prize is awarded in which city?
(a) Geneva (b) Stockholm (c) Oslo (d) Copenhagen
Answer: (c)
Solution: Stockholm is the most common trap because all other Nobel Prizes are awarded there, but the Peace Prize alone is awarded in Oslo, Norway, so the answer is Oslo.
Source: PYQ-style

46. Which Indian-born scientist won the Nobel Prize in Medicine in 1968?
(a) Subrahmanyan Chandrasekhar (b) Hargobind Khorana (c) Venkatraman Ramakrishnan (d) Amartya Sen
Answer: (b)
Solution: Chandrasekhar is a trap because he won in Physics in 1983; Hargobind Khorana won the 1968 Nobel in Medicine for work on the interpretation of the genetic code, so the answer is Hargobind Khorana.
Source: PYQ-style

47. The Sahitya Akademi Award is given for books written in:
(a) Only Hindi (b) Indian languages recognised by the Akademi, including English (c) Only English (d) Only Sanskrit
Answer: (b)
Solution: Only Hindi is a common misconception; the Sahitya Akademi honours works in 24 languages including English and recognised Indian languages, so the answer is Indian languages recognised by the Akademi, including English.
Source: PYQ-style

48. The Ramon Magsaysay Award, often called Asia's Nobel Prize, is given by a foundation based in:
(a) Philippines (b) Indonesia (c) Thailand (d) Japan
Answer: (a)
Solution: Japan is a trap because of the Kyoto Prize, but the Magsaysay Award, named after a Philippine president, is administered from Manila, so the answer is Philippines.
Source: PYQ-style

49. Which of the following statements about the Bharat Ratna is correct?
(a) It can be awarded to a maximum of five persons in a year (b) It can be awarded posthumously (c) It is awarded only to Indian citizens (d) It carries a cash prize of one crore rupees
Answer: (b)
Solution: The traps are the five-person limit (it is three per year) and the cash prize (there is none); the Bharat Ratna can indeed be awarded posthumously, as with Lal Bahadur Shastri in 1966, so the answer is that it can be awarded posthumously.
Source: PYQ-style

50. The Vyas Samman is awarded for outstanding literary work in:
(a) Hindi (b) Tamil (c) Bengali (d) Urdu
Answer: (a)
Solution: Urdu is a trap because several literary awards like the Iqbal Samman are associated with it, but the Vyas Samman, instituted by the K. K. Birla Foundation, honours Hindi literature, so the answer is Hindi.
Source: PYQ-style
