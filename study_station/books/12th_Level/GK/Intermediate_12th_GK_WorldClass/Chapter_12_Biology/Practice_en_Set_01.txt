1. Which is the largest gland in the human body?
(a) Pancreas (b) Liver (c) Thyroid (d) Pituitary
Answer: (b)
Solution: The pituitary is often chosen because it is called the "master gland", but it is the smallest endocrine gland, not the largest. The liver, weighing about 1.5 kg, is the largest gland of the human body, so the answer is Liver.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Biology

2. What is the normal human body temperature?
(a) 37.0°C (b) 36.0°C (c) 38.5°C (d) 35.5°C
Answer: (a)
Solution: Values like 36.0°C and 38.5°C look plausible because body temperature varies slightly through the day, but the standard normal value is 37.0°C (98.6°F), so the answer is 37.0°C.
Source: NCERT Class 7 Science

3. Which blood cells help in clotting of blood?
(a) Red blood cells (b) White blood cells (c) Plasma cells (d) Platelets
Answer: (d)
Solution: White blood cells are a tempting choice because they defend the body, but defence against germs is different from clotting. Platelets (thrombocytes) form the clot at the site of injury, so the answer is Platelets.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science

4. The functional unit of the kidney is called:
(a) Neuron (b) Alveolus (c) Nephron (d) Villus
Answer: (c)
Solution: Neuron is the trap here — it sounds similar to nephron but is the unit of the nervous system, not the kidney. The nephron filters blood and forms urine, so the answer is Nephron.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science

5. Which vitamin is produced in the human skin in the presence of sunlight?
(a) Vitamin D (b) Vitamin C (c) Vitamin A (d) Vitamin K
Answer: (a)
Solution: Vitamin A is often picked because it is linked with skin and eyes, but it comes from diet (carrots, green vegetables). Ultraviolet sunlight converts a cholesterol derivative in the skin into Vitamin D, so the answer is Vitamin D.
Source: NCERT Class 6 Science

6. The total number of bones in an adult human body is:
(a) 206 (b) 208 (c) 300 (d) 210
Answer: (a)
Solution: 300 is a common trap because a newborn baby has about 300 bones, but many fuse during growth. An adult human skeleton has 206 bones, so the answer is 206.
Source: NCERT Class 6 Science

7. Which organ produces insulin in the human body?
(a) Liver (b) Gall bladder (c) Spleen (d) Pancreas
Answer: (d)
Solution: The liver is tempting because it stores glycogen and regulates blood sugar, but it does not secrete insulin. Insulin is secreted by the beta cells of the islets of Langerhans in the pancreas, so the answer is Pancreas.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science

8. The smallest bone in the human body is found in the:
(a) Nose (b) Ear (c) Finger (d) Toe
Answer: (b)
Solution: Finger bones look small and are a frequent wrong guess, but the stapes (stirrup) in the middle ear is the smallest bone of the body, so the answer is Ear.
Source: NCERT Class 6 Science

9. Which blood group is called the universal donor?
(a) AB positive (b) B positive (c) AB negative (d) O negative
Answer: (d)
Solution: AB positive is the classic trap — it is the universal recipient, not the donor. O negative blood lacks A, B and Rh antigens, so it can be given to anyone, making the answer O negative.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Biology

10. The exchange of gases in the lungs takes place in the:
(a) Bronchi (b) Bronchioles (c) Alveoli (d) Trachea
Answer: (c)
Solution: Bronchioles are tempting because they are the finest air passages, but they only conduct air. Actual diffusion of oxygen and carbon dioxide occurs across the thin walls of the alveoli, so the answer is Alveoli.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science

11. Which part of the brain controls balance and posture of the body?
(a) Cerebrum (b) Medulla oblongata (c) Cerebellum (d) Hypothalamus
Answer: (c)
Solution: The cerebrum is a tempting answer because it is the largest part of the brain, but it handles thinking and voluntary actions. The cerebellum coordinates muscular movement and maintains balance, so the answer is Cerebellum.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science

12. The average number of heartbeats per minute in a healthy resting adult is about:
(a) 72 (b) 50 (c) 100 (d) 120
Answer: (a)
Solution: Values like 100 appear in some texts as the upper limit of the normal range (60–100), which confuses students. The standard average resting rate quoted in textbooks is about 72 beats per minute, so the answer is 72.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Biology

13. Which enzyme present in saliva begins the digestion of starch?
(a) Pepsin (b) Trypsin (c) Lipase (d) Amylase
Answer: (d)
Solution: Pepsin is a common wrong choice because it is a famous digestive enzyme, but it acts on proteins in the stomach, not on starch in the mouth. Salivary amylase (ptyalin) starts starch digestion, so the answer is Amylase.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science

14. The pigment that gives red colour to blood is:
(a) Chlorophyll (b) Haemoglobin (c) Melanin (d) Keratin
Answer: (b)
Solution: Melanin is tempting because it is a body pigment, but it colours the skin and hair. Haemoglobin, an iron-containing pigment in red blood cells, gives blood its red colour, so the answer is Haemoglobin.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science

15. Which is the longest bone in the human body?
(a) Humerus (b) Tibia (c) Fibula (d) Femur
Answer: (d)
Solution: The humerus is often chosen because the arm looks long, but the femur (thigh bone) is the longest and strongest bone of the body, so the answer is Femur.
Source: NCERT Class 6 Science

16. Deficiency of Vitamin C causes:
(a) Scurvy (b) Rickets (c) Beriberi (d) Night blindness
Answer: (a)
Solution: Rickets is the trap — it is a bone disease, but it is caused by Vitamin D deficiency, not Vitamin C. Vitamin C deficiency causes scurvy, with bleeding gums and poor wound healing, so the answer is Scurvy.
Source: NCERT Class 6 Science

17. In which organ of the human body is urea formed?
(a) Kidney (b) Pancreas (c) Liver (d) Spleen
Answer: (c)
Solution: The kidney is the classic trap because it excretes urea in urine, but excretion is not formation. Urea is produced in the liver from excess amino acids and ammonia, so the answer is Liver.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Biology

18. The master gland of the human body is:
(a) Pituitary (b) Adrenal (c) Thyroid (d) Pineal
Answer: (a)
Solution: The thyroid is tempting because it is the largest endocrine gland, but size is not the criterion here. The pituitary controls the activity of most other endocrine glands, so the answer is Pituitary.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Biology

19. Which part of the eye controls the amount of light entering it?
(a) Cornea (b) Retina (c) Iris (d) Lens
Answer: (c)
Solution: The lens is a frequent wrong answer because it focuses light, but focusing is different from regulating the amount of light. The iris adjusts the size of the pupil, so the answer is Iris.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science

20. The normal pH of human blood is approximately:
(a) 7.4 (b) 6.4 (c) 8.4 (d) 5.4
Answer: (a)
Solution: 6.4 is tempting because students remember blood as "slightly acidic", but blood is actually slightly alkaline. Its pH is maintained around 7.4, so the answer is 7.4.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Biology

21. Consider the following statements about the human heart:
1. The human heart has four chambers.
2. The right ventricle pumps oxygenated blood to the body.
3. The left atrium receives oxygenated blood from the lungs.
Which of the statements given above is/are correct?
(a) 1 and 3 only (b) 1 and 2 only (c) 2 and 3 only (d) 1, 2 and 3
Answer: (a)
Solution: Statement 2 is the trap — it sounds right because the ventricles do pump blood out, but the right ventricle pumps deoxygenated blood to the lungs, while the left ventricle sends oxygenated blood to the body. Statements 1 and 3 are correct, so the answer is 1 and 3 only.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science

22. Which of the following correctly matches the hormone with its source gland?
(a) Thyroxine – Adrenal gland (b) Adrenaline – Thyroid gland (c) Insulin – Pancreas (d) Growth hormone – Thyroid gland
Answer: (c)
Solution: The options deliberately swap the thyroid and adrenal glands, a classic similar-names trap: thyroxine comes from the thyroid and adrenaline from the adrenal medulla, while growth hormone comes from the pituitary. Only insulin from the pancreas is correctly matched, so the answer is Insulin – Pancreas.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science

23. Arrange the following parts of the human digestive tract in the correct order in which food passes through them:
1. Oesophagus 2. Stomach 3. Small intestine 4. Large intestine
(a) 2, 1, 4, 3 (b) 2, 1, 3, 4 (c) 1, 3, 2, 4 (d) 1, 2, 3, 4
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap options place the stomach before the oesophagus or the large intestine before the small intestine, testing chronological confusion. Food travels from the oesophagus to the stomach, then the small intestine, and finally the large intestine, so the answer is 1, 2, 3, 4.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science

24. Consider the following statements about human blood:
1. Red blood cells have no nucleus in mature mammals.
2. White blood cells are more numerous than red blood cells.
3. Plasma is about 90% water.
Which of the statements given above is/are correct?
(a) 1 and 2 only (b) 1 and 3 only (c) 2 and 3 only (d) 1, 2 and 3
Answer: (b)
Solution: Statement 2 is the trap — it is almost believable, but red blood cells (about 5 million per cubic mm) vastly outnumber white blood cells (about 6,000–8,000 per cubic mm). Mature RBCs lack a nucleus and plasma is about 90% water, so the answer is 1 and 3 only.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Biology

25. Which of the following statements about the human respiratory system is NOT correct?
(a) The trachea is supported by C-shaped rings of cartilage. (b) The epiglottis prevents food from entering the oesophagus. (c) Oxygen binds with haemoglobin to form oxyhaemoglobin. (d) The diaphragm contracts during inhalation.
Answer: (b)
Solution: This is a negative-statement trap: the epiglottis does exist, but it closes over the trachea (windpipe) to stop food entering it — food is meant to enter the oesophagus, so the statement is wrong. The other three statements are correct, so the answer is "The epiglottis prevents food from entering the oesophagus."
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science
