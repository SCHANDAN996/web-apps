126. Which of the following correctly matches the hormone with its source gland?
(a) Insulin – Alpha cells of pancreas (b) Glucagon – Beta cells of pancreas (c) Aldosterone – Adrenal medulla (d) Calcitonin – Thyroid gland
Answer: (d)
Solution: The tempting wrong options swap the pancreatic cells: insulin comes from beta cells and glucagon from alpha cells of the islets of Langerhans, so both are reversed. Aldosterone is secreted by the adrenal cortex, not the medulla. Calcitonin is correctly secreted by the parafollicular cells of the thyroid gland, so the answer is Calcitonin – Thyroid gland.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Biology (Chemical Coordination)

127. The volume of air that remains in the lungs even after a forceful expiration is called:
(a) Tidal volume (b) Vital capacity (c) Inspiratory reserve volume (d) Residual volume
Answer: (d)
Solution: Tidal volume (about 500 mL) is air moved in normal breathing, and inspiratory reserve volume is extra air inhaled beyond it — both can be expelled. Vital capacity is the maximum air expelled after maximum inspiration, so it also leaves the lungs. Residual volume (about 1100–1200 mL) cannot be expelled and keeps the alveoli from collapsing, so the answer is Residual volume.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Biology (Breathing and Exchange of Gases)

128. Which blood vessel carries oxygenated blood away from the heart to the liver?
(a) Hepatic artery (b) Hepatic vein (c) Hepatic portal vein (d) Pulmonary vein
Answer: (a)
Solution: The hepatic portal vein is the trap — it goes to the liver but carries deoxygenated, nutrient-rich blood from the intestine. The hepatic vein carries blood away from the liver to the vena cava, and the pulmonary vein goes to the heart, not away from it. The hepatic artery branches from the aorta and supplies oxygenated blood to the liver, so the answer is Hepatic artery.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Biology (Body Fluids and Circulation)

129. The pacemaker of the human heart, the sino-atrial node (SAN), is located in:
(a) The interventricular septum (b) The wall of the right atrium near the superior vena cava (c) The wall of the left ventricle (d) The floor of the right ventricle
Answer: (b)
Solution: The interventricular septum contains the bundle of His, not the SAN — a common confusion. The left ventricle and right ventricle floor have no nodal tissue. The SAN lies in the upper wall of the right atrium near the opening of the superior vena cava, so the answer is the wall of the right atrium near the superior vena cava.

130. Which enzyme present in saliva begins the digestion of starch in the mouth?
(a) Pepsin (b) Trypsin (c) Lipase (d) Ptyalin (salivary amylase)
Answer: (d)
Solution: Pepsin and trypsin digest proteins in the stomach and small intestine respectively, and lipase digests fats — none act in the mouth. Ptyalin, the salivary amylase, converts starch into maltose in the buccal cavity, so the answer is Ptyalin (salivary amylase).

131. The functional unit of the kidney is the:
(a) Neuron (b) Alveolus (c) Nephron (d) Glomerulus
Answer: (c)
Solution: The glomerulus is the trap — it is only a part (the capillary tuft) of the unit, not the whole unit. Neuron belongs to the nervous system and alveolus to the lungs. The nephron, comprising the glomerulus and renal tubule, is the structural and functional unit of the kidney, so the answer is Nephron.

132. Which of the following statements about human blood groups is correct?
(a) Group O has both A and B antigens on RBCs (b) Group AB has no antibodies in plasma (c) Group A has anti-A antibodies in plasma (d) Group B has B antibodies in plasma
Answer: (b)
Solution: Group O is the universal donor precisely because it has no antigens, so the first statement reverses the fact. Group A has anti-B antibodies and Group B has anti-A antibodies — the matching-letter options are traps. Group AB, the universal recipient, has both antigens and no antibodies in plasma, so the answer is Group AB has no antibodies in plasma.

133. The exchange of gases between blood and tissues occurs across the walls of:
(a) Capillaries (b) Veins (c) Arteries (d) Arterioles only
Answer: (a)
Solution: Arteries and veins have thick muscular walls that do not permit diffusion, and arterioles are merely small arteries. Capillaries have single-layered thin walls that allow exchange of gases, nutrients and wastes between blood and tissues, so the answer is Capillaries.

134. Which cranial nerve carries visual signals from the retina to the brain?
(a) Optic nerve (b) Olfactory nerve (c) Oculomotor nerve (d) Trigeminal nerve
Answer: (a)
Solution: The oculomotor nerve is the trap — its name suggests the eye, but it only moves the eyeball muscles. The olfactory nerve handles smell and the trigeminal handles facial sensation. The optic nerve (cranial nerve II) transmits visual impulses from retina to brain, so the answer is Optic nerve.

135. The normal pH of human arterial blood is closest to:
(a) 6.4 (b) 7.0 (c) 7.4 (d) 8.4
Answer: (c)
Solution: A pH of 7.0 is the trap because it is neutral, but blood is slightly alkaline; 6.4 would indicate dangerous acidosis and 8.4 severe alkalosis. Normal arterial blood pH is maintained around 7.4 (range 7.35–7.45), so the answer is 7.4.

136. Which part of the brain controls body temperature, hunger and thirst?
(a) Cerebellum (b) Medulla oblongata (c) Hypothalamus (d) Cerebrum
Answer: (c)
Solution: The medulla oblongata is the trap — it controls involuntary actions like heartbeat and breathing, not temperature or appetite. 137. In the human eye, the image is formed on the:
(a) Cornea (b) Retina (c) Lens (d) Iris
Answer: (b)
Solution: The lens is the trap — it only focuses light; it does not receive the image. The cornea refracts light and the iris controls pupil size. The retina contains photoreceptor cells (rods and cones) on which the inverted real image forms, so the answer is Retina.

138. Which vitamin deficiency causes night blindness?

Answer: (d)
Solution: Vitamin C deficiency causes scurvy, vitamin D causes rickets, and vitamin B12 causes pernicious anaemia — all distractors with their own diseases. Vitamin A is needed to form rhodopsin (visual purple) in rod cells, and its deficiency causes night blindness, so the answer is Vitamin A.

141. The oxygen in blood is mainly transported by:
(a) Haemoglobin in RBCs (b) Platelets (c) Plasma (d) White blood cells
Answer: (a)
Solution: Plasma is the trap — it does carry a small amount of dissolved oxygen (about 3%), but not the main share. Platelets clot blood and WBCs fight infection. About 97% of oxygen is carried bound to haemoglobin as oxyhaemoglobin inside red blood cells, so the answer is Haemoglobin in RBCs.

142. Which of the following sequences correctly traces the path of urine formation and excretion?
(a) Kidney → Urinary bladder → Ureter → Urethra (b) Kidney → Urethra → Ureter → Urinary bladder (c) Ureter → Kidney → Urethra → Urinary bladder (d) Kidney → Ureter → Urinary bladder → Urethra
Answer: (d)
Solution: The first option is the classic chronological trap — it swaps the ureter and bladder, but urine must travel down the ureter before being stored. The other two sequences are anatomically impossible. Urine forms in the kidney, passes through the ureter, is stored in the urinary bladder and exits via the urethra, so the answer is Kidney → Ureter → Urinary bladder → Urethra.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Biology (Excretory Products and their Elimination)

143. Which cells of the stomach secrete hydrochloric acid?
(a) Parietal (oxyntic) cells (b) Chief cells (c) Mucous cells (d) Goblet cells
Answer: (a)
Solution: Chief cells are the trap — they secrete pepsinogen, not acid. Mucous and goblet cells secrete protective mucus. Parietal (oxyntic) cells of the gastric glands secrete HCl, which activates pepsinogen into pepsin, so the answer is Parietal (oxyntic) cells.

Answer: (b)
Solution: 120 and 150 beats per minute occur during exercise or fever, and 32 is far below normal. The SAN fires about 70–75 times per minute, giving an average resting heart rate of about 72 beats per minute, so the answer is 72 beats per minute.

149. Which part of the nephron is primarily responsible for the filtration of blood?

Answer: (b)
Solution: The loop of Henle and tubules handle reabsorption and secretion, and the collecting duct concentrates urine — none filter blood. Ultrafiltration occurs when blood is forced through the glomerulus into Bowman's capsule, so the answer is Bowman's capsule with glomerulus.

150. Which of the following correctly pairs the disease with its cause?

Answer: (a)
Solution: The wrong options deliberately cross-pair causes: goitre is due to iodine deficiency (not calcium), scurvy to vitamin C deficiency (not iron), and anaemia commonly to iron deficiency (not vitamin C).
