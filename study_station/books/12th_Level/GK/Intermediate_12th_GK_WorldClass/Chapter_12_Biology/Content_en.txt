# Biology – Human Body

## 🎯 A Hook to Begin

In 1928, Alexander Fleming returned from holiday to find a mould killing bacteria on his lab plate — and penicillin was born. But here's the twist: the real hero was the human body itself. Even before antibiotics, your body was fighting millions of invaders every single day using an army of white blood cells, a chemical factory called the liver, and a pump that beats about 100,000 times daily without a holiday. Every competitive exam — SSC, Banking, Railway, State PCS — tests this machine you live in. Master it once, and these become the easiest marks in the GK section.

## 📌 Learning Objectives

By the end of this chapter you will be able to:
1. Recall the structure and functions of major human organ systems.
2. Answer questions on blood, vitamins, hormones, and diseases — the highest-yield areas.
3. Spot and dodge the examiner's favourite traps (similar names, reversed functions, "NOT" questions).

---

## 1. The Cell — Where It All Begins

The **cell** is the structural and functional unit of life. Robert Hooke coined the term "cell" in 1665; the **cell theory** was given by Schleiden and Schwann (1838–39).

Key cell organelles (exam favourites):
- **Nucleus** — controls the cell; contains DNA (chromosomes). Humans have **46 chromosomes (23 pairs)**.
- **Mitochondria** — the **"powerhouse of the cell"**; produces energy (ATP).
- **Ribosomes** — protein factories.
- **Lysosomes** — "suicidal bags"; digest waste (term linked to de Duve).
- **Cell membrane** — selectively permeable gatekeeper.

Plant cells differ: they have a **cell wall (cellulose)** and **chloroplasts**; animal cells do not.

🧠 **Active Recall:** Which organelle is called the powerhouse? How many chromosomes does a normal human cell have? Who proposed the cell theory?

## 2. Blood — The River of Life

Blood is the single most-asked topic from this chapter. Learn it cold.

**Composition:**
- **Plasma (~55%)** — mostly water; carries nutrients, hormones, waste.
- **Red Blood Cells (RBC / Erythrocytes)** — carry oxygen via **haemoglobin** (iron-containing pigment). Made in **bone marrow**; lifespan about **120 days**; destroyed in the **spleen** ("graveyard of RBCs"). RBCs have **no nucleus** (in mammals).
- **White Blood Cells (WBC / Leucocytes)** — soldiers of the immune system.
- **Platelets (Thrombocytes)** — clot blood; clotting needs **Vitamin K** and calcium.

**Blood Groups (Karl Landsteiner, 1901):**
- **Group O — universal donor** (no antigens).
- **Group AB — universal recipient** (no antibodies).
- **Rh factor** — discovered in Rhesus monkeys; Rh+ is common, Rh− is rare.

**Blood pressure:** normal ≈ **120/80 mm Hg**, measured with a **sphygmomanometer**.

🧠 **Active Recall:** Universal donor? Universal recipient? Where are RBCs made and destroyed? Which vitamin helps clotting?

## 3. The Circulatory System — Heart and Vessels

The **heart** is a four-chambered muscular pump: 2 atria (upper) + 2 ventricles (lower). It beats about **72 times per minute** at rest.

- **Arteries** carry blood **away** from the heart (usually oxygenated) — thick walls, high pressure.
- **Veins** carry blood **towards** the heart (usually deoxygenated) — have **valves** to prevent backflow.
- **Capillaries** — thin-walled exchange vessels.

**Two classic traps:**
- **Pulmonary artery** is the only artery carrying **deoxygenated** blood (heart → lungs).
- **Pulmonary vein** is the only vein carrying **oxygenated** blood (lungs → heart).

**Double circulation:** pulmonary (heart↔lungs) + systemic (heart↔body). The **largest artery** is the **aorta**.

🧠 **Active Recall:** Which artery carries deoxygenated blood? Which vessels have valves? Name the largest artery.

## 4. The Digestive System

Digestion starts in the **mouth** — salivary **amylase (ptyalin)** begins starch digestion.

**The journey:** Mouth → Oesophagus → Stomach → Small intestine → Large intestine → Rectum.

- **Stomach:** secretes **HCl** and **pepsin** (digests proteins). pH is highly acidic (~1.5–2).
- **Liver** — the **largest gland** of the body; produces **bile** (stored in the **gall bladder**). Bile **emulsifies fats** — it contains **no enzyme**.
- **Pancreas** — a **mixed gland**: digestive enzymes (exocrine) + **insulin and glucagon** (endocrine).
- **Small intestine (~6–7 m)** — the **longest part** of the alimentary canal; **complete digestion and absorption** happen here (villi increase surface area).
- **Large intestine** — absorbs water and salts.

**Key enzymes:** amylase (starch→sugar), pepsin/trypsin (proteins), lipase (fats).

🧠 **Active Recall:** Which organ makes bile, and where is it stored? Where does complete digestion occur? Which gland is both exocrine and endocrine?

---

## 5. The Respiratory System

Path: Nose → Pharynx → Larynx (**voice box**) → Trachea (**windpipe**) → Bronchi → Bronchioles → **Alveoli** (air sacs where gas exchange happens).

- **Breathing** is controlled by the **medulla oblongata**.
- Oxygen binds to **haemoglobin**; carbon dioxide is carried back mostly as bicarbonate.
- The **diaphragm** is the main breathing muscle.

**Trap alert:** The windpipe is the **trachea**; the food pipe is the **oesophagus** — examiners love swapping them.

🧠 **Active Recall:** Where does actual gas exchange occur? Which part of the brain controls breathing?

---

## 6. The Nervous System

- **Neuron** — the structural and functional unit; the **longest cell** in the body.
- **Brain parts:** **Cerebrum** (largest part — thinking, memory, voluntary actions), **Cerebellum** (balance and coordination), **Medulla oblongata** (involuntary actions — heartbeat, breathing).
- **Spinal cord** — reflex actions; protected by the vertebral column.
- The brain and spinal cord together = **central nervous system (CNS)**.

🧠 **Active Recall:** Which part of the brain maintains body balance? What is the unit of the nervous system?

---

## 7. The Excretory System

- **Kidneys** filter blood; the functional unit is the **nephron** (about a million per kidney).
- **Urea** is the main nitrogenous waste in humans.
- Path: Kidneys → Ureters → **Urinary bladder** (stores urine) → Urethra.
- The **skin** (sweat) and **lungs** (CO₂, water vapour) also excrete.

---

## 8. The Skeletal and Muscular Systems

- An adult human has **206 bones**; a newborn has about 300 (many fuse later).
- **Longest bone:** **Femur** (thigh bone). **Smallest bone:** **Stapes** (in the ear).
- The skeleton is made of bone and **cartilage**; bones store **calcium and phosphorus**.
- **Muscles:** voluntary (skeletal), involuntary (smooth), and **cardiac** (heart — involuntary but never tires).
- The **hardest substance** in the body is **tooth enamel**.

🧠 **Active Recall:** How many bones in an adult? Longest and smallest bones? Hardest body substance?

## 9. Hormones and Endocrine Glands — High-Yield Zone

| Gland | Hormone | Function |
|---|---|---|
| Pituitary ("master gland") | Growth hormone | Growth; excess → gigantism |
| Thyroid | Thyroxine (needs **iodine**) | Metabolism; deficiency → **goitre** |
| Pancreas | **Insulin** | Lowers blood sugar; deficiency → **diabetes mellitus** |
| Adrenal | **Adrenaline** ("emergency hormone") | Fight-or-flight |
| Testes / Ovaries | Testosterone / Oestrogen | Sexual characteristics |

- The **pituitary** is called the master gland, but it is controlled by the **hypothalamus**.
- **Largest endocrine gland:** thyroid. **Largest gland overall:** liver.

## 10. Vitamins and Deficiency Diseases — Memorise This Table

| Vitamin | Chemical name | Deficiency disease |
|---|---|---|
| A | Retinol | **Night blindness** |
| B1 | Thiamine | **Beri-beri** |
| B12 | Cobalamin | **Pernicious anaemia** |
| C | Ascorbic acid | **Scurvy** (bleeding gums) |
| D | Calciferol | **Rickets** (children), osteomalacia (adults) |
| E | Tocopherol | Sterility (in animals) |
| K | Phylloquinone | **Poor blood clotting** |

- **Fat-soluble:** A, D, E, K. **Water-soluble:** B-complex and C.
- Vitamin D is synthesised in skin by **sunlight** — the "sunshine vitamin".

🧠 **Active Recall:** Scurvy is caused by deficiency of? Which vitamin is made by sunlight? Which vitamins are fat-soluble?

## 11. Diseases — Who Causes What

| Disease | Cause |
|---|---|
| Tuberculosis (TB), Cholera, Typhoid, Plague, Tetanus, Leprosy | **Bacteria** |
| Common cold, Influenza, Polio, Rabies, AIDS, Measles, Dengue, Chickenpox, COVID-19 | **Virus** |
| **Malaria** (Plasmodium), Amoebic dysentery | **Protozoa** |
| Ringworm, Athlete's foot | **Fungus** |

- **Malaria** spreads by the **female Anopheles mosquito**; **dengue and chikungunya** by **Aedes aegypti**.
- **BCG** vaccine → TB; **BCG is given at birth**. Polio drops (OPV) → polio.
- **Edward Jenner** — father of vaccination (smallpox, 1796). **Louis Pasteur** — rabies vaccine, pasteurisation.
- **AIDS** is caused by **HIV**, which attacks the immune system (helper T-cells).

---

## 12. Sense Organs and Miscellany

- **Eye:** image forms on the **retina**; the **lens** focuses light. Myopia (short-sight) → concave lens; hypermetropia (long-sight) → convex lens.
- **Ear:** hearing + balance; smallest bone (stapes) sits here.
- **Normal body temperature:** **37°C (98.6°F)**.
- **pH of blood:** about **7.4** (slightly alkaline).
- **Largest organ:** **skin**. **Largest internal organ/gland:** liver.
- **DNA** — double helix, discovered by **Watson and Crick (1953)**; genes are segments of DNA.

## 🧠 Mnemonics That Stick

1. **"ADEK are Fat Friends"** — Vitamins **A, D, E, K** are **fat-soluble**; the rest (B, C) are water-soluble.
2. **"O Gives, AB Takes"** — Blood group **O** is the universal **donor** (gives to all); **AB** is the universal **recipient** (takes from all).
3. **"Pulmonary is Peculiar"** — the **pulmonary artery** carries deoxygenated blood and the **pulmonary vein** carries oxygenated blood — the **opposite** of every other artery and vein.
4. **"My Liver Stores Bile in a Small Bag"** — bile is **made in the liver**, **stored in the gall bladder** — never confuse the two.

## ⚠️ Caution: Examiner's Trap

**Trap 1 — "लगभग सही कथन" (Almost-True Statement):** "Bile is produced in the gall bladder and digests fats." Sounds 90% right — but bile is produced in the **liver** and only **stored** in the gall bladder; also, bile has **no enzyme**, it only emulsifies fats. Two tiny errors hidden in one smooth sentence.

**Trap 2 — "विपरीत कार्य-कारण" (Reversed Function):** "Arteries always carry oxygenated blood." The examiner swaps the rule using the **pulmonary artery** (deoxygenated) and **pulmonary vein** (oxygenated). Remember: arteries/veins are defined by **direction** (away from / towards the heart), not by oxygen content.

**Trap 3 — "समान नाम" (Similar Names):** **Trachea vs oesophagus**, **ureter vs urethra**, **mitochondria vs ribosome**, **thyroid vs thymus**. In a hurry, students tick the familiar-looking word. Read each option fully before answering.

**Trap 4 — "नकारात्मक वाक्यांश" (Hidden Negative):** "Which of the following is **NOT** a viral disease?" — with cholera, typhoid, TB (all bacterial) and measles (viral) as options. Underline the NOT before you even look at the options.

## 🔗 Static-Dynamic Link

The biology of viruses and immunity became headline news during the **COVID-19 pandemic (2020–21)**: exams began asking about **vaccines, antibodies, and viruses vs bacteria** far more often. Similarly, India's **Pulse Polio programme** keeps questions on the polio vaccine and **Albert Sabin / Jonas Salk** alive. Static concepts (virus structure, immunity) don't change — only their exam weight does.
