Card 1
Front: Which is the largest gland in the human body, and what digestive juice does it produce?
Back: The liver. It produces bile, which emulsifies fats. Bile is stored and concentrated in the gall bladder.

Card 2
Front: Which blood cells are called the "soldiers" of the body, and why?
Back: White blood cells (WBCs/leucocytes). They defend the body against infections and foreign particles.

Card 3
Front: The human heart has four chambers — name them and state which one pumps blood to the whole body.
Back: Right atrium, right ventricle, left atrium, left ventricle. The left ventricle pumps oxygenated blood to the entire body through the aorta.

Card 4
Front: Which is the longest and strongest bone in the human body, and where is it located?
Back: The femur (thigh bone), located in the thigh. The smallest bone is the stapes in the middle ear.

Card 5
Front: How many bones does an adult human skeleton have — and is it the same as a newborn's?
Back: An adult has 206 bones. A newborn has about 300 bones, many of which fuse during growth. (Trap: exams often swap 206 and 300.)

Card 6
Front: Which vitamin is synthesised in the skin with the help of sunlight, and what disease results from its deficiency?
Back: Vitamin D. Its deficiency causes rickets in children and osteomalacia in adults.

Card 7
Front: Which part of the brain controls balance and posture, and which part controls heartbeat and breathing?
Back: The cerebellum controls balance and posture; the medulla oblongata controls involuntary actions like heartbeat and breathing.

Card 8
Front: What is the normal human body temperature, and which instrument measures it?
Back: About 37°C (98.6°F), measured with a clinical thermometer.

Card 9
Front: Which blood group is called the "universal donor" and which the "universal recipient"? What is the common trap here?
Back: O negative is the universal donor; AB positive is the universal recipient. Trap: options often say just "O" and "AB" — strictly, it is O− and AB+ (Rh factor matters).

Card 10
Front: Which hormone regulates blood sugar, which gland secretes it, and what disease does its deficiency cause?
Back: Insulin, secreted by the beta cells of the islets of Langerhans in the pancreas. Its deficiency causes diabetes mellitus.

Card 11
Front: Mnemonic trick: How can you quickly remember the fat-soluble vitamins?
Back: "KEDA" or "A DEK" — Vitamins A, D, E, K are fat-soluble; B and C are water-soluble. Remember: "ADEK ko fat chahiye" (ADEK needs fat).

Card 12
Front: Mnemonic trick: How do you remember the cranial nerves count and the chambers' blood flow order?
Back: Cranial nerves = 12 pairs ("12 pairs, not 12 nerves"). Blood flow: Body → Right atrium → Right ventricle → Lungs → Left atrium → Left ventricle → Body. Remember "Right side = impure (deoxygenated), Left side = pure (oxygenated)" — R-I, L-P.

Card 13
Front: Examiner's trap: "The pancreas is only an exocrine gland." True or false?
Back: False. The pancreas is a mixed (heterocrine) gland — exocrine part secretes digestive enzymes, endocrine part (islets of Langerhans) secretes insulin and glucagon. Trap words: "only", "always", "never".

Card 14
Front: Examiner's trap: Which is the largest artery and which is the largest vein — do arteries always carry oxygenated blood?
Back: Largest artery: aorta; largest vein: inferior vena cava. Trap: arteries do NOT always carry oxygenated blood — the pulmonary artery carries deoxygenated blood to the lungs, and the pulmonary vein carries oxygenated blood to the heart.

Card 15
Front: What is the functional unit of the kidney, and roughly how many are there in each kidney?
Back: The nephron. Each kidney contains about one million (10 lakh) nephrons that filter blood and form urine.

Card 16
Front: Which pigment gives blood its red colour, and what element is essential for it?
Back: Haemoglobin, which contains iron. It carries oxygen; its deficiency causes anaemia.

Card 17
Front: Which is the master gland of the body, and why is it called so?
Back: The pituitary gland, because it controls the activity of most other endocrine glands. It is located at the base of the brain.

Card 18
Front: What is the average number of chromosomes in a normal human cell, and how is sex determined?
Back: 46 chromosomes (23 pairs). Sex is determined by the 23rd pair: XX = female, XY = male — the father's sperm decides the child's sex.

Card 19
Front: Which is the longest nerve and which is the largest cell in the human body?
Back: Longest nerve: the sciatic nerve. Largest cell: the female ovum (egg cell); longest cell: the neuron.

Card 20
Front: Where does most digestion and absorption of food take place, and what structures increase its surface area?
Back: The small intestine. Finger-like projections called villi increase the surface area for absorption of digested nutrients.
