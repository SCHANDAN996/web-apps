26. Which enzyme present in saliva begins the digestion of starch?
(a) Pepsin (b) Trypsin (c) Lipase (d) Ptyalin
Answer: (d)
Solution: Pepsin acts on proteins in the stomach, not in the mouth, so it is a tempting but wrong choice. Salivary amylase, also called ptyalin, converts starch into maltose in the mouth, so the answer is Ptyalin.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science

27. The functional unit of the kidney is the —
(a) Neuron (b) Alveolus (c) Nephron (d) Villus
Answer: (c)
Solution: Neuron is the unit of the nervous system and alveolus belongs to the lungs; both are similar-sounding traps. Filtration of blood and urine formation occur in the nephron, so the answer is Nephron.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science

28. Which blood group is called the universal donor?
(a) AB positive (b) O negative (c) O positive (d) AB negative
Answer: (b)
Solution: Many students pick O positive, forgetting that the Rh factor also matters in transfusion. O negative blood has neither A/B antigens nor Rh antigen, so it can be given to anyone, so the answer is O negative.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science

29. Which gland is known as the "master gland" of the human body?
(a) Pituitary (b) Adrenal (c) Thyroid (d) Pancreas
Answer: (a)
Solution: The thyroid is large and important but controls only metabolism, which makes it a tempting wrong option. The pituitary secretes hormones that regulate other endocrine glands, so the answer is Pituitary.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Biology

30. The normal pH of human arterial blood is closest to —
(a) 6.4 (b) 7.4 (c) 8.4 (d) 5.4
Answer: (b)
Solution: Blood is slightly alkaline, not neutral 7.0 or acidic, which traps students who assume neutrality. The normal pH range is 7.35–7.45, so the answer is 7.4.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Biology

31. Which vitamin is synthesized in human skin on exposure to sunlight?
(a) Vitamin A (b) Vitamin D (c) Vitamin C (d) Vitamin K
Answer: (b)
Solution: Vitamin K is made by gut bacteria, which confuses many students, but it is not linked to sunlight. Ultraviolet rays convert a cholesterol derivative in skin into Vitamin D, so the answer is Vitamin D.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Biology

32. The exchange of gases in the lungs takes place in the —
(a) Bronchi (b) Bronchioles (c) Alveoli (d) Trachea
Answer: (c)
Solution: Bronchioles only conduct air deeper into the lungs; they are a near-miss trap. Actual diffusion of oxygen and carbon dioxide occurs across the thin walls of the alveoli, so the answer is Alveoli.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science

33. Which part of the brain controls balance and coordination of the body?
(a) Cerebrum (b) Medulla oblongata (c) Cerebellum (d) Hypothalamus
Answer: (c)
Solution: The medulla controls involuntary actions like heartbeat, which is a common confusion, but not balance. The cerebellum coordinates muscular activity and posture, so the answer is Cerebellum.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science

34. Insulin is secreted by which cells of the pancreas?
(a) Beta cells of Islets of Langerhans (b) Alpha cells (c) Delta cells (d) Acinar cells
Answer: (a)
Solution: Alpha cells secrete glucagon, which raises blood sugar — the opposite action makes it a tempting trap. Insulin, which lowers blood glucose, comes from the beta cells of the Islets of Langerhans, so the answer is Beta cells of Islets of Langerhans.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Biology

35. The largest gland in the human body is the —
(a) Liver (b) Thyroid (c) Pancreas (d) Pituitary
Answer: (a)
Solution: The thyroid is the largest purely endocrine gland, which misleads many students, but overall the liver is bigger. The liver weighs about 1.5 kg and performs bile secretion and detoxification, so the answer is Liver.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Biology

36. Which of the following carries oxygenated blood from the lungs to the heart?
(a) Pulmonary artery (b) Vena cava (c) Aorta (d) Pulmonary vein
Answer: (d)
Solution: The pulmonary artery is the trap here because arteries usually carry oxygenated blood, but this one carries deoxygenated blood to the lungs. The pulmonary vein returns oxygenated blood to the left atrium, so the answer is Pulmonary vein.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science

37. The number of bones in an adult human skeleton is —
(a) 206 (b) 300 (c) 212 (d) 186
Answer: (a)
Solution: Babies have about 300 bones, and students often pick that figure forgetting that bones fuse with age. In adults the fused skeleton has 206 bones, so the answer is 206.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Biology

38. Which of the following is NOT a function of the liver?
(a) Bile production (b) Production of insulin (c) Detoxification (d) Storage of glycogen
Answer: (b)
Solution: The liver does produce bile, store glycogen and detoxify blood, so each of those is a true statement designed to confuse. Insulin is produced by the pancreas, not the liver, so the answer is Production of insulin.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Biology

39. The oxygen-carrying pigment in red blood cells is —
(a) Chlorophyll (b) Haemoglobin (c) Myosin (d) Keratin
Answer: (b)
Solution: Chlorophyll is the plant pigment and keratin is found in hair and nails; both are distractors. Haemoglobin, an iron-containing protein, binds oxygen in RBCs, so the answer is Haemoglobin.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science

40. Which part of the nephron is mainly responsible for filtration of blood?
(a) Loop of Henle (b) Collecting duct (c) Glomerulus (d) Distal tubule
Answer: (c)
Solution: The Loop of Henle concentrates urine, which sounds related but is a reabsorption structure, not a filter. Blood is filtered under pressure in the glomerulus inside Bowman's capsule, so the answer is Glomerulus.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science

41. Deficiency of iodine in the diet causes —
(a) Goitre (b) Night blindness (c) Scurvy (d) Rickets
Answer: (a)
Solution: Rickets from Vitamin D deficiency is a common mix-up because both are deficiency diseases. Iodine is needed for thyroxine synthesis, and its deficiency enlarges the thyroid causing goitre, so the answer is Goitre.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Biology

42. The average normal human body temperature is —
(a) 35.6°C (b) 39°C (c) 38.5°C (d) 37°C
Answer: (d)
Solution: 98.6°F is often memorised, and students who convert wrongly land on other values. 98.6°F equals about 37°C, so the answer is 37°C.

43. Which cells of the blood help in clotting?
(a) Platelets (b) White blood cells (c) Red blood cells (d) Plasma cells
Answer: (a)
Solution: White blood cells fight infection, which tempts students who link all blood functions to defence. Platelets (thrombocytes) initiate clot formation at injury sites, so the answer is Platelets.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science

44. The longest bone in the human body is the —
(a) Humerus (b) Tibia (c) Femur (d) Fibula
Answer: (c)
Solution: The humerus is long but belongs to the arm and is shorter, making it a near-miss trap. The femur, or thigh bone, is the longest and strongest bone, so the answer is Femur.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Biology

45. Which hormone is released in emergency situations and is called the "fight or flight" hormone?
(a) Insulin (b) Adrenaline (c) Thyroxine (d) Oxytocin
Answer: (b)
Solution: Thyroxine controls metabolism steadily, not sudden emergencies, which makes it a plausible but wrong option. Adrenaline from the adrenal medulla raises heart rate and glucose supply during stress, so the answer is Adrenaline.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science

46. In which organ does most of the chemical digestion and absorption of food occur?
(a) Stomach (b) Oesophagus (c) Large intestine (d) Small intestine
Answer: (d)
Solution: The stomach starts protein digestion, so students often over-credit it, but absorption there is minimal. The small intestine completes digestion and absorbs nutrients through villi, so the answer is Small intestine.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science

47. Which of the following statements about arteries is correct?
(a) They always carry oxygenated blood (b) They have valves to prevent backflow (c) They carry blood away from the heart (d) They have thin walls
Answer: (c)
Solution: The statement that arteries always carry oxygenated blood is the classic trap — the pulmonary artery carries deoxygenated blood. The defining feature is that arteries carry blood away from the heart, so the answer is They carry blood away from the heart.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science

48. The structural and functional unit of the nervous system is the —
(a) Nephron (b) Synapse (c) Axon (d) Neuron
Answer: (d)
Solution: Axon and synapse are parts or junctions of nerve cells, not the unit itself, which confuses students. The neuron is the complete structural and functional unit, so the answer is Neuron.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science

49. Which vitamin deficiency causes scurvy, characterised by bleeding gums?
(a) Vitamin A (b) Vitamin B12 (c) Vitamin D (d) Vitamin C
Answer: (d)
Solution: Vitamin A deficiency causes night blindness, and students mixing up deficiency diseases pick it wrongly. Scurvy results from lack of Vitamin C (ascorbic acid), needed for collagen formation, so the answer is Vitamin C.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Biology

50. Where does the fertilisation of the human egg normally take place?
(a) Fallopian tube (b) Ovary (c) Uterus (d) Cervix
Answer: (a)
Solution: The uterus is where the embryo implants and develops, so it is the most tempting wrong answer. Fertilisation normally occurs in the fallopian tube (oviduct), so the answer is Fallopian tube.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Biology
