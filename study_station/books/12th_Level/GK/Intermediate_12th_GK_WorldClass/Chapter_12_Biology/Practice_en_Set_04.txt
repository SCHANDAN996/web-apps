Questions 76–100: Medium

76. Which enzyme present in saliva begins the digestion of starch?
(a) Pepsin (b) Trypsin (c) Lipase (d) Ptyalin
Answer: (d)
Solution: Pepsin is the tempting wrong option, but it acts on proteins and is found in the stomach, not in saliva. Salivary amylase, also called ptyalin, converts starch into maltose in the mouth, so the answer is ptyalin.
Source: NCERT Class 7 Science

77. The functional unit of the kidney is the:
(a) Neuron (b) Villus (c) Alveolus (d) Nephron
Answer: (d)
Solution: Neuron is a trap because of its similar-sounding name, but it is the structural unit of the nervous system. The nephron filters blood and forms urine in the kidney, so the answer is nephron.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science

78. Which blood group is called the universal donor?
(a) O negative (b) AB negative (c) O positive (d) AB positive
Answer: (a)
Solution: O positive is the common trap; it can donate to all positive groups but not to negative groups. O negative lacks A, B and Rh antigens, so it can be given to anyone, making the answer O negative.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Biology

79. The exchange of gases in the lungs takes place in the:
(a) Bronchi (b) Bronchioles (c) Alveoli (d) Trachea
Answer: (c)
Solution: Bronchioles are a tempting option because they are tiny air passages, but they only conduct air. Actual diffusion of oxygen and carbon dioxide occurs across the thin walls of the alveoli, so the answer is alveoli.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science

80. Which hormone regulates blood sugar level?
(a) Thyroxine (b) Insulin (c) Adrenaline (d) Growth hormone
Answer: (b)
Solution: Adrenaline is the trap because it can raise blood glucose during emergencies, but it is not the primary regulator. Insulin, secreted by the beta cells of the pancreas, lowers blood glucose, so the answer is insulin.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science

81. The normal human body temperature is approximately:
(a) 35°C (b) 39°C (c) 37°C (d) 40°C
Answer: (c)
Solution: 35°C is a trap because it sounds close, but it indicates hypothermia. The normal core body temperature is about 37°C (98.6°F), so the answer is 37°C.
Source: NCERT Class 7 Science

82. Which part of the brain controls balance and coordination of the body?
(a) Cerebrum (b) Hypothalamus (c) Medulla oblongata (d) Cerebellum
Answer: (d)
Solution: Cerebrum is the tempting wrong option as it is the largest part of the brain, but it handles thinking and voluntary actions. The cerebellum maintains posture, balance and coordination, so the answer is cerebellum.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science

83. The average number of chromosomes in a normal human somatic cell is:
(a) 23 (b) 46 (c) 44 (d) 48
Answer: (b)
Solution: 23 is the trap because gametes contain 23 chromosomes, but the question asks about somatic (body) cells. Body cells have 23 pairs, i.e., 46 chromosomes, so the answer is 46.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Biology

84. Which vitamin is synthesized in human skin on exposure to sunlight?
(a) Vitamin A (b) Vitamin C (c) Vitamin K (d) Vitamin D
Answer: (d)
Solution: Vitamin K is a trap because it is also produced in the body, but by intestinal bacteria, not by skin. Ultraviolet rays convert a cholesterol derivative in the skin into vitamin D, so the answer is vitamin D.
Source: NCERT Class 6 Science

85. The largest gland in the human body is the:
(a) Pancreas (b) Thyroid (c) Liver (d) Pituitary
Answer: (c)
Solution: Thyroid is the trap because it is the largest endocrine gland, but the question asks about the largest gland overall. The liver, a mixed gland, is the largest gland of the body, so the answer is liver.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Biology

86. Which of the following carries oxygenated blood from the lungs to the heart?
(a) Pulmonary vein (b) Pulmonary artery (c) Aorta (d) Vena cava
Answer: (a)
Solution: Pulmonary artery is the classic trap because arteries usually carry oxygenated blood, but the pulmonary artery carries deoxygenated blood to the lungs. The pulmonary vein brings oxygenated blood back to the left atrium, so the answer is pulmonary vein.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science

87. The master gland of the human body is the:
(a) Pituitary gland (b) Adrenal gland (c) Thyroid (d) Pineal gland
Answer: (a)
Solution: Thyroid is a tempting option because it controls metabolism, but it does not control other glands. The pituitary secretes hormones that regulate most other endocrine glands, so the answer is pituitary gland.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science

88. Which blood cells help in clotting of blood?
(a) Platelets (b) White blood cells (c) Red blood cells (d) Plasma cells
Answer: (a)
Solution: White blood cells are the trap because they protect the body, but their role is fighting infection, not clotting. Platelets (thrombocytes) initiate the clotting process at injury sites, so the answer is platelets.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science

89. The smallest bone in the human body is the:
(a) Femur (b) Stapes (c) Tibia (d) Incus
Answer: (b)
Solution: Incus is the trap because it is also a tiny ear bone, but it is larger than the stapes. The stapes, located in the middle ear, is the smallest bone in the body, so the answer is stapes.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Biology

90. Which organ produces bile?
(a) Gall bladder (b) Pancreas (c) Liver (d) Stomach
Answer: (c)
Solution: Gall bladder is the most common trap because bile is stored there, but it is not produced there. Bile is synthesized by the liver and only stored and concentrated in the gall bladder, so the answer is liver.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science

91. The normal resting heart rate of a healthy adult is about:
(a) 40–50 beats per minute (b) 60–100 beats per minute (c) 110–130 beats per minute (d) 140–160 beats per minute
Answer: (b)
Solution: 40–50 beats per minute is a trap because trained athletes may show such rates, but it is below normal for an average adult. The standard resting range is 60–100 beats per minute, so the answer is 60–100 beats per minute.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Biology

92. Which part of the eye controls the amount of light entering it?
(a) Iris (b) Retina (c) Cornea (d) Lens
Answer: (a)
Solution: Lens is the tempting wrong option because it focuses light, but focusing is different from regulating the amount of light. The iris adjusts the size of the pupil to control light entry, so the answer is iris.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science

93. Deficiency of iodine in the diet causes:
(a) Night blindness (b) Goitre (c) Scurvy (d) Rickets
Answer: (b)
Solution: Rickets is a trap because it is also a deficiency disease, but it results from vitamin D deficiency. Iodine deficiency enlarges the thyroid gland, causing goitre, so the answer is goitre.
Source: NCERT Class 8 Science

94. Which of the following is the correct sequence of the passage of food through the alimentary canal?
(a) Mouth → Stomach → Oesophagus → Small intestine → Large intestine (b) Mouth → Stomach → Small intestine → Oesophagus → Large intestine (c) Mouth → Oesophagus → Small intestine → Stomach → Large intestine (d) Mouth → Oesophagus → Stomach → Small intestine → Large intestine
Answer: (d)
Solution: The first option is the trap because it places the stomach before the oesophagus, a chronological confusion examiners exploit. Food travels from the mouth through the oesophagus to the stomach, then the small and large intestines, so the answer is the sequence in option (d).
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science

95. Which hormone is secreted by the adrenal gland in emergency situations?
(a) Insulin (b) Adrenaline (c) Thyroxine (d) Oxytocin
Answer: (b)
Solution: Thyroxine is a trap because it also affects metabolism and heart rate, but it acts slowly over time. Adrenaline (epinephrine) is released rapidly during stress or emergency, preparing the body for fight or flight, so the answer is adrenaline.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science

96. The total number of bones in an adult human skeleton is:
(a) 206 (b) 210 (c) 300 (d) 350
Answer: (a)
Solution: 300 is the trap because infants have about 300 bones, but many fuse during growth. An adult human skeleton has 206 bones, so the answer is 206.
Source: NCERT Class 6 Science

97. Which part of the nephron is mainly responsible for filtration of blood?
(a) Loop of Henle (b) Distal tubule (c) Collecting duct (d) Glomerulus
Answer: (d)
Solution: Loop of Henle is the tempting wrong option because it is vital for concentrating urine, but it handles reabsorption, not filtration. Blood is filtered under pressure in the glomerulus inside Bowman's capsule, so the answer is glomerulus.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science

98. Which vitamin deficiency causes scurvy?
(a) Vitamin A (b) Vitamin B12 (c) Vitamin C (d) Vitamin D
Answer: (c)
Solution: Vitamin A is a trap because its deficiency is very common in exams, but it causes night blindness. Scurvy, marked by bleeding gums and poor wound healing, results from vitamin C deficiency, so the answer is vitamin C.
Source: NCERT Class 6 Science

99. The pacemaker of the human heart is the:
(a) AV node (b) Bundle of His (c) SA node (d) Purkinje fibres
Answer: (c)
Solution: AV node is the trap because it also conducts impulses and can pace the heart at a slower rate if the SA node fails. The sinoatrial (SA) node normally initiates each heartbeat and sets the rhythm, so the answer is SA node.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Biology

100. Which of the following statements about human respiration is correct?
(a) The diaphragm contracts and moves downward during inhalation (b) The diaphragm relaxes and moves downward during inhalation (c) The diaphragm contracts and moves upward during inhalation (d) The diaphragm plays no role in breathing
Answer: (a)
Solution: The second option is the trap because it pairs the correct movement (downward) with the wrong muscle state (relaxes), an almost-correct statement. During inhalation the diaphragm contracts and flattens downward, increasing chest volume, so the answer is the statement in option (a).
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science
