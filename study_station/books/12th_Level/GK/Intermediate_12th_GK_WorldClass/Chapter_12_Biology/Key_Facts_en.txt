# Key Facts & Figures — Biology: Human Body

## Part 1: High-Yield Facts

| Fact | Description | Examiner's Trap | Trap Type |
|---|---|---|---|
| Largest organ of the body | Skin | Liver is the largest *internal* organ — options mix both | Similar-name confusion |
| Largest gland | Liver | Pancreas is the largest *mixed* gland; pituitary is the "master gland" | Near-correct statement |
| Smallest bone | Stapes (in the ear) | Femur is the *longest* bone — both appear together | Similar-name confusion |
| Total bones in adult human | 206 | Infant has ~300 bones; question may not specify "adult" | Missing qualifier |
| Normal body temperature | 37°C (98.6°F) | Options swap °C and °F values | Unit swap |
| Normal blood pH | 7.4 (slightly alkaline) | Trap option says "slightly acidic" | Near-correct statement |
| Universal blood donor | O negative | AB positive is universal *recipient* — frequently swapped | Reversed pair |
| Blood discovered (circulation) | William Harvey | Landsteiner discovered blood *groups* — different person | Similar-name confusion |
| Powerhouse of the cell | Mitochondria | Ribosome makes proteins; nucleus controls the cell | Near-correct statement |
| Functional unit of kidney | Nephron | Neuron is the nerve cell — one-letter difference | Similar-name confusion |
| Functional unit of nervous system | Neuron | Confused with nephron (kidney) | Similar-name confusion |
| Largest artery | Aorta | Pulmonary artery carries *deoxygenated* blood — exception trap | Exception reversal |
| Only vein carrying oxygenated blood | Pulmonary vein | "All veins carry deoxygenated blood" is false | Negative/absolute statement |
| Normal RBC count | ~5 million per mm³ | WBC ~7,000–8,000; platelets ~1.5–4 lakh — values swapped | Number swap |
| RBC lifespan | 120 days | WBC lifespan is a few days; trap swaps them | Number swap |
| RBC formed in | Red bone marrow | In foetus, formed in liver/spleen | Missing qualifier |
| Haemoglobin (normal, men) | ~13–17 g/dL | Women's range ~12–15 g/dL; options mix them | Number swap |
| Insulin secreted by | Beta cells of Islets of Langerhans (pancreas) | Glucagon comes from *alpha* cells | Reversed pair |
| Deficiency of insulin | Diabetes mellitus | Diabetes *insipidus* is ADH deficiency — different disease | Similar-name confusion |
| Vitamin D deficiency | Rickets (children), Osteomalacia (adults) | Osteoporosis is calcium/age-related | Near-correct statement |
| Vitamin C deficiency | Scurvy | Beriberi is Vitamin B1 deficiency | Similar-name confusion |
| Night blindness | Vitamin A deficiency | Xerophthalmia is its severe eye form | Near-correct statement |
| Iron deficiency | Anaemia | Goitre is iodine deficiency | Similar-name confusion |
| Iodine deficiency | Goitre | Cretinism occurs in children (thyroid) | Near-correct statement |
| Master gland | Pituitary | Hypothalamus *controls* the pituitary — "master of master" | Near-correct statement |
| Largest endocrine gland | Thyroid | Pituitary is smaller but called master gland | Similar-name confusion |
| Normal heart rate | ~72 beats/min | Blood pressure 120/80 mmHg — numbers get swapped | Number swap |
| Instrument for blood pressure | Sphygmomanometer | Stethoscope listens to heart sounds | Similar-name confusion |
| ECG relates to | Heart | EEG is for brain — one-letter trap | Similar-name confusion |
| Largest part of brain | Cerebrum | Cerebellum controls balance/coordination | Similar-name confusion |
| Body's balance controlled by | Cerebellum | Medulla controls involuntary actions (heartbeat, breathing) | Near-correct statement |
| Longest nerve | Sciatic nerve | Largest cranial nerve is the vagus | Similar-name confusion |
| Total spinal nerves | 31 pairs | Cranial nerves are 12 pairs — swapped in options | Number swap |
| DNA discovered (structure) | Watson & Crick (1953) | Griffith/Avery did transformation experiments | Similar-name confusion |
| Total chromosomes in humans | 46 (23 pairs) | Gametes have 23 — question may not specify | Missing qualifier |
| Sex determination | Father's sperm (X or Y) | Trap: "mother determines sex" — false | Reversed pair |
| Digestion begins in | Mouth (salivary amylase) | Protein digestion begins in the *stomach* | Near-correct statement |
| Most digestion/absorption occurs in | Small intestine | Large intestine absorbs mainly water | Near-correct statement |
| Bile produced by | Liver | Bile is *stored* in the gallbladder — classic trap | Reversed pair |
| Bile function | Emulsification of fats | Bile contains no enzyme | Near-correct statement |
| pH of stomach | ~1.5–2 (acidic, HCl) | Trap says neutral or alkaline | Near-correct statement |
| Normal breathing rate | 12–20 per minute | Swapped with heart rate (72) | Number swap |
| Exchange of gases occurs in | Alveoli | Bronchi/trachea are only passages | Near-correct statement |
| Oxygen carried by | Haemoglobin (as oxyhaemoglobin) | CO₂ carried mainly as bicarbonate | Near-correct statement |
| CO poisoning gas binds | Haemoglobin (carboxyhaemoglobin) | CO binds ~200–250× more strongly than O₂ | Near-correct statement |
| Urine formed in | Kidney (nephron) | Bladder only *stores* urine | Reversed pair |
| Average human blood volume | ~5 litres (adult) | Trap options: 7–8 litres | Number swap |
| Blood is what % of body weight | ~7–8% | Confused with 5-litre volume figure | Number swap |
| Sweat glands type | Exocrine | Endocrine glands are ductless | Near-correct statement |
| Largest muscle | Gluteus maximus | Longest muscle is sartorius; strongest (by force) is masseter | Similar-name confusion |
| Total teeth in adult | 32 | Children have 20 milk teeth | Missing qualifier |
| Dental formula (adult) | 2:1:2:3 (per half jaw) | Milk teeth formula 2:1:0:2 | Number swap |
| Enamel | Hardest substance in the body | Bone is second — trap says bone is hardest | Near-correct statement |
| Water content of body | ~60–70% | Options give 90% (that's blood plasma ~90% water) | Number swap |
| ATP | Energy currency of the cell | Produced mainly in mitochondria | Near-correct statement |
| Antibodies produced by | Lymphocytes (WBC) | Antigens trigger the response | Reversed pair |

| Normal blood sugar (fasting) | ~70–100 mg/dL | Post-meal <140 mg/dL | Number swap |
| Haemophilia | Bleeding disorder (clotting fails) | Colour blindness is also X-linked — paired trap | Similar-name confusion |
| Thalassemia | Defective haemoglobin synthesis | Sickle-cell anaemia is a different Hb defect | Similar-name confusion |
