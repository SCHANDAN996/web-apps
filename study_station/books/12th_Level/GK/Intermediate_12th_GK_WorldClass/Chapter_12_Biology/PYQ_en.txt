# 📊 PYQ Analysis — Biology: Human Body

## 1. How Exams Ask Questions from This Chapter

Across SSC (CGL, CHSL, GD), RRB (NTPC, Group D), Banking (IBPS/SBI clerk-level GK) and State PCS prelims, the Human Body is one of the most reliably recurring areas of Biology. The questions are almost always **direct, fact-based, one-liners** — the exam does not test deep physiology, it tests whether you remember the *right* fact and can reject the *almost-right* one.

The pattern, in words:

- **"Which organ/gland…"** questions dominate: largest gland (liver), largest endocrine gland (thyroid), master gland (pituitary), largest organ (skin), smallest bone (stapes), longest bone (femur), strongest muscle (masseter/tongue as distractors).
- **Vitamin–deficiency pairs** are asked again and again: night blindness (A), scurvy (C), rickets (D), beriberi (B1), pellagra (B3). Examiners love swapping the pairs.
- **Blood facts**: pH of blood (~7.4), universal donor (O−) and universal recipient (AB+), RBC lifespan (120 days), RBC formation site (red bone marrow), haemoglobin's iron, blood discovered-circulation by William Harvey.
- **Disease–organism matching**: which diseases are viral (polio, rabies, measles, AIDS), bacterial (TB, cholera, typhoid, tetanus, plague), protozoan (malaria, kala-azar, amoebiasis). The classic trap: calling TB or cholera "viral".
- **Numbers**: 206 bones, 32 teeth, 23 pairs of chromosomes, normal body temperature 37°C (98.6°F), normal blood pressure 120/80 mmHg, heart beats ~72/minute.
- **Systems and their organs**: nephron as the unit of kidney, alveoli in lungs, neurons in the nervous system, insulin from the pancreas (islets of Langerhans), bile from the liver (stored in gall bladder).

## 2. Sub-Topics That Recur Most

In order of how often they appear in question papers:

1. **Vitamins and deficiency diseases** — the single most repeated sub-topic.
2. **Blood and circulation** — blood groups, RBC/WBC/platelets, heart structure.
3. **Bones, muscles, teeth** — counts and superlatives (largest/smallest/longest).
4. **Glands and hormones** — endocrine vs exocrine, insulin, thyroxine, adrenaline.
5. **Digestive system** — enzymes, where digestion of each nutrient begins, role of liver and pancreas.
6. **Diseases and pathogens** — viral vs bacterial classification, vaccines (BCG, DPT, OPV).
7. **Nervous system and sense organs** — brain parts (cerebrum, cerebellum, medulla), retina, cochlea.
8. **Excretion and respiration** — kidney/nephron, urea, exchange of gases.

## 3. Exam-Wise Flavour (in words, not numbers)

- **SSC CGL / CHSL**: superlatives and vitamin–disease pairs; options are deliberately close (e.g., "largest gland — liver / pancreas / thyroid / pituitary").
- **RRB NTPC / Group D**: very direct one-liners — bone count, blood groups, normal body temperature; often the simplest version of the fact.
- **Banking (IBPS/SBI)**: fewer questions, but when Biology appears it is usually vitamins, vaccines, or a disease in the news.
- **State PCS prelims**: slightly deeper — statement-based questions ("Which of the following statements is/are correct?") mixing two or three facts, e.g., insulin + pancreas + diabetes in one question.

## 4. The Examiner's Traps (with examples)

**Trap 1 — Almost Correct (लगभग-सही):** "Largest gland in the human body?" Options: (a) Pancreas (b) Liver (c) Thyroid (d) Pituitary. Thyroid is the largest *endocrine* gland — a tempting wrong answer if you misread. Correct: Liver.

**Trap 2 — Swapped Pairs:** "Night blindness is caused by deficiency of —" Options list vitamins A, B, C, D. The trap option is usually Vitamin D (rickets) placed first. Correct: Vitamin A.

**Trap 3 — Negative Phrasing (नकारात्मक वाक्य):** "Which of the following is NOT a viral disease?" Options: Polio, Rabies, Measles, Cholera. Students who skim miss the "NOT". Correct: Cholera (bacterial — *Vibrio cholerae*).

**Trap 4 — Similar-Sounding Names:** "Bile is stored in —" Options: Liver, Gall bladder, Pancreas, Spleen. Bile is *produced* in the liver but *stored* in the gall bladder. The question's verb decides the answer.

**Trap 5 — Number Confusion:** 206 bones vs 213 (childhood ~300, fused later); 23 pairs of chromosomes vs 23 chromosomes; 32 teeth in adults vs 20 milk teeth. Examiners place the childhood number as an option.

**Trap 6 — Universal Donor/Recipient Swap:** O− is the universal *donor*; AB+ is the universal *recipient*. Questions routinely swap the two in the options.

## 5. Ten High-Yield PYQ-Style Questions with Solutions

**Q1. The largest gland in the human body is —**
(a) Thyroid (b) Pituitary (c) Liver (d) Pancreas
**Answer: (c)**
Solution: The liver is the largest gland (and largest internal organ). Trap type: Almost Correct — thyroid is the largest *endocrine* gland, pituitary is the "master gland", neither is the largest overall.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Biology (Body Fluids/Digestion chapters)

**Q2. Deficiency of Vitamin C causes —**
(a) Rickets (b) Scurvy (c) Beriberi (d) Night blindness
**Answer: (b)**
Solution: Scurvy (bleeding gums) is caused by Vitamin C deficiency. Rickets → D, Beriberi → B1, Night blindness → A. Trap type: Swapped Pairs.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Biology / Class 6 Science (Components of Food)

**Q3. Which of the following diseases is NOT caused by a virus?**
(a) Polio (b) Measles (c) Tuberculosis (d) Rabies
**Answer: (c)**
Solution: TB is caused by the bacterium *Mycobacterium tuberculosis*; the other three are viral. Trap type: Negative Phrasing — read the "NOT" twice before answering.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Science (Why Do We Fall Ill)

**Q4. The universal blood donor group is —**
(a) AB+ (b) AB− (c) O+ (d) O−
**Answer: (d)**
Solution: O− has neither A/B antigens nor Rh antigen, so it can be given to anyone. AB+ is the universal *recipient* — the classic swap trap.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Biology

**Q5. The functional unit of the kidney is the —**
(a) Neuron (b) Alveolus (c) Nephron (d) Villus
**Answer: (c)**
Solution: Nephron filters blood in the kidney. Neuron = nerve cell, alveolus = lung air sac, villus = intestinal absorption structure. Trap type: Similar-Sounding Names (all are "functional units" of different organs).
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science (Life Processes)

**Q6. Insulin is secreted by —**
(a) Liver (b) Islets of Langerhans of pancreas (c) Thyroid (d) Adrenal gland
**Answer: (b)**
Solution: Beta cells of the islets of Langerhans (pancreas) secrete insulin; its deficiency causes diabetes mellitus. Adrenal → adrenaline is the distractor.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science (Control and Coordination)

**Q7. The number of bones in an adult human skeleton is —**
(a) 206 (b) 300 (c) 213 (d) 250
**Answer: (a)**
Solution: Adults have 206 bones; infants are born with about 300, many of which fuse. Trap type: Number Confusion — the childhood figure is planted as an option.
Source: NCERT Class 6 Science (Body Movements)

**Q8. Bile juice is stored in the —**
(a) Liver (b) Pancreas (c) Gall bladder (d) Duodenum
**Answer: (c)**
Solution: Bile is *produced* by the liver and *stored/concentrated* in the gall bladder. Trap type: Almost Correct — "liver" is right for production, wrong for storage.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science (Life Processes)

**Q9. The lifespan of a red blood cell (RBC) is approximately —**
(a) 20 days (b) 60 days (c) 120 days (d) 200 days
**Answer: (c)**
Solution: RBCs live about 120 days and are then destroyed mainly in the spleen ("graveyard of RBCs"). WBCs live much shorter — a common confusion.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Biology

**Q10. Which part of the brain controls balance and posture?**
(a) Cerebrum (b) Cerebellum (c) Medulla oblongata (d) Hypothalamus
**Answer: (b)**
Solution: The cerebellum coordinates balance and muscular movement; the medulla controls involuntary actions (heartbeat, breathing) — the two are routinely swapped in options.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science (Control and Coordination)

## 6. The 15/45-Second Rule Applied

**15-second questions (answer instantly, bank the time):** Pure recall with no statement-matching — Q2 (Vitamin C → scurvy), Q7 (206 bones), Q4 (O− donor). If you have revised the Key Facts sheet, these should take under 15 seconds. Do not re-read the options twice; mark and move.

**45-second questions (worth the extra time):** Negative-phrasing and statement questions — Q3 ("NOT caused by a virus") and any "which statements are correct" item in PCS papers. Here, spend up to 45 seconds: underline "NOT/EXCEPT" mentally, classify each option (viral/bacterial), then answer. Rushing these is exactly how the trap works.

**Skip signal:** If a question asks for a fact you have never seen (e.g., an obscure hormone or enzyme), do not gamble beyond 20 seconds — in negative-marking exams, a blank costs 0, a guess can cost 0.25–0.5 marks.

## 7. One-Line Revision Takeaways

- Largest gland = liver; largest *endocrine* gland = thyroid; master gland = pituitary.
- A→night blindness, B1→beriberi, C→scurvy, D→rickets.
- O− donates to all; AB+ receives from all.
- 206 bones, 32 teeth, 23 chromosome pairs, 120-day RBC, 37°C, 120/80 mmHg.
- TB, cholera, typhoid, tetanus, plague = bacterial; polio, rabies, measles, AIDS = viral; malaria = protozoan.
- Bile: made in liver, stored in gall bladder. Insulin: islets of Langerhans.
- Cerebellum = balance; medulla = heartbeat/breathing; cerebrum = thinking.
