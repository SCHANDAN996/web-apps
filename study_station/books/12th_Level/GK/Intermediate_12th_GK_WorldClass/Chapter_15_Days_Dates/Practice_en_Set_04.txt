Questions 76–100: Medium

76. World Environment Day is observed every year on which date?
(a) 16 September (b) 5 July (c) 5 June (d) 22 April
Answer: (c)
Solution: The tempting option 22 April is Earth Day, and 16 September is World Ozone Day — examiners often swap these three environment-related dates. World Environment Day, led by UNEP since 1973, is observed on 5 June, so the answer is 5 June.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Chemistry (Environmental Chemistry) / UNEP

77. International Yoga Day is celebrated on 21 June because this day marks which event in the Northern Hemisphere?
(a) Summer solstice (b) Winter solstice (c) Autumn equinox (d) Spring equinox
Answer: (a)
Solution: Many students pick the equinox options, but equinoxes fall in March and September, not June. 21 June is the longest day of the year in the Northern Hemisphere — the summer solstice — which is why PM Narendra Modi proposed this date at the UN in 2014, so the answer is summer solstice.
Source: NCERT Class 6 Social Science (Motions of the Earth)

78. National Voters' Day in India is observed on 25 January to commemorate which event?
(a) First general election of 1951–52 (b) Foundation of the Election Commission of India in 1950 (c) Adoption of the Constitution (d) First use of EVMs
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is the first general election option, since elections and voters seem naturally linked, but the first general election was held in 1951–52, not on 25 January. The Election Commission of India was established on 25 January 1950, and National Voters' Day has been observed since 2011 to mark this, so the answer is the foundation of the Election Commission of India in 1950.
Source: Election Commission of India (eci.gov.in)

79. World Health Day is observed on 7 April every year. What is its significance?
(a) Launch of the Alma-Ata Declaration (b) Eradication of smallpox (c) Founding of the World Health Organization in 1948 (d) Establishment of UNICEF
Answer: (c)
Solution: Smallpox eradication (1980) and the Alma-Ata Declaration (1978) are real WHO milestones used as distractors, but neither falls on 7 April. The World Health Organization was founded on 7 April 1948, and World Health Day marks that date, so the answer is the founding of the World Health Organization in 1948.
Source: WHO official website (who.int)

80. National Science Day is celebrated in India on 28 February to mark which discovery?
(a) Theory of relativity (b) Raman Effect by C.V. Raman (c) Discovery of the electron (d) Invention of the radio telescope
Answer: (b)
Solution: The other options are famous physics milestones but none is linked to 28 February. C.V. Raman announced the discovery of the Raman Effect on 28 February 1928, for which he won the Nobel Prize in 1930, and India observes National Science Day on this date, so the answer is the Raman Effect by C.V. Raman.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Physics

81. Which day is observed as World Water Day?
(a) 22 April (b) 22 March (c) 5 June (d) 11 July
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap here is 22 April (Earth Day), which students confuse with Water Day because both relate to nature. The UN designated 22 March as World Water Day in 1993, so the answer is 22 March.
Source: United Nations (un.org)

82. Hindi Diwas is celebrated in India on 14 September because on this day:
(a) The Constituent Assembly adopted Hindi in Devanagari script as the official language of the Union (b) Hindi was declared the national language (c) The first Hindi newspaper was published (d) Hindi was included in the Eighth Schedule
Answer: (a)
Solution: The classic trap is "national language" — India has no national language, and examiners exploit this confusion. On 14 September 1949, the Constituent Assembly adopted Hindi in Devanagari script as the official language of the Union, so the answer is the Constituent Assembly's adoption of Hindi as the official language.
Source: NCERT Class 8 Social Science / Department of Official Language (rajbhasha.gov.in)

83. World Population Day is observed on 11 July. This date was chosen because:
(a) World population reached 5 billion on this day in 1987 (b) The UN was founded on this day (c) The first world census was completed (d) The Family Planning Association was formed
Answer: (a)
Solution: The UN founding (24 October 1945) is a tempting distractor since the UN runs this observance, but the dates differ. World population crossed 5 billion on 11 July 1987, and the UNDP established World Population Day in 1989 inspired by that "Day of Five Billion", so the answer is that world population reached 5 billion on this day in 1987.
Source: United Nations (un.org)

84. National Unity Day (Rashtriya Ekta Diwas) is observed on 31 October, the birth anniversary of:
(a) Jawaharlal Nehru (b) Subhas Chandra Bose (c) Lal Bahadur Shastri (d) Sardar Vallabhbhai Patel
Answer: (d)
Solution: Shastri's birth anniversary (2 October, shared with Gandhi) and Bose's (23 January, Parakram Diwas) are common confusions. Sardar Vallabhbhai Patel was born on 31 October 1875, and since 2014 this day is observed as National Unity Day honouring his role in integrating princely states, so the answer is Sardar Vallabhbhai Patel.
Source: Government of India (india.gov.in)

85. World Red Cross Day is celebrated on 8 May, which is the birth anniversary of:
(a) Florence Nightingale (b) Mother Teresa (c) Henry Dunant (d) Louis Pasteur
Answer: (c)
Solution: Florence Nightingale is the tempting wrong choice because her birthday, 12 May, is International Nurses Day — just four days later, a deliberate chronological trap. Henry Dunant, founder of the Red Cross and first Nobel Peace Prize winner, was born on 8 May 1828, so the answer is Henry Dunant.
Source: International Committee of the Red Cross (icrc.org)

86. Constitution Day (Samvidhan Divas) in India is observed on 26 November because on this day:
(a) The Constitution came into force (b) The Constituent Assembly adopted the Constitution (c) The first Lok Sabha was constituted (d) The Preamble was amended
Answer: (b)
Solution: The biggest trap is "came into force" — that happened on 26 January 1950, which is Republic Day, and examiners routinely swap these two dates. The Constituent Assembly adopted the Constitution on 26 November 1949, observed as Constitution Day since 2015, so the answer is the adoption of the Constitution by the Constituent Assembly.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Political Science (Indian Constitution at Work)

87. International Women's Day is observed on:
(a) 24 October (b) 10 December (c) 11 February (d) 8 March
Answer: (d)
Solution: 10 December (Human Rights Day) is a distractor since women's rights are human rights, but the dates differ. International Women's Day has been observed on 8 March, with UN recognition since 1977, so the answer is 8 March.
Source: United Nations (un.org)

88. National Youth Day in India is celebrated on 12 January, the birth anniversary of:
(a) Swami Dayanand Saraswati (b) Bhagat Singh (c) Chandrashekhar Azad (d) Swami Vivekananda
Answer: (d)
Solution: Bhagat Singh (28 September) is a frequent confusion because he is also an iconic youth figure. Swami Vivekananda was born on 12 January 1863, and since 1985 this day is National Youth Day in India, so the answer is Swami Vivekananda.
Source: Government of India (india.gov.in)

89. World AIDS Day is observed on which date?
(a) 1 January (b) 1 December (c) 7 April (d) 24 March
Answer: (b)
Solution: 24 March (World Tuberculosis Day) is the trap, as TB and AIDS are often studied together in health chapters. World AIDS Day has been observed on 1 December since 1988, so the answer is 1 December.
Source: UNAIDS / WHO

90. Kargil Vijay Diwas is observed in India on:
(a) 7 December (b) 16 December (c) 26 July (d) 15 August
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is 16 December, which is Vijay Diwas marking the 1971 war victory over Pakistan — examiners exploit the similar names of the two victory days. Kargil Vijay Diwas marks 26 July 1999, when Operation Vijay ended with Indian forces recapturing the Kargil heights, so the answer is 26 July.
Source: Ministry of Defence, Government of India

91. World Book and Copyright Day is observed on 23 April. This date is associated with the death anniversary of which famous writer?
(a) Leo Tolstoy (b) Charles Dickens (c) William Shakespeare (d) Rabindranath Tagore
Answer: (c)
Solution: Tagore is a tempting choice for Indian aspirants, but he died on 7 August 1941. UNESCO chose 23 April because William Shakespeare (and Miguel de Cervantes) died on this date in 1616, so the answer is William Shakespeare.
Source: UNESCO (unesco.org)

92. National Education Day is observed on 11 November, the birth anniversary of:
(a) Dr. Sarvepalli Radhakrishnan (b) Pandit Madan Mohan Malaviya (c) Dr. B.R. Ambedkar (d) Maulana Abul Kalam Azad
Answer: (d)
Solution: Radhakrishnan is the classic trap — his birthday, 5 September, is Teachers' Day, and examiners swap the two education-related days. Maulana Abul Kalam Azad, India's first Education Minister, was born on 11 November 1888, and this day is National Education Day, so the answer is Maulana Abul Kalam Azad.
Source: Ministry of Education, Government of India

93. International Day of Non-Violence is observed on 2 October because it is the birth anniversary of:
(a) Nelson Mandela (b) Martin Luther King Jr. (c) Mahatma Gandhi (d) Gautam Buddha
Answer: (c)
Solution: Mandela and King are plausible distractors as global icons of non-violence, but the UN resolution of 2007 explicitly links the day to Gandhi. Mahatma Gandhi was born on 2 October 1869, and the UN observes this date as the International Day of Non-Violence, so the answer is Mahatma Gandhi.
Source: United Nations (un.org)

94. National Maritime Day in India is observed on 5 April to commemorate:
(a) The founding of the Indian Navy (b) The establishment of the Shipping Corporation of India (c) The launch of INS Vikrant (d) The maiden voyage of SS Loyalty from Mumbai to London in 1919
Answer: (d)
Solution: Navy Day (4 December) is the tempting confusion, but it marks the 1971 attack on Karachi harbour, not 5 April. On 5 April 1919, SS Loyalty, the first ship of the Scindia Steam Navigation Company, sailed from Mumbai to London, and National Maritime Day marks this voyage, so the answer is the maiden voyage of SS Loyalty in 1919.
Source: Directorate General of Shipping, Government of India

95. World Tourism Day is observed on 27 September. Which organization established it?
(a) UN World Tourism Organization (UNWTO) (b) UNESCO (c) International Air Transport Association (d) World Economic Forum
Answer: (a)
Solution: UNESCO is the trap because it manages World Heritage Sites and students associate all cultural observances with it. World Tourism Day was established by the UN World Tourism Organization in 1980, marking the adoption of its statutes on 27 September 1970, so the answer is the UN World Tourism Organization.
Source: UNWTO (unwto.org)

96. Human Rights Day is observed on 10 December to mark:
(a) The adoption of the Universal Declaration of Human Rights in 1948 (b) The founding of the UN (c) The end of apartheid in South Africa (d) The signing of the Geneva Conventions
Answer: (a)
Solution: The UN founding (24 October 1945, UN Day) is the common confusion since the UN sponsors this day too. The UN General Assembly adopted the Universal Declaration of Human Rights on 10 December 1948 in Paris, so the answer is the adoption of the Universal Declaration of Human Rights in 1948.
Source: United Nations (un.org)

97. National Sports Day in India is celebrated on 29 August, the birth anniversary of which legendary sportsperson?
(a) Sachin Tendulkar (b) Milkha Singh (c) P.T. Usha (d) Dhyan Chand
Answer: (d)
Solution: Milkha Singh is a tempting distractor as another national sporting legend, but no national day falls on his birthday. Hockey wizard Major Dhyan Chand was born on 29 August 1905, and India celebrates National Sports Day on this date, so the answer is Dhyan Chand.
Source: Ministry of Youth Affairs and Sports, Government of India

98. World Ozone Day (International Day for the Preservation of the Ozone Layer) is observed on 16 September to mark:
(a) The discovery of the ozone hole (b) The signing of the Montreal Protocol in 1987 (c) The Kyoto Protocol (d) The Rio Earth Summit
Answer: (b)
Solution: The Kyoto Protocol (1997) and Rio Summit (1992) are climate milestones used as distractors, but both concern climate change rather than the ozone layer specifically. The Montreal Protocol on substances that deplete the ozone layer was signed on 16 September 1987, so the answer is the signing of the Montreal Protocol in 1987.
Source: United Nations (un.org)

99. Which of the following pairs of day and date is correctly matched?
(a) World Consumer Rights Day — 15 March (b) World No Tobacco Day — 31 March (c) International Labour Day — 1 June (d) World Press Freedom Day — 3 April
Answer: (a)
Solution: Each wrong option shifts the correct date by exactly one month — a classic chronological-confusion trap: No Tobacco Day is 31 May, Labour Day is 1 May, and Press Freedom Day is 3 May. World Consumer Rights Day is correctly observed on 15 March, the date of President Kennedy's 1962 consumer rights address, so the answer is World Consumer Rights Day — 15 March.
Source: Consumers International / United Nations

100. Which of the following statements about important days is correct?
(a) Army Day is observed on 15 January, when General K.M. Cariappa took over as the first Indian Commander-in-Chief (b) Air Force Day is observed on 8 September (c) Navy Day is observed on 4 January (d) Flag Day is observed on 26 January
Answer: (a)
Solution: The wrong options use the correct day-of-month but wrong months — Air Force Day is 8 October (not September) and Navy Day is 4 December (not January), a trap built on partial memory. On 15 January 1949, General K.M. Cariappa became the first Indian Commander-in-Chief of the Army, and Army Day marks this, so the answer is the statement about Army Day on 15 January.
Source: Ministry of Defence, Government of India
