# 📊 PYQ Analysis — Important Days & Dates

## 1. How Exams Ask Questions from This Chapter

Questions on Important Days & Dates appear regularly in SSC (CGL, CHSL, MTS, GD), Banking (IBPS, SBI), Railway (NTPC, Group D) and State PCS prelims. UPSC asks them rarely and only when linked to a UN theme or a government scheme. The chapter is **purely factual** — there is no concept to apply, only memory to test. That is why examiners rely heavily on traps rather than difficulty.

**What the pattern looks like (in words, not numbers):**

- **Direct date recall** is the most common form: "When is National Science Day observed?" — one fact, one answer.
- **Theme-based questions** have grown in recent years: "What was the theme of World Environment Day 2023?" — these favour candidates who revise current-year themes.
- **Reverse questions**: "Which day is observed on 14 November?" — the examiner gives the date, you supply the day.
- **"First observed in which year?"** — asked for famous days like International Yoga Day (2015) or National Voters' Day (2011).
- **Who established/declared it?** — UN, UNESCO, WHO, Government of India — the declaring body is a favourite trap zone.
- **Month-cluster questions**: days falling in the same month (e.g., several days in March or October) are mixed together to create confusion.

**Sub-topics that recur most often:**

1. UN-declared international days (Environment Day, Yoga Day, Human Rights Day, Women's Day)
2. Indian national days tied to events or personalities (Science Day, Voters' Day, Kisan Diwas, Hindi Diwas, Constitution Day)
3. Days linked to armed forces (Vijay Diwas, Kargil Vijay Diwas, Navy Day, Air Force Day, Army Day)
4. Health-organisation days (World Health Day, World AIDS Day, No Tobacco Day)
5. Themes of the current or previous year (dynamic — revise before every exam)

## 2. Examiner's Trap Types (with examples)

| Trap Type | How It Works | Classic Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Almost Correct** | Option differs by one day or one date | World Environment Day — 5 June (trap: 5 July / 16 June) |
| **Chronological Confusion** | Two similar days in the same month swapped | National Youth Day 12 Jan vs. National Voters' Day 25 Jan |
| **Similar Names** | Two days with near-identical names | World Health Day (7 April) vs. World AIDS Day (1 December) |
| **Negative Phrasing** | "Which of the following is NOT correctly matched?" | One pair has a wrong date hidden among three correct pairs |
| **Declaring Body Swap** | UN vs. UNESCO vs. WHO vs. Government of India | World Book Day is UNESCO's, not UN General Assembly's |
| **Year of First Observance** | Off-by-one year options | International Yoga Day first observed 2015 (trap: 2014) |

## 3. Top 10 High-Yield PYQ-Style Questions (with Solutions)

**Q1. National Science Day is observed in India on —**
(a) 28 January (b) 28 February (c) 28 March (d) 28 April
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** 28 February marks C.V. Raman's discovery of the Raman Effect (1928). **Trap type: Almost Correct** — all options are the 28th of a month; only the month differs.
*Source: PYQ-style*

**Q2. International Yoga Day is observed on 21 June because —**
(a) it is the shortest day of the year
(b) it is the longest day of the year in the Northern Hemisphere
(c) it is the birthday of a famous yoga guru
(d) the UN was founded on this day
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** PM Modi proposed 21 June as it is the summer solstice — the longest day in the Northern Hemisphere. First observed in 2015. **Trap type: Almost Correct** — option (a) reverses longest/shortest.
*Source: PYQ-style*

**Q3. Kargil Vijay Diwas is observed every year on —**
(a) 16 December (b) 26 July (c) 7 December (d) 15 January
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** 26 July 1999 — Operation Vijay concluded. **Trap type: Similar Names / Chronological Confusion** — 16 December is Vijay Diwas (1971 war), the most common wrong pick.
*Source: PYQ-style*

**Q4. Which of the following pairs is NOT correctly matched?**
(a) World Environment Day — 5 June
(b) World Health Day — 7 April
(c) Human Rights Day — 10 December
(d) World AIDS Day — 10 November
**Answer: (d)**
**Solution:** World AIDS Day is 1 December, not 10 November. **Trap type: Negative Phrasing** — three correct pairs lull you into skimming; read every pair fully.
*Source: PYQ-style*

**Q5. National Voters' Day, observed on 25 January, marks the foundation day of —**
(a) the Supreme Court of India
(b) the Election Commission of India
(c) the Parliament of India
(d) the UPSC
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** The Election Commission was established on 25 January 1950; the day has been observed since 2011. **Trap type: Declaring Body Swap** — UPSC (1 October 1926) is the tempting wrong answer.
*Source: PYQ-style*

**Q6. World Book and Copyright Day, observed on 23 April, was designated by —**
(a) WHO (b) UNICEF (c) UNESCO (d) World Bank
**Answer: (c)**
**Solution:** UNESCO designated it in 1995; 23 April is linked to the death anniversaries of Shakespeare and Cervantes. **Trap type: Declaring Body Swap** — candidates often pick "UN" generically; UNESCO is the precise body.
*Source: PYQ-style*

**Q7. Hindi Diwas is observed on 14 September because on this day —**
(a) the Constitution was adopted
(b) Hindi in Devanagari script was adopted as an official language by the Constituent Assembly (1949)
(c) the first Hindi newspaper was published
(d) Hindi was declared the national language
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** The Constituent Assembly adopted Hindi in Devanagari script as an official language on 14 September 1949. **Trap type: Almost Correct** — option (d) is factually wrong (India has no "national language"), yet it is the most-picked wrong answer.
*Source: PYQ-style*

**Q8. Constitution Day (Samvidhan Divas) is observed on —**
(a) 26 January (b) 26 November (c) 15 August (d) 26 December
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** The Constitution was *adopted* on 26 November 1949 and *came into force* on 26 January 1950. **Trap type: Chronological Confusion** — adoption vs. enforcement is the single most repeated trap in this chapter.
*Source: PYQ-style*

**Q9. Indian Navy Day is observed on 4 December to commemorate —**
(a) the commissioning of INS Vikrant
(b) Operation Trident during the 1971 war
(c) the founding of the Indian Navy
(d) the Goa liberation
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** On the night of 4–5 December 1971, the Navy launched Operation Trident against Karachi harbour. **Trap type: Similar Names** — Air Force Day (8 October) and Army Day (15 January) are often mixed in the same question set.
*Source: PYQ-style*

**Q10. World No Tobacco Day is observed on —**
(a) 31 May (b) 1 May (c) 7 April (d) 11 July
**Answer: (a)**
**Solution:** Observed on 31 May under WHO's leadership since 1988. **Trap type: Almost Correct** — 1 May (Labour Day) and 7 April (Health Day) sit in the same "WHO days" memory cluster.
*Source: PYQ-style*

## 4. The 15/45-Second Rule Applied

**Example 1 — Q4 above (NOT correctly matched):**
- **First 15 seconds:** Read the word "NOT" twice, then check each pair against memory. If you instantly spot World AIDS Day = 1 December (not 10 November), mark (d) and move on.
- **If not sure by 45 seconds:** Do not re-read all four pairs a third time. Eliminate the pairs you are 100% sure of (Environment Day, Human Rights Day are almost universally known), guess between the remaining two, and move on. This question type eats time only if you let it.

**Example 2 — Q8 above (Constitution Day):**
- **First 15 seconds:** The moment you see both 26 January and 26 November as options, recall the adoption/enforcement distinction — answer is 26 November. Done in under 10 seconds if the hook is memorised.
- **If confused at 45 seconds:** Ask yourself one question — "Republic Day celebrates enforcement, so adoption must be the *other* date." This logic rescue takes 10 seconds and converts a guess into a sure answer.

**General rule for this chapter:** every question is either a 10-second recall or a skip. There is no calculation — if the fact is not in memory within 15 seconds, eliminate and guess within 45, and never leave it blank if there is no negative marking.

## 5. Revision Priorities Based on the Pattern

1. **First priority:** UN days + Indian national days with a historical reason (Science Day, Constitution Day, Hindi Diwas, Voters' Day) — these dominate.
2. **Second priority:** Armed forces days and their linked operations — the 1971 war cluster (Vijay Diwas, Navy Day) versus the 1999 cluster (Kargil Vijay Diwas).
3. **Third priority:** Declaring bodies — make a one-line list: UNESCO (Book Day, Teachers' Day-International), WHO (Health Day, No Tobacco Day), UNGA (Yoga Day, Environment Day).
4. **Before every exam:** revise only the *current and previous year's themes* — this is the one dynamic slice of an otherwise static chapter.
