# 🧒 The Feynman Corner — Important Days & Dates

## The Most Confusing Concept: "Why do so many days sound the same but fall on different dates?"

Let me tell you a secret. Students don't lose marks on days they *don't know*. They lose marks on days they *almost* know. The examiner's favourite weapon is the **look-alike pair** — two days that feel identical but are actually different.

---

## Explain It Like I'm 12

Imagine your school has two celebrations:

- **"Sports Day"** — held every year on a **fixed date**, say 15th March. Same date, every single year.
- **"Annual Function"** — held on the **second Saturday of March**. So the *date changes every year* (in 2024 it was 9th March, in 2025 it is 8th March), but the *rule* stays the same.

Important days work exactly like this. There are **two families**:

**Family 1 — Fixed-Date Days** (like Sports Day)
These never move. Examples:
- National Science Day → **28 February** (C.V. Raman's Raman Effect discovery, 1928)
- World Environment Day → **5 June**
- Human Rights Day → **10 December**
- National Youth Day → **12 January** (Swami Vivekananda's birthday)

**Family 2 — "Nth Weekday of the Month" Days** (like Annual Function)
These follow a *rule*, not a date. Examples:
- World Leprosy Day → **last Sunday of January**
- National Maritime Day (India) is fixed → **5 April**, but **World Maritime Day** → **last Thursday of September**
- Anti-Terrorism Day is fixed (21 May), but many international observances float by rule.

**The trap:** the examiner swaps the families. He writes "World Leprosy Day is observed on 30 January" — sounds right (30 January *is* Martyrs' Day!), but Leprosy Day is the **last Sunday of January**. You knew both facts separately; he mixed them together.

---

## A Concrete Daily-Life Example

Think of your phone's calendar. Your birthday is a **fixed-date event** — 14 August every year. But "Mom's kitty party — first Friday of every month" is a **rule-based event**. If someone asks "When is the kitty party?" and you answer "3rd," you'll be wrong most months. You must answer with the *rule*.

Same with days: never memorise a floating day as a date. Memorise it as a **rule**.

---

## The Unforgettable Trick: "FIX vs FLEX"

> **FIX days have a date. FLEX days have a formula.**

Make two columns in your notebook right now:

| FIX (date never changes) | FLEX (rule-based) |
|---|---|
| 5 June – Environment Day | Last Sunday of Jan – Leprosy Day |
| 28 Feb – Science Day | Last Thursday of Sept – World Maritime Day |
| 10 Dec – Human Rights Day | 2nd Sunday of May – Mother's Day (not an exam day, but a perfect example of the family!) |

**Memory hook:** *"FIX is a photo — it never moves. FLEX is a video — it keeps shifting."*

And one more classic confusion pair to lock in forever:
- **Martyrs' Day (India)** → **30 January** (Gandhiji's assassination, 1948)
- **Martyrs' Day (also observed)** → **23 March** (Bhagat Singh, Rajguru, Sukhdev, 1931)

Trick: *"January mein Bapu, March mein Inquilab"* — Gandhi in January, Revolutionaries in March.
