🪄 MEMORY HOOKS & SHORT TRICKS — Important Days & Dates

**Box 1 — The January Jog: "21-26-30, Run Before Republic!"**
January's big three: **21** = Tripura/Manipur/Meghalaya Statehood Day, **26** = Republic Day, **30** = Martyrs' Day (Gandhi ji's assassination, 1948).
Hook: *"Before the Republic (26) runs in, jog on 21, and bow your head on 30."*

---

**Box 2 — "MARTIN Luther King? No — MARTYRS on 30-1 and 23-3!"**
India has TWO Martyrs' Days — the classic examiner trap:
- **30 January** → Mahatma Gandhi (1948)
- **23 March** → Bhagat Singh, Rajguru, Sukhdev (1931)
Hook: *"Gandhi in January's end (30/1), Bhagat in March's heat (23/3) — G comes before B, J comes before M."*

---

**Box 3 — "April Fools? No — April FOOLS the Examiner!"**
- **5 April** = National Maritime Day
- **7 April** = World Health Day (WHO founded 1948)
- **18 April** = World Heritage Day
- **22 April** = Earth Day
Hook: *"Ships (5) sail to keep you Healthy (7), past Heritage (18) sites, around the Earth (22)."* — dates climb 5 → 7 → 18 → 22.

**Box 4 — "May Day, May Day! Workers on the 1st!"**

Bonus trap: Anti-Terrorism Day is **21 May** (Rajiv Gandhi's death anniversary, 1991) — *"Rajiv left on 21, so terror is fought on 21."*

## ⏭️ SKIP STRATEGY — When to Leave the Question

**Tip 1 — The "Theme of the Year" Trap:**
If a question asks for the *theme* of a day for a specific year (e.g., "Theme of World Environment Day 2023"), and you haven't revised current themes — **SKIP immediately**. Themes change yearly; guessing between four similar-sounding slogans is a coin toss with negative marking. Never gamble more than 5 seconds here.

**Tip 2 — The "Which Organisation Started It" Trap:**
If the question asks which body (UNESCO / WHO / UNGA / ILO) *declared* a day and you only remember the date, not the declaring body — **SKIP**. These options are designed to look identical. Attempt only if you can eliminate at least two options confidently (e.g., health days → WHO, education/science days → UNESCO).

**Tip 3 — The "Date Swap" Trap (15/45-Second Rule):**
If two dates in your memory are fighting (e.g., "Was Ozone Day 16 Sept or 16 Dec?"), give yourself **15 seconds** to recall via the mnemonic. If the hook doesn't fire, mark for review and move on — return only in the last **45 seconds** of the section. A confused date question costs more time than three easy ones. Never let one stubborn date eat your momentum.
