# 📋 Key Facts & Figures — Ancient Indian History

## Part 1: High-Yield Facts

| Fact | Description | Examiner's Trap | Trap Type |
|---|---|---|---|
| Indus Valley Civilization period | c. 3300–1300 BCE (Mature phase c. 2600–1900 BCE) | Options swap Mature and Early Harappan dates | Chronology confusion |
| Harappa discovered by | Daya Ram Sahni (1921), under John Marshall | Confused with R.D. Banerji, who excavated Mohenjo-daro (1922) | Similar names |
| Mohenjo-daro means | "Mound of the Dead" (Sindhi) | Given as "Mound of the Living" | Near-correct statement |
| Great Bath location | Mohenjo-daro | Attributed to Harappa or Lothal | Site swapping |
| Dockyard location | Lothal (Gujarat) | Attributed to Kalibangan or Dholavira | Site swapping |
| Dholavira's special feature | Three-part city division; water reservoirs; signboard with 10 signs | Said to have a dockyard (that is Lothal) | Feature swapping |
| Kalibangan evidence | Ploughed field, fire altars, camel bones | Fire altars also at Lothal — question asks "first/earliest" | Overlapping facts |
| Chanhudaro speciality | Bead-making factory; only city without a citadel | "All Harappan cities had citadels" — false | Absolute statement trap |
| Banawali evidence | Barley (high-quality) | Swapped with wheat | Crop confusion |
| Bronze dancing girl | Found at Mohenjo-daro (lost-wax/cire perdue method) | Attributed to Harappa | Site swapping |
| Pashupati seal | Mohenjo-daro; figure seated in yogic posture with animals | Called "Shiva temple" evidence — it is a seal, not a temple | Near-correct statement |
| Harappan script | Pictographic, written boustrophedon (right-to-left, then left-to-right); still undeciphered | "Deciphered by scholars" — false | Absolute statement trap |
| Harappan metal NOT known | Iron | "Harappans used iron tools" — false | Negative statement |
| Rig Veda composition | c. 1500–1200 BCE; oldest Veda; 10 Mandalas, 1028 hymns | Given as 8 Mandalas or 1000 hymns | Number confusion |
| Gayatri Mantra | Rig Veda, 3rd Mandala, addressed to Savitri | Attributed to Yajur Veda | Text swapping |
| "Tattvamasi" / Upanishads | Philosophical texts; 108 traditionally counted | Confused with Brahmanas/Aranyakas order | Sequence confusion |
| Sama Veda | Root of Indian music | Attributed to Atharva Veda | Text swapping |
| Atharva Veda | Charms, spells, medicine | Called "Veda of rituals" (that is Yajur Veda) | Text swapping |
| Sabha and Samiti | Tribal assemblies in Rig Vedic period | Said to be "royal courts of Later Vedic kings" | Period confusion |
| Painted Grey Ware (PGW) | Associated with Later Vedic period | Linked with Harappans | Culture-period mismatch |
| 16 Mahajanapadas | Listed in Anguttara Nikaya (Buddhist text) | Said to be listed in Puranas | Source confusion |
| Magadha's first capital | Rajagriha (Girivraja) | Given as Pataliputra (later capital, founded by Udayin) | Chronology confusion |
| Bimbisara | Founder of Haryanka dynasty; contemporary of Buddha | Confused with Ajatashatru (his son) | Similar names |
| Alexander's invasion | 326 BCE; Battle of Hydaspes (Jhelum) vs Porus | Given as 323 BCE or battle on Indus | Date/place confusion |
| Buddha's birth | 563 BCE, Lumbini (Nepal); clan: Shakya; mother: Mahamaya | Birthplace given as Kapilavastu (that was his home) | Place confusion |
| Buddha's enlightenment | Bodh Gaya, under Peepal (Bodhi) tree, on river Niranjana | Tree given as Banyan | Near-correct statement |
| First sermon | Sarnath (Dharmachakra Pravartana) | Given as Bodh Gaya | Event-place mismatch |
| Buddha's death (Mahaparinirvana) | 483 BCE, Kushinagar | Given as Sarnath or Bodh Gaya | Place confusion |
| First Buddhist Council | Rajagriha, 483 BCE, under Ajatashatru; presided by Mahakassapa | King given as Ashoka | Chronology confusion |
| Second Buddhist Council | Vaishali, 383 BCE, under Kalasoka | Confused with First Council venue | Venue swapping |
| Fourth Buddhist Council | Kundalvana (Kashmir), under Kanishka; split into Hinayana–Mahayana | Attributed to Ashoka (he held the Third, at Pataliputra) | King-council mismatch |
| Mahavira | 24th Tirthankara; born 540 BCE, Kundagrama (Vaishali); died 468 BCE, Pavapuri | Called the "founder" of Jainism (Parshvanatha, 23rd, preceded him) | Near-correct statement |
| First Tirthankara | Rishabhdev (Adinath) | Given as Mahavira | Sequence confusion |
| Jainism's triratna | Right faith, right knowledge, right conduct | Swapped with Buddha's Four Noble Truths | Doctrine mixing |
| Chandragupta Maurya | Founded Mauryan Empire c. 322 BCE with Chanakya (Kautilya); defeated Seleucus Nicator (305 BCE) | Defeat of Seleucus attributed to Ashoka | Person confusion |
| Megasthenes | Greek ambassador of Seleucus at Chandragupta's court; wrote Indica | Book given as "Indika of Arrian" or placed in Ashoka's court | Source/period confusion |
| Ashoka's Kalinga War | 261 BCE, 8th regnal year; described in Rock Edict XIII | Edict number given as XII or war dated 260 BCE | Number confusion |
| Ashoka's inscriptions deciphered by | James Prinsep (1837) | Attributed to William Jones or Max Müller | Similar names |
| Ashoka's script | Brahmi (mostly); Kharoshthi in north-west; Greek/Aramaic in Afghanistan | "All edicts in Sanskrit" — false; language is Prakrit | Language confusion |
| Lion Capital | Sarnath; adopted as National Emblem (26 January 1950) | Given as Sanchi | Place confusion |
| Pushyamitra Shunga | Founder of Shunga dynasty; killed last Mauryan king Brihadratha | Called a Mauryan general who "revolted against Ashoka" | Chronology confusion |
| Kanishka | Kushana ruler; started Saka Era (78 CE); capital Purushapura (Peshawar) | Saka Era attributed to Shalivahana or Vikramaditya | Era confusion |
| Vikram Samvat | Started 57 BCE, associated with Vikramaditya of Ujjain | Confused with Saka Era (78 CE) | Era confusion |
| Gandhara art | Greco-Buddhist; first Buddha images; blue-grey schist stone | Confused with Mathura art (red sandstone, indigenous) | Style confusion |
| Gautamiputra Satakarni | Greatest Satavahana ruler; called "destroyer of Shakas" | Attributed to Simuka (dynasty founder) | Person confusion |
| Sangam literature | Tamil; three Sangams; Tolkappiyam = earliest Tamil grammar | Tolkappiyam called an "epic" (epics are Silappadikaram, Manimekalai) | Text-type confusion |
| Silappadikaram | By Ilango Adigal; story of Kovalan–Kannagi | Author given as Sattanar (he wrote Manimekalai) | Author swapping |
| Gupta era start | 319–320 CE, by Chandragupta I | Given as 320 BCE or linked to Samudragupta | Date confusion |
| Samudragupta | "Napoleon of India" (V.A. Smith); Allahabad Pillar Inscription by Harisena | Title called a compliment — Smith's label is debated; inscription author asked | Near-correct statement |
| Chandragupta II (Vikramaditya) | Defeated Shakas; Fa-hien visited his court | Fa-hien placed in Harsha's court (that was Hiuen Tsang) | Traveller-king mismatch |
| Fa-hien (Faxian) | Chinese pilgrim, visited during Chandragupta II (c. 399–414 CE) | Confused with Hiuen Tsang (Harsha's time, 7th century) | Traveller confusion |
| Kalidasa | Court poet of Chandragupta II; wrote Abhijnanashakuntalam, Meghaduta, Raghuvamsha | Placed in Harsha's court (Harsha's poets: Banabhatta) | Court confusion |
| Aryabhata | Wrote Aryabhatiya (499 CE); concept of zero's use, earth's rotation | "Invented zero" — he used place-value; zero as number credited to Brahmagupta's rules | Near-correct statement |
| Iron Pillar (Mehrauli) | Chandra (usually identified as Chandragupta II); rust-resistant | Attributed to Ashoka | Person confusion |
| Ajanta paintings | Mostly Buddhist themes; Vakataka patronage (Harishena) | Called "Gupta-period Hindu paintings" | Theme/period confusion |
| Harshavardhana | Capital Kannauj; wrote Ratnavali, Nagananda, Priyadarshika; patron of Banabhatta (Harshacharita) | His plays attributed to Kalidasa | Author confusion |
| Hiuen Tsang | Visited India during Harsha's reign (c. 629–645 CE); called "Prince of Pilgrims" | Title given to Fa-hien | Title swapping |
| Pulakeshin II | Chalukya king who defeated Harsha on Narmada; Aihole inscription by Ravikirti | Defeat of Harsha attributed to a Pallava king | Person confusion |
| Nalanda University | Flourished under Guptas/Harsha; Kumaragupta I traditionally credited as founder | Called a "Buddhist monastery only" — it was a full university | Near-correct statement |

## Part 2: Time Management (15/45-Second Rule)

| Topic | Question Type | Time Rule | Score Maximizer? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Harappan sites & findings | Fact-based (site ↔ discovery match) | 15 sec | ✅ Yes |
| Vedic literature (Vedas, Upanishads) | Fact-based | 15 sec | ✅ Yes |
| Buddhism/Jainism events & councils | Fact-based (place, king, year) | 15 sec | ✅ Yes |
| Mahajanapadas & Magadha rulers | Fact-based | 15 sec | ✅ Yes |
| Mauryan administration & edicts | Fact-based + edict numbers | 15 sec | ✅ Yes |
| Foreign travellers (Megasthenes, Fa-hien, Hiuen Tsang) | Fact-based (traveller ↔ ruler match) | 15 sec | ✅ Yes |
| Gupta art, literature, science | Fact-based (author ↔ work match) | 15 sec | ✅ Yes |
| Chronology ordering (dynasties, councils, battles) | Analytical (sequence logic) | 45 sec | ❌ No |
| Statement-based questions (I, II, III correct?) | Analytical (each statement verified) | 45 sec | ❌ No |
| Harappan decline theories / Aryan debates | Analytical (elimination needed) | 45 sec | ❌ No |
| Art-style comparison (Gandhara vs Mathura) | Analytical (feature matching) | 45 sec | ❌ No |
| Inscription ↔ king ↔ language questions | Analytical (two-step recall) | 45 sec | ❌ No |

**Strategy:** Attempt all 15-sec fact questions first — they are pure recall and carry the same marks as 45-sec analytical ones. Mark analytical questions for the second pass.

## Part 3: Data & Formulas

| Category | Data |
|---|---|
| Key dates (BCE) | Harappa excavated 1921 CE; Mohenjo-daro 1922 CE; Alexander's invasion 326; Buddha's birth 563, enlightenment 528, death 483; Mahavira's birth 540, death 468; Mauryan Empire founded c. 322; Kalinga War 261 |
| Key dates (CE) | Saka Era 78; Gupta Era 319–320; Aryabhatiya 499; Fa-hien's visit c. 399–414; Hiuen Tsang's visit c. 629–645 |
| Era starting points | Vikram Samvat 57 BCE; Saka Samvat 78 CE; Gupta Samvat 319–320 CE |
| Rig Veda numbers | 10 Mandalas; 1028 hymns (Suktas); oldest Mandalas: II–VII (family books); 10th Mandala has Purusha Sukta |
| Buddhist Councils | 1st: Rajagriha, 483 BCE, Ajatashatru; 2nd: Vaishali, 383 BCE, Kalasoka; 3rd: Pataliputra, c. 250 BCE, Ashoka; 4th: Kundalvana (Kashmir), 1st–2nd c. CE, Kanishka |
| Jain Tirthankaras | Total 24; 1st = Rishabhdev; 23rd = Parshvanatha; 24th = Mahavira |
| Mahajanapadas | 16 (per Anguttara Nikaya); most powerful: Magadha, Kosala, Vatsa, Avanti |
| Ashoka's Major Rock Edicts | 14; Kalinga War in Edict XIII; Edict XII = religious tolerance |
| Three Sangams | Legendary Tamil literary academies under Pandyan patronage at Madurai (and earlier Kapatapuram) |
| No formulas | Ancient Indian History has no mathematical formulas — only dates, numbers of texts/edicts, and era conversions (Vikram Samvat − 57 = CE approx.; Saka Samvat + 78 = CE) |
