51. The Harappan site of Dholavira, known for its unique water management system and three-part city division, is located in which present-day state?
(a) Rajasthan (b) Gujarat (c) Haryana (d) Punjab
Answer: (b)
Solution: Dholavira is located on Khadir Bet island in the Rann of Kutch, Gujarat. It is famous for its reservoirs and a city divided into three parts — citadel, middle town and lower town. Rajasthan has Kalibangan, which often confuses students.
Source: NCERT Class 12 (Themes in Indian History Part I)

52. Which of the following Harappan sites is known as the "Mound of the Dead"?
(a) Mohenjo-daro (b) Lothal (c) Harappa (d) Chanhudaro
Answer: (a)
Solution: Mohenjo-daro in Sindh (Pakistan) literally means "Mound of the Dead" in Sindhi. It was excavated by R.D. Banerji in 1922. Harappa was excavated by Daya Ram Sahni in 1921 — a classic similar-names trap.
Source: NCERT Class 12 (Themes in Indian History Part I)

53. The Great Bath of the Indus Valley Civilization was found at:
(a) Mohenjo-daro (b) Harappa (c) Lothal (d) Banawali
Answer: (a)
Solution: The Great Bath, a large rectangular tank probably used for ritual bathing, was discovered at Mohenjo-daro. Lothal is famous for its dockyard, not the Great Bath.
Source: NCERT Class 12 (Themes in Indian History Part I)

54. Which Veda is a collection of melodies and chants, considered the origin of Indian music?
(a) Sama Veda (b) Rig Veda (c) Yajur Veda (d) Atharva Veda
Answer: (a)
Solution: The Sama Veda consists of hymns set to musical tunes and is regarded as the root of Indian classical music. The Atharva Veda deals with charms, spells and medicine — a frequent confusion.
Source: NCERT Class 11 (Themes in World History / Old NCERT Ancient India)

55. The famous Gayatri Mantra is found in which Veda?
(a) Atharva Veda (b) Yajur Veda (c) Sama Veda (d) Rig Veda
Answer: (d)
Solution: The Gayatri Mantra, addressed to the solar deity Savitri, appears in the third mandala of the Rig Veda. The Rig Veda is the oldest of the four Vedas.
Source: NCERT Old Ancient India (R.S. Sharma)

56. The term "Arya" in the Rig Vedic context primarily referred to:
(a) A racial group (b) A military class (c) A cultural and linguistic community (d) A priestly order
Answer: (c)
Solution: Modern scholarship holds that "Arya" denoted a cultural-linguistic community, not a race. The racial interpretation was a later colonial construct — a common examiner's trap with the "almost correct" racial option.
Source: NCERT Class 12 (Themes in Indian History Part I)

57. The Battle of the Ten Kings (Dasharajna) was fought on the banks of which river?
(a) Saraswati (b) Yamuna (c) Parushni (d) Vitasta
Answer: (c)
Solution: The Dasharajna battle was fought on the river Parushni, the Vedic name of the Ravi. King Sudas of the Bharata tribe defeated a confederacy of ten kings. Vitasta is the Jhelum — a similar-names trap.
Source: NCERT Old Ancient India (R.S. Sharma)

58. Which of the following was the first Magadhan ruler to be called a "universal monarch" and is credited with building the capital at Rajagriha's fortification?
(a) Ajatashatru (b) Bimbisara (c) Mahapadma Nanda (d) Shishunaga
Answer: (b)
Solution: Bimbisara of the Haryanka dynasty strengthened Magadha and developed Rajagriha (Rajgir) as the capital. His son Ajatashatru later shifted focus to Pataliputra's fortification — a chronological confusion trap.
Source: NCERT Old Ancient India (R.S. Sharma)

59. The first Buddhist Council was held at Rajagriha during the reign of:
(a) Bimbisara (b) Kalasoka (c) Ajatashatru (d) Ashoka
Answer: (c)
Solution: The First Buddhist Council (c. 483 BCE) was held at Rajagriha under Ajatashatru's patronage, soon after Buddha's death. The Second Council at Vaishali under Kalasoka is a frequent mix-up.
Source: NCERT Old Ancient India (R.S. Sharma)

60. Consider the following statements about Jainism:
P. Mahavira was the 24th and last Tirthankara.
Q. Parshvanatha, the 23rd Tirthankara, was a historical figure from Varanasi.
R. The first Tirthankara was Rishabhanatha.
Which of the above are correct?
(a) P and Q only (b) Q and R only (c) P, Q and R (d) P and R only
Answer: (c)
Solution: All three statements are correct. Rishabhanatha (Adinatha) was the first Tirthankara, Parshvanatha the 23rd, and Vardhamana Mahavira the 24th. Examiners often swap Parshvanatha's position to trap candidates.
Source: NCERT Class 12 (Themes in Indian History Part I)

61. Buddha delivered his first sermon, known as the "Dharmachakra Pravartana", at:
(a) Bodh Gaya (b) Kushinagar (c) Sarnath (d) Lumbini
Answer: (c)
Solution: The first sermon was delivered at Sarnath near Varanasi. Bodh Gaya is where he attained enlightenment, Kushinagar where he attained Mahaparinirvana, and Lumbini his birthplace — a classic site-confusion trap.
Source: NCERT Class 12 (Themes in Indian History Part I)

62. The Kalinga War, which transformed Ashoka's policy to Dhamma, was fought in which regnal year of Ashoka?
(a) 4th year (b) 8th year (c) 12th year (d) 26th year
Answer: (b)
Solution: According to Rock Edict XIII, the Kalinga War took place in the 8th regnal year of Ashoka (c. 261 BCE). The massive loss of life led him to renounce war and adopt Dhamma.
Source: NCERT Class 12 (Themes in Indian History Part I)

63. Ashoka's inscriptions were first deciphered by:
(a) James Prinsep (b) William Jones (c) Alexander Cunningham (d) John Marshall
Answer: (a)
Solution: James Prinsep deciphered the Brahmi script of Ashokan edicts in 1837. Alexander Cunningham is called the father of Indian archaeology — a similar-personalities trap.
Source: NCERT Class 12 (Themes in Indian History Part I)

64. Which Ashokan edict gives a vivid account of the Kalinga War and Ashoka's remorse?
(a) Rock Edict XIII (b) Rock Edict XII (c) Rock Edict X (d) Pillar Edict VII
Answer: (a)
Solution: Major Rock Edict XIII describes the Kalinga War, the death and deportation of people, and Ashoka's subsequent remorse and turn to Dhamma. Rock Edict XII deals with religious tolerance.
Source: NCERT Class 12 (Themes in Indian History Part I)

65. The Arthashastra, a treatise on statecraft, is traditionally attributed to:
(a) Megasthenes (b) Kalhana (c) Banabhatta (d) Kautilya
Answer: (d)
Solution: The Arthashastra is attributed to Kautilya (Chanakya/Vishnugupta), chief minister of Chandragupta Maurya. Megasthenes wrote Indica — a frequently confused pair.
Source: NCERT Old Ancient India (R.S. Sharma)

66. Megasthenes, the author of Indica, was an ambassador in the court of:
(a) Ashoka (b) Chandragupta Maurya (c) Bindusara (d) Pushyamitra Shunga
Answer: (b)
Solution: Megasthenes was the ambassador of Seleucus Nicator to the court of Chandragupta Maurya at Pataliputra. His work Indica survives only in fragments quoted by later writers.
Source: NCERT Class 12 (Themes in Indian History Part I)

67. Which dynasty immediately succeeded the Mauryas in Magadha?
(a) Shunga (b) Kanva (c) Satavahana (d) Gupta
Answer: (a)
Solution: The last Mauryan ruler Brihadratha was assassinated by his commander Pushyamitra Shunga (c. 185 BCE), who founded the Shunga dynasty. The Kanvas succeeded the Shungas — a chronological trap.
Source: NCERT Old Ancient India (R.S. Sharma)

68. The Satavahanas had their earliest capital at:
(a) Pratishthana (b) Amaravati (c) Nagarjunakonda (d) Kanchipuram
Answer: (a)
Solution: Pratishthana (modern Paithan in Maharashtra) on the Godavari was the early Satavahana capital. Gautamiputra Satakarni was their greatest ruler. Amaravati is famous for its stupa, not as the earliest capital.
Source: NCERT Old Ancient India (R.S. Sharma)

69. The Gandhara school of art is characterised by:
(a) Purely indigenous Indian style (b) Persian miniature techniques (c) Dravidian architectural features (d) Greco-Roman influence on Buddhist themes
Answer: (d)
Solution: Gandhara art blended Greek and Roman artistic techniques with Buddhist subject matter, flourishing under the Kushanas, especially Kanishka. The Mathura school, by contrast, was indigenous — a standard contrast question.
Source: NCERT Class 11 (An Introduction to Indian Art)

70. Kanishka is associated with which Buddhist Council?
(a) First (b) Second (c) Fourth (d) Third
Answer: (c)
Solution: The Fourth Buddhist Council was held in Kashmir (Kundalavana) under Kanishka's patronage, where Buddhism split clearly into Hinayana and Mahayana. The Third Council was held at Pataliputra under Ashoka.
Source: NCERT Old Ancient India (R.S. Sharma)

71. The Gupta ruler who assumed the title "Vikramaditya" after defeating the Shakas was:
(a) Chandragupta I (b) Samudragupta (c) Kumaragupta (d) Chandragupta II
Answer: (d)
Solution: Chandragupta II defeated the Shaka (Western Kshatrapa) rulers and took the title Vikramaditya. Samudragupta is known for the Allahabad Pillar Inscription by Harisena — a similar-names trap between father and son.
Source: NCERT Old Ancient India (R.S. Sharma)

72. The Allahabad Pillar Inscription (Prayaga Prashasti), which details the conquests of Samudragupta, was composed by:
(a) Kalidasa (b) Vatsyayana (c) Banabhatta (d) Harisena
Answer: (d)
Solution: Harisena, the court poet of Samudragupta, composed the Prayaga Prashasti. Banabhatta wrote the Harshacharita for Harshavardhana — a classic court-poet confusion.
Source: NCERT Class 12 (Themes in Indian History Part I)

73. The Iron Pillar at Mehrauli, which has not rusted for over 1500 years, is associated with which ruler?
(a) Ashoka (b) Chandragupta II (c) Harshavardhana (d) Samudragupta
Answer: (b)
Solution: The Mehrauli Iron Pillar inscription refers to a ruler named "Chandra", identified with Chandragupta II of the Gupta dynasty. It demonstrates the advanced metallurgical skill of the Gupta period.
Source: NCERT Class 12 (Themes in Indian History Part I)

74. Hiuen Tsang, the Chinese pilgrim, visited India during the reign of:
(a) Chandragupta II (b) Harshavardhana (c) Pulakeshin II (d) Ashoka
Answer: (b)
Solution: Hiuen Tsang (Xuanzang) visited India during Harshavardhana's reign (c. 630–644 CE) and wrote Si-Yu-Ki. Fa-Hien came during Chandragupta II's time — the most common chronological confusion in this topic.
Source: NCERT Old Ancient India (R.S. Sharma)

75. Which Chola ruler built the Brihadeshwara (Rajarajeshwara) Temple at Thanjavur?
(a) Rajendra I (b) Vijayalaya (c) Kulottunga I (d) Rajaraja I
Answer: (d)
Solution: The Brihadeshwara Temple at Thanjavur was completed by Rajaraja I in 1010 CE. His son Rajendra I built a similar temple at Gangaikondacholapuram — a father-son confusion trap frequently used by examiners.
Source: NCERT Class 11 (An Introduction to Indian Art)
