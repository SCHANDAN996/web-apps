Questions 76–100: Medium

76. The famous rock-cut Kailasa temple at Ellora was built during the reign of which dynasty?
(a) Chalukyas of Badami (b) Cholas (c) Pallavas (d) Rashtrakutas
Answer: (d)
Solution: The Kailasa temple at Ellora was carved out under the Rashtrakuta king Krishna I in the 8th century CE. The Chalukyas of Badami built rock-cut caves at Badami and Aihole, not the Kailasa temple — a common confusion.
Source: NCERT Class 11 (Themes in Indian History / An Introduction to Indian Art)

77. Which of the following pairs is correctly matched?
(a) Harshacharita — written by Vishakhadatta (b) Arthashastra — written by Banabhatta (c) Mudrarakshasa — written by Kalidasa (d) Milindapanho — dialogue between Menander and Nagasena
Answer: (d)
Solution: Milindapanho records the dialogue between Indo-Greek king Menander (Milinda) and the Buddhist monk Nagasena. Arthashastra is attributed to Kautilya, Mudrarakshasa to Vishakhadatta, and Harshacharita to Banabhatta — the other pairs are deliberately swapped.
Source: NCERT Class 12 (Themes in Indian History, Part I)

78. The Gandhara school of art is distinguished by which feature?
(a) Exclusive use of spotted red sandstone (b) Complete absence of Buddha images (c) Blend of Greek and Indian styles with Buddha shown in Greco-Roman drapery (d) Use of only black stone from Bihar
Answer: (c)
Solution: Gandhara art shows strong Hellenistic (Greek) influence — wavy hair, muscular bodies, toga-like drapery — and used bluish-grey schist stone. The Mathura school used spotted red sandstone, which is the classic trap here.
Source: NCERT Class 11 (An Introduction to Indian Art)

79. Which Gupta ruler is shown playing the veena on his coins?
(a) Samudragupta (b) Chandragupta I (c) Chandragupta II (d) Kumaragupta I
Answer: (a)
Solution: Samudragupta issued coins depicting him playing the veena, reflecting his patronage of music. The Allahabad Pillar Inscription of Harisena also praises his accomplishments.
Source: NCERT Class 12 (Themes in Indian History, Part I)

80. Consider the following events: 1. Accession of Ashoka, 2. Alexander's invasion of India, 3. Accession of Chandragupta Maurya, 4. Kalinga War. Which is the correct chronological order?
(a) 3, 2, 1, 4 (b) 2, 3, 1, 4 (c) 2, 1, 3, 4 (d) 3, 1, 2, 4
Answer: (b)
Solution: Alexander invaded around 326 BCE; Chandragupta Maurya founded the empire around 322 BCE; Ashoka ascended the throne around 268 BCE; the Kalinga War followed around 261 BCE. The trap is placing Chandragupta before Alexander.
Source: NCERT Class 12 (Themes in Indian History, Part I)

81. The term 'Sangam' in ancient Tamil history refers to:
(a) An assembly of Tamil poets and scholars (b) A religious sacrifice (c) A type of coinage (d) A military confederacy
Answer: (a)
Solution: Sangam refers to assemblies of Tamil poets held under Pandyan patronage at Madurai, whose compositions form the Sangam literature — our main source for early South Indian history.
Source: NCERT Class 12 (Themes in Indian History, Part I)

82. Which of the following statements about the Indus Valley Civilization is NOT correct?
(a) The Great Bath was found at Mohenjo-daro (b) Iron was commonly used for tools (c) Lothal had a dockyard (d) The script of the civilization remains undeciphered
Answer: (b)
Solution: The Harappans were a Bronze Age people; they did not know iron, which came into use in India much later (later Vedic period). The other statements are correct — this is a negative-statement trap.
Source: NCERT Class 12 (Themes in Indian History, Part I)

83. The Fourth Buddhist Council, which split Buddhism into Hinayana and Mahayana, was held under whose patronage?
(a) Ashoka (b) Harshavardhana (c) Kanishka (d) Ajatashatru
Answer: (c)
Solution: The Fourth Buddhist Council was held at Kundalvana (Kashmir) under Kanishka, where the Hinayana–Mahayana division crystallised. Ashoka patronised the Third Council at Pataliputra — a frequent confusion.
Source: NCERT Class 12 (Themes in Indian History, Part I)

84. The Prayaga Prashasti (Allahabad Pillar Inscription) was composed by:
(a) Harisena (b) Kalidasa (c) Banabhatta (d) Ravikirti
Answer: (a)
Solution: Harisena, the court poet of Samudragupta, composed the Prayaga Prashasti. Ravikirti composed the Aihole inscription of Pulakeshin II — the similar-sounding name is the examiner's trap.
Source: NCERT Class 12 (Themes in Indian History, Part I)

85. Which Chola ruler built the Brihadeshwara temple at Thanjavur?
(a) Rajendra I (b) Rajaraja I (c) Kulottunga I (d) Vijayalaya
Answer: (b)
Solution: Rajaraja I built the Brihadeshwara (Rajarajeshwara) temple at Thanjavur around 1010 CE. His son Rajendra I founded Gangaikondacholapuram and built a similar temple there — a classic father-son confusion.
Source: NCERT Class 7 (Our Pasts II)

86. The doctrine of 'Syadvada' is associated with which religion?
(a) Buddhism (b) Ajivikas (c) Jainism (d) Charvaka
Answer: (c)
Solution: Syadvada, the doctrine of 'maybe' or conditional predication, is a Jaina philosophical concept reflecting the idea that truth has many aspects (anekantavada).
Source: NCERT Class 12 (Themes in Indian History, Part I)

87. Which of the following Harappan sites is located in present-day Afghanistan?
(a) Shortughai (b) Dholavira (c) Kalibangan (d) Banawali
Answer: (a)
Solution: Shortughai, on the Oxus (Amu Darya) in Afghanistan, was a Harappan trading outpost, probably for lapis lazuli. Dholavira is in Gujarat, Kalibangan in Rajasthan, and Banawali in Haryana.
Source: NCERT Class 12 (Themes in Indian History, Part I)

88. The title 'Vikramaditya' was assumed by Chandragupta II after defeating which people?
(a) The Hunas (b) The Pallavas (c) The Kushanas (d) The Shakas
Answer: (d)
Solution: Chandragupta II defeated the Shaka (Western Kshatrapa) rulers of western India and took the title Vikramaditya. The Hunas invaded later, during the reign of Skandagupta — a chronological trap.
Source: NCERT Class 12 (Themes in Indian History, Part I)

89. Match the travellers with the rulers they visited: 1. Megasthenes, 2. Fa-Hien, 3. Hiuen Tsang. Rulers: P. Chandragupta Maurya, Q. Chandragupta II, R. Harshavardhana.
(a) 1-R, 2-Q, 3-P (b) 1-Q, 2-P, 3-R (c) 1-P, 2-R, 3-Q (d) 1-P, 2-Q, 3-R
Answer: (d)
Solution: Megasthenes (Indica) visited Chandragupta Maurya's court; Fa-Hien came during Chandragupta II's reign; Hiuen Tsang visited during Harshavardhana's reign. The two Chandraguptas are the deliberate confusion.
Source: NCERT Class 12 (Themes in Indian History, Part I)

90. The 'Tripitaka' texts are associated with which religion?
(a) Buddhism (b) Jainism (c) Hinduism (d) Zoroastrianism
Answer: (a)
Solution: The Tripitakas — Vinaya, Sutta and Abhidhamma Pitakas — are the canonical Buddhist texts written in Pali. Jaina canonical literature is called the Agamas.
Source: NCERT Class 12 (Themes in Indian History, Part I)

91. Which of the following statements about Ashoka's Dhamma is correct?
(a) It was a new religion founded by Ashoka (b) It was preached only in Pali (c) It banned all religious practices except Buddhism (d) It was a code of moral and social conduct emphasising tolerance and non-violence
Answer: (d)
Solution: Dhamma was not a new religion but an ethical code stressing non-violence, respect for elders, and religious tolerance. Statements claiming it was a new religion or that it banned other faiths are almost-correct traps.
Source: NCERT Class 12 (Themes in Indian History, Part I)

92. The earliest evidence of settled agriculture and domestication of animals in the Indian subcontinent is found at:
(a) Bhimbetka (b) Mohenjo-daro (c) Mehrgarh (d) Hastinapura
Answer: (c)
Solution: Mehrgarh (Balochistan) shows evidence of early farming and animal domestication from around 7000 BCE, predating the Harappan cities. Bhimbetka is famous for Mesolithic rock paintings, not farming.
Source: NCERT Class 12 (Themes in Indian History, Part I)

93. Which ruler performed the Ashvamedha sacrifice and issued gold coins commemorating it, though he was a Gupta and not a Maurya?
(a) Both Samudragupta and Kumaragupta I (b) Bindusara (c) Kumaragupta I (d) Samudragupta
Answer: (a)
Solution: Both Samudragupta and Kumaragupta I performed the Ashvamedha and issued commemorative gold coins showing the sacrificial horse. The question guards against the trap of assuming only one Gupta did so.
Source: NCERT Class 12 (Themes in Indian History, Part I)

94. The Aihole inscription, which mentions the defeat of Harshavardhana, was composed in praise of which ruler?
(a) Narasimhavarman I (b) Pulakeshin II (c) Mahendravarman I (d) Vikramaditya I
Answer: (b)
Solution: The Aihole inscription of Ravikirti praises the Chalukya king Pulakeshin II, who defeated Harshavardhana on the banks of the Narmada. Narasimhavarman I was the Pallava who later defeated Pulakeshin II — a reversal trap.
Source: NCERT Class 12 (Themes in Indian History, Part I)

95. In the Mauryan administration, the officer in charge of the royal harem and city administration was called:
(a) Samaharta (b) Sannidhata (c) Nagaraka (d) Sitadhyaksha
Answer: (c)
Solution: The Nagaraka was the city superintendent in Mauryan administration. Samaharta was the chief revenue collector, Sannidhata the treasurer, and Sitadhyaksha the superintendent of agriculture.
Source: NCERT Class 12 (Themes in Indian History, Part I)

96. Which of the following is the correct chronological order of these foreign powers entering India?
(a) Kushanas, Greeks, Shakas, Hunas (b) Shakas, Greeks, Kushanas, Hunas (c) Greeks, Kushanas, Shakas, Hunas (d) Greeks, Shakas, Kushanas, Hunas
Answer: (d)
Solution: The Indo-Greeks came first (early 2nd century BCE), followed by the Shakas, then the Kushanas (1st century CE), and finally the Hunas (5th–6th century CE). Shaka-before-Greek is the common chronological confusion.
Source: NCERT Class 12 (Themes in Indian History, Part I)

97. The concept of 'Bodhisattva' is central to which branch of Buddhism?
(a) Mahayana (b) Hinayana (c) Vajrayana only (d) Theravada
Answer: (a)
Solution: Mahayana Buddhism developed the ideal of the Bodhisattva — one who delays personal nirvana to help all beings attain salvation. Hinayana/Theravada emphasises individual liberation (arhat ideal).
Source: NCERT Class 12 (Themes in Indian History, Part I)

98. The iron pillar at Mehrauli, famous for its rust-resistant quality, is associated with which period?
(a) Mauryan period (b) Gupta period (c) Kushana period (d) Vardhana period
Answer: (b)
Solution: The Mehrauli iron pillar belongs to the Gupta period (associated with a ruler named Chandra, usually identified with Chandragupta II) and testifies to advanced Gupta metallurgy. The Mauryan attribution is a common trap.
Source: NCERT Class 12 (Themes in Indian History, Part I)

99. Which of the following pairs of ancient texts and their languages is correctly matched?
(a) Tripitaka — Sanskrit (b) Sangam literature — Sanskrit (c) Jaina Agamas — Prakrit (d) Arthashastra — Pali
Answer: (c)
Solution: Jaina Agamas were composed in Prakrit (Ardhamagadhi). The Tripitakas are in Pali, Sangam literature in Tamil, and the Arthashastra in Sanskrit — the other pairs deliberately swap the languages.
Source: NCERT Class 12 (Themes in Indian History, Part I)

100. The Uttaramerur inscription, which describes village self-government and election procedures, belongs to which dynasty?
(a) Pallavas (b) Cholas (c) Pandyas (d) Cheras
Answer: (b)
Solution: The Uttaramerur inscriptions of the Chola period (Parantaka I) detail the functioning of village assemblies (sabha and ur) and the pot-ticket (kudavolai) system of selecting committee members.
Source: NCERT Class 7 (Our Pasts II)
