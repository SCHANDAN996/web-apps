126. Which of the following pairs of Ashokan inscriptions and their locations is correctly matched?
(a) Sopara — Uttar Pradesh (b) Kalsi — Gujarat (c) Rummindei — birthplace of Buddha, Nepal (d) Maski — Tamil Nadu
Answer: (c)
Solution: The Rummindei (Lumbini) pillar inscription in Nepal records Ashoka's visit to Buddha's birthplace and tax reduction for the village. Kalsi is in Uttarakhand, Sopara in Maharashtra, and Maski in Karnataka — so the other pairs are wrong.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Themes in Indian History Part I

127. Consider the following statements about the Gandhara and Mathura schools of art:
1. Gandhara art shows strong Greco-Roman influence.
2. Mathura art used spotted red sandstone.
3. Gandhara art produced the earliest image of Buddha, while Mathura never depicted Buddha.
Which of the statements given above is/are correct?
(a) 1 and 2 only (b) 2 and 3 only (c) 1 and 3 only (d) 1, 2 and 3
Answer: (a)
Solution: Statements 1 and 2 are correct. Statement 3 is the trap: the Mathura school also produced Buddha images (in red sandstone) independently; it is false that Mathura never depicted Buddha.
Source: NCERT Class 11 An Introduction to Indian Art

128. The Prayaga Prashasti (Allahabad Pillar Inscription), which describes the conquests of Samudragupta, was composed by:
(a) Kalidasa (b) Ravikirti (c) Banabhatta (d) Harisena
Answer: (d)
Solution: Harisena, Samudragupta's court poet, composed the Prayaga Prashasti. Banabhatta wrote Harshacharita for Harsha, and Ravikirti composed the Aihole inscription of Pulakeshin II — a classic similar-names trap.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Themes in Indian History Part I

129. Arrange the following rulers in correct chronological order:
1. Chandragupta Maurya
2. Ashoka
3. Bindusara
4. Brihadratha
(a) 1-3-2-4 (b) 1-2-3-4 (c) 3-1-2-4 (d) 1-3-4-2
Answer: (a)
Solution: Chandragupta Maurya → his son Bindusara → his son Ashoka → Brihadratha, the last Mauryan ruler killed by Pushyamitra Shunga. Option with Ashoka before Bindusara is the chronological-confusion trap.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Themes in Indian History Part I

130. Which of the following statements about the Indus Valley Civilization is NOT correct?
(a) The Great Bath was found at Mohenjo-daro (b) Lothal had a dockyard (c) Iron was commonly used for tools (d) Dholavira was divided into three parts
Answer: (c)
Solution: The Harappans knew copper and bronze but not iron; iron came into use in the later Vedic period. The other statements are correct — the "almost correct" options make this a trap question.

131. The Aihole inscription, which mentions the defeat of Harshavardhana by Pulakeshin II, was composed by:
(a) Harisena (b) Ravikirti (c) Banabhatta (d) Dandin

Solution: Ravikirti, court poet of the Chalukya king Pulakeshin II, composed the Aihole prashasti. Harisena belongs to Samudragupta's court — a frequent similar-names confusion.

133. Which Gupta ruler assumed the title 'Vikramaditya' after defeating the Shakas?
(a) Samudragupta (b) Chandragupta II (c) Chandragupta I (d) Kumaragupta I
Answer: (b)
Solution: Chandragupta II defeated the Shaka rulers of western India and took the title Vikramaditya. Samudragupta is called the 'Napoleon of India' — do not confuse the two.

134. Match the following travellers with the rulers they visited:
1. Megasthenes — P. Chandragupta Maurya
2. Fa-Hien — Q. Chandragupta II
3. Hiuen Tsang — R. Harshavardhana
(a) 1-Q, 2-P, 3-R (b) 1-P, 2-Q, 3-R (c) 1-P, 2-R, 3-Q (d) 1-R, 2-Q, 3-P

Solution: Megasthenes (Indica) visited Chandragupta Maurya's court; Fa-Hien came during Chandragupta II's reign; Hiuen Tsang visited Harsha's court. The Fa-Hien–Hiuen Tsang swap is a classic trap.

135. Which of the following statements about Jainism is correct?
(a) Rishabhanatha is regarded as the first Tirthankara (b) Mahavira was the founder and first Tirthankara (c) Jainism rejects the doctrine of ahimsa (d) Mahavira was born at Bodh Gaya
Answer: (a)
Solution: Rishabhanatha (Adinatha) is the first Tirthankara; Mahavira was the 24th and last. Mahavira was born at Kundagrama near Vaishali, not Bodh Gaya — a birthplace-confusion trap with Buddha.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Themes in Indian History Part I

136. The concept of 'welfare state' in ancient India is best reflected in which Ashokan edict feature?
(a) The Schism Edict (b) The Kalinga war description (c) Appointment of Dhamma Mahamatras (d) The Bhabru Edict
Answer: (c)
Solution: Dhamma Mahamatras were officers appointed by Ashoka to propagate dhamma and look after the welfare of subjects, reflecting welfare-state ideas. The Kalinga war (Major Rock Edict XIII) shows his remorse, not administration.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Themes in Indian History Part I

138. The 'Milindapanho', a Buddhist text, records a dialogue between the Indo-Greek king Menander and which Buddhist monk?
(a) Nagarjuna (b) Vasubandhu (c) Asanga (d) Nagasena
Answer: (d)

139. Which of the following statements about the Fourth Buddhist Council is correct?
(a) It was held at Rajagriha under Ajatashatru (b) It was held at Kundalavana (Kashmir) under Kanishka (c) It resulted in the first split into Sthaviravadins and Mahasanghikas (d) It was presided over by Upali
Answer: (b)
Solution: The Fourth Council was held at Kundalavana in Kashmir under Kanishka, presided over by Vasumitra, and it formalised the Hinayana–Mahayana split. The first council was at Rajagriha; the Sthaviravadin–Mahasanghika split occurred at the second council — a chronological trap.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Themes in Indian History Part I

140. The iron pillar at Mehrauli, famous for its rust-resistant quality, is associated with which ruler?
(a) Ashoka (b) Harshavardhana (c) Chandragupta II (d) Samudragupta
Answer: (c)
Solution: The Mehrauli iron pillar inscription refers to a king 'Chandra', identified with Chandragupta II. Its rust-free composition shows advanced Gupta metallurgy. Do not confuse it with Ashokan pillars.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Themes in Indian History Part I

143. Arrange the following events in correct chronological order:
1. Alexander's invasion of India
2. Kalinga War of Ashoka
3. Accession of Kanishka
4. Foundation of the Mauryan Empire
(a) 4-1-2-3 (b) 1-4-3-2 (c) 4-2-1-3 (d) 1-4-2-3
Answer: (d)
Solution: Alexander invaded in 326 BCE; Chandragupta founded the Mauryan Empire around 322 BCE; the Kalinga War was fought around 261 BCE; Kanishka's accession is dated to 78 CE (start of the Shaka era). Hence 1-4-2-3.

144. The terms 'Sreni' and 'Sarthavaha' in ancient Indian inscriptions refer to:
(a) Land grants and priests (b) Taxes and tax collectors (c) Guilds of merchants/craftsmen and caravan leaders (d) Villages and village headmen
Answer: (c)
Solution: Sreni denoted guilds of merchants and craftsmen, while Sarthavaha meant the leader of a trading caravan. These terms appear frequently in inscriptions of the post-Mauryan and Gupta periods.

145. Which of the following statements about the Satavahanas is NOT correct?
(a) Their capital was at Pratishthana (Paithan) (b) Gautamiputra Satakarni was their greatest ruler (c) They issued coins mainly of gold (d) They patronised both Brahmanism and Buddhism
Answer: (c)
Solution: The Satavahanas issued coins mainly of lead, potin, copper and silver — not gold. The other statements are correct; the gold-coin claim is the trap, since gold coinage is associated with Kushanas and Guptas.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Themes in Indian History Part I

146. The 'Uttarapath' and 'Dakshinapath' in ancient Indian literature referred to:
(a) Two Buddhist sects (b) Two Vedic rituals (c) Two Mauryan provinces (d) The northern and southern trade routes
Answer: (d)
Solution: Uttarapatha was the great northern route (from the northwest to the Ganga valley) and Dakshinapatha the route to the Deccan and south. They were trade corridors, not sects or provinces.

147. Consider the following about the Chola administration:

2. The Uttaramerur inscription gives details of village self-government.
3. The Cholas belonged to the Sangam age only and had no later medieval phase.
Which of the statements given above is/are correct?

148. Which of the following inscriptions is the earliest epigraphic evidence of the practice of Sati in India?
(a) Eran inscription of Bhanugupta (b) Aihole inscription of Pulakeshin II (c) Junagarh inscription of Rudradaman (d) Allahabad Pillar inscription
Answer: (a)
Solution: The Eran (Madhya Pradesh) inscription of Bhanugupta, dated 510 CE, recording the Sati of Goparaja's wife, is regarded as the earliest epigraphic evidence of Sati. The other inscriptions deal with conquests and administration.
