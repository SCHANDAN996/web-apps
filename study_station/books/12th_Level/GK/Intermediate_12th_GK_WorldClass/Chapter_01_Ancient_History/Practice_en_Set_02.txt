Questions 26–50: Medium

26. The Harappan site of Dholavira, known for its sophisticated water management system, is located in which present-day state?
(a) Rajasthan (b) Haryana (c) Gujarat (d) Punjab
Answer: (c)
Solution: Dholavira lies on Khadir island in the Rann of Kutch, Gujarat. It is famous for reservoirs and a three-part city layout. Rajasthan is a trap because Kalibangan, another Harappan site, is in Rajasthan.
Source: NCERT Class 12 History (Themes in Indian History Part I)

27. Which Veda is a collection of melodies and chants used during sacrifices?
(a) Rigveda (b) Yajurveda (c) Samaveda (d) Atharvaveda
Answer: (c)
Solution: The Samaveda sets Rigvedic hymns to music for chanting at rituals. Yajurveda contains prose sacrificial formulas, which is the common confusion.
Source: NCERT Class 11 History / Old NCERT Ancient India

28. The famous Gayatri Mantra is found in which Mandala of the Rigveda?
(a) First Mandala (b) Tenth Mandala (c) Seventh Mandala (d) Third Mandala
Answer: (d)
Solution: The Gayatri Mantra, addressed to Savitri, occurs in the third Mandala of the Rigveda, attributed to Vishvamitra. The tenth Mandala contains the Purusha Sukta, a frequent mix-up.
Source: Old NCERT Ancient India (R.S. Sharma)

29. Who was the founder of the Haryanka dynasty of Magadha?
(a) Ajatashatru (b) Bimbisara (c) Udayin (d) Shishunaga
Answer: (b)
Solution: Bimbisara founded the Haryanka dynasty and ruled from Rajagriha. Ajatashatru, his son, is the trap option since he expanded the kingdom but did not found the dynasty.
Source: NCERT Class 12 History / Old NCERT Ancient India

30. The first Buddhist Council was held at Rajagriha during the reign of which ruler?
(a) Bimbisara (b) Ashoka (c) Kalasoka (d) Ajatashatru
Answer: (d)
Solution: The First Buddhist Council (c. 483 BCE) met at Rajagriha under Ajatashatru's patronage, with Mahakassapa presiding. Kalasoka presided over the Second Council at Vaishali — a classic chronological trap.
Source: Old NCERT Ancient India (R.S. Sharma)

31. Which of the following pairs is correctly matched?
(a) Second Buddhist Council — Pataliputra (b) Third Buddhist Council — Vaishali (c) First Buddhist Council — Vaishali (d) Fourth Buddhist Council — Kashmir
Answer: (d)
Solution: The Fourth Council was held at Kundalvana, Kashmir, under Kanishka. The Second was at Vaishali and the Third at Pataliputra, so the other options deliberately swap the venues.
Source: Old NCERT Ancient India (R.S. Sharma)

32. Megasthenes, author of Indica, was an ambassador in the court of which ruler?
(a) Chandragupta Maurya (b) Bindusara (c) Ashoka (d) Dasharatha
Answer: (a)
Solution: Megasthenes was sent by Seleucus Nicator to the court of Chandragupta Maurya at Pataliputra. Deimachus came to Bindusara's court, which is the similar-name trap.
Source: NCERT Class 12 History (Themes in Indian History Part I)

33. Which Ashokan inscription describes the Kalinga War and its impact on the emperor?
(a) Major Rock Edict XIII (b) Major Rock Edict X (c) Pillar Edict VII (d) Minor Rock Edict I
Answer: (a)
Solution: Major Rock Edict XIII narrates the Kalinga War, the mass suffering, and Ashoka's turn to Dhamma. This edict is omitted at sites in Kalinga itself, a detail examiners exploit.
Source: NCERT Class 12 History (Themes in Indian History Part I)

34. The Ashokan inscriptions were first deciphered by whom?
(a) James Prinsep (b) Alexander Cunningham (c) William Jones (d) Max Mueller
Answer: (a)
Solution: James Prinsep deciphered the Brahmi script of Ashokan edicts in 1837. Cunningham founded the Archaeological Survey — a frequently confused pair of names.
Source: NCERT Class 12 History (Themes in Indian History Part I)

35. Which of the following statements about the Mauryan administration is NOT correct?
(a) The Arthashastra is attributed to Kautilya (b) Megasthenes described seven castes in Indian society (c) The Mauryan capital was at Taxila (d) Ashoka appointed officers called Dhamma Mahamatras
Answer: (c)
Solution: The Mauryan capital was Pataliputra; Taxila was a major provincial and learning centre in the northwest. The other three statements are correct, making this a near-true-statement trap.
Source: NCERT Class 12 History / Old NCERT Ancient India

36. The Gandhara school of art flourished mainly under the patronage of which dynasty?
(a) Mauryas (b) Kushanas (c) Guptas (d) Satavahanas
Answer: (b)
Solution: Gandhara art, blending Greek and Indian styles, reached its peak under the Kushanas, especially Kanishka. The Mathura school also grew under Kushanas, but Gandhara's Greco-Buddhist style is the key marker.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Fine Arts (An Introduction to Indian Art)

37. Who among the following issued gold coins on the largest scale among ancient Indian rulers before the Guptas?
(a) Kushanas (b) Mauryas (c) Satavahanas (d) Sungas
Answer: (a)
Solution: The Kushanas issued abundant gold dinaras, reflecting flourishing trade with Rome. The Guptas later issued even finer gold coins, which is why Gupta is a tempting but wrong answer here.
Source: Old NCERT Ancient India (R.S. Sharma)

38. The Satavahana rulers claimed which gotra and performed which sacrifice to assert Kshatriya status?
(a) Vashishtha gotra — Ashvamedha (b) Kashyapa gotra — Rajasuya (c) Gautama gotra — Vajapeya (d) Bharadvaja gotra — Agnishtoma
Answer: (a)
Solution: Satavahana kings like Gautamiputra Satakarni claimed Vashishtha gotra and performed the Ashvamedha. The dynasty's matronymic naming (Gautamiputra, Vashishthiputra) is a related exam point.
Source: Old NCERT Ancient India (R.S. Sharma)

39. Which Gupta ruler assumed the title 'Vikramaditya' after defeating the Shakas?
(a) Chandragupta II (b) Samudragupta (c) Chandragupta I (d) Kumaragupta I
Answer: (a)
Solution: Chandragupta II defeated the Shaka (Western Kshatrapa) rulers and took the title Vikramaditya. Samudragupta is the trap — he was the great conqueror but fought mainly in the north and south, not against the Shakas.
Source: NCERT Class 12 History / Old NCERT Ancient India

40. The Allahabad Pillar Inscription (Prayaga Prashasti) of Samudragupta was composed by whom?
(a) Kalidasa (b) Banabhatta (c) Harisena (d) Vatsyayana
Answer: (c)
Solution: Harisena, Samudragupta's court poet and minister, composed the Prayaga Prashasti. Banabhatta wrote Harshacharita for Harsha — a similar-name, similar-role trap.
Source: NCERT Class 12 History (Themes in Indian History Part I)

41. Which Chinese pilgrim visited India during the reign of Chandragupta II?
(a) Hiuen Tsang (b) Xuanzang (c) I-Tsing (d) Fa-Hien
Answer: (d)
Solution: Fa-Hien visited India (c. 399–414 CE) during Chandragupta II's reign. Hiuen Tsang came under Harshavardhana — the most common chronological confusion in this topic.
Source: Old NCERT Ancient India (R.S. Sharma)

42. The Iron Pillar at Mehrauli, which has not rusted for centuries, is associated with which period?
(a) Mauryan (b) Kushana (c) Vardhana (d) Gupta
Answer: (d)
Solution: The Mehrauli Iron Pillar belongs to the Gupta period (associated with Chandragupta II) and showcases advanced metallurgy. Its inscription mentions a ruler called Chandra.
Source: NCERT Class 12 History / NCERT Class 11 Fine Arts

43. Which of the following is the correct chronological order?
(a) Nandas — Sungas — Mauryas — Kushanas (b) Mauryas — Nandas — Sungas — Kushanas (c) Nandas — Mauryas — Sungas — Kushanas (d) Mauryas — Sungas — Nandas — Kushanas
Answer: (c)
Solution: The order is Nandas (overthrown by Chandragupta Maurya), Mauryas, Sungas (Pushyamitra overthrew Brihadratha), then Kushanas. This is a classic chronological-confusion question.
Source: Old NCERT Ancient India (R.S. Sharma)

44. The Sangam literature is associated with which language?
(a) Telugu (b) Tamil (c) Kannada (d) Malayalam
Answer: (b)
Solution: Sangam literature comprises early Tamil texts composed in assemblies (Sangams) patronised by Pandyan kings at Madurai. Tolkappiyam is its earliest grammar work.
Source: NCERT Class 11 History / Old NCERT Ancient India

45. Which Chola ruler is credited with the naval expedition against Srivijaya (Southeast Asia)?
(a) Rajaraja I (b) Rajendra I (c) Rajadhiraja (d) Kulottunga I
Answer: (b)
Solution: Rajendra I sent a naval expedition against the Srivijaya kingdom (c. 1025 CE) and assumed the title Gangaikonda Chola after his northern campaign. Rajaraja I, his father, built the Brihadeshwara temple — a frequent mix-up.
Source: NCERT Class 12 History (Themes in Indian History Part II)

46. The Uttaramerur inscriptions, describing village self-governance, belong to which ruler?
(a) Narasimhavarman I (b) Rajaraja I (c) Parantaka I (Chola) (d) Rajendra I
Answer: (c)
Solution: The Uttaramerur inscriptions of Parantaka I detail the sabha, its committees (variyams), and election by lot (kudavolai system). They are a standard source on Chola local administration.
Source: NCERT Class 12 History (Themes in Indian History Part II)

47. Which of the following statements about Jainism is correct?
(a) Mahavira was the founder of Jainism (b) Jainism rejects the doctrine of ahimsa (c) The first Tirthankara was Parshvanatha (d) Mahavira was the 24th Tirthankara
Answer: (d)
Solution: Mahavira was the 24th and last Tirthankara; Rishabhanatha was the first and Parshvanatha the 23rd. Calling Mahavira the 'founder' is the classic almost-correct trap.
Source: NCERT Class 12 History (Themes in Indian History Part I)

48. The Tripitakas, the canonical texts of Buddhism, are written in which language?
(a) Sanskrit (b) Pali (c) Prakrit (d) Apabhramsha
Answer: (b)
Solution: The three Pitakas — Vinaya, Sutta, and Abhidhamma — are in Pali. Jain canonical texts (Agamas) are in Prakrit (Ardhamagadhi), which is the common confusion.
Source: NCERT Class 12 History (Themes in Indian History Part I)

49. The Hathigumpha inscription, recording the achievements of a Chedi (Chetaka) ruler, belongs to which king?
(a) Kharavela (b) Ashoka (c) Satakarni I (d) Rudradaman I
Answer: (a)
Solution: The Hathigumpha inscription at Udayagiri (Odisha) records Kharavela's deeds. Rudradaman I's record is the Junagarh inscription — the first major Sanskrit prashasti, a similar-inscription trap.
Source: Old NCERT Ancient India (R.S. Sharma)

50. The Junagarh inscription of Rudradaman I is significant because it is the first major inscription written in which language?
(a) Prakrit (b) Sanskrit (c) Pali (d) Brahmi
Answer: (b)
Solution: Rudradaman I's Junagarh inscription (c. 150 CE) is the first long inscription in classical Sanskrit; earlier inscriptions were in Prakrit. Brahmi is a script, not a language — a deliberate trap option.
Source: Old NCERT Ancient India (R.S. Sharma)
