Questions 1–20: Easy | Questions 21–25: Medium

1. The Harappan site of Mohenjo-daro is located on the bank of which river?
(a) Ganga (b) Beas (c) Ravi (d) Indus
Answer: (d)
Solution: Mohenjo-daro lies on the bank of the Indus river in present-day Sindh, Pakistan. Harappa, in contrast, is on the Ravi, so the two sites are often confused.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Themes in Indian History Part I

2. The Great Bath, a famous structure of the Indus Valley Civilization, was found at which site?
(a) Mohenjo-daro (b) Lothal (c) Harappa (d) Kalibangan
Answer: (a)
Solution: The Great Bath was excavated at Mohenjo-daro. Lothal is famous for its dockyard, which is the common trap in such questions.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Themes in Indian History Part I

3. Which metal was NOT known to the people of the Indus Valley Civilization?
(a) Copper (b) Iron (c) Bronze (d) Gold
Answer: (b)
Solution: The Harappans used copper, bronze, gold and silver, but iron was unknown to them. Iron came into common use in India much later, around the later Vedic period.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Themes in Indian History Part I

4. The dockyard of the Indus Valley Civilization has been found at which site?
(a) Dholavira (b) Lothal (c) Chanhudaro (d) Banawali
Answer: (b)
Solution: Lothal in Gujarat had a large dockyard, indicating maritime trade. Dholavira is known for its water management system and city divided into three parts.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Themes in Indian History Part I

5. The Gayatri Mantra is found in which Veda?
(a) Rig Veda (b) Sama Veda (c) Yajur Veda (d) Atharva Veda
Answer: (a)
Solution: The Gayatri Mantra, addressed to the solar deity Savitri, appears in the third mandala of the Rig Veda. The Rig Veda is the oldest of the four Vedas.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Themes in Indian History Part I

6. Gautama Buddha delivered his first sermon at which place?
(a) Bodh Gaya (b) Kushinagar (c) Sarnath (d) Lumbini
Answer: (c)
Solution: Buddha's first sermon, called the Dhammachakkapavattana (turning of the wheel of law), was given at Sarnath near Varanasi. Bodh Gaya is where he attained enlightenment, a frequent confusion.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Themes in Indian History Part I

7. Mahavira, the 24th Tirthankara of Jainism, was born at which place?
(a) Pavapuri (b) Kundagrama (c) Rajgriha (d) Vaishali
Answer: (b)
Solution: Mahavira was born at Kundagrama near Vaishali. He died at Pavapuri, and examiners often swap birth and death places to trap candidates.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Themes in Indian History Part I

8. Who founded the Mauryan Empire?
(a) Bindusara (b) Bimbisara (c) Ashoka (d) Chandragupta Maurya
Answer: (d)
Solution: Chandragupta Maurya founded the Mauryan Empire around 321 BCE with the help of Chanakya (Kautilya). Ashoka was his grandson, and Bimbisara was a Haryanka king of Magadha.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Themes in Indian History Part I

9. The Kalinga War, which transformed Ashoka, was fought in which year?
(a) 305 BCE (b) 261 BCE (c) 232 BCE (d) 185 BCE
Answer: (b)
Solution: The Kalinga War was fought in 261 BCE, in the eighth year of Ashoka's reign. The mass suffering it caused led him to embrace Buddhism and the policy of Dhamma.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Themes in Indian History Part I

10. The Arthashastra, a treatise on statecraft, is traditionally attributed to whom?
(a) Kautilya (b) Megasthenes (c) Kalidasa (d) Banabhatta
Answer: (a)
Solution: The Arthashastra is attributed to Kautilya (Chanakya), the minister of Chandragupta Maurya. Megasthenes wrote Indica, and Banabhatta wrote the Harshacharita.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Themes in Indian History Part I

11. Megasthenes, the author of Indica, was an ambassador in the court of which ruler?
(a) Ashoka (b) Chandragupta Maurya (c) Bindusara (d) Harshavardhana
Answer: (b)
Solution: Megasthenes was the ambassador of the Seleucid king Seleucus Nicator at the court of Chandragupta Maurya. His account Indica describes the Mauryan capital Pataliputra.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Themes in Indian History Part I

12. Which of the following was the first capital of the Magadha kingdom?
(a) Rajgriha (b) Pataliputra (c) Vaishali (d) Champa
Answer: (a)
Solution: Rajgriha (Girivraja) was the early capital of Magadha under the Haryanka dynasty. The capital was later shifted to Pataliputra, which is the usual trap in this question.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Themes in Indian History Part I

13. Alexander invaded India in which year?
(a) 261 BCE (b) 305 BCE (c) 326 BCE (d) 356 BCE
Answer: (c)
Solution: Alexander invaded north-western India in 326 BCE and fought the Battle of Hydaspes (Jhelum) against Porus. 305 BCE is associated with the treaty between Chandragupta Maurya and Seleucus Nicator.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Themes in Indian History Part I

14. The Fourth Buddhist Council, which split Buddhism into Hinayana and Mahayana, was held during the reign of which ruler?
(a) Kanishka (b) Ashoka (c) Harshavardhana (d) Chandragupta II
Answer: (a)
Solution: The Fourth Buddhist Council was held at Kundalvana (Kashmir) under the Kushana ruler Kanishka, where Buddhism formally split into Hinayana and Mahayana. Ashoka presided over the Third Council at Pataliputra.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Themes in Indian History Part I

15. Samudragupta's military achievements are described in which inscription?
(a) Allahabad Pillar inscription (b) Junagarh inscription (c) Aihole inscription (d) Hathigumpha inscription
Answer: (a)
Solution: The Allahabad Pillar inscription (Prayaga Prashasti), composed by Harisena, describes Samudragupta's conquests. The Junagarh inscription belongs to Rudradaman, and the Aihole inscription to Pulakeshin II.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Themes in Indian History Part I

16. Which Gupta ruler is known as the 'Napoleon of India'?
(a) Chandragupta I (b) Chandragupta II (c) Samudragupta (d) Kumaragupta
Answer: (c)
Solution: Historian V.A. Smith called Samudragupta the 'Napoleon of India' because of his extensive military campaigns. Chandragupta II is famous for defeating the Shakas and for the iron pillar at Mehrauli.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Themes in Indian History Part I

17. The Chinese pilgrim Fa-hien (Faxian) visited India during the reign of which ruler?
(a) Chandragupta II (b) Kanishka (c) Ashoka (d) Harshavardhana
Answer: (a)
Solution: Fa-hien visited India during the reign of Chandragupta II (Vikramaditya). Hiuen Tsang (Xuanzang) came later, during Harshavardhana's reign — the two pilgrims are a classic examiner's trap.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Themes in Indian History Part I

18. Harshavardhana's court poet who wrote the Harshacharita was whom?
(a) Kalidasa (b) Harisena (c) Banabhatta (d) Dandin
Answer: (c)
Solution: Banabhatta, the court poet of Harshavardhana, wrote the Harshacharita and Kadambari. Kalidasa is associated with the Gupta age, and Harisena was Samudragupta's court poet.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Themes in Indian History Part I

19. The famous rock-cut Kailasa temple at Ellora was built under which dynasty?
(a) Chalukyas (b) Cholas (c) Pallavas (d) Rashtrakutas
Answer: (d)
Solution: The Kailasa temple at Ellora was built by the Rashtrakuta king Krishna I. The Pallavas built the shore temple at Mahabalipuram, which is a common confusion.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Themes in Indian History Part I

20. The Brihadeshwara temple at Thanjavur was built by which Chola ruler?
(a) Rajendra I (b) Vijayalaya (c) Kulottunga I (d) Rajaraja I
Answer: (d)
Solution: The Brihadeshwara (Rajarajeshwara) temple at Thanjavur was built by Rajaraja I around 1010 CE. His son Rajendra I founded Gangaikonda Cholapuram and built a similar temple there.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Themes in Indian History Part I

21. Consider the following statements about the Indus Valley Civilization:
1. The people worshipped the Mother Goddess.
2. The Harappan script has been successfully deciphered.
3. The civilization was primarily urban in character.
Which of the statements given above is/are correct?
(a) 1 and 2 only (b) 2 and 3 only (c) 1 and 3 only (d) 1, 2 and 3
Answer: (c)
Solution: Statements 1 and 3 are correct: Mother Goddess figurines are found at Harappan sites, and the civilization was urban. Statement 2 is the trap — the Harappan script remains undeciphered to this day.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Themes in Indian History Part I

22. Arrange the following events in correct chronological order:
1. Kalinga War of Ashoka
2. Alexander's invasion of India
3. Accession of Kanishka
4. Foundation of the Mauryan Empire
(a) 2, 4, 1, 3 (b) 4, 2, 1, 3 (c) 2, 1, 4, 3 (d) 4, 1, 2, 3
Answer: (a)
Solution: Alexander invaded in 326 BCE, Chandragupta founded the Mauryan Empire around 321 BCE, the Kalinga War was in 261 BCE, and Kanishka's accession was in 78 CE. The trap is placing the empire's foundation before Alexander's invasion.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Themes in Indian History Part I

23. Consider the following pairs of Buddhist events and places:
1. Birth of Buddha — Lumbini
2. Enlightenment — Bodh Gaya
3. First sermon — Kushinagar
4. Death (Mahaparinirvana) — Sarnath
Which of the pairs given above are correctly matched?
(a) 2 and 4 only (b) 1, 2 and 3 only (c) 1 and 2 only (d) 1, 2, 3 and 4
Answer: (c)
Solution: Only pairs 1 and 2 are correct. The first sermon was at Sarnath and the Mahaparinirvana at Kushinagar — pairs 3 and 4 deliberately swap these two places, a classic examiner's trap.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Themes in Indian History Part I

24. Which of the following statements about Ashoka's Dhamma is correct?
(a) It was the propagation of Buddhism as the state religion (b) It banned all religious sects except Buddhism (c) It was a code of moral and ethical conduct applicable to all (d) It was a system of land revenue administration
Answer: (c)
Solution: Ashoka's Dhamma was a broad ethical code — tolerance, non-violence, respect for elders and humane conduct — not the imposition of Buddhism. Option (a) is the common trap; Ashoka patronised Buddhism but Dhamma itself was a moral code for all.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Themes in Indian History Part I

25. Match the following travellers with the rulers whose courts they visited:
1. Megasthenes — Chandragupta Maurya
2. Fa-hien — Chandragupta II
3. Hiuen Tsang — Harshavardhana
(a) 1 and 2 only (b) 1, 2 and 3 (c) 1 and 3 only (d) 2 and 3 only
Answer: (b)
Solution: All three pairs are correctly matched: Megasthenes (Seleucid ambassador) visited Chandragupta Maurya, Fa-hien came during Chandragupta II's reign, and Hiuen Tsang visited Harshavardhana's court. Examiners often swap Fa-hien and Hiuen Tsang between the Gupta and post-Gupta periods.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Themes in Indian History Part I
