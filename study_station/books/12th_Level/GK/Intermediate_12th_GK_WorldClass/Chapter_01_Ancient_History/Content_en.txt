# Ancient Indian History

## 🌟 The Hook: A City That Had Drains Before Rome Had Roads

Imagine this: around 2600 BCE, when most of the world lived in mud huts, the people of **Mohenjo-daro** (in present-day Pakistan) were taking baths in the **Great Bath**, sending waste water through **covered street drains**, and living in houses with private wells. No kings' palaces, no giant temples — just a stunningly planned city. Who ruled them? We still don't know, because **their script has never been deciphered**.

That mystery is your doorway into Ancient Indian History — a subject where one small fact (who wrote the Arthashastra? which script did Ashoka use?) decides one full mark in SSC, Railway, Banking and State PCS exams. Let's master it, chunk by chunk.

## 1. How Do We Know the Past? (Sources of History)

History is rebuilt from two kinds of sources:

- **Archaeological sources**: coins, inscriptions (like Ashoka's edicts), monuments, pottery, seals.
- **Literary sources**: religious texts (Vedas, Puranas, Jataka tales) and accounts of foreign travellers (Megasthenes, Fa-Hien, Hiuen Tsang).

**Key point:** The study of inscriptions is called **Epigraphy**, and the study of coins is called **Numismatics**. These two words appear directly in exams.

> 🧠 **Active Recall:** What is epigraphy? Which Greek ambassador wrote *Indica*? (Answers below — try before reading on!)

*Answers: Epigraphy = study of inscriptions. Megasthenes wrote Indica.*

## 2. The Indus Valley Civilization (c. 2500–1750 BCE)

Also called the **Harappan Civilization** (after Harappa, the first site excavated, in 1921). It was a **Bronze Age** civilization spread over present-day Pakistan and north-west India — larger in area than Egypt and Mesopotamia combined.

### Major Sites and Their Finds

| Site | River | Famous For |
|---|---|---|
| Harappa | Ravi | Granaries, cemetery |
| Mohenjo-daro | Indus | Great Bath, Great Granary, bronze "Dancing Girl" |
| Dholavira (Gujarat) | Luni (seasonal) | Water reservoirs, three-part city division |
| Lothal (Gujarat) | Bhogava | Dockyard — a port city |
| Kalibangan (Rajasthan) | Ghaggar | Ploughed field, fire altars |
| Chanhudaro | Indus | Bead-making factory (no citadel) |
| Banawali (Haryana) | Ghaggar | Barley grains, oval-shaped settlement |

### Key Features

- **Town planning**: grid-pattern streets, citadel (west) + lower town (east), covered drains.
- **Great Bath** at Mohenjo-daro — probably for ritual bathing.
- **Agriculture**: wheat and barley; they were the **earliest cotton growers** in the world.
- **Script**: pictographic, written **right to left** (boustrophedon style in some seals) — **still undeciphered**.
- **Worship**: Mother Goddess, Pashupati seal (a figure seated like Shiva with animals), peepal tree, humped bull.
- **No clear evidence** of temples, palaces, or iron — they used bronze and copper.

### 🧠 Active Recall
Which site had a dockyard? Which metal was unknown to the Harappans?

*Answers: Lothal; iron.*

### 🪄 Mnemonic 1 — "HaMo Do Lo Ka Cha"
**Ha**rappa, **Mo**henjo-daro, **Do**lavira, **Lo**thal, **Ka**libangan, **Cha**nhudaro — the six most-asked Harappan sites.

## 3. The Vedic Period (c. 1500–600 BCE)

The **Aryans** entered north-west India around 1500 BCE. Their literature — the **Vedas** — is our main source.

### The Four Vedas

| Veda | Content |
|---|---|
| **Rigveda** | Oldest; 1028 hymns in 10 mandalas; the famous **Gayatri Mantra** |
| **Samaveda** | Melodies/chants — root of Indian music |
| **Yajurveda** | Rituals and sacrificial formulas |
| **Atharvaveda** | Spells, medicine, everyday life |

- **Early (Rigvedic) period (1500–1000 BCE)**: pastoral society, cattle = wealth, tribal assemblies (**Sabha** and **Samiti**), the Battle of Ten Kings (**Dasharajna**) on the river Parushni (Ravi).
- **Later Vedic period (1000–600 BCE)**: shift eastward to the Ganga plains, agriculture grows, iron (**krishna ayas**) appears, the **varna (caste) system** hardens, kingdoms (janapadas) emerge.

**The Purusha Sukta** of the Rigveda (10th mandala) first mentions the four varnas.

> 🧠 **Active Recall:** Which Veda is the oldest? What were Sabha and Samiti?

*Answers: Rigveda; tribal assemblies.*

### 🪄 Mnemonic 2 — "RiSaYaAtha" → **"Rishi Sang Yajur Atharva"**
Order of the four Vedas: **Ri**gveda, **Sa**maveda, **Ya**jurveda, **Atha**rvaveda.

## 4. Buddhism and Jainism (6th Century BCE)

Around 500 BCE, sixteen large states called **Mahajanapadas** existed (Magadha, Kosala, Vatsa, Avanti were the strongest). Against ritual-heavy Brahmanism, two reform religions arose.

### Buddhism — Gautama Buddha (563–483 BCE)

- Born at **Lumbini** (Nepal); enlightenment at **Bodh Gaya** (under the Bodhi tree, on the river Niranjana); first sermon at **Sarnath** (**Dharmachakra Pravartana**); death (**Mahaparinirvana**) at **Kushinagar**.
- Core teaching: **Four Noble Truths** (life has suffering; desire causes suffering; suffering can end; the way is the **Eightfold Path — Ashtangika Marga**).
- Language of preaching: **Pali**. Sacred texts: **Tripitakas** (Vinaya, Sutta, Abhidhamma).

### Jainism — Mahavira (540–468 BCE)

- 24th and last **Tirthankara**; the first was **Rishabhdev (Adinath)**, the 23rd was **Parshvanath**.
- Born at **Kundagrama** (near Vaishali); died at **Pavapuri** (Rajgriha).
- Core teaching: **Five vows** — Ahimsa (non-violence), Satya (truth), Asteya (non-stealing), Aparigraha (non-possession), Brahmacharya (celibacy — added by Mahavira).
- Language: **Prakrit (Ardhamagadhi)**. Later split into **Shvetambara** (white-clad) and **Digambara** (sky-clad).

### Buddhist Councils (very frequently asked!)

| Council | Year | Place | King |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1st | 483 BCE | Rajgriha | Ajatashatru |
| 2nd | 383 BCE | Vaishali | Kalasoka |
| 3rd | 250 BCE | Pataliputra | Ashoka |
| 4th | 1st century CE | Kundalvana (Kashmir) | Kanishka |

> 🧠 **Active Recall:** Where did Buddha give his first sermon? Who was the 23rd Tirthankara?

*Answers: Sarnath; Parshvanath.*

### 🪄 Mnemonic 3 — Buddha's life in 4 L's → **"L-B-S-K"**
**L**umbini (birth) → **B**odh Gaya (enlightenment) → **S**arnath (first sermon) → **K**ushinagar (death). Remember: **"Little Boy Sings Kirtan."**

## 5. The Mauryan Empire (322–185 BCE)

- **Chandragupta Maurya** founded the empire in **322 BCE**, overthrowing the Nandas, guided by his mentor **Chanakya (Kautilya/Vishnugupta)**, author of the **Arthashastra**.
- **Megasthenes**, the Greek ambassador of Seleucus Nicator, lived at Pataliputra and wrote **Indica**.
- **Bindusara** ruled next; then came the greatest — **Ashoka (c. 268–232 BCE)**.

### Ashoka the Great

- The **Kalinga War (261 BCE)** — described in his **13th Rock Edict** — changed him; he embraced Buddhism and **Dhamma** (moral law, not a religion).
- His inscriptions were written mostly in **Brahmi script** (deciphered by **James Prinsep in 1837**), and in Kharoshthi, Greek and Aramaic in the north-west.
- The **Lion Capital of Sarnath** is India's national emblem; the **Ashoka Chakra** sits on our flag.
- The last Mauryan ruler, **Brihadratha**, was killed by his commander **Pushyamitra Shunga** (185 BCE), who founded the **Shunga dynasty**.

> 🧠 **Active Recall:** Who deciphered Brahmi? Which edict describes the Kalinga War?

*Answers: James Prinsep (1837); the 13th Rock Edict.*

## 6. Between the Empires (200 BCE – 300 CE)

- **Indo-Greeks**: **Menander (Milinda)** — his dialogue with monk Nagasena is the text **Milindapanho**.
- **Kushanas**: **Kanishka** started the **Shaka Era (78 CE)**; patron of the 4th Buddhist Council; Gandhara and Mathura schools of art flourished.
- **Satavahanas** ruled the Deccan; **Sangam literature** (Tamil) flourished in the far south under the **Cheras, Cholas and Pandyas**.

---

## 7. The Gupta Empire (c. 319–550 CE) — The "Golden Age"

- **Chandragupta I** founded the era (**Gupta Era, 319–320 CE**).
- **Samudragupta** — called the "**Napoleon of India**" by historian V.A. Smith; his conquests are recorded in the **Allahabad Pillar Inscription (Prayaga Prashasti)** composed by **Harisena**.
- **Chandragupta II (Vikramaditya)** — defeated the Shakas; the Chinese pilgrim **Fa-Hien** visited in his reign; his court had the **Navaratnas (nine gems)**, including **Kalidasa** (author of *Abhijnana Shakuntalam*, *Meghaduta*).

### Why "Golden Age"?

- **Aryabhata** — *Aryabhatiya*; explained the earth's rotation, used zero's place value concept.
- **Varahamihira** — *Brihat Samhita*.
- **Kalidasa** — greatest Sanskrit poet-dramatist.
- **Ajanta cave paintings** (mostly Buddhist themes) reached their peak.
- **Iron Pillar at Mehrauli** (Delhi) — rust-free even after 1,600 years, a marvel of metallurgy.
- **Nalanda University** — founded by **Kumaragupta I**; the Chinese pilgrim **Hiuen Tsang** (who came in Harshavardhana's reign, 7th century CE) studied there.

> 🧠 **Active Recall:** Who was called the Napoleon of India? Which traveller came during Chandragupta II's reign?

*Answers: Samudragupta; Fa-Hien. (Careful — Hiuen Tsang came later, in Harsha's time!)*

---

## 8. After the Guptas: Harsha and the South

- **Harshavardhana (606–647 CE)**: capital **Kannauj**; wrote plays (*Nagananda*, *Ratnavali*, *Priyadarshika*); **Banabhatta** wrote his biography, **Harshacharita** (and *Kadambari*). Hiuen Tsang visited his court.
- **Pulakeshin II** (Chalukya) defeated Harsha on the **Narmada** — recorded in the **Aihole inscription** by Ravikirti.
- **Pallavas** (Kanchipuram): built the **Shore Temple at Mahabalipuram** and Kailasanatha temple.
- **Cholas** (later, 9th–13th century): **Rajaraja I** built the **Brihadeshwara Temple at Thanjavur**; **Rajendra I** led a naval expedition to South-East Asia; famous for **village self-government** (ur, sabha, nagaram).

## ⚠️ Caution: Examiner's Trap

**Trap 1 — Chronological Confusion (कालानुक्रमिक भ्रम):** "Hiuen Tsang visited the court of Chandragupta II." ❌ It was **Fa-Hien** who came during Chandragupta II (Gupta); **Hiuen Tsang** came during **Harshavardhana** — nearly 200 years later. The two Chinese travellers are deliberately swapped.

**Trap 2 — Almost-Correct Statement (लगभग सही कथन):** "The Harappans used iron tools for farming." ❌ Everything sounds right except one word — the Harappans knew **bronze and copper, NOT iron**. Iron appears only in the **Later Vedic period**. One wrong metal = one lost mark.

**Trap 3 — Similar Names (समान नाम):** Chandragupta **Maurya** (322 BCE, mentor Chanakya) vs Chandragupta **I** (Gupta, 319 CE) vs Chandragupta **II Vikramaditya**. Also: Buddha's first sermon at **Sarnath** vs enlightenment at **Bodh Gaya** vs death at **Kushinagar** — examiners shuffle these three places mercilessly. Always read the full name and the full event before answering.
