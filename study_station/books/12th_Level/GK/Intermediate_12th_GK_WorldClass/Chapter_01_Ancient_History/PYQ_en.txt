# 📊 PYQ Analysis — Ancient Indian History

## 1. How Exams Ask Questions from This Chapter

Ancient Indian History is a permanent fixture in SSC (CGL, CHSL, MTS, GD), RRB (NTPC, Group D), Banking (IBPS, SBI) and State PCS prelims. The questions are almost always **one-liner factual MCQs** — a name, a place, a text, a ruler, a site. Analysis of past papers shows a clear pattern:

- **The Indus Valley Civilization** is the single most repeated sub-topic. Examiners love site–feature matching (which site had the Great Bath / dockyard / granary), the metals the Harappans knew (and did **not** know — iron), and the script (undeciphered, written boustrophedon).
- **Vedic literature** questions revolve around the four Vedas, their associated Brahmanas/Upanishads, and terms like *sabha*, *samiti*, *gana*, and the Battle of Ten Kings (Dasarajna) on the river Parushni (Ravi).
- **Buddhism and Jainism** appear in nearly every exam cycle: the Four Noble Truths, the Eightfold Path, Buddhist councils and their patrons, Mahavira's five vows, and the places of Buddha's birth (Lumbini), enlightenment (Bodh Gaya), first sermon (Sarnath) and death (Kushinagar).
- **The Mauryan Empire** is asked through Ashoka's edicts, the Kalinga War, Megasthenes' *Indica*, and the administrative terms in Arthashastra.
- **The Gupta period** is asked through "Golden Age" achievements — Aryabhata, Kalidasa's works, the Iron Pillar of Mehrauli, Fa-hien's visit, and Samudragupta's Allahabad Pillar inscription (composed by Harisena).
- **Foreign travellers** (Megasthenes, Fa-hien, Hiuen Tsang, Al-Biruni) and **inscriptions/coins** are classic one-mark questions.

## 2. Exam-wise Emphasis (in words, not numbers)

- **SSC CGL / CHSL:** Balanced coverage; strong tilt toward Indus Valley sites, Buddhist councils, and Gupta literature. Questions are direct and fact-based.
- **RRB NTPC / Group D:** Very simple recall questions — "Who wrote Arthashastra?", "Where did Buddha give his first sermon?" Speed matters more than depth.
- **Banking (IBPS/SBI):** Fewer questions, but when they come they are usually about books, authors, and "firsts" (first Buddhist council, first Tirthankara).
- **State PCS:** Slightly deeper — administrative terms, land grants, and regional dynasties of the ancient period.

## 3. The Examiner's Traps (Jaal)

1. **Almost Correct Statement:** "The Great Bath was found at Harappa." (It was Mohenjo-daro.) Sites and their features are swapped constantly.
2. **Chronological Confusion:** Mixing up the order of Buddhist councils, or placing Kanishka before Ashoka. Learn fixed sequences: Buddha → Mahavira (contemporaries) → Alexander's invasion (326 BCE) → Chandragupta Maurya → Ashoka → Kanishka → Samudragupta → Harsha.
3. **Similar-Sounding Names:** Chandragupta Maurya vs Chandragupta I (Gupta); Bindusara vs Bimbisara; Rajagriha vs Vaishali (council venues).
4. **Negative Phrasing:** "Which of the following was NOT known to the Harappans?" (Iron.) "Which is NOT a Noble Truth?" Read the NOT twice before answering.
5. **Traveller–Period Mismatch:** Fa-hien visited during Chandragupta II's reign; Hiuen Tsang during Harsha's. Examiners swap them.
6. **Language Trap:** Ashoka's edicts were mostly in Prakrit (Brahmi script), not Sanskrit. The "Sanskrit" option is the bait.

## 4. Top 10 High-Yield PYQ-Style Questions with Solutions

**Q1. The Great Bath of the Indus Valley Civilization was discovered at —**
(a) Harappa (b) Mohenjo-daro (c) Lothal (d) Kalibangan
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** The Great Bath is the hallmark of Mohenjo-daro. Lothal is famous for its dockyard — the most common swap trap.
**Trap type:** Almost Correct (site–feature swap).
**Source:** NCERT Class 6/12 History (Themes in Indian History Part I).

**Q2. Which metal was NOT known to the people of the Indus Valley Civilization?**
(a) Copper (b) Bronze (c) Iron (d) Gold
**Answer: (c)**
**Solution:** Harappans knew copper, bronze, gold and silver, but iron came with the later Vedic period. Negative-phrasing questions demand a slow first read.
**Trap type:** Negative Phrasing.
**Source:** NCERT Class 12, Themes in Indian History Part I.

**Q3. Gautama Buddha delivered his first sermon at —**
(a) Bodh Gaya (b) Sarnath (c) Kushinagar (d) Lumbini
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** Birth — Lumbini; Enlightenment — Bodh Gaya; First sermon (Dharmachakra Pravartana) — Sarnath; Death (Mahaparinirvana) — Kushinagar. All four are asked interchangeably.
**Trap type:** Similar-event confusion.
**Source:** NCERT Class 6 History.

**Q4. The Fourth Buddhist Council, which split Buddhism into Hinayana and Mahayana, was held under the patronage of —**
(a) Ashoka (b) Kanishka (c) Harsha (d) Chandragupta Maurya
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** The Fourth Council was held at Kundalvana (Kashmir) under Kanishka. Ashoka patronised the Third Council at Pataliputra — the classic bait option.
**Trap type:** Chronological Confusion.
**Source:** Standard ancient history texts (e.g., R.S. Sharma, NCERT).

**Q5. Megasthenes, the author of *Indica*, was an ambassador in the court of —**
(a) Ashoka (b) Bindusara (c) Chandragupta Maurya (d) Samudragupta
**Answer: (c)**
**Solution:** Megasthenes was sent by Seleucus Nicator to Chandragupta Maurya's court at Pataliputra. Do not confuse him with Fa-hien (Gupta era) or Hiuen Tsang (Harsha's era).
**Trap type:** Traveller–Period Mismatch.
**Source:** NCERT Class 12, Themes in Indian History Part I.

**Q6. The Allahabad Pillar Inscription (Prayaga Prashasti), describing the conquests of Samudragupta, was composed by —**
(a) Kalidasa (b) Harisena (c) Banabhatta (d) Vishakhadatta
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** Harisena was Samudragupta's court poet. Banabhatta wrote Harshacharita (for Harsha) — a similar-sounding pair examiners exploit.
**Trap type:** Similar-Sounding Names.
**Source:** NCERT Class 12, Themes in Indian History Part I.

**Q7. Which of the following is NOT one of the Four Noble Truths of Buddhism?**
(a) Life is suffering (b) Suffering has a cause (c) Suffering can be ended (d) Salvation comes only through rituals
**Answer: (d)**
**Solution:** The Four Noble Truths: suffering exists, it has a cause (desire), it can end, and the path is the Eightfold Path. Buddha rejected ritualism — option (d) contradicts his teaching.
**Trap type:** Negative Phrasing + Almost Correct.
**Source:** NCERT Class 6 History.

**Q8. The Iron Pillar at Mehrauli, famous for not rusting, is associated with the period of —**
(a) Mauryas (b) Guptas (c) Kushanas (d) Satavahanas
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** The Iron Pillar (associated with Chandragupta II, "Chandra" of the inscription) showcases Gupta metallurgical skill. The Mauryan option tempts because of Ashoka's pillars — but those are polished sandstone, not iron.
**Trap type:** Almost Correct (pillar association).
**Source:** NCERT Class 12, Themes in Indian History Part I.

**Q9. The Battle of the Ten Kings (Dasarajna) mentioned in the Rig Veda was fought on the banks of the river —**
(a) Saraswati (b) Yamuna (c) Parushni (Ravi) (d) Sindhu
**Answer: (c)**
**Solution:** The Dasarajna was fought on the Parushni, identified with the modern Ravi. King Sudas of the Bharata tribe won. Sindhu is the bait because the Rig Veda mentions it most often.
**Trap type:** Almost Correct (famous-river bait).
**Source:** NCERT / standard Vedic literature texts.

**Q10. Ashoka's inscriptions were mostly written in which language and script?**
(a) Sanskrit, Devanagari (b) Prakrit, Brahmi (c) Pali, Kharosthi (d) Sanskrit, Brahmi
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** Most edicts are in Prakrit written in Brahmi script (Kharosthi in the north-west, Greek and Aramaic in Afghanistan). "Sanskrit" is the most common wrong guess.
**Trap type:** Language Trap.
**Source:** NCERT Class 12, Themes in Indian History Part I.

## 5. The 15/45-Second Rule in Action

**15-second questions (answer instantly, bank the time):**
- Q3 above (Buddha's first sermon — Sarnath). If you have memorised the four-event chain, this is a reflex answer. Do not re-read the options twice.
- Q5 (Megasthenes → Chandragupta Maurya). A direct traveller–ruler pair; answer and move on.

**45-second questions (slow down, the trap is in the wording):**
- Q2 and Q7 (NOT-type questions). Underline "NOT" mentally, then evaluate each option one by one. Most errors here come from reading speed, not ignorance.
- Q4 (Buddhist councils). Reconstruct the sequence — 1st Rajagriha (Ajatashatru), 2nd Vaishali (Kalasoka), 3rd Pataliputra (Ashoka), 4th Kundalvana (Kanishka) — before locking the answer. Ten extra seconds here saves a negative mark.

**Skip strategy:** If a question asks about an obscure site, a minor Vedic term, or a ruler you have never seen, do not guess between two unfamiliar options. In negative-marking exams, skip it and return only if time remains.
