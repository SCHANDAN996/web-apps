101. The Harappan site of Dholavira, known for its sophisticated water management system, is located in which present-day state?
(a) Rajasthan (b) Gujarat (c) Haryana (d) Punjab
Answer: (b)
Solution: Dholavira lies on Khadir Bet island in the Rann of Kutch, Gujarat, and is famous for its reservoirs and three-part city layout. Rajasthan's Harappan site is Kalibangan, which often confuses students.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Themes in Indian History Part I

102. Which of the following Harappan sites is famous for the discovery of a dockyard?
(a) Harappa (b) Chanhudaro (c) Mohenjo-daro (d) Lothal
Answer: (d)
Solution: Lothal in Gujarat had a tidal dockyard, indicating maritime trade. Mohenjo-daro is known for the Great Bath, not a dockyard — a common trap in exams.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Themes in Indian History Part I

103. The 'Great Bath' of the Indus Valley Civilization was found at:
(a) Mohenjo-daro (b) Kalibangan (c) Lothal (d) Banawali
Answer: (a)
Solution: The Great Bath, a large rectangular tank with bitumen waterproofing, was excavated at Mohenjo-daro in Sindh (Pakistan). Kalibangan is known for ploughed fields and fire altars, not the Great Bath.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Themes in Indian History Part I

104. Which Veda is a collection of melodies (saman) meant for chanting during sacrifices?
(a) Rigveda (b) Samaveda (c) Yajurveda (d) Atharvaveda
Answer: (b)
Solution: The Samaveda sets Rigvedic hymns to music for ritual chanting. The Atharvaveda contains spells and medicine — do not confuse the two, as examiners often swap them.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Themes in Indian History Part I

105. The famous Gayatri Mantra is found in which Veda?
(a) Yajurveda (b) Samaveda (c) Rigveda (d) Atharvaveda
Answer: (c)
Solution: The Gayatri Mantra, addressed to Savitri (the sun deity), appears in the third mandala of the Rigveda and is attributed to sage Vishvamitra. The other Vedas were compiled later and draw heavily on the Rigveda.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Themes in Indian History Part I

106. The term 'Arya' in the Rigveda primarily referred to:
(a) A priestly title (b) A racial group (c) A military rank (d) A cultural and linguistic community
Answer: (d)
Solution: Modern scholarship holds that 'Arya' denoted a cultural-linguistic community, not a race. The racial interpretation was a colonial-era construct — a frequent trap in statement-based questions.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Themes in Indian History Part I

107. The first Buddhist Council was held at Rajgriha during the reign of:
(a) Bimbisara (b) Kalasoka (c) Ajatashatru (d) Ashoka
Answer: (c)
Solution: The First Buddhist Council (c. 483 BCE) met at Rajgriha under Ajatashatru of the Haryanka dynasty. Bimbisara was his father and Buddha's contemporary — a classic chronological trap.

108. Buddha delivered his first sermon, the 'Dharmachakra Pravartana', at:
(a) Bodh Gaya (b) Kushinagar (c) Lumbini (d) Sarnath
Answer: (d)
Solution: The first sermon was given at Sarnath near Varanasi. Bodh Gaya is where he attained enlightenment and Kushinagar where he attained Mahaparinirvana — examiners frequently interchange these four sites.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Themes in Indian History Part I

109. Mahavira, the 24th Tirthankara of Jainism, attained Kaivalya (omniscience) at:
(a) Pavapuri (b) Jrimbhikagrama (c) Vaishali (d) Kundagrama
Answer: (b)
Solution: Mahavira attained Kaivalya at Jrimbhikagrama on the river Rijupalika. He died at Pavapuri and was born at Kundagrama near Vaishali — similar-sounding places form a deliberate trap.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Themes in Indian History Part I

110. Which vow did Mahavira add to the four vows already taught by Parshvanatha?
(a) Ahimsa (b) Satya (c) Asteya (d) Brahmacharya
Answer: (d)
Solution: Parshvanatha taught four vows — Ahimsa, Satya, Asteya and Aparigraha; Mahavira added Brahmacharya (celibacy) as the fifth. The trap is assuming all five vows came from Parshvanatha.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Themes in Indian History Part I

111. The capital of the Magadha kingdom was shifted from Rajgriha to Pataliputra during the reign of:
(a) Udayin (b) Ajatashatru (c) Bimbisara (d) Shishunaga
Answer: (a)
Solution: Udayin, son of Ajatashatru, founded Pataliputra at the confluence of the Ganga and Son and shifted the capital there. Ajatashatru only fortified the village of Pataligrama — a subtle chronological trap.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Themes in Indian History Part I

112. Alexander's invasion of India took place in:
(a) 356 BCE (b) 261 BCE (c) 305 BCE (d) 326 BCE
Answer: (d)
Solution: Alexander crossed into the Indian subcontinent in 326 BCE and fought Porus at the Battle of the Hydaspes (Jhelum). The year 305 BCE marks Seleucus Nikator's conflict with Chandragupta Maurya.

113. Megasthenes, the author of 'Indica', was an ambassador of Seleucus Nikator at the court of:
(a) Ashoka (b) Bindusara (c) Chandragupta Maurya (d) Dasharatha
Answer: (c)
Solution: Megasthenes visited the court of Chandragupta Maurya at Pataliputra around 302 BCE. Deimachus came to Bindusara's court — a similar-name trap used by examiners.

114. The Kalinga War, which transformed Ashoka's policies, was fought in which regnal year of Ashoka?
(a) 4th year (b) 8th year (c) 12th year (d) 26th year
Answer: (b)
Solution: Major Rock Edict XIII states the Kalinga War occurred in the 8th regnal year (c. 261 BCE). The 12th year is when many major rock edicts were inscribed — a frequent confusion.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Themes in Indian History Part I

115. Ashoka's inscriptions were deciphered for the first time by:
(a) William Jones (b) Max Muller (c) James Prinsep (d) Alexander Cunningham
Answer: (c)
Solution: James Prinsep deciphered the Brahmi script of Ashokan edicts in 1837. Alexander Cunningham founded the Archaeological Survey of India — examiners often swap these two names.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Themes in Indian History Part I

116. Which Ashokan inscription mentions the five contemporary Hellenistic (Yavana) kings?
(a) Major Rock Edict XIII (b) Major Rock Edict II (c) Pillar Edict VII (d) Minor Rock Edict I
Answer: (a)
Solution: Major Rock Edict XIII names Antiochus II of Syria, Ptolemy II of Egypt, Antigonus of Macedon, Magas of Cyrene and Alexander of Epirus, besides describing the Kalinga War.

117. The Indo-Greek king Menander (Milinda) is famous for his dialogue with the Buddhist monk:
(a) Upagupta (b) Vasumitra (c) Nagasena (d) Ashvaghosha
Answer: (c)
Solution: The Milindapanho records the dialogue between King Milinda (Menander) and monk Nagasena. Ashvaghosha was associated with Kanishka's court — a similar-context trap.

118. Kanishka is credited with starting which era in 78 CE?
(a) Vikrama Samvat (b) Gupta Era (c) Saka Era (d) Kalachuri Era
Answer: (c)
Solution: The Saka Era beginning 78 CE is associated with Kanishka's accession and is used in India's national calendar. Vikrama Samvat begins in 57 BCE — a classic date trap.

119. The Fourth Buddhist Council, which split Buddhism into Hinayana and Mahayana, was held under:
(a) Ashoka (b) Kanishka (c) Harshavardhana (d) Kalasoka
Answer: (b)
Solution: The Fourth Council met at Kundalvana (Kashmir) under Kanishka, presided over by Vasumitra. Ashoka patronized the Third Council at Pataliputra — a frequent pairing trap.

120. The Sangam literature of ancient Tamilakam was composed in which language?
(a) Tamil (b) Malayalam (c) Kannada (d) Telugu
Answer: (a)
Solution: Sangam literature is a corpus of ancient Tamil texts composed in assemblies (sangams) patronized by the Pandya kings at Madurai. It includes the Ettutogai and Pattuppattu collections.

121. Consider the following statements about the Harappan Civilization:
1. The Harappans knew the use of iron for tools.
2. The Harappan script was at times written in the boustrophedon style.
3. The Harappans worshipped the Mother Goddess.
Which of the statements given above is/are correct?
(a) 1 and 2 only (b) 2 and 3 only (c) 1 and 3 only (d) 1, 2 and 3
Answer: (b)
Solution: Statement 1 is the trap: the Harappans were a Bronze Age culture and did NOT know iron. Statements 2 and 3 are correct — seals show boustrophedon writing and terracotta Mother Goddess figurines are well attested.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Themes in Indian History Part I

122. Arrange the following rulers in correct chronological order:
1. Bimbisara
2. Mahapadma Nanda
3. Chandragupta Maurya
4. Ashoka
(a) 2, 1, 3, 4 (b) 2, 3, 1, 4 (c) 1, 2, 3, 4 (d) 1, 3, 2, 4
Answer: (c)
Solution: Bimbisara (Haryanka dynasty) came first, followed by Mahapadma Nanda, then Chandragupta Maurya who overthrew the Nandas, and finally his grandson Ashoka. The Nanda–Maurya sequence is a common chronological trap.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Themes in Indian History Part I

123. Consider the following pairs of travellers and the rulers they visited:
1. Megasthenes — Chandragupta Maurya
2. Fa-Hien — Chandragupta II
3. Hiuen Tsang — Harshavardhana
Which of the pairs given above is/are correctly matched?
(a) 1 and 2 only (b) 1, 2 and 3 (c) 2 and 3 only (d) 1 only
Answer: (b)
Solution: All three pairs are correct: Megasthenes (c. 302 BCE), Fa-Hien (early 5th century CE, during Chandragupta II's reign) and Hiuen Tsang (7th century CE, at Harsha's court). The trap is doubting pair 2 because Fa-Hien never names Chandragupta II, but his visit falls within that reign.

124. Which of the following statements about the Arthashastra is correct?
(a) It is a biography of Harshavardhana. (b) It was written by Banabhatta in Sanskrit prose. (c) It is traditionally attributed to Kautilya and deals with statecraft, economy and military strategy. (d) It was composed during the Gupta period in Prakrit.
Answer: (c)
Solution: The Arthashastra is traditionally attributed to Kautilya (Chanakya/Vishnugupta) and covers statecraft, taxation and espionage. The Banabhatta and Harshavardhana options describe the Harshacharita — a deliberate similar-work trap.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Themes in Indian History Part I

125. Consider the following statements about Jainism:
1. Parshvanatha was the 23rd Tirthankara.
2. The first Jain Council was held at Pataliputra under Sthulabhadra.
3. Jainism recognizes the authority of the Vedas.
Which of the statements given above is/are correct?
(a) 1, 2 and 3 (b) 2 and 3 only (c) 1 only (d) 1 and 2 only
Answer: (d)
Solution: Statement 3 is the trap: Jainism, like Buddhism, rejected Vedic authority. Parshvanatha was indeed the 23rd Tirthankara, and the first Jain Council at Pataliputra (c. 300 BCE) under Sthulabhadra compiled the Jain canon.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Themes in Indian History Part I
