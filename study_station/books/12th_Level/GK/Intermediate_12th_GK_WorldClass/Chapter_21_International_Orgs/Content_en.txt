# International Organizations

International organizations are bodies formed by agreement between countries to work together on common goals — peace, health, trade, finance, and development. Questions on their headquarters, establishment years, member numbers, and heads appear regularly in SSC, Railway, Bank, and State exams. This chapter covers the organizations most frequently asked about.

## 1. The United Nations (UN)

- **Established:** 24 October 1945 (this date is celebrated as UN Day), after the Second World War. The UN Charter was signed on 26 June 1945 in San Francisco.
- **Headquarters:** New York, USA.
- **Predecessor:** The League of Nations (founded 1920, dissolved 1946), which failed to prevent the Second World War.
- **Members:** 193 member states.
- **Official languages:** Six — English, French, Spanish, Russian, Chinese, and Arabic.

### The six principal organs of the UN

| Organ | Headquarters / Seat | Function |
|---|---|---|
| General Assembly | New York | Main deliberative body; all 193 members have one vote each |
| Security Council | New York | Maintains international peace and security |
| ECOSOC (Economic and Social Council) | New York | Coordinates economic and social work |
| Trusteeship Council | New York | Supervised trust territories; suspended operations in 1994 after its work was completed |
| International Court of Justice (ICJ) | The Hague, Netherlands | Principal judicial organ; settles disputes between states |
| Secretariat | New York | Administrative body, headed by the Secretary-General |

**Exam tip:** The ICJ is the only principal organ *not* located in New York — this is a favourite trap question.

### The Security Council

- **15 members:** 5 permanent (P5) — USA, UK, France, Russia, China — and 10 non-permanent members elected for two-year terms.
- Each permanent member holds **veto power**: a single negative vote by a P5 member can block any substantive resolution.
- India has served multiple terms as a non-permanent member and seeks permanent membership.

### The Secretary-General

- Chief administrative officer of the UN, appointed by the General Assembly on the recommendation of the Security Council, for a five-year renewable term.
- The first Secretary-General was **Trygve Lie** of Norway. **António Guterres** of Portugal has held the office since 2017.
- India's **Vijayalakshmi Pandit** was the first woman President of the UN General Assembly (1953).

## 2. Major UN Agencies

| Agency | Full form | Founded | Headquarters |
|---|---|---|---|
| WHO | World Health Organization | 1948 | Geneva, Switzerland |
| UNESCO | UN Educational, Scientific and Cultural Organization | 1945 | Paris, France |
| UNICEF | UN Children's Fund | 1946 | New York, USA |
| FAO | Food and Agriculture Organization | 1945 | Rome, Italy |
| ILO | International Labour Organization | 1919 | Geneva, Switzerland |
| UNHCR | UN High Commissioner for Refugees | 1950 | Geneva, Switzerland |
| WFP | World Food Programme | 1961 | Rome, Italy |
| IAEA | International Atomic Energy Agency | 1957 | Vienna, Austria |
| UNEP | UN Environment Programme | 1972 | Nairobi, Kenya |
| IMF | International Monetary Fund | 1944 | Washington, D.C., USA |
| World Bank (IBRD) | International Bank for Reconstruction and Development | 1944 | Washington, D.C., USA |

**Key facts to remember:**

- **ILO (1919)** is the oldest — it was created along with the League of Nations and later became the first specialised agency of the UN. It is the only tripartite UN body, bringing together governments, employers, and workers.
- **WHO's** founding day, 7 April, is observed as World Health Day.
- **UNICEF** was originally created in 1946 to help children affected by the Second World War.
- The **World Food Programme** received the Nobel Peace Prize in 2020; **UNHCR** received it twice (1954, 1981); **UNICEF** in 1965; the **ILO** in 1969.

## 3. The Bretton Woods Twins: IMF and World Bank

Both were conceived at the **Bretton Woods Conference (1944)** in New Hampshire, USA — hence the name "Bretton Woods twins."

- **IMF (1944, Washington, D.C.):** Promotes international monetary cooperation and exchange-rate stability, and provides short-term financial assistance to countries facing balance-of-payments crises. It publishes the *World Economic Outlook* report.
- **World Bank (1944, Washington, D.C.):** Provides long-term loans and grants for development projects — infrastructure, education, health, poverty reduction. Its original institution is the **IBRD**; together with the **IDA** (International Development Association) it forms the core of the World Bank Group.

**Exam trap:** The IMF gives short-term help for balance-of-payments problems; the World Bank gives long-term development loans. Examiners often swap these roles in options.

## 4. World Trade Organization (WTO)

- **Established:** 1 January 1995, replacing the **GATT** (General Agreement on Tariffs and Trade, 1948).
- **Headquarters:** Geneva, Switzerland.
- **Function:** Administers global trade agreements, settles trade disputes between members, and provides a forum for trade negotiations.
- India is a founding member of the WTO and was also a member of GATT.

## 5. Other Important Organizations

| Organization | Founded | Headquarters | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| NATO (North Atlantic Treaty Organization) | 1949 | Brussels, Belgium | Military alliance of North American and European countries |
| European Union (EU) | 1993 (Maastricht Treaty) | Brussels, Belgium | Political and economic union of European countries |
| ASEAN (Association of Southeast Asian Nations) | 1967 | Jakarta, Indonesia | 10 Southeast Asian members |
| SAARC (South Asian Association for Regional Cooperation) | 1985 | Kathmandu, Nepal | 8 members; India is a founding member |
| BRICS | First summit 2009 | — (New Development Bank HQ: Shanghai) | Brazil, Russia, India, China, South Africa; expanded in 2024 |
| OPEC (Organization of the Petroleum Exporting Countries) | 1960 | Vienna, Austria | Coordinates petroleum policies of major oil exporters |
| Commonwealth of Nations | 1949 (modern form) | London, UK | Mostly former British territories; India is a member |
| Amnesty International | 1961 | London, UK | Human rights NGO; Nobel Peace Prize 1977 |
| Red Cross (ICRC) | 1863 | Geneva, Switzerland | Humanitarian aid; founded by Henry Dunant |
| Interpol | 1923 | Lyon, France | International police cooperation |
| ADB (Asian Development Bank) | 1966 | Manila, Philippines | Development finance for Asia-Pacific |
| AIIB (Asian Infrastructure Investment Bank) | 2016 | Beijing, China | India is a founding member |
| NDB (New Development Bank) | 2015 | Shanghai, China | Set up by BRICS countries |

**SAARC members (8):** Afghanistan, Bangladesh, Bhutan, India, Maldives, Nepal, Pakistan, Sri Lanka. Its secretariat is in **Kathmandu, Nepal** — a frequently asked fact.

## 6. India and International Organizations

- India is a **founding member** of the UN (1945), even before independence.
- India has contributed troops to UN peacekeeping missions for decades and is among the largest contributors.
- India is a member of the Commonwealth, SAARC, BRICS, G20, WTO, IMF, World Bank, ADB, and AIIB.
- India hosted the **G20 Summit in New Delhi in 2023** during its presidency.

## 7. How Exams Test This Chapter

- **Headquarters matching:** "Where is the headquarters of UNESCO?" (Paris) — the single most common question type.
- **Establishment years:** UN (1945), WTO (1995), ILO (1919), NATO (1949), SAARC (1985).
- **Odd-one-out:** Which of these is *not* a UN agency? (e.g., NATO, WTO, Amnesty International are not UN specialised agencies.)
- **Traps:** ICJ at The Hague (not New York); IMF vs World Bank roles swapped; GATT (1948) vs WTO (1995) confused; ILO's founding year (1919, before the UN itself).
- **Nobel Peace Prizes** to UN bodies (WFP 2020, UNHCR, UNICEF, ILO) appear in current-affairs-linked questions.

**Memory aid — Geneva cluster:** WHO, ILO, WTO, UNHCR, ICRC, and WIPO are all headquartered in **Geneva, Switzerland**. If an exam option says Geneva for any of these, it is correct.

**Memory aid — Washington, D.C. pair:** IMF and World Bank. **Rome pair:** FAO and WFP. **Paris:** UNESCO. **Vienna:** IAEA, OPEC.
