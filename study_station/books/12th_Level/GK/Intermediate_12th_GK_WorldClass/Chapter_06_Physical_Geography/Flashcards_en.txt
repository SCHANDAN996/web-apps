Card 1
Front: Which is the highest peak of India, and in which range does it lie?
Back: Kangchenjunga (8,586 m), in the Himalayas (on the India–Nepal border). Note: K2 (Godwin-Austen) lies in the Karakoram, in the Pakistan-occupied region — a common trap.

Card 2
Front: Into which three parallel ranges is the Himalaya divided from north to south?
Back: Himadri (Greater Himalaya), Himachal (Lesser Himalaya), and Shiwalik (Outer Himalaya).

Card 3
Front: What are the flat-floored valleys of the Shiwaliks called?
Back: Duns (e.g., Dehradun, Kotli Dun, Patli Dun).

Card 4
Front: Which is the largest physiographic division of India by area?
Back: The Peninsular Plateau — the oldest and most stable landmass of India.

Card 5
Front: Which river flows through a rift valley between the Vindhya and Satpura ranges?
Back: The Narmada — it flows westward into the Arabian Sea (Gulf of Khambhat).

Card 6
Front: Mnemonic trick: How do you remember the Himalayan ranges from north to south?
Back: "Hi-Hi-Shi" — Himadri, Himachal, Shiwalik (from highest/innermost to lowest/outermost).

Card 7
Front: Which is the highest peak of the Peninsular India (Western Ghats)?
Back: Anai Mudi (2,695 m) in Kerala.

Card 8
Front: What is the local name of the Western Ghats in Maharashtra, Karnataka, and Kerala–Tamil Nadu?
Back: Sahyadri (Maharashtra), and further south they are called the Nilgiri, Anaimalai, and Cardamom hills.

Card 9
Front: Examiner's trap: Is the Deccan Plateau bounded by the Eastern Ghats on the east as a continuous range?
Back: No — the Eastern Ghats are discontinuous and broken by rivers (Mahanadi, Godavari, Krishna, Kaveri), unlike the continuous Western Ghats.

Card 10
Front: Which is the largest saltwater lake in India, and where is it?
Back: Chilika Lake, in Odisha (a coastal lagoon at the mouth of the Daya River).

Card 11
Front: Which plain is formed by the Indus, Ganga, and Brahmaputra river systems?
Back: The Northern Plains (Indo-Gangetic-Brahmaputra Plains) — made of fertile alluvium.

Card 12
Front: What is the difference between 'Bhangar' and 'Khadar'?
Back: Bhangar = older alluvium (higher terraces, less fertile); Khadar = newer alluvium (floodplains, renewed every year, very fertile).

Card 13
Front: Mnemonic trick: How do you remember the west-flowing rivers of Peninsular India?
Back: "Na-Ta-Ma" — Narmada, Tapi (Tapti), Mahi — the major rivers flowing into the Arabian Sea.

Card 14
Front: Which is the only major river of India that flows westward through a rift valley besides the Narmada?
Back: The Tapi (Tapti) — it also flows through a rift valley between the Satpura and Ajanta ranges.

Card 15
Front: Examiner's trap: Which is the southernmost point of the Indian mainland — Kanyakumari or Indira Point?
Back: Kanyakumari is the southernmost point of the mainland; Indira Point (Great Nicobar) is the southernmost point of India's territory — and it submerged partly after the 2004 tsunami.

Card 16
Front: Which plateau is called the "Ruhr of India"?
Back: The Chota Nagpur Plateau — because of its rich mineral (coal, iron ore) deposits.

Card 17
Front: What is the name of the coastal plain between the Western Ghats and the Arabian Sea?
Back: The Konkan coast (north) and the Malabar coast (south, Kerala).

Card 18
Front: Which pass connects Srinagar with Leh?
Back: Zoji La (in the Zaskar range region of Ladakh).

Card 19
Front: Which island group of India is of coral origin, and which is of volcanic origin?
Back: Lakshadweep = coral islands; Andaman & Nicobar = volcanic/elevated islands (Barren Island has India's only active volcano).

Card 20
Front: What is the approximate latitudinal and longitudinal extent of mainland India?
Back: About 8°4′N to 37°6′N latitude and 68°7′E to 97°25′E longitude; the Tropic of Cancer (23°30′N) passes through 8 states.
