51. Which of the following statements about the Deccan Plateau is correct?
(a) It slopes from west to east (b) It is composed mainly of alluvial deposits (c) It is bounded by the Satpura Range in the south (d) Its highest peak is Anai Mudi
Answer: (a)
Solution: The tempting wrong option is Anai Mudi — it is the highest peak of South India but lies in the Western Ghats (Anaimalai Hills), not on the Deccan Plateau proper. The Deccan Plateau is a triangular landmass of old crystalline rock, bounded by the Satpuras in the north, and most of its rivers flow eastward into the Bay of Bengal, showing the plateau slopes from west to east, so the answer is that it slopes from west to east.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Geography (India Physical Environment)

52. The Barren Island, India's only active volcano, is located in which of the following?
(a) Lakshadweep Sea (b) Gulf of Mannar (c) Andaman Sea (d) Palk Strait
Answer: (c)
Solution: Many students confuse Barren Island with Narcondam Island — both are volcanic islands of the Andaman group, but Narcondam is a dormant volcano while Barren Island is the only active one. It lies in the Andaman Sea, east of the Great Andaman group, so the answer is the Andaman Sea.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Geography (India Physical Environment)

53. Which river is known as the "Sorrow of Bengal"?
(a) Kosi (b) Damodar (c) Hooghly (d) Teesta
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap here is Kosi, which is called the "Sorrow of Bihar" because of its frequent course changes and floods — a classic similar-sounding confusion. The Damodar, flowing through Jharkhand and West Bengal, earned the name "Sorrow of Bengal" due to its devastating floods before the Damodar Valley Corporation tamed it, so the answer is Damodar.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Geography (India Physical Environment)

54. The Nanda Devi peak is located in which state?
(a) Sikkim (b) Himachal Pradesh (c) Uttarakhand (d) Arunachal Pradesh
Answer: (c)
Solution: Sikkim is a tempting option because it contains Kangchenjunga, India's highest peak, and students often mix up the two famous Himalayan peaks. Nanda Devi (7,816 m), the second highest peak in India and the highest entirely within India, lies in the Kumaon Himalayas of Uttarakhand, so the answer is Uttarakhand.

55. Which of the following pairs of rivers and their origins is correctly matched?
(a) Godavari – Mahabaleshwar (b) Krishna – Trimbakeshwar (c) Kaveri – Talakaveri (d) Narmada – Mahabaleshwar
Answer: (c)
Solution: This question deliberately swaps the origins of Godavari and Krishna to trap students: Godavari rises at Trimbakeshwar near Nashik, while Krishna rises at Mahabaleshwar. The Kaveri rises at Talakaveri in the Brahmagiri hills of Kodagu district, Karnataka, so the answer is Kaveri – Talakaveri.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Geography (India Physical Environment)

56. The "Duns" are:
(a) Deep gorges cut by Himalayan rivers (b) High-altitude passes of the Trans-Himalaya (c) Glacial lakes of the Greater Himalaya (d) Longitudinal valleys lying between the Lesser Himalaya and the Shiwaliks
Answer: (d)
Solution: Students often confuse Duns with river gorges; gorges are cut by rivers across ranges, whereas Duns are structural longitudinal valleys. Duns such as Dehra Dun, Kotli Dun and Patli Dun lie between the Lesser Himalaya and the Shiwalik range, formed by the deposition of material in the longitudinal depression, so the answer is longitudinal valleys between the Lesser Himalaya and the Shiwaliks.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Geography (India Physical Environment)

57. Which of the following rivers does NOT form a delta?
(a) Mahanadi (b) Godavari (c) Narmada (d) Krishna
Answer: (c)
Solution: The tempting error is to assume all major Indian rivers form deltas. East-flowing rivers like the Mahanadi, Godavari and Krishna build large deltas on the Bay of Bengal coast, but the west-flowing Narmada flows swiftly through a rift valley and forms an estuary instead, so the answer is Narmada.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Geography (India Physical Environment)

58. The Tropic of Cancer passes through how many Indian states?
(a) Six (b) Nine (c) Seven (d) Eight
Answer: (d)
Solution: A common trap is to count seven states by forgetting Mizoram or by wrongly including Bihar or Odisha. The Tropic of Cancer passes through Gujarat, Rajasthan, Madhya Pradesh, Chhattisgarh, Jharkhand, West Bengal, Tripura and Mizoram — eight states in all, so the answer is eight.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Geography (India Physical Environment)

59. Which Himalayan range lies between the Zaskar and the Karakoram ranges?
(a) Pir Panjal (b) Shiwalik (c) Dhauladhar (d) Ladakh
Answer: (d)
Solution: Pir Panjal and Dhauladhar are tempting because they are well-known ranges, but both belong to the Lesser Himalaya, far south of the Zaskar. From south to north in the Trans-Himalayan region the order is Zaskar, Ladakh and Karakoram, so the range between the Zaskar and the Karakoram is the Ladakh range, and the answer is Ladakh.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Geography (India Physical Environment)

60. The Bhander, Rewa and Kaimur series of rocks belong to which system?
(a) Dharwar system (b) Cuddapah system (c) Vindhyan system (d) Gondwana system
Answer: (c)
Solution: The Gondwana system is a frequent wrong guess because it is the most famous for coal, but these series are sedimentary formations of ancient age without coal seams. The Vindhyan system includes the Bhander, Rewa and Kaimur series, known for sandstone, limestone and building stones, so the answer is the Vindhyan system.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Geography (India Physical Environment)

61. Which of the following statements about the Brahmaputra river system is correct?
(a) It enters India through Arunachal Pradesh under the name Siang (b) It originates from the Gangotri glacier (c) It flows westward after entering Bangladesh (d) Its largest tributary is the Teesta
Answer: (a)
Solution: The Gangotri glacier option is a trap — it is the source of the Bhagirathi (Ganga), not the Brahmaputra, which rises near Lake Mansarovar from the Chemayungdung glacier. The Brahmaputra enters India in Arunachal Pradesh where it is called the Siang (or Dihang), so the answer is that it enters India through Arunachal Pradesh under the name Siang.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Geography (India Physical Environment)

62. The Malwa Plateau is primarily composed of which type of rock?
(a) Basaltic lava (Deccan Trap) (b) Granite and gneiss (c) Sandstone (d) Limestone
Answer: (a)
Solution: Granite and gneiss tempt students who associate all plateaus with the Archaean basement, but the Malwa Plateau is part of the lava-covered Deccan region. It is built of Deccan Trap basalt, which weathers into fertile black (regur) soil, so the answer is basaltic lava (Deccan Trap).
Source: NCERT Class 11 Geography (India Physical Environment)

63. Which pass connects the Kashmir Valley with the outer plains?
(a) Zoji La (b) Nathu La (c) Banihal (d) Shipki La
Answer: (c)
Solution: Zoji La is the classic trap — it connects Srinagar with Leh (Ladakh), not with the outer plains. The Banihal pass, through the Pir Panjal range (with the Jawahar Tunnel/Banihal-Qazigund tunnel on it), links the Kashmir Valley with Jammu and the plains, so the answer is Banihal.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Geography (India Physical Environment)

64. The largest brackish water lagoon in India, Chilika Lake, is located in which state?
(a) Odisha (b) West Bengal (c) Andhra Pradesh (d) Kerala
Answer: (a)
Solution: Kerala is tempting because of its famous backwaters like Vembanad, but Vembanad is a freshwater-dominated backwater system, whereas Chilika is a brackish coastal lagoon. Chilika Lake, at the mouth of the Daya river, lies on the coast of Odisha, so the answer is Odisha.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Geography (India Physical Environment)

65. Which of the following is the correct north-to-south sequence of Himalayan divisions?
(a) Shiwalik – Lesser Himalaya – Greater Himalaya – Trans-Himalaya (b) Trans-Himalaya – Greater Himalaya – Lesser Himalaya – Shiwalik (c) Greater Himalaya – Lesser Himalaya – Shiwalik – Trans-Himalaya (d) Lesser Himalaya – Greater Himalaya – Trans-Himalaya – Shiwalik
Answer: (b)
Solution: Option one reverses the order by starting from the plains, which is the most common sequence confusion in this topic. Moving from north to south, the sequence is Trans-Himalaya (Karakoram, Ladakh, Zaskar), then the Greater Himalaya (Himadri), then the Lesser Himalaya, and finally the Shiwaliks adjoining the plains, so the answer is Trans-Himalaya – Greater Himalaya – Lesser Himalaya – Shiwalik.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Geography (India Physical Environment)

66. The river Luni, which drains into the Rann of Kutch, is an example of:
(a) A consequent river (b) A superimposed river (c) An antecedent river (d) An inland drainage river
Answer: (d)
Solution: Antecedent rivers like the Indus and Sutlej are a tempting wrong choice because they are frequently asked together, but they cut across rising mountains and reach the sea. The Luni originates in the Aravallis and ends in the marshy Rann of Kutch without reaching the sea, making it a classic case of inland drainage, so the answer is an inland drainage river.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Geography (India Physical Environment)

67. The Anai Mudi peak, the highest in South India, is situated in which hill range?
(a) Anaimalai Hills (b) Cardamom Hills (c) Nilgiri Hills (d) Palani Hills
Answer: (a)
Solution: The Nilgiri Hills are tempting because they contain Doddabetta, the second highest peak of South India, and students often swap the two. Anai Mudi (2,695 m) stands in the Anaimalai Hills of Kerala, part of the Western Ghats, so the answer is the Anaimalai Hills.

68. Which of the following rivers is a tributary of the Indus?
(a) Jhelum (b) Chambal (c) Betwa (d) Son
Answer: (a)
Solution: Chambal, Betwa and Son are placed as traps because all are famous tributaries — but of the Ganga system (Chambal and Betwa join the Yamuna; the Son joins the Ganga). The Jhelum is one of the five rivers of Punjab that join the Indus, so the answer is Jhelum.

69. The "Karewa" formations of the Kashmir Valley are famous for the cultivation of:
(a) Saffron (b) Tea (c) Coffee (d) Rubber
Answer: (a)
Solution: Tea is a tempting option because Kashmir is a Himalayan region and students associate Himalayan slopes with the tea gardens of Darjeeling and Assam. Karewas are lacustrine (lake) deposits of the Kashmir Valley whose soil is ideal for saffron (kesar) cultivation, so the answer is saffron.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Geography (India Physical Environment)

70. Which of the following statements about the Western Coastal Plain is correct?
(a) It is wider than the Eastern Coastal Plain (b) It is a submerged coast with estuaries (c) It has extensive deltas of the Godavari and Krishna (d) It extends from the Rann of Kutch to Kanyakumari as a single uniform plain
Answer: (b)
Solution: The delta option is the trap — the Godavari and Krishna deltas lie on the Eastern Coastal Plain, not the western one. The Western Coastal Plain is narrow, lies between the Western Ghats and the Arabian Sea, and is largely a submerged coast where rivers like the Narmada and Tapi form estuaries, so the answer is that it is a submerged coast with estuaries.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Geography (India Physical Environment)

71. The Gurushikhar peak, the highest point of the Aravalli Range, is located in:
(a) Gujarat (b) Rajasthan (c) Haryana (d) Madhya Pradesh
Answer: (b)
Solution: Gujarat is a frequent wrong answer because the Aravallis extend into Gujarat, but the range's highest section lies elsewhere. Gurushikhar (1,722 m) is on Mount Abu in the Sirohi district of Rajasthan, so the answer is Rajasthan.

72. Which is the largest tributary of the Godavari river?
(a) Manjira (b) Pranhita (c) Sabari (d) Penganga
Answer: (b)
Solution: The Indravati and Manjira are tempting because they are large, well-known Godavari tributaries, but neither carries the greatest volume. The Pranhita, formed by the confluence of the Wardha, Wainganga and Penganga, contributes the largest share of water to the Godavari, so the answer is Pranhita.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Geography (India Physical Environment)

73. The "Bhangar" and "Khadar" refer to:
(a) Types of black soil (b) Terraces of the Brahmaputra valley (c) Coastal sand dunes (d) Old and new alluvium of the northern plains
Answer: (d)
Solution: Students sometimes confuse Khadar with Karewa (Kashmir's lake deposits) — a similar-name trap. In the northern plains, Bhangar is the older alluvium lying above flood level, often with calcareous kankar nodules, while Khadar is the newer alluvium of the floodplains renewed almost every year, so the answer is old and new alluvium of the northern plains.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Geography (India Physical Environment)

74. Which of the following statements about the Lakshadweep islands is correct?
(a) They are of volcanic origin (b) They are coral islands (c) They are an extension of the Aravalli range (d) They lie in the Bay of Bengal
Answer: (b)
Solution: The volcanic-origin option is the trap — that description fits Barren and Narcondam islands in the Andaman group, not Lakshadweep. The Lakshadweep islands in the Arabian Sea are built of coral deposits (atolls and reefs), so the answer is that they are coral islands.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Geography (India Physical Environment)

75. The Pench, Kanhan and Wardha are tributaries of which river?
(a) Mahanadi (b) Tapi (c) Narmada (d) Godavari
Answer: (d)
Solution: The Narmada is a tempting guess because these rivers flow in central India near the Narmada basin, but the Narmada's tributaries are short streams like the Hiran and Tawa. The Pench, Kanhan and Wardha drain the Satpura–Mahadeo–Maikal region and join the Wainganga–Pranhita system of the Godavari, so the answer is Godavari.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Geography (India Physical Environment)
