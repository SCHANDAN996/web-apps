Questions 1–20: Easy | Questions 21–25: Medium

1. The Tropic of Cancer passes through how many Indian states?
(a) 6 (b) 8 (c) 7 (d) 9
Answer: (b)
Solution: Many students count only 7 states and forget that the Tropic of Cancer also passes through Mizoram, which is the trap. It actually passes through Gujarat, Rajasthan, Madhya Pradesh, Chhattisgarh, Jharkhand, West Bengal, Tripura and Mizoram, so the answer is 8.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Geography (India: Physical Environment)

2. Which is the northernmost range of the Himalayas?
(a) Himachal (b) Purvanchal (c) Shiwalik (d) Himadri
Answer: (d)
Solution: The Shiwalik is the southernmost range, which is the common confusion. The Himalayas from north to south are: Himadri (Greater Himalaya), Himachal (Lesser Himalaya) and Shiwalik (Outer Himalaya), so the answer is Himadri.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Geography (India: Physical Environment)

3. The highest peak of India, Kanchenjunga, is located in which state?
(a) Uttarakhand (b) Himachal Pradesh (c) Sikkim (d) Arunachal Pradesh
Answer: (c)
Solution: Students often pick Uttarakhand thinking of Nanda Devi or Kamet, which is the trap. Kanchenjunga (8,586 m), India's highest peak, lies in Sikkim on the India–Nepal border, so the answer is Sikkim.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Geography (India: Physical Environment)

4. Which river is known as the "Sorrow of Bihar"?
(a) Kosi (b) Ganga (c) Gandak (d) Son
Answer: (a)
Solution: The Damodar is called the "Sorrow of Bengal", which students mix up with Bihar — that is the trap. The Kosi, notorious for frequent course changes and floods, is called the "Sorrow of Bihar", so the answer is Kosi.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Geography (India: Physical Environment)

5. The Deccan Plateau is bounded on the west by which mountain range?
(a) Western Ghats (b) Eastern Ghats (c) Aravalli (d) Satpura
Answer: (a)
Solution: The Eastern Ghats bound the plateau on the east, not the west — a frequent directional confusion. The Deccan Plateau lies between the Western Ghats in the west and the Eastern Ghats in the east, so the answer is Western Ghats.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Geography (India: Physical Environment)

6. Which is the largest river island in India, located in the Brahmaputra?
(a) Divar Island (b) Srirangapatna (c) Majuli (d) Sagar Island
Answer: (c)
Solution: Sagar Island in the Hooghly is famous for the Gangasagar Mela and is a tempting wrong choice. Majuli in Assam, formed by the Brahmaputra, is India's largest river island, so the answer is Majuli.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Geography (India: Physical Environment)

7. The Aravalli range is an example of which type of mountain?
(a) Young fold mountain (b) Residual (relict) mountain (c) Volcanic mountain (d) Block mountain
Answer: (b)
Solution: The Himalayas are young fold mountains, and students often tag all Indian ranges the same way — that is the trap. The Aravallis are among the oldest fold mountains, now worn down by erosion into residual mountains, so the answer is residual (relict) mountain.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Geography (India: Physical Environment)

8. Which river does NOT flow into the Bay of Bengal?
(a) Mahanadi (b) Godavari (c) Narmada (d) Krishna
Answer: (c)
Solution: Godavari and Krishna are both east-flowing peninsular rivers, so they look plausible as answers. The Narmada flows westward through a rift valley into the Arabian Sea (Gulf of Khambhat), so the answer is Narmada.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Geography (India: Physical Environment)

9. The standard meridian of India (82°30' E) passes through which city?
(a) Allahabad (Prayagraj) (b) Varanasi (c) Patna (d) Bhopal
Answer: (a)
Solution: Varanasi is close to the meridian and is a tempting distractor. The Indian Standard Time meridian passes through Mirzapur near Allahabad (Prayagraj) in Uttar Pradesh, so the answer is Allahabad (Prayagraj).
Source: NCERT Class 11 Geography (India: Physical Environment)

10. Which is the highest peak of the Western Ghats?
(a) Anamudi (b) Doddabetta (c) Mahendragiri (d) Kalsubai
Answer: (a)
Solution: Doddabetta is the highest peak of the Nilgiri Hills, which confuses many students. Anamudi (2,695 m) in Kerala's Anaimalai Hills is the highest peak of the Western Ghats and of South India, so the answer is Anamudi.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Geography (India: Physical Environment)

11. The Lakshadweep islands are of which origin?
(a) Volcanic (b) Tectonic (c) Coral (d) Alluvial
Answer: (c)
Solution: The Andaman and Nicobar islands are partly of volcanic/tectonic origin, and students generalise this to all Indian islands — the trap. Lakshadweep islands are built of coral deposits, so the answer is coral.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Geography (India: Physical Environment)

12. Which river forms the famous Dhuandhar Falls near Jabalpur?
(a) Tapi (b) Narmada (c) Chambal (d) Betwa
Answer: (b)
Solution: The Chambal is also a Madhya Pradesh river and tempts students. The Narmada plunges through marble rocks at Bhedaghat near Jabalpur forming the Dhuandhar Falls, so the answer is Narmada.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Geography (India: Physical Environment)

13. The "Duns" are found in which region?
(a) Between Himadri and Himachal (b) In the Purvanchal hills (c) South of Shiwalik (d) Between Himachal and Shiwalik
Answer: (d)
Solution: Students often place duns south of the Shiwaliks, which is wrong. Duns are longitudinal valleys lying between the Lesser Himalaya (Himachal) and the Shiwaliks, e.g. Dehradun, so the answer is between Himachal and Shiwalik.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Geography (India: Physical Environment)

14. Which state shares its border with the maximum number of other Indian states?
(a) Madhya Pradesh (b) Uttar Pradesh (c) Maharashtra (d) Assam
Answer: (b)
Solution: Madhya Pradesh is central and looks like the obvious answer — the classic trap. Uttar Pradesh touches 8 states plus Delhi (the most), so the answer is Uttar Pradesh.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Geography (India: Physical Environment)

15. The Godavari is often called by which name because of its large size?
(a) Dakshin Ganga (b) Both of these names are used (c) Vridha Ganga (d) Neither name is used
Answer: (b)
Solution: Students who know only one name pick it and miss that both are standard. The Godavari, the largest peninsular river, is called both Dakshin Ganga (Ganga of the South) and Vridha Ganga (Old Ganga), so the answer is that both names are used.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Geography (India: Physical Environment)

16. Which plateau is known as the "storehouse of minerals" in India?
(a) Deccan Plateau (b) Meghalaya Plateau (c) Malwa Plateau (d) Chota Nagpur Plateau
Answer: (d)
Solution: The Deccan Plateau is vast and mineral-bearing, making it a tempting choice. The Chota Nagpur Plateau (Jharkhand region) holds India's richest concentration of coal, iron ore and mica, so the answer is Chota Nagpur Plateau.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Geography (India: Physical Environment)

17. The Palk Strait separates India from which country?
(a) Maldives (b) Myanmar (c) Bangladesh (d) Sri Lanka
Answer: (d)
Solution: The Gulf of Mannar also lies between India and Sri Lanka and confuses students about which water body is asked. The Palk Strait lies between Tamil Nadu and Sri Lanka, so the answer is Sri Lanka.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Geography (India: Physical Environment)

18. Which of the following is a west-flowing river of peninsular India?
(a) Pennar (b) Vaigai (c) Subarnarekha (d) Tapi
Answer: (d)
Solution: Pennar, Subarnarekha and Vaigai all flow east into the Bay of Bengal, so they are wrong. The Tapi (Tapti), like the Narmada, flows west through a rift valley into the Gulf of Khambhat, so the answer is Tapi.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Geography (India: Physical Environment)

19. The Barren Island, India's only active volcano, is located in which island group?
(a) Andaman and Nicobar (b) Lakshadweep (c) Gulf of Mannar (d) Sundarbans
Answer: (a)
Solution: Lakshadweep is the other major island group and is the common wrong guess. Barren Island, India's only confirmed active volcano, lies in the Andaman Sea within the Andaman and Nicobar Islands, so the answer is Andaman and Nicobar.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Geography (India: Physical Environment)

20. Which river is the largest tributary of the Ganga by volume of water?
(a) Yamuna (b) Gandak (c) Ghaghara (d) Kosi
Answer: (c)
Solution: The Yamuna is the longest tributary and the most tempting wrong answer — that is the trap. By discharge, the Ghaghara carries more water into the Ganga than any other tributary, so the answer is Ghaghara.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Geography (India: Physical Environment)

21. Consider the following statements about the Narmada river:
1. It flows through a rift valley between the Vindhya and Satpura ranges.
2. It forms an estuary before meeting the sea.
3. It is the longest peninsular river of India.
Which of the above is/are correct?
(a) 1, 2 and 3 (b) 2 and 3 only (c) 1 and 3 only (d) 1 and 2 only
Answer: (d)
Solution: Statement 3 is the trap: the Godavari, not the Narmada, is the longest peninsular river. The Narmada does flow in a rift valley between the Vindhyas and Satpuras and forms an estuary in the Gulf of Khambhat, so the answer is 1 and 2 only.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Geography (India: Physical Environment)

22. Arrange the following Himalayan peaks from west to east:
1. Nanga Parbat 2. Nanda Devi 3. Kanchenjunga 4. Namcha Barwa
(a) 2-1-3-4 (b) 1-2-3-4 (c) 1-3-2-4 (d) 2-3-1-4
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is placing Nanda Devi first because it lies in Uttarakhand, which feels western. Nanga Parbat (PoK) is the westernmost, then Nanda Devi (Uttarakhand), Kanchenjunga (Sikkim) and Namcha Barwa (Arunachal) at the eastern bend, so the answer is 1-2-3-4.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Geography (India: Physical Environment)

23. Consider the following statements about the Western and Eastern Ghats:
1. The Western Ghats are higher and more continuous than the Eastern Ghats.
2. The Eastern Ghats are dissected by rivers like the Godavari and Krishna.
3. The Western Ghats meet the Eastern Ghats at the Nilgiri Hills.
Which of the above are correct?
(a) 1 and 2 only (b) 2 and 3 only (c) 1 and 3 only (d) 1, 2 and 3
Answer: (d)
Solution: Some students reject statement 3, but the two ghats do converge at the Nilgiris — that is the trap. All three statements are factually correct, so the answer is 1, 2 and 3.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Geography (India: Physical Environment)

24. Match the rivers with their place of origin:
1. Indus — near Lake Mansarovar (Tibet)
2. Brahmaputra — Chemayungdung glacier (Tibet)
3. Ganga — Gangotri glacier
4. Yamuna — Yamunotri glacier
How many pairs are correctly matched?
(a) Only one (b) All four (c) Only three (d) Only two
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is assuming at least one origin is mis-stated, since examiners often swap Gangotri and Yamunotri. Here all four origins are correctly given, so the answer is all four.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Geography (India: Physical Environment)

25. Consider the following statements about the peninsular plateau:
1. It is composed mainly of Archaean gneisses and schists.
2. It is one of the oldest and most stable landmasses of the earth.
3. Its general slope is from east to west.
Which of the above is/are correct?
(a) 2 and 3 only (b) 1 and 2 only (c) 1 and 3 only (d) 1, 2 and 3
Answer: (b)
Solution: Statement 3 is the trap: the plateau slopes from west to east, which is why most peninsular rivers flow eastward into the Bay of Bengal. Statements 1 and 2 are correct, so the answer is 1 and 2 only.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Geography (India: Physical Environment)
