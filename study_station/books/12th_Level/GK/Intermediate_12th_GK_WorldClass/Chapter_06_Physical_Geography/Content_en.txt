# Physical Geography of India

## 🌄 The Hook: A Mountain That Is Still Growing

Every year, Mount Everest grows by a few millimetres. Why? Because the Indian plate is still pushing into the Eurasian plate — the same collision that began about 50 million years ago and created the Himalayas. India is literally a land still being built. When you understand this one story — a drifting plate, a collision, rising mountains, rivers fed by snow, plains made by those rivers — the entire physical geography of India falls into place like a puzzle.

That is exactly how we will study this chapter: one story, many facts.

## 1. India: Location and Size

- India lies entirely in the **Northern Hemisphere**, between **8°4′N and 37°6′N** latitude and **68°7′E and 97°25′E** longitude.
- The **Tropic of Cancer (23°30′N)** passes through **8 states**: Gujarat, Rajasthan, Madhya Pradesh, Chhattisgarh, Jharkhand, West Bengal, Tripura, and Mizoram.
- India is the **7th largest country** in the world by area (about **32.8 lakh sq km**) — roughly **2.4% of the world's land area**.
- **Land frontier:** about 15,200 km. **Coastline (mainland):** about 6,100 km; including islands, about **7,516.6 km**.
- The **82°30′E longitude** (passing near Mirzapur, UP) is India's **Standard Meridian** — Indian Standard Time (IST) is 5 hours 30 minutes ahead of GMT.
- North–South extent: about **3,214 km**; East–West extent: about **2,933 km**.
- The southernmost point of **mainland** India is **Kanyakumari**; the southernmost point of **Indian territory** is **Indira Point** (Great Nicobar Island) — it was partly submerged after the 2004 tsunami.

## 3. The Himalayas — The Young Fold Mountains

### How they formed
The Himalayas are **young fold mountains**, formed when the **Indian plate** collided with the **Eurasian plate**, folding up the sediments of the ancient **Tethys Sea**. They are still rising — that is why this zone is earthquake-prone.

### Three parallel ranges (North → South)

| Range | Also called | Key features |
|---|---|---|
| **Greater Himalayas (Himadri)** | Inner Himalayas | Highest peaks: Everest (Nepal), **Kanchenjunga (highest in India, 8,586 m)**, Nanda Devi; permanently snow-covered |
| **Lesser Himalayas (Himachal)** | Middle Himalayas | Pir Panjal, Dhauladhar, Mahabharat ranges; hill stations like Shimla, Mussoorie, Nainital |
| **Shiwaliks (Outer Himalayas)** | Sub-Himalayas | Lowest range; **duns** (valleys) like Dehradun, Kotli, Patli |

- Between the Himachal and Shiwaliks lie the famous **dun valleys**.
- **K2 (Godwin Austen, 8,611 m)** lies in the **Karakoram range** (in PoK) — it is the highest peak of the Karakoram, not of the Himalayas proper. This is a classic exam trap!
- The Himalayas are also divided regionally: **Punjab Himalayas → Kumaon Himalayas → Nepal Himalayas → Assam Himalayas** (west to east, divided by river gorges).

### 🪄 Mnemonic 2 — Himalayan ranges (North → South)
**"Great Little Shiv"** → **G**reater, **L**esser, **Sh**iwalik

## 4. The Northern Plains

- Formed by the **alluvium** deposited by the **Indus, Ganga, and Brahmaputra** river systems — one of the most fertile and densely populated regions on Earth.
- Divisions:
  - **Bhabar** — narrow belt of pebbles near the foothills; streams disappear here.
  - **Terai** — marshy, wet belt south of Bhabar where streams re-emerge; rich wildlife.
  - **Bhangar** — older alluvium, slightly elevated, contains **kankar** (calcareous deposits).
  - **Khadar** — newer alluvium, renewed by floods every year; most fertile.
- Regional names: **Punjab Plains** (west), **Ganga Plains** (middle), **Brahmaputra Plains** (east, Assam).

### 🪄 Mnemonic 3 — Plains from foothills downward
**"B-T-B-K"** → **"Big Tigers Bring Knowledge"** (Bhabar → Terai → Bhangar → Khadar)

🧠 **Active Recall:** Which zone has pebbles where rivers vanish? Which alluvium is newer — Bhangar or Khadar?

## 5. The Peninsular Plateau

- The **oldest landmass** of India — part of the ancient **Gondwanaland**. Made of hard igneous and metamorphic rocks.
- Broadly divided into:
  - **Central Highlands** — north of the Narmada: Malwa Plateau, Bundelkhand, Baghelkhand, Chotanagpur Plateau (rich in minerals).
  - **Deccan Plateau** — south of the Narmada, triangular, bounded by the **Western Ghats** and **Eastern Ghats**; slopes gently eastward (which is why most peninsular rivers flow east).
- **Western Ghats (Sahyadri):** continuous, higher; highest peak **Anamudi (2,695 m, Kerala)** — the highest peak of South India.
- **Eastern Ghats:** broken, discontinuous; highest peak **Mahendragiri** (Odisha).
- The Western and Eastern Ghats meet at the **Nilgiri Hills** (Doddabetta is the Nilgiris' highest peak).
- **Deccan Trap** — the black lava region of Maharashtra, famous for **black (regur) soil**, ideal for cotton.

🧠 **Active Recall:** Why do most peninsular rivers flow east? Which ghats are continuous? Where do the two ghats meet?

## 6. The Indian Desert, Coastal Plains, and Islands

### The Thar Desert
- Lies in western Rajasthan; also called the **Marusthali**.
- **Luni** is the only significant river — it is **seasonal** and its water turns **saline** as it flows toward the Rann of Kutch.

### Coastal Plains
- **Western Coastal Plain:** narrow, between Western Ghats and Arabian Sea. Sub-divisions: **Konkan** (Maharashtra–Goa), **Kannad** (Karnataka), **Malabar** (Kerala).
- **Eastern Coastal Plain:** wider, between Eastern Ghats and Bay of Bengal. Sub-divisions: **Northern Circars** (north) and **Coromandel Coast** (south, Tamil Nadu).
- **Lake Chilika (Odisha)** — largest coastal lagoon, on the eastern coast; **Pulicat Lake** lies on the Andhra–Tamil Nadu border.

### Islands
- **Andaman & Nicobar** (Bay of Bengal): separated by the **Ten Degree Channel**; **Barren Island** has India's only **active volcano**; **Saddle Peak** is the highest point.
- **Lakshadweep** (Arabian Sea): coral islands; **Kavaratti** is the capital; separated from the Maldives by the **Eight Degree Channel**.

🧠 **Active Recall:** Which channel separates the Andamans from the Nicobars? Where is India's only active volcano? Which coastal plain is wider — eastern or western?

## 7. River Systems of India

### Himalayan Rivers (perennial — fed by snow + rain)
| River | Origin | Key fact |
|---|---|---|
| **Indus** | Near Lake Mansarovar (Tibet) | Flows through Ladakh; tributaries: Jhelum, Chenab, Ravi, Beas, Sutlej |
| **Ganga** | Gangotri Glacier (as Bhagirathi) | Bhagirathi + Alaknanda meet at **Devprayag**; longest river of India; becomes **Padma** in Bangladesh |
| **Brahmaputra** | Near Mansarovar (as Tsangpo) | Enters India through Arunachal Pradesh; called **Jamuna** in Bangladesh; **Majuli** is a large river island |

### Peninsular Rivers (mostly seasonal, rain-fed)
- **East-flowing (into Bay of Bengal):** Mahanadi, Godavari, Krishna, Kaveri. **Godavari** is the longest peninsular river — called **"Dakshin Ganga"**.
- **West-flowing (into Arabian Sea):** **Narmada** and **Tapi** — they flow through **rift valleys** and form **estuaries**, not deltas.
- **Narmada** rises from **Amarkantak** (MP); **Kaveri** from **Brahmagiri Hills** (Karnataka).

### 🪄 Mnemonic 4 — East-flowing peninsular rivers (North → South)
**"M-G-K-K"** → **"My God Krishna Kaveri"** (Mahanadi, Godavari, Krishna, Kaveri)

🧠 **Active Recall:** Why do Narmada and Tapi form estuaries instead of deltas? Where do Bhagirathi and Alaknanda meet?

## 🔗 Static–Dynamic Link

The Himalayas' ongoing rise is not just textbook theory — the **2015 Nepal earthquake** and recurring seismic activity in the Himalayan belt are direct results of the same plate collision. Similarly, **Indira Point** losing land after the **2004 Indian Ocean tsunami** shows that even "static" geography keeps changing — examiners love asking about both events.

## ⚠️ Caution: Examiner's Trap

**Trap 1 — "लगभग सही कथन" (Almost-true statement):**
*"Kanchenjunga is the highest peak of India."* — Sounds right, and it IS the highest **Himalayan peak within India**, but examiners twist it against **K2**, which is higher but lies in the **Karakoram (PoK)**, not the Himalayas. Read whether the question says "highest peak of India," "of the Himalayas," or "of Indian territory."

**Trap 2 — "विपरीत कार्य-कारण" (Reverse causation):**
*"Peninsular rivers flow east because the Eastern Ghats are higher."* — Wrong. The plateau **slopes eastward**, and that slope (not the height of the Eastern Ghats) drives the rivers east. In fact, the Western Ghats are higher.

**Trap 3 — "नकारात्मक वाक्यांश" (Hidden negative):**
*"Which of the following is NOT a west-flowing river?"* — Under time pressure, students pick Narmada (the famous west-flowing one) and lose the mark. Circle the word **NOT/EXCEPT** before reading options.

**Trap 4 — "समान नाम" (Similar names):**
**Bhangar vs Khadar**, **Bhabar vs Terai**, **Himadri vs Himachal** — examiners place these pairs in the same question. Remember: **Khadar = New** (both have the "newer" sound of annual floods), **Bhangar = Old**.
