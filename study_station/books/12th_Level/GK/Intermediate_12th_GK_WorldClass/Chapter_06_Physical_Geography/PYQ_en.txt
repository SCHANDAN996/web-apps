# 📊 PYQ Analysis — Physical Geography of India

## 1. How Exams Ask Questions from This Chapter

Physical Geography of India is one of the most dependable areas of the GK paper. Across SSC (CGL, CHSL, MTS, GD), Railway (NTPC, Group D), Banking (IBPS, SBI) and State PCS prelims, questions from this chapter appear in almost every cycle. The examiners do not test obscure facts here — they repeatedly test a small set of core concepts, dressed up in traps.

**What the pattern looks like (in words, not numbers):**

- **Relief and structure** — Himalayas (divisions, peaks, passes), the Peninsular Plateau, and the Northern Plains are asked most often. Typical forms: "Which is the highest peak of the Aravalli range?", "Which pass connects Srinagar to Leh?", "The Deccan Plateau is bounded by which hills?"
- **Drainage** — rivers are the single most repeated sub-topic. Origin of rivers, left/right bank tributaries, east-flowing vs west-flowing rivers, and river-city pairs dominate. The Ganga, Brahmaputra, Godavari, Krishna, Kaveri, Narmada and Tapi systems account for the bulk of questions.
- **Climate and monsoon** — mechanism of the south-west monsoon, retreating monsoon, western disturbances, and rainfall distribution (wettest/driest regions) are regulars.
- **Soils and natural vegetation** — soil-type matching (alluvial, black, red, laterite) and forest-type questions appear frequently in SSC and Railway papers.
- **Coasts, islands and passes** — Andaman & Nicobar vs Lakshadweep, Gulf of Mannar, Palk Strait, and famous passes (Zoji La, Nathu La, Shipki La, Banihal) are favourite one-liners.

**Exam-wise flavour:**

- **SSC / Railway:** direct one-liners — origin of a river, highest peak, largest plateau, matching lists.
- **Banking:** fewer but conceptual — monsoon mechanism, soil-crop matching, national parks located in specific physical regions.
- **State PCS:** state-specific physical features (local rivers, local hills, local soils) mixed with national-level facts.

## 2. The Examiner's Traps (Jaal-Prakaar)

### Trap 1: Almost Correct (लगभग-सही कथन)
Options that are 90% right but swap one detail.
*Example:* "The Narmada flows through a rift valley between the Vindhya and **Satpura** ranges" — a wrong option will say "between the Vindhya and **Aravalli** ranges." Everything else sounds correct.

### Trap 2: Chronological / Sequence Confusion (क्रम भ्रम)
Questions asking for the correct order — peaks from west to east, rivers from north to south, Himalayan divisions from west to east. The examiner shuffles a nearly-correct sequence.

### Trap 3: Similar-Sounding Names (मिलते-जुलते नाम)
- **Godavari vs Kaveri** (both peninsular, both have famous deltas)
- **Zoji La vs Nathu La vs Shipki La** (all Himalayan passes, different states)
- **Guru Shikhar (Aravalli) vs Anamudi (Western Ghats) vs Doddabetta (Nilgiri)** — highest-peak confusion
- **Mahi vs Sabarmati vs Luni** (west-flowing rivers of Gujarat/Rajasthan)

### Trap 4: Negative Phrasing (नकारात्मक वाक्य)
"Which of the following is **NOT** a tributary of the Ganga?" / "Which river does **NOT** flow into the Bay of Bengal?" Students in a hurry miss the NOT and pick the first true statement.

### Trap 5: East-Flowing vs West-Flowing Confusion
Narmada and Tapi flow west into the Arabian Sea; most peninsular rivers flow east. Examiners love inserting "Mahi" or "Periyar" into a list of east-flowing rivers.

### Trap 6: Old Name vs New Name
- **Kanchenjunga** spelling variants; **Mount Godwin-Austen (K2)**; **Tsangpo = Brahmaputra**; **Sutlej enters India through Shipki La**.

## 3. Top 10 High-Yield Questions (PYQ-style) with Solutions

**Q1. The Narmada river flows through a rift valley between which two mountain ranges?**
(a) Vindhya and Aravalli (b) Vindhya and Satpura (c) Satpura and Western Ghats (d) Aravalli and Satpura
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** The Narmada flows westward through a rift valley bounded by the Vindhyas in the north and the Satpuras in the south. **Trap type: Almost Correct** — option (a) replaces Satpura with Aravalli, which sounds plausible because both are old ranges.
**Source:** NCERT Class 11 Geography (India Physical Environment)

**Q2. Which of the following is NOT a tributary of the river Ganga?**
(a) Yamuna (b) Gandak (c) Kosi (d) Chambal
**Answer: (d)**
**Solution:** The Chambal is a tributary of the **Yamuna**, not directly of the Ganga. Yamuna, Gandak and Kosi join the Ganga directly. **Trap type: Negative Phrasing + Almost Correct** — Chambal eventually reaches the Ganga system via the Yamuna, so it feels correct.
**Source:** NCERT Class 11 Geography

**Q3. Which is the highest peak of the Aravalli range?**
(a) Anamudi (b) Doddabetta (c) Guru Shikhar (d) Mahendragiri
**Answer: (c)**
**Solution:** Guru Shikhar (1,722 m) on Mount Abu is the highest Aravalli peak. Anamudi is the highest peak of South India (Western Ghats), Doddabetta is in the Nilgiris. **Trap type: Similar Names** — all four are "highest peaks" of some range.
**Source:** NCERT Class 11 Geography

**Q4. The Brahmaputra river is known as ______ in Tibet.**
(a) Siang (b) Dihang (c) Tsangpo (d) Lohit
**Answer: (c)**
**Solution:** In Tibet the Brahmaputra is called Tsangpo; it enters Arunachal Pradesh as the Siang/Dihang. Siang and Dihang are placed as options precisely because they are names of the *same river* in a *different stretch*. **Trap type: Similar Names / Chronological (course) Confusion.**
**Source:** NCERT Class 11 Geography

**Q5. Which of the following rivers flows into the Arabian Sea?**
(a) Godavari (b) Krishna (c) Kaveri (d) Tapi
**Answer: (d)**
**Solution:** The Tapi (Tapti) is a west-flowing river emptying into the Gulf of Khambhat (Arabian Sea). Godavari, Krishna and Kaveri all flow east into the Bay of Bengal. **Trap type: East/West-flowing Confusion.**
**Source:** NCERT Class 11 Geography

**Q6. The Zoji La pass connects:**
(a) Srinagar to Leh (b) Gangtok to Lhasa (c) Shimla to Tibet (d) Dehradun to Tibet
**Answer: (a)**
**Solution:** Zoji La lies on the Srinagar–Leh highway. Nathu La connects Sikkim to Tibet, Shipki La is in Himachal Pradesh. **Trap type: Similar Names** — all are famous Himalayan passes; examiners bank on pass-state mix-ups.
**Source:** NCERT Class 11 Geography

**Q7. Which soil is also known as 'Regur soil' and is ideal for cotton cultivation?**
(a) Alluvial soil (b) Black soil (c) Red soil (d) Laterite soil
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** Black soil, formed from Deccan trap basalt, is called Regur soil and retains moisture well, suiting cotton. **Trap type: Almost Correct** — red soil is also widespread in the Deccan, tempting students who confuse "Deccan" with "black soil region."
**Source:** NCERT Class 10 Geography

**Q8. The retreating monsoon causes rainfall in which part of India?**
(a) Western Ghats (b) Tamil Nadu coast (c) Punjab plains (d) Thar desert
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** During October–November, the retreating south-west monsoon picks up moisture over the Bay of Bengal and rains on the Coromandel (Tamil Nadu) coast. **Trap type: Chronological Confusion** — students mix up advancing monsoon (Western Ghats) with retreating monsoon (Tamil Nadu).
**Source:** NCERT Class 11 Geography

**Q9. Which of the following is the correct order of Himalayan divisions from west to east?**
(a) Punjab Himalaya – Kumaon Himalaya – Nepal Himalaya – Assam Himalaya
(b) Kumaon Himalaya – Punjab Himalaya – Nepal Himalaya – Assam Himalaya
(c) Punjab Himalaya – Nepal Himalaya – Kumaon Himalaya – Assam Himalaya
(d) Assam Himalaya – Nepal Himalaya – Kumaon Himalaya – Punjab Himalaya
**Answer: (a)**
**Solution:** The regional division runs Punjab (Indus–Satluj), Kumaon (Satluj–Kali), Nepal (Kali–Teesta), Assam (Teesta–Dihang). **Trap type: Sequence Confusion** — options (b) and (c) swap adjacent divisions.
**Source:** NCERT Class 11 Geography

**Q10. The Lakshadweep islands are separated from the Maldives by the:**
(a) Ten Degree Channel (b) Nine Degree Channel (c) Eight Degree Channel (d) Duncan Passage
**Answer: (c)**
**Solution:** The Eight Degree Channel separates Minicoy (Lakshadweep) from the Maldives; the Nine Degree Channel separates Minicoy from the main Lakshadweep group; the Ten Degree Channel separates the Andamans from the Nicobars. **Trap type: Similar Names** — three numbered channels, one correct pairing.
**Source:** NCERT Class 11 Geography

## 4. The 15/45-Second Rule Applied

**The rule:** If a question is a direct recall one-liner (peak, pass, origin, channel), you should answer within **15 seconds** — you either know it or you don't. If it is a statement/matching/sequence question, allow up to **45 seconds** for careful elimination. Never cross 45 seconds on a static GK question; mark and move.

**Applied to Q3 (Guru Shikhar):** This is a pure recall question. If you have revised the "highest peaks" list, the answer flashes in under 15 seconds. If you find yourself comparing Anamudi and Guru Shikhar for 40 seconds, you are guessing — mark it and move on. **Verdict: 15-second question.**

**Applied to Q9 (Himalayan divisions west to east):** This is a sequence question with four similar options. Here the 45-second budget is justified: write the rivers (Indus–Satluj–Kali–Teesta–Dihang) mentally as boundary markers, then eliminate (b) and (c). But if even after 45 seconds two options look identical, take your best elimination and move — do not burn 2 minutes. **Verdict: 45-second question.**

**Exam-hall takeaway:** In this chapter, most questions are direct recall type (rivers, peaks, passes, soils) where the 15-second rule applies; the rest are elimination type (NOT-questions, sequences, matching) where the 45-second budget is justified. Before the exam, sort each topic in your revision notes into "recall" or "elimination" — then, in the hall, decide within the first few seconds of reading a question which category it falls in, and set your time budget accordingly. This habit protects your score in tougher sections.

## 5. Sub-Topics to Prioritise (High-Yield List)

1. Rivers: origin, tributaries (left/right bank), east vs west flowing, river-city pairs
2. Himalayas: divisions, highest peaks (range-wise), famous passes with states
3. Peninsular Plateau: Deccan, Chotanagpur, Malwa; hills (Nilgiri, Cardamom, Aravalli, Satpura, Vindhya)
4. Monsoon: advancing vs retreating, western disturbances, rainfall extremes
5. Soils: type–region–crop matching
6. Islands and channels: Andaman–Nicobar, Lakshadweep, degree channels, straits
7. Northern Plains: bhabar, terai, alluvial divisions
8. Coasts: Coromandel, Malabar, Konkan, Northern Circars

Revise these eight clusters first — they cover the overwhelming majority of what examiners actually ask from this chapter.
