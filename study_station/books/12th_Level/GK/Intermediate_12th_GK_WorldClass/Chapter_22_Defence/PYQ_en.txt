PYQ Analysis: Indian Defence

How exams ask questions from this chapter

Questions on Indian Defence appear regularly in SSC (CGL, CHSL, GD, MTS), Railway (NTPC, Group D), Bank and State exams. They are almost always direct, fact-based, one-line questions — the kind you either know or you don't. The good news: the same sub-topics repeat again and again, so focused revision pays off quickly.

Sub-topics that are asked most often

1. The three services and their heads
- Chief of the Army Staff, Chief of the Naval Staff, Chief of the Air Staff — examiners love asking who heads which service, and the post of Chief of Defence Staff (CDS), created in 2019.
- Typical trap: mixing up the service with the wrong chief's designation (e.g., "Admiral" belongs to the Navy, "Air Chief Marshal" to the Air Force, "General" to the Army).

2. Ranks and their equivalents across services
- Matching Army, Navy and Air Force ranks of the same level (e.g., Colonel = Captain (Navy) = Group Captain) is a classic question.
- Trap: Field Marshal / Admiral of the Fleet / Marshal of the Air Force are five-star ranks; only a few officers have ever held them (Sam Manekshaw and K. M. Cariappa — Army; Arjan Singh — Air Force).

3. Missiles and weapon systems
- India's missile family under the Integrated Guided Missile Development Programme (IGMDP): Prithvi, Agni, Akash, Trishul, Nag. Questions ask the type (surface-to-surface, surface-to-air, anti-tank) or the range category.
- Trap: Akash is surface-to-air, Nag is anti-tank — these two are swapped in options very often. Agni-V is the longest-range missile in the Agni series.

4. Defence organisations
- DRDO (Defence Research and Development Organisation) — its role and headquarters (New Delhi).
- The difference between DRDO (research) and the armed forces (operations) is tested through statement-based questions.

5. Wars, operations and gallantry awards
- Major wars (1947–48, 1962, 1965, 1971, Kargil 1999) and famous operations (e.g., Operation Vijay — Kargil 1999).
- Gallantry awards in order of precedence: Param Vir Chakra, Maha Vir Chakra, Vir Chakra (wartime); Ashoka Chakra, Kirti Chakra, Shaurya Chakra (peacetime).
- Trap: Param Vir Chakra is the highest wartime gallantry award; Ashoka Chakra is the highest peacetime one — examiners swap "wartime" and "peacetime" in the statement.

6. Training academies and commands
- National Defence Academy (Khadakwasla, Pune), Indian Military Academy (Dehradun), Officers Training Academy (Chennai), Indian Naval Academy (Ezhimala), Air Force Academy (Dundigal).
- Number and location of Army/Navy/Air Force commands (e.g., Western Naval Command — Mumbai; Southern Naval Command — Kochi).

7. Joint exercises and defence deals
- Bilateral exercises (e.g., Yudh Abhyas with the USA, Varuna with France, Indra with Russia) — which country pairs with India.
- These change over time, so prepare them from current-affairs sources close to your exam date.

Common examiner traps (जाल)

- Near-correct statements: "The Chief of Defence Staff is the head of the Indian Navy" — one word wrong makes the whole statement false.
- Chronology confusion: asking which war came first, or which operation belongs to which war.
- Similar names: Akash vs. Nag vs. Astra; Vir Chakra vs. Shaurya Chakra.
- Negative wording: "Which of the following is NOT a surface-to-air missile?" — read the NOT carefully before answering.

How to prepare this chapter

- Make one-page tables: (a) ranks across the three services, (b) missiles with type and role, (c) gallantry awards in order, (d) academies with locations. Revise these tables, not paragraphs.
- For chiefs of services, exercises and recent deals, check the latest information near your exam date — these change.
- Time tip: these are recall questions — if you know the fact, answer in about 15 seconds; if not, mark and move on rather than spending 45 seconds guessing between two options.

Source: NCERT-based general knowledge and official sources (indianarmy.nic.in, indiannavy.nic.in, indianairforce.nic.in, drdo.gov.in); question patterns are PYQ-style analysis.
