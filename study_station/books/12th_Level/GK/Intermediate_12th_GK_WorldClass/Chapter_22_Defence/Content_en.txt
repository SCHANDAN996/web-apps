# Indian Defence & Security

## The Hook: A Night That Changed a Nation

On the night of 26 November 2008, ten terrorists entered Mumbai by sea. For nearly 60 hours, India watched its commandos — the NSG's "Black Cats" — fight room to room inside the Taj Hotel. One question echoed across the country: *Who protects us, and how?* The answer is a vast, layered system — the Army, Navy, Air Force, paramilitary forces, intelligence agencies, and a nuclear doctrine. This chapter maps that entire system, exactly the way examiners test it.

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this chapter, you will be able to:
- Describe the structure and role of India's three armed forces and their commands.
- Distinguish between the Army, paramilitary forces (CAPF), and intelligence agencies.
- Recall key missiles, submarines, aircraft, and defence organisations (DRDO, HAL, etc.).
- Explain India's nuclear doctrine and major defence exercises.
- Spot the traps examiners set with similar names and dates.

## 1. The Foundation: Who Commands Whom?

The **President of India** is the **Supreme Commander** of the Armed Forces (Article 53 of the Constitution). Real executive control, however, lies with the **Ministry of Defence (MoD)**, headed by the **Raksha Mantri** (Defence Minister).

Key posts to remember:
- **Chief of Defence Staff (CDS)** — created in **December 2019**; the first CDS was **General Bipin Rawat**. The CDS is the single-point military adviser to the government and heads the **Department of Military Affairs**.
- Each service has its own Chief: **Chief of Army Staff (COAS)**, **Chief of Naval Staff (CNS)**, **Chief of Air Staff (CAS)** — all four-star officers.

🧠 **Active Recall:** Who is the Supreme Commander of the armed forces, and which Article says so? When was the post of CDS created?

## 2. The Indian Army

- One of the largest standing armies in the world, with roots in the **British Indian Army**; the modern Army dates from **15 January 1949**, when **General K.M. Cariappa** became the first Indian Commander-in-Chief. This day is celebrated as **Army Day**.
- Motto: **"Seva Paramo Dharma"** (Service Before Self).
- Organised into **7 commands** — 6 operational + 1 training:

| Command | Headquarters |
|---|---|
| Northern Command | Udhampur |
| Western Command | Chandimandir |
| Eastern Command | Kolkata |
| Southern Command | Pune |
| Central Command | Lucknow |
| South Western Command | Jaipur |
| Army Training Command (ARTRAC) | Shimla |

**Mnemonic — "N-W-E-S-C-SW-T":** *"Never Walk East, Soldiers Can Swim Together"* (Northern, Western, Eastern, Southern, Central, South-Western, Training).

🧠 **Active Recall:** Where is the Northern Command headquartered? Who was the first Indian C-in-C of the Army?

## 3. The Indian Navy

- **Navy Day: 4 December** — commemorates **Operation Trident (1971)**, the missile-boat attack on Karachi harbour during the Indo-Pak war.
- Motto: **"Sham No Varunah"** (May the Lord of the Oceans be auspicious unto us).
- **3 Commands:** Western Naval Command (Mumbai), Eastern Naval Command (Visakhapatnam), Southern Naval Command (Kochi — the training command).
- Key assets:
  - **INS Vikramaditya** — aircraft carrier (from Russia, commissioned 2013).
  - **INS Vikrant (IAC-1)** — India's first **indigenously built** aircraft carrier, commissioned **September 2022**, built at Cochin Shipyard.
  - **INS Arihant** — India's first indigenous **nuclear-powered ballistic missile submarine (SSBN)**, completing the nuclear triad.
  - **INS Chakra** — nuclear-powered attack submarine leased from Russia.

🧠 **Active Recall:** Which carrier is indigenous — Vikramaditya or Vikrant? What type of submarine is INS Arihant?

## 4. The Indian Air Force

- Established **8 October 1932** — celebrated as **Air Force Day**.
- Motto: **"Nabha Sparsham Deeptam"** (Touch the Sky with Glory), from the Bhagavad Gita.
- **7 Commands:** Western (New Delhi), South-Western (Gandhinagar), Eastern (Shillong), Central (Prayagraj), Southern (Thiruvananthapuram), plus **Training Command** (Bengaluru) and **Maintenance Command** (Nagpur).
- Key aircraft: **Rafale** (France, first squadron at Ambala), **Sukhoi Su-30 MKI**, **Mirage-2000**, **MiG-21**, **Tejas** (indigenous, built by HAL), **Apache** and **Chinook** helicopters (USA).

**Mnemonic for commands:** *"We Eat South-Western Central Snacks, Then March"* — Western, Eastern, South-Western, Central, Southern, Training, Maintenance.

🧠 **Active Recall:** When is Air Force Day? Which company builds the Tejas?

## 5. Paramilitary / Central Armed Police Forces (CAPF)

Examiners love confusing these. Learn the **border each force guards**:

| Force | Primary Role |
|---|---|
| **BSF** (1965) | Borders with **Pakistan and Bangladesh** |
| **CRPF** (1939, as Crown Representative's Police) | Internal security, anti-Naxal operations |
| **CISF** (1969) | Security of airports, metros, vital installations |
| **ITBP** (1962) | **Indo-China** border |
| **SSB** (1963) | Borders with **Nepal and Bhutan** |
| **Assam Rifles** (1835 — oldest paramilitary force) | **Indo-Myanmar** border, North-East |
| **NSG** (1984) | Counter-terrorism — the "Black Cats" |

**Mnemonic:** *"B-Pak-Bang, I-China, S-Nepal-Bhutan, A-Myanmar"* — BSF, ITBP, SSB, Assam Rifles.

🧠 **Active Recall:** Which force guards the Indo-China border? Which is the oldest paramilitary force?

## 6. Intelligence Agencies

- **RAW (Research and Analysis Wing)** — external intelligence, formed **1968** after the 1962 war exposed intelligence failures. First chief: **R.N. Kao**.
- **IB (Intelligence Bureau)** — internal intelligence; India's **oldest** intelligence agency (1887, British era).
- **NIA (National Investigation Agency)** — formed **2008** (after Mumbai attacks) to investigate terrorism.

---

## 7. DRDO, Missiles and the Nuclear Doctrine

- **DRDO** (Defence Research and Development Organisation) — formed **1958**, HQ New Delhi. Developed the **Integrated Guided Missile Development Programme (IGMDP)** under **Dr. A.P.J. Abdul Kalam**.

**The missile family (know these cold):**

| Missile | Type | Range (approx.) |
|---|---|---|
| **Prithvi** | Surface-to-surface, short range | 150–350 km |
| **Agni-V** | Surface-to-surface, ICBM-class | 5,000+ km |
| **Aakash** | Surface-to-air | ~25–30 km |
| **Nag** | Anti-tank | ~4 km |
| **Trishul** | Short-range surface-to-air | ~9 km |
| **BrahMos** | Supersonic cruise missile (India–Russia JV) | ~290+ km (extended versions more) |

**Mnemonic — "P-A-A-N-T":** *Prithvi, Agni, Aakash, Nag, Trishul* — the five IGMDP missiles.

**Nuclear Doctrine:** India follows **"No First Use" (NFU)** and **Credible Minimum Deterrence**. The **nuclear triad** = ability to launch nuclear weapons from **land (Agni), air (aircraft), and sea (INS Arihant)**. India conducted nuclear tests in **1974 (Pokhran-I, "Smiling Buddha")** and **1998 (Pokhran-II, "Operation Shakti")**.

🧠 **Active Recall:** Which missile is a joint venture with Russia? What does NFU stand for?

## 8. Key Defence Institutions & Exercises

- **HAL** (Hindustan Aeronautics Limited) — Bengaluru; builds Tejas, helicopters.
- **National Defence Academy (NDA)** — Khadakwasla, **Pune**; tri-services training.
- **Indian Military Academy (IMA)** — **Dehradun**.
- **Major joint exercises:** **Yudh Abhyas** (USA), **Mitra Shakti** (Sri Lanka), **Hand-in-Hand** (China), **Surya Kiran** (Nepal), **Malabar** (USA, Japan, Australia — naval), **TARKASH** (USA, counter-terror).

The **Agnipath scheme (2022)** changed how soldiers, sailors and airmen are recruited — "Agniveers" serve for four years, with up to 25% retained. Also, the push for **"Atmanirbhar Bharat" in defence** (indigenisation lists, defence corridors in Uttar Pradesh and Tamil Nadu) links this static chapter directly to current affairs — expect questions connecting INS Vikrant, Tejas exports, and defence corridors.

## ⚠️ Caution: Examiner's Trap

**Trap 1 — Similar Names (समान नाम):** *INS Vikramaditya vs INS Vikrant.* Examiners swap "indigenous" between them. **Vikrant = indigenous (built in Kochi); Vikramaditya = Russian-origin.** One word flips the answer.

**Trap 2 — Chronological Confusion (कालानुक्रमिक भ्रम):** *RAW (1968), NSG (1984), NIA (2008), CDS (2019).* Questions ask "which was formed first/latest" or pair a force with the wrong year — e.g., "BSF was formed in 1962" (wrong; BSF = 1965, ITBP = 1962).

**Trap 3 — Almost-True Statement (लगभग सही कथन):** *"The Chief of Army Staff is the Supreme Commander of the Armed Forces."* Sounds plausible, but the **President** is the Supreme Commander (Article 53). Similarly: "Navy Day is celebrated on 8 October" — that's Air Force Day; Navy Day is 4 December.
