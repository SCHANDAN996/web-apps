# 📊 PYQ Analysis — Indian Constitution (Basic)

## 1. How Exams Ask Questions from This Chapter

Constitution questions appear in **every** major exam — UPSC, SSC (CGL/CHSL/GD), Banking (IBPS/SBI), Railway (NTPC/Group D), and State PCS. The pattern is remarkably stable because the Constitution itself does not change often. Examiners repeatedly test the same core areas:

- **Preamble** — its words, the 42nd Amendment changes (Socialist, Secular, Integrity), and whether it is a part of the Constitution (Kesavananda Bharati case).
- **Making of the Constitution** — Constituent Assembly (Cabinet Mission Plan 1946, first meeting 9 December 1946, Dr. Rajendra Prasad as President, B.N. Rau as Constitutional Adviser), adoption on 26 November 1949, enforcement on 26 January 1950.
- **Sources of the Constitution** — which feature was borrowed from which country (Parliamentary system — Britain; Fundamental Rights — USA; Directive Principles — Ireland; Emergency — Germany; Concurrent List — Australia; Amendment procedure — South Africa).
- **Parts, Articles, Schedules** — original 22 Parts, 395 Articles, 8 Schedules; now 12 Schedules.
- **Fundamental Rights** — Articles 12–35, which right is in which Article, rights available only to citizens vs. all persons.
- **DPSP and Fundamental Duties** — Articles 36–51, Article 51A (42nd Amendment, 1976, Swaran Singh Committee).
- **Amendments** — Article 368, landmark amendments (1st, 42nd, 44th, 52nd, 61st, 73rd, 74th, 86th, 101st).
- **Key functionaries** — President, PM, Speaker, CAG, Attorney General, Election Commission — their Articles, appointment, tenure.

## 2. Sub-Topic Weightage (in words, not numbers)

- **Most frequently asked:** Fundamental Rights, borrowed features/sources, Preamble, and Parts–Schedules–Articles matching. These four areas together dominate Constitution questions in SSC, Railway, and Banking exams.
- **Moderately frequent:** Constituent Assembly history, amendment procedure, Fundamental Duties, DPSP, and constitutional bodies (Election Commission, CAG, UPSC).
- **Occasionally asked:** Citizenship (Articles 5–11), writs (Article 32/226 — though often asked in the Rights section), and detailed amendment lists.
- **UPSC/PCS prelims** lean toward statement-based and conceptual questions (e.g., "Which of the following is/are features of the Parliamentary system?"), while **SSC/Railway/Banking** prefer direct one-liner factual questions.

## 3. Examiner's Traps — The Four Classic Types

**Trap 1: Almost Correct Statement**
A statement that is 90% right but wrong in one word. Example: "The words 'Socialist' and 'Secular' were added to the Preamble by the 44th Amendment." (Wrong — it was the **42nd** Amendment, 1976.) Always check the amendment number, year, and Article number.

**Trap 2: Chronological Confusion**
Events or dates shuffled. Example: "The Constitution was adopted on 26 January 1950." (Wrong — **adopted 26 November 1949**, **enforced 26 January 1950**.) Examiners love swapping these two dates.

**Trap 3: Similar-Name Confusion**
Mixing up look-alike pairs: Dr. Rajendra Prasad (President of Constituent Assembly) vs. Dr. B.R. Ambedkar (Chairman of Drafting Committee); B.N. Rau (Constitutional Adviser) vs. Sachchidananda Sinha (temporary/first Chairman of the Assembly). Also Article 32 (Ambedkar called it the "heart and soul") vs. Article 226.

**Trap 4: Negative Phrasing**
"Which of the following is **NOT** a Fundamental Right?" or "Which feature was **NOT** borrowed from Britain?" Students misread and pick the correct feature instead of the odd one out. Underline NOT/EXCEPT before answering.

## 4. Top 10 High-Yield PYQ-Style Questions with Solutions

**Q1.** The words 'Socialist', 'Secular' and 'Integrity' were added to the Preamble by which amendment?
(a) 42nd Amendment, 1976 (b) 44th Amendment, 1978 (c) 52nd Amendment, 1985 (d) 1st Amendment, 1951
**Answer: (a)**
**Solution:** The 42nd Amendment (1976), called the "Mini Constitution," added these three words. Trap type: **Almost Correct** — the 44th Amendment is the classic distractor.
**Source:** NCERT Class 11 Political Science (Indian Constitution at Work)

**Q2.** The Constitution of India was adopted by the Constituent Assembly on:
(a) 26 January 1950 (b) 26 November 1949 (c) 15 August 1947 (d) 9 December 1946
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** Adopted on 26 November 1949 (celebrated as Constitution Day); enforced on 26 January 1950 (Republic Day). Trap type: **Chronological Confusion**.
**Source:** NCERT Class 11 Political Science

**Q3.** The concept of Directive Principles of State Policy was borrowed from:
(a) USA (b) Ireland (c) Britain (d) Canada
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** DPSP came from the Irish Constitution (which itself borrowed from Spain). Trap type: **Similar-Name Confusion** — students often pick Britain because the parliamentary system came from there.
**Source:** NCERT Class 11 Political Science

**Q4.** Who was the Chairman of the Drafting Committee of the Constituent Assembly?
(a) Dr. Rajendra Prasad (b) B.N. Rau (c) Dr. B.R. Ambedkar (d) Jawaharlal Nehru
**Answer: (c)**
**Solution:** Dr. B.R. Ambedkar chaired the Drafting Committee (set up 29 August 1947). Dr. Rajendra Prasad was President of the Assembly; B.N. Rau was Constitutional Adviser. Trap type: **Similar-Name Confusion**.
**Source:** NCERT Class 11 Political Science

**Q5.** Which of the following is NOT a Fundamental Right under the Indian Constitution?
(a) Right to Equality (b) Right to Freedom of Religion (c) Right to Property (d) Right against Exploitation
**Answer: (c)**
**Solution:** Right to Property was removed from Fundamental Rights by the 44th Amendment (1978) and made a legal right under Article 300A. Trap type: **Negative Phrasing**.
**Source:** NCERT Class 11 Political Science

**Q6.** Fundamental Duties were added to the Constitution on the recommendation of which committee?
(a) Sarkaria Commission (b) Swaran Singh Committee (c) Balwant Rai Mehta Committee (d) Ashok Mehta Committee
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** The Swaran Singh Committee's recommendations led to Article 51A being inserted by the 42nd Amendment, 1976. Trap type: **Similar-Name Confusion** — the two Mehta committees relate to Panchayati Raj.
**Source:** NCERT Class 11 Political Science

**Q7.** Which Article of the Constitution deals with the amendment procedure?
(a) Article 360 (b) Article 368 (c) Article 370 (d) Article 356
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** Article 368 (Part XX) covers amendment. Article 356 = President's Rule; Article 360 = Financial Emergency. Trap type: **Almost Correct** — adjacent Article numbers are the distractors.
**Source:** NCERT Class 11 Political Science

**Q8.** The idea of the Concurrent List in the Indian Constitution was borrowed from:
(a) Australia (b) Canada (c) USA (d) Ireland
**Answer: (a)**
**Solution:** Concurrent List — Australia; Federation with strong centre — Canada; Fundamental Rights — USA; DPSP — Ireland. Trap type: **Similar-Name Confusion** in source-country matching.
**Source:** NCERT Class 11 Political Science

**Q9.** How many Schedules did the original Constitution of 1950 contain?
(a) 8 (b) 10 (c) 12 (d) 22
**Answer: (a)**
**Solution:** Originally 8 Schedules; today 12 (9th by 1st Amendment, 10th by 52nd, 11th by 73rd, 12th by 74th). Trap type: **Almost Correct** — 12 is the *current* number, not the original.
**Source:** NCERT Class 11 Political Science

**Q10.** Dr. B.R. Ambedkar described which Article as the "heart and soul" of the Constitution?
(a) Article 14 (b) Article 19 (c) Article 21 (d) Article 32
**Answer: (d)**
**Solution:** Article 32 (Right to Constitutional Remedies) — it lets citizens move the Supreme Court directly for enforcement of Fundamental Rights. Trap type: **Almost Correct** — Article 21 is a tempting distractor.
**Source:** NCERT Class 11 Political Science

## 5. The 15/45-Second Rule Applied

**Rule:** If a question is a direct fact you have revised (dates, names, borrowed features), answer within **15 seconds**. If it is statement-based or negatively phrased, give it up to **45 seconds** — never more.

**Example 1 — 15-second question (Q2 above):** "Constitution adopted on?" If you have memorised 26 November 1949 vs 26 January 1950, mark (b) instantly. Do not re-read the options; the date pair is a reflex fact. Spending 40 seconds here steals time from harder questions.

**Example 2 — 45-second question (Q5 above):** "Which is NOT a Fundamental Right?" This needs care: first underline NOT, then check each option against the six current Fundamental Rights. Right to Property is the odd one out. This deserves 30–45 seconds — but if you still cannot decide after 45 seconds, mark your best guess and move on. Never let one question consume two minutes.

**Skip strategy:** In statement-based questions with Roman numerals (I, II, III), if even one statement is unknown, skip and return later — such questions have the highest error rate under time pressure.

## 6. Key Takeaways for Revision

1. Master the **date pairs**: 9 Dec 1946 (first meeting), 26 Nov 1949 (adoption), 26 Jan 1950 (enforcement).
2. Make a one-page table of **borrowed features** — it is the single most repeated theme.
3. Learn **amendment numbers with years**: 1st (1951), 42nd (1976), 44th (1978), 52nd (1985), 61st (1989), 73rd & 74th (1992), 86th (2002), 101st (2016).
4. For every fact, ask: "How would the examiner twist this?" — that is exactly where the four traps come from.
