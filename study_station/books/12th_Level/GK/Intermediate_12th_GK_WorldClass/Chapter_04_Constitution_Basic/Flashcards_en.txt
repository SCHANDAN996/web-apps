Card 1
Front: Which country gave India the idea of the "Constituent Assembly"?
Back: France. The idea of a Constituent Assembly was borrowed from the French Revolution.

Card 2
Front: The Indian Constitution is called a "borrowed bag" — from how many countries did we borrow features?
Back: Features were borrowed from about 10 countries, with the largest influence from the Government of India Act, 1935 (about 250 provisions).

Card 3
Front: Who was the Chairman of the Drafting Committee, and how many members did it have?
Back: Dr. B.R. Ambedkar was the Chairman; the committee had 7 members.

Card 4
Front: How long did it take to make the Indian Constitution?
Back: 2 years, 11 months and 18 days (9 December 1946 to 26 November 1949).

Card 5
Front: The Constitution was adopted on 26 November 1949 — then why is Republic Day on 26 January?
Back: 26 January was chosen because on this date in 1930, "Purna Swaraj Day" was observed. The Constitution came into force on 26 January 1950.

Card 6
Front: Which Article is called the "heart and soul" of the Constitution, and by whom?
Back: Article 32 (Right to Constitutional Remedies) — called so by Dr. B.R. Ambedkar.

Card 7
Front: Mnemonic trick: How do you remember the 6 Fundamental Rights in Article 19(1)?
Back: Use "SPEARM" — Speech, Peaceful assembly, Association, Movement, Residence, Profession (originally 7; the Right to Property was removed by the 44th Amendment, 1978).

Card 8
Front: Mnemonic trick: How to remember the order of words added by the 42nd Amendment to the Preamble?
Back: "S-S-I" — Socialist, Secular, Integrity (added by the 42nd Amendment, 1976, called the "Mini Constitution").

Card 9
Front: Examiner's trap: Is the Right to Property still a Fundamental Right?
Back: No. It was removed from Fundamental Rights by the 44th Amendment (1978) and is now only a legal right under Article 300A. Trap options often list it as a Fundamental Right.

Card 10
Front: Examiner's trap: Are Fundamental Duties enforceable by courts?
Back: No. Fundamental Duties (Article 51A, Part IVA) are non-justiciable. They were added by the 42nd Amendment (1976) on the Swaran Singh Committee's recommendation — originally 10, now 11 (86th Amendment, 2002).

Card 11
Front: From which country did we borrow Fundamental Rights, and from where the Directive Principles?
Back: Fundamental Rights — USA; Directive Principles of State Policy — Ireland (which itself took them from Spain).

Card 12
Front: Which is the longest written constitution in the world, and which is the shortest?
Back: Longest — India; Shortest — USA.

Card 13
Front: Who was the first President of the Constituent Assembly (temporary), and who became the permanent President?
Back: Temporary — Dr. Sachchidananda Sinha; Permanent — Dr. Rajendra Prasad.

Card 14
Front: How many Articles, Parts and Schedules did the original Constitution (1950) have?
Back: 395 Articles, 22 Parts, 8 Schedules. (Today: about 448 Articles, 25 Parts, 12 Schedules.)

Card 15
Front: Which Schedule contains the list of official languages, and how many languages does it have now?
Back: The 8th Schedule; originally 14 languages, now 22.

Card 16
Front: Which Amendment lowered the voting age from 21 to 18 years?
Back: The 61st Constitutional Amendment, 1988 (effective 1989).

Card 17
Front: Which words describe India in Article 1 of the Constitution?
Back: "India, that is Bharat, shall be a Union of States." Note: "Union", not "Federation" — a favourite trap.

Card 18
Front: Who is known as the "Father of the Indian Constitution"?
Back: Dr. Bhimrao Ramji Ambedkar.

Card 19
Front: Examiner's trap: Did India borrow the concept of a "Republic" from Britain?
Back: No. The idea of a Republic came from France. Britain gave us the Parliamentary system, rule of law and single citizenship.

Card 20
Front: On which date was the first meeting of the Constituent Assembly held, and who boycotted it?
Back: 9 December 1946; the Muslim League boycotted it. Only 207 members attended.
