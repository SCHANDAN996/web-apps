26. The Constituent Assembly of India held its first meeting on —
(a) 9 December 1946 (b) 26 January 1950 (c) 15 August 1947 (d) 26 November 1949
Answer: (a)
Solution: 26 November 1949 is tempting because the Constitution was adopted on that date, but the first meeting of the Constituent Assembly took place on 9 December 1946 in the Constitution Hall, New Delhi. So the answer is 9 December 1946.

27. Who was the permanent Chairman (President) of the Constituent Assembly?
(a) Dr. Rajendra Prasad (b) Dr. B.R. Ambedkar (c) Jawaharlal Nehru (d) Dr. Sachchidananda Sinha
Answer: (a)
Solution: Dr. Sachchidananda Sinha is the trap — he was only the temporary (interim) chairman for the first two days. Dr. Rajendra Prasad was elected the permanent President of the Constituent Assembly on 11 December 1946. So the answer is Dr. Rajendra Prasad.

(a) Article 19 (b) Article 32 (c) Article 21 (d) Article 226

38. The President of India is elected by —
(a) Direct election by the people (b) Members of Parliament only (c) An electoral college of elected members of both Houses of Parliament and elected members of State Legislative Assemblies (d) Members of State Legislative Councils
Answer: (c)
Solution: 'Members of Parliament only' is the trap — it omits the state legislatures. Under Article 54, the President is elected by an electoral college of elected MPs of both Houses and elected MLAs of States and the Union Territories of Delhi and Puducherry. So the answer is the electoral college of elected members of Parliament and State Legislative Assemblies.

Answer: (b)
Solution: 'Either House' is the trap because ordinary bills can be introduced in either House, but under Article 109 a Money Bill can be introduced only in the Lok Sabha, and the Rajya Sabha can only recommend changes within 14 days. So the answer is Lok Sabha.

50. Which of the following is NOT a Fundamental Right under the present Constitution?
(a) Right to Equality (b) Right to Freedom of Religion (c) Right to Property (d) Right against Exploitation
Answer: (c)
Solution: Right against Exploitation is tempting as a distractor but it remains a Fundamental Right under Articles 23–24. The Right to Property was removed from Fundamental Rights by the 44th Amendment (1978) and made a legal right under Article 300A. So the answer is Right to Property.
