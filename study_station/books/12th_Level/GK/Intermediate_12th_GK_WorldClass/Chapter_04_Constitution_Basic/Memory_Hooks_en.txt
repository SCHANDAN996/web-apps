# 🪄 Memory Hooks & Short Tricks — Indian Constitution (Basic)

---

**Box 1 — Preamble Words: "JUST SIR"**
The Preamble's key values in order:
**J**ustice, **U**nity, **S**overeign, **T**ruth... no wait — use this:
**S**overeign **S**ocialist **S**ecular **D**emocratic **R**epublic → **"3S + D + R"**
Trick: *"Three Sikh Soldiers Danced Right"* — Sovereign, Socialist, Secular, Democratic, Republic.
Then the goals: **J**ustice, **L**iberty, **E**quality, **F**raternity → **"JLEF"** → *"Just Leave Everything Free."*

---

**Box 2 — Borrowed Features: "USA-UK-IRE-CAN-AUS-GER-USSR-SAFR"**
- **USA** → Fundamental Rights, Judicial Review, Impeachment of President
- **UK** → Parliamentary system, Rule of Law, Single Citizenship
- **IREland** → Directive Principles (DPSP), Nomination of Rajya Sabha members
- **CANada** → Federation with strong Centre
- **AUStralia** → Concurrent List, Joint Sitting
- **GERmany (Weimar)** → Emergency provisions
- **USSR** → Fundamental Duties, Five-Year Plans ideals
- **South AFRica** → Amendment procedure, Election of Rajya Sabha members

Hook: *"The Constitution went on a WORLD TOUR and brought back souvenirs."*

---

**Box 3 — Fundamental Rights: "25-30-32"**
- Article **25** → Freedom of Religion
- Article **30** → Minorities' right to run institutions
- Article **32** → Right to Constitutional Remedies (Ambedkar's "heart and soul")

Hook: *"At 25 you find religion, at 30 you build your own school, at 32 you run to court!"*

---

**Box 4 — Six Fundamental Rights: "CERES-C"**
**C**ultural & Educational, **E**quality, **R**eligion, **E**xploitation (against), **S**peech/Freedoms (Art 19), **C**onstitutional Remedies.
Hook: *"CERES the goddess guards your rights."*

---

**Box 5 — Writs: "Habeas Corpus = Bring the Body"**
- **Habeas Corpus** → "You may have the body" (illegal detention)
- **Mandamus** → "We command" (to a public official)
- **Prohibition** → Higher court STOPS a lower court (before judgment)
- **Certiorari** → Higher court QUASHES a lower court order (after judgment)
- **Quo Warranto** → "By what authority?" (illegal office-holder)

Hook: *"Prohibition = Prevent (before), Certiorari = Cancel (after). P before P, C after C."*

---

**Box 6 — Parts of the Constitution: "The Union Met Citizens First"**
- Part **I** → The **U**nion & its Territory
- Part **II** → **C**itizenship
- Part **III** → **F**undamental Rights
- Part **IV** → DPSP | Part **IV-A** → Fundamental Duties

Hook: *"I Union, II Citizens, III Rights, IV Duties-in-spirit (DPSP), IV-A Duties-in-action."*

---

**Box 7 — Fundamental Duties: "42-86, 11 Duties"**
Added by the **42nd Amendment, 1976** (10 duties), 11th added by the **86th Amendment, 2002** (parents to educate children aged 6–14).
Hook: *"42 brought 10, 86 brought 1 more — 42+86 = a full 11."*

---

**Box 8 — Schedules: "TEARS OF OLD PM" (12 Schedules)**
**T**erritory (1), **E**moluments/Salaries (2), **A**ffirmations/Oaths (3), **R**ajya Sabha seats (4), **S**cheduled Areas (5), **O**ther tribal areas — Assam/Meghalaya/Tripura/Mizoram (6), **F**ields of legislation — 3 Lists (7), **O**fficial Languages (8), **L**and Reforms (9), **D**efection — Anti-Defection (10), **P**anchayats (11), **M**unicipalities (12).
Hook: *"Even the PM sheds TEARS reading all 12 schedules!"*

---

**Box 9 — Language Schedule Count: "8th Schedule = 22"**
8th Schedule has **22** scheduled languages (originally 14).
Hook: *"8 + 14 = 22 — the schedule grew up."*

---

**Box 10 — Amendment Article: "368 = 3-6-8"**
Article **368** deals with amendment of the Constitution.
Hook: *"3-6-8, don't be late — amend the State!"*
Also: **Article 360** = Financial Emergency, **352** = National Emergency, **356** = President's Rule.
Hook: *"352 Nation, 356 State, 360 Money — even numbers climb by 4."*

---

**Box 11 — Constituent Assembly: "Dec 9, 1946 — 2 Years 11 Months 18 Days"**
First meeting: **9 December 1946**; Constitution adopted **26 November 1949**; enforced **26 January 1950**.
Hook: *"Met in '46, signed in '49, born in '50 — and 26 January was already special (Poorna Swaraj Day, 1930)."*

---

**Box 12 — Key People: "BR Rajendra Sachidanand"**
- **B.R. Ambedkar** → Chairman, Drafting Committee
- **Rajendra Prasad** → President of Constituent Assembly
- **Sachidanand Sinha** → Temporary (first) Chairman
Hook: *"Sinha opened the shop, Rajendra ran it, Ambedkar wrote the goods."*

---

**Box 13 — DPSP vs FR: "IV is a Wish, III is a Right"**
Part III (FR) = enforceable in court; Part IV (DPSP) = non-enforceable guidelines for the State.
Hook: *"III you can SUE for, IV you can only SUGGEST."*

---

**Box 14 — Citizenship: "Part II, Articles 5–11"**
Hook: *"Citizens caught between 5 and 11 — like a school timing!"*

---

**Box 15 — Single Citizenship: "One Nation, One Card"**
India (like UK) has single citizenship; USA has dual.
Hook: *"In India you belong to the country, not the state — one flag, one citizenship."*

## ⏭️ Skip Strategy — When to Skip a Question

1. **"Which schedule/list entry number" questions:** If a question asks the exact entry number of a subject in the Union/State/Concurrent List (e.g., "Entry 42 of Union List"), and you don't remember it, **skip immediately** — these are pure memory with no logic to eliminate options, and wrong guesses cost negative marks.

2. **"Which amendment in which year" chains:** If a question asks about a specific amendment number AND year together (e.g., "73rd Amendment — 1992 or 1993?") and you only half-remember, attempt only if you can eliminate two options confidently. Otherwise skip — amendment-number traps are the examiner's favourite.

3. **"Borrowed from which country" with 4 similar options:** If the options mix Ireland/Canada/Australia for a feature you can't place (e.g., Concurrent List vs. Federation), don't gamble blindly. Skip unless you recall the mnemonic (Box 2) clearly — near-identical country pairs are designed to trap guessers.
