Questions 76–100: Medium

76. The idea of the Directive Principles of State Policy in the Indian Constitution was borrowed from the Constitution of —
(a) Ireland (b) USA (c) Canada (d) Australia
Answer: (a)
Solution: The tempting wrong choice is Canada, from which we borrowed federation with a strong centre and residuary powers. The Directive Principles were taken from the Irish Constitution, which itself drew them from the Spanish Constitution, so the answer is Ireland.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Political Science (Indian Constitution at Work)

77. Which Article of the Indian Constitution abolishes untouchability?
(a) Article 14 (b) Article 15 (c) Article 18 (d) Article 17
Answer: (d)
Solution: Many students pick Article 15 because it also deals with discrimination, but Article 15 only prohibits discrimination on grounds of religion, race, caste, sex or place of birth. Article 17 specifically abolishes untouchability and makes its practice a punishable offence, so the answer is Article 17.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Political Science (Indian Constitution at Work)

78. The Fundamental Duties were added to the Constitution by which amendment?
(a) 42nd Amendment (b) 24th Amendment (c) 44th Amendment (d) 52nd Amendment
Answer: (a)
Solution: The 44th Amendment is a common trap because it also dealt with Fundamental Rights (removing property as a right), but it did not add the duties. The 42nd Amendment of 1976, on the recommendation of the Swaran Singh Committee, inserted Article 51A, so the answer is the 42nd Amendment.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Political Science (Indian Constitution at Work)

79. How many Fundamental Duties are currently listed in Article 51A?
(a) 11 (b) 10 (c) 12 (d) 9
Answer: (a)
Solution: Ten is the tempting answer because the 42nd Amendment originally added ten duties. The 86th Amendment of 2002 added one more duty — for parents to provide education to children aged 6 to 14 — taking the total to eleven, so the answer is 11.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Political Science (Indian Constitution at Work)

80. The words 'Socialist' and 'Secular' were added to the Preamble by —
(a) 1st Amendment, 1951 (b) 44th Amendment, 1978 (c) 52nd Amendment, 1985 (d) 42nd Amendment, 1976
Answer: (d)
Solution: The 44th Amendment is the trap here since it came soon after and reversed several 42nd Amendment changes, but it did not touch the Preamble's wording. The 42nd Amendment of 1976 added 'Socialist', 'Secular' and 'Integrity' to the Preamble, so the answer is the 42nd Amendment, 1976.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Political Science (Indian Constitution at Work)

81. Who presides over the joint sitting of the two Houses of Parliament?
(a) The President (b) The Vice-President (c) The Chief Justice of India (d) The Speaker of the Lok Sabha
Answer: (d)
Solution: The President is the tempting wrong answer because the President summons the joint sitting under Article 108. However, summoning and presiding are different functions; the sitting is presided over by the Speaker of the Lok Sabha, so the answer is the Speaker of the Lok Sabha.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Political Science (Indian Constitution at Work)

82. The minimum age to become a member of the Rajya Sabha is —
(a) 21 years (b) 30 years (c) 25 years (d) 35 years
Answer: (b)
Solution: Twenty-five years is the trap because it is the minimum age for the Lok Sabha and State Assemblies, and students often apply it to both Houses. For the Rajya Sabha the Constitution fixes the minimum age at 30 years, so the answer is 30 years.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Political Science (Indian Constitution at Work)

83. Which of the following is NOT a Fundamental Right under the Indian Constitution?
(a) Right to Equality (b) Right to Freedom of Religion (c) Right to Property (d) Right against Exploitation
Answer: (c)
Solution: This question tests a classic chronological confusion: the Right to Property was originally a Fundamental Right under Article 31, which makes it a tempting choice as a current right. The 44th Amendment of 1978 removed it and made it a legal right under Article 300A, so the answer is Right to Property.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Political Science (Indian Constitution at Work)

84. The concept of 'Judicial Review' in the Indian Constitution is borrowed from —
(a) USA (b) Britain (c) Ireland (d) South Africa
Answer: (a)
Solution: Britain is the tempting wrong option because much of our parliamentary system comes from there, but Britain follows parliamentary sovereignty, not judicial review. Judicial review was borrowed from the Constitution of the USA, so the answer is USA.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Political Science (Indian Constitution at Work)

85. The President of India can proclaim a National Emergency under which Article?
(a) Article 356 (b) Article 352 (c) Article 360 (d) Article 370
Answer: (b)
Solution: Article 356 is the trap because it also deals with emergency, but it concerns President's Rule in a state on the failure of constitutional machinery. National Emergency on grounds of war, external aggression or armed rebellion is proclaimed under Article 352, so the answer is Article 352.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Political Science (Indian Constitution at Work)

86. Who was the Chairman of the Drafting Committee of the Constituent Assembly?
(a) Jawaharlal Nehru (b) Dr. B. R. Ambedkar (c) Dr. Rajendra Prasad (d) Sardar Vallabhbhai Patel
Answer: (b)
Solution: Dr. Rajendra Prasad is the tempting wrong answer because of the similar-sounding high office — he was the President of the Constituent Assembly, not the Drafting Committee chairman. The Drafting Committee was chaired by Dr. B. R. Ambedkar, so the answer is Dr. B. R. Ambedkar.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Political Science (Indian Constitution at Work)

87. The Constitution of India was adopted by the Constituent Assembly on —
(a) 26 November 1949 (b) 26 January 1950 (c) 15 August 1947 (d) 26 January 1949
Answer: (a)
Solution: 26 January 1950 is the classic trap because that is when the Constitution came into force, celebrated as Republic Day. The Constitution was adopted and signed on 26 November 1949, now observed as Constitution Day, so the answer is 26 November 1949.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Political Science (Indian Constitution at Work)

88. Which Schedule of the Constitution contains the list of recognised languages?
(a) Eighth Schedule (b) Seventh Schedule (c) Ninth Schedule (d) Tenth Schedule
Answer: (a)
Solution: The Seventh Schedule is the trap because students confuse the two most-cited schedules; the Seventh contains the Union, State and Concurrent Lists of subjects. The Eighth Schedule lists the recognised languages, originally 14 and now 22, so the answer is the Eighth Schedule.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Political Science (Indian Constitution at Work)

89. The Tenth Schedule of the Indian Constitution deals with —
(a) Panchayati Raj (b) Municipalities (c) Languages (d) Anti-defection provisions
Answer: (d)
Solution: Panchayati Raj is the tempting wrong option because it was also added by an amendment around the same era, but it belongs to the Eleventh Schedule. The Tenth Schedule, added by the 52nd Amendment of 1985, contains the anti-defection law, so the answer is anti-defection provisions.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Political Science (Indian Constitution at Work)

90. Under Article 368, a Constitutional Amendment Bill requires —
(a) Simple majority in both Houses (b) Special majority in both Houses (c) Ratification by all states (d) Approval of the Supreme Court
Answer: (b)
Solution: Simple majority is the trap because most ordinary bills pass that way, and some constitutional changes (like creating new states) also need only a simple majority. An amendment bill under Article 368 needs a special majority — a majority of total membership and two-thirds of members present and voting in each House, so the answer is special majority in both Houses.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Political Science (Indian Constitution at Work)

91. The 'Basic Structure' doctrine of the Constitution was propounded by the Supreme Court in —
(a) Golaknath case (b) Minerva Mills case (c) Kesavananda Bharati case (d) Maneka Gandhi case
Answer: (c)
Solution: The Golaknath case is the trap because it is chronologically earlier and also dealt with amendability of Fundamental Rights, but it did not lay down the basic structure doctrine. That doctrine was propounded in the Kesavananda Bharati case of 1973, so the answer is Kesavananda Bharati case.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Political Science (Indian Constitution at Work)

92. Who is known as the 'Father of the Indian Constitution'?
(a) Mahatma Gandhi (b) Dr. Rajendra Prasad (c) Dr. B. R. Ambedkar (d) Jawaharlal Nehru
Answer: (c)
Solution: Jawaharlal Nehru is a tempting distractor because he moved the Objectives Resolution, which became the basis of the Preamble. But as Chairman of the Drafting Committee, Dr. B. R. Ambedkar is honoured as the Father of the Indian Constitution, so the answer is Dr. B. R. Ambedkar.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Political Science (Indian Constitution at Work)

93. The Right to Education as a Fundamental Right under Article 21A was inserted by —
(a) 42nd Amendment (b) 86th Amendment (c) 73rd Amendment (d) 91st Amendment
Answer: (b)
Solution: The 42nd Amendment is the trap because it is the most famous amendment and students attach many changes to it. Article 21A, guaranteeing free and compulsory education to children aged 6 to 14, was inserted by the 86th Amendment of 2002, so the answer is the 86th Amendment.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Political Science (Indian Constitution at Work)

94. The Vice-President of India is the ex-officio Chairman of —
(a) Lok Sabha (b) Inter-State Council (c) NITI Aayog (d) Rajya Sabha
Answer: (d)
Solution: Lok Sabha is the tempting wrong answer for students who confuse the presiding officers of the two Houses; the Lok Sabha is presided over by the Speaker. Under Article 64, the Vice-President is the ex-officio Chairman of the Rajya Sabha, so the answer is Rajya Sabha.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Political Science (Indian Constitution at Work)

95. Which Article empowers the Supreme Court to issue writs for the enforcement of Fundamental Rights?
(a) Article 143 (b) Article 226 (c) Article 136 (d) Article 32
Answer: (d)
Solution: Article 226 is the classic trap because it also deals with writs, but it empowers the High Courts, not the Supreme Court, and covers other legal rights too. Article 32, called the 'heart and soul of the Constitution' by Dr. Ambedkar, empowers the Supreme Court to issue writs, so the answer is Article 32.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Political Science (Indian Constitution at Work)

96. The 73rd Constitutional Amendment is related to —
(a) Municipalities (b) Anti-defection (c) Panchayati Raj Institutions (d) Right to Education
Answer: (c)
Solution: Municipalities is the tempting wrong answer because it was covered by the very next amendment, the 74th, and students routinely swap the two numbers. The 73rd Amendment of 1992 gave constitutional status to Panchayati Raj Institutions and added the Eleventh Schedule, so the answer is Panchayati Raj Institutions.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Political Science (Indian Constitution at Work)

97. The total number of members nominated by the President to the Rajya Sabha is —
(a) 12 (b) 10 (c) 2 (d) 14
Answer: (a)
Solution: Two is the trap because the President earlier nominated two Anglo-Indian members to the Lok Sabha, and students mix up the two provisions. The President nominates 12 members with special knowledge of literature, science, art and social service to the Rajya Sabha, so the answer is 12.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Political Science (Indian Constitution at Work)

98. Which of the following pairs is correctly matched?
(a) Languages — Tenth Schedule (b) Anti-defection — Ninth Schedule (c) Concurrent List — Seventh Schedule (d) Panchayats — Twelfth Schedule
Answer: (c)
Solution: Each wrong option shifts the schedule by one number, a deliberate near-miss trap: anti-defection is the Tenth Schedule, languages the Eighth, and Panchayats the Eleventh. The Union, State and Concurrent Lists are indeed in the Seventh Schedule, so the answer is Concurrent List — Seventh Schedule.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Political Science (Indian Constitution at Work)

99. The first meeting of the Constituent Assembly of India was held on —
(a) 13 December 1946 (b) 9 December 1946 (c) 11 December 1946 (d) 26 November 1949
Answer: (b)
Solution: 13 December 1946 is the trap because on that date Nehru moved the Objectives Resolution, a more famous event, but it was the second sitting. The Constituent Assembly first met on 9 December 1946 in the Constitution Hall, New Delhi, so the answer is 9 December 1946.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Political Science (Indian Constitution at Work)

100. Which Article of the Constitution deals with the amendment procedure?
(a) Article 356 (b) Article 360 (c) Article 368 (d) Article 370
Answer: (c)
Solution: Article 370 is the tempting wrong option because it is frequently in the news, but it dealt with the special status of Jammu and Kashmir, not the general amendment procedure. The power and procedure to amend the Constitution are laid down in Article 368, so the answer is Article 368.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Political Science (Indian Constitution at Work)
