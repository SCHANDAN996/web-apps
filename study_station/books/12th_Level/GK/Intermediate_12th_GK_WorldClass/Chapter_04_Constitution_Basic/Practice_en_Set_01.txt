1. The Constitution of India was adopted by the Constituent Assembly on:
(a) 26 November 1949 (b) 15 August 1947 (c) 26 January 1950 (d) 2 October 1950
Answer: (a)
Solution: Many students confuse the adoption date with the enforcement date and pick 26 January 1950, but that is when the Constitution came into force. The Constituent Assembly adopted the Constitution on 26 November 1949, which is now celebrated as Constitution Day, so the answer is 26 November 1949.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Indian Constitution at Work

2. Who was the Chairman of the Drafting Committee of the Constituent Assembly?
(a) Jawaharlal Nehru (b) Dr. B. R. Ambedkar (c) Dr. Rajendra Prasad (d) Sardar Vallabhbhai Patel
Answer: (b)
Solution: Dr. Rajendra Prasad was the President of the Constituent Assembly, not the Drafting Committee Chairman — a common mix-up. The Drafting Committee was chaired by Dr. B. R. Ambedkar, so the answer is Dr. B. R. Ambedkar.

4. The idea of the Preamble of the Indian Constitution was borrowed from the Constitution of:
(a) United States of America (b) United Kingdom (c) Ireland (d) Canada
Answer: (a)
Solution: Ireland gave us the Directive Principles, not the Preamble — a frequent confusion. The Preamble was borrowed from the Constitution of the United States of America, so the answer is United States of America.

5. How many Fundamental Rights were originally provided in the Constitution of India?
(a) Seven (b) Six (c) Eight (d) Five
Answer: (a)
Solution: Students often answer six because that is the current number, but the question asks about the original Constitution. Originally there were seven Fundamental Rights; the Right to Property was removed by the 44th Amendment in 1978, so the answer is seven.

6. Which Article of the Indian Constitution abolishes untouchability?
(a) Article 14 (b) Article 15 (c) Article 19 (d) Article 17
Answer: (d)
Solution: Article 14 deals with equality before law and Article 15 with prohibition of discrimination, which tempts many students. Article 17 specifically abolishes untouchability, so the answer is Article 17.

7. The Directive Principles of State Policy are contained in which Part of the Constitution?
(a) Part III (b) Part II (c) Part IVA (d) Part IV
Answer: (d)
Solution: Part III contains Fundamental Rights, which is the tempting wrong choice. Directive Principles are in Part IV (Articles 36–51), so the answer is Part IV.

8. Fundamental Duties were added to the Constitution by which amendment?
(a) 42nd Amendment (b) 24th Amendment (c) 44th Amendment (d) 52nd Amendment
Answer: (a)
Solution: The 44th Amendment is tempting because it removed the Right to Property, but it did not add Fundamental Duties. Fundamental Duties were added by the 42nd Amendment in 1976 on the recommendation of the Swaran Singh Committee, so the answer is the 42nd Amendment.

9. The words 'Socialist' and 'Secular' were added to the Preamble by:
(a) 73rd Amendment, 1992 (b) 44th Amendment, 1978 (c) 1st Amendment, 1951 (d) 42nd Amendment, 1976
Answer: (d)
Solution: The 44th Amendment is a common wrong pick because it also dealt with the Preamble's context, but it did not add these words. The 42nd Amendment of 1976 added 'Socialist', 'Secular' and 'Integrity' to the Preamble, so the answer is the 42nd Amendment, 1976.

12. Which Article of the Constitution deals with the Right to Constitutional Remedies?
(a) Article 19 (b) Article 21 (c) Article 32 (d) Article 226
Answer: (c)
Solution: Article 226 is tempting but it is the High Court's writ power, not the Fundamental Right itself. Article 32, called the 'heart and soul of the Constitution' by Dr. Ambedkar, guarantees the Right to Constitutional Remedies, so the answer is Article 32.

13. The Constituent Assembly of India was formed under which plan?
(a) Cripps Mission (b) Mountbatten Plan (c) Cabinet Mission Plan (d) Wavell Plan
Answer: (c)
Solution: The Cripps Mission of 1942 proposed a constituent body but its proposals were rejected, making it a tempting wrong answer. The Constituent Assembly was actually formed in 1946 under the Cabinet Mission Plan, so the answer is the Cabinet Mission Plan.

14. Which of the following is NOT a Fundamental Right under the Indian Constitution?
(a) Right to Equality (b) Right to Property (c) Right to Freedom (d) Right against Exploitation
Answer: (b)
Solution: This is a negative-wording trap; students in a hurry miss the word 'NOT'. The Right to Property was removed from Fundamental Rights by the 44th Amendment in 1978 and is now only a legal right under Article 300A, so the answer is Right to Property.

16. How many members are there in the Fundamental Duties list under Article 51A?
(a) 10 (b) 12 (c) 11 (d) 9
Answer: (c)
Solution: Ten is tempting because the 42nd Amendment originally added ten duties. The 86th Amendment of 2002 added one more duty (regarding education of one's child), making the total eleven, so the answer is 11.

17. The 'Objective Resolution' in the Constituent Assembly was moved by:
(a) Dr. B. R. Ambedkar (b) Dr. Rajendra Prasad (c) Sardar Patel (d) Jawaharlal Nehru
Answer: (d)
Solution: Dr. Rajendra Prasad presided over the Assembly, which tempts some students, but presiding is different from moving the resolution. Jawaharlal Nehru moved the Objective Resolution on 13 December 1946, which later became the basis of the Preamble, so the answer is Jawaharlal Nehru.

18. Which Part of the Indian Constitution deals with Fundamental Rights?
(a) Part II (b) Part III (c) Part IV (d) Part IVA
Answer: (b)
Solution: Part IV deals with Directive Principles and is the common confusion. Fundamental Rights are contained in Part III (Articles 12–35), so the answer is Part III.

19. The Indian Constitution is:
(a) Entirely rigid (b) Partly rigid and partly flexible (c) Entirely flexible (d) Unwritten
Answer: (b)
Solution: Some students pick 'entirely rigid' thinking of the difficult amendment process, but many provisions can be amended by simple majority. The Indian Constitution is a unique blend of rigidity and flexibility, so the answer is partly rigid and partly flexible.

21. Consider the following statements about the Preamble:
1. The Preamble is a part of the Constitution.
2. The Preamble can be amended.
3. The word 'Secular' was present in the original Preamble of 1950.
Which of the statements given above is/are correct?
(a) 1, 2 and 3 (b) 2 and 3 only (c) 1 and 3 only (d) 1 and 2 only
Answer: (d)
Solution: Statement 3 is the trap — 'Secular' was NOT in the original Preamble; it was inserted by the 42nd Amendment in 1976. The Supreme Court in the Kesavananda Bharati case held the Preamble is part of the Constitution and can be amended subject to the basic structure, so the answer is 1 and 2 only.

Solution: This is an almost-correct-statement trap; Articles 21, 19 and 25 are correctly matched, so students often miss the odd one. Article 23 prohibits human trafficking and forced labour, while abolition of titles is Article 18, so the answer is Article 23 — Abolition of Titles.

24. Arrange the following events in correct chronological order:
1. First session of the Constituent Assembly
2. Adoption of the Constitution
3. Enforcement of the Constitution
4. Cabinet Mission Plan
(a) 1, 4, 2, 3 (b) 4, 1, 2, 3 (c) 4, 2, 1, 3 (d) 1, 2, 4, 3
Answer: (b)
Solution: The chronological trap is placing the first session before the Cabinet Mission Plan, but the Assembly was formed under that very Plan. The correct order is Cabinet Mission Plan (1946), first session (9 December 1946), adoption (26 November 1949), enforcement (26 January 1950), so the answer is 4, 1, 2, 3.

25. Consider the following statements about the 42nd Constitutional Amendment:
1. It is known as the 'Mini Constitution'.
2. It added Fundamental Duties to the Constitution.
3. It was passed during the Prime Ministership of Morarji Desai.
Which of the statements given above is/are correct?
(a) 2 and 3 only (b) 1 and 2 only (c) 1 only (d) 1, 2 and 3
Answer: (b)
Solution: Statement 3 is the trap — the 42nd Amendment was passed in 1976 during Indira Gandhi's tenure, not Morarji Desai's; Desai's government brought the 44th Amendment. The 42nd Amendment is called the Mini Constitution and added Fundamental Duties, so the answer is 1 and 2 only.
