# Indian Constitution (Basic)

## 🎯 The Hook: A Book Written by 299 People

Imagine 299 people — lawyers, freedom fighters, economists, social reformers — sitting in one hall in Delhi, debating every single word of a book for **2 years, 11 months and 18 days**. That book became the **Constitution of India** — the longest written constitution of any country in the world.

When it was finally adopted on **26 November 1949**, it had 395 Articles and 8 Schedules. Today it has grown to 448 Articles and 12 Schedules. Every question in SSC, Banking, Railway and State PCS exams on "Polity" begins from this chapter. Master this, and 60% of Indian Polity becomes easy.

## 1. The Constituent Assembly: Who Wrote Our Constitution?

### How was it formed?

- The idea of a Constituent Assembly was first demanded by **M.N. Roy in 1934**.
- The **Indian National Congress** officially demanded it in 1935.
- The Assembly was constituted in **1946** under the **Cabinet Mission Plan**.
- Members were **indirectly elected** by the Provincial Legislative Assemblies (not directly by the people).

### Key facts about the Assembly

| Fact | Detail |
|---|---|
| First meeting | **9 December 1946** (Constitution Hall, New Delhi) |
| Temporary President (first meeting) | **Dr. Sachchidananda Sinha** (oldest member, French practice) |
| Permanent President | **Dr. Rajendra Prasad** (elected 11 December 1946) |
| Vice-Presidents | H.C. Mukherjee and V.T. Krishnamachari |
| Constitutional Advisor | **B.N. Rau** |
| Total members (originally) | 389 (after Partition: 299) |
| Total sittings | 11 sessions, 165 days |
| Time taken | 2 years, 11 months, 18 days |
| Money spent | About ₹64 lakh |

### The Drafting Committee

- Set up on **29 August 1947**.
- **Chairman: Dr. B.R. Ambedkar** — hence called the "Father of the Indian Constitution".
- It had **7 members**. Other important members: N. Gopalaswami Ayyangar, Alladi Krishnaswami Ayyar, K.M. Munshi, Syed Mohammad Saadullah, B.L. Mitter (later replaced by N. Madhava Rau), D.P. Khaitan (replaced after death by T.T. Krishnamachari).

## 2. The Three Golden Dates

This is the most-asked area — learn it perfectly.

| Date | Event |
|---|---|
| **26 November 1949** | Constitution **adopted** (passed and signed). Some provisions (citizenship, elections, provisional Parliament) came into force immediately. |
| **26 January 1950** | Constitution **fully enforced**. India became a Republic. |
| **26 January 1930** | **Purna Swaraj Day** — the reason 26 January was chosen for enforcement. |

**Why 26 January?** On 26 January 1930, the Congress observed "Purna Swaraj" (Complete Independence) Day after the Lahore Session (1929). To honour that date, the Constitution was enforced on 26 January 1950. This day is celebrated as **Republic Day**; 26 November is celebrated as **Constitution Day (Samvidhan Divas)** since 2015.

## 3. Sources of the Constitution (Borrowed Features)

Examiners love this table. Our Constitution is called a "bag of borrowings" — but wisely borrowed.

| Source Country | Features Borrowed |
|---|---|
| **Britain** | Parliamentary system, Rule of Law, Single Citizenship, Cabinet system, Bicameralism |
| **USA** | Fundamental Rights, Judicial Review, Independence of Judiciary, Impeachment of President, Preamble |
| **Ireland** | Directive Principles of State Policy (DPSP), Nomination of Rajya Sabha members, Method of election of President |
| **Canada** | Federation with a strong Centre, Residuary powers with the Centre, Appointment of Governors by Centre |
| **Australia** | Concurrent List, Joint sitting of both Houses, Freedom of trade |
| **Germany (Weimar)** | Emergency provisions |
| **USSR (Russia)** | Fundamental Duties, Five-Year Plans, ideals of justice (social, economic, political) in Preamble |
| **France** | Ideals of Republic, liberty–equality–fraternity in Preamble |
| **South Africa** | Amendment procedure, Election of Rajya Sabha members |
| **Japan** | Procedure established by Law |
| **Government of India Act, 1935** | Federal scheme, Office of Governor, Judiciary, Public Service Commissions, Emergency provisions, Administrative details (about 250 provisions) |

### 🧠 Mnemonic 1 — "B-U-I-C-A-G-R-F-S-J"
**B**ritain (Parliament), **U**SA (Fundamental Rights), **I**reland (DPSP), **C**anada (Strong-Centre Federation), **A**ustralia (Concurrent List), **G**ermany (Emergency), **R**ussia (Fundamental Duties), **F**rance (Republic ideals), **S**outh Africa (Amendment), **J**apan (Procedure established by law).

## 4. The Preamble: The Soul of the Constitution

The Preamble is the introduction or preface of the Constitution. It is based on the **Objectives Resolution** moved by **Jawaharlal Nehru** on 13 December 1946.

**Text (key words):**
> WE, THE PEOPLE OF INDIA, having solemnly resolved to constitute India into a **SOVEREIGN SOCIALIST SECULAR DEMOCRATIC REPUBLIC** and to secure to all its citizens:
> **JUSTICE** — social, economic and political;
> **LIBERTY** — of thought, expression, belief, faith and worship;
> **EQUALITY** — of status and of opportunity;
> **FRATERNITY** — assuring the dignity of the individual and the unity and integrity of the Nation.

### Key points

- The words **"Socialist", "Secular" and "Integrity"** were added by the **42nd Amendment, 1976** (the "Mini Constitution").
- The Preamble was held to be a **part of the Constitution** in the **Kesavananda Bharati case (1973)**.
- The Preamble is **not enforceable** in courts (not justiciable).
- It has been amended only **once** (42nd Amendment, 1976).

### 🧠 Mnemonic 2 — Preamble keywords: "S-S-S-D-R"
**S**overeign – **S**ocialist – **S**ecular – **D**emocratic – **R**epublic.
And the four promises: **J**ustice, **L**iberty, **E**quality, **F**raternity → **"JLEF"** (think: "Justice Lets Everyone Flourish").

🧠 **Active Recall:** Which three words were added by the 42nd Amendment? Is the Preamble enforceable in court?

## 5. Basic Features of the Constitution

1. **Longest written constitution** in the world.
2. **Parliamentary form of government** (British model) — real executive is the Council of Ministers headed by the Prime Minister.
3. **Federal system with unitary bias** — strong Centre (K.C. Wheare called it "quasi-federal").
4. **Single citizenship** (unlike the USA's dual citizenship).
5. **Universal Adult Franchise** — voting right to all adults from the very beginning.
6. **Independent judiciary** with the Supreme Court at the top.
7. **Fundamental Rights** (Part III) — justiciable.
8. **Directive Principles** (Part IV) — non-justiciable guidelines for the State.
9. **Fundamental Duties** (Part IV-A) — added by the 42nd Amendment, 1976, on the recommendation of the **Swaran Singh Committee**; originally 10 duties, now **11** (86th Amendment, 2002 added the 11th — education of one's child).
10. **Blend of rigidity and flexibility** in amendment (Article 368).

### Schedules of the Constitution

- Originally **8 Schedules**; now **12**.
- Added later: **9th** (1st Amendment, 1951 — land reforms), **10th** (52nd Amendment, 1985 — anti-defection), **11th** (73rd Amendment, 1992 — Panchayats), **12th** (74th Amendment, 1992 — Municipalities).

### 🧠 Mnemonic 3 — Schedules added: "1-52-73-74"
1st Amendment → 9th Schedule; 52nd → 10th; 73rd → 11th; 74th → 12th. Remember: **"Anti-defection came with Rajiv's 52, Panchayat-Nagar with 73-74."**

🧠 **Active Recall:** How many schedules were there originally? Which amendment added the 10th Schedule and what does it deal with?

- **Constitution Day (Samvidhan Divas)** is celebrated every year on **26 November** (started in 2015, marking 125th birth anniversary year of Dr. Ambedkar).

## ⚠️ Caution: Examiner's Trap

**Trap 1 — Chronological Confusion (कालानुक्रमिक भ्रम):**
"Constitution of India was adopted on 26 January 1950." — **Wrong!** Adopted on **26 November 1949**, enforced on **26 January 1950**. Examiners deliberately swap these two dates. Also, the first meeting (9 Dec 1946) is often swapped with the Objectives Resolution date (13 Dec 1946).

**Trap 2 — Almost-True Statement (लगभग सही कथन):**
"Dr. Rajendra Prasad was the Chairman of the Drafting Committee." — **Wrong!** He was the *President of the Constituent Assembly*; **Dr. B.R. Ambedkar** was the *Drafting Committee Chairman*. These two roles are the most commonly interchanged.

**Trap 3 — Similar Names / Sources (समान नाम/स्रोत):**
"DPSP was borrowed from the USA." — **Wrong!** DPSP came from **Ireland**; Fundamental Rights came from the **USA**. Similarly, "Procedure established by Law" is from **Japan**, while "Due Process of Law" is from the **USA** — examiners swap these constantly.

## ✅ Summary

The Indian Constitution was crafted by the Constituent Assembly (first meeting 9 Dec 1946) under President Dr. Rajendra Prasad, with Dr. B.R. Ambedkar chairing the Drafting Committee. Adopted on 26 November 1949 and enforced on 26 January 1950, it is the world's longest written constitution, borrowing wisely from many nations. The Preamble — its soul — promises Justice, Liberty, Equality and Fraternity. Master the dates, the sources table, and the key personalities, and this chapter will never cost you a mark again.
