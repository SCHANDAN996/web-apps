Questions 51–75: Medium

51. The idea of a Constituent Assembly for India was first put forward by
(a) Mahatma Gandhi (b) M. N. Roy (c) Jawaharlal Nehru (d) B. R. Ambedkar
Answer: (b)
Solution: Many students pick Nehru because he moved the Objectives Resolution, but the demand for a Constituent Assembly was first raised by M. N. Roy in 1934, so the answer is M. N. Roy.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Indian Constitution at Work

52. The Constituent Assembly of India was constituted under the scheme of the
(a) Cripps Mission (b) Mountbatten Plan (c) Cabinet Mission Plan (d) Wavell Plan
Answer: (c)
Solution: The Cripps Mission (1942) only proposed the idea; the Constituent Assembly was actually formed in 1946 under the Cabinet Mission Plan, so the answer is Cabinet Mission Plan.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Indian Constitution at Work

53. The first meeting of the Constituent Assembly was held on
(a) 15 August 1947 (b) 13 December 1946 (c) 26 January 1947 (d) 9 December 1946
Answer: (d)
Solution: 13 December 1946 is when Nehru moved the Objectives Resolution, a common chronological trap; the Assembly first met on 9 December 1946, so the answer is 9 December 1946.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Indian Constitution at Work

54. Who was the permanent President of the Constituent Assembly?
(a) Dr. Sachchidananda Sinha (b) B. R. Ambedkar (c) Dr. Rajendra Prasad (d) Jawaharlal Nehru
Answer: (c)
Solution: Dr. Sinha was only the temporary (interim) chairman for the first two days — the classic trap; the permanent President elected on 11 December 1946 was Dr. Rajendra Prasad, so the answer is Dr. Rajendra Prasad.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Indian Constitution at Work

55. The Chairman of the Drafting Committee of the Constituent Assembly was
(a) Jawaharlal Nehru (b) Sardar Patel (c) Dr. B. R. Ambedkar (d) Dr. Rajendra Prasad
Answer: (c)
Solution: Nehru chaired the Union Powers Committee and Patel the Provincial Constitution Committee — similar big names cause confusion; the Drafting Committee (set up 29 August 1947) was chaired by Dr. B. R. Ambedkar, so the answer is Dr. B. R. Ambedkar.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Indian Constitution at Work

56. The Constitution of India was adopted by the Constituent Assembly on
(a) 26 November 1949 (b) 26 January 1950 (c) 15 August 1947 (d) 26 January 1949
Answer: (a)
Solution: 26 January 1950 is the date of enforcement, not adoption — the most common chronological trap; the Constitution was adopted on 26 November 1949, so the answer is 26 November 1949.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Indian Constitution at Work

57. The Constitution of India came into force on
(a) 26 November 1949 (b) 15 August 1947 (c) 2 October 1950 (d) 26 January 1950
Answer: (d)
Solution: 26 November 1949 is the adoption date; the bulk of the Constitution came into force on 26 January 1950, chosen to honour Purna Swaraj Day (26 January 1930), so the answer is 26 January 1950.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Indian Constitution at Work

58. The words "Socialist" and "Secular" were added to the Preamble by the
(a) 44th Amendment, 1978 (b) 42nd Amendment, 1976 (c) 52nd Amendment, 1985 (d) 1st Amendment, 1951
Answer: (b)
Solution: The 44th Amendment is a tempting distractor because it also dealt with emergency-era changes, but "Socialist", "Secular" and "Integrity" were inserted by the 42nd Amendment, 1976, so the answer is 42nd Amendment, 1976.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Indian Constitution at Work

59. The Preamble of the Indian Constitution is based on the Objectives Resolution moved by
(a) Dr. B. R. Ambedkar (b) Sardar Patel (c) Jawaharlal Nehru (d) Dr. Rajendra Prasad
Answer: (c)
Solution: Ambedkar is tempting because he is called the "Father of the Constitution", but the Objectives Resolution (13 December 1946) was moved by Jawaharlal Nehru, so the answer is Jawaharlal Nehru.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Indian Constitution at Work

60. In which case did the Supreme Court hold that the Preamble is a part of the Constitution?
(a) Kesavananda Bharati case (b) Golaknath case (c) Minerva Mills case (d) Berubari Union case
Answer: (a)
Solution: In the Berubari Union case (1960) the Court said the Preamble is NOT a part of the Constitution — the classic trap; in Kesavananda Bharati (1973) it held the Preamble IS a part of the Constitution, so the answer is Kesavananda Bharati case.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Indian Constitution at Work

61. The concept of the Directive Principles of State Policy was borrowed from the Constitution of
(a) Ireland (b) Canada (c) USA (d) Australia
Answer: (a)
Solution: Australia is tempting because we borrowed the concurrent list from it, but the DPSPs were borrowed from the Irish Constitution (which itself took them from Spain), so the answer is Ireland.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Indian Constitution at Work

62. The Fundamental Rights in the Indian Constitution are inspired by the Constitution of
(a) UK (b) Canada (c) Ireland (d) USA
Answer: (d)
Solution: The UK gave us the parliamentary system and rule of law, not Fundamental Rights; the idea of Fundamental Rights came from the USA (Bill of Rights), so the answer is USA.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Indian Constitution at Work

63. Which Article of the Constitution abolishes untouchability?
(a) Article 14 (b) Article 17 (c) Article 15 (d) Article 18
Answer: (b)
Solution: Article 18 (abolition of titles) is the near-neighbour trap; Article 17 abolishes untouchability and forbids its practice in any form, so the answer is Article 17.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Indian Constitution at Work

64. The Right to Constitutional Remedies under Article 32 was called the "heart and soul of the Constitution" by
(a) Jawaharlal Nehru (b) Dr. Rajendra Prasad (c) Dr. B. R. Ambedkar (d) Sardar Patel
Answer: (c)
Solution: Nehru is a tempting guess for famous constitutional quotes, but it was Dr. B. R. Ambedkar who described Article 32 as the heart and soul of the Constitution, so the answer is Dr. B. R. Ambedkar.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Indian Constitution at Work

65. Which of the following writs can be issued against a private individual also?
(a) Mandamus (b) Prohibition (c) Habeas Corpus (d) Quo Warranto
Answer: (c)
Solution: Mandamus, Prohibition and Quo Warranto lie only against public authorities or courts; Habeas Corpus can be issued against a private person who has illegally detained someone, so the answer is Habeas Corpus.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Indian Constitution at Work

66. Fundamental Duties were added to the Constitution by the
(a) 73rd Amendment, 1992 (b) 44th Amendment, 1978 (c) 86th Amendment, 2002 (d) 42nd Amendment, 1976
Answer: (d)
Solution: The 86th Amendment only added the 11th duty (parents to educate children) — a frequent trap; the original ten Fundamental Duties were inserted by the 42nd Amendment, 1976 on the Swaran Singh Committee's recommendation, so the answer is 42nd Amendment, 1976.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Indian Constitution at Work

67. Fundamental Duties are contained in which Article of the Constitution?
(a) Article 51A (b) Article 51 (c) Article 50 (d) Article 52
Answer: (a)
Solution: Article 51 (promotion of international peace, a DPSP) is the look-alike trap; Fundamental Duties are in Article 51A, Part IVA, so the answer is Article 51A.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Indian Constitution at Work

68. The minimum age to become the President of India is
(a) 35 years (b) 30 years (c) 25 years (d) 40 years
Answer: (a)
Solution: 25 years (Lok Sabha) and 30 years (Rajya Sabha/Vice-President) are the classic confusion numbers; the President must be at least 35 years old, so the answer is 35 years.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Indian Constitution at Work

69. The President of India is elected by an electoral college consisting of
(a) Elected members of both Houses of Parliament only (b) Elected members of Parliament and elected members of State Legislative Assemblies (c) All members of Parliament and State Legislatures (d) Elected members of Parliament, State Assemblies and Union Territory Assemblies of Delhi and Puducherry
Answer: (d)
Solution: Option (b) is the almost-correct trap — it omits the elected MLAs of Delhi and Puducherry, who were included by the 70th Amendment, 1992; the full electoral college includes them, so the answer is the option naming Parliament, State Assemblies and the Delhi and Puducherry Assemblies.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Indian Constitution at Work

70. The Vice-President of India is the ex-officio Chairman of the
(a) Lok Sabha (b) Inter-State Council (c) NITI Aayog (d) Rajya Sabha
Answer: (d)
Solution: The Speaker presides over the Lok Sabha, a common mix-up; under Article 64 the Vice-President is the ex-officio Chairman of the Rajya Sabha, so the answer is Rajya Sabha.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Indian Constitution at Work

71. The maximum strength of the Lok Sabha envisaged by the Constitution is
(a) 545 (b) 552 (c) 550 (d) 560
Answer: (b)
Solution: 545 was the actual strength before the Anglo-Indian provision ended — the tempting number; the constitutional maximum is 552 (530 from states, 20 from UTs, 2 Anglo-Indians), so the answer is 552.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Indian Constitution at Work

72. Money Bills can be introduced only in the
(a) Rajya Sabha (b) Lok Sabha (c) Either House of Parliament (d) State Legislatures with prior sanction
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Either House" is tempting because ordinary bills can start anywhere, but under Article 109 a Money Bill can be introduced only in the Lok Sabha, so the answer is Lok Sabha.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Indian Constitution at Work

73. Who decides whether a bill is a Money Bill or not?
(a) The President (b) The Speaker of the Lok Sabha (c) The Chairman of the Rajya Sabha (d) The Finance Minister
Answer: (b)
Solution: The President is tempting since a Money Bill needs his prior recommendation, but the final decision on whether a bill is a Money Bill rests with the Speaker of the Lok Sabha (Article 110), so the answer is the Speaker of the Lok Sabha.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Indian Constitution at Work

74. The judges of the Supreme Court are appointed by the
(a) President (b) Prime Minister (c) Chief Justice of India alone (d) Parliament
Answer: (a)
Solution: The Chief Justice is tempting because of the collegium's role in recommending names, but formally Supreme Court judges are appointed by the President under Article 124, so the answer is President.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Indian Constitution at Work

75. A Supreme Court judge holds office until the age of
(a) 65 years (b) 62 years (c) 60 years (d) 70 years
Answer: (a)
Solution: 62 years is the retirement age of High Court judges — the classic similar-numbers trap; a Supreme Court judge holds office until 65 years, so the answer is 65 years.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Indian Constitution at Work
