101. Which Article of the Indian Constitution deals with the amendment procedure?
(a) Article 370 (b) Article 360 (c) Article 356 (d) Article 368
Answer: (d)
Solution: Article 356 (President's Rule) and Article 360 (financial emergency) are tempting because all three are high-numbered emergency-era Articles, but they deal with emergencies, not amendments. Article 368 in Part XX lays down the power and procedure of Parliament to amend the Constitution, so the answer is Article 368.

102. The idea of a Constituent Assembly for India was first put forward by:
(a) M.N. Roy (b) Mahatma Gandhi (c) Jawaharlal Nehru (d) B.R. Ambedkar
Answer: (a)
Solution: Nehru is the tempting wrong choice because he moved the Objectives Resolution, but moving a resolution is not the same as first proposing the idea. M.N. Roy, a pioneer of the communist movement in India, first put forward the idea of a Constituent Assembly in 1934, so the answer is M.N. Roy.

103. The Constitution of India was adopted by the Constituent Assembly on:
(a) 26 January 1950 (b) 15 August 1947 (c) 26 January 1949 (d) 26 November 1949
Answer: (d)
Solution: 26 January 1950 is the classic trap — that is the date of enforcement (commencement), not adoption. The Constitution was adopted on 26 November 1949 (now celebrated as Constitution Day) and came into force on 26 January 1950, so the answer is 26 November 1949.

104. How many members of the Constituent Assembly signed the Constitution of India?
(a) 292 (b) 299 (c) 389 (d) 284
Answer: (d)
Solution: 299 is tempting because it was the total strength of the Assembly after the Mountbatten Plan, but not all members were present to sign. On 24 January 1950, 284 members actually signed the Constitution, so the answer is 284.

105. The Directive Principles of State Policy are contained in which Part of the Constitution?
(a) Part III (b) Part IV (c) Part IVA (d) Part V
Answer: (b)
Solution: Part IVA is the trap — it looks similar but contains Fundamental Duties, added by the 42nd Amendment. Directive Principles are in Part IV (Articles 36–51), borrowed from the Irish Constitution, so the answer is Part IV.

106. Which of the following is NOT a Fundamental Right under the Indian Constitution?
(a) Right to Equality (b) Right to Property (c) Right against Exploitation (d) Right to Constitutional Remedies
Answer: (b)
Solution: Right to Property is the trap in reverse — it WAS a Fundamental Right under Articles 19(1)(f) and 31, but the 44th Amendment (1978) removed it and made it a legal right under Article 300A. The other three remain Fundamental Rights, so the answer is Right to Property.

107. The concept of 'Procedure established by Law' in Article 21 was borrowed from:
(a) USA (b) Japan (c) UK (d) Ireland
Answer: (b)
Solution: USA is the tempting wrong answer because the American Constitution inspired 'due process of law', which is a different and wider concept. The expression 'procedure established by law' in Article 21 was taken from the Japanese Constitution, so the answer is Japan.

108. Who was the Chairman of the Drafting Committee of the Constituent Assembly?
(a) Dr. Rajendra Prasad (b) Jawaharlal Nehru (c) Dr. B.R. Ambedkar (d) Sardar Patel
Answer: (c)
Solution: Dr. Rajendra Prasad is the classic confusion — he was the President of the Constituent Assembly, not the Drafting Committee Chairman. Dr. B.R. Ambedkar chaired the Drafting Committee set up on 29 August 1947, so the answer is Dr. B.R. Ambedkar.

109. The 42nd Constitutional Amendment (1976) is often called the:
(a) Anti-Defection Amendment (b) Golden Amendment (c) Mini Constitution (d) Panchayati Raj Amendment
Answer: (c)
Solution: The Anti-Defection Amendment is tempting but that was the 52nd Amendment (1985). The 42nd Amendment made such sweeping changes — adding 'Socialist', 'Secular', 'Integrity' to the Preamble and adding Fundamental Duties — that it is called the Mini Constitution, so the answer is Mini Constitution.

110. Under which Article can the President of India declare a National Emergency?
(a) Article 360 (b) Article 356 (c) Article 352 (d) Article 368
Answer: (c)
Solution: Article 356 is the trap — it deals with President's Rule in a State (failure of constitutional machinery), not a National Emergency. Article 352 empowers the President to proclaim a National Emergency on grounds of war, external aggression or armed rebellion, so the answer is Article 352.

111. The Fundamental Duties were added to the Constitution on the recommendation of which committee?
(a) Balwant Rai Mehta Committee (b) Sarkaria Commission (c) Shah Commission (d) Swaran Singh Committee
Answer: (d)
Solution: Sarkaria Commission is tempting but it dealt with Centre-State relations. The Swaran Singh Committee recommended the inclusion of Fundamental Duties, which were added as Part IVA (Article 51A) by the 42nd Amendment in 1976, so the answer is Swaran Singh Committee.

112. How many Fundamental Duties are currently listed in Article 51A?
(a) 10 (b) 11 (c) 12 (d) 9
Answer: (b)
Solution: 10 is the trap — the 42nd Amendment (1976) originally added 10 duties, but the 86th Amendment (2002) added one more (duty of parents to provide education to children aged 6–14). The current total is 11, so the answer is 11.

113. The Preamble of the Indian Constitution was amended for the first and only time by:
(a) 1st Amendment (b) 44th Amendment (c) 24th Amendment (d) 42nd Amendment
Answer: (d)
Solution: The 44th Amendment is tempting because it also followed the Emergency era, but it did not touch the Preamble. The 42nd Amendment (1976) added the words 'Socialist', 'Secular' and 'Integrity' to the Preamble — its only amendment so far, so the answer is 42nd Amendment.

114. Which Schedule of the Constitution contains the list of recognised languages?
(a) Eighth Schedule (b) Ninth Schedule (c) Seventh Schedule (d) Tenth Schedule
Answer: (a)
Solution: The Seventh Schedule is the trap — it contains the Union, State and Concurrent Lists, not languages. The Eighth Schedule lists the recognised languages (originally 14, now 22), so the answer is Eighth Schedule.

115. The Anti-Defection Law is contained in which Schedule of the Constitution?
(a) Ninth Schedule (b) Tenth Schedule (c) Eleventh Schedule (d) Twelfth Schedule
Answer: (b)
Solution: The Ninth Schedule is tempting because of its similar number, but it protects certain laws from judicial review (added by the 1st Amendment). The Tenth Schedule, added by the 52nd Amendment (1985), contains the Anti-Defection Law, so the answer is Tenth Schedule.

116. Who described the Directive Principles of State Policy as a 'novel feature' of the Indian Constitution?
(a) Dr. B.R. Ambedkar (b) Jawaharlal Nehru (c) Dr. Rajendra Prasad (d) Granville Austin
Answer: (a)
Solution: Granville Austin is tempting because he famously analysed the Constitution, but he described the Constitution as a 'seamless web' and called Fundamental Rights and Directive Principles together the 'conscience of the Constitution'. It was Dr. B.R. Ambedkar who called the Directive Principles a 'novel feature' of the Constitution, so the answer is Dr. B.R. Ambedkar.

117. The 73rd Constitutional Amendment is related to:
(a) Municipalities (b) Cooperative Societies (c) Panchayats (d) Official Language
Answer: (c)
Solution: Municipalities is the classic trap — that is the 74th Amendment, passed in the same year (1992), which causes chronological confusion. The 73rd Amendment gave constitutional status to Panchayati Raj institutions and added the Eleventh Schedule, so the answer is Panchayats.

118. Which Article empowers the Supreme Court to issue writs for the enforcement of Fundamental Rights?
(a) Article 226 (b) Article 136 (c) Article 32 (d) Article 143
Answer: (c)
Solution: Article 226 is the trap — it also deals with writs, but it empowers High Courts (and for purposes beyond Fundamental Rights too). Article 32, which Dr. Ambedkar called the 'heart and soul' of the Constitution, empowers the Supreme Court to issue writs, so the answer is Article 32.

119. The words 'Socialist' and 'Secular' in the Preamble imply that India:
(a) Has no state religion and aims to reduce inequality (b) Has a state religion and a fully state-owned economy (c) Bans all religious practices and private property (d) Follows the economic model of the USSR exactly
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is the extreme reading — 'secular' does not mean banning religion, and 'socialist' does not mean abolishing private property (India follows a mixed economy). 'Secular' means the state has no religion of its own and treats all religions equally, while 'socialist' reflects the aim of reducing inequality, so the answer is that India has no state religion and aims to reduce inequality.

120. The minimum age to become the President of India is:
(a) 25 years (b) 30 years (c) 35 years (d) 40 years
Answer: (c)
Solution: 25 years is tempting because that is the minimum age for Lok Sabha membership, and 30 years for Rajya Sabha — a classic similar-numbers trap. Article 58 requires a presidential candidate to be at least 35 years of age and qualified for election to the Lok Sabha, so the answer is 35 years.

121. Consider the following statements:
1. The Preamble is enforceable in a court of law.
2. The Preamble was held to be a part of the Constitution in the Kesavananda Bharati case.
Which of the statements given above is/are correct?
(a) 1 only (b) 2 only (c) Both 1 and 2 (d) Neither 1 nor 2
Answer: (b)
Solution: Statement 1 is the trap — it sounds plausible because the Preamble is part of the Constitution, but the Supreme Court has held that the Preamble is not enforceable in courts and is not a source of power. In Kesavananda Bharati v. State of Kerala (1973), the Court held the Preamble to be a part of the Constitution, so the answer is 2 only.

122. Arrange the following events in chronological order:
1. Objectives Resolution moved
2. Formation of the Drafting Committee
3. Adoption of the Constitution
4. First meeting of the Constituent Assembly
(a) 4, 2, 1, 3 (b) 1, 4, 2, 3 (c) 1, 2, 4, 3 (d) 4, 1, 2, 3
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is placing the Objectives Resolution before the first meeting — Nehru moved it on 13 December 1946, but the Assembly's first meeting was on 9 December 1946, four days earlier. The correct order is: first meeting (9 Dec 1946), Objectives Resolution (13 Dec 1946), Drafting Committee (29 Aug 1947), adoption (26 Nov 1949), so the answer is 4, 1, 2, 3.

124. Consider the following statements about the 44th Amendment (1978):
1. It removed the Right to Property from the list of Fundamental Rights.
2. It replaced the term 'internal disturbance' with 'armed rebellion' in Article 352.
Which of the statements given above is/are correct?
(a) Both 1 and 2 (b) 2 only (c) Neither 1 nor 2 (d) 1 only
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is assuming only one statement is correct because the two changes belong to different subjects (rights and emergency). In fact, the 44th Amendment did both: it deleted Articles 19(1)(f) and 31 (making property a legal right under Article 300A) and substituted 'armed rebellion' for 'internal disturbance' in Article 352, so the answer is Both 1 and 2.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Political Science (Indian Constitution at Work)

125. Consider the following statements:
1. Article 368 provides for amendment of the Constitution only by a simple majority.
2. Amendments affecting federal provisions require ratification by at least half of the States.
Which of the statements given above is/are correct?
(a) 2 only (b) Both 1 and 2 (c) 1 only (d) Neither 1 nor 2
Answer: (a)
Solution: Statement 1 is the trap — it is an almost-correct statement, since some constitutional changes (like creation of new states) do pass by simple majority, but those are outside Article 368. Article 368 requires a special majority (majority of total membership and two-thirds of members present and voting), and amendments to federal provisions additionally need ratification by at least half the State legislatures, so the answer is 2 only.
