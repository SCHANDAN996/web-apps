Questions 126–150: Hard

126. The oxidation number of sulphur in H₂S₂O₈ (peroxodisulphuric acid) is
(a) +4 (b) +6 (c) +8 (d) +7
Answer: (b)
Solution: The tempting answer is +8, obtained by blindly solving 2(+1) + 2x + 8(−2) = 0, but H₂S₂O₈ contains a peroxide (–O–O–) linkage where each of those two oxygens is −1, not −2. Solving 2 + 2x + 6(−2) + 2(−1) = 0 gives x = +6, so the answer is +6.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Chemistry (Redox Reactions)

127. Which of the following has the highest first ionisation enthalpy?
(a) Mg (b) Na (c) Si (d) Al
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is Mg, chosen because its fully filled 3s² subshell makes it more stable than Al — but that only explains why Mg beats Al, not Si. Actual values are Na 496, Mg 738, Al 577 and Si 786 kJ/mol, so the answer is Si.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Chemistry (Classification of Elements and Periodicity)

128. The number of sigma (σ) and pi (π) bonds in a molecule of benzene (C₆H₆) respectively is
(a) 6 σ and 3 π (b) 12 σ and 3 π (c) 12 σ and 6 π (d) 6 σ and 6 π
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is counting only the ring's C–C bonds (6 σ) and forgetting the six C–H bonds. Benzene has 6 C–C σ bonds + 6 C–H σ bonds = 12 σ bonds, and 3 delocalised π bonds, so the answer is 12 σ and 3 π.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Chemistry (Chemical Bonding)

129. Which gas is produced when zinc reacts with dilute sulphuric acid?
(a) Hydrogen (b) Sulphur dioxide (c) Oxygen (d) Hydrogen sulphide
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is sulphur dioxide, which is indeed released — but only with hot concentrated H₂SO₄, not dilute acid. With dilute H₂SO₄, zinc, being above hydrogen in the reactivity series, displaces hydrogen: Zn + H₂SO₄ → ZnSO₄ + H₂, so the answer is hydrogen.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science (Chemical Reactions and Equations)

130. The pH of a 0.001 M solution of HCl is
(a) 11 (b) 2 (c) 1 (d) 3
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is 11, chosen by those who compute pOH instead of pH. HCl is a strong acid and fully ionises, so [H⁺] = 10⁻³ M and pH = −log(10⁻³) = 3, so the answer is 3.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science (Acids, Bases and Salts)

131. Which of the following is an amphoteric oxide?
(a) CO₂ (b) CaO (c) Al₂O₃ (d) SO₂
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is CaO, which sounds like a "versatile" metal oxide but is purely basic. Al₂O₃ reacts with both acids (forming AlCl₃) and bases (forming NaAlO₂), making it amphoteric, so the answer is Al₂O₃.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science (Metals and Non-metals)

132. In the electrolytic refining of copper, the anode is made of
(a) pure copper (b) platinum (c) impure copper (d) graphite
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is pure copper, which is actually the cathode where pure metal deposits. In refining, the impure copper block is made the anode so that copper dissolves from it and deposits on the pure copper cathode, so the answer is impure copper.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science (Metals and Non-metals)

133. The IUPAC name of CH₃–CH(Br)–CH₃ is
(a) 2-bromopropane (b) propyl bromide (c) 3-bromopropane (d) 1-bromopropane
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is 1-bromopropane, chosen by numbering from the wrong end; numbering must give the substituent the lowest locant. The bromine is on the middle carbon of the three-carbon chain, giving 2-bromopropane, so the answer is 2-bromopropane.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science (Carbon and its Compounds)

134. Which of the following elements is a liquid at room temperature?
(a) Fluorine (b) Iodine (c) Bromine (d) Chlorine
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is iodine, which looks "wet" when it sublimes but is a solid at room temperature. Among the halogens, only bromine is a liquid at room temperature (mercury is the only liquid metal), so the answer is bromine.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science (Periodic Classification of Elements)

135. The electronic configuration 2, 8, 8 corresponds to which ion?
(a) Cl⁻ (b) Mg²⁺ (c) Na⁺ (d) F⁻
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is Na⁺ or Mg²⁺, but both of those have configuration 2, 8 (10 electrons). Cl⁻ has 17 + 1 = 18 electrons, giving 2, 8, 8 (argon configuration), so the answer is Cl⁻.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Science (Structure of the Atom)

136. Which of the following statements about the Modern Periodic Table is correct?
(a) Elements are arranged in order of increasing atomic number (b) Elements are arranged in order of increasing atomic mass (c) There are 8 periods and 18 groups (d) Isotopes are placed in separate groups
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is increasing atomic mass, which was Mendeleev's basis, not the modern law. Moseley's work established atomic number as the basis; the table has 7 periods and 18 groups, and isotopes share the same position, so the answer is that elements are arranged in order of increasing atomic number.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science (Periodic Classification of Elements)

137. When concentrated nitric acid is added to copper turnings, the gas evolved is
(a) NO (b) NO₂ (c) N₂O (d) H₂
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is NO, which is indeed produced — but with dilute HNO₃, not concentrated. Concentrated HNO₃ oxidises copper to Cu(NO₃)₂ and is itself reduced to brown nitrogen dioxide gas, so the answer is NO₂.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Chemistry (The p-Block Elements)

138. The number of water molecules of crystallisation in gypsum is
(a) 10 (b) 1 (c) 5 (d) 2
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is 5, which belongs to blue vitriol (CuSO₄·5H₂O), a frequently confused similar formula. Gypsum is CaSO₄·2H₂O; heating it drives off 1.5 H₂O to give plaster of Paris, so the answer is 2.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science (Acids, Bases and Salts)

139. Which of the following elements shows the phenomenon of catenation most strongly?
(a) Silicon (b) Sulphur (c) Carbon (d) Germanium
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is silicon, which lies just below carbon and does show some catenation, but Si–Si bonds are much weaker than C–C bonds. Carbon's small size and strong C–C bond energy make its catenation the strongest, so the answer is carbon.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science (Carbon and its Compounds)

140. The correct order of increasing reactivity of the metals is
(a) Fe < Zn < Mg < K (b) K < Mg < Zn < Fe (c) Fe < Mg < Zn < K (d) Zn < Fe < Mg < K
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is the reversed series, which lists metals in decreasing reactivity. The standard reactivity series runs K > Na > Ca > Mg > Al > Zn > Fe, so the increasing order is Fe < Zn < Mg < K, so the answer is Fe < Zn < Mg < K.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science (Metals and Non-metals)

141. Aqua regia is a mixture of concentrated HCl and concentrated HNO₃ in the ratio
(a) 1 : 3 (b) 3 : 1 (c) 2 : 1 (d) 1 : 1
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is 1 : 3, which simply reverses the correct ratio — a classic examiner's confusion. Aqua regia contains three parts concentrated hydrochloric acid to one part concentrated nitric acid and can dissolve gold, so the answer is 3 : 1.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Chemistry (The p-Block Elements)

142. Which of the following is used as a moderator in a nuclear reactor?
(a) Heavy water (b) Cadmium (c) Uranium-235 (d) Plutonium
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is cadmium, which is used in reactors but as control rods to absorb neutrons, not to slow them. Heavy water (D₂O) slows down fast neutrons to thermal speeds, acting as a moderator, so the answer is heavy water.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science (Sources of Energy)

143. The molecular formula of washing soda is
(a) Na₂CO₃·H₂O (b) Na₂CO₃ (c) NaHCO₃ (d) Na₂CO₃·10H₂O
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is Na₂CO₃, which is only anhydrous soda ash, not washing soda. Washing soda is the decahydrate, Na₂CO₃·10H₂O, obtained by recrystallising sodium carbonate, so the answer is Na₂CO₃·10H₂O.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science (Acids, Bases and Salts)

144. Which of the following statements about diamond and graphite is correct?
(a) Both conduct electricity (b) Diamond conducts electricity, graphite does not (c) Graphite conducts electricity, diamond does not (d) Neither conducts electricity
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is assuming both behave alike because both are pure carbon allotropes. In graphite each carbon is sp² hybridised with one delocalised free electron per atom, allowing conduction; diamond's sp³ structure has no free electrons, so the answer is that graphite conducts electricity while diamond does not.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science (Carbon and its Compounds)

145. The atomic number of the element placed in Group 17, Period 3 of the Modern Periodic Table is
(a) 9 (b) 35 (c) 53 (d) 17
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is 9, which is fluorine — a Group 17 element but in Period 2, a positional confusion. Period 3's halogen is chlorine with atomic number 17 (bromine 35 is Period 4, iodine 53 is Period 5), so the answer is 17.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science (Periodic Classification of Elements)

146. Which of the following reactions is an example of a disproportionation reaction?
(a) Zn + CuSO₄ → ZnSO₄ + Cu (b) 2H₂O₂ → 2H₂O + O₂ (c) CaCO₃ → CaO + CO₂ (d) CH₄ + 2O₂ → CO₂ + 2H₂O
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is the decomposition of CaCO₃, which is decomposition but not redox at all — no oxidation state changes. In H₂O₂, oxygen (−1) is simultaneously oxidised to O₂ (0) and reduced to H₂O (−2), which is disproportionation, so the answer is 2H₂O₂ → 2H₂O + O₂.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Chemistry (Redox Reactions)

147. The number of neutrons in ²³⁵U is
(a) 92 (b) 235 (c) 327 (d) 143
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is 92, which is the number of protons (atomic number), not neutrons — a classic similar-quantity confusion. Neutrons = mass number − atomic number = 235 − 92 = 143, so the answer is 143.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Science (Structure of the Atom)

148. Which of the following pairs is correctly matched?
(a) Baking soda — Na₂CO₃·10H₂O (b) Caustic soda — Na₂CO₃ (c) Blue vitriol — FeSO₄·7H₂O (d) Plaster of Paris — CaSO₄·½H₂O
Answer: (d)
Solution: The traps rely on similar names: baking soda is NaHCO₃, blue vitriol is CuSO₄·5H₂O (FeSO₄·7H₂O is green vitriol), and caustic soda is NaOH. Plaster of Paris is correctly calcium sulphate hemihydrate, CaSO₄·½H₂O, so the answer is Plaster of Paris — CaSO₄·½H₂O.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science (Acids, Bases and Salts)

149. An element X forms an oxide X₂O₃. The most likely valency and group of X are
(a) valency 2, Group 2 (b) valency 3, Group 13 (c) valency 3, Group 15 (d) valency 6, Group 16
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is Group 15: nitrogen does form N₂O₃, but the standard exam example of an X₂O₃ oxide is a Group 13 metal like aluminium (Al₂O₃). Since oxygen has valency 2, X must have valency 3, and the typical representative is Group 13, so the answer is valency 3, Group 13.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science (Periodic Classification of Elements)

150. Which of the following statements about the reaction MnO₂ + 4HCl → MnCl₂ + Cl₂ + 2H₂O is correct?
(a) HCl is oxidised and MnO₂ is reduced (b) HCl is reduced and MnO₂ is oxidised (c) Both HCl and MnO₂ are oxidised (d) It is not a redox reaction
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is reversing the roles because HCl "loses" hydrogen; in fact chlorine in HCl goes from −1 to 0 (loses electrons, oxidised), while manganese goes from +4 in MnO₂ to +2 in MnCl₂ (gains electrons, reduced), so the answer is that HCl is oxidised and MnO₂ is reduced.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science (Chemical Reactions and Equations)
