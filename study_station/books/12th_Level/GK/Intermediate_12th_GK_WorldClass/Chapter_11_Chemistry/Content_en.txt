# General Chemistry

## The Hook: The Salt That Built an Empire

In 1930, a frail man in a loincloth walked 240 miles to the sea at Dandi and picked up a handful of salt. That single act shook the British Empire. But have you ever wondered — what *is* salt, chemically? Why did the British tax it so heavily? Because sodium chloride (NaCl) is not just a seasoning — it is a compound so essential that life, industry, and even revolutions have been built around it.

Chemistry is not a subject locked in laboratories. It is in the iron that rusts on your gate, the baking soda in your kitchen, the LPG cylinder in your home, and the oxygen in your lungs. And in every competitive exam — SSC, Railway, Banking, State PCS — General Chemistry questions appear again and again, testing exactly these everyday substances.

This chapter will make you see chemistry everywhere — and score everywhere too.

## 1. Matter and Its Classification

Everything around you that has mass and occupies space is **matter**. Matter exists in three common states — **solid, liquid, and gas** (plasma and Bose-Einstein condensate are advanced states, rarely asked at this level).

Matter is classified as:

- **Element** — a pure substance made of only one kind of atom. Cannot be broken down further by chemical means. Example: Oxygen (O), Gold (Au), Iron (Fe). There are **118 known elements**, of which 94 occur naturally.
- **Compound** — two or more elements chemically combined in a fixed ratio. Example: Water (H₂O), Carbon dioxide (CO₂), Salt (NaCl).
- **Mixture** — two or more substances physically mixed, in any ratio. Can be separated by physical methods. Example: Air, milk, soil, brass.

**Key distinction the examiner loves:** A compound has properties *completely different* from its constituent elements. Sodium is a violently reactive metal; chlorine is a poisonous gas — yet their compound, common salt, sits peacefully on your dining table!

🧠 **Active Recall:** Is air an element, compound, or mixture? Is water? Why can a mixture be separated physically but a compound cannot?

## 2. Elements, Symbols, and the Periodic Table

### Chemical Symbols — Where Do They Come From?

Many symbols come from **Latin names**, and this is a classic exam trap:

| Element | Symbol | Latin Name |
|---|---|---|
| Sodium | Na | Natrium |
| Potassium | K | Kalium |
| Iron | Fe | Ferrum |
| Copper | Cu | Cuprum |
| Silver | Ag | Argentum |
| Gold | Au | Aurum |
| Mercury | Hg | Hydrargyrum |
| Lead | Pb | Plumbum |
| Tin | Sn | Stannum |
| Tungsten | W | Wolfram |

🪄 **Mnemonic for Latin-symbol elements:** "**Na**vratri **K**e **Fe**ast mein **Cu**p **Ag** **Au** **Hg** **Pb** **Sn**ack **W**ala" — silly, but you will never forget that these ten symbols don't match their English names!

### The Periodic Table

- Developed by **Dmitri Mendeleev (1869)**, who arranged elements by atomic mass and predicted undiscovered elements.
- The **modern periodic table** (based on Moseley's work) arranges elements by **atomic number** (number of protons).
- **18 groups** (vertical columns) and **7 periods** (horizontal rows).
- **Group 1** — Alkali metals (very reactive: Na, K)
- **Group 17** — Halogens (F, Cl, Br, I)
- **Group 18** — Noble gases (He, Ne, Ar, Kr, Xe, Rn) — chemically inert, hence called "noble."

🧠 **Active Recall:** Why is the symbol of potassium 'K' and not 'P'? Who gave the modern periodic law based on atomic number?

## 3. Acids, Bases, and Salts — The Heart of Exam Chemistry

### Acids

- Sour in taste, turn **blue litmus red**.
- Release H⁺ ions in water.
- Examples: Hydrochloric acid (HCl) — present in our **stomach**; Sulphuric acid (H₂SO₄) — called the "**King of Chemicals**"; Nitric acid (HNO₃); Acetic acid (CH₃COOH) — in **vinegar**; Citric acid — in **lemons**; Lactic acid — in **curd/sour milk**.

### Bases

- Bitter in taste, soapy to touch, turn **red litmus blue**.
- Examples: Sodium hydroxide (NaOH) — **caustic soda**; Potassium hydroxide (KOH) — caustic potash; Calcium hydroxide (Ca(OH)₂) — **slaked lime**; Ammonium hydroxide — in window cleaners.
- Bases soluble in water are called **alkalis**.

### The pH Scale

- Ranges from **0 to 14**.
- pH **7 = neutral** (pure water).
- pH **< 7 = acidic**; pH **> 7 = basic**.
- Human blood pH ≈ **7.4** (slightly alkaline).
- pH was introduced by **Sørensen**.

### Salts and Neutralisation

**Acid + Base → Salt + Water** (Neutralisation reaction)

Famous salts (memorise this table — it appears in every exam!):

| Common Name | Chemical Name | Formula |
|---|---|---|
| Common salt | Sodium chloride | NaCl |
| Baking soda | Sodium bicarbonate | NaHCO₃ |
| Washing soda | Sodium carbonate | Na₂CO₃ |
| Caustic soda | Sodium hydroxide | NaOH |
| Slaked lime | Calcium hydroxide | Ca(OH)₂ |
| Quicklime | Calcium oxide | CaO |
| Plaster of Paris | Calcium sulphate hemihydrate | CaSO₄·½H₂O |
| Gypsum | Calcium sulphate dihydrate | CaSO₄·2H₂O |
| Blue vitriol | Copper sulphate | CuSO₄·5H₂O |
| Green vitriol | Ferrous sulphate | FeSO₄·7H₂O |
| White vitriol | Zinc sulphate | ZnSO₄·7H₂O |
| Alum (fitkari) | Potash alum | K₂SO₄·Al₂(SO₄)₃·24H₂O |
| Laughing gas | Nitrous oxide | N₂O |
| Dry ice | Solid carbon dioxide | CO₂ (solid) |
| Marble/Limestone | Calcium carbonate | CaCO₃ |

🪄 **Mnemonic for vitriols:** "**B**lue **C**opper, **G**reen **I**ron, **W**hite **Z**inc" — Blue vitriol = Copper, Green vitriol = Iron (ferrous), White vitriol = Zinc.

🧠 **Active Recall:** What is the chemical formula of washing soda? Which acid is present in the stomach? What is the pH of pure water?

## 4. Metals, Non-Metals, and Alloys

### Metals

- Lustrous, malleable (can be beaten into sheets), ductile (drawn into wires), good conductors of heat and electricity.
- **Most ductile metal:** Gold. **Best conductor of electricity:** Silver (then copper).
- **Mercury** is the only metal liquid at room temperature (Gallium also melts near room temperature).
- **Most abundant metal in Earth's crust:** Aluminium. **Most abundant element overall:** Oxygen.

### Non-Metals

- Generally brittle, non-lustrous, poor conductors.
- **Exception (exam favourite!):** Graphite (a form of carbon) is a **good conductor of electricity** — that's why it's used in electrodes.
- **Diamond** (also carbon!) is the hardest natural substance and a *non-conductor*.
- **Bromine** is the only non-metal liquid at room temperature.

### Important Alloys

| Alloy | Composition | Use |
|---|---|---|
| Brass | Copper + Zinc | Utensils, fittings |
| Bronze | Copper + Tin | Statues, medals |
| Stainless steel | Iron + Chromium + Nickel | Cutlery, surgical tools |
| Solder | Lead + Tin | Joining electrical wires |
| Duralumin | Aluminium + Copper + Magnesium + Manganese | Aircraft bodies |
| German silver | Copper + Zinc + Nickel | Decorative items (contains NO silver!) |

⚠️ **Trap alert:** German silver contains **no silver at all** — examiners love this!

🧠 **Active Recall:** Which non-metal conducts electricity? What is the composition of bronze? Why is duralumin used in aircraft?

## 5. Important Gases and Fuels

- **Oxygen (O₂):** Supports combustion; discovered by **Joseph Priestley** (named by Lavoisier).
- **Hydrogen (H₂):** Lightest element; discovered by **Henry Cavendish**; called "inflammable air."
- **Nitrogen (N₂):** ~78% of air; discovered by **Daniel Rutherford**; used in fertilizers (urea).
- **Carbon dioxide (CO₂):** Used in fire extinguishers; solid CO₂ = dry ice.
- **Laughing gas (N₂O):** Used as an anaesthetic; discovered by **Priestley**, anaesthetic use by **Humphry Davy**.
- **LPG:** Mainly **propane and butane**; the smell comes from added **ethyl mercaptan** (a safety measure — LPG itself is odourless!).
- **CNG:** Mainly **methane (CH₄)** — the cleanest fossil fuel.
- **Biogas:** Mainly methane, produced from cow dung/organic waste.
- **Tear gas:** Chloropicrin / CS gas.
- **Mustard gas:** A chemical warfare agent (sulphur-based).

🧠 **Active Recall:** Why does LPG smell if it is odourless? Which gas is called laughing gas? What is the main component of CNG?

## 6. Carbon and Its Allotropes

- **Allotropes** = different physical forms of the same element.
- Carbon's allotropes: **Diamond** (hardest, non-conductor), **Graphite** (soft, conductor, used in pencil leads and lubricants), **Fullerene** (Buckminsterfullerene, C₆₀).
- Pencil "lead" contains **no lead** — it is graphite mixed with clay. (Another classic trap!)

---

## 7. Basic Organic Chemistry

- Organic chemistry = chemistry of **carbon compounds** (term linked to "vital force theory," broken by **Wöhler** in 1828 when he synthesised **urea** from an inorganic compound).
- **Methane (CH₄):** Simplest hydrocarbon; marsh gas.
- **Ethanol (C₂H₅OH):** Alcohol in beverages; used in hand sanitisers.
- **Methanol (CH₃OH):** Poisonous — causes blindness/death (spurious liquor tragedies).
- **Formaldehyde (HCHO):** Used to preserve biological specimens (formalin).
- **Chloroform (CHCl₃):** Early anaesthetic.
- **Iodoform (CHI₃):** Antiseptic.

## ⚠️ Caution: Examiner's Trap

**Trap 1 — "लगभग सही कथन" (Almost-Correct Statement):**
"German silver is an alloy of copper, zinc, and silver." — Sounds 90% right, but German silver contains **nickel, not silver**. Similarly: "Pencil lead contains lead" — wrong, it's **graphite**. The examiner changes just one word in a true statement.

**Trap 2 — "समान नाम/वर्ष" (Similar Names Confusion):**
Washing soda (Na₂CO₃) vs Baking soda (NaHCO₃) vs Caustic soda (NaOH) — three "sodas," three different compounds. Similarly, Quicklime (CaO) vs Slaked lime (Ca(OH)₂) vs Limestone (CaCO₃). Examiners deliberately place these side by side in options.

**Trap 3 — "नकारात्मक वाक्यांश" (Hidden Negative):**
"Which of the following is **NOT** an allotrope of carbon?" or "All are alloys **EXCEPT**." Students who read fast mark the first correct-looking option (diamond, brass) and lose the mark. Always circle the NOT/EXCEPT before reading options.
