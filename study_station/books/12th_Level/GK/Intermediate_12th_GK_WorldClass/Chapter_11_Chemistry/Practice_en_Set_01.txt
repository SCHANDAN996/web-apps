1. Which of the following is the chemical symbol for sodium?
(a) Na (b) Sd (c) So (d) N
Answer: (a)
Solution: Many students pick "So" assuming the symbol comes from the English name, but sodium's symbol comes from its Latin name "natrium". The correct symbol is Na, so the answer is Na.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Science

2. The atomic number of an element is equal to the number of:
(a) neutrons in the nucleus (b) nucleons (c) electrons plus neutrons (d) protons in the nucleus
Answer: (d)
Solution: Neutrons determine the isotope, not the element's identity; the atomic number (Z) is defined by the number of protons in the nucleus, which equals the electrons in a neutral atom. So the answer is protons in the nucleus.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Science

3. Which gas is commonly known as laughing gas?
(a) Nitrous oxide (b) Nitrogen dioxide (c) Nitric oxide (d) Ammonia
Answer: (a)
Solution: Nitric oxide (NO) and nitrogen dioxide (NO₂) sound similar and are common traps, but laughing gas is nitrous oxide (N₂O), used as an anaesthetic. So the answer is nitrous oxide.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science

4. The pH value of a neutral solution at 25°C is:
(a) 0 (b) 14 (c) 7 (d) 1
Answer: (c)
Solution: A pH of 0 is strongly acidic and 14 is strongly alkaline; neutrality at 25°C corresponds to pH 7. So the answer is 7.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science

5. Which of the following is a noble gas?
(a) Nitrogen (b) Oxygen (c) Chlorine (d) Argon
Answer: (d)
Solution: Chlorine is a reactive halogen and nitrogen and oxygen are common atmospheric gases, but argon has a complete outer shell and is chemically inert. So the answer is argon.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science

6. The chemical formula of common salt is:
(a) KCl (b) Na₂CO₃ (c) CaCl₂ (d) NaCl
Answer: (d)
Solution: Na₂CO₃ is washing soda, a frequent confusion; common table salt is sodium chloride, NaCl. So the answer is NaCl.

7. Which acid is present in vinegar?
(a) Citric acid (b) Lactic acid (c) Acetic acid (d) Oxalic acid
Answer: (c)
Solution: Citric acid is found in lemons and lactic acid in curd, which tempts students; vinegar contains 4–8% acetic acid (ethanoic acid). So the answer is acetic acid.

8. The most abundant gas in the Earth's atmosphere is:
(a) Oxygen (b) Nitrogen (c) Carbon dioxide (d) Hydrogen
Answer: (b)
Solution: Oxygen feels like the obvious answer because we breathe it, but it is only about 21%; nitrogen makes up about 78% of the atmosphere. So the answer is nitrogen.

9. Which of the following metals is liquid at room temperature?
(a) Sodium (b) Mercury (c) Aluminium (d) Gallium
Answer: (b)
Solution: Gallium melts near 30°C, which is a tempting distractor, but the standard exam answer for a metal liquid at room temperature is mercury. So the answer is mercury.

10. Rusting of iron is an example of:
(a) oxidation (b) reduction (c) neutralisation (d) displacement
Answer: (a)
Solution: Rust forms when iron reacts with oxygen and moisture to form hydrated iron oxide, which is an oxidation (corrosion) process, not reduction. So the answer is oxidation.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science

11. The chemical name of baking soda is:
(a) Sodium bicarbonate (b) Sodium carbonate (c) Calcium carbonate (d) Potassium bicarbonate
Answer: (a)
Solution: Sodium carbonate (washing soda) is the classic trap here; baking soda is sodium bicarbonate, NaHCO₃. So the answer is sodium bicarbonate.

12. Which of the following is used as a moderator in a nuclear reactor?
(a) Mercury (b) Petrol (c) Alcohol (d) Heavy water
Answer: (d)
Solution: A moderator slows down fast neutrons; heavy water (D₂O) and graphite serve this purpose, while the other options are irrelevant. So the answer is heavy water.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Physics/Chemistry

13. The isotopes of an element have the same number of:
(a) neutrons (b) protons (c) nucleons (d) mass number
Answer: (b)
Solution: Isotopes differ in neutrons and hence mass number, which is the trap; they share the same number of protons (same atomic number). So the answer is protons.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Science

14. Which gas is released when dilute hydrochloric acid reacts with zinc?
(a) Oxygen (b) Hydrogen (c) Chlorine (d) Carbon dioxide
Answer: (b)
Solution: Chlorine tempts students because HCl contains chlorine, but an active metal plus an acid liberates hydrogen gas. So the answer is hydrogen.

15. The chemical formula of washing soda is:
(a) Na₂CO₃·10H₂O (b) NaHCO₃ (c) NaOH (d) CaOCl₂
Answer: (a)
Solution: NaHCO₃ is baking soda, the usual confusion; washing soda is hydrated sodium carbonate, Na₂CO₃·10H₂O. So the answer is Na₂CO₃·10H₂O.

16. Which of the following is the hardest naturally occurring substance?
(a) Quartz (b) Diamond (c) Graphite (d) Iron

Solution: Graphite is also a form of carbon but is soft and slippery, a common trap; diamond is the hardest natural substance. So the answer is diamond.

17. Brass is an alloy of:
(a) copper and tin (b) iron and chromium (c) copper and zinc (d) aluminium and copper
Answer: (c)
Solution: Copper and tin make bronze, the classic confusion pair; brass is an alloy of copper and zinc. So the answer is copper and zinc.

18. Which of the following is a greenhouse gas?
(a) Oxygen (b) Nitrogen (c) Carbon dioxide (d) Argon
Answer: (c)
Solution: Oxygen and nitrogen make up most of the atmosphere but do not trap heat; carbon dioxide absorbs infrared radiation and is a major greenhouse gas. So the answer is carbon dioxide.

19. The process of coating iron with zinc to prevent rusting is called:
(a) annealing (b) crystallisation (c) galvanisation (d) vulcanisation
Answer: (c)
Solution: Vulcanisation relates to rubber, a tempting similar-sounding term; coating iron with a layer of zinc is galvanisation. So the answer is galvanisation.

20. Which of the following elements has the atomic number 1?
(a) Hydrogen (b) Helium (c) Lithium (d) Carbon

Solution: Helium has atomic number 2, which confuses students who think alphabetically; hydrogen, the lightest element, has atomic number 1. So the answer is hydrogen.

21. Consider the following statements about graphite and diamond:
1. Both are allotropes of carbon.
2. Graphite is a good conductor of electricity.
3. Diamond conducts electricity due to free electrons.
Which of the above statements is/are correct?
(a) 1 and 2 only (b) 2 and 3 only (c) 1 and 3 only (d) 1, 2 and 3
Answer: (a)
Solution: Statement 3 is the examiner's trap — it sounds plausible because conductivity is linked with free electrons, but in diamond all four valence electrons of each carbon are bonded, leaving no free electrons, so diamond does not conduct electricity. Statements 1 and 2 are correct, so the answer is 1 and 2 only.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science

22. Match the following acids with their sources:
1. Citric acid — P. Lemon
2. Lactic acid — Q. Curd
3. Tartaric acid — R. Tamarind
4. Formic acid — S. Ant sting
(a) 1-P, 2-R, 3-Q, 4-S (b) 1-Q, 2-P, 3-S, 4-R (c) 1-P, 2-Q, 3-R, 4-S (d) 1-S, 2-Q, 3-R, 4-P
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is swapping lactic and tartaric acid sources; lactic acid is in curd, tartaric acid in tamarind, citric acid in lemon, and formic acid (methanoic acid) in an ant's sting. So the answer is 1-P, 2-Q, 3-R, 4-S.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science

23. Which of the following statements about bleaching powder is correct?
(a) Its formula is CaCO₃ (b) It is prepared by the action of chlorine on dry slaked lime (c) It is prepared by heating limestone (d) It is used as a fertiliser
Answer: (b)
Solution: Heating limestone produces quicklime, not bleaching powder — a frequent process-confusion trap; bleaching powder, CaOCl₂, is made by passing chlorine over dry slaked lime, Ca(OH)₂. So the answer is that it is prepared by the action of chlorine on dry slaked lime.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science

24. Arrange the following metals in decreasing order of reactivity:
1. Potassium 2. Gold 3. Zinc 4. Iron
(a) 2 > 4 > 3 > 1 (b) 1 > 4 > 3 > 2 (c) 3 > 1 > 4 > 2 (d) 1 > 3 > 4 > 2
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is placing iron above zinc; in the reactivity series potassium is most reactive, followed by zinc, then iron, with gold the least reactive. So the answer is 1 > 3 > 4 > 2.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science

25. Consider the following statements:
1. Plaster of Paris is calcium sulphate hemihydrate.
2. On mixing with water, Plaster of Paris sets into a hard mass of gypsum.
3. Gypsum has two molecules of water of crystallisation.
Which of the above statements are correct?
(a) 1, 2 and 3 (b) 2 and 3 only (c) 1 and 3 only (d) 1 and 2 only
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap lies in the water molecules: Plaster of Paris is CaSO₄·½H₂O (hemihydrate), and on adding water it converts to gypsum, CaSO₄·2H₂O, a hard mass — all three statements are correct. So the answer is 1, 2 and 3.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science
