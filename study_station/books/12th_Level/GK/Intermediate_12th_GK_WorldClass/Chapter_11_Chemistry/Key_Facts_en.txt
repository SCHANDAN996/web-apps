# 📋 Key Facts & Figures — General Chemistry

## Part 1: High-Yield Facts

| Fact | Description | Examiner's Trap | Trap Type |
|---|---|---|---|
| Atomic number | Number of protons in the nucleus; defines the element | Confusing atomic number with mass number (protons + neutrons) | Similar-sounding terms |
| Isotopes | Same atomic number, different mass number (e.g., ¹H, ²H, ³H) | Saying isotopes differ in protons — they differ in neutrons | Near-correct statement |
| Isobars | Same mass number, different atomic number (e.g., ⁴⁰Ar, ⁴⁰Ca) | Swapping definitions of isotopes and isobars | Terminology swap |
| Isotones | Same number of neutrons (e.g., ³⁹K, ⁴⁰Ca) | Rarely asked directly; confused with isobars | Terminology swap |
| Modern Periodic Table | Based on atomic number (Moseley, 1913); 18 groups, 7 periods | Mendeleev's table was based on atomic mass — questions swap the two | Chronology confusion |
| Group 18 | Noble gases — He, Ne, Ar, Kr, Xe, Rn; chemically inert | Radon is radioactive; "all noble gases are stable" is misleading | Near-correct statement |
| Group 17 | Halogens — F, Cl, Br, I, At; most reactive non-metals | Fluorine is the most reactive, not chlorine | Similar-name trap |
| Group 1 | Alkali metals — Li, Na, K, Rb, Cs, Fr; soft, stored in kerosene | Hydrogen is in Group 1 but is NOT an alkali metal | Exception trap |
| Group 2 | Alkaline earth metals — Be, Mg, Ca, Sr, Ba, Ra | Confusing "alkali" with "alkaline earth" | Similar-sounding terms |
| Most electronegative element | Fluorine | Oxygen is often the tempting wrong answer | Popular misconception |
| Most electropositive element | Caesium (Francium theoretically, but rare/radioactive) | Options may offer both Cs and Fr — Cs is the standard answer | Near-correct statement |
| pH scale | 0–14; pH 7 neutral, <7 acidic, >7 basic | pH of pure water falls below 7 on heating — still neutral | Conceptual trap |
| pH of human blood | About 7.4 (slightly basic) | Options often give 7.0 as the trap | Near-correct value |
| Strong acid vs concentrated acid | Strength = degree of ionisation; concentration = amount per volume | Treating "strong" and "concentrated" as the same | Conceptual confusion |
| Common acid names | HCl — muriatic; H₂SO₄ — oil of vitriol; HNO₃ — aqua fortis | Swapping the common names | Terminology swap |
| Aqua regia | HNO₃ + HCl in ratio 1:3; dissolves gold and platinum | Ratio reversed (3:1) in wrong options | Numerical reversal |
| Acid in common items | Vinegar — acetic acid; lemon — citric acid; curd — lactic acid; ant sting — formic acid | Ant sting is formic (methanoic), not acetic | Similar-name trap |
| Baking soda | Sodium bicarbonate (NaHCO₃) | Confused with washing soda (Na₂CO₃·10H₂O) | Similar-compound trap |
| Washing soda | Sodium carbonate decahydrate (Na₂CO₃·10H₂O) | Confused with baking soda or caustic soda (NaOH) | Similar-compound trap |
| Plaster of Paris | Calcium sulphate hemihydrate (CaSO₄·½H₂O) | Confused with gypsum (CaSO₄·2H₂O) | Formula trap |
| Bleaching powder | Calcium oxychloride (CaOCl₂) | Formula often written wrongly as CaCl₂ in options | Formula trap |
| Laughing gas | Nitrous oxide (N₂O) | Confused with NO or NO₂ | Formula trap |
| Dry ice | Solid carbon dioxide (CO₂) | "Dry ice is frozen water" — wrong | Popular misconception |
| Rusting of iron | Hydrated ferric oxide (Fe₂O₃·xH₂O); needs both O₂ and moisture | Saying only oxygen is needed | Incomplete statement |
| Galvanisation | Coating iron with zinc to prevent rusting | Confused with tinning (coating with tin) | Process swap |
| Oxidation | Loss of electrons / gain of oxygen | Old vs electronic definitions mixed in options | Definition trap |
| Reduction | Gain of electrons / loss of oxygen | Reversed with oxidation in negative-worded questions | Negative statement |
| Oxidising agent | Itself gets reduced | "Oxidising agent is oxidised" — classic wrong statement | Near-correct statement |
| Catalyst | Speeds up reaction; unchanged in mass and composition at the end | "Catalyst is consumed in the reaction" — wrong | Near-correct statement |
| Common catalysts | Fe — Haber process; V₂O₅ — Contact process; Ni — hydrogenation of oils | Swapping catalysts between processes | Process swap |
| Allotropes of carbon | Diamond, graphite, fullerene, graphene | Diamond conducts electricity — wrong; graphite does | Near-correct statement |
| Diamond vs graphite | Diamond: hardest, non-conductor; Graphite: soft, conductor, lubricant | Properties swapped between the two | Property swap |
| Hardest natural substance | Diamond | Quartz is the tempting wrong option | Popular misconception |
| Lightest metal | Lithium | Hydrogen is lightest element but not a metal | Category trap |
| Liquid metals at room temperature | Mercury (Hg); Gallium melts near 30°C | "Mercury is the only liquid metal" ignores gallium's low melting point | Near-correct statement |
| Liquid non-metal | Bromine | Only liquid non-metal at room temperature | Unique-fact trap |
| Amalgam | Alloy of mercury with other metals | Asked as "which metal is always in an amalgam" | Definition trap |
| Brass | Copper + Zinc | Confused with bronze (Copper + Tin) | Alloy swap |
| Bronze | Copper + Tin | Confused with brass | Alloy swap |
| Stainless steel | Iron + Chromium + Nickel | Chromium gives corrosion resistance | Composition trap |
| German silver | Copper + Zinc + Nickel — contains NO silver | Name suggests silver; classic trap | Misleading name |
| Solder | Lead + Tin | Used for joining electrical wires | Composition trap |
| Metals in periodic table | Majority of elements are metals | "Non-metals are more numerous" — wrong | Near-correct statement |
| Metalloids | B, Si, Ge, As, Sb, Te — properties between metals and non-metals | Silicon is a metalloid, not a metal | Category trap |
| Atomic radius trend | Decreases left→right in a period; increases top→bottom in a group | Trends reversed in options | Trend reversal |
| Ionisation energy trend | Increases left→right; decreases top→bottom | Noble gases have highest ionisation energy in their period | Trend trap |
| Covalent bond | Sharing of electrons (e.g., H₂O, CH₄) | Confused with ionic bond (transfer of electrons, e.g., NaCl) | Bond-type swap |
| Ionic bond | Transfer of electrons; between metal and non-metal | Formed between two non-metals — wrong | Near-correct statement |
| Hard water | Contains Ca²⁺ and Mg²⁺ salts; does not lather with soap | Temporary hardness removed by boiling; permanent by washing soda | Process confusion |
| Heavy water | D₂O — used as moderator in nuclear reactors | Confused with hard water | Similar-sounding terms |
| Water gas | CO + H₂ mixture | Confused with producer gas (CO + N₂) | Gas-mixture swap |
| CNG main component | Methane (CH₄) | Confused with LPG (mainly butane/propane) | Fuel swap |
| Gobar gas / Biogas | Mainly methane | Asked with CNG/LPG as distractors | Fuel swap |
| Tear gas | Chloroacetophenone (common exam answer) | Options mix with mustard gas (a chemical weapon) | Similar-category trap |
| Global warming gas | CO₂ is the main greenhouse gas | Methane is more potent per molecule but less abundant | Near-correct statement |
| Ozone layer | O₃; absorbs UV radiation; depleted by CFCs | Ozone in stratosphere is good; at ground level it is a pollutant | Context trap |
| Acid rain | pH below ~5.6; caused by SO₂ and NOₓ | CO₂ alone is not the main cause of acid rain | Cause swap |
| Radioactivity discovered by | Henri Becquerel (1896) | Confused with the Curies (discovered polonium, radium) | Person swap |
| Electron discovered by | J.J. Thomson (1897) | Proton — Rutherford/Goldstein; Neutron — Chadwick (1932) | Person swap |
| Neutron discovered by | James Chadwick (1932) | Often swapped with Rutherford | Person swap |
| Rutherford's model | Nucleus discovered via gold foil (alpha scattering) experiment | Confused with Bohr's model (fixed energy levels) | Model swap |
| Avogadro's number | 6.022 × 10²³ particles per mole | Value misquoted as 6.022 × 10²² in options | Numerical trap |
| Molar volume at STP | 22.4 litres for 1 mole of any ideal gas | Asked with wrong conditions (room temperature) | Condition trap |
| Boyle's law | P ∝ 1/V at constant temperature | Confused with Charles's law (V ∝ T at constant pressure) | Law swap |
| Charles's law | V ∝ T at constant pressure | Temperature must be in kelvin | Condition trap |
| Absolute zero | 0 K = −273.15°C | Written as −273.15 K in trap options (kelvin has no negative) | Unit trap |
| Common salt | NaCl; iodised salt contains potassium iodate/iodide | "Iodine is added as pure I₂" — wrong | Near-correct statement |
| Chloroform formula | CHCl₃ | Confused with carbon tetrachloride (CCl₄) | Formula trap |
| Formalin | ~40% aqueous solution of formaldehyde (HCHO); preserves specimens | Confused with formaldehyde itself | Terminology trap |
| TNT | Trinitrotoluene — an explosive | Full form asked directly | Name trap |
| Vitamin C chemical name | Ascorbic acid | Asked as "which vitamin is an acid" | Cross-topic trap |

## Part 2: Time Management (15/45-Second Rule)

| Topic | Question Type | Time Rule | Score Maximizer? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Chemical names & formulas (baking soda, POP, bleaching powder) | Fact-based | 15 sec | ✅ Yes |
| Common names of acids and substances | Fact-based | 15 sec | ✅ Yes |
| Alloys and their composition | Fact-based | 15 sec | ✅ Yes |
| Discoveries (electron, proton, neutron, radioactivity) | Fact-based | 15 sec | ✅ Yes |
| Gases and their uses (laughing gas, dry ice, CNG, LPG) | Fact-based | 15 sec | ✅ Yes |
| Periodic table groups (alkali metals, halogens, noble gases) | Fact-based | 15 sec | ✅ Yes |
| pH values of common substances | Fact-based | 15 sec | ✅ Yes |
| Environmental chemistry (acid rain, ozone, greenhouse gases) | Fact-based | 15 sec | ✅ Yes |
| Periodic trends (atomic radius, ionisation energy, electronegativity) | Analytical | 45 sec | ⚠️ Moderate |
| Isotopes / isobars / isotones identification from given data | Analytical | 45 sec | ⚠️ Moderate |
| Oxidation–reduction identification in a reaction | Analytical | 45 sec | ⚠️ Moderate |
| Gas law numericals (Boyle's, Charles's) | Analytical | 45 sec | ❌ Skip if calculation-heavy |
| Mole concept numericals | Analytical | 45 sec | ❌ Skip if calculation-heavy |
| Balancing chemical equations | Analytical | 45 sec | ⚠️ Moderate |
| Statement-based questions (multiple statements on bonding/acids) | Analytical | 45 sec | ⚠️ Moderate |

**Strategy:** Chemistry in SSC/Railway/Bank exams is dominated by fact-based recall (formulas, alloys, common names, discoveries). Attempt all 15-second questions first; return to trend-based and numerical questions only in the second pass.
