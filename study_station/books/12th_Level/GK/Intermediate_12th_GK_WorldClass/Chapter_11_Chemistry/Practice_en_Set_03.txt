Questions 51–75: Medium

51. Which of the following is used as a moderator in a nuclear reactor?
(a) Cadmium (b) Heavy water (c) Graphite only in all reactors (d) Liquid sodium
Answer: (b)
Solution: Cadmium is a tempting wrong choice, but cadmium rods are control rods that absorb neutrons to regulate the reaction, not slow them down. A moderator slows fast neutrons to thermal speeds, and heavy water (D₂O) is an excellent moderator, so the answer is Heavy water.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Chemistry

52. The pH of a solution is 4. Its hydrogen ion concentration is:
(a) 4 mol/L (b) 10⁻¹⁰ mol/L (c) 10⁻⁴ mol/L (d) 10⁴ mol/L
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is choosing 10⁻¹⁰, which is the hydroxide ion concentration, not the hydrogen ion concentration. Since pH = −log[H⁺], [H⁺] = 10⁻ᵖᴴ = 10⁻⁴ mol/L, so the answer is 10⁻⁴ mol/L.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Chemistry

53. Which gas is produced when zinc reacts with dilute sulphuric acid?
(a) Hydrogen (b) Oxygen (c) Sulphur dioxide (d) Nitrogen
Answer: (a)
Solution: Sulphur dioxide is the tempting wrong option, but SO₂ is released only when metals react with hot concentrated sulphuric acid, not dilute acid. Zinc with dilute H₂SO₄ gives zinc sulphate and hydrogen gas, so the answer is Hydrogen.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science

54. The chemical formula of washing soda is:
(a) NaHCO₃ (b) Na₂CO₃·10H₂O (c) Na₂CO₃ (d) NaOH
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is Na₂CO₃, which is only anhydrous soda ash; washing soda is specifically the hydrated crystal. Washing soda is sodium carbonate decahydrate, so the answer is Na₂CO₃·10H₂O.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science

55. Which of the following acids is present in vinegar?
(a) Citric acid (b) Lactic acid (c) Acetic acid (d) Tartaric acid
Answer: (c)
Solution: Citric acid is a tempting distractor because it is found in sour citrus fruits, but vinegar's sourness comes from a different acid. Vinegar is a 4–8% solution of acetic acid (ethanoic acid) in water, so the answer is Acetic acid.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science

56. The oxidation number of sulphur in H₂SO₄ is:
(a) +4 (b) −2 (c) +2 (d) +6
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is +4, which is sulphur's oxidation state in H₂SO₃ (sulphurous acid), a commonly confused similar compound. In H₂SO₄: 2(+1) + x + 4(−2) = 0, giving x = +6, so the answer is +6.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Chemistry

57. Which of the following is an amphoteric oxide?
(a) Na₂O (b) Al₂O₃ (c) SO₂ (d) CaO
Answer: (b)
Solution: CaO is tempting because calcium is near aluminium in reactivity discussions, but CaO is purely basic. Aluminium oxide reacts with both acids and bases, making it amphoteric, so the answer is Al₂O₃.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science

58. Brass is an alloy of:
(a) Copper and tin (b) Zinc and tin (c) Copper and aluminium (d) Copper and zinc
Answer: (d)
Solution: Copper and tin is the classic trap — that combination makes bronze, not brass; the similar names confuse many candidates. Brass is an alloy of copper and zinc, so the answer is Copper and zinc.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science

59. The number of electrons in ¹⁸O²⁻ (oxide ion) is:
(a) 8 (b) 10 (c) 16 (d) 18
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is 18, which is the mass number, not the electron count — a deliberate confusion between mass number and electron number. Oxygen has 8 electrons, and the 2− charge adds 2 more, giving 10 electrons, so the answer is 10.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Chemistry

60. Which of the following is used in the manufacture of bleaching powder?
(a) Quicklime and chlorine (b) Slaked lime and chlorine (c) Limestone and chlorine (d) Slaked lime and hydrogen
Answer: (b)
Solution: Quicklime (CaO) is the tempting wrong option, but bleaching powder is made from slaked lime, not quicklime. Chlorine gas is passed over dry slaked lime Ca(OH)₂ to give bleaching powder CaOCl₂, so the answer is Slaked lime and chlorine.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science

61. The process of coating iron with zinc to prevent rusting is called:
(a) Electroplating (b) Anodising (c) Galvanisation (d) Alloying
Answer: (c)
Solution: Electroplating is a tempting distractor since zinc can also be electroplated, but the standard term for zinc-coating of iron is galvanisation. In galvanisation a thin layer of zinc protects iron from moisture and air, so the answer is Galvanisation.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science

62. Which of the following has the highest electronegativity?
(a) Oxygen (b) Nitrogen (c) Chlorine (d) Fluorine
Answer: (d)
Solution: Chlorine is a common trap because it has the highest electron affinity, but electron affinity and electronegativity are different properties. Fluorine is the most electronegative element (3.98 on the Pauling scale), so the answer is Fluorine.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Chemistry

63. The gas used in the Haber process along with nitrogen is:
(a) Hydrogen (b) Oxygen (c) Carbon dioxide (d) Chlorine
Answer: (a)
Solution: Oxygen is a tempting wrong choice since air is involved in ammonia-related industries, but the Haber process combines nitrogen with hydrogen. N₂ + 3H₂ ⇌ 2NH₃ over an iron catalyst, so the answer is Hydrogen.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Chemistry

64. Which of the following is a greenhouse gas?
(a) Nitrogen (b) Oxygen (c) Methane (d) Argon
Answer: (c)
Solution: Nitrogen is tempting because it dominates the atmosphere, but the major atmospheric gases N₂ and O₂ do not absorb infrared radiation. Methane traps heat in the atmosphere and is a potent greenhouse gas, so the answer is Methane.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Chemistry

65. The common name of sodium hydroxide is:
(a) Baking soda (b) Caustic soda (c) Washing soda (d) Caustic potash
Answer: (b)
Solution: Caustic potash is the trap — it sounds similar but is potassium hydroxide (KOH), not sodium hydroxide. Sodium hydroxide (NaOH) is commonly called caustic soda, so the answer is Caustic soda.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science

66. Which of the following bonds is present in a molecule of nitrogen (N₂)?
(a) Single bond (b) Double bond (c) Triple bond (d) Ionic bond
Answer: (c)
Solution: Double bond is a tempting wrong answer because oxygen (O₂) has a double bond, and the two diatomic gases are often confused. Each nitrogen atom shares three electron pairs, forming a triple bond, so the answer is Triple bond.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Chemistry

67. The metal that remains liquid at room temperature is:
(a) Mercury (b) Sodium (c) Gallium only above 50°C (d) Caesium
Answer: (a)
Solution: Gallium is a trap because it melts near 30°C, but at normal room temperature (~25°C) it is solid; caesium melts at about 28.5°C, also above typical room temperature. Mercury is the standard metal liquid at room temperature, so the answer is Mercury.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science

68. Which of the following is the hardest naturally occurring substance?
(a) Quartz (b) Corundum (c) Diamond (d) Graphite
Answer: (c)
Solution: Graphite is a trap because it is chemically the same element as diamond (carbon), yet it is one of the softest minerals. Diamond, with its tetrahedral covalent network, is the hardest natural substance, so the answer is Diamond.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science

69. The electrolyte used in a lead-acid storage battery is:
(a) Dilute sulphuric acid (b) Dilute hydrochloric acid (c) Concentrated nitric acid (d) Ammonium chloride
Answer: (a)
Solution: Ammonium chloride is the tempting wrong option, but it is the electrolyte in a dry cell, not a lead-acid battery — a classic similar-device confusion. Lead-acid batteries use dilute sulphuric acid (about 38% H₂SO₄), so the answer is Dilute sulphuric acid.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Chemistry

70. Which of the following elements is a metalloid?
(a) Sodium (b) Chlorine (c) Sulphur (d) Silicon
Answer: (d)
Solution: Sulphur is tempting because it sits near the metal–nonmetal dividing line, but it is a nonmetal. Silicon shows properties of both metals and nonmetals and is a classic metalloid, so the answer is Silicon.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Chemistry

71. The IUPAC name of CH₃–CH₂–OH is:
(a) Methanol (b) Ethanal (c) Propanol (d) Ethanol
Answer: (d)
Solution: Ethanal is the trap — it differs by only one letter but is an aldehyde (CH₃CHO), not an alcohol. The two-carbon alcohol CH₃CH₂OH is ethanol, so the answer is Ethanol.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science

72. Which of the following is used as an antacid?
(a) Magnesium hydroxide (b) Sodium chloride (c) Calcium carbonate only in bones (d) Acetic acid
Answer: (a)
Solution: Acetic acid is a trap because it is a household chemical, but adding an acid would worsen acidity, not relieve it. Magnesium hydroxide (milk of magnesia) is a mild base that neutralises excess stomach acid, so the answer is Magnesium hydroxide.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science

73. The atomic number of an element is 17. Its electronic configuration is:
(a) 2, 8, 7 (b) 2, 8, 8 (c) 2, 7, 8 (d) 2, 8, 6
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is 2, 8, 8, which is the configuration of the chloride ion (Cl⁻) or argon, not the neutral chlorine atom — a confusion between atom and ion. A neutral atom with atomic number 17 has 17 electrons arranged as 2, 8, 7, so the answer is 2, 8, 7.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science

74. Which of the following reactions is an example of a displacement reaction?
(a) CaCO₃ → CaO + CO₂ (b) NaOH + HCl → NaCl + H₂O (c) 2H₂ + O₂ → 2H₂O (d) Fe + CuSO₄ → FeSO₄ + Cu
Answer: (d)
Solution: NaOH + HCl is tempting because two compounds exchange ions, but that is a double displacement (neutralisation) reaction, not a simple displacement. In Fe + CuSO₄, the more reactive iron displaces copper from its salt solution, so the answer is Fe + CuSO₄ → FeSO₄ + Cu.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science

75. The gas released at the anode during the electrolysis of brine (concentrated NaCl solution) is:
(a) Chlorine (b) Oxygen (c) Hydrogen (d) Nitrogen
Answer: (a)
Solution: Hydrogen is the trap — it is indeed produced in this process, but at the cathode, not the anode; confusing the electrodes is a classic examiner trick. At the anode, chloride ions are oxidised to chlorine gas, so the answer is Chlorine.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science
