# 🪄 Memory Hooks & Short Tricks — General Chemistry

---

**Hook 1 — Reactivity Series of Metals**
> **"Please Stop Calling Me A Zebra, I Like Her Call Smart Goat"**
> **P**otassium > **S**odium > **C**alcium > **M**agnesium > **A**luminium > **Z**inc > **I**ron > **L**ead > **H**ydrogen > **C**opper > **S**ilver > **G**old
> Remember: metals *above* hydrogen displace it from acids; metals *below* (Cu, Ag, Au) don't.

---

**Hook 2 — First 20 Elements**
> **"Hi He Likes Beer But Could Not Offer Free Nine News. Na Mg Al SiPS ClAr K Ca"**
> H, He, Li, Be, B, C, N, O, F, Ne, Na, Mg, Al, Si, P, S, Cl, Ar, K, Ca — chant it like a rap twice and it sticks forever.

---

**Hook 3 — Common Acids & Where They Hide**
> **"Sour MILK, Burning BATTERY, Stinging ANT, Tangy VINEGAR"**
> **Lactic** acid → milk/curd | **Sulphuric** acid → car battery | **Formic** acid → ant sting | **Acetic** acid → vinegar | **Citric** acid → lemon | **Oxalic** acid → tomato/spinach.

---

**Hook 4 — pH Scale Direction**
> **"ABC: Acid Below, Base Crosses Seven"**
> pH < 7 = Acidic, pH = 7 = Neutral (pure water), pH > 7 = Basic. Trap alert: blood is slightly **basic** (pH ≈ 7.4), NOT acidic!

---

**Hook 5 — Common Salts & Their Names**
> **"WASH the TABLE, BAKE the CAKE, then PLASTER the wall"**
> Washing soda = Na₂CO₃·10H₂O | Table salt = NaCl | Baking soda = NaHCO₃ | Plaster of Paris = CaSO₄·½H₂O | Quicklime = CaO | Slaked lime = Ca(OH)₂.

---

**Hook 6 — Oxidation & Reduction (OIL RIG)**
> **"OIL RIG"** — **O**xidation **I**s **L**oss (of electrons), **R**eduction **I**s **G**ain (of electrons).
> The substance that gets oxidised is the **reducing agent** — it sacrifices itself. Opposite names, classic exam trap!

---

**Hook 7 — Alloys in One Line**
> **"Brass BZ, Bronze BS, Steel SI, Solder SL"**
> **B**rass = Cu + **Z**n | **B**ronze = Cu + **S**n | **S**teel = Fe + C (+ Cr in stainless) | **S**older = Pb + Sn. Trap: Brass has **Zinc**, Bronze has **Tin** — examiners swap them!

---

**Hook 8 — Hardest, Lightest, Heaviest**
> **"Diamond CUTS, Lithium FLOATS, Osmium SINKS"**
> Hardest natural substance = Diamond | Lightest metal = Lithium | Densest metal = Osmium | Lightest element = Hydrogen | Only liquid metal at room temp = Mercury | Only liquid non-metal = Bromine.

---

**Hook 9 — Gases & Their Nicknames**
> **"Laughing NITROUS, Marsh METHANE, Tear CHLORO-PIC"**
> Laughing gas = N₂O | Marsh gas = CH₄ | Tear gas = chloropicrin | Mustard gas = sulphur-based war gas | Dry ice = solid CO₂ (not ice at all!).

---

**Hook 10 — Periodic Table Inventors**
> **"MENDELEEV made the TABLE, MOSELEY gave it a NUMBER"**
> Mendeleev → periodic table based on atomic **mass** (1869) | Moseley → modern law based on atomic **number**. Trap: Dobereiner made **triads**, Newlands made the **Law of Octaves** — don't mix the four names!

---

**Hook 11 — Group Names in the Periodic Table**
> **"1 is ALKALI, 2 is ALKALINE EARTH, 17 is HALO, 18 is NOBLE"**
> Group 1 = Alkali metals | Group 2 = Alkaline earth metals | Group 17 = Halogens | Group 18 = Noble gases (inert). Noble gases have complete outer shells — that's why they don't react.

---

**Hook 12 — Rusting & Corrosion Colours**
> **"Iron RUSTS RED-BROWN, Copper GOES GREEN, Silver TURNS BLACK"**
> Rust = hydrated iron oxide (Fe₂O₃·xH₂O) | Green coating on copper = basic copper carbonate | Black on silver = silver sulphide (Ag₂S). Rusting needs **both** air (oxygen) AND water — a favourite statement trap.

---

**Hook 13 — Fertiliser Trio (NPK)**
> **"NPK = Nose, Palms, Knees — plants need all three"**
> N = Nitrogen (leaf growth), P = Phosphorus (roots), K = Potassium (overall health). Urea = CO(NH₂)₂, the most common nitrogenous fertiliser.

---

**Hook 14 — Atomic Structure Particles**
> **"Proton is Positive, Neutron is Neutral, Electron is Everywhere Else (negative)"**
> Proton & Neutron sit in the **nucleus**; electrons revolve outside. Atomic number = protons; Mass number = protons + neutrons. Isotopes = same protons, different neutrons (e.g., hydrogen's protium, deuterium, tritium).

---

**Hook 15 — Carbon Allotropes**
> **"Diamond is HARD & DULL conductor, Graphite is SOFT & SHINY conductor"**
> Diamond = hardest, non-conductor (each C bonded to 4 others) | Graphite = soft, slippery, conducts electricity (used in pencil leads & electrodes) | Fullerene = C₆₀ "buckyball". Trap: pencil "lead" contains **no lead** — it's graphite!

---

## ⏭️ Skip Strategy — When to Leave the Question

**Tip 1 — The "One Unknown Formula" Rule:**
If a question asks you to match four chemical formulas (e.g., washing soda, POP, quicklime, gypsum) and you are sure of only two, do NOT gamble on the remaining two by elimination under time pressure — wrong formula pairs are deliberately designed to look right. If you can't recall at least three confidently, **skip and return later**.

**Tip 2 — The "Statement-Stack" Trap:**
Questions like "Which of the following statements are correct? (1)…(2)…(3)…(4)…" with combination options (a) 1 & 2 (b) 2 & 3 (c) 1, 2 & 4 (d) All — these eat 2+ minutes. If even one statement involves a fact you've never seen (an obscure alloy composition or an unfamiliar gas), **skip immediately**; guessing across four combinations has poor odds.

**Tip 3 — The "Number-Heavy" Question:**
If a question demands exact atomic numbers, exact pH values, or exact percentage composition of an alloy and you only remember it "approximately," skip it. In negative-marking exams, an approximate memory is a liability — bank your time on fact-recall questions you know cold, and revisit only if time remains.
