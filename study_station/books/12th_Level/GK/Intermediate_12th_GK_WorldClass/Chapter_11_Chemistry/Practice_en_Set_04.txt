Questions 76–100: Medium

76. Which of the following is the correct order of increasing atomic radius?
(a) Na < Mg < Al < Si (b) Si < Al < Mg < Na (c) Al < Si < Na < Mg (d) Mg < Na < Si < Al
Answer: (b)
Solution: The tempting option assumes radius increases across a period, but atomic radius actually decreases left to right due to increasing nuclear charge. Silicon is smallest and sodium largest among these period-3 elements, so the answer is Si < Al < Mg < Na.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Chemistry

77. The pH of a 0.001 M HCl solution is:
(a) 1 (b) 2 (c) 3 (d) 11
Answer: (c)
Solution: Many students confuse 0.001 M with pH 1, forgetting that pH equals the negative logarithm of hydrogen ion concentration. Here [H+] = 10⁻³, so pH = 3, and the answer is 3.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Chemistry

78. Which gas is produced when zinc reacts with dilute sulphuric acid?
(a) Hydrogen (b) Oxygen (c) Sulphur dioxide (d) Chlorine
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is sulphur dioxide, which comes from concentrated acid reactions with some metals, not dilute acid with zinc. Zinc displaces hydrogen from dilute H₂SO₄, so the answer is hydrogen.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science

79. The oxidation number of sulphur in H₂SO₄ is:
(a) +4 (b) +6 (c) +2 (d) −2
Answer: (b)
Solution: Students often pick +4 by confusing sulphuric acid with sulphurous acid (H₂SO₃), where sulphur is indeed +4. In H₂SO₄, solving 2(+1) + x + 4(−2) = 0 gives x = +6, so the answer is +6.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Chemistry

80. Which of the following is an amphoteric oxide?
(a) Na₂O (b) SO₂ (c) Al₂O₃ (d) CaO
Answer: (c)
Solution: Calcium oxide and sodium oxide are basic, and sulphur dioxide is acidic; the trap is assuming all metal oxides are only basic. Aluminium oxide reacts with both acids and bases, so the answer is Al₂O₃.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Chemistry

81. The number of electrons in ¹⁸O²⁻ ion is:
(a) 8 (b) 10 (c) 16 (d) 18
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is 18, which is the mass number, not the electron count. Oxygen has 8 electrons, and the 2− charge adds 2 more, giving 10, so the answer is 10.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Science

82. Which of the following acids is present in vinegar?
(a) Citric acid (b) Acetic acid (c) Lactic acid (d) Oxalic acid
Answer: (b)
Solution: Citric acid (in lemons) is the common confusion here. Vinegar is a 4–8% solution of acetic acid (ethanoic acid) in water, so the answer is acetic acid.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science

83. The electronic configuration of chloride ion (Cl⁻) is:
(a) 2, 8, 7 (b) 2, 8, 8 (c) 2, 8, 6 (d) 2, 7, 8
Answer: (b)
Solution: The tempting answer 2, 8, 7 is the configuration of the neutral chlorine atom, not its ion. Gaining one electron fills the outer shell to 2, 8, 8, so the answer is 2, 8, 8.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Science

84. Which of the following is used as a moderator in a nuclear reactor?
(a) Plutonium (b) Cadmium rods (c) Uranium-235 (d) Heavy water
Answer: (d)
Solution: Cadmium rods are the trap — they are control rods that absorb neutrons, not slow them down. Heavy water (D₂O) slows fast neutrons, so the answer is heavy water.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science

85. In the reaction 2Mg + O₂ → 2MgO, magnesium is:
(a) Oxidised (b) Reduced (c) Neither oxidised nor reduced (d) Both oxidised and reduced
Answer: (a)
Solution: Some students think gaining anything means reduction, but here magnesium gains oxygen, which is oxidation. Magnesium loses electrons (0 to +2), so the answer is oxidised.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science

86. Which of the following has the highest electronegativity?
(a) Oxygen (b) Fluorine (c) Chlorine (d) Nitrogen
Answer: (b)
Solution: Oxygen is the tempting wrong choice because it is highly electronegative, but fluorine tops the Pauling scale at 4.0. Fluorine is the most electronegative element, so the answer is fluorine.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Chemistry

87. The common name of sodium carbonate decahydrate is:
(a) Baking soda (b) Soda lime (c) Caustic soda (d) Washing soda
Answer: (d)
Solution: Baking soda (sodium hydrogen carbonate) is the classic confusion with washing soda. Na₂CO₃·10H₂O is washing soda, so the answer is washing soda.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science

88. Which quantum number describes the shape of an orbital?
(a) Principal quantum number (b) Magnetic quantum number (c) Azimuthal quantum number (d) Spin quantum number
Answer: (c)
Solution: The principal quantum number is tempting because it is the best-known, but it only gives the size and energy of the shell. The azimuthal (angular momentum) quantum number determines orbital shape, so the answer is the azimuthal quantum number.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Chemistry

89. Which of the following is a greenhouse gas?
(a) Nitrogen (b) Oxygen (c) Methane (d) Argon
Answer: (c)
Solution: Nitrogen is tempting because it dominates the atmosphere, but it does not absorb infrared radiation. Methane traps heat and is a potent greenhouse gas, so the answer is methane.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Chemistry

90. The bond present in a molecule of nitrogen gas (N₂) is:
(a) Triple covalent bond (b) Double covalent bond (c) Single covalent bond (d) Ionic bond
Answer: (a)
Solution: Students often guess a double bond by analogy with O₂, which is the trap. Each nitrogen shares three electron pairs, forming a triple bond, so the answer is triple covalent bond.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science

91. Which of the following metals does not react with dilute hydrochloric acid?
(a) Zinc (b) Iron (c) Magnesium (d) Copper
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is assuming all metals liberate hydrogen from acids. Copper lies below hydrogen in the reactivity series and cannot displace it, so the answer is copper.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science

92. The isotope of hydrogen which contains one proton and one neutron is:
(a) Protium (b) Tritium (c) Deuterium (d) Helium
Answer: (c)
Solution: Tritium is tempting but it has two neutrons, and protium has none. Deuterium has one proton and one neutron, so the answer is deuterium.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Science

93. Which of the following solutions turns red litmus blue?
(a) Lemon juice (b) Hydrochloric acid (c) Vinegar (d) Sodium hydroxide solution
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is picking an acid because acids are more commonly tested. Only a base turns red litmus blue, and sodium hydroxide is a strong base, so the answer is sodium hydroxide solution.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science

94. The number of covalent bonds in a molecule of ethane (C₂H₆) is:
(a) 5 (b) 6 (c) 7 (d) 8
Answer: (c)
Solution: Students often count only the six C–H bonds and forget the C–C bond, which is the trap. Ethane has 6 C–H bonds plus 1 C–C bond, totalling 7, so the answer is 7.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science

95. Which of the following is the chemical formula of plaster of Paris?
(a) CaSO₄·½H₂O (b) CaSO₄·2H₂O (c) CaSO₄ (d) CaO
Answer: (a)
Solution: Gypsum (CaSO₄·2H₂O) is the tempting confusion since plaster of Paris is made from it. Heating gypsum removes water to give calcium sulphate hemihydrate, so the answer is CaSO₄·½H₂O.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science

96. In the modern periodic table, elements are arranged in increasing order of:
(a) Atomic number (b) Atomic mass (c) Number of neutrons (d) Density
Answer: (a)
Solution: Atomic mass is the trap because Mendeleev used it, but his table had anomalies like argon–potassium. Moseley's modern law uses atomic number, so the answer is atomic number.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science

97. Which of the following is an example of a disproportionation reaction?
(a) N₂ + 3H₂ → 2NH₃ (b) CaCO₃ → CaO + CO₂ (c) 2KClO₃ → 2KCl + 3O₂ (d) 2H₂O₂ → 2H₂O + O₂
Answer: (d)
Solution: Decomposition reactions like the heating of calcium carbonate are tempting, but in them no element is both oxidised and reduced. In hydrogen peroxide, oxygen (−1) goes to both −2 and 0, so the answer is 2H₂O₂ → 2H₂O + O₂.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Chemistry

98. The gas evolved at the cathode during the electrolysis of water is:
(a) Oxygen (b) Carbon dioxide (c) Chlorine (d) Hydrogen
Answer: (d)
Solution: Oxygen is the trap because students mix up the electrodes; oxygen forms at the anode. Hydrogen is liberated at the cathode, so the answer is hydrogen.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science

99. Which of the following statements about noble gases is correct?
(a) They have completely filled outermost shells (b) They readily form ionic compounds with all metals (c) They are all diatomic molecules (d) They have high chemical reactivity
Answer: (a)
Solution: The diatomic claim is tempting because gases like oxygen are diatomic, but noble gases are monatomic. Their stability comes from complete outer shells, so the answer is that they have completely filled outermost shells.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Chemistry

100. The molecular formula of benzene is:
(a) C₆H₆ (b) C₆H₁₂ (c) C₆H₁₀ (d) C₅H₆
Answer: (a)
Solution: C₆H₁₂ is the trap — that is cyclohexane, confused with benzene because both have six carbons. Benzene is C₆H₆ with alternating double bonds, so the answer is C₆H₆.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Chemistry
