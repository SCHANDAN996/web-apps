# 🧒 The Feynman Corner — General Chemistry

## The Concept Everyone Fumbles: Oxidation & Reduction (Redox)

Ask any exam aspirant "What is oxidation?" and watch the confusion begin. *"Oxygen is added... no wait, hydrogen is removed... no, electrons are lost... or was it gained?"* Four definitions, all true, all tangled. Let's untangle them the Feynman way.

---

## Explain It Like I'm 12

Imagine every atom is a kid sitting in a classroom, and **electrons are marbles**.

Some kids are marble-hoarders (like oxygen — it *loves* grabbing marbles). Some kids are careless and keep losing marbles (like sodium — it practically throws them away).

Now here's the game:

- **Losing marbles = Oxidation.** When an atom loses electrons, we say it got *oxidised*. Strange name, right? It's named after oxygen because oxygen is the biggest marble-snatcher in the playground — whenever oxygen shows up, someone loses marbles.
- **Gaining marbles = Reduction.** When an atom gains electrons, it's *reduced*. Why "reduced"? Because gaining negative electrons *reduces* its charge (a +2 charge becomes +1, or 0).

**The daily-life example: your rusting bicycle.**

Leave a cycle out in the rain. The iron (Fe) slowly turns into reddish-brown rust. What happened? Oxygen (the marble-snatcher) stole electrons from the iron. Iron got **oxidised** (lost marbles → became rust). Oxygen got **reduced** (gained marbles). That's why rusting is called "slow oxidation" — the same electron-snatching that happens in a fire, just lazier.

And the two always happen **together**. You can't snatch a marble unless someone loses it. That's why we say **redox** — reduction + oxidation, one coin, two sides.

---

## The Unforgettable Trick

**OIL RIG** — say it like a petrol pump in the sea:

- **O**xidation **I**s **L**oss (of electrons)
- **R**eduction **I**s **G**ain (of electrons)

And for the agents (the examiners' favourite trap):

- The **oxidising agent** is the *thief* — it oxidises others but gets **reduced itself**.
- The **reducing agent** is the *victim* — it reduces others but gets **oxidised itself**.

Memory line: *"The thief goes home richer — the agent that steals electrons gets reduced."*

---

## The Examiner's Trap, Defused

Examiners love asking: *"In the reaction Zn + CuSO₄ → ZnSO₄ + Cu, which substance is reduced?"*

Don't panic. Count the marbles:
- Zn → Zn²⁺ : zinc **lost** 2 electrons → **oxidised** (it's the reducing agent).
- Cu²⁺ → Cu : copper **gained** 2 electrons → **reduced** (CuSO₄ is the oxidising agent).

Answer: **Cu²⁺ (copper) is reduced.** The trap option is usually "Zn" — students pick it because zinc is "doing the reacting." Don't.
