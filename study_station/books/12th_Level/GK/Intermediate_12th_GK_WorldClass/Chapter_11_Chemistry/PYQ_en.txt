# 📊 PYQ Analysis — General Chemistry

## 1. How Exams Ask Questions from This Chapter

General Chemistry is one of the most dependable scoring areas in SSC (CGL, CHSL, MTS, GD), Railway (NTPC, Group D), Banking (IBPS, SBI) and State PCS prelims. Questions are almost always **direct, fact-based and NCERT-anchored** — the examiner does not test derivations, only recognition. Across exams, the pattern is remarkably stable:

- **Acids, Bases & Salts** — the single most repeated sub-topic. Typical asks: pH of common substances, chemical formula of washing soda / baking soda / plaster of Paris, which acid is present in vinegar / lemon / ant sting, and the colour of indicators (litmus, phenolphthalein, methyl orange).
- **Metals & Non-metals** — reactivity series order, which metal is liquid at room temperature (mercury), which non-metal is liquid (bromine), corrosion (rusting of iron, green coating on copper), and alloys (brass, bronze, stainless steel, solder).
- **Atomic Structure** — discovery of electron/proton/neutron (Thomson/Rutherford/Chadwick), atomic number vs mass number, isotopes/isobars/isotones, and Rutherford's scattering experiment.
- **Periodic Table** — Mendeleev's basis (atomic mass) vs modern basis (atomic number), group/period trends, alkali metals, halogens, noble gases.
- **Chemical Reactions & Bonding** — types of reactions (combination, decomposition, displacement), oxidation-reduction in everyday terms, ionic vs covalent bonds.
- **Carbon & Everyday Chemistry** — allotropes (diamond, graphite, fullerene), fuels, common chemicals (formula ↔ common name matching).

## 2. Sub-topic Weightage (in words, not numbers)

From a reading of recent papers, roughly speaking: **acids-bases-salts and metals-non-metals together dominate** — expect at least one question from these two in almost every SSC/Railway shift. **Atomic structure and the periodic table** appear regularly but slightly less often. **Carbon compounds and everyday chemistry** (formulas of common substances) is the steady filler. Banking exams lean toward everyday chemistry and formulas; Railways love "which acid is present in…" and alloy composition; SSC CGL occasionally pushes into slightly deeper NCERT Class 10 territory (reaction types, extraction of metals).

## 3. Examiner's Trap Types — Know Them Before the Exam

**Trap 1 — Almost Correct Statement.** Three options are perfectly true; the fourth changes one small detail. Example: "Washing soda is sodium carbonate decahydrate, Na₂CO₃·10H₂O" — the trap option says Na₂CO₃·7H₂O. *Defence:* memorise water-of-crystallisation numbers exactly.

**Trap 2 — Chronological / Discovery Confusion.** Electron–proton–neutron discoveries are swapped among Thomson, Rutherford, Millikan and Chadwick. *Defence:* fix the chain — Thomson (electron, 1897), Rutherford (proton), Chadwick (neutron, 1932).

**Trap 3 — Similar-Sounding Names.** Baking soda vs washing soda vs caustic soda; brass vs bronze; isotopes vs isobars. *Defence:* learn these as contrast pairs, never in isolation.

**Trap 4 — Negative Phrasing.** "Which of the following is **NOT** an allotrope of carbon?" Under time pressure students pick the first true allotrope they see. *Defence:* circle the NOT in the question before reading options.

**Trap 5 — pH Direction Confusion.** "pH less than 7" swapped with "greater than 7", or the colour of litmus reversed (acid turns blue litmus red — the trap says red litmus blue). *Defence:* one anchor memory — "Acid → Red" (A–R), everything else follows.

**Trap 6 — Liquid-at-room-temperature Swap.** Mercury (metal) and bromine (non-metal) are interchanged in options. *Defence:* remember the pair together.

## 4. Top 10 High-Yield PYQ-Style Questions with Solutions

**Q1.** The chemical formula of washing soda is:
(a) Na₂CO₃ (b) Na₂CO₃·10H₂O (c) NaHCO₃ (d) NaOH
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** Washing soda is sodium carbonate **decahydrate**, Na₂CO₃·10H₂O. Option (a) is anhydrous soda ash — the classic almost-correct trap. (c) is baking soda, (d) is caustic soda.
**Trap type:** Almost Correct | **Source:** NCERT Class 10 Science

**Q2.** Which acid is present in an ant's sting?
(a) Acetic acid (b) Citric acid (c) Formic acid (d) Lactic acid
**Answer: (c)**
**Solution:** Ant sting contains formic acid (methanoic acid) — which is why baking soda (a base) relieves it. Acetic acid = vinegar, citric acid = citrus fruits, lactic acid = curd.
**Trap type:** Similar-Sounding Names | **Source:** NCERT Class 10 Science

**Q3.** Which of the following is NOT an allotrope of carbon?
(a) Diamond (b) Graphite (c) Fullerene (d) Quartz
**Answer: (d)**
**Solution:** Quartz is silicon dioxide (SiO₂), not carbon. Diamond, graphite and fullerene (C₆₀) are all carbon allotropes. Note the negative phrasing — read the question twice.
**Trap type:** Negative Phrasing | **Source:** NCERT Class 10 Science

**Q4.** The neutron was discovered by:
(a) J.J. Thomson (b) Ernest Rutherford (c) James Chadwick (d) Robert Millikan
**Answer: (c)**
**Solution:** Chadwick discovered the neutron in 1932. Thomson discovered the electron; Rutherford discovered the nucleus and named the proton; Millikan measured the electron's charge (oil-drop experiment).
**Trap type:** Chronological Confusion | **Source:** NCERT Class 9 Science

**Q5.** Brass is an alloy of:
(a) Copper and tin (b) Copper and zinc (c) Iron and chromium (d) Copper and nickel
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** Brass = Cu + Zn. The trap is (a): copper + tin makes **bronze**. Iron + chromium (+ nickel) is stainless steel.
**Trap type:** Similar-Sounding Names | **Source:** NCERT Class 10 Science

**Q6.** The pH of a neutral solution at 25°C is:
(a) 0 (b) 14 (c) 7 (d) 1
**Answer: (c)**
**Solution:** pH 7 is neutral (pure water); below 7 acidic, above 7 basic. Options 0 and 14 are the scale extremes, planted to catch students who confuse "range" with "neutral point".
**Trap type:** Almost Correct | **Source:** NCERT Class 10 Science

**Q7.** Which non-metal is liquid at room temperature?
(a) Mercury (b) Bromine (c) Iodine (d) Chlorine
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** Bromine is the only liquid non-metal at room temperature. Mercury is liquid too — but it is a **metal**, which is exactly the swap the examiner wants you to miss. Iodine is solid, chlorine is gas.
**Trap type:** Liquid-Swap | **Source:** NCERT Class 10 Science

**Q8.** In the modern periodic table, elements are arranged according to their:
(a) Atomic mass (b) Atomic number (c) Density (d) Number of neutrons
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** Moseley's work established atomic number as the basis of the modern periodic law. Atomic mass (a) was **Mendeleev's** basis — the most frequently planted wrong option here.
**Trap type:** Chronological Confusion | **Source:** NCERT Class 10 Science

**Q9.** The green coating formed on copper vessels on exposure to moist air is due to:
(a) Copper oxide only (b) Basic copper carbonate (c) Copper sulphate (d) Copper chloride
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** Copper reacts slowly with CO₂, moisture and air to form a green layer of **basic copper carbonate** (CuCO₃·Cu(OH)₂). Option (a) is the almost-correct trap — copper oxide is black, not green.
**Trap type:** Almost Correct | **Source:** NCERT Class 10 Science

**Q10.** Isotopes of an element have the same number of ______ but different numbers of ______.
(a) neutrons, protons (b) protons, neutrons (c) electrons, protons (d) protons, electrons
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** Isotopes = same atomic number (same protons) but different mass numbers (different neutrons), e.g., ¹H, ²H, ³H. Option (a) is the exact reversal — read both blanks before answering.
**Trap type:** Almost Correct (reversal) | **Source:** NCERT Class 9 Science

## 5. The 15/45-Second Rule Applied

**Fast lane (15 seconds) — Q6 (pH of neutral solution) and Q7 (liquid non-metal):** These are pure recall. If you have revised the key-facts list, the answer should click the moment you finish reading the options. Do not re-read, do not second-guess — mark and move. Spending 40 seconds here steals time from statement-based questions.

**Slow lane (45 seconds) — Q3 (NOT an allotrope) and Q10 (isotopes fill-in):** Negative phrasing and two-blank questions are where haste kills. Budget the full 45 seconds: underline "NOT", check each option individually against the definition, and only then mark. A 15-second attempt on a NOT-question has a far higher error rate than a genuinely hard question.

**Rule of thumb for this chapter:** formula/name-matching and "which acid in…" questions → 15 seconds; NOT-questions, assertion-reason and match-the-list questions → 45 seconds. If a question needs more than 45 seconds, mark it for review and move on — chemistry questions in these exams never reward deep deliberation, only accurate recall.

## 6. Last-Minute Revision Pointers from PYQ Patterns

- Revise the **formula ↔ common name** table (washing soda, baking soda, caustic soda, quicklime, slaked lime, plaster of Paris, gypsum) — it appears in some form in nearly every exam cycle.
- Revise **acid sources** (vinegar, lemon, ant sting, curd, tamarind, tomato) as one list.
- Revise the **discovery chain** (electron/proton/neutron/nucleus) and **alloy compositions** (brass, bronze, steel, solder, amalgam) as contrast pairs.
- Practise reading **NOT/EXCEPT** questions slowly — this is where most avoidable marks are lost in this chapter.
