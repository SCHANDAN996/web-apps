Card 1
Front: Why does an iron nail rust faster in salty water than in pure water?
Back: Salt (NaCl) acts as an electrolyte and speeds up the flow of electrons in the oxidation of iron. Rusting is a slow oxidation process needing both oxygen and moisture; dissolved salts accelerate it.

Card 2
Front: Which gas is called "laughing gas", and what is its chemical formula?
Back: Nitrous oxide, N₂O. It is used as a mild anaesthetic in dentistry and surgery.

Card 3
Front: What is the pH of pure water at 25°C, and what does the pH scale measure?
Back: pH = 7 (neutral). The pH scale (0–14) measures the hydrogen ion concentration of a solution: below 7 is acidic, above 7 is basic (alkaline).

Card 4
Front: Which acid is present in vinegar, and what is its approximate concentration?
Back: Acetic acid (ethanoic acid, CH₃COOH), about 4–8% by volume. Vinegar is a dilute solution of acetic acid.

Card 5
Front: Memory trick: How can you quickly recall the reactivity series of common metals?
Back: Use the mnemonic "Please Stop Calling Me A Zebra, I Like Her Call Smart Goat" — Potassium, Sodium, Calcium, Magnesium, Aluminium, Zinc, Iron, Lead, Hydrogen, Copper, Silver, Gold. Metals above hydrogen displace it from acids.

Card 6
Front: Examiner's trap: Is rusting of iron a physical change or a chemical change — and why do students get it wrong?
Back: It is a chemical change because a new substance, hydrated iron(III) oxide (Fe₂O₃·xH₂O), is formed and the change is irreversible. Students wrongly call it physical because the iron "still looks like metal" underneath.

Card 7
Front: Which element is common to all acids, and which ion do acids release in water?
Back: Hydrogen. Acids release H⁺ ions (hydronium, H₃O⁺) when dissolved in water — this is the Arrhenius definition of an acid.

Card 8
Front: What is the chemical name and formula of washing soda, and how does it differ from baking soda?
Back: Washing soda is sodium carbonate decahydrate, Na₂CO₃·10H₂O. Baking soda is sodium hydrogen carbonate, NaHCO₃. Baking soda is milder and used in cooking; washing soda is used for cleaning and softening water.

Card 9
Front: Which gas is released when an acid reacts with a metal carbonate, and how do you test for it?
Back: Carbon dioxide (CO₂). Test: pass the gas through lime water (calcium hydroxide solution) — it turns milky due to formation of calcium carbonate.

Card 10
Front: Why is graphite a good conductor of electricity while diamond is not, though both are pure carbon?
Back: In graphite each carbon atom bonds to three others, leaving one free (delocalised) electron per atom that carries current. In diamond each carbon bonds to four others, so no free electrons exist. Both are allotropes of carbon.

Card 11
Front: Memory trick: How do you remember the colours of universal indicator from acid to base?
Back: Recall "ROYGBIV" order: strong acid = red, weak acid = orange/yellow, neutral = green, weak base = blue, strong base = violet/purple. Red means danger (strong acid), just like a traffic signal.

Card 12
Front: Examiner's trap: Does a base always contain a metal? Give the classic exception examiners use.
Back: No. Ammonium hydroxide (NH₄OH) is a base with no metal ion — the ammonium ion NH₄⁺ behaves like a metal ion. Examiners offer "all bases contain metals" as a tempting but false statement.

Card 13
Front: What is the chemical formula of plaster of Paris, and what happens when it is mixed with water?
Back: Calcium sulphate hemihydrate, CaSO₄·½H₂O. On adding water it sets into a hard mass of gypsum (CaSO₄·2H₂O) — used for setting fractured bones and moulds.

Card 14
Front: Which metal is liquid at room temperature, and which non-metal is also liquid at room temperature?
Back: Mercury (Hg) is the only metal liquid at room temperature; bromine (Br₂) is the only liquid non-metal. (Gallium and caesium melt just above room temperature.)

Card 15
Front: What is the difference between an atom, a molecule, and an ion?
Back: An atom is the smallest unit of an element; a molecule is two or more atoms chemically bonded (e.g., O₂, H₂O); an ion is a charged particle formed by loss or gain of electrons (cation = positive, anion = negative).

Card 16
Front: Which gas is used in the manufacture of ammonia by the Haber process, and what catalyst is used?
Back: Nitrogen (from air) reacts with hydrogen: N₂ + 3H₂ ⇌ 2NH₃. Iron is used as the catalyst, with high pressure and moderate temperature.

Card 17
Front: Why does dry HCl gas not change the colour of dry blue litmus paper?
Back: Acids show acidic behaviour only in the presence of water, because they must release H⁺ ions. Dry HCl gas has no water to ionise into, so it cannot affect dry litmus paper.

Card 18
Front: What is galvanisation, and which metal is coated on iron to prevent rusting?
Back: Galvanisation is coating iron or steel with a thin layer of zinc (Zn) to prevent rusting. Zinc protects iron even if scratched, because zinc is more reactive and corrodes preferentially (sacrificial protection).

Card 19
Front: Which acid is stored in the human stomach, and what is its role?
Back: Hydrochloric acid (HCl). It kills germs in food and creates the acidic medium needed for the enzyme pepsin to digest proteins. Excess acid causes acidity, treated with antacids like Mg(OH)₂.

Card 20
Front: What is the chemical formula of common salt, and which two important chemicals are manufactured from it?
Back: Sodium chloride, NaCl. From brine (its solution) we obtain sodium hydroxide (NaOH) and chlorine (Cl₂) by electrolysis (chlor-alkali process); hydrogen is also produced.
