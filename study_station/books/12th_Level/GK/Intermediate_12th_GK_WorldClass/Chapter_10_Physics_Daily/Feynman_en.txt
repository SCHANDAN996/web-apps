# 🧒 Feynman Technique — Physics in Everyday Life

## The Most Confusing Concept: Why Does a Pressure Cooker Cook Food Faster?

Most students memorise the line *"pressure cooker increases pressure, so boiling point rises"* — and then freeze when the examiner twists it: *"Why does food cook SLOWER on a mountain?"* or *"Why does water boil at a lower temperature at high altitude?"* Let's fix this forever.

### Explain It Like You're 12

Imagine water is a classroom full of restless children (water molecules). They all want to jump out of the classroom (escape as steam). But there's a heavy door pushing down on them — that door is the **air pressure** of the atmosphere.

- **In an open pot:** The door (air pressure) is normal weight. At **100°C**, the children become strong enough to push the door open — water boils. But here's the catch: once boiling starts, the temperature **stops rising**. All extra heat goes into pushing children out (making steam), not into making the water hotter. So your food cooks at a maximum of 100°C — slowly.

- **In a pressure cooker:** We make the door **much heavier** (the sealed lid traps steam, raising inside pressure to about 2 atmospheres). Now the children need to be far stronger to escape — water doesn't boil until about **120°C**. The food is now sitting in steam and water that is **hotter than 100°C**, so it cooks roughly **3–4 times faster**.

- **On a mountain:** The air is thin, so the door is **lighter**. Children escape easily — water boils at only about **90°C** (even lower on very high peaks). Boiling happens sooner, but the water is *cooler*, so food cooks **slower**. This is why hill-station cooks love pressure cookers!

**The golden insight:** Boiling point is not fixed at 100°C. It depends on pressure. **More pressure → higher boiling point → hotter cooking medium → faster cooking.**

### The Unforgettable Trick 🪄

> **"Heavy Door, Hot Water."**
>
> Pressure = weight of the door on the water.
> - **Heavy door (cooker)** → water must get **hotter** to boil → food cooks **fast**.
> - **Light door (mountain)** → water boils **early but cool** → food cooks **slow**.

Remember the pair: **Pressure UP → Boiling Point UP → Cooking time DOWN.** (And reverse everything for mountains.)

### Examiner's Traps to Watch 🚨

- **Trap 1:** "Water boils at 100°C everywhere." ❌ False — only at sea level (1 atm pressure).
- **Trap 2:** "A pressure cooker works because water boils faster." ❌ It actually boils *later* (at higher temperature) — that's the whole point.
- **Trap 3:** "On mountains, water takes longer to boil." ❌ It boils *sooner*, but at a lower temperature — so cooking takes longer. The examiner swaps "boiling" with "cooking" to catch you.
