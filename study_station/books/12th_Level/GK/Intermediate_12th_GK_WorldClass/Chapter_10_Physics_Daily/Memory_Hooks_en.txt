# 🪄 Memory Hooks & Short Tricks — Physics in Everyday Life

---

**Box 1 — Primary Colours of Light**
🔴🟢🔵 **"RGB = Really Good Bulbs"**
Red, Green, Blue — the three primary colours of *light* (they add up to white). Don't confuse with pigment primaries (used in paints)!

---

**Box 2 — Which Colour Scatters Most?**
💜 **"VIBGYOR runs away first — Violet Is Best Gone, Yet Others Remain"**
Violet scatters the MOST (shortest wavelength), Red the LEAST. The sky looks blue (not violet) because the Sun emits less violet light and our eyes are far more sensitive to blue. Danger signals are red because red scatters least and travels farthest through fog and dust.

---

**Box 3 — Why the Sky is Blue**
☁️ **"Tiny molecules, tiny waves — blue gets bounced all day"**
Air molecules are much smaller than light's wavelength → Rayleigh scattering → shorter (blue) wavelengths scatter much more than red, so scattered blue light reaches our eyes from all directions.

---

**Box 4 — Convex vs Concave Mirror**
🪞 **"CONvex = CONcentrated view, wide like a CONe"**
Convex mirror → always virtual, erect, DIMINISHED image → used as rear-view mirror (wide field).
Concave mirror → converging → shaving mirror, dentist's mirror, headlights, solar cookers.

---

**Box 5 — Lens Power Formula**
🔍 **"Power is the flip of focal length: P = 1/f (metres!)"**
Power in Dioptre = 1 / focal length in **metres**. Trap: if f is given in cm, convert first! f = 50 cm → P = 1/0.5 = +2 D.

---

**Box 6 — Myopia vs Hypermetropia**
👓 **"SHORT sight needs a SHORT (concave) cut; LONG sight needs a LONG (convex) pull"**
- Myopia (near-sightedness) → corrected by **concave** lens.
- Hypermetropia (far-sightedness) → corrected by **convex** lens.
- Presbyopia (old age, both) → **bifocal** lens.

---

**Box 7 — Ohm's Law Triangle**
⚡ **"VIR the warrior: V = I × R"**
Cover what you want: V on top, I and R below. V = IR, I = V/R, R = V/I.

---

**Box 8 — Series vs Parallel**
🔌 **"Series SHARES current, Parallel SHARES voltage"**
- Series: same current flows through all, voltage divides → R = R₁ + R₂ + …
- Parallel: same voltage across all, current divides → 1/R = 1/R₁ + 1/R₂ + …
Homes use **parallel** wiring — one appliance off doesn't kill the rest.

---

**Box 9 — Fuse Wire Facts**
🔥 **"Fuse = Low Melt, High Fight"**
Fuse wire has **low melting point** and **high resistance** (tin-lead alloy). It melts and breaks the circuit when current exceeds the safe limit. Always connected in **series** on the live wire.

---

**Box 10 — Heat Transfer Trio**
🌡️ **"Conduction needs Contact, Convection needs Currents (fluids), Radiation needs Nothing"**
- Conduction → solids (spoon in hot tea).
- Convection → liquids & gases (sea breeze, boiling water).
- Radiation → no medium needed (Sun's heat reaches Earth through vacuum).

---

**Box 11 — Why a Black Body in Winter, White in Summer**
⚫⚪ **"Black Absorbs All, White Waves Goodbye"**
Black surfaces absorb (and emit) radiation best; white/light surfaces reflect most. Hence light clothes in summer.

---

**Box 12 — Inertia Types**
🚌 **"REST wants REST, MOTION wants MOTION, DIRECTION wants DIRECTION"**
- Inertia of rest: bus starts suddenly → passengers jerk backward.
- Inertia of motion: bus stops suddenly → passengers lurch forward.
- Inertia of direction: turning car → passengers thrown outward.

---

**Box 13 — Density & Floating**
🧊 **"Lighter than water? Float like a feather. Heavier? Sink forever."**
Ice floats because its density (~0.92 g/cm³) is less than water (1 g/cm³) — and water has MAXIMUM density at **4°C**, which is why ponds freeze from the top.

---

**Box 14 — Sound vs Light Speed**
🌩️ **"See the flash, count the crash"**
Light (~3 × 10⁸ m/s) is vastly faster than sound (~332 m/s in air). Count seconds between lightning and thunder ÷ 3 ≈ distance in km. Sound CANNOT travel in vacuum — light can.

---

**Box 15 — Pressure Trick**
🔪 **"Sharp knife, small area, BIG pressure — P = F/A"**
Smaller area → more pressure for the same force. That's why nails are pointed, knives are sharp, and camels (wide feet) don't sink in sand.

---

## ⏭️ Skip Strategy — When to Leave the Question

1. **Numerical with unit conversion traps** (e.g., lens power with f in cm, or km/h ↔ m/s): if you can't do the conversion cleanly in your head within 20 seconds, mark it and move on — one wrong conversion guarantees a wrong answer and negative marking.

2. **"Which of the following pairs is correctly matched?" questions** (instrument ↔ principle, scientist ↔ discovery): if even ONE pair looks unfamiliar, don't gamble by elimination on partial knowledge — skip and return only if time remains.

3. **Statement-based questions with absolute words** ("always", "never", "only"): if you can't recall a definite exception or confirmation, skip. These are designed so that a half-remembered fact feels correct — the cost of guessing (−0.25/−0.50) usually beats the reward.
