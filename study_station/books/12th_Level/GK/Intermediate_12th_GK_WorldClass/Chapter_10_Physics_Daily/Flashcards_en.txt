Card 1
Front: Why does a steel ship float on water while a small steel needle sinks?
Back: The ship's hollow shape makes its average density less than water, so buoyant force exceeds its weight. The solid needle's density is greater than water, so it sinks.

Card 2
Front: Why does the sky appear blue during the day?
Back: Air molecules scatter shorter wavelengths (blue) of sunlight much more than longer ones — this is Rayleigh scattering, so scattered blue light reaches our eyes from all directions.

Card 3
Front: Why does the Sun look red at sunrise and sunset?
Back: Sunlight then travels a longer path through the atmosphere; most blue light is scattered away, leaving mainly red/orange wavelengths to reach us.

Card 4
Front: Why do we see lightning before we hear thunder?
Back: Light travels at about 3 × 10⁸ m/s while sound travels only about 330–340 m/s in air, so the flash arrives almost instantly and the sound takes seconds.

Card 5
Front: Why does a swimming pool look shallower than it really is?
Back: Refraction — light from the pool bottom bends away from the normal as it leaves water, so the bottom appears raised (apparent depth is less than real depth).

Card 6
Front: Why does a thick glass tumbler crack when hot water is suddenly poured into it?
Back: Uneven thermal expansion — the inner surface of thick glass heats and expands faster than the outer surface, creating stress that cracks the glass. Thin glass or borosilicate resists this. (A metal spoon placed in the glass helps, as metal conducts heat away and reduces the temperature difference.)

Card 7
Front: Why is it easier to pull a lawn roller than to push it?
Back: Pulling has an upward component that reduces effective weight and friction; pushing has a downward component that increases friction.

Card 8
Front: Why do passengers lurch forward when a moving bus stops suddenly?
Back: Inertia of motion (Newton's first law) — the upper body keeps moving forward even after the lower body stops with the bus.

Card 9
Front: Why does a black umbrella keep you cooler in summer than a white one — or does it?
Back: Trap alert! A black surface absorbs more heat, so a white/light umbrella is cooler. Black objects are good absorbers AND good radiators, but under the sun absorption dominates.

Card 10
Front: Why does ice float on water?
Back: Water has maximum density at 4°C; ice has an open crystal structure, making it about 9% less dense than liquid water, so it floats.

Card 11
Front: Why does a pressure cooker cook food faster?
Back: Increased pressure raises water's boiling point above 100°C, so food cooks at a higher temperature and faster.

Card 12
Front: Why do we hear an echo in an empty hall but not in a furnished room?
Back: Curtains, carpets and furniture absorb sound; bare walls reflect it. A distinct echo needs the reflected sound to arrive at least about 0.1 s after the original (minimum distance ≈ 17 m).

Card 13
Front: Why does a diamond sparkle so brilliantly?
Back: Its very high refractive index (~2.42) gives a small critical angle (~24°), so light undergoes repeated total internal reflection and emerges concentrated.

Card 14
Front: Which mnemonic helps you remember the colours of the rainbow in order?
Back: VIBGYOR — Violet, Indigo, Blue, Green, Yellow, Orange, Red (increasing wavelength from violet to red).

Card 15
Front: What memory trick tells you whether a concave mirror image is real or virtual?
Back: "Concave = Cave = Real in front" — a concave mirror forms real, inverted images in front of it (except when the object is between pole and focus, giving a virtual, erect, magnified image — the shaving-mirror case).

Card 16
Front: Examiner's trap: "The colour of the sky is blue because of reflection of sunlight." True or false?
Back: False. It is scattering (Rayleigh), not reflection. Examiners swap scattering, refraction and dispersion — always match: sky blue = scattering, rainbow = dispersion + internal reflection, pool depth = refraction.

Card 17
Front: Examiner's trap: "In space, astronauts can talk to each other normally without radio." True or false?
Back: False. Sound needs a material medium and cannot travel through the vacuum of space; astronauts use radio waves (electromagnetic), which need no medium.

Card 18
Front: Why does a cricketer pull his hands back while catching a fast ball?
Back: To increase the time of impact, which reduces the force on the hands (impulse = force × time; same momentum change over longer time means smaller force).

Card 19
Front: Why are the handles of cooking utensils made of plastic or wood?
Back: Plastic and wood are bad conductors of heat (insulators), so the handles stay cool while the metal body heats up.

Card 20
Front: Why does a piece of paper fall slowly while a crumpled paper ball falls fast?
Back: Air resistance — the flat sheet has a large surface area facing the air, so drag slows it; the crumpled ball presents less area. In vacuum, both would fall together (Galileo's principle).
