1. Why does a piece of iron feel colder than a piece of wood at the same room temperature?
(a) Iron is actually at a lower temperature (b) Iron absorbs cold from the surroundings (c) Wood produces heat on touching (d) Iron conducts heat away from the hand faster
Answer: (d)
Solution: Both objects are at the same temperature, so the claim that iron is colder is the trap. Iron is a good conductor of heat, so it draws heat from your warm hand quickly, making it feel colder, so the answer is that iron conducts heat away from the hand faster.
Source: NCERT Class 7 Science, Chapter: Heat

2. The working of a pressure cooker is based on which principle?
(a) Boiling point decreases with increase in pressure (b) Melting point decreases with pressure (c) Boiling point increases with increase in pressure (d) Steam has no latent heat
Answer: (c)
Solution: The tempting wrong idea is that pressure lowers the boiling point — in fact, lowering pressure lowers the boiling point, which is why water boils below 100°C on mountains. Inside a cooker, pressure rises, so water boils above 100°C and food cooks faster, so the answer is that boiling point increases with increase in pressure.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Physics, Chapter: Thermal Properties of Matter

3. Which colour of light is scattered the most in the atmosphere?
(a) Red (b) Yellow (c) Violet (d) Green
Answer: (c)
Solution: Many students pick red because the sky "looks blue", but scattering is inversely proportional to the fourth power of wavelength, so the shortest wavelength scatters most. Violet has the shortest wavelength among the options, so the answer is violet.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science, Chapter: Human Eye and the Colourful World

4. The sky appears blue because of:
(a) Reflection of sunlight by oceans (b) Dispersion of light by raindrops (c) Refraction of light by clouds (d) Scattering of light by air molecules
Answer: (d)
Solution: Dispersion by raindrops causes a rainbow, not the blue sky — that is the common confusion. Air molecules scatter shorter (blue) wavelengths more than longer ones, so the answer is scattering of light by air molecules.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science, Chapter: Human Eye and the Colourful World

5. A convex mirror is used as a rear-view mirror in vehicles because it:
(a) Gives a magnified image (b) Gives a real image (c) Gives an erect image and a wider field of view (d) Converges light to a point
Answer: (c)
Solution: A convex mirror always forms a virtual, erect and diminished image — "magnified image" is the trap. Its outward curve spreads reflected rays, covering a wider area behind the vehicle, so the answer is that it gives an erect image and a wider field of view.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science, Chapter: Light – Reflection and Refraction

6. The fuse wire in an electric circuit is made of a material having:
(a) Low melting point and high resistance (b) High melting point and high resistance (c) Low melting point and low resistance (d) High melting point and low resistance
Answer: (a)
Solution: A high melting point would defeat the purpose — the wire must melt quickly when excess current flows. Fuse wire (an alloy of tin and lead) has high resistance to heat up fast and a low melting point to break the circuit, so the answer is low melting point and high resistance.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science, Chapter: Electricity

7. Which phenomenon explains the twinkling of stars?
(a) Reflection (b) Atmospheric refraction (c) Total internal reflection (d) Dispersion
Answer: (b)
Solution: Total internal reflection explains optical fibres and the sparkle of diamonds, not twinkling — that is the trap. Starlight passes through layers of air of varying density, and the continuously changing refraction makes the star's apparent position and brightness fluctuate, so the answer is atmospheric refraction.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science, Chapter: Human Eye and the Colourful World

8. The SI unit of electric power is:
(a) Watt (b) Joule (c) Volt (d) Ampere
Answer: (a)
Solution: Joule is the unit of energy, not power — a frequent mix-up. Power is the rate of doing work or consuming energy, measured in watts (1 watt = 1 joule/second), so the answer is watt.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science, Chapter: Electricity

9. Why does a swimmer push the water backward?
(a) To get a forward reaction force (Newton's third law) (b) To reduce friction (c) To increase buoyancy (d) To decrease water pressure
Answer: (a)
Solution: Buoyancy acts vertically and is not changed by pushing water backward — that option is a distractor. By Newton's third law, the backward push on water produces an equal and opposite forward push on the swimmer, so the answer is to get a forward reaction force.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Science, Chapter: Force and Laws of Motion

10. An echo is heard distinctly only when the reflecting surface is at least about how far from the source (at normal temperature)?
(a) 5 metres (b) 10 metres (c) 34.4 metres (d) 17.2 metres
Answer: (d)
Solution: 34.4 m is the trap — it is the distance sound travels in 0.1 s one way, but the sound must go and return. Persistence of hearing is 0.1 s, so minimum total distance is 344 × 0.1 = 34.4 m, giving a one-way distance of 17.2 m, so the answer is 17.2 metres.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Science, Chapter: Sound

11. Which of the following is used in optical fibres?
(a) Refraction (b) Polarisation (c) Diffraction (d) Total internal reflection
Answer: (d)
Solution: Ordinary refraction would let light escape through the fibre walls — that is the trap. Light entering the fibre strikes the core–cladding boundary at angles greater than the critical angle and is totally internally reflected along the fibre, so the answer is total internal reflection.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Physics, Chapter: Ray Optics

12. The device used to measure electric current in a circuit is:
(a) Voltmeter (b) Ammeter (c) Galvanometer only (d) Ohmmeter
Answer: (b)
Solution: A voltmeter measures potential difference and is connected in parallel — confusing the two is the classic trap. An ammeter measures current and is connected in series with low resistance, so the answer is ammeter.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science, Chapter: Electricity

13. Food cooks faster in a pressure cooker mainly because:
(a) Steam is dry inside (b) Water boils at a higher temperature under pressure (c) The metal of the cooker is special (d) Heat cannot escape at all
Answer: (b)
Solution: No cooker is perfectly heat-tight, so "heat cannot escape at all" is false. Increased pressure raises the boiling point of water to about 120°C, and hotter steam cooks food faster, so the answer is that water boils at a higher temperature under pressure.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Physics, Chapter: Thermal Properties of Matter

14. The image formed by a plane mirror is:
(a) Virtual, erect and same size (b) Real, inverted and same size (c) Virtual, inverted and magnified (d) Real, erect and diminished
Answer: (a)
Solution: A plane mirror never forms a real image — options claiming "real" are traps. The image is virtual, erect, laterally inverted and of the same size as the object, so the answer is virtual, erect and same size.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science, Chapter: Light – Reflection and Refraction

15. Which of the following quantities remains unchanged when light passes from air into glass?
(a) Speed (b) Frequency (c) Wavelength (d) Direction (for oblique incidence)
Answer: (b)
Solution: Speed and wavelength both decrease in glass, so those options are traps. Frequency is determined by the source and does not change during refraction, so the answer is frequency.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Physics, Chapter: Wave Optics

16. A man standing in a moving bus falls forward when the bus suddenly stops. This is due to:
(a) Inertia of rest (b) Inertia of motion (c) Inertia of direction (d) Gravitational pull
Answer: (b)
Solution: Inertia of rest applies when a stationary body resists being moved — the opposite situation, and the trap here. The lower body stops with the bus while the upper body continues moving forward due to inertia of motion, so the answer is inertia of motion.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Science, Chapter: Force and Laws of Motion

17. The commercial unit of electrical energy is:
(a) Kilowatt-hour (b) Joule (c) Watt (d) Kilowatt
Answer: (a)
Solution: Kilowatt is a unit of power, not energy — dropping "hour" is the trap. One kilowatt-hour equals 3.6 × 10⁶ joules and is the "unit" on electricity bills, so the answer is kilowatt-hour.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science, Chapter: Electricity

18. Which mirror is used by dentists to examine teeth?
(a) Convex mirror (b) Plane mirror (c) Concave mirror (d) Cylindrical mirror
Answer: (c)
Solution: A convex mirror gives a diminished image, useless for seeing detail — that is the trap. A concave mirror forms an erect, magnified image when the object (tooth) is within its focal length, so the answer is concave mirror.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science, Chapter: Light – Reflection and Refraction

19. Sound travels fastest in:
(a) Air (b) Water (c) Steel (d) Vacuum
Answer: (c)
Solution: Vacuum is the trap — sound is a mechanical wave and cannot travel through vacuum at all. Sound travels fastest in solids because particles are closely packed, so among the options the answer is steel.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Science, Chapter: Sound

20. The red colour of the Sun at sunrise and sunset is due to:
(a) Dispersion of light (b) Scattering of shorter wavelengths away, leaving red (c) Reflection from clouds (d) Total internal reflection
Answer: (b)
Solution: Dispersion produces a spectrum (rainbow), not the red Sun — that is the trap. Near the horizon sunlight travels a longer path through the atmosphere; blue light is scattered away and the least-scattered red reaches the eye, so the answer is scattering of shorter wavelengths away, leaving red.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science, Chapter: Human Eye and the Colourful World

21. An electric bulb is rated 100 W, 220 V. Its resistance while glowing is:
(a) 2.2 Ω (b) 220 Ω (c) 100 Ω (d) 484 Ω
Answer: (d)
Solution: Simply dividing 220 by 100 gives 2.2 Ω, which wrongly treats the values as V and I — that is the trap. Using R = V²/P = 220 × 220 / 100 = 484 Ω, so the answer is 484 Ω.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science, Chapter: Electricity

22. A ray of light passes from water (refractive index 4/3) to glass (refractive index 3/2). The ray will:
(a) Bend towards the normal (b) Bend away from the normal (c) Pass undeviated (d) Undergo total internal reflection always
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Always total internal reflection" is the trap — it occurs only beyond the critical angle when going from denser to rarer medium, which is not the case here. Glass is optically denser than water, so light entering a denser medium bends towards the normal, so the answer is bend towards the normal.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science, Chapter: Light – Reflection and Refraction

23. Two resistors of 6 Ω and 3 Ω are connected in parallel. Their equivalent resistance is:
(a) 9 Ω (b) 3 Ω (c) 2 Ω (d) 0.5 Ω
Answer: (c)
Solution: Adding the values gives 9 Ω, which is the series formula — the most common trap. In parallel, R = (6 × 3)/(6 + 3) = 18/9 = 2 Ω, and note the result is less than the smallest resistor, so the answer is 2 Ω.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science, Chapter: Electricity

24. Which of the following statements about a concave mirror is correct?
(a) It always forms a virtual image (b) It always forms a real image (c) It can never form an image of the same size as the object (d) It forms a real image when the object is beyond the focus, and a virtual magnified image when the object is between the pole and the focus
Answer: (d)
Solution: "Always real" and "always virtual" are both traps — a concave mirror can do either depending on object position. Beyond the focus the image is real and inverted; inside the focus it is virtual, erect and magnified; at the centre of curvature it is same-sized, so the answer is the statement describing both cases correctly.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science, Chapter: Light – Reflection and Refraction

25. A person cannot see distant objects clearly and the image forms in front of the retina. The defect and its correction are:
(a) Myopia — concave lens (b) Hypermetropia — convex lens (c) Presbyopia — bifocal lens (d) Astigmatism — cylindrical lens
Answer: (a)
Solution: Hypermetropia with a convex lens is the trap — that pair applies when the image forms behind the retina (near objects blurred). Here the image forms in front of the retina, which is myopia (short-sightedness), corrected by a diverging concave lens, so the answer is myopia — concave lens.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science, Chapter: Human Eye and the Colourful World
