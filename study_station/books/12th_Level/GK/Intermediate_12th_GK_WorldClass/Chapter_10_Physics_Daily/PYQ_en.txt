# 📊 PYQ Analysis — Physics in Everyday Life

## 1. How Exams Ask Questions from This Chapter

Physics in Everyday Life is a favourite of SSC (CGL, CHSL, MTS, GD), RRB (NTPC, Group D), Banking (IBPS/SBI prelims GK) and State PCS prelims because it needs no formula-heavy calculation — only clear concepts. Across these exams, questions from this chapter appear in a few predictable patterns:

- **Direct concept recall** — "Which phenomenon causes a mirage?", "Why does the sky appear blue?" These are one-line questions testing a single named phenomenon (scattering, total internal reflection, surface tension, etc.).
- **Application-based questions** — a daily-life situation is described (a straw looks bent in water, a pressure cooker cooks faster, a soap bubble shows colours) and you must name the principle behind it.
- **Instrument–principle matching** — periscope, optical fibre, hydraulic brake, transformer, sonar — matched with the law or phenomenon they use.
- **Unit and quantity questions** — SI unit of power, pressure, frequency; dimensional mixing is a common trap.
- **Statement-based questions** (more in CGL/PCS) — two or three statements about one phenomenon, asking which are correct.

Sub-topics that recur most often: light (reflection, refraction, TIR, scattering, dispersion), heat (conduction/convection/radiation, specific heat, pressure cooker), sound (echo, Doppler, ultrasonic uses), fluids (surface tension, capillarity, Bernoulli, Archimedes), electricity in daily life (fuse, earthing, series vs parallel at home), and simple mechanics (inertia, friction, centrifugal effects).

## 2. Exam-wise Emphasis (in words, not numbers)

- **SSC CGL / CHSL**: light and everyday applications dominate; statement-based traps are common.
- **SSC MTS / GD, RRB Group D**: very direct one-liners — "phenomenon → name" or "device → principle".
- **RRB NTPC**: mixes units, instruments and applications; negative phrasing appears often.
- **Banking (IBPS/SBI)**: fewer questions, but when they come they are simple recall — units, inventors, common devices.
- **State PCS prelims**: slightly deeper — "why" questions (why does a metal feel colder than wood at the same temperature).

## 3. The Examiner's Traps (जाल) — Know Them Before the Exam

1. **Almost Correct** — the option is nearly right but swaps one word: "Mirage is due to *reflection* of light" (it is total internal *reflection* caused by *refraction* through hot air layers). Read every word.
2. **Chronological / Cause-Effect Confusion** — "Lightning is seen before thunder is heard because light travels *slower* than sound" — the order is right, the reason is reversed.
3. **Negative Phrasing** — "Which of the following is NOT an application of total internal reflection?" Under time pressure, students miss the NOT. Underline it mentally.
4. **Similar-Sounding Names** — refraction vs reflection vs diffraction; conduction vs convection; concave vs convex. Options are deliberately placed side by side.
5. **Unit Swap** — power in joule, work in watt, pressure in pascal vs newton. One option is always the unit of a *related* quantity.
6. **Everyday Misconception as an Option** — "A pressure cooker cooks faster because pressure *decreases* the boiling point" — sounds plausible, is wrong (pressure *raises* the boiling point).

## 4. Top 10 High-Yield PYQ-Style Questions with Solutions

**Q1.** The blue colour of the sky is due to —
(a) Reflection of light (b) Refraction of light (c) Scattering of light (d) Dispersion of light
**Answer: (c)**
**Solution:** Air molecules scatter shorter (blue) wavelengths most — Rayleigh scattering. Trap type: *Similar-Sounding Names* — dispersion (rainbow) and refraction are placed to confuse.
**Source:** NCERT Class 10 Science (Light chapter)

**Q2.** A stick immersed in water appears bent because of —
(a) Reflection (b) Refraction (c) Diffraction (d) Interference
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** Light changes speed (and bends) while passing from water to air, so the stick looks bent at the surface. Trap type: *Almost Correct* — reflection is the tempting wrong twin.
**Source:** NCERT Class 10 Science

**Q3.** Which of the following is NOT based on total internal reflection?
(a) Optical fibre (b) Sparkling of diamond (c) Mirage (d) Rainbow
**Answer: (d)**
**Solution:** A rainbow is caused by refraction, dispersion and *internal* reflection inside raindrops — it is not a TIR application like the other three. Trap type: *Negative Phrasing* — the NOT is the whole question.
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q4.** In a pressure cooker, food cooks faster because —
(a) Boiling point of water decreases (b) Boiling point of water increases (c) Water evaporates faster (d) Heat conductivity of the cooker is high
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** Higher pressure inside raises water's boiling point above 100°C, so food cooks at a higher temperature. Trap type: *Everyday Misconception* — option (a) states the exact opposite of the truth.
**Source:** NCERT Class 11 Physics (Kinetic Theory / Thermodynamics)

**Q5.** The SI unit of power is —
(a) Joule (b) Newton (c) Watt (d) Pascal
**Answer: (c)**
**Solution:** Power = work/time, measured in watt (J/s). Trap type: *Unit Swap* — joule (work) is the classic wrong pick.
**Source:** NCERT Class 9 Science (Work and Energy)

**Q6.** An echo is heard distinctly only when the reflecting surface is at least about how far from the listener (at normal temperature)?
(a) 5 m (b) 17 m (c) 34 m (d) 50 m
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** Sound takes ~0.1 s to return for the ear to distinguish it; with speed ≈ 340 m/s, distance ≈ 340 × 0.1 / 2 ≈ 17 m. Trap type: *Almost Correct* — 34 m is the *total path*, not the distance to the wall.
**Source:** NCERT Class 9 Science (Sound)

**Q7.** Hydraulic brakes in vehicles work on —
(a) Bernoulli's principle (b) Pascal's law (c) Archimedes' principle (d) Boyle's law
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** Pascal's law: pressure applied to an enclosed fluid is transmitted equally in all directions — this multiplies the braking force. Trap type: *Similar-Sounding Names* — all four are fluid-related laws.
**Source:** NCERT Class 11 Physics (Mechanical Properties of Fluids)

**Q8.** Why does a metal plate feel colder than a wooden table at the same room temperature?
(a) Metal is at a lower temperature (b) Metal conducts heat away from the hand faster (c) Wood produces heat (d) Metal absorbs cold from air
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** Both are at the same temperature; metal's high thermal conductivity drains heat from your skin quickly, so it *feels* colder. Trap type: *Everyday Misconception* — option (a) denies that both are in thermal equilibrium.
**Source:** NCERT Class 11 Physics (Thermal Properties of Matter)

**Q9.** A fuse wire used in domestic circuits has —
(a) High melting point and high resistance (b) Low melting point and high resistance (c) Low melting point and low resistance (d) High melting point and low resistance
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** The fuse must melt quickly when current exceeds the safe limit — hence low melting point; its relatively high resistance produces the heating. Trap type: *Almost Correct* — option (a) flips one property.
**Source:** NCERT Class 10 Science (Magnetic Effects of Electric Current)

**Q10.** The pitch of a sound depends on its —
(a) Amplitude (b) Frequency (c) Speed (d) Wavelength only
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** Higher frequency → higher pitch; amplitude controls loudness, not pitch. Trap type: *Similar-Sounding Names* — amplitude (loudness) is the planted confusion.
**Source:** NCERT Class 9 Science (Sound)

## 5. The 15/45-Second Rule Applied

**Rule:** If a question is pure recall (a named phenomenon, a unit, a device–principle pair), decide within **15 seconds** — you either know it or you don't; extra staring won't help. Reserve up to **45 seconds** only for questions needing a small calculation or careful statement-checking.

- **Q5 (unit of power)** — a 15-second question. You know watt or you don't. Mark and move. Never spend 45 seconds re-reading four units.
- **Q6 (echo distance)** — a legitimate 45-second question. It needs the half-distance logic (sound goes and returns). Do the quick mental math (340 × 0.1 ÷ 2), verify the trap option 34 m, then answer.
- **Q3 (NOT question)** — 15 seconds to spot the NOT, up to 45 seconds to verify each option against TIR. The extra time goes into *checking*, not guessing.

**Exam strategy:** In this chapter, roughly four out of five questions are 15-second recall. Bank that saved time for the few calculation/statement questions — that is where this chapter's marks are actually won or lost.
