Questions 76–100: Medium

76. A person pushes a wall with a force of 50 N but the wall does not move. The work done by the person on the wall is
(a) 50 J (b) 500 J (c) 0 J (d) Depends on the time of pushing
Answer: (c)
Solution: The tempting option is 50 J, which wrongly assumes force alone means work. Work done equals force multiplied by displacement in the direction of force; since the wall does not move, displacement is zero, so the work done is 0 J.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Science (Work and Energy)

77. An electric bulb is rated "100 W, 220 V". The resistance of its filament while glowing is
(a) 220 Ω (b) 2.2 Ω (c) 100 Ω (d) 484 Ω
Answer: (d)
Solution: Many students divide 220 by 100 and pick 2.2 Ω, which mixes up the formula. Using P = V²/R, we get R = V²/P = 220 × 220 / 100 = 484 Ω, so the answer is 484 Ω.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science (Electricity)

78. A fuse wire used in household circuits should have
(a) Low melting point and high resistance (b) High melting point and high resistance (c) Low melting point and low resistance (d) High melting point and low resistance
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is "high melting point", which sounds safe but would prevent the fuse from melting during overload. A fuse must melt quickly when current exceeds the safe limit, so it needs a low melting point and relatively high resistance, so the answer is low melting point and high resistance.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science (Magnetic Effects of Electric Current)

79. When a ray of light passes from air into water, it bends
(a) Away from the normal because water is rarer (b) Away from the normal because water is denser (c) Towards the normal because water is denser (d) It does not bend at all
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap pairs the correct direction with the wrong reason or the correct reason with the wrong direction. Water is optically denser than air, so light slows down and bends towards the normal, so the answer is towards the normal because water is denser.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science (Light – Reflection and Refraction)

80. The image formed by a plane mirror is always
(a) Virtual, erect and same size (b) Real, inverted and same size (c) Virtual, inverted and magnified (d) Real, erect and diminished
Answer: (a)
Solution: The tempting error is "real", because the image looks solid, but real images can be caught on a screen and a plane mirror's image cannot. A plane mirror always forms a virtual, erect image of the same size as the object, so the answer is virtual, erect and same size.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science (Light – Reflection and Refraction)

81. A concave mirror is used by dentists to see teeth because when the object is very close to it, the mirror forms
(a) A real, inverted and magnified image (b) A virtual, erect and magnified image (c) A virtual, erect and diminished image (d) A real, erect and same-size image
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is "real, inverted and magnified", which is true only when the object is beyond the focus. When the tooth lies between the pole and the focus, the concave mirror gives a virtual, erect and magnified image, so the answer is virtual, erect and magnified.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science (Light – Reflection and Refraction)

82. The sky appears blue because
(a) Blue light is scattered most by air molecules (b) Blue light is absorbed most by the atmosphere (c) The ocean reflects blue colour to the sky (d) Blue light travels fastest in air
Answer: (a)
Solution: The ocean-reflection idea is a popular myth and a common trap. Shorter wavelengths like blue are scattered far more strongly by air molecules than longer wavelengths (Rayleigh scattering), so the sky appears blue, so the answer is blue light is scattered most by air molecules.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science (Human Eye and the Colourful World)

83. The sun appears reddish at sunrise and sunset because
(a) The sun emits more red light in the morning (b) Light travels a longer path through the atmosphere, so most blue light is scattered away (c) Red light is scattered the most (d) The atmosphere absorbs all colours except red at noon
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap says red light is scattered the most, which is the exact opposite of reality — blue scatters most. Near the horizon sunlight crosses a thicker layer of air, scattering away most blue light and leaving red, so the answer is that light travels a longer path through the atmosphere.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science (Human Eye and the Colourful World)

84. A person suffering from myopia (near-sightedness) cannot see distant objects clearly. This defect is corrected using
(a) A concave lens (b) A convex lens (c) A cylindrical lens (d) A bifocal lens only
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is the convex lens, which corrects hypermetropia, not myopia — the similar names cause confusion. In myopia the image forms in front of the retina, and a concave (diverging) lens shifts it back onto the retina, so the answer is a concave lens.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science (Human Eye and the Colourful World)

85. Two resistors of 6 Ω and 3 Ω are connected in parallel. Their equivalent resistance is
(a) 9 Ω (b) 3 Ω (c) 2 Ω (d) 18 Ω
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is 9 Ω, obtained by simply adding the resistors, which is the series rule. For parallel combination, 1/R = 1/6 + 1/3 = 3/6, giving R = 2 Ω, so the answer is 2 Ω.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science (Electricity)

86. In household wiring in India, appliances are connected in parallel because
(a) Parallel connection saves electricity (b) Series connection is banned by law (c) Parallel wiring uses less copper wire (d) Each appliance gets the full mains voltage and can be operated independently
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is "saves electricity", which sounds practical but is not the reason. In parallel, every appliance receives the full 220 V and a fault or switch in one branch does not affect the others, so the answer is that each appliance gets the full mains voltage and can be operated independently.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science (Magnetic Effects of Electric Current)

87. A ball is thrown vertically upwards. At the highest point of its path, its
(a) Velocity and acceleration are both zero (b) Velocity is zero but acceleration is 9.8 m/s² downward (c) Velocity is maximum and acceleration is zero (d) Velocity and acceleration are both maximum
Answer: (b)
Solution: The strong trap is that both velocity and acceleration are zero at the top — but gravity never stops acting. At the highest point the velocity is momentarily zero while acceleration due to gravity remains 9.8 m/s² downward, so the answer is velocity is zero but acceleration is 9.8 m/s² downward.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Science (Motion/Gravitation)

88. When a bus suddenly starts moving, passengers standing inside tend to fall backwards. This is explained by
(a) Newton's third law of motion (b) Inertia of rest (c) Inertia of motion (d) Conservation of momentum
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is inertia of motion, which applies when a moving bus suddenly stops and passengers lurch forward. Here the body was at rest and tends to stay at rest while the bus moves forward, which is inertia of rest, so the answer is inertia of rest.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Science (Force and Laws of Motion)

89. It is easier to push a heavy box on a surface when rollers are placed underneath because
(a) Rollers reduce the weight of the box (b) Rollers increase the area of contact (c) Rolling friction is smaller than sliding friction (d) Rollers remove gravity
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is that rollers reduce weight — the weight stays exactly the same. Rolling friction is much smaller than sliding friction, so less force is needed to move the box, so the answer is rolling friction is smaller than sliding friction.
Source: NCERT Class 8 Science (Friction)

90. An iron nail sinks in water but a large iron ship floats. This is because
(a) The ship is made of a special light iron (b) Water exerts no upthrust on nails (c) The ship's hollow shape makes its average density less than water (d) The nail is heavier than the ship
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is "special light iron" — ships use ordinary steel. The hollow hull encloses air, so the ship's average density becomes less than that of water and the buoyant force can support it, so the answer is the ship's hollow shape makes its average density less than water.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Science (Gravitation – Buoyancy)

91. The loudness of sound depends on
(a) Amplitude of vibration (b) Frequency of vibration (c) Speed of sound in air (d) Wavelength only
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is frequency, which actually determines pitch, not loudness — a classic confusion between two similar properties. Greater amplitude means greater energy and louder sound, so the answer is amplitude of vibration.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Science (Sound)

92. Sound cannot travel through
(a) Water (b) Steel (c) Air (d) Vacuum
Answer: (d)
Solution: Some students pick steel thinking sound needs air, but sound travels fastest in solids. Sound is a mechanical wave and needs a material medium, so it cannot travel through a vacuum, so the answer is vacuum.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Science (Sound)

93. An echo is heard distinctly only when the reflecting surface is at least about 17 m away (at ordinary temperature) because
(a) The human ear can distinguish two sounds only if they arrive at least 0.1 s apart (b) Sound becomes louder after 17 m (c) Sound travels slower beyond 17 m (d) Reflection starts only after 17 m
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is that sound becomes louder with distance, which is false — sound weakens with distance. The persistence of hearing is about 0.1 s, and at 344 m/s the round trip over 17 m takes about 0.1 s, so the answer is that the ear can distinguish two sounds only if they arrive at least 0.1 s apart.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Science (Sound)

94. A pressure cooker cooks food faster because
(a) It traps heat and keeps the flame high (b) Steam inside has no role in cooking (c) It reduces the boiling point of water (d) Increased pressure raises the boiling point of water inside
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is that it reduces the boiling point, which is what happens at high altitudes where cooking is slow. Inside a cooker, higher pressure raises water's boiling point above 100 °C, so food cooks faster at the higher temperature, so the answer is increased pressure raises the boiling point of water inside.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Physics (Thermodynamics concept) / PYQ-style

95. On a cold morning, a metallic chair feels colder than a wooden chair kept in the same room because
(a) The metal chair is actually at a lower temperature (b) Wood produces heat (c) Metal is a good conductor and draws heat quickly from the hand (d) Metal reflects body heat
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is that the metal is actually colder — both chairs are at the same room temperature. Metal conducts heat away from the skin much faster than wood, so it feels colder, so the answer is metal is a good conductor and draws heat quickly from the hand.
Source: NCERT Class 7 Science (Heat)

96. The handle of a cooking pan is made of plastic or wood because these materials are
(a) Good conductors of heat (b) Bad conductors (insulators) of heat (c) Good reflectors of heat (d) Cheaper than metal only
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is "good conductors", which would burn the hand — the exact opposite of the requirement. Plastic and wood are poor conductors, so heat from the pan does not reach the hand, so the answer is bad conductors (insulators) of heat.
Source: NCERT Class 7 Science (Heat)

97. A moving fan continues to rotate for some time after the switch is turned off because of
(a) Inertia of motion (b) Inertia of rest (c) Magnetic force in the coil (d) Stored electric current in the wire
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is "stored electric current", which does not exist — current stops the moment the circuit opens. The blades possess inertia of motion and keep rotating until friction and air resistance stop them, so the answer is inertia of motion.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Science (Force and Laws of Motion)

98. Which of the following correctly lists the colours of the visible spectrum in order of decreasing wavelength?
(a) Violet, Indigo, Blue, Green, Yellow, Orange, Red (b) Red, Orange, Yellow, Green, Blue, Indigo, Violet (c) Red, Yellow, Blue, Green, Orange, Violet, Indigo (d) Violet, Blue, Red, Green, Yellow, Orange, Indigo
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is the VIBGYOR order read straight from the acronym, which is increasing wavelength, not decreasing — a classic reversal trap. Red has the longest wavelength and violet the shortest, so decreasing order runs from red to violet, so the answer is Red, Orange, Yellow, Green, Blue, Indigo, Violet.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science (Human Eye and the Colourful World)

99. A 60 W bulb and a 100 W bulb are both connected to the same 220 V supply. Which bulb glows brighter and why?
(a) The 60 W bulb, because it has higher resistance (b) The 60 W bulb, because it saves energy (c) Both glow equally bright (d) The 100 W bulb, because it draws more power at the same voltage
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is that higher resistance means brighter — that logic works only in series circuits, not in parallel household supply. At the same voltage, the 100 W bulb consumes more power per second and converts more of it to light, so the answer is the 100 W bulb, because it draws more power at the same voltage.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science (Electricity)

100. A man standing between two parallel cliffs fires a gun. He hears the first echo after 2 s and the second echo after 4 s. If the speed of sound is 340 m/s, the distance between the two cliffs is
(a) 680 m (b) 1360 m (c) 340 m (d) 1020 m
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is 680 m, obtained by forgetting that echo time covers the round trip. Distances to the cliffs are 340 × 2/2 = 340 m and 340 × 4/2 = 680 m, and their sum is 340 + 680 = 1020 m, so the answer is 1020 m.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Science (Sound) / PYQ-style
