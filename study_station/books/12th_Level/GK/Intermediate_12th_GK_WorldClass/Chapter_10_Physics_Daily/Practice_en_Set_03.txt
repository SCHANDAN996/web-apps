51. A person standing on a weighing machine inside a lift finds his weight to be more than normal. The lift is
(a) moving up with uniform velocity (b) accelerating upward (c) accelerating downward (d) in free fall
Answer: (b)
Solution: Uniform velocity gives no change in apparent weight, so the tempting option of moving up with uniform velocity is wrong; only acceleration matters. Upward acceleration increases the normal reaction above mg, so the answer is accelerating upward.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Physics

52. While travelling in a bus, passengers are jerked forward when the bus suddenly stops. This is due to
(a) gravitational pull of the earth (b) inertia of rest (c) inertia of direction (d) inertia of motion
Answer: (d)
Solution: Inertia of rest is the tempting wrong choice, but it applies to bodies at rest, whereas the passengers were moving with the bus. Their bodies tend to continue in motion when the bus stops, so the answer is inertia of motion.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Science

53. A cricket player lowers his hands while catching a fast ball in order to
(a) increase the force of impact (b) increase the time of impact and reduce the force (c) decrease the time of impact (d) change the direction of the ball
Answer: (b)
Solution: The tempting wrong idea is that lowering the hands decreases the time of impact, but it actually does the opposite. Increasing the time over which the ball's momentum becomes zero reduces the force on the hands, so the answer is increase the time of impact and reduce the force.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Physics

54. It is easier to roll a stone up a sloping road than to lift it vertically upwards because
(a) work done in both is the same but the rate of doing work is less in rolling (b) work done in lifting the stone is more than in rolling it (c) work done in rolling is more than in lifting (d) work done in rolling a stone is zero
Answer: (a)
Solution: The tempting wrong option claims the work in rolling is less, but the work done against gravity equals mgh in both cases since the height gained is the same. Rolling is easier because a smaller force (mg sin θ) applied over a longer distance means a smaller rate of doing work, so the answer is work done in both is the same but the rate of doing work is less in rolling.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Physics

55. When a body is taken from the equator to the poles, its weight
(a) decreases (b) increases (c) remains the same (d) becomes zero
Answer: (b)
Solution: The tempting wrong choice is that weight remains the same, which would hold only if g were uniform everywhere. The value of g is maximum at the poles because the earth is flattened there and the rotational effect is absent, so the answer is increases.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Physics

56. An artificial satellite orbiting the earth does not fall down because the attraction of the earth
(a) provides the necessary centripetal force (b) is balanced by the attraction of the moon (c) is balanced by the viscous drag of the atmosphere (d) vanishes at such distances
Answer: (a)
Solution: The tempting wrong option says gravity vanishes at that distance, but gravity is very much present and is exactly what keeps the satellite in orbit. Earth's gravitational pull supplies the centripetal force for circular motion, so the answer is provides the necessary centripetal force.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Physics

57. A sudden fall in barometer reading indicates
(a) storm (b) rain (c) calm weather (d) dry weather
Answer: (a)
Solution: Rain is the tempting wrong answer, but rain is usually associated with a gradual fall in pressure. A sudden, sharp fall indicates a steep pressure gradient and an approaching storm, so the answer is storm.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Physics

58. The working principle of a washing machine is based on
(a) diffusion (b) dialysis (c) centrifugation (d) reverse osmosis
Answer: (c)
Solution: Reverse osmosis is the tempting wrong option, but it is used in water purifiers, not washing machines. The spinning drum throws water droplets outward through the holes by centrifugal action, so the answer is centrifugation.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Physics

59. A boat which has a speed of 5 km/h in still water crosses a river of width 1 km along the shortest possible path in 15 minutes. The velocity of the river water is
(a) 1 km/h (b) 4 km/h (c) 3 km/h (d) √41 km/h
Answer: (c)
Solution: The tempting wrong answer is 4 km/h, which is actually the resultant speed across the river, not the river's own speed. Resultant speed = 1 km / 0.25 h = 4 km/h, and river speed = √(5² − 4²) = 3 km/h, so the answer is 3 km/h.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Physics

60. A bomb is dropped from an aeroplane flying horizontally with uniform velocity. The path of the bomb as observed by a person on the ground is
(a) hyperbolic (b) straight vertical line (c) circular (d) parabolic
Answer: (d)
Solution: A straight vertical line is the tempting wrong answer, which is how the path appears to the pilot, not to a ground observer. For a person on the ground the bomb has both horizontal velocity and vertical fall, tracing a parabola, so the answer is parabolic.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Physics

61. The sky appears blue because of
(a) dispersion of light (b) refraction of light (c) reflection of light (d) scattering of light
Answer: (d)
Solution: Dispersion is the tempting wrong option, but dispersion splits white light into colours as in a prism or rainbow. The blue colour of the sky is due to Rayleigh scattering of shorter wavelengths by air molecules, so the answer is scattering of light.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science

62. The danger signals installed at the top of tall buildings are red in colour because red light
(a) is scattered the most (b) is absorbed the most by air (c) is scattered the least by fog or smoke (d) moves fastest in air
Answer: (c)
Solution: The tempting wrong option claims red is scattered the most, which is true of blue and violet light, not red. Red has the longest wavelength and is scattered the least, so it can be seen from far through fog, and the answer is is scattered the least by fog or smoke.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science

63. An endoscope used by doctors to examine the inside of a patient's body works on the principle of
(a) total internal reflection of light (b) scattering of light (c) refraction of light (d) dispersion of light
Answer: (a)
Solution: Simple refraction is the tempting wrong answer, but refraction alone cannot guide light around bends inside the body. Optical fibres in an endoscope transmit light by repeated total internal reflection, so the answer is total internal reflection of light.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Physics

64. A convex lens of glass is immersed in water. Compared to its power in air, its power in water will
(a) increase (b) decrease (c) remain the same (d) become zero
Answer: (b)
Solution: The tempting wrong option is that power remains the same, assuming the lens itself is unchanged, but focal length depends on the surrounding medium. The refractive index contrast between glass and water is smaller than between glass and air, so the lens converges less strongly and the answer is decrease.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Physics

65. A person cannot see objects clearly beyond 2 m. The defect of vision and the lens needed to correct it are
(a) astigmatism, cylindrical lens (b) hypermetropia, convex lens (c) myopia, convex lens (d) myopia, concave lens
Answer: (d)
Solution: The tempting wrong pair is myopia with a convex lens, but a convex lens would worsen the defect by converging the rays even more. Inability to see distant objects is myopia, corrected by a concave (diverging) lens, so the answer is myopia, concave lens.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science

66. The phenomenon of mirage in deserts is caused by
(a) reflection of light (b) total internal reflection of light (c) interference of light (d) diffraction of light
Answer: (b)
Solution: Plain reflection is the tempting wrong option, but a mirror-like reflection is not what happens in hot air layers. Hot air near the ground is optically rarer, and light from the sky bends upward through successive layers until total internal reflection occurs, so the answer is total internal reflection of light.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Physics

67. Sound waves in air are
(a) longitudinal (b) polarised (c) electromagnetic (d) transverse
Answer: (a)
Solution: Transverse is the tempting wrong answer because many textbook wave examples are transverse, but air cannot sustain shear vibrations. Sound travels in air as compressions and rarefactions along the direction of propagation, so the answer is longitudinal.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Science

68. The pitch of a sound depends on its
(a) amplitude (b) wavelength only (c) speed (d) frequency
Answer: (d)
Solution: Amplitude is the tempting wrong option, but amplitude determines loudness, not pitch. Pitch is the sensation of shrillness and depends on the frequency of vibration, so the answer is frequency.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Science

69. Bats can fly in the dark because they
(a) have very sharp eyesight (b) detect heat radiations from obstacles (c) emit ultrasonic waves and detect their echoes (d) emit infrasonic waves
Answer: (c)
Solution: Sharp eyesight is the tempting wrong option, but bats have poor vision and darkness would defeat eyesight anyway. Bats produce ultrasonic squeaks and locate obstacles from the returning echoes, so the answer is emit ultrasonic waves and detect their echoes.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Science

70. An electric bulb is filled with an inert gas like argon mainly to
(a) increase the brightness of the filament (b) reduce the resistance of the filament (c) prevent evaporation of the tungsten filament (d) conduct heat away quickly
Answer: (c)
Solution: Increasing brightness is the tempting wrong answer, but the gas does not make the filament hotter or brighter. Argon, being inert, prevents oxidation and reduces evaporation of the hot tungsten filament, so the answer is prevent evaporation of the tungsten filament.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science

71. The fuse wire used in domestic circuits is made of an alloy of
(a) aluminium and copper (b) copper and zinc (c) tin and lead (d) nickel and chromium
Answer: (c)
Solution: Nickel and chromium is the tempting wrong option, but that alloy (nichrome) has a high melting point and is used in heating elements, the opposite of what a fuse needs. A fuse wire must have a low melting point, so the answer is tin and lead.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science

72. In a three-pin plug, the earth pin is made longer and thicker so that
(a) the appliance is earthed before the live connection is made (b) it fits tightly in the socket (c) it can carry more current continuously (d) it does not heat up
Answer: (a)
Solution: Carrying more current continuously is the tempting wrong answer, but the earth wire carries current only during a fault. The longer earth pin connects the appliance body to earth before the live pin makes contact, so the answer is the appliance is earthed before the live connection is made.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science

73. When a current-carrying conductor is placed in a magnetic field, it experiences a force. The direction of this force is given by
(a) Fleming's right-hand rule (b) Fleming's left-hand rule (c) right-hand thumb rule (d) Lenz's law
Answer: (b)
Solution: Fleming's right-hand rule is the tempting wrong option, but that rule gives the direction of induced current in a generator, not the force on a conductor. The force on a current-carrying conductor in a magnetic field is found by Fleming's left-hand rule, so the answer is Fleming's left-hand rule.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science

74. A transformer works on the principle of
(a) mutual induction (b) self-induction (c) heating effect of current (d) magnetic effect of current
Answer: (a)
Solution: Self-induction is the tempting wrong option, but self-induction involves a single coil opposing changes in its own current. A transformer transfers energy between two coils through a changing magnetic flux linking both, so the answer is mutual induction.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Physics

75. A transformer cannot work on direct current (DC) because
(a) DC has very high voltage (b) the core gets permanently magnetised (c) DC heats the coils excessively (d) DC produces no changing magnetic flux
Answer: (d)
Solution: Excessive heating is the tempting wrong answer, but heating is a possible consequence, not the fundamental reason. Mutual induction requires a changing magnetic flux, which steady DC cannot produce in the secondary coil, so the answer is DC produces no changing magnetic flux.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Physics
