101. A person standing on a weighing machine inside a lift finds his weight to be more than normal. The lift is:
(a) moving up with uniform velocity (b) in free fall (c) accelerating downward (d) accelerating upward
Answer: (d)
Solution: Uniform velocity gives no change in apparent weight, so option (a) is the tempting trap. Apparent weight increases only when the lift accelerates upward, because the normal reaction becomes m(g + a). In free fall the apparent weight is zero, so the answer is accelerating upward.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Physics

102. When a bus suddenly starts moving, passengers standing inside tend to fall backward. This is explained by:
(a) Newton's second law of motion (b) inertia of rest (c) Newton's third law of motion (d) inertia of direction
Answer: (b)
Solution: Many students pick the third law, but no action-reaction pair is involved here. The lower body moves with the bus while the upper body tends to remain at rest due to inertia of rest, so the answer is inertia of rest.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Physics

103. It is easier to roll a heavy drum than to slide it along the ground because:
(a) the drum has less weight while rolling (b) rolling friction is more than sliding friction (c) rolling friction is less than sliding friction (d) rolling reduces the gravitational force
Answer: (c)
Solution: Weight and gravity do not change with the mode of motion, which rules out the last two options. Rolling friction is always smaller than sliding friction for the same surfaces, so the answer is rolling friction is less than sliding friction.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Physics

104. A pressure cooker cooks food faster mainly because:
(a) the boiling point of water decreases under high pressure (b) the boiling point of water increases under high pressure (c) steam absorbs heat from the food (d) the metal of the cooker conducts heat faster
Answer: (b)
Solution: Option (a) is the classic trap — pressure lowers the boiling point only when pressure decreases, as on mountains. Inside a cooker, pressure rises, raising the boiling point above 100°C so food cooks faster, so the answer is the boiling point of water increases under high pressure.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Physics

105. The sky appears blue because:
(a) the atmosphere absorbs blue light (b) blue light has the longest wavelength (c) blue light is scattered the most by air molecules (d) the sky reflects the colour of the oceans
Answer: (c)
Solution: Option (b) is wrong because blue light has a short wavelength, and short wavelengths are scattered most strongly by air molecules (Rayleigh scattering). This scattered blue light reaches our eyes from all directions, so the answer is blue light is scattered the most by air molecules.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Physics

106. The sun appears reddish at sunrise and sunset because:
(a) the sun emits more red light at those times (b) light travels a longer path through the atmosphere and blue light is scattered away (c) red light is scattered the most (d) the atmosphere absorbs red light at noon
Answer: (b)
Solution: Red light is actually scattered the least, so option (c) is the trap. Near the horizon, sunlight passes through a thicker layer of atmosphere; most blue light is scattered away, leaving red, so the answer is light travels a longer path through the atmosphere and blue light is scattered away.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Physics

107. A diamond sparkles brilliantly because of:
(a) total internal reflection of light (b) dispersion only (c) refraction at a single surface (d) absorption of all colours
Answer: (a)
Solution: Dispersion adds colour but is not the main reason for brilliance, making option (b) a partial trap. Diamond's high refractive index gives a very small critical angle (about 24°), so light undergoes repeated total internal reflection, so the answer is total internal reflection of light.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Physics

108. An endoscope, used by doctors to see inside the body, works on the principle of:
(a) interference of light (b) diffraction of light (c) total internal reflection (d) polarisation of light
Answer: (c)
Solution: An endoscope uses optical fibres in which light is trapped by repeated total internal reflection along the fibre. Interference and diffraction are wave phenomena not used here, so the answer is total internal reflection.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Physics

109. When a person suffering from myopia (short-sightedness) is corrected, the lens used is:
(a) convex lens (b) concave lens (c) cylindrical lens (d) bifocal lens
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is the convex lens, which corrects hypermetropia (long-sightedness), not myopia. In myopia the image forms in front of the retina, and a diverging concave lens shifts it back onto the retina, so the answer is concave lens.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science

110. The twinkling of stars is caused by:
(a) reflection of starlight by clouds (b) emission of varying light by stars (c) atmospheric refraction of starlight (d) total internal reflection in the atmosphere
Answer: (c)
Solution: Stars do not actually vary their brightness, so option (b) is wrong. Continuous changes in atmospheric density refract starlight unevenly, making stars appear to twinkle, so the answer is atmospheric refraction of starlight.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science

111. Planets do not twinkle because:
(a) they are very far away (b) they emit their own light (c) they reflect sunlight (d) they appear as extended sources and the variations average out
Answer: (d)
Solution: Planets are actually much closer than stars, so option (a) is the trap. Being closer, they appear as small discs (extended sources); the twinkling effects from different points cancel out, so the answer is they appear as extended sources and the variations average out.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science

112. A piece of ice floats on water because:
(a) ice is heavier than water (b) the density of ice is less than that of water (c) water exerts no buoyant force on ice (d) ice has no weight
Answer: (b)
Solution: Water exerts buoyant force on every immersed object, so option (c) is false. Ice floats because its density (about 0.92 g/cm³) is less than that of water, so the answer is the density of ice is less than that of water.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Science

113. The handle of a pressure cooker is made of plastic or ebonite because these materials are:
(a) bad conductors of heat (b) good conductors of heat (c) good conductors of electricity (d) lighter than metals
Answer: (a)
Solution: Lightness is not the reason, so option (d) is the trap. Plastic and ebonite are poor conductors of heat, so the handle stays cool while the metal body becomes hot, so the answer is bad conductors of heat.
Source: NCERT Class 7 Science

114. Woollen clothes keep us warm in winter because:
(a) wool produces heat (b) wool is a good conductor of heat (c) air trapped in wool fibres is a bad conductor of heat (d) wool absorbs sunlight
Answer: (c)
Solution: No material produces heat by itself, so option (a) is wrong. The air trapped between woollen fibres is a poor conductor and prevents body heat from escaping, so the answer is air trapped in wool fibres is a bad conductor of heat.
Source: NCERT Class 7 Science

115. A metal spoon feels colder than a wooden spoon at the same room temperature because:
(a) metal conducts heat away from the hand faster (b) the metal spoon is actually at a lower temperature (c) wood produces heat (d) metal absorbs cold from the surroundings
Answer: (a)
Solution: Both spoons are at the same room temperature, so option (b) is the trap. Metal is a good conductor and quickly carries heat away from the hand, making it feel colder, so the answer is metal conducts heat away from the hand faster.
Source: NCERT Class 7 Science

116. The audible frequency range for a normal human ear is:
(a) 200 Hz to 20,000 Hz (b) 20 Hz to 2,000 Hz (c) 20 Hz to 20,000 Hz (d) below 20 Hz
Answer: (c)
Solution: Frequencies below 20 Hz are infrasonic and inaudible, so option (d) is wrong. The normal human hearing range extends from 20 Hz to 20,000 Hz (20 kHz), so the answer is 20 Hz to 20,000 Hz.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Science

117. Sound waves cannot travel through:
(a) water (b) vacuum (c) steel (d) air
Answer: (b)
Solution: Sound travels fastest in solids like steel, so option (c) is clearly wrong. Sound is a mechanical wave needing a material medium, so it cannot travel through vacuum, so the answer is vacuum.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Science

118. An echo is heard distinctly only if the reflecting surface is at a minimum distance of about:
(a) 17.2 m (b) 34.4 m (c) 10 m (d) 1 m
Answer: (a)
Solution: Option (b) is the trap — 34.4 m is the distance sound travels in 0.1 s, but the sound must go to the surface and return, so the surface needs to be only half that distance away. Hence the minimum distance is about 17.2 m, so the answer is 17.2 m.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Science

119. The pitch of a sound depends on its:
(a) amplitude (b) speed (c) frequency (d) wavelength in vacuum only
Answer: (c)
Solution: Amplitude determines loudness, not pitch, so option (a) is the trap. Higher frequency means a shriller (higher-pitched) sound, so the answer is frequency.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Science

120. A bomb explodes on the Moon. An astronaut standing on the Moon will:
(a) not hear the explosion at all (b) hear a faint explosion (c) hear a loud explosion (d) hear the explosion after a long delay
Answer: (a)
Solution: Distance or delay is irrelevant here, so option (d) is the trap. The Moon has no atmosphere, and sound needs a medium, so no sound reaches the astronaut, so the answer is not hear the explosion at all.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Science

121. Consider the following statements about mirage:
1. It is caused by total internal reflection of light.
2. It is commonly seen in deserts on hot days.
3. It occurs because hot air near the ground is denser than cool air above.
Which of the statements given above is/are correct?
(a) 1, 2 and 3 (b) 2 and 3 only (c) 1 and 3 only (d) 1 and 2 only
Answer: (d)
Solution: Statement 3 is the trap — hot air near the ground is actually less dense (rarer) than the cooler air above, which is what creates the conditions for total internal reflection. Statements 1 and 2 are correct, so the answer is 1 and 2 only.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Physics

122. A convex lens of focal length 20 cm forms a real, inverted image of the same size as the object. The object distance is:
(a) 40 cm (b) 20 cm (c) 10 cm (d) 60 cm
Answer: (a)
Solution: Placing the object at the focus (20 cm) forms the image at infinity, so option (b) is the trap. A real, same-sized image is formed only when the object is at 2F, i.e., 40 cm, so the answer is 40 cm.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science

123. Two resistors of 6 Ω and 3 Ω are connected in parallel across a 12 V battery. The total current drawn from the battery is:
(a) 2 A (b) 4 A (c) 1.33 A (d) 6 A
Answer: (d)
Solution: Option (c) is the trap — it comes from wrongly adding the resistances in series and dividing. In parallel, currents add: 12/6 + 12/3 = 2 + 4 = 6 A, so the answer is 6 A.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science

124. A ball is thrown vertically upward with a velocity of 20 m/s. Taking g = 10 m/s², the maximum height reached is:
(a) 20 m (b) 10 m (c) 40 m (d) 2 m
Answer: (a)
Solution: Using v² = u² − 2gh with final velocity zero: 0 = 400 − 20h, giving h = 20 m. Option (c) comes from forgetting the factor of 2 in the formula, so the answer is 20 m.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Science

125. Consider the following statements:
1. Gravitational force between two bodies depends on the medium between them.
2. The value of g is maximum at the poles of the Earth.
3. The value of g decreases as we go above the Earth's surface.
Which of the statements given above is/are correct?
(a) 1 and 2 only (b) 2 and 3 only (c) 1 only (d) 1, 2 and 3
Answer: (b)
Solution: Statement 1 is the trap — gravitational force, unlike electrostatic force, does not depend on the medium between the bodies. The value of g is maximum at the poles and decreases with height, so statements 2 and 3 are correct, so the answer is 2 and 3 only.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Physics
