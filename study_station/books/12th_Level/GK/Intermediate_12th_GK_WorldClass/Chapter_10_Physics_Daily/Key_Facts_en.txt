KEY FACTS & FIGURES — Physics in Everyday Life

## 1. High-Yield Facts

| Fact | Description | Examiner's Trap | Trap Type |
|---|---|---|---|
| Blue colour of the sky | Sunlight is scattered by air molecules; shorter wavelengths (blue) scatter more than longer ones (Rayleigh scattering) | "Violet scatters even more than blue, so the sky should look violet" — violet does scatter more, but sunlight contains less violet and our eyes are less sensitive to it | Conceptual misunderstanding |
| Red colour of sunrise/sunset | At the horizon, light travels a longer path through the atmosphere; most blue is scattered away, leaving red/orange | Confusing this with refraction or dispersion | Similar-phenomena confusion |
| Twinkling of stars | Atmospheric refraction — starlight passes through layers of air of varying density, so its apparent position keeps shifting | "Planets also twinkle" — planets are extended sources (closer), so the twinkling averages out; stars are point sources | Nearly-correct statement |
| Advanced sunrise / delayed sunset | Atmospheric refraction makes the Sun visible about 2 minutes before actual sunrise and 2 minutes after actual sunset | Attributing this to scattering instead of refraction | Similar-phenomena confusion |
| Rainbow | Dispersion of sunlight by water droplets, with internal reflection inside each drop; observer must stand with the Sun behind | "Rainbow is caused by scattering" — it is dispersion + total internal reflection | Conceptual misunderstanding |
| Mirage | Total internal reflection of light in hot air layers near the ground (hot air is optically rarer) | Confusing mirage (inferior image, hot surfaces) with looming (superior image, cold surfaces/sea) | Similar-phenomena confusion |
| Convex mirror in vehicles | Used as rear-view mirror — always gives erect, diminished image and a wider field of view | "Concave mirror is used as rear-view mirror" — concave mirrors are used in headlights and shaving mirrors, not rear-view | Reversed application |
| Concave mirror in headlights | Bulb placed at the focus gives a parallel beam of light | Swapping the uses of concave and convex mirrors | Reversed application |
| Fuse wire | Low melting point, high resistance alloy (tin-lead); connected in series with the live wire | "Fuse is connected in the neutral wire" — it must be in the live wire | Nearly-correct statement |
| Electric bulb glow | Filament (tungsten) heats up due to the heating effect of current (Joule heating); tungsten has a very high melting point (~3422 °C) | "Bulb works on magnetic effect of current" — that applies to electric bells/motors | Similar-phenomena confusion |
| Pressure cooker | Pressure inside rises, so the boiling point of water increases above 100 °C; food cooks faster | "Pressure cooker lowers the boiling point" — boiling point rises with pressure | Reversed relationship |
| Water pipes burst in cold regions | Water expands on freezing below 4 °C (anomalous expansion); ice has lower density than water | Assuming water contracts continuously on cooling like other liquids | Conceptual misunderstanding |
| Swimming / floating | Buoyant force (Archimedes' principle): a body floats when its average density is less than the liquid's | "A ship floats because it is light" — a steel ship floats because its hollow shape makes average density less than water | Conceptual misunderstanding |
| Hydraulic brakes / lift | Pascal's law — pressure applied to an enclosed fluid is transmitted equally in all directions | Confusing Pascal's law with Bernoulli's principle (aeroplane lift, sprayers) | Similar-phenomena confusion |
| Aeroplane wing lift | Bernoulli's principle — faster airflow over the curved upper surface lowers pressure above the wing | Attributing lift to Archimedes' principle | Similar-phenomena confusion |
| Inertia in daily life | Passengers jerk forward when a bus stops suddenly (inertia of motion); jerk backward when it starts (inertia of rest) | Swapping the two situations | Reversed application |
| Momentum in cricket | A fielder pulls hands back while catching to increase the time of impact and reduce the force (impulse = change in momentum) | "Pulling hands back reduces momentum" — momentum change is fixed; the force is reduced | Conceptual misunderstanding |
| Walking on road | Newton's third law — we push the ground backward; the ground pushes us forward (friction) | "Walking is possible without friction" — friction is essential | Nearly-correct statement |
| Sound needs a medium | Sound is a mechanical wave; it cannot travel through vacuum; light can | "Sound travels faster in vacuum" — sound cannot travel in vacuum at all | Nearly-correct statement |
| Speed of sound vs light | Lightning is seen before thunder is heard because light (~3 × 10⁸ m/s) travels much faster than sound (~332 m/s in air) | Using the time gap to estimate distance: roughly 1 km per 3 seconds | Conceptual application |
| Echo | Reflected sound heard distinctly only if it reaches the ear at least ~0.1 s after the original; minimum distance of reflecting surface ≈ 17 m | "Echo is possible at any distance" | Nearly-correct statement |
| SONAR | Uses ultrasonic waves (frequency > 20,000 Hz) to measure sea depth and detect objects underwater | Confusing ultrasound with infrasound (< 20 Hz) | Similar-phenomena confusion |
| Doppler effect | Apparent change in frequency of sound due to relative motion between source and observer (approaching siren sounds higher-pitched) | Applying it only to sound — it applies to light too (red shift) | Conceptual misunderstanding |
| Capillary action | Water rises in a narrow tube due to surface tension and adhesion; explains ink in blotting paper, oil in a wick | Confusing with osmosis or diffusion | Similar-phenomena confusion |
| Hot tea cools faster in a saucer | Larger surface area increases the rate of evaporation, and evaporation causes cooling | "Evaporation causes heating" — evaporation always causes cooling | Reversed relationship |
| Black body / colour and heat | Black surfaces are good absorbers and good emitters of heat; white/shiny surfaces are poor absorbers | "Black absorbs but does not emit well" — good absorbers are also good emitters | Nearly-correct statement |
| Sea breeze and land breeze | Sea breeze blows from sea to land during the day; land breeze from land to sea at night (convection currents) | Swapping day and night directions | Reversed application |
| Diamond shines | Very high refractive index (~2.42) gives a small critical angle (~24°), so light undergoes repeated total internal reflection | Attributing the sparkle to dispersion alone | Conceptual misunderstanding |
| Optical fibre | Works on total internal reflection; used in communication and endoscopy | "Optical fibre works on refraction" — TIR is the core principle | Nearly-correct statement |
| Myopia and hypermetropia | Myopia (short-sightedness) corrected by concave lens; hypermetropia (long-sightedness) by convex lens | Swapping the two lenses | Reversed application |

## 2. Numbers Worth Memorising

- Speed of light in vacuum: 3 × 10⁸ m/s
- Speed of sound in air (at ~0 °C): ~332 m/s (≈ 343 m/s at 20 °C)
- Audible range for humans: 20 Hz to 20,000 Hz
- Acceleration due to gravity (g): 9.8 m/s²
- Density of water: 1000 kg/m³ (maximum at 4 °C)
- 1 kilowatt-hour (1 unit) = 3.6 × 10⁶ joules
- Domestic supply voltage in India: 220 V, 50 Hz AC
- Refractive index: water ≈ 1.33, glass ≈ 1.5, diamond ≈ 2.42
- Minimum distance for a distinct echo: ≈ 17 m

## 3. Common Trap Patterns in Exams

- **Reversed pairs**: concave vs convex mirror/lens uses; sea breeze vs land breeze; myopia vs hypermetropia correction.
- **Similar-phenomena confusion**: scattering vs refraction vs dispersion vs total internal reflection — the four most-mixed optics concepts.
- **Nearly-correct statements**: "sound travels in vacuum", "fuse in neutral wire", "planets twinkle like stars".
- **Unit traps**: kWh is a unit of energy, not power; Hz measures frequency, not loudness (decibel measures loudness).

Source: NCERT Class 9–10 Science; NCERT Class 11–12 Physics (concepts only).
