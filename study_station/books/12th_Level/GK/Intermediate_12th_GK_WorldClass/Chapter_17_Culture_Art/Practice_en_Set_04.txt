Questions 76–100: Medium

76. The famous 'Dashavatara' panel carved in relief is found in which cave of Ellora?
(a) Cave 10 (b) Cave 15 (c) Cave 16 (d) Cave 21
Answer: (b)
Solution: Cave 16 (Kailasa temple) is the tempting wrong choice because it is the most famous Ellora cave, but the Dashavatara cave with the large sculpted panels of the ten incarnations of Vishnu is Cave 15, so the answer is Cave 15.
Source: NCERT Class 11 An Introduction to Indian Art

77. Which of the following pairs of classical dance and its traditional region is correctly matched?
(a) Mohiniyattam – Tamil Nadu (b) Sattriya – West Bengal (c) Kuchipudi – Odisha (d) Kathakali – Kerala
Answer: (d)
Solution: Mohiniyattam is a trap because it sounds South Indian like Tamil Nadu, but it belongs to Kerala; Kathakali is correctly paired with Kerala, so the answer is Kathakali – Kerala.
Source: NCERT Class 11 An Introduction to Indian Art

78. The 'Gandhara school of art' primarily flourished under the patronage of which ruler?
(a) Kanishka (b) Ashoka (c) Chandragupta II (d) Harshavardhana
Answer: (a)
Solution: Ashoka is a tempting wrong option because of his association with early Buddhist art, but the Gandhara school, with its Greco-Buddhist style, flourished under the Kushana ruler Kanishka, so the answer is Kanishka.
Source: NCERT Class 11 An Introduction to Indian Art

79. Which of the following temples is built in the Dravida (South Indian) style of architecture?
(a) Kandariya Mahadeva Temple, Khajuraho (b) Sun Temple, Konark (c) Lingaraja Temple, Bhubaneswar (d) Brihadeeswara Temple, Thanjavur
Answer: (d)
Solution: Konark and Lingaraja are traps because they are famous temples, but they follow the Kalinga (Nagara-variant) style of Odisha; the Brihadeeswara Temple built by Rajaraja I is a classic Dravida temple, so the answer is Brihadeeswara Temple, Thanjavur.
Source: NCERT Class 11 An Introduction to Indian Art

80. The 'Sattriya' dance form was introduced in the 15th century by which saint-reformer?
(a) Shankaradeva (b) Chaitanya Mahaprabhu (c) Kabir (d) Tulsidas
Answer: (a)
Solution: Chaitanya Mahaprabhu is a trap because he was a contemporary Vaishnavite saint, but he belonged to Bengal; Sattriya was introduced by the Assamese saint Shankaradeva as part of the neo-Vaishnavite movement, so the answer is Shankaradeva.
Source: NCERT Class 11 An Introduction to Indian Art

81. The Ajanta paintings mainly depict scenes from which texts?
(a) Puranas (b) Arthashastra (c) Vedas (d) Jataka tales
Answer: (d)
Solution: The Puranas are a tempting wrong option because many later temple sculptures depict Puranic stories, but the Ajanta murals chiefly illustrate the Jataka tales — stories of the Buddha's previous births — so the answer is Jataka tales.
Source: NCERT Class 11 An Introduction to Indian Art

82. Which Mughal emperor is associated with the construction of the Red Fort in Delhi?
(a) Akbar (b) Jahangir (c) Shah Jahan (d) Aurangzeb
Answer: (c)
Solution: Akbar is a trap because he built the Agra Fort and Fatehpur Sikri, but the Red Fort (Lal Qila) in Delhi was commissioned by Shah Jahan when he shifted his capital to Shahjahanabad, so the answer is Shah Jahan.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Themes in Indian History

83. The 'Dhrupad' style of Hindustani classical music was patronised at the court of which ruler?
(a) Krishnadevaraya (b) Babur (c) Alauddin Khilji (d) Akbar
Answer: (d)
Solution: Krishnadevaraya is a trap because he patronised Carnatic music in the South, but Dhrupad reached its peak at Akbar's court through Tansen, who was trained in the Dhrupad tradition, so the answer is Akbar.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Themes in Indian History

84. The famous 'Nataraja' bronze sculptures, depicting Shiva as the cosmic dancer, are associated with which dynasty?
(a) Pallavas (b) Pandyas (c) Cholas (d) Cheras
Answer: (c)
Solution: The Pallavas are a tempting wrong option because they pioneered South Indian temple art, but the exquisite Nataraja bronze images made by the lost-wax (cire perdue) technique are the hallmark of Chola craftsmanship, so the answer is Cholas.
Source: NCERT Class 11 An Introduction to Indian Art

85. Which of the following is the correct chronological order of these architectural monuments?
(a) Taj Mahal – Sanchi Stupa – Qutub Minar (b) Qutub Minar – Sanchi Stupa – Taj Mahal (c) Sanchi Stupa – Qutub Minar – Taj Mahal (d) Sanchi Stupa – Taj Mahal – Qutub Minar
Answer: (c)
Solution: This is a chronological-confusion trap: the Sanchi Stupa (Mauryan–Sunga period, 3rd century BCE onwards) is the oldest, the Qutub Minar (early 13th century CE) comes next, and the Taj Mahal (17th century CE) is the latest, so the answer is Sanchi Stupa – Qutub Minar – Taj Mahal.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Themes in Indian History

86. The 'Kuchipudi' dance-drama tradition originated in a village of which present-day state?
(a) Andhra Pradesh (b) Karnataka (c) Tamil Nadu (d) Kerala
Answer: (a)
Solution: Tamil Nadu is a trap because Bharatanatyam comes from there and students often mix up South Indian dance origins, but Kuchipudi takes its name from a village in the Krishna district of Andhra Pradesh, so the answer is Andhra Pradesh.
Source: NCERT Class 11 An Introduction to Indian Art

87. The 'Mathura school of art' is distinguished from the Gandhara school mainly because it:
(a) developed indigenously using spotted red sandstone (b) used only white marble (c) depicted only Jain themes (d) flourished only after the Gupta period
Answer: (a)
Solution: The Gandhara school used bluish-grey schist and showed Greek influence, while the Mathura school developed indigenously using the local spotted red sandstone and depicted Buddhist, Jain and Brahmanical themes, so the answer is that it developed indigenously using spotted red sandstone.
Source: NCERT Class 11 An Introduction to Indian Art

88. Which of the following festivals is celebrated as the 'chariot festival' (Rath Yatra) of Lord Jagannath?
(a) Puri, Odisha (b) Madurai, Tamil Nadu (c) Varanasi, Uttar Pradesh (d) Ujjain, Madhya Pradesh
Answer: (a)
Solution: Madurai is a trap because of the grand Meenakshi temple festivals, but the world-famous Rath Yatra of Lord Jagannath, with the three giant chariots, takes place at Puri in Odisha, so the answer is Puri, Odisha.
Source: NCERT Class 11 An Introduction to Indian Art

89. The 'Khajuraho group of temples' was built mainly by rulers of which dynasty?
(a) Paramaras (b) Chandelas (c) Chalukyas (d) Rashtrakutas
Answer: (b)
Solution: The Rashtrakutas are a tempting wrong option because they built the Kailasa temple at Ellora, but the Khajuraho temples in Madhya Pradesh were built by the Chandela rulers between the 10th and 11th centuries, so the answer is Chandelas.
Source: NCERT Class 11 An Introduction to Indian Art

90. Which classical music form is associated with the 'Kirana', 'Gwalior' and 'Patiala' traditions?
(a) Carnatic music (b) Rabindra Sangeet (c) Hindustani gharanas (d) Baul singing
Answer: (c)
Solution: Carnatic music is a trap because it also has classical ragas, but Kirana, Gwalior and Patiala are gharanas (stylistic schools) of Hindustani classical music of North India, so the answer is Hindustani gharanas.
Source: NCERT Class 11 An Introduction to Indian Art

91. The 'Elephanta Caves', famous for the Trimurti sculpture of Shiva, are located near which city?
(a) Aurangabad (b) Nashik (c) Mumbai (d) Pune
Answer: (c)
Solution: Aurangabad is a trap because Ajanta and Ellora are near it, but the Elephanta Caves are on an island in Mumbai harbour, a short ferry ride from the Gateway of India, so the answer is Mumbai.
Source: NCERT Class 11 An Introduction to Indian Art

92. Which of the following statements about Bharatanatyam is correct?
(a) It originated in Kerala as a martial dance (b) It is performed only by male dancers (c) It evolved from the temple dance tradition of Tamil Nadu (d) It uses no hand gestures (mudras)
Answer: (c)
Solution: The Kerala origin is an almost-correct trap because Mohiniyattam and Kathakali come from Kerala, but Bharatanatyam evolved from the Sadir temple-dance tradition of Tamil Nadu and was codified in the 20th century, so the answer is that it evolved from the temple dance tradition of Tamil Nadu.
Source: NCERT Class 11 An Introduction to Indian Art

93. The 'Charminar' of Hyderabad was built in 1591 by:
(a) Muhammad Quli Qutb Shah (b) Akbar (c) Aurangzeb (d) Tipu Sultan
Answer: (a)
Solution: Tipu Sultan is a trap because he was a famous Deccan ruler, but the Charminar was built by Muhammad Quli Qutb Shah, the fifth ruler of the Qutb Shahi dynasty of Golconda, so the answer is Muhammad Quli Qutb Shah.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Themes in Indian History

94. The 'Warli' tribal painting tradition belongs to which state?
(a) Madhya Pradesh (b) Chhattisgarh (c) Jharkhand (d) Maharashtra
Answer: (d)
Solution: Madhya Pradesh is a tempting wrong option because of its large tribal population and Gond art, but Warli painting is the tradition of the Warli tribe of the north Sahyadri region of Maharashtra, so the answer is Maharashtra.
Source: NCERT Class 11 An Introduction to Indian Art

95. Which of the following pairs of folk dance and state is NOT correctly matched?
(a) Bhangra – Punjab (b) Ghoomar – Haryana (c) Bihu – Assam (d) Garba – Gujarat
Answer: (b)
Solution: Bhangra–Punjab, Garba–Gujarat and Bihu–Assam are all correct pairs; the trap is Ghoomar, which is the famous folk dance of Rajasthan, not Haryana, so the answer is Ghoomar – Haryana.
Source: NCERT Class 11 An Introduction to Indian Art

96. The 'Mahabalipuram' (Mamallapuram) shore temples and rock-cut rathas were created under which dynasty?
(a) Cholas (b) Vijayanagara rulers (c) Pandyas (d) Pallavas
Answer: (d)
Solution: The Cholas are a trap because they dominated Tamil Nadu later, but the Pancha Rathas and the Shore Temple at Mahabalipuram were created under the Pallava kings Narasimhavarman I and II in the 7th–8th centuries, so the answer is Pallavas.
Source: NCERT Class 11 An Introduction to Indian Art

97. 'Madhubani' (Mithila) painting, characterised by intricate line work and natural dyes, is practised mainly in which region?
(a) Bundelkhand region of Uttar Pradesh (b) Mithila region of Bihar (c) Malwa region of Madhya Pradesh (d) Kutch region of Gujarat
Answer: (b)
Solution: Kutch is a trap because Gujarat has rich folk-art traditions, but Madhubani painting is the traditional art of women in the Mithila region of Bihar, so the answer is Mithila region of Bihar.
Source: NCERT Class 11 An Introduction to Indian Art

98. The 'Sangeet Natak Akademi', India's national academy for music, dance and drama, was established in which year?
(a) 1952 (b) 1947 (c) 1961 (d) 1972
Answer: (a)
Solution: 1947 is a tempting wrong option because many national institutions are associated with independence, but the Sangeet Natak Akademi was set up by the Government of India in 1952 as the first national academy of the arts, so the answer is 1952.
Source: Sangeet Natak Akademi (official)

99. Which of the following statements about the Konark Sun Temple is correct?
(a) It was built by Akbar as a victory monument (b) It is designed as a colossal chariot of the Sun God with twelve pairs of wheels (c) It is located on the banks of the Ganga (d) It was built entirely of white marble
Answer: (b)
Solution: The white-marble claim is an almost-correct trap borrowed from the Taj Mahal; the Konark temple, built by Narasimhadeva I of the Eastern Ganga dynasty in the 13th century, is conceived as the Sun God's chariot with twelve pairs of carved wheels and seven horses, so the answer is that it is designed as a colossal chariot of the Sun God with twelve pairs of wheels.
Source: NCERT Class 11 An Introduction to Indian Art

100. The 'Kathak' dance form traces its origins to:
(a) temple dancers of Kerala (b) wandering storytellers (Kathakars) of North India (c) royal courts of the Deccan (d) tribal rituals of the Northeast
Answer: (b)
Solution: The Deccan courts are a trap because Kathak later flourished in Mughal and Nawabi courts, but its origin lies with the Kathakars — wandering storytellers of North India who narrated epics through dance and mime, so the answer is wandering storytellers (Kathakars) of North India.
Source: NCERT Class 11 An Introduction to Indian Art
