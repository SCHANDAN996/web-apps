Questions 26–50: Medium

26. The famous 'Dashavatara' panel depicting ten incarnations of Vishnu is found in which temple?
(a) Kandariya Mahadeva Temple, Khajuraho (b) Shore Temple, Mamallapuram (c) Deogarh Temple, Uttar Pradesh (d) Lingaraja Temple, Bhubaneswar
Answer: (c)
Solution: The tempting wrong choice is Khajuraho, which is famous for sculpture but belongs to the Chandela period and is not the Dashavatara site. The Gupta-period Deogarh Temple (Lalitpur district, Uttar Pradesh) is renowned for its Dashavatara relief panels, so the answer is Deogarh Temple, Uttar Pradesh.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Fine Arts (An Introduction to Indian Art)

27. Which of the following classical dance forms originated in the villages of Andhra Pradesh and is named after a village?
(a) Kuchipudi (b) Odissi (c) Kathakali (d) Manipuri
Answer: (a)
Solution: Odissi tempts because it is also temple-based, but it comes from Odisha. Kuchipudi is named after the village Kuchipudi in Krishna district of Andhra Pradesh, where it developed as a dance-drama tradition, so the answer is Kuchipudi.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Fine Arts / Sangeet Natak Akademi

28. The 'Gandhara school of art' shows the strongest influence of which foreign artistic tradition?
(a) Persian (b) Chinese (c) Egyptian (d) Greco-Roman
Answer: (d)
Solution: Persian influence tempts because Gandhara lay on trade routes touching Persia, but the defining features — wavy hair, muscular bodies, draped garments — come from Greco-Roman (Hellenistic) art, so the answer is Greco-Roman.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Fine Arts (An Introduction to Indian Art)

29. Which Mughal emperor is associated with the construction of the Red Fort in Delhi?
(a) Akbar (b) Jahangir (c) Aurangzeb (d) Shah Jahan
Answer: (d)
Solution: Akbar tempts because he built the Agra Fort and Fatehpur Sikri, causing confusion between the two red sandstone forts. The Red Fort (Lal Qila) of Delhi was commissioned by Shah Jahan when he shifted his capital to Shahjahanabad in 1638, so the answer is Shah Jahan.
Source: NCERT Class 7 History (Our Pasts II)

30. The 'Natya Shastra', the foundational Sanskrit text on performing arts, is traditionally attributed to whom?
(a) Kalidasa (b) Banabhatta (c) Patanjali (d) Bharata Muni
Answer: (d)
Solution: Kalidasa tempts because he wrote famous Sanskrit dramas, but he was a playwright, not the author of the treatise on dramaturgy. The Natya Shastra is attributed to Bharata Muni, so the answer is Bharata Muni.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Fine Arts

31. The Brihadeeswarar Temple at Thanjavur was built by which Chola ruler?
(a) Rajendra Chola I (b) Rajaraja Chola I (c) Kulottunga I (d) Vijayalaya Chola
Answer: (b)
Solution: Rajendra Chola I tempts because he was Rajaraja's son and also a great temple builder (Gangaikonda Cholapuram), a classic similar-names trap. The Brihadeeswarar (Rajarajeswara) Temple was completed by Rajaraja Chola I around 1010 CE, so the answer is Rajaraja Chola I.
Source: NCERT Class 7 History (Our Pasts II)

32. Which of the following is NOT one of the eight classical dance forms recognised by the Sangeet Natak Akademi?
(a) Mohiniyattam (b) Chhau (c) Sattriya (d) Kathak
Answer: (b)
Solution: Sattriya tempts because it is less known, but it was recognised as a classical dance of Assam in 2000. Chhau of eastern India is a martial folk dance tradition, not among the eight classical forms, so the answer is Chhau.
Source: Sangeet Natak Akademi (official)

33. The Ajanta cave paintings mainly depict stories from which texts?
(a) Jatakas (b) Puranas (c) Vedas (d) Upanishads
Answer: (a)
Solution: Puranas tempt because later Indian art draws heavily on them, but Ajanta is a Buddhist site. Its murals illustrate the Jataka tales — stories of the previous lives of the Buddha — so the answer is Jatakas.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Fine Arts (An Introduction to Indian Art)

34. The 'Vesara' style of temple architecture is a blend of which two styles?
(a) Nagara and Buddhist (b) Dravida and Odisha (c) Nagara and Dravida (d) Dravida and Islamic
Answer: (c)
Solution: The Buddhist option tempts because of cave architecture, but Vesara is a temple style of the Deccan. It combines the northern Nagara and southern Dravida features, as seen in Chalukyan temples, so the answer is Nagara and Dravida.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Fine Arts (An Introduction to Indian Art)

35. Who composed the famous Sanskrit play 'Abhijnanashakuntalam'?
(a) Kalidasa (b) Bhasa (c) Shudraka (d) Vishakhadatta
Answer: (a)
Solution: Shudraka tempts because he wrote the famous play Mrichchhakatika, a similar-names/similar-works trap. Abhijnanashakuntalam, based on the Mahabharata story of Shakuntala and Dushyanta, is by Kalidasa, so the answer is Kalidasa.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Themes in Indian History

36. The 'Gita Govinda', celebrating the love of Radha and Krishna, was composed by which poet?
(a) Tulsidas (b) Vidyapati (c) Surdas (d) Jayadeva
Answer: (d)
Solution: Surdas tempts because he too sang of Krishna's love, but he wrote in Brajbhasha during the Mughal era. The Sanskrit Gita Govinda was composed by the 12th-century poet Jayadeva of Bengal, so the answer is Jayadeva.
Source: NCERT Class 7 History (Our Pasts II)

37. Which site is famous for the monolithic rock-cut Kailasa temple built by the Rashtrakutas?
(a) Elephanta (b) Badami (c) Ellora (d) Mahabalipuram
Answer: (c)
Solution: Mahabalipuram tempts because it also has monolithic rock-cut monuments (the rathas), a classic similar-features trap. The Kailasa temple (Cave 16) at Ellora was carved under Rashtrakuta king Krishna I, so the answer is Ellora.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Fine Arts (An Introduction to Indian Art)

38. The 'Sattriya' dance form was introduced by which Vaishnavite saint of Assam?
(a) Chaitanya Mahaprabhu (b) Shankaradeva (c) Ramanuja (d) Madhvacharya
Answer: (b)
Solution: Chaitanya Mahaprabhu tempts as a famous Vaishnavite saint, but he belonged to Bengal and inspired kirtan, not Sattriya. Sattriya was created by the 15th–16th century Assamese saint Shankaradeva as part of worship in sattras (monasteries), so the answer is Shankaradeva.
Source: Sangeet Natak Akademi (official)

39. The Iron Pillar at Mehrauli, Delhi, which has resisted rust for centuries, belongs to the period of which dynasty?
(a) Maurya (b) Gupta (c) Chola (d) Pallava
Answer: (b)
Solution: Maurya tempts because Ashokan pillars are the most famous pillars of ancient India, a chronological confusion trap. The Mehrauli Iron Pillar is attributed to the Gupta period (associated with Chandragupta II) and shows advanced metallurgy, so the answer is Gupta.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Themes in Indian History

40. Which of the following pairs is correctly matched?
(a) Mohiniyattam – Kerala (b) Kathakali – Tamil Nadu (c) Bharatnatyam – Andhra Pradesh (d) Manipuri – Odisha
Answer: (a)
Solution: Kathakali–Tamil Nadu tempts because Tamil Nadu is a southern state, but Kathakali belongs to Kerala while Bharatnatyam belongs to Tamil Nadu. Mohiniyattam is the classical dance of Kerala, so the answer is Mohiniyattam – Kerala.
Source: Sangeet Natak Akademi (official)

41. The 'Lion Capital' of Ashoka, adopted as the National Emblem of India, was found at which site?
(a) Bodh Gaya (b) Kushinagar (c) Sanchi (d) Sarnath
Answer: (d)
Solution: Sanchi tempts because of its famous stupas and gateways, but the Lion Capital crowned a pillar at Sarnath, where the Buddha gave his first sermon. It was adopted as the National Emblem on 26 January 1950, so the answer is Sarnath.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Themes in Indian History

42. Which Mughal emperor's reign is considered the golden age of Mughal painting, with artists like Bishan Das and Abu'l Hasan?
(a) Babur (b) Humayun (c) Akbar (d) Jahangir
Answer: (d)
Solution: Akbar tempts because he founded the imperial painting atelier and commissioned the Hamzanama, but Mughal painting reached its peak of refinement under Jahangir, himself a connoisseur, so the answer is Jahangir.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Fine Arts (An Introduction to Indian Art)

43. The 'Char Minar' of Hyderabad was built by which dynasty?
(a) Qutb Shahi (b) Bahmani (c) Adil Shahi (d) Nizam Shahi
Answer: (a)
Solution: Bahmani tempts because it was the parent Deccan sultanate from which the others split, a chronological confusion trap. The Char Minar was built in 1591 by Muhammad Quli Qutb Shah of the Qutb Shahi dynasty of Golconda, so the answer is Qutb Shahi.
Source: NCERT Class 7 History (Our Pasts II)

44. In Hindustani classical music, a 'gharana' refers to what?
(a) A musical instrument family (b) A type of raga (c) A school or style of music tied to a lineage (d) A rhythmic cycle
Answer: (c)
Solution: A rhythmic cycle tempts because tala terms are often confused with gharana, but a rhythmic cycle is called tala. A gharana is a stylistic school of music passed down through a family or teacher-disciple lineage, such as Gwalior or Kirana, so the answer is a school or style of music tied to a lineage.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Fine Arts

45. The Shore Temple at Mamallapuram (Mahabalipuram) was built under which dynasty?
(a) Chola (b) Pallava (c) Pandya (d) Chera
Answer: (b)
Solution: Chola tempts because of later Tamil temple glory, but Mamallapuram flourished earlier. The Shore Temple and the Pancha Rathas were built under the Pallavas, especially Narasimhavarman II (Rajasimha), so the answer is Pallava.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Fine Arts (An Introduction to Indian Art)

46. Which of the following statements about Kathak is correct?
(a) It originated in the temples of Kerala (b) It is performed only by women (c) It derives its name from 'kathakars' or storytellers (d) It has no Mughal influence
Answer: (c)
Solution: The Kerala option tempts by confusing Kathak with Kathakali, a classic similar-names trap. Kathak comes from 'katha' (story) and the kathakars who narrated epics; it later absorbed Mughal court influence in its Lucknow and Jaipur gharanas, so the answer is that it derives its name from 'kathakars' or storytellers.
Source: Sangeet Natak Akademi (official)

47. The famous bronze 'Nataraja' (dancing Shiva) sculptures are associated with which dynasty?
(a) Pallava (b) Chola (c) Rashtrakuta (d) Hoysala
Answer: (b)
Solution: Hoysala tempts because of its exquisite stone sculpture at Belur and Halebidu, but the lost-wax bronze casting of Nataraja icons reached perfection under the Cholas of Tamil Nadu, so the answer is Chola.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Fine Arts (An Introduction to Indian Art)

48. The 'Mrichchhakatika' (The Little Clay Cart) is a Sanskrit play written by whom?
(a) Kalidasa (b) Bhavabhuti (c) Shudraka (d) Bhasa
Answer: (c)
Solution: Kalidasa tempts as the most famous Sanskrit dramatist, but his plays are Shakuntalam, Malavikagnimitram and Vikramorvashiyam. Mrichchhakatika, the love story of Charudatta and Vasantasena, is attributed to Shudraka, so the answer is Shudraka.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Themes in Indian History

49. Which UNESCO World Heritage Site in India is a group of rock shelters with paintings dating back to the Mesolithic period?
(a) Bhimbetka (b) Ajanta (c) Ellora (d) Elephanta
Answer: (a)
Solution: Ajanta tempts because it is the most famous painted cave site, but its paintings are Buddhist and date from roughly the 2nd century BCE to 6th century CE, not the Mesolithic age. The Bhimbetka rock shelters in Madhya Pradesh contain prehistoric paintings, so the answer is Bhimbetka.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Fine Arts (An Introduction to Indian Art)

50. The 'Konark Sun Temple' in Odisha, designed as a colossal chariot of the Sun God, was built by which ruler?
(a) Narasimhadeva I (b) Anantavarman Chodaganga (c) Kharavela (d) Rajaraja Chola I
Answer: (a)
Solution: Anantavarman Chodaganga tempts because he was a great Eastern Ganga king who began the Jagannath Temple at Puri, a similar-dynasty trap. The Konark Sun Temple was built in the 13th century by Narasimhadeva I of the Eastern Ganga dynasty, so the answer is Narasimhadeva I.
Source: NCERT Class 7 History (Our Pasts II)
