# Indian Culture & Art

## 🎯 The Hook: A Dance That Survived an Empire

In 1930, a young woman named Rukmini Devi Arundale watched a performance that polite society called "vulgar" — Sadir, the temple dance of the devadasis. Instead of turning away, she learned it herself, refined it, and renamed it **Bharatanatyam**. Today it is one of the oldest and most respected classical dance forms of India. One person's courage saved an entire art form.

That is the story of Indian culture in miniature: arts that were banned, forgotten, or mocked — and yet survived for thousands of years. In your exam, this chapter is pure gold: the facts never change, the questions repeat in patterns, and a well-prepared student can answer most culture questions in under 15 seconds.

## 1. Classical Dances of India — The Big Eight

The **Sangeet Natak Akademi** (India's national academy for music, dance and drama) currently recognises **eight** classical dance forms. This number itself is a trap — older books say six or seven, because **Sattriya** was the most recent addition (recognised in 2000).

| Dance Form | State of Origin | Key Feature |
|---|---|---|
| Bharatanatyam | Tamil Nadu | Oldest; temple dance; geometric poses |
| Kathak | Uttar Pradesh (North India) | Spins (chakkars), footwork, Mughal influence |
| Kathakali | Kerala | Elaborate face make-up, stories from epics |
| Mohiniyattam | Kerala | Graceful, feminine; "dance of the enchantress" |
| Kuchipudi | Andhra Pradesh | Dance-drama; dancing on a brass plate |
| Odissi | Odisha | Sculpturesque poses (tribhanga); temple origin |
| Manipuri | Manipur | Soft, devotional; Ras Leela of Radha-Krishna |
| Sattriya | Assam | Monastery (Sattra) origin; by monks of Vaishnavite tradition |

**Memory trick — "BKKM KOMS":** **B**haratanatyam, **K**athak, **K**athakali, **M**ohiniyattam, **K**uchipudi, **O**dissi, **M**anipuri, **S**attriya.

**State trick:** Kerala has **two** (Kathakali + Mohiniyattam). Assam = Sattriya (both start with "S" sound... Sat-Assam). Manipur = Manipuri (same name — easiest!).

**Common confusion:** **Chhau** is NOT one of the eight classical dances — it is a traditional/folk dance (inscribed on UNESCO's Intangible Heritage list in 2010). Examiners love to slip Chhau into "classical dance" options.

🧠 **Active Recall:** Close the book. Name all 8 classical dances and their states. Which state contributes two forms? Which form was recognised most recently?

## 2. Classical Music — Two Great Streams

Indian classical music split into two systems around the 12th–13th century:

- **Hindustani music** — North India; influenced by Persian/Mughal culture. Great names: **Tansen** (Akbar's court, one of the Navaratnas), **Ustad Bismillah Khan** (shehnai, Bharat Ratna 2001), **Pandit Ravi Shankar** (sitar), **Ustad Zakir Hussain** (tabla).
- **Carnatic music** — South India; more purely devotional. The "Trinity" of Carnatic music: **Tyagaraja, Muthuswami Dikshitar, and Syama Sastri**. **M.S. Subbulakshmi** was the first musician to receive the Bharat Ratna (1998).

**Key terms examiners love:**
- **Gharana** — a school/lineage of Hindustani music (e.g., Gwalior gharana — the oldest; Kirana, Jaipur, Agra gharanas).
- **Raga** — melodic framework; **Tala** — rhythmic cycle.

**Mnemonic for the Carnatic Trinity — "TMS" (like a bus service!):** **T**yagaraja, **M**uthuswami Dikshitar, **S**yama Sastri.

🧠 **Active Recall:** Who was the first musician to get the Bharat Ratna? Which gharana is considered the oldest? Name the two streams of Indian classical music.

| Gujarat | Garba, Dandiya Raas |
| Maharashtra | Lavani |

| West Bengal | Chhau (Purulia), Jhumur |
| Odisha | Ghumura, Chhau (Mayurbhanj) |
| Andhra Pradesh | Kolattam |
| Tamil Nadu | Karagattam, Kummi |
| Karnataka | Yakshagana (dance-drama), Dollu Kunitha |

**Trap alert:** **Chhau** exists in three styles — **Purulia (West Bengal), Mayurbhanj (Odisha), Seraikella (Jharkhand)**. Only Seraikella and Purulia Chhau use masks; Mayurbhanj Chhau does **not**. This is a classic "almost-true statement" trap.

🧠 **Active Recall:** Which folk dance belongs to Assam? Name the three regional styles of Chhau. Which Chhau style does not use masks?

## 4. Painting Traditions

- **Madhubani (Mithila) painting** — Bihar; done by women, originally on walls; geometric patterns, gods and nature. It has a **GI (Geographical Indication) tag**.
- **Pattachitra** — Odisha/West Bengal; cloth-based scroll painting, mostly Jagannath and mythological themes.
- **Warli** — Maharashtra; tribal art using simple triangles and circles, white on mud walls.
- **Tanjore (Thanjavur) painting** — Tamil Nadu; rich colours, gold foil, glass beads.
- **Kalamkari** — Andhra Pradesh; hand-painted or block-printed cotton textile art.
- **Miniature paintings** — Mughal, Rajasthani, Pahari (Kangra, Basohli) and Deccan schools. **Kangra** paintings are famous for the Radha-Krishna theme and soft colours.
- **Phad** — Rajasthan; long scroll paintings narrating folk deities like Pabuji and Devnarayan.

**Mnemonic — "M-W-P-T" for the most-asked four:** **M**adhubani–Bihar, **W**arli–Maharashtra, **P**attachitra–Odisha, **T**anjore–Tamil Nadu.

## 5. Puppetry, Theatre & Crafts

**Four traditional puppet types of India:**
1. **String puppets (Marionettes)** — Kathputli (Rajasthan), Bommalattam (Tamil Nadu)
2. **Shadow puppets** — Tholu Bommalata (Andhra Pradesh), Togalu Gombeyaata (Karnataka)
3. **Rod puppets** — Putul Nautch (West Bengal), Yampuri (Bihar)
4. **Glove puppets** — Pavakoothu (Kerala)

**Traditional theatre forms:** Yakshagana (Karnataka), Nautanki (UP), Jatra (West Bengal), Bhavai (Gujarat), Tamasha (Maharashtra), Swang (Haryana/Punjab), Bhand Pather (Kashmir), Ankiya Naat (Assam — created by **Srimanta Sankardeva**, who also created Sattriya dance).

🧠 **Active Recall:** Kathputli belongs to which state and which puppet type? Who created Ankiya Naat and Sattriya?

## 6. UNESCO & Indian Culture (Static-Dynamic Link 🔗)

UNESCO's **Intangible Cultural Heritage** list includes several Indian elements. High-yield ones:

- **Koodiyattam** (Sanskrit theatre, Kerala) — among the earliest inscriptions (2008)
- **Vedic chanting** (2008)
- **Ramlila** (2008)
- **Chhau dance** (2010)
- **Kalbelia dance** (Rajasthan, 2010)
- **Yoga** (2016)
- **Kumbh Mela** (2017)
- **Durga Puja of Kolkata** (2021)
- **Garba of Gujarat** (2023)

**🔗 Static-Dynamic Link:** Garba's inclusion in 2023 shows that "static" culture keeps making news. Whenever a new Indian element enters the UNESCO list, expect a question in the next exam cycle — but the *older* entries (Yoga, Kumbh Mela, Durga Puja) are asked again and again.

## ⚠️ Caution: Examiner's Trap

**Trap 1 — "Almost-True Statement" (लगभग सही कथन):**
*"Kathakali is the classical dance of Tamil Nadu."* — 90% of students know Kathakali is South Indian, so they tick it in a hurry. Kathakali is from **Kerala**; Tamil Nadu's form is **Bharatanatyam**. Examiners swap states between neighbouring regions constantly.

**Trap 2 — "Similar Names" (समान नाम/वर्ष):**
**Kathak vs Kathakali vs Kuchipudi** — all start with "K", all classical, all confusing. Kathak = North India (UP), Kathakali = Kerala, Kuchipudi = Andhra Pradesh. Similarly **Mohiniyattam vs Manipuri** — both start with "M", both graceful, but Mohiniyattam = Kerala and Manipuri = Manipur.

**Trap 3 — "Negative Phrasing" (नकारात्मक वाक्यांश):**
*"Which of the following is NOT a classical dance?"* with options like Bharatanatyam, Odissi, **Bihu**, Sattriya. Students who read fast miss the "NOT" and pick Bharatanatyam. Bihu is a **folk** dance of Assam — that is the answer. Always underline "NOT/EXCEPT" in the question before looking at options.
