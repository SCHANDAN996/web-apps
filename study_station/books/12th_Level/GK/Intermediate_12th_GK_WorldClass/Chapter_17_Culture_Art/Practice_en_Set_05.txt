101. The famous 'Dashavatara' panels are carved on the walls of which temple?
(a) Vishnu Temple, Deogarh (b) Kailasa Temple, Ellora (c) Sun Temple, Konark (d) Brihadeeswara Temple, Thanjavur
Answer: (a)
Solution: The Kailasa Temple at Ellora is famous for its monolithic rock-cut architecture, not Dashavatara panels, which tempts many candidates. The Dashavatara (ten incarnations of Vishnu) relief panels are a celebrated feature of the Gupta-period Vishnu Temple at Deogarh in Uttar Pradesh, so the answer is Vishnu Temple, Deogarh.
Source: NCERT Class 11 An Introduction to Indian Art

102. The 'Gandhara school of art' flourished mainly under the patronage of which ruler?
(a) Ashoka (b) Harshavardhana (c) Chandragupta II (d) Kanishka
Answer: (d)
Solution: Ashoka is a tempting option because of his association with Buddhist art like Sanchi, but Gandhara art, with its Greco-Buddhist features, reached its peak under the Kushana ruler Kanishka in the 1st–2nd century CE, so the answer is Kanishka.
Source: NCERT Class 11 An Introduction to Indian Art

103. Which classical dance form uses the 'Chowk' and 'Tribhanga' as basic body positions?
(a) Kathak (b) Bharatanatyam (c) Manipuri (d) Odissi
Answer: (d)
Solution: Bharatanatyam is tempting because it is also a temple dance, but its basic position is the araimandi (half-sitting). The Chowk (square stance symbolising Jagannath) and the Tribhanga (three-body-bend pose) are the signature positions of Odissi, so the answer is Odissi.
Source: NCERT Class 11 An Introduction to Indian Art

104. The Ajanta paintings mainly depict themes from which texts?
(a) Puranas (b) Jataka tales (c) Vedas (d) Arthashastra
Answer: (b)
Solution: The Puranas tempt candidates because many later temple sculptures depict Puranic stories, but the Ajanta cave paintings (2nd century BCE–6th century CE) chiefly illustrate the Jataka tales — stories of the Buddha's previous births, so the answer is Jataka tales.
Source: NCERT Class 11 An Introduction to Indian Art

105. The 'Sattriya' dance tradition was introduced by which saint-reformer?
(a) Chaitanya Mahaprabhu (b) Ramanuja (c) Shankaradeva (d) Kabir
Answer: (c)
Solution: Chaitanya Mahaprabhu is a tempting distractor as he too was a Vaishnavite saint of the same era, but he belonged to Bengal and inspired kirtan traditions. Sattriya, the classical dance of Assam, was created in the 15th–16th century by Shankaradeva as part of worship in sattras (monasteries), so the answer is Shankaradeva.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Indian Culture (Performing Arts)

106. The Brihadeeswara Temple at Thanjavur was built by which Chola ruler?
(a) Rajaraja Chola I (b) Rajendra Chola I (c) Kulottunga I (d) Vijayalaya
Answer: (a)
Solution: Rajendra Chola I is the classic trap because he built a similar temple at Gangaikondacholapuram, confusing candidates. The Brihadeeswara (Rajarajeswara) Temple at Thanjavur, completed in 1010 CE, was built by Rajaraja Chola I, so the answer is Rajaraja Chola I.
Source: NCERT Class 7 Our Pasts II

107. Which of the following is a famous folk theatre form of Uttar Pradesh?

Solution: Tamasha is tempting as a popular folk theatre, but it belongs to Maharashtra; Jatra is of Bengal and Bhavai of Gujarat. Nautanki is the traditional folk theatre of Uttar Pradesh, so the answer is Nautanki.

108. The 'Phad' painting tradition belongs to which state?
(a) Rajasthan (b) Madhya Pradesh (c) Odisha (d) Bihar
Answer: (a)
Solution: Odisha tempts candidates because of its famous Pattachitra scrolls, which are also narrative paintings. Phad is a long scroll painting tradition of Rajasthan depicting tales of folk deities like Pabuji and Devnarayan, so the answer is Rajasthan.
Source: NCERT Class 11 An Introduction to Indian Art

109. The Kuchipudi dance-drama tradition originated in a village of which present-day state?
(a) Andhra Pradesh (b) Karnataka (c) Tamil Nadu (d) Kerala
Answer: (a)
Solution: Karnataka is a tempting wrong option because of the similar-sounding Yakshagana tradition there. Kuchipudi takes its name from Kuchipudi village in the Krishna district of present-day Andhra Pradesh, where it developed as a dance-drama, so the answer is Andhra Pradesh.

110. The 'Lingaraja Temple', a masterpiece of Kalinga architecture, is located at —

Solution: Puri is the trap because of the famous Jagannath Temple there, and Konark for the Sun Temple; both are in Odisha, causing confusion. The Lingaraja Temple, dedicated to Shiva, stands in Bhubaneswar, so the answer is Bhubaneswar.

113. The musical instrument 'Santoor' is traditionally associated with the music of which region?

Answer: (c)
Solution: Punjab is a tempting distractor because of its strong folk music culture, but the Santoor, a hundred-stringed hammered dulcimer, is the traditional instrument of Kashmiri Sufiana music, later adapted to Hindustani classical music, so the answer is Kashmir.

114. The 'Gopuram' is a characteristic feature of which style of temple architecture?
(a) Dravida (b) Vesara (c) Nagara (d) Hemadpanti
Answer: (a)
Solution: Nagara is the trap because it is the most commonly tested style, but Nagara temples are known for their curvilinear shikhara, not gopurams. The monumental gateway tower called gopuram is the hallmark of the Dravida (South Indian) style, so the answer is Dravida.
Source: NCERT Class 11 An Introduction to Indian Art

115. The 'Bihu' dance is performed during the festivals of which state?

Answer: (c)
Solution: West Bengal tempts some candidates due to its many folk dances, but Bihu — performed during the three Bihu festivals marking the agricultural cycle — is the most famous folk dance of Assam, so the answer is Assam.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Indian Culture (Performing Arts)

116. The 'Chola bronzes' were primarily cast using which technique?
(a) Repoussé (b) Filigree (c) Lost-wax (cire perdue) (d) Engraving
Answer: (c)
Solution: Repoussé (hammering metal from the reverse) is a tempting option as it is also a metalworking technique, but the exquisite Chola bronze images, such as the Nataraja, were cast by the lost-wax (cire perdue) process, so the answer is Lost-wax (cire perdue).
Source: NCERT Class 11 An Introduction to Indian Art

117. The 'Kalbeliya' dance, inscribed on UNESCO's Representative List of Intangible Cultural Heritage, belongs to —
(a) Gujarat (b) Rajasthan (c) Haryana (d) Madhya Pradesh
Answer: (b)
Solution: Gujarat is tempting because of its famous Garba, which is also on the UNESCO list, creating a similar-name confusion. Kalbeliya, the snake-charmer community's dance, belongs to Rajasthan and was inscribed by UNESCO in 2010, so the answer is Rajasthan.
Source: UNESCO Intangible Cultural Heritage List

118. The 'Sarnath Lion Capital' of Ashoka is made of which material?

Solution: Red sandstone is the trap because it was widely used in later Mughal buildings, but Mauryan pillars and capitals, including the Sarnath Lion Capital, were carved from polished buff-coloured Chunar sandstone, so the answer is Polished Chunar sandstone.
Source: NCERT Class 11 An Introduction to Indian Art

121. Consider the following statements about the Mathura school of art:
1. It used spotted red sandstone for sculptures.
2. It produced the earliest images of the Buddha in human form.
3. It flourished only under the Gupta rulers.
Which of the statements given above is/are correct?
(a) 1, 2 and 3 (b) 2 and 3 only (c) 1 and 3 only (d) 1 and 2 only
Answer: (d)
Solution: Statement 3 is the trap — it sounds plausible because Gupta art is famous, but Mathura art flourished much earlier, mainly under the Kushanas, and continued into the Gupta period; it did not flourish only under the Guptas. Mathura artists used spotted red sandstone and were among the first to carve the Buddha in human form, so the answer is 1 and 2 only.
Source: NCERT Class 11 An Introduction to Indian Art

122. Arrange the following monuments in correct chronological order of their construction:
1. Qutub Minar (2. Iron Pillar at Mehrauli (3. Alai Darwaza (4. Humayun's Tomb
(a) 2-1-3-4 (b) 1-2-3-4 (c) 2-3-1-4 (d) 1-3-2-4
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is placing the Qutub Minar first because it is the most famous structure at the site, but the Iron Pillar (4th century CE, Gupta period) predates it by centuries. The correct order is: Iron Pillar (Gupta era), Qutub Minar (begun c. 1199 by Qutbuddin Aibak), Alai Darwaza (1311, Alauddin Khalji), Humayun's Tomb (c. 1570), so the answer is 2-1-3-4.
Source: NCERT Class 7 Our Pasts II

123. Consider the following pairs of classical dances and their associated themes:
1. Kathakali — episodes from the Ramayana and Mahabharata
2. Mohiniyattam — the dance of the enchantress Mohini
3. Manipuri — themes centred on Radha-Krishna and Raas Leela
Which of the pairs given above is/are correctly matched?
(a) 1 and 2 only (b) 2 and 3 only (c) 1, 2 and 3 (d) 1 and 3 only
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is to assume one pair must be wrong because such questions usually have an error, but here all three are accurate: Kathakali draws on the epics, Mohiniyattam enacts the Mohini legend, and Manipuri centres on Radha-Krishna Raas Leela, so the answer is 1, 2 and 3.

124. Consider the following statements about the Vesara style of temple architecture:
1. It is a hybrid of the Nagara and Dravida styles.
2. It developed mainly in the Deccan under the Chalukyas and Rashtrakutas.
3. The temples at Pattadakal are examples of this style.
Which of the statements given above is/are correct?
(a) 1 only (b) 1, 2 and 3 (c) 2 and 3 only (d) 1 and 2 only
Answer: (b)
Solution: The tempting error is to mark Pattadakal as purely Dravida, but the Pattadakal group (a UNESCO site) shows both Nagara and Dravida experiments under the Early Chalukyas, exemplifying the Deccan hybrid tradition. All three statements are correct, so the answer is 1, 2 and 3.
Source: NCERT Class 11 An Introduction to Indian Art

125. Consider the following pairs of UNESCO-recognised intangible cultural heritage elements of India:
1. Koodiyattam — Sanskrit theatre of Kerala
2. Vedic chanting — oral tradition of reciting the Vedas
3. Ramlila — traditional performance of the Ramayana
Which of the pairs given above is/are correctly matched?
(a) 1 and 2 only (b) 1, 2 and 3 (c) 1 and 3 only (d) 2 and 3 only
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is to doubt Koodiyattam because Kathakali is the more famous Kerala form, but it is Koodiyattam, the ancient Sanskrit theatre of Kerala, that UNESCO recognised in 2008, along with Vedic chanting (2008) and Ramlila (2008). All three pairs are correct, so the answer is 1, 2 and 3.
Source: UNESCO Intangible Cultural Heritage List
