Card 1
Front: Which classical dance form of India traces its origins to the temples of Tamil Nadu and was earlier known as Sadir?
Back: Bharatanatyam. It was revived and renamed in the 20th century by Rukmini Devi Arundale.

Card 2
Front: Which is the only classical dance form that originated in North India and shows strong Mughal influence?
Back: Kathak. It developed in the courts of the Nawabs of Lucknow and the Mughals, with famous gharanas — Lucknow, Jaipur and Banaras.

Card 3
Front: How many classical dance forms are recognised by the Sangeet Natak Akademi?
Back: Eight — Bharatanatyam, Kathak, Kathakali, Mohiniyattam, Kuchipudi, Odissi, Manipuri and Sattriya.

Card 4
Front: Which ancient text, attributed to Bharata Muni, is the foundational treatise on Indian performing arts?
Back: The Natya Shastra (roughly 200 BCE–200 CE). It covers drama, dance and music.

Card 5
Front: Which two classical music traditions exist in India, and which region does each belong to?
Back: Hindustani (North India) and Carnatic (South India). Hindustani music absorbed Persian influences; Carnatic remained closer to older forms.

Card 6
Front: Who was the great Dhrupad maestro counted among the nine gems (Navaratnas) of Akbar's court, and with which ragas is he associated?
Back: Tansen (born Ramtanu Pandey). He is associated with ragas like Miyan ki Malhar and Miyan ki Todi. Note: the Khayal style is traditionally credited to Amir Khusro, not Tansen — a common exam trap.

Card 7
Front: Which dance-drama form from Kerala uses elaborate facial makeup and costumes, with characters classified as Pacha, Kathi, etc.?
Back: Kathakali. "Pacha" (green) represents noble characters; "Kathi" (knife) represents evil ones.

Card 8
Front: Which UNESCO World Heritage Site in Madhya Pradesh contains the oldest stone structure in India commissioned by Emperor Ashoka?
Back: The Great Stupa at Sanchi. It was originally built in the 3rd century BCE and later enlarged.

Card 9
Front: The famous Iron Pillar near Qutub Minar, which has not rusted for over 1,600 years, belongs to which dynasty?
Back: The Gupta dynasty (associated with Chandragupta II). It is a marvel of ancient Indian metallurgy.

Card 10
Front: Which school of art during the Kushana period blended Greek and Indian styles to create the first images of the Buddha?
Back: The Gandhara school of art. The Mathura school, in contrast, used red sandstone and indigenous styles.

Card 11
Front: Which folk painting style from Bihar is traditionally done by women on walls and uses natural dyes, and received a GI tag?
Back: Madhubani (Mithila) painting. Famous artists include Sita Devi and Ganga Devi.

Card 12
Front: Memory trick: How can you quickly recall the eight classical dances?
Back: Use the mnemonic "B-K-K-M-K-O-M-S" — Bharatanatyam, Kathak, Kathakali, Mohiniyattam, Kuchipudi, Odissi, Manipuri, Sattriya. Remember: "2 from Kerala (Kathakali, Mohiniyattam), 2 start with K from North/AP (Kathak, Kuchipudi)".

Card 13
Front: Memory trick: How do you remember which gharanas belong to Kathak?
Back: "L-J-B" — Lucknow, Jaipur, Banaras. Think: "Lucky Jaipur Banaras" — all three are in North India, matching Kathak's northern origin.

Card 14
Front: Examiner's trap: Sattriya is a classical dance of which state — Odisha or Assam?
Back: Assam. It was introduced by the saint Srimanta Sankardeva in the 15th–16th century. Examiners swap it with Odissi (Odisha) because the names sound similar.

Card 15
Front: Examiner's trap: Is the Kuchipudi dance form from Tamil Nadu or Andhra Pradesh?
Back: Andhra Pradesh (Kuchipudi village in Krishna district). Trap options often pair it with Tamil Nadu because Bharatanatyam belongs there.

Card 16
Front: Which Mughal emperor built the Red Fort and the Jama Masjid in Delhi, and in which city is the Taj Mahal located?
Back: Shah Jahan built both (Red Fort begun 1638, Jama Masjid 1650–56). The Taj Mahal is in Agra, built in memory of Mumtaz Mahal.

Card 17
Front: The Ajanta caves are famous for paintings, while the Ellora caves are famous for what — and which is the largest monolithic structure there?
Back: Ellora is famous for rock-cut architecture. The Kailasa temple (Cave 16), built under the Rashtrakutas, is the largest monolithic rock-cut structure in the world.

Card 18
Front: Which Indian classical musician is credited with popularising the sitar worldwide, and which instrument did Ustad Bismillah Khan play?
Back: Pandit Ravi Shankar popularised the sitar. Ustad Bismillah Khan played the shehnai and was a Bharat Ratna awardee (2001).

Card 19
Front: Which classical dance form is known for its sculpturesque poses inspired by the temples of Odisha, especially the Konark Sun Temple?
Back: Odissi. Its tribhanga (three-body-bend) posture mirrors temple sculptures.

Card 20
Front: Which festivals of classical dance are held every year against the backdrop of the Sun Temple at Konark and the Khajuraho temples?
Back: The Konark Dance Festival (Odisha) and the Khajuraho Dance Festival (Madhya Pradesh) — both celebrate classical dance forms at temple venues.
