76. Which of the following best describes "stagflation"?
(a) High growth with low inflation (b) High inflation with stagnant growth and high unemployment (c) Low inflation with high unemployment (d) Deflation with rising output
Answer: (b)
Solution: Many students pick the option with low inflation and high unemployment, confusing stagflation with a simple recession. Stagflation is the unusual combination of rising prices alongside stagnant output and joblessness, as seen in the 1970s oil-shock economies, so the answer is high inflation with stagnant growth and high unemployment.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Macroeconomics

77. The "Laffer Curve" illustrates the relationship between:
(a) Inflation and unemployment (b) Tax rates and tax revenue (c) Interest rates and savings (d) Income and consumption
Answer: (b)
Solution: The inflation-unemployment trade-off is the Phillips Curve, which is the tempting confusion here. The Laffer Curve shows that beyond a certain tax rate, revenue falls because high taxes discourage work and evasion rises, so the answer is tax rates and tax revenue.

78. Which institution is known as the "lender of last resort" in India?
(a) State Bank of India (b) NABARD (c) Reserve Bank of India (d) Ministry of Finance
Answer: (c)
Solution: SBI is the largest commercial bank, which tempts many, but it borrows from the central bank in a crisis rather than lending to others. The RBI lends to banks when no other source is available, so the answer is Reserve Bank of India.

79. "Helicopter money" refers to:
(a) Subsidies on air travel (b) Government borrowing from the RBI (c) Foreign investment in aviation (d) Printing currency and distributing it directly to the public to boost demand
Answer: (d)
Solution: Government borrowing from the RBI is deficit financing, a near-synonym trap. Helicopter money, a term popularised by Milton Friedman, means distributing newly created money directly to citizens to stimulate spending, so the answer is printing currency and distributing it directly to the public.

80. If the RBI wants to reduce money supply in the economy, it should:
(a) Decrease the repo rate (b) Buy government securities in the open market (c) Increase the Cash Reserve Ratio (d) Reduce the Statutory Liquidity Ratio
Answer: (c)
Solution: Buying securities injects money into the system — the opposite of what is asked, a classic reverse-logic trap. Raising the CRR forces banks to park more funds with the RBI, leaving less to lend, so the answer is increase the Cash Reserve Ratio.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Macroeconomics

81. The "Gini coefficient" measures:
(a) Inflation inequality between rural and urban areas (b) Gender disparity in education (c) Income or wealth inequality within a nation (d) Regional imbalance in development
Answer: (c)
Solution: A Gini of 0 means perfect equality and 1 means perfect inequality; it is not about inflation or gender, which are tempting distractors. It is derived from the Lorenz curve, so the answer is income or wealth inequality within a nation.

82. Which of the following is NOT a direct tax?
(a) Income Tax (b) Goods and Services Tax (c) Corporation Tax (d) Wealth Tax (when it was in force)
Answer: (b)
Solution: Wealth Tax sounds indirect to some because it is rarely discussed, but it was levied directly on net wealth. GST is collected from consumers through sellers, making it an indirect tax, so the answer is Goods and Services Tax.

83. "Crowding out" in economics occurs when:
(a) Private investment falls because of increased government borrowing (b) Exports fall due to imports (c) Population growth reduces per capita income (d) Foreign firms replace domestic firms
Answer: (a)
Solution: The population option tempts students who link "crowding" with people, but the term is about financial markets. Heavy government borrowing raises interest rates and absorbs loanable funds, discouraging private investment, so the answer is private investment falls because of increased government borrowing.

84. The base year currently used for calculating India's GDP (new series) is:
(a) 2004-05 (b) 2017-18 (c) 2014-15 (d) 2011-12
Answer: (d)
Solution: 2004-05 was the earlier base year and is the chronological-confusion trap. The CSO revised the GDP series to the 2011-12 base in 2015, so the answer is 2011-12.

85. Which of the following is an example of "transfer payment" in national income accounting?
(a) Salary of a government school teacher (b) Payment of interest on a business loan (c) Purchase of a new machine by a factory (d) Old-age pension paid by the government
Answer: (d)
Solution: A teacher's salary is payment for services rendered and counts in national income, which makes it the tempting wrong choice. A pension involves no current production of goods or services in return, so it is excluded from national income; the answer is old-age pension paid by the government.

86. "Disguised unemployment" is most commonly found in:
(a) The organised manufacturing sector (b) The IT services sector (c) The agricultural sector (d) Public administration
Answer: (c)
Solution: Students often pick manufacturing thinking of idle factory workers, but that is open unemployment. In Indian agriculture, more people work on a farm than needed, so removing some does not reduce output; the answer is the agricultural sector.

87. The "Phillips Curve" shows an inverse relationship between:
(a) Inflation and unemployment (b) Tax rates and revenue (c) Money supply and interest rates (d) Savings and investment
Answer: (a)
Solution: The tax rate-revenue option is the Laffer Curve, a similar-name trap examiners love. The Phillips Curve suggests that lower unemployment comes with higher inflation in the short run, so the answer is inflation and unemployment.

88. Which of the following is counted in the calculation of Gross Domestic Product?
(a) Sale of second-hand cars (b) Services of a housewife at home (c) Purchase of shares in the stock market (d) Value of a newly constructed house
Answer: (d)
Solution: Second-hand sales tempt many, but they were counted in the year of original production and are excluded to avoid double counting. A newly built house is current production of a capital good, so the answer is value of a newly constructed house.

89. "Fiscal deficit" is defined as:
(a) Total expenditure minus total receipts excluding borrowings (b) Total expenditure minus total revenue receipts (c) Revenue expenditure minus revenue receipts (d) Total receipts minus total expenditure
Answer: (a)
Solution: The revenue-expenditure-minus-revenue-receipts option defines revenue deficit, the classic near-correct trap. Fiscal deficit equals total expenditure minus all non-debt receipts, and it indicates total borrowing needs, so the answer is total expenditure minus total receipts excluding borrowings.

90. The term "parity" in "Purchasing Power Parity" refers to:
(a) Equality of purchasing power of currencies for a common basket of goods (b) Equality of exchange rates across countries (c) Equality of interest rates (d) Equality of wage levels
Answer: (a)
Solution: Equal exchange rates sounds plausible but PPP actually explains why market exchange rates differ from real buying power. PPP compares how much of a standard basket a currency can buy in different countries, so the answer is equality of purchasing power of currencies for a common basket of goods.

91. Which committee recommended the introduction of the Goods and Services Tax in India through the "First Discussion Paper"?
(a) Kelkar Committee (b) Empowered Committee of State Finance Ministers (c) Chelliah Committee (d) Rangarajan Committee
Answer: (b)
Solution: The Kelkar Committee worked on tax reforms and fiscal consolidation, making it the tempting similar-name trap. The First Discussion Paper on GST (2009) was released by the Empowered Committee of State Finance Ministers, so the answer is Empowered Committee of State Finance Ministers.

92. "Core inflation" excludes which of the following?
(a) Food and fuel prices (b) Manufactured goods (c) Housing costs (d) Services
Answer: (a)
Solution: Manufactured goods are actually the main component of core inflation in India, which misleads many. Core inflation strips out volatile food and fuel items to reveal underlying price trends, so the answer is food and fuel prices.

93. If the marginal propensity to consume (MPC) is 0.8, the value of the investment multiplier is:
(a) 4 (b) 8 (c) 5 (d) 0.2
Answer: (c)
Solution: Many students compute 1 divided by MPC and get 1.25 or pick 8 by confusing MPC with the multiplier itself. The multiplier equals 1 divided by (1 minus MPC), that is 1 divided by 0.2, so the answer is 5.

94. "Demonetisation" in India in 2016 involved withdrawal of legal tender status of which denominations?
(a) Rs 100 and Rs 500 (b) Rs 500 and Rs 1000 (c) Rs 1000 and Rs 2000 (d) Rs 500 and Rs 2000
Answer: (b)
Solution: The Rs 1000 and Rs 2000 option is a chronological trap, since Rs 2000 notes were introduced after demonetisation, not withdrawn by it. On 8 November 2016, Rs 500 and Rs 1000 notes lost legal tender status, so the answer is Rs 500 and Rs 1000.

95. Which of the following best defines "opportunity cost"?
(a) The value of the next best alternative foregone (b) The money spent on purchasing a good (c) The total cost of all alternatives (d) The cost of production including profit
Answer: (a)
Solution: Money spent is accounting cost, the tempting near-correct option. Opportunity cost is what you give up — the single best alternative sacrificed when a choice is made, so the answer is the value of the next best alternative foregone.

96. The "Twin Deficit" problem refers to:
(a) Fiscal deficit and revenue deficit (b) Revenue deficit and capital deficit (c) Trade deficit and budget deficit of states (d) Fiscal deficit and current account deficit
Answer: (d)
Solution: Fiscal plus revenue deficit looks convincing because both are budget terms, but that pairing has no special name. Twin deficits mean a government budget deficit occurring alongside a current account (external) deficit, so the answer is fiscal deficit and current account deficit.

97. Which of the following is a "flow" variable?
(a) National income of a country (b) Capital stock of a firm (c) Wealth of a household (d) Money supply on a given date
Answer: (a)
Solution: Wealth and capital stock are measured at a point of time, making them stocks — the tempting confusion. National income is measured over a period, usually a year, so the answer is national income of a country.

98. "Moral suasion" by the RBI refers to:
(a) Legal penalties on defaulting banks (b) Cancelling banking licences (c) Raising the bank rate (d) Persuasion and pressure on banks to follow desired policies without formal directives
Answer: (d)
Solution: Raising the bank rate is a quantitative tool and tempts students who think all RBI actions are rate-based. Moral suasion is a qualitative method where the RBI uses advice, meetings and informal pressure, so the answer is persuasion and pressure on banks to follow desired policies without formal directives.

99. The "Human Development Index" combines which three dimensions?
(a) Income, employment and trade (b) Income, education and health (c) Education, environment and equality (d) Health, housing and happiness
Answer: (b)
Solution: The happiness option tempts students who confuse HDI with Bhutan's Gross National Happiness. HDI, published by the UNDP, uses life expectancy (health), schooling (education) and GNI per capita (income), so the answer is income, education and health.

100. "Beggar-thy-neighbour" policies in international trade refer to:
(a) Aid given to poor neighbouring countries (b) Free trade agreements between neighbours (c) Protectionist measures like devaluation and tariffs that benefit one country at others' expense (d) Labour migration across borders
Answer: (c)
Solution: Aid to poor neighbours is a literal misreading of the phrase and the trap here. The term describes competitive devaluations and tariffs that export a country's unemployment to trading partners, so the answer is protectionist measures like devaluation and tariffs that benefit one country at others' expense.
