Questions 1–20: Easy | Questions 21–25: Medium

1. What does GDP stand for?
(a) Gross Development Plan (b) General Domestic Price (c) Gross Domestic Product (d) General Deposit Policy
Answer: (c)
Solution: GDP measures the total market value of all final goods and services produced within a country's borders in a given period. "General Domestic Price" is a tempting but fabricated expansion, so the answer is Gross Domestic Product.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Macroeconomics

2. Which institution in India is responsible for controlling inflation through monetary policy?
(a) Reserve Bank of India (b) Ministry of Finance (c) NITI Aayog (d) SEBI
Answer: (a)
Solution: The Ministry of Finance handles fiscal policy, not monetary policy — a common confusion. The RBI uses tools like the repo rate and CRR to control inflation, so the answer is Reserve Bank of India.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Macroeconomics

3. Inflation refers to:
(a) A sustained rise in the general price level (b) A fall in the value of exports (c) An increase in unemployment (d) A rise in interest rates only
Answer: (a)
Solution: Inflation is defined as a sustained increase in the general price level of goods and services, reducing purchasing power. A rise in interest rates is a response to inflation, not its definition, so the answer is a sustained rise in the general price level.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Macroeconomics

4. What is the full form of NITI Aayog?
(a) National Investment and Taxation Institute (b) National Institute of Trade and Industry (c) New India Trade Initiative (d) National Institution for Transforming India
Answer: (d)
Solution: NITI Aayog replaced the Planning Commission in 2015. "National Institute of Trade and Industry" sounds plausible but is wrong, so the answer is National Institution for Transforming India.
Source: niti.gov.in

5. Fiscal deficit is the difference between:
(a) Total expenditure and total receipts excluding borrowings (b) Revenue receipts and capital receipts (c) Exports and imports (d) Tax revenue and non-tax revenue
Answer: (a)
Solution: Fiscal deficit equals total expenditure minus total receipts excluding borrowings; it shows the government's borrowing requirement. The export–import gap is the trade deficit, not fiscal deficit, so the answer is total expenditure and total receipts excluding borrowings.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Macroeconomics

6. The repo rate is the rate at which:
(a) RBI lends to commercial banks (b) Commercial banks lend to RBI (c) Banks lend to customers (d) Government borrows from the public
Answer: (a)
Solution: The reverse repo is the rate at which banks park funds with the RBI — the classic trap. The repo rate is the rate at which the RBI lends short-term funds to commercial banks, so the answer is RBI lends to commercial banks.
Source: rbi.org.in

7. Which of the following is a direct tax?
(a) GST (b) Customs duty (c) Income tax (d) Excise duty
Answer: (c)
Solution: GST, customs and excise are indirect taxes because their burden can be shifted to consumers. Income tax is paid directly by the person on whom it is levied, so the answer is income tax.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Indian Economic Development

8. GNP differs from GDP by:
(a) Subsidies (b) Depreciation (c) Indirect taxes (d) Net factor income from abroad
Answer: (d)
Solution: GNP = GDP plus net factor income from abroad. Depreciation separates GNP from NNP, not GNP from GDP — a frequent mix-up, so the answer is net factor income from abroad.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Macroeconomics

9. Which sector of the economy is also called the service sector?
(a) Primary sector (b) Tertiary sector (c) Secondary sector (d) Quaternary sector
Answer: (b)
Solution: The primary sector covers agriculture and mining, the secondary covers manufacturing, and the tertiary covers services like banking, transport and education, so the answer is tertiary sector.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Economics

10. What does a 'budget deficit' in the revenue account indicate?
(a) Total expenditure exceeds GDP (b) Capital expenditure exceeds capital receipts (c) Revenue expenditure exceeds revenue receipts (d) Imports exceed exports
Answer: (c)
Solution: Revenue deficit occurs when the government's day-to-day (revenue) expenditure exceeds its revenue receipts. Imports exceeding exports is a trade deficit, so the answer is revenue expenditure exceeds revenue receipts.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Macroeconomics

11. Which organisation releases the 'World Economic Outlook' report?
(a) World Bank (b) IMF (c) WTO (d) ADB
Answer: (b)
Solution: The World Bank publishes the Global Economic Prospects, which is the tempting wrong choice. The World Economic Outlook is published by the International Monetary Fund, so the answer is IMF.
Source: imf.org

12. Disguised unemployment is most commonly found in:
(a) The IT sector (b) Agriculture (c) Banking (d) Railways
Answer: (b)
Solution: Disguised unemployment means more people are engaged in work than actually needed, typical of family farms in Indian agriculture, so the answer is agriculture.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Economics

13. What is 'stagflation'?
(a) High inflation with high growth (b) Zero inflation with full employment (c) Deflation with low growth (d) High inflation with stagnant growth and unemployment
Answer: (d)
Solution: Stagflation combines stagnation (slow growth, high unemployment) with inflation — an unusual combination. High inflation with high growth is overheating, not stagflation, so the answer is high inflation with stagnant growth and unemployment.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Macroeconomics

14. The base year currently used for calculating India's GDP (new series) is:
(a) 2004–05 (b) 2014–15 (c) 2011–12 (d) 1993–94
Answer: (c)
Solution: The 2004–05 series was the earlier base, which traps many candidates. The present GDP series uses 2011–12 as the base year, so the answer is 2011–12.
Source: mospi.gov.in

15. Which of the following is NOT a function of money?
(a) Medium of exchange (b) Source of inflation (c) Store of value (d) Measure of value
Answer: (b)
Solution: Money functions as a medium of exchange, a measure of value, a store of value and a standard of deferred payment. Inflation is an economic phenomenon, not a function of money, so the answer is source of inflation.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Macroeconomics

16. 'Per capita income' is calculated by dividing national income by:
(a) Total area (b) Working population (c) Number of households (d) Total population
Answer: (d)
Solution: Per capita income equals national income divided by the total population, giving average income per person, so the answer is total population.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Economics

17. Which tax was subsumed into GST in India?
(a) Excise duty (b) Corporate tax (c) Income tax (d) Customs duty on imports fully
Answer: (a)
Solution: Income tax and corporate tax are direct taxes and remain outside GST. Central excise duty on most goods was subsumed into GST from 1 July 2017, so the answer is excise duty.
Source: gstcouncil.gov.in

18. The 'Laffer Curve' relates to:
(a) Tax rates and tax revenue (b) Inflation and unemployment (c) Income and consumption (d) Money supply and prices
Answer: (a)
Solution: The Phillips curve links inflation and unemployment — the classic confusion. The Laffer Curve shows the relationship between tax rates and government tax revenue, so the answer is tax rates and tax revenue.
Source: PYQ-style

19. Which body prepares the National Income estimates in India?
(a) National Statistical Office (b) RBI (c) NITI Aayog (d) Finance Commission
Answer: (a)
Solution: The RBI handles monetary matters and NITI Aayog is a policy think tank. National income estimates are prepared by the National Statistical Office under MoSPI, so the answer is National Statistical Office.
Source: mospi.gov.in

20. 'Microfinance' primarily refers to:
(a) Loans to large corporations (b) Foreign direct investment (c) Government borrowing abroad (d) Small loans to low-income households
Answer: (d)
Solution: Microfinance provides small loans and financial services to low-income individuals, often through self-help groups and MFIs, so the answer is small loans to low-income households.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Indian Economic Development

21. Consider the following statements:
1. CRR is maintained by banks with the RBI.
2. SLR is maintained by banks with themselves in liquid assets.
Which of the above is/are correct?
(a) 1 only (b) 2 only (c) Both 1 and 2 (d) Neither 1 nor 2
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is assuming both ratios are kept with the RBI. CRR is cash kept with the RBI, while SLR is maintained by banks with themselves in cash, gold or approved securities, so the answer is both 1 and 2.
Source: rbi.org.in

22. If the RBI wants to reduce money supply in the economy, it should:
(a) Reduce the repo rate (b) Buy government securities in open market (c) Reduce CRR (d) Increase the repo rate
Answer: (d)
Solution: Reducing the repo rate or CRR and buying securities all inject money — the opposite of what is asked. Raising the repo rate makes borrowing costlier and contracts money supply, so the answer is increase the repo rate.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Macroeconomics

23. Arrange the following in chronological order of their establishment:
1. NITI Aayog
2. Planning Commission
3. RBI
(a) 2, 3, 1 (b) 3, 2, 1 (c) 1, 2, 3 (d) 3, 1, 2
Answer: (b)
Solution: The chronological-confusion trap places the Planning Commission before the RBI. The RBI was set up in 1935, the Planning Commission in 1950 and NITI Aayog in 2015, so the answer is 3, 2, 1.
Source: rbi.org.in; niti.gov.in

24. Which of the following best describes 'devaluation' of a currency?
(a) Market-driven fall in currency value (b) Deliberate official reduction of currency value under fixed exchange rate (c) Rise in currency value due to demand (d) Printing of new currency notes
Answer: (b)
Solution: A market-driven fall is depreciation, not devaluation — the near-identical term that traps candidates. Devaluation is a deliberate official reduction of a currency's value under a fixed exchange rate system, so the answer is deliberate official reduction of currency value under fixed exchange rate.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Macroeconomics

25. The 'Gini coefficient' is used to measure:
(a) Inflation (b) Unemployment (c) Income inequality (d) Economic growth
Answer: (c)
Solution: The Gini coefficient ranges from 0 (perfect equality) to 1 (perfect inequality) and measures income or wealth distribution. It has nothing to do with inflation or growth rates, so the answer is income inequality.
Source: PYQ-style
