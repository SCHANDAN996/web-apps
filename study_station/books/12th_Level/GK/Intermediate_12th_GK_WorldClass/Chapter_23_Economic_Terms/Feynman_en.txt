# 🧒 Feynman Technique — Economic Terms & Concepts

## The Hardest Concept, Made Simple: Repo Rate vs Reverse Repo Rate

Ask any exam aspirant which pair of terms they mix up most, and the answer is almost always the same: **Repo Rate and Reverse Repo Rate**. They sound like twins, they move together, and examiners love swapping them in options. Let's kill this confusion forever.

### Explain It Like I'm 12

Imagine your neighbourhood has one big "money shop" — that's the **RBI (Reserve Bank of India)**. All the small banks (SBI, HDFC, etc.) sometimes run short of cash, and sometimes they have extra cash lying idle.

**Case 1 — A bank is short of money.**
The bank goes to the RBI and says, "Lend me money for a few days. I'll keep my government securities with you as a promise, and buy them back later." The interest RBI charges for this loan is the **Repo Rate**.

👉 Memory anchor: **Repo = RBI gives money TO banks** (banks are *receiving*, so they *pay* interest).

**Case 2 — A bank has extra money.**
The bank parks its surplus cash with the RBI and earns some interest on it. The interest RBI pays the bank is the **Reverse Repo Rate**.

👉 Memory anchor: **Reverse Repo = RBI takes money FROM banks** (the flow is *reversed*).

### Why Should You Care? (The Daily-Life Example)

When RBI **raises the repo rate**, borrowing becomes expensive for banks → banks raise your home loan and car loan EMIs → people borrow and spend less → less money chasing goods → **inflation cools down**.

When RBI **cuts the repo rate**, loans become cheaper → people spend more → the economy gets a push.

So one number decided in Mumbai decides the EMI of a family in Patna. That's why newspapers scream every time the RBI's Monetary Policy Committee meets.

### The Unforgettable Trick

**"REPO = REceive, Pay Out."**
- Banks **RE**ceive money → they **P**ay interest → that's re**PO**.
- Reverse Repo is just the mirror: banks **give** money, RBI pays interest.

And one more lock: **Repo Rate is always higher than Reverse Repo Rate.** Why? RBI is not a charity — it lends dear and borrows cheap, just like any money shop. If a question shows Reverse Repo higher than Repo, that option is wrong on sight.

### Examiner's Trap Alert 🪤

- Trap 1: "Reverse Repo is the rate at which banks borrow from RBI" — **wrong**, that's Repo.
- Trap 2: "Repo rate is decided by the Finance Ministry" — **wrong**, it's the RBI's Monetary Policy Committee (MPC), headed by the RBI Governor.
- Trap 3: Options pairing "Repo ↑ → EMIs ↓" — **wrong**, higher repo means costlier loans, so EMIs rise.
