101. Which of the following best describes 'stagflation'?
(a) High growth with low inflation (b) Rapid growth with rising employment (c) Falling prices with rising output (d) Stagnant growth with high inflation and unemployment
Answer: (d)
Solution: Many students pick falling prices with rising output, confusing stagflation with deflation, but deflation means falling prices, not stagnation. Stagflation combines economic stagnation, high inflation and high unemployment, a term popularised in the 1970s oil-crisis era, so the answer is stagnant growth with high inflation and unemployment.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Macroeconomics

102. The 'Laffer Curve' illustrates the relationship between:
(a) Tax rates and tax revenue (b) Inflation and unemployment (c) Money supply and interest rates (d) Exports and imports
Answer: (a)
Solution: The inflation-unemployment trade-off is the tempting wrong choice, but that is the Phillips Curve, not the Laffer Curve. The Laffer Curve shows that beyond a certain tax rate, revenue falls as compliance and work incentives decline, so the answer is tax rates and tax revenue.
Source: PYQ-style

103. In India, 'Core Sector' industries number:
(a) Eight (b) Seven (c) Six (d) Ten
Answer: (a)
Solution: Students often answer six by recalling only coal, steel, cement and a few others, but the Index of Eight Core Industries covers coal, crude oil, natural gas, refinery products, fertilisers, steel, cement and electricity, so the answer is eight.
Source: Ministry of Commerce & Industry, Government of India

104. 'Disguised unemployment' is most commonly found in India in:
(a) Agriculture (b) The IT sector (c) Banking (d) Railways
Answer: (a)
Solution: The IT sector is a tempting wrong answer because of layoff news, but disguised unemployment means more workers are engaged than needed with near-zero marginal productivity, a classic feature of Indian agriculture, so the answer is agriculture.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Indian Economic Development

105. Which of the following is NOT a direct tax?
(a) Income Tax (b) Goods and Services Tax (c) Corporation Tax (d) Wealth Tax (when levied)
Answer: (b)
Solution: Wealth Tax looks like the odd one out and tempts many, but it was a direct tax on net wealth until its abolition in 2016. GST is levied on supply of goods and services and its burden can be shifted to the consumer, making it an indirect tax, so the answer is Goods and Services Tax.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Indian Economic Development

106. The 'Phillips Curve' shows an inverse relationship between:
(a) Inflation and unemployment (b) Savings and investment (c) Growth and inequality (d) Interest rate and money demand
Answer: (a)
Solution: Interest rate and money demand is the tempting wrong option, but that inverse relation belongs to liquidity preference theory. The Phillips Curve depicts that lower unemployment is associated with higher inflation in the short run, so the answer is inflation and unemployment.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Macroeconomics

107. 'Fiscal deficit' is calculated as:
(a) Total expenditure minus total revenue receipts (b) Total expenditure minus total receipts excluding borrowings (c) Revenue expenditure minus revenue receipts (d) Total receipts minus total expenditure
Answer: (b)
Solution: Total expenditure minus total revenue receipts is the tempting wrong option, but that ignores capital receipts like disinvestment and recoveries of loans. Fiscal deficit equals total expenditure minus total receipts excluding borrowings, so the answer is total expenditure minus total receipts excluding borrowings.
Source: Union Budget documents, Government of India

108. Which institution releases the 'World Development Report'?
(a) World Bank (b) IMF (c) WTO (d) UNDP
Answer: (a)
Solution: The IMF is the tempting wrong answer because it publishes the World Economic Outlook, a similarly named report. The World Development Report is the flagship annual publication of the World Bank, so the answer is World Bank.
Source: World Bank official website

109. 'Giffen goods' are goods for which demand:
(a) Rises as price rises (b) Falls as income rises (c) Is perfectly elastic (d) Is unrelated to price
Answer: (a)
Solution: Falling demand as income rises is the tempting wrong option, but that describes inferior goods generally; Giffen goods are a special inferior good where the income effect outweighs the substitution effect, so demand rises when price rises, and the answer is rises as price rises.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Microeconomics

110. In the balance of payments, 'remittances' from Indians working abroad are recorded under:
(a) Capital account (b) Reserve assets (c) Errors and omissions (d) Current account
Answer: (d)
Solution: The capital account is the tempting wrong option because remittances are large foreign inflows, but they are unilateral transfers, which are part of the current account, so the answer is current account.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Macroeconomics

111. 'Monetary policy' in India is primarily formulated by:
(a) Ministry of Finance (b) NITI Aayog (c) SEBI (d) Reserve Bank of India
Answer: (d)
Solution: The Ministry of Finance tempts many because it announces the Budget, but the Budget is fiscal policy. Monetary policy, including the repo rate, is decided by the RBI's Monetary Policy Committee, so the answer is Reserve Bank of India.
Source: RBI official website

112. The 'Gini coefficient' measures:
(a) Inflation (b) Income inequality (c) Unemployment (d) Fiscal deficit
Answer: (b)
Solution: Inflation is a tempting wrong guess because both are expressed as indices, but inflation is measured by CPI and WPI. The Gini coefficient ranges from 0 (perfect equality) to 1 (perfect inequality) and measures income or wealth inequality, so the answer is income inequality.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Indian Economic Development

113. 'Repo rate' is the rate at which:
(a) Banks lend to RBI (b) RBI lends to commercial banks (c) Banks lend to each other overnight (d) RBI borrows from the government
Answer: (b)
Solution: Banks lending to RBI is the tempting wrong option, but that is the reverse repo rate. The repo rate is the rate at which the RBI lends short-term funds to commercial banks against securities, so the answer is RBI lends to commercial banks.
Source: RBI official website

114. Which of the following is a 'capital receipt' for the Government of India?
(a) Income tax collection (b) GST collection (c) Disinvestment proceeds (d) Interest receipts on loans
Answer: (c)
Solution: Interest receipts tempt many because they are non-tax revenue, but they are recurring revenue receipts. Disinvestment proceeds reduce government assets and are classified as capital receipts, so the answer is disinvestment proceeds.
Source: Union Budget documents, Government of India

115. 'Purchasing Power Parity' exchange rates compare currencies based on:
(a) Gold reserves (b) Interest rate differentials (c) The cost of an identical basket of goods (d) Trade volumes only
Answer: (c)
Solution: Interest rate differentials is the tempting wrong option, but that relates to interest rate parity theory. PPP compares what a common basket of goods costs in two countries to judge the real value of currencies, so the answer is the cost of an identical basket of goods.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Macroeconomics

116. The 'Human Development Index' combines which three dimensions?
(a) Income, trade, literacy (b) Health, education, standard of living (c) Growth, inflation, employment (d) Agriculture, industry, services
Answer: (b)
Solution: Income, trade and literacy tempts students who remember income is included, but trade is not an HDI dimension. HDI, published by UNDP, combines life expectancy (health), schooling (education) and GNI per capita (standard of living), so the answer is health, education, standard of living.
Source: UNDP Human Development Report

117. 'Crowding out' refers to:
(a) Excess liquidity in banks (b) Foreign firms replacing domestic firms (c) Population pressure on resources (d) Private investment falling due to high government borrowing
Answer: (d)
Solution: Foreign firms replacing domestic firms is the tempting wrong option, but that describes displacement by competition, not crowding out. Crowding out occurs when heavy government borrowing raises interest rates and reduces private investment, so the answer is private investment falling due to high government borrowing.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Macroeconomics

118. Which of the following is included in the calculation of GDP by the expenditure method?
(a) Purchase of second-hand goods (b) Sale of shares (c) Transfer payments like pensions (d) Government spending on goods and services
Answer: (d)
Solution: Transfer payments like pensions is the tempting wrong option, but transfers are not payment for current production and are excluded. Government spending on goods and services (G) is a core component of GDP = C + I + G + (X − M), so the answer is government spending on goods and services.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Macroeconomics

119. 'Helicopter money' refers to:
(a) Defence spending on aircraft (b) Printing and distributing money directly to the public to boost demand (c) Foreign aid inflows (d) Emergency bank lending
Answer: (b)
Solution: Emergency bank lending tempts many because it also injects liquidity, but that is conventional central bank lending. Helicopter money, a term associated with Milton Friedman, means printing money and giving it directly to citizens to stimulate demand, so the answer is printing and distributing money directly to the public to boost demand.
Source: PYQ-style

120. The 'Base Year' for India's current GDP series is:
(a) 2011–12 (b) 2004–05 (c) 2014–15 (d) 2017–18
Answer: (a)
Solution: 2014–15 is the tempting wrong option because it coincides with a change of government, but base years are chosen for data availability, not politics. The current national accounts series uses 2011–12 as the base year, so the answer is 2011–12.
Source: Ministry of Statistics and Programme Implementation

121. Consider the following statements about 'Liquidity Trap':
1. Interest rates are very low.
2. Monetary policy becomes ineffective in stimulating demand.
3. People prefer to hold bonds rather than cash.
Which of the above is/are correct?
(a) 2 and 3 only (b) 1 and 2 only (c) 1 and 3 only (d) 1, 2 and 3
Answer: (b)
Solution: Statement 3 is the trap: in a liquidity trap people prefer holding cash, not bonds, because they expect interest rates to rise and bond prices to fall. Statements 1 and 2 correctly describe the Keynesian liquidity trap where near-zero rates make monetary policy powerless, so the answer is 1 and 2 only.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Macroeconomics

122. Arrange the following in chronological order of their establishment in India:
1. NABARD
2. RBI
3. SEBI
4. NITI Aayog
(a) 2, 1, 4, 3 (b) 1, 2, 3, 4 (c) 2, 3, 1, 4 (d) 2, 1, 3, 4
Answer: (d)
Solution: The chronological-confusion trap is placing SEBI before NABARD; SEBI was set up in 1988 (statutory in 1992), after NABARD. The correct order is RBI (1935), NABARD (1982), SEBI (1988), NITI Aayog (2015), so the answer is 2, 1, 3, 4.
Source: Official websites of RBI, NABARD, SEBI and NITI Aayog

123. If the Cash Reserve Ratio is increased by the RBI, the immediate effect is:
(a) Increase in lendable resources of banks (b) No effect on banks (c) Decrease in lendable resources of banks (d) Increase in fiscal deficit
Answer: (c)
Solution: Increase in lendable resources is the tempting wrong option because students confuse raising CRR with injecting liquidity; raising CRR actually locks more funds with the RBI. A higher CRR reduces the money banks can lend, contracting credit, so the answer is decrease in lendable resources of banks.
Source: RBI official website

124. Consider the following statements about 'Twin Deficits':
1. It refers to fiscal deficit and current account deficit occurring together.
2. A high fiscal deficit can worsen the current account deficit.
Which of the above is/are correct?
(a) 1 only (b) Both 1 and 2 (c) 2 only (d) Neither 1 nor 2
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is assuming the two deficits are unrelated and marking only statement 1; in fact, large government borrowing can raise demand and imports, widening the current account gap. Both statements correctly describe the twin deficit hypothesis, so the answer is both 1 and 2.
Source: Economic Survey, Government of India

125. 'Engel's Law' states that as income rises:
(a) Total spending on food rises proportionally (b) Savings fall (c) The proportion of income spent on food falls (d) Demand for luxuries falls
Answer: (c)
Solution: Total spending on food rising proportionally is the tempting wrong option, but Engel's Law is about proportions, not absolute amounts. It states that the share of income spent on food declines as income rises, even if absolute food spending grows, so the answer is the proportion of income spent on food falls.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Microeconomics
