# Economic Terms & Concepts

## The Hook: A Note That Became Worthless Overnight

In 1923, a German housewife needed a wheelbarrow full of banknotes just to buy a loaf of bread. Prices were doubling every few days. This was **hyperinflation** — one of the most dramatic economic concepts you will ever study. And here is the surprise: the same word "inflation" that destroyed savings in Germany appears in almost every SSC, Banking, and State PCS exam in India — sometimes directly, sometimes hidden inside a tricky statement.

Economics is not about memorising dry definitions. It is about understanding how money, markets, and governments behave — and examiners love testing exactly where students get confused. This chapter will make sure you never fall into those traps.

## Part 1: National Income — The Big Family (GDP, GNP, NNP)

### What is GDP?

**GDP (Gross Domestic Product)** is the total market value of all final goods and services produced **within the geographical boundary** of a country in one year.

Key word: **"Domestic" = within the country's borders.** It does not matter who produces it — an Indian company or a foreign company operating in India. If it is produced inside India, it counts in India's GDP.

### What is GNP?

**GNP (Gross National Product)** = GDP + Net Factor Income from Abroad (NFIA).

NFIA = income earned by Indians abroad **minus** income earned by foreigners in India.

Key word: **"National" = by the nation's people**, wherever they are in the world.

> **Simple formula to memorise:**
> **GNP = GDP + (Income of Indians abroad − Income of foreigners in India)**

### NNP and the Role of Depreciation

**NNP (Net National Product)** = GNP − Depreciation.

**Depreciation** (also called *consumption of fixed capital*) is the wear and tear of machines, buildings, and equipment during production.

- **Gross** = before removing depreciation
- **Net** = after removing depreciation

### Market Price vs Factor Cost

- **Market Price (MP):** includes indirect taxes, excludes subsidies.
- **Factor Cost (FC):** the actual cost of factors of production (land, labour, capital, entrepreneurship).

> **Factor Cost = Market Price − Indirect Taxes + Subsidies**

🧠 **Active Recall:** Without looking back — what is the difference between GDP and GNP in one line? What do we subtract from GNP to get NNP?

### Methods of Measuring National Income

1. **Product Method (Value Added Method)** — sum of value added at each production stage.
2. **Income Method** — sum of all factor incomes (wages + rent + interest + profit).
3. **Expenditure Method** — sum of all final spending (Consumption + Investment + Government spending + Net Exports), i.e., **C + I + G + (X − M)**.

### Who Calculates National Income in India?

The **Central Statistics Office (CSO)**, under the **Ministry of Statistics and Programme Implementation (MoSPI)**, calculates India's national income. India's GDP base year was revised to **2011–12** (a frequently asked fact).

## Part 2: Inflation and Its Family

### Inflation

**Inflation** is a sustained rise in the general price level of goods and services over time, which reduces the purchasing power of money.

Types by speed:
- **Creeping inflation:** slow, mild rise (around 2–3% per year).
- **Walking/Trotting inflation:** moderate (roughly 3–7%).
- **Running inflation:** fast (around 10–20%).
- **Hyperinflation (Galloping):** extremely rapid, out of control (prices rising 50%+ per month).

Types by cause:
- **Demand-Pull Inflation:** too much money chasing too few goods — demand exceeds supply.
- **Cost-Push Inflation:** production costs (wages, raw materials, fuel) rise, pushing prices up.

### The Confusing Cousins

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Deflation** | Sustained *fall* in the general price level |
| **Disinflation** | Inflation rate *slowing down* (prices still rising, but slower) |
| **Stagflation** | High inflation + high unemployment + stagnant growth (worst combination) |
| **Reflation** | Deliberate policy to expand output after a slowdown |

🧠 **Active Recall:** Your friend says "inflation came down, so prices fell." Is he right? (No — falling inflation means prices are rising more slowly, i.e., disinflation. Prices fall only in deflation.)

### Measuring Inflation in India

- **WPI (Wholesale Price Index):** measures price changes at the wholesale level; published by the **Office of the Economic Adviser, Ministry of Commerce and Industry**. Base year: **2011–12**.
- **CPI (Consumer Price Index):** measures retail prices paid by consumers; published by **NSO (National Statistical Office), MoSPI**. Base year: **2012**.
- RBI's inflation target under the monetary policy framework: **4% CPI inflation with a band of ±2%** (i.e., 2%–6%).

## Part 3: Banking & Monetary Policy Terms

### RBI — The Central Bank

The **Reserve Bank of India (RBI)** was established on **1 April 1935** under the RBI Act, 1934. It was **nationalised on 1 January 1949**. Headquarters: **Mumbai** (originally Kolkata).

### Key Policy Rates

- **Repo Rate:** The rate at which RBI lends short-term money to commercial banks against government securities. *Repo = Repurchase agreement.*
- **Reverse Repo Rate:** The rate at which RBI borrows money from banks (banks park their surplus funds with RBI).
- **CRR (Cash Reserve Ratio):** The percentage of a bank's total deposits (NDTL) that must be kept with the RBI **in cash**. Banks earn **no interest** on CRR.
- **SLR (Statutory Liquidity Ratio):** The percentage of deposits banks must maintain in the form of **liquid assets** — cash, gold, or approved government securities — with themselves.

> **Memory trick:** Repo = RBI **R**eceives interest (banks pay RBI). Reverse Repo = banks receive interest from RBI. CRR = **C**ash with RBI (no interest). SLR = **S**ecurities with the bank itself.

### How Rates Control Inflation

- Inflation high → RBI **increases** repo rate → loans become costly → less money in the market → demand falls → prices cool down.
- Growth slow → RBI **decreases** repo rate → cheaper loans → more spending → economy revives.

🧠 **Active Recall:** If RBI wants to reduce inflation, should it raise or cut the repo rate? What happens to CRR money — does the bank earn interest on it?

### Other Important Banking Terms

- **Bank Rate:** The long-term rate at which RBI lends to banks without securities; it signals long-term policy stance.
- **MSF (Marginal Standing Facility):** Emergency overnight borrowing window for banks against SLR securities, at a rate higher than repo.
- **Open Market Operations (OMO):** RBI buying/selling government securities to control money supply.
- **NPA (Non-Performing Asset):** A loan on which interest or principal remains overdue for **more than 90 days**.

## Part 4: Budget & Fiscal Terms

### The Union Budget

Presented under **Article 112** of the Constitution, where it is called the **"Annual Financial Statement."**

### Types of Deficits

- **Budget Deficit** = Total Expenditure − Total Receipts (rarely used now).
- **Revenue Deficit** = Revenue Expenditure − Revenue Receipts. Shows the government is borrowing for day-to-day expenses.
- **Fiscal Deficit** = Total Expenditure − Total Receipts (excluding borrowings). This is the **most watched** number — it equals the government's total borrowing requirement.
- **Primary Deficit** = Fiscal Deficit − Interest Payments. Shows the deficit of the *current* year's policies.

> **Mnemonic — "RF-P" ladder:** **R**evenue deficit is part of **F**iscal deficit; remove interest from Fiscal and you get **P**rimary. Remember: *"Revenue lives inside Fiscal; Primary is Fiscal minus Interest."*

### Direct vs Indirect Taxes

- **Direct Tax:** paid directly by the person on whom it is imposed — cannot be shifted. Examples: Income Tax, Corporation Tax.
- **Indirect Tax:** can be shifted to others. Examples: GST, Customs Duty.

**GST (Goods and Services Tax)** was launched on **1 July 2017** via the **101st Constitutional Amendment Act, 2016**. It is a destination-based indirect tax. The GST Council is a constitutional body under **Article 279A**.

## Part 5: Growth, Development & Miscellaneous Terms

- **Economic Growth:** quantitative increase in output/income (GDP rising).
- **Economic Development:** growth **plus** qualitative improvements — health, education, living standards.
- **HDI (Human Development Index):** published by **UNDP**; combines life expectancy, education, and per capita income.
- **Poverty Line in India:** historically estimated by the **Tendulkar Committee** (2009) and later the **Rangarajan Committee** (2014).
- **Gini Coefficient:** measures income inequality — 0 means perfect equality, 1 means perfect inequality.
- **Lorenz Curve:** the graphical curve behind the Gini coefficient.
- **Phillips Curve:** shows an inverse relationship between inflation and unemployment (short run).
- **Laffer Curve:** shows that beyond a point, higher tax rates reduce tax revenue.
- **Fiat Money:** currency with no intrinsic value, declared legal tender by government order (e.g., the rupee).
- **Legal Tender:** money that must be accepted by law for payment of debts.
- **Demonetisation:** withdrawal of a currency's legal tender status — India did this in **1946, 1978, and 2016**.

## 🧠 Mnemonics Corner

1. **"G-D-N" chain:** **G**ross → subtract **D**epreciation → **N**et. (Gross − Depreciation = Net, always.)
2. **"D for Door" (GDP):** **D**omestic = within the **door** (border) of the country. **N**ational = by the **nation's** people, anywhere.
3. **"CRR = Cash, RBI, Zero interest"** — three facts in one line.
4. **"Stagflation = Stagnant + Inflation"** — the word itself carries the definition.

## ⚠️ Caution: Examiner's Trap

**Trap 1 — "लगभग सही कथन" (Almost-Correct Statement):**
"CRR is maintained by banks with themselves in the form of cash and gold." — Sounds right, but this describes **SLR**. CRR is kept **with the RBI**, only in cash, and earns no interest. Examiners swap CRR and SLR features constantly.

**Trap 2 — "नकारात्मक वाक्यांश" (Negative Wording):**
"Which of the following is **NOT** a direct tax?" Students in a hurry miss the "NOT" and pick Income Tax. Slow down on every "NOT/EXCEPT" question — underline it mentally.

**Trap 3 — "समान नाम/अवधारणा" (Similar-Sounding Concepts):**
**Disinflation vs Deflation** — Disinflation = inflation rate falling (prices still rising); Deflation = prices actually falling. One word changes everything. Similarly, **Repo vs Reverse Repo** direction is frequently reversed in options.

## 🔗 Static-Dynamic Link

Repo rate, reverse repo rate, CRR, and SLR are **static concepts** but their **values change** whenever the RBI's Monetary Policy Committee (MPC) meets — roughly every two months (six times a year). For current values, always check the latest RBI monetary policy statement before your exam. The **MPC has 6 members** (3 from RBI, 3 appointed by the Government), headed by the RBI Governor — this structure is static and frequently asked.
