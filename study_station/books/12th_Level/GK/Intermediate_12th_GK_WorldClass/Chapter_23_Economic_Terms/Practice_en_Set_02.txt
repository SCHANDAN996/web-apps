Questions 26–50: Medium

26. Which of the following best describes 'stagflation'?
(a) High growth with low inflation (b) Falling prices with rising output (c) Stagnant growth combined with high inflation and unemployment (d) Rapid inflation caused by excess demand
Answer: (c)
Solution: Many students pick the excess-demand option because inflation is involved, but that describes demand-pull inflation, not stagflation. Stagflation is the unusual combination of a stagnant economy, high unemployment and high inflation together, so the answer is stagnant growth combined with high inflation and unemployment.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Economics (Introductory Macroeconomics)

27. The 'base year' in an index number is the year:
(a) in which the index is first published (b) against which prices of other years are compared (c) with the highest price level (d) chosen by the Finance Ministry every year
Answer: (b)
Solution: The tempting wrong idea is that the base year is the year of first publication; publication and comparison are different things. A base year is simply the reference year whose price level is set at 100 for comparing other years, so the answer is the year against which prices of other years are compared.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Statistics for Economics

28. Which tax is an example of a direct tax in India?
(a) Goods and Services Tax (b) Customs duty (c) Income tax (d) Excise duty
Answer: (c)
Solution: GST, customs and excise are all levied on goods and services and can be shifted to consumers, which makes them indirect taxes — a common trap. Income tax is paid directly by the person on whom it is imposed and cannot be shifted, so the answer is income tax.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Economics (Government Budget)

29. 'Fiscal deficit' of the government equals:
(a) Total expenditure minus total revenue receipts (b) Total receipts minus total expenditure (c) Revenue expenditure minus revenue receipts (d) Total expenditure minus total receipts excluding borrowings
Answer: (d)
Solution: The near-correct trap is subtracting only revenue receipts, which gives the revenue deficit, not the fiscal deficit. Fiscal deficit is total expenditure minus total receipts excluding borrowings, so the answer is total expenditure minus total receipts excluding borrowings.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Economics (Government Budget)

30. The Phillips curve shows the relationship between:
(a) Inflation and unemployment (b) Inflation and economic growth (c) Interest rates and savings (d) Exports and imports
Answer: (a)
Solution: Students often confuse it with a growth-inflation trade-off, but the original Phillips curve plots wage/price inflation against the unemployment rate, showing an inverse short-run relationship. So the answer is inflation and unemployment.
Source: PYQ-style

31. Which of the following is NOT a function of money?
(a) Medium of exchange (b) Creation of fiscal deficit (c) Measure of value (d) Store of value
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap here is the negative wording — three options are genuine textbook functions of money. Fiscal deficit is a budget concept of the government, not a function of money, so the answer is creation of fiscal deficit.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Economics (Money and Banking)

32. 'Repo rate' is the rate at which:
(a) Government borrows from RBI (b) Commercial banks lend to the public (c) RBI borrows from commercial banks (d) RBI lends short-term funds to commercial banks
Answer: (d)
Solution: The classic trap is confusing repo with reverse repo; reverse repo is the rate at which RBI absorbs funds from banks. Repo is the rate at which RBI lends short-term money to banks against securities, so the answer is RBI lends short-term funds to commercial banks.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Economics (Money and Banking)

33. The 'Laffer curve' relates tax rates to:
(a) Tax revenue (b) Inflation (c) Unemployment (d) Money supply
Answer: (a)
Solution: It is often confused with curves about inflation or unemployment, but the Laffer curve specifically shows that beyond a certain tax rate, higher rates reduce total tax revenue. So the answer is tax revenue.
Source: PYQ-style

34. Which of the following is a 'flow' variable?
(a) National income (b) Wealth of a household (c) Capital stock of a firm (d) Money supply on a date
Answer: (a)
Solution: Wealth, capital stock and money supply are measured at a point of time, making them stock variables — the tempting wrong choices. National income is measured over a period of time, so the answer is national income.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Economics (National Income Accounting)

35. 'Disguised unemployment' is most commonly found in:
(a) The industrial sector (b) The IT sector (c) The banking sector (d) The agricultural sector
Answer: (d)
Solution: A frequent trap is to link it to industry, but disguised unemployment — where more workers are engaged than actually needed — is characteristic of Indian agriculture, especially family farms. So the answer is the agricultural sector.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Indian Economic Development

36. If the marginal propensity to consume (MPC) is 0.8, the value of the investment multiplier is:
(a) 5 (b) 4 (c) 8 (d) 0.2
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is dividing by MPC itself, which gives 1.25, or confusing it with 1/MPS errors. Multiplier = 1/(1 − MPC) = 1/0.2 = 5, so the answer is 5.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Economics (Income Determination)

37. 'Balance of Payments' records:
(a) Only exports and imports of goods (b) Only capital flows of the government (c) All economic transactions between residents of a country and the rest of the world in a year (d) The government's budget transactions
Answer: (c)
Solution: The near-correct trap is limiting it to goods trade, which is only the visible trade balance. BoP is a comprehensive record of all economic transactions with the rest of the world during a year, so the answer is all economic transactions between residents of a country and the rest of the world in a year.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Economics (Open Economy Macroeconomics)

38. Which of the following is included in the calculation of GDP?
(a) Value of services of a paid domestic worker (b) Sale of second-hand goods (c) Transfer payments like old-age pensions (d) Purchase of shares in the stock market
Answer: (a)
Solution: Second-hand sales, pensions and share purchases are excluded because they involve no current production — each is a tempting trap. Paid domestic service is a currently produced service, so the answer is value of services of a paid domestic worker.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Economics (National Income Accounting)

39. 'Depreciation' of a currency occurs when:
(a) Its value falls under a flexible exchange rate system (b) The government officially lowers its value under a fixed rate system (c) Its value rises under a flexible system (d) RBI prints more currency notes
Answer: (a)
Solution: The chronological/terminological trap is confusing depreciation with devaluation; devaluation is an official reduction under a fixed rate regime. A market-driven fall in value under flexible rates is depreciation, so the answer is its value falls under a flexible exchange rate system.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Economics (Open Economy Macroeconomics)

40. The 'Gini coefficient' is used to measure:
(a) Inflation (b) Income inequality (c) Unemployment (d) Foreign trade balance
Answer: (b)
Solution: It is often confused with poverty ratios, but the Gini coefficient specifically measures inequality of income distribution, ranging from 0 (perfect equality) to 1 (perfect inequality). So the answer is income inequality.
Source: PYQ-style

41. Which of the following is a capital receipt of the government?
(a) Income tax collection (b) GST collection (c) Disinvestment proceeds from selling PSU shares (d) Interest received on loans given to states
Answer: (c)
Solution: Taxes and interest are recurring revenue receipts — the tempting wrong options. Disinvestment reduces government assets and is a capital receipt, so the answer is disinvestment proceeds from selling PSU shares.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Economics (Government Budget)

42. 'Liquidity trap', a term given by Keynes, refers to a situation where:
(a) Banks have no money to lend (b) Foreign exchange reserves are exhausted (c) Inflation is extremely high (d) Interest rates are so low that monetary policy becomes ineffective
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is reading it literally as banks lacking cash. In a liquidity trap, interest rates are near zero and people prefer holding cash, so further monetary expansion fails to stimulate the economy; hence the answer is interest rates are so low that monetary policy becomes ineffective.
Source: PYQ-style

43. Which organisation releases the 'World Development Report'?
(a) International Monetary Fund (b) World Bank (c) World Trade Organization (d) United Nations Development Programme
Answer: (b)
Solution: The similar-names trap is strong here: IMF publishes the World Economic Outlook and UNDP publishes the Human Development Report. The World Development Report is a flagship publication of the World Bank, so the answer is World Bank.
Source: PYQ-style

44. 'Core inflation' excludes which of the following?
(a) Manufactured goods (b) Housing (c) Services (d) Food and fuel items
Answer: (d)
Solution: Students often think core inflation excludes manufactured goods, but it actually strips out volatile food and fuel prices to reveal the underlying inflation trend. So the answer is food and fuel items.
Source: PYQ-style

45. If both GNP and GDP of a country are equal, it implies:
(a) The country has no foreign trade (b) The country has zero inflation (c) Net factor income from abroad is zero (d) The country has no taxes
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is assuming equality means no trade; trade affects the current account, not the GNP–GDP gap directly. GNP = GDP + net factor income from abroad, so equality means net factor income from abroad is zero, and the answer is net factor income from abroad is zero.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Economics (National Income Accounting)

46. 'Open market operations' by the RBI involve:
(a) Fixing interest rates for bank deposits (b) Buying and selling government securities (c) Printing new currency notes (d) Regulating stock exchanges
Answer: (b)
Solution: The tempting confusion is with the repo rate, but that is a policy rate, not open market operations. OMO means RBI buying or selling government securities to control liquidity, so the answer is buying and selling government securities.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Economics (Money and Banking)

47. Which of the following pairs is correctly matched?
(a) Microeconomics — study of national income (b) Macroeconomics — study of individual consumer behaviour (c) Microeconomics — study of individual markets and firms (d) Macroeconomics — study of a single firm's pricing
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap options swap the two branches: national income and economy-wide aggregates belong to macroeconomics, while individual consumers, firms and markets belong to microeconomics. So the answer is microeconomics — study of individual markets and firms.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Economics (Introductory Microeconomics)

48. 'Poverty line' in India was historically estimated using:
(a) Per capita income only (b) Calorie-based consumption expenditure (c) Literacy rates (d) Land ownership records
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is per capita income, which measures average income, not poverty. Indian poverty estimation (Lakdawala and Tendulkar committees) was based on consumption expenditure linked to calorie norms, so the answer is calorie-based consumption expenditure.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Indian Economic Development

49. 'Crowding out' effect refers to:
(a) Government borrowing reducing private investment by raising interest rates (b) Foreign firms driving out domestic firms (c) Population growth reducing per capita income (d) Imports replacing domestic production
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is the trade-based interpretation, but crowding out is a fiscal concept: heavy government borrowing pushes up interest rates and squeezes private investment. So the answer is government borrowing reducing private investment by raising interest rates.
Source: PYQ-style

50. Which of the following is the correct sequence of sectors by stage of economic activity?
(a) Primary → Tertiary → Secondary (b) Tertiary → Secondary → Primary (c) Secondary → Primary → Tertiary (d) Primary → Secondary → Tertiary
Answer: (d)
Solution: The chronological-confusion trap places services before industry, but economies historically move from agriculture (primary) to manufacturing (secondary) and then to services (tertiary). So the answer is Primary → Secondary → Tertiary.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Indian Economic Development
