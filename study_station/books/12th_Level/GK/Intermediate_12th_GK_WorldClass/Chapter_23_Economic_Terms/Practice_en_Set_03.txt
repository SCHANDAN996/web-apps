Questions 51–75: Medium

51. Which of the following best describes "stagflation"?
(a) High growth with high inflation (b) Falling prices with rising employment (c) Stagnant growth combined with high inflation and unemployment (d) Rapid growth with falling prices
Answer: (c)
Solution: The tempting wrong choice is high growth with inflation, which is simply demand-pull inflation during a boom; stagflation is the unusual combination of a stagnant economy, high inflation and rising unemployment, as seen in the 1970s oil-shock economies. So the answer is stagnant growth combined with high inflation and unemployment.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Macroeconomics

52. The "Laffer Curve" illustrates the relationship between:
(a) Inflation and unemployment (b) Tax rates and tax revenue (c) Money supply and price level (d) Interest rates and investment
Answer: (b)
Solution: Inflation-unemployment is the Phillips Curve, a classic similar-name trap; the Laffer Curve shows that beyond a certain tax rate, revenue falls because higher rates discourage work and encourage evasion. So the answer is tax rates and tax revenue.
Source: PYQ-style

53. Which of the following is NOT a function of money?
(a) Creation of credit (b) Measure of value (c) Standard of deferred payment (d) Medium of exchange
Answer: (a)
Solution: Creation of credit is a function of commercial banks, not of money itself — a common trap where a banking function is mixed into money's functions; money's four functions are medium of exchange, measure of value, store of value and standard of deferred payment. So the answer is creation of credit.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Macroeconomics

54. "Disguised unemployment" is most commonly found in India in:
(a) The agricultural sector (b) The manufacturing sector (c) The services sector (d) The mining sector
Answer: (a)
Solution: Manufacturing and services can have open unemployment, but disguised unemployment — where more workers are engaged than actually needed and marginal productivity is near zero — is characteristic of Indian agriculture with family farms. So the answer is the agricultural sector.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Indian Economic Development

55. If the Cash Reserve Ratio is increased by the RBI, the immediate effect is:
(a) Decrease in lending capacity of banks (b) Increase in lending capacity of banks (c) No change in money supply (d) Increase in fiscal deficit
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is assuming a higher CRR helps banks lend more; in fact a higher CRR means banks must keep more cash with the RBI, leaving less to lend, which contracts money supply. So the answer is decrease in lending capacity of banks.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Macroeconomics

56. "Giffen goods" are goods for which:
(a) Demand falls when income rises (b) Supply rises when price falls (c) Demand is perfectly elastic (d) Demand rises when price rises
Answer: (d)
Solution: The tempting trap is "demand falls when income rises," which describes inferior goods generally; Giffen goods are a special case where the income effect outweighs the substitution effect, so demand rises even when price rises. So the answer is demand rises when price rises.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Microeconomics

57. Which of the following is included in the calculation of GDP?
(a) Sale of second-hand goods (b) Government expenditure on new infrastructure (c) Services of a housewife (d) Value of intermediate goods
Answer: (b)
Solution: Second-hand sales, intermediate goods and unpaid household work are all excluded to avoid double counting or because they are non-market; only final goods and services count, so government spending on new infrastructure is included. So the answer is government expenditure on new infrastructure.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Macroeconomics

58. "Fiscal drag" refers to:
(a) Increased tax burden due to inflation pushing incomes into higher tax brackets (b) The slowing of the economy due to excessive government borrowing (c) Reduction in exports due to a strong currency (d) Delay in passing the budget
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is linking it to government borrowing, which relates to crowding out; fiscal drag occurs when inflation moves nominal incomes into higher tax slabs, raising the tax burden and dampening demand. So the answer is increased tax burden due to inflation pushing incomes into higher tax brackets.
Source: PYQ-style

59. The "Phillips Curve" shows an inverse relationship between:
(a) Tax rates and revenue (b) Inflation and unemployment (c) Savings and investment (d) Exports and imports
Answer: (b)
Solution: Tax rates and revenue belong to the Laffer Curve — the classic similar-name confusion examiners exploit; the Phillips Curve depicts the short-run trade-off between inflation and unemployment. So the answer is inflation and unemployment.
Source: PYQ-style

60. "Core inflation" in India excludes:
(a) Fuel and manufactured goods (b) Services and housing (c) Food and fuel items (d) Gold and silver
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is thinking fuel is included in core inflation; core inflation strips out volatile food and fuel components to reveal the underlying, persistent price trend. So the answer is food and fuel items.
Source: PYQ-style

61. Which of the following best defines "opportunity cost"?
(a) The value of the next best alternative foregone (b) The money spent on a good (c) The total cost of production (d) The sunk cost of a project
Answer: (a)
Solution: The tempting wrong option is the money spent, which is accounting cost; opportunity cost is the value of the best alternative you give up when making a choice. So the answer is the value of the next best alternative foregone.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Microeconomics

62. "Helicopter money" refers to:
(a) Money printed to finance defence purchases (b) Emergency loans given to banks (c) Foreign aid received by a country (d) Direct distribution of money to the public to stimulate demand
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is confusing it with bank bailouts or defence financing; helicopter money, a term popularised by Milton Friedman, means printing money and distributing it directly to citizens to boost demand. So the answer is direct distribution of money to the public to stimulate demand.
Source: PYQ-style

63. In the context of banking, "NPA" stands for:
(a) New Performing Asset (b) National Payment Authority (c) Net Profit Account (d) Non-Performing Asset
Answer: (d)
Solution: "New Performing Asset" is a fabricated near-correct trap; an NPA is a loan where interest or principal remains overdue for more than 90 days, making it a non-performing asset. So the answer is Non-Performing Asset.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Macroeconomics

64. Which of the following is an example of "transfer payment"?
(a) Salary of a government teacher (b) Old-age pension paid by the government (c) Purchase of defence equipment (d) Payment for construction of a road
Answer: (b)
Solution: Salaries and purchases involve goods or services in return, so they count in national income; transfer payments like old-age pensions involve no current production in exchange and are excluded from GDP. So the answer is old-age pension paid by the government.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Macroeconomics

65. "Demonetisation" in India in 2016 involved the withdrawal of which denominations?
(a) ₹1000 and ₹2000 (b) ₹100 and ₹500 (c) ₹500 and ₹1000 (d) ₹500 and ₹2000
Answer: (c)
Solution: The chronological trap is ₹2000, which was introduced after demonetisation, not withdrawn; on 8 November 2016 the ₹500 and ₹1000 notes ceased to be legal tender. So the answer is ₹500 and ₹1000.
Source: PYQ-style

66. The "multiplier effect" in economics refers to:
(a) The proportionate increase in national income resulting from an initial increase in investment (b) The rise in prices due to excess money supply (c) The increase in tax revenue from higher tax rates (d) The growth of bank deposits through lending
Answer: (a)
Solution: The deposit-growth option describes the credit multiplier of banks, a near-miss trap; the Keynesian investment multiplier shows that an initial rise in investment leads to a larger, multiplied rise in national income. So the answer is the proportionate increase in national income resulting from an initial increase in investment.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Macroeconomics

67. "Purchasing Power Parity" (PPP) is used to compare:
(a) Interest rates across countries (b) Stock market performance across countries (c) Standards of living and real income across countries (d) Tax structures across countries
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is treating PPP as an exchange-rate or market tool; PPP compares what a common basket of goods costs in different countries, giving a truer comparison of real incomes and living standards than nominal exchange rates. So the answer is standards of living and real income across countries.
Source: PYQ-style

68. Which of the following is a "regressive tax"?
(a) Income tax with rising slabs (b) Wealth tax (c) A uniform sales tax on essential goods (d) Corporate profit tax
Answer: (c)
Solution: Income tax with rising slabs is progressive, the tempting confusion; a uniform sales tax on essentials takes a larger share of a poor person's income than a rich person's, making it regressive. So the answer is a uniform sales tax on essential goods.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Indian Economic Development

69. "Liquidity trap" occurs when:
(a) Interest rates are so low that monetary policy becomes ineffective (b) Banks have excess liquidity but no borrowers (c) The government runs out of foreign exchange (d) Inflation rises despite high unemployment
Answer: (a)
Solution: The stagflation-like option is a distractor; a liquidity trap, described by Keynes, is a situation where interest rates are near zero, people prefer holding cash, and further monetary easing fails to stimulate the economy. So the answer is interest rates are so low that monetary policy becomes ineffective.
Source: PYQ-style

70. The "Base Year" for the current GDP series in India is:
(a) 2004-05 (b) 2011-12 (c) 2014-15 (d) 2017-18
Answer: (b)
Solution: The chronological trap is 2004-05, which was the earlier base year; the CSO revised the GDP series with base year 2011-12 in January 2015. So the answer is 2011-12.
Source: PYQ-style

71. "Crowding out" in economics means:
(a) Population pressure reducing per capita income (b) Foreign firms driving out domestic firms (c) Private investment falls because government borrowing raises interest rates (d) Excess imports reducing domestic production
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trade-related options are distractors playing on the phrase; crowding out occurs when heavy government borrowing pushes up interest rates, making loans costlier and reducing private investment. So the answer is private investment falls because government borrowing raises interest rates.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Macroeconomics

72. Which of the following is NOT a direct tax?
(a) Income tax (b) Corporation tax (c) Wealth tax (when it was in force) (d) Goods and Services Tax
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is assuming all taxes collected by the government are direct; GST is an indirect tax because its burden is passed on to the final consumer, unlike income or corporation tax paid directly by the earner. So the answer is Goods and Services Tax.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Indian Economic Development

73. "Marginal cost" is defined as:
(a) Total cost divided by output (b) The addition to total cost from producing one more unit (c) Fixed cost per unit (d) The cost of the first unit produced
Answer: (b)
Solution: Total cost divided by output is average cost — the classic near-correct trap; marginal cost measures the extra cost incurred in producing one additional unit of output. So the answer is the addition to total cost from producing one more unit.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Microeconomics

74. "Twin deficit" problem refers to the simultaneous existence of:
(a) Fiscal deficit and revenue deficit (b) Trade deficit and budget surplus (c) Revenue deficit and capital deficit (d) Fiscal deficit and current account deficit
Answer: (d)
Solution: Fiscal and revenue deficit sound plausible but both are part of the same budget — a near-miss trap; the twin deficit problem means a country faces both a fiscal deficit and a current account deficit together. So the answer is fiscal deficit and current account deficit.
Source: PYQ-style

75. "Engel's Law" states that as income rises:
(a) Expenditure on luxury goods falls (b) The proportion of income spent on food rises (c) Total savings fall (d) The proportion of income spent on food falls
Answer: (d)
Solution: The reversed statement is the tempting trap; Engel's Law observes that as household income increases, the percentage of income spent on food declines even if absolute food spending rises. So the answer is the proportion of income spent on food falls.
Source: PYQ-style
