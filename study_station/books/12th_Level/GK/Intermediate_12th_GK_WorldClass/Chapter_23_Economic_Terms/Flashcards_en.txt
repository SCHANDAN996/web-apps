Card 1
Front: If prices are rising but your salary stays the same, what is quietly shrinking?
Back: Your real income. Inflation reduces purchasing power — nominal income may be unchanged, but real income (what money can actually buy) falls.

Card 2
Front: Which "GDP" counts only goods and services produced within India's borders, even by foreign companies?
Back: GDP (Gross Domestic Product). GNP, in contrast, counts production by a country's nationals, wherever they are located.

Card 3
Front: What is the trick to remember the difference between GDP and GNP?
Back: Mnemonic: "D = Domestic (place), N = Nationals (people)." GDP → where it's produced; GNP → who produces it. GNP = GDP + Net Factor Income from Abroad (NFIA).

Card 4
Front: A country's central bank raises the repo rate. Is it trying to fight inflation or deflation?
Back: Inflation. A higher repo rate makes borrowing costlier for banks, reducing money flow and cooling demand-pull inflation.

Card 5
Front: Examiner's trap: "Repo rate is the rate at which RBI lends to banks for the long term." True or false?
Back: False. Repo is short-term (overnight/short duration) lending against securities. Long-term lending to banks happens at the Bank Rate. Examiners swap these two to trap you.

Card 6
Front: Which inflation measure tracks prices at the factory gate, before goods reach shops?
Back: WPI (Wholesale Price Index). CPI (Consumer Price Index) tracks retail prices paid by consumers — and CPI is what RBI uses for inflation targeting.

Card 7
Front: What is "stagflation" — and why is it a policymaker's nightmare?
Back: Stagnation + inflation together: high inflation with high unemployment and slow growth. Normal tools fail — cutting rates worsens inflation, raising rates worsens unemployment.

Card 8
Front: When the government spends more than it earns (excluding borrowings), what is that gap called?
Back: Fiscal deficit. Formula: Total expenditure − Total receipts (excluding borrowings). It shows how much the government must borrow.

Card 9
Front: Fiscal deficit minus interest payments gives which "cleaner" deficit figure?
Back: Primary deficit. It shows the current year's borrowing need, excluding the burden of past loans (interest payments).

Card 10
Front: Which tax can the payer not pass on to anyone else — direct or indirect?
Back: Direct tax (e.g., income tax). Indirect taxes (like GST) are paid by one person but the burden is shifted to the final consumer.

Card 11
Front: Memory trick: how to instantly classify a tax as direct or indirect?
Back: Mnemonic: "Direct = Directly from your pocket; Indirect = In the price tag." If the tax hides inside the price of a product (GST), it's indirect.

Card 12
Front: What do we call a budget where government revenue exactly equals its expenditure?
Back: A balanced budget. If revenue exceeds expenditure → surplus budget; if expenditure exceeds revenue → deficit budget.

Card 13
Front: Which institution is called the "lender of last resort" in India?
Back: The Reserve Bank of India (RBI). It lends to banks when no one else will, preventing banking panics.

Card 14
Front: What is "disinvestment" — selling government shares or buying private companies?
Back: Selling government equity (shares) in public sector undertakings (PSUs) to private buyers or the public. It raises revenue and can improve efficiency.

Card 15
Front: Examiner's trap: "Devaluation and depreciation of currency are the same thing." True or false?
Back: False. Devaluation is a deliberate government/central bank action (under fixed exchange rates); depreciation is a market-driven fall in value (under floating rates). Exams love swapping these.

Card 16
Front: What does "balance of payments" record that "balance of trade" does not?
Back: BoP records ALL economic transactions with the rest of the world — goods, services, income, transfers, and capital flows. Balance of trade covers only exports and imports of goods.

Card 17
Front: If a bank keeps ₹4 of every ₹100 deposit with RBI as cash, which tool is this?
Back: CRR (Cash Reserve Ratio) — the portion of deposits banks must keep with RBI in cash. SLR is the portion kept with themselves in liquid assets like gold and government securities.

Card 18
Front: What is "per capita income" and how is it calculated?
Back: National income divided by total population. It gives the average income per person and is used to compare living standards across countries.

Card 19
Front: Which poverty line concept measures the minimum calories/money needed for basic survival?
Back: The poverty line based on consumption expenditure (historically calorie-based in India — e.g., Tendulkar and Rangarajan committees estimated it using consumption data).

Card 20
Front: What is "GDP at constant prices" called, and why do economists prefer it?
Back: Real GDP. It removes the effect of price changes (inflation), so it shows true growth in output — unlike nominal GDP (current prices), which can rise just because prices rose.
