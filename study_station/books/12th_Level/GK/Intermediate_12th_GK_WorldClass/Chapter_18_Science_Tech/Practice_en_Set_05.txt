101. Which of the following is the correct increasing order of the wavelengths of electromagnetic waves?
(a) Infrared < Visible < Ultraviolet < X-rays (b) Ultraviolet < X-rays < Visible < Infrared (c) Visible < Ultraviolet < X-rays < Infrared (d) X-rays < Ultraviolet < Visible < Infrared
Answer: (d)
Solution: The tempting option reverses the order of X-rays and ultraviolet; X-rays have much shorter wavelengths than ultraviolet. The full order from shortest to longest wavelength is: gamma rays, X-rays, ultraviolet, visible, infrared, microwaves, radio waves, so the answer is X-rays < Ultraviolet < Visible < Infrared.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Physics

102. The pH value of human blood is approximately:
(a) 7.4 (b) 6.4 (c) 8.4 (d) 5.4
Answer: (a)
Solution: A common trap is 7.0, which is the pH of pure water, not blood. Human blood is slightly alkaline, maintained by buffer systems at about 7.35–7.45, so the answer is 7.4.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science

103. Which vitamin is synthesized in the human skin on exposure to sunlight?
(a) Vitamin A (b) Vitamin C (c) Vitamin D (d) Vitamin K
Answer: (c)
Solution: Vitamin K is a tempting distractor because it is also synthesized in the body, but it is made by intestinal bacteria, not by skin in sunlight. Ultraviolet rays convert 7-dehydrocholesterol in the skin into Vitamin D, so the answer is Vitamin D.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Biology

104. The SI unit of electric resistivity is:
(a) Ohm (b) Siemens (c) Ohm per metre (d) Ohm-metre
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is "Ohm", which is the unit of resistance, not resistivity; resistivity is a material property independent of dimensions. Since resistivity ρ = RA/L, its unit is ohm × metre²/metre, so the answer is Ohm-metre.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science

105. Which of the following diseases is caused by a virus?
(a) Tuberculosis (b) Cholera (c) Typhoid (d) Measles
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap options are all bacterial diseases: tuberculosis (Mycobacterium), cholera (Vibrio cholerae) and typhoid (Salmonella typhi). Measles is caused by the measles virus (a Morbillivirus), so the answer is Measles.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Science

106. In a nuclear reactor, the function of a moderator is to:
(a) Absorb excess neutrons (b) Cool the reactor core (c) Slow down fast neutrons (d) Produce neutrons
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is "absorb excess neutrons", which is the job of control rods (cadmium/boron), not the moderator. Moderators like heavy water or graphite slow fast neutrons to thermal speeds so fission can be sustained, so the answer is Slow down fast neutrons.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Physics

107. Which of the following is used as a moderator in the CANDU-type nuclear reactors?
(a) Ordinary water (b) Liquid sodium (c) Graphite only (d) Heavy water
Answer: (d)
Solution: Ordinary water is tempting because it moderates in many reactors, but it absorbs too many neutrons to work with natural uranium. CANDU reactors use heavy water (D₂O) as both moderator and coolant, so the answer is Heavy water.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Physics

108. The phenomenon of total internal reflection is the working principle of:
(a) Optical fibre (b) Periscope (c) Kaleidoscope (d) Simple microscope
Answer: (a)
Solution: A periscope is the tempting wrong choice, but it works on simple reflection by plane mirrors, not total internal reflection. Optical fibres guide light by repeated total internal reflection at the core-cladding boundary, so the answer is Optical fibre.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Physics

109. Which of the following pairs is correctly matched?
(a) Thyroxine — Thyroid gland (b) Insulin — Pituitary gland (c) Adrenaline — Pancreas (d) Glucagon — Adrenal gland
Answer: (a)
Solution: The traps swap the glands: insulin and glucagon come from the pancreas (islets of Langerhans), and adrenaline from the adrenal medulla. Thyroxine is secreted by the thyroid gland, so the answer is Thyroxine — Thyroid gland.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science

110. The escape velocity from the Earth's surface is approximately:
(a) 11.2 km/s (b) 7.9 km/s (c) 9.8 km/s (d) 3.0 km/s
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is 7.9 km/s, which is the orbital velocity of a near-Earth satellite, not the escape velocity. Escape velocity is √2 times the orbital velocity, about 11.2 km/s, so the answer is 11.2 km/s.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Physics

111. Which of the following is NOT a greenhouse gas?
(a) Carbon dioxide (b) Nitrogen (c) Nitrous oxide (d) Methane
Answer: (b)
Solution: Nitrous oxide is the trap — its name sounds like nitrogen, but N₂O is a potent greenhouse gas while N₂ is not. Nitrogen makes up about 78% of the atmosphere and does not absorb infrared radiation, so the answer is Nitrogen.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Chemistry

112. The chemical name of baking soda is:
(a) Sodium bicarbonate (b) Sodium carbonate (c) Calcium carbonate (d) Sodium hydroxide
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is sodium carbonate, which is washing soda (Na₂CO₃), not baking soda. Baking soda is sodium bicarbonate (NaHCO₃), so the answer is Sodium bicarbonate.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science

113. Which blood group is called the universal donor?
(a) O negative (b) AB positive (c) AB negative (d) O positive
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is AB positive, which is the universal recipient, not the donor. O negative blood has neither A/B antigens nor the Rh antigen, so it can be given to anyone, so the answer is O negative.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Biology

114. The working of a transformer is based on the principle of:
(a) Self-induction (b) Mutual induction (c) Magnetic effect of current (d) Heating effect of current
Answer: (b)
Solution: Self-induction is the tempting distractor, but a transformer needs two coils where a changing current in one induces EMF in the other. This is mutual induction, so the answer is Mutual induction.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Physics

115. Which of the following is the lightest metal?
(a) Aluminium (b) Magnesium (c) Sodium (d) Lithium
Answer: (d)
Solution: Aluminium is a common trap because it is famously "light" in everyday use, but its density (2.7 g/cm³) is far higher than lithium's (0.53 g/cm³). Lithium is the lightest metal, so the answer is Lithium.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science

116. The enzyme present in human saliva that digests starch is:
(a) Pepsin (b) Trypsin (c) Amylase (d) Lipase
Answer: (c)
Solution: Pepsin is the trap — it is a protein-digesting enzyme of the stomach, not saliva. Salivary amylase (ptyalin) breaks starch into sugars in the mouth, so the answer is Amylase.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science

117. Which of the following waves is used in radar systems?
(a) Infrared waves (b) Ultraviolet waves (c) Microwaves (d) Sound waves
Answer: (c)
Solution: Sound waves are the trap because sonar (not radar) uses them. Radar uses microwaves (radio-frequency electromagnetic waves) that reflect off objects, so the answer is Microwaves.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Physics

118. The deficiency of iodine in the human body causes:
(a) Night blindness (b) Anaemia (c) Scurvy (d) Goitre
Answer: (d)
Solution: Anaemia is the trap, but it is caused by iron deficiency, not iodine. Iodine deficiency reduces thyroxine production and enlarges the thyroid gland, causing goitre, so the answer is Goitre.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science

119. Which of the following is an example of a non-volatile memory?
(a) RAM (b) ROM (c) Cache memory (d) Register
Answer: (b)
Solution: RAM is the tempting wrong answer, but it loses all data when power is switched off, i.e., it is volatile. ROM retains its contents without power, so the answer is ROM.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Computer Science (Computer Fundamentals)

120. The process of conversion of a solid directly into vapour is called:
(a) Evaporation (b) Sublimation (c) Condensation (d) Fusion
Answer: (b)
Solution: Evaporation is the trap, but it is liquid-to-vapour conversion, not solid-to-vapour. Direct conversion of solid to vapour, as in camphor or dry ice, is sublimation, so the answer is Sublimation.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Science

121. Consider the following statements about LASER:
1. LASER light is coherent.
2. LASER light is monochromatic.
3. LASER stands for Light Amplification by Spontaneous Emission of Radiation.
Which of the statements given above is/are correct?
(a) 2 and 3 only (b) 1 and 2 only (c) 1 and 3 only (d) 1, 2 and 3
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is statement 3, which is almost correct — but LASER works by Stimulated Emission, not Spontaneous Emission; this single-word swap is a classic examiner's trick. Statements 1 and 2 are correct properties of laser light, so the answer is 1 and 2 only.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Physics

122. Arrange the following scientists in the correct chronological order of their discoveries/inventions:
1. Alexander Fleming — Penicillin
2. Edward Jenner — Smallpox vaccine
3. Robert Koch — Tuberculosis bacterium
(a) 2, 3, 1 (b) 2, 1, 3 (c) 3, 2, 1 (d) 1, 3, 2
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is placing Fleming before Koch; Fleming discovered penicillin in 1928, while Koch identified the TB bacillus in 1882. Jenner's smallpox vaccine (1796) came first, so the correct order is 2, 3, 1.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Biology

123. Which of the following statements about sound waves is correct?
(a) Sound travels faster in air than in steel (b) Sound waves are transverse waves in air (c) Sound cannot travel through vacuum (d) Sound waves can be polarised in air
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is the polarisation statement — only transverse waves can be polarised, and sound in air is longitudinal, so it cannot be polarised; sound also travels faster in steel than in air. Being a mechanical wave, sound needs a medium and cannot travel through vacuum, so the answer is Sound cannot travel through vacuum.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Physics

124. Match the following units with the quantities they measure:
1. Parsec — P. Distance
2. Decibel — Q. Intensity of sound
3. Fathom — R. Depth of water
(a) 1-P, 2-R, 3-Q (b) 1-Q, 2-P, 3-R (c) 1-P, 2-Q, 3-R (d) 1-R, 2-Q, 3-P
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is confusing parsec with a unit of time because of the word "second" in it; a parsec is actually an astronomical distance unit (about 3.26 light-years). Decibel measures sound intensity and fathom measures water depth, so the answer is 1-P, 2-Q, 3-R.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Physics

125. Consider the following statements about antibiotics:
1. Antibiotics are effective against viral infections.
2. Penicillin was the first antibiotic discovered.
3. Overuse of antibiotics can lead to antibiotic resistance.
Which of the statements given above is/are correct?
(a) 2 and 3 only (b) 1 and 2 only (c) 1 and 3 only (d) 1, 2 and 3
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is statement 1 — antibiotics kill or inhibit bacteria and are useless against viruses like those causing the common cold or flu. Penicillin (Fleming, 1928) was the first antibiotic, and misuse does breed resistance, so the answer is 2 and 3 only.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Biology
