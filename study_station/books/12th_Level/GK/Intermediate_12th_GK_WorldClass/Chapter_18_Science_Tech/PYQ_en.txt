# 📊 PYQ Analysis — Science & Technology (Basic)

## 1. How Exams Ask From This Chapter

Science & Technology is one of the most dependable scoring areas across SSC (CGL, CHSL, MTS, GD), RRB (NTPC, Group D), Banking (IBPS, SBI) and State PCS prelims. The questions are almost entirely **factual and NCERT-based** — the examiner rarely tests deep theory; instead, he tests whether you have confused two similar facts.

**Sub-topics that recur most often (in words, not numbers):**

- **Units and measurements** — SI units of force, work, power, pressure, frequency; instruments and what they measure (barometer, ammeter, odometer, lactometer).
- **Motion, force and energy** — Newton's laws in daily-life examples, inertia, momentum, work-energy, simple numericals on speed.
- **Light and sound** — reflection/refraction, mirrors and lenses in devices, colour of the sky, echo, ultrasonic uses, why sound cannot travel in vacuum.
- **Heat and electricity** — conductors/insulators, fuse wire, Ohm's law, series vs parallel, domestic wiring.
- **Chemistry in daily life** — common names vs chemical names (baking soda, washing soda, vinegar, lime), pH of common substances, metals/non-metals, alloys (brass, bronze, steel), rusting.
- **Biology basics** — vitamins and deficiency diseases, human organs and their functions, blood groups, photosynthesis, diseases and their pathogens (bacterial vs viral).
- **Space and defence tech (basic)** — ISRO missions, satellites vs launch vehicles, missile names, nuclear power plants.
- **Everyday technology** — internet terms, computer memory units, common abbreviations (LASER, RADAR, SONAR, Wi-Fi).

## 2. Exam-wise Flavour

- **SSC CGL / CHSL:** One-liner facts — SI units, vitamin deficiencies, chemical formulas, instruments. Options are designed with "almost correct" twins.
- **RRB NTPC / Group D:** Very direct NCERT-level questions; heavy on units, inventions/inventors, and everyday science (why the sky is blue, why a ship floats).
- **Banking (IBPS/SBI):** Fewer questions, but tilted toward technology — computers, internet, recent missions, abbreviations.
- **State PCS:** Adds applied questions — diseases, public health, agriculture-related science, environment basics.

## 3. The Examiner's Traps (with examples)

**Trap 1 — Almost Correct (लगभग-सही):** Options differ by one word.
*Example:* "SI unit of power" — options: Joule, Newton, **Watt**, Pascal. Joule (work) and Pascal (pressure) are placed to catch half-prepared students.

**Trap 2 — Chronological Confusion (कालक्रम भ्रम):** Missions, inventions, discoveries.
*Example:* Aryabhata (1975) vs Bhaskara-I (1979) vs Rohini (1980) — the examiner swaps years.

**Trap 3 — Negative Phrasing (नकारात्मक वाक्य):** "Which of the following is **NOT** a bacterial disease?" Students read fast and pick the first true statement. Underline NOT/EXCEPT in the exam.

**Trap 4 — Similar Names (मिलते-जुलते नाम):** Mitochondria vs ribosome; vitamin C vs vitamin D; brass (Cu+Zn) vs bronze (Cu+Sn); speed vs velocity.

**Trap 5 — Unit Swap:** Frequency in Hertz vs wavelength in metres; work in Joule vs power in Watt.

## 4. Top 10 High-Yield PYQ-Style Questions (with solutions)

**Q1. The SI unit of electric current is —**
(a) Volt (b) Ampere (c) Ohm (d) Watt
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** Ampere is the SI unit of current. Volt measures potential difference, Ohm resistance, Watt power — a classic unit-swap trap.
**Trap type:** Unit Swap | **Source:** NCERT Class 10 Science

**Q2. Which of the following diseases is NOT caused by a virus?**
(a) Measles (b) Polio (c) Tuberculosis (d) Rabies
**Answer: (c)**
**Solution:** Tuberculosis is caused by the bacterium *Mycobacterium tuberculosis*. Measles, polio and rabies are viral. The word NOT is the real question here.
**Trap type:** Negative Phrasing | **Source:** NCERT Class 9 Science

**Q3. The blue colour of the sky is due to —**
(a) Reflection (b) Refraction (c) Scattering of light (d) Dispersion
**Answer: (c)**
**Solution:** Shorter blue wavelengths scatter most in the atmosphere (Rayleigh scattering). Dispersion (d) explains the rainbow — the tempting wrong twin.
**Trap type:** Almost Correct | **Source:** NCERT Class 10 Science

**Q4. Deficiency of vitamin C causes —**
(a) Night blindness (b) Scurvy (c) Rickets (d) Beriberi
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** Scurvy ← vitamin C. Night blindness ← vitamin A, rickets ← vitamin D, beriberi ← vitamin B1. All four options are real deficiency diseases — pure memory pairing.
**Trap type:** Similar Names | **Source:** NCERT Class 6 Science

**Q5. Brass is an alloy of —**
(a) Copper and tin (b) Copper and zinc (c) Iron and carbon (d) Aluminium and copper
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** Brass = Cu + Zn; bronze = Cu + Sn. The examiner places bronze's composition as option (a) — the single most repeated alloy trap.
**Trap type:** Similar Names | **Source:** NCERT Class 10 Science

**Q6. Which instrument is used to measure atmospheric pressure?**
(a) Ammeter (b) Barometer (c) Lactometer (d) Odometer
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** Barometer → atmospheric pressure. Ammeter → current, lactometer → milk purity, odometer → distance travelled by a vehicle.
**Trap type:** Almost Correct | **Source:** NCERT Class 9 Science

**Q7. India's first satellite, launched in 1975, was —**
(a) Rohini (b) Bhaskara-I (c) Aryabhata (d) INSAT-1A
**Answer: (c)**
**Solution:** Aryabhata (1975, launched by the USSR) was first; Rohini (1980) was the first launched by India's own vehicle SLV-3. Years and "first by own rocket" vs "first satellite" are deliberately mixed.
**Trap type:** Chronological Confusion | **Source:** PYQ-style

**Q8. The chemical formula of washing soda is —**
(a) NaHCO₃ (b) Na₂CO₃·10H₂O (c) NaOH (d) CaOCl₂
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** Washing soda = sodium carbonate decahydrate. Option (a) NaHCO₃ is baking soda — the classic almost-correct twin.
**Trap type:** Almost Correct | **Source:** NCERT Class 10 Science

**Q9. Sound cannot travel through —**
(a) Water (b) Steel (c) Air (d) Vacuum
**Answer: (d)**
**Solution:** Sound needs a material medium; it travels fastest in solids like steel and not at all in vacuum. Light, unlike sound, travels through vacuum.
**Trap type:** Concept Reversal | **Source:** NCERT Class 9 Science

**Q10. The full form of LASER is —**
(a) Light Amplification by Stimulated Emission of Radiation
(b) Light Absorption by Stimulated Emission of Radiation
(c) Light Amplification by Spontaneous Emission of Radiation
(d) Light Arrangement by Stimulated Emission of Radiation
**Answer: (a)**
**Solution:** Amplification + Stimulated. Options (b) and (c) each change exactly one word — read every word before marking.
**Trap type:** Almost Correct | **Source:** PYQ-style

## 5. The 15/45-Second Rule Applied

**15-second questions (attempt instantly):** Q1, Q4, Q6, Q9 — pure recall. If you know the fact, mark and move; do not re-read the options twice. These are your "bank" questions that buy time for the rest of the paper.

**45-second questions (worth the pause):** Q2 and Q10.
- In **Q2**, the word NOT inverts the question. Spend the extra seconds underlining it mentally and verifying each option's pathogen — a rushed read costs you a negative mark.
- In **Q10**, all four options look identical at a glance. Read word-by-word: "Amplification… Stimulated…" — 45 seconds here converts a 50% guess into a sure mark.

**Skip signal:** If a question asks for a specific year, chemical formula or mission detail you have never seen, do not "logic-guess" science facts — skip and return only if time remains. Negative marking punishes brave guesses in factual chapters.

## 6. Preparation Takeaways

1. Master the **pair-lists**: unit–quantity, vitamin–disease, alloy–metals, instrument–use, disease–pathogen. Most traps attack exactly these pairs.
2. Always scan for **NOT / EXCEPT / INCORRECT** before reading options.
3. For space/defence, learn **firsts and their years** as a timeline, not as isolated facts.
4. Abbreviations (LASER, RADAR, SONAR, Wi-Fi, LED) must be memorised word-perfect — one-word swaps are the standard trap.
