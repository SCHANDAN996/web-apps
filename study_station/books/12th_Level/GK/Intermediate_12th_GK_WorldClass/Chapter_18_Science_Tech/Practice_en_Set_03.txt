Questions 51–75: Medium

51. Which of the following quantities has the SI unit "joule per second"?
(a) Force (b) Power (c) Pressure (d) Momentum
Answer: (b)
Solution: Force (newton) is tempting because it also involves motion, but joule per second is the definition of the watt, the unit of power. Pressure is newton per square metre and momentum is kg·m/s, so the answer is Power.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Science

52. An object weighs 60 N on Earth. Its weight on the Moon, where gravity is about one-sixth of Earth's, will be approximately:
(a) 60 N (b) 360 N (c) 10 N (d) 6 N
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is assuming mass changes with location; mass stays constant but weight depends on gravity. One-sixth of 60 N is 10 N, so the answer is 10 N.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Science

53. Which vitamin is synthesised in the human skin on exposure to sunlight?
(a) Vitamin D (b) Vitamin C (c) Vitamin A (d) Vitamin K
Answer: (a)
Solution: Vitamin K is a tempting distractor because it is also produced in the body, but it is made by gut bacteria, not skin. Sunlight converts 7-dehydrocholesterol in skin into Vitamin D, so the answer is Vitamin D.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Biology

54. The pH value of a neutral solution at 25°C is:
(a) 0 (b) 7 (c) 14 (d) 1
Answer: (b)
Solution: Students often confuse the ends of the scale; 0 is strongly acidic and 14 is strongly basic. A neutral solution like pure water has equal H⁺ and OH⁻ ions, giving pH 7, so the answer is 7.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science

55. Which of the following is used as a moderator in a nuclear reactor?
(a) Uranium-235 (b) Plutonium (c) Cadmium rods (d) Heavy water
Answer: (d)
Solution: Cadmium rods are the classic trap — they are control rods that absorb neutrons, not slow them down. Heavy water (D₂O) slows fast neutrons to thermal speeds, acting as a moderator, so the answer is Heavy water.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Physics

56. The phenomenon responsible for the blue colour of the sky is:
(a) Reflection (b) Scattering of light (c) Refraction (d) Total internal reflection
Answer: (b)
Solution: Refraction is tempting because it explains rainbows and mirages, but the sky's colour comes from Rayleigh scattering — shorter blue wavelengths scatter more in the atmosphere. So the answer is Scattering of light.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science

57. Which blood cells are primarily responsible for fighting infections?
(a) White blood cells (b) Red blood cells (c) Platelets (d) Plasma cells
Answer: (a)
Solution: Platelets are a common wrong pick, but they only help in clotting. White blood cells (leukocytes) produce antibodies and destroy pathogens, so the answer is White blood cells.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Biology

58. The chemical formula of washing soda is:
(a) NaHCO₃ (b) NaOH (c) Na₂CO₃·10H₂O (d) CaOCl₂
Answer: (c)
Solution: NaHCO₃ is the trap — it is baking soda, not washing soda. Washing soda is sodium carbonate decahydrate, so the answer is Na₂CO₃·10H₂O.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science

59. Which of the following waves requires a material medium for propagation?
(a) Light waves (b) Sound waves (c) Radio waves (d) X-rays
Answer: (b)
Solution: Light, radio waves and X-rays are all electromagnetic and travel through vacuum, which makes them tempting options. Sound is a mechanical wave needing a medium, so the answer is Sound waves.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Science

60. The instrument used to measure atmospheric pressure is:
(a) Barometer (b) Hydrometer (c) Ammeter (d) Thermometer
Answer: (a)
Solution: Hydrometer is the trap because it also measures a physical property, but it measures density of liquids. Atmospheric pressure is measured by a barometer, so the answer is Barometer.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Physics

61. Which gas is commonly known as laughing gas?
(a) Nitrogen dioxide (b) Carbon monoxide (c) Nitrous oxide (d) Sulphur dioxide
Answer: (c)
Solution: Nitrogen dioxide is the similar-name trap; it is a brown pollutant gas. Laughing gas is nitrous oxide (N₂O), used as an anaesthetic, so the answer is Nitrous oxide.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Chemistry

62. In photosynthesis, oxygen is released from:
(a) Carbon dioxide (b) Chlorophyll (c) Glucose (d) Water
Answer: (d)
Solution: Carbon dioxide is the tempting answer since it is the gas taken in, but experiments with isotopic tracers proved the released O₂ comes from the splitting of water molecules. So the answer is Water.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Biology

63. The SI unit of electric resistance is:
(a) Volt (b) Ampere (c) Watt (d) Ohm
Answer: (d)
Solution: Volt and ampere are tempting because they appear in Ohm's law, but they measure potential difference and current respectively. Resistance is measured in ohms, so the answer is Ohm.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science

64. Which of the following diseases is caused by a virus?
(a) Tuberculosis (b) Cholera (c) Measles (d) Typhoid
Answer: (c)
Solution: Tuberculosis, cholera and typhoid are all bacterial diseases, which makes them consistent distractors. Measles is caused by the measles virus, so the answer is Measles.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Science

65. The escape velocity from Earth's surface is approximately:
(a) 7.9 km/s (b) 11.2 km/s (c) 9.8 km/s (d) 3.0 km/s
Answer: (b)
Solution: 7.9 km/s is the classic trap — it is the orbital velocity of a near-Earth satellite, not escape velocity. Escape velocity is √2 times orbital velocity, about 11.2 km/s, so the answer is 11.2 km/s.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Physics

66. Which of the following is a non-renewable source of energy?
(a) Solar energy (b) Wind energy (c) Biogas (d) Natural gas
Answer: (d)
Solution: Biogas is the trap because it contains the word "gas" like natural gas, but biogas is renewable, produced from organic waste. Natural gas is a fossil fuel and non-renewable, so the answer is Natural gas.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science

67. The part of the human brain that controls balance and posture is:
(a) Cerebrum (b) Hypothalamus (c) Medulla oblongata (d) Cerebellum
Answer: (d)
Solution: Medulla oblongata is tempting because it controls vital functions like heartbeat and breathing, but balance and coordination of voluntary movement belong to the cerebellum. So the answer is Cerebellum.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Biology

68. Which of the following metals is liquid at room temperature?
(a) Mercury (b) Sodium (c) Aluminium (d) Gallium only in solid form
Answer: (a)
Solution: Sodium is a trap because it is soft enough to cut with a knife, but it is solid at room temperature. Mercury is the metal that remains liquid at room temperature, so the answer is Mercury.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science

69. The process by which a solid changes directly into vapour without passing through the liquid state is called:
(a) Sublimation (b) Condensation (c) Evaporation (d) Fusion
Answer: (a)
Solution: Evaporation is tempting but it is liquid-to-vapour change at the surface. Direct solid-to-vapour change, as in camphor or dry ice, is sublimation, so the answer is Sublimation.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Science

70. Which of the following correctly matches the scientist with the discovery?
(a) Proton — Rutherford's gold foil experiment (b) Electron — J.J. Thomson (c) Neutron — Millikan (d) Nucleus — Chadwick
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is the chronological/name confusion: Rutherford discovered the nucleus, Chadwick discovered the neutron, and Millikan measured the electron's charge. J.J. Thomson discovered the electron in 1897, so the answer is Electron — J.J. Thomson.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Science

71. The full form of LASER is:
(a) Light Amplification by Spontaneous Emission of Radiation (b) Light Absorption by Stimulated Emission of Radiation (c) Light Amplification by Stimulated Emission of Radiation (d) Light Amplification by Stimulated Energy of Radiation
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Absorption" and "Spontaneous" are almost-correct traps; a laser works by stimulated emission, not spontaneous emission or absorption. The correct expansion is Light Amplification by Stimulated Emission of Radiation, so the answer is Light Amplification by Stimulated Emission of Radiation.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Physics

72. Which of the following is the correct order of increasing wavelength?
(a) Radio waves < Visible light < X-rays (b) Radio waves < X-rays < Visible light (c) Visible light < X-rays < Radio waves (d) X-rays < Visible light < Radio waves
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is reversing the order; X-rays have the shortest wavelength and radio waves the longest in this list. So the increasing order is X-rays < Visible light < Radio waves, and the answer is X-rays < Visible light < Radio waves.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Physics

73. The deficiency of iodine in the human body causes:
(a) Goitre (b) Night blindness (c) Scurvy (d) Rickets
Answer: (a)
Solution: Night blindness (Vitamin A), scurvy (Vitamin C) and rickets (Vitamin D) are vitamin-deficiency traps. Iodine deficiency enlarges the thyroid gland, causing goitre, so the answer is Goitre.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Biology

74. Which of the following statements about sound is correct?
(a) Sound travels faster in solids than in gases (b) Sound can travel through vacuum (c) Sound travels faster in air than in steel (d) Sound is a transverse wave in air
Answer: (a)
Solution: The tempting wrong ideas are that sound travels in vacuum like light and that it is transverse in air; in fact sound needs a medium and is longitudinal in air. Sound travels fastest in solids, so the answer is Sound travels faster in solids than in gases.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Science

75. In the context of computers, one kilobyte (KB) is equal to:
(a) 1000 bytes (b) 512 bytes (c) 1024 bytes (d) 2048 bytes
Answer: (c)
Solution: 1000 bytes is the trap because "kilo" normally means 1000, but in binary computing 1 KB = 2¹⁰ = 1024 bytes. So the answer is 1024 bytes.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Computer Science
