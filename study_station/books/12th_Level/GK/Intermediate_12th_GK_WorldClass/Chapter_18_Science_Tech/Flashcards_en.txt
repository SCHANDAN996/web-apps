Card 1
Front: Which vitamin does sunlight help our skin produce?
Back: Vitamin D. Ultraviolet-B rays convert a cholesterol compound in the skin into Vitamin D, essential for calcium absorption and bone health.

Card 2
Front: Why does the sky appear blue?
Back: Because of Rayleigh scattering — air molecules scatter shorter (blue) wavelengths of sunlight much more than longer (red) wavelengths.

Card 3
Front: Which gas is used in the Haber process, and what does it produce?
Back: Nitrogen and hydrogen are combined to produce ammonia (NH₃), the basis of nitrogen fertilisers.

Card 4
Front: What is the pH value of pure water at 25°C, and what does pH below 7 indicate?
Back: pH 7 (neutral). Below 7 = acidic; above 7 = basic (alkaline).

Card 5
Front: Which blood cells fight infection, and which carry oxygen?
Back: White blood cells (leukocytes) fight infection; red blood cells (erythrocytes) carry oxygen via haemoglobin.

Card 6
Front: Mnemonic trick: How do you remember the order of planets from the Sun?
Back: "My Very Educated Mother Just Served Us Noodles" — Mercury, Venus, Earth, Mars, Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus, Neptune.

Card 7
Front: Mnemonic trick: How do you remember the colours of the rainbow in order?
Back: "VIBGYOR" — Violet, Indigo, Blue, Green, Yellow, Orange, Red (reverse of the order in a secondary rainbow).

Card 8
Front: Examiner's trap: Is the unit of electric current "volt" or "ampere"?
Back: Ampere. Volt measures potential difference (voltage); ampere measures current. Examiners often swap these two.

Card 9
Front: Examiner's trap: Which is the powerhouse of the cell — nucleus or mitochondria?
Back: Mitochondria. The nucleus controls cell activities and stores DNA; mitochondria produce energy (ATP). The two are frequently interchanged in options.

Card 10
Front: What does LED stand for, and why is it energy-efficient?
Back: Light Emitting Diode. It converts most electrical energy directly into light rather than heat, unlike incandescent bulbs.

Card 11
Front: Which scientist proposed the three laws of motion, and what does the first law state?
Back: Isaac Newton. First law (inertia): a body remains at rest or in uniform motion unless acted upon by an external force.

Card 12
Front: What is the chemical symbol of gold, and from which word does it come?
Back: Au, from the Latin "aurum". (Trap: silver is Ag from "argentum", iron is Fe from "ferrum".)

Card 13
Front: What is the difference between speed and velocity?
Back: Speed is scalar (magnitude only); velocity is vector (magnitude + direction). A common trap in statement-based questions.

Card 14
Front: Which organ in the human body produces insulin, and what happens when it fails?
Back: The pancreas (beta cells of islets of Langerhans). Failure or resistance causes diabetes mellitus (high blood sugar).

Card 15
Front: What does DNA stand for, and where is it mainly found in a cell?
Back: Deoxyribonucleic acid, found mainly in the nucleus (small amounts in mitochondria). It carries genetic information.

Card 16
Front: Which gas is most abundant in Earth's atmosphere — oxygen or nitrogen?
Back: Nitrogen (about 78%). Oxygen is only about 21%. A classic examiner's trap.

Card 17
Front: What is the SI unit of force, and how is it defined?
Back: Newton (N). 1 N = 1 kg·m/s² — the force that gives a 1 kg mass an acceleration of 1 m/s².

Card 18
Front: Why does a metal spoon feel colder than a wooden spoon at the same temperature?
Back: Metal is a good conductor of heat — it draws heat away from your hand faster than wood (an insulator), so it feels colder.

Card 19
Front: What is photosynthesis, and which pigment makes it possible?
Back: The process by which green plants make glucose from CO₂ and water using sunlight. Chlorophyll (in chloroplasts) captures the light energy.

Card 20
Front: What is the approximate speed of light in vacuum, and who first measured it reasonably accurately?
Back: About 3 × 10⁸ m/s (300,000 km/s). Ole Rømer made the first reasonable estimate in 1676 using Jupiter's moon Io.
