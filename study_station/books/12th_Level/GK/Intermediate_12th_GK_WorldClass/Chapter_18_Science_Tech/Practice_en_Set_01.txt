1. Which vitamin is synthesized in the human skin on exposure to sunlight?
(a) Vitamin D (b) Vitamin C (c) Vitamin A (d) Vitamin K
Answer: (a)
Solution: Vitamin A comes from diet (carrots, liver) and Vitamin K from gut bacteria and leafy vegetables — neither needs sunlight. Ultraviolet rays convert 7-dehydrocholesterol in the skin into Vitamin D, so the answer is Vitamin D.
Source: NCERT Class 6 Science

2. The SI unit of electric current is:
(a) Volt (b) Watt (c) Ohm (d) Ampere
Answer: (d)
Solution: Volt measures potential difference, Ohm measures resistance, and Watt measures power — all related to current but none is its unit. The SI unit of electric current is the Ampere, so the answer is Ampere.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science

3. Which gas is most abundant in the Earth's atmosphere?
(a) Oxygen (b) Nitrogen (c) Carbon dioxide (d) Hydrogen
Answer: (b)
Solution: Many students pick Oxygen because it is essential for life, but it forms only about 21% of air. Nitrogen makes up about 78% of the atmosphere, so the answer is Nitrogen.
Source: NCERT Class 7 Geography

4. The chemical symbol 'Fe' stands for:
(a) Fluorine (b) Lead (c) Iron (d) Zinc
Answer: (c)
Solution: Lead is Pb (from Latin plumbum) and Fluorine is F; the similar-looking symbols are a common trap. Fe comes from the Latin word ferrum, meaning iron, so the answer is Iron.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Science

5. Which organ of the human body produces insulin?
(a) Liver (b) Kidney (c) Pancreas (d) Heart
Answer: (c)
Solution: The liver regulates blood sugar by storing glycogen, which tempts many students, but it does not secrete insulin. Insulin is secreted by the beta cells of the islets of Langerhans in the pancreas, so the answer is Pancreas.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science

6. The speed of light in vacuum is approximately:
(a) 3 × 10⁶ m/s (b) 3 × 10⁸ m/s (c) 3 × 10¹⁰ m/s (d) 3 × 10⁴ m/s
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap here is confusing the powers of ten; sound travels at only about 340 m/s, far slower than any option. Light travels at about 3 × 10⁸ m/s in vacuum, so the answer is 3 × 10⁸ m/s.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science

7. Which of the following is a non-renewable source of energy?
(a) Solar energy (b) Wind energy (c) Coal (d) Hydro energy
Answer: (c)
Solution: Solar, wind and hydro energy are naturally replenished, so they are renewable. Coal is a fossil fuel that takes millions of years to form and gets exhausted, so the answer is Coal.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science

8. The pH value of a neutral solution at 25°C is:
(a) 7 (b) 0 (c) 14 (d) 1
Answer: (a)
Solution: Values below 7 are acidic and above 7 are basic; 0 and 14 are the extremes of the scale, not neutrality. Pure water is neutral with pH 7, so the answer is 7.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science

9. Which blood cells help in fighting infections in the human body?
(a) White blood cells (b) Red blood cells (c) Platelets (d) Plasma cells
Answer: (a)
Solution: Red blood cells carry oxygen and platelets help in clotting — both are tempting because all are blood components. White blood cells (leukocytes) defend the body against pathogens, so the answer is White blood cells.
Source: NCERT Class 7 Science

10. The process by which plants make their food is called:
(a) Respiration (b) Transpiration (c) Photosynthesis (d) Digestion
Answer: (c)
Solution: Respiration releases energy by breaking down food and transpiration is loss of water vapour — neither makes food. Photosynthesis uses sunlight, carbon dioxide and water to prepare food, so the answer is Photosynthesis.
Source: NCERT Class 7 Science

11. Which planet is known as the 'Red Planet'?
(a) Venus (b) Mars (c) Jupiter (d) Mercury
Answer: (b)
Solution: Venus is often confused here because it is called the 'Morning/Evening Star', but that name refers to brightness, not colour. Mars appears red due to iron oxide dust on its surface, so the answer is Mars.
Source: NCERT Class 6 Geography

12. The device used to measure atmospheric pressure is:
(a) Barometer (b) Thermometer (c) Ammeter (d) Odometer
Answer: (a)
Solution: A thermometer measures temperature and an ammeter measures current; the similar-sounding names are the trap. A barometer measures atmospheric pressure, so the answer is Barometer.
Source: NCERT Class 8 Science

13. Which of the following diseases is caused by a virus?
(a) Tuberculosis (b) Cholera (c) Typhoid (d) Polio
Answer: (d)
Solution: Tuberculosis, cholera and typhoid are all caused by bacteria, which makes this a classic trap question. Polio is caused by the poliovirus, so the answer is Polio.
Source: NCERT Class 8 Science

14. The full form of 'DNA' is:
(a) Deoxyribose Nucleic Acid (b) Deoxyribose Nitric Acid (c) Dinucleic Acid (d) Deoxyribonucleic Acid
Answer: (d)
Solution: 'Deoxyribose Nucleic Acid' looks almost correct but wrongly splits the word; 'Nitric Acid' is a different chemical altogether (HNO₃). DNA stands for Deoxyribonucleic Acid, so the answer is Deoxyribonucleic Acid.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science

15. Which metal is liquid at room temperature?
(a) Sodium (b) Aluminium (c) Mercury (d) Copper
Answer: (c)
Solution: Sodium is soft enough to cut with a knife, which tempts students, but it is solid at room temperature. Mercury is the only metal that remains liquid at room temperature, so the answer is Mercury.
Source: NCERT Class 8 Science

16. The unit of frequency is:
(a) Joule (b) Newton (c) Pascal (d) Hertz
Answer: (d)
Solution: Joule measures energy, Newton measures force and Pascal measures pressure — all SI units but of different quantities. Frequency is measured in Hertz (cycles per second), so the answer is Hertz.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Science

17. Which gas is released during photosynthesis?
(a) Oxygen (b) Carbon dioxide (c) Nitrogen (d) Hydrogen
Answer: (a)
Solution: Carbon dioxide is the tempting wrong choice because plants use it — but they absorb it, not release it, during photosynthesis. Oxygen is released as a by-product, so the answer is Oxygen.
Source: NCERT Class 7 Science

18. The largest gland in the human body is:
(a) Pancreas (b) Thyroid (c) Pituitary (d) Liver
Answer: (d)
Solution: The pituitary is called the 'master gland', which misleads many into thinking it is the largest — it is actually pea-sized. The liver is the largest gland of the human body, so the answer is Liver.
Source: NCERT Class 7 Science

19. Which of the following is used as a moderator in a nuclear reactor?
(a) Uranium-235 (b) Cadmium rods (c) Plutonium (d) Heavy water
Answer: (d)
Solution: Cadmium rods are used in reactors but as control rods to absorb neutrons, not to slow them — a classic trap. Heavy water (D₂O) slows down fast neutrons as a moderator, so the answer is Heavy water.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science

20. The phenomenon of bending of light as it passes from one medium to another is called:
(a) Reflection (b) Refraction (c) Diffraction (d) Dispersion
Answer: (b)
Solution: Reflection is the bouncing back of light from a surface, and dispersion is splitting into colours — both sound plausible. Bending of light due to change of medium is refraction, so the answer is Refraction.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science

21. Consider the following statements about sound:
1. Sound can travel through vacuum.
2. Sound travels faster in solids than in gases.
Which of the above is/are correct?
(a) 1 only (b) Both 1 and 2 (c) 2 only (d) Neither 1 nor 2
Answer: (c)
Solution: Statement 1 is the trap — it sounds plausible but sound is a mechanical wave and needs a material medium, so it cannot travel through vacuum. Statement 2 is correct because particles are closest in solids, so the answer is 2 only.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Science

22. Match the scientists with their discoveries:
1. Alexander Fleming — Penicillin
2. Edward Jenner — Smallpox vaccine
3. Louis Pasteur — Rabies vaccine
Which of the above are correctly matched?
(a) 1 and 2 only (b) 1, 2 and 3 (c) 1 and 3 only (d) 2 and 3 only
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is assuming at least one pair must be wrong; examiners often swap Fleming with Florey or Jenner with Pasteur, but here each pairing is historically accurate. All three matches are correct, so the answer is 1, 2 and 3.
Source: NCERT Class 8 Science

23. An electric bulb is rated 100 W at 220 V. The resistance of its filament while glowing is:
(a) 484 Ω (b) 220 Ω (c) 100 Ω (d) 2.2 Ω
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is dividing voltage by power (220/100 = 2.2 Ω), which misuses the formula. Using R = V²/P = 220 × 220 / 100 = 484 Ω, so the answer is 484 Ω.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science

24. Which of the following correctly arranges the electromagnetic waves in increasing order of wavelength?
(a) Radio waves < Visible light < X-rays < Gamma rays (b) Gamma rays < X-rays < Visible light < Radio waves (c) X-rays < Gamma rays < Radio waves < Visible light (d) Visible light < Radio waves < Gamma rays < X-rays
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is reversing the order — gamma rays have the highest frequency but the shortest wavelength, which confuses many students. Wavelength increases from gamma rays to X-rays to visible light to radio waves, so the answer is Gamma rays < X-rays < Visible light < Radio waves.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Physics

25. Consider the following statements about acids and bases:
1. All alkalis are bases, but all bases are not alkalis.
2. Bases turn blue litmus red.
Which of the above is/are correct?
(a) Both 1 and 2 (b) 2 only (c) 1 only (d) Neither 1 nor 2
Answer: (c)
Solution: Statement 2 is the trap — it reverses the litmus rule; acids turn blue litmus red, while bases turn red litmus blue. Statement 1 is correct because only water-soluble bases are called alkalis, so the answer is 1 only.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science
