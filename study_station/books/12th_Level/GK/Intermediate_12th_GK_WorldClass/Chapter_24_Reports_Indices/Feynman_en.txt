# 🧒 The Feynman Corner — Reports & Indices

## The Most Confusing Thing in This Chapter

Ask any aspirant what scares them in "Reports & Indices" and you'll hear the same answer:

> *"Who publishes WHAT? Was the Global Hunger Index released by the UN? By WHO? By some NGO? And is the Human Development Index from UNDP or the World Bank?"*

This is the single biggest trap in this chapter — **matching the report/index to its publishing organisation**. Exams like SSC CHSL, RRB NTPC and Banking prey on exactly this confusion. So let's fix it forever, the Feynman way.

---

## Explain It Like I'm 12

Imagine your school annual day. Many people give out prizes:

- The **Principal** gives the "Best Student" trophy.
- The **Sports Teacher** gives the "Fastest Runner" medal.
- An **outside guest** (say, a local newspaper editor) gives the "Most Disciplined Class" certificate.

Now, if someone asks you, *"Who gave the Fastest Runner medal?"* and you say "the Principal" — you're wrong, even though the Principal is the most important person in school. **The importance of the person doesn't matter; what matters is who actually handed out that specific prize.**

Reports and indices work exactly like this:

- The **UN** is like the school — big and famous. But the UN itself rarely publishes an index directly. Its **departments** do:
  - **UNDP** (the "development teacher") → **Human Development Index (HDI)**
  - **UNESCO** (the "education teacher") → education-related reports
  - **UNEP** (the "environment teacher") → **Emissions Gap Report**
- The **World Bank** is like the school accountant — it deals with money and business → **Ease of Doing Business** (now discontinued, replaced by **B-READY**), **Global Findex**.
- The **IMF** is like the bank manager next door → **World Economic Outlook**.
- **NGOs** are like the outside guests — not part of the government at all:
  - **Transparency International** (a Berlin-based NGO) → **Corruption Perceptions Index**
  - **Reporters Without Borders (RSF)** → **World Press Freedom Index**
  - **Concern Worldwide & Welthungerhilfe** (NGOs, NOT the UN!) → **Global Hunger Index**
  - **World Economic Forum** (a private foundation in Geneva, NOT a UN body) → **Global Gender Gap Report, Global Competitiveness Report**

The exam's favourite trick? Offering "United Nations" as an option for the Global Hunger Index or Press Freedom Index. It *feels* right because the UN feels official. It's wrong.

---

## A Concrete Daily-Life Example

Think of ordering food. Zomato delivers it, but the **restaurant** cooked it. If someone asks "who made this biryani?", the answer is the restaurant, not Zomato.

Similarly, when a newspaper headline says "India ranks 105 in Global Hunger Index," the newspaper is just the delivery boy. The "cook" is **Concern Worldwide + Welthungerhilfe**. Always answer with the cook, not the delivery boy.

---

## The Unforgettable Trick: "Match the NAME to the NATURE"

Most organisations publish indices that match their own name. Use that:

| Organisation | What its name tells you | Its famous index/report |
|---|---|---|
| **UNDP** — Development Programme | "Development" | **Human Development Index** |
| **Transparency International** | "Transparency" = anti-corruption | **Corruption Perceptions Index** |
| **Reporters Without Borders** | "Reporters" = press | **World Press Freedom Index** |
| **World Economic Forum** | "Economic" | **Gender Gap, Competitiveness** |
| **World Bank** | "Bank" = money/business | **Global Findex, B-READY** |
| **IMF** | "Monetary" = economy outlook | **World Economic Outlook** |
| **WHO** | "Health" | **World Health Statistics** |

**The two rebels you must memorise separately** (their names don't match their publishers):
1. **Global Hunger Index** → NGOs *Concern Worldwide + Welthungerhilfe* (NOT the UN, NOT FAO).
2. **Global Peace Index** → *Institute for Economics and Peace* (an independent think tank).

Memory line: **"Hungry people don't wait for the UN — NGOs feed them. Peace is made by think tanks, not parliaments."**
