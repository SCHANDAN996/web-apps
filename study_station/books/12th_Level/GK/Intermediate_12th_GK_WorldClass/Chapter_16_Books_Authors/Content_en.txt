# Books & Authors

## The Book That Changed a Nation

In 1852, an American woman named Harriet Beecher Stowe published a novel called **"Uncle Tom's Cabin"**. It sold 300,000 copies in its first year. When she later met President Abraham Lincoln during the Civil War, he reportedly said, *"So you're the little woman who wrote the book that started this great war!"* One book — just words on paper — helped shake an entire nation's conscience.

That is the power of books. And that is exactly why examiners love this topic: every book carries a story, an author, a year, and a trap waiting for the careless student.

Let's start at the very beginning. Ancient Indian literature is a favourite hunting ground for SSC and Railway examiners.

| Arthashastra | Kautilya (Chanakya) |
| Mudrarakshasa | Vishakhadatta |
| Abhijnanashakuntalam | Kalidasa |

| Raghuvamsha | Kalidasa |

## 🧠 Mnemonics That Actually Work

**Mnemonic 1 — Kalidasa's works: "RAM-K"**
**R**aghuvamsha, **A**bhijnanashakuntalam, **M**eghaduta, **K**umarasambhava — all Kalidasa.

**Mnemonic 2 — Booker Indians: "RADI"**
**R**oy (The God of Small Things), **A**diga (The White Tiger), **D**esai (The Inheritance of Loss), and rushd**I**e (Midnight's Children).

**Mnemonic 3 — Gandhi vs Keller: "Gandhi EXPERIMENTED, Keller LIVED"**
Gandhi → *Experiments* with Truth; Helen Keller → *Story of My Life*.

## ⚠️ Caution: Examiner's Trap

**Trap 1 — "लगभग सही कथन" (Almost-True Statement):**
*"The God of Small Things won Arundhati Roy the Pulitzer Prize."* — 90% true (right book, right author), but the award is wrong: it was the **Booker Prize (1997)**. Examiners swap Booker ↔ Pulitzer constantly. Remember: **Jhumpa Lahiri = Pulitzer; Roy, Adiga, Desai, Rushdie = Booker.**

**Trap 2 — "समान नाम/शीर्षक" (Similar Names/Titles):**
*"My Story"* vs *"My Experiments with Truth"* vs *"The Story of My Life"*. Gandhi, Nehru, and Helen Keller all have autobiographies with confusingly similar titles. Also: **Kiran Desai** (*The Inheritance of Loss*) vs **Anita Desai** (her mother, author of *Clear Light of Day*) — examiners love this mother-daughter mix-up.

**Trap 3 — "कालानुक्रमिक भ्रम" (Chronological Confusion):**
Questions like *"Which was written first?"* — *Hind Swaraj* (1909) came decades before *My Experiments with Truth* (1927). Similarly, *Gitanjali* (English translation 1912) → Nobel 1913. Know the rough timeline of the top 10 books, not just the pairs.
