Questions 26–50: Medium

26. Who wrote the novel "The Guide", which was also adapted into a famous Hindi film?
(a) Mulk Raj Anand (b) Raja Rao (c) R. K. Narayan (d) Khushwant Singh
Answer: (c)
Solution: Mulk Raj Anand wrote "Untouchable" and "Coolie", which is a tempting option because both Anand and Narayan are pioneers of Indian English fiction, but "The Guide" (1958) is by R. K. Narayan, so the answer is R. K. Narayan.
Source: NCERT Class 12 English (Indian Writing in English)

27. "The God of Small Things", winner of the Booker Prize in 1997, was written by:
(a) Jhumpa Lahiri (b) Kiran Desai (c) Anita Desai (d) Arundhati Roy
Answer: (d)
Solution: Kiran Desai also won the Booker (2006, "The Inheritance of Loss"), which creates confusion, but the 1997 Booker went to Arundhati Roy for "The God of Small Things", so the answer is Arundhati Roy.
Source: PYQ-style

28. Who is the author of the Sanskrit play "Abhijnanashakuntalam"?
(a) Banabhatta (b) Vishakhadatta (c) Kalidasa (d) Shudraka
Answer: (c)
Solution: Shudraka wrote "Mrichchhakatika" and Vishakhadatta wrote "Mudrarakshasa", which are often confused with Kalidasa's works, but "Abhijnanashakuntalam" is by Kalidasa, so the answer is Kalidasa.
Source: NCERT Class 11 (Themes in Indian History)

29. "Gitanjali", for which Rabindranath Tagore received the Nobel Prize in 1913, is a collection of:
(a) Novels (b) Poems (c) Short stories (d) Essays
Answer: (b)
Solution: Tagore wrote novels like "Gora" too, which tempts students, but "Gitanjali" (Song Offerings) is a collection of poems, so the answer is Poems.
Source: NCERT Class 10 English

30. Who wrote "Discovery of India"?
(a) Jawaharlal Nehru (b) Mahatma Gandhi (c) B. R. Ambedkar (d) Maulana Abul Kalam Azad
Answer: (a)
Solution: Azad wrote "India Wins Freedom" and Gandhi wrote "My Experiments with Truth", which are similar freedom-fighter memoirs, but "Discovery of India" was written by Jawaharlal Nehru in Ahmednagar jail, so the answer is Jawaharlal Nehru.
Source: PYQ-style

31. The book "Wings of Fire" is the autobiography of:
(a) A. P. J. Abdul Kalam (b) Homi Bhabha (c) Vikram Sarabhai (d) C. V. Raman
Answer: (a)
Solution: All four are famous Indian scientists, which is the trap, but "Wings of Fire" (1999) is the autobiography of former President and missile scientist A. P. J. Abdul Kalam, so the answer is A. P. J. Abdul Kalam.
Source: PYQ-style

32. Who authored the epic "Meghaduta"?
(a) Bhavabhuti (b) Bharavi (c) Magha (d) Kalidasa
Answer: (d)
Solution: Bharavi wrote "Kiratarjuniya" and Magha wrote "Shishupalavadha", which sound similar as Sanskrit mahakavyas, but "Meghaduta" (The Cloud Messenger) is by Kalidasa, so the answer is Kalidasa.
Source: NCERT Class 11 (Themes in Indian History)

33. "Midnight's Children", which won the Booker of Bookers, was written by:
(a) Salman Rushdie (b) V. S. Naipaul (c) Amitav Ghosh (d) Rohinton Mistry
Answer: (a)
Solution: V. S. Naipaul is a tempting option as a Nobel-winning Indian-origin author, but "Midnight's Children" (1981) is by Salman Rushdie, so the answer is Salman Rushdie.
Source: PYQ-style

34. Who wrote the Hindi novel "Godaan"?
(a) Premchand (b) Harivansh Rai Bachchan (c) Mahadevi Verma (d) Jaishankar Prasad
Answer: (a)
Solution: Jaishankar Prasad wrote "Kamayani", which confuses students of Hindi literature, but "Godaan" (1936) is the masterpiece of Munshi Premchand, so the answer is Premchand.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Hindi

35. "Anandamath", which contains the song "Vande Mataram", was written by:
(a) Rabindranath Tagore (b) Sarat Chandra Chatterjee (c) Bankim Chandra Chatterjee (d) Ishwar Chandra Vidyasagar
Answer: (c)
Solution: Sarat Chandra wrote "Devdas" and "Parineeta", which is a similar-name trap, but "Anandamath" (1882) is by Bankim Chandra Chatterjee, so the answer is Bankim Chandra Chatterjee.
Source: NCERT Class 10 History (Nationalism in India)

36. Who is the author of "A Suitable Boy"?
(a) Allan Sealy (b) Amitav Ghosh (c) Upamanyu Chatterjee (d) Vikram Seth
Answer: (d)
Solution: Amitav Ghosh wrote "The Shadow Lines" and "Sea of Poppies", which tempts students, but "A Suitable Boy" (1993), one of the longest novels in English, is by Vikram Seth, so the answer is Vikram Seth.
Source: PYQ-style

37. The book "My Experiments with Truth" was originally written by Mahatma Gandhi in which language?
(a) Hindi (b) Gujarati (c) English (d) Marathi
Answer: (b)
Solution: Many assume it was written in English because that version is most read, but Gandhi wrote his autobiography originally in Gujarati (1927), later translated into English by Mahadev Desai, so the answer is Gujarati.
Source: NCERT Class 12 History

38. Who wrote "The Inheritance of Loss"?
(a) Anita Desai (b) Jhumpa Lahiri (c) Arundhati Roy (d) Kiran Desai
Answer: (d)
Solution: Anita Desai is Kiran Desai's mother and a famous novelist herself, which is the classic trap, but "The Inheritance of Loss" (Booker 2006) is by Kiran Desai, so the answer is Kiran Desai.
Source: PYQ-style

39. "Panchatantra", the famous collection of animal fables, is attributed to:
(a) Chanakya (b) Vishnu Sharma (c) Patanjali (d) Vatsyayana
Answer: (b)
Solution: Chanakya wrote "Arthashastra", which is often confused because both are ancient didactic texts, but "Panchatantra" is attributed to Vishnu Sharma, so the answer is Vishnu Sharma.
Source: NCERT Class 6 (Ancient Indian Literature)

40. Who wrote the novel "Train to Pakistan"?
(a) Bhisham Sahni (b) Mulk Raj Anand (c) Khushwant Singh (d) Amrita Pritam
Answer: (c)
Solution: Bhisham Sahni wrote "Tamas", another famous Partition novel, which is the trap, but "Train to Pakistan" (1956) is by Khushwant Singh, so the answer is Khushwant Singh.
Source: PYQ-style

41. "Arthashastra", the ancient treatise on statecraft, was written by:
(a) Megasthenes (b) Kautilya (c) Kalhana (d) Banabhatta
Answer: (b)
Solution: Megasthenes wrote "Indica", which confuses students as both are Mauryan-era texts, but "Arthashastra" is by Kautilya (Chanakya), so the answer is Kautilya.
Source: NCERT Class 12 History (Themes in Indian History)

42. Who is the author of "The White Tiger", winner of the Man Booker Prize 2008?
(a) Aravind Adiga (b) Kiran Desai (c) Salman Rushdie (d) Vikram Seth
Answer: (a)
Solution: Kiran Desai won the Booker in 2006, which creates chronological confusion, but the 2008 Booker went to Aravind Adiga for "The White Tiger", so the answer is Aravind Adiga.
Source: PYQ-style

43. "Rajatarangini", a historical chronicle of Kashmir, was written by:
(a) Kalhana (b) Kalidasa (c) Banabhatta (d) Bilhana
Answer: (a)
Solution: Kalidasa is the tempting option due to the similar-sounding name, but "Rajatarangini" (River of Kings, 12th century) was written by Kalhana, so the answer is Kalhana.
Source: NCERT Class 12 History

44. Who wrote the famous Hindi work "Kamayani"?

Answer: (d)
Solution: Dinkar wrote "Rashmirathi" and "Kurukshetra", which tempts students of Chhayavad-era poetry, but "Kamayani" (1936) is by Jaishankar Prasad, so the answer is Jaishankar Prasad.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Hindi

(a) Dr. Zakir Husain (b) Khan Abdul Ghaffar Khan (c) Maulana Abul Kalam Azad (d) Rafi Ahmed Kidwai

46. Who wrote "The Argumentative Indian"?

Answer: (b)
Solution: Shashi Tharoor wrote "An Era of Darkness" and Guha wrote "India After Gandhi", both tempting options, but "The Argumentative Indian" (2005) is by Nobel laureate Amartya Sen, so the answer is Amartya Sen.
Source: PYQ-style

47. "Harshacharita", the biography of King Harshavardhana, was composed by:
(a) Kalhana (b) Harisena (c) Banabhatta (d) Ravikirti
Answer: (c)
Solution: Harisena composed the Prayaga Prashasti (Allahabad Pillar inscription), which confuses students, but "Harshacharita" was written by Banabhatta, Harsha's court poet, so the answer is Banabhatta.
Source: NCERT Class 12 History

48. Who is the author of the novel "The Namesake"?

Answer: (b)
Solution: Bharati Mukherjee wrote "Jasmine", a similar diaspora novel which is the trap, but "The Namesake" (2003) is by Pulitzer winner Jhumpa Lahiri, so the answer is Jhumpa Lahiri.
Source: PYQ-style

49. "Mrichchhakatika" (The Little Clay Cart) was written by:
(a) Kalidasa (b) Vishakhadatta (c) Bhavabhuti (d) Shudraka
Answer: (d)
Solution: Kalidasa is the most famous Sanskrit dramatist and hence the tempting wrong choice, but "Mrichchhakatika" is attributed to King Shudraka, so the answer is Shudraka.
Source: NCERT Class 11 (Themes in Indian History)

Answer: (a)
Solution: All four were revolutionary freedom fighters, which is the trap, but the essay "Why I Am an Atheist" was written by Bhagat Singh in Lahore jail in 1930, so the answer is Bhagat Singh.
