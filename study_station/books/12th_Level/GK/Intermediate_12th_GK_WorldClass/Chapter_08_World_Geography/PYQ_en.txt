# 📊 PYQ Analysis — World Geography (Basic)

## How Exams Test This Chapter

World Geography (Basic) is a **permanent fixture** in SSC (CGL, CHSL, MTS, GD), RRB (NTPC, Group D), Banking (IBPS/SBI prelims & mains GA), and State PCS prelims. Questions are almost always **one-liner factual MCQs** — a strait, a desert, a river, a capital, a "largest/smallest" superlative. UPSC asks fewer direct questions but uses world geography inside map-based and statement-based items.

**What repeats most often (pattern, not counts):**

| Sub-topic | How it is asked | Exams that love it |
|---|---|---|
| Continents, oceans, superlatives (largest/smallest/deepest) | Direct one-liner | SSC, RRB, State PCS |
| Straits & the water bodies they join | "X strait connects…" | SSC CGL/CHSL, Banking |
| Deserts & their continents | Match/identify | SSC, RRB |
| Rivers & cities on their banks | Direct or match | Banking, State PCS |
| Capitals & currencies | Direct one-liner | All exams |
| Mountain ranges, peaks, volcanoes | Identify/locate | SSC, RRB |
| Latitude/longitude, time zones, International Date Line | Conceptual one-liner | SSC CGL, UPSC-style |
| Grasslands, winds, ocean currents | Name-region match | SSC, State PCS |

## Examiner's Trap Types (with examples)

### Trap 1: Almost Correct (लगभग-सही)
Options differ by one small detail — the examiner bets on your half-memory.

**Q.** The Strait of Gibraltar connects —
(a) Mediterranean Sea and Red Sea
(b) Mediterranean Sea and Atlantic Ocean ✅
(c) Black Sea and Mediterranean Sea
(d) Atlantic Ocean and North Sea

**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** Gibraltar joins the Mediterranean to the Atlantic. Option (a) describes the Suez Canal route, (c) describes the Bosphorus/Dardanelles — classic almost-correct distractors.
**Source:** PYQ-style (NCERT Class 11 Geography — Fundamentals of Physical Geography)

### Trap 2: Chronological / Order Confusion
"Arrange from west to east" or "largest to smallest" — options shuffle the same four items.

**Q.** Arrange these oceans in decreasing order of area:
(a) Pacific > Atlantic > Indian > Arctic ✅
(b) Atlantic > Pacific > Indian > Arctic
(c) Pacific > Indian > Atlantic > Arctic
(d) Pacific > Atlantic > Arctic > Indian

**Answer: (a)**
**Solution:** Pacific is the largest, Arctic the smallest; Atlantic is bigger than the Indian Ocean. The trap swaps Atlantic and Indian.
**Source:** PYQ-style (NCERT Class 11 Geography)

### Trap 3: Negative Phrasing (नकारात्मक वाक्य)
"Which of the following is NOT…" — students miss the NOT under time pressure.

**Q.** Which of the following is NOT a cold ocean current?
(a) Labrador Current
(b) Oyashio Current
(c) Gulf Stream ✅
(d) California Current

**Answer: (c)**
**Solution:** The Gulf Stream is a warm current of the North Atlantic. Labrador, Oyashio and California are cold currents. The word NOT is the whole question.
**Source:** PYQ-style (NCERT Class 11 Geography)

### Trap 4: Similar-Sounding Names
Perth/Peshawar, Austria/Australia, Guinea/Guyana, Niger/Nigeria.

**Q.** Canberra is the capital of —
(a) Austria
(b) Australia ✅
(c) New Zealand
(d) South Africa

**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** Austria's capital is Vienna — the classic sound-alike trap. New Zealand's capital is Wellington; South Africa has three capitals (Pretoria, Cape Town, Bloemfontein).
**Source:** PYQ-style

### Trap 5: Superlative Swap
Largest vs longest vs deepest vs highest — the examiner swaps the adjective.

**Q.** The deepest point of the world's oceans is —
(a) Mariana Trench ✅
(b) Puerto Rico Trench
(c) Java Trench
(d) Tonga Trench

**Answer: (a)**
**Solution:** The Mariana Trench (Challenger Deep, about 11,000 m) in the Pacific is the deepest. Note: the *largest* ocean is the Pacific, the *longest* mountain range is the Andes — do not mix the adjectives.
**Source:** PYQ-style (NCERT Class 11 Geography)

## Top 10 High-Yield PYQ-Style Questions

**Q1.** The world's largest hot desert is —
(a) Sahara ✅
(b) Gobi
(c) Kalahari
(d) Arabian Desert

**Answer: (a)**
**Solution:** The Sahara (North Africa) is the world's largest **hot** desert. Note carefully: if the question asks for the largest desert *overall*, the answer is the **Antarctic Desert** (a cold desert, larger than the Sahara). Always read whether the question says "hot desert" or just "desert."
**Source:** PYQ-style (NCERT Class 7/11 Geography)

**Q2.** The river Nile flows into the —
(a) Red Sea
(b) Mediterranean Sea ✅
(c) Persian Gulf
(d) Arabian Sea

**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** The Nile drains northward into the Mediterranean. The Red Sea option is the almost-correct trap because it is geographically close.
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q3.** Which strait separates Asia and North America?
(a) Bering Strait ✅
(b) Malacca Strait
(c) Hormuz Strait
(d) Palk Strait

**Answer: (a)**
**Solution:** The Bering Strait separates Russia (Asia) from Alaska (North America). Palk Strait is between India and Sri Lanka — a frequent confusion.
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q4.** The International Date Line roughly follows —
(a) 0° longitude
(b) 180° longitude ✅
(c) 90°E longitude
(d) 23.5°N latitude

**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** The IDL follows 180° with zigzags to keep island groups on one side. 0° is the Prime Meridian (Greenwich) — the classic swap trap.
**Source:** PYQ-style (NCERT Class 6 Geography)

**Q5.** Mount Kilimanjaro is located in —
(a) Kenya
(b) Tanzania ✅
(c) Ethiopia
(d) Uganda

**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** Kilimanjaro, Africa's highest peak, is in Tanzania — though it is often *viewed* from Kenya, which is the trap.
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q6.** The grasslands of South America are called —
(a) Prairies
(b) Steppes
(c) Pampas ✅
(d) Veld

**Answer: (c)**
**Solution:** Pampas = Argentina; Prairies = North America; Steppes = Eurasia; Veld = South Africa. Examiners rotate these four names endlessly.
**Source:** PYQ-style (NCERT Class 7 Geography)

**Q7.** Which country is known as the "Land of the Rising Sun"?
(a) China
(b) Japan ✅
(c) South Korea
(d) Thailand

**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** Japan (Nippon = "sun's origin"). China is sometimes offered as the almost-correct Asian option.
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q8.** The Great Barrier Reef lies off the coast of —
(a) Brazil
(b) Australia ✅
(c) South Africa
(d) Mexico

**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** It lies off the north-east coast of Australia (Queensland), in the Coral Sea.
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q9.** Which of the following rivers does NOT flow through Europe?
(a) Danube
(b) Rhine
(c) Volga
(d) Mekong ✅

**Answer: (d)**
**Solution:** The Mekong flows through South-East Asia (China, Laos, Thailand, Cambodia, Vietnam). Negative phrasing — underline NOT before answering.
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q10.** The Tropic of Capricorn passes through which continent(s)?
(a) Only Africa
(b) Africa, South America and Australia ✅
(c) Only South America
(d) Asia and Africa

**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** The Tropic of Capricorn (23.5°S) crosses South America, Africa and Australia. It does **not** cross Asia — the Tropic of Cancer does.
**Source:** PYQ-style (NCERT Class 6/11 Geography)

## ⏱️ The 15/45-Second Rule

**15-second questions (answer instantly, bank the time):**
Direct superlatives and one-fact items — e.g., Q1 (largest hot desert), Q7 (Land of Rising Sun), Q8 (Great Barrier Reef). If you know it, mark it in 15 seconds; if you don't, **no amount of staring will help** — guess-flag and move.

**45-second questions (worth the pause):**
Order/arrangement and negative-phrasing items — e.g., the oceans-in-order question and Q9 (NOT through Europe). Spend up to 45 seconds to: (1) re-read for NOT/EXCEPT, (2) eliminate two options, (3) choose between the remaining pair. These are winnable with method, unlike pure recall.

**Rule of thumb:** Recall question → 15 s. Elimination/arrangement question → 45 s. Never cross 60 s on a static GK item.

## Revision Takeaways

1. Master the **superlative table** (largest/smallest/deepest/longest/highest) — it feeds the most questions. Remember the desert distinction: largest desert overall = Antarctic Desert; largest **hot** desert = Sahara.
2. Learn **straits as pairs**: what they connect AND what they separate.
3. Practise **grassland/wind/current name–region matches** as a set, not individually.
4. Train your eye to catch **NOT / EXCEPT** before reading options.
5. For every fact, also learn its **nearest confusion** (Austria↔Australia, Nile↔Red Sea, 0°↔180°) — that is exactly where the examiner hides the wrong option.
