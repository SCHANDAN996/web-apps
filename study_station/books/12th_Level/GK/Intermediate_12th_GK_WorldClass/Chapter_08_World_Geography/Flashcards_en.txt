Card 1
Front: Which is the largest continent by area, and roughly what share of Earth's land does it cover?
Back: Asia — about 30% of Earth's total land area. Africa is second (about 20%).

Card 2
Front: Which is the smallest continent, and why do students often confuse it with a country?
Back: Australia (Oceania). The trap: Australia is both a country and a continent — examiners ask "smallest continent" and students wrongly pick a country like Vatican City.

Card 3
Front: Which is the deepest point in the oceans, and in which ocean does it lie?
Back: Mariana Trench (Challenger Deep, about 10,935 m) in the Pacific Ocean, near the Mariana Islands.

Card 4
Front: Which is the largest ocean, and what fraction of Earth's water does it hold?
Back: Pacific Ocean — it covers about one-third of Earth's surface and holds roughly half of all ocean water.

Card 5
Front: Name the mnemonic to remember the five oceans in order of size (largest to smallest).
Back: "PAISA" — Pacific, Atlantic, Indian, Southern, Arctic. Remember: the Indian Ocean is larger than the Southern Ocean.

Card 6
Front: Which is the longest river in the world, and what is the classic examiner's trap here?
Back: The Nile (about 6,650 km) is traditionally accepted as longest. Trap: the Amazon carries the most water (largest by discharge) — questions swap "longest" and "largest by volume" to confuse you.

Card 7
Front: Which is the largest desert in the world? Careful — it's not the Sahara!
Back: The Antarctic Desert (polar desert). The Sahara is the largest HOT desert. Examiners exploit this exact confusion.

Card 8
Front: Which line of latitude divides the Earth into Northern and Southern Hemispheres, and what is its value?
Back: The Equator — 0° latitude.

Card 9
Front: What is the Prime Meridian, where does it pass through, and what is its value?
Back: 0° longitude, passing through Greenwich (London, UK). It is the reference for GMT/UTC time.

Card 10
Front: Which is the highest mountain peak above sea level, and in which range does it lie?
Back: Mount Everest (8,849 m) in the Himalayas, on the Nepal–China (Tibet) border.

Card 11
Front: Which is the largest lake in the world, and why is the "lake vs sea" wording a trap?
Back: The Caspian Sea — despite its name, it is a lake (largest by area). The largest freshwater lake by volume is Lake Baikal (Russia), which is also the world's deepest lake.

Card 12
Front: Which strait separates Asia from North America?
Back: The Bering Strait — between Russia (Siberia) and Alaska (USA).

Card 13
Front: Name a memory trick for the seven continents in order of size (largest to smallest).
Back: "A A A S A N E" — Asia, Africa, North America, South America, Antarctica, Europe, Australia. Trick: "Asia-Africa, North-South America, Antarctica, Europe, Australia."

Card 14
Front: Which is the only continent through which the Equator, Tropic of Cancer, and Tropic of Capricorn all pass?
Back: Africa — it is the most "tropical" continent, crossed by all three major latitude lines.

Card 15
Front: Which is the largest island in the world, and why is Australia not the answer?
Back: Greenland (Denmark). Australia is classified as a continent, not an island — a favourite examiner's trap.

Card 16
Front: What is the Ring of Fire, and where is it located?
Back: A horseshoe-shaped zone around the Pacific Ocean with frequent earthquakes and volcanic eruptions — about 75% of the world's active volcanoes lie here.

Card 17
Front: Which imaginary line at 180° longitude causes a day to be gained or lost when crossed?
Back: The International Date Line (in the Pacific Ocean). Cross westward → add a day; cross eastward → subtract a day.

Card 18
Front: Which is the longest mountain range in the world — and is it on land or under the sea?
Back: The mid-ocean ridge system (about 65,000 km, underwater). The longest range on land is the Andes (South America, about 7,000 km). Read the question carefully — this is a classic trap.

Card 19
Front: Which canal connects the Mediterranean Sea to the Red Sea, and which connects the Atlantic to the Pacific?
Back: Suez Canal (Egypt) — Mediterranean to Red Sea; Panama Canal — Atlantic to Pacific.

Card 20
Front: Which is the smallest ocean, and what trap do examiners set with ocean sizes?
Back: The Arctic Ocean. Trap: options swap the Southern and Indian Oceans — remember the Indian Ocean is larger than the Southern Ocean (order: Pacific > Atlantic > Indian > Southern > Arctic).
