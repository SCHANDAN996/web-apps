# World Geography (Basic)

## 🌍 The Hook: A Map That Changed History

In 1569, a Flemish cartographer named Gerardus Mercator drew a world map that sailors loved — because straight lines on it meant straight compass courses. But that map also quietly lied to the world: it made Greenland look as big as Africa, when in reality **Africa is about 14 times larger than Greenland**. For centuries, students grew up with a distorted picture of our planet.

The lesson? Geography is not just about memorising maps — it is about questioning them. And examiners know this. They love asking exactly the questions where your "common sense" from a wall map betrays you. This chapter will fix that.

## 1. The Earth: Our Home in Numbers

The Earth is the third planet from the Sun and the only known planet to support life. It is not a perfect sphere — it is slightly flattened at the poles and bulging at the equator. This shape is called a **geoid** (or an oblate spheroid).

Key numbers to remember:

| Fact | Figure |
|---|---|
| Equatorial diameter | ~12,756 km |
| Polar diameter | ~12,714 km |
| Equatorial circumference | ~40,075 km |
| Total surface area | ~510 million sq km |
| Land area | ~29% (about 148 million sq km) |
| Water area | ~71% (about 362 million sq km) |
| Average distance from the Sun | ~150 million km |
| Age of the Earth | ~4.5 billion years |

Notice the classic 29:71 land-water ratio — examiners ask this directly, and also in reverse ("What percentage of Earth's surface is water?").

### 🧠 Active Recall
Without looking back: Is the polar diameter longer or shorter than the equatorial diameter? By roughly how much? What fraction of Earth's surface is land?

## 2. Inside the Earth: The Three Layers

Think of the Earth as a boiled egg — shell, white, and yolk.

1. **Crust (Sial + Sima)** — the thin outermost layer. Continental crust is thicker (up to ~70 km under mountains); oceanic crust is thinner (~5–10 km). The most abundant elements in the crust are **oxygen, silicon, and aluminium** (mnemonic below).
2. **Mantle** — about 2,900 km thick; made of silicate rocks. The upper part, called the **asthenosphere**, is semi-molten — tectonic plates "float" on it.
3. **Core** — the innermost layer, made mainly of **iron and nickel** (hence called *Nife*). It has a liquid outer core and a solid inner core. Temperatures reach ~5,000–6,000°C.

**Boundaries you must know:**
- **Mohorovičić Discontinuity (Moho)** — between crust and mantle.
- **Gutenberg Discontinuity** — between mantle and core.

> 🪄 **Mnemonic — "Cool Monkeys Cook"**: **C**rust (coolest), **M**antle, **C**ore (hottest). Temperature rises as you go down.

> 🪄 **Mnemonic for crust elements — "OSA"**: **O**xygen > **S**ilicon > **A**luminium — the three most abundant elements in Earth's crust, in order. ("Iron" is the most abundant metal *in the whole Earth*, but aluminium is the most abundant metal *in the crust* — a favourite trap!)

### 🧠 Active Recall
Which layer is semi-molten? What is the core made of? Which discontinuity separates the mantle from the core?

## 3. Continents and Oceans

### The Seven Continents (largest to smallest)

**Asia > Africa > North America > South America > Antarctica > Europe > Australia**

> 🪄 **Mnemonic — "A A N S A E A"**: Say it as *"Aunt Annie Never Skips Any Evening Apple."* Asia is the largest (about 30% of world's land); Australia is the smallest continent.

Quick facts:
- **Asia** — largest continent; contains the highest point (Mt. Everest) and the lowest land point (Dead Sea shore).
- **Africa** — the only continent crossed by the Equator, Tropic of Cancer, *and* Tropic of Capricorn. Called the "Dark Continent" historically; has the most countries (~54).
- **Antarctica** — the coldest, driest, windiest continent; no permanent human population; governed by the Antarctic Treaty (1959).
- **Australia** — the smallest continent and the only one that is also a single country; called the "Island Continent."

### The Five Oceans (largest to smallest)

**Pacific > Atlantic > Indian > Southern > Arctic**

| Ocean | Key fact |
|---|---|
| Pacific | Largest and deepest; contains the **Mariana Trench** (deepest point, ~10,994 m, Challenger Deep) |
| Atlantic | Second largest; S-shaped; busiest shipping ocean |
| Indian | Only ocean named after a country; bounded by Asia, Africa, Australia |
| Southern | Surrounds Antarctica (recognised as the fifth ocean) |
| Arctic | Smallest and shallowest; mostly ice-covered |

### 🧠 Active Recall
Which is the deepest ocean? Which ocean is named after a country? Which continent is crossed by all three major latitudes?

## 4. Latitudes and Longitudes: The Earth's Grid

- **Latitudes (parallels)** run east–west and measure distance north/south of the **Equator (0°)**.
- **Longitudes (meridians)** run north–south and measure distance east/west of the **Prime Meridian (0°)**, which passes through **Greenwich, London**.

Important latitudes:

| Line | Value |
|---|---|
| Equator | 0° |
| Tropic of Cancer | 23.5° N |
| Tropic of Capricorn | 23.5° S |
| Arctic Circle | 66.5° N |
| Antarctic Circle | 66.5° S |

Key points:
- The Equator divides Earth into the Northern and Southern Hemispheres.
- The **International Date Line** roughly follows **180° longitude**; crossing it changes the date by one day.
- Time zones are based on longitude: Earth rotates 360° in 24 hours → **15° of longitude = 1 hour** of time difference. (India's standard meridian is 82.5° E, passing near Prayagraj/Mirzapur.)
- The **highest latitude** value possible is 90° (the poles); the highest longitude value is 180°.

### 🧠 Active Recall
Through which city does the Prime Meridian pass? How many degrees of longitude equal one hour? Which line is at 23.5° N?

## 5. Major Landforms of the World

### Mountains
- **Mount Everest (8,848.86 m)** — highest peak, in the Himalayas (Nepal–China border). This is the height jointly announced by Nepal and China in 2020.
- **K2 (8,611 m)** — second highest, in the Karakoram range.
- **Andes** — longest mountain range on land (South America, ~7,000 km).
- **Mid-ocean ridges** — the longest mountain system overall lies *underwater*.
- **Mount Kilimanjaro** — highest peak of Africa (Tanzania).
- **Mount Elbrus** — highest peak of Europe (Caucasus).
- **Mauna Kea (Hawaii)** — tallest mountain measured from its base on the ocean floor.

### Plateaus
- **Tibetan Plateau** — the highest and largest plateau, called the **"Roof of the World."**
- **Deccan Plateau** — India.
- **Plateau of Bolivia (Altiplano)** — South America.

### Deserts
- **Sahara** — the largest *hot* desert (Africa).
- **Antarctic Desert** — the largest desert overall (a cold desert!). Deserts are defined by low rainfall, not heat.
- **Gobi** — cold desert (Mongolia/China).
- **Atacama** (Chile) — the driest desert on Earth.
- **Thar** — India/Pakistan.

### Rivers
- **Nile (~6,650 km)** — longest river (Africa); flows into the Mediterranean Sea.
- **Amazon** — largest by water discharge and second longest; flows through South America into the Atlantic.
- **Yangtze** — longest river of Asia (China).
- **Mississippi–Missouri** — longest river system of North America.

### Lakes and Seas
- **Caspian Sea** — the largest lake in the world (it is a lake, despite the name "sea"!).
- **Lake Superior** — largest freshwater lake by surface area (North America).
- **Lake Baikal** (Siberia, Russia) — deepest lake in the world and largest by freshwater volume.
- **Dead Sea** — lowest point on land (~430 m below sea level); so salty that swimmers float easily.

### 🧠 Active Recall
Which is the largest desert — Sahara or Antarctica? Which is the largest lake? Which river carries the most water?

## 6. Islands, Straits, and Other Superlatives

- **Greenland** — the largest island in the world (Australia is a continent, not an island — classic trap!).
- **New Guinea** — second largest island.
- **Borneo** — third largest; shared by Indonesia, Malaysia, and Brunei.
- **Indonesia** — the largest archipelago (island nation) in the world.
- **Strait of Malacca** — between Malaysia and Sumatra; one of the busiest shipping lanes.
- **Bering Strait** — separates Asia (Russia) and North America (Alaska, USA).
- **Strait of Gibraltar** — connects the Atlantic Ocean to the Mediterranean Sea; separates Spain and Morocco.
- **Palk Strait** — between India and Sri Lanka.
- **Mariana Trench** — deepest oceanic trench (Pacific).
- **Mariana's Challenger Deep** — the deepest known point on Earth.

## 🔗 Static–Dynamic Link

Geography feels static, but it connects to today's headlines:
- **Climate change and the Arctic**: melting Arctic ice is opening new shipping routes (the Northern Sea Route), making the once-"frozen" Arctic Ocean geopolitically important.
- **Sea-level rise**: low-lying island nations like the **Maldives** and **Tuvalu** face existential threats — questions on "lowest-lying countries" now appear in current-affairs-linked GK.
- **Mt. Everest's height**: in 2020, Nepal and China jointly announced the revised height of Everest as **8,848.86 m** — a reminder that even "static" facts get updated.

## ⚠️ Caution: Examiner's Trap

**Trap 1 — "Almost-True Statement" (लगभग सही कथन)**
*"The Sahara is the largest desert in the world."* Sounds 100% right, and 90% of students mark it correct. But the **Antarctic Desert** is the largest desert overall; the Sahara is only the largest **hot** desert. Always check whether the question says "hot desert" or just "desert."

**Trap 2 — "Similar Names" (समान नाम)**
**Lake Superior vs. Lake Baikal vs. Caspian Sea** — all three are "largest" in some sense: Caspian = largest lake overall; Superior = largest *freshwater* lake by area; Baikal = *deepest* and largest by freshwater *volume*. Examiners deliberately mix these three in options. Read the qualifier (freshwater? area? volume? depth?) before answering.

**Trap 3 — "Negative Phrasing" (नकारात्मक वाक्यांश)**
*"Which of the following is NOT crossed by the Equator?"* — Students who know the facts still lose marks because they miss the word "NOT." The Equator passes through South America, Africa, and Asia (maritime/Indonesia) — but **not** through North America, Europe, Australia, or Antarctica. Underline negative words in the question paper.
