# 🧒 Feynman Technique: Government Schemes & Initiatives

## The Most Confusing Concept: Central Sector Schemes vs Centrally Sponsored Schemes

Ask any aspirant: *"Is MGNREGA a Central Sector scheme or a Centrally Sponsored scheme?"* — and watch them freeze. This single distinction is one of the most frequently tested and most frequently confused facts in this chapter. Let's fix it forever, the Feynman way.

---

## Explaining It to a 12-Year-Old

Imagine your father wants every child in the family to get a bicycle.

**Case 1 — Central Sector Scheme:**
Father says: *"I will buy the bicycle myself, with 100% of my own money, and give it directly to you."*
No involvement of anyone else. Full money, full control — all with the father.

That is a **Central Sector Scheme** — the Central Government funds it **100%** and implements it through its own machinery. Examples: PM-KISAN (income support paid directly to farmers by the Centre), Khelo India, and schemes run entirely by central ministries such as the Ministry of Culture.

**Case 2 — Centrally Sponsored Scheme:**
Father says: *"I will pay 60% (or 90% for some special families), but your uncle (the State) must pay the remaining 40% (or 10%), and uncle will actually buy and deliver the bicycle."*

That is a **Centrally Sponsored Scheme** — the Centre shares the cost with the States (usually **60:40**, and **90:10** for the North-Eastern and Himalayan states), and the **State implements** it. Examples: MGNREGA, PM POSHAN (Mid-Day Meal), National Health Mission, Samagra Shiksha, and Pradhan Mantri Awas Yojana (both Gramin and Urban).

**The one-line test:** *Who pays fully, and who delivers?*
- Centre pays 100% + Centre delivers → **Central Sector**
- Centre + State share money + State delivers → **Centrally Sponsored**

---

## A Concrete Daily-Life Example

Think of ordering food:
- **Central Sector** = You pay the full bill online and the restaurant's own delivery boy brings it. (Fully funded and fully controlled by one party.)
- **Centrally Sponsored** = You and your roommate split the bill 60:40, and your roommate goes to collect the food. You funded most of it, but the roommate is the one who actually executes.

If the roommate refuses to pay his 40%, the food never arrives — exactly why some states delay Centrally Sponsored Schemes!

---

## 🪄 The Unforgettable Trick: **"FULL = SECTOR, SHARE = SPONSORED"**

- **FULL** payment by Centre → Central **SECTOR** (both words feel "complete/whole")
- **SHARE** the payment → Centrally **SPONSORED** (a "sponsor" never pays everything alone — a sponsor *contributes*)

Memory hook: *"A sponsor at a cricket match doesn't own the team — he just chips in money. The team (State) still plays the game."*

---

## ⚠️ Examiner's Traps on This Concept

1. **Ratio trap:** The standard sharing pattern is 60:40, but for **North-Eastern and Himalayan states it is 90:10**. Examiners swap these.
2. **Classification trap:** PM-KISAN is a **Central Sector** scheme (100% Centre, DBT to farmers) — often wrongly marked as sponsored. Conversely, **Pradhan Mantri Awas Yojana (Gramin and Urban)** is a **Centrally Sponsored** scheme — often wrongly marked as Central Sector.
3. **Implementation trap:** In Centrally Sponsored Schemes, the **State implements** — the Centre only funds and frames guidelines. Questions flip this.

## ❓ Final Question (Test Yourself)

**Q. The National Health Mission is funded in a 60:40 ratio between the Centre and most States and is implemented by the States. Is it a Central Sector Scheme or a Centrally Sponsored Scheme — and what would the ratio be for Uttarakhand?**

*(Answer: Centrally Sponsored Scheme — because the cost is shared and the State implements it. For Uttarakhand, a Himalayan state, the ratio is 90:10.)*
