Card 1
Front: In coded direction problems, what is the very first step before solving anything?
Back: Decode the code — write down what each symbol/word means (e.g., "A means North") in plain directions before reading the movement steps.

Card 2
Front: If "P @ Q" means "P is to the North of Q", where is Q relative to P?
Back: South. Always reverse the decoded direction to find the second person's position relative to the first.

Card 3
Front: What is the quick mnemonic to remember the clockwise order of the four main directions?
Back: "Never Eat Soggy Wafers" — North → East → South → West (clockwise).

Card 4
Front: In a coded question, "A $ B" means "A is 5 m East of B". If B $ C also holds, where is A relative to C?
Back: 10 m East of C. Same-direction distances add up: 5 + 5 = 10 m.

Card 5
Front: Examiner's trap: "A is to the North-East of B" — which two directions combine here, and what angle separates them from B's perspective?
Back: North + East combine; NE lies exactly 45° between them. Trap: students treat NE as just "North" or just "East" and misplace the point.

Card 6
Front: When a code says "X # Y means X is South-West of Y", how do you place X on your rough diagram?
Back: Put Y at the centre, then place X diagonally down-left (between South and West) from Y.

Card 7
Front: What memory trick helps recall the four sub-directions' positions?
Back: "NEWS sandwich": NE sits between N and E, SE between S and E, SW between S and W, NW between N and W — each sub-direction is named by its two neighbours.

Card 8
Front: If "M % N" means "M is 4 km West of N" and "N % O" means "N is 4 km West of O", what is the shortest distance between M and O?
Back: 8 km. Both movements are along the same straight line (West), so distances add.

Card 9
Front: Examiner's trap: In coded directions, the same symbol may be redefined in the next question of the same set. What must you do?
Back: Re-read the code key for every new question — never carry the old meaning forward. Codes are question-specific.

Card 10
Front: "A & B" means "A is to the East of B". The question asks: "B is in which direction of A?" What is the answer and why?
Back: West. The relation is reversed when the question flips the reference point — East of B means B is West of A.

Card 11
Front: In a chain like P @ Q, Q @ R, R @ S (where @ = North of), what technique finds P's direction from S fastest?
Back: Stack all points vertically on one line: S at bottom, then R, Q, P going up. P is directly North of S.

Card 12
Front: If "A * B" means "A is 3 m North of B" and "B * C" is false but "C * B" is true, where is A relative to C?
Back: C is 3 m North of B, and A is 3 m North of B — so A and C are at the same point; distance between them is 0 m (they coincide).

Card 13
Front: What does a right-angle turn in a coded path (e.g., East then North) do to the distance calculation?
Back: Use Pythagoras: shortest distance = √(a² + b²). E.g., 3 m East + 4 m North → 5 m from start.

Card 14
Front: Mnemonic check: which simple phrase reminds you that "left of East is North, right of East is South" when facing the direction of movement?
Back: "Face the way you go" — always imagine yourself standing at the point facing the travel direction before deciding left/right.

Card 15
Front: "X ⊕ Y" means "X is South-East of Y". Where is Y relative to X?
Back: North-West. Opposite of SE is NW — reverse both components (S→N, E→W).

Card 16
Front: Examiner's trap: a coded question mixes two codes — "@ means North of" and "# means 5 m East of". What error do most students make?
Back: They apply one uniform meaning to all symbols. Each symbol has its own meaning; decode every relation separately before drawing.

Card 17
Front: How do you quickly find the opposite of any sub-direction like NW or SE?
Back: Flip each component: N↔S and E↔W. Opposite of NW = SE; opposite of NE = SW.

Card 18
Front: In "A is to the North of B, who is to the East of C", who is the reference point for placing B?
Back: C. "Who" refers to the immediately preceding noun (B), so B is East of C — place C first, then B, then A.

Card 19
Front: Why should you always draw a rough diagram in coded direction questions instead of solving mentally?
Back: Codes add a second layer of confusion; a diagram converts symbols into visible positions, making reversals and distance additions error-free.

Card 20
Front: If all movements in a coded chain are along one straight line (all North or all South), what is the shortcut for the final distance?
Back: Add distances in the same direction, subtract opposite ones. Net displacement = (sum of one direction) − (sum of the opposite).
