1. Statement: All pens are books. All books are chairs.
Conclusions:
I. All pens are chairs.
II. Some chairs are pens.
(a) Only conclusion I follows (b) Only conclusion II follows (c) Both I and II follow (d) Neither I nor II follows
Answer: (c)
Solution: Since all pens are books and all books are chairs, all pens must be chairs, so conclusion I follows. If all pens are chairs, then some chairs are definitely pens, so conclusion II also follows. The tempting error is to accept only the direct conclusion I and ignore the converse-based conclusion II. Both conclusions follow, so the answer is "Both I and II follow".

2. Statement: Some dogs are cats. All cats are rats.
Conclusions:
I. Some dogs are rats.
II. All rats are cats.
(a) Only conclusion I follows (b) Only conclusion II follows (c) Both I and II follow (d) Neither I nor II follows
Answer: (a)
Solution: The overlap between dogs and cats, combined with all cats being rats, guarantees that some dogs are rats, so conclusion I follows. Conclusion II reverses "all cats are rats" into "all rats are cats", which is the classic converse trap — a universal statement cannot be reversed. Only conclusion I follows, so the answer is "Only conclusion I follows".
