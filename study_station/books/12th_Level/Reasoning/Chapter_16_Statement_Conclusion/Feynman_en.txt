# 🧒 Feynman Technique — Statement & Conclusion

## The Detective Who Jumped Too Fast

Imagine your friend Ravi comes home from school and says:

> **"The street outside our house is wet."**

Now, Ravi's little sister immediately shouts: *"It rained today!"*

Wait. Did it? Maybe. But maybe a water tanker just passed by. Maybe someone washed their car. Maybe a pipe burst. The street being wet **tells us only one thing for sure — the street is wet.** Everything else is a guess.

That, right there, is the **entire secret of Statement & Conclusion questions.**

---

## The One Rule That Solves Everything

In these questions, the exam gives you a **Statement** (a fact — like "the street is wet") and then some **Conclusions** (guesses — like "it rained"). Your job is to decide: does the conclusion **definitely follow** from the statement, and *only* from the statement?

Think of yourself as a detective with a very strict boss. Your boss says:

> **"You may only use the evidence in front of you. No outside knowledge. No 'but usually…'. No 'common sense says…'. If the statement doesn't say it, you don't know it."**

This is the hardest part for students, because our brain *loves* to add extra information. The exam knows this — and it traps you with conclusions that feel true in real life but are **not guaranteed by the statement**.

---

## A Fun Story: The Mango Seller

**Statement:** "All the mangoes in Ramesh's cart are sweet."

Now test these conclusions like our strict detective:

1. **"Ramesh sells mangoes."** ❌ Doesn't follow! The statement only says the mangoes in his cart are sweet. It never says he *sells* them — maybe the cart is taking mangoes to his own house, or to a relative. "He sells mangoes" is an outside assumption smuggled in from real life.
2. **"Ramesh's mangoes are the sweetest in the market."** ❌ Doesn't follow! The statement never compared him to anyone else. Maybe the next cart's mangoes are even sweeter.
3. **"All mangoes in the world are sweet."** ❌ Doesn't follow! The statement is only about *Ramesh's cart*, not the whole world. Don't stretch a small fact into a giant claim.

See the pattern? Every one of these conclusions **jumped outside** the statement — one added an assumption, one added a comparison, one stretched the scope. In this chapter, jumping outside = wrong answer. A conclusion that *follows* would be something like: **"Some sweet mangoes are in Ramesh's cart"** — because if *all* the mangoes there are sweet, then certainly *some* sweet mangoes are there. That stays **inside** the statement.

---

## The Unforgettable Trick: 🏠 "THE LOCKED ROOM RULE"

> **The statement is a locked room. The conclusion must be built ONLY from furniture already inside that room. If even one piece of the conclusion had to be smuggled in from outside (your general knowledge, your assumptions, real life), the conclusion is WRONG.**

Before marking any conclusion as "follows," ask one question:

**"Can I prove this using ONLY the words in the statement — yes or no?"**

- Yes → it follows ✅
- No / Maybe / Probably / In real life it's true → it does NOT follow ❌

**Bonus trap-alarm words:** If a conclusion contains *all, every, always, never, best, only, must* — be extra careful. These extreme words are the examiner's favourite bait. They usually fail the Locked Room test unless the statement itself uses equally strong words.
