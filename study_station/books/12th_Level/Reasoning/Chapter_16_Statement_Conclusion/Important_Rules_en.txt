# Important Rules & Approaches — Statement & Conclusion

## Part 1: Key Rules / Approaches

| Rule Name | Description | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Rule of Direct Derivation | A conclusion is valid only if it follows **directly and certainly** from the statement. No outside knowledge allowed. | Statement: "All pens are books." → Conclusion "Some books are pens" follows; "All books are pens" does not. |
| Rule of Certainty (100% Test) | Accept a conclusion only if it is true in **every possible case**. If even one case makes it false, reject it. | "Some students passed" → "Some students failed" is NOT certain (all may have passed). |
| Rule of No Assumption | Do not use real-world facts, opinions, or general knowledge. Treat the statement as the only truth. | Statement: "Cats can fly." → Within the question, accept it as true. |
| Rule of Definite vs. Probable | Words like "may", "might", "possibly", "probably" make a conclusion weak — reject unless the statement itself uses them. | "He is rich" → "He may be happy" is a guess, not a conclusion. |
| Rule of Keywords Match | Conclusions with extreme words ("all", "never", "always", "only") need equally strong support in the statement. | "Some A are B" cannot support "All A are B". |
| Rule of Negative Statements | From a negative statement ("No A is B"), only negative conclusions can be drawn with certainty. | "No A is B" → "No B is A" follows; "Some A are not B" also follows. |
| Rule of Suggestion/Action | A conclusion suggesting what "should be done" is valid only if the statement clearly points to a problem needing that action. | "Road accidents are rising due to rash driving" → "Rash driving should be checked" follows. |
| Rule of Either-Or | When two conclusions are complementary (one positive, one negative, same subject-predicate) and neither follows alone, mark "either I or II follows". | Conclusions: "He passed" / "He did not pass" — if the statement gives no result, either-or applies. |
| Rule of Venn Diagram Check | Draw all possible Venn diagrams for the statement; a conclusion must hold in **all** diagrams. | "Some A are B" — check both overlapping and subset diagrams. |
| Rule of Scope Limit | The conclusion must stay within the scope (subject, place, time) of the statement. | "Crime rose in City X" → "Crime rose in the whole country" is out of scope. |

## Part 2: Time Management

| Situation | Time Rule | Action |
|---|---|---|
| Simple 1-statement, 2-conclusion question | 15-second rule | If the logical link is not clear within 15 seconds, mark it and move on; return later. |
| Standard question with Venn diagram needed | 30-second rule | Draw a quick diagram; if still doubtful after 30 seconds, choose the safer option (usually "neither follows") and proceed. |
| Complex either-or or multi-conclusion question | 45-second rule | Maximum 45 seconds per question. Never exceed this — Statement & Conclusion questions are scoring only when done fast. |
| Full section strategy | — | Attempt direct-derivation questions first; keep either-or and suggestion-type questions for the second pass. |
