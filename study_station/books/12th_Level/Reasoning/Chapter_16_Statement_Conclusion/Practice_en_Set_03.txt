51. Statement: "All students who study regularly pass the examination. Ravi studies regularly."
Conclusions:
I. Ravi will pass the examination.
II. Only students who study regularly pass the examination.
(a) Only conclusion II follows (b) Neither conclusion I nor conclusion II follows (c) Both conclusions I and II follow (d) Only conclusion I follows
Answer: (d)
Solution: The tempting option is "both follow", because conclusion II sounds like a restatement of the statement. But the statement says regular study is sufficient for passing, not that it is the only way — some students may pass without regular study, so II does not follow. Since Ravi studies regularly, he satisfies the stated condition, so the answer is only conclusion I follows.

52. Statement: "The company has decided to increase the salaries of all its employees by 15 percent from next month."

I. The company is earning good profits.
II. The employees of the company were demanding a salary hike.
(a) Both conclusions I and II follow (b) Only conclusion II follows (c) Neither conclusion I nor conclusion II follows (d) Only conclusion I follows
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap here is to assume a reason behind the decision. A salary hike may be due to profits, employee demands, inflation adjustment, retention policy or many other reasons — none of these is stated. Both conclusions are assumptions about causes, not deductions from the statement, so the answer is neither conclusion follows.
