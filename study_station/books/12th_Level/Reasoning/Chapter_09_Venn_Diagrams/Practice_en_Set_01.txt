1. In a class, some students play cricket and some play football. Which diagram best represents the relationship between Students, Cricket players, Football players?
(a) Three separate circles (b) Two overlapping circles inside a larger circle (c) One circle inside another, third separate (d) Two separate circles inside a larger circle
Answer: (b)
Solution: All cricket players and football players are students, so both circles lie inside the big "Students" circle. Since some students play both games, the two inner circles overlap. Option (d) is the trap — it ignores students who play both games. So the answer is two overlapping circles inside a larger circle.

2. Which diagram best represents: Men, Doctors, Fathers?
(a) Three separate circles (b) Three mutually overlapping circles (c) One circle inside another (d) Two overlapping circles, third separate
Answer: (b)
Solution: Some men are doctors, some men are fathers, and some doctors are fathers — all three groups can overlap with each other. Option (a) is the trap because it assumes the groups are totally different, but a doctor can also be a father. So the answer is three mutually overlapping circles.

4. In a class of 40 students, 25 like tea and 20 like coffee. If 10 like both, how many like only tea?
(a) 25 (b) 10 (c) 15 (d) 30
Answer: (c)
Solution: Only tea = total tea − both = 25 − 10 = 15. The trap is option (a), which forgets to subtract the students who like both. So the answer is 15.

21. In a class of 60 students, 30 play cricket, 25 play football, and 12 play both. How many students play exactly one of the two games?
(a) 31 (b) 43 (c) 25 (d) 18
Answer: (a)
Solution: Only cricket = 30 − 12 = 18; only football = 25 − 12 = 13. Exactly one game = 18 + 13 = 31. The trap is option (b), which gives the number playing at least one game (30 + 25 − 12 = 43), not exactly one. So the answer is 31.
