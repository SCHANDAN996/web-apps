101. In a class of 60 students, 35 play cricket, 28 play football and 12 play both. How many students play neither game?
(a) 7 (b) 11 (c) 9 (d) 15
Answer: (c)
Solution: Students playing at least one game = 35 + 28 − 12 = 51. The tempting wrong answer 12 is just the "both" figure, not the "neither" figure. Neither = 60 − 51 = 9, so the answer is 9.
Source: PYQ-style

102. In a survey, 40% of people read newspaper A, 30% read newspaper B and 10% read both. What percentage read neither?
(a) 40% (b) 30% (c) 50% (d) 60%
Answer: (a)
Solution: Reading at least one = 40 + 30 − 10 = 60%. The trap is to add 40 + 30 = 70 and subtract from 100 to get 30%, forgetting the overlap. Neither = 100 − 60 = 40%, so the answer is 40%.
Source: PYQ-style

103. Which Venn diagram best represents: Men, Fathers, Doctors?
(a) Three mutually exclusive circles (b) Three circles each intersecting the other two (c) Fathers circle wholly inside Men, Doctors intersecting both (d) One circle inside another, third completely separate
Answer: (c)
Solution: Every father is a man, so Fathers lies wholly inside Men; some doctors are men or fathers and some are not, so Doctors cuts across both. The trap is drawing three mutually intersecting circles, which wrongly allows a father who is not a man. So the answer is Fathers circle wholly inside Men, Doctors intersecting both.
Source: PYQ-style

104. Which diagram best represents: Animals, Dogs, Cats?
(a) Two separate circles inside a big circle (b) Two intersecting circles inside a big circle (c) Three intersecting circles (d) One circle inside another, third separate
Answer: (a)
Solution: All dogs and all cats are animals, but no dog is a cat, so the two inner circles must not overlap. The trap is drawing them intersecting, which would wrongly claim some dogs are cats. Hence the answer is two separate circles inside a big circle.
Source: PYQ-style

105. In a group of 100 people, 65 like tea, 45 like coffee and 25 like both. How many like only tea?
(a) 25 (b) 40 (c) 65 (d) 20
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Only tea" excludes those who like both. The trap is answering 65, which counts the both-group too. Only tea = 65 − 25 = 40, so the answer is 40.
Source: PYQ-style

106. In a school, 50 students play hockey, 40 play basketball and 20 play both. How many play exactly one of the two games?
(a) 90 (b) 70 (c) 30 (d) 50
Answer: (d)
Solution: Only hockey = 50 − 20 = 30; only basketball = 40 − 20 = 20. The trap is adding 50 + 40 = 90 without removing the overlap. Exactly one = 30 + 20 = 50, so the answer is 50.
Source: PYQ-style

108. Out of 80 students, 45 passed in Maths, 38 passed in Science and 15 passed in both. How many failed in both subjects?
(a) 18 (b) 10 (c) 12 (d) 22
Answer: (c)
Solution: Passed in at least one = 45 + 38 − 15 = 68. The trap is subtracting only the "both" figure from 80. Failed in both = 80 − 68 = 12, so the answer is 12.

109. Which diagram best represents: Earth, Asia, India?
(a) Three intersecting circles (b) Three separate circles (c) India inside Asia, Asia inside Earth (d) Asia and India intersecting, Earth separate
Answer: (c)
Solution: India is entirely within Asia, and Asia is entirely on Earth — a pure nesting relation. The trap is drawing intersections, which would mean part of India lies outside Asia. So the answer is India inside Asia, Asia inside Earth.

110. In a colony, 60% families own a scooter, 50% own a car and 20% own both. What percentage own exactly one vehicle?
(a) 40% (b) 90% (c) 50% (d) 70%
Answer: (d)
Solution: Only scooter = 60 − 20 = 40%; only car = 50 − 20 = 30%. The trap is answering 90% (60 + 50 − 20), which is "at least one", not "exactly one". Exactly one = 40 + 30 = 70%, so the answer is 70%.
Source: PYQ-style

111. In a class, every student studies Hindi or English or both. 42 study Hindi, 30 study English and 18 study both. How many students are in the class?
(a) 54 (b) 72 (c) 90 (d) 60
Answer: (a)
Solution: Since everyone studies at least one, total = 42 + 30 − 18 = 54. The trap is adding all three numbers to get 90, double-counting the overlap. So the answer is 54.
Source: PYQ-style

112. Which Venn diagram best represents: Fruits, Mangoes, Vegetables?
(a) Three intersecting circles (b) All three separate (c) Fruits inside Mangoes, Vegetables separate (d) Mangoes inside Fruits, Vegetables separate
Answer: (d)
Solution: All mangoes are fruits, but no vegetable is a fruit, so Vegetables stays outside. The trap is intersecting Fruits and Vegetables, which is logically false. So the answer is Mangoes inside Fruits, Vegetables separate.

113. Among 200 people, 120 can speak Hindi, 80 can speak English and 40 can speak neither. How many can speak both languages?
(a) 40 (b) 60 (c) 20 (d) 80
Answer: (a)
Solution: Speaking at least one = 200 − 40 = 160. Both = 120 + 80 − 160 = 40. The trap is reporting 160 or subtracting wrongly to get 20. So the answer is 40.
Source: PYQ-style

114. In a group, 25 persons like tea only, 20 like coffee only and 10 like both. If 5 like neither, what is the total number of persons?
(a) 55 (b) 65 (c) 50 (d) 60
Answer: (d)
Solution: Total = only tea + only coffee + both + neither = 25 + 20 + 10 + 5 = 60. The trap is forgetting the "neither" group and answering 55. So the answer is 60.
Source: PYQ-style

115. Which diagram best represents: Students, Cricket players, Football players?

Answer: (a)
Solution: All players here are students, and some students play both games, so the two inner circles intersect within the big circle. The trap is keeping the two inner circles separate, denying that a student can play both. So the answer is Cricket and Football circles intersecting, both inside Students.

116. In an examination, 70% passed in English, 60% passed in Maths and 40% passed in both. What percentage failed in both?
(a) 30% (b) 10% (c) 20% (d) 5%
Answer: (b)
Solution: Passed in at least one = 70 + 60 − 40 = 90%. The trap is taking 40%, the "both passed" figure, as the failure rate. Failed in both = 100 − 90 = 10%, so the answer is 10%.
Source: PYQ-style

117. In a class of 50, each student plays at least one of cricket and football. If 30 play cricket and 28 play football, how many play both?
(a) 6 (b) 12 (c) 10 (d) 8
Answer: (d)
Solution: Both = 30 + 28 − 50 = 8. The trap is subtracting 30 from 50 to get 20 or ignoring that the sum exceeds the class strength because of double counting. So the answer is 8.
Source: PYQ-style

118. Which Venn diagram best represents: Quadrilaterals, Rectangles, Squares?
(a) Three intersecting circles (b) Squares inside Rectangles, Rectangles inside Quadrilaterals (c) Rectangles and Squares separate, inside Quadrilaterals (d) Three separate circles
Answer: (b)
Solution: Every square is a rectangle and every rectangle is a quadrilateral — pure nesting. The trap is keeping Squares and Rectangles separate, which denies that a square is a rectangle. So the answer is Squares inside Rectangles, Rectangles inside Quadrilaterals.

120. Which diagram best represents: Doctors, Surgeons, Males?

Answer: (d)
Solution: Every surgeon is a doctor, so Surgeons lies inside Doctors; some males are doctors or surgeons, some are not, so Males cuts across. The trap is reversing the nesting and putting Doctors inside Surgeons. So the answer is Surgeons inside Doctors, Males intersecting both.

121. In a class, 40 students like Maths, 35 like Physics, 30 like Chemistry; 15 like Maths and Physics, 12 like Physics and Chemistry, 10 like Maths and Chemistry, and 5 like all three. How many like at least one subject?
(a) 73 (b) 68 (c) 78 (d) 63
Answer: (a)
Solution: At least one = 40 + 35 + 30 − 15 − 12 − 10 + 5 = 73. The trap is forgetting to add back the all-three group, giving 68. So the answer is 73.
Source: PYQ-style

122. In a class, 40 students like Maths, 35 like Physics, 30 like Chemistry; 15 like Maths and Physics, 12 like Physics and Chemistry, 10 like Maths and Chemistry, and 5 like all three. How many like only Maths?
(a) 30 (b) 25 (c) 20 (d) 15
Answer: (c)
Solution: Only Maths = 40 − (15 − 5) − (10 − 5) − 5 = 40 − 10 − 5 − 5 = 20. The trap is subtracting the full pairwise figures 15 and 10, which double-removes the all-three group and gives 15. So the answer is 20.
Source: PYQ-style

123. In a town, 65% of people watch TV, 40% read newspapers and 25% do both. If the town has 4000 people, how many do neither?
(a) 1000 (b) 600 (c) 800 (d) 1200
Answer: (c)
Solution: Doing at least one = 65 + 40 − 25 = 80%, so neither = 20%. The trap is taking 25%, the "both" figure, as the neither percentage. Neither = 20% of 4000 = 800, so the answer is 800.
Source: PYQ-style

124. In a group of 120 students, 80 play cricket, 60 play football, and each student plays at least one game. How many play only football?
(a) 20 (b) 40 (c) 60 (d) 30
Answer: (b)
Solution: Both = 80 + 60 − 120 = 20. Only football = 60 − 20 = 40. The trap is answering 60, which includes those playing both games — "football" and "only football" differ. So the answer is 40.
Source: PYQ-style

125. In an office, 50 employees know Hindi, 40 know English, 30 know French; 15 know Hindi and English, 10 know English and French, 8 know Hindi and French, and 4 know all three. How many know exactly two languages?
(a) 21 (b) 33 (c) 25 (d) 17
Answer: (a)
Solution: Exactly two = (15 − 4) + (10 − 4) + (8 − 4) = 11 + 6 + 4 = 21. The trap is adding the pairwise figures directly (15 + 10 + 8 = 33), which counts the all-three employees three times instead of zero times. So the answer is 21.
Source: PYQ-style
