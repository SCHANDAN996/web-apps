51. In a figure series, a dot moves one corner clockwise in each step, while a small cross moves one corner anticlockwise in each step. If in the first figure the dot is at the top-left corner and the cross is at the top-right corner, where will the dot and the cross be in the fourth figure?
(a) Dot at top-right, cross at top-left (b) Dot at bottom-right, cross at bottom-left (c) Dot at bottom-left, cross at bottom-right (d) Dot at top-left, cross at top-right
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is to move both symbols in the same direction or to count the starting figure as the first move. The dot moves clockwise: top-left, top-right, bottom-right, bottom-left; the cross moves anticlockwise: top-right, top-left, bottom-left, bottom-right. So the answer is dot at bottom-left, cross at bottom-right.
Source: PYQ-style

52. A square is divided into four equal smaller squares. In each successive figure, one more small square is shaded, starting from the top-left and moving clockwise. In the figure that has exactly three shaded squares, which small square is unshaded?
(a) Top-left (b) Top-right (c) Bottom-left (d) Bottom-right
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is to assume the shading moves anticlockwise, which would leave the top-right unshaded. The clockwise shading order is top-left, top-right, bottom-right, bottom-left, so with three squares shaded the only unshaded one is the last in the order. So the answer is bottom-left.
Source: PYQ-style

53. In a series, the number of sides of the outer figure increases by one each step (triangle, square, pentagon, …), while the number of inner lines decreases by one each step (4, 3, 2, …). If the first figure is a triangle with 4 inner lines, what is the fifth figure?
(a) Hexagon with 0 inner lines (b) Hexagon with 1 inner line (c) Heptagon with 1 inner line (d) Heptagon with 0 inner lines
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is counting the first figure as step zero, which gives a hexagon. Sides run 3, 4, 5, 6, 7 and inner lines run 4, 3, 2, 1, 0, so the fifth figure has 7 sides and no inner lines. So the answer is a heptagon with 0 inner lines.

54. Four figures are given. Three of them have an arrow inside a circle pointing in the same direction as a small triangle outside the circle. In one figure, the arrow and the triangle point in opposite directions. Which one is the odd one out?
(a) Figure with arrow up and triangle up (b) Figure with arrow right and triangle right (c) Figure with arrow down and triangle up (d) Figure with arrow left and triangle left
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is to classify by the direction itself rather than the relationship between the two symbols. In three figures the arrow and triangle point the same way; only the figure with arrow down and triangle up has them pointing oppositely. So the answer is the figure with arrow down and triangle up.

55. In a figure series, a black sector of a circle rotates 45° clockwise in each step, and the circle alternates between a single outline and a double outline. If figure 1 has the sector at the top (12 o'clock) with a single outline, what does figure 5 look like?
(a) Sector at bottom, single outline (b) Sector at top, double outline (c) Sector at bottom, double outline (d) Sector at top, single outline
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is rotating 90° per step instead of 45°, which would wrongly bring the sector back to the top. Four steps of 45° give 180°, moving the sector from top to bottom, and the outline sequence single, double, single, double, single makes figure 5 single. So the answer is sector at bottom with a single outline.

56. A figure series shows a square with a dot inside. The dot moves along the four corners clockwise, one corner per step, and the square itself rotates 90° anticlockwise each step. If the dot starts at the top-left corner of the page, where is it on the page in figure 4?
(a) Top-left (b) Bottom-right (c) Top-right (d) Bottom-left
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is to track the dot only relative to the square and forget the square's own rotation. The dot's clockwise shift and the square's anticlockwise rotation cancel each other, so the dot stays at the same page corner in every figure. So the answer is top-left.

61. In a figure series, the number of dots inside a circle doubles each step: 1, 2, 4, 8. A second circle appears whenever the dot count becomes a multiple of 4. How many circles and dots are in the fifth figure?
(a) Two circles with 16 dots (b) Two circles with 32 dots (c) One circle with 16 dots (d) One circle with 32 dots
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is to stop the doubling at 8 or to think the second circle appears only once. The fifth figure has 16 dots, and since 16 is a multiple of 4, the second circle is present. So the answer is two circles with 16 dots.

62. A series shows an arrow rotating 90° clockwise each step while its length alternates long, short, long, short. Figure 1 has a long arrow pointing up. What is figure 6?

Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is to rotate only four times or to start the length alternation with short. Directions run up, right, down, left, up, right, so figure 6 points right; lengths run long, short, long, short, long, short, so figure 6 is short. So the answer is a short arrow pointing right.

64. A figure series shows a triangle that gains one small circle at a vertex each step, going clockwise, and after all vertices are filled the next circle is added again at the first vertex. Figure 1 has one circle at the top vertex. How many circles does figure 4 have in total?
(a) 4 (b) 5 (c) 3 (d) 6
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is to think the series stops once all three vertices are occupied. One circle is added per step: figure 2 has circles at top and bottom-right, figure 3 fills all three vertices, and figure 4 adds a second circle at the top vertex, giving four circles in total. So the answer is 4.

Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is to move the square row by row, jumping to the next row's left cell. The actual path traces the border clockwise: top-left, top-middle, top-right, middle-right, bottom-right, bottom-middle. So the answer is bottom-middle.

68. A series shows figures with an increasing number of triangles: 1, 2, 4, 7, 11. How many triangles should the next figure have?
(a) 15 (b) 16 (c) 17 (d) 18
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is to assume doubling because the first three terms 1, 2, 4 look like doubling. The differences are +1, +2, +3, +4, so the next difference is +5, giving 11 + 5 = 16. So the answer is 16.

70. A figure series alternates two operations: first the figure rotates 90° clockwise, then a side is added (triangle to square to pentagon). Starting from an upright triangle, what is the figure after four operations?
(a) An upright pentagon (b) A pentagon rotated 180° (c) A square rotated 90° (d) A hexagon rotated 180°
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is to apply both operations in every step or to add two sides at once. Operation 1 rotates the triangle 90°, operation 2 makes it a square, operation 3 rotates it to 180° total, and operation 4 makes it a pentagon still at 180°. So the answer is a pentagon rotated 180°.

Shading alternates each step, so figure 5, being odd, matches figure 1 with a shaded outer square; the inner shape sequence gives circle in figures 1–2, square in figures 3–4, and triangle in figures 5–6. So the answer is a shaded outer square with a white triangle inside.

73. In a figure series, each figure is a row of small squares, some shaded. The shaded count follows 1, 1, 2, 3, 5. How many shaded squares are in the next figure?
(a) 8 (b) 7 (c) 9 (d) 6
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is to read the differences as +0, +1, +1, +2 and guess +2 again, giving 7. The sequence is Fibonacci-type, where each term is the sum of the previous two, so the next term is 3 + 5 = 8. So the answer is 8.

74. A series shows a pentagon with one vertex marked. The mark moves two vertices clockwise each step. Starting from the top vertex, at which vertex is the mark in figure 4?

Numbering the top as position 0, the mark lands on positions 0, 2, 4, and then wraps around to position 1 in figure 4, which is the first vertex clockwise from the top. So the answer is the first vertex clockwise from top.

75. In a classification question, four figures each contain exactly three shapes. In three figures all three shapes are different (for example, circle, triangle, square); in one figure two of the three shapes are identical. Which is the odd one out?
(a) Two circles and a triangle (b) Pentagon, circle, hexagon (c) Square, diamond, triangle (d) Circle, triangle, square
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is to classify by whether the shapes have curves or by the number of sides, both of which cut across the groups. The rule is repetition: three figures have all-different shapes, while one repeats a shape; a square and a diamond are drawn as distinct shapes here, so that figure still has three different shapes. So the answer is the figure with two circles and a triangle.
