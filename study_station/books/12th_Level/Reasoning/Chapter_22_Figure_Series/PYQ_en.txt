# 📊 PYQ Analysis — Figure Series / Classification (Non-Verbal Reasoning)

> **Note:** This analysis is based on the recurring pattern of questions asked in SSC (CGL, CHSL, MTS, GD), RRB (NTPC, Group D), and Banking (IBPS, SBI) exams. Instead of invented year-wise counts, we describe *what* is asked and *how* the examiner traps you.

---

## 1. How Often Does This Chapter Appear?

Figure Series and Figure Classification are **permanent fixtures** of the non-verbal reasoning section. In almost every shift of SSC CGL/CHSL and RRB NTPC, you can expect a small but guaranteed presence of these questions — typically 1–3 questions per paper from series, analogy, or odd-figure-out combined. Banking prelims use them less, but IBPS RRB and some state-level exams include them regularly.

**Key takeaway:** This is a *low-volume, high-certainty* chapter. You will not see 10 questions, but the 1–3 that appear are highly predictable in style — making them ideal "quick marks."

---

## 2. Sub-Topic Weightage (in Words, Not Numbers)

| Sub-topic | How often it appears | Difficulty trend |
|---|---|---|
| **Figure Series** (what comes next) | Most frequent | Rotation + addition/deletion of elements dominates |
| **Odd Figure Out / Classification** | Very frequent | Usually one property breaks the pattern (sides, shading, position) |
| **Figure Analogy** (A:B :: C:?) | Moderate | Mirror/rotation-based analogies are favourites |
| **Embedded / Completion figures** | Occasional | More common in SSC MTS/GD level |

**Recurring micro-patterns examiners love:**
- An element rotating 45°/90°/135° clockwise or anti-clockwise.
- Number of sides/lines/dots increasing or decreasing by one each step.
- Shading moving one corner at a time.
- Two elements moving in *opposite* directions simultaneously.
- In classification: four figures share a property (e.g., arrow points to the longer side); one violates it.

---

## 3. Exam-Wise Distribution (Pattern Description)

- **SSC CGL / CHSL:** Series questions with 2 simultaneous changes (rotation + element count). Classification questions based on geometric properties (number of sides vs. number of internal lines).
- **SSC MTS / GD:** Simpler single-rule series; odd-figure-out based on shading or direction.
- **RRB NTPC / Group D:** Series with dot/symbol movement along corners; analogy with mirror images.
- **Banking (IBPS/SBI):** Rare, but when present, uses 5-figure series with alternating rules.

---

## 4. The Examiner's Traps (Psychological Hooks)

1. **Two-rule camouflage:** The obvious rule (rotation) hides the real rule (element count). You pick the option matching rotation but wrong count.
2. **Decoy option:** One option continues the *previous* step's change instead of the *next* step's — it looks right if you only compare the last two figures.
3. **Alternating rules:** Odd positions follow one rule, even positions another. Students apply a single rule and get trapped.
4. **Classification red herring:** Three figures share *two* properties; the odd one shares the obvious property but breaks the subtle one (e.g., all have a dot inside the *smaller* enclosed region).
5. **Direction confusion:** Clockwise vs. anti-clockwise movement — the correct option differs from the decoy only in direction of an arrowhead.

---

## 5. Top 10 High-Yield PYQ-Style Questions (with Solutions & Trap Analysis)

**Q1.** A square contains a dot. In successive figures the dot moves one corner clockwise, while a small line segment is added alternately to the top and right side. What is the 5th figure?
**Answer:** Dot at top-left corner; two line segments (top and right).
**Solution:** Track each element separately. Dot: moving clockwise from TL goes TL→TR→BR→BL→TL, so the 5th figure has the dot back at TL. Lines: added alternately, so by figure 5 both top and right segments exist.
**Trap:** Options include dot at BL (the 4th position) — a decoy for those who miscount steps.
*Source: PYQ-style (SSC CGL pattern)*

**Q2.** Series: triangle (3 sides), square (4), pentagon (5), hexagon (6) — next?
**Answer:** Heptagon (7 sides).
**Solution:** Sides increase by one each step.
**Trap:** An option shows a hexagon rotated — rotation is irrelevant; count is the rule.
*Source: PYQ-style*

**Q3.** Classification: Four figures show a circle with a line through it; in three, the line passes through the centre; in one, it is off-centre. Odd one?
**Answer:** The off-centre figure.
**Solution:** Classification hinges on one geometric property — here, diameter vs. chord.
**Trap:** Students compare line *angles* (a shared variable) instead of position.
*Source: PYQ-style (SSC CHSL pattern)*

**Q4.** An arrow rotates 90° anti-clockwise each step: ↑, ←, ↓, →, ?
**Answer:** ↑
**Solution:** After four 90° rotations the arrow returns to the start.
**Trap:** Option ← tempts those who rotate clockwise by mistake.
*Source: PYQ-style*

**Q5.** Series: a square with 1 dot inside, then 2 dots, then 3, then 4 — but dots alternate between corners and centre. 5th figure?
**Answer:** 5 dots (4 corners + centre).
**Solution:** Count increases by one; positions fill corners first, then centre.
**Trap:** An option with 5 dots all at corners — position rule violated.
*Source: PYQ-style (RRB NTPC pattern)*

**Q6.** Classification: Figures A–D are polygons with internal lines. In A, B, D the number of internal lines equals the number of sides minus 2; in C it does not. Odd one?
**Answer:** C.
**Solution:** Verify the numerical relationship for each figure, not just appearance.
**Trap:** C looks visually similar; only counting reveals the break.
*Source: PYQ-style*

**Q7.** Analogy: Figure I → Figure II is a horizontal mirror flip. Apply the same to Figure III.
**Answer:** The horizontally flipped version of Figure III.
**Solution:** Identify the transformation (mirror, not rotation) before applying.
**Trap:** A 180° rotation option is offered — rotation ≠ mirror for asymmetric figures.
*Source: PYQ-style (SSC CGL pattern)*

**Q8.** Series with two elements: a star moves clockwise one corner per step; a circle moves anti-clockwise two corners per step. Given 4 figures, find the 5th.
**Answer:** Compute both movements independently and pick the option matching both.
**Solution:** Never track only the "salient" element (star); the circle's double-step eliminates two options.
**Trap:** Two options match the star's position but differ in the circle's.
*Source: PYQ-style*
⏭️ **Skip-rule verdict:** If both movements are not clear within 15 seconds, mark and skip — return with fresh eyes; dual-rule questions reward a second look.

**Q9.** Classification: Four figures each contain two shapes, one inside the other. In three, the inner shape has fewer sides than the outer; in one, inner has more. Odd one?
**Answer:** The figure where the inner shape has more sides.
**Solution:** Count sides of inner and outer shapes separately.
**Trap:** Shading differences distract from the side-count rule.
*Source: PYQ-style*

**Q10.** A 5-figure series where figures 1, 3, 5 follow one rule (element added) and figures 2, 4 follow another (element rotated). Find figure 6.
**Answer:** Apply the *even-position* rule (rotation) to figure 5.
**Solution:** Split the series into odd and even positions before concluding.
**Trap:** Applying the addition rule (odd positions) to figure 6 gives the decoy option.
*Source: PYQ-style (Banking pattern)*
⏭️ **Skip-rule verdict:** Alternating-rule series are the most time-consuming type here. If the alternating structure isn't visible in 15 seconds, skip immediately — guessing between two plausible options is a negative-marking risk.

---

## 6. The 15-Second / 45-Second Rule — Reviewed for This Chapter

- **15-second check:** Can you state the rule in one sentence ("dot moves clockwise one corner")? If yes, solve within 45 seconds total.
- **Skip signals:** (a) two simultaneous rules not separable quickly, (b) alternating odd/even rules, (c) classification where no property difference is visible after scanning all figures once.
- **Never skip:** single-rule rotation, side-count series, and shading-movement questions — these are the fastest marks in the whole reasoning paper.
- **Negative-marking caution:** In classification, if you narrow to two options, re-check *one specific property* (sides, position, direction) rather than guessing on "overall look."
