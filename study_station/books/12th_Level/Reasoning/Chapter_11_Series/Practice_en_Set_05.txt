101. Find the missing term: 3, 7, 15, 31, 63, ?
(a) 127 (b) 111 (c) 95 (d) 131
Answer: (a)
Solution: Each term is double the previous term plus 1: 3×2+1=7, 7×2+1=15, 15×2+1=31, 31×2+1=63, so 63×2+1=127. The tempting value 131 comes from wrongly adding 68 as if the gaps doubled without the +1 step. The answer is 127.
Source: PYQ-style

102. Find the missing term: 2, 6, 12, 20, 30, ?
(a) 40 (b) 44 (c) 42 (d) 36
Answer: (c)
Solution: The differences are 4, 6, 8, 10, so the next difference is 12, giving 30+12=42. Equivalently the terms are n(n+1): 1×2, 2×3, …, 6×7=42. The option 40 tempts those who add only 10 again. The answer is 42.

123. Find the missing term: 4, 18, 48, 100, 180, ?
(a) 288 (b) 294 (c) 280 (d) 300
Answer: (b)
Solution: The terms follow n²(n+1): 1²×2=4, 2²×3=12 does not fit, so check differences instead: 14, 30, 52, 80, whose second differences are 16, 22, 28 — growing by 6; the next second difference is 34, the next first difference is 80+34=114, giving 180+114=294. The trap option 288 tempts those who assume a clean ×2 or square pattern. The answer is 294.
