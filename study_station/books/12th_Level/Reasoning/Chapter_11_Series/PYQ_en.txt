# 📊 PYQ Analysis — Series (Verbal Reasoning)

## 1. How Exams Ask Questions from This Chapter

Series is one of the most reliable scoring areas in the reasoning section of SSC (CGL, CHSL, MTS, GD), Banking (IBPS, SBI), and Railway (NTPC, Group D) exams. Almost every paper contains questions from this chapter, usually in the first half of the reasoning section because they are quick to solve.

Examiners ask Series questions in four broad forms:

1. **Number Series** — find the missing or next number in a sequence.
2. **Alphabet Series** — find the missing or next letter/group of letters.
3. **Alpha-Numeric / Mixed Series** — letters and numbers combined.
4. **Wrong Number in Series** — identify the term that breaks the pattern.

The difficulty is rarely in the concept; it is in **pattern recognition speed**. The examiner's real weapon is the *attractive wrong option* — an option that fits the first two or three steps of a pattern but fails later.

---

## 2. Sub-topics That Repeat Most Often (Pattern Analysis in Words)

- **Number series with a constant or growing difference** (addition/subtraction) — the single most common type. Differences often themselves form a series (+2, +4, +6… or ×2, ×3…).
- **Multiplication and mixed operations** (×2 + 1, ×3 − 2, etc.) — very frequent in SSC CGL and Banking.
- **Squares and cubes** (n², n² ± k, n³ ± k) — a favourite of SSC CHSL and Railway exams.
- **Alternate (two interleaved) series** — odd-position terms follow one rule, even-position terms another. Common in Banking exams.
- **Alphabet series based on position gaps** (+2, +3, +4… or reverse counting from Z) — appears in nearly every SSC paper.
- **Letter-pair / letter-group series** (AB, DE, GH… or patterns like AZ, BY, CX) — frequent in SSC and Railway.
- **Wrong-number series** — a Banking-exam speciality; SSC asks it less often but it is appearing more in recent papers.

Less frequent but still seen: Fibonacci-type series (each term = sum of previous two), prime number series, and digit-based patterns (sum of digits, product of digits).

---

## 3. Exam-wise Tendencies

- **SSC CGL / CHSL:** Balanced mix of number and alphabet series. Mixed operations (×n ± k) and square/cube patterns dominate. Usually 1–3 questions.
- **SSC MTS / GD:** Simple difference-based number series and basic alphabet-gap series. Straightforward, high scoring.
- **Banking (IBPS PO/Clerk, SBI):** Wrong-number series and alternate (interleaved) series are the signature types. Patterns are slightly more layered.
- **Railway (NTPC, Group D):** Direct number series and alphabet series with clean gaps; occasionally letter-group series like AZ, BY, CX.

---

## 4. Examiner's Traps (Read Before the Questions)

1. **The "fits-the-first-three" trap:** An option matches the pattern for the first few terms but the actual rule is different (e.g., differences +2, +4, +6 vs. +2, +4, +8).
2. **Alternate-series blindness:** Students look for one rule across all terms and waste time; the series is actually two interleaved series.
3. **Reverse alphabet counting:** Gaps counted from Z (Z=1) confuse students who only memorised A=1…Z=26.
4. **Square/cube ± constant:** 26, 37, 50… looks like +11, +13 but is actually n² + 1 (5²+1, 6²+1, 7²+1).
5. **Wrong-number questions:** The "wrong" term often differs by just 1 or 2 from the correct value — a calculation slip makes you pick the right-looking term.
6. **Negative marking pressure:** Spending 2 minutes on one hard series question costs you 3 easy questions elsewhere.

## 5. Top 10 High-Yield PYQ-Style Questions (with Solutions and Trap Analysis)

**Q1.** Find the next term: 3, 6, 11, 18, 27, ?
(a) 36 (b) 38 (c) 40 (d) 42
**Answer: (b) 38**
**Solution:** Differences are +3, +5, +7, +9, so next difference is +11 → 27 + 11 = 38.
**Trap:** Option (a) 36 tempts students who assume differences of +3, +5, +7, +9, +9 (repeating) or who compute 27 + 9 = 36 by forgetting the difference must grow.
*Source: PYQ-style (SSC pattern)*

**Q2.** Find the missing term: 4, 9, 25, 49, 121, ?
(a) 144 (b) 169 (c) 196 (d) 225
**Answer: (b) 169**
**Solution:** These are squares of consecutive primes: 2², 3², 5², 7², 11², so next is 13² = 169.
**Trap:** Option (a) 144 = 12² tempts students who see "squares" and continue with 12² without noticing the bases are primes, not consecutive numbers.
*Source: PYQ-style (SSC/Banking pattern)*

**Q3.** Find the next term: 2, 6, 12, 20, 30, ?
(a) 36 (b) 40 (c) 42 (d) 56
**Answer: (c) 42**
**Solution:** Pattern is n² + n: 1²+1=2, 2²+2=6, 3²+3=12… so 6²+6 = 42. (Equivalently, differences +4, +6, +8, +10, +12.)
**Trap:** Option (a) 36 attracts students who see 30 and jump to 6² = 36, forgetting the "+n" part.
*Source: PYQ-style*

**Q4.** Find the next term: 5, 11, 23, 47, 95, ?
(a) 189 (b) 191 (c) 193 (d) 197
**Answer: (b) 191**
**Solution:** Each term = previous × 2 + 1: 5×2+1=11, 11×2+1=23, …, 95×2+1 = 191.
**Trap:** Option (a) 189 tempts students who compute 95×2 − 1, mixing up the sign. In ×n ± k patterns, verify the sign on at least two consecutive steps.
*Source: PYQ-style (Banking pattern)*

**Q5.** Find the missing term: 3, 5, 9, 17, 33, ?
(a) 65 (b) 63 (c) 61 (d) 49
**Answer: (a) 65**
**Solution:** Each term = previous × 2 − 1: 3×2−1=5, 5×2−1=9, …, 33×2−1 = 65.
**Trap:** Option (b) 63 = 33×2 − 3; students who misread the constant pick it. Also, differences (2, 4, 8, 16) doubling is an alternative route — cross-check both.
*Source: PYQ-style*

**Q6.** Find the next term: 8, 15, 28, 53, 102, ?
(a) 199 (b) 200 (c) 203 (d) 204
**Answer: (a) 199**
**Solution:** Each term = previous × 2 − 1, −2, −3, −4… : 8×2−1=15, 15×2−2=28, 28×2−3=53, 53×2−4=102, 102×2−5 = 199.
**Trap:** Option (c) 203 = 102×2 − 1 tempts students who assume a constant subtraction. This is a **two-layer pattern** — if the simple rule fails on the third step, look for a growing/shrinking correction term.
**Skip-rule verdict:** If you cannot spot the two-layer pattern within 45 seconds, mark and skip — this question type eats time.
*Source: PYQ-style (Banking pattern)*

**Q7.** Find the next term: 2, 5, 10, 17, 26, ?
(a) 35 (b) 36 (c) 37 (d) 39
**Answer: (c) 37**
**Solution:** Pattern is n² + 1: 1²+1=2, 2²+1=5, 3²+1=10, …, 6²+1 = 37.
**Trap:** Option (a) 35 tempts students using differences +3, +5, +7, +9 who then wrongly add +9 again instead of +11. Always extend the difference series one more step.
*Source: PYQ-style (SSC pattern)*

**Q8.** Find the next letters: AZ, BY, CX, DW, ?
(a) EU (b) EV (c) FV (d) EX
**Answer: (b) EV**
**Solution:** First letters: A, B, C, D, E (+1 each). Second letters: Z, Y, X, W, V (−1 each). So EV.
**Trap:** Option (a) EU attracts students who count the second letter backward but slip one step (W − 2). Write the alphabet positions (26, 25, 24, 23, 22) to avoid the slip.
*Source: PYQ-style (SSC/Railway pattern)*

**Q9.** Find the next term: 1, 4, 9, 16, 25, 36, ?
(a) 42 (b) 47 (c) 49 (d) 50
**Answer: (c) 49**
**Solution:** Perfect squares: 1² to 6², so next is 7² = 49.
**Trap:** Option (d) 50 tempts students who compute differences (+3, +5, +7, +9, +11) and then add +14 instead of +13 in haste. Easy questions are lost to arithmetic slips, not concepts.
*Source: PYQ-style*

**Q10.** Find the wrong number: 2, 3, 5, 8, 13, 21, 34, 55, 90
(a) 13 (b) 21 (c) 55 (d) 90
**Answer: (d) 90**
**Solution:** This is a Fibonacci-type series: each term = sum of the previous two. After 34 and 55, the next term should be 34 + 55 = 89, not 90.
**Trap:** The wrong term 90 is only 1 away from the correct 89 — deliberately placed so a quick glance misses it. In wrong-number questions, verify the rule on **every** term, not just up to the first suspicious one.
**Skip-rule verdict:** Wrong-number questions need full verification; if the pattern isn't visible in 45 seconds, skip and return later.
*Source: PYQ-style (Banking pattern)*

## 6. The 15-Second / 45-Second Rule — Reviewed Against Real Patterns

- **15-second check:** In the first 15 seconds, compute the differences (or ratios) between consecutive terms. If a clear pattern (constant difference, growing difference, ×n ± k, squares/cubes) appears, solve immediately — most Series questions fall here.
- **45-second limit:** If after 45 seconds you have tried differences, ratios, and square/cube checks with no result, the question is likely an alternate (interleaved) series or a two-layer pattern. **Mark it and move on.** Return only if time remains.
- **Alphabet series:** Convert letters to positions immediately (A=1…Z=26). If you haven't memorised positions, this conversion alone can exceed 45 seconds — practise positions until automatic.
- **Wrong-number series:** Budget up to 60 seconds because every term must be verified, but never more. One wrong-number question is not worth three easy ones.

**Bottom line:** Series rewards pattern recognition, not deep calculation. Train your eye on differences first, ratios second, squares/cubes third, interleaving fourth — and skip ruthlessly when none clicks in 45 seconds.
