Card 1
Front: What is the very first thing you should check when you see a number series?
Back: The gap (difference) between consecutive terms. If differences are constant, it's an arithmetic series; if differences themselves form a pattern, it's a second-level series.

Card 2
Front: In the series 2, 6, 12, 20, 30, ? — the differences are 4, 6, 8, 10. What comes next and why?
Back: 42. The differences increase by 2 each time (4, 6, 8, 10, 12), so 30 + 12 = 42. This is a "difference of differences" pattern.

Card 3
Front: How do you quickly recognize a geometric (multiplication) series?
Back: Divide each term by the previous one. If the ratio is constant (e.g., ×2, ×3), it's geometric. Example: 3, 6, 12, 24 — ratio is 2 throughout.

Card 4
Front: What is the mnemonic trick "D-M-S" for solving number series?
Back: Check in this order: Difference → Multiplication → Squares/Cubes. Most exam series fall into one of these three families, so test them in this sequence before trying anything complex.

Card 5
Front: In the series 1, 4, 9, 16, 25, ? — what pattern is used and what is the next term?
Back: Perfect squares: 1², 2², 3², 4², 5², so next is 6² = 36. Always check squares and cubes when differences keep growing.

Card 6
Front: What is a "mixed" or "two-rule" series? Give an example.
Back: A series where two operations alternate, e.g., ×2, +3, ×2, +3… Example: 2, 4, 7, 14, 17, 34 — next is 37 (34 + 3).

Card 7
Front: In an alphabet series like A, C, F, J, ? — how do you find the next letter?
Back: Convert letters to positions (A=1, C=3, F=6, J=10). Gaps are +2, +3, +4, so next gap is +5: J(10) + 5 = O(15).

Card 8
Front: What is the "A=1 to Z=26" memory trick every aspirant must master?
Back: Remember anchor letters: E=5, J=10, O=15, T=20, Y=25 (mnemonic: "EJOTY"). From these anchors you can count any letter's position in 2 seconds — e.g., Z is just one after Y, so Z=26.

Card 9
Front: Examiner's trap: In the series 3, 5, 9, 17, 33, ? — why is "65" correct but "49" a trap?
Back: Each term is (previous × 2) − 1: 3×2−1=5, 5×2−1=9… so 33×2−1=65. The trap option 49 comes from wrongly adding the last difference (33 + 16) instead of applying the ×2−1 rule.

Card 10
Front: What is a Fibonacci-type (addition) series? Give an example.
Back: Each term is the sum of the previous two: 1, 1, 2, 3, 5, 8, 13… In exams, variants like 2, 3, 5, 8, 13, 21 are common.

Card 11
Front: In the series 8, 27, 64, 125, ? — what is the pattern and the answer?
Back: Perfect cubes: 2³, 3³, 4³, 5³, so next is 6³ = 216. If numbers jump rapidly, think cubes, not multiplication.

Card 12
Front: What is an "alternate" (twin) series and how do you crack it?
Back: Two independent series are interleaved. Solve odd-position terms and even-position terms separately. Example: 1, 10, 3, 20, 5, 30, ? → odd terms 1, 3, 5, 7 → answer 7.

Card 13
Front: Examiner's trap: In letter series, why do students wrongly answer "Z, X, V, ?" with "S"?
Back: The gaps are −2 each: Z(26), X(24), V(22), so next is T(20), not S. The trap is assuming the gap increases or skipping the position-count step and guessing by "look."

Card 14
Front: In the series 2, 3, 5, 7, 11, 13, ? — what special pattern is this?
Back: Prime numbers in order. Next prime after 13 is 17. Examiners love prime-number series because students keep hunting for differences.

Card 15
Front: What should you do when no difference, ratio, or square pattern fits a series?
Back: Check for: (1) alternate/twin series, (2) digit-based patterns (sum of digits, reversed digits), (3) prime numbers, (4) two alternating operations like ×2, −1.

Card 16
Front: In the series 1, 2, 6, 24, 120, ? — what operation is used?
Back: Multiplication by increasing numbers: ×2, ×3, ×4, ×5, so next is ×6 → 720. (These are factorials: 1!, 2!, 3!, 4!, 5!, 6!.)

Card 17
Front: What is the 15-second rule for series questions in the exam?
Back: Spend at most 15 seconds identifying the pattern family (difference/ratio/square). If nothing clicks, mark it and move on — return only if time remains. Never burn 2 minutes on one series.

Card 18
Front: In a letter-number mixed series like A1, C4, F9, J16, ? — how do you solve it?
Back: Split into two tracks: letters A, C, F, J (gaps +2, +3, +4 → next +5 = O) and numbers 1, 4, 9, 16 (squares → next 25). Answer: O25.

Card 19
Front: Why is the series 4, 9, 25, 49, 121, ? tricky, and what is the answer?
Back: These are squares of prime numbers: 2², 3², 5², 7², 11², so next is 13² = 169. The trap is treating them as ordinary squares and answering 144 (12²).

Card 20
Front: What is the "wrong number in series" question type and the strategy to solve it?
Back: One term breaks the pattern. First find the rule using the majority of terms, then identify which term violates it. Never start by assuming the first term is wrong — verify the rule on at least 3 consecutive terms.
