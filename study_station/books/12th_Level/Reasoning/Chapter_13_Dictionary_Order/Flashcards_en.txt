Card 1
Front: What is the very first step when arranging words in dictionary order?
Back: Compare the first letter of every word. The word whose first letter comes earliest in the alphabet comes first. Only if first letters are the same do you move to the second letter.

Card 2
Front: If two words share the same first three letters, which letter decides their order?
Back: The fourth letter — you always move to the first position where the letters differ, and the word with the earlier letter at that position comes first.

Card 3
Front: Which comes first in a dictionary — "CAT" or "CATALOGUE"? Why?
Back: CAT comes first. When one word is a complete prefix of another, the shorter word comes first (think of the missing letter as "nothing", which beats any letter).

Card 4
Front: What is the quick mnemonic to remember the alphabet positions of E, J, O, T, Y?
Back: "EJOTY" — E=5, J=10, O=15, T=20, Y=25. These five anchor points let you find any letter's position in 2–3 seconds.

Card 5
Front: What is the reverse-position mnemonic trick for letters?
Back: "Sum to 27" — a letter's position from the end = 27 − position from the start. Example: C is 3rd from start, so 27−3 = 24th from end.

Card 6
Front: Examiner's trap: "APPLE, APPLICATION, APPLY, APPLAUSE" — what mistake do most students make here?
Back: They stop comparing too early. All four start with "APPL", so the 5th letter decides: APPLAUSE (A), APPLE (E), APPLICATION (I), APPLY (Y). Correct order: APPLAUSE → APPLE → APPLICATION → APPLY.

Card 7
Front: In "which word comes 3rd in dictionary order" questions, what must you do before answering?
Back: Arrange ALL the given words in complete dictionary order first, then count. Never guess from the first letters alone — ties at early positions change everything.

Card 8
Front: How do you handle words that are identical up to the point where one word ends?
Back: The shorter word always comes first. Example: "PEN" before "PENCIL", "GO" before "GOAT".

Card 9
Front: What is the fastest way to find which word comes LAST in dictionary order?
Back: Find the word whose first differing letter is latest in the alphabet — often the word starting with the letter closest to Z. Then verify ties at the next positions.

Card 10
Front: Examiner's trap: options like "MANGO, MANGOES, MANGROVE" — where do students slip?
Back: They assume MANGOES comes right after MANGO without checking MANGROVE. Compare 5th letter: MANGO (O), MANGOES (O), MANGROVE (R). MANGO < MANGOES (shorter first) < MANGROVE.

Card 11
Front: What does "dictionary order" ignore completely?
Back: Word meanings, word length (except the prefix rule), and pronunciation. Only the alphabetical sequence of letters matters.

Card 12
Front: If asked "which word comes between X and Y", what is the safe method?
Back: Write the full sorted list of all given words, then check which option falls strictly between X and Y in that sorted list.

Card 13
Front: How do capital vs small letters affect dictionary order in exams?
Back: They don't — exam questions treat all letters as the same case. Compare letters purely by alphabet position.

Card 14
Front: What is the position of M in the alphabet, and what trick confirms it instantly?
Back: M is 13th. Trick: M is the middle letter of the alphabet — 12 letters come before it (A–L) and 13 come after it (N–Z).

Card 15
Front: When arranging names like "Sharma, Shukla, Singh, Sinha", which letter position decides the order?
Back: First two letters "Sh/Si" split them: Sh- words before Si- words (H < I). Then Sharma vs Shukla: 3rd letter A < U. Order: Sharma → Shukla → Singh → Sinha (Singh vs Sinha: 4th letter G < H).

Card 16
Front: Why should you never skip writing the letter positions above each word in the exam?
Back: Writing positions (e.g., C=3, A=1, T=20) converts a visual comparison into number comparison, which is faster and removes careless errors on look-alike words.

Card 17
Front: What is the one-line rule for the whole chapter?
Back: Compare letter by letter from the left; the first position where letters differ decides the order; if one word ends first, it comes first.

Card 18
Front: In a question with 5 words, how many comparisons are usually enough to sort them?
Back: Rarely more than 2–3 letter positions per pair. Group words by first letter first — this cuts the work drastically before comparing within groups.
