# 📊 PYQ Analysis — Dictionary Order (Verbal Reasoning)

*(Pattern analysis based on the recurring style of questions in SSC, Banking, Railway and State exams. No fabricated year-wise counts — only verified question patterns.)*

---

## 1. How Exams Ask Questions from This Chapter

Dictionary Order questions appear in almost every Verbal Reasoning paper, but the **way** they are asked changes from exam to exam. Broadly, examiners use four formats:

**Format A — Direct Arrangement (most common)**
Five or six words are given; arrange them as they would appear in a dictionary, then answer "which word comes first / last / third?" This is the bread-and-butter question of SSC CHSL, RRB NTPC and IBPS Clerk.

**Format B — Serial Number Format**
Words are labelled (1), (2), (3), (4), (5) and the options are sequences like "(3), (1), (4), (2), (5)". The trap: students arrange correctly but misread the option sequence. SSC CGL loves this format.

**Format C — Letter-Substitution Format**
"If the first and third letters of each word are interchanged, which word will come first in a dictionary?" This is a two-step question and appears in Banking (IBPS PO) and SSC CGL Tier-I.

**Format D — Positional / Counting Format**
"How many words in the list begin with a letter that comes before 'M' in the alphabet?" or "Which letter will be in the middle when the letters of the word X are arranged alphabetically?" These blend Dictionary Order with Alphabet Test.

---

## 2. Sub-Topic Weightage (Qualitative)

- **Direct arrangement of 4–6 words** — the dominant pattern; expect at least one such question in almost every SSC/Railway shift.
- **Serial-number sequencing** — frequent in SSC CGL and CHSL; slightly more time-consuming.
- **Words differing only at the 3rd or 4th letter** — the examiner's favourite trap zone (e.g., *Praise, Prank, Prayer, Precise*).
- **Letter rearrangement inside a word, then dictionary order** — occasional, mostly Banking and CGL.
- **Reverse dictionary order (last to first)** — rare but deadly; students arrange correctly and then answer the wrong end.

---

## 3. Exam-Wise Distribution (Pattern, Not Numbers)

| Exam | Typical Style | Difficulty |
|---|---|---|
| SSC CHSL / MTS | Direct arrangement, 4–5 words | Easy |
| SSC CGL | Serial-number format, words differing at 3rd–4th letter | Easy–Moderate |
| RRB NTPC / Group D | Direct arrangement, sometimes reverse order | Easy |
| IBPS / SBI (Clerk & PO) | Letter-substitution + arrangement combos | Moderate |
| State PCS / Police | Direct arrangement, occasionally with Hindi-alphabet logic | Easy |

## 4. Top 10 High-Yield Questions (with Solutions & Trap Analysis)

---

**Q1.** Arrange the following words in dictionary order:
(1) Plaster (2) Planet (3) Plaque (4) Plasma (5) Plastic

(a) 2, 3, 4, 1, 5
(b) 2, 4, 3, 1, 5
(c) 3, 2, 4, 5, 1
(d) 2, 3, 4, 5, 1

**Answer: (a)**

**Solution:** All begin with "Pla". Compare the 4th letter: n (Planet), q (Plaque), s (Plasma, Plaster, Plastic). Order so far: Planet → Plaque → [s-group]. Among Plasma, Plaster, Plastic, compare the 5th letter: m (Plasma), s (Plaster), t (Plastic) → Plasma → Plaster → Plastic. Final order: 2, 3, 4, 1, 5.

**Trap:** Options (a) and (d) differ only in the last two positions. The examiner banks on students stopping the comparison at the 4th letter and guessing between Plaster and Plastic. **Never stop comparing until the tie is fully broken.**

---

**Q2.** Which word will come **first** in a dictionary?
(a) Excite (b) Exclaim (c) Excuse (d) Excerpt

**Answer: (d)**

**Solution:** All start with "Exc". Compare the 4th letter: i (Excite), l (Exclaim), u (Excuse), e (Excerpt). Alphabetically e < i < l < u, so **Excerpt** comes first.

**Trap:** This is the classic "almost-same prefix" trap. Students see "Exc" everywhere and rush. The deciding letter hides at position 4. *(In the exam hall, always write the deciding letter above each word.)*

---

**Q3.** Arrange in dictionary order:
(1) Mango (2) Manner (3) Manual (4) Mantle (5) Manage

(a) 5, 2, 3, 4, 1
(b) 5, 1, 2, 4, 3
(c) 5, 3, 2, 4, 1
(d) 5, 2, 3, 1, 4

**Answer: (b)**

**Solution:** All start with "Man". Compare the 4th letter: a (Manage), g (Mango), n (Manner), u (Manual), t (Mantle). Alphabetically: a < g < n < t < u. So the order is: Manage → Mango → Manner → Mantle → Manual = **5, 1, 2, 4, 3**.

**Trap:** Five words sharing a 3-letter prefix is the maximum-density trap. Note that Mango's 4th letter is **g** (M-a-n-**g**-o), not 'o' — misreading the deciding letter is the single most common error here. Examiners know students lose patience at the 4th letter.

---

**Q4.** If the words are arranged in dictionary order, which word will be in the **middle**?
(a) Fountain (b) Foundation (c) Founder (d) Foundry (e) Fount

**Answer: (d) Foundry**

**Solution:** All start with "Fou". Split into two groups at the 5th letter: "Found-" group (d) and "Fount" group (t); d < t, so the Found-group comes first. Within "Found-": Foundation (a), Founder (e), Foundry (r) → Foundation, Founder, Foundry. Within "Fount": Fount comes before Fountain (when one word is a prefix of another, the shorter word comes first). Full order: Foundation, Founder, Foundry, Fount, Fountain. Middle (3rd of 5) = **Foundry**.

**Trap:** Two hooks here — (i) "Found" vs "Fount" differ at the 5th letter (d vs t), and (ii) the prefix rule (Fount before Fountain). Students who forget the prefix rule place Fountain before Fount and pick the wrong middle.

---

**Q5.** Arrange in dictionary order and find which comes **last**:
(a) Serious (b) Service (c) Servant (d) Serpent

**Answer: (b) Service**

**Solution:** All start with "Ser". 4th letter: i (Serious), v (Service, Servant), p (Serpent). Order of 4th letters: i < p < v. So Serious first, Serpent second, then the v-group: Servant vs Service — 5th letter a vs i → Servant before Service. Full order: Serious, Serpent, Servant, Service. Last = **Service**.

**Trap:** The question asks for **last**, not first. Students who arrange correctly but answer the first word (Serious) fall into the "wrong end" trap. Underline "last" the moment you read it.

---

**Q6.** If the first and last letters of each word are interchanged, which word will come first in a dictionary?
(a) Master (b) Raster (c) Castor (d) Pastor

**Answer: (a) Master**

**Solution:** Transform every word first:
- Master → raste**M** (M↔r)
- Raster → raster (first and last letters are both 'r', unchanged)
- Castor → rasto**C** (C↔r)
- Pastor → rasto**P** (P↔r)

All four now start with "rast". Compare the 5th letter: e (rasteM, raster), o (rastoC, rastoP). Since e < o, the winner is between "rastem" and "raster". 6th letter: m vs r → m < r → **Master** comes first.

**Trap:** This is a two-step question. The lazy student compares the original words and picks Castor ('C' is earliest). The examiner rewards only those who transform **every** word first. **Skip-rule flag: if you cannot do the interchange mentally in 15 seconds, mark and move — this is a 45-second question.**

---

**Q7.** Which of the following words will appear **second** in a dictionary?
(a) Quality (b) Quarter (c) Quarrel (d) Quantity

**Answer: (d) Quantity**

**Solution:** All start with "Qua". 4th letter: l (Quality), r (Quarter, Quarrel), n (Quantity). Order: l < n < r → Quality, Quantity, then the r-group. Quarter vs Quarrel: 5th letter t vs r → r < t → Quarrel before Quarter. Full order: Quality, Quantity, Quarrel, Quarter. Second = **Quantity**.

**Trap:** Students assume the answer must lie in the look-alike pair (Quarter/Quarrel) and ignore that 'l' and 'n' come before 'r'. The look-alike pair is a decoy for positions 3 and 4, not 1 and 2.

---

**Q8.** Arrange the letters of the word "DANGER" in alphabetical order. Which letter will be at the 4th position?
(a) N (b) E (c) G (d) R

**Answer: (c) G**

**Solution:** Letters: D, A, N, G, E, R. Alphabetical order: A, D, E, G, N, R. The 4th letter is **G**.

**Trap:** DANGER has 6 letters — an even count — so it has no single "middle" letter (positions 3 and 4, i.e., E and G, share the middle). Examiners exploit the odd-length assumption by asking vaguely for "the middle"; here the question pins down the exact position, so read precisely what is asked. For n letters, a unique middle = (n+1)/2 th position exists only when n is odd.

---

**Q9.** Arrange in dictionary order:
(1) Episode (2) Epitaph (3) Epilogue (4) Epidemic (5) Epithet

(a) 4, 1, 3, 2, 5
(b) 4, 3, 1, 2, 5
(c) 4, 1, 3, 5, 2
(d) 1, 4, 3, 2, 5

**Answer: (b) 4, 3, 1, 2, 5**

**Solution:** All start with "Epi". 4th letter: d (Epidemic), l (Epilogue), s (Episode), t (Epitaph, Epithet). Order: d < l < s < t → Epidemic, Epilogue, Episode, then the t-group. Epitaph vs Epithet: 5th letter a vs h → Epitaph before Epithet. Final: 4, 3, 1, 2, 5.

**Trap:** Options (a) and (b) swap positions 2 and 3 (Epilogue vs Episode). The deciding letters are 'l' vs 's' at position 4 — a one-glance difference. This is the examiner's favourite "adjacent-swap" option design.

---

**Q10.** In a dictionary, which word will come **immediately after** "Humble"?
(a) Humid (b) Humming (c) Humility (d) Human

**Answer: (a) Humid**

**Solution:** All share "Hum". Compare 4th letters: Humble = b, Humid = i, Humming = m, Humility = i, Human = a. Since a < b, **Human comes before Humble** — it is the planted decoy. Among the rest, the i-group (Humid, Humility) beats Humming (m). Humid vs Humility: 4th letter both 'i'; 5th letter d vs l → d < l → Humid first. So the order begins: Human, Humble, **Humid**, Humility, Humming. The word immediately after Humble is **Humid**.

**Trap:** The decoy Human (a < b) catches students who never check whether an option actually falls on the correct side of the reference word. Defence: fix the reference word's deciding letter first, then test each option against it.

---

## 5. The 15-Second / 45-Second Rule — Applied

- **15-second questions (attempt immediately):** Q2, Q5, Q7 — single-step comparisons where only one letter position matters. Spot the first point of difference and answer.
- **45-second questions (attempt, but budget time):** Q1, Q3, Q4, Q9 — five words with long common prefixes; write the deciding letter above each word before answering.
- **Skip-first-pass questions:** Q6 (letter interchange) and Q10 (position relative to a reference word) — two-step logic with multiple candidates. Mark them, finish the easy ones, return in the second pass. In SSC CGL, spending 90 seconds on Q6 while three 15-second questions wait is a net loss of marks.

---

## 6. Examiner's Psychological Hooks — Summary

1. **Adjacent-swap options** — two options differ by one swapped pair (Q1, Q9). Defence: compare until the tie breaks fully.
2. **Wrong-end trap** — question says "last", student answers "first" (Q5). Defence: underline the keyword.
3. **Decoy look-alike pair** — the similar words distract from an earlier-position answer (Q7). Defence: always start comparing from letter 1, never from the interesting pair.
4. **Transformation-first trap** — answer requires modifying words before comparing (Q6). Defence: transform every option, not just the promising one.
5. **Boundary decoy** — in reference-word questions, one option lies on the wrong side of the boundary (Q10). Defence: check the reference word's deciding letter first.
6. **Even-count middle** — no unique middle letter exists (Q8). Defence: count letters before answering.
