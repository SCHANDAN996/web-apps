# 🧒 The Feynman Way — Blood Relations (Coded)

## The Hardest Part, Made Simple

The toughest thing in coded blood relations is not the family tree — it's the **code itself**. When you see `A + B × C – D`, your brain wants to do maths. But here `+` might mean "mother of" and `×` might mean "brother of". The symbols are wearing masks, and your job is to unmask them.

## A Story: The Secret Language of Riya's Family

Imagine 12-year-old Riya moves to a new school. Her classmates have invented a secret code so teachers can't understand their notes:

- `+` means "is the mother of"
- `–` means "is the brother of"
- `×` means "is the wife of"

One day Riya finds a note: **"P + Q – R × S"**.

Her friends giggle. Riya panics — it looks like maths homework! But then she remembers the trick her elder brother taught her: **"Don't read it like maths. Read it like a story, one pair at a time, and draw it."**

So she starts:

1. **P + Q** → P is the mother of Q. (P is female, one generation above Q.)
2. **Q – R** → Q is the brother of R. (Q is male, same generation as R.)
3. **R × S** → R is the wife of S. (R is female, S is male, same generation.)

Now she draws a quick family tree: P at the top, Q and R below her, and S married to R.

The note's question was: *"How is P related to S?"*

Riya looks at her tree: P is the mother of R, and R is the wife of S. So P is S's **mother-in-law**! The whole class claps. The "maths problem" was never maths at all — it was just a family story hiding behind symbols.

## The Unforgettable Trick: **"PAIR → PLACE → PICTURE"**

- **PAIR** — Break the code into pairs of two letters (A+B, then B×C, then C–D). Never try to swallow the whole chain at once.
- **PLACE** — Place each pair on your rough family tree: same generation on the same level, parents one level up, children one level down. Mark gender: circle for female, square for male.
- **PICTURE** — Only after the full picture is drawn, answer the question by *walking* along the tree from one person to the other.

**Golden rule:** In coded relations, the *first* letter usually performs the action on the *second* letter ("A + B" = A is mother of B). But always read the code key given in the question first — some exams reverse it! That 5-second check saves you from the examiner's favourite trap.

## Another Trap to Remember

If the question asks "How is **X related to Y**?" and X's gender is never revealed by the code, the answer might be "cannot be determined" — or the examiner may offer both "nephew" and "niece" as options to lure you. Trust only what the code tells you, never what you assume.
