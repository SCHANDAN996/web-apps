26. If 'A + B' means 'A is the father of B', 'A − B' means 'A is the mother of B', and 'A × B' means 'A is the sister of B', then how is P related to S in 'P × Q − R + S'?
(a) Aunt (b) Grandmother (c) Sister (d) Mother
Answer: (a)
Solution: The tempting answer is 'Sister', which only reads the first symbol and ignores the chain. P × Q means P is the sister of Q; Q − R means Q is the mother of R; R + S means R is the father of S. So Q is the grandmother of S and P, being Q's sister, is the aunt of S, so the answer is Aunt.
Source: PYQ-style

27. If 'P @ Q' means 'P is the wife of Q', 'P # Q' means 'P is the daughter of Q', and 'P $ Q' means 'P is the father of Q', then which expression shows that 'T is the daughter of M'?
(a) M $ T # R (b) R @ M $ T (c) T # R @ M (d) T @ R # M
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is the third option, where M $ T makes M the father of T, but T's gender is never fixed, so T could be a son. In the second option, T # R means T is the daughter of R, and R @ M means R is the wife of M, so M is T's father and T is the daughter of M, so the answer is T # R @ M.
Source: PYQ-style

28. Pointing to a photograph, Ravi said, "She is the daughter of my grandfather's only son." How is the woman in the photograph related to Ravi?
(a) Cousin (b) Aunt (c) Mother (d) Sister
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is 'Aunt', chosen by those who read 'grandfather's son' as Ravi's uncle. Grandfather's only son is Ravi's own father, and the daughter of Ravi's father is Ravi's sister, so the answer is Sister.

29. If 'A × B' means 'A is the brother of B', 'A ÷ B' means 'A is the mother of B', and 'A + B' means 'A is the father of B', then how is M related to T in 'M ÷ N × O + T'?
(a) Aunt (b) Grandmother (c) Mother (d) Sister
Answer: (b)
Solution: The tempting wrong answer is 'Mother', which stops after the first symbol. M ÷ N means M is the mother of N; N × O means N is the brother of O; O + T means O is the father of T. So N and O are sons of M, and T is O's child, making M the grandmother of T, so the answer is Grandmother.

30. A is the brother of B. B is the daughter of C. D is the father of C. How is A related to D?
(a) Grandson (b) Son (c) Brother (d) Nephew
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is 'Son', which skips one generation. D is the father of C, C is the parent of B (since B is C's daughter), and A is B's brother, so A is also C's child. Hence A is the grandson of D, so the answer is Grandson.

31. If 'P % Q' means 'P is the husband of Q', 'P & Q' means 'P is the daughter of Q', and 'P * Q' means 'P is the brother of Q', then how is X related to Z in 'X & Y % W * Z'?
(a) Niece (b) Daughter (c) Sister (d) Mother
Answer: (a)
Solution: The tempting answer is 'Daughter', which ignores the last link. X & Y means X is the daughter of Y; Y % W means Y is the husband of W; W * Z means W is the brother of Z. So X is the daughter of W, and W is Z's brother, making X the niece of Z, so the answer is Niece.

33. If 'M + N' means 'M is the sister of N', 'M − N' means 'M is the father of N', and 'M × N' means 'M is the son of N', then which expression means 'R is the paternal aunt of T'?
(a) R × S − T (b) S − R + T (c) R + S − T (d) T × R − S
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is the first option, where R × S makes R the son of S and S − T makes S the father of T, so R becomes T's brother, not aunt. In the third option, R + S means R is the sister of S, and S − T means S is the father of T; the sister of T's father is the paternal aunt, so the answer is R + S − T.

Solution: The trap is 'Aunt', which treats Q as S's aunt but forgets P's generation. Q and R are children of P, and S is the child of R, so P is the grandmother of S, so the answer is Grandmother.

37. If 'X > Y' means 'X is the father of Y', 'X < Y' means 'X is the daughter of Y', and 'X = Y' means 'X is the sister of Y', then how is A related to D in 'A < B > C = D'?
(a) Daughter (b) Mother (c) Sister (d) Niece
Answer: (c)
Solution: The tempting answer is 'Niece', which wrongly places A a generation below D. A < B means A is the daughter of B; B > C means B is the father of C; C = D means C is the sister of D. So C and D are children of B, and A is also a child of B, making A the sister of D, so the answer is Sister.

38. A man said to a lady, "Your mother's husband's sister is my aunt." How is the lady related to the man?
(a) Sister (b) Mother (c) Cousin (d) Aunt
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is 'Sister', which assumes both share the same parents. The lady's mother's husband is her father; her father's sister is her aunt, and that same woman is the man's aunt too. Two people sharing an aunt (but not parents) are cousins, so the answer is Cousin.

39. If 'P × Q' means 'P is the husband of Q', 'P + Q' means 'P is the daughter of Q', and 'P − Q' means 'P is the son of Q', then how is E related to H in 'E + F × G − H'?
(a) Granddaughter (b) Daughter (c) Mother (d) Sister
Answer: (a)
Solution: The tempting answer is 'Daughter', which stops midway. E + F means E is the daughter of F; F × G means F is the husband of G; G − H means G is the son of H. So F and G are parents of E, and G is H's son, making E the granddaughter of H, so the answer is Granddaughter.

40. R is the sister of S. S is the son of T. T is the wife of U. V is the son of U. How is V related to R?
(a) Father (b) Uncle (c) Brother (d) Cousin
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is 'Uncle', which adds an extra generation. T and U are husband and wife; S and V are both sons of U (and T), and R is S's sister, so R is also a child of T and U. Hence V is R's brother, so the answer is Brother.

(a) M * O / N (b) M / O * N (c) O * M / N (d) M − O / N
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is the third option, where O * M makes O the brother of M and M / N makes M the mother of N — the exact opposite of what is needed. In the second option, M * O means M is the brother of O, and O / N means O is the mother of N; a brother of N's mother is the maternal uncle, so the answer is M * O / N.

49. If 'M # N' means 'M is the daughter of N', 'M $ N' means 'M is the husband of N', and 'M @ N' means 'M is the brother of N', then which expression shows that 'W is the granddaughter of Z'?
(a) W # X # Z (b) W # Y @ Z (c) W # X $ Z (d) Z @ Y # W
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is the first option, where W is the daughter of X who is merely the husband of Z — that does not make W a granddaughter of Z. In the third option, W # X means W is the daughter of X, and X # Z means X is the daughter of Z, so W is the daughter of Z's daughter, i.e., the granddaughter of Z, so the answer is W # X # Z.
