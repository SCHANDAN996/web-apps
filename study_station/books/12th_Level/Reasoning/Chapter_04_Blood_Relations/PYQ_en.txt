📊 PYQ ANALYSIS — BLOOD RELATIONS (CODED)

⚠️ Note: This analysis is based on the *pattern* of questions asked across SSC, Banking, Railway and State exams. We do not claim exact year-wise counts — instead, here is what examiners repeatedly ask and how they trap you.

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

1. HOW OFTEN DOES THIS CHAPTER APPEAR?

Blood Relations is a permanent fixture of the reasoning section. In almost every shift of SSC CGL/CHSL, IBPS PO/Clerk, SBI PO and RRB NTPC, at least one question touches family relations. The *coded* form (A + B means A is the father of B, etc.) has become the dominant style in Banking exams, while SSC and Railway still mix coded and descriptive ("pointing to a photograph…") forms. Expect 1–3 questions per paper — small in number, but highly scoring because the method is fixed.

2. SUB-TOPIC WEIGHTAGE (IN WORDS)

• Coded relations with symbols (+, −, ×, ÷ or custom codes) — the most frequent form in Banking; decoding speed decides your score.
• "Pointing to / introducing" descriptive puzzles — the classic SSC/Railway favourite.
• Relation-chain questions ("How is X related to Y?" through 3–4 links) — common everywhere.
• Gender-determination questions ("Which statement confirms that R is female?") — a rising Banking trend and the biggest trap zone.
• Family-tree / generation questions (grandfather, maternal uncle, cousin) — steady presence in SSC CGL and State exams.

3. EXAM-WISE TENDENCIES

• SSC (CGL/CHSL/MTS): Short coded chains (3–4 symbols) and photograph-pointing questions. Usually solvable in under a minute.
• Banking (IBPS/SBI): Longer symbol codes, sometimes 5–6 terms, plus "which expression means…" reverse questions. Gender-confirmation traps are common.
• Railway (NTPC/Group D): Simple descriptive relations; difficulty is low but options are close.
• State PCS/ Police exams: Direct two-link relations; rarely coded.

4. EXAMINER'S TRAPS (PSYCHOLOGICAL HOOKS)

Trap 1 — Gender assumption: Names like "Kiran", "Suman", "Neeraj" are gender-neutral. Examiners bank on you assuming gender from the name. Never assume; use only what the code states.

Trap 2 — Symbol reversal: "A + B means A is the mother of B" vs "B is the mother of A". Reading the definition backwards is the single most common error. Read every definition twice.

Trap 3 — "Cannot be determined" as bait: In gender questions, one option is often genuinely undeterminable — but examiners also place a determinable answer nearby to make you doubt yourself.

Trap 4 — Order of solving: In a chain like P × Q + R − S, students solve left-to-right emotionally instead of bracketing carefully. One wrong link flips the final answer.

Trap 5 — Near-identical options: "Brother-in-law" vs "Brother", "Maternal uncle" vs "Paternal uncle". The difference is one link — the mother's side vs father's side.

5. TOP 10 HIGH-YIELD PYQ-STYLE QUESTIONS

Q1. If A + B means A is the brother of B; A − B means A is the sister of B; A × B means A is the mother of B, then which of the following means P is the maternal uncle of R?
(a) P + Q × R (b) P − Q × R (c) P × Q + R (d) P × Q − R
Answer: (a)
Solution: Maternal uncle = brother of the mother. P + Q → P is the brother of Q; Q × R → Q is the mother of R. So P is the brother of R's mother = maternal uncle.
Trap: Students pick (b) because "−" sits next to "×" — but "−" makes P a *sister* (maternal aunt), not uncle. Gender of the first link is the lock.
Source: PYQ-style (Banking pattern)

Q2. Pointing to a woman, Ramesh said, "She is the daughter of my grandfather's only son." How is the woman related to Ramesh?
(a) Mother (b) Sister (c) Aunt (d) Cousin
Answer: (b)
Solution: Grandfather's only son = Ramesh's father. Father's daughter = Ramesh's sister.
Trap: "Only son" is the hook — students overthink and imagine an uncle. "Only" locks it to the father.
Source: PYQ-style (SSC pattern)

Q3. If M × N means M is the wife of N; M + N means M is the son of N; M ÷ N means M is the daughter of N — what does P + Q ÷ R tell us about P's relation to R?
(a) P is R's grandson (b) P is R's son (c) P is R's brother (d) P is R's father
Answer: (a)
Solution: Solve left to right: P + Q → P is the son of Q. Q ÷ R → Q is the daughter of R. So P is the son of R's daughter = R's grandson.
Trap: Students who read the chain right-to-left get "P is R's son". Always decode in the written order, one link at a time.
Source: PYQ-style

Q4. Introducing a boy, a girl said, "He is the son of the only daughter of my mother's husband." How is the boy related to the girl?
(a) Brother (b) Nephew (c) Son (d) Cousin
Answer: (c)
Solution: Mother's husband = her father. Father's only daughter = the girl herself. So the boy is the son of the girl.
Trap: "Mother's husband" sounds like it could be a stepfather — students add an extra person. It is simply her father, and "only daughter" points back to the speaker.
Source: PYQ-style (SSC/Railway pattern)

Q5. A + B means A is brother of B; A − B means A is mother of B; A × B means A is father of B. Which expression shows that T is the paternal grandmother of K?
(a) T − R × K (b) T × R − K (c) T − R + K (d) T + R × K
Answer: (a)
Solution: T − R → T is mother of R; R × K → R is father of K. So T is the mother of K's father = paternal grandmother.
Trap: Option (c) makes R the brother of K — same generation, wrong. The hook is "paternal" vs "maternal": the middle person must be the *father*.
Source: PYQ-style (Banking pattern)

Q6. Pointing to a photograph, a man said, "I have no brother or sister, but that man's father is my father's son." Whose photograph is it?
(a) His own (b) His son's (c) His father's (d) His nephew's
Answer: (b)
Solution: "My father's son" = the man himself (he has no siblings). So "that man's father is me" → the photograph is of his son.
Trap: The classic. Students who skip "I have no brother or sister" answer "himself". That clause is the lock.
Source: PYQ-style (all exams)

Q7. If P $ Q means P is the wife of Q; P # Q means P is the sister of Q; P @ Q means P is the son of Q — how is A related to D in A # B $ C @ D?
(a) Sister (b) Sister-in-law (c) Aunt (d) Mother
Answer: (b)
Solution: Decode right to left: C @ D → C is the son of D. B $ C → B is the wife of C, so B is D's daughter-in-law. A # B → A is the sister of B. So A is the sister of D's daughter-in-law — i.e., A is the sister-in-law of C (D's son). Among the given options, A's relation to D's family is through marriage: A is the sister-in-law of D's son, and the keyed answer is (b) Sister-in-law.
Trap: Long chains with 3 symbols; students lose track at the middle link. Draw the tree — never solve 4-term chains mentally. Note also that the relation asked is "A to D" loosely; strictly, A is the sister of D's daughter-in-law, which standard keys record as sister-in-law (of the son).
Source: PYQ-style (Banking pattern) — ⏱ SKIP CANDIDATE: if you cannot decode the symbols within 15 seconds, mark and move on; this needs 45+ seconds of careful tree-drawing.

Q8. B is the brother of A. A is the mother of C. D is the father of B. How is D related to C?
(a) Grandfather (b) Father (c) Uncle (d) Cannot be determined
Answer: (a)
Solution: D is father of B; B is brother of A → D is also father of A. A is mother of C → D is C's grandfather (maternal).
Trap: Option (d) tempts students who think "maternal/paternal unclear" — but grandfather is correct regardless of side at this level of the question.
Source: PYQ-style (Railway pattern)

Q9. In a code, X * Y means X is the daughter of Y; X = Y means X is the wife of Y; X − Y means X is the brother of Y. Which of the following confirms that M is the mother of T?
(a) M = N − T (b) M * N = T (c) M = N * T (d) None of these
Answer: (d)
Solution: Check each: (a) M wife of N, N brother of T → M is T's sister-in-law. (b) M daughter of N, N wife of T → M is the daughter of T's wife — not mother. (c) M wife of N, N daughter of T → M is the wife of T's daughter — not mother. No option makes M the mother of T.
Trap: Students force-fit option (c). In "which confirms" questions, verify ALL options — the honest answer is often "none".
Source: PYQ-style (Banking pattern) — ⏱ SKIP CANDIDATE: three full decodings needed; if the first option check exceeds 15 seconds, park it for the second pass (45-second rule).

Q10. A is the sister of B. B is married to C. C is the mother of D. E is the father of C. How is A related to D?
(a) Aunt (b) Mother (c) Grandmother (d) Sister
Answer: (a)
Solution: B (male, married to C) is A's brother. C is D's mother, so B is D's father. A is the sister of D's father → aunt (bua).
Trap: "B is married to C" plus "C is the mother" — students who miss that marriage fixes B's gender as male may answer "cannot be determined". Marriage statements fix gender in these puzzles.
Source: PYQ-style (SSC pattern)

6. TIME RULE REVIEW (15-SEC / 45-SEC)

• 15-second check: Can you decode all symbol definitions and see the chain length? If the chain has ≤3 terms and one relation type — solve immediately (target: 30–40 seconds).
• 45-second rule: 4+ term chains, "which expression confirms" questions, and gender-determination questions belong to the second pass. Never let one blood-relation question eat 90 seconds — the next three questions are usually easier.
• Descriptive "pointing to" questions are almost always first-pass: convert the sentence to "my" and solve backwards from the end of the sentence.
