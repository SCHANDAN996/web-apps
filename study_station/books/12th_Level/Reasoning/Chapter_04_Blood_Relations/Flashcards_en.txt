Card 1
Front: In coded blood relations, what is the very first step before solving any expression?
Back: Decode the symbol meanings from the given code table, then break the expression into small pairs and solve from left to right, building a mini family tree.

Card 2
Front: If "A + B" means "A is the father of B", who is male in the expression "P + Q"?
Back: P is male (the father). The gender of Q is unknown — never assume the child's gender.

Card 3
Front: In "A × B means A is the sister of B", what is the gender of A and of B?
Back: A is definitely female; B's gender cannot be determined from this code alone.

Card 4
Front: Which mnemonic trick helps you remember the order of solving a coded relation chain?
Back: "Left to Right, Pair by Pair" — read the expression left to right, convert each pair into a relation, and link them like a chain.

Card 5
Front: What is the "Gender Tag" trick used in coded blood relations?
Back: Mark every person with (+) for male, (−) for female, and (?) for unknown as you decode each pair. A question can only be answered if the needed gender gets tagged.

Card 6
Front: In "P − Q means P is the mother of Q", and "Q + R means Q is the father of R" — can P and Q both be parents of R?
Back: No — this is a contradiction trap. If P is mother of Q, then Q is P's child; Q being father of R is fine, but always check that genders assigned in different pairs don't clash.

Card 7
Front: Examiner's trap: "A is the brother of B" — what wrong assumption do most students make about B?
Back: They assume B is male. "Brother" tells only A's gender (male); B can be a brother or a sister.

Card 8
Front: If "M @ N means M is the wife of N", what is N's gender and what extra fact do you get for free?
Back: N is male (husband), and you also learn M and N form a married couple — useful when the chain asks about their children.

Card 9
Front: How do you find "How is X related to Y?" quickly from a long coded chain?
Back: Trace the path from X to Y through the family tree, counting generations (up = parent side, down = child side) and checking X's gender tag at the end.

Card 10
Front: In a coded expression, what does a "generation gap" of two upward steps mean?
Back: Grandparent generation — e.g., father of father = grandfather; mother of mother = grandmother.

Card 11
Front: Which memory trick helps distinguish "A is father of B" vs "B is father of A" when symbols look similar?
Back: "Symbol sits with the subject" — the person written BEFORE the symbol holds the relation; the person after receives it. Read it as a full sentence: "A is the father of B."

Card 12
Front: If the code says "A # B means A is the son of B", and the question asks "How is B related to A?", what must you check first?
Back: B's gender. B is the parent of A, but whether B is "father" or "mother" depends on a gender clue elsewhere in the chain.

Card 13
Front: Examiner's trap: In "P is the maternal uncle of Q", which parent of Q is P related to?
Back: P is the brother of Q's MOTHER. Students often mix up maternal (mother's side) and paternal (father's side) relations.

Card 14
Front: What is the fastest way to verify your answer in a coded blood relation question?
Back: Reverse-check: read your final relation back through the tree in the opposite direction and confirm every pair matches the code table.

Card 15
Front: In "A $ B means A is the daughter of B", what two facts are fixed about A?
Back: A is female, and A is one generation below B. B's gender stays unknown unless another clue fixes it.

Card 16
Front: Why should you draw a family tree instead of solving coded relations mentally?
Back: Chains of 4–5 coded pairs overload short-term memory; a tree with gender tags prevents generation-mix and gender-swap errors — the two most common mistakes.

Card 17
Front: If "K is the only son of M's father", how is K related to M?
Back: K is M's brother (M's father has only one son, so that son must be M's brother). "Only son/daughter" codes are classic exam favourites.

Card 18
Front: What does "pointing to a photograph" type question become when combined with coded symbols?
Back: A two-step problem: first decode the speaker's statement into a relation chain, then apply the symbol codes — always identify who "I/me/my" refers to first.

Card 19
Front: In coded relations, when can you NOT answer "How is A related to B?" even after full decoding?
Back: When A's gender remains untagged — e.g., A is the "child" or "parent" of B but no clue fixes whether A is son/daughter or father/mother. Choose "cannot be determined".

Card 20
Front: What is the ideal time rule for a coded blood relation question in SSC/Bank exams?
Back: About 30–45 seconds: 15 seconds to decode symbols and tag genders, 15–20 seconds to build the chain, 10 seconds to answer. If the chain exceeds 5 pairs, mark and revisit.
