# 📊 PYQ Analysis — Order & Ranking

## 1. How Exams Test This Chapter (Pattern Analysis)

Order & Ranking is a **permanent fixture** of the reasoning section in SSC (CGL, CHSL, MTS, GD), Banking (IBPS PO/Clerk, SBI), and Railway (NTPC, Group D) exams. Based on the pattern of official papers over the years, the following tendencies are clearly visible:

**Sub-topics that recur most often:**

1. **Position from both ends** — "Ravi is 12th from the left and 15th from the right; how many in the row?" This is the single most repeated format. Exams love it because it takes under 30 seconds if you know the formula (Total = Left + Right − 1).
2. **Rank from top and bottom in a class** — same formula, vertical framing. Frequently combined with "how many students failed/passed" twists.
3. **Interchange of positions** — "A and B interchange their positions; A becomes 10th from the left…" These require tracking two positions and appear regularly in SSC CGL and IBPS.
4. **Number of persons between two people** — given both persons' positions from opposite ends. The trap version is when the sum of positions exceeds the total (overlapping case), where the answer is (sum − total − 2), not (total − sum − 1).
5. **Comparison-based ranking** — "Mohan is taller than Sohan but shorter than Rohan…" — arranging 4–6 people by height/weight/marks. Banking exams favour this; SSC asks shorter 3–4 person versions.
6. **Row of boys and girls / mixed conditions** — "In a row of girls, P is 8th from left; in a row of boys…" — less frequent but appears in CHSL and NTPC.

**Exam-wise flavour (qualitative):**

- **SSC CGL / CHSL:** Direct formula-based questions dominate; one question per paper is typical, occasionally two. Difficulty: easy to moderate.
- **Banking (IBPS/SBI):** Ranking appears inside puzzle sets (5 people, heights + cities) in mains; in prelims, standalone quick questions. Comparison chains are common.
- **Railway NTPC / Group D:** Very direct, formula-based; often the easiest question in the section.
- **UPSC CSAT:** Rare, but when it appears it is wrapped in a paragraph with a logical twist — no formula shortcut.

**Examiner's favourite traps (seen repeatedly):**

- Forgetting the **−1** in Total = Left + Right − 1 (the person is counted twice).
- The **overlapping "between" case**: when positions from two ends add up to more than the total, candidates blindly subtract and get a negative or wrong number.
- **Interchange questions** where candidates update only one person's position.
- Comparison chains with a **negative statement** ("X is NOT the shortest") — candidates misread it as "X is the tallest".
- "How many are **between** them" vs "how many **including** them" — one word changes the answer by 2.

## 2. Top 10 High-Yield Questions (with Solutions & Trap Analysis)

**Q1.** In a row of students, Anil is 14th from the left and 19th from the right. How many students are in the row?
(a) 31 (b) 32 (c) 33 (d) 34
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** Total = 14 + 19 − 1 = 32.
**Trap:** Option (c) 33 = 14 + 19, placed for those who forget the −1. This is the most classic trap in the chapter.
**Source:** PYQ-style (SSC pattern)

**Q2.** In a class of 45 students, Priya ranks 12th from the top. What is her rank from the bottom?
(a) 33 (b) 34 (c) 35 (d) 36
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** Rank from bottom = 45 − 12 + 1 = 34.
**Trap:** Option (a) 33 = 45 − 12, for those who forget the +1. Examiners always pair the two off-by-one errors as adjacent options.
**Source:** PYQ-style (SSC/Banking pattern)

**Q3.** In a row, Ram is 9th from the left and Shyam is 12th from the right. If they interchange positions, Ram becomes 15th from the left. How many persons are in the row?
(a) 25 (b) 26 (c) 27 (d) 28
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** After interchange, Ram occupies Shyam's place: 15th from left = 12th from right. Total = 15 + 12 − 1 = 26.
**Trap:** Using Ram's original position (9 + 12 − 1 = 20) — not even in options, but candidates then guess. The key is realising the new position belongs to Shyam's seat.
**Source:** PYQ-style (SSC CGL pattern)

**Q4.** In a row of 40 students, A is 10th from the left and B is 15th from the right. How many students sit between them?
(a) 14 (b) 15 (c) 16 (d) 17
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** B from left = 40 − 15 + 1 = 26th. Between 10th and 26th = 26 − 10 − 1 = 15.
**Trap:** Option (c) 16 = 26 − 10, for those who forget to subtract 1 for "between". Read "between" vs "including" twice.
**Source:** PYQ-style (Banking pattern)

**Q5.** In a row of 30 boys, P is 14th from the left and Q is 20th from the right. How many boys are between P and Q?
(a) 2 (b) 3 (c) 4 (d) 5
**Answer: (a)**
**Solution:** Q from left = 30 − 20 + 1 = 11th. P is 14th. Since 14 + 20 = 34 > 30, this is the overlapping case: between = 34 − 30 − 2 = 2.
**Trap:** Blindly applying 30 − 14 − 11 = 5 (option d) or 20 − 14 = 6. The overlapping case is the examiner's premium trap — always check whether the two positions cross each other.
**Skip?** Borderline — if you know the overlap formula, 45 seconds; if not, skip and return.
**Source:** PYQ-style (SSC CGL pattern)

**Q6.** Mohan is taller than Sohan. Rohan is taller than Mohan. Kunal is taller than Rohan. Who is the shortest?
(a) Mohan (b) Sohan (c) Rohan (d) Kunal
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** Chain: Kunal > Rohan > Mohan > Sohan. Shortest = Sohan.
**Trap:** Candidates who build the chain left-to-right carelessly reverse it and pick Kunal. Write the chain with ">" symbols, never in your head.
**Source:** PYQ-style (Railway pattern)

**Q7.** Five friends P, Q, R, S, T got different marks. R scored more than P but less than S. T scored more than S. Q scored less than P. Who scored the highest?
(a) R (b) S (c) T (d) Q
**Answer: (c)**
**Solution:** T > S > R > P > Q. Highest = T.
**Trap:** Option (b) S tempts those who stop reading after "T scored more than S" is processed incompletely, or who misread "more" as "less". In statement questions, every clause must enter the chain.
**Source:** PYQ-style (Banking prelims pattern)

**Q8.** In a class, Neha is 7th from the top and 28th from the bottom. How many students are in the class?
(a) 33 (b) 34 (c) 35 (d) 36
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** Total = 7 + 28 − 1 = 34.
**Trap:** Same off-by-one family as Q1. Exams repeat this format with fresh numbers almost every cycle — it is a guaranteed 15-second question.
**Source:** PYQ-style (SSC CHSL pattern)

**Q9.** In a row of girls facing north, Meena is 11th from the left. If she shifts 4 places to her right, she becomes 15th from the left. What is her new position from the right if the row has 25 girls?
(a) 10th (b) 11th (c) 12th (d) 13th
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** New position from left = 15. From right = 25 − 15 + 1 = 11th.
**Trap:** Using the original 11th from left (giving 15th from right — not listed, causing panic) or computing 25 − 15 = 10 (option a) without the +1. Two-step questions multiply off-by-one risk.
**Skip?** No — two clean steps, under 45 seconds.
**Source:** PYQ-style (SSC pattern)

**Q10.** In a queue, A is behind B. C is ahead of B but behind D. E is ahead of D. Who is exactly in the middle if there are 5 persons in the queue?
(a) B (b) C (c) D (d) A
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** Order front to back: E, D, C, B, A. The middle (3rd of 5) = C.
**Trap:** "Ahead of" vs "behind" reversal — candidates who translate even one clause wrongly end up with D (option c) or B. Draw five slots and place people one clue at a time, then count to the 3rd slot.
**Skip?** Yes, if your first arrangement attempt tangles — redrawing costs more than 45 seconds; mark and return.
**Source:** PYQ-style (CSAT/Banking pattern)

## 3. The 15-Second / 45-Second Rule — Reviewed Against Real Patterns

- **15-second questions (attempt instantly):** Single-formula questions — total from two positions (Q1, Q8), rank from the other end (Q2). These are the bulk of SSC/Railway ranking questions. Never skip these; they are the cheapest marks in the section.
- **45-second questions (attempt with care):** Interchange (Q3), between-two-persons (Q4), two-step shifts (Q9), short comparison chains (Q6, Q7). Attempt if the data fits on one line of your rough sheet.
- **Skip-and-return candidates:** Overlapping "between" cases when you don't recall the formula (Q5), and 5+ person arrangement chains where your first diagram fails (Q10). In Banking mains, ranking embedded in a multi-variable puzzle (height + city + colour) is a clear first-round skip.

**Bottom line:** In this chapter, roughly 7 out of 10 exam questions are formula-direct. Master the four core formulas and the overlap check, and Order & Ranking becomes a guaranteed-scoring zone rather than a risk zone.
