101. In a row of students, Ravi is 12th from the left end and 18th from the right end. How many students are there in the row?
(a) 31 (b) 29 (c) 30 (d) 28
Answer: (b)
Solution: Total = position from left + position from right − 1 = 12 + 18 − 1 = 29. The tempting answer 30 comes from simply adding 12 and 18 without subtracting the double-counted person, so the answer is 29.
Source: PYQ-style

102. In a class of 40 students, Mohan ranks 15th from the top. What is his rank from the bottom?
(a) 25th (b) 26th (c) 27th (d) 24th
Answer: (b)
Solution: Rank from bottom = Total − rank from top + 1 = 40 − 15 + 1 = 26. Many students write 25 by forgetting the +1 adjustment, so the answer is 26th.
Source: PYQ-style

103. In a queue, A is 7th from the front and B is 12th from the back. If they interchange their positions, A becomes 15th from the front. How many persons are in the queue?
(a) 27 (b) 26 (c) 25 (d) 28
Answer: (b)
Solution: After interchange, A is at B's original place, so B's position is 15th from front and 12th from back. Total = 15 + 12 − 1 = 26. Using A's original 7th position with 12 gives the wrong total 18, so the answer is 26.
Source: PYQ-style

104. Five boys P, Q, R, S and T are of different heights. P is taller than only T. R is taller than S but shorter than Q. Who is the tallest?
(a) Q (b) R (c) P (d) S
Answer: (a)
Solution: P is taller than only T, so P is 4th and T is 5th. Q > R > S fills the top three places with Q at the top. The order is Q > R > S > P > T, so the answer is Q.

105. In a row of 35 students, Kiran is 9th from the left and Suresh is 11th from the right. How many students are sitting between them?

116. In a row, Meena is 6th from the left and Reena is 9th from the right. If they swap positions, Meena becomes 14th from the left. What is Reena's new position from the right?
(a) 14th (b) 15th (c) 16th (d) 17th
Answer: (d)
Solution: After swapping, Meena sits at Reena's original place: 14th from left and 9th from right, so total = 14 + 9 − 1 = 22. Reena now sits at Meena's original place, 6th from left, so from right = 22 − 6 + 1 = 17. Assuming Reena simply takes the 14th position is the trap, so the answer is 17th.

120. Five students got different marks. Kavita scored more than Savita but less than Ankita. Ankita scored less than Mamta. Savita did not score the least. Who scored the least?

Answer: (a)
Solution: The known chain is Mamta > Ankita > Kavita > Savita, but the fifth student's position is not fixed. Since Savita is not the least, the least scorer must be the unnamed fifth student, whose identity is not given, so the answer is Cannot be determined.

121. In a row of students, A is 10th from the left, B is 15th from the right, and there are 4 students between A and B. If the total number of students is 31, how many students are to the left of A?

Answer: (a)
Solution: A is 10th from the left, so students to the left of A = 10 − 1 = 9. The extra data about B and the total is a deliberate distraction; the trap answer 10 counts A itself, so the answer is 9.

122. In a row of 40 students, P is 12th from the left and Q is 15th from the right. R sits exactly midway between P and Q. What is R's position from the left?
(a) 19th (b) 22nd (c) 21st (d) 20th
Answer: (a)
Solution: Q's position from left = 40 − 15 + 1 = 26. The exact middle of positions 12 and 26 is (12 + 26)/2 = 19. The trap answer 20 arises if Q's position is wrongly converted, so the answer is 19th.

124. In a row of 50 students, one student is 14th from the left and another is 19th from the right. A third student sits exactly midway between them. How many students are between the third student and the left end?
(a) 20 (b) 21 (c) 22 (d) 23
Answer: (c)
Solution: The second student's position from left = 50 − 19 + 1 = 32. The exact middle of 14 and 32 is (14 + 32)/2 = 23, so the third student sits 23rd from the left. Students strictly between him and the left end = 23 − 1 = 22. The trap 23 counts the third student himself, so the answer is 22.

125. Five persons M, N, O, P and Q are standing in a row by height. M is taller than N but shorter than O. P is taller than O. Q is shorter than N. If the tallest stands first and the shortest last, who stands third?
(a) N (b) P (c) O (d) M
Answer: (d)
Solution: The chain is P > O > M > N > Q. Counting from the tallest: P first, O second, M third. The trap is to count from the shortest end and pick N, so the answer is M.
