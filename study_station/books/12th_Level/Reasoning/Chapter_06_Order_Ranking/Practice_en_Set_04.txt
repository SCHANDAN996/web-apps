76. In a row of 40 students, Ravi is 12th from the left end. What is his position from the right end?
(a) 29th (b) 30th (c) 28th (d) 27th
Answer: (a)
Solution: The tempting wrong answer 28th comes from simply computing 40 − 12 = 28, which forgets that Ravi himself must be counted. Position from right = Total − Position from left + 1 = 40 − 12 + 1 = 29, so the answer is 29th.

77. In a class, Sunita ranks 8th from the top and 35th from the bottom. How many students are there in the class?
(a) 42 (b) 43 (c) 44 (d) 41
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is adding 8 + 35 = 43, which counts Sunita twice. Total students = Rank from top + Rank from bottom − 1 = 8 + 35 − 1 = 42, so the answer is 42.

78. In a row of boys, A is 15th from the left and B is 18th from the right. If they interchange their positions, A becomes 22nd from the left. How many boys are there in the row?
(a) 41 (b) 40 (c) 38 (d) 39
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is using A's original position 15 with 18, which mixes two different rows. After interchange, A occupies B's original position, so that position is 22nd from the left and 18th from the right; Total = 22 + 18 − 1 = 39, so the answer is 39.

99. In a queue of 90 people, Sohan is 30th from the front. Mohan is exactly in the middle of Sohan and the last person. What is Mohan's position from the front?
(a) 59th (b) 60th (c) 61st (d) 58th
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is averaging 30 and 90 directly as (30 + 90)/2 = 60 without checking, which luckily works here only because the count is odd; the safe method is: people from 30th to 90th = 90 − 30 + 1 = 61, whose middle is the 31st person counting from Sohan, i.e., 30 + 31 − 1 = 60. So the answer is 60th.

(a) 25th (b) 24th (c) 23rd (d) 22nd
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is adding 5 to his left position or subtracting from the old right position. Original total = 16 + 19 − 1 = 34; new total = 39; Karan stays 16th from the left, so from the right = 39 − 16 + 1 = 24, so the answer is 24th.
