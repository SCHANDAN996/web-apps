Card 1
Front: What exactly is an "assumption" in a Statement & Assumption question?
Back: An assumption is something the speaker takes for granted — an unstated belief that must be true for the statement to make sense. It is hidden, not written.

Card 2
Front: A statement says: "Buy X soap — it makes skin glow." What is the speaker assuming?
Back: That people want glowing skin and that advertising can influence them to buy. If nobody cared about glowing skin, the advertisement would be pointless.

Card 3
Front: What is the golden test to check whether an assumption is implicit?
Back: The "negation test": negate the assumption. If the statement collapses or becomes meaningless, the assumption is implicit. If the statement still stands, it is not.

Card 4
Front: What mnemonic reminds you that an assumption must be hidden, not stated?
Back: "HIT" — Hidden, Indispensable, Taken-for-granted. If it is already stated in the sentence, it cannot be an assumption.

Card 5
Front: What does the mnemonic "No Extreme, No Repeat" warn you about?
Back: Reject assumptions with extreme words (all, never, only, best) and reject assumptions that merely restate the statement in different words.

Card 6
Front: If an assumption simply repeats the statement in other words, is it implicit?
Back: No. A restatement is a conclusion or inference, not an assumption. An assumption must add an unstated underlying belief.

Card 7
Front: The statement is a notice: "Trespassers will be prosecuted." What is assumed?
Back: That people can read the notice and that the fear of prosecution will deter them. Without these, putting up the notice is useless.

Card 8
Front: In advice/suggestion statements ("You should join coaching to crack SSC"), what is typically assumed?
Back: That the advice will be followed (or can be followed) and that the suggested action actually helps achieve the goal.

Card 9
Front: Examiner's trap: an assumption says "Only hard work leads to success." Why is this usually NOT implicit?
Back: The word "only" makes it extreme. The speaker need not assume hard work is the sole path — just that it helps. Extreme-worded assumptions are almost always rejected.

Card 10
Front: Examiner's trap: the statement praises a government scheme, and one assumption says "The scheme will be 100% successful." Trap or truth?
Back: Trap. Announcing or praising a scheme only assumes it is expected to help, not guaranteed total success. Over-definite assumptions are rejected.

Card 11
Front: In appeal-type statements ("Donate generously for flood victims"), what is the standard implicit assumption?
Back: That people will respond to the appeal. An appeal is made only when the maker believes someone will listen.

Card 12
Front: Should an assumption be based on the real world's truth or the speaker's viewpoint?
Back: Always the speaker's viewpoint. Even if you personally disagree, ask: "What must the speaker believe to say this?"

Card 13
Front: A statement is in the past tense, but an assumption talks about the future. Can it still be implicit?
Back: Yes, if the statement's purpose points to the future (e.g., a warning or policy). Judge by the speaker's intention, not grammar alone.

Card 14
Front: What is the difference between an assumption and a conclusion?
Back: An assumption lies behind the statement (before it, unstated); a conclusion follows from the statement (after it, derived). Assumption = input; conclusion = output.

Card 15
Front: If a statement says "The company has decided to increase prices," is "The company wants more profit" always implicit?
Back: Not necessarily — price rise could be due to costs. But "The company believes customers will still buy at higher prices" is implicit, because the decision assumes demand survives.

Card 16
Front: What quick mnemonic helps decide between two close assumptions?
Back: "MIN" — Minimal, Indispensable, Neutral. Pick the weaker, more essential assumption; the stronger, extra claim is usually the trap.

Card 17
Front: In "instruction/order" statements ("Students must submit forms by Friday"), what is assumed?
Back: That the instruction can be understood and complied with, and that those addressed will obey it.

Card 18
Front: Why should you reject an assumption containing words like "all," "every," "always," "completely"?
Back: Because a speaker rarely needs to assume universal truths to make a specific statement. Such sweeping assumptions go beyond what is needed.

Card 19
Front: A statement reports a fact ("The train was late by two hours"). Can a factual report have assumptions?
Back: Usually very few or none. Pure factual reports carry minimal assumptions — don't force one. Assumptions are strongest in advice, appeals, notices, and advertisements.

Card 20
Front: What is the 20-second solving strategy for Statement & Assumption questions?
Back: Read the statement, identify its type (advice/appeal/notice/ad), apply the negation test to each assumption, and eliminate extreme or restated options.
