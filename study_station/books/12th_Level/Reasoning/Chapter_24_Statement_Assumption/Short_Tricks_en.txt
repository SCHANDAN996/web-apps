# Short Tricks — Statements & Assumptions

Quick, exam-ready techniques for solving Statement–Assumption questions in SSC, Railway, Bank and State exams. Use these only after you understand the basic concept; tricks save time, they do not replace understanding.

---

## Trick 1: The "Must Be True" Test

An assumption is something the speaker **must have taken for granted** while making the statement.

- Ask: "If this assumption were **false**, would the statement still make sense?"
- If the statement **collapses** without it → the assumption is **implicit (valid)**.
- If the statement **still stands** without it → the assumption is **not implicit**.

**Example:**
Statement: "Join our coaching institute to clear SSC CGL."
Assumption: "Coaching can help a candidate clear SSC CGL."
→ Without this, the advertisement is meaningless. **Implicit.**

---

## Trick 2: Possible ≠ Necessary

- If the assumption is **possible but not necessary**, it is **wrong**.
- Examiners love options that "sound nice" but are not required by the statement.
- Only what the speaker **must** believe counts — not what he **might** believe.

**Example:**
Statement: "Buy X soap for glowing skin."
Assumption: "X soap is the cheapest soap in the market."
→ Nice to have, but not needed for the statement. **Not implicit.**

---

## Trick 3: Watch for Extreme Words

Assumptions containing **all, only, none, always, never, best, every** are usually **not implicit**, because real-world statements rarely depend on absolutes.

- Statement: "Drink milk to stay healthy."
- Assumption: "Milk is the **only** way to stay healthy." → **Not implicit** (too extreme).

Exception: if the statement itself uses an absolute word, a matching absolute assumption may be valid.

---

## Trick 4: Restatement Is Not an Assumption

If an option simply **repeats the statement in different words**, reject it. An assumption must be **hidden and unstated**, not a copy.

- Statement: "The government should open more schools in villages."
- Option: "The government should increase the number of village schools." → Just a restatement. **Not an assumption.**

---

## Trick 5: Notice / Advertisement / Appeal Rule

For statements that are **notices, advertisements, appeals or requests**, the standard implicit assumptions are:

1. People **will read/notice** it.
2. It **will have some effect** (people may respond).
3. (For ads) The product/service **can deliver** what is promised.

Anything beyond these — price comparisons, competitor claims, guaranteed results — is usually **not implicit**.

---

## Trick 6: Suggestion / "Should" Rule

When the statement says someone **"should"** do something:

- Implicit: the suggested action is **possible/practicable**, and it will **help** the stated purpose.
- Not implicit: it is the **only** solution, or everyone **will follow** it.

---

## Trick 7: Eliminate in 15 Seconds

Use this order in the exam:

1. Read the statement once, carefully.
2. Check each assumption with the **Must Be True test** (Trick 1).
3. Instantly reject options with **extreme words** (Trick 3) or that **restate the statement** (Trick 4).
4. If two options remain, pick the one that is **more directly connected** to the statement's purpose.

---

## Skip Strategy — When to Skip the Question

- **If the statement is long and both assumptions look equally plausible after 30–40 seconds**, mark it and move on; these questions are designed to create doubt. Return only if time remains.
- **If the statement uses unfamiliar technical or legal terms**, skip — guessing between two vague assumptions is a negative-marking trap.
- **If you catch yourself re-reading the statement a third time**, skip. Statement–Assumption questions reward the first clear analysis, not repeated reading.

---

## One-Line Summary

> An assumption is what the speaker **must** believe, not what he **might** believe — reject the extreme, reject the restated, and keep only what the statement cannot survive without.
