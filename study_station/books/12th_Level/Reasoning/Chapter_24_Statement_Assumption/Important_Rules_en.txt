# Important Rules — Exam Strategy & Key Facts to Remember

## 1. Golden Rules for the Exam Hall

1. **Read the question twice before answering.** Most mistakes in SSC, Railway, Bank and State exams come from misreading negative words like "NOT", "EXCEPT" and "INCORRECT".
2. **The 15-second / 45-second rule:** If a question looks solvable in about 15 seconds, attempt it immediately. If it needs more thought, mark it and return within 45 seconds; if still stuck, skip it and move on. Never let one question eat the time meant for five others.
3. **Attempt easy questions first.** In the first round, solve only what you know confidently. Keep doubtful and lengthy questions for the second round.
4. **Beware of negative marking.** Most SSC, Railway and Bank exams deduct marks for wrong answers (commonly 0.25 or 1/3 of the question's marks — always check the official notification for your exam). Blind guessing usually lowers your score.
5. **Never leave OMR/bubble filling to the last minute** in offline exams; fill answers section by section.

## 2. Rules for Preparation

1. **NCERT first.** For static GK (History, Geography, Polity, Economy, Science), NCERT books of Classes 6–12 are the most reliable base.
2. **Revise more than you read.** One source revised three times beats three sources read once.
3. **Make short notes of facts that change with time** (appointments, awards, schemes, sports) and update them with the year.
4. **Practise previous-year pattern questions** to understand how examiners frame traps: nearly-correct statements, chronology confusion, similar-sounding names, and negative-worded questions.
5. **Take timed mock tests** and analyse every wrong answer — know *why* the correct option is right and why the tempting wrong option is wrong.

## 3. Common Traps Set by Examiners

1. **Nearly-correct statements** — one word changed (e.g., an article of the Constitution, a year, a river's direction) makes the whole statement wrong.
2. **Chronology confusion** — events, dynasties, battles or constitutional amendments placed in the wrong order.
3. **Similar names** — e.g., places, personalities or organisations with look-alike names swapped between options.
4. **Negative wording** — "Which of the following is NOT correct…" — underline the negative word before reading options.
5. **"All of the above" style distractors** — verify each option individually instead of assuming.

## 4. Facts-Checking Discipline

1. Trust only verifiable, stable facts from NCERT, official government sources (`*.gov.in`, `*.nic.in`) and official websites of institutions.
2. Any fact that changes with time (current office-holders, latest reports, rankings, budget figures) must always be read with its year attached.
3. If a fact cannot be verified from an official source, do not memorise it — unverified numbers and ranks are common in unreliable material.

## 5. Quick Revision Rules

1. Revise formulas, dates, articles and lists **in the last 24 hours** before the exam — not new topics.
2. Use flashcards for one-line facts and mind maps for connected topics.
3. Sleep adequately before the exam; a fresh mind recalls faster than a tired one.

**Remember:** Accuracy beats attempts. A calm, rule-based approach in the exam hall is worth more than extra hours of last-minute cramming.
