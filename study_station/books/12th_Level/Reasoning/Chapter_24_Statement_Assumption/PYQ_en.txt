# 📊 PYQ Analysis — Statement & Assumption

## 1. How Exams Use This Chapter

Statement & Assumption is a **regular but low-volume** chapter in SSC CGL, SSC CHSL, IBPS PO/Clerk, and RRB NTPC. It rarely dominates a paper — typically a handful of questions appear in the reasoning section — but it is a **high-accuracy scoring zone** because the logic is fixed and learnable. Banking exams (IBPS, SBI) ask it in a slightly harder, statement-heavy form; SSC and Railway ask shorter, crisper versions.

The chapter appears in two standard formats:

- **Format A (2 assumptions):** A statement followed by Assumption I and II. Options: only I implicit / only II implicit / either / neither / both.
- **Format B (3 assumptions):** Statement followed by I, II, III; options combine them ("Only I and II", "All", etc.). Banking exams prefer this.

## 2. Sub-Topics That Recur Most

Based on the pattern of past papers, examiners repeatedly draw statements from these themes:

1. **Government notices / instructions** — "The municipality directed residents to…" (assumption: people will comply; the problem exists).
2. **Advertisements and appeals** — "Buy X soap for glowing skin" (assumption: ads influence people; people want glowing skin).
3. **Advice / suggestions** — "The teacher advised students to practise daily" (assumption: advice can be followed; it will help).
4. **Warnings and threats** — "Trespassers will be prosecuted" (assumption: the warning will deter people).
5. **Cause–effect statements** — "Due to heavy rain, schools were closed" (assumption: rain disrupts schooling).
6. **Course-of-action style hybrids** — statements implying a problem needing a solution.

## 3. Examiner's Favourite Traps (Psychological Hooks)

- **Trap 1 — The restatement trap:** An "assumption" that merely repeats the statement in different words. A restatement is NOT an assumption; it is the statement itself.
- **Trap 2 — The extreme-word trap:** Assumptions containing *only, all, always, never, best, the only way*. These are almost never implicit — the speaker need not assume something absolute.
- **Trap 3 — The desirable-vs-assumed trap:** Something that *should* happen or would be *good* is not automatically what the speaker *assumed*. Test: did the speaker take it for granted while speaking?
- **Trap 4 — The real-world-knowledge trap:** An assumption that is true in real life but not needed for the statement. Implicitness is judged from the speaker's mind, not from general knowledge.
- **Trap 5 — The "both feel right" trap:** One assumption is clearly implicit; the second is a mild exaggeration of it. Students mark "both" emotionally. Check each independently.
- **Trap 6 — Negation confusion:** "Neither I nor II is implicit" vs "Either I or II" — under time pressure students misread the option itself. Read options twice.

## 4. High-Yield Practice Questions (PYQ-Style) with Trap Analysis

**Q1.** Statement: "Use our coaching institute's notes to clear the SSC exam." — an advertisement.
Assumptions:
I. People read and respond to advertisements.
II. These notes alone guarantee success in the SSC exam.
(a) Only I implicit (b) Only II implicit (c) Both implicit (d) Neither implicit
Answer: (a)
Solution: An advertiser always assumes the audience will notice and be influenced — I is implicit. II contains the extreme claim "alone guarantee"; no advertiser needs to assume a guarantee. Trap used: extreme-word trap (II).
Source: PYQ-style

**Q2.** Statement: The district collector ordered, "All shops in the market area must close by 9 PM."
Assumptions:
I. Shopkeepers will follow the order.
II. Shops currently remain open after 9 PM.
(a) Only I implicit (b) Only II implicit (c) Both implicit (d) Neither implicit
Answer: (c)
Solution: An order is issued assuming compliance (I), and the restriction makes sense only if shops presently stay open beyond 9 PM (II). Trap used: students often reject II as "extra information", but it is the very premise of the order.
Source: PYQ-style

**Q3.** Statement: "Practise meditation daily to reduce stress," the doctor told the patient.
Assumptions:
I. The patient can practise meditation daily.
II. Meditation is the only method to reduce stress.
(a) Only I implicit (b) Only II implicit (c) Both implicit (d) Neither implicit
Answer: (a)
Solution: Advice assumes the advised action is possible — I implicit. "Only method" is an extreme claim the doctor never needs to assume — II rejected. Trap used: extreme-word trap.
Source: PYQ-style

**Q4.** Statement: The bank issued a notice: "Customers are requested to update their KYC documents by 31 March to avoid account restrictions."
Assumptions:
I. Some customers have not yet updated their KYC documents.
II. Customers generally respond to such notices.
(a) Only I implicit (b) Only II implicit (c) Both implicit (d) Neither implicit
Answer: (c)
Solution: The notice presupposes pending KYCs (I) and is issued assuming customers will act on it (II). Trap used: "both feel right" — here both genuinely are implicit; do not over-apply the trap and reject one mechanically.
Source: PYQ-style

**Q5.** Statement: "Do not lean out of the window while the train is moving." — a railway warning.
Assumptions:
I. Passengers may lean out of windows.
II. The warning will prevent passengers from leaning out.
(a) Only I implicit (b) Only II implicit (c) Both implicit (d) Neither implicit
Answer: (c)
Solution: A warning exists because the risky behaviour is possible (I) and is given hoping it will deter (II). Trap used: students reject II thinking "warnings don't always work" — but the *issuer* assumes it will work; real-world effectiveness is irrelevant.
Source: PYQ-style

**Q6.** Statement: The company said, "We shall hire only graduates for this post."
Assumptions:
I. Graduates will apply for the post.
II. Non-graduates are never capable of doing this job.
(a) Only I implicit (b) Only II implicit (c) Both implicit (d) Neither implicit
Answer: (a)
Solution: Announcing eligibility assumes eligible candidates exist and will apply (I). II is an extreme judgement ("never capable") not needed for the hiring rule. Trap used: extreme-word trap.
Source: PYQ-style

**Q7.** Statement: "To improve English, read an English newspaper every day," the teacher told the class.
Assumptions:
I. Students have access to an English newspaper.
II. Reading newspapers improves English.
(a) Only I implicit (b) Only II implicit (c) Both implicit (d) Neither implicit
Answer: (c)
Solution: The advice assumes feasibility (I) and the underlying belief that the method works (II). Trap used: II looks like "general knowledge", but here it is exactly the speaker's premise — distinguish real-world truth from speaker's assumption by asking "is the advice meaningless without it?"
Source: PYQ-style

**Q8.** Statement: The government announced a subsidy on electric vehicles to reduce air pollution.
Assumptions:
I. A subsidy will encourage people to buy electric vehicles.
II. Air pollution is the most serious problem facing the country.
(a) Only I implicit (b) Only II implicit (c) Both implicit (d) Neither implicit
Answer: (a)
Solution: The policy assumes the incentive changes buying behaviour (I). II is a superlative ("most serious") — the government need not rank problems to act on one. Trap used: extreme-word trap with a plausible-sounding emotional claim.
Source: PYQ-style

**Q9.** Statement: "Attend the remedial classes if you want to pass the exam," the principal told the weak students.
Assumptions:
I. Weak students want to pass the exam.
II. Remedial classes guarantee that every student will pass.
(a) Only I implicit (b) Only II implicit (c) Both implicit (d) Neither implicit
Answer: (a)
Solution: The conditional advice presupposes the desire to pass (I). "Guarantee… every" is extreme — II rejected. Trap used: extreme-word trap.
Source: PYQ-style

**Q10.** Statement: Due to the cyclone warning, fishermen have been advised not to venture into the sea.
Assumptions:
I. The cyclone may endanger fishermen at sea.
II. Fishermen will heed the advice.
(a) Only I implicit (b) Only II implicit (c) Both implicit (d) Neither implicit
Answer: (c)
Solution: The advisory rests on the danger being real (I) and on expected compliance (II). Trap used: students mark only I, forgetting that every advisory assumes it will be followed.
Source: PYQ-style

## 5. The 15-Second / 45-Second Rule — Applied

- **15-second questions (attempt immediately):** Q1, Q3, Q6, Q9 — the moment you spot an extreme word (*only, guarantee, never, most*), the assumption dies instantly. Mark the surviving option and move on.
- **45-second questions (attempt with care):** Q2, Q4, Q5, Q10 — both assumptions look implicit; verify each with the negation test ("if this were false, would the statement still make sense?").
- **Skip candidates:** Q7 and Q8 are borderline-skip in a speed section. Q7 demands you separate "real-world truth" from "speaker's premise" — a judgement call that can eat 60+ seconds. Q8 pairs a clearly-implicit I with an emotionally attractive II; if you feel pulled toward "both", that pull itself is the trap signal — if not resolved in 45 seconds, mark (a) logic aside and skip. In SSC/Railway papers, a Statement–Assumption question that is not settled in 45 seconds is worth skipping and revisiting only if time remains.

## 6. Key Takeaways from the Pattern

- Extreme-word assumptions are wrong in the overwhelming majority of cases — treat *only/all/never/best/guarantee* as red flags.
- Every instruction, notice, warning, advice, or advertisement silently assumes **(a) the problem/behaviour exists** and **(b) people will respond** — this pair solves a large share of real exam questions.
- Judge implicitness from the **speaker's mind**, not from your own knowledge of the world.
- When torn between "only I" and "both", the second assumption is usually the exaggerated cousin of the first — re-read it for extreme wording before deciding.
