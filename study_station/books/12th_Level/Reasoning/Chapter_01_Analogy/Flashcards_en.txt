Card 1
Front: What is the very first step to solve any analogy question?
Back: Find the exact relationship between the given pair (A : B) — express it in a short sentence — then apply the same sentence to the third term. Never match by "looks similar."

Card 2
Front: Why is "Doctor : Patient" NOT the same relationship as "Teacher : Student" in a strict analogy?
Back: A doctor treats/cures the patient (service with a goal of healing), while a teacher teaches the student (imparting knowledge). Examiners pair such near-identical relations to trap you — always state the precise action.

Card 3
Front: In word analogies, which 5 relationship types appear most often in SSC/Bank exams?
Back: (1) Synonym/Antonym, (2) Part–Whole, (3) Tool–Work, (4) Worker–Workplace, (5) Cause–Effect. Classify the pair into one of these before checking options.

Card 4
Front: What is the mnemonic "S-P-T-W-C" used for in analogy?
Back: It recalls the five common word relations: Synonym, Part-whole, Tool, Worker/Workplace, Cause-effect. Run the pair through this checklist when the relation is not obvious.

Card 5
Front: In letter analogies like ABC : DEF, what should you check first?
Back: The shift pattern of each letter's position (A=1…Z=26). Check whether the shift is uniform (+3,+3,+3) or varying (+1,+2,+3), and whether letters are reversed.

Card 6
Front: What is the "position value" trick for letter analogies?
Back: Memorize A=1 to Z=26 (use EJOTY: E=5, J=10, O=15, T=20, Y=25 as anchors). Convert letters to numbers, find the arithmetic rule, apply it, convert back.

Card 7
Front: In number analogies like 8 : 64 :: 7 : ?, which operations should you test first, in order?
Back: (1) Square/cube, (2) multiply by a constant, (3) square ± adjustment, (4) digit operations (sum, reverse). Here 8²=64, so 7²=49.

Card 8
Front: A question gives "12 : 36 :: 15 : ?" and options include 45 and 39. What is the examiner's trap?
Back: Two rules both fit the given pair: ×3 (12×3=36 → 15×3=45) and +24 (12+24=36 → 15+24=39). The trap is picking the first rule that comes to mind. When two rules fit, prefer the simpler/standard one (×3 → 45) — and if a second given pair is provided, test both rules on it to decide.

Card 9
Front: What is the golden rule for choosing between two options that both seem to fit?
Back: The correct option must match the relationship in the same direction and same degree. If A:B is "young one : adult," the answer must also be "young one : adult," not reversed.

Card 10
Front: Why does the order of the pair matter in analogy?
Back: "Nest : Bird" (dwelling : dweller) is not the same as "Bird : Nest." If the question reverses the order, your answer must reverse too. Examiners flip order in one option to catch careless readers.

Card 11
Front: What is the mnemonic "ReDiO" for checking analogy options?
Back: Relation, Direction, Order — verify the Relation matches, the Direction (who acts on whom) matches, and the Order of terms matches. Reject any option failing even one.

Card 12
Front: In "Doctor : Stethoscope :: Carpenter : ?", why is "Wood" wrong even though carpenters use wood?
Back: The relation is worker : tool (instrument held/used), not worker : material. Wood is the material; the tool answer would be "Saw." Tool vs material is a classic examiner trap.

Card 13
Front: How do you handle analogy questions where the given pair has more than one possible relation?
Back: Choose the most specific relation that only one option satisfies. If two options still fit, look for a secondary feature (gender, size, category) to break the tie.

Card 14
Front: In number analogy "4 : 8 :: 6 : ?", why might 12 be a trap answer?
Back: 4×2=8 suggests 6×2=12, but if the intended rule is n : n+4 (4+4=8 → 6+4=10), 12 is wrong. When two rules fit the given pair, check which rule's answer is among the options — and prefer the rule that also fits a second given pair if provided.

Card 15
Front: What should you do when an analogy pair mixes categories (e.g., "Pen : Write :: Knife : ?")?
Back: Identify the function relation (tool : function). Knife's primary function is "cut." Don't pick "kitchen" (place) or "steel" (material) — match the grammatical type of the missing term (verb here).

Card 16
Front: What is the 15-second skip rule for analogy in exams?
Back: If you cannot state the relationship in one sentence within 15 seconds, mark and skip. Analogy questions are quick-scoring only when the relation clicks immediately; don't burn time forcing one.

Card 17
Front: In letter-pair analogies, what trap do examiners set with "reverse" patterns?
Back: They give ABC : CBA (reversal) but one option applies reversal while another applies a shift that coincidentally matches the first letter only. Always verify all letters, not just the first.

Card 18
Front: Why should you read all four options even when option (a) seems correct?
Back: In analogy, a "more exact" match may exist later. The best answer matches the relation most precisely — examiners place a partially-correct option first to trap hasty solvers.
