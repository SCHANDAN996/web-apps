# 🪄 Short Tricks & Mnemonics — Analogy

---

## 🧠 Memory Tricks (Mnemonics)

**Box 1 — The "PAIR CARE" Rule**
> **P**air → **A**lways **I**dentify **R**elation → **C**heck **A**ll options → **R**eject **E**xtras
First find the relation in the given pair, THEN look at options. Never start from the options!

---

**Box 2 — "Doctor : Patient" — The JOB-CLIENT Trick**
> Whenever you see a profession, shout: **"Who does he SERVE? Where does he WORK? What TOOL does he USE?"**
Three questions = three possible relations. Pick the one that matches the options.

---

**Box 3 — The "SAME DIRECTION" Rule**
> **"A is to B, as C is to ?" — the arrow must fly the SAME way both times.**
If A→B is "small to big", then C→? must also be "small to big". If an option reverses the direction (big to small), kill it instantly.

---

**Box 4 — "PART-WHOLE" Memory Hook**
> **"Page makes a Book, Book makes a Library."**
Small → Medium → Large. If the pair is Part : Whole, the answer must also be Part : Whole — NOT Whole : Part. Examiners love flipping this!

---

**Box 5 — The "OPPOSITE GENDER" Trap**
> **"Stallion : Mare" — Male first, Female second.**
If the question gives Male : Female, the answer CANNOT be Female : Male. Order is everything in Analogy! (Watch out: "Horse" alone is gender-neutral — the male horse is a Stallion, the female is a Mare.)

---

**Box 6 — "TOOL-ACTION" Chant**
> **"Knife CUTS, Needle SEWS, Pen WRITES."**
Tool : Function pairs — say the action aloud. If you can't make a sentence with the pair, the relation is wrong.

---

**Box 7 — The "SENTENCE TEST" (Most Powerful Trick!)**
> Make a sentence: **"A DOCTOR treats a PATIENT."**
Now fit each option into the SAME sentence. Only one will fit perfectly — that's your answer. If two fit, your sentence is too loose — make it tighter!

---

**Box 8 — "DEGREE LADDER" for Intensity Pairs**
> **Warm : Hot :: Cool : Cold** — climb the ladder the SAME number of steps.
Happy → Ecstatic is a big jump; Happy → Glad is a small step. Match the SIZE of the jump!

---

**Box 9 — "COUNTRY-CAPITAL-CURRENCY" Triangle**
> Three C's travel together: **Country → Capital → Currency.**
If the pair is Country : Capital, don't pick Country : Currency as the answer — same family, wrong relation!

---

**Box 10 — "YOUNG ONE" Zoo Trick**
> **"Dog-Puppy, Cat-Kitten, Lion-Cub, Horse-Foal, Goat-Kid."**
Make a mini zoo in your head. Confusing Cub/Kitten/Puppy is the examiner's favourite trap — memorise the odd ones: Deer→Fawn, Frog→Tadpole, Butterfly→Caterpillar.

---

**Box 11 — "UNIT-QUANTITY" Formula**
> **"What is MEASURED, and WHAT measures it?"**
Temperature : Thermometer (Quantity : Instrument) vs Temperature : Degree (Quantity : Unit). Read the pair twice before deciding!

---

**Box 12 — The "ELIMINATION HAMMER"**
> Can't find the relation? Hammer out wrong options:
> ❌ Wrong direction ❌ Wrong category ❌ Wrong gender order ❌ Part-whole flipped
> Whatever survives is your answer.

---

**Box 13 — "WORD : SYNONYM" vs "WORD : ANTONYM"**
> Ask: **"Same team or enemy team?"**
Same meaning = synonym pair; opposite = antonym pair. Never mix them — "Kind : Cruel" cannot answer "Happy : Joyful".

---

**Box 14 — The "GRAMMAR GUARD"**
> **Noun matches Noun, Verb matches Verb, Adjective matches Adjective.**
If the given pair is Noun : Noun, any option with a Verb is dead on arrival. Check word-type before meaning!

---

**Box 15 — "15-Second Flash Rule"**
> **15 seconds → relation found? Solve it. Not found? Mark and move.**
Analogy questions are sprinters, not marathon runners. The toppers' secret: they never wrestle with one analogy for 2 minutes.

---

## ⏭️ Skip Strategy — When to SKIP

**Tip 1 — The 15-Second Wall:**
If you cannot name the relation (tool-function, part-whole, synonym, etc.) within **15 seconds**, mark the question and move on. Analogy is a "know-it-or-don't" question — extra time rarely helps. Return only if time remains.

**Tip 2 — Two Options Survive the Sentence Test:**
If your sentence fits **two options equally well**, your relation is too general — but if you can't refine it within another 10 seconds, **skip**. Guessing between two close options in a negative-marking exam is a losing game.

**Tip 3 — Unfamiliar Vocabulary Pair:**
If the question is word-based (synonym/antonym analogy) and you don't know the meaning of **even one** key word, skip immediately. You cannot build a relation on an unknown word — blind guessing here costs more than it earns.
