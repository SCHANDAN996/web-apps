# 📊 PYQ Analysis — Analogy (Verbal Reasoning)

## 1. How Exams Ask Analogy Questions

Analogy is one of the most reliable scoring areas in SSC (CGL, CHSL, MTS, GD), Banking (IBPS, SBI), and Railway (RRB NTPC, Group D) exams. Questions appear in three standard formats:

- **A : B :: C : ?** — find the missing term.
- **A : B :: C : D** — identify which option pair has the *same* relationship as the given pair.
- **Odd-pair-out** — four pairs are given; three share one relationship, one does not.

The relationship tested is almost always one of a small set: synonym/antonym, part–whole, tool–function, worker–workplace, cause–effect, degree/intensity, number operations (square, cube, ×2+1, digit sum), letter shifting (positional ±n in the alphabet), and word–letter count.

## 2. Sub-topic Weightage (Pattern, Not Numbers)

Based on the recurring pattern across recent papers:

- **Number analogies** are the single most frequent type in SSC and Railway exams — squares, cubes, prime numbers, and "multiply then add/subtract" patterns dominate.
- **Letter/alphabet analogies** come next — forward/backward shifts, reverse order, and mixed patterns (first letter +2, second −2).
- **Word analogies** (synonym, antonym, tool–function, part–whole) are common in Banking exams and SSC CHSL, where vocabulary-based pairs appear often.
- **GK-based analogies** (country–capital, river–city, instrument–measurement) appear regularly in SSC CGL and RRB NTPC.
- **Image/figure analogies** belong to non-verbal reasoning and are not covered here.

## 3. Exam-wise Tendencies

- **SSC CGL / CHSL:** Balanced mix of number, letter, and word analogies; at least one GK-based pair is typical.
- **SSC MTS / GD:** Simpler synonym/antonym and basic number patterns (×2, square).
- **IBPS / SBI (Banking):** Word-relationship and letter-shift analogies; number analogies are rarer.
- **RRB NTPC / Group D:** Heavy on number analogies and simple letter shifts; occasionally unit–quantity pairs.

## 4. The Examiner's Traps (Psychological Hooks)

1. **Near-correct synonym:** Options contain a word that is *related* but not a true synonym (e.g., "frugal" vs "stingy" — one is positive, one negative).
2. **Reversed relationship:** Given pair is Tool : Function, but the option is Function : Tool — same words, wrong order.
3. **Wrong operation family:** The pair follows n² + 1, but an option follows n² − 1; the numbers look similar at a glance.
4. **Partial pattern in letters:** First two letters follow +3, tempting you to assume all do — the third letter follows −3.
5. **Category confusion:** "Apple : Fruit" vs "Carrot : Vegetable" — correct; but a trap option gives "Mango : Tree" (object : source, not object : category).
6. **Plural/tense mismatch:** Relationship holds only if word form is ignored — examiners exploit this in antonym questions.

## 5. Top 10 High-Yield PYQ-Style Questions

**Q1.** Doctor : Hospital :: Teacher : ?
(a) Student (b) School (c) Book (d) Examination
Answer: (b)
Solution: Worker : Workplace. (a) is the trap — a student is who the teacher *serves*, not where he works. Order matters.
Source: PYQ-style

**Q2.** 8 : 72 :: 9 : ?
(a) 81 (b) 90 (c) 99 (d) 18
Answer: (b)
Solution: Pattern is n : n × (n+1) → 8 × 9 = 72, so 9 × 10 = 90. Trap: (a) 81 tempts those who assume simple squaring.
Source: PYQ-style

**Q3.** FLOWER : GMPXFS :: GARDEN : ?
(a) HBSEFO (b) HBSFEO (c) HBSDFO (d) HBSEDO
Answer: (a)
Solution: Each letter shifts +1: F→G, L→M, O→P, W→X, E→F, R→S. So G→H, A→B, R→S, D→E, E→F, N→O = HBSEFO. Trap options swap the last letters.
Source: PYQ-style

**Q4.** Moon : Satellite :: Earth : ?
(a) Sun (b) Planet (c) Star (d) Galaxy
Answer: (b)
Solution: Object : Category. (a) is the trap — Earth revolves around the Sun, but that is a different relationship (revolver : centre).
Source: PYQ-style

**Q5.** 11 : 121 :: 12 : ?
(a) 144 (b) 169 (c) 132 (d) 124
Answer: (a)
Solution: Simple square: 11² = 121, 12² = 144. Trap: (c) 132 = 12 × 11 tempts those who overthink and look for a complex pattern.
Source: PYQ-style

**Q6.** Thermometer : Temperature :: Barometer : ?
(a) Humidity (b) Pressure (c) Rainfall (d) Wind speed
Answer: (b)
Solution: Instrument : Quantity measured. Trap: (a) humidity is measured by a hygrometer — a "weather instrument" association misleads.
Source: PYQ-style

**Q7.** ACE : GIK :: BDF : ?
(a) HJL (b) HJK (c) GIK (d) HKM
Answer: (a)
Solution: Each letter +6: A→G, C→I, E→K. So B→H, D→J, F→L = HJL. Trap: (b) breaks the pattern only in the last letter.
Source: PYQ-style

**Q8.** Water : Thirst :: Food : ?
(a) Hunger (b) Appetite (c) Taste (d) Energy
Answer: (a)
Solution: Thing : Need it satisfies. Trap: (d) energy is what food *gives*, not the need it removes — a cause–effect confusion.
Source: PYQ-style

**Q9.** 5 : 36 :: 7 : ?
(a) 49 (b) 64 (c) 56 (d) 50
Answer: (b)
Solution: n : (n+1)² → 5 : 6² = 36, so 7 : 8² = 64. Trap: (a) 49 = 7² tempts those who miss the +1 step.
Source: PYQ-style

**Q10.** India : Tiger :: USA : ?
(a) Lion (b) Bald Eagle (c) Bear (d) Kangaroo
Answer: (b)
Solution: Country : National animal/bird. The Bald Eagle is the national bird/emblem of the USA. Trap: (a) lion is associated with many countries but is not the US emblem; (d) kangaroo belongs to Australia.
Source: PYQ-style

## 6. The 15-Second / 45-Second Rule — Review

- **15-second rule:** If the relationship (synonym, square, +n shift) is visible within 15 seconds, solve immediately and move on. Q1, Q4, Q5, Q8 above are 15-second questions.
- **45-second rule:** If after 45 seconds the pattern is still unclear (especially multi-step number patterns like n² + n, or mixed letter shifts), mark and skip — return only if time remains.
- **Skip candidates here:** **Q3** (letter-by-letter coding with 6 letters — high error risk under time pressure; skip if the shift isn't obvious in the first two letters) and **Q9** (the (n+1)² pattern is non-obvious; if you test n² and n×(n+1) and both fail, skip rather than hunt for a third pattern).
- Never spend more than 60 seconds on any single analogy — the next question is almost always easier.

## 7. Key Takeaways

- Master the ~12 standard relationship types; 90% of exam questions use them.
- In number analogies, test in this order: square/cube → ×k → ×k±m → digit operations.
- In letter analogies, write alphabet positions (A=1…Z=26) above the letters immediately.
- Always check the **order** of the relationship in options — reversed pairs are the most common trap.
- GK-based analogies reward static GK revision (national symbols, instruments, capitals).
