# 🧒 The Feynman Way — Analogy Explained Like You're 12

## The Hardest Idea in Analogy (Made Simple)

Most students think Analogy is about "matching words." It isn't. The hardest — and most important — idea is this:

> **In an analogy, you never match the words. You match the *relationship* between the words.**

The question `Doctor : Hospital :: Teacher : ?` is not asking "what goes with a teacher?" It is asking: *"What is the relationship between Doctor and Hospital? Now find a pair with the exact same relationship for Teacher."*

Relationship: a doctor **works in** a hospital. So a teacher works in a **school**. Done.

If you can say the relationship in one clean sentence, you can solve any analogy. If you can't, you're guessing.

## A Story: Riya and the Shoe Shop

Imagine 12-year-old Riya goes to a shoe shop. The shopkeeper plays a game with her.

He holds up a **left shoe** and a **right shoe** and says: "These two are a pair. Now — I hold up a **lock**. What completes the pair?"

Riya almost shouts "door!" — because locks are found on doors. But wait. The shopkeeper's first pair wasn't "things found together." A left shoe and right shoe are **two halves that need each other to work**. A lock's other half is the **key**, not the door.

That's the whole trick of analogy. The obvious answer (door) is the trap. The correct answer (key) matches the *relationship*, not the *scene*.

Exam setters are exactly like that shopkeeper. They always place one option that "feels related" and one option that has the *same relationship*. Your job is to ignore the feeling and check the relationship.

## The Unforgettable Trick: **"Make a Sentence"** 🎯

Whenever you see `A : B :: C : ?`, do this:

1. **Make one short sentence** joining A and B. Example: `Knife : Cut` → *"A knife is used to cut."*
2. **Say the exact same sentence** with C: *"A ___ is used to cut."* Now check the options. Suppose they are `Paper`, `Handle`, `Sword`, `Sharp` — the sentence fits **Sword** ("a sword is used to cut").
3. **The sentence must be exact.** If your sentence is just "a knife cuts," then "paper cuts" also fits grammatically — trap! Tighten it: "a knife is a *tool* used to cut." Now Paper (not a tool), Handle (a part, not a cutting tool), and Sharp (a quality, not a thing) all fall away — only **Sword** survives.

**Golden rule: the tighter your sentence, the fewer traps can survive it.**

One more check — **order matters**. `Nest : Bird` is "a bird lives in a nest," so the answer pair must also be *home : creature* in the same order. `Den : Lion` ✓. `Lion : Den` ✗ — same words, wrong order, wrong answer.

## The 4 Relationships That Cover Most Questions

- **Function** — Knife : Cut (tool → its job)
- **Place** — Doctor : Hospital (person → workplace)
- **Part–Whole** — Petal : Flower (piece → the whole thing)
- **Opposites / Degree** — Hot : Cold, or Warm : Scorching (same family, different intensity)

When stuck, ask: *"Is it about what it does, where it lives, what it's part of, or how it compares?"* — one of these almost always works.
