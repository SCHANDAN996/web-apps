# 🪄 Short Tricks & Mnemonics — Classification

---

## 🧠 Mnemonic 1: The "ODD Squad" Rule
> **"Three Agree, One Flees!"**

In every classification question, first check: do **three** items share a property? If yes, the fourth one is your answer. Never start by studying one item deeply — always compare all four together.

---

## 🧠 Mnemonic 2: "FAMILY" for Word Groups
> **F**unction – **A**ppearance – **M**aterial – **I**dentity – **L**ocation – **Y**ield (use)

Run this checklist on word-based questions:
- **F**unction: Pen, Pencil, Eraser (writing tools) vs. Chair ❌
- **A**ppearance: shape, colour, size
- **M**aterial: wood, metal, plastic
- **I**dentity: category (fruit, planet, river)
- **L**ocation: where found/used
- **Y**ield: what it produces

If three match on any one letter, the fourth is odd.

---

## 🧠 Mnemonic 3: "POSSE" for Number Pairs
> **P**rime – **O**dd/Even – **S**quare – **S**um of digits – **E**xact divisibility

For number classification, test in this order:
1. **P**rime or composite?
2. **O**dd or even?
3. Perfect **S**quare or cube?
4. **S**um of digits (divisible by 3? 9?)
5. **E**xact divisibility (by 4, 5, 7, 11…)

Most number-oddity questions crack within POSSE — run this checklist before trying anything fancier.

---

## 🧠 Mnemonic 4: "GAP" for Letter Pairs
> **G**ap – **A**lphabet position – **P**attern (reverse/opposite)

For letter pairs like (BF, GK, LP, XZ):
- **G**ap: count letters between them
- **A**lphabet position: convert to numbers (B=2, F=6)
- **P**attern: are they opposite letters (A-Z, B-Y)? Same difference?

---

## 🧠 Mnemonic 5: The "Vowel Police"
> **"A E I O U — count the crew!"**

In letter groups, quickly count vowels. Three groups with 1 vowel and one with 2? Found your odd one. Also check: consecutive letters, letters from the same half of the alphabet (A–M vs N–Z).

---

## 🧠 Mnemonic 6: "King vs. Servant" Trap
> **"A king rules a kingdom — but a kingdom is not a king!"**

Beware of **part-whole** and **individual-group** traps:
- Star : Galaxy :: Soldier : Army (individual : group) ✔️
- If three pairs are "tool : work" and one is "worker : tool", the odd one flips the **order**. Always check the direction of the relationship!

---

## 🧠 Mnemonic 7: "SQUARE the Pair"
> For number pairs (a, b): check **S**um, **Q**uotient, **U**nit digit, **A**ddition pattern, **R**atio, **E**xponent.

Example: (3,9), (4,16), (5,25), (6,38) → three are n : n², one breaks it. Squares and cubes are the examiner's favourite — memorise squares up to 25 and cubes up to 12.

---

## 🧠 Mnemonic 8: The "Zoo Keeper" Trick
> **"Cage by class, not by colour!"**

For animals: classify by **biological class** (mammal, bird, reptile, amphibian, fish), not by looks. Classic trap: **Bat** (mammal) hiding among birds; **Whale** (mammal) hiding among fish; **Snake** (reptile) hiding among insects/worms.

---

## 🧠 Mnemonic 9: "Half-Alphabet Mirror"
> **A↔Z, B↔Y, C↔X … M↔N — "AZad BoY Cows X…"**

Opposite letters sum to **27** (A=1, Z=26). If three letter-pairs are opposites (sum 27) and one isn't, that's your answer. Just remember: **"27 is the magic mirror number."**

---

## 🧠 Mnemonic 10: "BODY" for Letter Clusters
> **B**eginning letter – **O**dd position letters – **D**ifference pattern – **Y** (last letter)

For 3–4 letter clusters (e.g., ACE, BDF, GIK, MOQ):
- Check the **B**eginning letters' gaps
- Check **O**dd-position letters separately
- Check the **D**ifference between consecutive letters inside each cluster
- Check the last letter (**Y** = end check)

---

## 🧠 Mnemonic 11: The "Impossible Pair" Detector
> **"If it can't exist, it must exit!"**

Some options are odd because they're **logically impossible or fake**: e.g., "February 30", a "square triangle", or a misspelled scientific term. When nothing else works, ask: *is one option simply not a real thing?*

---

## 🧠 Mnemonic 12: "GENDER & GRAMMAR" Check
> When meaning fails, check the **word itself**:
- Three nouns + one verb?
- Three masculine + one feminine (King, Prince, Duke vs. Queen)?
- Three singular + one plural?
- Three 5-letter words + one 6-letter word?

Examiners love hiding the answer in **grammar, not meaning**.

---

## 🧠 Mnemonic 13: The "Two-Truth" Warning
> **"If two answers look right, your rule is too loose."**

If you find a rule that eliminates option A, but another rule eliminates option C — your rules are weak ("both are even" type). The correct rule must make **exactly three** items share a **strong, specific** property (e.g., "divisible by 7", not "big numbers"). Tighten the rule until only one odd remains.

---

## 🧠 Mnemonic 14: "CHORD" for Pair Direction
> **C**ause→Effect, **H**abitat, **O**ccupation→Tool, **R**aw→Product, **D**egree (intensity)

The five most-tested analogy-pair relations in classification:
- **C**ause : Effect (Virus : Disease)
- **H**abitat (Lion : Den)
- **O**ccupation : Tool (Carpenter : Saw)
- **R**aw material : Product (Cotton : Cloth)
- **D**egree (Warm : Hot)

Three pairs will share one relation type; the odd pair uses another — or reverses the order!

---

# ⏭️ Skip Strategy — When to Move On

### 🚫 Skip Tip 1: The 15-Second Rule
If after **15 seconds** you haven't spotted even a *candidate* property (no POSSE hit, no category link), **mark and skip**. Classification questions are designed to be 20–30 second solvers — a blank brain at 15 seconds means the trick is obscure; return only if time remains.

### 🚫 Skip Tip 2: The "Double Answer" Dilemma
If **two options** both seem odd under two different rules and you can't decide which rule is "stronger" within 20 extra seconds — **skip**. Guessing between two is a 50% gamble with negative marking; a skipped question costs 0, a wrong one costs marks.

### 🚫 Skip Tip 3: The Unfamiliar-Vocabulary Trap
If the question is word-based and you **don't know the meaning of even one option** (e.g., rare instruments, currencies, scientific terms), don't burn time trying to infer — **skip immediately**. Meaning-based classification cannot be solved by logic alone, and wild guessing across four unknowns is a losing bet.
