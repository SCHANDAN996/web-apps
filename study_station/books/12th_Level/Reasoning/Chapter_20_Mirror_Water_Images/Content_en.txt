# Mirror & Water Images

## Outline & Learning Goals

By the end of this chapter, you will be able to:

1. Understand what a mirror image and a water image actually are — and how they differ.
2. Predict the mirror image of letters, numbers, words, clocks and figures in seconds.
3. Predict the water image of the same.
4. Spot the tiny changes examiners hide inside "almost correct" options.
5. Solve every question of this type in under 20 seconds with a fixed method.

---

## The Hook: One Line That Decides Everything

Imagine you are standing in front of a mirror holding a placard that says **AMBULANCE** — but written backwards. Why backwards? Because a driver looking into his rear-view mirror must read it correctly. That one real-life trick is the entire soul of this chapter: **a mirror swaps left and right, but never top and bottom.**

Master that one line, and half of this chapter is already done. The other half is its twin: **water swaps top and bottom, but never left and right.**

Two lines. One full chapter. Let's build it slowly.

---

## The Common Struggle (You Are Not Alone)

Most students lose marks here not because the concept is hard, but because:

- They try to "imagine" the whole figure at once and get confused.
- They mix up mirror image and water image under exam pressure.
- They fall for options that are 90% correct — one small curve flipped the wrong way.

The fix is simple: **never imagine the whole figure. Check it part by part.** We will train exactly that.

---

## Part 1: What Is a Mirror Image?

When an object is placed in front of a plane mirror, the image formed has these properties:

- **Left and right are interchanged** (this is called *lateral inversion*).
- **Top and bottom stay exactly the same.**
- The image is the same size, same distance "behind" the mirror as the object is in front.

In exams, the mirror is almost always placed on the **right side** (sometimes left) of the figure, shown by a vertical line. So the part of the figure closest to the mirror line appears closest to the line in the image too — just flipped.

**Golden Rule 1:** Mirror image = horizontal flip (left ↔ right). Top stays top.

### Mirror Images of Capital Letters

Some letters look the same in a mirror — these are called **laterally symmetrical** letters:

**A, H, I, M, O, T, U, V, W, X, Y** — these 11 letters have a vertical line of symmetry, so their mirror image is identical.

All other letters change: **B, C, D, E, F, G, J, K, L, N, P, Q, R, S, Z** appear reversed.

🧠 **Active Recall:** Without looking above — is the mirror image of **M** the same or different? What about **R**? Say it aloud before reading on.

### Mirror Images of Digits

Among digits 0–9:

- **0, 1, 8** look the same in a mirror (in standard printed form).
- **2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 9** appear reversed.

This is why number-based mirror questions are favourites of examiners — a reversed 2 and a reversed 5 can look confusingly similar in a hurry.

---

## Part 2: What Is a Water Image?

A water image is the reflection you see in still water — like a tree reflected in a lake. The water surface acts like a **horizontal mirror** placed *below* the object.

- **Top and bottom are interchanged.**
- **Left and right stay exactly the same.**

**Golden Rule 2:** Water image = vertical flip (top ↔ bottom). Left stays left.

### Water Images of Capital Letters

Letters with a **horizontal line of symmetry** look the same in water:

**B, C, D, E, H, I, K, O, X** — these remain unchanged in a water image (in standard block form).

Notice: **H, I, O, X** appear in *both* lists — they are symmetrical both ways, so neither mirror nor water changes them.

🧠 **Active Recall:** Which letters survive BOTH mirror and water unchanged? Close the book and write them down. (Answer: H, I, O, X.)

### Water Images of Digits

- **0, 1, 3, 8** look the same in water (standard form).
- The rest change.

## Part 3: The Memory Trick (Mnemonic)

Remember this pair:

> **"MIRROR flips the MIDDLE (left-right), WATER flips the WAIST-to-HEAD (top-bottom)."**

Or even shorter — think of the letters themselves:

- **M**irror → flip across a **M**ental vertical line → left-right swap.
- **W**ater → flip across the **W**ater surface (horizontal) → top-bottom swap.

Another quick check: **"Mirror = sideways, Water = upside-down."**

---

## Part 4: The 4-Step Solving Method (Works on Every Question)

When you see a mirror/water image question, do NOT stare at the whole figure. Follow this:

**Step 1 — Identify the type.** Vertical mirror line → mirror image (left-right flip). Horizontal water line → water image (top-bottom flip).

**Step 2 — Pick 2–3 "marker points."** Choose distinctive parts: an arrowhead, a dot, a curve, the position of a small circle. Note where each is (top-left, bottom-right, etc.).

**Step 3 — Flip only the markers.** In a mirror image, a marker at top-left moves to top-right (top stays). In a water image, top-left moves to bottom-left (left stays).

**Step 4 — Eliminate options.** Any option where even ONE marker is wrong is out. Usually 2–3 options die instantly, and the last one is verified in seconds.

This method converts a "visualisation" problem into a "checking" problem — and checking is always faster and more reliable than imagining.

🧠 **Active Recall:** A figure has a dot at its top-right corner. Where does the dot go in (a) the mirror image, (b) the water image? Answer before reading the next line. *(Mirror: top-left. Water: bottom-right.)*

## Part 5: Mirror Images of Clocks — The Famous Formula

Clock questions are the most repeated application of mirror images.

**Rule:** If the mirror is placed vertically beside a clock showing time **H : M**, then:

> **Mirror time = 11 : 60 − given time** (equivalently, subtract from 12:00 and adjust)

**Example:** Time shown = 4 : 20.
Mirror time = 11 : 60 − 4 : 20 = **7 : 40**.

Quick check: 4:20 + 7:40 = 12:00. ✔ (The two times always add to 12:00 — except when the given time itself is 12:00, which stays 12:00.)

**Special case:** If minutes = 00 (e.g., 5:00), subtract from 12:00 directly → mirror of 5:00 is 7:00.

**Water image of a clock** (rare, but asked): the reliable method is to flip the clock top-bottom mentally — 12 and 6 swap positions, 3 and 9 stay put, and each hand's position flips vertically. If you prefer a formula:

> **Water-image time = 6 : 30 − given time** (if the given time is 6:30 or earlier)
> **Water-image time = 18 : 30 − given time** (if the given time is later than 6:30)

**Example:** Time shown = 2 : 10.
Water-image time = 6 : 30 − 2 : 10 = **4 : 20**.

Check by flipping: at 2:10 the minute hand points to 2 (upper-right); flipped vertically it points to 4 (lower-right) — minute = 20. ✔

Practise this only after mastering the mirror formula.

## Part 6: Words and Mixed Figures

For a **word** in a mirror:

1. The **order of letters reverses** (last letter comes first).
2. **Each letter itself flips** left-right.

Example: Mirror image of **"BOX"** → order reverses to X, O, B, and B flips → you read it as a reversed-B, then O, then X.

For a **water image** of a word: the order of letters stays the **same** (left-right unchanged), but each letter flips top-bottom.

This difference is a classic trap: students reverse the letter order even in water-image questions. **Water never reverses order.**

🧠 **Active Recall:** In a water image of the word "CAT", does the T come first or stay last? *(Stays last — water flips top-bottom only.)*

## Summary Table

| Feature | Mirror Image | Water Image |
|---|---|---|
| Mirror line | Vertical (side) | Horizontal (below) |
| What swaps | Left ↔ Right | Top ↔ Bottom |
| What stays | Top & Bottom | Left & Right |
| Letter order in words | Reverses | Stays same |
| Letters unchanged | A H I M O T U V W X Y | B C D E H I K O X |
| Digits unchanged | 0, 1, 8 | 0, 1, 3, 8 |
| Clock shortcut | 11:60 − time | 6:30 − time (or 18:30 − time) |
| One-line memory | Sideways flip | Upside-down flip |

## ⚠️ Caution: Examiner's Trap

**Trap 1 — Blurry figures on screen/paper.**
In CBT exams the figure may be small or unclear, and a slightly-open curve vs. a closed one decides the answer. Don't rush: zoom mentally on the *marker points* (dots, arrowheads, gaps). If the original figure is ambiguous, check which option is consistent with the clearest part of the figure.

**Trap 2 — Options that differ in only one tiny detail.**
Examiners place 2–3 options that are perfect flips *except* one element — a dot on the wrong side, a flag pointing the old way, one letter not flipped. Never select the first option that "looks right." Verify ALL your marker points before marking.

**Trap 3 — Rotation mixed with reflection.**
The deadliest trap: an option that is the figure **rotated** 180° instead of mirrored. A 180° rotation swaps left-right AND top-bottom — a mirror swaps only one. If both seem to have changed, it's a rotation, not a reflection — reject it. Similarly, in water-image questions, an option with reversed letter *order* is a mirror image in disguise — reject it instantly.
