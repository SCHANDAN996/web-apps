Card 1
Front: In a mirror image, which sides of the figure actually swap places?
Back: Left and right swap (lateral inversion). Top and bottom stay unchanged. The mirror is assumed to be placed vertically on the right side of the figure unless stated otherwise.

Card 2
Front: In a water image, which sides of the figure swap places?
Back: Top and bottom swap (vertical inversion). Left and right stay unchanged. The water surface is assumed to be below the figure.

Card 3
Front: What is the quick mnemonic to remember mirror vs water image?
Back: "Mirror flips Left–Right, Water flips Top–Bottom." Or remember: mirror stands vertical → horizontal flip; water lies horizontal → vertical flip.

Card 4
Front: If the mirror is placed on the LEFT side of the figure instead of the right, does the answer change?
Back: No. The mirror image is the same whether the mirror is on the left or right — left and right are interchanged in both cases. Only the position of the image relative to the mirror changes.

Card 5
Front: What does the letter 'b' look like in a mirror?
Back: It looks like 'd'. Letters with left-right asymmetry (b, d, p, q) are the most commonly tested because they visibly change.

Card 6
Front: Which capital letters look exactly the same in a mirror image?
Back: A, H, I, M, O, T, U, V, W, X, Y — letters with vertical symmetry. Mnemonic: "AHIMOTUVWXY" — the vertically symmetric eleven.

Card 7
Front: Which capital letters look exactly the same in a water image?
Back: B, C, D, E, H, I, K (in most fonts), O, X — letters with horizontal symmetry. H, I, O, X appear unchanged in both mirror and water images.

Card 8
Front: What happens to the number '6' in a water image?
Back: It flips top-to-bottom, so it resembles an inverted 6 — it does NOT become 9 (that would be a 180° rotation). Examiner's trap: options often show a rotated figure instead of a flipped one.

Card 9
Front: Examiner's trap: What is the most common wrong option in mirror-image questions?
Back: A figure that is rotated 180° instead of laterally inverted. Rotation turns the whole figure; a mirror only swaps left and right while keeping top and bottom fixed.

Card 10
Front: How do you find the mirror image of a clock showing 3:00?
Back: Use the rule: mirror time = 11:60 − given time. So 11:60 − 3:00 = 8:60, which is 9:00. Logic check: the hour hand at 3 (pointing right) flips to 9 (pointing left). Answer: 9:00.

Card 11
Front: What is the formula for the mirror image of a clock time?
Back: Mirror time = 11:60 − given time (if minutes are non-zero, borrow 1 hour as 60 minutes). Shortcut for exact hours: mirror time = 12 − given hour (e.g., 4:00 → 8:00).

Card 12
Front: A figure has an arrow pointing to the upper-right. Where does it point in the mirror image?
Back: Upper-left. The horizontal component reverses (right → left), the vertical component (up) stays the same.

Card 13
Front: The same arrow (upper-right) — where does it point in the water image?
Back: Lower-right. The vertical component reverses (up → down), the horizontal component (right) stays the same.

Card 14
Front: Examiner's trap: In water-image questions, what sneaky change do wrong options often make?
Back: They also reverse left and right (making it a 180° rotation) or they keep the top unchanged. A true water image flips ONLY top and bottom — check one fixed left/right detail to eliminate fakes.

Card 15
Front: What is the fastest elimination trick for mirror-image options?
Back: Pick one asymmetric detail near the left edge of the original (a dot, hook, or letter). In the correct mirror image it must appear at the right edge, flipped. Any option failing this single check is eliminated.

Card 16
Front: If a question shows the mirror placed horizontally ABOVE the figure, what kind of image results?
Back: A top-bottom flip (like a water image, but inverted upward). A horizontal mirror line always produces a vertical flip; a vertical mirror line always produces a lateral flip.

Card 17
Front: Mnemonic: How do you instantly recall which letters change in a mirror?
Back: "Symmetric letters stay, asymmetric letters sway." If you can fold the letter along a vertical line and both halves match, the mirror image is identical; otherwise it changes.

Card 18
Front: How is a water image of a word like "MOM" different from its mirror image?
Back: Mirror image of MOM: M and O are vertically symmetric, so it still reads MOM (reversed order looks the same). Water image: M flips to a W-like shape, so it does not read MOM. Always test letter-by-letter symmetry.

Card 19
Front: In exams, when the figure contains both letters and shapes, what should you check first?
Back: Check the letters first — they have fixed, known mirror/water forms, so they expose wrong options faster than abstract shapes do.

Card 20
Front: Does the size of the figure change in a mirror or water image?
Back: No. Reflection preserves size and shape proportions; only orientation flips. Any option with a stretched, shrunk, or resized figure is automatically wrong.
