# 🪄 Short Tricks & Mnemonics — Sitting Arrangement (Intermediate)

---

## 🧠 Mnemonic 1: "FIX First, Float Later"
**Rule:** Always start with the most "fixed" clue (definite position), never with a floating clue ("somewhere to the left").
> 🗣️ *"A ship without an anchor drifts — a puzzle without a fixed clue never solves!"*
Anchor = definite clue. Drop the anchor FIRST.

---

## 🧠 Mnemonic 2: "FACE-IN = Flip It!"
**Rule:** In a circle facing the **centre**, a person's right hand appears on YOUR left side of the diagram — but it is still THEIR right.
> 🗣️ *"Face In — Flip It!"* When people face the centre, their left-right is opposite to yours on paper. Draw tiny arrows on each person!

---

## 🧠 Mnemonic 3: "FACE-OUT = Follow the Sun"
**Rule:** Facing **away** from centre, left-right matches YOUR left-right on the diagram.
> 🗣️ *"Back to the centre, same as the painter!"* (The painter = you, looking at the page.)

---

## 🧠 Mnemonic 4: "Between = Sandwich Rule"
**Rule:** "A sits between B and C" means B–A–C or C–A–B — A is the **filling**, never the bread.
> 🗣️ *"The filling never touches the plate!"* A can never be at an end if A is "between" two people in a row.

---

## 🧠 Mnemonic 5: "Second to the Right = Skip One"
**Rule:** "Second to the right of X" = leave ONE seat, then place.
> 🗣️ *"Second means Skip One, Third means Skip Two."*
Formula: **Nth to the right = skip (N−1) seats.**

---

## 🧠 Mnemonic 6: "Immediate = Glue, Neighbour = Glue"
**Rule:** "Immediate left/right" and "neighbour" both mean **zero gap** — glued seats.
> 🗣️ *"Immediate means glued — no air between them!"*

---

## 🧠 Mnemonic 7: "Opposite = Half the Circle"
**Rule:** In a circle of N people, opposite of position 1 = position (1 + N/2).
> 🗣️ *"To find the face across the cake, walk half the cake!"*
Works only for **even** N. Odd circle = no exact opposite!

---

## 🧠 Mnemonic 8: "Two Rows Facing = Mirror Trap"
**Rule:** In two parallel rows facing each other, the person "opposite" sits directly across — but their left-right is **mirrored**.
> 🗣️ *"Mirror, mirror, opposite row — left is right and right is left, you know!"*

---

## 🧠 Mnemonic 9: "At the End = Only One Neighbour"
**Rule:** In a linear row, end persons have exactly ONE neighbour — use this to test placements.
> 🗣️ *"Ends are lonely — one friend only!"*
If a clue says "X has two neighbours," X can never be at an end.

---

## 🧠 Mnemonic 10: "Who/Which = Second Noun Rule"
**Rule:** In "A, who sits third to the left of B, …" — "who" refers to **A** (the noun just before it).
> 🗣️ *"WHO sticks to the name it hugs!"*
Examiner's favourite trap: making you attach the clue to the wrong person.

---

## 🧠 Mnemonic 11: "Draw Big, Write Small"
**Rule:** Draw the diagram LARGE, write initials only (R for Ravi, S for Sita).
> 🗣️ *"Big circle, tiny letters, zero confusion!"*
Full names waste time and crowd the diagram.

---

## 🧠 Mnemonic 12: "Two Cases? Kill One Fast"
**Rule:** When a clue allows 2 arrangements, draw BOTH, then hunt for the clue that murders one.
> 🗣️ *"Two roads diverge — one clue is the axe that blocks one road!"*
Never erase the second case until a clue kills it.

---

## 🧠 Mnemonic 13: "Count the Heads Before the Seats"
**Rule:** Before drawing, count persons: 8 people = 8 seats. Mismatch = misread question.
> 🗣️ *"No head left behind!"*

---

## 🧠 Mnemonic 14: "Negative Clue = Pencil Mark"
**Rule:** "A does NOT sit next to B" — mark a small ✗ between possible A–B spots instead of placing anyone.
> 🗣️ *"Negative clues don't build houses — they put up 'No Entry' boards!"*
Use them at the END to eliminate, not at the start to place.

---

## 🧠 Mnemonic 15: "Verify = The 10-Second Wedding Check"
**Rule:** After solving, re-read EVERY clue once and check your diagram against it.
> 🗣️ *"Before the wedding, check the guest list — one wrong seat ruins the party!"*
One violated clue = entire arrangement wrong = all questions based on it gone.

---

# ⏭️ Skip Strategy — When to SKIP a Sitting Arrangement Question

### 🚫 Skip Tip 1: The 20-Second Clue Scan
Read all clues once. If you cannot find even **ONE definite (fixed) clue** to anchor the puzzle within 20 seconds, **mark it and move on**. A puzzle with all-relative clues ("somewhere left of…", "not adjacent to…") eats 3–4 minutes. Come back only if time remains.

### 🚫 Skip Tip 2: Two-Case Explosion
If after placing 3–4 persons you still have **2 or more parallel cases** alive and no clue seems to kill any of them, **skip**. In Intermediate-level exams, a puzzle needing 3+ parallel cases is a time-trap deliberately planted by the examiner. Invest maximum **90 seconds per puzzle set-up**, then decide: solve or skip.

### 🚫 Skip Tip 3: Single Question vs. Puzzle Set
If a long arrangement puzzle (6–8 clues) carries only **ONE question** instead of a set of 4–5, the marks-per-minute ratio is poor. **Skip it in round 1.** Always prioritise puzzle **sets** (one arrangement → 4–5 answers) over standalone arrangement questions — same effort, several times the reward.

> ⏱️ **Golden Time Rule:** A full puzzle set (arrangement + 4–5 questions) deserves **4–5 minutes maximum**. If the diagram isn't complete in 2.5 minutes, guess strategically from partial information or skip — never let one puzzle eat the time of 10 other questions.
