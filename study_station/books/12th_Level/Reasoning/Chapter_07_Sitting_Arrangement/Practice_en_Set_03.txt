51. Five persons P, Q, R, S and T sit in a row facing north. P sits at the left end and T sits at the right end. Q sits next to P. R sits between Q and S. Who sits in the middle?
(a) R (b) S (c) Q (d) T
Answer: (a)
Solution: The tempting answer is Q, because Q is placed right after the end-seat clue and feels central, but Q sits second from the left. The order is P, Q, R, S, T, so the third (middle) seat goes to R, so the answer is R.
Source: PYQ-style

52. Six persons A, B, C, D, E and F sit around a circular table facing the centre. A sits opposite D. B sits to the immediate right of A. C sits opposite B. Who sits opposite E?
(a) C (b) A (c) D (d) F
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is C, since C is already an "opposite" answer in the clues and feels reusable, but C is paired with B. Fixing A and D opposite, B next to A, and C opposite B leaves E and F in the last two seats, which are opposite each other, so the answer is F.
Source: PYQ-style

53. Five girls M, N, O, P and Q sit in a row facing south. M sits at one end. N sits second to the right of M. O sits between N and Q. Who sits in the middle?
(a) Q (b) P (c) O (d) N
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is O, because "sits between" sounds like the middle of the row, but O is only between N and Q, not in the row's centre. Facing south, right means the viewer's left, so M must be at the viewer's right end; the order from the viewer's left is Q, O, N, P, M. The third seat is N, so the answer is N.
Source: PYQ-style

54. Seven persons A to G sit in a row facing north. B sits third from the left end. E sits exactly between B and G. A sits to the immediate right of G. C sits at the right end. Who sits second from the right end?
(a) A (b) G (c) F (d) E
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is G, because A is described only as "right of G", making G look second-last, but C occupies the right end and pushes A next to it. The order is F/D, D/F, B, E, G, A, C, so second from the right is A, so the answer is A.

55. Six persons J, K, L, M, N and O sit around a hexagonal table facing the centre. K sits opposite N. L sits to the immediate right of K. M sits between N and O. Who sits opposite L?

Answer: (c)
Solution: The tempting answer is O, since M sits "between N and O" and O feels like the far seat, but betweenness forces M into the seat opposite L. With K and N opposite, L next to K, and N-M-O consecutive, the seat opposite L is M, so the answer is M.

56. In a row of students facing north, Ravi is 12th from the left and Suresh is 15th from the right. They interchange their positions, and now Ravi becomes 18th from the left. How many students are there in the row?
(a) 30 (b) 31 (c) 33 (d) 32
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is 33, obtained by adding 18 + 15 and forgetting the overlap. After interchange, Ravi is at Suresh's original seat, which is 15th from the right and now 18th from the left. Total = 18 + 15 − 1 = 32, so the answer is 32.
Source: PYQ-style

(a) S (b) Q (c) T (d) P
Answer: (c)
Solution: The tempting answer is S because it is described as "below Q", but S is also above T, so one box lies under it. The order from top is R, P, Q, S, T, so the bottom box is T, so the answer is T.

58. Eight friends sit in two rows of four each, facing each other. Row 1 (facing south): A, B, C, D. Row 2 (facing north): E, F, G, H. A faces G. B sits to the right of A. F sits to the left of G. Who faces B?
(a) G (b) H (c) F (d) E
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is G, because A faces G and B is next to A, so G feels close, but facing pairs depend on column position, not adjacency. With A facing G, B one column to A's right, and F one column to G's left on the same side, B aligns exactly with F, so the answer is F.

(a) Z (b) U (c) V (d) Y
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is V, because V is explicitly "opposite X" and oppositeness is fresh in mind, but W is a different seat. Placing V opposite X, U two seats to X's right, and W between X and Y on the only free side puts U exactly across from W, so the answer is U.

60. In a queue facing north, Mohan is 8th from the front and Sohan is 12th from the back. If there are 25 persons in the queue, how many persons stand between Mohan and Sohan?
(a) 5 (b) 6 (c) 4 (d) 7
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is 6, from subtracting 12 − 8 without converting Sohan's position from the back. Sohan is 25 − 12 + 1 = 14th from the front. Persons between 8th and 14th = 14 − 8 − 1 = 5, so the answer is 5.
Source: PYQ-style

Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is "second from the left", because O is "second to the right of N" and a direct shift of two seems natural, but the rows face opposite directions, so N's right is the viewer's right while M's row is read the other way. Counting columns carefully, O aligns with the middle person of row 1, so the answer is the person in the middle.

74. In a row facing north, the position of Tina from the left is the same as the position of Rina from the right, and there are 3 girls between them. If Tina is 10th from the left, how many girls are in the row?
(a) 23 (b) 24 (c) 22 (d) 25
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is 24, from adding 10 + 10 + 3 + 1 and double-counting one of the girls. Rina is 10 + 3 + 1 = 14th from the left and 10th from the right. Total = 14 + 10 − 1 = 23, so the answer is 23.

75. Six diplomats sit around a circular table facing the centre. The Indian sits between the American and the Frenchman. The American sits to the immediate left of the Indian. The Chinese sits opposite the Indian. The Russian sits to the immediate right of the Chinese. The Brazilian sits to the immediate left of the Chinese. Who sits opposite the Russian?
(a) The Frenchman (b) The Brazilian (c) The American (d) The Indian
Answer: (a)
Solution: Number the seats clockwise with the Indian at seat 0; facing the centre, left is clockwise. The American is at seat 1, so the Frenchman is at seat 5. The Chinese is at seat 3, the Russian at seat 2 and the Brazilian at seat 4. The trap is the American, who is next to the Russian; the seat opposite the Russian is seat 5, so the answer is the Frenchman.
