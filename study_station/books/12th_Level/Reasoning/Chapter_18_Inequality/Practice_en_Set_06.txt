Questions 126–150: Hard

126. Statements: P ≥ Q = R > S; T < S ≤ U
Conclusions: I. P > T  II. R > U
(a) Neither I nor II follows (b) Only II follows (c) Both I and II follow (d) Only I follows
Answer: (d)
Solution: The tempting trap is conclusion II: since S ≤ U and R > S, many assume R > U, but U could be much larger than R (e.g., S = 2, U = 10, R = 3), so II does not follow. For I, P ≥ Q = R > S > T gives P > T definitely. So the answer is: only I follows.
Source: PYQ-style

127. Statements: A < B ≤ C = D > E ≥ F
Conclusions: I. C > F  II. A < D
(a) Only I follows (b) Both I and II follow (c) Only II follows (d) Neither I nor II follows
Answer: (b)
Solution: C = D > E ≥ F gives C > F, so I follows. A < B ≤ C = D gives A < D, so II follows. Both conclusions are established by direct chains, so the answer is: both I and II follow.
