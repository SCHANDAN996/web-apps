# 🪄 Short Tricks — Inequality

---

## 🎭 Mnemonic Box 1 — "The Hungry Crocodile"
> **The crocodile's mouth always opens toward the BIGGER number.**
> `5 > 3` → the croc eats 5. `2 < 7` → the croc eats 7.
> Never decide direction by the symbol's shape — ask: *"Which side would the croc eat?"*

---

## 🎭 Mnemonic Box 2 — "GOLD = Greater Or Less, Decide!"
> When two conclusions are given and neither follows alone, check for **GOLD**:
> - **G** — are the two conclusions a **G**enuine pair (a > b and a < b, or a ≥ b and a ≤ b)?
> - **O** — do they cover **O**nly the same two elements?
> - **L** — are they **L**ogical opposites (all three relations possible)?
> - **D** — then the answer is **D**: "Either I or II follows."
> **GOLD pair = Either-Or!**

---

## 🎭 Mnemonic Box 3 — "The Bridge Rule"
> To connect A and C, you need a **bridge** through B.
> `A > B` and `B > C` → bridge exists → `A > C` ✔
> `A > B` and `B < C` → **bridge broken** (signs face away) → no relation ✘
> **"Same direction = bridge stands. Opposite direction = bridge collapses."**

---

## 🎭 Mnemonic Box 4 — "Strict Needs Strict — But One Strict Link Is Enough"
> `>` and `<` are **STRICT** (bouncers — no entry without proof).
> `≥` and `≤` are **SOFT** (friendly — equal is allowed).
> **"A strict conclusion needs at least one strict link in a same-direction chain."**
> - `A ≥ B ≥ C` → no strict link anywhere → only `A ≥ C` ✔, never `A > C` ✘
> - `A ≥ B > C` → the link `B > C` is strict and all signs point the same way → **`A > C` ✔**
> **"All-soft chain = soft conclusion. One strict link in a one-way chain = strict conclusion."**

---

## 🎭 Mnemonic Box 5 — "The Wall of ="
> `A = B` is a **wall**, not a door.
> `A > B = C` → A is greater than the whole wall → `A > C` ✔
> But `A = B > C` and conclusion `A > C` ✔ — the wall passes the relation through.
> **"Equals sign transmits, never blocks."**

---

## 🎭 Mnemonic Box 6 — "SOS: Same, Opposite, Single"
> For **Either-Or**, remember **SOS**:
> - **S**ame two elements in both conclusions?
> - **O**pposite relations (>, < or ≥, ≤)?
> - **S**ingle pair covers all three possibilities (>, <, =)?
> All three YES → Either-Or. Even one NO → Neither.

---

## 🎭 Mnemonic Box 7 — "The Grumpy Gatekeeper (≥)"
> `A ≥ B` means the gatekeeper lets in **two guests**: "greater" AND "equal."
> So if the only link between A and B is `≥` and the conclusion says `A > B`, the gatekeeper says: *"You forgot my equal guest — REJECTED!"*
> **"A single ≥ link alone can never prove >. You need a strict link somewhere in the chain."**

---

## 🎭 Mnemonic Box 8 — "Chain Train"
> Write the statement as a **train**: `P ≥ Q = R > S ≤ T`
> - Engine P, coaches Q, R, S, T.
> - To go from P to S, every coupling between them must point the **same way** (or be `=`).
> - `P ≥ Q = R > S`: couplings ≥, =, > — all "forward" and one of them (`R > S`) is strict → **P > S** ✔
> - But from S to T the coupling `≤` points backward → the train cannot pass → no relation between P and T.
> **"One-way train + at least one strict coupling = strict conclusion. One-way train, all soft = soft conclusion."**

---

## 🎭 Mnemonic Box 9 — "Coded Inequality = Decode First, Think Later"
> Symbols wear costumes: `@ means >`, `# means <`, `$ means =`, `% means ≥`, `& means ≤`.
> **Trick: rewrite the ENTIRE question in plain symbols before reading any conclusion.**
> 30 seconds of decoding saves 3 minutes of confusion.
> **"Decode once, solve five."**

---

## 🎭 Mnemonic Box 10 — "The Liar's Test (Definite vs Possible)"
> A conclusion "follows" only if it is true in **EVERY** possible arrangement.
> **Test: try to make the conclusion FALSE.** If you can build even one arrangement where it fails → it does NOT follow.
> **"One counter-example kills a conclusion."**

---

## 🎭 Mnemonic Box 11 — "Reverse Trap Alarm"
> Statement: `A > B`. Conclusion: `B < A`.
> Same thing, flipped! ✔ FOLLOWS.
> But Statement: `A ≥ B`. Conclusion: `B < A` — **TRAP!** Flipping ≥ gives ≤, not <.
> **"Flip the symbol, flip BOTH parts — strict stays strict, soft stays soft."**

---

## 🎭 Mnemonic Box 12 — "Three Musketeers of Either-Or"
> Between any two elements A and B, only three relations exist: **A > B, A = B, A < B.**
> A valid Either-Or pair must cover **all three musketeers**:
> - `A > B` + `A ≤ B` → covers all three ✔
> - `A > B` + `A < B` → misses "=" ✘ (Neither!)
> **"Miss one musketeer, lose the Either-Or."**

---

## 🎭 Mnemonic Box 13 — "The 20-Second Scan"
> Before solving, scan the conclusions:
> 1. Conclusion connects elements with a **direct single statement**? → Check instantly.
> 2. Conclusion jumps **3+ elements**? → Build the chain.
> 3. Conclusion has **opposite-facing signs** in its path? → Mark "doesn't follow" in 5 seconds.
> **"Broken bridge = instant reject."**

---

## 🎭 Mnemonic Box 14 — "Direction First, Strength Second"
> When a chain mixes symbols, check two things in order:
> 1. **Direction:** do all signs on the path point the same way (ignoring `=`)? If not → no relation at all.
> 2. **Strength:** if direction is fine, the conclusion is **strict (>, <)** if at least one strict link lies on the path; otherwise it is **soft (≥, ≤)**.
> - `A ≥ B > C` → same direction, one strict link → `A > C` ✔
> - `A ≥ B ≥ C` → same direction, no strict link → only `A ≥ C` ✔
> **"Direction decides IF you can conclude; the strictest link on the path decides HOW strongly."**

---

## 🎭 Mnemonic Box 15 — "The Mirror Check"
> For `A ≥ B ≥ C`, conclusion `A ≥ C` ✔ — mirror the chain, it holds.
> For `A ≥ B ≤ C`, conclusion `A ≥ C` ✘ — the mirror **cracks** at the sign change.
> **"Sign change in the middle = mirror cracks = no relation."**

---

# ⏭️ Skip Strategy — When to Skip an Inequality Question

### 🚫 Skip Tip 1 — "The 15-Second Decode Rule"
In **coded inequality**, if you cannot finish decoding the symbol table into plain `>, <, =, ≥, ≤` within **15 seconds**, mark it and move on. Come back only if time remains — a half-decoded question guarantees a silly error.

### 🚫 Skip Tip 2 — "The 4+ Variable Chain"
If the statement chains **4 or more variables with mixed directions** (e.g., `P > Q ≤ R = S < T`) AND the conclusions connect the two extreme ends, the chain is already broken — but if you can't spot the break in **10 seconds**, skip. Such questions are designed to eat 90+ seconds for 1 mark.

### 🚫 Skip Tip 3 — "Either-Or Doubt = Skip, Don't Guess"
If you've proven neither conclusion follows but are **unsure whether the Either-Or conditions (SOS rule)** are fully met, do NOT gamble. A wrong Either-Or guess costs negative marking. Skip it, bank the sure questions first, and return with a fresh eye — Either-Or traps are the #1 error source in this chapter.
