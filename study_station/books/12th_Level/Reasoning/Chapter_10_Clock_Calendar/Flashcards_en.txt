Card 1
Front: How many degrees does the minute hand cover in one minute?
Back: 6°. The minute hand completes 360° in 60 minutes, so 360 ÷ 60 = 6° per minute.

Card 2
Front: How many degrees does the hour hand cover in one minute?
Back: 0.5°. The hour hand completes 360° in 12 hours (720 minutes), so 360 ÷ 720 = 0.5° per minute.

Card 3
Front: What is the relative speed of the minute hand over the hour hand?
Back: 5.5° per minute (6° − 0.5°). This is the key to almost every clock-angle problem.

Card 4
Front: What is the shortcut formula for the angle between the two hands at H hours M minutes?
Back: Angle = |30H − 5.5M| degrees. If the result is more than 180°, subtract it from 360° to get the smaller angle.

Card 5
Front: How many times do the two hands of a clock coincide in 12 hours — and why not 12?
Back: 11 times. Between 11 and 1 o'clock they coincide only once (at 12:00), so one overlap is "lost" in every 12-hour cycle.

Card 6
Front: How many times are the clock hands at right angles (90°) in 12 hours?
Back: 22 times — twice in most hours, but the 2–3 and 8–9 slots share one right angle each with the neighbouring hour.

Card 7
Front: How many times are the hands in a straight line (180° apart) in 12 hours?
Back: 11 times. Between 5 and 7 o'clock only one 180° position occurs (at 6:00), so one is lost.

Card 8
Front: At what time between 4 and 5 o'clock are the hands together?
Back: At 4 : 21 9/11 minutes. Formula: M = (60/11) × H = (60/11) × 4 = 240/11 = 21 9/11 min past 4.

Card 9
Front: A clock gains 5 minutes every hour. If set right at 6 a.m., what will it show when the true time is 6 p.m.?
Back: 7 p.m. In 12 true hours it gains 12 × 5 = 60 minutes, so it shows 6:00 + 1:00 = 7:00.

Card 10
Front: What is an "odd day" in calendar problems?
Back: The remainder when total days are divided by 7. Odd days tell you how many days the weekday shifts forward.

Card 11
Front: How many odd days does an ordinary year have? And a leap year?
Back: Ordinary year: 1 odd day (365 = 52 weeks + 1). Leap year: 2 odd days (366 = 52 weeks + 2).

Card 12
Front: What is the mnemonic to remember odd days in 100, 200, 300 and 400 years?
Back: "5–3–1–0" → 100 years = 5 odd days, 200 years = 3, 300 years = 1, 400 years = 0. Remember: "Five Three One Zero — century's hero."

Card 13
Front: What is the mnemonic for the month codes used in calendar shortcuts?
Back: "033 614 625 035" — codes for January to December (Jan 0, Feb 3, Mar 3, Apr 6, May 1, Jun 4, Jul 6, Aug 2, Sep 5, Oct 0, Nov 3, Dec 5). Chant it as a phone number: 033-614-625-035.

Card 14
Front: Is the year 1900 a leap year? Why or why not?
Back: No. A century year is a leap year only if divisible by 400. 1900 ÷ 400 leaves a remainder, so 1900 is NOT a leap year — but 2000 is.

Card 15
Front: Examiner's trap: "The hands of a clock are at right angles 44 times in 24 hours" — true or false?
Back: True. 22 times in 12 hours × 2 = 44. The trap option says 48 (24 × 2), forgetting the two "lost" right angles per 12-hour cycle.

Card 16
Front: Examiner's trap: If today is Monday, what day will it be after 61 days? Where do students slip?
Back: Saturday. 61 ÷ 7 = 8 weeks + 5 odd days; Monday + 5 = Saturday (Mon+1=Tue, +2=Wed, +3=Thu, +4=Fri, +5=Sat). The trap is miscounting the starting day and answering Friday.

Card 17
Front: After how many years does the calendar of an ordinary year repeat itself?
Back: It depends on the leap-year pattern: an ordinary year's calendar repeats after 6, 11 or 11 years (the gaps within a 28-year cycle follow 6-11-11, or 11-11-6 depending on where the year falls). The full calendar cycle repeats every 400 years.

Card 18
Front: What was the day on 15 August 1947, and how can you verify it quickly?
Back: Friday. Using odd days: 1600 years (0) + 300 years (1) + 46 years (57 odd days → 1) + days up to 15 Aug 1947 (227 days → 3) = 0+1+1+3 = 5 → Friday (taking 0 = Sunday).

Card 19
Front: A watch shows 8:30. What is the angle between the hands — and what's the common wrong answer?
Back: 75°. Angle = |30×8 − 5.5×30| = |240 − 165| = 75°. The trap answer is 90°, which ignores that the hour hand has moved halfway between 8 and 9.

Card 20
Front: In a mirror image of a clock, what is the shortcut to find the real time?
Back: Subtract the mirror time from 11:60 (or 12:00 if minutes are 00). Example: mirror shows 2:30 → real time = 11:60 − 2:30 = 9:30.
