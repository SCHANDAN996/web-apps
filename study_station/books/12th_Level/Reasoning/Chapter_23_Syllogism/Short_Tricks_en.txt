# 🪄 Short Tricks & Mnemonics — Syllogism

---

## 🧠 Mnemonic 1: "A E I O — The Vowel Family"

**Trick:** Remember the four statement types by vowels:
- **A** = **A**ll (Universal Positive)
- **E** = **E**liminate/No (Universal Negative)
- **I** = **I**n some (Particular Positive)
- **O** = s**O**me not (Particular Negative)

**Memory hook:** *"All Elephants Inspire Others"* — A, E, I, O in order!

---

## 🧠 Mnemonic 2: "Middle Term Must Be Distributed — MMD"

**Trick:** The middle term (the one that appears in both statements but NOT in the conclusion) must be **distributed** (cover the whole class) at least once.

**Memory hook:** *"The middleman must deliver the FULL package at least once — no half deliveries!"*

---

## 🧠 Mnemonic 3: "Two Negatives = Dead End"

**Trick:** If **both statements are negative** (No/Some-not), **no valid conclusion** can ever be drawn.

**Memory hook:** *"Two NOs never make a YES in syllogism."*

---

## 🧠 Mnemonic 4: "Two Particulars = Party Crashers"

**Trick:** If **both statements are particular** (Some/Some-not), **no definite conclusion** follows.

**Memory hook:** *"Two 'SOME' guests can't decide anything — the party never starts!"*

---

## 🧠 Mnemonic 5: "Conclusion Follows the Weaker Statement"

**Trick:** If one statement is universal (All/No) and the other is particular (Some), the conclusion is always **particular** (Some).

**Memory hook:** *"The chain is only as strong as its weakest link — 'Some' always wins the tug-of-war."*

---

## 🧠 Mnemonic 6: "Negative In, Negative Out"

**Trick:** If even **one statement is negative**, the conclusion (if any) must be **negative**.

**Memory hook:** *"One rotten apple spoils the basket — one 'No' makes the answer 'No'."*

---

## 🧠 Mnemonic 7: "Venn First, Words Later"

**Trick:** Never solve by reading statements repeatedly. Draw **minimum Venn circles** first — one circle per term, overlap only what the statement forces.

**Memory hook:** *"Draw before you decide — the pen is faster than the brain."*

---

## 🧠 Mnemonic 8: "All A are B ≠ All B are A"

**Trick:** "All A are B" only means A sits **inside** B. B may be much bigger. Never reverse it.

**Memory hook:** *"All dogs are animals, but all animals are NOT dogs — the dog fits in the kennel, the kennel doesn't fit in the dog!"*

---

## 🧠 Mnemonic 9: "Possibility = Check the Opposite Diagram"

**Trick:** For "possibility" conclusions, ask: *Can I draw a diagram where this is true WITHOUT breaking the statements?* If yes → possibility follows.

**Memory hook:** *"Possibility is an optimist — if it's not impossible, it's possible!"*

---

## 🧠 Mnemonic 10: "Either-Or Twins"

**Trick:** An "either-or" answer works only when **both conclusions are individually false**, they have the **same subject and predicate**, and one is positive while the other is negative — a complementary pair such as **A + O** (All / Some-not) or **E + I** (No / Some).

**Memory hook:** *"Either-Or twins: same parents (subject-predicate), opposite moods (+/−), and BOTH must fail alone."*

---

## 🧠 Mnemonic 11: "Some is NOT Some-Not"

**Trick:** "Some A are B" does **not** imply "Some A are not B". Both can be true, but one never proves the other.

**Memory hook:** *"Some friends came to the party — that doesn't prove some didn't. Maybe ALL came!"*

---

## 🧠 Mnemonic 12: "Only = All, but Flipped"

**Trick:** "Only A are B" = "**All B are A**". Flip the terms, keep it universal.

**Memory hook:** *"ONLY is a mirror — it flips the sentence: Only men are priests → All priests are men."*

---

## 🧠 Mnemonic 13: "The 3-Circle Default"

**Trick:** For "All A are B, All B are C" — draw three **nested circles** (A inside B inside C). Nested circles solve most basic "All–All" questions instantly.

**Memory hook:** *"Russian dolls — smallest doll inside the middle, middle inside the biggest."*

---

## 🧠 Mnemonic 14: "Definite vs Possible — The Courtroom Rule"

**Trick:** A **definite** conclusion must be true in **every** possible diagram (guilty beyond doubt). A **possibility** needs just **one** valid diagram (reasonable doubt is enough).

**Memory hook:** *"Definite = proved in ALL courts. Possible = wins in ANY one court."*

---

## 🧠 Mnemonic 15: "No A is B = Total Divorce"

**Trick:** "No A is B" means circles A and B **never touch** — complete separation. And this one IS reversible: No A is B ⟺ No B is A. Note: "Some A are B" (I-type) is also reversible — Some A are B ⟺ Some B are A. But "All" and "Some-not" never reverse.

**Memory hook:** *"Only two statements can swap seats: 'No' and 'Some' — divorce and friendship both work two ways!"*

---

# ⏭️ Skip Strategy — When to Skip a Syllogism Question

### Tip 1: The 20-Second Diagram Rule
If you cannot set up the basic Venn diagram within **20 seconds** (e.g., 4+ terms, mixed "Only/Some-not" statements), **mark it and move on**. Return only if time remains — a tangled 4-statement syllogism can eat 2+ minutes for 1 mark.

### Tip 2: Attempt Direct Conclusions First
Questions combining **possibility conclusions with either-or pairs** need extra verification steps and take longer. In the first pass, **skip these** and attempt the direct definite-conclusion questions — they are faster and safer.

### Tip 3: Negative-Statement Trap Check
If a question has **two negative statements**, the answer is almost always "no conclusion follows" — answer in 10 seconds. But if you see **unfamiliar phrasing** like "Some A can never be B" or "A few" and can't convert it confidently within **15 seconds**, skip — mistranslating one statement guarantees a wrong answer.
