Questions 51–75: Medium

51. Statements: All pens are books. Some books are chairs.
Conclusions: I. Some pens are chairs. II. Some chairs are books.
(a) Only I follows (b) Neither follows (c) Both follow (d) Only II follows
Answer: (d)
Solution: The tempting trap is Conclusion I — "All pens are books" plus "Some books are chairs" does not force any pen to be a chair, because the books that are chairs may lie outside the pens circle. Conclusion II is just the conversion of "Some books are chairs", which always holds. So the answer is Only II follows.
Source: PYQ-style

52. Statements: Some dogs are cats. All cats are animals.
Conclusions: I. Some dogs are animals. II. All animals are cats.
(a) Neither follows (b) Only II follows (c) Both follow (d) Only I follows
Answer: (d)
Solution: Conclusion II is the classic reversed-implication trap: "All cats are animals" never means all animals are cats. Since some dogs are cats and every cat is an animal, those dogs that are cats are certainly animals, so Conclusion I follows. So the answer is Only I follows.
Source: PYQ-style

53. Statements: No apple is a banana. Some bananas are grapes.
Conclusions: I. Some grapes are not apples. II. No grape is an apple.
(a) Both follow (b) Only II follows (c) Only I follows (d) Neither follows
Answer: (c)
Solution: Conclusion II is the trap — a universal negative cannot be drawn from a particular statement; some grapes could still be apples. But the grapes that are bananas cannot be apples (since no banana is an apple), so "Some grapes are not apples" definitely follows. So the answer is Only I follows.
