101. Statements: All pens are books. All books are chairs.
Conclusions: I. All pens are chairs. II. All chairs are pens.
(a) Both follow (b) Only II follows (c) Only I follows (d) Neither follows
Answer: (c)
Solution: The tempting II reverses the relation; chairs may include many things besides pens, so it fails. Pens lie inside books, which lie inside chairs, so I follows. The answer is Only I follows.
Source: PYQ-style (Syllogism)

102. Statements: Some dogs are cats. Some cats are rats.
Conclusions: I. Some dogs are rats. II. Some cats are dogs.
(a) Only I follows (b) Only II follows (c) Both follow (d) Neither follows
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is I: two "some" statements with no universal link cannot force a dog–rat connection. II is just the converse of the first statement, which always holds for "some". The answer is Only II follows.
Source: PYQ-style (Syllogism)

Solution: I is the trap: apples may still be fruits through another route, since the negative statement only separates apples from bananas. Because all bananas are fruits and no banana is an apple, that part of fruits is not apples, so II follows. The answer is Only II follows.
