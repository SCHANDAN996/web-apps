Card 1
Front: What is a syllogism in reasoning?
Back: A logical argument where a conclusion is drawn from two given statements (premises), assuming the statements are true even if they contradict real-world facts.

Card 2
Front: In syllogism, should you use your real-world knowledge to judge the statements?
Back: No. Treat every statement as 100% true, even if it says "All cats are dogs." Only the logical relationship matters.

Card 3
Front: Which mnemonic helps you remember the four types of statements?
Back: "A-E-I-O": A = All (universal positive), E = No (universal negative), I = Some (particular positive), O = Some not (particular negative).

Card 4
Front: What is the golden rule for a definite conclusion?
Back: A conclusion is definitely true only if it holds in EVERY possible Venn diagram of the statements. If even one valid diagram breaks it, the answer is "does not follow."

Card 5
Front: From "All A are B," can we definitely conclude "All B are A"?
Back: No. "All A are B" only gives "Some B are A" (conversion). The reverse "All B are A" is a classic wrong option.

Card 6
Front: What does "Some A are B" convert into?
Back: "Some B are A." Particular positive (I-type) statements convert directly.

Card 7
Front: What does "No A is B" convert into?
Back: "No B is A." Universal negative (E-type) statements convert directly.

Card 8
Front: Can "All A are B" be converted directly?
Back: Not fully. It converts only to "Some B are A" (conversion by limitation) — never to "All B are A."

Card 9
Front: What is the "possibility" trick in syllogism?
Back: If a definite conclusion fails, check whether it is possible: if there is NO definite relation blocking it, a "possibility" conclusion (e.g., "Some A being B is a possibility") can be true.

Card 10
Front: When does a "possibility" conclusion become FALSE?
Back: When a definite negative relation exists — e.g., if "No A is B" is given, then "Some A being B is a possibility" is false.

Card 11
Front: Examiner's trap: Statements say "All pens are books" and "All books are chairs." Option: "All chairs are pens." Why is it wrong?
Back: The trap reverses the chain. Correct chain: All pens are chairs. Reversing "All" statements is invalid — only "Some chairs are pens" follows.

Card 12
Front: Examiner's trap: "Some A are B" + "Some B are C" — can we conclude "Some A are C"?
Back: No. Two particular (Some) statements never give a definite conclusion. Examiners place "Some A are C" as a tempting wrong option.

Card 13
Front: What is the "Either-Or" (complementary pair) case?
Back: When two conclusions form a complementary pair like "All A are B" and "Some A are not B" (A–O pair) or "No A is B" and "Some A are B" (E–I pair), both individually false but covering all possibilities — the answer is "either I or II follows."

Card 14
Front: What three conditions must an Either-Or pair satisfy?
Back: (1) Same subject and predicate in both conclusions, (2) both conclusions individually false, (3) one positive and one negative forming a valid complementary pair — A–O ("All" with "Some not") or E–I ("No" with "Some").

Card 15
Front: Quick memory trick: what is the minimum rule for combining two statements?
Back: The middle term (common term) must be "distributed" — at least one statement must say "All" or "No" about it. Two "Some" statements never yield a definite conclusion.

Card 16
Front: In "Only a few A are B," what two things are simultaneously true?
Back: "Some A are B" AND "Some A are not B." Both parts must be checked — missing the negative half is a common trap.

Card 17
Front: How do you solve syllogism fastest in the exam?
Back: Draw the minimum Venn diagram (circles), check each conclusion against it; only if a conclusion fails, draw an alternate diagram to test possibility or either-or.

Card 18
Front: From a negative statement like "No A is B," can any positive definite conclusion follow?
Back: No. Negative premises give only negative definite conclusions. Any positive definite conclusion from purely negative statements is invalid.

Card 19
Front: What does "At least some A are B" mean in syllogism language?
Back: It simply means "Some A are B" — a particular positive statement. Don't treat it as a new type.

Card 20
Front: If a conclusion is true in the real world but not derivable from the statements, is it correct?
Back: No. Syllogism tests logic, not facts. A real-world-true conclusion that doesn't follow from the statements is marked wrong.
