Card 1
Front: In "A + B × C − D", which operation do you perform first — and why do most students get this wrong?
Back: Multiplication first (B × C), then left to right for + and −. Students wrongly solve strictly left to right, ignoring the BODMAS priority rule.

Card 2
Front: What does each letter of BODMAS stand for?
Back: Bracket, Of (orders/powers), Division, Multiplication, Addition, Subtraction. Division and Multiplication have equal priority — solve left to right; same for Addition and Subtraction.

Card 3
Front: Which mnemonic trick helps you remember that '×' and '÷' are solved before '+' and '−'?
Back: "Big operations before small ones" — or remember BODMAS as "Beta, Of Divide Multiply, Add Subtract." Multiplication/Division are "stronger" and always jump the queue.

Card 4
Front: If '+' means '×', '×' means '−', '−' means '÷' and '÷' means '+', what is the very first step to solve such a question?
Back: Rewrite the entire expression with the substituted symbols BEFORE calculating anything. Never substitute mentally one symbol at a time — that is where errors creep in.

Card 5
Front: Examiner's trap: In symbol-substitution questions, why is solving left-to-right after substitution still wrong?
Back: Because after replacing symbols, BODMAS still applies. The new '×' or '÷' must be done before the new '+' or '−', regardless of position. Examiners place a '×' at the end to catch left-to-right solvers.

Card 6
Front: In "interchange of signs/numbers" questions, what is the fastest solving method?
Back: Test each option by actually interchanging and checking whether the equation balances. Start with the option that swaps the two most "suspicious" symbols (usually ÷ and −, or two digits).

Card 7
Front: What is the trick to remember that 'Of' comes before Division in BODMAS?
Back: Mnemonic: "Of is a hidden multiplication" — e.g., 1/2 of 8 = 4 must be done before any ÷. Remember: "OF = Order First."

Card 8
Front: If 'P' means '+', 'Q' means '−', 'R' means '×', 'S' means '÷', how do you avoid mixing up the letters?
Back: Write a small code table on the rough sheet first (P→+, Q→−, R→×, S→÷), then decode the full expression in one pass. Never decode letter-by-letter in your head.

Card 9
Front: Examiner's trap: In "which interchange makes the equation correct" questions, why do students waste 2 minutes?
Back: They check all four options fully. Smart approach: check the option where '÷' is involved first — division errors are the most common reason an equation fails to balance.

Card 10
Front: In balancing-equation questions (e.g., 7 * 3 * 4 = 25), what should you try first?
Back: Try combinations of × and + first (7 × 3 + 4 = 25). Multiplication grows numbers fast, so if the RHS is large, suspect '×'; if small, suspect '−' or '÷'.

Card 11
Front: What does "VBODMAS" add to BODMAS, and when does it matter?
Back: V = Vinculum (bar bracket), solved before all other brackets. It matters when a bar appears over part of an expression, e.g., 5 + 3−2̅ — solve the bar portion first.

Card 12
Front: In questions like "If 2 @ 3 = 13 and 4 @ 5 = 41, find 6 @ 7", what pattern should you test first?
Back: Test a² + b² (4+9=13 ✓, 16+25=41 ✓). So 6 @ 7 = 36 + 49 = 85. Always test squares and a×b ± (a+b) patterns first — they cover most such questions.

Card 13
Front: Why should you never assume the pattern from just ONE example in "define a new operation" questions?
Back: One example can fit multiple patterns (e.g., 2 @ 3 = 6 fits both a×b and a+b+1). Verify the pattern with the second given example before applying it.

Card 14
Front: In "correct the equation by interchanging two numbers" questions, what is the quickest check?
Back: Look at the digit that makes division or subtraction impossible/awkward (e.g., a number not divisible). Swap that digit with one that makes the division exact — that pair is usually the answer.

Card 15
Front: What is the 15-second rule for Mathematical Operations questions in the exam?
Back: If the substitution/decoding is done and the expression is set up within 15 seconds, solve it (30–45 sec total). If you cannot even decode the pattern in 15 seconds, mark it and move on — return later.

Card 16
Front: Examiner's trap: In "select the correct equation" questions where symbols are given new meanings, what do most students forget?
Back: They forget to apply BODMAS AFTER substitution in every option. All four options must be fully decoded and evaluated — the first option that "looks right" is often a near-miss trap.

Card 17
Front: In statements like "A ÷ B means A is the mother of B" (mixed operation-coding), what is the safe method?
Back: Convert each symbol into its meaning step by step and draw a mini family tree or relation chain. Never hold more than two relations in your head at once.

Card 18
Front: What is the bracket priority order when multiple brackets appear together?
Back: Vinculum (bar) → ( ) small/round → { } curly → [ ] square. Solve innermost first, then move outward.

Card 19
Front: In "find the missing sign" questions (e.g., 24 ? 6 ? 2 = 8), which sign should you test first and why?
Back: Test '÷' first, because division creates the cleanest reductions (24 ÷ 6 = 4, 4 × 2 = 8 ✓). Examiners design these so ÷ or × is almost always part of the answer.

Card 20
Front: Why is writing the decoded expression on paper faster than mental substitution, even for toppers?
Back: Mental substitution loads working memory twice (remembering codes + calculating). Writing it down frees the brain for pure calculation, cutting both time and sign-swap errors.
