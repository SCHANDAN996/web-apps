# The Filing Cabinet of Math Files

Imagine you are the manager of a busy office. The files on your desk are the numbers, and the BODMAS rule is the order in which you must clear those files. Just like in an office, you cannot pick up files randomly — there is a fixed sequence, and breaking it creates chaos. In the same way, solving a mathematical expression in the wrong order gives the wrong answer.

The trick is to remember the word **BODMAS** itself:

- **B** — Brackets first (solve whatever is inside brackets)
- **O** — Orders (powers and roots, like squares and square roots)
- **D** — Division
- **M** — Multiplication
- **A** — Addition
- **S** — Subtraction

Think of it as the office hierarchy: the Bracket is the Boss whose work is cleared first, and Subtraction is the last clerk whose turn comes at the end. Division and Multiplication are colleagues of equal rank — when both appear together, solve them left to right. The same holds for Addition and Subtraction.

For example, in 12 + 6 ÷ 3, do not add first. Division outranks Addition, so 6 ÷ 3 = 2, and then 12 + 2 = 14.

Now, close this book and take a blank sheet of paper. Try to write down all the key points of this chapter from memory — the full form of BODMAS, the order of operations, and one worked example. If you can explain it to a friend in your own words, you have truly learned it.
