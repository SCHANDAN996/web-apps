Card 1
Front: How many faces, edges, and vertices does a cube have?
Back: 6 faces, 12 edges, 8 vertices. Remember: F + V − E = 2 (Euler's formula) → 6 + 8 − 12 = 2.

Card 2
Front: In a standard die, what is the sum of numbers on any pair of opposite faces?
Back: Always 7. The pairs are: 1–6, 2–5, 3–4.

Card 3
Front: Two views of a die show the same face in the same position. How do you find the opposite faces?
Back: Write the other faces in clockwise order around the common face in each view; faces at the same position in the two sequences are opposite to each other. The common face is opposite to the face not shown in either view.

Card 4
Front: Two views of a die show one common face. What is the quick rule to find opposites?
Back: Write the faces around the common face in clockwise order for both views; faces at the same position in the two sequences are opposite to each other.

Card 5
Front: What is the mnemonic to remember the opposite pairs of a standard die?
Back: "1-6, 2-5, 3-4 — all make SEVEN." If two faces add to 7, they can never be adjacent (or visible together) on a standard die.

Card 6
Front: A cube is painted on all 6 faces and cut into n³ smaller cubes. How many small cubes have exactly 3 painted faces?
Back: Always 8 — only the corner cubes, regardless of n.

Card 7
Front: In an n×n×n painted cube, what is the formula for cubes with exactly 2 painted faces?
Back: 12(n − 2) — these lie on the 12 edges, excluding corners.

Card 8
Front: In an n×n×n painted cube, how many small cubes have exactly 1 painted face?
Back: 6(n − 2)² — the inner squares of each of the 6 faces.

Card 9
Front: In an n×n×n painted cube, how many small cubes have NO painted face?
Back: (n − 2)³ — the completely inner cubes. Mnemonic: "Unpainted = shrink by 2, then cube it."

Card 10
Front: What is the memory trick for all four painted-cube formulas?
Back: "3-2-1-0 → 8, 12(n−2), 6(n−2)², (n−2)³." Painted faces decrease 3→0; powers of (n−2) increase 0→3; coefficients are 8, 12, 6, 1 (vertices, edges, faces, core).

Card 11
Front: A cube is cut into 64 equal small cubes. How many have no painted face (all faces painted before cutting)?
Back: 64 = 4³, so n = 4. Unpainted = (4 − 2)³ = 8.

Card 12
Front: Examiner's trap: A die shows 1, 2, 3 on three faces meeting at a corner. Can 1 and 6 be adjacent on a standard die?
Back: No. On a standard die 1 and 6 are opposite (sum = 7), so they can never be adjacent or visible together. Options showing them together are traps.

Card 13
Front: Examiner's trap: Two views of a die look identical but are rotated. What mistake do students make?
Back: They treat the two views as different positions and wrongly conclude opposites. Always check whether one view is just a rotation of the other before applying the common-face rule.

Card 14
Front: If only one face is common between two views of a die, how do you find the face opposite to the common face?
Back: You cannot directly. The common-face rule gives opposites of the other faces; the common face is opposite to the one face that appears in neither view.

Card 15
Front: When a sheet is folded into a cube (cube-net questions), which faces become opposite?
Back: In a straight strip of 4 squares, the 1st is opposite the 3rd and the 2nd is opposite the 4th; the two remaining flaps are opposite each other.

Card 16
Front: How many different nets (unfolded patterns) can a cube have?
Back: 11 distinct nets. Any given net must be checked against these valid patterns — a 2×3 rectangle of 6 squares is NOT a valid cube net.

Card 17
Front: A cube is painted red on two opposite faces, green on two opposite faces, and blue on the remaining pair. After cutting into n³ cubes, what is the maximum number of faces any small cube can have painted?
Back: 3 — corner cubes always touch exactly 3 faces, one of each colour.

Card 18
Front: In dice problems, what does "two faces are adjacent" immediately tell you?
Back: They can never be opposite to each other. Collect all adjacency clues; the face left out of a face's four adjacents is its opposite.

Card 19
Front: Quick check: total small cubes after cutting = n³. How do you verify your painted-cube counts?
Back: Sum check: 8 + 12(n−2) + 6(n−2)² + (n−2)³ must equal n³. If the total doesn't match, one of the counts is wrong.

Card 20
Front: In a non-standard die question, what is the very first thing you must check?
Back: Whether opposite faces really sum to 7. If the question says "an ordinary die" it is standard; if views contradict the sum-7 rule, solve only from the given views, never assume 7.
