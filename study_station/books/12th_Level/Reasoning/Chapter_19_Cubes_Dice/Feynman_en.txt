# 🧒 The Feynman Way: Cubes & Dice Explained Like You're 12

## The Story of the Painted Box

Imagine your uncle gives you a big wooden cube for your birthday — a perfect cube, like a giant dice. You decide to paint the whole outside **red**. It looks beautiful!

Now comes the fun part. You take a saw and cut the cube into smaller cubes — 2 cuts along each edge, so each edge is divided into 3 pieces and you get **27 little cubes** (3 × 3 × 3), like a Rubik's Cube pulled apart.

You spread all 27 little cubes on the table and ask: *"How many of these little cubes have red paint on them? And on how many faces?"*

This is exactly the question examiners ask. Let's solve it like a detective.

---

## Think About WHERE Each Little Cube Was Sitting

A little cube's paint depends only on **where it lived** inside the big cube:

**1. The Corner Cubes — 3 painted faces**
A corner cube sticks out in 3 directions, so 3 of its faces got painted. A cube has **8 corners**, so there are always **8 cubes with 3 painted faces**. Always. Whether the big cube is cut into 8, 27, 64 or 1000 pieces — the 8 corners never change.

**2. The Edge Cubes — 2 painted faces**
Cubes sitting on an edge but NOT at the corner show 2 faces. Each edge has (n − 2) such cubes (the two corners are removed), and a cube has 12 edges:
**12 × (n − 2)** cubes with 2 painted faces.

**3. The Face-Centre Cubes — 1 painted face**
Cubes in the middle of a face show only 1 face. Each face has (n − 2)² of them, and there are 6 faces:
**6 × (n − 2)²** cubes with 1 painted face.

**4. The Hidden Core — 0 painted faces**
Deep inside, completely untouched by paint: **(n − 2)³** cubes.

Check with our 3 × 3 × 3 cube (n = 3):
- 3 faces painted: 8
- 2 faces painted: 12 × 1 = 12
- 1 face painted: 6 × 1 = 6
- 0 faces painted: 1
- Total: 8 + 12 + 6 + 1 = **27** ✓ It all adds up!

---

## The Unforgettable Trick: "The Onion Rule" 🧅

Think of the big cube as an onion:

- **Corners = 8** (memorize this — it NEVER changes)
- **Everything else = peel one layer off.** The unpainted core is the inner onion: (n − 2)³. Whatever is left on the outside "skin" is painted.

And here's the golden check: **all four answers must add up to n³**. If your numbers don't total n³, you made a mistake — recalculate!

For dice questions, remember the **Opposite Faces Trick**: on a standard dice, opposite faces add to 7 (1↔6, 2↔5, 3↔4). And if two views of a dice show **one common face in the same position**, the remaining faces are opposite to each other in order.
