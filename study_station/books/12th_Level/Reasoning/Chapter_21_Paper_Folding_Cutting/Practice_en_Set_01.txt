1. A square sheet of paper is folded once along its vertical centre line, and then a single hole is punched through the folded sheet. When the sheet is unfolded, how many holes will appear on the paper?
(a) 4 (b) 1 (c) 3 (d) 2
Answer: (d)
Solution: One fold creates two layers, so a single punch passes through both layers. On unfolding, the hole is mirrored across the fold line, giving 2 holes. The tempting answer 1 ignores the mirror image produced by the fold, so the answer is 2.
Source: PYQ-style

2. A rectangular sheet is folded once horizontally (top half down onto the bottom half) and a circle is cut on the folded sheet near the fold line but not touching it. How many circles will appear when the sheet is opened?
(a) 1 (b) 4 (c) 2 (d) 3
Answer: (c)
Solution: A single fold doubles the paper into two layers, so one cut produces two identical shapes, mirror images of each other across the horizontal fold. The answer 4 would need two folds, so the answer is 2.
Source: PYQ-style

3. A square paper is folded along both diagonals (one after the other) and then one hole is punched. How many holes will the opened sheet show?
(a) 4 (b) 2 (c) 3 (d) 8
Answer: (a)
Solution: Folding along one diagonal gives 2 layers; folding along the second diagonal doubles it to 4 layers. One punch through 4 layers gives 4 holes on unfolding. The answer 8 wrongly assumes each fold multiplies by 4, so the answer is 4.

Solution: The punch away from the folds gives 4 holes (4 layers). The punch on the horizontal fold line mirrors only across the vertical fold, giving 2 holes. Total = 4 + 2 = 6. The trap is treating both punches alike and answering 8, so the answer is 6.

25. A sheet is folded in half vertically, then in half horizontally. Three holes are punched on the folded sheet: one at the meeting point of the two folds, one on the vertical fold line only, and one away from all fold lines. How many holes appear on the opened sheet?
(a) 12 (b) 6 (c) 9 (d) 7
Answer: (d)
Solution: The punch at the meeting point of both folds gives 1 hole (it lies on both folds). The punch on the vertical fold line gives 2 holes (mirrored only across the horizontal fold). The punch away from the folds gives 4 holes. Total = 1 + 2 + 4 = 7. The trap is multiplying all three punches by 4 to get 12, ignoring that punches on fold lines merge, so the answer is 7.
