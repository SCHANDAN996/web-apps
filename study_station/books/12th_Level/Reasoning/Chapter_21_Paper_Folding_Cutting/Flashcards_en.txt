Card 1
Front: When a paper is folded twice and then punched, how do you find the total number of holes after unfolding?
Back: Multiply the number of punches by 2 for each fold. Two folds → each punch makes 4 holes (1 × 2 × 2), provided no punch lies on a fold line.

Card 2
Front: What is the golden rule for unfolding a folded-and-punched paper?
Back: Unfold in the exact reverse order of folding — last fold opens first. Each unfolding mirrors the holes across that fold line.

Card 3
Front: What does a hole punched exactly on a fold line produce after unfolding?
Back: It produces only one hole (not two) across that fold, because the punch lies on the mirror line itself — it has no separate mirror image.

Card 4
Front: What is the "Mirror Trick" mnemonic for paper folding questions?
Back: "Fold = Mirror." Every fold line acts as a mirror: whatever is punched on one side appears as a reflection on the other side when unfolded.

Card 5
Front: What is the "LIFO" memory trick for solving folding problems?
Back: "Last In, First Out" — the last fold made is the first fold you open. Reverse the folding sequence step by step, mirroring holes at each step.

Card 6
Front: If a paper is folded along a diagonal, where do the mirror images of punches appear?
Back: They appear reflected across the diagonal line — a punch near one corner maps to the corresponding position near the opposite corner across the diagonal.

Card 7
Front: Examiner's trap: A paper is folded and a corner is cut off. What mistake do most students make?
Back: They count the cut only once. A cut at a folded corner cuts through ALL layers at that point, so after unfolding, the cut appears at every corresponding corner/edge position.

Card 8
Front: How many layers exist at the centre of a paper folded in half three times?
Back: 2³ = 8 layers. A punch through the centre creates 8 holes after complete unfolding.

Card 9
Front: In paper cutting questions, what does a cut made along a folded edge become after unfolding?
Back: It becomes a symmetric shape (hole or slit) in the interior of the paper — the fold line becomes the axis of symmetry of that shape.

Card 10
Front: Examiner's trap: Two punches are made, one on a fold line and one away from it, with one fold. How many holes appear?
Back: Three holes — the off-fold punch gives 2 (itself + mirror image), but the on-fold punch gives only 1. Students often wrongly answer 4.

Card 11
Front: What is the first thing you should check in the answer figures of a folding question?
Back: Symmetry. The final unfolded figure must be symmetric about every fold line. Eliminate any option lacking this symmetry immediately.

Card 12
Front: If a square paper is folded into a smaller square using two folds (half, then half again), where do the four layers stack?
Back: All four layers stack exactly over one quarter of the original square — a punch there produces 4 holes, one in each quadrant, mirror-symmetric.

Card 13
Front: What is the "Count the Layers" trick for quick answers?
Back: Before checking options, count layers at the punch point: holes after unfolding = punches × layers at that point (minus any on-fold punches). This alone eliminates most wrong options.

Card 14
Front: In a folding sequence, does the direction of folding (up vs. down) change the final hole positions?
Back: No — only the position of the fold line matters for hole locations. Direction affects which layer is on top, not the symmetry of holes.

Card 15
Front: A paper is folded once vertically and once horizontally, then punched once at the folded corner. How many holes and where?
Back: Four holes, one in each quadrant, symmetric about both the vertical and horizontal centre lines.

Card 16
Front: What is the key difference between a "punch" question and a "cut" question?
Back: A punch creates identical point-holes mirrored across folds; a cut creates a shape/edge whose mirror image joins across the fold line to form a complete symmetric figure.

Card 17
Front: Examiner's trap: In the folding sequence, one fold is shown but the arrow indicates only a partial (half-width) fold. What should you watch for?
Back: The fold covers only part of the paper — punches through the folded region mirror only within that region's fold line, not across the whole sheet. Always check the fold's extent.

Card 18
Front: What is the fastest elimination strategy in paper folding MCQs?
Back: Check three things in order: (1) total hole count, (2) symmetry about each fold line, (3) holes on fold lines. Most options fail at step 1 or 2.

Card 19
Front: If a circular paper is folded into a semicircle and then a quarter-circle, how many holes does one punch make?
Back: Four holes (2 folds → 2² = 4), arranged symmetrically about both fold diameters of the circle.

Card 20
Front: Why should you never solve a folding question by imagining the final figure directly?
Back: Because the brain easily misplaces mirror images. Always unfold step by step in reverse order — one fold at a time — to keep every reflection accurate.
