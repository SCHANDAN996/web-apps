126. A square sheet is folded along one diagonal, and then the resulting triangle is folded along the other diagonal's direction (its axis of symmetry). A single circular hole is punched through all layers at a point not on any fold line. When unfolded, how many holes appear on the sheet?
(a) 8 (b) 2 (c) 4 (d) 6
Answer: (c)
Solution: The tempting answer is 8, obtained by doubling one time too many. There are only two folds, so the paper has 2 × 2 = 4 layers, and a punch away from the creases makes one hole per layer, so the answer is 4.
Source: PYQ-style

127. A rectangular sheet is folded in half along its length, then folded in half along its width, and a triangular cut is made at the corner where the two fold edges meet. On unfolding, the cut produces:
(a) 4 separate triangles (b) 4 diamond-shaped holes (c) 2 triangles (d) 1 diamond-shaped hole at the centre
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is choosing 4 separate triangles by counting layers only. The cut is made exactly where both fold lines meet, which is the centre of the original sheet, and reflections of the triangle across the two perpendicular folds join into a single diamond-shaped hole, so the answer is 1 diamond-shaped hole at the centre.
Source: PYQ-style

128. A square paper is folded along the vertical centre line, then along the horizontal centre line. A semicircular cut is made on the edge formed by the vertical fold only (not at the corner). After unfolding, how many circular holes appear?
(a) 2 (b) 1 (c) 8 (d) 4
Answer: (a)
Solution: The tempting answer is 4, assuming every cut multiplies by all folds. The semicircle lies on the vertical fold edge, so unfolding across that fold completes it into one full circle; that circle is off the horizontal fold, which mirrors it once, so the answer is 2.
Source: PYQ-style

129. A sheet is folded into quarters (half, then half again). A hole is punched exactly at the intersection point of the two fold lines (the folded corner). On unfolding, the number of holes is:
(a) 1 (b) 4 (c) 2 (d) 3
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is answering 4 by simply multiplying layers. A punch exactly on a fold line is shared by the layers on both sides of that fold, and being on both fold lines all four layer-positions coincide at one point on the opened sheet, so the answer is 1.

Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is thinking a cut near the fold merges into one slit. Since the cut is parallel to but away from the fold line, it does not touch the crease, and unfolding mirrors it across the diagonal, so the answer is two separate slits symmetric about the diagonal.
