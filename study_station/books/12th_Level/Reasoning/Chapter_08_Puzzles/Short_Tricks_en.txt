# 🪄 Short Tricks & Mnemonics — Puzzles (Intermediate)

---

## 🧠 Mnemonic Box 1 — The Golden First Step
**"FIX before you MIX"**
Before arranging anything, FIX the one person/object whose position is directly stated ("A sits at the extreme left"). Never start with a negative clue ("B is not next to C"). Fixed points first, relatives later.

---

## 🧠 Mnemonic Box 2 — Circular Seating Direction
**"FACE the centre? LEFT is your RIGHT hand's enemy!"**
In a circle facing the centre: a person's LEFT is on YOUR right side of the diagram. Chant: **"Facing IN — flip to win."** Facing outward? No flip. Draw tiny arrows on every head before placing anyone.

---

## 🧠 Mnemonic Box 3 — Blood Relation Fast-Decode
**"Sister's SON = Nephew, Brother's SON = Nephew — SIBLING's child is NEP/NIECE!"**
Any sibling's child = nephew/niece. Parent's sibling = uncle/aunt. No need to draw the whole tree — jump two generations with this one line.

---

## 🧠 Mnemonic Box 4 — Gender Symbols
**"PLUS she, MINUS he"**
Mark every person in a blood-relation puzzle with **(+)** for female and **(−)** for male the moment gender is revealed. Half of all trap options die instantly when you track gender.

---

## 🧠 Mnemonic Box 5 — "Only" Clues
**"ONLY = LOCK the door"**
"Only B sits between A and C" means EXACTLY one person — lock it. "B sits between A and C" (no "only") means AT LEAST one — keep the door open. The word "only" is a padlock; missing it is the examiner's favourite trap.

---

## 🧠 Mnemonic Box 6 — Floor/Stack Puzzles
**"Bottom-Up BOMBER"**
In floor puzzles, number floors 1 (ground) to N (top) and build from the most restricted floor upward. Never assume floor 1 is at the top of your page — draw it like a real building, ground at the bottom.

---

## 🧠 Mnemonic Box 7 — Ranking/Ordering
**"TALL-SMALL, draw the WALL"**
For height/rank/age puzzles, draw a vertical wall (line). Put tallest/best on top. Convert every clue into an inequality immediately: "A is taller than B but shorter than C" → **C > A > B**. Symbols beat sentences.

---

## 🧠 Mnemonic Box 8 — The "Who/Which" Table
**"GRID it or GRIND it"**
For 3-attribute puzzles (person–city–colour), make a grid table instantly. Tick (✓) confirmed, cross (✗) eliminated. One ✓ in a row crosses out the whole row and column — **"One tick, six kicks."**

---

## 🧠 Mnemonic Box 9 — Immediate Neighbours
**"NEXT TO = GLUE; BETWEEN = SANDWICH"**
"Next to" = glued pair, order unknown (AB or BA). "Between" = sandwich, middle fixed, ends swappable (ABC or CBA). Write glued pairs in brackets: **[AB]** — then treat the bracket as ONE block.

---

## 🧠 Mnemonic Box 10 — Negative Clue Parking
**"NOT now, NOTE now"**
Negative clues ("D is not at any end") can't be placed yet — park them in a "NOT list" at the side of your diagram. Revisit after every fixed placement. Most puzzles crack when the NOT list finally bites.

---

## 🧠 Mnemonic Box 11 — Two Cases Rule
**"Two roads? Drive BOTH, one will crash."**
When a clue allows two arrangements, draw both mini-diagrams. Keep solving both in parallel — one will contradict a later clue and crash. Never guess one road and hope.

---

## 🧠 Mnemonic Box 12 — Day/Month Scheduling
**"MONTH before MONDAY"**
In scheduling puzzles, fix the calendar skeleton first (dates/days in a column), then pin the "exactly between" and "immediately before/after" clues. "Immediately" = adjacent, no gap — **"IMME = Siamese twins."**

---

## 🧠 Mnemonic Box 13 — Box/Stack Puzzles
**"GAP = GAP"**
"Two boxes between P and Q" means |positions differ by 3|, not 2. Count the GAPS, not the boxes. Say it aloud: "Two between = three apart." This single counting error kills more answers than any other.

---

## 🧠 Mnemonic Box 14 — Final Verification Sweep
**"READ-BACK before you ANSWER-back"**
After solving, re-read every clue once and check your final diagram against each — 20 seconds. Examiners design one clue to be violated by the "obvious" arrangement. The read-back catches it.

---

## ⏭️ Skip Strategy — When to Skip a Puzzle Question

1. **The 60-Second Skeleton Rule:** If after 60 seconds you haven't drawn a clear diagram/table with at least 2 fixed placements, SKIP and mark for review. Intermediate puzzles are designed to look dense; a blank skeleton after a minute means the clue chain hasn't clicked — come back with fresh eyes.

2. **The "Two-Case Explosion" Skip:** If a seating/scheduling puzzle forces you into 3+ parallel cases (not the normal 2), the puzzle is a time-trap worth 1 mark but costing 4+ minutes. Skip it in round one; attempt only if time remains after easier sets.

3. **The "All-Clues-Negative" Skip:** If a puzzle's clues are almost all negative ("X is not…", "Y does not…") with no direct fixed position, it requires full trial-and-error. In exams with negative marking, skip rather than guess — a puzzle you can't anchor is a lottery ticket, not a question.
