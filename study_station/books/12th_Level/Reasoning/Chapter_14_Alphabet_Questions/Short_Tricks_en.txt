# 🪄 Short Tricks & Mnemonics — Alphabet Questions

---

**BOX 1 — "EJOTY: The Five Soldiers"**
Remember the positions 5, 10, 15, 20, 25 as five soldiers standing guard:
**E**lephant **J**umps **O**ver **T**all **Y**aks → E=5, J=10, O=15, T=20, Y=25.
Any letter's position = nearest soldier ± steps. Example: R? T is 20, go back 2 → R=18.

---

**BOX 2 — "CFILORUX: The Odd Squad"**
Letters whose positions are multiples of 3: C(3), F(6), I(9), L(12), O(15), R(18), U(21), X(24).
Chant: "**C**lever **F**oxes **I**nvite **L**azy **O**wls **R**ound **U**gly **X**mas."

---

**BOX 3 — "AZ BY CX: The Mirror Pairs"**
Opposite letters (sum = 27): A–Z, B–Y, C–X, D–W, E–V, F–U, G–T, H–S, I–R, J–Q, K–P, L–O, M–N.
Memory hook: "**AZad BY CX**" — a freedom fighter named Azad who came "by CX" train. Then just continue the chain.

---

**BOX 4 — "27 is the Magic Mirror"**
Opposite letter position = 27 − given position.
Trick: 27 = 3 × 9. "The mirror lives on the 27th floor — always subtract to reach it."

---

**BOX 5 — "Vowel VIPs: A E I O U = 1 5 9 15 21"**
Hook: "**A**ll **E**lephants **I**n **O**range **U**mbrellas" — positions 1, 5, 9, 15, 21.
Notice: after 9, jump by 6 (9→15→21). "Vowels march in sixes after I."

---

**BOX 6 — "Backward Counting? Flip It!"**
"13th from the right" = (27 − 13) = 14th from the left = N.
Mantra: "**Right is a lie — flip it to Left with 27.**"
Never count backwards from Z; convert and use EJOTY.

---

**BOX 7 — "Middle Letter: Add & Halve"**
Middle of two positions = (a + b) ÷ 2.
Hook: "**Two friends share a pizza — add their plates, cut in half.**"
If (a+b) is odd → no single middle letter; the question may ask for "letters between" instead.

---

**BOX 8 — "Between = Subtract, Minus One"**
Number of letters between positions a and b = |a − b| − 1.
Hook: "**Between two walls, the walls themselves don't count — remove 1.**"
Classic trap: students forget the −1 and pick |a−b|.

---

**BOX 9 — "Word-Formation: Vowel Hunt First"**
Before trying to form words, circle the vowels in the given letters.
Hook: "**No vowel, no word — hunt the vowels first, consonants follow.**"
If only one vowel exists, the answer is usually 0 or 1 word — don't waste time.

---

**BOX 10 — "Dictionary Order: First Letter Wins"**
Arranging words alphabetically? Compare only the first differing letter.
Hook: "**The referee stops the match at the first punch — first different letter decides.**"
Don't read whole words; scan letter by letter left to right.

---

**BOX 11 — "Pair-Counting (Forward = Backward)"**
"How many letter pairs in a word have as many letters between them as in the alphabet?"
Check both directions — forward AND backward.
Hook: "**Pairs walk both ways on the alphabet street — check the road in both directions.**"
Most students count only forward and lose the mark.

---

**BOX 12 — "Position Swap: Same Sum, New Letter"**
If letters interchange positions, track only the asked position.
Hook: "**Don't rearrange the whole parade — just follow the one soldier asked about.**"

---

**BOX 13 — "A=26 Shortcut for Reverse Series"**
In reverse alphabet (Z=1 … A=26), letter X's reverse position = 27 − normal position.
Hook: "**The alphabet in a mirror — same 27 mirror, new wardrobe.**"

---

**BOX 14 — "Letter Series: Write the Gap Numbers"**
For series like A, C, F, J, ? — write gaps above: +2, +3, +4 → next +5 → O.
Hook: "**Don't stare at letters, stare at the jumps. The jumps tell the story.**"

---

## ⏭️ Skip Strategy — When to Leave the Question

**Tip 1 — The 15-Second Pattern Rule:**
In letter-series or coding-type alphabet questions, if the gap pattern (+2, +3, … or ×2, −1, …) is not visible within 15 seconds, mark it and move on. Return only if time remains — patterns often "click" on a second look with a fresh mind.

**Tip 2 — Skip Heavy Pair-Counting on Long Words:**
If a word has 10+ letters and asks for alphabet-matching pairs in both directions, the checking time often exceeds the mark's value. Attempt it only if you finish the section early; a blind guess here is fine since there's no partial credit.

**Tip 3 — Skip "Meaningful Word" Questions with Rare Letters:**
If the given letters contain awkward letters (Q, X, Z, K, J) and no common vowel combination, the answer is very likely "no word" or "one word." Don't burn 60 seconds rearranging — make a quick 10-second check, and if nothing obvious forms, mark and move on.
