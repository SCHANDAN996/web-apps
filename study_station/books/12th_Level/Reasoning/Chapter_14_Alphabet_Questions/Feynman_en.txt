# 🧒 The Feynman Way: Alphabet Questions Explained Like You're 12

## The Hardest Concept: "Which letter is the Nth from the left/right of another letter?"

Most students don't fail alphabet questions because they're hard. They fail because of one sneaky sentence:

> *"Which letter is 5th to the **right** of the 8th letter from the **left**?"*

Your brain reads "right" and "left" in the same line and short-circuits. Let me fix that forever with a story.

---

## 🚌 The Story of the School Bus Line

Imagine 26 children standing in a line for the school bus. Child **A** is first, child **Z** is last. You are the bus monitor with a clipboard.

Now, here's the golden rule of any line in the world:

- **"From the left"** means: start counting from A (position 1, 2, 3…).
- **"To the right of"** means: move **forward** in the line → **ADD**.
- **"To the left of"** means: move **backward** in the line → **SUBTRACT**.

That's it. The whole chapter is just **walking in a line**.

**Try it:** "5th to the right of the 8th letter from the left."

1. Find the 8th letter from the left → that's **H** (A=1, B=2 … H=8).
2. Now walk 5 steps to the right (forward): 8 + 5 = 13.
3. The 13th letter is **M**. Done. ✅

**The trap version:** "5th to the **left** of the 8th letter from the **right**."

1. 8th from the right = 27 − 8 = **19th from the left** → that's **S**.
2. Walk 5 steps left (backward): 19 − 5 = 14 → **N**. ✅

Notice the magic number **27**? Since there are 26 letters, *any* position from the right converts to the left by: **27 − (right position)**. The 1st from the right (Z) is 27 − 1 = 26th from the left. It always works.

---

## 🎯 The Unforgettable Trick: "RIGHT is PLUS, LEFT is MINUS, and 27 is the Bridge"

Say it out loud three times:

> **"Right = Add, Left = Subtract, and 27 flips the side."**

The method has exactly two steps, always in this order:

1. **Flip first:** If the reference position is given "from the right", convert it to "from the left" using **27 − (right position)**. Now everything is measured from one side only.
2. **Then walk:** Apply the direction word to the converted position — **"right of" = add**, **"left of" = subtract**.

That's the whole trick. Don't try to add or subtract based on whether the two direction words "match" — just flip to the left side first, then walk. Example: "left of the 8th from the right" → flip (27 − 8 = 19), then walk left (19 − 5 = 14). The direction word alone tells you whether to add or subtract.

And for "middle letter between two letters" questions: add their positions, divide by 2. Between D (4) and J (10)? (4+10)/2 = 7 → **G**. If you get a ".5", there is no single middle letter — that's the examiner's trap!
