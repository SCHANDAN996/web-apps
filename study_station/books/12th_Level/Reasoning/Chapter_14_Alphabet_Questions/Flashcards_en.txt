Card 1
Front: What is the quickest way to recall the position of any letter in the English alphabet?
Back: Use the EJOTY mnemonic: E=5, J=10, O=15, T=20, Y=25. From the nearest anchor, count forward or backward. Example: R = 20 (T) − 2 = 18.

Card 2
Front: Which five letters form the EJOTY anchor points, and what are their positions?
Back: E (5), J (10), O (15), T (20), Y (25). Each is a multiple of 5, so any other letter is at most 2 steps away from an anchor.

Card 3
Front: What is the reverse (from-the-end) position formula for a letter?
Back: Reverse position = 27 − normal position. Example: D is 4th from the start, so 27 − 4 = 23rd from the end.

Card 4
Front: Which trick helps you instantly recall reverse positions of letters?
Back: Pair letters that sum to 27: A–Z, B–Y, C–X, D–W, E–V, F–U, G–T, H–S, I–R, J–Q, K–P, L–O, M–N. Learn these 13 opposite pairs and reverse positions become instant.

Card 5
Front: If a question asks "which letter is 7th to the right of the 10th letter from the left," what is the shortcut?
Back: Same side → add: 10 + 7 = 17th from the left = Q. Right of a left-position means moving toward Z, so positions add.

Card 6
Front: If a question asks "which letter is 5th to the left of the 9th letter from the right," how do you combine the directions?
Back: Opposite sides → add, then convert: 9 + 5 = 14th from the right; from the left = 27 − 14 = 13th = M.

Card 7
Front: Examiner's trap: "7th to the right of K" — do you count K itself?
Back: No. "To the right of K" excludes K; you start counting from L. K (11) + 7 = 18 = R. Counting K itself is the most common error in such questions.

Card 8
Front: Examiner's trap: In "How many letters are there between D and J?", should D and J be counted?
Back: No — "between" excludes both endpoints. Between D (4) and J (10): 10 − 4 − 1 = 5 letters (E, F, G, H, I). Including endpoints is the classic trap.

Card 9
Front: How many letters of the alphabet remain at the same position when the alphabet is written in reverse order?
Back: None. A letter stays fixed only if its position equals 27 − position, i.e., position 13.5, which does not exist. So the answer is zero letters.

Card 10
Front: In "meaningful word" questions (e.g., using letters of EXAMINATION), what are you usually asked to do?
Back: Pick the specified letters (e.g., 1st, 4th, 7th) and check if they can be rearranged into a real English word. If more than one word is possible, choose the "more than one" option.

Card 11
Front: How do you quickly find the middle letter between two letters, say C and K?
Back: Average their positions: (3 + 11) / 2 = 7 = G. If the average is not a whole number, there are two middle letters.

Card 12
Front: What is the trick for "letter pairs" questions like "How many pairs of letters in the word 'TEACHER' have as many letters between them as in the alphabet?"
Back: Write positions above each letter, then check each pair: the gap in the word must equal the gap in the alphabet sequence. Check both forward and backward directions — examiners count both.

Card 13
Front: In alphabet series like A, C, F, J, O, ? — what pattern should you test first?
Back: Check the gaps between consecutive terms: +2, +3, +4, +5 — so next gap is +6: O (15) + 6 = 21 = U. Increasing-gap patterns are the most common series type.

Card 14
Front: What is the trick for decreasing series like Z, X, V, T, ?
Back: Convert to positions: 26, 24, 22, 20 — a gap of −2 each time, so next is 18 = R. Always convert letters to numbers first; patterns become obvious.

Card 15
Front: In a two-letter series like AB, DE, GH, JK, ? — what is the pattern?
Back: Each pair has consecutive letters, and pairs skip one letter between them (skip C, F, I). Next pair after JK (skip L) = MN.

Card 16
Front: How do you solve "If the first half of the alphabet is reversed, which letter is 8th from the left?"
Back: First half A–M reversed becomes M–A, occupying positions 1–13. 8th from left = 8th letter of the reversed half: M(1), L(2), K(3), J(4), I(5), H(6), G(7), F(8) = F.

Card 17
Front: What is the position-sum trick to check if two letters are "opposite" (equidistant from the ends)?
Back: Their positions must add to 27. Example: H (8) and S (19): 8 + 19 = 27, so they are an opposite pair.

Card 18
Front: Examiner's trap: In dictionary-order questions with words like APPLE, APPLY, APPROACH — what decides the order?
Back: Compare letter by letter from the left; the first differing letter decides. APPLE vs APPLY: 5th letter E vs L → APPLE comes first. Don't stop at common prefixes — go to the first point of difference.

Card 19
Front: How many vowels and consonants are there in the English alphabet, and why does it matter in exams?
Back: 5 vowels (A, E, I, O, U) and 21 consonants. Many questions ask for "the letter midway between two vowels" or series built only on vowels/consonants, so instant recall saves time.

Card 20
Front: What is the 15-second rule for alphabet position questions in the exam?
Back: If you can anchor with EJOTY and answer within 15 seconds, attempt it; if the question involves multiple reversals or long words, mark it and return later — alphabet questions are scoring only when done fast and without recounting.
