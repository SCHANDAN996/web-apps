Card 1
Front: What is the very first thing you must check before judging any course of action?
Back: Whether the course of action logically follows from the statement. The action must directly address the problem given in the statement — not a bigger or different problem.

Card 2
Front: A course of action says "The government should resign." Should it follow?
Back: No. Extreme, drastic or politically charged actions (resignation, war, banning everything) never follow. Only practical, legal and balanced actions follow.

Card 3
Front: What is the "Problem–Solution Match" test?
Back: Ask: does this action solve or reduce the exact problem in the statement? If the action targets a different issue, it does not follow — even if it sounds noble.

Card 4
Front: Which mnemonic reminds you of the three qualities every valid course of action must have?
Back: "P-P-L" — Practical, Positive, Legal. If an action fails any one of these three, reject it.

Card 5
Front: A course of action merely repeats the statement in different words. Does it follow?
Back: No. Restating the problem is not a solution. A valid action must add a concrete step, not paraphrase the statement.

Card 6
Front: Examiner's trap: Statement says "Many accidents occur at the railway crossing." Action: "Trains should be stopped permanently." Why is this wrong?
Back: It is an extreme action. The correct type of action would be practical — e.g., building an overbridge or posting a gateman. Examiners pair a real problem with an absurdly drastic "solution" to tempt you.

Card 7
Front: If a course of action is based on an assumption not supported by the statement, does it follow?
Back: No. Actions resting on unverified assumptions ("People are careless, so…") do not follow. The action must be grounded in facts given in the statement.

Card 8
Front: What does the answer option "if neither I nor II follows" usually signal in exams?
Back: That at least one action is a trap — often extreme, negative, or unrelated. Check each action independently before combining; never assume both must stand or fall together.

Card 9
Front: A course of action suggests "an enquiry committee should be set up." When does this follow?
Back: When the statement reports an incident whose causes or facts are unclear (accidents, scams, complaints). Enquiry/investigation is a valid first step when facts need establishing.

Card 10
Front: Which mnemonic helps you decide between "only I follows" and "both follow"?
Back: "One-by-One" rule: judge each action separately with P-P-L (Practical, Positive, Legal). Never let a strong Action I make you automatically accept a weak Action II.

Card 11
Front: Should a course of action that punishes without verification follow?
Back: No. "The clerk should be dismissed immediately" does not follow if the statement only reports a complaint. Due process (enquiry first, action later) is the logical order.

Card 12
Front: Examiner's trap: The statement is about a one-time local incident, and the action proposes a nationwide permanent law. What's wrong?
Back: Disproportionate response. The action must match the scale of the problem. Examiners inflate the solution's scope to test whether you notice the mismatch.

Card 13
Front: Do actions suggesting awareness campaigns, training, or counselling usually follow?
Back: Yes — when the problem stems from ignorance, negligence or lack of skill (e.g., low vaccination turnout, poor performance). These are positive, practical, non-extreme steps.

Card 14
Front: A course of action begins with "The government should provide free everything to…" — likely valid or invalid?
Back: Invalid. "Free everything / unlimited funds" actions are impractical and financially unrealistic. Valid actions are feasible within normal administrative means.

Card 15
Front: What is the correct approach when both actions seem reasonable but contradict each other?
Back: Choose "either I or II follows" only when the two actions are mutually exclusive solutions to the same problem and both are individually valid. Otherwise, both may follow together.

Card 16
Front: Does a course of action follow if it solves the problem but violates law or rights?
Back: No. Legality is non-negotiable (the L in P-P-L). "Evict all protesters by force overnight" fails even if it would clear the road.

Card 17
Front: In how many seconds should an average Statement & Course of Action question be solved, and how?
Back: About 30–40 seconds: 10 seconds to identify the core problem in the statement, then 10–15 seconds per action applying the P-P-L filter. Don't re-read the statement repeatedly.

Card 18
Front: Why do "should be encouraged / should be motivated" actions often follow in performance-related statements?
Back: Because they are positive, non-coercive steps that address low morale or poor results without punishing anyone — fitting all three P-P-L criteria.

Card 19
Front: Statement reports a problem; Action I says "the matter should be looked into." Does it follow?
Back: Yes, generally. "Look into / investigate / review the matter" is a mild, practical first response and follows whenever responsibility or cause is not yet established.

Card 20
Front: What single question should you ask yourself before marking any action as "follows"?
Back: "If I were the authority, would a sensible, law-abiding administrator actually do this for THIS problem?" If yes — it follows; if it sounds extreme, vague or off-target — it doesn't.
