# 📊 PYQ Analysis — Statement & Course of Action

## 1. How Exams Use This Chapter

Statement & Course of Action is a regular feature of **Banking exams (IBPS PO/Clerk, SBI PO, RBI Assistant)** and appears in **SSC CGL/CHSL** and **Railway (RRB NTPC)** in the reasoning/GA-reasoning mix. Banking exams ask it most consistently, usually in the mains reasoning section and sometimes in prelims.

**What the pattern looks like (in words, not numbers):**

- A short **statement** describes a problem, event, or situation (a civic issue, a government failure, a business problem, a social trend).
- It is followed by **two or three courses of action** numbered I, II (and III).
- You must decide which course(s) **logically follow** — i.e., which are practical, problem-solving, and directly connected to the statement.
- Standard answer options: "Only I follows", "Only II follows", "Both I and II follow", "Neither I nor II follows", "Either I or II follows".

## 2. Sub-topics That Repeat Most Often

1. **Civic/administrative problems** — water shortage, traffic jams, power cuts, overflowing drains. Correct action: a concrete administrative step (repair, regulation, awareness drive). Wrong action: extreme or unrelated steps.
2. **Crime and law & order** — thefts rising, chain-snatching. Correct: increased patrolling, investigation. Wrong: "all suspects should be jailed" (violates due process).
3. **Health and epidemics** — disease outbreak. Correct: medical camps, vaccination, sanitation. Wrong: panic-driven or impossible steps.
4. **Education and institutions** — falling results, dropouts. Correct: teacher training, infrastructure review. Wrong: closing the institution.
5. **Business/organisational decisions** — company losses, employee unrest. Correct: analysis, negotiation, restructuring. Wrong: sacking everyone.
6. **Government policy statements** — a scheme failing. Correct: review, correct implementation gaps. Wrong: scrap everything instantly.

## 3. Exam-wise Tendencies

- **Banking (IBPS/SBI):** Longest statements, three courses of action, subtle traps — one action looks emotional but is actually practical. This is where the chapter is hardest.
- **SSC (CGL/CHSL):** Shorter statements, usually two courses of action; one is clearly extreme. Easier, faster.
- **Railway (RRB NTPC):** Straightforward; the correct action is usually the moderate, administrative one.

## 4. The Examiner's Traps (Psychological Hooks)

1. **The "extreme action" trap** — "The government should ban all private vehicles." Sounds decisive; it is impractical. Reject.
2. **The "revenge/punishment" trap** — "The negligent officials should be dismissed immediately." Punishment without inquiry is not a logical course of action.
3. **The "too vague" trap** — "Something should be done about the problem." Not a course of action at all.
4. **The "unrelated good deed" trap** — Statement about water shortage; action about building a stadium. Good-sounding but unconnected.
5. **The "repeats the problem" trap** — Action merely restates that the problem is serious. No solution = does not follow.
6. **The "either–or" confusion** — Two mutually exclusive actions (e.g., "shift the factory" vs "keep the factory and install filters"). Students mark "both"; the answer is "either I or II".
7. **The "long-term vs immediate" trap** — Statement needs an immediate step (disease outbreak); action proposes a 10-year research project. Too slow = does not follow alone.

## 5. Top 10 High-Yield Questions (PYQ-style, with Solutions)

**Q1.** Statement: Many people in the city are falling ill due to contaminated drinking water supplied by the municipal corporation.
Courses of Action:
I. The municipal corporation should immediately repair and clean the water pipelines.
II. Residents should be advised to boil water before drinking until the supply is safe.
(a) Only I follows (b) Only II follows (c) Both follow (d) Neither follows
**Answer: (c)**
**Solution:** I is the permanent administrative fix; II is the immediate protective step. Both are practical and directly address the problem. **Trap:** Students think "advising people" is too soft — but interim safety measures are valid courses of action.

**Q2.** Statement: Cases of chain-snatching have increased sharply in the city over the last few months.
Courses of Action:
I. Police patrolling in crowded areas should be increased.
II. All persons with a past criminal record should be arrested immediately.
(a) Only I follows (b) Only II follows (c) Both follow (d) Neither follows
**Answer: (a)**
**Solution:** Increased patrolling is a direct, lawful deterrent. Arresting people without cause violates due process — the classic **punishment trap**.

**Q3.** Statement: A large number of students failed in the Class X board examination from government schools this year.
Courses of Action:
I. The government should close down the poorly performing schools.
II. The government should review teaching quality and provide remedial classes.
(a) Only I follows (b) Only II follows (c) Both follow (d) Neither follows
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** Closing schools punishes students, not the problem — **extreme action trap**. Review and remedial teaching directly attack the cause.

**Q4.** Statement: The company has been incurring heavy losses for the last three quarters.
Courses of Action:
I. The management should analyse the reasons for the losses.
II. All employees should be dismissed.
(a) Only I follows (b) Only II follows (c) Both follow (d) Neither follows
**Answer: (a)**
**Solution:** Diagnosis must precede action; mass dismissal is extreme and doesn't address the cause. **Trap:** "Dismissal" feels like a strong business decision — it is not logical.

**Q5.** Statement: Heavy waterlogging occurs on the main road every monsoon, disrupting traffic.
Courses of Action:
I. The drainage system along the road should be desilted and upgraded before the monsoon.
II. The road should be closed permanently during the rainy season.
(a) Only I follows (b) Only II follows (c) Both follow (d) Neither follows
**Answer: (a)**
**Solution:** Fixing drainage solves the root cause. Permanent closure is impractical — **extreme action trap**.

**Q6.** Statement: Despite a government scheme, many farmers in the district have not received crop insurance payouts.
Courses of Action:
I. The implementation of the scheme should be reviewed and pending claims settled.
II. The scheme should be scrapped immediately.
(a) Only I follows (b) Only II follows (c) Both follow (d) Neither follows
**Answer: (a)**
**Solution:** The problem is implementation, not the scheme itself. Scrapping is the **extreme trap**; review-and-settle is the logical fix.

**Q7.** Statement: There is a severe shortage of teachers in rural schools of the state.
Courses of Action:
I. The government should recruit and post more teachers to rural schools.
II. Rural schools should be merged with urban schools.
(a) Only I follows (b) Only II follows (c) Both follow (d) Neither follows
**Answer: (a)**
**Solution:** Recruitment directly fills the gap. Merging is impractical (distance, access) and doesn't solve the shortage.

**Q8.** Statement: An epidemic of dengue has broken out in the city.
Courses of Action:
I. Fogging and anti-larval drives should be carried out immediately.
II. A long-term research project on mosquito behaviour should be launched.
(a) Only I follows (b) Only II follows (c) Both follow (d) Neither follows
**Answer: (a)**
**Solution:** An epidemic demands an **immediate** response; a long-term research project does not address the current crisis — the **long-term vs immediate trap**. (Note: if the statement were about *preventing future outbreaks*, II could follow.)

**Q9.** Statement: The factory's effluents are polluting the nearby river, affecting villagers' health.
Courses of Action:
I. The factory should be shifted far away from the river.
II. The factory should be directed to install effluent treatment plants.
(a) Only I follows (b) Only II follows (c) Either I or II follows (d) Neither follows
**Answer: (c)**
**Solution:** Both actions solve the pollution problem but are **mutually exclusive** — if the factory moves, treatment at the river site is moot; if it treats effluents, moving is unnecessary. Hence "either–or". **Trap:** Marking "both" because both sound good.

**Q10.** Statement: Road accidents at a particular crossing have risen because drivers jump the red light.
Courses of Action:
I. Traffic police should be deployed at the crossing and violators fined.
II. CCTV cameras should be installed to monitor the crossing.
(a) Only I follows (b) Only II follows (c) Both follow (d) Neither follows
**Answer: (c)**
**Solution:** Enforcement (I) and surveillance (II) are complementary, not exclusive — both reduce violations. **Trap:** Treating this like Q9; ask "can both happen together?" — yes here, no there.

## 6. The 15-Second / 45-Second Rule — Reviewed Against PYQ Patterns

- **15-second questions (attempt instantly):** SSC/Railway-style short statements where one action is clearly extreme ("ban", "close down", "arrest all", "scrap"). Q2, Q3, Q5, Q6 above are 15-second questions — the extreme option is visible at a glance.
- **45-second questions (worth the time):** Banking-style three-action statements and **either–or** cases (Q9). These need you to test mutual exclusivity — budget up to 45 seconds.
- **Skip candidates:**
  - **Q9-type either–or questions under time pressure in prelims:** if you cannot quickly decide whether the two actions are mutually exclusive or complementary, mark and move on — this is where most silly errors happen.
  - **Three-action Banking mains questions where all three actions sound partially reasonable:** these are deliberately ambiguous; if your first read gives no clear verdict, skip and return. Never guess between "Only I and II" and "Only II and III" — the trap is designed for guessers.

**Golden rule from the pattern:** The correct course of action is almost always the **moderate, practical, administrative** step. The wrong one is almost always **extreme, emotional, vague, or unrelated**. When in doubt, ask: "Would a sensible officer actually do this?" If no — it does not follow.
