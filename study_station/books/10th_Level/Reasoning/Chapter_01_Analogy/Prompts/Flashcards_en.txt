🃏 Flashcard 1
Q: You see “A : B :: C : ?” What does the symbol “::” actually mean?
A: “::” means “has the same relationship as.” So “A is to B as C is to ?”

🃏 Flashcard 2
Q: What’s the first thing your brain should scream when you spot an analogy question? (Hint: 4-letter mnemonic)
A: WING — Word, Integer, Number (Letter), General Knowledge. Instantly classifies the type.

🃏 Flashcard 3
Q: Why is WING a perfect mnemonic for analogy types?
A: It covers all exam varieties: W=Word meanings, I=Integer maths, N=aN alphabet position, G=GK facts.

🃏 Flashcard 4
Q: If “Fish : Water” makes you think “home,” how do you make sure “Bird : Sky” wins over “Bird : Nest”?
A: Use the “Because-Therefore” test. “Because a fish lives in water, therefore water is its natural home.” For bird, only “sky” passes the same natural-home relationship; nest is man-made.

🃏 Flashcard 5
Q: You’re stuck between two answer choices that both seem to fit. What’s the golden rule?
A: Your relationship isn’t specific enough. Add one more detail until only one key fits the lock.

🃏 Flashcard 6
Q: What is the “Because-Therefore” sentence trick and why does it beat simple word-matching?
A: It forces you to define the exact relationship (cause and effect), filtering out weak matches that only look similar on the surface.

🃏 Flashcard 7
Q: In “Doctor : Hospital :: Teacher : ?”, why is “School” right but “Classroom” wrong?
A: Hospital is a complete workplace, not a part of it. Likewise, school is the whole workplace; classroom is just a room inside.

🃏 Flashcard 8
Q: “Pen : Write :: Knife : ?” If “cut” and “stab” are options, which one wins and why?
A: “Cut.” Because a pen’s main function is to write; a knife’s main function is to cut. “Stab” is too narrow and aggressive.

🃏 Flashcard 9
Q: How can the examiner trap you with difficult words like “Sarang : Mrig” (deer:deer)?
A: They use rare synonyms to make you think vocabulary is the key. You should guess the relationship from the answer choices and look for clues, not panic.

🃏 Flashcard 10
Q: What’s the second examiner’s trap when two answer choices both seem perfect?
A: They create double relationships—e.g., one option fits a “part-whole” relation, another fits “function.” You must detect which relationship is the primary one by checking the given pair’s tighter definition.

🃏 Flashcard 11
Q: “4 : 16 :: 5 : ?” Why is the answer 25 and not 10 (if you add 12)?
A: The relationship is “square,” not addition. Always scan for common operations first: square, cube, multiply, etc.

🃏 Flashcard 12
Q: “ACE : GIK :: MOQ : ?” What’s the hidden pattern?
A: Each letter jumps +6 positions forward (A→G, C→I, E→K). So MOQ +6 each gives SUW.

🃏 Flashcard 13
Q: “ABC : ZYX :: PQR : ?” Quick, how do you find the opposite letter instantly?
A: Sum of positions of opposite letters = 27. So P(16) opposite is 27-16=11(K), Q(17)->10(J), R(18)->9(I) → KJI.

🃏 Flashcard 14
Q: In a number analogy, you see “7 : 50 :: 9 : ?” and options 80, 82, 64, 78. The trick?
A: The relationship is (number × 7 + 1). 7×7+1=50, so 9×9+1=82. Don’t assume just multiplication.

🃏 Flashcard 15
Q: “India : New Delhi :: Japan : ?” is GK analogy. What’s the one second check you must do?
A: Verify the fact is indisputable. Never guess a capital unless sure. If you don’t know, skip.

🃏 Flashcard 16
Q: “Lawyer : Court :: Doctor : ?” Where do traps hide?
A: Trap choices: “patient,” “medicine.” Court is a workplace for lawyer; hospital is workplace for doctor. Patient is who they treat, not where they work.

🃏 Flashcard 17
Q: What’s the one-liner trick to recall all four analogy types even in panic?
A: “WING it! – Word, Integer, aN alphabet, General Knowledge.”

🃏 Flashcard 18
Q: After solving an analogy, what’s the 5-second test to confirm your answer is airtight?
A: Reverse the relationship: If A→B, then does C→your answer match exactly? No mismatched minor detail. If yes, you’re safe.
