🃏 Card 1
Front: You see “A : B :: C : ?” What does the symbol “::” actually mean?
Back: “::” means “has the same relationship as.” So the question reads: “A is to B as C is to ?”

🃏 Card 2
Front: What’s the first thing your brain should do when you spot an analogy question? (Hint: 4-letter mnemonic)
Back: WING — Word, Integer, Number (Letter), General Knowledge. Instantly classifies the type.

🃏 Card 3
Front: Why is WING a useful mnemonic for analogy types?
Back: It covers all common exam varieties: W = Word meanings, I = Integer maths, N = aN alphabet position, G = GK facts.

🃏 Card 4
Front: If “Fish : Water” makes you think “home,” how do you make sure “Bird : Sky” wins over “Bird : Nest”?
Back: Use the “Because-Therefore” test: “Because a fish lives in water, therefore water is its natural home.” For a bird, only “sky” passes the same natural-home relationship; a nest is built, not the natural medium.

🃏 Card 5
Front: You’re stuck between two answer choices that both seem to fit. What’s the golden rule?
Back: Your relationship isn’t specific enough. Add one more detail to the relationship until only one option fits.

🃏 Card 6
Front: What is the “Because-Therefore” sentence trick and why does it beat simple word-matching?
Back: It forces you to define the exact relationship in a full sentence, filtering out weak matches that only look similar on the surface.

🃏 Card 7
Front: In “Doctor : Hospital :: Teacher : ?”, why is “School” right but “Classroom” wrong?
Back: A hospital is a complete workplace, not a part of one. Likewise, a school is the whole workplace; a classroom is just a room inside it.

🃏 Card 8
Front: “Pen : Write :: Knife : ?” If “cut” and “stab” are options, which one wins and why?
Back: “Cut.” A pen’s main function is to write; a knife’s main function is to cut. “Stab” is too narrow and not the primary use.

🃏 Card 9
Front: How can the examiner trap you with difficult or rare words in word analogies?
Back: They use rare synonyms to make you think vocabulary is the key. Don’t panic — infer the relationship from the answer choices and look for clues.

🃏 Card 10
Front: What’s the examiner’s trap when two answer choices both seem perfect?
Back: They create double relationships — e.g., one option fits a “part-whole” relation, another fits “function.” Detect which relationship is the primary one by checking the given pair’s tighter definition.

🃏 Card 11
Front: “4 : 16 :: 5 : ?” Why is the answer 25 and not 10 (if you add 6)?
Back: The relationship is “square,” not addition. Always scan for common operations first: square, cube, multiply, etc.

🃏 Card 12
Front: “ACE : GIK :: MOQ : ?” What’s the hidden pattern?
Back: Each letter jumps +6 positions forward (A→G, C→I, E→K). So M→S, O→U, Q→W, giving SUW.

🃏 Card 13
Front: “ABC : ZYX :: PQR : ?” Quick, how do you find the opposite letter instantly?
Back: The positions of opposite letters sum to 27. So P(16) → 27−16 = 11 (K), Q(17) → 10 (J), R(18) → 9 (I) → KJI.

🃏 Card 14
Front: In a number analogy, you see “7 : 50 :: 9 : ?” with options 80, 82, 64, 78. What’s the trick?
Back: The relationship is n² + 1 (number × number + 1): 7×7+1 = 50, so 9×9+1 = 82. Don’t assume plain multiplication or a fixed multiplier.

🃏 Card 15
Front: “India : New Delhi :: Japan : ?” is a GK analogy. What’s the one-second check you must do?
Back: Verify the fact is indisputable (Japan : Tokyo). Never guess a capital unless sure; if you don’t know, skip rather than gamble.

🃏 Card 16
Front: “Lawyer : Court :: Doctor : ?” Where do traps hide?
Back: Trap choices: “patient,” “medicine.” A court is a lawyer’s workplace; a hospital is a doctor’s workplace. A patient is whom they treat, not where they work.

🃏 Card 17
Front: What’s the one-liner trick to recall all four analogy types even in panic?
Back: “WING it! — Word, Integer, aN alphabet, General Knowledge.”

🃏 Card 18
Front: After solving an analogy, what’s the 5-second test to confirm your answer is airtight?
Back: Reverse the relationship: if A→B holds, does C→your answer match exactly, with no mismatched detail? If yes, you’re safe.
