Imagine you and your best friend are spies. Every day, you receive a secret note with a string of numbers: 2, 10, 4, 20, 6, 30. You know this note is actually two messages tangled together — one from Agent Alpha and one from Agent Bravo. Alpha always sends a simple pattern using the first, third, and fifth numbers. Bravo uses the second, fourth, and sixth numbers. If you try to read the note as a single message, it looks like nonsense. But the moment you unbraid the note into two separate strands, the secret becomes clear.

This is the hardest concept in Number/Letter Series — the Alternate Series (also called Double Series). And here’s a story to make it stick forever.

🕵️ The Twin Rope Trick

When you see a series like 3, 15, 6, 12, 9, 9, ?, don’t panic. Picture two ropes twisted together to make one thick rope. Your job is to pull the ropes apart gently.

Rope 1 (Odd positions): 1st, 3rd, 5th terms → 3, 6, 9, ?

Rope 2 (Even positions): 2nd, 4th, 6th terms → 15, 12, 9

Now each rope has its own simple pattern:

Rope 1: +3 each time → 3, 6, 9, 12 (so next term is 12).

Rope 2: –3 each time → 15, 12, 9 (no need for next, but it’s consistent).

The missing 7th term belongs to Rope 1, so the answer is 12. This trick works for letters too: A, Z, B, Y, C, X, ?. Separate into A, B, C (forward +1) and Z, Y, X (backward –1). Next is D.

🧠 The Unforgettable Trick: “Even-Odd Unbraid”

Whenever a series seems to have no single rule, immediately underline the odd positions and circle the even positions. Give each group its own mini-series. Solve them independently. This one habit will save you from the examiner’s favourite trap.

Why is this hard? Because your brain wants one rule for the whole series. But the examiner deliberately weaves two stories together. Once you unbraid them, it’s just two easy series.
