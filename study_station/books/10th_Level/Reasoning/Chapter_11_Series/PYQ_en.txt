4. Top 10 High-Yield PYQs with Solutions

Q1. SSC CGL 2015 (9 Aug, Shift 2): 0, 7, 26, 63, ?

Solution: Pattern = n³ − 1 (starting n=1). 1³−1=0, 2³−1=7, 3³−1=26, 4³−1=63, 5³−1=124.-
5

🧠 Psychological Hook: "0, 7" — many students think it's n²−1 because 0=1²−1, but 7 is not 2²−1 (3). The examiner deliberately uses a number that fits two different initial patterns, knowing students will latch onto the first one. Always verify on at least 3 terms.

⏱️ Skip Rule: Attempt. The cube pattern emerges within 10 seconds if you know cubes up to 5. Do NOT skip.

Q2. SSC CHSL 2019 (5 July, Shift 1): 16, 18, 21, 26, 33, ?

Solution: Differences: +2, +3, +5, +7 → consecutive prime numbers. Next prime = 11, so 33+11 = 44.-
16

🧠 Psychological Hook: Differences are prime numbers — but 2 is a prime. Many students misclassify 2 as "even" and abandon the prime theory. The examiner exploits the common blind spot that 2 is the only even prime. Also, the differences (2,3,5,7) look like they could be "alternate +1,+2" but they aren't — a double trap.

⏱️ Skip Rule: SKIP if >45 seconds. The prime-difference pattern is subtle. If you don't spot it within 30 sec, the probability of a correct guess is low.

Q3. SSC CGL (Set-3): 1, 3, 8, 19, 42, ?

Solution: Pattern = ×2+1, ×2+2, ×2+3, ×2+4, ×2+5. So 42×2+5 = 89.-
1

🧠 Psychological Hook: The pattern changes — the constant being added increases (+1, +2, +3, ...). Students who blindly test ×2+1 across all terms fail. The examiner banks on candidates testing only the first two terms and concluding wrongly.

⏱️ Skip Rule: Attempt. Write differences and you'll see the pattern in 20 seconds.

Q4. SSC CGL (Set-3): 95, 115, 145, 155, ?

Solution: 5×19=95, 5×23=115, 5×29=145, 5×31=155, 5×37=185. The multipliers are consecutive prime numbers.-
1

🧠 Psychological Hook: The numbers seem random — 95, 115, 145, 155 — but they're all multiples of 5. The student who checks divisibility by 5 cracks it; the one who goes straight to differences (+20, +30, +10) gets lost because differences are inconsistent. The examiner's trap: hidden common factor.

⏱️ Skip Rule: SKIP if >45 seconds and difference ladder fails. This is a "factor-based" series — rare but dangerous.

Q5. SSC CGL (Set-3): 216, 209, 202, 522, 515, 508, 633, 626, ?

Solution: Three interleaved rows, each decreasing by 7. Third row: 633, 626, 619. So ? = 619.-
1

🧠 Psychological Hook: The series length (9 terms) and the sudden jump from 202 to 522 screams "split me!" But students conditioned to look at the whole series as one unit miss the row structure. The examiner exploits the tendency to not count terms and check for repeating patterns.

⏱️ Skip Rule: Attempt. Spot the −7 pattern in the first 3 terms and you'll solve it in 20 seconds.

Q6. SSC CGL 2014 (Tier 1, 20 July): X, Q, K, F, ?

Solution: Convert to reverse positions: X=24 (from A), Q=17, K=11, F=6. Differences: −7, −6, −5, so next −4 → 2 → B. (Note: Here positions are counted from reverse — Z=1, Y=2, ...; X=3, etc. But the standard forward conversion also works if you spot the gap pattern.) Using forward A=1: X=24, Q=17 (diff −7), K=11 (diff −6), F=6 (diff −5), next 6−4=2 → B.-
59

🧠 Psychological Hook: Decreasing gaps (−7, −6, −5) — but students count forward and backward inconsistently. The examiner traps those who guess letters by alphabet position without calculating gaps. Always convert to numbers.

⏱️ Skip Rule: Attempt. Letter-to-number conversion takes 10 sec; pattern emerges immediately.

Q7. Banking (IBPS PO): 5531, 5506, 5425, 5304, 5135, 4910, 4621 (Find wrong term)

Solution: Differences: −25, −81, −121, −169, −225, −289 → these are 5², 9², 11², 13², 15², 17². But 9²=81 while the first difference is −25 (which is 5²). The pattern should be consecutive odd squares: 5², 7², 9², 11², 13², 15². The second term difference should be −49 (7²), not −81. So 5506 is wrong — it should be 5531−49=5482.-

🧠 Psychological Hook: The squares appear correct at a glance — 25, 81, 121 all look like squares. But the missing 49 (7²) is the trap. Banking examiners love wrong-term questions where the mistake is an omitted step in an otherwise perfect pattern. Students verify the first difference (25=5²) and assume correctness.

⏱️ Skip Rule: SKIP if >45 seconds. This requires second-level difference analysis. Banking aspirants: attempt only if you've practiced similar questions.

Q8. RRB NTPC: 31, 37, 41, 43, 47, 51, 53 (Choose incorrect)

Solution: The series should be consecutive prime numbers: 31, 37, 41, 43, 47, 53, 59. But 51 is NOT prime (51=3×17). So 51 is wrong.-
32

🧠 Psychological Hook: 51 looks "prime-ish" — it's odd, not obviously divisible by 3 unless you check digit sum (5+1=6, divisible by 3). The examiner exploits weak prime-number knowledge. Know primes up to 100 cold: 51 and 57 are classic traps.

⏱️ Skip Rule: Do NOT skip. Primes check takes 10 seconds if memorised.

Q9. SSC Tier 2 Special: 24, X, 12, 18, 36, 90. Find X.

Solution: Multipliers between consecutive terms: 18/12=1.5, 36/18=2, 90/36=2.5 → pattern +0.5. Working backward: multiplier before 1.5 must be 1.0 and 0.5. So 24×0.5=12, and 12×1.0=12. Therefore X=12 (Option b).-
43

🧠 Psychological Hook: The sudden drop from 24 to 12 is deliberately confusing. Most students test addition/subtraction first (24−12=12) but then the plateau (12, 12) breaks that. The "U-turn" — drop then steady rise — is a CSAT signature trap. The student who thinks in terms of multipliers, not differences, wins.

⏱️ Skip Rule: SKIP if >45 seconds. CSAT series are conceptual. If the ratio method doesn't click within 30 sec, mark for review.

Q10. SSC Tier 2 Special: 1, 3, 6, 11, 18, X, 42. Find X.

Solution: Differences: 2, 3, 5, 7, ?, ? — consecutive prime numbers. Next prime after 7 is 11. So X = 18+11 = 29. Verify: 29+13=42.-
43

🧠 Psychological Hook: Same prime-difference trap as Q2, but in CSAT the options are closer (26, 27, 29, 30). The student who misses that 2 is prime and starts from 3 as the first prime gets 27 (18+9), which is a given option. The examiner deliberately places the "false answer" in the options. This is the most dangerous trap in all series questions.

⏱️ Skip Rule: Attempt. If you know primes, 20 seconds; if not, guess and move. The CSAT has no negative marking for this section? Actually, Delhi Police / State Exams has 1/3 negative marking. So skip if unsure.

7. Key Takeaways from 10-Year PYQ Analysis

SSC CGL & CHSL: Master Square/Cube ± n and Difference Ladder. ~60% of questions fall here.

Banking PO: Mixed operations (×2+1, ×3−2 etc.) and Wrong Term detection dominate. Practice "second-level difference."

Railway: Basic arithmetic series with small gaps. Speed matters — aim to solve in 15-20 seconds.

Delhi Police / State Exams: Conceptual patterns — ratios, primes, and factor-based. Options are deliberately close. Verify on all terms.

Universal Trap: The "Fake Easy Start" — the first 2-3 terms suggest one pattern, but the 4th term breaks it. Always verify on minimum 4 terms.

Prime Numbers: Know primes up to 100 (especially the traps: 51, 57, 87, 91). These appear in SSC, Banking, and CSAT.

Letter Series: The "sum=27" opposite-pair rule (AZBY) appears in some form every year in SSC.-
