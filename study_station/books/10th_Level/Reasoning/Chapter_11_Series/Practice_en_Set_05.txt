101. 8, 13, 20, 29, 40, ?
(a) 54 (b) 51 (c) 52 (d) 53
Answer: (d)
Solution: The differences are +5, +7, +9, +11, rising by 2 each time, so the next difference is +13. The trap is assuming a constant difference of +11, which gives 51. Adding 13 to 40, the answer is 53.

102. 3, 6, 11, 18, 27, ?
(a) 38 (b) 36 (c) 37 (d) 39
Answer: (a)
Solution: Each term is n²+2: 1²+2=3, 2²+2=6, 3²+2=11, 4²+2=18, 5²+2=27. The trap is treating the differences +3, +5, +7, +9 as ending, but the next difference is +11, giving 27+11=38. So the answer is 38.
Source: PYQ-style

104. 2, 12, 36, 80, ?
(a) 120 (b) 140 (c) 160 (d) 150
Answer: (d)
Solution: Each term is n²(n+1): 1²×2=2, 2²×3=12, 3²×4=36, 4²×5=80, so the next is 5²×6=150. The trap is using n³+n, which coincidentally matches the early terms but also gives 150 only if applied correctly; guessing a round number like 160 fails. So the answer is 150.
Source: PYQ-style

108. Find the wrong term: 1, 2, 6, 24, 120, 700, 5040
(a) 6 (b) 24 (c) 120 (d) 700
Answer: (d)
Solution: The series is factorials: 1!=1, 2!=2, 3!=6, 4!=24, 5!=120, 6!=720, 7!=5040. The trap is that 700 looks like a plausible round number after 120, but 6! is 720, not 700. So the wrong term is 700.

110. 2, 3, 10, 15, 26, 35, ?
(a) 48 (b) 49 (c) 50 (d) 51
Answer: (c)
Solution: Odd-position terms are n²+1 (1²+1=2, 3²+1=10, 5²+1=26) and even-position terms are n²−1 (2²−1=3, 4²−1=15, 6²−1=35). The trap is applying n²−1 to the 7th term, which gives 48. The 7th term is odd-positioned, so 7²+1=50, and the answer is 50.
