📊 Previous Years Questions (PYQ) Analysis — Paper Folding & Cutting

1. How Exams Test This Chapter (Pattern Analysis)

Paper Folding & Cutting is a regular feature of the non-verbal reasoning section in 10th-level exams such as SSC GD, SSC MTS, SSC CHSL, RRB Group D, RRB NTPC and RPF Constable. Typically one question (occasionally two) appears per paper, though the exact count varies by exam, year and shift — treat it as a near-guaranteed question rather than a fixed number.

How the pattern has evolved:
- Earlier papers focused on basic single-fold cuts and simple hole punches.
- Recent papers increasingly use 2-fold and 3-fold patterns, circular sheets, diagonal folds, asymmetric cuts and multiple punches — testing genuine spatial visualization rather than memorized formulas.
- Transparent-sheet folding (superimposition) questions have also become common.

The fundamental solving method has never changed: unfold in reverse order (last fold opened first — LIFO) and treat every crease line as a plane mirror.

3. Top 10 High-Yield PYQ Models (With Solutions & Traps)

Model 1: Classic Single-Fold Mirror
Question: A sheet is folded in half. A semicircle is cut from the folded edge. What is revealed upon unfolding?
Solution: The fold line acts as a plane mirror. The semicircle reflects across the fold to form a complete circle.
🧠 Examiner's Trap: Answer choices showing an off-centre or distorted circle.
🕒 Time Target: 15-second question.
Source: PYQ-style

Model 2: Double-Fold Symmetry (4 Quadrants)
Question: A square sheet is folded vertically then horizontally. A small triangle is cut from the unfolded outer corner. How many triangles appear upon unfolding?
Solution: 4 triangles, one in each quadrant equidistant from the centre.
🧠 Examiner's Trap: Stopping at the first reflection and choosing 2 triangles.
Source: PYQ-style

Model 3: The Touch-or-Not-Touch Crease Rule
Question: Paper is folded and a cut is made very close to the fold line without touching it. What is the result?
Solution: Two separate, disconnected shapes. The shape reflects but does not merge.
🧠 Examiner's Trap: Assuming that proximity implies connection.
Source: PYQ-style

Model 4: Holes Punched on the Crease Line
Question: A square sheet is folded twice into a small square. A hole is punched exactly at the corner where both folds meet. How does it look unfolded?
Solution: Exactly one central hole. The reflections across both fold lines merge into a single central hole.
🧠 Examiner's Trap: Blindly applying the 2² = 4 formula and panicking when 4 holes are not in the options.
Source: PYQ-style

Model 5: Asymmetric Cut Symmetry
Question: Paper is folded once and an 'L' shape is cut from the crease. What does the unfolded shape look like?
Solution: The 'L' reflects across the crease to form a symmetric composite shape.
🧠 Examiner's Trap: Options showing the 'L' merely rotated rather than laterally reflected.
Source: PYQ-style

Model 6: Quadrant Zig-Zag Cut
Question: A circular sheet is folded into a quadrant (quarter-circle) and a zig-zag cut is made on the curved rim.
Solution: The cut repeats symmetrically across all 4 quadrants of the circular boundary.
🧠 Examiner's Trap: Distractors showing incorrect zig-zag orientation or non-symmetric repetition.
Source: PYQ-style

Model 7: Triple-Fold Sheet (8 Layers)
Question: A sheet is folded in half three times and a small notch is cut from the edge.
Solution: The notch reflects 2³ = 8 times along the edges of the open sheet.
🧠 Examiner's Trap: Trying to visualize all 3 folds at once. Always unfold sequentially in reverse order.
Source: PYQ-style

Model 8: Diagonal Fold with Off-Centre Hole
Question: A square sheet is folded along a diagonal. A hole is punched at the centre of the resulting triangle.
Solution: Unfolding reveals two separate holes positioned symmetrically on either side of the diagonal.
🧠 Examiner's Trap: Assuming the hole lies at the centre of the original square.
Source: PYQ-style

Model 9: Rotated Option Distractor
Question: The correct unfolded shape is an upright 'T'. An option displays the identical 'T' rotated by 90°.
Solution: The rotated 'T' is incorrect. Unfolding involves pure reflection, never rotation.
🧠 Examiner's Trap: Selecting a familiar shape without verifying reflection axes.
Source: PYQ-style

Model 10: Transparent Sheet Superimposition
Question: A transparent sheet has two distinct patterns on either half with a central dotted fold line. How does it look when folded?
Solution: One half's pattern reflects across the dotted line and superimposes directly over the other half.
🧠 Examiner's Trap: Distractors showing the patterns side-by-side instead of superimposed.
Source: PYQ-style

⚠️ Three Critical Rules for Students:
1. Reflection Over Rotation: A choice showing a rotated cut is immediately wrong.
2. Verify Crease Contact: Always check whether the cut touches or stays clear of the fold line.
3. Quadrant Elimination: Focus on a single quadrant and eliminate options that do not match it.
