Set 1: Paper Folding & Cutting MCQs (English)
Questions 1–16: Easy | Questions 17–20: Medium

Q1. One fold and one hole — how many holes appear?
(a) 1 (b) 2 (c) 3 (d) 4
Answer: (b) 2
Solution: 1 × 2 = 2.
Source: NCERT Reasoning

Q2. Two folds and one hole — how many holes?
(a) 2 (b) 3 (c) 4 (d) 8
Answer: (c) 4
Solution: 1 × 2² = 4.
Source: SSC MTS

Q3. Each fold does what to the holes?
(a) halves (b) doubles (c) same (d) triples
Answer: (b) doubles
Solution: Each fold doubles the number of holes.
Source: SSC CHSL

Q4. Three folds and one hole — how many holes?
(a) 3 (b) 6 (c) 8 (d) 9
Answer: (c) 8
Solution: 1 × 2³ = 8.
Source: RRB NTPC

Q5. When opened, holes are:
(a) copied directly (b) mirrored across the fold line (c) removed (d) doubled at the centre
Answer: (b) mirrored across the fold line
Solution: Holes are mirrored across the fold line.
Source: NCERT Reasoning

Q6. One fold and two holes — how many holes?
(a) 2 (b) 3 (c) 4 (d) 6
Answer: (c) 4
Solution: 2 × 2 = 4.
Source: SSC MTS

Q7. In what order do you open a folded paper?
(a) same as folded (b) reverse (c) any (d) middle-out
Answer: (b) reverse
Solution: Open the last fold first.
Source: SSC CGL

Q8. Four folds and one hole — how many holes?
(a) 4 (b) 8 (c) 12 (d) 16
Answer: (d) 16
Solution: 1 × 2⁴ = 16.
Source: RRB Group D

Q9. Two folds and two holes — how many holes?
(a) 4 (b) 6 (c) 8 (d) 16
Answer: (c) 8
Solution: 2 × 2² = 8.
Source: SSC CHSL

Q10. The formula for the number of holes is:
(a) holes + folds (b) holes × 2^folds (c) holes × folds (d) holes − folds
Answer: (b) holes × 2^folds
Solution: Each fold doubles the holes.
Source: NCERT Reasoning

Q11. One fold and three holes — how many holes?
(a) 3 (b) 6 (c) 9 (d) 12
Answer: (b) 6
Solution: 3 × 2 = 6.
Source: SSC MTS

Q12. Three folds and two holes — how many holes?
(a) 8 (b) 12 (c) 16 (d) 24
Answer: (c) 16
Solution: 2 × 2³ = 16.
Source: SSC CGL

Q13. An opened paper is always:
(a) random (b) symmetric across the fold lines (c) blank (d) torn
Answer: (b) symmetric across the fold lines
Solution: It is balanced (symmetric) across the fold lines.
Source: NCERT Reasoning

Q14. The fold made first opens:
(a) first (b) last (c) in the middle (d) never
Answer: (b) last
Solution: Open in reverse order, so the first fold opens last.
Source: SSC MTS

Q15. Two folds and three holes — how many holes?
(a) 6 (b) 9 (c) 12 (d) 18
Answer: (c) 12
Solution: 3 × 2² = 12.
Source: RRB NTPC

Q16. A quick way to eliminate options is to check:
(a) colour (b) the number of holes (c) paper size (d) fold thickness
Answer: (b) the number of holes
Solution: Wrong hole counts can be cut immediately.
Source: NCERT Reasoning

Q17. (Medium) Four folds and two holes — how many holes?
(a) 16 (b) 24 (c) 32 (d) 8
Answer: (c) 32
Solution: 2 × 2⁴ = 32.
Source: SSC CGL

Q18. (Medium) If a hole is at the centre of a paper folded once along the middle, opening shows:
(a) 1 hole (b) 2 holes at the fold line touching (c) 4 holes (d) no hole
Answer: (b) 2 holes at the fold line touching
Solution: A centre hole on the fold gives two holes meeting along the fold line.
Source: SSC CGL

Q19. (Medium) Five folds and one hole — how many holes?
(a) 10 (b) 16 (c) 32 (d) 25
Answer: (c) 32
Solution: 1 × 2⁵ = 32.
Source: RRB NTPC

Q20. (Medium) Holes are mirrored, not copied. This is because folding creates:
(a) more paper (b) symmetry across the fold (c) thicker paper (d) random layers
Answer: (b) symmetry across the fold
Solution: The layers are mirror images across the fold line.
Source: SSC MTS
