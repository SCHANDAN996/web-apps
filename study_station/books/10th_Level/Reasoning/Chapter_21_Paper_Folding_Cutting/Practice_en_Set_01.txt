1. A square paper is folded in half along a vertical midline (left half over right half). Two small square holes are cut on the folded packet away from the crease. When completely unfolded, what pattern of square holes appears?
(a) A single square hole in the middle (b) Two square holes stacked on the midline (c) Four square holes at the four outer corners (d) Two pairs of symmetric square holes on both sides of the midline
Answer: (d)
Solution: The tempting wrong choice is four corner holes, but the cuts are made away from the crease and away from the corners, so they cannot land at the corners. Each cut passes through 2 layers and reflects across the vertical fold line, giving two symmetric pairs, so the answer is two pairs of symmetric square holes on both sides of the midline.
Source: PYQ-style

2. A square paper is folded along a diagonal. A triangular cut is made on the resulting triangle with its base lying along the diagonal fold line. Upon unfolding, what shape will be formed?
(a) A diamond (rhombus) formed by two triangles joined base-to-base (b) A single triangle (c) Two separate triangles on opposite sides (d) A square centered at the corner
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is to imagine two separate triangles, but since the base of the cut lies exactly on the fold, the two reflected triangles share that base and merge. The result is a rhombus, so the answer is a diamond formed by two triangles joined base-to-base.
Source: PYQ-style

3. ⚠️ A square paper is folded in half vertically, then in half horizontally. A small square is cut out from the corner where both folded edges meet. What pattern appears upon unfolding?
(a) Two square holes along the horizontal crease (b) A single central square hole (c) Four separate square holes in the quadrants (d) Four square holes at the four outer corners
Answer: (b)
Solution: Examiner's Trap: the corner where both folded edges meet is the exact centre of the original sheet, not an outer corner, so four corner holes is wrong. Cutting there removes the centre of all four layers, which join into one hole, so the answer is a single central square hole.
Source: PYQ-style

4. A square paper is folded in half vertically, then in half horizontally. A small circular hole is punched at the open corner opposite both folded edges. What pattern of holes appears upon unfolding?
(a) Two circles along the diagonal (b) One circle at the centre (c) One circle at each of the four corners (d) Four circles, one in each quadrant's centre
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is to assume four quadrant-centre holes, but the open corner of the packet is formed by the four outer corners of the original sheet stacked together. The punch therefore makes one hole at each outer corner, so the answer is one circle at each of the four corners.
Source: PYQ-style

(a) A single large triangle at the centre (b) Two separate triangles on the sides (c) Four small triangles at the corners (d) Two triangles joined base-to-base along the horizontal midline
Answer: (d)
Solution: The tempting error is two separate triangles, but the base lies on the crease, so the reflected triangles share that base and merge into a diamond-like shape. Hence the answer is two triangles joined base-to-base along the horizontal midline.

6. A square paper is folded along a diagonal. A small square is cut out from the right-angled corner of the resulting triangle. Upon unfolding, what appears?
(a) Two square holes at two adjacent corners (b) Two square holes at two opposite corners of the sheet (c) One square hole at the centre (d) Four square holes at all corners
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is to pick adjacent corners, but when a square is folded along a diagonal, the right-angled corner of the triangle is formed by the two corners that lie off the diagonal — and those are opposite corners of the sheet, stacked together. Cutting there makes one hole at each, so the answer is two square holes at two opposite corners.

(a) Two separate semicircles on left and right (b) Only one semicircle on the left (c) A single complete circular hole (d) Two semicircles touching at a single point
Answer: (c)
Solution: The tempting wrong option is two separate semicircles, but the diameter lies on the crease, so the two reflected semicircles share the diameter and merge seamlessly. The result is one full circle, so the answer is a single complete circular hole.

8. A square paper is folded along a diagonal, and the resulting triangle is folded in half again by bringing its acute corners together. A hole is punched away from every crease and corner. How many holes appear upon full unfolding?
(a) 4 (b) 8 (c) 2 (d) 6
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is to count 8 by over-multiplying; two folds give only 2 × 2 = 4 layers. Since the punch is away from all creases, each layer gives a distinct hole, so the answer is 4.

12. A square paper is folded along a diagonal. A straight slit is cut perpendicular to the folded crease, starting from the crease and extending inward. What will be seen upon unfolding?
(a) A V-shaped notch (b) An X-shaped cut (c) A single continuous straight slit perpendicular to the diagonal (d) Two parallel slits
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is a V-shape, but the slit meets the crease at 90°, so its mirror image is collinear with it, not angled. The two segments unite into one straight slit, so the answer is a single continuous straight slit perpendicular to the diagonal.

13. A square paper is folded in half vertically, then in half horizontally. A hole is punched at the exact centre of the resulting quarter-square packet. How many holes appear upon full unfolding?
(a) 2 (b) 1 (c) 8 (d) 4
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is 1 hole, confusing the centre of the packet with the centre of the sheet; the packet's centre lies away from both creases. With 4 layers, the punch makes one hole per quadrant, so the answer is 4.

Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is one diamond, but only the vertex lies on the crease, not the whole shape, so the cut reflects into a second diamond touching the first at that vertex. Hence the answer is two diamonds sharing one vertex on the horizontal midline.

16. A square paper is folded in half vertically. A straight cut is made from a point on the crease to the upper open corner, removing the triangular flap. When unfolded, what is the shape of the cut-out hole?

Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is two separate triangles, but the cut starts on the crease, so the two mirrored triangles share the vertex on the midline and join. They form a kite or rhombus shape, so the answer is a rhombus-shaped hole symmetric about the midline.

21. ⚠️ A square paper is folded in half vertically, then in half horizontally (producing 4 layers). A hole is punched at a point lying exactly on the horizontal crease of the packet, away from the intersection of the two folds. How many holes appear upon unfolding?
(a) 4 (b) 2 (c) 1 (d) 8
Answer: (b)
Solution: Examiner's Trap: most students apply 2 × 2 = 4, but a punch made exactly on a crease is shared by the two halves across that crease, so each pair merges into one hole. Unfolding the vertical fold then mirrors it once, so the answer is 2.

Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is four holes around the centre, but the midpoint of the hypotenuse of the diagonal fold is the exact centre of the original sheet, and every layer passes through that point. All punches coincide, so the answer is a single hole at the exact centre.

25. A square paper is folded in half along the vertical midline, then folded in half from top to bottom, and the resulting packet is folded in half once more along its vertical midline. (a) 8 (b) 2 (c) 4 (d) 16
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is 16 from multiplying by 2 four times; only three folds are made, giving 2 × 2 × 2 = 8 layers. The cut corner avoids all creases, so each layer yields a distinct hole, and the answer is 8.
