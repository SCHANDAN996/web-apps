127. (Examiner’s Trap – Double relationship/misleading layers)
A square paper is folded along one diagonal. The two acute corners of the resulting triangle are folded to meet at the midpoint of the hypotenuse. A hole is punched through all layers exactly at the point where the two acute corners meet. How many holes are seen after completely unfolding the paper?
A) 1
B) 2
C) 4
D) 8
Correct: A
Trap: Many test‑takers assume 4 holes because of 4 layers. However, the point where the acute corners meet is the midpoint of the hypotenuse, which is the exact centre of the original square. In all four layers this point coincides; therefore the hole is made at the identical location on every layer and they overlap perfectly, giving just one hole.
Solution: First fold (diagonal) → 2 layers, centre of square is the midpoint of the hypotenuse. Second fold (corners to that midpoint) → 4 layers, and the meeting point remains the centre. Punch at that point → a single central hole on unfolding.
Source: Bank PO Mains – 2020

128. A square paper is folded in half vertically. Then the left half of the rectangle is folded over to the right. A small square is cut out from the top right corner of the final folded shape (through all layers). How many square holes will be seen after complete unfolding?
A) 2
B) 3
C) 4
D) 6
Correct: C
Solution: First vertical fold → 2 layers. Second fold (left half over right) brings the left‑side layers on top of the right‑side layers, making 4 layers. The top right corner of the packet corresponds to four distinct original corners (two top corners and two right‑side corners in the original square). Hence four square holes appear.
Source: PYQ-style

129. A square paper is folded in half from top to bottom. The bottom right corner of the resulting rectangle is folded up so that it touches the midpoint of the top edge. A) 2
B) 3
C) 4
D) 5
Correct: C
Solution: First horizontal fold → 2 layers. The oblique corner fold adds 2 more layers in that region, making 4 layers at the slanted folded edge. The cut lies on the fold, removing a semicircular piece from each layer. When opened, these pair up into 4 full circles arranged symmetrically (two pairs).
Source: PYQ-style

C) 4
D) 8
Correct: B
Trap: Because the paper has 4 layers, many candidates expect 4 holes. However, the hole is made on a folded edge, so each pair of layers that share that fold combine their half‑circle cut‑outs into one complete circle. The 4 layers yield 2 complete circular holes.
Solution: Two diagonal folds → 4 layers. Cut on the final fold → a semicircular piece is removed from each of the 4 layers. The two layers on one side of the fold meet their counterparts from the other side, joining the semicircles into 2 full circles.
Source: PYQ-style

145. A square paper is folded in half vertically, then the bottom edge is folded up to meet the top edge. A heart‑shaped cut is made such that the cleft of the heart lies exactly on the final folded edge. When unfolded, which pattern emerges?
A) Two hearts joined at the cleft
B) A single large heart
C) Four hearts around the centre
D) Two separate hearts not touching
Correct: A
Solution: The final folded edge is horizontal. Cutting a heart with its cleft on that fold removes a half‑heart from each of the two layers meeting there. When opened, these two half‑hearts join perfectly at the cleft to form two complete heart shapes, one above and one below, each symmetric about the fold line. The remaining layers (4 total due to the two folds) produce two such joined pairs, but because of the way the folds overlap, the visible pattern is two hearts joined at the cleft.
Source: PYQ-style

148. (Examiner’s Trap – Coincident holes on folded edge)
A square paper is folded in half vertically (left edge over to right edge). Then the left half of the resulting rectangle is folded over to the right. A hole is punched at the midpoint of the left edge of the final folded shape. When completely unfolded, how many holes are seen?
A) 1
B) 2
C) 3
D) 4
Correct: C
Trap: The natural tendency is to count 4 layers and answer 4 holes. However, the left edge of the final packet is a folded edge where two layers meet. Punching on this folded edge creates a single hole shared by those two layers (they will show one hole on the fold line when opened). The other two layers, which were originally the right half of the square, get a hole each at symmetric positions away from the fold. Total holes: 3.
Solution: After first vertical fold, 2 layers; after folding left half over right, 4 layers. The left edge of the packet is the new fold line. A hole punched there goes through all 4 layers. The pair of layers that meet at that fold share the same hole location (one hole will appear exactly on the vertical midline of the original square). The other pair have no edge there, so they produce two distinct holes on the right side. Thus 3 holes appear.
Source: PYQ-style
