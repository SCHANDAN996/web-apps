🃏 Flashcards — Paper Folding & Cutting

Card 1
Front: What invisible magic appears every time you fold a paper?
Back: A fold line acts like a flat mirror — anything cut on one side instantly gets a mirror image on the other side.

Card 2
Front: You folded a paper three times, then made one punch through all layers. How many holes appear on unfolding?
Back: 2³ = 8 holes, provided the punch goes through all layers and no holes overlap.

Card 3
Front: What is the golden rule for unfolding multiple folds?
Back: Always unfold in reverse order — the last fold you made must be the first one you open. (Like removing a jacket before a sweater.)

Card 4
Front: A half-heart cut along the fold line becomes what on opening?
Back: A full heart — because the fold line mirror joins the two halves into one connected shape.

Card 5
Front: What does the mnemonic "MUFLI" stand for?
Back: Mirror (fold line = mirror), Unfold reverse, Focus on cut's position (touching fold or not), Layer count, Inspect final image.

Card 6
Front: How does "MUFLI" help avoid careless mistakes?
Back: It forces a step-by-step check — especially 'F'ocus on whether the cut touches the fold line, which decides if shapes will be joined or separate.

Card 7
Front: In a double-folded paper, you cut a triangle that doesn’t touch any fold line. After full opening, how many triangles appear?
Back: Four separate triangles — two mirrors each create a new copy, and because the cut never touched a fold line, they remain unjoined.

Card 8
Front: Why can’t you just guess the final pattern after seeing only the final folded state?
Back: Because the paper must be mentally unfolded step by step. Each fold line adds a new mirror reflection; skipping steps leads to wrong counting or shape.

Card 9
Front: What is the most common examiner’s trap in Paper Cutting options?
Back: Offering images that look almost perfect but have slight rotation. Remember: cuts never rotate upon unfolding; they only mirror across fold lines.

Card 10
Front: How do examiners trick you with "touch vs. not-touch" cuts?
Back: They give two options — one with joined shapes (cut touched fold) and one with separate shapes (cut didn't touch). Rushing makes you pick the wrong one. Always check position.

Card 11
Front: A paper is folded into a triangle, then a tiny semicircle is cut on the open edge not touching any fold. Unfold. What do you get?
Back: Two separate semicircles. One fold line mirror duplicates the cut, but since the cut didn't touch the fold, they remain unjoined.

Card 12
Front: How can you quickly verify the hole count after multiple folds and punches?
Back: Use the 2ⁿ rule (n = number of folds). But watch out — if holes overlap because punches are on fold lines, the visible count may be less.

Card 13
Front: After unfolding, a cut that touched a fold line merges with its mirror image. What if it only touched the fold line at one tiny point?
Back: It still merges — any contact along the fold line, even a single point, will connect the two mirror halves into one unified shape.

Card 14
Front: What's the "Reverse Unfolding Dance" trick?
Back: Physically fold your arms (fold 1), then cross your legs (fold 2). To return to normal, you uncross legs first, then arms. That’s exactly how you undo paper folds — last fold first.

Card 15
Front: You are stuck between two answer options that are mirror images of each other. What single question will help you pick the right one?
Back: Ask "Which side of the fold line was the cut on in the final folded figure?" The mirror must place the copied cut on the opposite side of that line.

Card 16
Front: In Paper Punching problems, what happens if the punch goes exactly on a fold line?
Back: When unfolded, the holes from adjacent layers overlap and appear as a single hole along that fold line. The total count will be less than 2ⁿ.

Card 17
Front: What is the simplest mental tool to start any Paper Folding & Cutting question?
Back: Take the final folded image, draw an imaginary mirror on the last fold line, and place the reflection of the cut across it. Repeat for each previous fold in reverse order.🃏 Flashcards — Paper Folding & Cutting
