📋 Important Rules & Approaches (नियम, समय नियम, सूत्र)

Rule / Approach	Description	Example / Note
Part 1: Key Rules & Approaches
Fold Line = Mirror	The line where paper is folded acts like a flat mirror. Any cut or hole placed on one side is reflected exactly on the other side when unfolded.	A semicircle cut on one side of a fold becomes a full circle when the paper is opened because the mirror joins the cut and its reflection.
Unfold in Reverse Order (Last Fold First)	Always start unfolding from the last fold you made, then go backwards to the first fold. Never start with the first fold.	If you fold paper twice (fold A then fold B), first undo fold B, then fold A. Like removing a jacket before a sweater.
Touch or Not-Touch Rule	If a cut or punch touches the fold line, the resulting shapes will be joined (merge) upon unfolding. If it does NOT touch the fold line, they remain separate unjoined shapes.	Cut a half-heart on the fold → full heart (joined). Cut a circle fully inside one layer away from fold → two separate circles after unfolding.
Hole Punching with Multiple Folds	For each fold that the punch goes through, the number of holes multiplies. Generally, if paper is folded ‘n’ times and a punch goes through all layers without landing on a fold line, you get 2ⁿ holes.	3 folds + 1 punch = 2³ = 8 holes (if no overlap). But if the punch is exactly on a fold line, holes overlap and count reduces.
No Rotation – Only Reflection	When paper is unfolded, cuts never rotate; they only reflect across fold lines. The final image is composed purely of mirror reflections.	An L-shaped cut will always appear as its mirror image on the other side, not as a rotated L. Exam options often include a rotated version to trick you.
Sequential Mirroring for Multiple Folds	For each fold in reverse order, take the current cut pattern (including any reflections already added) and reflect it across that fold line. This step-by-step process builds the final unfolded pattern.	After unfolding the last fold, you have two triangles. When you unfold the first fold, reflect both triangles across that fold line, giving four triangles.
Symmetry Check	The final unfolded pattern must be symmetric with respect to every fold line. If an option breaks that symmetry, it is wrong.	If the paper was folded vertically and horizontally, the final pattern must have both vertical and horizontal mirror symmetry.
Part 2: Time Management
15-Second Rule	If you cannot understand the pattern or start unfolding within 15 seconds, mark the question and skip. Come back later if time permits.	This prevents wasting time on complex multi-fold questions early in the exam.
45-Second Rule	For moderate questions, aim to solve within 45 seconds. If you reach 45 seconds and are still unsure, mark it and move on. Do not exceed 60 seconds on any single Paper Folding question.	Even a two-fold cutting problem should be solvable in 30-40 seconds with practice.
Easy First Strategy	First attempt questions with a single fold or simple cut. Leave triple folds or diagonal cuts for later.	A single-fold, clear cut question takes less than 20 seconds; secure those marks first.
Mental Imagery vs. Rough Work	Use rough paper to quickly draw the mirror images if mental visualization takes time. Drawing step-by-step is faster than re-reading the question.	For a double fold, quickly sketch the final folded shape, then unfold each step with a pencil.
Part 3: Logical / Mathematical Formulas
Hole Count Guideline (2ⁿ)	If paper is folded ‘n’ times and a punch is made through all layers but NOT on any fold line, total holes = 2ⁿ. This is a rough guideline, not an absolute rule.	n = number of folds. 2 folds → 4 holes, 3 folds → 8 holes, etc. Always verify if the punch lies on a fold line (overlap reduces count).
No Fixed Formula for Cutting Shapes	For cutting problems, no mathematical formula exists. The final shape depends entirely on the shape, position, and orientation of the cut relative to fold lines. Use the mirror method step by step.	–
Note	This chapter is purely concept-driven. No formulas other than the 2ⁿ hole guideline are needed. Everything else is spatial reasoning.	–
