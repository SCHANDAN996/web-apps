Paper Folding & Cutting – Set 5 (Questions 101–125)
*Medium (101–120), Hard (121–125) • 4 options each • Step-by-step solutions • Source mentioned*

Medium Level (Questions 101–120)

101. A square sheet of paper is folded once along the vertical midline (left half over right half). A small square is cut out from the centre of the folded edge. When the paper is unfolded, which pattern appears?
A) One square hole at the centre
B) Two square holes placed symmetrically left and right of the vertical midline
C) Two square holes one above the other along the midline
D) Four square holes at the four corners
Correct: B
Solution: The vertical fold creates two layers. Cutting a square from the folded edge removes a square from both layers at the mirror-image positions. Unfolding gives two identical square holes symmetric about the vertical fold line.
Source: NTSE Stage 1 – 2019

102. A square paper is folded along a diagonal. A small circle is cut out from the folded edge, close to one acute corner. When unfolded, how many circles appear and where?
A) One circle on the diagonal
B) Two circles symmetric about the diagonal
C) Four circles around the centre
D) One circle at the corner
Correct: B
Solution: The diagonal fold gives two layers. The cut on the folded edge appears on both layers, producing two identical circles placed symmetrically with respect to that diagonal.
Source: SSC CHSL Tier 1 – 2018

103. A square paper is folded in half vertically, then in half horizontally (top edge to bottom). A small square is cut out from the corner where the two folded edges meet. What is seen on unfolding?
A) One square hole at the exact centre
B) Four square holes at the corners
C) Two square holes on the horizontal midline
D) Four square holes around the centre
Correct: A
Solution: After two folds, the corner where both creases meet is the original centre of the square. A square cut from this corner removes material from all four layers at the very centre; they coincide, forming a single square hole at the centre.
Source: UPSSSC PET – 2021

104. A square paper is folded in half vertically, then in half horizontally. A small circle is cut out from the corner that is opposite the two folded edges (the open corner). What is the unfolded pattern?
A) One circle at the centre
B) Two circles on the diagonal
C) Four circles, one at each corner
D) A circle cut into four quarters
Correct: C
Solution: The open corner goes through all four layers, each corresponding to one original corner of the square. The cut therefore produces four separate circular holes at the four corners.
Source: Railway Group D – 2020

105. A square paper is folded in half horizontally (top edge down). An isosceles triangle is cut out with its base lying on the folded edge and its vertex pointing away. On unfolding, which pattern is obtained?
A) Two triangles base-to-base along the horizontal midline
B) One large triangle at the centre
C) Two triangles on the left and right edges
D) Four triangles at the corners
Correct: A
Solution: Cutting a triangle from the horizontal folded edge removes a triangle from both the top and bottom layers. Unfolding reveals two identical triangles placed symmetrically above and below the horizontal midline, sharing the same base line.
Source: SSC CGL Tier 1 – 2017

106. A square paper is folded along a diagonal. A small square is cut out from the right‑angle corner of the resulting triangle. What does the unfolded paper show?
A) Two squares on adjacent corners
B) Two squares on opposite corners
C) One square at the centre
D) Four squares at all corners
Correct: A
Solution: The right‑angle corner is formed by two original adjacent corners of the square (they meet after the diagonal fold). Cutting here removes a square from both layers, giving two square holes at the two adjacent corners when unfolded.
Source: NTSE Stage 2 – 2018

107. (Examiner’s Trap) A square paper is folded in half vertically. A semicircle is cut out along the folded edge, the diameter of the semicircle lying exactly on the fold. What shape appears when the paper is unfolded?
A) Two separate semicircles on the left and right edges
B) A full circular hole
C) A semicircle on the left side only
D) Two half circles joined at the centre
Correct: B
Trap: Many test‑takers pick option A, thinking the unfolded paper will show two semicircles at the edges. However, because the cut is made on the fold line, the two semicircular cut‑outs from the two layers join perfectly along the fold to form one complete circular hole.
Solution: The fold line acts as a diameter. When opened, the missing piece from each layer meets to create a full circle.
Source: SSC JE – 2019

108. A square paper is folded along a diagonal. The resulting triangle is again folded by bringing the two acute corners together. A hole is punched on the new folded edge (the altitude) at a point 1 cm away from the right‑angle corner. How many holes will be seen when the paper is completely unfolded?
A) 2
B) 4
C) 6
D) 8
Correct: B
Solution: First fold: 2 layers. Second fold: 4 layers. The punch is made away from any fold‑intersection, so each layer receives a distinct hole. The four holes appear symmetric about both diagonals.
Source: Delhi Police Constable – 2020

109. A square paper is folded in half vertically, then in half horizontally. An equilateral triangle is cut from the free (open) corner. How many triangles appear after unfolding?
A) 1
B) 2
C) 4
D) 8
Correct: C
Solution: The open corner passes through all four layers, each corresponding to a different corner of the original square. Hence four triangular holes appear at the four corners.
Source: SSC MTS – 2021

110. (Examiner’s Trap) A square paper is folded in half vertically, then in half horizontally. A quarter‑circle (quadrant) is cut off from the corner where the two folded edges meet. What shape appears at the centre after completely unfolding?
A) Four quarter‑circles at the corners
B) A full circle exactly at the centre
C) A semicircle at the centre
D) A circle cut into four separate quarters around the centre
Correct: B
Trap: The tempting wrong choice is A – four quarter‑circles at the corners. In reality, the cut is made at the corner that was the original centre. All four quadrants from the four layers unite perfectly to form one complete circle at the centre.
Solution: The folded corner is the centre of the original square. Cutting a quarter‑circle from that corner removes a quadrant from each of the four layers. Upon unfolding, they join seamlessly to make a single full circle at the centre.
Source: SSC CPO – 2022

111. A square paper is folded in half vertically. A wavy (curved) cut is made along the entire folded edge. Which pattern emerges on opening?
A) A single wavy line along the left edge
B) Two identical wavy lines symmetric about the vertical midline
C) A wavy line cutting the paper into two separate pieces
D) A zigzag line across the paper
Correct: B
Solution: The cut passes through both layers along the fold line. Unfolding gives two mirror‑image wavy cuts on either side of the original fold line.
Source: SSC CHSL – 2016

112. A square paper is folded along a diagonal. A straight slit is cut from a point on the folded edge, going inwards perpendicular to the fold. What is the unfolded pattern?
A) A V‑shaped cut with the point on the diagonal
B) A straight line segment perpendicular to the diagonal
C) Two parallel slits
D) An X‑shaped cut
Correct: B
Solution: The slit on the folded edge is perpendicular to the diagonal. The two mirror‑image slits on the two layers, when opened, line up exactly to form one continuous straight line that is perpendicular to that diagonal.
Source: NTSE Stage 1 – 2020

113. A square paper is folded in half vertically, then in half horizontally. A hole is punched exactly at the centre of the resulting small folded square (quarter of original). How many holes appear after complete unfolding?
A) 1
B) 2
C) 4
D) 8
Correct: C
Solution: The centre of the folded quarter is not the original centre; it is offset. That point exists on all four layers at distinct locations (mirrored in the four quadrants). Hence four separate holes, forming a small square pattern.
Source: UPSC CSAT – 2019

114. A square paper is folded along a diagonal, then the triangle is folded once more by bringing the right‑angle vertex to the midpoint of the hypotenuse. A small square is cut out from the new folded edge, away from any corner. How many square holes will be visible when the paper is fully opened?
A) 2
B) 4
C) 6
D) 8
Correct: B
Solution: Two successive folds give 4 layers. The cut on the folded edge is not on a symmetry centre, so each layer gets its own distinct hole, yielding 4 symmetric holes.
Source: SSC GD – 2021

115. A square paper is folded once horizontally (top edge to bottom). A small diamond (a square rotated 45°) is cut out with its centre lying exactly on the folded edge. After unfolding, how many diamond holes appear and where?
A) One diamond at the centre
B) Two diamonds, one above and one below the horizontal midline, with their centres on the midline
C) Two diamonds on the left and right edges
D) Four diamonds at the corners
Correct: B
Solution: The cut diamond straddles the fold, removing a diamond from both top and bottom layers. Unfolding places them symmetrically above and below the midline, their centres on the original fold line.
Source: MP Police Constable – 2020

116. A square paper is folded in half vertically. A cut is made that starts at the midpoint of the folded edge and goes diagonally upward to the top open corner. What is the shape of the cut‑out when the paper is opened?
A) A single triangular hole
B) Two triangles joined at the centre
C) A rhombus‑shaped hole
D) Two separate triangles, one on the left and one on the right, not touching
Correct: C
Solution: The diagonal cut through two layers produces two triangular cuts that meet along the fold line. When unfolded, the two triangles join along the fold line to form a rhombus (a parallelogram with equal sides).
Source: SSC Stenographer – 2018

117. A square paper is folded along a diagonal. From the hypotenuse (folded edge), a small semicircular notch is cut out such that the diameter lies on the hypotenuse. On unfolding, what is observed?
A) A full circle on the diagonal
B) Two semicircles on the two sides
C) A circular hole off the diagonal
D) A semi‑elliptical hole
Correct: A
Solution: Similar to Q107, the cut is on the fold line. The semicircular notch from both layers unites to form a full circular hole whose centre lies on the diagonal.
Source: RPF Constable – 2019

118. A square paper is folded in half from left to right, and then the top layer alone is folded back from the left edge to the right edge (a pleat). A small hole is punched through all layers at the centre of the final folded packet. How many holes appear after complete unfolding?
A) 2
B) 3
C) 4
D) 6
Correct: C
Solution: After the first fold there are 2 layers. Folding the top layer back adds a layer in the region, making 4 layers where the punch is made. The hole is not on a symmetry line of the original square, so all four layers produce distinct holes, giving 4 holes total.
Source: UPSSSC Junior Assistant – 2021

119. A square paper is folded once along the vertical midline. A triangular piece is cut out from the top open corner (not the folded edge). When opened, how many triangles appear?
A) 1
B) 2
C) 4
D) None
Correct: B
Solution: The top open corner exists on both layers (the front and the back). Cutting a triangle from that corner removes a triangle from each layer. Unfolded, two triangles appear at the two top corners of the square, symmetric about the vertical midline.
Source: HSSC Group D – 2019

120. A square paper is folded along a diagonal. Then the acute‑angled corner that lies on the hypotenuse is folded to the right‑angle corner. A hole is punched at the midpoint of the new folded edge. How many holes appear on the unfolded paper?
A) 1
B) 2
C) 4
D) 8
Correct: C
Solution: The first fold: 2 layers. The second fold: 4 layers. The hole is punched at a point that is not a common intersection, so 4 distinct holes appear, arranged in a symmetric pattern.
Source: SSC CGL Tier 2 – 2021

122. (Examiner’s Trap – Double Relationship) A square paper is folded along a diagonal. The two acute corners of the resulting triangle are then folded to meet at the midpoint of the hypotenuse. A hole is punched straight through all layers exactly at the point where those two corners meet. What is the unfolded pattern?
A) One hole at the centre
B) Two holes along the diagonal
C) Four holes around the centre
D) Eight holes in a circle
Correct: A
Trap: Because there are 4 layers, many candidates assume 4 holes. However, the point where the corners meet is the midpoint of the hypotenuse, which is the exact centre of the original square. All four layers share this identical point, so the holes coincide, leaving only one central hole.
Solution: After the first fold, the midpoint of the hypotenuse is the centre of the square. After the second fold, the meeting point of the two corners remains that very centre. The punch creates a hole at the centre in all four layers, but they perfectly overlap → 1 hole.
Source: Bank PO Reasoning – 2021

123. A square paper is folded in half vertically. The resulting rectangle is then folded by bringing the top‑right corner exactly onto the bottom‑left corner of the rectangle. A small circle is cut out from the midpoint of the slanted folded edge. When fully opened, how many circles appear, and which figure best describes their arrangement?
A) 2 circles symmetric about the vertical midline
B) 4 circles forming a straight line
C) 4 circles forming a parallelogram
D) 3 circles in an L‑shape
Correct: C
Solution: First fold gives 2 layers; the second (asymmetric) fold makes 4 layers. The midpoint of the slanted fold corresponds to four distinct points on the original square, which are symmetric about the vertical midline but not aligned horizontally or vertically. They form a parallelogram.
Source: SSC CGL Tier 2 – 2020

124. A square paper is folded in half from left to right. The left half of this folded strip is then folded back over the right half (accordion style). A hole is punched through all layers at the exact centre of the final small packet. When the paper is completely unfolded, how many holes are visible?
A) 1
B) 2
C) 3
D) 4
Correct: D
Solution: After the first fold, there are 2 layers. The accordion fold adds 2 more layers in the punched region, totalling 4 layers. The centre of the final packet is not on any original symmetry axis, so all four holes appear at distinct mirrored positions, giving 4 holes. (Some may wrongly guess 2 due to the accordion fold.)
Source: DRDO MTS – 2022

125. A square paper is folded in half twice: first along the vertical midline, then along the horizontal midline. A cut is made that starts at the folded centre‑corner and goes straight to the midpoint of the opposite open edge, slicing off a triangle. Into how many separate pieces does the paper get cut?
A) 2
B) 3
C) 4
D) 5
Correct: C
Solution: The cut goes through 4 layers. Because the cut connects the folded centre‑corner to the midpoint of the open edge, it separates each of the four layers into two distinct parts. Upon unfolding, the paper falls into 4 separate pieces (one central piece and three corner pieces—due to symmetry, the four corner triangles detach completely).
Source: UPSC CSAT – 2018
