Flashcard 1
Front: What’s the trick to instantly untangle a series that seems to jump around randomly?
Back: Split it into odd and even positions — they’re often two separate, simple series. This is the “Split and High-Five” trick.

Flashcard 2
Front: You see: 3, 21, 5, 18, 7, 15, ?, ?. What’s your first move?
Back: Separate odd positions (3, 5, 7 → +2 → 9) and even positions (21, 18, 15 → –3 → 12). Answer: 9, 12.

Flashcard 3
Front: How can you recall the alphabet positions of E, J, O, T, Y forever?
Back: Mnemonic EJOTY: E=5, J=10, O=15, T=20, Y=25 — all multiples of 5. Count forward/backward from these anchors.

Flashcard 4
Front: What’s the quick rule to find any letter’s opposite in the alphabet?
Back: The sum of their positions is always 27. Subtract the letter’s position from 27. E.g., opposite of H(8) is 27–8=19=S.

Flashcard 5
Front: What’s a common examiner trick when options include two numbers that both could fit a different pattern?
Back: They design a series where two different rules lead to numbers present in the options. Always choose the pattern that applies uniformly to all terms, not just the first two.

Flashcard 6
Front: Why might a series with symbols like @3, #6, $9, %12 confuse you, and what should you focus on?
Back: Symbols are just decoration — ignore them and focus on the number pattern (here: +3 each time) and maybe the symbol sequence separately.

Flashcard 7
Front: How do you crack a number series where the gap keeps changing, e.g., 4, 6, 10, 16, 24, ?
Back: Check the differences: +2, +4, +6, +8. Those differences form a pattern (+2 each time), so next difference is +10 → answer 34. It's a second-order series.

Flashcard 8
Front: What’s the secret sign that a series involves squares?
Back: Look for numbers like 1, 4, 9, 16, 25, 36... or numbers that are exactly 1 less/more than squares (e.g., 0, 3, 8, 15 = n²–1).

Flashcard 9
Front: When you see an alphanumeric series like A2, D4, G8, J16, what’s the first thing to do?
Back: Split letters and numbers. Letters: A(1), D(4), G(7), J(10) → +3 → M(13). Numbers: 2, 4, 8, 16 → ×2 → 32. Answer: M32.

Flashcard 10
Front: In a 3×3 grid with one missing number, where should you look first for the pattern?
Back: Check row-wise: does first + second = third? If not, try multiplication, then columns, and finally diagonals or combined operations.

Flashcard 11
Front: What’s the special rule in a series like 1, 1, 2, 3, 5, 8, ?
Back: Each term is the sum of the previous two — the Fibonacci sequence. Next term: 8+5=13.

Flashcard 12
Front: How do you recognize a prime number series?
Back: Each number is divisible only by 1 and itself: 2, 3, 5, 7, 11, 13, 17... (Note: 1 is not prime).

Flashcard 13
Front: Without memorizing a chart, how do you find the opposite of letter C quickly?
Back: C=3, 27–3=24=X. So opposite is X. Use the “sum to 27” rule for any letter.

Flashcard 14
Front: If a series alternates two operations like ×2+1 and +3, what’s the best way to handle it?
Back: Treat it as a double series. Write the operations for odd and even positions separately and solve each mini-series.

Flashcard 15
Front: Why is it dangerous to assume a pattern after just looking at the first two terms?
Back: A true pattern must hold for every term. Always verify with at least three pairs of consecutive terms before finalizing the rule.

Flashcard 16
Front: What does the mnemonic EJOTY help you quickly find?
Back: The position of any letter in the alphabet in seconds. E=5, J=10, O=15, T=20, Y=25; count from these anchors.

Flashcard 17
Front: If you see a series 3, 10, 5, 20, 7, 30, ?, ?, what trick will save you?
Back: Split into odd (3,5,7,... → +2 → 9) and even (10,20,30,... → +10 → 40). Answer: 9, 40.

Flashcard 18
Front: What is the “Split and High-Five” trick in missing term series?
Back: When a series seems chaotic, split it into two (odd and even positions) and find each pattern separately — then high-five yourself for cracking it.
