🧠 Mnemonics for Missing Term Rules & Methods

1. The "Plus-Minus" Pattern Song
When the difference between terms is constant.
🎵 "Add the same, subtract the same, life is simple, play the game!"
Whenever you see 5, 10, 15, 20... just sing this and remember: constant gap = add the fixed number.

2. The "Gap of Gaps" Detective
*For second-order differences (like 2, 6, 12, 20, 30... where gaps are +4, +6, +8, +10).*
🔍 "First gap didn't work? Check the gap of the gap — the hidden pattern lives there."
Think of yourself as Sherlock Holmes: the first clue (difference) is a decoy; the real pattern is in the second layer.

3. The "Double Date" Trick for Double Series
When the series alternates two patterns (e.g., 3, 21, 5, 18, 7, 15...)
❤️ "Odd and Even go on a date, but they never share a plate. Split them apart, you'll find their heart."
Make two lists: 1st, 3rd, 5th terms on the left, 2nd, 4th, 6th on the right. Like a dating app for numbers.

4. The "EJOTY" Cheerleader Chant
*To remember alphabet positions: E=5, J=10, O=15, T=20, Y=25.*
📣 "Every Joker On Tinder Yells!"
Or the Hindi twist: "E Ji Oye Toh Yummy!"
Once you have these anchors, you can bounce forward or backward to find any letter's position in seconds.

5. The "27 Club" for Opposite Letters
The sum of any letter and its opposite is always 27.
💔 "A+Z = 27, B+Y = 27... They're soulmates but never meet — sum always 27."
If you see H, its opposite is 27 - 8 = 19 = S. Just like a couple that always adds to the magic number.

6. The "Multiply or Die" Monster
For multiplication series (3, 12, 48, 192...)
👾 "Terms are hungry, eating numbers — ×2, ×3, ×4... If it grows too fast, multiplication's the beast!"
When numbers explode (2, 6, 30, 210...), it's usually a chain of multipliers (primes, odd numbers, etc.).

7. The "Square Dance" Mnemonic
Squares and cubes patterns (1, 4, 9, 16, 25...)
💃 "1², 2², 3², 4² — do the square dance on the floor! 1³, 2³, 3³, 4³ — now you're a cube, care to dance with me?"
Link the shape: square = flat (n²), cube = 3D (n³). Visualize the dance to recall which is which.

8. The "Fibonacci Rabbit" Story
*Fibonacci: each term = sum of previous two (1, 1, 2, 3, 5, 8...)*
🐇 "Two rabbits meet, then three, then five, then eight — they multiply without mating!"
The classic rabbit population story makes it unforgettable. Every pair breeds a new pair.

9. The "Prime Time" Alarm
Prime number series: 2, 3, 5, 7, 11, 13...
⏰ "Prime time TV starts at 2, goes to 3, skips 4, goes 5, 7, 11... You can't divide them, they're the drama queens of the number world!"
Link with "prime time television" — the numbers that can't be broken.

10. The "Matrix Parent Trap"
For 3x3 matrix missing numbers.
🎭 "Rows say 'Follow me', Columns whisper 'Look at me!' — don't be fooled, check both!"
The examiner often hides the pattern in columns when rows look tempting. Sing this to remind yourself to check both directions.

11. The "Alphanumeric Split" Magic Trick
For A2, C4, E8, G16...
🪄 "Letters and numbers — like magician's two rings, separate them to see the trick!"
Say "Abracadabra!" and mentally cut the letter and number apart. Each follows its own simple rule.

12. The "Wrap-around" Reminder for Alphabet
*When alphabet position exceeds 26 (e.g., Y + 4 = 26+4? → 30, 30-26=4=D).*
🌍 "When the alphabet ends, it doesn't stop — it wraps around like a globe!"
If you reach Z, don't panic; subtract 26 and land at A again.

13. The "Difference Ladder" Climbing Rule
*Differences that increase: +4, +9, +16 (squares), or +2, +4, +6 (even numbers).*
🪜 "If the gap isn't flat, climb the ladder — see what's under each rung."
Think of each difference as a rung on a ladder; the steps between the differences (second-order) will reveal the pattern.

14. The "N³ ± 1" Cookie Jar
*Series like 0, 7, 26, 63 (n³ - 1) or 2, 9, 28, 65 (n³ + 1).*
🍪 "The cube cookie jar is always missing or adding just one cookie."
If numbers look like cubes but off by 1, check n³ ± 1 immediately.

15. The "Operation Circus" Master Key
*For mixed operation series (e.g., ×2 +1, ×3 -2, etc.).*
🎪 "The ringmaster changes the act every time — spot the sequence of operations!"
In series like 2, 5, 12, 27 (×2+1, ×2+2, ×2+3), the multiplier might be constant while the added number changes. Identify the ringmaster's pattern.

🚀 Skip Strategy for Missing Term Questions

The 15-Second "First Glance" Rule

If you cannot spot the pattern (constant difference, simple ×/÷, obvious squares) within 15 seconds, mark the question and skip.

Most easy/medium questions reveal themselves quickly. Staring longer often leads to frustration and time waste.

The "Multi-Layer" Warning — Skip if 2+ Operations Intertwine

If a series involves alternating patterns (double series), or changing multipliers/additions (like ×1+1, ×2+2...), and you haven't cracked it in 45 seconds, skip.

These are high-trap questions designed to consume time. You can return with a fresh mind later.

The "Matrix Time Trap" Trigger

In a 3×3 matrix, if you've checked rows and columns and still can't find a logical connection in 30-40 seconds, skip.

Matrix questions sometimes require an operation like "a² + b²" or "(a × b) + a". If it doesn't click, it's not worth the guess; you'll likely get it wrong anyway.
