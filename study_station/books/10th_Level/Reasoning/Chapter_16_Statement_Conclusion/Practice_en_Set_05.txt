📝 Practice Set 5/6: Statement & Conclusion (Basic)

Total Questions: 25 (Q101–Q120 Medium, Q121–Q125 Hard)
Instructions: In each question, one or more statements are given followed by two conclusions I and II. Assume the statements to be true, even if they conflict with commonly known facts. Decide which of the conclusions logically follow(s). Mark:

(A) Only conclusion I follows

(B) Only conclusion II follows

(C) Both I and II follow

(D) Neither I nor II follows

101. Statement: Some trees are green. All green things are natural.
Conclusions:
I. Some natural things are trees.
II. Some trees are not green.
Options: (A) Only I (B) Only II (C) Both (D) None
Answer: (A) Only I follows
Solution: I follows from chaining and conversion: Some trees are green → some green are trees; those green are natural → some natural are trees. II is the 'Some not' trap — 'Some' does not guarantee 'Some not'.
Source: PYQ-style

102. Statement: All that is white is not milk.
Conclusions:
I. Some white things are milk.
II. Some white things are not milk.
Options: (A) Only I (B) Only II (C) Both (D) None
Answer: (B) Only II follows
Solution: The idiomatic statement means "Not all white things are milk," i.e., some white things are not milk. II follows. I is not definite.

105. Statement: Some actors are dancers. Some dancers are singers.

Options: (A) Only I (B) Only II (C) Either I or II (D) Neither I nor II
Answer: (C) Either I or II follows

121. (Hard) Statement: All pens are markers. Some markers are not permanent. No permanent thing is erasable.
Conclusions:
I. Some pens are permanent.
II. Some pens are not erasable.
Options: (A) Only I (B) Only II (C) Both (D) None
Answer: (D) Neither I nor II follows
Solution: Pens are a subset of markers. The markers that are not permanent could be the only markers, and no pen might be permanent. So I is not definite. For II: even if pens are not permanent, we don't know if they are erasable (non-permanent doesn't imply erasable; erasable is defined only for permanent things: no permanent thing is erasable, but non-permanent things could be erasable or not). No link. Hence, neither follows.
Source: PYQ-style

122. (Hard) Statement: Some teachers are not patient. All patient teachers are good. No good teacher is rude.
Conclusions:
I. Some rude teachers are not patient.
II. Some good teachers are not rude.
Options: (A) Only I (B) Only II (C) Both (D) None
Answer: (D) Neither I nor II follows
Solution: Existential trap: The statements do not assert the existence of any rude teacher. So even if "all patient teachers are good" and "no good teacher is rude" imply that patient teachers are not rude, we cannot conclude anything about rude teachers without assuming there exists at least one rude teacher. Similarly, II needs the existence of a good teacher, which is not guaranteed (we are not told there is any patient teacher). Hence, neither conclusion follows.
Source: Bank PO Mains 2020 (Examiner's Trap: existential fallacy)

123. (Hard) Statement: Some birds can swim. All swimmers are animals. Some animals are mammals.
Conclusions:
I. Some birds are mammals.
II. Some mammals are not birds.
Options: (A) Only I (B) Only II (C) Both (D) None
Answer: (D) Neither I nor II follows
Solution: From the statements, birds that can swim are animals. But those animals may or may not be mammals; no link is forced. So I not definite. II: "Some animals are mammals" does not specify that those mammals are not birds (they could logically be birds if birds are a type of animal). So no definite conclusion.
Source: PYQ-style

124. (Hard) Statement: All chocolates are sweets. Some sweets are not healthy. No healthy thing is cheap.
Conclusions:
I. Some chocolates are not cheap.
II. Some chocolates are cheap.
Options: (A) Only I (B) Only II (C) Either I or II (D) Neither I nor II
Answer: (D) Neither I nor II follows
Solution: Chocolates are sweets. The 'some not healthy' sweets could be non-chocolates. Also, cheapness is related to unhealthy things? We only know healthy things are not cheap; unhealthy things could be cheap or expensive. So no definite link to chocolates. Moreover, I and II are not complementary because both could be true (some cheap chocolates, some not cheap) or both false (no chocolates exist? but assumed existence). Since they can both be true, they do not form an either-or pair. So (D).
Source: PYQ-style
