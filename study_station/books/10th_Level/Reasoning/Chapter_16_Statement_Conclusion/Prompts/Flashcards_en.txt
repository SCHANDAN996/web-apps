🃏 Flashcards: Statement & Conclusion (Basic)

Card 1
❓ Q: I am the information you must accept as 100% true without any question. What am I?
✅ A: Statement (कथन)

Card 2
❓ Q: I am a sentence that must forcefully and inevitably follow from the given Statement. What am I?
✅ A: Conclusion (निष्कर्ष)

Card 3
❓ Q: "All cats are animals." Can I definitely say "Some animals are cats"?
✅ A: Yes. "All" always contains "Some." This is a valid conversion.

Card 4
❓ Q: "Some pens are red." Can I definitely say "Some pens are NOT red"?
✅ A: No! This is the biggest trap. "Some" means at least one, but it could be all. So "some not" is never definite.

Card 5
❓ Q: "No river is a mountain." Can I convert this to "No mountain is a river"?
✅ A: Yes. "No A is B" always converts to "No B is A."

Card 6
❓ Q: "All apples are fruits." Can I definitely say "All fruits are apples"?
✅ A: No. This is invalid reversal. Fruits could include mangoes, bananas, etc.

Card 7
❓ Q: What is the golden rule when reading any Statement?
✅ A: Be a ROBOT. Only use the data given in the statement. Delete all outside knowledge, assumptions, and real-world facts.

Card 8
❓ Q: What does the mnemonic T.E.A. stand for?
✅ A: T – True as per statement only. E – Eliminate outside knowledge. A – Avoid "Some not" from "Some."

Card 9
❓ Q: I am the trick where you turn into a machine that only knows what's typed into it. What am I called?
✅ A: Robot Brain Trick (रोबोट मित्र ट्रिक)

Card 10
❓ Q: What are the three types of basic relationships tested in Statement & Conclusion (Basic)?
✅ A: 1. All A are B. 2. Some A are B. 3. No A is B.

Card 11
❓ Q: "Some students play cricket." Can I conclude "All students play cricket"?
✅ A: No. "Some" does not guarantee "All." It's only a possibility, not a certainty.

Card 12
❓ Q: If a conclusion is true only in some possible diagrams but false in others, is it a valid conclusion?
✅ A: No. A valid conclusion must be true in every possible diagram or situation.

Card 13
❓ Q: What is the one phrase you should NEVER derive from a "Some" statement?
✅ A: "Some not." This is the examiner's favorite trap.

Card 14
❓ Q: ⚠️ Examiner's Trap: The statement says, "Some lawyers are honest." The conclusion says, "Some lawyers are not honest." Why is this wrong?
✅ A: Because "Some" includes the possibility of "All." If all lawyers are honest, the statement is still true but the conclusion becomes false. So it's not definite.

Card 15
❓ Q: ⚠️ Examiner's Trap: "Persons who are not criminals are trustworthy." What should you do when you see such heavy phrasing?
✅ A: Mentally translate it to simple language: "Innocent persons are trustworthy." Don't get scared by complex wording. The logic remains the same.

Card 16
❓ Q: What is the Venn Diagram test for "Some A are B"?
✅ A: Two circles that partially overlap. But remember — one circle completely inside the other also satisfies "Some." Check both possibilities.

Card 17
❓ Q: "No A is B." Can I conclude "Some A are B"?
✅ A: Never. If no connection exists, any positive link is impossible.

Card 18
❓ Q: What question should you ask yourself before accepting any conclusion?
✅ A: "If the statement is true, is this conclusion FORCED to be true? Or can I imagine a situation where the statement is true but this conclusion fails?"

Card 19
❓ Q: What is the "Yellow Elephant" memory trick?
✅ A: "Yellow Elephant only sees its own space, nothing outside." Just like that, a student must only see the statement, nothing from the outside world.

Card 20
❓ Q: After "All A are B," which ONE conclusion is always safe?
✅ A: "Some B are A." Also, "Some A are B" is implicit. Nothing more is guaranteed.
