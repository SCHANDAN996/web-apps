Q1. (Easy) Statements: All roses are flowers.
Conclusions:
I. Some flowers are roses.
II. All flowers are roses.
(A) Only conclusion I follows (B) Only conclusion II follows (C) Both conclusions I and II follow (D) Neither conclusion I nor II follows

Answer: (A) Only conclusion I follows

Solution:
From 'All roses are flowers', the valid conversion is 'Some flowers are roses' (Conclusion I). 'All flowers are roses' is an invalid reversal. Hence, the correct option is (A).

Q4. (Easy) ⚠️ Statements: Some fruits are apples.
Conclusions:
I. Some fruits are not apples.
II. Some apples are fruits.
(A) Only conclusion I follows (B) Neither conclusion I nor II follows (C) Both conclusions I and II follow (D) Only conclusion II follows

Answer: (D) Only conclusion II follows

Solution:
'Some fruits are apples' converts validly to 'Some apples are fruits' (II follows). Examiner's Trap: In formal logic, 'some' means 'at least one' and does not guarantee 'some are not'. Therefore, conclusion I is invalid. Hence, the correct option is (D).

Q5. (Easy) Statements: All lions are predators. All predators are animals.
Conclusions:
I. All lions are animals.
II. Some animals are lions.
(A) Both conclusions I and II follow (B) Only conclusion II follows (C) Only conclusion I follows (D) Neither conclusion I nor II follows

Answer: (A) Both conclusions I and II follow

Solution:
By transitive inclusion, Lions ⊂ Predators ⊂ Animals, so all lions are animals (I follows). Since all lions are animals, some animals are lions (II follows). Hence, the correct option is (A).
