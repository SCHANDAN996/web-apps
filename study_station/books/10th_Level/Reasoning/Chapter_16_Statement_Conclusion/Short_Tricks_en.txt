🪄 Short Tricks + Skip Strategy

🧠 Mnemonic 1: T.E.A. — The Robot’s Drink

T – True as per statement only. If the statement doesn’t say it, it doesn’t exist.

E – Eliminate outside knowledge. Even if you know the sun rises in the east, don’t bring it in.

A – Avoid “Some not” from “Some”. Some means at least one, it can be ALL.

How to remember: Every time you see a conclusion, imagine a robot sipping TEA. The robot’s TEA tastes terrible if you add outside sugar (outside knowledge) or try to make “some not” from “some”.

🤖 Mnemonic 2: The Brainless Robot

Imagine a robot named B.R.A.I.N. who is completely brainless except for one chip that stores the statement. B.R.A.I.N. cannot guess, imagine, or use the real world. If the conclusion needs extra data, the robot short-circuits. Conclusion: If the robot wouldn’t say “yes”, you say “no”.

🔁 Mnemonic 3: Conversion RAP — “All to Some, Some to Some, No to No”

All A are B ➜ Some B are A (The BIG turns into a SMALL on the other side)

Some A are B ➜ Some B are A (It’s a tango — partners just swap)

No A is B ➜ No B is A (Neither side wants the other — totally mutual)

How to remember: Rap it like a song: “All to some, some to some, no to no — just flip ’em and you’re ready to go!”

🕳️ Mnemonic 4: The Black Hole of “Some Not”

“Some” is a gentle giant. It says “at least one”. But the examiner wants you to create a black hole by saying “Some are NOT”.
Never go from “Some” to “Some not” — that black hole swallows your marks. Every time you see “Some” and then a conclusion with “Some not”, scream “BLACK HOLE!” and reject it.

🎻 Mnemonic 5: The Either-Or Violin

When two conclusions are “Some” and “No” (or “Some” and “No” linking the same two terms), and you cannot prove either definitely but they cover all possibilities, they become an either-or pair. Imagine a violin string — if one note is not played, the other must be heard. If you see complementary pairs (I: Some, II: No), think “Either I or II plays the tune”.

📦 Mnemonic 6: “Only a Few” Birthday Box

“Only a few” is a birthday gift box: it contains both “Some” (the gift) and “Some not” (the empty space). So “Only a few A are B” always gives:

Some A are B

Some A are not B.
When you open the box, both come out.

💯 Mnemonic 7: Certainty vs. Possibility — The Locked Safe

A conclusion is a locked safe. If the statement gives you the exact combination (100% certainty), the safe opens. If you think “it might be”, the safe remains locked. In Basic level, only open the safe if the combination is exact — no “may”, “could”, “might” allowed (unless the question specifically asks for possibility). Think: “No guessing the code.”

🔴🔵 Mnemonic 8: Venn Diagram — The Google Maps Overlay

You are Google Maps. The statement draws two routes (circles). The conclusion must be true in every possible traffic view (every possible Venn diagram). If the conclusion is false on even one alternate route, it’s a traffic jam — don’t take that path. Always check: “Is this true in all possible circle arrangements?”

🥇 Mnemonic 9: The Gold That Glitters — The Shakespeare Trick

“All that glitters is not gold” means “Not everything that glitters is gold.” Logically, it becomes: “Some glittering things are not gold.” So whenever you see an idiomatic statement, translate it into “Some … are not …”. Then the conclusion “Some … are not …” follows, but “No…” does not. Imagine Shakespeare holding a spray-painted brick: it glitters but isn’t gold.

🪓 Mnemonic 10: The Axe of Outside Knowledge — “Chop It!”

The moment you read a statement, a little voice in your head will whisper real-world facts. That’s the examiner’s trap. Carry an imaginary axe. Chop that voice! “Chop the real-world knowledge!” The only world is the statement’s world.

🚫 Mnemonic 11: Negative + Some not = No Link

If a statement is negative (No, Some not) and you have only “Some not” premise, you cannot jump to a positive link. Imagine two islands: “Some not” island doesn’t have a bridge to “All” or “Some positive”. You can’t walk across. So if the conclusion tries to build a positive bridge from “Some not”, it collapses.

🗝️ Mnemonic 12: The Master Key — “In Every Possible World”

Before ticking a conclusion, ask: “Is this forced in every possible world of the statement?” If even one imaginary world disagrees, the conclusion is invalid. Master key: “One world false = conclusion false.”

⏭️ Skip Strategy Tips

15-Second Fog Rule: If after 15 seconds you still don’t understand the logical chain or the pattern (e.g., multiple “some not” and “only a few” with 3+ statements), mark for review and skip immediately. Return only if time permits and your mind is fresh.

Idiom Hazard Rule: If a statement contains a heavy idiom like “All that glitters is not gold” or a negated proverb, and you cannot translate it to a clear “Some not” form within 15 seconds, skip. The translation error will cost you the question.

Mental Fog Rule: If you find yourself drawing the third Venn diagram because you’re confused, you’ve already spent 45+ seconds. The question is a time trap. Skip. There’s a high chance you’ll still get it wrong due to overthinking. Move to a fresh, direct question.
