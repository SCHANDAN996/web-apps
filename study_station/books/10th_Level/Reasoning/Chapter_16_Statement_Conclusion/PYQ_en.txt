📊 PYQ Analysis: Statement & Conclusion (Basic) — Exam Pattern

1. How Exams Use This Chapter

Statement & Conclusion (syllogism-based deduction) is a regular feature of the reasoning sections of SSC CGL, SSC CHSL, RRB NTPC, Bank PO/Clerk, and SSC MTS. Questions appear in almost every cycle of these exams, usually as a small but reliable cluster within the logical reasoning portion.

What examiners consistently test:
- Direct conversion: "All A are B" → "Some B are A" (valid) vs "All B are A" (invalid).
- Transitive chains: All A are B, All B are C → All A are C.
- Disjoint sets: No A is B, All C are B → No C is A.
- Complementary (Either-Or) pairs: "Some A are B" vs "No A is B" when neither is independently certain.
- Possibility vs certainty: a conclusion that *could* be true is not the same as one that *must* be true.

2. Common Examiner Traps

- Invalid reversal: treating "All A are B" as if it means "All B are A".
- Inferring "Some not" from "Some": "Some A are B" says nothing about the remaining A.
- Positive conclusions from negative premises: "No A is B" cannot yield a definite "Some A are C".
- Word-swap traps: the conclusion uses a similar-looking but different word (astronomer vs astronaut).
- Real-world bias: using outside knowledge instead of strictly following the given statements.
- Missing the Either-Or pair and answering "neither follows" when the two conclusions are contradictory complements.

3. High-Yield Practice Questions with Solutions

🔹 Question 1
Statements: No police officer is a doctor. Some doctors are specialists. All engineers are doctors.
Conclusions:
I. Some engineers are police officers.
II. No engineer is a police officer.
III. Some doctors are engineers.
Solution:
I is false: All engineers are inside doctors, and no doctor is a police officer, so no engineer can be a police officer.
II is true: From the above reasoning, no engineer is a police officer.
III is true: Since all engineers are doctors, it directly follows that some doctors are engineers.
Answer: Only II and III follow.
Psychological Trap: Conclusion I tempts students because "Some" feels safe. Never guess without visualizing the sets.
⏱️ Time Rule: 45 seconds (three conclusions).
Source: PYQ-style

🔹 Question 2
Statements: All Hindus are God fearing. No Japanese is a Hindu.
Conclusions:
I. The Japanese are not God fearing.
II. All God fearing are Hindus.
Solution:
I is false: Japanese are not Hindus, but that does not preclude them from being God fearing through other avenues.
II is false: "All A are B" does not convert to "All B are A".
Answer: Neither I nor II follows.
Psychological Trap: Classic "Invalid Reversal" trap.
⏱️ Time Rule: 15 seconds.
Source: PYQ-style

🔹 Question 3
Statements: All astronomers are scientists. Some scientists are pilots.
Conclusions:
I. Some astronauts are scientists.
II. Some pilots are astronomers.
Solution:
I cannot be verified: The statement mentions astronomers, not astronauts (word-swap trap).
II is uncertain: Astronomers and pilots are both connected to scientists, but may not overlap with each other.
Answer: Neither I nor II follows.
⏱️ Time Rule: 15 seconds.
Source: PYQ-style

🔹 Question 4
Statements: Some books are papers. All papers are files.
Conclusions:
I. Some books are files.
II. All files are books.
Solution:
I is true: Books overlap with papers, and all papers are files, so files necessarily contain that overlapping part of books.
II is false: Over-generalization.
Answer: Only conclusion I follows.
⏱️ Time Rule: 15 seconds.
Source: PYQ-style

🔹 Question 5
Statements: All rivers are water. Some water is clean.
Conclusions:
I. Some rivers are clean.
II. No river is clean.
Solution:
Rivers and clean water may or may not overlap. Individually, neither conclusion is certain. However, I ("Some are") and II ("No is") form a complementary contradictory pair. Thus, either I or II must be true.
Answer: Either I or II follows.
Psychological Trap: Missing the complementary pair and incorrectly choosing "neither follows".
⏱️ Time Rule: 30 seconds.
Source: PYQ-style

🔹 Question 6
Statements: All roses are flowers. No flower is green.
Conclusions:
I. No rose is green.
II. Some flowers are roses.
Solution:
I is true: Since all roses are inside flowers, and no flower is green, no rose can be green.
II is true: "All roses are flowers" validly converts to "Some flowers are roses".
Answer: Both conclusions I and II follow.
⏱️ Time Rule: 20 seconds.
Source: PYQ-style

🔹 Question 7
Statements: Some mobile phones are computers. Some computers are tablets.
Conclusions:
I. Some mobile phones are tablets.
II. No mobile phone is a tablet.
Solution:
Mobile phones and tablets may or may not overlap. Neither conclusion follows independently. However, they form an Either-Or complementary pair.
Answer: Either I or II follows.
⏱️ Time Rule: 25 seconds.
Source: PYQ-style

🔹 Question 8
Statements: All cats are animals. All animals are mammals.
Conclusions:
I. All cats are mammals.
II. Some mammals are cats.
Solution:
I is true: Transitive inclusion: Cats ⊂ Animals ⊂ Mammals.
II is true: Valid conversion of "All cats are mammals".
Answer: Both conclusions I and II follow.
⏱️ Time Rule: 15 seconds.
Source: PYQ-style

🔹 Question 9
Statements: Some cupboards are chairs. All chairs are tables.
Conclusions:
I. Some cupboards are tables.
II. Some tables are chairs.
Solution:
I is true: Cupboards overlap with chairs, which are entirely inside tables.
II is true: Since all chairs are tables, some tables must be chairs.
Answer: Both conclusions I and II follow.
Psychological Trap: Overthinking simple transitive deductions.
⏱️ Time Rule: 20 seconds.
Source: PYQ-style

🔹 Question 10
Statements: No plastic is metal. All iron is metal. Some plastic is wood.
Conclusions:
I. No iron is plastic.
II. Some wood is not metal.
Solution:
I is true: Iron is inside metal, and metal is completely disjoint from plastic.
II is true: The portion of wood that is plastic cannot be metal.
Answer: Both conclusions I and II follow.
⏱️ Time Rule: 30 seconds.
Source: PYQ-style
