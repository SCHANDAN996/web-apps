51. Choose the odd one out.
(a) EV
(b) GT
(c) HS
(d) IQ
Answer: (d) IQ
Solution: Opposite letter pairs should sum to 27. E(5)+V(22)=27, G(7)+T(20)=27, H(8)+S(19)=27, I(9)+Q(17)=26. IQ breaks the sum rule.

52. (⚠️ Examiner’s Trap: double relationship – perfect squares vs palindrome)
Choose the odd one out.
(a) 121
(b) 144
(c) 169
(d) 196
Answer: (a) 121
Solution: All are perfect squares (11²,12²,13²,14²). However, 121 is the only palindrome. The primary intended base is the palindromic property. Trap: a student might see all squares and think there is no odd one, but 121 stands out as a palindrome.
Source: PYQ-style

54. Choose the odd one out.
(a) B2D
(b) E5G
(c) H8J
(d) K12M
Answer: (d) K12M
Solution: Pattern: first letter position = middle number, last letter = first letter + 2. B(2) → 2D(4), E(5)→5G(7), H(8)→8J(10). K has position 11, so middle number should be 11; it is 12 here. Hence K12M is odd.
Source: PYQ-style

55. Choose the odd one out.
(a) (8, 24)
(b) (12, 36)
(c) (15, 45)
(d) (18, 56)
Answer: (d) (18, 56)
Solution: Second number = first × 3. 8×3=24, 12×3=36, 15×3=45, but 18×3=54 (given 56). (18,56) breaks the pattern.

(b) C3X
(c) D4W
(d) E6V
Answer: (d) E6V
Solution: The pattern: first letter position = middle number, and opposite of first letter is given as the last letter? Actually B(2) opposite Y(25) sum 27; C(3) opposite X(24); D(4) opposite W(23); E(5) opposite should be V(22). The middle number is the position of the first letter: 2,3,4,5. In (d) it is 6, breaking the rule. So odd.
