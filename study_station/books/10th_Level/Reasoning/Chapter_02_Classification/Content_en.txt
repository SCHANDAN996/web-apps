# Classification

Put an apple, a banana, a mango, and a tennis ball in one basket — which does not belong? The tennis ball, because the rest are fruits. That is classification — finding the "odd" or "different" one.

**Definition:** In classification, several items are given where most share a common property, and one item is different from that property. We must identify that odd one out.

🧠 Recall: In classification, are we looking for the "similar" item or the "different" one?

## The Golden Rule

**First identify the shared property of the majority, then see which one falls outside it.**

Example: Rose, Lotus, Jasmine, Mango
Here Rose, Lotus, Jasmine are all flowers. Mango is a fruit. So the odd one = Mango.

## Main Types of Classification

**1. Word Classification**
Dog, Cat, Cow, Tiger → the tiger is wild, the rest are domestic. Odd one = Tiger.

**2. Letter Classification**
Look at the pattern of gaps between letters.
AC, EG, IK, MN → AC, EG, IK have a gap of +2 (one letter skipped), but MN has only +1. Odd one = MN.

**3. Number Classification**
Find the shared mathematical property — prime, square, cube, even/odd, etc.
3, 5, 7, 9, 11 → all are prime except 9 (9 = 3×3, composite). Odd one = 9.
4, 9, 16, 20, 25 → all are perfect squares except 20. Odd one = 20.

**4. General Knowledge Based**
India, Nepal, Tokyo, China → Tokyo is a city, the rest are countries. Odd one = Tokyo.

🧠 Recall: In 8, 27, 64, 100, 125 — which is the odd one and why?

## The Examiner's Trap

⚠️ **Trap 1 — more than one basis:** Sometimes two items may seem different (say by colour and by shape). Choose the strongest, clearest basis that links all the rest.

⚠️ **Trap 2 — hidden property:** In numbers the property may be direct (prime) or hidden (digit sum, square ±1). If one rule fails, try another.

⚠️ **Trap 3 — hurrying:** The item that looks different at first glance is not always the answer. Check the shared property of all options, then decide.
