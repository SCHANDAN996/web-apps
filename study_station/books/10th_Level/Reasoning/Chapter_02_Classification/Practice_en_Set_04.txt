76. Choose the odd one out:
(a) GN (b) EV (c) FU (d) DW
Answer: (a)
Solution: The tempting trap is to treat all pairs as random letters. In DW, EV and FU the two letters are opposite letters whose positions add to 27 (4+23, 5+22, 6+21), but G and N add to only 21, so the answer is GN.
Source: PYQ-style

77. Choose the odd one out:
(a) (9, 82) (b) (7, 51) (c) (11, 123) (d) (13, 171)
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is to check only whether the second number is larger. In the other pairs the second number equals the square of the first plus 2 (49+2=51, 121+2=123, 169+2=171), but 9²+2 = 83, not 82, so the answer is (9, 82).
Source: PYQ-style

78. Choose the odd one out:
(a) Orange (b) Apple (c) Tomato (d) Banana
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is that botanically a tomato is also a fruit, which makes it look similar. In everyday and culinary use, however, tomato is cooked and eaten as a vegetable, while banana, apple and orange are eaten as fresh fruits, so the answer is Tomato.
Source: PYQ-style

79. Choose the odd one out:
(a) 121 (b) 144 (c) 182 (d) 169
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is that all four are three-digit numbers close to each other. 121, 144 and 169 are perfect squares (11², 12², 13²) while 182 is not a perfect square, so the answer is 182.
Source: NCERT Class 8 Mathematics

80. Choose the odd one out:
(a) 64 (b) 100 (c) 27 (d) 8
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is that 100 is a perfect square, which feels like a strong pattern too. But 8, 27 and 64 are perfect cubes (2³, 3³, 4³) while 100 is not a cube, so the answer is 100.
Source: NCERT Class 8 Mathematics

81. Choose the odd one out:
(a) CE (b) JN (c) FH (d) IK
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is to assume any pair of letters with a gap follows the same rule. In CE, FH and IK the second letter is exactly 2 places ahead of the first, but in JN the gap is 4 places, so the answer is JN.
Source: PYQ-style

82. Choose the odd one out:
(a) H8I (b) E5F (c) B2C (d) K12L
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is to check only that the letters are consecutive around the number. In B2C, E5F and H8I the number equals the alphabet position of the first letter (B=2, E=5, H=8), but K is the 11th letter, not the 12th, so the answer is K12L.
Source: PYQ-style

83. Choose the odd one out:

Solution: The trap is that all four are heavenly bodies seen in the sky. Mars, Venus and Earth are planets revolving around the Sun, while the Moon is a natural satellite revolving around the Earth, so the answer is Moon.

84. Choose the odd one out:

89. Choose the odd one out:

(a) January (b) February (c) March (d) July
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is that all four are months of the year, so they look identical in category. January, March and July all have 31 days, whereas February has 28 days (29 in a leap year), so the answer is February.

95. Choose the odd one out:
(a) F8I (b) C4F (c) D5G (d) E6H
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is to check only that the last letter follows the first. In C4F, D5G and E6H the number is one more than the position of the first letter (C=3→4, D=4→5, E=5→6), but F is the 6th letter and the number given is 8, so the answer is F8I.
Source: PYQ-style

97. Choose the odd one out:
(a) WVU (b) TSR (c) ZYX (d) QPN
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is that all groups are in reverse alphabetical order. ZYX, TSR and WVU are three consecutive letters in descending order, but QPN skips a letter (Q, P, then N instead of O), so the answer is QPN.

100. Choose the odd one out:
(a) 19 (b) 21 (c) 13 (d) 17
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is that 21 is odd, and all four numbers are odd, which hides the real rule. 13, 19 and 17 are prime numbers, whereas 21 is divisible by 3 and 7, so the answer is 21.
