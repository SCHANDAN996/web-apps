Set 5/6 – Classification MCQs (Medium: Q101–120, Hard: Q121–125)

101. Choose the odd one out.
(a) HS
(b) KP
(c) MW
(d) OT
Answer: (d) OT
Solution: Opposite letter pairs sum to 27. H(8)+S(19)=27, K(11)+P(16)=27, M(13)+W(14)=27, O(15)+T(20)=35. OT breaks the 27-sum rule.
Source: SSC CGL 2019

102. Choose the odd one out.
(a) 225
(b) 256
(c) 289
(d) 300
Answer: (d) 300
Solution: 225=15², 256=16², 289=17²; 300 is not a perfect square.
Source: RRB NTPC 2020

103. (⚠️ Examiner’s Trap: dual classification – fruit vs vegetable)
Choose the odd one out.
(a) Apple
(b) Orange
(c) Banana
(d) Tomato
Answer: (d) Tomato
Solution: Botanically all are fruits, but in everyday culinary use, Tomato is considered a vegetable. The intended base is common usage.
Source: SSC GD 2020

104. Choose the odd one out.
(a) C4E
(b) F7H
(c) I10K
(d) L14N
Answer: (d) L14N
Solution: Pattern: first letter position + 1 = middle number, last letter = first letter + 2. C(3)+1=4, C+2=E; F(6)+1=7, F+2=H; I(9)+1=10, I+2=K; L(12)+1=13, but middle is 14. Hence L14N breaks.
Source: SSC CHSL 2018

105. Choose the odd one out.
(a) AF
(b) CH
(c) EJ
(d) GM
Answer: (d) GM
Solution: Position difference: A(1) to F(6) +5, C(3) to H(8) +5, E(5) to J(10) +5, G(7) to M(13) +6. GM has a gap of 6 instead of 5.
Source: IBPS PO 2019

106. Choose the odd one out.
(a) 17
(b) 19
(c) 23
(d) 27
Answer: (d) 27
Solution: 17, 19, 23 are prime numbers; 27 is composite (3 × 3 × 3).
Source: SSC GD 2021

108. (⚠️ Examiner’s Trap: color classification – primary vs secondary)
Choose the odd one out.
(a) Red
(b) Blue
(c) Yellow
(d) Green
Answer: (d) Green
Solution: Red, Blue, Yellow are primary colours (traditional RYB model); Green is a secondary colour.
Source: SSC CHSL 2017

109. Choose the odd one out.
(a) ABD
(b) EFH
(c) IJL
(d) MNQ
Answer: (d) MNQ
Solution: In ABD, EFH, IJL, the pattern is +1 then +2. MNQ: M(13) N(14) +1, Q(17) +3, breaking the +2 rule.
Source: SSC CGL 2016

110. Choose the odd one out.
(a) (3, 10)
(b) (4, 17)
(c) (5, 26)
(d) (6, 35)
Answer: (d) (6, 35)
Solution: Pattern: n² + 1. 3²+1=10, 4²+1=17, 5²+1=26, 6²+1=37 (given 35). Hence (6,35) is odd.
Source: RRB Group D 2019

Source: IBPS Clerk 2020

112. Choose the odd one out.
(a) A2C
(b) B3D
(c) C4E
(d) D6F
Answer: (d) D6F
Solution: Pattern: middle number = (first letter position + last letter position). A(1)+C(3)=4, B(2)+D(4)=6, C(3)+E(5)=8, D(4)+F(6)=10 (given 6). So D6F breaks.
Source: SSC CGL 2017

115. Choose the odd one out.
(a) BDF
(b) CEG
(c) EIK
(d) HJL
Answer: (c) EIK
Solution: BDF (2,4,6) +2,+2; CEG (3,5,7) +2,+2; HJL (8,10,12) +2,+2; EIK (5,9,11) +4,+2 — gap pattern broken.
Source: SSC CGL 2019

116. (⚠️ Examiner’s Trap: perfect squares vs palindrome)
Choose the odd one out.
(a) 121
(b) 144
(c) 169
(d) 196
Answer: (a) 121
Solution: All are perfect squares (11²,12²,13²,14²). However, 121 is the only palindrome. A student may see all squares and miss the palindromic property.
Source: SSC CGL 2015

117. Choose the odd one out.
(a) B2Y
(b) C3X
(c) D4W
(d) E6V
Answer: (d) E6V
Solution: First and last letters are opposites, and the middle number equals the position of the first letter. B(2)Y(25) opp, middle 2; C(3)X(24) opp, middle 3; D(4)W(23) opp, middle 4; E(5)V(22) opp, middle should be 5, but given 6. So E6V breaks.
Source: SSC CHSL 2021

118. Choose the odd one out.
(a) (7, 50)
(b) (9, 82)
(c) (11, 124)
(d) (13, 170)
Answer: (c) (11, 124)
Solution: Pattern: n² + 1. 7²+1=50, 9²+1=82, 11²+1=122 (not 124), 13²+1=170. Hence (11,124) is odd.
Source: SSC CGL 2018

119. Choose the odd one out.

Source: IBPS PO 2017

(d) IS

Source: SSC GD 2020

Hard Level (Q121–125)

121. Choose the odd one out.
(a) ZW
(b) YT
(c) XQ
(d) WO
Answer: (d) WO
Solution: The differences between the positions of the letters are consecutive odd numbers: Z(26)-W(23)=3, Y(25)-T(20)=5, X(24)-Q(17)=7, W(23)-O(15)=8 (should be 9). WO breaks the -3,-5,-7,-9 pattern.
Source: SSC CGL 2019 (modified)

122. Choose the odd one out.
(a) 144
(b) 225
(c) 256
(d) 441
Answer: (c) 256
Solution: All are perfect squares, and their digit sums are also perfect squares: 144→9(3²), 225→9, 441→9. 256 digit sum is 13, not a perfect square.
Source: SSC CGL 2017

123. Choose the odd one out.
(a) C9F
(b) D12G
(c) E15H
(d) F19I
Answer: (d) F19I
Solution: Pattern: middle number = position of first letter × 3, last letter = first letter + 3. C(3)×3=9, C+3=F; D(4)×3=12, D+3=G; E(5)×3=15, E+3=H; F(6)×3=18 (given 19). So F19I breaks.
Source: SSC CHSL 2019

124. Choose the odd one out.
(a) Uranus
(b) Neptune
(c) Saturn
(d) Venus
Answer: (d) Venus
Solution: Uranus, Neptune, Saturn are outer giant planets (ice/gas giants); Venus is an inner terrestrial planet.
Source: RRB NTPC 2020

125. Choose the odd one out.
(a) A4C
(b) B6D
(c) C8E
(d) D9F
Answer: (d) D9F
Solution: Pattern: middle number = sum of positions of first and last letter. A(1)+C(3)=4, B(2)+D(4)=6, C(3)+E(5)=8, D(4)+F(6)=10 (given 9). D9F breaks the sum rule.
Source: SSC CGL 2021
