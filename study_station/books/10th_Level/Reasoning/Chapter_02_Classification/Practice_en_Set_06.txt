126. Choose the odd one out:
(a) CFJ (b) ADG (c) BEH (d) DGJ
Answer: (a)
Solution: The tempting trap is to assume all groups rise by equal steps. In ADG, BEH and DGJ each letter is 3 places ahead of the previous one, but in CFJ the gap is +3 then +4, so the answer is CFJ.
Source: PYQ-style

127. Choose the odd one out:
(a) (5, 126) (b) (3, 28) (c) (4, 68) (d) (2, 9)
Answer: (c)
Solution: The rule is second number = cube of the first plus 1: 2³+1 = 9, 3³+1 = 28, 5³+1 = 126. The trap is that 68 looks like a cube-related number, but 4³+1 = 65, not 68, so the answer is (4, 68).
Source: PYQ-style

129. Choose the odd one out:

Answer: (c)

132. Choose the odd one out:
(a) 2197 (b) 1330 (c) 1728 (d) 2744
Answer: (b)
Solution: 2197 = 13³, 1728 = 12³ and 2744 = 14³ are perfect cubes. The trap is that 1330 is very close to 1331 = 11³, but 1330 itself is not a cube, so the answer is 1330.

140. Choose the odd one out:
(a) 408 (b) 371 (c) 370 (d) 153
Answer: (a)
Solution: 153, 370 and 371 are Armstrong numbers: each equals the sum of the cubes of its digits (1³+5³+3³ = 153). The trap is that 408 sits next to 407, which is an Armstrong number, but 4³+0³+8³ = 576, not 408, so the answer is 408.
Source: NCERT Class 8 Mathematics

149. Choose the odd one out:
(a) 511 (b) 730 (c) 342 (d) 215
Answer: (b)
Solution: The rule is each number is one less than a perfect cube: 215 = 6³−1, 342 = 7³−1 and 511 = 8³−1. The trap is that 730 is very close to 728 = 9³−1, but 730 itself equals 9³+1 and breaks the pattern, so the answer is 730.
Source: NCERT Class 8 Mathematics
