🪄 Short Tricks + Skip Strategy

🟡 CHAIN GANG RULE
"Same direction, no objection. Opposite direction, break connection."

Imagine arrows as a gang marching the same way – they stick together. If one turns around (opposite direction), the chain splits and no conclusion can pass.

🟡 THE ALLIGATOR MOUTH (Symbol Trick)
"The alligator always eats the bigger number."

The open side of > or < faces the larger quantity. ≥ and ≤ are alligators on a diet – they allow equality too.

🟡 THE SMUDGED LINE (Either-Or)
"Smudged line between A and B? Either greater or equal, you see."

When you see A ≥ B or A ≤ B, imagine the line connecting them is smudged – it could be > or =. If two conclusions claim these two possibilities and neither is definite, they form an Either-Or pair.

🟡 THE BROKEN BRIDGE (Opposite Signs)
" > meets < at a variable? The bridge is broken – don't cross."

A > B < C – B is a broken bridge. You cannot connect A and C. No definite relation exists.

🟡 THE OGRE AND THE BRIDGE
"Ogre ≥ guards the bridge. Only those strictly greater (>) may pass; equals are stopped."

From A ≥ B > C, you can definitely say A > C because the strict > forces the ogre to step aside. But from A ≥ B ≥ C, you can't say A > C – the ogre might be equal.

🟡 THE DANCING COUPLE (Complementary Pair)
" > and = dance together. < and = dance together. But > and < never dance – they fight!"

Complementary pairs for Either-Or are (>, =) or (<, =), or sometimes (≥, ≤). > and < alone miss equality, so they aren't a pair.

🟡 THE DECODER RING (Coded Inequalities)
"Before you fight the dragon, decode the magic symbols into plain language."

In coded inequalities, first write the actual meaning of each symbol (≤, ≥, etc.) on your sheet. Then the problem becomes ordinary inequality.

🟡 THE TRAIN COACHES (Combining)
"Coaches must face the same direction to attach. If one coach faces the other way, the train breaks."

When combining inequalities, signs must point the same way (A > B > C). If they face opposite (A > B < C), no connection.

🟡 THE THREE-HEADED MONSTER (≥ or ≤)
"A monster with three heads: greater, equal, or unknown. You can't be sure which head will bite."

A statement with ≥ or ≤ gives three possibilities with an outer variable if the chain breaks. Don't assume one head is certain.

🟡 THE EQUALITY TRAP (Sneaky =)
"Equal is a silent snake. You don't see it, but it can bite."

When a conclusion says A > B and the statement is A ≥ B, the snake of equality might be hiding – A = B is possible, so A > B isn't definite.

🟡 THE 3-STEP DETECTIVE
"Combine, check definite, look for Either-Or – like a detective connecting clues."

Step 1: Combine all statements into a single chain if possible. Step 2: Check each conclusion for 100% certainty. Step 3: Among false conclusions, look for complementary pair (Either-Or).

🟡 THE MAGIC "NOT" PHRASES (Decoding)
"Not greater than → ≤, Not less than → ≥. Neither greater nor equal → <. Neither less nor equal → >."

Memorize these translations by chanting: "Not greater, less or equal; Not less, greater or equal."

🟡 THE DOUBLE POSSIBILITY DJ
" ≥ is a DJ mixing two tracks: > and =. Don't ask which track is playing – both are possible."

When you see a conclusion picking only one track (> or =), it's not definite unless the other track is muted by the chain.

🟡 THE STRICT PARENT ( > or < )
"A strict parent says 'No equality!' – once a strict > or < enters the chain, equality cannot pass."

If a chain has a strict > or < linking A to B, then A = B is impossible.

🟡 THE TWO-FACED GOD (Either-Or)
"Janus has two faces: one looks at >, the other at =. You don't know which face is real."

When A ≥ B is given, the relationship is two-faced. Two conclusions capturing these two faces create an Either-Or case.

⏭️ Skip Strategy Tips

15-second Rule (Coded Inequality):
If you cannot decode the coded symbols and write their actual meanings within 15 seconds, skip the question immediately. Return later with a fresh mind.

45-second Rule (Complex Either-Or + Multiple Conclusions):
If a single inequality question with three conclusions and an Either-Or case takes more than 45 seconds, mark it for review and move on. Don't let one question consume time for three easy ones.

"Broken Chain" Skip Cue:
If you spot opposite symbols meeting at a variable in the main statement chain (e.g., A > B < C), and the conclusions try to relate the outer variables, immediately mark "no relation" and move on. No need to check further possibilities – the bridge is broken.
