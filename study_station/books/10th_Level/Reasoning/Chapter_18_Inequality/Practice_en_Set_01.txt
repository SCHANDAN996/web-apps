Q1. (Easy) Statement: A > B > C
Conclusions: I. A > C II. B < A
(A) Both follow (B) Only II follows (C) Only I follows (D) None follows

Answer: (A) Both follow

Solution:
From A > B > C, we get A > C (True). Also B < A (True). Hence both follow. Hence, the correct option is (A). Hence, the correct option is (A).

Q2. (Easy) ⚠️ ⚠️ Statement: P ≥ Q = R
Conclusions: I. P > R II. P = R
(A) Only I follows (B) Either I or II follows (C) Only II follows (D) Neither I nor II follows

Answer: (B) Either I or II follows

Solution:
P ≥ Q = R ⇒ P ≥ R. This allows both P > R and P = R. Neither is individually definite, but together they cover all possibilities. Hence either I or II follows. Trap: Students often mark only I or only II, forgetting the double possibility hidden in ‘≥’. Hence, the correct option is (B). Hence, the correct option is (B).

Solution:

Q7. (Easy) ⚠️ ⚠️ Statement: X ≥ Y

(A) Only I follows (B) Only II follows (C) Neither I nor II follows (D) Either I or II follows

Answer: (C) Neither I nor II follows

Solution:
X ≥ Y means X is greater than or equal to Y. Neither X > Y nor X < Y is definite individually. Moreover, the pair (>, <) is not complementary because the equality possibility is missing. Hence neither follows. Trap: Many students mistake > and < for an Either–Or pair. A complementary pair must cover all possibilities (here = is not covered). Hence, the correct option is (C). Q19. (Easy) (Coded) Symbols: ‘A + B’ ⇒ A ≥ B, ‘A - B’ ⇒ A ≤ B, ‘A × B’ ⇒ A < B, ‘A ÷ B’ ⇒ A > B, ‘A = B’ ⇒ A = B

Q21. (Medium) ⚠️ ⚠️ (Coded) Symbols: ‘A @ B’ ⇒ A ≤ B, ‘A # B’ ⇒ A ≥ B, ‘A B’ ⇒ A > B, ‘A % B’ ⇒ A < B Statements: P # Q, Q % R, R S

Solution:

Trap: The presence of > and < tempts students to apply Either–Or without checking the equality possibility. Hence, the correct option is (A). Hence, the correct option is (A).
