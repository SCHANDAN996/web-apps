Front: If A > B and B > C, what is the relation between A and C?
Back: A > C (the chain joins in the same direction).

Front: What are the five inequality signs and their meanings?
Back: > greater, < less, = equal, ≥ greater or equal, ≤ less or equal.

Front: What follows from A > B = C?
Back: A > C (equality in between still works).

Front: In A > B < C, what is the relation between A and C?
Back: No definite relation (the direction changed).

Front: When does the chain join?
Back: When all signs are in the same direction.

Front: What does '≥' mean?
Back: Greater than or equal (> or =).

Front: What does '≤' mean?
Back: Less than or equal (< or =).

Front: When does the Either-Or rule apply?
Back: When one conclusion has > and the other = on the same pair, and neither is correct alone.

Front: What is the "either-or" form of A ≥ C?
Back: "A > C or A = C" — together correct.

Front: What follows from A ≥ B and B > C?
Back: A > C.

Front: What to conclude immediately when the direction changes?
Back: "No definite relation" between the ends.

Front: In A < B < C, which is the greatest?
Back: C.

Front: What is the diagram method to check a conclusion?
Back: Draw a number line/arrows — all arrows one way means joined.

Front: What is the most common trap?
Back: Drawing a direct conclusion even when the direction changes.

Front: What is the inequality mnemonic?
Back: "Same direction joins, changed direction breaks".
