Q51. (Medium) In a certain code: ‘A @ B’ means A ≤ B, ‘A # B’ means A ≥ B, ‘A $ B’ means A < B, ‘A % B’ means A > B, ‘A * B’ means A = B
Statements: X # Y, Y @ Z, Z $ A, A % B
Conclusions:
I. X % A
II. X * A
(A) Neither I nor II follows (B) Only II follows (C) Either I or II follows (D) Only I follows

Answer: (A) Neither I nor II follows

Solution:
Decoded: X ≥ Y, Y ≤ Z, Z < A, A > B. Between X and A, signs reverse at Y (≥ and ≤), so no definite relation exists. Neither I nor II follows. Hence, the correct option is (A).

Source: SSC CGL

Q52. (Medium) Statements:
A > B = C ≥ D < E; F ≤ D > G
Conclusions:
I. A > F
II. B > G
(A) Only I follows (B) Both follow (C) Only II follows (D) Neither I nor II follows

Answer: (B) Both follow

Solution:
From A > B = C ≥ D ≥ F ⇒ A > F (I follows). From B = C ≥ D > G ⇒ B > G (II follows). Both are definite. Hence, the correct option is (B).

Source: SSC CHSL

Q53. (Medium) In a certain code:
‘A α B’ means A > B
‘A β B’ means A < B
‘A γ B’ means A = B
‘A δ B’ means A ≥ B
‘A ε B’ means A ≤ B
Statements:
P α Q
Q β R
R γ S
S ε T
Conclusions:
I. P β T
II. P α T
(A) Only I follows (B) Only II follows (C) Neither I nor II follows (D) Either I or II follows

Answer: (C) Neither I nor II follows

Solution:
Decoded: P > Q, Q < R, R = S, S ≤ T. Chain: P > Q < R = S ≤ T. The connection between P and T breaks at Q because of opposite signs. All relations between P and T (>, <, =) are possible. I (P < T) and II (P > T) are individually false and do not form a complementary pair (equality missing). Thus neither follows. Hence, the correct option is (C).

Source: SSC MTS

Q54. (Medium) Which of the following expressions will make ‘M ≤ N’ definitely true?
(A) M > O ≥ N (B) M ≥ O < N (C) M = O > N (D) M < O ≤ N

Answer: (D) M < O ≤ N

Solution:
In option (b): M < O ≤ N ⇒ M < N, which always satisfies M ≤ N. Other options do not guarantee M ≤ N. Hence, the correct option is (D).

Source: RRB NTPC

Q55. (Medium) ⚠️ Statements:
M ≥ N > O = P
Q ≤ P
Conclusions:
I. M > O
II. M = O
(A) Only I follows (B) Only II follows (C) Either I or II follows (D) Neither I nor II follows

Answer: (A) Only I follows

Solution:
Combine: M ≥ N > O ⇒ M > O (definite). Thus I is true. Conclusion II (M = O) is false. Trap: The statement M ≥ N > O might appear to give M ≥ O, tempting students to treat I and II as an Either-Or pair. However, the chain actually forces M > O, so II cannot be true. Hence, the correct option is (A).

Source: RRB Group D

Q56. (Medium) In a certain code:
‘A © B’ means A ≤ B
‘A ® B’ means A > B
‘A µ B’ means A = B
‘A β B’ means A ≥ B
‘A ∑ B’ means A < B
Statements:

Q µ R
R ∑ S
S © T
Conclusions:
I. P ® T
II. P ∑ T
(A) Only I follows (B) Neither I nor II follows (C) Either I or II follows (D) Only II follows

Answer: (B) Neither I nor II follows

Solution:
Decoded: P > Q = R < S ≤ T. Chain: P > Q = R < S ≤ T ⇒ P > R and R < T. No definite relation between P and T. I (P > T) and II (P < T) are not definitely true and do not cover the equality possibility, so they are not complementary. Hence, the correct option is (B).

Q57. (Medium) Statements:
A = B > C ≥ D; E < D ≤ F; G > F
Conclusions:
I. A > E
II. G > C
III. B ≥ F
(A) II and III follow (B) I and II follow (C) Only I follows (D) I and III follow

Answer: (C) Only I follows

Solution:
Combine: A = B > C ≥ D > E ⇒ A > E (I true). G > F ≥ D ≤ C — the chain breaks at D; no definite G > C (II false). B > C ≥ D ≤ F — no relation between B and F (III false). Hence, the correct option is (C).

Q58. (Medium) Statements:

S ≤ R
Conclusions:
I. P > S
II. P = S
(A) Only I follows (B) Only II follows (C) Neither I nor II follows (D) Either I or II follows

Answer: (D) Either I or II follows

Solution:
Combine: P ≥ Q = R ≥ S ⇒ P ≥ S. Thus, either P > S or P = S is true. Neither is definite individually, but together they cover all possibilities. Hence, the correct option is (D).

Q60. (Medium) Which set of symbols should replace the blanks in the expression ‘A _ B _ C _ D’ so that both ‘A ≥ C’ and ‘B > D’ definitely hold?
(A) >, ≥, > (B) ≥, >, = (C) =, ≥, < (D) ≥, =, ≥

Answer: (B) ≥, >, =

Solution:
Expression: A ≥ B > C = D. From A ≥ B > C, we get A > C, hence A ≥ C true. From B > C = D, we get B > D true. Other options do not satisfy both conditions simultaneously. Hence, the correct option is (B).

Q61. (Medium) Which set of symbols should replace the blanks in ‘M _ N _ O _ P’ so that both ‘M > O’ and ‘N ≤ P’ definitely hold?

Answer: (C) >, =, ≤

Solution:

Q62. (Medium) Statements:
P > Q ≥ R = S; T < S; U ≤ T
Conclusions:
I. P > U
II. Q > T
III. R ≤ U
(A) Only I follows (B) All follow (C) II and III follow (D) I and II follow

Answer: (D) I and II follow

Solution:
Chain: P > Q ≥ R = S > T ≥ U. P > U (I true). Q ≥ R = S > T ⇒ Q > T (II true). R = S > T ≥ U ⇒ R > U, so R ≤ U is false (III false). Hence, the correct option is (D).

Q64. (Medium) Statements:
A > B = C ≤ D < E; F = E > G; H < G
Conclusions:
I. A > C
II. B < E
III. H < D
(A) II and III follow (B) I and II follow (C) I and III follow (D) All follow

Answer: (B) I and II follow

Solution:
A > B = C ⇒ A > C (I true). B = C ≤ D < E ⇒ B < E (II true). H < G < E and D < E; no relation between H and D (III false). Hence, the correct option is (B).

Q65. (Medium) Statements:
P = Q > R ≥ S; T < S; U ≥ T
Conclusions:
I. Q > U
II. R > T
III. P ≥ S
(A) Only II follows (B) I and II follow (C) II and III follow (D) All follow

Answer: (C) II and III follow

Solution:
Q > R ≥ S > T and U ≥ T, but U could be larger than Q (e.g., Q=10, T=5, U=20), so I is false. R ≥ S > T ⇒ R > T (II true). P = Q > R ≥ S ⇒ P > S, so P ≥ S is true (III true). Hence, the correct option is (C).

Source: RRB Group D
