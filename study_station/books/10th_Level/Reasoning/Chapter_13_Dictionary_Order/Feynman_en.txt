🧒 Feynman Technique: Explain to a 12-Year-Old

Imagine you are standing outside a large movie theater. There is a long queue, and the ticket checker is admitting people one by one. He has a unique rule: everyone must state their name letter by letter, and whoever finishes their name first gets to enter first.

Now two brothers are standing in line — one named "Arm" and the other named "Army". Both start speaking at the same time:
A... R... M...
The brother named "Arm" stops speaking after three letters. His name is finished.
The brother named "Army" is still speaking: Y...
The ticket checker smiles and says, "Whoever finishes their name first enters first."
So "Arm" walks right in, with "Army" right behind him.

This illustrates the most essential, unbreakable rule of dictionary order:
When a shorter word is completely contained within the beginning of a longer word, the shorter word always comes first.
Remember this simple motto: "The word that finishes first enters first." 🏁

Unforgettable Trick: The Librarian's Finger
Whenever you solve a dictionary order problem, trace your finger over the words letter by letter, just as a librarian runs a finger along the spines of books on a shelf. Pause at each letter. As soon as one word runs out of letters while the other still has letters remaining, stop immediately — the word that ended first wins! This technique prevents careless mistakes.
