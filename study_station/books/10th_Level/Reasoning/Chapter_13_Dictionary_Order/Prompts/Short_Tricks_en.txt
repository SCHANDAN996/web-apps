1. The Snail’s Pace S.T.R.E.T.C.H.
Stop. Take a breath. Really look at the Every single Tiny Character. Haste makes waste. Imagine a snail moving one letter at a time down the page. There’s no prize for guessing—only for getting it right.

2. SHORTY ALWAYS WINS (The “Mini-Me” Rule)
When two words start the same but one runs out of letters, the shorter word barges in first—like a little kid ducking under the rope. Arm before Army, Bat before Batch. Picture a toddler shouting “Me first!” and the bigger sibling sighing.

3. THE GHOSTBUSTERS HYPHEN & SPACE TRICK
Hyphens and spaces are invisible ghosts. Don’t let them spook you. X-ray becomes Xray, Post office becomes Postoffice. Just imagine crossing them out with a proton pack. Who you gonna call? The Alphabet!

4. THE IDENTICAL TWIN DOUBLE-LETTER DECEPTION
Double letters are like identical twins—they look special but count as two separate kids. No skipping the line just because they’re twins. Compare each ‘t’ in Butter one by one, don’t let the twin magic fool you.

5. CASE? THE DICTIONARY DOESN’T CARE (Whisper & Shout)
Whether you whisper “apple” or shout “APPLE”, the dictionary hears the same word. A = a. So stop stressing over capitals. They’re just different outfits on the same letter.

6. THE LAZY LIBRARIAN’S FINGER
Imagine an ancient librarian dragging a lazy finger across the page, checking one letter at a time. The moment two words disagree at a letter, the finger stops. The word with the earlier letter gets shelved first. Move your own finger like that librarian—no jumping!

7. FIRST DIFFERENCE DECIDES ALL
The first spot where letters differ is like the finish line. Don’t run past it. If you see Ball vs Bat, the ‘l’ vs ‘t’ decides right there. Further letters are irrelevant. The race is over.

8. GRAPE CLUSTER GROUPING
When words share the first 2-3 letters, they’re a bunch of grapes on a stem. Don’t pull them all at once. Group by the next differing letter. For Butter, Butler, Button: stem “But”, then group by 4th letter: l, t, t. Pluck the ‘l’ cluster first.

9. REVERSE ORDER ROCKET LAUNCH
Reverse order (Z to A) is just a countdown. Normal order is 1,2,3…; reverse is 3,2,1… blast off! Think: “Zulu soldiers charge backwards.” Or simply flip your forward list. Don’t re‑compare backwards; it’s a trap.

10. DON’T BE A CHEETAH, BE A SLOTH
Cheetahs sprint and stumble. Sloths move one deliberate claw‑hold at a time and never miss. In Dictionary Order, speed kills accuracy. Channel your inner sloth. Arrive late but correct.

11. THE ALPHABET SOUP SWIRL
Imagine all the first letters of your words floating in alphabet soup. The one that bobs up earliest (closest to A) gets eaten first. Then stir the pot for the next letter. A silly visual stops you from guessing.

12. THE JEALOUS NEIGHBOUR LETTER
Each letter wants to be earlier than its neighbour. ‘C’ is jealous of ‘B’, ‘B’ of ‘A’. When comparing, the letter that is closer to ‘A’ always wins the argument. The closer to A, the better the seat.

🚦 Skip Strategy Tips
Condition	Action
5+ words share 3 or more starting letters (e.g., Conservation, Conversation, Conversion…)	Skip after 60 seconds if no clear split emerges. These are designed time‑sinks.
First two letters don’t immediately separate the list and you feel lost within 15 seconds.	Mark, skip, and return only after attempting all easier questions.
Reverse dictionary order is asked and you haven’t even made the forward list in 30 seconds.	Skip. The forward list must be solid first. Reverse is just a flip; if forward is shaky, reverse will be wrong.
