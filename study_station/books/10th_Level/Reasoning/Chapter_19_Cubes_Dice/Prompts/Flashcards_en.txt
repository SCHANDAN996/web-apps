🃏 Front: What is the one unbreakable rule about opposite faces in a standard dice?
Back: Opposite faces always add up to 7. So 1↔6, 2↔5, 3↔4.

🃏 Front: How do you spot a non-standard dice in an exam question?
Back: The sum of opposite faces is NOT 7. You must find opposites using the common-face method; never assume the sum rule.

🃏 Front: You see two views of a dice: both show ‘3’ on top. In one view, the front is 1, right is 2. In the other, front is 5, right is 6. Without a third view, can you tell which number is opposite 1?
Back: Yes! Use the clockwise rule: from 3, move clockwise → 1,2 in first view and 5,6 in second. So 1 opposite 5, and 2 opposite 6. 3 opposite 4.

🃏 Front: What simple phrase helps you remember that opposite faces never touch in an open dice?
Back: “O-CAN”: Opposite Cannot be Adjacent, Never. In the flat net, they never share an edge.

🃏 Front: In the cross-shaped net, there is a 4-face row. How do you find all three opposite pairs instantly?
Back: In that row, the 1st and 3rd are opposites, the 2nd and 4th are opposites. The lonely face above the middle is opposite the lonely face below the middle.

🃏 Front: A large cube (side = 5 small cubes) is painted and then cut into 1×1×1 cubes. How many small cubes have exactly two faces painted?
Back: 12 × (5 - 2) = 12 × 3 = 36 cubes. (They lie on the edges, minus the corners.)

🃏 Front: How many small cubes have zero faces painted when you cut a painted cube of side 3 into unit cubes?
Back: (3 - 2)³ = 1³ = 1 cube. Only the innermost core remains unpainted.

🃏 Front: Recite the ‘Paint Song’ mnemonic to remember 3-face, 2-face, 1-face, 0-face painted cubes.
Back: “Three on corners, eight my friend, / Two on edges, formula trend, / One on faces, six flat squares, / Zero inside, nobody cares.” (Sequence 3-2-1-0 from outside in.)

🃏 Front: In the painted cube formula, why are there always exactly 8 cubes with three faces painted?
Back: Because a cube has exactly 8 corners, and only those tiny cubes touch three painted faces.

🃏 Front: What’s the trap in a question that says “at least 2 faces coloured” instead of “only 2 faces coloured”?
Back: “At least 2” includes both 2-face painted AND 3-face painted cubes. “Only 2” excludes the 8 corner cubes. One word changes the final count dramatically.

🃏 Front: Why might you miss cubes when counting an irregular solid from the front view?
Back: Tall columns at the back are hidden by shorter ones in front. Always rely on the top view or column method to count every stack’s height.

🃏 Front: Which mnemonic reminds you how to check for opposite faces in an open dice?
Back: O-CAN (Opposite Cannot be Adjacent, Never). It tells you to first rule out faces that touch, then check the cross-net rule for alternates.

🃏 Front: If an open dice net has a 'T' shape, the two faces at the ends of the T’s arms are adjacent or opposite after folding?
Back: They become adjacent. Folding the vertical stem brings those arms together; they are never opposite.

🃏 Front: A cube of side 4 is cut from an unpainted block, then all its faces are painted. How many unit cubes have exactly one face painted?
Back: 6 × (4 - 2)² = 6 × 4 = 24 cubes. These sit at the centre of each face.

🃏 Front: In a solid cube puzzle, the top view shows a 3×3 grid with numbers 1,2,1 in the first row; 2,3,2 in second; 1,2,1 in third. How many total unit cubes?
Back: Sum all heights: 1+2+1+2+3+2+1+2+1 = 15 cubes. The top view gives the exact height of each column.

🃏 Front: What is the examiner’s trick with a dice that shows the same number twice in different views?
Back: It forces you to realise the dice may be non-standard or the views might be impossible. Stick to the common-face method; don't auto-assume sum=7.

🃏 Front: You are given two dice views with no common face. How do you proceed to find opposites?
Back: Either use a third given view or unfold the dice into a net by marking adjacent faces. Keep adjoining faces consistent, then apply the open-dice rules.

🃏 Front: Which tiny detail in a ‘painted cube’ question makes you count 3-face cubes as part of the answer?
Back: The phrase “at least 2 faces coloured” or “more than 1 face coloured” – then you must add the 8 corner cubes.

🃏 Front: How can you instantly check if a given number is opposite to another using the ‘sum=7’ rule for a standard dice?
Back: Subtract the number from 7. For example, opposite of 2 is 7-2=5. But only if the dice is confirmed standard.

🃏 Front: In the open dice cross net, if the top lonely face is 6 and the bottom lonely face is 1, what is the opposite of the 2nd face in the middle row if the row is [3,4,5,2]?
Back: 1st and 3rd are opposite (3↔5), 2nd and 4th are opposite (4↔2). So the 2nd face (4) is opposite the 4th face (2). The top 6 is opposite bottom 1.
