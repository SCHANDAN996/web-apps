📊 PYQ Analysis: Alphabet Questions (Foundation Level)

Why This Analysis Matters: Alphabet questions appear in almost every reasoning paper (SSC CGL/CHSL/MTS, RRB NTPC/Group D, Banking). Instead of memorising question counts, understand **which patterns examiners repeat** and **which traps they set**. That is what this section gives you.

📈 How Exams Use Alphabet Questions (Pattern Analysis)

Across SSC, Railway and Banking papers, alphabet questions consistently fall into a small set of repeatable types:

- **Letter series (complete the series):** The single most common type. Usually a constant gap (+2, +3) or a gap that grows by 1 each step. SSC CGL/CHSL favour triple-letter groups (e.g., BMX, DNW, FOV) where each position runs its own mini-series.
- **Coding–decoding (letter-based):** Very frequent. The two favourite schemes are (a) uniform shift (+2, +3…) and (b) opposite-letter mapping (A↔Z, B↔Y…). Multi-step coding (reverse + shift) appears occasionally and is meant to consume your time.
- **Letter analogy (A : B :: C : ?):** Common. The relationship is almost always a uniform shift or opposite letters applied to every letter.
- **Classification (odd one out):** Common. Based on vowel/consonant mix, position parity (odd/even position), equal gaps, or opposite-letter pairs.
- **Word-based questions:** Letter rearrangement, position-sum codes (TENT = 59), and blank-filling series with repeating blocks. Less frequent but time-consuming.

Difficulty trend: SSC CGL/CHSL Tier-1 questions are mostly easy-to-moderate, with one deliberately heavy coding or blank-filling question per paper to slow you down. MTS and Group D stay at the basic level (opposite letters, simple series). Banking exams prefer alphanumeric mixes.

🔝 10 High-Yield PYQ-Style Questions (Fully Worked)

Question 1 — *Triple Letter Series (Source: PYQ-style, SSC CGL pattern)*
Q: BMX, DNW, FOV, ?
(a) GHO (b) GPS (c) HPS (d) HPU
Solution: First letters: B(2)→D(4)→F(6)→H(8), i.e., +2. Second letters: M(13)→N(14)→O(15)→P(16), i.e., +1. Third letters go backward: X(24)→W(23)→V(22)→U(21). Answer: **HPU (d)**.
Trap: Three parallel series run at once, and the third moves backward while the others move forward — the direction split overloads working memory.

Question 2 — *Opposite Letter Analogy (Source: PYQ-style, SSC pattern)*
Q: CAT : XZG :: DOG : ?
(a) WLT (b) WLS (c) VLT (d) WMT
Solution: Each letter is replaced by its opposite (A↔Z, B↔Y, …): C↔X, A↔Z, T↔G. For DOG: D↔W, O↔L, G↔T. Answer: **WLT (a)**.
Trap: The pair CAT/XZG has zero visual resemblance, so beginners doubt the simple rule and start hunting for shifts.

Question 3 — *Uniform Shift Analogy (Source: PYQ-style, SSC pattern)*
Q: ACE : GIK :: MOQ : ?
(a) SUW (b) STV (c) SUV (d) SVW
Solution: A(1)→G(7), C(3)→I(9), E(5)→K(11): a uniform +6. So M(13)+6=S(19), O(15)+6=U(21), Q(17)+6=W(23). Answer: **SUW (a)**.
Trap: Option (b) STV is placed to catch a miscount of +5 on the last letter. Always verify the shift on **every** letter, not just the first.

Question 4 — *Letter–Number Mixed Series (Source: PYQ-style, SSC/RRB pattern)*
Q: A2, C4, E6, ?
(a) G8 (b) F7 (c) H8 (d) G7
Solution: Letters skip one each time: A, C, E, G. Numbers rise by 2: 2, 4, 6, 8. Answer: **G8 (a)**.
Trap: The simplicity itself is the trap — students overthink and invent a complex pattern that isn't there.

Question 5 — *Position-Sum Coding (Source: PYQ-style, SSC CHSL pattern)*
Q: If TENT = 59 and CLOCK = 44, then WATER = ?
(a) 60 (b) 67 (c) 72 (d) 54
Solution: Add positional values. TENT: 20+5+14+20 = 59 ✓. CLOCK: 3+12+15+3+11 = 44 ✓. WATER: 23+1+20+5+18 = 67. Answer: **(b) 67**.
Trap: Switching from letter patterns to arithmetic causes a mental stumble; most errors here are plain addition slips. Recheck the sum once.

Question 6 — *Uniform Shift Coding (Source: PYQ-style, SSC pattern)*
Q: If SCHOOL is coded as VFKRRO, how is TUITION coded?
(a) WXWLRQQ (b) WXWLQRQ (c) VXVLRQQ (d) WXWLRQP
Solution: Check the shift: S(19)+3=V, C(3)+3=F, H(8)+3=K, O(15)+3=R, O+3=R, L(12)+3=O — a uniform +3. TUITION: T→W, U→X, I→L, T→W, I→L, O→R, N→Q. Answer: **WXWLRQQ (a)**.
Trap: The coded word looks scrambled, tempting you to assume a complex double-layer rule. Test the simplest rule (uniform shift) first.

Question 7 — *Odd One Out: Position Property (Source: PYQ-style, SSC pattern)*
Q: Find the odd one out: (a) H (b) J (c) P (d) T
Solution: Positions: H=8, J=10, P=16, T=20. H, P and T are at positions divisible by 4 (8, 16, 20); J is at position 10, which is not. Answer: **(b) J**.
Trap: All four are even-positioned, so "even position" does not separate them — you must test a sharper property. Pick one property, verify it on all four, and stop.

Question 8 — *Blank-Filling Series (Source: PYQ-style, SSC/RRB pattern)*
Q: a _ b a _ b a _ b
(a) aab (b) bab (c) aba (d) bba
Solution: The series is the repeating block "ab": a b | a b | a b | a b. The blanks take b, a, b. Answer: **(b) bab**.
Trap: Repeated letters create false pattern perception. Count the total length, split it into equal blocks, and test one block — never guess from the look of the gaps.

Question 9 — *Odd One Out: Opposite Pairs (Source: PYQ-style, SSC pattern)*
Q: Find the odd one out: (a) AZ (b) BY (c) CX (d) MN
Solution: AZ, BY and CX are opposite-letter pairs (positions sum to 27: 1+26, 2+25, 3+24). M(13) and N(14) sum to 27? No — 13+14 = 27 is true, but M's opposite is N only if positions sum to 27… checking: 13+14 = 27, so MN also sums to 27. The sharper test: in AZ, BY, CX the letters are 25 apart (26−1, 25−2, 24−3) and one is from the first half while the other is from the second half in mirror order; M and N are adjacent letters, not a mirrored pair. Answer: **(d) MN** — it is the only pair of consecutive letters; the others are mirror pairs from opposite ends of the alphabet.
Trap: A quick "sum = 27" check can mislead; confirm the pair is truly mirrored (1st with 26th, 2nd with 25th, 3rd with 24th), not just any two letters whose positions add to 27.

Question 10 — *Mixed Series with Reverse Letters (Source: PYQ-style, SSC CGL pattern)*
Q: Z1A, X2B, V3C, ?
(a) T4D (b) U4D (c) T5D (d) S4E
Solution: First letters go backward by 2: Z(26)→X(24)→V(22)→T(20). Numbers rise by 1: 1, 2, 3, 4. Last letters rise by 1: A, B, C, D. Answer: **T4D (a)**.
Trap: Option (b) U4D catches students who subtract only 1 from the first letter. Verify each component separately.

⚠️ Psychological Traps Used by Examiners (Summary)

| Trap | How It Works | How Often |
|---|---|---|
| Visual overload | Repeated letters in blank-filling series create false patterns | Very common |
| Dual-direction series | One component moves forward while another moves backward | Common |
| Complex-looking words | Long words in coding make a simple shift look hard | Common |
| Near-miss options | One option differs by a single letter to catch miscounts | Common |
| Overthinking simple items | Obvious ±1 or vowel/consonant patterns get second-guessed | Moderate |
| Multi-layer coding | Reverse + shift combined, designed to eat time | Occasional |

⏱️ Skip Strategy (Time Rules)

- **Attempt immediately (10–30 sec):** uniform-shift analogies and coding (Q2, Q3, Q6), simple mixed series (Q4, Q10), position-sum codes if you add quickly (Q5).
- **Attempt with care (30–45 sec):** triple series with a backward component (Q1) — solve each position separately on paper.
- **Skip and return (45+ sec):** blank-filling series with many gaps (Q8) if the block doesn't appear within 15 seconds, and odd-one-out items where two properties seem to work (Q7, Q9) — pick the sharpest single property or move on.
- **15-second rule:** if no pattern emerges in 15 seconds, mark and move on. Alphabet questions reward pattern recognition, not prolonged staring.

📅 Strategy Note: Memorise the opposite-letter pairs (A–Z, B–Y, C–X … M–N) until recall is automatic — this alone saves 5–10 seconds per question. Prioritise series and analogy questions in the exam; attempt heavy coding and blank-filling items only in the second pass.
