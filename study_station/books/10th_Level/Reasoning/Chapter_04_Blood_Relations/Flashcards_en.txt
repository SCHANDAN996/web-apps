Card 1
Front: A man says, "She is the only daughter of my father's only son." Who is "she"?
Back: His daughter. Father's only son = himself. His only daughter = his daughter.

Card 2
Front: A boy points to a girl and says, "She is the daughter of my mother's only son." Who is the girl?
Back: His daughter. Mother's only son = himself. So the girl is his daughter.

Card 3
Front: What two simple shapes can replace a dictionary of relationship names when you're drawing a family tree?
Back: Square (□) for male and circle (○) for female. Add a horizontal line for marriage and a vertical line for parent-child.

Card 4
Front: In the P-M Trick, what do the letters P and M stand for to instantly distinguish paternal from maternal relatives?
Back: P for Papa's side (paternal) and M for Mummy's side (maternal). So paternal uncle = chacha/tau, maternal uncle = mama.

Card 5
Front: Your friend reads a coded relation: "A + B means A is the brother of B; A × B means A is the wife of B." She tries to solve P × Q + R in her head and gets confused. What is the one non-negotiable rule she must follow?
Back: Always draw the symbols one step at a time; never solve mentally. Translate each code to a visual link.

Card 6
Front: What is the "Lego Tower Trick" in Blood Relations?
Back: Build a relationship chain by adding one person at a time, clicking each new person to the last person you drew, just like stacking Lego bricks. Never skip or try to build the whole tower in your head at once.

Card 7
Front: When a question says "She is the daughter of my grandfather's only son," why does the word "only" act like a superpower?
Back: Because it collapses possibilities. "Only son" means there is exactly one son, so grandfather's only son = my father. Without "only", there could be multiple sons, making it ambiguous.

Card 8
Front: In a coded relation puzzle like "P @ Q means P is the father of Q; P # Q means P is the sister of Q", what is the most common mistake students make while decoding the chain?
Back: They mix up the direction. "P @ Q" is P father of Q, not Q father of P. Always read the code exactly as given and draw arrows accordingly.

Card 9
Front: You're solving a puzzle and the gender of a key person named "Kamal" is not revealed. The options list "Brother", "Sister", and "Cannot be determined". Which one should you choose and why?
Back: "Cannot be determined." Names do not guarantee gender in competitive exams. Never assume; wait for a gender marker like "he", "she", "brother", "wife".

Card 10
Front: A statement says "A is the only brother of B and also the son of C." How many roles does A play in this tree, and why is this a classic examiner's trap?
Back: Two roles: brother to B and son to C. The trap is dual relations. If you miss that A is both, you'll incorrectly answer questions about B's relation to C.

Card 11
Front: What is the first step you must do when you see a Blood Relation question with multiple statements?
Back: Find the anchor — the person who is speaking or pointing — and mark them as "ME" in your diagram. This gives a fixed starting point.

Card 12
Front: Why should you always trace the path backward from B to A when the question asks "How is A related to B?"
Back: Because relationships are often defined in one direction (e.g., A is the son of B). Starting from B and tracing to A reveals the exact reciprocal relation without confusion.

Card 13
Front: You are solving a Blood Relation question and you see the phrase "only brother-in-law". Why is this phrase a potential trap if you don't draw the tree?
Back: "Brother-in-law" can mean sister's husband, wife's brother, or husband's brother. Without drawing, you might assume the wrong one. The tree clarifies which relation fits the context.

Card 14
Front: What is the "Generation Row Rule" when drawing a family tree, and why does it save you from cross-generation confusion?
Back: Place every person of the same generation on the same horizontal row. This visually prevents mixing up someone's uncle with their cousin.

Card 15
Front: In a coded relation puzzle, you see "A % B means A is the mother of B". You have the expression "X % Y % Z". What two-step visual action will stop you from getting lost?
Back: First draw X (female) with a vertical line down to Y, then Y (female) with a vertical line down to Z. So X is the grandmother of Z. Draw immediately; don't mentally process the whole chain.

Card 16
Front: After building a full family tree, you still feel unsure about the answer. What is the final sanity check you should do?
Back: Re-read the original question's final line ("How is A related to B?") and physically trace the path from B to A in your tree, naming each relationship along the way.

Card 17
Front: Why is the word "only" in "my father's only brother" a superpower that instantly tells you the relationship without drawing?
Back: It means your father has exactly one brother, so that brother is your only paternal uncle (chacha or tau depending on age). The "only" removes all other possibilities.

Card 18
Front: You're told "P is the brother of Q, Q is the sister of R, and R is the daughter of S." Before drawing, what common mistake might you make about the gender of P and Q?
Back: Assuming gender from the name alone. The statement says Q is the "sister" of R, so Q is female — but names like "Kamal" can trick you. Always use the relationship word, not the name.

Card 19
Front: In a puzzle, a man says, "She is my only sister's father's only daughter." Who is she?
Back: His sister. Only sister's father = his father. Father's only daughter = his sister (since he is a man, he is a son, so the only daughter is his sister).

Card 20
Front: A girl says, "He is the son of my mother's only brother-in-law." The options are cousin, brother, uncle, or cannot be determined. What is the answer, and why is it safe to pick it?
Back: Cousin. The mother's only brother-in-law is either the father's brother or the mother's sister's husband — in both cases, his son is the girl's cousin. So the answer is cousin either way; drawing the tree confirms it.
