📊 PYQ Analysis – Order & Ranking

1. How Exams Use This Chapter (Pattern Analysis)

Order & Ranking is a low-volume but high-reliability topic: when it appears, it is almost always a direct, formula-based question that rewards speed.

- Banking exams (IBPS/SBI PO & Clerk prelims): The most consistent home for this topic. Expect questions on "persons between two people", overlap cases, and occasionally data-sufficiency framing. Difficulty: moderate, because relative order is often deliberately hidden.
- SSC (CGL, CHSL, MTS, GD): Usually 0–1 question per shift, often merged with Direction Sense or Seating Arrangement. SSC GD/MTS favour direct application of T = L + R – 1 and simple left–right conversion.
- Railways (RRB NTPC, Group D): Direct rank-conversion and "persons between" questions; occasionally odd/even rank pattern puzzles.

Frequently repeated sub-themes (in words, not numbers):
- Single-person rank from the opposite end (T = L + R – 1)
- Number of persons between two given ranks
- Interchange of positions and its effect on rank from the other end
- Minimum/maximum total strength when gaps are given
- "Cannot be determined" cases where relative order is not stated
- Comparison puzzles (height/weight/marks) — less frequent, mostly in banking

Examiner's favourite traps:
- Off-by-one errors: forgetting the +1 in T = L + R – 1, or the –1 when counting persons between two ranks.
- Left–right confusion when a question mixes "from front" and "from back".
- Assuming a relative order that the question never states (the classic route to a wrong "confident" answer).
- Near-correct options: the correct answer ±1 is almost always present as a distractor.

2. Exam‑Wise Difficulty & Trap Profile

| Exam | Average Difficulty | Common Traps |
|---|---|---|
| SSC CGL | Easy–Moderate | Forgetting –1; Left‑Right confusion; "Cannot be determined" |
| Banking (PO) | Moderate | Hidden relationships; Overlap ambiguity; Data sufficiency traps |
| Railway (NTPC) | Easy–Moderate | Odd/even rank patterns; Missing total in interchange |

3. Ten High‑Yield PYQ‑Style Questions (With Solutions & Psychological Traps)

Note: These are PYQ-style questions built on the exact patterns exams repeat. Where a concept comes from a textbook, the source is cited; otherwise the source is marked "PYQ-style".

Q1 – Basic Formula (Source: PYQ-style)

In a row of 50 students, Ravi is 15th from the left. What is his rank from the right?

Solution: Right = 50 – 15 + 1 = 36th.

Psychological Trap: The option "35" is always present, preying on the impulse to skip the +1. Your brain feels "50 – 15 = 35 is clean" and wants to move on.

⏱️ 15‑Sec Rule: This should be solved within 15 seconds. If you hesitate, mark and revisit.

Q2 – Between Two Persons (Source: PYQ-style)

In a row of 40 boys, Sameer is 14th from front; F is 9th from bottom. How many boys between them?

Solution: F from front = 40 – 9 + 1 = 32nd. Sameer = 14th. Between = |32 – 14| – 1 = 17.

Psychological Trap: The mind wants to compute directly: "9th from bottom means 31st from front?" (off‑by‑one). The options include 16, 17, 18 – rewarding the –1 error.

⏱️ 15‑Sec Rule: Straight conversion. Solve within 15 seconds.

Q3 – Overlap Ambiguity (Source: PYQ-style)

M is 72nd from the back and N is 67th from the front in a queue. No one stands between them. What is the total number of people?

Solution: Two cases are possible because the question never says who is ahead.
- Case 1 (N ahead of M, adjacent): Total = 67 + 72 = 139.
- Case 2 (M ahead of N, adjacent): M must be 66th from front (one place before N). Total = 66 + 72 – 1 = 137.
Both 137 and 139 satisfy all conditions. Answer: Cannot be determined.

Psychological Trap: The phrase "no one between" makes students assume a single arrangement and confidently add 72 + 67 – 1 = 138 — a number that is impossible here (adjacency gives either no overlap, +0, or full overlap of one position, never the "–1" of the standard between-formula). The examiner banks on this mechanical formula recall.

⏱️ 45‑Sec Rule: If the two‑case logic doesn't crystallise within 45 seconds, skip.

Q4 – Odd/Even Rank Pattern (Source: PYQ-style)

A class of 37 students. For how many students is the rank odd from both top and bottom?

Solution: Sum of top and bottom ranks = 38 (constant, even). Both ranks odd ⇒ odd + odd = even ✓, so every odd top rank works. Odd numbers from 1 to 37 = 19. Answer: 19.

Psychological Trap: The question sounds like a puzzle. Most students attempt to list ranks, get lost, and guess. It's a pattern‑recognition test disguised as ranking.

⏱️ 45‑Sec Rule: If you don't spot the "sum = constant" trick within 45 seconds, skip and return.

Q5 – Max Possible Number (Source: PYQ-style)

A is at the beginning. 6 boys between A and B, 3 between B and C. 12 more after C. Largest possible number?

Solution: To maximise, place all sequentially: 1(A) + 6 + 1(B) + 3 + 1(C) + 12 = 24.

Psychological Trap: The phrase "largest possible" signals that arrangement matters. Some students try to overlap B and C to reduce count, misreading "largest" as "minimum."

⏱️ 15‑Sec Rule: Sequential addition; solve quickly.

Q6 – Queue with Midpoint (Source: PYQ-style)

123 people. P is 62nd from front, R is 30th from back. Q is exactly midway between P and R. Q's position from back?

Solution: R from front = 123 – 30 + 1 = 94th. Gap = 94 – 62 = 32. Half = 16. Q from front = 62 + 16 = 78th. Q from back = 123 – 78 + 1 = 46th.

Psychological Trap: The "midway" logic feels like simple averaging – students do (62+94)/2 = 78th from front, which is correct. But then they forget to convert back to "from back" and mark "78" – a classic option trap.

⏱️ 45‑Sec Rule: Multi‑step. If you lose track of which end you're counting from, skip.

Q7 – Ambiguous Total (Source: PYQ-style)

Sumit is 7th from beginning, Ram is 13th from end. 5 people between them. Total?

Solution: Two arrangements possible.
- Sumit before Ram: Ram is at 7 + 5 + 1 = 13th from beginning and also 13th from end → Total = 13 + 13 – 1 = 25.
- Ram before Sumit: positions differ by 6, so Ram is 1st from beginning and 13th from end → Total = 1 + 13 – 1 = 13 (positions 1 to 7 have exactly 5 people between ✓).
Both work. Answer: Cannot be determined.

Psychological Trap: The examiner deliberately hides the relative order. The temptation is to assume Sumit is before Ram and confidently mark 25. The option "Cannot be determined" is often avoided by students who feel "I must have missed something."

⏱️ 45‑Sec Rule: Classic skip candidate. If you start drawing cases and feel uncertain, leave it.

Q8 – Minimum Persons with Gaps (Source: PYQ-style)

A, B, C in a queue. 4 persons between A & B, 7 between B & C. 11 ahead of C, 13 behind A. Minimum persons?

Solution: 11 ahead of C → C is 12th from front. B is before C with 7 between → B is 12 – 7 – 1 = 4th from front. A cannot be before B (that would need a position ≤ 0), so A is after B: A = 4 + 4 + 1 = 9th from front. 13 behind A → Total = 9 + 13 = 22. Answer: 22.

Psychological Trap: The question combines "gaps" with "ahead/behind" constraints. The mind struggles to hold three variables. Candidates often forget to subtract the person themselves when calculating position from gaps.

⏱️ 45‑Sec Rule: If the spatial arrangement doesn't form in your mind within 45 seconds, draw a line and mark; if still unclear, skip.

Q9 – Interchange (Source: PYQ-style)

Anil is 15th from front, Mukesh 18th from front. After they interchange positions, Anil becomes 15th from behind. What was Anil's original position from behind?

Solution: After the swap, Anil stands at Mukesh's old spot → his new front position = 18, and his new behind position = 15. So Total = 18 + 15 – 1 = 32. Anil's original behind rank = 32 – 15 + 1 = 18th.

Psychological Trap: The interchange question appears symmetrical, luring you to assume Anil's original behind rank = 15 (mirroring the new behind rank). The correct answer, 18, seems "too far" intuitively.

⏱️ 45‑Sec Rule: Interchange questions often involve 3–4 steps. Set a 45‑second limit; sketch the line.

Q10 – Data Sufficiency (Source: PYQ-style)

Statement 1: Priya is 4 ranks below Seema and is 31st from bottom. Statement 2: Ena is 2 ranks above Seema and is 37th from bottom. Question: What is Seema's rank from the top in a class of 40?
(a) Statement 1 alone is sufficient (b) Statement 2 alone is sufficient (c) Both together sufficient (d) Each alone sufficient.

Solution: From St 2: Ena from bottom = 37 → from top = 40 – 37 + 1 = 4th. Ena is 2 above Seema → Seema is 6th from top. St 2 alone sufficient. From St 1: Priya from bottom = 31 → from top = 40 – 31 + 1 = 10th. Priya is 4 below Seema → Seema is 6th from top. St 1 alone also sufficient. Answer: (d) Each alone sufficient.

Psychological Trap: The statements look identical in structure, tricking you into thinking "both are needed." Many choose (c) by default in data‑sufficiency questions when unsure. The examiner relies on confirmation bias.

⏱️ 45‑Sec Rule: Data‑sufficiency with ranking can be time‑saving if solved methodically. However, if the logic chain doesn't snap into place, skip after 45 seconds.

📋 Summary: Skip‑Strategy Table

| Question Type | Time Limit | Action If Stuck |
|---|---|---|
| Direct T = L + R – 1 | 15 sec | Mark, revisit later |
| Opposite‑end conversion | 15 sec | Flag if calculation seems off |
| Between / Overlap (non‑ambiguous) | 45 sec | Draw line; skip if unclear |
| Interchange | 45 sec | Sketch both "before" and "after" |
| Min‑Max / Ambiguous Total | 45 sec | Write both cases; if >2 cases, skip |
| Data Sufficiency (Ranking) | 45 sec | Test each statement independently |

Golden Rule: Never spend more than 60 seconds on any Order & Ranking question. The cost of one extra mark isn't worth losing time for 3 easier questions elsewhere.
