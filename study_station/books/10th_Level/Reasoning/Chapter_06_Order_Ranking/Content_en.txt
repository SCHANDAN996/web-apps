Order & Ranking: Crack It in One Go!
🎯 Outline & Learning Goals

By the end of this chapter, you will:

Instantly recall the "Left + Right – 1" formula and know when to use it.

Convert between left-end and right-end ranks without a pen.

Find the number of persons between two people – in both overlapping and non‑overlapping cases.

Solve interchange problems with a simple visual trick.

🔥 The Hook – Why This Chapter Is a Goldmine

Imagine you are standing in a long queue for a concert. A volunteer shouts, “You are 27th from the front!” Your friend turns back and says, “You are 13th from the end.” Can you, in a split second, tell how many people are in the queue? That’s exactly what Order & Ranking does – it turns a real‑life line into a 2‑second mathematical answer. Master this, and you’ll never waste time on arrangement puzzles again.

Most students think Order & Ranking is “easy,” so they don’t practise it. Then, in the exam, they lose a mark because of a silly ±1 mistake. Don’t be that student. Treat this chapter as a speed weapon – every second saved here is a second gained for tougher puzzles.

🤯 Common Struggle – The “Where Did That 1 Come From?” Syndrome

If you’ve ever written Total = Left + Right and then wondered why the answer is wrong by one, you are not alone. Almost everyone forgets that when you add left‑end rank and right‑end rank of the same person, you count that person twice. That’s why we subtract 1. Accept this fact now: the formula is always Total = L + R – 1.

Now, let’s break the entire chapter into bite‑sized chunks that your brain will never forget.

🧱 Chunk 1 – The Universal Formula (T = L + R – 1)

If a person is L‑th from the left and R‑th from the right,
Total persons = L + R – 1.

Why? The person is included in both counts. Draw a stick‑figure with a left arrow and a right arrow – you’ll see it immediately.

Example: Rohan is 15th from left, 9th from right.
Total = 15 + 9 – 1 = 23.

🧠 Active Recall: A girl is 8th from left and 11th from right. What is the total? (Answer: 8 + 11 – 1 = 18)

🧱 Chunk 2 – Finding the Opposite‑End Rank

When total (T) and one rank are given,
Opposite rank = T – Given Rank + 1

Example: In a class of 40, Ashish’s rank from the top is 13. Rank from bottom = 40 – 13 + 1 = 28.

Never write 40 – 13 = 27. The +1 is non‑negotiable.

🧠 Active Recall: In a row of 50, Neha is 22nd from the left. What is her rank from the right? (Answer: 50 – 22 + 1 = 29)

🧱 Chunk 3 – Persons Between Two People (Non‑Overlap)

When two people face the same direction and their positions from that end are known,
Persons between = |Position₁ – Position₂| – 1

If they face opposite ends (one left, one right), first convert both to the same end.

Example: 50 students. A is 22nd from left, B is 32nd from right.
B’s left rank = 50 – 32 + 1 = 19. A is 22nd from left.
Between = |22 – 19| – 1 = 3 – 1 = 2 students.

🧠 Active Recall: 60 people. P is 15th from left, Q is 20th from right. How many between? (First convert Q: 60 – 20 + 1 = 41; Between = 41 – 15 – 1 = 25)

🧱 Chunk 4 – Overlapping Case

When the sum of opposite‑end ranks exceeds the total, the two people overlap (their counting regions cross each other).
Persons between = (L₁ + R₂) – T – 2 (where L₁ is the left rank of one person and R₂ is the right rank of the other)

But a simpler logic: If T = 20, and A is 12th from left, B is 15th from right. Sum = 12 + 15 = 27 > 20, so overlap.
A’s right rank = 20 – 12 + 1 = 9. B’s right rank = 15. Between = 15 – 9 – 1 = 5.

Always draw a tiny line with marks to confirm.

🧠 Active Recall: T = 30. X is 18th from left, Y is 20th from right. Do they overlap? (18 + 20 = 38 > 30, yes). Persons between? (X’s right rank = 30 – 18 + 1 = 13; Y’s right rank = 20; between = 20 – 13 – 1 = 6)

🧱 Chunk 5 – Interchange of Positions

When two people swap places, the total remains the same. The key insight: after the swap, each person occupies the other’s original position. So the new rank of one person = the old rank of the other person from the same end.

Example: P is 7th from left, Q is 9th from right. They swap. Now P is 11th from left. Find the total.
Since P now sits where Q used to sit, Q was originally 11th from left. Q’s own right rank is unchanged at 9.
Total = 11 + 9 – 1 = 19.

🧠 Active Recall: P is 10th from left, Q is 14th from right. After swap, P becomes 18th from left. Find total. (P’s new left rank 18 means Q was originally 18th from left. Q’s right rank is unchanged at 14, so T = 18 + 14 – 1 = 31)

🧱 Chunk 6 – Minimum Possible Persons in a Row

Sometimes the question gives two different people – say, “A is 9th from the left and B is 7th from the right” – and asks for the minimum possible total strength of the row.

Key idea: A and B are two different persons, so they can never be the same person. The total is smallest when their counting regions overlap as much as possible – that is, when the person with the smaller rank count stands inside the other person’s count.

Minimum total = larger of (L, R)

Check with the example: A is 9th from left, B is 7th from right. Try T = 9. Then A is at the rightmost end (9th from left in a row of 9), and B’s left position = 9 – 7 + 1 = 3rd from left. Both exist, they are different persons, and no smaller row can contain someone who is 9th from left. So minimum total = 9.

What about the maximum? There is no fixed maximum. You can keep adding extra people between A and B (or beyond them) and both ranks stay the same. So unless the question adds a condition like “there are exactly k persons between them,” the maximum cannot be determined. If an option says “cannot be determined,” give it serious thought.

🧠 Active Recall: A is 5th from left, B is 8th from right. What is the minimum possible total? (Larger of 5 and 8 = 8. Check: in a row of 8, B is rightmost; A’s right rank = 8 – 5 + 1 = 4, so A is 5th from left – valid and distinct from B.)

🧠 Memory Mnemonic – “L + R – 1 = Total” sung to “Luv-Rav minus one”

Think of Luv and Rav – two friends standing in a line. To find the Total number of people, you join them (add) but one spot is shared (minus 1). Say it aloud: “Luv plus Rav minus one, total count is done!”
And for opposite‑end rank: “Total minus given plus one, runs the calculation just for fun.”

⚠️ Caution: Examiner’s Trap

The “Almost Same Rank” Illusion
Examiners give options like *36* and *37* when the correct answer is *36*. They know you’ll do L + R and forget –1. Always pause and ask, “Did I subtract the double‑counted person?”

Hidden Direction Twists
A question says, “A is 10th from the left and B is 8th from the left” – but then asks “how many are between them?” The trap: you might use 10 – 8 = 2 and forget to subtract 1. The correct answer is |10 – 8| – 1 = 1. They also love mixing left‑right without telling you to convert, making you calculate directly and get a wrong number. Always convert to a common end first.

The “Same Person” Shortcut Trap
In minimum‑total questions, many students blindly write L + R – 1, which assumes A and B are the same person. But the question says they are two different people, so that formula is invalid. Use “larger of (L, R)” instead – and if the question also fixes the number of persons between them, the total becomes fully determined, so read every condition twice.

Double Relationship / “Can’t Be Determined” Traps
In questions like “A is 6th from top, B is 12th from bottom. How many students?” the order of A and B is often left ambiguous, so the exact total cannot be determined. Many students assume a fixed answer and lose marks. If the relative position of A and B is not given, check whether multiple arrangements are possible before picking a number.
