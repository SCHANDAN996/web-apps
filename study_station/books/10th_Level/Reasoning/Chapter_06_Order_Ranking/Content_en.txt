Order & Ranking: Crack It in One Go!
🎯 Outline & Learning Goals

By the end of this chapter, you will:

🤯 Common Struggle – The “Where Did That 1 Come From?” Syndrome

If you’ve ever written Total = Left + Right and then wondered why the answer is wrong by one, you are not alone. Almost everyone forgets that when you add left‑end rank and right‑end rank of the same person, you count that person twice. That’s why we subtract 1. Accept this fact now: the formula is always Total = L + R – 1.

Now, let’s break the entire chapter into bite‑sized chunks that your brain will never forget.

🧱 Chunk 1 – The Universal Formula (T = L + R – 1)

If a person is L‑th from the left and R‑th from the right,
Total persons = L + R – 1.

Why? The person is included in both counts. Draw a stick‑figure with a left arrow and a right arrow – you’ll see it immediately.

Example: Rohan is 15th from left, 9th from right.
Total = 15 + 9 – 1 = 23.

🧠 Active Recall: A girl is 8th from left and 11th from right. What is the total? (Try mentally before reading on)

🧱 Chunk 2 – Finding the Opposite‑End Rank

When total (T) and one rank are given,
Opposite rank = T – Given Rank + 1

Example: In a class of 40, Ashish’s rank from the top is 13. Rank from bottom = 40 – 13 + 1 = 28.

Never write 40 – 13 = 27. The +1 is non‑negotiable.

🧠 Active Recall: In a row of 50, Neha is 22nd from the left. What is her rank from the right? (Answer: 50–22+1=29)

🧱 Chunk 3 – Persons Between Two People (Non‑Overlap)

When two people face the same direction and their positions from that end are known,
Persons between = |Position₁ – Position₂| – 1

If they face opposite ends (one left, one right), first convert both to the same end.

Example: 50 students. A is 22nd from left, B is 32nd from right.
B’s left rank = 50–32+1 = 19. A is 22.
Between = |22–19| – 1 = 3–1 = 2 students.

🧠 Active Recall: 60 people. P is 15th from left, Q is 20th from right. How many between? (First convert Q: 60–20+1=41; Between = 41–15–1 = 25)

🧱 Chunk 4 – Overlapping Case

When the sum of opposite‑end ranks exceeds the total, the two people overlap.
Persons between = (L₁ + R₂) – T – 2 (where L₁ is left rank of one, R₂ is right rank of the other)

But a simpler logic: If T = 20, and A is 12th from left, B is 15th from right. Sum = 12+15=27 > 20, so overlap.
A’s right rank = 20–12+1 = 9. B’s right rank = 15. Between = 15–9–1 = 5.

Always draw a tiny line with marks to confirm.

🧠 Active Recall: T=30. X is 18th from left, Y is 20th from right. Do they overlap? (18+20=38>30, yes). Persons between? (X right rank = 30–18+1=13; Y right rank =20; between = 20–13–1=6)

🧱 Chunk 5 – Interchange of Positions

When two people swap places and a new rank is given, the total remains the same. Use the swap to find the other’s original rank.

Trick: If P and Q swap, and P becomes 11th from left (was 7th), then the number of people between them = new left rank of P – old left rank of P = 4. So Q must have been 4 positions to the right of P originally. If P was 7th left, Q’s old left rank = 7+4+1 = 12 (if Q is to the right), etc. Better yet, a single formula:

When two persons swap and one’s rank from an end is given before and after:
Movement = |new rank – old rank| = number of people between them + 1 (if they move past each other). But more reliably, sketch a line.

Example: P is 7th from left, Q is 9th from right. They swap. Now P is 11th from left. Total?
Q’s new left rank = old left rank of P + (new left rank of P – old left rank of P) = 7 + (11–7) = 11? Actually careful: P moved 4 places to the right, meaning Q was originally 4 places to the right of P. So Q’s left rank originally = 7+4+1? No, P 7th, Q must be 11th from left originally (since P after swap goes to Q’s place, and P is now 11th from left). So Q originally was 11th from left. Q’s right rank is still 9. Then total = 11 + 9 – 1 = 19. This is a standard approach: the person who swaps takes the other’s position, so their new rank = the other’s old rank from that end. Then total can be found by using that person’s new rank from one end and their own unchanged rank from the opposite end.

🧠 Active Recall: P is 10th from left, Q is 14th from right. After swap, P becomes 18th from left. Find total. (P’s new left rank 18 means that originally Q was 18th from left. Q’s right rank unchanged 14, so T = 18+14–1=31)

🧱 Chunk 6 – Minimum / Maximum Persons in a Row

When “A is 9th from left and B is 7th from right” and they interchange, but we don’t know the order, the minimum total occurs when there is maximum overlap, and maximum total when there is no overlap.

Minimum T (when they are the same person): L + R – 1 = 9+7–1 = 15.

Maximum T (when they are at opposite extremes with no one between): L + R = 9+7 = 16. But if condition says “there are some persons between”, adjust accordingly.

Examiners love asking “What can be the maximum possible strength?” – So always check both extremes.

🧠 Active Recall: A is 5th from left, B is 8th from right. What is the minimum possible total? (5+8–1=12)

🧠 Memory Mnemonic – “L + R – 1 = Total” sung to “Luv-Rav minus one”

Think of Luv and Rav – two friends standing in a line. To find the Total number of people, you join them (add) but one spot is shared (minus 1). Say it aloud: “Luv plus Rav minus one, total count is done!”
And for opposite‑end rank: “Total minus given plus one, runs the calculation just for fun.”

⚠️ Caution: Examiner’s Trap

The “Almost Same Rank” Illusion
Examiners give options like *36* and *37* when the correct answer is *36*. They know you’ll do L+R and forget –1. Always pause and ask, “Did I subtract the double‑counted person?”

Hidden Direction Twists
A question says, “A is 10th from the left and B is 8th from the left” – but then asks “how many are between them?” The trap: you might use 10–8=2 and forget to subtract 1. The correct answer is |10–8| – 1 = 1. They also love mixing left‑right without telling you to convert, making you calculate directly and get a wrong number. Always convert to a common end first.

Double Relationship / “Can’t Be Determined” Traps
In questions like “A is 6th from top, B is 12th from bottom. How many students?” they often leave the order ambiguous, making the answer “cannot be determined.” Many students assume a fixed answer and lose marks. If the relative position of A and B is not given, check if multiple arrangements are possible.
