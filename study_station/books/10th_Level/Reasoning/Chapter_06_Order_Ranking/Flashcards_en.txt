### 🃏 Flashcards – Order & Ranking

Card 1
Front: What is the golden formula when the same person is counted from both ends?
Back: Total = Left rank + Right rank – 1 (T = L + R – 1)

Card 2
Front: In a row of 50, Priya is 22nd from the left. What is her rank from the right?
Back: 50 – 22 + 1 = 29th from right. (Opposite rank = Total – Given rank + 1)

Card 3
Front: Why do we subtract 1 in T = L + R – 1?
Back: Because the person is counted twice — once from the left, once from the right. Subtracting 1 corrects the double-counting.

Card 4
Front: Ravi is 11th from the front and 14th from the back. How many people are in the queue?
Back: 11 + 14 – 1 = 24 people.

Card 5
Front: A is 15th from left, B is 12th from right, total strength is 30. Do they overlap?
Back: No, because 15 + 12 = 27, which is less than 30. Overlap occurs only when the sum of the two ranks is greater than the total.

Card 6
Front: A is 10th from left, B is 8th from right. Total people = 18. How many persons are between them?
Back: Convert B to left: 18 – 8 + 1 = 11th from left. Between = |10 – 11| – 1 = 0. They are adjacent.

Card 7
Front: In an interchange problem, how do you find the total strength?
Back: After the swap, one person's new rank from one end equals the other person's old rank from that same end. So: Total = New rank of one person from one end + Old rank of the other person from the opposite end – 1.

Card 8
Front: What mnemonic helps to instantly recall the total formula?
Back: "Left plus Right, minus one — Total done, oh what fun!"

Card 9
Front: What simple trick helps avoid confusion in overlap cases?
Back: Always convert both ranks to the same end (say left) first. Then use |L1 – L2| – 1 to find persons between. This eliminates the risk of mixing opposite ends.

Card 10
Front: A row has 40 students. Rohit is 18th from left. How many students are to his right?
Back: Total – Left rank = 40 – 18 = 22 students to his right (not counting Rohit himself).

Card 11
Front: What is the minimum possible total when A is 6th from left and B is 9th from right, with no other information?
Back: Minimum total = 6 + 9 – 1 = 14 (maximum overlap — A and B are the same person).

Card 12
Front: What is the maximum possible total when A is 6th from left and B is 9th from right, with no other information?
Back: Maximum total = 6 + 9 = 15 (no overlap — they are at opposite extremes).

Card 13
Front: Why might an examiner's trap answer be "cannot be determined" in an Order & Ranking question?
Back: When the relative positions of two persons are not specified, multiple arrangements are possible. For example, "A is 5th from left, B is 4th from right" without saying whether A is left or right of B — the total cannot be uniquely fixed.

Card 14
Front: What is the quick trick to find the number of persons between two people when both ranks are from the same end?
Back: Difference of ranks minus 1. E.g., 15th and 21st from left → (21 – 15) – 1 = 5 persons between.

Card 15
Front: P is 7th from left, Q is 13th from right. They interchange places and P becomes 15th from left. Find the total.
Back: P's new left rank (15) = Q's original left rank. Q's right rank stays 13. So total = 15 + 13 – 1 = 27.

Card 16
Front: In an overlapping case, A is 14th from left, B is 11th from right, total 20. How many are between them?
Back: A's right rank = 20 – 14 + 1 = 7. B's right rank = 11. Between = 11 – 7 – 1 = 3.

Card 17
Front: What common mistake do students make when using T = L + R?
Back: They forget to subtract 1, counting the person twice — an off-by-one error. Examiners often include both L+R and L+R–1 among the options.

Card 18
Front: What is the memory aid for converting opposite-end ranks?
Back: "Total minus given plus one, runs the calculation just for fun." (Opposite rank = Total – Given rank + 1)

Card 19
Front: A is 9th from left, B is 9th from right. Can they be the same person?
Back: Yes, if total = 9 + 9 – 1 = 17, they are exactly the same person.

Card 20
Front: What should you check when a question says "some persons are sitting between them" but gives no exact number?
Back: The problem may allow multiple possibilities or a range of totals. Check whether the answer is "cannot be determined" or whether you must find the minimum/maximum. Never assume a fixed total.
