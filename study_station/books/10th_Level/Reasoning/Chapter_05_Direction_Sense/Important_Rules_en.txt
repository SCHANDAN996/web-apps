Key Rules/Approaches
Rule Name	Description	Example
Golden Rule: Update Facing Direction	After every turn, pause and identify your current facing direction before moving.	Facing North, turn left → now West. Next, turn right → now North again.
Nose Compass Trick	Physically (or mentally) turn your nose to match each directional change; the final nose direction is your answer.	Start facing East, right turn → nose South, left turn → nose East again.
Left/Right Turn Mapping	Left = 90° anticlockwise from current facing. Right = 90° clockwise from current facing.	Facing South: left → East, right → West.
About Turn = 180°	“Turned back” or “about turn” means facing the exact opposite direction.	Facing North, about turn → South.
Shadow Direction (Time-Based)	Morning (Sun East) → shadow West. Evening (Sun West) → shadow East. Noon (Sun South, N hemisphere) → shadow North.	At 7 AM, shadow always falls West. If shadow is to your right, deduce face direction.
Shortest Distance (Displacement)	If path ends at a right angle from start, use Pythagoras theorem for straight-line distance.	Walk 6 km East, 8 km North → displacement = √(6²+8²) = 10 km.
Visual Path Drawing	Always draw arrows for each leg, labelling distances and updating orientation.	Drawing prevents mixing up left/right and helps calculate final position.
Time Management
Rule	Description
15‑Second Skip Rule	If within 15 seconds you cannot identify the initial facing direction or the first turn, skip the question immediately.
45‑Second Mark & Move Rule	If a question involves more than 4 consecutive turns, or complex shadow + time conditions, and you haven’t drawn a clear path within 45 seconds, mark it and move on. Return only if time permits.
Quick Visual Check	For simple 2‑3 leg problems, you should solve in under 30 seconds by drawing a small diagram without overthinking.
Avoid Double‑Reading	Do not re‑read the entire problem repeatedly; extract the sequence of moves once and work from your diagram.
