76. At 8 a.m., a man stands in a field. His shadow falls exactly behind him. In which direction is he facing?
(a) West (b) East (c) North (d) South
Answer: (b)
Solution: In the morning the Sun is in the East, so shadows fall toward the West. The tempting option is West, but if he faced West his shadow would fall in front of him, not behind. Since the shadow is behind him, he is facing East.

77. A person walks 20 m East, then turns left and walks 20 m, and then turns right and walks 10 m. What is the shortest distance from the starting point?
(a) 30 m (b) 10√13 m (c) 20 m (d) 25 m
Answer: (b)
Solution: Net East displacement = 20 + 10 = 30 m and North displacement = 20 m. The trap is to add all distances (50 m) or just take 30 m, but the shortest distance is the diagonal: √(30² + 20²) = √1300 = 10√13 m.

78. A boy walks 30 m South, turns right and walks 40 m, turns right and walks 30 m, and finally turns left and walks 20 m. In which direction is he from his starting point?
(a) North (b) East (c) West (d) South
Answer: (c)
Solution: The 30 m South and the later 30 m North cancel out. The 40 m and 20 m are both walked toward the West, giving 60 m West. The trap is to treat the last left turn as East, so the answer is West.

80. A is 5 m North of B. C is 12 m East of A. D is 5 m South of C. What is the shortest distance between A and D?
(a) 10 m (b) 12 m (c) 5 m (d) 13 m
Answer: (d)
Solution: From A to C is 12 m East and from C to D is 5 m South, forming a right triangle with legs 12 and 5. The trap is to add 12 + 5 = 17 or take just 12, but AD = √(12² + 5²) = √169 = 13 m.

81. A person is facing North. He turns 45° clockwise, then 90° anticlockwise, and finally 135° clockwise. In which direction is he facing now?
(a) North (b) South (c) East (d) West
Answer: (c)
Solution: Net rotation = 45° − 90° + 135° = 90° clockwise. The trap is to add all three angles without signs, which gives 270°, but turning 90° clockwise from North gives East.

Answer: (c)
Solution: The 15 m North and 15 m South cancel out. Net horizontal displacement = 8 − 6 = 2 m toward West; the trap is to subtract the wrong way and say 2 m East. So he is 2 m West of the start.

Answer: (a)
Solution: At noon the Sun is roughly toward the South in India (which lies north of the equator), so shadows fall toward the North. The trap is to think the shadow falls behind him toward the South, but a shadow always falls opposite the Sun, so the answer is North.

84. If 'East' is called 'North', 'West' as 'South', 'North' as 'West', and 'South' as 'East', what will 'North-West' be called?
(a) South-West (b) North-East (c) South-East (d) West-North
Answer: (a)
Solution: North becomes West and West becomes South, so North-West becomes West-South, i.e. South-West. The trap is to swap only one component, which wrongly gives North-East or South-East. So the answer is South-West.

Answer: (c)
Solution: He reaches (10, 0), goes 10 m North to (10, 10), then reverses and walks 20 m South to (10, −10). The trap is to cancel the 10 m North with only 10 m of the return walk and stop at North-East, but the full 20 m takes him South, so the answer is South-East.

90. If 'South-East' is called 'North', what will 'West' be called? (assuming uniform rotation)
(a) South-East (b) North-East (c) South-West (d) North-West
Answer: (a)
Solution: South-East shifting to North is a 135° anticlockwise rotation. Applying the same 135° anticlockwise shift to West gives South-East; the trap is to rotate clockwise instead, which gives North-West. So the answer is South-East.
