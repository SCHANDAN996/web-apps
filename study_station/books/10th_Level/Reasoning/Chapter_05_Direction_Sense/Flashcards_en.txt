🃏 Flashcard 1
Front: You are facing North. You turn left, then right, then left again. Which direction are you facing now?
Back: West. (North → left → West, right → North, left → West.)

🃏 Flashcard 2
Front: What simple phrase helps you recall the clockwise order of the four main directions?
Back: “Never Eat Soggy Waffles” – North, East, South, West.

🃏 Flashcard 3
Front: A man walks 6 km east and 8 km north. What is his shortest distance from the start?
Back: 10 km. (Right triangle 6‑8‑10, by Pythagoras theorem.)

🃏 Flashcard 4
Front: At sunrise, which direction does your shadow point?
Back: West.

🃏 Flashcard 5
Front: You are facing South. Your left hand points to which direction?
Back: East. (From South, left is East; right is West.)

🃏 Flashcard 6
Front: What is the trick of using your body to keep track of turns in direction questions called in the chapter?
Back: The Nose Compass — you physically (or mentally) turn your nose to update facing direction.

🃏 Flashcard 7
Front: Ravi walks 10 m west, turns left and walks 5 m, then turns left again and walks 10 m. How far is he from his starting point?
Back: 5 metres. (He forms a rectangle and returns to a point directly south of start.)

🃏 Flashcard 8
Front: In the evening, a boy’s shadow falls exactly to his left. Which direction is he facing?
Back: North. (Sun in west → shadow east. East on his left means he faces north.)

🃏 Flashcard 9
Front: What is an “about turn” in direction sense?
Back: Turning 180° to face the exact opposite direction.

🃏 Flashcard 10
Front: Examiner’s Trap: Why do students often lose marks in shadow-based direction questions?
Back: They forget to check the time of day (morning/evening/noon) before applying the shadow rule. A shadow ‘in front’ at 7 AM means something very different from 4 PM.

🃏 Flashcard 11
Front: A girl is facing North-East. She turns 45° clockwise. Which direction is she facing now?
Back: East. (NE + 45° clockwise = East.)

🃏 Flashcard 12
Front: A policeman chases a thief: 40 m east, 30 m north, 40 m west. Is he back at the starting point?
Back: No, he is 30 m north of the start.

🃏 Flashcard 13
Front: Examiner’s Trap: A question says “he walked 5 m north, 4 m east, then turned back and walked 5 m”. What is the common mistake?
Back: Students forget that “turned back” means a 180° reversal, not a left/right turn. They miscalculate the final leg direction.

🃏 Flashcard 14
Front: Which three Pythagorean triples are most useful for direction sense questions?
Back: 3‑4‑5, 5‑12‑13, 8‑15‑17.

🃏 Flashcard 15
Front: If it is noon and a man’s shadow is exactly to his right, which direction is he facing? (Assume northern hemisphere)
Back: West. (Sun in south → shadow north. North on right means facing west.)

🃏 Flashcard 16
Front: How can you quickly convert a left turn into the new facing direction without memorising a table?
Back: Move 90° anti‑clockwise from your current facing direction. Use the “Nose Compass” trick.

🃏 Flashcard 17
Front: What is the “golden rule” to never get lost in multi‑turn direction problems?
Back: After every single turn, pause and ask yourself “Which way am I facing now?” Update mentally before the next move.

🃏 Flashcard 18
Front: A person walks 1 km east, 1.5 km north, 1 km west, and 0.5 km south. Draw the path. Is he now east or west of his starting point?
Back: He is 1 km north of the start, neither east nor west (east and west cancel).
