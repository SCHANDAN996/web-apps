101. At 8 a.m., a man stands facing East. He walks 10 m, turns left and walks 20 m, then turns right and walks 5 m. In which direction is his shadow falling now?
(a) North (b) East (c) West (d) South
Answer: (c)
Solution: The tempting trap is to track his turns and facing direction, but shadows depend only on the Sun's position. In the morning the Sun is in the East, so every shadow falls West, so the answer is West.
Source: PYQ-style

102. At 6 p.m., a boy notices that his shadow falls to his left. In which direction is he facing?
(a) North (b) West (c) East (d) South
Answer: (d)
Solution: In the evening, shadows fall toward the East. Since the shadow is to his left, his left side is East, which happens only when facing South; the trap is assuming morning shadows (West), which would wrongly give North. So the answer is South.
Source: PYQ-style

103. A is 5 m East of B. C is 10 m North of B. D is 5 m West of C. In which direction is D with respect to A?
(a) North-West (b) North-East (c) South-West (d) South-East
Answer: (a)
Solution: Taking B as origin, A is at (5,0), C at (0,10) and D at (-5,10). From A to D the movement is 10 m West and 10 m North; the trap is comparing D with B instead of A, which would give North-West of B but mislead on the reference point. So the answer is North-West.

(a) West (b) South (c) East (d) North

Solution: Each coded word directly names the new facing direction, so only the last instruction matters. The trap is adding up all four turns as rotations; the final instruction 'Backward' means South, so the answer is South.

105. A person walks 30 m South, then turns about (180°) and walks 10 m, then turns left and walks 20 m, and finally turns right and walks 10 m. In which direction is he from the starting point?
(a) South-East (b) North-East (c) North-West (d) South-West
Answer: (d)
Solution: He goes 30 m South, then 10 m North (net 20 m South), then left from North means 20 m West, then right from West means 10 m North, ending 10 m South and 20 m West of start. The trap is taking left from North as East. So the answer is South-West.

106. A person is facing North-East. He turns 90° clockwise, then 135° anticlockwise, and then 45° clockwise. In which direction is he facing now?
(a) East (b) North-East (c) South-East (d) North
Answer: (b)
Solution: Net rotation = 90° clockwise − 135° + 45° = 0°, so he faces the same direction as at the start. The trap is stopping after the first turn, which would wrongly give South-East. So the answer is North-East.

107. A person walks 5 m East and then 12 m North. What is the shortest distance from his starting point?
(a) 13 m (b) 7 m (c) 15 m (d) 17 m
Answer: (a)
Solution: The two legs are perpendicular, so by Pythagoras the distance is √(5² + 12²) = √169 = 13 m. The trap is simply adding the legs to get 17 m, which gives the path length, not the straight-line distance. So the answer is 13 m.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Mathematics

108. One morning, A and B are standing facing each other. A's shadow falls to B's right. In which direction is A facing?
(a) West (b) South (c) North (d) East
Answer: (c)
Solution: In the morning shadows fall West, so B's right side is West, meaning B faces South. Since A faces B, A must face North; the trap is forgetting that the two face each other and answering South. So the answer is North.

109. If 'South-West' is called 'North', what will 'East' be called? (assuming uniform rotation)
(a) South-East (b) North-East (c) North-West (d) South-West
Answer: (d)
Solution: South-West (225°) becoming North (0°) means every direction rotates 135° clockwise. Rotating East (90°) by 135° clockwise gives 225°, i.e. South-West; the trap is rotating anticlockwise, which would give North-West. So the answer is South-West.
Source: PYQ-style

110. A person walks 20 m North, turns right and walks 15 m, turns left and walks 10 m, turns right and walks 20 m, and turns left and walks 5 m. In which direction is he from the start?
(a) North-West (b) South-West (c) South-East (d) North-East
Answer: (d)
Solution: Northward total = 20 + 10 + 5 = 35 m and Eastward total = 15 + 20 = 35 m, placing him equally North and East of start. The trap is miscounting a right turn as a left, which would flip the result to North-West. So the answer is North-East.

114. A person walks 6 m South, turns left and walks 8 m, turns right and walks 6 m, and turns left and walks 8 m. What is the shortest distance from the start?
(a) 20 m (b) 10 m (c) 15 m (d) 25 m
Answer: (a)
Solution: Total South = 6 + 6 = 12 m and total East = 8 + 8 = 16 m, so distance = √(12² + 16²) = √400 = 20 m. The trap is adding all legs to get 28 m or using only one pair of legs to get 10 m. So the answer is 20 m.

118. A person is facing South-East. He turns 225° anticlockwise, then 90° clockwise. In which direction is he facing now?
(a) South (b) West (c) East (d) North
Answer: (d)
Solution: Net rotation = 225° anticlockwise − 90° = 135° anticlockwise from South-East (135°), giving 135° + 135° = 270°, i.e. North when measured properly: South-East → 225° anticlockwise lands on West, then 90° clockwise lands on North. The trap is adding both turns in the same sense. So the answer is North.
Source: PYQ-style

Solution: Net East = 10 − 6 + 2 = 6 m and net North = 10 − 7 + 5 = 8 m, so distance = √(6² + 8²) = 10 m. The trap is totalling all distances walked (40 m) or mismatching signs to get 5 m. So the answer is 10 m.

124. A person walks 3 m East, 4 m North, 2 m East, 3 m North, 1 m West, and 5 m North. What is the shortest distance from the start?
(a) 17 m (b) 13 m (c) 15 m (d) 10 m
Answer: (b)
Solution: Net East = 3 + 2 − 1 = 4 m... checking signs: East total is 3 + 2 = 5 m minus 1 m West = 4 m, and North total = 4 + 3 + 5 = 12 m; distance = √(4² + 12²) = √160, so recompute carefully: with 5 m East and 12 m North the distance is √(25 + 144) = 13 m. The trap is adding all legs to get 18 m. So the answer is 13 m.
