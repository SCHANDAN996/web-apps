Front: What is the biggest weapon for solving puzzles?
Back: Making a table/grid — persons on one side, attributes on the other.

Front: What is the correct order of filling clues?
Back: First definite (direct), then negative (cross out cells), then relative (above/below/between).

Front: What to fill first in a floor puzzle?
Back: The definite clue — like "Ram on the topmost floor".

Front: Difference between "immediately above" and "above"?
Back: "Immediately above" = the very next floor; "above" = anywhere higher.

Front: What is the use of negative clues?
Back: Clues like "X not on Monday" cross out cells and speed up the solution.

Front: What are the three main types?
Back: Floor puzzle, scheduling/day puzzle, and matching puzzle.

Front: What to do with "immediately above/below" pairs?
Back: Always keep them together — they never separate.

Front: 3 people on 3 floors; A on top, C at the bottom — where is B?
Back: In the middle (the middle floor).

Front: What must you do at the end of a puzzle?
Back: Re-check every clue — if any breaks, there is a mistake.

Front: Strategy for a big (7+ variable) puzzle?
Back: Solve small (3-5 person) puzzles first, big ones later.

Front: Why fill the definite clue first?
Back: Because it is certain; from it the other cells begin to fix themselves.

Front: What is matched in a matching puzzle?
Back: Two or more attributes like person ↔ city ↔ colour ↔ liking.

Front: Why build the table in one direction?
Back: So there is no up/down confusion and clues fit the right place.

Front: What is the most common trap?
Back: Guessing early without filling the definite clues.

Front: What is the puzzle mnemonic?
Back: "Make a table, fill definite first".
