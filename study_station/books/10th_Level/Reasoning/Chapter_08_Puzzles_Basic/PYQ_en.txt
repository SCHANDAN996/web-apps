📊 PYQ Analysis: Simple Puzzles

6.1 How Often Do Puzzles Appear? (Pattern Overview)

We do not have verified year-wise question counts, so instead of numbers, here is the reliable qualitative pattern every aspirant should know:

- **Banking exams (SBI/IBPS PO & Clerk, RRB Clerk):** Puzzles and seating arrangement together form the single largest block of the Reasoning section — typically several sets of 4–5 linked questions each in prelims, and even more in mains. A candidate who masters puzzles effectively masters bank reasoning.
- **SSC CGL / CHSL / MTS / GD:** Puzzles appear regularly but in small numbers — usually one short set or 1–3 standalone questions (ranking, direction, simple seating). Difficulty is easy to moderate.
- **RRB NTPC:** Puzzles and seating arrangement are a consistent part of the reasoning portion, asked as short sets or standalone questions of easy-to-moderate difficulty.

**Trend insight:** In banking exams, puzzle sets have grown longer and more constraint-heavy over the years (mixed facing, uncertain number of persons, floor + scheduling combinations). SSC and RRB have kept puzzles short and scoring.

6.2 Which Sub-Types Matter Most

Based on the recurring pattern across exams (qualitative, not percentage-based):

| Sub-Topic | Where It Dominates | Trend |
|---|---|---|
| Linear Seating (single/two row) | All exams | Most frequently asked subtype everywhere |
| Circular Seating (facing centre/outside/mixed) | Banking mainly; occasionally SSC | "Mixed facing" variant rising in Bank PO |
| Comparison / Ranking puzzles | SSC, RRB NTPC, all exams | Short, quick, high-yield |
| Floor / Building / Box-stacking puzzles | Banking, RRB | Rising trend in recent years |
| Scheduling (day/month/year based) | Banking Clerk, PO | Consistent; usually one full set |
| Direction-Distance mini puzzles | SSC, RRB NTPC | Standalone 1-mark questions |

6.3 Exam-Wise Focus (Qualitative)

| Exam | Role of Puzzles | Typical Difficulty | Key Focus Sub-Types |
|---|---|---|---|
| SSC CGL | A few questions per paper | Easy | Linear seating, ranking, direction |
| SSC MTS / GD | Occasional standalone questions | Easy | Direction-sense, simple ranking |
| SBI/IBPS PO | Dominant — multiple full sets | Moderate–Hard | Circular (mixed facing), floor, scheduling, uncertain-row |
| IBPS/RRB Clerk | Dominant — multiple full sets | Moderate | Linear, floor, day-based, comparison |
| RRB NTPC | Regular short sets | Easy–Moderate | Linear seating, ranking, direction |

**Strategy takeaway:** For banking, puzzles are the make-or-break topic — practise full 5-question sets under time pressure. For SSC/RRB, treat puzzles as quick scoring questions and never let one set eat more than 2 minutes.

6.4 Top 10 High-Yield Questions (With Solutions & Trap Analysis)

**Q1. Linear Seating — "Second to the Left" Trap (PYQ-style)**

Six persons P, Q, R, S, T, U sit in a row facing north. R sits at the extreme right end. P sits second to the left of Q. S sits immediately to the left of P. T sits between Q and R. Who sits at the extreme left end?

(a) P (b) S (c) T (d) U

**Solution:** Positions 1–6 left to right; R = 6. "T between Q and R" forces Q = 4 and T = 5. Then P (second left of Q) = 2, and S (immediate left of P) = 1. U fills 3. Extreme left = **S → (b)**.

**Trap:** Students read "second to the left" as "immediately left" and place P at 3, breaking the whole row. Always count the gap: "second to the left" means one person sits in between.

**Skip Advice:** Do NOT skip — a single-row puzzle with all definite clues is solvable in under 60 seconds.

**Q2. Linear Uncertain-Persons Trap (PYQ-style, Bank pattern)**

A certain number of persons sit in a row facing north. A sits 4th from the left end. B sits 3rd from the right end. Three persons sit between A and B. Only two persons sit to the left of C. How many persons are there in the row?

(a) 9 (b) 10 (c) 11 (d) 12

**Solution:** A is at position 4. B is 3rd from right, i.e., at position n−2. Three persons between A and B means their positions differ by 4: (n−2) − 4 = 4 → n = 10. (C sits at position 3 — consistent with the row.) Answer: **10 → (b)**.

**Trap (Extra Relationship):** The clue about C is inserted after you have already solved for A and B. Students often restart the entire puzzle instead of simply using the row length they already found. This wastes 60–90 seconds on redundant recalculation.

**Skip Advice:** Attempt, but set a hard 90-second limit. If the algebra stalls, mark and move on.

**Q3. Floor-Based Puzzle (PYQ-style, RRB pattern)**

A, B, C, D, E live on 5 floors (1 = bottom, 5 = top). D lives immediately above E. C lives immediately below B. E and C live on even-numbered floors. A does not live immediately below E. Who lives on the top floor?

(a) B (b) C (c) D (d) E

**Solution:** E and C occupy floors 2 and 4 in some order.
- Case 1: E = 2, D = 3, C = 4, B = 5 → A = 1. But then A (floor 1) is immediately below E (floor 2) — violates the condition. Rejected.
- Case 2: E = 4, D = 5, C = 2, B = 3 → A = 1. A is not immediately below E (floors 1 and 4 are far apart) — valid.
Top floor = **D → (c)**.

**Trap (Vague "Below"):** "Below" alone does not mean "immediately below." Students who assume it does will wrongly eliminate the correct case. Only trust "immediately" when the word is actually written.

**Skip Advice:** Do NOT skip — a clean two-case puzzle with a definite resolution.

**Q4. Comparison / Ranking Trap (PYQ-style)**

Five friends B, C, D, E, A compare heights. C is taller than only one person. E is neither the tallest nor the shortest. Two persons are taller than A. B is not the tallest. Who is the tallest?

(a) B (b) E (c) A (d) D

**Solution:** "C taller than only one" → C is 4th. "Two persons taller than A" → A is 3rd. E is neither tallest nor shortest → E is 2nd. B is not tallest → B is 5th (shortest), leaving D at the top. Order: D > E > A > C > B. Tallest = **D → (d)**.

**Trap:** The negative clue "B is not the tallest" is placed last deliberately, so you first build a full order and then must retro-fit B. Handle negative clues as hard constraints from the start, not as afterthoughts.

**Skip Advice:** Do NOT skip — 40 seconds, guaranteed marks.

**Q5. Day-Based Scheduling (PYQ-style, Bank Clerk pattern)**

Seven persons K, L, M, N, P, Q and one more take leave on different days from Monday to Sunday. L takes leave on Friday. Only two persons take leave between L and Q. P takes leave one day before M. N takes leave after K but before P. K does not take leave on Monday. Who takes leave on Wednesday?

(a) N (b) Q (c) K (d) P

**Solution:** Number the days Mon = 1 … Sun = 7. L = 5 (Friday). "Two persons between L and Q" means positions differ by 3 → Q = 2 (Tuesday), since 5 + 3 = 8 is invalid. Remaining days: 1, 3, 4, 6, 7. P immediately before M → consecutive pair; the only consecutive pair available is 6–7, so P = 6, M = 7. K < N < P with K ≠ 1 → K = 3, N = 4. Wednesday (day 3) = **K → (c)**.

**Trap ("Between" Misinterpretation):** "Only two persons take leave between L and Q" counts *persons*, not calendar days. Two persons between means a position gap of 3. Misreading this as a 2-day gap collapses the whole arrangement.

**Skip Advice:** Attempt, but if the "between" clue confuses you beyond 60 seconds, mark for review and return.

**Q6. Two-Row Arrangement, Opposite Facing (PYQ-style, SSC pattern)**

Six persons sit in two rows of three. A, B, C sit in Row-1 facing south; D, E, F sit in Row-2 facing north, each directly opposite a Row-1 person. B sits opposite the person who is immediately to the right of E. A sits at an extreme end of Row-1. D does not sit opposite A. Who sits opposite C?

(a) D (b) E (c) F (d) A

**Solution:** Number positions 1–3 from the viewer's left in both rows. A north-facing person's right hand points to the viewer's left. Place E at Row-2 position 2; the person immediately to E's right is at Row-2 position 1. B sits opposite that person → B is at Row-1 position 1. A must be at an extreme end → A = Row-1 position 3, so C = Row-1 position 2. D does not sit opposite A → D is not at Row-2 position 3; E is at 2, so D = Row-2 position 1 and F = Row-2 position 3. Opposite C (Row-1 position 2) = **E → (b)**.

**Trap (Direction Reversal):** A south-facing person's left is the viewer's right; a north-facing person's left is the viewer's left. The examiner counts on you applying the same left-right logic to both rows, producing a mirror-image error. Always fix facing direction *before* reading "left/right" clues.

**Skip Advice:** Do NOT skip if you have practised parallel rows. Skip only if the facing logic forces more than 2 cases.

**Q7. Weight Comparison with Numbers (PYQ-style, RRB pattern)**

P, Q, R, S, T compare their weights. Q is lighter than only S. R is not the lightest. P is heavier than R but lighter than Q. The heaviest person weighs 84 kg. The second lightest person weighs 46 kg. What could be the weight of T?

(a) 48 kg (b) 57 kg (c) 42 kg (d) 82 kg

**Solution:** "Q lighter than only S" → S heaviest, Q second. "P heavier than R but lighter than Q" → Q > P > R. "R not lightest" → T is lightest. Order: S > Q > P > R > T. Second lightest = R = 46 kg, so T < 46 kg. Only option below 46 is **42 kg → (c)**.

**Trap (Quantitative Overlay):** The numbers (84 kg, 46 kg) disguise a pure ranking puzzle. Students switch into "arithmetic mode" and try to compute exact weight ranges, when all that is needed is: T is the lightest, so T < 46.

**Skip Advice:** Do NOT skip — a 30-second puzzle wearing a quantitative costume.

**Q8. Box Stacking Puzzle (PYQ-style, SSC/Bank pattern)**

Seven boxes A to G are kept one above another, numbered 1 (bottom) to 7 (top). G is kept on a perfect-square-numbered position. C is kept three boxes above G. A is kept just below D. E is kept two boxes below A. Neither B nor F is kept adjacent to G. B is kept above F. How many boxes are kept between A and F?

(a) 1 (b) 2 (c) 3 (d) 4

**Solution:** Perfect squares between 1 and 7: 1 and 4. G = 4 (G = 1 fails because C must be three above G and the remaining clues cannot fit). Then C = 7. A just below D with E two below A → D = 6, A = 5, E = 3. Remaining positions 1 and 2: B above F → B = 2, F = 1. Check: B (2) and F (1) are not adjacent to G (4) ✓. Boxes between A (5) and F (1): positions 2, 3, 4 → **3 boxes → (c)**.

**Trap (Perfect Square Distractor):** Many students forget that **1 is a perfect square** and only test 4 — or conversely waste time listing squares beyond 7. Fix the candidate positions first, then test each case quickly.

**Skip Advice:** Attempt with a 45-second rule: if G's position is still undecided after two quick cases, move on.

**Q9. Circular Number-Pattern Puzzle (PYQ-style)**

40 children stand in a circle, numbered 1 to 40. Child-1 holds a ring and passes it clockwise: first 1 place ahead, then 2 places ahead, then 3, and so on (each pass is one place longer than the previous). After how many passes does the ring first return to child-1?

(a) 12 (b) 15 (c) 20 (d) 40

**Solution:** After n passes, the ring has moved a total of 1 + 2 + … + n = n(n+1)/2 places. It returns to child-1 when this total is a multiple of 40. Testing n: n = 15 gives 15 × 16 / 2 = 120 = 3 × 40 ✓ (and no smaller n works). Answer: **15 → (b)**.

**Trap (Pattern Disguise):** This looks like a simple circle game but is really a triangular-number problem. Students start simulating pass by pass, lose count midway, and must restart — a classic time-sink.

**Skip Advice:** SKIP on the first pass (15-second rule). If you spot the triangular-number shortcut n(n+1)/2 ≡ 0 (mod 40), solve it; otherwise return only if time remains.

**Q10. Mixed-Facing Circular Puzzle (Bank PO pattern)**

Eight persons sit around a circular table; some face the centre, some face outside. Clues combine relative positions ("second to the left of…"), facing constraints ("immediate neighbours of E face outside"), and an adjacency rule ("no two persons facing the same direction sit together"). A typical set asks 3–5 linked questions on the final arrangement.

**Approach:** (1) Anchor the person whose facing is given (e.g., E faces centre). (2) Derive neighbours' facing from the adjacency rule before placing anyone else. (3) Fix "opposite" pairs next — they are the strongest positional clues. (4) Only then place "second to the left/right" clues, remembering that left/right reverses for outside-facing persons. Expect 2–3 cases; usually only one survives all constraints.

**Trap (Combination Layering):** The puzzle stacks three difficulty elements — mixed facing, circular positions, and a facing-adjacency rule. Students who handle one or two elements still fail on the third, and options are often drawn from incomplete cases, so a half-solved diagram actively misleads you.

**Skip Advice:** Apply the 45-second rule strictly. If you have not fixed at least 4 positions (with facing) in 45 seconds, skip the entire linked set and return at the end. This is the highest time-risk puzzle type in banking exams.

6.5 Time Rule Review: Which Questions to Skip?

| Skip Rule | When to Apply | Example |
|---|---|---|
| 15-Second Rule | Puzzle type unclear or hidden pattern not recognised | Q9 — numerical pattern disguised as a verbal puzzle |
| 45-Second Rule | More than 2 cases still open; no anchor fixed | Q10 — mixed-facing circular with adjacency rule |
| Skip If | 3+ interlocking variables; "uncertain number of persons" with no quick equation | Uncertain-row Bank PO sets where the diagram won't stabilise |
| Never Skip | Simple ranking, basic linear seating, floor puzzles with at most 2 cases | Q4, Q7 (30–40 sec each), Q3, Q1 |

**Golden Strategy:** In banking exams, where puzzles dominate the reasoning section, the attempt-vs-skip decision itself determines whether you clear the cut-off. Solve all easy sets first (ranking, single-row, two-case floor puzzles), then return to moderate ones. Attempt complex mixed-facing circular sets (Q10 type) last — only if time permits. In SSC/RRB, where puzzles are few and easy, never leave them unattempted.
