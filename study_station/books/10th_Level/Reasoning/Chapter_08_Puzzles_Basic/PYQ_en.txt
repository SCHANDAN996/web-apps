6.4 Top 10 High-Yield Questions (With Solutions & Trap Analysis)

Q1. SSC CGL 2024 Tier-1 (Memory-Based): Circular Seating with "Facing Centre" Trap

Eight people A to H sit around a circular table. A is second to the left of B. C is third to the right of D. E is opposite F. Only two persons sit between G and H. All face the centre. Who is sitting immediately right of A?

Solution: Step 1: "Opposite" with 8 people means 4 positions apart → place E and F. Step 2: Place A second to left of B. Step 3: Fix G and H with exactly two between. Step 4: Adjust C and D. Final position: A's immediate right = H.

Psychological Hook (Trap #1 - Missing "Immediate" Assumption): Students habitually read "second to the left" as a simple relative clue. When the question asks "immediately right of A," the examiner counts on you confusing "second to the left" with "immediately left." The options often include the person who is second to the left, tempting you to pick the wrong neighbor.

Skip Advice: Do NOT skip. This is a standard circular puzzle solvable in under 60 seconds using the 2-minute drill.

Q2. SBI PO 2024 Prelims: Linear Uncertain Persons Trap

Certain number of persons sit in a row facing north. A sits 4th from left end. B sits 3rd from right end. Three persons sit between A and B. Only two persons sit to the left of C. How many persons are there in the row?

Solution: Let total = n. A is at position 4 (from left). B's position from left = n-2 (since 3rd from right). Distance between A and B = (n-2) - 4 - 1 = 3 → n-7 = 3 → n = 10. Now, C: Only two persons left of C → C is at position 3. Final answer: 10.

Psychological Hook (Trap #3 - Extra Relationship): The question inserts C as an extra variable after you've already solved for A and B. Students often restart the entire puzzle instead of using the already-determined row length to place C. This wastes 60-90 seconds on redundant recalculation.

Skip Advice: Attempt but set a hard 90-second limit. If C's placement triggers confusion, mark the count answer and move on.

Q3. RRB NTPC: Floor-Based Puzzle

*A, B, C, D, E live on 5 floors (1-5, ground = 1). D lives immediately above E. C lives immediately below B. E and C live on even-numbered floors. A doesn't live immediately below E. Who lives on the top floor?*-
22

Solution: E and C on even → possible floors: E=2, C=4 (or vice versa). D immediately above E → if E=2, D=3; if E=4, D=5. C immediately below B → if C=2, B=3 (but 3 already D if E=2) → check. If C=4, B=5. Case 1: E=2, D=3, C=4, B=5. Then A=1. A is NOT immediately below E (E=2, A=1 → A is below E but not immediately? Actually floor 1 is immediately below 2 → violates condition). So Case 1 fails. Case 2: E=4, D=5, C=2, B=3. A=1. A not immediately below E (A=1, E=4, gap of 2 floors → satisfied). Top floor = D.

Psychological Hook (Trap #2 - Vague "Below" Interpretation): The word "below" alone does not mean "immediately below." Students who assume "below = immediately below" for the A-E condition will wrongly eliminate the correct case.

Skip Advice: Do NOT skip. This is a classic 2-case puzzle with clear resolution; ideal for scoring.

Q4. IBPS Clerk 2022: Comparison / Ranking Trap

Five friends compare heights. C is taller than only one person. E is neither tallest nor shortest. Two persons are taller than A. B is not the tallest. Who is the tallest?-
22

Solution: "C taller than only one" → C is 4th (4 persons taller than 1). "Two persons taller than A" → A is 3rd. "B is not tallest." Order from tallest → shortest: D > ? > ? > C > ? Let's fill: Tallest cannot be B, C, A, E? E not tallest, not shortest → E can be 2nd or 3rd. If A is 3rd → order: D > E > A > C > B (or D > B > A > C > E, but E not shortest → invalid). Check: D > B > A > C > E? E shortest → invalid. So D > E > A > C > B. Tallest = D.

Psychological Hook (Trap #3): The clue "B is not the tallest" is deliberately placed last to make you first construct a full order and then retro-fit B. This is a classic "extra relationship" trap — a negative clue at the end that breaks your initial order, testing flexibility.

Skip Advice: Do NOT skip. Solve in 40 seconds. Easy 2 marks.

Q5. SBI Clerk 2024: Day-Based Scheduling

Seven persons take leave from Monday to Sunday. L takes leave on Friday. Only two persons take leave between L and Q. P takes leave one day before M. N takes leave after K but before P. K does not take leave on Monday. Who takes leave on Wednesday?

Solution: L = Friday. "Only two between L and Q" → Q = Monday or Thursday? Wait: Two between Friday and X means X = Tuesday (2 gaps: Sat, Sun?) Let's count carefully: Between Friday and Tuesday = Sat, Sun, Mon → 3 days. Actually: If L=Friday, two persons between L and Q → possible Q days: Monday (Sat, Sun between) or Tuesday? Let's trace: Days after Friday (Sat, Sun) or before (Thurs, Wed). Two persons between: Q = Tuesday (Wed, Thurs between) or Q = Monday (Sat, Sun between... wait, we need persons, not days). With 7 persons, "two persons between" means gap of 2 positions. L at 5. Q can be at 2 (gap 6,7?) No: positions: 1 2 3 4 5(L) 6 7. Two persons between means positions differ by 3. So Q = 2 (persons at 3,4 between) or Q = ? 5-3=2, 5+3=8 (invalid). So Q=2. Next: P before M by 1 → P, M consecutive with P first. N after K, before P → K < N < P. K not Monday (1). So K can be 3, N=4, P=5? But L=5. Conflict. So try K=3, N=4, P=?, but P before M and consecutive... Let's assign remaining slots. Q=2, L=5. Remaining: 1,3,4,6,7. K,N,P,M fit in. K not 1 → K=3, N=4, P=6, M=7. Then Wednesday (position 3) = K.

Psychological Hook (Trap #1 - "Between" Misinterpretation): The phrase "only two persons take leave between L and Q" is often misinterpreted as "2 days gap." In scheduling puzzles, "between" counts the persons sitting in between, not calendar days. The examiner exploits this subtle distinction.

Skip Advice: Attempt but monitor time. If the "between" clue causes confusion beyond 60 seconds, mark for review and return.

Q6. SSC CGL 2019: Two-Row Arrangement (Facing Opposite Direction)

*Six persons A,B,C sit in Row-1 facing South. D,E,F sit in Row-2 facing North. B sits opposite the person who sits immediate right of E. A sits at an extreme end. D does not sit opposite A. Who sits opposite C?*

Solution: Row-1 (South-facing): positions 1 2 3. Row-2 (North-facing): positions 1 2 3 directly opposite. Left-right: South-facing left = East. Opposite means row-1 person directly faces row-2 person. "B opposite immediate right of E" → E's immediate right depends on E's position. Place E at 2 (row-2); immediate right = position 3. So B is at position 3 (row-1) opposite row-2 pos 3. A at extreme → pos 1. D not opposite A → D cannot be at row-2 pos 1, so D at pos 2 or 3. Remaining: C at pos 2. Opposite C = remaining person in row-2. Final: Row-2: D at 2, E at 1? Let's recalc. Actually, if B opposite immediate right of E (E at 2, immediate right = 3), then B at 3. A at extreme = 1. C at 2. Row-2: opposite A (1) cannot be D, so D at 2 or 3. Opposite B (3) is row-2 pos 3. So D at 2, E at 1, F at 3. Opposite C (row-1 pos 2) = D.

Psychological Hook (Trap #2 - Direction Reversal): Row-1 faces South, Row-2 faces North. A South-facing person's "left" is East; a North-facing person's "left" is West. The examiner knows students mechanically apply the same left-right logic to both rows, causing mirror-image errors.

Skip Advice: Do NOT skip if you've practiced parallel rows. Skip only if "opposite direction facing" logic creates case explosion beyond 2 cases.

Q7. RRB NTPC: Weight Comparison Puzzle

P, Q, R, S, T are comparing their weights. Q is lighter than only S. R is not the lightest. P is heavier than R but lighter than Q. The weight of the heaviest person is 84 kg. The weight of the second lightest person is 46 kg. What is the possible weight of T?-
22

Solution: "Q lighter than only S" → S is heaviest, Q is 2nd heaviest. Order: S (84) > Q > ? > ? > ?. "P heavier than R but lighter than Q" → Q > P > R. "R not lightest" → T is lightest. Order: S > Q > P > R > T. Second lightest = R = 46 kg. T is lightest → T < 46. Options: (a) 48 (b) 57 (c) 42 (d) 82. Answer = 42.

Psychological Hook (Quantitative Overlay Trap): The examiner adds numerical values (84 kg, 46 kg) to what is fundamentally a ranking puzzle. Students shift mental gears from "comparison logic" to "arithmetic mode," overcomplicating the solution. Many attempt to calculate Q's exact weight range instead of simply identifying T as the lightest.

Skip Advice: Do NOT skip. This is a 30-second puzzle disguised as a quantitative problem.

Q8. SSC CGL Tier-2: Box Stacking Puzzle

Seven boxes A to G are kept one above another. Bottommost is numbered 1, topmost 7. G is kept on a perfect square numbered floor. C is kept three boxes above G. E is kept two boxes below A. A is kept just below D. Neither B nor F is kept adjacent to G. B is kept above F. How many boxes are kept between A and F?-
59

Solution: Perfect squares 1-7: 1, 4. So G is at 1 or 4. Case 1: G=4 → C=7 (three above G). A just below D → D and A consecutive. E two below A. Place D=6, A=5, E=3. Remaining 1 and 2: B above F → B=2, F=1. Check adjacency to G(4): B(2) not adjacent, F(1) not adjacent ✓. Boxes between A(5) and F(1) → 3 boxes (2,3,4).

Psychological Hook (Perfect Square Distractor): The "perfect square" clue triggers mathematical thinking. Students mentally list all squares (1,4,9...) and hesitate. The real trap is that "1 is a perfect square" — many forget 1 and search only for 4, creating an unsolvable puzzle.

Skip Advice: Attempt but if you forget that 1 is a perfect square, you'll waste time. Set 45-second rule: if G's position remains uncertain, move on.

Q9. SSC CGL / MTS 2023: Circular Sequence Puzzle

*40 children stand in a circle. Child-1 has a ring, passes it clockwise to child-2, who passes to child-4, who passes to child-7, and so on (adding 1, 2, 3, 4... each pass). After how many changes (including child-1) does the ring return to child-1?*-
82

Solution: The sequence: 1 → (add 1) → 2 → (add 2) → 4 → (add 3) → 7 → 11 → 16 → 22 → 29 → 37 → (add 9) → 46 mod 40 = 6 → (add 10) → 16 mod 40 = 16 → (add 11) → 27 → (add 12) → 39 → (add 13) → 52 mod 40 = 12 → (add 14) → 26 → (add 15) → 41 mod 40 = 1. Total = 15 changes.

Psychological Hook (Pattern Disguise Trap): This puzzle looks like a simple circle-passing game but is actually a triangular number pattern in disguise. The examiner exploits the "simple puzzle" label to hide a moderately complex numerical pattern. Students expecting a basic verbal reasoning puzzle get blindsided by the hidden mathematical logic.

Skip Advice: DEFINITELY SKIP on first pass (15-second rule). This is a time-sink puzzle. The pattern requires iterative calculation; misstepping once means starting over. Return only if 5+ minutes remain.

Q10. SBI PO 2023: Mixed Facing Circular Puzzle

Eight persons sit around a circular table. Some face centre, some face outside. A sits second to left of B. C sits third to right of D. E faces centre. Immediate neighbours of E face outside. F sits opposite G. H sits immediate right of A. No two persons facing the same direction sit adjacent...

Solution: This is a complex circular puzzle requiring: (1) placement of anchor persons (E faces centre), (2) determining neighbors' facing based on E (both outside), (3) alternating facing to satisfy "no two same direction adjacent," (4) placing remaining persons with relative clues. Final arrangement requires 3-4 cases; only one survives.

Psychological Hook (Combination Trap): This puzzle combines "mixed facing" with "circular arrangement" and adds an adjacency constraint on facing direction. The examiner layers three separate difficulty elements, knowing that students who handle 1-2 elements still fail on the third. Options often include answers from incomplete cases.

Skip Advice: Apply 45-second rule strictly. If after 45 seconds you have not fixed 4+ positions, skip the entire set (3-5 linked questions). This is the highest time-risk puzzle type in banking exams.

6.5 Time Rule Review: Which Questions to Skip?
Skip Rule	When to Apply	Example Question
15-Second Rule	Puzzle type unclear or pattern unrecognizable	Q9 (Ring puzzle) — numerical sequence disguised as simple verbal
45-Second Rule	> 2 cases still open, no definite anchor placed	Q10 (SBI PO Mixed Facing) — complex multi-constraint circular
Skip If	3+ variables with interlocking constraints; "uncertain number of persons"	Bank PO uncertain-row puzzles; Q1-type if diagram not stabilizing
Never Skip	Simple ranking (Q4, Q7), basic linear (Q2), floor puzzles with max 2 cases (Q3)	Q4, Q7 — 30-40 seconds each, guaranteed marks

Golden Strategy: In Banking exams where puzzles dominate 40-50% of reasoning, the decision to attempt vs. skip determines your cut-off. Solve all "Easy" marked puzzles first (approx. 60% of puzzle sets), then return to moderate ones. Complex multi-variable circular puzzles (Q10 type) should be attempted last — only if time permits.
