101. Seven persons A, B, C, D, E, F, G sit in a row facing north. F sits at the extreme left end. G does not sit at the extreme right end. A sits fourth to the left of G. B sits second to the right of C. D sits immediate left of E. Who sits immediate right of A?
(a) D (b) B (c) C (d) E
Answer: (a)
Solution: Positions 1–7 (left to right). F = 1. G not 7, so G can be 2–6. A = G – 4, so G must be 5 or 6. If G = 6, A = 2. B = C + 2. D = E – 1. Remaining positions 3, 4, 5, 7. B second right of C: possible C = 3, B = 5; C = 5, B = 7. Try C = 3, B = 5. Then D, E on remaining 4, 7: D = 4, E = 5 (conflict B). Try C = 5, B = 7: then D, E on 3, 4: D = 3, E = 4. Works. Arrangement: 1:F, 2:A, 3:D, 4:E, 5:C, 6:G, 7:B. Immediate right of A (2) is D (3). If G = 5, A = 1 (conflict F). So unique.
Source: PYQ-style

102. Eight persons A, B, C, D, E, F, G, H sit around a circular table facing the centre. A sits second to the left of B. C sits opposite D. E sits immediate right of F. G sits third to the right of H. B is not an immediate neighbour of C. Who sits opposite H?
(a) A (b) B (c) C (d) D
Answer: (b)
Solution: Arrangement (clockwise 1 to 8): 1-B, 2-D, 3-F, 4-E, 5-H, 6-C, 7-A, 8-G. Directly opposite H (position 5) sits B (position 1).
Source: PYQ-style

103. Seven persons M, N, O, P, Q, R, S attend lectures Monday to Sunday. M attends on Wednesday. N attends immediately before O. P attends on Saturday. Q attends after R but before S. Only one person attends between M and R. Who attends on Friday?
(a) N (b) Q (c) O (d) S
Answer: (c)
Solution: Mon=1, Tue=2, Wed=3, Thu=4, Fri=5, Sat=6, Sun=7. M=3, P=6. One person between M and R ⇒ |3 – R| = 2 ⇒ R = 1 or 5. N = O – 1. Q after R before S ⇒ R < Q < S. If R = 1, then Q and S from remaining 2,4,5,7 with Q < S. Try N=4, O=5. Then Q=2, S=7 works. Friday (5) = O. If R=5, then Q after 5 impossible because only days left are 7 and maybe 2,4 but Q<S and Q>5 not possible. So unique. Friday = O.
Source: PYQ-style

104. Seven persons A, B, C, D, E, F, G live on floors 1 to 7 (1 = bottom, 7 = top). A lives on an even floor. B lives three floors above C. D lives immediately above E. F lives on the top floor. G lives below A but above B. Who lives on floor 5?
(a) A (b) B (c) F (d) G
Answer: (d)
Solution: A even: 2,4,6. F=7. B = C + 3. D = E + 1. G between B and A (B < G < A). Try A=6. G between B and 6; B≥4 (C≥1). If B=4, C=1, G=5. D=E+1 on remaining 2,3: D=2, E=3. Arrangement: 1:C, 2:D, 3:E, 4:B, 5:G, 6:A, 7:F. Floor 5 = G. A=4 fails (B<G<4 impossible as B≥4). Unique.
Source: PYQ-style

105. Five friends P, Q, R, S, T have different heights. P is taller than Q but shorter than R. S is taller than T. T is not the shortest. R is shorter than S. Who is the shortest?
(a) Q (b) P (c) R (d) T
Answer: (a)
Solution: R > P > Q. S > T, and S > R (R shorter than S). So S > R > P > Q. T not shortest, and T < S. Since Q is already less than P and R, Q must be less than T as well (if T < Q, T would be shortest, contradicting T not shortest). Hence T > Q. Order: S > R > P > T > Q. Shortest = Q.
Source: PYQ-style

106. A boy walks 30 m east, turns left and walks 40 m north, turns left and walks 30 m west. How far is he from his starting point?
(a) 30 m (b) 40 m (c) 50 m (d) 70 m
Answer: (b)
Solution: Path: East 30, left (North) 40, left (West) 30. Net displacement: East 30 – West 30 = 0; North 40. Distance = 40 m.
Source: PYQ-style

107. Six persons sit in two parallel rows: Row1 – A, B, C faces South; Row2 – X, Y, Z faces North. C sits at the extreme left end of Row1. A sits opposite the person who sits immediate right of Y. X sits opposite B. Who sits immediate left of Z?
(a) X (b) A (c) Y (d) B
Answer: (c)
Solution: Row1 positions 1,2,3 (left to right). Row2 positions 1,2,3 directly opposite. C extreme left ⇒ C=1. X opposite B ⇒ B and X are opposite. A opposite immediate right of Y. For north-facing, immediate right is higher index. Try Y=1, immediate right=2. Then A opposite 2, so A=2 Row1. Then B=3 (remaining). X opposite B=3 ⇒ X=3 Row2. Thus Row2: 1:Y, 2:Z (remaining), 3:X. Immediate left of Z (2) is Y. Works.
Source: PYQ-style

108. A certain number of persons sit in a row facing north. A sits 5th from the left end. B sits 4th from the right end. Only one person sits between A and B. What is B's position from the left end?
(a) 3 (b) 7 (c) 10 (d) Cannot be determined
Answer: (d)
Solution: A = 5. B from right = 4 ⇒ B from left = n – 3. One person between ⇒ |5 – (n – 3)| = 2 ⇒ |8 – n| = 2 ⇒ n = 6 or 10. n=6: A=5, B left=3. n=10: A=5, B left=7. Both valid. Hence B's position cannot be uniquely determined. (Examiner’s trap: two possible row lengths.)
Source: PYQ-style

109. Six persons P, Q, R, S, T, U sit in a row facing north. P sits second to the left of Q. U sits exactly between P and Q. R sits immediate left of S. T sits at an extreme end. Who sits immediate right of T?
(a) Cannot be determined (b) P (c) R (d) U
Answer: (a)
Solution: P = Q – 2. U exactly between ⇒ U = P + 1 = Q – 1. So P, U, Q are consecutive. T = 1 or 6. Block of three can be (2,3,4), (3,4,5), (4,5,6). Case 1: T=1, block (2,3,4) ⇒ P=2, U=3, Q=4. R immediate left of S on remaining 5,6: R=5, S=6. Immediate right of T(1) is 2 = P. Case 2: T=1, block (4,5,6) ⇒ P=4, U=5, Q=6. R,S on 2,3: R=2, S=3. Immediate right of T(1) = 2 = R. Two different answers. Hence cannot be determined. (Examiner’s trap: multiple arrangements.)
Source: PYQ-style

110. Six persons A, B, C, D, E, F have different salaries. A earns more than B. C earns less than D but more than E. E earns more than F. B earns less than E. Who earns the highest?
(a) A (b) Cannot be determined (c) D (d) E
Answer: (b)
Solution: D > C > E > F, and E > B, A > B. No relation between A and D. A could be above or below D. So highest cannot be determined. (Examiner’s trap: incomplete comparison.)
Source: PYQ-style

111. Six persons A, B, C, D, E, F sit around a circular table facing the centre. A sits opposite D. B sits immediate right of A. C sits second to the left of B. Who sits immediate left of D?
(a) E (b) F (c) Cannot be determined (d) C
Answer: (c)
Solution: A opposite D ⇒ A and D are 3 apart. Place A=1, D=4. B immediate right (clockwise) of A = 2. C second left of B: anticlockwise 2 from 2 → 2→1→6, so C=6. Remaining E, F at 3 and 5. Immediate left of D (4) anticlockwise is 3, which may be E or F. Not uniquely determined.
Source: PYQ-style

112. Seven boxes A, B, C, D, E, F, G are stacked (1=bottom, 7=top). A is on an even floor. B is three boxes above C. D is immediately above E. F is on the top floor. G is below A but above B. Which box is on floor 2?
(a) B (b) C (c) E (d) D
Answer: (d)
Solution: A even (2,4,6). F=7. B = C+3. D = E+1. G between B and A (B < G < A). Try A=6. G between B and 6; B≥4 (C≥1). If B=4, C=1, G=5. D=E+1 on remaining 2,3: D=2, E=3. Floor 2 = D. Unique.
Source: PYQ-style

113. Seven persons A, B, C, D, E, F, G attend a meeting Monday to Sunday. A attends on Thursday. B attends immediately before C. D attends on Monday. E attends after F but before G. G attends on Sunday. Only one person attends between A and F. Who attends on Tuesday?
(a) F (b) E (c) B (d) C
Answer: (a)
Solution: Mon=1, Tue=2, Wed=3, Thu=4, Fri=5, Sat=6, Sun=7. D=1, A=4, G=7. One person between A and F ⇒ |4 – F|=2 ⇒ F=2 or 6. E after F before G ⇒ F < E < 7. If F=2, E can be 3,4,5,6. A=4 so E=3 or 5,6. B=C-1 on remaining 5,6 if E=3. Then B=5, C=6 works. So Tuesday=2=F. If F=6, E>6 impossible. So unique.
Source: PYQ-style

114. Six persons sit in two parallel rows: Row1 A,B,C faces South; Row2 P,Q,R faces North. A sits at the extreme right end of Row1. B sits opposite the person who sits immediate left of R. P sits opposite C. Who sits opposite A?
(a) P (b) R (c) Q (d) Cannot be determined
Answer: (b)
Solution: Row1 positions 1,2,3; Row2 positions 1,2,3. A extreme right ⇒ A=3. C opposite P. B opposite immediate left of R. For north-facing, immediate left of R is R–1. If B=1, then B opposite 1 ⇒ immediate left of R = 1 ⇒ R=2. Then C=2 opposite P ⇒ P=2 conflict R. So B=2, then B opposite 2 ⇒ immediate left of R = 2 ⇒ R=3. Then C=1 opposite P ⇒ P=1. Row2: 1:P, 2:Q (remaining), 3:R. A=3 opposite R. Answer R.
Source: PYQ-style

115. A man walks 20 m north, turns right and walks 10 m east, turns right and walks 20 m south, turns left and walks 20 m east. How far is he from his starting point?
(a) 10 m (b) 20 m (c) 30 m (d) 40 m
Answer: (c)
Solution: Path: N20, E10, S20, left from south = East 20. Net N=20-20=0, E=10+20=30. Distance = 30 m.

116. Seven persons P, Q, R, S, T, U, V live on floors 1–7. P lives on an even floor. Q lives immediately above R. S lives three floors above T. U lives on floor 5. V lives below P but above Q. Who lives on floor 3?
(a) Q (b) R (c) T (d) V
Answer: (d)
Solution: P even: 2,4,6. U=5. Q = R + 1. S = T + 3. V between Q and P (Q < V < P). Try P=6. V between Q and 6. Q can be 2,3,4. If Q=2, R=1, V between 2 and 6 ⇒ V=3,4,5. U=5 so V=3 or 4. S=T+3 on remaining 3,4,7 if V=3; then T=4, S=7 works. So V=3. Arrangement: 1:R, 2:Q, 3:V, 4:T, 5:U, 6:P, 7:S. Floor 3 = V.
Source: PYQ-style

117. Among A, B, C, D, E, each has a different weight. A is heavier than B. C is heavier than D. E is lighter than A but heavier than C. Who is the heaviest?
(a) A (b) B (c) C (d) E
Answer: (a)
Solution: A > E > C > D, and A > B. No one exceeds A. So A is the heaviest.

118. Seven persons A, B, C, D, E, F, G sit in a row facing north. C sits at the extreme left end. B sits immediate right of C. G sits at the extreme right end. A sits second to the left of D. E sits second to the right of F. Who sits immediate left of E?
(a) B (b) A (c) C (d) F
Answer: (b)
Solution: Positions 1–7. C=1, B=2, G=7. A second left of D ⇒ A = D – 2. E second right of F ⇒ E = F + 2. Remaining 3,4,5,6. Try D=6, A=4. Then E=F+2 on 3,5: F=3, E=5 works. Arrangement: 1:C,2:B,3:F,4:A,5:E,6:D,7:G. Immediate left of E(5) is 4 = A.

119. Seven persons A, B, C, D, E, F, G attend a seminar Monday to Sunday. A attends on Thursday. B attends immediately before C. D attends on Tuesday. E attends after F but before G. G attends on Sunday. Only two persons attend between A and F. Who attends on Friday?
(a) A (b) C (c) B (d) E
Answer: (c)
Solution: Mon=1, Tue=2, Wed=3, Thu=4, Fri=5, Sat=6, Sun=7. D=2, A=4, G=7. Two persons between A and F ⇒ |4 – F|=3 ⇒ F=1 or 7. G=7 so F=1. E after F before G ⇒ 1<E<7. B=C–1. Remaining 3,5,6. E can be 3,5,6. B=C–1: possible B=5, C=6. Then E=3 works. Friday(5) = B.
Source: PYQ-style

120. Seven persons A, B, C, D, E, F, G live on floors 1–7. A lives on a prime numbered floor. B lives immediately above C. D lives three floors below E. F lives on floor 6. G lives below A but above E. Who lives on floor 4?
(a) A (b) B (c) C (d) E

Source: PYQ-style

123. Six persons A, B, C, D, E, and F are sitting in a row facing north. A sits second to the left of B. C sits exactly between A and B. D sits to the immediate right of E. F sits at one of the extreme ends. E does not sit adjacent to A. Who sits to the immediate left of A?
(a) C (b) D (c) Cannot be determined (d) F
Answer: (c)
Solution: Two arrangements are possible: (F, A, C, B, E, D) and (E, D, A, C, B, F), leading to F and D respectively to the immediate left of A. Hence, it cannot be determined.
