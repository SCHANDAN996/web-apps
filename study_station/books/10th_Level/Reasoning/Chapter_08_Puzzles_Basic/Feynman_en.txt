🧒 Feynman Teaching (फेनमैन तकनीक)

The "Left-Right" Monster in Puzzles

Imagine you are at a birthday party. The cake is in the middle of a round table. Six kids — Avi, Bela, Cheenu, Dolly, Ehsaan, and Farah — sit around it. Some face the cake (centre), some face the wall (outside). Now I ask:

“Avi is sitting second to the left of Bela.”

You look and say, “Oh, easy! Left means the left hand side.” But wait.

If Avi faces the cake, his left hand points along the circle in one direction (anticlockwise).

If Avi faces the wall, his left hand points the opposite way (clockwise)!

So the same person, Avi, can be sitting physically on the right side of Bela if we look from the ceiling — and yet it’s called “left” because Avi’s body defines it. This single confusion makes students lose 4–5 marks in puzzles. Let's kill it forever.

A Story That Sticks Forever: The Twin Brothers and the Magic Mirror

Raju and Shyamu are twin brothers. They sit on a round rotating chair in their garden. One day, Raju says, “Brother, I’ll sit facing the mango tree (centre). You sit facing the street (outside).” Both wear a red ribbon on their left wrist.

Their friend Chinki comes and asks, “Who is sitting to the left of Raju?”

If you stand on the roof and look down, the answer depends on whose left we are using. But puzzles don’t care about the bird’s view — they care about the left hand of the person we are referring to.

Now the trick: When a person faces CENTRE, their LEFT = your ANTICLOCKWISE.
When a person faces OUTSIDE, their LEFT = your CLOCKWISE.

But you can remember it even faster: “Centre mein left-anticlockwise, outside mein left-clockwise” — C-LAC, O-LC. (Pronounce: “K-Lak, O-El-C”)

Say it once: “Centre -> Left Anticlockwise, Outside -> Left Clockwise.”

The “Immediate” Demon — Another Story

In a school assembly, six students stand in a straight line facing the principal. The sports teacher gives a command: “Rohan, stand to the left of Sohan.” Rohan goes and stands somewhere — maybe three places away. But the principal wanted Rohan to stand just beside Sohan. He says, “No! I meant immediately left!”

In puzzles, the word “immediately” (तुरंत, ठीक) changes everything. If the puzzle says “A is left of B,” it’s anywhere on the left. If it says “immediately left,” it means shoulder-to-shoulder, no gap. Always hunt for the word “immediately” or “just/right/ठीक”. If missing, never assume a gap is zero.

Unforgettable Trick: When you read a puzzle clue, imagine a ghost sitting in every possible gap. Unless the clue kicks the ghost out by saying “immediately,” the ghost can live there.

Why This Is the Hardest, Made Easy

The hardest part of simple puzzles is not logic — it is knowing whose “left” matters and whether gaps are allowed. Once you anchor these two ideas with the stories above, the rest is just drawing neat little boxes.

Now, take a deep breath. You have a superpower:

For circular: “Centre L-Anti, Outside L-Clock” (C-LAC, O-LC).

For row: “No ‘immediately’ = space allowed.”

That’s it. The monster is dead.
