Front: What is the single most important question to ask yourself to check if a course of action is truly practical?
Back: "Can I actually do this now, exactly as written, without any magic wand?"

Front: What does the 'C' in the CLEAR filter stand for?
Back: Concrete step – a clear, specific action, not a vague idea.

Front: Why is “People should be more careful” almost never a valid course of action?
Back: It is a wish, not a concrete administrative step that directly solves the problem.

Front: What does the 'L' in CLEAR check?
Back: Linked – the action must be directly connected to the core problem in the statement.

Front: What danger does the 'E' in CLEAR warn you about?
Back: Extreme actions — e.g. "ban everything", "dismiss all employees" without investigation.

Front: What does the 'A' in CLEAR remind you to avoid?
Back: Assumption – never add information that is not given in the statement.

Front: How does the 'R' in CLEAR sharpen your selection?
Back: It checks that the action is Relevant and practically feasible for a real authority to carry out.

Front: What is the VIP D mnemonic used for?
Back: Rapid rejection of wrong options – Vague, Irrelevant, Punishment first, Demands impossible.

Front: What trap does the 'P' in VIP D protect you from?
Back: Punishment as the very first step — without any inquiry or warning, it is usually invalid.

Front: Picture a small fire right in front of you. How do you apply the "Fire Extinguisher Test"?
Back: Ask: "Would this suggestion put out this fire immediately?" If not, it's not the right immediate action.

Front: Statement: "Theft in the office has increased." Which is the real course of action and why: "Install CCTV cameras" or "All employees should take an honesty pledge"?
Back: Installing CCTV cameras – it is concrete, directly linked, and practical. A pledge is just a wish.

Front: What is a classic examiner's trap where two courses of action both seem acceptable?
Back: One action solves the immediate problem, the other is a long‑term policy. The trap works when you forget that the statement demands a now action.

Front: An option says "Administrative restructuring should be undertaken." How do you avoid this heavy‑word trap?
Back: Ask: "What will this step actually do?" If it remains vague and impractical, it's a trap despite its official sound.

Front: What is the "dual trap" in Statement & Course of Action?
Back: Two actions each address a different part of the problem. You must pick the one that hits the root cause, not a symptom.

Front: Can a "public awareness campaign" ever be a correct course of action?
Back: Yes – if the problem stems from ignorance and awareness is the direct, practical solution. But not when immediate physical action is needed.

Front: What is the quick mnemonic phrase to remember the CLEAR filter?
Back: "CLEAR it or leave it." If an option does not pass CLEAR, leave it immediately.

Front: Why does "Order a formal inquiry" often qualify as a valid course of action?
Back: It is a concrete administrative step when the cause of the problem is unknown and must be investigated before further action.

Front: What is the single, ultimate test for any course of action described in this chapter?
Back: The action must be solution‑oriented, practical, directly linked, extreme‑free, and based only on the facts given in the statement.
