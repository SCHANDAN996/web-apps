# Course of Action

Dengue suddenly spreads in a city. What should be done? "Run a cleanliness drive" — this is a correct course of action. But "evacuate the whole city" — this is too extreme. Course-of-action questions test choosing this 'right step'.

**Definition:** In these questions, a problem or situation (statement) is given, then some suggested courses of action. We must decide which action is logical and practical for solving the problem.

🧠 Recall: What matters most when choosing a course of action — solving the problem, or punishment?

## The Golden Rule: Three Checks

A course of action is correct only if it is:
1. **Directly related** to the problem (it solves or reduces it).
2. **Practical** — it can actually be carried out.
3. **Not extreme** — not too harsh, impossible, or unreasonable.

## Key Ideas

**Solution-oriented vs Extreme**
- "Disease has spread → run a vaccination drive" (correct, solution-oriented).
- "Disease has spread → jail everyone" (wrong, extreme).

**Prevention vs Punishment**
A good course of action often prevents the problem, not just punishes.

🧠 Recall: Should a "too harsh or impossible" action be considered correct?

## The Examiner's Trap

⚠️ **Trap 1 — extreme steps:** Overly harsh actions like "shut everything down", "remove everyone" are usually not correct.

⚠️ **Trap 2 — unrelated action:** An action not directly related to the problem is not correct.

⚠️ **Trap 3 — assuming both are correct:** Sometimes both actions are correct, sometimes neither — don't rush.
