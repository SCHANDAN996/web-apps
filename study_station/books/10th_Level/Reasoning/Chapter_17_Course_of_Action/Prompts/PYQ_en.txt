🧠 Sub-Topic Weightage

The core logical principle is always tested, but the scenarios vary in complexity and subject matter.

Data-Driven / Policy	15%	Statistical report (crop yield, revenue loss) demanding policy intervention.

🏛️ Exam-Wise Distribution

The question format adapts to the exam’s need for speed vs. depth.

SSC (CGL, CHSL): Typically 1–2 questions, easy to moderate. Often tests a simple, direct Problem-and-Solution scenario. Answers rarely require complex justification.

Banking (IBPS PO, SBI PO): 1–2 questions, moderate difficulty. Frequently uses Data-Driven or Policy scenarios. Options often include a mix of a strong administrative step and an extreme/impractical one.

Railway (RRB NTPC, Group D): 0–1 question, easy. Usually a straightforward, fact-based statement with a single, obvious action.

UPSC (CSAT): Rare (0–1 per year). When it appears, the question is highly deceptive. The trap lies in making an extreme or assumption-based option appear deceptively "strong" and "decisive."

🔥 Top 10 High-Yield PYQs with Solutions & Psychological Traps

1. (SSC CHSL 2022)
Statement: The traffic congestion in the city has significantly increased during peak hours.
Courses: I. Improve public transportation infrastructure. II. Impose hefty fines on individual drivers during peak hours.
Answer: Only I follows.
Psychological Trap: The allure of "tough action." Option II feels strong and punitive, but punishing a legal activity is illogical—an overreach trap. I is a direct, feasible solution.
Skip Rule: Do not skip; apply the "legal action" test within 15 sec.

2. (SSC CGL Tier II, 2023)
Statement: A large increase in villagers migrating to urban areas due to repeated crop failure and financial problems.
Courses: I. Provide alternate income sources in villages. II. Give migrants access to all urban housing.
Answer: Only I follows.
Psychological Trap: The "humane diversion." II is compassionate but addresses a symptom, not the root cause. I is the sustainable administrative solution.
Skip Rule: Do not skip; identify the root cause in 20 sec.

3. (SBI PO 2010)
Statement: A large private bank retrenched one-third employees due to huge losses.
Courses: (A) Govt asks public to stop transactions. (B) Govt directs bank to refrain. (C) Govt initiates an enquiry.
Answer: Only (C) follows.
Psychological Trap: The "urgent crisis" illusion. (A) and (B) are extreme, hasty decisions. (C) is the only prudent, administrative step before acting—a classic procedural trap.
Skip Rule: 45‑sec rule; if not seeing the “enquiry first” logic, mark for review and move on.

4. (UPSC CSAT 2018)
Statement: A major railway accident was averted due to a signalman's presence of mind.
Courses: I. Signalman rewarded. II. Track cleared of all traffic for 50 km.
Answer: Only I follows.
Psychological Trap: The "overcorrection" trap. II is an extreme, impractical measure. I is a direct, logical consequence of the given praise.

5. (RRB NTPC)
Statement: A high number of accidents occur at a railway crossing due to lack of proper signaling.
Courses: I. Install automated signaling immediately. II. Divert all trains.
Answer: Only I follows.
Psychological Trap: The "perfect safety" fallacy. II feels absolutely safe but is utterly impractical. I is a direct, feasible technical fix.

6. (Bank PO)
Statement: Many people have encroached on government land and built houses.
Courses: I. Govt should immediately remove all unauthorized constructions. II. Govt should regularize structures built before a cut-off date.
Answer: Neither follows; a phased, legal process is needed.
Psychological Trap: The "binary simplification" trap. Both options are extreme endpoints—wholesale demolition or blanket regularization. The logical action is a middle-path, procedural step, which is absent here.
Skip Rule: 45‑sec rule; if forced, flag as an "extreme binary" trap.

7. (SSC CGL)
Statement: The condition of all major roads has deteriorated due to incessant rain.
Courses: I. Immediate repairs. II. A committee to investigate road quality.
Answer: Only I follows (immediate relief).
Psychological Trap: The "process vs. action" conflict. II is procedural and slow; I is the direct, immediate action the situation demands.

8. (IBPS PO)
Statement: The prices of food grains have substantially increased due to a prolonged truck owners' strike.
Courses: I. Government makes alternative supply arrangements. II. Government cancels all vehicle licenses.
Answer: Only I follows.
Psychological Trap: The "collective punishment" trap. II punishes the entire association without investigating the cause of the strike—an extreme, unfeasible overreaction.

9. (SSC CHSL)
Statement: Many students fell ill after consuming food from the school canteen.
Courses: I. Immediately terminate the canteen contract. II. Advise students not to eat from the canteen. III. Immediately arrest the owner.
Answer: Only II follows.
Psychological Trap: The "instant justice" fallacy. I and III are extreme, premature actions before any investigation. II is a precautionary, immediate, and practical step.

10. (Bank PO)
Statement: The meteorological department has forecast less rainfall during next year’s monsoon.
Courses: I. Farmers advised to use drought-resistant seeds. II. Government should prepare a water conservation plan. III. Immediate evacuation of the area.
Answer: Both I and II follow.
Psychological Trap: The "emergency exaggeration" trap. III is a baseless, extreme reaction to a forecast. I and II are proactive, data-driven policy steps.

⏱️ Time Management & Skip Strategy

15-Second Rule (Detection):
If you cannot identify the core problem in the statement within 15 seconds (e.g., a complex, multi-clause statement or ambiguous language), mark the question for review and skip it immediately. Data-heavy questions are the primary candidate for this rule.

45-Second Rule (Resolution):
If you are still debating between two options after 45 seconds, you have fallen into a trap. The examiner has likely placed an "almost correct" but extreme, irrelevant, or assumption-based option next to the correct one. In this case, quickly re-apply the CLEAR filter. If still stuck, select the option that is least extreme, most practical, and most directly linked to the root cause, mark for review, and move on.
