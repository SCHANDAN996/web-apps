Set 6: Statement & Course of Action (126–150)
Foundation Level • Verbal Reasoning • All Hard

Instructions: In each question below is given a statement followed by two courses of action numbered I and II. A course of action is a step or administrative decision to be taken for improvement, follow‑up, or further action in regard to the problem, policy, etc. On the basis of the information given in the statement, you have to assume everything in the statement to be true and then decide which of the suggested courses of action logically follow(s) for pursuing.

Give answer:
(a) if only I follows
(b) if only II follows
(c) if both I and II follow
(d) if neither I nor II follows

126.
Statement: The majority of citizens in a city are suffering from respiratory diseases, and a study attributes 70% of the pollution to vehicular emissions.
Courses of Action:
I. The city administration should phase out all private diesel and petrol vehicles within a year and replace them with electric vehicles.
II. An immediate odd‑even vehicle rationing scheme should be introduced during peak pollution months, along with an expansion of public transport.

(a) only I follows
(b) only II follows
(c) both I and II follow
(d) neither I nor II follows

Answer: (b) only II follows
Solution: I is an extreme and impractical deadline; a year is unrealistic for complete replacement without infrastructure, and it ignores economic feasibility. II is a balanced, immediate, and tested measure to quickly reduce emissions, coupled with strengthening alternatives. Hence only II follows.
Source: UPSC CSAT 2018 (adapted)

127. (Examiner’s Trap – Both Follow, but One Seems Secondary)
Statement: A sudden and severe flash flood has devastated several villages, destroying homes and crops.
Courses of Action:
I. Immediate rescue, relief, and temporary shelter should be provided to the affected people.
II. A high‑level committee should be set up immediately to investigate the causes and recommend long‑term flood prevention measures.

(a) only I follows
(b) only II follows
(c) both I and II follow
(d) neither I nor II follows

Answer: (c) both I and II follow
Solution: I is the emergency life‑saving step. II, though a future‑looking action, is an immediate administrative decision to set up a committee; starting the investigation now is logical and standard practice. Both are independent and do not conflict. The trap is to discard II as not urgent, but initiating an enquiry is a concrete immediate step.
Source: SSC CGL 2021 (Tier II)

132. (Examiner’s Trap – Neither Follows Because Both Are Extreme/Impractical)
Statement: A few cases of a rare contagious disease have been detected in a remote village.
Courses of Action:
I. The entire village should be quarantined, and all movement banned indefinitely.
II. All villagers should be forcibly vaccinated with an experimental vaccine.

(a) only I follows
(b) only II follows
(c) both I and II follow
(d) neither I nor II follows

Answer: (d) neither I nor II follows
Solution: I uses "indefinitely", making it an extreme, unscientific measure without defined criteria. II is unethical and impractical without proven vaccine efficacy and consent. Both are disproportionate for a few cases. A logical response would involve contact tracing, limited quarantine, and medical observation, neither of which are given. Thus neither follows.
Source: UPSC CSAT 2019

134.
Statement: A fast‑spreading forest fire is approaching a heavily populated township on the forest fringe.
Courses of Action:
I. The township should be evacuated immediately, and fire lines cut to contain the blaze.
II. A helicopter‑based water bombing operation should be launched to douse the fire completely.

(a) only I follows
(b) only II follows
(c) both I and II follow
(d) neither I nor II follows

Answer: (a) only I follows
Solution: I is the essential life‑saving and containment step. II, while helpful, cannot be the sole action, and the statement does not indicate availability; "should be launched" assumes resources and feasibility that might not exist. Moreover, complete dousing is not a guaranteed immediate outcome. In logic‑based decision, I is the primary, imperative action; II is a supplementary operational decision that may not be universally applicable. Hence only I follows as a certain logical step.
Source: SSC CGL 2022

143. (Examiner’s Trap – Both Follow with a Twist of Timing)
Statement: A major oil‑producing country has unexpectedly halved its production, causing global crude prices to spike.
Courses of Action:
I. The government should immediately release strategic petroleum reserves to cushion domestic prices.
II. A long‑term policy to diversify energy sources and reduce import dependence should be accelerated.

(a) only I follows
(b) only II follows
(c) both I and II follow
(d) neither I nor II follows

Answer: (c) both I and II follow
Solution: I is a direct, immediate measure to stabilise the market. II is a necessary strategic response that can be initiated or accelerated concurrently; a long‑term policy decision is a valid administrative action when a crisis exposes vulnerability. Both are logical and not contradictory. The trap is to think II is only for the future and thus not follow, but accelerating an existing policy is an action.
Source: UPSC CSAT 2016 (adapted)

148. (Examiner’s Trap – Neither Follows Because Both Are Wishful/Vague)
Statement: There is a general feeling among the public that corruption is increasing in all spheres of life.
Courses of Action:
I. The government should take strong action to eliminate corruption completely.
II. An awareness campaign should be launched to change the public perception that corruption is rising.

(a) only I follows
(b) only II follows
(c) both I and II follow
(d) neither I nor II follows

Answer: (d) neither I nor II follows
Solution: I is a vague, undefined wish; "strong action" is not a concrete step. II attempts to alter perception without addressing the actual problem; a campaign to change perception when the statement only indicates a 'general feeling' and not a proven fact is not a logical administrative response. Both lack concreteness and direct linkage. Hence neither follows.
Source: UPSC CSAT 2017
