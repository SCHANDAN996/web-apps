Part 1: Key Rules & Approaches

Rule / Approach	Description	Example
Concrete Action Test	The course of action must be a definite, workable step rather than mere wishful thinking or vague advice.	Statement: "Road accidents are increasing." Correct: "Deploy traffic police at intersections." Incorrect: "People should drive carefully."
Direct Link	The action must target the root cause or immediate effect described in the statement.	Statement: "Villagers lack drinking water." Correct: "Dispatch water tankers immediately." Incorrect: "Construct a new sports stadium."
No Extreme Steps	Harsh, unrealistic, or unprovoked full bans, dismissals, or closures are invalid.	Statement: "Some employees arrive late." Incorrect: "Terminate all employees."
No External Assumptions	Do not import external unmentioned facts or make ungrounded assumptions.	Statement: "Protest on the highway." Incorrect: "Accede to all demands" (demands are unknown).
Administrative Feasibility	A responsible authority must be realistically able to execute the step using available resources.	Statement: "Forest fire spreading." Incorrect: "Change the weather pattern" (infeasible).
C.L.E.A.R. Filter	Test every action against: Concrete, Linked, Extreme-free, Assumption-free, and Realistic.	Public awareness drives work for ignorance-based issues, while structural enforcement is needed for immediate hazards.

Part 2: Time Management

Time Rule	Strategy
15-Second Rule	If after reading the statement and courses of action the core issue remains unclear or ambiguous, skip and flag for review.
45-Second Rule	Spend a maximum of 45 seconds per question. Apply the C.L.E.A.R. filter, eliminate extreme options, and choose the most balanced action.
