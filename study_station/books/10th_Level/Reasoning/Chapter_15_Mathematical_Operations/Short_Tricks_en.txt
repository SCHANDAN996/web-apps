🪄 Short Tricks & Skip Strategy

🔮 Mnemonic Magic Box

1. The RDB Rule
"Rohan's grandmother bakes."
Replace → Decode → BODMAS
When you see changed symbols, replace all signs first, then decode the expression, and finally apply BODMAS. No matter how tricky the question, this three‑step dance never fails.

2. Sign Swap Mantra
"SIGN बदलो, BALANCE ढूँढो"
For equation‑balancing problems, never solve from scratch. Simply swap the two signs given in the option, recalculate, and check if the balance is restored. This tiny habit saves precious minutes.

3. BODMAS Buddy
"Big Orange Dinosaurs Munch And Swallow"
Brackets → Order (powers) → Division → Multiplication → Addition → Subtraction.
And remember, within the same family (D‑M and A‑S), work from left to right like a lazy lizard crawling across the page.

4. Unmask Before You Task
"Symbols are masks; unmask before you task."
Every coded sign wears a costume. The moment you see ‘+’ means ‘–’, strip off the mask. Write the real expression on paper and let BODMAS dance on the real operation, not the costume.

5. The 15‑45 Skip Beat
"15 – Decide, 45 – Slide."
If the pattern isn’t clear in 15 seconds, mark the question and skip.
If you’ve tested 2‑3 options and still stuck at 45 seconds, slide away—don’t let one question steal your whole show.

6. Option Test, No Stress
"Option Test is Best."
For “which two signs should be interchanged?” always test the options one by one. It’s faster than solving from scratch and eliminates silly BODMAS mistakes. Swap signs, compute, and if it fits, you win.

7. The GEM Rule for Double Relations
"G.E.M. – Greater, Equal, Minor — first translate, then meditate."
When symbols represent >, <, =, first translate the whole statement into inequalities, then meditate (evaluate). Never assume; write the chain.

8. Heavy Language Trap Escape
"Read Like a Snail, Not a Whale."
When you see “one more than,” “half of,” or “not less than,” slow down. Underline the exact meaning. A whale swallows everything and chokes; a snail chews each word and survives.

9. Square Eyes, Zero Fears
"When you spot a custom operation, Square your eyes first."
In patterns like 4 * 6 = 52, immediately check a² + b². The most common hidden operation in verbal reasoning is a square dance. Once you spot it, the answer screams.

10. Map the Family Tree
"Family first, maths later."
For hybrid questions mixing operations with relation (e.g., P + Q means father), forget the mathematical meaning of the signs. First map the relationships on paper, then read the question. Maths will automatically follow.

11. Interchange of Signs and Digits – Double Dance
"Digits dance first, signs prance next."
When a question says “interchange signs + and –, and also digits 2 and 5,” always change the digits first (the mask of numbers), then prance the signs. Order matters, or you’ll end up with a messy answer.

12. BODMAS Left‑to‑Right Jingle
"Division‑Multiplication: left to right lane. Addition‑Subtraction: same path again."
Never do multiplication before division if division appears first. Treat them as equal partners walking hand‑in‑hand from left to right.

13. The Un‑Equal Trap
"Equality often equals trouble."
If a question codes ‘=’ as “not equal to” or “either greater than or equal to,” highlight it. The examiner bets on your habit of treating ‘=’ as equal. A single underline can save a precious mark.

🎯 Skip Strategy – When to Move On

If the pattern of symbol substitution or custom operation is not understood within 15 seconds, mark the question for review and jump to the next. The mind often figures it out subconsciously; you can return with fresh eyes.

In sign‑interchange or equation‑balancing problems, if you have tested three options and none works, do not test the fourth frantically. There is a high chance the equation is already correct or your test is flawed. Skip it and revisit later if time permits.

Double‑relation or hybrid (family/direction + math) questions that feel like a jigsaw puzzle, with heavy language and multiple steps, are prime candidates for a 45‑second skip. You can answer two easier questions in the same time.
