📊 PYQ Analysis | Mathematical Operations (गणितीय संक्रियाएँ)
===

**1. How Exams Ask This Chapter (Pattern Analysis)**

This chapter appears regularly in SSC CGL, SSC CHSL, IBPS/SBI PO and RRB NTPC — typically one or two questions per paper. The questions fall into four recurring types:

- **Symbol Substitution (most frequent):** A code is given ("'+' means '–', '×' means '÷' …") and you must replace every symbol, then apply BODMAS. This is the backbone of the chapter and appears in almost every SSC shift.
- **Sign Interchange / Equation Balancing:** "Which two signs should be interchanged to make the equation correct?" These are fastest when you test the given options one by one — never solve from scratch.
- **Letter-coded Operators:** Letters (A, B, C, D or P, Q, R, S) stand for operations. The trap is alphabetical-order bias — students assume A = add, B = subtract.
- **Custom / Pattern-based Operations:** "If 2 * 3 * 4 = 24 …" — a hidden rule must be discovered. More common in Bank exams; these are the most time-consuming type.

**Examiner's favourite traps:**
- Almost-right option pairs in interchange questions (one pair gives 24, another 21 — only one matches).
- Negative results after substitution (sign slips in the last step).
- "Simultaneous replacement" wording — if you replace one symbol at a time you get a wrong answer.
- Treating a relational sign ('>') as an operator in letter-coded questions.

**2. High-Yield PYQ-Style Questions with Verified Solutions**

|#|Question|Answer & Solution|Trap|Skip?|
|---|---|---|---|---|
|1|**Q.** If '+' means '–', '–' means '×', '×' means '÷' and '÷' means '+', then 18 ÷ 3 × 2 – 4 + 6 = ?|**Ans:** 18. Replace: 18 + 3 ÷ 2 × 4 – 6. BODMAS: 3 ÷ 2 × 4 = 6, so 18 + 6 – 6 = **18**.|Multiple symbol flips overload working memory; students forget to finish division/multiplication before addition.|**45-sec skip if you re-read the code more than twice.**|
|2|**Q.** If I denotes '÷', J denotes '×', K denotes '–' and L denotes '+', then 118 L (16 I 4) K (72 J 2) L 28 L (39 J 3) = ?|**Ans:** 123. Replace: 118 + (16÷4) – (72×2) + 28 + (39×3) = 118 + 4 – 144 + 28 + 117 = **123**.|Brackets + multiple substitutions create heavy load; students mis-substitute inside brackets.|**15-sec skip if you can't write the full expression in that time.**|
|3|**Q.** a+b means a–b; a–b means a×b; a×b means a÷b; a÷b means a+b. Then 10 + 30 – 100 × 50 ÷ 25 = ?|**Ans:** –25. Replace simultaneously: 10 – 30 × 100 ÷ 50 + 25. BODMAS: 30 × 100 ÷ 50 = 60, so 10 – 60 + 25 = **–25**.|"Simultaneous replacement" — replacing one symbol at a time gives 0 or +15 instead.|**Do NOT skip — this classic type appears in every exam.**|
|4|**Q.** Which two signs should be interchanged to make the equation correct? 8 × 2 + 5 – 3 = 21|**Ans:** × and –. Swap: 8 – 2 + 5 × 3 = 6 + 15 = **21** ✓. (Other pairs fail: ×↔+ gives 8+2×5–3 = 15; +↔– gives 8×2–5+3 = 14.)|Options contain "almost-right" pairs (15, 14, 21). Mental testing without writing causes confusion.|**15-sec rule: write out the first two option tests; if not found, mark & move.**|
|5|**Q.** If 'A' denotes '+', 'B' denotes '×', 'C' denotes '–' and 'D' denotes '÷', then (48 D 12) C 71 A 14 B (96 D 6) A 3 B 12 = ?|**Ans:** 193. Replace: (48÷12) – 71 + 14×(96÷6) + 3×12 = 4 – 71 + 224 + 36 = **193**.|Alphabet-based substitution — students confuse A with addition and B with subtraction due to alphabetical-order bias.|**15-sec skip if the code mapping isn't crystal-clear.**|
|6|**Q.** If P denotes 'multiplied by', R denotes 'subtracted from', S denotes 'added to' and Q denotes 'divided by', which equation is true? One option: 8 P 2 S 4 R 6 Q 3 = 18|**Ans:** That option is correct: 8×2 + 4 – 6÷3 = 16 + 4 – 2 = **18** ✓.|Multiple equations in options; aspirants test only one and mark, missing the one that actually fits.|**45-sec skip if you've tested 3 options and none fit.**|
|7|**Q.** If 2 * 3 * 4 = 24 and 3 * 4 * 5 = 60, then 4 * 5 * 6 = ?|**Ans:** 120. Logic: a * b * c = a × b × c. Check: 2×3×4 = 24 ✓, 3×4×5 = 60 ✓. So 4×5×6 = **120**.|Hidden-pattern traps — students hunt for exotic rules (squares, concatenation) when the rule is simple multiplication.|**45-sec skip — if no pattern emerges quickly, these are time-sinks.**|
|8|**Q.** If '+' means '÷', '÷' means '–', '–' means '×' and '×' means '+', then 12 + 6 ÷ 3 – 2 × 8 = ?|**Ans:** 4. Replace: 12 ÷ 6 – 3 × 2 + 8. BODMAS: 12÷6 = 2 and 3×2 = 6, so 2 – 6 + 8 = **4**.|Sign slip: after substitution, the subtraction-multiplication mix tempts students into left-to-right evaluation instead of BODMAS.|**15-sec rule: if you can't write the replaced expression, skip.**|
|9|**Q.** Select the correct combination of mathematical signs to replace the asterisks: 5 * 3 * 2 = 13|**Ans:** × and –. Insert: 5 × 3 – 2 = 15 – 2 = **13** ✓. (Check alternatives: 5+3×2 = 11; 5×3+2 = 17; 5–3×2 = –1 — none equal 13.)|Students insert signs randomly instead of testing each option systematically against the RHS.|**45-sec skip if more than two placeholders — trial-and-error cost is high.**|
|10|**Q.** If K denotes '×', T denotes '>', V denotes '+' and R denotes '–', which of the following is correct? One option: 5 K 3 T 10 V 2 R 1|**Ans:** That option is correct: 5×3 > 10+2–1 → 15 > 11 → **True**.|Symbols for inequalities — students treat '>' as an operator rather than a relational sign.|**15-sec skip if mapping isn't done in one go.**|

**3. Summary & Strategy**

- **Symbol Substitution** is the most frequently asked type — master the RDB method: **Replace → Decode → BODMAS**.
- **Sign Interchange** questions are fastest when solved by testing options — never derive from scratch.
- **Letter-coded operators** punish alphabetical assumptions; write the mapping down before touching the expression.
- **Custom operations** are rising in Bank exams; test the simplest rule (product, sum, difference) first before exotic patterns.

**Time-Management Review**

|Rule|Applicable To|Action|
|---|---|---|
|15-sec Rule|Q2, Q4, Q5, Q8, Q10|Skip if the mapping isn't clear and replacement hasn't started.|
|45-sec Rule|Q1, Q3, Q6, Q7, Q9|Skip if 2–3 options/patterns tested and still stuck.|

**Note:** SSC and Bank exams consistently ask 1–2 questions from this chapter per paper, usually at easy-to-moderate difficulty; Railway NTPC variants tend to be simpler and pattern-based. Aspirants who master symbol substitution and option-testing for interchange questions can secure these marks quickly.
