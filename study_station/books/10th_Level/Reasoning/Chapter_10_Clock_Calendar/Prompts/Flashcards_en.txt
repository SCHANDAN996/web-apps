Here are 18 flashcards for the chapter, with curiosity-invoking questions, concise answers, mnemonic tricks, and examiner's traps.

Flashcard 1
Front: You glance at a clock and it shows 3:15. What’s the exact angle between the hour and minute hands right now — and why do most students get it wrong?
Back: 7.5°. At 3:15, the minute hand is at 90°, but the hour hand has moved 7.5° ahead of 3 (since it moves 0.5°/min). So |90 – 97.5| = 7.5° (not 0°).

Flashcard 2
Front: What is the one magic number that solves “when will the hands be together, opposite, or at right angles” — and how is it derived?
Back: 5.5° per minute — the relative speed of the minute hand with respect to the hour hand (6° – 0.5°). For full lap (coincidence again): 360/5.5 = 65⁵⁄₁₁ minutes.

Flashcard 3
Front: A clock shows 4:40. You look at its reflection in a mirror. What time does the mirror show? (Bonus: what’s the trick to never confuse mirror and water?)
Back: Mirror time = 11:60 – 4:40 = 7:20. Trick: “Mirror to Midnight” (subtract from 11:60), “Water to Wet evening” (subtract from 18:30).

Flashcard 4
Front: The clock in your exam hall shows 10:10. What is the water image (reflection in a pond) of this time?
Back: Water image = 18:30 – 10:10 = 8:20. Be careful: water uses 18:30 (6:30 PM), mirror uses 11:60. The examiner’s trap is mixing them; underline the word “water” or “mirror” before solving.

Flashcard 5
Front: How many times do the hands of a clock overlap in 12 hours? And why is it 11, not 12?
Back: 11 times. Because between 12:00 and 12:00 again, the first overlap is just after 1:05, and the 11th overlap is exactly at 12:00 — but that belongs to the next cycle. So 11.

Flashcard 6
Front: Your grandfather says, “My watch gains 5 minutes every day. If I set it right at noon today, when will it show the correct time again?”
Back: 144 days. To show correct time again, it must be 12 hours (720 minutes) fast. 720 ÷ 5 = 144 days.

Flashcard 7
Front: What is the month code for April in an ordinary year? Use the Pizza Mnemonic to recall.
Back: 6. “April Fool’s joke makes you walk 6 extra steps to the pizza.” (Jan=0, Feb=3, Mar=3, Apr=6, May=1, Jun=4, Jul=6, Aug=2, Sep=5, Oct=0, Nov=3, Dec=5)

Flashcard 8
Front: In a leap year, two month codes change. Which ones, and to what?
Back: January changes from 0 to 6, February changes from 3 to 2. All others remain the same. (Think: “Leap year gives January a 6-pack and February loses a step.”)

Flashcard 9
Front: What is the century code for any year in the 1900s? Why is it not the same as the 2000s?
Back: 1900–1999: code 0. 2000–2099: code 6. Because 1900 was not a leap year (century rule), which changes the odd day count for the century.

Flashcard 10
Front: Calculate the day of 15th August 1947 using the day formula: (Date + Month code + Year code + Century code) mod 7.
Back: Friday. (15 + Aug code 2 + Year code (47 + 11) mod 7 = 2 + Century code 0 = 19 mod 7 = 5, i.e., Friday)

Flashcard 11
Front: Without pen and paper, quickly tell: If 1st January 2024 is Monday, what day is 31st December 2024? (2024 is leap)
Back: Tuesday. Leap year has 2 odd days. So 31st Dec = Monday + 2 = Wednesday? Wait: 1 Jan Mon, 31 Dec is the last day of the year. Number of days from 1 Jan to 31 Dec is 365 days (leap). 1 Jan to next year 1 Jan is 366 days, 2 odd days. So 1 Jan 2025 would be Wednesday (Mon+2). But 31 Dec 2024 is the day before that, so it's Tuesday. Yes.

Flashcard 14
Front: Examiner’s trap: When the question says “between 4 and 5 o’clock, when will the hands be opposite each other?” you solve and get 4 hr 54⁶⁄₁₁ min. But the options include 54⁶⁄₁₁ min past 4 and also 54⁶⁄₁₁ min past 5. How do you avoid the trap?
Back: Carefully note the hour range. Opposite hands occur at 4 hr (60×4+?)/11 etc. The formula gives minutes past 4. The correct answer is 4 hr 54⁶⁄₁₁ min. The trap option “5 hr 54⁶⁄₁₁ min” is wrong because after 5, it would be a different equation.

Flashcard 15
Front: Another examiner’s trap: “What is the angle between the hands at 12:15?” You apply formula |30H – 5.5M| and get 82.5°. But that’s not the acute angle. What’s the right answer?
Back: 82.5° is already acute, no problem. Wait, 12:15 -> H=12 (or 0?), formula |30*12 – 5.5*15| = |360 – 82.5| = 277.5°, acute = 360-277.5 = 82.5°. So okay. But trap: if H is taken as 0, |0 – 82.5| = 82.5° (correct). Some students mistakenly use H=12 and get 277.5°, then forget to subtract from 360 and pick 277.5° if it’s an option. So trap: always give acute angle unless asked otherwise.

Flashcard 16
Front: How do you quickly know whether a century year is leap or not without memorizing?
Back: A century year (ending in 00) is leap only if it is divisible by 400. So 2000 yes, 1900 no. The Pizza delivery man skips a house every century to keep the route accurate.

Flashcard 17
Front: On 1st January 2001, it was Monday. What was the day on 1st January 2000? (2000 is leap)
Back: Saturday. From 1 Jan 2000 to 1 Jan 2001 is 366 days (leap year) = 52 weeks + 2 odd days. So 1 Jan 2001 is Monday, so go back 2 days: Saturday.

Flashcard 18
Front: Final trick: When solving calendar problems, what’s the one mistake that causes more errors than any other? (Hint: It’s about month codes in leap years.)
Back: Forgetting to change January and February codes if the date is in a leap year (Jan=6, Feb=2) and the date falls before 29th Feb. Students use ordinary year codes and get the wrong day.
