🧠 Mnemonic Tricks for Clock & Calendar

1. Minute Hand Speed

"Minute hand is a Motorbike — 6° per minute, vroom vroom!"
(M for Minute, Motorbike, and 6 looks like a bike's wheel when you squint.)

2. Hour Hand Speed

"Hour hand is a lazy snail — only 0.5° per minute. Slow and steady."
(H for Hour, Half degree — snail pace.)

3. Relative Speed 5.5°

"55 is the race number of the minute hand overtaking the hour hand. 5.5° per minute, like two 5's in a sports car."
(Imagine a car with number 55 zooming past a snail.)

4. Angle Formula: |30H – 5.5M|

"30 Hot Moms minus 5.5 Mad Men in absolute anger!"
(30×H – 5.5×M, take the absolute value. Absurd image sticks.)

5. Mirror Time: 11:60 trick

"Mirror = Minus from Midnight (11:60)."
"When you look into a mirror at night, you see 11:60 – your time."
(Alliteration: Mirror, Minus, Midnight.)

6. Water Image: 18:30 trick

"Water = Wet evening at 6:30 PM (18:30). Subtract your time from this wet puddle."
(W for Water, Wet; 18:30 is 6:30 PM — you imagine splashing in a puddle.)

7. Month Codes – Pizza Mnemonic

"Jan – No pizza (0)
Feb – 3 cold slices (3)
March – March in place eating 3 (3)
Apr – 6 extra steps for a prank (6)
May – 1 Maypole dance, one twirl (1)
June – 4 wings on a June bug (4)
July – 6 fireworks sparks (6)
August – 2 'u's (2)
September – 5 school subjects (5)
October – 0, ghost pizza (0)
November – 3 pies (3)
December – 5 golden rings (5)"
*(Link each number to a silly image. For leap year: Jan gets a 6-pack, Feb loses a slice (2).)*

8. Century Codes – 1900s vs 2000s

"1900s – 0dd one out, zero leap.
2000s – 6uper leap, 6-pack century."
(1900 was not a leap year, 2000 was — so 1900s code 0, 2000s code 6. "Zero leap" vs "6uper leap".)

9. Odd Days – Yearly Shift

"Ordinary year – 1 day ahead (like stepping one step forward).
Leap year – 2 days ahead (like a frog leaping two steps)."
(Visualise a person stepping vs a frog jumping.)

10. Same Calendar Year – Ordinary vs Leap

"Ordinary repeats like a 6-11 store visit (6 or 11 years).
Leap repeats after a long 28-day February month… wait, 28 years!"
*(Connect 28 with February's 28 days in non-leap. Actually February has 28 days, so leap year calendar repeats after 28 years.)*

11. Coincidence/Meeting Time

"60H ÷ 11 – Hot Hands Hug at 60H/11 minutes."
*(H is the hour number; the hands hug (meet) at M = 60H/11 minutes past H.)*

12. Right Angle (90°) Times

"90 degree salute — add or subtract 90 from 60H and divide by 11. Like a soldier's salute."
*(M = (60H ± 90)/11. Salute with a 90° arm.)*

13. Opposite (180°) Times

"180 degree flip — add or subtract 180 from 60H, divide by 11. Do a complete flip."
*(M = (60H ± 180)/11. Visualise a gymnast doing a 180° split.)*

14. Faulty Clock – When Correct Again

"720 rule – 720 minutes (12 hours) divided by gain/loss per day gives days to be right again. Think of a 720° skateboard spin to reset."
*(If watch gains x min/day, days = 720/x. A skateboarder doing two full spins (720°) lands correctly.)*

15. Calendar Day Calculation – The Stack

"DaMoYeCe – Date + Month code + Year code + Century code, all mod 7. Sounds like 'Damn! Oh, yes!' – DaMoYeCe!"
(Damoyece! A funny exclamation to recall the order.)

⏭️ Skip Strategy: When to Move On

15-Second Rule: If you don't recognise the question pattern within 15 seconds — e.g., a convoluted faulty clock with alternating gains/losses, or an unusual calendar date before 1900 with century leap adjustments — skip immediately. Mark it for review and move to a surer question.

45-Second Rule: Even if you know the method, if the calculation is stretching beyond 45 seconds (e.g., detailed odd‑day counting for a distant year or a complex multi‑step faulty clock), skip. You can return after completing easier, high‑accuracy questions.

Trap Spotting: If a Clock/Calendar question has options that are suspiciously close (e.g., 165°, 180°, 195° for a 12:30 angle) or asks for "water image" but also lists the mirror image in options, it's a trap. If you're low on time, skip and guess; otherwise, underline the key word ("water" or "mirror") before solving.
