101. At what time between 5 and 6 o'clock will the hands of a clock be at right angles for the first time?
(a) 5:21 9/11 (b) 5:10 10/11 (c) 5:43 7/11 (d) 5:16 4/11
Answer: (b)
Solution: The tempting option 5:21 9/11 comes from using 150 + 90 instead of 150 − 90, which gives the second right angle, not the first. Here |150 − 5.5M| = 90 gives 5.5M = 60, so M = 120/11 = 10 10/11 minutes, so the answer is 5:10 10/11.
Source: PYQ-style

102. A clock shows 6:50. What is the time shown in its mirror image?
(a) 5:10 (b) 4:10 (c) 5:50 (d) 6:10
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is subtracting from 12:00, which mis-handles the borrowing of minutes; the correct rule is 11:60 − time. Mirror time = 11:60 − 6:50 = 5:10, so the answer is 5:10.
Source: PYQ-style

103. What day of the week was 26th January 2015?
(a) Sunday (b) Tuesday (c) Wednesday (d) Monday
Answer: (d)
Solution: The tempting Sunday comes from counting 26 days forward from 1st January instead of 25 days ahead of it. 1st January 2015 was a Thursday; 26th January is 25 days later, and 25 mod 7 = 4, so Thursday + 4 days = Monday, so the answer is Monday.
Source: PYQ-style

104. A clock gains 6 minutes every day. If set right at 12 noon on Monday, after how many days will it show the correct time again?
(a) 60 days (b) 120 days (c) 150 days (d) 90 days
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is thinking the clock must gain 24 hours; a clock shows the correct time again after gaining just 12 hours = 720 minutes. Days needed = 720 / 6 = 120, so the answer is 120 days.
Source: PYQ-style

105. What is the angle between the hands of a clock at 9:30?
(a) 90° (b) 120° (c) 105° (d) 135°
Answer: (c)
Solution: The tempting 90° forgets that at 9:30 the hour hand has moved halfway towards 10. Angle = |30(9) − 5.5(30)| = |270 − 165| = 105°, so the answer is 105°.
Source: PYQ-style

106. At what time between 8 and 9 o'clock will the hands of a clock coincide?
(a) 8:43 7/11 (b) 8:40 1/11 (c) 8:44 6/11 (d) 8:38 2/11
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is using M = 60(H−1)/11, which suits the wrong hour. For coincidence M = 60H/11 = 480/11 = 43 7/11 minutes, so the answer is 8:43 7/11.
Source: PYQ-style

107. If 1st January 2024 was Monday, what day of the week was 1st January 2025?
(a) Tuesday (b) Sunday (c) Wednesday (d) Thursday
Answer: (c)
Solution: The tempting Tuesday comes from adding only 1 odd day, forgetting that 2024 is a leap year with 366 days = 2 odd days. Monday + 2 days = Wednesday, so the answer is Wednesday.
Source: PYQ-style

108. A clock strikes the number of the hour at each hour, and strikes once at each quarter-hour (15, 30, 45 minutes). How many times will it strike from 8:00 AM to 12:00 noon (both inclusive)?
(a) 58 (b) 60 (c) 62 (d) 64
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is counting 4 quarter-hour strikes per hour including the hour itself; only 3 quarter positions fall strictly inside each hour. Hourly strikes = 8 + 9 + 10 + 11 + 12 = 50, quarter strikes = 3 × 4 = 12, total = 62, so the answer is 62.
Source: PYQ-style

109. What is the smaller angle between the hands of a clock at 2:50?
(a) 145° (b) 140° (c) 135° (d) 155°
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is reporting 215° directly; the question asks for the smaller angle. |30(2) − 5.5(50)| = 215°, so the smaller angle = 360° − 215° = 145°, so the answer is 145°.
Source: PYQ-style

110. What day of the week was 15th August 1947?
(a) Saturday (b) Wednesday (c) Thursday (d) Friday
Answer: (d)
Solution: The tempting Saturday comes from a one-day error in counting odd days across 1900–1946, where 1900 must not be counted as a leap year. Correct odd-day reckoning (and the well-known record of Independence Day) gives Friday, so the answer is Friday.
Source: PYQ-style

111. At what time between 1 and 2 o'clock will the hands of a clock point in opposite directions?
(a) 1:42 8/11 (b) 1:38 2/11 (c) 1:36 4/11 (d) 1:45 6/11
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is setting 30(1) − 5.5M = 180, which gives a negative minute value and a wrong time. Correctly, 5.5M − 30 = 180 gives M = 420/11 = 38 2/11 minutes, so the answer is 1:38 2/11.
Source: PYQ-style

112. What day of the week was 15th August 2020?
(a) Friday (b) Thursday (c) Sunday (d) Saturday
Answer: (d)
Solution: The tempting Friday comes from forgetting that 2020 is a leap year, which shifts the count by one day after February. 1st January 2020 was a Wednesday; 15th August is the 228th day, and 227 mod 7 = 3, so Wednesday + 3 days = Saturday, so the answer is Saturday.
Source: PYQ-style

113. A clock is set right at 8:00 AM. It loses 5 minutes per hour. What is the approximate true time when the clock indicates 11:00 AM on the same day?
(a) 11:12 AM (b) 11:20 AM (c) 11:16 AM (d) 11:25 AM
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is simply adding 15 lost minutes to get 11:15-ish guesses without the ratio method. The clock covers 55 minutes per true hour, so true minutes = 180 × 60/55 ≈ 196.4 minutes = 3 hr 16 min, so the answer is 11:16 AM.
Source: PYQ-style

114. At what times between 3 and 4 o'clock will the hands of a clock be 8 minutes apart?
(a) 3:07 7/11 and 3:25 1/11 (b) 3:10 10/11 and 3:30 (c) 3:12 and 3:32 (d) 3:05 5/11 and 3:28 4/11
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is treating 8 minutes apart as 8° instead of 8 × 6 = 48°. |90 − 5.5M| = 48 gives M = 84/11 = 7 7/11 or M = 276/11 = 25 1/11, so the answer is 3:07 7/11 and 3:25 1/11.
Source: PYQ-style

115. Which year after 2019 will have the exact same calendar as the year 2019?
(a) 2025 (b) 2030 (c) 2026 (d) 2024
Answer: (b)
Solution: The tempting 2025 ignores that the odd days must sum to a multiple of 7 and the year type must match. Odd days from 2019 to 2029 sum to 14 ≡ 0 (mod 7), and 2030 is also an ordinary year, so the answer is 2030.
Source: PYQ-style

116. A clock is set right at 10:00 AM on Monday. It gains 4 minutes per hour. What is the true time when the clock indicates 6:00 PM on the same day?
(a) 5:45 PM (b) 5:20 PM (c) 6:30 PM (d) 5:30 PM
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is subtracting the total gain of 32 minutes directly from 6:00 PM. The clock runs 64 minutes per true hour, so true elapsed = 8 × 60/64 = 7.5 hours, giving 5:30 PM, so the answer is 5:30 PM.
Source: PYQ-style

117. A clock shows 7:55. What is the time shown in its mirror image?
(a) 5:05 (b) 4:05 (c) 6:05 (d) 3:55
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is subtracting from 12:00, which gives 4:05 only if minutes borrow correctly; many wrongly get 5:05. Mirror time = 11:60 − 7:55 = 4:05, so the answer is 4:05.
Source: PYQ-style

118. How many times do the hands of a clock coincide between 10:00 AM and 10:00 PM?
(a) 12 (b) 10 (c) 13 (d) 11
Answer: (d)
Solution: The tempting 12 assumes one coincidence per hour, but between 11 and 1 the hands coincide only once (at 12). In any 12-hour span they coincide exactly 11 times, so the answer is 11.
Source: PYQ-style

119. A clock gains 15 seconds per hour. It was set right at 8:00 AM. What time will it show at 8:00 PM on the same day?
(a) 8:03 PM (b) 8:02 PM (c) 8:06 PM (d) 8:05 PM
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is treating 15 seconds as 15 minutes or computing for 24 hours. In 12 hours the gain = 12 × 15 = 180 seconds = 3 minutes, so the answer is 8:03 PM.
Source: PYQ-style

120. What day of the week was 1st January 1900?
(a) Tuesday (b) Monday (c) Sunday (d) Wednesday
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is counting 1900 as a leap year; it is not, since 1900 is not divisible by 400. Standard Gregorian reckoning gives 1st January 1900 as Monday, so the answer is Monday.
Source: PYQ-style

121. How many leap years are there in a period of 400 consecutive years?
(a) 100 (b) 96 (c) 97 (d) 98
Answer: (c)
Solution: The tempting 100 forgets that century years like 100, 200 and 300 are not leap years unless divisible by 400. Leap years = 100 − 3 = 97, so the answer is 97.
Source: PYQ-style

122. If 1st January 2000 was Saturday, what day of the week was 31st December 2000?
(a) Tuesday (b) Monday (c) Saturday (d) Sunday
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is treating 2000 as an ordinary year, which would make the last day the same as the first. Since 2000 is a leap year, the last day is 1 day ahead: Saturday + 1 = Sunday, so the answer is Sunday.
Source: PYQ-style

123. Which year after 2024 will have the exact same calendar as 2024?
(a) 2028 (b) 2048 (c) 2052 (d) 2032
Answer: (c)
Solution: The tempting 2048 fails because the odd days from 2024 to 2047 total 30, which is not a multiple of 7. A leap year's calendar repeats after 28 years within the same century pattern: 2024 + 28 = 2052, with odd days summing to 35 ≡ 0 (mod 7), so the answer is 2052.
Source: PYQ-style

124. The mirror image of a clock shows 3:15. What is the actual time?
(a) 8:45 (b) 8:15 (c) 9:15 (d) 9:45
Answer: (a)
Solution: The tempting 9:45 comes from subtracting from 12:00 instead of 11:60. Actual time = 11:60 − 3:15 = 8:45, so the answer is 8:45.
Source: PYQ-style

125. A clock gains 30 minutes every day. If it shows the correct time at 12 noon today, after how many days will it show the correct time again?
(a) 24 days (b) 12 days (c) 36 days (d) 48 days
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is computing for a 24-hour gain (1440/30 = 48); a clock repeats correctly after gaining only 12 hours = 720 minutes. Days = 720 / 30 = 24, so the answer is 24 days.
Source: PYQ-style
