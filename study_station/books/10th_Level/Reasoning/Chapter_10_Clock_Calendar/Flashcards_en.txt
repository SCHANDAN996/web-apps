🃏 Flashcards — Clock & Calendar (20 Cards)

Card 1
Front: You glance at a clock and it shows 3:15. What's the exact angle between the hour and minute hands right now — and why do most students get it wrong?
Back: 7.5°. At 3:15, the minute hand is at 90°, but the hour hand has drifted 7.5° ahead of 3. Formula: |30×3 – 5.5×15| = |90 – 82.5| = 7.5°. The common mistake is assuming 0°.

Card 2
Front: What is the one magic number that solves "when will the hands be together, opposite, or at right angles" — and how is it derived?
Back: 5.5° per minute. Minute hand speed = 6°/min, hour hand speed = 0.5°/min. Relative speed = 6 – 0.5 = 5.5°/min. Time to gain 360° = 360/5.5 = 65⁵⁄₁₁ minutes.

Card 3
Front: A clock shows 4:40. You look at its reflection in a mirror. What time does the mirror show? (Bonus: what's the trick to never confuse mirror and water?)
Back: Mirror time = 11:60 – 4:40 = 7:20.
Mnemonic: "Mirror → Minus from Midnight (11:60)" and "Water → Wet evening (18:30)".

Card 4
Front: The clock in your exam hall shows 10:10. What is the water image (reflection in a pond) of this time?
Back: 18:30 – 10:10 = 8:20. Trap: Many use the mirror rule (11:60) and get 1:50. Underline "water" or "mirror" in the question before solving.

Card 5
Front: How many times do the hands of a clock overlap in 12 hours? And why is it 11, not 12?
Back: 11 times. Between 12:00 and 12:00 again, the first overlap is just after 1:05, and the 11th overlap is exactly at 12:00 — but that 12:00 belongs to the next cycle.

Card 6
Front: Your grandfather says, "My watch gains 5 minutes every day. If I set it right at noon today, when will it show the correct time again?"
Back: 144 days. To show the correct time, it must be 12 hours (720 minutes) fast. 720 ÷ 5 = 144 days.

Card 7
Front: What is the month code for April in an ordinary year? Use the Pizza Mnemonic to recall.
Back: 6. "April Fool's joke makes you walk 6 extra steps."
Month codes: Jan=0, Feb=3, Mar=3, Apr=6, May=1, Jun=4, Jul=6, Aug=2, Sep=5, Oct=0, Nov=3, Dec=5.

Card 8
Front: In a leap year, two month codes change. Which ones, and to what?
Back: January changes from 0 to 6, February changes from 3 to 2. All others remain the same.
Trick: "Leap year gives January a 6-pack and February loses a step."

Card 9
Front: What is the century code for any year in the 1900s? Why is it not the same as the 2000s?
Back: 1900–1999 → code 0. 2000–2099 → code 6.
Because 1900 was not a leap year (century rule), changing the odd day count.

Card 10
Front: Calculate the day of 15th August 1947 using the formula: (Date + Month code + Year code + Century code) mod 7.
Back: Friday.
Date=15, Aug code=2, Year code (47 + 11) mod 7 = 2, Century code (1900)=0 → 15+2+2+0=19, 19 mod 7 = 5 → Friday.

Card 11
Front: If 1st January 2024 is Monday, what day is 31st December 2024? (2024 is leap)
Back: Tuesday. A leap year has 2 odd days, so 1st Jan 2025 would be Wednesday (Mon+2), making 31st Dec 2024 the day before: Tuesday.

Card 12
Front: An examiner asks: "How many times do the hands of a clock form a right angle between 2 PM and 10 PM?" The option 16 is a trap. What's the correct count?
Back: 14 times. In 12 hours there are 22 right angles (not 24), because in the 2–3 and 8–9 stretches one right angle is shared with the neighbouring hour. Over 8 hours the count is 14, not the naive 2 × 8 = 16.

Card 14
Front: Examiner's Trap: "Between 4 and 5 o'clock, when will the hands be opposite each other?" Options include 4 hr 54⁶⁄₁₁ min and 5 hr 54⁶⁄₁₁ min. How do you avoid the trap?
Back: The formula gives minutes past 4, so the answer is 4 hr 54⁶⁄₁₁ min. The option with 5 hr is a deliberate trap for those who misread the hour. Always check the hour range given in the question.

Card 15
Front: Examiner's Trap: "What is the angle between the hands at 12:15?" You apply |30H – 5.5M| with H=12 and get 277.5°. But that's not the acute angle. The options include 277.5° and 82.5°. Which one do you pick?
Back: 82.5° (the acute angle). Unless the question explicitly asks for the reflex angle, always give the smaller angle (360° – θ if θ > 180°). Many students pick 277.5° because they forget this rule.

Card 16
Front: How do you quickly know if a century year (like 1900 or 2000) is leap without memorizing all rules?
Back: A century year (ending in 00) is leap only if divisible by 400. 2000 → Yes (2000÷400=5), 1900 → No. The Pizza delivery man skips a house every century to keep the route accurate.

Card 17
Front: On 1st January 2001, it was Monday. What was the day on 1st January 2000? (2000 is leap)
Back: Saturday. 1 Jan 2000 to 1 Jan 2001 is 366 days = 52 weeks + 2 odd days. Going back 2 days from Monday gives Saturday.

Card 18
Front: Final Trap Alert: When solving calendar problems, what's the one mistake that causes more errors than any other?
Back: Forgetting to change January and February codes in a leap year (Jan=6, Feb=2) when the date is before 29th February. Students use ordinary year codes and get the wrong day. Always check: Is the year leap? Is the date before March? Then adjust.

Card 19
Front: A clock gains 10 minutes per day. If set correctly at 12 noon on Monday, what will it show at 12 noon on Friday?
Back: It will show 12:40 PM (gained 10 min × 4 days = 40 min). Trap: Some calculate 5 days instead of 4 (Monday noon to Friday noon is exactly 4 days).

Card 20
Front: What's the quick trick to find the day of any date in the 2000s without a full formula?
Back: Use century code 6 for 2000–2099, year code = (YY + ⌊YY/4⌋) mod 7, then add month code and date, and take mod 7. Result 0 = Sunday, 1 = Monday, … 6 = Saturday. Remember to use leap-year month codes (Jan=6, Feb=2) for dates before March in a leap year.
