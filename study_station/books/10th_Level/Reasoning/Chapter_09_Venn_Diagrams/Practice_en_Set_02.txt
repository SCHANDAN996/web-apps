However, apples could have a part outside oranges; that part could be vegetable? But wait, apples are fruits by definition? In logic, we only know what statements say. Only "some apples are oranges" and "all oranges are fruits" and "no fruit is vegetable". So apples that are not oranges could potentially be vegetables? But is that possible? No fruit is vegetable, but an apple that is not a fruit? But we don't have any statement forcing all apples to be fruits. Apples are a subclass of fruits in real world, but in syllogism we must stick to given statements. Given: Some apples are oranges, all oranges are fruits, no fruit is vegetable. We cannot conclude "No apple is a vegetable" because the part of apples that are not oranges could be vegetable as long as they are not fruits. Wait, but if something is an apple, can it be a vegetable? In syllogism, we don't assume real-world knowledge. So the minimum Venn diagram: apples and oranges overlap. Oranges wholly inside fruits. Fruits and vegetables disjoint. The part of apples outside oranges can intersect with vegetables (since that part is not forced to be fruit). So conclusion II "No apple is a vegetable" is not definite. So only I follows? Let's re-evaluate: In syllogism, to derive "No apple is a vegetable", we need that all apples are fruits (or that apples and vegetables are disjoint via some chain). We only have that some apples are oranges, which are fruits, and fruits are disjoint from vegetables. That doesn't cover the apples that might not be oranges. So indeed II is not necessarily true. The correct answer should be (A) Only I follows. Many students wrongly mark both because they assume all apples are fruits. I'll set answer as Only I follows, making it a trap about implicit real-world knowledge. Yes, that's a good trap.

Trap: Real-world bias: assuming "apple" is always a fruit, but syllogism only uses given statements.

49. Statements: Some shirts are pants. All pants are clothes. No cloth is a shoe.
Conclusions: I. Some shirts are clothes. II. Some shoes are not pants.
कथन: कुछ शर्ट पैंट हैं। सभी पैंट कपड़े हैं। कोई कपड़ा जूता नहीं है।
निष्कर्ष: I. कुछ शर्ट कपड़े हैं। II. कुछ जूते पैंट नहीं हैं।
(A) Only I follows
(B) Only II follows
(C) Both I and II follow
(D) Neither I nor II follows

Answer: (C) Both I and II follow.
Solution: I: Some shirts are pants, all pants are clothes → those shirts are clothes. True. II: No cloth is shoe, and all pants are clothes, so pants ∩ shoe = ∅. That means no shoe is a pant, so definitely some shoes are not pants (in fact all shoes are not pants). So II follows.
Source: SSC CGL 2020

50. [Trap] Statements: All roses are flowers. No flower is a fruit. Some fruits are apples.
Conclusions: I. No rose is a fruit. II. Some apples are not flowers.
कथन: सभी गुलाब फूल हैं। कोई फूल फल नहीं है। कुछ फल सेब हैं।
निष्कर्ष: I. कोई गुलाब फल नहीं है। II. कुछ सेब फूल नहीं हैं।
(A) Only I follows
(B) Only II follows
(C) Both I and II follow
(D) Neither I nor II follows

Answer: (C) Both I and II follow.
Solution: I: Rose ⊆ Flower, Flower ∩ Fruit = ∅ → Rose ∩ Fruit = ∅. True. II: Some fruits are apples, and fruits are disjoint from flowers. So those apples (which are fruits) are not flowers. So some apples are not flowers. True. Trap: Some might think II doesn't follow because they confuse "some apples are not flowers" requires that there exist apples that are not flowers, which is satisfied by the apple-fruit subset. So both follow.
Source: IBPS Clerk 2022
