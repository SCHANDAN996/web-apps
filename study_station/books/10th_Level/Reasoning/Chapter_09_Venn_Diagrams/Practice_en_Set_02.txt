26. Which of the following Venn diagrams best represents the relationship among: Cricket, Bat, Ball?
(a) Three separate circles (b) One large circle containing two separate circles (c) Three overlapping circles (d) One circle inside another, third separate
Answer: (a)
Solution: Cricket is a sport; bat and ball are pieces of equipment. They are not subclasses of cricket, nor do they share a class relationship. All three are disjoint sets.

33. Statements: Some apples are oranges. All oranges are fruits. No fruit is a vegetable. Conclusions: I. Some apples are fruits. II. No apple is a vegetable.
(a) Only II follows (b) Both I and II follow (c) Neither I nor II follows (d) Only I follows
Answer: (d)
Solution: From statements, some apples are oranges, and all oranges are fruits, so some apples are definitely fruits (I follows). Pure syllogistic logic does not assume real-world facts, so II does not necessarily follow. Only I follows.
Source: PYQ-style

34. Statements: All phones are gadgets. Some gadgets are expensive. No expensive thing is cheap. Conclusions: I. Some phones are expensive. II. No phone is cheap.
(a) Neither I nor II follows (b) Only I follows (c) Only II follows (d) Both I and II follow
Answer: (a)
Solution: Phones ⊆ Gadgets. Gadgets and Expensive overlap. Expensive ∩ Cheap = ∅. I: Phones may be entirely inside the part of gadgets that is not expensive, so not definite. II: Phones are gadgets; gadgets may not be expensive; but could phones be cheap? Cheap is disjoint from expensive, but phones might be in non-expensive gadgets, and those might be cheap? The statement doesn't forbid gadgets from being cheap, only expensive things are not cheap. A non-expensive phone could be cheap. So II not definite.
Source: PYQ-style
