126. Which of the following Venn diagrams best represents the relationship among: Square, Rhombus, Quadrilateral?
(a) Square inside Rhombus, Rhombus inside Quadrilateral (b) Square and Rhombus overlapping inside Quadrilateral (c) Rhombus inside Square, Square inside Quadrilateral (d) Square and Rhombus separate, both inside Quadrilateral
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is treating square and rhombus as merely overlapping, but every square has four equal sides, so every square is a rhombus, and every rhombus is a quadrilateral. Hence three nested circles, so the answer is Square inside Rhombus inside Quadrilateral.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Mathematics

127. Statements: All cats are animals. Some animals are dogs. Conclusions: I. Some cats are dogs. II. Some animals are cats.
(a) Only I follows (b) Only II follows (c) Both I and II follow (d) Neither I nor II follows
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is accepting I, but the animals that are dogs need not include any cat, so I is not definite. Since all cats are animals, the animals that are cats certainly exist, so II follows, and the answer is that only II follows.

What is the ratio of the number of people who are in at least two of these to those who are in exactly one?
(a) 22:45 (b) 18:45 (c) 26:45 (d) 22:49
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is forgetting to add the "all three" group into "at least two". Exactly one = 12 + 15 + 18 = 45; at least two = 5 + 7 + 6 + 4 = 22, so the answer is 22:45.

Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is nesting ice and steam inside water because they are the same substance chemically, but as classes of physical states they are mutually exclusive — a portion of matter is solid, liquid or gas at a time. Hence the answer is three separate circles.

(a) 40% (b) 30% (c) 46% (d) 36%
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is using only the "tea and coffee only" figure of 8 and forgetting the 10 who like all three. Tea and coffee together = 8 + 10 = 18 out of 50 tea drinkers, which is 18/50 = 36%, so the answer is 36%.

136. In a group of 200 people, 120 like cricket, 110 like hockey, 100 like football. 50 like all three, 30 like cricket and hockey only, 20 like hockey and football only, 10 like cricket and football only. How many like exactly one sport?
(a) 40 (b) 50 (c) 45 (d) 60
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is subtracting only the "all three" figure from each sport's total. Only cricket = 120 − (30 + 10 + 50) = 30, only hockey = 110 − (30 + 20 + 50) = 10, only football = 100 − (10 + 20 + 50) = 20, giving 30 + 10 + 20 = 60, so the answer is 60.
