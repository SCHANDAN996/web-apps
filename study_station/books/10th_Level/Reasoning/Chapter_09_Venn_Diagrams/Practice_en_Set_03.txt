51. Which of the following Venn diagrams best represents the relationship among: Sun, Moon, Stars?
(a) Three separate circles (b) One large circle containing two overlapping circles (c) Three overlapping circles (d) One circle inside another, third separate
Answer: (a)
Solution: Sun is a star, but "Stars" as a class includes many celestial bodies. Sun is a member of Stars; Moon is a natural satellite; they are distinct classes with no subset relationships among the given words as general categories. In standard logical Venn, Sun, Moon, and Stars are treated as disjoint because Moon is not a star, Sun is a specific star but the class "Stars" is larger and "Sun" is not a subclass; so three separate circles.
Source: PYQ-style

55. [Trap] Select the Venn diagram that correctly shows the relationship among Book, Author, Publisher.
(a) Three separate circles (b) One large circle containing two separate circles (c) Author inside Book, Publisher separate (d) Book inside Author, Publisher overlapping
Answer: (a)
Solution: Book is an object, Author is a person, Publisher is an entity/company. They belong to entirely different categories. No subset or overlap relationship exists in class logic. Many students mistakenly relate them functionally (author writes book, publisher publishes book), but Venn deals with class membership, not association. Hence three disjoint circles. 56. Statements: All pencils are pens. Some pens are erasers. No eraser is a sharpener. Conclusions: I. Some pencils are erasers. II. Some pens are not sharpeners.
(a) Only I follows (b) Only II follows (c) Both I and II follow (d) Neither I nor II follows
Answer: (b)
Solution: I: Pencils ⊆ Pens. Some pens are erasers does not guarantee that the pencil part overlaps erasers. So I false. II: The pens that are erasers (some pens) are definitely not sharpeners (since no eraser is sharpener). So those pens are not sharpeners. Hence II true.
Source: PYQ-style

57. In a Venn diagram with three sets A, B, C, it is given that only A=8, only B=6, only C=10, A∩B only=4, B∩C only=5, A∩C only=3, all three=2. How many elements are in at least two sets?
(a) 12 (b) 10 (c) 14 (d) 16
Answer: (c)
Solution: At least two = (exactly two) + (all three) = (4+5+3) + 2 = 12+2 = 14.

66. [Trap] Which Venn diagram best fits Fruit, Apple, Seed?
(a) Apple inside Fruit, Seed separate (b) Seed inside Fruit, Apple inside Seed (c) Apple inside Fruit, Seed overlapping Fruit (d) Apple inside Fruit, Seed inside Apple
Answer: (d)
Solution: All apples are fruits. Seeds are a part of an apple; in class logic, a seed is not a subclass of apple but can be considered a component. However, standard competitive exams often treat "Seed" as a part, so they might show it separate. But the most common answer in such word grouping: Apple ⊆ Fruit, and Seed is a part of Apple, so nested. Many students get confused because seed is also a part of fruit directly. Still, the best representation is Apple inside Fruit, Seed inside Apple (since seeds are inside apples). Trap: Part-whole relationship might confuse; students may think Seed is separate from Apple or Fruit, but the most plausible logical nesting is used.
Source: PYQ-style

75. Which option correctly shows Players, Cricket, Hockey?
(a) Cricket inside Players, Hockey separate (b) Cricket and Hockey inside Players, no overlap (c) Three overlapping circles (d) Players inside Cricket, Hockey inside Cricket
Answer: (b)
Solution: Cricket and Hockey are sports; but here "Cricket" likely refers to people who play cricket, i.e., cricketers. In word-to-diagram questions, "Cricket" often means the players of cricket. So both cricketers and hockey players are subsets of Players, and they are distinct (unless some play both, but generally disjoint in these questions). However, many such questions treat them as separate specialties within Players. Overlap could be possible, but standard answer in SSC is disjoint subsets inside Players. We'll go with (A).
