1. Which of the following Venn diagrams best represents the relationship among: India, Asia, Earth?
(a) One circle inside another, and both inside a third (concentric circles) (b) Three separate circles (c) Two separate circles inside one large circle (d) Three mutually overlapping circles
Answer: (a)
Solution: Three separate circles would wrongly suggest India is not part of Asia. India is entirely within Asia, and Asia is entirely on Earth, so the answer is three concentric nested circles.
Source: PYQ-style

2. Which Venn diagram shows the correct relationship among: Pen, Pencil, Eraser?
(a) Three mutually overlapping circles (b) Two intersecting circles, third separate (c) One large circle containing two smaller circles (d) Three separate (mutually disjoint) circles
Answer: (d)
Solution: Overlapping circles would wrongly imply some pens are pencils. Pen, pencil, and eraser are distinct items with no subset or overlap relation, so the answer is three separate circles.
Source: PYQ-style

3. Select the Venn diagram that best illustrates the relationship: Women, Mothers, Engineers.
(a) Three completely separate circles (b) Two separate circles inside a large circle (c) One circle (Mothers) completely inside another (Women), and a third circle (Engineers) intersecting both (d) Three separate circles with no nested subset
Answer: (c)
Solution: Separate circles would wrongly deny that all mothers are women. All mothers are women, and some women and some mothers are engineers, so the answer is Mothers inside Women with Engineers intersecting both.

4. Which Venn diagram best represents the classes: Doctors, Surgeons, Neurologists?
(a) Three concentric circles (b) One large circle (Doctors) containing two smaller non-overlapping circles (c) Three mutually overlapping circles (d) Two overlapping circles inside a large circle
Answer: (b)
Solution: Concentric circles would wrongly make every neurologist a surgeon. Both surgeons and neurologists are doctors, but they are distinct specializations, so the answer is two disjoint circles inside a large circle.

6. In a geometric Venn diagram: Triangle = Teachers, Circle = Graduates, Rectangle = Women. Numbers are: only teachers = 4, only graduates = 6, only women = 7, teachers and graduates (not women) = 3, graduates and women (not teachers) = 5, all three = 1. How many graduate women are NOT teachers?
(a) 6 (b) 5 (c) 7 (d) 8
Answer: (b)
Solution: Adding the triple intersection (giving 6) is the trap, because those women are also teachers. The region common to Circle and Rectangle outside Triangle is 5, so the answer is 5.
Source: PYQ-style

7. In a Venn diagram, circle A = Men, circle B = Employed, circle C = Educated. Which region represents men who are educated but NOT employed?
(a) Region only inside circle A (b) Intersection of A and C excluding B (c) Intersection of A and B (d) Intersection of all three circles
Answer: (b)
Solution: The intersection of all three is the trap, because it includes employed men. Men who are educated but not employed lie in the intersection of A and C excluding B, so the answer is that region.
Source: PYQ-style

8. In a class of 60 students, 30 play cricket, 25 play football, and 10 play both. How many students play neither game?
(a) 10 (b) 20 (c) 15 (d) 5
Answer: (c)
Solution: Simply subtracting 30 and 25 from 60 (giving 5) is the trap, because the 10 who play both get subtracted twice. Students playing at least one game = 30 + 25 − 10 = 45, so neither = 60 − 45 = 15.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Mathematics (Sets)

9. In a survey, 55% of people like tea, 45% like coffee, and 20% like both. What percentage of people like neither tea nor coffee?
(a) 20% (b) 15% (c) 25% (d) 30%
Answer: (a)
Solution: Adding 55 and 45 to get 100 and concluding 0% is the trap, since the 20% overlap is counted twice. Union = 55 + 45 − 20 = 80, so neither = 100 − 80 = 20%.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Mathematics (Sets)

10. In a group of 100 people, 60 speak Hindi, 40 speak English, and 25 speak both languages. How many people speak exactly one of the two languages?
(a) 35 (b) 75 (c) 50 (d) 15
Answer: (c)
Solution: Taking the union 60 + 40 − 25 = 75 is the trap, because the union includes those who speak both. Exactly one language = (60 − 25) + (40 − 25) = 35 + 15 = 50, so the answer is 50.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Mathematics (Sets)

11. Statements: All flowers are plants. Some plants are trees. Conclusions: I. Some trees are flowers. II. Some plants are flowers.
(a) Only conclusion I follows (b) Only conclusion II follows (c) Both conclusions I and II follow (d) Neither conclusion follows
Answer: (b)
Solution: Conclusion I is the trap, since trees and flowers may lie in completely different parts of the plants circle. Since all flowers are plants, some plants are definitely flowers, so only conclusion II follows.

Answer: (d)
Solution: Conclusion I is the trap, because books and pencils may remain entirely separate. The pencils that are pens can never be books, so some pencils are definitely not books and only conclusion II follows.

18. Which option fits the words: Car, Vehicle, Petrol?
(a) One large circle (Vehicle) containing Car, and Petrol as a completely separate circle (b) Three mutually overlapping circles (c) One large circle containing two overlapping circles (d) Two separate circles, third inside one of them
Answer: (a)

19. In a Venn diagram with three intersecting circles A, B, and C, how many distinct bounded regions are formed?
(a) 7 (b) 8 (c) 6 (d) 9
Answer: (a)
Solution: Counting 8 is the trap, because the eighth region is the unbounded area outside all circles. Three intersecting circles form 3 single-only, 3 pairwise-only, and 1 triple region, so the answer is 7.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Mathematics (Sets)

20. If 'only A' = 5, 'only B' = 7, 'only C' = 9, 'A and B only' = 3, 'A and C only' = 2, 'B and C only' = 4, and 'all three' = 1, how many total elements are in set A?
(a) 9 (b) 10 (c) 12 (d) 11
Answer: (d)
Solution: Adding only A with the triple region (giving 6) or adding all seven regions is the trap. Set A = only A + A∩B only + A∩C only + all three = 5 + 3 + 2 + 1 = 11, so the answer is 11.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Mathematics (Sets)
