1. Two Basic Types of Sitting Arrangement

Every elementary sitting arrangement problem is either Linear (Row) or Circular. Recognizing the layout and orientation is the key to solving.

Type | Layout | Direction & End Points
Linear (Row) | A straight line (North-facing, South-facing, or general Left-to-Right) | Definite Left and Right; identifying people at the two ends (extremities) is crucial
Circular | Around a round table (Facing the centre or Facing outward) | Neighbours' Left and Right are determined clockwise/anticlockwise; there are no end points

2. The Golden Rule of Direction

To eliminate direction confusion forever, remember one core rule: "Look at the world through the eyes of the person sitting in that seat."

If all people face the same direction (e.g. all facing North in a row):
Your Left = Their Left, Your Right = Their Right. This is the simplest case.

If all people face the centre (Circular, inside facing):
Clockwise movement is to their Left, and Anticlockwise movement is to their Right.
Mnemonic: "Clock-L" — Clockwise is Left for centre-facing arrangements.

If all people face outward (Circular, outside facing):
The rule reverses completely. Clockwise movement is now to their Right, and Anticlockwise movement is to their Left.
Trick: Whenever you see "facing outward", mentally stand in their spot facing away from the table to determine their left and right.

3. The 4-Step Magic Strategy for Solving

Step 1: Sort the Clues
- Definite clues directly specify positions, such as "Aman is at the third position from the left."
- Relative clues specify relationships, such as "Aman is to the left of Beena" or "There are two persons between Aman and Beena."
- Always place definite clues into your diagram first.

Step 2: Draw the Skeleton
- For Linear: Draw empty boxes in a line, e.g., [ ] [ ] [ ] [ ] [ ]
- For Circular: Draw a circle with tick marks corresponding to the number of seats.

Step 3: Apply Clues Incrementally
- Start with the definite clues to anchor the arrangement.
- Next, test relative clues. If two valid possibilities arise, draw parallel branches rather than erasing your work.

Step 4: Verify and Check for Traps
- Test each condition one by one against your final diagram.
- Eliminate any branch that contradicts any condition. The single remaining valid branch is your correct arrangement.

4. The 3 Most Powerful Clues in Linear Arrangements

1. "A is immediately left/right of B" → A and B must sit together as a fixed block [AB] or [BA]. Treat them as a single unit.
2. "There are two persons between A and B" → Gap = 2, i.e., [A _ _ B] or [B _ _ A]. Consider both orientations unless one is ruled out.
3. "A sits at one of the extreme ends" → Fix A immediately at the first or last position, and build the remaining seats around A.

Example:
5 friends sit in a row facing North:
- B sits immediately to the right of C.
- Exactly one person sits between A and E.
- D does not sit at either extreme end.
Solution: C and B form the block [CB]. D must be in one of the three middle seats (2, 3, or 4). Testing [CB] and A-gap-E placements quickly leaves the unique valid arrangement: A - C - B - D - E (or E - C - B - D - A).

5. Circular Arrangements: Thinking in 360 Degrees

A circle has no beginning and no end. Therefore, the most effective strategy is to anchor (fix) one person arbitrarily at the 6 o'clock position facing North/centre.

- If "A sits second to the left of B" (facing centre): Place B at the bottom (facing North), so B's left is to the clockwise direction. Skip one seat and place A.
- "B sits opposite A" — This is only possible when the total number of persons is even (6, 8, etc.).

🧠 Active Recall: In a 6-person circular table facing the centre, who sits directly opposite person A? (Hint: Skip exactly 2 persons on either side).

6. When Orientations Vary: Mixed Facing Directions

Examiners often create challenging puzzles where some persons face North and others face South in a row, or some face inward and others outward in a circle.
- Simplest technique: Draw a small arrow (↑ or ↓) above each person's label.
- Determine Left and Right strictly based on that individual's arrow.
- Example: If A faces North (↑) and B faces South (↓), A's right hand points in the same direction as B's left hand.

Mnemonic:
"Face-to-face reverses left and right; back-to-back keeps the compass bright."

⚠️ Watch Out: Common Examiner Traps

1. Ambiguity in Multiple Valid Scenarios
Examiners craft options that only hold true in one of two possible cases. If "Who sits at the right end?" could be either P or Q depending on the case, do not jump to conclusions without verifying whether the question asks "who could sit" vs "who definitely sits".

2. Intimidating Vocabulary
Phrases like "not adjacent to", "alternate positions", and "diametrically opposite" are merely formal ways of expressing "not next to", "leaving one gap", and "straight across the table". Translate them immediately into plain English.

3. Dual-Condition Clues
"A is a neighbour of B and also sits second to the left of C." Here, two independent conditions apply to A. Place the more restrictive clue first, then test whether the second condition holds. Do not overlook the second part of a compound clue.
