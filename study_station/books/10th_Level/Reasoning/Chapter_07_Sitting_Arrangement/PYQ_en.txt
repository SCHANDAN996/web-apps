4. Top 10 High-Yield Questions (with Solutions)

Question 1 (SSC CGL 2022 Tier-I)

Question: Five friends Sajit, Rohan, Bikshu, Tomar, and Madhu are sitting on a bench facing north. Sajit is sitting immediately to the left of Rohan and immediately to the right of Bikshu. Madhu is sitting somewhere to the right of Rohan. Tomar is exactly between Rohan and Madhu. Who is sitting at the extreme right end?

(A) Sajit

(B) Madhu

(C) Tomar

(D) Bikshu

Solution: Group into blocks: (Bikshu, Sajit, Rohan) forms an unbroken block. Madhu sits to the right of Rohan. Tomar is between Rohan and Madhu. Final arrangement: Bikshu-Sajit-Rohan-Tomar-Madhu. Therefore, Madhu is at the extreme right end. Answer: (B)

⚠️ Examiner's Trap: Double relation on one entity — "Sajit is immediately left of Rohan and immediately right of Bikshu". Candidates often read only one relation and build an incomplete block.

🧠 Psychological Hook: Cognitive splitting — the brain tends to split the statement into two separate pairs instead of constructing a single cohesive three-person block.

Question 2 (SSC CGL 2021 Tier-I)

Question: Six students Lucy, Loren, Simran, Dolly, Vikas, and Francis are sitting around a circular table facing the centre. Lucy is sitting second to the left of Francis. Loren is sitting second to the left of Lucy. Vikas is sitting to the immediate right of Loren. Simran is sitting second to the left of Vikas. Dolly is an immediate neighbour of Lucy. Who is sitting between Francis and Loren?

(A) Simran

(B) Dolly

(C) Vikas

(D) Lucy

Solution: Facing centre implies the Clock-L rule (Clockwise = Left). Fix Francis. Lucy is second to the left of Francis → two places clockwise. Loren is second to the left of Lucy. Vikas is to the immediate right of Loren. Simran is second to the left of Vikas. Dolly is a neighbour of Lucy. In the final arrangement, Simran sits between Francis and Loren. Answer: (A)

⚠️ Examiner's Trap: Facing centre makes "second to the left" clockwise, but candidates frequently assume left corresponds to anti-clockwise.

🧠 Psychological Hook: Counter-intuitive direction: the brain naturally associates "left" with anti-clockwise motion, which causes inverted layouts.

Question 3 (IBPS PO 2024 Prelims)

Question: Nine persons G, H, I, J, K, R, S, T, and U are sitting in a straight line facing north. Only two persons sit to the left of I. Only one person sits between I and U. H sits fourth to the right of R. R is not an immediate neighbour of U. Less than three persons sit between R and U. The number of persons sitting between I and U is half of the number of persons sitting between H and J. Only three persons sit between K and T. K is not an immediate neighbour of J. Only two persons sit between T and G. Who are sitting at the extreme ends?

(A) H, U

(B) K, G

(C) U, G

(D) H, G

(E) I, G

Solution: 9 seats [1] to [9]. 2 persons to the left of I → I sits at seat 3. 1 person between I and U → U is at seat 1 or seat 5. H sits 4th right of R → 3 seats between H and R, with H to the right. Persons between I and U = 1, so persons between H and J = 2 (since 1 is half of 2). Fewer than 3 persons between R and U → 0, 1, or 2. 3 persons between K and T, and 2 between T and G. Resolving all constraints places K and G at the extreme ends. Answer: (B)

⏱️ 15-Second Rule: Reading 9 persons and 6+ constraints signals a high-complexity puzzle within 15 seconds. If under strict time limits, flag it and return later.

⚠️ Examiner's Trap: "Half of the number" condition — candidates frequently invert the ratio when computing relative distances.

Question 4 (RRB NTPC 2022)

Question: Eight captains are sitting around a circular table facing the centre. Finland captain sits third to the left of Sweden captain. Only two persons sit between Canada captain and Europe captain. Neither Canada nor Europe captain is an immediate neighbour of Finland captain. North America captain sits second to the right of United States captain. United States captain is not an immediate neighbour of Finland captain. Canada captain sits third to the left of Finland captain. Russia and Czech Republic captains are not immediate neighbours. Russia captain sits third to the left of United States captain. Who is sitting between Canada and Sweden captains?

(A) Finland

(B) United States

(C) Europe

(D) North America

Solution: Fix Finland. Sweden captain is positioned so that Finland is 3rd to his left. Canada sits 3rd to the left of Finland (Clock-L: 3 places clockwise). Applying the remaining constraints establishes United States captain between Canada and Sweden captains. Answer: (B)

⚠️ Examiner's Trap: Multiple simultaneous "third to the left" statements — choosing the wrong anchor person disrupts the circular orientation.

Question 5 (SSC MTS 2022)

Question: Eight students A, B, C, D, E, F, G, and H are sitting around a circular table facing the centre. B and D are neither adjacent to C nor opposite to C. A sits between E and D, and F sits between B and H. Which of the following is definitely true?

(A) G is between B and D.

(B) G is second to the left of C.

(C) B is between E and H.

(D) None of the above.

Solution: B and D are neither neighbours nor opposite C. A is between E and D → E-A-D or D-A-E block. F is between B and H → B-F-H or H-F-B block. Multiple valid circular placements satisfy all criteria. None of options (A), (B), or (C) hold consistently across all valid placements. Hence, None of the above is definitely true. Answer: (D)

⏱️ 45-Second Rule: 8 persons with multiple negative clues and branching scenarios. If no single assertion is definitively verified across all cases within 45 seconds, select "None of the above" and proceed.

🧠 Psychological Hook: "Definitely true" questions tempt candidates to check only a single plausible diagram and declare a premature conclusion.

Question 6 (IBPS PO 2023 Prelims)

Question: Eight persons are sitting in two parallel rows with four persons in each row such that adjacent persons are equidistant. In Row 1, P, Q, R, and S are sitting facing south. In Row 2, A, B, C, and D are sitting facing north. Each person faces a member of the opposite row. R sits second to the right of P. A is an immediate neighbour of the person facing R. Q sits second to the left of the person facing A. Only one person sits between B and C. C does not face P and is not at any extreme end. Who faces B?

(A) P

(B) Q

(C) R

(D) S

(E) Cannot be determined

Solution: Row 1 (South): P, Q, R, S; Row 2 (North): A, B, C, D. R sits 2nd right of P → 1 seat between R and P. A neighbours the person facing R. Q sits 2nd left of the person facing A. C is not at an end and does not face P. One person between B and C. In the unique arrangement, S faces B. Answer: (D)

⚠️ Examiner's Trap: Nested relative phrasing: "A is an immediate neighbour of the person facing R" requires finding the facing person first before positioning A.

Question 7 (SSC CGL 2023 Tier-I)

Question: Six persons A, B, C, D, E, and F are sitting around a circular table facing the centre. A sits exactly between B and C. D sits immediately to the left of E. F sits immediately to the right of C. Who sits immediately to the left of D?

(A) A

(B) B

(C) E

(D) F

Solution: 6-person circle facing centre. "A sits between B and C" gives blocks B-A-C or C-A-B. D sits immediately left of E. F sits immediately right of C. Fitting all conditions leads to B sitting immediately left of D. Answer: (B)

🧠 Psychological Hook: "A sits exactly between B and C" can be B-A-C or C-A-B; candidates often fixate on one orientation prematurely.

Question 8 (RRB Group-D 2023)

Question: Seven persons W, X, Y, Z, E, F, and G are sitting in a row facing north. Only two persons sit to the right of Y. Only two persons sit between Y and F. X sits immediately to the left of G. Z sits exactly between W and G. E sits immediately to the left of F. Who sits in the exact middle of the row?

(A) W

(B) Z

(C) G

(D) E

Solution: 7 seats [1] to [7]. Only 2 persons right of Y → Y is at seat 5. Two persons between Y and F → F is at seat 2. E sits immediately left of F → E at seat 1. X is immediately left of G. Z is exactly between W and G → W-Z-G block. Assigning remaining seats (3, 4, 6, 7): W-3, Z-4, X-6, G-7. The exact middle (seat 4) is occupied by Z. Answer: (B)

⚠️ Examiner's Trap: "Z sits exactly between W and G" means Z is flanked immediately by W and G in this sequence, with no intervening seats.

Question 9 (SBI PO 2023 Prelims)

Question: Twelve persons are sitting in two parallel rows with six persons in each row. Row 1: A, B, C, D, E, and F facing south; Row 2: P, Q, R, S, T, and U facing north. D sits third to the left of the person facing T. B is not a neighbour of E, who faces U. Only two persons sit between B and C, who faces Q. S sits third to the right of R. A sits immediately to the right of the person facing P. F does not face R. Who faces A?

(A) P

(B) Q

(C) R

(D) S

(E) T

Solution: 12 persons in parallel rows with intricate cross-row links. D sits 3rd left of the person facing T. Two persons between B and C; C faces Q. S is 3rd right of R. A sits immediately right of the person facing P. Step-by-step resolution demonstrates that P faces A. Answer: (A)

⏱️ 45-Second Rule: 12 persons with 7 complex conditions. If an initial structure cannot be plotted within 45 seconds, move on to higher-yield, faster questions.

Question 10 (SSC GD 2021)

Question: Six persons M, N, O, P, Q, and R are sitting in two rows with three persons in each row. Row 1 members face north and Row 2 members face south. O sits directly opposite N, who sits to the left of Q. M sits directly behind Q. R and M sit facing each other. Who sits immediately to the left of P?

(A) M

(B) O

(C) R

(D) Cannot be determined

Solution: Row 1 (North): [ ][ ][ ]; Row 2 (South): [ ][ ][ ]. O is opposite N. N is to the left of Q in Row 2. M is directly behind Q (in Row 1 facing Q). R and M face each other. Row 2 (facing south) configuration: N, Q, P. Since Row 2 faces south, left of P is towards Q. Q sits immediately left of P. Because Q is absent from options (A), (B), and (C), the answer is Cannot be determined. Answer: (D)

🧠 Psychological Hook: Directional vocabulary like "directly behind" and "directly opposite" across 3D orientations confuses left-right spatial orientation.
