🧒 Feynman Technique: The Hardest Concept in Sitting Arrangement Explained to a 12-Year-Old

Imagine you are at a birthday party. Five of your friends — Aarav, Beena, Chirag, Diya, and Ehan — are standing around a circular table. Some are facing inward towards the birthday cake, while others are looking outward towards the room. Your mom tells you, "Aarav's right hand is resting on Diya's shoulder." But Diya is facing outward and Aarav is facing inward. Now think — which side is "right" for Aarav, and which side is "right" for Diya? That is the entire secret of sitting arrangements.

The Hardest Concept:
When people face different directions, "Left" and "Right" become personal, relative directions for each individual. There is no single universal "left" or "right" — everyone sees the world from their own perspective.

Story:
Richard Feynman once explained relativity to children like this: "Hold your hands out in front of you. Your left hand is always on the side of your heart. Now if you turn around and stand upside down, your heart hand is pointing in the opposite direction relative to the room." In sitting arrangements, every person carries their own personal compass.

Unforgettable Trick: "Put Yourself in Their Shoes"
Whenever a problem states "X sits to the left of Y" and Y faces inward or outward:
1. Draw a small circle representing Y, with an arrow pointing in the direction Y is facing.
2. Look through Y's eyes — identify which way is left and which way is right for Y. Place X there.
3. For a round table: If facing the centre, clockwise is their Left (Clock-L) and anticlockwise is their Right. If facing outward, anticlockwise is their Left and clockwise is their Right.
