📦 Mnemonic Boxes: Sitting Arrangement (Basic)

📦 Clock‑L for Centre‑Facing Circles
When everyone faces the centre, Clockwise is their Left.
Just chant: “Centre mein Clock, Left ko Lock!”
(Translation: In the centre, the clock locks onto left.)

📦 Anti‑Clock‑R for Outside‑Facing Circles
When everyone faces outward, the rule flips: Anticlockwise becomes their Left, so Right is Clockwise.
Remember: “Baahar baithe, toh ghadi ulṭi – Right ab Clockwise!”
(Outside, the clock is reversed – Right is now Clockwise.)

📦 Arrow Trick for Mixed Directions
Draw a tiny arrow (↑ or ↓) above every person’s head. That arrow is their compass.
“Jahan arrow, wahan left‑right clear ho!”
(Where there’s an arrow, left‑right becomes clear.)

📦 Fix One to Rule the Circle
In a circular puzzle, place any one person at a random seat to anchor the whole diagram.
“Ek ko fix karo, baaki circus apne aap set ho jayega!”
(Fix one, and the rest of the circus will arrange itself.)

📦 Certain First, Relative Later
Place the “definite” clues first (extreme ends, exact positions). Only then apply the “relative” clues (left/right/between).
“Pehle pakka, phir rishtedaar – kheer pehle, phir saar!”
(First the certain, then the relatives – like eating the solid part before the syrup.)

📦 Face‑to‑Face, Left‑Right Reverse
When two people face each other, their left‑right becomes opposite.
“Aamne‑saamne, left‑right badle – jaise mirror ho!”
(Face‑to‑face, left‑right swaps – just like a mirror!)

📦 Back‑to‑Back, Same Side Track
When two people have their backs to each other, their left‑right stays the same.
“Peeṭh‑peeṭh, left‑right same – dono ki ek hi lane!”
(Back‑to‑back, left‑right same – both are in the same lane.)

📦 Glued Pairs – Stick Like Fevicol
If “A is immediate left of B”, treat them as a single block that moves together.
“Jodi ko Fevicol se chipkao – sath rakhoge toh alag nahi honge!”
(Glue the pair with Fevicol – if you keep them together, they won’t separate.)

📦 Mind the Gap – London Tube Style
For clues like “two persons between A and B”, count the empty seats as gaps.
“Gap ko gino, jaise Metro mein ‘Mind the Gap’ bolte hain!”
(Count the gaps, just like they say “Mind the Gap” in the Metro.)

📦 Branching Tree for Possibilities
When two arrangements are possible, keep both branches alive and test further conditions.
“Do raaste? Ped ki tarah daali banao – jo sookhe, kaat do!”
(Two paths? Branch out like a tree – cut the one that withers.)

📦 Double Relationship = Double Trouble
When a single person has two relations (e.g., “A is both immediate right of B and second left of C”), solve carefully, or skip if it eats time.
“Do rishte ek saath? Double dhamaka – ya toh solve karo, ya chhodo!”
(Double relations? Double blast – either solve it or leave it!)

📦 15‑Second Sniff Test
If within 15 seconds you cannot identify the type (row/circle) and the strongest clue, skip the question.
“15 second mein pattern nahi pakda? Aage bhaago!”
(If you didn’t catch the pattern in 15 seconds, run ahead!)

📦 45‑Second Gateway
If you have started but the diagram is not finalised within 45 seconds, skip immediately.
“45 second mein pura circle nahi bana? Pen chhodo, sawaal chhodo!”
(If the full circle isn’t formed in 45 seconds, drop the pen and the question.)

📦 Examiner’s Trap – The Sweetshop Trick
Examiners use heavy words like “सन्निकट” (adjacent) or “प्रतिवेशी” (opposite direction) just to confuse you. Translate them into simple words instantly.
“Bade shabd = mithai ka lalach – andar sirf simple condition hai!”
(Big words = sweet temptation – inside, there’s only a simple condition.)

🪄 Skip Strategy Tips

The 15‑Second Rule: Glance at the puzzle. If you can’t figure out whether it’s a row or circle and spot the anchor clue within 15 seconds, mark it and move to the next. Your subconscious will chew on it while you score easy marks elsewhere.

The 45‑Second Limit: Once you start drawing, if the arrangement isn’t locking into a single possibility after 45 seconds, abandon ship. Stubbornness here is the enemy of a good rank.

Double‑Relation Detector: If a person is linked to two others simultaneously (e.g., “A is B’s immediate left and also C’s second right”), treat it as a red flag. Solve only if you see a clear path; otherwise, skip and return if time permits. These are designed to be time‑sinks.
