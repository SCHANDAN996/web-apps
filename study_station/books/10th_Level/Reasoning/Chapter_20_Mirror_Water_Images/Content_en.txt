When reflections twist your mind – see them clearly, solve them fast.

🔮 The Mirror Magic – A Powerful Hook

Imagine you are standing in front of a huge, silent mirror. You raise your right hand. The you in the mirror raises his left hand. You tilt your head; he tilts his too, but somehow everything is reversed. Mirrors don’t lie – they just swap left and right. And water? Water flips your world upside down, like a reflection in a calm lake, where the sky appears below your feet. These simple ideas can become the fastest scoring section in non-verbal reasoning if you train your brain to see the flip. Yet, every year, clever examiners hide tiny changes that make thousands of students lose easy marks. Not you. Let’s turn this mirror into your magic.

🧩 Why Do Students Struggle With Mirror & Water Images?

Most learners treat this topic as "just guess the opposite." That’s a trap. Without a solid rule, the mind starts creating its own logic – and the examiner’s close options break that logic. The struggle comes from:

Mixing vertical mirror (left–right reversal) with horizontal mirror/water (top–bottom reversal).

Forgetting that some letters look exactly the same in a mirror but change completely in water (M ↔ W).

Relying on visual imagination instead of a quick, reliable algorithm.

The good news? Once you internalise three core rules, you will never guess again. And that’s what we’ll do right now, chunk by chunk.

📐 Small Chunk 1 – The Two Types of Mirrors

1. Vertical Mirror (Mirror placed on the right or left side)

Flips: LEFT ↔ RIGHT

Does NOT flip: TOP ↔ BOTTOM

Example: An arrow pointing right → Mirror image points left. Arrow pointing up → still points up.

2. Horizontal Mirror (Mirror placed above or below the object)

Flips: TOP ↔ BOTTOM

Does NOT flip: LEFT ↔ RIGHT

This is exactly like a Water Image (calm water acts as a horizontal mirror). So the rule for horizontal mirror = rule for water image.

🧠 Active Recall Prompt: Close your eyes. If a mirror is placed on the right of the word "AB", what changes? Left–right or top–bottom? (Answer: left–right. A and B swap their left-right orientation.)

📝 Small Chunk 2 – Letter and Digit Magic

Vertical Mirror (Left–Right reversal)
Letters that remain the same: A, H, I, M, O, T, U, V, W, X, Y.
Letters that change dramatically: B → ᗺ, C → Ↄ, D → ᗡ, E → Ǝ, etc.
Digits: 0, 1, 8 stay same; 2,3,4,5,6,7,9 change.

Water Image (Top–Bottom reversal)
Letters that remain same: C, E, H, I, O, S, X, Z (many horizontal lines look similar when flipped). Surprise: M ↔ W! M in water becomes W, and W becomes M.
Digits: 0, 1, 3, 8 stay recognisable? Actually, 3 flips to Ɛ, 8 stays 8. The super trap: 6 and 9. In water, 6 ↔ 9. In vertical mirror, 6 becomes something else (reverse-6), not 9.

🧠 Active Recall Prompt: What is the water image of "MOM"? (Answer: WOW) What is the vertical mirror image of "MOM"? (Answer: MOM, because M, O, M are vertically symmetric.) Notice how one rule gives identical image, the other changes the word completely!

⏰ Small Chunk 3 – Clock Images Formula

Vertical Mirror Clock Image
Formula: *Mirror time = 12:00 – Actual time*
(If minutes aren’t 00, use 11:60 – Actual time)
Example: Actual 4:25 → 11:60 – 4:25 = 7:35.

Water Image (Horizontal Mirror) Clock Image
Formula: *Water image time = 18:30 – Actual time*
(If result > 12:00, use 12-hour format; if negative, add 12 hours.)
Example: Actual 3:15 → 18:30 – 3:15 = 15:15 = 3:15 PM, so clock shows 3:15. Many times it won’t match – practice with 7:40 → 18:30 – 7:40 = 10:50.

🧠 Active Recall Prompt: Without looking, what is the vertical mirror time for 9:20? (11:60 – 9:20 = 2:40) What about water image of 2:45? (18:30 – 2:45 = 15:45 = 3:45)

🖼️ Small Chunk 4 – Geometrical Figures

When an irregular shape is given, instead of imagining the whole thing, use the “dot method”:

For vertical mirror: For every important point, measure its horizontal distance from the mirror line and put a point on the opposite side at the same distance, keeping vertical position unchanged.

For water image: Keep horizontal distance same, flip vertical position.

Practice by drawing a simple L-shape. Mirror on right of it: L becomes a backward L. Water below it: L flips upside down, looks like a 7 maybe.

🧠 Active Recall Prompt: Draw a triangle pointing up. Mirror on right side. Will the triangle still point up? (Yes, left–right reversal doesn’t change vertical orientation. The triangle may shift left-right but still points up.)

🧠 Mnemonic Trick to Never Confuse Mirror vs Water

“Mirror says: Left-Right, Water says: Up-Down.”
Rhyme: Mirror paltay left-right, Water ultay top-bottom site!

Or, remember the letter “M” of Mirror also stands for Midline vertical flip. “W” of Water stands for World upside down. So M ↔ W is the iconic trap – Mirror keeps M as M, Water turns M into W.

⚠️ Caution: Examiner’s Trap

स्क्रीन पर अस्पष्ट आकृतियाँ: On screen, subtle curves appear symmetrical. The examiner blurs the difference so that your mind fills in the wrong direction. Always verify with the actual letter chart.

एक जैसी दिखने वाली आकृतियों में अंतर छिपाना: Two options look almost identical – the only difference is a small notch or arrow direction. Don’t rush. Apply the dot method even if the figure looks “obviously” correct.

घुमाव/प्रतिबिंब में थोड़ा बदलाव: A rotated version of the mirror image is given as an option. Many students think a rotation is the same as a reflection. Remember, reflection is a flip, not a turn. Watch out for options that are rotated by 90° or 180°—they are almost always wrong unless specifically asked.
