📊 PYQ Pattern Analysis (Mirror & Water Images)

1. How Exams Ask Questions from This Chapter

Mirror and Water Image questions are a regular feature of the non-verbal reasoning section in SSC (CGL, CHSL, GD, MTS, CPO), Railway (RRB NTPC, Group D, ALP) and State-level police/administrative exams. Banking exams (IBPS, SBI) ask them occasionally. Typically, one or two questions from this chapter appear in a paper — a small count, but these are quick, high-accuracy marks if the rules are clear.

The recurring question types are:

- **Mirror image of letters, words and numbers** — a vertical mirror is shown on the left or right, and the candidate must pick the laterally inverted (left ↔ right flipped) option.
- **Water image of letters, words and figures** — the figure is flipped vertically (top ↔ bottom), while the left-to-right order stays unchanged.
- **Mirror image of a clock** — solved with the 11:60 (or 23:60) subtraction rule.
- **Water image of a clock** — solved with the 18:30 (or 17:90) subtraction rule.
- **Symmetry-based counting** — e.g., how many capital letters look the same in a mirror or in water.
- **Mirror placed horizontally** — a trap type where a horizontal mirror behaves exactly like a water surface.

2. Examiner's Common Traps

- **Rotation vs reflection:** Options that are merely rotated versions of the figure, not true reflections.
- **Sequence reversal in water images:** In a water image the left-to-right order does NOT change; only the top-bottom flip happens. Options that reverse the order are mirror traps.
- **Mirror position confusion:** A mirror on the right flips left-right just like a mirror on the left (the image appears behind the mirror); a mirror below the figure produces a water-type image.
- **Clock formula mix-up:** Using 11:60 for water images or 18:30 for mirror images. Remember: Mirror → 11:60, Water → 18:30.
- **Near-identical letters:** Letters like b/d, p/q, and digits like 6/9 are used to test careful observation.

3. High-Yield Practice Questions with Solutions

Question 1:
If a clock shows 11:50, what time will appear in its mirror image?
Solution:
Formula: Mirror Time = 11:60 − Actual Time
Calculation: 11:60 − 11:50 = 00:10, which corresponds to 12:10.
Examiner's Trap: Students often misread '00:10' as 1:10 or 12:00. Subtracting from 11:60 gives 00:10, i.e., 12:10.
Source: PYQ-style

Question 2:
Find the correct water image of the word "QUALITY".
Solution:
In a water image, inversion is strictly vertical (top ↔ bottom), while the left-to-right order of letters remains unchanged. Each letter is flipped vertically, reading from left to right: Q-U-A-L-I-T-Y.
Examiner's Trap: Students often laterally reverse the whole word as in a mirror image. In water reflection, the first letter stays on the left, but upside down.
Source: PYQ-style

Question 3:
Find the mirror image of a figure having an arrow pointing left and a square on the right, when the mirror is placed on the right.
Solution:
When a vertical mirror is on the right, left and right swap: the arrow moves to the right side and points right, while the square moves to the left.
Examiner's Trap: Watch for options where positions are swapped but the arrow's pointing direction is mistakenly left unchanged.
Source: PYQ-style

Question 4:
How many capital letters of the English alphabet have identical water images?
Solution:
Letters with horizontal symmetry remain identical in water reflection: B, C, D, E, H, I, K, O, X (total 9 letters).
Examiner's Trap: Do not confuse mirror symmetry (A, H, I, M, O, T, U, V, W, X, Y — 11 letters) with water symmetry. Letters like A, M, W change completely in water reflection.
Source: PYQ-style

Question 5:
Find the water image of the time 8:20 on a clock.
Solution:
Formula: Water Time = 18:30 − Actual Time
Calculation: 18:30 − 8:20 = 10:10.
Examiner's Trap: If minutes exceed 30, borrow 60 minutes and subtract from 17:90. For minutes up to 30, subtract directly from 18:30.
Source: PYQ-style

Question 6:
If the clock displays 4:45, what is its water image?
Solution:
Since the minutes (45) exceed 30, use 17:90:
17:90 − 4:45 = 13:45, which converts to 1:45.
Examiner's Trap: Do not mix up the formulas — using the mirror formula (11:60) here would give a wrong answer. For water images always subtract from 18:30 (or 17:90 when minutes > 30).
Source: PYQ-style

Question 7:
Which digits from 0 to 9 have identical mirror and water images?
Solution:
The digits 0 and 8 possess both vertical and horizontal symmetry, so they remain unchanged in both mirror and water reflections.
Examiner's Trap: Digit 1 is often drawn with an asymmetric serif; the standard symmetrical digits are 0 and 8.
Source: PYQ-style

Question 8:
Find the mirror image of "BRIDGE" when the mirror is placed vertically to its right.
Solution:
The word is read in reverse order from right to left: E-G-D-I-R-B, with each individual letter laterally inverted.
Examiner's Trap: A common mistake is flipping each letter in place without reversing the letter sequence.
Source: PYQ-style

Question 9:
What is the image of a shape when the mirror is placed horizontally below it?
Solution:
A mirror placed horizontally below an object produces an exact water image (vertical flip: top becomes bottom, left-right unchanged).
Examiner's Trap: A mirror placed horizontally is NOT a lateral (left-right) inversion; it acts identically to a water reflection surface.
Source: PYQ-style

Question 10:
Find the water image of "DISC8".
Solution:
Letters and digits are inverted vertically: D, I, C and 8 have horizontal symmetry and look identical; S flips vertically (its top and bottom curves invert). The sequence stays left to right.
Examiner's Trap: Reversing the order of characters is a mirror trap; in water reflection the sequence stays left-to-right.
Source: PYQ-style
