🃏 Flashcards – Mirror & Water Images

Front: Ever waved your right hand at a mirror and seen your reflection wave its left? What’s the secret rule?
Back: A standing (vertical) mirror flips left ↔ right and keeps top ↔ bottom unchanged.

Front: You look into a still lake and see your head at the bottom. What kind of flip does water perform?
Back: Water acts as a horizontal mirror, flipping top ↔ bottom while keeping left ↔ right the same.

Front: Why does the letter “M” look like itself in a mirror but turn into “W” in water?
Back: M is left–right symmetric, so a vertical mirror leaves it unchanged. Water flips top–bottom, turning M upside down into W.

Front: What is the water image of the word “MOM”?
Back: WOW. Each M flips to W, and O stays almost the same because O is top–bottom symmetric.

Front: What’s the quick clock formula for a mirror image (vertical mirror)?
Back: Mirror time = 12:00 – actual time. If minutes aren’t 00, use 11:60 – actual time.

Front: How do you find the water image of a clock showing 7:40?
Back: Water time = 18:30 – actual time → 18:30 – 7:40 = 10:50. The water reflection shows 10:50.

Front: Which capital letters stay exactly the same in a vertical mirror?
Back: A, H, I, M, O, T, U, V, W, X, Y. They are vertically symmetrical.

Front: True or False: In a mirror, 6 becomes 9, just like in water.
Back: False. In a vertical mirror, 6 flips to something like ᔕ (reversed 6), not 9. In water, 6 ↔ 9 because of top–bottom flip.

Front: What’s the body trick that helps you never forget the difference between mirror and water flips?
Back: Cross your hands as if shaking your opposite hand → mirror flips left–right. Turn both hands upside down and clap → water flips top–bottom.

Front: Give an easy Hindi mnemonic to remember mirror vs water flip.
Back: “Mirror paltay left-right, Water ultay top-bottom site!” Or: खड़ा शीशा बाएँ-दाएँ, लेटा पानी ऊपर-नीचे।

Front: You’re solving a mirror image question with a complex shape. What’s the no-guess method?
Back: Use the dot method: for each key point, measure horizontal distance from the mirror line and mark it on the opposite side at the same distance; keep vertical position unchanged. For water, keep horizontal same, flip vertical.

Front: What’s a common examiner trap in mirror/water questions involving letters?
Back: Giving options where M and W are swapped incorrectly. Students often forget that M stays M in a mirror but becomes W in water. Always apply the specific flip rule, not intuition.

Front: In water image questions, many students unknowingly flip left–right as well. Why is that a trap?
Back: Because the resulting image is diagonally flipped, which matches neither a pure mirror nor a pure water image – and examiners place such a wrong option to catch the confused.

Front: How do examiners use “almost identical” options to trick you in non-verbal mirror/water questions?
Back: Two options look nearly the same except for a tiny detail – a small arrow direction, a notch, or a curve. The trap is to make you overlook it. Always check every corner using the dot method.

Front: A clock shows 12:00. What does its vertical mirror image show?
Back: 12:00 – 12:00 = 0:00, which on a 12-hour clock is 12:00. The hands overlap at 12, so the image looks the same.

Front: What happens to the digit “8” in both mirror and water images?
Back: 8 is symmetric both vertically and horizontally, so it remains 8 in mirror and water images alike.

Front: You’re doing a screen-based exam and the reflection looks symmetrical but slightly blurry. What should you do?
Back: Don’t guess. Recall your standard letter/number mirror chart. Screen blur is an examiner trap to make you misjudge the curve direction.

Front: Why is it important to check whether the mirror is placed vertically or horizontally before answering?
Back: Because a vertical mirror flips left–right, while a horizontal mirror (like water) flips top–bottom. Using the wrong flip gives a completely wrong image.
