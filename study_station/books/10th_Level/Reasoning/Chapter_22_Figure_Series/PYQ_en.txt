📊 PYQ Analysis: Figure Series/Classification — Foundation Level

This analysis is based on the pattern of reasoning questions asked in major competitive exams (SSC CGL, CHSL, CPO, MTS, GD, Railway NTPC/Group D, and State Police Exams). It shows not only what is asked, but also how the examiner expects you to think under pressure.

1. How Often This Topic Appears

Figure Series and Figure Classification questions appear in almost every shift of SSC and Railway exams. Typically:

- SSC CGL Tier-I: a few questions per paper, often the most difficult ones in the reasoning section.
- SSC CHSL / CPO: 1–3 questions, usually single-rule patterns.
- RRB NTPC / Group D: 1–2 questions, mostly easy, single-rule patterns.
- SSC MTS / GD: 1–2 straightforward questions — highly scoring with practice.

Because the topic appears so reliably, it is a dependable scoring area. The exact count varies from shift to shift, so do not rely on memorised numbers — rely on mastering the pattern types below.

2. Sub-Topic Weightage (Pattern Analysis, in Words)

Based on the recurring pattern types seen across these exams:

- **Movement / Shifting (Position Change):** The most frequently asked type. An element (dot, circle, arrow) moves corner-to-corner or side-to-side. Often combined with a second rule.
- **Rotation (Clockwise/Anticlockwise):** Consistently asked. Watch for 45° and 135° turns, not just 90°.
- **Number Change (Lines, Dots, Sides):** Common in CHSL and CPO. The "+1 line per step" variant is a classic.
- **Alternating Pattern (Dual Sequence):** A rising trend in recent SSC CGL papers — odd positions follow one rule, even positions another.
- **Shading / Fill Change:** Steady appearance; often tested with striped/dotted fills that cause confusion on screen.
- **Classification — Open/Closed, Symmetry:** Favoured in State and Railway exams; tests precise observation.
- **Combined Pattern (Two rules simultaneously):** Less frequent, but this is the "high-difficulty differentiator" question in SSC CGL.
- **Classification — Element Position, Intersections:** Lower frequency, but easy marks when they appear.

Key Insight: Movement/Shifting and Rotation together dominate this topic. Combined and Alternating patterns, though less frequent, are the top discriminators between average and high scorers.

3. Exam-Wise Pattern (Qualitative)

- **SSC CGL Tier-I:** Asks the most challenging Figure Series questions, often embedding two simultaneous rules. Medium–High difficulty.
- **SSC CHSL:** Rotation and Number Change dominate. Easy–Medium.
- **SSC CPO:** Movement and Shading are common. Medium.
- **RRB NTPC CBT-1:** Movement and Classification. Medium.
- **RRB Group D:** Number Change and Rotation. Easy.
- **SSC MTS / GD:** Straightforward single-rule questions. Easy and highly scoring.

4. Top 10 High-Yield Questions with Solutions

Note: Figures are described precisely. Visualise or sketch them as you read. Sources are given as "PYQ-style" because these illustrate the exact pattern types asked in the exams named, rather than reproducing a specific verified paper question.

Q1. [PYQ-style — SSC CGL pattern] — Combined Pattern (Movement + Rotation)

Problem Figures:
(i) Top-left: a filled circle. Right side: a vertical line with a small triangle at its top, pointing right.
(ii) Circle shifts to top-right; triangle now points down.
(iii) Circle shifts to bottom-right; triangle points left.
(iv) Circle shifts to bottom-left; triangle points up.
(v) ?

Solution: The circle moves clockwise corner-to-corner. The triangle rotates 90° clockwise each step. After step (iv), the circle should move to the top-left again, and the triangle, currently pointing up, should rotate to point right. Match the option with circle at top-left and a right-pointing triangle.

Psychological Hook: Two elements changing simultaneously overload working memory. The natural instinct is to track only the more obvious element (the circle). The examiner forces you to hold both changes in your head.

⏱️ 15-Sec Rule: SKIP if both patterns aren't isolated within 15 seconds. Spot one pattern fully (circle path), then focus solely on the other. Attempt only after marking for review.

Q2. [PYQ-style — SSC CGL pattern] — Alternating Pattern (Odd-Even Trap)

Problem Figures:
(i) A square containing a "×" (cross).
(ii) The same square, but the cross becomes a "+" (plus sign).
(iii) The square rotates 90° clockwise; contains "×" again.
(iv) Square with "+" sign.
(v) ?

Solution:
Odd positions (1, 3) → Square with "×" + rotation (90° CW). Even positions (2, 4) → Square with "+" and no rotation. Position (v) is odd → apply the odd rule: square with "×", rotated 90° CW from position (iii).

Psychological Hook: Students spot the "×" to "+" alternation, assume the next is "+", and pick the even-rule figure. The trap is forgetting that an additional rotation rule applies only to odd positions.

⏱️ 45-Sec Rule: If you caught the alternation but missed the rotation, this question can still be solved within 45 seconds by elimination.

Q3. [PYQ-style — SSC CHSL pattern] — Number Change (Line Addition)

Problem Figures:
(i) A single horizontal line.
(ii) Two lines forming an "L".
(iii) Three lines forming a "U".
(iv) Four lines forming a square with one side missing (open box).
(v) ?

Solution: Each step adds one line to extend the shape: 1, 2, 3, 4 lines. The next figure should have 5 lines — typically the open box gaining one more extending line (or the shape continuing to build toward closure, depending on the options). The rule to verify is the exact line count, not the overall look of the shape.

Psychological Hook: A "near-complete" shape tempts you to select the closed figure before verifying the counting logic. Always count lines exactly; do not rely on the gestalt of the shape alone.

Q4. [PYQ-style — RRB NTPC pattern] — Movement (Corner Travel)

Problem Figures:
A square with a black dot. (i) Dot at top-left corner. (ii) Dot at top-right corner. (iii) Dot at bottom-right corner. (iv) Dot at bottom-left corner. (v) ?

Solution: The dot moves clockwise to the next corner each step. After bottom-left, the next corner is top-left again.

Psychological Hook: So simple that overconfidence causes errors. Some variants rotate the square itself while the dot stays fixed — watch for the frame of reference.

Q5. [PYQ-style — SSC CPO pattern] — Shading Change (Fill Cycle)

Problem Figures:
A circle divided into four quadrants. (i) Top-left quadrant black; rest white. (ii) Top-right black. (iii) Bottom-right black. (iv) Bottom-left black. (v) ?

Solution: The black quadrant moves clockwise. After bottom-left, it returns to top-left.

Psychological Hook: On a computer screen, low resolution can make the quadrant boundaries blur, causing confusion between adjacent options. Apply the "Screen Blur Illusion" check: zoom mentally on the dividing lines.

Q6. [PYQ-style — Banking/SSC pattern] — Classification (Open vs Closed)

Figures:
(a) Closed circle, (b) Closed triangle, (c) Closed square, (d) Closed pentagon, (e) A square with a tiny gap in one side.

Solution: (e) is the odd one; all others are fully closed.

Psychological Hook: The "Twin Figures" trap — (c) and (e) both look like squares at a glance. Only side-by-side comparison reveals the gap.

Q7. [PYQ-style — SSC CGL pattern] — Rotation + Element Count (Combined High-Difficulty)

Problem Figures:
(i) A triangle (3 sides) pointing up.
(ii) A square (4 sides) pointing up-right (45° rotated).
(iii) A pentagon (5 sides) pointing right (90° from original).
(iv) A hexagon (6 sides) pointing down-right (135° from original).
(v) ?

Solution: Number of sides increases by 1 each step (3→4→5→6→7). Orientation rotates 45° clockwise each step. Step (v) should be a heptagon (7 sides) rotated an additional 45° from step (iv), pointing straight down.

Psychological Hook: The dual cognitive load — counting sides and tracking rotation — is designed to exceed typical working memory. Most students correctly predict a 7-sided shape but forget the rotation and pick the wrong orientation.

Q8. [PYQ-style — SSC CHSL pattern] — Odd-One-Out Classification (Symmetry)

Figures:
(a) Square (4 symmetry lines), (b) Rectangle (2 symmetry lines), (c) Equilateral triangle (3 symmetry lines), (d) Circle (infinite symmetry lines), (e) Isosceles triangle (1 symmetry line).

Solution: (e) is the odd one because it has only 1 line of symmetry, while all others have 2 or more.

Psychological Hook: Multiple valid classification bases can exist. The examiner expects you to find the simplest and most clear-cut one.

Q9. [PYQ-style — SSC CGL pattern] — Mirror/Reflection Alternation

Problem Figures:
(i) "F" shape. (ii) Mirror image of "F" (backward F). (iii) "F" rotated 90° CW (pointing right). (iv) Mirror image of (iii). (v) ?

Solution: Odd positions: the letter is rotated (original orientation, then 90°, then 180°). Even positions: the mirror image of the preceding figure. Position (v) is odd → it should be the figure from (iii) rotated another 90° CW (pointing down).

Psychological Hook: This is the classic "Rotation + Reflection Mix-up" trap. Students see the mirror and assume the entire sequence is about mirroring, ignoring the embedded rotation cycle.

Q10. [PYQ-style — RRB Group D pattern] — Classification (Intersection Points)

Figures:
(a) Two intersecting lines forming an "X" (1 intersection). (b) A triangle with a line cutting across (2 intersections). (c) A square with both diagonals (1 intersection at centre). (d) Three lines all crossing at one point (1 intersection). (e) A grid of 2×2 lines (4 intersections).

Solution: (e) has 4 intersection points; all others have 1 or 2. (e) is clearly the odd one out.

Psychological Hook: The examiner uses visually busy figures to hide the counting task. The key is to ignore the "visual noise" and focus on a single metric: intersection points.

6. Skip Strategy Based on 15-Sec / 45-Sec Rules

⏱️ 15-Second Skip Candidates (Mark for Review):

- Q1-type: If both movement patterns aren't isolated quickly.
- Q7-type: If you haven't counted sides and identified rotation within 15 seconds.
- Q9-type: If the alternating mirror/rotation logic isn't immediately clear.

⏱️ 45-Second Maximum — Educated Guess Strategy:

If you've partially isolated the rule (e.g., you see the circle path in Q1 but not the triangle rotation), use elimination: remove options where the circle is in the wrong position. This narrows the choice to 1–2 options. Pick one and move on.

7. Review Check: Time Rules

Time Rule	When to Apply
15-Second Scan	Pattern not identified within 15 seconds → mark and skip.
45-Second Max	If still unsure at 45 seconds, eliminate clearly wrong options and take an educated guess.
3-Second Classification Scan	For all classification questions, run the checklist: "All closed? Same corners? Same shading area?"

These time rules are practical advice drawn from the overall time pressure of these exams: in SSC CGL Tier-I you get well under a minute per reasoning question on average. Spending more than 45 seconds on any single Figure Series question directly steals time from other, potentially easier questions.
