Mnemonic 1: The Spinning Disco Ball
Rotation pattern? Picture a disco ball spinning in a fixed beat — 45°, 90°, 135°. The arrow on the ball tells you how much it turned. “Disco never stops; just count the clicks.”

Mnemonic 2: The Traveling Snail
Movement/Shifting: Imagine a snail that leaves a slime trail from corner to corner. “Where’s the slime now? Top-left, top-right, bottom-right, bottom-left — he’s doing a victory lap.”

Mnemonic 3: Counting Sheep… with a Plot Twist
Number change: Each step adds (or subtracts) a sheep jumping over a fence. “One sheep, two sheep, three… oops, now they’re back to one? Count carefully — the farmer might have let one out.”

Mnemonic 4: The Disco Light Switch
Shading/Fill change: Think of a dance floor where the lights flip every beat — black, white, striped, dotted. “The DJ flips the switch every step. Follow the rhythm, not the color!”

Mnemonic 5: Build-a-Bear Workshop
Addition/Deletion: Each step, a new part is added to a teddy bear — an arm, a leg, a bow. “Is it getting fatter or skinnier? The workshop never stops; just watch the new part.”

Mnemonic 6: The Two-Faced Janus
Alternating pattern: Roman god Janus has two faces looking opposite ways. Odd steps look left, even steps look right. “Janus says, ‘What’s odd for me is even for you.’ Check whose turn it is!”

Mnemonic 7: Odd-Even Tango
To detect alternating patterns: Imagine odd numbers dancing a waltz and even numbers dancing a tango. “If the dance changes every other step, split the sequence: watch odds and evens separately.”

Mnemonic 8: SHAPES – Super Heroes Always Protect Every Shape
The classic mnemonic: Shading/Shifting, Half-turn/Rotation, Addition/Alternate, Position change, Element count, Symmetry/Size change. Picture a team of superheroes, each guarding a different transformation. “Call the SHAPES squad when patterns attack!”

Mnemonic 9: The Leaky Bucket
Classification (open vs closed): Four buckets hold water perfectly; one has a tiny hole. “If one figure leaks, it’s the odd one. Look for the hairline gap — the examiner’s favorite pinhole.”

Mnemonic 10: Identical Twins, But One Has a Mole
Twin figure trap: Two answer options look identical except for a tiny detail — a missing dot, a line 1 mm shorter. “The examiner gives you twins. Find the mole. Compare them side by side like a detective with a magnifying glass.”

Mnemonic 11: Vain Mirror Selfie
Mirror/Reflection alternation: A figure takes a selfie every other step. “Odd steps: original pose. Even steps: mirror selfie. If you see a flip, ask, ‘Is this a selfie moment?’”

Mnemonic 12: The Two-Headed Dragon
Combined pattern: A dragon breathes fire (rotation) and swings its tail (movement) at the same time. “Don’t just watch the flames — the tail will knock you out. Track both heads.”

Mnemonic 13: 15-Second Bomb Defusal
Time management: A bomb is ticking. If you don’t see the pattern within 15 seconds, mark the question and run (skip). “Better to defuse another bomb than explode on a stubborn one.”

Mnemonic 14: Sherlock’s Trash Can
Elimination strategy: Sherlock Holmes eliminates the impossible. “Trash the options that clearly violate the rule. Whatever remains, however improbable, is your answer — or at least a 50-50 guess.”

Mnemonic 15: The Foggy Glasses Trap
Screen blur illusion: In CBT exams, a striped shade looks solid, an open figure looks closed. “Clean your glasses (zoom mentally) on corners and gaps. The examiner hopes you’re wearing foggy glasses.”

Skip Strategy Tips

Tip 1 – 15-Second Rule: If no pattern, movement, or classification basis clicks in 15 seconds, mark for review and jump to the next question. You can return with fresher eyes instead of burning 2 minutes on a single figure.

Tip 2 – 45-Second Hard Limit: Never exceed 45 seconds on a Figure Series/Classification question. At the 45-second mark, eliminate clearly wrong options and make an educated guess. The time you save can be invested in easier marks later.

Tip 3 – Twin Option Rescue: When two options look nearly identical, one of them is almost certainly the correct answer (the examiner’s trap). Compare them element by element; the tiny difference is your clue. If you still can’t decide, pick one and move on — you’ve already spent too much time.
