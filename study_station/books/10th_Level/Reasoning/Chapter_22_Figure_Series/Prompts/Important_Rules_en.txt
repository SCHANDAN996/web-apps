📋 Important Rules & Approaches: Figure Series/Classification
Rule/Approach Name	Description	Example
1. Rotation Pattern	Figure turns by a fixed angle (45°, 90°, 135°, 180°) in a consistent direction (CW/ACW).	An arrow rotates 90° clockwise in each step.
2. Movement/Shifting Pattern	An element moves stepwise between positions (corners, sides, inside/outside).	A dot moves from top-left → top-right → bottom-right → bottom-left.
3. Number/Element Count Pattern	Number of lines, dots, sides, or shapes changes systematically (+1, –1, ×2).	Triangle (3 sides) → Square (4) → Pentagon (5) → Hexagon (6).
4. Shading/Fill Change Pattern	Fill style cycles among solid, striped, dotted, or blank in a fixed order.	Circle: half-shaded → fully shaded → blank → half-shaded.
5. Addition/Deletion Pattern	A line or shape is added or removed at a specific location each step.	One line added to form a square from a 'U' shape; next, another line added to close it.
6. Alternating (Odd-Even) Pattern	Two different rules operate on odd (1,3,5) and even (2,4,6) positions separately.	Odd: rotate 90°; Even: mirror image. Next depends on position parity.
7. Combined Pattern	Two or more simple patterns appear simultaneously.	Rotation + element count increase, or shifting + shading change.
8. Open vs Closed Classification	Figures with fully closed boundaries vs one with even a tiny gap.	4 fully closed polygons, 1 with a hairline opening – odd one out.
9. Symmetry Classification	Odd figure has different number of symmetry lines or lacks symmetry entirely.	4 figures have 1 symmetry line, one has 2 – it is the odd one.
10. Intersection Points Classification	Count of line intersections differs in the odd figure.	4 figures with 4 intersection points, one with 2 – that is odd.
11. Shape/Type Classification	All figures are of one geometric family except one.	4 triangles, 1 square – square is odd.
12. Position of Element Classification	An inner element (dot, arrow) is inside in all except one where it's outside.	Dot inside circle for 4, dot outside for 1 – odd figure.

Time Management

Rule	Description	Application
15-Second Scan Rule	If the pattern or odd-one basis isn't spotted within 15 seconds, mark the question for review and move on.	Prevents time sink; return with fresher eyes later.
45-Second Max Rule	Do not spend more than 45 seconds on a single figure series or classification question. If no answer, use elimination and take an educated guess.	Maintains overall section pace; each question gets ≤45 sec.
3-Second Classification Scan	For classification, ask three quick questions: "All closed?", "Same number of corners?", "Shading in same area?" This often reveals the odd one instantly.	Speeds up the odd-one-out search dramatically.
Elimination First	In series, first eliminate answer options that clearly violate the observed rule, then compare the remaining closely.	Saves 10–15 seconds by narrowing choices quickly.
Odd-Even Split Check	If the pattern seems broken after two steps, instantly check odd and even positions separately.	Prevents getting stuck on alternating patterns; saves time.
