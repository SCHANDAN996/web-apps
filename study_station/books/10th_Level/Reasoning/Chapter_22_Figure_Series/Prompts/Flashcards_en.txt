Back: "Whose turn is it?" — check if an alternating (odd-even) pattern is at play.

Front: Your brain screams “It’s rotating!” for the first two figures, but the third one flips. What trap have you walked into?
Back: The examiner's “Rotation + Reflection Mix-up” — two rules take turns. Use the odd-even check.

Front: If figures 1, 3, and 5 show a dot moving clockwise and figures 2, 4, and 6 show a line growing longer, what is the name of this pattern?
Back: Alternating Pattern (Dual Sequence). Odd positions follow one rule; even positions follow another.

Front: What does the letter 'S' stand for twice in the SHAPES mnemonic?
Back: Shading/Shifting and Symmetry/Size change.

Front: A figure sequence adds a side at every step: triangle (3), square (4), pentagon (5), hexagon (6). What kind of transformation is this?
Back: Element Count Change (Number change) — specifically, side addition.

Front: Why should you zoom your eyes on the corners and tiny gaps of a figure during a computer-based test?
Back: The “Screen Blur Illusion” can make a striped shade look solid or a tiny opening appear closed.

Front: In classification, you see five figures: four are closed shapes, one has a hairline gap. What is the odd-one-out basis?
Back: Open vs. Closed Figure. The tiniest gap breaks the closure.

Front: A figure series shows a black square moving from top-left → top-right → bottom-right → bottom-left. What is the pattern?
Back: Movement/Shifting (Clockwise corner-to-corner travel).

Front: Two answer options look almost identical — only one tiny dot is missing in one of them. What should you do before clicking?
Back: Force a side-by-side, element-by-element comparison (avoid the "Twin Figures" trap).

Front: Which mnemonic word helps you recall all 5 major pattern types? Spell it out.
Back: SHAPES — Shading/Shifting, Half-turn/Rotation, Addition/Alternate, Position change, Element count, Symmetry/Size change.

Front: If every alternate figure in a series is a mirror image of the previous, what is the hidden choreography?
Back: The sequence uses alternating mirroring and another rule (like rotation), like Riya spinning and Raj flipping.

Front: How can you instantly separate two intertwined rules in a figure series?
Back: Number the figures 1,2,3,4,5; observe odd numbers (1,3,5) for one rule and even numbers (2,4) for the other.

Front: A series shows a circle becoming half-shaded, then fully shaded, then half-shaded again. What rule is being applied?
Back: Shading/ Fill Change (a cyclic pattern between white, black, or striped).

Front: What is the danger of assuming a single transformation rule after looking at only the first two figures?
Back: You might miss an alternating pattern or a combined dual rule and choose the wrong next figure.

Front: In figure classification, four shapes have exactly one line of symmetry, and one has two. What is the classification basis?
Back: Symmetry count — odd one out has a different number of symmetry lines.

Front: After how many days should you re-draw the five pattern types from memory to lock them in long-term memory?
Back: 2 days (spaced retrieval) — then create your own mini-series for each type.

Front: A figure loses one small line at each step but also rotates 45° clockwise. What kind of pattern is this?
Back: Combined Pattern (Deletion + Rotation). Always identify the dominant change first.
