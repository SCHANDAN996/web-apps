Card 1
Front: What are the two genders (लिंग) in Hindi grammar?
Back: The two genders are "पुल्लिंग" (masculine) and "स्त्रीलिंग" (feminine). Every noun belongs to one of these.

Card 2
Front: How can you often identify a "पुल्लिंग" noun ending?
Back: Many "पुल्लिंग" nouns end in "आ" (e.g., "लड़का", "घर", "काम"), but there are exceptions like "पानी", "हाथी".

Card 3
Front: How can you often identify a "स्त्रीलिंग" noun ending?
Back: Many "स्त्रीलिंग" nouns end in "ई" or "इ" (e.g., "लड़की", "नदी", "किताब"), but exceptions exist like "मेज", "आग".

Card 4
Front: Give two examples of nouns that change form completely between genders.
Back: "घोड़ा" (masculine) → "घोड़ी" (feminine); "राजा" (masculine) → "रानी" (feminine).

Card 5
Front: What is "वचन" (number) in Hindi?
Back: "वचन" indicates singular or plural. Two numbers: "एकवचन" (singular) and "बहुवचन" (plural).

Card 6
Front: How is "बहुवचन" typically formed for "आ"-ending "पुल्लिंग" nouns?
Back: Replace "आ" with "ए": "लड़का" → "लड़के", "घर" → "घर" (unchanged), "काम" → "काम" (unchanged). Note: not all "आ"-ending nouns follow this.

Card 7
Front: How is "बहुवचन" typically formed for "ई"-ending "स्त्रीलिंग" nouns?
Back: Replace "ई" with "इयाँ": "लड़की" → "लड़कियाँ", "नदी" → "नदियाँ", "किताब" → "किताबें" (consonant-ending adds "एँ").

Card 8
Front: What are "कारक" (case) in Hindi?
Back: "कारक" show the relation of a noun/pronoun to the verb. There are 8 कारक, each with a विभक्ति (case marker).

Card 9
Front: Name the 8 कारक with their common विभक्ति.
Back: 1. कर्ता (नॉमिनेटिव) — no marker / "ने" (transitive past). 2. कर्म (एक्यूजेटिव) — "को". 3. करण (इंस्ट्रूमेंटल) — "से". 4. संप्रदान (डेटिव) — "को/के लिए". 5. अपादान (एब्लेटिव) — "से". 6. संबंध (जेनिटिव) — "का/के/की". 7. अधिकरण (लोकेटिव) — "में/पर". 8. संबोधन (वोकेटिव) — "हे/ओ" or bare stem.

Card 10
Front: When does the कर्ता take "ने"?
Back: In transitive verbs, past tense (perfective aspect): "राम ने खाना खाया". Intransitive or non-past: "राम खाता है" (no "ने").

Card 11
Front: Difference between कर्म "को" and संप्रदान "को"?
Back: कर्म "को" marks direct object (definite animate): "मैंने राम को देखा". संप्रदान "को" marks indirect object/beneficiary: "मैंने राम को किताब दी".

Card 12
Front: Give an example where "से" shows करण (instrument) vs अपादान (source).
Back: करण: "मैंने कलम से लिखा" (with pen). अपादान: "वह घर से आया" (from home). Same विभक्ति, different कारक by meaning.

Card 13
Front: How does the संबंध विभक्ति agree with the possessed noun?
Back: "का/के/की" agrees in gender/number with the noun possessed: "राम का बेटा" (masc sg), "राम के बेटे" (masc pl), "राम की बेटी" (fem sg/pl).

Card 14
Front: When is "में" used vs "पर" for अधिकरण (location)?
Back: "में" = inside/in: "कमरे में". "पर" = on/at surface: "मेज पर". Both can indicate time: "सुबह में", "सोमवार को" (note: "को" also for time).

Card 15
Front: What happens to the noun stem before adding विभक्ति in plural oblique?
Back: Masc "आ"-ending: "लड़का" → oblique pl "लड़कों" (add "ओं" + विभक्ति). Fem "ई"-ending: "लड़की" → oblique pl "लड़कियों" (add "यों" + विभक्ति). Consonant-ending masc: "घर" → "घरों".

Card 16
Front: Identify the कारक in: "शिक्षक ने छात्र को किताब दी।"
Back: "शिक्षक ने" = कर्ता (ने), "छात्र को" = संप्रदान (को), "किताब" = कर्म (no को, inanimate), "दी" = verb.

Card 17
Front: Common mistake: using "को" with inanimate direct object.
Back: Incorrect: "मैंने मेज को देखा". Correct: "मैंने मेज देखी" (inanimate direct object usually takes no "को" unless personified/definite).

Card 18
Front: Common mistake: wrong agreement of संबंध विभक्ति.
Back: Incorrect: "राम की बेटा". Correct: "राम का बेटा" (masc sg). Incorrect: "सीता का किताब". Correct: "सीता की किताब" (fem sg). Agreement is with possessed noun, not possessor.
