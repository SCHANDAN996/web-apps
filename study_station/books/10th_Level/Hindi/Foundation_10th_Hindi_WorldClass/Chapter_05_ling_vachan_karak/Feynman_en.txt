# Gender, Number & Case — Feynman-Style Explanation

Think of Hindi grammar as a **three-layer filter** that every noun passes through before it can sit in a sentence.

---

## Layer 1 — लिंग (Gender) : The "Team Jersey"

Every noun wears a jersey: **पुल्लिंग** (masculine) or **स्त्रीलिंग** (feminine).  
It has nothing to do with biology — "किताब" (book) wears the feminine jersey, "कमरा" (room) wears the masculine one.

**Everyday test**: Put "अच्छा" before the noun.  
- "अच्छा लड़का" → sounds right → **पुल्लिंग**  
- "अच्छी लड़की" → sounds right → **स्त्रीलिंग**  
- "अच्छा किताब" ❌ → "अच्छी किताब" ✅ → **स्त्रीलिंग**

**Trick**: Nouns ending in **आ** are usually पुल्लिंग ("लड़का", "घर"), ending in **ई** are usually स्त्रीलिंग ("लड़की", "नदी"). Exceptions exist ("दादा", "मछली") — memorise the high-frequency ones.

---

## Layer 2 — वचन (Number) : Solo vs Group

**एकवचन** (singular) = one item. **बहुवचन** (plural) = more than one.

| Ending in एकवचन | बहुवचन rule | Example |
|---|---|---|
| आ → ए | "लड़का" → "लड़के" |
| ई → इयाँ | "लड़की" → "लड़कियाँ" |
| consonant (no vowel) → एँ | "कमरा" → "कमरे" (already ends in आ) |
| उ/ऊ → एँ/ओँ | "गुरु" → "गुरुएँ", "हाथी" → "हाथी" (no change) |

**Common mistake**: "पानी" (water) is uncountable — no plural. "सूचनाएँ" (information) looks plural but is treated as singular in formal Hindi.

---

## Layer 3 — कारक (Case) : The Role Badge

कारक tells **what job the noun does** in the sentence. Eight badges, each with a postposition (विभक्ति चिह्न):

| कारक | Question it answers | विभक्ति चिह्न | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| कर्ता (Subject) | Who did it? | (none) | "राम खाता है" |
| कर्म (Object) | Whom/what? | को | "राम **सेब को** खाता है" |
| करण (Instrument) | With what? | से / द्वारा | "राम **चम्मच से** खाता है" |
| संप्रदान (Recipient) | To whom? | को / के लिए | "राम **श्याम को** देता है" |
| अपादान (Source) | From where? | से | "राम **बाजार से** आया" |
| संबंध (Possession) | Whose? | का / के / की | "**राम का** घर" |
| अधिकरण (Location) | Where? | में / पर | "राम **घर में** है" |
| संबोधन (Vocative) | Hey you! | (none / हे) | "**राम**, इधर आओ" |

**Golden rule**: The postposition **controls the noun's form** (oblique form).  
- Direct: "लड़का"  
- After को/से/में/का: "लड़के" (oblique singular)  
- After को/से/में/के: "लड़कों" (oblique plural)

## 5 Recall Questions

**Q1.** "कमरा" का बहुवचन क्या होगा?  
**Options:** (a) कमरे (b) कमराएँ (c) कमरेँ (d) कमराओं  
**Answer:** (a)  
**Explanation:** "कमरा" ends in आ → change to ए. No nasalisation needed.

**Q2.** "लड़की **को** बुलाओ" में "लड़की" किस कारक में है?  
**Options:** (a) कर्ता (b) कर्म (c) संप्रदान (d) अधिकरण  
**Answer:** (b)  
**Explanation:** को marks the direct object (कर्म) here — the girl is being called, not the recipient of a gift.

**Q3.** "राम **ने** खाना खाया" — "ने" किस कारक का चिह्न है?  
**Options:** (a) कर्ता (b) कर्म (c) करण (d) संप्रदान  
**Answer:** (a)  
**Explanation:** ने marks the agent (कर्ता) in perfective transitive sentences. "राम" is in oblique form because of ने.

**Q4.** "पेड़ **के** नीचे" में "पेड़" का रूप कैसा है?  
**Options:** (a) प्रथमा एकवचन (b) प्रथमा बहुवचन (c) संबंध कारक एकवचन (d) अधिकरण कारक बहुवचन  
**Answer:** (c)  
**Explanation:** के shows possession/relation (संबंध). "पेड़" → oblique singular "पेड़" (no change for consonant-ending masculine).

**Q5.** Which sentence uses the **instrumental case** correctly?  
**Options:**  
(a) "वह **कलम से** लिखता है"  
(b) "वह **कलम को** लिखता है"  
(c) "वह **कलम में** लिखता है"  
(d) "वह **कलम का** लिखता है"  
**Answer:** (a)  
**Explanation:** से marks करण (instrument) — "with a pen". को would make the pen the object, में would mean location, का would mean possession.

---

## Quick Mental Checklist Before Every Sentence

1. **Gender** — does the adjective/verb ending match the noun's jersey?  
2. **Number** — is the noun solo or in a group? Adjust noun + verb.  
3. **Case** — which postposition sits after the noun? Put the noun in its **oblique form** first, then add the postposition.

Drill: Pick any noun ("किताब", "बच्चा", "पानी") and run it through all three layers aloud. Five minutes daily beats rote tables.
