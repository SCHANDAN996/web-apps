# Gender, Number and Case (लिंग, वचन और कारक)

## Introduction

This chapter covers three fundamental building blocks of Hindi grammar: **लिंग (Gender)**, **वचन (Number)** and **कारक (Case)**. Mastery of these topics is essential for SSC GD, MTS, Railway Group D and state police examinations where Hindi comprehension and grammar form a significant portion of the paper. Questions typically test your ability to identify the correct gender, number or case of a given word, to transform sentences from singular to plural (or vice versa), and to spot errors in case endings.

All Hindi words and sentences that appear in examination questions are shown in **double quotes** and in **Devanagari** throughout this lesson. Explanatory text is in English.

---

## 1. लिंग (Gender)

### Definition
**लिंग** indicates the grammatical gender of a noun or pronoun. Hindi has two genders:
- **पुल्लिंग (Masculine)** — denotes male beings or personified masculine objects.
- **स्त्रीलिंग (Feminine)** — denotes female beings or personified feminine objects.

There is no neuter gender in Hindi. Inanimate objects are assigned masculine or feminine gender by convention.

### Identification Rules (Guidelines, Not Absolute)

| Pattern | Typical Gender | Examples |
|---------|----------------|----------|
| Ends in **आ** (aa) | Masculine | **"लड़का"**, **"कमरा"**, **"पेड़ा"** |
| Ends in **ई** (ii) or **इया** (iyaa) | Feminine | **"लड़की"**, **"किताबी"**, **"चिड़िया"** |
| Ends in **अ** (a) — consonant ending | Mostly Masculine | **"घर"**, **"फल"**, **"काम"** |
| Ends in **ि** (i) or **ी** (ii) — abstract nouns | Often Feminine | **"बुद्धि"**, **"शक्ति"**, **"भक्ति"** |
| Ends in **पन**, **पना**, **ई**, **आई** (abstract suffixes) | Feminine | **"बचपन"**, **"मित्रता"**, **"अच्छाई"** |

**Important:** Many nouns do not follow these patterns. Gender must be memorised for high-frequency words (e.g., **"पानी"** masculine, **"नदी"** feminine; **"दूध"** masculine, **"चाय"** feminine).

### Gender Pairs (Common Transformations)

| Masculine | Feminine | Rule |
|-----------|----------|------|
| **"लड़का"** | **"लड़की"** | आ → ई |
| **"बेटा"** | **"बेटी"** | आ → ई |
| **"भाई"** | **"बहन"** | Irregular |
| **"पिता"** | **"माता"** | Irregular |
| **"राजा"** | **"रानी"** | आ → ानी |
| **"घोड़ा"** | **"घोड़ी"** | आ → ी |
| **"शेर"** | **"शेरनी"** | Add नी |
| **"मोर"** | **"मोरनी"** | Add नी |

Adjectives and verbs agree with the noun's gender. Example: **"अच्छा लड़का"** (masculine) vs **"अच्छी लड़की"** (feminine).

## 2. वचन (Number)

### Definition
**वचन** indicates whether a noun, pronoun, adjective or verb refers to one or more than one. Hindi has two numbers:
- **एकवचन (Singular)** — one person/thing.
- **बहुवचन (Plural)** — more than one.

### Plural Formation Rules

| Singular Ending | Plural Ending | Examples |
|-----------------|---------------|----------|
| **आ** (masc.) | **ए** | **"लड़का" → "लड़के"**, **"कमरा" → "कमरे"** |
| **इ/ई** (fem.) | **इयाँ / इयाँ** | **"लड़की" → "लड़कियाँ"**, **"नदी" → "नदियाँ"** |
| Consonant (masc.) | **एँ / ओँ** | **"घर" → "घर" (same) or "घरों"**, **"फल" → "फल" or "फलों"** |
| Consonant (fem.) | **एँ / इयाँ** | **"किताब" → "किताबें"**, **"बात" → "बातें"** |
| **उ/ऊ** | **एँ / ओँ** | **"गुरु" → "गुरु" or "गुरुओं"**, **"हाथी" → "हाथी" or "हाथियों"** |

**Notes:**
- Many masculine nouns ending in a consonant do not change in the direct plural (e.g., **"घर"**, **"फल"**, **"पानी"**). The plural is shown by the verb or postposition.
- Feminine nouns ending in a consonant usually take **एँ** (e.g., **"किताबें"**, **"बातें"**).
- Polite/respectful plural: **"आप"**, **"वे"**, **"ये"** trigger plural verb forms even for singular referents.

### Verb Agreement with Number
- Singular subject → singular verb: **"लड़का जाता है"**
- Plural subject → plural verb: **"लड़के जाते हैं"**
- Respectful singular → plural verb: **"पिताजी जाते हैं"**

## 3. कारक (Case)

### Definition
**कारक** shows the relationship of a noun or pronoun to other words in the sentence (typically the verb). Hindi uses **postpositions** (words placed after the noun) to mark case. The noun form often changes (oblique form) before a postposition.

### Eight Cases (कारक) with Postpositions

| कारक (Case) | Sanskrit Name | Postposition(s) | Function | Example |
|--------------|---------------|-----------------|----------|---------|
| **कर्ता** (Nominative) | कर्ता | — (no postposition) | Subject / doer | **"राम खाता है"** |
| **कर्म** (Accusative) | कर्म | **को** | Direct object (definite animate) | **"राम ने सीता को देखा"** |
| **करण** (Instrumental) | करण | **से** | Instrument / agent (passive) | **"राम ने कलम से लिखा"** |
| **सम्प्रदान** (Dative) | सम्प्रदान | **को / के लिए** | Indirect object / beneficiary | **"राम ने श्याम को पुस्तक दी"** |
| **अपादान** (Ablative) | अपादान | **से** | Source / separation | **"राम घर से आया"** |
| **सम्बन्ध** (Genitive) | सम्बन्ध | **का / के / की** | Possession / relation | **"राम का घर"** |
| **अधिकरण** (Locative) | अधिकरण | **में / पर** | Location / time | **"राम घर में है"** |
| **सम्बोधन** (Vocative) | सम्बोधन | **हे / अरे / ओ** | Addressing | **"हे राम!"** |

### Oblique Forms (Before Postpositions)

Nouns change to an **oblique base** before postpositions.

| Type | Singular Direct | Singular Oblique | Plural Direct | Plural Oblique |
|------|-----------------|------------------|---------------|----------------|
| Masc. ending in **आ** | **"लड़का"** | **"लड़के"** | **"लड़के"** | **"लड़कों"** |
| Masc. consonant | **"घर"** | **"घर"** | **"घर"** | **"घरों"** |
| Fem. ending in **ई/इ** | **"लड़की"** | **"लड़की"** | **"लड़कियाँ"** | **"लड़कियों"** |
| Fem. consonant | **"किताब"** | **"किताब"** | **"किताबें"** | **"किताबों"** |

**Examples with Postpositions:**
- **"लड़के ने"** (कर्ता + ने — ergative in perfective)
- **"लड़के को"** (कर्म)
- **"लड़के से"** (करण / अपादान)
- **"लड़के के लिए"** (सम्प्रदान)
- **"लड़के का / के / की"** (सम्बन्ध)
- **"लड़के में / पर"** (अधिकरण)

### Special Case: Ergative **ने**
In perfective (past) transitive clauses, the subject takes **ने** and the verb agrees with the object.
- **"राम ने खाना खाया"** (masc. singular object → verb masculine singular)
- **"राम ने रोटी खाई"** (fem. singular object → verb feminine singular)
- **"राम ने सेब खाए"** (masc. plural object → verb masculine plural)

## 4. Six Worked Examples

Each example follows the pattern: **Question → Analysis → Answer → Explanation**. All Hindi test items are in double quotes.

### Example 1: Gender Identification
**Question:** Identify the feminine noun among the following:  
(a) **"पानी"**  (b) **"नदी"**  (c) **"दूध"**  (d) **"आकाश"**

**Analysis:**  
- **"पानी"** (water) — masculine (consonant ending, but conventionally masculine).  
- **"नदी"** (river) — ends in **ई**, feminine.  
- **"दूध"** (milk) — masculine.  
- **"आकाश"** (sky) — masculine.

**Answer:** (b) **"नदी"**

**Explanation:** Nouns ending in **ई** are typically feminine. **"नदी"** follows this pattern. The other three are masculine by convention.

---

### Example 2: Singular to Plural Transformation
**Question:** Change **"लड़का"** to its plural form.  
(a) **"लड़की"**  (b) **"लड़के"**  (c) **"लड़कों"**  (d) **"लड़कियाँ"**

**Analysis:**  
- **"लड़का"** is masculine, ends in **आ**.  
- Direct plural of masculine **आ**-ending nouns: **आ → ए**.  
- **"लड़के"** is the direct plural. **"लड़कों"** is the oblique plural (used with postpositions). The question asks for the plural form without a postposition, so the direct plural is correct.

**Answer:** (b) **"लड़के"**

**Explanation:** Rule: Masculine nouns ending in **आ** form direct plural by changing **आ** to **ए**. Oblique plural adds **ओं** (→ **"लड़कों"**).

---

### Example 3: Case Identification (Accusative)
**Question:** In the sentence **"राम ने सीता को देखा"**, the word **"सीता को"** represents which कारक?  
(a) कर्ता  (b) कर्म  (c) करण  (d) सम्प्रदान

**Analysis:**  
- **"राम ने"** — subject (कर्ता) with ergative **ने** (perfective transitive).  
- **"सीता को"** — direct object, marked by **को** because the object is definite and animate.  
- **"देखा"** — verb agrees with object (**सीता**, feminine singular).

**Answer:** (b) कर्म

**Explanation:** **कर्म कारक** marks the direct object. The postposition **को** is used for definite animate objects.

---

### Example 4: Oblique Form with Postposition
**Question:** Choose the correct form to fill the blank:  
**"मैं ______ घर गया।"**  
(a) **"राम"**  (b) **"राम का"**  (c) **"राम के"**  (d) **"राम को"**

**Analysis:**  
- The verb **"गया"** (went) indicates motion toward a location.  
- **"घर"** is the destination. The phrase **"राम के घर"** means "Ram's house" (possessive/genitive).  
- **"राम का"** — oblique singular + **का** (masc. singular possession).  
- **"राम के"** — oblique singular + **के** (masc. plural/oblique possession). Since **"घर"** is masculine singular direct, the possessive must be **"का"**. But the blank precedes **"घर"**, so we need the genitive phrase **"राम का"**. However, the options show **"राम का"** and **"राम के"**. The correct agreement is **"राम का घर"**. Option (b) matches.

**Answer:** (b) **"राम का"**

**Explanation:** **सम्बन्ध कारक** (genitive) shows possession. **"राम"** (masc. consonant) → oblique **"राम"** + **का** (agreeing with **"घर"**, masc. singular).

---

### Example 5: Instrumental Case
**Question:** Which sentence correctly uses the instrumental case?  
(a) **"राम कलम से लिखा"**  
(b) **"राम ने कलम से लिखा"**  
(c) **"राम कलम लिखा"**  
(d) **"राम ने कलम लिखा"**

**Analysis:**  
- Instrumental case (करण कारक) is marked by **से** and indicates the instrument.  
- In perfective transitive, the subject takes **ने** (ergative).  
- **"राम ने कलम से लिखा"** = "Ram wrote with a pen." Correct: subject **राम ने**, instrument **कलम से**.  
- (a) misses **ने** for perfective transitive.  
- (c) and (d) lack **से** for instrument.

**Answer:** (b) **"राम ने कलम से लिखा"**

**Explanation:** Perfective transitive requires ergative **ने** on subject. Instrument takes **से**. Both are present in (b).

---

### Example 6: Agreement in Number and Gender (Verb)
**Question:** Select the correct verb form:  
**"दोनों लड़कियाँ ______।"**  
(a) **"जाता है"**  (b) **"जाते हैं"**  (c) **"जाती है"**  (d) **"जाती हैं"**

**Analysis:**  
- Subject: **"दोनों लड़कियाँ"** — **"लड़कियाँ"** is feminine plural (direct plural of **"लड़की"**). **"दोनों"** reinforces plurality.  
- Verb must agree in gender (feminine) and number (plural).  
- Feminine plural verb: **"जाती हैं"**.

**Answer:** (d) **"जाती हैं"**

**Explanation:** Verb agreement follows the subject's gender and number. Feminine plural → **"जाती हैं"**. Option (b) is masculine plural; (c) is feminine singular.

## 5. Common Errors and How to Avoid Them

| Error Type | Typical Mistake | Correct Form | Why It Is Wrong |
|------------|-----------------|--------------|-----------------|
| **Gender of inanimate nouns** | Treating **"पानी"**, **"दूध"**, **"तेल"** as feminine | **"पानी ठंडा है"**, **"दूध गरम है"** | These are masculine by convention; adjective/verb must be masculine. |
| **Plural of consonant-ending masculine** | Adding **एँ** to **"घर"** → **"घरें"** | **"घर"** (direct plural same) or **"घरों"** (oblique) | Masculine consonant stems often have zero-change direct plural. |
| **Plural of feminine consonant stems** | **"किताब" → "किताबों"** (direct) | **"किताबें"** (direct), **"किताबों"** (oblique) | Direct plural takes **एँ**; oblique takes **ओं**. |
| **Ergative ने in non-perfective** | **"राम ने खाता है"** (present) | **"राम खाता है"** | **ने** only in perfective (past) transitive. Present/future use direct subject. |
| **Missing को for definite animate object** | **"राम ने सीता देखा"** | **"राम ने सीता को देखा"** | Definite animate objects require **को**. |
| **Genitive agreement error** | **"राम की घर"** | **"राम का घर"** | **"घर"** is masculine singular → possessive **"का"**. |
| **Locative confusion: में vs पर** | **"राम मेज में बैठा"** | **"राम मेज पर बैठा"** | **"पर"** for on/at a surface; **"में"** for inside/enclosed. |
| **Verb agreement with nearest noun** | **"राम और सीता जाता है"** | **"राम और सीता जाते हैं"** | Compound subject joined by **और** → plural verb. |
| **Respectful singular verb** | **"पिताजी जाता है"** | **"पिताजी जाते हैं"** | Honorific subjects take plural verb. |
| **Oblique form before postposition** | **"लड़का को बुलाओ"** | **"लड़के को बुलाओ"** | **"लड़का"** (masc. आ-ending) → oblique **"लड़के"** before **को**. |

## 6. Quick Revision Checklist

1. **Gender**: Memorise gender of 50+ high-frequency inanimate nouns (water, milk, tea, river, mountain, book, table, chair, etc.).
2. **Plural Rules**: Master the four main patterns — **आ → ए**, **ई → इयाँ**, consonant masc. (zero/ओं), consonant fem. (एँ/ओं).
3. **Case Postpositions**: Know the eight cases and their postpositions by heart. Practise converting direct nouns to oblique before each postposition.
4. **Ergative ने**: Only in perfective transitive. Verb agrees with object (if object has no **को**) or defaults to masculine singular.
5. **Agreement Chains**: Noun → Adjective → Verb all match in gender and number. Postposition forces oblique form.
6. **Error Spotting**: In exams, read the full sentence. Check subject-verb agreement first, then case markers, then gender of adjectives.

---

## 7. Examination Tips

- **Spot-the-error questions**: Scan for subject-verb mismatch, wrong postposition, incorrect oblique form, gender disagreement.
- **Fill-in-the-blanks**: Identify the case required by the verb/context, then apply the correct postposition and oblique form.
- **Transformation (singular ↔ plural)**: Change noun, adjective, verb, and any postposition-marked forms consistently.
- **Time management**: Grammar questions are high-yield. Aim to solve each in **15–20 seconds**. Do not over-analyse; trust the rules.
