# Letters and Spelling — Feynman Explanation

Think of Hindi letters as **building blocks**. Just as English has 26 letters that combine into words, Hindi has a systematic set of sounds organised by *where* and *how* they are produced in the mouth. Mastering the blocks and the rules for joining them is what "वर्ण और वर्तनी" tests.

---

## 1. The Sound Map (वर्णमाला)

Hindi sounds are grouped by **place of articulation**:

- **कण्ठ्य (Velar)**: "क", "ख", "ग", "घ", "ङ" — back of tongue touches soft palate.  
- **तालव्य (Palatal)**: "च", "छ", "ज", "झ", "ञ" — middle of tongue touches hard palate.  
- **मूर्धन्य (Retroflex)**: "ट", "ठ", "ड", "ढ", "ण" — tongue tip curls back.  
- **दन्त्य (Dental)**: "त", "थ", "द", "ध", "न" — tongue tip touches upper teeth.  
- **ओष्ठ्य (Labial)**: "प", "फ", "ब", "भ", "म" — both lips.

**Vowels (स्वर)** are open vocal-tract sounds: "अ", "आ", "इ", "ई", "उ", "ऊ", "ऋ", "ए", "ऐ", "ओ", "औ".  
**Consonants (व्यंजन)** need a vowel to be pronounced; the default is "अ" (schwa).

*Analogy*: Vowels are **electricity**; consonants are **appliances**. An appliance (consonant) does nothing until plugged into a socket (vowel).

---

## 2. Matras — The Plugs (मात्राएँ)

Each vowel has a **matra** (diacritic) that attaches to a consonant, changing its sound:

| Vowel | Matra | Example |
|-------|-------|---------|
| "आ" | ा | "क" + ा = "का" |
| "इ" | ि | "क" + ि = "कि" |
| "ई" | ी | "क" + ी = "की" |
| "उ" | ु | "क" + ु = "कु" |
| "ऊ" | ू | "क" + ू = "कू" |
| "ए" | े | "क" + े = "के" |
| "ऐ" | ै | "क" + ै = "कै" |
| "ओ" | ो | "क" + ो = "को" |
| "औ" | ौ | "क" + ौ = "कौ" |

**Common trap**: "कि" (short *i*) vs "की" (long *ī*). The matra length changes meaning: "दिन" (day) vs "दीन" (poor/helpless).

---

## 3. Half Letters & Conjuncts (संयुक्त व्यंजन)

When two consonants meet without a vowel between them, the first becomes **half** (हलन्त/विराम चिह्न ्):

- "क्" + "ष" = "क्ष" (as in "क्षमा")  
- "स्" + "्थ" = "स्थ" (as in "स्थान")  
- "द्" + "य" = "द्य" (as in "विद्या")

*Analogy*: Two people holding hands — the first lets go of its default "अ" vowel and leans into the second.

**Frequent error**: Writing "सथान" instead of "स्थान", or "विदया" instead of "विद्या". The half-form is mandatory; skipping it changes pronunciation and spelling.

---

## 4. Anusvara & Visarga (अनुस्वार और विसर्ग)

- **अनुस्वार (ं)** — nasal dot: "अंगूर", "संगीत". Before "क/ख/ग/घ" it sounds like "ङ"; before "च/छ/ज/झ" like "ञ"; before "ट/ठ/ड/ढ" like "ण"; before "त/थ/द/ध" like "न"; before "प/फ/ब/भ" like "म".  
- **विसर्ग (ः)** — two dots: "दुःख", "मनः". Adds a soft *h* echo.

**Trap**: "अं" vs "अँ" (चन्द्रबिन्दु). Use चन्द्रबिन्दु (ँ) for nasalised vowels in words like "हँसना", "सँभालना"; use अनुस्वार (ं) elsewhere.

---

## 5. Common Spelling Pitfalls

| Error | Correct | Why |
|-------|---------|-----|
| "किराना" | "किराना" (no error) | — |
| "किराना" written as "किराणा" | "किराना" | "ण" is retroflex; "न" is dental — different sounds. |
| "शिक्षा" | "शिक्षा" | "ष" (retroflex s) + "क" = "ष्क"; not "श्क". |
| "व्याकरण" | "व्याकरण" | "य" + "आ" = "या"; half "क्" + "र" = "क्र". |
| "प्रभाव" | "प्रभाव" | "प्" + "र" = "प्र"; "भा" + "व" = "भाव". |

**Rule of thumb**: Pronounce slowly, identify each consonant-vowel unit, then write the matra or half-form that matches.

---

## 6. Five Recall Questions

**Q1.** Which matra represents the long vowel "ई" when attached to a consonant?  
**Answer:** ी  
**Explanation:** The short "इ" uses ि (e.g., "कि"), while long "ई" uses ी (e.g., "की"). The extra vertical stroke signals length.

**Q2.** Write the conjunct formed by "स्" + "थ".  
**Answer:** स्थ  
**Explanation:** Half "स्" (स्) joins full "थ" → "स्थ" as in "स्थान". Writing "सथ" misses the half-form.

**Q3.** In the word "अंगूर", the dot above "अ" is called?  
**Answer:** अनुस्वार  
**Explanation:** अनुस्वार (ं) denotes a nasal sound homorganic with the following consonant; here it represents "ङ" before "ग".

**Q4.** Choose the correctly spelt word: (a) "विदया" (b) "विद्या" (c) "विद्द्या" (d) "विद्या"  
**Answer:** (b) "विद्या"  
**Explanation:** "द्" + "य" = "द्य". Option (a) misses the half-form; (c) doubles "द" incorrectly; (d) is a duplicate of (b).

**Q5.** Which of these uses चन्द्रबिन्दु (ँ) correctly? (a) "हंसना" (b) "हँसना" (c) "हंस्ना" (d) "हँस्ना"  
**Answer:** (b) "हँसना"  
**Explanation:** Nasalised vowel "अँ" in "हँसना" requires चन्द्रबिन्दु. Option (a) lacks nasalisation; (c) and (d) misuse half-forms.

---

## 7. Quick Self-Check Routine

1. **Say the word aloud** — break into syllables.  
2. **Identify each consonant** — is it full or half?  
3. **Match the vowel sound** — pick the exact matra (short vs long).  
4. **Check nasalisation** — अनुस्वार or चन्द्रबिन्दु?  
5. **Write once, read back** — does it sound identical?

Treat every spelling as a **pronunciation map**. If the map is accurate, the reader reaches the right sound every time.
