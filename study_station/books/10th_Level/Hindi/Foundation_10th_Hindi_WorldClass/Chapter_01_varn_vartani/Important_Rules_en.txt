Important Rules of Hindi Varna (Letters) and Vartani (Spelling)

This chapter covers the foundational rules of the Hindi alphabet and spelling conventions that are frequently tested in SSC GD and similar examinations. Master these rules to avoid common errors in word formation, dictation, and error-spotting questions.

---

### Rule 1: Classification of Varna (वर्ण-विभाजन)

Hindi varna are divided into two main groups: **Svar (स्वर)** — vowels, and **Vyanjan (व्यंजन)** — consonants.

- **Svar (स्वर)**: Independent sounds produced without obstruction. There are 11 svar in standard Hindi:  
  "अ", "आ", "इ", "ई", "उ", "ऊ", "ऋ", "ए", "ऐ", "ओ", "औ".
- **Vyanjan (व्यंजन)**: Sounds produced with obstruction of airflow. There are 33 vyanjan in the traditional varnamala, grouped by place of articulation (कण्ठ्य, तालव्य, मूर्धन्य, दन्त्य, ओष्ठ्य) and manner (स्पर्श, अन्तःस्थ, ऊष्म).

**Example**: In the word "कमल", "क" and "म" are vyanjan; "अ" (inherent in क) and "अ" (in म) are svar.

---

### Rule 2: Hrasva (Short) and Dirgha (Long) Vowels

Each svar (except "ऋ") has a short (ह्रस्व) and long (दीर्घ) form. The length changes meaning.

| Hrasva (Short) | Dirgha (Long) | Example Pair |
|----------------|---------------|--------------|
| "अ"            | "आ"           | "कल" (tomorrow/machine) vs "काल" (time/death) |
| "इ"            | "ई"           | "दिन" (day) vs "दीन" (poor) |
| "उ"            | "ऊ"           | "फल" (fruit) vs "फूल" (flower) |
| "ऋ"            | —             | "ऋषि" (sage) — no long form in common use |

**Exception**: "ए" and "ओ" are traditionally considered long vowels; they have no short counterparts in standard Hindi. "ऐ" and "औ" are diphthongs treated as long.

---

### Rule 3: Matra (मात्रा) — Vowel Signs

When a svar follows a vyanjan, it is written as a **matra** (diacritic) attached to the consonant. The inherent "अ" in a consonant has no visible matra.

| Svar | Matra Symbol | Example with "क" |
|------|--------------|------------------|
| "आ"  | ा            | "का" |
| "इ"  | ि            | "कि" |
| "ई"  | ी            | "की" |
| "उ"  | ु            | "कु" |
| "ऊ"  | ू            | "कू" |
| "ऋ"  | ृ            | "कृ" |
| "ए"  | े            | "के" |
| "ऐ"  | ै            | "कै" |
| "ओ"  | ो            | "को" |
| "औ"  | ौ            | "कौ" |

**Important**: The matra for "इ" (ि) is written **before** the consonant (pre-base), but pronounced after it. Example: "कि" is read as "ki", not "ik".

---

### Rule 4: Halant (हलन्त) / Virama (विराम)

A **halant** (्) removes the inherent "अ" from a consonant, making it a **half-consonant** (अर्धव्यंजन). It is used in conjuncts (संयुक्त व्यंजन) or at word-end in Sanskritised words.

- "क्" + "ष" = "क्ष" (as in "क्षमा")
- "स्" + "्थ" = "स्थ" (as in "स्थान")
- Word-final halant: "योग्य्" (rare in modern Hindi; usually "योग्य")

**Common Error**: Writing "क्म" instead of "कम" — the halant must not be used where a full vowel sound exists.

---

### Rule 5: Anusvara (अनुस्वार) — ं

**Anusvara** (dot above the letter) represents a nasal sound homorganic with the following consonant. In Devanagari, it is written as a dot (ं) above the letter or matra.

**Rule**: Before the five vargas (क-वर्ग to म-वर्ग), anusvara is pronounced as the nasal of that varga.

| Varga | Anusvara Pronunciation | Example |
|-------|------------------------|---------|
| क-वर्ग (क, ख, ग, घ, ङ) | "ङ" (velar nasal) | "अंग" (aṅga), "संग" (saṅga) |
| च-वर्ग (च, छ, ज, झ, ञ) | "ञ" (palatal nasal) | "अंजन" (añjana), "पञ्च" (pañca) |
| ट-वर्ग (ट, ठ, ड, ढ, ण) | "ण" (retroflex nasal) | "अण्डा" (aṇḍā), "मण्डल" (maṇḍala) |
| त-वर्ग (त, थ, द, ध, न) | "न" (dental nasal) | "अन्त" (anta), "सन्त" (santa) |
| प-वर्ग (प, फ, ब, भ, म) | "म" (bilabial nasal) | "अम्बर" (ambara), "संभव" (sambhava) |

**Before य, र, ल, व, श, ष, स, ह**: Anusvara is pronounced as a generic nasal (often "न्" or "म्" depending on region).  
Examples: "संयोग" (saṃyoga), "संरक्षण" (saṃrakṣaṇ), "संवाद" (saṃvāda), "संशय" (saṃśaya).

**Spelling Rule**: In modern standard Hindi, **anusvara is preferred over writing the full nasal consonant** in the middle of words.  
Write "अंगूर" not "अङ्गूर"; "संभाल" not "सम्भाल" (though both occur, anusvara is standard).

---

### Rule 6: Anunasik (अनुनासिक) — ँ (Chandrabindu)

**Chandrabindu** (ँ) indicates **vowel nasalisation** — the vowel is pronounced through the nose. It is used:

1. **When the vowel is long (दीर्घ) or a diphthong (ए, ऐ, ओ, औ)** and nasalised.  
   Examples: "हँसना" (haṃsnā), "मँहगा" (maṃhagā), "कुँआ" (kuṃā), "मैं" (maĩ), "हैं" (haĩ).

2. **When there is no space for anusvara** because the matra occupies the top space (e.g., "ई", "ऊ", "ए", "ऐ", "ओ", "औ" matras).  
   Example: "सीँह" (sīṃha), "कूँदना" (kūṃdnā), "सैंया" (saiyā̃).

**Exception**: In modern printing, chandrabindu is often replaced by anusvara (ं) for simplicity, but **exams test the distinction**.  
Correct: "हँसना" (chandrabindu on short "अ" with nasalisation) vs "हंसना" (non-nasal).  
Note: On short "अ" with nasalisation, chandrabindu is used: "हँसना", "बँद" (बंद with nasal nuance).

---

### Rule 7: Visarga (विसर्ग) — ः

**Visarga** (two dots : ) represents a voiceless glottal fricative (like a soft "h" sound). It occurs mainly in **Sanskrit loanwords (तत्सम शब्द)**.

Examples: "दुःख" (duḥkha), "मनः" (manaḥ), "पुनः" (punaḥ), "अतः" (ataḥ).

**Sandhi Rule**: Visarga changes before voiced sounds:
- Before voiced consonants (ग, घ, द, ध, ब, भ, य, र, ल, व, म, न, ण): becomes "ओ" sound (संधि: विसर्ग संधि).  
  "दुःख" → "दुःख" (no change before ख, voiceless)  
  "मनः + यत्" → "मनोयत्" (mano-yat)
- Before "श", "ष", "स": becomes "श्", "ष्", "स्" respectively (शट्व, षट्व, सत्व संधि).  
  "मनः + शक्तिः" → "मनश्शक्तिः" (manaśśaktiḥ)

**Exam Tip**: Visarga is **never used in native Hindi (तद्भव) words**. Write "दुख" (tadbhava), not "दुःख" — but "दुःख" is correct as a tatsama form.

---

### Rule 8: Nukta (नुक़्ता) — The Subscript Dot

A **dot below a consonant** (़) modifies its pronunciation to a sound borrowed from Persian/Arabic/English. It creates distinct phonemes.

| Base Letter | With Nukta | Sound | Example |
|-------------|------------|-------|---------|
| "क"         | "क़"       | /q/ (uvular k) | "क़लम" (qalam), "क़िला" (qilā) |
| "ख"         | "ख़"       | /x/ (voiceless velar fricative) | "ख़ुश" (xush), "ख़ास" (xās) |
| "ग"         | "ग़"       | /ɣ/ (voiced velar fricative) | "ग़ज़ल" (ġazal), "ग़लत" (ġalat) |
| "ज"         | "ज़"       | /z/ (voiced alveolar fricative) | "ज़मीन" (zamīn), "ज़रूरी" (zarūrī) |
| "ड"         | "ड़"       | /ɽ/ (retroflex flap) | "बड़ा" (baṛā), "कड़वा" (kaṛvā) |
| "ढ"         | "ढ़"       | /ɽʱ/ (aspirated retroflex flap) | "ढ़लना" (ḍhalnā) |
| "फ"         | "फ़"       | /f/ (voiceless labiodental fricative) | "फ़र्क" (farq), "फ़ाइल" (fāil) |
| "य"         | "य़"       | /y/ (rare, for Arabic ي) | "य़ा" (yā) — rarely used |

**Critical Rule**: **Nukta changes the word identity**.  
"कलम" (pen, from Sanskrit कलम) vs "क़लम" (pen, from Arabic قَلَم) — both exist but "क़लम" is standard for the Arabic loan.  
"जमीन" (incorrect spelling) vs "ज़मीन" (correct, land).  
"फर्क" (incorrect) vs "फ़र्क" (correct, difference).

**Exam Focus**: Questions often ask: "Which word is correctly spelled?" — options will differ only by nukta. Always use nukta for the Perso-Arabic phonemes.

---

### Rule 9: Conjunct Consonants (संयुक्त व्यंजन)

When two or more consonants combine without an intervening vowel, they form a **conjunct (संयुक्त व्यंजन)**. The first consonant(s) take half-form (with halant) and attach to the full form of the last consonant.

**Types**:
1. **Vertical stacking** (top-bottom): "क् + ष = क्ष", "स् + थ = स्थ", "द् + ध = द्ध", "श् + च = श्च"
2. **Horizontal merging** (side-by-side): "द् + ग = द्ग", "न् + य = न्य", "क् + त = क्त"
3. **Special forms**: "र्" as **reph** (र्‍) above the next consonant: "कर्म" (क + र् + म), "धर्म" (ध + र् + म).  
   "र्" as **rakar** (subscript hook) after a consonant with vertical stroke: "क्रोध" (क + र् + ओ + ध), "प्रथम" (प + र् + थ + म).

**Common Conjuncts to Memorise**:
- "क्ष" (क्+ष) — "क्षमा", "लक्ष्य"
- "त्र" (त्+र) — "त्रि", "पत्र"
- "ज्ञ" (ज्+ञ) — "ज्ञान", "विज्ञापन"
- "श्र" (श्+र) — "श्रेष्ठ", "श्रम"
- "द्य" (द्+य) — "अध्ययन", "विद्या"
- "क्त" (क्+त) — "शक्ति", "युक्ति"
- "ष्ट" (ष्+ट) — "षट्कोण", "दृष्टि"

**Spelling Rule**: Do not insert a vowel where a conjunct is required.  
Wrong: "शक्ति" → Correct: "शक्ति"  
Wrong: "विदया" → Correct: "विद्या"

---

### Rule 10: Doubled Consonants (द्वित्व व्यंजन)

When the same consonant appears twice in succession (first with halant, second with vowel), it is written as a **conjunct of the consonant with itself**. This indicates gemination (lengthened consonant sound).

Examples:
- "त्त" (त्+त) — "पत्ता" (pattā), "कित्ता" (kittā)
- "क्क" (क्+क) — "अक्कड़" (akkāṛ), "चक्कर" (cakkār)
- "म्म" (म्+म) — "अम्मा" (ammā), "सम्भावना" (sambhāvnā — here म्+भ, not doubled)
- "ल्ल" (ल्+ल) — "गल्ला" (gallā), "पल्ला" (pallā)
- "स्स" (स्+स) — "अस्सी" (assī), "कस्सी" (kassī)

**Distinction**: "पता" (address) vs "पत्ता" (leaf). "कटा" (cut) vs "कट्टा" (bamboo stick / thick).

**Exam Tip**: In dictation and error-spotting, missing a doubled consonant is a frequent error. Listen for the lengthened consonant sound.

---

### Rule 11: Use of "ड़" and "ढ़" (Retroflex Flap)

The letters "ड़" (ṛa) and "ढ़" (ṛha) are **allophones of "ड" and "ढ"** occurring **intervocalically** (between vowels) or after a vowel in native Hindi (tadbhava) words. They are written with nukta.

**Rule**: In tadbhava words, original "ड" and "ढ" become "ड़" and "ढ़" between vowels.
- Sanskrit "कड" → Hindi "कड़वा" (kaṛvā)
- Sanskrit "मड" → Hindi "मड़ई" (maṛaī)
- Sanskrit "पढ़" → Hindi "पढ़ना" (paṛhnā) — but "पढ़ाई" (paṛhāī) retains "ढ़"

**Exceptions (Tatsama words retain ड/ढ)**:
- "कड़क" (kaṛak — tadbhava) vs "कडक" (kaḍaka — tatsama, rare)
- "मण्डल" (maṇḍala — tatsama, retains ड) vs "मंडली" (maṇḍalī — often written with ड)
- "वृद्ध" (vṛddha — tatsama) vs "बूढ़ा" (būṛhā — tadbhava)

**Exam Rule**: If the word is a **native Hindi (तद्भव) form**, use "ड़/ढ़" intervocalically. If it is a **Sanskrit loan (तत्सम)**, retain "ड/ढ".  
Common test pairs: "बड़ा" (correct) vs "बडा" (wrong); "पढ़ना" (correct) vs "पढना" (wrong); but "मण्डल" (correct tatsama) vs "मंडल" (accepted modern spelling).

---

### Rule 12: "श", "ष", "स" — Three Sibilants

Hindi has three distinct sibilant sounds. Confusing them is a major spelling error.

| Letter | Place of Articulation | Sound | Common Words |
|--------|----------------------|-------|--------------|
| "श" (श) | Palatal (तालव्य) | /ʃ/ (like 'sh' in shoe) | "शहर", "शिक्षा", "आशा", "देश" |
| "ष" (ष) | Retroflex (मूर्धन्य) | /ʂ/ (retroflex 'sh') | "षट्कोण", "षड्यन्त्र", "पुष्प", "काष्ठ" |
| "स" (स) | Dental (दन्त्य) | /s/ (like 's' in sun) | "सूरज", "समय", "आस", "बस" |

**Distribution Rules**:
1. **"ष" occurs almost only in Tatsama (Sanskrit) words** and usually before retroflex consonants (ट, ठ, ड, ढ, ण) or at word-start in learned vocabulary.  
   Examples: "षट्", "षड्", "षण्", "षि" (rare).
2. **"श" is common in both Tatsama and Tadbhava words**.  
   Tatsama: "शिक्षा", "शान्ति"  
   Tadbhava: "शहर", "शेर", "शाम"
3. **"स" is the default sibilant in Tadbhava and foreign loans**.  
   "स्कूल", "स्टेशन", "साइकिल", "हस्पताल" (from Persian)

**Common Confusion Pairs (Exam Favourites)**:
- "शिक्षा" (correct) vs "षिक्षा" (wrong) vs "सिक्षा" (wrong)
- "देश" (correct) vs "देष" (wrong) vs "देश" — only "श" correct
- "पुष्प" (correct) vs "पुष्प" — "ष" before प् (labial) is standard tatsama
- "काष्ठ" (correct) vs "काष्ठ" — "ष" before ठ्
- "आशा" (correct) vs "आषा" (wrong)

**Mnemonic**: "ष" is the **retroflex specialist** — it appears mainly before ट, ठ, ड, ढ, ण, or in a few fixed tatsama words. Elsewhere, use "श" (palatal) or "स" (dental).

### Rule 13: "र" and "ड़" — Reph vs Rakar vs Flap

The letter "र" has three written forms depending on position:

1. **Reph (र्‍)** — superscript hook **above** the next consonant, when "र" is the **first** member of a conjunct.  
   Examples: "कर्म" (क + र्‍ + म), "धर्म" (ध + र्‍ + म), "बर्तन" (ब + र्‍ + त + न), "कर्नल" (क + र्‍ + न + ल).

2. **Rakar (subscript hook)** — attached **below** a consonant with a vertical stroke (क, ख, ग, घ, प, फ, ब, भ, म, य, व, श, ष, स), when "र" is the **second** member.  
   Examples: "क्रोध" (क + ्र + ओ + ध), "प्रथम" (प + ्र + थ + म), "ग्राम" (ग + ्र + ा + म), "श्रम" (श + ्र + म).

3. **Full form "र"** — when standalone or after a consonant without vertical stroke (ट, ठ, ड, ढ, ण, त, थ, द, ध, न, ल, ज, झ, ञ, च, छ).  
   Examples: "त्रि" (त + र + ि), "दर" (द + र), "पर" (प + र — but "प्र" uses rakar), "मरना" (म + र + न + ा).

**Critical Distinction**: "ड़" (ṛa) is a **different letter** (with nukta), pronounced as a retroflex flap. It never takes reph or rakar forms.  
- "बड़ा" (baṛā) — uses "ड़", not "र"  
- "कड़वा" (kaṛvā) — uses "ड़"  
- "बढ़ना" (baṛhnā) — uses "ढ़"

**Exam Trap**: Options may show "बडा", "बडा", "बड़ा", "बडा" — only "बड़ा" (with nukta on ड) is correct for the adjective "big".

---

### Rule 14: "य" and "य़" — Semivowel vs Nukta Form

The letter "य" (ya) is a palatal semivowel /j/. With nukta, "य़" represents /y/ in Arabic loans (rarely used; "य" usually suffices).

**Conjunct Behaviour of "य"**:
- As second member: takes **subscript form (य़-like hook)** after consonants with vertical stroke: "क्या" (क + ्य + ा), "प्यार" (प + ्य + ा + र), "व्यापार" (व + ्य + ा + प + ा + र).
- After consonants without vertical stroke: full "य" appears: "त्याग" (त + य + ा + ग), "न्याय" (न + य + ा + य), "स्याही" (स + य + ा + ह + ी).

**Spelling Rule**: The **half-form of "य" (the subscript hook) is written only under consonants that have a vertical right stroke**.  
Wrong: "तयाग" → Correct: "त्याग"  
Wrong: "नयाय" → Correct: "न्याय"  
Wrong: "सयाही" → Correct: "स्याही"

**Nukta "य़"**: Appears in a few Arabic words like "य़ा" (yā), "य़तीम" (yatīm). In modern Hindi, plain "य" is used: "यतीम". Exams rarely test "य़".

---

### Rule 15: Word-Boundary and Sandhi Spelling Rules (संधि-वर्तनी)

In Hindi writing, **sandhi (euphonic combination) is usually not reflected in spelling** — words are written separately. This differs from Sanskrit.

**Rule**: Write compound words and postpositional phrases as **separate words** unless they have become a single lexical item.

| Type | Sanskrit Style (Not Used) | Hindi Standard Spacing |
|------|---------------------------|------------------------|
| Noun + Postposition | "रामेण" | "राम ने" |
| Verb + Auxiliary | "गयाआ" | "गया है" |
| Compound (Samanadhikarana) | "महापुरुष" | "महा पुरुष" (but "महापुरुष" is now a single word) |
| Reduplication | "चलतचलत" | "चलते चलते" |

**Fixed Compounds (Written as One Word)**:
- "घरबार", "रोजगार", "आमदनी", "जिम्मेदारी", "किरायेदारी"
- "उत्तरप्रदेश" (but "उत्तर प्रदेश" also accepted; official name is one word)

**Prefixes (उपसर्ग) — Usually Joined**:
- "अन्याय", "असत्य", "अहिंसा", "दुराचार", "सुशिक्षा", "प्रधान", "उपाध्यक्ष"
- Exception: "वि + विद्या" → "विद्या" (not "विविद्या"); "अ + कर" → "अकर" (rare).

**Suffixes (प्रत्यय) — Usually Joined**:
- "लड़कपन", "सुन्दरता", "पढ़ाई", "लिखवैया", "खिलौना", "बड़प्पन"

**Exam Focus**: Questions test whether you incorrectly join or separate words.
- Wrong: "रामने", "गयाहै", "चलतेचलते"
- Correct: "राम ने", "गया है", "चलते चलते"
- Wrong: "सुन्दर ता", "लड़का पन"
- Correct: "सुन्दरता", "लड़कपन"

**Special Case**: "कोई" + "नहीं" → "कोई नहीं" (two words). "कुछ" + "नहीं" → "कुछ नहीं".  
But "कोईनहीं" is a common error.

### Summary Checklist for Exam

1. **Vowel length** — "कल" vs "काल", "दिन" vs "दीन"
2. **Matra placement** — "कि" (pre-base), "की" (post-base)
3. **Anusvara vs Chandrabindu** — "अंगूर" (ं), "हँसना" (ँ)
4. **Nukta letters** — "क़लम", "ज़मीन", "फ़र्क", "बड़ा", "पढ़ना"
5. **Conjuncts** — "शक्ति", "विद्या", "ज्ञान", "क्षमा"
6. **Doubled consonants** — "पत्ता", "चक्कर", "अम्मा"
7. **Sibilants** — "शिक्षा" (श), "पुष्प" (ष), "सूरज" (स)
8. **र forms** — Reph (कर्म), Rakar (क्रोध), Full (त्रि) — vs "ड़" (बड़ा)
9. **य conjuncts** — "क्या", "त्याग", "न्याय"
10. **Word spacing** — "राम ने", "गया है", "लड़कपन"

Memorise the **high-frequency error pairs** listed in each rule. Practice writing 20 words daily from dictation focusing on these rules. In the exam, read each option letter-by-letter before selecting.
