Key Facts — Letters and Spelling (वर्ण और वर्तनी)

1. The Hindi alphabet is divided into two main groups: "स्वर" (vowels) and "व्यंजन" (consonants). There are 11 vowels and 33 consonants in the standard Devanagari chart.

2. The 11 vowels ("अ आ इ ई उ ऊ ऋ ए ऐ ओ औ") are independent letters that can start a word or follow a consonant. Each vowel has a corresponding "मात्रा" (vowel sign) used when the vowel follows a consonant.

3. Vowel signs ("ा ि ी ु ू ृ े ै ो ौ") attach to the consonant's top, bottom, left, or right. For example, "क" + "ा" = "का", "क" + "ि" = "कि". The inherent "अ" sound of a consonant is cancelled when any other vowel sign is added.

4. The vowel "अ" has no separate sign; it is the inherent sound of every consonant. To remove this inherent "अ", the "हलन्त" sign ("्") is placed below the consonant (e.g., "क्").

5. Consonants are grouped by place of articulation: "कवर्ग" (velar), "चवर्ग" (palatal), "टवर्ग" (retroflex), "तवर्ग" (dental), "पवर्ग" (labial). Each group has five letters: unaspirated voiceless, aspirated voiceless, unaspirated voiced, aspirated voiced, and nasal.

6. The semivowels "य र ल व" and the sibilants "श ष स" plus "ह" are arranged after the five vargas. "क्ष", "त्र", "ज्ञ", "श्र" are traditional conjuncts often taught as additional letters.

7. "अनुस्वार" ("ं") represents a nasal sound homorganic with the following consonant. Before "कवर्ग" it sounds like "ङ", before "चवर्ग" like "ञ", before "टवर्ग" like "ण", before "तवर्ग" like "न", before "पवर्ग" like "म". Example: "संग" (saṅga), "संचय" (sañcaya).

8. "अनुनासिक" (nasalisation) is marked by "चन्द्रबिन्दु" ("ँ") above a vowel when space permits (e.g., "हँसना", "मुँह"). If the vowel has a top-line sign ("ा े ै ो ौ"), "अनुस्वार" ("ं") is used instead (e.g., "हाँसना" is incorrect; correct: "हँसना"; "मैं" uses "ँ" because "ै" occupies the top).

9. "विसर्ग" ("ः") adds a voiceless breath after a vowel, echoing the vowel quality. Example: "दुःख" (duḥkha), "मनः" (manaḥ). It occurs mainly in Sanskrit loanwords.

10. "संयुक्त व्यंजन" (conjunct consonants) are formed when two or more consonants join without an intervening vowel. The first consonant loses its inherent "अ" and takes half-form or ligature. Example: "क् + ष = क्ष", "त् + र = त्र", "ज् + ञ = ज्ञ".

11. Half-forms are created by dropping the vertical stroke of the consonant: "क्" (from "क"), "त्" (from "त"), "न्" (from "न"), "म्" (from "म"). Some consonants have special ligatures: "द् + द = द्द", "क् + त = क्त", "श् + च = श्च".

12. The letter "र" has two special forms in conjuncts: (a) "र्" as first member → superscript "र्" (रेफ) above the next consonant: "र्क", "र्त", "र्प". (b) "र" as second member → subscript diagonal stroke below the preceding consonant: "क्र", "त्र", "प्र", "ग्र".

13. "नुक्ता" (a dot below a letter) modifies certain consonants for Perso-Arabic sounds: "क़" (q), "ख़" (x), "ग़" (ɣ), "ज़" (z), "ड़" (ṛ), "ढ़" (ṛh), "फ़" (f). In pure Sanskrit words, nuktā is not used.

14. Spelling rule: "अनुस्वार" ("ं") is used before conjunct consonants and at word-end. "चन्द्रबिन्दु" ("ँ") is used before single consonants when the vowel has space above. Example: "संगति" (saṅgati) but "सँग" (saṅga — colloquial nasalisation).

15. Spelling rule: The vowel "ऋ" (ṛ) in Sanskrit loans often becomes "रि" or "इर" in Hindi. Example: "ऋषि" → "ऋषि" (retained), "कृष्ण" → "कृष्ण" (retained), but "ऋण" often written "रिण" or "ऋण".

16. Spelling rule: "श", "ष", "स" distinction — "श" (palatal) in "शब्द", "शेर"; "ष" (retroflex) in "षट्कोण", "षड्यंत्र"; "स" (dental) in "सब", "समय". Misusing them changes meaning or marks non-standard spelling.

17. Spelling rule: "ड़" and "ढ़" are flap consonants occurring medially or finally, never initially. They derive from "ड" and "ढ" with nuktā. Example: "बड़ा", "सीढ़ी". Writing "बडा" or "सीढी" is incorrect.

18. Spelling rule: "य" and "े" (e-mātrā) confusion — "देना" (denā) uses "े", "दया" (dayā) uses "य". The vowel sign "े" sits above the consonant; "य" is a full consonant.

19. Spelling rule: "व" and "ो" (o-mātrā) confusion — "वो" (vo) uses "ो" on "व"; "वो" as pronoun is spelled "वो". "वो" (vo) vs "वो" (same spelling, context decides). "व" as consonant vs "ो" as vowel sign.

20. Spelling rule: Gemination (doubling) shown by conjunct: "पत्ता" (pattā) = "प् + त", not "पतत". "अक्कड़" (akkāṛ) = "अ् + क् + क". Single consonant between vowels stays single: "पता" (patā).

21. Common mistake: Writing "क्युं" for "क्यों" — correct is "क् + यो + ँ" → "क्यों". The vowel "ो" goes on "य", then "ँ" above.

22. Common mistake: "जहाँ" vs "जहा" — "ँ" marks nasalisation on "ा"; omitting it changes pronunciation and is non-standard.

23. Common mistake: "सँभालना" (correct, nasalised "अ") vs "संभालना" (non-nasalised). Many verbs with "सं-" prefix retain nasalisation: "सँभालना", "सँग", "सँदेश" (though "संदेश" is also accepted).

24. Common mistake: "है" vs "हैं" — plural marker "ँ" on "ै". "वह है" (singular), "वे हैं" (plural). Omitting "ँ" is a grammatical error.

25. Common mistake: "कहा" (kahā — said) vs "कहाँ" (kahā̃ — where). The "ँ" distinguishes past tense verb from interrogative adverb. Similarly "जाना" (jānā — to go) vs "जान" (jān — life/knowing stem).
