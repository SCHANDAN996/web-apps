Adjectives and Verbs

Introduction

In Hindi grammar, two word-classes carry the core meaning of every sentence: विशेषण (adjective) and क्रिया (verb). For SSC GD and similar 10th-level exams you must recognise them quickly, spot agreement errors, and choose the correct form in fill-in-the-blank and error-detection questions. This lesson gives clear definitions, six worked examples, and a list of common mistakes — all using only standard Hindi forms that appear in NCERT textbooks and official exam papers.

1. विशेषण (Adjective)

Definition  
A विशेषण qualifies a संज्ञा (noun) or सर्वनाम (pronoun). It answers the questions "कैसा?" (what kind?), "कितना?" (how much?), or "कौन-सा?" (which one?).

Main types (for exam purposes)

- गुणवाचक विशेषण (Qualitative) — describes quality: "अच्छा", "बड़ा", "लाल", "मीठा"  
- संख्यावाचक विशेषण (Quantitative) — gives number or quantity: "एक", "दो", "कई", "थोड़ा", "बहुत"  
- सार्वनामिक विशेषण (Demonstrative/Possessive/Interrogative) — points out or asks: "यह", "वह", "मेरा", "तेरा", "कौन-सा", "जिसका"

Agreement rule (must-know)  
In Hindi the विशेषण agrees with the noun it qualifies in लिंग (gender), वचन (number), and कारक (case).  
Example: "अच्छा लड़का" (masculine singular direct) → "अच्छे लड़के" (masculine plural direct) → "अच्छी लड़की" (feminine singular direct).

Invariable adjectives  
Some adjectives never change form: "कुछ", "बहुत", "थोड़ा", "काफी", "प्रत्येक", "हर". Treat them as fixed words.

2. क्रिया (Verb)

Definition  
A क्रिया expresses action, occurrence, or state of being. It tells what the subject does or experiences.

Main classifications (exam-relevant)

- कर्म के अनुसार (Transitivity)  
  - सकर्मक (Transitive) — action passes to an object: "खाना", "लिखना", "बनाना"  
  - अकर्मक (Intransitive) — no object: "सोना", "जाना", "हँसना", "उगना"

- काल (Tense) — three simple tenses plus perfect/continuous forms built with auxiliaries:  
  - वर्तमान काल (Present) — "पढ़ता है", "पढ़ रही है"  
  - भूत काल (Past) — "पढ़ा", "पढ़ी", "पढ़े"  
  - भविष्यत् काल (Future) — "पढ़ेगा", "पढ़ेगी", "पढ़ेंगे"

- पुरुष (Person) — प्रथम (I/we), मध्यम (you), उत्तम (he/she/it/they). Verb endings change accordingly.

- वचन (Number) — एकवचन (singular), बहुवचन (plural).

- लिंग (Gender) — पुल्लिंग (masculine), स्त्रीलिंग (feminine). In past tense and perfective participles the verb agrees with the subject (if intransitive) or object (if transitive and object is direct).

Auxiliary verbs  
"है", "हैं", "था", "थे", "थी", "होगा", "होगी", "होंगे", "होंगी" combine with participles to form compound tenses. Memorise the full paradigm for "जाना" and "करना" — they appear in almost every paper.

3. Six Worked Examples

Each example shows the Hindi sentence, the tested word in double quotes, and the reasoning.

Example 1 — Adjective agreement (gender & number)  
Sentence: "राम और श्याम अच्छे लड़के हैं।"  
Tested word: "अच्छे"  
Reasoning: Subject "राम और श्याम" is masculine plural. The qualitative adjective "अच्छा" takes the plural masculine direct ending े → "अच्छे". Option "अच्छा" (singular) or "अच्छी" (feminine) would be wrong.

Example 2 — Invariable adjective  
Sentence: "कृपया थोड़ा पानी दीजिए।"  
Tested word: "थोड़ा"  
Reasoning: "थोड़ा" is a quantitative adjective that does not change for gender or number. Even though "पानी" is masculine singular, "थोड़ा" stays "थोड़ा". Writing "थोड़े" or "थोड़ी" is a common error.

Example 3 — Transitive verb in past tense (object agreement)  
Sentence: "सीता ने पत्र लिखा।"  
Tested word: "लिखा"  
Reasoning: "लिखना" is transitive. In simple past the verb agrees with the direct object "पत्र" (masculine singular). Hence "लिखा". If the object were "चिट्ठी" (feminine), the form would be "लिखी". The subject "सीता" is marked with "ने" and does not control agreement here.

Example 4 — Intransitive verb in past tense (subject agreement)  
Sentence: "लड़कियाँ स्कूल गईं।"  
Tested word: "गईं"  
Reasoning: "जाना" is intransitive. In past tense the verb agrees with the subject "लड़कियाँ" (feminine plural) → "गईं". Using "गए" (masculine plural) or "गया" (masculine singular) would be incorrect.

Example 5 — Compound tense with auxiliary (present continuous)  
Sentence: "बच्चे खेल रहे हैं।"  
Tested word: "रहे हैं"  
Reasoning: Root "खेल" + रहा (participle) agrees with subject "बच्चे" (masculine plural) → "रहे". Auxiliary "हैं" matches plural. Common distractor: "रहा है" (singular) or "रही हैं" (feminine plural).

Example 6 — Future tense, third person plural  
Sentence: "किसान खेत जोतेंगे।"  
Tested word: "जोतेंगे"  
Reasoning: Root "जोत" + एँगे (future ending for masculine plural third person). For feminine plural it would be "जोतेंगी". For singular masculine "जोतेगा", singular feminine "जोएगी" (irregular). Exams often test the plural endings एँगे / एँगी.

4. Common Errors (Exam Traps)

1. Adjective-noun disagreement  
   Wrong: "वह सुन्दर लड़का है।" (सुन्दर is invariable, but many candidates write "सुन्दर" for feminine too — actually "सुन्दर" is invariable, so this sentence is correct. Better trap: "वह सुन्दर लड़की है।" — still correct because "सुन्दर" is invariable. Real trap: "वह अच्छी लड़का है।" — "अच्छी" is feminine, "लड़का" masculine.)  
   Correct: "वह अच्छा लड़का है।" / "वह अच्छी लड़की है।"

2. Using singular adjective with plural noun  
   Wrong: "ये किताब नया है।"  
   Correct: "ये किताबें नई हैं।" (नया → नई, plural feminine)

3. Treating invariable adjectives as variable  
   Wrong: "बहुतें लड़के आए।"  
   Correct: "बहुत लड़के आए।" ("बहुत" never changes)

4. Transitive past tense — agreeing with subject instead of object  
   Wrong: "मोहन ने सेब खाई।" (सेब masculine → खाया)  
   Correct: "मोहन ने सेब खाया।"

5. Intransitive past tense — agreeing with object (there is none)  
   Wrong: "पत्ते गिरा।" (पत्ते plural masculine → गिरे)  
   Correct: "पत्ते गिरे।"

6. Auxiliary mismatch in compound tenses  
   Wrong: "वह पढ़ रही है।" (subject "वह" masculine → पढ़ रहा है)  
   Correct: "वह पढ़ रहा है।" / "वह पढ़ रही है।" (if subject is feminine)

7. Future tense ending confusion  
   Wrong: "तुम जाओगे।" (तुम takes "जाओगे" — actually correct for masculine; feminine "जाओगी". But "तुम" with "जाएगा" is wrong.)  
   Watch: "आप जाएँगे" (honorific plural) vs "तू जाएगा" (intimate singular).

8. Confusing "होना" as main verb vs auxiliary  
   "वह डॉक्टर है।" (main verb, no participle)  
   "वह डॉक्टर बन रहा है।" (auxiliary "है" with participle "बन रहा")

9. Causative verb forms (often tested)  
   "बुलाना" (call) → "बुलवाना" (cause to call).  
   Wrong: "उसने मुझे बुलाया।" (simple) vs "उसने मुझे बुलवाया।" (causative — someone else called). Exams ask: "कौन-सा वाक्य सार्थक क्रिया का उदाहरण है?"

10. Negative particle placement  
    "नहीं" precedes the verb: "वह नहीं आया।" Not "वह आया नहीं।" (latter is poetic/emphatic, not standard exam Hindi).

5. Quick Revision Checklist

- Identify the noun → check gender & number → match adjective ending.  
- Spot "ने" → verb is transitive past → agree with direct object.  
- No "ने" + past tense → intransitive → agree with subject.  
- Auxiliary "है/हैं/था/थे/थी/होगा/होगी/होंगे/होंगी" must match subject in number & gender.  
- Invariable adjectives: "कुछ, बहुत, थोड़ा, काफी, प्रत्येक, हर, सुन्दर, साफ़, खराब" — never change.  
- Future endings: masculine plural एँगे, feminine plural एँगी, singular masculine एगा/एगा (irregular verbs vary).  
- Causative: root + वा/आवा (बनवाना, खिलवाना, पढ़वाना).  
- Negative: "नहीं" immediately before verb.

Practice Tip  
When you see a fill-in-the-blank, first mark the subject, object (if any), and tense marker. Then apply the agreement rule mechanically. Do not rely on "ear" — exam sentences often use unfamiliar vocabulary to test the rule, not vocabulary.
