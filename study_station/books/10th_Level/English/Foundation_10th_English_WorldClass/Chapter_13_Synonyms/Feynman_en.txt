🧒 FEYNMAN TECHNIQUE — Synonyms Explained to a 12-Year-Old

Imagine you have a box of crayons. You have "red", but you also have "scarlet", "crimson", and "cherry". They all colour almost the same — but each one is a slightly different shade. Synonyms are exactly like that: different words, nearly the same meaning.

Now here is the secret most students miss: **synonyms are "almost-same" words, not "exactly-same" words.** "Happy" and "delighted" both mean feeling good — but "delighted" is a much stronger feeling, like getting a new bicycle versus just finding a toffee. The examiner's favourite trick is to give you a word that is *close* but not the *closest*. Your job is to pick the crayon whose shade matches best.

🎯 The Daily-Life Example

Think of the word "big". In your daily life you already use its synonyms without knowing it:
- a **large** pizza
- a **huge** crowd at the fair
- an **enormous** elephant
- a **massive** mountain

You never say "an enormous toffee" or "a massive biscuit" — your brain already knows which synonym fits which situation. In the exam, do the same thing: put the option word into a simple sentence in your head. If it sounds natural in place of the given word, that's your answer.

🔑 The Unforgettable Trick — "The Sentence Swap Test"

Don't just stare at the word. **Swap it into a sentence.**

Question: Synonym of BRAVE
(a) Timid  (b) Cowardly  (c) Courageous  (d) Weak

Step 1: Make a sentence — "The soldier was **brave**."
Step 2: Swap each option in — "The soldier was courageous." ✔ Sounds right.
Step 3: Timid, cowardly, weak are all *opposites* — the examiner planted them because they belong to the same "family" of meaning (fear vs. courage). Same-family words are the biggest trap!

💡 Remember it like this: **"Family words are trap words."** If an option is related to the given word's topic but means the opposite, reject it first.

🧠 Why Your Brain Learns Synonyms Best in Groups

Don't memorise one word at a time. Learn word-families:
- HAPPY → joyful, cheerful, glad, delighted
- SAD → unhappy, gloomy, sorrowful, miserable
- FAST → quick, rapid, swift, speedy

One family learned can cover several exam questions at once. That's smart studying!

📝 Blurting Sheet Instruction

1. What a synonym is, in your own words.
