# Synonyms — Words That Mean the Same

## 🎯 Why This Chapter Matters

Synonym questions are among the most direct questions in the English sections of SSC, Railway, and Bank exams — a word in capital letters, four options, one correct meaning-match. No passage to read, no grammar to untangle. Just: *do you know this word?* That makes synonyms some of the **fastest marks** in the English section — if your vocabulary is ready, each question takes barely 15 seconds.

But here is the catch: examiners don't test random dictionary words. They test a **repeatable bank of words** — words like *abandon, candid, fragile, obsolete, reluctant* — that appear again and again across exams. Master the patterns in this chapter, and you convert vocabulary from a memory gamble into a scoring habit.

## 📌 Learning Objectives

By the end of this chapter, you will be able to:

1. Define what a synonym is and recognise how exams frame synonym questions.
2. Use **Greek and Latin roots** to decode unfamiliar words in the exam hall.
3. Apply the **Elimination Method** when you don't know the exact meaning.
4. Avoid the classic traps: near-synonyms, opposite-meaning decoys, and "sound-alike" words.
5. Build a personal revision habit using the 2-day review cycle.

## 1. What Exactly Is a Synonym?

A **synonym** is a word that has the **same or nearly the same meaning** as another word.

- **Happy** = Glad, Joyful, Cheerful
- **Begin** = Start, Commence, Initiate
- **Brave** = Courageous, Bold, Valiant

Notice the phrase *nearly the same*. Very few word pairs are 100% interchangeable in every sentence. "Begin" and "commence" mean the same thing, but "commence" is formal — you'd say "the ceremony commenced," not "the ceremony began" in an official notice. For exam purposes, you only need the **closest meaning** among the four options, not a perfect twin.

**How exams ask it:**

> Choose the word which is most nearly the **SAME** in meaning as: **ABANDON**
> (a) keep (b) forsake (c) cherish (d) protect

Here *forsake* (to give up/leave) matches *abandon*. Options (a), (c), (d) are all *opposites* — a classic trap pattern we'll meet again.

## 2. The Three Types of Synonym Questions

**Type 1 — Direct Synonym (most common):** One word given, pick the same-meaning option.
> **CANDID** → (a) frank (b) secretive (c) rude (d) clever → **frank**

**Type 2 — Synonym in a Sentence:** The word sits inside a sentence; context helps you.
> "The manager gave a **brief** reply." → (a) lengthy (b) short (c) angry (d) written → **short**

**Type 3 — Synonym + Antonym Confusion:** The paper asks for a synonym, but two options are *antonyms* of the given word. Read the instruction twice! Many students lose marks by picking the opposite because they misread "same" as "opposite."

**Golden habit:** The moment you see a vocabulary question, underline (mentally) whether it says **SAME** or **OPPOSITE**. This one-second check saves marks every single paper.

## 3. The Root-Word Superpower (Morphology)

You cannot memorise the whole dictionary — but you don't need to. Most tough English words are built from **Greek and Latin roots**. Learn one root, and you unlock 10–20 words at once.

| Root | Meaning | Derived Words (with meaning) |
|---|---|---|
| **bene** (Latin) | good | benefit (advantage), benevolent (kind), benediction (blessing) |
| **mal** (Latin) | bad | malicious (spiteful), malnutrition (bad nutrition), malfunction (faulty working) |
| **dict** (Latin) | say/speak | predict (say before), dictate (speak orders), verdict (decision spoken) |
| **port** (Latin) | carry | transport (carry across), export (carry out), portable (can be carried) |
| **chron** (Greek) | time | chronic (long-lasting), chronology (time order), synchronize (same time) |
| **phon** (Greek) | sound | telephone (far sound), symphony (sounds together), microphone (small-sound device) |
| **vid/vis** (Latin) | see | visible (can be seen), vision (sight), revise (see again) |
| **scrib/script** (Latin) | write | describe (write about), manuscript (handwritten), inscription (written on) |
| **auto** (Greek) | self | automatic (self-working), autobiography (self-life-writing), autograph (self-writing) |
| **bio** (Greek) | life | biology (study of life), biography (life-writing), antibiotic (against life of germs) |

**Example in action:** You see the word **BENEVOLENT** in the exam. You don't know it — but you know *bene = good*. Options: (a) cruel (b) kind (c) greedy (d) lazy. Only *kind* relates to "good." Answer found in 10 seconds, without ever having memorised the word.

## 5. The Elimination Method — When You Don't Know the Word

Even toppers meet unknown words. They survive using **elimination**:

1. **Guess the tone** — is the word positive or negative? (*Jubilant* sounds bright; *gloomy* sounds dark.)
2. **Strike out opposites** — if the word feels positive, delete negative options.
3. **Strike out unrelated words** — options from a different field (e.g., a legal word among emotion words).
4. **Choose between the last two** by root, prefix, or sound-memory.

**Worked example:** **FRAGILE** → (a) strong (b) delicate (c) heavy (d) rigid
- *Fragile* feels negative/weak → delete (a) strong and (c) heavy.
- *Rigid* means stiff — the opposite of breakable → delete (d).
- Left: **(b) delicate** ✓

### 🧠 Active Recall #4
Apply elimination to: **OBSOLETE** → (a) modern (b) outdated (c) useful (d) new. Which options did you delete first, and why?

## 6. 🧠 Mnemonic of the Chapter

**"SAME = Synonym, ANTI = Antonym"**
- **S**ynonym = **S**ame (both start with S!)
- **ANT**onym = **ANT**i = against = opposite

Never confuse the two instructions again — just remember: *"S goes with S."*

## ⚠️ Examiner's Trap Box

**Trap 1 — The Opposite Decoy:** The question asks for the SAME meaning, but 2–3 options are antonyms of the given word (e.g., ABANDON → keep, cherish, protect). Examiners know that hurried students grab a "related-feeling" word. *Defence:* read SAME/OPPOSITE twice.

**Trap 2 — The Sound-Alike:** Options that *sound* like the given word but mean something else. **ADAPT** (adjust) vs **ADOPT** (take up) vs **ADEPT** (skilful). *Defence:* never answer by sound; answer by meaning.

**Trap 3 — The Near-Synonym (partial match):** Two options look close, but one matches only *part* of the meaning. **FRUGAL** means careful with money — *thrifty* ✓, but *miserly* (hates spending, negative shade) is the trap. *Defence:* prefer the neutral, closest-meaning option over the extreme one.

## 🔁 Linguistic Bridge — Hindi से English

Hindi speakers often carry Hindi sentence habits into English. Watch these:

| ❌ Hindi-style English | ✅ Correct English |
|---|---|
| "What you are doing?" (क्या तुम कर रहे हो?) | "What **are you** doing?" |
| "He is having a car." (उसके पास गाड़ी है) | "He **has** a car." |
| "I am knowing the answer." (मैं जवाब जानता हूँ) | "I **know** the answer." |
| "Cousin brother" (चचेरा भाई) | Just "**cousin**" — English doesn't add brother/sister |
| "He told me that why are you late." | "He **asked** me why I was late." |

**Why this matters for synonyms:** words like *know, have, tell, say* appear in vocabulary questions too. If your mental Hindi translation is shaky, you misjudge the English meaning. Learn the word in an **English sentence**, not as a Hindi equivalent alone.
