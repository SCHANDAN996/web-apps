# Sentence Structure — The Skeleton of English

## 🎯 Why This Chapter Can Change Your Score

Every single question in the English section of SSC GD, SSC MTS, RRB Group D and State Police exams — whether it is Error Spotting, Fill in the Blanks, Sentence Improvement or Rearrangement — is secretly a **Sentence Structure** question. If you can see the skeleton of a sentence (Subject + Verb + Object), you can catch errors that other candidates never even notice. Toppers don't "feel" the answer; they **see the structure**. This chapter teaches you exactly that.

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this chapter, you will be able to:

1. Identify the **Subject, Verb and Object** in any sentence within seconds.
2. Recognise the **five basic sentence patterns** of English.
3. Classify sentences as **Simple, Compound, Complex and Compound-Complex**.
4. Apply the **12 Golden Rules** of sentence structure to error-spotting questions.
5. Avoid the typical mistakes Hindi speakers make in English word order.

## 1. What Is a Sentence?

A **sentence** is a group of words that expresses a **complete thought** and contains at least one **Subject** (who/what the sentence is about) and one **Verb** (what the subject does or is).

- ✅ *The boy runs.* (Subject: The boy | Verb: runs — complete thought)
- ❌ *Running in the park.* (No subject — incomplete thought; this is a **fragment**)

**The two compulsory parts:**

| Part | Question it answers | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Subject | Who or what? | *The train* arrived late. |
| Predicate (Verb + rest) | What about the subject? | The train *arrived late*. |

🧠 **Active Recall:** Close the book. What two things must every complete sentence have? Is "Under the table" a sentence? Why not?

## 2. The Core Trio: Subject – Verb – Object (SVO)

English follows a fixed word order: **Subject → Verb → Object**.

- *Ravi (S) eats (V) mangoes (O).*
- *The police (S) caught (V) the thief (O).*

**How to find them in an exam:**
1. Find the **verb** first (the action/state word).
2. Ask **"who/what + verb?"** → that is the **subject**.
3. Ask **"verb + whom/what?"** → that is the **object**.

Example: *The farmer grows wheat.*
- Verb = grows → Who grows? = the farmer (Subject) → Grows what? = wheat (Object).

⚠️ Not every sentence has an object. Verbs like *sleep, run, arrive, die* (intransitive verbs) take no object: *The baby sleeps.*

🧠 **Active Recall:** In "The students solved the puzzle quickly," find the verb, subject and object. Does "quickly" answer "what?" — or something else?

## 3. The Five Basic Sentence Patterns

Every English sentence, however long, is built on one of these five frames:

| # | Pattern | Example |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | S + V | Birds *fly*. |
| 2 | S + V + O | She *reads novels*. |
| 3 | S + V + Complement | He *is a doctor*. / The soup *tastes good*. |
| 4 | S + V + Indirect Object + Direct Object | Mother *gave me a gift*. |
| 5 | S + V + O + Complement | We *elected him captain*. |

**Exam tip:** In Pattern 3, the verb is a **linking verb** (is, seem, become, look, taste, feel). After a linking verb we use an **adjective**, not an adverb: *The food smells good* ✅ (not "well").

---

## 4. Phrase, Clause, Sentence — Know the Difference

- **Phrase** = a group of words with **no subject-verb pair**: *in the morning, running fast, a tall man.*
- **Clause** = a group of words **with** a subject-verb pair:
  - **Independent clause** — stands alone: *She sings well.*
  - **Dependent clause** — cannot stand alone; begins with words like *because, when, if, although, which*: *because she was tired…* (incomplete feeling!)

🧠 **Active Recall:** "Although he worked hard" — phrase, dependent clause or independent clause? What word makes it dependent?

## 5. The Four Types of Sentences (by Structure)

| Type | Structure | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Simple** | 1 independent clause | *Ram plays cricket.* |
| **Compound** | 2+ independent clauses joined by **FANBOYS** (for, and, nor, but, or, yet, so) | *Ram plays cricket, and Sita plays tennis.* |
| **Complex** | 1 independent + 1+ dependent clause | *Although it rained, the match continued.* |
| **Compound-Complex** | 2+ independent + 1+ dependent | *Although it rained, the match continued, and the crowd cheered.* |

**🔑 Mnemonic — "FANBOYS":** the seven coordinating conjunctions that join independent clauses in a compound sentence — **F**or, **A**nd, **N**or, **B**ut, **O**r, **Y**et, **S**o.

**Joining words for dependent clauses (subordinating conjunctions):** because, since, although, though, when, while, if, unless, until, after, before, as, that, which, who.

🧠 **Active Recall:** "He was ill, so he stayed at home." — simple, compound or complex? Which FANBOYS word joins the clauses?

## 6. Sentences by Purpose

| Purpose | Name | Example |
|---|---|---|
| States something | Assertive/Declarative | *The exam is on Monday.* |
| Asks something | Interrogative | *Is the exam on Monday?* |
| Commands/requests | Imperative | *Open the door.* (subject "you" is hidden) |
| Shows strong feeling | Exclamatory | *What a beautiful day!* |

## ⚠️ Examiner's Trap — The 12 Golden Rules of Sentence Structure

1. **Every sentence needs a finite verb.** ❌ *He being late.* (a fragment — "being" is not a finite verb) → ✅ *He was late.*
2. **A dependent clause alone is a fragment.** ❌ *Because he was ill.* (standing alone) → ✅ *Because he was ill, he stayed at home.*
3. **Two independent clauses joined by only a comma = comma splice.** ❌ *He came, he sat down.* → ✅ *He came, and he sat down.* / *He came; he sat down.*
4. **After a linking verb, use an adjective.** *She looks beautiful* (not "beautifully").
5. **The subject is never inside a prepositional phrase.** *The quality of the mangoes **was** good* (subject = quality, not mangoes).
6. **Inversion after negative openers:** *Never **have I** seen such a sight.* / *Hardly **had he** arrived when…*
7. **Embedded questions use statement order:** *I don't know where he **is**.* (not "where is he").
8. **"There" is not the subject.** *There **are** three books on the table* (subject = books).
9. **Each/Every/One of + plural noun takes a singular verb.** *Each of the boys **has** a pen.*
10. **Correlative pairs must be parallel:** *Neither Ram **nor** Shyam*, *not only…**but also***, *either…**or***.
11. **A sentence must not shift tense without reason.** ❌ *He entered the room and sits down.* → ✅ *He entered the room and sat down.*
12. **Imperative sentences have a hidden "you".** Tag: *Open the window, **will you?***

**Common traps in exams:** (a) an intervening phrase hiding the real subject (Rule 5), (b) a fragment dressed up as a full sentence (Rules 1–2), (c) adjective–adverb swap after linking verbs (Rule 4).

## 🔁 Linguistic Bridge — Hindi से English

| हम हिंदी की आदत से कहते हैं ❌ | सही English ✅ |
|---|---|
| What you are doing? | What **are you** doing? (question में verb पहले) |
| I am having a car. | I **have** a car. (have = ownership, continuous नहीं) |
| He is more taller than me. | He is **taller** than me. (double comparative गलत) |
| My all friends came. | **All my** friends came. |
| She told that… (बिना object) | She **told me** that… (tell के बाद object ज़रूरी; say के बाद नहीं) |
| I and Ravi went. | **Ravi and I** went. (अंग्रेज़ी में "I" आख़िर में) |

**याद रखें:** हिंदी में क्रिया अंत में आती है (राम आम खाता है), English में क्रिया बीच में (Ram **eats** mangoes)। Word order बदलना ही आधी grammar है।
