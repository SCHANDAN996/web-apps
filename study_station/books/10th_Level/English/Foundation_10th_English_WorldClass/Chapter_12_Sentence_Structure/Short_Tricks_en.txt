# 🪄 Short Tricks + Skip Strategy — Sentence Structure

---

## Memory Boxes (Mnemonics & Tricks)

**📦 Box 1 — The SVA Test (Subject-Verb Agreement)**
> **"Cross out the middle, check the ends."**
> In "The box of chocolates **is** on the table," cross out "of chocolates." Now read: "The box is on the table." The verb must match the real subject, not the nearest noun.

**📦 Box 2 — FANBOYS (Coordinating Conjunctions)**
> **F**or, **A**nd, **N**or, **B**ut, **O**r, **Y**et, **S**o
> These 7 join two complete sentences. If you see a comma + one of these, check that BOTH sides can stand alone.

**📦 Box 3 — The "Because" Rule**
> **"Because starts a reason, never a sentence alone."**
> "Because I was late." ❌ (incomplete)
> "Because I was late, I missed the bus." ✅

**📦 Box 4 — W-H Family for Questions**
> **"W-H words flip the verb."**
> Statement: "You **are going**." → Question: "Where **are** you **going**?"
> The helping verb jumps BEFORE the subject. Never say "Where you are going?"

**📦 Box 5 — There Is / There Are**
> **"Look AFTER 'there' for the real subject."**
> "There **is** a boy." / "There **are** three boys."
> The noun after the verb decides is/are — not the word "there."

**📦 Box 6 — The Fragment Finder**
> **"No subject + no verb + no complete thought = Fragment."**
> Ask three questions: WHO? DID WHAT? COMPLETE IDEA? If any answer is missing, it's a fragment.

**📦 Box 7 — Run-on Repair Kit (3 fixes)**
> **"Period, Semicolon, or FANBOYS."**
> Run-on: "I was tired I slept early."
> Fix 1: "I was tired**.** I slept early."
> Fix 2: "I was tired**;** I slept early."
> Fix 3: "I was tired**, so** I slept early."

**📦 Box 8 — Simple, Compound, Complex in One Line**
> **"1 idea = Simple | 2 equal ideas = Compound | 1 boss + 1 servant = Complex."**
> Simple: "She sings."
> Compound: "She sings, and he dances."
> Complex: "She sings **while** he dances." (while = servant marker)

**📦 Box 9 — Servant Words (Subordinating Conjunctions)**
> **"AAAWWUBBIS"** — **A**fter, **A**lthough, **A**s, **W**hen, **W**hile, **U**ntil, **B**ecause, **B**efore, **I**f, **S**ince
> Any clause starting with these CANNOT stand alone. It needs a main clause.

**📦 Box 10 — Question Tag Polarity**
> **"Positive sentence → Negative tag | Negative sentence → Positive tag."**
> "You are coming, **aren't you**?" / "He isn't late, **is he**?"

**📦 Box 11 — Do/Does/Did for Emphasis & Questions**
> **"DO + base verb, never DO + past form."**
> "He **does** go" ✅ | "He does went" ❌
> After do/does/did, the main verb always returns to its base form.

**📦 Box 12 — The Inversion Signal**
> **"Negative starters flip the order."**
> "Never, Rarely, Hardly, Seldom, No sooner" at the start → helping verb before subject.
> "**Never have I** seen such a thing." (Not: "Never I have seen…")

**📦 Box 13 — Active vs Passive in 3 Seconds**
> **"Find the doer. Doer first = Active. Receiver first = Passive."**
> "Ram ate the mango." (Ram = doer, first → Active)
> "The mango was eaten by Ram." (mango = receiver, first → Passive)

**📦 Box 14 — The "ING Trap" Detector**
> **"An -ing word alone is NOT a verb."**
> "The boy running in the park." ❌ (fragment — "running" needs a helper)
> "The boy **is** running in the park." ✅

**📦 Box 15 — Order of Adjectives (OSASCOMP)**
> **O**pinion, **S**ize, **A**ge, **S**hape, **C**olour, **O**rigin, **M**aterial, **P**urpose
> "A beautiful **(O)** small **(S)** old **(A)** round **(S)** red **(C)** Indian **(O)** wooden **(M)** table."
> Exams usually test only 2–3 adjectives: opinion comes before size, size before colour.

## ⏭️ Skip Strategy (When to Move On)

**Tip 1 — Skip long "find the error" sentences with 4+ underlined parts on the first pass.**
These are 45-second questions. If the error doesn't jump out in one reading, mark it and return. Never re-read the same sentence more than twice — your brain starts "hearing" the wrong version as correct.

**Tip 2 — Skip sentence rearrangement (PQRS) questions if the opening sentence isn't obvious in 20 seconds.**
Rearrangement questions eat time. Solve them only if you can instantly spot the first or last sentence. Otherwise, attempt them in the final minutes using the "mandatory pair" trick (find two parts that MUST be together — that alone often eliminates 2 options).

**Tip 3 — Never skip one-word structure questions (is/are, has/have, do/does).**
These are 15-second fact-based questions — pure marks. The subject-verb agreement and there is/there are types are the fastest points in the whole section. Do these FIRST, then tackle the longer ones.

---

## ⚡ The 15/45-Second Split (Quick Reminder)

| 15-Second Questions (Fact-based) | 45-Second Questions (Inference-based) |
|---|---|
| Choose is/are, was/were | Find the error in a long sentence |
| Identify simple/compound/complex | Sentence rearrangement (PQRS) |
| Pick the correct question tag | Choose the best-combined sentence |
| Spot the fragment (short sentences) | Active ↔ Passive with tense change |

**Golden Rule:** If a question needs you to hold more than two clauses in your head at once, it's a 45-second question. Budget accordingly.
