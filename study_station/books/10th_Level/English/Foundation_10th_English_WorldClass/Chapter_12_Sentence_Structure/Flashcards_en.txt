Card 1
Front: What are the two essential parts of every complete sentence?
Back: Subject (who/what the sentence is about) and Predicate (what the subject does or is). Example: "Ravi (subject) runs fast (predicate)."

Card 2
Front: What is an independent clause?
Back: A group of words with a subject and verb that expresses a complete thought and can stand alone as a sentence. Example: "She passed the exam."

Card 3
Front: What is a dependent (subordinate) clause?
Back: A clause with a subject and verb that cannot stand alone; it begins with a subordinating conjunction like because, although, when, if. Example: "Because it rained…" — incomplete on its own.

Card 4
Front: Mnemonic — Name the 7 coordinating conjunctions using FANBOYS.
Back: For, And, Nor, But, Or, Yet, So. They join two independent clauses (use a comma before them).

Card 5
Front: What is a simple sentence? Give an example.
Back: One independent clause — one subject + one predicate. Example: "The boy kicked the ball."

Card 6
Front: What is a compound sentence? Give an example.
Back: Two or more independent clauses joined by a coordinating conjunction or semicolon. Example: "I was tired, so I slept early."

Card 7
Front: What is a complex sentence? Give an example.
Back: One independent clause + at least one dependent clause. Example: "Although it was late, she kept studying."

Card 8
Front: What is a compound-complex sentence?
Back: At least two independent clauses + at least one dependent clause. Example: "When the bell rang, the students stood up, and they left the class."

Card 9
Front: Examiner's Trap — "The list of items is/are on the table." Which verb is correct and why?
Back: "Is" — the subject is "list" (singular), not "items." Words in an intervening phrase (of items) do not change the subject's number.

Card 10
Front: What is a sentence fragment?
Back: An incomplete sentence missing a subject, a verb, or a complete thought. Example: "Running down the street." (Who is running? Not stated.)

Card 11
Front: What is a run-on sentence, and how do you fix it?
Back: Two independent clauses joined without proper punctuation or conjunction. Fix with a full stop, semicolon, or comma + FANBOYS. Wrong: "He came he sat." Right: "He came, and he sat."

Card 12
Front: Examiner's Trap — "Neither of the boys has/have done his homework." Which is correct?
Back: "Has" — "neither of," "either of," and "each of" always take a singular verb, even though a plural noun follows.

Card 13
Front: What is the basic word order of an English statement?
Back: Subject + Verb + Object (SVO). Example: "She (S) reads (V) books (O)." Hindi uses SOV, which causes errors like "She books reads."

Card 14
Front: Mnemonic — How do you remember the correct order of adjectives before a noun?
Back: OSASCOMP: Opinion, Size, Age, Shape, Colour, Origin, Material, Purpose. Example: "a beautiful small old round red Indian wooden dining table."

Card 15
Front: What is a phrase? How is it different from a clause?
Back: A phrase is a group of words without a subject-verb pair (e.g., "in the morning"). A clause always has a subject and a verb.

Card 16
Front: In questions with "do/does/did," what form does the main verb take?
Back: The base form (V1). "Does he go?" — not "Does he goes?" The helping verb already carries the tense and the -s.

Card 17
Front: What is an imperative sentence, and what is its subject?
Back: A command or request; the subject "you" is understood but not written. Example: "Open the door." = "(You) open the door."

Card 18
Front: "One of my friends is/are coming." Which verb is correct?
Back: "Is" — the subject is "one," not "friends." Pattern: "One of + plural noun + singular verb."

Card 19
Front: What punctuation joins two closely related independent clauses without a conjunction?
Back: A semicolon (;). Example: "The rain stopped; the match resumed."

Card 20
Front: How do you quickly identify the subject in a sentence starting with "There is/are"?
Back: The real subject comes after the verb. "There are three books on the desk" — subject is "books," so the verb is plural ("are").
