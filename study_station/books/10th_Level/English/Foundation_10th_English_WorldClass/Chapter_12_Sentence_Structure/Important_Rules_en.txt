# 📋 Important Grammar Rules & Tips — Sentence Structure

## (a) 12 High-Yield Rules

| # | Rule | Example | Examiner's Trap |
|---|------|---------|-----------------|
| 1 | Every sentence needs a **Subject + Verb**; a group of words without both is a fragment. | ❌ "Running in the park." ✅ "She is running in the park." | Long phrases are made to *look* like sentences — check for a finite verb. |
| 2 | The verb agrees with the **subject**, not with the noun nearest to it (intervening phrase). | "The quality of the mangoes **is** good." | "of the mangoes" sits next to the verb to pull you toward "are". |
| 3 | Two subjects joined by **and** take a plural verb. | "Ram and Shyam **are** friends." | Exception trap: "Bread and butter **is** my breakfast" (one idea = singular). |
| 4 | Subjects joined by **or / either…or / neither…nor** — verb agrees with the **nearer** subject. | "Neither the teacher nor the students **were** present." | The plural noun is placed far away so you match the wrong subject. |
| 5 | **Each, every, everyone, somebody, nobody** take a singular verb. | "Everybody **is** ready." | "Everybody are" *sounds* right in speech — sound-right trap. |
| 6 | **Collective nouns** (team, committee, jury) are singular when acting as one unit. | "The team **has** won the match." | If members act separately, use plural: "The jury **were** divided." |
| 7 | A sentence must have **one clear tense logic** — don't shift tense without reason. | ❌ "He came and sits down." ✅ "He came and sat down." | Tense is switched mid-sentence to test sequence of tenses. |
| 8 | In questions, the helping verb comes **before** the subject (inversion). | "What **are you** doing?" (not "What you are doing?") | Hindi word order ("आप क्या कर रहे हैं?") pushes learners to skip inversion. |
| 9 | **There + verb** agrees with the real subject that follows it. | "There **is** a book on the table." / "There **are** two books." | "There is two books" is common in speech — don't copy it. |
| 10 | A clause beginning with **because / although / since** cannot stand alone. | ❌ "Because he was ill." ✅ "Because he was ill, he stayed home." | A dependent clause is presented as a complete sentence (fragment trap). |
| 11 | Use **only one negative** in a clause (no double negative). | ❌ "He didn't do nothing." ✅ "He didn't do anything." | Double negatives sound emphatic in Hindi but are wrong in standard English. |
| 12 | Modifiers must sit **next to** the word they describe. | ❌ "She almost drove her kids to school every day." (if she did drive) ✅ "She drove her kids to school almost every day." | "Almost / only / nearly" are moved around to change the meaning — read carefully. |

## (b) Time Management — The 15/45-Second Rule

| Type | What it looks like | Time | Strategy |
|------|-------------------|------|----------|
| **Fact-based (15 sec)** | Spot the error in agreement, tense, fragment; fill in the correct verb; identify subject/predicate. | 15 sec | Apply the rule directly. If you know the rule, answer and move on — never re-read twice. |
| **Inference-based (45 sec)** | Sentence rearrangement (PQRS), choosing the best-improved sentence, identifying the correctly framed sentence among four. | 45 sec | Eliminate options with rule violations first; then check meaning and flow. If still stuck after 45 sec, mark and skip. |

**Rule of thumb:** If the question tests *one rule*, it's a 15-second question. If it tests *order, meaning, or comparison of full sentences*, give it 45 seconds — no more.

## (c) Morphology — Root, Affix, Example

| Part | Meaning | Example | Breakdown |
|------|---------|---------|-----------|
| **Root** | Core word carrying the main meaning | *struct* (build) | structure, construct, instruct |
| **Prefix** | Added before the root; changes meaning | *re-* (again) | re + build = rebuild |
| **Suffix** | Added after the root; often changes word class | *-tion* (makes a noun) | construct + tion = construction (verb → noun) |
| **Combined** | Prefix + root + suffix | *un-* + *predict* + *-able* | unpredictable = "not able to be predicted" |

**Exam use:** In sentence-structure questions, suffixes help you spot the word class quickly — e.g., a blank before a noun usually needs an adjective (*-ful, -ous, -ive*), and a blank after the subject usually needs a verb.
