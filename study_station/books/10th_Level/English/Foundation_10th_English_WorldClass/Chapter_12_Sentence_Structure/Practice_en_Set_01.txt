Questions 1–20: Easy | Questions 21–25: Medium

1. A sentence must always contain which two essential parts?
(a) A noun and an adjective (b) A verb and an adverb (c) A subject and a predicate (d) A phrase and a clause
Answer: (c)
Solution: Many learners think a noun and an adjective are enough, but an adjective only describes; it cannot make a complete thought. Every complete sentence needs a subject (who or what) and a predicate (what is said about the subject), so the answer is a subject and a predicate.
Source: NCERT Class 6 English Grammar

2. Which of the following is a complete sentence?
(a) The boy runs in the park (b) Running in the park (c) Because he was late (d) Under the table
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Running in the park" has no subject, "Because he was late" is a dependent clause, and "Under the table" is only a phrase. Only "The boy runs in the park" has a subject and a finite verb expressing a complete thought, so the answer is "The boy runs in the park."
Source: NCERT Class 7 English Grammar

3. Identify the subject in the sentence: "The tall girl won the race."
(a) won (b) The tall girl (c) the race (d) tall
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Won" is the verb and "the race" is the object, so they cannot be the subject. The doer of the action is "The tall girl", so the answer is "The tall girl."
Source: NCERT Class 6 English Grammar

4. Identify the predicate in the sentence: "My father works in a bank."
(a) works in a bank (b) My father (c) in a bank (d) My
Answer: (a)
Solution: "My father" is the subject, and "in a bank" alone is just a phrase within the predicate. Everything said about the subject — "works in a bank" — is the predicate, so the answer is "works in a bank."
Source: NCERT Class 6 English Grammar

5. Which sentence is an example of the pattern Subject + Verb?
(a) They made him captain (b) She gave me a book (c) He is a doctor (d) Birds fly
Answer: (d)
Solution: "She gave me a book" has an indirect and direct object, "He is a doctor" has a complement, and "They made him captain" has an object complement. "Birds fly" has only a subject and a verb, so the answer is "Birds fly."
Source: NCERT Class 8 English Grammar

6. In the sentence "She sings sweetly", the word "sweetly" is:
(a) the subject (b) the object (c) a complement (d) an adverb modifying the verb
Answer: (d)
Solution: "Sweetly" is not the subject (that is "She") and not an object, because "sings" here takes no object. It tells how she sings, modifying the verb, so the answer is an adverb modifying the verb.
Source: NCERT Class 7 English Grammar

7. Which of the following is a declarative sentence?
(a) Close the door (b) What a beautiful day! (c) The sun rises in the east (d) Where are you going?
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Close the door" is imperative, "What a beautiful day!" is exclamatory, and "Where are you going?" is interrogative. "The sun rises in the east" states a fact, so the answer is "The sun rises in the east."
Source: NCERT Class 6 English Grammar

8. An interrogative sentence always ends with:
(a) a question mark (b) an exclamation mark (c) a full stop (d) a comma
Answer: (a)
Solution: A full stop ends statements, an exclamation mark ends exclamations, and a comma never ends a sentence. A question ends with a question mark, so the answer is a question mark.
Source: NCERT Class 6 English Grammar

9. "Please help me." is what type of sentence?
(a) Declarative (b) Imperative (c) Interrogative (d) Exclamatory
Answer: (b)
Solution: It does not state a fact, ask a question, or express strong emotion; it makes a request. A sentence giving a command, request, or advice is imperative, so the answer is Imperative.
Source: NCERT Class 6 English Grammar

10. "What a lovely flower!" is what type of sentence?
(a) Declarative (b) Interrogative (c) Exclamatory (d) Imperative
Answer: (c)
Solution: The sentence expresses sudden joy or admiration, not a plain statement or question. Such sentences showing strong feeling are exclamatory, so the answer is Exclamatory.
Source: NCERT Class 6 English Grammar

11. In the sentence "The teacher gave the students homework", the indirect object is:
(a) The teacher (b) gave (c) homework (d) the students
Answer: (d)
Solution: "Homework" is the direct object (what was given); the tempting wrong choice is to pick it as the indirect object. The receiver of the direct object is "the students", so the answer is "the students."
Source: NCERT Class 8 English Grammar

12. Which sentence follows the Subject + Verb + Object pattern?
(a) She is happy (b) He sleeps (c) They elected her leader (d) Ravi kicked the ball
Answer: (d)
Solution: "She is happy" has a complement, "He sleeps" has no object, and "They elected her leader" has an object complement. "Ravi kicked the ball" has subject, verb, and object, so the answer is "Ravi kicked the ball."
Source: NCERT Class 8 English Grammar

13. A group of words that has a subject and a verb of its own is called:
(a) a phrase (b) a clause (c) an object (d) a complement
Answer: (b)
Solution: A phrase has no subject-verb pair, which is the tempting confusion here. A clause always contains its own subject and finite verb, so the answer is a clause.
Source: NCERT Class 8 English Grammar

14. "In the morning" is an example of:
(a) a clause (b) a sentence (c) a predicate (d) a phrase
Answer: (d)
Solution: It has no subject or finite verb, so it cannot be a clause or a sentence. A group of words without a subject-verb pair is a phrase, so the answer is a phrase.
Source: NCERT Class 7 English Grammar

15. Which of the following is a simple sentence?
(a) She opened the window (b) Although it rained, we went out (c) The sun set and the birds returned (d) He worked hard, so he passed
Answer: (a)
Solution: The first three options each contain two clauses joined by a conjunction, making them compound or complex. "She opened the window" has only one subject-verb pair, so the answer is "She opened the window."
Source: NCERT Class 8 English Grammar

16. A compound sentence is joined by:
(a) a coordinating conjunction like "and" (b) a subordinating conjunction like "because" (c) a relative pronoun like "who" (d) a preposition like "under"
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Because" and "who" create dependent clauses, which make a sentence complex, not compound — a common trap. Compound sentences join independent clauses with coordinating conjunctions such as "and", so the answer is a coordinating conjunction like "and".
Source: NCERT Class 8 English Grammar

17. "Because he was ill, he stayed at home." This is a:
(a) simple sentence (b) compound sentence (c) exclamatory sentence (d) complex sentence
Answer: (d)
Solution: It is not compound because the two parts are not equal; "Because he was ill" cannot stand alone. One independent clause plus one dependent clause makes a complex sentence, so the answer is complex sentence.
Source: NCERT Class 8 English Grammar

18. In the sentence "He is a brave soldier", the words "a brave soldier" act as:
(a) the object (b) the adverbial (c) the subject complement (d) the indirect object
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Is" is a linking verb and takes no object, so calling "a brave soldier" the object is the trap. Words that complete the meaning after a linking verb and describe the subject form the subject complement, so the answer is the subject complement.
Source: NCERT Class 8 English Grammar

19. Which word is the finite verb in: "Walking daily, the old man stays fit"?
(a) Walking (b) stays (c) daily (d) fit
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Walking" is a non-finite form (a participle) and does not change with the subject, which tempts many learners. "Stays" changes with tense and subject, so the answer is "stays".
Source: NCERT Class 9 English Grammar

20. The usual word order of an English statement is:
(a) Subject + Verb + Object (b) Verb + Subject + Object (c) Object + Verb + Subject (d) Subject + Object + Verb
Answer: (a)
Solution: Hindi often uses Subject + Object + Verb ("Ram ne ball maari"), which misleads Hindi speakers. English statements follow Subject + Verb + Object, so the answer is Subject + Verb + Object.
Source: NCERT Class 6 English Grammar

21. Spot the error: Part P: "One of my friend" / Part Q: "has gone" / Part R: "to Delhi yesterday." / Part S: No error
(a) Part P (b) Part Q (c) Part R (d) No error
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is to focus on the verb "has", which correctly agrees with "One". The real error is in Part P: after "one of", a plural noun is required, so it should be "one of my friends". The error lies in Part P, so the answer is Part P.
Source: PYQ-style (SSC pattern)

22. Spot the error: Part P: "The news" / Part Q: "were broadcast" / Part R: "last night." / Part S: No error
(a) Part P (b) Part Q (c) Part R (d) No error
Answer: (b)
Solution: "News" looks plural because of the final "s", which is the examiner's trap, but it is an uncountable singular noun. The verb must be "was broadcast", so the error is in Part Q, and the answer is Part Q.
Source: PYQ-style (SSC pattern)

23. Choose the correctly framed question:
(a) Where you are going? (b) Where are you going? (c) Where going you are? (d) You are going where?
Answer: (b)
Solution: Hindi speakers often say "Where you are going?" because Hindi keeps statement order in questions, but English requires inversion of the subject and auxiliary verb. The correct inverted form is "Where are you going?", so the answer is "Where are you going?"
Source: NCERT Class 7 English Grammar

24. "The committee has taken its decision." The collective noun "committee" takes a singular verb here because:
(a) collective nouns are always singular (b) the members acted separately (c) the members acted as one united body (d) the verb agrees with "decision"
Answer: (c)
Solution: Saying collective nouns are always singular is an over-simplification and the tempting wrong idea; they take a plural verb when members act individually. Here the committee acted as one body, so the answer is the members acted as one united body.
Source: NCERT Class 9 English Grammar

25. Which sentence has the correct structure?
(a) She explained me the rule (b) She explained to me the rule yesterday evening in the class quickly (c) She explained the rule to me (d) Explained she the rule to me
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Explain" cannot take an indirect object directly, so "She explained me the rule" is wrong — a very common Hindi-speaker error ("usne mujhe samjhaya"). The verb "explain" must be followed by the direct object and then "to" plus the person, so the answer is "She explained the rule to me."
Source: PYQ-style (Bank exam pattern)
