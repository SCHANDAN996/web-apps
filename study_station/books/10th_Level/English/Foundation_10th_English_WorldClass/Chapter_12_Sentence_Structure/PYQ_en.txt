# 📊 PYQ Analysis — Sentence Structure

## How Exams Test This Chapter

Sentence Structure questions appear in every major government exam — SSC (CGL, CHSL, GD, MTS), Banking (IBPS, SBI), Railways (NTPC, Group D), and State exams. They come in four disguises:

1. **Spot the Error** — a sentence split into parts; find the wrong part.
2. **Sentence Improvement** — an underlined part; choose the best replacement.
3. **Fill in the Blanks** — choose the correct verb/pronoun/conjunction.
4. **Sentence Arrangement (PQRS)** — order jumbled parts into a logical sentence.

## Pattern Analysis (What Examiners Actually Ask)

Across recent papers, the same handful of sub-topics keep repeating:

- **Subject–Verb Agreement** — the single most tested area. The trap is almost always an *intervening phrase* between subject and verb ("The quality of the mangoes **was** good").
- **Tense Consistency** — mixing past and present inside one sentence; wrong tense after "since/for", "hardly…when", "no sooner…than".
- **Pronoun Agreement & Case** — "between you and I" (wrong), "each of the boys has his book".
- **Parallel Structure** — "He likes reading, swimming, and to jog" (broken parallelism).
- **Misplaced/Dangling Modifiers** — "Walking down the road, the building came into view."
- **Sentence Arrangement** — testing logical flow: general → specific, cause → effect, time order.

## The Examiner's Four Favourite Traps

| Trap Type | How It Works | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Intervening Phrase** | Extra words hide the real subject | "One of my friends **is** (not are) a doctor." |
| **Collective Noun** | Team/jury/committee usually takes singular | "The team **is** playing well." |
| **Sound-Right** | What sounds natural in speech is grammatically wrong | "What you are doing?" → "What **are you** doing?" |
| **Over-thinking** | The simple, direct option is correct; the fancy one is wrong | "He **denied** the charge" (not "refused the charge") |

## Top 10 High-Yield Question Types (with Solutions)

**Q1.** The quality of the apples (a) / were not good (b) / No error (c)
**Answer:** (b) — **Trap: Intervening Phrase.** Subject is "quality" (singular), not "apples". Correct: *was not good*. *Source: PYQ-style*

**Q2.** Each of the students (a) / have submitted (b) / the assignment. (c)
**Answer:** (b) — **Trap: Intervening Phrase.** "Each" is always singular → *has submitted*. *Source: PYQ-style*

**Q3.** Hardly had he reached the station (a) / than the train left. (b)
**Answer:** (b) — **Trap: Sound-Right.** "Hardly…**when**", "No sooner…**than**". Correct: *when the train left*. *Source: PYQ-style*

**Q4.** He is one of the best players (a) / that has ever played (b) / for the country. (c)
**Answer:** (b) — **Trap: Intervening Phrase.** After "one of the + plural noun", the relative clause verb agrees with the plural noun → *that **have** ever played*. *Source: PYQ-style*

**Q5.** Walking through the park, (a) / the flowers looked beautiful. (b)
**Answer:** (b) — **Trap: Dangling Modifier.** Flowers can't walk. Correct: *Walking through the park, I found the flowers beautiful.* *Source: PYQ-style*

**Q6.** Between you and I, (a) / this plan will fail. (b)
**Answer:** (a) — **Trap: Sound-Right.** After a preposition use object case → *between you and **me***. *Source: PYQ-style*

**Q7.** She likes singing, dancing, (a) / and to paint. (b)
**Answer:** (b) — **Trap: Parallel Structure.** Keep the same form → *and painting*. *Source: PYQ-style*

**Q8.** The committee (a) / have taken its decision. (b)
**Answer:** (b) — **Trap: Collective Noun.** Acting as one body → *has taken its decision*. (Plural only when members act separately.) *Source: PYQ-style*

**Q9.** No sooner did the bell ring (a) / when the students rushed out. (b)
**Answer:** (b) — **Trap: Sound-Right.** Pair is "No sooner…**than**". *Source: PYQ-style*

**Q10.** Arrange: (P) and won the match (Q) the team played brilliantly (R) in the final overs (S) by two wickets
**Answer:** Q → R → P → S — **Trap: Over-thinking.** Follow the natural action flow: played brilliantly → in the final overs → and won → by two wickets. *Source: PYQ-style*

## Applying the 15/45-Second Rule

**15-second question (Fact-based):** Q2 above. The moment you see "Each of the…", the rule fires instantly — singular verb. No re-reading needed. Spot the trigger word, mark, move on.

**45-second question (Inference-based):** Q10 (arrangement). You must read all four parts, find the opening sentence (the one with the main subject and no connector), then chain connectors ("and", "by") logically. Budget the full 45 seconds — rushing arrangement questions is the most common way to lose easy marks.

## Key Takeaways

- Master subject–verb agreement first; it dominates this chapter.
- Learn fixed pairs by heart: hardly…when, no sooner…than, not only…but also, neither…nor.
- In error questions, read the sentence *without* the middle phrase to find the true subject.
- In arrangement questions, find the independent opening sentence first — it solves half the puzzle.
