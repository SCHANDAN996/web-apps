51. Choose the grammatically correct sentence.
(a) She don't like coffee. (b) She doesn't likes coffee. (c) She not like coffee. (d) She doesn't like coffee.
Answer: (d)
Solution: With "doesn't", the main verb stays in base form, so "likes" is wrong; "don't" fails subject-verb agreement with "she". Only "She doesn't like coffee" is correct, so the answer is (d).
Source: NCERT Class 9 English Grammar

52. Identify the subject in the sentence: "The tall boy in the red shirt won the race."
(a) boy (b) race (c) shirt (d) red
Answer: (a)
Solution: The intervening phrase "in the red shirt" tempts learners to pick "shirt", but it only describes the boy. The doer of the action "won" is "boy", so the answer is (a).

53. Choose the correct word order.
(a) She is always late. (b) Always she is late. (c) She always is late being. (d) Is she always late being.
Answer: (a)
Solution: Adverbs of frequency like "always" come after the verb "be" (is/am/are/was/were). "She is always late" follows this rule, so the answer is (a).

54. Which sentence is a simple sentence?
(a) He ran fast, but he missed the bus. (b) The children played in the park. (c) Because it rained, we stayed home. (d) When she arrived, the meeting started.
Answer: (b)
Solution: A simple sentence has one subject and one predicate with no conjunction joining clauses. "The children played in the park" has a single clause, so the answer is (b).

55. Choose the grammatically correct sentence.
(a) There is many books on the table. (b) There be many books on the table. (c) There are many books on the table. (d) There am many books on the table.
Answer: (c)
Solution: In "there is/are" sentences, the verb agrees with the real subject that follows — here "many books" (plural). "There are many books" is correct, so the answer is (c).

(a) Ravi (b) ball (c) the (d) kicked
Answer: (b)
Solution: The object receives the action of the verb. Ravi (subject) kicked (verb) the ball (receiver of the action), so the answer is (b).

57. Choose the correct question form.

Answer: (b)
Solution: In wh-questions, the helping verb comes before the subject: "Where are you going?" Hindi speakers often say "Where you are going?" by translating directly, which is wrong in English, so the answer is (b).

58. Which of these is a compound sentence?
(a) She sings beautifully. (b) Although he was tired, he kept working. (c) Walking down the road, he saw a friend. (d) I wanted tea, so I boiled some water.
Answer: (d)
Solution: A compound sentence joins two independent clauses with a coordinating conjunction (FANBOYS). "I wanted tea, so I boiled some water" has two full clauses joined by "so", so the answer is (d).

59. Choose the grammatically correct sentence.
(a) He is more taller than his brother. (b) He is taller than his brother. (c) He is tallest than his brother. (d) He is tall than his brother.
Answer: (b)
Solution: "More taller" is a double comparative — a classic examiner's trap; use either "more" or "-er", never both. "He is taller than his brother" is correct, so the answer is (b).

(a) sing (b) birds (c) sweetly (d) morning
Answer: (a)
Solution: The verb shows the action. "Birds" is the subject, "sweetly" is an adverb, and "sing" is the action word, so the answer is (a).

61. Spot the error: "One of my friend (P) / is coming (Q) / to the party tonight. (R)"
(a) Part P (b) Part Q (c) Part R (d) No error
Answer: (a)
Solution: After "one of", we always use a plural noun — "one of my friends". The singular verb "is" in Part Q is actually correct because the subject is "one". The error is in Part P, so the answer is (a).

62. Choose the correctly punctuated and structured sentence.
(a) Being a rainy day, I stayed at home. (b) I being a rainy day stayed home. (c) Being a rainy day, staying at home. (d) It being a rainy day, I stayed at home.
Answer: (d)
Solution: "Being a rainy day, I stayed at home" is a dangling modifier — it wrongly suggests "I" am the rainy day. Adding the subject "It" fixes the structure, so the answer is (d).

63. Spot the error: "The teacher, along with the students, (P) / were present (Q) / at the function. (R)"
(a) Part Q (b) Part P (c) Part R (d) No error
Answer: (a)
Solution: With "along with", the verb agrees with the first subject — "the teacher" (singular) — so it should be "was present". The plural-sounding phrase "the students" is the trap. The error is in Part Q, so the answer is (a).

64. Choose the correct sentence.
(a) Neither of the two boys have done his homework. (b) Neither of the two boys has done his homework. (c) Neither of the two boys has done their homework. (d) Neither of the two boys have done their homework.
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Neither" is singular, so it takes "has" and the singular pronoun "his". "Their" sounds natural in speech but is the sound-right trap in formal grammar, so the answer is (b).

65. Identify the type of sentence: "Although it was late, she continued to study."
(a) Simple (b) Complex (c) Compound (d) Compound-complex
Answer: (b)
Solution: The sentence has one dependent clause ("Although it was late") and one independent clause ("she continued to study") — the mark of a complex sentence. There is no coordinating conjunction joining two independent clauses, so the answer is (b).

66. Spot the error: "Each of the girls (P) / have brought (Q) / her own lunch. (R)"
(a) Part P (b) Part R (c) Part Q (d) No error
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Each" is singular, so the verb must be "has brought", not "have brought". The nearby plural word "girls" is the intervening-phrase trap. The error is in Part Q, so the answer is (c).

71. Identify the correctly framed complex sentence.
(a) He was ill, so he did not come. (b) He was ill and did not come. (c) Because he was ill, he did not come. (d) Being ill, not coming he was.
Answer: (c)
Solution: A complex sentence needs a subordinating conjunction joining a dependent and an independent clause. "Because he was ill, he did not come" fits; "so" and "and" make compound sentences, so the answer is (c).

73. Choose the correct sentence.
(a) My friend and I went to the market. (b) I and my friend went to the market. (c) Me and my friend went to the market. (d) My friend and me went to the market.
Answer: (a)
Solution: As subjects, use "I" (not "me"), and polite English puts the other person first. "Me and my friend" is the sound-right trap from everyday speech. "My friend and I went" is correct, so the answer is (a).

74. Choose the correct passive form: "She writes a letter."
(a) A letter written is by her. (b) A letter is wrote by her. (c) A letter was written by her. (d) A letter is written by her.
Answer: (d)
Solution: Present simple active becomes "is/am/are + V3" in passive. "Wrote" is V2, not V3, and "was" changes the tense. "A letter is written by her" is correct, so the answer is (d).
Source: NCERT Class 9 English Grammar

75. Spot the error: "Neither the teacher nor the students (P) / was present (Q) / in the classroom. (R)"
(a) Part P (b) Part R (c) Part Q (d) No error
Answer: (c)
Solution: With "neither…nor", the verb agrees with the nearer subject — "students" (plural) — so it should be "were present". Choosing "was" by looking at "teacher" is the trap. The error is in Part Q, so the answer is (c).
