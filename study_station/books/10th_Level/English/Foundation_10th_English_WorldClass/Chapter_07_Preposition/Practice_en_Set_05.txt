Questions 101–120: Easy | Questions 121–125: Moderate

101. He has been suffering from fever ___ Monday.
(a) from (b) since (c) for (d) by
Answer: (b)
Solution: "For" is tempting because it expresses duration, but it is used with a period of time (for three days), not a point of time. "Monday" is a point of time, so the answer is since.
Source: NCERT Class 9 English Grammar

102. The train arrived exactly ___ time.
(a) in (b) at (c) by (d) on
Answer: (d)
Solution: "In time" is the tempting wrong choice, but it means before the deadline. "On time" means exactly at the scheduled moment, which fits "exactly", so the answer is on.
Source: PYQ-style

103. She is junior ___ me by two years.
(a) than (b) to (c) from (d) with
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Than" is the trap because comparatives usually take "than", but Latin comparatives like junior, senior, superior and inferior always take "to", so the answer is to.
Source: NCERT Class 10 English Grammar

104. He was accused ___ theft by the police.
(a) of (b) for (c) with (d) on
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Accused for" sounds plausible to Hindi speakers, but the fixed pattern is "accused of" a crime, so the answer is of.
Source: PYQ-style

105. Spot the error: Part P: He is fond / Part Q: to play cricket / Part R: every evening. / Part S: No error
(a) Part P (b) Part Q (c) Part R (d) No error
Answer: (b)
Solution: Part Q is wrong because "fond" is always followed by "of" plus a gerund, not an infinitive; it should be "of playing cricket". Parts P and R are correct, so the answer is Part Q.
Source: PYQ-style

106. The children were divided ___ two groups.
(a) into (b) between (c) among (d) in
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Between" is tempting because there are two groups, but "divide" as a verb of splitting takes "into" (divide something into parts); "between" would need "divided between the two groups" with the groups named as recipients. The natural fixed phrase here is "divided into", so the answer is into.
Source: NCERT Class 8 English Grammar

107. He lives ___ 45, Gandhi Road, Delhi.
(a) in (b) on (c) at (d) by
Answer: (c)
Solution: "In" is tempting because we say "live in Delhi", but a specific house number with a street address takes "at", so the answer is at.
Source: PYQ-style

108. I am looking forward ___ meeting you soon.
(a) towards (b) for (c) at (d) to
Answer: (d)
Solution: "For" tempts learners who think of purpose, but "look forward to" is a fixed phrase where "to" is a preposition followed by a gerund, so the answer is to.
Source: PYQ-style

109. Spot the error: Part P: Despite of the rain, / Part Q: the match continued / Part R: without interruption. / Part S: No error
(a) Part P (b) Part Q (c) Part R (d) No error
Answer: (a)
Solution: Part P is wrong: "despite" never takes "of" (that is the trap from "in spite of"); it should be "Despite the rain". Parts Q and R are fine, so the answer is Part P.
Source: PYQ-style

110. He died ___ cancer last year.
(a) from (b) with (c) of (d) by
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Die from" is used for external causes like injuries, which tempts many learners, but diseases take "die of", so the answer is of.
Source: NCERT Class 9 English Grammar

111. The manager was angry ___ his clerk for the delay.
(a) with (b) upon (c) on (d) at
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Angry at" tempts because it exists, but it is used with things or situations; with a person we say "angry with", so the answer is with.
Source: PYQ-style

112. We should abide ___ the rules of the institution.
(a) to (b) with (c) by (d) on
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Abide with" sounds possible but means to stay with someone; the fixed phrase for following rules is "abide by", so the answer is by.
Source: PYQ-style

113. Spot the error: Part P: He entered into the room / Part Q: and sat down / Part R: quietly. / Part S: No error
(a) No error (b) Part Q (c) Part R (d) Part P
Answer: (d)
Solution: Part P is wrong: "enter" is never followed by "into" when it means to go inside ("enter into" is used only for agreements or discussions). Parts Q and R are correct, so the answer is Part P.
Source: PYQ-style

114. She has been waiting here ___ two hours.
(a) since (b) for (c) from (d) at
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Since" is the trap because the sentence is in the perfect continuous tense, but "since" needs a point of time; "two hours" is a period, so the answer is for.
Source: NCERT Class 9 English Grammar

115. He is eligible ___ this post.
(a) for (b) to (c) of (d) at
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Eligible to" tempts because it exists before a verb (eligible to apply), but before a noun like "this post" the preposition is "for", so the answer is for.
Source: PYQ-style

116. The thief was caught ___ the police red-handed.
(a) from (b) through (c) with (d) by
Answer: (d)
Solution: "With" tempts Hindi speakers, but in a passive sentence the doer of the action is introduced by "by", so the answer is by.
Source: NCERT Class 8 English Grammar

117. Spot the error: Part P: I ordered for a cup of tea / Part Q: as soon as / Part R: I reached the café. / Part S: No error
(a) Part Q (b) Part R (c) Part P (d) No error
Answer: (c)
Solution: Part P is wrong: "order" takes a direct object without "for" (we order tea, not order for tea). Parts Q and R are correct, so the answer is Part P.
Source: PYQ-style

118. He congratulated me ___ my success.
(a) for (b) about (c) on (d) at
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Congratulated for" is the common Hindi-speaker trap, but the fixed pattern is "congratulate someone on something", so the answer is on.
Source: PYQ-style

119. The bridge was built ___ the river Ganga.
(a) on (b) over (c) above (d) upon
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Above" tempts because it means higher than, but a bridge spans across and covers the river, which requires "over", so the answer is over.
Source: NCERT Class 8 English Grammar

120. He is devoid ___ any sense of responsibility.
(a) from (b) off (c) with (d) of
Answer: (d)
Solution: "Devoid from" sounds parallel to "free from", which is the trap, but "devoid" is always followed by "of", so the answer is of.
Source: PYQ-style

121. Spot the error: Part P: She insisted / Part Q: to go with me / Part R: to the market. / Part S: No error
(a) Part Q (b) No error (c) Part R (d) Part P
Answer: (a)
Solution: Part Q is wrong: "insist" is followed by "on" plus a gerund, not an infinitive; it should be "on going with me". Parts P and R are correct, so the answer is Part Q.
Source: PYQ-style

122. The two brothers quarrelled ___ themselves over property.
(a) within (b) between (c) among (d) with
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Among" is the classic trap because "quarrel among themselves" is often heard, but standard usage reserves "among" for more than two; with two people the correct reflexive phrase is "between themselves", so the answer is between.
Source: PYQ-style

123. He was absorbed ___ his studies when I entered.
(a) with (b) at (c) in (d) on
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Absorbed with" tempts by analogy with "busy with", but the fixed adjective-preposition pair is "absorbed in", so the answer is in.
Source: PYQ-style

124. Spot the error: Part P: He is capable / Part Q: to handle / Part R: any difficult situation. / Part S: No error
(a) Part Q (b) Part P (c) Part R (d) No error
Answer: (a)
Solution: Part Q is wrong: "capable" takes "of" plus a gerund, not an infinitive; it should be "of handling". Parts P and R are correct, so the answer is Part Q.
Source: PYQ-style

125. I prefer tea ___ coffee.
(a) than (b) over (c) from (d) to
Answer: (d)
Solution: "Than" is the strongest trap because preference implies comparison, but "prefer" is a Latin comparative and always takes "to", so the answer is to.
