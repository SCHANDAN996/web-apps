# 🧒 Feynman Technique — Prepositions Explained Like You're 12

## The Hardest Concept: Why do we say "good **at**" but "interested **in**"?

Imagine prepositions are **glue words**. Every noun and every verb in English has its own favourite glue, and it refuses to stick with any other glue. There is no big logic — it's a habit the language has built over hundreds of years. Your job is not to find logic; your job is to **learn the pairs**, the way you learn that "tea" goes with "cup", not with "plate".

## A Daily-Life Example

Think of your school bus stop.

- You stand **at** the bus stop. (a point/place)
- You get **on** the bus. (a surface you board)
- You sit **in** the bus. (inside an enclosed space)
- You arrive **at** school **at** 8 o'clock.

See? **AT = point**, **ON = surface**, **IN = inside**. This one picture solves many preposition questions:

| Glue | Think of | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **IN** | inside a box / a big area / a long time | in the room, in India, in 2024, in January |
| **ON** | touching a surface / a specific day | on the table, on Monday, on 15th August |
| **AT** | an exact point / exact time | at the door, at 5 pm, at the station |

## The Unforgettable Trick: **"The Box, The Table, The Dot"**

Draw three things in your notebook:
1. 📦 A **box** → anything *inside* it = **IN** (in the box, in Delhi, in the morning)
2. 🪑 A **table** → anything *on top* of it = **ON** (on the table, on Sunday, on the wall)
3. 📍 A **dot** → any exact *point* = **AT** (at 6 o'clock, at the corner, at home)

Whenever confused, ask: *Is it inside, on top, or a point?* — Box, Table, Dot!

## But What About "married to", "afraid of", "depend on"?

These are **fixed pairs** — like best friends who always sit together. Learn them as one word:

- afraid **of** (not "afraid from")
- depend **on** (not "depend upon with")
- married **to** (not "married with")
- listen **to** (not "listen")
- good **at** (not "good in")

**Trick:** Make a "Best Friends List" of 20 such pairs and read it aloud daily for a week — your ear will start catching the wrong ones automatically.

## ⚠️ The Hindi-Speaker Trap

In Hindi we say *"शाम को"*, *"सोमवार को"* — the same word "को" for everything! But English forces you to choose: **in** the evening, **on** Monday, **at** night. Never translate word-for-word from Hindi — use the Box-Table-Dot picture instead.
