Card 1
Front: What is a preposition?
Back: A word placed before a noun or pronoun to show its relationship with another word in the sentence — e.g., "The book is ON the table."

Card 2
Front: Which preposition is used with "good" to show skill?
Back: "Good AT" — She is good at mathematics. (Trap: never "good in" for skills.)

Card 3
Front: Fill in: He has been living in Delhi ______ 2015.
Back: SINCE — "since" is used with a point of time (2015, Monday, January); "for" is used with a period of time (two years, a long time).

Card 4
Front: Mnemonic: How do you remember the difference between "in", "on", and "at" for time?
Back: "AT a point, ON a day, IN a period" — AT 5 o'clock (exact point), ON Monday (day/date), IN July / 2024 / the morning (longer period).

Card 5
Front: Fill in: The train arrived ______ time.
Back: ON — "on time" = punctual, exactly at the scheduled time; "in time" = early enough, before a deadline.

Card 6
Front: Examiner's Trap: "He is senior ______ me." — which preposition?
Back: TO — senior, junior, superior, inferior, prior, and prefer always take "to", never "than".

Card 7
Front: Which preposition follows "married"?
Back: TO — "She is married to Ravi." (Trap: never "married with".)

Card 8
Front: Fill in: I am fond ______ music.
Back: OF — fond of, afraid of, proud of, jealous of, aware of all take "of".

Card 9
Front: What is the difference between "between" and "among"?
Back: "Between" is used for two persons/things (or clear one-to-one relations); "among" is used for more than two — "Distribute the sweets among the children."

Card 10
Front: Mnemonic: How do you remember prepositions that never follow certain verbs?
Back: "R-E-A-C-H, E-N-T-E-R, A-T-T-A-C-K — no preposition attack!" — reach, enter, attack, resemble, order, and accompany take NO preposition: "He entered the room" (not "entered into").

Card 11
Front: Fill in: She jumped ______ the well.
Back: INTO — "into" shows movement from outside to inside; "in" shows position (rest) — "She is in the room."

Card 12
Front: Examiner's Trap: "Despite ______ his hard work, he failed." — is "of" needed?
Back: NO — "Despite his hard work" is correct. "Despite" never takes "of"; "in spite of" always takes "of".

Card 13
Front: Which preposition follows "angry" with a person? With a thing?
Back: Angry WITH a person; angry AT/ABOUT a thing — "angry with him", "angry at the delay".

Card 14
Front: Fill in: He died ______ cancer.
Back: OF — die OF a disease/hunger; die FROM an injury/wound; die IN an accident/war; die FOR a cause.

Card 15
Front: What preposition is used before modes of transport in general, and what is the exception?
Back: "BY" — by bus, by train, by air. Exception: "ON foot". Also: "in a car/taxi", "on a bus/train" when a specific vehicle is meant.

Card 16
Front: Fill in: I have been waiting here ______ two hours.
Back: FOR — "for" + period of time (two hours, five days); "since" + point of time (5 o'clock, Monday).

Card 17
Front: Which is correct — "different from", "different than", or "different to"?
Back: "Different FROM" is the standard, exam-safe form — "My book is different from yours."

Card 18
Front: Fill in: The child is afraid ______ dogs.
Back: OF — afraid of, scared of, frightened of all take "of".

Card 19
Front: What is the rule for prepositions at the end of a question?
Back: In questions starting with Who/What/Which, the preposition goes to the end — "What are you looking AT?" (not "At what are you looking?" in everyday/exam English).

Card 20
Front: Fill in: He is inferior ______ his brother in studies.
Back: TO — inferior/superior/senior/junior + to. These Latin comparatives never take "than".
