# 📊 PYQ Analysis — Preposition

## How Exams Test Prepositions (Pattern Analysis)

Prepositions appear in **every** SSC, Railway, Bank and State exam paper — inside Spot the Error, Fill in the Blanks, Sentence Improvement and Cloze Test. Across these exams, the question style is remarkably stable:

- **Fill in the Blanks** is the most common format: one blank before a noun/gerund, four prepositions as options (in / on / at / by, or since / for / from / to).
- **Spot the Error** hides the preposition mistake in the middle of a long sentence — usually a wrong fixed preposition after a verb or adjective (*discuss about, comprise of, married with*).
- **Sentence Improvement** asks you to replace an underlined preposition phrase (*"since two hours" → "for two hours"*).

**Sub-topics that repeat most often:**

1. **Fixed prepositions** after verbs/adjectives/nouns (*afraid of, depend on, good at, insist on, differ from*) — the single biggest source of questions.
2. **Time prepositions** — *since* vs *for*; *in / on / at* for time.
3. **Place prepositions** — *in / on / at*; *between* vs *among*; *beside* vs *besides*.
4. **Banned prepositions** — verbs that take **no** preposition: *discuss, enter, reach, order, accompany, comprise, attack, resemble*.
5. **Preposition + gerund** — *"insisted on going"*, not *"insisted on to go"*.

**Examiner's favourite traps (in words, not numbers):**

- **Sound-Right trap:** "discuss about" *sounds* fine to Hindi speakers ("के बारे में चर्चा करना") — it is wrong.
- **Intervening Phrase trap:** a long phrase between the verb and the blank distracts you from the fixed preposition the verb demands.
- **Near-synonym trap:** *beside/besides, in/into, on/upon, between/among* — options differ by one letter or one shade of meaning.
- **Over-thinking trap:** the simple, common preposition is correct, but a fancy option tempts you.

## Top 10 High-Yield Questions (with Solutions & Trap Classification)

**Q1.** He has been living in Delhi ______ 2015.
(a) for (b) since (c) from (d) by
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** *Since* is used with a **point of time** (2015, Monday, January); *for* is used with a **period** (two years). 2015 is a point → *since*.
**Trap type:** Sound-Right (Hindi "से" covers both, so *for* tempts).
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q2.** The teacher discussed ______ the problem with the students.
(a) about (b) on (c) over (d) No preposition
**Answer: (d)**
**Solution:** *Discuss* is a transitive verb — it never takes a preposition. "Discussed the problem" is complete. Same rule: *enter, reach, order, comprise, resemble, accompany*.
**Trap type:** Sound-Right
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q3.** She is good ______ mathematics but weak ______ English.
(a) in / at (b) at / in (c) at / at (d) in / in
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** Fixed adjective-preposition pairs: **good at**, **weak in**. Both must be memorised as a pair; mixing them is the classic error.
**Trap type:** Near-synonym / fixed-preposition
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q4.** The train arrived ______ time.
(a) in (b) on (c) at (d) by
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** **On time** = punctual, at the scheduled moment. **In time** = before the deadline (with time to spare). Exams love this pair.
**Trap type:** Near-synonym
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q5.** He insisted ______ going with me.
(a) on (b) upon to (c) for (d) in
**Answer: (a)**
**Solution:** *Insist on* + **gerund (V-ing)**. Never "insist on to go" — after a preposition, the verb takes -ing.
**Trap type:** Over-thinking (option (b) looks elaborate but is doubly wrong)
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q6.** He sat ______ the two guests.
(a) among (b) between (c) in (d) amidst
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** *Between* for **two** persons/things; *among/amidst* for more than two. Count the objects first — "two guests" → *between*.
**Trap type:** Near-synonym
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q7.** I have been waiting here ______ two hours.
(a) since (b) for (c) from (d) at
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** "Two hours" is a **duration/period** → *for*. Compare Q1: point of time → *since*. Learn the pair as: **Since = Start point, For = Full length** (both begin with the same letter as their meaning's clue: **S**ince–**S**tart, **F**or–**F**ull).
**Trap type:** Sound-Right
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q8.** He jumped ______ the river to save the child.
(a) in (b) into (c) on (d) upon
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** *Into* shows **movement from outside to inside**; *in* shows position (already inside). "Jumped" is motion → *into*.
**Trap type:** Near-synonym
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q9.** The committee is composed ______ five members.
(a) with (b) from (c) of (d) by
**Answer: (c)**
**Solution:** Fixed phrase: **composed of**. Note the twin trap: *comprise* takes **no** preposition ("The committee comprises five members"), but *composed* takes **of**.
**Trap type:** Sound-Right / fixed-preposition
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q10.** ______ English, she speaks French and German.
(a) Beside (b) Besides (c) Between (d) Among
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** **Besides** = in addition to; **beside** = next to (place). The sentence lists languages she speaks *in addition* to English → *Besides*.
**Trap type:** Near-synonym (one letter changes the meaning)
**Source:** PYQ-style

## Applying the 15/45-Second Rule

**15-second question (Fact-based):** Q7 — *for two hours*. The moment you see a duration ("two hours"), the rule "for + period" fires instantly. No reading of the whole sentence needed beyond the time phrase. **Target: 15 seconds or less.**

**45-second question (Inference-based):** Q6 — the *between/among* type. You must ignore the decoration of the sentence, find the real object ("two guests"), count them, and then apply the rule. In the exam this type often comes wrapped in a longer sentence, so **budget up to 45 seconds** — and never let extra words rush you into *among*.

**Exam strategy:** In the paper, fixed-preposition and since/for questions are your 15-second bankers — solve them first. Long-sentence between/among or error-spotting questions go in the 45-second bucket; mark and return if the sentence has more than one clause.

---

## One-Line Revision of the Pattern

> Most preposition questions are won before the exam — by memorising fixed pairs (*good at, depend on, composed of*), the banned-preposition verbs (*discuss, enter, reach, comprise*), and the since/for, in/into, beside/besides, between/among contrasts. The paper only tests whether you revised them.
