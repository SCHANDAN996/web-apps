126. He has been suffering from fever ___ Monday.
(a) for (b) since (c) from (d) by
Answer: (b)
Solution: "For" tempts learners because it also shows time, but "for" is used with a period of time (for two days), while "since" is used with a point of time. Monday is a point of time, so the answer is "since".
Source: NCERT Class 9 English Grammar

127. The train arrived exactly ___ time.
(a) in (b) at (c) on (d) by
Answer: (c)
Solution: "In time" means before the deadline, which is the tempting wrong choice here, but the sentence stresses exact punctuality. "On time" means at the scheduled moment, so the answer is "on".

128. She is junior ___ me by two years.
(a) to (b) from (c) than (d) with
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Than" is the trap because comparatives usually take "than", but Latin comparatives like junior, senior, superior and inferior take "to". So the answer is "to".

129. He jumped ___ the river to save the child.
(a) in (b) inside (c) upon (d) into
Answer: (d)
Solution: "In" suggests a static position, which misleads many students, but the verb "jumped" shows movement from outside to inside, which demands "into". So the answer is "into".

130. We should abide ___ the rules of the institution.
(a) with (b) by (c) to (d) on
Answer: (b)
Solution: "With" sounds natural after many verbs, which is the trap, but the fixed phrase is "abide by" meaning to follow. So the answer is "by".

131. The child hid ___ the bed when he heard the noise.

136. Spot the error: He is (P) / capable to handle (Q) / this responsibility (R) / alone. (S)

Answer: (b)
Solution: Part R looks suspicious because "responsibility alone" seems odd, but it is grammatically fine; the real error is in Part Q, where "capable to" should be "capable of handling" — "capable" takes "of" plus a gerund. So the answer is Part Q.
