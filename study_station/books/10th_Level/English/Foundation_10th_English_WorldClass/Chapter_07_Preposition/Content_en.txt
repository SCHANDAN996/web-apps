# Preposition — The Small Words That Carry Big Marks

## 🎯 Why This Chapter Matters

In almost every government exam — SSC GD, SSC MTS, RRB Group D, Police Constable — prepositions appear regularly in error-spotting, fill-in-the-blank and sentence-improvement questions. They look tiny ("in", "on", "at"), but examiners know that Hindi-speaking students make the maximum mistakes exactly here. Why? Because **Hindi postpositions (में, पर, से, को) do not map one-to-one onto English prepositions.** Master this chapter and you convert your weakest area into guaranteed marks.

## 📌 Learning Objectives

After this chapter you will be able to:

1. Define a preposition and identify it in any sentence.
2. Use prepositions of **time, place, and direction** correctly.
3. Apply the **12 Golden Rules** that examiners test again and again.
4. Spot the classic traps: *in/on/at* confusion, wrong fixed phrases (*married with* ❌), and unnecessary prepositions (*discuss about* ❌).
5. Answer fact-based preposition questions in under 15 seconds.

---

## 1. What Is a Preposition?

A **preposition** is a word placed **before** a noun or pronoun to show its **relation** with another word in the sentence.

- The book is **on** the table. (relation between *book* and *table*)
- She came **after** lunch. (relation between *came* and *lunch*)

The noun/pronoun after the preposition is called its **object**. Together they form a **prepositional phrase**: *on the table*, *after lunch*, *in the morning*.

> 💡 Memory hook: Think of a preposition as a word that can fit in the blank — "The mouse ran ___ the box." (*in, on, under, over, behind, near…*) Any word that fits there is likely a preposition.

### 🧠 Active Recall
1. What is the object of the preposition in "She sat **beside me**"?
2. Fill in the blank with any three prepositions: "The cat jumped ___ the wall."

## 2. Prepositions of Time: IN / ON / AT

This is the **most tested** area. Learn it as a pyramid — from big time to exact time:

| Preposition | Used for | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **AT** (exact point) | clock time, festivals as points, night/noon | at 5 o'clock, at noon, at midnight, at dawn, at Diwali |
| **ON** (a day/date) | days, dates, day + part of day | on Monday, on 15th August, on Sunday morning, on my birthday |
| **IN** (long period) | months, years, seasons, centuries, parts of day | in July, in 2024, in winter, in the morning/evening |

**Mnemonic 🧠 — "A Point ON a day IN a period":**
**AT** = a point (smallest), **ON** = a day (middle), **IN** = a long period (biggest). Small → Medium → Large: **A–O–I**.

⚠️ **No preposition** before *today, tomorrow, yesterday, last night, this morning, next week*:
- ❌ I will go **on tomorrow**. → ✅ I will go tomorrow.
- ❌ He came **in last night**. → ✅ He came last night.

### 🧠 Active Recall
1. ___ 26th January 2. ___ the afternoon 3. ___ 6 p.m. 4. ___ last Monday (trick question!)

## 3. Prepositions of Place: IN / ON / AT

| Preposition | Used for | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **AT** | a specific point | at the bus stop, at the door, at the station |
| **ON** | a surface | on the table, on the wall, on the road |
| **IN** | an enclosed area / large place | in the room, in Delhi, in the box, in the river |

More place prepositions you must know:
- **above / over** (higher than), **below / under** (lower than)
- **between** (two) vs **among** (more than two)
- **beside** (next to) vs **besides** (in addition to) — a favourite trap!
- **behind, in front of, near, opposite, across**

### 🧠 Active Recall
1. Choose: The picture hangs ___ the wall. (in/on)
2. Choose: The secret is ___ you and me. (between/among)

---

## 4. Prepositions of Direction & Movement

- **to** → destination: go **to** school
- **into** → movement inside: jumped **into** the well (vs **in** = already inside)
- **onto** → movement to a surface: climbed **onto** the roof
- **from** → starting point: came **from** Agra
- **towards, through, across, along, up, down**

⚠️ Trap: **arrive/reach/enter** —
- arrive **at** a station, arrive **in** a city/country ✅
- **reach** Delhi (no preposition!) ❌ reached *to* Delhi
- **enter** the room (no preposition!) ❌ entered *into* the room

## 5. Fixed (Dependent) Prepositions — The Examiner's Goldmine

Certain verbs, adjectives and nouns take a **fixed** preposition. These must be memorised:

| Word | Fixed Preposition | Example |
|---|---|---|
| afraid | **of** | afraid of dogs |
| angry (with a person) | **with** | angry with me |
| angry (about a thing) | **about/at** | angry about the result |
| good / bad | **at** | good at maths |
| interested | **in** | interested in music |
| depend | **on** | depends on you |
| listen | **to** | listen to the teacher |
| wait | **for** | wait for the bus |
| married | **to** | married to Ravi (❌ married *with*) |
| different | **from** | different from mine |
| agree (with person / to proposal) | **with / to** | agree with you; agree to the plan |
| suffer | **from** | suffers from fever |
| die | **of** | died of hunger |
| consist | **of** | consists of five chapters |
| believe | **in** | believe in hard work |

### 🧠 Active Recall
1. He is married ___ Sita. 2. She is good ___ singing. 3. We are waiting ___ the train.

## 6. Verbs That Take NO Preposition (Very High-Yield!)

Hindi influence makes students add extra prepositions. These verbs are **complete in themselves**:

| Wrong ❌ | Right ✅ |
|---|---|
| discuss **about** the plan | discuss the plan |
| order **for** tea | order tea |
| reach **to** Delhi | reach Delhi |
| enter **into** the room | enter the room |
| accompany **with** me | accompany me |
| request **for** help | request help |
| resemble **with** his father | resemble his father |
| comprise **of** ten members | comprise ten members |

---

## ⚠️ Examiner's Trap Box

**Trap 1 — "Sound-Right" Hindi translation:** *"He is angry on me"* sounds fine in Hindi (वह मुझ **पर** नाराज़ है) but English needs **angry with me**. Never translate prepositions word-for-word from Hindi.

**Trap 2 — in/into confusion:** "The frog jumped **in** the well" ❌ — movement needs **into**. "The frog is **in** the well" ✅ (position, no movement).

**Trap 3 — Double preposition:** "Where are you going **to**?" is acceptable in speech, but in error-spotting questions watch for *discuss about, return back, repeat again* — the extra word is the error.

## 🔁 Linguistic Bridge — Hindi vs English

| Hindi-style English ❌ | Correct English ✅ | Why |
|---|---|---|
| He is angry **on** me. (मुझ पर) | He is angry **with** me. | "angry" takes *with* for persons |
| She is suffering from fever **since two days**. (दो दिन से) | She is suffering from fever **for two days**. | *since* + point of time (since Monday); *for* + period of time (for two days) |
| I go to school **by walk**. (पैदल से) | I go to school **on foot**. | fixed phrase: *on foot*, but *by bus/train* |
| He came **in** last night. | He came last night. | no preposition before *last night* |
| What is the time **in** your watch? (घड़ी में) | What is the time **by** your watch? | fixed usage: *by your watch* |

**Rule of thumb:** If you translated the preposition directly from Hindi, double-check it — that is exactly where the examiner hides the error.

## 🏆 The 12 Golden Rules of Prepositions

1. **AT** for exact time; **ON** for days/dates; **IN** for months/years/seasons.
2. **Since** + point of time (Monday, 2010); **for** + period of time (two days, five years).
3. **Between** two, **among** more than two.
4. **Beside** = next to; **besides** = in addition to.
5. **In** = position inside; **into** = movement inside.
6. **On** = position on surface; **onto** = movement to surface.
7. Arrive **at** (small place) / arrive **in** (city, country); **reach** takes no preposition.
8. **Married to, different from, afraid of, good at, interested in, depend on** — fixed pairs.
9. **Discuss, order, reach, enter, accompany, resemble, comprise** — no preposition after them.
10. No preposition before **today, tomorrow, yesterday, last night, next week, this morning**.
11. **By** + agent/vehicle (by bus, by Ram); **with** + instrument (with a pen, with a knife).
12. A preposition is followed by a noun/pronoun in **objective case**: *between you and **me*** (❌ between you and I).
