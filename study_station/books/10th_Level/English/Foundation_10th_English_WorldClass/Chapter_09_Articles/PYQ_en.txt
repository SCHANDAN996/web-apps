# 📊 PYQ Analysis — Articles (a, an, the)

## How Exams Test This Chapter

Articles look small, but examiners love them because they are **invisible traps** — a missing "the" or a wrong "an" hides inside an otherwise correct sentence. Across SSC (CGL, CHSL, GD, MTS), RRB (NTPC, Group D), Bank and State exams, questions on articles appear in these recurring forms:

**1. Fill in the blank with a/an/the/zero article** — the most common form. The blank sits before a noun, and the examiner builds the trap around:
- **Sound vs. spelling**: words starting with a vowel *letter* but a consonant *sound* ("a university", "a one-rupee note") or a consonant letter with a vowel sound ("an hour", "an honest man", "an M.P.").
- **Superlatives and ordinals**: "the best", "the first" — the article is dropped in the wrong option.
- **Unique things**: "the sun", "the moon", "the earth", "the equator".

**2. Spot the error** — the article error is hidden in one part of a long sentence. Typical traps:
- "the" used before uncountable/abstract nouns in general sense ("The honesty is the best policy" ❌).
- "the" before names of meals, games, languages ("the breakfast", "the cricket" ❌).
- Missing "the" before rivers, oceans, mountain ranges, holy books, newspapers.

**3. Sentence improvement** — replace the underlined article phrase; one option is "No improvement".

**4. Fixed expressions** — "at once", "in a hurry", "a few" vs "few" vs "the few", "a little" vs "little".

**Recurring sub-topics (in order of how often examiners return to them):**
1. a/an choice by **sound**, not spelling
2. **the** with superlatives, ordinals, unique nouns
3. **Zero article** with proper nouns, meals, games, languages, abstract nouns
4. **the** with rivers, seas, mountain ranges, deserts, holy books
5. a few / few / the few and a little / little / the little
6. "the" turning an adjective into a class ("the rich", "the poor")
7. Same noun, different meaning with/without "the" ("go to school" vs "go to the school")

**Examiner's favourite traps (Trap Types):**
- **Sound-Right Trap**: an option that *sounds* natural in Hindi-influenced speech but is grammatically wrong ("He is an European").
- **Intervening Phrase Trap**: extra words between the article and the noun distract you ("___ honest man of the village" — decide by "honest", not "man of the village").
- **Over-thinking Trap**: the simple rule applies, but a complicated-looking option tempts you away.
- **Hindi-Transfer Trap**: Hindi has no articles, so students either drop them everywhere or insert "the" everywhere.

## Top 10 High-Yield Questions (with Solutions)

**Q1.** He is ___ honest man and everyone trusts him.
(a) a (b) an (c) the (d) no article
**Answer:** (b)
**Solution:** "Honest" begins with a silent 'h', so the first *sound* is a vowel /ɒ/. Use "an". Trap: choosing "a" by looking at the letter 'h'.
**Trap Type:** Sound-Right | **Source:** PYQ-style

**Q2.** ___ Ganga is one of the longest rivers in India.
(a) A (b) An (c) The (d) No article
**Answer:** (c)
**Solution:** Names of rivers always take "the" — the Ganga, the Yamuna, the Nile.
**Trap Type:** Over-thinking (students hesitate because "Ganga" is a proper noun) | **Source:** NCERT Class 6–8 English grammar

**Q3.** She goes to ___ school by bus every day. (as a student)
(a) a (b) an (c) the (d) no article
**Answer:** (d)
**Solution:** When "school" means the *purpose* (studying), no article is used: go to school, go to bed, go to church. "The school" would mean the building (e.g., parents visiting).
**Trap Type:** Sound-Right (Hindi speakers insert "the") | **Source:** PYQ-style

**Q4.** Kalidasa is ___ Shakespeare of India.
(a) a (b) an (c) the (d) no article
**Answer:** (c)
**Solution:** When a famous person's name is used as a comparison ("the Shakespeare of India" = the greatest dramatist), "the" is required.
**Trap Type:** Over-thinking | **Source:** PYQ-style

**Q5.** I saw ___ one-eyed man near the market.
(a) a (b) an (c) the (d) no article
**Answer:** (a)
**Solution:** "One" is pronounced /wʌn/ — it starts with a consonant sound /w/, so "a" is correct. Same family: a university, a European, a union, a useful book.
**Trap Type:** Sound-Right (vowel letter 'o' misleads) | **Source:** PYQ-style

**Q6.** ___ honesty is the best policy. (Choose the correct option)
(a) A (b) An (c) The (d) No article
**Answer:** (d)
**Solution:** Abstract nouns (honesty, kindness, truth) used in a *general* sense take no article. "The honesty" would refer to a specific person's honesty.
**Trap Type:** Hindi-Transfer | **Source:** NCERT Class 9 English

**Q7.** He is ___ M.L.A. from our district.
(a) a (b) an (c) the (d) no article
**Answer:** (b)
**Solution:** "M" is pronounced /em/ — a vowel sound — so "an". Rule: for abbreviations, judge by the sound of the *first letter spoken*: an M.P., an S.P., an F.I.R., but a B.A., a Ph.D.
**Trap Type:** Sound-Right | **Source:** PYQ-style

**Q8.** ___ rich should help the poor.
(a) A (b) An (c) The (d) No article
**Answer:** (c)
**Solution:** "The + adjective" denotes a whole class of people: the rich, the poor, the blind, the old. The verb that follows is plural.
**Trap Type:** Over-thinking | **Source:** PYQ-style

**Q9.** There is ___ university in our town.
(a) a (b) an (c) the (d) no article
**Answer:** (a)
**Solution:** "University" begins with the sound /juː/ ("yoo") — a consonant sound — so "a". First mention, non-specific → indefinite article.
**Trap Type:** Sound-Right | **Source:** PYQ-style

**Q10.** I had ___ lunch at 2 p.m. and then took ___ rest.
(a) a, a (b) the, the (c) no article, a (d) no article, the
**Answer:** (c)
**Solution:** Names of meals (breakfast, lunch, dinner) take no article when routine: "have lunch". But "take a rest" is a fixed expression with "a".
**Trap Type:** Hindi-Transfer (double trap in one sentence) | **Source:** PYQ-style

## Applying the 15/45-Second Rule

**15-second question (Fact-based recall) — Q2 (The Ganga):**
The moment you see a river's name, the rule "rivers take *the*" should fire instantly. No analysis needed. Read → recall rule → mark (c) → move on. Target: under 15 seconds. All "fixed-rule" questions (rivers, superlatives, unique things, meals, games) belong here.

**45-second question (Inference-based) — Q3 (go to school):**
Here you must *interpret meaning*, not just recall a rule. Ask: does "school" mean the purpose (studying) or the building? The clue "by bus every day" shows routine activity → purpose → zero article. If the sentence were "The committee visited ___ school to inspect the building", the answer would flip to "the". Such meaning-dependent questions deserve up to 45 seconds — read the whole sentence twice before answering.

**Strategy summary:** If the blank depends on a memorised rule → 15 seconds. If it depends on the *meaning* of the sentence (purpose vs. place, general vs. specific, a few vs. few) → 45 seconds. Never spend 45 seconds on a rule-based question, and never rush a meaning-based one.
