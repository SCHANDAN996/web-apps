51. Choose the correct adverb to complete the sentence: She sings ______.
(a) beautiful (b) beauty (c) beautifully (d) beautify
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Beautiful" is an adjective and cannot modify the verb "sings" — that is the tempting trap. A verb is modified by an adverb, and the adverb form of "beautiful" is "beautifully", so the answer is beautifully.
Source: NCERT Class 7 English Grammar

52. Identify the adverb in the sentence: The train arrived late.
(a) train (b) arrived (c) the (d) late
Answer: (d)
Solution: "Arrived" is the verb, not the adverb — a common confusion. "Late" tells when the train arrived, so it is an adverb of time. The answer is late.
Source: NCERT Class 6 English Grammar

53. Choose the correctly spelt adverb.
(a) truly (b) truely (c) truelyy (d) truley
Answer: (a)
Solution: Many students keep the "e" and write "truely", which is the trap. When "true" becomes an adverb, the "e" is dropped before adding "-ly". The answer is truly.
Source: NCERT Class 8 English Grammar

54. He works ______ to support his family.
(a) hard (b) hardly (c) harderly (d) hardness
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Hardly" is the big trap — it means "almost not", which reverses the meaning. The adverb meaning "with great effort" is "hard" itself. The answer is hard.
Source: NCERT Class 8 English Grammar

55. Identify the adverb of frequency: "I always brush my teeth twice a day."
(a) always (b) brush (c) twice (d) teeth
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Twice" shows number of times, not frequency of habit — that is the tempting wrong choice. "Always" tells how often the action happens, so it is the adverb of frequency. The answer is always.
Source: NCERT Class 6 English Grammar

56. Choose the correct sentence.
(a) She speaks English fluent. (b) She speaks English fluently. (c) She fluently speaks English fluent. (d) She speaks fluently English fluent.
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Fluent" is an adjective and cannot describe the verb "speaks" — that is the trap in the first option. The verb needs the adverb "fluently". The answer is "She speaks English fluently."
Source: NCERT Class 7 English Grammar

57. The adverb in "The children played happily in the park" is:
(a) children (b) happily (c) played (d) park
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Played" is the verb, which many students pick by mistake. "Happily" tells how the children played, so it is the adverb of manner. The answer is happily.
Source: NCERT Class 6 English Grammar

58. Choose the correct adverb: Please drive ______ on this road.
(a) careful (b) caring (c) care (d) carefully
Answer: (d)
Solution: "Careful" is an adjective and is the common trap here. The verb "drive" needs an adverb of manner, which is "carefully". The answer is carefully.
Source: NCERT Class 7 English Grammar

59. Identify the adverb of place: "Come here and sit beside me."
(a) come (b) sit (c) here (d) beside
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Beside" is a preposition showing position relative to "me", not an adverb — that is the trap. "Here" answers the question "where?", so it is the adverb of place. The answer is here.
Source: NCERT Class 6 English Grammar

60. Choose the correct sentence.
(a) He runs very fastly. (b) He runs very faster. (c) He runs very fast. (d) He runs very fasting.
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Fastly" does not exist in English — that is the classic trap. "Fast" is both an adjective and an adverb, so no "-ly" is added. The answer is "He runs very fast."
Source: NCERT Class 7 English Grammar

61. Spot the error: Part P: He is / Part Q: enough tall / Part R: to reach the shelf. / Part S: No error
(a) Part Q (b) Part P (c) Part R (d) No error
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is that "enough tall" sounds acceptable in casual speech, but "enough" as an adverb always comes after the adjective it modifies. The correct form is "tall enough". The error is in Part Q.

62. Choose the correct sentence.
(a) I have seen that movie yesterday. (b) I saw that movie yesterday. (c) I see that movie yesterday. (d) I am seeing that movie yesterday.
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is using the present perfect with a definite past time adverb like "yesterday" — this is never allowed. Definite past time requires the simple past tense. The answer is "I saw that movie yesterday."
Source: PYQ-style

63. Spot the error: Part P: She sings / Part Q: more sweeter / Part R: than her sister. / Part S: No error
(a) Part P (b) Part R (c) Part Q (d) No error
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is doubling the comparative — "more" plus "-er" together is always wrong. The correct form is simply "sweeter". The error is in Part Q.
Source: PYQ-style

64. Choose the correct option: "He ______ goes to the gym; maybe once a year."
(a) always (b) seldom (c) often (d) usually
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Often" is tempting because people go to gyms often, but the clue "maybe once a year" demands a negative-frequency adverb. "Seldom" means "rarely". The answer is seldom.
Source: NCERT Class 8 English Grammar

65. Spot the error: Part P: The teacher / Part Q: explained the lesson / Part R: very clearly yesterday evening. / Part S: No error
(a) No error (b) Part Q (c) Part R (d) Part P
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is over-thinking — students hunt for an error that is not there. The adverbs "very clearly" (manner) and "yesterday evening" (time) are correctly placed after the object. There is no error, so the answer is No error.
Source: PYQ-style

66. Choose the correct sentence.
(a) He only earns five hundred rupees a day. (b) Only he earns five hundred rupees a day only. (c) He earns only five hundred rupees a day. (d) He earns five hundred rupees only a day.
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is placing "only" before the verb, which changes the meaning to "he does nothing but earn". "Only" must sit just before the word it limits — "five hundred rupees". The answer is "He earns only five hundred rupees a day."
Source: PYQ-style

67. Spot the error: Part P: I am / Part Q: very much / Part R: tired today. / Part S: No error
(a) Part P (b) Part R (c) Part Q (d) No error
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is that "very much tired" sounds natural to Hindi speakers ("बहुत ज़्यादा थका"), but "very much" modifies verbs, not adjectives. Before an adjective we use "very" alone: "very tired". The error is in Part Q.
Source: PYQ-style

68. Choose the correct option: "The patient is ______ better today than yesterday."
(a) much (b) more (c) very (d) most
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Very" is the trap because it sounds smooth, but "very" cannot modify a comparative. Comparatives are strengthened by "much" or "far". The answer is much.
Source: NCERT Class 8 English Grammar

69. Spot the error: Part P: He returned / Part Q: back home / Part R: late at night. / Part S: No error
(a) Part P (b) No error (c) Part R (d) Part Q
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is that "returned back" is heard everywhere in speech, but "return" already contains the idea of "back", so "back" is redundant. The correct phrase is "returned home". The error is in Part Q.
Source: PYQ-style

70. Choose the correct sentence.
(a) She is too good to not pass. (b) She is too good not to pass. (c) She is so good to pass not. (d) She is good too to pass.
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is splitting the infinitive "to pass" with "not", which is considered faulty in exam grammar. The structure "too + adjective + not to + verb" is the accepted form. The answer is "She is too good not to pass."
Source: PYQ-style

71. Spot the error: Part P: No sooner did he arrive / Part Q: when / Part R: the meeting started. / Part S: No error
(a) Part Q (b) Part P (c) Part R (d) No error
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is pairing "no sooner" with "when" because "when" feels natural for time. The fixed correlative pair is "No sooner … than". The error is in Part Q.
Source: PYQ-style

72. Choose the correct option: "Hardly ______ I reached the station when the train left."
(a) was (b) have (c) did (d) had
Answer: (d)
Solution: "Did" is the trap because inversion suggests a simple past helper, but "hardly … when" takes the past perfect for the earlier action. The correct form is "Hardly had I reached". The answer is had.
Source: PYQ-style

73. Spot the error: Part P: He speaks / Part Q: English / Part R: very good. / Part S: No error
(a) Part P (b) Part Q (c) No error (d) Part R
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is that "good" is a real word and looks harmless, but it is an adjective and cannot modify the verb "speaks". The adverb "well" is required: "speaks English very well". The error is in Part R.
Source: PYQ-style

74. Choose the correct sentence.
(a) He is enough intelligent to solve this puzzle. (b) Enough he is intelligent to solve this puzzle. (c) He enough is intelligent to solve this puzzle. (d) He is intelligent enough to solve this puzzle.
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is placing "enough" before the adjective, copying the noun pattern "enough money". With adjectives and adverbs, "enough" always follows. The answer is "He is intelligent enough to solve this puzzle."
Source: NCERT Class 8 English Grammar

75. Spot the error: Part P: She / Part Q: seldom goes / Part R: to the temple, doesn't she? / Part S: No error
(a) Part P (b) Part R (c) Part Q (d) No error
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is the negative tag "doesn't she", which follows the usual rule — but "seldom" is already a negative adverb, so the statement counts as negative and the tag must be positive: "does she?". The error is in Part R.
