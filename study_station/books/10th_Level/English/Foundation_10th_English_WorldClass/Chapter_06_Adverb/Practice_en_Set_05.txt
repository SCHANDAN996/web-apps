101. Choose the correctly spelt adverb.
(a) Truely (b) Truli (c) Truley (d) Truly
Answer: (d)
Solution: "Truely" tempts because we keep the "e" of "true", but adjectives ending in "-ue" drop the "e" before adding "-ly" (true → truly, due → duly). So the answer is truly.
Source: NCERT Class 9 English Grammar

102. Identify the adverb in the sentence: "The soldiers fought bravely in the battle."
(a) bravely (b) fought (c) battle (d) soldiers
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Fought" tempts because it is the action word, but it is the verb, not the adverb. "Bravely" tells how the soldiers fought, so it modifies the verb. So the answer is bravely.
Source: NCERT Class 8 English Grammar

103. Choose the correct sentence.
(a) He drives very fast. (b) He drives very fastly. (c) He drives very faster. (d) He drives very fasting.
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Fastly" tempts because most adverbs end in "-ly", but "fast" is both an adjective and an adverb; "fastly" does not exist in standard English. So the answer is "He drives very fast."

104. Spot the error: "She sings (P) / more sweeter (Q) / than her sister. (R) / No error (S)"
(a) Part R (b) Part P (c) Part Q (d) No error
Answer: (c)
Solution: Part R tempts because "than her sister" looks like the usual trouble spot, but the comparison itself is fine. The error is in Part Q: "sweeter" is already comparative, so "more" cannot be used with it; it should be "more sweetly" or simply "sweeter". So the error is in Part Q.

105. Choose the correct option: "Hardly had he reached the station ___ the train left."
(a) then (b) that (c) than (d) when
Answer: (d)
Solution: "Than" tempts because it pairs with "no sooner", but "hardly" and "scarcely" always take "when". So the answer is when.

106. Choose the correctly framed sentence.
(a) He is enough tall to reach the shelf. (b) He is too enough tall to reach the shelf. (c) He is tall enough to reach the shelf. (d) Enough he is tall to reach the shelf.
Answer: (c)
Solution: The option with "enough tall" tempts because in Hindi we say "kaafi lamba", but in English "enough" comes after the adjective or adverb it modifies (tall enough, fast enough). So the answer is "He is tall enough to reach the shelf."

107. Identify the type of the underlined adverb: "She visits her grandmother twice a week."
(a) Adverb of manner (b) Adverb of frequency (c) Adverb of place (d) Adverb of degree
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Adverb of manner" tempts because "twice" describes the action, but it answers "how often?", not "how?". Words like twice, often, always show frequency. So the answer is adverb of frequency.

108. Choose the correct sentence.
(a) I have seen that film yesterday. (b) I saw that film yesterday. (c) I had seen that film yesterday. (d) I am seeing that film yesterday.
Answer: (b)
Solution: The present perfect option tempts because "have seen" feels complete, but the present perfect tense can never be used with a definite past-time adverb like "yesterday"; the simple past is required. So the answer is "I saw that film yesterday."

Answer: (a)
Solution: Part Q tempts because the amount looks odd, but the real fault is the position of "only". "Only" must sit next to the word it limits; here it limits "five hundred", so it should be "He earns only five hundred rupees a month." So the error is in Part P.

Answer: (d)
Solution: "Very" tempts because it is the commonest intensifier, but "very" is not used before a comparative; "much" is used before comparatives (much better, much taller). So the answer is much.

Answer: (c)
Solution: "Stayed" tempts because it carries the action, but it is a verb. "Therefore" joins the two clauses and shows result, so it is a conjunctive adverb. So the answer is therefore.

Solution: "Very much sorry" tempts because "very much" strengthens verbs like "thank", but with adjectives like "sorry" we use "very" alone; "very much" is used with verbs or comparatives. So the answer is "I am very sorry for the delay."

Answer: (d)
Solution: "No sooner" tempts because it also links two quick events, but it needs inversion and "than" ("No sooner had the rain stopped than..."). The simple structure here takes "as soon as". So the answer is As soon as.
