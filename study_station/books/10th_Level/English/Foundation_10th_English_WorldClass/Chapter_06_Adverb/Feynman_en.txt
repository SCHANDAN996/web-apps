🧒 FEYNMAN TECHNIQUE — Adverbs Explained Like You're 12

**The hardest concept: Adverb vs Adjective — and where to put the adverb.**

Imagine your friend Rohan is playing cricket. You want to describe him.

- "Rohan is a **good** player." → Here *good* describes **Rohan** (a person, a noun). So *good* is an **Adjective**.
- "Rohan plays **well**." → Here *well* describes **plays** (an action, a verb). So *well* is an **Adverb**.

That's the whole secret:

👉 **Adjective describes a THING (noun). Adverb describes an ACTION (verb).**

Think of it like this: the **noun is the actor**, the **verb is the acting**. If you praise the actor, use an adjective. If you praise the acting, use an adverb.

**The Unforgettable Trick — "The Mirror Test" 🪞**

Ask yourself: *"What am I describing?"*

1. Point your finger at the word being described.
2. If your finger lands on a **person/thing** → Adjective.
3. If your finger lands on an **action** → Adverb.

Try it:
- "She sings **beautifully**." → Finger on *sings* (action) → Adverb ✅
- "She is **beautiful**." → Finger on *She* (person) → Adjective ✅

**The trap that fools everyone:** Verbs like *look, feel, smell, taste, seem* are "mirror verbs" — they reflect back to the person, not the action.

- "The cake smells **good**." ✅ (The cake IS good — describing the cake, not the smelling!)
- "The cake smells **well**." ❌

**Where does the adverb sit?** Simple rule for beginners: an adverb of manner (slowly, carefully, well) usually sits **after the verb or after the object** — never between the verb and its object.

- "He speaks English **fluently**." ✅
- "He speaks fluently English." ❌
