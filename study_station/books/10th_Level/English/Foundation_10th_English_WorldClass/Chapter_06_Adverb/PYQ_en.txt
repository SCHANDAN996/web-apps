# 📊 PYQ ANALYSIS — ADVERB

## 1. Pattern Analysis

### What the exams ask

SSC (CGL, CHSL, MTS, GD), RRB (NTPC, Group D), and Banking exams (IBPS PO/Clerk) test adverbs mainly through **error spotting, fill in the blanks, and sentence improvement**. The recurring areas are:

- **Types of adverbs** — identifying whether a word is an adverb of manner (how), time (when), place (where), degree (how much), or frequency (how often).
- **Position of adverbs** — especially adverbs of frequency (*always, often, never*) before the main verb but after the verb *be*: "He is always late."
- **Adjective vs. Adverb confusion** — choosing between *quick/quickly*, *good/well*, *hard/hardly*, *late/lately*.
- **Order of adverbs** — when several adverbs occur together, the usual order is manner → place → time (MPT): "She sang beautifully at the concert yesterday."
- **Adverbs with the same form as adjectives** — *fast, hard, early, daily* used as both.
- **Degree adverbs** — *very, too, quite, rather, enough, almost* and their correct placement (*enough* comes after the adjective/adverb it modifies: "tall enough").
- **Inversion after negative adverbs** — *Hardly/Scarcely/No sooner/Rarely/Never* at the start of a sentence take inverted word order: "No sooner did he arrive than…"
- **Fixed pairs** — *hardly…when*, *scarcely…when*, *no sooner…than*.

### Examiner's traps

- **Near-correct pairs**: *hard* (with effort) vs. *hardly* (almost not); *late* (not on time) vs. *lately* (recently); *most* vs. *mostly*. Exams swap these in error-spotting questions.
- **Adjective after verb where adverb is needed**: "He sings good" instead of "He sings well."
- **Misplaced *only* / *even***: meaning changes with position — a favourite in sentence-improvement questions.
- **Double negatives**: "He does not know nothing" — tested as an error.
- ***Very* vs. *much***: *very* with positive degree, *much* with comparatives ("much better", not "very better").
- **Wrong order of adverbs**: time before place ("yesterday at home") flagged as an error.

### Preparation advice

- Learn the five adverb types with two examples each; most direct questions reduce to this.
- For error spotting, check the word right after the verb first — adjective/adverb confusion is the most common trap.
- Memorise the three fixed pairs (*hardly…when, scarcely…when, no sooner…than*) — they appear regularly.
- Practise placing *enough* and frequency adverbs correctly; these are quick, high-accuracy marks.
