126. Choose the correctly spelt adverb.
(a) Truely (b) Trully (c) Truley (d) Truly
Answer: (d)
Solution: "Truely" tempts because the adjective is "true", but adjectives ending in -ue drop the final e before -ly (true → truly, due → duly). The correct spelling is truly.
Source: NCERT Class 9 English

127. Identify the adverb of degree in the sentence: "The soup is too hot to drink."
(a) too (b) soup (c) hot (d) drink
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Hot" is an adjective, not an adverb, which tempts many students. "Too" modifies the adjective "hot" and tells the degree, so it is an adverb of degree. The answer is too.
Source: NCERT Class 8 English

128. Choose the correct sentence.
(a) He drives very fastly. (b) He drives very fast. (c) He drives very faster. (d) He drives very fasting.
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Fastly" tempts because most adverbs take -ly, but "fast" is both an adjective and an adverb; "fastly" does not exist in standard English. The correct sentence is "He drives very fast."

129. Spot the error: "She sings (P) / more sweeter (Q) / than her sister. (R) / No error (S)"
(a) Part P (b) Part Q (c) Part R (d) No error
Answer: (b)
Solution: Part P looks fine, so students skip ahead, but the real error is in Part Q: "sweeter" is already comparative, so "more" cannot be used with it; it should be "more sweetly" or simply "sweeter". The error is in Part Q.

130. Choose the correct option: "Hardly had he reached the station ___ the train left."
(a) then (b) than (c) when (d) that
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Than" tempts because "no sooner" pairs with "than", but "hardly" and "scarcely" always pair with "when". The correct word is when.

131. Choose the correctly framed sentence.
(a) He always is late for class. (b) He is always late for class. (c) Always he is late for class. (d) He is late always for class.
Answer: (b)
Solution: Putting "always" before "is" tempts learners who translate from Hindi, but adverbs of frequency come after the verb "to be" (is/am/are/was/were). The correct sentence is "He is always late for class."

132. Identify the type of adverb: "The children played merrily in the park."
(a) Adverb of time (b) Adverb of place (c) Adverb of frequency (d) Adverb of manner
Answer: (d)
Solution: "In the park" shows place, which tempts students, but the question asks about "merrily", which tells how the children played. It is an adverb of manner.

133. Choose the correct sentence.
(a) I have seen that film yesterday. (b) I had seen that film yesterday. (c) I saw that film yesterday. (d) I see that film yesterday.
Answer: (c)
Solution: The present perfect in the first option tempts because the action is finished, but adverbs of definite past time like "yesterday" require the simple past, not the present perfect. The correct sentence is "I saw that film yesterday."

134. Choose the correct option: "She speaks English ___ than her brother."
(a) more fluently (b) fluently (c) fluent (d) most fluently
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Fluently" tempts, but "than" signals a comparison, and "fluently" is a long adverb that forms its comparative with "more". The correct option is more fluently.

135. Spot the error: "He is (P) / enough tall (Q) / to reach the shelf. (R) / No error (S)"
(a) Part P (b) Part Q (c) Part R (d) No error
Answer: (b)
Solution: Part R looks suspicious to many, but it is correct; the error is in Part Q because "enough" comes after the adjective it modifies — it should be "tall enough". The error is in Part Q.

136. Choose the correct sentence.
(a) He earns only five hundred rupees a day. (b) He only earns five hundred rupees a day. (c) Only he earns five hundred rupees a day. (d) He earns five hundred rupees only a day.
Answer: (a)
Solution: Placing "only" before "earns" tempts many, but that would mean earning is the only thing he does; "only" must sit next to the word it limits, here "five hundred rupees". The correct sentence is "He earns only five hundred rupees a day."

137. Spot the error: "Scarcely she had (P) / entered the room (Q) / when the phone rang. (R) / No error (S)"
(a) No error (b) Part Q (c) Part R (d) Part P
Answer: (d)
Solution: Part R tempts because "when" looks odd, but "scarcely…when" is correct; the error is in Part P — when a negative adverb begins a sentence, inversion is needed: "Scarcely had she entered". The error is in Part P.

138. Choose the correct option: "The two brothers love ___ deeply."
(a) themselves (b) one another (c) each other (d) one other
Answer: (c)
Solution: "One another" tempts as the traditional rule reserves it for more than two, but here only two brothers are involved, so "each other" is the standard choice. The answer is each other.

139. Choose the correctly punctuated and worded sentence.
(a) He behaved cowardly in the crisis. (b) He behaved cowardlily in the crisis. (c) He behaved in a cowardly manner in the crisis. (d) He behaved coward in the crisis.
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Cowardly" tempts because it ends in -ly, but it is an adjective, not an adverb, and "cowardlily" is not standard usage; the adverbial idea is expressed as "in a cowardly manner". The correct sentence is "He behaved in a cowardly manner in the crisis."

(a) Part Q (b) Part P (c) Part R (d) No error
Answer: (a)

141. Choose the correct sentence.
(a) She is too good to not pass the exam. (b) She is too good not to pass the exam. (c) She is so good to pass the exam. (d) She is enough good to pass the exam.
Answer: (b)
Solution: Splitting the infinitive as in the second option tempts some, but the idiomatic structure is "too + adjective + not to + verb", meaning she is so good that she cannot fail. The correct sentence is "She is too good not to pass the exam."
Source: PYQ-style

142. Identify the sentence where "still" is used as an adverb of time.
(a) He is still working on the project. (b) Still waters run deep. (c) The still of the night was calming. (d) He stood still and listened.
Answer: (a)
Solution: "He stood still" tempts, but there "still" is an adjective meaning motionless; in the correct option "still" means "even now", showing time. The answer is "He is still working on the project."

143. Choose the correct option: "___ did she realise her mistake."
(a) After the meeting only (b) After only the meeting (c) The meeting only after (d) Only after the meeting
Answer: (d)
Solution: The other orders tempt because they sound natural in speech, but when "only + adverbial phrase" begins a sentence, it must come first and cause inversion ("did she realise"). The correct option is Only after the meeting.

Answer: (c)
Solution: Part Q tempts because "so…that" looks heavy, but it is correct; the error is in Part R — "hardly" is already negative, so "cannot hardly" is a double negative; it should be "can hardly". The error is in Part R.

145. Choose the correct sentence.
(a) Seldom I have seen such a beautiful sunset. (b) Seldom I saw such a beautiful sunset. (c) Seldom have I seen such a beautiful sunset. (d) I seldom have seen such a beautiful sunset rarely.
Answer: (c)
Solution: Keeping the normal word order after "seldom" tempts many, but negative adverbs like seldom, rarely, never at the start of a sentence require inversion of subject and auxiliary. The correct sentence is "Seldom have I seen such a beautiful sunset."

146. Choose the correct option: "This medicine is ___ bitter ___ I cannot take it."
(a) much / to (b) too / to (c) very / that (d) so / that
Answer: (d)
Solution: "Too…to" tempts because it also shows excess, but that structure takes a bare infinitive, not a clause with "I cannot take it"; a full clause needs "so…that". The correct pair is so / that.

148. Choose the sentence in which the adverb modifies the whole sentence.
(a) She quickly finished her homework. (b) They arrived quite late at night. (c) He spoke very politely to the guests. (d) Fortunately, nobody was hurt in the accident.
Answer: (d)
Solution: "Very" and "quite" tempt as strong modifiers, but they modify single words; "fortunately" is a sentence adverb expressing the speaker's attitude to the whole statement. The answer is "Fortunately, nobody was hurt in the accident."

149. Choose the correct option: "The patient is ___ better today than yesterday."
(a) more (b) much (c) most (d) very
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Very" tempts because it intensifies adjectives, but it cannot modify a comparative; "much" (or "far") correctly strengthens "better". The correct word is much.

Answer: (a)
Solution: Part Q tempts because "such a grand" looks heavy, but it is correct; the error is in Part P — after "never before" the auxiliary must come before the subject: "Never before have I witnessed". The error is in Part P.
