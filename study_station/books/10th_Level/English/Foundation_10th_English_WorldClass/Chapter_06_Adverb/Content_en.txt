# Adverb

## 1. What is an Adverb?

An **adverb** is a word that modifies (adds meaning to) a **verb**, an **adjective**, or **another adverb**. Just as an adjective describes a noun, an adverb describes *how, when, where, how often,* or *to what extent* an action happens.

- She sings **beautifully**. (modifies the verb *sings*)
- The movie was **very** interesting. (modifies the adjective *interesting*)
- He ran **quite** fast. (modifies the adverb *fast*)

**Exam tip:** Some adverbs can also modify a whole sentence: *Fortunately, he passed the exam.*

## 2. Types of Adverbs

### (a) Adverb of Manner — *how?*
slowly, carefully, well, fast, hard, politely
- The soldiers fought **bravely**.

### (b) Adverb of Time — *when?*
now, then, today, yesterday, soon, already, lately, before
- I have **already** finished my work.

### (c) Adverb of Place — *where?*
here, there, everywhere, up, down, in, out, away
- Come **here** at once.

### (d) Adverb of Frequency — *how often?*
always, usually, often, sometimes, seldom, rarely, never, once, twice
- He **seldom** comes late.

### (e) Adverb of Degree / Quantity — *how much?*
very, too, quite, rather, almost, enough, fully, partly
- The tea is **too** hot to drink.

### (f) Interrogative Adverbs — used to ask questions
when, where, why, how, how often, how far
- **Why** did you leave early?

### (g) Relative Adverbs — join clauses (like relative pronouns)
when, where, why
- This is the house **where** I was born.

### (h) Sentence Adverbs — modify the whole sentence
certainly, obviously, fortunately, surely, indeed
- **Obviously**, he was telling the truth.

## 3. Formation of Adverbs

1. **Adjective + -ly:** quick → quickly, careful → carefully, brave → bravely
2. **Adjectives ending in -y:** change *y* to *i* + -ly: happy → happily, easy → easily
3. **Adjectives ending in -le:** drop *e*, add *y*: gentle → gently, simple → simply
4. **Adjectives ending in -ic:** add -ally: tragic → tragically (exception: public → publicly)
5. **Same form as adjective (no -ly):** fast, hard, early, late, daily, enough, much
   - He is a **hard** worker. (adjective) / He works **hard**. (adverb)
6. **Two forms with different meanings:**
   - hard (with effort) / hardly (almost not) — *He works hard. He hardly rests.*
   - late (not on time) / lately (recently)
   - near (close) / nearly (almost)
   - free (without cost) / freely (without restriction)

**Common trap:** *Hardly* and *scarcely* are negative in meaning — never use *not* with them.
- Wrong: He does not hardly work. → Right: He **hardly** works.

## 4. Position of Adverbs

1. **Adverbs of manner** usually come after the verb or its object: *She spoke politely.*
2. **Adverbs of frequency** come before the main verb but after an auxiliary/be verb:
   - He **always** reaches on time. / He is **always** punctual. / He has **never** lied.
3. **Adverbs of degree** come before the word they modify: *very tall, quite fast, too late.*
4. **Enough** comes **after** the adjective/adverb it modifies but **before** a noun:
   - He is tall **enough**. / He has **enough** money.
5. **Only** should be placed next to the word it limits:
   - He ate **only** bread. (nothing else) vs. He **only** ate bread. (did nothing else with it)

## 5. Comparison of Adverbs

Like adjectives, adverbs have three degrees:

| Positive | Comparative | Superlative |
|---|---|---|
| fast | faster | fastest |
| hard | harder | hardest |
| early | earlier | earliest |
| well | better | best |
| badly | worse | worst |
| much | more | most |
| little | less | least |
| carefully | more carefully | most carefully |

- Ram runs **faster** than Shyam.
- Of all the boys, Mohan worked **the hardest**.

## 6. Words Used as Both Adjective and Adverb

| Word | As Adjective | As Adverb |
|---|---|---|
| fast | a fast train | He runs fast. |
| early | an early train | He came early. |
| daily | a daily newspaper | The paper comes daily. |
| much | much time | He talks much. |
| enough | enough food | good enough |

**Trap:** *Friendly, costly, cowardly, lonely, lovely, orderly* end in -ly but are **adjectives**, not adverbs. To use them adverbially, say *in a friendly manner*.

## 7. Common Errors Tested in Exams

1. **Very vs. Too:** *very* = to a high degree (neutral); *too* = more than needed (negative sense).
   - The weather is **very** pleasant. / The box is **too** heavy to lift.
2. **Too…to** structure: *He is too weak to walk.* (= so weak that he cannot walk)
3. **So…that:** *He is so weak that he cannot walk.*
4. **Much vs. Very:** *very* modifies positive degree; *much* modifies comparative degree.
   - She is **very** beautiful. / She is **much** taller than her sister.
5. **Fairly vs. Rather:** *fairly* for pleasant ideas, *rather* for unpleasant ones.
   - The weather is **fairly** good. / The coffee is **rather** bitter.
6. **Since vs. Ago:** *ago* is used with simple past; *since* with perfect tenses (point of time).
   - He left two years **ago**. / He has been ill **since** Monday.
7. **Yet / Already:** *yet* in negatives and questions (end of sentence); *already* in affirmatives.
   - Have you finished **yet**? / I have **already** finished.
8. **No sooner…than / Hardly…when / Scarcely…when:** fixed pairs — do not mix them.
   - **No sooner** did he arrive **than** it rained.

## 8. PYQ Pattern Analysis (SSC / Railway / Bank)

Examiners typically test adverbs through **error-spotting** and **fill-in-the-blank** questions built around:

- **Confusing pairs:** hard/hardly, late/lately, near/nearly, very/too, much/very, fairly/rather — the most repeated area.
- **Position traps:** placement of *enough, only,* and frequency adverbs (*always, never, seldom*).
- **Double negatives:** sentences wrongly using *not* with *hardly/scarcely/seldom*.
- **Adjective vs. adverb choice:** after verbs of sense (*look, feel, taste, smell, sound*) an **adjective** is used, not an adverb — *The soup tastes good* (not *well*).
- **Comparison errors:** using *more* with adverbs that take *-er* (e.g., *more faster* is wrong).
- **-ly lookalikes:** *friendly, costly, cowardly* offered as adverbs in options.

**Strategy:** First find what the word modifies. If it modifies a verb, adjective, or adverb, you need an adverb; if it modifies a noun, you need an adjective. Then check meaning (hard vs. hardly) and position (enough, only).
