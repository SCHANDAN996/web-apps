Card 1
Front: What is an adverb?
Back: A word that modifies a verb, an adjective, or another adverb. It answers how, when, where, how often, or to what extent. Example: She sings beautifully.

Card 2
Front: How are most adverbs of manner formed?
Back: By adding -ly to an adjective: quick → quickly, careful → carefully. (Adjectives ending in -y change y to i: happy → happily.)

Card 3
Front: Name 5 adjectives that do NOT take -ly to become adverbs.
Back: Fast, hard, late, early, daily — the same word works as both adjective and adverb. "He is a fast runner" / "He runs fast."

Card 4
Front: 🧠 Mnemonic — How to remember what an adverb modifies?
Back: "VAA" — Verb, Adjective, Adverb. An adverb can modify all three, but NEVER a noun. (A noun is modified by an adjective.)

Card 5
Front: ⚠️ Examiner's Trap: "She sings beautiful." — Right or wrong?
Back: Wrong. "Sings" is a verb, so it needs an adverb: "She sings beautifully." Examiners place an adjective after a verb to tempt you.

Card 6
Front: Where does an adverb of frequency (always, often, never) usually go in a sentence?
Back: Before the main verb but after the verb "to be": "He always comes late." / "He is always late."

Card 7
Front: What is the difference between "hard" and "hardly"?
Back: Hard = with great effort ("He works hard"). Hardly = almost not ("He hardly works" = he barely works). They are NOT interchangeable.

Card 8
Front: What is the difference between "late" and "lately"?
Back: Late = not on time ("He arrived late"). Lately = recently ("I haven't seen him lately").

Card 9
Front: ⚠️ Examiner's Trap: "I am very much tired." — Spot the error.
Back: "Very" modifies adjectives/adverbs; "much" modifies comparatives and past participles used verbally. Correct: "I am very tired." But: "much better", "much surprised" (in formal use).

Card 10
Front: Which is correct — "He runs faster than me" or "He runs more faster than me"?
Back: "He runs faster than me." Double comparatives (more + -er) are wrong. Same trap: "most fastest" ❌.

Card 11
Front: 🧠 Mnemonic — Order of adverbs in a sentence?
Back: "MPT" — Manner, Place, Time. "She sang beautifully (M) at the hall (P) yesterday (T)."

Card 12
Front: What type of adverb is "too" in "The tea is too hot"?
Back: An adverb of degree — it means "more than enough" and carries a negative sense. "Too hot to drink" = cannot be drunk.

Card 13
Front: "Enough" — where is it placed with adjectives vs. nouns?
Back: After adjectives/adverbs: "tall enough", "fast enough". Before nouns: "enough money", "enough time".

Card 14
Front: What are conjunctive adverbs? Give 3 examples.
Back: Adverbs that connect two independent clauses: however, therefore, moreover, consequently, otherwise. Example: "He was ill; therefore, he stayed home."

Card 15
Front: What are interrogative adverbs?
Back: Adverbs used to ask questions: when, where, why, how, how often. Example: "Where does he live?"

Card 16
Front: "He only earns five hundred rupees a month." vs "He earns only five hundred rupees a month." — Which is correct and why?
Back: "He earns only five hundred rupees a month." The adverb "only" must sit next to the word it modifies (five hundred), otherwise the meaning changes.

Card 17
Front: Which adverbs have the same form as their adjectives? (Give 4)
Back: Fast, hard, early, straight. "This is a hard problem" (adjective) / "He hit the ball hard" (adverb).

Card 18
Front: What is wrong with: "He is enough tall to reach the shelf"?
Back: "Enough" must come AFTER the adjective: "He is tall enough to reach the shelf."

Card 19
Front: Identify the adverb type: "He visits his grandmother weekly."
Back: Adverb of frequency (definite frequency). Others: daily, monthly, twice, often (indefinite).

Card 20
Front: "Scarcely had he arrived when the meeting began." — Why is "when" used and not "than"?
Back: "Scarcely/Hardly" pair with "when"; "No sooner" pairs with "than". Mixing them ("scarcely...than") is a classic examiner trap.
