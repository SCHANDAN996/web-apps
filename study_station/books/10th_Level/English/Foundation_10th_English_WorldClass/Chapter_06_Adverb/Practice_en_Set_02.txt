26. Choose the correctly spelt adverb.
(a) truly (b) truely (c) trully (d) truelyy
Answer: (a)
Solution: The adverb of "true" drops the silent "e" before adding "-ly", giving "truly". "Truely" is a very common spelling trap in exams, so the answer is truly.
Source: NCERT Class 8 English Grammar

27. Identify the adverb in the sentence: "She sings beautifully."
(a) She (b) sings (c) beautifully (d) None of these
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Beautifully" modifies the verb "sings" and tells how she sings, so it is an adverb of manner. "Sings" is the verb, not the adverb, so the answer is beautifully.
Source: NCERT Class 7 English Grammar

28. He worked ______ to pass the exam.
(a) harding (b) hardly (c) harderly (d) hard
Answer: (d)
Solution: "Hardly" is the trap — it means "almost not", which reverses the meaning. "Hard" itself works as both adjective and adverb, so the answer is hard.
Source: PYQ-style

29. The train arrived ______.
(a) late (b) lately (c) lating (d) lated
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Lately" means "recently" and does not fit here; it is the tempting wrong option. "Late" is both an adjective and an adverb meaning "after the expected time", so the answer is late.
Source: NCERT Class 8 English Grammar

30. Identify the type of adverb in: "He always speaks the truth."
(a) Adverb of manner (b) Adverb of time (c) Adverb of place (d) Adverb of frequency
Answer: (d)
Solution: "Always" tells how often an action happens, so it is an adverb of frequency. It does not tell the manner of speaking, so the answer is adverb of frequency.
Source: NCERT Class 7 English Grammar

31. Choose the sentence with the adverb in the correct position.
(a) She her work quickly finished always. (b) She finished quickly her work always. (c) Quickly she her work finished. (d) She quickly finished her work.
Answer: (d)
Solution: Adverbs of manner usually come after the verb or its object; "She quickly finished her work" is natural and correct. The other options break standard word order, so the answer is "She quickly finished her work."
Source: PYQ-style

32. Which word is an adverb of place?
(a) there (b) yesterday (c) very (d) often
Answer: (a)
Solution: "There" shows place/direction. "Yesterday" shows time, "often" shows frequency, and "very" shows degree, so the answer is there.
Source: NCERT Class 7 English Grammar

33. He is ______ tired to walk.
(a) too (b) to (c) two (d) very
Answer: (a)
Solution: The structure "too + adjective + to" expresses excess; "to" and "two" are sound-alike traps. "Very tired to walk" is grammatically incomplete, so the answer is too.
Source: NCERT Class 8 English Grammar

34. Identify the adverb of degree: "The soup is very hot."
(a) soup (b) is (c) very (d) hot
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Very" modifies the adjective "hot" and tells to what degree, so it is an adverb of degree. "Hot" is an adjective, not an adverb, so the answer is very.
Source: NCERT Class 7 English Grammar

35. She ______ visits her grandmother on Sundays.
(a) frequent (b) frequently (c) frequency (d) frequenter
Answer: (b)
Solution: The verb "visits" needs an adverb, and "frequently" is the adverb form. "Frequent" is an adjective and "frequency" is a noun, so the answer is frequently.
Source: PYQ-style

36. Choose the correct sentence.
(a) He drives fastly. (b) He drives fasterly. (c) He drives fasting. (d) He drives fast.
Answer: (d)
Solution: "Fast" has no "-ly" form; it is the trap word "fastly" that examiners use. "Fast" itself serves as the adverb, so the answer is "He drives fast."
Source: NCERT Class 8 English Grammar

37. Identify the interrogative adverb: "When will you come?"
(a) When (b) will (c) you (d) come
Answer: (a)
Solution: "When" asks a question about time and modifies the verb "come", making it an interrogative adverb. "Will" is an auxiliary verb, so the answer is When.
Source: NCERT Class 7 English Grammar

38. The children played ______ in the park.
(a) happy (b) happily (c) happiness (d) happier
Answer: (b)
Solution: The verb "played" needs an adverb of manner; "happily" is correct. "Happy" is an adjective and cannot modify a verb, so the answer is happily.
Source: NCERT Class 7 English Grammar

39. I ______ go to bed early.
(a) usual (b) using (c) use (d) usually
Answer: (d)
Solution: An adverb of frequency is needed before the verb "go"; "usually" fits. "Usual" is an adjective, so the answer is usually.
Source: PYQ-style

40. Spot the error: Part P: He runs / Part Q: very fastly / Part R: in the morning. / Part S: No error
(a) Part P (b) No error (c) Part R (d) Part Q
Answer: (d)
Solution: Part Q contains "fastly", which does not exist in standard English — "fast" is itself the adverb. This is a classic examiner trap, so the error is in Part Q.
Source: PYQ-style

41. Choose the correct sentence.
(a) He is enough tall to reach the shelf. (b) He is tall enough to reach the shelf. (c) He is too tall enough to reach the shelf. (d) Enough he is tall to reach the shelf.
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Enough" comes after the adjective it modifies, so "tall enough" is correct; placing it before the adjective is the common trap. Hence the answer is "He is tall enough to reach the shelf."
Source: NCERT Class 9 English Grammar

42. She speaks English ______ than her brother.
(a) fluent (b) fluently (c) more fluently (d) most fluently
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Than" signals a comparison, so the comparative "more fluently" is needed. "Fluently" alone has no comparative force, so the answer is more fluently.
Source: PYQ-style

43. Identify the sentence where "only" is correctly placed to mean she ate nothing else.
(a) She only ate the rice. (b) Only she ate the rice. (c) She ate only the rice. (d) She ate the rice only yesterday.
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Only" modifies the word closest to it; "She ate only the rice" means she ate nothing but rice. Option (a) wrongly suggests eating was the only thing she did, so the answer is "She ate only the rice."
Source: NCERT Class 9 English Grammar

44. He ______ goes to the gym; maybe twice a year.
(a) always (b) often (c) seldom (d) usually
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Twice a year" shows rarity, so "seldom" (rarely) fits. "Always", "often" and "usually" contradict the clue, so the answer is seldom.
Source: PYQ-style

45. Spot the error: Part P: She sings / Part Q: more sweeter / Part R: than her sister. / Part S: No error
(a) Part P (b) Part Q (c) Part R (d) No error
Answer: (b)
Solution: Part Q has a double comparative — "more sweeter" is wrong because "sweeter" is already comparative (or use "more sweet"). This double-comparative trap is common in exams, so the error is in Part Q.
Source: PYQ-style

46. Choose the correctly framed sentence.
(a) He almost lost all his money. (b) Almost he lost all his money. (c) He lost almost all his money. (d) He lost all almost his money.
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Almost" should sit directly before what it modifies — "almost all his money". Option (a) changes the meaning to "nearly lost", so the answer is "He lost almost all his money."
Source: NCERT Class 9 English Grammar

47. The movie was ______ interesting that we watched it twice.
(a) very (b) so (c) too (d) much
Answer: (b)
Solution: The pattern is "so + adjective + that" for result clauses. "Too...that" is not standard and "very" does not pair with "that", so the answer is so.
Source: NCERT Class 9 English Grammar

48. Identify the adverb in: "He is quite intelligent."
(a) quite (b) is (c) He (d) intelligent
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Quite" modifies the adjective "intelligent" and shows degree, so it is an adverb. "Intelligent" is the adjective being modified, so the answer is quite.
Source: NCERT Class 8 English Grammar

49. Choose the correct option: "I have ______ finished my homework."
(a) yet (b) already (c) since (d) ever
Answer: (b)
Solution: In a positive present-perfect sentence, "already" is correct; "yet" is used in negatives and questions, which is the trap here. Hence the answer is already.
Source: PYQ-style

50. Spot the error: Part P: He explained / Part Q: the problem / Part R: very clearly yesterday evening. / Part S: No error
(a) No error (b) Part Q (c) Part R (d) Part P
Answer: (a)
Solution: The sentence is correct: "very" modifies the adverb "clearly", which modifies the verb "explained", and the time phrase is correctly placed. There is no error, so the answer is No error.
