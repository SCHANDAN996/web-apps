# 🧒 Feynman Technique — Idioms & Phrases, Explained to a 12-Year-Old

## The Hardest Concept: "Why can't I just translate the words?"

Imagine your friend Rahul says in Hindi: *"मेरी बात का मत सोचो"* — and you translate it word by word into English: *"Don't think of my talk."* An English speaker would be completely confused! The correct English is: *"Don't mind what I said."*

That is exactly what an **idiom** is. An idiom is a group of words whose real meaning is **different** from the meaning of each word added together.

### The Cricket Example 🏏

Suppose your teacher says: *"Ravi hit the books all night before the exam."*

- Did Ravi actually **hit** (मारना) his books? ❌ No!
- "Hit the books" = **to study hard**.

If you translate word by word, you get a funny picture of Ravi beating his books with a bat. But the idiom means something totally different. **The words are a disguise — the meaning hides behind them.**

### The Unforgettable Trick: 🎁 "The Gift Box Rule"

Think of every idiom as a **gift box**:

- The **words** are the wrapping paper — pretty, but not the gift.
- The **real meaning** is the gift **inside** the box.
- You can never guess the gift by staring at the wrapping!

So when you see *"to let the cat out of the bag"*, don't imagine a cat. Open the box: it means **"to reveal a secret."**

**Memory hook:** *"Idiom = Gift Box. Never judge the gift by its wrapper!"*

### Why do examiners love idioms?

Because they know most students try to translate word by word — and fall into the trap. In SSC, Railway and Bank exams, the wrong options are always the "word-by-word translation" meanings. The student who knows the **hidden meaning** wins in 15 seconds.

### Try it yourself (Blurting Sheet) 📝

Take a blank page. Without looking anywhere, **blurt out** (write fast) the meanings of these 5 idioms in your own words:

1. A piece of cake
2. To burn the midnight oil
3. Once in a blue moon
4. To kill two birds with one stone
5. Under the weather

Now check: (1) very easy, (2) to study/work late at night, (3) very rarely, (4) to achieve two things with one action, (5) feeling slightly ill.

**Question for you:** If your friend says *"I am feeling under the weather today,"* should you carry an umbrella for him — or ask about his health? Explain why, in one sentence, as if teaching your younger sibling.
