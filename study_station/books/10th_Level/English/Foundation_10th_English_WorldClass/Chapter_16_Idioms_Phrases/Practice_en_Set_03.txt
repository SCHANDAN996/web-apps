51. The idiom "to break the ice" means:
(a) To start a conversation and remove initial awkwardness (b) To destroy something frozen (c) To end a friendship (d) To cool down an argument
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Break the ice" refers to easing social tension at the start of a meeting or conversation, not literal ice. The literal meaning in option one is the classic trap. So the answer is to start a conversation and remove initial awkwardness.
Source: NCERT Class 9 English (Beehive)

52. "A piece of cake" means:
(a) Something very easy to do (b) A small portion (c) A delicious dessert (d) A birthday celebration
Answer: (a)
Solution: The idiom describes a task that is effortless. Learners often pick the food-related meaning, which is the literal trap. So the answer is something very easy to do.
Source: PYQ-style

53. "To hit the sack" means:
(a) To punch a bag (b) To go to sleep (c) To lose a job (d) To pack luggage
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Hit the sack" (or "hit the hay") is informal English for going to bed. Losing a job is "to get the sack", a near-identical phrase with a different meaning — a common confusion. So the answer is to go to sleep.
Source: PYQ-style

54. "Once in a blue moon" means:
(a) Every night (b) Once a month (c) During a festival (d) Very rarely
Answer: (d)
Solution: A blue moon is an uncommon event, so the idiom means something that happens very rarely. "Once a month" sounds plausible because of the word "moon" but is wrong. So the answer is very rarely.
Source: NCERT Class 8 English (Honeydew)
