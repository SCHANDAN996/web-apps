Card 1
Front: What is an idiom?
Back: A group of words whose meaning is different from the literal meaning of the individual words. Example: "to kick the bucket" = to die, not to hit a bucket.

Card 2
Front: What does "a blessing in disguise" mean?
Back: Something that seems bad at first but turns out to be good. Example: Losing that job was a blessing in disguise — he found a better one.

Card 3
Front: What does "to burn the midnight oil" mean?
Back: To study or work late into the night. (Oil lamps were used for light at night in old times.)

Card 4
Front: What does "once in a blue moon" mean?
Back: Very rarely. A "blue moon" (second full moon in one month) is a rare event.

Card 5
Front: MNEMONIC — How to remember "to bite the dust"?
Back: Picture a soldier falling face-first into the DUST in a battle — it means to fail, be defeated, or die. "Bite the Dust = Defeated in the Dust."

Card 6
Front: What does "to let the cat out of the bag" mean?
Back: To reveal a secret, usually by mistake.

Card 7
Front: EXAMINER'S TRAP — "He is in the good books of his boss." Does this mean he reads good books?
Back: No. "To be in someone's good books" means to be in someone's favour / to be liked by them. Trap: literal reading of "books".

Card 8
Front: What does "to hit the nail on the head" mean?
Back: To say or do exactly the right thing; to describe a situation perfectly.

Card 9
Front: What does "an apple of one's eye" mean?
Back: Someone who is loved very much; very dear. Example: His daughter is the apple of his eye.

Card 10
Front: What does "to add insult to injury" mean?
Back: To make a bad situation even worse.

Card 11
Front: MNEMONIC — How to remember "to sit on the fence"?
Back: Imagine sitting ON a fence between two fields — you cannot choose either side. It means to avoid taking sides / remain neutral. "Fence = can't decide."

Card 12
Front: What does "to break the ice" mean?
Back: To start a conversation and remove initial awkwardness or silence.

Card 13
Front: EXAMINER'S TRAP — "She has a bee in her bonnet about cleanliness." Is there a real bee?
Back: No. "To have a bee in one's bonnet" means to be obsessed with an idea; to keep thinking and talking about one thing. Trap: literal image of a bee in a hat.

Card 14
Front: What does "to face the music" mean?
Back: To accept the unpleasant consequences of one's actions; to face criticism or punishment.

Card 15
Front: What does "a red herring" mean?
Back: Something that misleads or distracts from the real issue. (Very common in comprehension and reasoning questions too.)

Card 16
Front: What does "to kill two birds with one stone" mean?
Back: To achieve two results with a single action.

Card 17
Front: What does "at the drop of a hat" mean?
Back: Immediately, without hesitation. Example: He is ready to help at the drop of a hat.

Card 18
Front: What does "to wash one's dirty linen in public" mean?
Back: To discuss private or family problems openly in front of others.

Card 19
Front: What does "a bolt from the blue" mean?
Back: A sudden, unexpected event or shock (like lightning from a clear blue sky).

Card 20
Front: What does "to take something with a grain of salt" mean?
Back: To not completely believe something; to doubt whether it is fully true.
