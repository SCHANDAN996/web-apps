1. The idiom "to bite the dust" means:
(a) to eat food in a hurry (b) to work very hard (c) to clean the floor (d) to suffer defeat or failure
Answer: (d)
Solution: "To bite the dust" is a fixed idiom meaning to be defeated, fail, or come to an end. The literal image of dust misleads students into choosing options about eating or cleaning. The correct answer is to suffer defeat or failure.
Source: PYQ-style

(a) a good thing that seemed bad at first (b) a gift from God (c) a hidden curse (d) a person in a mask
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is to take "disguise" literally and pick the option about a mask. The idiom means something that appears bad but turns out to be good, so the answer is a good thing that seemed bad at first.

3. Choose the correct meaning of "to break the ice":
(a) to end a friendship (b) to destroy something valuable (c) to cool down a drink (d) to start a conversation and remove awkwardness
Answer: (d)
Solution: "Breaking the ice" means initiating friendly talk in a tense or silent situation. It has nothing to do with actual ice or destruction, so the answer is to start a conversation and remove awkwardness.
