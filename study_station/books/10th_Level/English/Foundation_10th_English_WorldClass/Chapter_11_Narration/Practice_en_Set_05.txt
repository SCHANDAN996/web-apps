101. He said to me, "Do you know where the station is?"
(a) He asked me did I know where the station was. (b) He asked me if I knew where the station was. (c) He asked me that if I knew where the station is. (d) He asked me whether do I know where the station was.
Answer: (b)
Solution: The tempting wrong option keeps the question-word order "did I know", but in reported speech the clause becomes a statement, so the subject comes before the verb. "Do you know" is a yes/no question, so we use "if/whether", and "know" shifts to "knew", "is" to "was". So the answer is "He asked me if I knew where the station was."

102. She said, "I was cooking dinner when the lights went out."
(a) She said that she is cooking dinner when the lights went out. (b) She said that she was cooking dinner when the lights had gone out. (c) She said that she had been cooking dinner when the lights went out. (d) She said that she had cooked dinner when the lights went out.
Answer: (c)
Solution: Past continuous ("was cooking") changes to past perfect continuous ("had been cooking") in reported speech, while the simple past "went" stays as simple past after "when". The option that changes "went" to "had gone" over-shifts the tense. So the answer is "She said that she had been cooking dinner when the lights went out."
Source: NCERT Class 10 English

103. The teacher said, "The earth revolves around the sun."
(a) The teacher said that the earth revolved around the sun. (b) The teacher said that the earth had revolved around the sun. (c) The teacher said that the earth was revolving around the sun. (d) The teacher said that the earth revolves around the sun.
Answer: (d)
Solution: Universal truths and scientific facts do not change tense in reported speech, even when the reporting verb is in the past. The trap option "revolved" wrongly applies the backshift rule to a permanent fact. So the answer is "The teacher said that the earth revolves around the sun."

(a) He told her please lend him her pen. (b) He requested her that lend him your pen. (c) He requested to her to lend him her pen. (d) He requested her to lend him her pen.
Answer: (d)
Solution: A polite request with "please" is reported with "requested + object + to-infinitive"; "your" changes to "her" to match the listener. "Requested to her" is wrong because "request" takes the object directly without "to". So the answer is "He requested her to lend him her pen."

105. Mother said to me, "Do not go out in the rain."
(a) Mother told me do not go out in the rain. (b) Mother said me not to go out in the rain. (c) Mother ordered me to not went out in the rain. (d) Mother forbade me to go out in the rain.
Answer: (d)
Solution: A negative imperative is reported as "forbade + object + to-infinitive" or "told + object + not to + verb". "Said me" is ungrammatical (say takes "to" before the person), and "to not went" mixes infinitive with past tense. So the answer is "Mother forbade me to go out in the rain."

Answer: (b)
Solution: Exclamations with "what a" are reported with "exclaimed that" and "very" replaces the exclamatory force; "is" shifts to "was" and "this" to "it/that". Keeping "what a" inside the reported clause is the common trap. So the answer is "She exclaimed that it was a very beautiful garden."
