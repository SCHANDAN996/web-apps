51. He said, "I am happy."
(a) He said that he is happy. (b) He said that I was happy. (c) He said that he was happy. (d) He said that he had been happy.
Answer: (c)
Solution: In indirect speech, the present simple "am" changes to past simple "was", and "I" changes to "he" to match the subject. The tempting option keeps "is" unchanged, which is wrong when the reporting verb is in the past. So the answer is "He said that he was happy."

52. She said to me, "I like your book."
(a) She told me that she liked my book. (b) She told me that she likes my book. (c) She told me that I liked my book. (d) She said me that she liked my book.
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Said to me" becomes "told me"; "like" becomes "liked" and "your" becomes "my". The trap option "said me" is ungrammatical because "say" never takes a direct personal object. So the answer is "She told me that she liked my book."

Answer: (d)
Solution: Universal truths do not change tense in indirect speech, so "rises" stays "rises". The tempting option changes it to "rose", which is the common trap. So the answer is "The teacher said that the sun rises in the east."

Solution: Negative commands use "forbade" or "told... not to". The trap option "told me to not go" splits the infinitive awkwardly; the standard form is "not to go" or "forbade". So the answer is "Mother forbade me to go out in the rain."
