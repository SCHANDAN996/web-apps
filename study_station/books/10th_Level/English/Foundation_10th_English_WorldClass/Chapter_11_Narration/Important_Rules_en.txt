# 📋 Important Grammar Rules & Tips — Direct & Indirect Speech (Narration)

## (a) 12 High-Yield Rules

| # | Rule | Example | Examiner's Trap |
|---|------|---------|-----------------|
| 1 | **Backshift of tense**: When the reporting verb is in the past, the tense inside the quote moves one step back (Present Simple → Past Simple, Present Continuous → Past Continuous, Present Perfect → Past Perfect, Past Simple → Past Perfect). | He said, "I **am** tired." → He said that he **was** tired. | If the reporting verb is in the **present/future** ("He says…"), the tense does **NOT** change. Don't backshift blindly. |
| 2 | **Universal truths & habitual facts never change tense**, even after a past reporting verb. | The teacher said, "The earth **revolves** around the sun." → The teacher said that the earth **revolves** around the sun. | Options often show "revolved" — it looks like correct backshift but is wrong. |
| 3 | **Pronoun change (SON rule)**: **S**ubject of reporting verb governs 1st person; **O**bject governs 2nd person; **N**o change for 3rd person. | He said to me, "**I** will help **you**." → He told me that **he** would help **me**. | "You" changes according to the **object** (me → me; her → her), not always to "me". |
| 4 | **'Said to' → 'told'** (with an object), but **'said' stays 'said'** when there is no object. Never write "told that" without an object. | He said to me, "I am busy." → He **told me** that he was busy. / He said, "I am busy." → He **said** that he was busy. | "He told that he was busy" ❌ — "told" must have an object (told me/him/her). |
| 5 | **Time & place words change**: now→then, today→that day, tomorrow→the next day, yesterday→the previous day, ago→before, here→there, this→that, these→those. | She said, "I came here **yesterday**." → She said that she had gone there **the previous day**. | "Last night" → "the previous night / the night before" — not "the last night". |
| 6 | **Questions with a question word (Wh-)**: keep the Wh-word as connector; change the question into statement order (subject before verb); remove the question mark. | He said to me, "**Where** do you live?" → He asked me **where I lived**. | Trap: "where did I live" ❌ — indirect speech has no inversion and no "do/does/did". |
| 7 | **Yes/No questions**: use **if/whether** as connector; statement order; no question mark. | She said to him, "**Are** you ready?" → She asked him **if/whether he was** ready. | "Whether or not" is acceptable, but "if" + question order ("if was he") is a classic wrong option. |
| 8 | **Commands/Requests (Imperatives)**: reporting verb → ordered/requested/advised/forbade + object + **to + V1** (negative: **not to + V1**). | He said to me, "Open the door." → He **ordered me to open** the door. / "Don't run." → He **forbade me to run** / ordered me **not to run**. | "Please" signals **requested**, not "ordered". Match the verb to the tone. |
| 9 | **Exclamations**: reporting verb → exclaimed with joy/sorrow/surprise + that; "What a/How" structure becomes "very/great". | He said, "**What a** beautiful day!" → He exclaimed with joy that it **was a very** beautiful day. | "Alas!" = sorrow, "Hurrah!" = joy, "Wow!/Oh!" = surprise/wonder — exams swap these. |
| 10 | **Modals**: can→could, may→might, shall→should/would, will→would. **Could, would, should, might, ought to, must (deduction)** stay unchanged. | He said, "I **can** swim." → He said that he **could** swim. | "Must" for obligation → "had to"; "must" for deduction ("You must be tired") stays "must". |
| 11 | **'Let' sentences**: suggestion → proposed/suggested + that + subject + should + V1; permission → requested + to be allowed to. | He said, "**Let** us go for a walk." → He **suggested that they should go** for a walk. | "Let me do it" (permission) ≠ "Let us play" (suggestion) — different conversions. |
| 12 | **Optative (wishes/prayers)**: reporting verb → wished/prayed/blessed/cursed + that + subject + might + V1. | She said, "**May** God bless you!" → She **prayed that God might bless** me. | "Goodbye/Good morning" → "bade/wished him goodbye/good morning" — not "said that". |

## (b) Time Management — The 15/45-Second Rule

| Type | What it looks like | Time | Strategy |
|------|-------------------|------|----------|
| **Fact-based (15 sec)** | Direct tense backshift, pronoun change, said→told, time-word change, can→could. One clear rule, one clear answer. | ⏱️ 15 sec | Spot the reporting verb tense → apply the single rule → eliminate options with unchanged tense or wrong pronoun → lock the answer. Do NOT re-read. |
| **Inference-based (45 sec)** | Mixed sentences (question + command), "let" sentences, must/had to distinction, universal-truth traps, choosing between ordered/requested/advised/forbade based on tone. | ⏱️ 45 sec | Read the full sentence twice. Identify (1) sentence type, (2) tone of the speaker, (3) any frozen element (universal truth, could/would). Then convert step by step. |

**Rule of thumb:** If only the tense/pronoun changes → 15 sec. If you must judge the speaker's *intention* (order vs request vs advice) or handle "let/must/universal truth" → 45 sec.

## (c) Morphology — Roots & Affixes in Narration Vocabulary

| Word | Root / Affix | Meaning of root | Breakdown | Related words |
|------|--------------|-----------------|-----------|---------------|
| **Narration** | Latin *narrare* = to tell | tell | narrate + -ion (act of) | narrator, narrative, narrate |
| **Report** | Latin *re-* (back) + *portare* (carry) | carry back | re + port | reporter, portable, transport, import |
| **Dictate / Dictum** | Latin *dicere/dictum* = to say | say | dict- | predict, verdict, dictionary, contradict |
| **Exclaim** | Latin *ex-* (out) + *clamare* (shout) | shout out | ex + claim | exclamation, proclaim, clamour, acclaim |
| **Pronoun** | Latin *pro-* (for) + *nomen/noun* (name) | in place of a name | pro + noun | pronoun, pronominal |
| **Assert** | Latin *ad-* (to) + *serere* (join) | join to, declare | as + sert | assertion, insert, desert (verb: to abandon) |
| **Request** | Latin *re-* (again) + *quaerere* (seek) | seek again | re + quest | question, query, acquire, require |
| **Statement** | Latin *stare* = to stand | stand (firm) | state + -ment | static, station, status, statue |

**Memory tip:** Narration is literally "re-porting" — *carrying back* (re + port) what someone said. Every tense backshift is the sentence "travelling back" in time with the report.
