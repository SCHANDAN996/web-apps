# Direct & Indirect Speech (Narration)

## 🎯 The Hook: You Are a Messenger, Not a Parrot

Imagine your friend Ravi says to you: **"I will come to Delhi tomorrow."**
An hour later, your teacher asks, "What did Ravi say?"

You have two choices:
1. Repeat his exact words → *Ravi said, "I will come to Delhi tomorrow."* (**Direct Speech**)
2. Report the message in your own words → *Ravi said that he would come to Delhi the next day.* (**Indirect Speech**)

Every exam — SSC GD, SSC MTS, RRB Group D, State Police — tests whether you can be a **smart messenger**: changing tenses, pronouns, and time words correctly. Narration questions appear in almost every English paper, so mastering this chapter pays off again and again.

---

## 📌 Learning Objectives

By the end of this chapter, you will be able to:

1. Identify Direct and Indirect Speech instantly.
2. Apply the **backshift of tense** rule without confusion.
3. Change pronouns using the **SON rule** (Subject–Object–No change).
4. Convert time and place expressions (now → then, today → that day).
5. Handle all 5 sentence types: Statements, Questions, Commands, Exclamations, and Optatives.
6. Spot the examiner's favourite traps in under 15 seconds.

## 1️⃣ What Are Direct and Indirect Speech?

**Direct Speech** = the speaker's *exact words*, kept inside inverted commas (" ").
**Indirect (Reported) Speech** = the *message* reported by someone else, without inverted commas.

| Part | Example | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| Reporting Verb | He **said** | the verb that introduces the speech |
| Reported Speech | **"I am happy."** | the actual words spoken |

> He said **that** he **was** happy. ✅ (Indirect)

**Key idea:** When we report speech, we usually take **one step back in time** (present → past). This is called the **backshift**.

🧠 **Active Recall:** In "She said that she was tired," which word is the reporting verb? What tense shift happened to "am"?

## 2️⃣ The Golden Backshift Table (Tense Changes)

When the reporting verb is in the **past** (said, told), the tense inside the speech moves one step back:

| Direct Speech | Indirect Speech | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Simple Present | Simple Past | "I **play** cricket." → He said he **played** cricket. |
| Present Continuous | Past Continuous | "I **am playing**." → He said he **was playing**. |
| Present Perfect | Past Perfect | "I **have won**." → He said he **had won**. |
| Simple Past | Past Perfect | "I **went**." → He said he **had gone**. |
| will / shall | would / should | "I **will go**." → He said he **would go**. |
| can | could | "I **can swim**." → He said he **could swim**. |
| may | might | "It **may rain**." → He said it **might rain**. |

⚠️ **No change needed** when:
- The reporting verb is in present/future: He **says**, "I am ill." → He says that he **is** ill.
- The sentence is a **universal truth**: The teacher said, "The sun **rises** in the east." → The teacher said that the sun **rises** in the east.

🧠 **Active Recall:** Convert — She said, "I have finished my work." (Answer: She said that she had finished her work.)

## 3️⃣ Pronoun Changes — The SON Rule

Pronouns change according to the **SON rule**:

- **S** — First person (I, we, my, our) changes according to the **Subject** of the reporting verb.
- **O** — Second person (you, your) changes according to the **Object** of the reporting verb.
- **N** — Third person (he, she, it, they) — **No change**.

**Example:** He said to me, "**I** will help **you**."
→ He told me that **he** would help **me**.
(I → he, matching subject "He"; you → me, matching object "me")

## 4️⃣ Time & Place Word Changes

| Direct | Indirect |
|---|---|
| now | then |
| today | that day |
| tonight | that night |
| yesterday | the day before / the previous day |
| tomorrow | the next day / the following day |
| ago | before |
| here | there |
| this | that |
| these | those |
| last night | the night before |

## 5️⃣ The Five Sentence Types

### (a) Statements — use *said / told + that*
- He said to me, "I am busy." → He **told me that** he was busy.
- ⚠️ *Told* must have an object (told **me**); *said* does not need one.

### (b) Questions
- **Yes/No questions** → use *asked + if/whether*; make it a statement (no question mark, subject before verb).
  - He said to me, "**Are** you **ill**?" → He asked me **if I was ill**.
- **Wh-questions** → use *asked + wh-word*; again, statement order.
  - She said, "**Where do** you **live**?" → She asked me **where I lived**.

### (c) Commands & Requests — use *ordered / requested / advised + to / not to*
- The teacher said, "**Open** your books." → The teacher **ordered us to open** our books.
- He said, "Please help me." → He **requested me to help** him.
- Mother said, "**Do not** go out." → Mother **forbade me to go** out. (or: told me **not to go** out)

### (d) Exclamations — use *exclaimed with joy/sorrow/wonder + that*
- He said, "**What a** beautiful day!" → He **exclaimed with joy that** it was a very beautiful day.
- She said, "**Alas!** I am ruined." → She **exclaimed with sorrow that** she was ruined.

### (e) Optatives (wishes) — use *wished/prayed + that*
- He said, "**May** God bless you!" → He **prayed that** God might bless me.

## 🧠 Mnemonic: **"P-T-T" — The Messenger's Checklist**

> **P**ronoun (SON rule) → **T**ense (one step back) → **T**ime words (now → then)
> Check all three, and your conversion is complete!

## ⚠️ EXAMINER'S TRAP BOX

**Trap 1 — The Question Mark Trick:**
❌ He asked me where did I live?
✅ He asked me where I lived.
*Indirect questions are statements — no inversion, no question mark.*

**Trap 2 — Universal Truth Trap:**
❌ The teacher said that the earth **revolved** around the sun.
✅ The teacher said that the earth **revolves** around the sun.
*Universal truths never change tense.*

**Trap 3 — "Said to" vs "Told":**
❌ He told that he was tired.
✅ He said that he was tired. / He told **me** that he was tired.
*"Told" always needs an object.*

---

## 🏆 12 Golden Rules of Narration

1. Reporting verb in past → tense goes one step back.
2. Reporting verb in present/future → **no tense change**.
3. Universal truths and habitual facts → **no tense change**.
4. Pronouns follow the **SON rule**.
5. "Said to" + object becomes **"told"**; "told" must have an object.
6. Statements join with **that**.
7. Yes/No questions join with **if/whether**; use statement order.
8. Wh-questions keep the **wh-word** as the connector.
9. Commands/requests → **to + V1**; negative commands → **not to + V1**.
10. Exclamations → **exclaimed with joy/sorrow + that**; remove "What a/How".
11. "May" in wishes → **might** with *wished/prayed*.
12. Time words shift: now→then, today→that day, tomorrow→the next day.

## 🔁 Linguistic Bridge: Hindi Speaker's Common Errors

| हिंदी में सोच | ❌ Wrong English | ✅ Correct English |
|---|---|---|
| "उसने मुझसे कहा कि मैं कहाँ जा रहा था" | He told me that where I was going. | He told me where I was going. |
| "उसने पूछा कि तुम कहाँ रहते हो?" | He asked me where do I live. | He asked me where I lived. |
| "उसने कहा मैं कल आऊँगा" | He said that he will come tomorrow. | He said that he would come the next day. |
| "मत जाओ" (आदेश) | He said me to not go. | He told me not to go. |

*Remember: Hindi keeps the question form inside reported speech; English flattens it into a statement.*
