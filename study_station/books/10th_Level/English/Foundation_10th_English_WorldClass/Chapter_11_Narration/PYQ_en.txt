# 📊 PYQ Analysis — Direct & Indirect Speech (Narration)

## How Exams Test This Chapter

Narration is one of the most **reliable, high-frequency** topics in SSC (CGL, CHSL, MTS, GD), Railway (NTPC, Group D), Bank and State exams. Almost every English paper carries questions from it, and the pattern is remarkably stable. Examiners do not invent new grammar — they recycle the **same conversion rules** through fresh sentences. If you master the rules, these are the fastest marks in the paper.

### What Examiners Repeatedly Ask (Pattern in Words)

- **Tense back-shift** in reported statements (Present Simple → Past Simple, Present Perfect → Past Perfect, will → would). This is the single most tested idea.
- **Pronoun changes** (I → he/she, my → his/her, we → they) using the S-O-N logic (Subject–Object–No change).
- **Time/place word changes**: now → then, today → that day, tomorrow → the next day, yesterday → the previous day, here → there, this → that, ago → before.
- **Reporting verb choice**: said → told (with object), asked, ordered, requested, exclaimed, advised.
- **Question conversions**: Wh-questions keep the Wh-word; Yes/No questions take **if/whether**; the question becomes a **statement** (no inversion, no question mark).
- **Imperative sentences**: ordered/requested/advised + object + **to-infinitive**; negative commands → "not to".
- **Exclamatory sentences**: exclaimed with joy/sorrow/surprise + that + statement form ("What a!" / "How!" removed).
- **Universal truths and habitual facts**: tense does **not** change ("The sun rises in the east" stays as it is).

### The Examiner's Favourite Traps

1. **The "told" trap** — options like "He told that…" with no object. *Told* must take an object (told me/him); *said* does not.
2. **The inversion trap** — keeping question order in indirect speech: "He asked where was I" ❌. Correct: "He asked where I was."
3. **The question-mark trap** — an indirect question never ends with "?".
4. **The universal-truth trap** — changing the tense of a fact just because the reporting verb is past.
5. **The "if" trap in Wh-questions** — "He asked if where I lived" ❌. *If/whether* is only for Yes/No questions.
6. **The time-word trap** — forgetting to convert "tomorrow/yesterday/now" even when the tense is converted correctly.

## Top 10 High-Yield Questions (with Solutions)

**Q1.** He said, "I am busy today."
(a) He said that he is busy today.
(b) He said that he was busy that day.
(c) He said that he was busy today.
(d) He said that he is busy that day.

**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** Reporting verb is past, so "am" → "was" and "today" → "that day". Options (a) and (d) fail the tense back-shift; (c) fails the time-word change.
**Trap type:** Sound-Right (half-converted options feel correct).
**Source:** PYQ-style

---

**Q2.** She said to me, "Do you like tea?"
(a) She asked me that did I like tea.
(b) She asked me if I liked tea.
(c) She asked me if did I like tea.
(d) She asked me whether I like tea.

**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** Yes/No question → "asked + if/whether" + statement order. "Do you like" → "I liked" (back-shift). (a) and (c) keep the helping verb "did" — the inversion trap; (d) misses the tense change.
**Trap type:** Intervening Phrase (extra "did/that" inserted to confuse).
**Source:** PYQ-style

---

**Q3.** The teacher said, "The earth revolves around the sun."
(a) The teacher said that the earth revolved around the sun.
(b) The teacher said that the earth had revolved around the sun.
(c) The teacher said that the earth revolves around the sun.
(d) The teacher said that the earth was revolving around the sun.

**Answer: (c)**
**Solution:** Universal truths and scientific facts do **not** change tense in indirect speech, even with a past reporting verb.
**Trap type:** Over-thinking (students mechanically back-shift everything).
**Source:** NCERT Class 9 English (Beehive — grammar)

---

**Q4.** He said to her, "Where do you live?"
(a) He asked her where did she live.
(b) He asked her where she lived.
(c) He asked her that where she lived.
(d) He asked her if where she lived.

**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** Wh-question → keep the Wh-word, use statement order, back-shift "do you live" → "she lived". No "that", no "if", no inversion.
**Trap type:** Intervening Phrase (that/if wrongly inserted before the Wh-word).
**Source:** PYQ-style

---

**Q5.** The captain said to the soldiers, "March forward."
(a) The captain said the soldiers to march forward.
(b) The captain ordered the soldiers to march forward.
(c) The captain ordered the soldiers that march forward.
(d) The captain told the soldiers march forward.

**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** Imperative (command) → ordered + object + to-infinitive. "Said to" becomes "ordered" because it is a command, not a request.
**Trap type:** Sound-Right ("said… to" looks close to the original).
**Source:** PYQ-style

---

**Q6.** Mother said to me, "Do not go out in the rain."
(a) Mother told me do not go out in the rain.
(b) Mother forbade me to go out in the rain.
(c) Mother said me not to go out in the rain.
(d) Mother requested me to not go out in the rain.

**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** Negative command → "forbade + object + to-infinitive" (or "told me not to go"). "Forbade" already carries the negative sense, so "not" is dropped. (a) has no structure; (c) misuses "said me"; (d) splits the infinitive awkwardly and changes command to request.
**Trap type:** Over-thinking (keeping "not" with "forbade" would be a double negative).
**Source:** PYQ-style

---

**Q7.** He said, "Alas! I have lost my wallet."
(a) He said with sorrow that he lost his wallet.
(b) He exclaimed with sorrow that he had lost his wallet.
(c) He exclaimed with joy that he had lost his wallet.
(d) He cried that he has lost his wallet.

**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** "Alas!" → exclaimed with sorrow; "have lost" → "had lost" (Present Perfect → Past Perfect). (c) has the wrong emotion; (a) and (d) miss the tense shift.
**Trap type:** Sound-Right (emotion word swapped).
**Source:** PYQ-style

---

**Q8.** She said, "I will help you tomorrow."
(a) She said that she will help me tomorrow.
(b) She said that she would help me the next day.
(c) She said that she would help me tomorrow.
(d) She said that she will help me the next day.

**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** "will" → "would", "you" → "me" (object of reporting verb's listener), "tomorrow" → "the next day". Both changes must happen together.
**Trap type:** Sound-Right (options change only one of the two).
**Source:** PYQ-style

---

**Q9.** He said, "I wrote a letter yesterday."
(a) He said that he had written a letter the previous day.
(b) He said that he wrote a letter yesterday.
(c) He said that he has written a letter the previous day.
(d) He said that he had written a letter yesterday.

**Answer: (a)**
**Solution:** Past Simple → Past Perfect ("wrote" → "had written"); "yesterday" → "the previous day". Both conversions are compulsory.
**Trap type:** Intervening Phrase (mixing correct tense with unchanged time word).
**Source:** PYQ-style

---

**Q10.** The boy said, "Hurrah! We have won the match."
(a) The boy exclaimed with joy that they had won the match.
(b) The boy said with joy that they have won the match.
(c) The boy exclaimed with sorrow that they had won the match.
(d) The boy exclaimed with joy that they won the match yesterday.

**Answer: (a)**
**Solution:** "Hurrah!" → exclaimed with joy; "we" → "they"; "have won" → "had won". No time word exists in the original, so none should be added — (d) invents "yesterday".
**Trap type:** Over-thinking (adding information not present in the original).
**Source:** PYQ-style

## Applying the 15/45-Second Rule

**Q3 (Universal Truth) — 15 seconds, Fact-based.**
Spot the reporting verb "said" + a scientific fact. Rule: universal truths never change tense. Eliminate (a), (b), (d) instantly. **Do not overthink** — this is a pure recall question. Target: 15 seconds.

**Q6 (Negative Command) — 45 seconds, Application-based.**
This needs two steps: (1) identify it as a negative imperative, (2) choose between "told me not to go" and "forbade me to go". Check each option for structure errors — "said me" is wrong, "do not go" without "to" is wrong, "to not go" is a split infinitive. Elimination takes longer, so budget up to 45 seconds. If still unsure after 45 seconds, mark "forbade" (the standard exam answer for negative commands) and move on.

---

## Key Takeaways from the Pattern

- **One question, two conversions** — examiners almost always test tense + time-word or tense + pronoun together. Check both before answering.
- **Statement order in indirect questions** is the highest-yield single rule — master it and a large share of narration questions become 15-second questions.
- **Universal truths, "told" + object, and if/whether** appear in some form in nearly every exam cycle.
- Narration questions are **fact-based, not inference-based** — if you are spending more than 45 seconds, you are over-thinking; apply the rule and move on.
