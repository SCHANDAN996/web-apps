Card 1
Front: What is Direct Speech?
Back: Reporting the exact words of the speaker inside inverted commas (" "). Example: He said, "I am tired."

Card 2
Front: What is Indirect (Reported) Speech?
Back: Reporting the speaker's words in our own words without inverted commas. Example: He said that he was tired.

Card 3
Front: What happens to the tense when the reporting verb is in the past tense?
Back: The tense shifts one step back: Present Simple → Past Simple, Present Continuous → Past Continuous, Present Perfect → Past Perfect, Past Simple → Past Perfect, will → would, can → could.

Card 4
Front: Mnemonic: How do you remember the pronoun change rule (S-O-N)?
Back: S-O-N = "SON": First person changes according to the Subject, Second person according to the Object, Third person No change.

Card 5
Front: He said to me, "I will help you." — Convert to Indirect Speech.
Back: He told me that he would help me. (said to → told; will → would; I → he (subject); you → me (object))

Card 6
Front: EXAMINER'S TRAP: If the reporting verb is in the present or future tense (says/will say), does the tense of the reported speech change?
Back: No! Only pronouns and time/place words change. He says, "I am busy." → He says that he is busy. (NOT "was busy")

Card 7
Front: How do time and place words change in Indirect Speech?
Back: now → then, today → that day, tomorrow → the next day, yesterday → the previous day, here → there, this → that, ago → before.

Card 8
Front: How is a Yes/No question converted into Indirect Speech?
Back: Use "if" or "whether" (no "that"), change the question into statement order, and use asked/enquired. He said, "Are you ready?" → He asked if/whether I was ready.

Card 9
Front: How is a Wh-question converted into Indirect Speech?
Back: Keep the Wh-word as the connector (no "that"), and use statement order (subject before verb). She said, "Where do you live?" → She asked where I lived.

Card 10
Front: EXAMINER'S TRAP: She asked me where did I live. — What is wrong?
Back: In reported questions, use statement order, not question order. Correct: She asked me where I lived. (Remove "did"; verb shifts to past.)

Card 11
Front: How are commands and requests converted into Indirect Speech?
Back: Use ordered/commanded/requested + object + to-infinitive (for negative: not to). He said, "Open the door." → He ordered me to open the door. "Don't run." → He told me not to run.

Card 12
Front: How are exclamatory sentences converted?
Back: Use exclaimed with joy/sorrow/surprise + that; remove the exclamation mark and interjections. He said, "Alas! I am ruined." → He exclaimed with sorrow that he was ruined.

Card 13
Front: Mnemonic: How do you remember time-word changes?
Back: "Today is THAT DAY, Tomorrow is the NEXT DAY, Yesterday is the PREVIOUS DAY, Now becomes THEN, Here becomes THERE." — chant it as "That–Next–Previous–Then–There".

Card 14
Front: When does the tense NOT change even with a past reporting verb?
Back: For universal truths, proverbs and habitual facts: He said, "The sun rises in the east." → He said that the sun rises in the east. (Note: a present/future reporting verb is a separate case — then too the tense stays unchanged.)

Card 15
Front: He said, "I have finished my work." — Convert.
Back: He said that he had finished his work. (Present Perfect → Past Perfect)

Card 16
Front: How are sentences beginning with "Let" (suggestions/proposals) converted?
Back: Use proposed/suggested + that + subject + should + verb. He said, "Let us go for a walk." → He suggested that they should go for a walk.

Card 17
Front: The teacher said, "Honesty is the best policy." — Convert.
Back: The teacher said that honesty is the best policy. (Universal truth — tense does not change.)

Card 18
Front: What changes happen to "said to" in different sentence types?
Back: Statement → told; Question → asked/enquired; Command/Request → ordered/requested/advised; Exclamation → exclaimed; Wish/Prayer → wished/prayed.

Card 19
Front: She said to me, "Do you like tea?" — Convert.
Back: She asked me if/whether I liked tea. (Yes/No question → if/whether; do you like → I liked.)

Card 20
Front: He said, "I was playing cricket yesterday." — Convert.
Back: He said that he had been playing cricket the previous day. (Past Continuous → Past Perfect Continuous; yesterday → the previous day.)
