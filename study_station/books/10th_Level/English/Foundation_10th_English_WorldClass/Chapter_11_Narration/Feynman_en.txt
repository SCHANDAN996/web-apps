# 🧒 Feynman Technique — Direct & Indirect Speech

## The Hardest Concept, Explained to a 12-Year-Old

Imagine you and your friend Ravi are playing cricket. Ravi shouts:

> **Ravi said, "I am hitting the ball now."**

Now you run home and tell your mother what Ravi said. You have **two ways** to do it:

**Way 1 — Copy-paste (Direct Speech):**
You repeat his *exact* words, inside quotes:
> Ravi said, **"I am hitting the ball now."**

**Way 2 — WhatsApp forward (Indirect Speech):**
You *report* it in your own words, no quotes:
> Ravi said **that he was hitting the ball then.**

That's it. That is the whole chapter. Direct Speech = a **photocopy** of the words. Indirect Speech = a **forwarded message** — same meaning, but the words adjust to the new situation.

## Why Do the Words Change? The "Time Travel" Trick 🕰️

When you forward the message, three things travel and must change their tickets:

1. **The Person (Pronoun):** Ravi's "I" becomes "he" — because now *you* are talking about *him*.
2. **The Time (Tense):** "am hitting" steps one step back into the past → "was hitting". Why? Because Ravi said it *earlier*, and you're reporting it *later*. Everything moves one step back in time.
3. **The Place/Time words:** "now" → "then", "here" → "there", "today" → "that day".

### The Unforgettable Trick: **"SON" Rule**

> **S** — Said that (reporting verb + that)
> **O** — One step back (tense goes one step into the past)
> **N** — Now becomes Then (all time/place words shift)

If you remember **SON**, you can convert 90% of exam sentences.

### Watch it work:

| Direct (photocopy) | Indirect (forward) |
|---|---|
| He said, "I **am** happy **today**." | He said that he **was** happy **that day**. |
| She said, "I **will** come **tomorrow**." | She said that she **would** come **the next day**. |
| He said to me, "**Do** you like tea?" | He asked me **if** I liked tea. |

Notice the last one — a **question** doesn't take "that"; it takes **if/whether** (for yes/no questions) or the **Wh-word** itself. And the question mark disappears — the sentence becomes a normal statement: "if I liked tea", not "if did I like tea".

## The One Thing That NEVER Changes

If the reporting verb is in the **present or future** ("He says…", "He will say…"), the tense inside **does not move back**:
> He says, "I am tired." → He says that he **is** tired. ✅ (No time travel — the message is fresh!)

Also, **universal truths** never change tense:
> The teacher said, "The sun rises in the east." → The teacher said that the sun **rises** in the east. ✅
