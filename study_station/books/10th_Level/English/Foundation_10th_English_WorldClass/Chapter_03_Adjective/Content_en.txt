# ADJECTIVE — The Word That Describes

## 🎯 Why This Chapter Can Win You 3–5 Marks

Imagine two sentences:

- "I saw a dog."
- "I saw a **huge, black, ferocious** dog."

The second sentence paints a picture. That picture-painting power comes from **Adjectives** — and examiners at SSC GD, SSC MTS, RRB Group D and State Police exams love testing them. Almost every error-spotting or fill-in-the-blank section contains at least one adjective question: *"She is more taller than her sister"* ❌ — can you spot the mistake? By the end of this chapter, you will.

### Learning Objectives
After this chapter you will be able to:
1. Define an adjective and identify it in any sentence.
2. Recognise the **8 types** of adjectives.
3. Use **Degrees of Comparison** (Positive, Comparative, Superlative) without errors.
4. Apply the **correct order** of adjectives (OSASCOMP).
5. Avoid the **12 Golden Rule traps** that examiners set every year.

## 1. What is an Adjective?

An **adjective** is a word that describes or modifies a **noun** or a **pronoun**. It answers questions like: *What kind? How many? How much? Which one? Whose?*

- She is a **brave** girl. (What kind?)
- He has **five** pens. (How many?)
- **This** book is mine. (Which one?)

**Position of adjectives:**
1. **Attributive** — before the noun: "a **tall** boy"
2. **Predicative** — after a linking verb (is, am, are, was, seem, look): "The boy is **tall**."

⚠️ Some adjectives are used **only predicatively**: *afraid, asleep, awake, alive, alone, ill*.
✅ "The baby is asleep." ❌ "an asleep baby"

🧠 **Active Recall:** In the sentence "The tired soldier drank cold water," find (a) the attributive adjectives, (b) tell whether "asleep" can be used before a noun.

## 2. Types of Adjectives (The Big 8)

| # | Type | Work | Examples |
|---|------|------|----------|
| 1 | **Quality (Descriptive)** | tells what kind | good, tall, honest, red |
| 2 | **Quantity** | tells how much (uncountable) | some, much, little, whole |
| 3 | **Number (Numeral)** | tells how many / order | two, five, first, many, few |
| 4 | **Demonstrative** | points out | this, that, these, those, such |
| 5 | **Distributive** | refers to each one | each, every, either, neither |
| 6 | **Interrogative** | asks a question | what, which, whose |
| 7 | **Possessive** | shows ownership | my, our, your, his, her, its, their |
| 8 | **Proper** | made from proper nouns | Indian, Chinese, French |

**Memory hooks:**
- *This, that, these, those* + noun = demonstrative **adjective**. Used alone = demonstrative **pronoun**.
  - "**This** pen is mine." (adjective) | "**This** is mine." (pronoun)
- Same test works for *what/which/whose* (interrogative) and *my/your/his* (possessive).

🧠 **Active Recall:** Identify the type: (a) Every student passed. (b) Whose bag is this? (c) She drank some milk. (d) I like Indian food.

## 3. Degrees of Comparison — The Heart of This Chapter

Adjectives of quality have **three degrees**:

| Degree | Use | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Positive** | describes one thing | Ram is **tall**. |
| **Comparative** | compares **two** | Ram is **taller than** Shyam. |
| **Superlative** | compares **more than two** | Ram is **the tallest** boy in the class. |

### How degrees are formed

1. **Add -er / -est:** tall → taller → tallest; fast → faster → fastest
2. **Double the last letter** (short vowel + single consonant): big → bigger → biggest; hot → hotter → hottest
3. **-y becomes -i:** happy → happier → happiest
4. **more / most** (long adjectives): beautiful → more beautiful → most beautiful
5. **Irregular (memorise!):**

| Positive | Comparative | Superlative |
|---|---|---|
| good | better | best |
| bad / ill | worse | worst |
| many / much | more | most |
| little | less | least |
| far | farther / further | farthest / furthest |
| old | older / elder | oldest / eldest |
| late | later / latter | latest / last |

### 🔑 The Latin Comparatives (a favourite trap!)

These words end in **-ior** and take **"to"**, NOT "than":
**superior, inferior, senior, junior, prior, posterior, anterior**

✅ "She is senior **to** me." ❌ "She is senior **than** me."

### 🎵 Mnemonic: "**S-I-S-J-P** takes TO"
**S**uperior, **I**nferior, **S**enior, **J**unior, **P**rior — "SIS-JP goes TO school, never THANks anyone!"

🧠 **Active Recall:** Correct these: (a) He is more stronger than me. (b) My brother is elder than me. (c) This is the most unique design.

## 4. Elder vs Older, Later vs Latter — Confusing Pairs

- **Elder / Eldest** → only for **family members**, never with "than".
  ✅ "He is my elder brother." ❌ "He is elder than me." → "He is **older** than me."
- **Older / Oldest** → for people and things, with "than".
- **Later** → related to **time**: "I will come later."
- **Latter** → the **second of two** things: "Of tea and coffee, I prefer the latter." (opposite: *former*)
- **Farther** → physical **distance**; **Further** → **additional**: "further information"
- **Latest** → most recent in time; **Last** → final in order.

## 5. Order of Adjectives — OSASCOMP

When many adjectives come before one noun, English follows a fixed order:

**O-S-A-S-C-O-M-P** → **O**pinion, **S**ize, **A**ge, **S**hape, **C**olour, **O**rigin, **M**aterial, **P**urpose

🎵 Mnemonic: "**O**ld **S**tudents **A**lways **S**tudy **C**arefully **O**n **M**onday **P**eriods"

✅ "a **beautiful small old round black Indian wooden** table"
❌ "a wooden black Indian old small round beautiful table"

You will rarely use more than 2–3 together, but exams test: *"a red big car"* ❌ → *"a **big red** car"* ✅ (size before colour).

🧠 **Active Recall:** Arrange: "a / leather / brown / lovely / bag" → ?

## 6. Adjectives Used as Nouns — "The + Adjective"

**The + adjective** = the whole class of people (plural meaning, plural verb):

- **The rich** should help **the poor**. (= rich people, poor people)
- **The blind** deserve our sympathy.
- **The dead** cannot return.

❌ "The rich is greedy." ✅ "The rich **are** greedy."

## 7. ⚠️ EXAMINER'S TRAP BOX — The 12 Golden Rules

| # | Golden Rule | ❌ Wrong | ✅ Right |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | No double comparative/superlative | more taller, most best | taller, best |
| 2 | Latin comparatives take **to** | senior than me | senior to me |
| 3 | Comparative for **two**, superlative for **many** | He is the best of the two | He is the **better** of the two |
| 4 | Superlative takes **the** | He is tallest boy | He is **the** tallest boy |
| 5 | "One of the" + superlative + **plural noun** | one of the best player | one of the best **players** |
| 6 | **Elder** never with "than" | elder than me | older than me |
| 7 | Absolute adjectives have **no degrees**: unique, perfect, universal, supreme, complete, empty, round, dead | most unique, more perfect | unique, perfect |
| 8 | **Much** with uncountable, **many** with countable | much books | many books |
| 9 | **Fewer** (countable) vs **less** (uncountable) | less students | fewer students |
| 10 | Adjective (not adverb) after linking verbs: look, seem, feel, smell, taste, sound | She looks beautifully | She looks **beautiful** |
| 11 | "The + adjective" takes plural verb | The poor is honest | The poor **are** honest |
| 12 | Prefer takes **to**, not than | I prefer tea than coffee | I prefer tea **to** coffee |

**Trap 1 — Sound-Right trap:** "She is more wiser than her sister" *sounds* fine to many ears because we hear "more" everywhere. Rule 1 kills it: **wiser** alone.

**Trap 2 — Intervening-phrase trap:** "One of the most intelligent **students** of my class **has** failed." The long phrase hides the structure — "one of the + plural noun" but singular verb.

**Trap 3 — Over-thinking trap:** "He is senior to me by five years" is already correct; students wrongly "fix" *to* → *than*.

## 8. 🔁 Linguistic Bridge — Hindi vs English

| हम हिंदी से सोचकर कहते हैं ❌ | Correct English ✅ | Why |
|---|---|---|
| "He is more taller than me." (वह मुझसे ज़्यादा लंबा है) | He is **taller** than me. | -er already means "more" |
| "My elder brother is elder than me." | My elder brother is **older** than me. | elder never takes "than" |
| "She is senior than me." (वह मुझसे सीनियर है) | She is senior **to** me. | Latin comparative rule |
| "I have much friends." | I have **many** friends. | friends = countable |
| "The poor is suffering." (गरीब कष्ट सह रहा है) | The poor **are** suffering. | "the poor" = all poor people |
| "This is most unique painting." | This is a **unique** painting. | unique = one of a kind, no degrees |
