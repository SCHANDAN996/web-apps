26. Choose the correct sentence.
(a) She is more taller than her sister. (b) She is taller than her sister. (c) She is tallest than her sister. (d) She is much more taller than her sister.
Answer: (b)
Solution: "More taller" is a double comparative, a classic examiner trap — never use "more" with an "-er" form. "Tallest" needs "the" and compares three or more. The correct comparative is simply "taller", so the answer is "She is taller than her sister."
Source: PYQ-style

27. Fill in the blank: This is the _______ movie I have ever seen.
(a) good (b) better (c) best (d) most good
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Ever seen" shows comparison among all movies, so the superlative is needed. "Most good" is wrong because "good" has irregular forms: good–better–best. The correct superlative is "best", so the answer is "best".
Source: NCERT Class 7 English

28. Choose the correctly spelt adjective.
(a) beautifull (b) beautiful (c) beutiful (d) beautifal
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Beautifull" with a double "l" is the common spelling trap. The word ends with a single "l". The correct spelling is "beautiful", so the answer is "beautiful".

29. Fill in the blank: There is _______ water in the jug.
(a) much (b) many (c) few (d) several
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Water" is uncountable, so "many", "few" and "several" — all used with countable nouns — are wrong. "Much" is used with uncountable nouns, so the answer is "much".

30. Choose the correct sentence.
(a) I have many money. (b) I have a money. (c) I have much money. (d) I have moneys.
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Money" is uncountable: it takes no article "a" and no plural "s", and "many" is only for countable nouns. The correct sentence is "I have much money", so the answer is "I have much money."

31. Fill in the blank: Mount Everest is the _______ peak in the world.
(a) highest (b) higher (c) high (d) most high
Answer: (a)
Solution: Comparison is among all peaks of the world, so the superlative is required. "Most high" is wrong because "high" forms its superlative with "-est". The correct form is "highest", so the answer is "highest".

32. Choose the correct order of adjectives: She bought a _______ dress.
(a) red beautiful silk (b) silk red beautiful (c) beautiful red silk (d) red silk beautiful
Answer: (c)
Solution: English adjective order is opinion → colour → material. Putting "red" before "beautiful" is the tempting trap. "Beautiful" (opinion) comes before "red" (colour), which comes before "silk" (material), so the answer is "beautiful red silk".
Source: PYQ-style

33. Fill in the blank: He is _______ honest man.
(a) an (b) a (c) the (d) no article
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Honest" begins with a silent "h", so the sound is a vowel sound and takes "an", not "a" — a favourite examiner trap. The correct article is "an", so the answer is "an".

34. Choose the correct sentence.
(a) The riches should help the poors. (b) Rich should help poor. (c) The rich should help the poor. (d) The richs should help the poor.
Answer: (c)
Solution: "The + adjective" (the rich, the poor) denotes a whole class of people and never takes "-s"; adding "s" is the trap. The correct sentence is "The rich should help the poor", so the answer is "The rich should help the poor."
Source: PYQ-style

35. Fill in the blank: Of the two brothers, Ravi is the _______.
(a) tallest (b) tall (c) taller (d) more tall
Answer: (c)
Solution: When only two persons are compared and "the" is used before the blank, English uses the comparative, not the superlative — "tallest" is the classic trap here. The correct form is "the taller", so the answer is "taller".

36. Choose the correct sentence.
(a) He is more senior than me. (b) He is senior than me. (c) He is senior from me. (d) He is senior to me.
Answer: (d)
Solution: Latin comparatives like senior, junior, superior and inferior take "to", never "than" — the most repeated trap in this chapter. The correct sentence is "He is senior to me", so the answer is "He is senior to me."

37. Fill in the blank: Delhi is _______ than Jaipur in population.
(a) bigger (b) most big (c) biggest (d) big
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Than" signals the comparative degree. "Most big" is wrong because "big" doubles its last letter and adds "-er". The correct comparative is "bigger", so the answer is "bigger".

38. Choose the correct sentence.
(a) She gave me an useful book. (b) She gave me a useful book. (c) She gave me an university book. (d) She gave me a honest book.
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Useful" begins with a "yoo" sound, which is a consonant sound, so it takes "a", not "an" — a sound-based trap. The correct sentence is "She gave me a useful book", so the answer is "She gave me a useful book."

39. Fill in the blank: _______ students passed the exam; only two failed.
(a) Few (b) A few (c) Little (d) Much
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Few" means almost none (negative sense), which contradicts "only two failed" — that is the trap. "A few" means some, and "students" is countable, so "little" and "much" are wrong. The answer is "A few".

40. Choose the correct sentence.
(a) My brother is elder to me. (b) My brother is elder than me. (c) My brother is more elder than me. (d) My brother is eldest to me.
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Elder" is used for family members but, like "senior", it takes "to", never "than" — "elder than" is the classic trap. "More elder" is a double comparative. The correct sentence is "My brother is elder to me", so the answer is "My brother is elder to me."
Source: PYQ-style

Answer: (b)
Solution: The tempting doubt is Part R, but "no sooner... than" is the correct correlative pair, and the sentence structure is flawless. There is no error anywhere, so the answer is "No error".

(a) far (b) very (c) much far (d) by far
Answer: (d)
Solution: "Very" before a superlative is the tempting trap, but the standard intensifier before superlatives is "by far". The correct phrase is "by far the best", so the answer is "by far".

43. Choose the correct sentence.
(a) Gold is more precious than any other metal. (b) Gold is more precious than any metal. (c) Gold is the most precious than all metals. (d) Gold is precious than any other metal.
Answer: (a)
Solution: Without "other", gold would be compared with itself, which is illogical — the classic "any other" trap. Mixing the superlative with "than" is also wrong. The correct sentence is "Gold is more precious than any other metal", so the answer is "Gold is more precious than any other metal."

Answer: (d)

Answer: (c)
Solution: "Prefer" already contains the comparative sense, so adding "more" or "than" is a double-comparison trap; it takes "to". The correct sentence is "I prefer tea to coffee", so the answer is "I prefer tea to coffee."

47. Fill in the blank: There is _______ hope of his recovery; the doctors have given up.
(a) little (b) a little (c) the little (d) much
Answer: (a)
Solution: "The doctors have given up" shows a negative sense, so "little" (almost none) fits. "A little" would mean some hope exists, which contradicts the context — that is the trap. The answer is "little".

48. Choose the correct sentence.
(a) The Ganga is one of the longest river in India. (b) The Ganga is one of longest rivers in India. (c) The Ganga is one of the most longest rivers in India. (d) The Ganga is one of the longest rivers in India.
Answer: (d)
Solution: After "one of the + superlative", the noun must be plural — the singular "river" is the classic trap, and "most longest" is a double superlative. The correct sentence is "The Ganga is one of the longest rivers in India", so the answer is "The Ganga is one of the longest rivers in India."

(a) Part P (b) No error (c) Part R (d) Part Q
Answer: (b)
Solution: Students often suspect "farther" and want "further", but "farther" is correct for physical distance, which is the case here. The sentence is error-free, so the answer is "No error".

50. Fill in the blank: _______ information you gave me was very useful.
(a) Many (b) The few (c) Several (d) The little
Answer: (d)
Solution: "Information" is uncountable, so "many" and "several" are eliminated. "The little" means the small amount that exists — here, the specific information given — and fits the definite sense. The answer is "The little".
