🧒 FEYNMAN TECHNIQUE — ADJECTIVE

The Hardest Concept: Order of Adjectives (Why "a beautiful small old round red Italian wooden table"?)

Imagine you are 12 years old and you have a brand-new cricket bat. Your friend asks, "Which bat?" You say:

❌ "My wooden new brown big bat."
✅ "My big new brown wooden bat."

Both sentences use the same words, but the first one sounds wrong — even to a child who has never opened a grammar book. Why? Because English has a hidden queue. Adjectives stand in a fixed line before a noun, and if they break the line, the sentence feels "off."

The Daily-Life Example

Think of a school assembly line. The students don't stand randomly — the tallest at the back, the shortest in front. Adjectives are the same. Before a noun, they line up like this:

Opinion → Size → Age → Shape → Colour → Origin → Material → Purpose → NOUN

(Remember: OSASCOMP)

So: "a beautiful (opinion) small (size) old (age) round (shape) red (colour) Italian (origin) wooden (material) dining (purpose) table."

In exams, you will rarely see 8 adjectives together — usually just 2 or 3. But the queue never changes:

✅ "a lovely old house" (opinion before age)
❌ "an old lovely house"

✅ "a black leather bag" (colour before material)
❌ "a leather black bag"

The Unforgettable Trick: "OSASCOMP — Oh! Sir, A Smart Child Obeyed My Parents"

- Oh! = Opinion (beautiful, lovely, terrible)
- Sir = Size (big, small, tall)
- A = Age (old, new, ancient)
- Smart = Shape (round, square, flat)
- Child = Colour (red, black, golden)
- Obeyed = Origin (Indian, Chinese, American)
- My = Material (wooden, plastic, gold)
- Parents = Purpose (walking stick, dining table, sleeping bag)

One more trick for the second hardest idea — Adjective vs. Adverb: Ask the verb a question. If the word describes a person/thing (noun), it's an adjective. If it describes an action (verb), it's an adverb.

- "She is beautiful." → describes She (a person) → adjective ✅
- "She sings beautifully." → describes sings (an action) → adverb ✅

Exam trap: After verbs like look, feel, smell, taste, seem (sense verbs), use an ADJECTIVE, not an adverb — because you are describing the subject, not the action:
✅ "The cake smells good." ❌ "The cake smells well."
✅ "He feels bad about it." ❌ "He feels badly about it."
