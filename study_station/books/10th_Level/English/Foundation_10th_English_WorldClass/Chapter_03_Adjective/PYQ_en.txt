# 📊 PYQ Analysis — Adjective

## How Exams Test This Chapter

Adjectives appear in almost every SSC, Railway, Bank and State exam — but rarely as a direct "identify the adjective" question. Examiners hide adjective errors inside **Spot the Error**, **Fill in the Blanks**, and **Sentence Improvement** questions. The pattern is remarkably stable across years.

### Where Adjective Questions Hide

| Question Type | How Adjective Appears | Typical Trap |
|---|---|---|
| Spot the Error | Wrong degree, adjective vs adverb confusion | "He runs **fastly**" / "She is **more taller**" |
| Fill in the Blanks | Choosing between little/a little, few/a few, elder/older | Both options look correct |
| Sentence Improvement | Comparative/superlative misuse | "best of the two" instead of "better of the two" |
| One-word / Cloze | Adjective order, quantity words | "much people" instead of "many people" |

### Most Repeated Sub-Topics (Pattern, Not Counts)

1. **Degrees of Comparison** — the single most tested area. Double comparatives ("more better"), superlative with "the", and "than" after comparative are evergreen traps.
2. **little / a little / the little** and **few / a few / the few** — asked again and again in SSC CGL, CHSL and Bank exams.
3. **Adjective vs Adverb** — after verbs of sense (look, feel, smell, taste, sound) an **adjective** is used: "The soup tastes *good*", not "well".
4. **elder / older** — "elder" only for family members, never followed by "than".
5. **Order of adjectives** — "a beautiful small round table" (opinion → size → shape).
6. **much / many, less / fewer** — countable vs uncountable confusion.
7. **Comparative for two, superlative for three+** — "the taller of the two brothers".

### Examiner's Favourite Traps (Trap-Type Classification)

- **Sound-Right Trap:** "He is more smarter than me" — sounds fine in casual speech, grammatically wrong (double comparative).
- **Intervening Phrase Trap:** "The quality of the mangoes **were** good" — the adjective/verb must match "quality", not "mangoes".
- **Over-thinking Trap:** A perfectly correct sentence marked as erroneous because the student hunts for a mistake that isn't there.
- **Near-Miss Vocabulary Trap:** "later/latter", "farther/further", "older/elder" — pairs that differ by one letter but differ in rule.

## Top 10 High-Yield PYQ-Style Questions with Solutions

**Q1.** He is more wiser than his brother.
(a) He is more wiser (b) than his brother (c) No error (d) He is more wisest
**Answer:** (a) → should be "wiser" only.
**Solution:** "Wiser" is already comparative; adding "more" makes a double comparative. **Trap type: Sound-Right.**
**Source:** PYQ-style (SSC pattern)

**Q2.** There is ______ milk left in the fridge; we can make tea.
(a) little (b) a little (c) the little (d) few
**Answer:** (b)
**Solution:** "A little" = some (enough for tea). "Little" = almost none (negative sense). **Trap type: Near-Miss Vocabulary.**
**Source:** PYQ-style (Bank pattern)

**Q3.** She is the best of the two sisters.
(a) She is the best (b) of the two sisters (c) No error (d) She is best
**Answer:** (a) → should be "the better of the two sisters".
**Solution:** For comparing **two** persons, use comparative degree, not superlative. **Trap type: Over-thinking reversed — the error hides in a familiar phrase.**
**Source:** PYQ-style (SSC CGL pattern)

**Q4.** The food smells ______.
(a) well (b) good (c) nicely (d) sweetly
**Answer:** (b)
**Solution:** Verbs of sense (smell, taste, look, feel, sound) take an **adjective**, not an adverb. **Trap type: Sound-Right.**
**Source:** PYQ-style (Railway pattern)

**Q5.** My ______ brother lives in Delhi.
(a) older than me (b) elder (c) elder than (d) eldest than
**Answer:** (b)
**Solution:** "Elder" is used for family members and is **never** followed by "than". **Trap type: Near-Miss Vocabulary.**
**Source:** PYQ-style (State exam pattern)

**Q6.** ______ students passed the exam, so the teacher was disappointed.
(a) A few (b) Few (c) The few (d) Many
**Answer:** (b)
**Solution:** "Few" = almost none (negative), matching "disappointed". "A few" would be positive. **Trap type: Intervening context clue.**
**Source:** PYQ-style (SSC CHSL pattern)

**Q7.** He bought a ______ table.
(a) wooden beautiful round (b) beautiful round wooden (c) round wooden beautiful (d) wooden round beautiful
**Answer:** (b)
**Solution:** Adjective order: Opinion → Size → Shape → Age → Colour → Origin → Material. **Trap type: Sound-Right (all orders "sound" possible).**
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q8.** There were ______ people at the rally than expected.
(a) less (b) fewer (c) little (d) much
**Answer:** (b)
**Solution:** "People" is countable → "fewer". "Less" is for uncountable nouns. **Trap type: Sound-Right ("less people" is common in speech).**
**Source:** PYQ-style (Bank pattern)

**Q9.** Mount Everest is the ______ peak in the world.
(a) high (b) higher (c) highest (d) most high
**Answer:** (c)
**Solution:** Comparing one against all (in the world) → superlative with "the". **Trap type: Over-thinking — students sometimes pick "higher" seeing "than" in memory.**
**Source:** NCERT Class 7 English

**Q10.** The scenery of Kashmir is more beautiful than ______.
(a) Shimla (b) that of Shimla (c) those of Shimla (d) Shimla's one
**Answer:** (b)
**Solution:** Comparison must be between two **sceneries**, so "that of Shimla". Comparing scenery with Shimla itself is a classic mismatch. **Trap type: Intervening Phrase / false comparison.**
**Source:** PYQ-style (SSC CGL pattern)

## ⏱️ Applying the 15/45-Second Rule

**15-Second (Fact/Rule-based):** Q3 and Q9. The moment you see "of the two" → comparative; "in the world" → superlative. Pure rule recall — answer in 15 seconds, don't re-read.

**45-Second (Inference-based):** Q6 and Q10. Q6 needs you to read the mood of the whole sentence ("disappointed" → negative → "Few"). Q10 needs you to check *what is being compared*. These deserve a careful 45 seconds — rushing here is exactly what the examiner wants.

**Exam Strategy:** In Spot-the-Error, scan first for degree words (more, most, -er, -est), quantity words (little/few/much/many), and sense verbs (looks/tastes/feels). These three scans catch most adjective errors in under 20 seconds.
