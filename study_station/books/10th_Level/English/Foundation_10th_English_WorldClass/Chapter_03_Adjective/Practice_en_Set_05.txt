101. Choose the correctly spelt adjective.
(a) Superfluous (b) Superfluos (c) Superflous (d) Superflus
Answer: (a)
Solution: The tempting wrong option drops the second 'u', which looks natural because many learners shorten double vowels. The correct spelling keeps both 'u's: super-fluous, meaning "more than needed". So the answer is Superfluous.

102. Spot the error: "He is (P) / more superior (Q) / to me in (R) / computer skills. (S)"
(a) Part Q (b) Part P (c) Part R (d) No error
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is that "more superior" sounds natural in everyday speech, but adjectives like superior, inferior, senior, junior and prior already carry comparison, so "more" cannot be added. Part Q should read simply "superior". So the answer is Part Q.

103. Choose the correct sentence.
(a) Gold is a precious metal. (b) The gold is a precious metal. (c) A gold is a precious metal. (d) The gold is precious metal.
Answer: (a)
Solution: The tempting option uses "the" because Hindi speakers often translate "सोना एक कीमती धातु है" with an article, but material nouns used in a general sense take no article. So the answer is "Gold is a precious metal."

104. Fill in the blank: "Of the two brothers, Ramesh is the ______."
(a) tallest (b) taller (c) more tall (d) most tall
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is choosing the superlative "tallest" because two people are being discussed, but when exactly two are compared, the comparative degree is used with "the". So the answer is taller.

105. Spot the error: "She gave me (P) / an useful (Q) / piece of (R) / advice yesterday. (S)"
(a) Part P (b) Part R (c) Part Q (d) No error
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is that "useful" begins with the vowel letter 'u', so learners pick "an", but the sound is a consonant sound /juː/, so the article must be "a useful". Part Q is wrong. So the answer is Part Q.

106. Choose the correct order of adjectives: "She bought a ______ bag."
(a) leather black beautiful (b) leather beautiful black (c) black beautiful leather (d) beautiful black leather
Answer: (d)
Solution: The tempting wrong order puts colour before opinion because that feels natural in Hindi, but English follows the order: opinion – size – age – shape – colour – origin – material. "Beautiful" (opinion) comes before "black" (colour) and "leather" (material). So the answer is "beautiful black leather".

Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is "most bad", which mimics the regular pattern of adding "most", but "bad" is irregular: bad – worse – worst. A superlative is needed after "the" with "ever seen". So the answer is worst.

108. Choose the sentence with the correct use of "little".
(a) There is little milk in the fridge, so we can make tea. (b) There is the little milk in the fridge, so we can make tea. (c) There is a little milk in the fridge, so we can make tea. (d) There is few milk in the fridge, so we can make tea.
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is plain "little", which means "almost none" and would contradict "we can make tea". "A little" means "some, enough". "Few" is wrong because milk is uncountable. So the answer is "There is a little milk in the fridge, so we can make tea."

Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is placing "enough" before the adjective as in Hindi ("काफी लंबा"), but in English "enough" comes after the adjective it modifies. So the answer is "He is tall enough to reach the shelf."

116. Choose the correct comparative sentence.
(a) Delhi is hotter than any city in India. (b) Delhi is hotter than any other city in India. (c) Delhi is hotter than all city in India. (d) Delhi is more hotter than any other city in India.
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is dropping "other", which would illogically compare Delhi with itself since Delhi is also a city in India. "More hotter" is a double comparative and wrong. So the answer is "Delhi is hotter than any other city in India."

125. Choose the correct sentence.
(a) He is more cleverer than his brother. (b) He is most clever than his brother. (c) He is cleverer than his brother. (d) He is clever than his brother.
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is the double comparative "more cleverer", a very common examiner's trap; only one comparative marker is allowed. "Than" rules out the superlative, and "clever" forms its comparative with "-er". So the answer is "He is cleverer than his brother."
