# 📋 Important Grammar Rules & Tips — Adjective

## (a) 12 High-Yield Rules

| # | Rule | Example | Examiner's Trap |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Adjective of quality** answers "what kind?" and sits before the noun or after a linking verb. | She is a *brave* girl. / The girl is *brave*. | Using an adverb after a linking verb: "She looks *beautifully*" ❌ → "She looks *beautiful*" ✔ |
| 2 | **Order of adjectives** (before a noun): Opinion → Size → Age → Shape → Colour → Origin → Material → Purpose (OSASCOMP). | a *beautiful small old round black Italian wooden* table | "a wooden old table" ❌ → "an old wooden table" ✔ |
| 3 | **Comparative (-er / more)** compares two; **Superlative (-est / most)** compares more than two. | Ram is *taller* than Shyam. / Ram is the *tallest* of the three. | "He is the taller of the three boys" ❌ → "the tallest" ✔ |
| 4 | **Double comparatives/superlatives are wrong.** Never use "more + -er" or "most + -est". | better, worse, more beautiful | "more better" ❌, "most unkindest" ❌ |
| 5 | **Some adjectives have no degrees** (absolute adjectives): unique, perfect, universal, complete, empty, supreme, extreme, chief. | This plan is *perfect*. | "most unique" ❌, "more complete" ❌ |
| 6 | **Latin comparatives** (senior, junior, superior, inferior, prior, posterior) take **to**, not *than*, and have no comparative form. | He is *senior to* me. | "senior than me" ❌; "more superior" ❌ |
| 7 | **'The + adjective' = a whole class of people** (plural sense, plural verb). | *The rich* should help *the poor*. | "The rich is happy" ❌ → "The rich are…" ✔ |
| 8 | **Adjectives used as nouns** (the blind, the dead, the accused) — but note: *the accused* can be singular. | *The accused* was acquitted. | Confusing "the poor" (plural) with "the accused" (can be singular). |
| 9 | **Comparisons must be between similar things** — compare like with like. | The climate of Pune is better than *that of* Delhi. | "The climate of Pune is better than Delhi" ❌ (climate vs. city!) |
| 10 | **'One of the + superlative + plural noun'.** | One of the *best players* of the team. | "one of the best player" ❌ → plural noun required ✔ |
| 11 | **Adjectives after verbs of sense** (look, feel, smell, taste, sound, seem) — use adjective, not adverb. | The soup *tastes good*. | "tastes well" ❌ (well = adverb; "well" as adjective means healthy only) |
| 12 | **Enough** comes *after* an adjective but *before* a noun. | He is *tall enough*. / He has *enough money*. | "enough tall" ❌; "money enough" ❌ |

## (b) Time Management — 15/45-Second Rule

**⚡ 15-Second Questions (Fact/Rule-based — answer instantly):**
- Spotting "more better", "most unique", "senior than" errors → pure rule recall.
- Choosing comparative vs. superlative (two things vs. many).
- "One of the + superlative + plural noun" pattern.
- Adjective vs. adverb after linking verbs (looks happy / sings happily).
- Order of adjectives in short phrases (an old wooden chair).

**🕐 45-Second Questions (Inference/Application — think first):**
- Long error-spotting sentences with an **intervening phrase** hiding the real noun: "The quality of the mangoes *was/were* good" — check what the adjective/adverb actually modifies.
- Sentences where **two rules clash**: e.g., "He is the most senior of all" — senior has no superlative; re-read before answering.
- Fill-in-the-blank where two options *sound* right (good/well, later/latter, elder/older) — apply the rule, not the ear.
- Improvement-of-sentence questions needing comparison of similar things ("that of / those of").

**Strategy:** If a question is rule-recall, answer in 15 seconds and bank the time. If you catch yourself re-reading twice, it is a 45-second question — slow down, find the noun being described, then decide.

## (c) Morphology — Roots, Affixes & Adjective Formation

**How adjectives are built:** Root word + suffix (most common) or prefix + root.

**Common adjective-forming suffixes:**

| Suffix | Meaning | Root → Adjective |
|---|---|---|
| -ful | full of | beauty → beautiful, care → careful |
| -less | without | hope → hopeless, care → careless |
| -ous / -ious | having the quality of | danger → dangerous, glory → glorious |
| -able / -ible | capable of being | read → readable, sense → sensible |
| -ive | tending to | act → active, attract → attractive |
| -al / -ial | relating to | nation → national, industry → industrial |
| -ic | relating to | hero → heroic, history → historic |
| -ish | somewhat / like a | child → childish, red → reddish |
| -ly | having the quality of (adjective!) | friend → friendly, love → lovely |
| -y | full of / like | rain → rainy, dust → dusty |
| -ent / -ant | having the quality of | differ → different, please → pleasant |
| -ern (direction) | direction | east → eastern, north → northern |

**Common prefixes that flip meaning:**

| Prefix | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| un- | not | happy → unhappy |
| in- / im- / il- / ir- | not | active → inactive, possible → impossible, legal → illegal, regular → irregular |
| dis- | not / opposite of | honest → dishonest |
| pre- / post- | before / after | war → prewar, war → postwar |

**⚠️ Trap:** Not every word ending in **-ly** is an adverb! *Friendly, lovely, costly, cowardly, orderly, deadly* are **adjectives**. "She spoke friendly" ❌ → "She spoke in a friendly manner" ✔.

**Exam tip:** When a blank needs a describing word before a noun, check the suffix — "-ous/-ive/-al/-ful" words are adjectives; "-ly" words are usually adverbs (with the exceptions above).
