Card 1
Front: What is an adjective?
Back: A word that describes or modifies a noun or pronoun — it tells us about quality, quantity, number, or identity. Example: "a tall boy", "five apples", "this pen".

Card 2
Front: What are the three degrees of comparison? Give an example.
Back: Positive (tall), Comparative (taller), Superlative (tallest). Comparative compares two; superlative compares more than two.

Card 3
Front: Which adjectives have irregular comparison forms? (Mnemonic card)
Back: Mnemonic: "Good Boys Make Little Fun" — Good–Better–Best, Bad–Worse–Worst, Much/Many–More–Most, Little–Less–Least, Far–Farther/Further–Farthest/Furthest.

Card 4
Front: "She is more taller than her sister." — Correct or wrong? Why?
Back: Wrong. Double comparative is an error. Use either "taller" or "more tall" (here: "taller"). Never use "more/most" with -er/-est forms.

Card 5
Front: When do we use "the" before an adjective to mean a whole class of people?
Back: Adjective + "the" = the whole group: "the rich" (rich people), "the poor", "the blind", "the elderly". These take a plural verb: "The poor need help."

Card 6
Front: What is the correct order of adjectives before a noun? (Mnemonic card)
Back: Mnemonic: "OSASCOMP" — Opinion, Size, Age, Shape, Colour, Origin, Material, Purpose. Example: "a beautiful small old round red Indian wooden dining table."

Card 7
Front: "He is senior than me." — Spot the error.
Back: Use "to", not "than", with Latin comparatives: senior, junior, superior, inferior, prior, posterior, prefer. Correct: "He is senior to me."

Card 8
Front: What is the difference between "elder" and "older"?
Back: "Elder" is used only for family members and never with "than" ("my elder brother"). "Older" is general and takes "than" ("This building is older than that one").

Card 9
Front: "I have less friends than you." — Correct or wrong?
Back: Wrong. "Less" is for uncountable nouns (less water); "fewer" is for countable nouns. Correct: "I have fewer friends than you."

Card 10
Front: What is the difference between "farther" and "further"?
Back: "Farther" = physical distance ("Delhi is farther than Agra"). "Further" = additional/more ("further information", "further discussion").

Card 11
Front: "The two first chapters are easy." — Spot the error.
Back: Ordinal numbers come before cardinal numbers. Correct: "The first two chapters are easy."

Card 12
Front: When do we use "each" and when "every"?
Back: "Each" = for two or more, one by one, individual focus ("each of the two boys"). "Every" = for more than two, group focus ("every student in the class").

Card 13
Front: "He is the best of the two brothers." — Correct or wrong?
Back: Wrong. Superlative is used for three or more. For two, use comparative: "He is the better of the two brothers."

Card 14
Front: What is the difference between "little", "a little", and "the little"?
Back: "Little" = almost none (negative sense). "A little" = some, at least ("a little milk is left"). "The little" = all that small amount ("he spent the little money he had"). Same pattern with few/a few/the few.

Card 15
Front: Examiner's Trap: "No sooner had he come than..." — which comparative pair does the exam confuse with "than"?
Back: Traps: "no sooner...than" (not "when"), "hardly/scarcely...when" (not "than"). Also "rather than", "other than". Mixing these pairs is a classic error question.

Card 16
Front: "She is more cleverer than wise." — Spot the error.
Back: When comparing two qualities of the same person, use "more + positive degree", not comparative: "She is more clever than wise."

Card 17
Front: Examiner's Trap: "Gold is a precious metal." vs "This is the most unique design." — which one hides an error?
Back: The second. "Unique" means one of a kind — it has no degrees. Words like unique, perfect, universal, supreme, extreme, complete cannot take "more/most".

Card 18
Front: What is a "participle adjective"? Give one pair that confuses students.
Back: Adjectives formed from verbs: -ing (the thing/person causing the feeling) vs -ed (the person feeling it). "The movie is boring" (movie causes it) vs "I am bored" (I feel it).

Card 19
Front: "He gave me many an advice." — Spot the error.
Back: "Advice" is uncountable — never use "many" or "an" with it. Say "much advice" or "a piece of advice". Same for information, furniture, luggage, news.

Card 20
Front: Where does an adjective go with words like "something", "anything", "nothing"?
Back: After them: "something interesting", "nothing new", "anything special" — never "interesting something".
