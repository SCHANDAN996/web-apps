# 🧒 Feynman Technique — Spelling Check

## The Hardest Concept, Explained Like You're 12

**The problem:** English spelling doesn't follow sound. "Receive" has "ei", but "believe" has "ie". "Necessary" has one C and two S's, but "occasion" has two C's and one S. How do you remember which is which when they all *sound* almost the same?

**Think of it like this:** Imagine English words are like your school friends. You don't recognise your friends by the *sound* of their footsteps — you recognise their **faces**. Spelling is the *face* of a word. If you only listen (sound), you'll keep mixing up twins like "their/there" and "principal/principle". You must learn to see the word's face.

**A daily-life example:** Your mother sends you to buy "**separate**" soap. You write "seperate" on the list — and the shopkeeper gives you the wrong thing because he can't read it. The trick? There's **"A RAT" in sep-A-RAT-e**. Once you see the rat hiding inside, you'll never write "seperate" again. 🐀

**The unforgettable trick — "Words have families, not sounds":**
- **Necessary** = 1 **C**ollar, 2 **S**leeves (one C, two S's) — a shirt!
- **Accommodate** = 2 **C**ots + 2 **M**attresses — a big room fits everyone.
- **Embarrass** = 2 **R**'s + 2 **S**'s — you blush **R**eally **R**eally **S**o **S**o much.
- **I before E, except after C** — bel**ie**ve, but rec**ei**ve. (Watch out: weird, height, their are rule-breakers!)

**Why this works:** Your brain forgets letters but never forgets *pictures and stories*. A rat inside "separate" is a picture. "Collar and sleeves" is a picture. Sound-based spelling fails in the exam hall; picture-based spelling survives.

## 📝 Blurring Sheet Instruction

Close this book. Take a blank sheet. In 60 seconds, **blurt out** (write fast, without thinking):
1. The 4 picture-tricks above (rat, shirt, room, blush).
2. The "i before e" rule and its 3 rule-breakers.
3. 5 words you personally misspell, written correctly 3 times each.

Now open the book and check. Whatever you missed — that's your weak face. Learn that word's face tonight.

**Question for you:** If "occurrence" happens **twice**, how many C's and R's does it have? (Hint: double everything — o-**cc**u-**rr**ence!)
