## ⚡ Rapid-Fire Rules (5-Second Checks)

1. **Silent E rule:** Word ending in silent 'e' + suffix starting with a vowel → drop the e (hope → hoping, make → making). Suffix starting with a consonant → keep the e (hope → hopeful).
2. **Y to I rule:** Word ending in consonant + y → change y to i before suffix (happy → happily, beauty → beautiful). Exception: keep y before -ing (cry → crying).
3. **Doubling rule:** One-syllable word, one vowel, one final consonant → double it before -ed/-ing (run → running, stop → stopped). Two-syllable words double only if stress is on the last syllable (begin → beginning, but open → opened).

## 🚫 Skip Strategy (Smart Time Management)

**Tip 1 — The 15-Second Scan:** Spelling questions are pure Fact-based — you either know the word or you don't. Give yourself a strict 15 seconds. Read all four options once, pick the odd one out, and move on. Re-reading a spelling option five times will not make the correct spelling appear in your memory.

**Tip 2 — Skip the "All Look Right" Trap:** If all four options look correct to you, the trap is usually a subtle double-letter error (occurence / occurrence) or an ie/ei swap (recieve / receive). If you cannot spot it in 15 seconds, mark it, skip, and return only if time remains. Never burn 2 minutes on one spelling question — that time can fetch you 3–4 grammar questions.

**Tip 3 — Trust Your First Instinct on Familiar Words:** Over-thinking is the biggest enemy in spelling questions. If "government" looked wrong to you at first glance and "goverment" looked fine, your first instinct was probably right. Change your answer only if you find a concrete rule (like "i before e") that proves your first choice wrong — never change it on a vague feeling.
