# 📊 PYQ Analysis — Spelling Check

## How Exams Test Spelling (Pattern Analysis)

Spelling questions appear in almost every foundation-level exam — SSC GD, SSC MTS, SSC CHSL, RRB Group D, RRB NTPC, and state police/constable papers. The question is always one of two formats:

**Format 1 — "Find the correctly spelt word":** Three options are misspelt, one is correct. This is the more common format.

**Format 2 — "Find the incorrectly spelt word":** Three options are correct, one is misspelt. Read the instruction twice — many students lose marks by answering the opposite of what is asked.

**Sub-topics that repeat most often:**

- **Double-letter confusion** — words like *accommodate, committee, beginning, occasion, embarrass*. Examiners love removing or adding one letter (*occurence, begining, embarass*).
- **-ie- vs -ei- words** — *receive, believe, achieve, height, foreign*. The trap option usually flips the pair (*recieve, beleive*).
- **Silent letters** — *honest, knife, psychology, doubt, column*. The wrong option often inserts a sound-based spelling (*dout, coloum*).
- **-able vs -ible endings** — *responsible, comfortable, visible, advisable*.
- **-ance/-ence and -ant/-ent** — *attendance, independent, existence, relevant*.
- **Commonly confused everyday words** — *separate, definite, government, environment, necessary, privilege*.

## The Examiner's Traps (in words, not numbers)

1. **Sound-Right Trap:** The wrong option is spelt the way the word *sounds* — *goverment, enviroment, definately*. Our ears drop letters; the dictionary does not.
2. **Double-Letter Trap:** One letter doubled or halved — *ocassion, comming, neccessary*. These look "almost right" at a glance.
3. **Over-thinking Trap:** The correct spelling sometimes *looks* wrong because we have seen the misspelling so often (e.g., *separate* — many students pick *seperate* because it feels familiar). Trust the rule, not familiarity.
4. **Negative-Wording Trap:** "Select the INCORRECTLY spelt word" — students spot the first correct-looking word and mark it. Underline "incorrectly" in the question before answering.

## Top 10 High-Yield Questions (PYQ-style)

**Q1. Select the correctly spelt word.**
(a) Recieve (b) Receive (c) Riceive (d) Receeve
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** Rule: "i before e, except after c." After *c*, it is *-ei-* → **receive**. Option (a) is the classic Sound-Right trap.
**Trap type:** Sound-Right | **Source:** PYQ-style

**Q2. Select the correctly spelt word.**
(a) Occasion (b) Ocassion (c) Occassion (d) Ocasion
**Answer: (a)**
**Solution:** *Occasion* has double **c**, single **s**. (b) and (d) drop a letter; (c) doubles both — the Double-Letter trap.
**Trap type:** Double-Letter | **Source:** PYQ-style

**Q3. Select the incorrectly spelt word.**
(a) Committee (b) Beginning (c) Embarass (d) Accommodate
**Answer: (c)**
**Solution:** The correct spelling is **embarrass** — double *r*, double *s*. Remember: *accommodate* = 2C + 2M; *committee* = 2M + 2T + 2E.
**Trap type:** Double-Letter | **Source:** PYQ-style

**Q4. Select the correctly spelt word.**
(a) Seperate (b) Separate (c) Saparate (d) Separat
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** **Separate** has *-par-* in the middle — "there's A RAT in sepARATe." (a) is the Over-thinking trap: *seperate* looks familiar because it is the most common misspelling in India.
**Trap type:** Over-thinking | **Source:** PYQ-style

**Q5. Select the correctly spelt word.**
(a) Goverment (b) Governmant (c) Government (d) Govarnment
**Answer: (c)**
**Solution:** **Government** = govern + ment. The *n* before *-ment* is silent in fast speech, so (a) is the Sound-Right trap.
**Trap type:** Sound-Right | **Source:** PYQ-style

**Q6. Select the incorrectly spelt word.**
(a) Necessary (b) Privilege (c) Definately (d) Existence
**Answer: (c)**
**Solution:** The correct spelling is **definitely** (from *finite* → *definite* + *-ly*). *Necessary*: one C, double S — "one Collar, two Sleeves."
**Trap type:** Sound-Right | **Source:** PYQ-style

**Q7. Select the correctly spelt word.**
(a) Enviroment (b) Environment (c) Envirnment (d) Invironment
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** **Environment** = environ + ment; the *n* before *-ment* must be written even though we rarely pronounce it.
**Trap type:** Sound-Right | **Source:** PYQ-style

**Q8. Select the correctly spelt word.**
(a) Beleive (b) Believe (c) Belive (d) Beleeve
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** No *c* before, so "i before e" → **believe**. Compare Q1: *receive* has *c*, so it flips to *-ei-*.
**Trap type:** Sound-Right | **Source:** PYQ-style

**Q9. Select the incorrectly spelt word.**
(a) Attendance (b) Independent (c) Relevant (d) Persistant
**Answer: (d)**
**Solution:** The correct spelling is **persistent** (ends in *-ent*, from *persist*). *Attendance* and *relevant* take *-ance/-ant*; *independent* takes *-ent*.
**Trap type:** Double-Letter / ending confusion | **Source:** PYQ-style

**Q10. Select the correctly spelt word.**
(a) Psycology (b) Psychology (c) Sychology (d) Psichology
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** **Psychology** keeps the silent *p* and the *ch* — from the Greek root *psyche* (mind) + *logos* (study). Silent letters are never dropped in spelling.
**Trap type:** Sound-Right (silent letter) | **Source:** PYQ-style

## Applying the 15/45-Second Rule

**15-second question (Fact-based recall):** Q1 (*receive*). You either know the "i before e except after c" rule or you don't. Scan the four options, spot *-ei-* after *c*, mark, move on. Do not re-read the options — re-reading invites the Over-thinking trap. Target: 15 seconds.

**45-second question (Careful comparison):** Q3 (*embarrass*). Here three options are correctly spelt and you must find the wrong one. You must verify **each** option against its rule: *committee* (2M, 2T, 2E ✓), *beginning* (double N ✓), *embarass* (needs 2R + 2S ✗), *accommodate* (2C + 2M ✓). Checking four words against memory takes longer — budget up to 45 seconds, and if two options both look doubtful after that, mark the one with the double-letter issue and move on.

**Exam strategy summary:** Spelling is a *fact-recall* topic — most questions should fall in the 15-second bucket. If you find yourself staring at a word for over a minute, your familiarity is misleading you; apply the rule (double letters, i/e order, silent letters), decide, and never leave it blank for "later" — spelling rarely becomes clearer with time.
