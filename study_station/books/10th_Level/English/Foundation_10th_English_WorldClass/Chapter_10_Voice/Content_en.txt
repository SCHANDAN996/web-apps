# Active & Passive Voice (वाच्य)

## 🎯 The Hook

Imagine two headlines:

- **"The police arrested the thief."**
- **"The thief was arrested by the police."**

Same event. Same thief. Same police. Yet the *focus* has completely shifted — in the first sentence, the police are the star; in the second, the thief is. This magical ability to shift the spotlight of a sentence is called **Voice**, and it is one of the most frequently tested grammar topics in SSC, Railway, Bank and State exams. Master it once, and you will never lose these marks again.

---

## 📌 Learning Objectives

By the end of this chapter, you will be able to:

1. Identify whether a sentence is in Active or Passive Voice.
2. Convert any tense from Active to Passive (and back) using a fixed formula.
3. Handle special cases — modals, imperatives, questions, and sentences with two objects.
4. Spot the examiner's favourite traps instantly.

---

## 1. What is Voice?

**Voice** tells us whether the **subject** of a sentence *performs* the action or *receives* the action.

- **Active Voice** → The subject *does* the action.
  *Ram eats a mango.* (Ram = doer)
- **Passive Voice** → The subject *receives* the action.
  *A mango is eaten by Ram.* (Mango = receiver)

**The Golden Formula for Passive:**

> **Object + be-verb (is/am/are/was/were/been/being) + V3 (Past Participle) + by + Subject**

The tense never changes — only the *form* of the verb changes. "Eats" becomes "is eaten"; the time (present) stays the same.

### 🧠 Active Recall
1. In "The letter was written by Sita," who is the doer of the action?
2. What two parts must every passive **verb** contain?

*(Answers: 1. Sita — she performs the writing even though "letter" is the subject. 2. A be-verb + V3 (past participle) — e.g., "was written". The object and the "by + doer" belong to the sentence, not to the verb itself.)*

---

## 2. The Conversion Rule — Step by Step

To change Active → Passive, follow these four steps:

1. **Swap positions**: The object of the active sentence becomes the subject of the passive sentence.
2. **Choose the correct "be" form** according to the tense and the new subject.
3. **Use V3** (third form) of the main verb.
4. **Add "by" + doer** (omit the doer if it is unknown or unimportant).

**Example (Present Simple):**
- Active: *She teaches English.*
- Passive: *English **is taught** by her.*

**Example (Past Simple):**
- Active: *Mohan broke the window.*
- Passive: *The window **was broken** by Mohan.*

### 🧠 Active Recall
Convert: "The cat chased the mouse."
*(Answer: The mouse was chased by the cat.)*

---

## 3. Tense-wise Conversion Table

| Tense | Active | Passive |
|---|---|---|
| Present Simple | He writes a letter. | A letter **is written** by him. |
| Present Continuous | He is writing a letter. | A letter **is being written** by him. |
| Present Perfect | He has written a letter. | A letter **has been written** by him. |
| Past Simple | He wrote a letter. | A letter **was written** by him. |
| Past Continuous | He was writing a letter. | A letter **was being written** by him. |
| Past Perfect | He had written a letter. | A letter **had been written** by him. |
| Future Simple | He will write a letter. | A letter **will be written** by him. |
| Future Perfect | He will have written a letter. | A letter **will have been written** by him. |

**⚠️ Note:** Perfect Continuous tenses (has been writing, had been writing) and Future Continuous (will be writing) are **NOT** converted into passive in standard exam English.

### 🧠 Active Recall
1. What is the passive of "She has completed the work"?
2. Can "He has been singing" be converted to passive?

*(Answers: 1. The work has been completed by her. 2. No — Perfect Continuous has no passive form.)*

---

## 4. Modals in Passive Voice

With modals (can, could, may, might, shall, should, will, would, must, ought to), the structure is simple:

> **Modal + be + V3**

- Active: *You **must obey** the rules.* → Passive: *The rules **must be obeyed**.*
- Active: *She **can solve** this problem.* → Passive: *This problem **can be solved** by her.*

**Mnemonic 🧠: "M-B-V3"** — *Modal, Be, Verb-3* — remember it as **"My Best Victory"** (M-B-V3). Every modal passive follows My Best Victory!

---

## 5. Special Cases

**(a) Imperative sentences (orders/requests):** Use **"Let + object + be + V3"**.
- *Open the door.* → *Let the door be opened.*
- For advice: *Help the poor.* → *The poor should be helped.*

**(b) Questions:** Keep the question structure; only the verb changes.
- *Did he write the letter?* → *Was the letter written by him?*
- *Why did she beat the boy?* → *Why was the boy beaten by her?*

**(c) Two objects (direct + indirect):** Either object can become the passive subject.
- *He gave me a book.* → *I was given a book by him.* / *A book was given to me by him.*

**(d) Preposition-verbs:** Keep the preposition attached to the verb.
- *They laughed at the beggar.* → *The beggar was laughed at by them.* (NOT "was laughed by them")

### 🧠 Active Recall
Convert: "Shut the window."
*(Answer: Let the window be shut.)*

---

## ⚠️ EXAMINER'S TRAP BOX

**Trap 1 — The "by" agent trap:** Examiners add "by" where it is not needed. If the doer is obvious or unknown (someone, they, people), drop it: *My wallet has been stolen.* (NOT "by someone").

**Trap 2 — The missing "being":** In continuous passives, students forget "being": *The work is being done* (NOT "is done" — that is simple present).

**Trap 3 — Intransitive verbs:** Verbs with no object (go, come, sleep, die, happen) **cannot** become passive. *"The accident was happened"* is WRONG — "happen" has no object.

---

## 🏆 12 Golden Rules of Voice

1. Passive verb = **be + V3**, always.
2. Tense never changes during conversion — only verb form changes.
3. Object of Active → Subject of Passive.
4. Pronouns change: I→me, he→him, she→her, we→us, they→them (and reverse).
5. Modals take **modal + be + V3**.
6. Imperatives take **Let + object + be + V3**.
7. Perfect Continuous & Future Continuous have **no passive**.
8. Intransitive verbs (go, die, sleep, happen) have **no passive**.
9. Prepositions attached to verbs stay attached in passive.
10. "By" is dropped when the doer is unknown/obvious.
11. With two objects, either can become the passive subject.
12. Verbs like *said, reported, believed* use: *It is said that…* / *He is said to be…*

## 🔁 Linguistic Bridge — Hindi vs English

| In Hindi we think… | Wrong English | Correct English |
|---|---|---|
| "मुझसे गलती हो गई" | *By me a mistake was happened.* | *A mistake was made by me.* ("happen" has no passive; use "make") |
| "यह काम किया जा सकता है" | *This work can be do.* | *This work can be done.* (V3, not V1, after "be") |
| "दरवाज़ा खोला गया" | *The door was open by him.* | *The door was opened by him.* ("open" as verb needs -ed) |
| "उसे सज़ा दी गई" | *He was gave punishment.* | *He was given punishment.* (V3 = given, not gave) |

**Key habit:** After any form of "be" (is/was/been/being), the main verb must be in its **third form (V3)** — never V1 or V2.
