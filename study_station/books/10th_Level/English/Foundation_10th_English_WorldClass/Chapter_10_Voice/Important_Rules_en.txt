# 📋 Important Grammar Rules & Tips — Active-Passive Voice

## (a) 12 High-Yield Rules

| # | Rule | Example | Examiner's Trap |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Only **transitive verbs** (verbs with an object) can be changed into passive. Intransitive verbs have no passive. | Active: She sings a song. → Passive: A song is sung by her. | "He slept" / "The baby cried" — no object, so no passive. Don't force it. |
| 2 | The **object of the active becomes the subject** of the passive; the active subject becomes "by + agent" (often dropped). | Ram eats mangoes. → Mangoes are eaten (by Ram). | Students forget to change pronoun case: "I" → "by me", "he" → "by him". |
| 3 | Passive structure = **be + V3 (past participle)**. The "be" form must match the tense of the active verb. | is/am/are + V3; was/were + V3; will be + V3 | Using V2 instead of V3: "The letter was *wrote*" ❌ → "was written" ✅ |
| 4 | **Present Continuous**: is/am/are + being + V3 | She is writing a letter. → A letter is being written by her. | Dropping "being": "A letter is written by her" changes the tense. |
| 5 | **Present Perfect**: has/have + been + V3 | He has finished the work. → The work has been finished by him. | "has/have" must agree with the NEW subject: "The letters have been…" not "has been". |
| 6 | **Past Perfect**: had + been + V3 | They had sold the house. → The house had been sold by them. | Confusing with simple past passive ("was sold") — check the active tense first. |
| 7 | **Future**: will/shall + be + V3 | She will help me. → I shall be helped by her. | "will be help" ❌ — always V3 after "be". |
| 8 | **Modals**: modal + be + V3 | You can solve this. → This can be solved by you. | "can be solve" ❌; also "should be + V3", "must be + V3". |
| 9 | **Imperative sentences**: Let + object + be + V3 (or "You are ordered/requested to…") | Open the door. → Let the door be opened. | "Let the door be open" ❌ — V3 (opened) is compulsory. |
| 10 | **Two objects** (direct + indirect): either can become the passive subject, but the indirect object is preferred in exams. | He gave me a book. → I was given a book by him. / A book was given to me by him. | Forgetting "to" when the direct object becomes subject: "A book was given me" ❌ |
| 11 | **Prepositional verbs**: keep the preposition attached to the verb in passive. | They laughed at him. → He was laughed at. | Dropping the preposition: "He was laughed" ❌ |
| 12 | **By-agent is dropped** when the doer is unknown, obvious, or unimportant (someone, people, they, police). | Someone stole my watch. → My watch was stolen. | Adding "by someone / by them" unnecessarily — examiners test whether you know when to drop it. |

## (b) Time Management — 15/45-Second Rule

| Type | What it looks like | Time | Strategy |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Fact-based (15 sec)** | Direct conversion of a single sentence in a clear tense (Present Simple, Past Simple, Future, Modals). "Choose the correct passive." | 15 sec | Spot the tense → apply be + V3 → match options. Don't re-read. |
| **Fact-based (15 sec)** | Identify the sentence that CANNOT be passivized (intransitive verb). | 15 sec | Look for verbs like sleep, cry, run, arrive, die — no object, no passive. |
| **Inference-based (45 sec)** | Imperative sentences, two-object sentences, prepositional verbs, sentences with "let/suggest/propose". | 45 sec | Recall the special rule (Rules 9–11), then eliminate options missing "be + V3" or the preposition. |
| **Inference-based (45 sec)** | Questions mixing tense + pronoun case + subject-verb agreement in the passive. | 45 sec | Check three things in order: (1) tense of "be", (2) V3 form, (3) has/have or is/are agreement with the new subject. |

**Golden habit:** In any voice question, first underline the verb of the active sentence — 80% of errors are tense errors, and the verb tells you the answer in one look.

## (c) Morphology — Roots & Affixes for Voice Questions

| Root / Affix | Meaning | Example in Voice Context |
|---|---|---|
| **act** (Latin: *agere*) | to do, drive | **act**ive = the doer performs the action |
| **pass** (Latin: *pati*) | to suffer, undergo | **pass**ive = the subject undergoes the action |
| **trans-** (prefix) | across | **trans**itive = action passes *across* to an object |
| **in-** (prefix) | not | **in**transitive = action does *not* cross to an object |
| **agent** (Latin: *agere*) | one who acts | In passive, the agent follows "by" |
| **-ed / -en** (suffix) | past participle marker | writt**en**, brok**en**, play**ed** — the V3 needed in every passive |
| **object** (Latin: *ob + jacere*) | thrown before | The object of the active is "thrown forward" to become the passive subject |

**Memory hook:** *Trans*itive = action travels on a **train** across to the object. *In*transitive = the train never leaves the station — no object, no passive.
