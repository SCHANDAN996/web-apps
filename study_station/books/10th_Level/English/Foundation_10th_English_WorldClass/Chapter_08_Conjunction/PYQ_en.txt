# 📊 PYQ Analysis — Conjunction (Foundation Level)

## How Exams Test This Chapter

Conjunctions appear in almost every SSC GD, SSC MTS, RRB Group D, and State Police/Constable paper, but rarely as a direct "name the conjunction" question. Examiners hide them inside three favourite formats:

1. **Fill in the blank** — a sentence with a missing connector (most common). The trap is usually a pair that *sounds* right but breaks a rule, e.g., "Although he is rich, **but** he is not happy."
2. **Spot the error** — the conjunction itself is the error: wrong pair (neither…or), double conjunction (because…so), or wrong choice after a fixed word (no sooner…*when* instead of *than*).
3. **Sentence improvement** — the underlined connector must be replaced with the correct one.

**Sub-topics that repeat most often:**
- Correlative pairs: either…or, neither…nor, not only…but also, both…and
- Fixed partners: no sooner…than, hardly/scarcely…when, such…as, so…that, too…to
- Although/though vs. but (never together)
- Because/since/as vs. so (never together)
- Until vs. unless (negative meaning of unless)
- That vs. if/whether after verbs of doubt and asking

**Examiner's favourite traps (in words, not numbers):**
- *Sound-Right trap:* "Neither Ram nor Shyam **were** present" — sounds fine in Hindi-English speech, but the verb follows the nearer subject.
- *Double-conjunction trap:* Hindi allows "क्योंकि…इसलिए" logic, so students accept "Because he was ill, so he stayed home." English forbids it.
- *Pair-confusion trap:* swapping "no sooner…than" with "no sooner…when" — the two fixed families (than/when) are deliberately mixed in options.
- *Over-thinking trap:* a simple "and/but/or" blank where students pick a fancy connector like "moreover" and lose a sitter.

## Top 10 High-Yield Questions (with Solutions)

**Q1.** Although he worked hard, ______ he failed the exam.
(a) but (b) yet (c) so (d) (no word needed)
**Answer:** (d)
**Solution:** "Although" and "but/yet" cannot be used together in standard English. The sentence is complete with "although" alone.
**Trap type:** Double-conjunction (Sound-Right)
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q2.** No sooner did the bell ring ______ the students rushed out.
(a) when (b) then (c) than (d) that
**Answer:** (c)
**Solution:** "No sooner" always pairs with "than". "When" belongs with hardly/scarcely.
**Trap type:** Pair-confusion
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q3.** He is ______ a liar ______ a cheat.
(a) either…or (b) neither…nor (c) not only…but also (d) both…as well as
**Answer:** (c)
**Solution:** The sentence lists two negative qualities together, so "not only…but also" fits. "Either…or" would need a choice, not an addition.
**Trap type:** Over-thinking
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q4.** ______ you work hard, you will not pass.
(a) If (b) Unless (c) Until (d) Till
**Answer:** (b)
**Solution:** "Unless" means "if…not", which matches the intended warning: work hard, otherwise you will not pass. "If" would give the opposite meaning. "Until/till" refer to time, not condition.
**Trap type:** Sound-Right
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q5.** Hardly had she reached the station ______ the train left.
(a) than (b) when (c) then (d) that
**Answer:** (b)
**Solution:** "Hardly/Scarcely" pair with "when"; "No sooner" pairs with "than". Do not mix the two families.
**Trap type:** Pair-confusion
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q6.** Because he was late, ______ he missed the bus.
(a) so (b) therefore (c) hence (d) (no word needed)
**Answer:** (d)
**Solution:** "Because" and "so" cannot appear together — one shows cause, the other result; using both is a classic Hindi-speaker error ("क्योंकि…इसलिए").
**Trap type:** Double-conjunction (Sound-Right)
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q7.** Neither the teacher nor the students ______ present in the hall.
(a) was (b) were (c) is (d) has been
**Answer:** (b)
**Solution:** With "neither…nor", the verb agrees with the nearer subject ("students" — plural), so "were".
**Trap type:** Intervening/subject-proximity
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q8.** He is ______ weak ______ walk.
(a) so…that (b) too…to (c) very…to (d) such…as
**Answer:** (b)
**Solution:** "Too…to" expresses excessive degree with a negative result. "So…that" would need a clause ("so weak that he cannot walk").
**Trap type:** Pair-confusion
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q9.** I doubt ______ he will come today.
(a) that (b) if (c) whether (d) both (b) and (c)
**Answer:** (d)
**Solution:** After "doubt" (uncertainty), both "if" and "whether" are correct. After "do not doubt" (certainty), use "that".
**Trap type:** Over-thinking
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q10.** Walk fast ______ you will miss the train.
(a) and (b) but (c) or (d) so
**Answer:** (c)
**Solution:** "Or" here means "otherwise" — a warning. "And" would wrongly suggest both actions happen together.
**Trap type:** Sound-Right
**Source:** PYQ-style

## Applying the 15/45-Second Rule

**15-second question (Fact-based):** Q2 and Q5. If you have memorised the fixed pairs (no sooner…than, hardly…when), the answer is instant recall — read, spot the first half of the pair, mark the partner, move on. Never spend more than 15 seconds here.

**45-second question (Inference-based):** Q4 and Q9. These need meaning analysis: in Q4 you must check whether the sentence already contains a negative ("not pass") before choosing unless; in Q9 you must judge whether "doubt" expresses certainty or uncertainty. Read the full sentence twice, test each option in the blank, then answer — worth the full 45 seconds.

**Strategy summary:** Fixed-pair and double-conjunction questions = 15 sec (pure memory). Unless/until, doubt-that/whether, and verb-agreement-with-neither-nor questions = 45 sec (meaning + logic).
