101. Choose the correct conjunction: "He worked hard ______ he could pass the exam."
(a) so that (b) because (c) although (d) unless
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Because" is tempting but it gives a reason, while the sentence shows purpose — he worked hard in order to pass. Purpose is expressed by "so that", so the answer is "so that".
Source: NCERT Class 9 English Grammar

102. Choose the correctly framed sentence.
(a) Unless you do not hurry, you will miss the train. (b) Unless you hurry, you will miss the train. (c) Until you hurry, you will miss the train. (d) Without you hurry, you will miss the train.
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is using a negative after "unless"; "unless" itself means "if not", so "do not hurry" makes a double negative. "Until" refers to time, not condition. The correct sentence is "Unless you hurry, you will miss the train."

103. Spot the error: "Scarcely he had reached (P)/ the station when (Q)/ the train left. (R)/ No error (S)"
(a) Part Q (b) Part P (c) Part R (d) No error
Answer: (b)
Solution: Part Q looks suspicious because learners expect "than", but "scarcely... when" is the correct pair. The real error is in Part P: after a negative adverb like "scarcely" at the start, inversion is needed — "Scarcely had he reached". So the error is in Part P.

Answer: (a)
Solution: Part P looks fine and learners often suspect "neither", but the pair "neither... nor" is correct. With "neither... nor", the verb agrees with the nearer subject, "students", which is plural, so "was" should be "were". The error is in Part R.

107. Choose the correct sentence.

Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap options misplace "not only"; correlative conjunctions must be placed just before the words they join. Here both joined items are nouns after the verb, so "not only a singer... but also a dancer" is balanced. The correct sentence is "He is not only a singer but also a dancer."

(a) not only... but also (b) neither... nor (c) either... or (d) both... or
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Neither... nor" would make both qualities negative, which contradicts the praise in the sentence, and "both... or" is not a valid pair. The sentence adds two positive qualities, so the answer is "not only... but also".

111. Choose the correctly framed sentence.
(a) He came late because of he missed the bus. (b) He came late because for missing the bus. (c) He came late because he missed the bus. (d) He came late due to he missed the bus.
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is "because of" and "due to", which are prepositions and must be followed by a noun phrase, not a full clause like "he missed the bus". A clause needs the conjunction "because", so the correct sentence is "He came late because he missed the bus."
