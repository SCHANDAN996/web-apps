Practice Set 6 — Conjunction (Questions 126–150)
Difficulty: Questions 126–135: Medium | Questions 136–150: Hard

126. Choose the correct sentence:
(a) Scarcely he had reached the station when the train left. (b) Scarcely had he reached the station when the train left. (c) Scarcely he had reached the station than the train left. (d) Scarcely had he reached the station than the train left.
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Scarcely/Hardly" at the start of a sentence needs inversion (had he), and it pairs with "when", not "than" — "than" pairs with "no sooner", which is the trap in the last option. So the answer is "Scarcely had he reached the station when the train left."
Source: PYQ-style

127. No sooner did the bell ring ___ the students rushed out.
(a) when (b) that (c) then (d) than
Answer: (d)
Solution: "No sooner" is always followed by "than", never "when" — "when" tempts because it pairs with hardly/scarcely. So the answer is "than".
Source: NCERT Class 9 English Grammar

128. He is ___ intelligent ___ hardworking.
(a) not only / but also (b) both / as well (c) either / but also (d) not only / and
Answer: (a)
Solution: The correct correlative pair is "not only … but also". "Both" pairs with "and" and "either" pairs with "or", so the other options break the fixed pairs. So the answer is "not only / but also".
Source: PYQ-style

129. ___ you work hard, you will fail.
(a) Unless (b) If (c) Until (d) Provided
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Unless" means "if not" and fits the negative result "you will fail". "If you work hard, you will fail" reverses the logic, which is the tempting trap. So the answer is "Unless".
Source: PYQ-style

130. Choose the correctly framed sentence:
(a) Although he is rich, but he is not happy. (b) Although he is rich, therefore he is not happy. (c) Although he is rich, however he is not happy. (d) Although he is rich, yet he is not happy.
Answer: (d)
Solution: "Although" must never be followed by "but" — that double conjunction is the classic examiner trap; "yet" is the acceptable partner, while "however" and "therefore" cannot follow "although". So the answer is "Although he is rich, yet he is not happy."
Source: NCERT Class 10 English Grammar

131. He worked hard ___ he might pass the examination.
(a) until (b) because (c) so that (d) unless
Answer: (c)
Solution: Purpose with "may/might" is expressed by "so that". "Because" shows reason, not purpose, which is the trap here. So the answer is "so that".
Source: PYQ-style

132. ___ he is poor, he is honest.
(a) Since (b) Because (c) As (d) Though
Answer: (d)
Solution: The sentence shows contrast between poverty and honesty, so a concessive conjunction is needed. "Because/Since/As" show cause, which reverses the meaning. So the answer is "Though".
Source: PYQ-style

133. Neither the teacher nor the students ___ present in the hall.
(a) were (b) was (c) is (d) has been
Answer: (a)
Solution: With "neither…nor", the verb agrees with the nearer subject — "students" is plural, so a plural verb is needed; "was" tempts by agreeing with "teacher". So the answer is "were".
Source: NCERT Class 9 English Grammar

134. He will wait here ___ you return.
(a) as (b) since (c) unless (d) until
Answer: (d)
Solution: Waiting up to a point of time needs "until/till". "Unless" (if not) changes the meaning entirely and is the trap. So the answer is "until".
Source: PYQ-style

135. Choose the correct sentence:
(a) He is both a poet as well as a painter. (b) He is both a poet and a painter. (c) He is both a poet or a painter. (d) He is both a poet but also a painter.
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Both" pairs only with "and". "As well as" and "or" break the correlative pair — the most common trap with "both". So the answer is "He is both a poet and a painter."
Source: PYQ-style

136. Spot the error: Hardly had I (P) / entered the room (Q) / than the phone rang. (R) / No error (S)
(a) Part P (b) Part Q (c) No error (d) Part R
Answer: (d)
Solution: "Hardly" pairs with "when", not "than" — Part R should read "when the phone rang". The inversion in Part P is correct, so it is not the error. So the answer is Part R.
Source: PYQ-style

137. Spot the error: Unless you do not (P) / apologise, I shall (Q) / not forgive you. (R) / No error (S)
(a) Part Q (b) Part R (c) Part P (d) No error
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Unless" already carries a negative sense, so "do not" in Part P creates a double negative; it should be "Unless you apologise". This double-negative trap is a favourite of examiners. So the answer is Part P.
Source: PYQ-style

138. Spot the error: No sooner she had (P) / finished her speech (Q) / than the audience started clapping. (R) / No error (S)
(a) No error (b) Part P (c) Part R (d) Part Q
Answer: (b)
Solution: After "No sooner" the helping verb must come before the subject: "No sooner had she". Part P has the wrong word order; the "than" in Part R is correct. So the answer is Part P.
Source: PYQ-style

139. Spot the error: Being a rainy day, (P) / I stayed (Q) / at home. (R) / No error (S)
(a) Part P (b) Part Q (c) Part R (d) No error
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Being a rainy day" is a dangling phrase — "I" am not a rainy day. It should be "As it was a rainy day" or "It being a rainy day"; Parts Q and R are fine. So the answer is Part P.
Source: PYQ-style

140. Spot the error: He not only (P) / plays cricket (Q) / but also football. (R) / No error (S)
(a) Part Q (b) Part R (c) Part P (d) No error
Answer: (c)
Solution: Correlatives must be placed just before the parallel words: "He plays not only cricket but also football." Part P misplaces "not only" before the verb, breaking parallelism; Part R looks odd but is acceptable once Part P is fixed. So the answer is Part P.
Source: NCERT Class 10 English Grammar

141. Choose the sentence with correct use of conjunctions:
(a) Because he was ill, so he did not come. (b) Because he was ill, he did not come. (c) Since he was ill, therefore he did not come. (d) Because he was ill, hence he did not come.
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Because/Since" must not be paired with "so/therefore/hence" — one conjunction is enough; doubling them is the sound-right trap. So the answer is "Because he was ill, he did not come."
Source: PYQ-style

142. ___ had the match started ___ it began to rain.
(a) No sooner / when (b) Scarcely / than (c) Hardly / when (d) No sooner / then
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Hardly…when" is the correct pair with inversion. "Scarcely…than" and "No sooner…when" mix up the two fixed pairs — the examiner's favourite confusion. So the answer is "Hardly / when".
Source: PYQ-style

143. He is ___ a liar ___ a thief.
(a) either / nor (b) neither / or (c) neither / nor (d) either / and
Answer: (c)
Solution: The fixed pairs are "either…or" and "neither…nor"; mixing them ("either/nor", "neither/or") is the trap. So the answer is "neither / nor".
Source: NCERT Class 9 English Grammar

144. Spot the error: Supposing if (P) / he refuses, (Q) / what will you do? (R) / No error (S)
(a) Part R (b) Part Q (c) Part P (d) No error
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Supposing" and "if" mean the same; using both is redundant. Part P should be either "Supposing he refuses" or "If he refuses". So the answer is Part P.
Source: PYQ-style

145. Choose the correct sentence:
(a) He came late owing to he missed the bus. (b) He came late because of he missed the bus. (c) He came late due to he missed the bus. (d) He came late because he missed the bus.
Answer: (d)
Solution: "Because" is a conjunction and takes a clause; "because of/due to/owing to" are prepositions and take a noun phrase, not "he missed the bus". So the answer is "He came late because he missed the bus."
Source: PYQ-style

146. Spot the error: Neither of the two boys (P) / have done (Q) / his homework. (R) / No error (S)
(a) Part P (b) Part Q (c) Part R (d) No error
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Neither of" takes a singular verb, so Part Q should be "has done". The plural verb tempts because "boys" is plural — the intervening-phrase trap. So the answer is Part Q.
Source: PYQ-style

147. ___ the rain stopped, the players returned to the field.
(a) Unless (b) As soon as (c) Until (d) Lest
Answer: (b)
Solution: Two events happening one immediately after the other need "as soon as". "Until" would reverse the time logic, which is the trap. So the answer is "As soon as".
Source: PYQ-style

148. Spot the error: Walk carefully (P) / lest you may (Q) / fall down. (R) / No error (S)
(a) Part Q (b) No error (c) Part R (d) Part P
Answer: (a)
Solution: After "lest", use "should" or the bare verb, not "may": "lest you should fall". "May" after "lest" is a classic error tested repeatedly. So the answer is Part Q.
Source: NCERT Class 10 English Grammar

149. Choose the correctly punctuated and conjoined sentence:
(a) He was tired; therefore, he took rest. (b) He was tired; therefore he took rest. (c) He was tired therefore, he took rest. (d) He was tired, therefore he took rest.
Answer: (a)
Solution: A conjunctive adverb like "therefore" joining two clauses needs a semicolon before it and a comma after it. Using only a comma is a comma splice — the tempting trap. So the answer is "He was tired; therefore, he took rest."
Source: PYQ-style

150. Spot the error: The reason why he failed (P) / was because (Q) / he did not study. (R) / No error (S)
(a) Part Q (b) No error (c) Part R (d) Part P
Answer: (a)
Solution: "The reason…was because" is redundant; the correct form is "The reason…was that he did not study". "Because" after "the reason is" is a well-known sound-right trap, so the sentence is not error-free. So the answer is Part Q.
Source: PYQ-style
