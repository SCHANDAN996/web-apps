🧒 FEYNMAN TECHNIQUE — Conjunction Explained Like You're 12

**The Hardest Concept: Why "Although... but" is WRONG**

Imagine you have two best friends, Ravi and Mohan. They both want to hold your hand at the same time — one on the left, one on the right. Sounds fine? Now imagine BOTH of them trying to do the SAME job — both pulling your left hand. Chaos, right? You'd say, "Hey! Only ONE of you can do this job!"

That's exactly what happens with **"Although... but"**.

- **Although** = a worker whose job is to show *contrast* ("even though this happened...")
- **But** = a worker whose job is ALSO to show *contrast*

If you write: ❌ *"Although he was tired, but he kept working."*
You have hired TWO workers for ONE job. The sentence gets confused. The examiner knows students make this mistake, so this exact trap appears again and again in SSC, Railway, and Bank exams.

✅ Correct: *"Although he was tired, he kept working."*
✅ Also correct: *"He was tired, but he kept working."*

**One contrast = One conjunction. Never two.**

---

**The Unforgettable Trick: 🚪 "One Door, One Key"**

Think of every sentence as a room with ONE door. Each door needs exactly ONE key:

| Door (Job) | Keys (pick only ONE) |
|---|---|
| Contrast | although / though / but / yet |
| Reason | because / since / as |
| Time | when / while / until / before |
| Condition | if / unless / provided that |

❌ *Because he was ill, so he didn't come.* → Two keys (because + so) in one door!
✅ *Because he was ill, he didn't come.*
✅ *He was ill, so he didn't come.*

**Memory line:** *"Although-but, since-so, because-therefore — ek aayega toh doosra jayega!"* (If one comes, the other must go!)

---

**The Second Hardest Concept: Correlative Pairs are Married Couples 💍**

Some conjunctions always come in pairs, and they are *married* — you cannot swap partners:

- **Either ... or** ✅ (❌ Either ... nor)
- **Neither ... nor** ✅ (❌ Neither ... or)
- **Not only ... but also** ✅
- **Both ... and** ✅
- **Hardly/Scarcely ... when** ✅ (❌ Hardly ... than)
- **No sooner ... than** ✅ (❌ No sooner ... when)

**Trick:** *"No sooner" has "soon" → it marries "than" (both talk about speed). "Hardly/Scarcely" are slow, shy words → they marry "when".*

Also remember: these couples demand **parallel structure** — whatever follows the first partner must match what follows the second:
❌ *He not only plays cricket but also football.*
✅ *He plays not only cricket but also football.*
