# 📋 Important Grammar Rules & Tips — Conjunction

## (a) 12 High-Yield Rules

| # | Rule | Example | Examiner's Trap |
|---|------|---------|-----------------|
| 1 | **Correlative conjunctions come in pairs** — either…or, neither…nor, not only…but also, both…and, whether…or. Never split or swap them. | Either you work hard **or** you fail. | "Neither…or" ❌ — it is always "neither…**nor**". |
| 2 | **Parallel structure after correlatives** — the same part of speech must follow both halves of the pair. | He is not only a singer **but also** a dancer. (noun + noun) | "Not only he sings but also dances" ❌ — verb after "not only", noun expected. |
| 3 | **Neither…nor / Either…or → verb agrees with the nearer subject.** | Neither the teacher nor the students **were** present. | Students see "teacher" and write "was" ❌ — check the subject closest to the verb. |
| 4 | **No "that" after "so" in so…that confusion; use "so…that" for result, "such…that" with nouns.** | He was so tired **that** he slept at once. / It was such a hot day **that** we stayed in. | "So a hot day that…" ❌ — "so" takes an adjective, "such" takes a noun phrase. |
| 5 | **"Because" and "so" never come together** — one sentence, one connector of cause. | Because it rained, the match was cancelled. **or** It rained, so the match was cancelled. | "Because it rained, so the match was cancelled" ❌ — classic double-conjunction trap. |
| 6 | **"Although/Though" and "but/yet" never come together.** | Although he is rich, he is humble. **or** He is rich, yet humble. | "Although he is rich, but he is humble" ❌ — the most repeated error in SSC/Bank exams. |
| 7 | **"Since" (time) takes a point of time; "for" takes a period.** | I have lived here **since** 2015 / **for** nine years. | "Since two years" ❌ — two years is a period, so use "for". |
| 8 | **"Until" = time, "unless" = condition.** | Wait **until** I return. / Don't go **unless** I call you. | "Unless the bell rings" for time ❌ — unless means "if…not", not "till". |
| 9 | **"Unless" already means "if…not" — never add another "not".** | Unless you work hard, you will fail. | "Unless you do **not** work hard…" ❌ — double negative reverses the meaning. |
| 10 | **"Scarcely/Hardly…when" and "No sooner…than" — fixed pairs, past perfect before them.** | No sooner had he arrived **than** it rained. | "No sooner…when" ❌ and "Scarcely…than" ❌ — examiners swap the partners. |
| 11 | **"Lest" is followed by "should" and never by "not".** | Walk fast lest you **should** miss the train. | "Lest you should **not** miss…" ❌ — lest itself carries the negative sense. |
| 12 | **"As if / As though" takes past tense for unreal present.** | He talks as if he **were** a king. | "As if he **is** a king" ❌ — unreal comparison needs past (were, not was, for formal English). |

## (b) Time Management — the 15/45-Second Rule

**⚡ 15-Second Questions (Fact / Rule-based — answer instantly):**
- Fill-in-the-blank with a fixed pair: "No sooner had he come ___ it started raining." → *than* (Rule 10). If you know the pair, don't re-read.
- Spot-the-error with double conjunctions: "Although…but", "Because…so", "Unless…not" (Rules 5, 6, 9). The moment you see the pair, mark the error.
- "Since vs For" and "Until vs Unless" blanks (Rules 7, 8) — pure recall.

**🕐 45-Second Questions (Inference / Structure-based — slow down):**
- Sentence improvement where **all four options look grammatical** — check parallel structure after correlatives (Rule 2) and verb agreement with the nearer subject (Rule 3).
- Error spotting in long sentences where the correlative pair is split across two lines ("Not only did he… but also…") — you must match both halves.
- Questions mixing tense with conjunctions ("as if", "no sooner", "lest") — verify the tense, not just the connector.

**Strategy:** If a conjunction question has no fixed pair and no obvious double-conjunction, it is a 45-second question — read the full sentence twice before answering.

## (c) Morphology — Roots & Affixes Behind "Conjunction"

| Root / Affix | Meaning | Derived Words |
|---|---|---|
| **junct / join** (Latin *jungere*) | to join | con**junct**ion, **junct**ion, ad**junct**, dis**junct**ion, **join**t |
| **con-** (Latin) | together, with | **con**nect, **con**join, **con**tract, **con**gregate |
| **dis-** (Latin) | apart, away | **dis**join, **dis**connect, **dis**junction |
| **sub-** (Latin) | under, below | **sub**ordinate (subordinating conjunction = joins a clause "under" the main clause) |
| **co- / cor-** (Latin) | together | **co**ordinate (coordinating conjunction = joins equals), **cor**relative |
| **-tion / -ion** (Latin suffix) | act or state of | conjunc**tion**, junc**tion**, connec**tion** |

**Memory hook:** *Conjunction = con (together) + junct (join) + tion (act) → "the act of joining together."* A coordinating conjunction joins **equals** (co- = together, ordinate = rank); a subordinating conjunction puts one clause **under** another (sub- = under).
