Card 1
Front: What is a conjunction?
Back: A word that joins words, phrases, or clauses. Example: Ram and Shyam are friends. ("and" joins two nouns.)

Card 2
Front: Name the three main types of conjunctions.
Back: 1. Coordinating (FANBOYS) 2. Subordinating (because, although, if, when...) 3. Correlative (either...or, neither...nor, not only...but also).

Card 3
Front: 🧠 Mnemonic: How do you remember the 7 coordinating conjunctions?
Back: FANBOYS — For, And, Nor, But, Or, Yet, So.

Card 4
Front: Which conjunctions always work in pairs? Give two examples.
Back: Correlative conjunctions. Examples: either...or, neither...nor, both...and, not only...but also, no sooner...than, hardly...when.

Card 5
Front: ⚠️ Examiner's Trap: "Although he is rich, but he is not happy." — Correct or wrong?
Back: Wrong. "Although" and "but" cannot be used together. Correct: "Although he is rich, he is not happy." OR "He is rich, but he is not happy."

Card 6
Front: What is the rule for "neither...nor" with two subjects?
Back: The verb agrees with the subject nearer to it. "Neither the teacher nor the students were present." (students = plural → were)

Card 7
Front: Fill in: "No sooner did he reach the station ___ the train left."
Back: than. "No sooner...than" is a fixed pair. (Trap: students wrongly write "when".)

Card 8
Front: Which pair is used with "hardly" and "scarcely"?
Back: Hardly/Scarcely...when. Example: "Hardly had she slept when the phone rang."

Card 9
Front: ⚠️ Examiner's Trap: "Unless you do not work hard, you will fail." — Find the error.
Back: "Unless" is already negative, so remove "not". Correct: "Unless you work hard, you will fail."

Card 10
Front: What is the difference between "because" and "so"?
Back: "Because" gives the reason; "so" gives the result. Never use both together: "Because he was ill, so he stayed home" is wrong.

Card 11
Front: 🧠 Mnemonic: How to remember common subordinating conjunctions?
Back: "A WHITE BUS" — After, When, If, Though, Although, Because, Until, Since.

Card 12
Front: Choose: "He is ___ intelligent ___ hardworking." (both...and / either...or)
Back: both...and. The sentence shows two positive qualities together.

Card 13
Front: When do we use "yet" as a conjunction?
Back: To show contrast, like "but". Example: "He is poor, yet he is honest."

Card 14
Front: "Until" vs "unless" — what is the difference?
Back: "Until" refers to time (Wait until I come). "Unless" refers to condition (Unless you run, you will miss the bus).

Card 15
Front: Correct the sentence: "He not only plays cricket but also football."
Back: "Not only" must be placed just before the parallel element: "He plays not only cricket but also football."

Card 16
Front: Which conjunction shows choice between two options?
Back: "Or" (coordinating) and "either...or" (correlative). Example: "Take tea or coffee."

Card 17
Front: Fill in: "___ it was raining, we went out." (Although / Because)
Back: Although. The sentence shows contrast, not reason.

Card 18
Front: ⚠️ Examiner's Trap: "Scarcely had he entered than it started raining." — Correct?
Back: Wrong. "Scarcely" pairs with "when", not "than". Correct: "Scarcely had he entered when it started raining."

Card 19
Front: What type of conjunction is "therefore"?
Back: It is a conjunctive adverb (transitional word), not a pure conjunction. It shows result: "He was late; therefore, he missed the train."

Card 20
Front: One-line rule: can a sentence start with "Because" or "Although"?
Back: Yes, if it is a complete sentence with a main clause. "Because it rained, the match was cancelled." ✓ But "Because it rained." alone is a fragment.
