# Feynman Technique — Verb

## The Hardest Idea: "Which form of the verb comes after which word?"

Students know the verb but pick the wrong form: *has went*, *can sings*, *did came*. The trick is to notice that some little words act like **signals** — they decide the form of the next verb.

## A Story: The Traffic Signals

Imagine a road where every verb is a car. At each crossing stands a traffic signal:

- 🟢 **Modal signal (can, will, must, should…)** — "Go plain!" The car must stay in its **base form**: *can swim, will go*.
- 🟡 **Have signal (has, have, had)** — "Show your third form!" The car becomes the **past participle**: *has gone, had eaten*.
- 🔵 **Be signal (is, am, are, was, were)** — "Keep moving!" The car takes **-ing**: *is running*. (In the passive, the be-signal also asks for the third form: *was written*.)
- 🔴 **Do signal (do, does, did)** — "Back to plain!" The car returns to its **base form**: *did go, does play*.

So "She can sings" is a car that ignored the green signal, and "He has went" is a car that showed the wrong card at the yellow one.

## The Unforgettable Trick: "Look left before you choose"

Before choosing a verb form, **look at the word just before it**. Modal or do → base form. Has/have/had → third form. Is/are/was → -ing (or third form in the passive).
