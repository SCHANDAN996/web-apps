# Verb (क्रिया)

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this chapter you will be able to:

1. Identify the verb in any sentence.
2. Tell main verbs from helping (auxiliary) verbs and modals.
3. Use the correct form — base, past, past participle, -ing and -s.
4. Make the verb agree with its subject.
5. Tell transitive verbs from intransitive verbs.

## The Hook: The Engine of the Sentence

A sentence without a verb is like a bus without an engine — it has seats (nouns) and paint (adjectives), but it goes nowhere. "Riya … to school." What did Riya do? *Walked? Ran? Cycled?* The verb tells us the **action** or the **state**, and only then does the sentence move.

## Why Students Struggle

- They use Hindi word order and forget the helping verb ("She going" instead of "She is going").
- They mix up past and past participle ("I have went").
- They forget the -s for he/she/it in the present tense ("He go to school").

🧠 **Active Recall:** Find the verb in "The baby slept peacefully." What does it tell us?

## Chunk 1: What Is a Verb?

A verb shows an **action** (run, write, eat) or a **state** (is, seem, belong).
- Action: *Aman **plays** cricket.*
- State: *The milk **is** hot.*

## Chunk 2: Main Verbs, Helping Verbs and Modals

| Type | Examples | In a sentence |
|------|----------|---------------|
| Main verb | write, eat, go | She **writes** letters. |
| Helping verb (be, have, do) | is, are, was, has, did | She **is** writing. They **have** gone. |
| Modal | can, could, may, might, must, should, will, would | You **must** wear a helmet. |

After a modal, always use the **base form**: *He can **swim*** (not *swims*).

## Chunk 3: The Five Forms of a Verb

| Base | -s form | Past | Past participle | -ing form |
|------|---------|------|-----------------|-----------|
| go | goes | went | gone | going |
| write | writes | wrote | written | writing |
| eat | eats | ate | eaten | eating |
| play | plays | played | played | playing |
| begin | begins | began | begun | beginning |

- **Regular verbs** add -ed (play → played).
- **Irregular verbs** change in their own way (go → went → gone). Learn them by heart.
- The **past participle** comes after *has / have / had* and in the passive: *She **has written**. The letter **was written**.*

Mnemonic — **"Had, Has, Have — Participle Behave"**: after these three words, use the third form.

🧠 **Active Recall:** Correct the sentence: "I have went to Delhi."

## Chunk 4: Subject–Verb Agreement

- He / She / It / a singular noun + verb with **-s** in the present: *She **reads**.*
- I / You / We / They / plural nouns + base form: *They **read**.*
- **Each, every, either, neither, everyone** → singular: *Each of the boys **has** a bag.*
- **One of the + plural noun** → singular verb: *One of my friends **is** a doctor.*
- Words like **news, mathematics** → singular: *The news **is** good.*

## Chunk 5: Transitive and Intransitive Verbs

- **Transitive** verbs need an object: *She **bought** a pen.* (bought what? a pen)
- **Intransitive** verbs do not: *The baby **slept**.*

Ask "**what?**" or "**whom?**" after the verb. If there is an answer, the verb is transitive.

## 🔁 Linguistic Bridge

| In Hindi we say… | Literal English (wrong) | Correct English |
|------------------|-------------------------|-----------------|
| वह स्कूल जा रही है | She going to school. | She **is** going to school. |
| मैं दिल्ली गया हूँ | I have went to Delhi. | I have **gone** to Delhi. |
| वह रोज़ पढ़ता है | He read daily. | He **reads** daily. |
| तुम क्या कर रहे हो? | What you are doing? | What **are you** doing? |

## Summary Table

| Point | Rule | Example |
|-------|------|---------|
| Helping verb with -ing | is/am/are/was/were + -ing | She is reading. |
| Perfect tense | has/have/had + past participle | They have finished. |
| Modal | modal + base form | He can swim. |
| Present, he/she/it | verb + s/es | She watches TV. |
| Each/every/one of | singular verb | Each student has a book. |

## ⚠️ Caution: Examiner's Trap

1. **Past vs past participle:** "has went", "had ate" — after has/have/had use the third form (gone, eaten).
2. **Modal + -s:** "She can sings" is wrong — after a modal the verb stays in the base form.
3. **"One of the …":** the noun after it is plural, but the verb agrees with "one" — singular.
