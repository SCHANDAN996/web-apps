# 📊 PYQ Analysis — Fill in the Blanks (Basic)

## 1. What Exams Actually Ask (Pattern Analysis)

Fill in the Blanks (FIB) appears in **every** foundation-level paper — SSC GD, SSC MTS, SSC CHSL, RRB NTPC, RRB Group D, IBPS Clerk, and state police/constable exams. Based on the pattern of official papers, the questions fall into a small number of repeatable families:

**Family 1 — Subject–Verb Agreement (the biggest family).**
The examiner hides the real subject behind an intervening phrase: *"The quality of the mangoes ___ good."* Students match the verb with "mangoes" and fall into the trap. Almost every paper has at least one such question.

**Family 2 — Articles (a / an / the).**
Tested through vowel *sound* (an hour, a university), fixed uses (the Ganga, the sun), and zero article (___ honesty is the best policy).

**Family 3 — Prepositions.**
Fixed combinations dominate: good **at**, angry **with**, afraid **of**, depend **on**, listen **to**, married **to**, different **from**. These are pure memory questions — 15-second territory.

**Family 4 — Tenses.**
Signal words give the answer away: *yesterday* → past simple; *since/for* → present perfect; *by tomorrow* → future perfect. The examiner's trap is mixing a past signal with a perfect-tense option.

**Family 5 — Vocabulary in context.**
One blank, four similar-looking words (accept/except, advice/advise, principal/principle, lose/loose). Meaning must fit the sentence, not just the spelling.

**Family 6 — Conjunctions & connectors.**
Although/because/unless/until — testing whether you understand the *logic* of the sentence (contrast vs. cause vs. condition).

**Family 7 — Pronouns & determiners.**
Each/every, much/many, few/a few, little/a little, who/whom/whose.

## 2. The Examiner's Four Traps (with names)

| Trap | How it works | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Intervening Phrase** | Extra words between subject and verb hide the real subject | "The box of chocolates ___ (is/are) mine." |
| **Collective Noun** | Team, family, committee, jury — singular or plural? | "The team ___ (is/are) playing well." |
| **Sound-Right** | The wrong option *sounds* natural because of Hindi speech habits | "He is more taller" / "return back" |
| **Over-thinking** | The simple, obvious answer is correct; students reject it as "too easy" | "She ___ tea every morning. (drinks)" |

## 3. Top 10 High-Yield Questions (with Solutions)

**Q1.** The quality of the apples ___ not good.
(a) are (b) were (c) was (d) have been
**Answer: (c)**
**Solution:** Subject is "quality" (singular), not "apples". The phrase "of the apples" is an intervening phrase planted to mislead. Singular subject → *was*.
**Trap type:** Intervening Phrase | **Source:** PYQ-style

**Q2.** He has been working here ___ 2015.
(a) for (b) since (c) from (d) by
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** *Since* + point of time (2015); *for* + period of time (ten years). "From" is a Sound-Right trap from Hindi ("2015 से").
**Trap type:** Sound-Right | **Source:** NCERT Class 9 English

**Q3.** One of my friends ___ gone to Delhi.
(a) have (b) has (c) are (d) were
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** "One of + plural noun" always takes a **singular** verb — the real subject is "one".
**Trap type:** Intervening Phrase | **Source:** PYQ-style

**Q4.** She is ___ honest woman.
(a) a (b) an (c) the (d) no article
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** "Honest" begins with a vowel *sound* (h is silent) → *an*. Articles follow sound, not spelling.
**Trap type:** Sound-Right | **Source:** NCERT Class 8 English

**Q5.** The committee ___ taken its decision.
(a) have (b) has (c) are (d) were
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** A collective noun acting as one unit takes a singular verb. (Plural only when members act separately: "The jury *are* divided.")
**Trap type:** Collective Noun | **Source:** PYQ-style

**Q6.** I ___ this movie yesterday.
(a) have seen (b) see (c) saw (d) had seen
**Answer: (c)**
**Solution:** A finished past time word (*yesterday*) forces the simple past. Present perfect (*have seen*) can never stand with "yesterday" — the most repeated tense trap.
**Trap type:** Sound-Right | **Source:** PYQ-style

**Q7.** Please accept my ___ on your success.
(a) advise (b) advice (c) advices (d) advising
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** A noun is needed after "my". *Advice* = noun; *advise* = verb. "Advices" is wrong because advice is uncountable.
**Trap type:** Over-thinking (students pick the fancy-looking option) | **Source:** PYQ-style

**Q8.** ___ he is rich, he is not happy.
(a) Because (b) Although (c) Unless (d) Until
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** The two clauses show *contrast* (rich, yet unhappy) → *Although*. "Because" reverses the logic — read the meaning, not just the grammar.
**Trap type:** Over-thinking | **Source:** NCERT Class 10 English

**Q9.** There is ___ water in the pot; you can drink it.
(a) little (b) a little (c) few (d) the few
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** "You can drink it" shows some water *exists* → positive sense → *a little*. "Little" would mean almost none (negative sense). Water is uncountable, so few/fewer are impossible.
**Trap type:** Sound-Right | **Source:** NCERT Class 9 English

**Q10.** Bread and butter ___ my favourite breakfast.
(a) are (b) were (c) is (d) have been
**Answer: (c)**
**Solution:** Two nouns joined by "and" that form **one single dish/idea** take a singular verb. Compare: "Bread and butter *are* sold here" (two separate items).
**Trap type:** Collective Noun | **Source:** PYQ-style

## 4. The 15/45-Second Rule Applied

**15-second question (Fact-based) — Q2 above:**
See "2015" → a point of time → *since*. No reading of the full sentence needed beyond the blank. Recall rule → answer → move. Target: 15 seconds.

**45-second question (Inference-based) — Q8 above:**
You must read *both* clauses, understand the relationship (contrast), then test each connector against the meaning. This needs full comprehension of the sentence — allow 45 seconds, never rush it.

**Exam strategy:** In a paper, do all 15-second FIBs first (prepositions, articles, since/for, signal-word tenses). Mark the logic/meaning questions and return with fresh eyes.

## 5. Weightage & Preparation Priority (in words)

Across foundation exams, FIB is a **high-frequency, low-time-cost** question type — it rewards rule memory more than deep analysis. Within the chapter, prepare in this order:

1. **Subject–verb agreement** (most asked, most trapped)
2. **Prepositions** (pure recall — easiest marks)
3. **Articles** (sound rule + fixed uses)
4. **Tenses with signal words** (yesterday/since/by tomorrow)
5. **Confusable word pairs** (accept/except, advice/advise)
6. **Connectors** (logic-based — the only true 45-second family)

Master families 1–4 and you cover the large majority of what foundation papers actually ask.
