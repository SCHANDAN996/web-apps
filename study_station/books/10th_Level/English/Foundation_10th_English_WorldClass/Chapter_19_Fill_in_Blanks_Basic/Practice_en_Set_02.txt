Questions 26–40: Easy | Questions 41–50: Medium

26. She ______ to school every day.
(a) go (b) going (c) goes (d) gone
Answer: (c)
Solution: With a singular subject "she" in the simple present tense, the verb takes "-s". "Go" is tempting because it matches the base form, but it is used only with I, you, we, they and plural nouns, so the answer is goes.
Source: NCERT Class 6 English Grammar

27. I ______ my homework before dinner yesterday.
(a) finish (b) finishes (c) finishing (d) finished
Answer: (d)
Solution: The word "yesterday" signals the simple past tense, so the past form is needed. "Finish" is tempting but it is present tense, so the answer is finished.
Source: NCERT Class 7 English Grammar

28. There ______ a book on the table.
(a) are (b) be (c) were (d) is
Answer: (d)
Solution: "A book" is singular, so it takes "is". "Are" is tempting because "there" often confuses learners, but the verb agrees with the noun after it, so the answer is is.
Source: NCERT Class 6 English Grammar

29. The children ______ playing in the park now.
(a) is (b) are (c) was (d) be
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Children" is plural and "now" shows present continuous tense, so "are" fits. "Is" is tempting for learners who treat "children" as one group, but it is plural, so the answer is are.
Source: NCERT Class 6 English Grammar

30. He is ______ honest man.
(a) an (b) a (c) the (d) no article
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Honest" begins with a silent "h", so the sound is a vowel sound and takes "an". "A" is tempting because the spelling starts with "h", but articles follow sound, not spelling, so the answer is an.
Source: NCERT Class 7 English Grammar

31. We ______ cricket every Sunday.
(a) plays (b) played (c) play (d) playing
Answer: (c)
Solution: "We" is plural and "every Sunday" shows a habitual action in the simple present, so the base verb is used. "Plays" is tempting but it is only for singular third-person subjects, so the answer is play.
Source: NCERT Class 6 English Grammar

32. The cat is sitting ______ the chair.
(a) in (b) on (c) at (d) by
Answer: (b)
Solution: We sit "on" a chair (on its surface). "In" is tempting because we say "in a room", but a chair with a flat surface takes "on", so the answer is on.
Source: NCERT Class 6 English Grammar

33. She has lived here ______ 2015.
(a) for (b) from (c) since (d) at
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Since" is used with a point of time (a year, a date), while "for" is used with a duration. "For" is the classic tempting wrong option here, but 2015 is a point of time, so the answer is since.
Source: NCERT Class 8 English Grammar

34. ______ you like some tea?
(a) Should (b) Will (c) Shall (d) Would
Answer: (d)
Solution: "Would you like…?" is the fixed polite expression for offering something. "Will" is tempting because it also asks about the future, but it is not used for polite offers, so the answer is Would.
Source: NCERT Class 7 English Grammar

35. My brother is ______ than me.
(a) tall (b) taller (c) tallest (d) more tall
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Than" requires the comparative degree, and "tall" forms its comparative as "taller". "More tall" is tempting for learners who overuse "more", but short adjectives take "-er", so the answer is taller.
Source: NCERT Class 7 English Grammar

36. The train ______ at 6 a.m. tomorrow.
(a) leave (b) left (c) leaving (d) leaves
Answer: (d)
Solution: Fixed timetables and schedules are expressed in the simple present even for future time, and "the train" is singular. "Leave" is tempting because tomorrow feels plural-free, but the singular subject needs "-s", so the answer is leaves.
Source: NCERT Class 8 English Grammar

37. I have ______ finished my work.
(a) yet (b) since (c) already (d) ever
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Already" is used in positive present perfect sentences to show completion. "Yet" is tempting because it also pairs with the present perfect, but it is used in negatives and questions, so the answer is already.
Source: NCERT Class 8 English Grammar

38. Neither Ram nor his friends ______ present.
(a) were (b) is (c) was (d) be
Answer: (a)
Solution: With "neither…nor", the verb agrees with the nearer subject, which is "friends" (plural). "Was" is tempting because "Ram" is singular and comes first — this is the examiner's proximity trap — so the answer is were.
Source: PYQ-style (subject-verb agreement)

39. He is fond ______ music.
(a) of (b) in (c) at (d) with
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Fond" always takes the preposition "of". "In" is tempting because Hindi speakers say "mein ruchi", but English fixes "fond of", so the answer is of.
Source: NCERT Class 8 English Grammar

40. Please ______ the door; it is cold outside.
(a) closing (b) closed (c) closes (d) close
Answer: (d)
Solution: Imperative sentences use the base form of the verb. "Closes" is tempting because of the singular feel of the request, but imperatives never take "-s", so the answer is close.
Source: NCERT Class 6 English Grammar

41. Each of the boys ______ given a prize.
(a) were (b) are (c) have (d) was
Answer: (d)
Solution: "Each of" is always followed by a singular verb, even though "boys" is plural. "Were" is tempting because the plural noun sits right before the blank — the classic intervening-phrase trap — so the answer is was.
Source: PYQ-style (subject-verb agreement)

42. She ______ in this city since 2018.
(a) lives (b) has been living (c) is living (d) lived
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Since 2018" with an action continuing up to now requires the present perfect continuous. "Lives" is tempting because Hindi uses the present for such sentences, but English needs the perfect form, so the answer is has been living.
Source: NCERT Class 9 English Grammar

43. The Ganga ______ one of the holiest rivers of India.
(a) is (b) are (c) were (d) have been
Answer: (a)
Solution: The subject is "The Ganga" (singular); "one of the holiest rivers" is only a phrase describing it. "Are" is tempting because "rivers" is plural and nearest to the blank, but the verb agrees with the real subject, so the answer is is.
Source: PYQ-style (subject-verb agreement)

44. I ______ him at the market yesterday evening.
(a) saw (b) have seen (c) see (d) had seen
Answer: (a)
Solution: A definite past time ("yesterday evening") takes the simple past, never the present perfect. "Have seen" is tempting because learners overuse the perfect tense, but it cannot go with a finished past time, so the answer is saw.
Source: NCERT Class 8 English Grammar

45. He is ______ university student.
(a) an (b) a (c) the (d) no article
Answer: (b)
Solution: "University" begins with a "yoo" sound, which is a consonant sound, so it takes "a". "An" is tempting because the spelling starts with the vowel "u" — a favourite examiner trap — so the answer is a.
Source: NCERT Class 7 English Grammar

46. You should ______ your elders.
(a) respects (b) respected (c) respect (d) respecting
Answer: (c)
Solution: After a modal like "should", the base form of the verb is used. "Respects" is tempting because of the singular "you" feeling, but modals are always followed by the plain verb, so the answer is respect.
Source: NCERT Class 7 English Grammar

47. Bread and butter ______ my favourite breakfast.
(a) are (b) were (c) is (d) have been
Answer: (c)
Solution: When two nouns joined by "and" name one single dish or idea, the verb is singular. "Are" is tempting because two nouns appear before the blank, but "bread and butter" is one combined meal, so the answer is is.
Source: PYQ-style (subject-verb agreement)

48. She has been waiting here ______ two hours.
(a) for (b) from (c) since (d) at
Answer: (a)
Solution: "For" is used with a period or duration of time, and "two hours" is a duration. "Since" is tempting because it also pairs with the perfect continuous, but it needs a point of time, so the answer is for.
Source: NCERT Class 8 English Grammar

49. If it rains tomorrow, we ______ at home.
(a) would stay (b) will stay (c) stayed (d) stay
Answer: (b)
Solution: In a first conditional (real future possibility), the if-clause takes the simple present and the main clause takes "will + verb". "Would stay" is tempting because it sounds polite, but it belongs to unreal conditions, so the answer is will stay.
Source: NCERT Class 9 English Grammar

50. One of my friends ______ gone to Delhi.
(a) has (b) have (c) are (d) were
Answer: (a)
Solution: The real subject is "One", which is singular, so it takes "has". "Have" is tempting because "friends" stands right before the blank — the intervening-phrase trap examiners love — so the answer is has.
Source: PYQ-style (subject-verb agreement)
