126. The committee ______ divided in their opinion on the new policy.
(a) were (b) was (c) is (d) has been
Answer: (a)
Solution: The tempting choice is "was" because "committee" looks singular, but the phrase "divided in their opinion" shows the members are acting separately, so a plural verb is needed. When a collective noun's members act individually, use a plural verb, so the answer is "were".

127. Neither the manager nor the employees ______ aware of the change in schedule.
(a) was (b) is (c) were (d) has been
Answer: (c)
Solution: Many pick "was" by matching "manager", but with "neither…nor" the verb agrees with the nearer subject, which is "employees" (plural). The trap is agreeing with the first subject instead of the closest one, so the answer is "were".

128. He is one of those students who ______ always punctual.
(a) is (b) are (c) was (d) has been
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is "is", chosen because "one" feels singular. But the relative pronoun "who" refers to "students" (plural), so the verb must be plural. In "one of those + plural noun + who", the verb agrees with the plural noun, so the answer is "are".

129. The quality of the mangoes ______ not good this season.
(a) was (b) were (c) are (d) have been

Solution: The tempting error is "are", agreeing with "mangoes", but the true subject is "quality", which is singular. The intervening phrase "of the mangoes" is a classic examiner trap, so the answer is "was".

130. Hardly ______ the station when the train left.
(a) I had reached (b) I reached (c) had I reached (d) did I reach
Answer: (c)
Solution: "I had reached" sounds natural but breaks the inversion rule: after "hardly" at the start of a sentence, the auxiliary comes before the subject. The correct pattern is "Hardly had + subject + past participle", so the answer is "had I reached".
