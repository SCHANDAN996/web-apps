# 🧒 Feynman Technique — Fill in the Blanks (Basic)

## The Hardest Concept, Explained Like You're 12

**The hardest thing in Fill in the Blanks is not knowing words — it is knowing WHICH word the sentence is asking for.**

Imagine this. Your mother says: "Please ___ the door, it's cold outside."

You don't need a dictionary. Your brain screams "close!" Why? Because the rest of the sentence — "cold outside" — is a **clue**. The sentence itself tells you the answer.

Fill in the Blanks works exactly like this. Every blank has **clues hiding around it**. Your job is not to know every English word. Your job is to be a **detective** who finds the clue.

## The Three Clues Every Blank Hides

**Clue 1 — Grammar (the shape of the word)**
Look at the words next to the blank. They tell you the *type* of word needed.

- "He ___ to school every day." → After "He" we need a **verb with -s** → *goes* ✓ (not *go*, not *going*)
- "She is a ___ girl." → Between "a" and a noun we need an **adjective** → *honest* ✓

**Clue 2 — Meaning (the story of the sentence)**
Read the whole sentence like a story. What is it trying to say?

- "The medicine made him feel ___." → Medicine helps, so a *positive* word → *better* ✓ (not *worse*)

**Clue 3 — Word Partners (collocations)**
Some words are best friends. They always sit together.

- We **make** a mistake (not *do* a mistake)
- We **take** a bath (not *make* a bath)
- We **pay** attention (not *give* attention)

## The Daily-Life Example You'll Never Forget

Think of a **puzzle piece**. The blank is a missing puzzle piece. You don't try every piece in the box — you look at the **hole's shape** (grammar) and the **picture around it** (meaning). Only one piece fits both.

Same way: an option that is grammatically correct BUT doesn't match the meaning is a wrong puzzle piece. An option that matches the meaning BUT breaks grammar is also wrong. **The answer must fit BOTH.**

## The Unforgettable Trick: "SHAPE → STORY → PARTNER"

Before choosing any option, ask three questions in order:

1. **SHAPE** — Does the word's form fit? (tense, singular/plural, adjective/adverb)
2. **STORY** — Does the meaning fit the sentence's story? (positive or negative? cause or result?)
3. **PARTNER** — Does this word normally go with its neighbours? (make a mistake, take rest)

If an option fails even ONE question — reject it. This kills 2–3 wrong options in seconds.

## Watch Out: The "Sounds Right" Trap

Your ear can cheat you. "He don't know" *sounds* okay because we hear it in movies — but it is wrong ("He **doesn't** know"). In the exam, trust **rules**, not your ear. If an option "sounds natural" but breaks a grammar rule, it is a trap.
