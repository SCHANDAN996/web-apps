# Fill in the Blanks (Basic) — Content

## 🎯 The Hook

Imagine this: in SSC GD, SSC MTS, or RRB Group D, you get a sentence with one small gap — "She ___ to school every day." Four options stare at you: *go, goes, going, gone*. You have 20 seconds. One word decides one mark. Fill in the Blanks (FIB) is the **highest-return question type** in foundation-level English papers — it tests grammar, vocabulary, and common sense all at once, and it rewards students who have a *method*, not just a "feeling" for English.

This chapter gives you that method.

## 🎯 Learning Objectives

By the end of this chapter you will be able to:

1. Identify what a blank is really testing — tense, subject-verb agreement, preposition, article, or vocabulary.
2. Apply the **3-Step Blank Method** to solve any basic FIB in under 30 seconds.
3. Spot and avoid the three most common examiner traps.
4. Eliminate wrong options even when you don't know the exact answer.

---

## 📖 What Exactly Is a Fill in the Blank Question?

A sentence is given with one (sometimes two) missing words. You must choose the option that makes the sentence **grammatically correct AND meaningful**. Both conditions matter — an option can be grammatically fine but meaningless in context, and it will still be wrong.

Example:
> He has been working here ___ 2019.
> (a) since (b) for (c) from (d) at

"Since + point of time" (2019 is a point) → **(a) since**. "For" is used with a *duration* (for five years). Grammar + meaning together give the answer.

🧠 **Active Recall:** Without looking back — what two conditions must the correct option satisfy? What is the difference between "since" and "for"?

---

## 📖 The 3-Step Blank Method

**Step 1 — Read the full sentence, not just the blank.** The clue is almost always somewhere else in the sentence: a time word (yesterday, since, every day), a subject (he, the team, everyone), or a paired word (not only... but also).

**Step 2 — Predict before you peek.** Before looking at the options, say your own word in the blank. If your predicted word (or its close cousin) is in the options, you are probably right. This protects you from "sound-right" traps.

**Step 3 — Test the winner, kill the losers.** Put your chosen word back into the sentence and read it aloud in your head. Then quickly check why each rejected option fails — wrong tense? wrong preposition? wrong meaning?

Example:
> Neither of the boys ___ done his homework.
> (a) have (b) has (c) are (d) were

Step 1: clue = "Neither of" → singular. Step 2: predict "has". Step 3: "has done" ✓; "have" breaks agreement. Answer: **(b)**.

🧠 **Active Recall:** Recite the three steps. In Step 2, why do we predict *before* looking at the options?

## 📖 The Five Families of Blanks

Almost every basic FIB belongs to one of five families. Naming the family tells you which rule to apply.

| # | Family | Clue in sentence | Example answer |
|---|--------|------------------|----------------|
| 1 | Tense / Verb form | yesterday, since, already, every day | went, has gone, goes |
| 2 | Subject-Verb Agreement | everyone, each, neither, the team | singular verb |
| 3 | Prepositions | verbs/adjectives that demand fixed prepositions | depend **on**, good **at** |
| 4 | Articles (a/an/the) | before a noun | an honest man, the Ganga |
| 5 | Vocabulary / Word choice | meaning of the whole sentence | accept/except, advice/advise |

🧠 **Active Recall:** Which family does this belong to — "She is good ___ painting"? What is the answer?

## ⚠️ Examiner's Trap

> **Trap 1 — The Sound-Right Trap:** Options are chosen so the *wrong* one sounds natural to a Hindi speaker's ear. "He is more smarter than me" sounds fine to many, but "more smarter" is a double comparative — wrong. Never trust your ear alone; check the rule.
>
> **Trap 2 — The Intervening Phrase Trap:** "The quality of the mangoes ___ (was/were) good." The word nearest the blank is "mangoes" (plural), so students pick "were". But the subject is "quality" (singular) → **was**. Always find the *real* subject, not the nearest noun.
>
> **Trap 3 — The Half-Grammar Trap:** An option that is grammatically possible but meaningless in context. "He ___ the letter yesterday" — "writes" is grammatical English, but "yesterday" demands past tense → **wrote**. Read the whole sentence.

## 🔁 Linguistic Bridge (हिंदी से English)

| We think in Hindi | Wrong English | Correct English |
|---|---|---|
| वह रोज़ स्कूल जाता है | He is going to school every day | He **goes** to school every day |
| मैं तुमसे सहमत हूँ | I am agree with you | I **agree** with you |
| उसने मुझसे कहा | He told to me | He **told me** / He **said to** me |
| मैं यहाँ 2019 से हूँ | I am here since 2019 | I **have been** here since 2019 |
| तुम क्या कर रहे हो? | What you are doing? | What **are you** doing? |

**Rule of thumb:** Hindi's present continuous ("जाता है" vs "जा रहा है") maps differently — English uses *simple present* for daily habits and *present continuous* only for actions happening right now.

## 📖 Word-Choice Blanks: Confusing Pairs

Foundation exams love words that look or sound alike:

- **accept** (स्वीकार करना) / **except** (के अलावा)
- **advice** (noun, सलाह) / **advise** (verb, सलाह देना)
- **principal** (प्रधानाचार्य / मुख्य) / **principle** (सिद्धांत)
- **stationary** (स्थिर) / **stationery** (लेखन सामग्री)
- **lose** (खोना) / **loose** (ढीला)

🧠 **Active Recall:** "Please ___ my apology" — accept or except? "The school ___ addressed the students" — principal or principle?

---

## 🧠 Mnemonic: **"BLANK"**

- **B** — Before options, predict your own word
- **L** — Look for the clue word (time, subject, pair)
- **A** — Agreement check (subject-verb, tense)
- **N** — Name the family (tense / preposition / article / vocab)
- **K** — Kill the wrong options with a reason

## 📋 Summary Table

| Concept | Key Rule | Quick Example |
|---|---|---|
| since / for | since + point, for + duration | since Monday, for two days |
| Habits | simple present | She goes daily |
| neither/either/each | singular verb | Each boy has |
| Fixed prepositions | memorise as pairs | good at, depend on |
| a / an | sound, not spelling | an hour, a university |
| Confusing pairs | meaning decides | advice (noun) / advise (verb) |
