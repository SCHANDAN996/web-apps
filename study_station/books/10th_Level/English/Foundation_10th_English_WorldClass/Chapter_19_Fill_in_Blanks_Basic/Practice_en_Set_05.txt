101. The committee ______ divided in their opinion about the new policy.
(a) was (b) is (c) were (d) has been
Answer: (c)
Solution: The tempting wrong choice is "was" because "committee" looks singular, but the phrase "divided in their opinion" shows the members are acting separately, so a plural verb is needed. When a collective noun shows division among members, it takes a plural verb, so the answer is "were".

102. Neither the teacher nor the students ______ present in the hall.
(a) was (b) is (c) were (d) has been
Answer: (c)
Solution: Many learners pick "was" because "students" feels far away, but with "neither...nor" the verb agrees with the nearer subject, which is "students" (plural). Singular "is" and "has been" also break this proximity rule, so the answer is "were".

103. She has been working in this office ______ 2015.
(a) since (b) from (c) for (d) by
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is "for", which is used with a period of time like "for ten years", not with a point of time. "Since" is used with a fixed starting point such as a year, so the answer is "since".

104. He is one of the best players who ______ ever played for India.
(a) have (b) has (c) was (d) is
Answer: (a)
Solution: The tempting wrong choice is "has" because "one" sounds singular, but the relative pronoun "who" refers to "players" (plural), not to "one". After "one of the + plural noun + who", the verb is plural, so the answer is "have".

106. I ______ this book twice, so I know the story well.
(a) read (b) have read (c) had read (d) am reading
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is simple past "read", but "twice" with a life experience up to now needs the present perfect. "Had read" needs a past reference point and "am reading" shows an ongoing action, so the answer is "have read".

Solution: The trap is "I had reached" because the past perfect tense is right but the word order is wrong; after "hardly" at the start, inversion is compulsory. "Did I reach" uses the wrong tense for the earlier action, so the answer is "had I reached".

Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is "to steal" because many verbs take the infinitive, but "deny" is always followed by a gerund (verb + ing), never an infinitive. Bare "steal" and "stolen" are grammatically impossible here, so the answer is "stealing".
