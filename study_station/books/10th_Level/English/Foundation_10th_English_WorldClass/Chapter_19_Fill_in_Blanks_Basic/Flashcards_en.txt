Card 1
Front: What is the first step in solving any Fill in the Blanks question?
Back: Read the whole sentence first and identify the clue words (conjunctions, tone words like "although", "because", "despite") before looking at the options.

Card 2
Front: What does the word "although" at the start of a sentence tell you about the blank?
Back: It signals contrast — the blank must contain a word opposite in tone to the rest of the sentence. Example: "Although he was tired, he worked with great ___. (energy)"

Card 3
Front: MNEMONIC — What does "FANBOYS" stand for?
Back: The seven coordinating conjunctions: For, And, Nor, But, Or, Yet, So. They join two equal clauses and often decide the tone of the blank.

Card 4
Front: Fill in the blank: "She is good ___ mathematics." (at / in / for / with)
Back: "at" — the fixed preposition with "good" is "at". "Good at" = skilled in something.

Card 5
Front: EXAMINER'S TRAP — "He has been working here ___ 2015." Should you use "since" or "for"?
Back: "since" — use "since" with a point of time (2015, Monday, January) and "for" with a duration (two years, a long time). Examiners swap these to trap you.

Card 6
Front: What is a "collocation" and why does it matter in Fill in the Blanks?
Back: A collocation is a natural word partnership, e.g., "make a mistake" (not "do a mistake"), "heavy rain" (not "strong rain"). Most basic blanks test collocations, not dictionary meanings.

Card 7
Front: Fill in the blank: "The teacher asked the students to ___ attention." (pay / give / make / take)
Back: "pay" — the correct collocation is "pay attention". "Give attention" sounds possible but is not standard English usage.

Card 8
Front: When should you eliminate an option immediately without checking grammar?
Back: When the word does not fit the tone of the sentence — e.g., a negative word in a clearly positive sentence ("He worked hard and ___ succeeded" cannot take "barely").

Card 9
Front: MNEMONIC — How do you remember the difference between "affect" and "effect"?
Back: RAVEN: Remember Affect = Verb, Effect = Noun. "The news affected him" (verb) / "The effect was visible" (noun).

Card 10
Front: Fill in the blank: "Neither of the two boys ___ present." (were / was / are / have been)
Back: "was" — "neither of" takes a singular verb. The plural noun "boys" right before the blank is an intervening-phrase trap.

Card 11
Front: EXAMINER'S TRAP — Why is "He is more taller than me" wrong even though it sounds natural to many Hindi speakers?
Back: Double comparative error — "taller" already shows comparison, so "more" is redundant. Use either "taller than" or "more tall" (rare); never both together.

Card 12
Front: What is the correct preposition: "He died ___ hunger." (of / from / by / with)
Back: "of" — "die of" is used for disease, hunger, thirst; "die from" is used for injuries or external causes.

Card 13
Front: Fill in the blank: "I have ___ finished my homework." (yet / already / since / ever)
Back: "already" — used in positive sentences to show completion before expectation. "Yet" is used in negatives and questions.

Card 14
Front: What does the word "but" in the middle of a sentence tell you about the blank?
Back: It signals contrast — the second half opposes the first. Example: "He is rich ___ not generous." The blank needs a contrasting connector/idea.

Card 15
Front: Fill in the blank: "One of my friends ___ a doctor." (are / is / were / have been)
Back: "is" — the subject is "One" (singular), not "friends". "One of + plural noun" always takes a singular verb.

Card 16
Front: What is the difference between "few" and "a few"?
Back: "Few" = almost none (negative sense): "Few students passed" (hardly any). "A few" = some (positive sense): "A few students passed" (at least some did).

Card 17
Front: Fill in the blank: "She has been ill ___ Monday." (since / for / from / at)
Back: "since" — Monday is a point of time. Use "for" only with a duration (for two days).

Card 18
Front: What should you do when two options both look grammatically correct?
Back: Check the collocation and the tone. Pick the word that forms the natural English partnership with the surrounding words (e.g., "commit a crime", not "do a crime").

Card 19
Front: Fill in the blank: "The committee ___ divided in its opinion." (was / were / are / have)
Back: "was" — when a collective noun's members act separately (divided opinion), traditional exam English still prefers singular with "its"; the key trap is matching the pronoun "its" (singular), so "was" is correct.

Card 20
Front: What is the 15/45-second rule for Fill in the Blanks?
Back: Fact-based blanks (fixed prepositions, collocations, subject-verb agreement) should be solved in about 15 seconds. Tone/inference-based blanks (where you must judge positive/negative or contrast) may take up to 45 seconds — never more.
