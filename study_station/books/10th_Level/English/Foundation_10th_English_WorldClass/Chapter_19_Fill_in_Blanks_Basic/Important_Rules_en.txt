# 📋 Important Grammar Rules & Tips — Fill in the Blanks (Basic)

## (a) 12 High-Yield Rules

| # | Rule | Example | Examiner's Trap |
|---|------|---------|-----------------|
| 1 | Singular subject takes singular verb; plural subject takes plural verb. | The list of items **is** on the table. | Intervening phrase: "The list of items **are**…" — the verb agrees with *list*, not *items*. |
| 2 | "Each / Every / Everyone / Somebody" take a singular verb. | Each of the boys **has** a pen. | "Each of the boys **have**…" sounds right but is wrong. |
| 3 | Two subjects joined by "and" take a plural verb. | Ram and Shyam **are** friends. | But "Bread and butter **is** my breakfast" (one idea = singular). |
| 4 | With "either…or / neither…nor", the verb agrees with the nearer subject. | Neither the teacher nor the students **were** present. | "Neither the students nor the teacher **were**…" — wrong; nearer subject is *teacher*. |
| 5 | Uncountable nouns (news, information, furniture, advice) take singular verbs. | The news **is** good. | "The news **are**…" — *news* looks plural but is singular. |
| 6 | After "did / does / do", use the base form (V1) of the verb. | He did not **go** there. | "He did not **went**…" — double past tense is wrong. |
| 7 | Use "fewer" for countable nouns, "less" for uncountable nouns. | **Fewer** students, **less** water. | "Less students" is a classic trap in exams. |
| 8 | "Between" for two, "among" for more than two. | Distribute the sweets **among** the children. | "Between the three brothers" — wrong; use *among*. |
| 9 | Use "since" for a point of time, "for" for a period of time. | He has lived here **since** 2015 / **for** ten years. | "Since ten years" — wrong; *since* needs a point, not a duration. |
| 10 | Adjectives (not adverbs) follow verbs like look, seem, feel, taste, smell. | The soup tastes **good**. | "The soup tastes **well**" — *well* is an adverb; here we describe the soup. |
| 11 | "One of the + plural noun + singular verb." | One of my friends **is** a doctor. | "One of my friend **is**…" — noun must be plural, verb singular. |
| 12 | Use "much" with uncountable, "many" with countable nouns. | **Much** time, **many** books. | "Much people" — wrong; people is countable. |

## (b) Time Management — The 15/45-Second Rule

| Type | What it looks like | Time | Strategy |
|------|-------------------|------|----------|
| **Fact-based (15 sec)** | Direct grammar rule: subject-verb agreement, article, preposition, tense form. The blank can be filled by recalling one rule. | 15 seconds | Read the sentence once, spot the subject/trigger word, apply the rule, mark the answer. Do not re-read all options. |
| **Inference-based (45 sec)** | Vocabulary-in-context, tone of the sentence, connector choice (although/because/despite), phrasal verbs. You must understand the whole sentence's meaning. | 45 seconds | Read the full sentence, predict your own word before looking at options, then match. Check the tone (positive/negative) of the sentence. |

**Rule of thumb:** If the blank tests a *rule*, answer in 15 seconds. If it tests *meaning*, give it 45 seconds. Never spend more than 60 seconds on one blank — mark and move on.

## (c) Morphology — Root, Affix, Example

| Root / Affix | Meaning | Example Word | Meaning of Word |
|--------------|---------|--------------|-----------------|
| port (Latin) | carry | transport | carry across |
| dict (Latin) | say | predict | say before |
| spect (Latin) | see, look | inspect | look into |
| scrib/script (Latin) | write | describe | write about |
| tele (Greek) | far | telephone | sound from far |
| bio (Greek) | life | biology | study of life |
| un- (prefix) | not | unhappy | not happy |
| re- (prefix) | again | rewrite | write again |
| -ful (suffix) | full of | hopeful | full of hope |
| -less (suffix) | without | careless | without care |

**Why this matters for blanks:** If you know *mal-* means "bad" (malfunction, malnutrition), you can reject a positive word in a negative sentence even if you have never seen the word before.
