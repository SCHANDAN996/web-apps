1. Choose the correct improvement: He do not like tea.
(a) He does not like tea. (b) He do not likes tea. (c) He not do like tea. (d) No improvement
Answer: (a)
Solution: With "he" (third person singular), the auxiliary must be "does", not "do". The tempting error is keeping "do" because it sounds fine in speech, but grammar demands subject-verb agreement, so the answer is "He does not like tea."
Source: NCERT Class 8 English Grammar

2. Choose the correct improvement: She is more taller than her sister.
(a) She is taller than her sister. (b) No improvement (c) She is more tall than her sister. (d) She is most taller than her sister.
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Taller" is already comparative, so adding "more" makes a double comparative — a classic examiner's trap. "Most taller" is also wrong, so the answer is "She is taller than her sister."

3. Choose the correct improvement: I have been living here since five years.
(a) since five years ago (b) from five years (c) for five years (d) No improvement
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Since" is used with a point of time (since 2019), while a period of time takes "for". "From five years" is never used with the present perfect continuous, so the answer is "for five years."

4. Choose the correct improvement: The news are very shocking.
(a) The news were very shocking. (b) The news is very shocking. (c) The news have very shocking. (d) No improvement
Answer: (b)
Solution: "News" looks plural but is an uncountable noun and always takes a singular verb — a sound-right trap because "are" feels natural after an -s word. Hence the answer is "The news is very shocking."

5. Choose the correct improvement: He is senior than me.
(a) senior to me (b) senior from me (c) senior of me (d) No improvement
Answer: (a)
Solution: Latin comparatives like senior, junior, superior and inferior take "to", never "than". The trap is "than", which fits ordinary comparatives, so the answer is "senior to me."

Answer: (a)
Solution: After "one of", the noun must be plural (friends) but the verb stays singular (lives). The option with "friends live" fixes the noun but breaks the verb, so the answer is "One of my friends lives in Delhi."

Answer: (d)
Solution: In polite English the speaker comes last, and as subject we need "I", not "me". "Me and Ram" is a common spoken error, so the answer is "Ram and I went."

9. Choose the correct improvement: He did not came to school yesterday.
(a) did not comes (b) No improvement (c) did not coming (d) did not come
Answer: (d)
Solution: After "did/did not", the main verb returns to its base form; the past tense is already carried by "did". "Came" after "did" is a double-past trap, so the answer is "did not come."

Answer: (a)
Solution: "Each" is singular, so the verb must be "has"; the plural word "boys" in between is an intervening-phrase trap. Hence the answer is "Each of the boys has a pen."

21. Choose the correct improvement: Scarcely he had entered the room when the phone rang.
(a) No improvement (b) Scarcely he had entered the room than the phone rang. (c) Scarcely had he entered the room when the phone rang. (d) Scarcely had he entered the room then the phone rang.
Answer: (c)
Solution: When a negative adverb like "scarcely" begins a sentence, inversion is required — "had he entered". The trap is fixing "when" but missing the inversion, so the answer is "Scarcely had he entered the room when the phone rang."
