# 🪄 Short Tricks + Skip Strategy — Sentence Improvement (Basic)

## Memory Boxes (Mnemonics)

**Box 1 — SVA: "The Verb Loves the Real Subject"**
> Cross out everything between the subject and the verb (with, along with, as well as, together with, of, in).
> *The captain, along with his men, **was** (not were) rescued.*
> Trick: **"Phrases are passengers — the driver (subject) alone decides the verb."**

**Box 2 — FANBOYS (Coordinating Conjunctions)**
> **F**or, **A**nd, **N**or, **B**ut, **O**r, **Y**et, **S**o
> Two complete sentences joined? You need a FANBOYS word + comma, or a semicolon.

**Box 3 — "Neither–Nor / Either–Or: Verb Follows the NEAR Subject"**
> *Neither the teacher nor the **students were** present.*
> Trick: **"Proximity Rule"** — the verb agrees with whichever subject is standing closest.

**Box 4 — Collective Noun Test: "One Body or Many Minds?"**
> Team acting together → singular (*The team **wins**.*)
> Members acting separately → plural (*The team **are** changing their jerseys.*)
> Trick: **"United = it; Divided = they."**

**Box 5 — Tense Time-Markers**
> **Since + point of time** (since 2010, since Monday) → Perfect/Perfect Continuous
> **For + duration** (for two years) → Perfect/Perfect Continuous
> **Yesterday / ago / last** → Simple Past (never Present Perfect!)
> Trick: **"Ago = Past, no has/have."**

**Box 6 — "One of the + Plural Noun + Singular Verb"**
> *One of my **friends is** (not are) coming.*
> Trap: the plural noun sits next to the verb to fool you. Remember: **"ONE is the boss."**

**Box 7 — Each / Every / Everyone → Singular**
> **"E-words are lonely words"** — Each, Every, Everyone, Everybody, Everything always take a singular verb.
> *Everyone **is** (not are) ready.*

**Box 8 — No sooner…than / Hardly…when / Scarcely…when**
> Fixed pairs — never mix them!
> *No sooner had he left **than** it rained.* (❌ No sooner…when)
> Trick: **"Sooner–Than rhyme; Hardly–When chime."**

**Box 9 — Comparisons Need Equals: "Apples with Apples"**
> *The climate of Delhi is hotter than **that of** Mumbai.* (❌ than Mumbai)
> Trick: **"Compare things, not places — use 'that of' / 'those of'."**

**Box 10 — Sound-Right Traps (What your ear accepts, grammar rejects)**
> ❌ He is more superior → ✅ He is superior (superior/inferior/senior/junior + **to**, no "more")
> ❌ Return back → ✅ Return
> ❌ Revert back → ✅ Revert
> ❌ Enter into the room → ✅ Enter the room
> Trick: **"Latin comparatives never take 'more'; 'back' words never take 'back'."**

**Box 11 — If-Clause Formula**
> If + Past Simple → would + V1 (*If I **were** a bird, I **would fly**.*)
> Trick: **"Were for all in dreams"** — with wish/hypothetical, use *were* even with I/he/she.

**Box 12 — Order of Adjectives: "OSASCOMP"**
> **O**pinion – **S**ize – **A**ge – **S**hape – **C**olour – **O**rigin – **M**aterial – **P**urpose
> *a beautiful small old round black Indian wooden table*
> Trick: **"OSASCOMP — Opinion Speaks, Age & Size Come, Origin & Material Protect."**

**Box 13 — Two Subjects Joined by "and" → Plural… Unless One Idea**
> *Bread and butter **is** my breakfast.* (one dish)
> *Bread and butter **are** on the table.* (two items)
> Trick: **"One plate, one verb."**

**Box 14 — Preposition Glue (Fixed Pairs)**
> good **at**, interested **in**, afraid **of**, depend **on**, listen **to**, married **to**, angry **with** (person) / **at** (thing)
> Trick: **"Prepositions are glued — never swap the glue."**

**Box 15 — The 3-Second Substitution Test**
> Confused between two options? Replace the phrase with a simpler word and read aloud:
> *He (is/are) coming* → say "He comes" — your ear confirms singular.
> Trick: **"Shrink the sentence, hear the answer."**

## ⏭️ Skip Strategy (When to Move On)

**Tip 1 — The 45-Second Ceiling**
If you've read the sentence twice and both remaining options "sound fine," mark your first instinct and move on. In Sentence Improvement, **first instinct beats over-thinking** — extra time rarely changes the answer; it only steals time from Reading Comprehension.

**Tip 2 — Skip the "No Improvement" Doubt Spiral**
If you've checked the underlined part against SVA, tense, and preposition rules and found **no error**, choose "No improvement" confidently and skip ahead. Don't invent an error that isn't there — examiners plant correct sentences precisely to punish over-thinkers.

**Tip 3 — Long Sentence? Chop, Don't Skip**
Never skip a question just because the sentence is long. Chop it: find subject → find verb → check agreement → check tense marker. 90% of long-sentence questions test only these two things. But if a question needs knowledge of an idiom you've never seen, **guess-and-go** — idioms can't be derived, so don't burn 45 seconds on them.

**Tip 4 — Two-Pass Rule**
Pass 1: Solve all Fact-based questions (SVA, tense markers, fixed prepositions) — 15 seconds each.
Pass 2: Return to Inference-based ones (tone, best phrasing, parallel structure) — up to 45 seconds each.
Never let a Pass-2 question block a Pass-1 question.
