# 📋 Important Grammar Rules & Tips — Sentence Improvement (Basic)

## (a) 12 High-Yield Rules

| # | Rule | Example | Examiner's Trap |
|---|------|---------|-----------------|
| 1 | **Subject-Verb Agreement:** The verb agrees with the subject, not with the words in between. | The quality of the mangoes **was** good. (not "were") | Intervening phrase trap: "The box of chocolates **is/are** on the table." → *is* (subject = box). |
| 2 | **Each / Every / Everyone / Everybody** take a singular verb. | Each of the boys **has** a pen. | "Each of the students **have**…" ❌ → *has*. |
| 3 | **Two subjects joined by 'and'** take a plural verb — unless they mean one thing/person. | Ram and Shyam **are** friends. BUT Bread and butter **is** my breakfast. | "The poet and philosopher **are** dead" (one person) → *is* dead. |
| 4 | **Either…or / Neither…nor:** verb agrees with the **nearer** subject. | Neither the teacher nor the students **were** present. | "Neither the boys nor Ram **were**…" ❌ → *was* (Ram is nearer). |
| 5 | **Collective nouns** (team, committee, jury, family) take a singular verb when acting as one unit. | The committee **has** taken its decision. | Sound-right trap: "The jury **has** divided in their opinions" ❌ → when divided, use plural: *have* divided. |
| 6 | **Uncountable nouns** (news, information, furniture, advice, luggage, scenery) are always singular. | The news **is** good. | "These furnitures are new" ❌ → *This furniture is* new. |
| 7 | **Since vs For:** *since* + point of time; *for* + period of time. | I have lived here **since** 2015 / **for** nine years. | "I know him since five years" ❌ → *for* five years. |
| 8 | **Present Perfect** is never used with a definite past time (yesterday, ago, last year). | I **saw** him yesterday. (not "have seen") | "I have met him last Monday" ❌ → *met*. |
| 9 | **Comparative degree** needs *than*; don't compare a thing with itself — use "any other". | Kanpur is bigger than **any other** city in the region. | "He is more smarter than me" ❌ → double comparative; *smarter* alone. |
| 10 | **One of the + plural noun + singular verb.** | One of my friends **has** gone to Delhi. | "One of the boy is…" ❌ → *boys* (plural noun after "one of the"). |
| 11 | **No sooner…than / Hardly…when / Scarcely…when** — fixed pairs, with past perfect + inversion. | No sooner **had** she entered **than** it rained. | "No sooner had she entered **when**…" ❌ → *than*. |
| 12 | **Gerund after prepositions & certain verbs** (enjoy, avoid, mind, suggest, look forward to). | I look forward to **meeting** you. | "I am looking forward to **meet** you" ❌ → here *to* is a preposition, so *meeting*. |

## (b) Time Management — The 15/45-Second Rule

| Type | What it looks like | Time | Strategy |
|------|-------------------|------|----------|
| **Fact-based (15 sec)** | Direct rule test: subject-verb agreement, since/for, one of the…, fixed pairs (no sooner…than), uncountable nouns. | 15 sec | Spot the keyword (each, one of, since, news), recall the rule, mark, move on. Never re-read. |
| **Inference-based (45 sec)** | Two options look grammatically fine; you must check meaning, tense logic, or which option is *most* natural. Sentence with an intervening phrase hiding the real subject. | 45 sec | Strip the sentence to subject + verb. Read each option inside the full sentence once. Eliminate two, compare the remaining two on meaning. |

**Rule of thumb:** If you know the rule the moment you see the keyword → 15 seconds. If you must "feel" which option sounds right → give it 45 seconds, and if still unsure, mark and skip.

## (c) Morphology — Root, Affix, Example

| Root / Affix | Meaning | Example Word | Meaning of Word |
|--------------|---------|--------------|-----------------|
| **spect** (Latin) | to look | inspect | to look into |
| **port** (Latin) | to carry | transport | carry across |
| **dict** (Latin) | to say | predict | to say before |
| **un-** (prefix) | not | unhappy | not happy |
| **re-** (prefix) | again | rewrite | write again |
| **pre-** (prefix) | before | preview | view before |
| **-ful** (suffix) | full of | hopeful | full of hope |
| **-less** (suffix) | without | careless | without care |
| **-ness** (suffix) | state/quality | kindness | state of being kind |
| **-ly** (suffix) | in a manner | quickly | in a quick manner |

**Why this matters for Sentence Improvement:** Many options differ only by word form — *careless / carelessly / carelessness*. Knowing that adverbs (-ly) modify verbs and adjectives modify nouns lets you eliminate wrong forms in seconds: "She sings **beautiful**" ❌ → "She sings **beautifully**" ✔.
