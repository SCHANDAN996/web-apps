# 📊 PYQ Analysis — Sentence Improvement (Basic)

## How Exams Test This Chapter

Sentence Improvement is one of the most reliable scoring areas in SSC (CGL, CHSL, MTS, GD), Banking (IBPS, SBI), and Railway (NTPC, Group D) exams. The pattern is stable: a sentence is given with an underlined (or bold) part, and you must choose the option that improves it — with "No improvement" as one of the choices.

**What examiners repeatedly ask (pattern analysis in words, not numbers):**

- **Subject–Verb Agreement** — the single most repeated sub-topic. Traps: intervening phrases ("The quality of apples *were*…"), collective nouns, "each/everyone/none", "one of the + plural noun + singular verb".
- **Tense consistency** — mixing past and present in one sentence; wrong tense after "since/for", "hardly…when", "no sooner…than".
- **Wrong preposition** — "discuss about", "comprise of", "married with", "angry on".
- **Article errors** — missing "the" before superlatives and unique nouns.
- **Pronoun errors** — "me and my friend went", "between you and I".
- **Redundancy** — "return back", "repeat again", "free gift".
- **Comparative/superlative misuse** — "more better", "most unique".
- **Conditional sentences** — "If it will rain…" type errors.

**Examiner's favourite traps (learn to name them):**

1. **Intervening Phrase Trap** — extra words between subject and verb hide the real subject.
2. **Sound-Right Trap** — the wrong option *sounds* natural because we hear it in spoken English ("What you are doing?").
3. **Collective Noun Trap** — "team/committee/family" treated as plural when acting as one unit.
4. **Over-thinking Trap** — the sentence is already correct; the answer is "No improvement", but doubt pushes students to change it.

## Top 10 High-Yield Questions (with Solutions)

**Q1.** The quality of the mangoes **were** not good.
(a) was (b) are (c) have been (d) No improvement
**Answer: (a)**
**Solution:** The subject is "quality" (singular), not "mangoes". "Of the mangoes" is an intervening phrase. So: *was*.
**Trap type:** Intervening Phrase.
**Source:** PYQ-style (SSC pattern)

**Q2.** One of my friends **are** going to Delhi tomorrow.
(a) is (b) were (c) have been (d) No improvement
**Answer: (a)**
**Solution:** "One of + plural noun" takes a **singular** verb — the real subject is "One".
**Trap type:** Intervening Phrase.
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q3.** He **is working** in this factory since 2015.
(a) has been working (b) was working (c) worked (d) No improvement
**Answer: (a)**
**Solution:** "Since + point of time" demands present perfect continuous: *has been working*.
**Trap type:** Sound-Right (spoken English accepts "is working since", grammar does not).
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q4.** The teacher, along with the students, **have** gone on a picnic.
(a) has (b) were (c) are (d) No improvement
**Answer: (a)**
**Solution:** With "along with / as well as / together with", the verb agrees with the **first** subject ("teacher" — singular).
**Trap type:** Intervening Phrase.
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q5.** I **have seen** him yesterday at the market.
(a) saw (b) had seen (c) see (d) No improvement
**Answer: (a)**
**Solution:** Definite past time words (yesterday, last week, ago) never take present perfect. Use simple past: *saw*.
**Trap type:** Sound-Right.
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q6.** She is **more taller** than her sister.
(a) taller (b) most taller (c) much more taller (d) No improvement
**Answer: (a)**
**Solution:** Double comparative is wrong. "Taller" alone is correct; never "more taller".
**Trap type:** Sound-Right.
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q7.** Hardly had he reached the station **than** the train left.
(a) when (b) then (c) that (d) No improvement
**Answer: (a)**
**Solution:** Fixed pair: "Hardly/Scarcely … **when**"; "No sooner … **than**". Do not mix the pairs.
**Trap type:** Sound-Right (the two pairs get swapped).
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q8.** Each of the boys **have** done his homework.
(a) has (b) have been (c) are (d) No improvement
**Answer: (a)**
**Solution:** "Each of" always takes a singular verb: *has*.
**Trap type:** Intervening Phrase.
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q9.** He **returned back** the book to the library.
(a) returned (b) returned again (c) has returned back (d) No improvement
**Answer: (a)**
**Solution:** "Return" already means "give back" — "back" is redundant. Same family: repeat again, enter into, free gift.
**Trap type:** Sound-Right.
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q10.** Bread and butter **are** his daily breakfast.
(a) is (b) were (c) have been (d) No improvement
**Answer: (a)**
**Solution:** Two nouns joined by "and" that form **one idea/dish** take a singular verb.
**Trap type:** Over-thinking + Collective idea.
**Source:** PYQ-style

## Applying the 15/45-Second Rule

**15-second question (Fact-based): Q6 — "more taller"**
The moment you see a double comparative, the rule fires instantly: *more + -er* is impossible. No need to read all options deeply. Spot → recall rule → mark (a). Target: **15 seconds**.

**45-second question (Inference-based): Q4 — "along with the students"**
Here you must (1) identify the real subject, (2) recall the "along with" exception, (3) reject the tempting plural verb "have" that sits next to "students". This needs two reasoning steps, so allow yourself **45 seconds** — but do not exceed it; mark and move.

**Strategy note:** In Sentence Improvement, roughly two-thirds of questions are fact/rule-based (15 sec) and one-third need careful subject-hunting or meaning checks (45 sec). If a question needs more than 45 seconds, mark your best guess, flag it, and move on — the next question may be a 10-second "No improvement" gift.
