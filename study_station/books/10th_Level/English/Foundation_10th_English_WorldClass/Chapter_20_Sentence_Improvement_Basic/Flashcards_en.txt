Card 1
Front: What is "Sentence Improvement" in competitive exams?
Back: A question type where part of a sentence is underlined and you must choose the option that makes it grammatically correct. If the sentence is already correct, choose "No improvement."

Card 2
Front: Fill in the blank: "Neither of the two boys ___ done his homework."
Back: "has" — After "neither of / either of / each of," the verb is always singular.

Card 3
Front: 🧠 Mnemonic: How do you remember verbs that are always followed by a gerund (V-ing)?
Back: "MILD SAFE" — Mind, Imagine, Miss, Deny, Suggest, Avoid, Finish, Enjoy. Example: "I enjoy reading" (not "to read"). Note: "like" is NOT in this list — it can take both gerund and infinitive ("I like reading / I like to read").

Card 4
Front: Correct the sentence: "He is more taller than his brother."
Back: "He is taller than his brother." — Never use "more" with a comparative adjective ending in -er (double comparative error).

Card 5
Front: ⚠️ Examiner's Trap: "The news ___ very shocking." (is/are)
Back: "is" — "News" looks plural but is an uncountable noun and takes a singular verb. Same with: physics, mathematics, politics.

Card 6
Front: When do we use "fewer" and when "less"?
Back: "Fewer" for countable nouns (fewer students); "less" for uncountable nouns (less water). Trap: "less people" is wrong — say "fewer people."

Card 7
Front: Fill in the blank: "One of my friends ___ a doctor."
Back: "is" — After "one of + plural noun," the verb is singular because the subject is "one."

Card 8
Front: Correct the sentence: "I have seen him yesterday."
Back: "I saw him yesterday." — With definite past time words (yesterday, last year, ago), use Simple Past, not Present Perfect.

Card 9
Front: 🧠 Mnemonic: How do you remember adjectives that take "to" (not "than") for comparison?
Back: "SIPJ" — Superior, Inferior, Prior, Junior (also Senior, Prefer). Example: "He is senior to me" (not "senior than me").

Card 10
Front: ⚠️ Examiner's Trap: "The teacher, along with his students, ___ gone." (has/have)
Back: "has" — Phrases like "along with, together with, as well as" are intervening phrases; the verb agrees with the first subject only.

Card 11
Front: What is the correct order of adjectives before a noun?
Back: Opinion–Size–Age–Shape–Colour–Origin–Material–Purpose. Example: "a beautiful small old round red Indian wooden table."

Card 12
Front: Correct the sentence: "He is working here since 2015."
Back: "He has been working here since 2015." — "Since/for" with an action continuing till now needs Present Perfect (Continuous).

Card 13
Front: "Each of the girls ___ brought her book." — which verb?
Back: "has" — "Each" is always singular, so singular verb + singular pronoun (her).

Card 14
Front: Correct the sentence: "What you are doing?"
Back: "What are you doing?" — In questions, the helping verb comes before the subject (inversion). Hindi word order misleads here.

Card 15
Front: When do we use "between" and when "among"?
Back: "Between" for two persons/things; "among" for more than two. Example: "between the two brothers," "among the students."

Card 16
Front: Fill in the blank: "No sooner had he arrived ___ it started raining."
Back: "than" — "No sooner" pairs with "than"; "Hardly/Scarcely" pairs with "when." Examiners swap these to trap you.

Card 17
Front: Correct the sentence: "He is one of the best player in the team."
Back: "He is one of the best players in the team." — After "one of the + superlative," the noun must be plural.

Card 18
Front: What is the rule for "used to" vs "is used to"?
Back: "Used to + V1" = past habit ("I used to play cricket"). "Is used to + V-ing" = accustomed to ("I am used to waking early").

Card 19
Front: Correct the sentence: "The furnitures in this room are new."
Back: "The furniture in this room is new." — "Furniture" is uncountable; no plural form, singular verb. Same family: luggage, information, advice.

Card 20
Front: ⚠️ Examiner's Trap: When should you choose "No improvement"?
Back: Only when the underlined part is fully correct in grammar, usage, and idiom. Don't pick it out of fear — check subject-verb agreement, tense, and prepositions first; if all are right, "No improvement" is the answer.
