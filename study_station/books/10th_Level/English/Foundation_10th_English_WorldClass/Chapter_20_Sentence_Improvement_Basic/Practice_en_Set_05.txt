101. Choose the correct improvement: "He is more wiser than his brother."
(a) more wise (b) wiser (c) most wise (d) No improvement
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Wiser" is already a comparative adjective, so adding "more" creates a double comparative — the tempting option "more wise" keeps the same error. Use "wiser" alone, so the answer is wiser.
Source: PYQ-style

102. Choose the correct improvement: "One of my friend has gone to Delhi."
(a) One of my friends (b) One from my friend (c) One of friend (d) No improvement
Answer: (a)
Solution: After "one of", the noun must be plural ("friends") even though the verb stays singular. "One of friend" drops the plural marker, so the answer is One of my friends.
Source: PYQ-style

103. Choose the correct improvement: "I have been living in this city since five years."
(a) from five years (b) since five years ago (c) for five years (d) No improvement
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Since" is used with a point of time (since 2019), while a period of time takes "for". "From five years" is unidiomatic, so the answer is for five years.
Source: NCERT-based grammar rule

104. Choose the correct improvement: "She denied to help me in the exam."
(a) denied helping (b) denied for helping (c) denied to helping (d) No improvement
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Deny" is always followed by a gerund, never an infinitive — "denied helping" is the only grammatically correct form. "To help" after "deny" is the trap, so the answer is denied helping.
Source: PYQ-style

105. Choose the correct improvement: "Unless you do not work hard, you will fail."
(a) If you do not work hard not (b) Until you do not work hard (c) Unless you work hard (d) No improvement
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Unless" already carries a negative meaning, so adding "not" makes a double negative — the trap in "No improvement". Remove "not", so the answer is Unless you work hard.
Source: PYQ-style

106. Choose the correct improvement: "He is working here since 2015."
(a) is working here from 2015 (b) was working here since 2015 (c) has been working here since 2015 (d) No improvement
Answer: (c)
Solution: An action that began in the past and continues now needs the present perfect continuous with "since". The simple present "is working" cannot take "since 2015", so the answer is has been working here since 2015.
Source: PYQ-style

107. Choose the correct improvement: "The teacher asked the students to not make noise."
(a) to not to make noise (b) not to make noise (c) to make not noise (d) No improvement
Answer: (b)
Solution: The negative of an infinitive is formed by placing "not" before "to" — "not to make". "To not make" splits the infinitive awkwardly in standard exam usage, so the answer is not to make noise.
Source: NCERT-based grammar rule

108. Choose the correct improvement: "No sooner did he reach the station when the train left."
(a) then the train left (b) No improvement (c) but the train left (d) than the train left
Answer: (d)
Solution: "No sooner" is always paired with "than", never "when" — "when" is the tempting trap. The correct correlative pair is "No sooner…than", so the answer is than the train left.
Source: PYQ-style

109. Choose the correct improvement: "He is one of the best player in the team."
(a) No improvement (b) one of the best players (c) one of the best playing (d) one of best player
Answer: (b)
Solution: After "one of the + superlative", the noun must be plural — "players". Keeping "player" singular is the common trap, so the answer is one of the best players.
Source: PYQ-style

110. Choose the correct improvement: "I prefer tea more than coffee."
(a) tea than coffee (b) No improvement (c) tea to coffee (d) tea over than coffee
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Prefer" takes the preposition "to", not "than" — "more than" doubles the comparison wrongly. The correct form is "prefer tea to coffee", so the answer is tea to coffee.
Source: PYQ-style

111. Choose the correct improvement: "He as well as his brothers were present at the meeting."
(a) were present in (b) was present at (c) are present at (d) No improvement
Answer: (b)
Solution: With "as well as", the verb agrees with the first subject ("He"), so it must be singular — "were" is the trap that agrees with the nearer noun "brothers". The correct verb is "was", so the answer is was present at.
Source: PYQ-style

112. Choose the correct improvement: "The Ganges is one of the longest river in India."
(a) longest rivers in India (b) longest river of India (c) longer river in India (d) No improvement
Answer: (a)
Solution: "One of the + superlative" must be followed by a plural noun — "rivers". The singular "river" is the error, so the answer is longest rivers in India.
Source: PYQ-style

113. Choose the correct improvement: "Hardly had he entered the room than the phone rang."
(a) then the phone rang (b) as the phone rang (c) No improvement (d) when the phone rang
Answer: (d)
Solution: "Hardly/Scarcely" pairs with "when", while "No sooner" pairs with "than" — mixing them is the classic trap. Here the correct partner is "when", so the answer is when the phone rang.
Source: PYQ-style

114. Choose the correct improvement: "He told me that why I was late."
(a) that why was I late (b) that why I am late (c) No improvement (d) why I was late
Answer: (d)
Solution: In indirect speech with a wh-word, "that" is not used — "told me why I was late" is correct. Keeping "that why" is redundant, so the answer is why I was late.
Source: NCERT-based grammar rule

115. Choose the correct improvement: "Each of the boys have done his homework."
(a) have done their homework (b) has done their homework (c) No improvement (d) has done his homework
Answer: (d)
Solution: "Each of" takes a singular verb — "has". "Have" is the trap because "boys" sits just before the verb, so the answer is has done his homework.
Source: PYQ-style

116. Choose the correct improvement: "He is more superior to me in rank."
(a) more superior than me (b) superior than me (c) superior to me (d) No improvement
Answer: (c)
Solution: Latin comparatives like "superior" already contain comparison and take "to", never "more" or "than". "More superior" is a double comparative, so the answer is superior to me.
Source: PYQ-style

117. Choose the correct improvement: "The police has arrested the thief."
(a) have arrested the thief (b) is arrested the thief (c) had being arrested the thief (d) No improvement
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Police" is a plural noun in English and always takes a plural verb — "has" is the trap because it sounds like one body. The correct verb is "have", so the answer is have arrested the thief.
Source: PYQ-style

118. Choose the correct improvement: "I and my friend went to the market."
(a) Me and my friend went (b) No improvement (c) My friend and me went (d) My friend and I went
Answer: (d)
Solution: English politeness and grammar place the first person last, and as subject it must be "I", not "me". "I and my friend" violates both, so the answer is My friend and I went.
Source: NCERT-based grammar rule

119. Choose the correct improvement: "He is living in Delhi for the last ten years."
(a) No improvement (b) has been living in Delhi (c) was living in Delhi (d) is being lived in Delhi
Answer: (b)
Solution: "For the last ten years" shows an action continuing up to now, which needs the present perfect continuous. The simple present continuous is the trap, so the answer is has been living in Delhi.
Source: PYQ-style

120. Choose the correct improvement: "Neither of the two boys have passed the exam."
(a) have passed their exam (b) has passed the exam (c) have been passed the exam (d) No improvement
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Neither of" takes a singular verb — "has". "Have" tempts because "boys" is plural and nearest, so the answer is has passed the exam.
Source: PYQ-style

121. Choose the correct improvement: "Scarcely he had reached home when it started raining."
(a) Scarcely had he reached home (b) Scarcely he reached home (c) Scarcely did he reached home (d) No improvement
Answer: (a)
Solution: When a sentence begins with "Scarcely", inversion is required — the auxiliary comes before the subject: "Scarcely had he reached". The normal word order is the trap, so the answer is Scarcely had he reached home.
Source: PYQ-style

122. Choose the correct improvement: "The furniture in this room are very old."
(a) have been very old (b) were very old (c) is very old (d) No improvement
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Furniture" is an uncountable noun and always takes a singular verb — "are" is the trap. The correct verb is "is", so the answer is is very old.
Source: NCERT-based grammar rule

123. Choose the correct improvement: "He prevented me to go there."
(a) prevented me from going (b) prevented me for going (c) prevented me going to (d) No improvement
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Prevent" is followed by "from + gerund" — "prevented me from going". The infinitive "to go" is the common error, so the answer is prevented me from going.
Source: PYQ-style

124. Choose the correct improvement: "It is high time we start the work."
(a) No improvement (b) we will start the work (c) we shall start the work (d) we started the work
Answer: (d)
Solution: After "It is high time", the past simple is used to express urgency even though the meaning is present — "we started". The present tense "start" is the trap, so the answer is we started the work.
Source: PYQ-style

125. Choose the correct improvement: "The principal, along with the teachers, were present in the hall."
(a) was present in the hall (b) were present at the hall (c) are present in the hall (d) No improvement
Answer: (a)
Solution: With "along with", the verb agrees with the first subject ("the principal"), so it must be singular — "were" is the trap set by the nearer plural "teachers". The correct verb is "was", so the answer is was present in the hall.
