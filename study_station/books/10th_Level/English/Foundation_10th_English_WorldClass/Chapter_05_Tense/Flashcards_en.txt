Card 1
Front: What are the three main tenses in English?
Back: Present, Past, and Future. Each has four forms: Simple, Continuous, Perfect, and Perfect Continuous — 12 forms in total.

Card 2
Front: What is the structure of the Present Perfect tense?
Back: Subject + has/have + V3 (past participle). Example: "She has finished her work." Use "has" with he/she/it and "have" with I/we/you/they.

Card 3
Front: When do we use the Present Continuous tense?
Back: For actions happening right now (at the time of speaking) and for fixed future arrangements. Example: "I am reading now." / "We are leaving tomorrow."

Card 4
Front: MNEMONIC — How do you remember the signal words of Present Perfect?
Back: Remember "JAY-RENS": Just, Already, Yet, Recently, Ever, Never, Since. These words almost always signal Present Perfect: "I have just eaten."

Card 5
Front: What is the difference between "since" and "for"?
Back: "Since" is used with a point of time (since Monday, since 2010, since morning). "For" is used with a period of time (for two days, for five years).

Card 6
Front: EXAMINER'S TRAP — "I ___ here since 2015." Should you use "am living" or "have been living"?
Back: "Have been living." With "since/for + time," an action continuing from the past till now takes Present Perfect Continuous, not Present Continuous. This is a very common error among Hindi-speaking learners.

Card 7
Front: Which tense is used for universal truths and habitual actions?
Back: Simple Present. Example: "The sun rises in the east." / "He goes to school daily."

Card 8
Front: What is the structure of the Past Perfect tense, and when is it used?
Back: Subject + had + V3. Used for the earlier of two past actions: "The train had left before I reached the station." (Train left first, I reached later.)

Card 9
Front: In a sentence with two past actions joined by "before" or "after," which action takes Past Perfect?
Back: The earlier action takes Past Perfect (had + V3); the later action takes Simple Past. "The doctor came after the patient had died."

Card 10
Front: What is the structure of the Future Perfect tense?
Back: Subject + will/shall + have + V3. Example: "She will have completed the project by Monday." It shows an action finished before a future point of time.

Card 11
Front: MNEMONIC — How do you quickly identify Past Continuous signal words?
Back: Remember "WAW": While, As, When. "While I was sleeping, the phone rang." These words often point to Past Continuous.

Card 12
Front: EXAMINER'S TRAP — "He is working here for ten years." Is this correct?
Back: No. Correct: "He has been working here for ten years." Hindi speakers often translate "वह दस साल से यहाँ काम कर रहा है" directly into Present Continuous — but with "for/since + time," English demands Perfect Continuous.

Card 13
Front: Which tense follows time clauses with "when, as soon as, until, before" for future meaning?
Back: Simple Present, not Simple Future. "I will call you when he arrives" (not "when he will arrive").

Card 14
Front: What is the difference between Past Perfect and Past Perfect Continuous?
Back: Past Perfect (had + V3) shows a completed earlier action; Past Perfect Continuous (had been + V1-ing) shows an earlier action continuing over time: "He had been waiting for two hours when she arrived."

Card 15
Front: Which verbs are normally NOT used in continuous tenses?
Back: Stative verbs — verbs of mind, emotion, possession, and senses: know, believe, love, hate, own, belong, seem, understand. Say "I know him," not "I am knowing him."

Card 16
Front: What tense is used in a conditional sentence like "If it rains, the match will be cancelled"?
Back: If-clause takes Simple Present; main clause takes Simple Future. Never use "will" in the if-clause: "If it will rain" is wrong.

Card 17
Front: How is the Future Continuous tense formed, and what does it show?
Back: Subject + will/shall + be + V1-ing. It shows an action in progress at a future time: "At 8 pm tomorrow, I will be travelling."

Card 18
Front: What does the Present Perfect Continuous tense express?
Back: An action that started in the past and is still continuing (or just stopped), usually with since/for: "They have been playing since morning."

Card 19
Front: In reported or narrative sentences, why does "Hardly/Scarcely... when" take Past Perfect?
Back: With "Hardly/Scarcely... when" and "No sooner... than," the first action takes Past Perfect: "Hardly had I reached home when it rained."

Card 20
Front: Quick check — "By next year, she ___ her degree." Which form fits?
Back: "Will have completed" (Future Perfect). The phrase "by + future time" is a strong signal for Future Perfect tense.
