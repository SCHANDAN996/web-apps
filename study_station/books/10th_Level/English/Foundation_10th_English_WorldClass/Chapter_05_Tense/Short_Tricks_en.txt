# 🪄 Short Tricks + Skip Strategy — Tense

## Memory Boxes (Mnemonics)

**Box 1 — "SHE SINGS" (Present Simple 's' rule)**
> **S**ingular **H**e/**S**he/**I**t **E**nds with **S** — **S**ubject **I**s **N**ot plural, **G**et **S**!
> He *plays*, She *goes*, It *rains*. Plural subjects never take 's': They *play*.

**Box 2 — "V1-V2-V3 = 1-2-3 Tenses"**
> Present uses **V1** (go), Past uses **V2** (went), Perfect uses **V3 + have/has/had** (has gone).
> Remember: **"1 now, 2 then, 3 with HAVE."**

**Box 3 — "ING = Happening"**
> If you see **-ing**, the action is **happening** right at that time → Continuous tense.
> But -ing never stands alone! It always needs a helper: is/am/are/was/were/will be.
> ❌ He going. ✅ He **is** going.

**Box 4 — "SINCE = Point, FOR = Period"**
> **S**ince = **S**pecific point (since Monday, since 2010, since 5 o'clock).
> **F**or = **F**ull duration (for two days, for five years).
> Trick: **"Since is a dot, For is a line."**

**Box 5 — "YAST — Yet, Already, Since, Today"**
> Signal words for **Present Perfect**: **Y**et, **A**lready, **S**ince, **T**oday/this week, just, never, ever, recently.
> See any of these + have/has + V3 → Present Perfect.

**Box 6 — "Ago = V2, Go!"**
> The word **ago** always pulls the sentence into **Simple Past (V2)**.
> ❌ He has come two days ago. ✅ He **came** two days ago.
> Trick: **"Ago never goes with has/have."**

**Box 7 — "WILL = Tomorrow's Promise"**
> Future markers: tomorrow, next week, soon, in 2030 → **will/shall + V1**.
> No 's', no -ing, no V2 after will. **"After WILL, verb stays still (V1)."**

**Box 8 — "HAD = Past of the Past"**
> Two past actions? The **earlier** one takes **had + V3**, the later one takes V2.
> The train **had left** (earlier) before I **reached** (later) the station.
> Trick: **"First action = HAD, second action = V2."**

**Box 9 — "By the time = Perfect"**
> "By the time", "by 2025", "by tomorrow" → use a **Perfect** tense (had/will have + V3).
> By 2026, she **will have completed** her degree.

**Box 10 — "Stative Verbs Never Dance (-ing)"**
> Verbs of mind, feeling, possession — know, believe, love, hate, own, belong, understand, want — **never take -ing**.
> ❌ I am knowing him. ✅ I **know** him.
> Trick: **"The brain and heart don't dance — no -ing!"**

**Box 11 — "When/While Split"**
> **While** + Past Continuous (long action), **When** + Simple Past (short interruption).
> **While** I **was sleeping**, the phone **rang**.
> Trick: **"While = long line, When = short dot."**

**Box 12 — "Universal Truth = Present Forever"**
> Scientific facts and universal truths stay in **Present Simple**, even inside past sentences.
> The teacher said that the earth **revolves** (not revolved) around the sun.

**Box 13 — "If + V2 = Dream, If + had V3 = Regret"**
> If I **were** a bird… (unreal present/dream)
> If I **had studied**, I would have passed. (past regret)
> Trick: **"V2 dreams of now, Had-V3 cries over the past."**

**Box 14 — "This Morning Test"**
> "This morning/evening" — check if the time is over:
> Finished time → V2: I **met** him this morning. (said in the evening)
> Still running → Present Perfect: I **have met** him this morning. (said in the morning)

## ⏭️ Skip Strategy (Exam Hall Tactics)

**Tip 1 — Skip the "Double Perfect" trap question on first pass.**
Questions mixing Past Perfect + Past Simple with "before/after" (e.g., "The doctor came after the patient ___") eat 45+ seconds because both options *sound* right. Mark it, finish the 15-second fact questions (spot the 's', spot the -ing helper, ago→V2), then return with a fresh eye. Rule on return: **earlier action = had + V3**.

**Tip 2 — If two options differ only by has/have/had, don't skip — it's a 15-second gift.**
Just match the helper to the subject and time: singular+now → has, plural/I/you+now → have, past-of-past → had. These are free marks; never leave them.

**Tip 3 — Skip long "find the error" sentences with stative verbs until the end.**
Sentences hiding errors like "I am loving this movie" inside 3–4 clauses take time to scan. Do the short fill-in-the-blanks first (they average 15 seconds), then hunt the stative-verb and since/for errors in the leftover time. One wrong guess here costs the same as one easy question — protect the easy marks first.
