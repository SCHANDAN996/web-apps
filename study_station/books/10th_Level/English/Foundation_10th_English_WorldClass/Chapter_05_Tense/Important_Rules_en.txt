# 📋 Important Grammar Rules & Tips — Tense

## (a) 12 High-Yield Rules

| # | Rule | Example | Examiner's Trap |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Universal truths, facts and habits take **Simple Present**. | The sun **rises** in the east. | Don't use "is rising" for a permanent fact. |
| 2 | With **since** (point of time) use Present Perfect/Perfect Continuous; with **for** (duration) also Perfect/Perfect Continuous. | He has lived here **since 2015** / **for ten years**. | "Since ten years" ❌ — since takes a point, for takes a period. |
| 3 | In "if" conditional type-1, use **Simple Present** in the if-clause, **will + V1** in the main clause. | If it **rains**, we **will stay** home. | "If it will rain" ❌ — never "will" after if. |
| 4 | After **did / didn't**, always use the **base form (V1)** of the verb. | He didn't **go** to school. | "He didn't went" ❌ — double past is wrong. |
| 5 | Two past actions: the earlier action takes **Past Perfect (had + V3)**, the later one **Simple Past**. | The train **had left** before I **reached** the station. | Using Simple Past for both hides the sequence. |
| 6 | **By / by the time + future point** → Future Perfect (will have + V3). | She **will have finished** the work by 6 pm. | "Will finish by 6 pm" is weaker; exams want "will have finished". |
| 7 | Actions happening **now / around now** take Present Continuous (is/am/are + V-ing). | Look! The children **are playing**. | Stative verbs (know, believe, love) don't take -ing: "I am knowing" ❌. |
| 8 | **Just, already, yet, recently, ever, never** signal Present Perfect (has/have + V3). | I **have just completed** my homework. | "I just have completed" — wrong word order; also don't mix with past time ("yesterday"). |
| 9 | Past Continuous (was/were + V-ing) shows an action **in progress** when another past action interrupted it. | While I **was reading**, the phone **rang**. | "While I read, the phone was ringing" reverses the logic. |
| 10 | With **hardly/scarcely…when** and **no sooner…than**, use Past Perfect in the first clause. | **No sooner had** she entered **than** it rained. | "No sooner she entered" ❌ — inversion (had + subject) is required. |
| 11 | Future time clauses after **when, as soon as, before, until** take Simple Present, not "will". | I will call you when I **reach** Delhi. | "When I will reach" ❌ — a classic trap. |
| 12 | Wish / If only / It's time + **Simple Past** expresses a present unreal desire. | I wish I **were** a bird. / It's time you **went** home. | "I wish I was" is informal; exams prefer **were** for all persons. |

## (b) Time Management — The 15/45-Second Rule

**⏱️ 15-Second Questions (Fact-based — spot the signal word, apply the rule):**
- Fill-in-the-blank with clear time markers: *since, for, yesterday, by 2025, just, already, when, while*.
- Direct rule application: verb after *did/didn't*, after *no sooner/hardly*, in if-clauses.
- Example: "He has been working here ___ 2010." → see "since/for" → answer in seconds.

**⏱️ 45-Second Questions (Inference-based — read the whole sentence, check the timeline):**
- Error-spotting where two clauses must be sequenced (Past Perfect vs Simple Past).
- Sentences with **no time marker** — you must infer the tense from context/meaning.
- Complex conditionals and "wish / if only / it's time" constructions.
- Strategy: identify (1) when each action happens, (2) which happened first, (3) match the rule — then answer.

**Golden advice:** Attempt all 15-second questions first in the exam; mark the 45-second ones and return to them. Never spend more than 45 seconds on a single tense question.

## (c) Morphology — Roots & Affixes in Tense-Related Words

| Root / Affix | Meaning | Example Words |
|---|---|---|
| **chron** (Greek) | time | chronic, chronology, synchronize, anachronism |
| **tempor** (Latin) | time | temporary, contemporary, tempo, extempore |
| **ann / enn** (Latin) | year | annual, anniversary, perennial, biennial |
| **dur** (Latin) | last, hard | duration, durable, endure, during |
| **pre-** (prefix) | before | predict, previous, prevent, prelude |
| **post-** (prefix) | after | postpone, postscript, posthumous |
| **re-** (prefix) | again | repeat, revise, recur, renew |
| **-ed** (suffix) | marks past tense / participle | played, worked, finished |
| **-ing** (suffix) | marks continuous action | running, writing, singing |
| **-en / -n** (suffix) | past participle marker | written, broken, eaten, driven |

**Tip:** Knowing *chron* = time and *tempor* = time helps you guess meanings of unseen vocabulary in the same exam — e.g., "contemporary" = of the same time = modern.
