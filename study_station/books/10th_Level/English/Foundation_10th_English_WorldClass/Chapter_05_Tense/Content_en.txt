## 1. What Is Tense?

**Tense = the form of the verb that shows the TIME of an action.**

The word "tense" comes from the Latin *tempus*, meaning **time**. In English, the verb changes its shape to tell us *when* something happened:

- Ram **plays** cricket. (now / regularly)
- Ram **played** cricket. (earlier, finished)
- Ram **will play** cricket. (later)

English has **3 times** and **4 forms**, giving **12 tenses**:

| Time ↓ / Form → | Indefinite (Simple) | Continuous | Perfect | Perfect Continuous |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Present** | plays | is playing | has played | has been playing |
| **Past** | played | was playing | had played | had been playing |
| **Future** | will play | will be playing | will have played | will have been playing |

**Memory hook:** *3 × 4 = 12.* Three times, four forms — that's the whole map of tense.

## 2. The Four Forms — What Each One Really Means

Don't memorize tables blindly. Understand the **feeling** of each form:

**① Indefinite (Simple)** — a habit, fact, or completed action. No emphasis on duration.
- *She **teaches** in a school.* (her regular job)

**② Continuous** — an action **in progress** at a particular moment.
- *She **is teaching** right now.* (happening at this moment)

**③ Perfect** — an action **completed**, with a connection to a reference point.
- *She **has taught** this lesson.* (finished; result matters now)

**④ Perfect Continuous** — an action that **started earlier and is/was still continuing**, with emphasis on **duration**.
- *She **has been teaching** since 2015.* (duration is the point)

**Mnemonic — "I C P PC" → "I Can Pass, Perfectly Confident"**
**I**ndefinite → **C**ontinuous → **P**erfect → **P**erfect **C**ontinuous. Say it once, and the order of the four forms is yours forever.

## 3. Present Tense Family (with Signal Words)

**Present Indefinite** — habits, routines, universal truths, timetables.
- Structure: Subject + V1 (+ s/es with he/she/it)
- Signals: *always, daily, usually, every day, often, never, generally*
- *The sun **rises** in the east.* (universal truth — always present!)

**Present Continuous** — happening now, or a fixed near-future arrangement.
- Structure: is/am/are + V1 + ing
- Signals: *now, at present, at this moment, today, Look!, Listen!*
- *Look! The children **are playing** in the park.*

**Present Perfect** — just completed, or past action with present result.
- Structure: has/have + V3
- Signals: *just, already, yet, recently, ever, never, so far, today*
- *I **have just finished** my lunch.*

**Present Perfect Continuous** — started in the past, still continuing; duration matters.
- Structure: has/have + been + V1 + ing + since/for
- Signals: *since (point of time), for (period of time)*
- *She **has been waiting** for two hours.*

⚠️ **Since vs For** — the examiner's favourite:
- **since** = point of time → *since Monday, since 2010, since morning, since childhood*
- **for** = period of time → *for two days, for five years, for a long time*

## 4. Past Tense Family

**Past Indefinite** — a completed action at a definite past time.
- Structure: Subject + V2
- Signals: *yesterday, last night, ago, in 2010, the other day*
- *India **won** the match yesterday.*

**Past Continuous** — an action in progress at a past moment; often interrupted by another action.
- Structure: was/were + V1 + ing
- *While I **was sleeping**, the phone rang.* (long action = continuous; short interrupting action = past indefinite)

**Past Perfect** — the **earlier** of two past actions. "The past of the past."
- Structure: had + V3
- *The train **had left** before I reached the station.*
- Rule: earlier action → **had + V3**; later action → **V2**.

**Past Perfect Continuous** — a long action continuing up to a past point.
- Structure: had + been + V1 + ing + since/for
- *He **had been working** there for ten years before he retired.*

## 5. Future Tense Family

**Future Indefinite** — simple future, promises, predictions, instant decisions.
- Structure: will/shall + V1
- *I **will help** you.*

**Future Continuous** — an action that will be in progress at a future time.
- *At 8 pm tomorrow, we **will be watching** the match.*

**Future Perfect** — an action completed **before** a future point.
- Signals: *by tomorrow, by next week, before*
- *She **will have completed** the project by Friday.*

**Future Perfect Continuous** — duration up to a future point (rarely asked, but know the shape).
- *By December, he **will have been working** here for five years.*

## 6. ⭐ The 12 Golden Rules of Tense

1. **Universal truths, proverbs, and scientific facts** → always **Present Indefinite**, even after a past reporting verb. *The teacher said that the earth **revolves** around the sun.*
2. **Since** (point of time) and **for** (period) with an action still continuing → **Perfect Continuous** (or Perfect). *He **has been living** here since 2018.*
3. Two past actions → earlier one in **Past Perfect (had + V3)**, later one in **Past Indefinite**. *The patient **had died** before the doctor **came**.*
4. With **before**: Past Perfect + before + Past Indefinite. With **after**: Past Indefinite + after + Past Perfect. *He left **after** the meeting **had ended**.*
5. **Yesterday, ago, last week, in 2015** → **Past Indefinite only**, never Present Perfect. ❌ *I have met him yesterday.* ✅ *I **met** him yesterday.*
6. **Just, already, yet, recently, ever, never** → **Present Perfect**. *I **have already eaten**.*
7. **If-clause / time-clause** about the future → **Present Indefinite** in the clause, **will** in the main clause. *If it **rains**, we **will stay** home.* (❌ *If it will rain…*)
8. **While** + Past Continuous; **when** + Past Indefinite for the interrupting action. *While I **was reading**, the lights **went** off.*
9. **By + future time** → **Future Perfect**. *By 6 pm, she **will have reached** home.*
10. **Wish, if only, would that** for present regret → **Past Indefinite** (unreal past). *I wish I **were** a bird.*
11. **It is time / It is high time** + subject → **Past Indefinite** (though meaning is present). *It is high time we **started** working.*
12. **Hardly/Scarcely … when** and **No sooner … than** → **Past Perfect** in the first clause. *No sooner **had** she **entered** than the phone rang.*

## 7. ⚠️ Examiner's Trap Box

**Trap 1 — The "Yesterday + Have" Trap.**
*I have visited Agra last year.* ❌ Sounds smooth, but a definite past time word (**last year, yesterday, ago**) **kills** the Present Perfect. Correct: *I **visited** Agra last year.* Examiners hide "ago/yesterday" at the end of a long sentence so you miss it.

**Trap 2 — The "Since + Past" Trap.**
*He is working here since 2015.* ❌ With **since/for** and an action still going on, English demands Perfect Continuous: *He **has been working** here since 2015.* Hindi speakers make this error constantly because Hindi uses the present here ("वह 2015 से यहाँ काम कर रहा है").

**Trap 3 — The "Said That + Past" Trap.**
*He said that the sun **rose** in the east.* ❌ Universal truths stay in **Present Indefinite** even in reported speech: *He said that the sun **rises** in the east.* The examiner changes a true fact into past tense to test Rule 1.

## 8. 🔁 Linguistic Bridge — Hindi से English

| हिंदी में हम सोचते हैं | गलत English | सही English |
|---|---|---|
| "मैं यहाँ 5 साल से रह रहा हूँ" (present लगता है) | I am living here for 5 years. | I **have been living** here for 5 years. |
| "मैंने कल उससे मिला हूँ" | I have met him yesterday. | I **met** him yesterday. |
| "ट्रेन जा चुकी थी जब मैं पहुँचा" (दोनों past में उलझन) | The train left before I had reached. | The train **had left** before I **reached**. |
| "अगर बारिश होगी तो…" | If it **will rain**, I will stay. | If it **rains**, I will stay. |
| "काश मैं पक्षी होता!" | I wish I **am** a bird. | I wish I **were** a bird. |

**Bridge rule:** Hindi often uses one verb form where English splits time into two. Whenever you see *से (since/for)*, *चुका है (already/just)*, or two past events — stop, and ask: *which form does English demand here?*
