Practice Set 6 — Tense (Questions 126–150)
Difficulty: Questions 126–135 Medium | Questions 136–150 Hard

126. By the time we reached the theatre, the film ______.
(a) had started (b) has started (c) started (d) was starting
Answer: (a)
Solution: "By the time + past simple" signals that one past action was completed before another, so the earlier action takes the past perfect. "Started" alone would suggest the two actions happened together, which is the tempting trap. The correct answer is "had started".
Source: NCERT Class 9 English Grammar

127. She ______ in this city since 2015.
(a) is living (b) has been living (c) was living (d) lived
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Since 2015" with an action continuing up to now requires the present perfect continuous. "Is living" ignores the duration from 2015, which is the trap. The correct answer is "has been living".
Source: NCERT Class 10 English Grammar

128. Choose the correctly framed sentence.
(a) I know the answer. (b) I am knowing the answer. (c) I am know the answer. (d) I knowing the answer.
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Know" is a stative verb and is not normally used in the continuous form, so "I am knowing" is the classic trap that sounds acceptable in Hindi-influenced English. The correct answer is "I know the answer."
Source: PYQ-style

129. When I met him yesterday, he ______ for the same company for ten years.
(a) has worked (b) had been working (c) is working (d) works
Answer: (b)
Solution: The meeting is in the past, and the work continued for ten years before that past point, so the past perfect continuous is needed. "Has worked" wrongly connects the action to the present, which is the trap. The correct answer is "had been working".
Source: NCERT Class 10 English Grammar

130. The train ______ at 6 a.m. tomorrow.
(a) will leave (b) is leaving (c) leaves (d) has left
Answer: (c)
Solution: Fixed timetables and schedules are expressed in the simple present even for future time. "Will leave" is grammatically possible but not the standard choice for a timetable, which is the trap. The correct answer is "leaves".
Source: NCERT Class 9 English Grammar

131. Hardly ______ the station when the train left.
(a) I had reached (b) did I reach (c) I reached (d) had I reached
Answer: (d)
Solution: After "Hardly/Scarcely" at the start of a clause, inversion is required, and the earlier action takes the past perfect: "Hardly had I reached". "I had reached" lacks inversion, which is the trap. The correct answer is "had I reached".
Source: PYQ-style

132. He said that he ______ the letter the previous day.
(a) posted (b) posts (c) has posted (d) had posted
Answer: (d)
Solution: In reported speech, a past simple in direct speech shifts back to the past perfect. "Posted" fails to make the backshift, which is the trap. The correct answer is "had posted".
Source: NCERT Class 10 English Grammar

133. Look! The children ______ in the garden.
(a) play (b) have played (c) are playing (d) played
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Look!" signals an action happening right now, so the present continuous is required. "Play" would describe a habit, not an action in progress, which is the trap. The correct answer is "are playing".
Source: NCERT Class 8 English Grammar

134. I ______ this book twice already.
(a) have read (b) read (c) had read (d) am reading
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Already" with an experience counted up to the present takes the present perfect. "Read" (past simple) would need a finished past time marker, which is the trap. The correct answer is "have read".
Source: NCERT Class 9 English Grammar

135. By next December, she ______ her course.
(a) will have completed (b) will complete (c) has completed (d) completes
Answer: (a)
Solution: "By + future time" requires the future perfect for an action completed before that deadline. "Will complete" does not show completion before the deadline, which is the trap. The correct answer is "will have completed".
Source: NCERT Class 10 English Grammar

136. Spot the error: Part P: I have been / Part Q: waiting for you / Part R: since two hours. / Part S: No error
(a) Part P (b) Part Q (c) No error (d) Part R
Answer: (d)
Solution: "Since" is used with a point of time (since Monday, since 2015), while a duration like "two hours" takes "for". Learners often accept "since two hours" because it sounds natural in Hindi-influenced usage, which is the trap. The error is in Part R.
Source: PYQ-style

137. Spot the error: Part P: No sooner had she / Part Q: finished her speech / Part R: when the audience started clapping. / Part S: No error
(a) Part P (b) Part Q (c) No error (d) Part R
Answer: (d)
Solution: "No sooner" pairs with "than", not "when"; "when" pairs with "hardly/scarcely". Mixing the two correlative pairs is a favourite examiner trap. The error is in Part R.
Source: PYQ-style

138. Choose the correct sentence.
(a) I saw him yesterday. (b) I have seen him yesterday. (c) I had seen him yesterday. (d) I see him yesterday.
Answer: (a)
Solution: A definite past time marker like "yesterday" cannot be used with the present perfect, so "I have seen him yesterday" is the classic trap. The correct answer is "I saw him yesterday."
Source: NCERT Class 9 English Grammar

139. Spot the error: Part P: When I entered the room, / Part Q: I found that somebody / Part R: has stolen my watch. / Part S: No error
(a) Part P (b) Part Q (c) No error (d) Part R
Answer: (d)
Solution: The stealing happened before the past action of entering, so it needs the past perfect "had stolen". "Has stolen" wrongly ties the action to the present, which is the trap. The error is in Part R.
Source: PYQ-style

140. Choose the correct sentence.
(a) He is working here since 2010. (b) He works here since 2010. (c) He was working here since 2010. (d) He has been working here since 2010.
Answer: (d)
Solution: An action that began in the past and continues now, with a "since" phrase, takes the present perfect continuous. "Is working here since 2010" is the most common Hindi-speaker error and the trap. The correct answer is "He has been working here since 2010."
Source: NCERT Class 10 English Grammar

141. Spot the error: Part P: I wish / Part Q: I was / Part R: a millionaire. / Part S: No error
(a) Part Q (b) Part P (c) Part R (d) No error
Answer: (a)
Solution: After "I wish", an unreal present condition takes "were" for all persons, so "I was" is the tempting but wrong choice. The error is in Part Q.
Source: PYQ-style

142. Choose the correct sentence.
(a) If it will rain, we will stay at home. (b) If it rained, we will stay at home. (c) If it rains, we will stay at home. (d) If it rain, we will stay at home.
Answer: (c)
Solution: In a first conditional, the if-clause takes the simple present and the main clause takes "will". "If it will rain" wrongly puts "will" in the if-clause, which is the trap. The correct answer is "If it rains, we will stay at home."
Source: NCERT Class 9 English Grammar

143. Spot the error: Part P: He told me / Part Q: that he will / Part R: come the next day. / Part S: No error
(a) Part P (b) Part Q (c) Part R (d) No error
Answer: (b)
Solution: In reported speech with a past reporting verb, "will" backshifts to "would". "He will come" keeps the direct-speech tense, which is the trap. The error is in Part Q.
Source: NCERT Class 10 English Grammar

144. Choose the correct sentence.
(a) I am having a car. (b) I am have a car. (c) I have a car. (d) I having a car.
Answer: (c)
Solution: When "have" shows possession it is stative and does not take the continuous form; "I am having a car" is the trap. The correct answer is "I have a car."
Source: NCERT Class 8 English Grammar

145. Spot the error: Part P: By the end of this year, / Part Q: they will / Part R: complete the project. / Part S: No error
(a) Part P (b) Part R (c) Part Q (d) No error
Answer: (b)
Solution: "By the end of this year" demands the future perfect "will have completed" to show completion before the deadline. "Will complete" only promises the action, not its completion by that point, which is the trap. The error is in Part R.

146. Choose the correct sentence.
(a) She left the office an hour ago. (b) She has left the office an hour ago. (c) She had left the office an hour ago. (d) She leaves the office an hour ago.
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Ago" always pairs with the simple past, never with the present perfect, so "has left … ago" is the trap. The correct answer is "She left the office an hour ago."
Source: NCERT Class 9 English Grammar

147. Spot the error: Part P: While he was driving / Part Q: to office, / Part R: he has seen an accident. / Part S: No error
(a) Part P (b) Part R (c) Part Q (d) No error
Answer: (b)
Solution: A shorter action interrupting a past continuous background takes the simple past "saw". "Has seen" breaks the past-time frame, which is the trap. The error is in Part R.
Source: PYQ-style

148. Choose the correct sentence.
(a) I have finished my work before he arrived. (b) I finished my work before he had arrived. (c) I had finished my work before he arrived. (d) I finish my work before he arrived.
Answer: (c)
Solution: With two past actions, the earlier one takes the past perfect and the later one the simple past. Option with "before he had arrived" wrongly puts the past perfect on the later action, which is the trap. The correct answer is "I had finished my work before he arrived."
Source: NCERT Class 10 English Grammar

149. Spot the error: Part P: This is the first time / Part Q: I am visiting / Part R: the Taj Mahal. / Part S: No error
(a) Part P (b) Part R (c) Part Q (d) No error
Answer: (c)
Solution: After "This is the first time", the present perfect is required: "I have visited". The present continuous "am visiting" sounds natural to many learners, which is the trap. The error is in Part Q.
Source: PYQ-style

150. Choose the correct sentence.
(a) He has been living in Delhi since 2010 before he moved to Jaipur. (b) He had been living in Delhi since 2010 before he moved to Jaipur. (c) He is living in Delhi since 2010 before he moved to Jaipur. (d) He lived in Delhi since 2010 before he moved to Jaipur.
Answer: (b)
Solution: The stay in Delhi continued up to a past point (the move to Jaipur), so the past perfect continuous is required. "Has been living" wrongly connects the stay to the present, which is the trap. The correct answer is "He had been living in Delhi since 2010 before he moved to Jaipur."
