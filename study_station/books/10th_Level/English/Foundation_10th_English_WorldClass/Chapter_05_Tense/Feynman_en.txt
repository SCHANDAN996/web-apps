# 🧒 Feynman Technique — Tense Explained to a 12-Year-Old

## The Hardest Concept: Present Perfect vs Simple Past

Most students lose marks not on "what is a tense" but on **when to use Present Perfect and when to use Simple Past**. Let's crack it like a story.

### Imagine Your Tiffin Box 🍱

It's lunch break. Your friend asks: *"Did you eat your tiffin?"*

Now listen carefully to two answers:

- **"I ate my tiffin at 12 o'clock."** → The action is **finished, closed, done** — and you even said *when*. The door on that action is **shut**. This is **Simple Past**.
- **"I have eaten my tiffin."** → You didn't say when. What matters is the **result now**: your stomach is full *right now*. The action touches the present. This is **Present Perfect**.

### The One-Line Trick 🔑

> **"Time mentioned → Simple Past. Time hidden → Present Perfect."**

If the sentence carries a **dead time word** — *yesterday, last year, in 2019, ago, that morning* — the action is buried in the past. Use **Simple Past** (V2).

If the sentence has **no finished time** — or words like *just, already, yet, ever, never, today, this week* — the action is still connected to now. Use **Present Perfect** (has/have + V3).

### Test Yourself Like a 12-Year-Old

1. "I ___ (see) that movie yesterday." → *yesterday* = dead time → **saw** ✅ (never "have seen")
2. "I ___ (see) that movie three times." → no dead time, experience till now → **have seen** ✅
3. "She ___ just ___ (finish) her homework." → *just* = fresh result → **has just finished** ✅

### The Unforgettable Picture 🖼️

Think of **Simple Past as a closed photo album** — the event is over, dated, filed away.
Think of **Present Perfect as a live phone notification** — it just popped up and affects your screen *right now*.

You would never write "I have lost my keys yesterday" — that's like putting a live notification inside a closed photo album. Impossible!
