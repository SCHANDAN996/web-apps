🧒 FEYNMAN TECHNIQUE — Explain It Like I'm 12

The hardest thing about One-word Substitution is not learning new words — it is remembering them in the exam hall when 200 words are swimming in your head. So let me explain the whole chapter with one simple idea.

🧠 The "Name Tag" Idea

Imagine your school has 500 students. If you had to describe your friend every time by saying "that boy who sits on the last bench, wears glasses, and always tops the class," you would be exhausted. Instead, you just say his name — "Rohan." One word replaces a whole description.

That is exactly what One-word Substitution is. English gives a "name tag" to long descriptions:

- "A person who loves mankind" → PHILANTHROPIST
- "A place where birds are kept" → AVIARY
- "One who cannot read or write" → ILLITERATE

🏠 The Daily-Life Example

Think of your kitchen. You don't say "the thing in which we keep water cold" — you say "fridge." You don't say "the person who repairs water pipes" — you say "plumber." You already use one-word substitutions in Hindi too: "जो बहुत बोलता है" → "बातूनी" (talkative). English is doing the same trick — you just need to learn its name tags.

🔑 The Unforgettable Trick: Break the Word Like a Toy

Never memorise a word as one long string. Break it into roots, like breaking a toy into parts:

PHILANTHROPIST = PHIL (love) + ANTHROP (mankind) + IST (person)
→ "A person who loves mankind." Now you can never forget it — and as a bonus, you can guess PHILATELIST (stamp-lover = stamp collector) and ANTHROPOLOGY (study of mankind) for free!

One root = 5–10 words. Learn 20 roots, and you unlock 150+ words. That is the real shortcut.

⚠️ The Trap to Avoid

Don't confuse look-alike name tags:
- Optimist (sees the bright side) vs Pessimist (sees the dark side)
- Introvert (turned inward) vs Extrovert (turned outward)
- Biennial (once in 2 years) vs Biannual (twice in 1 year)

Examiners put these pairs in the same question on purpose. Always check the root: BI = two, ANN = year.

📝 Blurring Sheet Instruction

Close this book. Take a blank sheet and in 2 minutes blurt out everything you remember: the name-tag idea, the PHIL + ANTHROP + IST breakdown, and the optimist/pessimist trap. Then open the book and check what you missed. Whatever you missed — that is what you revise tonight.
