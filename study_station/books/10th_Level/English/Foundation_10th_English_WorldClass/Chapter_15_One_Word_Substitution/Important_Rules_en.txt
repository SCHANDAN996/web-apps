# 📋 Important Rules & Tips — One-word Substitution

## (a) 12 High-Yield Rules

| # | Rule | Example | Examiner's Trap |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Root first, word later** — Identify the Greek/Latin root in the description before looking at options. | "Fear of water" → root *hydro* (water) + *phobia* (fear) = **Hydrophobia** | Options like "Aquaphobia" and "Hydrophobia" both look right — both are accepted; pick the one given in standard lists (Hydrophobia). |
| 2 | **-phobia = fear, -phile = lover, -phobe = hater** — never mix these three suffixes. | Bibliophile = lover of books; Bibliophobe = one who hates books | "Anglophile" vs "Anglophobe" differ by one letter — read the description twice. |
| 3 | **-cide = killing** (homicide, fratricide, regicide); the person who kills is also often the same word. | Regicide = killing of a king | "Patricide" (killing one's father) vs "Matricide" (killing one's mother) — the trap pair. |
| 4 | **-cracy = rule/government** — check WHO rules. | Democracy = rule by the people; Autocracy = rule by one person | "Bureaucracy" (rule by officials) vs "Aristocracy" (rule by nobles) — both end in -cracy but mean very different things. |
| 5 | **Person vs. thing vs. act** — decide what the description asks for. | "One who eats human flesh" = **Cannibal** (person); "murder of one's brother" = **Fratricide** (act) | Options mix a person-word with an act-word; match the category first. |
| 6 | **One who... / That which...** — "One who" signals a person-noun; "That which" signals a thing/quality. | "One who speaks two languages" = **Bilingual**; "That which cannot be avoided" = **Inevitable** | "Inevitable" vs "Invincible" (cannot be defeated) — sound-alike trap. |
| 7 | **Negative prefixes mark impossibility**: in-/im-/ir-/un- + root. | Incredible = that which cannot be believed; Inaudible = that which cannot be heard | "Invisible" (cannot be seen) vs "Invincible" (cannot be conquered) — classic confusion. |
| 8 | **-ist / -ian / -er endings usually name a person.** | Pianist, Vegetarian, Astronomer | "Optimist" (sees the bright side) vs "Pessimist" (sees the dark side) — the most repeated pair in SSC. |
| 9 | **Latin plurals and formal words** appear in "a collection of..." descriptions. | A collection of books = **Library**; a collection of flowers = **Bouquet** | "Anthology" = collection of poems/writings, NOT flowers — a frequent trap. |
| 10 | **Animal/voice words are fixed pairs** — memorise as pairs, not singles. | The cry of a dog = **Bark**; of a lion = **Roar** | Options give four animal sounds; only one matches the animal in the question. |
| 11 | **Government/place words**: -arium, -ery, -ory often mark places. | Apiary = place where bees are kept; Aviary = place for birds | "Apiary" (bees) vs "Aviary" (birds) — one letter apart, both in every exam list. |
| 12 | **When two options seem correct, choose the more specific one.** | "A word no longer in use" = **Obsolete** (more specific than "old") | "Archaic" vs "Obsolete": archaic = old-fashioned but may still be used; obsolete = completely out of use. |

## (b) Time Management — 15/45-Second Rule

| Type | What it looks like | Time | Strategy |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Fact-based (15 sec)** | Direct recall: "One who loves books", "Fear of heights", "Killing of one's mother" | 15 sec | If you know it, mark it instantly. If not, do NOT guess randomly — flag and move on. Never spend 60 seconds trying to "derive" a word you have never seen. |
| **Inference-based (45 sec)** | Long descriptions, unfamiliar words, or two close options: "A person who pretends to have beliefs he does not hold" | 45 sec | Break the description into root clues (pretend + beliefs → hypo-crite). Eliminate two options by category (person/act/thing), then compare the remaining two word-by-word. |

**Rule of thumb:** Most one-word substitution questions are direct recall — you either know the word or you don't. Only a few questions need inference from roots. Answer the recall questions quickly, bank that time, and spend it only on the few inference questions.

## (c) Morphology — Root + Affix Table

| Root/Affix | Origin | Meaning | Example Words |
|---|---|---|---|
| **bio** | Greek | life | Biology, Biography, Biodegradable |
| **geo** | Greek | earth | Geography, Geology, Geocentric |
| **auto** | Greek | self | Autocracy, Autobiography, Autograph |
| **phil / phile** | Greek | love | Philanthropist, Bibliophile, Philosophy |
| **phobia** | Greek | fear | Claustrophobia, Hydrophobia, Xenophobia |
| **cide** | Latin (caedere) | kill | Homicide, Suicide, Fratricide |
| **cracy / crat** | Greek (kratos) | rule/power | Democracy, Autocracy, Bureaucrat |
| **omni** | Latin | all | Omnipotent, Omniscient, Omnivorous |
| **poly** | Greek | many | Polyglot, Polygon, Polygamy |
| **mono** | Greek | one/single | Monotheism, Monologue, Monogamy |
| **-ist / -ian** | Latin | one who (person) | Optimist, Vegetarian, Linguist |
| **in- / im- / ir-** | Latin | not | Inevitable, Impossible, Irreparable |

**How to use this table in the exam:** Read the description → spot the key idea (fear? killing? one who? cannot be?) → match it to a root/suffix → scan options for that root. This converts a "guessing" question into a 15-second elimination question.
