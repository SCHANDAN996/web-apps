# 📊 PYQ Analysis — One-word Substitution

## How Exams Test This Chapter

One-word substitution is one of the most **predictable** vocabulary areas in SSC, Railway, Bank and State exams. The pattern across years is remarkably stable:

- **Where it appears:** SSC CGL, CHSL, MTS, GD, CPO; IBPS PO/Clerk; RRB NTPC & Group D; State PSC prelims. Almost every paper carries a few questions from this chapter.
- **How it is asked:** A phrase or definition is given ("A person who speaks many languages") and you pick the single word ("Polyglot"). Occasionally the reverse is asked — a word is given and you pick its meaning.
- **What repeats:** The same core family of words recycles across years and exams — words about **people** (philanthropist, misanthropist, optimist), **fear** (claustrophobia, hydrophobia), **killing** (homicide, suicide, regicide), **government** (democracy, autocracy, bureaucracy), **writing/speech** (biography, autobiography, extempore), and **places** (apiary, aviary, orphanage).
- **Difficulty trend:** Foundation-level exams (SSC MTS, GD, Group D) ask direct recall of common words. Higher tiers (CGL Tier-II, IBPS PO) use less common words and options that are near-synonyms of each other.

## The Examiner's Favourite Traps (Trap Types)

| Trap Type | What it looks like | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Sound-Right Trap** | An option that "sounds" correct because of a familiar word part | "One who loves books" → *Bibliophile* ✔, not *Bibliography* (a list of books) |
| **Near-Synonym Trap** | Two options almost mean the same; only one fits exactly | *Optimist* vs *Idealist* — the question's wording decides |
| **Root-Confusion Trap** | Options built from similar Greek/Latin roots | *Homicide* (killing a man) vs *Matricide* (killing one's mother) |
| **Over-thinking Trap** | The simple, famous word is correct, but students pick the rare one | "A place where bees are kept" → *Apiary*, not some exotic word |

## Top 10 High-Yield Questions (with Solutions)

**Q1.** A person who speaks many languages.
(a) Bilingual (b) Polyglot (c) Multilingual speaker (d) Linguist
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** *Polyglot* = poly (many) + glot (tongue/language). A *linguist* studies languages but need not speak many — classic Sound-Right Trap. **Trap type: Sound-Right.**

**Q2.** One who does not believe in the existence of God.
(a) Agnostic (b) Atheist (c) Ascetic (d) Apostate
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** *Atheist* = a (without) + theos (God). *Agnostic* believes God's existence is unknowable — the most repeated Near-Synonym Trap in exams. **Trap type: Near-Synonym.**

**Q3.** The murder of one's own brother.
(a) Homicide (b) Fratricide (c) Patricide (d) Regicide
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** *Frater* (Latin) = brother → fratricide. *Patricide* = killing one's father; *regicide* = killing a king. **Trap type: Root-Confusion.**

**Q4.** A place where birds are kept.
(a) Apiary (b) Aviary (c) Aquarium (d) Stable
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** *Avis* (Latin) = bird → aviary. *Apiary* is for bees — examiners place these two side by side deliberately. **Trap type: Root-Confusion.**

**Q5.** A speech delivered without preparation.
(a) Extempore (b) Soliloquy (c) Monologue (d) Rhetoric
**Answer: (a)**
**Solution:** *Extempore* = spoken without prior preparation. *Soliloquy* = talking to oneself (common in drama). **Trap type: Sound-Right.**

**Q6.** One who loves mankind.
(a) Misanthropist (b) Philanthropist (c) Anthropologist (d) Humanist
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** *Phil* (love) + *anthropos* (man) = philanthropist. *Misanthropist* is its exact opposite — a favourite pairing in options. **Trap type: Near-Synonym.**

**Q7.** Government by the people.
(a) Autocracy (b) Bureaucracy (c) Democracy (d) Oligarchy
**Answer: (c)**
**Solution:** *Demos* (people) + *kratos* (rule). *Oligarchy* = rule by a few; *autocracy* = rule by one. **Trap type: Root-Confusion.**

**Q8.** Fear of closed spaces.
(a) Hydrophobia (b) Claustrophobia (c) Acrophobia (d) Xenophobia
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** *Claustrum* (Latin) = closed place. *Acrophobia* = fear of heights; *xenophobia* = fear of foreigners. **Trap type: Root-Confusion.**

**Q9.** The life story of a person written by himself.
(a) Biography (b) Autobiography (c) Memoir (d) Bibliography
**Answer: (b)**
**Solution:** *Auto* (self) + *bio* (life) + *graphy* (writing). *Bibliography* (list of books) is the Sound-Right decoy. **Trap type: Sound-Right.**

**Q10.** A word or law no longer in use.
(a) Obsolete (b) Ancient (c) Archaic monument (d) Extinct
**Answer: (a)**
**Solution:** *Obsolete* = out of date, no longer used. *Extinct* applies to species, not words or laws — a subtle Over-thinking Trap. **Trap type: Over-thinking.**

## Applying the 15/45-Second Rule

**15-second question (Fact-based recall):** Q4 above — "A place where birds are kept." If you know *aviary*, you answer in under 15 seconds. No analysis needed; either you know it or you don't. **Strategy:** answer instantly or mark and move on — never burn 60 seconds guessing a recall item.

**45-second question (Root-analysis):** Q3 above — "The murder of one's own brother." Even if you forgot *fratricide*, you can work it out in ~45 seconds: all options end in *-cide* (killing), so decode the prefixes — *homo* = man, *frater* = brother, *pater* = father, *rex* = king. Root knowledge converts a "hard" question into a solvable one.

## Key Takeaways

- Master the recurring families first: **-cide, -phobia, -cracy, phil-/mis-, -graphy, -arium/-ary places**.
- When two options look alike, decode the **root**, not the sound.
- Direct recall = 15 seconds; root decoding = 45 seconds; beyond that, skip and return.
- Revise this chapter's flashcards after 2 days — one-word substitutions fade fast without spaced repetition.
