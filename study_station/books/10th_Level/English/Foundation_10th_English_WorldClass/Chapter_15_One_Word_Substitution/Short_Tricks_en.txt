# 🪄 Short Tricks + Skip Strategy — One-word Substitution

## Trick 1: Root-First Attack
```
┌─────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ Don't memorise the word — memorise the ROOT.│
│ See "cide" → killing. See "phobia" → fear.  │
│ 1 root = 5–10 exam words unlocked.          │
└─────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

## ⏭️ Skip Strategy

**Tip 1 — Skip the "Unknown Root" question (15-second rule).**
If none of the four options contains a root you recognise, do NOT burn 60 seconds guessing letter by letter. Mark it, move on, and return only if time remains. One-word substitution is a **fact-based** question — you either know it or you don't. Budget: **15 seconds max**.

**Tip 2 — Eliminate by root, don't select by sound.**
Even when you don't know the answer, you can often eliminate 2 options whose roots clearly don't match (e.g., the question is about "fear of water" — kill any option without *hydro/aqua*). Eliminating 2 of 4 doubles your odds from 25% to 50% in under 10 seconds.

**Tip 3 — Beware the "Almost-Right" twin.**
Examiners place a near-correct word beside the correct one (e.g., *epitaph* vs *epigraph*; *biography* vs *autobiography*). If two options look like twins, slow down for 10 extra seconds — the trap is always in the small prefix (*auto-*, *epi-*, *mono-*). Read the question's key word ("oneself", "tombstone", "one wife") and match it to the prefix.
