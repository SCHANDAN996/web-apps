126. A person who speaks many languages is called:
(a) Bilingual (b) Polyglot (c) Linguist (d) Orator
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Bilingual" tempts because it involves languages, but it means speaking only two languages. "Linguist" is one who studies language scientifically, not necessarily a speaker of many. "Polyglot" (poly = many, glot = tongue) means one who speaks many languages, so the answer is Polyglot.

127. A government by the people, of the people, for the people is:
(a) Democracy (b) Autocracy (c) Bureaucracy (d) Theocracy
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Autocracy" (rule by one person) is the direct opposite and traps those who confuse the roots. "Bureaucracy" is rule by officials and "Theocracy" is rule by religious leaders. Rule by the people (demos = people, kratos = rule) is Democracy, so the answer is Democracy.

128. One who looks at the bright side of things:
(a) Pessimist (b) Realist (c) Idealist (d) Optimist
Answer: (d)
Solution: "Pessimist" is the exact opposite (one who sees the dark side) and is the classic trap pair. "Idealist" believes in perfect ideals, not necessarily cheerfulness. One who sees the bright side is an Optimist, so the answer is Optimist.

129. A place where dead bodies are kept for identification:
(a) Cemetery (b) Mortuary (c) Crematorium (d) Mausoleum
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Cemetery" tempts because it deals with the dead, but it is a burial ground, not a place for keeping bodies for identification. "Mausoleum" is a grand tomb. A place where dead bodies are kept temporarily for identification or post-mortem is a Mortuary, so the answer is Mortuary.

130. That which cannot be corrected:

Answer: (a)
Solution: "Invincible" (cannot be defeated) and "Inevitable" (cannot be avoided) trap students who only register the "in-" negative prefix. "Indelible" means that which cannot be erased. That which cannot be corrected or reformed is Incorrigible, so the answer is Incorrigible.

Answer: (b)

132. A speech delivered without preparation:

Answer: (d)

133. One who does not believe in the existence of God:
(a) Agnostic (b) Apostate (c) Ascetic (d) Atheist
Answer: (d)
Solution: "Agnostic" is the classic examiner's trap: an agnostic says God's existence cannot be known or proven, while an atheist firmly denies God's existence. "Ascetic" is one who lives austerely. One who does not believe in God is an Atheist, so the answer is Atheist.

134. A list of books available in a library:
(a) Bibliography (b) Glossary (c) Index (d) Catalogue
Answer: (d)
Solution: "Index" tempts, but it is an alphabetical list of topics at the end of one book, not of all books in a library. "Glossary" explains difficult words; "Bibliography" is a list of books on a subject or by an author. A library's list of its books is a Catalogue, so the answer is Catalogue.

135. Murder of one's own brother:

Answer: (a)
Solution: "Homicide" (killing of any human) is too general and traps careless readers. "Patricide" is killing one's father and "Regicide" is killing a king. Killing one's brother (frater = brother) is Fratricide, so the answer is Fratricide.

Answer: (c)
Solution: "Translucent" is the subtle trap: it allows light to pass but objects cannot be clearly seen through it (like frosted glass). "Opaque" allows no light at all. That which can be clearly seen through is Transparent, so the answer is Transparent.

142. One who is indifferent to pleasure or pain:
(a) Stoic (b) Sadist (c) Masochist (d) Cynic
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Cynic" tempts, but a cynic distrusts human motives; it says nothing about bearing pleasure or pain calmly. "Sadist" enjoys others' pain and "Masochist" enjoys one's own pain. One unmoved by pleasure or pain is a Stoic, so the answer is Stoic.

(a) Epic (b) Elegy (c) Ode (d) Ballad

Answer: (c)
