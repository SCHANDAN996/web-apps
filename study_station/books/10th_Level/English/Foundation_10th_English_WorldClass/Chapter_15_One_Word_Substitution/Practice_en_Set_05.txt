101. A person who can use both hands equally well is called:
(a) Ambivalent (b) Ambidextrous (c) Amphibian (d) Ambiguous
Answer: (b)
Solution: Ambivalent (having mixed feelings) is the tempting trap because it shares the prefix "ambi-" (both), but it refers to emotions, not hands. Ambidextrous comes from Latin "ambi" (both) + "dexter" (right-handed), so the answer is Ambidextrous.
Source: NCERT-based vocabulary / PYQ-style

102. A speech delivered without any preparation is:
(a) Monologue (b) Soliloquy (c) Rhetoric (d) Extempore
Answer: (d)
Solution: Soliloquy tempts students because it is also a speech, but it means speaking one's thoughts aloud alone, usually in drama. Extempore (Latin "ex tempore" — out of the moment) means spoken without preparation, so the answer is Extempore.
Source: PYQ-style

103. One who does not believe in the existence of God:
(a) Ascetic (b) Agnostic (c) Atheist (d) Apostate
Answer: (c)
Solution: Agnostic is the classic trap — an agnostic is unsure whether God exists, while an atheist firmly denies God's existence. The question says "does not believe," so the answer is Atheist.

104. The scientific study of birds is called:
(a) Entomology (b) Anthropology (c) Ornithology (d) Etymology
Answer: (c)
Solution: Entomology (study of insects) is the common confusion because both end in "-logy" and deal with small creatures. Ornithology comes from Greek "ornis" (bird), so the answer is Ornithology.

105. A person who speaks many languages:
(a) Linguist (b) Polyglot (c) Bilingual (d) Multilateral
Answer: (b)
Solution: Linguist is tempting, but a linguist studies language scientifically and may know only one language. Polyglot (Greek "poly" = many, "glotta" = tongue) means one who speaks many languages, so the answer is Polyglot.

106. Murder of one's own brother is called:
(a) Fratricide (b) Homicide (c) Patricide (d) Regicide
Answer: (a)
Solution: Patricide (killing one's father) is the trap because both words share "-cide" and sound similar. Fratricide comes from Latin "frater" (brother) + "caedere" (to kill), so the answer is Fratricide.

107. A place where dead bodies are kept for identification:
(a) Mortuary (b) Cemetery (c) Crematorium (d) Sanatorium
Answer: (a)
Solution: Cemetery is tempting since it also relates to the dead, but it is a burial ground, not a place for keeping bodies for identification. Mortuary (Latin "mors" = death) is the correct term, so the answer is Mortuary.

108. One who loves mankind:
(a) Misanthrope (b) Philosopher (c) Misogynist (d) Philanthropist
Answer: (d)
Solution: Philosopher is the trap because "philo-" means love, but a philosopher loves wisdom, not mankind. Philanthropist (philo + anthropos = man) means lover of mankind, so the answer is Philanthropist.

109. A government by the people, of the people, for the people:
(a) Autocracy (b) Bureaucracy (c) Democracy (d) Aristocracy
Answer: (c)
Solution: Bureaucracy tempts some because it is a common form of administration, but it means rule by officials. Democracy (Greek "demos" = people, "kratos" = rule) matches the definition, so the answer is Democracy.

110. That which cannot be corrected:
(a) Invincible (b) Incorrigible (c) Inevitable (d) Indelible
Answer: (b)
Solution: Inevitable (that which cannot be avoided) is the frequent confusion because both start with "in-" and express impossibility. Incorrigible (in + corrigere = to correct) means beyond correction, so the answer is Incorrigible.

Answer: (b)
Solution: Colleague is tempting since colleagues often live in the same era, but it specifically means a co-worker. Contemporary (con = together, tempus = time) means living at the same time, so the answer is Contemporary.

Answer: (a)
Solution: Index is the trap — an index lists topics inside a single book, not the books of a whole library. A catalogue is the systematic list of a library's holdings, so the answer is Catalogue.

(a) Monogamy (b) Polygamy (c) Bigamy (d) Polyandry
Answer: (a)

(a) Anagram (b) Acronym (c) Palindrome (d) Homonym
Answer: (c)
Solution: Anagram tempts because it also involves rearranging letters, but an anagram forms a different word (e.g., "listen" → "silent"). A palindrome like "madam" reads the same both ways, so the answer is Palindrome.

Answer: (a)
Solution: Idealist is the subtle trap — an idealist believes in perfect standards, which is not the same as expecting good outcomes. Optimist (Latin "optimus" = best) means one who sees the bright side, so the answer is Optimist.
