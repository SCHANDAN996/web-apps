# One-word Substitution

## Why This Chapter Can Win You Easy Marks

Imagine the examiner gives you this sentence: *"A person who speaks many languages."* Four options stare back at you — *polyglot, bilingual, linguist, orator*. One word. One mark. Ten seconds. That is the beauty of **One-word Substitution (OWS)**: no calculation, no grammar puzzle — just vocabulary. In SSC GD, SSC MTS, SSC CHSL, RRB NTPC and Group D exams, OWS questions appear regularly, and they are among the **fastest marks** you can earn — if your word bank is ready.

The secret? Most OWS words are built from a small set of **Greek and Latin roots**. Learn 30 roots, and you unlock 300+ words. This chapter teaches you exactly that.

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this chapter, you will be able to:

1. Replace a long phrase with the correct single word in under 15 seconds.
2. Decode unknown words using Greek/Latin roots (e.g., *phobia* = fear).
3. Avoid the examiner's favourite traps — look-alike words like *epidemic* vs *endemic*.
4. Classify words into families: people, places, fears, killing, government, science.

---

## 1. What Is One-word Substitution?

One-word Substitution means expressing a **phrase, clause, or definition** with **one precise word**.

- "A place where dead bodies are kept" → **Mortuary**
- "One who loves mankind" → **Philanthropist**
- "A government by the people" → **Democracy**

English borrows heavily from Greek and Latin, so most of these words follow a pattern: **root + root** or **root + suffix**. Crack the root, crack the word.

## 2. The Root Method — Your Master Key

Here are the **10 highest-yield roots** for foundation-level exams:

| Root | Meaning | Derived Words |
|---|---|---|
| **phobia** (Greek) | fear | claustrophobia, hydrophobia, xenophobia |
| **phile / philo** (Greek) | love | bibliophile, philanthropist, philatelist |
| **cide** (Latin) | killing | homicide, suicide, patricide, insecticide |
| **cracy / crat** (Greek) | rule/power | democracy, autocracy, bureaucracy |
| **logy / logist** (Greek) | study | biology, geology, cardiologist |
| **poly** (Greek) | many | polyglot, polygamy, polygon |
| **mono** (Greek) | one | monogamy, monotheism, monologue |
| **bi** (Latin) | two | bilingual, bigamy, binoculars |
| **mort** (Latin) | death | mortuary, immortal, post-mortem |
| **anthropo** (Greek) | mankind | anthropology, philanthropist, misanthrope |

**How to use it:** See the phrase "fear of water"? Split it: *hydro* (water) + *phobia* (fear) = **hydrophobia**. Even if you have never memorised the word, the root builds it for you.

### 🧠 Active Recall #2
Using the table: what does *misanthrope* mean? (*mis* = hate + *anthropo* = mankind → one who hates mankind.) And what is "killing of one's father"? (*patri* = father + *cide* = killing → **patricide**.)

## 3. The Six Exam Families

Examiners pick OWS words from predictable families. Master these six:

**A. People & their nature**
- One who loves books → **Bibliophile**
- One who loves oneself excessively → **Narcissist**
- One who eats human flesh → **Cannibal**
- One who lives on vegetables only → **Vegetarian**; one who eats all kinds of food → **Omnivore**
- One who can use both hands equally → **Ambidextrous**

**B. Places**
- Where birds are kept → **Aviary**
- Where bees are kept → **Apiary**
- Where dogs are kept → **Kennel**
- Where horses are kept → **Stable**
- Where coins are made → **Mint**
- Where wine is stored → **Cellar**

**C. Killing (-cide family)**
- Killing of a human being → **Homicide**
- Killing of one's mother → **Matricide**
- Killing of one's brother → **Fratricide**
- Killing of an infant → **Infanticide**
- Killing of a king → **Regicide**

**D. Government (-cracy family)**
- Rule by the people → **Democracy**
- Rule by one person with absolute power → **Autocracy**
- Rule by officials → **Bureaucracy**
- Rule by the rich → **Plutocracy**
- Rule by the nobles → **Aristocracy**
- Absence of government → **Anarchy**

**E. Fears (-phobia family)**
- Fear of heights → **Acrophobia**
- Fear of closed spaces → **Claustrophobia**
- Fear of strangers/foreigners → **Xenophobia**
- Fear of water → **Hydrophobia**

**F. Speech & writing**
- One who speaks many languages → **Polyglot**
- A speech made without preparation → **Extempore**
- Something that cannot be read → **Illegible**
- Something that cannot be heard → **Inaudible**
- Writing on a gravestone → **Epitaph**

### 🧠 Active Recall #3
Quick fire: (1) Rule by the rich? (2) Fear of heights? (3) Where coins are made? (Answers: Plutocracy, Acrophobia, Mint.)

## 4. 🧩 Mnemonic: "The CIDE Family Is Deadly"

Remember the killing family with one sentence:

> **"The SUI-HOMI-PATRI-MATRI-FRATRI gang committed REGICIDE."**

- **SUI**cide — killing oneself
- **HOMI**cide — killing a human
- **PATRI**cide — killing father
- **MATRI**cide — killing mother
- **FRATRI**cide — killing brother
- **REGI**cide — killing a king

Say it aloud three times. You will never confuse these six in the exam hall.

## 5. ⚠️ Examiner's Trap

> **Trap 1 — Look-alike pairs.** *Epidemic* (disease spreading among **many people** in a region) vs *Endemic* (disease **regularly found** in a particular area). Examiners swap these constantly. Also: *emigrant* (leaves the country) vs *immigrant* (enters the country).
>
> **Trap 2 — Near-miss options.** For "one who loves mankind," options will include *philanthropist, misanthrope, anthropologist, philatelist*. Three of them contain tempting roots. Only **philanthropist** = lover of mankind. *Philatelist* collects stamps!
>
> **Trap 3 — The "sounds right" trap.** "A place where animals are kept" — many students pick *zoo*, but if the phrase says "kept for breeding/study in confinement," the expected word may be **menagerie** (a collection of wild animals). Always match the **exact shade of meaning**, not the approximate one.

## 6. 🔁 Linguistic Bridge

> **In Hindi we think in phrases; English compresses them into one word.**
>
> - Hindi thought: *"जो बहुत भाषाएँ बोलता है"* → students write "many language speaker" ❌ → Correct: **Polyglot** ✅
> - Hindi thought: *"जो माँस नहीं खाता"* → "non-meat eater" ❌ → **Vegetarian** ✅
> - Common spoken error: "He is having fear of heights." ❌ → "He suffers from **acrophobia**." ✅
>
> **Rule of thumb:** whenever Hindi uses *"जो … करता/होता है"* (one who…), English usually has a single word ending in **-ist, -er, -or, -phile, or -phobe**. Hunt for it in the options first.

## 7. Reading Comprehension: Roots in Action

Read this short practice passage and notice the words you just learned:

> *A team of **anthropologists** studying a remote Himalayan village reported a surprising finding. The village elder, a **polyglot** fluent in five dialects, explained that his community practised **monogamy** and lived under no formal government — a peaceful **anarchy**, some joked. Yet modern fears had arrived: many young villagers showed **xenophobia** towards outsiders, and one researcher, a famous **bibliophile**, noted that fear of the unknown often grows where books are absent. The team's report warned that unless education spread, this **endemic** suspicion could become an **epidemic** of distrust across the region.*

**Check yourself:** Which word means "rule by none"? Which word means "lover of books"? Which two words form the epidemic/endemic trap pair? If you answered all three, the root method is working.
