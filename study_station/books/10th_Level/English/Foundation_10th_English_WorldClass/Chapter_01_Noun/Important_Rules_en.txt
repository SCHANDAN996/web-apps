# 📋 Important Grammar Rules & Tips — Noun

## (a) 12 High-Yield Rules

| # | Rule | Example | Examiner's Trap |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Some nouns are always singular and take a singular verb: news, information, furniture, luggage, advice, machinery, scenery, poetry, stationery, crockery. | The news **is** good. / His luggage **was** lost. | "The news **are** good" ❌ — "news" looks plural but is singular. |
| 2 | Some nouns are always plural and take a plural verb: scissors, trousers, spectacles, goggles, tongs, pliers, premises, wages, thanks, proceeds, belongings. | My spectacles **are** on the table. | "A scissors is…" ❌ — use "a **pair of** scissors". |
| 3 | Nouns that look plural but are singular: Mathematics, Physics, Economics, Ethics, Politics, Innings, Mumps, Measles, Rickets, Billiards. | Mathematics **is** a difficult subject. | "Politics **are** boring" ❌ (as a subject of study it is singular). |
| 4 | Collective nouns (team, committee, jury, family, government, audience) take a singular verb when acting as one unit, plural verb when members act separately. | The jury **has** given its verdict. / The jury **are** divided in their opinion. | Examiners mix both ideas in one sentence — check whether the group acts as one or as individuals. |
| 5 | Nouns expressing number, quantity or measure used with a numeral stay singular: dozen, score, hundred, thousand, lakh, pair. | He bought **three dozen** eggs. | "Three **dozens** eggs" ❌ — but "dozens **of** eggs" ✔ (no numeral). |
| 6 | Some nouns have the same form in singular and plural: sheep, deer, fish, swine, aircraft, series, species, apparatus. | One sheep is grazing; ten sheep **are** grazing. | "Ten **sheeps**" ❌ — never add -s to these. |
| 7 | "One of the + plural noun + singular verb." | One of my **friends has** come. | "One of my friend has…" ❌ — noun must be plural, verb singular. |
| 8 | Two nouns joined by "and" referring to the same person/thing take a singular verb and one article. | The poet and philosopher **is** dead. (one person) | "The poet and **the** philosopher is dead" ❌ — two articles = two persons = plural verb. |
| 9 | Possessive case ('s) is used mainly with living things; for non-living things use "of". | Ram's book ✔ but the leg **of the table** ✔ | "The table's leg" is avoided in formal exam English. |
| 10 | Double possessive is wrong: do not use two apostrophes or 's with "of" together for the same noun. | the wife of my brother / my brother's wife | "My brother's wife's…" chains and "the wife of my brother's" ❌ are classic traps. |
| 11 | Some nouns change meaning in plural: air–airs (manners), good–goods (articles), iron–irons (fetters), force–forces (army), manner–manners (behaviour), work–works (writings/factory). | He put on **airs**. / The **goods** were delivered. | Exams test the plural meaning: "goods" = merchandise, not "good things". |
| 12 | Uncountable nouns never take "a/an" or plural -s: advice, information, furniture, homework, knowledge, progress, bread. | He gave me **a piece of advice**. | "He gave me **an advice** / many informations" ❌ — the most repeated trap in SSC/Bank exams. |

## (b) Time Management — the 15/45-Second Rule

| Type | What it looks like | Time |
|---|---|---|
| **Fact-based (15 sec)** | Direct rule recall: choose the correct form of news/furniture/scissors; spot "an advice"; "one of the + plural noun"; singular–plural same form (sheep, deer). If you know the rule, answer instantly and move on. | 15 seconds |
| **Inference-based (45 sec)** | Collective noun with a long intervening phrase ("The committee, along with its members, … have/has"); sentences where the real subject is hidden far from the verb; double-meaning plurals (goods, manners, works) in context; error-spotting with two suspicious parts. Read twice, find the true subject, then decide. | 45 seconds |

**Strategy:** In the exam, never spend 45 seconds on a Rule-1–12 recall question. If a noun question is not solved in 15 seconds, mark it, skip, and return in the second pass.

## (c) Morphology — Root, Affix, Example

| Root / Affix | Meaning | Noun formed | Example sentence |
|---|---|---|---|
| -tion / -sion (suffix) | act or state of | decide → **decision**, educate → **education** | His decision was final. |
| -ness (suffix) | state or quality | kind → **kindness**, dark → **darkness** | Her kindness touched everyone. |
| -er / -or / -ar (suffix) | one who does | teach → **teacher**, act → **actor**, beg → **beggar** | The actor received an award. |
| -ment (suffix) | result or action | govern → **government**, move → **movement** | The movement gained support. |
| -ity / -ty (suffix) | quality or state | honest → **honesty**, pure → **purity** | Honesty is the best policy. |
| -ist (suffix) | person who practises | art → **artist**, novel → **novelist** | The artist painted a portrait. |
| -dom (suffix) | state or realm | free → **freedom**, king → **kingdom** | Freedom is our birthright. |
| -ship (suffix) | state, skill, office | friend → **friendship**, scholar → **scholarship** | He won a scholarship. |
| -age (suffix) | action, result, collection | marry → **marriage**, pack → **package** | The package arrived today. |
| -al (suffix) | act of | arrive → **arrival**, refuse → **refusal** | His refusal surprised us. |

**Exam tip:** In "fill in the blank with the correct form of the word" questions, check what the sentence needs — after "the" or a possessive, a **noun** is required, so convert the verb/adjective using the right suffix (decide → decision, not "decidingly").
