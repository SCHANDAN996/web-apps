# 📋 Important Grammar Rules & Tips — Pronoun

## (a) 12 High-Yield Rules

| # | Rule | Example | Examiner's Trap |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | A pronoun must agree with its antecedent in number and gender. | Every student must bring **his/her** book. | "Every student must bring **their** book" — sounds modern, but in exam grammar "every + singular noun" takes a singular pronoun. |
| 2 | Pronouns joined by "and" → plural pronoun; joined by "or / either…or / neither…nor" → pronoun agrees with the **nearer** subject. | Neither the teacher nor the students did **their** work. | "Neither the students nor the teacher did **their** work" — wrong; nearer subject is "teacher" → **his/her**. |
| 3 | Order of personal pronouns: **231** (You, He/She, I) for good sense; **123** (I, You, He) for confessing a fault. | You, he and I are friends. / I, you and he are to blame. | "I, you and he are invited" — wrong order for a pleasant sense. |
| 4 | After "than / as", use the case the full sentence demands. | He is taller than **I** (am). / I love you more than **him** (I love him). | "He earns more than **me**" — trap; meaning decides: "than I (earn)". |
| 5 | After "let", "between", "but (except)", prepositions → **objective** case. | Between you and **me**, he is wrong. | "Between you and **I**" — the classic Sound-Right trap. |
| 6 | "Each / every / everyone / everybody / anyone / someone / no one / either / neither" → **singular** pronoun. | Everyone should do **his** duty. | "Everyone should do **their** duty" — common in speech, marked wrong in exams. |
| 7 | "One", if used as subject, must be followed by **one's / one / oneself** — never his/her. | One should do **one's** duty. | "One should do **his** duty" — a favourite trap. |
| 8 | Reflexive pronouns (myself, himself…) are used when subject and object are the same; never as a bare subject. | He hurt **himself**. / **I myself** did it. | "**Myself** and Ravi went there" — wrong; say "Ravi and I". |
| 9 | Verbs like avail, absent, pride, acquit, resign, apply, adapt, revenge → take a reflexive pronoun. "Enjoy" takes a reflexive only when it has no other object ("enjoy oneself"). | He **availed himself of** the chance. / They **enjoyed themselves**. | "He availed of the chance" — missing reflexive. But "enjoy" alone is fine when an object follows: "enjoyed the party" — no reflexive needed. |
| 10 | "Each other" → two persons; "one another" → more than two. | Ram and Shyam help **each other**. | "The two brothers love **one another**" — trap on number. |
| 11 | Relative pronoun: **who/whom** for persons, **which** for things/animals, **that** after superlatives, all, only, any, same, none, and after "who" to avoid repetition. | This is the best book **that** I have. | "The best book **which** I have" — after a superlative, use "that". |
| 12 | "Whose" shows possession (persons, and things in formal use); "It's" = it is, while "**its**" is the possessive — no apostrophe. | The dog wagged **its** tail. | "The dog wagged **it's** tail" — apostrophe trap; "it's" always means "it is". |

## (b) Time Management — 15/45-Second Rule

**Fact-based (15 seconds) — answer on sight, don't re-read:**
- Case of pronoun after preposition ("between you and ___").
- Reflexive after avail/absent/pride.
- its vs it's; who vs whom; each other vs one another.
- Pronoun order (231/123).
- "that" after superlative / all / only.

**Inference-based (45 seconds) — find the antecedent first, then decide:**
- Agreement with an intervening phrase: "The captain, along with his men, did **his** duty" — locate the real subject.
- "Neither…nor / either…or" — check which noun is nearer the pronoun.
- "than/as" case questions — expand the sentence mentally ("than I am" vs "than me") and check the meaning.
- Error-spotting sentences with collective nouns (team, jury, committee) — singular if acting as one unit, plural if members act separately.

**Strategy:** If the antecedent is one word away → 15 sec. If a phrase sits between the noun and the pronoun → slow down to 45 sec; that gap is where the examiner hides the trap.

## (c) Morphology — Roots & Affixes in Pronoun Words

| Root / Affix | Meaning | Example words |
|---|---|---|
| **pro-** (Latin) | "for, in place of" | **pro**noun (a word used *in place of* a noun), propose, protect |
| **noun / nomen** (Latin *nomen*) | "name" | pronoun, noun, nominate, nominal |
| **re-** (Latin) | "back, again" | **re**flexive (bends *back* to the subject), return, recall |
| **flect / flex** (Latin *flectere*) | "to bend" | re**flex**ive, reflect, flexible |
| **-self / -selves** (Old English) | one's own person | my**self**, them**selves**, oneself |
| **inter-** (Latin) | "between, among" | **inter**rogative (asking *among* possibilities), interrupt |
| **re- + lat-** (Latin *referre, relatum*) | "carry back" | **re**lative pronoun (carries you *back* to the antecedent), relate, refer |
| **-ive** (Latin suffix) | "having the nature of" | reflex**ive**, relat**ive**, interrogat**ive** |

**Memory hook:** *Pro-noun* = "for-noun" — a pronoun literally stands **for** a noun. *Re-flex-ive* = "bending back" — the action bends back onto the doer. If you can decode the word, you can decode the rule.
