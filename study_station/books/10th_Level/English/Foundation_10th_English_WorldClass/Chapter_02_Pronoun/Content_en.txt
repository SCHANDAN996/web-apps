# Pronoun — सर्वनाम

## 🎯 Why This Chapter Can Win You Easy Marks

Every English paper in SSC GD, SSC MTS, RRB Group D and State Police exams contains questions where a single wrong pronoun — *he/him, who/whom, each/every* — decides your mark. The good news: pronoun questions are **rule-based**, not luck-based. Master the 12 Golden Rules in this chapter and these become your fastest, safest marks in the paper.

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this chapter you will be able to:
1. Identify all types of pronouns (personal, possessive, reflexive, relative, indefinite, demonstrative, interrogative, reciprocal).
2. Apply the 12 Golden Rules to spot pronoun errors in seconds.
3. Avoid the classic Hindi-speaker traps (e.g., *"Myself went to Delhi"*).
4. Solve error-spotting and fill-in-the-blank questions within the 15-second rule.

---

## 1. What is a Pronoun?

A **pronoun** is a word used **in place of a noun** to avoid repetition.

> *Ravi is a boy. Ravi goes to school. Ravi plays cricket.* ❌ (awkward)
> *Ravi is a boy. **He** goes to school. **He** plays cricket.* ✅

The noun that a pronoun replaces is called its **antecedent**. In the example above, *Ravi* is the antecedent of *he*.

**Golden Principle:** A pronoun must agree with its antecedent in **number, person and gender**.

🧠 **Active Recall:** What is an antecedent? In the sentence *"Sita lost her bag,"* which word is the pronoun and which is the antecedent?

## 2. Types of Pronouns

| Type | Words | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Personal | I, we, you, he, she, it, they / me, us, him, her, them | *She gave **me** a pen.* |
| Possessive | mine, ours, yours, his, hers, theirs | *This book is **mine**.* |
| Reflexive | myself, yourself, himself, herself, itself, ourselves, yourselves, themselves | *He hurt **himself**.* |
| Relative | who, whom, whose, which, that | *The boy **who** came is my friend.* |
| Demonstrative | this, that, these, those | ***These** are my books.* |
| Interrogative | who, whom, whose, which, what | ***Whom** did you meet?* |
| Indefinite | everyone, everybody, someone, somebody, anybody, nobody, none, all, some | ***Everyone** is present.* |
| Reciprocal | each other, one another | *They love **each other**.* |

⚠️ **Note:** *Each* and *every* are **determiners**, not pronouns — they come **before a noun** (*each boy, every student*). But the pronouns built from them — *each one, everyone, everybody* — are indefinite pronouns and follow the singular rule (Rule 5 below).

🧠 **Active Recall:** Which type is *"whom"* in *"Whom did you call?"* — relative or interrogative? Why?

## 3. The 12 Golden Rules of Pronouns ⭐

**Rule 1 — Pronoun after "let":** Use the **objective case** after *let*.
✅ *Let **him** go.* ❌ *Let he go.*

**Rule 2 — Subject vs Object case:** After a verb or preposition, use the **objective case** (me, him, her, us, them).
✅ *Between you and **me**, he is wrong.* ❌ *Between you and I…*

**Rule 3 — "Each other" vs "One another":** *Each other* for **two**; *one another* for **more than two**.
✅ *Ram and Shyam help **each other**.* / *The players help **one another**.*

**Rule 4 — Reflexive pronouns cannot be subjects.**
❌ *Myself went to the market.* ✅ *I went to the market myself.*

**Rule 5 — Indefinite pronouns (everyone, everybody, somebody, anybody, nobody, each one) take a SINGULAR pronoun/verb.** The determiners *each* and *every* before a noun also make it singular.
✅ *Everyone should do **his** duty.* ❌ *…their duty.*
✅ *Every student has done **his** work.*

**Rule 6 — "Each of / Every one of / Either of / Neither of" → singular pronoun.**
✅ *Each of the boys has done **his** work.*

**Rule 7 — Two nouns joined by "and" → plural pronoun; joined by "or/nor/either…or/neither…nor" → pronoun agrees with the NEARER subject.**
✅ *Neither the teacher nor the students did **their** best.*
✅ *Neither the students nor the teacher did **his** best.*

**Rule 8 — "Who" for persons, "which" for things/animals, "that" for both (especially after superlatives, all, only, any).**
✅ *This is the best book **that** I have read.*
✅ *The boy **who** came first won the prize.*

**Rule 9 — "Who" vs "Whom":** *Who* = subject (he); *whom* = object (him).
✅ ***Whom** did you invite?* (You invited **him**.)

**Rule 10 — Possessive pronouns never take an apostrophe.**
✅ *The book is **hers**.* ❌ *her's* / *it's* (= it is!)

**Rule 11 — After "than/as" in comparisons, choose the case by completing the sentence.**
✅ *She is taller than **I** (am).* / *I love you more than (**I love**) **her**.*

**Rule 12 — "One" as subject takes "one/one's", not "his/their".**
✅ *One should do **one's** duty.*

🧠 **Active Recall:** Without looking — which case follows *let*? When do we use *one another* instead of *each other*? Why is *"Everyone did their best"* wrong in exam English?

## 4. ⚠️ Examiner's Trap Box

> **Trap 1 — The "Between you and I" trap:** After prepositions (*between, to, for, with*), always use the objective case: *between you and **me***. Examiners love the polite-sounding but wrong *"between you and I"*.
>
> **Trap 2 — The "Everyone… their" trap:** In modern speech people say *"Everyone should do their duty,"* but in SSC/Bank exam English, *everyone/everybody/each one* is **singular** → *his* (or *his or her*). Don't fall for the sound-right option.
>
> **Trap 3 — The "Myself" trap:** *Myself/himself* can never be the subject. *"Myself and Ravi went"* is always wrong → *"Ravi and I went."*

## 5. 🔁 Linguistic Bridge (Hindi → English)

| Hindi thought | ❌ Common error | ✅ Correct English |
|---|---|---|
| मैं खुद गया | *Myself went there.* | *I went there myself.* |
| तुम और मैं | *You and me will go.* | *You and I will go.* |
| यह किताब मेरी है | *This book is mines.* | *This book is mine.* |
| उसने मुझे मारा | *He beat to me.* | *He beat me.* |
| हर एक ने अपना काम किया | *Everyone did their work.* | *Everyone did his work.* |

**Remember:** Hindi has no case-change problem (मैं/मुझे feel interchangeable in speech), but English strictly separates **I/me, he/him, who/whom**.

## 6. Mnemonic 🧩

**"S-O-S for Pronouns"** — **S**ubject does the action, **O**bject receives it, **S**ingular for each/every/everyone.

Or remember the object-case list with: **"M-H-H-U-T"** — *Me, Him, Her, Us, Them* — all come after verbs and prepositions.
