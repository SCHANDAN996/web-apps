# 🪄 Short Tricks + Skip Strategy — Pronoun

## Memory Boxes (Mnemonics)

**Box 1 — Order of Persons: "231 Good, 123 Bad"**
For good/positive things → 2nd, 3rd, 1st person: *You, he and I will go.*
For bad/negative things (blame, confession) → 1st, 2nd, 3rd: *I, you and he are guilty.*
Trick: **Good news comes last for "I"; bad news comes first for "I".**

**Box 2 — "Each other = 2, One another = Many"**
Count the letters: **EACH** has 4 letters → smaller number (two people).
**ONE ANOTHER** is longer → more people (three or more).
*Ram and Shyam love each other. / The five brothers help one another.*

**Box 3 — "After BUT/EXCEPT, use OBJECT"**
But / Except = prepositions here → object pronoun follows.
*Everyone but **him** was present.* (not "he")
Trick: **"But me, not but I."**

**Box 4 — "Than/As + I or me?"**
Complete the hidden sentence in your head:
*She is taller than I (am).* ✅ — if the verb is implied, use subject form.
*He loves you more than (he loves) me.* — object form when object is meant.
Trick: **Finish the sentence, then choose.**

**Box 5 — "Let + OBJECT"**
*Let **him and me** go.* — never "let he and I".
Trick: **"Let me" sounds right because it IS right — keep everything after 'let' in object form.**

**Box 6 — "It is I who am / It is me who…? NO!"**
After "It is…", the pronoun is nominative, and the verb agrees with THAT pronoun:
*It is **I who am** wrong. / It is **you who are** wrong.*
Trick: **"Who" copies the pronoun before it.**

**Box 7 — "One → One, never He"**
If the sentence starts with **one**, all later pronouns must be **one/one's/oneself**:
*One should do **one's** duty.* (not "his duty")

**Box 8 — "Each/Every/Everyone → SINGULAR"**
Trick: **"E-words are lonely"** — Each, Every, Everyone, Everybody, Either, Neither all take singular verb + singular pronoun.
*Each of the boys has done **his** work.*

**Box 9 — "Or / Nor → agree with the NEAREST"**
*Neither the teacher nor the students have done **their** work.*
Trick: **The pronoun marries the closest noun.**

**Box 10 — "Possessive + Gerund"**
*I object to **his** going there.* (not "him going")
Trick: **Before an -ing noun, show ownership: my, his, their.**

**Box 11 — "Who vs Whom: He/Him test"**
Answer the question with he/him:
- *Who came?* → **He** came → WHO ✅
- *Whom did you call?* → I called **him** → WHOM ✅
Trick: **He = Who, HiM = WhoM (both end in M).**

**Box 12 — "The same… that, not who"**
*This is the same man **that** I saw yesterday.*
Trick: **"Same" pairs only with "that/as", never with who/which.**

**Box 13 — "Avoideen the Reflexive Trap"**
Myself/himself/themselves can NEVER be the subject:
❌ *Myself went to Delhi.* ✅ *I went to Delhi.*
Trick: **A "-self" word needs its owner already in the sentence.**

**Box 14 — "Between you and ME"**
Preposition (between, to, for, with) + object pronoun:
❌ *between you and I* ✅ *between you and me*
Trick: **"Between us" — you'd never say "between we".**

**Box 15 — "None = Not one → usually singular"**
*None of the answers **is** correct.* (formal/exam usage)
Trick: **Split it: N-ONE → ONE → singular.**

## ⏭️ Skip Strategy

**Tip 1 — Skip the "Sound-Right" trap question.**
If two options both "sound" correct (e.g., *than I* vs *than me*), don't guess by ear — English ears trained on Hindi mislead here. Apply the hidden-sentence test (Box 4). If you can't complete the test in 45 seconds, mark and move on; return after finishing the section.

**Tip 2 — Skip long intervening-phrase questions on first pass.**
Questions like *"The captain, along with his five teammates and the coach, ___ (has/have)…"* hide the real subject. First pass: underline the true subject and ignore everything between commas. If the sentence has 3+ commas and you feel rushed, skip it and return — these are 45-second questions, not 15-second ones.

**Tip 3 — Never skip a pure recall question.**
Order of persons (231/123), each other vs one another, let + object — these are 15-second gift marks. Attempt them instantly; save your skipping budget for inference-heavy error-spotting items where a wrong guess costs negative marks.
