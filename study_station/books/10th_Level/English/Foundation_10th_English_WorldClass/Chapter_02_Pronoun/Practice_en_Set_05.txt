101. Each of the boys in the class has submitted ___ assignment on time.
(a) their (b) his (c) its (d) them
Answer: (b)
Solution: The tempting option is "their", which sounds natural in speech, but "each of the boys" is singular and takes a singular pronoun. "Each" is always followed by a singular verb and singular pronoun, so the answer is "his".
Source: NCERT Class 9 English Grammar

102. One should do ___ duty honestly.
(a) one's (b) their (c) his (d) your
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is "his", which was used in older textbooks, but the rule is that the pronoun for "one" must be "one's" throughout the sentence. Mixing "one" with "his" or "their" is a classic examiner trap, so the answer is "one's".

103. Neither of the two sisters has brought ___ books.
(a) their (b) its (c) his (d) her
Answer: (d)
Solution: "Their" tempts because two sisters are mentioned, but "neither of" is singular and refers to one person at a time. Since the sisters are female, the singular feminine pronoun fits, so the answer is "her".

104. Spot the error: Part P: The committee / Part Q: have divided / Part R: their opinions on the issue. / Part S: No error
(a) Part P (b) Part Q (c) No error (d) Part R
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is to mark Part Q, assuming a collective noun always takes a singular verb. But when the members of a collective noun act separately, a plural verb and plural pronoun are correct; here the committee members hold divided opinions, so the sentence is correct and the answer is "No error".

105. It is ___ who are responsible for this mistake.
(a) I (b) me (c) we (d) us
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Us" tempts because "it is me/us" is common in speech, but after "it is", the pronoun must be in the nominative case, and the verb "are" demands a plural subject. The nominative plural pronoun matching "are" is "we", so the answer is "we".

106. The teacher praised the boy ___ had won the first prize.
(a) which (b) who (c) whom (d) whose
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Whom" tempts because the boy receives the praise, but the pronoun is the subject of "had won", not the object of "praised". For a person as subject of the relative clause, "who" is required, so the answer is "who".

107. Spot the error: Part P: Avail / Part Q: the opportunity / Part R: before it is too late. / Part S: No error
(a) Part Q (b) Part P (c) Part R (d) No error
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is to look for an error in Part Q or Part R, which are perfectly fine. "Avail" must be followed by a reflexive pronoun — "avail yourself of the opportunity" — so the error lies in Part P, and the answer is Part P.

108. Between you and ___, this plan will never work.
(a) I (b) mine (c) myself (d) me
Answer: (d)
Solution: "I" tempts because people think "you and I" always sounds polite, but "between" is a preposition and must be followed by the objective case. The objective form of the first person singular is "me", so the answer is "me".

109. The two brothers love ___ very much.
(a) one another (b) themselves (c) each other (d) one other
Answer: (c)
Solution: "One another" tempts because it also expresses reciprocity, but traditionally "each other" is used for two persons and "one another" for more than two. Since there are two brothers, the answer is "each other".

110. Spot the error: Part P: Myself and Ravi / Part Q: went to / Part R: the market yesterday. / Part S: No error
(a) Part Q (b) Part P (c) Part R (d) No error
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is to accept the sentence because "myself" sounds formal and correct in Indian English speech. A reflexive pronoun cannot be the subject of a sentence; it should be "Ravi and I", so the error is in Part P and the answer is Part P.

111. This is the house ___ roof was damaged in the storm.
(a) who (b) which (c) that (d) whose
Answer: (d)
Solution: "Which" tempts because the house is a thing, but the sentence shows possession — the roof belongs to the house. The relative pronoun showing possession for both persons and things is "whose", so the answer is "whose".

112. Everyone should respect ___ elders.
(a) one's (b) their (c) his (d) its
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Their" tempts as modern usage, but in traditional exam grammar "everyone" is singular and takes a singular pronoun. The standard exam answer is "his", so the answer is "his".

113. He is one of those students who ___ always punctual.
(a) is (b) was (c) has been (d) are
Answer: (d)
Solution: "Is" tempts because "one" appears in the sentence, but the relative pronoun "who" refers to "students" (plural), not to "one". A plural antecedent takes a plural verb, so the answer is "are".

(a) Part Q (b) Part P (c) Part R (d) No error
Answer: (a)

115. ___ of the two answers is correct?
(a) What (b) Whom (c) Whose (d) Which
Answer: (d)
Solution: "What" tempts because it is used for asking questions, but "what" is used when the choice is unlimited, while "which" is used when selecting from a definite, limited number. Since the choice is between two answers, the answer is "Which".

116. The manager, along with his assistants, ___ left the office.
(a) has (b) have (c) are (d) were
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Have" tempts because "assistants" is the nearest noun, but with "along with", the verb agrees with the first subject, "the manager", which is singular. The intervening phrase must be ignored, so the answer is "has".

117. She hurt ___ while cutting vegetables.
(a) her (b) herself (c) hers (d) she
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Her" tempts as the object form, but when the subject and object are the same person, a reflexive pronoun is required. The reflexive form of "she" is "herself", so the answer is "herself".

Answer: (a)

119. None of the milk ___ spilt on the floor.
(a) was (b) have been (c) were (d) are
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Were" tempts because "none" can sometimes take a plural verb, but here "none" refers to "milk", an uncountable noun, which is always singular. A singular subject takes a singular verb, so the answer is "was".

120. It was I who ___ the window yesterday.
(a) break (b) breaks (c) broke (d) broken
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Break" tempts because "I" normally takes a plural verb, but the time marker "yesterday" demands the simple past tense. The past form of "break" is "broke", so the answer is "broke".

121. Spot the error: Part P: Each student and each teacher / Part Q: have attended / Part R: the meeting. / Part S: No error
(a) Part Q (b) Part P (c) Part R (d) No error
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is to think two nouns joined by "and" always take a plural verb. When "each" precedes both nouns, the subject is treated as singular and takes "has", so the error is in Part Q and the answer is Part Q.

122. The dog wagged ___ tail when it saw its master.
(a) it's (b) his (c) its (d) their
Answer: (c)
Solution: "It's" tempts because of the apostrophe, but "it's" means "it is", while the possessive pronoun of "it" is "its" without an apostrophe. The sentence needs the possessive form, so the answer is "its".

123. Those who live in glass houses should not throw stones at ___.
(a) other (b) others (c) another (d) the other
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Other" tempts because it looks similar, but "other" is an adjective and needs a noun after it; here a pronoun is needed to stand alone. The plural pronoun meaning "other people" is "others", so the answer is "others".

(a) me (b) myself (c) mine (d) I
Answer: (d)
Solution: "Me" tempts because it is common in casual speech, but the pronoun here is part of the subject of the verb "went", so the nominative case is required. The nominative form is "I", so the answer is "I".
