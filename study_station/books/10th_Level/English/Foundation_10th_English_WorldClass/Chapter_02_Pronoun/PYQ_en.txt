# 📊 PYQ Analysis — Pronoun

## How Exams Test Pronouns (Pattern Analysis)

Across SSC (CGL, CHSL, MTS, GD), RRB (NTPC, Group D), Bank (IBPS, SBI) and State exams, pronoun questions appear in **three formats**:

1. **Spot the Error** — one part of the sentence has a wrong pronoun (most common).
2. **Fill in the Blank** — choose the correct pronoun form (I/me, who/whom, its/it's).
3. **Sentence Improvement** — replace the underlined pronoun phrase.

**Sub-topics that repeat most often:**
- Pronoun after "each/every/everyone/somebody" → singular (his/her, not their)
- Pronoun after "one" → one's, not his
- Case after "than/as" and after "let/between" (I vs me)
- Reflexive pronouns (himself/themselves) — missing or wrongly used
- Relative pronouns: who vs which vs that; whose for possession
- Agreement with collective nouns (team/jury/family — it or they?)
- Possessive confusion: its vs it's, your vs you're

**Examiner's favourite traps (in words, not numbers):**
- **Intervening Phrase trap** — a long phrase between subject and pronoun hides the real subject ("Each of the boys… *his*").
- **Sound-Right trap** — "between you and I" *sounds* polite but is wrong ("between you and me").
- **Over-thinking trap** — a simple, correct sentence made to look suspicious; the confident student changes a right answer.
- **Reference confusion (संदर्भ भ्रम)** — pronoun with two possible antecedents ("Ravi told Mohan that *he* had won").

## Top 10 High-Yield Questions (with Solutions)

**Q1.** Each of the students (a)/ have submitted (b)/ his assignment on time. (c)/ No error (d)
**Answer:** (b)
**Solution:** "Each of + plural noun" takes a **singular** verb — "has submitted". The pronoun "his" is correctly singular. **Trap type:** Intervening Phrase ("of the students" hides "each").
**Source:** PYQ-style (concept: NCERT Class 9 Grammar)

**Q2.** Between you and I, (a)/ this plan (b)/ will never work. (c)/ No error (d)
**Answer:** (a)
**Solution:** After a preposition ("between"), use the **objective case** — "between you and **me**". Politeness does not change grammar. **Trap type:** Sound-Right.
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q3.** One should do (a)/ his duty (b)/ honestly. (c)/ No error (d)
**Answer:** (b)
**Solution:** When the subject is "one", the pronoun must be **one's** — "One should do one's duty." **Trap type:** Sound-Right ("his" feels natural in Hindi-influenced English).
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q4.** The jury (a)/ has given (b)/ their verdict. (c)/ No error (d)
**Answer:** (c)
**Solution:** "Has given" (singular verb) shows the jury acts as **one body**, so the pronoun must be **its** — "its verdict". Use "their" only when members act separately. **Trap type:** Collective Noun.
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q5.** He is one of the best players (a)/ that has ever played (b)/ for this team. (c)/ No error (d)
**Answer:** (b)
**Solution:** After "one of the + plural noun + that/who", the relative pronoun refers to the **plural** noun — "that **have** ever played". **Trap type:** Intervening Phrase.
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q6.** Myself and Ravi (a)/ went to (b)/ the market yesterday. (c)/ No error (d)
**Answer:** (a)
**Solution:** A reflexive pronoun cannot be a subject. Say "**Ravi and I** went…" (other person first, then I). **Trap type:** Sound-Right (very common in spoken Indian English).
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q7.** The dog wagged (a)/ it's tail (b)/ when it saw its master. (c)/ No error (d)
**Answer:** (b)
**Solution:** Possessive of "it" is **its** (no apostrophe). "It's" = "it is". **Trap type:** Sound-Right / spelling confusion.
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q8.** Everyone should bring (a)/ their own books (b)/ to the class. (c)/ No error (d)
**Answer:** (b)
**Solution:** "Everyone" is singular; in traditional exam grammar the pronoun is **his/her (his or her)** — "his own book". **Trap type:** Sound-Right (modern speech accepts "their", but SSC/Bank keys follow the singular rule).
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q9.** She is taller (a)/ than me. (b)/ No error (c)
**Answer:** (b)
**Solution:** After "than", the case matches the compared subject — "taller than **I** (am)". **Trap type:** Sound-Right ("than me" dominates spoken English; exams test the formal rule).
**Source:** PYQ-style

**Q10.** Ravi told Suresh (a)/ that he had won (b)/ the first prize. (c)/ No error (d)
**Answer:** (b)
**Solution:** "He" is ambiguous — it could mean Ravi or Suresh. Rewrite: "Ravi told Suresh, 'You have won the first prize'" or "…that Suresh had won…". A pronoun must have **one clear antecedent**. **Trap type:** Reference confusion.
**Source:** PYQ-style

## Applying the 15/45-Second Rule

**Q2 (Between you and I) — 15-second question.**
This is **fact-based**: a fixed rule (preposition → objective case). Do not re-read the sentence. Spot "between", check the pronoun, mark "me", move on. Target: 15 seconds.

**Q10 (Ravi told Suresh that he had won) — 45-second question.**
This is **inference-based**: you must test both possible meanings of "he", see that both are grammatically possible, and conclude the reference is unclear. Rushing here causes the classic mistake of calling it "No error". Budget: up to 45 seconds.

**Rule of thumb:** Case/agreement errors (I/me, its/it's, each…his) = 15 sec. Reference, collective-noun logic, and "one of the…" constructions = 45 sec.
