# 🧒 Feynman Technique: Pronoun — Explained to a 12-Year-Old

## The Hardest Concept: Pronoun–Antecedent Agreement (and Case Confusion)

Imagine you have a **name tag** for every person in your class. Now imagine you lose the name tags and start pointing instead — "he did it," "she said so," "they went there." A **pronoun** is just a pointing word. It works ONLY if everyone knows **who you are pointing at**. That "who" is called the **antecedent** (the noun the pronoun replaces).

### The Daily-Life Example 🍫

Rohan and Sohan are fighting over one chocolate. You say:

> "Rohan gave **his** chocolate to Sohan because **he** was hungry."

Wait — WHO was hungry? Rohan or Sohan? The pointing word "he" is confusing because it could point at two people. In exams, the examiner does exactly this to you — he hides the real antecedent behind extra words, and your brain grabs the nearest noun instead of the correct one.

### The Unforgettable Trick: **"The Pointing Finger Test"** 👉

Whenever you see a pronoun in a sentence, do this:

1. **Point back** — Ask: "Which noun is this pronoun pointing at?"
2. **Match the number** — One person/thing → he/she/it/his/her. More than one → they/their/them.
3. **Ignore the middle crowd** — Words like *along with, as well as, together with, besides* are just **crowd standing between you and the real noun**. Push them aside!

> **Each of the boys, along with his friends, has done ___ work.**
> Push aside "along with his friends" → the real subject is **Each** → answer: **his** work. ✅

### Case Confusion — The "Doer vs Receiver Test" 🚪

Students panic over **I vs me, who vs whom, he vs him**. Here's the correct rule:

- **The doer of the action (the subject)** → **I, he, she, we, they, who**.
- **The receiver of the action (object of a verb)** or **after a preposition** (to, for, with, between) → **me, him, her, us, them, whom**.
- **Special case:** after "is/am/are/was/were" (being-verbs), formal grammar uses the subject form again — "It is **I**" — because the pronoun is not receiving any action; it is the same person as the subject.

> "Between you and ___" → "between" is a preposition → **me** (not I!). ✅
> "___ am going." → the doer of "am going" → **I** (not me!). ✅
> "___ did you see?" → "you" is the doer; the blank receives the action → **Whom** (not Who!). ✅

**Memory line:** *"Preposition ke baad, -m wala yaad"* — after a preposition, pick the pronoun ending in **-m**: hi**m**, the**m**, who**m**, and **me** (rhymes with them!).

### One More Classic Trap: Everyone/Each → SINGULAR

Words like **everyone, everybody, each, someone, nobody** *sound* like a crowd, but grammatically they are **one person**:

> "Everybody brought **his** lunch." (not "their" — in strict exam grammar)

**Trick:** *"Every-body is ONE body."* 🧍
