126. Each of the boys in the class has submitted ___ assignment on time.
(a) their (b) them (c) his (d) theirs
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Their" tempts because "boys" is plural, but the subject is "Each," which is singular, so the pronoun must be singular. With "each of + plural noun," the verb and pronoun stay singular. Hence the answer is "his."

127. Neither of the two sisters has brought ___ books today.
(a) their (b) them (c) her (d) hers
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Their" is the trap because "sisters" sounds plural, but "neither of" takes a singular pronoun in formal exam English. The correct choice is "her."

128. It was I who ___ responsible for the delay.
(a) was (b) am (c) were (d) is
Answer: (a)
Solution: After "It is/was + pronoun + who," the verb agrees with the pronoun, here "I," and the past tense of the main clause ("was") controls the sentence, so "am" is wrong. The correct answer is "was."

129. One should do ___ duty honestly.
(a) his (b) their (c) one's (d) our
Answer: (c)
Solution: "His" was once accepted, but the standard rule is that the pronoun for "one" must remain "one" throughout — "one should do one's duty." Mixing "one" with "his/their" is a classic examiner trap. The answer is "one's."

(a) their (b) them (c) his (d) its
