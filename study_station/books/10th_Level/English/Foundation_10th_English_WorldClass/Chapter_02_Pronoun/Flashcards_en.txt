Card 1
Front: What is a pronoun?
Back: A word used in place of a noun to avoid repetition. Example: "Ravi is a boy. He plays cricket." — "He" replaces "Ravi."

Card 2
Front: Which pronoun is used after "let"? — "Let ___ go." (I / me)
Back: "Me." After "let," the objective case is used: "Let me go," "Let him come." Trap: "Let I go" is always wrong.

Card 3
Front: "Between you and ___" — I or me?
Back: "Me." Prepositions (between, for, with, to) are always followed by the objective case: "between you and me." "Between you and I" is a classic examiner trap.

Card 4
Front: 🧠 Mnemonic: How to remember the three cases of pronouns?
Back: "SOP" — Subject, Object, Possessive. I→me→my, he→him→his, she→her→her, we→us→our, they→them→their. Say "SOP" and recall all three columns.

Card 5
Front: "It is ___ who called you." (I / me)
Back: "I." After "It is/was," the nominative (subject) case is used when the pronoun is the subject of the following clause: "It is I who called you."

Card 6
Front: ⚠️ Examiner Trap: "Each of the boys have done his work." — Find the error.
Back: "Have" is wrong; use "has." After "each of / every one of / either of / neither of + plural noun," the verb and pronoun are singular: "Each of the boys has done his work."

Card 7
Front: When do we use "one" as a pronoun, and what follows it?
Back: "One" is an indefinite pronoun for people in general. It must be followed by "one's/oneself," never "his/himself": "One should do one's duty" (not "his duty").

Card 8
Front: "The two brothers love ___." (each other / one another)
Back: "Each other." Use "each other" for two persons; "one another" for more than two. Trap: exams swap these deliberately.

Card 9
Front: What is a reflexive pronoun? Give the full list.
Back: A pronoun ending in -self/-selves used when the subject and object are the same: myself, yourself, himself, herself, itself, ourselves, yourselves, themselves. "He hurt himself."

Card 10
Front: ⚠️ Examiner Trap: "He himself went there." vs "He went there hisself." — Which is correct and why?
Back: "He himself went there" is correct. "Hisself" and "theirselves" do not exist in standard English — only "himself" and "themselves." Examiners test these fake forms.

Card 11
Front: "This is the man ___ helped me." (who / whom / which)
Back: "Who." "Who" is the subject of "helped." Use "whom" only when the pronoun is the object: "the man whom I met" (I met him).

Card 12
Front: 🧠 Mnemonic: How to choose between "who" and "whom" in 5 seconds?
Back: The "him test": answer the question with he/him. If "him" fits → whom; if "he" fits → who. "___ did you invite?" → "I invited him" → whom. Him = whom (both end in m).

Card 13
Front: Which relative pronoun is used after "all," "everything," "something," "the only," and superlatives?
Back: "That" (not "which/who"): "All that glitters is not gold," "This is the best book that I have read."

Card 14
Front: "One should respect ___ parents." (one's / his / their)
Back: "One's." The pronoun must agree with "one": "One should respect one's parents." Mixing "one…his" is a frequent error-spotting item.

Card 15
Front: "Neither of the two answers ___ correct." (is / are)
Back: "Is." "Neither of / either of" takes a singular verb even though a plural noun follows: "Neither of the boys is guilty."

Card 16
Front: What is the difference between "its" and "it's"?
Back: "Its" = possessive pronoun (The dog wagged its tail). "It's" = contraction of "it is/it has" (It's raining). Apostrophe never shows possession in pronouns.

Card 17
Front: "He is taller than ___." (I / me) — formal exam answer?
Back: "I" (than I am, with "am" understood). In comparative clauses with "than/as," the nominative case is preferred in exams: "She is as clever as he." Note: "than me" is common in speech but marked wrong in error-spotting.

Card 18
Front: "The teacher, as well as the students, praised ___." (him / them)
Back: "Him." With "as well as / along with / together with," the pronoun agrees with the first subject (the teacher), not the phrase in between — the intervening-phrase trap.

Card 19
Front: Which pronoun is used for countries, ships, and the moon in traditional exam English?
Back: "She/her" may be used for ships and sometimes countries ("India is proud of her culture"); otherwise things take "it/its." Animals take "it" unless treated as pets ("his/her").

Card 20
Front: "Everyone should do ___ duty." (his / their / one's)
Back: "His" (traditional exam answer). Everyone, everybody, someone, anyone, nobody are singular and take singular pronouns: "Everyone should do his duty." "Their" is accepted in modern usage but exams prefer the singular.
