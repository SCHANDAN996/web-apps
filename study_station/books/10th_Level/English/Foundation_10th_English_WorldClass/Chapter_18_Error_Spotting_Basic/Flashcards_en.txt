Card 1
Front: What is the first thing you should check in an Error Spotting sentence?
Back: The Subject–Verb Agreement. Find the real subject, ignore the words in between, and match the verb to it. Example: "The quality of apples WAS (not were) good."

Card 2
Front: "One of my friends ___ coming." — is or are?
Back: IS. After "one of + plural noun", the verb is singular because the real subject is "one". Trap: the plural noun "friends" sits right before the verb to confuse you.

Card 3
Front: MNEMONIC: Which words always take a singular verb? (Hint: think "EACH-EVERY-ONE")
Back: Each, Every, Everyone, Everybody, Someone, Anyone, Nobody, Either, Neither — all take singular verbs. "Everybody knows (not know) the answer."

Card 4
Front: EXAMINER'S TRAP: "The teacher, as well as the students, ___ present." — was or were?
Back: WAS. With "as well as / along with / together with / in addition to", the verb agrees with the FIRST subject only. The phrase in between is a decoy.

Card 5
Front: What is wrong with: "He is more taller than his brother"?
Back: Double comparative error. "Taller" is already comparative — never use "more" with -er forms. Correct: "He is taller than his brother."

Card 6
Front: "I have been living here ___ 2015." — since or for?
Back: SINCE. Use "since" with a point of time (2015, Monday, January) and "for" with a period of time (two years, a long time).

Card 7
Front: MNEMONIC: How do you remember adjective order before a noun?
Back: OSASCOMP — Opinion, Size, Age, Shape, Colour, Origin, Material, Purpose. "A beautiful small old round red Indian wooden table."

Card 8
Front: What is wrong with: "She is senior than me"?
Back: "Senior, junior, superior, inferior, prefer" take TO, not THAN. Correct: "She is senior to me."

Card 9
Front: EXAMINER'S TRAP: "The news ___ shocking." — is or are?
Back: IS. "News" looks plural but is singular. Same family: physics, mathematics, politics, economics (as subjects) — all singular.

Card 10
Front: "Neither Ram nor his brothers ___ at home." — is or are?
Back: ARE. With "neither…nor / either…or / not only…but also", the verb agrees with the NEAREST subject ("brothers" → are).

Card 11
Front: What is wrong with: "He did not came to school"?
Back: After "did / did not", always use the base form (V1) of the verb. Correct: "He did not come to school."

Card 12
Front: "I have been working here ___ five years." — since or for?
Back: FOR — five years is a period of time. Note the tense: with since/for showing duration, use present perfect continuous ("have been working"), not simple present continuous.

Card 13
Front: What is wrong with: "He is more cleverer than me"?
Back: Double comparative again — "cleverer" already means "more clever". Drop "more". Correct: "He is cleverer than me."

Card 14
Front: "Bread and butter ___ my favourite breakfast." — is or are?
Back: IS. When two nouns joined by "and" express ONE idea/dish (bread and butter, rice and curry, the poet and philosopher = one person), use a singular verb.

Card 15
Front: What is wrong with: "I have seen him yesterday"?
Back: "Yesterday" is a definite past time — use simple past, not present perfect. Correct: "I saw him yesterday." Present perfect never pairs with yesterday/last week/ago.

Card 16
Front: "Each of the boys ___ a book." — has or have?
Back: HAS. "Each of + plural noun" takes a singular verb. The plural noun before the verb is the examiner's favourite trap.

Card 17
Front: What is wrong with: "He is one of the best player in the team"?
Back: After "one of the + superlative", the noun must be PLURAL. Correct: "one of the best players". (But the verb stays singular: "one of the best players IS…")

Card 18
Front: MNEMONIC: Which verbs are followed by a gerund (-ing), not "to + verb"?
Back: Think "MED-SAFE": Mind, Enjoy, Deny, Suggest, Avoid, Finish, Escape. "I enjoy swimming (not to swim)."

Card 19
Front: EXAMINER'S TRAP: "The number of students ___ increasing." — is or are?
Back: IS. "The number of" = singular; "A number of" = plural. Compare: "A number of students ARE absent."

Card 20
Front: What is wrong with: "What you are doing?"
Back: In a question, the helping verb comes before the subject. Correct: "What ARE you doing?" (Hindi word order "तुम क्या कर रहे हो?" causes this error — in English, invert it.)
