Questions 51–60: Easy | Questions 61–75: Medium

51. One of my friend / has gone / to Delhi / yesterday.
(a) Part Q (b) Part P (c) Part R (d) No error
Answer: (b)
Solution: Part P is tempting because "one of my friend" sounds natural in speech, but after "one of" we always use a plural noun — "one of my friends". Parts Q, R and the time word "yesterday" are all fine, so the answer is Part P.
Source: PYQ-style

52. He do not / like tea / in the morning / at all.
(a) Part P (b) Part Q (c) Part R (d) No error
Answer: (a)
Solution: With "he" (third person singular) the auxiliary must be "does", so Part P should read "does not". Learners often pick Part Q thinking "like" is wrong, but after "do/does not" the base verb is correct. The error is in Part P.
Source: NCERT Class 8 English Grammar

53. She is / more taller / than her sister / in the family.
(a) Part Q (b) Part R (c) Part P (d) No error
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Taller" is already comparative, so adding "more" makes a double comparative — Part Q should be just "taller". Part R with "than" is correct usage. The error is in Part Q.
Source: PYQ-style

54. I have / a urgent / piece of work / to finish today.
(a) Part P (b) Part R (c) Part Q (d) No error
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Urgent" begins with a vowel sound, so the article must be "an", not "a" — Part Q is wrong. Part P "I have" and Part R are grammatically fine. The error is in Part Q.
Source: NCERT Class 9 English Grammar

55. The Ganga / is / one of the longest river / in India.
(a) Part P (b) Part R (c) Part Q (d) No error
Answer: (b)
Solution: After "one of the" the noun must be plural — "one of the longest rivers". Part R wrongly uses the singular "river". Parts P and Q are correct, so the answer is Part R.
Source: PYQ-style

56. He and me / are going / to the market / this evening.
(a) Part Q (b) Part P (c) Part R (d) No error
Answer: (b)
Solution: As subjects of the sentence we need the nominative pronoun "I", not the objective "me" — Part P should be "He and I". The verb "are going" in Part Q agrees with the plural subject. The error is in Part P.
Source: NCERT Class 8 English Grammar

57. She has been / living in this city / since five years / with her parents.
(a) Part P (b) Part Q (c) No error (d) Part R
Answer: (d)
Solution: "Since" is used with a point of time; for a duration like "five years" we use "for" — Part R is wrong. The present perfect continuous in Parts P and Q is correct. The error is in Part R.
Source: PYQ-style

58. Each of the boys / have / brought his own lunch / today.
(a) Part Q (b) Part P (c) Part R (d) No error
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Each of" takes a singular verb, so Part Q should be "has", not "have". The plural "boys" nearby tempts students toward a plural verb, but the true subject is "each". The error is in Part Q.
Source: PYQ-style

59. I am / senior than / my colleague / by two years.
(a) Part P (b) No error (c) Part Q (d) Part R
Answer: (c)
Solution: Words like senior, junior, superior and inferior take "to", not "than" — Part Q should read "senior to". This is a classic examiner trap because "than" feels natural after a comparison. The error is in Part Q.
Source: PYQ-style

60. The news / are / very shocking / indeed.
(a) Part P (b) No error (c) Part R (d) Part Q
Answer: (d)
Solution: "News" looks plural but is singular in English, so the verb must be "is" — Part Q is wrong. Parts P and R need no change. The error is in Part Q.
Source: NCERT Class 9 English Grammar

61. Neither of the two brothers / have passed / the examination / this year.
(a) No error (b) Part P (c) Part R (d) Part Q
Answer: (d)
Solution: "Neither of" takes a singular verb, so Part Q should be "has passed". The plural noun "brothers" sitting next to the verb is the trap that pushes students toward "have". The error is in Part Q.
Source: PYQ-style

62. He is / one of the best player / in our team / this season.
(a) Part P (b) No error (c) Part Q (d) Part R
Answer: (c)
Solution: After "one of the best" the noun must be plural — "players". Part Q wrongly keeps it singular. The rest of the sentence is correct, so the answer is Part Q.
Source: PYQ-style

63. The teacher asked / the students that / why they were / late for class.
(a) Part Q (b) Part P (c) Part R (d) No error
Answer: (a)
Solution: In indirect speech with a wh-word, we do not use "that" before "why" — Part Q should be just "the students why". Many students accept "that" because it appears in reported statements, which is the trap. The error is in Part Q.
Source: NCERT Class 10 English Grammar

64. My father / along with my mother / were present / at the function.
(a) Part R (b) Part Q (c) No error (d) Part P
Answer: (a)
Solution: With "along with", the verb agrees with the first subject "my father" (singular), so Part R should be "was present". The nearby plural phrase "my mother" misleads students into choosing a plural verb. The error is in Part R.
Source: PYQ-style

65. No sooner did he / enter the room / when the phone / started ringing.
(a) Part P (b) Part R (c) No error (d) Part Q
Answer: (b)
Solution: "No sooner" is paired with "than", not "when" — Part R should read "than the phone". "When" pairs with "hardly/scarcely", and mixing the two pairs is a favourite examiner trap. The error is in Part R.
Source: PYQ-style

66. She is / enough tall / to reach / the top shelf.
(a) No error (b) Part P (c) Part R (d) Part Q
Answer: (d)
Solution: "Enough" comes after the adjective it qualifies — "tall enough", so Part Q is wrong. Students often keep the Hindi word order "काफी लंबी" and miss this. The error is in Part Q.
Source: PYQ-style

67. The principal, / with all the teachers, / have gone / to attend the meeting.
(a) Part P (b) Part Q (c) Part R (d) No error
Answer: (c)
Solution: The subject is "the principal" (singular); the phrase "with all the teachers" does not change the number, so Part R should be "has gone". The plural noun just before the verb is the deliberate trap. The error is in Part R.
Source: PYQ-style

68. He denied / to help / his old friend / in need.
(a) Part P (b) Part R (c) Part Q (d) No error
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Deny" is followed by a gerund, not an infinitive — "denied helping", so Part Q is wrong. Verbs like enjoy, avoid, mind and deny all take the -ing form. The error is in Part Q.
Source: NCERT Class 10 English Grammar

69. I have seen / that movie / yesterday / with my friends.
(a) Part Q (b) Part R (c) Part P (d) No error
Answer: (c)
Solution: With a definite past time like "yesterday" we use the simple past — "I saw", so Part P is wrong. The present perfect "have seen" cannot sit with "yesterday", which is the trap here. The error is in Part P.
Source: PYQ-style

70. Bread and butter / are / his daily breakfast / these days.
(a) Part P (b) No error (c) Part R (d) Part Q
Answer: (d)
Solution: "Bread and butter" as a combined meal is treated as singular, so the verb should be "is" — Part Q is wrong. The word "and" tempts students to pick a plural verb, which is exactly the trap. The error is in Part Q.
Source: PYQ-style

71. Unless you do not / work hard, / you will not / pass the exam.
(a) Part Q (b) Part P (c) Part R (d) No error
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Unless" already carries a negative meaning, so "do not" must be dropped — Part P should read "Unless you work hard". Using a double negative feels right to Hindi speakers ("जब तक नहीं"), which is the trap. The error is in Part P.
Source: PYQ-style

72. The sceneries / of Kashmir / are very beautiful / in spring.
(a) Part Q (b) Part P (c) Part R (d) No error
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Scenery" is an uncountable noun and has no plural form — Part P should be "The scenery". The plural verb "are" in Part R then seems odd, but the real fix is the noun itself. The error is in Part P.
Source: NCERT Class 9 English Grammar

73. He is / working here / since 2015 / without any leave.
(a) No error (b) Part R (c) Part Q (d) Part P
Answer: (d)
Solution: With "since 2015" we need the present perfect continuous — "has been working" — so the helping verb in Part P must change from "is" to "has been". Part Q "working here" is fine once the auxiliary is corrected, which is why blaming Part Q is the trap. The error is in Part P.
Source: PYQ-style

74. One should / do his / duty honestly / in life.
(a) Part Q (b) Part R (c) Part P (d) No error
Answer: (a)
Solution: When the subject is "one", the possessive must also be "one's" — Part Q should read "do one's duty". Using "his" sounds acceptable in casual speech, which is why examiners set this trap. The error is in Part Q.
Source: PYQ-style

75. The police / has arrested / the thief / last night.
(a) Part Q (b) Part P (c) Part R (d) No error
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Police" is always plural in English, so the verb must be "have arrested" — Part Q is wrong. The singular-looking word "police" is the trap that pulls students toward "has". The error is in Part Q.
Source: PYQ-style
