# 📊 PYQ Analysis — Error Spotting (Basic)

## How Exams Test This Chapter

Error Spotting is a permanent fixture of SSC (CGL, CHSL, MTS, GD), RRB (NTPC, Group D), and Bank (IBPS, SBI) exams. The questions are almost always presented in one of two formats:

1. **Sentence divided into parts (a), (b), (c), (d)** — find the part containing the error.
2. **"Spot the error" with a "No error" option** — the whole sentence may be correct.

Across foundation-level exams, the same handful of grammar areas keep returning:

- **Subject–Verb Agreement** — the single most tested area. Examiners love inserting a long phrase between subject and verb ("The quality of the mangoes *were* good").
- **Articles (a/an/the)** — missing article before a singular countable noun, or wrong article before vowel/consonant sounds.
- **Tense consistency** — mixing past and present in one sentence, or using past tense after "did" ("He did not *went*").
- **Prepositions** — fixed combinations (good *at*, afraid *of*, married *to*, different *from*).
- **Pronouns** — wrong case after prepositions ("between you and *I*"), or agreement with collective nouns.
- **Nouns** — uncountable nouns made plural ("informations", "furnitures", "advices").
- **Adjective vs Adverb** — "He runs *fastly*" type traps.

## The Examiner's Favourite Traps

| Trap Type | What It Looks Like | Why Students Fall |
|---|---|---|
| **Intervening Phrase** | "The box of chocolates *were* empty." | The ear matches the verb with the nearest noun ("chocolates"), not the real subject ("box"). |
| **Collective Noun** | "The team *are* playing well." (when acting as one unit) | Students memorise "team = plural" or "team = singular" without checking whether members act together or separately. |
| **Sound-Right** | "He is more superior than me." | "Superior" already contains comparison — "more superior" sounds fine to a Hindi speaker but is wrong. |
| **Over-thinking** | A perfectly correct sentence with a "No error" option. | Students hunt so hard for an error that they invent one. Trust your rules, not your anxiety. |
| **Hindi-Transfer** | "What you are doing?" / "He is having a car." | Direct translation from Hindi word order or usage. |

## Top 10 High-Yield Question Patterns (with Solutions)

**Q1.** The quality of the apples (a) / were not (b) / good enough. (c) / No error (d)
**Answer:** (b)
**Solution:** The subject is "quality" (singular), not "apples". The phrase "of the apples" only describes the quality. Correct: "was not".
**Trap type:** Intervening Phrase.

**Q2.** One of my friends (a) / are going (b) / to Delhi tomorrow. (c) / No error (d)
**Answer:** (b)
**Solution:** After "one of + plural noun", the verb agrees with "one" (singular). Correct: "is going".
**Trap type:** Intervening Phrase.

**Q3.** He did not (a) / went to (b) / school yesterday. (c) / No error (d)
**Answer:** (b)
**Solution:** After "did/did not", always use the base form of the verb. Correct: "go to".
**Trap type:** Sound-Right (the past tense "went" feels natural after "yesterday").

**Q4.** I have (a) / many works (b) / to finish today. (c) / No error (d)
**Answer:** (b)
**Solution:** "Work" (meaning tasks/job) is uncountable. Correct: "much work" or "many pieces of work".
**Trap type:** Hindi-Transfer ("बहुत काम" feels countable).

**Q5.** She is (a) / more taller than (b) / her sister. (c) / No error (d)
**Answer:** (b)
**Solution:** Double comparative is wrong. Use either "taller than" or "much taller than", never "more taller".
**Trap type:** Sound-Right.

**Q6.** Between you (a) / and I, (b) / this plan will fail. (c) / No error (d)
**Answer:** (b)
**Solution:** After a preposition ("between"), use the object case: "between you and me".
**Trap type:** Sound-Right ("you and I" is drilled as polite, so students over-apply it).

**Q7.** The furniture in this room (a) / are (b) / very old. (c) / No error (d)
**Answer:** (b)
**Solution:** "Furniture" is uncountable and takes a singular verb. Correct: "is".
**Trap type:** Collective/Uncountable Noun.

**Q8.** He is (a) / senior than (b) / me by two years. (c) / No error (d)
**Answer:** (b)
**Solution:** Latin comparatives (senior, junior, superior, inferior, prior) take "to", not "than". Correct: "senior to".
**Trap type:** Sound-Right.

**Q9.** Each of the boys (a) / have done (b) / his homework. (c) / No error (d)
**Answer:** (b)
**Solution:** "Each of + plural noun" takes a singular verb. Correct: "has done".
**Trap type:** Intervening Phrase.

**Q10.** I am living (a) / in this city (b) / since 2015. (c) / No error (d)
**Answer:** (a)
**Solution:** With "since/for" showing duration up to now, use present perfect continuous: "I have been living".
**Trap type:** Hindi-Transfer ("मैं 2015 से रह रहा हूँ" — present continuous in Hindi).

## Applying the 15/45-Second Rule

**15-second question (Fact-based):** Q3 above — "He did not went to school." The moment you see "did not", your rule fires: base verb required. No need to read the rest twice. Spot, mark, move on. **Target: 15 seconds.**

**45-second question (Inference-based):** Q1 — "The quality of the apples were not good enough." Here you must (1) strip the sentence to its skeleton, (2) identify the true subject ("quality"), (3) check the verb against it. This takes deliberate thought. **Target: up to 45 seconds** — and it is worth every one of them, because this is exactly where the examiner separates careful readers from guessers.

## Key Takeaway

Foundation-level error spotting is a **rules game, not a vocabulary game**. If you master subject–verb agreement, articles, tense after auxiliaries, fixed prepositions, and uncountable nouns, you can solve the large majority of questions asked in SSC, Railway, and Bank exams — most of them within 15–20 seconds each.
