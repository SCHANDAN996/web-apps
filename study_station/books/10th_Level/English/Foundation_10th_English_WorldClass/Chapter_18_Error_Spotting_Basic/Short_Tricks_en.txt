# 🪄 Short Tricks + Skip Strategy — Error Spotting (Basic)

## 🧠 Memory Tricks (Mnemonics)

**Trick 1 — "SVA: The Verb Follows the REAL Subject"**
> 📦 **Rule:** Cross out everything between the subject and the verb (with, along with, as well as, together with, in addition to).
> *The captain, along with his soldiers, **was** (not were) killed.*
> 🎯 **Shortcut:** "Along with = Alone" — the verb stays with the first subject only.

**Trick 2 — "FANBOYS" for Conjunctions**
> 📦 **F**or, **A**nd, **N**or, **B**ut, **O**r, **Y**et, **S**o — these join two equal parts.
> If a sentence starts with "Although", never add "but" later.
> ❌ *Although he is rich, but he is a miser.* ✅ *Although he is rich, he is a miser.*

**Trick 3 — "Each / Every / Either / Neither = SINGULAR"**
> 📦 **Mnemonic: "E-E-E-N is One"** — Each, Every, Either, Neither always take a singular verb.
> *Neither of the two boys **was** present.*

**Trick 4 — "One of the + Plural Noun + Singular Verb"**
> 📦 *One of my **friends** **is** a doctor.*
> 🎯 Noun = plural, Verb = singular. The examiner swaps these two to trap you!

**Trick 5 — "SCARE" Words Take Singular Verb**
> 📦 **S**cenery, **C**rockery, **A**dvice, **R**esearch, **E**quipment — uncountable, no plural, no "a/an".
> ❌ *He gave me many advices.* ✅ *He gave me much advice.*

**Trick 6 — "News is Good News (Singular!)"**
> 📦 News, Physics, Mathematics, Politics, Economics — look plural, act singular.
> *The news **is** shocking.*

**Trick 7 — "Since vs For — Point vs Period"**
> 📦 **Since** = point of time (Monday, 2010, childhood). **For** = period of time (two days, five years).
> 🎯 **Mnemonic:** "Since is a dot, For is a line."

**Trick 8 — "Comparative Needs 'than', Superlative Needs 'the'"**
> 📦 *He is taller **than** me. He is **the** tallest boy.*
> ❌ Never: *more taller*, *most tallest* — double comparison is always wrong.

**Trick 9 — "Prefer, Senior, Junior, Superior → TO (not than)"**
> 📦 **Mnemonic: "PSJS loves TO"**
> *I prefer tea **to** coffee. He is senior **to** me.*

**Trick 10 — "Hardly/Scarcely → when | No sooner → than"**
> 📦 *Hardly had I reached **when** it rained. No sooner had I reached **than** it rained.*
> 🎯 **Mnemonic:** "Hardly When, Sooner Than" — examiners swap these pairs!

**Trick 11 — "Lest... should"**
> 📦 *Work hard **lest** you **should** fail.* — never use "not" after lest.
> ❌ *Lest you should not fail.*

**Trick 12 — "The + Adjective = Plural Verb"**
> 📦 *The rich **are** not always happy. The poor **need** help.*

**Trick 13 — "Hair, Bread, Furniture, Luggage, Information — No Plural"**
> 📦 **Mnemonic: "HBF-LI never multiplies"**
> ❌ *furnitures, luggages, informations* — always wrong in exams.

**Trick 14 — "Two Subjects Joined by 'or/nor' → Verb Follows the NEARER Subject"**
> 📦 *Neither the teacher nor the students **were** present.*
> 🎯 **Mnemonic:** "Verb loves its neighbour."

**Trick 15 — "It is high time + Past Tense"**
> 📦 *It is high time we **went** home.* (not "go")
> 🎯 Sounds wrong, but is grammatically right — a classic Sound-Right trap!

## ⏭️ Skip Strategy (When to Skip & Move On)

**Skip Tip 1 — The 45-Second Wall**
> If you have read the sentence twice and still cannot spot the error, **mark it and move on**. Error spotting questions are fact-based (15–20 sec); if the error isn't visible in two readings, it is testing a rule you don't remember — guessing costs marks in negative marking. Come back only if time remains.

**Skip Tip 2 — Don't Hunt for Errors That Aren't There**
> If the question has a "No error" option and the sentence passes your SVA + Tense + Preposition checklist, **choose "No error" confidently**. Over-thinking makes students invent errors in correct sentences — this is the examiner's favourite trap. One clean checklist pass is enough; a third reading breeds doubt.

**Skip Tip 3 — Eliminate First, Decide Later**
> Never read all four parts equally. Run the checklist in order: **(1) Subject-Verb match → (2) Tense consistency → (3) Article/Preposition → (4) Parallel structure**. The moment one part fails, mark it — do NOT keep reading the rest "to confirm". Confirmation reading wastes 10–15 seconds per question, which adds up to several minutes over a full English section.
