# Error Spotting (Basic) — Content

## 🔥 The Hook

In SSC GD, SSC MTS, RRB Group D and Police Constable exams, **one small grammar mistake is worth one full mark** — the same mark you earn by solving a long maths problem. The difference? A maths question takes 60 seconds. An error-spotting question takes **15 seconds** — *if* you know the rules. This chapter gives you those rules. Master them, and error spotting becomes the fastest marks in your entire paper.

## 🎯 Learning Objectives

By the end of this chapter, you will be able to:

1. Spot the error in a sentence within 15–45 seconds using a fixed checking order.
2. Apply the **12 Golden Rules** that cover most errors asked in foundation-level exams.
3. Recognise the examiner's favourite traps (intervening phrases, sound-right errors, Hindi-to-English translation errors).
4. Avoid mistakes that Hindi speakers naturally make in English.

---

## 📖 What is Error Spotting?

The examiner gives you a sentence divided into parts — usually (a), (b), (c) — and sometimes (d) "No error". One part contains a grammar mistake. Your job: **find which part is wrong**. You do NOT need to correct it (in most foundation exams) — only identify it.

Example:
> (a) One of my friend / (b) is coming / (c) to the party. / (d) No error

The error is in part (a): it should be "One of my **friends**". (Rule: "one of + plural noun".)

### The 3-Step Checking Order (use this every time)

1. **Step 1 — Subject & Verb:** Find the real subject. Does the verb match it in number (singular/plural)?
2. **Step 2 — Tense & Time words:** Look for time markers (yesterday, since, already). Does the tense match?
3. **Step 3 — Articles, Prepositions, Pronouns:** Check a/an/the, in/on/at, he/him/they/them.

Most foundation-level errors are caught in Step 1 itself.

🧠 **Active Recall:** In "One of my friend is coming", which step catches the error? What is the correct form?

## 🏆 The 12 Golden Rules (Foundation Level)

**Rule 1 — One of / Each of / Every one of + PLURAL noun + SINGULAR verb.**
✅ One of the **boys has** come. ❌ One of the boy have come.

**Rule 2 — Each, Every, Everyone, Everybody, Someone, Anyone, Nobody → SINGULAR verb.**
✅ Everybody **is** ready. ❌ Everybody are ready.

**Rule 3 — Two subjects joined by "and" → PLURAL verb.**
✅ Ram and Shyam **are** friends.
⚠️ But if the two words name ONE thing/person: ✅ Bread and butter **is** my breakfast.

**Rule 4 — Subjects joined by "either…or / neither…nor / not only…but also / or" → verb agrees with the NEARER subject.**
✅ Neither the teacher nor the **students were** present.
✅ Neither the students nor the **teacher was** present.

**Rule 5 — Intervening phrases do NOT change the subject.**
Words like *along with, together with, as well as, in addition to, besides* are traps — ignore them for verb agreement.
✅ The captain, along with his players, **was** honoured. (Subject = captain, singular.)

**Rule 6 — Collective nouns (team, committee, family, jury, audience) → usually SINGULAR.**
✅ The team **is** playing well.
(Plural only when members act separately: The jury **were** divided in their opinion.)

**Rule 7 — Uncountable nouns are always SINGULAR.**
News, information, furniture, luggage, advice, work, scenery, bread, knowledge — no plural, no "a/an".
✅ The news **is** good. ❌ The news are good. ❌ an information

**Rule 8 — Some nouns look plural but are singular:** Mathematics, Physics, Economics, Politics, Athletics (as subjects of study).
✅ Mathematics **is** difficult. ❌ Mathematics are difficult.

**Rule 9 — Some nouns are always plural (no singular form):** scissors, trousers, spectacles, goods, earnings, premises, surroundings.
✅ The scissors **are** on the table. ❌ The scissor is…

**Rule 10 — "Since" + point of time, "For" + period of time — with perfect/perfect continuous tense.**
✅ He has been working here **since** 2015 / **for** nine years.
❌ He is working here since 2015.

**Rule 11 — After "did / does / do" and modals (can, should, must, will), use the BASE form (V1) of the verb.**
✅ He did not **go**. ❌ He did not went.
✅ She can **sing**. ❌ She can sang.

**Rule 12 — Adjectives of number/quantity:** "many + countable plural", "much + uncountable"; "fewer + countable", "less + uncountable".
✅ Many students, much water, fewer mistakes, less time.

🧠 **Active Recall:** Without looking — which rule covers "The news are shocking"? Which rule covers "Neither Ram nor his brothers ___ (is/are) coming"?

### 🧠 Mnemonic: **"S-T-A-P"** — your 15-second scan
- **S** — Subject-Verb agreement
- **T** — Tense & Time words
- **A** — Articles (a/an/the)
- **P** — Prepositions & Pronouns

Scan the sentence in this order, and the error almost always reveals itself.

## ⚠️ Examiner's Trap Box

**Trap 1 — The Intervening Phrase.** The examiner puts extra words between subject and verb so you match the verb with the wrong word.
> "The quality of the mangoes **were** not good." ❌
Subject = *quality* (singular) → **was**. "Of the mangoes" is just decoration.

**Trap 2 — The Sound-Right Error.** Some wrong sentences *sound* correct because we hear them in daily speech.
> "He is more smarter than me." ❌ (double comparative — "smarter" alone is enough)
> "Return back my book." ❌ ("return" already means "give back")

**Trap 3 — The Hindi-Translation Error.** The sentence is a word-for-word translation from Hindi and feels natural to us.
> "He is having a car." ❌ → "He **has** a car." (possession verbs don't take -ing)
> "I and Ram went." ❌ → "**Ram and I** went." (politeness: first person comes last)

## 🔁 Linguistic Bridge — Hindi से English

| हम हिंदी से सोचकर कहते हैं ❌ | Correct English ✅ | Why |
|---|---|---|
| What you are doing? | What **are you** doing? | Question में helping verb subject से पहले |
| He is having two brothers. | He **has** two brothers. | Possession में -ing नहीं |
| I am agree with you. | I **agree** with you. | "agree" itself is a verb |
| He told that… (बिना object) | He **said** that… / He **told me** that… | "tell" needs an object |
| My all friends came. | **All my** friends came. | "all" comes before the possessive |
| He is more taller than me. | He is **taller** than me. | Double comparative is wrong |
| Cattle is grazing. | Cattle **are** grazing. | "cattle" is always plural |
| I and my friend went. | **My friend and I** went. | First person comes last |

🧠 **Active Recall:** Translate into correct English — "वह मुझसे सहमत है" and "मेरे पास एक साइकिल है" (without using "am agree" or "is having").

## 📊 Summary Table — Quick Revision

| Checkpoint | Rule | Example ✅ |
|---|---|---|
| One of… | plural noun + singular verb | One of the girls **has** won |
| Each/Every/Everybody | singular verb | Everybody **is** here |
| Either…or / Neither…nor | verb matches nearer subject | Neither he nor they **are** wrong |
| Along with / as well as | ignore the phrase | Ram, as well as his brothers, **is** going |
| News, information, furniture | always singular | The furniture **is** new |
| Mathematics, Physics | singular | Physics **is** tough |
| Scissors, trousers | always plural | The trousers **are** blue |
| Since / For | perfect tense | He has lived here **since** 2020 |
| did / modals | base verb (V1) | He did not **come** |
| Many/Much, Fewer/Less | countable vs uncountable | Many books, much milk |

## 🔁 Review after 2 Days

Come back after 48 hours and do this **without re-reading the chapter**:

1. Write the 12 Golden Rules from memory (just the rule, not examples).
2. Say the S-T-A-P scan order aloud.
3. Correct these three sentences: (i) "One of my friend are ill." (ii) "The news were good." (iii) "He did not went home."

Any rule you forgot — mark it, re-read only that rule, and review again after 2 more days. Spaced repetition is what moves these rules from "I read it" to "I can't get it wrong in the exam hall."
