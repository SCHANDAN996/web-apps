# 📋 Important Grammar Rules & Tips — Error Spotting (Basic)

## (a) 12 High-Yield Rules

| # | Rule | Example | Examiner's Trap |
|---|------|---------|-----------------|
| 1 | **Subject-Verb Agreement**: Verb agrees with the subject, not the words in between. | The quality of apples *was* good. | Intervening phrase trap: "The quality of apples *were*…" ❌ |
| 2 | **Each / Every / Either / Neither** take a singular verb. | Each of the boys *has* a pen. | "Each of the boys *have*…" ❌ — "boys" lures you to plural. |
| 3 | **Collective nouns** (team, jury, committee) take singular verb when acting as one unit. | The team *is* playing well. | Plural verb used because the noun "feels" plural (Sound-Right trap). |
| 4 | **One of + plural noun + singular verb.** | One of my friends *is* a doctor. | "One of my friend…" ❌ — noun must be plural, verb singular. |
| 5 | **Since vs For**: Since + point of time; For + period of time. | since Monday / for two years | "since two years" ❌ — the most repeated trap in SSC. |
| 6 | **Comparative degree**: "than" needs comparative (-er/more), not positive. | He is *taller than* me. | "more taller" ❌ — double comparative. |
| 7 | **Superlative**: use "the" before superlative; "of/in" after it. | He is *the* best player *in* the team. | Missing "the": "He is best player…" ❌ |
| 8 | **Uncountable nouns** (news, furniture, information, advice) are always singular. | The news *is* good. | "The news *are*…" ❌ — "news" looks plural, sounds plural. |
| 9 | **Scarcely/Hardly … when; No sooner … than.** | No sooner did he come *than* it rained. | "No sooner… *when*" ❌ — pair swapping trap. |
| 10 | **After "did", use base verb (V1).** | He did not *go* there. | "He did not *went*…" ❌ — double past tense. |
| 11 | **Two subjects joined by "and"** take plural verb; but joined by "as well as / along with", verb agrees with the first subject. | Ram, as well as his brothers, *is* going. | "Ram as well as his brothers *are*…" ❌ |
| 12 | **Adjective vs Adverb**: Adverb modifies a verb/adjective; adjective modifies a noun. | She sings *beautifully*. | "She sings *beautiful*." ❌ — Sound-Right trap for Hindi speakers. |

## (b) Time Management — The 15/45-Second Rule

| Type | What it looks like | Time | Strategy |
|------|-------------------|------|----------|
| **Fact-based (15 sec)** | Direct rule application: subject-verb agreement, since/for, did + V1, one of + plural noun. | 15 sec | Spot the keyword (each, since, did, one of) → apply the rule instantly → mark and move. Never re-read. |
| **Inference-based (45 sec)** | Long sentences with intervening phrases, collective nouns, correlative pairs, or "find the part with error" where the error hides mid-sentence. | 45 sec | Strip the sentence to Subject + Verb + Object. Ignore phrases between commas. Check the pair words (no sooner/than). |

**Golden rule:** If a question is fact-based and you cross 20 seconds, you are over-thinking — your first rule-based answer is almost always right.

## (c) Morphology — Root, Affix, Example

Knowing word parts helps you spot wrong word forms (e.g., adjective used where adverb is needed).

| Root / Affix | Meaning | Example Word | Error-Spotting Use |
|--------------|---------|--------------|--------------------|
| **-ly** (suffix) | makes an adverb | quick → quickly | "He ran *quick*." ❌ → quickly |
| **-ness** (suffix) | makes a noun | happy → happiness | "Her *happy* was clear." ❌ → happiness |
| **-tion / -sion** (suffix) | makes a noun | decide → decision | "Take a *decide*." ❌ → decision |
| **un- / in- / dis-** (prefix) | not, opposite | un + able = unable | Wrong negative: "*unpossible*" ❌ → impossible |
| **-er / -est** (suffix) | comparative / superlative | tall → taller → tallest | "more taller" ❌ — never double the comparative |
| **-ful / -less** (suffix) | full of / without | care → careful / careless | Confusing opposites changes meaning — read carefully |
| **re-** (prefix) | again | re + write = rewrite | "He *re-wrote again*." ❌ — double meaning |

**Quick check habit:** In every error question, ask — *Is this word the right form (noun/verb/adjective/adverb) for its position in the sentence?*
