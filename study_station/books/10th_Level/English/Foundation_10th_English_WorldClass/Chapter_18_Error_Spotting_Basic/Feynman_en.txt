# 🧒 Feynman Technique — Error Spotting (Basic)

## The Hardest Concept, Explained Simply: Subject–Verb Agreement

Imagine you are 12 years old and you have a pet dog named Tommy.

Now look at these two sentences:

- ✅ **Tommy runs fast.**
- ❌ **Tommy run fast.**

You can *feel* the second one is wrong, right? That feeling is your ear catching a grammar error. Error Spotting is nothing but training this feeling so it works even in long, tricky exam sentences.

### The Daily-Life Example

Think of a **subject and verb as best friends who must match their clothes**:

- One friend (singular subject) → wears ONE badge → verb takes **-s** → *He **plays**.*
- Many friends (plural subject) → no badge → verb stays plain → *They **play**.*

So the rule is just: **count your friends first, then dress the verb.**

### Where the Examiner Tricks You

The examiner hides your "friend" behind a crowd:

> ❌ *The quality **of these mangoes** were good.*

Your eyes see "mangoes" (many) and shout *were*! But wait — who is the real subject? **Quality** (one thing). The mangoes are just standing in between, like a crowd blocking your view.

> ✅ *The quality of these mangoes **was** good.*

### The Unforgettable Trick: 🎯 "Chop the Middle"

Whenever a sentence feels confusing, **chop out everything between the subject and the verb** and read what remains:

- *The quality ~~of these mangoes~~ was good.* ✔️
- *The boys ~~who live near my house~~ play cricket.* ✔️

If the chopped sentence sounds right, the original is right. If it sounds broken, you found the error!

### The 3-Step Detective Method (use on every question)

1. **Find the real subject** — ask "who/what is doing the action?"
2. **Chop the middle** — ignore phrases like *of…, with…, along with…, as well as…*
3. **Match the verb** — singular friend = verb with *-s*; plural friends = plain verb.
