76. Spot the error: (P) One of my friend / (Q) who lives in Delhi / (R) has invited me / (S) to his wedding.
(a) Part Q (b) Part P (c) Part R (d) No error
Answer: (b)
Solution: Part P tempts because "one of my friend" sounds natural in speech, but after "one of" the noun must be plural — "one of my friends". Parts Q, R and S are grammatically correct, so the answer is Part P.
Source: PYQ-style

77. Spot the error: (P) The principal, along with the teachers, / (Q) were present / (R) at the annual function / (S) held last week.
(a) Part R (b) Part P (c) No error (d) Part Q
Answer: (d)
Solution: Part Q is the trap — "teachers" sits just before the verb, so "were" feels right, but with "along with" the verb agrees with the first subject "the principal" (singular), so it should be "was". The intervening phrase does not change the subject, so the answer is Part Q.
Source: PYQ-style

78. Spot the error: (P) Neither of the two boys / (Q) have done / (R) his homework / (S) on time.
(a) Part Q (b) No error (c) Part R (d) Part P
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Neither of" takes a singular verb, so Part Q should read "has done", not "have done". Part R correctly uses the singular "his", confirming the singular sense, so the answer is Part Q.
Source: PYQ-style

79. Spot the error: (P) She is / (Q) more wiser / (R) than her / (S) elder sister.
(a) Part P (b) Part Q (c) Part R (d) No error
Answer: (b)
Solution: Double comparatives are wrong — "wiser" is already comparative, so "more wiser" should be just "wiser". Learners pick Part R thinking "elder" is wrong, but "elder sister" is correct usage, so the answer is Part Q.
Source: PYQ-style

80. Spot the error: (P) I have been / (Q) living in this city / (R) since five years / (S) with my family.
(a) Part P (b) Part R (c) Part Q (d) No error
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Since" is used with a point of time (since 2019), while a period of time takes "for" — "for five years". The present perfect continuous in Part P is correct, so the answer is Part R.

81. Spot the error: (P) The Ganges / (Q) is one of the / (R) longest river / (S) in India.
(a) Part R (b) Part P (c) No error (d) Part Q
Answer: (a)
Solution: After "one of the" the noun must be plural — "one of the longest rivers". Part P is correct because holy rivers take "the", so the answer is Part R.

82. Spot the error: (P) He denied / (Q) to help me / (R) although I had / (S) asked him politely.
(a) Part Q (b) Part P (c) Part R (d) No error
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Deny" is followed by a gerund, not an infinitive — "denied helping me". "Refused to help" would be correct, which is why the infinitive tempts, so the answer is Part Q.

(a) Part P (b) Part Q (c) No error (d) Part R
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Each of" takes a singular verb — "was given". The plural "students" right before the verb is the examiner's trap, so the answer is Part Q.

84. Spot the error: (P) No sooner did / (Q) the bell ring / (R) when the students / (S) rushed out of the class.
(a) Part R (b) Part Q (c) Part P (d) No error
Answer: (a)
Solution: "No sooner" pairs with "than", not "when" — "no sooner did the bell ring than the students rushed out". "Hardly" and "scarcely" pair with "when", which causes the confusion, so the answer is Part R.
Source: PYQ-style

85. Spot the error: (P) My elder brother / (Q) is senior than me / (R) by three years / (S) in service.
(a) Part R (b) Part P (c) No error (d) Part Q
Answer: (d)
Solution: Latin comparatives like senior, junior, superior and inferior take "to", not "than" — "senior to me". Part P is fine because "elder brother" is correct, so the answer is Part Q.
Source: PYQ-style

86. Spot the error: (P) The teacher asked / (Q) the students that / (R) why they were / (S) late for the class.
(a) Part R (b) No error (c) Part Q (d) Part P
Answer: (c)
Solution: In indirect speech with a wh-word, "that" is not used — "asked the students why they were late". The conjunction "that" in Part Q is superfluous, so the answer is Part Q.
Source: PYQ-style

87. Spot the error: (P) Scarcely he had / (Q) reached the station / (R) when the train / (S) started moving.
(a) Part Q (b) No error (c) Part R (d) Part P
Answer: (d)
Solution: When a negative adverb like "scarcely" begins a sentence, inversion is required — "Scarcely had he reached". Part R correctly uses "when" with "scarcely", so the answer is Part P.
Source: PYQ-style

88. Spot the error: (P) He is / (Q) enough tall / (R) to reach / (S) the top shelf.
(a) Part R (b) Part P (c) No error (d) Part Q
Answer: (d)
Solution: "Enough" comes after an adjective but before a noun — "tall enough". Hindi word order ("पर्याप्त लंबा") misleads learners here, so the answer is Part Q.
Source: PYQ-style

89. Spot the error: (P) Bread and butter / (Q) are his favourite / (R) breakfast every / (S) single morning.
(a) Part P (b) Part Q (c) Part R (d) No error
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Bread and butter" is a single combined dish, so it takes a singular verb — "is his favourite breakfast". Two nouns joined by "and" are plural only when they are separate things, so the answer is Part Q.
Source: PYQ-style

90. Spot the error: (P) I and Ravi / (Q) went to / (R) the market / (S) yesterday evening.
(a) Part R (b) Part Q (c) Part P (d) No error
Answer: (c)
Solution: English politeness rule puts the first person last — "Ravi and I". Hindi speakers say "मैं और रवि", which causes this classic error, so the answer is Part P.

91. Spot the error: (P) The furniture in this room / (Q) are very old / (R) and needs / (S) to be replaced.
(a) Part P (b) Part Q (c) Part R (d) No error
Answer: (b)
Solution: "Furniture" is an uncountable noun and always takes a singular verb — "is very old". Part R correctly uses the singular "needs", which confirms it, so the answer is Part Q.

92. Spot the error: (P) He told me / (Q) that he will / (R) come to the party / (S) the next day.
(a) Part P (b) No error (c) Part R (d) Part Q
Answer: (d)
Solution: In reported speech after a past-tense reporting verb, "will" changes to "would" — "that he would come". Part S correctly uses "the next day", so the answer is Part Q.

93. Spot the error: (P) Unless you do not / (Q) work hard, / (R) you will not / (S) pass the examination.
(a) Part P (b) Part R (c) Part Q (d) No error
Answer: (a)
Solution: "Unless" is already negative in meaning, so "do not" makes a double negative — it should be "Unless you work hard". This is a very common examiner trap, so the answer is Part P.

Solution: "Not only... but also" is correctly balanced, and the inversion "did he lose" after "not only" at the start is right. Every part is grammatically sound, so the answer is No error.

98. Spot the error: (P) Hardly had I / (Q) entered the room / (R) than the phone / (S) started ringing.
(a) No error (b) Part P (c) Part Q (d) Part R
Answer: (d)
Solution: "Hardly" pairs with "when", not "than" — "Hardly had I entered the room when the phone started ringing". "Than" belongs with "no sooner", and mixing the two pairs is a favourite examiner trap, so the answer is Part R.

99. Spot the error: (P) Being a rainy day, / (Q) I decided / (R) to stay at home / (S) and read a book.
(a) Part R (b) Part Q (c) Part P (d) No error
Answer: (c)
Solution: This is a dangling modifier — "being a rainy day" cannot describe "I"; it should be "It being a rainy day, I decided...". The rest of the sentence is correct, so the answer is Part P.

100. Spot the error: (P) I wish / (Q) I was / (R) a millionaire / (S) and could travel the world.
(a) Part P (b) Part R (c) Part Q (d) Part S
Answer: (c)
Solution: After "I wish", an unreal or imaginary situation takes the subjunctive "were", not "was" — "I wish I were a millionaire". Part P tempts because "wish" feels informal, but it is perfectly correct here, so the answer is Part Q.
