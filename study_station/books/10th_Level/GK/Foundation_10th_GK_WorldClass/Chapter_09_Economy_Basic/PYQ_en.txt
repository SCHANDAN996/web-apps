📊 PYQ Pattern Analysis: Indian Economy (Basic)

Exams this chapter prepares you for: SSC GD Constable, SSC MTS, RRB Group D, and State Police/Constable exams. This section describes how these exams ask questions from this chapter — which sub-topics keep coming back and which traps they use. It is a pattern guide, not a count of past questions.

1. How the Questions Come (Pattern by Exam Type)
Exam type	What they usually ask	Style
SSC GD / SSC MTS	Full forms (GDP, CRR, SLR, GST, NITI), RBI dates, who issues the ₹1 note, HQ of WTO/IMF	One-liners, direct recall
RRB Group D	Sectors of the economy, Five-Year Plan focus, GST launch, banking terms	Direct factual, sometimes "which is NOT"
State Police / Constable	Planning Commission and NITI Aayog, poverty and unemployment types, government schemes with year	Factual, mixed with state-level GK
All of the above	Simple formulas (GNP, NNP, primary deficit), repo rate effect on inflation	Short calculation or cause-effect

2. Sub-Topics That Keep Returning (Qualitative Priority)
Sub-Topic	Priority	Why it matters
RBI: dates, functions, ₹1 note, repo/CRR/SLR	Very High	Easiest to frame as one-liners; traps on years and "all notes"
Sectors (primary/secondary/tertiary), organised/unorganised	High	Straight from NCERT Class 10; classify-the-job questions
National income terms (GDP, GNP, NDP, NNP, per capita income)	High	Definition and simple formula questions
Planning: Planning Commission, NITI Aayog, Five-Year Plans	High	Dates and "which plan focused on what"
Budget and taxes: GST, direct/indirect tax, deficits, Articles	High	GST facts and "which is NOT a direct tax"
Inflation: WPI/CPI, types	Medium	Definition traps (deflation vs disinflation)
1991 reforms (LPG), FEMA, WTO	Medium	Full forms and years
Poverty line, unemployment types, MGNREGA	Medium	Situation-based identification
International bodies and their HQ	Medium	Pair-matching questions
Stock market: SEBI, BSE, Sensex/Nifty	Low–Medium	Occasional one-liners

3. Favourite Traps of Examiners
Trap type	How it appears in this chapter
Almost True Statement	"All currency notes are issued by RBI" (₹1 note is the exception); "Planning Commission was a constitutional body"
Similar Name/Year	RBI nationalised 1949 vs banks nationalised 1969; WTO Geneva vs IMF Washington; CRR vs SLR
Reverse Causation	"RBI cuts repo rate to control inflation"; "largest GDP sector is also largest employer"
Chronological Confusion	Mahalanobis model in 1st Plan (actually 2nd); FERA replacing FEMA (reverse is true)
Negative Phrasing	"Which of the following is NOT a direct tax / NOT a function of RBI?"

4. Top 10 High-Yield Questions with Solutions and Trap Analysis

Question 1
In which year was the Reserve Bank of India nationalised?
(A) 1935 (B) 1949 (C) 1969 (D) 1980
Answer: (B) 1949
Explanation: RBI was set up on 1 April 1935 and nationalised on 1 January 1949. 1969 and 1980 are years of commercial bank nationalisation.
Trap Type: Similar Name/Year — 1969 is the most tempting wrong option.
15/45-sec Rule: 15-second fact. Use the chain "35–49–69–80".
Source: PYQ-style

Question 2
Who signs the ₹1 currency note in India?
(A) Governor, RBI (B) Finance Minister (C) Finance Secretary (D) Prime Minister
Answer: (C) Finance Secretary
Explanation: The ₹1 note is issued by the Government of India and carries the Finance Secretary's signature; all other notes carry the RBI Governor's signature.
Trap Type: Almost True Statement — "RBI Governor signs all notes".
15/45-sec Rule: 15-second fact.
Source: PYQ-style

Question 3
To control inflation, the RBI is most likely to:
(A) cut the repo rate (B) raise the repo rate (C) cut the CRR (D) buy government securities
Answer: (B) raise the repo rate
Explanation: A higher repo rate makes bank loans costlier, reduces money in the market and cools prices. Options A, C and D all add money to the market.
Trap Type: Reverse Causation.
15/45-sec Rule: 45-second analytical — run the chain "Repo ↑ → loans costly → spending ↓ → inflation ↓".
Source: PYQ-style

Question 4
Banking, transport and communication belong to which sector?
(A) Primary (B) Secondary (C) Tertiary (D) Quaternary
Answer: (C) Tertiary
Explanation: These activities do not produce goods but support production; NCERT calls this the tertiary or service sector.
Trap Type: Almost True Statement — transport "moves goods", so students wrongly pick secondary.
15/45-sec Rule: 15-second fact.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Economics (Sectors of the Indian Economy)

Question 5
NITI Aayog replaced which body?
(A) Finance Commission (B) National Development Council (C) Planning Commission (D) Inter-State Council
Answer: (C) Planning Commission
Explanation: NITI Aayog was set up on 1 January 2015 in place of the Planning Commission (1950).
Trap Type: Similar Name — NDC and Finance Commission sound related.
15/45-sec Rule: 15-second fact.
Source: PYQ-style

Question 6
GST was implemented in India from:
(A) 1 April 2017 (B) 1 July 2017 (C) 1 January 2017 (D) 8 November 2016
Answer: (B) 1 July 2017
Explanation: GST was rolled out from 1 July 2017 under the 101st Constitutional Amendment Act, 2016. 1 April is the start of the financial year; 8 November 2016 was demonetisation.
Trap Type: Chronological Confusion.
15/45-sec Rule: 15-second fact.
Source: PYQ-style

Question 7
Which of the following is NOT a direct tax?
(A) Income tax (B) Corporation tax (C) Goods and Services Tax (D) Tax on capital gains
Answer: (C) Goods and Services Tax
Explanation: GST is an indirect tax because its burden is passed on to the buyer; the other three are paid by the person on whom they are levied.
Trap Type: Negative Phrasing.
15/45-sec Rule: 15-second fact, but read "NOT" twice.
Source: PYQ-style

Question 8
If GDP of a country is ₹1,000 crore and net factor income from abroad is −₹50 crore, its GNP is:
(A) ₹1,050 crore (B) ₹1,000 crore (C) ₹950 crore (D) ₹900 crore
Answer: (C) ₹950 crore
Explanation: GNP = GDP + NFIA = 1,000 + (−50) = 950.
Trap Type: Almost True Statement — adding 50 without noticing the minus sign gives 1,050.
15/45-sec Rule: 45-second analytical — write the formula, then substitute with sign.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Macroeconomics

Question 9
Five members of a family work on a small farm where three workers are enough. This is an example of:
(A) Seasonal unemployment (B) Disguised unemployment (C) Frictional unemployment (D) Structural unemployment
Answer: (B) Disguised unemployment
Explanation: Extra workers add nothing to output; NCERT calls this disguised (hidden) unemployment, common in agriculture.
Trap Type: Similar Name — seasonal unemployment is also rural, but it is about months without work.
15/45-sec Rule: 45-second analytical — read the situation.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Economics (People as Resource)

Question 10
The headquarters of the World Trade Organization is at:
(A) Washington, D.C. (B) New York (C) Geneva (D) Paris
Answer: (C) Geneva
Explanation: WTO (1 January 1995) is headquartered in Geneva. IMF and World Bank are in Washington, D.C.
Trap Type: Similar Name — Washington is the home of the other two economic bodies.
15/45-sec Rule: 15-second fact.
Source: PYQ-style

5. Strategy Summary
Fact questions (dates, full forms, HQ, Articles): answer in 15 seconds; these are score maximizers.
Formula and cause-effect questions (GNP, deficits, repo effect): give 45 seconds; write the formula before choosing.
Any option with "all", "every", "only" or "never": stop and check for the exception (₹1 note, CRR vs SLR).
