Set 4/6 – Indian Economy (Basic) MCQs (Foundation)
Questions 76–100: Medium

76. Which of the following is a feature of the organised sector?
(a) Paid leave and provident fund for workers (b) No fixed working hours (c) No registration with the government (d) Daily wages with no job security
Answer: (a)
Solution: Organised-sector units are registered and follow rules, giving benefits like paid leave and provident fund. The other three describe the unorganised sector.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Economics

77. Which of the following is an example of the public sector?
(a) Indian Railways (b) A family-owned grocery shop (c) A private software company (d) A privately owned bus service
Answer: (a)
Solution: Indian Railways is owned and run by the government, so it is in the public sector. The others are owned by individuals or companies.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Economics

78. The word "Socialist" was added to the Preamble of the Indian Constitution by which Amendment?
(a) 24th Amendment (b) 42nd Amendment (c) 44th Amendment (d) 52nd Amendment
Answer: (b)
Solution: The words "Socialist" and "Secular" were added to the Preamble by the 42nd Amendment, 1976.
Source: Constitution of India

79. What type of economy did India adopt after Independence?
(a) Capitalist economy (b) Socialist economy (c) Mixed economy (d) Barter economy
Answer: (c)
Solution: India chose a mixed economy, where the public sector and the private sector work side by side.
Source: PYQ-style

80. Inflation caused by a rise in the cost of production, such as fuel prices and wages, is called:
(a) Cost-push inflation (b) Demand-pull inflation (c) Disinflation (d) Deflation
Answer: (a)
Solution: When production costs rise, producers push prices up — cost-push inflation. Demand-pull comes from excess demand.
Source: PYQ-style

81. "Too much money chasing too few goods" describes:
(a) Demand-pull inflation (b) Cost-push inflation (c) Deflation (d) Stagflation
Answer: (a)
Solution: Excess money with buyers and limited goods pull prices up — demand-pull inflation.
Source: PYQ-style

82. The Phillips curve shows the relationship between:
(a) Inflation and unemployment (b) Tax rate and tax revenue (c) Income and its distribution among people (d) Demand and supply of money
Answer: (a)
Solution: The Phillips curve shows an inverse relation between inflation and unemployment in the short run. Tax rate and revenue is the Laffer curve — a similar-name trap.
Source: PYQ-style

83. The Laffer curve shows the relationship between:
(a) Tax rate and tax revenue (b) Inflation and unemployment (c) Income and inequality (d) Money supply and interest rate
Answer: (a)
Solution: The Laffer curve shows how tax revenue changes as the tax rate changes; very high rates can reduce revenue.
Source: PYQ-style

84. The Lorenz curve and the Gini coefficient are used to measure:
(a) Inequality of income (b) Rate of inflation (c) Rate of unemployment (d) Growth of GDP
Answer: (a)
Solution: Both measure how unequally income is distributed among people.
Source: PYQ-style

85. Revenue deficit is equal to:
(a) Revenue expenditure minus revenue receipts (b) Total expenditure minus total receipts (c) Fiscal deficit minus interest payments (d) Capital expenditure minus capital receipts
Answer: (a)
Solution: Revenue deficit = Revenue expenditure − Revenue receipts. Option (c) is primary deficit.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Macroeconomics

86. Which of the following is a capital receipt of the government?
(a) Income tax collected (b) Borrowings by the government (c) Dividends received from public sector companies (d) GST collected
Answer: (b)
Solution: Borrowings create a liability, so they are capital receipts. Taxes and dividends are revenue receipts (tax and non-tax).
Source: NCERT Class 12 Macroeconomics

87. Disinvestment means:
(a) The government selling its shares in public sector enterprises (b) The government buying shares in private companies (c) The RBI selling its gold (d) Foreign companies leaving India
Answer: (a)
Solution: Disinvestment is the sale of government equity in public sector enterprises; it is a capital receipt and part of privatisation.
Source: PYQ-style

88. In which year was the Fiscal Responsibility and Budget Management (FRBM) Act enacted?
(a) 1991 (b) 1999 (c) 2003 (d) 2017
Answer: (c)
Solution: The FRBM Act was passed to bring fiscal discipline and limit deficits. It was enacted in 2003.
Source: PYQ-style

89. Since 2017, the Union Budget is generally presented on:
(a) The last working day of February (b) 1 February (c) 1 April (d) 1 March
Answer: (b)
Solution: From 2017 the Budget date moved from the last working day of February to 1 February, so that it can be passed before the financial year starts.
Source: PIB (Government of India)

90. The Economic Survey of India is prepared by:
(a) Department of Economic Affairs, Ministry of Finance (b) Reserve Bank of India (c) NITI Aayog (d) Comptroller and Auditor General
Answer: (a)
Solution: The Economic Survey is prepared by the Department of Economic Affairs under the Chief Economic Adviser and is presented before the Budget.
Source: PYQ-style

91. A tax whose rate rises as income rises is called:
(a) Progressive tax (b) Proportional tax (c) Regressive tax (d) Indirect tax
Answer: (a)
Solution: In a progressive tax, people with higher income pay a higher rate, as in income tax. A regressive tax takes a bigger share from the poor.
Source: PYQ-style

92. GST in India is a:
(a) Origin-based tax (b) Destination-based tax (c) Direct tax (d) Tax on income
Answer: (b)
Solution: GST is a destination-based consumption tax — it goes to the State where goods or services are consumed. It is an indirect tax, not a direct tax.
Source: PIB (Government of India)

93. IGST is levied on:
(a) Supplies from one State to another (inter-State) (b) Supplies within the same State only (c) Income of companies (d) Agricultural land
Answer: (a)
Solution: Integrated GST (IGST) is levied on inter-State supply. Within a State, CGST and SGST are levied together.
Source: PIB (Government of India)

94. A foreign company sets up and manages its own factory in India. This is an example of:
(a) Foreign Direct Investment (FDI) (b) Foreign Portfolio Investment (FPI) (c) External Commercial Borrowing (d) Remittance
Answer: (a)
Solution: FDI is long-term investment with control over the business. FPI is only buying shares or bonds and can leave quickly.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Economics

95. The current account of the Balance of Payments includes:
(a) Trade in goods and services and remittances (b) Foreign Direct Investment (c) Foreign Portfolio Investment (d) External loans
Answer: (a)
Solution: The current account records trade in goods and services and transfers like remittances. FDI, FPI and loans are in the capital account.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Macroeconomics

96. The World Trade Organization (WTO) replaced:
(a) GATT (b) IMF (c) UNCTAD (d) OECD
Answer: (a)
Solution: The WTO came into being on 1 January 1995, replacing the General Agreement on Tariffs and Trade (GATT) of 1947.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Economics

97. Unemployment caused by a mismatch between workers' skills and available jobs, due to a change in the structure of the economy, is called:
(a) Structural unemployment (b) Frictional unemployment (c) Seasonal unemployment (d) Disguised unemployment
Answer: (a)
Solution: Structural unemployment arises when the economy changes and old skills no longer match new jobs.
Source: PYQ-style

98. A person leaves one job and spends a few weeks searching for a better one. This is:
(a) Frictional unemployment (b) Disguised unemployment (c) Seasonal unemployment (d) Structural unemployment
Answer: (a)
Solution: A short gap while moving between jobs is frictional unemployment. It is temporary and normal in any economy.
Source: PYQ-style

99. The Minimum Support Price (MSP) for crops is announced by the government on the recommendation of:
(a) Commission for Agricultural Costs and Prices (CACP) (b) Food Corporation of India (c) NABARD (d) Reserve Bank of India
Answer: (a)
Solution: CACP recommends MSPs; the government announces them. FCI procures and stores grain — the similar-role trap.
Source: PYQ-style

100. The Yellow Revolution is related to the production of:
(a) Oilseeds (b) Fish (c) Milk (d) Eggs
Answer: (a)
Solution: Yellow Revolution = oilseeds. Fish is the Blue Revolution and milk is the White Revolution.
Source: PYQ-style
