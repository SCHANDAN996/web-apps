126. NNP at market price is ₹5,000 crore, indirect taxes are ₹600 crore and subsidies are ₹100 crore. What is the national income (NNP at factor cost)?
(a) ₹5,500 crore (b) ₹4,300 crore (c) ₹4,500 crore (d) ₹5,700 crore
Answer: (c)
Solution: Factor cost = Market price − Indirect taxes + Subsidies. Subtracting subsidies too is the trap. National income = 5,000 − 600 + 100 = ₹4,500 crore.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Macroeconomics

127. A country's GDP is ₹3,000 crore, net factor income from abroad is −₹100 crore and depreciation is ₹250 crore. What is its NNP?
(a) ₹2,850 crore (b) ₹2,750 crore (c) ₹3,150 crore (d) ₹2,650 crore
Answer: (d)
Solution: GNP = 3,000 + (−100) = 2,900. Ignoring the minus sign is the trap. NNP = 2,900 − 250 = ₹2,650 crore.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Macroeconomics

128. The national income of a country is ₹50,000 crore and its population is 25 crore. What is its per capita income?
(a) ₹200 (b) ₹2,000 (c) ₹20,000 (d) ₹1,250
Answer: (b)
Solution: Per capita income = National income ÷ Population. Both figures are in crore, so the crores cancel: 50,000 ÷ 25 = ₹2,000.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Economics

129. A government's total expenditure is ₹1,500 crore, revenue receipts are ₹900 crore, non-debt capital receipts are ₹100 crore and interest payments are ₹250 crore. What is the primary deficit?
(a) ₹200 crore (b) ₹250 crore (c) ₹500 crore (d) ₹750 crore
Answer: (b)
Solution: Fiscal deficit = 1,500 − (900 + 100) = 500. Stopping at the fiscal deficit is the trap. Primary deficit = 500 − 250 = ₹250 crore.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Macroeconomics

130. Assertion: The RBI raises the repo rate to control inflation.
Reason: A higher repo rate makes bank credit costlier and reduces the money supply.
Choose the correct option.
(a) Both Assertion and Reason are true, and Reason is the correct explanation of Assertion (b) Both are true, but Reason is not the correct explanation of Assertion (c) Assertion is true, but Reason is false (d) Assertion is false, but Reason is true
Answer: (a)
Solution: A higher repo rate raises the cost of loans, people borrow and spend less, and price pressure falls. The Reason directly explains the Assertion.
Source: RBI official website

131. Consider the following statements:
I. An increase in the repo rate tends to reduce money supply.
II. Purchase of government securities by the RBI reduces money supply.
III. An increase in CRR reduces the lending capacity of banks.
Which of the statements given are correct?
(a) I and II only (b) I and III only (c) II and III only (d) I, II and III
Answer: (b)
Solution: When the RBI buys securities it pays money into the market, so money supply rises; statement II is false. Statements I and III are correct.
Source: RBI official website

Solution: The Union Budget is prepared by the Ministry of Finance and presented by the Finance Minister under Article 112. NITI Aayog is a policy think tank.

133. Which of the following steps by the RBI will NOT increase money supply in the economy?
(a) Cutting the repo rate (b) Buying government securities in the open market (c) Reducing the CRR (d) Raising the SLR
Answer: (d)
Solution: A higher SLR forces banks to lock more funds in liquid assets, leaving less to lend. The other three steps add money to the market.
Source: RBI official website

137. In a year, nominal GDP rises by about 10% and the general price level also rises by about 10%. What happens to real GDP?
(a) It rises by about 20% (b) It falls by about 10% (c) It remains roughly unchanged (d) It rises by about 10%
Answer: (c)
Solution: Real GDP removes the effect of price change. If the whole rise in nominal GDP is due to prices, real output has hardly changed.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Macroeconomics

139. Which of the following is a constitutional body?
(a) NITI Aayog (b) Planning Commission (c) National Development Council (d) Finance Commission
Answer: (d)
Solution: The Finance Commission is created under Article 280 of the Constitution. NITI Aayog, the Planning Commission and the NDC were set up by executive resolutions.

140. In disguised unemployment, the marginal productivity of the extra workers is:
(a) Equal to the wage rate (b) Zero (c) Greater than average productivity (d) Always negative
Answer: (b)
Solution: Removing the extra workers does not reduce total output, which means their marginal productivity is zero (or close to zero).
Source: NCERT Class 9 Economics

141. The fiscal deficit of the government indicates:
(a) The total borrowing requirement of the government (b) Only the shortfall in revenue account (c) Only the interest payments due (d) The trade deficit of the country
Answer: (a)
Solution: Fiscal deficit = Total expenditure − (Revenue receipts + Non-debt capital receipts); this gap must be met by borrowing. Option (b) describes revenue deficit.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Macroeconomics

144. A country's GDP is ₹800 crore. Its citizens working abroad earn ₹60 crore, while foreigners working in the country earn ₹90 crore. What is its GNP?
(a) ₹830 crore (b) ₹770 crore (c) ₹860 crore (d) ₹710 crore
Answer: (b)
Solution: NFIA = 60 − 90 = −30. Adding both incomes is the trap. GNP = 800 + (−30) = ₹770 crore.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Macroeconomics

147. Which combination of RBI actions would most effectively reduce inflation?
(a) Raise the repo rate and sell government securities (b) Cut the repo rate and buy government securities (c) Cut the CRR and cut the repo rate (d) Raise the repo rate and buy government securities
Answer: (a)
Solution: Both raising the repo rate and selling securities pull money out of the market. In option (d) the two actions work against each other.
Source: RBI official website

148. GDP at market price is ₹1,000 crore, indirect taxes are ₹120 crore and subsidies are ₹20 crore. What is GDP at factor cost?
(a) ₹1,100 crore (b) ₹880 crore (c) ₹900 crore (d) ₹1,140 crore
Answer: (c)
Solution: Net indirect taxes = 120 − 20 = 100. Ignoring subsidies gives 880, the trap. GDP at factor cost = 1,000 − 100 = ₹900 crore.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Macroeconomics

150. A government's revenue receipts are ₹1,200 crore, revenue expenditure is ₹1,500 crore, capital expenditure is ₹500 crore and non-debt capital receipts are ₹200 crore. What is the fiscal deficit?
(a) ₹300 crore (b) ₹600 crore (c) ₹800 crore (d) ₹500 crore
Answer: (b)
Solution: Total expenditure = 1,500 + 500 = 2,000. Revenue deficit alone would be 300, the trap. Fiscal deficit = 2,000 − (1,200 + 200) = ₹600 crore.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Macroeconomics
