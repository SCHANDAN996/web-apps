🧒 Feynman Technique: Why does RBI RAISE the repo rate to bring prices DOWN?

This is the idea that confuses students most in this chapter. It feels upside down: to make things cheaper, RBI makes loans costlier? Let's make it so simple that a 12-year-old can teach it to the class.

Step 1: Picture a water tank

Think of the whole economy as a village with one big water tank — the RBI. Every house (bank) gets water (money) from this tank through a tap. The price of the tap water is the repo rate — the rate at which RBI lends short-term money to banks.

Step 2: Too much water = flood

When there is too much money in people's hands but the same amount of goods in shops, everyone wants to buy the same things. Shopkeepers see long queues and raise prices. This is inflation — "too much money chasing too few goods".

Step 3: Make the tap water costlier

RBI raises the repo rate. Now banks pay more to get money from RBI. So banks also charge you more interest on a home loan, bike loan or shop loan. People borrow less and spend less. Less money reaches the market. Shopkeepers no longer see crazy queues, so prices stop rising fast.

Step 4: The opposite case

When business is slow and factories are idle, RBI cuts the repo rate. Loans become cheaper, people borrow and spend more, factories get orders, and jobs grow.

The daily-life example

Your mother gives you pocket money every week. If she suddenly doubles it, you and all your friends rush to the school canteen. The canteen uncle sees the rush and raises the samosa price. Now suppose your mother says, "Pocket money is now a loan, and you must return it with extra." You will spend carefully. The rush ends, and the samosa price settles. Your mother just acted like the RBI!

The same logic works for other tools:

CRR raised → banks must park more cash with RBI → less money to lend → prices cool down.

RBI sells government securities (OMO) → people pay money to RBI → money leaves the market → prices cool down.

The unforgettable trick

"Repo UP, Rupee in pocket DOWN, Rate of prices DOWN."
Or just remember the arrow chain: Repo ↑ → Loans costly ↑ → Spending ↓ → Inflation ↓

And one more line to beat the trap: "To fight inflation, RBI tightens the tap — it never opens it."

Who does what?

RBI does this through monetary policy; the Monetary Policy Committee (6 members, formed in 2016) decides the repo rate. The government's tax-and-spend plan is fiscal policy — a different tool in a different hand.
