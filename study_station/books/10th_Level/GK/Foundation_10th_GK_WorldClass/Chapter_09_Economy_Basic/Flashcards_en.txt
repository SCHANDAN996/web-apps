Flashcard Deck: Indian Economy (Basic) (Foundation)

Card 1
Front: A Japanese company makes cars in a factory in Gujarat. Does the value of those cars go into India's GDP or Japan's GDP?
Back: India's GDP. GDP counts production inside a country's borders, whoever owns the factory.

Card 2
Front: Your bank clerk, your bus driver and your teacher — which sector do all three belong to?
Back: Tertiary (service) sector. They do not produce goods; they provide services.

Card 3
Front: The tertiary sector earns the most in GDP. So it must also employ the most people — true or false?
Back: False (examiner's trap — reverse causation). NCERT: the primary sector still employs the largest share of workers.

Card 4
Front: Born in 1935, made government property in 1949 — who is this "bank of banks"?
Back: The Reserve Bank of India. Set up on 1 April 1935 under the RBI Act, 1934; nationalised on 1 January 1949.

Card 5
Front: Every note in your wallet has the RBI Governor's signature — except one. Which note, and whose signature?
Back: The ₹1 note. It is issued by the Government of India and signed by the Finance Secretary.

Card 6
Front: Prices are rising too fast. Will RBI raise or cut the repo rate?
Back: Raise it. Loans become costlier, spending falls, inflation cools. (Trap: "cut repo to control inflation" is wrong.)

Card 7
Front: Mnemonic check: "C for Central, S for Self" — what does it help you remember?
Back: CRR is kept as cash with the Central bank (RBI); SLR is kept by the bank itself in liquid assets (cash, gold, government securities).

Card 8
Front: How many members sit on the Monetary Policy Committee, and who has the casting vote?
Back: 6 members (3 from RBI, 3 appointed by the government). The RBI Governor has the casting vote.

Card 9
Front: RBI's inflation target is a number with a band. What are they, and which index is used?
Back: 4% CPI inflation, with a band of 2% to 6%. In March 2026 the government kept it for 2026–31. It is CPI, not WPI.

Card 10
Front: Prices still rise, but more slowly than last year. Is this deflation?
Back: No — it is disinflation. Deflation means prices actually fall.

Card 11
Front: Which index leaves out services, and who releases it?
Back: WPI (Wholesale Price Index), released by the Office of the Economic Adviser, DPIIT.

Card 12
Front: The Planning Commission was neither in the Constitution nor made by an Act. How was it born, and when?
Back: By a Cabinet resolution on 15 March 1950. It was replaced by NITI Aayog on 1 January 2015.

Card 13
Front: Mnemonic check: "1 Krishi, 2 Karkhana" — what does it stand for?
Back: 1st Five-Year Plan focused on agriculture (Harrod-Domar model); 2nd Plan focused on heavy industry (Mahalanobis model).

Card 14
Front: Fiscal deficit is ₹500 crore and interest payments are ₹200 crore. What is the primary deficit?
Back: ₹300 crore. Primary deficit = Fiscal deficit − Interest payments.

Card 15
Front: GST has a council like a cabinet of finance ministers. Which Article creates it, and who chairs it?
Back: Article 279A; chaired by the Union Finance Minister. GST came on 1 July 2017 through the 101st Amendment Act, 2016.

Card 16
Front: India's foreign exchange ran very low in 1991. What three-letter reform followed?
Back: LPG — Liberalisation, Privatisation, Globalisation. New Industrial Policy on 24 July 1991.

Card 17
Front: Five family members work on a field that needs only three. Removing two changes nothing. What is this?
Back: Disguised unemployment — common in agriculture.

Card 18
Front: Why is the rural calorie norm for the poverty line (2400) higher than the urban one (2100)?
Back: Because people in rural areas do more physical work, so they need more calories (NCERT Class 9).

Card 19
Front: IMF and World Bank sit in Washington. Where does the WTO sit, and when was it born?
Back: Geneva; 1 January 1995, replacing GATT. (Trap: do not put WTO in Washington.)

Card 20
Front: Who publishes the Human Development Index — the World Bank, the IMF or the UNDP?
Back: UNDP, in its Human Development Report (first in 1990), based on health, education and standard of living.
