101. The Blue Revolution is related to:
(a) Fish production (b) Milk production (c) Oilseed production (d) Fertiliser production
Answer: (a)
Solution: Blue Revolution = fish and aquaculture. Milk is the White Revolution and oilseeds the Yellow Revolution.
Source: PYQ-style

102. Dr. Verghese Kurien is associated with which revolution?
(a) White Revolution (b) Green Revolution (c) Blue Revolution (d) Yellow Revolution
Answer: (a)
Solution: Dr. Verghese Kurien led Operation Flood, which made India a leading milk producer — the White Revolution.
Source: PYQ-style

103. During the Green Revolution in India, the crop that benefited the most in the early phase was:
(a) Wheat (b) Pulses (c) Jute (d) Cotton
Answer: (a)
Solution: High-yielding variety seeds first gave the biggest jump in wheat production, mainly in Punjab, Haryana and western Uttar Pradesh.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Geography

104. The Human Development Index measures development on which three dimensions?
(a) Health, education and standard of living (b) GDP, population and exports (c) Inflation, unemployment and poverty (d) Agriculture, industry and services
Answer: (a)
Solution: HDI combines life expectancy (health), years of schooling (education) and GNI per capita (standard of living).
Source: NCERT Class 10 Economics

105. In which year was the first Human Development Report published by UNDP?
(a) 1980 (b) 1990 (c) 1995 (d) 2000
Answer: (b)
Solution: The first Human Development Report, based on the ideas of Mahbub ul Haq, was published in 1990.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Economics

106. The rate at which the RBI gives long-term loans to banks without buying securities from them is called:
(a) Bank rate (b) Repo rate (c) Reverse repo rate (d) Cash reserve ratio
Answer: (a)
Solution: Bank rate is for long-term lending without a repurchase agreement. Repo rate is for short-term lending against government securities.
Source: RBI official website

107. The reverse repo rate is the rate at which:
(a) The RBI borrows money from commercial banks (b) The RBI lends money to commercial banks (c) Commercial banks lend to the public (d) The government borrows from abroad
Answer: (a)
Solution: Under reverse repo, banks park surplus money with the RBI and earn this rate, so the RBI is the borrower. Option (b) is the repo rate.
Source: RBI official website

108. If the RBI reduces the Cash Reserve Ratio (CRR), then:
(a) Banks will have more money to lend (b) Banks will have less money to lend (c) There will be no effect on bank lending (d) Government tax revenue will rise directly
Answer: (a)
Solution: A lower CRR means banks keep less cash with the RBI, freeing more money for loans. Money supply rises.
Source: RBI official website

109. In which year was the Monetary Policy Committee (MPC) constituted in India?
(a) 2014 (b) 2016 (c) 2017 (d) 2019
Answer: (b)
Solution: The MPC was set up under the amended RBI Act, 1934, and flexible inflation targeting began the same year, 2016.
Source: RBI official website

110. In the Monetary Policy Committee, who has the casting vote in case of a tie?
(a) Governor of RBI (b) Union Finance Minister (c) Deputy Governor of RBI (d) Chief Economic Adviser
Answer: (a)
Solution: The RBI Governor chairs the MPC and has a casting vote. The Finance Minister is not a member.
Source: RBI official website

111. The CPI inflation target for the RBI is 4%. What are its lower and upper tolerance levels?
(a) 2% and 6% (b) 3% and 5% (c) 1% and 4% (d) 4% and 8%
Answer: (a)
Solution: The target was retained in March 2026 for April 2026 to March 2031 with tolerance band 2% to 6%.
Source: Government of India notification (Department of Economic Affairs)

112. Who issues coins in India?
(a) Government of India (b) Reserve Bank of India (c) State Bank of India (d) SEBI
Answer: (a)
Solution: Coins are issued by the Government of India and are put into circulation through the RBI. The RBI issues currency notes other than the ₹1 note.
Source: RBI official website

113. Since 1956, the RBI issues currency notes under which system?
(a) Minimum Reserve System (b) Proportional Reserve System (c) Fixed Fiduciary System (d) Gold Standard
Answer: (a)
Solution: In 1956 the Proportional Reserve System was replaced by the Minimum Reserve System, under which RBI keeps a fixed minimum of gold and foreign exchange.
Source: RBI official website

114. In which year was the central office of the RBI permanently moved from Kolkata to Mumbai?
(a) 1935 (b) 1937 (c) 1947 (d) 1949
Answer: (b)
Solution: RBI started in Kolkata in 1935; its central office moved permanently to Mumbai in 1937.
Source: RBI official website

115. The Reserve Bank of India was set up on the recommendations of:
(a) Hilton Young Commission (b) Simon Commission (c) Narasimham Committee (d) Mahalanobis Committee
Answer: (a)
Solution: The Hilton Young Commission (Royal Commission on Indian Currency and Finance) recommended a central bank. The Narasimham Committee came much later, on banking reforms.
Source: RBI official website

116. Six more commercial banks were nationalised in India in:
(a) 1969 (b) 1975 (c) 1980 (d) 1991
Answer: (c)
Solution: 1969 saw the first round of 14 banks. The second round, of six banks, took place in 1980.
Source: RBI official website

117. Consider the following statements:
I. CRR is maintained by banks with the RBI.
II. Gold can be counted for maintaining SLR.
Which of the statements given is/are correct?
(a) I only (b) II only (c) Both I and II (d) Neither I nor II
Answer: (c)
Solution: CRR is cash kept with the RBI, and SLR can be kept in cash, gold and approved government securities. Both statements are correct.

118. Consider the following statements:

119. Which of the following pairs is correctly matched?
(a) First Plan – Heavy industry (b) Second Plan – Mahalanobis model (c) Third Plan – Garibi Hatao (d) Twelfth Plan – 1997 to 2002
Answer: (b)
Solution: The Second Plan used the Mahalanobis model. The First Plan focused on agriculture, Garibi Hatao was the Fifth Plan, and the Twelfth Plan ran from 2012 to 2017.
Source: PYQ-style

120. Arrange the following in chronological order:
I. Setting up of the Planning Commission
II. Establishment of the Reserve Bank of India
III. Formation of NITI Aayog
IV. Nationalisation of 14 commercial banks
(a) II, I, IV, III (b) I, II, IV, III (c) II, IV, I, III (d) I, II, III, IV
Answer: (a)
Solution: RBI was established in 1935, the Planning Commission in 1950, 14 banks were nationalised in 1969, and NITI Aayog was formed in 2015. So the order is II, I, IV, III.
Source: PYQ-style

121. A country's GDP at market price is ₹2,000 crore, depreciation is ₹200 crore and net factor income from abroad is ₹100 crore. What is its NNP at market price?
(a) ₹1,700 crore (b) ₹1,900 crore (c) ₹2,100 crore (d) ₹2,300 crore
Answer: (b)
Solution: GNP = 2,000 + 100 = 2,100; then subtract depreciation. NNP = 2,100 − 200 = ₹1,900 crore.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Macroeconomics

122. The fiscal deficit of a government is ₹800 crore and its interest payments are ₹350 crore. What is the primary deficit?
(a) ₹350 crore (b) ₹450 crore (c) ₹1,150 crore (d) ₹800 crore
Answer: (b)
Solution: Primary deficit = Fiscal deficit − Interest payments. Adding the two is the trap. Primary deficit = 800 − 350 = ₹450 crore.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Macroeconomics

123. A government's total expenditure is ₹1,000 crore, revenue receipts are ₹600 crore and non-debt capital receipts are ₹100 crore. What is the fiscal deficit?
(a) ₹300 crore (b) ₹400 crore (c) ₹100 crore (d) ₹500 crore
Answer: (a)
Solution: Fiscal deficit = Total expenditure − (Revenue receipts + Non-debt capital receipts). Forgetting non-debt capital receipts is the trap. Fiscal deficit = 1,000 − 700 = ₹300 crore.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Macroeconomics

124. In a year, nominal GDP is ₹1,200 crore and real GDP is ₹1,000 crore. What is the GDP deflator?
(a) 83.3 (b) 100 (c) 120 (d) 220
Answer: (c)
Solution: GDP deflator = (Nominal GDP ÷ Real GDP) × 100. Dividing the other way gives 83.3, the trap. Deflator = (1,200 ÷ 1,000) × 100 = 120.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Macroeconomics

125. Consider the following statements:
I. The Planning Commission was set up by an Act of Parliament in 1950.
II. NITI Aayog is chaired by the Prime Minister.
III. The National Development Council was set up in 1952.
Which of the statements given are correct?
(a) I and II only (b) II and III only (c) I and III only (d) I, II and III
Answer: (b)
Solution: The Planning Commission was set up by a Cabinet resolution, not by an Act, so statement I is wrong. Statements II and III are correct.
Source: PYQ-style
