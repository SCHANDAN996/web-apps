51. Gross National Product (GNP) is obtained by adding which item to GDP?
(a) Net factor income from abroad (b) Depreciation (c) Indirect taxes (d) Subsidies
Answer: (a)
Solution: GNP = GDP + Net Factor Income from Abroad (NFIA). Depreciation is subtracted to get "net" figures, not added.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Macroeconomics

52. Net Domestic Product (NDP) is obtained by subtracting which item from GDP?
(a) Depreciation (b) Net factor income from abroad (c) Indirect taxes (d) Subsidies
Answer: (a)
Solution: NDP = GDP − Depreciation. Every "net" figure removes the wear and tear of machines and buildings.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Macroeconomics

53. National income at factor cost is obtained from NNP at market price by:
(a) Subtracting indirect taxes and adding subsidies (b) Adding indirect taxes and subtracting subsidies (c) Subtracting depreciation only (d) Adding net factor income from abroad
Answer: (a)
Solution: Factor cost = Market price − Indirect taxes + Subsidies. Option (a) reverses the signs — an almost-true trap.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Macroeconomics

54. Real GDP is calculated using:
(a) Constant prices of a base year (b) Current year prices (c) Wholesale prices only (d) International dollar prices only
Answer: (a)
Solution: Real GDP uses base-year (constant) prices so that the effect of price rise is removed. Nominal GDP uses current prices.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Macroeconomics

55. Per capita income of a country is calculated as:
(a) National income divided by population (b) Population divided by national income (c) GDP multiplied by population (d) National income minus population
Answer: (a)
Solution: Per capita income = National income ÷ Population. NCERT uses it as average income but notes that it hides inequality.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Economics

56. The main difficulty of the barter system is the lack of:
(a) Double coincidence of wants (b) Inflation control (c) Fiscal discipline (d) Depreciation
Answer: (a)
Solution: In barter both sides must want what the other has — the double coincidence of wants. Money removes this problem.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Economics

57. Deposits in bank accounts that can be withdrawn whenever the account holder wants are called:
(a) Time deposits (b) Demand deposits (c) Statutory deposits (d) Reserve deposits
Answer: (b)
Solution: Demand deposits can be withdrawn on demand, and cheques are paid from them. Time (fixed) deposits are withdrawn after a fixed period.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Economics

58. Broad money (M3) is equal to:
(a) M1 minus currency with the public (b) M1 plus time deposits with banks (c) Currency with the public only (d) M1 plus RBI's gold reserves
Answer: (b)
Solution: M1 is narrow money (currency with public + demand deposits + other deposits with RBI). M3 adds time deposits with banks to M1.
Source: RBI official website

59. Who was the first Indian Governor of the Reserve Bank of India?
(a) Sir Osborne Smith (b) C.D. Deshmukh (c) Manmohan Singh (d) B. Rama Rau
Answer: (b)
Solution: Sir Osborne Smith was the first Governor of RBI, but C.D. Deshmukh was the first Indian Governor — a similar-question trap.
Source: RBI official website

60. In which year did the Imperial Bank of India become the State Bank of India?
(a) 1949 (b) 1955 (c) 1969 (d) 1980
Answer: (b)
Solution: The Imperial Bank of India was converted into the state-owned State Bank of India on 1 July 1955.
Source: RBI official website

61. How many major commercial banks were nationalised on 19 July 1969?
(a) 6 (b) 14 (c) 12 (d) 20
Answer: (b)
Solution: Six banks were nationalised later, in the second round. The first round of bank nationalisation covered 14.
Source: RBI official website

62. NABARD is mainly concerned with financing:
(a) Foreign trade (b) Agriculture and rural development (c) Stock market regulation (d) Insurance companies
Answer: (b)
Solution: The National Bank for Agriculture and Rural Development (NABARD), set up in 1982, is the apex body for agriculture and rural credit.

63. SEBI regulates which of the following?
(a) Commercial banks (b) Securities and capital market (c) Insurance sector (d) Foreign exchange reserves
Answer: (b)
Solution: The Securities and Exchange Board of India regulates the stock (capital) market. Banks are regulated by the RBI.

64. How many companies make up the BSE Sensex?

65. Which is the oldest stock exchange in Asia?
(a) National Stock Exchange (b) Tokyo Stock Exchange (c) Bombay Stock Exchange (d) Hong Kong Stock Exchange

66. In which year was the symbol ₹ adopted for the Indian Rupee?
(a) 2008 (b) 2012 (c) 2010 (d) 2016
Answer: (c)
Solution: 2016 is the demonetisation year, a chronological trap. The rupee symbol designed by D. Udaya Kumar was adopted in 2010.

67. On which date was the demonetisation of the old ₹500 and ₹1000 notes announced?

68. "Removal of poverty" (Garibi Hatao) was a main objective of which Five-Year Plan?
(a) Third Plan (b) Fourth Plan (c) Fifth Plan (d) Sixth Plan
Answer: (c)
Solution: The Fifth Plan (1974–79) aimed at removal of poverty (Garibi Hatao) and self-reliance. It ended a year early.
Source: PYQ-style

69. The period of "Plan Holiday" in India was:
(a) 1990–92 (b) 1978–80 (c) 1966–69 (d) 1961–66
Answer: (c)
Solution: After the Third Plan failed, three Annual Plans were run; this Plan Holiday lasted from 1966–69.
Source: PYQ-style

70. The "Rolling Plan" in India was in operation during:
(a) 1966–69 (b) 1985–90 (c) 1990–92 (d) 1978–80
Answer: (d)
Solution: 1966–69 was the Plan Holiday, a chronological trap. The Rolling Plan followed the early end of the Fifth Plan, in 1978–80.
Source: PYQ-style

71. Which was the last Five-Year Plan of India?
(a) 10th Plan (b) 11th Plan (c) 13th Plan (d) 12th Plan
Answer: (d)
Solution: After the Planning Commission was replaced by NITI Aayog, no new plan was made; the last was the 12th.
Source: PYQ-style

72. The First Five-Year Plan of India was based on which model?
(a) Bombay Plan (b) Mahalanobis model (c) Gandhian model (d) Harrod-Domar model
Answer: (d)
Solution: The First Plan (1951–56) focused on agriculture and irrigation and was based on the Harrod-Domar model. Mahalanobis guided the Second Plan.
Source: PYQ-style

73. Who wrote the book "Planned Economy for India" (1934)?
(a) Jawaharlal Nehru (b) Dadabhai Naoroji (c) M.N. Roy (d) M. Visvesvaraya
Answer: (d)
Solution: M. Visvesvaraya, the engineer-statesman, wrote "Planned Economy for India". M.N. Roy is linked with the People's Plan.
Source: PYQ-style

74. Who put forward the "Drain Theory" and first estimated India's per capita income?
(a) M. Visvesvaraya (b) Gopal Krishna Gokhale (c) Bal Gangadhar Tilak (d) Dadabhai Naoroji
Answer: (d)
Solution: Dadabhai Naoroji, in "Poverty and Un-British Rule in India", explained how wealth was drained to Britain and estimated per capita income.
Source: PYQ-style

75. Which of the following activities is NOT part of the tertiary sector?
(a) Banking (b) Transport (c) Telecommunication (d) Brick-making
Answer: (d)
Solution: Brick-making turns clay into a new product, so it is a secondary-sector activity. Banking, transport and telecommunication are services.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Economics
