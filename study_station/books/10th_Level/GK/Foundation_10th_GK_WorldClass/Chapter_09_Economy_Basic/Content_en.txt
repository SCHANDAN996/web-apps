1. What Is an Economy? Three Sectors, One Nation

An economy is simply the system by which people produce, distribute and consume goods and services. Every job you can think of falls into one of three sectors (NCERT Class 10, Understanding Economic Development):

Primary sector: uses natural resources directly — agriculture, dairy, fishing, forestry. Also called the agriculture and related sector.

Secondary sector: turns raw material into new products — manufacturing (sugar from sugarcane, cloth from cotton), construction, electricity and gas supply. Also called the industrial sector.

Tertiary sector: does not produce a good but supports the other two — transport, trade, banking, communication, teaching, health. Also called the service sector.

NCERT points out two facts that look like opposites: the tertiary sector became the largest producing sector in India's GDP (overtaking the primary sector), but the primary sector still employs the largest share of workers. "Biggest earner" and "biggest employer" are not the same sector — remember this.

Other ways to classify work:

Organised sector: registered, follows rules — fixed hours, paid leave, provident fund.

Unorganised sector: small, scattered units, mostly outside government control, low job security.

Public sector: owned by the government (Indian Railways, post office). Private sector: owned by individuals or companies.

Economic systems: Capitalist (market decides, private ownership), Socialist (state decides and owns), Mixed (both together). India chose a mixed economy after Independence. The word "Socialist" was added to the Preamble by the 42nd Amendment, 1976.

🧠 Pop quiz (Active Recall): A bank clerk works in which sector? (Tertiary)
Which sector gives employment to the largest number of people in India according to NCERT? (Primary)

Mnemonic — sectors in order: "P-S-T = Pick, Shape, Transport". Pick from nature (primary), Shape into goods (secondary), Transport and serve (tertiary).

2. Measuring an Economy: National Income

GDP (Gross Domestic Product): market value of all final goods and services produced within the geographical boundary of a country in one year — no matter who produces them. A Japanese company's car factory in India adds to India's GDP.

GNP (Gross National Product): GDP + Net Factor Income from Abroad (NFIA). It counts what the nationals earn, wherever they are.

NDP = GDP − Depreciation. NNP = GNP − Depreciation. (Depreciation = wear and tear of machines and buildings.)

Market price vs factor cost: Factor cost = Market price − Indirect taxes + Subsidies. NNP at factor cost is called National Income.

Nominal vs real GDP: Nominal GDP uses current prices; real GDP uses prices of a fixed base year, so it removes the effect of inflation. GDP Deflator = (Nominal GDP ÷ Real GDP) × 100.

Per capita income = National income ÷ Population. NCERT uses it to compare countries, but warns it hides inequality.

Who measures it? The National Statistical Office (NSO) under the Ministry of Statistics and Programme Implementation (MoSPI). The NSO was formed in 2019 by merging the CSO and NSSO.

History: Dadabhai Naoroji first estimated India's per capita income (for 1867–68) in "Poverty and Un-British Rule in India" and gave the Drain Theory. After Independence, the National Income Committee (1949) was chaired by P.C. Mahalanobis.

Human Development Index (HDI): published by UNDP in its Human Development Report (first in 1990), idea of Mahbub ul Haq with Amartya Sen. Three dimensions: health (life expectancy), education (years of schooling), standard of living (GNI per capita).

🧠 Recall: GDP counts production inside the border or by nationals? (Inside the border)
NDP is GDP minus what? (Depreciation)
Who brings out the HDI? (UNDP — not the World Bank, not the IMF)

Mnemonic — "Domestic = Door, National = Nagrik": Domestic means inside India's door (territory); National means by India's nagrik (citizens), wherever they are.

3. Money and the Reserve Bank of India

Before money, people used barter, which needs a "double coincidence of wants" — both sides must want what the other has (NCERT Class 10, Money and Credit). Money removes this problem.

Demand deposits: money in a savings or current account that can be withdrawn on demand; cheques are paid from these.

Narrow money (M1) = currency with the public + demand deposits with banks + other deposits with RBI. Broad money (M3) = M1 + time (fixed) deposits with banks.

Reserve Bank of India — the central bank:

Set up on 1 April 1935 under the RBI Act, 1934 (on the recommendation of the Hilton Young Commission).

Central office first at Kolkata, moved permanently to Mumbai in 1937.

Nationalised on 1 January 1949 — now fully owned by the Government of India.

First Governor: Sir Osborne Smith. First Indian Governor: C.D. Deshmukh.

Issues all currency notes except the ₹1 note. The ₹1 note and all coins are issued by the Government of India (Ministry of Finance); the ₹1 note bears the signature of the Finance Secretary, other notes bear the RBI Governor's signature.

Note issue: since 1956 India follows the Minimum Reserve System (earlier Proportional Reserve System).

Banker to the government, banker's bank, lender of last resort, custodian of foreign exchange reserves, regulator of banks.

Other banking milestones: Imperial Bank of India became State Bank of India on 1 July 1955. 14 major banks were nationalised on 19 July 1969 and 6 more in 1980. NABARD (1982) for agriculture and rural credit. The rupee symbol ₹ was adopted in 2010 (designed by D. Udaya Kumar).

🧠 Recall: Who signs the ₹1 note? (Finance Secretary)
In which year was RBI nationalised? (1949 — not 1935, not 1969)

Mnemonic — "RBI: 35 born, 49 nationalised, 69 banks taken": 1935 RBI set up, 1949 RBI nationalised, 1969 fourteen banks nationalised.

4. Monetary Policy: How RBI Controls Money

Monetary policy is run by the RBI; fiscal policy (tax and spending) is run by the government. Do not mix them.

Repo rate: the rate at which RBI lends short-term money to banks against government securities. Repo up → loans costlier → less money in market → inflation falls.

Reverse repo rate: the rate at which RBI borrows (absorbs) money from banks.

Bank rate: rate at which RBI gives long-term loans to banks, without buying securities.

CRR (Cash Reserve Ratio): share of a bank's deposits kept as cash with the RBI. Bank earns no interest on it.

SLR (Statutory Liquidity Ratio): share of deposits a bank keeps with itself in liquid assets — cash, gold, approved government securities.

OMO (Open Market Operations): RBI buys or sells government securities. Buying puts money into the market; selling pulls money out.

Monetary Policy Committee (MPC): set up in 2016 under the amended RBI Act; 6 members (3 from RBI including the Governor, 3 appointed by the government); the Governor has a casting vote. It decides the repo rate.

Inflation target: under flexible inflation targeting (adopted 2016), the government notifies a CPI inflation target of 4% with a band of 2% to 6%. In March 2026 the government kept the same target for 1 April 2026 – 31 March 2031.

🧠 Recall: CRR is kept where — with RBI or with the bank itself? (With RBI)
To fight inflation, RBI will raise or cut the repo rate? (Raise)

Mnemonic — "C for Central, S for Self": CRR goes to the Central bank; SLR stays with the bank's Self.

5. Inflation: When Your ₹100 Buys Less

Inflation is a continuous rise in the general price level; the value of money falls.

Demand-pull inflation: too much money chasing too few goods.

Cost-push inflation: production cost rises (fuel, wages, raw material) and pushes prices up.

Deflation: general fall in prices. Disinflation: inflation is still positive but slowing down. Stagflation: high inflation + high unemployment + slow growth together.

WPI (Wholesale Price Index): prices at the wholesale level; released by the Office of the Economic Adviser, DPIIT (Ministry of Commerce and Industry). It does not cover services.

CPI (Consumer Price Index): retail prices paid by consumers, includes services; CPI (Combined) is released by the NSO, MoSPI. RBI's inflation target is based on CPI, not WPI.

Phillips curve: shows an inverse relation between inflation and unemployment (in the short run).

🧠 Recall: Which index does the RBI use for its inflation target? (CPI Combined)
Prices still rising but more slowly — what is it called? (Disinflation, not deflation)

6. Planning in India: From Planning Commission to NITI Aayog

Early ideas: M. Visvesvaraya's "Planned Economy for India" (1934), the Bombay Plan (1944) by industrialists, the Gandhian Plan and the People's Plan.

Planning Commission: set up on 15 March 1950 by a Cabinet resolution (not by the Constitution, not by an Act). Chairman: the Prime Minister. The National Development Council (NDC) came in 1952.

Five-Year Plans:

1st Plan (1951–56): agriculture and irrigation; based on the Harrod-Domar model.

2nd Plan (1956–61): heavy industry; based on the P.C. Mahalanobis model.

3rd Plan (1961–66): failed due to the 1962 and 1965 wars and drought.

Plan Holiday (1966–69): three Annual Plans.

5th Plan (1974–79): "Garibi Hatao" (removal of poverty); ended a year early, followed by a Rolling Plan (1978–80).

8th Plan (1992–97): started after the 1991 reforms.

12th Plan (2012–17): the last Five-Year Plan.

NITI Aayog (National Institution for Transforming India): replaced the Planning Commission on 1 January 2015. It is a policy think tank; Chairperson is the Prime Minister.

🧠 Recall: Which plan followed the Mahalanobis model? (Second)
How was the Planning Commission created? (By a Cabinet resolution — a non-constitutional, non-statutory body)

Mnemonic — "1 Krishi, 2 Karkhana": the 1st Plan focused on agriculture (krishi), the 2nd Plan on heavy industry (karkhana).

7. The Budget, Taxes and GST

Financial year in India: 1 April to 31 March.

Union Budget = "Annual Financial Statement" under Article 112 of the Constitution. Since 2017 it is presented on 1 February, and the Railway Budget has been merged into it from 2017.

Economic Survey: prepared by the Department of Economic Affairs (under the Chief Economic Adviser), presented just before the Budget.

Revenue receipts: tax and non-tax income (no liability created). Capital receipts: borrowings, disinvestment, loan recoveries.

Fiscal Deficit = Total expenditure − (Revenue receipts + non-debt capital receipts). It shows how much the government must borrow.

Revenue Deficit = Revenue expenditure − Revenue receipts.

Primary Deficit = Fiscal deficit − Interest payments.

FRBM Act, 2003: law for fiscal discipline.

Direct tax: burden cannot be shifted — income tax, corporation tax. Indirect tax: burden is passed on to the buyer — GST, customs duty.

Progressive tax: rate rises as income rises (income tax). Regressive tax: takes a bigger share from the poor.

GST (Goods and Services Tax): brought by the 101st Constitutional Amendment Act, 2016; rolled out from 1 July 2017. It is a destination-based tax. GST Council under Article 279A is chaired by the Union Finance Minister. Types: CGST, SGST, UTGST and IGST (on inter-state supply).

Finance Commission: Article 280; constituted by the President every five years; recommends sharing of taxes between Centre and States.

🧠 Recall: Fiscal deficit minus interest payments equals? (Primary deficit)
Which Article sets up the GST Council? (279A)

Mnemonic — "Budget = 112, Finance Commission = 280, GST Council = 279A". Say it like a phone number: "One-One-Two, Two-Eight-Zero, Two-Seven-Nine-A".

8. 1991 Reforms: LPG

In 1991 India faced a Balance of Payments crisis — foreign exchange reserves had fallen very low. The government of P.V. Narasimha Rao, with Dr. Manmohan Singh as Finance Minister, launched the New Economic Policy.

Liberalisation: removing licences and controls (the "Licence Raj"). The New Industrial Policy came on 24 July 1991.

Privatisation: reducing the role of the public sector; disinvestment.

Globalisation: opening to foreign trade and investment. India is a founder member of the WTO (1 January 1995).

Related laws: FEMA, 1999 replaced FERA, 1973. The Competition Act, 2002 replaced the MRTP Act, 1969.

FDI (Foreign Direct Investment): long-term investment with control (a foreign company setting up a factory). FPI (Foreign Portfolio Investment): buying shares and bonds, can leave quickly.

Balance of Payments: Current account (trade in goods and services, remittances) + Capital account (FDI, FPI, loans).

🧠 Recall: What does LPG stand for? (Liberalisation, Privatisation, Globalisation)
Which law replaced FERA? (FEMA, 1999)

9. Poverty, Unemployment and Agriculture

Poverty line (NCERT Class 9): earlier based on calorie needs — 2400 calories per person per day in rural areas and 2100 in urban areas. Rural norm is higher because rural work is more physical.

Types of unemployment:

Disguised unemployment: more people work than needed; removing some does not reduce output. Common in agriculture.

Seasonal unemployment: no work in some months of the year (farm workers after harvest).

Structural unemployment: skills do not match available jobs, due to change in the economy's structure.

Frictional unemployment: short gap while changing jobs.

MGNREGA, 2005: guarantees 100 days of wage employment in a year to every rural household whose adult members volunteer for unskilled manual work.

Revolutions in agriculture:

Green Revolution (mid-1960s): high-yielding variety seeds, mainly wheat; M.S. Swaminathan in India, Norman Borlaug worldwide.

White Revolution: milk; Operation Flood (1970) led by Dr. Verghese Kurien.

Blue Revolution: fish. Yellow Revolution: oilseeds.

Minimum Support Price (MSP): announced by the government on the recommendation of the CACP (Commission for Agricultural Costs and Prices).

🧠 Recall: Five people work on a farm where three are enough. What type of unemployment is this? (Disguised)
Operation Flood is linked to which revolution? (White Revolution — milk)

10. International Economic Bodies

IMF and World Bank (IBRD): created at the Bretton Woods Conference, 1944; both headquartered in Washington, D.C.

WTO: came into being on 1 January 1995, replacing GATT (1947); headquarters Geneva.

Asian Development Bank: headquarters Manila (Philippines). New Development Bank (BRICS): Shanghai. AIIB: Beijing.

Stock market regulator SEBI: set up in 1988, made a statutory body in 1992. Bombay Stock Exchange (1875) is Asia's oldest stock exchange; its index Sensex has 30 companies. NSE's index Nifty 50 has 50 companies.

🧠 Recall: Where is the WTO headquartered? (Geneva — not Washington)
Which body replaced GATT? (WTO)

⚠️ Caution: Examiner's Trap

Trap 1: "RBI was nationalised in 1969"

Type: समान नाम/वर्ष (Similar Name/Year)

Trap statement: "The Reserve Bank of India was nationalised in 1969 along with 14 banks."

Reality: RBI was nationalised on 1 January 1949. 1969 is the year 14 commercial banks were nationalised. RBI is not a commercial bank.

Smart move: RBI = 1949; commercial banks = 1969 and 1980.

Trap 2: "All currency notes are issued by the RBI"

Type: लगभग सही कथन (Almost True Statement)

Trap statement: "Every currency note in India carries the RBI Governor's signature."

Reality: 90% true — but the ₹1 note is issued by the Government of India and signed by the Finance Secretary.

Smart move: Whenever you see "all/every" with currency, think of the ₹1 note.

Trap 3: Repo rate and inflation

Type: विपरीत कार्य-कारण (Reverse Causation)

Trap statement: "RBI cuts the repo rate to control inflation."

Reality: Cutting the repo rate makes loans cheaper and increases money in the market — it can raise inflation. To control inflation RBI raises the repo rate.

Smart move: Repo up → money down → prices down.

Trap 4: "Which of the following is NOT a direct tax?"

Type: नकारात्मक वाक्यांश (Negative Phrasing)

Reality: Income tax and corporation tax are direct; GST and customs duty are indirect. Read the word NOT twice before choosing.

🔗 Static-Dynamic Link: From Textbook to Now

Inflation target: The static fact "MPC targets CPI inflation of 4% ±2%" was renewed by the Government in March 2026 for the period 1 April 2026 – 31 March 2031. Exams may frame it as a recent decision — the number did not change.

GST: The static fact "GST came on 1 July 2017" is the base for every news item about the GST Council changing tax rates. Rates change; the Article (279A) and the Chairperson (Union Finance Minister) do not.

NITI Aayog: The Planning Commission's last plan was the 12th (2012–17). NITI Aayog now prepares vision and strategy documents instead of Five-Year Plans.
