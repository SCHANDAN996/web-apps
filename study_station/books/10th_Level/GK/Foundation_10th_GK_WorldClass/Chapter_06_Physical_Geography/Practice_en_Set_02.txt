Set 2/6 – Physical Geography of India MCQs (Foundation)
Questions 26–50: Medium

26. The Pir Panjal, the longest and most important range of its belt, belongs to which part of the Himalayas?
(a) Himadri (Great Himalaya)
(b) Himachal (Lesser Himalaya)
(c) Shiwaliks
(d) Purvachal
Answer: (b)
Solution: In the Himachal or Lesser Himalaya, the Pir Panjal forms the longest and most important range; the Dhaula Dhar and Mahabharat ranges are also part of it.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Geography (Contemporary India-I)

27. The Purvachal hills of north-east India are mostly composed of:
(a) Granite
(b) Basalt
(c) Strong sandstones
(d) Marble
Answer: (c)
Solution: The Purvachal hills (Patkai, Naga, Manipur and Mizo hills) are mostly composed of strong sandstones, which are sedimentary rocks. Granite forms the core of the Great Himalaya.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Geography (Contemporary India-I)

28. The part of the Himalayas lying between the Satluj and the Kali rivers is known as:
(a) Kumaon Himalaya
(b) Punjab Himalaya
(c) Nepal Himalaya
(d) Assam Himalaya
Answer: (a)
Solution: Satluj to Kali is the Kumaon Himalaya. Indus to Satluj is the Punjab Himalaya, Kali to Teesta is the Nepal Himalaya, and Teesta to Dihang is the Assam Himalaya.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Geography (Contemporary India-I)

29. On reaching which peak does the Brahmaputra take a 'U' turn and enter India through a gorge?
(a) Kanchenjunga
(b) Nanda Devi
(c) Kamet
(d) Namcha Barwa
Answer: (d)
Solution: The Brahmaputra flows east parallel to the Himalayas and takes a 'U' turn at Namcha Barwa, entering Arunachal Pradesh through a gorge, where it is called the Dihang.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Geography (Contemporary India-I)

30. Majuli, the largest inhabited riverine island in the world, lies in which river?
(a) Ganga
(b) Brahmaputra
(c) Godavari
(d) Mahanadi
Answer: (b)
Solution: Majuli lies in the Brahmaputra in Assam. The braided Brahmaputra forms many riverine islands because it deposits a large amount of silt.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Geography (Contemporary India-I)

31. The word 'Doab' literally means:
(a) Land of five rivers
(b) Land between two hills
(c) Land between two rivers ('do' = two, 'ab' = water)
(d) Land below sea level
Answer: (c)
Solution: Doab is made of 'do' (two) and 'ab' (water), i.e. the land between two rivers. 'Punjab' means land of five waters — do not mix the two.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Geography (Contemporary India-I)

32. The Punjab Plains, the western part of the Northern Plain, are formed by:
(a) The Indus and its tributaries
(b) The Ganga and its tributaries
(c) The Brahmaputra
(d) The Luni and the Ghaggar
Answer: (a)
Solution: The Punjab Plains are formed by the Indus and its tributaries (Jhelum, Chenab, Ravi, Beas, Satluj); the larger part of this plain lies in Pakistan.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Geography (Contemporary India-I)

33. The Wular Lake, the largest freshwater lake of India, was formed by tectonic activity. In which region is it located?
(a) Himachal Pradesh
(b) Uttarakhand
(c) Jammu and Kashmir
(d) Sikkim
Answer: (c)
Solution: Wular Lake is in Jammu and Kashmir. Most Himalayan freshwater lakes are of glacial origin, but Wular is the result of tectonic activity.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Geography (Contemporary India-I)

34. The water of the Sambhar Lake in Rajasthan is mainly used for:
(a) Hydroelectricity
(b) Drinking water for Jaipur
(c) Inland navigation
(d) Producing salt
Answer: (d)
Solution: Sambhar is a salt water lake in a region of inland drainage, and its water is used for producing salt.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Geography (Contemporary India-I)

35. Which statement about 'Khadar' is correct?
(a) It is older alluvium with kankar
(b) It is newer alluvium of the floodplains, renewed almost every year
(c) It is a belt of pebbles at the foot of the Shiwaliks
(d) It is a swampy forest belt
Answer: (b)
Solution: Khadar is the newer, younger alluvium of the floodplains, renewed almost every year, so it is fertile and ideal for intensive agriculture. Older alluvium with kankar is Bhangar.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Geography (Contemporary India-I)

36. 'Barchans', which cover large areas of the Indian desert, are:
(a) Crescent-shaped sand dunes
(b) Seasonal salt lakes
(c) Dry river beds
(d) Rocky residual hills
Answer: (a)
Solution: Barchans are crescent-shaped dunes; longitudinal dunes are more prominent near the India–Pakistan border.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Geography (Contemporary India-I)

37. The Kaveri river rises in:
(a) Amarkantak hills
(b) Mahabaleshwar
(c) Brahmagiri range of the Western Ghats
(d) Nasik district
Answer: (c)
Solution: The Kaveri rises in the Brahmagiri range of the Western Ghats and reaches the Bay of Bengal south of Cuddalore in Tamil Nadu.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Geography (Contemporary India-I)

38. The Krishna river rises from a spring near:
(a) Amarkantak
(b) Mahabaleshwar
(c) Nasik
(d) Betul
Answer: (b)
Solution: The Krishna rises from a spring near Mahabaleshwar and flows about 1400 km to the Bay of Bengal. Nasik is linked with the Godavari.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Geography (Contemporary India-I)

39. The Narmada river rises in:
(a) Betul district of Madhya Pradesh
(b) Brahmagiri range
(c) Nasik district of Maharashtra
(d) Amarkantak hills of Madhya Pradesh
Answer: (d)
Solution: The Narmada rises in the Amarkantak hills in Madhya Pradesh and flows west in a rift valley formed due to faulting. Betul is the source area of the Tapi.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Geography (Contemporary India-I)

40. The Tapi river rises in the Satpura ranges in which district of Madhya Pradesh?
(a) Betul
(b) Jabalpur
(c) Bhopal
(d) Indore
Answer: (a)
Solution: The Tapi rises in the Satpura ranges in the Betul district of Madhya Pradesh and flows in a rift valley parallel to the Narmada.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Geography (Contemporary India-I)

41. The 'Marble rocks' and the 'Dhuadhar falls' near Jabalpur are formed by which river?
(a) Tapi
(b) Narmada
(c) Son
(d) Mahanadi
Answer: (b)
Solution: Near Jabalpur, the Narmada flows through a deep gorge in the Marble rocks and plunges over steep rocks at the Dhuadhar falls.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Geography (Contemporary India-I)

42. Which of the following is NOT a tributary of the Godavari?
(a) Wardha
(b) Manjra
(c) Tungabhadra
(d) Wainganga
Answer: (c)
Solution: The Tungabhadra is a tributary of the Krishna. The Purna, Wardha, Pranhita, Manjra, Wainganga and Penganga are tributaries of the Godavari.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Geography (Contemporary India-I)

43. Pulicat Lake, a coastal lagoon, lies on the border of which two states?
(a) Andhra Pradesh and Tamil Nadu
(b) Odisha and Andhra Pradesh
(c) Kerala and Tamil Nadu
(d) Goa and Karnataka
Answer: (a)
Solution: Pulicat Lake lies on the Andhra Pradesh–Tamil Nadu border on the east coast. Like Chilika and Kolleru, it is a lagoon formed by spits and bars.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Geography (Contemporary India-I)

44. 'Mango showers' are:
(a) Winter rains of north-west India
(b) Rains of the retreating monsoon in Tamil Nadu
(c) Pre-monsoon showers in Kerala and Karnataka that help mangoes ripen early
(d) Thunderstorms of West Bengal in summer
Answer: (c)
Solution: Towards the end of summer, pre-monsoon showers in Kerala and Karnataka help the early ripening of mangoes and are called mango showers. West Bengal's summer storms are Kaal Baisakhi.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Geography (Contemporary India-I)

45. Winter rainfall in the plains of north and north-west India is caused mainly by:
(a) The south-west monsoon
(b) Tropical cyclones of the Bay of Bengal
(c) The loo
(d) Western cyclonic disturbances from the Mediterranean region
Answer: (d)
Solution: Western disturbances originating over the Mediterranean Sea and western Asia bring winter rain to the plains and snow to the mountains; this rain is important for rabi crops.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Geography (Contemporary India-I)

46. The 'loo' is:
(a) A cold wind of the Himalayas in winter
(b) A strong, gusty, hot and dry wind of north and north-west India in summer
(c) A pre-monsoon shower of Kerala
(d) A sea breeze of the Konkan coast
Answer: (b)
Solution: The loo is a strong, gusty, hot, dry wind that blows during the day over north and north-western India in the hot weather season.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Geography (Contemporary India-I)

47. The Tamil Nadu coast gets a large portion of its rainfall during:
(a) October and November
(b) June and July
(c) March and April
(d) January and February
Answer: (a)
Solution: Most parts of India get rain from June to September, but the Tamil Nadu coast gets a large portion of its rain in October and November.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Geography (Contemporary India-I)

48. In which state are the violent summer thunderstorms called 'Kaal Baisakhi'?
(a) Kerala
(b) Punjab
(c) West Bengal
(d) Rajasthan
Answer: (c)
Solution: In West Bengal the violent evening thunderstorms of the hot season are known as Kaal Baisakhi. Kerala's pre-monsoon rain is called mango showers.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Geography (Contemporary India-I)

49. Which of the following statements about black soil is NOT correct?
(a) It is made up of extremely fine, clayey material
(b) It is rich in phosphoric content
(c) It has a high capacity to hold moisture
(d) It develops deep cracks during hot weather
Answer: (b)
Solution: Black soil is rich in calcium carbonate, magnesium, potash and lime but is generally poor in phosphoric content. The other three statements are correct.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Geography (Contemporary India-II)

50. Red soil gets its reddish colour mainly because of:
(a) High humus content
(b) Salt deposits
(c) Lime and kankar
(d) Diffusion of iron in crystalline and metamorphic rocks
Answer: (d)
Solution: Red soil develops a reddish colour due to diffusion of iron in crystalline and metamorphic rocks; it looks yellow when it occurs in a hydrated form.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Geography (Contemporary India-II)
