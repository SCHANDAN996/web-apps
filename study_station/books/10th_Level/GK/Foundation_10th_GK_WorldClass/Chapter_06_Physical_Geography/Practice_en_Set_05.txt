Set 5/6 – Physical Geography of India MCQs (Foundation)
Questions 101–120: Medium | Questions 121–125: Hard

101. At Rudraprayag, the Alaknanda is joined by which river?
(a) Mandakini
(b) Pindar
(c) Bhagirathi
(d) Yamuna
Answer: (a)
Solution: The Mandakini (Kali Ganga) meets the Alaknanda at Rudraprayag. The Pindar joins at Karnaprayag and the Bhagirathi at Devprayag.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Geography (India: Physical Environment)

102. Flowing west from Tibet, the Indus enters India in which region?
(a) Himachal Pradesh
(b) Punjab
(c) Ladakh
(d) Sikkim
Answer: (c)
Solution: The Indus rises in Tibet near Lake Mansarovar, flows west and enters India in Ladakh, where it forms a picturesque gorge.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Geography (Contemporary India-I)

103. The Satluj, Beas, Ravi, Chenab and Jhelum join together and enter the Indus near:
(a) Attock
(b) Karachi
(c) Ambala
(d) Mithankot in Pakistan
Answer: (d)
Solution: These five rivers join together to enter the Indus near Mithankot in Pakistan. Attock is where the Indus emerges from the mountains.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Geography (Contemporary India-I)

104. Under the provisions of the Indus Water Treaty (1960), India can use what share of the total water carried by the Indus river system?
(a) Only 20 per cent
(b) 40 per cent
(c) 50 per cent
(d) 80 per cent
Answer: (a)
Solution: Under the Indus Water Treaty of 1960, India can use only 20 per cent of the total water of the Indus system, mainly for irrigation in Punjab, Haryana and Rajasthan. India's share is 20 per cent.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Geography (Contemporary India-I)

105. Which Peninsular river has the largest drainage basin among the Peninsular rivers?
(a) Krishna
(b) Godavari
(c) Mahanadi
(d) Narmada
Answer: (b)
Solution: The Godavari is the largest Peninsular river and its drainage basin is also the largest among the Peninsular rivers; about half of its basin lies in Maharashtra.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Geography (Contemporary India-I)

106. The Mahanadi river rises in the highlands of:
(a) Chhattisgarh
(b) Jharkhand
(c) Odisha
(d) Maharashtra
Answer: (a)
Solution: The Mahanadi rises in the highlands of Chhattisgarh and flows through Odisha to the Bay of Bengal. Choosing Odisha confuses its mouth with its source.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Geography (Contemporary India-I)

107. The Tungabhadra is a tributary of which river?
(a) Godavari
(b) Kaveri
(c) Krishna
(d) Mahanadi
Answer: (c)
Solution: The Tungabhadra, Koyna, Ghatprabha, Musi and Bhima are tributaries of the Krishna.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Geography (Contemporary India-I)

108. Which of the following is NOT a tributary of the Kaveri?
(a) Amaravati
(b) Bhavani
(c) Kabini
(d) Bhima
Answer: (d)
Solution: The Bhima is a tributary of the Krishna. The Amaravati, Bhavani, Hemavati and Kabini are the main tributaries of the Kaveri.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Geography (Contemporary India-I)

109. At Farakka the Ganga bifurcates. Which distributary flows south through the deltaic plains of West Bengal to the Bay of Bengal?
(a) Padma
(b) Bhagirathi–Hooghly
(c) Meghna
(d) Jamuna
Answer: (b)
Solution: At Farakka, the Bhagirathi–Hooghly distributary flows south through West Bengal, while the mainstream enters Bangladesh and is joined by the Brahmaputra.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Geography (Contemporary India-I)

110. Which of the following is NOT a part of the Purvachal hills?
(a) Patkai hills
(b) Naga hills
(c) Garo hills
(d) Mizo hills
Answer: (c)
Solution: The Purvachal comprises the Patkai, Naga, Manipur and Mizo hills. The Garo hills are part of the Meghalaya plateau, an extension of the Peninsular plateau.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Geography (Contemporary India-I)

111. The Nepal Himalaya lies between which two rivers?
(a) Indus and Satluj
(b) Satluj and Kali
(c) Teesta and Dihang
(d) Kali and Teesta
Answer: (d)
Solution: The Kali and the Teesta rivers mark the Nepal Himalaya. Satluj to Kali is the Kumaon Himalaya.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Geography (Contemporary India-I)

112. The Assam Himalaya lies between which two rivers?
(a) Teesta and Dihang
(b) Kali and Teesta
(c) Satluj and Kali
(d) Indus and Satluj
Answer: (a)
Solution: The part of the Himalayas between the Teesta and the Dihang rivers is the Assam Himalaya. Beyond the Dihang gorge the mountains bend south as the Purvachal.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Geography (Contemporary India-I)

113. Which belt of the Northern Plains is wet, swampy and marshy, was thickly forested, and has the Dudhwa National Park?
(a) Bhabar
(b) Terai
(c) Bhangar
(d) Khadar
Answer: (b)
Solution: South of the Bhabar, streams re-emerge and create the wet, swampy Terai, once thickly forested and full of wildlife; Dudhwa National Park is in this belt.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Geography (Contemporary India-I)

114. In which year were the Laccadive, Minicoy and Amindive islands named Lakshadweep?
(a) 1956
(b) 1962
(c) 1973
(d) 1987
Answer: (c)
Solution: The coral islands earlier known as Laccadive, Minicoy and Amindive were named Lakshadweep in 1973.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Geography (Contemporary India-I)

115. The southern part of the eastern coastal plain is known as the:
(a) Coromandel Coast
(b) Northern Circar
(c) Kannad Plain
(d) Konkan
Answer: (a)
Solution: The eastern coastal plain is called the Northern Circar in the north and the Coromandel Coast in the south. Konkan and Kannad are parts of the western coast.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Geography (Contemporary India-I)

116. Kolleru Lake, a lake of the eastern coast, lies in which state?
(a) Odisha
(b) Andhra Pradesh
(c) Tamil Nadu
(d) West Bengal
Answer: (b)
Solution: Kolleru Lake lies in Andhra Pradesh between the Godavari and Krishna deltas. Chilika, the largest salt water lake, is in Odisha.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Geography (Contemporary India-I)

117. Loktak, an important freshwater lake, is in which state?
(a) Assam
(b) Meghalaya
(c) Tripura
(d) Manipur
Answer: (d)
Solution: Loktak Lake is in Manipur; NCERT lists it with Dal, Bhimtal, Nainital and Barapani as important freshwater lakes.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Geography (Contemporary India-I)

118. The 'burst' of the monsoon refers to:
(a) The sudden withdrawal of the monsoon
(b) A cyclone hitting the east coast
(c) A sudden increase in rainfall that continues for several days at the onset
(d) The first snowfall in the Himalayas
Answer: (c)
Solution: At the onset of the monsoon, rainfall increases suddenly and continues for several days; this is the 'burst' of the monsoon, different from pre-monsoon showers.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Geography (Contemporary India-I)

119. The small amount of winter rainfall in north-west India, locally called 'mahawat', is important for:
(a) Rabi crops
(b) Kharif crops
(c) Tea plantations
(d) Jute cultivation
Answer: (a)
Solution: Western disturbances bring winter rain called mahawat; though small in amount, it is of immense importance for the cultivation of rabi crops.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Geography (Contemporary India-I)

120. Which is the outermost range of the Himalayas?
(a) Himadri
(b) Shiwaliks
(c) Himachal
(d) Karakoram
Answer: (b)
Solution: The Shiwaliks are the outermost range, 10–50 km wide and 900–1100 m high, made of unconsolidated river sediments.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Geography (Contemporary India-I)

121. Consider the following statements:
1. The Indus and the Brahmaputra rise north of the Himalayan ranges and have cut through the mountains, making gorges.
2. A large number of the Peninsular rivers are seasonal, as their flow depends on rainfall.
3. The Narmada and the Tapi flow from west to east into the Bay of Bengal.
Which of the statements given above is/are correct?
(a) 1 and 2 only
(b) 2 and 3 only
(c) 1 and 3 only
(d) 1, 2 and 3
Answer: (a)
Solution: Statements 1 and 2 are correct. Statement 3 reverses the direction: the Narmada and the Tapi flow east to west into the Arabian Sea.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Geography (Contemporary India-I)

122. Arrange the following confluences on the Alaknanda from upstream to downstream:
1. Rudraprayag
2. Vishnuprayag
3. Devprayag
4. Karnaprayag
(a) 2, 1, 4, 3
(b) 2, 4, 1, 3
(c) 4, 2, 1, 3
(d) 2, 4, 3, 1
Answer: (b)
Solution: Upstream to downstream the order is Vishnuprayag (near Joshimath), Karnaprayag (Pindar), Rudraprayag (Mandakini) and Devprayag (Bhagirathi), i.e. 2, 4, 1, 3.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Geography (India: Physical Environment)

123. How many of the following pairs (Peak – Range) are correctly matched?
1. Anai Mudi – Western Ghats
2. Doda Betta – Nilgiri hills
3. Mahendragiri – Western Ghats
4. Guru Shikhar – Aravali
(a) Only one
(b) Only two
(c) Only three
(d) All four
Answer: (c)
Solution: Pairs 1, 2 and 4 are correct. Mahendragiri is the highest peak of the Eastern Ghats, not the Western Ghats.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Geography (Contemporary India-I)

124. Assertion: The Mahanadi, Godavari, Krishna and Kaveri have formed extensive deltas on the eastern coast.
Reason: These rivers flow eastwards and drain into the Bay of Bengal across a wide and level coastal plain.
Choose the correct option.
(a) Both Assertion and Reason are true, and Reason is the correct explanation of Assertion
(b) Both Assertion and Reason are true, but Reason is not the correct explanation of Assertion
(c) Assertion is true, but Reason is false
(d) Assertion is false, but Reason is true
Answer: (a)
Solution: Both statements are true and linked: the east-flowing rivers deposit their silt on the wide, level eastern plain and build deltas.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Geography (Contemporary India-I)

125. Which of the following statements is correct?
(a) Cherrapunji receives the highest average rainfall in the world
(b) Rivers re-emerge in the Bhabar belt
(c) The Godavari forms an estuary
(d) Mawsynram receives the highest average rainfall in the world
Answer: (d)
Solution: Mawsynram in the Khasi Hills receives the highest average rainfall in the world. Streams disappear in the Bhabar and re-emerge in the Terai, and the Godavari forms a delta.
