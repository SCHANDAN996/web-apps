Set 4/6 – Physical Geography of India MCQs (Foundation)
Questions 76–100: Medium

76. Consider the following statements about the Himalayas:
1. The core of the Great Himalaya (Himadri) is composed of granite.
2. The Lesser Himalaya contains the valleys of Kashmir, Kangra and Kullu.
3. The Shiwaliks are composed of unconsolidated sediments brought down by rivers.
Which of the statements given above is/are correct?
(a) 1 and 2 only
(b) 2 and 3 only
(c) 1 and 3 only
(d) 1, 2 and 3
Answer: (d)
Solution: All three are correct: Himadri has a granite core, the Lesser Himalaya has the Kashmir, Kangra and Kullu valleys, and the Shiwaliks are made of unconsolidated river sediments.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Geography (Contemporary India-I)

77. An elevated area, such as a mountain or an upland, that separates two drainage basins is called a:
(a) Water divide
(b) Tributary
(c) Distributary
(d) Delta
Answer: (a)
Solution: An upland separating two drainage basins is a water divide. A distributary is a channel into which a river splits in its lower course.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Geography (Contemporary India-I)

78. Which town is located on the water divide between the Indus and the Ganga river systems?
(a) Haridwar
(b) Dehradun
(c) Ambala
(d) Agra
Answer: (c)
Solution: Ambala lies on the water divide between the Indus and Ganga systems; the plains from Ambala to the Sundarban have a very gentle slope.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Geography (Contemporary India-I)

79. Saddle Peak, the highest peak of the Andaman and Nicobar Islands, is in:
(a) South Andaman
(b) Middle Andaman
(c) North Andaman
(d) Great Nicobar
Answer: (c)
Solution: Saddle Peak is in North Andaman. Mount Diavolo is in Middle Andaman and Mount Thuiller is in Great Nicobar.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Geography (India: Physical Environment)

80. Black soil, also called regur, is ideal for growing:
(a) Tea
(b) Rice
(c) Cotton
(d) Cashew nut
Answer: (c)
Solution: Black soil is ideal for cotton and is also called black cotton soil. Tea and coffee suit laterite soils of hilly areas.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Geography (Contemporary India-II)

81. The Zoji La pass connects:
(a) Kashmir Valley with Ladakh
(b) Himachal Pradesh with Tibet
(c) Sikkim with Tibet
(d) Arunachal Pradesh with Tibet
Answer: (a)
Solution: Zoji La on the Srinagar–Leh route links the Kashmir Valley with Ladakh. Shipki La links Himachal Pradesh with Tibet and Nathu La links Sikkim with Tibet.
Source: PYQ-style

82. Through which pass does the Satluj river enter India from Tibet?
(a) Zoji La
(b) Nathu La
(c) Shipki La
(d) Jelep La
Answer: (c)
Solution: The Satluj passes through the Shipki La and enters the Punjab plains. It is an antecedent river that feeds the Bhakra Nangal canals.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Geography (India: Physical Environment)

83. Consider the following pairs (River – Where it meets the sea):
1. Narmada – Gulf of Khambhat
2. Tapi – Gulf of Kachchh
3. Mahi – Gulf of Khambhat
Which of the pairs given above is/are correctly matched?
(a) 1 only
(b) 1 and 3 only
(c) 2 and 3 only
(d) 1, 2 and 3
Answer: (b)
Solution: The Narmada, Tapi and Mahi all drain into the Gulf of Khambhat. Pair 2 is wrong because the Tapi does not go to the Gulf of Kachchh.
Source: PYQ-style

84. Vembanad Lake is located in which state?
(a) Tamil Nadu
(b) Kerala
(c) Karnataka
(d) Goa
Answer: (b)
Solution: Vembanad is a large backwater lake (kayal) on the Kerala coast.
Source: PYQ-style

85. The 'Kayals' (backwaters), used for fishing, inland navigation and tourism, are a feature of which coast?
(a) Konkan coast
(b) Northern Circar coast
(c) Coromandel coast
(d) Malabar coast of Kerala
Answer: (d)
Solution: The Malabar coast of Kerala has the Kayals (backwaters); the Nehru Trophy boat race is held in Punnamada Kayal in Kerala.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Geography (India: Physical Environment)

86. Which statement correctly compares the two coastal plains of India?
(a) The western plain is narrow and submerged; the eastern plain is broader and emergent
(b) The western plain is broad and emergent; the eastern plain is narrow and submerged
(c) Both plains are equally broad and emergent
(d) The eastern plain has estuaries; the western plain has large deltas
Answer: (a)
Solution: The western coastal plain is a narrow, submerged plain, while the eastern plain is broader, emergent, and has well-developed deltas of the Mahanadi, Godavari, Krishna and Kaveri.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Geography (India: Physical Environment)

87. The Duncan Passage separates:
(a) North Andaman and Middle Andaman
(b) South Andaman and Little Andaman
(c) Andaman and Nicobar
(d) Minicoy and the Maldives
Answer: (b)
Solution: The Duncan Passage lies between South Andaman and Little Andaman. Andaman and Nicobar are separated by the 10° Channel.
Source: PYQ-style

88. Consider the following statements:
1. The Western Ghats are continuous and can be crossed only through passes.
2. The Eastern Ghats are discontinuous and are cut by rivers draining into the Bay of Bengal.
3. Anai Mudi is the highest peak of the Eastern Ghats.
Which of the statements given above is/are correct?
(a) 1 and 2 only
(b) 2 and 3 only
(c) 1 and 3 only
(d) 1, 2 and 3
Answer: (a)
Solution: Statements 1 and 2 are correct. Anai Mudi is the highest peak of the Western Ghats; the highest peak of the Eastern Ghats is Mahendragiri.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Geography (Contemporary India-I)

89. The Meghalaya plateau is separated from the Chotanagpur plateau by the:
(a) Narmada rift
(b) Palghat gap
(c) Rann of Kachchh
(d) Malda fault
Answer: (d)
Solution: The north-eastern extension of the Peninsular plateau (Meghalaya, Karbi Anglong) is separated from the Chotanagpur plateau by the Malda fault in West Bengal.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Geography (India: Physical Environment)

90. Which is the correct order of the hill ranges of the Meghalaya plateau from west to east?
(a) Khasi, Garo, Jaintia
(b) Garo, Khasi, Jaintia
(c) Jaintia, Khasi, Garo
(d) Garo, Jaintia, Khasi
Answer: (b)
Solution: The three prominent hill ranges of the Meghalaya plateau from west to east are the Garo, the Khasi and the Jaintia Hills.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Geography (Contemporary India-I)

91. The oppressive weather called 'October heat' during the retreat of the monsoon is caused by:
(a) Snowfall in the Himalayas
(b) Western disturbances
(c) High temperature combined with high humidity
(d) Dust storms from the Thar
Answer: (c)
Solution: After the monsoon retreats, day temperatures rise while the land is still moist; high temperature and humidity make the weather oppressive, known as October heat.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Geography (Contemporary India-I)

92. Bundelkhand and Baghelkhand are the eastward extensions of the:
(a) Central Highlands
(b) Deccan Plateau
(c) Meghalaya Plateau
(d) Shiwalik hills
Answer: (a)
Solution: The Central Highlands, north of the Narmada, are wider in the west and narrower in the east; their eastward extensions are Bundelkhand and Baghelkhand.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Geography (Contemporary India-I)

93. The Chotanagpur plateau, a further eastward extension of the Central Highlands, is drained by which river?
(a) Narmada
(b) Mahi
(c) Damodar
(d) Chambal
Answer: (c)
Solution: The Chotanagpur plateau is drained by the Damodar river.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Geography (Contemporary India-I)

94. With proper soil conservation, laterite soil in the hilly areas of Karnataka, Kerala and Tamil Nadu is very useful for growing:
(a) Wheat and gram
(b) Cotton and jowar
(c) Tea and coffee
(d) Jute and rice
Answer: (c)
Solution: After soil conservation in the hilly areas of Karnataka, Kerala and Tamil Nadu, laterite soil is very useful for tea and coffee. Cotton is linked with black soil.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Geography (Contemporary India-II)

95. The south-west monsoon generally arrives at the southern tip of the Indian peninsula by:
(a) The first week of May
(b) The first week of June
(c) The first week of July
(d) The first week of August
Answer: (b)
Solution: The monsoon generally arrives at the southern tip of the peninsula by the first week of June.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Geography (Contemporary India-I)

96. In arid soils, the lower layers are occupied by kankar because:
(a) Humus increases downwards
(b) Iron content increases downwards
(c) Salt is washed away completely
(d) Calcium content increases downwards
Answer: (d)
Solution: The lower horizons of arid soil are occupied by kankar because of increasing calcium content downwards; the kankar layer restricts the infiltration of water.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Geography (Contemporary India-II)

97. In Tibet, the Brahmaputra is known as the:
(a) Tsang Po
(b) Jamuna
(c) Dihang
(d) Meghna
Answer: (a)
Solution: The Brahmaputra is called the Tsang Po in Tibet, the Dihang where it enters Arunachal Pradesh, and the Jamuna in Bangladesh.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Geography (Contemporary India-I)

98. In Bangladesh, the Brahmaputra is known as the:
(a) Padma
(b) Jamuna
(c) Hooghly
(d) Tsang Po
Answer: (b)
Solution: The Brahmaputra is called the Jamuna in Bangladesh. Tsang Po is its name in Tibet; Padma is a name of the Ganga's main stream in Bangladesh.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Geography (Contemporary India-I)

99. The Shivasamudram Falls, whose hydroelectric power is supplied to Mysuru and Bengaluru, are on which river?
(a) Krishna
(b) Godavari
(c) Kaveri
(d) Sharavati
Answer: (c)
Solution: The Kaveri makes the Shivasamudram Falls, described as the second biggest waterfall in India; their power goes to Mysuru, Bengaluru and the Kolar Gold Field.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Geography (Contemporary India-I)

100. The Jog Falls of Karnataka are formed by which river?
(a) Kaveri
(b) Tungabhadra
(c) Krishna
(d) Sharavati
Answer: (d)
Solution: The Jog Falls are on the west-flowing Sharavati river in Karnataka. Shivasamudram is the waterfall on the Kaveri.
Source: PYQ-style
