Set 3/6 – Physical Geography of India MCQs (Foundation)
Questions 51–75: Medium

51. Which is the correct order of the Himalayan ranges from south to north?
(a) Himadri, Himachal, Shiwaliks (b) Himachal, Shiwaliks, Himadri (c) Shiwaliks, Himachal, Himadri (d) Shiwaliks, Himadri, Himachal
Answer: (c)
Solution: Many students place the Himadri first because it is the highest, but height is not the same as position. The Shiwaliks are the outermost southern range, the Himachal lies north of them, and the Himadri is the northernmost, so the answer is Shiwaliks, Himachal, Himadri.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Geography (Contemporary India-I)

52. The Narmada and the Tapi make estuaries and not deltas. Which feature of these rivers is linked with this?
(a) They are fed by Himalayan glaciers (b) They flow west through rift valleys formed by faulting (c) They flow into the Bay of Bengal (d) They flow over the wide eastern coastal plain
Answer: (b)
Solution: The tempting wrong choice is the Bay of Bengal option, but these rivers never reach the Bay of Bengal at all. The Narmada and the Tapi flow west through rift valleys and enter the Arabian Sea as estuaries, so the answer is that they flow west through rift valleys formed by faulting.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Geography (Contemporary India-I)

53. Consider the following statements about the Indian desert:
1. The Luni, its only large river, drains into the Arabian Sea.
2. It lies towards the western margins of the Aravali hills.
3. Barchans (crescent-shaped dunes) cover large areas of it.
Which of the statements given above is/are correct?
(a) 1 and 2 only (b) 1, 2 and 3 (c) 1 and 3 only (d) 2 and 3 only
Answer: (d)
Solution: Statement 1 is the trap: the Luni ends in the marshy Rann of Kachchh, not in the open Arabian Sea. Statements 2 and 3 are correct, so the answer is 2 and 3 only.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Geography (Contemporary India-I)

54. Black soil is typical of the Deccan Trap region. In which of these areas is it mainly found?
(a) Punjab and Haryana (b) Kerala and coastal Karnataka (c) Maharashtra, Saurashtra and Malwa (d) Assam and Meghalaya
Answer: (c)
Solution: Punjab and Haryana tempt students because they are famous farming regions, but they have alluvial soil. Black soil covers the Deccan Trap region of Maharashtra, Saurashtra, Malwa, Madhya Pradesh and Chhattisgarh, so the answer is Maharashtra, Saurashtra and Malwa.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Geography (Contemporary India-II)

55. Which river, flowing through a rift valley on the eastern margin of the Chotanagpur plateau, was once known as the 'sorrow of Bengal'?
(a) Damodar (b) Kosi (c) Teesta (d) Son
Answer: (a)
Solution: The Kosi is the tempting wrong option, but it is called the 'sorrow of Bihar'. The Damodar flows through a rift valley on the eastern margin of the Chotanagpur plateau and was the 'sorrow of Bengal' before the Damodar Valley Corporation tamed it, so the answer is Damodar.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Geography (India: Physical Environment)

56. Which river is known as the 'sorrow of Bihar' because it frequently changes its course?
(a) Gandak (b) Son (c) Ghaghara (d) Kosi
Answer: (d)
Solution: The Ghaghara and Gandak also flood, but the Kosi is notorious for bringing huge sediment and frequently shifting its course. Hence it is called the 'sorrow of Bihar', so the answer is Kosi.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Geography (India: Physical Environment)

57. The 8° Channel separates:
(a) Minicoy and the main Lakshadweep group (b) Andaman and Nicobar (c) Minicoy and the Maldives (d) South Andaman and Little Andaman
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is the Minicoy–Lakshadweep option, which is actually the 9° Channel; the 10° Channel separates Andaman from Nicobar. The 8° Channel lies between Minicoy and the Maldives, so the answer is Minicoy and the Maldives.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Geography (India: Physical Environment)

58. Which soil is the result of intense leaching due to heavy rain in a climate with alternate wet and dry seasons?
(a) Laterite soil (b) Alluvial soil (c) Black soil (d) Arid soil
Answer: (a)
Solution: Arid soil tempts because of the dry season, but leaching needs heavy rain, which arid regions lack. Laterite soil develops under tropical and subtropical climate with alternate wet and dry seasons through intense leaching, so the answer is laterite soil.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Geography (Contemporary India-II)

59. Which is the correct order of the regional divisions of the Himalayas from west to east?
(a) Assam, Nepal, Kumaon, Punjab (b) Kumaon, Punjab, Nepal, Assam (c) Punjab, Nepal, Kumaon, Assam (d) Punjab, Kumaon, Nepal, Assam
Answer: (d)
Solution: Students often swap Kumaon and Nepal, but Kumaon lies west of Nepal. Indus to Satluj is Punjab Himalaya, Satluj to Kali is Kumaon, Kali to Teesta is Nepal, and Teesta to Dihang is Assam, so the answer is Punjab, Kumaon, Nepal, Assam.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Geography (Contemporary India-I)

60. The Banas, the only significant tributary of the Chambal coming from the west, originates in which hills?
(a) Vindhya (b) Satpura (c) Aravali (d) Kaimur
Answer: (c)
Solution: The Vindhya and Kaimur ranges tempt because most tributaries of the Yamuna system rise there, but the Banas is the exception. It originates in the Aravalis, so the answer is Aravali.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Geography (India: Physical Environment)

61. Which of the following is NOT a tributary of the Ganga?
(a) Ghaghara (b) Wardha (c) Son (d) Gandak
Answer: (b)
Solution: The Ghaghara and Gandak join the Ganga from the Himalayas and the Son from the Peninsular uplands, so they are traps for those who misread the NOT. The Wardha is a tributary of the Godavari, so the answer is Wardha.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Geography (Contemporary India-I)

62. The Ghaghara, the Gandak and the Kosi, which flood parts of the Northern Plains every year, come down from the:
(a) Peninsular uplands (b) Nepal Himalaya (c) Aravali hills (d) Purvachal hills
Answer: (b)
Solution: The Peninsular uplands tempt because rivers like the Son rise there, but the Ghaghara, Gandak and Kosi all rise in the Nepal Himalaya. Their floods damage but also enrich the plains, so the answer is Nepal Himalaya.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Geography (Contemporary India-I)

63. Consider the following pairs:
1. Chilika Lake – Odisha
2. Pulicat Lake – Kerala
3. Vembanad Lake – Karnataka
Which of the pairs given above is/are correctly matched?
(a) 2 only (b) 1 only (c) 1 and 2 only (d) 1, 2 and 3
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is swapping the states of Pulicat and Vembanad: Pulicat lies on the Andhra Pradesh–Tamil Nadu border and Vembanad is in Kerala. Only Chilika–Odisha is correctly matched, so the answer is 1 only.

64. Barren Island, which has India's only active volcano, lies in the:
(a) Arabian Sea (b) Andaman Sea (c) Palk Strait (d) Gulf of Mannar
Answer: (b)
Solution: The Arabian Sea tempts because Lakshadweep lies there, but Barren Island belongs to the Andaman and Nicobar group. It lies in the Andaman Sea, east of the main Andaman Islands, so the answer is Andaman Sea.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Geography (Contemporary India-I)

65. The Yamuna meets the Ganga as a right bank tributary at:
(a) Haridwar (b) Kanpur (c) Varanasi (d) Prayagraj (Allahabad)
Answer: (d)
Solution: Haridwar tempts because the Ganga enters the plains there, but the Yamuna is not there. The Yamuna flows parallel to the Ganga and meets it at Prayagraj (Allahabad), so the answer is Prayagraj (Allahabad).
Source: NCERT Class 9 Geography (Contemporary India-I)

66. Which is the correct order of the parts of the western coastal plain from north to south?
(a) Konkan, Malabar coast, Kannad Plain (b) Malabar coast, Kannad Plain, Konkan (c) Kannad Plain, Konkan, Malabar coast (d) Konkan, Kannad Plain, Malabar coast
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is placing Malabar in the middle, but Malabar is the southernmost stretch. The northern part is the Konkan, the central stretch is the Kannad Plain, and the southern stretch is the Malabar coast, so the answer is Konkan, Kannad Plain, Malabar coast.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Geography (Contemporary India-I)

67. The northern part of the eastern coastal plain is called:
(a) Coromandel Coast (b) Konkan (c) Northern Circar (d) Malabar
Answer: (c)
Solution: The Coromandel Coast tempts, but it is the southern part of the eastern plain; Konkan and Malabar are on the west coast. The northern part along the Bay of Bengal is the Northern Circar, so the answer is Northern Circar.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Geography (Contemporary India-I)

68. The Eastern Ghats are discontinuous and irregular mainly because:
(a) They are older than the Himalayas and have worn flat (b) Rivers like the Mahanadi, Godavari, Krishna and Kaveri cut through them (c) Glaciers have carved deep valleys into them (d) Volcanic eruptions broke them into separate blocks
Answer: (b)
Solution: The age option tempts because the Eastern Ghats are indeed old, but age alone does not make them discontinuous. They are dissected by the rivers flowing east to the Bay of Bengal, so the answer is that rivers like the Mahanadi, Godavari, Krishna and Kaveri cut through them.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Geography (Contemporary India-I)

69. Which of the following is NOT a feature of most Peninsular rivers?
(a) They are perennial because they are fed by glaciers (b) They have shorter and shallower courses than Himalayan rivers (c) Most of them rise in the Western Ghats and flow to the Bay of Bengal (d) Their flow depends on rainfall, so many are seasonal
Answer: (a)
Solution: The first three options are true features, so they trap those who miss the NOT. Glacier-fed perennial flow belongs to Himalayan rivers, so the answer is that they are perennial because they are fed by glaciers.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Geography (Contemporary India-I)

70. The Gondwana rocks of the Damodar, Mahanadi and Godavari valleys are best known for:
(a) Coal (b) Petroleum (c) Gold (d) Diamonds
Answer: (a)
Solution: Petroleum tempts because it is also a fossil fuel, but it is found in sedimentary basins like Assam and Gujarat, not Gondwana rocks. The Gondwana system holds most of India's coal, so the answer is coal.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Geography (India: Physical Environment)

71. How much rainfall does the Indian desert receive in a year?
(a) 1000–1500 mm (b) 500–750 mm (c) Below 150 mm (d) Above 2000 mm
Answer: (c)
Solution: The higher ranges tempt because deserts do get occasional heavy showers, but the yearly total is what matters. The Indian desert has an arid climate with yearly rainfall below 150 mm, so the answer is below 150 mm.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Geography (Contemporary India-I)

72. The Chenab, the largest tributary of the Indus, is formed by the joining of which two streams?
(a) Shyok and Hunza (b) Zaskar and Nubra (c) Chandra and Bhaga (d) Dhauli and Vishnu Ganga
Answer: (c)
Solution: The Dhauli and Vishnu Ganga tempt, but they form the Alaknanda in the Ganga system. The Chandra and the Bhaga join at Tandi near Keylong to form the Chenab, so the answer is Chandra and Bhaga.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Geography (India: Physical Environment)

73. The Bhagirathi and the Alaknanda meet to form the Ganga at:
(a) Rudraprayag (b) Karnaprayag (c) Vishnuprayag (d) Devprayag
Answer: (d)
Solution: Rudraprayag and Karnaprayag tempt because they are also confluences on the Alaknanda, but the Mandakini and Pindar join there. The Bhagirathi meets the Alaknanda at Devprayag, where the river takes the name Ganga, so the answer is Devprayag.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Geography (Contemporary India-I)

74. The Himalayas are geologically young fold mountains formed by the:
(a) Collision of the Indian plate with the Eurasian plate (b) Erosion of the Peninsular plateau (c) Sinking of the Gangetic basin only (d) Volcanic eruptions of the Deccan
Answer: (a)
Solution: The Deccan volcanic option tempts, but that formed the Peninsular trap rocks, not the Himalayas. The Himalayas formed when the northward-moving Indian plate collided with the Eurasian plate, so the answer is the collision of the Indian plate with the Eurasian plate.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Geography (India: Physical Environment)

75. Consider the following statements about Lakshadweep:
1. It is made up of small coral islands.
2. Kavaratti island is its administrative headquarters.
3. Pitti island, which is uninhabited, has a bird sanctuary.
Which of the statements given above is/are correct?
(a) 1 and 2 only (b) 1, 2 and 3 (c) 1 and 3 only (d) 2 and 3 only
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is assuming one statement must be wrong, as in most such questions, but here all three are correct. Lakshadweep lies close to the Malabar coast and is built of coral deposits, so the answer is 1, 2 and 3.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Geography (Contemporary India-I)
