Set 3/6 – Physical Geography of India MCQs (Foundation)
Questions 51–75: Medium

51. Which is the correct order of the Himalayan ranges from south to north?
(a) Himadri, Himachal, Shiwaliks
(b) Shiwaliks, Himachal, Himadri
(c) Himachal, Shiwaliks, Himadri
(d) Shiwaliks, Himadri, Himachal
Answer: (b)
Solution: The Shiwaliks are the outermost (southernmost) range, the Himachal or Lesser Himalaya lies north of them, and the Himadri is the northernmost range.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Geography (Contemporary India-I)

52. The Narmada and the Tapi make estuaries and not deltas. Which feature of these rivers is linked with this?
(a) They are fed by Himalayan glaciers
(b) They flow into the Bay of Bengal
(c) They flow west through rift valleys formed by faulting
(d) They flow over the wide eastern coastal plain
Answer: (c)
Solution: The Narmada and the Tapi flow west through rift valleys and reach the Arabian Sea as estuaries. The east-flowing rivers that cross the broad eastern plain build deltas.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Geography (Contemporary India-I)

53. Consider the following statements about the Indian desert:
1. The Luni, its only large river, drains into the Arabian Sea.
2. It lies towards the western margins of the Aravali hills.
3. Barchans (crescent-shaped dunes) cover large areas of it.
Which of the statements given above is/are correct?
(a) 1 and 2 only
(b) 2 and 3 only
(c) 1 and 3 only
(d) 1, 2 and 3
Answer: (b)
Solution: Statement 1 is wrong because the Luni ends in the Rann of Kachchh, not the open sea. Statements 2 and 3 are correct.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Geography (Contemporary India-I)

54. Black soil is typical of the Deccan Trap region. In which of these areas is it mainly found?
(a) Maharashtra, Saurashtra and Malwa
(b) Kerala and coastal Karnataka
(c) Punjab and Haryana
(d) Assam and Meghalaya
Answer: (a)
Solution: Black soil covers the plateaus of Maharashtra, Saurashtra, Malwa, Madhya Pradesh and Chhattisgarh, the Deccan Trap (basalt) region.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Geography (Contemporary India-II)

55. Which river, flowing through a rift valley on the eastern margin of the Chotanagpur plateau, was once known as the 'sorrow of Bengal'?
(a) Kosi
(b) Damodar
(c) Teesta
(d) Son
Answer: (b)
Solution: The Damodar flows through a rift valley on the eastern margin of the Chotanagpur plateau and joins the Hugli. It was the 'sorrow of Bengal' before the Damodar Valley Corporation tamed it.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Geography (India: Physical Environment)

56. Which river is known as the 'sorrow of Bihar' because it frequently changes its course?
(a) Gandak
(b) Son
(c) Ghaghara
(d) Kosi
Answer: (d)
Solution: The Kosi brings huge amounts of sediment and is notorious for frequently changing its course, hence the 'sorrow of Bihar'. Do not confuse it with the Damodar, the 'sorrow of Bengal'.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Geography (India: Physical Environment)

57. The 8° Channel separates:
(a) Minicoy and the Maldives
(b) Andaman and Nicobar
(c) Minicoy and the main Lakshadweep group
(d) South Andaman and Little Andaman
Answer: (a)
Solution: The 8° Channel lies between Minicoy (India) and the Maldives. The 9° Channel separates Minicoy from the main Lakshadweep group, and the 10° Channel separates Andaman from Nicobar.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Geography (India: Physical Environment)

58. Which soil is the result of intense leaching due to heavy rain in a climate with alternate wet and dry seasons?
(a) Black soil
(b) Alluvial soil
(c) Laterite soil
(d) Arid soil
Answer: (c)
Solution: Laterite soil develops under tropical and subtropical climate with alternate wet and dry seasons and is the result of intense leaching due to heavy rain.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Geography (Contemporary India-II)

59. Which is the correct order of the regional divisions of the Himalayas from west to east?
(a) Punjab, Kumaon, Nepal, Assam
(b) Kumaon, Punjab, Nepal, Assam
(c) Punjab, Nepal, Kumaon, Assam
(d) Assam, Nepal, Kumaon, Punjab
Answer: (a)
Solution: Indus to Satluj is Punjab Himalaya, Satluj to Kali is Kumaon, Kali to Teesta is Nepal, and Teesta to Dihang is Assam Himalaya.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Geography (Contemporary India-I)

60. The Banas, the only significant tributary of the Chambal coming from the west, originates in which hills?
(a) Vindhya
(b) Aravali
(c) Satpura
(d) Kaimur
Answer: (b)
Solution: The Banas is the only significant tributary of the Chambal that originates in the Aravalis. Most tributaries of the Yamuna rise in the Vindhyan and Kaimur ranges.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Geography (India: Physical Environment)

61. Which of the following is NOT a tributary of the Ganga?
(a) Ghaghara
(b) Son
(c) Wardha
(d) Gandak
Answer: (c)
Solution: The Wardha is a tributary of the Godavari. The Ghaghara and Gandak join the Ganga from the Himalayas, and the Son joins it from the Peninsular uplands.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Geography (Contemporary India-I)

62. The Ghaghara, the Gandak and the Kosi, which flood parts of the Northern Plains every year, come down from the:
(a) Nepal Himalaya
(b) Peninsular uplands
(c) Aravali hills
(d) Purvachal hills
Answer: (a)
Solution: The Ghaghara, the Gandak and the Kosi rise in the Nepal Himalaya; their floods cause damage but also enrich the soil of the plains.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Geography (Contemporary India-I)

63. Consider the following pairs:
1. Chilika Lake – Odisha
2. Pulicat Lake – Kerala
3. Vembanad Lake – Karnataka
Which of the pairs given above is/are correctly matched?
(a) 2 only
(b) 1 and 2 only
(c) 1 only
(d) 1, 2 and 3
Answer: (c)
Solution: Chilika is in Odisha. Pulicat lies on the Andhra Pradesh–Tamil Nadu border and Vembanad is in Kerala, so only pair 1 is correct.
Source: PYQ-style

64. Barren Island, which has India's only active volcano, lies in the:
(a) Arabian Sea
(b) Andaman Sea
(c) Palk Strait
(d) Gulf of Mannar
Answer: (b)
Solution: Barren Island belongs to the Andaman and Nicobar group and lies in the Andaman Sea, east of the main Andaman Islands.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Geography (Contemporary India-I)

65. The Yamuna meets the Ganga as a right bank tributary at:
(a) Haridwar
(b) Kanpur
(c) Varanasi
(d) Prayagraj (Allahabad)
Answer: (d)
Solution: The Yamuna rises from the Yamunotri Glacier, flows parallel to the Ganga and meets it at Prayagraj (Allahabad).
Source: NCERT Class 9 Geography (Contemporary India-I)

66. Which is the correct order of the parts of the western coastal plain from north to south?
(a) Konkan, Kannad Plain, Malabar coast
(b) Malabar coast, Kannad Plain, Konkan
(c) Kannad Plain, Konkan, Malabar coast
(d) Konkan, Malabar coast, Kannad Plain
Answer: (a)
Solution: The northern part of the west coast is the Konkan (Mumbai–Goa), the central stretch is the Kannad Plain, and the southern stretch is the Malabar coast.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Geography (Contemporary India-I)

67. The northern part of the eastern coastal plain is called:
(a) Coromandel Coast
(b) Northern Circar
(c) Konkan
(d) Malabar
Answer: (b)
Solution: Along the Bay of Bengal, the northern part of the plain is the Northern Circar and the southern part is the Coromandel Coast.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Geography (Contemporary India-I)

68. The Shevroy Hills and the Javadi Hills are located to the south-east of the:
(a) Western Ghats
(b) Aravali hills
(c) Eastern Ghats
(d) Satpura range
Answer: (c)
Solution: The Shevroy and Javadi Hills lie to the south-east of the Eastern Ghats in Tamil Nadu.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Geography (Contemporary India-I)

69. Which of the following is NOT a feature of most Peninsular rivers?
(a) Their flow depends on rainfall, so many are seasonal
(b) They have shorter and shallower courses than Himalayan rivers
(c) Most of them rise in the Western Ghats and flow to the Bay of Bengal
(d) They are perennial because they are fed by glaciers
Answer: (d)
Solution: Glacier-fed perennial flow is a feature of Himalayan rivers. Peninsular rivers depend on rainfall, have shorter and shallower courses, and most rise in the Western Ghats.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Geography (Contemporary India-I)

70. The Gondwana rocks of the Damodar, Mahanadi and Godavari valleys are best known for:
(a) Petroleum
(b) Coal
(c) Gold
(d) Diamonds
Answer: (b)
Solution: The Gondwana system holds most of India's coal, found mainly in the Damodar, Mahanadi and Godavari valleys.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Geography (India: Physical Environment)

71. How much rainfall does the Indian desert receive in a year?
(a) Below 150 mm
(b) 500–750 mm
(c) 1000–1500 mm
(d) Above 2000 mm
Answer: (a)
Solution: The Indian desert has an arid climate with low vegetation cover; its yearly rainfall is below 150 mm.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Geography (Contemporary India-I)

72. The Chenab, the largest tributary of the Indus, is formed by the joining of which two streams?
(a) Chandra and Bhaga
(b) Zaskar and Nubra
(c) Shyok and Hunza
(d) Dhauli and Vishnu Ganga
Answer: (a)
Solution: The Chandra and the Bhaga join at Tandi near Keylong in Himachal Pradesh to form the Chenab, also called the Chandrabhaga.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Geography (India: Physical Environment)

73. The Bhagirathi and the Alaknanda meet to form the Ganga at:
(a) Rudraprayag
(b) Karnaprayag
(c) Devprayag
(d) Vishnuprayag
Answer: (c)
Solution: At Devprayag in Uttarakhand, the Bhagirathi meets the Alaknanda and the river is called the Ganga from here.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Geography (Contemporary India-I)

74. The Himalayas are geologically young fold mountains formed by the:
(a) Volcanic eruptions of the Deccan
(b) Erosion of the Peninsular plateau
(c) Sinking of the Gangetic basin only
(d) Collision of the Indian plate with the Eurasian plate
Answer: (d)
Solution: The Himalayas formed when the northward-moving Indian plate collided with the Eurasian plate, folding the sediments between them into young fold mountains.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Geography (India: Physical Environment)

75. Consider the following statements about Lakshadweep:
1. It is made up of small coral islands.
2. Kavaratti island is its administrative headquarters.
3. Pitti island, which is uninhabited, has a bird sanctuary.
Which of the statements given above is/are correct?
(a) 1 and 2 only
(b) 2 and 3 only
(c) 1 and 3 only
(d) 1, 2 and 3
Answer: (d)
Solution: All three statements are correct. Lakshadweep lies close to the Malabar coast of Kerala and is built of coral deposits.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Geography (Contemporary India-I)
