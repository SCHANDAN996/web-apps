Books & Authors — Who Wrote What

◆ In 1944 a political prisoner sat in a cell of Ahmednagar Fort with no library and almost no news from outside. In about five months he wrote a long book about his country's past, from the Indus cities to his own time. The prisoner was Jawaharlal Nehru, and the book was "The Discovery of India" (published 1946). Many of the books in this chapter were born like that — in jails, in royal courts, on long journeys. When you know the story behind a book, you will never again forget who wrote it.

PART A — ANCIENT INDIA

1. Politics, Grammar and Drama

Arthashastra — Kautilya (Chanakya), adviser of Chandragupta Maurya. A book on statecraft, administration and economy.
Indica — Megasthenes, the Greek ambassador of Seleucus at the court of Chandragupta Maurya. The original is lost; we know it through quotations by later Greek writers.
Ashtadhyayi — Panini, the great grammarian of Sanskrit.
Mahabhashya — Patanjali, a commentary on Panini's grammar. Patanjali is also linked with the Yoga Sutra.
Natyashastra — Bharata (Bharatamuni), the oldest Indian text on drama, music and dance.
Ramayana — Valmiki (called the "Adi Kavi", the first poet). Mahabharata — Ved Vyasa.

2. Kalidasa and the Court Poets

Kalidasa, the greatest Sanskrit poet of the Gupta period, wrote:
Abhijnanashakuntalam (play), Meghadutam (poem — a cloud carries a message), Raghuvamsham, Kumarasambhavam, Malavikagnimitram, Vikramorvashiyam and Ritusamhara.

Other works of the same tradition:
Mudrarakshasa — Vishakhadatta (a play about Chanakya and Chandragupta Maurya)
Mrichchhakatika ("The Little Clay Cart") — Shudraka
Swapnavasavadattam — Bhasa
Harshacharita and Kadambari — Banabhatta, the court poet of King Harshavardhana
Ratnavali, Nagananda and Priyadarshika — King Harsha himself
Buddhacharita — Ashvaghosha (court of Kanishka)
Prayag Prashasti (Allahabad Pillar inscription praising Samudragupta) — Harishena
Panchatantra — Vishnu Sharma; Hitopadesha — Narayana Pandit
Amarakosha (a Sanskrit dictionary) — Amarasimha
Rajatarangini (history of the kings of Kashmir, 12th century) — Kalhana
Gita Govinda — Jayadeva
Prithviraj Raso — Chand Bardai, court poet of Prithviraj Chauhan

Mnemonic for Kalidasa's four big works: "Shakuntala sent a Megh (cloud) to Raghu and Kumar" — Abhijnanashakuntalam, Meghadutam, Raghuvamsham, Kumarasambhavam.

🧠 Active Recall: Who wrote Harshacharita, and who wrote Ratnavali? (Banabhatta wrote about Harsha; Harsha himself wrote Ratnavali.)

3. Science and Medicine

Aryabhatiya — Aryabhata (mathematics and astronomy)
Brihat Samhita and Pancha Siddhantika — Varahamihira
Brahmasphutasiddhanta — Brahmagupta
Lilavati (part of Siddhanta Shiromani) — Bhaskaracharya (Bhaskara II)
Charaka Samhita (medicine) — Charaka
Sushruta Samhita (surgery) — Sushruta

4. Tamil Classics (Sangam and after)

Tolkappiyam — Tolkappiyar (the oldest Tamil grammar)
Tirukkural — Thiruvalluvar (couplets on virtue, wealth and love)
Silappadikaram — Ilango Adigal (story of Kannagi and Kovalan)
Manimekalai — Sattanar (Sittalai Sattanar)

🧠 Active Recall: Charaka or Sushruta — who is linked with surgery? (Sushruta.)

PART B — MEDIEVAL INDIA

5. Foreign Travellers and Their Books

Fa-Hien (Faxian) — Chinese monk, came in the time of Chandragupta II (Gupta period).
Hiuen Tsang (Xuanzang) — Chinese monk, came in the time of Harshavardhana; his account is known as Si-Yu-Ki.
Al-Biruni — came with Mahmud of Ghazni; wrote Kitab-ul-Hind (also called Tahqiq-i-Hind) in Arabic.
Marco Polo — Venetian traveller of the 13th century; wrote "The Travels".
Ibn Battuta — from Morocco; came in the time of Muhammad bin Tughlaq; his travel book is the Rihla, in Arabic.
François Bernier — French physician; "Travels in the Mughal Empire".

Order to remember (oldest to newest): Megasthenes → Fa-Hien → Hiuen Tsang → Al-Biruni → Marco Polo → Ibn Battuta → Bernier.

6. Sultanate and Mughal Chronicles

Tabaqat-i-Nasiri — Minhaj-us-Siraj
Tarikh-i-Firoz Shahi — Ziauddin Barani
Khazain-ul-Futuh, Tughlaq Nama, Nuh Sipihr — Amir Khusrau (called "Tuti-e-Hind", the parrot of India)
Baburnama (Tuzuk-i-Baburi) — Babur himself, written in Turkish; later translated into Persian by Abdul Rahim Khan-i-Khanan
Humayun-nama — Gulbadan Begum, Babur's daughter (Humayun's sister)
Akbarnama — Abul Fazl; its third volume is the Ain-i-Akbari
Muntakhab-ut-Tawarikh — Abdul Qadir Badauni (often critical of Akbar)
Tuzuk-i-Jahangiri (Jahangirnama) — Jahangir himself
Padshahnama — Abdul Hamid Lahori (history of Shah Jahan's reign)
Majma-ul-Bahrain and Sirr-i-Akbar (Persian translation of the Upanishads) — Dara Shikoh
Amuktamalyada (Telugu) — Krishnadevaraya of Vijayanagara

Hook: "Abul Fazl did Akbar a Fazl (favour)" — he wrote the Akbarnama and Ain-i-Akbari. His brother Faizi was a poet at the same court — do not mix the brothers.

🧠 Active Recall: Which Mughal emperor wrote his own memoir in Turkish? (Babur — the Baburnama.)

7. Bhakti and Early Hindi Literature

Ramcharitmanas (in Awadhi) — Tulsidas; he also wrote Vinay Patrika and Kavitavali
Sursagar (in Braj Bhasha) — Surdas
Padmavat (in Awadhi) — Malik Muhammad Jayasi
Bijak — the sayings of Kabir
Bihari Satsai — Bihari

Adi Granth — compiled by Guru Arjan Dev in 1604

⚠️ Caution: Examiner's Trap

Trap 1 — "Similar Name": Kalidasa and Kalhana sound alike. Kalidasa wrote plays and poems (Shakuntalam, Meghadutam); Kalhana wrote the history of Kashmir (Rajatarangini). Similarly Bankim Chandra (Anandamath) vs Sarat Chandra (Devdas), Panini (grammar) vs Patanjali (commentary, yoga), Kiran Desai vs Anita Desai, Abul Fazl vs his brother Faizi.

Trap 2 — "Almost True Statement": "Hind Swaraj was written by Tilak" — Tilak gave the slogan "Swaraj is my birthright"; the book Hind Swaraj is Gandhi's. Or "Ain-i-Akbari was written by Akbar" — it was written by Abul Fazl about Akbar's rule.

Trap 3 — "Chronological Confusion": Fa-Hien came in the Gupta age (Chandragupta II), Hiuen Tsang in Harsha's time. Ibn Battuta came under Muhammad bin Tughlaq, not under the Mughals.

Trap 4 — "Negative Phrasing": "Which of the following was NOT written by Kalidasa?" — Mudrarakshasa or Mrichchhakatika are planted among his real works.
