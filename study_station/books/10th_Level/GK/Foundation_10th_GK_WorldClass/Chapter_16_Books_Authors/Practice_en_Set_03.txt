Set 3/6 – Books & Authors MCQs (Foundation)
Questions 51–75: Medium

51. The Chinese monk Fa-Hien (Faxian) visited India during the reign of:
(a) Chandragupta Maurya (b) Chandragupta II (c) Harshavardhana (d) Ashoka
Answer: (b)
Solution: Fa-Hien came in the Gupta period, in the reign of Chandragupta II. Trap: the name "Chandragupta" in option (a) points to the Maurya king, who lived centuries earlier.
Source: PYQ-style

52. The Chinese pilgrim Hiuen Tsang (Xuanzang) visited India during the reign of:
(a) Chandragupta II (b) Ashoka (c) Harshavardhana (d) Kanishka
Answer: (c)
Solution: Hiuen Tsang came in the time of Harshavardhana; his account is known as Si-Yu-Ki. Trap: Fa-Hien, the other Chinese monk, came earlier under Chandragupta II.
Source: PYQ-style

53. "Kitab-ul-Hind" was written by:
(a) Ibn Battuta (b) Al-Biruni (c) Amir Khusrau (d) Minhaj-us-Siraj
Answer: (b)
Solution: Al-Biruni wrote Kitab-ul-Hind in Arabic. Trap: Ibn Battuta also wrote in Arabic, but his book is the Rihla.
Source: NCERT Class 12 History (Themes in Indian History Part II)

54. Al-Biruni came to India along with:
(a) Mahmud of Ghazni (b) Muhammad Ghori (c) Babur (d) Timur
Answer: (a)
Solution: Al-Biruni came to India with Mahmud of Ghazni. Muhammad Ghori, Babur and Timur came later.
Source: NCERT Class 12 History (Themes in Indian History Part II)

55. Ibn Battuta, the traveller from Morocco, came to India during the reign of:
(a) Alauddin Khalji (b) Akbar (c) Iltutmish (d) Muhammad bin Tughlaq
Answer: (d)
Solution: Ibn Battuta came during the reign of Muhammad bin Tughlaq. Trap: he is sometimes wrongly placed in the Mughal age (Akbar).
Source: NCERT Class 12 History (Themes in Indian History Part II)

56. The travel account of Ibn Battuta is known as:
(a) Rihla (b) Kitab-ul-Hind (c) Si-Yu-Ki (d) Indica
Answer: (a)
Solution: Ibn Battuta's travel book, written in Arabic, is the Rihla. Kitab-ul-Hind is Al-Biruni's, Si-Yu-Ki is Hiuen Tsang's and Indica is Megasthenes'.
Source: NCERT Class 12 History (Themes in Indian History Part II)

57. "Travels in the Mughal Empire" was written by:
(a) Marco Polo (b) Ibn Battuta (c) François Bernier (d) Al-Biruni
Answer: (c)
Solution: François Bernier, a French physician, wrote Travels in the Mughal Empire. Marco Polo and Ibn Battuta came before the Mughal period.
Source: NCERT Class 12 History (Themes in Indian History Part II)

58. Marco Polo, who wrote "The Travels", came from:
(a) Morocco (b) Venice (c) France (d) Portugal
Answer: (b)
Solution: Marco Polo was a 13th-century traveller from Venice. Trap: Morocco is the home of Ibn Battuta, and France of François Bernier.
Source: PYQ-style

59. "Tarikh-i-Firoz Shahi" is associated with which historian of the Delhi Sultanate?
(a) Ziauddin Barani (b) Minhaj-us-Siraj (c) Amir Khusrau (d) Abul Fazl
Answer: (a)
Solution: Ziauddin Barani wrote the Tarikh-i-Firoz Shahi. Minhaj-us-Siraj wrote Tabaqat-i-Nasiri, and Abul Fazl belongs to the Mughal court.
Source: PYQ-style

60. "Tabaqat-i-Nasiri" was written by:
(a) Ziauddin Barani (b) Abdul Qadir Badauni (c) Minhaj-us-Siraj (d) Abdul Hamid Lahori
Answer: (c)
Solution: Minhaj-us-Siraj wrote the Tabaqat-i-Nasiri. Barani wrote Tarikh-i-Firoz Shahi; Badauni and Lahori were historians of the Mughal period.
Source: PYQ-style

61. Which poet of the Delhi Sultanate is called "Tuti-e-Hind" (the parrot of India)?
(a) Amir Khusrau (b) Faizi (c) Abul Fazl (d) Malik Muhammad Jayasi
Answer: (a)
Solution: Amir Khusrau, who wrote Khazain-ul-Futuh and Tughlaq Nama, is called Tuti-e-Hind. Faizi and Abul Fazl belong to Akbar's court.
Source: PYQ-style

62. The "Humayun-nama" was written by:
(a) Humayun (b) Gulbadan Begum (c) Abul Fazl (d) Jahangir
Answer: (b)
Solution: Gulbadan Begum, Babur's daughter and Humayun's sister, wrote the Humayun-nama. Trap: Humayun's name is in the title, but he did not write it.
Source: NCERT Class 12 History (Themes in Indian History Part II)

63. In which language did Babur write his memoir, the Baburnama?
(a) Persian (b) Arabic (c) Turkish (d) Urdu
Answer: (c)
Solution: Babur wrote the Baburnama in Turkish. Trap: it was later translated into Persian by Abdul Rahim Khan-i-Khanan, so "Persian" looks correct.
Source: PYQ-style

64. "Muntakhab-ut-Tawarikh", a history often critical of Akbar, was written by:
(a) Abul Fazl (b) Faizi (c) Abdul Hamid Lahori (d) Abdul Qadir Badauni
Answer: (d)
Solution: Abdul Qadir Badauni wrote the Muntakhab-ut-Tawarikh. Trap: Abul Fazl wrote the Akbarnama, which praises Akbar.
Source: PYQ-style

65. The "Padshahnama", a history of Shah Jahan's reign, was written by:
(a) Abdul Hamid Lahori (b) Abul Fazl (c) Abdul Qadir Badauni (d) Jahangir
Answer: (a)
Solution: Abdul Hamid Lahori wrote the Padshahnama on Shah Jahan's reign. Abul Fazl and Badauni wrote about Akbar's time.
Source: NCERT Class 12 History (Themes in Indian History Part II)

66. "Tuzuk-i-Jahangiri" (Jahangirnama) was written by:
(a) Nur Jahan (b) Jahangir (c) Abul Fazl (d) Shah Jahan
Answer: (b)
Solution: Jahangir wrote his own memoir, the Tuzuk-i-Jahangiri. Trap: Nur Jahan was his queen, which makes (a) tempting.
Source: PYQ-style

67. "Sirr-i-Akbar", a Persian translation of the Upanishads, was prepared by:
(a) Akbar (b) Aurangzeb (c) Dara Shikoh (d) Abul Fazl
Answer: (c)
Solution: Dara Shikoh, son of Shah Jahan, translated the Upanishads into Persian as Sirr-i-Akbar. Trap: the word "Akbar" in the title tempts students to choose the emperor Akbar.
Source: PYQ-style

68. "Amuktamalyada", a Telugu work, was written by:
(a) Krishnadevaraya (b) Harihara (c) Bukka (d) Tenali Rama
Answer: (a)
Solution: Krishnadevaraya, the Vijayanagara ruler, wrote Amuktamalyada in Telugu. Harihara and Bukka founded the Vijayanagara empire but did not write this book.
Source: NCERT Class 12 History (Themes in Indian History Part II)

69. "Padmavat" was written by:
(a) Tulsidas (b) Surdas (c) Kabir (d) Malik Muhammad Jayasi
Answer: (d)
Solution: Malik Muhammad Jayasi wrote Padmavat in Awadhi. Trap: Tulsidas also wrote in Awadhi (the Ramcharitmanas), so (a) looks close.
Source: PYQ-style

70. Surdas wrote "Sursagar" in which language?
(a) Awadhi (b) Braj Bhasha (c) Maithili (d) Bhojpuri
Answer: (b)
Solution: Sursagar is in Braj Bhasha. Trap: Awadhi is the language of the Ramcharitmanas and Padmavat.
Source: PYQ-style

71. The "Bijak" is a collection of the sayings of:
(a) Kabir (b) Guru Nanak (c) Raidas (d) Dadu Dayal
Answer: (a)
Solution: The Bijak contains the sayings of Kabir. The other three were also Bhakti saints, which makes them tempting.
Source: PYQ-style

72. The Adi Granth was compiled in 1604 by:
(a) Guru Nanak Dev (b) Guru Gobind Singh (c) Guru Arjan Dev (d) Guru Tegh Bahadur
Answer: (c)
Solution: Guru Arjan Dev compiled the Adi Granth in 1604. Trap: Guru Nanak Dev's hymns are in it, but he did not compile it.
Source: NCERT Class 12 History (Themes in Indian History Part II)

73. The "Dnyaneshwari" of Sant Dnyaneshwar is a Marathi commentary on:
(a) the Ramayana (b) the Bhagavad Gita (c) the Upanishads (d) the Puranas
Answer: (b)
Solution: The Dnyaneshwari is a Marathi commentary on the Bhagavad Gita. The other texts are also religious works, so only the fact helps.
Source: PYQ-style

74. Which of the following was NOT written by Tulsidas?
(a) Vinay Patrika (b) Kavitavali (c) Sursagar (d) Ramcharitmanas
Answer: (c)
Solution: Sursagar was written by Surdas. Vinay Patrika, Kavitavali and Ramcharitmanas are all works of Tulsidas.
Source: PYQ-style

75. Which is the correct chronological order (earliest first) of these travellers' visits to India?
(a) Hiuen Tsang → Fa-Hien → Al-Biruni → Ibn Battuta (b) Fa-Hien → Hiuen Tsang → Al-Biruni → Ibn Battuta (c) Fa-Hien → Al-Biruni → Hiuen Tsang → Ibn Battuta (d) Al-Biruni → Fa-Hien → Ibn Battuta → Hiuen Tsang
Answer: (b)
Solution: Fa-Hien (Chandragupta II) → Hiuen Tsang (Harsha) → Al-Biruni (Mahmud of Ghazni) → Ibn Battuta (Muhammad bin Tughlaq). Trap: (a) swaps the two Chinese monks.
Source: PYQ-style
