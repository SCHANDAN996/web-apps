📊 PYQ Pattern Analysis: Books & Authors

Exams this chapter serves: SSC GD, SSC MTS, RRB Group D, state police / constable exams, and the general-awareness part of other state-level tests.

Note: this is a pattern analysis — what kinds of questions these exams like to ask from this chapter and how they trap you. We do not give year-wise counts or percentages, because no reliable data for them exists. The model questions below are written in the exam style; their sources are the textbooks or official bodies behind each fact.

1. What Exams Like to Ask (Sub-topics, in plain words)

Ancient classics — Arthashastra, Indica, Kalidasa's works, Harshacharita, Rajatarangini, Ashtadhyayi, Panchatantra.

Science of ancient India — Aryabhatiya, Charaka Samhita, Sushruta Samhita, Lilavati.

Foreign travellers — Fa-Hien, Hiuen Tsang, Al-Biruni, Ibn Battuta, Bernier: their books and the ruler of their time.

Mughal chronicles — Baburnama, Humayun-nama, Akbarnama / Ain-i-Akbari, Tuzuk-i-Jahangiri, Padshahnama.

Bhakti and Hindi classics — Ramcharitmanas, Padmavat, Sursagar; Premchand, Prasad, Dinkar, Bachchan, Mahadevi Verma.

Freedom struggle — Anandamath, Hind Swaraj, Gita Rahasya, Gulamgiri, Poverty and Un-British Rule, Discovery of India, India Wins Freedom; newspapers like Kesari, Young India, Al-Hilal.

Autobiographies — of leaders (Gandhi, Nehru, Kalam, Mandela) and of sportspersons (Sachin Tendulkar, Milkha Singh, Mary Kom).

Awards linked to books — Nobel 1913 (Tagore), Booker winners of Indian origin, first Jnanpith, and the latest Jnanpith / International Booker (current-affairs side).

2. Exam-wise Style (qualitative)

SSC GD / SSC MTS — short, direct one-liners: "Who wrote …?" and "Whose autobiography is …?". Speed matters.

RRB Group D — similar direct questions, with "Which of the following is NOT …" phrasing and occasional pair-matching.

State police / constable exams — direct facts plus extra weight on writers of the home state's language.

Higher exams that use these same basics (CHSL, NTPC, CGL) add "match the following", "arrange in order" and statement-based questions.

3. Top 10 High-Yield Model Questions with Solutions and Trap Analysis

Question 1
Who wrote the Arthashastra?
(a) Megasthenes (b) Kautilya (c) Vishakhadatta (d) Banabhatta
Answer: (b) Kautilya
Trap Type: Similar Name — Vishakhadatta wrote Mudrarakshasa, a play about Chanakya; Megasthenes wrote Indica about the same court.
15/45-sec Rule: 15-second fact. Score maximizer.
Source: NCERT Class 12 History (Themes in Indian History Part I)

Question 2
Rajatarangini, a history of Kashmir, was written by:
(a) Kalidasa (b) Kalhana (c) Bilhana (d) Jayadeva
Answer: (b) Kalhana
Trap Type: Similar Name — Kalidasa and Bilhana sound close to Kalhana.
15/45-sec Rule: 15-second fact.
Source: PYQ-style

Question 3
Which of the following was NOT written by Kalidasa?
(a) Meghadutam (b) Raghuvamsham (c) Mudrarakshasa (d) Kumarasambhavam
Answer: (c) Mudrarakshasa
Trap Type: Negative Phrasing — Mudrarakshasa is by Vishakhadatta; a student who misses "NOT" picks the first familiar Kalidasa title.
15/45-sec Rule: 45 seconds — read "NOT" twice, then tick each real Kalidasa work.
Source: PYQ-style

Question 4
Kitab-ul-Hind was written by:
(a) Ibn Battuta (b) Al-Biruni (c) Amir Khusrau (d) Ziauddin Barani
Answer: (b) Al-Biruni
Trap Type: Similar Name — Ibn Battuta is the other famous Arabic-writing traveller (Rihla).
15/45-sec Rule: 15-second fact.
Source: NCERT Class 12 History (Themes in Indian History Part II), Through the Eyes of Travellers

Question 5
Ain-i-Akbari was written by:
(a) Akbar (b) Faizi (c) Abul Fazl (d) Badauni
Answer: (c) Abul Fazl
Trap Type: Almost True Statement — the title has Akbar's name; Faizi was Abul Fazl's brother; Badauni wrote a critical history of Akbar.
15/45-sec Rule: 15-second fact.
Source: NCERT Class 12 History (Themes in Indian History Part II), Kings and Chronicles

Question 6
The song "Vande Mataram" is taken from which novel?
(a) Devdas (b) Gora (c) Anandamath (d) Durgeshnandini
Answer: (c) Anandamath
Trap Type: Similar Name — Durgeshnandini is also by Bankim Chandra; Devdas is by Sarat Chandra.
15/45-sec Rule: 15-second fact. Score maximizer.
Source: PYQ-style

Question 7
"Hind Swaraj" was written by:
(a) Bal Gangadhar Tilak (b) Mahatma Gandhi (c) Lala Lajpat Rai (d) Dadabhai Naoroji
Answer: (b) Mahatma Gandhi
Trap Type: Almost True Statement — Tilak's slogan "Swaraj is my birthright" pulls students towards (a).
15/45-sec Rule: 15-second fact.
Source: NCERT Class 12 History (Themes in Indian History Part III)

Question 8
Arrange these travellers in the order of their visit to India:
I. Hiuen Tsang II. Ibn Battuta III. Megasthenes IV. Al-Biruni
(a) III, I, IV, II (b) I, III, IV, II (c) III, IV, I, II (d) III, I, II, IV
Answer: (a) III, I, IV, II
Trap Type: Chronological Confusion — students often place Al-Biruni before Hiuen Tsang.
15/45-sec Rule: 45-second analytical question. Write the ruler beside each name first (Chandragupta Maurya, Harsha, Mahmud of Ghazni, Muhammad bin Tughlaq).
Source: PYQ-style

Question 9
"The Inheritance of Loss", which won the Booker Prize in 2006, was written by:
(a) Anita Desai (b) Kiran Desai (c) Arundhati Roy (d) Jhumpa Lahiri
Answer: (b) Kiran Desai
Trap Type: Similar Name — Anita Desai is her mother and also a well-known novelist.
15/45-sec Rule: 15-second fact.
Source: thebookerprizes.com

Question 10
"Wings of Fire" is the autobiography of:
(a) Vikram Sarabhai (b) A. P. J. Abdul Kalam (c) Homi J. Bhabha (d) C. V. Raman
Answer: (b) A. P. J. Abdul Kalam
Trap Type: Similar field — all four are scientists, so the field does not help; only the fact does.
15/45-sec Rule: 15-second fact.
Source: PYQ-style

4. How to Use This Analysis

Make your own two-column list of "book — author" pairs for each part (ancient, medieval, modern, world); these are the quickest marks in the paper.

Every time you see "NOT", "except" or "incorrectly matched", slow down — that is a 45-second question, not a 15-second one.

For travellers and chronicles, learn the ruler of each author's time; that one link answers both "who wrote" and "arrange in order" questions.

For awards, always learn the year with the name — exams change the year in the options.
