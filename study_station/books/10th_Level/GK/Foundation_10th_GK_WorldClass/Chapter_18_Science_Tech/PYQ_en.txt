📊 PYQ Pattern Analysis: Science & Technology (Basic)

Exams this chapter serves: SSC GD, SSC MTS, RRB Group D, state police / constable exams, and the general-awareness part of other state-level tests.

Note: this is a pattern analysis — what kinds of questions these exams like to ask from this chapter and how they trap you. We do not give year-wise counts or percentages, because no reliable data for them exists. The model questions below are written in the exam style; their sources are the NCERT chapters or official bodies (ISRO, PIB) they test.

1. What Exams Like to Ask (Sub-topics, in plain words)

Inventors and inventions — the classic one-liner: telephone, bulb, aeroplane, television, radio, dynamite, World Wide Web.

Discoverers — electron, nucleus, neutron, X-rays, radioactivity, penicillin, vaccines, blood groups, DNA structure, periodic table.

Units — SI unit of a quantity (force, pressure, power, frequency, current, resistance), the 7 base units, and "what does this unit measure?" (light-year, knot, decibel, angstrom).

Computer basics — full forms (CPU, RAM, ROM, URL, HTML, USB), input versus output devices, RAM versus ROM, bit–byte–KB, generations, Charles Babbage.

Space programme — ISRO's founding, Aryabhata, SLV-3, Rakesh Sharma, Chandrayaan-1/2/3, Mangalyaan, Aditya-L1, launch centres, and recent missions with dates.

Nuclear and defence — Pokhran-I and II, BARC, Bhabha, DRDO, IGMDP missiles and their types, BrahMos, INS Vikrant.

Indian scientists and science days — Raman, J. C. Bose, Ramanujan, S. N. Bose, Kalam, Sarabhai; 28 February, 11 May, 23 August, 22 December.

Institutions and cities — CDRI, CFTRI, CCMB, NIO, NCL, PRL, TIFR, IISc.

2. Exam-wise Style (qualitative)

SSC GD / SSC MTS — short one-liners: inventor, unit, full form, first Indian satellite, ISRO headquarters. Speed matters.

RRB Group D — many unit and discovery questions (close to the NCERT science syllabus), plus "Which is NOT …" phrasing.

State police / constable exams — direct facts, with a preference for recent ISRO missions and national days.

Higher exams that use these same basics (CHSL, NTPC, CGL) add statement-based questions on missions and "arrange in order" questions on milestones.

3. Top 10 High-Yield Model Questions with Solutions and Trap Analysis

Question 1
The neutron was discovered by:
(a) J. J. Thomson (b) Ernest Rutherford (c) James Chadwick (d) Niels Bohr
Answer: (c) James Chadwick
Trap Type: Similar Name — all four worked on the atom; Thomson found the electron and Rutherford the nucleus.
15/45-sec Rule: 15-second fact. Score maximizer.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Science, Structure of the Atom

Question 2
Which of the following is NOT an SI base unit?
(a) Kelvin (b) Candela (c) Newton (d) Mole
Answer: (c) Newton
Trap Type: Negative Phrasing — newton is an SI unit, but a derived one (kg m s^-2).
15/45-sec Rule: 45 seconds — read "NOT" twice, then recall the 7 base units with the mnemonic.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Physics, Units and Measurement

Question 3
"Light-year" is a unit of:
(a) Time (b) Distance (c) Speed of light (d) Intensity of light
Answer: (b) Distance
Trap Type: Almost True Statement — the word "year" pulls students towards time.
15/45-sec Rule: 15-second fact.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Physics, Units and Measurement

Question 4
India's first satellite Aryabhata was launched in 1975 with the help of:
(a) USA (b) France (c) Soviet Union (d) Japan
Answer: (c) Soviet Union
Trap Type: Almost True Statement — ISRO built Aryabhata, so "India launched it" sounds right; the rocket was Soviet.
15/45-sec Rule: 15-second fact.
Source: ISRO (isro.gov.in)

Question 5
Chandrayaan-3's Vikram lander touched down on the Moon on:
(a) 14 July 2023 (b) 23 August 2023 (c) 2 September 2023 (d) 22 July 2019
Answer: (b) 23 August 2023
Trap Type: Chronological Confusion — 14 July 2023 is the launch date, 2 September 2023 is Aditya-L1's launch, and 22 July 2019 is Chandrayaan-2's launch.
15/45-sec Rule: 15-second fact. Score maximizer.
Source: ISRO / PIB, 2023

Question 6
Which memory loses its contents when the power is switched off?
(a) ROM (b) Hard disk (c) RAM (d) Pen drive
Answer: (c) RAM
Trap Type: Almost True Statement — ROM and RAM differ by one letter; ROM is non-volatile.
15/45-sec Rule: 15-second fact.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Computer Science, Computer System

Question 7
Which of the following was NOT one of the five missiles of the Integrated Guided Missile Development Programme?
(a) Prithvi (b) Nag (c) BrahMos (d) Trishul
Answer: (c) BrahMos
Trap Type: Negative Phrasing + Similar Name — BrahMos is a well-known Indian missile, but it came from an India–Russia joint venture, not from IGMDP.
15/45-sec Rule: 45 seconds — run through "Prithvi, Agni, Akash, Trishul, Nag" first.
Source: DRDO (drdo.gov.in)

Question 8
National Technology Day is observed on 11 May to mark:
(a) The launch of Aryabhata (b) The Pokhran-II nuclear tests of 1998 (c) The Pokhran-I test of 1974 (d) The landing of Chandrayaan-3
Answer: (b) The Pokhran-II nuclear tests of 1998
Trap Type: Chronological Confusion — Pokhran-I was on 18 May 1974, a different date.
15/45-sec Rule: 15-second fact.
Source: PIB (pib.gov.in)

Question 9
The laws of heredity were given by:
(a) Dmitri Mendeleev (b) Gregor Mendel (c) Charles Darwin (d) Robert Hooke
Answer: (b) Gregor Mendel
Trap Type: Similar Name — Mendeleev gave the periodic table; Darwin gave the theory of evolution.
15/45-sec Rule: 15-second fact.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science, Heredity

Question 10
Central Food Technological Research Institute (CFTRI) is located in:
(a) Lucknow (b) Mysuru (c) Hyderabad (d) Pune
Answer: (b) Mysuru
Trap Type: Similar Name — CDRI (Lucknow), CCMB (Hyderabad) and NCL (Pune) are other CSIR labs with similar-looking abbreviations.
15/45-sec Rule: 15-second fact.
Source: CSIR (csir.res.in)

4. How to Use This Analysis

Make your own list of inventor–invention and quantity–unit pairs; these are the quickest marks in the paper.

Every time you see "NOT", "except" or "incorrectly matched", slow down — that is a 45-second question, not a 15-second one.

For space and defence, learn the sequence first (1962 → 1963 → 1969 → 1972 → 1974 → 1975 → 1980 → 1984 → 1998 → 2008 → 2013 → 2019 → 2023) and then attach events to each year. For missions after 2023, always learn the exact date from isro.gov.in or pib.gov.in.
