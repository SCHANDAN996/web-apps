Set 3/6 – Science & Technology MCQs (Foundation)
Questions 51–75: Medium

51. The double-helix structure of DNA was proposed in 1953 by:
(a) Mendel and Morgan (b) Banting and Best (c) Pierre and Marie Curie (d) Watson and Crick
Answer: (d)
Solution: Banting and Best are linked with insulin and the Curies with radium, so those pairs are traps. James Watson and Francis Crick proposed the double-helix model of DNA in 1953, so the answer is Watson and Crick.
Source: PYQ-style

52. Blood groups (A, B, O) were discovered by:
(a) Robert Koch (b) William Harvey (c) Karl Landsteiner (d) Edward Jenner
Answer: (c)
Solution: William Harvey described the circulation of blood, which is a tempting but different blood-related discovery. The ABO blood groups were discovered by Karl Landsteiner, so the answer is Karl Landsteiner.
Source: PYQ-style

53. The periodic table of elements (1869) was given by:
(a) Gregor Mendel (b) Dmitri Mendeleev (c) John Dalton (d) Antoine Lavoisier
Answer: (b)
Solution: The similar name Gregor Mendel is the trap — he gave the laws of heredity, not the periodic table. Dmitri Mendeleev arranged the known elements by atomic mass in 1869, so the answer is Dmitri Mendeleev.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Chemistry, Classification of Elements

54. The laws of inheritance were worked out by experiments on pea plants by:
(a) Gregor Mendel (b) Charles Darwin (c) Dmitri Mendeleev (d) Hugo de Vries
Answer: (a)
Solution: Mendeleev is the similar-name trap — he made the periodic table. Gregor Mendel studied pea plants and gave the laws of inheritance, so the answer is Gregor Mendel.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science, Heredity

55. Electromagnetic induction, the principle behind the electric generator, was discovered by:
(a) Michael Faraday (b) Isaac Newton (c) Alessandro Volta (d) Thomas Edison
Answer: (a)
Solution: Edison is a trap because he is linked with electrical inventions like the bulb, not with induction. Michael Faraday discovered electromagnetic induction in 1831, on which generators work, so the answer is Michael Faraday.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science, Magnetic Effects of Electric Current

56. Dynamite was invented by:
(a) Alfred Nobel (b) Rudolf Diesel (c) Robert Boyle (d) James Watt
Answer: (a)
Solution: James Watt (steam engine) and Rudolf Diesel (diesel engine) are invention-related traps. Alfred Nobel invented dynamite in 1867 and his will set up the Nobel Prizes, so the answer is Alfred Nobel.
Source: PYQ-style

57. The printing press with movable metal type was developed in Europe by:
(a) William Caxton (b) Galileo Galilei (c) Johannes Gutenberg (d) Johannes Kepler
Answer: (c)
Solution: Caxton is the trap — he set up printing in England later. Johannes Gutenberg developed the printing press with movable metal type around 1440 in Germany, so the answer is Johannes Gutenberg.
Source: NCERT Class 10 History, Print Culture and the Modern World

58. The first public demonstration of television was given by:
(a) John Logie Baird (b) Alexander Graham Bell (c) Guglielmo Marconi (d) Samuel Morse
Answer: (a)
Solution: Marconi (radio) and Morse (telegraph code) are communication-related traps. John Logie Baird gave the first public demonstration of television in 1926, so the answer is John Logie Baird.
Source: PYQ-style

59. Kelvin is the SI unit of:
(a) Temperature (b) Heat (c) Energy (d) Pressure
Answer: (a)
Solution: Heat is the trap — it is a form of energy and is measured in joule, not kelvin. Kelvin (K) is the SI base unit of temperature, so the answer is temperature.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Science, Matter in Our Surroundings

60. The joule is the SI unit of:
(a) Force (b) Power (c) Momentum (d) Work and energy
Answer: (d)
Solution: Power is the trap — it is measured in watt, which is joule per second. Work and energy are both measured in joule, where 1 joule = 1 newton × 1 metre, so the answer is work and energy.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Science, Work and Energy

61. "Knot" is a unit used to measure:
(a) Depth of sea (b) Distance between stars (c) Speed of ships (d) Intensity of sound
Answer: (c)
Solution: Depth of sea is the trap — it is measured in fathoms, while sound level is in decibels. A knot is one nautical mile per hour, used for the speed of ships and aircraft, so the answer is speed of ships.
Source: PYQ-style

62. The power of a lens is measured in:
(a) Candela (b) Watt (c) Lux (d) Dioptre
Answer: (d)
Solution: Watt is the trap — it is the unit of power in mechanics and electricity, not of a lens. The power of a lens is the reciprocal of its focal length in metres and its unit is the dioptre, so the answer is dioptre.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science, Light – Reflection and Refraction

63. Decibel (dB) is a unit used for:
(a) Frequency of sound (b) Sound level (loudness) (c) Wavelength of sound (d) Speed of sound
Answer: (b)
Solution: Frequency is the trap — it is measured in hertz, and speed in metre per second. The decibel measures the level (loudness) of sound, so the answer is sound level.
Source: PYQ-style

64. Which of the following is an output device?
(a) Scanner (b) Printer (c) Mouse (d) Microphone
Answer: (b)
Solution: Scanner, mouse and microphone are traps because they all send data into the computer. A printer gives the result of processing on paper, so the answer is printer.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Computer Science, Computer System

65. The full form of ROM is:
(a) Random Only Memory (b) Run Only Memory (c) Read Output Memory (d) Read Only Memory
Answer: (d)
Solution: "Random Only Memory" is the trap — it mixes up ROM with RAM (Random Access Memory). ROM stands for Read Only Memory, which is non-volatile and stores start-up instructions, so the answer is Read Only Memory.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Computer Science, Computer System

66. HTML stands for:
(a) Home Tool Markup Language (b) HyperText Markup Language (c) HyperText Main Link (d) High Transfer Machine Language
Answer: (b)
Solution: "High Transfer Machine Language" is the trap — it imitates HTTP (HyperText Transfer Protocol), the rule for sending web pages. HTML is HyperText Markup Language, used to create web pages, so the answer is HyperText Markup Language.
Source: PYQ-style

67. First-generation computers used:
(a) Integrated circuits (b) Transistors (c) Vacuum tubes (d) Microprocessors
Answer: (c)
Solution: Transistors are the trap — they came in the second generation, and ICs in the third. First-generation computers such as ENIAC (1946) used vacuum tubes, so the answer is vacuum tubes.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Computer Science, Computer System

68. India's first sounding rocket was launched from Thumba in:
(a) 1963 (b) 1975 (c) 1969 (d) 1980
Answer: (a)
Solution: 1969 is the trap — that is the year ISRO was formed, while Aryabhata went up in 1975 and SLV-3 flew in 1980. The first sounding rocket rose from Thumba in 1963, so the answer is 1963.
Source: ISRO (isro.gov.in)

69. Thumba, the site of India's first rocket launch, is near:
(a) Sriharikota (b) Balasore (c) Chennai (d) Thiruvananthapuram
Answer: (d)
Solution: Sriharikota is the trap — it is India's main spaceport, but it is in Andhra Pradesh, not Kerala. Thumba Equatorial Rocket Launching Station is near Thiruvananthapuram, so the answer is Thiruvananthapuram.
Source: ISRO (isro.gov.in)

70. SLV-3, India's first satellite launch vehicle, placed which satellite in orbit in 1980?
(a) Aryabhata (b) Bhaskara (c) Rohini (d) INSAT-1B
Answer: (c)
Solution: Aryabhata is the trap — it was India's first satellite (1975) but was launched by a Soviet rocket. On 18 July 1980 SLV-3 put the Rohini satellite into orbit from Sriharikota, so the answer is Rohini.
Source: ISRO (isro.gov.in)

71. Who was the project director of SLV-3?
(a) Vikram Sarabhai (b) A. P. J. Abdul Kalam (c) Satish Dhawan (d) K. Kasturirangan
Answer: (b)
Solution: Vikram Sarabhai is the trap — he founded India's space programme but died in 1971, before SLV-3 flew. A. P. J. Abdul Kalam was the project director of SLV-3, so the answer is A. P. J. Abdul Kalam.
Source: ISRO (isro.gov.in)

72. Chandrayaan-2 was launched in 2019 by:
(a) PSLV-C11 (b) PSLV-C25 (c) SLV-3 (d) GSLV Mk III
Answer: (d)
Solution: PSLV-C11 is the trap — it launched Chandrayaan-1, while PSLV-C25 carried the Mars Orbiter Mission. Chandrayaan-2 was launched on 22 July 2019 by GSLV Mk III (now called LVM3), so the answer is GSLV Mk III.
Source: ISRO (isro.gov.in)

73. The landing site of Chandrayaan-3 on the Moon was named:
(a) Tiranga Point (b) Shiv Shakti Point (c) Jawahar Point (d) Sarabhai Point
Answer: (b)
Solution: Tiranga Point is the trap — it marks the spot where Chandrayaan-2's lander crashed in 2019. The Chandrayaan-3 landing site was named Shiv Shakti Point in 2023, so the answer is Shiv Shakti Point.
Source: PIB (pib.gov.in), 2023

74. INS Vikrant, commissioned on 2 September 2022, is India's first indigenous:
(a) Destroyer (b) Nuclear submarine (c) Aircraft carrier (d) Frigate
Answer: (c)
Solution: Nuclear submarine is the trap — INS Arihant fills that role, not Vikrant. INS Vikrant, built at Cochin Shipyard, is India's first indigenously built aircraft carrier, so the answer is aircraft carrier.
Source: PIB (pib.gov.in), 2022

75. C. V. Raman was awarded the Nobel Prize in Physics in:
(a) 1930 (b) 1913 (c) 1928 (d) 1983
Answer: (a)
Solution: 1928 is the trap — that is the year the Raman Effect was announced, not the Nobel year; 1983 belongs to S. Chandrasekhar. Raman received the Nobel Prize in Physics in 1930, so the answer is 1930.
Source: PYQ-style
