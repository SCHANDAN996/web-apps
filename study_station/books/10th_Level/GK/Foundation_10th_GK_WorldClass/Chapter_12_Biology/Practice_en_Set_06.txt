Set 6/6 – Biology: Human Body MCQs (Foundation)
Questions 126–150: Hard

126. Consider the following statements:
Statement I: Bile is made in the gall bladder.
Statement II: Bile contains no digestive enzyme.
Which of the statements given is/are correct?
(a) I only (b) II only (c) Both I and II (d) Neither I nor II
Answer: (b)
Solution: Bile is made in the liver and only stored in the gall bladder, so Statement I is wrong. Bile has no enzyme; it emulsifies fats, so Statement II is correct.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science (Life Processes)

127. Consider the following statements:
Statement I: The pulmonary artery carries deoxygenated blood from the heart to the lungs.
Statement II: The pulmonary vein carries oxygenated blood from the lungs to the heart.
Which of the statements given is/are correct?
(a) I only (b) II only (c) Both I and II (d) Neither I nor II
Answer: (c)
Solution: Both statements describe the lung route correctly. Vessels are named by direction: arteries leave the heart and veins return to it, whatever the oxygen content.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science (Life Processes)

128. Consider the following statements:
Statement I: Red blood cells are formed in the bone marrow.
Statement II: Mature human red blood cells have a nucleus.
Which of the statements given is/are correct?
(a) I only (b) II only (c) Both I and II (d) Neither I nor II
Answer: (a)
Solution: Red blood cells are made in the bone marrow, so Statement I is correct. Mature human red blood cells lose their nucleus, so Statement II is wrong.
Source: PYQ-style

129. Consider the following statements:
Statement I: The cerebrum is a part of the hind-brain.
Statement II: The medulla controls thinking and memory.
Which of the statements given is/are correct?
(a) I only (b) II only (c) Both I and II (d) Neither I nor II
Answer: (d)
Solution: The cerebrum belongs to the fore-brain, and thinking and memory are its jobs; the medulla controls involuntary actions. Both statements are wrong.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science (Control and Coordination)

130. Which of the following statements about cell organelles is correct?
(a) Ribosomes are the powerhouse of the cell (b) Lysosomes contain digestive enzymes (c) Animal cells have a cell wall made of cellulose (d) The Golgi apparatus stores hereditary information
Answer: (b)
Solution: Lysosomes are membrane-bound sacs full of digestive enzymes. Mitochondria, not ribosomes, are the powerhouse; animal cells have no cell wall; hereditary information is in the nucleus.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Science (The Fundamental Unit of Life)

131. Which organ acts both as an endocrine gland and as a gland that pours digestive juice into the intestine?
(a) Thyroid (b) Pituitary (c) Adrenal (d) Pancreas
Answer: (d)
Solution: The pancreas secretes pancreatic juice into the small intestine and also secretes the hormone insulin into the blood. Thyroid, pituitary and adrenal are only endocrine.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science (Life Processes)

132. Why is a person with blood group O called a universal donor?
(a) The red blood cells carry neither A nor B antigen (b) The plasma has no antibodies at all (c) The red blood cells carry both A and B antigens (d) The blood has no plasma
Answer: (a)
Solution: Since group O red cells have no A or B antigen, the antibodies in the recipient's plasma do not react against them. Option (c) describes group AB.
Source: PYQ-style

133. In the ABO system, a person with blood group AB can receive red blood cells from donors of group:
(a) AB only (b) A and B only (c) A, B, AB and O (d) O only
Answer: (c)
Solution: Group AB plasma has neither anti-A nor anti-B antibodies, so red cells of any ABO group can be accepted. That is why AB is the universal recipient.
Source: PYQ-style

134. Consider the following statements:
Statement I: Breathing is the process in which energy is released from food inside the cells.
Statement II: Cellular respiration releases energy from food, mostly in the mitochondria when oxygen is present.
Which of the statements given is/are correct?
(a) I only (b) II only (c) Both I and II (d) Neither I nor II
Answer: (b)
Solution: Breathing is only the movement of air into and out of the lungs; releasing energy from food is respiration. Statement I mixes up the two terms.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science (Life Processes)

135. Which of the following correctly pairs an organism with the products of its anaerobic respiration?
(a) Yeast – lactic acid and energy (b) Human muscle – ethanol and carbon dioxide (c) Yeast – ethanol and carbon dioxide (d) Human muscle – carbon dioxide and water
Answer: (c)
Solution: Yeast ferments pyruvate into ethanol and carbon dioxide. Human muscles short of oxygen make lactic acid; carbon dioxide and water come from aerobic respiration.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science (Life Processes)

136. Which of the following shows the correct path of air when we breathe in?
(a) Nostrils → trachea → larynx → alveoli → bronchi (b) Nostrils → bronchi → trachea → pharynx → alveoli (c) Nostrils → larynx → pharynx → bronchi → trachea → alveoli (d) Nostrils → pharynx → larynx → trachea → bronchi → alveoli
Answer: (d)
Solution: Air enters through the nostrils, passes the throat (pharynx) and voice box (larynx), goes down the trachea into the bronchi and bronchioles, and finally reaches the alveoli.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science (Life Processes)

137. Which of the following shows the correct order of the parts of the alimentary canal?
(a) Mouth → oesophagus → stomach → small intestine → large intestine (b) Mouth → stomach → oesophagus → large intestine → small intestine (c) Mouth → oesophagus → small intestine → stomach → large intestine (d) Mouth → stomach → small intestine → oesophagus → large intestine
Answer: (a)
Solution: Food goes from the mouth through the oesophagus to the stomach, then to the small intestine and finally to the large intestine.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science (Life Processes)

138. Arrange the following in chronological order: cell theory by Schleiden and Schwann; discovery of the nucleus by Robert Brown; naming of the cell by Robert Hooke; "cells arise from pre-existing cells" by Virchow.
(a) Hooke, Virchow, Brown, Schleiden–Schwann (b) Brown, Hooke, Schleiden–Schwann, Virchow (c) Hooke, Brown, Schleiden–Schwann, Virchow (d) Hooke, Schleiden–Schwann, Brown, Virchow
Answer: (c)
Solution: Hooke named the cell in 1665, Brown discovered the nucleus in 1831, Schleiden and Schwann gave the cell theory in 1838–39, and Virchow added his idea in 1855.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Science (The Fundamental Unit of Life)

139. Which of the following pairs consists of two water-soluble vitamins?
(a) A and C (b) B1 and C (c) D and K (d) E and B12
Answer: (b)
Solution: B-group vitamins and Vitamin C are water-soluble, so B1 and C are both water-soluble. Each of the other pairs contains at least one fat-soluble vitamin among A, D, E and K.
Source: PYQ-style

140. Which of the following is NOT a function of the liver?
(a) Making bile (b) Forming urea (c) Secreting insulin (d) Storing glycogen
Answer: (c)
Solution: Insulin is secreted by the pancreas. The liver makes bile, forms urea and stores glycogen. Trap: both the liver and the pancreas are digestive glands.
Source: PYQ-style

141. Why do antibiotics not cure the common cold?
(a) The common cold is caused by a virus, and antibiotics block pathways used by bacteria (b) The common cold is caused by a bacterium that resists all antibiotics (c) Antibiotics work only against fungi (d) The common cold is caused by a protozoan
Answer: (a)
Solution: Antibiotics block biochemical pathways that bacteria need; viruses do not use these pathways. Since the common cold is viral, antibiotics have no effect on it.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Science (Why Do We Fall Ill)

142. Swelling of the neck (goitre) was once common in some hilly areas. Shortage of which nutrient, affecting which gland, leads to it?
(a) Iron – adrenal gland (b) Iodine – pituitary gland (c) Calcium – thyroid gland (d) Iodine – thyroid gland
Answer: (d)
Solution: The thyroid gland needs iodine to make thyroxin; without enough iodine it enlarges, causing goitre. Iodised salt helps prevent it.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science (Control and Coordination)

143. Consider the following statements:
Statement I: Myopia is corrected by a convex lens.
Statement II: Hypermetropia is corrected by a convex lens.
Which of the statements given is/are correct?
(a) I only (b) II only (c) Both I and II (d) Neither I nor II
Answer: (b)
Solution: Myopia (near-sightedness) needs a concave lens, so Statement I is wrong. Hypermetropia (far-sightedness) is corrected by a convex lens, so Statement II is correct.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science (The Human Eye and the Colourful World)

144. Consider the following statements:
Statement I: Rod cells of the retina respond to dim light.
Statement II: Cone cells of the retina help us see colours.
Which of the statements given is/are correct?
(a) I only (b) II only (c) Both I and II (d) Neither I nor II
Answer: (c)
Solution: Rods respond to the intensity of light, including dim light, and cones respond to colour. Both statements are correct.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science (The Human Eye and the Colourful World)

145. Which of the following pairs is INCORRECTLY matched?
(a) Femur – longest bone (b) Stapes – smallest bone (c) Skin – largest organ (d) Thyroid – largest gland
Answer: (d)
Solution: The liver, not the thyroid, is the largest gland of the human body. The other three pairs are correct.
Source: PYQ-style

146. When you touch a hot object, you pull your hand away almost before you feel the pain. This is possible mainly because:
(a) The reflex arc connecting the sensory and motor nerves is formed in the spinal cord (b) The cerebellum sends the command after thinking (c) Hormones carry the message to the hand (d) The medulla controls all voluntary actions
Answer: (a)
Solution: In a reflex action, the connection between the input and output nerves is made in the spinal cord, so the response is quick. Hormonal messages are much slower.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science (Control and Coordination)

147. Which of the following pairs of hormone and condition is INCORRECTLY matched?
(a) Insulin – diabetes (b) Thyroxin – goitre (c) Growth hormone – dwarfism (d) Adrenaline – rickets
Answer: (d)
Solution: Rickets is caused by Vitamin D deficiency, not by adrenaline. The other three pairs correctly link a hormone with the condition caused by its deficiency.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science (Control and Coordination)

148. Consider the following statements about sex determination in human beings:
Statement I: The mother's egg always carries an X chromosome.
Statement II: The sex of the child is decided by the chromosome present in the mother's egg.
Which of the statements given is/are correct?
(a) I only (b) II only (c) Both I and II (d) Neither I nor II
Answer: (a)
Solution: All eggs carry an X chromosome, so Statement I is correct. Whether the child is a boy or a girl depends on whether the sperm carries X or Y, so Statement II is wrong.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science (Heredity)

149. Which of the following sets of disease, type of pathogen and vector is entirely correct?
(a) Malaria – virus – Aedes (b) Dengue – virus – Aedes (c) Filaria – bacterium – Anopheles (d) Kala-azar – protozoan – Tsetse fly
Answer: (b)
Solution: Dengue is a viral disease spread by Aedes. Malaria is protozoan and spread by female Anopheles; filaria is a worm spread by Culex; kala-azar is spread by the sandfly.
Source: PYQ-style

150. Consider the following statements:
Statement I: The World Health Organization declared smallpox eradicated in 1980.
Statement II: The World Health Organization certified India polio-free in 2014.
Which of the statements given is/are correct?
(a) I only (b) II only (c) Both I and II (d) Neither I nor II
Answer: (c)
Solution: WHO declared the global eradication of smallpox in 1980, and India, with the South-East Asia Region, was certified polio-free by WHO in March 2014. Both statements are correct.
Source: PYQ-style
