📊 PYQ Pattern Analysis: Biology – Human Body

Exams this level prepares for: SSC GD, SSC MTS, RRB Group D, State Police / Constable exams.
This section describes how these exams usually ask questions from this chapter — the favourite sub-topics, the question shapes and the traps. It does not give counts or percentages; the sample questions below are written in the exam style and carry their textbook source.

1. Sub-topics exams return to again and again
Vitamins and deficiency diseases — the single most dependable area: vitamin–disease pairs, chemical names (ascorbic acid, retinol, calciferol), fat-soluble vs water-soluble.
Diseases and their causes — bacterial vs viral vs protozoan; which mosquito or fly spreads which disease; vaccines such as BCG.
Blood and heart — blood groups (universal donor / recipient), components of blood and their jobs, chambers of the heart, arteries vs veins, blood pressure instrument.
Glands and hormones — master gland, thyroxin–iodine–goitre, insulin–diabetes, adrenaline.
Superlatives of the body — largest gland, longest bone, smallest bone, longest cell, largest organ.
Cell basics — organelle nicknames (powerhouse, suicide bag), who discovered the cell.
Digestion — where pepsin, trypsin and bile are made and what they do.
Eye — lens used to correct myopia and hypermetropia; rods and cones.

2. Question shapes you will meet
One-liner recall: "Deficiency of which vitamin causes scurvy?" — the commonest shape at this level.
Match the following: vitamin ↔ disease, disease ↔ vector, gland ↔ hormone.
Odd one out: "Which of these is not a viral disease?"
Two-statement questions: "Statement 1 … Statement 2 … Which is/are correct?" — less common at this level but rising.
Superlative questions: "Which is the largest gland of the human body?"

3. Traps examiners like in this chapter
Almost Correct Statement — "All arteries carry oxygenated blood"; "Bile digests fats" (bile only emulsifies).
Similar Names — cerebrum / cerebellum; pepsin / trypsin; neuron / nephron; Anopheles / Aedes; Robert Hooke / Robert Brown.
Negative Phrasing — "Which of the following is NOT a fat-soluble vitamin?"; "Which disease is NOT caused by a virus?"
Reverse Causation — "Cramps produce lactic acid" (oxygen shortage → lactic acid → cramps).
Chronological Confusion — Jenner's smallpox vaccine (1796) vs Fleming's penicillin (1928); Hooke's cell (1665) vs the cell theory (1838–39).

4. Top 10 High-Yield Questions (exam-style, with trap analysis)

Question 1
Deficiency of which vitamin causes night blindness?
(a) Vitamin A (b) Vitamin B1 (c) Vitamin C (d) Vitamin D
Answer: (a) Vitamin A
Explanation: Vitamin A (retinol) is needed for the light-sensitive pigment of the retina. B1 → beriberi, C → scurvy, D → rickets.
Trap Type: Similar Names — "night blindness" and "colour blindness" sound alike; colour blindness is genetic, not a vitamin deficiency.
15/45-sec Rule: 15-second fact. Score maximizer.
Source: NCERT Class 6 Science (Components of Food)

Question 2
Which blood group is known as the universal donor?
(a) A (b) B (c) AB (d) O
Answer: (d) O
Explanation: Group O red cells carry neither A nor B antigen, so they are not attacked by the recipient's anti-A or anti-B antibodies. AB is the universal recipient.
Trap Type: Similar Names — swapping donor and recipient.
15/45-sec Rule: 15 seconds if you know the mnemonic "O Gives, AB Grabs".
Source: PYQ-style

Question 3
Which of the following blood vessels carries deoxygenated blood?
(a) Pulmonary vein (b) Pulmonary artery (c) Aorta (d) Coronary artery
Answer: (b) Pulmonary artery
Explanation: The pulmonary artery takes blood from the right ventricle to the lungs to collect oxygen. The pulmonary vein brings oxygenated blood back.
Trap Type: Almost Correct Statement — students apply "arteries carry oxygenated blood" without the exception.
15/45-sec Rule: 45-second analytical. Read every option; the word "vein" in option (a) is the bait.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science (Life Processes)

Question 4
Which of the following is NOT a fat-soluble vitamin?
(a) Vitamin A (b) Vitamin C (c) Vitamin D (d) Vitamin K
Answer: (b) Vitamin C
Explanation: A, D, E and K are fat-soluble; B-group vitamins and C are water-soluble.
Trap Type: Negative Phrasing — missing the word "NOT" leads to picking A.
15/45-sec Rule: 15 seconds, but underline "NOT" first.
Source: PYQ-style

Question 5
Malaria is caused by:
(a) a bacterium (b) a virus (c) a protozoan (d) a fungus
Answer: (c) a protozoan
Explanation: Malaria is caused by the protozoan Plasmodium and spread by the female Anopheles mosquito.
Trap Type: Similar Names — "spread by mosquito" makes many think of dengue, which is viral.
15/45-sec Rule: 15-second fact.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Science (Why Do We Fall Ill)

Question 6
Which part of the human brain maintains balance and posture of the body?
(a) Cerebrum (b) Cerebellum (c) Medulla (d) Hypothalamus
Answer: (b) Cerebellum
Explanation: The cerebellum, part of the hind-brain, controls posture and balance. The cerebrum handles thinking; the medulla controls involuntary actions.
Trap Type: Similar Names — cerebrum vs cerebellum.
15/45-sec Rule: 15-second fact.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science (Control and Coordination)

Question 7
Which is the largest gland of the human body?
(a) Pancreas (b) Thyroid (c) Liver (d) Pituitary
Answer: (c) Liver
Explanation: The liver is the largest gland; it makes bile. Pituitary is the "master gland" — a different superlative.
Trap Type: Similar Names — "largest gland" vs "master gland".
15/45-sec Rule: 15-second fact.
Source: PYQ-style

Question 8
Goitre is caused by the deficiency of:
(a) Iron (b) Iodine (c) Calcium (d) Vitamin A
Answer: (b) Iodine
Explanation: The thyroid needs iodine to make thyroxin; shortage makes the gland swell (goitre). Iron deficiency causes anaemia.
Trap Type: Similar Names — iron and iodine both begin with "I".
15/45-sec Rule: 15-second fact.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science (Control and Coordination)

Question 9
Consider the following statements:
Statement I: Bile is produced by the liver and stored in the gall bladder.
Statement II: Bile contains an enzyme that digests fats.
Which of the statements given is/are correct?
(a) I only (b) II only (c) Both I and II (d) Neither I nor II
Answer: (a) I only
Explanation: Bile is made in the liver and stored in the gall bladder, but it has no enzyme — it emulsifies fats so that lipase from the pancreas can digest them.
Trap Type: Almost Correct Statement — Statement II sounds right because bile does help fat digestion.
15/45-sec Rule: 45-second analytical — check each statement separately.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science (Life Processes)

Question 10
Who developed the first vaccine, against smallpox?
(a) Louis Pasteur (b) Alexander Fleming (c) Edward Jenner (d) Robert Koch
Answer: (c) Edward Jenner
Explanation: Edward Jenner developed the smallpox vaccine in 1796. Alexander Fleming discovered penicillin (an antibiotic) in 1928.
Trap Type: Chronological Confusion — mixing up the first vaccine with the first antibiotic.
15/45-sec Rule: 15-second fact.
Source: PYQ-style

5. How to use this analysis
Learn the vitamin, disease-vector and gland tables until you can write them from memory — they convert into quick marks.
For every "all / always / only / NOT" question, slow down and hunt for the exception.
Keep the similar-name pairs (cerebrum–cerebellum, pepsin–trypsin, neuron–nephron, iron–iodine) on one revision card.
