Set 4/6 – Biology: Human Body MCQs (Foundation)
Questions 76–100: Medium

76. Which part of a neuron receives information from other cells?
(a) Axon (b) Dendrite (c) Nerve ending (d) Myelin sheath
Answer: (b)
Solution: Information is picked up at the dendritic tips of a neuron, travels through the cell body and then along the axon to its end.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science (Control and Coordination)

77. The junction or gap between two neurons, across which a message is passed by a chemical, is called a:
(a) Nephron (b) Reflex arc (c) Dendrite (d) Synapse
Answer: (d)
Solution: At the end of an axon, a chemical is released that crosses the gap, called a synapse, and starts a similar impulse in the next neuron.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science (Control and Coordination)

78. Thinking, memory, intelligence and voluntary actions are controlled by the:
(a) Cerebrum (b) Cerebellum (c) Medulla (d) Pons
Answer: (a)
Solution: The cerebrum, the main thinking part of the fore-brain, controls voluntary actions, memory and intelligence. Trap: the cerebellum, with a similar name, controls balance.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science (Control and Coordination)

79. Involuntary actions such as blood pressure, salivation and vomiting are controlled by the:
(a) Cerebrum (b) Cerebellum (c) Medulla in the hind-brain (d) Spinal cord alone
Answer: (c)
Solution: The medulla in the hind-brain controls involuntary actions including blood pressure, salivation and vomiting.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science (Control and Coordination)

80. In a reflex action, such as pulling the hand away from a hot object, the reflex arc is formed in the:
(a) Cerebrum (b) Cerebellum (c) Spinal cord (d) Medulla
Answer: (c)
Solution: Reflex arcs are formed in the spinal cord, so the response is quick; the information also reaches the brain afterwards.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science (Control and Coordination)

81. The brain is protected by a bony box called the:
(a) Skull (cranium) (b) Rib cage (c) Pelvic girdle (d) Vertebral column
Answer: (a)
Solution: The brain sits inside the skull and is cushioned by a fluid-filled balloon that absorbs shocks. The vertebral column protects the spinal cord.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science (Control and Coordination)

82. The spinal cord is protected by the:
(a) Skull (b) Rib cage (c) Sternum (d) Vertebral column
Answer: (d)
Solution: The spinal cord runs inside the vertebral column (backbone), which protects it.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science (Control and Coordination)

83. Iodine is necessary for the thyroid gland to make which hormone?
(a) Insulin (b) Thyroxin (c) Adrenaline (d) Growth hormone
Answer: (b)
Solution: The thyroid gland needs iodine to make thyroxin, which regulates the metabolism of carbohydrates, proteins and fats. Iodised salt helps prevent goitre.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science (Control and Coordination)

84. Which hormone prepares the body to deal with an emergency, increasing the heartbeat?
(a) Adrenaline (b) Insulin (c) Thyroxin (d) Oestrogen
Answer: (a)
Solution: Adrenaline from the adrenal glands is released in emergencies; it speeds up the heartbeat and supplies more oxygen to the muscles.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science (Control and Coordination)

85. Deficiency of growth hormone in childhood leads to:
(a) Goitre (b) Diabetes (c) Rickets (d) Dwarfism
Answer: (d)
Solution: Growth hormone is secreted by the pituitary gland; its deficiency in childhood leads to dwarfism. Rickets is a Vitamin D deficiency disease.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science (Control and Coordination)

86. Diabetes is caused by the deficiency of which hormone?
(a) Thyroxin (b) Adrenaline (c) Insulin (d) Testosterone
Answer: (c)
Solution: Insulin from the pancreas regulates blood sugar; when it is not secreted in proper amounts, blood sugar rises, causing diabetes.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science (Control and Coordination)

87. Testosterone is secreted by the:
(a) Ovaries (b) Testes (c) Thyroid (d) Adrenal glands
Answer: (b)
Solution: Testes produce testosterone, which brings about changes in boys at puberty. Ovaries produce oestrogen.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science (Control and Coordination)

88. Oestrogen, the female hormone, is produced by the:
(a) Pituitary (b) Ovaries (c) Pancreas (d) Testes
Answer: (b)
Solution: The ovaries produce oestrogen, which is responsible for changes in girls at puberty.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science (Control and Coordination)

89. The three small bones of the human middle ear are:
(a) Malleus, incus and stapes (b) Femur, tibia and fibula (c) Radius, ulna and humerus (d) Atlas, axis and sternum
Answer: (a)
Solution: The middle ear has three ossicles — malleus (hammer), incus (anvil) and stapes (stirrup) — which pass sound vibrations to the inner ear.
Source: PYQ-style

90. Which is the hardest substance in the human body?
(a) Bone (b) Cartilage (c) Nail (d) Tooth enamel
Answer: (d)
Solution: Enamel, the outer covering of teeth, is the hardest substance in the human body. Bone is hard but not as hard as enamel.
Source: PYQ-style

91. How many teeth are there in the permanent set of an adult human?
(a) 28 (b) 30 (c) 32 (d) 36
Answer: (c)
Solution: Milk teeth are replaced by a permanent set; an adult human has 32 permanent teeth in total, including the wisdom teeth.
Source: PYQ-style

92. The light-sensitive layer of the eye on which the image is formed is the:
(a) Retina (b) Cornea (c) Iris (d) Pupil
Answer: (a)
Solution: The eye lens forms an inverted real image on the retina, which has light-sensitive cells that send signals to the brain through the optic nerve.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science (The Human Eye and the Colourful World)

93. Which part of the eye controls the size of the pupil?
(a) Cornea (b) Retina (c) Iris (d) Optic nerve
Answer: (c)
Solution: The iris is a dark muscular diaphragm behind the cornea that controls the size of the pupil and thus the amount of light entering the eye.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science (The Human Eye and the Colourful World)

94. Cone cells in the retina are sensitive to:
(a) Dim light only (b) Sound (c) Heat (d) Colour
Answer: (d)
Solution: Cones respond to bright light and help us see colours; rods respond to dim light.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science (The Human Eye and the Colourful World)

95. Hypermetropia (far-sightedness) is corrected by using a:
(a) Concave lens (b) Convex lens (c) Plane glass (d) Cylindrical lens
Answer: (b)
Solution: A hypermetropic person cannot see nearby objects clearly; a convex lens of suitable power corrects this. Trap: concave lenses correct myopia.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science (The Human Eye and the Colourful World)

96. The near point of a normal human eye is about:
(a) 10 cm (b) 25 cm (c) 50 cm (d) 100 cm
Answer: (b)
Solution: The minimum distance at which a normal eye can see objects clearly without strain is called the near point, which is about 25 cm.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science (The Human Eye and the Colourful World)

97. In cataract, the:
(a) Eye lens becomes cloudy (b) Retina gets detached (c) Eye ball becomes too long (d) Iris stops working
Answer: (a)
Solution: In cataract, the crystalline lens of the eye becomes milky and cloudy, causing partial or complete loss of vision; it can be treated by surgery.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science (The Human Eye and the Colourful World)

98. How many chromosomes are present in a normal human body cell?
(a) 23 (b) 44 (c) 46 (d) 48
Answer: (c)
Solution: Human body cells have 23 pairs of chromosomes, one set from each parent. Counting every chromosome gives a total of 46.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science (Heredity)

99. In human beings, the sex of the child is determined by:
(a) The mother's egg (b) The food eaten during pregnancy (c) The climate (d) The type of sperm from the father
Answer: (d)
Solution: The mother's egg always carries an X chromosome; the father's sperm carries either X or Y. A child getting Y from the father is a boy, so the father's sperm decides.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science (Heredity)

100. Who is known as the father of genetics?
(a) Gregor Johann Mendel (b) Charles Darwin (c) Robert Hooke (d) Louis Pasteur
Answer: (a)
Solution: Gregor Mendel worked out the basic rules of inheritance from his experiments on garden peas, so he is called the father of genetics.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science (Heredity)
