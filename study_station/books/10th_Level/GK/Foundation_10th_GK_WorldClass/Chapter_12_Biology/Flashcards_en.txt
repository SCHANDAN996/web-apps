Flashcard Deck: Biology – Human Body (Foundation)

Card 1
Front: In 1665 a man looked at a slice of cork and saw tiny "rooms". Who was he, and what did he name them?
Back: Robert Hooke. He named them cells.

Card 2
Front: Which part of the cell would go dark first if the "power" were cut?
Back: Mitochondria — the powerhouse of the cell. They release energy as ATP.

Card 3
Front: Which cell organelle is nicknamed the "suicide bag", and why?
Back: Lysosome. It is full of digestive enzymes; if it bursts, it can digest its own cell.

Card 4
Front: Blood flows, yet biologists call it a connective tissue. What kind exactly?
Back: A fluid connective tissue.

Card 5
Front: Bile helps digest fat, but it has a surprising "missing" item. What is missing, and where is bile made?
Back: Bile has no digestive enzyme; it only emulsifies fats. It is made in the liver and stored in the gall bladder.

Card 6
Front: Pepsin or trypsin — which one is made by the stomach?
Back: Pepsin. Trypsin comes from the pancreas.

Card 7
Front: Why do your legs cramp after a sudden sprint?
Back: Muscles run short of oxygen and switch to anaerobic respiration, making lactic acid, which causes cramps.

Card 8
Front: Examiner's trap: "All arteries carry oxygenated blood." True or false?
Back: False. The pulmonary artery carries deoxygenated blood from the heart to the lungs. Trap Type: Almost Correct Statement.

Card 9
Front: Which heart chamber does the heaviest lifting, and how can you tell?
Back: The left ventricle. It has the thickest wall because it pumps blood to the whole body.

Card 10
Front: Mnemonic check: what does "O Gives, AB Grabs" remind you of?
Back: Blood group O is the universal donor; blood group AB is the universal recipient.

Card 11
Front: Kidneys have millions of tiny filters. What is each filtering unit called?
Back: Nephron.

Card 12
Front: You ride a bicycle without falling. Which part of the brain deserves the credit?
Back: Cerebellum — it controls balance and posture.

Card 13
Front: Which gland is called the "master gland"?
Back: Pituitary gland.

Card 14
Front: A swollen neck in hilly villages was once common. Which mineral was missing, and which gland suffered?
Back: Iodine was missing; the thyroid gland enlarges, causing goitre.

Card 15
Front: The longest bone is in your thigh. Where is the smallest one hiding?
Back: In the middle ear — the stapes. The longest is the femur.

Card 16
Front: Mnemonic check: which vitamins "hide in fat" (ADEK)?
Back: Vitamins A, D, E and K are fat-soluble. B and C are water-soluble.

Card 17
Front: Sailors on long voyages once suffered bleeding gums. Which vitamin did they lack?
Back: Vitamin C — the disease is scurvy.

Card 18
Front: Which vitamin can your own skin make in sunlight, and what happens to children who lack it?
Back: Vitamin D. Its deficiency causes rickets (soft, bent bones).

Card 19
Front: Examiner's trap: "Antibiotics are a cure for the common cold." Why is this wrong?
Back: The common cold is caused by a virus, and antibiotics act on bacteria, not viruses. Trap Type: Almost Correct Statement.

Card 20
Front: Malaria, dengue, filaria — which mosquito carries which?
Back: Female Anopheles – malaria; Aedes – dengue; Culex – filaria.
