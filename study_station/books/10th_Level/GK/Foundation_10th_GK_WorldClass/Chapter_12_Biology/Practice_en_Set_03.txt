Set 3/6 – Biology: Human Body MCQs (Foundation)
Questions 51–75: Medium

51. In the lungs, the exchange of oxygen and carbon dioxide takes place in the:
(a) Trachea (b) Bronchi (c) Alveoli (d) Larynx
Answer: (c)
Solution: Alveoli are tiny balloon-like sacs surrounded by blood capillaries; oxygen enters the blood and carbon dioxide leaves it here. The trachea and bronchi only carry air.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science (Life Processes)

52. What prevents the trachea (wind pipe) from collapsing when there is less air in it?
(a) Rings of cartilage (b) Bones (c) Muscles of the diaphragm (d) Mucus
Answer: (a)
Solution: Rings of cartilage present in the throat and trachea keep the air passage open even when there is little air in it.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science (Life Processes)

53. Which muscular sheet below the lungs helps in breathing in and out?
(a) Pericardium (b) Pleura (c) Epiglottis (d) Diaphragm
Answer: (d)
Solution: When the diaphragm flattens and the ribs lift, the chest cavity becomes larger and air is sucked into the lungs; the reverse pushes air out.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science (Life Processes)

54. The end products of aerobic respiration in human cells are:
(a) Lactic acid and energy (b) Carbon dioxide, water and energy (c) Ethanol, carbon dioxide and energy (d) Glucose and oxygen
Answer: (b)
Solution: In the presence of oxygen, pyruvate is broken down in mitochondria into carbon dioxide and water, releasing a large amount of energy. Lactic acid forms only when oxygen is short.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science (Life Processes)

55. During anaerobic respiration, yeast produces:
(a) Lactic acid and energy (b) Carbon dioxide and water (c) Ethanol and carbon dioxide (d) Oxygen and glucose
Answer: (c)
Solution: In yeast, pyruvate is converted into ethanol and carbon dioxide without oxygen; this is fermentation. Human muscles make lactic acid instead.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science (Life Processes)

56. The first step of respiration, in which glucose is broken down into pyruvate, takes place in the:
(a) Mitochondria (b) Cytoplasm (c) Nucleus (d) Ribosome
Answer: (b)
Solution: Glucose is broken into pyruvate in the cytoplasm. The further breakdown of pyruvate using oxygen happens in the mitochondria.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science (Life Processes)

57. Which molecule is called the energy currency of the cell?
(a) Glucose (b) DNA (c) Haemoglobin (d) ATP
Answer: (d)
Solution: The energy released during respiration is stored as ATP, which the cell uses to power its activities, so ATP is called the energy currency.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science (Life Processes)

58. In human beings, oxygen is carried by the red blood cells with the help of:
(a) Plasma (b) Haemoglobin (c) Platelets (d) Lymph
Answer: (b)
Solution: Haemoglobin, the respiratory pigment in red blood cells, has a high affinity for oxygen and carries it to the tissues.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science (Life Processes)

59. Which metal is present in haemoglobin?
(a) Magnesium (b) Copper (c) Iron (d) Zinc
Answer: (c)
Solution: Haemoglobin contains iron, which is why iron deficiency leads to anaemia. Trap: magnesium is present in chlorophyll, the green pigment of plants.
Source: PYQ-style

60. Which chamber of the human heart has the thickest muscular wall?
(a) Left ventricle (b) Right ventricle (c) Left atrium (d) Right atrium
Answer: (a)
Solution: The left ventricle pumps oxygenated blood to the whole body, so its wall is the thickest. The right ventricle pumps blood only to the lungs.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science (Life Processes)

61. Deoxygenated blood from the body first enters which chamber of the heart?
(a) Left atrium (b) Left ventricle (c) Right ventricle (d) Right atrium
Answer: (d)
Solution: Deoxygenated blood from the body comes through the vena cava into the right atrium. Oxygenated blood from the lungs enters the left atrium.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science (Life Processes)

62. Human beings are said to have double circulation because:
(a) The heart has two sides (b) Blood passes through the heart twice in one complete cycle (c) There are two types of blood cells (d) Blood flows in both directions in arteries
Answer: (b)
Solution: In one complete cycle, blood goes through the heart once on its way to the lungs and once on its way to the body. That is double circulation.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science (Life Processes)

63. What is the function of the valves in the heart?
(a) To prevent the backward flow of blood (b) To make the heart beat faster (c) To produce blood cells (d) To filter waste from blood
Answer: (a)
Solution: Valves make sure that blood flows in one direction only and does not flow backwards when the chambers contract.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science (Life Processes)

64. Blood vessels that carry blood away from the heart to various organs are called:
(a) Arteries (b) Veins (c) Capillaries (d) Lymph vessels
Answer: (a)
Solution: Arteries carry blood away from the heart and have thick, elastic walls because the blood in them is under high pressure.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science (Life Processes)

65. The smallest blood vessels, whose walls are one cell thick and where exchange of materials takes place, are:
(a) Arteries (b) Veins (c) Aorta (d) Capillaries
Answer: (d)
Solution: Arteries divide into extremely thin capillaries; exchange of materials between blood and surrounding cells takes place across their thin walls.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science (Life Processes)

66. Which component of blood helps in clotting at the site of an injury?
(a) Red blood cells (b) Platelets (c) White blood cells (d) Plasma proteins only
Answer: (b)
Solution: Platelets circulate in the blood and plug leaks by helping blood to clot at the point of injury.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science (Life Processes)

67. Which blood cells fight infection in the body?
(a) Red blood cells (b) Platelets (c) White blood cells (d) Nerve cells
Answer: (c)
Solution: White blood cells protect the body by fighting germs. Red blood cells carry oxygen; platelets help in clotting.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Science (Tissues)

68. Mature red blood cells of human beings do not have a:
(a) Cytoplasm (b) Cell membrane (c) Haemoglobin (d) Nucleus
Answer: (d)
Solution: Mature human red blood cells lose their nucleus, which leaves more space for haemoglobin to carry oxygen.
Source: PYQ-style

69. Who discovered the ABO blood group system?
(a) William Harvey (b) Edward Jenner (c) Louis Pasteur (d) Karl Landsteiner
Answer: (d)
Solution: Karl Landsteiner discovered the ABO blood groups. William Harvey is known for describing blood circulation, a different discovery.
Source: PYQ-style

70. Which blood group is called the universal recipient?
(a) O (b) AB (c) A (d) B
Answer: (b)
Solution: The plasma of group AB has neither anti-A nor anti-B antibodies, so it can receive red cells of any ABO group. Trap: O is the universal donor.
Source: PYQ-style

71. The normal blood pressure of a healthy adult is about:
(a) 140/100 mm Hg (b) 100/60 mm Hg (c) 120/80 mm Hg (d) 160/90 mm Hg
Answer: (c)
Solution: Normal systolic pressure is about 120 mm of Hg and diastolic pressure about 80 mm of Hg, written as 120/80 mm Hg.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science (Life Processes)

72. Lymph is a fluid that:
(a) Carries digested and absorbed fat and drains excess fluid back into the blood (b) Carries oxygen using haemoglobin (c) Is red because it contains red blood cells (d) Is made in the kidneys
Answer: (a)
Solution: Lymph is colourless and has few cells; it carries digested fat from the intestine and drains extra fluid from tissues back into the blood.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science (Life Processes)

73. Urea, the main nitrogenous waste in humans, is formed in the:
(a) Kidneys (b) Liver (c) Urinary bladder (d) Lungs
Answer: (b)
Solution: Urea is formed in the liver and carried by blood to the kidneys, which remove it in urine. Trap: kidneys remove urea but do not make it.
Source: PYQ-style

74. What is the correct path of urine after it is formed?
(a) Urethra → urinary bladder → ureter (b) Kidney → urethra → ureter → urinary bladder (c) Urinary bladder → kidney → ureter (d) Kidney → ureter → urinary bladder → urethra
Answer: (d)
Solution: Urine formed in each kidney passes through a ureter into the urinary bladder, where it is stored, and leaves the body through the urethra.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science (Life Processes)

75. The artificial kidney used to filter the blood of a patient whose kidneys have failed works on the method of:
(a) Transfusion (b) Vaccination (c) Dialysis (d) Fermentation
Answer: (c)
Solution: In haemodialysis, the patient's blood is passed through a device that removes nitrogenous wastes, much like a kidney does.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science (Life Processes)
