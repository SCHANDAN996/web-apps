The most confusing fact in this chapter is one tiny sentence that ruins many answer sheets: "Arteries carry oxygenated blood and veins carry deoxygenated blood." It is almost right — and that is exactly why it is dangerous. Let us fix it forever.

Imagine your heart as a bus depot in the middle of a city. Buses that leave the depot are called "outgoing buses". Buses that come back to the depot are called "incoming buses". Notice something: the name depends only on the direction — leaving the depot or returning to it. It does not depend on who is sitting inside the bus.

That is the real rule:
Artery = any vessel that carries blood AWAY from the heart.
Vein = any vessel that brings blood BACK TO the heart.
The oxygen inside has nothing to do with the name.

Now, the heart runs two bus routes.

Route 1 — the body route: The left ventricle sends fresh, oxygen-rich blood out through the aorta to the whole body. These outgoing buses (arteries) are full of oxygen. The used blood comes back through veins, low on oxygen. This is the route most people remember, so they think "artery = oxygen".

Route 2 — the lung route: The right ventricle sends used, oxygen-poor blood out to the lungs to pick up fresh oxygen. This vessel is leaving the heart, so it must be called an artery — the pulmonary artery. But its passengers are tired and low on oxygen. After the lungs refill the blood with oxygen, it returns to the heart through the pulmonary vein. It is coming back, so it is a vein — yet it is full of oxygen.

The daily-life example:
Think of a school bus. In the morning it leaves the school empty to pick up children. In the afternoon it leaves the school full of children to drop them home. Both trips are "leaving the school". The bus is called the same thing whether it is empty or full. Arteries are the same — "leaving the heart" whether the blood is rich or poor in oxygen.

The unforgettable trick:
A for Away — Artery goes Away from the heart.
V for Visit back — Vein comes back to Visit the heart.
And remember the lung route is the "upside-down route": Pulmonary artery = poor blood, Pulmonary vein = pure blood. "P-artery Poor, P-vein Pure."

How the examiner uses this:
"All arteries carry oxygenated blood." — False (the pulmonary artery does not).
"Which blood vessel carries oxygenated blood from the lungs to the heart?" — Pulmonary vein.
"The pulmonary artery carries blood from the heart to the lungs." — True.
