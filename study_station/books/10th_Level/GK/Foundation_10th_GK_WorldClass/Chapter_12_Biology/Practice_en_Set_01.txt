Set 1/6 – Biology: Human Body MCQs (Foundation)
Questions 1–20: Easy | Questions 21–25: Medium

1. Who first observed and named the cell?
(a) Robert Brown (b) Robert Hooke (c) Rudolf Virchow (d) Theodor Schwann
Answer: (b)
Solution: Robert Hooke saw tiny compartments in a thin slice of cork in 1665 and called them cells. Robert Brown later discovered the nucleus.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Science (The Fundamental Unit of Life)

2. Which cell organelle is known as the powerhouse of the cell?
(a) Ribosome (b) Nucleus (c) Mitochondria (d) Lysosome
Answer: (c)
Solution: Mitochondria release energy from food in the form of ATP, so they are called the powerhouse of the cell. Ribosomes make proteins.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Science (The Fundamental Unit of Life)

3. Which blood group is called the universal donor?
(a) A (b) B (c) AB (d) O
Answer: (d)
Solution: Red blood cells of group O carry neither A nor B antigen, so they can be given to people of any ABO group. AB is the universal recipient, not the donor.
Source: PYQ-style

4. Which is the largest gland of the human body?
(a) Liver (b) Pancreas (c) Thyroid (d) Pituitary
Answer: (a)
Solution: The liver is the largest gland of the body and makes bile. The pituitary is called the master gland, which is a different title.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science (Life Processes)

5. How many bones are there in the body of an adult human?
(a) 196 (b) 206 (c) 212 (d) 300
Answer: (b)
Solution: An adult human skeleton has 206 bones. A newborn has more bones because several of them fuse with age; the adult count is 206.
Source: PYQ-style

6. Deficiency of which vitamin causes night blindness?
(a) Vitamin A (b) Vitamin B1 (c) Vitamin C (d) Vitamin K
Answer: (a)
Solution: Vitamin A is needed for healthy eyes; its deficiency causes night blindness. Vitamin B1 deficiency causes beriberi.
Source: NCERT Class 6 Science (Components of Food)

7. Scurvy, in which the gums bleed, is caused by the deficiency of:
(a) Vitamin A (b) Vitamin D (c) Vitamin C (d) Vitamin K
Answer: (c)
Solution: Lack of Vitamin C (ascorbic acid) causes scurvy. Citrus fruits such as lemon and amla are rich in Vitamin C.
Source: NCERT Class 6 Science (Components of Food)

8. Rickets in children is caused by the deficiency of:
(a) Vitamin B12 (b) Vitamin E (c) Vitamin K (d) Vitamin D
Answer: (d)
Solution: Vitamin D deficiency makes bones soft and bent in children, a condition called rickets. The skin makes Vitamin D in sunlight.
Source: NCERT Class 6 Science (Components of Food)

9. Goitre is caused by the deficiency of which mineral?
(a) Iron (b) Iodine (c) Calcium (d) Sodium
Answer: (b)
Solution: The thyroid gland needs iodine to make thyroxin; shortage of iodine makes the gland swell (goitre). Iron deficiency causes anaemia, not goitre.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science (Control and Coordination)

10. Malaria is spread by the bite of which mosquito?
(a) Female Anopheles (b) Aedes (c) Culex (d) Male Anopheles
Answer: (a)
Solution: Malaria parasites are spread by the female Anopheles mosquito. Aedes spreads dengue and Culex spreads filaria; male mosquitoes do not bite.
Source: NCERT Class 8 Science (Microorganisms)

11. Which is the longest bone of the human body?
(a) Tibia (b) Humerus (c) Femur (d) Radius
Answer: (c)
Solution: The femur, or thigh bone, is the longest and strongest bone of the human body.
Source: PYQ-style

12. Which is the smallest bone of the human body?
(a) Incus (b) Malleus (c) Femur (d) Stapes
Answer: (d)
Solution: The stapes, one of the three tiny bones of the middle ear, is the smallest bone. Malleus and incus are its neighbours but are larger.
Source: PYQ-style

13. Which instrument is used to measure blood pressure?
(a) Stethoscope (b) Sphygmomanometer (c) Thermometer (d) Barometer
Answer: (b)
Solution: Blood pressure is measured with a sphygmomanometer. A stethoscope is used to listen to heartbeats.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science (Life Processes)

14. Insulin is secreted by which organ?
(a) Pancreas (b) Liver (c) Kidney (d) Stomach
Answer: (a)
Solution: Insulin is a hormone secreted by the pancreas; it controls the level of sugar in the blood. Its deficiency causes diabetes.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science (Control and Coordination)

15. What is the basic filtering unit of the kidney called?
(a) Neuron (b) Alveolus (c) Nephron (d) Villus
Answer: (c)
Solution: Each kidney contains a large number of nephrons, the basic filtering units. A neuron is the unit of the nervous system — a similar-sounding name.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science (Life Processes)

16. Which gland is known as the master gland?
(a) Thyroid (b) Pituitary (c) Adrenal (d) Pancreas
Answer: (b)
Solution: The pituitary gland controls several other endocrine glands, so it is called the master gland. It also secretes growth hormone.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science (Control and Coordination)

17. Which is the largest organ of the human body?
(a) Liver (b) Lungs (c) Brain (d) Skin
Answer: (d)
Solution: The skin is the largest organ of the body. The liver is the largest internal organ and the largest gland.
Source: PYQ-style

18. Which of the following diseases is caused by a virus?
(a) Dengue (b) Tuberculosis (c) Cholera (d) Typhoid
Answer: (a)
Solution: Dengue is a viral disease spread by the Aedes mosquito. Tuberculosis, cholera and typhoid are caused by bacteria.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Science (Why Do We Fall Ill)

19. The BCG vaccine is given to protect against which disease?
(a) Polio (b) Measles (c) Tuberculosis (d) Tetanus
Answer: (c)
Solution: BCG (Bacillus Calmette–Guérin) is the vaccine against tuberculosis. Polio is prevented by polio drops or injection.
Source: PYQ-style

20. How many chambers does the human heart have?
(a) 2 (b) 3 (c) 4 (d) 5
Answer: (c)
Solution: The human heart has two upper chambers (atria) and two lower chambers (ventricles), making a total of 4.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science (Life Processes)

21. Which blood vessel carries oxygenated blood from the lungs to the heart?
(a) Pulmonary artery (b) Pulmonary vein (c) Vena cava (d) Aorta
Answer: (b)
Solution: The pulmonary vein brings oxygen-rich blood from the lungs to the left atrium. Trap: students assume all veins carry deoxygenated blood and pick the artery.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science (Life Processes)

22. The enzyme pepsin, which begins the digestion of proteins, is secreted by:
(a) Stomach (b) Pancreas (c) Liver (d) Salivary glands
Answer: (a)
Solution: Gastric glands of the stomach secrete pepsin. Trap: trypsin, a similar-sounding protein-digesting enzyme, comes from the pancreas.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science (Life Processes)

23. Muscle cramps after sudden heavy exercise are caused by the accumulation of:
(a) Ethanol (b) Carbon dioxide (c) Uric acid (d) Lactic acid
Answer: (d)
Solution: When muscles run short of oxygen, pyruvate is converted into lactic acid, and its build-up causes cramps. Ethanol forms in yeast, not in human muscles.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science (Life Processes)

24. Which of the following is NOT a fat-soluble vitamin?
(a) Vitamin A (b) Vitamin D (c) Vitamin C (d) Vitamin K
Answer: (c)
Solution: Vitamins A, D, E and K dissolve in fat; Vitamin C dissolves in water. Trap: the word NOT is easy to miss.
Source: PYQ-style

25. Myopia (near-sightedness) is corrected by using a:
(a) Concave lens (b) Convex lens (c) Bifocal lens (d) Cylindrical lens
Answer: (a)
Solution: A myopic eye cannot see distant objects clearly; a concave lens of suitable power corrects it. A convex lens is used for hypermetropia.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science (The Human Eye and the Colourful World)
