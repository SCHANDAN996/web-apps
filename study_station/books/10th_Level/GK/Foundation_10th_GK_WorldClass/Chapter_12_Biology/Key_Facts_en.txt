1. High-Yield Facts
Fact	Description	Examiner's Trap	Trap Type
Cell named by Robert Hooke	Saw cells in a thin slice of cork in 1665.	Offering Robert Brown, who discovered the nucleus (1831).	Similar Names
Cell theory	Schleiden (1838) and Schwann (1839); Virchow (1855) added "cells arise from pre-existing cells".	Crediting Virchow with the original cell theory.	Chronological Confusion
Mitochondria – powerhouse	Release energy as ATP; site of aerobic respiration.	Calling ribosomes or the nucleus the powerhouse.	Similar Names
Lysosome – suicide bag	Contains digestive enzymes; cleans waste.	Offering Golgi apparatus, which forms lysosomes.	Almost Correct Statement
Cell wall and plastids	Present in plant cells, absent in animal cells.	"Animal cells have a cell wall made of cellulose."	Almost Correct Statement
Blood – connective tissue	Blood is a fluid connective tissue.	Classifying blood as epithelial or muscular tissue.	Similar Names
Ligament vs tendon	Ligament: bone to bone. Tendon: muscle to bone.	Swapping the two definitions.	Similar Names
Pepsin vs trypsin	Pepsin from the stomach; trypsin from the pancreas.	"Trypsin is secreted by the stomach."	Similar Names
Bile	Made in the liver, stored in the gall bladder; emulsifies fats; has no enzyme.	"Bile is made in the gall bladder" or "bile digests fat with an enzyme".	Almost Correct Statement
Villi	Finger-like projections in the small intestine; increase surface area for absorption.	Placing villi in the stomach or large intestine.	Almost Correct Statement
Lactic acid and cramps	Oxygen shortage in muscles → lactic acid → cramps.	"Cramps cause lactic acid to form."	Reverse Causation
Pulmonary artery / vein	Artery carries deoxygenated blood to the lungs; vein brings oxygenated blood to the heart.	"All arteries carry oxygenated blood."	Almost Correct Statement
Left ventricle	Thickest wall; pumps blood to the whole body.	Offering the right ventricle or right atrium.	Similar Names
Blood groups	ABO discovered by Karl Landsteiner; O universal donor, AB universal recipient.	Swapping donor and recipient.	Similar Names
Nephron	Basic filtering unit of the kidney.	Offering neuron (unit of the nervous system).	Similar Names
Urea	Made in the liver, removed by the kidneys.	"Urea is made in the kidneys."	Almost Correct Statement
Cerebellum	Balance and posture.	Offering cerebrum (thinking) or medulla (involuntary actions).	Similar Names
Reflex arc	Passes through the spinal cord.	"All reflexes are controlled by the cerebrum."	Almost Correct Statement
Pituitary – master gland	Controls several other glands; growth hormone.	Offering thyroid or adrenal as the master gland.	Similar Names
Thyroxin and iodine	Iodine shortage → goitre.	Linking goitre to Vitamin A or iron deficiency.	Similar Names
Insulin	From the pancreas; shortage → diabetes.	"Insulin is secreted by the liver."	Almost Correct Statement
Bones	206 in an adult; femur longest; stapes smallest.	Offering the stapes as "smallest muscle" or the femur as "largest organ".	Similar Names
Myopia	Near-sightedness; corrected by a concave lens.	Offering a convex lens (used for hypermetropia).	Similar Names
Fat-soluble vitamins	A, D, E, K.	"Which is NOT fat-soluble?" — C is the answer.	Negative Phrasing
Vitamin C	Scurvy (bleeding gums).	Linking scurvy to Vitamin D.	Similar Names
Vitamin D	Rickets; made in skin in sunlight.	Linking rickets to calcium only, with Vitamin D also offered.	Almost Correct Statement
Vitamin B12	Contains cobalt; pernicious anaemia.	Offering iron as the metal in B12 (iron is in haemoglobin).	Similar Names
Antibiotics	Act on bacteria, not on viruses.	"Antibiotics cure the common cold."	Almost Correct Statement
Malaria	Protozoan Plasmodium; spread by female Anopheles.	Offering Aedes (dengue) or Culex (filaria); calling malaria viral.	Similar Names
Smallpox vaccine	Edward Jenner, 1796.	Offering Louis Pasteur or Alexander Fleming (penicillin, 1928).	Chronological Confusion

2. Time Management (15/45-Second Rule)
Fact / Topic	Type	Score Maximizer?	Reason
Organelle nicknames (powerhouse, suicide bag)	15 s (Fact)	Yes	Direct one-line recall.
Vitamin – deficiency disease pairs	15 s (Fact)	Yes	Most asked one-liners of this chapter.
Disease – pathogen type – vector	15 s (Fact)	Yes	Simple matching once the table is learnt.
Largest / longest / smallest (gland, bone, cell)	15 s (Fact)	Yes	Fixed answers, no calculation.
Pulmonary artery / vein exception	45 s (Analytical)	Yes	Needs careful reading of "always" or "all".
Statement questions on digestion (enzyme source vs action)	45 s (Analytical)	Yes	Two or three facts must be checked together.
Blood group donor / recipient logic	45 s (Analytical)	Yes	Requires antigen–antibody reasoning.
Breathing vs respiration; aerobic vs anaerobic products	45 s (Analytical)	Moderate	Product lists (lactic acid vs ethanol) are easily confused.
Eye defects and lens type	15 s (Fact)	Yes	Myopia–concave, hypermetropia–convex.
Brain parts and functions	15 s (Fact), 45 s if in statement form	Yes	Cerebrum / cerebellum names are close.
