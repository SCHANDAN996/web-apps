🪄 Memory Hooks & Short Tricks

🧠 Hook 1: Cell organelles and their jobs
"My Rich Granny Loves Nuts"
My → Mitochondria (power, ATP)
Rich → Ribosomes (protein)
Granny → Golgi apparatus (packing and dispatch)
Loves → Lysosomes (suicide bags, cleaning)
Nuts → Nucleus (control room)
Visual: A rich granny with a power bank, packing protein bars into boxes and throwing the wrappers into a dustbin.

🧠 Hook 2: Cell discoveries in time order
"Hooke Brought Shoes, Virchow Varnished"
Hooke 1665 (cell) → Brown 1831 (nucleus) → Schleiden–Schwann 1838–39 (cell theory) → Virchow 1855 (cells from cells).

🧠 Hook 3: Ligament vs Tendon
"LBB – TMB"
Ligament = Bone to Bone. Tendon = Muscle to Bone.
Picture: a Ligament is a Link between two bones; a Tendon Tugs the bone when a muscle pulls.

🧠 Hook 4: Pepsin vs Trypsin
"Pepsin in the Pouch, Trypsin from the Tummy-gland"
Pepsin works in the stomach (the pouch); trypsin comes from the pancreas. Both digest proteins.

🧠 Hook 5: Bile — the soap of the body
"Liver Makes, Gall bladder Keeps"
Bile is like dish soap: it breaks big fat drops into tiny droplets (emulsification) but does not "eat" the fat — no enzyme.

🧠 Hook 6: Arteries and veins
"A for Away, V for Visit back"
Arteries go away from the heart; veins come back to visit it.
Exception: "P-artery Poor, P-vein Pure" — the pulmonary artery carries poor (deoxygenated) blood, the pulmonary vein carries pure (oxygenated) blood.

🧠 Hook 7: Blood groups
"O Gives, AB Grabs"
O = universal donor. AB = universal recipient.

🧠 Hook 8: Brain parts
"Cerebrum = Clever, Cerebellum = Balance-um, Medulla = Machine mode"
Cerebrum thinks; cerebellum balances; medulla runs automatic jobs like blood pressure, salivation and vomiting.

🧠 Hook 9: Glands
"Pituitary is the Principal; Thyroid Takes Iodine; Pancreas Pumps insulin; Adrenal = Alarm"
Pituitary is the master gland; thyroid needs iodine (else goitre); pancreas makes insulin (else diabetes); adrenaline is the emergency alarm hormone.

🧠 Hook 10: Fat-soluble vitamins
"ADEK hide in fat"
A, D, E, K dissolve in fat. B and C wash away in water.

🧠 Hook 11: Vitamin deficiency diseases
"All Night, Bitter Beri, C Scurvy, D Daylight-Rickets, K Klotting"
A → night blindness; B1 → beriberi; C → scurvy; D → rickets (made in daylight); K → clotting delay.
Bonus: "B12 = Blue Cobalt" — B12 is the only vitamin with cobalt.

🧠 Hook 12: Mosquito and disease
"A-M, A-D, C-F"
Anopheles–Malaria, Aedes–Dengue, Culex–Filaria.
Visual: "Anu went to Mall (malaria), Ada went to the Dentist (dengue), Cute Cul went to the File room (filaria)."

🧠 Hook 13: Bones
"Fat Femur, Small Stapes, 2-0-6 to fix"
Femur — longest; stapes — smallest (in the ear); 206 bones in an adult.
Middle ear bones, outside to inside: "MIS" — Malleus, Incus, Stapes (hammer, anvil, stirrup).

🧠 Hook 14: Eye defects
"Myopia — Minus (concave); Hypermetropia — Hug (convex)"
A concave lens is a minus (diverging) lens for myopia; a convex lens bulges outward like a hug for hypermetropia.

🧠 Hook 15: Antibiotics
"Antibiotics are Anti-Bacteria"
They kill bacteria, not viruses — useless against the common cold, flu or dengue virus.

⏭️ Skip Strategy

1. If a question asks for an exact count you never studied (for example, "how many muscles are in the human body"), skip it immediately. Textbook counts you must know are few: 206 bones, 4 heart chambers, 32 adult teeth, 46 chromosomes.

2. In a "match the following" on vitamins, diseases and chemical names, first fix the two pairs you are sure of (for example, C–scurvy, D–rickets) and eliminate options. If two options still remain and you cannot decide in 15 seconds, mark it for review and move on.

3. If a statement question contains "all", "always" or "only" and you cannot recall the exception within 45 seconds, skip and return later — these words usually hide an exception like the pulmonary artery.
