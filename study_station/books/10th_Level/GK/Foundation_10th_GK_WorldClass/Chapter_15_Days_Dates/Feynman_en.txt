🧒 Feynman Corner: Why do so many days come in "look-alike pairs" — and how do you never mix them up?

The most confusing thing in this chapter is not one single date. It is the pairs: Teachers' Day on 5 September and World Teachers' Day on 5 October; National Youth Day on 12 January and International Youth Day on 12 August; Hindi Diwas on 14 September and World Hindi Day on 10 January. Let's make it simple.

The daily-life example: your two birthdays

Imagine a boy named Raju. At home, his family celebrates his birthday on the day he was born — 5 September. At school, the class celebrates all the September birthdays together on one fixed day, say 5 October. Same boy, two celebrations, two different dates — one chosen by his family, one chosen by the school.

Days work the same way. India is the "family": it picks a day that matters to Indians — usually the birthday of an Indian hero. Dr Radhakrishnan was born on 5 September, so India's Teachers' Day is 5 September. Swami Vivekananda was born on 12 January, so India's Youth Day is 12 January.

The world is the "school": the UN or UNESCO picks a day that matters to all countries — usually the day an organisation was founded or an agreement was signed. World Teachers' Day, 5 October, recalls the international Recommendation on the status of teachers adopted on 5 October 1966 (ILO–UNESCO). It has nothing to do with any one Indian's birthday.

So the rule is:
National day → "Whose birthday?" (an Indian person)
World / International day → "Which organisation or agreement?" (an event)

Hindi Diwas and World Hindi Day follow the rule too. Hindi Diwas (14 September) is the day in 1949 when the Constituent Assembly made Hindi the official language of the Union — an Indian decision. World Hindi Day (10 January) is the day the first World Hindi Conference opened in Nagpur in 1975 — a gathering for Hindi lovers from many countries.

The unforgettable trick

"Desh ka din = janmadin; duniya ka din = sthapna din."
(The nation's day is a birthday; the world's day is a founding day.)
When an option says "World" or "International", move your mind away from Indian birthdays and towards organisations — WHO (7 April), FAO (16 October), UN (24 October), UPU (9 October).
