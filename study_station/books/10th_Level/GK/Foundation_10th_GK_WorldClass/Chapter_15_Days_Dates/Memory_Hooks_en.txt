🪄 Memory Hooks & Short Tricks

🧠 Mnemonic 1: January's three heroes
Sentence: "Youth at 12, Bose at 23, Gandhi at 30"
12 January → Vivekananda (National Youth Day) | 23 January → Netaji (Parakram Diwas) | 30 January → Gandhi (Martyrs' Day)
Visual: a young monk, a soldier in uniform and an old man with a stick standing on a calendar, each a little further along January.

🧠 Mnemonic 2: The three services
Rule: "Army Jan, Air Oct, Navy Dec — 15, 8, 4"
The months move forward through the year; the dates count down: 15 → 8 → 4.
Bonus: Flag Day is 7 December — "Navy 4, Flag 7, Vijay 16" for December.

🧠 Mnemonic 3: Two 26s
Story: "In November the Constitution was signed off; in January it was switched on."
26 November 1949 — adopted (Constitution Day). 26 January 1950 — came into force (Republic Day).

🧠 Mnemonic 4: Science and Technology
Rule: "Raman ran in February, Pokhran boomed in May."
28 February — National Science Day (Raman Effect, 1928). 11 May — National Technology Day (Pokhran, 1998).

🧠 Mnemonic 5: March water-weather-TB
Rule: "Water 22, Weather 23, TB 24" — three back-to-back days in the same week.
Picture: rain (water) → clouds (weather) → a cough (TB).

🧠 Mnemonic 6: October's founders' days
Rule: "Post 9, Food 16, UN 24" — UPU (1874), FAO (1945), UN Charter (1945).
Picture: a postman bringing a food parcel to the UN building.

🧠 Mnemonic 7: The "same date, different month" pairs
Rule: "India in September, World in October" for teachers — 5 September / 5 October.
"Youth: India in January, World in August" — 12 January / 12 August.

🧠 Mnemonic 8: Hindi's two days
Rule: "Desh ka Hindi Diwas in September, Videsh ka Hindi Diwas in January."
14 September (1949 decision) vs 10 January (Nagpur conference, 1975).

🧠 Mnemonic 9: November's birthdays — "Patel, Azad, Nehru, Birsa"
31 October Patel (Unity) → 11 November Azad (Education) → 14 November Nehru (Children) → 15 November Birsa Munda (Janjatiya Gaurav).
Picture: four leaders walking in a row from the end of October into mid-November.

🧠 Mnemonic 10: December's last week
Rule: "22 Maths, 23 Kisan, 24 Consumer, 25 Good Governance, 26 Veer Baal"
Ramanujan → Charan Singh → Consumer Protection Act 1986 → Vajpayee → Sahibzadas. Five days in a row, one per date.

🧠 Mnemonic 11: Victory days
Rule: "Kargil in the monsoon, Bangladesh in the winter."
26 July (Kargil, 1999) vs 16 December (1971 war).

🧠 Mnemonic 12: Health-world birthdays
Rule: "Red Cross 8, Nurses 12" — May: Henry Dunant (8 May), Florence Nightingale (12 May).
"Blood 14 June — Landsteiner; Diabetes 14 November — Banting." The two 14s of medicine.

🧠 Mnemonic 13: Yoga on the longest day
Rule: "Longest day, longest stretch" — 21 June, the summer solstice. UN said yes in 2014, the world stretched in 2015.

⏭️ Skip Strategy

1. If a question asks for the yearly theme of a day and you have not read that year's current affairs, skip it — themes change every year and cannot be worked out by logic.

2. If a "match the following" lists four days and you are sure of fewer than two pairs, skip it in the first round. When you know two pairs, check which option keeps both — often only one survives.

3. If two options are look-alikes (5 September / 5 October, 14 September / 10 January), do not skip — the question is testing exactly that pair. Read the word "World/International" again and answer.
