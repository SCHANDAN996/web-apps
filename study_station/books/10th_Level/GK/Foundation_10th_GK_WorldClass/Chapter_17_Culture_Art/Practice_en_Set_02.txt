Set 2/6 – Indian Culture & Art MCQs (Foundation)
Questions 26–50: Medium

26. Sattriya, one of the eight classical dances, belongs to:
(a) Assam (b) Manipur (c) Tripura (d) West Bengal
Answer: (a)
Solution: Sattriya was created by Srimanta Sankardev in the sattras (Vaishnava monasteries) of Assam. Manipur's classical dance is Manipuri.
Source: Sangeet Natak Akademi (list of classical dances)

27. The classical dance Kuchipudi is named after a village in:
(a) Tamil Nadu (b) Karnataka (c) Andhra Pradesh (d) Kerala
Answer: (c)
Solution: Kuchipudi takes its name from the village Kuchipudi in Krishna district, Andhra Pradesh. Trap: Kathakali also begins with "K" but belongs to Kerala.
Source: Sangeet Natak Akademi (list of classical dances)

28. In which classical dance does the performer dance on the rim of a brass plate in the item called "Tarangam"?
(a) Bharatanatyam (b) Kuchipudi (c) Odissi (d) Kathak
Answer: (b)
Solution: Tarangam, danced on the edge of a brass plate, is a well-known item of Kuchipudi.
Source: PYQ-style

29. The "tribhanga" (three-bend) posture is a characteristic feature of:
(a) Kathak (b) Manipuri (c) Kathakali (d) Odissi
Answer: (d)
Solution: Odissi is built on the tribhanga posture, in which the body bends at the neck, torso and knee; temple sculptures of Odisha show these poses.
Source: NCERT Class 11 An Introduction to Indian Art

30. The Raas Leela of Radha and Krishna is the heart of which classical dance?
(a) Manipuri (b) Sattriya (c) Mohiniyattam (d) Kuchipudi
Answer: (a)
Solution: Manipuri dance, with its soft, flowing movements, is centred on the Raas Leela. Sattriya is also Vaishnava but comes from Assam's monasteries.
Source: PYQ-style

31. Kalakshetra, the famous institution for Bharatanatyam in Chennai, was founded in 1936 by:
(a) Sonal Mansingh (b) Yamini Krishnamurthy (c) Rukmini Devi Arundale (d) Mrinalini Sarabhai
Answer: (c)
Solution: Rukmini Devi Arundale founded Kalakshetra and helped revive Bharatanatyam. Mrinalini Sarabhai founded the Darpana Academy in Ahmedabad.
Source: PYQ-style

32. Pandit Birju Maharaj was a leading exponent of:
(a) Bharatanatyam (b) Kathak (c) Odissi (d) Kathakali
Answer: (b)
Solution: Birju Maharaj belonged to the Lucknow gharana of Kathak.
Source: PYQ-style

33. Guru Kelucharan Mohapatra is associated with which dance form?
(a) Odissi (b) Manipuri (c) Kuchipudi (d) Mohiniyattam
Answer: (a)
Solution: Kelucharan Mohapatra was a great guru of Odissi, the classical dance of Odisha.
Source: PYQ-style

34. Pandit Shivkumar Sharma made which instrument famous in Indian classical music?
(a) Sarod (b) Sitar (c) Santoor (d) Sarangi
Answer: (c)
Solution: Shivkumar Sharma is known as the master of the santoor, a stringed instrument from Jammu and Kashmir played with small hammers. Trap: all four options begin with "S".
Source: PYQ-style

35. Ustad Amjad Ali Khan is a famous player of the:
(a) Sitar (b) Shehnai (c) Tabla (d) Sarod
Answer: (d)
Solution: Amjad Ali Khan is a sarod maestro. Trap: the sitar is linked with Ravi Shankar and Vilayat Khan, the shehnai with Bismillah Khan.
Source: PYQ-style

36. Ustad Zakir Hussain was a world-famous player of the:
(a) Flute (b) Tabla (c) Violin (d) Veena
Answer: (b)
Solution: Zakir Hussain, son of the tabla master Ustad Alla Rakha, was one of the best-known tabla players in the world.
Source: PYQ-style

37. Who is called the "father" (Pitamaha) of Carnatic music?
(a) Purandara Dasa (b) Tyagaraja (c) Tansen (d) Amir Khusrau
Answer: (a)
Solution: Purandara Dasa is called the Pitamaha of Carnatic music. Trap: Tyagaraja is part of the Carnatic Trinity, while Tansen and Amir Khusrau belong to the Hindustani tradition.
Source: PYQ-style

38. Which of the following is NOT a member of the "Trinity" of Carnatic music?
(a) Tyagaraja (b) Muthuswami Dikshitar (c) Syama Shastri (d) Purandara Dasa
Answer: (d)
Solution: The Trinity is Tyagaraja, Muthuswami Dikshitar and Syama Shastri, all born in Tiruvarur. Purandara Dasa is the earlier "Pitamaha", not part of the Trinity.
Source: PYQ-style

39. Tansen was a famous musician at the court of:
(a) Babur (b) Humayun (c) Akbar (d) Shah Jahan
Answer: (c)
Solution: Tansen was the celebrated singer of Akbar's court.
Source: NCERT Class 7 History

40. Warli painting, with white stick-like figures on mud walls, belongs to:
(a) Bihar (b) Maharashtra (c) Odisha (d) Madhya Pradesh
Answer: (b)
Solution: Warli is the tribal art of the Warli community of Maharashtra. Trap: Madhubani (Bihar) is the other folk painting most often asked.
Source: PYQ-style

41. Pattachitra, a traditional painting on cloth, is associated with:
(a) Odisha (b) Bihar (c) Gujarat (d) Kerala
Answer: (a)
Solution: Pattachitra ("patta" = cloth, "chitra" = picture) is a famous painting tradition of Odisha.
Source: PYQ-style

42. Kalamkari, the art of "pen-work" painting on cloth, is mainly associated with:
(a) Rajasthan (b) West Bengal (c) Andhra Pradesh (d) Punjab
Answer: (c)
Solution: Kalamkari (kalam = pen) is a cloth-painting tradition of Andhra Pradesh, with Srikalahasti and Machilipatnam as its centres.
Source: PYQ-style

43. Phad painting, made on long cloth scrolls telling the stories of folk heroes like Pabuji, belongs to:
(a) Gujarat (b) Madhya Pradesh (c) Haryana (d) Rajasthan
Answer: (d)
Solution: Phad scrolls of Rajasthan narrate the stories of Pabuji and Devnarayan. Gujarat's ritual painting is Pithora.
Source: PYQ-style

44. The Gol Gumbaz at Bijapur (Vijayapura) was built by:
(a) Muhammad Quli Qutb Shah (b) Muhammad Adil Shah (c) Ibrahim Lodi (d) Alauddin Khalji
Answer: (b)
Solution: The Gol Gumbaz, famous for its whispering gallery, is the tomb of Muhammad Adil Shah of Bijapur. Trap: Muhammad Quli Qutb Shah built the Charminar in Hyderabad.
Source: PYQ-style

45. Akbar built the Buland Darwaza at Fatehpur Sikri to mark his victory in:
(a) Gujarat (b) Bengal (c) Mewar (d) Kashmir
Answer: (a)
Solution: The Buland Darwaza celebrates Akbar's victory in Gujarat.
Source: NCERT Class 7 History

46. The Hawa Mahal, built in 1799 by Sawai Pratap Singh, is in:
(a) Udaipur (b) Jodhpur (c) Jaipur (d) Bikaner
Answer: (c)
Solution: The Hawa Mahal ("Palace of Winds") stands in Jaipur. Udaipur is known for the City Palace and Jodhpur for the Mehrangarh Fort.
Source: PYQ-style

47. The temples of Khajuraho in Madhya Pradesh were built by the:
(a) Cholas (b) Chandelas (c) Pallavas (d) Rashtrakutas
Answer: (b)
Solution: The Khajuraho temples, in the Nagara style, were built by the Chandela rulers. The Cholas and Pallavas built in the south in the Dravida style.
Source: NCERT Class 7 History

48. Nuakhai, the festival of eating the new rice of the season, is mainly celebrated in:
(a) Assam (b) Odisha (c) Kerala (d) Punjab
Answer: (b)
Solution: Nuakhai ("nua" = new, "khai" = eat) is an agricultural festival of Odisha, especially western Odisha.
Source: PYQ-style

49. The Kumbh Mela is held by turn at four places. Which of the following is NOT one of them?
(a) Prayagraj (b) Haridwar (c) Varanasi (d) Nashik
Answer: (c)
Solution: The four Kumbh sites are Prayagraj, Haridwar, Ujjain and Nashik. Trap: Varanasi is a famous holy city on the Ganga, but it is not a Kumbh site.
Source: PYQ-style

50. Khangchendzonga National Park, India's only "mixed" World Heritage site, is in:
(a) Arunachal Pradesh (b) Sikkim (c) Uttarakhand (d) Himachal Pradesh
Answer: (b)
Solution: Khangchendzonga National Park in Sikkim was inscribed in 2016 for both its natural and cultural value. Nanda Devi (Uttarakhand) and the Great Himalayan National Park (Himachal Pradesh) are natural sites.
Source: whc.unesco.org (India)
