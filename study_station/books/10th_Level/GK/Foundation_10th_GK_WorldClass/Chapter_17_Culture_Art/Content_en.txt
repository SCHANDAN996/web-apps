Indian Culture & Art — A Country That Dances, Sings and Builds

◆ Take a ₹10 note out of your pocket and turn it over. You will see a huge stone wheel — a wheel of the Sun Temple at Konark, built in the 13th century in Odisha. Turn over a ₹100 note: a stepwell from Gujarat, Rani-ki-Vav. A ₹500 note: the Red Fort. Without knowing it, you carry India's art history in your wallet every day. This chapter teaches you to read that history — dances and the states they come from, music and its masters, paintings, temples and forts, festivals, and the places the world has declared "World Heritage".

PART A — DANCE

1. The 8 Classical Dances

The Sangeet Natak Akademi (India's national academy for music, dance and drama) recognises eight classical dance forms. All of them trace their rules to the Natyashastra, the ancient text on drama, dance and music attributed to Bharata Muni.

Bharatanatyam — Tamil Nadu. Grew out of the temple dance tradition called Sadir. Rukmini Devi Arundale gave it a new life and founded Kalakshetra in Chennai (1936).
Kathak — North India (Uttar Pradesh and nearby). From "katha" = story; famous for fast footwork and spins (chakkar). Gharanas: Lucknow, Jaipur, Banaras. Great name: Pandit Birju Maharaj (Lucknow gharana).
Kathakali — Kerala. Dance-drama with heavy make-up and big head-dresses; green make-up (pacha) for noble heroes. Kerala Kalamandalam was founded by the poet Vallathol Narayana Menon.
Mohiniyattam — Kerala. "Dance of the enchantress" (Mohini); graceful, swaying, traditionally a solo female dance.
Kuchipudi — Andhra Pradesh. Named after the village Kuchipudi (Krishna district). Famous item: Tarangam, danced on the rim of a brass plate.
Odissi — Odisha. Based on the tribhanga (three-bend) posture; sculptures of Konark show its poses. Great name: Guru Kelucharan Mohapatra.
Manipuri — Manipur. Soft, flowing movements; the Raas Leela of Radha and Krishna is its heart.
Sattriya — Assam. Created by the saint Srimanta Sankardev (15th–16th century) in the sattras (Vaishnava monasteries). It was the last to be recognised as classical, in 2000.

Two from Kerala: Kathakali and Mohiniyattam. Students forget this and look for "one dance per state".

Mnemonic: "Big Kids Keep Moving, Kids Only Make Sounds" — Bharatanatyam, Kathak, Kathakali, Mohiniyattam, Kuchipudi, Odissi, Manipuri, Sattriya.

🧠 Active Recall: Which two classical dances belong to Kerala, and which classical dance was created in the monasteries of Assam? (Kathakali and Mohiniyattam; Sattriya.)

Punjab – Bhangra (men), Giddha (women) | Gujarat – Garba, Dandiya Raas | Rajasthan – Ghoomar, Kalbelia | Assam – Bihu | Maharashtra – Lavani | Karnataka – Yakshagana (dance-drama), Dollu Kunitha | Kerala – Theyyam | Himachal Pradesh – Nati | Jammu & Kashmir – Rouf | Mizoram – Cheraw (bamboo dance) | Tripura – Hojagiri | Chhattisgarh – Raut Nacha | Jharkhand – Karma | Bihar – Jat-Jatin | Chhau – Odisha, Jharkhand and West Bengal (a masked martial dance; Purulia Chhau and Seraikela Chhau use masks).

PART B — MUSIC

3. Two Great Streams

Indian classical music has two streams:
Hindustani — North India; vocal forms Dhrupad (the oldest), Khayal, Thumri, Tappa.
Carnatic — South India; based on compositions (kritis) sung to a raga and tala.

Carnatic pioneers: Purandara Dasa is called the "father" (Pitamaha) of Carnatic music. The "Trinity" of Carnatic music — Tyagaraja, Muthuswami Dikshitar and Syama Shastri — were all born in Tiruvarur (Tamil Nadu).

Hindustani legends: Amir Khusrau (13th–14th century) is linked by tradition with Qawwali; Tansen was a famous singer at Akbar's court.

4. Gharanas (schools of Hindustani music)

A gharana is a "family" or school that passes a style from guru to shishya.
Gwalior — the oldest gharana of Khayal singing.
Kirana — named after Kairana (Uttar Pradesh); founder figure Ustad Abdul Karim Khan; singers Pandit Bhimsen Joshi and Gangubai Hangal.
Agra — Ustad Faiyaz Khan.
Patiala — Ustad Bade Ghulam Ali Khan.
Jaipur-Atrauli — Ustad Alladiya Khan; later Kishori Amonkar.
Mewati — Pandit Jasraj.

5. Instruments and Their Masters

The Natyashastra divides instruments into four groups:
Tata vadya — string (sitar, veena, sarod, santoor, violin)
Sushira vadya — wind (flute, shehnai, nadaswaram)
Avanaddha vadya — skin-covered drums (tabla, mridangam, pakhawaj)
Ghana vadya — solid instruments struck together (manjira, ghatam, jaltarang)

Instrument – famous player (for exams):
Sitar – Pandit Ravi Shankar, Ustad Vilayat Khan
Sarod – Ustad Ali Akbar Khan, Ustad Amjad Ali Khan
Shehnai – Ustad Bismillah Khan
Santoor – Pandit Shivkumar Sharma
Flute (bansuri) – Pandit Hariprasad Chaurasia, Pannalal Ghosh
Tabla – Ustad Alla Rakha, Ustad Zakir Hussain, Pandit Kishan Maharaj
Sarangi – Pandit Ram Narayan
Violin – Lalgudi Jayaraman, L. Subramaniam
Mandolin – U. Srinivas
Mohan Veena – Pandit Vishwa Mohan Bhatt

Bharat Ratna musicians: M. S. Subbulakshmi (1998 — the first musician to receive it), Pandit Ravi Shankar (1999), Lata Mangeshkar and Ustad Bismillah Khan (2001), Pandit Bhimsen Joshi, and Bhupen Hazarika (2019, posthumous).

Mnemonic: "Bismillah blows, Ravi plucks, Shiv strikes" — Bismillah Khan = shehnai (wind), Ravi Shankar = sitar (string), Shivkumar Sharma = santoor (struck with small hammers).

🧠 Active Recall: Which group does the tabla belong to — tata, sushira, avanaddha or ghana? (Avanaddha — skin-covered drum.)

PART C — PAINTING

6. From Rock Shelters to Ajanta

Bhimbetka (Madhya Pradesh) — prehistoric rock shelters with paintings of animals, hunting and dancing; discovered by V. S. Wakankar. A World Heritage site since 2003.
Ajanta (Maharashtra) — Buddhist caves with wall paintings of Jataka stories; the painting of Bodhisattva Padmapani is famous.
Ellora (Maharashtra) — caves of three faiths: Buddhist, Hindu (Brahmanical) and Jain. The Kailasa temple (Cave 16) was cut out of one rock under the Rashtrakuta king Krishna I.

7. Miniature Painting

Mughal school — grew in Akbar's workshop (the Hamzanama), reached its height under Jahangir.
Rajasthani (Rajput) schools — Mewar, Bundi, Kishangarh. The Kishangarh painting "Bani Thani" by Nihal Chand is called the "Mona Lisa of India".
Pahari schools — Basohli and Kangra (hills of Jammu and Himachal Pradesh).

8. Folk and Tribal Painting — with States

Madhubani (Mithila) – Bihar | Warli – Maharashtra (white figures on mud walls) | Pattachitra – Odisha (cloth scroll) | Kalamkari – Andhra Pradesh ("pen-work" on cloth) | Gond – Madhya Pradesh | Phad – Rajasthan (scrolls of folk heroes Pabuji and Devnarayan) | Pithora – Gujarat | Kalighat – West Bengal | Tanjore – Tamil Nadu (gold foil) | Mysore painting – Karnataka | Cheriyal scroll – Telangana | Thangka – Buddhist scroll paintings of Sikkim, Ladakh and Himachal Pradesh.

Modern painters: Raja Ravi Varma (Kerala) — oil paintings of gods and epics, printed widely. Abanindranath Tagore — "Bharat Mata", father of the Bengal School. Nandalal Bose — illustrated the original Constitution of India. Amrita Sher-Gil, Jamini Roy, M. F. Husain.

Mnemonic: "My Wise Pet Kitten" — Madhubani (Bihar), Warli (Maharashtra), Pattachitra (Odisha), Kalamkari (Andhra Pradesh): the four folk paintings asked most often.

🧠 Active Recall: Warli and Madhubani — which state each? Who painted "Bharat Mata"? (Maharashtra, Bihar; Abanindranath Tagore.)

PART D — ARCHITECTURE

9. Three Temple Styles

Nagara (North) — a curved tower called shikhara over the sanctum. Examples: Khajuraho (Chandelas, Madhya Pradesh), Lingaraja (Bhubaneswar), Sun Temple Konark.
Dravida (South) — a pyramid-like tower called vimana over the sanctum, and huge gateways called gopurams. Examples: Brihadeshwara Temple, Thanjavur (built by the Chola king Rajaraja I), Shore Temple, Mahabalipuram (Pallavas).
Vesara (Deccan) — a mix of the two. Examples: temples of the Chalukyas at Pattadakal and the Hoysala temples of Belur and Halebidu.

Konark Sun Temple — built by King Narasimhadeva I of the Eastern Ganga dynasty in the 13th century; shaped as the chariot of the Sun god with 24 wheels and 7 horses; also called the "Black Pagoda".

Mahabalipuram (Tamil Nadu) — Pallava monuments: the five Rathas (each cut from a single rock), the rock relief "Descent of the Ganga" (Arjuna's Penance) and the Shore Temple.

Hampi (Karnataka) — capital of the Vijayanagara empire; the stone chariot of the Vitthala temple (shown on the ₹50 note).

Buddhist and Jain monuments: Sanchi Stupa (Madhya Pradesh; begun under Ashoka; famous carved gateways called toranas); Mahabodhi Temple, Bodh Gaya (Bihar); Dilwara Jain temples of white marble, Mount Abu (Rajasthan).

🧠 Active Recall: Shikhara or vimana — which belongs to the Nagara style? (Shikhara.)

10. Indo-Islamic and Mughal Buildings

Qutb Minar (Delhi) — begun by Qutbuddin Aibak, completed by Iltutmish.
Alai Darwaza (Delhi) — Alauddin Khalji.
Humayun's Tomb (Delhi) — built after Humayun's death, in Akbar's reign; the first garden-tomb of the subcontinent.
Fatehpur Sikri (Uttar Pradesh) — Akbar's capital; the Buland Darwaza marks his victory in Gujarat.
Taj Mahal (Agra) — Shah Jahan, in memory of Mumtaz Mahal.
Red Fort and Jama Masjid (Delhi) — Shah Jahan.
Bibi ka Maqbara (Aurangabad, Maharashtra) — Aurangzeb's son Azam Shah, for his mother; called the "Taj of the Deccan".
Gol Gumbaz (Vijayapura/Bijapur, Karnataka) — Muhammad Adil Shah; famous for its whispering gallery.
Charminar (Hyderabad) — Muhammad Quli Qutb Shah, 1591.

11. Rajput and Colonial Buildings

Hawa Mahal (Jaipur) — Sawai Pratap Singh, 1799.
Jantar Mantar observatories — Sawai Jai Singh II (Delhi, Jaipur, Ujjain, Varanasi, Mathura). The Jaipur one is a World Heritage site.
Vijay Stambha (Chittorgarh) — Rana Kumbha.
Gateway of India (Mumbai) — built to mark the 1911 visit of King George V and Queen Mary.
India Gate (New Delhi) — designed by Edwin Lutyens; a memorial to Indian soldiers who died mainly in the First World War.
Victoria Memorial — Kolkata. Chhatrapati Shivaji Terminus — Mumbai (World Heritage 2004).

Mnemonic (time order): "Queen Ate Hot Tea" — Qutb Minar (13th century, Aibak and Iltutmish) → Alai Darwaza (14th century, Khalji) → Humayun's Tomb (16th century, Akbar's reign) → Taj Mahal (17th century, Shah Jahan).

🧠 Active Recall: Who completed the Qutb Minar? Who built the Charminar? (Iltutmish; Muhammad Quli Qutb Shah.)

PART E — FESTIVALS

12. Festivals of the States

Pongal – Tamil Nadu (harvest) | Onam – Kerala (boat races, King Mahabali) | Bihu – Assam (Rongali/Bohag Bihu in April) | Lohri, Baisakhi – Punjab | Chhath – Bihar (worship of the Sun) | Durga Puja – West Bengal | Ganesh Chaturthi – Maharashtra | Navratri with Garba – Gujarat | Ugadi – Andhra Pradesh, Telangana, Karnataka (new year) | Gudi Padwa – Maharashtra (new year) | Bathukamma, Bonalu – Telangana | Nuakhai – Odisha (new rice) | Rath Yatra – Puri, Odisha | Hornbill Festival – Nagaland (1–10 December, Kisama village near Kohima) | Wangala – Meghalaya (Garo harvest festival) | Losar – Ladakh, Sikkim, Arunachal (Tibetan Buddhist new year) | Hemis festival – Ladakh | Sarhul, Karma – Jharkhand | Pushkar fair – Rajasthan (camel fair) | Thrissur Pooram – Kerala (elephants).

Kumbh Mela is held by turn at four places: Prayagraj, Haridwar, Ujjain and Nashik.

PART F — UNESCO

13. World Heritage Sites (as of 2026)

As of 2026, India has 45 World Heritage sites: 37 cultural, 7 natural and 1 mixed (source: whc.unesco.org).

The first four (1983): Ajanta Caves, Ellora Caves, Agra Fort, Taj Mahal.
The only mixed site: Khangchendzonga National Park, Sikkim (2016).
Natural sites: Kaziranga, Manas, Keoladeo (all 1985), Sundarbans (1987), Nanda Devi and Valley of Flowers (1988), Western Ghats (2012), Great Himalayan National Park (2014).
First Indian city on the list: Historic City of Ahmedabad (2017); then Jaipur City (2019).
Recent additions: Dholavira and Kakatiya Rudreshwara (Ramappa) Temple, Telangana (2021); Santiniketan and Sacred Ensembles of the Hoysalas (2023); Moidams — the mound-burials of the Ahom kings, Assam (2024); Maratha Military Landscapes of India — 12 forts (2025); Ancient Buddhist Site of Sarnath (2026).

14. Intangible Cultural Heritage (UNESCO)

Living traditions also get UNESCO recognition. Indian elements include Kutiyattam, Vedic chanting and Ramlila (2008), Ramman of Garhwal (2009), Chhau, Kalbelia and Mudiyettu (2010), Buddhist chanting of Ladakh (2012), Sankirtana of Manipur (2013), Thatheras of Jandiala Guru (2014), Yoga (2016), Kumbh Mela (2017), Durga Puja in Kolkata (2021), Garba of Gujarat (2023) and Deepavali (2025).

⚠️ Caution: Examiner's Trap

Trap 1 — "Similar Name": Kathak (Uttar Pradesh) vs Kathakali (Kerala); Kuchipudi (Andhra Pradesh) vs Kathakali. Read the full name before choosing the state.

Trap 2 — "Almost True Statement": "Qutb Minar was built by Qutbuddin Aibak." Only half true — Aibak began it, Iltutmish completed it. If the option says "completed by", the answer is Iltutmish.

Trap 3 — "Chronological Confusion": Sattriya is an old dance (Sankardev, 15th–16th century), but it was recognised as classical only in 2000. Likewise, the Chola temples were listed in 1987 but extended in 2004.

Trap 4 — "Negative Phrasing": "Which of these is NOT a classical dance?" — Chhau, Garba and Yakshagana are famous, but they are not among the eight.
