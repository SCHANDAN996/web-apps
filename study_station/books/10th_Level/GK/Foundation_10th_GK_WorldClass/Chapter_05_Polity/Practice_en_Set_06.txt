Q126. Which of the following statements regarding the nature of the Indian federation is correct?
(a) It is a federation of independent states.
(b) The Union is indestructible but the states are destructible.
(c) The federation is based on an agreement between states.
(d) States have the right to secede from the Union.
Answer: (b) The Union is indestructible but the states are destructible.
Solution: Unlike the USA, where the federation is based on an agreement and states cannot be easily altered, in India Parliament can reorganise states under Article 3. India is an indestructible Union of destructible states, as Dr. Ambedkar said.
Source: PYQ-style
Trap: Almost Correct Statement — options (a) and (c) mimic features of other federations; (d) is a common misconception.

Q127. The Supreme Court’s power to review its own judgments is conferred by:
(a) Article 137
(b) Article 141
(c) Article 142
(d) Article 143
Answer: (a) Article 137
Solution: Article 137 empowers the Supreme Court to review its own judgments, subject to laws made by Parliament. Article 141 concerns binding force of its judgments, Article 142 complete justice, Article 143 advisory jurisdiction.

Q128. The 97th Constitutional Amendment Act, 2011 made the right to form co‑operative societies a:
(a) Fundamental Right under Article 19(1)(c)
(b) Directive Principle under Article 43B
(c) Constitutional right only
(d) Both (a) and (b)
Answer: (d) Both (a) and (b)
Solution: The 97th Amendment inserted “or co‑operative societies” in Article 19(1)(c), making it a Fundamental Right. It also added Article 43B (DPSP) to promote co‑operatives, and Part IXB for their governance.
Source: PYQ-style
Trap: Almost Correct Statement — students often miss that it became both a FR and a DPSP.

Q129. The doctrine of “Prospective Overruling” was first applied by the Supreme Court in:
(a) Golaknath v. State of Punjab
(b) Kesavananda Bharati v. State of Kerala
(c) Minerva Mills v. Union of India
(d) Maneka Gandhi v. Union of India
Answer: (a) Golaknath v. State of Punjab
Solution: In Golaknath (1967), the Court held that Parliament could not amend Fundamental Rights, but applied the ruling only prospectively to avoid unsettling past amendments. Kesavananda introduced basic structure; Minerva applied it.
Source: PYQ-style
Trap: Chronological Confusion — mixing Golaknath and Kesavananda doctrines.

Q130. When the President neither gives assent nor returns a Bill passed by Parliament, this is known as:
(a) Absolute veto
(b) Suspensive veto
(c) Pocket veto
(d) Qualified veto
Answer: (c) Pocket veto
Solution: Pocket veto is exercised when the President simply keeps the Bill pending indefinitely without any action. The President of India can exercise pocket veto, unlike the US President where a Bill automatically becomes law after 10 days (except during adjournment). Absolute veto is refusal of assent; suspensive veto is returning for reconsideration.
Source: PYQ-style

Q131. Which of the following is not a constitutional body?
(a) Election Commission of India
(b) National Human Rights Commission
(c) Union Public Service Commission
(d) Finance Commission
Answer: (b) National Human Rights Commission
Solution: NHRC is a statutory body established under the Protection of Human Rights Act, 1993. The others are constitutional bodies under Articles 324, 315, and 280 respectively.

Q132. Which of the following salaries/emoluments is not charged on the Consolidated Fund of India?
(a) Salary of the President
(b) Salary of the Speaker of the Lok Sabha
(c) Salary of the Comptroller and Auditor General
(d) Salary of the Attorney General of India
Answer: (d) Salary of the Attorney General of India
Solution: The salaries of the President (Art 59), Speaker/Deputy Speaker (Art 97), and CAG (Art 148) are charged on the Consolidated Fund. The Attorney General’s remuneration is not fixed by the Constitution; it is determined by the President and is not charged expenditure.
Source: PYQ-style
Trap: Almost Correct Statement — many believe all constitutional functionaries have charged salaries.

Q133. The right to vote in elections to the Lok Sabha and State Legislative Assemblies is a:
(a) Fundamental Right
(b) Constitutional right under Article 326
(c) Statutory right only
(d) Legal right under the Constitution but not enforceable
Answer: (b) Constitutional right under Article 326
Solution: Article 326 provides for adult suffrage, making the right to vote a constitutional right. However, it is not a fundamental right, and the manner of its exercise is regulated by statute (RPA, 1950 & 1951).
Source: PYQ-style
Trap: Almost Correct Statement — confusing constitutional right with fundamental right.

Q134. The Sixth Schedule of the Constitution provides for the administration of tribal areas in the States of:
(a) Assam, Meghalaya, Tripura, and Mizoram
(b) All North‑Eastern States including Nagaland and Manipur
(c) All States having a tribal population
(d) Assam, Meghalaya, Tripura, Mizoram, and Nagaland
Answer: (a) Assam, Meghalaya, Tripura, and Mizoram
Solution: The Sixth Schedule (Articles 244(2) and 275(1)) provides for Autonomous District Councils in the tribal areas of these four states. The Fifth Schedule deals with other tribal areas. Nagaland has special provisions under Article 371A.
Source: PYQ-style

Q135. The “Doctrine of Severability” in Indian constitutional law applies to:
(a) Pre‑constitutional laws inconsistent with Fundamental Rights
(b) Post‑constitutional laws inconsistent with Fundamental Rights
(c) Both pre‑ and post‑constitutional laws
(d) Laws inconsistent with Directive Principles
Answer: (b) Post‑constitutional laws inconsistent with Fundamental Rights
Solution: The Doctrine of Severability is applied to post‑constitutional laws. If a part of a law is inconsistent with Fundamental Rights, only that part is void; the rest may remain if separable. Pre‑constitutional laws are dealt with under the Doctrine of Eclipse.
Source: PYQ-style
Trap: Similar Concept Confusion — mixing Eclipse and Severability doctrines.

Q136. Which of the following statements about the Preamble is not correct?

(b) It has been amended only once.
(c) It is not a part of the Constitution as per the Supreme Court.
(d) It states that the source of the Constitution is “the people of India”.
Answer: (c) It is not a part of the Constitution as per the Supreme Court.
Solution: The Supreme Court in Kesavananda Bharati (1973) held that the Preamble is a part of the Constitution. All other statements are correct.

Q137. The Prime Minister of India is:
(a) Elected directly by the people
(b) Appointed by the President
(c) Elected by the Lok Sabha
(d) Elected by an electoral college
Answer: (b) Appointed by the President
Solution: Article 75(1) says “The Prime Minister shall be appointed by the President.” Conventionally, the President appoints the leader of the majority party/coalition in the Lok Sabha, but constitutionally it is an appointment, not an election.
Source: PYQ-style
Trap: Almost Correct Statement — many assume PM is elected by Parliament.

Q138. The maximum gap between two sessions of Parliament cannot exceed:
(a) 3 months
(b) 4 months
(c) 6 months
(d) 2 months
Answer: (c) 6 months
Source: Article 85(1) mandates that the President shall summon each House at such intervals that not more than six months shall intervene between two sessions.

Q139. A joint sitting of the two Houses of Parliament under Article 108 is presided over by the:
(a) President
(b) Vice‑President
(c) Speaker of the Lok Sabha
(d) Chairman of the Rajya Sabha
Answer: (c) Speaker of the Lok Sabha

(a) Article 249
(b) Article 250
