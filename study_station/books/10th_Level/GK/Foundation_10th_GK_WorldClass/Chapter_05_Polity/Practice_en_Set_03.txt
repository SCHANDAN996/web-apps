51. Which of the following statements about the Preamble of the Indian Constitution is incorrect?
(a) The Preamble declares India to be a Sovereign, Socialist, Secular, Democratic Republic. (b) It has been amended only once so far, by the 42nd Amendment. (c) The Preamble is regarded as a part of the Constitution. (d) The Preamble was adopted by the Constituent Assembly before the main body of the Constitution was adopted.
Answer: (d)
Solution: The tempting error is to assume the Preamble came first because it appears first in the printed Constitution; in fact the Constituent Assembly adopted it after the rest of the Constitution, so that it matched the whole document. The other three statements are correct, so the incorrect statement is that the Preamble was adopted before the main body.

52. The expression 'Procedure established by law' in Article 21 of the Indian Constitution was borrowed from the Constitution of:
(a) United States (b) United Kingdom (c) Japan (d) Canada
Answer: (c)
Solution: The tempting wrong choice is the United States, but the US Constitution gives 'due process of law', which is wider; 'procedure established by law' was taken from the Japanese Constitution. So the answer is Japan.

53. Which of the following Fundamental Rights is available only to citizens of India and not to foreigners?
(a) Right to freedoms under Article 19 (b) Protection in respect of conviction for offences under Article 20 (c) Right to life and personal liberty under Article 21 (d) Right against exploitation under Article 23
Answer: (a)
Solution: Articles 20, 21 and 23 protect every person, including foreigners, so they are the tempting wrong options. The six freedoms under Article 19, such as freedom of speech and expression, are reserved for citizens only, so the answer is the right to freedoms under Article 19.

54. Under Article 15(5), the State may make special provisions for admission of socially and educationally backward classes, Scheduled Castes and Scheduled Tribes to educational institutions, including private aided and unaided institutions, but excluding:
(a) Central universities (b) Minority educational institutions (c) Deemed universities (d) Technical institutions
Answer: (b)
Solution: Article 15(5), inserted by the 93rd Amendment Act, 2005, covers private aided and unaided institutions, which makes 'technical institutions' a tempting wrong option. The express exception is minority educational institutions referred to in Article 30(1), so the answer is minority educational institutions.

55. The Supreme Court's interpretation of the Right to Life under Article 21 has included the right to:
(a) A clean and healthy environment (b) Carry on any trade or business (c) Practise any profession (d) Vote in elections
Answer: (a)
Solution: Trade, business and profession are tempting options but they fall under Article 19(1)(g), and voting is a statutory right, not a fundamental right. The Supreme Court has read the right to a clean and healthy environment into Article 21, so the answer is the right to a clean and healthy environment.

Solution: The 44th Amendment is the tempting wrong option because it also followed the Emergency, but it did not alter the Preamble. The 42nd Amendment Act, 1976 added 'Socialist', 'Secular' and 'Integrity' to the Preamble, so the answer is the 42nd Amendment.

59. Who administers the oath of office to the President of India?
(a) The Vice-President (b) The Speaker of the Lok Sabha (c) The Prime Minister (d) The Chief Justice of India
Answer: (d)
Solution: The Vice-President is tempting because of the Vice-President's high rank, but Article 60 provides that the President takes oath before the Chief Justice of India or, in the Chief Justice's absence, the senior-most judge of the Supreme Court. So the answer is the Chief Justice of India.

61. The President's power to grant pardon under Article 72 extends to:
(a) Only sentences by a Court Martial (b) Only death sentences (c) Only offences against State laws (d) All cases of Court Martial sentences, death sentences, and offences against laws on matters within the Union's executive power
Answer: (d)
Solution: Each of the other three options names only one category, which is the trap; Article 72 covers all three categories together, including death sentences, Court Martial sentences and offences against Union laws. So the answer is all cases of Court Martial sentences, death sentences and offences within the Union's executive power.

62. The Rajya Sabha can delay a Money Bill for a maximum period of:
(a) 30 days (b) 7 days (c) 14 days (d) 6 months
Answer: (c)
Solution: Six months is tempting because that is the period for resolving a deadlock on an ordinary bill, but under Article 109 the Rajya Sabha must return a Money Bill within 14 days, after which it is deemed passed by both Houses. So the answer is 14 days.

Solution: The President is tempting because bills are presented for assent, but Article 110(3) makes the decision of the Speaker of the Lok Sabha final on whether a bill is a Money Bill. So the answer is the Speaker of the Lok Sabha.

Answer: (a)
Solution: Thirty-five is tempting as a round figure, but the Supreme Court (Number of Judges) Amendment Act, 2019 fixed the strength at 34 judges in total, that is, the Chief Justice plus 33 other judges. So the answer is 33 other judges.

65. The Supreme Court of India has exclusive original jurisdiction under Article 131 in disputes:
(a) Between the Government of India and one or more States (b) Between two private citizens (c) Relating to criminal offences (d) Relating to election petitions
Answer: (a)
Solution: Disputes between citizens are tempting but they go to ordinary civil courts, not to the Supreme Court's original jurisdiction. Article 131 covers disputes between the Centre and one or more States, or between States themselves, so the answer is disputes between the Government of India and one or more States.

66. Which of the following is a correct statement about Public Interest Litigation (PIL)?
(a) It can be filed only by the aggrieved person. (b) Any public-spirited citizen may file it on behalf of those unable to approach the court. (c) It can be filed only in the Supreme Court. (d) It strictly follows the traditional rule of locus standi.
Answer: (b)
Solution: The rule that only the aggrieved person may sue is the tempting wrong idea; PIL relaxes the locus standi rule so that any public-spirited person can seek justice for the poor or disadvantaged, in High Courts as well as the Supreme Court. So the answer is that any public-spirited citizen may file it.

74. Which of the following is correct about a National Emergency under Article 352?
(a) It can be proclaimed on the ground of war, external aggression or armed rebellion. (b) It automatically suspends all Fundamental Rights. (c) It can be proclaimed by the Prime Minister. (d) It does not require approval by Parliament.
Answer: (a)
Solution: Automatic suspension of all Fundamental Rights is the tempting wrong option; only Article 19 is automatically suspended, and other rights are suspended only if the President's order says so. Article 352 permits proclamation on the ground of war, external aggression or armed rebellion, so the answer is that it can be proclaimed on those three grounds.

75. Which of the following is not a Constitutional body in India?
(a) National Commission for Scheduled Castes (b) NITI Aayog (c) Finance Commission (d) Election Commission of India
Answer: (b)
Solution: The other three are constitutional bodies — Finance Commission under Article 280, National Commission for Scheduled Castes under Article 338 and the Election Commission under Article 324 — which makes them tempting wrong options. NITI Aayog was created by a resolution of the Union Cabinet, so the answer is NITI Aayog.
