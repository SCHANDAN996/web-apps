51. Which of the following statements about the Preamble of the Indian Constitution is incorrect?
(a) The Preamble declares India as a Sovereign, Socialist, Secular, Democratic, Republic. (b) It has been amended only once so far. (c) It is a part of the basic structure of the Constitution. (d) The Preamble was adopted before the main body of the Constitution.
Answer: (d)
Solution: The Preamble was adopted by the Constituent Assembly after the main body of the Constitution was already adopted. It was taken up last to ensure it was consistent with the entire Constitution. The other statements are correct.

52. The term ‘Procedure established by law’ in Article 21 of the Indian Constitution was borrowed from the Constitution of:
(a) United States (b) United Kingdom (c) Japan (d) Canada
Answer: (a)
Solution: ‘Procedure established by law’ is taken from the Japanese Constitution. The USA has the concept of ‘due process of law’, which is broader and includes substantive due process. The Indian Supreme Court has expanded Article 21 to include ‘due process’ in Maneka Gandhi case.

53. Which of the following rights is not available to foreigners in India?
(a) Right to life and personal liberty under Article 21 (b) Protection against double jeopardy under Article 20 (c) Right to elementary education under Article 21A (d) Right against exploitation under Article 23
Answer: (a)
Solution: Article 21A provides free and compulsory education to children aged 6-14 years, but this is a right guaranteed to citizens only. The rights under Articles 20, 21, and 23 are available to both citizens and foreigners (though some aspects of Article 19 are citizen-only). Article 21A was inserted by the 86th Amendment and is specifically for citizens.

Solution: The Supreme Court has interpreted Article 21 to include the right to a clean and healthy environment as part of the right to life. Carrying on trade/business and practicing a profession are covered under Article 19(1)(g). Voting is a statutory right, not a fundamental right.

66. Which of the following is a correct statement about a Public Interest Litigation (PIL)?

Solution: PIL is a relaxation of the traditional rule of locus standi, allowing any public-spirited person to approach the court for the enforcement of legal and constitutional rights of a person or class of persons who cannot approach the court due to poverty, disability, etc.

73. The provisions of the Constitution relating to Emergency are borrowed from the:
(a) Weimar Constitution of Germany (b) Constitution of the United States (c) Government of India Act, 1935 (d) Constitution of Canada
Answer: (c)
Solution: The emergency provisions (especially the powers of the Centre over States during an emergency) are inspired by the Government of India Act, 1935. The suspension of Fundamental Rights during emergency was influenced by the Weimar Constitution, but the structural emergency provisions are from the 1935 Act. (Note: Some sources say Weimar for suspension, 1935 for federal emergency. The question asks broadly about emergency provisions; often UPSC links to 1935 Act for the scheme of emergency powers.) We'll stick with (c) as the primary model for federal emergency and President's Rule (Section 93 of the 1935 Act).
Source: NCERT Class 11 Indian Constitution at Work

74. Which of the following is correct about a National Emergency?
(a) It can be proclaimed on grounds of war, external aggression, or armed rebellion. (b) It must be approved by Parliament within one month. (c) Once approved, it remains in force for six months. (d) All Fundamental Rights are automatically suspended.
Answer: (a)
Solution: Article 352 provides that the President may proclaim National Emergency on grounds of war, external aggression, or armed rebellion. It must be approved by Parliament within one month (or two months if proclaimed when Lok Sabha is dissolved). Once approved, it continues for six months and can be extended indefinitely with parliamentary approval every six months. Only Article 19 is automatically suspended; other rights are suspended only if specifically mentioned in the order.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Indian Constitution at Work

75. Which of the following is not a Constitutional Body in India?
(a) Finance Commission (b) National Commission for Scheduled Castes (c) NITI Aayog (d) Election Commission
Answer: (c)
Solution: NITI Aayog is not a constitutional body; it was established by a cabinet resolution. The others are constitutional bodies: Finance Commission (Art 280), National Commission for SCs (Art 338), Election Commission (Art 324).
