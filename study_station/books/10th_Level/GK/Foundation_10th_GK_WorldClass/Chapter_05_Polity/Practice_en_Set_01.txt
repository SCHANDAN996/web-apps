1. Who was the permanent President of the Constituent Assembly of India?
(a) Dr. Rajendra Prasad (b) Dr. B.R. Ambedkar (c) Jawaharlal Nehru (d) Sardar Vallabhbhai Patel
Answer: (a)
Solution: The Constituent Assembly elected Dr. Rajendra Prasad as its permanent President on 11 December 1946. Dr. Ambedkar chaired the Drafting Committee.

2. The Indian Constitution was adopted by the Constituent Assembly on:
(a) 26 November 1949 (b) 26 January 1950 (c) 15 August 1947 (d) 26 January 1949
Answer: (a)
Solution: The Constitution was adopted on 26 November 1949 (Constitution Day) and came into effect on 26 January 1950.

6. Which Article of the Indian Constitution is known as the “Heart and Soul of the Constitution”?
(a) Article 19 (b) Article 21 (c) Article 32 (d) Article 44
Answer: (c)
Solution: Dr. Ambedkar described Article 32 (Right to Constitutional Remedies) as the heart and soul because it allows direct access to the Supreme Court for enforcement of Fundamental Rights.

7. The Right to Property was removed from the list of Fundamental Rights by which Amendment?
(a) 42nd Amendment (b) 86th Amendment (c) 24th Amendment (d) 44th Amendment
Answer: (d)
Solution: The 44th Amendment (1978) deleted the Right to Property (Article 31) from Part III and made it a legal right under Article 300A.

8. The Directive Principles of State Policy (DPSP) are enumerated in which part of the Constitution?
(a) Part III (b) Part V (c) Part IV-A (d) Part IV
Answer: (d)
Solution: DPSPs are contained in Part IV (Articles 36–51). Part III is Fundamental Rights, Part IV-A is Fundamental Duties.

15. The President of India can proclaim National Emergency under Article 352 on which of the following grounds?
(a) War, external aggression or armed rebellion (b) Failure of constitutional machinery (c) Financial instability (d) Internal disturbance
Answer: (a)
Solution: Article 352 permits emergency on war, external aggression, or armed rebellion. ‘Internal disturbance’ was replaced by ‘armed rebellion’ by the 44th Amendment. Failure of constitutional machinery is Article 356, financial instability Article 360.

20. The President of India is elected by an electoral college consisting of:
(a) Elected members of Parliament only (b) Elected members of State Legislatures only (c) Elected members of both Houses of Parliament and elected members of State Legislative Assemblies (d) All members of Parliament and State Legislatures
Answer: (c)
Solution: As per Article 54, the electoral college comprises the elected members of both Houses of Parliament (Lok Sabha and Rajya Sabha) and the elected members of the Legislative Assemblies of States and UTs (Delhi, Puducherry, J&K). Nominated members do not participate.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Indian Constitution at Work

21. Which of the following statements is correct regarding the Vice‑President of India?
(a) He is elected by an electoral college consisting of elected members of both Houses of Parliament and State Legislatures. (b) He can be removed by a resolution passed by the Lok Sabha alone. (c) He acts as the ex-officio Chairman of the Rajya Sabha. (d) He holds office for a term of 6 years.
Answer: (c)
Solution: The Vice‑President is elected by all members (elected + nominated) of both Houses of Parliament, not including State Legislatures (→ a wrong). He can be removed by a resolution of the Rajya Sabha passed by an effective majority and agreed to by the Lok Sabha (→ b wrong). He is ex-officio Chairman of Rajya Sabha (→ c correct). His term is 5 years (→ d wrong).
Source: NCERT Class 11 Indian Constitution at Work

22. The doctrine of ‘Basic Structure’ of the Constitution was propounded by the Supreme Court in which case?
(a) Golaknath v. State of Punjab (b) Kesavananda Bharati v. State of Kerala (c) Minerva Mills v. Union of India (d) Maneka Gandhi v. Union of India
Answer: (b)
Solution: The Supreme Court in Kesavananda Bharati (1973) held that Parliament can amend the Constitution but cannot destroy its basic structure.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Indian Constitution at Work

23. Which of the following is NOT a Fundamental Duty under Article 51A?
(a) To abide by the Constitution and respect its ideals and institutions. (b) To defend the country and render national service when called upon. (c) To pay taxes honestly and regularly. (d) To promote harmony and the spirit of common brotherhood.
Answer: (c)
Solution: Paying taxes is a legal obligation, not listed as a Fundamental Duty. Duties include abiding by the Constitution, defending the country, promoting harmony, etc.

25. During a Financial Emergency under Article 360, the President can:
(a) Suspend all Fundamental Rights. (b) Issue directions to States to observe canons of financial propriety. (c) Dissolve the Lok Sabha. (d) Assume to himself all powers of the State Governments.
Answer: (b)
Solution: Under Article 360, the President can direct reduction of salaries and can direct States to observe certain financial principles. Suspension of Fundamental Rights occurs under National Emergency (Article 358/359).
