76. The Constitution of India was adopted by the Constituent Assembly on:
(a) 26 January 1950 (b) 26 November 1949 (c) 15 August 1947 (d) 9 December 1946
Answer: (b)
Solution: The tempting option is 26 January 1950, but that is the date the Constitution came into force, not when it was adopted. The Constitution was adopted and enacted on 26 November 1949, now celebrated as Constitution Day, so the answer is 26 November 1949.

77. Which of the following Fundamental Rights is available only to citizens and not to foreigners?
(a) Equality before law (Article 14) (b) Freedom of speech and expression (Article 19) (c) Protection of life and personal liberty (Article 21) (d) Protection against double jeopardy (Article 20)
Answer: (b)
Solution: Articles 14, 20 and 21 protect all "persons", including foreigners, which makes them tempting wrong choices. The six freedoms under Article 19 are reserved exclusively for citizens of India, so the answer is Article 19.

78. The writ 'Mandamus' is issued by a court to:
(a) Order a public official to perform a duty (b) Quash an order of a lower court (c) Produce a detained person before the court (d) Inquire into the legality of holding a public office
Answer: (a)
Solution: Producing a detained person describes Habeas Corpus, which is the common trap. Mandamus literally means "we command" and compels a public authority to perform a legal duty it has failed or refused to perform, so the answer is ordering a public official to perform a duty.

79. Which of the following Directive Principles of State Policy was added by the 44th Amendment Act, 1978?
(a) Article 38(2) – Minimise inequalities in income (b) Article 43A – Participation of workers in management (c) Article 39A – Free legal aid (d) Article 48A – Protection of environment
Answer: (a)
Solution: Articles 39A, 43A and 48A were all inserted by the 42nd Amendment of 1976, which is the trap. The 44th Amendment of 1978 inserted clause (2) in Article 38, directing the State to minimise inequalities in income, status and facilities, so the answer is Article 38(2).

80. The President of India can be removed from office by impeachment under Article 61 on the ground of:
(a) Incapacity and violation of the Constitution (b) Proved misbehaviour and incapacity (c) Violation of the Constitution (d) Corruption and moral turpitude
Answer: (c)
Solution: "Proved misbehaviour or incapacity" is the ground for removing judges, not the President, which is the trap. Article 61 states only "violation of the Constitution" as the ground for impeachment, so the answer is violation of the Constitution.

81. Which of the following is not a power or function of the Vice-President of India?
(a) Acting as ex-officio Chairman of Rajya Sabha (b) Presiding over joint sittings of Parliament (c) Acting as President when the President resigns (d) Discharging the functions of the President in his absence
Answer: (b)
Solution: The Vice-President chairs the Rajya Sabha and acts as President when needed, so those options are traps. A joint sitting of Parliament under Article 108 is presided over by the Speaker of the Lok Sabha, so the answer is presiding over joint sittings of Parliament.

85. The salaries and allowances of the judges of a High Court are charged on the:
(a) Consolidated Fund of India (b) Contingency Fund of the State (c) Consolidated Fund of the State (d) Public Account of India
Answer: (c)
Solution: The Consolidated Fund of India is the trap, since Supreme Court judges' salaries are charged on it. High Court judges' salaries and allowances are charged on the Consolidated Fund of the State, ensuring their financial independence, so the answer is Consolidated Fund of the State.

86. The Governor of a State is constitutionally bound to reserve a Bill for the consideration of the President when the Bill:
(a) Relates to any matter in the State List (b) Is a Money Bill (c) Is passed by a simple majority of the Legislative Assembly (d) In his opinion, would endanger the position of the High Court
Answer: (d)
Solution: A Money Bill cannot be reserved in this manner, which makes it a wrong lure. Under Article 200, the Governor must reserve a Bill that in his opinion would so derogate from the powers of the High Court as to endanger its constitutional position, so the answer is a Bill endangering the position of the High Court.

88. Which of the following statements about the Legislative Council of a State is correct?
(a) Its members are directly elected by the people. (b) One-third of its members retire every year. (c) It is a permanent body and not subject to dissolution. (d) It has a maximum strength of 100 members.
Answer: (c)
Solution: The retirement trap is that one-third of members retire every two years, not every year. The Legislative Council is a continuing chamber never dissolved, with indirectly elected members and a maximum strength of one-third of the Assembly's strength, so the answer is that it is a permanent body not subject to dissolution.

91. The 'Doctrine of Eclipse' applies to:
(a) Pre-constitutional laws inconsistent with Fundamental Rights (b) All void laws (c) Laws inconsistent with Directive Principles (d) Post-constitutional laws inconsistent with Fundamental Rights
Answer: (a)
Solution: Post-constitutional laws violating Fundamental Rights are void ab initio, which is the trap. The Doctrine of Eclipse holds that a pre-constitutional law inconsistent with Fundamental Rights becomes inoperative (eclipsed), not dead, and can revive if the inconsistency is removed, so the answer is pre-constitutional laws inconsistent with Fundamental Rights.

92. Which of the following writs can be issued against a private individual?
(a) Habeas Corpus (b) Certiorari (c) Prohibition (d) Mandamus
Answer: (a)
Solution: Mandamus, Certiorari and Prohibition lie only against public authorities or courts and tribunals, which is the trap. Habeas Corpus can be issued against any person, public or private, who has detained someone, so the answer is Habeas Corpus.

93. The President can proclaim President's Rule under Article 356 in a State on the receipt of a report from the:
(a) Chief Minister of the State (b) State Legislature (c) Chief Justice of the High Court (d) Governor of the State
Answer: (d)
Solution: The Chief Minister is a tempting but wrong choice, since he heads the very government being displaced. Article 356(1) provides that the President may act on receipt of a report from the Governor of the State or otherwise, so the answer is the Governor of the State.

94. Which Constitutional Amendment is known as the 'Mini-Constitution' of India?
(a) 24th Amendment (b) 44th Amendment (c) 42nd Amendment (d) 73rd Amendment
Answer: (c)
Solution: The 44th Amendment is the trap because it reversed many 42nd Amendment changes and is often confused with it. The 42nd Amendment of 1976 made sweeping changes — Preamble words, Fundamental Duties, curtailing judicial review — earning the name Mini-Constitution, so the answer is the 42nd Amendment.

95. The right to vote in elections in India is a:
(a) Fundamental Right (b) Natural Right (c) Statutory (legal) right (d) Human Right enforceable under Article 32
Answer: (c)
Solution: Calling it a Fundamental Right is the classic trap; the Supreme Court has repeatedly held it is not one. Although Article 326 provides adult suffrage, the right to vote is governed by the Representation of the People Acts and is a statutory right, so the answer is statutory (legal) right.

(a) The expansion of the scope of Article 21 (b) The original jurisdiction of the Supreme Court (c) The power of Parliament to amend the Constitution (d) The advisory jurisdiction of the Supreme Court
Answer: (a)
Solution: Judicial review in general is a tempting answer, but activism goes beyond ordinary review. Indian judicial activism has largely grown through a liberal reading of Article 21 to include rights to privacy, livelihood and a clean environment, so the answer is the expansion of the scope of Article 21.

Solution: Division of powers, a supreme written Constitution and an independent judiciary are all federal features present in India, so they are traps. India provides single citizenship, not dual citizenship, so the answer is dual citizenship.

100. Which of the following is correct about the position of the Prime Minister of India?
(a) He is the constitutional head of the Union Executive. (b) He is the real executive head of the government. (c) He is appointed by the Vice-President. (d) His term is fixed for five years.
Answer: (b)
Solution: A fixed five-year term is the trap; the Prime Minister holds office during the pleasure of the President so long as he commands a Lok Sabha majority. The President is the constitutional (de jure) head while the Prime Minister is the real (de facto) executive head, so the answer is that he is the real executive head of the government.
