76. The Constitution of India was enacted and adopted by the Constituent Assembly on:
(a) 26 January 1950 (b) 26 November 1949 (c) 15 August 1947 (d) 9 December 1946
Answer: (a)
Solution: The Constitution was adopted on 26 November 1949 (Constitution Day) and came into full force on 26 January 1950. 9 December 1946 was the first meeting of the Constituent Assembly.

77. Which of the following Fundamental Rights is available only to citizens and not to foreigners?
(a) Equality before law (Article 14) (b) Protection of life and personal liberty (Article 21) (c) Freedom of speech and expression (Article 19) (d) Protection against double jeopardy (Article 20)
Answer: (c)
Solution: The rights under Article 19 (six freedoms) are available exclusively to citizens of India. Articles 14, 20, 21, etc., are available to all persons, whether citizens or foreigners.

78. The writ ‘Mandamus’ is issued by a court to:
(a) Quash an order of a lower court (b) Order a public official to perform a duty (c) Produce a detained person before the court (d) Inquire into the legality of holding a public office
Answer: (d)
Solution: Mandamus literally means ‘we command’. It is issued to compel a public authority to perform a legal duty that it has failed or refused to perform. Certiorari quashes an order; Habeas Corpus produces a body; Quo Warranto inquires into legality of office.

85. The independence of the High Courts is ensured by:

88. Which of the following statements about the Legislative Council of a State is correct?
(a) It is a permanent body and not subject to dissolution. (b) One-third of its members retire every year. (c) Its members are directly elected by the people. (d) It has a maximum strength of 100 members.
Answer: (a)
Solution: The Legislative Council (Vidhan Parishad) is a continuing chamber, not subject to dissolution. However, one-third of its members retire every two years (not every year). Members are indirectly elected (by local bodies, MLAs, etc.), not directly by people. Its maximum strength is one-third of the Legislative Assembly's strength, not fixed at 100.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Indian Constitution at Work

91. The ‘Doctrine of Eclipse’ applies to:
(a) Laws inconsistent with Fundamental Rights (b) Void laws (c) Pre-constitutional laws inconsistent with Fundamental Rights (d) Post-constitutional laws
Answer: (c)
Solution: The Doctrine of Eclipse provides that a pre-constitutional law which is inconsistent with Fundamental Rights is not void ab initio but becomes inoperative (eclipsed) to the extent of the inconsistency. It can be revived if the inconsistency is removed.

100. Which of the following is correct about the position of the Prime Minister of India?
(a) He is the constitutional head of the Union Executive. (b) He is appointed by the Vice‑President. (c) He is the real executive head of the government. (d) His term is fixed for five years.
Answer: (c)
Solution: The Prime Minister is the real executive (de facto head), while the President is the constitutional head (de jure). The Prime Minister is appointed by the President, not Vice‑President. The term is not fixed; he holds office at the pleasure of the President, subject to majority support in Lok Sabha. The Constitution was adopted on 26 November 1949 (celebrated as Constitution Day) and came into force on 26 January 1950. 9 December 1946 was the first meeting of the Constituent Assembly. The six freedoms under Article 19 are exclusively for citizens. Articles 14, 20, and 21 are available to all persons. Mandamus (“we command”) compels a public authority to discharge a legal duty it has refused or failed to perform. Certiorari quashes orders; Habeas Corpus produces a body; Quo Warranto inquires into office. The 44th Amendment inserted clause (2) in Article 38, directing the State to minimise inequalities. The other articles were added by the 42nd Amendment. Article 61 simply states “violation of the Constitution” as the ground for impeachment. The Constitution does not define what constitutes violation. The Speaker of the Lok Sabha presides over joint sittings (Article 108). The Vice‑President does not have this role. Article 80 lays down the composition of the Council of States, including representation of States and UTs, and nomination of members. The President, Vice‑President, and members of Rajya Sabha (elected by MLAs) are all elected by the single transferable vote system. Article 143 empowers the President to seek the advisory opinion of the Supreme Court on any question of law or fact of public importance. High Court judges can be removed only by impeachment (like Supreme Court judges) and their salaries are charged on the Consolidated Fund, ensuring financial independence. They are appointed by the President, not the Governor. Article 200 requires the Governor to reserve any Bill that, in his opinion, would derogate from the powers of the High Court so as to endanger its constitutional position. The 73rd Amendment inserted Part IX (Articles 243–243O) on Panchayats. The 74th Amendment dealt with Municipalities. The Legislative Council is a continuing house, never dissolved. One‑third of its members retire every two years (not every year). Members are elected indirectly. Its maximum strength is one‑third of the Legislative Assembly’s total strength. India provides for single citizenship, following the British model. The USA has dual citizenship (national and state). Article 24 specifically prohibits child labour in hazardous industries. Article 23 deals with forced labour, Article 21A with education, and Article 45 (DPSP) with early childhood care. The Doctrine of Eclipse holds that pre‑constitutional laws inconsistent with Fundamental Rights are not void ab initio but become inoperative. They can be revived if the inconsistency is removed. Habeas Corpus can be issued against any person or authority, public or private, to produce a detained person. The other writs are generally directed against public authorities. Article 356(1) states that the President may act on receipt of a report from the Governor or otherwise. The 42nd Amendment (1976) brought sweeping changes — adding words to the Preamble, Fundamental Duties, curtailing judicial review, etc. — earning it the nickname ‘Mini‑Constitution’. Article 326 guarantees adult suffrage, making the right to vote a constitutional right, though its procedure is governed by statute. It is not a fundamental right. Judicial activism in India has largely manifested through a broad interpretation of Article 21, incorporating rights to privacy, clean environment, livelihood, etc. India has a single citizenship; the USA has dual citizenship. The other three are features of the Indian federation. CAG is a constitutional body under Article 148. Finance Commission — Article 280, UPSC — Article 315, Election Commission — Article 324. The normal term of a Vidhan Sabha is five years, unless dissolved earlier. It may be extended during a National Emergency by Parliament for one year at a time. The Prime Minister is the real (de facto) executive head; the President is the constitutional (de jure) head. The PM is appointed by the President, and holds office at the pleasure of the President subject to majority support in the Lok Sabha, so no fixed term.
