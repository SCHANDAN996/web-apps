# Past Year Question (PYQ) Pattern Analysis: Indian Polity

## 1. Exam Weightage & Topic Trends
Questions from Indian Polity form a core pillar of general awareness sections across competitive examinations (SSC CGL, CHSL, MTS, Railway NTPC, State Group C/B services).
- **Executive (Union & State):** High frequency of questions on the President (Articles 52–62, 72 pardoning power, election by electoral college), Governor (Article 153–161, ordinance power Art 213), and Prime Minister/Council of Ministers.
- **Legislature (Parliament & State Assemblies):** Regular focus on Money Bills (Article 110), Joint Sitting (Article 108), Speaker's role, Rajya Sabha special powers (Articles 249, 312), and parliamentary procedures (Zero Hour, Question Hour).
- **Judiciary:** Structure and powers of the Supreme Court (Articles 124–147) and High Courts (Article 214–226), writ jurisdiction comparison (Article 32 vs 226), and judicial review.
- **Constitutional & Statutory Bodies:** Election Commission (Article 324), Comptroller and Auditor General (Article 148), Finance Commission (Article 280), and Public Service Commissions (Article 315).
- **Local Governance:** 73rd and 74th Amendments (Parts IX and IX-A, 11th and 12th Schedules, 3-tier Panchayati Raj system, Balwant Rai Mehta committee).

## 2. Common Question Archetypes & Exam Traps
1. **Direct Article Matching:** Matching major constitutional offices to their respective constitutional articles (e.g., CAG - Art 148, Finance Commission - Art 280, Election Commission - Art 324).
2. **Electoral College Traps:** Testing whether nominated members vote in Presidential elections (they do not vote for President, but participate in Vice-Presidential election).
3. **Panchayati Raj Committees:** Chronological order of committees (Balwant Rai Mehta 1957, Ashok Mehta 1977, G.V.K. Rao 1985, L.M. Singhvi 1986).
4. **Veto Powers & Ordinances:** Presidential ordinance power (Article 123) versus Governor's ordinance power (Article 213).

## 3. High-Yield Practice Questions (PYQ-Style)

1. Who among the following constitutes the electoral college for the election of the President of India?
(a) Elected members of Lok Sabha only (b) Elected members of both Houses of Parliament and elected members of State Legislative Assemblies (c) All members of Parliament and State Assemblies (d) Elected and nominated members of Rajya Sabha
Answer: (b)
Solution: Under Article 54, the electoral college consists of the elected members of both Houses of Parliament and the elected members of the Legislative Assemblies of the States (including Delhi and Puducherry). Nominated members do not participate.
Source: PYQ-style

2. Under which Article of the Indian Constitution does the President have the power to promulgate ordinances during recess of Parliament?
(a) Article 110 (b) Article 123 (c) Article 213 (d) Article 352
Answer: (b)
Solution: Article 123 empowers the President to promulgate ordinances when either House of Parliament is not in session. The Governor has corresponding ordinance powers under Article 213.
Source: PYQ-style

3. Which House of Parliament has exclusive constitutional authority to authorize Parliament to create a new All-India Service under Article 312?
(a) Lok Sabha (b) State Legislative Assembly (c) Rajya Sabha (d) Union Cabinet
Answer: (c)
Solution: Under Article 312, if the Rajya Sabha passes a resolution supported by not less than two-thirds of the members present and voting declaring it necessary in national interest, Parliament can create new All-India Services.
Source: PYQ-style

4. Who decides whether a legislative bill is a 'Money Bill' or not, and whose decision in this regard is final?
(a) The President of India (b) The Finance Minister (c) The Speaker of the Lok Sabha (d) The Chairman of the Rajya Sabha
Answer: (c)
Solution: Under Article 110(3), if any question arises whether a Bill is a Money Bill or not, the decision of the Speaker of the Lok Sabha thereon is final and conclusive.
Source: PYQ-style

5. What is the maximum permitted gap between two consecutive sessions of the Indian Parliament under Article 85?
(a) 3 months (b) 4 months (c) 6 months (d) 9 months
Answer: (c)
Solution: Article 85(1) states that six months shall not intervene between the last sitting in one session and the date appointed for its first sitting in the next session.
Source: PYQ-style

6. Under which Article of the Indian Constitution is the Comptroller and Auditor General (CAG) of India appointed?
(a) Article 76 (b) Article 148 (c) Article 280 (d) Article 324
Answer: (b)
Solution: Article 148 provides for an independent Comptroller and Auditor General of India, who is appointed by the President and audits all receipts and expenditure of the Union and State governments.
Source: PYQ-style

7. The retirement age of a judge of the Supreme Court and a judge of a High Court in India respectively is:
(a) 62 years and 60 years (b) 65 years and 62 years (c) 65 years and 65 years (d) 60 years and 62 years
Answer: (b)
Solution: Under Article 124(2), a Supreme Court judge holds office until attaining the age of 65 years. Under Article 217(1), a High Court judge holds office until attaining the age of 62 years.
Source: PYQ-style

8. Which Constitutional Amendment Act accorded constitutional status to Panchayati Raj Institutions by inserting Part IX and the Eleventh Schedule?
(a) 42nd Amendment Act, 1976 (b) 44th Amendment Act, 1978 (c) 73rd Amendment Act, 1992 (d) 86th Amendment Act, 2002
Answer: (c)
Solution: The 73rd Constitutional Amendment Act, 1992 came into force on 24 April 1993, adding Part IX and the 11th Schedule (containing 29 functional subjects) for Panchayati Raj.
Source: PYQ-style

9. The concept of a 'Joint Sitting' of both Houses of Parliament to resolve a deadlock on an ordinary bill is borrowed from the constitution of which country?
(a) United Kingdom (b) Australia (c) United States (d) Canada
Answer: (b)
Solution: The provision for a Joint Sitting under Article 108 was borrowed from the Australian Constitution. It is presided over by the Speaker of the Lok Sabha.
Source: PYQ-style

10. Who acts as the ex-officio Chairman of the Rajya Sabha (Council of States) in India?
(a) The Chief Justice of India (b) The Prime Minister (c) The Vice-President of India (d) The Speaker of the Lok Sabha
Answer: (c)
Solution: Under Article 64 and Article 89(1), the Vice-President of India is the ex-officio Chairman of the Rajya Sabha.
Source: PYQ-style
