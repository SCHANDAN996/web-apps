🃏 Flashcards

Flashcard 1
Front: Who called Article 32 the “heart and soul of the Constitution”, and why?
Back: Dr. B.R. Ambedkar — because it allows citizens to directly approach the Supreme Court if any Fundamental Right is violated.

Flashcard 2
Front: What mnemonic helps recall the six Fundamental Rights in order?
Back: “Every Free Indian Reads Constitutional Chapters” → Equality, Freedom, against Exploitation, Religious, Cultural & educational, Constitutional remedies.

Flashcard 3
Front: True or False: The Preamble cannot be amended under any circumstances.
Back: False. It can be amended (Kesavananda Bharati, 1973) as long as the “basic structure” is untouched. It was amended once — 42nd Amendment, 1976.

Flashcard 4
Front: During a National Emergency, do all Fundamental Rights automatically get suspended?
Back: No — only Article 19. Articles 20 and 21 (life and personal liberty) remain alive. Examiner’s trap: “all” is the red flag.

Flashcard 5
Front: What is the trick “PREM” and when is it used?
Back: It helps remember the Presidential Electoral College: Parliament (Lok Sabha) elected members, Rajya Sabha elected members, state Assembly elected members. Nominated members do NOT vote.

Flashcard 6
Front: Which Fundamental Right was removed from Part III by the 44th Amendment (1978)?
Back: Right to Property — it is now a legal right under Article 300A.

Flashcard 7
Front: The words “Socialist, Secular, Integrity” were inserted into the Preamble by which amendment?
Back: 42nd Amendment, 1976.

Flashcard 8
Front: The Vice‑President is elected by “All PMs”. What does this mnemonic mean?
Back: All Parliament Members (both elected and nominated in Lok Sabha and Rajya Sabha). State Assemblies do not participate. Opposite of the President’s election.

Flashcard 9
Front: What happens if Rajya Sabha does not return a Money Bill within 14 days?
Back: The Bill is deemed passed by both Houses. Rajya Sabha cannot reject a Money Bill — only recommend changes.

Flashcard 10
Front: How is Article 226 different from Article 32?
Back: Article 32: only for Fundamental Rights, directly in the Supreme Court. Article 226: High Court can enforce Fundamental Rights and ordinary legal rights. Trap: Article 32 does not cover all legal rights.

Flashcard 11
Front: How many Fundamental Duties exist today, and which amendment added the 11th?
Back: 11 duties. The 42nd Amendment (1976) added 10; the 86th Amendment (2002) added the 11th — education of children aged 6-14.

Flashcard 12
Front: Which part of the Constitution is called the “conscience of the Constitution”?
Back: Directive Principles of State Policy (DPSP). They are non-justiciable but guide the state towards a welfare model.

Flashcard 13
Front: “Dr. B.R. Ambedkar was the President of the Constituent Assembly.” Correct or not?
Back: Incorrect. He was Chairman of the Drafting Committee. The permanent President of the Constituent Assembly was Dr. Rajendra Prasad. (Trap: similar roles, names swapped.)

Flashcard 14
Front: In which case was the “Basic Structure” doctrine laid down, and what does it mean?
Back: Kesavananda Bharati v. State of Kerala (1973). Parliament can amend any part, including Fundamental Rights, but cannot destroy the Constitution’s basic structure — e.g., secularism, democracy, judicial review.

Flashcard 15
Front: What is the maximum strength of Rajya Sabha, and how many members are nominated?
Back: Maximum 250. 238 elected from States and UTs; 12 nominated by the President from literature, science, art, and social service.

Flashcard 16
Front: Which emergency has been imposed over 100 times, and under which Article?
Back: President’s Rule (State Emergency) under Article 356. Often controversial for misuse.

Flashcard 17
Front: The mnemonic “PM CHQ” lists the five writs. Name them.
Back: Prohibition, Mandamus, Certiorari, Habeas Corpus, Quo Warranto.

Flashcard 18
Front: What did the 104th Amendment (2019) discontinue regarding the Anglo-Indian community?
Back: Nomination of 2 Anglo-Indian members to the Lok Sabha and State Assemblies, as the community’s population had become negligible.
