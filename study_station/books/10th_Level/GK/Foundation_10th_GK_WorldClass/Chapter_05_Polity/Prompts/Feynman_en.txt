🧒 Feynman — The Basic Structure Doctrine
“You can't change a tree by cutting its roots.”

Imagine you get a beautiful old house from your grandfather. It has a deed that says, “You can change any room — add a new kitchen, paint the walls, even build an extra floor.” So you start renovating. One day you think, “This house would look much better if I removed the main load‑bearing wall on the ground floor.” Can you do it? If you remove that one wall, the whole house collapses. The deed didn't say you couldn't, but any sensible person knows you just can't — because that wall is part of the basic structure that keeps the house standing.

The Indian Constitution is that house.
Article 368 gives Parliament the power to amend any part of the Constitution. From 1951 to 1973, Parliament used that power to even change Fundamental Rights. Then came the Kesavananda Bharati case (1973) — the Supreme Court said: “Stop! You can amend everything, but you cannot destroy the basic structure of the Constitution. Some things are so fundamental that without them the Constitution would lose its identity.”

The Court never wrote down a final list of what the “basic structure” includes, but over the years it has pointed to things like:

Supremacy of the Constitution

Republican and democratic form of government

Secular character

Separation of powers

Federal character

Judicial review

Rule of law

So, Parliament can change the wording of an article, add new parts, even curtail some rights — as long as it doesn’t break the soul of the document.

🍕 Daily‑Life Analogy

Think of a pizza restaurant. The owner can change the menu — add a new topping, remove pineapple (please!), change the prices. But the owner cannot change one thing: the pizza must remain a pizza. If the owner starts selling only soup, it stops being a pizza restaurant. The “pizza-ness” is its basic structure. The Constitution’s “pizza-ness” includes things like democracy, rule of law, and secularism — they cannot be removed, no matter how many amendments Parliament passes.

🔖 An Unforgettable Trick

“DNA trick” — Imagine the Constitution has a DNA. You can change its hair colour, its clothes, even its height (ordinary amendments), but you cannot change its species from human to something else. The Basic Structure is the species‑defining DNA.
Visualise a double helix stamped with the words: Supremacy of Constitution, Democracy, Secularism, Judicial Review. Whenever a question asks, “Can Parliament amend Fundamental Rights under Article 368?”, remember: Yes, but only if it doesn’t alter the Constitution’s DNA.
