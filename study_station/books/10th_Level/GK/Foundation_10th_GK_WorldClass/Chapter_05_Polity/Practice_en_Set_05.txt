101. Which one of the following is not a feature of the parliamentary system of government in India?
(a) Collective responsibility of the Council of Ministers to the Legislature (b) Leadership of the Prime Minister (c) Presence of a nominal and real executive (d) Separation of powers between the executive and the legislature
Answer: (d)
Solution: The parliamentary system in India is based on the fusion of powers between the executive and the legislature, not strict separation. The Council of Ministers is drawn from Parliament and is collectively responsible to it. Separation of powers is a feature of the presidential system (e.g., USA).

102. The President of India can return an ordinary bill for reconsideration of Parliament:
(a) Only once (b) Any number of times (c) Twice (d) Only for Money Bills
Answer: (a)
Solution: Under Article 111, if a Bill (other than a Money Bill) is presented to the President, he may return it to Parliament for reconsideration. If Parliament passes it again with or without amendments, the President must give his assent. He can return it only once.

103. The concept of ‘Judicial Review’ in India is derived from the Constitution of:
(a) United States of America (b) United Kingdom (c) Canada (d) Ireland
Answer: (a)
Solution: The power of the judiciary to review legislative and executive actions and declare them void if they violate the Constitution is borrowed from the US Constitution. The UK does not have judicial review in the same sense due to parliamentary sovereignty.

121. Consider the following statements about the Preamble of the Constitution:
It is a source of power to the legislature.
It is a part of the Constitution.
It can be amended.
It is justiciable.
Which of the above statements are correct?
(a) 1 and 2 only (b) 2 and 3 only (c) 1, 2 and 3 (d) 2, 3 and 4
Answer: (b)
Solution: Statement 1 is false: The Preamble is not a source of power; it is a key to open the minds of the makers. It does not grant substantive power. Statement 2 is true: In the Kesavananda Bharati case, the Supreme Court held that the Preamble is a part of the Constitution. Statement 3 is true: The Preamble can be amended under Article 368, provided the basic structure is not destroyed. Statement 4 is false: The Preamble is non-justiciable; it cannot be enforced in a court of law. Thus, 2 and 3 are correct.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Indian Constitution at Work

124. With reference to the legislative powers of the President, consider the following:
He can promulgate ordinances when both Houses of Parliament are not in session.
An ordinance can be retrospective in effect.
The maximum life of an ordinance is six months and six weeks from the reassembly of Parliament.
Which of the statements given above is/are correct?
(a) 1 and 2 only (b) 2 only (c) 1 and 3 only (d) 1, 2 and 3
Answer: (d)
Solution: Statement 1 is correct: Article 123 empowers the President to promulgate ordinances when Parliament is not in session. Statement 2 is correct: An ordinance can have retrospective effect. Statement 3 is correct: The ordinance must be approved by Parliament within six weeks of its reassembly, and ceases to operate after six weeks from reassembly (if not approved), or earlier; thus, effectively, the maximum life is six months and six weeks from reassembly. All statements are correct.

125. The concept of ‘Equality before law’ in Article 14 is borrowed from the Constitution of:
(a) United States (b) United Kingdom (c) France (d) Canada
Answer: (b)
Solution: ‘Equality before law’ is a concept from the British (English) common law system. The Indian Constitution includes both ‘Equality before law’ (British) and ‘Equal protection of the laws’ (American) in Article 14.
