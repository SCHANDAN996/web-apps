Set 1/6 – Environment & Ecology MCQs (Foundation)
Questions 1–20: Easy | Questions 21–25: Medium

1. The term "ecosystem" was coined by:
(a) Ernst Haeckel (b) A. G. Tansley (c) Charles Darwin (d) Charles Elton
Answer: (b)
Solution: A. G. Tansley coined the term "ecosystem" in 1935. Trap: Ernst Haeckel coined the word "ecology", which sounds similar.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Biology (Ecosystem)

2. In a food chain, green plants are called:
(a) Primary consumers (b) Decomposers (c) Secondary consumers (d) Producers
Answer: (d)
Solution: Green plants make their own food by photosynthesis, so they are producers and form the first trophic level. Herbivores that eat them are the primary consumers.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science (Our Environment)

3. Which of the following are decomposers?
(a) Bacteria and fungi (b) Grass and shrubs (c) Deer and rabbits (d) Frogs and snakes
Answer: (a)
Solution: Bacteria and fungi break down dead plants and animals and return nutrients to the soil. Grass and shrubs are producers; deer, rabbits, frogs and snakes are consumers.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science (Our Environment)

4. According to the 10% law, roughly what share of energy passes from one trophic level to the next?
(a) 1% (b) 50% (c) 10% (d) 90%
Answer: (c)
Solution: Most of the energy is used in life processes and lost as heat; only a small part moves up. Trap: 90% is the part lost, not the part passed on. So the answer is 10%.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science (Our Environment)

5. The ozone layer is found mainly in which layer of the atmosphere?
(a) Troposphere (b) Stratosphere (c) Mesosphere (d) Thermosphere
Answer: (b)
Solution: The ozone layer lies in the stratosphere and absorbs harmful ultraviolet rays. Trap: ozone in the troposphere (near the ground) is a pollutant, not a protective layer.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science (Our Environment)

6. Which chemicals are mainly responsible for the depletion of the ozone layer?
(a) Carbon dioxide (b) Nitrogen (c) Argon (d) Chlorofluorocarbons (CFCs)
Answer: (d)
Solution: CFCs used in refrigerators, air-conditioners and sprays release chlorine in the stratosphere, and chlorine destroys ozone. Carbon dioxide is mainly linked with global warming.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science (Our Environment)

7. Which gas makes the largest contribution to global warming?
(a) Oxygen (b) Carbon dioxide (c) Nitrogen (d) Helium
Answer: (b)
Solution: Carbon dioxide, released mainly by burning coal, petrol and diesel, is the biggest contributor to global warming. Oxygen and nitrogen are not greenhouse gases.
Source: PYQ-style

8. World Environment Day is observed every year on:
(a) 22 April (b) 16 September (c) 5 June (d) 2 February
Answer: (c)
Solution: Trap: 22 April is Earth Day, 16 September is World Ozone Day and 2 February is World Wetlands Day. World Environment Day marks the opening of the 1972 Stockholm Conference and falls on 5 June.
Source: PYQ-style

9. With which person is the Chipko Movement most closely associated?
(a) Medha Patkar (b) Salim Ali (c) M. S. Swaminathan (d) Sunderlal Bahuguna
Answer: (d)
Solution: Sunderlal Bahuguna (along with Chandi Prasad Bhatt) is the best-known leader of the Chipko Movement. Trap: Medha Patkar led the Narmada Bachao Andolan.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Geography (Forest and Wildlife Resources)

10. Minamata disease is caused by poisoning from:
(a) Mercury (b) Cadmium (c) Lead (d) Arsenic
Answer: (a)
Solution: Minamata disease (Japan) was caused by mercury in fish. Trap: cadmium caused a different Japanese disease, Itai-itai.
Source: PYQ-style

11. Which gas leaked in the Bhopal gas tragedy of December 1984?
(a) Carbon monoxide (b) Methyl isocyanate (c) Chlorine (d) Ammonia
Answer: (b)
Solution: Methyl isocyanate (MIC) leaked from the Union Carbide pesticide plant in Bhopal on the night of 2–3 December 1984.
Source: PYQ-style

12. Gir National Park in Gujarat is famous for:
(a) One-horned rhinoceros (b) Royal Bengal tiger (c) Asiatic lion (d) Hangul
Answer: (c)
Solution: Gir is the home of the Asiatic lion in the wild. The one-horned rhinoceros is at Kaziranga and the Hangul at Dachigam.
Source: PYQ-style

13. Kaziranga National Park in Assam is famous for:
(a) Asiatic lion (b) Sangai deer (c) Great Indian Bustard (d) One-horned rhinoceros
Answer: (d)
Solution: Kaziranga is known for the one-horned rhinoceros. The Sangai deer lives in Keibul Lamjao (Manipur) and the Great Indian Bustard in the Thar desert.
Source: PYQ-style

14. Project Tiger was launched in India in the year:
(a) 1972 (b) 1973 (c) 1986 (d) 1992
Answer: (b)
Solution: Trap: 1972 is the year of the Wildlife (Protection) Act and 1992 is Project Elephant. Project Tiger began in 1973.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Geography (Forest and Wildlife Resources)

15. Which of the following is biodegradable?
(a) Plastic bag (b) Glass bottle (c) Vegetable peels (d) Aluminium can
Answer: (c)
Solution: Vegetable peels are broken down by bacteria and fungi, so they are biodegradable. Plastic, glass and aluminium are not broken down by microbes.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science (Our Environment)

16. Acid rain is mainly caused by which gases?
(a) Sulphur dioxide and nitrogen oxides (b) Oxygen and nitrogen (c) Helium and argon (d) Oxygen and hydrogen
Answer: (a)
Solution: Sulphur dioxide and nitrogen oxides dissolve in rain water to form acids. The other gases do not form acid rain.
Source: NCERT Class 8 Science (Pollution of Air and Water)

17. The loudness of noise is measured in:
(a) Hertz (b) Decibels (c) Joules (d) Pascals
Answer: (b)
Solution: Loudness of sound is measured in decibels (dB). Hertz measures frequency, not loudness.
Source: PYQ-style

18. Which ecological pyramid is always upright?
(a) Pyramid of numbers (b) Pyramid of biomass (c) Pyramid of energy (d) Pyramid of numbers of a tree
Answer: (c)
Solution: Energy is lost at every trophic level, so the pyramid of energy can never be inverted. The pyramid of numbers is inverted for a tree, and the pyramid of biomass is inverted in the sea.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Biology (Ecosystem)

19. The increase in concentration of harmful chemicals like DDT at each higher level of a food chain is called:
(a) Eutrophication (b) Biological magnification (c) Biodegradation (d) Bioremediation
Answer: (b)
Solution: DDT is non-biodegradable, so it builds up at every higher trophic level — biological magnification. Eutrophication is nutrient enrichment of water bodies.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science (Our Environment)

20. When excess fertilisers reach a lake and cause a heavy growth of algae, the process is called:
(a) Eutrophication (b) Acid rain (c) Biological magnification (d) Desertification
Answer: (a)
Solution: Nitrates and phosphates from fertilisers cause an algal bloom; this nutrient enrichment is eutrophication. It later lowers the oxygen in water and fish die.
Source: NCERT Class 8 Science (Pollution of Air and Water)

21. Consider the following statements:
I. Ozone in the stratosphere absorbs harmful ultraviolet rays of the Sun.
II. Ozone near the ground is harmless and useful for breathing.
Which of the statements given is/are correct?
(a) Only I (b) Only II (c) Both I and II (d) Neither I nor II
Answer: (a)
Solution: Statement I is correct. Statement II is wrong — ground-level ozone is a harmful pollutant that irritates the lungs. Hook: ozone is good up high, bad nearby.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science (Our Environment)

22. The Montreal Protocol (1987) is related to:
(a) Conservation of wetlands (b) Trade in endangered species (c) Reduction of greenhouse gases (d) Ozone-depleting substances
Answer: (d)
Solution: The Montreal Protocol phases out substances that deplete the ozone layer, such as CFCs. Trap: the Kyoto Protocol deals with greenhouse gases, Ramsar with wetlands and CITES with trade in endangered species.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science (Our Environment)

23. The Ramsar Convention, signed in 1971 at Ramsar (Iran), is related to the conservation of:
(a) Wetlands (b) The ozone layer (c) Deserts (d) Hazardous waste
Answer: (a)
Solution: The Ramsar Convention protects wetlands of international importance; World Wetlands Day (2 February) marks its signing. The Basel Convention deals with hazardous waste.
Source: PYQ-style

24. Which of the following is the correct food chain?
(a) Grass → Frog → Grasshopper → Snake (b) Snake → Frog → Grasshopper → Grass (c) Grass → Grasshopper → Frog → Snake (d) Grasshopper → Grass → Frog → Snake
Answer: (c)
Solution: A food chain starts with a producer (grass), then a herbivore (grasshopper), then the animal that eats it (frog), then the snake. Option (b) is the right animals in reverse order.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science (Our Environment)

25. Which of the following is an example of ex-situ conservation?
(a) National park (b) Wildlife sanctuary (c) Biosphere reserve (d) Botanical garden
Answer: (d)
Solution: A botanical garden protects plants outside their natural home, so it is ex-situ. National parks, sanctuaries and biosphere reserves protect species in their natural habitat (in-situ).
Source: NCERT Class 12 Biology (Biodiversity and Conservation)
