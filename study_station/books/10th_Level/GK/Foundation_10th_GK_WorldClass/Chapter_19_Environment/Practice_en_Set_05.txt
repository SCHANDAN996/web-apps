Set 5/6 – Environment & Ecology MCQs (Foundation)

101. Acid rain is primarily caused by atmospheric emissions of which two polluting gases?
(a) Sulphur dioxide (SO2) and Nitrogen oxides (NOx) (b) Carbon dioxide and Chlorofluorocarbons (c) Methane and Ozone (d) Carbon monoxide and Hydrogen sulphide
Answer: (a)
Solution: Sulphur dioxide and nitrogen oxides react with water vapour and oxygen in clouds to form sulphuric acid (H2SO4) and nitric acid (HNO3), precipitating as acid rain.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Chemistry, Environmental Chemistry

102. The total column thickness of the stratospheric ozone layer is conventionally measured in:
(a) Dobson Units (DU) (b) Decibels (dB) (c) Becquerels (Bq) (d) Pascals (Pa)
Answer: (a)
Solution: One Dobson Unit (DU) corresponds to a layer of pure ozone 0.01 mm thick at standard temperature and pressure; normal levels average around 300 DU.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Biology, Environmental Issues

103. Which landmark international treaty was adopted in 1987 to protect the ozone layer by phasing out ozone-depleting substances?
(a) Montreal Protocol (b) Kyoto Protocol (c) Basel Convention (d) Ramsar Convention
Answer: (a)
Solution: The Montreal Protocol was adopted on 16 September 1987 to halt the production of chlorofluorocarbons (CFCs) and halons; 16 September is celebrated as World Ozone Day.
Source: UNEP (unep.org)

104. "Eutrophication" in lakes and rivers is an ecological process triggered primarily by the excessive runoff of:
(a) Nitrogen and Phosphorus (b) Mercury and Lead (c) Carbon monoxide and Cyanide (d) Sodium and Potassium
Answer: (a)
Solution: Runoff of agricultural fertilizers containing nitrogen and phosphorus fuels explosive algal blooms; when these algae die, decomposers consume dissolved oxygen, causing hypoxia.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Biology, Environmental Issues

105. Minamata disease, which broke out in Minamata Bay in Japan, was caused by severe industrial poisoning from:
(a) Methylmercury (b) Cadmium (c) Arsenic (d) Lead
Answer: (a)
Solution: The Chisso chemical factory discharged methylmercury into Minamata Bay; it bioaccumulated in marine fish, causing severe neurological damage in consumers.
Source: PYQ-style

106. Photochemical smog, commonly observed in sunny metropolitan cities during summer, is formed by solar reactions involving:
(a) Nitrogen oxides (NOx) and Volatile Organic Compounds (VOCs) (b) Sulphur dioxide and smoke particles (c) Carbon dioxide and water vapour (d) Methane and nitrogen gas
Answer: (a)
Solution: Sunlight triggers photochemical reactions between automotive NOx and hydrocarbons (VOCs), producing secondary pollutants such as ozone, aldehydes, and peroxyacetyl nitrate (PAN).
Source: NCERT Class 11 Chemistry, Environmental Chemistry

107. Classical smog (often called London smog) differs from photochemical smog because it occurs in:
(a) Hot and arid sunny conditions (b) Cool and humid climate conditions (c) High-altitude stratosphere (d) Desert sandstorm environments
Answer: (b)
Solution: Classical smog is a reducing mixture of smoke, fog, and sulphur dioxide that forms predominantly in cool, humid winter conditions. Trap: photochemical smog requires intense sunlight.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Chemistry, Environmental Chemistry

108. The National Air Quality Index (AQI) launched in India in 2015 considers how many criteria pollutants?
(a) Five pollutants (b) Eight pollutants (c) Twelve pollutants (d) Sixteen pollutants
Answer: (b)
Solution: The AQI monitors eight pollutants: PM10, PM2.5, Nitrogen dioxide (NO2), Sulphur dioxide (SO2), Carbon monoxide (CO), Ozone (O3), Ammonia (NH3), and Lead (Pb).
Source: CPCB (cpcb.nic.in)

109. Which potent greenhouse gas is released in substantial amounts from flooded paddy fields and ruminant livestock digestion?
(a) Nitrous oxide (b) Methane (CH4) (c) Sulphur dioxide (d) Carbon monoxide
Answer: (b)
Solution: Methanogenic bacteria in anaerobic flooded rice paddies and ruminant animal enteric fermentation produce massive volumes of methane (CH4).
Source: NCERT Class 12 Biology, Environmental Issues

110. The phenomenon whereby persistent toxic chemicals become increasingly concentrated in tissues at higher levels of a food chain is:
(a) Biodegradation (b) Biomagnification (c) Eutrophication (d) Bioremediation
Answer: (b)
Solution: Biomagnification occurs because fat-soluble toxins (such as DDT and mercury) cannot be metabolized or excreted, multiplying in concentration from zooplankton to top predator birds.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Biology, Environmental Issues

111. Chronic consumption of groundwater containing excessive levels of fluoride over prolonged periods causes:
(a) Minamata disease (b) Fluorosis (skeletal and dental damage) (c) Itai-itai disease (d) Blue baby syndrome
Answer: (b)
Solution: Excess fluoride intake (above 1.5 mg/L) causes dental fluorosis (mottled enamel) and crippling skeletal fluorosis, affecting bones and joints in many Indian states.
Source: PYQ-style

112. The "Kigali Amendment" (2016) amended the Montreal Protocol to legally mandate the phased reduction of:
(a) Chlorofluorocarbons (CFCs) (b) Hydrofluorocarbons (HFCs) (c) Carbon dioxide (CO2) (d) Sulphur dioxide (SO2)
Answer: (b)
Solution: Although HFCs do not deplete ozone, they are extremely potent greenhouse gases; the Kigali Amendment entered into force to phase down global HFC production.
Source: UNEP (unep.org)

113. Under the Plastic Waste Management Amendment Rules, 2021, India prohibited identified single-use plastic items effective from:
(a) 1 January 2022 (b) 1 July 2022 (c) 15 August 2022 (d) 2 October 2022
Answer: (b)
Solution: The manufacture, import, stocking, distribution, sale and use of identified single-use plastic commodities with low utility and high littering potential were banned from 1 July 2022.
Source: MoEFCC (moef.gov.in)

114. The catastrophic collapse of vulture populations across the Indian subcontinent in the 1990s was caused by which veterinary drug?
(a) Paracetamol (b) Penicillin (c) Diclofenac (d) Ivermectin
Answer: (c)
Solution: Cattle treated with the anti-inflammatory drug diclofenac left toxic residues in carcasses; when vultures fed on them, they suffered acute renal failure, causing population crashes.
Source: MoEFCC (moef.gov.in)

115. "Blackfoot disease", a severe peripheral vascular disorder that gangrenes lower limbs, is caused by chronic exposure to:
(a) Lead (b) Cadmium (c) Arsenic (d) Mercury
Answer: (c)
Solution: Chronic consumption of groundwater contaminated with inorganic arsenic leads to arsenicosis, hyperpigmentation, skin cancers, and peripheral vascular Blackfoot disease.
Source: PYQ-style

116. Clean, unpolluted natural rainwater is slightly acidic with a pH of approximately 5.6 due to the atmospheric presence of:
(a) Sulphur dioxide (b) Nitric oxide (c) Carbon dioxide (CO2) (d) Methane
Answer: (c)
Solution: Atmospheric carbon dioxide dissolves in falling raindrops to form weak carbonic acid (H2CO3), naturally lowering the pH of unpolluted rain to about 5.6.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Chemistry, Environmental Chemistry

117. Respirable airborne particulate matter with an aerodynamic diameter equal to or smaller than 2.5 micrometres is denoted as:
(a) PM10 (b) PM50 (c) PM2.5 (d) PM100
Answer: (c)
Solution: PM2.5 consists of fine inhalable particles smaller than 2.5 microns that penetrate deeply into the lungs and enter the bloodstream, posing severe cardiovascular health hazards.
Source: CPCB (cpcb.nic.in)

118. Which layer of the Earth's atmosphere contains the vital ozone layer that filters out harmful solar ultraviolet-B (UV-B) radiation?
(a) Troposphere (b) Mesosphere (c) Stratosphere (d) Thermosphere
Answer: (c)
Solution: The ozone layer is concentrated in the stratosphere between 15 and 35 km altitude, shielding biological life on Earth from lethal solar UV-B rays.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Geography, Physical Geography

119. In waste management legislation, the statutory mechanism making brand owners and manufacturers responsible for the recycling of their post-consumer packaging is:
(a) Environmental Impact Assessment (EIA) (b) Corporate Social Responsibility (CSR) (c) Extended Producer Responsibility (EPR) (d) Clean Development Mechanism (CDM)
Answer: (c)
Solution: Extended Producer Responsibility (EPR) mandates that producers and brand owners establish collection systems and finance the environmentally sound end-of-life disposal of plastic packaging.
Source: MoEFCC (moef.gov.in)

120. The concept of the "Ecological Footprint", which quantifies human consumption against Earth's biocapacity in global hectares, was developed by:
(a) Rachel Carson (b) James Lovelock (c) Ernst Haeckel (d) Mathis Wackernagel and William Rees
Answer: (d)
Solution: Mathis Wackernagel and William Rees conceived the Ecological Footprint model in the early 1990s at the University of British Columbia.
Source: Global Footprint Network

121. Which synthetic greenhouse gas, extensively utilized in electrical switchgear, has the highest Global Warming Potential (GWP) of approximately 23,500 over a 100-year timescale?
(a) Methane (CH4) (b) Nitrous oxide (N2O) (c) Carbon dioxide (CO2) (d) Sulphur hexafluoride (SF6)
Answer: (d)
Solution: Sulphur hexafluoride (SF6), an inorganic fluorinated gas used in high-voltage circuit breakers, has a GWP of 23,500 relative to carbon dioxide over 100 years.
Source: IPCC Assessment Report

122. In ecological communities, the "Edge Effect" specifically denotes:
(a) The extinction of apex predators along continental shelves (b) The thinning of tree bark near forest boundaries (c) The decline of biomass in desert interiors (d) Greater species diversity and density in ecotones where two ecosystems meet
Answer: (d)
Solution: An ecotone (the transition zone between two ecosystems, like a forest and a grassland) often exhibits an edge effect with higher species richness and unique biological interactions.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Biology, Ecology

123. The term "The Evil Quartet", highlighted in biodiversity conservation, identifies the four primary drivers of:
(a) Acid rain formation (b) Desertification of grasslands (c) Depletion of stratospheric ozone (d) Species extinction and biodiversity loss
Answer: (d)
Solution: The Evil Quartet identifies habitat loss and fragmentation, over-exploitation, alien species invasions, and co-extinctions as the four major causes of contemporary biodiversity loss.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Biology, Biodiversity and Conservation

124. Under the Stockholm Convention, Persistent Organic Pollutants (POPs) such as DDT and Dioxins are characterized by:
(a) Rapid biodegradation in soil (b) Complete water solubility and quick flushing (c) Zero toxicity to mammals (d) Environmental persistence, bioaccumulation in lipids, and long-range atmospheric transport
Answer: (d)
Solution: POPs resist photolytic, biological and chemical degradation; they accumulate in fatty tissues of living organisms and travel across global boundaries via atmospheric grasshopper effects.
Source: Stockholm Convention (pops.int)

125. Atmospheric precipitation is scientifically classified as "acid rain" when its pH drops below which precise value?
(a) 7.0 (b) 6.5 (c) 6.0 (d) 5.6
Answer: (d)
Solution: Because unpolluted rain naturally has a pH of approximately 5.6 due to dissolved carbon dioxide, rainfall is classified as acid rain only when its pH falls below 5.6.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Chemistry, Environmental Chemistry
