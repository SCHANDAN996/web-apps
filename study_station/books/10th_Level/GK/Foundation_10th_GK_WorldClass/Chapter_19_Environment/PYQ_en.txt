📊 PYQ Pattern Analysis: Environment & Ecology (Basic)

Exams this chapter serves: SSC GD, SSC MTS, RRB Group D, state police / constable exams, and the general-awareness part of other state-level tests.

Note: this is a pattern analysis — what kinds of questions these exams like to ask from this chapter and how they trap you. We do not give year-wise counts or percentages, because no reliable data for them exists. The model questions below are written in the exam style; their sources are the NCERT chapters or official documents they test.

1. What Exams Like to Ask (Sub-topics, in plain words)

Ecosystem basics — producers, consumers, decomposers; who coined "ecosystem"; food chain order; trophic levels.

Energy flow — the 10% law, simple energy calculations, and which pyramid is always upright.

Pollution — pollutant–disease pairs (Minamata, Itai-itai, Blue baby, fluorosis), acid rain gases, smog, eutrophication, BOD, biological magnification of DDT.

Ozone and climate — where the ozone layer is, what depletes it, which gases are greenhouse gases, and which treaty deals with which problem.

Biodiversity and conservation — hotspots in India, in-situ vs ex-situ, famous parks and their animals, Project Tiger and Project Elephant years.

Laws — years of the Wildlife, Water, Forest, Air and Environment Protection Acts; Articles 48A and 51A(g).

International conventions and days — Ramsar, CITES, Montreal, Kyoto, Paris, Rio, Stockholm; World Environment Day, Earth Day, Ozone Day, Wetlands Day.

Environmental movements — Chipko, Appiko, Bishnoi, Silent Valley, Narmada Bachao Andolan.

2. Exam-wise Style (qualitative)

SSC GD / SSC MTS — short, direct one-liners: "Minamata disease is caused by?", "World Environment Day is on?", "Who led the Chipko movement?". Speed matters.

RRB Group D — similar direct questions, plus science-flavoured ones from NCERT Class 10 "Our Environment" (food chains, 10% law, ozone, biodegradable vs non-biodegradable).

State police / constable exams — direct facts, with extra weight on the home state's national parks, sanctuaries, wetlands and movements.

Higher exams that use these same basics (CHSL, NTPC, CGL) add statement-based questions, "arrange in order" questions on treaties and Acts, and "match the following".

3. Top 10 High-Yield Model Questions with Solutions and Trap Analysis

Question 1
The term "ecosystem" was coined by:
(a) Ernst Haeckel (b) A. G. Tansley (c) Charles Darwin (d) E. P. Odum
Answer: (b) A. G. Tansley
Trap Type: Similar Name — Haeckel coined "ecology", a word that looks and sounds close.
15/45-sec Rule: 15-second fact. Score maximizer.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Biology (Ecosystem)

Question 2
Minamata disease is caused by poisoning from:
(a) Cadmium (b) Lead (c) Mercury (d) Arsenic
Answer: (c) Mercury
Trap Type: Similar Name — Itai-itai (cadmium) is the other Japanese disease, so cadmium is placed first to catch you.
15/45-sec Rule: 15-second fact.
Source: PYQ-style

Question 3
If the producers in a food chain have 10,000 J of energy, how much energy will be available to the secondary consumers?
(a) 1,000 J (b) 100 J (c) 10 J (d) 1 J
Answer: (b) 100 J
Trap Type: Almost True Statement — 1,000 J is the energy at the primary consumer level, one step too early.
15/45-sec Rule: 45 seconds — write 10,000 → 1,000 → 100 before choosing.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science (Our Environment)

Question 4
The Montreal Protocol is related to:
(a) Reduction of greenhouse gases (b) Protection of the ozone layer (c) Conservation of wetlands (d) Trade in endangered species
Answer: (b) Protection of the ozone layer
Trap Type: Similar Name — Kyoto (greenhouse gases), Ramsar (wetlands) and CITES (trade) are all placed as options.
15/45-sec Rule: 15-second fact.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science (Our Environment)

Question 5
Which of the following is NOT a greenhouse gas?
(a) Methane (b) Nitrous oxide (c) Nitrogen (d) Carbon dioxide
Answer: (c) Nitrogen
Trap Type: Negative Phrasing + Similar Name — "nitrogen" looks like "nitrous oxide".
15/45-sec Rule: 45 seconds — read NOT twice, then check each gas.
Source: PYQ-style

Question 6
Which of the following is an example of ex-situ conservation?
(a) National park (b) Wildlife sanctuary (c) Seed bank (d) Biosphere reserve
Answer: (c) Seed bank
Trap Type: Almost True Statement — three options are protected areas, and students who mix up "in" and "ex" pick a park.
15/45-sec Rule: 15 seconds with the hook "EX-situ = EXit the jungle".
Source: NCERT Class 12 Biology (Biodiversity and Conservation)

Question 7
Arrange in chronological order:
I. Environment (Protection) Act
II. Wildlife (Protection) Act
III. Air (Prevention and Control of Pollution) Act
IV. Water (Prevention and Control of Pollution) Act
(a) II, IV, III, I (b) IV, II, III, I (c) II, III, IV, I (d) II, IV, I, III
Answer: (a) II, IV, III, I — 1972, 1974, 1981, 1986
Trap Type: Chronological Confusion — Water (1974) and Air (1981) are often swapped.
15/45-sec Rule: 45 seconds — write the year beside each Act first.
Source: Government of India (Ministry of Environment, Forest and Climate Change)

Question 8
Consider the following statements:
I. The pyramid of energy is always upright.
II. The pyramid of biomass in the sea is always upright.
Which of the statements given is/are correct?
(a) Only I (b) Only II (c) Both I and II (d) Neither I nor II
Answer: (a) Only I
Trap Type: Almost True Statement — the biomass pyramid is upright on land but inverted in the sea.
15/45-sec Rule: 45 seconds — check each statement separately.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Biology (Ecosystem)

Question 9
The Chipko Movement started in 1973 in which present-day state?
(a) Himachal Pradesh (b) Uttarakhand (c) Karnataka (d) Rajasthan
Answer: (b) Uttarakhand
Trap Type: Similar Name — Karnataka is the home of the Appiko Movement ("Chipko of the south"); Rajasthan is linked with the Bishnois.
15/45-sec Rule: 15-second fact.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Geography (Forest and Wildlife Resources)

Question 10
World Environment Day is observed on:
(a) 22 April (b) 5 June (c) 16 September (d) 2 February
Answer: (b) 5 June
Trap Type: Similar Name (similar "green days") — 22 April is Earth Day, 16 September is Ozone Day, 2 February is Wetlands Day.
15/45-sec Rule: 15-second fact.
Source: PYQ-style

4. How to Use This Analysis

Make one card for every pollutant–disease pair and every park–animal pair; these are the quickest marks in the paper.

Every time you see "NOT", "except" or "incorrectly matched", slow down — that is a 45-second question, not a 15-second one.

For years, learn the sequence first (1971 Ramsar → 1972 Stockholm/Wildlife Act → 1973 CITES/Chipko/Project Tiger → 1974 Water Act → 1980 Forest Act → 1981 Air Act → 1984 Bhopal → 1986 EPA → 1987 Montreal → 1992 Rio/Project Elephant → 1997 Kyoto → 2002 Biodiversity Act → 2010 NGT → 2015 Paris) and then attach each event to its year.

For counts that change (Ramsar sites, tiger reserves), always learn the number with its date, and check the latest official figure before the exam.
