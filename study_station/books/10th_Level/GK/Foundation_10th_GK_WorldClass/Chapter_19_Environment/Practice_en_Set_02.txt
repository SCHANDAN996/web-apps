Set 2/6 – Environment & Ecology MCQs (Foundation)
Questions 26–50: Medium

26. In which year was the Kyoto Protocol on greenhouse gases adopted?
(a) 1987 (b) 1992 (c) 1997 (d) 2015
Answer: (c)
Solution: Trap: 1987 is the Montreal Protocol (ozone), 1992 is the Rio Earth Summit and 2015 is the Paris Agreement. The Kyoto Protocol was adopted in 1997.
Source: PYQ-style

27. The Paris Agreement (2015) aims to keep the rise in global average temperature well below how many degrees Celsius above pre-industrial levels?
(a) 1°C (b) 2°C (c) 3°C (d) 5°C
Answer: (b)
Solution: The Paris Agreement also asks countries to try to limit warming to 1.5°C, but its main limit is well below 2°C.
Source: Paris Agreement (UNFCCC), 2015

28. CITES is an international agreement related to:
(a) International trade in endangered wild animals and plants (b) Climate change (c) Wetlands (d) The ozone layer
Answer: (a)
Solution: CITES (1973) controls international trade in endangered species of wild fauna and flora. Trap: wetlands belong to Ramsar, the ozone layer to Montreal and climate change to the UNFCCC.
Source: PYQ-style

29. The Environment (Protection) Act was passed after the Bhopal gas tragedy in the year:
(a) 1984 (b) 1986 (c) 1972 (d) 1981
Answer: (b)
Solution: Trap: 1984 is the year of the Bhopal tragedy itself, 1972 is the Wildlife Act and 1981 is the Air Act. The Environment (Protection) Act came in 1986.
Source: Government of India (Ministry of Environment, Forest and Climate Change)

30. The Wildlife (Protection) Act of India was enacted in:
(a) 1972 (b) 1974 (c) 1980 (d) 1986
Answer: (a)
Solution: Trap: 1974 is the Water Act, 1980 the Forest (Conservation) Act and 1986 the Environment (Protection) Act. The Wildlife (Protection) Act was enacted in 1972.
Source: Government of India (Ministry of Environment, Forest and Climate Change)

31. The Central Pollution Control Board (CPCB) was first constituted under which Act?
(a) Air (Prevention and Control of Pollution) Act, 1981 (b) Environment (Protection) Act, 1986 (c) Wildlife (Protection) Act, 1972 (d) Water (Prevention and Control of Pollution) Act, 1974
Answer: (d)
Solution: The CPCB was set up under the Water Act; the Air Act later gave it powers for air pollution too. So the correct choice is the Water (Prevention and Control of Pollution) Act, 1974.
Source: Government of India (Central Pollution Control Board)

32. Which Article of the Constitution makes it a Fundamental Duty of every citizen to protect and improve the natural environment?
(a) Article 48A (b) Article 51A(g) (c) Article 21 (d) Article 32
Answer: (b)
Solution: Trap: Article 48A is the Directive Principle asking the State to protect the environment. The citizen's Fundamental Duty is in Article 51A(g).
Source: Constitution of India (42nd Amendment, 1976)

33. By the 42nd Amendment (1976), "forests" and "protection of wild animals and birds" were moved to which list?
(a) Union List (b) State List (c) Concurrent List (d) Residuary powers
Answer: (c)
Solution: These subjects were moved from the State List to the Concurrent List, so both the Centre and the States can make laws on them.
Source: Constitution of India (42nd Amendment, 1976)

34. The National Green Tribunal (NGT) was established in the year:
(a) 2010 (b) 2002 (c) 2006 (d) 2015
Answer: (a)
Solution: Trap: 2002 is the Biological Diversity Act and 2006 is the Forest Rights Act. The NGT was set up in October 2010 under the National Green Tribunal Act, 2010.
Source: Government of India (Ministry of Environment, Forest and Climate Change)

35. The National Biodiversity Authority, set up under the Biological Diversity Act, 2002, has its headquarters at:
(a) New Delhi (b) Chennai (c) Dehradun (d) Bengaluru
Answer: (b)
Solution: The National Biodiversity Authority is located in Chennai. Trap: Dehradun is the headquarters of the Forest Survey of India.
Source: Government of India (National Biodiversity Authority)

36. The Earth Summit, where the UNFCCC and the Convention on Biological Diversity were opened for signature, was held at Rio de Janeiro in:
(a) 1972 (b) 1987 (c) 1997 (d) 1992
Answer: (d)
Solution: Trap: 1972 is the Stockholm Conference, 1987 the Montreal Protocol and 1997 the Kyoto Protocol. The Rio Earth Summit was held in 1992.
Source: PYQ-style

37. The headquarters of the United Nations Environment Programme (UNEP) is in:
(a) Geneva (b) New York (c) Nairobi (d) Gland
Answer: (c)
Solution: UNEP, created after the 1972 Stockholm Conference, is based in Nairobi, Kenya. Trap: Gland (Switzerland) is the headquarters of IUCN.
Source: PYQ-style

38. The Red List (Red Data Book) of threatened species is published by:
(a) UNEP (b) IUCN (c) WWF (d) UNESCO
Answer: (b)
Solution: The International Union for Conservation of Nature (IUCN) prepares the Red List of threatened species. UNESCO runs the Man and the Biosphere programme, not the Red List.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Biology (Biodiversity and Conservation)

39. In which ecosystem is the pyramid of biomass generally inverted?
(a) Grassland (b) Forest (c) Desert (d) Ocean
Answer: (d)
Solution: In the sea, the biomass of tiny phytoplankton at any one time is less than that of the fish feeding on them, so the pyramid is inverted. On land it is upright.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Biology (Ecosystem)

40. In which case is the pyramid of numbers inverted?
(a) A big tree with many insects feeding on it (b) A grassland (c) A pond (d) A crop field
Answer: (a)
Solution: One tree supports a large number of insects, so the base is narrow and the next level wide — an inverted pyramid of numbers. In a grassland the producers are the most numerous.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Biology (Ecosystem)

41. Which was the first national park of India, set up in 1936?
(a) Gir National Park (b) Kaziranga National Park (c) Hailey National Park (d) Kanha National Park
Answer: (c)
Solution: Hailey National Park (1936) is now called Jim Corbett National Park in Uttarakhand. Gir and Kaziranga are famous parks but came later.
Source: PYQ-style

42. Which was the first biosphere reserve of India, set up in 1986?
(a) Sundarbans (b) Nilgiri (c) Nanda Devi (d) Gulf of Mannar
Answer: (b)
Solution: The Nilgiri Biosphere Reserve (Tamil Nadu, Kerala and Karnataka) was India's first biosphere reserve. The other three were set up later.
Source: PYQ-style

43. "Blue baby syndrome" is caused by an excess of which substance in drinking water?
(a) Fluoride (b) Arsenic (c) Nitrate (d) Mercury
Answer: (c)
Solution: Excess nitrate in drinking water affects the blood's ability to carry oxygen in infants, causing blue baby syndrome. Trap: excess fluoride causes fluorosis.
Source: PYQ-style

44. Itai-itai disease is caused by poisoning from:
(a) Cadmium (b) Mercury (c) Lead (d) Zinc
Answer: (a)
Solution: Itai-itai disease in Japan was caused by cadmium and made bones painful and weak. Trap: mercury caused Minamata disease.
Source: PYQ-style

45. Consider the following statements about BOD (Biochemical Oxygen Demand):
I. A high BOD shows that water has a lot of organic pollution.
II. Clean drinking water has a high BOD.
Which of the statements given is/are correct?
(a) Only I (b) Only II (c) Both I and II (d) Neither I nor II
Answer: (a)
Solution: More organic matter means microbes need more oxygen to break it down, so polluted water has a high BOD. Clean water has a low BOD, so statement II is wrong.
Source: PYQ-style

46. Lichens are used as indicators of:
(a) Water pollution by nitrates (b) Air pollution by sulphur dioxide (c) Soil salinity (d) Noise pollution
Answer: (b)
Solution: Lichens are very sensitive to sulphur dioxide and do not grow in areas where its level is high, so they indicate air pollution.
Source: PYQ-style

47. Why is carbon monoxide dangerous for human beings?
(a) It depletes the ozone layer (b) It causes acid rain (c) It causes eutrophication (d) It combines with haemoglobin and reduces the oxygen carried by blood
Answer: (d)
Solution: Carbon monoxide binds to haemoglobin much more strongly than oxygen, so the blood carries less oxygen. Acid rain is caused by sulphur dioxide and nitrogen oxides, not carbon monoxide.
Source: NCERT Class 8 Science (Pollution of Air and Water)

48. Rhizobium bacteria fix atmospheric nitrogen in the root nodules of:
(a) Wheat (b) Rice (c) Leguminous plants such as pea and gram (d) Maize
Answer: (c)
Solution: Rhizobium lives in the root nodules of leguminous plants and fixes nitrogen, which is why pulses enrich the soil. Wheat, rice and maize are cereals and do not have such nodules.
Source: NCERT Class 8 Science (Crop Production and Management)

49. Parthenium (carrot grass) and Lantana spreading in India are examples of:
(a) Endemic species (b) Invasive alien species (c) Keystone species (d) Extinct species
Answer: (b)
Solution: These plants came from outside and spread fast, harming native plants — they are invasive alien species, one of the "Evil Quartet" causes of biodiversity loss. Endemic species are native species found only in one area.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Biology (Biodiversity and Conservation)

50. Which invasive aquatic weed has been called the "Terror of Bengal"?
(a) Lotus (b) Hydrilla (c) Lantana (d) Water hyacinth
Answer: (d)
Solution: Water hyacinth (Eichhornia) spreads over ponds and lakes, blocks light and uses up oxygen; it is called the "Terror of Bengal". Lantana is a land weed, not an aquatic one.
Source: PYQ-style
