🃏 Flashcard Deck: Environment & Ecology (Foundation)

Card 1
Front: One night in Bhopal changed India's environment law. What leaked, when, and which Act followed?
Back: Methyl isocyanate (MIC), on the night of 2–3 December 1984; the Environment (Protection) Act, 1986 followed.

Card 2
Front: Two scientists, two words. Who gave us "ecology" and who gave us "ecosystem"?
Back: Ernst Haeckel — ecology; A. G. Tansley (1935) — ecosystem.

Card 3
Front: If grass holds 10,000 J of energy, how much reaches the frog in Grass → Grasshopper → Frog?
Back: About 100 J (10,000 → 1,000 → 100), by the 10% law.

Card 4
Front: Which ecological pyramid can never stand on its head?
Back: The pyramid of energy — it is always upright.

Card 5
Front: Examiner's trap: "The pyramid of biomass is always upright." True or false?
Back: False. In the sea it is inverted (phytoplankton weigh less at any one time than the fish feeding on them).

Card 6
Front: Why do eagles and fish-eating birds carry the most DDT, though they never touch the crop that was sprayed?
Back: Biological magnification — non-biodegradable DDT builds up at every higher trophic level.

Card 7
Front: A lake turns green, then the fish die. What happened?
Back: Eutrophication — extra nitrates and phosphates cause an algal bloom; decaying algae use up the dissolved oxygen.

Card 8
Front: Two Japanese diseases, two metals. Match them.
Back: Minamata disease — mercury; Itai-itai disease — cadmium.

Card 9
Front: Ozone is a friend up high and an enemy nearby. Where is the friendly ozone, and what does it block?
Back: In the stratosphere; it absorbs harmful ultraviolet (UV) rays.

Card 10
Front: Mnemonic check: what does "Umbrella torn, blanket thick" stand for?
Back: Ozone hole = torn umbrella (more UV gets in, Montreal Protocol); global warming = blanket too thick (more heat stays in, Kyoto/Paris).

Card 11
Front: Examiner's trap: Nitrogen or nitrous oxide — which one is a greenhouse gas?
Back: Nitrous oxide (N2O). Nitrogen gas (N2), 78% of the air, is not a greenhouse gas.

Card 12
Front: Mnemonic check: "Wild Water Forest Air Environment" — give the years.
Back: Wildlife (Protection) Act 1972, Water Act 1974, Forest (Conservation) Act 1980, Air Act 1981, Environment (Protection) Act 1986.

Card 13
Front: Which Article makes protecting nature a Fundamental Duty of every citizen, and which amendment added it?
Back: Article 51A(g), added by the 42nd Amendment (1976). (Article 48A is the Directive Principle.)

Card 14
Front: A treaty named after a city in Iran protects which kind of place?
Back: Wetlands — the Ramsar Convention (1971). World Wetlands Day is 2 February.

Card 15
Front: Zoo or national park — which is in-situ conservation?
Back: National park (protection in the natural home). Zoos, botanical gardens and seed banks are ex-situ.

Card 16
Front: India's first national park was named after a British governor. What is it called today?
Back: Hailey National Park (1936) is now Jim Corbett National Park, Uttarakhand.

Card 17
Front: Villagers hugged trees in 1973. Name the movement and two leaders.
Back: Chipko Movement (Chamoli, now Uttarakhand); Sunderlal Bahuguna and Chandi Prasad Bhatt (Gaura Devi led the women of Reni).

Card 18
Front: Which bacterium turns air into fertiliser inside pea and gram roots?
Back: Rhizobium, in the root nodules of leguminous plants — nitrogen fixation.

Card 19
Front: How many Ramsar sites did India have when Glaw Lake was added, and when was that?
Back: 101 — Glaw Lake (Arunachal Pradesh) was announced as the 101st on 3 August 2026 (PIB). Check the latest count before your exam.

Card 20
Front: 5 June, 16 September, 22 April — which green days are these?
Back: World Environment Day, World Ozone Day, Earth Day.
