26. Which department was created by Alauddin Khilji to enforce his market control regulations?
(a) Diwan-i-Khairat (b) Diwan-i-Insha (c) Diwan-i-Riyasat (d) Diwan-i-Arz
Answer: (c)
Solution: Diwan-i-Arz was the military department under Balban, a tempting mix-up, but the market control department of Alauddin Khilji was the Diwan-i-Riyasat, supervised by the Shahana-i-Mandi. So the answer is Diwan-i-Riyasat.
Source: PYQ-style

27. Which ruler introduced the court practices of Sijda (prostration) and Paibos (kissing the feet)?
(a) Balban (b) Iltutmish (c) Alauddin Khilji (d) Muhammad bin Tughlaq
Answer: (a)
Solution: Alauddin Khilji is remembered for market reforms, not court etiquette. Balban enforced the theory of kingship and made nobles perform Sijda and Paibos before the Sultan. So the answer is Balban.
Source: PYQ-style

28. Who among the following was NOT a Navratna in Akbar's court?
(a) Todar Mal (b) Birbal (c) Malik Kafur (d) Raja Man Singh
Answer: (c)
Solution: Raja Man Singh, Todar Mal and Birbal were all jewels of Akbar's court. Malik Kafur was a general of Alauddin Khilji who led the Deccan campaigns, so the answer is Malik Kafur.
Source: PYQ-style

29. The Zabti system of land revenue during Akbar's reign was introduced by:
(a) Birbal (b) Abul Fazl (c) Raja Man Singh (d) Todar Mal
Answer: (d)
Solution: Abul Fazl wrote the Akbarnama but did not design revenue policy. Raja Todar Mal, Akbar's finance minister, introduced the Zabti (Dahsala) system based on measurement of land and average produce. So the answer is Todar Mal.
Source: NCERT Class 7 History (Our Pasts II)

30. Which Mughal emperor is known for the Chain of Justice (Zanjir-e-Adl)?
(a) Akbar (b) Jahangir (c) Aurangzeb (d) Shah Jahan
Answer: (b)
Solution: Akbar is associated with Din-i-Ilahi, which distracts many candidates. It was Jahangir who hung a golden chain with bells outside his palace so that any subject could pull it to seek royal justice. So the answer is Jahangir.
Source: PYQ-style

31. The English ambassador Sir Thomas Roe visited the court of which Mughal emperor?
(a) Akbar (b) Shah Jahan (c) Jahangir (d) Aurangzeb
Answer: (c)
Solution: William Hawkins and Captain Hawkins also came in Jahangir's time, which causes confusion with Akbar's era. Sir Thomas Roe arrived in 1615 and obtained trading concessions from Jahangir. So the answer is Jahangir.
Source: PYQ-style

32. Who built the Buland Darwaza at Fatehpur Sikri?
(a) Babur (b) Jahangir (c) Shah Jahan (d) Akbar
Answer: (d)
Solution: Shah Jahan built the Red Fort and Taj Mahal, which tempts many, but the Buland Darwaza was built by Akbar in 1601 to commemorate the conquest of Gujarat. So the answer is Akbar.
Source: NCERT Class 7 History (Our Pasts II)

33. The famous Peacock Throne (Takht-i-Taus) was made during the reign of:
(a) Akbar (b) Shah Jahan (c) Jahangir (d) Aurangzeb
Answer: (b)
Solution: Aurangzeb's austere reign rules him out, though he sat on the throne. The jewel-studded Peacock Throne was commissioned by Shah Jahan and later carried away by Nadir Shah in 1739. So the answer is Shah Jahan.
Source: PYQ-style

34. Which ruler reimposed Jizya after it had been abolished by Akbar in 1564?
(a) Aurangzeb (b) Shah Jahan (c) Jahangir (d) Bahadur Shah I
Answer: (a)
Solution: Shah Jahan is sometimes wrongly picked because of his orthodox leanings, but it was Aurangzeb who reimposed Jizya in 1679. So the answer is Aurangzeb.
Source: NCERT Class 7 History (Our Pasts II)

35. The Ashtapradhan, a council of eight ministers, was introduced by:
(a) Akbar (b) Sher Shah Suri (c) Shivaji (d) Krishnadeva Raya
Answer: (c)
Solution: Krishnadeva Raya had able ministers but not an Ashtapradhan council. Shivaji's Ashtapradhan had eight ministers, each heading a department such as the Peshwa and the Amatya. So the answer is Shivaji.
Source: NCERT Class 7 History (Our Pasts II)

36. Which Maratha tax amounted to one-fourth of the land revenue collected from non-Maratha territories?
(a) Sardeshmukhi (b) Zabti (c) Chauth (d) Kist
Answer: (c)
Solution: Sardeshmukhi is the classic trap — it was an additional 10% levy, not one-fourth. Chauth was 25% of the revenue, collected as a protection tax. So the answer is Chauth.
Source: NCERT Class 7 History (Our Pasts II)

37. The Treaty of Purandar (1665) was signed between Shivaji and:
(a) Aurangzeb (b) Shaista Khan (c) Jai Singh (d) Afzal Khan
Answer: (c)
Solution: Afzal Khan and Shaista Khan were Shivaji's adversaries in other famous episodes, which causes confusion. The Treaty of Purandar was signed with Raja Jai Singh of Amber, Aurangzeb's commander. So the answer is Jai Singh.
Source: PYQ-style

38. Harihara and Bukka founded the Vijayanagar empire under the guidance of which saint?
(a) Vidyaranya (b) Ramanujacharya (c) Shankaracharya (d) Basavanna
Answer: (a)
Solution: Ramanujacharya is associated with the Bhakti movement, a common distractor. The sage Vidyaranya of the Sringeri monastery guided the brothers in founding Vijayanagar in 1336. So the answer is Vidyaranya.
Source: NCERT Class 7 History (Our Pasts II)

39. The Battle of Talikota (1565) resulted in the defeat of which power?
(a) Bahmani kingdom (b) Vijayanagar empire (c) Maratha kingdom (d) Mughal empire
Answer: (b)
Solution: The Bahmani kingdom had already fragmented, which misleads some candidates. At Talikota the combined Deccan Sultanates decisively defeated the Vijayanagar empire. So the answer is the Vijayanagar empire.
Source: NCERT Class 7 History (Our Pasts II)

40. Which Vijayanagar ruler, author of Amuktamalyada, is regarded as the greatest of the dynasty?
(a) Devaraya II (b) Harihara I (c) Bukka I (d) Krishnadeva Raya
Answer: (d)
Solution: Devaraya II was a capable ruler but not the author of Amuktamalyada. Krishnadeva Raya (1509–29) wrote that Telugu work and built the Vitthala and Hazara Rama temples. So the answer is Krishnadeva Raya.
Source: NCERT Class 7 History (Our Pasts II)

41. The Grand Trunk Road (Sadak-e-Azam) from Bengal to the Indus was rebuilt by:
(a) Ashoka (b) Akbar (c) Shah Jahan (d) Sher Shah Suri
Answer: (d)
Solution: Ashoka built roads in ancient times, which tempts some, but the medieval GT Road from Sonargaon to Attock, with sarais and shade trees, was the work of Sher Shah Suri. So the answer is Sher Shah Suri.
Source: NCERT Class 7 History (Our Pasts II)

42. The terms Patta and Kabuliyat, introduced by Sher Shah, relate to:
(a) Land revenue records (b) Military recruitment (c) Trade contracts (d) Religious endowments
Answer: (a)
Solution: Trade contracts is a plausible guess but wrong. Patta was the state's record of the peasant's land and revenue demand, while Kabuliyat was the peasant's deed of acceptance. So the answer is land revenue records.
Source: PYQ-style

43. Babur's autobiography, the Tuzuk-i-Baburi, was originally written in which language?
(a) Turkish (b) Arabic (c) Persian (d) Urdu
Answer: (a)
Solution: Persian is the trap because the famous translation is called the Baburnama in Persian. Babur actually wrote his memoirs in Chagatai Turkish. So the answer is Turkish.
Source: NCERT Class 7 History (Our Pasts II)

44. The Ain-i-Akbari, which details Akbar's administration, was written by:
(a) Faizi (b) Abul Fazl (c) Badayuni (d) Badauni's contemporary Nizamuddin
Answer: (b)
Solution: Badayuni wrote a critical account of Akbar, which confuses many. The Ain-i-Akbari, the third part of the Akbarnama, was written by Abul Fazl. So the answer is Abul Fazl.
Source: NCERT Class 7 History (Our Pasts II)

45. Which Sikh Guru compiled the Adi Granth and built the Harmandir Sahib at Amritsar?
(a) Guru Arjan Dev (b) Guru Angad (c) Guru Nanak (d) Guru Tegh Bahadur
Answer: (a)
Solution: Guru Angad devised the Gurmukhi script, a tempting option, but the fifth Guru, Guru Arjan Dev, compiled the Adi Granth in 1604 and built the Harmandir Sahib. So the answer is Guru Arjan Dev.
Source: PYQ-style

46. In the Mughal Mansabdari system, the term Mansab denoted:
(a) A piece of land granted to a noble (b) A silver coin (c) A religious decree (d) A rank or position of an officer
Answer: (d)
Solution: A land grant (jagir) is the common confusion, but jagir was the payment, not the rank. Mansab meant a rank, with Zat fixing personal status and Sawar fixing the cavalry contingent. So the answer is a rank or position of an officer.
Source: NCERT Class 7 History (Our Pasts II)

47. Which of the following monuments is NOT correctly matched with its builder?
(a) Alai Darwaza – Alauddin Khilji (b) Red Fort (Delhi) – Shah Jahan (c) Moti Masjid (Agra) – Shah Jahan (d) Shalimar Bagh (Srinagar) – Shah Jahan
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is that Shah Jahan did build Shalimar Gardens at Lahore, so the pairing looks right. But the Shalimar Bagh in Srinagar was laid out by Jahangir for Nur Jahan. So the wrongly matched pair is Shalimar Bagh (Srinagar) – Shah Jahan.
Source: PYQ-style

48. Assertion: Muhammad bin Tughlaq transferred his capital from Delhi to Daulatabad. Reason: Daulatabad's central location helped in better administration of the Deccan and the whole empire.
(a) Both are true, but the reason does not explain the assertion (b) Both the assertion and the reason are true, and the reason correctly explains the assertion (c) The assertion is true, the reason is false (d) The assertion is false, the reason is true
Answer: (b)
Solution: Some think the transfer was only a whim, making the reason seem false, but contemporary accounts cite Daulatabad's central position for controlling the Deccan. Both statements are true and the reason explains the move. So the answer is that both are true and the reason is the correct explanation.
Source: NCERT Class 7 History (Our Pasts II)

49. Which battle was fought between the Marathas and Ahmad Shah Abdali in 1761?
(a) First Battle of Panipat (b) Third Battle of Panipat (c) Second Battle of Panipat (d) Battle of Khanwa
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is chronological confusion: the First Panipat (1526) was Babur versus Ibrahim Lodi and the Second (1556) was Akbar versus Hemu. The Third Battle of Panipat (1761) pitted Sadashivrao Bhau's Marathas against Ahmad Shah Abdali. So the answer is the Third Battle of Panipat.
Source: NCERT Class 8 History (Our Pasts III)

50. Muhammad bin Tughlaq's token currency was issued in which metal?
(a) Copper and bronze (b) Gold (c) Leather (d) Silver
Answer: (a)
Solution: Leather is a popular myth and the classic trap; no leather tokens were issued. The token coins were of copper and bronze, meant to circulate at the value of silver tankas, but the scheme failed due to forgery. So the answer is copper and bronze.
Source: NCERT Class 7 History (Our Pasts II)
