📊 PYQ Pattern Analysis: Medieval Indian History

Exams this chapter serves: SSC GD, SSC MTS, RRB Group D, state police / constable exams, and the general-awareness part of other state-level tests.

Note: this is a pattern analysis — what kinds of questions these exams like to ask from this chapter and how they trap you. We do not give year-wise counts or percentages, because no reliable data for them exists. The model questions below are written in the exam style; their sources are the NCERT chapters they test.

1. What Exams Like to Ask (Sub-topics, in plain words)

Delhi Sultanate founders and chronology — the order of the five dynasties: Slave → Khilji → Tughlaq → Sayyid → Lodi. Key founders and prominent rulers (Qutbuddin Aibak, Iltutmish, Razia Sultan, Balban, Alauddin Khilji, Muhammad bin Tughlaq, Firoz Shah Tughlaq, Sikandar Lodi).

Administrative terms and systems — Iqta system (Iltutmish), Dagh and Chehra (Alauddin Khilji), market control departments (Shahana-i-Mandi, Diwan-i-Riyasat), charity department (Diwan-i-Khairat), agriculture department (Diwan-i-Kohi).

Mughal battles and chronologies — Panipat I (1526), Khanwa (1527), Chanderi (1528), Ghagra (1529), Chausa (1539), Kannauj/Bilgram (1540), Panipat II (1556), Talikota (1565), Haldighati (1576).

Mughal administrative and religious policies — Mansabdari system (Zat and Sawar ranks), Zabti revenue system (Todar Mal's Bandobast), Din-i-Ilahi, abolition and re-imposition of Jaziya (1564 by Akbar, 1679 by Aurangzeb).

Monuments and builders — Qutb Minar, Alai Darwaza, Siri Fort, Tughlaqabad, Agra Fort, Fatehpur Sikri (Buland Darwaza), Taj Mahal, Red Fort, Jama Masjid, Gol Gumbaz.

Vijayanagar and Marathas — Harihara and Bukka (1336), Krishnadeva Raya (Tuluva dynasty, Amuktamalyada), Ashtadiggajas, Ashtapradhan of Chhatrapati Shivaji, Chauth and Sardeshmukhi taxes.

Literary works and foreign travelers — Al-Biruni (Kitab-ul-Hind), Amir Khusrau (Tuti-e-Hind), Ibn Battuta (Rihla), Baburnama/Tuzuk-i-Baburi, Ain-i-Akbari / Akbarnama (Abul Fazl).

2. Exam-wise Style (qualitative)

SSC GD / SSC MTS — short, direct one-liners: ruler–title, monument–builder, battle–year, term–meaning. Speed and accuracy matter.

RRB Group D — similar direct questions, frequently using negative phrasing ("Which of the following is NOT correctly matched?") and battle/monument pairs.

State police / constable exams — direct facts with extra focus on regional battles, forts, and local medieval history.

Higher exams that use these same basics (CHSL, NTPC, CGL) add statement-based, pair-matching and "arrange in chronological order" questions.

3. Common Traps (in words)

Almost true statement — one attribution changed: "Qutb Minar was completed by Qutbuddin Aibak" (it was started by Aibak, completed by Iltutmish).

Chronological confusion — placing Tughlaq before Khilji, or Khanwa before Panipat.

Similar names — Sikandar Lodi (founded Agra in 1504) confused with Akbar (built Agra Fort); or Amir Khusrau confused with Abul Fazl.

Negative phrasing — "Which of the following was NOT introduced by Alauddin Khilji?" missing the 'NOT'.

False reasoning — Assertion–Reason questions where both statements are historically true facts, but the reason does not explain the assertion.

4. Top 10 High-Yield Model Questions with Solutions and Trap Analysis

Question 1
Who among the following completed the construction of the Qutb Minar?
(a) Qutbuddin Aibak (b) Iltutmish (c) Balban (d) Alauddin Khilji
Answer: (b) Iltutmish
Trap Type: Similar Name / Chronology — Aibak started the construction, but Iltutmish added three storeys and completed it.
15/45-sec Rule: 15-second fact. Score maximizer.
Source: NCERT Class 7 History (Our Pasts-II), Ch 3 The Delhi Sultans

Question 2
The Iqta system was introduced in the Delhi Sultanate by which ruler?
(a) Balban (b) Alauddin Khilji (c) Iltutmish (d) Muhammad bin Tughlaq
Answer: (c) Iltutmish
Trap Type: Almost True — Alauddin reformed revenue, but Iqta was established by Iltutmish.
15/45-sec Rule: 15-second fact.
Source: NCERT Class 7 History (Our Pasts-II), Ch 3 The Delhi Sultans

Question 3
The market control policy (price regulation) and the Dagh and Chehra systems were initiated by:
(a) Iltutmish (b) Alauddin Khilji (c) Ghiyasuddin Tughlaq (d) Firoz Shah Tughlaq
Answer: (b) Alauddin Khilji
Trap Type: Similar Name — Alauddin Khilji is celebrated for strict market controls under Shahana-i-Mandi.
15/45-sec Rule: 15-second fact.
Source: NCERT Class 7 History (Our Pasts-II), Ch 3 The Delhi Sultans

Question 4
In which year was the First Battle of Panipat fought?
(a) 1526 (b) 1556 (c) 1565 (d) 1527
Answer: (a) 1526
Trap Type: Chronological Confusion — 1556 is Panipat II, 1565 is Talikota, 1527 is Khanwa.
15/45-sec Rule: 15-second fact.
Source: NCERT Class 7 History (Our Pasts-II), Ch 4 The Mughal Empire

Question 5
Buland Darwaza at Fatehpur Sikri was constructed by Akbar to commemorate his victory over:
(a) Malwa (b) Mewar (c) Gujarat (d) Bengal
Answer: (c) Gujarat
Trap Type: Almost True — students often guess Mewar or Chittor; Akbar built it after the conquest of Gujarat (1572–73).
15/45-sec Rule: 15-second fact.
Source: NCERT Class 7 History (Our Pasts-II), Ch 4 The Mughal Empire

Question 6
The silver coin called 'Rupiya' was first introduced in medieval India by:
(a) Akbar (b) Sher Shah Suri (c) Iltutmish (d) Alauddin Khilji
Answer: (b) Sher Shah Suri
Trap Type: Chronological Confusion — Iltutmish introduced the silver Tanka; Sher Shah introduced the standard silver Rupiya.
15/45-sec Rule: 15-second fact.
Source: NCERT Class 7 History (Our Pasts-II), Ch 4 The Mughal Empire

Question 7
Ibn Battuta, the famous traveler who visited India during the reign of Muhammad bin Tughlaq, hailed from:
(a) Morocco (b) Persia (c) Turkey (d) Italy
Answer: (a) Morocco
Trap Type: Similar Name — Marco Polo came from Italy; Al-Biruni from Khwarizm/Central Asia; Ibn Battuta came from Tangier, Morocco.
15/45-sec Rule: 15-second fact.
Source: NCERT Class 12 History (Themes in Indian History Part II), Ch 5 Through the Eyes of Travellers

Question 8
The famous medieval poet Amir Khusrau was popularly known as:
(a) Tuti-e-Hind (Parrot of India) (b) Zinda Pir (c) Lakh Baksh (d) Alamgir
Answer: (a) Tuti-e-Hind (Parrot of India)
Trap Type: Similar Titles — Zinda Pir was Aurangzeb; Lakh Baksh was Aibak.
15/45-sec Rule: 15-second fact.
Source: NCERT Class 7 History (Our Pasts-II), Ch 3 The Delhi Sultans

Question 9
The Vijayanagar Empire was founded in 1336 along the banks of the river:
(a) Godavari (b) Krishna (c) Tungabhadra (d) Kaveri
Answer: (c) Tungabhadra
Trap Type: Geography Confusion — Tungabhadra is the major tributary of Krishna along which Hampi/Vijayanagar flourished.
15/45-sec Rule: 15-second fact.
Source: NCERT Class 12 History (Themes in Indian History Part II), Ch 7 An Imperial Capital: Vijayanagara

Question 10
In Shivaji's council of ministers known as 'Ashtapradhan', the Prime Minister was termed:
(a) Amatya (b) Peshwa (c) Senapati (d) Sumant
Answer: (b) Peshwa
Trap Type: Administrative Terms — Amatya was finance/accounts; Sumant was foreign minister; Peshwa was the chief minister/prime minister.
15/45-sec Rule: 15-second fact.
Source: NCERT Class 7 History (Our Pasts-II), Ch 10 Eighteenth-Century Political Formations
