1. Delhi Sultanate Dynasties – "Gul Khil Tug Say Lo"
Imagine a flower girl (Gul) cooking khil (rice pops) on a tug (a rope), saying "Lo" (take it). She's handing you the five dynasties in order: Gulam, Khilji, Tughlaq, Sayyid, Lodi.

2. Mughal Emperors – "BHAJ SA" (like "bhajiya" but missing the 'ya')
You're frying bhajiya but the 'ya' fell away. The sizzling pan shouts BHAJ SA: Babur, Humayun, Akbar, Jahangir, Shah Jahan, Aurangzeb. Smell the power!

3. Three Panipat Battles – The "BA-HA-MA" Sound
Battle 1: Babur (1526) – "Ba"
Hamu vs Akbar (1556) – "Ha-A"
Marathas vs Abdali (1761) – "Ma-A"
Now sing: "BA-HA-MA, Panipat drama!" BA (Babur), HA (Hem-Akbar), MA (Maratha-Abdali).

4. Alauddin's Reforms – "DAM Cheap"
Dagh (horse branding)
Alai Darwaza
Market control (Shahana-i-Mandi)
"Cheap" reminds you he fixed prices. His whole rule was "DAM Cheap"!

5. Muhammad bin Tughlaq's Experiments – "ToCaDa"
Token currency
Capital shift
Da (Doab) tax hike
"ToCaDa" – a crazy tune only he could dance to. He tried all three and tripped each time.

6. Sher Shah Suri's Gifts – "GRR" (like a tiger's growl)
Grand Trunk Road
Rupiya
Records (Patta-Kabuliyat)
Sher Shah roars "GRR" – his three lasting contributions that still echo.

7. Akbar's Religious Steps – "JIB" (like the sail of a boat)
Jizya abolished (1564)
Ibadat Khana (1575)
B (Din-i-Ilahi, 1582 – "B" for Birbal, its first member)
The JIB sail pushed Akbar's ship of secularism.

8. Shivaji's Ministers – "A Sta Pradhan" (Imagine a 'star Pradhan')
Ashtapradhan = 8 ministers. Picture a glowing star Pradhan (prime minister) with 8 rays, each a minister. The star reminds you "Ashta" (eight) – a council of 8.

9. Vijayanagar Founders – "HaBi" (Hari-Bukka)
A smiling "HaBi" (Hindi slang for 'slow') cat named Hari and his brother Bukka. They're slow but built a huge empire with saint Vidyaranya's blessings.

10. Battle of Talikota (1565) – "Taali Do, Kota Khatam"
If you clap ("Taali do"), the Kota (fort) is finished. Exactly – Talikota ended Vijayanagar's fort-city. The four Sultanates clapped together.

11. Sikh Gurus' End – "Arjan to Gobind" (Think: Arjun and Gobind)
Arjan Dev compiled Adi Granth and was executed by Jahangir. Gobind Singh created Khalsa. Link them: "Arjan laid the script, Gobind picked the sword." Arjan for scripture, Gobind for martial.

12. Razia Sultan – "Ra-Zia, Rise Up"
Razia – she rose up to be the only woman sultan. She rode elephants, wore male attire. Remember "Razia Rise" to never forget her uniqueness.

13. Firoz Shah Tughlaq – "Firoz Separated Jizya"
Say "Firoz Separated Jizya" like a tongue twister. The 'F' and 'S' alliteration locks it: Firoz separated jizya from land tax.

14. Ibn Battuta – "Battuta in Tughlaq's Battu"
Ibn Battuta visited Muhammad bin Tughlaq. Imagine him inside a "battu" (a lumpy shape) shaped like Tughlaq. Battuta → Battu → Tughlaq.

15. Krishnadeva Raya – "Krishna, the Ray of Light"
He was the brightest ruler of Vijayanagar. His name means "Krishna, the ray (Raya)." Imagine a shining ray behind Krishna, illuminating Hampi.

Skip Strategy Tips

Founder Mix-Up Trap: If a question asks for the founder of a dynasty and you can't recall whether it's the famous ruler (like Alauddin) or the actual founder (Jalaluddin), skip immediately. You'll waste time debating.

Reverse Chronology Questions: If asked "Which event came last?" or "Arrange in reverse order" and you only remember forward sequence, skip. Mental reversal under pressure causes errors; mark for review if time permits.

Obscure Regional Ruler Names: If a question presents four little-known regional sultans or temples not covered in your mnemonics, don't guess. The exam intentionally includes one odd name you won't remember; skip and move to the next sure-shot fact.
