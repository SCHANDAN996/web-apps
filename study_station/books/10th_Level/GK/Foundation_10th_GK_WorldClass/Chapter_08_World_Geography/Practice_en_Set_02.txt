Set 2/6 – World Geography (Basic) MCQs (Foundation)
Questions 26–50: Medium

26. The Earth's axis makes an angle of how many degrees with its orbital plane?
(a) 23½° (b) 66½° (c) 90° (d) 45°
Answer: (b)
Solution: The axis is tilted and makes 66½° with the orbital plane. 23½° is its tilt from the vertical, a common trap; the answer is 66½°.
Source: NCERT Class 6 Geography (The Earth: Our Habitat)

27. The Earth is nearest to the Sun (perihelion) around:
(a) 4 July (b) 21 June (c) 3 January (d) 22 December
Answer: (c)
Solution: Perihelion falls around 3 January and aphelion around 4 July. So the Earth is nearest to the Sun during the Northern winter.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Geography (Fundamentals of Physical Geography)

28. On which two dates are day and night equal all over the world (equinoxes)?
(a) 21 June and 22 December (b) 21 March and 23 September (c) 3 January and 4 July (d) 21 March and 21 June
Answer: (b)
Solution: On 21 March and 23 September the Sun is overhead on the Equator, so day and night are equal everywhere. 21 June and 22 December are solstices.
Source: NCERT Class 6 Geography (The Earth: Our Habitat)

29. A difference of 1° of longitude makes a difference in local time of:
(a) 15 minutes (b) 1 minute (c) 60 minutes (d) 4 minutes
Answer: (d)
Solution: The Earth turns 360° in 24 hours (1,440 minutes), so 1,440 ÷ 360 gives a time difference of 4 minutes per degree.
Source: NCERT Class 6 Geography (The Earth: Our Habitat)

30. How many meridians of longitude are there in all?
(a) 180 (b) 181 (c) 360 (d) 361
Answer: (c)
Solution: 181 is the number of parallels of latitude, which is the trap. Meridians are 180 east plus 180 west, so the total is 360.
Source: NCERT Class 6 Geography (The Earth: Our Habitat)

31. The Arctic Circle lies at:
(a) 90°N (b) 23½°N (c) 66½°S (d) 66½°N
Answer: (d)
Solution: The Arctic Circle is at 66½°N. 66½°S is the Antarctic Circle, 23½°N is the Tropic of Cancer and 90°N is the North Pole.
Source: NCERT Class 6 Geography (The Earth: Our Habitat)

32. Which layer of the Earth is called NIFE?
(a) Crust (b) Mantle (c) Core (d) Hydrosphere
Answer: (c)
Solution: The core is made mainly of nickel (Ni) and iron (Fe), hence NIFE. The crust is SIAL/SIMA.
Source: NCERT Class 7 Geography (Our Environment)

33. The continental crust (SIAL) is made mainly of silica and:
(a) magnesium (b) alumina (c) iron (d) nickel
Answer: (b)
Solution: SIAL = silica + alumina (continental crust). SIMA = silica + magnesium (oceanic crust), which is the tempting wrong option.
Source: NCERT Class 7 Geography (Our Environment)

34. Marble is the metamorphosed form of which rock?
(a) Sandstone (b) Shale (c) Limestone (d) Granite
Answer: (c)
Solution: Limestone changes into marble under heat and pressure. Sandstone becomes quartzite, shale becomes slate and granite becomes gneiss.
Source: NCERT Class 7 Geography (Our Environment)

35. Which of the following is an example of a block mountain?
(a) Alps (b) Himalayas (c) Vosges (d) Andes
Answer: (c)
Solution: The Vosges (France) and the Black Forest (Germany) are block mountains with the Rhine rift valley between them. The others are fold mountains.
Source: NCERT Class 7 Geography (Our Environment)

36. Which of the following is an igneous rock?
(a) Marble (b) Slate (c) Sandstone (d) Granite
Answer: (d)
Solution: Granite forms when magma cools slowly inside the Earth, so it is igneous. Marble and slate are metamorphic; sandstone is sedimentary.
Source: NCERT Class 7 Geography (Our Environment)

37. The ozone layer, which protects us from ultraviolet rays, lies in the:
(a) Stratosphere (b) Troposphere (c) Mesosphere (d) Thermosphere
Answer: (a)
Solution: The ozone layer is in the stratosphere. The troposphere below it is where weather happens, which is the usual confusion.
Source: NCERT Class 7 Geography (Our Environment)

38. Meteorites entering from space burn up in which layer of the atmosphere?
(a) Troposphere (b) Stratosphere (c) Exosphere (d) Mesosphere
Answer: (d)
Solution: Meteorites burn up in the mesosphere, the third layer, which extends up to about 80 km.
Source: NCERT Class 7 Geography (Our Environment)

39. The ionosphere, which reflects radio waves back to the Earth, is part of the:
(a) Thermosphere (b) Troposphere (c) Stratosphere (d) Mesosphere
Answer: (a)
Solution: The ionosphere lies in the thermosphere and helps radio transmission by reflecting radio waves back to the Earth.
Source: NCERT Class 7 Geography (Our Environment)

40. The warm local wind called "Chinook" (snow eater) blows in the:
(a) Alps (b) Rockies (c) Sahara (d) Andes
Answer: (b)
Solution: Chinook is a warm, dry wind on the eastern slopes of the Rockies in North America. The similar warm wind of the Alps is the Foehn.
Source: PYQ-style

41. The dry wind "Harmattan", called "the doctor", blows in:
(a) West Africa (b) Australia (c) Japan (d) Argentina
Answer: (a)
Solution: The Harmattan is a dry wind of West Africa. It is called the doctor because it gives relief from humid heat.
Source: PYQ-style

42. The temperate grasslands known as "Pampas" are found mainly in:
(a) Brazil (b) Argentina (c) Australia (d) South Africa
Answer: (b)
Solution: The Pampas are in Argentina. Brazil has the tropical Campos, Australia the Downs and South Africa the Velds — similar-name trap.
Source: NCERT Class 7 Geography (Our Environment)

43. The "Downs" are temperate grasslands of:
(a) New Zealand (b) South Africa (c) Eurasia (d) Australia
Answer: (d)
Solution: The Downs are in Australia. New Zealand has the Canterbury grasslands, South Africa the Velds and Eurasia the Steppes.
Source: NCERT Class 7 Geography (Our Environment)

44. The Gulf Stream is a:
(a) cold current of the Pacific Ocean (b) warm current of the North Atlantic Ocean (c) cold current of the Indian Ocean (d) warm current of the South Pacific Ocean
Answer: (b)
Solution: The Gulf Stream is a warm current flowing in the North Atlantic. It meets the cold Labrador Current near Newfoundland.
Source: NCERT Class 7 Geography (Our Environment)

45. Which river carries the largest volume of water in the world?
(a) Nile (b) Congo (c) Amazon (d) Mississippi
Answer: (c)
Solution: The Amazon is the largest river by volume. The Nile is the longest river, but not the largest by volume — a similar-records trap.
Source: NCERT Class 6 Geography (The Earth: Our Habitat)

46. Which is the deepest lake in the world?
(a) Caspian Sea (b) Lake Superior (c) Lake Victoria (d) Lake Baikal
Answer: (d)
Solution: Lake Baikal in Russia is the deepest lake. The Caspian Sea is the largest lake and Lake Superior is the largest freshwater lake by area.
Source: PYQ-style

47. Which strait separates India and Sri Lanka?
(a) Palk Strait (b) Strait of Malacca (c) Bering Strait (d) Strait of Hormuz
Answer: (a)
Solution: The Palk Strait lies between Tamil Nadu (India) and Sri Lanka. The Strait of Malacca lies between Malaysia and Sumatra.
Source: PYQ-style

48. The Panama Canal connects the:
(a) Atlantic Ocean and Pacific Ocean (b) Mediterranean Sea and Red Sea (c) Arabian Sea and Red Sea (d) Pacific Ocean and Indian Ocean
Answer: (a)
Solution: The Panama Canal (opened 1914) joins the Atlantic and the Pacific. The Mediterranean–Red Sea link is the Suez Canal.
Source: PYQ-style

49. The Durand Line is the boundary between:
(a) India and China (b) India and Pakistan (c) Pakistan and Afghanistan (d) North Korea and South Korea
Answer: (c)
Solution: The Durand Line separates Pakistan and Afghanistan. India–China is the McMahon Line and India–Pakistan is the Radcliffe Line.
Source: PYQ-style

50. Which of the following statements is correct?
(a) Mercury is the hottest planet (b) Venus is the largest planet (c) Jupiter is the largest planet (d) Mars is called Earth's twin
Answer: (c)
Solution: Jupiter is the largest planet. Venus (not Mercury) is the hottest, and Venus (not Mars) is called Earth's twin.
Source: NCERT Class 6 Geography (The Earth: Our Habitat)
