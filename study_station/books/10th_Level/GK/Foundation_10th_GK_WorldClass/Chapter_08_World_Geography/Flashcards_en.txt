Flashcard Deck: World Geography (Basic) — Foundation

Card 1
Front: Mercury is closest to the Sun, yet it is not the hottest planet. Which planet wins that title, and why?
Back: Venus. Its thick carbon dioxide atmosphere traps heat like a closed car in the sun.

Card 2
Front: Until 2006, school books listed nine planets. What changed?
Back: In 2006 the International Astronomical Union reclassified Pluto as a dwarf planet. The solar system now has 8 planets.

Card 3
Front: The Earth is nearest to the Sun in early January. So why is India cold then?
Back: Seasons depend on the tilt of the Earth's axis, not on distance. In December–January the Northern Hemisphere leans away from the Sun, so its rays fall slanting.

Card 4
Front: On 21 June the Sun stands overhead on one special line. Which one?
Back: The Tropic of Cancer (23½°N). It is the longest day in the Northern Hemisphere (summer solstice).

Card 5
Front: A place is 30° east of you. Is its clock ahead or behind yours, and by how much?
Back: Ahead by 2 hours. 1° = 4 minutes, so 30° × 4 = 120 minutes. East is ahead.

Card 6
Front: Indian Standard Time is set by a line passing near a town in Uttar Pradesh. Which meridian and which town?
Back: 82°30'E, passing near Mirzapur. IST is 5 hours 30 minutes ahead of GMT.

Card 7
Front: Magellan's crew came home in 1522 and found they had "lost" a day. Which imaginary line explains this today?
Back: The International Date Line (roughly 180°). Going west across it, the date moves one day forward.

Card 8
Front: What is NIFE, and where will you find it?
Back: The Earth's core, made mainly of nickel (Ni) and iron (Fe).

Card 9
Front: Marble starts life as which rock?
Back: Limestone. Heat and pressure change it into marble (a metamorphic rock).

Card 10
Front: Which continent is crossed by the Equator, the Tropic of Cancer and the Tropic of Capricorn?
Back: Africa — the only continent through which all three pass.

Card 11
Front: The deepest known point on Earth is hidden in which ocean?
Back: The Pacific Ocean — the Mariana Trench, about 11 km deep.

Card 12
Front: Only one ocean is named after a country. Which one?
Back: The Indian Ocean, named after India.

Card 13
Front: Aeroplanes like a calm layer above the weather, which also holds the ozone. Name it.
Back: The stratosphere (up to about 50 km).

Card 14
Front: A warm wind in the Rockies melts snow so fast that people call it the "snow eater". Its name?
Back: Chinook.

Card 15
Front: Mnemonic check: what does "PaPa SteVe Down in Canterbury" help you remember?
Back: Temperate grasslands — Prairies (North America), Pampas (Argentina), Steppes (Eurasia), Velds (South Africa), Downs (Australia), Canterbury (New Zealand).

Card 16
Front: Mnemonic check: "The Strong Man Throws Eggs" stands for what?
Back: The layers of the atmosphere from the ground up — Troposphere, Stratosphere, Mesosphere, Thermosphere, Exosphere.

Card 17
Front: Which canal joins the Mediterranean Sea and the Red Sea, and when did it open?
Back: The Suez Canal (Egypt), opened in 1869. The Panama Canal (Atlantic–Pacific) opened in 1914.

Card 18
Front: Examiner's trap: "The largest river in the world is the Nile." True or false?
Back: False as written. The Nile is the longest river; the Amazon is the largest by volume of water. Trap type: Similar Names / Similar Records.

Card 19
Front: Examiner's trap: "A ship going from Japan to the USA across the Date Line moves its calendar one day forward." True or false?
Back: False. Going east (Asia → America) the date goes back one day; going west it goes forward. Trap type: Reversed Direction.

Card 20
Front: Which line separates Pakistan and Afghanistan — Durand or McMahon?
Back: Durand Line. The McMahon Line is between India and China.
