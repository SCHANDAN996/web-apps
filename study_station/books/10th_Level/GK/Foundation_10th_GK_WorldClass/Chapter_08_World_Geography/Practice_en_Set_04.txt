Set 4/6 – World Geography (Basic) MCQs (Foundation)
Questions 76–100: Medium

76. When it is 12 noon at Greenwich (0°), what is the local time at a place on 30°E longitude?
(a) 10 a.m. (b) 2 p.m. (c) 1 p.m. (d) 3 p.m.
Answer: (b)
Solution: 30 × 4 = 120 minutes = 2 hours. The place is east of Greenwich, so it is ahead: 12 noon + 2 hours = 2 p.m.
Source: NCERT Class 6 Geography (The Earth: Our Habitat)

77. Indian Standard Time is ahead of Greenwich Mean Time by:
(a) 4 hours 30 minutes (b) 5 hours 30 minutes (c) 6 hours 30 minutes (d) 5 hours
Answer: (b)
Solution: IST meridian is 82°30'E. 82.5 × 4 = 330 minutes, which equals 5 hours 30 minutes ahead of GMT.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Geography (Contemporary India I)

78. Counting the Equator, how many parallels of latitude are there in all?
(a) 90 (b) 180 (c) 181 (d) 360
Answer: (c)
Solution: 360 is the number of meridians. There are 90 parallels in each hemisphere plus the Equator, so the total is 181.
Source: NCERT Class 6 Geography (The Earth: Our Habitat)

79. Because the Earth takes about 365¼ days for one revolution, a leap year comes:
(a) every 2 years (b) every 3 years (c) every 4 years (d) every 5 years
Answer: (c)
Solution: The extra quarter day each year adds up to one full day (29 February) in four years, so a leap year comes every 4 years.
Source: NCERT Class 6 Geography (The Earth: Our Habitat)

80. Due to the Earth's rotation (Coriolis force), winds in the Northern Hemisphere are deflected:
(a) straight towards the north (b) straight towards the south (c) to their left (d) to their right
Answer: (d)
Solution: Winds bend to the right in the Northern Hemisphere and to the left in the Southern Hemisphere (Ferrel's law). Reversing the hemispheres is the trap.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Geography (Fundamentals of Physical Geography)

81. The trade winds blow towards the:
(a) Equator (b) poles (c) Arctic Circle (d) 60° latitudes
Answer: (a)
Solution: Trade winds blow from the subtropical high-pressure belts towards the low-pressure belt at the Equator. The westerlies blow towards the poles.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Geography (Fundamentals of Physical Geography)

82. Spring tides (the highest high tides) occur on:
(a) the first quarter of the Moon (b) full moon and new moon days (c) the last quarter of the Moon (d) equinox days only
Answer: (b)
Solution: On full moon and new moon days the Sun, Moon and Earth are in a line, so their pull adds up and gives spring tides. Quarter-moon days give neap tides.
Source: NCERT Class 7 Geography (Our Environment)

83. Which of the following is a cold ocean current?
(a) Gulf Stream (b) Kuroshio (c) Brazil Current (d) Labrador Current
Answer: (d)
Solution: The Labrador Current is cold and flows south along the coast of Canada. The Gulf Stream, Kuroshio and Brazil Current are warm.
Source: NCERT Class 7 Geography (Our Environment)

84. The Grand Banks, a famous fishing ground where the warm Gulf Stream meets the cold Labrador Current, lies near:
(a) Newfoundland (b) Madagascar (c) Sri Lanka (d) New Zealand
Answer: (a)
Solution: The Grand Banks lie near Newfoundland, off eastern Canada. Mixing of warm and cold water helps plankton grow, which attracts fish.
Source: NCERT Class 7 Geography (Our Environment)

85. The cold Humboldt (Peru) Current flows along the:
(a) east coast of South America (b) west coast of South America (c) west coast of Africa (d) east coast of Australia
Answer: (b)
Solution: The Humboldt or Peru Current flows north along the west coast of South America. The cold current off south-west Africa is the Benguela.
Source: PYQ-style

86. Tropical cyclones called "Hurricanes" occur in the:
(a) China Sea (b) Bay of Bengal (c) north-west Australia (d) Caribbean Sea and Atlantic
Answer: (d)
Solution: Hurricanes form in the Caribbean and Atlantic. In the China Sea they are typhoons and in north-west Australia willy-willies.
Source: PYQ-style

87. Tropical cyclones called "Typhoons" occur in the:
(a) West Pacific and China Sea (b) Caribbean Sea (c) Gulf of Mexico (d) Mediterranean Sea
Answer: (a)
Solution: Typhoons strike the West Pacific and China Sea region. Hurricanes are the Caribbean–Atlantic name.
Source: PYQ-style

88. "Loo" is:
(a) a cold wind of Kashmir (b) a hot, dry wind of the northern plains of India (c) the monsoon wind of Kerala (d) a sea breeze of Mumbai
Answer: (b)
Solution: Loo is a hot and dry local wind that blows over the northern plains of India in summer afternoons.
Source: NCERT Class 7 Geography (Our Environment)

89. The warm, dry local wind "Foehn" blows in the:
(a) Alps (b) Rockies (c) Andes (d) Himalayas
Answer: (a)
Solution: Foehn blows down the northern slopes of the Alps in Europe. The similar wind of the Rockies is the Chinook.
Source: PYQ-style

90. "Sirocco" is a:
(a) hot, dusty wind blowing from the Sahara towards Italy (b) cold wind of Siberia (c) warm wind of the Rockies (d) wet wind of Japan
Answer: (a)
Solution: Sirocco is a hot, dusty wind that blows from the Sahara across the Mediterranean towards Italy.
Source: PYQ-style

91. The Equator passes through which of the following countries?
(a) India (b) Egypt (c) Mexico (d) Kenya
Answer: (d)
Solution: The Equator passes through Kenya in East Africa. India and Mexico are crossed by the Tropic of Cancer, and so is Egypt.
Source: PYQ-style

92. The Tropic of Capricorn passes through which of the following countries?
(a) India (b) Egypt (c) Australia (d) Mexico
Answer: (c)
Solution: The Tropic of Capricorn passes through Australia. India, Egypt and Mexico lie on the Tropic of Cancer — a similar-name trap.
Source: PYQ-style

93. The river Nile finally drains into the:
(a) Red Sea (b) Atlantic Ocean (c) Indian Ocean (d) Mediterranean Sea
Answer: (d)
Solution: The Nile flows northward through Egypt and drains into the Mediterranean Sea.
Source: NCERT Class 6 Geography (The Earth: Our Habitat)

94. The Victoria Falls is located on which river?
(a) Nile (b) Congo (c) Zambezi (d) Limpopo
Answer: (c)
Solution: Victoria Falls is on the Zambezi, on the Zambia–Zimbabwe border. Lake Victoria (not the falls) is linked with the Nile — a similar-name trap.
Source: PYQ-style

95. Angel Falls, the highest waterfall in the world, is located in:
(a) Brazil (b) Venezuela (c) Canada (d) Zimbabwe
Answer: (b)
Solution: Angel Falls is in Venezuela. Niagara Falls is on the USA–Canada border and Victoria Falls between Zambia and Zimbabwe.
Source: PYQ-style

96. Which is the largest island in the world?
(a) Greenland (b) Australia (c) New Guinea (d) Borneo
Answer: (a)
Solution: Greenland is the largest island. Australia is larger but is treated as a continent (the island continent), which is the trap.
Source: PYQ-style

97. The Great Barrier Reef, the largest coral reef system, lies off the coast of:
(a) New Zealand (b) Indonesia (c) Australia (d) Philippines
Answer: (c)
Solution: The Great Barrier Reef lies off the coast of Queensland in north-eastern Australia.
Source: PYQ-style

98. Niagara Falls lies on the border between:
(a) USA and Mexico (b) USA and Canada (c) Brazil and Argentina (d) Zambia and Zimbabwe
Answer: (b)
Solution: Niagara Falls lies on the USA–Canada border, on the Niagara River between Lake Erie and Lake Ontario.
Source: PYQ-style

99. The Isthmus of Panama joins:
(a) Asia and Africa (b) Europe and Asia (c) Africa and Europe (d) North America and South America
Answer: (d)
Solution: An isthmus is a narrow strip of land joining two land masses. The Isthmus of Panama joins North America and South America.
Source: NCERT Class 6 Geography (The Earth: Our Habitat)

100. Consider the following statements:
Statement I: The Sahara is the largest hot desert in the world.
Statement II: If cold (polar) deserts are also counted, Antarctica is the largest desert.
Which of the statements is/are correct?
(a) Only I (b) Only II (c) Both I and II (d) Neither I nor II
Answer: (c)
Solution: Both are correct. Antarctica receives extremely little precipitation, so it counts as a polar desert and is the largest; among hot deserts the Sahara is the largest.
Source: PYQ-style
