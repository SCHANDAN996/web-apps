Questions 126–150: Hard

126. Consider the following statements:
Statement I: Perihelion occurs around 3 January.
Statement II: Aphelion occurs around 4 July.
Statement III: The Earth is nearest to the Sun during the Northern Hemisphere summer.
Which of the statements are correct?
(a) I and II only (b) II and III only (c) I and III only (d) I, II and III
Answer: (a)
Solution: Perihelion (nearest) is around 3 January and aphelion (farthest) around 4 July, so I and II are correct. The Earth is nearest during the Northern winter, so III is wrong.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Geography (Fundamentals of Physical Geography)

127. Place X is on 120°E and place Y is on 30°E. When it is 10:00 a.m. at Y, what is the local time at X?
(a) 4:00 a.m. (b) 4:00 p.m. (c) 6:00 p.m. (d) 2:00 p.m.
Answer: (b)
Solution: Difference = 120 − 30 = 90°, so 90 × 4 = 360 minutes = 6 hours. X is east of Y, so it is ahead: 10:00 a.m. + 6 hours = 4:00 p.m.
Source: NCERT Class 6 Geography (The Earth: Our Habitat)

128. The local time of a place is 6 hours behind Greenwich Mean Time. The longitude of the place is:
(a) 90°E (b) 60°W (c) 45°W (d) 90°W
Answer: (d)
Solution: 6 hours = 360 minutes; 360 ÷ 4 = 90°. Being behind GMT means the place is west of Greenwich, so it is 90°W. 90°E would be 6 hours ahead.
Source: NCERT Class 6 Geography (The Earth: Our Habitat)

129. Which of the following pairs (strait – water bodies it joins) is correctly matched?
(a) Gibraltar – Red Sea and Mediterranean Sea (b) Bering – Pacific Ocean and Arctic Ocean (c) Hormuz – Red Sea and Gulf of Aden (d) Palk – Arabian Sea and Red Sea
Answer: (b)
Solution: The Bering Strait joins the Pacific and the Arctic. Gibraltar joins the Atlantic and the Mediterranean, Hormuz joins the Persian Gulf and the Gulf of Oman, and Palk lies between India and Sri Lanka.

131. Which of the following is NOT a warm ocean current?
(a) Gulf Stream (b) Kuroshio (c) Benguela Current (d) Brazil Current
Answer: (c)
Solution: The Benguela Current off south-west Africa is cold. The Gulf Stream, Kuroshio and Brazil Current are warm currents.
Source: NCERT Class 7 Geography (Our Environment)

132. Which of the following is NOT a young fold mountain range?
(a) Alps (b) Andes (c) Rockies (d) Appalachians
Answer: (d)
Solution: The Appalachians of North America are old fold mountains, worn down over a long time. The Alps, Andes and Rockies are young fold mountains.
Source: NCERT Class 7 Geography (Our Environment)

133. Consider the following statements about the atmosphere:
Statement I: The troposphere has an average height of about 13 km.
Statement II: The ozone layer lies in the mesosphere.
Statement III: The ionosphere reflects radio waves back to the Earth.
Which of the statements are correct?
(a) I and II only (b) I and III only (c) II and III only (d) I, II and III
Answer: (b)
Solution: Statements I and III are correct. The ozone layer lies in the stratosphere, not the mesosphere, so Statement II is wrong.
Source: NCERT Class 7 Geography (Our Environment)

134. Which is the correct descending order of the following continents by area?
(a) Asia, North America, Africa, South America (b) Asia, Africa, South America, North America (c) Africa, Asia, North America, South America (d) Asia, Africa, North America, South America
Answer: (d)
Solution: By area the order is Asia, Africa, North America, South America (then Antarctica, Europe, Australia). Swapping Africa and North America is the usual trap.
Source: NCERT Class 6 Geography (The Earth: Our Habitat)

135. Which is the correct order of the layers of the atmosphere from the Earth's surface upward?
(a) Troposphere, Mesosphere, Stratosphere, Thermosphere (b) Troposphere, Stratosphere, Mesosphere, Thermosphere (c) Stratosphere, Troposphere, Mesosphere, Thermosphere (d) Troposphere, Stratosphere, Thermosphere, Mesosphere
Answer: (b)
Solution: From the ground up: Troposphere, Stratosphere, Mesosphere, Thermosphere and then Exosphere ("The Strong Man Throws Eggs").
Source: NCERT Class 7 Geography (Our Environment)

136. Which of the following is true for 21 March?
(a) The Sun is overhead on the Tropic of Cancer (b) It is the longest day in the Northern Hemisphere (c) Day and night are equal all over the world (d) The Sun is overhead on the Tropic of Capricorn
Answer: (c)
Solution: 21 March is an equinox: the Sun is overhead on the Equator and day and night are equal everywhere. The longest northern day is 21 June.
Source: NCERT Class 6 Geography (The Earth: Our Habitat)

137. Due to the Coriolis force, winds in the Southern Hemisphere are deflected:
(a) to their left (b) to their right (c) not at all (d) always towards the Equator
Answer: (a)
Solution: Ferrel's law: winds turn right in the Northern Hemisphere and left in the Southern Hemisphere. Applying the northern rule to the south is the trap.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Geography (Fundamentals of Physical Geography)

139. Which of the following pairs (desert – country) is NOT correctly matched?

140. Consider the following statements:
Statement I: The Suez Canal was opened in 1869.
Statement II: The Panama Canal joins the Atlantic and Pacific Oceans and was opened in 1914.
Which of the statements is/are correct?
(a) Only I (b) Only II (c) Both I and II (d) Neither I nor II
Answer: (c)
Solution: Both are correct: Suez (Mediterranean–Red Sea) opened in 1869 and Panama (Atlantic–Pacific) in 1914. Swapping the two years is the chronological trap.

141. The Equator passes through all the countries of which group?
(a) Kenya, Brazil, Indonesia (b) India, Kenya, Brazil (c) Egypt, Brazil, Indonesia (d) Kenya, Mexico, Indonesia
Answer: (a)
Solution: The Equator passes through Kenya, Brazil and Indonesia. India, Egypt and Mexico lie on the Tropic of Cancer instead.

142. Which of the following statements about Australia is NOT correct?
(a) It is the smallest continent (b) It lies entirely in the Southern Hemisphere (c) The Tropic of Capricorn passes through it (d) The Equator passes through it
Answer: (d)
Solution: Australia lies entirely in the Southern Hemisphere, so the Equator does not pass through it. The Tropic of Capricorn does cross it.
Source: NCERT Class 6 Geography (The Earth: Our Habitat)

143. A flight leaves a city on 0° longitude at 9:00 a.m. local time and lands after 8 hours at a city on 60°E. What is the local time at the arrival city when it lands?
(a) 5:00 p.m. (b) 1:00 p.m. (c) 9:00 p.m. (d) 9:00 a.m.
Answer: (c)
Solution: Landing time at 0° = 9:00 a.m. + 8 hours = 5:00 p.m. The arrival city is 60 × 4 = 240 minutes = 4 hours ahead, so local time = 5:00 p.m. + 4 hours = 9:00 p.m.
Source: NCERT Class 6 Geography (The Earth: Our Habitat)

144. Which of the following pairs (local wind – region) is correctly matched?
(a) Harmattan – West Africa (b) Chinook – Alps (c) Foehn – Rockies (d) Loo – Argentina
Answer: (a)
Solution: Harmattan is the dry wind of West Africa. Chinook belongs to the Rockies, Foehn to the Alps and Loo to northern India — the first two are swapped in the options.

146. Consider the following statements about the Earth's interior:
Statement I: The mantle extends to a depth of about 2,900 km.
Statement II: The core is made mainly of silica and alumina.
Statement III: The continental crust is thicker than the oceanic crust.
Which of the statements are correct?
(a) I and II only (b) I and III only (c) II and III only (d) I, II and III
Answer: (b)
Solution: Statements I and III are correct (crust about 35 km on continents, 5 km under oceans). The core is NIFE (nickel and iron); silica and alumina make SIAL, so Statement II is wrong.
Source: NCERT Class 7 Geography (Our Environment)

147. Which of the following statements about tides is correct?
(a) Spring tides occur only in the spring season (b) Neap tides occur on full moon days (c) Spring tides occur when the Sun, the Moon and the Earth are in a straight line (d) Neap tides are the highest tides of the month
Answer: (c)
Solution: Spring tides have nothing to do with the season; they come on full and new moon days when the three bodies are in a line. Neap tides, at the quarter moons, have the smallest range.
Source: NCERT Class 7 Geography (Our Environment)

148. If the Earth's axis were not tilted at all, which of the following would most likely happen?
(a) There would be no day and night (b) There would be no change of seasons during the year (c) The Earth would stop revolving around the Sun (d) A year would become 24 hours long
Answer: (b)
Solution: Seasons come from the tilt of the axis during revolution. Without tilt, the Sun's rays would fall the same way all year, so seasons would not change; day and night would still occur because of rotation.
Source: NCERT Class 6 Geography (The Earth: Our Habitat)

149. The rich fishing ground near Newfoundland is formed by the meeting of which two currents?
(a) Gulf Stream and Labrador Current (b) Kuroshio and Oyashio (c) Humboldt and Brazil Current (d) Benguela and Agulhas Current
Answer: (a)
Solution: Near Newfoundland the warm Gulf Stream meets the cold Labrador Current, creating the Grand Banks. Kuroshio and Oyashio meet near Japan, a different fishing area.
Source: NCERT Class 7 Geography (Our Environment)

150. Which of the following rivers does NOT drain into the Atlantic Ocean?
(a) Amazon (b) Congo (c) Niger (d) Zambezi
Answer: (d)
Solution: The Zambezi flows east and drains into the Indian Ocean. The Amazon, Congo and Niger all reach the Atlantic.
