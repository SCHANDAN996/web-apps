1. High-Yield Facts
Fact	Description	Examiner's Trap	Trap Type
Hottest planet – Venus	Thick carbon dioxide atmosphere traps heat; also the brightest planet in our sky.	Giving Mercury (nearest planet) as the hottest.	Almost True Statement
Largest / smallest planet	Jupiter is the largest; Mercury is the smallest.	Swapping Saturn in for Jupiter because of its famous rings.	Similar Names
Pluto	Reclassified as a dwarf planet by the IAU in 2006; the solar system has 8 planets.	Options showing "9 planets" from older books.	Chronological Confusion
Rotation vs Revolution	Rotation (about 24 hours, west to east) gives day and night; revolution (about 365¼ days) with the tilted axis gives seasons.	"Revolution causes day and night."	Reverse Causation
Perihelion / Aphelion	Earth is nearest to the Sun around 3 January and farthest around 4 July.	"Summer comes because Earth is nearest to the Sun."	Reverse Causation
Solstices	21 June – Sun overhead on Tropic of Cancer; 22 December – Sun overhead on Tropic of Capricorn.	Swapping the two Tropics between the dates.	Chronological Confusion
Equinoxes	21 March and 23 September – Sun overhead on the Equator; equal day and night everywhere.	Calling 21 June an equinox.	Similar Names
Important parallels	Tropic of Cancer 23½°N, Tropic of Capricorn 23½°S, Arctic Circle 66½°N, Antarctic Circle 66½°S.	Giving 66½° for the Tropics or 23½° for the Polar Circles.	Almost True Statement
Prime Meridian	0° longitude through Greenwich, near London.	Placing it through Paris or calling it a latitude.	Similar Names
IST meridian	82°30'E, passes near Mirzapur (UP); IST = GMT + 5 h 30 min.	Writing GMT – 5:30 or 82°E exactly.	Almost True Statement
International Date Line	Roughly along 180°; going east the date goes back one day, going west it goes forward one day.	Reversing the direction rule.	Negative Phrasing / Reversed Direction
Earth's interior	Crust (SIAL on continents, SIMA under oceans), Mantle, Core (NIFE – nickel + iron).	Calling the mantle NIFE or the oceanic crust SIAL.	Similar Names
Metamorphic rocks	Limestone → marble, shale → slate, sandstone → quartzite, granite → gneiss.	"Marble is formed from sandstone."	Almost True Statement
Block mountains	Black Forest (Germany), Vosges (France); Rhine rift valley between them.	Listing the Alps (fold) as a block mountain.	Similar Names
Largest continent / smallest	Asia (about one-third of world land) / Australia (island continent).	Calling Antarctica the smallest continent.	Almost True Statement
Africa and lines	Crossed by the Equator, the Tropic of Cancer and the Tropic of Capricorn.	Saying South America is crossed by all three.	Almost True Statement
Pacific Ocean	Largest and deepest; contains the Mariana Trench.	Placing the Mariana Trench in the Atlantic or Indian Ocean.	Similar Names
Indian Ocean	The only ocean named after a country.	Saying the Atlantic is named after a country.	Negative Phrasing
Ozone layer	Lies in the stratosphere; protects from ultraviolet rays.	Placing ozone in the troposphere (where weather happens).	Similar Names
Coriolis effect	Winds bend right in the Northern Hemisphere, left in the Southern (Ferrel's law).	Reversing the two hemispheres.	Reverse Causation
Gulf Stream vs Labrador	Gulf Stream is warm; Labrador is cold; they meet near Newfoundland (Grand Banks fishing ground).	Calling the Gulf Stream a cold current.	Almost True Statement
Temperate grasslands	Prairies (N. America), Pampas (Argentina), Steppes (Eurasia), Velds (S. Africa), Downs (Australia), Canterbury (New Zealand).	Matching Velds with Australia or Downs with Africa.	Similar Names
Nile vs Amazon	Nile – longest river; Amazon – largest by volume of water.	"Largest river = Nile."	Similar Names
Caspian / Superior / Baikal	Largest lake (salty) / largest freshwater lake by area / deepest lake.	"Largest freshwater lake = Caspian."	Almost True Statement
Suez vs Panama Canal	Suez (1869) – Mediterranean to Red Sea; Panama (1914) – Atlantic to Pacific.	Swapping the years or the seas.	Chronological Confusion
Boundary lines	Radcliffe – India/Pakistan; McMahon – India/China; Durand – Pakistan/Afghanistan; 38th Parallel – two Koreas; 49th Parallel – USA/Canada.	Swapping Durand and McMahon.	Similar Names
2. Time Management (15/45-sec rule)
Fact / Topic	Type (15s fact / 45s analytical)	Score Maximizer?	Reason
Planet records (hottest, largest, smallest)	15s (fact)	Yes	Direct recall; very frequent.
Grassland–continent matching	15s (fact)	Yes	One mnemonic covers six names.
Nicknames of countries	15s (fact)	Yes	One-line recall, almost every paper has one.
Straits and canals – which seas they join	15s (fact)	Yes	Map-memory questions; quick marks.
Layers of the atmosphere and their features	15s (fact)	Yes	Order mnemonic gives instant answer.
Time difference from longitude	45s (analytical)	Yes	Needs calculation: degrees × 4 minutes, then add/subtract.
International Date Line direction	45s (analytical)	Normal	Direction must be reasoned out; easy to reverse.
Seasons, perihelion and solstice statements	45s (analytical)	Yes	Statement-based; cause-effect must be checked.
Matching lists (rivers–cities, lines–countries)	45s (analytical)	Normal	Four pairs to check; use elimination.
Warm vs cold ocean currents	15s if one current; 45s if a list	Normal	Lists mix both types to confuse.
