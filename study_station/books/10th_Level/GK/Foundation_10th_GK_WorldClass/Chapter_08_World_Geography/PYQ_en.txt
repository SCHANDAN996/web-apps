📊 PYQ Pattern Analysis: World Geography (Basic)

Exams this chapter serves: SSC GD, SSC MTS, RRB Group D, State Police / Constable exams, and the foundation for SSC CHSL and RRB NTPC.

Note: This is a pattern analysis — what kind of questions these exams ask from this chapter and which traps they use. It does not claim year-wise counts or percentages.

1. Which Sub-topics Come Up Again and Again
Sub-topic	How it is usually asked	Typical difficulty
Solar system and planets	One-liners: hottest, largest, smallest, red planet, Earth's twin, dwarf planet Pluto	Easy
Motions of the Earth	Statements on rotation/revolution, solstice and equinox dates, perihelion/aphelion	Easy to Medium
Latitudes and longitudes	Degree of the Tropics and Polar Circles, Prime Meridian, IST meridian, 1° = 4 minutes	Easy to Medium
Time calculation and Date Line	Local time of a place from longitude difference; direction of date change	Medium
Interior of the Earth and rocks	Layers (SIAL, SIMA, NIFE), rock types, marble/slate origin	Easy
Landforms	Fold, block and volcanic mountain examples; highest peaks of continents	Easy to Medium
Continents and oceans	Largest/smallest, lines passing through Africa, Mariana Trench, Indian Ocean naming	Easy
Atmosphere	Gas percentages, layer order, ozone in stratosphere, ionosphere and radio waves	Easy
Winds and currents	Local winds (Loo, Chinook, Foehn, Harmattan, Sirocco), warm vs cold currents, cyclone names	Medium
Grasslands and deserts	Matching grassland to continent/country; desert to country	Easy
Rivers, lakes and records	Longest vs largest river, largest/deepest lake, cities on rivers, waterfalls	Easy to Medium
Straits, canals and boundary lines	Which seas a strait/canal joins; which countries a line divides	Easy
Nicknames	Land of Rising Sun, Midnight Sun, Thousand Lakes, Thunder Dragon, Sugar Bowl	Easy

2. How Different Exams Treat This Chapter
Exam group	Style of questions	What to prepare most
SSC GD / SSC MTS	Short one-line facts with four single-word options	Records, nicknames, straits, grasslands, planet facts
RRB Group D	Direct facts, often from NCERT Class 6–7 geography	Atmosphere layers, Earth's interior, motions of the Earth, latitudes
State Police / Constable	Mixed static GK, often matching and "which is not" type	Boundary lines, rivers–cities, local winds, continents
SSC CHSL / RRB NTPC (next level)	Statement-based and time-calculation questions appear more often	IST, time difference, seasons, ocean currents

3. Trap Types Used in This Chapter
Trap type	How it appears here	Defence
Almost True Statement	"Mercury is the hottest planet" (nearest is right, hottest is wrong)	Separate each record: nearest, hottest, largest
Reverse Causation	"Seasons occur because the Earth's distance from the Sun changes"	Tilt causes seasons; Earth is nearest in January
Chronological Confusion	Solstice dates swapped; "9 planets"; Suez and Panama years swapped	Fix dates with mnemonics: June-north, December-south; S (1869) before P (1914)
Negative Phrasing	"Which of the following is NOT a warm current?"	Circle the word NOT; list warm ones first
Similar Names / Records	Longest vs largest river; Caspian vs Superior vs Baikal; Durand vs McMahon	Read the adjective before matching

4. Top 10 High-Yield Questions (PYQ-style) with Solutions

Question 1
Which planet is the hottest in the solar system?
(a) Mercury (b) Venus (c) Mars (d) Jupiter
Answer: (b) Venus
Explanation: Venus has a thick carbon dioxide atmosphere that traps heat. Mercury is nearest to the Sun but is not the hottest.
Trap Type: Almost True Statement — "nearest" is mistaken for "hottest".
15/45-sec Rule: 15-second fact. Score maximizer.
Source: NCERT Class 6 Geography

Question 2
On which date is the Sun overhead on the Tropic of Capricorn?
(a) 21 March (b) 21 June (c) 23 September (d) 22 December
Answer: (d) 22 December
Explanation: On 22 December (winter solstice in the north) the Sun's rays fall vertically on the Tropic of Capricorn. On 21 June they fall on the Tropic of Cancer; on the equinoxes, on the Equator.
Trap Type: Chronological Confusion — the two solstice dates are swapped.
15/45-sec Rule: 15-second fact once the "June north, December south" mnemonic is fixed.
Source: NCERT Class 6 Geography

Question 3
Consider the following statements:
Statement I: The Earth is nearest to the Sun in early January.
Statement II: Seasons occur mainly because of the change in the Earth's distance from the Sun.
Which of the statements is/are correct?
(a) Only I (b) Only II (c) Both I and II (d) Neither I nor II
Answer: (a) Only I
Explanation: Perihelion is around 3 January, so Statement I is correct. Seasons are caused by the tilt of the axis during revolution, not by distance, so Statement II is wrong.
Trap Type: Reverse Causation.
15/45-sec Rule: 45-second analytical — two statements, one cause-effect check.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Geography (Fundamentals of Physical Geography)

Question 4
Indian Standard Time is based on which meridian?
(a) 82°30'E (b) 85°30'E (c) 80°E (d) 75°30'E
Answer: (a) 82°30'E
Explanation: The standard meridian of India is 82°30'E, passing near Mirzapur. 82.5 × 4 = 330 minutes, so IST is GMT + 5 hours 30 minutes.
Trap Type: Almost True Statement — nearby degree values.
15/45-sec Rule: 15-second fact.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Geography

Question 5
When it is 12 noon at Greenwich (0°), what is the local time at a place on 45°E?
(a) 9:00 a.m. (b) 2:00 p.m. (c) 3:00 p.m. (d) 3:45 p.m.
Answer: (c) 3:00 p.m.
Explanation: 45 × 4 = 180 minutes = 3 hours. The place is east, so its time is ahead: 12 noon + 3 hours = 3:00 p.m.
Trap Type: Reversed Direction — subtracting instead of adding gives 9:00 a.m.
15/45-sec Rule: 45-second analytical — calculation plus direction.
Source: NCERT Class 6 Geography

Question 6
Which layer of the atmosphere contains the ozone layer?
(a) Troposphere (b) Stratosphere (c) Mesosphere (d) Exosphere
Answer: (b) Stratosphere
Explanation: The ozone layer lies in the stratosphere and absorbs harmful ultraviolet rays. Weather occurs in the troposphere.
Trap Type: Similar Names — troposphere and stratosphere.
15/45-sec Rule: 15-second fact.
Source: NCERT Class 7 Geography

Question 7
The temperate grasslands of South Africa are called:
(a) Pampas (b) Downs (c) Velds (d) Steppes
Answer: (c) Velds
Explanation: Velds are in South Africa; Pampas in Argentina, Downs in Australia, Steppes in Eurasia.
Trap Type: Similar Names.
15/45-sec Rule: 15-second fact. Score maximizer.
Source: NCERT Class 7 Geography

Question 8
Which of the following is NOT a warm ocean current?
(a) Gulf Stream (b) Kuroshio (c) Labrador (d) Brazil Current
Answer: (c) Labrador
Explanation: The Labrador Current is cold; it flows south along the coast of Canada. The Gulf Stream, Kuroshio and Brazil Current are warm.
Trap Type: Negative Phrasing.
15/45-sec Rule: 15 seconds if the list of cold currents is ready.
Source: NCERT Class 7 Geography

Question 9
The Suez Canal connects:
(a) Mediterranean Sea and Red Sea (b) Atlantic Ocean and Pacific Ocean (c) Red Sea and Arabian Sea (d) Black Sea and Mediterranean Sea
Answer: (a) Mediterranean Sea and Red Sea
Explanation: The Suez Canal in Egypt (opened 1869) joins the Mediterranean and the Red Sea. The Panama Canal (opened 1914) joins the Atlantic and the Pacific.
Trap Type: Similar Names — Suez and Panama swapped.
15/45-sec Rule: 15-second fact.
Source: PYQ-style

Question 10
Which line separates Pakistan and Afghanistan?
(a) McMahon Line (b) Radcliffe Line (c) Durand Line (d) 38th Parallel
Answer: (c) Durand Line
Explanation: The Durand Line is the Pakistan–Afghanistan boundary. McMahon is India–China, Radcliffe is India–Pakistan, and the 38th Parallel divides North and South Korea.
Trap Type: Similar Names.
15/45-sec Rule: 15-second fact.
Source: PYQ-style

5. Smart Preparation Pattern
Most questions in this chapter are 15-second facts: records, nicknames, matching. Finish these first and bank the marks.
Keep 45 seconds only for time calculations, Date Line direction and two-statement questions.
For every "largest / longest / deepest" question, underline the adjective before you look at the options.
