Set 3/6 – Ancient Indian History MCQs (Foundation)
Questions 51–75: Medium

51. Consider the following statements about the Rigvedic period:
I. Iron was widely used in the Rigvedic period.
II. The Sabha and the Samiti were assemblies that could check the king's power.
III. The Battle of Ten Kings (Dasharajna) was fought on the banks of the Parushni (Ravi).
Which of the statements given is/are correct?
(a) I and II only (b) II and III only (c) I and III only (d) I, II and III
Answer: (b)
Solution: Statement I is wrong — iron came into wide use only in the Later Vedic age. The Sabha and Samiti were tribal assemblies, and the Dasharajna battle on the Parushni is described in the Rigveda.
Source: NCERT (old) Class 11 Ancient India, R.S. Sharma

52. Which of the following pairs of Harappan site and nearby river is NOT correctly matched?
(a) Harappa – Ravi (b) Mohenjodaro – Indus (c) Kalibangan – Ghaggar (d) Lothal – Mahi
Answer: (d)
Solution: Lothal lies near the Bhogava river (a tributary of the Sabarmati) in Gujarat, not on the Mahi. The other three pairs are correct.
Source: PYQ-style

53. Which Harappan city in the Rann of Kutch was divided into three parts and is known for its water reservoirs?
(a) Dholavira (b) Lothal (c) Kalibangan (d) Banawali
Answer: (a)
Solution: Dholavira (Gujarat) had a citadel, a middle town and a lower town, and a remarkable system of water storage. Lothal is also in Gujarat, but it is known for its dockyard.
Source: PYQ-style

54. Which of the following was the capital of the Kosala Mahajanapada?
(a) Ujjain (b) Vaishali (c) Kaushambi (d) Shravasti
Answer: (d)
Solution: Kosala's capital was Shravasti. Ujjain was the capital of Avanti, Vaishali of Vajji and Kaushambi of Vatsa.
Source: PYQ-style

55. "He was the first Nanda ruler, called 'Ekarat' in the Puranas, and is described as a destroyer of the Kshatriyas." This describes:
(a) Dhanananda (b) Mahapadma Nanda (c) Kalashoka (d) Bimbisara
Answer: (b)
Solution: Mahapadma Nanda, founder of the Nanda dynasty, is called 'Ekarat' (sole sovereign). Dhanananda was the last Nanda, Kalashoka a Shishunaga and Bimbisara a Haryanka king.
Source: PYQ-style

56. The 'Saptanga' theory of the state (seven limbs of the state) is explained in:
(a) Arthashastra (b) Manusmriti (c) Indica (d) Mahabhashya
Answer: (a)
Solution: Kautilya's Arthashastra describes the state as having seven limbs — king, ministers, territory, fort, treasury, army and ally. Indica is Megasthenes' account, a common wrong pick.
Source: PYQ-style

57. Which Ashokan inscription describes the Kalinga War and Ashoka's remorse?
(a) Major Rock Edict I (b) Minor Rock Edict I (c) Major Rock Edict XIII (d) Bhabru Edict
Answer: (c)
Solution: Major Rock Edict XIII describes the suffering of the Kalinga War and Ashoka's turn to Dhamma-vijaya (conquest by Dhamma).
Source: NCERT Class 6 History (Our Pasts-I), "Ashoka, the Emperor Who Gave Up War"

58. The 'Hathigumpha inscription' is associated with which ruler?
(a) Ashoka (b) Kharavela (c) Chandragupta II (d) Pulakeshin II
Answer: (b)
Solution: The Hathigumpha (Elephant Cave) inscription in the Udayagiri hills near Bhubaneswar records the achievements of Kharavela, king of Kalinga. Ashoka is the trap because he too is linked with Kalinga.
Source: PYQ-style

59. In Gupta administration, the term 'Kumaramatya' refers to:
(a) The crown prince (b) The commander of the cavalry (c) A class of high-ranking officials (d) The minister for foreign affairs
Answer: (c)
Solution: Kumaramatyas were a cadre of high officers who served in important posts, including provincial ones. The word 'Kumara' (prince) tempts students towards 'crown prince'.
Source: PYQ-style

60. The Mandsaur inscription of the Gupta period is known for information about:
(a) A guild of silk weavers (b) The conquests of Samudragupta (c) Fa-hien's travels (d) The Kalinga War
Answer: (a)
Solution: The Mandsaur (Madhya Pradesh) inscription records that a guild of silk weavers who had migrated from Gujarat built a Sun temple — evidence of strong guilds in the Gupta age.
Source: PYQ-style

61. Which of the following statements about the Chalcolithic cultures of India is correct?
(a) They used iron ploughs widely. (b) They used copper tools along with stone tools. (c) They lived in large planned cities with covered drains. (d) They wrote in the Brahmi script.
Answer: (b)
Solution: 'Chalcolithic' means copper-stone age: copper and stone tools were used together. Iron came later, planned cities with drains belong to the Harappans, and Brahmi appears only in Ashoka's time.
Source: PYQ-style

62. The Sangam assemblies of Tamil poets are traditionally associated with which city?
(a) Madurai (b) Kanchipuram (c) Thanjavur (d) Uraiyur
Answer: (a)
Solution: Sangam poems are so called because they were composed and compiled in assemblies (sangams) held in Madurai, the city of the Pandyas. Kanchipuram was the later Pallava capital.
Source: NCERT Class 6 History (Our Pasts-I), "Traders, Kings and Pilgrims"

63. In Buddhism, the term 'Arhat' refers to:
(a) A future Buddha (b) A newly ordained monk (c) One who has attained Nirvana (d) A Bodhisattva who delays his own salvation
Answer: (c)
Solution: An Arhat is a person who has attained enlightenment and is freed from rebirth. A Bodhisattva who delays his own nirvana to help others is a Mahayana ideal.
Source: PYQ-style

64. The Ajanta cave paintings mostly show themes from:
(a) The lives of Jain Tirthankaras (b) The Ramayana and Mahabharata (c) The Jataka tales and the life of the Buddha (d) Mughal court life
Answer: (c)
Solution: Ajanta was a Buddhist site; its paintings mainly show stories from the Jatakas and scenes from the Buddha's life.
Source: NCERT Class 6 History (Our Pasts-I), "Buildings, Paintings and Books"

65. Which of the following is a Neolithic site in Bihar?
(a) Burzahom (b) Mehrgarh (c) Bagor (d) Chirand
Answer: (d)
Solution: Chirand (Saran district, Bihar) is a Neolithic site. Burzahom is in Kashmir, Mehrgarh in Balochistan, and Bagor (Rajasthan) is a Mesolithic site.
Source: NCERT Class 6 History (Our Pasts-I), "From Gathering to Growing Food"

66. Ashoka's Rummindei (Lumbini) pillar inscription records:
(a) Ashoka's visit to the Buddha's birthplace and a tax reduction for the village (b) The Kalinga War (c) The building of the Great Stupa at Sanchi (d) The Third Buddhist Council
Answer: (a)
Solution: The Rummindei pillar says Ashoka visited Lumbini, where the Buddha was born, and reduced the village's tax share to one-eighth.
Source: PYQ-style

67. Consider the following pairs of text and author:
I. Harshacharita – Banabhatta
II. Abhijnanashakuntalam – Kalidasa
III. Mudrarakshasa – Shudraka
Which of the pairs given is/are correctly matched?
(a) I only (b) I and II only (c) II and III only (d) I, II and III
Answer: (b)
Solution: I and II are correct. Mudrarakshasa was written by Vishakhadatta; Shudraka wrote Mrichchhakatika.
Source: PYQ-style

68. The hymns of the Rigveda were composed in:
(a) Prakrit (b) Pali (c) Old or Vedic Sanskrit (d) Tamil
Answer: (c)
Solution: The Rigveda is in old or Vedic Sanskrit, which is different from the Sanskrit learnt in school today. Prakrit was the language of Ashoka's edicts — the trap.
Source: NCERT Class 6 History (Our Pasts-I), "What Books and Burials Tell Us"

69. The 'Nagara' style of temple architecture is mainly associated with:
(a) South India (b) North India (c) Sri Lanka (d) Central Asia
Answer: (b)
Solution: The Nagara style, with its curved shikhara, developed in North India. The Dravida style with a pyramid-like vimana is the South Indian style.
Source: PYQ-style

70. Which Rashtrakuta king is known as a patron of Kannada literature, with the Kavirajamarga linked to his court?
(a) Dantidurga (b) Krishna I (c) Govinda III (d) Amoghavarsha I
Answer: (d)
Solution: Amoghavarsha I patronised Kannada and Sanskrit scholars; the Kavirajamarga, an early Kannada work on poetics, is linked to his court. Krishna I is known for the Kailasa temple at Ellora.
Source: PYQ-style

71. Brihadratha, the last Maurya ruler, was killed by:
(a) Pushyamitra Shunga (b) Vasudeva Kanva (c) Simuka (d) Kharavela
Answer: (a)
Solution: His commander Pushyamitra Shunga killed Brihadratha and founded the Shunga dynasty. Vasudeva Kanva later replaced the Shungas — the order is Maurya → Shunga → Kanva.
Source: PYQ-style

72. The 'Dhamma Mahamatras' appointed by Ashoka were:
(a) Officers in charge of military campaigns (b) Officials who spread Dhamma and looked after people's welfare (c) Provincial governors (d) Tax collectors
Answer: (b)
Solution: Ashoka appointed officials, known as dhamma mahamatta, who went from place to place teaching people about Dhamma.
Source: NCERT Class 6 History (Our Pasts-I), "Ashoka, the Emperor Who Gave Up War"

73. The Udayagiri and Khandagiri caves near Bhubaneswar are associated with which religion?
(a) Buddhism (b) Ajivika sect (c) Jainism (d) Zoroastrianism
Answer: (c)
Solution: These rock-cut caves were made for Jain monks, mainly in the time of Kharavela. Buddhist caves are far more common elsewhere, which makes (a) tempting.
Source: PYQ-style

74. The astronomical work 'Panchasiddhantika' was composed by:
(a) Aryabhata (b) Varahamihira (c) Brahmagupta (d) Bhaskara I
Answer: (b)
Solution: Varahamihira's Panchasiddhantika summarises five earlier astronomical systems. Aryabhata wrote the Aryabhatiya and Brahmagupta the Brahmasphutasiddhanta.
Source: PYQ-style

75. Consider the following statements:
I. The Satavahanas used Prakrit in their inscriptions.
II. The earliest Satavahana inscriptions were in Sanskrit.
Which of the statements given is/are correct?
(a) Only I (b) Only II (c) Both I and II (d) Neither I nor II
Answer: (a)
Solution: Satavahana inscriptions, such as those at Naneghat and Nasik, are in Prakrit. Statement II is wrong; Sanskrit royal inscriptions became common later, after Rudradaman's Junagadh inscription.
Source: PYQ-style
