126. Consider the following statements regarding the Neolithic site of Mehrgarh:

It provides the earliest evidence of wheat and barley cultivation in the subcontinent.

Evidence of dental drilling has been found here.

The site shows a transition from semi-nomadic herding to settled agriculture.
Which of the statements given above are correct?
(A) 1 and 2 only
(B) 2 and 3 only
(C) 1 and 3 only
(D) 1, 2 and 3

Answer: (D)
Solution: All three statements are correct. Mehrgarh (c. 7000 BCE) has yielded early wheat/barley, drilled teeth indicating early dentistry, and evidence of a gradual shift from pastoralism to farming.
Source: PYQ-style

127. With reference to the Rigvedic geography, the river ‘Sadanira’ mentioned in the Shatapatha Brahmana has been identified with:
(A) Gomati
(B) Gandak
(C) Ghaggar
(D) Saryu

Answer: (B)
Solution: The Shatapatha Brahmana describes the Videgha Mathava migration across the Sadanira, which marked the boundary of Kosala-Videha. It is identified with the modern Gandak.
Source: PYQ-style

128. The early Buddhist texts mention ‘Ashtamahasthanas’. These refer to:
(A) Eight great cities of the Mahajanapada period
(B) Eight sacred places associated with the life of the Buddha
(C) Eight chapters of the Vinaya Pitaka
(D) Eight great monastic universities

Answer: (B)
Solution: Ashtamahasthanas are the eight great pilgrimage sites related to Buddha’s birth, enlightenment, first sermon, mahaparinirvana, and four miracles.
Source: PYQ-style

129. Which of the following is the correct chronological sequence of the composition of these texts?
(A) Rig Veda, Brahmanas, Upanishads, Aranyakas
(B) Rig Veda, Brahmanas, Aranyakas, Upanishads
(C) Rig Veda, Upanishads, Brahmanas, Aranyakas
(D) Rig Veda, Aranyakas, Brahmanas, Upanishads

Answer: (B)
Solution: The Vedic literature followed the order: Samhitas (Rig Veda earliest) → Brahmanas (ritual explanations) → Aranyakas (forest treatises) → Upanishads (philosophical).
Source: PYQ-style
⚠️ Trap Type: Chronological Confusion – many reverse Aranyakas and Upanishads, forgetting that Upanishads emerge from the Aranyaka layer.

130. Consider the following pairs of Harappan sites and their distinctive artefacts:

Daimabad – Bronze chariot

Chanhudaro – Bead-making factory

Amri – Pre-Harappan and Harappan pottery
Which of the pairs given above is/are correctly matched?
(A) 1 and 2 only
(B) 2 and 3 only
(C) 1 and 3 only
(D) 1, 2 and 3

Answer: (D)
Solution: Daimabad is famous for its bronze animal sculptures and a chariot; Chanhudaro was a centre of bead-making and lacks a citadel; Amri shows an evolution from pre-Harappan to mature Harappan culture. All three are correct.
Source: PYQ-style

131. The ‘Sutras’ of Panini, Pingala, and Kalidasa belong respectively to which disciplines?
(A) Grammar, prosody, dance
(B) Dance, astronomy, drama
(C) Grammar, prosody, poetry/drama
(D) Astronomy, grammar, medicine

Answer: (C)
Solution: Panini composed the Ashtadhyayi (Sanskrit grammar), Pingala the Chandahshastra (prosody, also linked to binary mathematics), and Kalidasa excelled in poetry and drama.
Source: PYQ-style

132. The concept of ‘Tirthankara’ in Jainism is often misconstrued. Which of the following statements about Tirthankaras is NOT correct?
(A) All Tirthankaras were Kshatriyas.
(B) Mahavira was the 24th Tirthankara.
(C) Parshvanath’s symbol was the serpent.
(D) Rishabhanath is associated with the bull symbol.

Answer: (A)
Solution: Statement A is incorrect because the 19th Tirthankara Mallinatha was a female according to Shvetambara tradition (though Digambaras dispute this, the blanket statement is inaccurate). Also, Rishabhanath is considered an Ikshvaku (royal), not all were necessarily Kshatriyas in the traditional varna sense. The exam often marks “all were Kshatriyas” as an overgeneralization.
Source: PYQ-style
⚠️ Trap Type: Almost True Statement – the statement "all were Kshatriyas" is appealing but factually contested; Mallinatha’s gender and lineage challenge this generalization.

133. The ‘Uttarapatha’ in ancient India referred to:
(A) The northern route connecting Taxila to Tamralipti
(B) The southern trade route
(C) The royal highway built by Ashoka
(D) The path of the monsoon winds

Answer: (A)
Solution: Uttarapatha was the great northern trade route stretching from the northwest (Taxila) to the port of Tamralipti (Bengal). Dakshinapatha was the southern route.
Source: PYQ-style

134. With reference to the Pallava-Pandya conflict, the ‘Velvikkudi grant’ gives crucial information about the revival of the Pandya kingdom under:
(A) Kadungon
(B) Arikesari Maravarman
(C) Nedunjeliyan
(D) Jatila Parantaka

Answer: (A)
Solution: The Velvikkudi copper plate (8th century) describes how Kadungon, the Pandya ruler, ended the Kalabhra interregnum and revived the Pandya kingdom.
Source: PYQ-style

135. Which of the following Buddhist councils led to the formal division of the Sangha into Sthaviravada and Mahasanghika?
(A) First Council
(B) Second Council
(C) Third Council
(D) Fourth Council

Answer: (B)
Solution: The Second Council at Vaishali resulted in the first major schism; the orthodox elders (Sthaviravada) and the majority community (Mahasanghika) split over disciplinary rules.
Source: PYQ-style

136. The Acharanga Sutra and Kalpa Sutra are sacred texts of the:
(A) Shvetambara Jains
(B) Digambara Jains
(C) Theravada Buddhists
(D) Mahayana Buddhists

Answer: (A)
Solution: Acharanga Sutra (monastic conduct) and Kalpa Sutra (life of Tirthankaras, esp. Mahavira) are part of the Shvetambara Jain canon.
Source: PYQ-style

137. With reference to the Gupta economy, the term ‘Agrahara’ denotes:
(A) Tax-free land granted to Brahmins
(B) A merchant guild
(C) A coastal port
(D) The royal treasury

Answer: (A)
Solution: Agrahara was a grant of land or a village, usually tax-free, given to Brahmins for religious and educational purposes.
Source: PYQ-style

138. The ‘Nasik inscription’ of Gautami Balashri eulogizes:
(A) Chandragupta Maurya
(B) Gautamiputra Satakarni
(C) Ashoka
(D) Rudradaman I

Answer: (B)
Solution: The Nasik cave inscription, issued by Gautami Balashri (mother of Gautamiputra Satakarni), praises the Satavahana king for restoring the glory of the dynasty and defeating the Shakas.
Source: PYQ-style

139. The doctrine of ‘Syadvada’ in Jainism is most accurately described as:
(A) The theory of non-violence
(B) The theory of conditioned predication or relativity of knowledge
(C) The path of total renunciation
(D) The doctrine of karma

Answer: (B)
Solution: Syadvada (the theory of ‘maybe’ or conditional assertion) expresses that truth is relative to perspective and can be stated in seven ways (saptabhangi naya).
Source: PYQ-style

140. Which of the following texts is a part of the ‘Prasthanatrayi’ of Vedanta philosophy?
(A) Manusmriti, Arthashastra, Mahabhashya
(B) Brahmasutras, Bhagavad Gita, Principal Upanishads
(C) Puranas, Dharmashastras, Vedangas
(D) Ramayana, Mahabharata, Harivamsa

Answer: (B)
Solution: The Prasthanatrayi refers to the three foundational texts for Vedanta schools: the Upanishads (shruti prasthana), the Bhagavad Gita (smriti prasthana), and the Brahmasutras (nyaya prasthana).
Source: PYQ-style

141. Consider the following statements about the Harappan civilization:

Rice husk has been found at Lothal and Rangpur.

Evidence of a ploughed field comes from Kalibangan.

A terracotta model of a plough has been found at Banawali.
Which of the statements given above are correct?
(A) 1 and 2 only
(B) 2 and 3 only
(C) 1 and 3 only
(D) 1, 2 and 3

Answer: (D)
Solution: All three statements are correct. Lothal and Rangpur (Gujarat) have rice remains. Kalibangan (Rajasthan) gives evidence of pre-Harappan ploughed fields. A terracotta plough model was recovered from Banawali (Haryana).
Source: PYQ-style

142. The earliest definite reference to the practice of ‘image worship’ in Hinduism (Vaishnavism) from an inscription is found in the:
(A) Junagadh inscription of Rudradaman I
(B) Besnagar inscription of Heliodorus
(C) Allahabad pillar inscription
(D) Hathigumpha inscription

Answer: (B)
Solution: The Besnagar (Vidisha) pillar inscription of Heliodorus (c. 110 BCE), a Greek ambassador who became a Bhagavata, refers to the worship of Vasudeva (Vishnu) and is the earliest epigraphic reference to image worship in Vaishnavism.
Source: PYQ-style

143. With reference to the Ashokan pillars, consider the following statements:

The pillars are monoliths of polished sandstone (Chunar).

They carry edicts relating to Dhamma.

The pillar capitals include animals like lions, elephants, and bulls.
Which of the statements given above are correct?
(A) 1 and 2 only
(B) 2 and 3 only
(C) 1 and 3 only
(D) 1, 2 and 3

Answer: (D)
Solution: All three are correct. Ashokan pillars were carved from a single block of Chunar sandstone, had a lustrous polish, and bore Dhamma edicts. The capitals featured lions (Sarnath), a single lion (Lauriya Nandangarh), an elephant (Sankissa), and a bull (Rampurva).
Source: PYQ-style

144. The ‘Sangam poems’ classify the land into five tinais based on landscape. The ‘Palai’ tinai represents:
(A) Hills and forests
(B) Coastal region
(C) Arid/desert region
(D) Riverine agricultural tract

Answer: (C)
Solution: Sangam geography: Kurinji (hills), Mullai (forests), Marutam (riverine plains), Neytal (coast), Palai (dry/arid lands, often associated with separation).
Source: PYQ-style

145. Which of the following statements is correct regarding the ‘Barabar caves’?
(A) They were built during the Gupta period for Buddhist monks.
(B) They are the earliest rock-cut caves in India, dedicated by Ashoka to Ajivikas.
(C) They contain paintings from the Jataka tales.
(D) They are located in the Western Ghats.

Answer: (B)
Solution: The Barabar hills (Bihar) have four rock-cut caves, the earliest in India, donated by Ashoka and Dasharatha to the Ajivika sect. They are not Buddhist; Ajanta caves contain Jataka paintings.
Source: PYQ-style
⚠️ Trap Type: Similar Name – Barabar vs. Ajanta/Ellora; many associate rock-cut caves with Buddhism and the Western Ghats.

146. In the context of ancient Indian history, the ‘Parivrajaka’ referred to:
(A) A class of tax collectors
(B) Wandering ascetics who renounced household life
(C) Royal messengers
(D) Foreign ambassadors

Answer: (B)
Solution: Parivrajakas were wandering mendicants and ascetics, often seen as critics of the Vedic ritualism, and many joined heterodox sects.
Source: PYQ-style

147. The ‘Allahabad pillar’ originally contained an inscription of:
(A) Chandragupta Maurya
(B) Ashoka
(C) Samudragupta
(D) Both Ashoka and Samudragupta

Answer: (D)
Solution: The Allahabad pillar has both an Ashokan edict (Minor Pillar Edict, also called Queen’s Edict) and the later prashasti of Samudragupta inscribed on it. This is a tricky detail often overlooked.
Source: PYQ-style
⚠️ Trap Type: Almost True Statement – choosing only Samudragupta misses the fact that the pillar itself was originally erected by Ashoka and carries his inscription too.

148. The ‘Gathasaptasati’ is a collection of Prakrit poems attributed to a king of which dynasty?
(A) Gupta
(B) Satavahana
(C) Maurya
(D) Kushana

Answer: (B)
Solution: Gathasaptasati (700 verses) is ascribed to the Satavahana king Hala. It contains lyrical poems on love and rural life in Maharashtri Prakrit.
Source: PYQ-style

150. The ‘Sapta Sindhu’ region in the Rigveda refers to the land of the:
(A) Seven sacred rivers of eastern India
(B) Indus and its five tributaries along with the Saraswati
(C) Seven great Mahajanapadas
(D) Seven hills of the Deccan

Answer: (B)
Solution: Sapta Sindhu (land of seven rivers) encompasses the Indus, its five tributaries (Jhelum, Chenab, Ravi, Beas, Sutlej), and the Saraswati (now dry).
