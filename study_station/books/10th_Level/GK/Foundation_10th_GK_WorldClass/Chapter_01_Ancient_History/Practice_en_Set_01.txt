Set 1/6 – Ancient Indian History MCQs (Foundation)
Questions 1–20: Easy | Questions 21–25: Medium

1. Which of the following Neolithic sites is famous for pit-dwellings?
(a) Mehrgarh (b) Burzahom (c) Chirand (d) Lothal
Answer: (b)
Solution: People at Burzahom in Kashmir lived in pits dug into the ground. Mehrgarh is the early farming site in Balochistan, Chirand is a Neolithic site in Bihar, and Lothal is a Harappan port.
Source: NCERT Class 6 History (Our Pasts-I), "From Gathering to Growing Food"

2. The Great Bath is located at which Harappan site?
(a) Harappa (b) Dholavira (c) Lothal (d) Mohenjodaro
Answer: (d)
Solution: The Great Bath, a large brick-lined tank in the citadel, was found at Mohenjodaro. Lothal is known for its dockyard, Dholavira for water management.
Source: NCERT Class 12 History (Themes in Indian History Part I), "Bricks, Beads and Bones"

3. In later Vedic texts, the term 'Shyama Ayas' (dark metal) refers to:
(a) Copper (b) Iron (c) Silver (d) Gold
Answer: (b)
Solution: Later Vedic texts call iron 'shyama ayas' or 'krishna ayas', the dark/black metal. Copper was known much earlier, so it is the tempting wrong choice.
Source: PYQ-style

4. Who among the following was the royal physician of Bimbisara?
(a) Jivaka (b) Sushruta (c) Charaka (d) Vagbhata
Answer: (a)
Solution: Jivaka was the royal physician of Bimbisara, the Haryanka king of Magadha. Charaka and Sushruta are known for medical texts, not for serving Bimbisara.
Source: PYQ-style

5. The First Buddhist Council was held at:
(a) Vaishali (b) Pataliputra (c) Rajgriha (d) Kashmir
Answer: (c)
Solution: The First Council met at Rajgriha soon after the Buddha's death, under the patronage of Ajatashatru. Vaishali, Pataliputra and Kashmir hosted the second, third and fourth councils.
Source: PYQ-style

6. Which Mauryan ruler is known for the Kalinga War?
(a) Chandragupta Maurya (b) Bindusara (c) Brihadratha (d) Ashoka
Answer: (d)
Solution: Ashoka fought the Kalinga War (c. 261 BCE); the bloodshed made him give up war and spread Dhamma.
Source: NCERT Class 6 History (Our Pasts-I), "Ashoka, the Emperor Who Gave Up War"

7. The 'Allahabad Prashasti' (Prayag Prashasti) is associated with which Gupta ruler?
(a) Chandragupta I (b) Samudragupta (c) Chandragupta II (d) Skandagupta
Answer: (b)
Solution: The Allahabad Pillar Inscription, composed by the court poet Harishena, praises the conquests of Samudragupta.
Source: NCERT Class 6 History (Our Pasts-I), "New Empires and Kingdoms"

8. Fa-hien, the Chinese pilgrim, visited India during the reign of:
(a) Ashoka (b) Harshavardhana (c) Chandragupta II (d) Skandagupta
Answer: (c)
Solution: Fa-hien came during the reign of the Gupta king Chandragupta II. The trap is Harshavardhana, who was visited by Hiuen Tsang about two centuries later.
Source: NCERT Class 6 History (Our Pasts-I), "Traders, Kings and Pilgrims"

9. The famous 'Junagadh Rock Inscription' in Sanskrit belongs to which ruler?
(a) Ashoka (b) Samudragupta (c) Rudradaman I (d) Harsha
Answer: (c)
Solution: The Junagadh inscription of the Shaka ruler Rudradaman I (c. 150 CE) is an early long royal inscription in Sanskrit. The same rock also carries an edict of Ashoka, but in Prakrit — that is the trap.
Source: PYQ-style

10. Which of the following is NOT a site of the Harappan (Indus Valley) Civilisation?
(a) Pataliputra (b) Surkotada (c) Kalibangan (d) Dholavira
Answer: (a)
Solution: Pataliputra (modern Patna) was the capital of Magadha and of the Mauryas, much later than the Harappan age. The other three are Harappan sites.
Source: NCERT Class 12 History (Themes in Indian History Part I), "Bricks, Beads and Bones"

11. The words 'Satyameva Jayate' written below the State Emblem of India are taken from:
(a) Rig Veda (b) Mundaka Upanishad (c) Bhagavad Gita (d) Arthashastra
Answer: (b)
Solution: 'Satyameva Jayate' (Truth alone triumphs) comes from the Mundaka Upanishad. The emblem itself is adapted from Ashoka's Lion Capital at Sarnath.
Source: PYQ-style

12. Who among the following is known as the 'Napoleon of India'?
(a) Chandragupta Maurya (b) Ashoka (c) Harsha (d) Samudragupta
Answer: (d)
Solution: Historians call Samudragupta the 'Napoleon of India' because of his many military campaigns described in the Prayag Prashasti.
Source: PYQ-style

13. The Mahajanapada 'Vajji', with its capital at Vaishali, had which type of government?
(a) Monarchy (b) Republic (Ganasangha) (c) Theocracy (d) Foreign rule
Answer: (b)
Solution: The Vajji was a gana or sangha, where many rulers governed together through an assembly, unlike Magadha, which was a monarchy.
Source: NCERT Class 6 History (Our Pasts-I), "Kingdoms, Kings and an Early Republic"

14. Which Veda is a collection of melodies and chants?
(a) Rig Veda (b) Sama Veda (c) Yajur Veda (d) Atharva Veda
Answer: (b)
Solution: The Sama Veda arranges hymns for singing. The Rig Veda is a collection of hymns, the Yajur Veda has sacrificial formulas, and the Atharva Veda has charms and spells.
Source: PYQ-style

15. The Third Buddhist Council was held under the patronage of:
(a) Ajatashatru (b) Kanishka (c) Ashoka (d) Harsha
Answer: (c)
Solution: The Third Council met at Pataliputra under Ashoka, presided over by Moggaliputta Tissa. Kanishka was the patron of the Fourth Council.
Source: PYQ-style

16. Which metal came into wide use in the Later Vedic period, helping people clear forests and expand farming?
(a) Copper (b) Bronze (c) Iron (d) Silver
Answer: (c)
Solution: Iron tools and ploughshares spread in the Later Vedic age. Copper and bronze were already known to the Harappans.
Source: PYQ-style

17. Lothal, a famous Harappan site, is known for its:
(a) Dockyard (b) Great Bath (c) Stone bust of a bearded priest (d) Ploughed field
Answer: (a)
Solution: Lothal in Gujarat had a dockyard. The Great Bath and the bearded priest are from Mohenjodaro, and the ploughed field from Kalibangan.
Source: NCERT Class 12 History (Themes in Indian History Part I), "Bricks, Beads and Bones"

18. The title 'Devanampiya Piyadassi' was used in the inscriptions of:
(a) Chandragupta Maurya (b) Bindusara (c) Kanishka (d) Ashoka
Answer: (d)
Solution: Ashoka calls himself 'Devanampiya Piyadassi' — beloved of the gods, of pleasing appearance — in his inscriptions.
Source: NCERT Class 6 History (Our Pasts-I), "Ashoka, the Emperor Who Gave Up War"

19. The Gupta era began in:
(a) 78 CE (b) 319 CE (c) 606 CE (d) 150 BCE
Answer: (b)
Solution: 78 CE is the start of the Shaka era and 606 CE marks the start of Harsha's reign; the Gupta era, linked with Chandragupta I, is counted from 319 CE.
Source: PYQ-style

20. The 'Milinda Panho' records a dialogue between:
(a) Menander and Nagasena (b) Kanishka and Vasumitra (c) Ashoka and Moggaliputta Tissa (d) Harsha and Hiuen Tsang
Answer: (a)
Solution: 'Milinda Panho' (Questions of Milinda) is a Pali text recording the questions of the Indo-Greek king Menander (Milinda) and the answers of the Buddhist monk Nagasena.
Source: PYQ-style

21. Consider the following pairs of dynasty and ruler:
I. Haryanka – Bimbisara
II. Nanda – Mahapadma
III. Maurya – Chandragupta
Which of the pairs given is/are correctly matched?
(a) I and II only (b) II and III only (c) I, II and III (d) I only
Answer: (c)
Solution: All three are correct: Bimbisara was a Haryanka king, Mahapadma founded the Nanda dynasty and Chandragupta founded the Maurya dynasty. The trap is to think Mahapadma was a Maurya.
Source: NCERT Class 6 History (Our Pasts-I), "Kingdoms, Kings and an Early Republic"

22. The famous 'Aihole inscription' of Pulakeshin II was composed by:
(a) Harishena (b) Ravikirti (c) Banabhatta (d) Kalidasa
Answer: (b)
Solution: The Aihole prashasti of the Chalukya king Pulakeshin II was written by his court poet Ravikirti. Harishena composed the Allahabad prashasti — a similar-name trap.
Source: NCERT Class 6 History (Our Pasts-I), "New Empires and Kingdoms"

23. Arrange the places of the Buddhist councils in chronological order:
I. Vaishali
II. Rajgriha
III. Pataliputra
IV. Kashmir
(a) II, I, III, IV (b) I, II, IV, III (c) II, III, I, IV (d) III, II, I, IV
Answer: (a)
Solution: Rajgriha (First) → Vaishali (Second) → Pataliputra (Third) → Kashmir (Fourth), i.e. II, I, III, IV. Remember R-V-P-K.
Source: PYQ-style

24. Consider the following statements about the Gupta period:
I. Aryabhata wrote the Surya Siddhanta.
II. The Iron Pillar at Mehrauli is generally linked with Chandragupta II.
III. Kalidasa adorned the court of Samudragupta.
Which of the statements given is/are correct?
(a) I and II only (b) II only (c) II and III only (d) I, II and III
Answer: (b)
Solution: Only II is correct. Aryabhata's own work is the Aryabhatiya, and Kalidasa is traditionally linked with the court of Chandragupta II, not Samudragupta.
Source: PYQ-style

25. The 'Mattavilasa Prahasana' was written by a king of which dynasty?
(a) Gupta (b) Pallava (c) Chalukya (d) Rashtrakuta
Answer: (b)
Solution: The Pallava king Mahendravarman I wrote this satirical play. The Chalukyas were the Pallavas' rivals, which makes (c) tempting.
Source: PYQ-style
