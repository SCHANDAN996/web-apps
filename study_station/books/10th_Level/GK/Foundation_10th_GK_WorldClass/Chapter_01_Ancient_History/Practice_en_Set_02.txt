Set 2/6 – Ancient Indian History MCQs (Foundation)
Questions 26–50: Medium

26. Which of the following Vedas is often called the 'book of magical formulas'?
(a) Rig Veda (b) Sama Veda (c) Yajur Veda (d) Atharva Veda
Answer: (d)
Solution: The Atharva Veda contains charms and spells for healing, protection and success. The Sama Veda is about melodies, which is why it is a common wrong pick.
Source: PYQ-style

27. In the Rigvedic period, the tribe — the people as a whole, headed by the rajan — was called:
(a) Jana (b) Kula (c) Grama (d) Rashtra
Answer: (a)
Solution: The Rigveda speaks of the 'jana' (people/tribe) as the main political unit under a rajan. Kula was the family and grama the village; 'Rashtra' as a territorial state became important only in the Later Vedic age.
Source: NCERT Class 6 History (Our Pasts-I), "What Books and Burials Tell Us"

28. According to NCERT, the Harappan script was generally written:
(a) From left to right (b) From right to left (c) From top to bottom (d) Only in circles
Answer: (b)
Solution: NCERT notes that the Harappan script was written from right to left; it has still not been deciphered. Left to right is the direction of Brahmi, a later script.
Source: NCERT Class 12 History (Themes in Indian History Part I), "Bricks, Beads and Bones"

29. Consider the following statements:
I. The Harappan people were completely unfamiliar with the horse.
II. Surkotada has yielded bones that some archaeologists identify as horse bones.
Which of the statements given is/are correct?
(a) Only I (b) Only II (c) Both I and II (d) Neither I nor II
Answer: (b)
Solution: Statement II is correct, though the identification is debated. Statement I is wrong because of the absolute word "completely" — the trap in such questions.
Source: PYQ-style

30. Who was the last ruler of the Nanda dynasty and a contemporary of Alexander?
(a) Dhanananda (b) Mahapadma Nanda (c) Kalashoka (d) Udayin
Answer: (a)
Solution: Dhanananda ruled Magadha at the time of Alexander's invasion and was overthrown by Chandragupta Maurya. Mahapadma was the first Nanda king, not the last.
Source: PYQ-style

31. The 'Gayatri Mantra' is found in which Mandala of the Rig Veda?
(a) First Mandala (b) Tenth Mandala (c) Ninth Mandala (d) Third Mandala
Answer: (d)
Solution: The Gayatri Mantra, addressed to Savitri (the Sun), is in the Third Mandala of the Rig Veda.
Source: PYQ-style

32. The Boghazkoi inscription (c. 14th century BCE), found in present-day Turkey, is important because it:
(a) Mentions the Vedic gods Indra, Mitra, Varuna and the Nasatyas (b) Is the earliest inscription in the Brahmi script (c) Records Alexander's invasion of India (d) Describes Harappan trade with Mesopotamia
Answer: (a)
Solution: This treaty inscription invokes Indra, Mitra, Varuna and the Nasatyas, the same gods found in the Rigveda. It has nothing to do with Brahmi or Alexander, who came about a thousand years later.
Source: PYQ-style

33. Which of the following pairs of Ashoka's Major Rock Edicts and their content is NOT correctly matched?
(a) Major Rock Edict I – Prohibition of animal sacrifice (b) Major Rock Edict II – Medical care for humans and animals (c) Major Rock Edict XIII – Kalinga War and Dhamma-vijaya (d) Major Rock Edict VII – Appointment of Dhamma Mahamatras
Answer: (d)
Solution: The appointment of Dhamma Mahamatras is in Major Rock Edict V; Edict VII deals with tolerance among all sects. The other three pairs are correct.
Source: PYQ-style

34. The Maski inscription (Karnataka) is historically important because it:
(a) Is the first royal inscription in Sanskrit (b) Mentions Ashoka by his personal name (c) Records the battle on the Narmada (d) Is the earliest Harappan seal
Answer: (b)
Solution: Most edicts call the king only 'Devanampiya Piyadassi'; the Maski minor rock edict uses the name 'Asoka', which helped identify the author of the edicts.
Source: PYQ-style

35. Which of the following Gupta-period temples is an early example of a stone temple with a shikhara?
(a) Kailasanatha Temple, Kanchi (b) Brihadeshwara Temple, Thanjavur (c) Dashavatara Temple, Deogarh (d) Shore Temple, Mamallapuram
Answer: (c)
Solution: The Dashavatara Temple at Deogarh (Uttar Pradesh) is a Gupta-period stone temple with an early shikhara. The other three are later Pallava and Chola temples of South India.
Source: PYQ-style

36. The earliest known inscriptional evidence of 'Sati' in India is found in the:
(a) Junagadh inscription of Rudradaman (b) Eran inscription of Bhanugupta (c) Aihole inscription of Pulakeshin II (d) Allahabad pillar inscription of Samudragupta
Answer: (b)
Solution: The Eran (Madhya Pradesh) inscription of the time of Bhanugupta (c. 510 CE) records that the wife of the general Goparaja burnt herself with her husband — the earliest inscriptional record of sati.
Source: PYQ-style

37. Consider the following statements about Nalanda:
I. The mahavihara is traditionally credited to the Gupta ruler Kumaragupta I.
II. Hiuen Tsang studied and taught at Nalanda.
III. It was destroyed around the end of the 12th century.
Which of the statements given is/are correct?
(a) I and II only (b) II and III only (c) I and III only (d) I, II and III
Answer: (d)
Solution: All three are correct: Kumaragupta I is credited with founding it, Hiuen Tsang spent years there in Harsha's time, and it was destroyed by the invading army of Bakhtiyar Khalji around the end of the 12th century.
Source: PYQ-style

38. The Great Stupa at Sanchi, first built in brick in Ashoka's time, was enlarged and given a stone casing during the period of the:
(a) Mauryas (b) Shungas (c) Kushanas (d) Guptas
Answer: (b)
Solution: The brick stupa of Ashoka's time was enlarged with stone in the Shunga period, and the gateways were added later. 'Mauryas' is the trap because the first stupa was Mauryan.
Source: NCERT Class 12 History (Themes in Indian History Part I), "Thinkers, Beliefs and Buildings"

39. Which of the following sites is NOT associated with Jainism?
(a) Shravanabelagola (b) Udayagiri-Khandagiri (c) Amaravati (d) Pavapuri
Answer: (c)
Solution: Amaravati (Andhra Pradesh) is famous for its Buddhist stupa. Shravanabelagola (Gomateshwara), Udayagiri-Khandagiri (Jain caves of Odisha) and Pavapuri (Mahavira's nirvana) are Jain sites.
Source: PYQ-style

40. The 'Mrichchhakatika' (The Little Clay Cart) was written by:
(a) Shudraka (b) Kalidasa (c) Bhasa (d) Vishakhadatta
Answer: (a)
Solution: Shudraka wrote Mrichchhakatika. Kalidasa wrote Abhijnanashakuntalam, Bhasa wrote Svapnavasavadatta, and Vishakhadatta wrote Mudrarakshasa.
Source: PYQ-style

41. Which one of the following is the correct order of the Haryanka rulers of Magadha?
(a) Bimbisara – Udayin – Ajatashatru (b) Bimbisara – Ajatashatru – Udayin (c) Ajatashatru – Bimbisara – Udayin (d) Udayin – Bimbisara – Ajatashatru
Answer: (b)
Solution: Bimbisara was followed by his son Ajatashatru, and then by Udayin, who is credited with making Pataliputra the capital.
Source: PYQ-style

42. The doctrine of 'Anekantavada' is a core idea of:
(a) Buddhism (b) The Ajivika sect (c) Jainism (d) Charvaka philosophy
Answer: (c)
Solution: Anekantavada (many-sidedness of truth) is a Jain doctrine; Syadvada follows from it. The Ajivikas are a tempting option because they too arose in the same age.
Source: PYQ-style

43. Which of the following statements about the Chalukya king Pulakeshin II is correct?
(a) He was defeated by Harsha. (b) He himself wrote the Aihole inscription. (c) His court poet was Banabhatta. (d) He stopped Harsha's advance into the Deccan.
Answer: (d)
Solution: Pulakeshin II defeated Harsha near the Narmada. The Aihole inscription was written by his court poet Ravikirti, and Banabhatta was Harsha's court poet.
Source: NCERT Class 6 History (Our Pasts-I), "New Empires and Kingdoms"

44. According to Buddhist tradition, Ashoka sent his son Mahendra (Mahinda) to spread Buddhism in:
(a) China (b) Myanmar (c) Afghanistan (d) Sri Lanka
Answer: (d)
Solution: Buddhist tradition says Mahendra (and Sanghamitra) took Buddhism to Sri Lanka. China and Myanmar received Buddhism through other routes and later monks.
Source: PYQ-style

45. The Kushana king Kanishka is traditionally associated with which era that began in 78 CE?
(a) Vikram Samvat (b) Shaka era (c) Gupta era (d) Harsha era
Answer: (b)
Solution: The Shaka era begins in 78 CE and is linked with Kanishka's accession; it is also the base of India's national calendar. Vikram Samvat begins in 57 BCE and the Gupta era in 319 CE.
Source: PYQ-style

46. In the Later Vedic period, the royal official called 'Bhagadugha' was the:
(a) Tax collector (b) Army chief (c) Royal priest (d) Charioteer
Answer: (a)
Solution: The Bhagadugha collected the 'bhaga', the king's share of produce. The charioteer was the 'suta' and the priest the 'purohita'.
Source: NCERT (old) Class 11 Ancient India, R.S. Sharma

47. Before Pataliputra, which city was the capital of the Magadha Mahajanapada?
(a) Vaishali (b) Champa (c) Rajgriha (d) Shravasti
Answer: (c)
Solution: Rajagriha (Rajgir) in Bihar was Magadha's capital for several years; later the capital shifted to Pataliputra. Vaishali was the Vajji capital and Shravasti the Kosala capital.
Source: NCERT Class 6 History (Our Pasts-I), "Kingdoms, Kings and an Early Republic"

48. The 'Jataka' tales are part of which Buddhist Pitaka?
(a) Vinaya Pitaka (b) Sutta Pitaka (c) Abhidhamma Pitaka (d) Jain Agamas
Answer: (b)
Solution: The Jatakas (stories of the Buddha's earlier births) belong to the Khuddaka Nikaya of the Sutta Pitaka. The Vinaya Pitaka has rules for monks.
Source: PYQ-style

49. Consider the following pairs of Harappan sites and present-day states:
I. Kalibangan – Rajasthan
II. Surkotada – Gujarat
III. Ropar – Haryana
IV. Alamgirpur – Uttar Pradesh
Which of the pairs given are correctly matched?
(a) I, II and III only (b) I, II and IV only (c) II and IV only (d) I, III and IV only
Answer: (b)
Solution: Ropar (Rupnagar) is in Punjab, not Haryana; the other three pairs are correct. Students often place Ropar in Haryana because Rakhigarhi and Banawali are there.
Source: PYQ-style

50. Sarnath is important in the life of the Buddha because there he:
(a) Was born (b) Attained enlightenment (c) Gave his first sermon (d) Attained mahaparinirvana
Answer: (c)
Solution: The Buddha gave his first sermon at Sarnath near Varanasi. He was born at Lumbini, attained enlightenment at Bodh Gaya and passed away at Kushinagar.
Source: NCERT Class 6 History (Our Pasts-I), "New Questions and Ideas"
