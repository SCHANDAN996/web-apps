101. The earliest evidence of settled agriculture in the Indian subcontinent comes from:
(A) Burzahom
(B) Chirand
(C) Mehrgarh
(D) Inamgaon

Answer: (C)
Solution: Mehrgarh (Balochistan, Pakistan), dating to c. 7000 BCE, has yielded the earliest evidence of wheat and barley cultivation and domestication of sheep and goat. Burzahom is Neolithic but later; Chirand and Inamgaon are also later sites.
Source: PYQ-style

102. The ‘Pashupati seal’ discovered at Mohenjodaro depicts:
(A) A horned deity surrounded by animals
(B) A king in a chariot
(C) A mother goddess with a child
(D) A priest performing a fire sacrifice

Answer: (A)
Solution: The Pashupati seal shows a three-faced horned figure seated in a yogic posture with animals around, often interpreted as a proto-Shiva.
Source: PYQ-style

103. Which of the following is NOT a feature of the Indus Valley Civilization?
(A) Grid-pattern town planning
(B) Underground drainage system
(C) Use of iron implements
(D) Baked brick structures

Answer: (C)
Solution: The Harappans used copper and bronze; iron was unknown to them. The other features are hallmarks of the civilization.
Source: PYQ-style

104. In the Later Vedic period, which of the following became the most important sacrifice associated with claims to imperial sovereignty?
(A) Rajasuya
(B) Vajapeya
(C) Ashvamedha
(D) Agnishtoma

Answer: (C)
Solution: The Ashvamedha (horse sacrifice) was performed by a king to assert his suzerainty over other rulers. Rajasuya was the coronation; Vajapeya was a chariot race sacrifice.
Source: PYQ-style

105. The ‘Ganapati’ or ‘Ganas’ chief mentioned in the Vedic literature refers to:
(A) The commander of the army
(B) The head of a republican clan
(C) The chief priest
(D) The village headman

Answer: (B)
Solution: In the context of the ganasanghas (republics), Ganapati or Ganaraja was the head of the clan assembly, an elective position.
Source: PYQ-style

106. Which of the following rulers convened the Second Buddhist Council?
(A) Ajatashatru
(B) Kalashoka
(C) Ashoka
(D) Kanishka

Answer: (B)
Solution: The Second Council was held at Vaishali under the patronage of King Kalashoka, about 100 years after the Buddha’s mahaparinirvana.
Source: PYQ-style

107. Which Ashokan edict provides the earliest reference to the Andhra people?
(A) Major Rock Edict I
(B) Major Rock Edict V
(C) Major Rock Edict XIII
(D) Major Rock Edict II

Answer: (C)
Solution: Major Rock Edict XIII, which describes the Kalinga war and Ashoka’s Dhamma missions, mentions the Andhras among the peoples on the empire’s borders.
Source: PYQ-style

108. The ‘Milinda Panha’ is a conversation between the Indo-Greek king Menander and the Buddhist monk:
(A) Moggaliputta Tissa
(B) Nagasena
(C) Upagupta
(D) Buddhaghosa

Answer: (B)
Solution: The Milinda Panha (Questions of Milinda) records the dialogue between King Milinda (Menander) and the elder Nagasena on Buddhist doctrine.
Source: PYQ-style

109. Which of the following Gupta-period authors wrote the play ‘Mudrarakshasa’?
(A) Kalidasa
(B) Vishakhadatta
(C) Shudraka
(D) Bharavi

Answer: (B)
Solution: Vishakhadatta’s ‘Mudrarakshasa’ is a political drama about the rise of Chandragupta Maurya and Chanakya’s stratagems. Kalidasa wrote ‘Abhijnanashakuntalam’, Shudraka ‘Mrichchhakatika’.
Source: PYQ-style

110. Consider the following statements about the Satavahanas:

They issued lead and potin coins.

The official language was Sanskrit.

Gautamiputra Satakarni defeated the Shaka Kshatrapa Nahapana.
Which of the statements given above is/are correct?
(A) 1 and 2 only
(B) 2 and 3 only
(C) 1 and 3 only
(D) 1, 2 and 3

Answer: (C)
Solution: Statement 2 is false – the Satavahanas used Prakrit as their official language, not Sanskrit. They are known for coins of lead, potin, copper, and silver, and Gautamiputra Satakarni’s victory over Nahapana is historically recorded.
Source: PYQ-style
⚠️ Trap Type: Almost True Statement – statement 2 appears plausible because many dynasties later used Sanskrit, but the Satavahanas patronized Prakrit.

111. The ‘Kailashnath temple’ at Ellora was built by:
(A) Pulakeshin II
(B) Krishna I (Rashtrakuta)
(C) Mahendravarman I
(D) Rajendra Chola

Answer: (B)
Solution: The monolithic rock-cut Kailash temple (Cave 16) at Ellora was commissioned by Rashtrakuta king Krishna I in the 8th century.
Source: PYQ-style

112. The earliest known specimen of Indian sculpture representing a deity is:
(A) The Pashupati seal
(B) The bronze dancing girl
(C) The stone torso from Harappa
(D) The terracotta mother goddess from Mehrgarh

Answer: (D)
Solution: Terracotta figurines of the mother goddess found at Mehrgarh (c. 3000 BCE) are among the earliest representations of a deity in the Indian subcontinent.
Source: PYQ-style

113. With reference to ancient Indian history, the term ‘Nishka’ in the Vedic period referred to:
(A) A gold ornament or coin used as a medium of exchange
(B) A type of chariot
(C) A class of priests
(D) A land measure

Answer: (A)
Solution: Nishka was a gold ornament or a piece of gold used as a unit of value in the Rigvedic period, an early form of currency.
Source: PYQ-style

114. The ‘Gandhara school of art’ flourished during the period of:
(A) Mauryas
(B) Shungas
(C) Kushanas
(D) Guptas

Answer: (C)
Solution: The Gandhara school, blending Greco-Roman and Indian styles, reached its peak under the Kushanas, especially Kanishka.
Source: PYQ-style

115. Which of the following Gupta rulers issued the highest number of gold coins?
(A) Samudragupta
(B) Chandragupta II
(C) Kumaragupta I
(D) Skandagupta

Answer: (A)
Solution: Samudragupta issued a large variety of gold coins depicting his military achievements (e.g., the veena-player type, ashvamedha type). While the Kushanas as a whole issued more gold coins than the Guptas, among Gupta kings, Samudragupta’s gold coinage is the most prolific.
Source: PYQ-style

116. The ‘Lion Capital’ at Sarnath originally consisted of:
(A) Four lions standing back to back with a Dharmachakra and animal figures on the abacus
(B) A single lion seated facing east
(C) A bull and a horse below two lions
(D) An elephant and a lion below a lotus

Answer: (A)
Solution: The Sarnath pillar of Ashoka had a capital with four addorsed lions, a Dharmachakra (wheel) originally on top, and an abacus with a horse, bull, elephant, and lion.
Source: PYQ-style

117. The famous ‘Aihole inscription’ of Pulakeshin II was written by:
(A) Harishena
(B) Ravikirti
(C) Banabhatta
(D) Kalidasa

Answer: (B)
Solution: Ravikirti, a Jain poet in Pulakeshin II’s court, composed the Aihole prashasti.
Source: PYQ-style

118. Consider the following statements about the ‘Ajivika’ sect:

It was founded by Makkhali Gosala.

It believed in absolute determinism (Niyati).

Ashoka and his grandson Dasharatha dedicated caves to the Ajivikas.
Which of the statements given above are correct?
(A) 1 and 2 only
(B) 2 and 3 only
(C) 1 and 3 only
(D) 1, 2 and 3

Answer: (D)
Solution: All three statements are correct. The Ajivikas, founded by Makkhali Gosala, held that everything is pre-determined. The Barabar and Nagarjuni hills caves were dedicated to them by Ashoka and Dasharatha.
Source: PYQ-style

119. The ‘Kharoshthi’ script was used predominantly in which region during Ashoka’s time?
(A) Eastern India
(B) Southern India
(C) North-western India
(D) Central India

Answer: (C)
Solution: Ashoka’s inscriptions in the north-western frontier (modern Pakistan, Afghanistan) were written in Kharoshthi, a script written from right to left.
Source: PYQ-style

120. Who among the following was the founder of the Sunga dynasty?
(A) Agnimitra
(B) Pushyamitra Sunga
(C) Vasudeva Kanva
(D) Simuka

Answer: (B)
Solution: Pushyamitra Sunga, who was the commander-in-chief of the last Maurya ruler Brihadratha, killed him and established the Sunga dynasty.
Source: PYQ-style

121. (Hard) With reference to ancient Indian history, consider the following pairs of term and its meaning:

Bhagadugha : Collector of taxes

Sangrihitri : Treasurer

Suta : Charioteer and court bard

Kshattri : Chamberlain
Which of the pairs given above are correctly matched?
(A) 1 and 2 only
(B) 2, 3 and 4 only
(C) 1, 3 and 4 only
(D) 1, 2, 3 and 4

Answer: (D)
Solution: All four are correctly matched. In the Later Vedic administration, Bhagadugha collected the Bhaga share, Sangrihitri was the treasurer, Suta was charioteer and court minstrel, and Kshattri served as chamberlain.
Source: PYQ-style

122. (Hard) The ‘Mundaka Upanishad’ is associated with which Veda?
(A) Rig Veda
(B) Sama Veda
(C) Atharva Veda
(D) Yajur Veda

Answer: (C)
Solution: The Mundaka Upanishad, from which the motto ‘Satyameva Jayate’ is taken, belongs to the Atharva Veda.
Source: PYQ-style
⚠️ Trap Type: Similar Name – many associate the Upanishads generally with the Yajur Veda, but specific Upanishads belong to different Vedas.

123. (Hard) Consider the following statements about the Indus Valley Civilization script:

The script was pictographic.

It was written in the boustrophedon style.

It has been deciphered through bilingual inscriptions.
Which of the statements given above is/are correct?
(A) 1 and 2 only
(B) 2 and 3 only
(C) 1 only
(D) None

Answer: (A)
Solution: Statements 1 and 2 are correct. The script remains undeciphered because no bilingual inscription (like the Rosetta Stone) has been found; statement 3 is false.
Source: PYQ-style
⚠️ Trap Type: Almost True Statement – many candidates incorrectly believe the script has been deciphered or that bilinguals exist.

124. (Hard) Which of the following Puranas is known for containing the most detailed historical (vamsanucharita) section on ancient dynasties?
(A) Bhagavata Purana
(B) Vishnu Purana
(C) Vayu Purana
(D) Matsya Purana

Answer: (C)
Solution: The Vayu Purana (and the closely related Brahmanda Purana) provides the most systematic list of ancient dynasties including the Mauryas, Sungas, and Guptas. While Vishnu Purana also has genealogy, the Vayu Purana is specifically noted for its historical content.
Source: PYQ-style

125. (Hard) The ‘Mrichchhakatika’ (The Little Clay Cart) gives a vivid picture of urban society during which period?
(A) Vedic age
(B) Mauryan age
(C) Gupta age
(D) Post-Mauryan age (Sunga-Kushana period)

Answer: (D)
Solution: Though the author Shudraka’s date is uncertain, the play’s description of cities like Ujjayini, flourishing trade, and a mature monetary economy aligns best with the post-Mauryan period (Sunga-Kushana). It is not typically placed in the Gupta or Mauryan age.
Source: PYQ-style
