101. The earliest evidence of settled agriculture in the Indian subcontinent comes from:
(a) Mehrgarh (b) Chirand (c) Burzahom (d) Inamgaon
Answer: (a)
Solution: Mehrgarh (Balochistan), dating to c. 7000 BCE, has yielded the earliest evidence of wheat and barley cultivation and animal domestication; Burzahom, Chirand and Inamgaon are later Neolithic/Chalcolithic sites, so the answer is Mehrgarh.
Source: PYQ-style

102. The ‘Pashupati seal’ discovered at Mohenjodaro depicts:
(a) A king in a chariot (b) A horned deity surrounded by animals (c) A mother goddess with a child (d) A priest performing a fire sacrifice
Answer: (b)
Solution: The seal shows a horned figure in yogic posture with animals around, often interpreted as proto-Shiva; the mother goddess is a tempting option because terracotta mother goddess figurines are also found at Harappan sites, but the Pashupati seal is the horned deity, so the answer is the horned deity surrounded by animals.
Source: PYQ-style

103. Which of the following is NOT a feature of the Indus Valley Civilization?
(a) Grid-pattern town planning (b) Underground drainage system (c) Baked brick structures (d) Use of iron implements
Answer: (d)
Solution: The Harappans used copper and bronze; iron was unknown to them, so the answer is use of iron implements.
Source: PYQ-style

104. In the Later Vedic period, which sacrifice became associated with claims to imperial sovereignty?
(a) Rajasuya (b) Ashvamedha (c) Vajapeya (d) Agnishtoma
Answer: (b)
Solution: The Ashvamedha (horse sacrifice) asserted suzerainty over other rulers; Rajasuya was the coronation and Vajapeya a chariot-race sacrifice, so the answer is Ashvamedha.
Source: PYQ-style

105. The ‘Ganapati’ mentioned in Vedic literature in the context of ganasanghas refers to:
(a) The commander of the army (b) The village headman (c) The chief priest (d) The head of a republican clan
Answer: (d)
Solution: In the ganasanghas (republics), Ganapati or Ganaraja was the elective head of the clan assembly, so the answer is the head of a republican clan.
Source: PYQ-style

106. Which ruler convened the Second Buddhist Council at Vaishali?
(a) Ajatashatru (b) Kanishka (c) Ashoka (d) Kalashoka
Answer: (d)
Solution: The Second Council was held at Vaishali under King Kalashoka, about 100 years after the Buddha's mahaparinirvana; Ajatashatru patronised the First Council, so the answer is Kalashoka.
Source: PYQ-style

107. Which Ashokan edict provides the earliest reference to the Andhra people?
(a) Major Rock Edict I (b) Major Rock Edict XIII (c) Major Rock Edict V (d) Major Rock Edict II
Answer: (b)
Solution: Major Rock Edict XIII, describing the Kalinga war and Dhamma missions, mentions the Andhras among the peoples on the empire's borders, so the answer is Major Rock Edict XIII.
Source: PYQ-style

108. The ‘Milinda Panha’ is a conversation between the Indo-Greek king Menander and the Buddhist monk:
(a) Moggaliputta Tissa (b) Upagupta (c) Nagasena (d) Buddhaghosa
Answer: (c)
Solution: The Milinda Panha records the dialogue between King Milinda (Menander) and the elder Nagasena; Moggaliputta Tissa was Ashoka's teacher, so the answer is Nagasena.
Source: PYQ-style

109. Who wrote the play ‘Mudrarakshasa’?
(a) Kalidasa (b) Bharavi (c) Shudraka (d) Vishakhadatta
Answer: (d)
Solution: Vishakhadatta's ‘Mudrarakshasa’ is a political drama about Chandragupta Maurya and Chanakya; Kalidasa wrote ‘Abhijnanashakuntalam’, so the answer is Vishakhadatta.
Source: PYQ-style

110. Consider the following statements about the Satavahanas:
1. They issued lead and potin coins.
2. The official language was Sanskrit.
3. Gautamiputra Satakarni defeated the Shaka Kshatrapa Nahapana.
Which of the statements given above is/are correct?
(a) 1 and 3 only (b) 2 and 3 only (c) 1 and 2 only (d) 1, 2 and 3
Answer: (a)
Solution: Statement 2 is false — the Satavahanas used Prakrit, not Sanskrit; they issued lead and potin coins and Gautamiputra Satakarni defeated Nahapana, so the answer is 1 and 3 only.
Source: PYQ-style

111. The ‘Kailashnath temple’ at Ellora was built by:
(a) Krishna I (b) Pulakeshin II (c) Mahendravarman I (d) Rajendra Chola
Answer: (a)
Solution: The monolithic rock-cut Kailash temple (Cave 16) at Ellora was commissioned by Rashtrakuta king Krishna I in the 8th century, so the answer is Krishna I.
Source: PYQ-style

112. The earliest known specimen of Indian sculpture representing a deity is:
(a) The terracotta mother goddess from Mehrgarh (b) The bronze dancing girl (c) The stone torso from Harappa (d) The Pashupati seal
Answer: (a)
Solution: Terracotta mother goddess figurines from Mehrgarh (c. 3000 BCE) are among the earliest deity representations; the Pashupati seal is a seal, not sculpture, so the answer is the terracotta mother goddess from Mehrgarh.
Source: PYQ-style

113. In the Vedic period, the term ‘Nishka’ referred to:
(a) A type of chariot (b) A gold ornament or piece of gold used as a unit of value (c) A class of priests (d) A land measure
Answer: (b)
Solution: Nishka was a gold ornament or piece of gold used as a unit of value in the Rigvedic period, so the answer is a gold ornament or piece of gold used as a unit of value.
Source: PYQ-style

114. The ‘Gandhara school of art’ flourished during the period of:
(a) Mauryas (b) Kushanas (c) Shungas (d) Guptas
Answer: (b)
Solution: The Gandhara school, blending Greco-Roman and Indian styles, reached its peak under the Kushanas, especially Kanishka, so the answer is Kushanas.
Source: PYQ-style

115. Which Gupta ruler issued the highest number of gold coins?
(a) Samudragupta (b) Chandragupta II (c) Kumaragupta I (d) Skandagupta
Answer: (c)
Solution: Kumaragupta I issued the largest number and variety of gold coins among Gupta rulers; Samudragupta's coins are more varied in types but Kumaragupta I's gold coinage is the most prolific, so the answer is Kumaragupta I.
Source: PYQ-style

116. The ‘Lion Capital’ at Sarnath originally consisted of:
(a) An elephant and a lion below a lotus (b) A single lion seated facing east (c) A bull and a horse below two lions (d) Four lions standing back to back with a Dharmachakra and animal figures on the abacus
Answer: (d)
Solution: The Sarnath capital had four addorsed lions, a Dharmachakra on top, and an abacus with horse, bull, elephant and lion, so the answer is four lions standing back to back with a Dharmachakra and animal figures on the abacus.
Source: PYQ-style

117. The ‘Aihole inscription’ of Pulakeshin II was written by:
(a) Ravikirti (b) Harishena (c) Banabhatta (d) Kalidasa
Answer: (a)
Solution: Ravikirti, a Jain poet in Pulakeshin II's court, composed the Aihole prashasti; Harishena wrote the Allahabad Pillar inscription of Samudragupta, so the answer is Ravikirti.
Source: PYQ-style

118. Consider the following statements about the ‘Ajivika’ sect:
1. It was founded by Makkhali Gosala.
2. It believed in absolute determinism (Niyati).
3. Ashoka and his grandson Dasharatha dedicated caves to the Ajivikas.
Which of the statements given above are correct?
(a) 1 and 2 only (b) 2 and 3 only (c) 1, 2 and 3 (d) 1 and 3 only
Answer: (c)
Solution: All three statements are correct — the Ajivikas were founded by Makkhali Gosala, believed in Niyati, and the Barabar and Nagarjuni caves were dedicated to them by Ashoka and Dasharatha, so the answer is 1, 2 and 3.
Source: PYQ-style

119. The ‘Kharoshthi’ script was used predominantly in which region during Ashoka's time?
(a) Eastern India (b) Southern India (c) North-western India (d) Central India
Answer: (c)
Solution: Ashoka's inscriptions in the north-western frontier were written in Kharoshthi, a right-to-left script, so the answer is north-western India.
Source: PYQ-style

120. Who was the founder of the Sunga dynasty?
(a) Agnimitra (b) Simuka (c) Vasudeva Kanva (d) Pushyamitra Sunga
Answer: (d)
Solution: Pushyamitra Sunga, commander-in-chief of the last Maurya ruler Brihadratha, killed him and established the Sunga dynasty, so the answer is Pushyamitra Sunga.
Source: PYQ-style

121. With reference to ancient Indian history, consider the following pairs:
1. Bhagadugha : Collector of taxes
2. Sangrihitri : Treasurer
3. Suta : Charioteer and court bard
4. Kshattri : Chamberlain
Which of the pairs given above are correctly matched?
(a) 1 and 2 only (b) 1, 2, 3 and 4 (c) 1, 3 and 4 only (d) 2, 3 and 4 only
Answer: (b)
Solution: All four are correctly matched in Later Vedic administration, so the answer is 1, 2, 3 and 4.
Source: PYQ-style

122. The ‘Mundaka Upanishad’ is associated with which Veda?
(a) Rig Veda (b) Sama Veda (c) Yajur Veda (d) Atharva Veda
Answer: (d)
Solution: The Mundaka Upanishad, from which ‘Satyameva Jayate’ is taken, belongs to the Atharva Veda, so the answer is Atharva Veda.
Source: PYQ-style

123. Consider the following statements about the Indus Valley Civilization script:
1. The script was pictographic.
2. It was written in the boustrophedon style.
3. It has been deciphered through bilingual inscriptions.
Which of the statements given above is/are correct?
(a) 1 only (b) 2 and 3 only (c) 1 and 2 only (d) 1, 2 and 3
Answer: (c)
Solution: Statements 1 and 2 are correct; the script remains undeciphered because no bilingual inscription has been found, so the answer is 1 and 2 only.
Source: PYQ-style

124. Which Purana is known for the most detailed vamsanucharita (dynastic) section on ancient dynasties?
(a) Bhagavata Purana (b) Vishnu Purana (c) Vayu Purana (d) Matsya Purana
Answer: (c)
Solution: The Vayu Purana provides the most systematic list of ancient dynasties including the Mauryas, Sungas and Guptas, so the answer is Vayu Purana.
Source: PYQ-style

125. The ‘Mrichchhakatika’ (The Little Clay Cart) gives a vivid picture of urban society during which period?
(a) Post-Mauryan age (Sunga-Kushana period) (b) Mauryan age (c) Gupta age (d) Vedic age
Answer: (a)
Solution: The play's description of cities like Ujjayini, flourishing trade and a mature monetary economy aligns best with the post-Mauryan period, so the answer is the post-Mauryan age (Sunga-Kushana period).
Source: PYQ-style
