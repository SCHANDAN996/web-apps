Flashcard Deck: Ancient Indian History (Foundation)

Card 1
Front: If you dug a pit in Kashmir 5,000 years ago, you might have called it home. Which Neolithic site is famous for pit-dwellings?
Back: Burzahom (Kashmir). People lived in underground pits.

Card 2
Front: A 4,500-year-old port with a dockyard — in Gujarat, not today’s Mumbai. Name this Harappan site.
Back: Lothal (Gujarat). Dockyard and rice husk discovered.

Card 3
Front: “Shyama Ayas” — does it sound like a dark metal? Which metal do later Vedic texts mean by this term?
Back: Iron. ‘Shyama ayas’ (or ‘krishna ayas’) literally means the dark/black metal; iron came into use in the Later Vedic age.

Card 4
Front: Bimbisara had a star doctor. What was his name, and why do exams love him?
Back: Jivaka. He was the royal physician to Bimbisara, often asked as a unique fact about the Haryanka dynasty.

Card 5
Front: Ashoka’s messages travelled far — but in the northwest they were written right-to-left. Which script was that?
Back: Kharoshthi. Used in Shahbazgarhi and Mansehra; most other edicts were in Brahmi (left-to-right).

Card 6
Front: Who is called the ‘Napoleon of India’, and on whose pillar inscription do we read his story?
Back: Samudragupta. His exploits are described in the Allahabad Pillar Inscription (Prayag Prashasti) composed by Harishena.

Card 7
Front: He defeated Seleucus, hosted Megasthenes, and had a shrewd advisor named Chanakya. Name the ruler.
Back: Chandragupta Maurya. Founder of the Maurya Empire (322 BCE).

Card 8
Front: Three Pitakas, but one was compiled a little late — at the Third Buddhist Council. Which Pitaka and which king?
Back: Abhidhamma Pitaka, added at the Third Council held at Pataliputra under Ashoka.

Card 9
Front: Kanishka’s council was not in Pataliputra. Where did the Fourth Buddhist Council take place?
Back: Kashmir (Kundalavana). Presided over by Vasumitra, it compiled the Mahavibhasha commentary.

Card 10
Front: What is the mnemonic to remember the order of Magadha dynasties before Maurya? (Haryanka, Shishunaga, Nanda)
Back: “Haryanka-Shishunaga-Nanda, Maurya later in command-a.” — A rhyming trick to keep them in correct chronological order.

Card 11
Front: Recall the mnemonic for the four Buddhist Councils' locations in sequence.
Back: R V P K — Rajgriha, Vaishali, Pataliputra, Kashmir. Think: “Ravi’s Very Pretty Kite” or in Hindi, “राजा वै पाक” (Raja ne pakaya).

Card 12
Front: Spot the trap: “Chandragupta Maurya was called ‘Ekarat’ (sole sovereign).” Is this statement safe?
Back: No, it’s a trap (Almost True Statement). The Puranas call Mahapadma Nanda, the first Nanda king, ‘Ekarat’. The Mauryas came after the Nandas and built the first empire over most of the subcontinent.

Card 13
Front: Spot the trap: “Gupta period is called Golden Age because of the highest number of gold coins issued.” What’s wrong?
Back: Trap: Reverse Causation. The Gupta age is ‘Golden’ because of cultural achievements (literature, art, science), not because of coins. NCERT: the first gold coins were issued by the Kushanas; some of the most spectacular gold coins were issued by the Guptas.

Card 14
Front: Harsha was undefeated until a Deccan king stopped him. Who, where, and which river?
Back: Pulakeshin II (Chalukya of Badami) defeated Harsha on the banks of the Narmada.

Card 15
Front: A Chinese visitor came, a Chinese visitor returned — Fa-hien and Hiuen Tsang. Match them to the right kings.
Back: Fa-hien → Chandragupta II (Vikramaditya) of Gupta dynasty. Hiuen Tsang → Harshavardhana of Vardhana dynasty.

Card 16
Front: Which satirical play did a Pallava king write? Bonus: what’s funny about a king writing comedy?
Back: Mattavilasa Prahasana by Mahendravarman I (Pallava). It mocks Buddhist monks and Kapalikas — a rare royal satire.

Card 17
Front: Name the first royal Sanskrit inscription. Who issued it?
Back: Junagadh Rock Inscription of Rudradaman I (Shaka Kshatrapa, c. 150 CE), the earliest long royal inscription in Sanskrit. Not a Gupta — this often appears as a “first Sanskrit inscription” trap.

Card 18
Front: Megasthenes wrote ‘Indica’ and saw a city managed by 6 committees. Which city and which empire?
Back: Pataliputra (Maurya Empire). The 6 committees handled different municipal functions.

Card 19
Front: The Great Bath, the Dancing Girl, and the Bearded Priest — all from one site. Which one?
Back: Mohenjodaro (Indus Valley Civilization).

Card 20
Front: You find a Harappan site with a stadium and a giant signboard with 10 large signs. Which site is this?
Back: Dholavira (Gujarat). Known for advanced water management and unique layout.
