📊 PYQ Pattern Analysis: Ancient Indian History

Exams this chapter serves: SSC GD, SSC MTS, RRB Group D, state police / constable exams, and the general-awareness part of other state-level tests.

Note: this is a pattern analysis — what kinds of questions these exams like to ask from this chapter and how they trap you. We do not give year-wise counts or percentages, because no reliable data for them exists. The model questions below are written in the exam style; their sources are the NCERT chapters they test.

1. What Exams Like to Ask (Sub-topics, in plain words)

Harappan sites and their finds — the most common one-liner: "Which site had the dockyard / Great Bath / ploughed field?" (Lothal, Mohenjodaro, Kalibangan, Dholavira, Rakhigarhi) and "In which state is site X?"

Stone Age sites — Bhimbetka (rock paintings), Burzahom (pit-dwellings), Mehrgarh (earliest farming).

Vedic age — the four Vedas and what each contains, Early vs Later Vedic changes (iron, settled farming, varna), Sabha and Samiti.

Magadha and the Mahajanapadas — 16 Mahajanapadas and their capitals, Vajji as a republic, the order Haryanka → Shishunaga → Nanda → Maurya.

Mauryas and Ashoka — Kalinga War, Dhamma, scripts and language of the edicts, Megasthenes and Indica.

Post-Mauryan dynasties — Kanishka (78 CE, Fourth Council), Rudradaman (Junagadh inscription), Satavahanas, Indo-Greek Menander.

Guptas and Harsha — "Napoleon of India", Prayag Prashasti and Harishena, Fa-hien and Hiuen Tsang, Aryabhata and Kalidasa, Harsha's defeat by Pulakeshin II.

Buddhism and Jainism — the four Buddhist councils (place, patron), the three Pitakas, Tirthankaras, Mahavira's life places, Triratna.

Title-and-author pairs — Harshacharita (Banabhatta), Aihole inscription (Ravikirti), Mattavilasa Prahasana (Mahendravarman I), Mudrarakshasa (Vishakhadatta).

2. Exam-wise Style (qualitative)

SSC GD / SSC MTS — short, direct one-liners: site–find, ruler–title, text–author. Speed matters.

RRB Group D — similar direct questions, often with "Which of the following is NOT …" phrasing and pairs of sites and states.

State police / constable exams — direct facts, with extra weight on sites and rulers connected to the home state (for example, Bihar: Pataliputra, Nalanda, Vaishali; Gujarat: Lothal, Dholavira, Junagadh).

Higher exams that use these same basics (CHSL, NTPC, CGL) add statement-based, pair-matching and "arrange in chronological order" questions.

3. Common Traps (in words)

Almost true statement — one word changed: "Chandragupta Maurya was called Ekarat" (it was Mahapadma Nanda).

Absolute words — "all", "only", "never": "All Ashokan edicts are in Brahmi" is false because the north-western ones are in Kharoshthi.

Chronological confusion — which council came first, Shunga before Kanva, Nanda before Maurya.

Similar names — Chandragupta Maurya, Chandragupta I and Chandragupta II; Fa-hien and Hiuen Tsang; Harishena and Ravikirti.

Wrong reason — Assertion–Reason pairs where both parts sound right but the reason is wrong (the Golden Age is about culture, not coins).

4. Top 10 High-Yield Model Questions with Solutions and Trap Analysis

Question 1
Which of the following Harappan sites is known for a dockyard?
(a) Dholavira (b) Lothal (c) Kalibangan (d) Surkotada
Answer: (b) Lothal
Trap Type: Similar Name — Dholavira (water management) and Lothal (dockyard) are both in Gujarat and often confused.
15/45-sec Rule: 15-second fact. Score maximizer.
Source: NCERT Class 12 History (Themes in Indian History Part I), "Bricks, Beads and Bones"

Question 2
Consider the following statements about Ashoka's edicts:
I. All Ashokan edicts are in the Brahmi script.
II. The language used in them is Sanskrit.
Which of the statements given is/are correct?
(a) Only I (b) Only II (c) Both I and II (d) Neither I nor II
Answer: (d) Neither I nor II
Explanation: Most edicts are in Brahmi, but the north-western ones are in Kharoshthi. The language is Prakrit, not Sanskrit.
Trap Type: Absolute word ("all") + Almost True Statement.
15/45-sec Rule: 45 seconds — check each statement separately.
Source: NCERT Class 6 History (Our Pasts-I), "Ashoka, the Emperor Who Gave Up War"

Question 3
Who among the following is known as the 'Napoleon of India'?
(a) Chandragupta Maurya (b) Samudragupta (c) Harsha (d) Skandagupta
Answer: (b) Samudragupta
Trap Type: Similar Name — students mix up Chandragupta Maurya (founder of the Mauryas) with the Gupta warrior-king Samudragupta.
15/45-sec Rule: 15-second fact.
Source: NCERT Class 6 History (Our Pasts-I), "New Empires and Kingdoms"

Question 4
In later Vedic texts, the term 'Shyama Ayas' (dark metal) refers to:
(a) Copper (b) Iron (c) Silver (d) Gold
Answer: (b) Iron
Trap Type: Chronological Confusion — copper was known much earlier; iron is the marker of the Later Vedic age.
15/45-sec Rule: 15-second fact.
Source: PYQ-style

Question 5
Which ruler was the patron of the Fourth Buddhist Council?
(a) Ashoka (b) Kanishka (c) Harsha (d) Chandragupta II
Answer: (b) Kanishka
Trap Type: Chronological Confusion — Ashoka was the patron of the Third Council; the Fourth was held in Kashmir under Kanishka.
15/45-sec Rule: 15-second fact; use the R-V-P-K mnemonic.
Source: PYQ-style

Question 6
Assertion (A): The Gupta period is called the Golden Age of ancient India.
Reason (R): The Gupta age is called golden because the Guptas were the first rulers in India to issue gold coins.
(a) Both A and R are true, and R is the correct explanation of A.
(b) Both A and R are true, but R is not the correct explanation of A.
(c) A is true, but R is false.
(d) A is false, but R is true.
Answer: (c)
Explanation: The Gupta age is called golden because of its achievements in literature, art and science. R is false: NCERT says the first gold coins were issued around the first century CE by the Kushanas.
Trap Type: Wrong reason (Reverse Causation).
15/45-sec Rule: 45 seconds — judge A and R separately, then the link.
Source: NCERT Class 12 History (Themes in Indian History Part I), "Kings, Farmers and Towns"

Question 7
Arrange the following dynasties in chronological order:
I. Shunga II. Nanda III. Maurya IV. Kanva
(a) II, III, I, IV (b) III, II, IV, I (c) II, I, III, IV (d) I, II, III, IV
Answer: (a) Nanda → Maurya → Shunga → Kanva
Trap Type: Chronological Confusion — Shunga and Kanva are often reversed.
15/45-sec Rule: 45 seconds — draw a quick timeline.
Source: PYQ-style

Question 8
The Junagadh rock inscription, an early long royal inscription in Sanskrit, belongs to:
(a) Ashoka (b) Samudragupta (c) Rudradaman I (d) Harsha
Answer: (c) Rudradaman I
Trap Type: Chronological Confusion — Ashoka's edicts are in Prakrit; the Gupta inscriptions in Sanskrit come later.
15/45-sec Rule: 15-second fact.
Source: PYQ-style

Question 9
Which Pallava ruler wrote the satirical play 'Mattavilasa Prahasana'?
(a) Narasimhavarman I (b) Mahendravarman I (c) Pulakeshin II (d) Simhavishnu
Answer: (b) Mahendravarman I
Trap Type: Similar Name — Narasimhavarman I is famous for Mamallapuram; Pulakeshin II was a Chalukya, not a Pallava.
15/45-sec Rule: 15-second fact.
Source: PYQ-style

Question 10
Consider the following pairs:
I. Bimbisara – Jivaka (royal physician)
II. Chandragupta Maurya – Megasthenes
III. Harsha – Fa-hien
Which of the pairs given is/are correctly matched?
(a) I and II only (b) II and III only (c) I and III only (d) I, II and III
Answer: (a) — Fa-hien came during the reign of Chandragupta II; Hiuen Tsang visited Harsha.
Trap Type: Similar Name — mixing the two Chinese pilgrims and their host kings.
15/45-sec Rule: 45 seconds — check every pair.
Source: NCERT Class 6 History (Our Pasts-I), "New Empires and Kingdoms"

5. How to Use This Analysis

Make your own two-column list: site → find, ruler → title, text → author. These are the quickest marks in the paper.

Every time you see "NOT", "all", "only" or "incorrectly matched", slow down — that is a 45-second question, not a 15-second one.

For chronology, learn the backbone first (Harappan → Vedic → Mahajanapadas → Nanda → Maurya → Shunga → Kanva → Kushana/Satavahana → Gupta → Harsha) and then hang rulers and events on it.
