4. Top 10 High-Yield Questions with Solutions and Trap Analysis

Question 1
Which of the following Harappan sites is known for a dockyard?
(A) Dholavira (B) Lothal (C) Kalibangan (D) Surkotada
Answer: (B) Lothal
Trap Type: Similar Name – Dholavira (water management) often confused with Lothal (dockyard).
15/45-sec Rule: 15-second fact. Direct recall, no analysis needed. Score maximizer.

Question 2
Consider the following statements about Ashoka’s edicts:

All Ashokan edicts are in Brahmi script.

The language used is Sanskrit.
Which of the above is/are correct?
(A) 1 only (B) 2 only (C) Both (D) None
Answer: (D) None
Explanation: Most edicts are in Brahmi, but north-western ones are in Kharoshthi. Language is Prakrit, not Sanskrit.
Trap Type: Negative Phrasing (use of "all") + Chronological Confusion (language). Statement 1 is an Almost True Statement.
15/45-sec Rule: 45-second analytical – need to recall script details and language, and evaluate the absolute "all."

Question 3
Who among the following is known as the ‘Napoleon of India’?
(A) Chandragupta Maurya (B) Samudragupta (C) Harsha (D) Skandagupta
Answer: (B) Samudragupta
Trap Type: Similar Name – Candidates often confuse Chandragupta Maurya (founder) with Samudragupta (warrior).
15/45-sec Rule: 15-second fact.

Question 4
The term ‘Shyam Ayas’ in Shatapatha Brahmana refers to:
(A) Copper (B) Iron (C) Silver (D) Gold
Answer: (B) Iron
Trap Type: Almost True Statement – may mislead by associating ‘Shyam’ with dark metal, but one must recall the exact text.
15/45-sec Rule: 15-second fact.

Question 5
Which one of the following rulers convened the Fourth Buddhist Council?
(A) Ashoka (B) Kanishka (C) Harsha (D) Chandragupta II
Answer: (B) Kanishka
Trap Type: Chronological Confusion – mixing councils and patrons; many think Ashoka (3rd council) also did the 4th.
15/45-sec Rule: 15-second fact; recall the mnemonic RVPK for location and corresponding king.

Question 6
Assertion (A): The Gupta period is called the Golden Age of ancient India.
Reason (R): The Guptas issued the highest number of gold coins.
Codes:
(A) Both A and R are true, and R is the correct explanation.
(B) Both A and R are true, but R is NOT the correct explanation.
(C) A is true but R is false.
(D) A is false but R is true.
Answer: (C)
Explanation: Gupta age is golden due to cultural achievements. The highest number of gold coins were issued by the Kushanas.
Trap Type: Reverse Causation – the reason given is factually wrong and the cause-effect is inverted.
15/45-sec Rule: 45-second analytical – must separate the two facts and assess the logical connection.

Question 7
Arrange the following dynasties in chronological order:

Shunga 2. Nanda 3. Maurya 4. Kanva
Select the correct answer using the codes:
(A) 2-3-1-4 (B) 3-2-4-1 (C) 2-1-3-4 (D) 1-2-3-4
Answer: (A) Nanda → Maurya → Shunga → Kanva
Trap Type: Chronological Confusion – many reverse Shunga and Kanva or place Nanda after Maurya.
15/45-sec Rule: 45-second analytical – requires clear timeline memory; best solved with a mental timeline.

Question 8
The first royal inscription in Sanskrit was issued by:
(A) Ashoka (B) Samudragupta (C) Rudradaman I (D) Harsha
Answer: (C) Rudradaman I (Junagadh inscription)
Trap Type: Chronological Confusion + Similar Name – Ashoka (Prakrit) and Guptas (later Sanskrit) often chosen wrongly.
15/45-sec Rule: 15-second fact.

Question 9
Which Pallava ruler authored the satirical play ‘Mattavilasa Prahasana’?
(A) Narasimhavarman I (B) Mahendravarman I (C) Pulakeshin II (D) Simhavishnu
Answer: (B) Mahendravarman I
Trap Type: Similar Name – Narasimhavarman I is famous for Mamallapuram, leading to mix-up.
15/45-sec Rule: 15-second fact.

Question 10
Consider the following pairs:

Bimbisara – Jivaka (royal physician)

Chandragupta Maurya – Megasthenes

Harsha – Fa-hien
Which of the above pairs is/are correctly matched?
(A) 1 and 2 only (B) 2 and 3 only (C) 1 and 3 only (D) 1, 2, and 3
Answer: (A) – Fa-hien visited Chandragupta II, not Harsha. Harsha was visited by Hiuen Tsang.
Trap Type: Similar Name – mixing the two Chinese pilgrims and their host kings.
15/45-sec Rule: 45-second analytical – must verify each pairing; one wrong match can lead to wrong option.

Note: The 15/45-second rule classification helps prioritise quick scoring (15s) and deeper thinking (45s) during the exam.
