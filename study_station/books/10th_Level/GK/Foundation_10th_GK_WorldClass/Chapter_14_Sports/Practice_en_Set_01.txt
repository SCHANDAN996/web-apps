Questions 1–20: Easy | Questions 21–25: Medium

1. How many players does a cricket team have on the field?
(a) 11 (b) 10 (c) 9 (d) 12
Answer: (a)
Solution: Cricket, football and hockey are all played with the same team size on the field. Each side in a cricket match fields 11 players.
Source: Laws of Cricket (MCC)

2. How many players of one team are on the court in volleyball?
(a) 6 (b) 5 (c) 7 (d) 9
Answer: (a)
Solution: Trap: basketball has a smaller team and kabaddi a bigger one, so do not mix the three court games. In volleyball each team has 6 players on the court.
Source: PYQ-style

3. In kabaddi, how many players of each team are on the court?
(a) 7 (b) 9 (c) 6 (d) 5
Answer: (a)
Solution: Kabaddi is an Indian contact game in which a raider enters the opponents' half. Trap: kho-kho has more players on the field. In kabaddi each side has 7 players on court.
Source: PYQ-style

4. The Durand Cup is associated with which sport?
(a) Football (b) Hockey (c) Cricket (d) Badminton
Answer: (a)
Solution: The Durand Cup, started in 1888 at Shimla, is India's oldest football tournament. Trap: the Beighton Cup, which sounds similar, is a hockey tournament.
Source: PYQ-style

5. The Ranji Trophy is associated with:
(a) Cricket (b) Football (c) Tennis (d) Hockey
Answer: (a)
Solution: The Ranji Trophy is India's national first-class cricket championship, named after K. S. Ranjitsinhji. The Santosh Trophy is the football championship of state teams.
Source: PYQ-style

6. The Thomas Cup is associated with which sport?
(a) Badminton (b) Table tennis (c) Tennis (d) Golf
Answer: (a)
Solution: The Thomas Cup is the world men's team championship of badminton. Trap: the Davis Cup is tennis and the Swaythling Cup is table tennis.
Source: PYQ-style

7. The term "googly" is used in:
(a) Hockey (b) Cricket (c) Golf (d) Polo
Answer: (b)
Solution: A googly is a ball bowled by a wrist spinner that turns the opposite way from the batter's expectation. It is a cricket term.
Source: PYQ-style

8. "Checkmate" is a term used in:
(a) Carrom (b) Chess (c) Bridge (d) Billiards
Answer: (b)
Solution: Checkmate ends a game of chess, when the king is under attack and cannot escape. Stalemate and castling are other chess terms.
Source: FIDE Laws of Chess

9. National Sports Day is celebrated in India on:
(a) 23 June (b) 29 August (c) 15 August (d) 5 September
Answer: (b)
Solution: The day marks the birth anniversary of hockey wizard Major Dhyan Chand. Trap: Olympic Day falls in June. National Sports Day is on 29 August.
Source: Ministry of Youth Affairs & Sports

10. Major Dhyan Chand is famous for which sport?
(a) Cricket (b) Hockey (c) Football (d) Athletics
Answer: (b)
Solution: Dhyan Chand, called "The Wizard", helped India win Olympic hockey gold in 1928, 1932 and 1936. India's highest sports award is named after him.
Source: Olympics.com

11. The first modern Olympic Games were held in 1896 at:
(a) Paris (b) Athens (c) London (d) Rome
Answer: (b)
Solution: The modern Olympics began at Athens, Greece, in 1896 through the efforts of Pierre de Coubertin. Trap: Paris hosted the second Games in 1900, the first with women athletes.
Source: Olympics.com

12. How many rings are there in the Olympic symbol?
(a) 4 (b) 5 (c) 7 (d) 6
Answer: (b)
Solution: The Olympic symbol has interlocking rings in blue, yellow, black, green and red, standing for the union of the continents. The symbol has 5 rings.
Source: Olympics.com

13. Which is the highest sporting honour of India?
(a) Arjuna Award (b) Major Dhyan Chand Khel Ratna (c) Dronacharya Award (d) Padma Shri
Answer: (b)
Solution: The Major Dhyan Chand Khel Ratna (started 1991–92) is India's highest sporting honour. Trap: the Arjuna Award is older (1961) but ranks below it; the Padma Shri is a civilian award, not a sports award.
Source: Ministry of Youth Affairs & Sports

14. The Dronacharya Award is given to:
(a) Universities (b) Players (c) Coaches (d) Sports journalists
Answer: (c)
Solution: The Dronacharya Award (1985) honours coaches who produce medal winners — Dronacharya was the teacher of Arjuna in the Mahabharata. The Arjuna Award is for players.
Source: Ministry of Youth Affairs & Sports

15. The Wimbledon tennis championship is played on which surface?
(a) Hard court (b) Clay (c) Grass (d) Carpet
Answer: (c)
Solution: Wimbledon (London), the oldest Grand Slam (1877), is played on grass. Trap: the French Open is played on clay.
Source: PYQ-style

16. Eden Gardens cricket stadium is located in:
(a) Mumbai (b) Chennai (c) Kolkata (d) Delhi
Answer: (c)
Solution: Eden Gardens is in Kolkata. Mumbai has the Wankhede Stadium, Chennai has Chepauk (M. A. Chidambaram) and Delhi has the Arun Jaitley Stadium.
Source: PYQ-style

17. The first Asian Games were held in 1951 at:
(a) Tokyo (b) Manila (c) New Delhi (d) Beijing
Answer: (c)
Solution: The first Asian Games were held in New Delhi in 1951. Trap: Manila and Tokyo hosted the next two editions.
Source: Olympic Council of Asia

18. In which year did India win the Cricket World Cup (ODI) for the first time?
(a) 1975 (b) 1987 (c) 1983 (d) 2011
Answer: (c)
Solution: Trap: the win under M. S. Dhoni was India's second title. India, led by Kapil Dev, beat West Indies at Lord's to win its first World Cup in 1983.
Source: ICC (icc-cricket.com)

19. Abhinav Bindra is associated with which sport?
(a) Boxing (b) Archery (c) Shooting (d) Wrestling
Answer: (c)
Solution: Abhinav Bindra won gold in the 10 m air rifle event at the 2008 Beijing Olympics — India's first individual Olympic gold.
Source: Olympics.com

20. M. C. Mary Kom is famous for which sport?
(a) Wrestling (b) Weightlifting (c) Badminton (d) Boxing
Answer: (d)
Solution: Mary Kom of Manipur is a boxer, a six-time world champion and bronze medallist at the 2012 London Olympics.
Source: Olympics.com

21. Which of the following pairs is NOT correctly matched?
(a) Uber Cup – Badminton (b) Davis Cup – Tennis (c) Ranji Trophy – Cricket (d) Beighton Cup – Football
Answer: (d)
Solution: The Beighton Cup is one of the oldest hockey tournaments (Kolkata), so pairing it with football is wrong. Trap: the similar-sounding Durand Cup is the football one.
Source: PYQ-style

22. Who won India's first individual Olympic gold medal?
(a) Rajyavardhan Singh Rathore (b) Neeraj Chopra (c) Sushil Kumar (d) Abhinav Bindra
Answer: (d)
Solution: Abhinav Bindra won 10 m air rifle gold at Beijing 2008. Trap (almost true): Neeraj Chopra won India's first athletics gold (Tokyo 2020, held in 2021), not the first individual gold; Rathore won silver in 2004.
Source: Olympics.com

23. Consider the following statements:
I. The Government of India has officially declared hockey as the national game.
II. India has won Olympic gold in hockey eight times.
Which of the statements given is/are correct?
(a) Only I (b) Neither I nor II (c) Both I and II (d) Only II
Answer: (d)
Solution: In an RTI reply (January 2020) the Sports Ministry said no sport has been declared the national game, so statement I is wrong. India won hockey gold in 1928, 1932, 1936, 1948, 1952, 1956, 1964 and 1980 — eight times.
Source: Ministry of Youth Affairs & Sports (RTI reply, 2020)

24. Arrange the following events in the order in which their first edition was held:
I. Modern Olympic Games
II. Asian Games
III. Commonwealth Games
IV. Cricket World Cup (ODI)
(a) I, II, III, IV (b) I, III, IV, II (c) III, I, II, IV (d) I, III, II, IV
Answer: (d)
Solution: Modern Olympics 1896 (Athens), Commonwealth Games 1930 (Hamilton), Asian Games 1951 (New Delhi), Cricket World Cup 1975 (England). Trap: the Asian Games feel "older" than the Commonwealth Games, but they came 21 years later.
Source: Olympics.com; Olympic Council of Asia

25. India has hosted the Asian Games in which years?
(a) 1962 and 1982 (b) 1951 and 2010 (c) 1982 and 2010 (d) 1951 and 1982
Answer: (d)
Solution: New Delhi hosted the first Asian Games in 1951 and the ninth in 1982 (mascot Appu). Trap: 2010 in Delhi was the Commonwealth Games, not the Asian Games.
Source: Olympic Council of Asia
