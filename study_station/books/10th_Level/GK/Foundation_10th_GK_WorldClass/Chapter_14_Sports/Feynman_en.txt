🧒 Feynman Corner: Khel Ratna, Arjuna, Dronacharya — who gets which award?

This is the fact that confuses students most in this chapter. The names sound grand and similar, the years are close, and one award even changed its name in 2021. Let's make it simple.

The daily-life example: your school's sports day

Imagine your school holds a big sports day at the end of the year. The principal wants to say "well done" to different people:

The one champion who did something truly special this year — say, won a national gold — gets the biggest trophy on the stage. That is the Khel Ratna, India's highest sporting honour (now named after Major Dhyan Chand).

Students who played well again and again over the years get a smaller, but still proud, medal. That is the Arjuna Award — for consistent good performance. In the Mahabharata, Arjuna was the best pupil, so this award is for players.

The PT teacher who trained all those winners gets thanked too. That is the Dronacharya Award — for coaches. In the Mahabharata, Dronacharya was Arjuna's teacher.

The old teacher who has served the school for thirty years gets a "thank you for a lifetime" award. That is the lifetime award (the Dhyan Chand Award from 2002; given as the Arjuna Award (Lifetime) in the National Sports Awards 2024).

And the school house (team) that did best overall gets the rolling trophy. In real life, that is the Maulana Abul Kalam Azad (MAKA) Trophy, which goes to the best university, not to a player.

The unforgettable trick

"Guru gets Drona, Shishya gets Arjuna, the Ratna goes to the best, the University gets MAKA."

And for the years, remember the order they were born: Arjuna (1961) → Dronacharya (1985) → Khel Ratna (1991–92). The pupil's award came first, the teacher's next, the jewel last. The jewel changed its name on 6 August 2021 — from Rajiv Gandhi Khel Ratna to Major Dhyan Chand Khel Ratna — but it is still the same highest award, and its first winner was Viswanathan Anand.

Blurting Sheet

Now, without looking up, write down everything you remember about this concept: the name of each award, who receives it (player, coach, veteran, university, organisation), the year each started, the old and new name of the Khel Ratna, and its first winner.

Then check: Did you write "coach" for Dronacharya? Did you write "university" for MAKA? Did you put Arjuna before Khel Ratna in time?

Question for you: If a coach and her pupil are both honoured in the same year, which two awards will they receive — and which of the two awards is older?
