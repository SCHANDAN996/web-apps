📊 PYQ Pattern Analysis: Sports & Competitions

Exams this chapter serves: SSC GD, SSC MTS, RRB Group D, state police / constable exams, and the general-awareness part of other state-level tests.

Note: this is a pattern analysis — what kinds of questions these exams like to ask from this chapter and how they trap you. We do not give year-wise counts or percentages, because no reliable data for them exists. The model questions below are written in the exam style; their sources are the official bodies (Olympics.com, Ministry of Youth Affairs & Sports, ICC, FIFA) or "PYQ-style".

1. What Exams Like to Ask (Sub-topics, in plain words)

Cups and trophies — the most common one-liner: "The Santosh Trophy is associated with which sport?" (Durand, Subroto, Beighton, Aga Khan, Ranji, Irani, Thomas, Uber, Davis, Swaythling).

Terms of games — "Googly", "chukker", "deuce", "snatch", "half nelson", "penalty corner": name the game.

Number of players — polo, kabaddi, volleyball, basketball, kho-kho, water polo.

National sports awards — which award is for coaches, which is the highest, when each started, the 2021 renaming of the Khel Ratna, and recent Khel Ratna winners with the year.

Olympics — first modern Games, IOC headquarters, rings and motto, India's landmark medals (Pritchard, hockey golds, Jadhav, Malleswari, Bindra, Neeraj), medal counts of recent Games.

Asian Games and Commonwealth Games — first edition and host city, India as host (1951, 1982; 2010), mascots, recent medal tallies.

World Cups — first winners (West Indies 1975, Uruguay 1930), India's titles with captain and year.

Stadiums and cities, bodies and headquarters, nicknames and autobiographies, National Sports Day.

2. Exam-wise Style (qualitative)

SSC GD / SSC MTS — short, direct one-liners: trophy–sport, term–sport, award–purpose, stadium–city. Speed matters.

RRB Group D — similar direct questions, often "Which of the following is NOT related to …" and pair-matching.

State police / constable exams — direct facts plus extra weight on the home state's sportspersons, stadiums and recent events.

Higher exams that use these same basics (CHSL, NTPC, CGL) add statement-based questions on recent events (with years) and "arrange in order" questions.

3. Top 10 High-Yield Model Questions with Solutions and Trap Analysis

Question 1
The Uber Cup is a world team championship for:
(a) Men's badminton (b) Women's badminton (c) Women's tennis (d) Mixed badminton
Answer: (b) Women's badminton
Trap Type: Similar Name — the Thomas Cup is for men and the Sudirman Cup is mixed; all three are badminton.
15/45-sec Rule: 15-second fact. Score maximizer.
Source: PYQ-style

Question 2
Which of the following awards is given to sports coaches in India?
(a) Arjuna Award (b) Dronacharya Award (c) Major Dhyan Chand Khel Ratna (d) Maulana Abul Kalam Azad Trophy
Answer: (b) Dronacharya Award
Trap Type: Similar Name — Arjuna (the pupil) is for players; MAKA is for a university.
15/45-sec Rule: 15-second fact.
Source: Ministry of Youth Affairs & Sports

Question 3
Who won independent India's first individual Olympic medal?
(a) Milkha Singh (b) K. D. Jadhav (c) Leander Paes (d) Karnam Malleswari
Answer: (b) K. D. Jadhav (bronze, wrestling, Helsinki 1952)
Trap Type: Almost True Statement — Milkha Singh was a famous Olympian but finished fourth in 1960 and won no Olympic medal.
15/45-sec Rule: 15-second fact.
Source: Olympics.com

Question 4
Which of the following terms is NOT associated with cricket?
(a) Googly (b) Maiden over (c) Penalty corner (d) Yorker
Answer: (c) Penalty corner
Trap Type: Negative Phrasing — three options are cricket words; a student who misses "NOT" picks the first familiar one.
15/45-sec Rule: 45 seconds — read the question twice, then tick each option against cricket.
Source: PYQ-style

Question 5
Arrange the following Olympic medal wins of India in chronological order:
I. Abhinav Bindra – gold II. K. D. Jadhav – bronze III. Karnam Malleswari – bronze IV. Neeraj Chopra – gold
(a) II, III, I, IV (b) II, I, III, IV (c) III, II, I, IV (d) II, III, IV, I
Answer: (a) Jadhav (1952), Malleswari (2000), Bindra (2008), Neeraj (2021)
Trap Type: Chronological Confusion — Malleswari (2000) and Bindra (2008) are close in time and often swapped.
15/45-sec Rule: 45-second analytical question. Write the year beside each name first.
Source: Olympics.com

Question 6
The first Asian Games were held in 1951 at:
(a) Tokyo (b) New Delhi (c) Manila (d) Jakarta
Answer: (b) New Delhi
Trap Type: Chronological Confusion — Manila (1954) and Tokyo (1958) hosted the next two Asian Games, so both look "early" enough to be first.
15/45-sec Rule: 15-second fact. Score maximizer.
Source: Olympic Council of Asia

Question 7
Consider the following statements:
I. The Major Dhyan Chand Khel Ratna was earlier called the Rajiv Gandhi Khel Ratna.
II. The Khel Ratna is older than the Arjuna Award.
Which of the statements given is/are correct?
(a) Only I (b) Only II (c) Both I and II (d) Neither I nor II
Answer: (a) Only I
Trap Type: Chronological Confusion — the "highest" award sounds as if it must be the oldest, but Arjuna started in 1961 and the Khel Ratna in 1991–92.
15/45-sec Rule: 45 seconds — check each statement separately.
Source: Ministry of Youth Affairs & Sports

Question 8
National Sports Day in India is celebrated on 29 August, the birth anniversary of:
(a) Milkha Singh (b) Major Dhyan Chand (c) Sachin Tendulkar (d) P. T. Usha
Answer: (b) Major Dhyan Chand
Trap Type: Similar Name — 23 June (Olympic Day) is sometimes offered in date-based versions of this question.
15/45-sec Rule: 15-second fact.
Source: Ministry of Youth Affairs & Sports

Question 9
"Chukker" is a term associated with:
(a) Golf (b) Polo (c) Chess (d) Billiards
Answer: (b) Polo
Trap Type: Similar Name — golf and billiards both have many unusual terms; "chukker" is a period of play in polo.
15/45-sec Rule: 15-second fact.
Source: PYQ-style

Question 10
In 2025, India won its first ICC Women's ODI World Cup. Whom did India beat in the final?
(a) Australia (b) England (c) South Africa (d) New Zealand
Answer: (c) South Africa (final on 2 November 2025 at Navi Mumbai)
Trap Type: Almost True Statement — Australia is the most successful team in this event, so it "feels" like the final opponent.
15/45-sec Rule: 15-second fact if you have read the year carefully.
Source: ICC (icc-cricket.com)

4. How to Use This Analysis

Make your own list of cup–sport and term–sport pairs; these are the quickest marks in the paper.

Every time you see "NOT", "except" or "incorrectly matched", slow down — that is a 45-second question, not a 15-second one.

For recent events, always learn the year with the fact ("Khel Ratna 2024 — Manu Bhaker, Gukesh, Harmanpreet, Praveen Kumar"); exam options often swap the year or the opponent.
