126. Consider the following statements about isotopes:
1. Isotopes of an element have the same number of protons.
2. Isotopes of an element have identical physical properties.
3. Isotopes of an element have similar chemical properties.
Which of the statements given above are correct?
(a) 1 and 2 only (b) 1 and 3 only (c) 2 and 3 only (d) 1, 2 and 3
Answer: (b)
Solution: Same protons and same electrons give similar chemical properties, but different numbers of neutrons give different masses and therefore different physical properties. Statement 2 is the almost-correct trap.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Science, Ch 4 Structure of the Atom

127. What is the molecular mass of sulphuric acid, H2SO4 (H = 1 u, S = 32 u, O = 16 u)?
(a) 49 u (b) 96 u (c) 98 u (d) 100 u
Answer: (c)
Solution: Two hydrogen, one sulphur and four oxygen atoms: 2 × 1 + 32 + 4 × 16 = 2 + 32 + 64 = 98 u.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Science, Ch 3 Atoms and Molecules

128. Match List I (scientist) with List II (contribution):
List I
A. Döbereiner
B. Newlands
C. Mendeleev
D. Moseley
List II
1. Law of octaves
2. Atomic number as the basis of classification
3. Triads
4. Periodic table based on atomic mass
Choose the correct code:
(a) A-1, B-3, C-4, D-2 (b) A-3, B-1, C-2, D-4 (c) A-3, B-1, C-4, D-2 (d) A-4, B-1, C-3, D-2
Answer: (c)
Solution: Döbereiner grouped elements in triads, Newlands gave the law of octaves, Mendeleev arranged elements by atomic mass and Moseley showed that atomic number is the true basis. Option (b) swaps Mendeleev and Moseley.

129. An element has atomic number 19. Where is it placed in the Modern Periodic Table?
(a) Period 3, Group 1 (b) Period 4, Group 1 (c) Period 4, Group 2 (d) Period 3, Group 9
Answer: (b)
Solution: Its configuration is 2, 8, 8, 1 — four shells mean period 4, and one valence electron means group 1. The element is potassium.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science, Periodic Classification of Elements

130. Which pair has the same number of electrons?
(a) Na and Ne (b) Cl and Ar (c) K and Ar (d) Na+ and Ne
Answer: (d)
Solution: A sodium atom (11 electrons) loses one electron to become Na+ with 10 electrons, the same as neon (2, 8). The neutral Na atom has one electron more than Ne.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science, Ch 3 Metals and Non-metals

131. Which of the following is NOT a redox reaction?
(a) CuO + H2 → Cu + H2O (b) ZnO + C → Zn + CO (c) NaOH + HCl → NaCl + H2O (d) MnO2 + 4HCl → MnCl2 + 2H2O + Cl2
Answer: (c)
Solution: The reaction of NaOH with HCl is neutralisation; no substance gains or loses oxygen or hydrogen. In the other three, one substance is oxidised while another is reduced.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science, Ch 1 Chemical Reactions and Equations

132. Assertion: Gold used for jewellery in India is generally 22 carat, not 24 carat.
Reason: Pure gold is very soft.
Choose the correct option:
(a) Both Assertion and Reason are true, and Reason correctly explains Assertion (b) Both are true, but Reason does not explain Assertion (c) Assertion is true but Reason is false (d) Assertion is false but Reason is true
Answer: (a)
Solution: Because pure 24-carat gold is too soft for ornaments, it is alloyed with copper or silver to make 22-carat gold. The reason directly explains the assertion.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science, Ch 3 Metals and Non-metals

133. In the electrolytic refining of copper, which of the following is used as the anode?
(a) A thin strip of pure copper (b) A thick block of impure copper (c) A graphite rod (d) A zinc plate
Answer: (b)
Solution: Impure copper is the anode and pure copper the cathode, with acidified copper sulphate as electrolyte. Pure copper deposits on the cathode and insoluble impurities settle below the anode as anode mud.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science, Ch 3 Metals and Non-metals

134. Element X has configuration 2, 8, 1 and element Y has configuration 2, 8, 7. The compound formed between them and its type of bond is:
(a) XY2, covalent (b) X2Y, ionic (c) XY, ionic (d) XY, covalent
Answer: (c)
Solution: X (sodium) gives one electron to Y (chlorine); both reach an octet and form oppositely charged ions held by an ionic bond, giving XY, like NaCl.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science, Ch 3 Metals and Non-metals

135. How many single covalent bonds are present in a molecule of ethane, C2H6?
(a) 6 (b) 7 (c) 8 (d) 9
Answer: (b)
Solution: Each carbon is bonded to three hydrogens and the two carbons are bonded to each other: 6 C–H bonds + 1 C–C bond = 7 single bonds.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science, Ch 4 Carbon and its Compounds

136. How many different structures (structural isomers) are possible for butane, C4H10?
(a) 1 (b) 3 (c) 2 (d) 4
Answer: (c)
Solution: The four carbon atoms can form a straight chain or a branched chain, so butane has 2 structural isomers.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science, Ch 4 Carbon and its Compounds

137. What are the products when baking soda (NaHCO3) is heated?
(a) NaOH and CO2 (b) Na2O, CO2 and H2 (c) Na, CO2 and H2O (d) Na2CO3, H2O and CO2
Answer: (d)
Solution: 2NaHCO3 → Na2CO3 + H2O + CO2 on heating. The carbon dioxide released is what makes baked items rise.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science, Ch 2 Acids, Bases and Salts

138. Which arrangement shows the substances in increasing order of pH?
(a) Gastric juice < Pure water < Blood < Milk of magnesia (b) Blood < Gastric juice < Pure water < Milk of magnesia (c) Milk of magnesia < Blood < Pure water < Gastric juice (d) Pure water < Gastric juice < Milk of magnesia < Blood
Answer: (a)
Solution: Gastric juice is strongly acidic, pure water is neutral, blood is slightly basic and milk of magnesia is clearly basic. Option (c) gives the decreasing order — a reverse trap.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science, Ch 2 Acids, Bases and Salts

139. Consider the following statements:
1. Soaps are sodium or potassium salts of long-chain carboxylic acids.
2. Detergents work well even in hard water.
3. In a micelle, the ionic end of the soap molecule points towards the oil droplet.
Which of the statements given above are correct?
(a) 2 and 3 only (b) 1 and 3 only (c) 1 and 2 only (d) 1, 2 and 3
Answer: (c)
Solution: In a micelle the hydrocarbon tail dissolves in the oil and the ionic end faces the water, so statement 3 reverses it. Statements 1 and 2 are correct.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science, Ch 4 Carbon and its Compounds

140. Match List I (common name) with List II (formula):
List I
A. Washing soda
B. Bleaching powder
C. Baking soda
D. Plaster of Paris
List II
1. CaOCl2
2. NaHCO3
3. Na2CO3·10H2O
4. CaSO4·½H2O
Choose the correct code:

141. Which pair of metals does NOT give hydrogen gas with dilute hydrochloric acid?
(a) Zinc and iron (b) Magnesium and aluminium (c) Sodium and calcium (d) Copper and silver
Answer: (d)
Solution: Copper and silver lie below hydrogen in the activity series, so they cannot displace hydrogen from dilute acids. All the other metals listed are above hydrogen.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science, Ch 3 Metals and Non-metals

142. Element P burns in air to form an oxide whose solution in water turns blue litmus red. P is most likely:
(a) Sulphur (b) Sodium (c) Magnesium (d) Calcium
Answer: (a)
Solution: Turning blue litmus red shows an acidic oxide, which non-metals form; sulphur dioxide dissolves in water to give sulphurous acid. Oxides of sodium, magnesium and calcium are basic.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science, Ch 3 Metals and Non-metals

143. Which of the following was NOT a limitation of Mendeleev's Periodic Table?
(a) Hydrogen could not be given a fixed position (b) Isotopes of an element could not be placed properly (c) Atomic masses did not always increase in a regular manner (d) He left gaps for elements that were yet to be discovered
Answer: (d)
Solution: Leaving gaps and predicting elements such as gallium was a great strength of Mendeleev's table, not a limitation. The other three were real problems — read the word NOT carefully.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science, Periodic Classification of Elements

144. An atom has atomic number 17 and mass number 35. Its numbers of protons, neutrons and electrons are respectively:
(a) 17, 18, 17 (b) 17, 17, 18 (c) 18, 17, 17 (d) 35, 17, 17
Answer: (a)
Solution: Protons = atomic number = 17; neutrons = 35 − 17 = 18; a neutral atom has as many electrons as protons, which is 17.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Science, Ch 4 Structure of the Atom

145. Iron nails are kept in three test tubes for a few days.
Tube I: nails in ordinary tap water, open to air.
Tube II: nails in boiled distilled water covered with a layer of oil.
Tube III: nails in dry air over anhydrous calcium chloride.
In which tube will the nails rust?
(a) Tubes I and III (b) Tube I only (c) Tube II only (d) All three tubes
Answer: (b)
Solution: Rusting needs both air and water. Tube II has no dissolved air and Tube III has no moisture, so only the nails in Tube I rust.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science, Ch 3 Metals and Non-metals

146. Which of the following is an endothermic process?
(a) Respiration (b) Burning of natural gas (c) Decomposition of calcium carbonate into quicklime and carbon dioxide (d) Reaction of quicklime with water
Answer: (c)
Solution: Calcium carbonate decomposes only when heat is supplied, so the reaction absorbs heat. Respiration, burning and slaking of lime all release heat.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science, Ch 1 Chemical Reactions and Equations

147. Which pair of compounds belongs to the same homologous series?
(a) C2H4 and C2H6 (b) C2H2 and C3H8 (c) CH4 and C2H6 (d) CH4 and C2H4
Answer: (c)
Solution: Methane and ethane are both alkanes (CnH2n+2) and differ by one CH2 unit. In the other pairs one compound is an alkane and the other an alkene or alkyne.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science, Ch 4 Carbon and its Compounds

148. Chlorine has atomic number 17. Its position in the Modern Periodic Table is:
(a) Group 7, Period 3 (b) Group 17, Period 2 (c) Group 1, Period 3 (d) Group 17, Period 3
Answer: (d)
Solution: Configuration 2, 8, 7 has three shells (period 3) and seven valence electrons, which places it in group 10 + 7 = 17. Option (a) uses the old short-form group number, a trap.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science, Periodic Classification of Elements

149. When the equation Fe + H2O → Fe3O4 + H2 is balanced, what is the coefficient of H2O?
(a) 2 (b) 3 (c) 4 (d) 6
Answer: (c)
Solution: Balancing oxygen first and then iron and hydrogen gives 3Fe + 4H2O → Fe3O4 + 4H2, so the coefficient of water is 4.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science, Ch 1 Chemical Reactions and Equations

150. Consider the following statements about ethanol:
1. Ethanol reacts with sodium to give hydrogen gas.
2. Ethanol heated at 443 K with excess concentrated sulphuric acid gives ethene.
3. Ethanol turns blue litmus red.
Which of the statements given above are correct?
(a) 2 and 3 only (b) 1 and 2 only (c) 1 and 3 only (d) 1, 2 and 3
Answer: (b)
Solution: Sodium reacts with ethanol to release hydrogen, and hot concentrated sulphuric acid removes water from ethanol to give ethene. Ethanol is neutral to litmus, so statement 3 is false.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science, Ch 4 Carbon and its Compounds
