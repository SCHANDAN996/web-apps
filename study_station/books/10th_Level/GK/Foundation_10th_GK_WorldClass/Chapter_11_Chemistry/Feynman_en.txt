The most confusing idea in this chapter is oxidation and reduction. Students learn the definitions, then sit in the exam, look at CuO + H2 → Cu + H2O, and freeze: "Copper oxide has 'oxide' in its name, so surely copper oxide is being oxidised?" No. Let us make this so simple that you can never get it wrong again.

Think of oxygen as a school bag.

In a reaction, oxygen is passed from one substance to another, like a school bag passed between two friends. Whoever RECEIVES the bag is oxidised. Whoever GIVES AWAY the bag is reduced. That is all.

The daily-life example:
Imagine Copper and Hydrogen standing at the school gate. Copper is carrying the oxygen bag — that is why he is called copper oxide, CuO. Hydrogen walks up and takes the bag. Now Hydrogen is carrying oxygen and has become H2O, water. Copper is left empty-handed as plain copper, Cu.

Who received the bag? Hydrogen → hydrogen is oxidised.
Who gave the bag away? Copper oxide → copper oxide is reduced.

The trap is the name. "Copper OXIDE" already has oxygen, so it can only lose it. A substance that already holds the bag cannot "gain" it again in this reaction.

The same story works everywhere:
Rusting: iron takes oxygen (and water) from the air → iron is oxidised → rust.
Rancidity: oil in chips takes oxygen from air → oil is oxidised → bad smell. Fill the packet with nitrogen and there is no oxygen bag to take.
Respiration: glucose takes oxygen and turns into carbon dioxide and water → glucose is oxidised, and energy is released.

There is a second, smaller rule for hydrogen: losing hydrogen is also oxidation, and gaining hydrogen is reduction. In everyday exam questions, the oxygen-bag story is enough for almost every case.

Also remember: the two always happen together. If one friend receives the bag, the other must have given it. That is why the reaction is called redox — REDuction plus OXidation in one go.

The unforgettable trick:
"Bag in = Oxidised, Bag out = Reduced." Oxygen comes IN → OXidised (both begin with "Ox"). Oxygen goes out and the substance becomes lighter → REDUCED (its oxygen is reduced).
