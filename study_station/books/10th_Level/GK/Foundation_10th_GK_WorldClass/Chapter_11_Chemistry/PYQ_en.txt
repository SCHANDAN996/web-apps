📊 PYQ Pattern Analysis: General Chemistry

Exams this chapter serves: SSC GD, SSC MTS, RRB Group D, state police / constable exams, and the general-science part of other state-level tests.

Note: this is a pattern analysis — what kinds of questions these exams like to ask from this chapter and how they trap you. We do not give year-wise counts or percentages, because no reliable data for them exists. The model questions below are written in the exam style; their sources are the NCERT chapters they test.

1. What Exams Like to Ask (Sub-topics, in plain words)

Common names and formulas — baking soda, washing soda, bleaching powder, Plaster of Paris, gypsum, blue vitriol, quicklime and slaked lime. The most common one-liner of this chapter.

Natural acids — "Which acid is found in tamarind / tomato / curd / vinegar / ant sting?"

Acids, bases and pH — indicator colours, neutral pH, pH of the mouth and tooth decay, antacids, treatment of stings.

Metals and non-metals — the exception list (mercury, bromine, gallium, graphite, iodine, diamond), storage of sodium and phosphorus, reactivity series, alloys, aqua regia, rusting and galvanisation.

Structure of the atom — discoverers of the electron, proton (canal rays), nucleus and neutron; atomic number, mass number, neutrons; isotopes vs isobars; valency.

Periodic table — Döbereiner, Newlands, Mendeleev, Moseley; groups and periods; eka-elements.

Chemical reactions — types of reaction, oxidation and reduction, rancidity, corrosion, exothermic vs endothermic.

Matter and mixtures — sublimation, evaporation and cooling, kelvin conversion, Tyndall effect, separation techniques.

Carbon compounds — allotropes, catenation, CNG / LPG, ethanol and ethanoic acid, soaps and detergents, hydrogenation of oils.

2. Exam-wise Style (qualitative)

SSC GD / SSC MTS — short, direct one-liners: common name to formula, acid in a fruit, discoverer of a particle, liquid metal. Speed matters.

RRB Group D — science carries more weight here; besides one-liners it asks simple calculations (neutrons from Z and A, molecular mass), reaction types and "Which of the following is NOT …" questions.

State police / constable exams — direct daily-life chemistry: salts, acids, alloys, fuels, rusting.

Higher exams that use these same basics (CHSL, NTPC, CGL) add statement-based questions and "match the following".

3. Top 10 High-Yield Model Questions with Solutions and Trap Analysis

Question 1
What is the chemical formula of Plaster of Paris?
(a) CaSO4·2H2O (b) CaSO4 (c) CaSO4·½H2O (d) CaCO3
Answer: (c) CaSO4·½H2O
Trap Type: Almost Correct Statement — (a) is gypsum. Heating gypsum at 373 K removes water and gives Plaster of Paris.
15/45-sec Rule: 15-second fact. Score maximizer.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science, Ch 2 Acids, Bases and Salts

Question 2
Which acid is present in tamarind?
(a) Oxalic acid (b) Tartaric acid (c) Citric acid (d) Lactic acid
Answer: (b) Tartaric acid
Trap Type: Similar Name — oxalic acid is in tomato; tamarind and tomato both start with "T", so students swap them.
15/45-sec Rule: 15-second fact.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science, Ch 2 Acids, Bases and Salts

Question 3
Which of the following statements is NOT correct?
(a) Mercury is a metal that is liquid at room temperature.
(b) Bromine is a non-metal that is liquid at room temperature.
(c) All non-metals are bad conductors of electricity.
(d) Iodine is a lustrous non-metal.
Answer: (c)
Trap Type: Negative Phrasing + absolute word — graphite, a non-metal, conducts electricity, so "all" makes (c) wrong. A student who misses "NOT" picks (a) at once because it is a familiar true fact.
15/45-sec Rule: 45 seconds — read the question twice, then test each option against the exception list.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science, Ch 3 Metals and Non-metals

Question 4
Who discovered the neutron?
(a) J.J. Thomson (b) E. Goldstein (c) Ernest Rutherford (d) James Chadwick
Answer: (d) James Chadwick (1932)
Trap Type: Similar Name — Thomson discovered the electron, Goldstein the canal rays, Rutherford the nucleus. All four are "atom scientists".
15/45-sec Rule: 15-second fact.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Science, Ch 4 Structure of the Atom

Question 5
Which of the following pairs are isobars?
(a) Carbon-12 and carbon-14 (b) Argon-40 and calcium-40 (c) Chlorine-35 and chlorine-37 (d) Protium and deuterium
Answer: (b) Argon-40 and calcium-40
Trap Type: Similar Name — the other three pairs are isotopes (same element, same atomic number). Isobars are different elements with the same mass number.
15/45-sec Rule: 15 seconds if you know "isotoPes = Protons, isobArs = mAss".
Source: NCERT Class 9 Science, Ch 4 Structure of the Atom

Question 6
In the reaction CuO + H2 → Cu + H2O, which substance is oxidised?
(a) CuO (b) H2 (c) Cu (d) H2O
Answer: (b) H2
Trap Type: Reverse Causation — copper oxide loses oxygen, so it is reduced, not oxidised. Hydrogen gains oxygen and is oxidised.
15/45-sec Rule: 45 seconds — follow the oxygen from left to right before choosing.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science, Ch 1 Chemical Reactions and Equations

Question 7
Brass is an alloy of:
(a) Copper and tin (b) Copper and zinc (c) Lead and tin (d) Iron and nickel
Answer: (b) Copper and zinc
Trap Type: Similar Name — copper and tin make bronze; lead and tin make solder.
15/45-sec Rule: 15-second fact.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science, Ch 3 Metals and Non-metals

Question 8
An atom of chlorine has atomic number 17 and mass number 35. How many neutrons does it have?
(a) 17 (b) 18 (c) 35 (d) 52
Answer: (b) 18
Trap Type: Almost Correct — 17 is the number of protons (and electrons); 52 comes from wrongly adding the two numbers. Neutrons = mass number − atomic number = 35 − 17 = 18.
15/45-sec Rule: 45 seconds — a one-step subtraction, but read which particle is asked.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Science, Ch 4 Structure of the Atom

Question 9
The Modern Periodic Table arranges elements in order of increasing:
(a) Atomic mass (b) Atomic number (c) Number of neutrons (d) Density
Answer: (b) Atomic number
Trap Type: Chronological Confusion — Mendeleev used atomic mass; Moseley (1913) showed that atomic number is the true basis.
15/45-sec Rule: 15-second fact.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science, Periodic Classification of Elements

Question 10
Consider the following statements:
I. Phenolphthalein turns pink in a basic solution.
II. Methyl orange turns yellow in an acidic solution.
Which of the statements given is/are correct?
(a) Only I (b) Only II (c) Both I and II (d) Neither I nor II
Answer: (a) Only I
Trap Type: Almost Correct Statement — methyl orange does turn yellow, but in a basic solution; in acid it is red.
15/45-sec Rule: 45 seconds — check each statement separately.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science, Ch 2 Acids, Bases and Salts

4. How to Use This Analysis

Make one page of "common name — formula — use" for the salts, and one page of "fruit — acid". These two pages answer a large share of the one-liners from this chapter.

Every time you see "NOT", "all", "only" or "never", slow down — that is a 45-second question. Run through the exception list: mercury, bromine, gallium, graphite, iodine, diamond.

For numericals, write the formula first (neutrons = A − Z; molecular mass = sum of atomic masses × subscripts) and only then look at the options.
