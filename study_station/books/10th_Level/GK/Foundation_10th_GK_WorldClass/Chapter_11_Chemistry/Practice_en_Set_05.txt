Set 5/6 – General Chemistry MCQs (Foundation)
Questions 101–120: Medium | Questions 121–125: Hard

101. Cream is separated from milk by which method?
(a) Filtration (b) Sublimation (c) Centrifugation (d) Distillation
Answer: (c)
Solution: In centrifugation the milk is spun fast; the denser particles are forced to the bottom and the lighter cream stays on top. The same idea is used in washing-machine dryers.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Science, Ch 2 Is Matter Around Us Pure

102. The different coloured dyes present in black ink can be separated by:
(a) Chromatography (b) Evaporation (c) Separating funnel (d) Sieving
Answer: (a)
Solution: Chromatography separates components of a mixture that dissolve in the same solvent but move at different speeds on paper.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Science, Ch 2 Is Matter Around Us Pure

103. Oxygen, nitrogen and argon are separated from air by:
(a) Filtration of air (b) Centrifugation (c) Chromatography (d) Fractional distillation of liquid air
Answer: (d)
Solution: Air is compressed and cooled to a liquid, then warmed slowly; each gas boils off at its own boiling point.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Science, Ch 2 Is Matter Around Us Pure

104. A mixture of kerosene oil and water is best separated using:
(a) A separating funnel (b) Sublimation (c) Crystallisation (d) Chromatography
Answer: (a)
Solution: Kerosene and water do not mix and form two layers; the lower water layer is drained off through the stopcock of a separating funnel.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Science, Ch 2 Is Matter Around Us Pure

105. A mixture of common salt and ammonium chloride can be separated by:
(a) Filtration (b) Sublimation (c) Centrifugation (d) Separating funnel
Answer: (b)
Solution: On heating, ammonium chloride sublimes and its vapour can be collected separately, leaving common salt behind.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Science, Ch 2 Is Matter Around Us Pure

106. Which of the following shows the Tyndall effect?
(a) Salt solution (b) Sugar solution (c) Copper sulphate solution (d) Milk
Answer: (d)
Solution: Milk is a colloid; its particles are large enough to scatter a beam of light (Tyndall effect). True solutions like salt or sugar solution do not scatter light.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Science, Ch 2 Is Matter Around Us Pure

107. Which of the following is a homogeneous mixture?
(a) Sand in water (b) Chalk in water (c) Brass (d) Oil and water
Answer: (c)
Solution: Brass is an alloy of copper and zinc; it has a uniform composition throughout, so it is a homogeneous mixture. Alloys are mixtures, not compounds — a common trap.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Science, Ch 2 Is Matter Around Us Pure

108. Which of the following is a compound and not a mixture?
(a) Air (b) Water (c) Brass (d) Sea water
Answer: (b)
Solution: Water (H2O) has hydrogen and oxygen in a fixed ratio by mass, and its properties differ from both elements, so it is a compound. Air, brass and sea water are mixtures.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Science, Ch 2 Is Matter Around Us Pure

109. Who established the law of conservation of mass?
(a) Joseph L. Proust (b) Antoine L. Lavoisier (c) John Dalton (d) J.J. Thomson
Answer: (b)
Solution: Lavoisier established the law of conservation of mass. Proust stated the law of constant proportions — the similar-name trap, as both laws appear together in NCERT.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Science, Ch 3 Atoms and Molecules

110. Plaster of Paris is made by heating gypsum at what temperature?
(a) 273 K (b) 573 K (c) 373 K (d) 1273 K
Answer: (c)
Solution: On heating at 373 K, gypsum loses part of its water and becomes calcium sulphate hemihydrate. The temperature is 373 K.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science, Ch 2 Acids, Bases and Salts

111. Why must Plaster of Paris be stored in a moisture-proof container?
(a) It explodes in moist air (b) It changes into lime (c) It loses its white colour (d) On absorbing water it sets into gypsum, a hard solid mass
Answer: (d)
Solution: Plaster of Paris mixed with water changes back to gypsum and sets hard. That is why it is used for plaster casts on fractured bones and must be kept dry.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science, Ch 2 Acids, Bases and Salts

112. After whitewashing, the walls become shiny in two to three days. This is due to the formation of:
(a) Calcium carbonate (b) Calcium oxide (c) Calcium hydroxide (d) Calcium chloride
Answer: (a)
Solution: Calcium hydroxide on the wall reacts slowly with carbon dioxide in air: Ca(OH)2 + CO2 → CaCO3 + H2O. The thin layer of calcium carbonate gives the shine.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science, Ch 1 Chemical Reactions and Equations

113. Which is a use of washing soda?
(a) Plaster casts for fractured bones (b) Removing permanent hardness of water (c) As an antacid (d) Disinfecting drinking water
Answer: (b)
Solution: Washing soda removes permanent hardness of water and is used in glass, soap and paper industries. Antacid use belongs to baking soda and disinfecting water to bleaching powder.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science, Ch 2 Acids, Bases and Salts

114. Bleaching powder is produced by the action of chlorine on:
(a) Quicklime (b) Limestone (c) Dry slaked lime (d) Gypsum
Answer: (c)
Solution: Ca(OH)2 + Cl2 → CaOCl2 + H2O. The chlorine is passed over dry slaked lime, not quicklime — the almost-correct trap.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science, Ch 2 Acids, Bases and Salts

115. "Blue vitriol" is the common name of:
(a) Copper sulphate pentahydrate (b) Ferrous sulphate heptahydrate (c) Zinc sulphate (d) Sodium carbonate decahydrate
Answer: (a)
Solution: Blue vitriol is CuSO4·5H2O. Ferrous sulphate heptahydrate (FeSO4·7H2O) is green vitriol — the colour in the name tells you the metal.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science, Ch 2 Acids, Bases and Salts

116. Compressed Natural Gas (CNG) is mainly:
(a) Butane (b) Propane (c) Ethane (d) Methane
Answer: (d)
Solution: Methane is a major component of biogas and CNG. LPG is mainly butane — the two fuels are often swapped in questions.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science, Ch 4 Carbon and its Compounds

117. Which gas is known as "laughing gas"?
(a) Nitrous oxide, N2O (b) Nitrogen dioxide, NO2 (c) Nitric oxide, NO (d) Ammonia, NH3
Answer: (a)
Solution: Nitrous oxide (N2O) is called laughing gas and has been used as a mild anaesthetic. Nitrogen dioxide is the brown, toxic gas.
Source: PYQ-style

118. Which gas is present in the largest proportion in the air around us?
(a) Oxygen (b) Argon (c) Nitrogen (d) Carbon dioxide
Answer: (c)
Solution: Nitrogen makes up the largest part of air, about four-fifths of it. Oxygen comes second — choosing it is the common slip.
Source: PYQ-style

119. Heavy water, used as a moderator in some nuclear reactors, is:
(a) H2O2 (b) D2O (c) Water with many dissolved salts (d) Water at 4 °C
Answer: (b)
Solution: Heavy water is deuterium oxide, D2O, where deuterium is an isotope of hydrogen. H2O2 is hydrogen peroxide.
Source: PYQ-style

120. What is the boiling point of water at normal atmospheric pressure on the Kelvin scale?
(a) 273 K (b) 100 K (c) 373 K (d) 0 K
Answer: (c)
Solution: 273 K is the melting point of ice, the tempting wrong option. Water boils at 100 °C, and 100 + 273 = 373 K.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Science, Ch 1 Matter in Our Surroundings

121. What is the molecular mass of carbon dioxide (C = 12 u, O = 16 u)?
(a) 28 u (b) 32 u (c) 44 u (d) 40 u
Answer: (c)
Solution: CO2 has one carbon and two oxygen atoms: 12 + 2 × 16 = 44 u.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Science, Ch 3 Atoms and Molecules

122. Consider the following statements:
1. All metals are solids at room temperature.
2. Iodine is a non-metal but it is lustrous.
3. Graphite is a non-metal that conducts electricity.
Which of the statements given above are correct?
(a) 1 and 2 only (b) 2 and 3 only (c) 1 and 3 only (d) 1, 2 and 3
Answer: (b)
Solution: Statement 1 is false because mercury is a liquid metal; the word "all" is the trap. Statements 2 and 3 are standard exceptions and are correct.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science, Ch 3 Metals and Non-metals

123. An element X has the electronic configuration 2, 8, 2. What will be the formula of its oxide?
(a) X2O (b) XO (c) XO2 (d) X2O3
Answer: (b)
Solution: X (magnesium) has valency two and oxygen also has valency two, so they combine in a one-to-one ratio to give XO, like MgO.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Science, Ch 3 Atoms and Molecules

124. A metal gives hydrogen with dilute hydrochloric acid, and its oxide reacts with both acids and bases. The metal is:
(a) Copper (b) Silver (c) Gold (d) Zinc
Answer: (d)
Solution: Zinc is above hydrogen in the activity series and zinc oxide is amphoteric. Copper, silver and gold are below hydrogen and do not give hydrogen with dilute acids.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science, Ch 3 Metals and Non-metals

125. How many atoms in total are present in one molecule of glucose, C6H12O6?
(a) 12 (b) 18 (c) 36 (d) 24
Answer: (d)
Solution: Carbon 6 + hydrogen 12 + oxygen 6 gives a total of 24 atoms.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Science, Ch 3 Atoms and Molecules
