Set 4/6 – General Chemistry MCQs (Foundation)
Questions 76–100: Medium

76. Which two metals are the most malleable?
(a) Lead and mercury (b) Gold and silver (c) Sodium and potassium (d) Iron and zinc
Answer: (b)
Solution: Gold and silver are the most malleable metals and can be beaten into very thin sheets. They are also the most ductile.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science, Ch 3 Metals and Non-metals

77. Which two metals are the best conductors of heat?
(a) Lead and mercury (b) Iron and aluminium (c) Silver and copper (d) Sodium and calcium
Answer: (c)
Solution: Silver and copper are the best conductors of heat. Lead and mercury are comparatively poor conductors — the exact opposite, placed as a trap.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science, Ch 3 Metals and Non-metals

78. Which non-metal is a good conductor of electricity?
(a) Diamond (b) Sulphur (c) Iodine (d) Graphite
Answer: (d)
Solution: Graphite, an allotrope of carbon, conducts electricity and is used in electrodes. Diamond, another form of carbon, does not.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science, Ch 3 Metals and Non-metals

79. Which non-metal is lustrous (shiny)?
(a) Iodine (b) Sulphur (c) Phosphorus (d) Coal
Answer: (a)
Solution: Lustre is normally a property of metals, but iodine is a non-metal that is lustrous. This is a standard exception asked in exams.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science, Ch 3 Metals and Non-metals

80. Which pair consists of amphoteric oxides (react with both acids and bases)?
(a) Sodium oxide and potassium oxide (b) Carbon dioxide and sulphur dioxide (c) Aluminium oxide and zinc oxide (d) Magnesium oxide and calcium oxide
Answer: (c)
Solution: Al2O3 and ZnO show both acidic and basic behaviour, so they are amphoteric. Non-metal oxides like CO2 and SO2 are acidic.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science, Ch 3 Metals and Non-metals

81. Which order of reactivity is correct (most reactive first)?
(a) K > Na > Ca > Mg (b) Na > K > Mg > Ca (c) Cu > Fe > Zn > Mg (d) Au > Ag > Cu > Fe
Answer: (a)
Solution: In the activity series potassium is the most reactive, followed by sodium, calcium and magnesium. Gold is at the bottom, so option (d) is upside down.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science, Ch 3 Metals and Non-metals

82. The thermit reaction between iron(III) oxide and aluminium is used for:
(a) Making soap (b) Joining railway tracks and cracked machine parts (c) Purifying drinking water (d) Bleaching cloth
Answer: (b)
Solution: Fe2O3 + 2Al → 2Fe + Al2O3 releases so much heat that the iron formed is molten and is used for welding.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science, Ch 3 Metals and Non-metals

83. Cinnabar is an ore of which metal?
(a) Aluminium (b) Iron (c) Lead (d) Mercury
Answer: (d)
Solution: Cinnabar (HgS) is the ore of mercury. Bauxite is an ore of aluminium, haematite of iron and galena of lead.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science, Ch 3 Metals and Non-metals

84. Highly reactive metals such as sodium, magnesium and aluminium are extracted from their compounds by:
(a) Heating alone (b) Reduction with carbon (c) Electrolytic reduction (d) Washing with water
Answer: (c)
Solution: These metals hold oxygen more strongly than carbon does, so carbon cannot reduce their oxides. They are obtained by electrolysis of their molten chlorides.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science, Ch 3 Metals and Non-metals

85. Solder, used for welding electrical wires, is an alloy of:
(a) Lead and tin (b) Copper and tin (c) Copper and zinc (d) Iron and carbon
Answer: (a)
Solution: Solder (lead + tin) has a low melting point, which makes it suitable for joining wires. Copper + tin is bronze.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science, Ch 3 Metals and Non-metals

86. Gold used for ornaments in India is usually 22 carat. This means:
(a) 22% of it is gold (b) 22 parts of pure gold alloyed with 2 parts of copper or silver (c) It is pure gold (d) 2 parts of gold with 22 parts of silver
Answer: (b)
Solution: Pure gold (24 carat) is too soft, so 22 parts of gold are mixed with 2 parts of copper or silver to make it harder.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science, Ch 3 Metals and Non-metals

87. Anodising is a process of:
(a) Forming a thick oxide layer on aluminium (b) Coating iron with zinc (c) Coating copper with tin (d) Removing the oxide layer from silver
Answer: (a)
Solution: In anodising, aluminium is made the anode in electrolysis so that a thick protective oxide layer forms on it. Coating iron with zinc is galvanisation.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science, Ch 3 Metals and Non-metals

88. Carbon forms a very large number of compounds mainly because of:
(a) Ionic bonding and high density (b) Catenation and tetravalency (c) Its metallic nature (d) Its low melting point
Answer: (b)
Solution: Carbon can link with other carbon atoms to form long chains and rings (catenation), and its valency of four lets it bond with many other atoms.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science, Ch 4 Carbon and its Compounds

89. Which allotrope of carbon has its atoms arranged in the shape of a football?
(a) Diamond (b) Graphite (c) Coal (d) Buckminsterfullerene (C-60)
Answer: (d)
Solution: C-60 looks like a football and resembles the geodesic dome designed by architect Buckminster Fuller, hence the name fullerene.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science, Ch 4 Carbon and its Compounds

90. What is the general formula of alkenes?
(a) CnH2n+2 (b) CnH2n (c) CnH2n−2 (d) CnHn
Answer: (b)
Solution: Alkenes have one carbon–carbon double bond and the formula CnH2n (for example ethene, C2H4). CnH2n+2 is for alkanes and CnH2n−2 for alkynes.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science, Ch 4 Carbon and its Compounds

91. Two successive members of a homologous series differ by:
(a) A CH2 unit (b) A CH unit (c) A CH3 unit (d) A CH4 unit
Answer: (a)
Solution: Each next member has one extra CH2 group, which adds 14 u to the molecular mass. Chemical properties stay similar within the series.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science, Ch 4 Carbon and its Compounds

92. Vinegar is a 5–8% solution in water of:
(a) Ethanol (b) Methanol (c) Ethanoic acid (d) Methanoic acid
Answer: (c)
Solution: A 5–8% solution of ethanoic (acetic) acid in water is vinegar, used as a preservative in pickles. Methanoic acid is the acid in ant stings.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science, Ch 4 Carbon and its Compounds

93. Ethanol reacts with ethanoic acid in the presence of an acid catalyst to form:
(a) A sweet-smelling ester (b) Soap (c) An alkane (d) An aldehyde
Answer: (a)
Solution: This reaction is esterification. Esters are sweet-smelling and are used in perfumes and flavouring agents.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science, Ch 4 Carbon and its Compounds

94. Saponification is the reaction in which:
(a) Vegetable oil reacts with hydrogen to form ghee (b) An ester reacts with sodium hydroxide to give soap and alcohol (c) Ethanol burns in oxygen (d) Large hydrocarbons are broken down
Answer: (b)
Solution: An ester treated with an alkali (NaOH) gives back the alcohol and the sodium salt of the acid; this reaction is used to make soap. Option (a) is hydrogenation.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science, Ch 4 Carbon and its Compounds

95. Vegetable oils are converted into solid fats (vanaspati) by hydrogenation using which catalyst?
(a) Iron (b) Copper (c) Zinc (d) Nickel
Answer: (d)
Solution: Unsaturated oils add hydrogen in the presence of a nickel (or palladium) catalyst to form saturated fats.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science, Ch 4 Carbon and its Compounds

96. Soap does not lather well in hard water because:
(a) Hard water is acidic (b) Soap evaporates in hard water (c) Hard water has too much sodium (d) Soap reacts with calcium and magnesium salts to form insoluble scum
Answer: (d)
Solution: Calcium and magnesium salts in hard water form an insoluble precipitate (scum) with soap, so much soap is wasted. Detergents do not form scum.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science, Ch 4 Carbon and its Compounds

97. Saturated hydrocarbons generally burn in sufficient air with:
(a) A yellow sooty flame (b) A clean blue flame (c) A green flame (d) No flame
Answer: (b)
Solution: Saturated hydrocarbons give a clean blue flame. A yellow sooty flame is typical of unsaturated hydrocarbons — the almost-correct swap.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science, Ch 4 Carbon and its Compounds

98. Industrial ethanol is made unfit for drinking ("denatured alcohol") by adding:
(a) Water (b) Methanol (c) Ethanoic acid (d) Glucose
Answer: (b)
Solution: Poisonous methanol is added to industrial ethanol, and dyes colour it blue so it can be identified. Methanol is oxidised to methanal in the liver and can cause death.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science, Ch 4 Carbon and its Compounds

99. What is the molecular formula of ethyne?
(a) C2H6 (b) C2H4 (c) C2H2 (d) CH4
Answer: (c)
Solution: Ethyne has a carbon–carbon triple bond and the formula C2H2. Ethane is C2H6 and ethene is C2H4 — all three names start with "eth".
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science, Ch 4 Carbon and its Compounds

100. Pure ethanoic acid is called glacial acetic acid because:
(a) It is found in glaciers (b) It is icy blue in colour (c) Its melting point is 290 K, so it often freezes in cold winters (d) It is used to make ice
Answer: (c)
Solution: Pure ethanoic acid melts at 290 K, a little below normal room temperature, so it freezes like ice in cold climates. That is the reason for the name.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science, Ch 4 Carbon and its Compounds
