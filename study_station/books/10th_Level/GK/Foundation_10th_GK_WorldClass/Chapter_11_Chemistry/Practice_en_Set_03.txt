Set 3/6 – General Chemistry MCQs (Foundation)
Questions 51–75: Medium

51. The reaction CaO + H2O → Ca(OH)2 is best described as:
(a) Decomposition and endothermic (b) Displacement and exothermic (c) Double displacement and endothermic (d) Combination and exothermic
Answer: (d)
Solution: Two substances combine to form one product (slaked lime) and a large amount of heat is released, so it is a combination and exothermic reaction.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science, Ch 1 Chemical Reactions and Equations

52. In the electrolysis of water, hydrogen and oxygen are collected in which ratio by volume?
(a) 1 : 2 (b) 2 : 1 (c) 1 : 1 (d) 1 : 8
Answer: (b)
Solution: 2H2O → 2H2 + O2, so the volume of hydrogen is double that of oxygen. The ratio 1 : 8 is by mass, not volume — the trap.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science, Ch 1 Chemical Reactions and Equations

53. When iron reacts with copper sulphate solution, the colour of the solution changes from:
(a) Green to blue (b) Colourless to blue (c) Blue to light green (d) Blue to colourless
Answer: (c)
Solution: Blue copper sulphate becomes light green iron sulphate as iron displaces copper. Option (a) is the reverse order.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science, Ch 1 Chemical Reactions and Equations

54. Na2SO4 + BaCl2 → BaSO4 + 2NaCl is which type of reaction?
(a) Double displacement (precipitation) (b) Combination (c) Decomposition (d) Single displacement
Answer: (a)
Solution: The two compounds exchange ions and an insoluble white precipitate of barium sulphate forms, so it is a double displacement (precipitation) reaction.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science, Ch 1 Chemical Reactions and Equations

55. In the reaction CuO + H2 → Cu + H2O, which substance is oxidised?
(a) CuO (b) Cu (c) H2 (d) H2O
Answer: (c)
Solution: Hydrogen gains oxygen to form water, so H2 is oxidised. Copper oxide loses oxygen and is reduced — choosing CuO is the reverse-causation trap.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science, Ch 1 Chemical Reactions and Equations

56. To prevent rancidity, packets of chips are flushed with which gas?
(a) Oxygen (b) Nitrogen (c) Hydrogen (d) Chlorine
Answer: (b)
Solution: Nitrogen is unreactive and keeps oxygen away, so the fats in chips are not oxidised. Oxygen is the gas that causes rancidity.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science, Ch 1 Chemical Reactions and Equations

57. Chemically, rust is mainly:
(a) Iron(II) sulphate (b) Hydrated iron(III) oxide, Fe2O3·xH2O (c) Iron carbide (d) Iron(II) chloride
Answer: (b)
Solution: Iron reacts with oxygen and water to form reddish-brown hydrated iron(III) oxide. Both air and water are needed for rusting.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science, Ch 3 Metals and Non-metals

58. Galvanisation protects iron by coating it with:
(a) Tin (b) Chromium (c) Copper (d) Zinc
Answer: (d)
Solution: Galvanisation is coating iron or steel with a thin layer of zinc. Galvanised iron stays protected even if the zinc coat is broken.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science, Ch 3 Metals and Non-metals

59. Silver articles turn black after some time in air. This is due to the formation of:
(a) Silver sulphide (b) Silver oxide (c) Silver chloride (d) Silver nitrate
Answer: (a)
Solution: Silver reacts with sulphur compounds in air to form a black coating of silver sulphide. Copper, by contrast, gets a green coat of basic copper carbonate.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science, Ch 3 Metals and Non-metals

60. Silver chloride turns grey in sunlight. Which statement about this reaction is correct?
(a) It is thermal decomposition used to make cement (b) It is decomposition by light, used in black-and-white photography (c) It is electrolytic decomposition used in electroplating (d) It is a combination reaction used to make mirrors
Answer: (b)
Solution: 2AgCl → 2Ag + Cl2 in sunlight; this light-driven decomposition is used in black-and-white photography.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science, Ch 1 Chemical Reactions and Equations

61. What are the products when green ferrous sulphate crystals are strongly heated?
(a) Fe + SO3 (b) FeO + SO2 (c) Fe2O3 + H2S (d) Fe2O3 + SO2 + SO3
Answer: (d)
Solution: After losing water, 2FeSO4 → Fe2O3 + SO2 + SO3. The smell of burning sulphur comes from the sulphur oxides.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science, Ch 1 Chemical Reactions and Equations

62. On heating lead nitrate, brown fumes are seen. These fumes are of:
(a) Nitrogen dioxide (b) Oxygen (c) Nitrous oxide (d) Lead oxide
Answer: (a)
Solution: 2Pb(NO3)2 → 2PbO + 4NO2 + O2. Nitrogen dioxide is the brown gas; oxygen is colourless.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science, Ch 1 Chemical Reactions and Equations

63. Tooth decay starts when the pH of the mouth falls below:
(a) 7.4 (b) 6.5 (c) 8.5 (d) 5.5
Answer: (d)
Solution: Bacteria produce acids from food left in the mouth; tooth enamel starts corroding when the pH is lower than 5.5.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science, Ch 2 Acids, Bases and Salts

64. Which home remedy gives relief from a bee sting?
(a) Vinegar (b) Lemon juice (c) Baking soda (d) Common salt
Answer: (c)
Solution: The sting injects methanoic acid; rubbing a mild base like baking soda neutralises it. Vinegar and lemon juice are acids and will not help.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science, Ch 2 Acids, Bases and Salts

65. Which of the following is an olfactory indicator?
(a) Litmus (b) Clove oil (c) Turmeric (d) Phenolphthalein
Answer: (b)
Solution: Olfactory indicators change their smell in acid or base; onion, vanilla essence and clove oil are examples. The others change colour.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science, Ch 2 Acids, Bases and Salts

66. Litmus solution is extracted from:
(a) Lichen (b) Turmeric (c) Red cabbage (d) Hydrangea
Answer: (a)
Solution: Litmus is a purple dye extracted from lichen, a plant of the division Thallophyta. Red cabbage and turmeric are other natural indicators.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science, Ch 2 Acids, Bases and Salts

67. Electrolysis of brine (sodium chloride solution) in the chlor-alkali process gives:
(a) Sodium hydroxide, chlorine and hydrogen (b) Baking soda, chlorine and oxygen (c) Washing soda, hydrogen and oxygen (d) Sodium metal and chlorine only
Answer: (a)
Solution: 2NaCl + 2H2O → 2NaOH + Cl2 + H2. The name "chlor-alkali" comes from chlorine and the alkali, sodium hydroxide.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science, Ch 2 Acids, Bases and Salts

68. Baking powder is a mixture of:
(a) Washing soda and vinegar (b) Bleaching powder and water (c) Baking soda and a mild edible acid such as tartaric acid (d) Pure sodium carbonate and sugar
Answer: (c)
Solution: Baking soda with tartaric acid releases carbon dioxide on heating or wetting, which makes cakes soft and spongy, and the acid removes the bitter taste of sodium carbonate.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science, Ch 2 Acids, Bases and Salts

69. How many molecules of water of crystallisation are present in one formula unit of washing soda?
(a) 5 (b) 2 (c) 7 (d) 10
Answer: (d)
Solution: Washing soda is Na2CO3·10H2O, so it has 10 molecules of water of crystallisation.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science, Ch 2 Acids, Bases and Salts

70. The blue colour of copper sulphate crystals is due to:
(a) Copper metal (b) Water of crystallisation (c) Sulphur (d) Impurities
Answer: (b)
Solution: CuSO4·5H2O is blue; on heating it loses its water of crystallisation and becomes white anhydrous copper sulphate. Adding water brings the blue colour back.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science, Ch 2 Acids, Bases and Salts

71. An acid reacts with a metal carbonate to give:
(a) Salt and hydrogen (b) Salt, water and carbon dioxide (c) Only a salt (d) A base and water
Answer: (b)
Solution: For example, Na2CO3 + 2HCl → 2NaCl + H2O + CO2. The gas turns lime water milky. Salt and hydrogen are formed with a metal, not a carbonate.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science, Ch 2 Acids, Bases and Salts

72. Milk of magnesia, used as an antacid, is chemically:
(a) Magnesium carbonate (b) Magnesium sulphate (c) Magnesium hydroxide (d) Magnesium oxide
Answer: (c)
Solution: Milk of magnesia is magnesium hydroxide, a mild base that neutralises excess acid in the stomach.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science, Ch 2 Acids, Bases and Salts

73. What is the safe way to dilute a concentrated acid?
(a) Add water to the acid quickly (b) Add the acid slowly to water with constant stirring (c) Heat the acid and then add water (d) Mix both at once in a closed bottle
Answer: (b)
Solution: Dilution is highly exothermic. Adding water to acid can make the mixture splash out and the glass container may break, so acid is always added slowly to water.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science, Ch 2 Acids, Bases and Salts

74. Aqua regia, which can dissolve gold, is a fresh mixture of concentrated hydrochloric acid and concentrated nitric acid in the ratio:
(a) 1 : 3 (b) 3 : 1 (c) 1 : 1 (d) 2 : 1
Answer: (b)
Solution: Aqua regia ("royal water") is 3 parts HCl to 1 part HNO3. Option (a) reverses the ratio — a favourite trap.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science, Ch 3 Metals and Non-metals

75. Hydrogen gas is generally NOT evolved when a metal reacts with nitric acid. Why?
(a) Nitric acid is a weak acid (b) Nitric acid has no hydrogen in it (c) Nitric acid is a base (d) Nitric acid is a strong oxidising agent that oxidises the hydrogen to water
Answer: (d)
Solution: HNO3 oxidises the hydrogen produced to water and itself gets reduced to oxides of nitrogen. Only magnesium and manganese give hydrogen with very dilute nitric acid.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science, Ch 3 Metals and Non-metals
