🪄 Memory Hooks & Short Tricks

🧠 Mnemonic 1: Reactivity series of metals (most to least reactive)
Sentence: "Please Send Cats, Monkeys And Zebras In Large Hired Cages Made of Silver and Gold"
Please → K | Send → Na | Cats → Ca | Monkeys → Mg | And → Al | Zebras → Zn | In → Fe | Large → Pb | Hired → H | Cages → Cu | Made → Hg | Silver → Ag | Gold → Au
Rule: every metal before "Hired" (hydrogen) gives hydrogen gas with dilute acids; copper, mercury, silver and gold do not.

🧠 Mnemonic 2: Isotopes vs Isobars
Rule: "IsotoPes keep the same Protons; IsobArs keep the same mAss."
Isotopes: carbon-12 and carbon-14 (same element). Isobars: argon-40 and calcium-40 (different elements, same mass number 40).

🧠 Mnemonic 3: Who found which particle
Sentence: "Thomson Tagged Electrons, Goldstein Got Protons, Chadwick Chose Neutrons."
Add Rutherford as the "Gold-foil Rutherford" who found the nucleus. Visual: a tailor (Thomson) tagging tiny electrons on a Christmas pudding.

🧠 Mnemonic 4: The two T-fruits
Rule: "Tamarind-Tartaric, TOmato-Oxalic."
Tamarind and tartaric both start with "Ta"; tomato has an "O" for oxalic. These are the two acids students swap most often.

🧠 Mnemonic 5: The rest of the kitchen acids
Story: "Lemon goes to the City, Lassi is Lactic, Vinegar is Acetic, the Ant carries a Methanoic sting."
Lemon / orange → citric | curd, lassi → lactic | vinegar → acetic | ant sting, nettle sting → methanoic.

🧠 Mnemonic 6: Water of crystallisation
Sentence: "Gypsum drinks 2 glasses, Plaster of Paris only half; Blue vitriol 5, Washing soda 10."
CaSO4·2H2O | CaSO4·½H2O | CuSO4·5H2O | Na2CO3·10H2O
Trap check: if the formula shows 2H2O, it is gypsum, not Plaster of Paris.

🧠 Mnemonic 7: Indicator colours
"Phenolphthalein Pinks in base" — colourless in acid, pink in base.
"MORA — Methyl Orange is Red in Acid" — and yellow in base.
"Blue litmus Reddens in acid" — litmus comes from lichen.

🧠 Mnemonic 8: pH in one line
"Below 7 is Sour, 7 is Pure, above 7 is Soapy."
Tooth decay: "five-and-a-half, tooth in half" — decay starts when the pH in the mouth falls below 5.5.

🧠 Mnemonic 9: Brass, bronze and solder
"BraZZ has Zinc" → brass = copper + zinc.
"BronTIN" → bronze = copper + tin.
"Sold a Lead Tin" → solder = lead + tin.

🧠 Mnemonic 10: Mendeleev's three empty seats
Sentence: "Boring Scandal, Allu's Gala, Silly German"
eka-Boron → Scandium | eka-Aluminium → Gallium | eka-Silicon → Germanium.

🧠 Mnemonic 11: Oxidation or reduction?
Rule: "Oxygen in = Oxidised; Oxygen out = reduced."
In CuO + H2 → Cu + H2O, hydrogen takes the oxygen (oxidised); copper oxide gives it away (reduced).

🧠 Mnemonic 12: Separation techniques
Sentence: "Cream Circles, Colours Climb, Camphor Climbs Away."
Cream from milk → centrifugation (circling) | dyes in ink → chromatography (colours climb the paper) | camphor, ammonium chloride → sublimation.

🧠 Mnemonic 13: Hydrocarbon formulas
"ANE adds 2, ENE adds nothing, YNE takes 2 away."
Alkane CnH2n+2 (methane CH4) | Alkene CnH2n (ethene C2H4) | Alkyne CnH2n−2 (ethyne C2H2).
Flame: saturated = clean blue; unsaturated = yellow and sooty.

🧠 Mnemonic 14: Aqua regia
"Three guards, one king" — 3 parts concentrated HCl to 1 part concentrated HNO3. The "royal water" dissolves the king of metals, gold.

🧠 Mnemonic 15: Where dangerous elements sleep
"Sodium and Potassium Sleep in Kerosene; Phosphorus Plays in water."
Sodium and potassium react violently with air and water, so they are kept under kerosene; white phosphorus catches fire in air, so it is kept in water.

⏭️ Skip Strategy

1. If a question asks you to balance a long equation or to find a coefficient, and the first try does not balance within 45 seconds, mark it and move on — come back only after finishing all the one-line fact questions.

2. If a "match the following" lists four salts or four natural acids and you are sure of fewer than two pairs, skip it. If you are sure of two pairs, eliminate options using those pairs before guessing.

3. If a question asks about a rare compound, an unusual alloy or an industrial process that is not in NCERT Class 9–10 Science, skip it in the first round — at the Foundation level such questions are few, and the time is better spent on the reactivity series, salts, acids and discoverers.
