Flashcard Deck: General Chemistry (Foundation)

Card 1
Front: A metal that flows like water on your table at room temperature — which one is it?
Back: Mercury. It is the only metal that is liquid at room temperature.

Card 2
Front: Gallium melts in your palm. Does that make it a liquid metal at room temperature?
Back: No. Gallium (and caesium) melt because of their very low melting points, but at room temperature they are solids. Only mercury is a liquid metal.

Card 3
Front: Which non-metal is a liquid, and which non-metal conducts electricity?
Back: Bromine is the liquid non-metal. Graphite (carbon) is the non-metal that conducts electricity.

Card 4
Front: Why does the water in an earthen pot (matka) stay cool in summer?
Back: Water seeps out through the pores and evaporates. Evaporation takes heat from the remaining water, so it cools.

Card 5
Front: Four scientists, four particles or ideas: who discovered the electron, canal rays, the nucleus and the neutron?
Back: Electron – J.J. Thomson; canal rays – E. Goldstein; nucleus – Ernest Rutherford (gold foil); neutron – James Chadwick (1932).

Card 6
Front: Argon-40 and calcium-40 — isotopes or isobars? (Examiner's trap)
Back: Isobars — different elements with the same mass number. Isotopes have the same atomic number, like carbon-12 and carbon-14.

Card 7
Front: What is the mnemonic to tell isotopes and isobars apart?
Back: "IsotoPes keep the same Protons; IsobArs keep the same mAss."

Card 8
Front: Mendeleev left gaps and gave them funny names. What did eka-boron, eka-aluminium and eka-silicon turn out to be?
Back: Scandium, gallium and germanium respectively.

Card 9
Front: How many groups and periods does the Modern Periodic Table have, and on what is it based?
Back: 18 groups and 7 periods, based on atomic number (Moseley, 1913).

Card 10
Front: In CuO + H2 → Cu + H2O, which substance is oxidised? (Examiner's trap)
Back: Hydrogen is oxidised, because it gains oxygen. Copper oxide loses oxygen and is reduced — choosing CuO is the trap.

Card 11
Front: Why are chips packets puffed up with gas, and which gas is it?
Back: Nitrogen. It keeps oxygen away and prevents rancidity (oxidation of oils and fats).

Card 12
Front: Tamarind and tomato both begin with T. Which acid does each contain?
Back: Tamarind – tartaric acid; tomato – oxalic acid. Also: vinegar – acetic, curd – lactic, lemon/orange – citric, ant sting – methanoic.

Card 13
Front: Below which pH does tooth decay begin?
Back: Below pH 5.5. Toothpastes, which are generally basic, neutralise the excess acid.

Card 14
Front: Gypsum or Plaster of Paris — which one is CaSO4·½H2O?
Back: Plaster of Paris. Gypsum is CaSO4·2H2O. Heating gypsum at 373 K gives Plaster of Paris.

Card 15
Front: What is the mnemonic for the reactivity series of metals?
Back: "Please Send Cats, Monkeys And Zebras In Large Hired Cages Made of Silver and Gold" — K, Na, Ca, Mg, Al, Zn, Fe, Pb, H, Cu, Hg, Ag, Au.

Card 16
Front: Brass and bronze both contain copper. What is the second metal in each?
Back: Brass = copper + zinc; bronze = copper + tin. Solder = lead + tin.

Card 17
Front: Which "royal water" can dissolve gold, and in what ratio is it made?
Back: Aqua regia — a fresh mixture of concentrated hydrochloric acid and concentrated nitric acid in the ratio 3 : 1.

Card 18
Front: Why does carbon form so many compounds?
Back: Catenation (carbon atoms link to each other in long chains and rings) and tetravalency (valency of four).

Card 19
Front: Why is pure ethanoic acid called "glacial" acetic acid?
Back: Its melting point is 290 K, so it often freezes in cold winters, like a glacier.

Card 20
Front: Why does soap fail to lather in hard water, while a detergent works?
Back: Calcium and magnesium salts in hard water react with soap to form insoluble scum. Detergents do not form scum, so they work in hard water too.
