Set 1/6 – General Chemistry MCQs (Foundation)
Questions 1–20: Easy | Questions 21–25: Medium

1. What is the chemical symbol of sodium?
(a) S (b) So (c) Na (d) Sd
Answer: (c)
Solution: The symbol Na comes from the Latin name of sodium, Natrium. S is the symbol of sulphur — a common trap.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Science, Ch 3 Atoms and Molecules

2. Which metal is a liquid at room temperature?
(a) Gallium (b) Mercury (c) Sodium (d) Caesium
Answer: (b)
Solution: Mercury is the only metal that is liquid at room temperature. Gallium and caesium melt on the palm because of their low melting points, but they are solids at room temperature.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science, Ch 3 Metals and Non-metals

3. What is the common name of sodium hydrogencarbonate (NaHCO3)?
(a) Washing soda (b) Baking soda (c) Caustic soda (d) Bleaching powder
Answer: (b)
Solution: NaHCO3 is baking soda. Washing soda is Na2CO3·10H2O and caustic soda is NaOH — the three "sodas" are often swapped in options.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science, Ch 2 Acids, Bases and Salts

4. What is the chemical formula of quicklime?
(a) CaCO3 (b) Ca(OH)2 (c) CaO (d) CaCl2
Answer: (c)
Solution: Quicklime is calcium oxide, CaO. Ca(OH)2 is slaked lime and CaCO3 is limestone.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science, Ch 1 Chemical Reactions and Equations

5. Which gas is released when zinc granules react with dilute sulphuric acid?
(a) Oxygen (b) Carbon dioxide (c) Sulphur dioxide (d) Hydrogen
Answer: (d)
Solution: Zn + H2SO4 → ZnSO4 + H2. The hydrogen gas burns with a pop sound when a burning candle is brought near it.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science, Ch 2 Acids, Bases and Salts

6. What is the pH of pure water at 25 °C?
(a) 0 (b) 5.5 (c) 7 (d) 14
Answer: (c)
Solution: Pure water is neither acidic nor basic. A neutral solution has pH 7.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science, Ch 2 Acids, Bases and Salts

7. What colour does phenolphthalein show in a basic solution?
(a) Colourless (b) Pink (c) Red (d) Yellow
Answer: (b)
Solution: Phenolphthalein is colourless in acid and pink in base. Yellow in base is the colour of methyl orange — do not mix the two indicators.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science, Ch 2 Acids, Bases and Salts

8. Which acid is present in vinegar?
(a) Citric acid (b) Lactic acid (c) Acetic acid (d) Tartaric acid
Answer: (c)
Solution: Vinegar is a dilute solution of acetic acid (ethanoic acid). Citric acid is in lemon and orange, tartaric acid in tamarind.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science, Ch 2 Acids, Bases and Salts

9. Which acid is present in curd (sour milk)?
(a) Lactic acid (b) Oxalic acid (c) Methanoic acid (d) Citric acid
Answer: (a)
Solution: Sour milk and curd contain lactic acid. Oxalic acid is found in tomato.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science, Ch 2 Acids, Bases and Salts

10. The sting of an ant injects which acid?
(a) Acetic acid (b) Methanoic acid (c) Oxalic acid (d) Hydrochloric acid
Answer: (b)
Solution: Ant sting and nettle sting both contain methanoic acid (formic acid), which causes pain and irritation.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science, Ch 2 Acids, Bases and Salts

11. Which is the hardest natural substance known?
(a) Graphite (b) Iron (c) Quartz (d) Diamond
Answer: (d)
Solution: Diamond, an allotrope of carbon, is the hardest natural substance. Graphite is also carbon but is soft and slippery.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science, Ch 3 Metals and Non-metals

12. Which non-metal is a liquid at room temperature?
(a) Mercury (b) Bromine (c) Iodine (d) Chlorine
Answer: (b)
Solution: Bromine is the liquid non-metal. Mercury is also liquid but it is a metal, which is the trap here.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science, Ch 3 Metals and Non-metals

13. What is the atomic number of carbon?
(a) 6 (b) 8 (c) 12 (d) 14
Answer: (a)
Solution: Carbon-12 and carbon-14 are mass numbers of its isotopes, a common trap; the atomic number (protons) of carbon is 6.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Science, Ch 4 Structure of the Atom

14. Who discovered the electron?
(a) J.J. Thomson (b) Ernest Rutherford (c) James Chadwick (d) E. Goldstein
Answer: (a)
Solution: J.J. Thomson discovered the electron. Goldstein discovered canal rays, Chadwick discovered the neutron and Rutherford proposed the nucleus.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Science, Ch 4 Structure of the Atom

15. Who discovered the neutron?
(a) J.J. Thomson (b) Niels Bohr (c) James Chadwick (d) John Dalton
Answer: (c)
Solution: James Chadwick discovered the neutron in 1932. The neutron has no charge and a mass nearly equal to that of a proton.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Science, Ch 4 Structure of the Atom

16. Which metal is stored under kerosene to prevent accidental fire?
(a) Sodium (b) Iron (c) Copper (d) Aluminium
Answer: (a)
Solution: Sodium and potassium react so vigorously with air and water that they are kept in kerosene. White phosphorus, a non-metal, is stored in water instead.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science, Ch 3 Metals and Non-metals

17. What is the chemical formula of common salt?
(a) KCl (b) Na2CO3 (c) CaCl2 (d) NaCl
Answer: (d)
Solution: Common salt is sodium chloride, NaCl. Na2CO3 is sodium carbonate (soda ash).
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science, Ch 2 Acids, Bases and Salts

18. Dry ice is the solid form of which substance?
(a) Water (b) Carbon dioxide (c) Nitrogen (d) Ammonia
Answer: (b)
Solution: Solid carbon dioxide is called dry ice. It changes directly into gas (sublimes) without becoming a liquid.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Science, Ch 1 Matter in Our Surroundings

19. Brass is an alloy of which two metals?
(a) Copper and tin (b) Lead and tin (c) Iron and nickel (d) Copper and zinc
Answer: (d)
Solution: Brass = copper + zinc. Copper + tin is bronze and lead + tin is solder — the classic "similar alloy" trap.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science, Ch 3 Metals and Non-metals

20. Camphor changes directly from solid to vapour on heating. What is this process called?
(a) Evaporation (b) Condensation (c) Sublimation (d) Fusion
Answer: (c)
Solution: Change of a solid directly into vapour without becoming liquid is sublimation. Camphor, naphthalene and ammonium chloride sublime.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Science, Ch 1 Matter in Our Surroundings

21. What is the chemical formula of Plaster of Paris?
(a) CaSO4·2H2O (b) CaSO4·½H2O (c) CaSO4 (d) CaCO3
Answer: (b)
Solution: Plaster of Paris is calcium sulphate hemihydrate, CaSO4·½H2O. CaSO4·2H2O is gypsum — the almost-correct option.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science, Ch 2 Acids, Bases and Salts

22. Which formula does NCERT give for bleaching powder?
(a) CaCl2 (b) NaOCl (c) CaOCl2 (d) CaCO3
Answer: (c)
Solution: Bleaching powder is written as CaOCl2 and is made by passing chlorine over dry slaked lime. NaOCl is sodium hypochlorite, a different bleach.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science, Ch 2 Acids, Bases and Salts

23. Which of the following is a chemical change?
(a) Melting of ice (b) Dissolving sugar in water (c) Rusting of iron (d) Sublimation of camphor
Answer: (c)
Solution: Rusting forms a new substance, hydrated iron(III) oxide, so it is a chemical change. Melting, dissolving and sublimation only change the state or form.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science, Ch 1 Chemical Reactions and Equations

24. An iron nail is dipped in blue copper sulphate solution. Which statement is correct?
(a) The nail becomes coated with silver (b) Nothing happens because copper is more reactive (c) Iron displaces copper and the solution turns light green (d) The solution turns deep blue
Answer: (c)
Solution: Iron is more reactive than copper, so Fe + CuSO4 → FeSO4 + Cu. Brown copper coats the nail and the solution turns light green due to iron sulphate.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science, Ch 1 Chemical Reactions and Equations

25. Isotopes of an element have the same:
(a) Mass number (b) Number of neutrons (c) Atomic number (d) Physical properties
Answer: (c)
Solution: Isotopes have the same atomic number but different mass numbers. Same mass number with different atomic numbers describes isobars — the similar-name trap.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Science, Ch 4 Structure of the Atom
