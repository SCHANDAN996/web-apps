📊 PYQ Pattern Analysis: Awards & Honours

Exams this chapter serves: SSC GD, SSC MTS, RRB Group D, state police / constable exams, and the general-awareness part of other state-level tests.

Note: this is a pattern analysis — what kinds of questions these exams like to ask from this chapter and how they trap you. We do not give year-wise counts or percentages, because no reliable data for them exists. The model questions below are written in the exam style; their sources are the official bodies that give these awards.

1. What Exams Like to Ask (Sub-topics, in plain words)

Field of an award — the most common one-liner: "Dronacharya Award is given in which field?", "Dadasaheb Phalke Award is related to?", "Jnanpith Award is given for?"

Given by / country — "Ramon Magsaysay Award is given by which country?", "Kalinga Prize is given by?", "Who gives the Saraswati Samman?"

First recipients — first Bharat Ratna, first woman Bharat Ratna, first Phalke Award, first Jnanpith, first Khel Ratna, first Param Vir Chakra, first Indian Nobel, first Indian Oscar.

Order and classes — Bharat Ratna and the Padma awards; war-time and peace-time gallantry ladders.

Year instituted / renamed — Bharat Ratna 1954, Padma 1954/1955, Khel Ratna 1991–92/2021, Phalke 1969, Gandhi Peace Prize 1995.

Indian Nobel laureates — name, year and field (Tagore, Raman, Khorana, Mother Teresa, Chandrasekhar, Sen, Ramakrishnan, Satyarthi, Banerjee).

Recent winners — the latest Bharat Ratna, Khel Ratna, Phalke, Jnanpith and Nobel winners. These change every year; always learn them with the year.

2. Exam-wise Style (qualitative)

SSC GD / SSC MTS — short, direct one-liners: field of an award, first recipient, country of an international award. Speed matters.

RRB Group D — similar direct questions, plus recent winners from the year before the exam, often with "Which of the following is NOT …" phrasing.

State police / constable exams — direct facts plus the state's own awards and famous recipients from that state.

Higher exams that use these same basics (CHSL, NTPC, CGL) add statement-based questions and "match the following" sets of award–field or award–country pairs.

3. Top 10 High-Yield Model Questions with Solutions and Trap Analysis

Question 1
The Dronacharya Award is given to:
(a) Best sportspersons (b) Sports coaches (c) Sports journalists (d) Best universities in sports
Answer: (b) Sports coaches
Trap Type: Similar Name — the Arjuna Award goes to players; the MAKA Trophy goes to universities.
15/45-sec Rule: 15-second fact. Score maximizer.
Source: Ministry of Youth Affairs & Sports

Question 2
Who was the first recipient of the Dadasaheb Phalke Award?
(a) Prithviraj Kapoor (b) Raj Kapoor (c) Devika Rani (d) Satyajit Ray
Answer: (c) Devika Rani
Trap Type: Chronological Confusion — the other three are also famous Phalke Award winners, but later.
15/45-sec Rule: 15-second fact.
Source: Ministry of Information & Broadcasting (National Film Awards)

Question 3
Which of the following did NOT receive the Bharat Ratna in 2024?
(a) Karpoori Thakur (b) Pranab Mukherjee (c) Chaudhary Charan Singh (d) M. S. Swaminathan
Answer: (b) Pranab Mukherjee
Trap Type: Negative Phrasing — Pranab Mukherjee is a recent Bharat Ratna too (2019), so he feels right.
15/45-sec Rule: 45 seconds — read "NOT" twice, then recall the five names of 2024.
Source: Ministry of Home Affairs / DD News, March 2024

Question 4
The Ramon Magsaysay Award is given by which country?
(a) Thailand (b) Philippines (c) Indonesia (d) Malaysia
Answer: (b) Philippines
Trap Type: Similar Name — all four are South-East Asian countries.
15/45-sec Rule: 15-second fact.
Source: Ramon Magsaysay Award Foundation

Question 5
Arrange the following Indian Nobel laureates in the order in which they received the prize:
I. Amartya Sen II. C. V. Raman III. Mother Teresa IV. Kailash Satyarthi
(a) II, III, I, IV (b) III, II, I, IV (c) II, I, III, IV (d) II, III, IV, I
Answer: (a) Raman (1930), Mother Teresa (1979), Amartya Sen (1998), Kailash Satyarthi (2014)
Trap Type: Chronological Confusion — Mother Teresa (1979) and Amartya Sen (1998) are often swapped.
15/45-sec Rule: 45-second analytical question. Write the year beside each name first.
Source: nobelprize.org

Question 6
Which of the following is a peace-time gallantry award?
(a) Maha Vir Chakra (b) Vir Chakra (c) Kirti Chakra (d) Param Vir Chakra
Answer: (c) Kirti Chakra
Trap Type: Almost True Statement — all four end in "Chakra"; only the Ashoka–Kirti–Shaurya series is for peace time.
15/45-sec Rule: 15-second fact if you know "P-M-V for war, A-K-S for peace".
Source: Ministry of Defence (gallantryawards.gov.in)

Question 7
Consider the following statements:
I. The Jnanpith Award is given by the Bharatiya Jnanpith.
II. The first Jnanpith Award was given to Sumitranandan Pant.
Which of the statements given is/are correct?
(a) Only I (b) Only II (c) Both I and II (d) Neither I nor II
Answer: (a) Only I
Trap Type: Almost True Statement — Pant was the first Hindi writer to win (1968); the first winner was G. Sankara Kurup (1965).
15/45-sec Rule: 45 seconds — check each statement separately.
Source: Bharatiya Jnanpith

Question 8
The Major Dhyan Chand Khel Ratna was earlier known as:
(a) Arjuna Award (b) Rajiv Gandhi Khel Ratna (c) Dhyan Chand Award (d) Bharat Khel Ratna
Answer: (b) Rajiv Gandhi Khel Ratna
Trap Type: Similar Name — "Dhyan Chand Award" was a separate lifetime-achievement award.
15/45-sec Rule: 15-second fact.
Source: Ministry of Youth Affairs & Sports (renamed 6 August 2021)

Question 9
Who was the first Indian to win an Oscar?
(a) Satyajit Ray (b) A. R. Rahman (c) Bhanu Athaiya (d) Resul Pookutty
Answer: (c) Bhanu Athaiya
Trap Type: Chronological Confusion — Ray's Honorary Oscar came in 1992, Rahman and Pookutty won in 2009; Athaiya won in 1983.
15/45-sec Rule: 15-second fact.
Source: Academy of Motion Picture Arts and Sciences

Question 10
The Nobel Peace Prize is presented in:
(a) Stockholm (b) Geneva (c) Oslo (d) Copenhagen
Answer: (c) Oslo
Trap Type: Almost True Statement — all other Nobel Prizes are given in Stockholm, so Stockholm looks right.
15/45-sec Rule: 15-second fact.
Source: nobelprize.org

4. How to Use This Analysis

Make a "four-box card" for every award: What field? Who gives it? Since when? First winner? These four boxes answer most questions.

Every time you see "NOT", "except" or "incorrectly matched", slow down — that is a 45-second question, not a 15-second one.

For recent winners, keep a separate page with the year written first ("2024 Bharat Ratna: …"). Never learn a recent name without its year.
