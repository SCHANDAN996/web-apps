🪄 Memory Hooks & Short Tricks

🧠 Mnemonic 1: First Bharat Ratna trio (1954)
Code: "RRR" — Rajagopalachari, Radhakrishnan, Raman.
Visual: the poster of the film RRR, but the three heroes are a statesman, a philosopher and a scientist.

🧠 Mnemonic 2: Civilian ladder
Sentence: "Bharat's Very Big Star" — Bharat Ratna → Vibhushan → Bhushan → Shri.
The longer the Padma name, the higher the award: Vi-bhushan (longest) > Bhushan > Shri (shortest).

🧠 Mnemonic 3: Gallantry — war and peace
"War = P-M-V (Param, Maha, Vir); Peace = A-K-S (Ashoka, Kirti, Shaurya)."
Picture: in war, soldiers shout "P-M-V!"; in peace, Emperor Ashoka (who gave up war) sits on top of the peace ladder.

🧠 Mnemonic 4: Years of the gallantry ladders
"50 for war, 52 for peace, 67 for new names."
War-time series 1950; Ashoka Chakra Class I–III 1952; renamed Ashoka–Kirti–Shaurya 1967.

🧠 Mnemonic 5: Bharat Ratna "firsts" in a line
"Shastri Saluted, Indira Inspired, Ghaffar Gave, Sachin Smashed."
Shastri — first posthumous (1966); Indira — first woman (1971); Ghaffar Khan — first non-Indian citizen (1987); Sachin — first sportsperson and youngest (2014).

🧠 Mnemonic 6: Bharat Ratna 2024 — five names
"KAC-RS": Karpoori Thakur, Advani, Charan Singh, Rao (Narasimha), Swaminathan.
Hook: two former Prime Ministers (Charan Singh, Narasimha Rao), one former Deputy Prime Minister (Advani), one former Chief Minister of Bihar (Karpoori Thakur) and one scientist (Swaminathan).

🧠 Mnemonic 7: Player or coach?
"Arjuna shoots, Drona teaches." Arjuna Award → players (1961); Dronacharya Award → coaches (1985).

🧠 Mnemonic 8: Khel Ratna's two names
"Born 91, Renamed 21." Rajiv Gandhi Khel Ratna from 1991–92 (first: Viswanathan Anand); Major Dhyan Chand Khel Ratna from 2021.

🧠 Mnemonic 9: Phalke Award
"69 — Phalke's 100th, Devika first."
The award began in 1969, Phalke's birth centenary year; Devika Rani was the first winner.

🧠 Mnemonic 10: Jnanpith
"Set up in 61, first flute in 65."
Established 1961; first award in 1965 to G. Sankara Kurup for "Odakkuzhal" (The Bamboo Flute).
And: "Pant came in 68" — first Hindi winner Sumitranandan Pant, 1968.

🧠 Mnemonic 11: Who gives which literary award?
"Jnanpith gives Jnanpith; Birla gives Saraswati and Vyas; Akademi gives Akademi."
Bharatiya Jnanpith → Jnanpith Award; K. K. Birla Foundation → Saraswati Samman, Vyas Samman; Sahitya Akademi → Sahitya Akademi Award.

🧠 Mnemonic 12: Nobel — places and years
"Peace in Oslo, the rest in Stockholm." First prizes 1901; Economics 1969.
Indian years: "13 Tagore, 30 Raman, 79 Teresa, 98 Sen, 14 Satyarthi, 19 Banerjee."

🧠 Mnemonic 13: Magsaysay
"M for Manila, M for Magsaysay." The award is from the Philippines (capital Manila), started 1957; first Indian — Vinoba Bhave (1958), the "land-gift" (Bhoodan) saint.

🧠 Mnemonic 14: Booker twins
"Booker = written in English; International Booker = translated into English."
Geetanjali Shree (Hindi, 2022) and Banu Mushtaq (Kannada, 2025) won the International Booker, not the Booker.

🧠 Mnemonic 15: First Indian Oscar
"Bhanu dressed Gandhi." Bhanu Athaiya, costume design for "Gandhi", 1983.

⏭️ Skip Strategy

1. If a question asks for the exact cash amount of an award and you have not read it in an official notice, skip it in the first round — prize money is revised over time and the options are built to confuse.

2. If a "recent winner" question is about an award from a field you have never followed (for example, a minor international prize) and you cannot eliminate even two options, skip it. Spend that time on the field / country / first-winner questions, which are sure marks.

3. If an "arrange in chronological order" question has five or more awards or winners and you can place fewer than two with certainty, leave it for the end — it eats 45 seconds or more.
