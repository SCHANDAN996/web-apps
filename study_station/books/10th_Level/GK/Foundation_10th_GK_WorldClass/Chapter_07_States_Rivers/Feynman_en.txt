🧒 Feynman Corner: Why do almost all big rivers of South India run east — and why do the Narmada and Tapi run west?

This is the fact that confuses students most in this chapter. The Godavari rises near Nashik, not far from the Arabian Sea — yet it travels right across India to reach the Bay of Bengal. Meanwhile the Narmada and Tapi go the other way. Let's make it simple.

The daily-life example: a tilted roof

Imagine the roof of your house is tilted — high on one side, low on the other. Pour a bucket of water on the high edge. Where does the water go? Down the slope, to the low edge. It does not care how close the other edge is.

Peninsular India is that tilted roof. Its high edge is the Western Ghats, close to the west coast. The land slopes gently down towards the east. So rain that falls on the Western Ghats runs downhill — east — to the Bay of Bengal. That is why the Godavari, Krishna, Kaveri and Mahanadi all flow east. The Western Ghats are the "ridge of the roof", which geographers call the main water divide.

Now the Narmada and Tapi: a crack in the roof

Suppose your roof has a long crack running from the middle towards the west. Water that falls into the crack flows along the crack, not down the main slope. Between the Vindhya and Satpura ranges, the Earth's crust long ago cracked and sank, making a long trough called a rift valley. The Narmada and the Tapi flow along such troughs — and the troughs point west. So these two rivers run west into the Arabian Sea.

Bonus: why estuaries, not deltas?

A delta forms when a river dumps lots of silt at its mouth, like sand piling up at the end of a pipe. East-flowing rivers cross wide plains and carry plenty of silt, so they build deltas. The Narmada and Tapi flow through narrow valleys of hard rock and carry little silt to deposit, so their mouths stay open as estuaries.

The unforgettable trick

"Ghats are the roof, east is the floor — Narmada and Tapi use the crack door."
Roof slope = east-flowing rivers with deltas. Crack (rift) = west-flowing Narmada and Tapi with estuaries.
