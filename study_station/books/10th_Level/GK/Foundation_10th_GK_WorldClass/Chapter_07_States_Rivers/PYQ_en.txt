📊 PYQ Pattern Analysis: States & Rivers

Exams this chapter serves: SSC GD, SSC MTS, RRB Group D, state police / constable exams, and the general-awareness part of other state-level tests.

Note: this is a pattern analysis — what kinds of questions these exams like to ask from this chapter and how they trap you. We do not give year-wise counts or percentages, because no reliable data for them exists. The model questions below are written in the exam style; their sources are the NCERT chapters they test.

1. What Exams Like to Ask (Sub-topics, in plain words)

Rivers and cities — the most common one-liner: "Which city is on the bank of river X?" (Kota–Chambal, Surat–Tapi, Ujjain–Shipra, Hyderabad–Musi, Cuttack–Mahanadi).

Dams and rivers — a favourite pair-matching area: Hirakud, Bhakra, Tehri, Sardar Sarovar, Nagarjuna Sagar, Mettur, Krishnaraja Sagar.

Source of rivers — Amarkantak (Narmada, Son), Multai (Tapi), Trimbakeshwar (Godavari), Mahabaleshwar (Krishna), Brahmagiri (Kaveri), Gangotri (Bhagirathi), Yamunotri (Yamuna).

Confluences and names — Devprayag, Prayagraj, the Brahmaputra's names (Tsangpo, Dihang, Jamuna), the Ganga as Padma.

Titles — "Sorrow of Bihar" (Kosi), "Sorrow of Bengal" (Damodar), "Dakshin Ganga" (Godavari).

State formation — first linguistic state, 1956 Act, the 2000 trio, Telangana 2014, J&K and Ladakh UTs 2019, count of states and UTs.

Capitals and borders — North-East capitals, states on the Tropic of Cancer, states bordering Bangladesh, Nepal, Myanmar.

Largest / smallest — by area and by population (Census 2011).

2. Exam-wise Style (qualitative)

SSC GD / SSC MTS — short, direct one-liners: capitals, river–city, dam–river, sources. Speed matters.

RRB Group D — similar direct questions, often with "Which of the following is NOT …" phrasing and dam–river pairs.

State police / constable exams — direct facts plus extra weight on the home state's rivers, dams and districts.

Higher exams that use these same basics (CHSL, NTPC, CGL) add statement-based and "arrange in order" questions.

3. Top 10 High-Yield Model Questions with Solutions and Trap Analysis

Question 1
The Bhagirathi and the Alaknanda meet to form the Ganga at:
(a) Rudraprayag (b) Devprayag (c) Haridwar (d) Karnaprayag
Answer: (b) Devprayag
Trap Type: Similar Name — all four are holy places on the same route; Rudraprayag and Karnaprayag are other Prayags of the Alaknanda.
15/45-sec Rule: 15-second fact. Score maximizer.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Geography, Ch. 3 Drainage

Question 2
Sardar Sarovar Dam is built on which river?
(a) Tapi (b) Mahi (c) Narmada (d) Sabarmati
Answer: (c) Narmada
Trap Type: Almost True Statement — all four are west-flowing rivers of Gujarat; the Tapi is the closest look-alike.
15/45-sec Rule: 15-second fact.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Geography, Ch. 3 Water Resources

Question 3
Which of the following states does NOT share a border with Bangladesh?
(a) Tripura (b) Meghalaya (c) Manipur (d) Mizoram
Answer: (c) Manipur
Trap Type: Negative Phrasing — a student who misses "NOT" picks the first familiar border state.
15/45-sec Rule: 45 seconds — read the question twice, then run through the five states (WB, Assam, Meghalaya, Tripura, Mizoram).
Source: NCERT Class 9 Geography, Ch. 1 India – Size and Location

Question 4
The Krishnaraja Sagar dam is built on:
(a) Krishna (b) Kaveri (c) Tungabhadra (d) Godavari
Answer: (b) Kaveri
Trap Type: Similar Name — "Krishnaraja" pulls students towards the Krishna.
15/45-sec Rule: 15-second fact, if you have learnt the trap.
Source: PYQ-style

Question 5
Arrange the following states in the order of their formation:
I. Haryana II. Nagaland III. Sikkim IV. Telangana
(a) I, II, III, IV (b) II, I, III, IV (c) II, III, I, IV (d) I, III, II, IV
Answer: (b) Nagaland (1963), Haryana (1966), Sikkim (1975), Telangana (2014)
Trap Type: Chronological Confusion — Nagaland and Haryana are only three years apart.
15/45-sec Rule: 45-second analytical question. Write the years beside each name first.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Political Science, Ch. 1

Question 6
Which river is known as the "Sorrow of Bihar"?
(a) Gandak (b) Damodar (c) Kosi (d) Son
Answer: (c) Kosi
Trap Type: Similar Name — the Damodar is the "Sorrow of Bengal".
15/45-sec Rule: 15-second fact.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Geography, Ch. 3 Drainage System

Question 7
Consider the following statements:
I. The Narmada rises at Amarkantak.
II. The Narmada forms a large delta in the Gulf of Khambhat.
Which of the statements given is/are correct?
(a) Only I (b) Only II (c) Both I and II (d) Neither I nor II
Answer: (a) Only I
Trap Type: Almost True Statement — statement II sounds right because big rivers usually form deltas, but the Narmada forms an estuary.
15/45-sec Rule: 45 seconds — check each statement separately.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Geography, Ch. 3 Drainage

Question 8
The Tropic of Cancer does NOT pass through:
(a) Jharkhand (b) Odisha (c) Tripura (d) Chhattisgarh
Answer: (b) Odisha
Trap Type: Negative Phrasing + map memory — Odisha is a neighbour of Jharkhand and Chhattisgarh, so it "feels" right.
15/45-sec Rule: 15 seconds if you know the 8-state mnemonic.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Geography, Ch. 1 India – Size and Location

Question 9
In Bangladesh, the Brahmaputra is known as the:
(a) Padma (b) Meghna (c) Jamuna (d) Tsangpo
Answer: (c) Jamuna
Trap Type: Similar Name — Padma is the Ganga in Bangladesh; Tsangpo is the Tibetan name.
15/45-sec Rule: 15-second fact.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Geography, Ch. 3 Drainage

Question 10
Ladakh became a Union Territory on:
(a) 5 August 2019 (b) 31 October 2019 (c) 26 January 2020 (d) 15 August 2019
Answer: (b) 31 October 2019
Trap Type: Chronological Confusion — 5 August 2019 is when the change was announced in Parliament; 26 January 2020 is the Dadra–Daman merger.
15/45-sec Rule: 15-second fact.
Source: Government of India (Ministry of Home Affairs)

4. How to Use This Analysis

Make your own list of river–city and dam–river pairs; these are the quickest marks in the paper.

Every time you see "NOT", "except" or "incorrectly matched", slow down — that is a 45-second question, not a 15-second one.

For dates, learn the sequence first (1953 → 1956 → 1960 → 1963 → 1966 → 1971 → 1972 → 1975 → 1987 → 2000 → 2014 → 2019 → 2020) and then attach states to each year.
