Set 2/6 – States & Rivers MCQs (Foundation)
Questions 26–50: Medium

26. The Chenab is formed by the union of which two streams?
(a) Chandra and Bhaga (b) Bhagirathi and Alaknanda (c) Dibang and Lohit (d) Tunga and Bhadra
Answer: (a)
Solution: The Chandra and the Bhaga meet at Tandi in Lahaul (Himachal Pradesh) to form the Chenab, also called the Chandrabhaga. Tunga and Bhadra form the Tungabhadra.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Geography (India: Physical Environment), Ch. 3 Drainage System

27. The Jhelum rises from a spring at:
(a) Gaumukh (b) Talakaveri (c) Verinag (d) Multai
Answer: (c)
Solution: The Jhelum rises from the Verinag spring in the south-east of the Kashmir Valley. Gaumukh is the snout of the Gangotri glacier.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Geography (India: Physical Environment), Ch. 3 Drainage System

28. Which lake does the Jhelum flow through?
(a) Loktak (b) Wular (c) Pangong (d) Chilika
Answer: (b)
Solution: The Jhelum flows through the Wular Lake in Jammu & Kashmir, the largest freshwater lake of India as per NCERT. Loktak is in Manipur.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Geography (Contemporary India I), Ch. 3 Drainage

29. Under the Indus Waters Treaty of 1960, which rivers were allotted to India for unrestricted use?
(a) Indus, Jhelum and Chenab (b) Ravi, Beas and Sutlej (c) Jhelum, Ravi and Beas (d) Indus, Sutlej and Chenab
Answer: (b)
Solution: India got the three eastern rivers (Ravi, Beas, Sutlej); the western rivers (Indus, Jhelum, Chenab) went mainly to Pakistan. Trap: option mixing one eastern and one western river.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Geography (Contemporary India I), Ch. 3 Drainage

30. The Sutlej enters India through which pass?
(a) Nathu La (b) Zoji La (c) Bomdi La (d) Shipki La
Answer: (d)
Solution: The Sutlej rises near Lake Mansarovar in Tibet and enters India (Himachal Pradesh) at Shipki La.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Geography (India: Physical Environment), Ch. 3 Drainage System

31. The Beas meets the Sutlej at:
(a) Harike (Punjab) (b) Tandi (Himachal Pradesh) (c) Devprayag (Uttarakhand) (d) Ropar (Punjab)
Answer: (a)
Solution: The Beas joins the Sutlej at Harike in Punjab. Tandi is where the Chandra and Bhaga meet.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Geography (India: Physical Environment), Ch. 3 Drainage System

32. Which state was carved out of Punjab on 1 November 1966?
(a) Himachal Pradesh (b) Haryana (c) Uttarakhand (d) Jammu & Kashmir
Answer: (b)
Solution: Haryana was formed from Punjab on 1 November 1966. Trap: Himachal Pradesh got some hill areas of Punjab then, but became a full state only in 1971.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Political Science (Politics in India since Independence), Ch. 1 Challenges of Nation Building

33. Sikkim became a state of India in:
(a) 1950 (b) 1972 (c) 1975 (d) 1987
Answer: (c)
Solution: Sikkim became the 22nd state through the 36th Constitutional Amendment, effective in 1975.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Political Science (Politics in India since Independence), Ch. 1 Challenges of Nation Building

34. Which three states were created in November 2000?
(a) Chhattisgarh, Uttarakhand and Jharkhand (b) Telangana, Jharkhand and Goa (c) Mizoram, Arunachal Pradesh and Goa (d) Meghalaya, Manipur and Tripura
Answer: (a)
Solution: Chhattisgarh (from Madhya Pradesh), Uttarakhand (from Uttar Pradesh) and Jharkhand (from Bihar) were created in November 2000. Mizoram, Arunachal Pradesh and Goa became states in 1987.
Source: Government of India (Ministry of Home Affairs) notification

35. Arrange the following states in the order in which they were formed in November 2000:
I. Jharkhand
II. Chhattisgarh
III. Uttarakhand
(a) I, II, III (b) III, II, I (c) II, I, III (d) II, III, I
Answer: (d)
Solution: Chhattisgarh came first (1 November), then Uttarakhand (9 November), then Jharkhand (15 November 2000). Trap type: chronological confusion within the same month.
Source: Government of India (Ministry of Home Affairs) notification

36. The Madras State was renamed Tamil Nadu in:
(a) 1956 (b) 1969 (c) 1973 (d) 1987
Answer: (b)
Solution: Madras State was renamed Tamil Nadu with effect from 14 January 1969. Trap: 1973 is the year Mysore became Karnataka; the answer is 1969.
Source: Government of India (Ministry of Home Affairs) notification

37. The Mysore State was renamed Karnataka in:
(a) 1956 (b) 1966 (c) 1973 (d) 1980
Answer: (c)
Solution: Mysore State was renamed Karnataka on 1 November 1973. The state itself was reorganised in 1956, but the new name came in 1973.
Source: Government of India (Ministry of Home Affairs) notification

38. Which of the following is a right-bank tributary of the Ganga?
(a) Gomti (b) Ghaghara (c) Son (d) Gandak
Answer: (c)
Solution: The Son (from the Amarkantak plateau) joins the Ganga from the south, i.e. the right bank. The Gomti, Ghaghara and Gandak join from the left (north) bank.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Geography (Contemporary India I), Ch. 3 Drainage

39. The Chambal rises in the:
(a) Janapav hills near Mhow, Madhya Pradesh (b) Amarkantak plateau, Madhya Pradesh (c) Aravalli hills near Ajmer, Rajasthan (d) Satpura range near Multai, Madhya Pradesh
Answer: (a)
Solution: The Chambal rises near Mhow in the Malwa plateau (Janapav hills). The Luni rises in the Aravallis near Ajmer and the Tapi near Multai.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Geography (India: Physical Environment), Ch. 3 Drainage System

40. The Chambal is a tributary of which river?
(a) Son (b) Yamuna (c) Narmada (d) Betwa
Answer: (b)
Solution: The Chambal flows north-east through Rajasthan and Madhya Pradesh and joins the Yamuna in Uttar Pradesh. The Betwa is also a Yamuna tributary, not a river the Chambal joins.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Geography (Contemporary India I), Ch. 3 Drainage

41. Which river valley is known for its badlands (deep ravines)?
(a) Kosi (b) Teesta (c) Periyar (d) Chambal
Answer: (d)
Solution: The Chambal valley has badlands formed by gully erosion of soft soil. The Kosi is known for floods and change of course, not ravines.
Source: PYQ-style

42. Which river is called the 'Sorrow of Bengal'?
(a) Hooghly (b) Damodar (c) Teesta (d) Kosi
Answer: (b)
Solution: The Damodar, which flows through the Chota Nagpur plateau into West Bengal, was called the 'Sorrow of Bengal' due to floods. Trap: the Kosi is the 'Sorrow of Bihar'.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Geography (India: Physical Environment), Ch. 3 Drainage System

43. Where does the Tapi rise?
(a) Satpura range near Multai, Betul district (Madhya Pradesh) (b) Amarkantak hills (Madhya Pradesh) (c) Trimbakeshwar, Nashik (Maharashtra) (d) Mahabaleshwar (Maharashtra)
Answer: (a)
Solution: NCERT: the Tapi rises in the Satpura ranges in the Betul district of Madhya Pradesh (Multai). Amarkantak is the source of the Narmada.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Geography (Contemporary India I), Ch. 3 Drainage

44. Which city stands on the Tapi?
(a) Bharuch (b) Ahmedabad (c) Surat (d) Vadodara
Answer: (c)
Solution: Surat is on the Tapi near its mouth. Trap: Bharuch is near the mouth of the Narmada, the 'twin' west-flowing river.
Source: PYQ-style

45. The Sabarmati flows past which city?
(a) Surat (b) Ahmedabad (c) Rajkot (d) Bhopal
Answer: (b)
Solution: The Sabarmati rises in the Aravallis and flows past Gandhinagar and Ahmedabad into the Gulf of Khambhat.
Source: PYQ-style

46. The Luni river of Rajasthan finally ends in the:
(a) Gulf of Khambhat (b) Sambhar Lake (c) Arabian Sea near Dwarka (d) Rann of Kutch
Answer: (d)
Solution: The Luni rises in the Aravallis near Ajmer and ends in the marshy Rann of Kutch without reaching the open sea. Sambhar is a salt lake, not the Luni's end point.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Geography (India: Physical Environment), Ch. 3 Drainage System

47. The Brahmaputra enters India in which state?
(a) Assam (b) Sikkim (c) Arunachal Pradesh (d) Meghalaya
Answer: (c)
Solution: After flowing east through Tibet, the river takes a U-turn and enters India in Arunachal Pradesh. Trap: Assam is where it gets the name Brahmaputra, not where it enters India.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Geography (Contemporary India I), Ch. 3 Drainage

48. In Arunachal Pradesh, the Brahmaputra is known as the:
(a) Dihang (Siang) (b) Tsangpo (c) Jamuna (d) Subansiri
Answer: (a)
Solution: It is called the Dihang (Siang) in Arunachal Pradesh. Tsangpo is its Tibetan name; the Subansiri is a separate tributary.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Geography (Contemporary India I), Ch. 3 Drainage

49. Before entering India, the Brahmaputra takes a U-turn around which peak?
(a) Kangchenjunga (b) Namcha Barwa (c) Nanda Devi (d) Kamet
Answer: (b)
Solution: NCERT: on reaching the Namcha Barwa peak, the river takes a U-turn and enters Arunachal Pradesh through a gorge.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Geography (Contemporary India I), Ch. 3 Drainage

50. Consider the following statements about the Kosi:
I. It is a tributary of the Ganga that flows through Bihar.
II. It joins the Ganga from its right (south) bank.
Which of the statements given is/are correct?
(a) Only I (b) Only II (c) Both I and II (d) Neither I nor II
Answer: (a)
Solution: Statement I is correct. Statement II is wrong: the Kosi comes from the Himalayas (Nepal) and joins the Ganga from the left (north) bank.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Geography (Contemporary India I), Ch. 3 Drainage
