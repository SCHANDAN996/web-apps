Set 5/6 – States & Rivers MCQs (Foundation)

101. According to Census 2011, which is the most populous state of India?
(a) Maharashtra (b) Uttar Pradesh (c) Bihar (d) West Bengal
Answer: (b)
Solution: Uttar Pradesh had the largest population in Census 2011, followed by Maharashtra and Bihar. Trap: Rajasthan is largest by area, not population.
Source: Census of India 2011 (Office of the Registrar General of India)

102. According to Census 2011, which is the least populous state of India?
(a) Goa (b) Sikkim (c) Mizoram (d) Arunachal Pradesh
Answer: (b)
Solution: Sikkim has the smallest population. Trap type: similar fact — Goa is the smallest state by area, so it is a tempting wrong choice.
Source: Census of India 2011 (Office of the Registrar General of India)

103. According to Census 2011, which state has the highest sex ratio (females per 1000 males)?
(a) Kerala (b) Tamil Nadu (c) Haryana (d) Goa
Answer: (a)
Solution: Kerala had the highest sex ratio (1084) in Census 2011, while Haryana had the lowest among states.
Source: Census of India 2011 (Office of the Registrar General of India)

104. According to Census 2011, which state has the highest population density?
(a) West Bengal (b) Kerala (c) Bihar (d) Uttar Pradesh
Answer: (c)
Solution: Bihar had the highest density among states (1106 persons per sq km). Trap: Uttar Pradesh has the largest population, but Bihar packs more people per sq km.
Source: Census of India 2011 (Office of the Registrar General of India)

105. Orissa was officially renamed Odisha in which year?
(a) 2007 (b) 2011 (c) 2014 (d) 2000
Answer: (b)
Solution: Orissa became Odisha in 2011. Trap: 2007 is when Uttaranchal became Uttarakhand; the answer here is 2011.
Source: Government of India (Ministry of Home Affairs) notification

106. Uttaranchal was renamed Uttarakhand with effect from 1 January of which year?
(a) 2000 (b) 2011 (c) 2007 (d) 2014
Answer: (c)
Solution: The state was created as Uttaranchal in 2000 and renamed Uttarakhand from 1 January 2007.
Source: Government of India (Ministry of Home Affairs) notification

107. Mizoram and Arunachal Pradesh became full states in:
(a) 1972 (b) 1975 (c) 1987 (d) 2000
Answer: (c)
Solution: Both became states on 20 February 1987, and Goa followed on 30 May 1987.
Source: Government of India (Ministry of Home Affairs) notification

108. Before becoming a state in 1987, Goa was part of which Union Territory?
(a) Goa, Daman and Diu (b) Dadra and Nagar Haveli (c) Puducherry (d) Lakshadweep
Answer: (a)
Solution: Goa was part of the UT of Goa, Daman and Diu; when Goa became a state, Daman and Diu remained a separate UT.
Source: Government of India (Ministry of Home Affairs) notification

109. Which three states became full states on 21 January 1972?
(a) Mizoram, Arunachal Pradesh and Goa (b) Nagaland, Sikkim and Assam (c) Haryana, Himachal Pradesh and Punjab (d) Manipur, Tripura and Meghalaya
Answer: (d)
Solution: Manipur, Tripura and Meghalaya became states on 21 January 1972 under the North-Eastern Areas (Reorganisation) Act, 1971. Mizoram, Arunachal Pradesh and Goa came in 1987.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Political Science (Politics in India since Independence), Ch. 1 Challenges of Nation Building

110. Nagaland became a state of India in:
(a) 1963 (b) 1972 (c) 1956 (d) 1987
Answer: (a)
Solution: Nagaland was carved out of Assam and became a state on 1 December 1963 — the first new state of the North-East after Independence; answer 1963.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Political Science (Politics in India since Independence), Ch. 1 Challenges of Nation Building

111. Himachal Pradesh became a full state in:
(a) 1966 (b) 1971 (c) 1972 (d) 1956
Answer: (b)
Solution: Trap: in 1966 Himachal (then a UT) only received hill areas from Punjab. It became a full state on 25 January 1971.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Political Science (Politics in India since Independence), Ch. 1 Challenges of Nation Building

112. The States Reorganisation Act, which reorganised state boundaries largely on linguistic lines, was passed in:
(a) 1950 (b) 1953 (c) 1956 (d) 1960
Answer: (c)
Solution: The States Reorganisation Commission was set up in 1953; on its report, the States Reorganisation Act was passed in 1956.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Political Science (Politics in India since Independence), Ch. 1 Challenges of Nation Building

113. Who chaired the States Reorganisation Commission set up in 1953?
(a) Fazl Ali (b) K. M. Panikkar (c) H. N. Kunzru (d) Potti Sriramulu
Answer: (a)
Solution: Justice Fazl Ali was the chairman; K. M. Panikkar and H. N. Kunzru were members. Potti Sriramulu was the leader whose fast led to Andhra State.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Political Science (Politics in India since Independence), Ch. 1 Challenges of Nation Building

114. When rivers flow outward in all directions from a central highland, such as the Amarkantak range, the drainage pattern is called:
(a) Dendritic (b) Radial (c) Trellis (d) Centripetal
Answer: (b)
Solution: Rivers flowing out in all directions from a central peak or dome form a radial pattern. Trap: centripetal is the reverse — rivers flowing into a central lake or basin.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Geography (Contemporary India I), Ch. 3 Drainage

115. When streams from all directions drain into a lake or depression, as at Loktak Lake in Manipur, the drainage pattern is:
(a) Radial (b) Trellis (c) Centripetal (d) Dendritic
Answer: (c)
Solution: Rivers discharging into a central lake or depression from all sides form a centripetal pattern (NCERT example: Loktak Lake). Radial is the opposite case.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Geography (India: Physical Environment), Ch. 3 Drainage System

116. A drainage pattern that looks like the branches of a tree is called:
(a) Radial (b) Trellis (c) Centripetal (d) Dendritic
Answer: (d)
Solution: The dendritic pattern looks like the branches of a tree and develops where the rock is uniform; it is common in the northern plains. The trellis pattern forms in folded rocks of alternating hardness.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Geography (Contemporary India I), Ch. 3 Drainage

117. Which of the following is a feature of Himalayan rivers?
(a) They are perennial, fed by both melting snow and rain (b) They are seasonal and depend only on rainfall (c) They have short and shallow courses (d) They mostly end in estuaries on the west coast
Answer: (a)
Solution: Himalayan rivers are perennial because they get water from melting snow as well as rain. Seasonal flow and short, shallow courses are features of peninsular rivers.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Geography (Contemporary India I), Ch. 3 Drainage

118. Which city lies on the water divide between the Indus and the Ganga river systems?
(a) Chandigarh (b) Ambala (c) Ludhiana (d) Dehradun
Answer: (b)
Solution: NCERT: Ambala is located on the water divide between the Indus and Ganga systems. Ludhiana is on the Sutlej side, i.e. in the Indus system.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Geography (Contemporary India I), Ch. 3 Drainage

119. The Sundarban delta gets its name from:
(a) a king named Sundar (b) the Sundar river (c) the Sundari tree (d) the beauty of its tigers
Answer: (c)
Solution: NCERT: the Sundarban delta is named after the Sundari tree, which grows well in its marshland. It is also the home of the Royal Bengal tiger.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Geography (Contemporary India I), Ch. 3 Drainage

120. After entering Bangladesh, the main stream of the Ganga is known as the:
(a) Meghna (b) Jamuna (c) Hooghly (d) Padma
Answer: (d)
Solution: In Bangladesh the Ganga is called the Padma. Trap type: similar name — Jamuna is the Brahmaputra in Bangladesh, and the Meghna is the combined stream lower down.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Geography (Contemporary India I), Ch. 3 Drainage

121. Arrange the following Prayags of the Alaknanda in order from upstream to downstream:
I. Rudraprayag
II. Vishnuprayag
III. Devprayag
IV. Nandprayag
V. Karnaprayag
(a) II, IV, V, I, III (b) IV, II, V, I, III (c) II, V, IV, I, III (d) II, IV, I, V, III
Answer: (a)
Solution: Going downstream: Vishnuprayag, Nandprayag, Karnaprayag, Rudraprayag and finally Devprayag, where the Alaknanda meets the Bhagirathi. Memory hook: 'Vi-Na-Ka-Ru-De'.
Source: PYQ-style

122. Which pair of Prayag and the river that joins the Alaknanda there is correctly matched?
(a) Rudraprayag – Pindar (b) Karnaprayag – Mandakini (c) Nandprayag – Nandakini (d) Vishnuprayag – Bhagirathi
Answer: (c)
Solution: The Nandakini joins at Nandprayag. Correct pairs are Vishnuprayag – Dhauliganga, Karnaprayag – Pindar, Rudraprayag – Mandakini and Devprayag – Bhagirathi; the wrong options swap these.
Source: PYQ-style

123. The Krishna flows through which group of states?
(a) Maharashtra, Karnataka, Telangana, Andhra Pradesh (b) Maharashtra, Madhya Pradesh, Telangana, Andhra Pradesh (c) Karnataka, Tamil Nadu, Andhra Pradesh, Telangana (d) Maharashtra, Karnataka, Kerala, Andhra Pradesh
Answer: (a)
Solution: The Krishna rises at Mahabaleshwar (Maharashtra), crosses Karnataka, enters Telangana and reaches the Bay of Bengal through Andhra Pradesh. Tamil Nadu is a Kaveri state, not a Krishna state.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Geography (India: Physical Environment), Ch. 3 Drainage System

124. Consider the following statements about the Indus:
I. It rises in Tibet, near Lake Mansarovar.
II. Under the Indus Waters Treaty (1960), India can use only about 20 per cent of the total water carried by the Indus system (as per NCERT).
III. It enters India in Himachal Pradesh.
Which of the statements given are correct?
(a) II and III only (b) I and II only (c) I and III only (d) I, II and III
Answer: (b)
Solution: Statements I and II are correct (NCERT). Statement III is wrong: the Indus enters India in Ladakh; it is the Sutlej that enters through Himachal Pradesh.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Geography (Contemporary India I), Ch. 3 Drainage

125. Which of the following rivers does NOT rise in the Amarkantak region?
(a) Narmada (b) Son (c) Johila (d) Mahanadi
Answer: (d)
Solution: The Narmada, Son and Johila rise around Amarkantak. The Mahanadi rises near Sihawa in Dhamtari district of Chhattisgarh. Trap type: negative phrasing.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Geography (India: Physical Environment), Ch. 3 Drainage System
