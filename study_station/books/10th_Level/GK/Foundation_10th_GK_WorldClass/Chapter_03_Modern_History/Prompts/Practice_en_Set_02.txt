📝 Practice Set 2 (Questions 26–50)

All Medium Difficulty

26. The Partition of Bengal (1905) was annulled in 1911 by which Viceroy?
(a) Lord Curzon
(b) Lord Minto
(c) Lord Hardinge II
(d) Lord Chelmsford

Answer: (c) Lord Hardinge II
Step-by-step: Curzon partitioned Bengal in 1905; his successor Minto did not reverse it. Hardinge II annulled it at the Delhi Durbar in 1911, also transferring the capital from Calcutta to Delhi.
Source: SSC CGL 2019
🧪 Trap Type: Chronological Confusion (students often wrongly assume the partition was reversed by Curzon or Minto).

27. Arrange the following in correct chronological order:

Montagu Declaration

Rowlatt Act

Jallianwala Bagh massacre

Launch of Non-Cooperation Movement

Codes:
(a) 2 → 1 → 3 → 4
(b) 1 → 2 → 3 → 4
(c) 2 → 3 → 1 → 4
(d) 1 → 3 → 2 → 4

Answer: (b) 1 → 2 → 3 → 4
Step-by-step: Montagu Declaration (August 1917), Rowlatt Act (March 1919), Jallianwala Bagh (April 1919), Non-Cooperation Movement (launched in 1920). The correct order is 1, 2, 3, 4.
Source: UPSC Prelims 2018

28. Which of the following statements regarding the Government of India Act 1935 is/are correct?

It abolished Dyarchy in the provinces.

It introduced Dyarchy at the Centre.

The federal scheme was fully implemented.

Select the correct answer using the codes below:
(a) 1 and 2 only
(b) 2 and 3 only
(c) 1 only
(d) 1, 2 and 3

Answer: (a) 1 and 2 only
Step-by-step: The Act abolished provincial Dyarchy, gave provincial autonomy; introduced Dyarchy at the Centre. However, the all‑India federation never came into being. So statement 3 is incorrect.
Source: State PCS 2020
🧪 Trap Type: Nearly True Statement (statement 3 is false; the federal scheme was not fully implemented, a detail often missed).

29. The “Safety Valve” theory regarding the foundation of the Indian National Congress was popularised by:
(a) Dadabhai Naoroji
(b) Lala Lajpat Rai
(c) A.O. Hume
(d) Dufferin

Answer: (b) Lala Lajpat Rai
Step-by-step: In his book Unhappy India, Lajpat Rai argued that the INC was formed by the British to act as a safety valve for Indian discontent. Hume is the founder, not the one who popularised the theory.
Source: UPSC 2017

30. Who among the following presided over the Karachi session of the Indian National Congress (1931)?
(a) Sardar Vallabhbhai Patel
(b) Jawaharlal Nehru
(c) Mahatma Gandhi
(d) C. Rajagopalachari

Answer: (a) Sardar Vallabhbhai Patel
Step-by-step: The Karachi session (March 1931) was presided over by Sardar Patel and is famous for the resolution on Fundamental Rights and Economic Policy.
Source: SSC CHSL 2020

31. The Ilbert Bill controversy took place during the viceroyalty of:
(a) Lord Lytton
(b) Lord Ripon
(c) Lord Curzon
(d) Lord Dufferin

Answer: (b) Lord Ripon
Step-by-step: The Ilbert Bill (1883) sought to allow Indian judges to try European offenders. Ripon faced intense opposition from the European community.
Source: Railway NTPC 2019

32. The Chittagong Armoury Raid (1930) was led by:
(a) Bhagat Singh
(b) Surya Sen
(c) Rash Behari Bose
(d) Chandrashekhar Azad

Answer: (b) Surya Sen
Step-by-step: Masterda Surya Sen led the raid on the police armoury at Chittagong, now in Bangladesh.
Source: IBPS PO 2019

33. The Royal Indian Navy Mutiny of 1946 began at:
(a) Bombay
(b) Karachi
(c) Calcutta
(d) Vishakhapatnam

Answer: (a) Bombay
Step-by-step: The mutiny by ratings of the Royal Indian Navy started in Bombay harbour on 18 February 1946 and spread to other ports.
Source: UPSC Prelims 2015

34. Who gave the call “Dilli Chalo”?
(a) Mahatma Gandhi
(b) Subhas Chandra Bose
(c) Jawaharlal Nehru
(d) Bal Gangadhar Tilak

Answer: (b) Subhas Chandra Bose
Step-by-step: Bose, as commander of the INA, gave the call “Dilli Chalo” to march towards Delhi and liberate India.
Source: SSC CGL 2018

35. The Swaraj Party was established by:
(a) Mahatma Gandhi and Jawaharlal Nehru
(b) C.R. Das and Motilal Nehru
(c) Lala Lajpat Rai and Bal Gangadhar Tilak
(d) Sardar Patel and Rajendra Prasad

Answer: (b) C.R. Das and Motilal Nehru
Step-by-step: After the withdrawal of the Non‑Cooperation Movement, C.R. Das and Motilal Nehru founded the Swaraj Party (1923) to enter legislative councils.
Source: State PCS 2017

36. The Muslim League was founded in 1906 at:
(a) Calcutta
(b) Dhaka
(c) Aligarh
(d) Lucknow

Answer: (b) Dhaka
Step-by-step: The All India Muslim League was formed under the leadership of Aga Khan and Nawab Salimullah during the Muhammadan Educational Conference at Dhaka in 1906.
Source: Banking 2019

37. The Simon Commission (1928) was boycotted because:
(a) It recommended the partition of India.
(b) It had no Indian member.
(c) It rejected the Nehru Report.
(d) It proposed separate electorates for Sikhs.

Answer: (b) It had no Indian member.
Step-by-step: The seven-member commission consisted entirely of British MPs, leading to widespread protests and the slogan “Simon Go Back.”
Source: SSC CHSL 2020

38. The August Offer (1940) proposed:
(a) Complete independence after the war.
(b) Dominion status after the war, with expansion of the Viceroy’s Executive Council.
(c) Partition of India.
(d) A federal constitution drafted by Indians.

Answer: (b) Dominion status after the war, with expansion of the Viceroy’s Executive Council.
Step-by-step: The offer recognized the right of Indians to frame their own constitution after the war, but only if the British won. Congress rejected it due to its insistence on immediate self-government.
Source: UPSC Prelims 2016

39. The parallel government set up during the Quit India Movement in Satara was led by:
(a) Aruna Asaf Ali
(b) Nana Patil
(c) Chittu Pandey
(d) Matangini Hazra

Answer: (b) Nana Patil
Step-by-step: In Satara (Maharashtra), a parallel government was established under leaders like Nana Patil and others. Aruna Asaf Ali was active in Bombay; Chittu Pandey led in Ballia.
Source: State PCS 2020

40. The term “First War of Independence” to describe the Revolt of 1857 was first used by:
(a) Jawaharlal Nehru
(b) V.D. Savarkar
(c) Subhas Chandra Bose
(d) Mahatma Gandhi

Answer: (b) V.D. Savarkar
Step-by-step: Savarkar’s book The Indian War of Independence (1909) used this terminology; it is not a contemporary label.
Source: SSC CGL 2019

41. The Kuka Movement (Namdhari) was a socio‑religious uprising in which region?
(a) Bengal
(b) Punjab
(c) Maharashtra
(d) Bihar

Answer: (b) Punjab
Step-by-step: The Kuka movement, founded by Baba Ram Singh, was a Sikh reform movement in Punjab that also turned into an anti‑British revolt in the 1870s.
Source: Railway Group D 2020

42. Who among the following was associated with the Kakori Conspiracy Case?
(a) Bhagat Singh and Sukhdev
(b) Ram Prasad Bismil and Ashfaqulla Khan
(c) Surya Sen and Pritilata Waddedar
(d) Batukeshwar Dutt and Rajguru

Answer: (b) Ram Prasad Bismil and Ashfaqulla Khan
Step-by-step: The Kakori train robbery in 1925 was carried out by members of the Hindustan Republican Association; Bismil and Ashfaqulla were prominent among them and later executed.
Source: Banking 2018

43. Who was the first Indian to be elected to the British House of Commons?
(a) Dadabhai Naoroji
(b) Surendranath Banerjee
(c) Gopal Krishna Gokhale
(d) A.O. Hume

Answer: (a) Dadabhai Naoroji
Step-by-step: Naoroji was elected to the House of Commons from Central Finsbury in 1892 as a Liberal Party candidate.
Source: UPSC Prelims 2014

44. With reference to the Morley‑Minto Reforms (1909), consider the following statements:

They granted separate electorates to Muslims.

They allowed the election of Indians to legislative councils for the first time.
Which of the statements given above is/are correct?
(a) 1 only
(b) 2 only
(c) Both 1 and 2
(d) Neither 1 nor 2

Answer: (c) Both 1 and 2
Step-by-step: The 1909 Act introduced separate electorates for Muslims and also permitted Indians to be elected to legislative councils (earlier they were only nominated). So both are correct.
Source: UPSC 2013
🧪 Trap Type: Nearly True Statement (some students think statement 2 is false because Indians had been nominated before; however, the Act introduced the principle of election for Indians).

45. Which of the following events occurred earliest?
(a) Formation of the Swaraj Party
(b) Formation of the Home Rule League
(c) Chauri Chaura incident
(d) Jallianwala Bagh massacre

Answer: (b) Formation of the Home Rule League
Step-by-step: The Home Rule Leagues were launched in 1916 (Tilak in April, Besant in September). Jallianwala Bagh occurred in April 1919, Chauri Chaura in February 1922, and the Swaraj Party was formed in January 1923.
Source: SSC CGL 2021

46. The “Drain of Wealth” theory is most closely associated with:
(a) Dadabhai Naoroji
(b) R.C. Dutt
(c) Mahatma Gandhi
(d) G.V. Joshi

Answer: (a) Dadabhai Naoroji
Step-by-step: Although R.C. Dutt also wrote about economic exploitation, the term and theory were systematically developed and popularised by Naoroji in his work Poverty and Un‑British Rule in India.
Source: Banking 2020

47. With reference to the Revolt of 1857, who was the leader in Kanpur?
(a) Kunwar Singh
(b) Nana Sahib
(c) Begum Hazrat Mahal
(d) Rani Lakshmibai

Answer: (b) Nana Sahib
Step-by-step: Nana Sahib, the adopted son of Peshwa Baji Rao II, led the revolt in Kanpur. Kunwar Singh was in Bihar, Begum Hazrat Mahal in Lucknow, Rani Lakshmibai in Jhansi.
Source: Railway NTPC 2019

48. The Gandhi‑Irwin Pact (1931) was signed to:
(a) End the Non‑Cooperation Movement.
(b) Suspend the Civil Disobedience Movement and participate in the Round Table Conference.
(c) Partition India.
(d) Release Bhagat Singh from jail.

Answer: (b) Suspend the Civil Disobedience Movement and participate in the Round Table Conference.
Step-by-step: Gandhi agreed to suspend the CDM and attend the Second Round Table Conference, while the British agreed to release political prisoners.
Source: SSC MTS 2020

49. The Indian National Army (INA) trials were held at:
(a) Bombay
(b) Delhi
(c) Calcutta
(d) Madras

Answer: (b) Delhi
Step-by-step: The INA trials of 1945–46 were conducted at the Red Fort in Delhi.
Source: IBPS Clerk 2018

50. The Treaty of Allahabad (1765) was signed between Robert Clive and:
(a) Siraj‑ud‑Daula
(b) Mir Qasim
(c) Shah Alam II
(d) Shuja‑ud‑Daula

Answer: (c) Shah Alam II
Step-by-step: After the Battle of Buxar (1764), Clive signed the Treaty of Allahabad with Mughal Emperor Shah Alam II, who granted the Diwani of Bengal, Bihar, and Orissa to the Company.
Source: State PCS 2019
