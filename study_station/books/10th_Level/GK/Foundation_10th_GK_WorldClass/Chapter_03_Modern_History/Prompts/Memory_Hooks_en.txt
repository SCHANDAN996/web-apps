🧭 Skip Strategy for Modern Indian History

If asked about a founder of a dynasty or organisation and you can't recall the name, skip immediately. Do not spend time trying to reconstruct. Instead, come back later if time permits. The brain often does better with recognition than forced recall.

If a question asks “Which of the following is NOT correctly matched?” or “Which statement is incorrect?” involving four pairings, and you are unsure about one, don’t attempt to solve all. Mark the one you are certain is wrong. If still ambiguous, skip and return; these negative-phrasing questions are high trap zones.

If a question gives a year and asks what happened, and you don't instantly recall, skip. Years are either known instantly or they lead to confusion. Wasting time on a single date can cost you three easy current affairs marks later. Flag it and move.
