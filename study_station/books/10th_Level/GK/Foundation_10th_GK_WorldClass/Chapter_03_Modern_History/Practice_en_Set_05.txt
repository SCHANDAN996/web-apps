Questions 101–125: Medium

101. Who among the following was the first Indian to become a member of the Viceroy's Executive Council?
(a) Satyendra Prasanna Sinha (b) B. R. Ambedkar (c) Tej Bahadur Sapru (d) C. Rajagopalachari
Answer: (a)
Solution: Ambedkar is the tempting wrong option because he is famous as a Law Member, but he joined the Viceroy's Executive Council only in 1942. Under the Morley–Minto Reforms of 1909, Satyendra Prasanna Sinha was appointed Law Member, becoming the first Indian in the Council, so the answer is Satyendra Prasanna Sinha.
Source: PYQ-style

102. The Kheda Satyagraha (1918) was related to:
(a) Indigo cultivation (b) Plague bonus for workers (c) Revenue remission during crop failure (d) Forest rights
Answer: (c)
Solution: Indigo cultivation is the trap because it relates to Champaran (1917), while the plague bonus relates to the Ahmedabad mill strike. At Kheda in Gujarat, Gandhi led peasants demanding suspension of land revenue after crops failed, so the answer is revenue remission during crop failure.
Source: PYQ-style

103. Which of the following Acts introduced separate electorates for Muslims in the Central Legislature?
(a) Indian Councils Act 1909 (b) Government of India Act 1919 (c) Indian Councils Act 1892 (d) Government of India Act 1935
Answer: (a)
Solution: The Government of India Act 1919 is the tempting wrong option, but it introduced dyarchy in the provinces, not separate electorates. The Morley–Minto Reforms, enacted as the Indian Councils Act 1909, created separate electorates for Muslims, so the answer is the Indian Councils Act 1909.
Source: PYQ-style

104. The Lucknow Pact (1916) was an agreement between:
(a) Muslim League and the British government (b) Congress and the British government (c) Congress and the Home Rule League (d) Congress and the Muslim League
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is assuming the pact was negotiated with the British; it was actually an internal agreement between two Indian parties. At the joint Lucknow session, Congress accepted separate electorates in return for joint demands for self-government with the League, so the answer is Congress and the Muslim League.
Source: PYQ-style

105. Arrange the following events in chronological order:
1. Dandi March
2. Gandhi–Irwin Pact
3. First Round Table Conference
4. Poona Pact
Codes:
(a) 1, 2, 3, 4 (b) 1, 3, 2, 4 (c) 1, 3, 4, 2 (d) 3, 1, 2, 4
Answer: (b)
Solution: The tempting trap is placing the Gandhi–Irwin Pact before the First Round Table Conference, but the conference (November 1930) happened first and the pact (March 1931) enabled Gandhi to attend the second conference. The correct order is Dandi March (1930), First Round Table Conference (1930), Gandhi–Irwin Pact (1931), Poona Pact (1932), so the answer is 1, 3, 2, 4.
Source: PYQ-style

106. The Treaty of Allahabad (1765) was signed between Robert Clive and:
(a) Siraj-ud-Daula (b) Shah Alam II (c) Shuja-ud-Daula (d) Mir Jafar
Answer: (b)
Solution: Siraj-ud-Daula is the trap because he belongs to the Battle of Plassey (1757), not Buxar. After the Battle of Buxar (1764), Clive signed the Treaty of Allahabad with the Mughal Emperor Shah Alam II, obtaining the Diwani rights of Bengal, Bihar, and Orissa, so the answer is Shah Alam II.
Source: PYQ-style

107. The first synchronous (regular decennial) Census of India was conducted in 1881 during the viceroyalty of:
(a) Lord Mayo (b) Lord Lytton (c) Lord Dufferin (d) Lord Ripon
Answer: (d)
Solution: Lord Mayo is the common trap because a census was attempted in 1872 during his tenure, but it was non-synchronous and incomplete. The first complete, synchronous census was held in 1881 under Lord Ripon, so the answer is Lord Ripon.
Source: PYQ-style

108. Who was the founder of the Ghadar Party?
(a) Lala Hardayal (b) Bhagat Singh (c) Sachindranath Sanyal (d) Rash Behari Bose
Answer: (a)
Solution: Rash Behari Bose is the tempting wrong option because he was closely associated with the Ghadar movement, but he joined it later. The Ghadar Party was founded in 1913 in San Francisco by Lala Hardayal, Sohan Singh Bhakna, and others, so the answer is Lala Hardayal.
Source: PYQ-style

109. The Eka Movement (1921–22) took place in which region?
(a) Malabar (b) Punjab (c) United Provinces (Uttar Pradesh) (d) Gujarat
Answer: (c)
Solution: Malabar is the trap because the Moplah rebellion occurred there in the same period (1921). The Eka Movement was a peasant uprising in the Awadh region of the United Provinces against high rents and forced labour, so the answer is the United Provinces.
Source: PYQ-style

110. The Simon Commission arrived in India in:
(a) 1927 (b) 1928 (c) 1929 (d) 1930
Answer: (b)
Solution: 1927 is the tempting wrong year because the commission was appointed that year, but appointment and arrival are different events. The all-British commission, boycotted with the slogan "Simon Go Back", arrived in India in February 1928, so the answer is 1928.
Source: PYQ-style

111. Which of the following leaders gave the slogan "Delhi Chalo"?
(a) Mahatma Gandhi (b) Bal Gangadhar Tilak (c) Subhas Chandra Bose (d) Jawaharlal Nehru
Answer: (c)
Solution: Gandhi is the trap because his slogans are the most frequently asked, but his famous call was "Do or Die". As Supreme Commander of the INA, Bose gave the call "Delhi Chalo" for the march towards the capital, so the answer is Subhas Chandra Bose.
Source: PYQ-style

112. The Indian Councils Act 1892 is significant because it:
(a) Allowed discussion of the budget (b) Introduced separate electorates (c) Introduced direct elections (d) Allowed Indians to enter the Viceroy's Executive Council
Answer: (a)
Solution: Separate electorates is the trap, but that provision came only with the Act of 1909. The 1892 Act permitted non-official members to discuss the annual financial statement and ask questions, though not vote on it, so the answer is that it allowed discussion of the budget.
Source: PYQ-style

113. The Chauri Chaura incident (1922) occurred in which district?
(a) Basti (b) Faizabad (c) Allahabad (d) Gorakhpur
Answer: (d)
Solution: The other districts of the United Provinces are distractors that sound plausible because of their role in the freedom struggle. Chauri Chaura, where a mob burnt a police station killing 22 policemen, was in the Gorakhpur district, after which Gandhi withdrew the Non-Cooperation Movement, so the answer is Gorakhpur.
Source: PYQ-style

114. The Nehru Report (1928) recommended:
(a) Dyarchy at the Centre (b) Complete independence (c) Separate electorates for all minorities (d) Dominion Status for India
Answer: (d)
Solution: Complete independence is the trap, but that demand was adopted only at the Lahore session in 1929. The Motilal Nehru Committee recommended Dominion Status within the British Commonwealth and rejected separate electorates in favour of joint electorates, so the answer is Dominion Status for India.
Source: PYQ-style

115. The All India Trade Union Congress (AITUC) was founded in:
(a) 1920 (b) 1921 (c) 1922 (d) 1919
Answer: (a)
Solution: The nearby years are traps designed to catch those who remember only the approximate period of the early labour movement. AITUC was established on 31 October 1920 in Bombay with Lala Lajpat Rai as its first president, so the answer is 1920.
Source: PYQ-style

116. Which of the following pairs (Organisation : Founder) is correctly matched?
(a) Brahmo Samaj – Swami Dayanand Saraswati (b) Arya Samaj – Raja Ram Mohan Roy (c) Ramakrishna Mission – Swami Vivekananda (d) Satyashodhak Samaj – Swami Dayanand Saraswati
Answer: (c)
Solution: The swapped founders are the trap: Brahmo Samaj was founded by Raja Ram Mohan Roy, Arya Samaj by Dayanand Saraswati, and Satyashodhak Samaj by Jyotiba Phule. Only the Ramakrishna Mission–Vivekananda pair (1897) is correct, so the answer is Ramakrishna Mission – Swami Vivekananda.
Source: PYQ-style

117. The "August Offer" (1940) was made by which Viceroy?
(a) Lord Irwin (b) Lord Wavell (c) Lord Mountbatten (d) Lord Linlithgow
Answer: (d)
Solution: Lord Wavell is the trap because his Wavell Plan (1945) is also a famous wartime proposal, but it came five years later. Lord Linlithgow announced the August Offer in 1940, proposing Dominion Status after the war and expansion of the Viceroy's Executive Council, so the answer is Lord Linlithgow.
Source: PYQ-style

118. The Peshwaship was abolished by which Governor-General?
(a) Lord Dalhousie (b) Lord Wellesley (c) Lord Cornwallis (d) Lord Hastings
Answer: (d)
Solution: Dalhousie is the tempting wrong option because he annexed many states, but he is associated with the Doctrine of Lapse, not the Peshwas. After the Third Anglo-Maratha War (1817–18), Hastings abolished the post of Peshwa and pensioned off Baji Rao II, so the answer is Lord Hastings.
Source: PYQ-style

119. Who delivered the speech containing the phrase "Do or Die"?
(a) Subhas Chandra Bose (b) Jawaharlal Nehru (c) Mahatma Gandhi (d) Sardar Vallabhbhai Patel
Answer: (c)
Solution: Bose is the trap because his slogans "Delhi Chalo" and "Jai Hind" are equally famous, but they belong to the INA. Gandhi launched the Quit India Movement on 8 August 1942 with the call "Do or Die" at Gowalia Tank Maidan, Bombay, so the answer is Mahatma Gandhi.
Source: PYQ-style

120. The Bardoli Satyagraha (1928) was led by:
(a) Mahatma Gandhi (b) Jawaharlal Nehru (c) Sardar Vallabhbhai Patel (d) C. Rajagopalachari
Answer: (c)
Solution: Gandhi is the trap because he led most Gujarat movements, but he was not the leader of this one. Patel organised the peasants of Bardoli against a steep increase in land revenue, and the women of Bardoli gave him the title "Sardar", so the answer is Sardar Vallabhbhai Patel.
Source: PYQ-style

121. The Poona Pact (1932) was an agreement between Mahatma Gandhi and:
(a) B. R. Ambedkar (b) Muhammad Ali Jinnah (c) Ramsay MacDonald (d) Lord Irwin
Answer: (a)
Solution: Ramsay MacDonald is the trap because he announced the Communal Award, but the pact modified that award and was not signed with him. The Poona Pact was signed between Gandhi and Ambedkar, replacing separate electorates for the Depressed Classes with reserved seats in joint electorates, so the answer is B. R. Ambedkar.
Source: PYQ-style

122. Consider the following statements regarding the Indian National Army (INA):
1. The INA was reorganised under the leadership of Subhas Chandra Bose in Singapore in 1943.
2. The INA fought alongside the British in the Burma campaign.
3. The Rani of Jhansi Regiment was an all-women unit of the INA.
4. The INA trials were held at the Red Fort in Delhi in 1945–46.
Which of the statements given above are correct?
(a) 1, 2, and 3 only (b) 1, 2, 3, and 4 (c) 2 and 4 only (d) 1, 3, and 4 only
Answer: (d)
Solution: Statement 2 is the trap: the INA fought against the British, alongside Japan, in the Burma campaign, not alongside the British. Statements 1, 3, and 4 are historically accurate, so the answer is 1, 3, and 4 only.
Source: PYQ-style

123. Arrange the following Governor-Generals in chronological order of their tenures:
1. Lord Cornwallis
2. Lord Wellesley
3. Lord Hastings
4. Lord Dalhousie
Codes:
(a) 1, 2, 4, 3 (b) 2, 1, 3, 4 (c) 1, 3, 2, 4 (d) 1, 2, 3, 4
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is swapping Wellesley and Hastings, but Wellesley (Subsidiary Alliance) preceded Hastings (Anglo-Nepal and Third Anglo-Maratha wars). The correct order is Cornwallis (1786–93), Wellesley (1798–1805), Hastings (1813–23), Dalhousie (1848–56), so the answer is 1, 2, 3, 4.
Source: PYQ-style

124. With reference to the August Declaration of 1917, consider the following statements:
1. It promised the gradual development of self-governing institutions in India.
2. It was issued by Lord Chelmsford.
3. It directly led to the Montagu-Chelmsford Reforms.
Which of the statements given above is/are correct?
(a) 1 and 2 only (b) 1, 2, and 3 (c) 1 and 3 only (d) 2 and 3 only
Answer: (c)
Solution: Statement 2 is the trap because the reforms carry Chelmsford's name, but the declaration was made by Edwin Montagu, the Secretary of State for India, not by the Viceroy. Statements 1 and 3 are correct, so the answer is 1 and 3 only.
Source: PYQ-style

125. The Faizpur session (1936) of the Indian National Congress is notable for being:
(a) The session that adopted the demand for Pakistan (b) The first session held in a rural area (c) The session where the Quit India Movement was launched (d) The session that accepted the Cabinet Mission Plan
Answer: (b)
Solution: The Quit India option is the trap, but that movement was launched in Bombay in 1942, not at Faizpur. The 1936 session, presided over by Jawaharlal Nehru, was held in the village of Faizpur in Maharashtra, the first Congress session organised in a rural setting, so the answer is the first session held in a rural area.
Source: PYQ-style
