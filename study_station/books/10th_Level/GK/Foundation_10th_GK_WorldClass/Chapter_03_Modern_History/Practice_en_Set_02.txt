26. The Partition of Bengal (1905) was annulled in 1911 by which Viceroy?
(a) Lord Curzon (b) Lord Hardinge II (c) Lord Minto (d) Lord Chelmsford
Answer: (b)
Solution: Curzon partitioned Bengal in 1905, so he is the tempting wrong choice, but the annulment came six years later. Lord Hardinge II announced the annulment at the Delhi Durbar of 1911, so the answer is Lord Hardinge II.
Source: PYQ-style

27. Arrange the following in correct chronological order: 1. Montagu Declaration 2. Rowlatt Act 3. Jallianwala Bagh massacre 4. Launch of Non-Cooperation Movement
(a) 1 → 2 → 3 → 4 (b) 1 → 3 → 2 → 4 (c) 2 → 3 → 1 → 4 (d) 2 → 1 → 3 → 4
Answer: (a)
Solution: The Montagu Declaration came in August 1917, the Rowlatt Act in March 1919, Jallianwala Bagh in April 1919, and Non-Cooperation was launched in 1920. The trap is placing the Rowlatt Act before 1917, so the answer is 1 → 2 → 3 → 4.
Source: PYQ-style

28. Which of the following statements regarding the Government of India Act 1935 is/are correct? 1. It abolished Dyarchy in the provinces. 2. It introduced Dyarchy at the Centre. 3. The federal scheme was fully implemented.
(a) 1 only (b) 2 and 3 only (c) 1 and 2 only (d) 1, 2 and 3
Answer: (c)
Solution: The Act abolished provincial dyarchy and provided for dyarchy at the Centre, but the federal scheme never came into operation, so statement 3 is the trap. Hence the answer is 1 and 2 only.
Source: PYQ-style

29. The “Safety Valve” theory regarding the foundation of the Indian National Congress was popularised by:
(a) Dadabhai Naoroji (b) Lord Dufferin (c) A.O. Hume (d) Lala Lajpat Rai
Answer: (d)
Solution: A.O. Hume founded the Congress, which makes him the tempting option, but the theory that the Congress was a British “safety valve” for discontent was popularised by extremist leader Lala Lajpat Rai. So the answer is Lala Lajpat Rai.

(a) C. Rajagopalachari (b) Jawaharlal Nehru (c) Mahatma Gandhi (d) Sardar Vallabhbhai Patel
Answer: (d)
Solution: Gandhi attended the session, which makes him the tempting choice, but the 1931 Karachi session — famous for the resolution on Fundamental Rights — was presided over by Sardar Vallabhbhai Patel. So the answer is Sardar Vallabhbhai Patel.

31. The Ilbert Bill controversy took place during the viceroyalty of:
(a) Lord Ripon (b) Lord Dufferin (c) Lord Curzon (d) Lord Lytton
Answer: (a)
Solution: The Ilbert Bill (1883) sought to let Indian judges try European offenders and provoked fierce European opposition. It belongs to Lord Ripon’s liberal tenure, not Lytton’s reactionary one, so the answer is Lord Ripon.
Source: PYQ-style

32. The Chittagong Armoury Raid (1930) was led by:
(a) Bhagat Singh (b) Surya Sen (c) Rash Behari Bose (d) Chandrashekhar Azad
Answer: (b)
Solution: Bhagat Singh is the tempting name for any revolutionary act of that period, but the Chittagong Armoury Raid of April 1930 was led by Surya Sen, popularly called Masterda. So the answer is Surya Sen.

33. The Royal Indian Navy Mutiny of 1946 began at:
(a) Vishakhapatnam (b) Karachi (c) Calcutta (d) Bombay
Answer: (d)
Solution: The mutiny began in February 1946 aboard HMIS Talwar in Bombay and then spread to Karachi and other ports. Karachi is the trap because it saw later spread, so the answer is Bombay.

35. The Swaraj Party was established by:
(a) C.R. Das and Motilal Nehru (b) Sardar Patel and Rajendra Prasad (c) Lala Lajpat Rai and Bal Gangadhar Tilak (d) Mahatma Gandhi and Jawaharlal Nehru
Answer: (a)
Solution: After the withdrawal of Non-Cooperation, C.R. Das and Motilal Nehru formed the Swaraj Party in 1923 to enter the councils and wreck them from within. The Gandhi–Nehru pairing is the trap, so the answer is C.R. Das and Motilal Nehru.

(a) It recommended the partition of India. (b) It had no Indian member. (c) It rejected the Nehru Report. (d) It proposed separate electorates for Sikhs.

Solution: The all-white Simon Commission was boycotted with the cry “Simon Go Back” because not a single Indian was included in it. Partition was not its subject, so the answer is that it had no Indian member.

Answer: (c)

44. With reference to the Morley-Minto Reforms (1909), consider the following statements: 1. They granted separate electorates to Muslims. 2. They allowed the election of Indians to legislative councils for the first time.
(a) 2 only (b) 1 only (c) Both 1 and 2 (d) Neither 1 nor 2
Answer: (b)
Solution: Statement 2 is the trap: Indians were first associated with legislative councils through the Indian Councils Act of 1861 and indirect election elements came with the 1892 Act, so 1909 was not the first time. The 1909 Act did introduce separate electorates for Muslims, so the answer is 1 only.
Source: PYQ-style
