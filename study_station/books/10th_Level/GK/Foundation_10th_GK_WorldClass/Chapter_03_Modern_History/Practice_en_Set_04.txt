Questions 76–100: Medium

76. Which of the following princely states was annexed by Lord Dalhousie in 1856 on the grounds of misgovernance, rather than under the Doctrine of Lapse?
(a) Satara (b) Jhansi (c) Awadh (d) Nagpur
Answer: (c)
Solution: Satara, Jhansi, and Nagpur were annexed through the Doctrine of Lapse, which is the tempting confusion here. Awadh was annexed in 1856 on the pretext of misrule by Nawab Wajid Ali Shah, so the answer is Awadh.
Source: PYQ-style

77. Who among the following was the first Indian to qualify for the Indian Civil Service?
(a) Romesh Chunder Dutt (b) Surendranath Banerjee (c) Satyendranath Tagore (d) Dadabhai Naoroji
Answer: (c)
Solution: Surendranath Banerjee is the tempting wrong option because he too cleared the exam in 1869 but was later disqualified over an age dispute. Satyendranath Tagore qualified in 1863, becoming the first Indian ICS officer, so the answer is Satyendranath Tagore.
Source: PYQ-style

78. The Rowlatt Act (1919) is also known as:
(a) The Seditious Meetings Act (b) The Defence of India Act (c) The Black Act (d) The Vernacular Press Act
Answer: (c)
Solution: The Vernacular Press Act (1878) is a tempting distractor since it too was a repressive law, but it dealt with the Indian-language press. The Rowlatt Act, allowing imprisonment without trial, was nicknamed the Black Act, so the answer is the Black Act.
Source: PYQ-style

79. With reference to the Government of India Act 1935, which of the following statements is NOT correct?
(a) It introduced provincial autonomy. (b) The all-India federation proposed by it came into effect. (c) It separated Burma from India. (d) It abolished dyarchy in the provinces.
Answer: (b)
Solution: The Act did introduce provincial autonomy, abolished provincial dyarchy, and separated Burma in 1937, so those statements are true. The proposed all-India federation never materialised because the princely states did not join, so the incorrect statement is that the federation came into effect.
Source: PYQ-style

80. The Hunter Commission was appointed by the British government to:
(a) Investigate the Chauri Chaura incident. (b) Inquire into the Jallianwala Bagh massacre. (c) Recommend constitutional reforms. (d) Enquire into the Kakori conspiracy.
Answer: (b)
Solution: Chauri Chaura (1922) is a tempting distractor as another infamous episode of violence, but it led to Gandhi withdrawing the Non-Cooperation Movement, not a commission. The Hunter Commission (1919) probed General Dyer's actions at Jallianwala Bagh, so the answer is the Jallianwala Bagh massacre.
Source: PYQ-style

81. Which of the following movements was the first to be led by Mahatma Gandhi on an all-India scale?
(a) Champaran Satyagraha (b) Non-Cooperation Movement (c) Kheda Satyagraha (d) Ahmedabad Mill Strike
Answer: (b)
Solution: Champaran, Kheda, and Ahmedabad are tempting because they were Gandhi's earliest satyagrahas, but all were localised struggles. The Non-Cooperation Movement (1920–22) was his first nationwide mass movement, so the answer is the Non-Cooperation Movement.
Source: PYQ-style

82. The resolution on Fundamental Rights and National Economic Policy was passed at which Congress session?
(a) Lahore (1929) (b) Faizpur (1936) (c) Haripura (1938) (d) Karachi (1931)
Answer: (d)
Solution: Lahore 1929 is tempting because of the Purna Swaraj resolution, but that session dealt with complete independence. The Karachi session of 1931, presided over by Sardar Patel, adopted the resolutions on Fundamental Rights and Economic Policy, so the answer is Karachi (1931).
Source: PYQ-style

83. The book "Poverty and Un-British Rule in India" was authored by:
(a) Mahatma Gandhi (b) R. C. Dutt (c) Dadabhai Naoroji (d) Gopal Krishna Gokhale
Answer: (c)
Solution: R. C. Dutt is tempting since he too wrote on India's economic history, but his work was "Economic History of India". Naoroji's book systematised the Drain of Wealth theory, so the answer is Dadabhai Naoroji.
Source: PYQ-style

84. Who was the Viceroy of India during the Partition of Bengal (1905)?
(a) Lord Elgin (b) Lord Minto (c) Lord Hardinge (d) Lord Curzon
Answer: (d)
Solution: Hardinge is tempting because the partition was annulled in 1911 during his tenure, but annulment is not the same as the original act. Lord Curzon announced and effected the partition in 1905, so the answer is Lord Curzon.
Source: PYQ-style

85. The Vernacular Press Act was passed by which Viceroy?
(a) Lord Lytton (b) Lord Ripon (c) Lord Dufferin (d) Lord Lansdowne
Answer: (a)
Solution: Ripon is the tempting wrong option because he repealed the Act in 1882, and repealing is often confused with enacting. Lord Lytton passed the Vernacular Press Act in 1878, so the answer is Lord Lytton.
Source: PYQ-style

86. The Ahmedabad Mill Strike (1918) was primarily related to:
(a) Increase in wages (b) Plague bonus (c) Reduction in working hours (d) Demand for trade union rights
Answer: (b)
Solution: A general wage increase is the tempting option since most strikes concern wages, but this dispute was specific. Gandhi supported the workers' demand for a 35% plague bonus for staying on during the epidemic, so the answer is plague bonus.
Source: PYQ-style

87. Consider the following pairs: 1. Bal Gangadhar Tilak – Swaraj Party; 2. C. R. Das – Home Rule League; 3. Annie Besant – Theosophical Society; 4. Motilal Nehru – Indian National Army. Which of the pairs given above is/are correctly matched?
(a) 1 and 2 only (b) 2 and 4 only (c) 3 only (d) 3 and 4 only
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is that Tilak and Das seem interchangeable, but Tilak ran a Home Rule League while Das co-founded the Swaraj Party after Tilak's death; Motilal Nehru was a Swarajist, not INA. Only Annie Besant's association with the Theosophical Society is correct, so the answer is pair 3 only.
Source: PYQ-style

88. Who founded the Ramakrishna Mission in 1897?
(a) Swami Vivekananda (b) Sister Nivedita (c) Ramakrishna Paramahamsa (d) Keshab Chandra Sen
Answer: (a)
Solution: Ramakrishna Paramahamsa is the tempting wrong option because the Mission bears his name, but he died in 1886 and founded no organisation. His disciple Swami Vivekananda founded the Mission in 1897, so the answer is Swami Vivekananda.

89. The Chittagong Armoury Raid (1930) was led by:
(a) Bhagat Singh (b) Surya Sen (c) Chandrashekhar Azad (d) Batukeshwar Dutt
Answer: (b)
Solution: Bhagat Singh is tempting as the most famous revolutionary of that era, but he was associated with the HSRA in north India. Masterda Surya Sen led the Chittagong raid in Bengal, so the answer is Surya Sen.

90. The Shimla Conference of 1945 was held to discuss:
(a) The Cripps Mission proposals (b) The Cabinet Mission Plan (c) The Wavell Plan (d) The Mountbatten Plan
Answer: (c)
Solution: The Cabinet Mission Plan is tempting because it too involved negotiations with Indian leaders, but that came in 1946. Lord Wavell convened the Shimla Conference in June 1945 to discuss his plan for an interim government, so the answer is the Wavell Plan.

91. Who was the first Indian Governor-General of independent India?
(a) C. Rajagopalachari (b) Lord Mountbatten (c) Dr. Rajendra Prasad (d) Dr. S. Radhakrishnan
Answer: (a)
Solution: Mountbatten is the classic trap since he was the first Governor-General of independent India, but he was British, not Indian. C. Rajagopalachari succeeded him in June 1948 as the first and last Indian Governor-General, so the answer is C. Rajagopalachari.
Source: PYQ-style

92. The Ilbert Bill controversy (1883–84) occurred during the viceroyalty of:
(a) Lord Lytton (b) Lord Dufferin (c) Lord Curzon (d) Lord Ripon
Answer: (d)
Solution: Lytton is tempting because of his other controversial measures like the Vernacular Press Act, but the Ilbert Bill belonged to the liberal phase that followed. It was introduced during Lord Ripon's viceroyalty, so the answer is Lord Ripon.
Source: PYQ-style

93. Which of the following events occurred earliest?
(a) Formation of the Muslim League (b) Passage of the Morley-Minto Reforms (c) Surat split of the Congress (d) Partition of Bengal
Answer: (d)
Solution: The Muslim League (1906) is tempting as it feels like an early event, but it came a year after the partition. The Partition of Bengal took place in 1905, before the League (1906), Surat split (1907), and Morley-Minto Reforms (1909), so the answer is the Partition of Bengal.
Source: PYQ-style

94. The slogan "Swaraj is my birthright and I shall have it" was popularised by Bal Gangadhar Tilak during:
(a) The Quit India Movement (b) The Non-Cooperation Movement (c) The Civil Disobedience Movement (d) The Home Rule Movement
Answer: (d)
Solution: The Non-Cooperation Movement is tempting as the first great Gandhian mass struggle, but Tilak died in August 1920, before it began. Tilak popularised this slogan while leading the Home Rule Movement of 1916–18, so the answer is the Home Rule Movement.
Source: PYQ-style

95. Who among the following presided over the Lahore Session of the Indian National Congress (1929) that declared Purna Swaraj?
(a) Jawaharlal Nehru (b) Mahatma Gandhi (c) Sardar Vallabhbhai Patel (d) Subhas Chandra Bose
Answer: (a)
Solution: Gandhi is tempting because he dominated the freedom struggle, but he rarely held the Congress presidency. Jawaharlal Nehru presided over the Lahore session of December 1929, so the answer is Jawaharlal Nehru.

Answer: (a)
Solution: Punjab is tempting because of its strong nationalist tradition, but the Red Shirts were a Pashtun movement. Khan Abdul Ghaffar Khan organised the Khudai Khidmatgars in the North-West Frontier Province, so the answer is the North-West Frontier Province.

Answer: (a)
Solution: Dalhousie is the tempting wrong option because of his aggressive annexations, but his tool was the Doctrine of Lapse. Lord Wellesley introduced the Subsidiary Alliance system in 1798, so the answer is Lord Wellesley.

100. Which of the following statements is incorrect regarding the Revolt of 1857?
(a) Mangal Pandey was executed at Barrackpore. (b) The revolt led to the immediate grant of Dominion Status to India. (c) Rani Lakshmibai fought against the British and died at Gwalior. (d) Bahadur Shah Zafar was proclaimed the leader of the revolt in Delhi.
Answer: (b)
Solution: The first three statements are historically accurate, which makes them tempting picks for the "incorrect" one. The revolt ended Company rule and brought direct Crown rule in 1858, while Dominion Status came only in 1947, so the incorrect statement is the grant of Dominion Status.
