📊 PYQ Pattern Analysis: Modern Indian History

Exams this chapter serves: SSC GD, SSC MTS, RRB Group D, state police / constable exams, and the general-awareness part of other state-level tests.

Note: this is a pattern analysis — what kinds of questions these exams like to ask from this chapter and how they trap you. We do not give year-wise counts or percentages, because no reliable data for them exists. The model questions below are written in the exam style; their sources are the NCERT chapters they test.

1. What Exams Like to Ask (Sub-topics, in plain words)

Foundational British battles — Battle of Plassey (1757, Clive vs Siraj-ud-Daulah), Battle of Buxar (1764, Hector Munro vs Mir Qasim, Shuja-ud-Daula, Shah Alam II), Treaty of Allahabad (1765, grant of Diwani).

Land revenue settlements — Permanent Settlement (1793, Lord Cornwallis, Bengal/Bihar/Odisha), Ryotwari System (Thomas Munro & Alexander Read, Madras/Bombay), Mahalwari System (Holt Mackenzie, North-Western Provinces/Punjab).

Revolt of 1857 — Immediate cause (greased cartridges, Enfield rifle), Mangal Pandey at Barrackpore, outbreak at Meerut (10 May 1857), leaders & centers (Delhi: Bahadur Shah Zafar & Bakht Khan; Kanpur: Nana Saheb & Tatya Tope; Lucknow: Begum Hazrat Mahal; Jhansi: Rani Lakshmibai; Bihar/Jagdishpur: Kunwar Singh; Bareilly: Khan Bahadur Khan).

Socio-religious reform movements — Brahmo Samaj (1828, Raja Ram Mohan Roy, abolition of Sati in 1829 by William Bentinck), Arya Samaj (1875, Swami Dayanand Saraswati, 'Satyarth Prakash', 'Go back to the Vedas'), Ramakrishna Mission (1897, Swami Vivekananda, Chicago Parliament 1893), Satyashodhak Samaj (1873, Jyotirao Phule, 'Gulamgiri'), Young Bengal (Henry Vivian Derozio), Prarthana Samaj (1867, Atmaram Pandurang).

Indian National Congress & early phases — Founded in December 1885 at Bombay (Gokuldas Tejpal Sanskrit College), initiated by A.O. Hume, presided over by W.C. Bonnerjee, attended by 72 delegates. Moderate vs Extremist (Surat Split 1907, Lucknow Pact 1916).

Partition of Bengal & Swadeshi — Announced by Lord Curzon (1905), Boycott and Swadeshi movement, Vande Mataram, annulment of partition in 1911 by Lord Hardinge II and shifting capital from Calcutta to Delhi.

Gandhian era — Arrival in India (9 January 1915, Pravasi Bharatiya Divas), Champaran Satyagraha (1917, Tinkathia system), Kheda & Ahmedabad (1918), Rowlatt Act & Jallianwala Bagh massacre (13 April 1919), Non-Cooperation Movement (1920–22, suspended after Chauri Chaura on 4 Feb 1922), Dandi March & Civil Disobedience (12 March – 6 April 1930), Gandhi-Irwin Pact (1931), Poona Pact (1932), Quit India Movement (8 August 1942, 'Do or Die').

Revolutionary movement — Kakori Train Action (1925), Hindustan Socialist Republican Association (HSRA 1928), Bhagat Singh, Sukhdev, Rajguru (martyred 23 March 1931), Chittagong Armoury Raid (1930, Surya Sen), Subhas Chandra Bose & INA (Forward Bloc 1939).

2. Exam-wise Style (qualitative)

SSC GD / SSC MTS — direct fact-based questions: leader–center, organization–founder, slogan–personality, act–year, book–author. Speed is critical.

RRB Group D — factual matching, chronological ordering of movements, and negative questions ("Which of the following was NOT associated with...?").

State police / constable exams — national movement milestones plus regional leaders and uprisings.

Higher exams that use these same basics (CHSL, NTPC, CGL) add multi-statement questions, detailed treaty clauses, and act provisions.

3. Common Traps (in words)

Chronological confusion — mixing the order of Gandhian movements: Champaran (1917) before Kheda (1918); Non-Cooperation (1920) before Civil Disobedience (1930); Quit India (1942) after Cripps Mission.

Similar organizations — Brahmo Samaj (1828) vs Prarthana Samaj (1867) vs Arya Samaj (1875); HRA (1924, Kanpur) vs HSRA (1928, Feroz Shah Kotla, Delhi).

Almost true attribution — attributing the abolition of Sati to Dalhousie instead of William Bentinck (1829); or the founding of INC to Lord Dufferin instead of A.O. Hume.

Negative phrasing — "Who among the following was NOT an extremist leader?" missing 'NOT'.

Date confusion — Jallianwala Bagh was 1919 (not 1920); Dandi March was 1930 (not 1931).

4. Top 10 High-Yield Model Questions with Solutions and Trap Analysis

Question 1
In which year was the historic Battle of Plassey fought?
(a) 1757 (b) 1764 (c) 1761 (d) 1765
Answer: (a) 1757
Trap Type: Chronological Confusion — 1764 was Buxar, 1761 was Third Panipat, 1765 was Treaty of Allahabad.
15/45-sec Rule: 15-second fact. Score maximizer.
Source: NCERT Class 8 History (Our Pasts-III), Ch 2 From Trade to Territory

Question 2
The 'Permanent Settlement' (Zamindari Bandobast) was introduced in Bengal and Bihar in 1793 by:
(a) Warren Hastings (b) Lord Cornwallis (c) Lord Wellesley (d) Lord William Bentinck
Answer: (b) Lord Cornwallis
Trap Type: Similar Name / Reformer — Cornwallis is renowned for the Permanent Settlement and Civil Services reforms.
15/45-sec Rule: 15-second fact.
Source: NCERT Class 8 History (Our Pasts-III), Ch 3 Ruling the Countryside

Question 3
Who among the following led the Revolt of 1857 at Jagdishpur (Arrah) in Bihar?
(a) Nana Saheb (b) Kunwar Singh (c) Khan Bahadur Khan (d) Maulvi Ahmadullah
Answer: (b) Kunwar Singh
Trap Type: Similar Names — Nana Saheb led at Kanpur; Khan Bahadur Khan at Bareilly; Kunwar Singh at Bihar.
15/45-sec Rule: 15-second fact.
Source: NCERT Class 8 History (Our Pasts-III), Ch 5 When People Rebel 1857 and After

Question 4
'Brahmo Samaj' was founded in Calcutta in 1828 by:
(a) Swami Vivekananda (b) Swami Dayanand Saraswati (c) Raja Ram Mohan Roy (d) Ishwar Chandra Vidyasagar
Answer: (c) Raja Ram Mohan Roy
Trap Type: Similar Reformers — Dayanand Saraswati founded Arya Samaj (1875); Vivekananda founded Ramakrishna Mission (1897).
15/45-sec Rule: 15-second fact.
Source: NCERT Class 8 History (Our Pasts-III), Ch 8 Women, Caste and Reform

Question 5
The first session of the Indian National Congress in December 1885 was presided over by:
(a) A.O. Hume (b) W.C. Bonnerjee (c) Dadabhai Naoroji (d) Badruddin Tyabji
Answer: (b) W.C. Bonnerjee
Trap Type: Founder vs President — A.O. Hume was the founder/general secretary; Womesh Chandra Bonnerjee was the first president.
15/45-sec Rule: 15-second fact.
Source: NCERT Class 8 History (Our Pasts-III), Ch 9 The Making of the National Movement

Question 6
The infamous Jallianwala Bagh massacre took place at Amritsar on:
(a) 13 April 1919 (b) 13 April 1920 (c) 10 May 1857 (d) 6 April 1919
Answer: (a) 13 April 1919
Trap Type: Date Confusion — 13 April 1919 (Baisakhi day) is the exact date of General Dyer's firing on peaceful gathering.
15/45-sec Rule: 15-second fact.
Source: NCERT Class 8 History (Our Pasts-III), Ch 9 The Making of the National Movement

Question 7
Mahatma Gandhi suspended the Non-Cooperation Movement in February 1922 due to:
(a) Arrest of prominent leaders (b) Chauri Chaura incident (c) Signing of Gandhi-Irwin Pact (d) Jallianwala Bagh massacre
Answer: (b) Chauri Chaura incident
Trap Type: Chronological Cause — A violent clash at Chauri Chaura (Gorakhpur) on 4 Feb 1922 led Gandhi to call off the non-violent movement.
15/45-sec Rule: 15-second fact.
Source: NCERT Class 8 History (Our Pasts-III), Ch 9 The Making of the National Movement

Question 8
The historic 'Dandi March' began from Sabarmati Ashram on:
(a) 12 March 1930 (b) 6 April 1930 (c) 8 August 1942 (d) 26 January 1930
Answer: (a) 12 March 1930
Trap Type: Start vs End Date — The march started on 12 March 1930 and reached Dandi on 6 April 1930.
15/45-sec Rule: 15-second fact.
Source: NCERT Class 8 History (Our Pasts-III), Ch 9 The Making of the National Movement

Question 9
The slogan 'Do or Die' (Karo ya Maro) was given by Mahatma Gandhi during which national movement?
(a) Non-Cooperation Movement (b) Civil Disobedience Movement (c) Quit India Movement (d) Champaran Satyagraha
Answer: (c) Quit India Movement
Trap Type: Slogan Assignment — Gandhi delivered the 'Do or Die' call at Gowalia Tank maidan, Bombay on 8 August 1942.
15/45-sec Rule: 15-second fact.
Source: NCERT Class 8 History (Our Pasts-III), Ch 9 The Making of the National Movement

Question 10
Who authored the influential book 'Poverty and Un-British Rule in India' expounding the Drain of Wealth theory?
(a) Gopal Krishna Gokhale (b) Dadabhai Naoroji (c) R.C. Dutt (d) M.G. Ranade
Answer: (b) Dadabhai Naoroji
Trap Type: Economic Historians — Dadabhai Naoroji (Grand Old Man of India) authored this landmark critique.
15/45-sec Rule: 15-second fact.
Source: NCERT Class 8 History (Our Pasts-III), Ch 9 The Making of the National Movement
