Flashcard Deck: Physics in Everyday Life (Foundation)

Card 1
Front: It has "year" in its name, but you cannot measure your age with it. What does a light year measure?
Back: Distance — the distance light travels in one year, about 9.46 × 10^15 m.

Card 2
Front: Your electricity bill says "120 units". One unit of what, exactly?
Back: Energy. 1 unit = 1 kilowatt-hour (kWh) = 3.6 × 10^6 J.

Card 3
Front: The bus brakes suddenly and you lurch forward. Which law of Newton just pushed you?
Back: The First Law (inertia of motion) — your body tends to keep moving even after the bus stops.

Card 4
Front: Why does a fielder pull his hands back while catching a fast ball?
Back: To increase the time of impact; the same change in momentum over a longer time means a smaller force on the hands.

Card 5
Front: A rocket has nothing to push against in space. So how does it move forward?
Back: Newton's Third Law — gases are pushed out backward (action), and the rocket is pushed forward (reaction).

Card 6
Front: On the Moon, does a 60 kg astronaut become a 10 kg astronaut?
Back: No. Mass stays 60 kg. Only the weight becomes about one-sixth, because g on the Moon is about one-sixth of Earth's.

Card 7
Front: Dal takes forever to cook in the hills, but whistles quickly in a pressure cooker. What is the common physics?
Back: Boiling point depends on pressure. Low pressure on hills → water boils below 100 °C; high pressure in a cooker → above 100 °C.

Card 8
Front: Which law lets a mechanic lift a car with a small push on a hydraulic jack?
Back: Pascal's law — pressure on an enclosed liquid is transmitted equally in all directions.

Card 9
Front: Steam at 100 °C and water at 100 °C — same temperature. Why does steam burn more?
Back: Steam also releases its latent heat of vaporisation (about 22.5 × 10^5 J/kg) when it condenses on the skin.

Card 10
Front: Lakes freeze in winter, yet fish survive. Which strange property of water saves them?
Back: Anomalous expansion — water is densest at 4 °C, so the 4 °C water stays at the bottom while ice forms on top.

Card 11
Front: Can two astronauts on the Moon hear each other shout without radios?
Back: No. Sound needs a medium; the Moon has no air.

Card 12
Front: A bat flies in total darkness without hitting walls. What does it use?
Back: Ultrasound (above 20 kHz) and its echoes — echolocation.

Card 13
Front: Why is the sky blue and the danger signal red?
Back: Air scatters blue light the most (blue sky); red is scattered the least, so it travels farthest (danger signals).

Card 14
Front: Which mirror sits on the side of your scooter, and why that one?
Back: Convex mirror — it always gives an erect, smaller image and a wider field of view.

Card 15
Front: Mnemonic check: "MyopiA → concAve". What does it help you remember?
Back: Myopia (short-sightedness) is corrected by a concave lens; hypermetropia by a convex lens.

Card 16
Front: Mnemonic check: "Red Runs, Black Back, Green Ground" — what is it about?
Back: Household wire colours (NCERT): live — red, neutral — black, earth — green.

Card 17
Front: A fuse is a thin wire of low melting point. Where exactly is it connected?
Back: In series, in the live wire. It melts and breaks the circuit when too much current flows.

Card 18
Front: Examiner's trap: "Astronauts float because there is no gravity in space." True or false?
Back: False (Almost True Statement trap). Gravity acts in orbit; astronauts are weightless because they are in free fall with the spacecraft.

Card 19
Front: Examiner's trap: "Horsepower and kilowatt-hour are both units of power." Spot the error.
Back: Horsepower is a unit of power (about 746 W); kilowatt-hour is a unit of energy (Similar Name trap).

Card 20
Front: Your full image in a plane mirror — how tall must the mirror be, at least?
Back: Half your height, whatever your distance from the mirror.
