📊 PYQ Pattern Analysis: Physics in Everyday Life

How exams ask this chapter
In SSC GD, SSC MTS, RRB Group D and State police/constable papers, physics in the General Science part is asked mostly as one-line "everyday reason" questions and simple unit/instrument matching. Calculations, when they come, are short: a unit conversion, a temperature change, an echo distance or an electricity bill. The questions test whether you can link a daily-life event to the correct principle.

Sub-topics that exams like to ask (qualitative)
Very often asked:
SI units and the quantity they measure (newton, joule, watt, pascal, hertz; light year as distance)
Instruments and their use (barometer, hygrometer, lactometer, anemometer, seismograph, ammeter/voltmeter)
Light in daily life — rear-view mirror, blue sky, red danger signal, twinkling stars, eye defects and lenses, rainbow
Sound — ultrasound and infrasound, echo, bats, sound in vacuum, pitch and loudness
Heat — pressure cooker, matka, steam burn, anomalous expansion of water, temperature conversion

Often asked:
Newton's laws with examples (inertia in a bus, rocket, gun recoil)
Pressure examples (sharp knife, broad straps), Pascal's law, Archimedes' principle and floating
Household electricity — fuse, earthing, wire colours, parallel wiring, kWh
Energy conversions (motor, generator, cell, solar cell)

Sometimes asked:
Mass vs weight on the Moon, weightlessness, escape velocity
Surface tension and capillarity examples
Simple numericals: v = fλ, P = VI, series–parallel resistance, equations of motion

Traps that examiners use
Almost True Statement — one word changed: "convex lens for myopia", "fuse in parallel", "boiling point rises on hills".
Similar Name — light year (time vs distance), hygrometer vs hydrometer, ultrasound vs infrasound, kWh vs kW.
Reverse Causation — "sky is blue because sea is blue", "motor converts mechanical to electrical".
Negative Phrasing — "Which of the following is NOT an example of inertia?"
Chronological Confusion — sea breeze (day) vs land breeze (night).

Top 10 High-Yield Questions (with solutions)

Q1. The SI unit of pressure is:
(a) newton (b) pascal (c) joule (d) watt
Answer: (b) pascal
Solution: Pressure = force ÷ area = N/m² = pascal. Newton is force, joule is energy, watt is power.
Trap Type: Similar Name (all four are SI units named after scientists).
Source: NCERT Class 9 Science
⏱ 15-second rule: Pure recall — answer in 15 seconds and move on.

Q2. A light year is a unit of:
(a) time (b) distance (c) speed of light (d) intensity of light
Answer: (b) distance
Solution: It is the distance light travels in one year, about 9.46 × 10^15 m.
Trap Type: Similar Name ("year" suggests time).
Source: PYQ-style

Q3. Which mirror is used as a rear-view mirror in vehicles?
(a) Plane (b) Concave (c) Convex (d) Cylindrical
Answer: (c) Convex
Solution: A convex mirror always forms an erect, diminished image and gives a wider field of view.
Trap Type: Similar Name (concave vs convex).
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science

Q4. The sky appears blue because of:
(a) reflection of light from the sea (b) scattering of light by air molecules (c) refraction of light (d) dispersion of light by clouds
Answer: (b)
Solution: Air molecules scatter shorter-wavelength blue light much more than red light.
Trap Type: Reverse Causation (the sea reflects the blue sky, not the other way).
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science

Q5. Short-sightedness (myopia) is corrected by using a:
(a) convex lens (b) concave lens (c) cylindrical lens (d) bifocal lens
Answer: (b) concave lens
Solution: In myopia the image of a distant object forms in front of the retina; a concave (diverging) lens moves it back onto the retina.
Trap Type: Almost True Statement (lens swapped).
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science

Q6. Bats can fly in the dark because they use:
(a) infrasound (b) ultrasound (c) light waves (d) radio waves
Answer: (b) ultrasound
Solution: Bats emit ultrasound (above 20 kHz) and detect the echoes from obstacles and prey.
Trap Type: Similar Name (ultra vs infra).
Source: NCERT Class 9 Science

Q7. Food cooks faster in a pressure cooker because:
(a) the boiling point of water decreases (b) the boiling point of water increases (c) heat is produced by pressure (d) steam is cooler
Answer: (b)
Solution: Higher pressure inside raises the boiling point of water above 100 °C, so food gets more heat.
Trap Type: Reverse Causation.
Source: PYQ-style

Q8. A fuse wire is connected in a household circuit:
(a) in parallel with the live wire (b) in series with the live wire (c) in the earth wire (d) in series with the neutral wire
Answer: (b)
Solution: The fuse must be in series in the live wire so that when it melts the appliance is cut off from the live supply.
Trap Type: Almost True Statement.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science

Q9. A man shouts towards a cliff and hears the echo after 2 s. If the speed of sound is 340 m/s, how far is the cliff?
(a) 170 m (b) 340 m (c) 680 m (d) 1360 m
Answer: (b) 340 m
Solution: Sound goes to the cliff and comes back, so distance = (speed × time) ÷ 2 = (340 × 2) ÷ 2 = 340 m.
Trap Type: Almost True Statement (680 m forgets to divide by 2).
Source: NCERT Class 9 Science
⏱ 45-second rule: Write the formula d = vt/2 first, then calculate — 45 seconds is enough. Do not mark 680 m in a hurry.

Q10. At what temperature do the Celsius and Fahrenheit scales show the same reading?
(a) 0° (b) −32° (c) −40° (d) 40°
Answer: (c) −40°
Solution: Put °F = °C = x in °F = (9/5)°C + 32: x = 1.8x + 32 → −0.8x = 32 → x = −40.
Trap Type: Almost True Statement (−32 looks familiar).
Source: PYQ-style
⏱ 45-second rule: If you remember the fact, answer in 15 seconds; otherwise solve the one-line equation in 45 seconds.

How to use this analysis
First master the "very often asked" list — these are mostly 15-second questions.
For every principle, remember one daily-life example; exams almost always ask through an example.
For numericals, write the formula, put the numbers, and check the unit of the answer.
