Set 4/6 – Physics in Everyday Life MCQs (Foundation)
Questions 76–100: Medium

76. Two plane mirrors are placed at an angle of 60° to each other. How many images of an object kept between them will be formed?
(a) 6 (b) 5 (c) 3 (d) 4
Answer: (b)
Solution: Number of images = 360/θ − 1 (when 360/θ is even). Here 360/60 = 6, so images = 6 − 1 = 5.
Source: PYQ-style

77. A girl stands 2 m in front of a plane mirror. What is the distance between the girl and her image?
(a) 2 m (b) 4 m (c) 1 m (d) 8 m
Answer: (b)
Solution: The image is as far behind the mirror as the object is in front, that is 2 m behind. Distance between girl and image = 2 + 2 = 4 m.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science

78. What is the minimum length of a plane mirror needed for a person 1.6 m tall to see his full image?
(a) 1.6 m (b) 0.8 m (c) 0.4 m (d) 3.2 m
Answer: (b)
Solution: The minimum length needed is half the height of the person: 1.6 ÷ 2 = 0.8 m.
Source: PYQ-style

79. Which of the following uses a concave mirror?
(a) Rear-view mirror of a scooter (b) Mirror at a blind turn on a hill road (c) Reflector of a car headlight (d) Periscope
Answer: (c)
Solution: A concave mirror sends light from a bulb at its focus as a strong parallel beam, so it is used in headlights and torches. Rear-view and road-turn mirrors are convex; a periscope uses plane mirrors.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science

80. A swimming pool is 2 m deep. If the refractive index of water is 4/3, how deep will it appear when viewed from directly above?
(a) 2.67 m (b) 1.5 m (c) 2 m (d) 0.75 m
Answer: (b)
Solution: Apparent depth = real depth ÷ refractive index = 2 ÷ (4/3) = 2 × 3/4 = 1.5 m. The pool looks shallower than it is.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Physics

81. The refractive index of glass is 1.5. What is the speed of light in glass? (speed of light in vacuum = 3 × 10^8 m/s)
(a) 4.5 × 10^8 m/s (b) 2 × 10^8 m/s (c) 3 × 10^8 m/s (d) 1.5 × 10^8 m/s
Answer: (b)
Solution: Refractive index n = c ÷ v, so v = c ÷ n = 3 × 10^8 ÷ 1.5 = 2 × 10^8 m/s.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science

82. A rainbow is seen in the sky:
(a) in the direction opposite to the Sun, with the Sun behind the observer (b) in the direction of the Sun (c) only when there are no water droplets in the air (d) directly around the Sun at noon
Answer: (a)
Solution: A rainbow is formed by dispersion, refraction and internal reflection of sunlight in raindrops, and it is always seen with the Sun behind the observer.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science

83. The Sun is visible about 2 minutes before the actual sunrise because of:
(a) scattering of light (b) atmospheric refraction (c) dispersion of light (d) total internal reflection
Answer: (b)
Solution: Light from the Sun just below the horizon bends towards the Earth while passing through the atmosphere, so we see the Sun earlier (and also about 2 minutes after the actual sunset).
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science

84. To an astronaut at a very high altitude, the sky appears dark instead of blue because:
(a) the Sun stops shining at that height (b) the astronaut's eyes cannot see blue in space (c) blue light is absorbed by the spacecraft (d) there is almost no air to scatter sunlight
Answer: (d)
Solution: The blue colour of the sky comes from scattering of light by air molecules. At very high altitude there is hardly any air, so no scattering, and the sky looks dark.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science

85. A mirage seen on a hot road in summer is caused by:
(a) scattering of light (b) dispersion of light (c) total internal reflection of light (d) diffraction of light
Answer: (c)
Solution: Air near the hot road is hotter and optically rarer than the air above. Light from the sky bends more and more and finally gets totally internally reflected, looking like water on the road.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Physics

86. Clouds appear white because:
(a) clouds are made of white dust (b) large water droplets scatter all colours of light nearly equally (c) clouds absorb all colours except white (d) clouds reflect only blue light
Answer: (b)
Solution: Very small particles scatter mainly blue light, but cloud droplets are large and scatter all wavelengths nearly equally, so clouds look white.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science

87. Which of the following produces infrasound (frequency below 20 Hz)?
(a) A bat (b) A dolphin (c) An earthquake (d) A dog whistle
Answer: (c)
Solution: Earthquakes produce low-frequency infrasound before the main shock waves. Bats and dolphins use ultrasound, and a dog whistle produces sound above the human range — the infra/ultra trap.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Science

88. As an ambulance with its siren on comes towards you, the siren sounds:
(a) higher in pitch (b) lower in pitch (c) the same in pitch (d) completely silent
Answer: (a)
Solution: This is the Doppler effect: when the source approaches, more waves reach you per second, so the apparent frequency and pitch rise; when it moves away the pitch falls.
Source: PYQ-style

89. We can recognise a friend's voice on the phone without seeing him because of the sound's:
(a) pitch (b) loudness (c) speed (d) quality (timbre)
Answer: (d)
Solution: Quality or timbre lets us tell two sounds apart even when they have the same pitch and loudness; every person's voice has its own quality.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Science

90. Lightning is seen, and its thunder is heard 3 s later. If the speed of sound is 340 m/s, how far away was the lightning? (Ignore the time taken by light.)
(a) 113 m (b) 340 m (c) 1020 m (d) 2040 m
Answer: (c)
Solution: Light reaches almost instantly, so the 3 s is the travel time of sound one way. Distance = speed × time = 340 × 3 = 1020 m. Do not divide by 2 — this is not an echo.
Source: PYQ-style

91. In a thermos flask, the vacuum between the double walls reduces heat transfer by:
(a) radiation only (b) conduction and convection (c) evaporation only (d) reflection
Answer: (b)
Solution: Conduction and convection both need a medium, so a vacuum stops them. Radiation is reduced by the silvered walls, not by the vacuum.
Source: PYQ-style

92. Woollen clothes keep us warm in winter because:
(a) wool traps air, which is a poor conductor of heat (b) wool produces heat (c) wool is a good conductor of heat (d) wool absorbs heat from the Sun only
Answer: (a)
Solution: Wool fibres trap a lot of air between them; air is a poor conductor, so body heat does not escape easily.
Source: NCERT Class 7 Science

93. Small gaps are left between the rails of a railway track because:
(a) to save iron (b) to allow rainwater to flow (c) rails expand on heating in summer (d) to make the train run faster
Answer: (c)
Solution: Steel rails expand on heating; without gaps they would bend in summer heat. This is thermal expansion.
Source: PYQ-style

94. The usual range of a clinical thermometer is:
(a) 0 °C to 100 °C (b) 35 °C to 42 °C (c) -10 °C to 110 °C (d) 30 °C to 50 °C
Answer: (b)
Solution: The range -10 °C to 110 °C belongs to a laboratory thermometer. Body temperature normally stays between 35 °C and 42 °C, so a clinical thermometer reads from 35 °C to 42 °C.
Source: NCERT Class 7 Science

95. A patient has a fever of 104 °F. What is this on the Celsius scale?
(a) 40 °C (b) 72 °C (c) 58 °C (d) 36 °C
Answer: (a)
Solution: Option (b) forgets to multiply by 5/9. Correct: °C = (°F − 32) × 5/9 = (104 − 32) × 5/9 = 72 × 5/9 = 40 °C.
Source: PYQ-style

96. One kilowatt-hour is equal to:
(a) 3.6 × 10^3 J (b) 1000 J (c) 3.6 × 10^6 J (d) 36 × 10^6 J
Answer: (c)
Solution: 1 kWh = 1000 W × 3600 s = 3,600,000 J = 3.6 × 10^6 J.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science

97. A bird can sit safely on a single high-voltage overhead wire because:
(a) the bird's feet are insulators (b) there is no potential difference across its body, so no current flows through it (c) current does not flow in overhead wires (d) birds have zero resistance
Answer: (b)
Solution: Both feet are on the same wire at the same potential, so no current passes through the bird. If it touched two wires at once, current would flow.
Source: PYQ-style

98. The main purpose of the earth wire in household wiring is to:
(a) carry leakage current safely to the ground and protect the user from electric shock (b) increase the voltage of the supply (c) save electricity (d) make appliances work faster
Answer: (a)
Solution: The earth wire connects the metal body of an appliance to the ground. If current leaks to the body, it flows to the earth instead of through the person touching it.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science

99. An electromagnet differs from a permanent magnet because:
(a) its magnetism can be switched on and off by switching the current on and off (b) it attracts all metals, including copper (c) it works without any electric current (d) its poles can never be reversed
Answer: (a)
Solution: An electromagnet is magnetic only while current flows through its coil; reversing the current reverses its poles. That is why it is used in cranes and electric bells.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science

100. A gun of mass 4 kg fires a bullet of mass 0.02 kg with a speed of 400 m/s. What is the recoil speed of the gun?
(a) 2 m/s (b) 0.5 m/s (c) 8 m/s (d) 20 m/s
Answer: (a)
Solution: By conservation of momentum, momentum of gun = momentum of bullet = 0.02 × 400 = 8 kg m/s (option (c) is this momentum, not the speed). Recoil speed = 8 ÷ 4 = 2 m/s.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Science
