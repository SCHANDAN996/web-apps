🧒 Feynman Technique: Why Do Astronauts Float If Gravity Is Still There?

The confusing fact
Most students believe: "Astronauts float in the space station because there is no gravity in space." Exams love this line — and it is wrong. At the height where the International Space Station orbits (a few hundred kilometres up), Earth's gravity is still strong — only a little less than on the ground. So why do astronauts float?

Explain it to a 12-year-old
Imagine you are standing on a weighing scale inside a lift. The scale shows your weight because the floor is pushing up on your feet.

Now imagine the lift cable breaks (don't worry, it is only a thought experiment). You fall. The lift falls. The scale falls. Everything falls together at the same speed. Your feet stop pressing on the scale, so the scale reads zero. You feel weightless — but gravity has not switched off. In fact, gravity is the very reason you are falling!

A space station is like that falling lift, with one difference: it is also moving sideways very fast. It keeps falling towards Earth, but because it is moving sideways so fast, the Earth's curved surface keeps "falling away" below it. So it falls around the Earth forever. That is what an orbit is — endless falling.

Astronauts and the station fall together, so nothing presses on anything. That is weightlessness.

The daily-life example
Throw a ball to a friend. While it is in the air, the ball is in free fall. If a tiny ant sat inside a hollow ball, it would float inside it for that one second — just like an astronaut.
On a swing or a giant wheel, when you suddenly drop down, you feel your stomach "lift". For that moment, you are partly in free fall and feel lighter.

The connection with weight
Weight = the force with which gravity pulls you (W = mg).
What a scale shows = how hard you press on it.
In free fall you press on nothing, so the scale shows zero. Your mass (amount of matter) never changes.

The unforgettable trick
"Floating is FALLING, not FREEDOM from gravity."
Remember the three F's: Falling lift, Falling ball, Falling station — all feel weightless.

Watch the trap
Statement: "Astronauts are weightless because the Moon's gravity cancels the Earth's gravity." — Wrong.
Statement: "Astronauts are weightless because they are in free fall along with the spacecraft." — Right.
Statement: "On the Moon an astronaut's mass becomes one-sixth." — Wrong. Weight becomes about one-sixth; mass stays the same.
