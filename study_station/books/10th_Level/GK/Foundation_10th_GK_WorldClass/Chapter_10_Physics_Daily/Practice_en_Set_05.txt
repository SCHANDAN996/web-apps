101. The upward lift on the wings of an aeroplane is best explained by:
(a) Pascal's law (b) Archimedes' principle (c) Bernoulli's principle (d) Newton's law of gravitation
Answer: (c)
Solution: Air flows faster over the curved upper surface of the wing, so pressure there is lower than below the wing; this pressure difference gives lift (Bernoulli's principle).
Source: NCERT Class 11 Physics

102. During a storm, the roofs of some houses are blown off because:
(a) the air pressure above the roof becomes higher than inside (b) fast-moving air above the roof has lower pressure than the still air inside (c) the roof becomes lighter in a storm (d) gravity becomes weaker during a storm
Answer: (b)
Solution: Fast wind over the roof lowers the pressure above it, while the air inside is at normal pressure; the higher pressure from inside pushes the roof up.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Physics

103. Racing cars are built low and wide mainly to:
(a) keep their centre of gravity low and make them stable (b) make them heavier (c) increase air resistance (d) reduce the number of wheels needed
Answer: (a)
Solution: A low centre of gravity and a wide base make a body more stable, so the car does not topple at high speed on turns.
Source: PYQ-style

104. A cyclist leans inward while taking a turn on a circular track in order to:
(a) reduce his speed (b) reduce his weight (c) increase air resistance (d) provide the centripetal force needed for turning
Answer: (d)
Solution: Motion in a circle needs a force directed towards the centre. By leaning inward, part of the road's reaction acts towards the centre and supplies this centripetal force.
Source: PYQ-style

105. Detergents clean clothes better than plain water because they:
(a) increase the surface tension of water (b) reduce the surface tension of water (c) increase the density of water (d) make water hotter
Answer: (b)
Solution: By lowering surface tension, detergent solution spreads and soaks into the fine spaces of the cloth, so it removes dirt and grease better.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Physics

106. Ice at 0 °C cools a drink more than the same mass of water at 0 °C because:
(a) ice is heavier than water (b) ice has a lower specific heat (c) ice absorbs latent heat of fusion while melting (d) ice is a good conductor of heat
Answer: (c)
Solution: To melt, ice takes in its latent heat of fusion (about 3.34 × 10^5 J/kg) from the drink without any rise in its own temperature, so it removes much more heat.
Source: PYQ-style

107. How much heat is needed to raise the temperature of 2 kg of water from 20 °C to 70 °C? (specific heat of water = 4200 J/kg °C)
(a) 42 kJ (b) 420 kJ (c) 4200 kJ (d) 210 kJ
Answer: (b)
Solution: Q = m × c × rise in temperature = 2 × 4200 × (70 − 20) = 2 × 4200 × 50 = 420000 J = 420 kJ.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Physics

108. A refrigerator cools the food inside it mainly because:
(a) the refrigerant evaporates inside the pipes and absorbs heat from the inside (b) ice is stored in its walls (c) its door keeps out sunlight (d) cold air is pumped in from outside
Answer: (a)
Solution: The liquid refrigerant evaporates in the pipes inside the cabinet; evaporation absorbs heat, which is then released outside at the back of the refrigerator.
Source: PYQ-style

109. Ink sometimes leaks from a fountain pen in an aeroplane flying high because:
(a) ink becomes heavier at height (b) the temperature of ink rises sharply (c) the air pressure outside the pen decreases at height (d) gravity increases at height
Answer: (c)
Solution: At height, the outside air pressure is lower than the pressure of the air trapped inside the pen, so the trapped air pushes the ink out.
Source: PYQ-style

110. At sea level, the height of the mercury column in a simple barometer is about:
(a) 76 mm (b) 76 cm (c) 7.6 m (d) 760 cm
Answer: (b)
Solution: Normal atmospheric pressure (about 1.01 × 10^5 Pa) supports a mercury column about 76 cm (760 mm) high. Writing 76 mm is the unit trap.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Physics

111. In a hydraulic lift, a force of 100 N is applied on a small piston of area 0.01 m². What force is produced on the large piston of area 1 m²?
(a) 100 N (b) 1000 N (c) 10000 N (d) 1 N
Answer: (c)
Solution: By Pascal's law, pressure is the same on both pistons: 100 ÷ 0.01 = 10000 Pa. Force on large piston = pressure × area = 10000 × 1 = 10000 N.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Physics

112. A stone is dropped from the top of a building 20 m high. How long does it take to reach the ground? (g = 10 m/s², ignore air resistance)
(a) 1 s (b) 2 s (c) 4 s (d) 20 s
Answer: (b)
Solution: Using s = ut + ½gt² with u = 0: 20 = ½ × 10 × t², so t² = 4 and t = 2 s.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Science

113. A car moving at 20 m/s is brought to rest in 4 s by applying brakes uniformly. How far does it travel after the brakes are applied?
(a) 80 m (b) 40 m (c) 20 m (d) 5 m
Answer: (b)
Solution: a = (0 − 20) ÷ 4 = −5 m/s². s = ut + ½at² = 20 × 4 + ½ × (−5) × 16 = 80 − 40 = 40 m.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Science

114. What is the momentum of a cricket ball of mass 0.15 kg moving with a speed of 20 m/s?
(a) 3 kg m/s (b) 30 kg m/s (c) 0.3 kg m/s (d) 133 kg m/s
Answer: (a)
Solution: Momentum p = mass × velocity = 0.15 × 20 = 3 kg m/s.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Science

115. An electric heater is rated 1100 W, 220 V. What current does it draw when working normally?
(a) 0.2 A (b) 5 A (c) 10 A (d) 50 A
Answer: (b)
Solution: P = VI, so I = P ÷ V = 1100 ÷ 220 = 5 A.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science

116. What is the resistance of the filament of a bulb rated 100 W, 220 V when it glows normally?
(a) 2.2 Ω (b) 484 Ω (c) 22000 Ω (d) 4.84 Ω
Answer: (b)
Solution: P = V²/R, so R = V² ÷ P = (220 × 220) ÷ 100 = 48400 ÷ 100 = 484 Ω.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science

117. A 2 kW room heater is used for 3 hours a day for 30 days. If electricity costs ₹5 per unit, what is the cost?
(a) ₹90 (b) ₹180 (c) ₹900 (d) ₹1800
Answer: (c)
Solution: Energy = 2 kW × 3 h × 30 = 180 kWh = 180 units (option (b) is the units, not the cost). Cost = 180 × 5 = ₹900.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science

118. The north pole of a freely suspended magnetic needle points towards the geographic north because:
(a) the Earth's magnetic north pole is near the geographic north (b) the Sun attracts the needle (c) the Earth's magnetic south pole lies near the geographic north (d) iron ores are found only in the north
Answer: (c)
Solution: Unlike poles attract. The north pole of the needle is attracted towards the geographic north, so the Earth's magnetic pole there must behave as a south pole. Option (a) is the almost-true trap.
Source: PYQ-style

119. X-rays were discovered by:
(a) Wilhelm Röntgen (b) Henri Becquerel (c) Marie Curie (d) J.J. Thomson
Answer: (a)
Solution: Wilhelm Conrad Röntgen discovered X-rays in 1895. Becquerel discovered radioactivity, and Marie Curie worked on radioactive elements — similar-period names used as traps.
Source: PYQ-style

120. The ozone layer in the upper atmosphere protects living beings on Earth from harmful:
(a) infrared rays (b) ultraviolet rays (c) radio waves (d) visible light
Answer: (b)
Solution: Ozone absorbs most of the harmful ultraviolet radiation from the Sun, which can cause skin cancer and harm to the eyes.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science

121. Consider the following statements:
Statement I: Sound travels faster in water than in air.
Statement II: Light travels faster in water than in air.
Statement III: The speed of sound in air increases with a rise in temperature.
Which of the statements are correct?
(a) Statement I and Statement II only (b) Statement I and Statement III only (c) Statement II and Statement III only (d) Statement I, Statement II and Statement III
Answer: (b)
Solution: Sound is faster in liquids than in gases, and faster in warmer air, so I and III are correct. Light is the opposite — it slows down in water (refractive index about 1.33), so II is wrong.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Science

122. Two identical bulbs, each rated 100 W, 220 V, are connected in series across a 220 V supply. What is the total power consumed?
(a) 200 W (b) 100 W (c) 50 W (d) 25 W
Answer: (c)
Solution: Resistance of each bulb = V²/P = 48400 ÷ 100 = 484 Ω. In series, R = 484 + 484 = 968 Ω. Total power = V²/R = 48400 ÷ 968 = 50 W.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science

123. A ball is thrown vertically upward with a speed of 20 m/s. What maximum height will it reach? (g = 10 m/s², ignore air resistance)
(a) 40 m (b) 20 m (c) 10 m (d) 2 m
Answer: (b)
Solution: At the highest point v = 0. Using v² = u² − 2gh: 0 = 20² − 2 × 10 × h, so 20h = 400 and h = 20 m.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Science

124. A convex lens of power +2 D is placed in contact with a concave lens of power −0.5 D. What are the power and focal length of the combination?
(a) +1.5 D and 66.7 cm (b) +2.5 D and 40 cm (c) −1.5 D and −66.7 cm (d) +1.5 D and 150 cm
Answer: (a)
Solution: Powers add: P = +2 + (−0.5) = +1.5 D. Focal length f = 1/P = 1/1.5 m ≈ 0.667 m = 66.7 cm (converging). Option (d) wrongly writes 1.5 m instead of 1/1.5 m.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science

125. If the length of a simple pendulum is made four times, its time period becomes:
(a) four times (b) two times (c) half (d) unchanged
Answer: (b)
Solution: T = 2π√(L/g), so T is proportional to √L. If L becomes 4L, T becomes √4 = 2 times. Option (a) forgets the square root.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Physics
