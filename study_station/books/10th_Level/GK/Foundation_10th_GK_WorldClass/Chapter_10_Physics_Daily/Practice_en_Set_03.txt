Set 3/6 – Physics in Everyday Life MCQs (Foundation)
Questions 51–75: Medium

51. A hygrometer is used to measure:
(a) atmospheric pressure (b) relative humidity of air (c) density of liquids (d) speed of wind
Answer: (b)
Solution: A hygrometer measures the humidity (moisture) of air. Do not confuse it with a hydrometer, which measures the relative density of liquids — a similar-name trap.
Source: PYQ-style

52. Which instrument is used to measure the speed of wind?
(a) Altimeter (b) Seismograph (c) Anemometer (d) Odometer
Answer: (c)
Solution: An anemometer measures wind speed. An altimeter measures altitude, a seismograph records earthquake waves and an odometer measures the distance travelled by a vehicle.
Source: PYQ-style

53. In an electric circuit, an ammeter and a voltmeter are connected respectively:
(a) in series; in parallel (b) in parallel; in series (c) in series; in series (d) in parallel; in parallel
Answer: (a)
Solution: An ammeter measures the current through a part, so it is placed in series. A voltmeter measures the potential difference across a part, so it is placed in parallel.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science

54. Consider the following statements:
Statement I: The mass of a body is the same on the Earth and on the Moon.
Statement II: A body weighs slightly more at the poles than at the equator.
Which of the statements is/are correct?
(a) Only Statement I (b) Only Statement II (c) Both Statement I and Statement II (d) Neither Statement I nor Statement II
Answer: (c)
Solution: Mass is the amount of matter and does not change with place. The value of g is slightly greater at the poles because the Earth is flattened there, so weight (mg) is slightly more at the poles.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Science

55. The acceleration due to gravity at the centre of the Earth is:
(a) 9.8 m/s² (b) maximum (c) infinite (d) zero
Answer: (d)
Solution: As we go deeper, g decreases; at the centre the pull of the Earth's mass from all sides cancels out, so g becomes zero.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Physics

56. The escape velocity from the surface of the Earth is about:
(a) 7.9 km/s (b) 11.2 km/s (c) 3 km/s (d) 42 km/s
Answer: (b)
Solution: The minimum speed needed for a body to escape the Earth's gravity from its surface is about 11.2 km/s. About 7.9 km/s is the speed for a low orbit near the surface — the tempting trap.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Physics

57. The time period of a simple pendulum does NOT depend on:
(a) the mass of the bob (b) the length of the string (c) the value of g (d) the place where the pendulum is used
Answer: (a)
Solution: T = 2π√(L/g), so the time period depends on length and on g (which changes from place to place), but not on the mass of the bob.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Physics

58. Tides in the sea are caused mainly by:
(a) winds blowing over the sea (b) the gravitational pull of the Moon (c) the rotation of the Sun (d) earthquakes under the sea
Answer: (b)
Solution: The Moon's gravitational pull on the Earth's oceans is the main cause of tides; the Sun's pull also contributes, but less.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Science

59. If the speed of a moving car is doubled, its kinetic energy becomes:
(a) the same (b) two times (c) four times (d) half
Answer: (c)
Solution: KE = ½mv². If v becomes 2v, KE becomes ½m(2v)² = 4 × ½mv², that is, four times.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Science

60. What is the kinetic energy of a ball of mass 2 kg moving with a speed of 3 m/s?
(a) 6 J (b) 9 J (c) 18 J (d) 3 J
Answer: (b)
Solution: KE = ½mv² = ½ × 2 × 3² = ½ × 2 × 9 = 9 J.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Science

61. A box of mass 10 kg is kept on a shelf 5 m above the ground. What is its potential energy? (g = 9.8 m/s²)
(a) 50 J (b) 490 J (c) 500 J (d) 98 J
Answer: (b)
Solution: Option (c) wrongly takes g = 10 m/s². With g = 9.8 m/s², PE = mgh = 10 × 9.8 × 5 = 490 J.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Science

62. A boy of mass 50 kg runs up a staircase 10 m high in 20 s. What power does he develop? (g = 10 m/s²)
(a) 250 W (b) 500 W (c) 25 W (d) 5000 W
Answer: (a)
Solution: Work done = mgh = 50 × 10 × 10 = 5000 J. Power = work ÷ time = 5000 ÷ 20 = 250 W.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Science

63. A man stands still holding a heavy suitcase on his head for 10 minutes. The work done by him on the suitcase, in physics, is:
(a) very large (b) equal to weight × time (c) zero (d) equal to weight × height of the suitcase
Answer: (c)
Solution: Work = force × displacement. The suitcase does not move, so displacement is zero and the work done is zero, even though the man gets tired.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Science

64. A solar cell converts:
(a) heat energy into light energy (b) chemical energy into electrical energy (c) electrical energy into light energy (d) light energy into electrical energy
Answer: (d)
Solution: A solar cell (usually made of silicon) converts sunlight directly into electricity. A battery converts chemical energy into electrical energy.
Source: PYQ-style

65. A potential difference of 12 V is applied across a resistor of 4 Ω. What current flows through it?
(a) 48 A (b) 3 A (c) 0.33 A (d) 16 A
Answer: (b)
Solution: By Ohm's law, I = V ÷ R = 12 ÷ 4 = 3 A.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science

66. Two resistors of 6 Ω and 3 Ω are connected in parallel. What is their equivalent resistance?
(a) 9 Ω (b) 2 Ω (c) 18 Ω (d) 4.5 Ω
Answer: (b)
Solution: For parallel, 1/R = 1/6 + 1/3 = 1/6 + 2/6 = 3/6 = 1/2. So R = 2 Ω.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science

67. Electrical appliances in a house are connected in parallel because:
(a) each appliance gets the same voltage and can be switched on or off independently (b) the total resistance increases (c) less current is drawn from the mains (d) all appliances must work together
Answer: (a)
Solution: In parallel, every appliance gets the full supply voltage and has its own switch; if one fails, the others keep working.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science

68. The filament of an electric bulb is made of:
(a) copper (b) nichrome (c) tungsten (d) aluminium
Answer: (c)
Solution: Tungsten has a very high melting point, so it can glow white-hot without melting. Nichrome is used in heating elements.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science

69. The heating element of an electric iron is usually made of:
(a) copper (b) nichrome (c) tungsten (d) silver
Answer: (b)
Solution: Nichrome is an alloy with high resistivity that does not oxidise (burn) readily at high temperature, so it is ideal for heating elements.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science

70. A transformer:
(a) works only on AC (b) works only on DC (c) works equally on AC and DC (d) converts AC into DC
Answer: (a)
Solution: A transformer works on electromagnetic induction, which needs a changing current; steady DC produces no change in magnetic field, so a transformer does not work on DC.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Physics

71. Who discovered electromagnetic induction — that a changing magnetic field can produce an electric current?
(a) Hans Christian Oersted (b) Michael Faraday (c) Georg Simon Ohm (d) Isaac Newton
Answer: (b)
Solution: Michael Faraday discovered electromagnetic induction, the principle of the generator. Oersted showed the opposite effect — that a current produces a magnetic field — a similar-name trap.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science

72. A person can see distant objects clearly but cannot see nearby objects clearly. The defect and the lens used to correct it are:
(a) hypermetropia; convex lens (b) myopia; concave lens (c) hypermetropia; concave lens (d) myopia; convex lens
Answer: (a)
Solution: Difficulty with near objects is hypermetropia (long-sightedness); the image forms behind the retina, and a convex (converging) lens corrects it. Option (b) is the correct pair for the opposite defect.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science

73. The image formed on the retina persists for about:
(a) 1/16 of a minute (b) 1 second (c) 1/16 of a second (d) 1/100 of a second
Answer: (c)
Solution: Persistence of vision lasts about 1/16 of a second; that is why a fast sequence of still pictures in a film looks like continuous motion.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science

74. Which of the following has the longest wavelength?
(a) X-rays (b) Ultraviolet rays (c) Radio waves (d) Infrared rays
Answer: (c)
Solution: In order of decreasing wavelength: radio waves, microwaves, infrared, visible, ultraviolet, X-rays, gamma rays. So radio waves have the longest wavelength.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Physics

75. The remote control of a television set commonly uses:
(a) ultraviolet rays (b) X-rays (c) microwaves (d) infrared rays
Answer: (d)
Solution: A TV remote sends signals as pulses of infrared light from an LED to a sensor on the TV.
Source: PYQ-style
