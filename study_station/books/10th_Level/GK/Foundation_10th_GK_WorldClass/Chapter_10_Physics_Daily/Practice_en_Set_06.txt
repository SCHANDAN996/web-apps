Set 6/6 – Physics in Everyday Life MCQs (Foundation)
Questions 126–150: Hard

126. Consider the following statements:
Statement I: A convex mirror always forms a virtual, erect and diminished image.
Statement II: A concave mirror always forms a real image.
Statement III: A plane mirror forms a laterally inverted image.
Which of the statements are correct?
(a) Statement I and Statement II only (b) Statement I and Statement III only (c) Statement II and Statement III only (d) Statement I, Statement II and Statement III
Answer: (b)
Solution: A convex mirror always gives a virtual, erect, smaller image, and a plane mirror shows lateral inversion. A concave mirror gives a virtual, erect, magnified image when the object is between the pole and the focus (shaving mirror), so Statement II is the almost-true trap.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science

127. Which of the following pairs is NOT correctly matched?
(a) Hygrometer – relative humidity (b) Anemometer – speed of wind (c) Hydrometer – atmospheric pressure (d) Seismograph – earthquake waves
Answer: (c)
Solution: A hydrometer measures the relative density of liquids; atmospheric pressure is measured by a barometer. The other three pairs are correct. Note the word "NOT".
Source: PYQ-style

128. A 2 Ω resistor is connected in series with a parallel combination of a 3 Ω and a 6 Ω resistor. The arrangement is connected to a 12 V battery. What current flows from the battery?
(a) 1.09 A (b) 3 A (c) 6 A (d) 4 A
Answer: (b)
Solution: Parallel part = (3 × 6) ÷ (3 + 6) = 18 ÷ 9 = 2 Ω. Total resistance = 2 + 2 = 4 Ω. Current I = V ÷ R = 12 ÷ 4 = 3 A.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science

129. In a house, five 100 W bulbs and a 1000 W heater are switched on together on a 220 V supply. Which is the lowest fuse rating among the following that will NOT blow?
(a) 1 A (b) 2 A (c) 5 A (d) 10 A
Answer: (d)
Solution: Total power = 5 × 100 + 1000 = 1500 W. Current = 1500 ÷ 220 ≈ 6.8 A, which would blow a 5 A fuse, so the lowest safe rating here is 10 A.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science

130. A stone is dropped from rest and falls freely. How far does it fall during the 3rd second of its fall? (g = 10 m/s²)
(a) 45 m (b) 25 m (c) 30 m (d) 15 m
Answer: (b)
Solution: Distance in 3 s = ½ × 10 × 3² = 45 m; distance in 2 s = ½ × 10 × 2² = 20 m. Distance in the 3rd second = 45 − 20 = 25 m.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Science

131. A car of mass 1000 kg moving at 20 m/s is stopped in 5 s by its brakes. What is the average braking force?
(a) 4000 N (b) 20000 N (c) 100000 N (d) 400 N
Answer: (a)
Solution: Force = change in momentum ÷ time = (1000 × 20) ÷ 5 = 20000 ÷ 5 = 4000 N.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Science

132. An object weighs 60 N on the Earth (g = 10 m/s²). If g on the Moon is one-sixth of that on the Earth, what are its weight on the Moon and its mass?
(a) 10 N and 6 kg (b) 60 N and 6 kg (c) 10 N and 1 kg (d) 360 N and 6 kg
Answer: (a)
Solution: Mass = W ÷ g = 60 ÷ 10 = 6 kg, the same everywhere. Weight on the Moon = 60 ÷ 6 = 10 N. Option (c) wrongly reduces the mass too.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Science

133. A man standing between two parallel cliffs claps once. He hears the first echo after 2 s and the second echo after 3 s. If the speed of sound is 340 m/s, what is the distance between the two cliffs?
(a) 850 m (b) 1700 m (c) 425 m (d) 510 m
Answer: (a)
Solution: Distance to nearer cliff = 340 × 2 ÷ 2 = 340 m; to farther cliff = 340 × 3 ÷ 2 = 510 m. Distance between cliffs = 340 + 510 = 850 m.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Science

134. What is the wavelength in air of a sound of frequency 20 kHz, if the speed of sound is 340 m/s?
(a) 1.7 cm (b) 17 cm (c) 1.7 m (d) 6.8 m
Answer: (a)
Solution: λ = v ÷ f = 340 ÷ 20000 = 0.017 m = 1.7 cm. Remember to change 20 kHz to 20000 Hz.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Science

135. A coin at the bottom of a beaker of water appears to be 6 cm below the water surface. If the refractive index of water is 4/3, what is the real depth of the water?
(a) 4.5 cm (b) 8 cm (c) 6 cm (d) 10 cm
Answer: (b)
Solution: Refractive index = real depth ÷ apparent depth, so real depth = 6 × 4/3 = 8 cm. Option (a) divides instead of multiplying.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Physics

136. The average distance between the Sun and the Earth is about 1.5 × 10^11 m. How long does sunlight take to reach the Earth? (speed of light = 3 × 10^8 m/s)
(a) 50 s (b) 500 s (c) 5000 s (d) 8 s
Answer: (b)
Solution: Time = distance ÷ speed = (1.5 × 10^11) ÷ (3 × 10^8) = 0.5 × 10^3 s, that is about 8 minutes 20 seconds = 500 s.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science

137. The near point of a long-sighted person is 50 cm. What lens is needed so that he can read a book held at 25 cm?
(a) +2 D, convex lens (b) -2 D, concave lens (c) +4 D, convex lens (d) -4 D, concave lens
Answer: (a)
Solution: The lens must form a virtual image at 50 cm of a book at 25 cm: u = −25 cm, v = −50 cm. 1/f = 1/v − 1/u = −1/50 + 1/25 = 1/50, so f = +50 cm = 0.5 m. Power = 1/0.5 = +2 D.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science

138. The far point of a short-sighted person is 2 m. What is the power of the lens needed to correct his vision?
(a) -0.5 D (b) +0.5 D (c) -2 D (d) +2 D
Answer: (a)
Solution: A concave lens of focal length equal to the far point is needed: f = −2 m. Option (c) forgets to take the reciprocal. Power = 1/f = 1/(−2) = -0.5 D.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science

139. In a house, an electric iron of 1000 W is used for 30 minutes a day and four tube lights of 40 W each are used for 10 hours a day. How many units of electricity are used in 30 days?
(a) 63 units (b) 48 units (c) 15 units (d) 630 units
Answer: (a)
Solution: Iron: 1 kW × 0.5 h × 30 = 15 kWh. Tube lights: 4 × 0.04 kW × 10 h × 30 = 48 kWh. Total = 15 + 48 = 63 units.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science

140. 1 kg of water at 80 °C is mixed with 1 kg of water at 20 °C. If no heat is lost, what is the final temperature?
(a) 50 °C (b) 60 °C (c) 40 °C (d) 100 °C
Answer: (a)
Solution: Heat lost by hot water = heat gained by cold water: 1 × c × (80 − T) = 1 × c × (T − 20). So 80 − T = T − 20, giving 2T = 100 and T = 50 °C.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Physics

141. How much heat is needed to melt 2 kg of ice at 0 °C into water at 0 °C? (latent heat of fusion of ice = 3.34 × 10^5 J/kg)
(a) 6.68 × 10^5 J (b) 3.34 × 10^5 J (c) 1.67 × 10^5 J (d) 8.4 × 10^3 J
Answer: (a)
Solution: Q = m × L = 2 × 3.34 × 10^5 = 6.68 × 10^5 J. The temperature stays at 0 °C while the ice melts.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Science

142. At what temperature is the reading on the Fahrenheit scale exactly twice the reading on the Celsius scale?
(a) 160 °C (b) 320 °C (c) 80 °C (d) 40 °C
Answer: (a)
Solution: Put F = 2C in F = 1.8C + 32: 2C = 1.8C + 32, so 0.2C = 32 and C = 160 °C (then F = 320 °F, which is twice 160).
Source: PYQ-style

143. Consider the following statements about household wiring:
Statement I: Household appliances are connected in series with each other.
Statement II: The fuse is connected in the live wire.
Statement III: The earth wire is connected to the metal body of appliances.
Which of the statements are correct?
(a) Statement I and Statement II only (b) Statement I and Statement III only (c) Statement II and Statement III only (d) Statement I, Statement II and Statement III
Answer: (c)
Solution: Appliances are connected in parallel, not in series, so Statement I is wrong. The fuse is in the live wire and the earth wire is joined to metal bodies for safety, so II and III are correct.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science

144. What is the pressure due to water alone at a depth of 10 m in a lake? (density of water = 1000 kg/m³, g = 10 m/s²)
(a) 10000 Pa (b) 100000 Pa (c) 1000 Pa (d) 1000000 Pa
Answer: (b)
Solution: Pressure = h × ρ × g = 10 × 1000 × 10 = 100000 Pa.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Physics

145. A metal block of volume 0.002 m³ is completely immersed in water. What is the buoyant force on it? (density of water = 1000 kg/m³, g = 10 m/s²)
(a) 2 N (b) 20 N (c) 200 N (d) 0.2 N
Answer: (b)
Solution: Buoyant force = weight of water displaced = V × ρ × g = 0.002 × 1000 × 10 = 20 N.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Science

146. A wooden block of density 600 kg/m³ floats in water (density 1000 kg/m³). What fraction of its volume stays under water?
(a) 40% (b) 60% (c) 100% (d) 6%
Answer: (b)
Solution: For a floating body, the fraction under water = density of body ÷ density of liquid = 600 ÷ 1000 = 0.6, that is 60%.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Science

147. A pendulum has a time period of 2 s on the Earth. What will be its time period on the Moon, where g is one-sixth of that on the Earth?
(a) 2 s (b) 4.9 s (c) 12 s (d) 0.8 s
Answer: (b)
Solution: T = 2π√(L/g), so T is proportional to 1/√g. When g becomes g/6, T becomes √6 times: 2 × √6 ≈ 2 × 2.45 = 4.9 s.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Physics

148. If the momentum of a body is doubled while its mass stays the same, its kinetic energy becomes:
(a) two times (b) four times (c) eight times (d) unchanged
Answer: (b)
Solution: KE = p² ÷ 2m. If p becomes 2p, KE becomes (2p)² ÷ 2m = 4 × p² ÷ 2m, that is four times. "Two times" is the tempting almost-true option.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Science

149. An electric motor lifts a load of 200 kg through a height of 15 m in 30 s. What useful power does it deliver? (g = 10 m/s²)
(a) 1000 W (b) 100 W (c) 3000 W (d) 30000 W
Answer: (a)
Solution: Work done = mgh = 200 × 10 × 15 = 30000 J. Power = work ÷ time = 30000 ÷ 30 = 1000 W.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Science

150. An object is placed 10 cm in front of a concave mirror of focal length 15 cm (like a shaving mirror). What kind of image is formed?
(a) Magnified 3 times, erect and virtual (b) Magnified 3 times, inverted and real (c) Magnified 1.5 times, erect and virtual (d) Diminished, inverted and real
Answer: (a)
Solution: With u = −10 cm and f = −15 cm, 1/v = 1/f − 1/u = −1/15 + 1/10 = 1/30, so v = +30 cm (behind the mirror, virtual). Magnification m = −v/u = −30/(−10) = +3: erect and three times larger.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science
