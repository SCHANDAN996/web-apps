Set 2/6 – Physics in Everyday Life MCQs (Foundation)
Questions 26–50: Medium

26. Which of the following is NOT an SI base unit?
(a) kelvin (b) candela (c) newton (d) mole
Answer: (c)
Solution: The seven base units are metre, kilogram, second, ampere, kelvin, mole and candela. Newton (kg m/s²) is a derived unit. Watch the word "NOT" — it is a negative-phrasing trap.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Physics

27. One horsepower is approximately equal to:
(a) 746 W (b) 1000 W (c) 100 W (d) 3600 W
Answer: (a)
Solution: Horsepower is a unit of power; 1 kW = 1000 W is a different unit. One horsepower is about 746 W.
Source: PYQ-style

28. A car starts from rest and reaches a speed of 20 m/s in 5 s with uniform acceleration. What is its acceleration?
(a) 4 m/s² (b) 100 m/s² (c) 0.25 m/s² (d) 25 m/s²
Answer: (a)
Solution: Using v = u + at: 20 = 0 + a × 5, so a = 20 ÷ 5 = 4 m/s².
Source: NCERT Class 9 Science

29. A cricket fielder pulls his hands backward while catching a fast ball. This:
(a) reduces the momentum of the ball to zero instantly (b) increases the time of contact, so the force on the hands decreases (c) increases the force on the hands (d) increases the speed of the ball
Answer: (b)
Solution: Force = change in momentum ÷ time. The change in momentum is fixed, so a longer time of contact means a smaller force on the hands.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Science

30. Which of the following is an example of Newton's third law of motion?
(a) Dust falling off a carpet when it is beaten (b) A swimmer pushing water backward to move forward (c) Passengers falling forward when a bus brakes (d) A coin falling into a glass when the card below it is flicked
Answer: (b)
Solution: The swimmer pushes water backward (action) and the water pushes the swimmer forward (reaction). The other three are examples of inertia (first law).
Source: NCERT Class 9 Science

31. Ball bearings are used in machines because:
(a) they increase friction (b) they make machines heavier (c) rolling friction is less than sliding friction (d) they produce more heat
Answer: (c)
Solution: Ball bearings convert sliding into rolling; rolling friction is much smaller than sliding friction, so parts move easily.
Source: NCERT Class 8 Science

32. A force of 500 N acts on an area of 0.25 m². What is the pressure?
(a) 125 Pa (b) 2000 Pa (c) 500 Pa (d) 1000 Pa
Answer: (b)
Solution: Pressure = force ÷ area = 500 ÷ 0.25 = 2000 Pa.
Source: NCERT Class 8 Science

33. School bags are provided with broad straps because broad straps:
(a) increase the area of contact and reduce pressure on the shoulders (b) decrease the area and increase pressure (c) reduce the weight of the bag (d) increase the force on the shoulders
Answer: (a)
Solution: Pressure = force ÷ area. For the same weight, a bigger area of contact gives less pressure, so the bag is comfortable to carry.
Source: NCERT Class 8 Science

34. Hydraulic brakes in vehicles work on:
(a) Archimedes' principle (b) Bernoulli's principle (c) Newton's third law (d) Pascal's law
Answer: (d)
Solution: Pascal's law: pressure applied on an enclosed liquid is transmitted equally in all directions. Archimedes' principle is about buoyancy — a similar-sounding trap.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Physics

35. Cooking food is difficult at high mountains because:
(a) the boiling point of water increases due to high pressure (b) the boiling point of water decreases due to low air pressure (c) there is less oxygen for the fire to burn (d) water does not boil at all at high altitude
Answer: (b)
Solution: Air pressure is lower at high altitude, so water boils below 100 °C and the food does not get enough heat to cook quickly.
Source: PYQ-style

36. An iron nail sinks in water, but a ship made of iron floats because:
(a) iron becomes lighter in a ship (b) sea water has no effect on iron (c) the hollow ship displaces water equal to its own weight (d) a ship has no weight in water
Answer: (c)
Solution: By Archimedes' principle, the hollow shape of the ship displaces a large volume of water whose weight equals the ship's weight, so it floats. A solid nail displaces too little water.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Science

37. It is easier to float in sea water than in river water because:
(a) sea water is denser, so it gives a greater buoyant force (b) sea water is warmer (c) sea water has waves (d) river water is denser
Answer: (a)
Solution: Dissolved salts make sea water denser; the same volume of displaced sea water weighs more, so the upward buoyant force is greater.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Science

38. Raindrops are spherical in shape because of:
(a) viscosity (b) surface tension (c) capillarity (d) gravity
Answer: (b)
Solution: Surface tension makes a liquid surface shrink to the smallest area; for a given volume, a sphere has the least surface area.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Physics

39. Oil rises up the wick of a lamp because of:
(a) surface tension alone (b) viscosity (c) capillary action (d) gravity
Answer: (c)
Solution: The narrow spaces between the threads of the wick act like fine tubes, and oil rises in them by capillary action.
Source: PYQ-style

40. What is 50 °C on the Kelvin scale (take K = °C + 273)?
(a) 223 K (b) 323 K (c) 373 K (d) 50 K
Answer: (b)
Solution: K = °C + 273 = 50 + 273 = 323 K.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Science

41. A sea breeze blows:
(a) during the day, from sea to land (b) during the night, from sea to land (c) during the day, from land to sea (d) during the night, from land to sea
Answer: (a)
Solution: In the day land heats faster than the sea (water has a high specific heat), warm air over land rises and cooler air from the sea moves in. At night the land breeze blows from land to sea — the chronological trap.
Source: NCERT Class 7 Science

42. Water kept in an earthen pot (matka) remains cool because of:
(a) conduction (b) radiation (c) condensation (d) evaporation
Answer: (d)
Solution: Water seeps through the tiny pores of the pot and evaporates from its outer surface; evaporation takes heat from the pot and the water inside, cooling it.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Science

43. A sound wave has a frequency of 500 Hz and a wavelength of 0.68 m. What is its speed?
(a) 340 m/s (b) 735 m/s (c) 680 m/s (d) 170 m/s
Answer: (a)
Solution: Speed = frequency × wavelength = 500 × 0.68 = 340 m/s.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Science

44. The pitch of a sound depends on its:
(a) amplitude (b) loudness (c) frequency (d) intensity
Answer: (c)
Solution: Higher frequency means higher pitch. Amplitude decides loudness — the tempting swap.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Science

45. The sensation of sound persists in our brain for about 0.1 s. If the speed of sound is 344 m/s, what is the minimum distance of a reflecting surface for an echo to be heard?
(a) 34.4 m (b) 17.2 m (c) 3.44 m (d) 1.72 m
Answer: (b)
Solution: In 0.1 s sound travels 344 × 0.1 = 34.4 m, which is the path to the wall and back. So the minimum distance = 34.4 ÷ 2 = 17.2 m.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Science

46. Danger signals and brake lights are red because:
(a) red light has the shortest wavelength (b) red light is scattered the most (c) red is the only colour the eye can see in fog (d) red light is scattered the least by air and fog
Answer: (d)
Solution: Red has the longest wavelength in visible light and is scattered the least, so it travels farthest and can be seen from a distance.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science

47. The twinkling of stars is due to:
(a) atmospheric refraction (b) reflection of light (c) scattering of light (d) total internal reflection
Answer: (a)
Solution: Starlight passes through layers of air whose refractive index keeps changing, so the apparent position and brightness of the star keep changing — twinkling.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science

48. The brilliant sparkle of a diamond is mainly due to:
(a) dispersion only (b) total internal reflection (c) scattering (d) diffraction
Answer: (b)
Solution: Diamond has a high refractive index (about 2.42) and a small critical angle, so light entering it is totally internally reflected many times before coming out.
Source: PYQ-style

49. What is the power of a convex lens of focal length 50 cm?
(a) +0.5 D (b) +2 D (c) +50 D (d) -2 D
Answer: (b)
Solution: Focal length must be in metres: f = 50 cm = 0.5 m. Power P = 1/f = 1/0.5 = +2 D (positive for a convex lens).
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science

50. In household wiring, a fuse is connected:
(a) in parallel with the appliance (b) in the earth wire (c) in series in the live wire (d) in series in the neutral wire
Answer: (c)
Solution: The fuse must be in series in the live wire so that when it melts the appliance is cut off from the live supply. A fuse in the neutral wire would leave the appliance live — an almost-true trap.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Science
