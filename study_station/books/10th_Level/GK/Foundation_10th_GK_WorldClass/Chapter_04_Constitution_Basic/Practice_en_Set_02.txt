📝 Practice MCQs – Set 2 (25 Questions: 26–50)

26. (Medium) Which one of the following correctly describes the term ‘Republic’ as used in the Preamble?
(a) The head of the state is hereditary.
(b) The head of the state is elected, directly or indirectly, for a fixed tenure.
(c) The country has a monarch as its nominal head.
(d) The head of the state holds office for life.

Answer: (b) The head of the state is elected, directly or indirectly, for a fixed tenure.
Solution: A republic means the head of the state (President in India) is elected and not a hereditary monarch. The President is indirectly elected for a term of five years.
Source: SSC CGL 2020

27. (Medium) The Drafting Committee of the Constituent Assembly was chaired by:
(a) Dr. Rajendra Prasad
(b) Jawaharlal Nehru
(c) Dr. B.R. Ambedkar
(d) Sardar Vallabhbhai Patel

Answer: (c) Dr. B.R. Ambedkar
Solution: The Drafting Committee, set up on 29 August 1947, had Dr. B.R. Ambedkar as its Chairman. He played a pivotal role in shaping the final text of the Constitution.
Source: Railway NTPC 2019
Trap type: Similar roles (Dr. Rajendra Prasad was President of the Constituent Assembly, not Drafting Committee)

28. (Medium) Which of the following is not a feature of the Indian Constitution?
(a) Dual citizenship
(b) Single citizenship
(c) Independent judiciary
(d) Universal adult suffrage

Answer: (a) Dual citizenship
Solution: India provides single citizenship to all its citizens, unlike the USA which provides dual citizenship (national and state). Features (b), (c), and (d) are present in India.
Source: Banking PO 2021
Trap type: Negative phrasing (“not”) + almost correct feature (USA’s dual citizenship often confused)

29. (Medium) Which part of the Indian Constitution deals with the amendment procedure?
(a) Part X
(b) Part XI
(c) Part XVIII
(d) Part XX

Answer: (d) Part XX
Solution: Article 368, which provides for the amendment of the Constitution, is contained in Part XX. The amendment procedure requires a special majority in Parliament and, in some cases, ratification by half of the state legislatures.
Source: SSC CHSL 2022

30. (Medium) The “Right to Freedom of Speech and Expression” is enshrined under which Article?
(a) Article 19(1)(a)
(b) Article 21
(c) Article 14
(d) Article 32

Answer: (a) Article 19(1)(a)
Solution: Article 19(1)(a) guarantees the right to freedom of speech and expression to all citizens. However, this right is subject to reasonable restrictions under Article 19(2) on grounds like sovereignty, security, public order, etc.
Source: UPSC 2017

31. (Medium) Which of the following schedules contains the forms of oaths or affirmations for various constitutional functionaries?
(a) Second Schedule
(b) Third Schedule
(c) Fourth Schedule
(d) Fifth Schedule

Answer: (b) Third Schedule
Solution: The Third Schedule provides the forms of oath and affirmation for Union Ministers, Members of Parliament, Judges of the Supreme Court, etc.
Source: SSC CGL 2021

32. (Medium) The doctrine of “severability” or “separability” is associated with:
(a) The Preamble
(b) Fundamental Rights
(c) Directive Principles of State Policy
(d) Emergency provisions

Answer: (b) Fundamental Rights
Solution: Under Article 13, the doctrine of severability allows a court to strike down only that part of a law that violates Fundamental Rights while keeping the rest of the law intact.
Source: UPSC 2016 (adapted)

33. (Medium) The concept of “Rule of Law” in the Indian Constitution has been adopted from:
(a) The USA
(b) Britain
(c) France
(d) Ireland

Answer: (b) Britain
Solution: The Rule of Law is a British concept. It means that no person is above the law, and the same law applies to all, including government officials. This principle is reflected in Article 14 (Equality before law).
Source: SSC CHSL 2020

34. (Medium) Which Amendment Act made the Right to Education a Fundamental Right?
(a) 86th Amendment Act, 2002
(b) 42nd Amendment Act, 1976
(c) 73rd Amendment Act, 1992
(d) 61st Amendment Act, 1989

Answer: (a) 86th Amendment Act, 2002
Solution: The 86th Amendment inserted Article 21A which makes free and compulsory education a Fundamental Right for children aged 6-14 years. It also added a new Fundamental Duty under Article 51A(k).
Source: Railway Group D 2022

35. (Medium) Which one of the following is a correct statement about the Directive Principles of State Policy?
(a) They are enforceable by the courts.
(b) They are fundamental in the governance of the country and the State must apply them in making laws.
(c) They are merely suggestions and not binding on the government.
(d) They can be suspended during emergencies.

Answer: (b) They are fundamental in the governance of the country and the State must apply them in making laws.
Solution: Article 37 states that DPSPs are “fundamental in the governance of the country and it shall be the duty of the State to apply these principles in making laws,” but they are not enforceable by any court. Option (b) correctly reflects this constitutional mandate.
Source: UPSC 2018
Trap type: Almost correct (option (c) seems plausible but undervalues their constitutional importance)

36. (Medium) The power to form new States or alter boundaries of existing States lies with:
(a) The Parliament by a simple majority
(b) The President by executive order
(c) The Supreme Court
(d) The concerned State Legislature

Answer: (a) The Parliament by a simple majority
Solution: Article 3 empowers Parliament to form new States, alter boundaries, or change names of States by passing a law with a simple majority, subject to certain procedural requirements (e.g., President’s recommendation, State Legislature’s views).
Source: SSC CGL 2023

37. (Medium) Which of the following provisions shows the “unitary bias” of the Indian Constitution?
(a) Division of powers between Centre and States
(b) Independent judiciary
(c) Appointment of Governors by the President
(d) Bicameral legislature at the Centre

Answer: (c) Appointment of Governors by the President
Solution: The Governor is appointed by the President and holds office during his pleasure, which gives the Centre substantial control over states. This is a unitary feature in a federal framework. Other options are federal features.
Source: UPSC 2019

38. (Medium) Which Article of the Constitution deals with the appointment of the Attorney General of India?
(a) Article 74
(b) Article 76
(c) Article 143
(d) Article 124

Answer: (b) Article 76
Solution: Article 76 provides for the appointment of the Attorney General of India, the highest law officer of the country. The AG is appointed by the President and holds office during his pleasure.
Source: Banking PO 2022

39. (Medium) The 42nd Amendment Act, 1976 is called the “Mini-Constitution” because:
(a) It was the first amendment to the Constitution
(b) It made the Constitution shorter
(c) It brought extensive changes to the Constitution
(d) It was drafted by a mini committee

Answer: (c) It brought extensive changes to the Constitution
Solution: The 42nd Amendment made sweeping changes—amending the Preamble, adding Fundamental Duties, curtailing judicial review, and strengthening Directive Principles—hence it is called the Mini-Constitution.
Source: SSC CHSL 2021

40. (Medium) What is the significance of the 10th Schedule of the Constitution?
(a) It deals with the official languages
(b) It contains provisions regarding anti-defection
(c) It lists the powers of Panchayats
(d) It deals with land reforms

Answer: (b) It contains provisions regarding anti-defection
Solution: The 10th Schedule (added by the 52nd Amendment Act, 1985) deals with disqualification of members of Parliament and State Legislatures on grounds of defection. It is commonly known as the Anti-Defection Law.
Source: Railway NTPC 2020

41. (Medium) Which of the following is not a part of the basic structure doctrine as identified by the Supreme Court in various judgments?
(a) Secularism
(b) Federal character
(c) Judicial review
(d) Unlimited power of Parliament to amend the Constitution

Answer: (d) Unlimited power of Parliament to amend the Constitution
Solution: The basic structure doctrine limits Parliament’s amending power; thus, unlimited power cannot be part of the basic structure. All other options are elements of the basic structure.
Source: UPSC 2020
Trap type: Negative phrasing (“not”) + reverse causation (opposite of Basic Structure)

42. (Medium) The writ of Habeas Corpus means:
(a) To command a person to perform a public duty
(b) To produce the body of a person illegally detained before a court
(c) To restrain a person from holding a public office
(d) To quash an order of a lower court

Answer: (b) To produce the body of a person illegally detained before a court
Solution: Habeas Corpus literally means “you may have the body.” It is a writ used to secure the release of a person who has been unlawfully detained.
Source: SSC CGL 2019

43. (Medium) Which one of the following statements about the Preamble of the Indian Constitution is incorrect?
(a) It is based on the Objective Resolution moved by Nehru in 1946.
(b) It declares India as a Sovereign, Socialist, Secular, Democratic, Republic.
(c) It is justiciable in a court of law.
(d) It was amended only once in 1976.

Answer: (c) It is justiciable in a court of law.
Solution: The Preamble is not enforceable in a court of law; it is non-justiciable. It serves as a guiding light for interpretation. All other statements are correct.
Source: UPSC 2021
Trap type: Almost correct (non-justiciability is a common misconception)

44. (Medium) Which emergency provision allows the President to take over the administration of a state?
(a) Article 352
(b) Article 356
(c) Article 360
(d) Article 365

Answer: (b) Article 356
Solution: Article 356 provides for the imposition of President’s Rule in a state if the constitutional machinery breaks down. Article 352 deals with National Emergency, Article 360 with Financial Emergency, and Article 365 with failure to comply with Union directions.
Source: Banking PO 2020

45. (Medium) The Constitution of India provides for a parliamentary system of government because:
(a) The President is the real head of the executive
(b) The Council of Ministers is collectively responsible to the Lok Sabha
(c) The Prime Minister is directly elected by the people
(d) The President is the supreme commander of armed forces

Answer: (b) The Council of Ministers is collectively responsible to the Lok Sabha
Solution: The hallmark of a parliamentary system is the collective responsibility of the executive to the legislature. The Council of Ministers (headed by the PM) is collectively responsible to the Lok Sabha under Article 75(3).
Source: SSC CGL 2018

46. (Medium) Which of the following Articles prohibits “traffic in human beings and forced labour”?
(a) Article 23
(b) Article 24
(c) Article 17
(d) Article 15

Answer: (a) Article 23
Solution: Article 23 prohibits traffic in human beings, begar (forced labour), and other similar forms of forced labour. Article 24 prohibits employment of children below 14 in hazardous industries.
Source: UPSC 2017

47. (Medium) The total number of members in the Constituent Assembly after partition was:
(a) 389
(b) 299
(c) 324
(d) 292

Answer: (b) 299
Solution: The Constituent Assembly originally had 389 members, but after partition, the strength was reduced to 299 (229 from British India provinces and 70 from princely states).
Source: SSC CHSL 2022

48. (Medium) Which of the following pairs is correctly matched?
(a) 61st Amendment Act – Reduction of voting age to 18
(b) 42nd Amendment Act – Anti-defection provisions
(c) 73rd Amendment Act – Fundamental Duties
(d) 86th Amendment Act – Introduction of Goods and Services Tax

Answer: (a) 61st Amendment Act – Reduction of voting age to 18
Solution: The 61st Amendment (1989) reduced the voting age from 21 to 18. Anti-defection is under the 52nd Amendment, Fundamental Duties were introduced by the 42nd, and GST by the 101st.
Source: Railway NTPC 2021

49. (Medium) The “Concurrent List” in the Seventh Schedule is borrowed from the Constitution of:
(a) USA
(b) Canada
(c) Australia
(d) South Africa

Answer: (c) Australia
Solution: The idea of a Concurrent List, where both Centre and States can legislate, has been taken from the Australian Constitution.
Source: SSC CGL 2023

50. (Medium) Consider the following statements regarding the writ jurisdiction of the Supreme Court and High Courts:

The Supreme Court can issue writs only for the enforcement of Fundamental Rights.

High Courts can issue writs for the enforcement of Fundamental Rights and for any other purpose.
Which of the statements is/are correct?
(a) 1 only
(b) 2 only
(c) Both 1 and 2
(d) Neither 1 nor 2

Answer: (c) Both 1 and 2
Solution: Under Article 32, the Supreme Court issues writs only for enforcement of Fundamental Rights. Under Article 226, High Courts have wider power to issue writs for enforcement of Fundamental Rights as well as for “any other purpose” (e.g., ordinary legal rights).
Source: UPSC 2018

Examiner’s Traps in Set 2:

Q27: Confusing roles of Dr. Rajendra Prasad and Dr. B.R. Ambedkar (similar names/roles trap).

Q28: Negative phrasing (“not”) with dual citizenship vs single citizenship.

Q35: Almost correct statement—DPSP are more than “mere suggestions” but less than enforceable rights.

Q41: Negative phrasing and reverse causation (unlimited amendment power vs basic structure limitation).

Q43: Almost correct—Preamble is non-justiciable, a classic misconception.

Q50: Statement-based question requiring precise distinction between Article 32 and 226.
