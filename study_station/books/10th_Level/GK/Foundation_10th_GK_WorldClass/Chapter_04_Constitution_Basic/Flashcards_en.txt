🃏 Flashcards: Indian Constitution (Basic)

1.
Front: ✨ Did you know the Indian Constitution wasn’t enforced on the day it was signed? Why do we celebrate Republic Day on 26 January, not 26 November?
Back: The Constitution was adopted on 26 November 1949 (now Constitution Day), but enforced on 26 January 1950 — the anniversary of the 1930 Purna Swaraj Declaration. This honors the freedom pledge.

2.
Front: 🧠 You’re trying to list the key features. What’s the one-word trick that hides 7 features using “BIG FUSE”?
Back: B – Biggest written, I – Integrated single citizenship, G – Guardian of Basic Structure, F – Federal with Unitary Bias, U – Universal Adult Suffrage, S – Strong Parliamentary system, E – Emergency provisions.

3.
Front: 🤔 A friend says, “The Preamble originally started with ‘We, the people of India, having solemnly resolved to constitute India into a Sovereign, Socialist, Secular, Democratic Republic…’” Spot the mistake.
Back: The words “Socialist” and “Secular” were NOT in the original Preamble. They were added by the 42nd Amendment (1976). Originally it was only “Sovereign Democratic Republic”.

4.
Front: 📅 How can you quickly recall the number of schedules using the phrase “TEARS OF OLD PM”?
Back: Each letter corresponds to a schedule: Territory, Emoluments, Affirmations, Rajya Sabha, Scheduled Areas, Other Tribal Areas (now removed), Federal (removed), Official languages, Land reforms, Defection, Panchayats, Municipalities. Currently 12 schedules.

5.
Front: ⚖️ Which article did Dr. Ambedkar call the “heart and soul of the Constitution”?
Back: Article 32 – Right to Constitutional Remedies. It lets citizens directly approach the Supreme Court for enforcement of Fundamental Rights.

6.
Front: 🌍 The Indian Constitution is called a “bag of borrowings.” From which country did we borrow the Directive Principles of State Policy (DPSP)?
Back: Ireland. (And Fundamental Rights from the USA, Parliamentary system from the UK.)

7.
Front: 🔍 “Article 356 imposes President’s Rule in a state.” But what national emergency provision is found in Article 352?
Back: Article 352 deals with National Emergency (on grounds of war, external aggression, or armed rebellion). Don’t confuse the two!

8.
Front: 🏛️ Which landmark case gave birth to the “Basic Structure Doctrine” that limits Parliament’s amending power?
Back: Kesavananda Bharati v. State of Kerala (1973). The Supreme Court ruled that Parliament cannot destroy the Constitution’s basic structure.

9.
Front: 🧨 EXAMINER TRAP CARD: “The Constitution of India was enforced on 26 November 1949.” True or false? What’s the trap?
Back: False. 26 Nov is the adoption date; enforcement was 26 Jan 1950. Trap type: Chronological Confusion. The statement is “almost correct” with a tiny date error.

10.
Front: 🧨 EXAMINER TRAP CARD: “Which of the following is NOT a Fundamental Right? (a) Right to Equality (b) Right to Work (c) Right to Religion (d) Right to Constitutional Remedies.”
Back: Right to Work (Art. 41) is a Directive Principle, not a Fundamental Right. Trap type: Negative phrasing plus mixing FRs with DPSP. Always check for “not”/“except”.

11.
Front: 📚 How many articles were there in the original Constitution (1950), and how many schedules?
Back: 395 articles, 22 parts, and 8 schedules. Today it has grown to about 470 articles, 25 parts, and 12 schedules.

12.
Front: 👀 According to the Preamble, India is a “Union of States” – under which Article is this declared?
Back: Article 1 – “India, that is Bharat, shall be a Union of States.” It emphasizes the indestructible nature of the Union.

13.
Front: 🎤 The Constituent Assembly’s Drafting Committee was chaired by Dr. B.R. Ambedkar. But who was the permanent President of the Assembly?
Back: Dr. Rajendra Prasad. He was elected after Dr. Sachchidananda Sinha served as temporary chairman.

14.
Front: 🧮 How many Fundamental Duties do we have today, and via which amendment were they added?
Back: 11 Fundamental Duties under Article 51A, added by the 42nd Amendment (1976) (and one more by the 86th Amendment in 2002).

15.
Front: 💡 Why does the mnemonic “SO-SE-DE-RE” help you remember the Preamble’s key words in order?
Back: It stands for Sovereign, Socialist, Secular, Democratic, Republic — the exact sequence in the Preamble. Just think “So-Se-De-Re” as a single chant.

16.
Front: 🇮🇳 India’s citizenship is often called “single citizenship.” Which country’s Constitution inspired this feature?
Back: Britain (UK). Unlike the USA, we have only one citizenship for the whole nation, not separate state citizenships.

17.
Front: 🧱 The Constitution is both rigid and flexible. Which article tells us the procedure for amendment?
Back: Article 368. It lays out the methods — simple majority, special majority, or special majority plus ratification by states, depending on the provision.

18.
Front: 🗓️ How long did it take to draft the Constitution from the first meeting of the Assembly?
Back: 2 years, 11 months, and 18 days. The first meeting was on 9 Dec 1946, and the final adoption on 26 Nov 1949.

Blurting tip: Cover the right side, read the question, and blurt the answer aloud. Then check. Cards 2,4,9,10,15 are must-do for mnemonics and traps.
