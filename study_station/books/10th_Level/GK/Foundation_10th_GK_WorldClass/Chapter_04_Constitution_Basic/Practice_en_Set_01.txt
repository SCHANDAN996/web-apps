📝 Practice MCQs – Set 1 (25 Questions)

1. The Indian Constitution was adopted by the Constituent Assembly on:
(a) 26 January 1950
(b) 15 August 1947
(c) 26 November 1949
(d) 9 December 1946

Answer: (c) 26 November 1949
Solution: The Constituent Assembly adopted the Constitution on 26 November 1949, now celebrated as Constitution Day. It came into force on 26 January 1950.
Source: PYQ-style
Trap type: Chronological confusion (enforcement date vs adoption date)

2. The idea of a Constituent Assembly for India was first put forward by:
(a) Mahatma Gandhi
(b) M.N. Roy
(c) Jawaharlal Nehru
(d) B.R. Ambedkar

Answer: (b) M.N. Roy
Solution: In 1934, M.N. Roy first proposed the idea of an Indian Constituent Assembly. The actual assembly was formed under the Cabinet Mission Plan, 1946.
Source: PYQ-style

3. Which Article of the Indian Constitution is called the “heart and soul” by Dr. B.R. Ambedkar?
(a) Article 19
(b) Article 21
(c) Article 32
(d) Article 226

Answer: (c) Article 32
Solution: Article 32 provides the right to constitutional remedies and is considered the most important fundamental right. Ambedkar called it the “heart and soul” because it guarantees enforcement of all other rights.
Source: PYQ-style

4. The words “Socialist” and “Secular” were added to the Preamble by which amendment?
(a) 24th Amendment
(b) 42nd Amendment
(c) 44th Amendment
(d) 86th Amendment

Answer: (b) 42nd Amendment (1976)
Solution: The 42nd Amendment Act, 1976, also known as the “Mini-Constitution”, inserted the words ‘Socialist’, ‘Secular’, and ‘Integrity’ into the Preamble.
Source: PYQ-style
Trap type: Similar numbers (44th Amendment also a major amendment, often confused)

5. The original Constitution of India consisted of how many Articles and Schedules?
(a) 395 Articles, 8 Schedules
(b) 448 Articles, 12 Schedules
(c) 395 Articles, 12 Schedules
(d) 448 Articles, 8 Schedules

Answer: (a) 395 Articles, 8 Schedules
Solution: As adopted in 1949, the Constitution had 395 Articles, 22 Parts, and 8 Schedules. Over time, through amendments, the numbers have increased to around 470 Articles and 12 Schedules.
Source: PYQ-style

6. Which country’s Constitution provided the model for the Directive Principles of State Policy?
(a) United Kingdom
(b) United States of America
(c) Ireland
(d) Canada

Answer: (c) Ireland
Solution: The Directive Principles of State Policy (DPSP) in Part IV of the Indian Constitution are borrowed from the Irish Constitution.
Source: PYQ-style

7. Fundamental Duties are enshrined in which part of the Indian Constitution?
(a) Part III
(b) Part IV
(c) Part IV-A
(d) Part V

Answer: (c) Part IV-A
Solution: Fundamental Duties were added by the 42nd Amendment, 1976, under Article 51A in a new Part IV-A. There are currently 11 duties.
Source: Banking PO 2021

8. The Basic Structure Doctrine was laid down in which landmark case?
(a) Golaknath v. State of Punjab
(b) Kesavananda Bharati v. State of Kerala
(c) Minerva Mills v. Union of India
(d) Maneka Gandhi v. Union of India

Answer: (b) Kesavananda Bharati v. State of Kerala (1973)
Solution: In this case, the Supreme Court ruled that Parliament cannot alter the basic structure of the Constitution, thereby limiting the amending power under Article 368.
Source: PYQ-style

9. Which of the following is NOT a Fundamental Right under the Indian Constitution?
(a) Right to Equality
(b) Right to Property
(c) Right to Freedom of Religion
(d) Right against Exploitation

Answer: (b) Right to Property
Solution: The Right to Property was a Fundamental Right under Article 31 originally, but the 44th Amendment Act, 1978 removed it from Part III, making it a legal right under Article 300A.
Source: PYQ-style
Trap type: Negative phrasing (“NOT”) + almost correct (Property was a FR historically)

10. Who presided over the first meeting of the Constituent Assembly as its temporary Chairman?
(a) Dr. Rajendra Prasad
(b) Dr. Sachchidananda Sinha
(c) Pt. Jawaharlal Nehru
(d) Dr. B.R. Ambedkar

Answer: (b) Dr. Sachchidananda Sinha
Solution: The first meeting on 9 December 1946 was chaired by Dr. Sachchidananda Sinha as the provisional President. Dr. Rajendra Prasad became the permanent President later.
Source: PYQ-style
Trap type: Similar names/roles (Dr. Rajendra Prasad was permanent President, not temporary)

11. The Constitution of India describes India as a:
(a) Federation of States
(b) Union of States
(c) Confederation of States
(d) Association of States

Answer: (b) Union of States
Solution: Article 1 declares “India, that is Bharat, shall be a Union of States.” The term ‘Union’ implies that the federation is not the result of an agreement among states and the states cannot secede.
Source: PYQ-style

12. The total number of Fundamental Duties in the Indian Constitution is:
(a) 10
(b) 11
(c) 12
(d) 9

Answer: (b) 11
Solution: Originally 10 duties were added by the 42nd Amendment in 1976. One more duty (concerning the right to education of children) was added by the 86th Amendment Act, 2002, making the total 11.
Source: PYQ-style

13. The Indian federal system is described as ‘quasi-federal’ because:
(a) It has a weak centre
(b) States have power to secede
(c) It is unitary in spirit with federal features
(d) The judiciary is not independent

Answer: (c) It is unitary in spirit with federal features
Solution: The Indian Constitution provides a strong central government and single citizenship, yet has federal features like division of powers. Hence it is called ‘quasi-federal’ or ‘federal with a unitary bias’.
Source: PYQ-style

14. The Constituent Assembly took how long to draft the Constitution?
(a) 2 years, 11 months, 18 days
(b) 3 years, 11 months, 2 days
(c) 2 years, 5 months, 5 days
(d) 3 years

Answer: (a) 2 years, 11 months, 18 days
Solution: The Constituent Assembly first met on 9 December 1946 and adopted the Constitution on 26 November 1949, taking exactly 2 years, 11 months, and 18 days.
Source: PYQ-style

15. Which of the following schedules deals with the allocation of seats in the Rajya Sabha?
(a) First Schedule
(b) Second Schedule
(c) Third Schedule
(d) Fourth Schedule

Answer: (d) Fourth Schedule
Solution: The Fourth Schedule deals with the allocation of seats in the Council of States (Rajya Sabha) to each State and Union Territory.
Source: Banking PO 2020

16. The 42nd Amendment to the Constitution is also known as:
(a) The Bill of Rights Amendment
(b) The Mini-Constitution
(c) The Federal Amendment
(d) The Anti-Defection Amendment

Answer: (b) The Mini-Constitution
Solution: The 42nd Amendment, 1976, brought extensive changes, adding the words Socialist, Secular, and Integrity to the Preamble, Fundamental Duties, and curtailed judicial review in some aspects. Hence it is called the Mini-Constitution.
Source: PYQ-style

17. Which Fundamental Right is available to both citizens and non-citizens?
(a) Right to equality of opportunity in public employment (Article 16)
(b) Right to six freedoms (Article 19)
(c) Protection of life and personal liberty (Article 21)
(d) Cultural and educational rights (Articles 29-30)

Answer: (c) Protection of life and personal liberty (Article 21)
Solution: Article 21 is available to all persons, citizens and non-citizens. Articles 15, 16, 19, 29, 30 are available only to citizens.
Source: PYQ-style

18. The system of Public Interest Litigation (PIL) emerged from:
(a) The original text of the Constitution
(b) Judicial interpretation of Article 32
(c) A Parliamentary Act
(d) A Presidential Order

Answer: (b) Judicial interpretation of Article 32
Solution: The Supreme Court, through a liberal interpretation of Article 32, allowed any public-spirited individual to file a writ for enforcement of fundamental rights even if the aggrieved person is unable to, leading to the PIL mechanism.
Source: PYQ-style

19. Which Amendment removed the Right to Property from the list of Fundamental Rights?
(a) 24th Amendment
(b) 42nd Amendment
(c) 44th Amendment
(d) 86th Amendment

Answer: (c) 44th Amendment (1978)
Solution: The 44th Amendment Act deleted Article 31 from Part III and inserted Article 300A in Part XII, making the right to property an ordinary legal right.
Source: PYQ-style

20. In the Preamble, the word “Sovereign” implies that India is:
(a) A monarchy
(b) Dependent on external authority
(c) An independent state, internally and externally
(d) A protectorate of a larger power

Answer: (c) An independent state, internally and externally
Solution: Sovereign means that India is no longer under the control of any foreign power and has the supreme power to make its own decisions.
Source: Banking PO 2019

21. (Medium) Consider the following statements about the Indian Constitution:

It is the longest written constitution in the world.

It is completely federal in nature.

It provides for single citizenship.
Which of the statements given above is/are correct?
(a) 1 and 2 only
(b) 1 and 3 only
(c) 2 and 3 only
(d) 1, 2 and 3

Answer: (b) 1 and 3 only
Solution: Statement 2 is incorrect because the Indian Constitution is not purely federal; it is ‘quasi-federal’ or ‘federal with a unitary bias’. Statements 1 and 3 are correct.
Source: PYQ-style
Trap type: Almost correct (statement 2 seems plausible but is inaccurate)

22. (Medium) Match the following features with the countries from which they are borrowed:
List I (Feature)
A. Parliamentary system
B. Fundamental Rights
C. Directive Principles of State Policy
D. Concurrent List
List II (Country)

USA

Ireland

UK

Australia
Select the correct answer using the codes below:
(a) A-3, B-1, C-2, D-4
(b) A-4, B-3, C-1, D-2
(c) A-2, B-4, C-3, D-1
(d) A-1, B-2, C-4, D-3

Answer: (a) A-3, B-1, C-2, D-4
Solution: Parliamentary system → UK; Fundamental Rights → USA; DPSP → Ireland; Concurrent List → Australia. This is a standard match.

23. (Medium) Which of the following statements is/are correct regarding the Preamble of the Indian Constitution?

The Preamble is enforceable in a court of law.

The Preamble declares the source of the Constitution to be the people of India.

The Preamble was amended for the first time by the 42nd Amendment.
Select the correct answer:
(a) 1 and 2 only
(b) 2 and 3 only
(c) 1 and 3 only
(d) 1, 2 and 3

Answer: (b) 2 and 3 only
Solution: The Preamble is not enforceable by courts (it is non-justiciable). It declares that the Constitution derives its authority from the people of India. It was amended only once, by the 42nd Amendment, 1976. Thus, statements 2 and 3 are correct.
Source: PYQ-style
Trap type: Almost correct (statement 1 is a common misconception)

24. (Medium) The 44th Amendment (1978) made which of the following changes?
(a) Added Fundamental Duties
(b) Removed Right to Property from Fundamental Rights
(c) Added Socialist and Secular to the Preamble
(d) Reduced the voting age to 18

Answer: (b) Removed Right to Property from Fundamental Rights
Solution: The 44th Amendment removed the Right to Property as a Fundamental Right and made it a legal right. Option (a) was done by 42nd Amendment, (c) by 42nd, (d) by 61st Amendment, 1989.
Source: PYQ-style

25. (Medium) Assertion (A): The Indian Constitution is called a ‘living document’.
Reason (R): It can be amended according to the changing needs of society.
Choose the correct option:
(a) Both A and R are true and R is the correct explanation of A.
(b) Both A and R are true but R is not the correct explanation of A.
(c) A is true, but R is false.
(d) A is false, but R is true.

Answer: (a) Both A and R are true and R is the correct explanation of A.
Solution: A ‘living document’ means it is dynamic and adaptable; the amendment procedure (Article 368) allows the Constitution to evolve with society. Therefore, R explains A correctly.
Source: PYQ-style

Examiner’s Traps in this Set:

Q9 uses negative phrasing (“NOT”) and historical change (Right to Property removed) to trick.

Q10 confuses temporary and permanent chairman of the Constituent Assembly, a case of similar names/roles.

Q21 presents an almost correct statement (federal nature) that is not entirely true.

Q23 mixes true statements with one non-justiciable misconception.

Q24 requires precise recall of which amendment did what, easily confused among major amendments.
