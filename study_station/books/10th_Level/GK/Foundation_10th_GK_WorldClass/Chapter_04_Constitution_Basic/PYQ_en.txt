# Previous Years' Question (PYQ) Pattern Analysis: Indian Constitution (Basic)

## 1. Exam Pattern & Topic-Wise Trends (In Words)

In 10th-level and secondary competitive examinations (such as SSC MTS, SSC CHSL, Railway NTPC, Railway Group D, State Police, and other matriculation-level recruitment exams), the Indian Constitution is consistently among the highest-yielding areas of General Knowledge. Rather than deep legal philosophy, examiners prioritize factual accuracy, core dates, major articles, and landmark constitutional provisions.

Analysis of previous years' papers reveals five consistently tested thematic pillars:

1. **Making and Adoption of the Constitution:**
   Questions frequently test specific historical milestones: the role of the Cabinet Mission Plan (1946), the first meeting of the Constituent Assembly (9 December 1946), the temporary President (Dr. Sachchidananda Sinha) versus the permanent President (Dr. Rajendra Prasad), the Chairman of the Drafting Committee (Dr. B. R. Ambedkar), and the total drafting duration (2 years, 11 months, 18 days). A perennial favorite is testing whether a candidate knows the difference between the date of adoption (26 November 1949) and the date of enforcement (26 January 1950).

2. **The Preamble and Constitutional Philosophy:**
   Examiners frequently test the precise sequence of terms in the Preamble: "Sovereign, Socialist, Secular, Democratic, Republic." Another common question asks which three words were added by the 42nd Constitutional Amendment Act of 1976 ("Socialist", "Secular", and "Integrity").

3. **Fundamental Rights (Part III, Articles 12–35):**
   Questions focus on key fundamental rights: Article 14 (Equality before Law), Article 17 (Abolition of Untouchability), Article 21 (Protection of Life and Personal Liberty), Article 21A (Right to Education added by the 86th Amendment 2002), and Article 32 (Right to Constitutional Remedies, famously called the "heart and soul of the Constitution" by Dr. Ambedkar). Candidates are often asked to identify which writ applies to unlawful detention (Habeas Corpus) or non-performance of public duty (Mandamus).

4. **Directive Principles of State Policy and Fundamental Duties:**
   Questions regularly test the distinction between justiciable Fundamental Rights (Part III) and non-justiciable DPSPs (Part IV). Common high-yield articles include Article 40 (Village Panchayats), Article 44 (Uniform Civil Code), and Article 50 (Separation of Judiciary from Executive). For Fundamental Duties (Part IV-A, Article 51A), examiners consistently test the recommending committee (Swaran Singh Committee), the enabling amendment (42nd Amendment 1976), the initial number of duties (10), and the addition of the 11th duty (86th Amendment 2002).

5. **Borrowed Features and Schedules:**
   A substantial portion of questions asks candidates to match constitutional features with their source countries: DPSP from Ireland, Fundamental Rights and Judicial Review from the USA, Parliamentary Government and Rule of Law from the UK, and Concurrent List from Australia. Among the 12 Schedules, the 7th (Union, State, Concurrent Lists), 8th (22 recognized languages), 10th (Anti-Defection Law), and 11th (Panchayats) appear most frequently.

## 2. Common Examiner Traps & How to Overcome Them

Examiners at this level rely on predictable patterns to test attention to detail:

1. **The Adoption vs. Enforcement Trap:**
   Examiners present questions asking when the Constitution "came into force" or was "enacted/adopted." Many candidates rush and pick 26 November 1949 instead of 26 January 1950.
   *Strategy:* Circle the verb—"adopted" means 26 November 1949; "enforced" or "came into force" means 26 January 1950.

2. **The 42nd vs. 44th Amendment Trap:**
   The 42nd Amendment (1976) and 44th Amendment (1978) are the two most frequently tested amendments. Candidates often confuse which amendment added Fundamental Duties and words to the Preamble (42nd) with which amendment removed the Right to Property from the list of Fundamental Rights (44th).
   *Strategy:* Associate the 42nd with additions (Duties, Socialist, Secular) and the 44th with restoration/removal (Right to Property removed to Article 300A).

3. **The Temporary vs. Permanent President Trap:**
   When asked for the President of the Constituent Assembly, candidates often pick Dr. Rajendra Prasad without checking whether the question specifies the "first/temporary" President (Dr. Sachchidananda Sinha on 9 December 1946).
   *Strategy:* Check whether the question asks for the temporary chairman of the inaugural meeting or the permanent president.

4. **The Justiciability Confusion:**
   Examiners often list three Fundamental Rights and one Directive Principle (e.g., Right to Work, Article 41), asking which right cannot be directly enforced in a court of law.
   *Strategy:* Memorize that Part III rights are enforceable under Article 32, whereas Part IV principles are fundamental in the governance of the country but non-justiciable.

## 3. High-Yield Model Practice Questions

1. On which date was the Indian Constitution formally adopted by the Constituent Assembly?
(a) 26 January 1950
(b) 15 August 1947
(c) 26 November 1949
(d) 9 December 1946
Answer: (c) 26 November 1949
Solution: The Constituent Assembly adopted the Constitution on 26 November 1949. It came into full effect two months later on 26 January 1950.
Source: PYQ-style
Trap Type: Chronological Confusion (swapping adoption date with commencement date).

2. The words "Socialist" and "Secular" were added to the Preamble of the Constitution by which Constitutional Amendment?
(a) 24th Amendment Act
(b) 42nd Amendment Act
(c) 44th Amendment Act
(d) 86th Amendment Act
Answer: (b) 42nd Amendment Act
Solution: The 42nd Constitutional Amendment Act of 1976 added three words to the Preamble: "Socialist", "Secular", and "Integrity".
Source: PYQ-style
Trap Type: Numerical Confusion (confusing 42nd and 44th Amendments).

3. Who among the following served as the temporary President of the Constituent Assembly during its inaugural meeting?
(a) Dr. Rajendra Prasad
(b) Dr. B. R. Ambedkar
(c) Dr. Sachchidananda Sinha
(d) B. N. Rau
Answer: (c) Dr. Sachchidananda Sinha
Solution: As the oldest member, Dr. Sachchidananda Sinha was elected temporary President on 9 December 1946 following French practice. Dr. Rajendra Prasad was elected permanent President on 11 December 1946.
Source: PYQ-style
Trap Type: Similar Roles (temporary versus permanent President).

4. Which Article of the Indian Constitution was termed the "heart and soul of the Constitution" by Dr. B. R. Ambedkar?
(a) Article 14
(b) Article 19
(c) Article 21
(d) Article 32
Answer: (d) Article 32
Solution: Article 32 guarantees the Right to Constitutional Remedies, empowering citizens to approach the Supreme Court directly for the enforcement of fundamental rights.
Source: PYQ-style

5. The Directive Principles of State Policy (DPSP) contained in Part IV of the Indian Constitution were borrowed from which country?
(a) United States of America
(b) Ireland
(c) United Kingdom
(d) Canada
Answer: (b) Ireland
Solution: The concept of Directive Principles of State Policy was borrowed from the Irish Constitution (which had copied them from the Spanish Constitution).
Source: PYQ-style

6. Under which Constitutional Amendment Act was the Right to Education added as a Fundamental Right under Article 21A?
(a) 73rd Amendment Act, 1992
(b) 44th Amendment Act, 1978
(c) 86th Amendment Act, 2002
(d) 91st Amendment Act, 2003
Answer: (c) 86th Amendment Act, 2002
Solution: The 86th Amendment Act of 2002 inserted Article 21A, providing free and compulsory education for all children between the ages of 6 and 14 years.
Source: PYQ-style

7. Which of the following writs literally means "to have the body of"?
(a) Mandamus
(b) Quo-Warranto
(c) Certiorari
(d) Habeas Corpus
Answer: (d) Habeas Corpus
Solution: "Habeas Corpus" is a Latin term meaning "to have the body of." It is issued by courts against unlawful detention of a person.
Source: PYQ-style

8. The Tenth Schedule was added to the Constitution of India by the 52nd Amendment Act (1985) to deal with:
(a) Allocation of seats in Rajya Sabha
(b) Official languages of India
(c) Disqualification on ground of defection
(d) Administration of Scheduled Areas
Answer: (c) Disqualification on ground of defection
Solution: The 10th Schedule contains the Anti-Defection Law, added in 1985 to curb political defections.
Source: PYQ-style

9. Fundamental Duties were incorporated into the Indian Constitution following the recommendations of which committee?
(a) Sarkaria Commission
(b) Swaran Singh Committee
(c) Verma Committee
(d) Balwant Rai Mehta Committee
Answer: (b) Swaran Singh Committee
Solution: The Swaran Singh Committee (1976) recommended the inclusion of Fundamental Duties, leading to the enactment of the 42nd Amendment Act.
Source: PYQ-style

10. Which part of the Indian Constitution is non-justiciable, meaning its provisions cannot be enforced through a court of law?
(a) Part III (Fundamental Rights)
(b) Part IV (Directive Principles of State Policy)
(c) Part II (Citizenship)
(d) Part I (The Union and its Territory)
Answer: (b) Part IV (Directive Principles of State Policy)
Solution: Article 37 explicitly declares that the provisions of Part IV shall not be enforceable by any court, but are nevertheless fundamental in the governance of the country.
Source: PYQ-style
Trap Type: Justiciability Confusion (confusing Part III and Part IV enforceability).
