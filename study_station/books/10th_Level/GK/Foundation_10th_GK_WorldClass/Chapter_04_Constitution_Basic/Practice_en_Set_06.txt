📝 Practice MCQs – Set 6 (25 Questions: 126–150)

126. (Hard) Consider the following statements regarding the Preamble of the Indian Constitution:

It is a source of power for the legislature.

It is non-justiciable in a court of law.

It was amended by the 44th Amendment Act, 1978.

It declares the date of adoption of the Constitution.
Which of the statements given above is/are correct?
(a) 2 only
(b) 2 and 4 only
(c) 1 and 2 only
(d) 2, 3 and 4 only

Answer: (b) 2 and 4 only
Solution: Statement 1 is incorrect—the Preamble is not a source of legislative power (it is a key to interpretation). Statement 3 is incorrect—the Preamble was amended by the 42nd Amendment (1976), not the 44th. Statement 2 is correct—it is non-justiciable. Statement 4 is correct—it ends with “…this twenty-sixth day of November, 1949, do hereby adopt, enact and give to ourselves this Constitution.”
Source: PYQ-style
Trap type: Almost correct (confusing 42nd and 44th Amendments)

127. (Hard) The Supreme Court in the Minerva Mills case (1980) struck down which of the following provisions?

Clause (4) of Article 368

Clause (5) of Article 368

Section 4 of the 42nd Amendment Act

Article 31C as it stood after the 42nd Amendment
Select the correct answer:
(a) 1 and 2 only
(b) 1, 2 and 3 only
(c) 1, 2 and 4 only
(d) All of the above

Answer: (c) 1, 2 and 4 only
Solution: In Minerva Mills, the SC struck down clauses (4) and (5) of Article 368 (which barred judicial review of amendments and gave unlimited amending power) and the expanded version of Article 31C (which gave primacy to all DPSPs over FRs). The original Article 31C (only for Articles 39(b) and (c)) was upheld. Statement 3 is not precise; the clauses were part of the 42nd Amendment but the court didn't strike down the entire Section 4.
Source: PYQ-style

128. (Hard) Which of the following writs can be issued only against a public authority and not against a private individual?
(a) Habeas Corpus
(b) Mandamus
(c) Quo Warranto
(d) Certiorari

Answer: (b) Mandamus
Solution: Mandamus is a command to a public official to perform a legal duty; it cannot be issued against a private individual. Habeas corpus can be issued against both public and private individuals, quo warranto against a person holding a public office, certiorari against judicial/quasi-judicial bodies.
Source: PYQ-style

129. (Hard) Consider the following statements regarding fundamental rights and their suspension during a National Emergency (Article 352):

Article 19 is automatically suspended under Article 358.

Article 20 and 21 remain enforceable even during an emergency under Article 359.

The 44th Amendment made Article 20 and 21 non-suspendable even under Article 359.
Which of the statements is/are correct?
(a) 1 and 2 only
(b) 2 and 3 only
(c) 1 and 3 only
(d) 1, 2 and 3

Answer: (c) 1 and 3 only
Solution: Article 358 automatically suspends Article 19. Article 359 empowers the President to suspend the right to move courts for enforcement of specified FRs. The 44th Amendment amended Article 359 to ensure that Articles 20 and 21 cannot be suspended even during an emergency. Thus, statement 2 is incorrect because prior to the 44th Amendment, they could be suspended.
Source: PYQ-style

130. (Hard) The concept of “equality before law” as provided in Article 14 is taken from which country?
(a) USA
(b) Britain
(c) France
(d) Ireland

Answer: (b) Britain
Solution: “Equality before law” is a British concept, while “equal protection of laws” is taken from the US Constitution. Article 14 combines both. France gave the ideals of liberty, equality, and fraternity; Ireland gave DPSP.
Source: PYQ-style

131. (Hard) Which of the following is not a constitutional amendment that required ratification by at least half of the state legislatures?
(a) Election of the President (Article 54 and 55)
(b) Representation of states in Parliament (Articles 80-81)
(c) Fundamental Rights (Part III)
(d) Seventh Schedule (distribution of legislative powers)

Answer: (c) Fundamental Rights (Part III)
Solution: Under Article 368, amendments to the federal provisions listed in the proviso require ratification by half the states. These include the election of the President, extent of executive power, Supreme Court/High Courts, representation of states in Parliament, Seventh Schedule, and Article 368 itself. Fundamental Rights can be amended by special majority alone (subject to basic structure). So (c) is not in the ratification list.
Source: PYQ-style
Trap type: Negative phrasing (“not”) + mixing amendment requirements

132. (Hard) The right to form cooperative societies was made a Fundamental Right by which Constitutional Amendment?
(a) 86th Amendment
(b) 97th Amendment
(c) 73rd Amendment
(d) 74th Amendment

Answer: (b) 97th Amendment
Solution: The 97th Amendment Act, 2011, inserted Article 19(1)(c) (right to form cooperative societies) and added Article 43B in DPSP. The 86th Amendment deals with Right to Education, 73rd with Panchayats, 74th with Municipalities.

133. (Hard) Which of the following schedules of the Indian Constitution is correctly matched with its subject?
(a) Second Schedule – Forms of oath and affirmation
(b) Third Schedule – Emoluments of constitutional functionaries
(c) Eighth Schedule – Official languages
(d) Tenth Schedule – Distribution of powers between Union and States

Answer: (c) Eighth Schedule – Official languages
Solution: The Second Schedule deals with emoluments, Third with oaths, Eighth with official languages, Tenth with anti-defection. The distribution of powers is in the Seventh Schedule.

134. (Hard) Consider the following statements regarding the Directive Principles of State Policy:

They are enforceable in courts.

They are borrowed from the Irish Constitution.

Article 44 deals with a Uniform Civil Code.

The 42nd Amendment added Article 43A (workers’ participation in management).
Which of the statements given above are correct?
(a) 2 and 3 only
(b) 2, 3 and 4
(c) 1, 2 and 3
(d) 1 and 4

Answer: (b) 2, 3 and 4
Solution: Statement 1 is incorrect because DPSPs are non-justiciable (Article 37). Statement 2 is correct—DPSPs are borrowed from Ireland. Statement 3 is correct—Article 44 directs the state to secure a Uniform Civil Code. Statement 4 is correct—Article 43A was added by the 42nd Amendment (1976).
Source: PYQ-style

135. (Hard) Which of the following Articles of the Indian Constitution deals with the “protection of monuments and places and objects of national importance”?
(a) Article 29
(b) Article 49
(c) Article 51
(d) Article 51A

Answer: (b) Article 49
Solution: Article 49 (DPSP) directs the State to protect monuments and places of national importance. Article 29 protects cultural rights, Article 51 promotes international peace, Article 51A is Fundamental Duties.
Source: PYQ-style

136. (Hard) The power of the Supreme Court to review its own judgments is derived from:
(a) Article 137
(b) Article 141
(c) Article 143
(d) Article 136

Answer: (a) Article 137
Solution: Article 137 empowers the Supreme Court to review its own judgments or orders, subject to rules made by the court. Article 141 deals with law declared by SC to be binding; Article 143 advisory jurisdiction; Article 136 special leave to appeal.
Source: Banking PO 2021 (Hard-level adaptation)

137. (Hard) Which of the following is not a correct statement regarding the Attorney General of India?
(a) He is appointed by the President.
(b) He has the right to speak and take part in proceedings of both Houses of Parliament.
(c) He can vote in Parliament.
(d) He holds office during the pleasure of the President.

Answer: (c) He can vote in Parliament.
Solution: The Attorney General (Article 76) has the right to speak in and take part in parliamentary proceedings but does not have the right to vote. All other statements are correct.

138. (Hard) The “Doctrine of Laches” means:
(a) Delay in filing a writ petition may be fatal to the claim
(b) A law inconsistent with FR is void ab initio
(c) Parliament can amend any part of the Constitution
(d) The President can exercise discretion in certain cases

Answer: (a) Delay in filing a writ petition may be fatal to the claim
Solution: The doctrine of laches provides that if there is an unreasonable delay in seeking a writ remedy, the court may refuse to grant the relief, though Fundamental Rights are not subject to statutory limitation. The doctrine is based on equity.
Source: PYQ-style

139. (Hard) Under which of the following Articles can the Governor of a state reserve a bill for the consideration of the President?
(a) Article 200
(b) Article 201
(c) Article 213
(d) Article 254

Answer: (a) Article 200
Solution: Article 200 provides the Governor with the option to either give assent, withhold assent, or reserve the bill for the President’s consideration. Article 201 deals with bills reserved for consideration.
Source: PYQ-style

140. (Hard) Which of the following amendments to the Indian Constitution added the Ninth Schedule?
(a) First Amendment, 1951
(b) Fourth Amendment, 1955
(c) Seventeenth Amendment, 1964
(d) Twenty-fourth Amendment, 1971

Answer: (a) First Amendment, 1951
Solution: The Ninth Schedule was introduced by the First Amendment (1951) to protect land reform laws from judicial review. The Fourth Amendment extended this protection.

141. (Hard) The “Right to Privacy” was declared a Fundamental Right by the Supreme Court in which case?
(a) Maneka Gandhi v. Union of India
(b) K.S. Puttaswamy v. Union of India
(c) S.R. Bommai v. Union of India
(d) I.R. Coelho v. State of Tamil Nadu

Answer: (b) K.S. Puttaswamy v. Union of India
Solution: In the landmark K.S. Puttaswamy judgment (2017), a nine-judge bench unanimously declared that the Right to Privacy is a Fundamental Right under Article 21 and part of the basic structure. Maneka Gandhi expanded Article 21 interpretation, but did not specifically declare privacy.
Source: PYQ-style

142. (Hard) Consider the following regarding the 73rd Constitutional Amendment, 1992:

It added Part IX to the Constitution.

It added the Eleventh Schedule.

Which of the statements given above are correct?
(a) 1, 2 and 3 only
(b) 1, 2 and 4 only
(c) 2, 3 and 4 only
(d) 1, 2, 3 and 4

Answer: (d) 1, 2, 3 and 4

143. (Hard) Which one of the following Articles of the Indian Constitution empowers the Supreme Court to transfer cases from one High Court to another?
(a) Article 139A
(b) Article 140
(c) Article 141
(d) Article 142

Answer: (a) Article 139A
Solution: Article 139A allows the Supreme Court to transfer cases, appeals, or other proceedings pending before one High Court to another, or from a High Court to itself, if cases involve the same or substantially the same questions of law.
Source: Banking PO 2020 (Hard-level adaptation)

145. (Hard) Which of the following amendments imposed restrictions on the Fundamental Right to property before its deletion?
(a) 1st and 4th
(b) 1st and 7th
(c) 4th and 7th
(d) 1st, 4th, and 17th

Answer: (d) 1st, 4th, and 17th
Solution: The 1st Amendment (1951) added Articles 31A and 31B; the 4th (1955) restricted compensation; the 17th (1964) further protected land reform laws from judicial review. All dealt with property rights before it was deleted as a FR by the 44th Amendment.

146. (Hard) The Writ of Quo Warranto is issued to:
(a) Enforce a public duty
(b) Restrain a person from holding a public office to which he is not entitled
(c) Quash an illegal order
(d) Produce a detained person

Answer: (b) Restrain a person from holding a public office to which he is not entitled
Solution: Quo Warranto (“by what authority”) challenges the legality of a person’s claim to a public office and seeks to oust him if found unlawful.
Source: PYQ-style

147. (Hard) Which of the following is not an element of the basic structure doctrine as identified by the Supreme Court in various decisions?
(a) Judicial review
(b) Parliamentary supremacy in all matters
(c) Secularism
(d) Federalism

Answer: (b) Parliamentary supremacy in all matters
Solution: The basic structure limits Parliamentary power; thus absolute parliamentary supremacy is contrary to the doctrine. Judicial review, secularism, federalism are part of the basic structure.
Source: PYQ-style
Trap type: Negative phrasing + reverse causation

148. (Hard) Assertion (A): The Constitution of India is quasi-federal.
Reason (R): It provides for division of powers between the Centre and States and an independent judiciary.
Select the correct answer:
(a) Both A and R are true and R is the correct explanation.
(b) Both A and R are true but R is not the correct explanation.
(c) A is true, R is false.
(d) A is false, R is true.

Answer: (b) Both A and R are true but R is not the correct explanation.
Solution: The division of powers and independent judiciary are federal features. However, India is called quasi-federal because of its unitary bias (strong Centre, single Constitution, single citizenship, etc.). Thus R does not explain the “quasi” aspect.
Source: PYQ-style

149. (Hard) The 39th Amendment Act, 1975, placed which of the following beyond judicial review?
(a) Election of the Prime Minister and Speaker
(b) Election of the President, Vice President, Prime Minister, and Speaker
(c) Only the Prime Minister’s election
(d) All anti-defection cases

Answer: (b) Election of the President, Vice President, Prime Minister, and Speaker
Solution: The 39th Amendment (1975) inserted Article 329A to exclude judicial review of the election of the President, Vice President, Prime Minister, and Speaker of Lok Sabha. The 44th Amendment later repealed this.
Source: PYQ-style
