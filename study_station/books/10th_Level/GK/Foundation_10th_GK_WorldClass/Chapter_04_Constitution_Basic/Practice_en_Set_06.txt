126. Consider the following statements regarding the Preamble of the Indian Constitution:
1. It is a source of power to the legislature.
2. It is not enforceable in any court of law.
3. It cannot be amended by Parliament.
4. It states the basic objectives of the Constitution.
Which of the statements given above are correct?
(a) 2 only (b) 1 and 2 only (c) 2, 3 and 4 only (d) 2 and 4 only
Answer: (d)
Solution: The tempting trap is statement 3 — the Preamble was in fact amended once, by the 42nd Amendment Act of 1976. The Preamble is non-justiciable and states the basic objectives of the Constitution, but it is neither a source of power nor unamendable, so the answer is 2 and 4 only.

127. In the landmark Minerva Mills case (1980), the Supreme Court struck down provisions of the 42nd Amendment Act that:
1. Gave unlimited amending power to Parliament under Article 368.
2. Subordinated Fundamental Rights broadly to Directive Principles.
3. Abolished the Right to Property.
4. Excluded judicial review of constitutional amendments.
Which of the statements given above are correct?
(a) 1 and 2 only (b) 1, 2 and 3 only (c) 1, 2, 3 and 4 (d) 1, 2 and 4 only
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is statement 3 — the Right to Property was removed from Fundamental Rights by the 44th Amendment of 1978, not the 42nd. Minerva Mills struck down clauses 4 and 5 of Article 368 which gave unlimited amending power and barred judicial review, so the answer is 1, 2 and 4 only.

128. Which of the following writs can be issued even against a private individual who is unlawfully detaining another person?
(a) Mandamus (b) Quo Warranto (c) Certiorari (d) Habeas Corpus
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is Mandamus, which lies only against public officials and bodies for the performance of a public duty. Habeas Corpus can be directed against any person, public or private, who holds another in unlawful detention, so the answer is Habeas Corpus.

138. The 'Doctrine of Laches' in constitutional law means that:
(a) A law inconsistent with Fundamental Rights is void ab initio (b) Parliament can amend any provision of the Constitution (c) Delay in filing a writ petition may be fatal to the claim (d) The Governor can reserve any bill for the President
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is confusing laches with the doctrine of eclipse or severability, which deal with void laws. Laches means an unreasonable, unexplained delay in seeking writ relief can defeat the claim, so the answer is that delay in filing a writ petition may be fatal to the claim.

(a) Release a person from unlawful custodial detention (b) Quash an order passed without jurisdiction by an inferior tribunal (c) Prevent unlawful usurpation of a public office (d) Enforce performance of a mandatory statutory duty

Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is assuming Parliament's wide powers amount to supremacy; unlike Britain, India's Parliament is bound by the Constitution. Judicial review, secularism and federalism are recognised basic features, so the answer is absolute parliamentary supremacy.
