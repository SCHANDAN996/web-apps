101. Two unbiased coins are tossed. What is the probability of getting at most one tail?
(a) 1/4 (b) 3/4 (c) 1/2 (d) 1
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is counting only "exactly one tail" and getting 1/2. Sample space = {HH, HT, TH, TT}; at most one tail means 0 or 1 tail, i.e. {HH, HT, TH} = 3 outcomes, so the answer is 3/4.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Mathematics, Chapter 15

102. One card is drawn from a well-shuffled deck of 52 cards. Find the probability that the card is a red ace.
(a) 1/13 (b) 1/26 (c) 2/13 (d) 1/52
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is taking all four aces and getting 1/13. Only the ace of hearts and the ace of diamonds are red aces, i.e. 2 cards, so the answer is 2/52 = 1/26.

103. A die is thrown once. What is the probability of getting a prime number?
(a) 1/2 (b) 1/3 (c) 2/3 (d) 5/6
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is counting 1 as prime and getting 4/6 = 2/3. Prime numbers on a die are 2, 3, 5 only, i.e. 3 outcomes out of 6, so the answer is 1/2.

104. Two dice are rolled. Find the probability that the sum of the numbers is a perfect square.
(a) 1/6 (b) 7/36 (c) 5/36 (d) 1/12
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is counting only the sum 4 and getting 3/36 = 1/12. Perfect square sums are 4 and 9; sum 4 occurs in 3 ways and sum 9 in 4 ways, giving 7 favourable outcomes, so the answer is 7/36.

105. A bag contains 5 red, 3 green and 2 blue balls. Two balls are drawn at random without replacement. What is the probability that both are green?
(a) 2/15 (b) 1/15 (c) 1/10 (d) 3/20
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is computing (3/10)×(3/10) as if the ball were replaced. Total ways = 10C2 = 45; ways to draw 2 green from 3 = 3C2 = 3, so the answer is 3/45 = 1/15.
Source: PYQ-style

106. A box contains 4 defective and 6 non-defective items. Two items are selected at random. What is the probability that exactly one is defective?
(a) 4/15 (b) 2/15 (c) 8/15 (d) 3/5
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is taking only 4/10 × 6/10 = 6/25 style reasoning and ignoring combinations. Total ways = 10C2 = 45; favourable = 4 × 6 = 24, so the answer is 24/45 = 8/15.
Source: PYQ-style

107. If P(A) = 0.4, P(B) = 0.5, and A and B are mutually exclusive, find P(A ∪ B).
(a) 0.2 (b) 0.9 (c) 0.7 (d) 0.6
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is multiplying the probabilities and getting 0.2. For mutually exclusive events P(A ∪ B) = P(A) + P(B) = 0.4 + 0.5, so the answer is 0.9.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Mathematics, Chapter 16

108. Three coins are tossed simultaneously. What is the probability of getting at most 2 heads?
(a) 3/8 (b) 5/8 (c) 7/8 (d) 1/8
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is counting only "exactly 2 heads" and getting 3/8. At most 2 heads means every outcome except HHH, i.e. 7 of the 8 outcomes, so the answer is 7/8.

109. Two dice are thrown. Find the probability that the product of the numbers is even.
(a) 5/12 (b) 1/4 (c) 1/2 (d) 3/4
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is assuming half the products are even and answering 1/2. The product is odd only when both dice show odd numbers: 3/6 × 3/6 = 1/4, so P(even product) = 1 − 1/4, and the answer is 3/4.
Source: PYQ-style

110. A bag contains 7 white and 5 black balls. Two balls are drawn with replacement. What is the probability that one is white and the other black?
(a) 35/72 (b) 35/144 (c) 5/12 (d) 7/12
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is counting only one order, white then black, and getting 35/144. Both orders count: (7/12 × 5/12) + (5/12 × 7/12) = 70/144, so the answer is 35/72.
Source: PYQ-style

111. Two cards are drawn from a standard deck without replacement. What is the probability that both are face cards?
(a) 1/17 (b) 12/221 (c) 11/221 (d) 4/51
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is using 12/52 × 12/52 as if the card were replaced. There are 12 face cards; P = (12/52) × (11/51) = 132/2652, so the answer is 11/221.
Source: PYQ-style

112. In a single throw of three dice, what is the probability of getting a sum of 15 or more?
(a) 5/54 (b) 1/9 (c) 1/12 (d) 7/108
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is undercounting the ways for sum 15 and landing on 1/12. Ways: sum 15 → 10, sum 16 → 6, sum 17 → 3, sum 18 → 1, total 20 out of 216, so the answer is 20/216 = 5/54.
Source: PYQ-style

114. A number is selected at random from the first 30 natural numbers. What is the probability that it is divisible by both 3 and 5?
(a) 1/5 (b) 2/15 (c) 1/10 (d) 1/15
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is adding multiples of 3 and multiples of 5 separately. Divisible by both means divisible by 15; only 15 and 30 qualify, i.e. 2 numbers out of 30, so the answer is 1/15.
Source: PYQ-style

115. Two dice are thrown together. Find the probability that the sum is a multiple of 3 or 5.
(a) 19/36 (b) 13/36 (c) 17/36 (d) 5/12
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is missing the sums 3 and 12 and getting 13/36. Multiples of 3 (sums 3, 6, 9, 12) give 2+5+4+1 = 12 ways; multiples of 5 (sums 5, 10) give 4+3 = 7 ways; no overlap since 15 is impossible, so the answer is 19/36.
Source: PYQ-style

116. A bag contains 4 red, 5 blue and 1 green ball. Two balls are drawn at random. What is the probability that they are of the same colour?
(a) 11/45 (b) 4/9 (c) 2/9 (d) 16/45
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is forgetting that the single green ball can never form a pair and miscomputing the total. Total ways = 10C2 = 45; same colour = 4C2 + 5C2 + 0 = 6 + 10 = 16, so the answer is 16/45.
Source: PYQ-style

119. A box contains 6 red, 4 white and 5 blue balls. Three balls are drawn at random without replacement. What is the probability that all are of different colours?
(a) 2/13 (b) 20/91 (c) 24/91 (d) 18/91
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is multiplying probabilities with replacement, (6/15)(4/15)(5/15). Total ways = 15C3 = 455; favourable = 6 × 4 × 5 = 120, so the answer is 120/455 = 24/91.

120. A coin is tossed 5 times. What is the probability of getting exactly 3 heads?
(a) 5/32 (b) 1/2 (c) 5/16 (d) 5/8
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is using 5C3 = 10 as the probability numerator over 5 tosses wrongly, e.g. answering 5/8. Total outcomes = 2^5 = 32; favourable = 5C3 = 10, so the answer is 10/32 = 5/16.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Mathematics, Chapter 16

121. Two dice are rolled. If it is known that the first die shows an odd number, what is the probability that the sum of the two dice is 7?
(a) 1/3 (b) 1/6 (c) 1/9 (d) 1/2
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is using the full 36-outcome space and getting 6/36 = 1/6 by luck, or worse, ignoring the condition entirely. Given the first die is odd, there are 3 × 6 = 18 equally likely cases; sum 7 occurs for (1,6), (3,4), (5,2), i.e. 3 cases, so the answer is 3/18 = 1/6.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Mathematics, Chapter 13

122. A bag contains 5 red and 8 black balls. Three balls are drawn at random without replacement. What is the probability that exactly two of them are red?
(a) 35/143 (b) 70/143 (c) 28/143 (d) 40/143
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is computing 5C2 / 13C3 alone and ignoring the black ball, getting 10/286. Favourable = 5C2 × 8C1 = 10 × 8 = 80 out of 13C3 = 286, so the answer is 80/286 = 40/143.
Source: PYQ-style

123. A fair coin is tossed 6 times. What is the probability of getting at least 4 heads?
(a) 15/32 (b) 21/32 (c) 11/32 (d) 7/32
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is counting only exactly 4 heads and getting 15/64. At least 4 heads means 4, 5 or 6 heads: 6C4 + 6C5 + 6C6 = 15 + 6 + 1 = 22 out of 64, so the answer is 22/64 = 11/32.
Source: PYQ-style

124. A problem is given to three students whose chances of solving it are 1/2, 1/3 and 1/4 respectively. What is the probability that the problem is solved?
(a) 3/4 (b) 1/4 (c) 1/2 (d) 2/5
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is adding the three probabilities and getting more than 1, or averaging them. P(solved) = 1 − P(none solves) = 1 − (1/2 × 2/3 × 3/4) = 1 − 1/4, so the answer is 3/4.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Mathematics, Chapter 13

125. Two cards are drawn from a well-shuffled deck of 52 cards with replacement. What is the probability that the first is a king and the second is a queen?
(a) 1/169 (b) 2/169 (c) 4/663 (d) 1/221
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is using 4/52 × 4/51 = 4/663, which applies only without replacement. With replacement the draws are independent: (4/52) × (4/52) = 1/13 × 1/13, so the answer is 1/169.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Mathematics, Chapter 15
