51. Two coins are tossed simultaneously. What is the probability of getting no head?
(a) 1/2 (b) 3/4 (c) 1/4 (d) 0
Answer: (c)
Solution: Total outcomes are 4: HH, HT, TH, TT. No head means TT only, which is 1 outcome, so the probability is 1/4. The trap is 1/2, chosen by treating "head" and "no head" as two equal cases and ignoring that the four outcomes are equally likely. So the answer is 1/4.

52. A die is thrown once. What is the probability of getting a number less than 3?
(a) 1/6 (b) 1/3 (c) 1/2 (d) 2/3
Answer: (b)
Solution: Numbers less than 3 are 1 and 2, giving 2 favourable outcomes out of 6, so the probability is 2/6 = 1/3. The trap is 1/2, which wrongly counts 3 also as "less than 3". So the answer is 1/3.

53. A bag contains 4 red and 6 blue balls. One ball is drawn at random. Find the probability that it is either red or blue.
(a) 3/5 (b) 4/5 (c) 2/5 (d) 1
Answer: (d)
Solution: Every ball in the bag is either red or blue, so this is a sure event with probability 1. The trap is 4/5, which adds the two separate probabilities without noticing the event covers all outcomes. So the answer is 1.

56. One card is drawn from a well-shuffled deck of 52 cards. Find the probability that the card is a spade or an ace.
(a) 4/13 (b) 1/13 (c) 17/52 (d) 7/26
Answer: (a)
Solution: Spades are 13 and aces are 4, but the ace of spades is counted twice, so favourable cards are 13 + 4 − 1 = 16, giving 16/52 = 4/13. The trap is 17/52, which forgets to subtract the overlapping ace of spades. So the answer is 4/13.

57. If the probability of an event happening is 2/7, what is the probability that the event does not happen?
(a) 2/7 (b) 5/7 (c) 3/7 (d) 1
Answer: (b)
Solution: The probability of the complement is 1 − 2/7 = 5/7. The trap is 2/7 itself, chosen by those who forget that an event and its complement must add to 1. So the answer is 5/7.

58. A and B are mutually exclusive events with P(A) = 0.25 and P(B) = 0.35. Find P(A or B).
(a) 0.1 (b) 0.6 (c) 0.0875 (d) 0.5
Answer: (b)
Solution: For mutually exclusive events, P(A or B) = P(A) + P(B) = 0.25 + 0.35 = 0.60. The trap is 0.0875, which multiplies the probabilities, a rule meant for independent events occurring together. So the answer is 0.6.

60. A bag contains 10 white and 5 red balls. Two balls are drawn at random without replacement. What is the probability that both are white?
(a) 2/7 (b) 1/3 (c) 3/7 (d) 9/35
Answer: (c)
Solution: Total ways to draw 2 balls from 15 are 105; ways to draw 2 white from 10 are 45, so the probability is 45/105 = 3/7. The trap is squaring 10/15 as if the draws were with replacement, which ignores that the first draw changes the bag. So the answer is 3/7.

61. A box contains 4 defective and 6 good bulbs. Two bulbs are drawn at random. What is the probability that exactly one is defective?
(a) 8/15 (b) 4/15 (c) 2/15 (d) 7/15
Answer: (a)
Solution: Total ways to choose 2 bulbs from 10 are 45. Exactly one defective means 1 from 4 defective and 1 from 6 good, giving 4 × 6 = 24 ways, so the probability is 24/45 = 8/15. The trap is 4/15, which counts only one order of defective and good. So the answer is 8/15.

62. What is the probability that a leap year selected at random will contain 53 Sundays?
(a) 1/7 (b) 2/7 (c) 3/7 (d) 5/7
Answer: (b)
Solution: A leap year has 52 weeks and 2 extra days, which can be 7 possible consecutive pairs. Sunday appears in 2 of these pairs: (Saturday, Sunday) and (Sunday, Monday), so the probability is 2/7. The trap is 1/7, which is the answer for a non-leap year, not a leap year. So the answer is 2/7.

63. In a non-leap year, the probability of getting 53 Fridays is:
(a) 5/7 (b) 2/7 (c) 3/7 (d) 1/7
Answer: (d)
Solution: A non-leap year has 52 weeks and 1 extra day, which can be any of the 7 days. For 53 Fridays, that extra day must be Friday, giving 1 favourable case out of 7. The trap is 2/7, which is the leap-year answer. So the answer is 1/7.

66. A bag contains 2 red, 3 green, and 4 blue balls. If two balls are drawn at random, what is the probability that they are of different colours?
(a) 5/9 (b) 5/18 (c) 4/9 (d) 13/18
Answer: (d)
Solution: Total ways to draw 2 balls from 9 are 36. Same-colour pairs: both red = 1, both green = 3, both blue = 6, totalling 10, so different colours = 36 − 10 = 26, giving 26/36 = 13/18. The trap is 5/18, which is the probability of the same colour, the exact opposite of what is asked. So the answer is 13/18.

Answer: (a)
Solution: At most one tail means 0 tails (HHH) or 1 tail (THH, HTH, HHT), giving 4 favourable outcomes out of 8, so the probability is 4/8 = 1/2. The trap is 3/8, which counts only the one-tail cases and forgets the no-tail case HHH. So the answer is 1/2.

68. A number is chosen at random from the numbers 1 to 50. Find the probability that it is divisible by 6 or 8.
(a) 6/25 (b) 7/25 (c) 13/50 (d) 8/25
Answer: (a)
Solution: Multiples of 6 up to 50 are 8; multiples of 8 are 6; common multiples of 24 (24 and 48) are 2, so favourable numbers are 8 + 6 − 2 = 12, giving 12/50 = 6/25. The trap is 7/25, which adds 8 and 6 without subtracting the overlap of 24 and 48. So the answer is 6/25.

71. If P(A) = 0.6, P(B) = 0.5, and P(A ∪ B) = 0.8, find P(A ∩ B).
(a) 0.1 (b) 0.2 (c) 0.3 (d) 0.4
Answer: (c)
Solution: Using P(A ∪ B) = P(A) + P(B) − P(A ∩ B), we get 0.8 = 0.6 + 0.5 − P(A ∩ B), so P(A ∩ B) = 1.1 − 0.8 = 0.3. The trap is 0.1, obtained by carelessly computing 0.8 − 0.6 − 0.5 with a sign error. So the answer is 0.3.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Mathematics, Chapter Probability

72. A bag contains 3 red and 7 black balls. Two balls are drawn with replacement. What is the probability that one is red and the other is black?
(a) 21/100 (b) 3/10 (c) 21/50 (d) 9/50
Answer: (c)
Solution: With replacement, P(red then black) = (3/10) × (7/10) = 21/100 and P(black then red) = (7/10) × (3/10) = 21/100, so the total is 42/100 = 21/50. The trap is 21/100, which counts only one order of the two colours. So the answer is 21/50.
Source: PYQ-style

73. A die is thrown twice. What is the probability that at least one of the throws results in a number greater than 4?
(a) 1/3 (b) 5/9 (c) 4/9 (d) 2/9
Answer: (b)
Solution: P(not greater than 4 in one throw) = 4/6 = 2/3, so P(neither throw exceeds 4) = (2/3)² = 4/9, and P(at least one exceeds 4) = 1 − 4/9 = 5/9. The trap is 4/9, which is the probability of the complementary event, not the one asked. So the answer is 5/9.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Mathematics, Chapter Probability
