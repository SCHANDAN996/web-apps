26. Three unbiased coins are tossed simultaneously. What is the probability of getting exactly two heads?
(a) 1/4 (b) 1/2 (c) 3/8 (d) 5/8
Answer: (c)
Solution: The tempting option 1/4 comes from counting only 2 favorable outcomes, but there are 3: HHT, HTH, THH. Total outcomes = 2³ = 8, so the answer is 3/8.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Mathematics, Probability

27. Three unbiased coins are tossed. Find the probability of getting at least two heads.
(a) 1/2 (b) 3/8 (c) 1/4 (d) 5/8
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is 3/8, which counts only exactly two heads and forgets HHH. Favorable outcomes: HHT, HTH, THH, HHH = 4 out of 8, so the answer is 1/2.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Mathematics, Probability

28. Two dice are thrown simultaneously. What is the probability that the sum of the numbers is a multiple of 3?
(a) 1/6 (b) 1/3 (c) 1/4 (d) 1/2
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is 1/4, from wrongly assuming 9 favorable outcomes. Sums that are multiples of 3 are 3, 6, 9, 12 with 2 + 5 + 4 + 1 = 12 ways out of 36, so the answer is 1/3.

29. In a single throw of two dice, what is the probability of getting a sum less than 5?
(a) 1/12 (b) 1/6 (c) 1/4 (d) 1/3
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is 1/12, which counts only sums 2 and 3 and forgets sum 4. Sums less than 5 are 2, 3, 4 with 1 + 2 + 3 = 6 ways out of 36, so the answer is 1/6.

30. One card is drawn from a well-shuffled deck of 52 cards. What is the probability that it is either a face card or a red card?
(a) 19/26 (b) 8/13 (c) 7/13 (d) 15/26
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is 19/26, from adding 12 + 26 = 38 without removing the 6 red face cards counted twice. Favorable = 12 + 26 − 6 = 32, so the answer is 32/52 = 8/13.

32. A bag contains 6 white and 4 black balls. Two balls are drawn at random. What is the probability that one is white and one is black?

Solution: The trap is 4/15, which counts only one order (white first, black second). Both orders give 6 × 4 = 24 favorable pairs out of ⁴⁵C₂ = 45, so the answer is 24/45 = 8/15.

34. A and B are two independent events. If P(A) = 0.4 and P(B) = 0.5, what is P(A ∩ B)?
(a) 0.9 (b) 0.1 (c) 0.45 (d) 0.2
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is 0.9, from adding the probabilities, which is wrong for intersection. For independent events P(A ∩ B) = P(A) × P(B) = 0.4 × 0.5, so the answer is 0.2.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Mathematics, Probability

35. For two events A and B, P(A) = 0.5, P(B) = 0.4, and P(A ∪ B) = 0.7. Find P(A ∩ B).
(a) 0.1 (b) 0.2 (c) 0.3 (d) 0.9
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is 0.1, from subtracting 0.7 from 0.5 only. Using P(A ∪ B) = P(A) + P(B) − P(A ∩ B): 0.7 = 0.9 − P(A ∩ B), so the answer is 0.2.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Mathematics, Probability

36. A fair die is thrown twice. What is the probability that the first throw gives a 6 and the second throw gives an odd number?
(a) 1/6 (b) 1/9 (c) 1/18 (d) 1/12
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is 1/6, which ignores the second condition. P(first 6) = 1/6 and P(second odd) = 3/6 = 1/2; the throws are independent, so the answer is (1/6) × (1/2) = 1/12.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Mathematics, Probability

37. A coin is tossed three times. What is the probability of getting a head on the first toss and tails on the other two?
(a) 1/4 (b) 1/8 (c) 3/8 (d) 1/6
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is 3/8, which counts all arrangements of one head and two tails, but here the order is fixed as HTT. Each toss has probability 1/2, so the answer is (1/2)³ = 1/8.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Mathematics, Probability

40. Two cards are drawn from a standard deck without replacement. What is the probability that at least one is an ace?
(a) 11/26 (b) 1/221 (c) 32/221 (d) 33/221
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is 32/221, an arithmetic slip in the complement. P(no ace) = (⁴⁸C₂)/(⁵²C₂) = 1128/1326 = 188/221, so P(at least one ace) = 1 − 188/221, and the answer is 33/221.

46. The probability of rain on any given day in a city is 0.3. What is the probability that it does not rain on that day?
(a) 0.3 (b) 0.5 (c) 0.7 (d) 0.9
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is 0.3, which simply repeats the given probability. Rain and no rain are complementary events, so P(no rain) = 1 − 0.3, and the answer is 0.7.
