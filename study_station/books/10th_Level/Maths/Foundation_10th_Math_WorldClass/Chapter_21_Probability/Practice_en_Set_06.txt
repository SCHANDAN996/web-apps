126. Two dice are rolled. Given that the sum is odd, what is the probability that the sum is a multiple of 3?
(a) 1/6 (b) 1/2 (c) 1/3 (d) 1/9
Answer: (c)
Solution: Odd sums occur in 18 outcomes (one die even, one odd). Multiples of 3 among odd sums are 3 and 9, giving 2 + 4 = 6 outcomes. The trap is dividing by all 36 outcomes instead of the 18 conditional ones, so the answer is 1/3.

127. A bag contains 7 white and 5 black balls. Two balls are drawn without replacement. What is the probability that the second ball drawn is white?
(a) 1/2 (b) 35/144 (c) 7/12 (d) 6/11
Answer: (c)
Solution: P(second white) = (7/12)(6/11) + (5/12)(7/11) = 42/132 + 35/132 = 77/132 = 7/12. The trap is using only the case where the first ball is white, so the answer is 7/12.

128. A fair coin is tossed 5 times. Given that the first toss is a head, what is the probability of getting exactly 3 heads in total?

Answer: (a)
Solution: With the first head fixed, we need exactly 2 heads in the remaining 4 tosses: C(4,2) = 6 outcomes out of 16. The trap is counting 3 heads among all 5 tosses, so the answer is 3/8.

131. A problem is given to three students whose chances of solving it are 1/2, 1/3 and 1/4 respectively. What is the probability that exactly two of them solve it?

133. A die is thrown three times. What is the probability of getting at least one six?

Answer: (a)
Solution: P(no six in three throws) = (5/6)³ = 125/216, so P(at least one six) = 1 − 125/216 = 91/216. The trap is computing 3 × (1/6) = 1/2 by simple addition, so the answer is 91/216.

Solution: P(A wins) = (1/6) / (1 − (5/6)²) = (1/6)/(11/36) = 6/11, so P(B wins) = 1 − 6/11 = 5/11. The trap is assuming the first player's advantage does not matter, so the answer is 5/11.

143. If P(A | B) = 0.4, P(B) = 0.5 and P(A) = 0.3, what is P(B | A)?
(a) 2/3 (b) 3/5 (c) 4/15 (d) 1/5
Answer: (a)
Solution: P(A ∩ B) = P(A | B) × P(B) = 0.4 × 0.5 = 0.2, so P(B | A) = 0.2/0.3 = 2/3. The trap is assuming P(B | A) equals P(A | B), so the answer is 2/3.

150. The probability that student A solves a problem is 0.6 and that student B solves it is 0.5. Assuming independence, what is the probability that at least one of them solves it?
(a) 0.7 (b) 0.8 (c) 0.3 (d) 0.55
Answer: (b)
Solution: P(at least one) = 1 − P(neither) = 1 − (0.4)(0.5) = 1 − 0.2 = 0.8. The trap is simply adding 0.6 + 0.5 = 1.1, which exceeds 1, so the answer is 0.8.
Source: NCERT Class 12 Mathematics (Independent Events)
