## 1. The Golden Triangle (D = S × T)

I want you to meet three friends: Distance, Speed, and Time. They are so close that if you cover one, the other two call out the answer. Write this in your memory like a photograph:

```
        Distance
      ------------
      Speed  |  Time
```

Meaning:

- Distance = Speed × Time
- Speed = Distance ÷ Time
- Time = Distance ÷ Speed

Everything else in this chapter is just this triangle wearing different masks.

🧠 Active Recall Prompt: Close your eyes. Picture the triangle. If a car travels at 60 km/h for 2 hours, what is the distance? Now, if a man walks 15 km at 5 km/h, how much time does he take? Answer silently before you read ahead.

## 2. The Unit Trap – km/h vs m/s

Examiners love to give speed in km/h but distance in metres and time in seconds. They want you to stumble. Stumble no more.

Conversion rule:

1 km/h = 1000 m / 3600 s = 5/18 m/s

So to go from km/h to m/s, multiply by 5/18.
From m/s to km/h, multiply by 18/5.

But why 5 and 18? Here is your weird, never-forget mnemonic:

🦎 The Lizard Feast Trick
Imagine a 5-headed Komodo dragon (5) swallowing an 18-legged centipede (18). It does this every time you go FROM km/h TO m/s – the lizard (5) eats the centipede (18) during the conversion. The lizard is the numerator, the centipede is the denominator.
To reverse (m/s to km/h), the lizard spits the centipede back out – 18/5.

Recall it just once, and the image will make you smile inside the exam hall.

🧠 Active Recall Prompt: Convert 90 km/h into m/s using the lizard feast. (90 × 5/18 = 25 m/s). Now convert 10 m/s into km/h. (10 × 18/5 = 36 km/h). Done? Good.

## 3. Average Speed – The Cheetah That Walked

A cheetah runs at 100 km/h for 1 hour and then walks at 10 km/h for another hour. Is the average speed (100+10)/2 = 55 km/h? Yes, because the time is the same. But what if the cheetah runs 100 km at 100 km/h and the next 100 km at 10 km/h? Then the times differ, and the simple average fails.

When the distances are equal, use the harmonic mean:

Average speed = 2xy / (x + y)

Here, x = 100, y = 10 → Average speed = (2 × 100 × 10) / 110 = 2000/110 ≈ 18.18 km/h. Wow.

The lesson: Average speed is never just averaging speeds unless the time periods are equal. Always find total distance and total time.

Pocket rule:
Average Speed = Total Distance ÷ Total Time. This is your eternal anchor.

🧠 Active Recall Prompt: A man goes from A to B at 20 km/h and returns at 30 km/h. What is his average speed for the whole journey? Use the equal-distance formula if you remember, or the total distance method. (Answer: 24 km/h)

## 4. Relative Speed – When Two Bodies Move

This is the heart of train and meeting problems. It is the speed at which one body sees the other moving.

Same direction: They seem to move at the difference of speeds.
Relative speed = Faster – Slower.
Imagine you are in a car at 60 km/h and a car beside you is at 50 km/h in the same direction. To you, it is going backward at 10 km/h.

Opposite direction: They rush towards each other. Speeds add up.
Relative speed = Speed1 + Speed2.
If you are at 60 km/h and a car heads straight at you at 50 km/h, your closing speed is 110 km/h. It feels faster, and it is.

Story detector for trains:

- A train crossing a pole or a man – the distance to cover is the train's own length.
- A train crossing a platform – distance = length of train + length of platform.
- Two trains crossing each other – distance = sum of their lengths; speed = relative speed (add if opposite, subtract if same direction).

Now, you are the detective. The train itself becomes a measuring tape.

🧠 Active Recall Prompt: A train travelling at 72 km/h crosses a pole in 12 seconds. Find its length. (Convert speed to m/s: 72 × 5/18 = 20 m/s. Distance = 20 × 12 = 240 m. That is the length.) Solve it in your head again.

## 5. The Train-Platform Tango (One Problem, Two Scenes)

Examiners' favourite: A train crosses a pole in T1 seconds and a platform in T2 seconds. The difference in time is the time the train takes to cover the platform's length (since the train's own length is covered in both scenarios).

Method:
Let length of train = L, speed = v.

- Pole crossing: L = v × t1.
- Platform crossing: L + Platform = v × t2.
- Subtract: Platform = v × (t2 – t1).

From there, you can find v and then L.

Visualise the train's nose as a point. When it touches the pole, the timer starts; when the tail passes, the timer stops. For the platform, the nose touches the start of the platform, the timer starts; the tail leaves the end of the platform, the timer stops. This is pure cinematography.

🧠 Active Recall Prompt: A train crosses a pole in 10 s and a 150 m long platform in 18 s. Can you smell the speed? (v = Platform / time difference = 150 / (18 – 10) = 150/8 = 18.75 m/s. Length = 18.75 × 10 = 187.5 m.)

## 6. The Memory Booster – "S.O.S Triangle" Mnemonic

Remember the three musketeers D, S, T with S.O.S:

- Suppose you want Speed, hide Distance and Time fights – Speed = D/T.
- If you want Distance, Speed and Time unite – D = S × T.
- If you want Time, Speed is the divider – T = D/S.

But even better, store the image of a traffic signal:
🟥 D on top, 🟡 S and 🟢 T on the two lower lights. When one light goes off, the other two make the formula. It is weird, but it works.
