## 1. The Shocking Story of Rahul’s Batting
Rahul is a cricket lover. After 10 innings, his batting average is 45. He decides:
> *“I want my average to become 50 after the next innings. How many runs must I score?”*

If your mind immediately says 50, you are not alone — **95% of students think the same**.

The real answer is **100 runs**.
Yes, Rahul needs 100, not 50! This one idea, if you master it today, will set you apart from the crowd in every competitive exam.

🧠 **Before reading further, guess why the answer is 100?**
*(Hint: 50 is not enough because the new innings increases the divisor as well!)*

## 2. Let’s Build Empathy — Why Does Average Feel Tricky?
Most learners confuse average with the “middle number.” They think adding a new value will move the average by an equal amount.

The truth is, average is a **balancing act** — and every new value must lift the total weight of the entire group, not just itself. This chapter will gently erase that confusion forever.

---

## 3. The Golden Formula (Smallest Possible Chunk)
Think of average as **equal sharing**:
If 5 friends have ₹100 and share equally, each gets ₹20. That ₹20 is the average.

The formula in words:
$$\text{Average} = \frac{\text{Total Sum}}{\text{Number of Items}}$$

In symbols:
$$A = \frac{S}{N}$$

From this one simple formula, three superhero versions emerge:
1. $S = A \times N$ (Find total sum if you know average and count)
2. $N = \frac{S}{A}$ (Find how many items if you know sum and average)
3. $A = \frac{S}{N}$ (The foundational formula)

If you memorize only these three, you can solve 80% of all average questions.

🧠 **Cover the screen. Say the three forms out loud like a chant:**
> *“A = S/N, S = A × N, N = S/A.”*

## 4. Crack the Batting Puzzle (Step-by-Step Chunk)
- **Old situation:** 10 innings, average 45.
  $$\text{Old total runs} = 10 \times 45 = 450$$
- **New situation after 11 innings:** average wanted 50.
  $$\text{New total runs needed} = 11 \times 50 = 550$$
- **Runs required in 11th innings:**
  $$\text{Runs} = 550 - 450 = 100 \text{ runs}$$

This is called an **inclusion problem**. Always use:
$$\text{New value} = (\text{New Avg} \times \text{New Count}) - (\text{Old Avg} \times \text{Old Count})$$
No guesswork, no panic.

🧠 **Mental Drill:** A batsman has an average of 30 after 8 innings. How many runs in the 9th innings to make his average 35?
*(Answer: $9 \times 35 - 8 \times 30 = 315 - 240 = 75$ runs)*

## 5. The Weird and Funny Memory Trick (Mnemonic)
Meet the **SAD Panda 🐼**.
He sits under a bamboo tree and repeats:
> *“Sum all the values. Amount (number) count karo. Divide karo.”*
> **SAD = Sum, Amount, Divide.**

Why is the panda sad? Because whenever a student forgets to divide at the end, the panda cries!
To make the panda happy, always do the **D step last**.

**Reverse Magic:** If someone gives you Average and Amount, multiply them to get the Sum (panda hugs his bamboo!).

## 6. Weighted Average — The Trap That Hurts
Imagine a class: 4 boys weigh 50 kg on average, 6 girls weigh 40 kg on average.
Many students do:
$$\frac{50 + 40}{2} = 45 \quad \text{❌ WRONG!}$$
That would be true only if boys and girls were equal in number. Here the weights (counts) are different!

**Correct Weighted Average:**
$$A_w = \frac{(4 \times 50) + (6 \times 40)}{4 + 6} = \frac{200 + 240}{10} = \frac{440}{10} = 44 \text{ kg}$$

Always keep the total weighted sum in the numerator and the sum of weights in the denominator. Never fall for the 50–50 trap.

🧠 **Practice:** In a factory, 10 workers earn ₹300 on average, 5 earn ₹500 on average. Find overall average.
*(Answer: $\frac{10 \times 300 + 5 \times 500}{15} = \frac{3000 + 2500}{15} = 366.67$)*

## 7. Consecutive Numbers Shortcut (No Addition Needed)
For consecutive numbers like $1, 2, 3, \dots, 100$:
$$\text{Average} = \frac{\text{First} + \text{Last}}{2} = \frac{1 + 100}{2} = 50.5$$

- For an **odd count** of terms: e.g., $11, 12, 13, 14, 15$ $\implies$ average is the exact middle term = $13$.
- For an **even count** of terms: e.g., $11 \text{ to } 20$ $\implies$ average = $\frac{11 + 20}{2} = 15.5$.

This turns a 5-minute manual addition into a 5-second answer!

🧠 **Quick Quiz:** Average of numbers from 101 to 200?
*(Answer: $\frac{101 + 200}{2} = 150.5$)*

---

## 8. Correcting Mistakes (Error Correction Questions)
If someone mistakenly recorded 56 instead of 65 while summing, and the average of 20 items came out as 40:
1. **Wrong sum:** $40 \times 20 = 800$.
2. **Correct sum:** $800 - 56 + 65 = 809$.
3. **Correct average:** $\frac{809}{20} = 40.45$.

**The Golden Rule:** Fix the sum first, then divide again!

## 9. Summary Table — Your Quick Reference
| Concept | Formula / Shortcut |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Basic Average** | $A = \text{Sum} \div N$ |
| **Total Sum** | $\text{Sum} = A \times N$ |
| **New Entry (Inclusion)** | $\text{New value} = (\text{New } A \times \text{New } N) - (\text{Old } A \times \text{Old } N)$ |
| **Effect of One Value** | $\text{New } A = \text{Old } A + \frac{\text{New value} - \text{Old } A}{\text{New } N}$ |
| **Weighted Average** | $A_w = \frac{w_1 x_1 + w_2 x_2 + \dots}{w_1 + w_2 + \dots}$ |
| **Consecutive Numbers (AP)** | $\text{Average} = \frac{\text{First} + \text{Last}}{2}$ |
| **Error Correction** | $\text{Correct Sum} = \text{Wrong Sum} - \text{Wrong Value} + \text{Right Value}$ |
