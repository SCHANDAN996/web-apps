Chapter Outline & Learning GoalsBy the end of this chapter, you will be able to:• Explain what an average really means (equal sharing).• Recall and apply the three golden forms of the average formula instantly.• Avoid the most dangerous trap in weighted average.• Solve “new entry” and “wrong average” questions with confidence.• Use a hilarious memory trick to never mix up the steps again.The Shocking Story of Rahul’s BattingRahul is a cricket lover. After 10 innings, his batting average is 45. He decides, “I want my average to become 50 after the next innings.” How many runs must he score?If your mind says 50, you are not alone — 95% of students think the same.The real answer is 100 runs.Yes, Rahul needs 100, not 50. This one idea, if you master it today, will set you apart from the crowd in every exam.🧠 Before reading further, guess why the answer is 100?(Hint: 50 is not enough because the new innings increases the divisor too.)Let’s Build Empathy — Why Does Average Feel Tricky?Most learners confuse average with the “middle number.” They think adding a new value will move the average by an equal amount.The truth is, average is a balancing act — and every new value must lift the total weight of the entire group, not just itself. This chapter will gently erase that confusion forever.The Golden Formula (Smallest Possible Chunk)Think of average as equal sharing:If 5 friends have 100 rupees and share equally, each gets 20. That 20 is the average.The formula in words:Average = Total Sum ÷ Number of ItemsNumbers version: A = S / NFrom this one simple formula, three superhero versions emerge:S = A × N  (Find total if you know average and number)N = S / A  (Find how many items if you know total and average)A = S / N  (The original)If you memorize only these three, you can solve 80% of all average questions.🧠 Cover the screen. Say the three forms out loud like a chant: “A = S/N, S = A×N, N = S/A.”Crack the Batting Puzzle (Step-by-Step Chunk)Old situation: 10 innings, average 45.→ Old total runs = 10 × 45 = 450.New situation after 11 innings: average wanted 50.→ New total runs needed = 11 × 50 = 550.So the 11th innings must add: 550 – 450 = 100 runs.This is called an inclusion problem. Always use:New value = (New Avg × New Count) – (Old Avg × Old Count).No guesswork, no panic.🧠 A batsman has avg 30 after 8 innings. How many runs in the 9th innings to make avg 35? Solve mentally before looking.*(Answer: 9×35 – 8×30 = 315 – 240 = 75)*The Weird and Funny Memory Trick (Mnemonic)Meet the SAD Panda 🐼.He sits under a tree and repeats:“Sum all the values. Amount (number) count karo. Divide karo.”SAD = Sum, Amount, Divide.Why is the panda sad? Because whenever a student forgets to divide, the panda cries. So to make the panda happy, always do the D step last.Reverse magic: If someone gives you Average and Amount, multiply them to get the Sum (panda hugs his bamboo).🧠 What does the SAD panda say? Recite the three words and the action.Weighted Average — The Trap That HurtsImagine a class: 4 boys weigh 50 kg on average, 6 girls weigh 40 kg on average.Many students do (50+40)/2 = 45. Wrong! That would be true only if boys and girls were equal in number.Here the “weight” (number of people) is different.Correct weighted average:= (4×50 + 6×40) / (4+6)= (200 + 240) / 10 = 440/10 = 44 kg.Always keep the weights in the numerator and the sum of weights in the denominator. Never fall for the 50–50 trap.🧠 *In a factory, 10 workers earn ₹300 avg, 5 earn ₹500 avg. Find overall average. (Answer: (10×300 + 5×500)/15 = (3000+2500)/15 = 366.67)*Consecutive Numbers Shortcut (No Addition Needed)Numbers like 1,2,3,…100. Average = (First + Last)/2 = (1+100)/2 = 50.5.For odd count, e.g., 11,12,13,14,15 → average is middle term = 13.For even count, e.g., 11 to 20 → average = (11+20)/2 = 15.5.This turns a 5-minute addition into a 5-second answer.🧠 Average of 101 to 200? (Ans: (101+200)/2 = 150.5)Correcting Mistakes (Error Questions)If a student wrote 56 instead of 65 while summing, and average came out 40 for 20 items:Wrong sum = 40 × 20 = 800.Correct sum = 800 – 56 + 65 = 809.New average = 809/20 = 40.45.The trick: fix the sum first, then divide again.Summary Table — Your Cheat SheetConceptFormula / ShortcutBasic AverageA = Sum ÷ NTotal SumSum = A × NNew entry (inclusion)New value = (New A × New N) – (Old A × Old N)Effect of one valueNew A = Old A + (New value – Old A)/New total NWeighted Average(w₁·x₁ + w₂·x₂ + …) / (w₁ + w₂ + …)Consecutive numbers(First + Last) / 2Error correctionCorrect Sum = Wrong Sum – Wrong value + Right value📅 Review This After 2 Days (Spaced Repetition Promise)Rewrite the SAD panda story without looking.Solve the Rahul question again — this time for an average rise from 45 to 52 after 12 innings.Attempt at least 5 mixed average questions using only the summary table.Teach the concept to a friend (or a pillow) — if you can explain it, you own it.You are now equipped.
