Now here’s the never-forget trick: The “n-1” rule. If something becomes “n” times (2 times, 3 times, etc.), the interest is always (n-1) times the original money. For doubling (n=2): interest = (2-1) × P = 1P. For tripling: interest = 2P. For becoming 4 times: interest = 3P. That’s it! No matter what numbers they throw at you — you just subtract one and multiply by principal, then plug into the formula to find missing rate or time.

So next time you see “a sum becomes 4 times in 20 years at simple interest,” you can smirk: interest = 3P. Put it in: 3P = (P × R × 20)/100. Cancel P, and 3 = (20R)/100 → R = 15%. Done in seconds.

Fix this in your brain: Simple interest doesn’t grow — only the original money pays rent. And when something multiplies, just subtract 1 to find the rent.
