1.
Front: What does each letter stand for in the formula SI = (P × R × T) / 100?
Back: P = Principal (starting money), R = Rate of interest per year (in %), T = Time in years, and 100 is the divider to convert percentage.

2.
Front: Why is simple interest called "simple"?
Back: Because each year, interest is calculated only on the original Principal. It never includes interest from previous years.

3.
Front: You lend ₹500 at 10% per annum for 3 years. What’s the simple interest?
Back: SI = (500 × 10 × 3) / 100 = ₹150.

4.
Front: How do you find the total amount you get back after adding simple interest?
Back: Amount (A) = Principal (P) + Simple Interest (SI).

5.
Front: ₹1000 becomes ₹1300 in 2 years at simple interest. What is the rate?
Back: SI = 300. So 300 = (1000 × R × 2)/100 → 300 = 20R → R = 15%.

6.
Front: How do you convert 6 months into years for the SI formula?
Back: Divide by 12 → 6/12 = 0.5 years.

7.
Front: How do you convert 73 days into years for the SI formula?
Back: Divide by 365 → 73/365 = 0.2 years (unless a leap year is specifically mentioned).

8.
Front: What does the mnemonic "M÷12, D÷365" help you remember?
Back: Convert Months to years by dividing by 12, and Days to years by dividing by 365.

9.
Front: What is the "n-1" trick in simple interest?
Back: If a sum becomes n times itself, the interest earned is (n-1) × Principal. So SI = (n-1)P.

10.
Front: A sum doubles (2 times) in 5 years at simple interest. Use the n-1 trick to find the rate.
Back: n=2 → SI = (2-1)P = P. Then P = (P × R × 5)/100 → 1 = (5R)/100 → R = 20%.

11.
Front: What is the funny mnemonic "Please Return The 100 rupees you borrowed, you PRT!" used for?
Back: It reminds the order: P×R×T on top, divided by 100 — PRT/100. P=Principal, R=Rate, T=Time.

12.
Front: If the rate is given as 5% half-yearly, what is the effective annual rate for simple interest?
Back: Multiply by 2 → 5% × 2 = 10% per annum. Then use T in years.

13.
Front: If the rate is 2% quarterly, how do you compute simple interest for 1 year?
Back: Convert to annual rate = 2% × 4 = 8%. Then apply SI formula with T=1 year.

14.
Front: Two principals P1 and P2 (P1 > P2) have same rate & time. The difference in their SI is ₹360, difference in principals is ₹2000, time is 3 years. Find the rate.
Back: (SI1 - SI2) = (P1 - P2) × R × T /100 → 360 = 2000 × R × 3 /100 → R = (360×100)/(2000×3) = 6%.

15.
Front: What is the most common trap when time is given in months?
Back: Forgetting to convert months to years. Using months directly as T gives a hugely incorrect (12 times larger) interest.

16.
Front: In simple interest, does the interest amount per year ever increase?
Back: No, the yearly interest stays constant because it's always a fixed percent of the same original principal.

17.
Front: A problem gives "amount after 3 years is ₹X" and the SI is ₹Y. How do you find the principal?
Back: Principal = Amount - SI = ₹X - ₹Y. Never treat the amount as the principal directly.

18.
Front: What is the simple interest on ₹2000 at 8% per annum for 1½ years (1.5 years)?
Back: SI = (2000 × 8 × 1.5)/100 = (2000 × 12)/100 = ₹240.

19.
Front: Using a mnemonic: What does the "dirty rat" image represent in SI?
Back: The rat is named PRT (Principal × Rate × Time) and holds a sign "/100". It cements the formula: SI = (PRT)/100.

20.
Front: A sum triples in 8 years at simple interest. Using the n-1 trick, find the rate.
Back: n=3 → SI = (3-1)P = 2P. 2P = (P × R × 8)/100 → 2 = (8R)/100 → R = 25%.
