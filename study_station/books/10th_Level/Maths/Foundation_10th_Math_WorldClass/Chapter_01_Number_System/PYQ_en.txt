🎣 Top 10 High-Yield PYQs with "Hook & Trap" Analysis

Here are 10 archetypal questions, each with its solution and the psychological "hook" or "trap" it uses to trick aspirants.

Q1. (SSC CGL) A 4-digit number is formed by repeating a 2-digit number, e.g., 1515, 3737. Any number of this form is always exactly divisible by?
(a) 7 (b) 11 (c) 13 (d) 101

Solution: Any 4-digit number of the form 'abab' can be written as 1000a + 100b + 10a + b = 1010a + 101b = 101(10a + b). Therefore, it is always a multiple of 101. Ans: (d) 101.

The Hook / Trap: The "Obvious Guess" Trap. The options 7, 11, and 13 are common divisibility rule numbers. An unprepared student guesses one of these, while the answer is the less "cool" number 101. The trick is to use algebra, not mental rules.

Q2. (SSC CGL) A number when divided by 136 leaves a remainder of 36. If the same number is divided by 17, what will be the remainder?
(a) 9 (b) 7 (c) 3 (d) 2

Solution: Let the number be N. N = 136q + 36. We need to find the remainder when N is divided by 17. Note that 136 = 17 × 8 (divisible by 17). So, N = 17(8q) + 36. Now divide 36 by 17: 36 = 17 × 2 + 2. Therefore, the remainder is 2. Ans: (d) 2.

The Hook / Trap: The "Complex Divisor" Trap. 136 looks random, and students get stuck trying to find the number. The key is recognizing that 136 is a multiple of the new divisor, 17. The anxiety of large numbers masks a simple logic.

Q3. (Bank PO) The sum of five consecutive even numbers and four consecutive odd numbers is 96. If the smallest even number is 4, what’s the difference between the largest even number and the smallest odd number?
(a) 0 (b) 1 (c) 2 (d) 3

Solution: Five consecutive even numbers starting with 4 are 4, 6, 8, 10, 12. Their sum = 40. The sum of the four odd numbers = 96 - 40 = 56. Let the odd numbers be x, x+2, x+4, x+6. Their sum: 4x + 12 = 56 → 4x = 44 → x = 11. The odd numbers are 11, 13, 15, 17. The smallest odd is 11 and the largest even is 12. Difference = 12 - 11 = 1. Ans: (b) 1.

The Hook / Trap: The "Information Overload" Trap. The question bombards you with data (five evens, four odds, sums, differences). The trap is freezing up. The solution is simple, step-by-step decoding.

Q4. (Bank PO) A two-digit number becomes 63 larger when its digits are reversed. The sum of the digits is 11. Find the number.
(a) 47 (b) 38 (c) 29 (d) 56

Solution: Let the number be 10a + b. The reversed number is 10b + a. According to the problem: (10b + a) - (10a + b) = 63 → 9b - 9a = 63 → b - a = 7. Also, a + b = 11. Solving these two equations: 2b = 18 → b = 9, so a = 2. The number is 29. Ans: (c) 29.

The Hook / Trap: The "Reverse and Subtract" Trap. Students often incorrectly set up the equation as (10a + b) - (10b + a) = 63, leading to a - b = 7 and getting a negative digit. The wording "becomes 63 larger" defines the correct order.

Q5. (RRB NTPC) Which of the following is a prime number?
(a) 33 (b) 87 (c) 93 (d) 97

Solution: Check each number. 33 is divisible by 3 and 11. 87 is divisible by 3 (8+7=15). 93 is divisible by 3 (9+3=12). 97 is not divisible by any prime number less than its square root (~10), i.e., 2, 3, 5, 7. Therefore, 97 is prime. Ans: (d) 97.

The Hook / Trap: The "Looks Like a Prime" Trap. 91 (7x13) is a famous composite "imposter," but even with other numbers, students who don't check the sum of digits for divisibility by 3 will guess wrong.

Q7. (RRB NTPC) What is the largest 4-digit number exactly divisible by 9, 12, and 18?
(a) 9936 (b) 9960 (c) 9990 (d) 9972

Solution: First, find the LCM of 9, 12, and 18, which is 36. The largest 4-digit number is 9999. Divide 9999 by 36: 9999 ÷ 36 gives a quotient of 277 and a remainder of 27. The required number is 9999 - 27 = 9972. Ans: (d) 9972.

The Hook / Trap: The "Direct LCM" Trap. A careless student might multiply the numbers (9x12x18=1944) and then look for multiples of 1944. The key step is finding the least common multiple first.
