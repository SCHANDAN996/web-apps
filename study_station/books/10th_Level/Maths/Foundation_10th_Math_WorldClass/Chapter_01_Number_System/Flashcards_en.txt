Flashcard 1
Front: What is the smallest natural number – and why isn’t it zero?
Back: 1. Natural numbers start at 1 (counting numbers). Zero joins the “whole numbers” club.

Flashcard 2
Front: I’m a number that can be written as a fraction of two integers. What am I called?
Back: Rational number. Examples: ½, 0.75, -3 (since -3 = -3/1).

Flashcard 3
Front: What makes an irrational number different from a rational number?
Back: An irrational number cannot be expressed as a fraction of two integers. Its decimal goes on forever without repeating. Examples: √2, π.

Flashcard 4
Front: √9 is 3. Is √9 rational or irrational? What about √10?
Back: √9 = 3 (rational, because it’s 3/1). √10 is irrational because 10 is not a perfect square.

Flashcard 5
Front: True or false: Every integer is a rational number.
Back: True. Every integer can be written as itself over 1. For example, -7 = -7/1.

Flashcard 6
Front: What is the only even prime number? Why?
Back: 2. All other even numbers are divisible by 2, so they have at least three factors (1, 2, and themselves), making them composite.

Flashcard 7
Front: A number greater than 1 with exactly two distinct factors. What do we call it?
Back: A prime number. Examples: 3 (factors 1,3), 11 (factors 1,11).

Flashcard 8
Front: What’s special about the number 1 in the prime/composite classification?
Back: 1 is neither prime nor composite. It has only one factor (itself).

Flashcard 9
Front: Is zero a rational number? If yes, write it as a fraction.
Back: Yes, zero is rational. 0 = 0/1 (or 0/any non-zero integer).

Flashcard 10
Front: Can a number have a decimal that never ends but still be rational?
Back: Yes, if the decimal repeats. For example, 0.333... = 1/3, which is rational.

Flashcard 11
Front: I’m a number with decimal 0.1010010001… (non-terminating, non-repeating). Am I rational or irrational?
Back: Irrational. Because it never ends and never repeats a pattern.

Flashcard 12
Front: What does the symbol Z represent, and why is it used?
Back: Z represents integers. It comes from the German word “Zahlen” meaning numbers.

Flashcard 13
Front: Which set is larger: Whole numbers or Integers?
Back: Integers. Whole numbers = {0,1,2,3,…}. Integers add all negative versions too: {…,-2,-1,0,1,2,…}.

Flashcard 14
Front: Use the mnemonic trick: “Naughty Wombats Zigzag Quietly on Roads.” What number family order does this stand for?
Back: Natural (Naughty), Whole (Wombats), Integer (Zigzag? Actually Z for Integers), Rational (Quietly), Real (Roads). (Note: The weird wombat helps recall N, W, Z, Q, R – the main number sets in increasing order.)

Flashcard 15
Front: What animal mnemonic helps you remember the primes up to 19?
Back: “Two Tigers Chased Five Elephants, Eleven Lions, Thirteen Monkeys, Seventeen Zebras, Nineteen Kangaroos.” The numbers: 2, 3, 5, 7, 11, 13, 17, 19.

Flashcard 16
Front: If a number is inside a square root and the radicand is a perfect square, is the root rational or irrational?
Back: Rational. Because it becomes a whole number (or fraction). Example: √25 = 5.

Flashcard 17
Front: Are all square roots of natural numbers that are not perfect squares irrational? Give an example.
Back: Yes. √2, √3, √5, √7, √11… are all irrational.

Flashcard 18
Front: Why did the Greek mathematicians get so upset about √2?
Back: Because they believed all numbers could be written as fractions (rational), but √2 proved this was impossible, breaking their perfect mathematical world.
