📊 PYQ Analysis – Statistics (2016–2026)

How the Paper-Setter Thinks (and How You Can Outthink Him)

Statistics in competitive exams (SSC CGL, CHSL, CPO, MTS, RRB NTPC, Group D, Bank PO/Clerk) is a goldmine of low-effort, high-return marks. The catch? It’s not a math test—it’s a psychology test. The paper-setter weaponizes your haste and your half-memorized formulas. Here’s the decade-long blueprint.

The Three Rules of the Statistics Battlefield

The Time Trap: Statistics questions are placed to consume your clock. The average solving time for a grouped-data median question is over 3 minutes for an untrained mind; a trained mind does it in 45 seconds. Each question below is tagged with a Target Time—beat it.

The Distractor Science: Wrong options aren’t random. They are engineered. Skipping a sorting step, using n/2 instead of (n+1)/2 for ungrouped median (when n is odd), or grabbing the modal class but plugging f₀ and f₂ in the reverse order—these are the traps that fill the other three bubbles. Every question below exposes the exact trap.

The Repetition Doctrine: At least 70% of any given year’s statistics questions are clones of questions from the last five years, with numbers swapped. Master the ten below, and you’ve seen the entire playbook.

🔥 HIGH-YIELD PYQ 1

[SSC MTS | 14/08/2019 | Morning Shift]
A set of data is given. The mean of the set is 35 while the median is set as 60. Find the mode of the data.

A. 75

B. 85

C. 95

D. 110

Target Time: 25 seconds | Frequency: 4 times in 5 years

Solution:
Empirical relation: Mode = 3 × Median – 2 × Mean
Mode = 3 × 60 – 2 × 35 = 180 – 70 = 110

Correct Answer: D. 110

🧠 What the paper-setter is doing:
This is the most repeated question skeleton in the last decade. The trap is subtle: the formula looks like Mode = 3(Mean) – 2(Median) if you misremember it. The distractor 75 is exactly what you get if you plug numbers into that wrong formula. Hook: The examiner isn't testing your calculation; he's testing whether you memorised the formula in the correct order.

🔥 HIGH-YIELD PYQ 2

[SSC CGL Tier-II (JSO) | 2021 Official Paper | Held 10 August 2022]
The mean and median of the distribution are 10 and 12 respectively, then the mode equals to:

A. 18

B. 16

C. 14

D. 20

Target Time: 20 seconds | Frequency: 6 times in 10 years

Solution:
Using the empirical formula: Mode = 3 × Median – 2 × Mean
Mode = 3 × 12 – 2 × 10 = 36 – 20 = 16

Correct Answer: B. 16

🧠 What the paper-setter is doing:
This is the classic "flipped numbers" test. A panicked mind might subtract 3 × Mean from 2 × Median instead. The year-on-year repetition of this identical structure (only the numbers change) means the examiner is rewarding rote accuracy. Pro move: Memorise it as "Mode = 3Mdn – 2Mn"—alphabetical: Mdn before Mn.

🔥 HIGH-YIELD PYQ 3

[SSC CHSL Tier-II | 10/01/2024]
The runs scored by 11 players in a cricket match are as follows:
7, 16, 121, 51, 101, 81, 1, 16, 9, 11, 16
Calculate the median of the data.

A. 51

B. 81

C. 101

D. 16

Target Time: 35 seconds | Frequency: 8 times in 10 years

Solution:
Step 1 — Sort ascending: 1, 7, 9, 11, 16, 16, 16, 51, 81, 101, 121
Step 2 — n = 11 (odd). Median position = (n+1)/2 = 6th term.
Step 3 — 6th term = 16

Correct Answer: D. 16

🧠 What the paper-setter is doing:
The most dangerous number here is 51—the mean of this dataset is approximately 39.09, and the unsorted middle number (if you take them as given) is 1, 81, or anything. The trap: students who don't sort and simply pick the "middle of the list as printed" will choose 51 or 81. The psychological hook: "Cricket runs" feels friendly and lulls you into casual solving. You must sort, always, no exceptions.

🔥 HIGH-YIELD PYQ 4

[SSC CGL Tier-II | 26/10/2023]
*A frequency distribution table has the mode = 48.5. The lower boundary of the modal class is 46.5. The frequency of the modal class is 34; the frequency of the class preceding the modal class is 32; the frequency of the class succeeding the modal class is 25. What is the width of the modal class?*

A. 12

B. 10

C. 11

D. 9

Target Time: 60 seconds | Frequency: 3 times in 8 years

Solution:
Mode = l + [(f₁ – f₀) / (2f₁ – f₀ – f₂)] × h
48.5 = 46.5 + [(34 – 32) / (2×34 – 32 – 25)] × h
48.5 = 46.5 + [2 / (68 – 57)] × h
48.5 = 46.5 + (2/11) × h
2 = (2/11) × h → h = 11

Correct Answer: C. 11

🧠 What the paper-setter is doing:
This is a reverse-engineered formula question. Instead of giving you h and asking for Mode, you're given Mode and asked for h. The trap: 90% of students practise formulas in only one direction. When the unknown shifts, panic sets in and algebraic steps are skipped. Also, the denominator calculation 2f₁ – f₀ – f₂ produces 11, which happens to be the answer—students often confuse this intermediate with h and stop early, or second-guess themselves. Lesson: Practise extracting any variable from the formula.

🔥 HIGH-YIELD PYQ 5

[SSC MTS | 19/08/2019 | Evening Shift]
Let x be the median of data: 33, 42, 28, 49, 32, 37, 52, 57, 35, 41.
*If 32 is replaced by 36 and 41 by 63, then the median of the data, so obtained, is y. What is the value of (x + y)?*

A. 77.5

B. 78.5

C. 79.5

D. 76.5

Target Time: 75 seconds | Frequency: 5 times in 8 years

Solution:
Original data: 33, 42, 28, 49, 32, 37, 52, 57, 35, 41
Sort: 28, 32, 33, 35, 37, 41, 42, 49, 52, 57
n = 10 (even). Median x = (5th + 6th)/2 = (37 + 41)/2 = 39

Modified data: Replace 32→36, 41→63
New set: 33, 42, 28, 49, 36, 37, 52, 57, 35, 63
Sort: 28, 33, 35, 36, 37, 42, 49, 52, 57, 63
Median y = (37 + 42)/2 = 39.5

x + y = 39 + 39.5 = 78.5

Correct Answer: B. 78.5

🧠 What the paper-setter is doing:
The "swap-and-compare" trap. Two medians to calculate, two sorts to perform—this is a working-memory overload attack. The natural impulse is to eyeball the middle numbers without re-sorting after replacement. If you shortcut the second sort, you'll misidentify the new middle pair. Combat: Physically cross out the old numbers and rewrite the list. Do not do it "in your head."

🔥 HIGH-YIELD PYQ 6

[SSC CPO | 10/11/2022 | Evening Shift]
Calculate the mean from the following table:

Scores	Frequency
0–10	2
10–20	4
20–30	12
30–40	21
40–50	6
50–60	3
60–70	2

A. 33.4

B. 35.0

C. 34.6

D. 32.8

Target Time: 90 seconds | Frequency: 7 times in 10 years

Solution:
Compute class marks (midpoints): 5, 15, 25, 35, 45, 55, 65.
Σf = 2+4+12+21+6+3+2 = 50
Σ(f × x) = (2×5)+(4×15)+(12×25)+(21×35)+(6×45)+(3×55)+(2×65)
= 10 + 60 + 300 + 735 + 270 + 165 + 130 = 1670
Mean = 1670/50 = 33.4

Correct Answer: A. 33.4

🧠 What the paper-setter is doing:
Seven classes, frequencies up to 21—this is a patience trap designed to make you rush and either misplace a decimal or skip the exact class mark calculation. The most common error is using (20+30)/2 = 25 correctly but then for 40–50 accidentally taking 40 as the midpoint. The psychological hook: Long tables invite rounding. Don't round. Also, the option 35.0 is perfectly placed to catch anyone who uses the class-midpoint of the modal class as the mean.

🔥 HIGH-YIELD PYQ 7

[RRB NTPC | 31 March 2016 | Shift 2]
Find the mean, mode, and median of the given data:
9, 5, 8, 9, 9, 7, 8, 9, 8

A. 8, 9, 8

B. 8, 9, 9

C. 9, 8, 8

D. 8, 8, 9

Target Time: 40 seconds | Frequency: 6 times in 10 years

Solution:
Mean: Sum = 9+5+8+9+9+7+8+9+8 = 72; n = 9; Mean = 72/9 = 8
Mode: 9 appears 4 times, 8 appears 3 times. Mode = 9
Median: Sort: 5,7,8,8,8,9,9,9,9. n=9 odd → (9+1)/2 = 5th term = 8
Order: Mean = 8, Mode = 9, Median = 8

Correct Answer: A. 8, 9, 8

🧠 What the paper-setter is doing:
The ordering trap. The question asks for "mean, mode, and median" in that specific sequence. A student who calculates correctly but writes the answer as "9, 8, 8" (median, mode, mean) or in their mental order will pick option C. Always read what sequence the question demands. This tiny detail is why statistics has a high "silly-mistake" rate.

🔥 HIGH-YIELD PYQ 8

[SSC MTS | 08/08/2019 | Morning Shift]
What is the difference of mean and median of the given data?
4, 13, 8, 15, 9, 21, 18, 23, 35, 1

A. 0.7

B. 1.3

C. 0.5

D. 1.0

Target Time: 55 seconds | Frequency: 4 times in 7 years

Solution:
Mean: Sum = 4+13+8+15+9+21+18+23+35+1 = 147. n=10. Mean = 14.7
Median: Sort: 1,4,8,9,13,15,18,21,23,35. n=10 even → (5th+6th)/2 = (13+15)/2 = 14
Difference = 14.7 – 14 = 0.7

Correct Answer: A. 0.7

🧠 What the paper-setter is doing:
This is a "quick-sum" error incubator. The numbers look small, encouraging mental math. But 4+13+8+...+35 is not instantly obvious: many mis-sum at 137 or 157. If you get mean = 13.7, difference = –0.3 (not an option, causing a loop and time loss). Also, 1.3 is the absolute difference if you accidentally swap median and mean. Hook: The data includes "1" at the end, which is easily missed when scanning for the sum.

🔥 HIGH-YIELD PYQ 9

[SSC CGL | Empirical Relation Variant]
The mean of a data is 42 and its median is 53. The mode (using empirical relation) of the data is:

A. 68

B. 75

C. 53

D. 57

Target Time: 25 seconds | Frequency: 4 times in 9 years

Solution:
Empirical relation: Mode = 3 × Median – 2 × Mean
Mode = 3 × 53 – 2 × 42 = 159 – 84 = 75

Correct Answer: B. 75

🧠 What the paper-setter is doing:
The sign-reversal trap. Here, Mean (42) < Median (53), indicating a negatively skewed distribution. Some candidates incorrectly assume Mode should lie between Mean and Median. The computed mode (75) is larger than both, which feels "wrong" intuitively, causing students to reject their own correct calculation. Rule: Trust the formula, not your gut. In a negatively skewed distribution, Mode > Median > Mean.

🔥 HIGH-YIELD PYQ 10

[Bank & Railway Composite | Data Sufficiency / Conceptual]
In a moderately skewed distribution, which of the following relationship holds?

A. Mean – Mode = 3 (Mean – Median)

B. Median – Mode = 3 (Mean – Median)

C. Mean – Median = 3 (Median – Mode)

D. Mode – Median = 3 (Mean – Median)

Target Time: 15 seconds | Frequency: 5 times in 10 years

Solution:
The empirical relationship for a moderately skewed distribution is:
Mode = 3 Median – 2 Mean
Rearranging: Mean – Mode = 3 (Mean – Median)

Correct Answer: A. Mean – Mode = 3 (Mean – Median)

🧠 What the paper-setter is doing:
This is the "symbol-shuffle" trap. All four options look plausible to an unprepared eye. The examiner is testing whether you have derived the relationship or merely glanced at it. If you memorised the formula in its standard form (Mode = 3Mdn – 2Mn), you can test each option by plugging in simple numbers (say, Mean=4, Median=5, then Mode should be 7). For option A: 4 – 7 = 3(4 – 5) → –3 = –3 ✓. For others, the equality fails. Strategy: Don't memorise rearranged forms—just know the parent formula.

🎯 Your 48-Hour Spaced Repetition Checklist

☐ Solve PYQ 4 again (widest trap range; if you get 11, you've mastered grouped-data mode).

☐ Write the empirical formula three times on a blank sheet without peeking.

☐ Sort a random 10-number list, find median, then change two numbers and re-sort—time yourself.

☐ Recall: What is the exact sequence of terms in the grouped median formula? (l, n/2, cf, f, h).

☐ Solve one "find the mean from a frequency table" question with at least 7 classes, under 90 seconds.

The candidates who beat statistics are not the ones who studied the most hours. They are the ones who walked into the exam knowing exactly how the paper-setter planned to trick them—and refused to be tricked.
