🃏 Flashcards – Statistics

Card 1
Front: What is the one number that can turn a room full of common people into billionaires on paper?
Back: The mean (average). A single extreme value (like a billionaire’s income) pulls the mean up and makes it a liar.

Card 2
Front: If your maths marks are 5, 5, 5, 5, 5, what is the mean, median, and mode?
Back: All three are 5. When all values are identical, the three musketeers agree perfectly.

Card 3
Front: Why do governments report median household income, not mean income?
Back: Because a few ultra‑rich can skyrocket the mean, making the whole country look wealthy. The median stays unaffected by extremes.

Card 4
Front: A shoe company wants to know which size to manufacture most. Which central tendency should they use?
Back: Mode. It tells the most frequent shoe size sold—exactly what they need for production planning.

Card 5
Front: What is the class mark of the interval 20–30?
Back: 25. Class mark = (lower limit + upper limit) ÷ 2 = (20+30)/2 = 25.

Card 6
Front: Without any formula, how do you find the median of 1, 3, 5, 7, 9?
Back: Arrange them (already sorted). Since there are 5 numbers (odd), the median is the 3rd value = 5.

Card 7
Front: In a company of 10 people, 9 earn ₹20,000 and the owner earns ₹2,00,000. Mean = ₹38,000, Median = ₹20,000. Which number tells the truth about a typical employee’s salary?
Back: Median. The mean is pulled up by the owner’s high salary; the median sits calmly in the middle.

Card 9
Front: Which part of the median formula makes sure you find the correct middle class?
Back: The cumulative frequency (cf). The median class is the one where cf first becomes ≥ n/2.

Card 11
Front: How can you spot the median from an ogive (cumulative frequency curve) without any formula?
Back: Locate n/2 on the y‑axis, draw a horizontal line to the curve, then drop a vertical line down to the x‑axis. Where it hits is the median.

Card 12
Front: What does “MAD Middle Most” help you remember?
Back:

Mean = Average (All values Added then divided)

Median = middle value (after sorting)

Mode = Most Often
Think of a MAD scientist chanting “Average, Middle, Most often!”

Card 13
Front: What is the drone‑and‑postman trick for recalling the difference between mean and median?
Back: The mean is like a drone that gets pulled way up by a tall tower and gives a false high average. The median is like a postman who only cares about the middle house on the street—totally ignores the tower.

Card 14
Front: If mean > median > mode, is the data skewed left or right?
Back: Positively skewed (right‑tailed). A few high values pull the mean to the right, leaving median and mode behind.

Card 15
Front: When data is symmetric, what is the relationship between mean, median, and mode?
Back: They are equal: mean = median = mode. This is the ideal bell‑shaped curve.

Card 16
Front: You have marks: 2, 2, 2, 8, 10. Find the mode.
Back: Mode = 2. It appears three times—more than any other value. Notice how mode can be far from the centre.

Card 18
Front: What is the “middle child” of central tendency that ignores the drama of extreme values?
Back: Median. It splits the data into two equal halves and doesn’t get swayed by outliers.
