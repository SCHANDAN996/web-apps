## Top 10 High-Yield Practice Questions with Solutions

**Q1. Recurring Decimal to Fraction (Practice Question)**

Question: Simplify: (0.0347)³ + (0.9653)³ divided by [(0.0347)² − (0.0347)(0.9653) + (0.9653)²], i.e.,

$$\frac{(0.0347)^3 + (0.9653)^3}{(0.0347)^2 - (0.0347)(0.9653) + (0.9653)^2}$$

Options: (a) 0.9306 (b) 1 (c) 0 (d) 0.0347

Solution:
Let a = 0.0347 and b = 0.9653. Notice a + b = 1.
Numerator = a³ + b³, Denominator = a² – ab + b².
Recall: a³ + b³ = (a + b)(a² – ab + b²)
So (a³ + b³)/(a² – ab + b²) = a + b = 1.

Answer: (b) 1

🧠 Psychological Hook: The question looks terrifying with its long decimals, triggering panic and a calculator reflex. But it's actually a simple algebra identity in disguise. The trap: students waste 2–3 minutes on brute-force multiplication and still make errors. The topper reflex: spot that 0.0347 + 0.9653 = 1 and immediately recognise a³ + b³ form.

Source: PYQ-style

**Q2. Recurring Decimal Operations (Practice Question)**

Question: Calculate: 0.5\overline{6} − 0.7\overline{23} + 0.3\overline{9} × 0.\overline{7}

Options: (a) 0.154 (b) 0.15 (c) 0.16 (d) 0.1\overline{54}

Solution:
Step 1 — Convert each to fraction:
- 0.5\overline{6} = 5/10 + 6/90 = 51/90 = 17/30
- 0.7\overline{23} = 7/10 + 23/990 = 716/990 = 358/495
- 0.3\overline{9} = 3/10 + 9/90 = 0.4 = 2/5 (tricky: 0.3999… = 0.4)
- 0.\overline{7} = 7/9

Step 2 — Compute: 17/30 − 358/495 + (2/5)(7/9) = 17/30 − 358/495 + 14/45
LCM of 30, 495, 45 = 990:
= 561/990 − 716/990 + 308/990 = 153/990 = 17/110 = 0.1\overline{54}

Answer: (d) 0.1\overline{54}

🧠 Psychological Hook: The trap is 0.3\overline{9} — students treat it as a standard mixed recurring decimal but fail to notice the result is exactly 0.4, because 0.3999… = 0.4 mathematically. The second trap: rushing through the LCM step and picking a wrong denominator.

Source: PYQ-style

**Q3. Fraction Comparison (Practice Question)**

Question: Which is the largest? 3/5, 7/10, 11/15, 13/20

Options: (a) 11/15 (b) 13/20 (c) 7/10 (d) 3/5

Solution:
Convert to decimals: 3/5 = 0.60, 7/10 = 0.70, 11/15 ≈ 0.7333, 13/20 = 0.65. Largest is 11/15.

Answer: (a) 11/15

🧠 Psychological Hook: The visual trap — 13/20 has the largest numerator and denominator, so students instinctively pick it as "biggest." The distractor 7/10 (0.70) is close to 11/15 (0.7333), and students who approximate 11/15 as ~0.7 might wrongly select 7/10.

Source: PYQ-style

**Q4. LCM of Decimals (Practice Question)**

Question: Find the LCM of 1.2, 1.8, 2, and 2.5.

Options: (a) 25 (b) 30 (c) 90 (d) 120

Solution:
Step 1 — Remove decimals: 1.2 = 12/10, 1.8 = 18/10, 2 = 20/10, 2.5 = 25/10.
Step 2 — LCM of numerators 12, 18, 20, 25: 12 = 2²×3, 18 = 2×3², 20 = 2²×5, 25 = 5² → LCM = 2²×3²×5² = 900.
Step 3 — Divide by denominator 10: 900 ÷ 10 = 90.

Answer: (c) 90

🧠 Psychological Hook: Many students compute LCM of 12, 18, 20, 25 = 900 and stop, or pick 120. The trap is forgetting to divide back by 10 because the original numbers were decimals.

Source: PYQ-style

**Q5. Original Fraction — % Change (Practice Question)**

Question: If the numerator of a fraction is increased by 80% and the denominator is increased by 120%, the resultant fraction becomes 5/11. Find the original fraction.

Options: (a) 5/9 (b) 3/7 (c) 5/12 (d) 7/16

Solution:
Let original fraction = N/D.
Increased numerator = 1.8N, increased denominator = 2.2D.
1.8N / 2.2D = 5/11 ⇒ 9N/11D = 5/11 ⇒ 9N = 5D ⇒ N/D = 5/9.

Answer: (a) 5/9

🧠 Psychological Hook: "Increased by 80%" means becomes 180% (1.8×), NOT 0.8×. Also, students often confuse "increased by" with "increased to".

Source: PYQ-style

**Q6. BODMAS with Mixed Fractions (Practice Question)**

Question: Simplify: (3/4 of 16) ÷ (2/5 of 10) + 1/2

Options: (a) 3 (b) 3.5 (c) 4 (d) 7/2

Solution:
3/4 of 16 = 12; 2/5 of 10 = 4; 12 ÷ 4 = 3; 3 + 1/2 = 3.5.

Answer: (b) 3.5

🧠 Psychological Hook: The "of" operator is the deadliest BODMAS trap. "Of" means multiplication and has higher priority than division. Students who treat "of" as division or ignore it entirely will solve left-to-right and get a wrong answer.

Source: PYQ-style

**Q7. HCF of Decimals (Practice Question)**

Question: Find the HCF of 0.25, 2.08, and 8.1.

Options: (a) 0.01 (b) 0.02 (c) 0.05 (d) 0.1

Solution:
Equalise decimal places: 0.25, 2.08, 8.10 → multiply by 100: 25, 208, 810.
25 = 5², 208 = 2⁴×13, 810 = 2×3⁴×5 → common factor = 1.
Adjust back: HCF = 1 ÷ 100 = 0.01.

Answer: (a) 0.01

🧠 Psychological Hook: Students often compute HCF = 1 and pick option (d) 0.1, forgetting that the decimal needs to be scaled back. Another trap: not equalising decimal places first, leading to wrong integer conversion.

Source: PYQ-style

**Q8. Decimal of an Hour (Practice Question)**

Question: What decimal of an hour is a second?

Options: (a) 0.0025 (b) 0.0256 (c) 0.00027 (d) 0.000126

Solution:
1 hour = 3600 seconds. 1 second = 1/3600 of an hour = 0.0002777… ≈ 0.00027.

Answer: (c) 0.00027

🧠 Psychological Hook: The unit conversion trap: students use 1 hour = 60 seconds (wrong), getting 1/60 = 0.0167, then panic. Others divide 1 by 3600 correctly but misplace the decimal point, selecting 0.0025.

Source: PYQ-style

**Q9. Fraction Equation — Original Fraction (Practice Question)**

Question: Sum of four times a fraction and 6 times its reciprocal is 11. What is the fraction?

Options: (a) 3/4 (b) 4/3 (c) 4/7 (d) 7/4

Solution:
4x + 6/x = 11 ⇒ 4x² − 11x + 6 = 0 ⇒ (4x − 3)(x − 2) = 0 ⇒ x = 3/4 or x = 2. Since x is a fraction less than 1, x = 3/4.

Answer: (a) 3/4

🧠 Psychological Hook: The equation gives two possible answers — 3/4 and 2. The trap is not checking which answer fits the context.

Source: PYQ-style

**Q10. Algebraic Fraction Identity (Practice Question)**

Question: If (5x − y)/(5x + y) = 3/7, then the value of (4x² + y² − 4xy)/(9x² + 16y² + 24xy) is:

Options: (a) 0 (b) 3/7 (c) 18/49 (d) 1/6

Solution:
7(5x − y) = 3(5x + y) ⇒ 35x − 7y = 15x + 3y ⇒ 20x = 10y ⇒ y = 2x.
Numerator: 4x² + y² − 4xy = (2x − y)² = 0, so the fraction = 0.

Answer: (a) 0

🧠 Psychological Hook: Recognising that 4x² + y² − 4xy = (2x − y)² is the key. Students who expand and substitute blindly waste 2–3 minutes; those who see the identity solve it in 20 seconds. The trap: picking (b) 3/7 because it matches the given ratio.

Source: PYQ-style

## 🧠 Master Trap Matrix — 7 Psychological Patterns

| # | Trap Name | How It Works | Defence |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | The Identity Blind Spot | Complex expression hides a simple identity (a³+b³, (a–b)²). You brute-force. | Before solving, scan for algebraic patterns. |
| 2 | The "of" Operator Ambush | "Of" means multiply and has high priority. | VBODMAS: V→B→O→D→M→A→S. Circle every "of". |
| 3 | The Percentage Multiplier Switcheroo | "Increased by 80%" means ×1.8, not ×0.8. | Increased by P% = (100+P)/100. |
| 4 | The Decimal Scaling Amnesia | You find LCM/HCF of integers correctly, then forget to scale the result back. | Before final answer, ask: "Did I multiply or divide earlier? Reverse it." |
| 5 | The Visual Size Illusion | 13/20 looks bigger than 11/15 because numbers are larger. | Always convert to decimals or cross-multiply. |
| 6 | The 0.999… Equivalence Shock | 0.3\overline{9} = 0.3999… = 0.4. | If the repeating digit is 9, the number simplifies to a terminating decimal. |
| 7 | The Option Mirror Trap | A distractor option matches a partial result. | Solve fully, then match. |

---

**Bottom line:** In fraction-decimal questions, examiners hide a simple identity, a BODMAS priority rule, or a decimal-recurring shortcut behind a visually intimidating facade. Train your eye to see the trick before reaching for the pen.
