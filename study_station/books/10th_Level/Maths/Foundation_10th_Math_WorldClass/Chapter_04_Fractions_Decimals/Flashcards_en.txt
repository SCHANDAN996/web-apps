🃏 Flashcards – Fractions & Decimals

Card 1

Front: What does a fraction really represent?

Back: A division problem that hasn’t been solved yet. Numerator ÷ Denominator.

Card 2

Front: How do you instantly recognize a proper fraction?

Back: Numerator is smaller than denominator. Example: ⅔. Value is less than 1.

Card 3

Front: Convert 0.125 to a fraction in simplest form.

Back: 125/1000 = 1/8.

Card 4

Front: What does the number of digits after the decimal point tell you?

Back: The number of zeros in the denominator’s power of 10 (e.g., 0.007 → 7/1000, three zeros).

Card 5

Front: Compare 5/7 and 3/4 in 5 seconds. Which is larger?

Back: Cross multiply: 5×4=20, 3×7=21. 21>20, so 3/4 > 5/7.

Card 6

Front: What is the “gap” method for comparing fractions close to 1?

Back: Look at how much each fraction is less than 1. The smaller the gap, the larger the fraction. e.g., 11/12 (gap 1/12) vs 14/15 (gap 1/15) → 14/15 is larger.

Card 7

Front: What is the golden rule before adding or subtracting fractions?

Back: Make denominators the same (find LCM). Never add denominators directly.

Card 8

Front: What mnemonic saves you when dividing fractions? Explain each letter.

Back: KFC: Keep the first fraction, Flip the second, Change ÷ to ×. (Remember Colonel Sanders flipping a fraction like a piece of chicken.)

Card 9

Front: Solve ⅔ ÷ 4/5 using the KFC trick.

Back: Keep ⅔, Flip 4/5 → 5/4, Change ÷ to × → ⅔ × 5/4 = 10/12 = ⅚.

Card 10

Front: How do you multiply two fractions without hesitation?

Back: Multiply numerators together, multiply denominators together. e.g., ⅔ × 4/5 = 8/15.

Card 11

Front: What does MAD stand for, and when do you use it?

Back: MAD: Multiply whole number × denominator, Add numerator, Denominator stays same. Used to convert a mixed number (like 3⅖) to an improper fraction (17/5).

Card 12

Front: Convert 3⅖ to an improper fraction using MAD.

Back: Multiply 3×5=15, Add 2=17, Denominator 5 → 17/5.

Card 13

Front: How do you turn a pure recurring decimal like 0.6̅ into a fraction?

Back: Write the repeating digit(s) over as many 9s as the number of repeating digits. 0.6̅ = 6/9 = 2/3.

Card 14

Front: Convert 0.2̅3̅ to a fraction.

Back: 23/99 (two repeating digits, so denominator 99). No simplification possible.

Card 15

Front: What’s the trick for multiplying decimals like 0.4 × 0.02?

Back: Multiply as whole numbers (4×2=8), then count total decimal places (1+2=3), put the point: 0.008.

Card 16

Front: How do you safely divide a decimal by another decimal, e.g., 0.125 ÷ 0.5?

Back: Shift the decimal point in both numbers until the divisor is a whole number. 0.125 ÷ 0.5 → shift one place right → 1.25 ÷ 5 = 0.25.

Card 17

Front: Which mnemonic helps you add mixed numbers? (Trick question: there isn’t one, but what’s the safe method?)

Back: Convert mixed numbers to improper fractions first, then find LCM, then add. Or add whole numbers and fractions separately, but be careful of carry-over.

Card 18

Front: 0.8̅3̅ has a non-repeating digit “8” and repeating “3”. What fraction is it?

Back: Numerator = 83 – 8 = 75; Denominator = one 9 (for repeating) and one 0 (for non-repeating) = 90 → 75/90 = 5/6.

Card 19

Front: Why should you never memorize 0.333... as 1/3 without understanding? (But what’s the quick conversion?)

Back: Because understanding that 3/9 = 1/3 from the recurring decimal rule helps you convert any repeating decimal. Pure 0.3̅ = 3/9 = 1/3.

Card 20

Front: Your friend says ½ + ⅓ = ⅖ . What trap did they fall into, and what’s the right answer?

Back: They added numerators and denominators directly, which is wrong. Right method: LCM of 2,3 = 6 → 3/6 + 2/6 = 5/6.
