🪄 Unforgettable Mnemonics for Time & Work

1. The "WET" Formula

Work = Efficiency × Time
Imagine a WET cat that refuses to leave the bathtub. The longer it stays (Time) and the more vigorously it splashes (Efficiency), the more water (Work) it spills on the floor. So, Work equals Efficiency × Time — the WET cat makes a mess.

2. The Pizza Slice – One Day’s Work

One Day’s Work = 1 / Total Days
Picture a giant pizza cut into D slices. If you take D days to finish it, each day you eat exactly one slice — 1/D of the pizza. That’s your daily bite, and the whole pizza is the job.

3. Lazy Cat Method (LCM-Total Work)

L – LCM of the days
C – Calculate efficiency (Total Work ÷ Days)
F – Find combined efficiency (sum them)
T – Time = Total Work ÷ Combined efficiency

Visualize a Lazy Cat sleeping on a pile of bricks (Total Work). It yawns, counts the bricks (LCM), and lazily divides them among workers to see how fast they can finish. The cat’s name is LCFT (pronounced “licked”).

4. The Cookie Equation (Man‑Days Formula)

M₁D₁H₁ / W₁ = M₂D₂H₂ / W₂
Think of a massive cookie with layers: Men, Days, Hours, and Work. You must balance the cookie on a fork — both sides must be equal otherwise it crumbles. So, (Men₁ × Days₁ × Hours₁) / Work₁ equals the other side. That’s the Cookie Equation.

5. ET the Inverse (Efficiency & Time)

Efficiency ∝ 1/Time
Recall the movie E.T. — the alien and Elliott are inseparable but inverse buddies: when one is up, the other is down. Here, E (Efficiency) and T (Time) behave the same way — they are inversely proportional, never equal. If efficiency shoots up, time crashes down. "E.T. is inverse."

6. The Seesaw of Doom (Ratio Inversion)

Efficiency Ratio a : b → Time Ratio b : a
Picture a seesaw with a fat cat (Efficiency) and a tiny mouse (Time). If the cat’s number is bigger, the mouse’s number is smaller. Just flip the ratio to switch between efficiency and time. The cat laughs, the mouse screams, but they always balance.

7. The Wage Whisperer

Wages ∝ Work Done (not time spent)
A rich boss pays workers by the weight of the bricks they laid, not by the hours they sat around. So, shout “Work = Wealth” — the one who does more work gets more money. Never split wages based on time; split them in the ratio of (Efficiency × Days worked).

8. Alternate Days – The Tango Trick

2‑day cycle work = (A + B)
Picture a couple dancing the Tango: A and B alternate turns. In one complete dance (2 beats), they cover a certain number of steps (work). To find how many such dances (cycles) fit into the full song, count the steps per dance, then add the leftover solo steps. “Tango in twos, then solo.”

9. MWB – My Weird Brother (Men, Women, Boys)

Set up the equation: (Men × M + Women × W + Boys × B) × Days = Total Work
Imagine your weird brother collects Marbles, Walnuts, and Buttons. He fills boxes by mixing them and asks you to calculate how many boxes he can make. Always equate the total collection from different days using the same weird formula. So, whenever you see men, women, boys — it’s My Weird Brother’s problem.

10. The Half‑Efficiency Headache

“Times more efficient” handles on efficiency, not time.
If someone says “A is 50% more efficient than B,” don’t halve the days! Instead, imagine a speed knob on a mixer: turning it to 150% increases the revolutions per minute, not the time. So, efficiency multiplies by 1.5, then time divides by that factor (inverse). Use the knob for efficiency, not time.

11. The Leaving/Joining Locomotive

Work done = (segment₁) + (segment₂) + …
Picture a train journey where people get on and off. At each station, the number of passengers changes. The total distance covered is the sum of distances each group travels. In work terms, each “leg” is (number of men × days they worked). So, “Train of work segments” — add them up to reach the destination.

12. Fraction‑Flipper for Together

If A+B together do 1/T work a day, total time = T
When you add fractions like 1/a + 1/b, the result is the combined one‑day work. Then just flip it to get the total days. Think of it as a pancake: the recipe says “mix 1/a cup and 1/b cup, then flip the pancake to cook the whole job.” The flip gives you the time.

13. The Unit‑less Wonder (LCM Method)

Work = LCM of days
When days are messy (like 7, 13), fractions become a nightmare. Instead, turn the work into bricks — the LCM of the days gives the exact number of bricks that each person’s pace fits perfectly. Now you’re in a brick‑counting world with no fractions, only whole numbers. Bricks are always better than fractions!

14. The Three‑Ring Circus (A, B, C Sums)

2(A+B+C) = Sum of the three pairs
For problems like “A and B together take 12 days, B and C 15, C and A 20,” imagine three acrobats holding hands in a circle. The sum of their pair‑holds, when added, gives twice the triple‑hold. So, add all pairs → halve it → individual = triple – complementary pair. It’s a circus trick you’ll never forget.

15. The “More is Less” Mantra

More efficiency → Less time; More men → Less days
Stand before a mirror and chant: “The more I am, the faster we finish.” If the number of workers multiplies, the days shrink proportionally. It’s the simple inverse‑proportion mirror. Whatever boosts the work‑rate on one side shrinks the time on the other. Mantra: “More power, fewer hours.”
