📊 PYQ Analysis: Time & Work

Pattern Analysis (What Exams Actually Ask)

A review of SSC (CGL, CHSL), Banking (IBPS/SBI PO & Clerk), and Railway (RRB NTPC, Group D) papers shows that Time & Work is a reliably recurring topic in the Quantitative Aptitude section. Rather than chasing exact counts, focus on the *types* of questions that appear again and again:

Recurring Archetypes:

- Basic Together/Alone (most common)
- Efficiency Ratio
- Leaving/Joining Midway
- Alternate Days
- Wages
- Man-Woman-Boy (especially in Bank & Railway exams)
- M₁D₁H₁/W₁ = M₂D₂H₂/W₂ variations

The most frequently tested concepts are the LCM–Total Work method and the inverse relationship between efficiency and time. Questions that blend two concepts (e.g., efficiency + wages) are increasingly common.

Examiner's favourite traps: nearly-correct statements, confusing efficiency ratio with time ratio, forgetting work already done, and applying percentage changes to days instead of efficiency.

Top 10 High-Yield PYQ-Style Questions with Solutions

Question 1 (Basic Together/Alone)

A alone can complete a piece of work in 60 days and B alone can complete it in 30 days. They worked together for 8 days, and then A left the job. In how many more days will B complete the remaining work?

(a) 18 days (b) 15 days (c) 12 days (d) 10 days

Source: PYQ-style

Solution:

Total work = LCM(60, 30) = 60 units.

A's efficiency = 60/60 = 1 unit/day. B's efficiency = 60/30 = 2 units/day.

Combined efficiency = 3 units/day.

Work done in 8 days = 8 × 3 = 24 units.

Remaining work = 60 − 24 = 36 units.

Days for B alone = 36 / 2 = 18 days.

Answer: (a) 18 days.

🧠 Hook/Trap: Students often forget to subtract the work already done and divide the *whole* work by B's rate. The trap is miscalculating "remaining work."

Question 2 (LCM–Total Work Method)

A can do a piece of work in 10 days, B in 15 days. They work together for 3 days. How much work is left?

(a) 1/2 (b) 7/10 (c) 3/10 (d) 1/5

Source: PYQ-style

Solution:

Total work = LCM(10, 15) = 30 units.

A's efficiency = 30/10 = 3 units/day.

B's efficiency = 30/15 = 2 units/day.

Combined efficiency = 5 units/day.

Work done in 3 days = 3 × 5 = 15 units.

Work left = 30 − 15 = 15 units.

Fraction of work left = 15/30 = 1/2.

Answer: (a) 1/2.

🧠 Hook/Trap: Some students calculate "fraction of work done" and forget to subtract from 1. Others add fractions incorrectly (1/10 + 1/15 = 1/6, then 3 × 1/6 = 1/2 done, remaining = 1/2 — correct but error-prone for larger numbers).

Question 3 (Efficiency Ratio)

E is twice as efficient as F. If they both complete a work in 10 days by working together, how many days does E alone take to complete the same work?

(a) 15 days (b) 7.5 days (c) 30 days (d) 10 days

Source: PYQ-style

Solution:

Efficiency ratio E : F = 2 : 1.

Combined efficiency = 3 units.

They complete the work in 10 days → Total work = 3 × 10 = 30 units.

E alone: Time = Total work / E's efficiency = 30/2 = 15 days.

Answer: (a) 15 days.

🧠 Hook/Trap: The most common error is thinking "twice as efficient means half the time" and directly halving 10 days to get 5 days. But 10 days is the *combined* time, not F's or E's individual time. Always use the ratio method.

Question 4 (Leaving/Joining)

40 men can complete a piece of work in 18 days. 9 days after they start working together, 5 more men join them. How many more days will the work now take to complete?

(a) 8 days (b) 9 days (c) 7 days (d) 6 days

Source: PYQ-style

Solution:

Total work = 40 × 18 = 720 man-days.

Work done in first 9 days = 40 × 9 = 360 man-days.

Remaining work = 720 − 360 = 360 man-days.

Now, number of men = 40 + 5 = 45.

Days required = 360 / 45 = 8 days.

Answer: (a) 8 days.

🧠 Hook/Trap: The man-days approach is cleaner than LCM when the workforce changes midway. The classic trap: forgetting to add the 5 men and calculating 360/40 = 9 days (option b).

Question 5 (Alternate Days)

A can do a work in 5 days. B can do the same work in 15 days. If they work on alternate days starting with B, in how many days will they complete thrice the work?

(a) 20 days (b) 21 days (c) 22 days (d) 23 days

Source: PYQ-style

Solution:

Total work (original) = LCM(5, 15) = 15 units.

A's efficiency = 15/5 = 3 units/day.

B's efficiency = 15/15 = 1 unit/day.

Thrice the work = 3 × 15 = 45 units.

Work pattern (starting with B): Day 1 (B): 1 unit, Day 2 (A): 3 units. Each 2-day cycle = 4 units.

Number of full cycles = 45 ÷ 4 = 11 cycles (44 units) with remainder 1 unit.

11 cycles = 22 days.

Remainder 1 unit: Day 23 is B's turn (pattern starts with B). B does exactly 1 unit. Total = 23 days.

Answer: (d) 23 days.

🧠 Hook/Trap: The twist is "thrice the work" — students often solve for a single work. Also, misidentifying whose turn it is for the remainder day is a classic error.

Question 6 (Wages)

Hari can do a piece of work in 30 days while Giri can do the same work in 20 days. Working together they complete the work for which they are paid Rs 1000 in total. What is Hari's share?

(a) Rs 400 (b) Rs 500 (c) Rs 600 (d) Rs 300

Source: PYQ-style

Solution:

Total work = LCM(30, 20) = 60 units.

Hari's efficiency = 60/30 = 2 units/day.

Giri's efficiency = 60/20 = 3 units/day.

Ratio of efficiencies = 2 : 3.

Wages are divided in the ratio of work done. Since both work for the same duration, work ratio = efficiency ratio = 2 : 3.

Hari's share = (2/5) × 1000 = Rs 400.

Answer: (a) Rs 400.

🧠 Hook/Trap: Students sometimes divide wages in the ratio of time taken (30:20 = 3:2, giving Hari Rs 600), mistakenly thinking "more time means more money." The correct principle: wages ∝ work done ∝ efficiency (when time is equal).

Question 7 (Men, Women, Boys — Man-Days Equation)

9 men and 12 boys finish a job in 12 days. 12 men and 12 boys finish it in 10 days. In how many days will 10 men and 10 boys finish the job?

(a) 8 days (b) 10 days (c) 12 days (d) 15 days

Source: PYQ-style

Solution:

Let 1 man's 1-day work = M, 1 boy's 1-day work = B.

Total work: (9M + 12B) × 12 = (12M + 12B) × 10.

108M + 144B = 120M + 120B.

24B = 12M → M = 2B.

Total work = (9M + 12B) × 12 = (18B + 12B) × 12 = 30B × 12 = 360B.

10 men + 10 boys = 10(2B) + 10B = 30B per day.

Days = 360B / 30B = 12 days.

Answer: (c) 12 days.

🧠 Hook/Trap: This cannot be solved by taking a simple LCM of the given days because two different groups are involved. You must first set up equations to relate M and B.

Question 8 (Efficiency with Percentage — Advanced)

A is 100% more efficient than B, and C is 50% less efficient than B. Working together, they can complete a task in 20 days. In how many days will A alone complete the task?

(a) 28 days (b) 35 days (c) 42 days (d) 49 days

Source: PYQ-style

Solution:

Let B's efficiency = 2 units/day (base).

A is 100% more efficient → A's efficiency = 4 units/day.

C is 50% less efficient than B → C's efficiency = 1 unit/day.

Ratio A : B : C = 4 : 2 : 1.

Combined efficiency = 4 + 2 + 1 = 7 units/day.

Total work = 7 × 20 = 140 units.

A alone: Time = 140 / 4 = 35 days.

Answer: (b) 35 days.

🧠 Hook/Trap: "100% more efficient" means efficiency is *doubled* (2 times), and "50% less efficient" means efficiency is *halved* — these apply to efficiency, not directly to days. Confusing efficiency percentage with time percentage is the primary trap. Convert to efficiency first, then use the inverse relation with time.

Question 9 (Chain of Workers — Combined Equations)

A and C together can complete a work in 120 days, C and E together can complete the same work in 80 days, and A and E together can complete the same work in 160 days. In how many days can A, C and E together complete the same work?

(a) 120/13 days (b) 480/13 days (c) 240/13 days (d) 960/13 days

Source: PYQ-style

Solution:

Let A's, C's, and E's 1-day work be a, c, and e.

a + c = 1/120

c + e = 1/80

a + e = 1/160

Adding all three: 2(a + c + e) = 1/120 + 1/80 + 1/160.

LCM of 120, 80, 160 = 480.

1/120 = 4/480, 1/80 = 6/480, 1/160 = 3/480.

Sum = 13/480.

So 2(a + c + e) = 13/480 → a + c + e = 13/960.

Combined time = 1 / (13/960) = 960/13 days.

Answer: (d) 960/13 days.

🧠 Hook/Trap: The mistake here is forgetting to divide the sum by 2. Many students add the three equations and directly take the reciprocal, getting 480/13 days (option b). The sum is 2(a+c+e), so you must halve it.

Question 10 (Reduced Efficiency)

A and B can do a piece of work in 16 days and 15 days respectively. As they were ill, they could work at only 80% and 50% of their respective efficiencies. How many days will they take to complete the work together?

(a) 10 days (b) 12 days (c) 15 days (d) 20 days

Source: PYQ-style

Solution:

Total work = LCM(16, 15) = 240 units.

A's normal efficiency = 240/16 = 15 units/day.

A's reduced efficiency = 80% of 15 = 12 units/day.

B's normal efficiency = 240/15 = 16 units/day.

B's reduced efficiency = 50% of 16 = 8 units/day.

Combined reduced efficiency = 12 + 8 = 20 units/day.

Time = 240 / 20 = 12 days.

Answer: (b) 12 days.

🧠 Hook/Trap: A common mistake is applying the percentage reduction to the number of days instead of efficiency (e.g., making A take 16/0.8 = 20 days). Efficiency and time are inversely related — always reduce the *efficiency*, never stretch the days directly.

Summary: Psychological Traps by Category

| Trap Type | Question No. | Key Insight |
|---|---|---|
| Misidentifying remaining work | Q1, Q2 | Always subtract work done from total |
| Confusing efficiency ratio with time ratio | Q3, Q8 | They are inverses — always flip |
| Ignoring changes in workforce | Q4, Q7 | Use man-days or recalculate for each phase |
| Wrong turn on alternate days | Q5 | Track the remainder and whose turn it is |
| Dividing wages by time, not work | Q6 | Wages ∝ work done = efficiency × time |
| Forgetting to halve the sum | Q9 | When adding symmetric equations, watch the factor of 2 |
| Applying percentage to days, not efficiency | Q10 | Reduce efficiency, not days |
