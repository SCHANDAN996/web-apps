51. A can do a piece of work in 8 days and B in 12 days. With the help of C, they finish the work in 3 days. In how many days can C alone do the work?
(a) 6 days (b) 8 days (c) 9 days (d) 12 days
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is to assume C's efficiency is the difference of the two times. Take total work = LCM(8, 12) = 24 units; A does 3 and B does 2 units per day, while all three together do 24/3 = 8 units per day, so C does 8 − 3 − 2 = 3 units per day and needs 24/3 = 8 days. So the answer is 8 days.
Source: PYQ-style

52. A and B can complete a work in 10 days and 15 days respectively. They work together for 2 days, then A leaves and B alone completes the remaining work. For how many days in total did B work?
(a) 12 days (b) 11 days (c) 10 days (d) 13 days
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is to report only B's solo time of 10 days and forget the first 2 days. Total work = 30 units; A = 3, B = 2 units per day; together they do 10 units in 2 days, leaving 20 units which B finishes in 20/2 = 10 days, so B worked 2 + 10 = 12 days. So the answer is 12 days.
Source: NCERT Class 8 Mathematics

53. 16 men can complete a work in 12 days. They work for 4 days, and then 8 more men join them. In how many more days will the remaining work be completed?
(a) 5 days (b) 5⅓ days (c) 6 days (d) 6⅔ days
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is to divide the remaining work by the original 16 men instead of the new strength of 24. Total work = 16 × 12 = 192 man-days; 64 man-days are done in 4 days, leaving 128, which 24 men finish in 128/24 = 16/3 days. So the answer is 5⅓ days.
Source: PYQ-style

54. A is 25% more efficient than B, and B is 20% more efficient than C. If C alone can complete a work in 30 days, in how many days can A alone complete it?
(a) 12 days (b) 20 days (c) 18 days (d) 15 days
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is to add the percentages and take A as 45% more efficient than C, giving about 20.7 days and tempting a wrong pick. Correctly, B = 1.2C and A = 1.25 × 1.2C = 1.5C, so A's time = 30/1.5 = 20 days. So the answer is 20 days.
Source: PYQ-style

55. P, Q and R can do a work in 20, 30 and 60 days respectively. P works alone for 5 days, then Q joins and both work together for 5 days, after which R also joins. In how many more days will the remaining work be completed?
(a) 3 days (b) 3⅓ days (c) 4 days (d) 4⅔ days
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is to count the total time from the start instead of only the days after R joins. Total work = 60 units; P = 3, Q = 2, R = 1 unit per day; P alone does 15 units and P + Q do 25 units, leaving 20 units for all three at 6 units per day, i.e. 20/6 = 10/3 days. So the answer is 3⅓ days.
Source: PYQ-style

56. A and B can do a piece of work in 12 days and 15 days respectively. They begin together, but A leaves 6 days before the completion of the work. In how many days is the work completed?
(a) 16 days (b) 12 days (c) 14 days (d) 10 days
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is to subtract 6 days from a guessed total instead of setting up the equation. Total work = 60 units; A = 5, B = 4 units per day; if the work ends in x days, then 5(x − 6) + 4x = 60, giving 9x = 90 and x = 10. So the answer is 10 days.
Source: PYQ-style

57. 16 men or 24 women can do a piece of work in 20 days. In how many days will 10 men and 15 women together complete the work?
(a) 16 days (b) 15 days (c) 14 days (d) 18 days
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is to average the two given times instead of converting men into women. Since 16M × 20 = 24W × 20, we get M = 1.5W; then 10 men + 15 women = 15W + 15W = 30W, and the work of 480W takes 480/30 = 16 days. So the answer is 16 days.
Source: PYQ-style

58. A, B and C together can finish a work in 4 days. A alone can do it in 12 days and B alone in 18 days. How many days will C alone take to do the same work?
(a) 12 days (b) 10 days (c) 9 days (d) 15 days
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is to subtract the two times (12 − 4 or 18 − 4) instead of subtracting rates. Total work = LCM(4, 12, 18) = 36 units; together they do 9 units per day, A does 3 and B does 2, so C does 4 units per day and needs 36/4 = 9 days. So the answer is 9 days.
Source: NCERT Class 8 Mathematics

59. A, B and C can do a work in 10, 15 and 20 days respectively. They all work together for 2 days, then C leaves; after 1 more day B also leaves, and A alone completes the remaining work. If the total wages are ₹1500, what is A's share?
(a) ₹600 (b) ₹750 (c) ₹1050 (d) ₹900
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is to divide wages equally or by efficiency alone, ignoring that A worked more days. Total work = 60 units; A = 6, B = 4, C = 3 units per day; A works 7 days (42 units), B works 3 days (12 units), C works 2 days (6 units), so the ratio is 7 : 2 : 1 and A gets (7/10) × 1500 = ₹1050. So the answer is ₹1050.
Source: PYQ-style

60. 20 men can complete a work in 30 days. After working for 12 days, 8 men leave. In how many days will the remaining men complete the remaining work?
(a) 20 days (b) 24 days (c) 30 days (d) 28 days
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is to use 20 men for the remaining work instead of 12. Total work = 600 man-days; 240 man-days are done in 12 days, leaving 360 man-days for 12 men, which takes 360/12 = 30 days. So the answer is 30 days.
Source: PYQ-style

61. A and B can do a work in 12 days, B and C in 15 days, and C and A in 20 days. In how many days will B alone complete the work?
(a) 20 days (b) 24 days (c) 30 days (d) 36 days
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is to halve the sum of the three pair-times instead of the rates. Adding the rates, 2(A + B + C) = 1/12 + 1/15 + 1/20 = 1/5, so A + B + C = 1/10; subtracting the rate of A + C (1/20) gives B's rate 1/20, i.e. 20 days. So the answer is 20 days.
Source: PYQ-style

62. A can do a piece of work in 24 days and B in 30 days. With the help of C, they finish the work in 8 days. If the total wages are ₹6000, how much does C receive?
(a) ₹2400 (b) ₹1600 (c) ₹2000 (d) ₹1200
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is to split ₹6000 equally among the three. Total work = 120 units; A = 5, B = 4 units per day, and all three do 15 units per day, so C = 6; the work ratio is 5 : 4 : 6 and C's share is (6/15) × 6000 = ₹2400. So the answer is ₹2400.
Source: PYQ-style

63. 2 men and 3 women can complete a work in 10 days, while 3 men and 2 women can complete it in 8 days. What is the ratio of the efficiency of a man to that of a woman?
(a) 3 : 2 (b) 4 : 3 (c) 5 : 3 (d) 7 : 2
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is to compare the numbers of men and women directly (3 : 2) instead of equating total work. From (2M + 3W) × 10 = (3M + 2W) × 8 we get 20M + 30W = 24M + 16W, so 14W = 4M and M : W = 7 : 2. So the answer is 7 : 2.
Source: PYQ-style

64. A is 20% more efficient than B. B alone can complete a work in 22 days. In how many days will A and B together complete the work?
(a) 13 days (b) 11 days (c) 12 days (d) 10 days
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is to take 20% off 22 days, which wrongly gives about 17.6 days. Efficiency ratio A : B = 6 : 5; with B's rate 5 units per day the work is 110 units, and together they do 11 units per day, taking 110/11 = 10 days. So the answer is 10 days.
Source: NCERT Class 8 Mathematics

65. A, B and C can complete a work in 10, 12 and 15 days respectively. They all start together; after 2 days A leaves, after another 2 days B also leaves, and C finishes the remaining work. For how many days in total did C work?
(a) 7 days (b) 6 days (c) 8 days (d) 9 days
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is to count only C's solo days and forget that C worked in all phases. Total work = 60 units; A = 6, B = 5, C = 4 units per day; the first 2 days give 30 units, the next 2 days give 18 units, leaving 12 units which C does in 3 days, so C worked 2 + 2 + 3 = 7 days. So the answer is 7 days.
Source: PYQ-style

66. 30 men can complete a piece of work in 20 days. 20 women can complete the same work in 30 days. In how many days will 12 men and 18 women together complete the work?
(a) 20 days (b) 18 days (c) 15 days (d) 24 days
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is to add the two times or average them. Total work = 30 × 20 = 600 man-days = 20 × 30 = 600 woman-days, so one man equals one woman; 12 men + 18 women = 30 workers, giving 600/30 = 20 days. So the answer is 20 days.
Source: PYQ-style

67. A, B and C can do a piece of work in 10, 12 and 15 days respectively. They work on alternate days — A on the first day, B on the second, C on the third — repeating the cycle. In how many days will the work be completed?
(a) 10 days (b) 11 days (c) 12 days (d) 13 days
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is to add their combined one-day rate and divide, ignoring the alternation. Total work = 60 units; A = 6, B = 5, C = 4 units per day, so each 3-day cycle completes 15 units; after 4 cycles, i.e. 12 days, exactly 60 units are done. So the answer is 12 days.
Source: PYQ-style

68. A, B and C can complete a work in 20, 30 and 60 days respectively. They start together, but A leaves 4 days before the completion of the work. In how many total days is the work completed?
(a) 10 days (b) 11 days (c) 13 days (d) 12 days
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is to assume all three work for the full duration. Total work = 60 units; A = 3, B = 2, C = 1 unit per day; if the work ends in x days, then 3(x − 4) + 2x + x = 60, giving 6x = 72 and x = 12. So the answer is 12 days.
Source: PYQ-style

69. A and B together can complete a work in 12 days, B and C in 15 days, and C and A in 20 days. In how many days can B alone complete the work?
(a) 20 days (b) 18 days (c) 15 days (d) 24 days
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is to take B's time as the average of the three pair-times. Adding rates, 2(A + B + C) = 1/12 + 1/15 + 1/20 = 1/5, so A + B + C = 1/10; B's rate = 1/10 − 1/20 = 1/20, so B alone takes 20 days. So the answer is 20 days.
Source: PYQ-style

70. The efficiency of A is 25% less than that of B. B alone can complete a work in 24 days. In how many days will A alone complete the work?
(a) 28 days (b) 32 days (c) 30 days (d) 36 days
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is to add 25% of 24 days, giving 30 days, but efficiency and time are inversely related. Efficiency ratio A : B = 3 : 4, so time ratio is 4 : 3; B's 24 days correspond to 3 parts, hence A takes (4/3) × 24 = 32 days. So the answer is 32 days.
Source: NCERT Class 8 Mathematics

71. 4 men or 8 women can do a piece of work in 15 days. In how many days will 2 men and 4 women complete the same work?
(a) 12 days (b) 15 days (c) 18 days (d) 20 days
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is to halve the time because the group looks smaller. Since 4M = 8W, one man equals two women, so 2 men + 4 women = 8 women, exactly the group that does the work in 15 days. So the answer is 15 days.
Source: NCERT Class 8 Mathematics

72. A and B can do a piece of work in 14 days and 21 days respectively. They start together, but after 4 days A leaves and B alone completes the remaining work. In how many total days was the work finished?
(a) 12 days (b) 13 days (c) 14 days (d) 15 days
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is to report only B's solo time of 11 days. Total work = 42 units; A = 3, B = 2 units per day; in 4 days together they finish 20 units, leaving 22 units which B does in 11 days, so the total is 4 + 11 = 15 days. So the answer is 15 days.
Source: NCERT Class 8 Mathematics

73. The efficiency ratio of A, B and C is 2 : 3 : 4. They together complete a work and receive wages of ₹2700. How much does B receive?
(a) ₹600 (b) ₹1200 (c) ₹900 (d) ₹1500
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is to divide ₹2700 equally into three parts of ₹900 each by luck — the method must use the ratio. Wages follow work done, so B's share = (3/(2 + 3 + 4)) × 2700 = (3/9) × 2700 = ₹900. So the answer is ₹900.
Source: PYQ-style

74. A alone can do a work in 30 days. B is 50% more efficient than A. In how many days will they together complete the work?
(a) 10 days (b) 18 days (c) 15 days (d) 12 days
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is to halve 30 days, giving 15 days, which ignores that B is faster than A. Efficiency ratio A : B = 2 : 3; taking A's rate as 2 units per day, the work is 60 units and together they do 5 units per day, taking 60/5 = 12 days. So the answer is 12 days.
Source: NCERT Class 8 Mathematics

75. 15 men can do a piece of work in 20 days. How many more men should be added to complete the same work in 12 days?
(a) 5 (b) 15 (c) 10 (d) 25
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is to answer 25, which is the total men required, not the extra men. Total work = 15 × 20 = 300 man-days; to finish in 12 days needs 300/12 = 25 men, so the extra men required are 25 − 15 = 10. So the answer is 10.
Source: NCERT Class 8 Mathematics
