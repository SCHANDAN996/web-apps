101. A and B together can do a work in 15 days. B alone can do it in 20 days. In how many days can A alone do it?
(a) 60 days (b) 40 days (c) 50 days (d) 30 days
Answer: (a)
Solution: A's 1-day work = 1/15 − 1/20 = (4 − 3)/60 = 1/60. The trap is subtracting the days directly (20 − 15 = 5), which is meaningless; only rates can be subtracted. So the answer is 60 days.
Source: NCERT Class 8 Mathematics

102. A is thrice as efficient as B. Working together they can finish a work in 12 days. In how many days can A alone finish it?
(a) 14 days (b) 16 days (c) 18 days (d) 20 days
Answer: (b)
Solution: Efficiency ratio A : B = 3 : 1, so combined efficiency is 4 units/day and total work = 4 × 12 = 48 units. A alone needs 48/3 = 16 days; the trap is dividing 12 by 3 to get 4 days, which ignores that the 12 days is the combined time. So the answer is 16 days.
Source: NCERT Class 8 Mathematics

103. 20 men can do a work in 25 days. After working for 5 days, 5 more men are hired. In how many total days will the work be completed?
(a) 21 days (b) 22 days (c) 23 days (d) 24 days
Answer: (a)
Solution: Total work = 20 × 25 = 500 man-days; work done in 5 days = 100, leaving 400 for 25 men, which takes 400/25 = 16 more days. The trap is computing only the remaining 16 days and forgetting the first 5. Total = 5 + 16, so the answer is 21 days.

104. A can do a work in 10 days, B in 15 days. They start together, but after 3 days A leaves. In how many more days will B complete the remaining work?
(a) 5.5 days (b) 6 days (c) 8 days (d) 7.5 days
Answer: (d)
Solution: Take work = LCM(10, 15) = 30 units; A does 3/day, B does 2/day. In 3 days together they finish 15 units, leaving 15 units for B alone at 2/day. The trap is dividing 15 by the combined rate 5, giving 3 days. So the answer is 7.5 days.
Source: NCERT Class 8 Mathematics

105. 10 men can do a piece of work in 12 days. How many men are needed to complete the same work in 8 days?
(a) 12 (b) 16 (c) 15 (d) 18
Answer: (c)
Solution: Men and days are inversely proportional: 10 × 12 = M × 8, so M = 120/8 = 15. The trap is using direct proportion (10 × 8/12), which gives a smaller number when fewer days actually need more men. So the answer is 15 men.
Source: NCERT Class 8 Mathematics

106. A can do a work in 12 days, B in 18 days. They work on alternate days, starting with B. In how many days will the work be completed?
(a) 14.5 days (b) 15 days (c) 15.5 days (d) 14 days
Answer: (a)
Solution: Work = LCM(12, 18) = 36 units; A = 3/day, B = 2/day. Each 2-day cycle completes 5 units, so 7 cycles (14 days) finish 35 units; the last 1 unit falls on B's turn and takes 1/2 day. The trap is assuming A takes the last turn, which would give 14⅓ days. So the answer is 14.5 days.
Source: PYQ-style

107. A can do a certain work in 20 days. B is 25% more efficient than A. In how many days will B alone do the same work?
(a) 18 days (b) 15 days (c) 16 days (d) 22 days
Answer: (c)
Solution: Efficiency ratio A : B = 100 : 125 = 4 : 5, so time ratio is 5 : 4. A's 20 days correspond to 5 parts, hence 1 part = 4 days and B needs 4 × 4 = 16 days. The trap is reducing 20 by 25% to get 15 days; efficiency and time are inversely, not directly, related. So the answer is 16 days.
Source: NCERT Class 8 Mathematics

108. A, B and C together can complete a work in 6 days. A alone can do it in 18 days, B alone in 36 days. In how many days can C alone do the work?
(a) 9 days (b) 12 days (c) 10 days (d) 15 days
Answer: (b)
Solution: Work = LCM(6, 18, 36) = 72 units; combined rate = 12/day, A = 4/day, B = 2/day, so C = 12 − 4 − 2 = 6/day and needs 72/6 = 12 days. The trap is computing 6 + 18 + 36 style combinations instead of subtracting rates. So the answer is 12 days.
Source: NCERT Class 8 Mathematics

109. A and B can do a work in 12 days, B and C in 15 days, C and A in 20 days. How many days will A, B and C together take to finish the work?
(a) 10 days (b) 8 days (c) 12 days (d) 15 days
Answer: (a)
Solution: Adding the three pair-rates: 2(A+B+C) = 1/12 + 1/15 + 1/20 = (5+4+3)/60 = 1/5, so A+B+C = 1/10 per day. The trap is forgetting the factor of 2 and answering 5 days. So the answer is 10 days.
Source: PYQ-style

110. 10 men can complete a work in 15 days. 15 women can complete the same work in 20 days. In how many days will 5 men and 10 women together complete the work?
(a) 12 days (b) 18 days (c) 15 days (d) 20 days
Answer: (c)
Solution: 10M × 15 = 15W × 20 gives 150M = 300W, so 1 man = 2 women. Then 5 men + 10 women = 20 women, and the work equals 15 × 20 = 300 woman-days, needing 300/20 = 15 days. The trap is averaging the two given times instead of converting to one workforce. So the answer is 15 days.
Source: PYQ-style

111. A can do a work in 15 days. B is 20% more efficient than A. In how many days can B alone do the same work?
(a) 10 days (b) 12 days (c) 12.5 days (d) 14 days
Answer: (c)
Solution: Efficiency ratio A : B = 100 : 120 = 5 : 6; total work = 5 × 15 = 75 units, so B needs 75/6 = 12.5 days. The trap is cutting 15 days by 20% to get 12 days — a 20% rise in efficiency is not a 20% fall in time. So the answer is 12.5 days.
Source: NCERT Class 8 Mathematics

112. A man, a woman and a boy can complete a work individually in 4 days, 6 days and 12 days respectively. How many days will they take if they all work together?
(a) 4 days (b) 2.5 days (c) 3 days (d) 2 days
Answer: (d)
Solution: Work = LCM(4, 6, 12) = 12 units; rates are 3, 2 and 1 unit/day, together 6 units/day, so time = 12/6 = 2 days. The trap is averaging 4, 6 and 12 to get about 7 days. So the answer is 2 days.
Source: NCERT Class 8 Mathematics

113. A and B together can do a work in 12 days. A alone can do it in 20 days. They work together for 6 days, then B leaves. In how many days will A alone complete the remaining work?
(a) 8 days (b) 15 days (c) 12 days (d) 10 days
Answer: (d)
Solution: Work = LCM(12, 20) = 60 units; A+B = 5/day, A = 3/day. In 6 days together they finish 30 units, leaving 30 units for A at 3/day. The trap is finding B's rate (2/day) and then using it for the remaining work even though B has left. So the answer is 10 days.
Source: NCERT Class 8 Mathematics

114. 5 men or 10 women can do a piece of work in 12 days. In how many days will 8 men and 4 women complete the same work?
(a) 5 days (b) 7 days (c) 8 days (d) 6 days
Answer: (d)
Solution: 5M = 10W gives 1 man = 2 women, so 8 men + 4 women = 20 women. Work = 10 × 12 = 120 woman-days, hence time = 120/20 = 6 days. The trap is adding 8 + 4 = 12 workers and dividing 12 days proportionally without converting men to women. So the answer is 6 days.
Source: NCERT Class 8 Mathematics

115. A and B can do a piece of work in 30 days, B and C in 40 days, A and C in 60 days. In how many days can A alone do the work?
(a) 80 days (b) 70 days (c) 60 days (d) 90 days
Answer: (a)
Solution: 2(A+B+C) = 1/30 + 1/40 + 1/60 = 9/120 = 3/40, so A+B+C = 3/80 per day. A alone = 3/80 − 1/40 = 1/80 per day. The trap is subtracting the B+C rate from the doubled sum 3/40, which gives a negative or wrong value. So the answer is 80 days.
Source: PYQ-style

116. A is 40% less efficient than B. If B alone can complete a work in 16 days, in how many days will A and B together complete it?
(a) 12 days (b) 9 days (c) 10 days (d) 14 days
Answer: (c)
Solution: Efficiency ratio A : B = 60 : 100 = 3 : 5; work = 5 × 16 = 80 units, combined rate = 8/day, so time = 80/8 = 10 days. The trap is treating "40% less efficient" as "A takes 40% more time", which misuses the percentage. So the answer is 10 days.
Source: NCERT Class 8 Mathematics

117. 12 men can do a work in 15 days. After working for 6 days, 4 men leave. In how many more days will the remaining men complete the work?
(a) 12 days (b) 16 days (c) 14 days (d) 13.5 days
Answer: (d)
Solution: Work = 12 × 15 = 180 man-days; 6 days of work by 12 men finishes 72, leaving 108 man-days for 8 men, needing 108/8 = 13.5 days. The trap is using the original 12 men for the remaining work, which gives 9 days. So the answer is 13.5 days.
Source: PYQ-style

118. A and B can do a piece of work in 25 days and 20 days respectively. They began together, but A left after some days and B alone completed the remaining work in 11 days. After how many days of working together did A leave?
(a) 4 days (b) 5 days (c) 7 days (d) 6 days
Answer: (b)
Solution: Work = LCM(25, 20) = 100 units; A = 4/day, B = 5/day. B alone did 5 × 11 = 55 units, so together they did 45 units at 9/day, i.e. 5 days. The trap is solving for B's total days (16) instead of the days they worked together. So the answer is 5 days.
Source: PYQ-style

119. A can do a work in 8 days and B can do the same work in 12 days. In how many days will they complete the work working together?
(a) 4 days (b) 4.8 days (c) 5 days (d) 6 days
Answer: (b)
Solution: Combined rate = 1/8 + 1/12 = (3 + 2)/24 = 5/24 per day, so time = 24/5 = 4.8 days. The trap is averaging 8 and 12 to get 10 days, or halving it to 5 days; rates must be added, not times. So the answer is 4.8 days.
Source: NCERT Class 8 Mathematics

120. A completes 2/3 of a work in 12 days. In how many days can he complete the whole work at the same rate?
(a) 18 days (b) 15 days (c) 20 days (d) 24 days
Answer: (a)
Solution: If 2/3 of the work takes 12 days, the whole work takes 12 × 3/2 = 18 days. The trap is computing 12 × 2/3 = 8 days, which finds the time for the remaining 1/3 logic incorrectly applied. So the answer is 18 days.
Source: NCERT Class 8 Mathematics

121. A alone can do a work in 10 days and B alone in 15 days. They work together and finish the work, earning ₹500 in total. What is A's share of the wages?
(a) ₹350 (b) ₹250 (c) ₹200 (d) ₹300
Answer: (d)
Solution: Wages are divided in the efficiency ratio 1/10 : 1/15 = 3 : 2, so A gets 3/5 × 500 = ₹300. The trap is splitting equally (₹250) or in the ratio of days 10 : 15, which reverses the shares. So the answer is ₹300.
Source: NCERT Class 8 Mathematics

122. 15 men can complete a work in 20 days. In how many days will 25 men complete the same work?
(a) 12 days (b) 14 days (c) 16 days (d) 10 days
Answer: (a)
Solution: Total work = 15 × 20 = 300 man-days; with 25 men, time = 300/25 = 12 days. The trap is direct proportion (15 × 20/25 misapplied as 25 × 20/15), which gives more days for more men. So the answer is 12 days.
Source: NCERT Class 8 Mathematics

123. A and B together can do a work in 10 days. A alone can do it in 15 days. In how many days can B alone do it?
(a) 20 days (b) 25 days (c) 30 days (d) 35 days
Answer: (c)
Solution: B's 1-day work = 1/10 − 1/15 = (3 − 2)/30 = 1/30, so B needs 30 days. The trap is subtracting the times (15 − 10 = 5 days); only work rates can be subtracted. So the answer is 30 days.

124. A works for 4 days and completes 1/3 of a work. Working at the same rate, in how many days will he complete the whole work?
(a) 10 days (b) 12 days (c) 14 days (d) 16 days
Answer: (b)
Solution: If 1/3 of the work takes 4 days, the full work takes 4 × 3 = 12 days. The trap is adding 4 + 3 = 7 or computing 4 × 1/3, both of which confuse the fraction with the rate. So the answer is 12 days.
Source: NCERT Class 8 Mathematics

125. A, B and C can do a work individually in 20 days, 30 days and 60 days respectively. In how many days will they finish the work working together?
(a) 12 days (b) 10 days (c) 15 days (d) 18 days
Answer: (b)
Solution: Combined rate = 1/20 + 1/30 + 1/60 = (3 + 2 + 1)/60 = 6/60 = 1/10 per day, so they take 10 days. The trap is averaging 20, 30 and 60 or adding them; only the rates add. So the answer is 10 days.
Source: NCERT Class 8 Mathematics
