26. A is twice as efficient as B, and B is thrice as efficient as C. If C alone can finish a work in 36 days, in how many days can A alone finish the same work?
(a) 4 days (b) 9 days (c) 6 days (d) 12 days
Answer: (c)
Solution: Combining the ratios gives A : B : C = 6 : 3 : 1, so total work = 1 × 36 = 36 units and A's time = 36/6 = 6 days. The trap is taking A as only twice C (giving 18 days) instead of six times C, so the answer is 6 days.
Source: PYQ-style

27. A, B and C can complete a work in 12, 15 and 20 days respectively. They start working together, but C leaves after 2 days. In how many more days will A and B together finish the remaining work?
(a) 4 days (b) 5 days (c) 6 days (d) 8 days
Answer: (a)
Solution: Total work = LCM(12, 15, 20) = 60 units; efficiencies are 5, 4, 3. In 2 days they do 24 units, leaving 36, and A + B do 9 units/day, so 36/9 = 4 days. The trap is dividing 36 by 12 (all three), giving 3 days, so the answer is 4 days.
Source: NCERT Class 8 Mathematics

28. 10 men can complete a piece of work in 15 days. After working for 5 days, 5 more men join them. In how many days will the remaining work be completed?
(a) 5 days (b) 6 days (c) 7 days (d) 6⅔ days
Answer: (d)
Solution: Total work = 10 × 15 = 150 man-days; 50 are done in 5 days, leaving 100 for 15 men, so time = 100/15 = 6⅔ days. The trap is using only the original 10 men, which gives 10 days, so the answer is 6⅔ days.
Source: PYQ-style

29. A and B together can do a piece of work in 20 days, B and C together in 24 days, and A and C together in 30 days. In how many days can all three together complete the work?
(a) 12 days (b) 14 days (c) 16 days (d) 18 days
Answer: (c)
Solution: Adding the three pair rates gives 2(A + B + C) = 1/20 + 1/24 + 1/30 = 15/120 = 1/8, so the trio's rate is 1/16 and they take 16 days. The trap is forgetting to halve the sum, which gives 8 days, so the answer is 16 days.
Source: PYQ-style

30. A and B can complete a work in 15 days and 10 days respectively. They start together, but B leaves after 2 days and A finishes the rest alone. For how many total days did A work?
(a) 8 days (b) 10 days (c) 12 days (d) 14 days
Answer: (c)
Solution: Total work = 30 units; A does 2/day, B does 3/day. In 2 days together they finish 10 units, leaving 20, which A does in 10 days, so A works 2 + 10 = 12 days. The trap is reporting only the 10 days A works alone, so the answer is 12 days.
Source: NCERT Class 8 Mathematics

31. The efficiency ratio of A, B and C is 3 : 5 : 7. Working together they complete a work in 6 days. In how many days will B alone complete the work?
(a) 14 days (b) 16 days (c) 20 days (d) 18 days
Answer: (d)
Solution: Total efficiency = 15 units/day, so total work = 15 × 6 = 90 units; B at 5 units/day needs 90/5 = 18 days. The trap is dividing 90 by the total 15, which just returns 6 days, so the answer is 18 days.

32. 5 men and 8 women can complete a work in 34 days, while 4 men and 18 women can complete the same work in 28 days. What is the ratio of the efficiency of a man to that of a woman?
(a) 3 : 1 (b) 4 : 1 (c) 5 : 2 (d) 2 : 1
Answer: (b)
Solution: Equating total work, 34(5M + 8W) = 28(4M + 18W) gives 170M + 272W = 112M + 504W, so 58M = 232W and M : W = 4 : 1. The trap is inverting the ratio to 1 : 4, so the answer is 4 : 1.
Source: PYQ-style

33. 3 men and 4 women can complete a work in 14 days, while 4 men and 6 women can complete the same work in 10 days. In how many days will 5 women alone complete the work?
(a) 24 days (b) 28 days (c) 26 days (d) 30 days
Answer: (b)
Solution: From 14(3M + 4W) = 10(4M + 6W) we get 2M = 4W, i.e. M = 2W, so total work = 14(6W + 4W) = 140W. Five women do 5W per day, needing 140/5 = 28 days; the trap is using 10 women (the whole first crew in woman-units), giving 14 days, so the answer is 28 days.
Source: PYQ-style

34. A and B together can do a work in 24 days, B and C together in 30 days, and C and A together in 40 days. In how many days will A, B and C together complete the work?
(a) 15 days (b) 18 days (c) 20 days (d) 22 days
Answer: (c)
Solution: 2(A + B + C) = 1/24 + 1/30 + 1/40 = 12/120 = 1/10, so the trio's daily work is 1/20 and they take 20 days. The trap is answering 10 days by forgetting the factor of 2, so the answer is 20 days.
Source: PYQ-style

35. A is 50% more efficient than B. B alone can finish a work in 20 days. In how many days will A and B together finish the work?
(a) 6 days (b) 10 days (c) 8 days (d) 12 days
Answer: (c)
Solution: Efficiency ratio A : B = 3 : 2; taking B = 2 units/day, total work = 2 × 20 = 40 units, and together they do 5 units/day, so 40/5 = 8 days. The trap is halving 20 to get 10 days, ignoring that A is faster than B, so the answer is 8 days.
Source: NCERT Class 8 Mathematics

36. 45 men can complete a work in 16 days. After how many days should 15 men leave so that the remaining work is completed by the remaining men in 18 days?
(a) 4 days (b) 3 days (c) 5 days (d) 6 days
Answer: (a)
Solution: Total work = 45 × 16 = 720 man-days; the last phase needs 30 × 18 = 540 man-days, so 180 man-days are done first by 45 men, taking 180/45 = 4 days. The trap is computing 720/45 = 16 or subtracting days wrongly, so the answer is 4 days.
Source: PYQ-style

37. A and B can complete a work in 5 days and 10 days respectively. They work on alternate days, starting with A. In how many days will the work be completed?
(a) 6 days (b) 6.5 days (c) 7 days (d) 7.5 days
Answer: (b)
Solution: Total work = 10 units; A does 2/day, B does 1/day, so each 2-day cycle completes 3 units. After 3 cycles (6 days) 9 units are done, and A finishes the last unit in half a day, giving 6.5 days; the trap is rounding up to 7 full days, so the answer is 6.5 days.
Source: PYQ-style

38. A and B can complete a work in 12 days and 18 days respectively. They work together for 3 days, then B leaves; A works alone for 4 more days and leaves; C alone finishes the rest in 6 days. In how many days can C alone complete the whole work?
(a) 20 days (b) 30 days (c) 28 days (d) 24 days
Answer: (d)
Solution: Total work = 36 units; A = 3/day, B = 2/day. Together they do 15 units in 3 days, A adds 12 units in 4 days, leaving 9 units which C does in 6 days, so C's rate is 1.5/day and C alone needs 36/1.5 = 24 days. The trap is crediting C with all 27 remaining units, so the answer is 24 days.
Source: PYQ-style

39. The efficiency of A is 40% more than that of B. B alone can do a work in 35 days. In how many days will A alone do the same work?
(a) 25 days (b) 20 days (c) 28 days (d) 30 days
Answer: (a)
Solution: Efficiency ratio A : B = 7 : 5, so time ratio is 5 : 7; with 7 parts = 35 days, one part = 5 days and A takes 25 days. The trap is reducing 35 by 40% to get 21 days, which misuses the percentage on time instead of efficiency, so the answer is 25 days.

Answer: (a)
Solution: Total work = 15 × 12 = 180 man-days; 60 are done in 4 days, leaving 120 for 20 men, so 120/20 = 6 days. The trap is keeping 15 men, which gives 8 days, so the answer is 6 days.

41. A, B and C can complete a work in 10, 12 and 15 days respectively. On the first day all three work together; on the second day only A and B work. What fraction of the work remains after two days?
(a) 19/30 (b) 11/30 (c) 13/30 (d) 17/30
Answer: (d)
Solution: Total work = 60 units; rates are 6, 5, 4. Day 1 completes 15 units and day 2 completes 11, so 26 units are done and 34 remain, i.e. 34/60 = 17/30. The trap is reporting the work done (13/30) instead of the work left, so the answer is 17/30.

42. A, B and C together can do a piece of work in 8 days. A alone can do it in 24 days and B alone in 36 days. How many days will C alone take?
(a) 18 days (b) 12 days (c) 20 days (d) 24 days
Answer: (a)
Solution: Total work = LCM(8, 24, 36) = 72 units; the trio's rate is 9/day, A's is 3 and B's is 2, so C's rate is 4/day and C needs 72/4 = 18 days. The trap is subtracting days (24 − 8 etc.) instead of rates, so the answer is 18 days.
Source: NCERT Class 8 Mathematics

43. A and B together can do a work in 6 days. B alone can do it in 10 days. A alone worked for 3 days and then left. In how many days will B alone complete the remaining work?
(a) 5 days (b) 8 days (c) 7 days (d) 6 days
Answer: (b)
Solution: Total work = 30 units; A + B = 5/day and B = 3/day, so A = 2/day. A does 6 units in 3 days, leaving 24, which B finishes in 24/3 = 8 days. The trap is using A's rate for the remaining work, giving 12 days, so the answer is 8 days.
Source: NCERT Class 8 Mathematics

44. 6 men and 8 women can complete a work in 10 days, while 26 men and 48 women can complete the same work in 2 days. Find the efficiency ratio of a man to a woman.
(a) 2 : 1 (b) 3 : 2 (c) 4 : 3 (d) 5 : 4
Answer: (a)
Solution: Equating work, 10(6M + 8W) = 2(26M + 48W) gives 60M + 80W = 52M + 96W, so 8M = 16W and M : W = 2 : 1. The trap is comparing headcounts (6 : 8 = 3 : 4) instead of solving the equation, so the answer is 2 : 1.
Source: PYQ-style

45. The efficiency ratio of A, B and C is 3 : 2 : 1. Working together they finish a work in 6 days. In how many days will C alone finish the work?
(a) 36 days (b) 30 days (c) 40 days (d) 48 days
Answer: (a)
Solution: Combined efficiency = 6 units/day, so total work = 6 × 6 = 36 units; C at 1 unit/day needs 36 days. The trap is multiplying 6 days by 3 (C's "share" misread), giving 18 days, so the answer is 36 days.

46. A is thrice as good a workman as B, and together they finish a piece of work in 12 days. In how many days will A alone finish the work?
(a) 15 days (b) 20 days (c) 18 days (d) 16 days
Answer: (d)
Solution: Efficiency ratio A : B = 3 : 1, so together they do 4 units/day and total work = 48 units; A alone needs 48/3 = 16 days. The trap is taking one-third of 12 (4 days) or three-fourths blindly, so the answer is 16 days.

47. A, B and C together complete a work and are paid ₹660. A and B together do 7/11 of the work, and B and C together do 6/11 of the work. How much should A be paid?
(a) ₹360 (b) ₹300 (c) ₹240 (d) ₹220
Answer: (b)
Solution: Out of 11 parts, C = 11 − 7 = 4 and A = 11 − 6 = 5, so A's share = (5/11) × 660 = ₹300. The trap is paying A for the 7/11 done by A and B together (₹420), so the answer is ₹300.

50. A, B and C together can do a piece of work in 6 days. A alone can do it in 12 days and B alone in 18 days. In how many days can C alone do it?
(a) 24 days (b) 27 days (c) 30 days (d) 36 days
Answer: (d)
Solution: Total work = LCM(6, 12, 18) = 36 units; the trio's rate is 6/day, A's is 3 and B's is 2, so C's rate is 1/day and C needs 36 days. The trap is computing 1/6 − 1/12 − 1/18 incorrectly as 1/12 (12 days), so the answer is 36 days.
