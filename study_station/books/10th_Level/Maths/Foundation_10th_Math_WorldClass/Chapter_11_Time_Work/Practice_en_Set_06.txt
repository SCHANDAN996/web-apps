126. A and B can do a piece of work in 12 days and 15 days respectively. They work together for 4 days, then A leaves. B works for 4 more days and then B also leaves. C alone completes the remaining work in 2 days. In how many days can C alone do the whole work?
(a) 12 days (b) 15 days (c) 20 days (d) 24 days
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is to count only the first 4 days of joint work and forget B's extra 4 solo days. Total work = LCM(12, 15) = 60 units, so A does 5 and B does 4 units per day. Joint work = 9 × 4 = 36, then B alone = 4 × 4 = 16, leaving 8 units which C does in 2 days, so C's rate is 4 units per day and C alone needs 60/4 = 15 days.
Source: PYQ-style

127. A is 20% more efficient than B, and B is 25% more efficient than C. C alone can do a work in 40 days. They all start together, but after 3 days A leaves, and after another 3 days B also leaves. In how many total days will the work be completed?
(a) 24 days (b) 28 days (c) 30 days (d) 26 days
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is to merge the two percentage increases into one wrong ratio; correctly A : B = 6 : 5 and B : C = 5 : 4, giving A : B : C = 6 : 5 : 4. Total work = 4 × 40 = 160 units; first 3 days give 15 × 3 = 45, next 3 days give 9 × 3 = 27, leaving 88 units for C alone at 4 per day = 22 days. Total time = 3 + 3 + 22 = 28 days.

128. A can do a work in 15 days and B in 20 days. They work together for 5 days, after which A leaves. In how many more days will B finish the remaining work?
(a) 9 days (b) 7 days (c) 8⅓ days (d) 10 days
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is to compute the total time instead of only the extra days B needs after A leaves. Total work = LCM(15, 20) = 60 units, A = 4 and B = 3 units per day; in 5 days together they finish 35 units, leaving 25 units. B alone needs 25/3 = 8⅓ more days, so the answer is 8⅓ days.

129. The efficiency ratio of A, B and C is 4 : 3 : 2. They complete a work together, all working for the full duration, and receive ₹2700 in total. What is A's share?
(a) ₹900 (b) ₹1500 (c) ₹1200 (d) ₹1350
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is to divide ₹2700 equally among the three, ignoring that wages follow work done. Since all work the same number of days, the wage ratio equals the efficiency ratio 4 : 3 : 2, with 9 total parts. A's share = (4/9) × 2700 = ₹1200, so the answer is ₹1200.
Source: PYQ-style

130. Pipe A can fill a tank in 10 hours and pipe B in 15 hours, while a leak at the bottom can empty the full tank in 30 hours. If all three are opened together, in how many hours will the tank be filled?
(a) 7½ hours (b) 8 hours (c) 9 hours (d) 6 hours
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is to add the leak's rate instead of subtracting it, which gives an impossibly small time. Net rate = 1/10 + 1/15 − 1/30 = (3 + 2 − 1)/30 = 4/30 = 2/15 of the tank per hour. Time = 15/2 = 7½ hours, so the answer is 7½ hours.
Source: PYQ-style

131. 3 men, 2 women and 1 boy can complete a work in 10 days. 2 men, 3 women and 1 boy can complete it in 12 days. 1 man, 2 women and 3 boys can complete it in 15 days. In how many days will 1 man, 1 woman and 1 boy together complete the work?
(a) 24 days (b) 22 days (c) 20 days (d) 26 days
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is to assume a man, a woman and a boy are equally efficient. With total work 60 units, the three group rates are 6, 5 and 4 units per day; subtracting pairs of equations gives M = W + 1 and W = B, and solving yields M = 1.5, W = 0.5, B = 0.5. One of each does 2.5 units per day, so the time is 60/2.5 = 24 days.

132. A, B and C can complete a work in 10, 12 and 15 days respectively. They all work together for 2 days, after which A leaves. In how many total days will the work be completed?
(a) 6 days (b) 5 days (c) 5⅓ days (d) 4⅔ days
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is to report only the time after A leaves and forget the first 2 joint days. Total work = LCM(10, 12, 15) = 60 units, with rates A = 6, B = 5, C = 4; in 2 days all three finish 30 units, leaving 30 units for B and C at 9 per day = 3⅓ days. Total time = 2 + 3⅓ = 5⅓ days, so the answer is 5⅓ days.
Source: PYQ-style

133. A can do a work in 12 days and B in 16 days. They work on alternate days, starting with A. In how many days will the work be completed?
(a) 13⅔ days (b) 14 days (c) 13½ days (d) 13⅓ days
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is to stop after the full 2-day cycles and ignore the leftover fraction of a day. Total work = LCM(12, 16) = 48 units, A = 4 and B = 3 per day, so each 2-day cycle completes 7 units; 6 cycles (12 days) finish 42 units, leaving 6. On day 13 A does 4, leaving 2, which B finishes in 2/3 of day 14, so the total time is 13⅔ days.

134. A and B can do a piece of work in 24 days and 30 days respectively. They start working together. After some days, A leaves. B works alone for 1 day, then C joins B and they complete the remaining work in 5 days. If C alone takes 40 days to do the work, after how many days from the start did A leave?
(a) 7 days (b) 8 days (c) 10 days (d) 9 days
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is to forget B's single solo day when setting up the equation. Total work = LCM(24, 30, 40) = 120 units, with A = 5, B = 4, C = 3; if A worked x days with B, then 9x + 4 + (4 + 3) × 5 = 120, giving 9x = 81. So A left after 9 days, and the answer is 9 days.

135. A is 20% more efficient than B. B is 25% more efficient than C. Working together they can finish a work in 15 days. In how many days will A and C together finish the work?
(a) 21½ days (b) 20 days (c) 22½ days (d) 24 days
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is to average the two percentages and get a wrong combined ratio; correctly A : B : C = 6 : 5 : 4. Combined efficiency is 15 units per day, so total work = 15 × 15 = 225 units. A and C together do 10 units per day, needing 225/10 = 22½ days, so the answer is 22½ days.
Source: PYQ-style

136. A certain number of men can complete a piece of work in some days. If 2 men leave, the work takes 8 more days to complete. If 4 men join, the work is completed 4 days earlier. How many men are there initially?
(a) 3 (b) 6 (c) 5 (d) 4
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is to treat the change in days as proportional to the change in men directly. With M men and D days, (M − 2)(D + 8) = MD gives 4M − D = 8, and (M + 4)(D − 4) = MD gives D = M + 4; substituting yields 3M = 12. So the initial number of men is 4.
Source: PYQ-style

137. A, B and C can do a work in 10, 15 and 20 days respectively. They all start together. After 2 days A leaves. B and C continue for 3 more days, then B leaves and C finishes the work. If the total wage for the work is ₹2100, what is B's share?
(a) ₹500 (b) ₹700 (c) ₹800 (d) ₹600
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is to split the wage by days worked instead of by units of work done. Total work = 60 units with rates A = 6, B = 4, C = 3; A works 2 days (12 units), B works 5 days (20 units), and C does the remaining 28 units, giving the ratio 3 : 5 : 7. B's share = (5/15) × 2100 = ₹700, so the answer is ₹700.
Source: PYQ-style

138. A and B can do a work in 12 days and 15 days respectively. They work together for 4 days, then A leaves. B works alone for 4 days, then A rejoins and they complete the work. For how many days did A work in total?
(a) 4⁸⁄₉ days (b) 4⅔ days (c) 5⅓ days (d) 5⁵⁄₉ days
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is to count A's absence period as working time. Total work = 60 units, A = 5, B = 4; the first 4 joint days finish 36 units, B's 4 solo days finish 16, leaving 8 units done jointly in 8/9 of a day. A worked 4 + 8/9 = 4⁸⁄₉ days, so the answer is 4⁸⁄₉ days.
Source: PYQ-style

139. Pipes A, B and C can fill a tank in 6 hours, 8 hours and 12 hours respectively. They are opened simultaneously. After 2 hours, B is closed. After another 1 hour, C is also closed. In how many total hours is the tank filled?
(a) 3⅓ hours (b) 2½ hours (c) 3 hours (d) 4 hours
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is to assume more time is needed after C closes without checking whether the tank is already full. Combined rate of all three is (4 + 3 + 2)/24 = 3/8 per hour, filling 3/4 of the tank in 2 hours; then A and C at 1/4 per hour fill the remaining 1/4 in exactly 1 hour. The tank is full in 3 hours, so the answer is 3 hours.
Source: PYQ-style

140. A and B can do a work in 12 days and 15 days respectively. They work on alternate days, starting with A. After some days, B leaves, and the remaining work is finished by A alone in 3 days. For how many days did they work alternately before B left?
(a) 8 days (b) 12 days (c) 10 days (d) 14 days
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is to count the final 3 solo days of A as part of the alternate period. Total work = 60 units, A = 5, B = 4; each 2-day alternate cycle completes 9 units, and A's last 3 days account for 15 units, so the cycles completed 45 units, i.e. 5 cycles. The alternate work lasted 2 × 5 = 10 days, so the answer is 10 days.

141. A is 25% less efficient than B. C is 20% more efficient than A. Together they can complete a work in 10 days. In how many days will B alone complete the work?
(a) 25 days (b) 30 days (c) 28 days (d) 26½ days
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is to take C as 20% more efficient than B instead of than A. Taking B = 20 units, A = 15 and C = 18, so the combined rate is 53 units per day and total work = 530 units. B alone needs 530/20 = 26½ days, so the answer is 26½ days.

142. A and B together can do a piece of work in 12 days. A alone can do it in 24 days. They start together for 4 days, then B leaves. A works alone for 4 days, then C joins A and they finish the remaining work in 6 days. In how many days can C alone do the work?
(a) 24 days (b) 28 days (c) 30 days (d) 20 days
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is to assume C is faster than A because the last stretch finishes quickly. Total work = 24 units, A + B = 2 and A = 1, so B = 1; the first 4 days finish 8 units, A's next 4 days finish 4, leaving 12 units done by A and C in 6 days, so their combined rate is 2 and C's rate is 1. C alone needs 24/1 = 24 days, so the answer is 24 days.

143. The efficiency ratio of A and B is 5 : 4. Together they can complete 2/3 of a work in 8 days. In how many days will A alone complete the full work?
(a) 20 days (b) 21⅗ days (c) 24 days (d) 25 days
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is to scale 8 days up to the full work (12 days) and hand that to A, forgetting B's contribution. Combined rate is 9 units per day, so 8 days give 72 units = 2/3 of the work, making the total 108 units. A alone at 5 units per day needs 108/5 = 21⅗ days, so the answer is 21⅗ days.
Source: PYQ-style

144. 18 men or 27 women can complete a work in 20 days. In how many days will 12 men and 18 women complete the same work?
(a) 12 days (b) 15 days (c) 18 days (d) 20 days
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is to add 12 and 18 and compare directly with 18 or 27 without converting units. Since 18 men = 27 women, 1 man = 1.5 women, so 12 men + 18 women = 36 women-equivalents against a total of 27 × 20 = 540 woman-days. Time = 540/36 = 15 days, so the answer is 15 days.

145. A and B can do a piece of work in 30 days, B and C in 12 days, A and C in 15 days. They all start together. After 5 days, A leaves. B and C continue for 3 more days, then B leaves. In how many days will C alone complete the remaining work?
(a) 3 days (b) 4 days (c) 6 days (d) 5 days
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is to use the pair rates directly instead of first extracting individual rates. Adding the pair rates gives 2(A + B + C) = 11/60, so individual rates on 120 units are A = 1, B = 3, C = 7; the first 5 days finish 55 units and the next 3 days finish 30, leaving 35 units. C alone needs 35/7 = 5 days, so the answer is 5 days.
Source: PYQ-style

146. A can do a work in 20 days, B in 30 days. They work together for 4 days. Then C, whose efficiency is the average of A and B, joins them. In how many total days will the work be finished?
(a) 8⅓ days (b) 9 days (c) 10 days (d) 9⅓ days
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is to take C's rate as the sum of A's and B's rates instead of their average. A + B = 1/12 of the work per day, so in 4 days they finish 1/3; C's rate is 1/24, making the triple rate 1/8 per day, and the remaining 2/3 takes 16/3 = 5⅓ days. Total time = 4 + 5⅓ = 9⅓ days, so the answer is 9⅓ days.
Source: PYQ-style

147. A and B can do a work in 12 days and 18 days respectively. They work on alternate days, starting with A. On the days B works, he is assisted by C who is half as efficient as B. In how many days will the work be completed?
(a) 10 days (b) 11 days (c) 13 days (d) 12 days
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is to ignore C and compute the plain alternate-day cycle of A and B. Total work = 36 units, A = 3, B = 2, C = 1; odd days give 3 units and even days give B + C = 3 units, so each 2-day cycle completes 6 units. Six cycles finish all 36 units, so the work is completed in 12 days.

148. The efficiency ratio of A, B and C is 7 : 5 : 4. They work together to complete a work for ₹8400. A and B work on all days, but C works only on every alternate day. What is C's share?
(a) ₹1200 (b) ₹1000 (c) ₹1400 (d) ₹1600
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is to divide ₹8400 in the ratio 7 : 5 : 4 directly, ignoring that C works only half the days. With total duration T, the work ratio is 7T : 5T : 4 × (T/2) = 7 : 5 : 2, with 14 parts. C's share = (2/14) × 8400 = ₹1200, so the answer is ₹1200.
Source: PYQ-style

149. A and B together can do a work in 25 days, B and C in 20 days, C and A in 15 days. They work in a cyclic order: A alone on day 1, B alone on day 2, C alone on day 3, repeating. In how many days will the work be completed?
(a) 38¹²⁄₂₃ days (b) 37½ days (c) 36 days (d) 40 days
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is to assume the work ends exactly at the close of a 3-day cycle. Adding pair rates gives 2(A + B + C) = 47/300, so on 600 units the rates are A = 17, B = 7, C = 23 and each cycle completes 47 units; 12 cycles (36 days) finish 564, leaving 36, of which A does 17 and B does 7, leaving 12 for C. C needs 12/23 of day 39, so the total time is 38¹²⁄₂₃ days.
Source: PYQ-style

150. A certain number of men can complete a work in 24 days. They start working, but after 10 days, 5 men leave. After another 10 days, 5 more men leave, and the remaining work is completed by the remaining men in the next 10 days. Find the initial number of men.
(a) 20 (b) 25 (c) 30 (d) 35
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is to assume the workforce drops only once instead of in two stages. With N initial men, total work = 24N; the three 10-day phases contribute 10N, 10(N − 5) and 10(N − 10), and setting their sum equal to 24N gives 30N − 150 = 24N, so 6N = 150. The initial number of men is 25, so the answer is 25.
Source: PYQ-style
