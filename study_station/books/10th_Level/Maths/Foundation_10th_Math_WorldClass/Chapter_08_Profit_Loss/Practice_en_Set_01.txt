Set 1: Profit & Loss Multiple Choice Questions (Easy to Medium)
Questions 1–20: Easy | Questions 21–25: Medium

1. An article was bought for ₹500 and sold for ₹600. What is the profit percentage?
(a) 20% (b) 25% (c) 15% (d) 16.67%
Answer: (a) 20%
Solution: Profit = Selling Price - Cost Price = 600 - 500 = ₹100. Profit % = (Profit / Cost Price) × 100 = (100 / 500) × 100 = 20%.
Source: SSC CGL

2. A shirt was purchased for ₹800 and sold for ₹720. What is the loss percentage?
(a) 8% (b) 10% (c) 12% (d) 15%
Answer: (b) 10%
Solution: Loss = Cost Price - Selling Price = 800 - 720 = ₹80. Loss % = (Loss / Cost Price) × 100 = (80 / 800) × 100 = 10%.
Source: SSC CHSL

3. An article is sold for ₹150 at a profit of 25%. What is the cost price of the article?
(a) ₹110 (b) ₹115 (c) ₹120 (d) ₹125
Answer: (c) ₹120
Solution: Cost Price = (Selling Price × 100) ÷ (100 + Profit %) = (150 × 100) ÷ 125 = 150 × (4/5) = ₹120.
Source: SSC MTS

4. A shopkeeper sells an article for ₹450 suffering a loss of 10%. What was the cost price of the article?
(a) ₹405 (b) ₹480 (c) ₹495 (d) ₹500
Answer: (d) ₹500
Solution: Trap alert: Option (c) ₹495 incorrectly calculates 10% on the selling price (450 + 45 = 495)! Cost Price = Selling Price ÷ (1 - Loss %) = 450 ÷ 0.90 = ₹500.
Source: SSC CGL

5. By selling a table for ₹1,320, a trader gains 10%. What is the cost price of the table?
(a) ₹1,200 (b) ₹1,188 (c) ₹1,220 (d) ₹1,250
Answer: (a) ₹1,200
Solution: Cost Price = Selling Price ÷ (1 + Profit %) = 1320 ÷ 1.10 = ₹1,200.
Source: RRB NTPC

6. A person buys a bicycle for ₹1,400 and sells it at a loss of 15%. What is the selling price of the bicycle?
(a) ₹1,160 (b) ₹1,190 (c) ₹1,210 (d) ₹1,220
Answer: (b) ₹1,190
Solution: Selling Price = Cost Price × (1 - Loss %) = 1400 × (1 - 0.15) = 1400 × 0.85 = ₹1,190.
Source: SSC GD

7. A merchant purchased an old fan for ₹450 and spent ₹50 on its repair. He then sold it for ₹600. What is his profit percentage?
(a) 15% (b) 18% (c) 20% (d) 25%
Answer: (c) 20%
Solution: Effective Cost Price = Purchase price + Repair cost = 450 + 50 = ₹500. Profit = 600 - 500 = ₹100. Profit % = (100 / 500) × 100 = 20%.
Source: SSC CHSL

8. The cost price of 15 articles is equal to the selling price of 10 articles. What is the profit percentage?
(a) 25% (b) 33.33% (c) 40% (d) 50%
Answer: (d) 50%
Solution: 15 × CP = 10 × SP => SP / CP = 15 / 10 = 3 / 2. Profit % = [(SP - CP) / CP] × 100 = [(3 - 2) / 2] × 100 = (1/2) × 100 = 50%.
Source: SSC CGL

9. If the cost price of an article is 95% of its selling price, what is the profit percentage?
(a) 5.26% (b) 5.00% (c) 4.76% (d) 5.50%
Answer: (a) 5.26%
Solution: Let Selling Price = ₹100. Then Cost Price = ₹95. Profit = 100 - 95 = ₹5. Profit % = (5 / 95) × 100 = 100/19% ≈ 5.26%.
Source: SSC MTS

10. A man sold two chairs for ₹400 each. On one chair he gained 20% and on the other he lost 20%. What was his overall gain or loss percentage?
(a) No profit no loss (b) 4% loss (c) 4% profit (d) 2% loss
Answer: (b) 4% loss
Solution: Trap alert: Option (a) 'No profit no loss' is an intuitive trap because +20% and -20% seem to balance! When two items are sold at the same selling price, one at a gain of r% and the other at a loss of r%, there is always an overall loss of (r/10)² % = (20/10)² % = 4% loss.
Source: SSC CGL

11. If the selling price of an article is 4/3 times its cost price, what is the profit percentage?
(a) 25% (b) 30% (c) 33.33% (d) 40%
Answer: (c) 33.33%
Solution: SP = (4/3) × CP => SP / CP = 4 / 3. Profit % = [(4 - 3) / 3] × 100 = 100/3% ≈ 33.33%.
Source: RRB Group D

12. A fruit vendor bought oranges at 6 for ₹5 and sold them at 5 for ₹6. What is his profit percentage?
(a) 36% (b) 40% (c) 42% (d) 44%
Answer: (d) 44%
Solution: CP of 1 orange = ₹5/6. SP of 1 orange = ₹6/5. Profit % = [(6/5 - 5/6) ÷ (5/6)] × 100 = [(36 - 25)/30 ÷ (25/30)] × 100 = (11/25) × 100 = 44%.
Source: SSC CGL

13. An article is sold for ₹178 at a loss of 11%. At what price should it be sold to earn a profit of 11%?
(a) ₹222 (b) ₹220 (c) ₹224 (d) ₹226
Answer: (a) ₹222
Solution: Cost Price = 178 ÷ (1 - 0.11) = 178 ÷ 0.89 = ₹200. Selling Price for 11% profit = 200 × (1 + 0.11) = 200 × 1.11 = ₹222.
Source: SSC CHSL

14. The ratio of the cost price to the selling price of an article is 20 : 21. What is the profit percentage?
(a) 4% (b) 5% (c) 6% (d) 7.5%
Answer: (b) 5%
Solution: Profit % = [(21 - 20) ÷ 20] × 100 = (1/20) × 100 = 5%.
Source: SSC MTS

15. By selling an article for ₹96, a shopkeeper makes a profit whose percentage is numerically equal to its cost price in rupees. What is the cost price of the article?
(a) ₹50 (b) ₹55 (c) ₹60 (d) ₹65
Answer: (c) ₹60
Solution: Equation: CP + CP² / 100 = 96 => CP² + 100(CP) - 9600 = 0. Factoring: (CP + 160)(CP - 60) = 0. Since cost price must be positive, CP = ₹60. (Check: 60 + 60% of 60 = 60 + 36 = 96).
Source: SSC CGL

16. A dishonest dealer professes to sell his goods at cost price, but uses a false weight of 900 grams for a 1 kg weight. What is his profit percentage?
(a) 9.09% (b) 10% (c) 12.5% (d) 11.11%
Answer: (d) 11.11%
Solution: Profit % = [Error ÷ (True Weight - Error)] × 100 = [100 ÷ 900] × 100 = 100/9% ≈ 11.11%.
Source: Delhi Police Constable

17. A dealer marks his goods 20% above the cost price and allows a discount of 10% on the marked price. What is his profit percentage?
(a) 8% (b) 10% (c) 12% (d) 6%
Answer: (a) 8%
Solution: Let CP = 100. Marked Price = 120. Selling Price = 120 × (1 - 0.10) = 120 × 0.90 = ₹108. Profit % = 108 - 100 = 8%.
Source: SSC CGL

18. If the loss incurred on selling an article is one-third of its selling price, what is the loss percentage on cost price?
(a) 20% (b) 25% (c) 33.33% (d) 16.67%
Answer: (b) 25%
Solution: Let Selling Price = ₹3. Then Loss = ₹1. Cost Price = Selling Price + Loss = 3 + 1 = ₹4. Loss % on CP = (1 / 4) × 100 = 25%.
Source: SSC CHSL

19. A person incurs a loss of 5% by selling a watch for ₹1,140. At what price should the watch be sold to gain 5%?
(a) ₹1,200 (b) ₹1,240 (c) ₹1,260 (d) ₹1,280
Answer: (c) ₹1,260
Solution: Cost Price = 1140 ÷ 0.95 = ₹1,200. Selling Price for 5% gain = 1200 × 1.05 = ₹1,260.
Source: RRB NTPC

20. A vendor bought bananas at 3 for ₹2 and sold them at 2 for ₹3. What is the vendor's profit percentage?
(a) 100% (b) 110% (c) 120% (d) 125%
Answer: (d) 125%
Solution: CP of 1 banana = ₹2/3. SP of 1 banana = ₹3/2. Profit % = [(3/2 - 2/3) ÷ (2/3)] × 100 = [(9 - 4)/6 ÷ (2/3)] × 100 = (5/4) × 100 = 125%.
Source: SSC MTS

21. A man bought 20 dozen eggs for ₹720. If 2 dozen eggs were broken during transit, at what price per dozen must he sell the remaining eggs to make an overall profit of 20%?
(a) ₹48 (b) ₹45 (c) ₹50 (d) ₹52
Answer: (a) ₹48
Solution: Total Cost Price = ₹720. Target Selling Price for 20% profit = 720 × 1.20 = ₹864. Remaining eggs = 20 - 2 = 18 dozen. Required Selling Price per dozen = 864 ÷ 18 = ₹48 per dozen.
Source: SSC CGL

22. A shop offers two successive discounts of 20% and 10% on a jacket. What is the single equivalent discount percentage?
(a) 30% (b) 28% (c) 25% (d) 26%
Answer: (b) 28%
Solution: Equivalent discount = d1 + d2 - (d1 × d2)/100 = 20 + 10 - (20 × 10)/100 = 30 - 2 = 28%.
Source: SSC CHSL

23. An article is sold at a gain of 15%. Had it been sold for ₹27 more, the gain would have been 20%. What is the cost price of the article?
(a) ₹500 (b) ₹520 (c) ₹540 (d) ₹560
Answer: (c) ₹540
Solution: Difference in gain percentage = 20% - 15% = 5% of CP. 5% of CP = ₹27 => Cost Price = (27 ÷ 5) × 100 = ₹540.
Source: SSC CGL

24. By selling 33 meters of cloth, a merchant gains the selling price of 11 meters of cloth. What is the merchant's gain percentage?
(a) 25% (b) 33.33% (c) 40% (d) 50%
Answer: (d) 50%
Solution: Profit = SP of 11 meters = SP of 33 meters - CP of 33 meters => CP of 33 meters = SP of 22 meters => SP / CP = 33 / 22 = 3 / 2. Gain % = [(3 - 2) / 2] × 100 = 50%.
Source: SSC MTS

25. A trader marks his goods at such a price that after allowing a discount of 15%, he still makes a profit of 19%. By what percentage is the marked price above the cost price?
(a) 35% (b) 40% (c) 45% (d) 50%
Answer: (b) 40%
Solution: Ratio MP / CP = (100 + Profit %) ÷ (100 - Discount %) = (100 + 19) ÷ (100 - 15) = 119 ÷ 85 = 7 / 5 = 1.40. Marked price is (1.40 - 1) × 100 = 40% above cost price.
Source: SSC CGL
