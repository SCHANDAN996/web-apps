Set 5: Profit & Loss Multiple Choice Questions (Medium to Hard)
Questions 101–120: Medium | Questions 121–125: Hard

101. Under the promotional scheme 'Buy 3, Get 1 Free', what is the effective discount percentage?
(a) 25% (b) 33.33% (c) 20% (d) 15%
Answer: (a) 25%
Solution: Total items received = 3 + 1 = 4. Number of free items = 1. Effective discount % = (Free items ÷ Total items) × 100 = (1 ÷ 4) × 100 = 25%.
Source: SSC CGL

102. An article is sold at 10% loss. Had it been sold for ₹90 more, there would have been a profit of 5%. What is the cost price of the article?
(a) ₹550 (b) ₹600 (c) ₹650 (d) ₹700
Answer: (b) ₹600
Solution: Percentage difference = 5% - (-10%) = 15% of CP. 15% of CP = ₹90 => Cost Price = (90 ÷ 15) × 100 = ₹600.
Source: SSC CHSL

103. A shopkeeper sells 1/3 of his stock at a profit of 15%, 1/3 at a profit of 20%, and the remaining at a loss of 5%. What is his overall percentage profit on the entire stock?
(a) 8% (b) 9% (c) 10% (d) 12%
Answer: (c) 10%
Solution: Overall profit % = (1/3)(15) + (1/3)(20) + (1/3)(-5) = (15 + 20 - 5) ÷ 3 = 30 ÷ 3 = 10%.
Source: RRB Group D

104. Under the promotional scheme 'Buy 4, Get 1 Free', what is the discount percentage?
(a) 25% (b) 16.67% (c) 22.5% (d) 20%
Answer: (d) 20%
Solution: Trap alert: Option (a) 25% incorrectly divides 1 by 4 instead of total items (4 + 1 = 5)! Effective discount % = (Free items ÷ Total items) × 100 = (1 ÷ 5) × 100 = 20%.
Source: SSC CGL

105. A trader marks his goods 30% above the cost price, but allows a discount of 10% for cash payment. What is his profit percentage?
(a) 17% (b) 18% (c) 20% (d) 15%
Answer: (a) 17%
Solution: Net profit % = Markup - Discount - (Markup × Discount)/100 = 30 - 10 - (30 × 10)/100 = 20 - 3 = 17%.
Source: SSC MTS

106. The cost price of 24 apples is equal to the selling price of 18 apples. What is the profit percentage?
(a) 25% (b) 33.33% (c) 30% (d) 28.5%
Answer: (b) 33.33%
Solution: Trap alert: Option (a) 25% divides the difference (6) by 24 instead of the selling quantity 18! Profit % = [(24 - 18) ÷ 18] × 100 = (6 ÷ 18) × 100 = 100/3% ≈ 33.33%.
Source: SSC CGL

107. A merchant buys 80 kg of rice at ₹45 per kg and another 120 kg at ₹55 per kg. At what rate per kg must he sell the mixture to make an overall profit of 10%?
(a) ₹55.00 (b) ₹56.00 (c) ₹56.10 (d) ₹57.20
Answer: (c) ₹56.10
Solution: Total Cost = (80 × 45) + (120 × 55) = 3600 + 6600 = ₹10,200. Total weight = 200 kg. Cost per kg = 10200 ÷ 200 = ₹51. Required SP per kg for 10% profit = 51 × 1.10 = ₹56.10 per kg.
Source: Delhi Police Constable

108. A dealer sells a refrigerator for ₹18,000 at a loss of 10%. At what price should he sell it to gain 10%?
(a) ₹20,000 (b) ₹21,000 (c) ₹21,600 (d) ₹22,000
Answer: (d) ₹22,000
Solution: Cost Price = 18000 ÷ (1 - 0.10) = 18000 ÷ 0.90 = ₹20,000. Selling Price for 10% gain = 20000 × 1.10 = ₹22,000.
Source: SSC CHSL

109. Three successive discounts of 10%, 20%, and 30% are equivalent to a single discount percentage of:
(a) 49.6% (b) 50% (c) 48.4% (d) 52%
Answer: (a) 49.6%
Solution: Multiplier = (1 - 0.10) × (1 - 0.20) × (1 - 0.30) = 0.90 × 0.80 × 0.70 = 0.504. Equivalent single discount = (1 - 0.504) × 100 = 49.6%.
Source: RRB NTPC

110. By selling a toy for ₹24, a person loses as much percent as the cost price of the toy in rupees. What is the cost price of the toy?
(a) ₹30 or ₹70 (b) ₹40 or ₹60 (c) ₹20 or ₹80 (d) ₹45 or ₹55
Answer: (b) ₹40 or ₹60
Solution: Equation: CP - CP² / 100 = 24 => CP² - 100(CP) + 2400 = 0 => (CP - 40)(CP - 60) = 0. Therefore, CP = ₹40 or ₹60.
Source: SSC GD

111. A dishonest shopkeeper professes to sell his goods at cost price, but uses a false weight of 850 grams for 1 kg. What is his profit percentage?
(a) 15% (b) 16.67% (c) 17.65% (d) 18%
Answer: (c) 17.65%
Solution: Profit % = [Error ÷ (True Weight - Error)] × 100 = [150 ÷ 850] × 100 = (3 ÷ 17) × 100 = 300/17% ≈ 17.65%.
Source: SSC CGL

112. A person sells two watches for ₹1,980 each. On one watch he gains 10% and on the other he loses 10%. What is his overall gain or loss in rupees on the entire transaction?
(a) ₹20 loss (b) ₹20 gain (c) No loss no gain (d) ₹40 loss
Answer: (d) ₹40 loss
Solution: Total SP = 1980 + 1980 = ₹3,960. CP1 = 1980 ÷ 1.10 = ₹1,800. CP2 = 1980 ÷ 0.90 = ₹2,200. Total CP = 1800 + 2200 = ₹4,000. Loss = 4000 - 3960 = ₹40 loss.
Source: SSC MTS

113. If the selling price of 16 items is equal to the cost price of 20 items, what is the profit percentage?
(a) 25% (b) 20% (c) 30% (d) 18.75%
Answer: (a) 25%
Solution: 16 SP = 20 CP => SP / CP = 20 / 16 = 5 / 4. Profit % = [(5 - 4) ÷ 4] × 100 = 25%.
Source: SSC CGL

114. A trader sells goods at 20% above cost price. If he allows a discount of 5% on this marked price, what is his profit percentage?
(a) 15% (b) 14% (c) 13% (d) 16%
Answer: (b) 14%
Solution: Net profit % = 20 - 5 - (20 × 5)/100 = 15 - 1 = 14%.
Source: SSC CHSL

115. An article is sold at a loss of 8%. Had it been sold for ₹72 more, there would have been a gain of 4%. What is the cost price of the article?
(a) ₹500 (b) ₹550 (c) ₹600 (d) ₹650
Answer: (c) ₹600
Solution: Percentage difference = 4% - (-8%) = 12% of CP. 12% of CP = ₹72 => CP = (72 ÷ 12) × 100 = ₹600.
Source: RRB Group D

116. By selling 60 meters of cloth, a merchant gains the cost price of 15 meters of cloth. What is his gain percentage?
(a) 15% (b) 20% (c) 30% (d) 25%
Answer: (d) 25%
Solution: Profit = CP of 15 meters. Total CP = CP of 60 meters. Gain % = (15 ÷ 60) × 100 = 25%.
Source: SSC MTS

117. A dealer marked an article at ₹750 and sold it after allowing two successive discounts of 20% and 10%. What was the selling price?
(a) ₹540 (b) ₹520 (c) ₹550 (d) ₹560
Answer: (a) ₹540
Solution: Selling Price = 750 × (1 - 0.20) × (1 - 0.10) = 750 × 0.80 × 0.90 = 750 × 0.72 = ₹540.
Source: Delhi Police Constable

118. If the ratio of cost price to selling price of an article is 5 : 6, what is the profit percentage?
(a) 16.67% (b) 20% (c) 25% (d) 15%
Answer: (b) 20%
Solution: Profit % = [(6 - 5) ÷ 5] × 100 = (1/5) × 100 = 20%.
Source: SSC CGL

119. A fruit seller bought 100 apples for ₹350 and sold them at the rate of ₹48 per dozen. What is his percentage profit?
(a) 12.5% profit (b) 15% profit (c) 14.29% profit (d) 14.29% loss
Answer: (c) 14.29% profit
Solution: CP of 100 apples = ₹350. SP of 1 apple = 48 ÷ 12 = ₹4. SP of 100 apples = 100 × 4 = ₹400. Profit = 400 - 350 = ₹50. Profit % = (50 ÷ 350) × 100 = 100/7% ≈ 14.29% profit.
Source: SSC CHSL

120. A shopkeeper gives 3 articles free on the purchase of 12 articles. What is the effective discount percentage?
(a) 25% (b) 15% (c) 18% (d) 20%
Answer: (d) 20%
Solution: Total articles given = 12 + 3 = 15. Free articles = 3. Effective discount % = (3 ÷ 15) × 100 = 20%.
Source: RRB NTPC

121. A dishonest shopkeeper professes to sell his goods at 10% loss on cost price, but uses a false weight of 800 grams for 1 kg. What is his actual profit percentage?
(a) 12.5% (b) 10% (c) 15% (d) 11.11%
Answer: (a) 12.5%
Solution: Let CP of 1000 g = ₹100. Cost of 800 g given = ₹80. Selling price charged = 100 × (1 - 0.10) = ₹90. Profit = 90 - 80 = ₹10. Actual profit % = (10 ÷ 80) × 100 = 12.5%.
Source: SSC CGL

122. A trader purchases 30 kg of rice at ₹17.50 per kg and 30 kg at ₹18.50 per kg. He mixes them together and sells the mixture at ₹18.60 per kg. What is his percentage profit?
(a) 3% (b) 3.33% (c) 3.5% (d) 4%
Answer: (b) 3.33%
Solution: Since equal quantities are mixed, average CP = (17.50 + 18.50) ÷ 2 = ₹18.00 per kg. SP = ₹18.60 per kg. Profit per kg = 18.60 - 18.00 = ₹0.60. Profit % = (0.60 ÷ 18.00) × 100 = 10/3% ≈ 3.33%.
Source: SSC CGL

123. A person bought two articles for ₹1,600. He sold the first at a profit of 10% and the second at a profit of 20%. If he had sold the first at 20% profit and the second at 10% profit, he would have received ₹5 more. What is the difference between the cost prices of the two articles?
(a) ₹40 (b) ₹45 (c) ₹50 (d) ₹60
Answer: (c) ₹50
Solution: Let cost prices be x and y. Profit difference = (0.20x + 0.10y) - (0.10x + 0.20y) = 0.10(x - y) = ₹5 => x - y = 5 ÷ 0.10 = ₹50.
Source: SSC CHSL

124. An article is marked 20% above its cost price. A discount of 10% is allowed on the marked price. If the article is sold for ₹1,080, what is the profit in rupees?
(a) ₹60 (b) ₹70 (c) ₹75 (d) ₹80
Answer: (d) ₹80
Solution: SP = CP × 1.20 × 0.90 = 1.08 CP = ₹1,080 => CP = 1080 ÷ 1.08 = ₹1,000. Profit = SP - CP = 1080 - 1000 = ₹80.
Source: SSC CGL

125. A shopkeeper buys 144 items at 90 paise each. In transit 20 items are broken. He sells the remaining items at ₹1.20 each. What is his overall profit percentage?
(a) 12.5% (b) 14.81% (c) 15.2% (d) 16.4%
Answer: (b) 14.81%
Solution: Total CP = 144 × 0.90 = ₹129.60. Remaining items = 144 - 20 = 124. Total SP = 124 × 1.20 = ₹148.80. Profit = 148.80 - 129.60 = ₹19.20. Profit % = (19.20 ÷ 129.60) × 100 = 400/27% ≈ 14.81%.
Source: SSC CGL
