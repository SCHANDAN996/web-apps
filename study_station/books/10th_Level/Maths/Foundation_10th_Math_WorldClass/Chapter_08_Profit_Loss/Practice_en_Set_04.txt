Set 4: Profit & Loss Multiple Choice Questions (Medium to Hard)
Questions 76–95: Medium | Questions 96–100: Hard

76. If the profit earned is 20% when calculated on the selling price, what is the profit percentage when calculated on the cost price?
(a) 25% (b) 20% (c) 24% (d) 16.67%
Answer: (a) 25%
Solution: Let Selling Price = ₹100. Then Profit = ₹20. Cost Price = Selling Price - Profit = 100 - 20 = ₹80. Profit % on CP = (20 ÷ 80) × 100 = 25%.
Source: SSC CGL

77. By selling an article for ₹600, a person makes a profit of 25%. For how much should he sell it to incur a loss of 10%?
(a) ₹420 (b) ₹432 (c) ₹440 (d) ₹450
Answer: (b) ₹432
Solution: Cost Price = 600 ÷ 1.25 = ₹480. Selling Price for 10% loss = 480 × (1 - 0.10) = 480 × 0.90 = ₹432.
Source: SSC CHSL

78. A dealer buys an article marked at ₹250 with two successive discounts of 20% and 10%. He spends ₹20 on transportation and sells it for ₹220. What is his profit percentage?
(a) 8% (b) 9% (c) 10% (d) 12%
Answer: (c) 10%
Solution: Purchase price = 250 × 0.80 × 0.90 = ₹180. Total CP = 180 + 20 = ₹200. Profit = 220 - 200 = ₹20. Profit % = (20 ÷ 200) × 100 = 10%.
Source: RRB Group D

79. A shopkeeper sells an article at a discount of 25% on the marked price and still makes a profit of 20%. If his actual profit is ₹40, what is the marked price of the article?
(a) ₹280 (b) ₹300 (c) ₹350 (d) ₹320
Answer: (d) ₹320
Solution: Trap alert: Option (b) ₹300 erroneously calculates the marked price directly from profit and discount percentages! Profit = 20% of CP = ₹40 => CP = 40 ÷ 0.20 = ₹200. Selling Price = 200 + 40 = ₹240. Marked Price = SP ÷ (1 - 0.25) = 240 ÷ 0.75 = ₹320.
Source: SSC CGL

80. A dishonest grocer professes to sell his goods at cost price, but uses a false weight of 950 grams for 1 kg. What is his percentage gain?
(a) 5.26% (b) 5.00% (c) 5.50% (d) 4.76%
Answer: (a) 5.26%
Solution: Profit % = [Error ÷ (True Weight - Error)] × 100 = [50 ÷ 950] × 100 = 100/19% ≈ 5.26%.
Source: SSC MTS

81. What is the difference between a single discount of 30% and two successive discounts of 20% and 10% on a bill of ₹10,000?
(a) ₹0 (b) ₹200 (c) ₹100 (d) ₹300
Answer: (b) ₹200
Solution: Trap alert: Option (a) ₹0 assumes that 20% + 10% equals 30%! Two successive discounts of 20% and 10% equal 20 + 10 - (20 × 10)/100 = 28%. The difference is 30% - 28% = 2% of ₹10,000 = ₹200.
Source: SSC CGL

82. If the selling price of 5 articles is equal to the cost price of 6 articles, what is the gain percentage?
(a) 15% (b) 18% (c) 20% (d) 25%
Answer: (c) 20%
Solution: 5 SP = 6 CP => SP / CP = 6 / 5. Gain % = [(6 - 5) ÷ 5] × 100 = (1/5) × 100 = 20%.
Source: Delhi Police Constable

83. A retailer marks up the price of goods by 25% above cost price and offers a discount of 12% on the marked price. What is the retailer's percentage profit?
(a) 8% (b) 9% (c) 12% (d) 10%
Answer: (d) 10%
Solution: Net profit % = 25 - 12 - (25 × 12)/100 = 13 - 3 = 10%.
Source: SSC CHSL

84. A man sells a plot of land at 6% profit. If he had sold it at 10% profit, he would have received ₹200 more. What was the cost price of the plot?
(a) ₹5,000 (b) ₹4,800 (c) ₹5,200 (d) ₹6,000
Answer: (a) ₹5,000
Solution: Difference in profit % = 10% - 6% = 4% of CP. 4% of CP = ₹200 => Cost Price = (200 ÷ 4) × 100 = ₹5,000.
Source: RRB NTPC

85. A vendor purchased toffees at 6 for a rupee. How many toffees for a rupee must he sell to gain 20%?
(a) 4 (b) 5 (c) 6 (d) 7
Answer: (b) 5
Solution: CP of 1 toffee = ₹1/6. Required SP of 1 toffee = (1/6) × 1.20 = 1.2/6 = ₹1/5. Number of toffees sold for ₹1 = 5 toffees.
Source: SSC GD

86. A watch was sold at a loss of 10%. If it had been sold for ₹140 more, there would have been a profit of 4%. What is the cost price of the watch?
(a) ₹900 (b) ₹950 (c) ₹1,000 (d) ₹1,050
Answer: (c) ₹1,000
Solution: Percentage difference = 4% - (-10%) = 14% of CP. 14% of CP = ₹140 => CP = 140 ÷ 0.14 = ₹1,000.
Source: SSC CGL

87. By selling 45 lemons for ₹40, a man loses 20%. How many lemons should he sell for ₹24 to gain 20% in the transaction?
(a) 16 (b) 20 (c) 22 (d) 18
Answer: (d) 18
Solution: SP of 1 lemon = 40/45 = ₹8/9. CP of 1 lemon = (8/9) ÷ 0.80 = ₹10/9. Required SP of 1 lemon for 20% gain = (10/9) × 1.20 = ₹4/3. Number of lemons for ₹24 = 24 ÷ (4/3) = 24 × (3/4) = 18 lemons.
Source: SSC MTS

88. The marked price of an article is ₹500. It is sold at two successive discounts of 20% and x%. If the final selling price is ₹360, what is the value of x?
(a) 10% (b) 12% (c) 15% (d) 8%
Answer: (a) 10%
Solution: Price after first discount of 20% = 500 × (1 - 0.20) = ₹400. Second discount amount = 400 - 360 = ₹40. Second discount rate x = (40 ÷ 400) × 100 = 10%.
Source: SSC CGL

89. A person sells two chairs for ₹120 each. On one chair he gains 25%, and on the other he loses 25%. What is his total net loss in rupees on the transaction?
(a) ₹12 (b) ₹16 (c) ₹20 (d) ₹24
Answer: (b) ₹16
Solution: Total SP = 120 + 120 = ₹240. CP1 = 120 ÷ 1.25 = ₹96. CP2 = 120 ÷ 0.75 = ₹160. Total CP = 96 + 160 = ₹256. Net Loss = 256 - 240 = ₹16.
Source: SSC CHSL

90. If the purchase cost is ₹80, overhead transportation expenses are ₹20, and the selling price is ₹120, what is the profit percentage?
(a) 15% (b) 18% (c) 20% (d) 25%
Answer: (c) 20%
Solution: Total CP = Purchase price + Overhead expenses = 80 + 20 = ₹100. Profit = 120 - 100 = ₹20. Profit % = (20 ÷ 100) × 100 = 20%.
Source: RRB Group D

91. By selling an article for ₹700, a merchant loses 30%. At what price should he sell it to gain 30%?
(a) ₹1,200 (b) ₹1,250 (c) ₹1,280 (d) ₹1,300
Answer: (d) ₹1,300
Solution: Cost Price = 700 ÷ (1 - 0.30) = 700 ÷ 0.70 = ₹1,000. Selling Price for 30% gain = 1000 × 1.30 = ₹1,300.
Source: SSC MTS

92. If a loss is 25% when calculated on cost price, what is the loss percentage when calculated on selling price?
(a) 33.33% (b) 25% (c) 20% (d) 30%
Answer: (a) 33.33%
Solution: Let CP = 100 => Loss = 25 => SP = 100 - 25 = ₹75. Loss % on SP = (25 ÷ 75) × 100 = 100/3% ≈ 33.33%.
Source: Delhi Police Constable

93. A shopkeeper buys 10 articles for ₹8 and sells 8 articles for ₹10. What is his profit percentage?
(a) 50% (b) 56.25% (c) 60% (d) 52.5%
Answer: (b) 56.25%
Solution: CP of 1 article = ₹8/10 = ₹0.80. SP of 1 article = ₹10/8 = ₹1.25. Profit % = [(1.25 - 0.80) ÷ 0.80] × 100 = (0.45 ÷ 0.80) × 100 = 56.25%.
Source: SSC CGL

94. A merchant marks an article at ₹800. After allowing two successive discounts of 10% and 5%, what is the net selling price?
(a) ₹680 (b) ₹690 (c) ₹684 (d) ₹700
Answer: (c) ₹684
Solution: Price after 10% discount = 800 × 0.90 = ₹720. Final SP after 5% discount = 720 × 0.95 = ₹684.
Source: SSC CHSL

95. The cost prices of two articles A and B are in the ratio 4 : 5. While selling these articles, the shopkeeper makes a profit of 10% on article A and 20% on article B. If the difference in their selling prices is ₹480, what is the cost price of article A?
(a) ₹1,000 (b) ₹1,100 (c) ₹1,150 (d) ₹1,200
Answer: (d) ₹1,200
Solution: Let CP(A) = 400x and CP(B) = 500x. SP(A) = 400x × 1.10 = 440x. SP(B) = 500x × 1.20 = 600x. Difference in SP = 600x - 440x = 160x = ₹480 => x = 3. Cost price of A = 400 × 3 = ₹1,200.
Source: RRB NTPC

96. A dishonest dealer sells his goods using a balance which reads 1,200 grams for 1 kg. He also marks up his goods by 10% above cost price. What is his actual profit percentage?
(a) 32% (b) 30% (c) 28% (d) 35%
Answer: (a) 32%
Solution: Let CP of 1000 g = ₹100. Price charged for 1200 g with 10% markup = 1.20 × 110 = ₹132. Goods delivered = 1000 g (Cost = ₹100). Profit = 132 - 100 = ₹32. Profit % = 32%.
Source: SSC CGL

97. A publisher sells books to a retail bookseller at ₹5 per copy but allows 25 copies to be counted as 24 copies. If the retailer allows a discount of 2% on the marked price of ₹6 per copy to the public, what is the retailer's percentage profit?
(a) 20% (b) 22.5% (c) 25% (d) 24%
Answer: (b) 22.5%
Solution: Retailer's cost for 25 copies = 24 × 5 = ₹120. SP of 1 copy = 6 × (1 - 0.02) = ₹5.88. Total SP for 25 copies = 25 × 5.88 = ₹147. Profit = 147 - 120 = ₹27. Profit % = (27 ÷ 120) × 100 = 22.5%.
Source: SSC CGL

98. A manufacturer fixes the marked price of shoes at ₹1,000. He sells to a dealer at 16% discount. The dealer sells the shoes to a customer at a 4% discount on the manufacturer's marked price. What is the dealer's profit percentage on his cost?
(a) 12% (b) 13.5% (c) 14.29% (d) 15%
Answer: (c) 14.29%
Solution: Dealer's Cost Price = 1000 × (1 - 0.16) = ₹840. Dealer's Selling Price = 1000 × (1 - 0.04) = ₹960. Profit = 960 - 840 = ₹120. Profit % = (120 ÷ 840) × 100 = 100/7% ≈ 14.29%.
Source: SSC CHSL

99. A person bought a certain number of articles at 4 for ₹5 and an equal number of articles at 5 for ₹4. He mixed them together and sold them at 2 for ₹2 (₹1 each). What is his net gain or loss percentage?
(a) 2.44% gain (b) No gain no loss (c) 2% loss (d) 2.44% loss
Answer: (d) 2.44% loss
Solution: Let he buys 20 articles of each kind (total 40 articles). CP1 = 20 × (5/4) = ₹25. CP2 = 20 × (4/5) = ₹16. Total CP = 25 + 16 = ₹41. Total SP = 40 × 1 = ₹40. Net Loss = 41 - 40 = ₹1. Loss % = (1 ÷ 41) × 100 = 100/41% ≈ 2.44% loss.
Source: SSC CGL

100. A tea merchant blends two varieties of tea costing ₹240 per kg and ₹360 per kg in the ratio 5 : 3 by weight. If he sells the blended tea at ₹315 per kg, what is his profit percentage?
(a) 10% (b) 10.53% profit (c) 12% profit (d) 10.53% loss
Answer: (b) 10.53% profit
Solution: Cost of 8 kg blend = (5 × 240) + (3 × 360) = 1200 + 1080 = ₹2,280. Cost per kg = 2280 ÷ 8 = ₹285. Profit per kg = 315 - 285 = ₹30. Profit % = (30 ÷ 285) × 100 = 200/19% ≈ 10.53% profit.
Source: SSC CGL
