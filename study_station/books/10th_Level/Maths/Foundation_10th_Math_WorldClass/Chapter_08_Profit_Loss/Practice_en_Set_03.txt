Set 3: Profit & Loss Multiple Choice Questions (Medium to Hard)
Questions 51–70: Medium | Questions 71–75: Hard

51. If an article is sold at 5% gain instead of 5% loss, the seller gets ₹67.20 more. What is the cost price of the article?
(a) ₹672 (b) ₹650 (c) ₹700 (d) ₹720
Answer: (a) ₹672
Solution: Difference in percentage = 5% - (-5%) = 10% of CP. 10% of CP = ₹67.20 => Cost Price = 67.20 × 10 = ₹672.
Source: SSC CGL

52. By selling a cap for ₹144, a person loses 1/7 of his outlay. What was the cost price of the cap?
(a) ₹160 (b) ₹168 (c) ₹175 (d) ₹180
Answer: (b) ₹168
Solution: Loss = CP / 7 => SP = CP - CP/7 = (6/7) CP. Given (6/7) CP = 144 => CP = (144 × 7) ÷ 6 = 24 × 7 = ₹168.
Source: SSC CHSL

53. A trader buys two horses for ₹19,500. He sells one at a 20% loss and the other at a 15% profit. If the selling prices of both horses are equal, what is the cost price of the first horse?
(a) ₹10,500 (b) ₹11,000 (c) ₹11,500 (d) ₹12,000
Answer: (c) ₹11,500
Solution: Given SP1 = SP2 => 0.80 × CP1 = 1.15 × CP2 => CP1 / CP2 = 1.15 / 0.80 = 23 / 16. Total parts = 23 + 16 = 39 parts = ₹19,500 => 1 part = ₹500. Cost Price of the first horse = 23 × 500 = ₹11,500.
Source: RRB Group D

54. A shopkeeper sells an article at 20% profit after allowing a discount of 10% on the marked price. What is the ratio of the marked price to the cost price?
(a) 11 : 10 (b) 13 : 10 (c) 3 : 2 (d) 4 : 3
Answer: (d) 4 : 3
Solution: Trap alert: Option (a) 11 : 10 mistakenly adds or subtracts percentages directly! Relationship: MP × (1 - 0.10) = CP × (1 + 0.20) => 0.90 × MP = 1.20 × CP => MP / CP = 1.20 / 0.90 = 4 / 3 = 4 : 3.
Source: SSC CGL

55. A trader gives two successive discounts of 15% and 10% on a watch marked at ₹2,000. How much does a customer pay for it?
(a) ₹1,530 (b) ₹1,500 (c) ₹1,550 (d) ₹1,520
Answer: (a) ₹1,530
Solution: Final Price = 2000 × (1 - 0.15) × (1 - 0.10) = 2000 × 0.85 × 0.90 = 2000 × 0.765 = ₹1,530.
Source: SSC MTS

56. The cost price of 12 pens is equal to the selling price of 8 pens. What is the gain percentage?
(a) 33.33% (b) 50% (c) 40% (d) 25%
Answer: (b) 50%
Solution: Trap alert: Option (a) 33.33% mistakenly divides the difference by 12 instead of the base quantity 8! Gain % = [(12 - 8) ÷ 8] × 100 = (4 / 8) × 100 = 50%.
Source: SSC CGL

57. A man bought a horse and a carriage for ₹40,000. He sold the horse at 10% profit and the carriage at 5% loss, earning an overall profit of 1% on the whole. What was the cost price of the horse?
(a) ₹14,000 (b) ₹15,000 (c) ₹16,000 (d) ₹18,000
Answer: (c) ₹16,000
Solution: By rule of alligation: Ratio of CP(Horse : Carriage) = [1 - (-5)] : [10 - 1] = 6 : 9 = 2 : 3. Cost Price of Horse = (2/5) × 40,000 = ₹16,000.
Source: Delhi Police Constable

58. By selling 100 pencils, a shopkeeper gains the selling price of 20 pencils. What is his gain percentage?
(a) 16.67% (b) 20% (c) 30% (d) 25%
Answer: (d) 25%
Solution: Profit = 20 SP = 100 SP - 100 CP => 100 CP = 80 SP => SP / CP = 100 / 80 = 5 / 4. Gain % = [(5 - 4) ÷ 4] × 100 = 25%.
Source: SSC CHSL

59. A retailer buys 40 pens at the marked price of 36 pens from a wholesaler. If he sells all these pens giving a discount of 1% on the marked price, what is his profit percentage?
(a) 10% (b) 9% (c) 11% (d) 12%
Answer: (a) 10%
Solution: Let marked price of 1 pen = ₹1. Total CP of 40 pens = ₹36. Selling price of 40 pens = 40 × (1 - 0.01) = 40 × 0.99 = ₹39.60. Profit = 39.60 - 36 = ₹3.60. Profit % = (3.60 ÷ 36) × 100 = 10%.
Source: RRB NTPC

60. A dealer marks his goods 30% above the cost price and allows a discount of 15% on the marked price. What is his profit percentage?
(a) 10% (b) 10.5% (c) 11% (d) 12%
Answer: (b) 10.5%
Solution: Net profit % = Markup - Discount - (Markup × Discount)/100 = 30 - 15 - (30 × 15)/100 = 15 - 4.5 = 10.5%.
Source: SSC GD

61. A merchant lost 20% by selling an article for ₹96. At what price must he sell it to gain 15%?
(a) ₹132 (b) ₹135 (c) ₹138 (d) ₹140
Answer: (c) ₹138
Solution: Cost Price = 96 ÷ (1 - 0.20) = 96 ÷ 0.80 = ₹120. Required Selling Price for 15% gain = 120 × 1.15 = ₹138.
Source: SSC CGL

62. What single discount percentage is equivalent to two successive discounts of 15% and 5%?
(a) 19.5% (b) 19.75% (c) 20% (d) 19.25%
Answer: (d) 19.25%
Solution: Equivalent single discount = d1 + d2 - (d1 × d2)/100 = 15 + 5 - (15 × 5)/100 = 20 - 0.75 = 19.25%.
Source: SSC MTS

63. If the cost price of 25 chairs is equal to the selling price of 30 chairs, what is the loss percentage?
(a) 16.67% (b) 20% (c) 15% (d) 18.5%
Answer: (a) 16.67%
Solution: 25 CP = 30 SP => SP / CP = 25 / 30 = 5 / 6. Loss % = [(6 - 5) ÷ 6] × 100 = 100/6% ≈ 16.67%.
Source: SSC CGL

64. A vendor purchased lemons at 4 for ₹3 and sold them at 3 for ₹4. What is his profit percentage?
(a) 75% (b) 77.78% (c) 80% (d) 72.5%
Answer: (b) 77.78%
Solution: CP of 1 lemon = ₹3/4. SP of 1 lemon = ₹4/3. Profit % = [(4/3 - 3/4) ÷ (3/4)] × 100 = [(7/12) ÷ (9/12)] × 100 = (7/9) × 100 = 700/9% ≈ 77.78%.
Source: SSC CHSL

65. A table is sold for ₹1,800 at a loss of 10%. At what price should it be sold to earn a profit of 15%?
(a) ₹2,200 (b) ₹2,250 (c) ₹2,300 (d) ₹2,350
Answer: (c) ₹2,300
Solution: Cost Price = 1800 ÷ 0.90 = ₹2,000. Selling Price for 15% profit = 2000 × 1.15 = ₹2,300.
Source: RRB Group D

66. A person bought 50 kg of rice at ₹20 per kg. He sold 30 kg at 10% profit and the remaining 20 kg at 5% loss. What was his net profit in rupees on the whole transaction?
(a) ₹30 (b) ₹35 (c) ₹45 (d) ₹40
Answer: (d) ₹40
Solution: Cost of 30 kg = 30 × 20 = ₹600; Profit = 10% of 600 = +₹60. Cost of 20 kg = 20 × 20 = ₹400; Loss = 5% of 400 = -₹20. Net profit = 60 - 20 = ₹40.
Source: SSC MTS

67. A dealer offers a discount of 10% on the marked price of a bicycle and still makes a profit of 17%. If the cost price is ₹900, what is the marked price?
(a) ₹1,170 (b) ₹1,150 (c) ₹1,200 (d) ₹1,140
Answer: (a) ₹1,170
Solution: MP / CP = (100 + 17) ÷ (100 - 10) = 117 ÷ 90 = 1.30. MP = 900 × 1.30 = ₹1,170.
Source: Delhi Police Constable

68. By selling an umbrella for ₹300, a shopkeeper gains 20%. During a clearance sale, the shopkeeper allows a discount of 10% on this selling price of ₹300. What is his profit percentage during the sale?
(a) 6% (b) 8% (c) 10% (d) 12%
Answer: (b) 8%
Solution: Original CP = 300 ÷ 1.20 = ₹250. New sale SP = 300 × 0.90 = ₹270. Profit = 270 - 250 = ₹20. Profit % = (20 ÷ 250) × 100 = 8%.
Source: SSC CGL

69. If a merchant marks his goods 25% above cost price and allows a discount of 12% on the marked price, what is his net gain percentage?
(a) 8% (b) 9% (c) 10% (d) 12%
Answer: (c) 10%
Solution: Net gain % = 25 - 12 - (25 × 12)/100 = 13 - 3 = 10%.
Source: SSC CHSL

70. An article was sold at a loss of 15%. If the selling price had been increased by ₹150, there would have been a gain of 10%. What was the cost price of the article?
(a) ₹500 (b) ₹550 (c) ₹580 (d) ₹600
Answer: (d) ₹600
Solution: Difference in percentage = 10% - (-15%) = 25% of CP. 25% of CP = ₹150 => Cost Price = 150 × 4 = ₹600.
Source: RRB NTPC

71. A reduction of 20% in the price of sugar enables a purchaser to obtain 4 kg more for ₹160. What was the original price of sugar per kg?
(a) ₹10 per kg (b) ₹8 per kg (c) ₹12 per kg (d) ₹9 per kg
Answer: (a) ₹10 per kg
Solution: Amount saved by 20% reduction = 20% of 160 = ₹32. This ₹32 buys 4 kg extra => Reduced price per kg = 32 ÷ 4 = ₹8 per kg. Original price = 8 ÷ (1 - 0.20) = 8 ÷ 0.80 = ₹10 per kg.
Source: SSC CGL

72. A shopkeeper buys two qualities of tea: one at ₹180 per kg and another at ₹260 per kg. In what ratio should he mix them so that by selling the mixture at ₹252 per kg, he earns a profit of 20%?
(a) 3 : 5 (b) 5 : 3 (c) 4 : 3 (d) 2 : 3
Answer: (b) 5 : 3
Solution: Cost price of mixture = 252 ÷ 1.20 = ₹210 per kg. By alligation: Ratio = (260 - 210) : (210 - 180) = 50 : 30 = 5 : 3.
Source: SSC CGL

73. A trader sells two items for ₹2,400 each. On the first item he gains 20%, and on the second he loses 20%. What is his total net loss in rupees on the entire transaction?
(a) ₹150 (b) ₹180 (c) ₹200 (d) ₹240
Answer: (c) ₹200
Solution: Total SP = 2400 + 2400 = ₹4,800. CP1 = 2400 ÷ 1.20 = ₹2,000. CP2 = 2400 ÷ 0.80 = ₹3,000. Total CP = 2000 + 3000 = ₹5,000. Total Loss = 5000 - 4800 = ₹200.
Source: SSC CHSL

74. A merchant marks his goods up by 40% above cost price and allows a discount of 20% to cash customers and 10% to credit customers. If three-fourths of the goods are sold for cash and the remainder on credit, what is his overall profit percentage?
(a) 14.5% (b) 15.0% (c) 16.0% (d) 15.5%
Answer: (d) 15.5%
Solution: Let CP = 100 => Marked Price = 140. Cash SP = 140 × 0.80 = ₹112 (for 3/4 goods). Credit SP = 140 × 0.90 = ₹126 (for 1/4 goods). Weighted average SP = (3 × 112 + 1 × 126) ÷ 4 = (336 + 126) ÷ 4 = 462 ÷ 4 = ₹115.50. Overall profit % = 115.50 - 100 = 15.5%.
Source: SSC CGL

75. A milkman adds 5 liters of water to a certain quantity of pure milk costing ₹30 per liter. He sells the diluted mixture at ₹30 per liter and makes a profit of 25%. How many liters of pure milk were there initially?
(a) 15 liters (b) 20 liters (c) 25 liters (d) 30 liters
Answer: (b) 20 liters
Solution: Since the mixture is sold at the cost price of pure milk, the entire profit arises from the added water: Profit % = (Volume of Water ÷ Volume of Pure Milk) × 100. 25 = (5 ÷ Milk) × 100 => Milk = (5 ÷ 25) × 100 = 20 liters.
Source: SSC CGL
