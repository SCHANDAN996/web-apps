Set 6: Profit & Loss Multiple Choice Questions (Medium to Hard)
Questions 126–145: Medium | Questions 146–150: Hard

126. If an article is sold at a gain of 6% instead of 6% loss, the seller gets ₹60 more. What is the cost price of the article?
(a) ₹500 (b) ₹480 (c) ₹520 (d) ₹550
Answer: (a) ₹500
Solution: Percentage difference = 6% - (-6%) = 12% of CP. 12% of CP = ₹60 => Cost Price = (60 ÷ 12) × 100 = ₹500.
Source: SSC CGL

127. By selling an article for ₹720, a shopkeeper loses 10%. At what price should he sell it to gain 5%?
(a) ₹820 (b) ₹840 (c) ₹850 (d) ₹860
Answer: (b) ₹840
Solution: Cost Price = 720 ÷ (1 - 0.10) = 720 ÷ 0.90 = ₹800. Selling Price for 5% gain = 800 × 1.05 = ₹840.
Source: SSC CHSL

128. A dishonest shopkeeper uses a false weight of 900 grams instead of 1 kg and sells goods at cost price. What is his profit percentage?
(a) 10% (b) 10.5% (c) 11.11% (d) 12.5%
Answer: (c) 11.11%
Solution: Profit % = [Error ÷ (True Weight - Error)] × 100 = [100 ÷ 900] × 100 = 100/9% ≈ 11.11%.
Source: RRB Group D

129. An article is sold at two successive discounts of 15% and 25%. If the marked price is ₹800, what is the final selling price?
(a) ₹480 (b) ₹500 (c) ₹520 (d) ₹510
Answer: (d) ₹510
Solution: Trap alert: Option (a) ₹480 incorrectly adds 15% + 25% = 40% discount (800 - 320 = 480)! Price multiplier = (1 - 0.15) × (1 - 0.25) = 0.85 × 0.75 = 0.6375. Final SP = 800 × 0.6375 = ₹510.
Source: SSC CGL

130. The cost price of 15 books is equal to the selling price of 12 books. What is the gain percentage?
(a) 25% (b) 20% (c) 30% (d) 16.67%
Answer: (a) 25%
Solution: 15 CP = 12 SP => SP / CP = 15 / 12 = 5 / 4. Profit % = [(5 - 4) ÷ 4] × 100 = (1/4) × 100 = 25%.
Source: SSC MTS

131. The ratio of the cost price to the selling price of an article is 20 : 21. What is the gain percentage?
(a) 4.76% (b) 5% (c) 5.5% (d) 6%
Answer: (b) 5%
Solution: Trap alert: Option (a) 4.76% incorrectly divides profit 1 by SP 21 instead of CP 20! Profit % = [(21 - 20) ÷ 20] × 100 = (1/20) × 100 = 5%.
Source: SSC CGL

132. A dealer marks his goods 20% above the cost price and allows a discount of 10% on the marked price. What is his profit percentage?
(a) 10% (b) 9% (c) 8% (d) 7%
Answer: (c) 8%
Solution: Net profit % = 20 - 10 - (20 × 10)/100 = 10 - 2 = 8%.
Source: Delhi Police Constable

133. If the marked price of an article is ₹250 and it is sold for ₹210, what is the discount percentage?
(a) 14% (b) 15% (c) 18% (d) 16%
Answer: (d) 16%
Solution: Discount amount = 250 - 210 = ₹40. Discount % = (40 ÷ 250) × 100 = 16%.
Source: SSC CHSL

134. By selling 33 meters of cloth, a shopkeeper gains the selling price of 11 meters. What is his gain percentage?
(a) 50% (b) 33.33% (c) 25% (d) 40%
Answer: (a) 50%
Solution: Gain = 11 SP = 33 SP - 33 CP => 33 CP = 22 SP => SP / CP = 33 / 22 = 3 / 2. Gain % = [(3 - 2) ÷ 2] × 100 = 50%.
Source: RRB NTPC

135. Two successive discounts of 20% and 5% are equivalent to a single discount percentage of:
(a) 25% (b) 24% (c) 23% (d) 22%
Answer: (b) 24%
Solution: Single equivalent discount = 20 + 5 - (20 × 5)/100 = 25 - 1 = 24%.
Source: SSC GD

136. A person incurs a loss of 5% by selling a watch for ₹1,140. At what price should the watch be sold to earn a 5% profit?
(a) ₹1,240 (b) ₹1,250 (c) ₹1,260 (d) ₹1,280
Answer: (c) ₹1,260
Solution: Cost Price = 1140 ÷ 0.95 = ₹1,200. Selling Price for 5% profit = 1200 × 1.05 = ₹1,260.
Source: SSC CGL

137. A man bought an old bicycle for ₹1,500. He spends ₹500 on its repairs and sells it for ₹2,400. What is his profit percentage?
(a) 15% (b) 18% (c) 25% (d) 20%
Answer: (d) 20%
Solution: Total Cost Price = 1500 + 500 = ₹2,000. Profit = 2400 - 2000 = ₹400. Profit % = (400 ÷ 2000) × 100 = 20%.
Source: SSC MTS

138. If an article is sold at 200% profit, what is the ratio of its cost price to its selling price?
(a) 1 : 3 (b) 1 : 2 (c) 2 : 3 (d) 1 : 4
Answer: (a) 1 : 3
Solution: Let CP = 100. Profit = 200% of 100 = 200. SP = 100 + 200 = 300. Ratio CP : SP = 100 : 300 = 1 : 3.
Source: SSC CGL

139. A vendor purchased bananas at 6 for ₹10 and sold them at 4 for ₹8. What is his percentage profit?
(a) 18% (b) 20% (c) 22% (d) 25%
Answer: (b) 20%
Solution: CP of 1 banana = ₹10/6 = ₹5/3. SP of 1 banana = ₹8/4 = ₹2. Profit per banana = 2 - 5/3 = ₹1/3. Profit % = [(1/3) ÷ (5/3)] × 100 = (1/5) × 100 = 20%.
Source: SSC CHSL

140. If a discount of 20% on the marked price of an article enables a customer to save ₹50, what was the marked price?
(a) ₹200 (b) ₹240 (c) ₹250 (d) ₹300
Answer: (c) ₹250
Solution: Discount = 20% of Marked Price = ₹50 => Marked Price = (50 ÷ 20) × 100 = ₹250.
Source: RRB Group D

141. A merchant bought 150 eggs at ₹4 per egg. In transit 30 eggs broke. He sells the remaining unbroken eggs at ₹6 per egg. What is his overall profit percentage?
(a) 15% (b) 18% (c) 25% (d) 20%
Answer: (d) 20%
Solution: Total CP = 150 × 4 = ₹600. Remaining eggs = 150 - 30 = 120 eggs. Total SP = 120 × 6 = ₹720. Profit = 720 - 600 = ₹120. Profit % = (120 ÷ 600) × 100 = 20%.
Source: SSC MTS

142. An item is sold for ₹1,870 at a loss of 15%. What was its cost price?
(a) ₹2,200 (b) ₹2,100 (c) ₹2,150 (d) ₹2,250
Answer: (a) ₹2,200
Solution: Cost Price = 1870 ÷ (1 - 0.15) = 1870 ÷ 0.85 = ₹2,200.
Source: Delhi Police Constable

143. What single discount percentage is equivalent to three successive discounts of 10%, 10%, and 10%?
(a) 30% (b) 27.1% (c) 28.5% (d) 26.8%
Answer: (b) 27.1%
Solution: Net multiplier = (0.90)³ = 0.729. Single equivalent discount = (1 - 0.729) × 100 = 27.1%.
Source: SSC CGL

144. A seller marks his goods 40% above cost price and allows a 20% discount on the marked price. What is his profit percentage?
(a) 10% (b) 11% (c) 12% (d) 14%
Answer: (c) 12%
Solution: Net profit % = 40 - 20 - (40 × 20)/100 = 20 - 8 = 12%.
Source: SSC CHSL

145. If the cost price of 50 oranges is equal to the selling price of 40 oranges, what is the profit percentage?
(a) 20% (b) 22.5% (c) 30% (d) 25%
Answer: (d) 25%
Solution: Profit % = [(50 - 40) ÷ 40] × 100 = (10 ÷ 40) × 100 = 25%.
Source: RRB NTPC

146. A shopkeeper marks an article at a price such that after giving a discount of 25%, he still makes a profit of 20%. If the cost price of the article is ₹460, what is the marked price?
(a) ₹736 (b) ₹720 (c) ₹750 (d) ₹760
Answer: (a) ₹736
Solution: MP / CP = (100 + Profit %) ÷ (100 - Discount %) = (100 + 20) ÷ (100 - 25) = 120 ÷ 75 = 8 / 5. Marked Price = (8 / 5) × 460 = 8 × 92 = ₹736.
Source: SSC CGL

147. A dishonest dealer professes to sell his goods at 20% profit on cost price, but uses a false weight of 800 grams for 1 kg. What is his actual overall profit percentage?
(a) 45% (b) 50% (c) 52.5% (d) 55%
Answer: (b) 50%
Solution: Let CP of 1000 g = ₹100. Cost of 800 g given = ₹80. Price charged for 1000 g with 20% profit = ₹120. Overall Profit = 120 - 80 = ₹40. Profit % = (40 ÷ 80) × 100 = 50%.
Source: SSC CGL

148. A merchant has 1,000 kg of sugar, part of which he sells at 8% profit and the rest at 18% profit. He gains 14% on the whole. What quantity did he sell at 18% profit?
(a) 400 kg (b) 500 kg (c) 600 kg (d) 700 kg
Answer: (c) 600 kg
Solution: By rule of alligation: Ratio of quantity at 8% to quantity at 18% = (18 - 14) : (14 - 8) = 4 : 6 = 2 : 3. Quantity sold at 18% profit = [3 ÷ (2 + 3)] × 1000 = (3/5) × 1000 = 600 kg.
Source: SSC CHSL

149. A man buys a table and a chair for ₹3,000. He sells the table at 10% profit and the chair at 20% profit, making a total profit of ₹380 on the whole. What was the cost price of the table?
(a) ₹1,800 (b) ₹2,000 (c) ₹2,400 (d) ₹2,200
Answer: (d) ₹2,200
Solution: Let cost price of table = T and chair = 3000 - T. Total Profit = 0.10 T + 0.20(3000 - T) = 380 => 0.10 T + 600 - 0.20 T = 380 => 0.10 T = 220 => T = ₹2,200.
Source: SSC CGL

150. A trader marks his goods at such a price that after allowing a discount of 12.5% on the marked price, he makes a profit of 20%. If an article cost him ₹1,400, what is its marked price?
(a) ₹1,850 (b) ₹1,920 (c) ₹1,950 (d) ₹2,000
Answer: (b) ₹1,920
Solution: Selling Price = 1400 × 1.20 = ₹1,680. Discount = 12.5% = 1/8 => SP = (7/8) × Marked Price. (7/8) MP = 1680 => MP = (1680 × 8) ÷ 7 = 240 × 8 = ₹1,920.
Source: SSC CGL
