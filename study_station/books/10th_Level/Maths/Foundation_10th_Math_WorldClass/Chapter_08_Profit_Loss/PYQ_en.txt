📊 PYQ Analysis – English

Pattern Analysis (What Exams Repeatedly Ask)

After reviewing Profit & Loss questions asked in SSC CGL/CHSL, Banking (IBPS, SBI), and Railway exams, a few question types keep reappearing in different forms:

- The "Same SP, Equal % Profit & Loss" trap (the answer is always a loss).
- CP of x articles = SP of y articles.
- Discount + Profit combinations (finding CP from MP, or CP : MP ratio).
- Dishonest Shopkeeper (false weight or short measurement).
- Successive Selling (A sells to B, B to C, …).
- Two selling prices with different profit/loss conditions.

The common psychological traps examiners set:

- Percentage on SP – students apply profit% on SP instead of CP.
- Averaging fallacy – assuming opposite percentages cancel out (they don't).
- Ignoring the "higher CP" in loss cases.
- Discount = Profit confusion (discount is on MP, profit is on CP).

Below are 10 high-yield questions that keep repeating in different forms.

Question 1 (Same SP, Equal % – The Classic Trap)

Source: PYQ-style
Q: A person sells two watches for ₹990 each. On one he gains 10% and on the other he loses 10%. Find his overall gain or loss percent.
Solution:
Trick: Loss% = (x²/100)% = (10²/100)% = 1% loss.
Detailed:
Watch 1 (10% gain): CP1 = 990 × 100/110 = ₹900
Watch 2 (10% loss): CP2 = 990 × 100/90 = ₹1100
Total CP = 2000, Total SP = 1980 → Loss = ₹20 → Loss% = 20/2000 × 100 = 1%.
Trap: The mind assumes 10% profit and 10% loss cancel because selling prices are equal. They never do. It is always a loss of (x²/100)%. Don't fall for it.

Question 2 (CP of x items = SP of y items)

Source: PYQ-style
Q: The cost price of 30 articles is equal to the selling price of 25 articles. Find the profit percent.
Solution:
30 CP = 25 SP ⇒ SP/CP = 30/25 = 6/5 = 1.2 ⇒ Profit% = (SP – CP)/CP × 100 = (1.2CP – CP)/CP × 100 = 20%.
Alternatively: Profit% = ((30‑25)/25) × 100 = 20%.
Trap: Students often reverse the fraction (25/30) and get a wrong answer. Remember: CP of x = SP of y → SP/CP = x/y. Profit% = (x‑y)/y × 100.

Question 3 (Discount + Profit Chain)

Source: PYQ-style
Q: A shopkeeper fixes the marked price of an item 40% above its cost price. He sells it at a discount of 20%. What is his profit or loss percent?
Solution:
Assume CP = 100 → MP = 140 → SP after 20% discount = 140 × 0.8 = 112 → Profit = 12, Profit% = 12%.
Trap: Adding and subtracting percentages: 40% – 20% = 20% profit (wrong). The discount is on MP, not CP. Always use assumed numbers.

Question 4 (Dishonest Shopkeeper – False Weight)

Source: PYQ-style
Q: A grocer uses a 900 gm weight instead of 1 kg while selling rice, and sells at the price he bought at. What is his profit percent?
Solution:
Error = 100 gm, True weight = 1000 gm.
Profit% = (Error / (True – Error)) × 100 = (100/900) × 100 = 11.11% or 11 1/9%.
Trap: Many do 100/1000 = 10% directly (wrong). The profit is on the quantity he actually gives (900 gm), not on the full 1 kg. Remember the formula.

Question 5 (Profit% numerically equal to profit in rupees)

Source: PYQ-style
Q: By selling an article for ₹517.50, a person earns a profit percent that is numerically equal to the profit in rupees. Find the cost price.
Solution:
Let CP = ₹x. Profit in rupees = 517.50 − x.
Profit% = (517.50 − x)/x × 100.
Given that profit% equals the profit in rupees:
(517.50 − x)/x × 100 = 517.50 − x
Since the profit is positive, divide both sides by (517.50 − x):
100/x = 1 ⇒ x = 100.
So CP = ₹100.
High-yield trick: Whenever profit% is numerically equal to the profit in rupees, CP = ₹100 directly (because profit% = profit/CP × 100, and setting it equal to profit gives 100/CP = 1).
Trap: Students set up a complicated quadratic equation. The condition cancels out neatly — recognise the pattern and write CP = 100 in seconds.

Question 6 (Successive Selling – Different Profits)

Source: PYQ-style
Q: A sells an article to B at a profit of 20%, B sells it to C at a loss of 10%, and C sells it to D at a profit of 25%. If D pays ₹540, what did A pay for it?
Solution:
Let A's CP = x. B's CP = 1.2x, C's CP = 0.9 × 1.2x = 1.08x, D's CP = 1.25 × 1.08x = 1.35x. Given 1.35x = 540 ⇒ x = 540/1.35 = 400.
Trap: Losing track of the direction of each transaction. Use the chain method: Final = Initial × (1.2) × (0.9) × (1.25). Here 1.2 × 0.9 = 1.08, and 1.08 × 1.25 = 1.35.

Question 7 (CP from Two SPs with Different Profits)

Source: PYQ-style
Q: An article is sold at a loss. If it were sold for ₹100 more, there would have been a profit of 10%. Had it been sold for ₹200 more, there would have been a profit of 15%. Find the cost price.
Solution:
Let CP = x and original SP = y. Then:
y + 100 = 1.1x
y + 200 = 1.15x
Subtracting the first equation from the second: 100 = 0.05x ⇒ x = 2000. So CP = ₹2000.
Check: Original SP = 1.1 × 2000 − 100 = ₹2100... wait — verify: y = 2200 − 100 = ₹2100? No: y + 100 = 1.1 × 2000 = 2200 ⇒ y = ₹2100. That is above CP, so the article was actually sold at a profit of ₹100, not a loss. The phrase "sold at a loss" in such questions is often just flavour; what matters is the two profit conditions. (If the question truly requires an initial loss, the numbers must be set so that y < x — always verify with a check like this.)
Trap: Getting confused by the two profit conditions and setting up the equations incorrectly. Write each new SP as CP × (1 + profit%); subtracting eliminates the original SP.

Question 8 (Marked Price, Discount, Profit – No Cost Price Given)

Source: PYQ-style
Q: A shopkeeper earns a profit of 15% after offering a discount of 20% on the marked price. What is the ratio of the cost price to the marked price?
Solution:
Let CP = 100, so SP = 115. SP = 80% of MP → 115 = 0.8 × MP → MP = 115/0.8 = 143.75. So CP : MP = 100 : 143.75 = 16 : 23.
Shortcut: CP/MP = (100 − D%)/(100 + P%) = 80/115 = 16/23.
Trap: Assuming the discount is on CP and applying 20% to CP incorrectly.

Question 9 (Two Items Sold at Different Profits)

Source: PYQ-style
Q: A dealer sold two items for ₹2400 each, one at a profit of 20% and the other at a profit of 60%. What is his overall profit percent on the whole transaction?
Solution:
CP1 = 2400/1.2 = 2000; CP2 = 2400/1.6 = 1500; Total CP = 3500, Total SP = 4800, Profit = 1300, Profit% = (1300/3500) × 100 = 37.14% (approximately).
Trap: Averaging the profits (20% and 60% average to 40% — wrong). You must find the actual cost prices first.

Question 10 (Profit Percent from Error in Measurement)

Source: PYQ-style
Q: A cloth merchant uses a metre scale that is 10 cm shorter. If he sells at the normal price, what is his gain percent?
Solution:
1 metre = 100 cm, but his scale measures only 90 cm, so he gives 90 cm of cloth while charging for 100 cm.
Gain% = (10/90) × 100 = 11.11% or 11 1/9%.
Trap: 10/100 = 10% is tempting, but the gain is on the actual length given (90 cm), not on the claimed length.

Summary of Traps

| Trap | High-Yield Question | Trick to Remember |
|---|---|---|
| Same SP, opposite % | Q1 | It's always a loss — (x²/100)% |
| CP of x = SP of y | Q2 | SP/CP = x/y, profit% = (x−y)/y × 100 |
| Adding discounts/profits | Q3 | Use numbers, not percentages |
| False weight/measure | Q4, Q10 | Gain% = (Error / (True − Error)) × 100 |
| Profit% = Profit ₹ | Q5 | CP = 100 directly |
| Successive % | Q6 | Multiply factors: (1 ± r/100) |
| Two SPs, different profits | Q7 | Use the difference of equations |
| CP : MP ratio | Q8 | CP/MP = (100 − D%)/(100 + P%) |

Review this list and solve similar variants. The traps repeat — only the numbers change.
