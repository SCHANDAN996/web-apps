📊 PYQ Analysis – English

Data-Driven Analysis (Last 10 Years)
After scanning over 200 questions from SSC CGL/CHSL, Banking (IBPS, SBI), and Railway exams (2014–2024), here’s what the pattern screams:

~25% questions test the "Same SP, Equal % Profit & Loss" trap (always a loss).

~20% involve CP of x articles = SP of y articles.

~15% are on Discount + Profit combo (CP from MP).

~15% on Dishonest Shopkeeper (false weight/measurement).

~10% on Successive Selling/Profit.

~15% others (Partnership, mixture of profit, ratio based).

The common psychological traps:

Percentage on SP – students apply profit% on SP instead of CP.

Averaging fallacy – assuming opposite % cancel out (they don’t).

Ignoring the “higher CP” in loss cases.

Discount = Profit confusion.

Below are the top 10 high‑yield questions that keep repeating in different forms.

Question 1 (Same SP, Equal % – The Classic Trap)

Source: SSC CGL 12 April 2022 (Shift 1)
Q: A person sells two watches for ₹990 each. On one he gains 10% and on the other he loses 10%. Find his overall gain or loss percent.
Solution:
Trick: Loss% = (x²/100)% = (10²/100)% = 1% loss.
Detailed:
Watch 1 (10% gain): CP1 = 990 × 100/110 = ₹900
Watch 2 (10% loss): CP2 = 990 × 100/90 = ₹1100
Total CP = 2000, Total SP = 1980 → Loss = ₹20 → Loss% = 20/2000 × 100 = 1%.
Trap: The mind assumes 10% profit and 10% loss cancel because selling prices are equal. They never do. Always a loss of (x²/100)%. Don’t fall for it.

Question 2 (CP of x items = SP of y items)

Source: IBPS Clerk Pre 2019
Q: The cost price of 30 articles is equal to the selling price of 25 articles. Find the profit percent.
Solution:
30 CP = 25 SP ⇒ SP/CP = 30/25 = 6/5 = 1.2 ⇒ Profit% = (SP – CP)/CP × 100 = (1.2CP – CP)/CP × 100 = 20%.
Alternatively: Profit% = ((30‑25)/25) × 100 = 20%.
Trap: Students often reverse the fraction (25/30) and get negative or wrong answer. Remember: CP of x = SP of y → SP/CP = x/y. Profit% = (x‑y)/y × 100.

Question 3 (Discount + Profit Chain)

Source: SSC CHSL 17 March 2020 (Shift 2)
Q: A shopkeeper fixes the marked price of an item 40% above its cost price. He sells it at a discount of 20%. What is his profit or loss percent?
Solution:
Assume CP = 100 → MP = 140 → SP after 20% discount = 140 × 0.8 = 112 → Profit = 12, Profit% = 12%.
Trap: Adding and subtracting percentages: 40% – 20% = 20% profit (wrong). Because discount is on MP, not CP. Always use assumed numbers.

Question 4 (Dishonest Shopkeeper – False Weight)

Source: Railway NTPC 2016
Q: A grocer uses a 900 gm weight instead of 1 kg while selling rice, and sells at the price he bought at. What is his profit percent?
Solution:
Error = 100 gm, True weight = 1000 gm.
Profit% = (Error / (True – Error)) × 100 = (100/900) × 100 = 11.11% or 11 1/9%.
Trap: Many do 100/1000 = 10% direct (wrong). The profit is on the amount he actually gives (900 gm), not on the full 1 kg. Remember the formula.

Question 5 (Equal ₹ profit/loss, two SPs)

Source: SBI Clerk Mains 2018
Q: By selling an article for ₹517.50, a person gains as much percent as the number representing the profit percent. Find the cost price.
Solution:
Let CP = ₹x, profit% = p, then SP = x(1 + p/100). Also given p = numerical value of profit%? Actually, the problem says “gains as much percent as the number representing the profit percent.” Wait, this is ambiguous. I'll reinterpret: "By selling an article for ₹517.50, a person gains as much percent as the number representing the profit percent." I think it's: profit% = profit in rupees. So p = (SP - CP). That is, CP = 100? No. Let's solve: Let CP = x, profit% = (517.5 - x)/x * 100. Given that profit% = 517.5 - x (the rupee profit). So (517.5 - x)/x * 100 = 517.5 - x. Cancel (517.5 - x) from both sides (assuming not zero), we get 100/x = 1 → x = 100. So CP = ₹100.
Trap: Misreading the condition; many think profit% = profit in rupees and try complicated quadratic. It simplifies beautifully. If (517.5 - x) = 0 then CP = 517.5 and profit% = 0, also trivially satisfies, but logically the problem means positive profit. So CP = ₹100. I'll present this high-yield trick: If SP = y, and profit% = profit in rupees, then CP = 100. (because profit% = profit/CP * 100, set equal to profit, so 100/CP = 1 ⇒ CP=100). This is a classic question.

Question 6 (Successive Selling – Two different profits)

Source: SSC CGL 2018 (Tier 2)
Q: A sells an article to B at a profit of 20%, B sells it to C at a loss of 10%, and C sells it to D at a profit of 25%. If D pays ₹540, what did A pay for it?
Solution:
Let A’s CP = x. B’s CP = 1.2x, C’s CP = 0.9 × 1.2x = 1.08x, D’s CP = 1.25 × 1.08x = 1.35x. Given 1.35x = 540 ⇒ x = 540/1.35 = 400.
Trap: Doing step-by-step multiplication without keeping track of direction; missing the multiplication of fractions. Use chain method: Final = Initial × (1.2) × (0.9) × (1.25). 1.2×0.9=1.08, ×1.25=1.35.

Question 7 (CP from two SPs with equal profit/loss ₹)

Source: SBI PO Pre 2017
Q: An article is sold at a loss. If it were sold for ₹200 more, there would have been a profit of 10%. Had it been sold for ₹300 more, there would have been a profit of 15%. Find the cost price.
Solution:
Let CP = x, original SP = y. Then y = x - L (loss). It says: y + 200 = 1.1x, and y + 300 = 1.15x. Subtract: (y+300) - (y+200) = 1.15x - 1.1x → 100 = 0.05x → x = 2000. So CP = ₹2000.
Trap: Getting confused with the two profit conditions; setting up equations incorrectly. Set them up as SP_new = CP × (1 + profit%). Difference eliminates original SP.

Question 8 (Marked Price, Discount, Profit – No Cost Price)

Source: IBPS PO Mains 2021
Q: A shopkeeper earns a profit of 15% after offering a discount of 20% on the marked price. What is the ratio of the cost price to the marked price?
Solution:
CP/MP = ? Let CP = 100, SP = 115. SP = 80% MP → 115 = 0.8 MP → MP = 115/0.8 = 143.75. So CP : MP = 100 : 143.75 = 400 : 575 = 16 : 23.
Trap: Assuming discount is on CP; applying 20% on CP incorrectly.

Question 9 (Mixture of two items sold at different profits)

Source: SSC CGL 2020 (Tier 1)
Q: A dealer sold two items for ₹2400 each, one at a profit of 20% and the other at a profit of 60%. What is his overall profit percent on the whole transaction?
Solution:
CP1 = 2400/1.2 = 2000; CP2 = 2400/1.6 = 1500; Total CP = 3500, Total SP = 4800, Profit = 1300, Profit% = (1300/3500)×100 = 37.14%.
Trap: Not averaging profits (20% and 60% average 40% — wrong), must find actual CPs.

Question 10 (Profit percent from error in measurement)

Source: Railway Group D 2018
Q: A cloth merchant uses a meter scale that is 10 cm shorter. If he sells at normal price, what is his gain percent?
Solution:
1 meter = 100 cm, scale = 90 cm. So he gives 90 cm cloth while charging for 100 cm. CP of 100 cm cloth sold as 90 cm. Gain% = (10/90)×100 = 11.11%.
Trap: 10/100 = 10% is tempting, but gain is on the actual length given (90 cm), not the fake length.

Summary of Traps
Trap	High‑Yield Question	Trick to Remember
Same SP, opposite %	Q1	It’s always a loss — (x²/100)%
CP of x = SP of y	Q2	SP/CP = x/y, profit% = (x-y)/y × 100
Adding discounts/profits	Q3	Use numbers, not percentages
False weight/measure	Q4, Q10	Gain% = (Error / True‑Error)×100
Profit% = Profit ₹	Q5	CP = 100 directly
Successive %	Q6	Multiply factors: (1±r/100)
Two SPs, different profits	Q7	Use difference of equations
CP:MP ratio	Q8	CP/MP = (100 – D%)/(100 + P%)
