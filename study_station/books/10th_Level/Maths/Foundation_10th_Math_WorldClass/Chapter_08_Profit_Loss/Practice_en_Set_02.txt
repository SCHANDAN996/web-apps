26. A shopkeeper allows a discount of 20% on the marked price of an article and still makes a profit of 25%. If the cost price of the article is ₹400, what is its marked price?
(a) ₹625 (b) ₹600 (c) ₹650 (d) ₹640
Answer: (a) ₹625
Solution: Formula: MP / CP = (100 + Profit %) ÷ (100 - Discount %) = (100 + 25) ÷ (100 - 20) = 125 ÷ 80 = 25 / 16. MP = (25 / 16) × 400 = 25 × 25 = ₹625.
Source: SSC CGL

27. By selling an article for ₹825, a shopkeeper gains 10%. At what price should he sell it to gain 20%?
(a) ₹880 (b) ₹900 (c) ₹920 (d) ₹950
Answer: (b) ₹900
Solution: Cost Price = 825 ÷ 1.10 = ₹750. Selling Price for 20% gain = 750 × 1.20 = ₹900.
Source: SSC CHSL

28. A dishonest shopkeeper uses a weight of 800 grams instead of 1 kg and sells his goods at cost price. What is his percentage profit?
(a) 20% (b) 22.5% (c) 25% (d) 28%
Answer: (c) 25%
Solution: Profit % = [Error ÷ (True Weight - Error)] × 100 = [200 ÷ 800] × 100 = (1/4) × 100 = 25%.
Source: RRB Group D

29. An article is sold with two successive discounts of 10% and 20%. If the marked price is ₹500, what is the final selling price?
(a) ₹350 (b) ₹375 (c) ₹380 (d) ₹360
Answer: (d) ₹360
Solution: Trap alert: Option (a) ₹350 simply adds 10% + 20% = 30% discount (500 - 150 = 350)! Equivalent single discount = 10 + 20 - (10 × 20)/100 = 28%. Final SP = 500 × (1 - 0.28) = 500 × 0.72 = ₹360.
Source: SSC CGL

30. A dealer purchased a washing machine for ₹12,000 and spent ₹1,000 on transportation. If he sells it for ₹15,600, what is his profit percentage?
(a) 20% (b) 22% (c) 25% (d) 18%
Answer: (a) 20%
Solution: Total Cost Price = 12000 + 1000 = ₹13,000. Profit = 15600 - 13000 = ₹2,600. Profit % = (2600 ÷ 13000) × 100 = 20%.
Source: SSC MTS

31. The cost price of 20 articles is equal to the selling price of x articles. If the profit is 25%, what is the value of x?
(a) 25 (b) 16 (c) 15 (d) 18
Answer: (b) 16
Solution: Trap alert: Option (a) 25 erroneously multiplies 20 by 1.25! Profit % = [(20 - x) ÷ x] × 100 = 25 => (20 - x) / x = 1/4 => 80 - 4x = x => 5x = 80 => x = 16.
Source: SSC CGL

32. If a discount of 10% is allowed on the marked price of ₹1,200, the dealer still gains 8%. What is the cost price of the item?
(a) ₹950 (b) ₹980 (c) ₹1,000 (d) ₹1,020
Answer: (c) ₹1,000
Solution: Selling Price = 1200 × (1 - 0.10) = ₹1,080. Cost Price = 1080 ÷ 1.08 = ₹1,000.
Source: Delhi Police Constable

33. A person sells two articles at ₹9,900 each. On one he gets 10% profit and on the other he incurs 10% loss. What is his net gain or loss in rupees on the whole transaction?
(a) ₹100 loss (b) No gain no loss (c) ₹200 gain (d) ₹200 loss
Answer: (d) ₹200 loss
Solution: Total SP = 9900 + 9900 = ₹19,800. CP1 = 9900 ÷ 1.10 = ₹9,000. CP2 = 9900 ÷ 0.90 = ₹11,000. Total CP = 9000 + 11000 = ₹20,000. Net Loss = 20000 - 19800 = ₹200 loss.
Source: SSC CHSL

34. Goods are purchased for ₹450. If one-third of the goods are sold at a loss of 10%, at what profit percentage should the remainder be sold to gain 20% on the whole transaction?
(a) 35% (b) 30% (c) 28% (d) 40%
Answer: (a) 35%
Solution: Total CP = ₹450. Target total SP = 450 × 1.20 = ₹540. CP of one-third = 450 ÷ 3 = ₹150. SP of one-third = 150 × 0.90 = ₹135. Required SP for remaining goods = 540 - 135 = ₹405. CP of remaining goods = ₹300. Profit on remaining = 405 - 300 = ₹105. Profit % = (105 ÷ 300) × 100 = 35%.
Source: RRB NTPC

35. A milkman buys milk at ₹24 per liter. He adds water to it (water being free of cost) and sells the mixture at ₹24 per liter, thereby making a profit of 20%. What is the ratio of water to milk in the mixture?
(a) 1 : 4 (b) 1 : 5 (c) 1 : 6 (d) 2 : 5
Answer: (b) 1 : 5
Solution: When selling at cost price, Profit % = (Quantity of Water ÷ Quantity of Milk) × 100. 20 = (Water / Milk) × 100 => Water / Milk = 20 / 100 = 1 / 5 = 1 : 5.
Source: SSC GD

36. If the selling price of an article is doubled, the profit triples. What is the original profit percentage?
(a) 50% (b) 75% (c) 100% (d) 120%
Answer: (c) 100%
Solution: Let CP = c and SP = s. Original profit = s - c. New profit = 2s - c = 3(s - c) => 2s - c = 3s - 3c => s = 2c. Profit = 2c - c = c. Profit % = (c ÷ c) × 100 = 100%.
Source: SSC CGL

37. A trader sells two cows for ₹19,550 each. He gains 15% on one cow and loses 15% on the other. What is his overall loss percentage?
(a) 1.5% (b) 2.0% (c) 2.5% (d) 2.25%
Answer: (d) 2.25%
Solution: When two items are sold at the same price with r% profit and r% loss, overall loss % = (r / 10)² % = (15 / 10)² % = 2.25% loss.
Source: SSC MTS

38. A dishonest grocer sells rice at a profit of 10% on cost price and uses a weight that is 20% less than the true weight. What is his total overall profit percentage?
(a) 37.5% (b) 35% (c) 32.5% (d) 40%
Answer: (a) 37.5%
Solution: Let CP of 1000 g = ₹100. He gives 800 g (which costs him ₹80) and charges 10% above CP = ₹110. Profit = 110 - 80 = ₹30. Profit % = (30 ÷ 80) × 100 = 37.5%.
Source: SSC CGL

39. Three successive discounts of 20%, 10%, and 5% are equivalent to a single discount percentage of:
(a) 35% (b) 31.6% (c) 32.4% (d) 30%
Answer: (b) 31.6%
Solution: Net multiplier = (1 - 0.20) × (1 - 0.10) × (1 - 0.05) = 0.80 × 0.90 × 0.95 = 0.684. Equivalent single discount = (1 - 0.684) × 100 = 31.6%.
Source: SSC CHSL

40. A merchant marks his goods up by 40% above cost price and allows a discount of 25% on the marked price. What is his profit percentage?
(a) 4% (b) 6% (c) 5% (d) 8%
Answer: (c) 5%
Solution: Net profit % = Markup - Discount - (Markup × Discount)/100 = 40 - 25 - (40 × 25)/100 = 15 - 10 = 5%.
Source: RRB Group D

41. A radio is sold for ₹990 at a profit of 10%. What would have been the actual profit or loss percentage if it had been sold for ₹890?
(a) 1% profit (b) 1% loss (c) 1.11% profit (d) 1.11% loss
Answer: (d) 1.11% loss
Solution: Cost Price = 990 ÷ 1.10 = ₹900. If sold for ₹890: Loss = 900 - 890 = ₹10. Loss % = (10 ÷ 900) × 100 = 10/9% ≈ 1.11% loss.
Source: SSC MTS

42. A shopkeeper bought 80 kg of sugar at ₹13.50 per kg and mixed it with 120 kg of sugar at ₹16 per kg. At what rate per kg should he sell the mixture to gain 16%?
(a) ₹17.40 (b) ₹16.80 (c) ₹18.00 (d) ₹17.60
Answer: (a) ₹17.40
Solution: Total Cost = (80 × 13.50) + (120 × 16) = 1080 + 1920 = ₹3,000. Total weight = 200 kg. Cost per kg = 3000 ÷ 200 = ₹15. Required SP per kg for 16% gain = 15 × 1.16 = ₹17.40.
Source: Delhi Police Constable

43. An item was sold at a loss of 12%. If it had been sold for ₹49 more, there would have been a gain of 2%. What is the cost price of the item?
(a) ₹320 (b) ₹350 (c) ₹360 (d) ₹380
Answer: (b) ₹350
Solution: Difference in percentage = 2% - (-12%) = 14% of CP. 14% of CP = ₹49 => CP = (49 ÷ 14) × 100 = 3.5 × 100 = ₹350.
Source: SSC CGL

44. By selling 144 hens, Mahesh suffered a loss equal to the selling price of 6 hens. What was his loss percentage?
(a) 3% (b) 3.5% (c) 4% (d) 4.5%
Answer: (c) 4%
Solution: Loss = 6 SP = 144 CP - 144 SP => 150 SP = 144 CP => SP / CP = 144 / 150 = 24 / 25. Loss % = [(25 - 24) ÷ 25] × 100 = (1/25) × 100 = 4%.
Source: SSC CHSL

45. The marked price of a watch is ₹1,600. After two successive discounts, it is sold for ₹1,224. If the first discount rate is 10%, what is the second discount percentage?
(a) 12% (b) 14% (c) 16% (d) 15%
Answer: (d) 15%
Solution: Price after 1st discount = 1600 × (1 - 0.10) = ₹1,440. Second discount amount = 1440 - 1224 = ₹216. Second discount % = (216 ÷ 1440) × 100 = 15%.
Source: RRB NTPC

46. A manufacturer sells an article to a wholesale dealer at 10% profit. The wholesaler sells it to a shopkeeper at 20% profit, and the shopkeeper sells it to a customer for ₹56,100 at 25% profit. What was the cost of production for the manufacturer?
(a) ₹34,000 (b) ₹35,000 (c) ₹36,000 (d) ₹32,000
Answer: (a) ₹34,000
Solution: Equation: CP × 1.10 × 1.20 × 1.25 = 56100. 1.10 × 1.20 × 1.25 = 1.65. CP = 56100 ÷ 1.65 = ₹34,000.
Source: SSC CGL

47. A person bought two articles A and B for a total of ₹5,000. He sold article A at 20% profit and article B at 10% loss, thereby gaining 2% overall on the whole outlay. What was the cost price of article A?
(a) ₹1,800 (b) ₹2,000 (c) ₹2,400 (d) ₹3,000
Answer: (b) ₹2,000
Solution: Using alligation: Ratio of CP of A to B = [2 - (-10)] : [20 - 2] = 12 : 18 = 2 : 3. Total units = 2 + 3 = 5 units = ₹5,000 => 1 unit = ₹1,000. Cost Price of A = 2 × 1000 = ₹2,000.
Source: SSC CGL

48. A vendor sells an article at 20% profit. If he had bought it at 20% less and sold it for ₹18 less, he would have gained 25%. What is the original cost price of the article?
(a) ₹80 (b) ₹85 (c) ₹90 (d) ₹95
Answer: (c) ₹90
Solution: Let original CP = 100x => Original SP = 120x. New CP = 80x. New SP = 80x × 1.25 = 100x. Difference in SP = 120x - 100x = 20x. Given 20x = ₹18 => Original CP (100x) = 18 × 5 = ₹90.
Source: SSC CHSL

49. A merchant marks an article 50% above its cost price and allows a trade discount of 20%. If he further gives a cash discount of 5% on the discounted price, what is his net profit percentage?
(a) 12% (b) 15% (c) 16% (d) 14%
Answer: (d) 14%
Solution: Let CP = 100 => Marked Price = 150. Price after 20% trade discount = 150 × 0.80 = ₹120. Final SP after 5% cash discount = 120 × 0.95 = ₹114. Net profit % = 114 - 100 = 14%.
Source: SSC CGL

50. A trader purchases 12 articles for ₹10 and sells 10 articles for ₹12. What is his percentage profit?
(a) 40% (b) 44% (c) 48% (d) 50%
Answer: (b) 44%
Solution: Cost Price of 1 article = ₹10/12 = ₹5/6. Selling Price of 1 article = ₹12/10 = ₹6/5. Profit % = [(6/5 - 5/6) ÷ (5/6)] × 100 = [(11/30) ÷ (25/30)] × 100 = (11/25) × 100 = 44%.
Source: SSC CGL
