🃏 Flashcards — Percentage (English)

Card 1
Front: What does "per cent" literally mean, and what animal can you imagine to never forget it?
Back: "Per hundred." Imagine a centipede with 100 legs wearing a top hat with a % symbol, handing you a "/100" board.

Card 2
Front: Convert 40% into a fraction in its lowest terms.
Back: 40% = 40/100 = 2/5.

Card 3
Front: What is the quickest way to convert a fraction like 3/5 into a percentage?
Back: Multiply by 100%: (3/5) × 100% = 300/5 % = 60%.

Card 4
Front: 12.5% is equivalent to which super-handy fraction?
Back: ⅛ (1/8). So 12.5% of any number is just that number divided by 8.

Card 5
Front: How do you convert a decimal like 0.63 into a percentage instantly?
Back: Multiply by 100: 0.63 × 100 = 63%.

Card 6
Front: What is 15% of 200? (No calculator!)
Back: 15% = 15/100, so 15/100 × 200 = 15 × 2 = 30. Or 10% (20) + 5% (10) = 30.

Card 7
Front: What is the formula for percentage increase?
Back: % Increase = (Increase / Original) × 100.

Card 8
Front: A bike price rose from ₹40,000 to ₹50,000. What is the percentage increase?
Back: Increase = ₹10,000. (10,000/40,000) × 100 = 25%.

Card 9
Front: Why does a 20% rise followed by a 20% cut not bring you back to the original? (Name the memory trick.)
Back: The bases differ. 20% rise is on original, 20% cut is on the larger new number. Remember the Yo-Yo Trap: it never returns exactly to your hand.

Card 10
Front: What is the multiplier for a 15% decrease?
Back: (1 - 15/100) = 0.85.

Card 11
Front: How do you find the net effect of two successive changes +25% and -20%?
Back: Use A + B + (A×B)/100 = 25 - 20 + (25×-20)/100 = 5 - 5 = 0%. Net zero change.

Card 12
Front: A shirt's price is first increased by 10% then decreased by 10%. What is the overall change? (Quick formula)
Back: Net loss = (r²/100)% when both rates are same r. Here r=10, so (100)/100 = 1% loss. Shirt ends at 99% of original.

Card 13
Front: If you score 60% on a test out of 50 marks and 40% on another out of 100 marks, is your overall percentage 50%?
Back: No! Actual marks: 30 + 40 = 70 out of 150. Overall % = (70/150)×100 = 46.67%. Never average percentages with different bases.

Card 14
Front: What is 1% of 1,000,000?
Back: 1/100 × 1,000,000 = 10,000. (1% simply moves decimal two places left.)

Card 15
Front: If a price is reduced by 25%, by what percentage should it be increased to get back to the original?
Back: Not 25%! Multiplier: 0.75. To revert, multiply by 1/0.75 ≈ 1.3333, so 33.33% increase needed.

Card 16
Front: What weird memory trick helps you instantly put any % over 100?
Back: The Centipede Trick. "Cent" = 100, centipede ≈ 100 legs. Picture the centipede with a % hat handing you a "/100" sign. 30% becomes 30/100.

Card 17
Front: Convert 8.33% into a fraction. (Hint: think 1/12)
Back: 8.33% = 8.33/100 ≈ 1/12 exactly. (Because 100/12 = 8.33...). So 8.33% of a number = divide by 12.

Card 18
Front: A town's population increases by 5% annually. What single multiplier gives the population after 2 years?
Back: (1.05)² = 1.1025, i.e., 10.25% total increase, not 10%.
