Set 6 — Mensuration MCQs (All Hard: 126–150)

126. A solid metallic sphere of radius 10 cm is melted and recast into a cone whose base radius equals the sphere's radius. The height of the cone is
(a) 10 cm (b) 20 cm (c) 30 cm (d) 40 cm
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is forgetting the cone's 1/3 factor, which gives 13.3 cm or other wrong values. Sphere volume = (4/3)π×10³ = 4000π/3 cm³; cone volume = (1/3)π×10²×h = (100π/3)h. Equating gives h = 40, so the answer is 40 cm.
Source: PYQ-style

127. A cone, a hemisphere and a cylinder have equal bases and the same height, equal to the radius. The ratio of their volumes is
(a) 2 : 1 : 3 (b) 1 : 1 : 1 (c) 1 : 2 : 3 (d) 1 : 3 : 2
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is assuming equal height means equal volume. With radius r and height r, cone = (1/3)πr³, hemisphere = (2/3)πr³, cylinder = πr³, so the answer is 1 : 2 : 3.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Mathematics

128. A hollow sphere with internal and external radii 3 cm and 6 cm is melted to form a solid cylinder of height 7 cm. The radius of the cylinder is
(a) 3 cm (b) 6 cm (c) 4 cm (d) 5 cm
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is using the external radius only, ignoring the hollow part. Metal volume = (4/3)π(6³ − 3³) = (4/3)π×189 = 252π cm³. Then πR²×7 = 252π gives R² = 36, so the answer is 6 cm.
Source: PYQ-style

129. A rectangular sheet of paper 44 cm × 10 cm is rolled along its length to form a cylinder. The curved surface area of the resulting cylinder is
(a) 880 cm² (b) 220 cm² (c) 440 cm² (d) 1320 cm²
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is computing 2πrh after finding r = 7 cm and then doubling it by mistake. The paper's area itself becomes the curved surface, so CSA = 44 × 10, and the answer is 440 cm².
Source: NCERT Class 8 Mathematics

130. A solid cylinder of radius 5 cm and height 10 cm is melted and recast into small cylinders each of radius 1 cm and height 2 cm. The number of small cylinders is
(a) 125 (b) 150 (c) 75 (d) 100
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is dividing only the heights or only the base areas instead of volumes. Large volume = π×25×10 = 250π cm³; small volume = π×1×2 = 2π cm³; 250π/2π = 125, so the answer is 125.
Source: NCERT Class 8 Mathematics

131. The radius of a sphere is increased by 50%. The ratio of the surface area of the new sphere to that of the original sphere is
(a) 9 : 4 (b) 3 : 2 (c) 4 : 9 (d) 2 : 3
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is using the linear ratio 1.5 : 1 = 3 : 2 directly. Surface area varies as r², so the ratio is (1.5)² = 2.25 = 9/4, and the answer is 9 : 4.
Source: PYQ-style

132. A right circular cone fits exactly inside a cube of volume 216 cm³, its base touching the sides of one face and its vertex touching the opposite face. The volume of the cone (π = 22/7) is approximately
(a) 42.43 cm³ (b) 84.86 cm³ (c) 113.14 cm³ (d) 56.57 cm³
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is taking the cone's radius as 6 cm instead of 3 cm, which quadruples the result. Cube edge = 6 cm, so cone radius = 3 cm and height = 6 cm; volume = (1/3)×(22/7)×9×6 ≈ 56.57, so the answer is 56.57 cm³.
Source: PYQ-style

133. The perimeter of a sector of a circle of radius 10 cm is 40 cm. The area of the sector is
(a) 200 cm² (b) 150 cm² (c) 50 cm² (d) 100 cm²
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is treating the whole 40 cm as the arc length. Arc length = 40 − 2×10 = 20 cm; sector area = (1/2)×r×arc = (1/2)×10×20 = 100, so the answer is 100 cm².
Source: NCERT Class 10 Mathematics

134. A spherical lead ball of diameter 3 cm is melted and recast into three smaller balls. Two of them have diameters 1.5 cm and 2 cm. The diameter of the third ball is
(a) 3 cm (b) 2.5 cm (c) 1 cm (d) 2 cm
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is adding diameters instead of volumes: 1.5 + 2 + d = 3 wrongly suggests a negative or zero value. Volumes add, so cubes of diameters add: 1.5³ + 2³ + d³ = 3³ → 3.375 + 8 + d³ = 27 → d³ = 15.625, so the answer is 2.5 cm.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Mathematics

135. A rectangular tank 30 cm × 20 cm × 12 cm is completely filled with water. The water is poured into a cylindrical vessel of base radius 7 cm. The height of water in the vessel (π = 22/7) is
(a) 36 cm (b) 20 cm (c) 46.75 cm (d) 10 cm
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is using the tank's height 12 cm or diameter 14 cm in place of radius. Water volume = 7200 cm³; base area = (22/7)×49 = 154 cm²; height = 7200/154 = 46.75, so the answer is 46.75 cm.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Mathematics

136. A cylindrical tub of radius 12 cm contains water up to a depth of 20 cm. A spherical iron ball is dropped in and the water level rises by 6.75 cm. The radius of the ball is
(a) 12 cm (b) 10 cm (c) 8 cm (d) 9 cm
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is equating the ball's volume to the whole water volume instead of only the risen part. Displaced volume = π×12²×6.75 = 972π cm³; (4/3)πr³ = 972π gives r³ = 729, so the answer is 9 cm.
Source: PYQ-style

137. A solid cone of base radius 6 cm and height 8 cm is melted and recast into small spheres each of radius 2 cm. The number of spheres obtained is
(a) 6 (b) 9 (c) 12 (d) 8
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is cancelling the 1/3 and 4/3 factors incorrectly and getting 12 or 6. Cone volume = (1/3)π×36×8 = 96π cm³; sphere volume = (4/3)π×8 = 32π/3 cm³; 96π ÷ (32π/3) = 9, so the answer is 9.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Mathematics

138. A hemispherical depression is cut out from one face of a cubical block of side 21 cm, the hemisphere's diameter equalling the cube's edge. The total surface area of the remaining solid (π = 22/7) is
(a) 2646 cm² (b) 3465 cm² (c) 2992.5 cm² (d) 2310 cm²
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is subtracting the depression from the cube's area; actually a circle of πr² is removed but 2πr² of curved surface is added, a net gain of πr². With r = 10.5 cm, πr² = 346.5 cm², so TSA = 2646 + 346.5, and the answer is 2992.5 cm².
Source: NCERT Class 10 Mathematics

139. A right circular cylinder fits exactly inside a cube of side 8 cm, its circular bases touching two opposite square faces. The ratio of the cylinder's volume to the cube's volume is
(a) π : 3 (b) π : 6 (c) π : 4 (d) π : 2
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is taking the cylinder's radius as 8 cm. Radius = 4 cm, height = 8 cm, so cylinder volume = 128π cm³ and cube volume = 512 cm³; 128π : 512 simplifies, so the answer is π : 4.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Mathematics

140. A solid metallic sphere of radius 12 cm is melted and drawn into a wire of diameter 8 mm. The length of the wire is
(a) 288 m (b) 144 m (c) 216 m (d) 72 m
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is forgetting to convert 8 mm to 0.8 cm (radius 0.4 cm), which throws the answer off by a factor of 100. Sphere volume = (4/3)π×12³ = 2304π cm³; wire area = π×0.16 cm²; length = 2304π/0.16π = 14400 cm, so the answer is 144 m.
Source: PYQ-style

141. A cylindrical bucket of base diameter 28 cm and height 20 cm, full of sand, is emptied to form a conical heap of radius 14 cm. The height of the heap is
(a) 60 cm (b) 15 cm (c) 30 cm (d) 45 cm
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is ignoring the cone's 1/3 factor, which gives 20 cm. Sand volume = π×14²×20 = 3920π cm³; (1/3)π×14²×h = 3920π gives h = 60, so the answer is 60 cm.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Mathematics

142. A solid cone of height 24 cm and base radius 7 cm is melted and recast into a solid cylinder of base radius 14 cm. The height of the cylinder is
(a) 2 cm (b) 4 cm (c) 6 cm (d) 8 cm
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is doubling the radius and expecting the height to halve; the radius is squared, so the effect is fourfold. Cone volume = (1/3)π×49×24 = 392π cm³; π×196×h = 392π gives h = 2, so the answer is 2 cm.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Mathematics

143. A sphere of diameter 6 cm is dropped into a cylindrical vessel of diameter 12 cm partly filled with water and is completely submerged. The rise in water level is
(a) 2 cm (b) 1 cm (c) 3 cm (d) 4 cm
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is using the sphere's diameter 6 cm as its radius. Sphere volume = (4/3)π×3³ = 36π cm³; vessel base area = π×6² = 36π cm²; rise = 36π/36π = 1, so the answer is 1 cm.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Mathematics

144. A rectangular metal sheet 44 cm × 10 cm is rolled along its length to form a cylinder. The volume of the cylinder (π = 22/7) is
(a) 1540 cm³ (b) 770 cm³ (c) 6160 cm³ (d) 3080 cm³
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is taking 10 cm as the circumference instead of 44 cm, which gives a much smaller volume. Here 2πr = 44 gives r = 7 cm and height = 10 cm; volume = (22/7)×49×10 = 1540, so the answer is 1540 cm³.
Source: NCERT Class 8 Mathematics

145. A cone of height 30 cm has a small cone cut off the top by a plane parallel to the base. If the small cone's volume is 1/27 of the original cone's volume, the plane is at a height above the base of
(a) 20 cm (b) 25 cm (c) 10 cm (d) 15 cm
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is reporting 10 cm, which is the small cone's height measured from the tip, not from the base. Volume ratio 1/27 means height ratio 1/3, so small cone height = 10 cm from the tip; 30 − 10 = 20, so the answer is 20 cm.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Mathematics

146. A cylindrical tub of radius 8 cm contains water up to a depth of 25 cm. A solid spherical ball of radius 5 cm is completely immersed in it. The rise in water level is approximately
(a) 3.9 cm (b) 2.6 cm (c) 5.2 cm (d) 1.3 cm
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is using the ball's diameter or forgetting the 4/3 factor, which doubles or quarters the result. Ball volume = (4/3)π×125 = 500π/3 cm³; tub base area = 64π cm²; rise = 500/(3×64) ≈ 2.6, so the answer is 2.6 cm.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Mathematics

147. A solid consists of a cylinder with hemispherical ends. Its total length is 19 cm and the cylinder's diameter is 7 cm. The total surface area of the solid (π = 22/7) is
(a) 836 cm² (b) 704 cm² (c) 528 cm² (d) 418 cm²
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is taking the cylinder's height as 19 cm; the two hemispheres consume 7 cm of the length, leaving 12 cm. CSA of cylinder = 2×(22/7)×3.5×12 = 264 cm²; two hemispheres = 4πr² = 154 cm²; total = 418, so the answer is 418 cm².
Source: PYQ-style

148. A solid sphere of radius 9 cm is melted and drawn into a cylindrical wire of radius 3 mm. The length of the wire is
(a) 216 m (b) 324 m (c) 54 m (d) 108 m
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is treating 3 mm as 3 cm or forgetting the cm-to-m conversion. Sphere volume = (4/3)π×729 = 972π cm³; wire radius = 0.3 cm, area = 0.09π cm²; length = 972π/0.09π = 10800 cm, so the answer is 108 m.
Source: PYQ-style

149. A solid cylinder of radius 7 cm and height 20 cm is melted and recast into right circular cones each of base radius 2 cm and height 5 cm. The number of cones formed is
(a) 196 (b) 98 (c) 147 (d) 49
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is dropping the cone's 1/3 factor, which gives 49. Cylinder volume = π×49×20 = 980π cm³; cone volume = (1/3)π×4×5 = 20π/3 cm³; 980π ÷ (20π/3) = 147, so the answer is 147.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Mathematics

150. A well of diameter 3 m is dug 14 m deep. The earth taken out is spread evenly around it to form an embankment of width 4 m. The height of the embankment (π = 22/7) is
(a) 1.125 m (b) 2.25 m (c) 0.75 m (d) 1.5 m
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is using the outer circle's full area π×5.5² instead of the ring area. Earth volume = π×1.5²×14 = 31.5π m³; ring area = π(5.5² − 1.5²) = 28π m²; height = 31.5/28 = 1.125, so the answer is 1.125 m.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Mathematics
