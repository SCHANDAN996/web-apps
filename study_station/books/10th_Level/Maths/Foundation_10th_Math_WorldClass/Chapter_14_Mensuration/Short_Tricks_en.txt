1. Rectangle Area — "Long Bread"
Imagine a long loaf of bread (l × b). Whenever you need the area of a rectangle, picture yourself slicing a Long Bread into equal squares.
Area = L × B.

2. Rectangle Perimeter — "Two Long, Two Broad"
Walk all around a rectangular field. You'll pass two Long sides and two Broad sides. So you shout: "Two Longs, Two Broads; add them all!"
Perimeter = 2(L + B).

3. Triangle Area — "Half a Sandwich"
A triangle is exactly half of a rectangle sandwich with the same base and height. The recipe: take the whole sandwich (base × height) and eat half.
Area = ½ × base × height.

4. Circle Circumference — "Two Pies Are Round the Fence"
Two delicious pies (2π) are placed on a Round fence (r). So the fence length is 2πr.
Circumference = 2πr.

5. Circle Area — "Pie Are Squared"
For the area of the pie, shout "These pies are squared!" although pies are round. That sounds exactly like π r².
Area = πr².

6. Semicircle Perimeter — "Half a Pie, Plus the Line"
When taking half a pie (πr), never forget to add the straight cut (diameter = 2r). Picture a half-eaten pizza with its crust and the straight knife cut.
Perimeter of semicircle = πr + 2r.

7. Cone Volume — "One-Third its Cylinder Brother"
A cone is jealous of a cylinder with the same base and height because the cylinder holds three times as much. Remember, it takes exactly three cones to fill the cylinder.
Volume of cone = ⅓ πr²h.

8. Sphere Volume — "Four-Thirds Pie Are Cubic"
The sphere is a fat, cubic pie. Say it weirdly: "Four thirds pie are cubic" (4/3 π r³). The 'cubic' reminds you of the ³ on radius.

9. Sphere Surface Area — "Four Pie Are Square"
The surface of a sphere is made of four circular areas (πr²) laid out flat. Imagine unrolling the globe into four round pizzas.
Surface area = 4πr².

10. Cylinder CSA — "Two Pie Are High"
The curved surface is like a label wrapped around: its length is the circumference (2πr) and its height is h. So "two pie are high".
CSA = 2πrh.

11. Cylinder TSA — "Two Pie Are (Radius plus Height)"
The total area is the label (2πrh) plus two circular lids (2 × πr²). Factor 2πr out: 2πr (r + h). Think: "Two pie are r plus h".

12. Cone Slant Height & CSA — "Pie Roll"
The curved surface of a cone is a pie crust rolled out to a radius equal to slant height (l). So CSA = π r l. To find l, remember the right triangle inside the cone: l² = r² + h² (Pythagoras).

13. Equilateral Triangle Area — "Root Three Over Four, Side Square No More"
Sing this rhyme: "Root three over four, side square no more." It directly gives (√3/4) a². Picture a triangle with a square inside getting root-three treatment.

14. Wire Bending — "Perimeter is Permanent"
When a wire changes shape, the length remains the same — perimeter or circumference is permanent. Area changes, but perimeter stays. Think: "Perimeter is Permanent, Area is Adjustable".

15. Percentage Change in Area/Volume — "Don't Add, Multiply the Factors"
If dimensions change by a%, b%, never add percentages. Convert to multiplication factors (1 ± a/100), multiply them, then find percentage change. A weird mental image: two sales percentages don't add, they multiply — like stacking discounts.
