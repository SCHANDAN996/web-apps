Set 5 — Mensuration MCQs (Medium: 101–120 | Hard: 121–125)

101. The perimeter of a rectangle is 40 cm and its length is 4 cm more than its breadth. The area of the rectangle is
(a) 80 cm² (b) 120 cm² (c) 96 cm² (d) 84 cm²
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is taking length = 4 cm instead of length = breadth + 4. Here 2(b + b + 4) = 40 gives b = 8 cm and l = 12 cm, so the answer is 96 cm².
Source: NCERT Class 8 Mathematics

102. The area of a circle is equal to the area of a rectangle of length 14 cm and breadth 11 cm. The circumference of the circle (π = 22/7) is
(a) 88 cm (b) 22 cm (c) 44 cm (d) 132 cm
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is stopping at r = 7 and reporting the diameter or radius instead of the circumference. Rectangle area = 154 cm², so πr² = 154 gives r = 7 cm and circumference = 2πr = 44 cm, so the answer is 44 cm.
Source: NCERT Class 7 Mathematics

103. A square and a circle have equal perimeters. If the area of the square is 121 cm², the area of the circle (π = 22/7) is
(a) 196 cm² (b) 144 cm² (c) 154 cm² (d) 77 cm²
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is using the side 11 cm directly as the circle's radius. Square perimeter = 4 × 11 = 44 cm = 2πr, giving r = 7 cm and area = πr² = 154 cm², so the answer is 154 cm².
Source: NCERT Class 7 Mathematics

104. The radii of two circles are in the ratio 2:3. If the sum of their areas is 117π cm², the radius of the larger circle is
(a) 12 cm (b) 6 cm (c) 9 cm (d) 15 cm
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is solving 2k + 3k from the area sum instead of using areas 4k²π and 9k²π. Here 13k²π = 117π gives k = 3, so the larger radius is 3 × 3 = 9 cm, so the answer is 9 cm.
Source: NCERT Class 7 Mathematics

105. The area of a circular garden is 38.5 m². The cost of fencing it at ₹20 per metre (π = 22/7) is
(a) ₹220 (b) ₹440 (c) ₹110 (d) ₹330
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is multiplying the area by the rate instead of the circumference. From πr² = 38.5, r = 3.5 m and circumference = 22 m, so cost = 22 × 20 = ₹440, so the answer is ₹440.
Source: PYQ-style

106. A triangle has sides of lengths 6 cm, 8 cm, and 10 cm. Its area is
(a) 30 cm² (b) 48 cm² (c) 40 cm² (d) 24 cm²
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is computing ½ × 8 × 10 = 40 by taking the hypotenuse as a side of the right angle. Since 6² + 8² = 10², the triangle is right-angled with legs 6 and 8, so the answer is 24 cm².
Source: NCERT Class 9 Mathematics

107. The area of an equilateral triangle is 16√3 cm². Its perimeter is
(a) 48 cm (b) 12 cm (c) 36 cm (d) 24 cm
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is reporting the side 8 cm × 2 or forgetting to multiply by 3. From (√3/4)a² = 16√3, a² = 64 and a = 8 cm, so perimeter = 3 × 8 = 24 cm, so the answer is 24 cm.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Mathematics

108. The base of a parallelogram is twice its height. If its area is 128 cm², the base is
(a) 16 cm (b) 32 cm (c) 12 cm (d) 8 cm
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is reporting the height 8 cm instead of the base. With base = 2h, area = 2h² = 128 gives h = 8 cm and base = 16 cm, so the answer is 16 cm.
Source: NCERT Class 8 Mathematics

109. A rhombus has diagonals of lengths 12 cm and 16 cm. Its perimeter is
(a) 40 cm (b) 56 cm (c) 28 cm (d) 80 cm
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is adding the diagonals (12 + 16 = 28) as if they were sides. Side = √(6² + 8²) = 10 cm, so perimeter = 4 × 10 = 40 cm, so the answer is 40 cm.
Source: NCERT Class 8 Mathematics

110. The height of a cone is 9 cm and the diameter of its base is 14 cm. Its volume (π = 22/7) is
(a) 924 cm³ (b) 462 cm³ (c) 1386 cm³ (d) 616 cm³
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is forgetting the factor 1/3, which gives 1386 cm³. Volume = (1/3) × (22/7) × 49 × 9 = 462 cm³, so the answer is 462 cm³.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Mathematics

111. A cylinder of radius 7 cm and height 20 cm is completely filled with water. This water is poured into a rectangular tank of base dimensions 22 cm by 10 cm. The height of water in the tank (π = 22/7) is
(a) 22 cm (b) 14 cm (c) 7 cm (d) 28 cm
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is equating heights instead of volumes. Cylinder volume = (22/7) × 49 × 20 = 3080 cm³ and tank base area = 220 cm², so height = 3080/220 = 14 cm, so the answer is 14 cm.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Mathematics

112. The diameter of a sphere is decreased by 50%. The percentage decrease in its surface area is
(a) 50% (b) 87.5% (c) 25% (d) 75%
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is assuming surface area also falls by 50%; area scales with the square of the diameter. New area = (0.5)² = 0.25 of the old, a decrease of 75%, so the answer is 75%.
Source: PYQ-style

113. A solid metallic cone of radius 7 cm and height 15 cm is melted and recast into a solid cylinder of radius 5 cm. The height of the cylinder (π = 22/7) is
(a) 7 cm (b) 4.9 cm (c) 14 cm (d) 9.8 cm
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is dropping the 1/3 in the cone's volume, which triples the result. Cone volume = (1/3)π × 49 × 15 = 245π and 25πh = 245π gives h = 9.8 cm, so the answer is 9.8 cm.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Mathematics

114. The total surface area of a cube is numerically one-third of its volume. The edge length of the cube is
(a) 6 units (b) 12 units (c) 18 units (d) 9 units
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is setting 6a² = a³ directly, which gives 6. Here a³ = 3 × 6a² = 18a², so a = 18, so the answer is 18 units.
Source: NCERT Class 8 Mathematics

115. A wire 88 cm long is bent first into a circle, then into a square. The difference between the areas enclosed (π = 22/7) is
(a) 88 cm² (b) 132 cm² (c) 176 cm² (d) 44 cm²
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is comparing perimeters instead of areas. Circle: r = 14 cm, area = 616 cm²; square: side = 22 cm, area = 484 cm²; difference = 132 cm², so the answer is 132 cm².
Source: NCERT Class 7 Mathematics

116. The circumferences of two circles are in the ratio 5:3. The ratio of their areas is
(a) 5:3 (b) 25:9 (c) 3:5 (d) 9:25
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is keeping the ratio 5:3 for areas; areas scale with the square of the radii. Since circumference ratio = radius ratio = 5:3, area ratio = 25:9, so the answer is 25:9.
Source: NCERT Class 7 Mathematics

117. The perimeter of a semicircle (including the diameter) is 72 cm. Its area (π = 22/7) is
(a) 616 cm² (b) 308 cm² (c) 154 cm² (d) 462 cm²
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is using πr = 72 (ignoring the diameter) or computing the full circle's area 616 cm². Here r(22/7 + 2) = 72 gives r = 14 cm and area = ½πr² = 308 cm², so the answer is 308 cm².
Source: PYQ-style

118. A solid cylinder of radius 5 cm and height 8 cm is melted and recast into small cones of radius 1 cm and height 2 cm. The number of cones obtained is
(a) 200 (b) 150 (c) 400 (d) 300
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is forgetting the cone's 1/3 factor, which gives 100. Cylinder volume = 200π and each cone = 2π/3, so number = 200π ÷ (2π/3) = 300, so the answer is 300.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Mathematics

119. The curved surface area of a cone is 550 cm² and its slant height is 25 cm. Its height (π = 22/7) is
(a) 24 cm (b) 7 cm (c) 15 cm (d) 20 cm
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is reporting the radius 7 cm as the height. From πrl = 550, r = 7 cm, and h = √(25² − 7²) = √576 = 24 cm, so the answer is 24 cm.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Mathematics

120. The dimensions of a cuboid are in the ratio 2:1:1. If its volume is 54 cm³, its total surface area is
(a) 90 cm² (b) 72 cm² (c) 126 cm² (d) 108 cm²
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is using x = 3 directly as a dimension. Volume = 2x³ = 54 gives x = 3, so dimensions are 6, 3, 3 cm and TSA = 2(18 + 9 + 18) = 90 cm², so the answer is 90 cm².
Source: NCERT Class 8 Mathematics

121. A solid is formed by joining a cone on top of a cylinder of the same base radius 3 cm. The total height of the solid is 14 cm and the height of the cone is 4 cm. The total surface area of the solid (π = 22/7) is
(a) 264 cm² (b) 330 cm² (c) 198 cm² (d) 396 cm²
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is adding both circular bases or the cone's base, which are not exposed. Cylinder height = 10 cm, slant height = 5 cm; TSA = 2πrh + πrl + πr² = 60π + 15π + 9π = 84π = 264 cm², so the answer is 264 cm².
Source: NCERT Class 10 Mathematics

122. A toy is made by joining a hemisphere and a cone of the same base radius 7 cm. The height of the cone is 24 cm. The total surface area of the toy, excluding the flat base, (π = 22/7) is
(a) 858 cm² (b) 462 cm² (c) 1012 cm² (d) 704 cm²
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is adding the cone's base circle, which is hidden inside the joint. Slant height = √(7² + 24²) = 25 cm; exposed area = πrl + 2πr² = 175π + 98π = 273π = 858 cm², so the answer is 858 cm².
Source: NCERT Class 10 Mathematics

123. A solid is composed of a cylinder with hemispherical ends. The total length of the solid is 20 cm and the diameter of each hemisphere is 14 cm. Its total surface area (π = 22/7) is
(a) 968 cm² (b) 792 cm² (c) 880 cm² (d) 1056 cm²
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is taking the cylinder length as 20 cm instead of subtracting the two hemisphere radii. Cylinder length = 20 − 14 = 6 cm; TSA = 2πrh + 2 × 2πr² = 84π + 196π = 280π = 880 cm², so the answer is 880 cm².
Source: PYQ-style

124. A solid hemisphere of radius 9 cm is melted and drawn into a wire of diameter 3 mm. The length of the wire (in metres) is
(a) 108 m (b) 324 m (c) 432 m (d) 216 m
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is forgetting to convert 3 mm to 0.3 cm (radius 0.15 cm). Hemisphere volume = (2/3)π × 729 = 486π cm³; length = 486π / (0.0225π) = 21600 cm = 216 m, so the answer is 216 m.
Source: PYQ-style

125. A cylindrical water pipe has external diameter 16 cm and thickness 2 cm. Water flows through it at 3.5 m/s. The volume of water discharged in 1 minute (in litres) is (π = 22/7)
(a) 2376 L (b) 1584 L (c) 3168 L (d) 792 L
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is using the external radius 8 cm instead of the inner radius 6 cm. Inner area = 36π cm² = 36π × 10⁻⁴ m²; discharge = 36π × 10⁻⁴ × 3.5 × 60 = 2.376 m³ = 2376 L, so the answer is 2376 L.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Mathematics
