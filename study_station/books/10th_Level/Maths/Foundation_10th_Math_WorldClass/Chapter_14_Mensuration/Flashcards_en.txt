Flashcards: Mensuration

Card 1
Front: What is the one-word difference between 'fencing' a garden and 'carpeting' a room in math language?
Back: Perimeter (fencing – distance around) vs. Area (carpeting – surface covered).

Card 2
Front: If I give you a rectangle with length l and breadth b, how do you quickly find the total length of its boundary?
Back: Perimeter = 2(l + b). It's like walking along two lengths and two breadths.

Card 3
Front: A square's side is 'a'. How many times do I need to walk its side to complete a full round?
Back: 4 times. Perimeter = 4a.

Card 4
Front: For a triangle with sides a, b, c, what is the simplest perimeter formula?
Back: Just add them: Perimeter = a + b + c.

Card 5
Front: A circle's perimeter has a special name. What is it?
Back: Circumference. Formula: C = 2πr or πd.

Card 6
Front: Why do we multiply length and breadth to get the area of a rectangle?
Back: Because you count the number of unit squares that fit inside; it's rows times columns. Area = l × b.

Card 7
Front: If a triangle has base b and height h, why is its area exactly half of a rectangle with the same base and height?
Back: Because any triangle is exactly half of a parallelogram (or rectangle) when you duplicate and flip it. So Area = ½ × b × h.

Card 8
Front: What's the shortcut formula for the area of an equilateral triangle with side a?
Back: Area = (√3/4) a². Derived from height = (√3/2)a.

Card 9
Front: True or False: For a given perimeter, a circle gives you the largest possible area among all shapes.
Back: True. That's Queen Dido's secret; a circle encloses maximum area for a given boundary length.

Card 10
Front: Which has more cheese: one 14-inch diameter pizza or two 7-inch diameter pizzas? (Use πr²)
Back: One 14-inch pizza (radius 7) area = 49π; two 7-inch pizzas (each radius 3.5) total area = 2 × 12.25π = 24.5π. The single large pizza gives double! So one 14-inch wins.

Card 11
Front: What is the weird mnemonic to remember that circumference = 2πr and area = πr² using "pie"?
Back: "Two pies are round the fence" for 2πr (circumference), and "Pie are squared" for πr² (area). Picture a square pie shouting "I'm squared!"

Card 12
Front: In the mnemonic "Two pies are round the fence," what does the word 'round' represent?
Back: "Round" hints at the circular fence, i.e., circumference. So 2πr is the round fence protecting two pies.

Card 13
Front: Why does the area formula of a circle contain r² but circumference only r?
Back: Area is like covering a surface (two-dimensional), so radius gets squared. Circumference is like a length (one-dimensional), so radius appears once.

Card 14
Front: A cow is tied to a peg with a rope 7 m long. How much grass area can it graze? (Take π = 22/7)
Back: Area = πr² = (22/7) × 7 × 7 = 154 m².

Card 15
Front: How do you remember that a semicircle's perimeter is πr + 2r, not just πr?
Back: You need the curved part (half circumference = πr) plus the straight diameter (2r). Always add the straight edge! Memory cue: "Half a pie, plus the line."

Card 16
Front: If you stretch a wire to make a square of side 10 cm, then reshape it into a circle, what stays constant?
Back: The perimeter/circumference stays constant. Perimeter of square = 40 cm becomes circumference = 2πr = 40, so r = 20/π cm.

Card 17
Front: What's the fastest way to recall that a triangle's area is ½ base × height?
Back: Think of a right triangle as half of a rectangle. Or remember "Half a sandwich" – base × height is the whole sandwich, you get half.

Card 18
Front: In the playground, your teacher asks you to run along the boundary once. Are you measuring perimeter or area?
Back: Perimeter – you are tracing the distance around the playground.

Card 19
Front: If I give you a rectangle 8 cm by 3 cm, and cut it along the diagonal, what is the area of each piece?
Back: Each triangle has area = ½ × 8 × 3 = 12 cm².

Card 20
Front: Which formula would you use to find the length of a ribbon needed to go exactly once around a circular cake of radius r?
Back: Circumference = 2πr.
