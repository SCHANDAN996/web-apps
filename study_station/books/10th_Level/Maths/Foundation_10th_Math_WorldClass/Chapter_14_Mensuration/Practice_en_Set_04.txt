Set 4 — Mensuration MCQs (All Medium: 76–100)

76. A rectangular field has an area of 240 m² and its breadth is 12 m. Find the cost of fencing it at ₹15 per metre.
(a) ₹720 (b) ₹1440 (c) ₹960 (d) ₹1800
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is fencing only two sides or using the area directly as the perimeter. Length = 240/12 = 20 m, perimeter = 2(20+12) = 64 m, so cost = 64 × 15, and the answer is ₹960.
Source: NCERT Class 6 Mathematics

77. The ratio of the length to the breadth of a rectangle is 3:2. If the perimeter is 100 cm, then its area is
(a) 300 cm² (b) 400 cm² (c) 600 cm² (d) 500 cm²
Answer: (c)
Solution: Taking 3x and 2x as the sides, 2(3x+2x) = 100 gives x = 10, so the sides are 30 cm and 20 cm. The trap is treating 3x × 2x = 6x² with x = 10 as 60 cm²; the area is 30 × 20, so the answer is 600 cm².
Source: NCERT Class 6 Mathematics

78. The side of an equilateral triangle is increased by 20%. The percentage increase in its area is
(a) 44% (b) 40% (c) 20% (d) 33%
Answer: (a)
Solution: Area varies as the square of the side, so the new area is 1.2² = 1.44 times the old. The trap is doubling 20% to get 40%; the actual increase is 0.44 × 100, so the answer is 44%.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Mathematics

79. A wire is bent in the form of a circle of radius 35 cm. It is then straightened and bent into a square. The area of the square is (π = 22/7)
(a) 3025 cm² (b) 12100 cm² (c) 1960 cm² (d) 3850 cm²
Answer: (a)
Solution: Circumference = 2 × (22/7) × 35 = 220 cm, which becomes the perimeter of the square, so the side is 55 cm. The trap is using 220 cm as the side itself; squaring 55 gives the answer, 3025 cm².
Source: NCERT Exemplar Class 7 Mathematics

80. The area of a circle is 154 cm². If its radius is doubled, the circumference of the new circle is (π = 22/7)
(a) 176 cm (b) 88 cm (c) 44 cm (d) 352 cm
Answer: (b)
Solution: From πr² = 154, r² = 49, so r = 7 cm and the new radius is 14 cm. The trap is doubling the original circumference of 44 cm twice to get 176 cm; the new circumference is 2 × (22/7) × 14, so the answer is 88 cm.
Source: NCERT Class 7 Mathematics

81. The area of a semicircle is 77 cm². Its perimeter (including the diameter) is (π = 22/7)
(a) 36 cm (b) 22 cm (c) 44 cm (d) 50 cm
Answer: (a)
Solution: ½ × (22/7) × r² = 77 gives r² = 49, so r = 7 cm. The trap is reporting only the curved part πr = 22 cm and forgetting the diameter; the perimeter is 22 + 14, so the answer is 36 cm.
Source: PYQ-style

82. A sector of a circle of radius 14 cm and central angle 90° is folded to form a cone. The slant height of the cone is
(a) 14 cm (b) 7 cm (c) 28 cm (d) 10.5 cm
Answer: (a)
Solution: When a sector is folded into a cone, the radius of the sector becomes the slant height of the cone. The trap is halving the radius thinking of the quarter angle; the slant height equals the sector's radius, so the answer is 14 cm.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Mathematics

83. The volume of a cube is 729 cm³. Its total surface area is
(a) 324 cm² (b) 972 cm² (c) 729 cm² (d) 486 cm²
Answer: (d)
Solution: a³ = 729 gives a = 9 cm. The trap is computing the lateral surface area 4a² = 324 cm² instead of all six faces; TSA = 6 × 81, so the answer is 486 cm².
Source: NCERT Class 8 Mathematics

84. A cuboid of dimensions 12 cm × 9 cm × 8 cm is melted and recast into smaller cubes of edge 2 cm. The number of smaller cubes obtained is
(a) 54 (b) 216 (c) 108 (d) 81
Answer: (c)
Solution: Volume of the cuboid is 12 × 9 × 8 = 864 cm³ and each small cube has volume 8 cm³. The trap is dividing the surface areas instead of the volumes; 864/8 gives the answer, 108 cubes.
Source: NCERT Class 8 Mathematics

85. The curved surface area of a cylinder is 2640 cm² and its base radius is 15 cm. Its volume is (π = 22/7)
(a) 17820 cm³ (b) 23100 cm³ (c) 19800 cm³ (d) 16500 cm³
Answer: (c)
Solution: 2 × (22/7) × 15 × h = 2640 gives h = 28 cm. The trap is using h = 14 cm by dropping a factor of 2 from the CSA formula; volume = (22/7) × 225 × 28, so the answer is 19800 cm³.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Mathematics

86. A cone and a cylinder have the same base radius and equal volumes. If the height of the cylinder is 15 cm, the height of the cone is
(a) 5 cm (b) 15 cm (c) 45 cm (d) 30 cm
Answer: (c)
Solution: πr² × 15 = (1/3)πr² × h gives h = 3 × 15. The trap is dividing by 3 to get 5 cm instead of multiplying; the cone must be three times as tall, so the answer is 45 cm.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Mathematics

87. The surface area of a sphere is 616 cm². If its radius is tripled, the volume of the new sphere is (π = 22/7)
(a) 2772 cm³ (b) 12936 cm³ (c) 38808 cm³ (d) 1848 cm³
Answer: (c)
Solution: 4πr² = 616 gives r² = 49, so r = 7 cm and the new radius is 21 cm. The trap is tripling the original volume instead of cubing the scale factor; volume = (4/3) × (22/7) × 9261, so the answer is 38808 cm³.
Source: PYQ-style

88. A hemispherical bowl of radius 10.5 cm is completely filled with water. This water is poured into a cylindrical vessel of base radius 7 cm. The height of water in the cylinder is (π = 22/7)
(a) 31.5 cm (b) 10.5 cm (c) 21 cm (d) 15.75 cm
Answer: (d)
Solution: Volume of the hemisphere = (2/3) × (22/7) × (10.5)³ = 2425.5 cm³, and the cylinder's base area is (22/7) × 49 = 154 cm². The trap is using the full sphere's volume, which doubles the height; 2425.5/154 gives the answer, 15.75 cm.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Mathematics

89. The area of a rhombus is 300 cm² and one of its diagonals is 24 cm. The length of the other diagonal is
(a) 25 cm (b) 12.5 cm (c) 20 cm (d) 15 cm
Answer: (a)
Solution: Area = ½ d₁d₂, so 300 = ½ × 24 × d₂. The trap is forgetting the factor ½ and getting 12.5 cm; d₂ = 300/12, so the answer is 25 cm.
Source: NCERT Class 8 Mathematics

90. A rectangular park measures 60 m by 40 m. A 2.5 m wide path runs around it on the outside. The area of the path is
(a) 625 m² (b) 525 m² (c) 425 m² (d) 725 m²
Answer: (b)
Solution: The outer dimensions are 65 m and 45 m, giving an outer area of 2925 m² against an inner area of 2400 m². The trap is adding 2.5 m only once to each side; the path area is 2925 − 2400, so the answer is 525 m².
Source: NCERT Class 7 Mathematics

91. The sides of a triangle are 13 cm, 14 cm, and 15 cm. Its area is
(a) 84 cm² (b) 42 cm² (c) 168 cm² (d) 96 cm²
Answer: (a)
Solution: By Heron's formula, s = 21 and area = √(21 × 8 × 7 × 6) = √7056. The trap is using s = 42 (the full perimeter) inside the formula; √7056 = 84, so the answer is 84 cm².
Source: NCERT Class 9 Mathematics

92. The perimeter of a right-angled triangle is 60 cm and its hypotenuse is 26 cm. The area of the triangle is
(a) 240 cm² (b) 120 cm² (c) 60 cm² (d) 130 cm²
Answer: (b)
Solution: The legs satisfy a + b = 34 and a² + b² = 676, so (a+b)² − (a²+b²) = 2ab = 480, giving ab = 240. The trap is reporting ab = 240 cm² as the area; the area is ½ab, so the answer is 120 cm².
Source: NCERT Class 10 Mathematics

93. Water flows through a cylindrical pipe of inner diameter 14 cm at a speed of 5 metres per second. The number of litres of water discharged by the pipe in one minute is (π = 22/7)
(a) 462000 L (b) 4620 L (c) 6600 L (d) 3300 L
Answer: (b)
Solution: Radius = 0.07 m, cross-section = (22/7) × 0.0049 = 0.0154 m², and in 60 s the water column is 300 m long, giving 4.62 m³. The trap is a unit slip that yields 462000 L; since 1 m³ = 1000 L, the answer is 4620 L.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Mathematics

94. A sphere of diameter 12 cm is dropped into a cylindrical vessel of base diameter 20 cm partially filled with water. The rise in the water level is
(a) 1.44 cm (b) 4.32 cm (c) 3.6 cm (d) 2.88 cm
Answer: (d)
Solution: The sphere's volume is (4/3)π × 6³ = 288π cm³ and the vessel's base area is 100π cm². The trap is using the sphere's diameter 12 as its radius, which inflates the rise eightfold; 288π/100π gives the answer, 2.88 cm.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Mathematics

95. The total surface area of a cone is 704 cm² and its slant height is 25 cm. Its height is (π = 22/7)
(a) 7 cm (b) 24 cm (c) 15 cm (d) 20 cm
Answer: (b)
Solution: πr(l + r) = 704 gives r(25 + r) = 224, so r = 7 cm. The trap is reporting the radius 7 cm as the height; h = √(25² − 7²) = √576, so the answer is 24 cm.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Mathematics

96. A solid hemisphere of radius 3 cm is melted and recast into a right circular cone of height 9 cm. The base radius of the cone is
(a) 3 cm (b) 1.5 cm (c) √3 cm (d) √6 cm
Answer: (d)
Solution: Hemisphere volume = (2/3)π × 27 = 18π, and cone volume = (1/3)πr² × 9 = 3πr². The trap is assuming the radius stays 3 cm; equating gives r² = 6, so the answer is √6 cm.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Mathematics

97. The length of a rectangle is increased by 25% and its breadth is decreased by 20%. The percentage change in its area is
(a) 5% increase (b) 5% decrease (c) 4% decrease (d) 0%
Answer: (d)
Solution: The new area is 1.25 × 0.80 = 1.00 times the old area. The trap is subtracting the percentages to get a 5% increase; since the multiplier is exactly 1, the answer is 0%.
Source: PYQ-style

98. The diagonal of a rectangle is 26 cm and one of its sides is 24 cm. Its perimeter is
(a) 68 cm (b) 72 cm (c) 56 cm (d) 64 cm
Answer: (a)
Solution: The other side is √(26² − 24²) = √100 = 10 cm. The trap is adding the diagonal into the perimeter; the perimeter is 2(24 + 10), so the answer is 68 cm.
Source: NCERT Class 8 Mathematics

99. The area of a trapezium is 150 cm² and the lengths of its parallel sides are in the ratio 2:3. If the height is 10 cm, the difference between the lengths of the parallel sides is
(a) 10 cm (b) 5 cm (c) 4 cm (d) 6 cm
Answer: (d)
Solution: With sides 2x and 3x, area = ½ × 5x × 10 = 25x = 150, so x = 6. The trap is reporting the sum 5x = 30 cm or the longer side 18 cm; the difference is 3x − 2x = x, so the answer is 6 cm.
Source: NCERT Class 8 Mathematics

100. A solid metallic cylinder of base radius 5 cm and height 10 cm is melted and drawn into a wire of diameter 4 mm. The length of the wire (in metres) is
(a) 125 m (b) 62.5 m (c) 31.25 m (d) 25 m
Answer: (b)
Solution: Cylinder volume = π × 25 × 10 = 250π cm³, and the wire's cross-section is π × (0.2)² = 0.04π cm². The trap is using 4 mm as the wire's radius, which quarters the length; 250π/0.04π = 6250 cm, so the answer is 62.5 m.
Source: PYQ-style
