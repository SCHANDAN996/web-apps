Set 2 — Mensuration MCQs (All Medium: 26–50)

26. A wire is bent into a square of side 22 cm. If the same wire is re-bent into a circle, the area of the circle is (Take π = 22/7)
(a) 154 cm² (b) 484 cm² (c) 308 cm² (d) 616 cm²
Answer: (d)
Solution: The tempting wrong option 484 cm² is just 22², the area of the square, not the circle. Perimeter of square = 4×22 = 88 cm = circumference, so 2πr = 88 gives r = 14 cm and area = (22/7)×14² = 616 cm², so the answer is 616 cm².
Source: PYQ-style

27. The perimeter of a rectangle is equal to the perimeter of a square of side 15 cm. If the length of the rectangle is 20 cm, its area is
(a) 250 cm² (b) 150 cm² (c) 100 cm² (d) 200 cm²
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is 150 cm², obtained by wrongly taking breadth as 7.5 cm (half of 15). Square perimeter = 60 cm, so l + b = 30, giving b = 10 cm and area = 20×10 = 200 cm², so the answer is 200 cm².
Source: NCERT Exemplar Class 8 Mathematics

28. The area of a sector of a circle with radius 6 cm and central angle 60° is (π = 3.14)
(a) 37.68 cm² (b) 9.42 cm² (c) 18.84 cm² (d) 12.56 cm²
Answer: (c)
Solution: The tempting 37.68 cm² comes from using 120° instead of 60°. Sector area = (60/360)×3.14×36 = (1/6)×113.04 = 18.84 cm², so the answer is 18.84 cm².
Source: NCERT Class 10 Mathematics

29. A rectangular plot is 40 m long and 30 m wide. A 2 m wide path runs inside the plot along its perimeter. The area of the path is
(a) 300 m² (b) 240 m² (c) 280 m² (d) 264 m²
Answer: (d)
Solution: The common trap is subtracting only 2 m from each side instead of 4 m, which inflates the path area. Inner dimensions are 36 m × 26 m = 936 m², so path area = 1200 − 936 = 264 m², so the answer is 264 m².
Source: NCERT Class 7 Mathematics

30. The volume of a cube is numerically equal to its total surface area. Its edge length is
(a) 12 units (b) 3 units (c) 6 units (d) 1 unit
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is 3 units, which comes from confusing TSA 6a² with lateral area 4a². Setting a³ = 6a² gives a = 6, so the answer is 6 units.
Source: NCERT Class 8 Mathematics

31. A solid sphere of radius 7 cm is melted and recast into small spheres of radius 3.5 cm. How many small spheres are obtained?
(a) 8 (b) 4 (c) 2 (d) 6
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is 4, from squaring the radius ratio instead of cubing it. Number of spheres = (7/3.5)³ = 2³ = 8, so the answer is 8.
Source: PYQ-style

32. The curved surface area of a cylinder is 528 cm² and its height is 14 cm. Its volume (π = 22/7) is
(a) 1584 cm³ (b) 2772 cm³ (c) 1848 cm³ (d) 1386 cm³
Answer: (a)
Solution: The tempting 1848 cm³ results from an arithmetic slip in solving 2πrh = 528. Here 88r = 528 gives r = 6 cm, and volume = (22/7)×36×14 = 1584 cm³, so the answer is 1584 cm³.
Source: PYQ-style

33. The diagonal of a rectangle is 10 cm and its length is 8 cm. The area of the rectangle is
(a) 60 cm² (b) 48 cm² (c) 36 cm² (d) 80 cm²
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is 80 cm², from multiplying diagonal by length. Breadth = √(10² − 8²) = 6 cm, so area = 8×6 = 48 cm², so the answer is 48 cm².
Source: NCERT Class 8 Mathematics

34. Two cubes each of volume 64 cm³ are joined end to end. The surface area of the resulting cuboid is
(a) 128 cm² (b) 160 cm² (c) 192 cm² (d) 144 cm²
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is 192 cm², which is just 2×6×16, ignoring that two faces are hidden on joining. Edge = 4 cm, cuboid is 8×4×4, TSA = 2(32+16+32) = 160 cm², so the answer is 160 cm².
Source: NCERT Class 10 Mathematics

35. The area of an equilateral triangle with side 4√3 cm is
(a) 6√3 cm² (b) 24√3 cm² (c) 12√3 cm² (d) 16√3 cm²
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is 24√3 cm², from forgetting the factor 1/4 in the formula. Area = (√3/4)×(4√3)² = (√3/4)×48 = 12√3 cm², so the answer is 12√3 cm².
Source: NCERT Class 9 Mathematics

36. The radius of a sphere is increased by 50%. The percentage increase in its volume is
(a) 50% (b) 125% (c) 150% (d) 237.5%
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is 125%, which is the increase in surface area type thinking or (1.5)² confusion. Volume scales as r³, so new volume = 3.375 times, an increase of 237.5%, so the answer is 237.5%.
Source: PYQ-style

37. A right circular cone of radius 6 cm and slant height 10 cm has a curved surface area of (π = 3.14)
(a) 282.6 cm² (b) 188.4 cm² (c) 125.6 cm² (d) 376.8 cm²
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is 376.8 cm², from using 2πrl (the cylinder formula). CSA of cone = πrl = 3.14×6×10 = 188.4 cm², so the answer is 188.4 cm².
Source: NCERT Class 9 Mathematics

38. The perimeter of the floor of a rectangular room is 36 m and its height is 4 m. The area of the four walls of the room is
(a) 108 m² (b) 72 m² (c) 288 m² (d) 144 m²
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is 288 m², from doubling the correct product. Area of four walls = height × perimeter of floor = 4×36 = 144 m², so the answer is 144 m².
Source: NCERT Class 9 Mathematics

39. A wire is in the shape of a circle of radius 42 cm. It is cut and bent into a square. The side of the square is
(a) 66 cm (b) 33 cm (c) 44 cm (d) 88 cm
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is 33 cm, from dividing the circumference by 8 instead of 4. Circumference = 2×(22/7)×42 = 264 cm, so side = 264/4 = 66 cm, so the answer is 66 cm.
Source: NCERT Exemplar Class 8 Mathematics

40. The dimensions of a cuboid are in the ratio 2:3:4 and its total surface area is 468 cm². The volume of the cuboid is
(a) 432 cm³ (b) 648 cm³ (c) 288 cm³ (d) 504 cm³
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is 432 cm³, from using 26x² = 468 and forgetting the factor 2 in TSA. Here 52x² = 468 gives x = 3, dimensions 6, 9, 12, volume = 648 cm³, so the answer is 648 cm³.
Source: PYQ-style

41. A rectangular field has its length and breadth in the ratio 5:3. Its area is 3.75 hectares. The cost of fencing it at ₹5 per metre is
(a) ₹4000 (b) ₹2000 (c) ₹1000 (d) ₹400
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is ₹2000, from forgetting to convert hectares to m² (1 hectare = 10000 m²). Area = 37500 m² gives x = 50, sides 250 m and 150 m, perimeter 800 m, cost = 800×5 = ₹4000, so the answer is ₹4000.
Source: PYQ-style

42. The volume of a sphere is 4851 cm³. Its surface area is (π = 22/7)
(a) 1155 cm² (b) 616 cm² (c) 1386 cm² (d) 1732 cm²
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is 616 cm², which is the surface area for radius 7 cm, not the radius found here. Solving (4/3)πr³ = 4851 gives r = 10.5 cm, and 4πr² = 4×(22/7)×110.25 = 1386 cm², so the answer is 1386 cm².
Source: PYQ-style

43. The slant height of a cone is 25 cm and its curved surface area is 550 cm². Its volume is (π = 22/7)
(a) 1232 cm³ (b) 2156 cm³ (c) 616 cm³ (d) 3080 cm³
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is 2156 cm³, from using slant height 25 cm instead of vertical height in the volume formula. From πrl = 550, r = 7 cm, h = √(625−49) = 24 cm, volume = (1/3)×(22/7)×49×24 = 1232 cm³, so the answer is 1232 cm³.
Source: NCERT Exemplar Class 9 Mathematics

44. A tank is 25 m long, 12 m wide, and 6 m deep. The cost of plastering its walls and bottom at 75 paise per sq. m is
(a) ₹558 (b) ₹279 (c) ₹5580 (d) ₹2790
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is ₹5580, from treating 75 paise as ₹75. Walls area = 2×6×(25+12) = 444 m², bottom = 300 m², total 744 m², cost = 744×0.75 = ₹558, so the answer is ₹558.
Source: PYQ-style

45. A solid metallic cylinder of base radius 3 cm and height 5 cm is melted to form a solid cone of height 15 cm. The radius of the cone is
(a) 3 cm (b) 1.5 cm (c) 2 cm (d) 4 cm
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is 1.5 cm, from halving the radius unnecessarily. Equating volumes, π×9×5 = (1/3)πR²×15 gives R² = 9, so R = 3 cm, so the answer is 3 cm.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Mathematics

46. If the length of a rectangle is halved and the breadth is doubled, then the area of the new rectangle
(a) becomes half (b) remains the same (c) becomes double (d) becomes four times
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is "becomes double", from focusing only on the breadth change. New area = (l/2)×(2b) = lb, unchanged, so the answer is that the area remains the same.
Source: NCERT Class 7 Mathematics

47. The perimeter of a semicircular protractor whose radius is 7 cm is (π = 22/7)
(a) 44 cm (b) 58 cm (c) 22 cm (d) 36 cm
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is 22 cm, which is only the curved part πr and forgets the diameter. Perimeter = πr + 2r = 22 + 14 = 36 cm, so the answer is 36 cm.
Source: PYQ-style

48. A cone, a hemisphere, and a cylinder have equal bases and the same height (equal to the radius). The ratio of their volumes is
(a) 1 : 3 : 2 (b) 2 : 1 : 3 (c) 1 : 2 : 3 (d) 3 : 1 : 2
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is 1:3:2, from misplacing the hemisphere's volume. With h = r, cone = (1/3)πr³, hemisphere = (2/3)πr³, cylinder = πr³, giving 1:2:3, so the answer is 1 : 2 : 3.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Mathematics

49. The area of a circle is 154 cm². If its radius is tripled, the area of the new circle is
(a) 462 cm² (b) 1232 cm² (c) 1386 cm² (d) 616 cm²
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is 462 cm², from tripling the area instead of multiplying by 9. Original r = 7 cm, new r = 21 cm, new area = (22/7)×441 = 1386 cm², so the answer is 1386 cm².
Source: NCERT Class 7 Mathematics

50. A rectangular sheet of paper 44 cm × 20 cm is rolled along its length to form a cylinder. The volume of the cylinder is (π = 22/7)
(a) 6160 cm³ (b) 3080 cm³ (c) 1540 cm³ (d) 2200 cm³
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is 6160 cm³, from taking 44 cm as the height instead of the circumference. Here 2πr = 44 gives r = 7 cm, h = 20 cm, volume = (22/7)×49×20 = 3080 cm³, so the answer is 3080 cm³.
Source: NCERT Class 8 Mathematics
