Practice Set 2 — Mixture & Alligation (Questions 26–50: All Medium)

26. In what ratio must a shopkeeper mix two varieties of sugar costing ₹45 per kg and ₹55 per kg so that by selling the mixture at ₹60 per kg he earns a profit of 20%?
(a) 2 : 1 (b) 1 : 2 (c) 3 : 2 (d) 1 : 1
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is applying alligation directly on ₹60, but ₹60 is the selling price, not the cost price. CP of the mixture = 60 × 100/120 = ₹50 per kg. Alligation gives (55 − 50) : (50 − 45) = 5 : 5, so the answer is 1 : 1.
Source: PYQ-style

27. A vessel contains 72 litres of a mixture of milk and water in the ratio 7 : 5. How much water must be added to make the ratio 1 : 1?
(a) 6 litres (b) 10 litres (c) 8 litres (d) 12 litres
Answer: (d)
Solution: Milk = (7/12) × 72 = 42 L and water = 30 L; the trap is adding water to the total instead of only balancing water with milk. If x litres of water are added, 42 = 30 + x, giving x = 12. So the answer is 12 litres.
Source: NCERT Class 8 Mathematics

28. From a 100-litre mixture of acid and water in the ratio 3 : 2, 20 litres of the mixture is drawn off and replaced with water. What is the new ratio of acid to water?
(a) 13 : 12 (b) 1 : 1 (c) 12 : 13 (d) 2 : 3
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is assuming only water is removed; the 20 L drawn off contains both acid and water in the same 3 : 2 ratio. Acid left = 60 − 12 = 48 L, water left = 40 − 8 + 20 = 52 L, giving 48 : 52. So the answer is 12 : 13.
Source: PYQ-style

29. A mixture of 40 litres of milk and water contains 30% water. How much water must be evaporated so that the resulting mixture has 20% water?
(a) 5 litres (b) 4 litres (c) 6 litres (d) 8 litres
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is reducing 30% to 20% of the original 40 L; evaporation changes the total volume while milk stays fixed at 28 L. Since 28 L is 80% of the new total, new total = 35 L, so water evaporated = 40 − 35 = 5 litres.

30. Two solutions of alcohol and water contain 30% and 50% alcohol respectively. In what ratio must they be mixed to get a solution containing 35% alcohol?
(a) 1 : 3 (b) 3 : 2 (c) 3 : 1 (d) 2 : 1
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is reversing the alligation differences; the quantity of the weaker solution pairs with the larger difference. Alligation gives (50 − 35) : (35 − 30) = 15 : 5 for the 30% and 50% solutions respectively. So the answer is 3 : 1.
Source: NCERT Class 8 Mathematics

31. A trader has 50 kg of rice, a part of which he sells at 14% profit and the rest at 6% loss. On the whole, his gain is 4%. What is the quantity sold at 14% profit?
(a) 25 kg (b) 15 kg (c) 20 kg (d) 10 kg
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is treating the 6% loss as +6% in alligation; it must be taken as −6%. Alligation on +14% and −6% with mean +4% gives (14 − 4) : (4 − (−6)) = 10 : 10 = 1 : 1, so half the rice is sold at 14% profit. So the answer is 25 kg.
Source: PYQ-style

32. A jar contains 60 litres of a mixture of milk and water in the ratio 2 : 1. How much of the mixture must be taken out and replaced with water so that the ratio of milk to water becomes 1 : 1?
(a) 10 litres (b) 15 litres (c) 12 litres (d) 20 litres
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is removing only milk; the drawn-off mixture removes milk and water in the 2 : 1 ratio. With x litres replaced, milk left = 40 − 2x/3 and water = 20 + 2x/3; equating them gives 20 = 4x/3. So the answer is 15 litres.
Source: PYQ-style

33. A milkman buys milk at ₹24 per litre and mixes it with water. He sells the mixture at ₹24 per litre and makes a profit of 20%. What is the ratio of milk to water in the mixture?
(a) 5 : 2 (b) 10 : 1 (c) 5 : 1 (d) 6 : 1
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is using ₹24 as the cost price of the mixture; it is the selling price. CP of mixture = 24 × 100/120 = ₹20, and alligation between ₹24 (milk) and ₹0 (water) gives water : milk = 4 : 20. So the answer is 5 : 1.
Source: PYQ-style

34. A container has 120 litres of a mixture of spirit and water in the ratio 5 : 3. 40 litres of this mixture is taken out and replaced with water, and this process is repeated once more. Find the ratio of spirit to water in the final mixture.
(a) 25 : 38 (b) 25 : 47 (c) 25 : 65 (d) 25 : 56
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is applying the reduction factor only once; the process happens twice. Spirit left = 75 × (1 − 40/120)² = 75 × 4/9 = 100/3 L, so water = 120 − 100/3 = 260/3 L, giving 100 : 260. So the answer is 25 : 65.
Source: PYQ-style

35. A solution contains alcohol and water in the ratio 3 : 1. 20 litres of the solution is drawn out and 12 litres of water is added. The new ratio of alcohol to water becomes 5 : 3. Find the initial quantity of the solution.
(a) 80 litres (b) 64 litres (c) 48 litres (d) 96 litres
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is forgetting that the 20 L drawn off removes alcohol and water in the 3 : 1 ratio, i.e. 15 L alcohol and 5 L water. Taking the initial volume as 4x, (3x − 15)/(x + 7) = 5/3 gives 9x − 45 = 5x + 35, so x = 20. So the answer is 80 litres.
Source: PYQ-style

36. Two alloys A and B contain copper and zinc in the ratios 7 : 3 and 3 : 2 respectively. In what ratio must they be mixed to obtain an alloy containing copper and zinc in the ratio 53 : 27?
(a) 3 : 5 (b) 4 : 3 (c) 5 : 3 (d) 3 : 4
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is applying alligation on the ratios directly instead of on copper fractions 7/10, 3/5 and 53/80. Alligation gives (7/10 − 53/80) = 3/80 and (53/80 − 3/5) = 5/80, so A : B = 5/80 : 3/80. So the answer is 5 : 3.
Source: PYQ-style

37. A vessel contains 80 litres of a mixture of alcohol and water in the ratio 5 : 3. If 16 litres of the mixture is taken out and replaced with pure alcohol, what is the ratio of alcohol to water in the resultant mixture?
(a) 5 : 2 (b) 3 : 1 (c) 2 : 1 (d) 7 : 3
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is adding 16 L of alcohol without first removing alcohol and water in the 5 : 3 ratio. Alcohol left = 50 − 10 + 16 = 56 L and water left = 30 − 6 = 24 L, giving 56 : 24. So the answer is 7 : 3.
Source: PYQ-style

38. A milkman buys 40 litres of pure milk at ₹50 per litre. He adds some water and sells the mixture at ₹50 per litre, thereby making a profit of 25%. How many litres of water did he add?
(a) 10 (b) 8 (c) 5 (d) 12
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is computing 25% of 40 L as 10 L without checking the selling price logic; here it works only through total SP. CP = ₹2000, SP = ₹2500, and at ₹50 per litre the total volume sold is 50 L. So the answer is 10 litres.

39. A solution of 60 litres of acid and water contains 40% acid. How much water must be evaporated so that the new solution contains 60% acid?
(a) 15 litres (b) 20 litres (c) 25 litres (d) 30 litres
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is taking 60% of the original 60 L; evaporation keeps the acid fixed at 24 L while the total shrinks. Since 24 L is 60% of the new total, new total = 40 L, so water evaporated = 60 − 40 = 20 litres.

40. Two alloys A and B contain copper and zinc in the ratios 5 : 4 and 1 : 1 respectively. If equal weights of A and B are melted together, what is the ratio of copper to zinc in the new alloy?
(a) 19 : 17 (b) 10 : 11 (c) 11 : 10 (d) 17 : 19
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is adding the ratio numbers directly (5 + 1 : 4 + 1); equal weights require adding fractions with a common denominator. Copper = 5/9 + 1/2 = 19/18 and zinc = 4/9 + 1/2 = 17/18 of the unit weight. So the answer is 19 : 17.
Source: PYQ-style

41. A mixture of 50 litres of milk and water contains 25% water. 10 litres of this mixture is taken out and replaced with 20 litres of water. What is the percentage of water in the final mixture?
(a) 40% (b) 50% (c) 45% (d) 55%
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is keeping the total at 50 L; removing 10 L and adding 20 L makes the final volume 60 L. Water left = 12.5 − 2.5 + 20 = 30 L, so water percentage = 30/60 × 100. So the answer is 50%.

42. A trader mixes 30 kg of rice at ₹10 per kg with 20 kg of rice at ₹15 per kg. He wants to make a profit of 20% by selling the mixture. At what price per kg should he sell the mixture?
(a) ₹12.00 (b) ₹14.40 (c) ₹13.20 (d) ₹15.00
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is adding 20% to ₹15 or averaging the two prices as ₹12.50; the correct mean CP is weighted. CP per kg = (300 + 300)/50 = ₹12, and SP = 12 × 1.2. So the answer is ₹14.40.
Source: PYQ-style

43. A jar has 80 litres of milk. 8 litres is withdrawn and replaced with water, and this process is carried out two more times. What is the quantity of milk left in the jar?
(a) 61.44 litres (b) 59.04 litres (c) 60.00 litres (d) 58.32 litres
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is subtracting 8 L three times (80 − 24 = 56 L); each withdrawal removes only 90% retention of the remaining milk. Milk left = 80 × (1 − 8/80)³ = 80 × 0.729. So the answer is 58.32 litres.

44. A mixture contains alcohol and water in the ratio 5 : 2. How much pure alcohol must be added to 28 litres of this mixture to make the ratio 3 : 1?
(a) 2 litres (b) 4 litres (c) 6 litres (d) 8 litres
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is changing both components; only alcohol is added, so water stays fixed at 8 L. Alcohol initially = 20 L, and (20 + x)/8 = 3/1 gives x = 4. So the answer is 4 litres.

45. In a 100-litre mixture of milk and water, the ratio of milk to water is 7 : 3. 20 litres of the mixture is drawn off and replaced with water. What is the difference between the quantities of milk and water in the final mixture?
(a) 12 litres (b) 10 litres (c) 16 litres (d) 20 litres
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is assuming the 20 L drawn off is pure milk; it removes milk and water in the 7 : 3 ratio. Milk left = 70 − 14 = 56 L and water = 30 − 6 + 20 = 44 L. So the answer is 12 litres.

46. Two varieties of pulses costing ₹50 per kg and ₹75 per kg are mixed in the ratio 3 : 2. If the mixture is sold at ₹75 per kg, what is the profit percentage?
(a) 15% (b) 25% (c) 20% (d) 30%
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is comparing ₹75 with the dearer variety's ₹75 and concluding zero profit; the mixture's cost is lower. CP per kg = (150 + 150)/5 = ₹60, so profit = (15/60) × 100. So the answer is 25%.

47. A container holds 96 litres of a mixture of acid and water in the ratio 2 : 1. Some quantity of the mixture is taken out and replaced with water so that the ratio of acid to water becomes 1 : 2. Find the quantity of mixture replaced.
(a) 36 litres (b) 72 litres (c) 60 litres (d) 48 litres
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is removing only acid; the drawn-off mixture removes acid and water in the 2 : 1 ratio. With x litres replaced, (64 − 2x/3)/(32 + 2x/3) = 1/2 gives 128 − 4x/3 = 32 + 2x/3, so x = 48. So the answer is 48 litres.
Source: PYQ-style

48. Three vessels of equal capacity contain mixtures of milk and water in the ratios 3 : 1, 1 : 1 and 3 : 5 respectively. If the contents of all three vessels are mixed, what is the ratio of milk to water in the final mixture?
(a) 13 : 11 (b) 11 : 13 (c) 7 : 5 (d) 5 : 7
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is adding the ratio terms directly (3 + 1 + 3 : 1 + 1 + 5); equal capacities require averaging the milk fractions. Average milk fraction = (3/4 + 1/2 + 3/8)/3 = 13/24, so milk : water = 13 : 11. So the answer is 13 : 11.
Source: PYQ-style

49. A milkman has 40 litres of pure milk. He adds some water and sells the mixture at the cost price of pure milk per litre, thereby making a profit of 20%. How many litres of water did he add?
(a) 4 (b) 6 (c) 10 (d) 8
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is thinking no profit is possible when selling at cost price; the profit comes from the free water increasing the volume. With CP ₹1 per litre, CP = ₹40 and SP = ₹48, so 48 L is sold. So the answer is 8 litres.
Source: PYQ-style

50. In what ratio must rice at ₹10 per kg be mixed with rice at ₹18 per kg so that the mixture costs ₹11 per kg?
(a) 1 : 7 (b) 4 : 1 (c) 7 : 1 (d) 1 : 4
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is writing the differences in the same order as the prices; in alligation each quantity pairs with the opposite difference. The cheaper rice pairs with (18 − 11) = 7 and the dearer with (11 − 10) = 1. So the answer is 7 : 1.
Source: NCERT Class 8 Mathematics
