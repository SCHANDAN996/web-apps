🪄 Memory Tricks for Mixture & Alligation

1. The Cow and the Deer (Alligation Cross)

Picture a fat Cow (Cheaper) and a majestic Deer (Dearer) standing on either side of a seesaw. The Cow holds a placard saying "D – M" (the Deer's difference). The Deer holds a placard saying "M – C" (the Cow's difference).
Rule: Cow holds Deer's difference; Deer holds Cow's difference.
So Cheaper quantity = D – M, Dearer quantity = M – C.

2. Difference Dances Diagonally

Imagine a ballroom dance inside the alligation cross.
The difference on the left (D – M) waltzes across to the right and becomes the quantity of the Cheaper ingredient.
The difference on the right (M – C) twirls to the left and becomes the quantity of the Dearer ingredient.
The differences literally dance diagonally across the cross.

3. The Seesaw Swap

Visualize a playground seesaw with Expensive on one end, Cheap on the other, and the Mean as the balancing fulcrum.
The length of the Cheap side's arm is exactly the Expensive price's distance from the Mean (D – M).
The length of the Expensive side's arm is the Cheap price's distance from the Mean (M – C).
Longer arm = more quantity on the opposite side. The seesaw arms swap the differences.

4. Dear Cat, Meow Carefully

D – M (Dearer Minus Mean) gives the quantity of the Cheaper (Cat).
M – C (Mean Minus Cheaper) gives the quantity of the Dearer (Deer).
The phrase reminds you:
Dear (D) minus Mean (M) -> Cheaper (Cat)
Mean (M) minus Cheaper (C) -> Dearer (Deer)
"Dear Cat, Meow Carefully" – the first letter of each word links the pairs.

5. The Monkey and Patience (Replacement Formula)

A mischievous monkey steals a fraction of your patience every time you sit to study. That stolen patience is replaced by daydreams.
Formula: Patience left = Initial Patience × (1 – fraction stolen)^sessions.
Same as: Pure substance left = Original × (1 – replaced/total)^n
The monkey reminds you: removal is a fraction, then you multiply repeatedly.

6. "Water is Free, Milk is Money" (Dishonest Milkman Trick)

When solving dishonest milkman problems, remember:
"Water is Free, Milk is Money."
Water cost = ₹0, Milk cost = Actual price.
Profit % = (Water added / Pure milk) × 100.
Just think of a crying cow watching the milkman pour free tap water into her precious milk. The more water, the more profit – and the cow moos in protest!

7. The Replacement Wave (Successive Removal)

Think of the pure substance as a sandcastle on a beach. Each removal is a wave that washes away a fixed fraction of what remains. After n waves, the castle's height shrinks exponentially.
Formula: Remaining = Initial × (1 – wave strength)^n.
Wave strength = Volume removed / Total volume.
This visual of a crumbling sandcastle locks the (1 – r/t)^n formula forever.

8. Profit Alligation: "Same Base or Bust"

When using alligation for profit percentages on mixing, remember:
"Same Base or Bust!"
If the cost price per unit of the two goods is NOT the same, you cannot directly alligate profit percentages. You must first convert to actual profit per unit (e.g., profit per kg).
Otherwise, your answer will "bust" – like a piñata that explodes with wrong numbers.

9. The Concert Stage (Weighted Average)

Imagine a concert stage where each item is a singer. The loudness of each singer is their price, and the time they sing is their weight (quantity).
The average loudness (weighted average) = (Loudness1 × Time1 + Loudness2 × Time2) / Total Time.
Alligation is simply the reverse: given the average loudness and individual loudness, find how long each singer performed.
The stage balances the performance!

10. The Mixer Tap Analogy (Mixture of Mixtures)

Visualize two taps. Tap A pours a certain milk-water mix, Tap B pours a different mix. You want a specific final mix.
First, find the "milk-ness" (concentration) of each tap. Then use the Alligation Cross on those concentrations.
Think: "Taps have strengths; the cross finds the time each tap should run."

11. The Magic Triangle (Alligation Cross Shortcut)

Draw a triangle with points C (Cheaper), D (Dearer), and M (Mean) at the bottom center.
Connect the letters. The differences appear automatically:
   C         D
    \       /
      M
    /      \
(D–M)   (M–C)
The base numbers (D–M) and (M–C) are your ratio. Just remember: the difference on the right side goes to the left ingredient, and vice versa – like a crossover cable.

12. The "Don't Miss" Mantra

D – M  →  "Don't Miss"  →  Quantity of the Cheaper.
The twist: D – M gives the Cheap quantity because the Dearer's extra money buys the Cheap stuff. So, "Dearer's Extra Buys Cheap" – DEBC (think of "DEBT" but with C). Silly, but sticky.

13. The Crying Cow Profit Trick

To find water added when a milkman sells at cost price and makes profit%:
Water added = (Profit% / 100) × Pure milk.
Think: The cow cries one jug of water for every 1% profit. If 25% profit, the cow fills jugs equal to 25% of the milk. Weird image: a cow shedding water jugs instead of tears.

14. The One-Third, One-Fourth Replacement Sequence

For multi-step replacement problems where fractions change, imagine a set of Russian dolls. Each time you remove a fraction, you're taking out a smaller doll from the previous one. After n removals, the pure substance is the innermost doll, shrunk by (1 – fraction)^n.
The doll metaphor reminds you it's multiplicative, not additive.

15. The Alligation Ghost (Final Reminder)

The Ghost of Alligation haunts every mixing problem: "Cross me and you shall find the ratio!"
Whenever you see "mix two things to get a middle value," the ghost whispers:
(Dearer – Mean) : (Mean – Cheaper)
The ghost crosses the differences over to the opposite quantities. If you forget, it rattles chains that sound like "CRISS-CROSS!"
