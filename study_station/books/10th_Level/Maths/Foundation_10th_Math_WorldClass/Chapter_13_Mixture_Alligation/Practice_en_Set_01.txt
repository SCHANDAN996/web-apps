Set 1: Mixture & Alligation (Easy 20 + Medium 5)

1. In what ratio must rice at ₹40 per kg be mixed with rice at ₹60 per kg so that the mixture is worth ₹52 per kg?
(a) 1 : 2 (b) 2 : 3 (c) 3 : 2 (d) 2 : 1
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is swapping the differences and writing 12 : 8 = 3 : 2. By alligation, cheaper quantity : dearer quantity = (60 − 52) : (52 − 40) = 8 : 12 = 2 : 3, and indeed 2 kg at ₹40 plus 3 kg at ₹60 costs ₹260 for 5 kg, so the answer is 2 : 3.
Source: NCERT Class 8 Mathematics

2. A mixture of 40 litres of milk and water contains 10% water. How much water must be added to make the water content 20%?
(a) 5 litres (b) 10 litres (c) 8 litres (d) 4 litres
Answer: (a)
Solution: Water initially = 10% of 40 = 4 L. If x litres are added, (4 + x)/(40 + x) = 1/5, giving 20 + 5x = 40 + x, so x = 5. The trap is taking 10% of the new total directly, which wrongly suggests 4 litres; the answer is 5 litres.
Source: NCERT Class 8 Mathematics

3. A milkman buys milk at ₹30 per litre and mixes it with water, which costs nothing. He sells the mixture at ₹30 per litre and gains 25%. What is the ratio of milk to water in the mixture?
(a) 4 : 1 (b) 3 : 1 (c) 5 : 1 (d) 2 : 1
Answer: (a)
Solution: Cost price of the mixture = 30 × 100/125 = ₹24 per litre. By alligation between milk at ₹30 and water at ₹0, milk : water = (24 − 0) : (30 − 24) = 24 : 6 = 4 : 1. The trap is reporting water to milk instead of milk to water; the answer is 4 : 1.
Source: PYQ-style

4. If 30 kg of sugar costing ₹45 per kg is mixed with 20 kg of sugar costing ₹50 per kg, what is the average cost per kg of the mixture?
(a) ₹46 (b) ₹47 (c) ₹48 (d) ₹49
Answer: (b)
Solution: Total cost = 30 × 45 + 20 × 50 = 1350 + 1000 = ₹2350 over 50 kg, so the average is 2350/50 = ₹47. The trap is taking the plain average of 45 and 50, which gives ₹47.50 and misleads toward ₹48; the answer is ₹47.
Source: NCERT Class 8 Mathematics

5. A vessel contains 80 litres of pure milk. 8 litres of milk is taken out and replaced with water, and this process is repeated once more. How much milk is left in the vessel?
(a) 64 litres (b) 64.8 litres (c) 68.2 litres (d) 72 litres
Answer: (b)
Solution: Milk left = 80 × (1 − 8/80)² = 80 × 0.81 = 64.8 litres. The trap is subtracting 8 litres twice to get 64 litres, ignoring that the second draw removes a mixture, not pure milk; the answer is 64.8 litres.
Source: PYQ-style

6. Two equal glasses contain mixtures of milk and water in the ratios 5 : 3 and 3 : 5 respectively. If both are poured into a third vessel, what is the ratio of milk to water in the new mixture?
(a) 5 : 3 (b) 1 : 1 (c) 3 : 5 (d) 4 : 5
Answer: (b)
Solution: Take each glass as 8 units: milk totals 5 + 3 = 8 and water totals 3 + 5 = 8. The trap is averaging the ratios to get 5 : 3 or 3 : 5; the totals are equal, so the answer is 1 : 1.
Source: PYQ-style

7. A shopkeeper sells part of 50 kg of rice at 8% profit and the rest at 18% profit, gaining 14% overall. How many kg did he sell at 18% profit?
(a) 20 kg (b) 25 kg (c) 35 kg (d) 30 kg
Answer: (d)
Solution: Alligation on profits gives (18 − 14) : (14 − 8) = 4 : 6 = 2 : 3, so the part at 18% is 3/5 of 50 = 30 kg. The trap is taking the 2/5 share, which gives 20 kg; the answer is 30 kg.
Source: PYQ-style

8. In what ratio must a grocer mix pulses costing ₹85 per kg and ₹100 per kg to get a mixture worth ₹92 per kg?
(a) 3 : 5 (b) 7 : 8 (c) 5 : 3 (d) 8 : 7
Answer: (d)
Solution: The differences are 100 − 92 = 8 and 92 − 85 = 7, so cheaper : dearer = 8 : 7. The trap is reversing the differences to get 7 : 8; the answer is 8 : 7.
Source: NCERT Class 8 Mathematics

9. A 50-litre solution of acid and water contains 60% acid. How much water must be added to reduce the acid concentration to 40%?
(a) 20 litres (b) 30 litres (c) 25 litres (d) 35 litres
Answer: (c)
Solution: Acid = 60% of 50 = 30 L. If x litres of water are added, 30/(50 + x) = 2/5, so 150 = 100 + 2x and x = 25. The trap is computing 20% of 50 = 10 and adding it to get 20 litres; the answer is 25 litres.
Source: NCERT Class 8 Mathematics

10. A 30-litre mixture contains milk and water in the ratio 7 : 3. How much milk must be added to change the ratio to 3 : 1?
(a) 4 litres (b) 5 litres (c) 6 litres (d) 8 litres
Answer: (c)
Solution: Milk = 21 L and water = 9 L. Water stays fixed, so for a 3 : 1 ratio the milk must become 27 L, meaning 27 − 21 = 6 L is added. The trap is scaling both components up instead of keeping water constant; the answer is 6 litres.
Source: NCERT Class 8 Mathematics

11. A 60-litre solution of alcohol and water contains 40% alcohol. How many litres of alcohol must be added to make the solution 50% alcohol?
(a) 10 litres (b) 15 litres (c) 12 litres (d) 20 litres
Answer: (c)
Solution: Alcohol = 40% of 60 = 24 L. If x litres are added, (24 + x)/(60 + x) = 1/2, giving 48 + 2x = 60 + x, so x = 12. The trap is adding 10% of 60 = 6 litres and doubling it wrongly; the answer is 12 litres.
Source: PYQ-style

12. Two alloys contain gold and copper in the ratios 1 : 1 and 5 : 7 respectively. If equal weights of both are melted together, what is the ratio of gold to copper in the new alloy?
(a) 1 : 1 (b) 13 : 11 (c) 11 : 13 (d) 5 : 6
Answer: (c)
Solution: Gold fractions are 1/2 and 5/12, so total gold = 6/12 + 5/12 = 11/12 and copper = 6/12 + 7/12 = 13/12. The trap is adding the ratio terms directly, which gives 6 : 8 and misleads toward 1 : 1; the answer is 11 : 13.
Source: PYQ-style

13. A vessel contains 90 litres of a milk-water mixture in the ratio 2 : 1. If 30 litres of the mixture is taken out and replaced with pure milk, what is the final ratio of milk to water?
(a) 7 : 2 (b) 5 : 2 (c) 7 : 3 (d) 11 : 4
Answer: (a)
Solution: Initially milk = 60 L, water = 30 L. Removing 30 L of mixture removes 20 L milk and 10 L water; adding 30 L milk gives milk = 70 L, water = 20 L. The trap is forgetting that the removed mixture also carries away water; the answer is 7 : 2.
Source: PYQ-style

14. A 40-litre mixture of alcohol and water contains 25% alcohol. How many litres of water must be added so that the alcohol content becomes 20%?
(a) 15 litres (b) 10 litres (c) 12 litres (d) 8 litres
Answer: (b)
Solution: Alcohol = 25% of 40 = 10 L. If x litres of water are added, 10/(40 + x) = 1/5, so 50 = 40 + x and x = 10. The trap is taking 5% of 40 = 2 litres as the shortfall; the answer is 10 litres.
Source: NCERT Class 8 Mathematics

15. In what ratio must tea at ₹62 per kg be mixed with tea at ₹72 per kg so that the mixture costs ₹64.50 per kg?
(a) 3 : 1 (b) 3 : 2 (c) 5 : 2 (d) 5 : 1
Answer: (a)
Solution: The differences are 72 − 64.50 = 7.5 and 64.50 − 62 = 2.5, giving 7.5 : 2.5 = 3 : 1. The trap is mishandling the decimal and getting 5 : 1; the answer is 3 : 1.
Source: NCERT Class 8 Mathematics

16. A shopkeeper mixes 26 kg of rice at ₹20 per kg with 30 kg of rice at ₹36 per kg and sells the mixture at ₹30 per kg. Find his profit percentage.
(a) 5% (b) 4% (c) 8% (d) 6%
Answer: (a)
Solution: Total cost = 520 + 1080 = ₹1600 for 56 kg; total selling price = 56 × 30 = ₹1680, so profit = 80 and profit% = 80/1600 × 100 = 5%. The trap is comparing ₹30 with the simple average cost ₹28; the answer is 5%.
Source: PYQ-style

17. A vessel is full of 60 litres of pure milk. 12 litres of milk is drawn and replaced with water, and then 12 litres of the mixture is drawn and replaced with water. Find the quantity of milk left.
(a) 38.4 litres (b) 40 litres (c) 36 litres (d) 42 litres
Answer: (a)
Solution: Milk left = 60 × (1 − 12/60)² = 60 × 16/25 = 38.4 litres. The trap is subtracting 12 litres twice to get 36 litres; the second draw removes only 80% milk, so the answer is 38.4 litres.
Source: PYQ-style

18. A grocer mixes 20 kg of wheat at ₹12 per kg with 10 kg of wheat at ₹18 per kg. At what price per kg must he sell the mixture to gain 20%?
(a) ₹16.80 (b) ₹15.60 (c) ₹14.40 (d) ₹18.00
Answer: (a)
Solution: Cost price per kg = (240 + 180)/30 = ₹14, so the selling price at 20% gain is 14 × 1.2 = ₹16.80. The trap is stopping at the cost price stage or misreading the weights, which leads toward ₹14.40; the answer is ₹16.80.
Source: PYQ-style

19. A jar contains liquids A and B in the ratio 3 : 2. When 10 litres of the mixture is taken out and replaced by liquid B, the ratio becomes 1 : 4. What was the initial quantity of the mixture?
(a) 20 litres (b) 30 litres (c) 25 litres (d) 15 litres
Answer: (d)
Solution: Let the mixture be 5x litres. After removal, A = 3x − 6 and B = 2x + 6, so (3x − 6)/(2x + 6) = 1/4, giving 12x − 24 = 2x + 6 and x = 3. The trap is forgetting the removed 10 litres contains both liquids; the answer is 15 litres.
Source: PYQ-style

20. In what ratio must rice at ₹15 per kg be mixed with rice at ₹20 per kg to make a mixture worth ₹16.50 per kg?
(a) 3 : 7 (b) 7 : 3 (c) 5 : 7 (d) 7 : 5
Answer: (b)
Solution: The differences are 20 − 16.50 = 3.5 and 16.50 − 15 = 1.5, so cheaper : dearer = 3.5 : 1.5 = 7 : 3. The trap is reversing the order to get 3 : 7; the answer is 7 : 3.
Source: NCERT Class 8 Mathematics

21. A vessel contains 60 litres of pure milk. 12 litres of milk is drawn out and replaced with water, and again 12 litres of the mixture is drawn out and replaced with water. What is the final ratio of milk to water?
(a) 25 : 9 (b) 9 : 16 (c) 16 : 9 (d) 4 : 1
Answer: (c)
Solution: Milk left = 60 × (4/5)² = 38.4 L, so water = 21.6 L and the ratio is 38.4 : 21.6 = 16 : 9. The trap is inverting the result to 9 : 16; the answer is 16 : 9.
Source: PYQ-style

22. How many litres of a 25% acid solution must be added to 30 litres of a 40% acid solution to obtain a 30% acid solution?
(a) 50 litres (b) 40 litres (c) 70 litres (d) 60 litres
Answer: (d)
Solution: Alligation gives (40 − 30) : (30 − 25) = 10 : 5 = 2 : 1, so the 25% solution needed is 2 × 30 = 60 litres. The trap is using the 1 : 2 reading and answering 15 litres, which is not even listed; the answer is 60 litres.
Source: PYQ-style

23. A container holds a liquid in which 4 parts are water and 5 parts are syrup. What fraction of the mixture must be drawn off and replaced with water so that the mixture becomes half water and half syrup?
(a) 1/8 (b) 1/9 (c) 1/7 (d) 1/10
Answer: (d)
Solution: If fraction x is replaced, final water = (4/9)(1 − x) + x = 4/9 + 5x/9 = 1/2, giving 8 + 10x = 9, so x = 1/10. The trap is using the syrup fraction 5/9 in place of water, which leads to 1/9; the answer is 1/10.
Source: PYQ-style

24. Two vessels contain milk and water in the ratios 4 : 3 and 2 : 3 respectively. In what ratio must these mixtures be taken to obtain a new mixture with milk and water in the ratio 1 : 1?
(a) 14 : 5 (b) 5 : 7 (c) 5 : 14 (d) 7 : 5
Answer: (d)
Solution: Milk fractions are 4/7 and 2/5, with target 1/2. Alligation gives (1/2 − 2/5) : (4/7 − 1/2) = 1/10 : 1/14 = 7 : 5, and indeed 7 L of the first with 5 L of the second gives 6 L milk and 6 L water. The trap is reversing the differences to get 5 : 7; the answer is 7 : 5.
Source: PYQ-style

25. A cask contains 100 litres of wine. 20 litres of wine is drawn out and the cask is filled with water, and this operation is performed three times in all. How much wine is left in the cask?
(a) 64 litres (b) 56 litres (c) 51.2 litres (d) 48.8 litres
Answer: (c)
Solution: Wine left = 100 × (1 − 20/100)³ = 100 × 64/125 = 51.2 litres. The trap is applying the factor only twice, which gives 64 litres; the answer is 51.2 litres.
