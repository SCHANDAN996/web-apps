Set 6: Mixture & Alligation (All 25 Hard)

126. A vessel contains a mixture of milk and water in the ratio 7 : 5. 24 litres of the mixture is withdrawn and replaced with water. Then 32 litres of the new mixture is withdrawn and replaced with water. The ratio of milk to water in the final mixture becomes 7 : 13. Find the initial quantity of the mixture.
(a) 96 litres (b) 108 litres (c) 144 litres (d) 120 litres
Answer: (d)
Solution: Taking initial quantity as 12x, milk after two replacements is (7x – 14)(3x – 8)/(3x), and setting this equal to 7/20 of 12x gives 3x² – 35x + 40 = 0, so x = 8. The trap is applying the simple repeated-replacement formula, which only works when equal quantities are replaced each time. Initial quantity = 12 × 8 = 120 litres.
Source: PYQ-style

127. A vessel contains 125 litres of a mixture of alcohol and water in the ratio 5 : 2. A certain quantity of the mixture is taken out and replaced with water. Then one-third of the resulting mixture is taken out and replaced with alcohol. The final ratio of alcohol to water becomes 3 : 2. Find the quantity of mixture initially removed.
(a) 55 litres (b) 50 litres (c) 45 litres (d) 60 litres
Answer: (a)
Solution: If x litres is removed first, alcohol becomes (5/7)(125 – x) and water (250 + 5x)/7; after the second step the condition 2(alcohol) + 125 = 3(water) gives 10(125 – x) + 875 = 3(250 + 5x). The trap is forgetting that the second replacement adds alcohol, not water, which reverses the usual formula. Solving, 25x = 1375, so x = 55 litres.
Source: PYQ-style

128. Three varieties of sugar costing ₹18, ₹24 and ₹36 per kg are mixed. The ratio of the first two varieties is 3 : 2. The mixture is sold at ₹33 per kg, giving a profit of 10%. Find the ratio in which the three varieties are mixed.
(a) 3 : 2 : 5 (b) 2 : 3 : 8 (c) 3 : 2 : 8 (d) 2 : 3 : 5
Answer: (c)
Solution: Cost price of the final mixture is 33/1.1 = ₹30; the first two mixed in 3 : 2 cost (54 + 48)/5 = ₹20.4 per kg. Alligating ₹20.4 with ₹36 about mean ₹30 gives (36 – 30) : (30 – 20.4) = 6 : 9.6 = 5 : 8, and since the first two already total 5 parts, the trap of swapping the first two varieties must be avoided. The ratio is 3 : 2 : 8.
Source: PYQ-style

129. A jar contains a mixture of milk and water with 60% milk. 25 litres of the mixture is taken out and replaced with water. Then 10 litres of pure milk is added to the jar. If the percentage of milk in the final mixture is 50%, find the initial quantity of mixture in the jar.
(a) 80 litres (b) 90 litres (c) 100 litres (d) 110 litres
Answer: (c)
Solution: With initial volume V, milk after the operations is 0.6V – 15 + 10 and total volume is V + 10; setting (0.6V – 5)/(V + 10) = 1/2 gives 0.2V = 20. The trap is keeping the total volume at V after the 10 litres of milk is added — the total actually rises to V + 10. So V = 100 litres.
Source: PYQ-style

130. A vessel contains wine. 8 litres is drawn out and the vessel is filled with water. This operation is performed three times. The ratio of wine to water in the resulting mixture is 8 : 19. Find the capacity of the vessel.
(a) 20 litres (b) 32 litres (c) 28 litres (d) 24 litres
Answer: (d)
Solution: Wine left = V(1 – 8/V)³ and the final wine fraction is 8/27, so (1 – 8/V)³ = 8/27, giving 1 – 8/V = 2/3. The trap is using 8/19 as the wine fraction instead of 8/(8 + 19) = 8/27. Hence V = 24 litres.
Source: PYQ-style

131. A jar contains a mixture of two liquids A and B in the ratio 3 : 2. When 15 litres of the mixture is taken out and replaced with B, the ratio becomes 3 : 5. Find the capacity of the jar.
(a) 36 litres (b) 48 litres (c) 40 litres (d) 60 litres
Answer: (c)
Solution: With capacity 5x, after replacement A = 3x – 9 and B = 2x + 9; then (3x – 9)/(2x + 9) = 3/5 gives 9x = 72. The trap is subtracting the full 15 litres from A alone instead of removing A and B in the 3 : 2 proportion. Capacity = 5 × 8 = 40 litres.
Source: PYQ-style

132. A vessel contains 80 litres of a mixture of milk and water with 30% water. 20 litres of the mixture is taken out and replaced with water. Then 16 litres of the new mixture is taken out and replaced with milk. What is the final percentage of milk?
(a) 60% (b) 62% (c) 64% (d) 66%
Answer: (b)
Solution: Milk goes 56 → 42 (after the first replacement) → 42 – 8.4 + 16 = 49.6 litres out of 80 litres. The trap is treating the second replacement as also adding water, which would wrongly lower the milk percentage. Milk percentage = 49.6/80 × 100 = 62%.
Source: PYQ-style

133. Two vessels A and B contain alcohol and water in the ratios 2 : 3 and 4 : 1 respectively. They are mixed in the ratio x : y. After that, 25% of the mixture is taken out and replaced with water. The final ratio of alcohol to water becomes 1 : 1. Find the ratio x : y.
(a) 2 : 1 (b) 1 : 2 (c) 1 : 1 (d) 3 : 1
Answer: (b)
Solution: Alcohol fractions are 0.4 and 0.8; after 25% replacement the mixed fraction must satisfy 0.75 × m = 1/2, so m = 2/3. Alligating 0.4 and 0.8 about 2/3 gives (0.8 – 2/3) : (2/3 – 0.4) = 2/15 : 4/15 = 1 : 2, and the trap is reversing this to 2 : 1. So x : y = 1 : 2.
Source: PYQ-style

134. A can contains 100 litres of pure milk. 10 litres is taken out and replaced with water. This process is repeated four times in total. After that, 20 litres of the resulting mixture is taken out and replaced with water. What is the ratio of milk to water in the final mixture?
(a) 21 : 19 (b) 19 : 21 (c) 11 : 9 (d) 9 : 11
Answer: (a)
Solution: After four replacements milk = 100 × 0.9⁴ = 65.61 litres; removing 20 litres takes out 13.122 litres of milk, leaving 52.488 litres of milk and 47.512 litres of water. The trap is applying the 10% factor to the last 20-litre step as well, instead of computing the actual quantities removed. The ratio 52.488 : 47.512 simplifies to 21 : 19.
Source: PYQ-style

135. A vessel contains 60 litres of a mixture of milk and water in the ratio 5 : 3. How much of the mixture must be taken out and replaced with water so that the ratio of milk to water becomes 1 : 1?
(a) 10 litres (b) 15 litres (c) 12 litres (d) 18 litres
Answer: (c)
Solution: Milk is 37.5 litres and water 22.5 litres; replacing x litres raises water by (5/8)x, so 22.5 + (5/8)x = 30. The trap is assuming the full x litres is the net water gain, ignoring the water removed with the mixture. Solving, x = 7.5 × 8/5 = 12 litres.
Source: PYQ-style

136. Three vessels contain milk and water mixtures in the ratios 1 : 1, 2 : 1 and 3 : 1 respectively. They are mixed in the ratio 2 : 3 : 5 by volume. Find the ratio of milk to water in the final mixture.
(a) 13 : 7 (b) 27 : 13 (c) 7 : 13 (d) 13 : 27
Answer: (b)
Solution: Taking 2, 3 and 5 litres, milk = 2(1/2) + 3(2/3) + 5(3/4) = 1 + 2 + 3.75 = 6.75 litres out of 10 litres, so water = 3.25 litres. The trap is averaging the three milk fractions directly instead of weighting them by the mixing volumes. The ratio is 6.75 : 3.25 = 27 : 13.
Source: PYQ-style

137. In what ratio must milk solutions containing 75% milk and 40% milk be mixed to obtain a solution containing 60% milk?
(a) 2 : 3 (b) 3 : 2 (c) 4 : 3 (d) 1 : 1
Answer: (c)
Solution: By alligation, (75 – 60) : (60 – 40) = 15 : 20 = 3 : 4 for the 40% solution to the 75% solution, so the required order 75% : 40% is 4 : 3. The trap is writing the differences in the same order as the question, which gives the inverted 3 : 4. The answer is 4 : 3.
Source: NCERT Class 8 Mathematics

138. A vessel contains 90 litres of pure milk. 30 litres is drawn out and replaced with water, and this operation is repeated once more. How much milk is left in the vessel?
(a) 30 litres (b) 35 litres (c) 45 litres (d) 40 litres
Answer: (d)
Solution: Each operation retains (90 – 30)/90 = 2/3 of the milk, so milk left = 90 × (2/3)² = 40 litres. The trap is subtracting 30 litres twice to get 30 litres, ignoring that later removals contain only part milk. The answer is 40 litres.
Source: PYQ-style

139. A vessel contains 81 litres of a mixture of milk and water in the ratio 7 : 2. How much water must be added so that the ratio becomes 7 : 3?
(a) 9 litres (b) 6 litres (c) 12 litres (d) 15 litres
Answer: (a)
Solution: Milk = 63 litres and water = 18 litres; for ratio 7 : 3, water must be (3/7) × 63 = 27 litres, so 27 – 18 = 9 litres is added. The trap is changing the milk quantity too, though only water is added. The answer is 9 litres.
Source: NCERT Class 8 Mathematics

140. Two alloys contain gold and copper in the ratios 5 : 2 and 3 : 4 respectively. Equal weights of both alloys are melted together. Find the ratio of gold to copper in the new alloy.
(a) 8 : 9 (b) 4 : 3 (c) 1 : 1 (d) 5 : 4
Answer: (b)
Solution: Taking 7 kg of each, gold = 5 + 3 = 8 kg and copper = 2 + 4 = 6 kg, giving 8 : 6 = 4 : 3. The trap is adding the ratios term by term as 8 : 6 without checking that both alloys have the same total of 7 parts — here they do, but the gold figure 8 : 9 comes from mis-adding. The answer is 4 : 3.
Source: PYQ-style

141. A milkman mixes water (which costs him nothing) with pure milk and sells the mixture at the cost price of pure milk, earning a profit of 25%. What is the percentage of water in the mixture?
(a) 16⅔% (b) 25% (c) 20% (d) 33⅓%
Answer: (c)
Solution: A 25% profit on cost means 1 litre of mixture contains milk worth only 1/1.25 = 4/5 litre of pure milk, so water is 1/5 = 20%. The trap is answering 25%, confusing profit on cost with the water share of the mixture. The answer is 20%.
Source: PYQ-style

142. A vessel contains 729 litres of pure milk. One-third of the contents is drawn out and replaced with water, and this process is repeated three times in all. How much milk remains?
(a) 162 litres (b) 243 litres (c) 192 litres (d) 216 litres
Answer: (d)
Solution: Milk left = 729 × (1 – 1/3)³ = 729 × 8/27 = 216 litres. The trap is computing 729 × (2/3) = 486 and stopping after one step, or subtracting a fixed one-third of the original each time. The answer is 216 litres.
Source: PYQ-style

143. A mixture contains liquids A and B in the ratio 5 : 3. When 16 litres of the mixture is taken out and replaced with B, the ratio becomes 3 : 5. Find the total quantity of the original mixture.
(a) 32 litres (b) 40 litres (c) 36 litres (d) 48 litres
Answer: (b)
Solution: With total 8x, after replacement A = 5x – 10 and B = 3x + 10; then (5x – 10)/(3x + 10) = 3/5 gives 25x – 50 = 9x + 30, so x = 5. The trap is forgetting that the removed 16 litres also carries away 6 litres of B. Total = 8 × 5 = 40 litres.
Source: PYQ-style

144. How many litres of pure alcohol must be added to 40 litres of a 25% alcohol solution to make it a 40% alcohol solution?
(a) 10 litres (b) 8 litres (c) 12 litres (d) 15 litres
Answer: (a)
Solution: Alcohol initially is 10 litres; if x litres is added, (10 + x)/(40 + x) = 0.4 gives 10 + x = 16 + 0.4x, so 0.6x = 6. The trap is keeping the denominator at 40 litres instead of 40 + x. The answer is 10 litres.

145. Tea costing ₹120 per kg is mixed with tea costing ₹180 per kg. The mixture is sold at ₹165 per kg, earning a profit of 10%. Find the ratio in which the two teas are mixed.
(a) 1 : 1 (b) 2 : 1 (c) 1 : 2 (d) 3 : 2
Answer: (a)
Solution: Cost price of the mixture is 165/1.1 = ₹150 per kg; alligation gives (180 – 150) : (150 – 120) = 30 : 30 = 1 : 1. The trap is alligating about the selling price ₹165 instead of the cost price ₹150. The answer is 1 : 1.

146. A vessel contains 80 litres of pure milk. 8 litres is drawn out and replaced with water, and this operation is repeated three times in all. How much milk is left in the vessel?

Answer: (a)
Solution: Each step retains 72/80 = 0.9 of the milk, so milk left = 80 × 0.9³ = 58.32 litres. The trap is subtracting 8 litres three times to get 56 litres, which ignores the dilution after the first replacement. The answer is 58.32 litres.

147. A mixture contains milk and water in the ratio 4 : 1. When 10 litres of water is added, the ratio becomes 2 : 1. Find the initial quantity of the mixture.
(a) 40 litres (b) 45 litres (c) 60 litres (d) 50 litres
Answer: (d)
Solution: With milk 4x and water x, adding 10 litres of water gives 4x/(x + 10) = 2/1, so 4x = 2x + 20 and x = 10. The trap is adding the 10 litres to milk or changing both components. Initial quantity = 5 × 10 = 50 litres.

148. Two containers hold milk and water in the ratios 3 : 2 and 7 : 3 respectively. In what ratio should the mixtures be combined so that the new mixture has milk and water in the ratio 2 : 1?
(a) 1 : 1 (b) 1 : 2 (c) 2 : 1 (d) 3 : 1
Answer: (b)
Solution: Milk fractions are 3/5 = 0.6 and 7/10 = 0.7, and the target is 2/3; alligation gives (0.7 – 2/3) : (2/3 – 0.6) = 1/30 : 1/15 = 1 : 2. The trap is alligating the ratio numbers 3, 2, 7, 3 directly instead of converting to milk fractions. The answer is 1 : 2.
Source: PYQ-style

149. How many litres of a 60% alcohol solution must be mixed with 50 litres of a 40% alcohol solution to obtain a 55% alcohol solution?
(a) 150 litres (b) 120 litres (c) 125 litres (d) 100 litres
Answer: (a)
Solution: Alligation gives (60 – 55) : (55 – 40) = 5 : 15 = 1 : 3, so the 60% solution must be 3 times the 40% solution, i.e. 3 × 50 = 150 litres. The trap is inverting the ratio and answering 50/3 or treating the differences as the answer directly. The answer is 150 litres.
Source: PYQ-style

150. A vessel contains 60 litres of a mixture of milk and water in the ratio 5 : 1. Some quantity of the mixture is taken out and replaced with water, after which the ratio of milk to water becomes 5 : 3. Find the quantity replaced.
(a) 10 litres (b) 12 litres (c) 18 litres (d) 15 litres
Answer: (d)
Solution: Milk = 50 litres and water = 10 litres; replacing x litres leaves milk 50 – 5x/6 and water 10 + 5x/6, and 3(50 – 5x/6) = 5(10 + 5x/6) gives 100 = 40x/6. The trap is assuming milk stays at 50 litres because only water is added, ignoring the milk removed with the mixture. Solving, x = 15 litres.
Source: PYQ-style
