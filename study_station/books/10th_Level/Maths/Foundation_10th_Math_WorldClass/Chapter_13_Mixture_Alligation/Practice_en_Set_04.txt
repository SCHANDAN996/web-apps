Set 4: Mixture & Alligation — All 25 questions Medium difficulty

76. A shopkeeper mixes two varieties of rice costing ₹30 per kg and ₹40 per kg. He sells the mixture at ₹37.40 per kg and earns a profit of 10%. In what ratio must the two varieties be mixed?
(a) 2 : 3 (b) 3 : 2 (c) 1 : 1 (d) 5 : 3
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is applying alligation on the selling price ₹37.40 directly, which gives a wrong ratio. First find the cost price of the mixture: CP = 37.40 × 100/110 = ₹34 per kg. Alligation on ₹30 and ₹40 with mean ₹34 gives (40 − 34) : (34 − 30) = 6 : 4, so the answer is 3 : 2.

77. A vessel contains 125 litres of pure milk. 25 litres is drawn off and replaced with water. This process is repeated once more. What is the quantity of milk left?
(a) 80 litres (b) 75 litres (c) 100 litres (d) 60 litres
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is subtracting 25 litres twice (125 − 50 = 75), which ignores that the second draw removes a mixture, not pure milk. Milk left = 125 × (1 − 25/125)² = 125 × (4/5)² = 125 × 16/25, so the answer is 80 litres.

78. In what ratio must a 30% alcohol solution be mixed with a 45% alcohol solution to get a 35% alcohol solution?
(a) 1 : 2 (b) 2 : 1 (c) 1 : 1 (d) 3 : 1
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is writing the differences in the wrong order and answering 1 : 2. By alligation, quantity of 30% solution : quantity of 45% solution = (45 − 35) : (35 − 30) = 10 : 5, so the answer is 2 : 1.

79. A jar contains 80 litres of a mixture of milk and water in the ratio 7 : 3. How much water must be added so that the ratio of milk to water becomes 7 : 5?
(a) 12 litres (b) 16 litres (c) 20 litres (d) 24 litres
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is changing both quantities; only water is added, so milk stays fixed at (7/10) × 80 = 56 litres. For ratio 7 : 5, water must be (5/7) × 56 = 40 litres, and 40 − 24 = 16, so the answer is 16 litres.

80. A container contains a mixture of two liquids A and B in the ratio 7 : 5. When 12 litres of the mixture is drawn off and the container is filled with B, the ratio of A to B becomes 7 : 9. Find the capacity of the container.
(a) 48 litres (b) 42 litres (c) 36 litres (d) 54 litres
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is forgetting that the 12 litres drawn off removes A and B in the same 7 : 5 ratio, i.e. 7 litres of A and 5 litres of B. Let capacity be 12x; then (7x − 7)/(5x − 5 + 12) = 7/9 gives 63x − 63 = 35x + 49, so x = 4 and the answer is 48 litres.

81. A milkman dilutes 20 litres of milk with water to make a profit of 15% by selling the mixture at the cost price of pure milk. How much water did he add?
(a) 6 litres (b) 4 litres (c) 5 litres (d) 3 litres
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is taking 15% of a wrong base; profit here comes entirely from the free water sold at milk's price. Let CP be ₹1 per litre: CP of 20 litres is ₹20, and for 15% profit the selling must fetch ₹23, i.e. 23 litres sold, so the answer is 3 litres.

82. A 72-litre mixture of milk and water contains milk and water in the ratio 5 : 1. How much water must be added to make the ratio of milk to water 2 : 3?
(a) 66 litres (b) 72 litres (c) 84 litres (d) 78 litres
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is solving for the final water quantity (90 litres) instead of the water to be added. Milk = (5/6) × 72 = 60 litres stays fixed; 60/(12 + x) = 2/3 gives 180 = 24 + 2x, so x = 78 and the answer is 78 litres.

83. A mixture of 50 litres of milk and water contains 60% milk. How much water must be evaporated so that the percentage of milk becomes 75%?
(a) 5 litres (b) 15 litres (c) 10 litres (d) 20 litres
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is reducing milk along with water; evaporation removes only water, so milk stays at 30 litres. If 30 litres is 75% of the new mixture, the new total is 30/0.75 = 40 litres, and 50 − 40 = 10, so the answer is 10 litres.

84. Two alloys X and Y contain copper and zinc in the ratios 3 : 1 and 1 : 3 respectively. Equal weights of the two are melted together. What is the ratio of copper to zinc in the resulting alloy?
(a) 1 : 3 (b) 3 : 1 (c) 1 : 1 (d) 2 : 1
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is averaging the ratios as (3+1) : (1+3) = 4 : 4 without checking fractions, which luckily matches here, but the correct method uses copper fractions 3/4 and 1/4. With equal weights, total copper = 3/4 + 1/4 = 1 and total zinc = 1/4 + 3/4 = 1, so the answer is 1 : 1.

85. A solution of 100 litres contains 40% acid. How much pure acid must be added so that the resulting solution contains 60% acid?
(a) 50 litres (b) 45 litres (c) 40 litres (d) 55 litres
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is keeping the total volume at 100 litres in the equation; adding acid increases the total too. Solving (40 + x)/(100 + x) = 3/5 gives 200 + 5x = 300 + 3x, so x = 50 and the answer is 50 litres.

86. A container contains 160 litres of a mixture of milk and water in the ratio 3 : 1. 40 litres of the mixture is taken out and replaced with water. What is the ratio of milk to water in the resultant mixture?
(a) 3 : 2 (b) 7 : 9 (c) 9 : 7 (d) 2 : 3
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is assuming the 40 litres removed is all milk or all water; it is removed in the 3 : 1 ratio, i.e. 30 litres milk and 10 litres water. Remaining milk = 90 litres, water = 30 + 40 = 70 litres, so the answer is 9 : 7.

87. A trader mixes 50 kg of sugar at ₹32 per kg with 30 kg of sugar at ₹40 per kg. He sells the mixture at ₹42 per kg. What is his profit percentage?
(a) 10% (b) 20% (c) 15% (d) 25%
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is taking the simple average cost ₹36 instead of the weighted average. Total CP = 50 × 32 + 30 × 40 = ₹2800 for 80 kg, so CP per kg = ₹35; profit = 42 − 35 = ₹7, and (7/35) × 100 = 20, so the answer is 20%.

88. A vessel contains 60 litres of a mixture of milk and water in the ratio 2 : 1. How much of this mixture must be taken out and replaced with water so that the ratio becomes 1 : 2?
(a) 30 litres (b) 35 litres (c) 40 litres (d) 25 litres
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is replacing only water in the equation; the removed mixture takes away milk in the 2 : 1 ratio too. Milk left = 40 − 2x/3 and water = 20 + 2x/3; setting (40 − 2x/3)/(20 + 2x/3) = 1/2 gives 80 − 4x/3 = 20 + 2x/3, so x = 30 and the answer is 30 litres.

89. A bottle contains 80 ml of a mixture of alcohol and water with 25% alcohol. How much alcohol must be evaporated so that the alcohol percentage becomes 20%? (Assume only alcohol evaporates.)
(a) 4 ml (b) 10 ml (c) 8 ml (d) 5 ml
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is keeping the total volume at 80 ml; evaporation of alcohol reduces the total volume as well. Solving (20 − x)/(80 − x) = 1/5 gives 100 − 5x = 80 − x, so 4x = 20 and the answer is 5 ml.

90. A dealer purchases 40 kg of rice at ₹18 per kg and 40 kg at ₹22 per kg. He mixes them and sells the mixture at ₹21 per kg. What is his profit percentage?
(a) 4% (b) 6% (c) 5% (d) 8%
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is computing profit on the selling price instead of the cost price. Total CP = 40 × 18 + 40 × 22 = ₹1600 for 80 kg, so CP per kg = ₹20; profit per kg = ₹1, and (1/20) × 100 = 5, so the answer is 5%.

91. A container has 90 litres of a mixture of spirit and water with 60% spirit. Some quantity of the mixture is taken out and replaced with water so that spirit becomes 45%. Find the quantity of mixture replaced.
(a) 18.5 litres (b) 27 litres (c) 22.5 litres (d) 31.5 litres
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is computing the spirit removed (54 − 40.5 = 13.5 litres) and reporting it as the answer; the mixture removed contains only 60% spirit. So 0.6x = 13.5 gives x = 22.5, and the answer is 22.5 litres.

92. In what ratio must a 20% alcohol solution be mixed with a 35% alcohol solution to get a 28% alcohol solution?
(a) 5 : 3 (b) 8 : 7 (c) 7 : 8 (d) 3 : 5
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is reversing the alligation differences and answering 8 : 7. Quantity of 20% solution : quantity of 35% solution = (35 − 28) : (28 − 20) = 7 : 8, so the answer is 7 : 8.

93. A container holds 200 litres of pure milk. 40 litres is taken out and replaced with water, and then 40 litres of the mixture is again taken out and replaced with water. Find the quantity of milk remaining.
(a) 120 litres (b) 144 litres (c) 160 litres (d) 128 litres
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is subtracting 40 litres twice (200 − 80 = 120), ignoring that the second draw is only 80% milk. Milk left = 200 × (1 − 40/200)² = 200 × (4/5)² = 200 × 16/25, so the answer is 128 litres.

94. A mixture of 60 litres of milk and water contains 20% water. How much milk must be added to make the water percentage 15%?
(a) 20 litres (b) 15 litres (c) 25 litres (d) 30 litres
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is letting the water quantity change; only milk is added, so water stays at 12 litres. If 12 litres is 15% of the new mixture, the new total is 12/0.15 = 80 litres, and 80 − 60 = 20, so the answer is 20 litres.

95. Two vessels contain mixtures of milk and water in the ratios 9 : 1 and 1 : 4 respectively. In what ratio must they be mixed to get a mixture containing milk and water in the ratio 7 : 3?
(a) 3 : 2 (b) 2 : 3 (c) 2 : 5 (d) 5 : 2
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is applying alligation on the ratios directly instead of converting to milk fractions: 9/10, 1/5 = 2/10 and target 7/10. First vessel : second vessel = (7/10 − 2/10) : (9/10 − 7/10) = 5/10 : 2/10, so the answer is 5 : 2.

96. A solution of 144 litres contains acid and water in the ratio 1 : 1. How much water must be evaporated so that the ratio of acid to water becomes 2 : 1?
(a) 36 litres (b) 42 litres (c) 56 litres (d) 28 litres
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is halving the water to 36 litres and reporting that as the evaporated amount; 36 litres is the water remaining, not removed. Acid stays at 72 litres, so water must become 36 litres, and 72 − 36 = 36, so the answer is 36 litres.

97. A shopkeeper bought 50 kg of rice at ₹16 per kg and 75 kg at ₹20 per kg. He mixed them and sold the mixture at ₹21 per kg. His profit percentage is approximately:
(a) 12% (b) 14% (c) 18% (d) 16%
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is using the simple average cost ₹18 instead of the weighted average. Total CP = 50 × 16 + 75 × 20 = ₹2300 for 125 kg, so CP per kg = ₹18.40; profit = ₹2.60 per kg, and (2.60/18.40) × 100 ≈ 14.1%, so the answer is 14%.

98. A vessel contains 60 litres of a mixture of milk and water in the ratio 2 : 1. How much milk must be added so that the ratio of milk to water becomes 5 : 2?
(a) 6 litres (b) 10 litres (c) 8 litres (d) 12 litres
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is computing the final milk quantity (50 litres) instead of the milk to be added. Water stays fixed at 20 litres, so milk needed = (5/2) × 20 = 50 litres, and 50 − 40 = 10, so the answer is 10 litres.

99. A jar contains a mixture of two liquids A and B in the ratio 4 : 1. When 10 litres of the mixture is taken out and 12 litres of liquid B is added, the ratio of A to B becomes 1 : 1. Find the initial quantity of the mixture.
(a) 45 litres (b) 35 litres (c) 40 litres (d) 30 litres
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is removing 10 litres of only one liquid; the draw removes A and B in the 4 : 1 ratio, i.e. 8 litres of A and 2 litres of B. Let the initial quantity be 5x; then 4x − 8 = x − 2 + 12 gives 3x = 18, x = 6, so the answer is 30 litres.

100. A 52-litre mixture of milk and water contains milk and water in the ratio 7 : 6. How much water must be added to make the ratio 7 : 8?
(a) 8 litres (b) 10 litres (c) 12 litres (d) 14 litres
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is reporting the final water quantity (32 litres) instead of the water to be added. Milk stays fixed at (7/13) × 52 = 28 litres; for ratio 7 : 8, water must be (8/7) × 28 = 32 litres, and 32 − 24 = 8, so the answer is 8 litres.
