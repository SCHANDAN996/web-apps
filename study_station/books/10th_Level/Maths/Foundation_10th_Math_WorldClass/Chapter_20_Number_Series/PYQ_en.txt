📊 PYQ Analysis: Number Series (SSC, Banking, Railways)

Pattern Insights

Analysis of previous-year questions on number series shows that most questions fall into a small set of predictable families: constant difference, increasing difference, squares/cubes (with or without an offset), multiplication-based steps, and combinations of these. A smaller share tests alternating series, fractions/decimals, or mixed operations. The practical lesson: train yourself to check differences first, then differences of differences, then multiplication/division — in that order — before assuming anything exotic.

Note: The questions below are PYQ-style questions built on patterns repeatedly seen in SSC, Banking and Railway exams. Where an exact official paper reference could not be verified, the source is marked "PYQ-style".

Top 10 High-Yield Questions with Solutions and Psychological Traps

Q1. Source: PYQ-style (SSC pattern)

Question: 8, 15, 26, ?, 60, 83

Options: (a) 39 (b) 41 (c) 43 (d) 45

Step-by-Step Solution:

First differences: 15 – 8 = 7; 26 – 15 = 11.

Second difference so far: 11 – 7 = 4. Assume the second difference stays constant at 4.

Then the differences grow as 7, 11, 15, 19, 23.

Missing term = 26 + 15 = 41.

Verify forward: 41 + 19 = 60 ✓ and 60 + 23 = 83 ✓. The pattern holds for all given terms.

Answer: 41

Psychological Hook: Numbers like 26 and 60 sit close to squares (25, 64) and tempt you into forcing a square pattern. The differences hold the real key — always verify your assumed pattern against every given term, not just the first few.

Q2. Source: PYQ-style (SSC pattern)

Question: 4, 5, 7, 11, 19, ?

Options: (a) 35 (b) 37 (c) 39 (d) 41

Step-by-Step Solution:

Differences: 5 – 4 = 1, 7 – 5 = 2, 11 – 7 = 4, 19 – 11 = 8.

Difference sequence: 1 (= 2⁰), 2 (= 2¹), 4 (= 2²), 8 (= 2³).

Next difference = 2⁴ = 16.

Next term = 19 + 16 = 35.

Answer: 35

Psychological Hook: The small starting values (4, 5, 7) make candidates add 1, then 2, then 4 — and stop without checking whether the added values themselves follow a pattern. The trap is ignoring the second-order pattern because the numbers look "easy."

Q3. Source: PYQ-style (SSC pattern)

Question: 115, ?, 134, 145, 157, 170

Options: (a) 115 (b) 124 (c) 125 (d) 126

Step-by-Step Solution:

From the known part: 145 – 134 = 11, 157 – 145 = 12, 170 – 157 = 13.

The differences increase by 1, so reverse-engineer: the difference before 134 should be 10.

? = 134 – 10 = 124.

Check: 124 – 115 = 9. Full pattern: +9, +10, +11, +12, +13. ✓

Answer: 124

Psychological Hook: A missing term early in the series hides the pattern at first glance. The fix is to calculate backward from the later known terms instead of staring at the gap.

Q4. Source: PYQ-style (SSC pattern)

Question: 5, 12, 26, 54, ?

Options: (a) 112 (b) 92 (c) 110 (d) 108

Step-by-Step Solution:

Differences: 12 – 5 = 7, 26 – 12 = 14, 54 – 26 = 28.

The differences are doubling: 7 × 2 = 14, 14 × 2 = 28.

Next difference = 28 × 2 = 56.

Next term = 54 + 56 = 110.

Answer: 110

Psychological Hook: The series looks additive but is multiplicative in its differences. Candidates who write "+7, +14, +28" without checking how the differences themselves behave miss the doubling.

Q5. Source: PYQ-style (SSC pattern — wrong-number type)

Question: Identify the number that does NOT belong: 2, 6, 14, 30, 62, 126, 250

Options: (a) 6 (b) 14 (c) 250 (d) 126

Step-by-Step Solution:

Test the rule: each term = previous term × 2 + 2.

Verify: 2 × 2 + 2 = 6 ✓, 6 × 2 + 2 = 14 ✓, 14 × 2 + 2 = 30 ✓, 30 × 2 + 2 = 62 ✓, 62 × 2 + 2 = 126 ✓, 126 × 2 + 2 = 254.

The series shows 250 instead of 254, so 250 is the wrong number.

Answer: 250

Psychological Hook: The wrong term is the last one, and under time pressure candidates accept the last term as given and try to force a pattern backward — a classic anchoring trap. In wrong-number questions, verify the rule across the whole series.

Q6. Source: PYQ-style (SSC pattern)

Question: 5, 13, 22, 34, 51, 75, 108, 152, ?

Options: (a) 204 (b) 205 (c) 209 (d) 210

Step-by-Step Solution:

First differences: 8, 9, 12, 17, 24, 33, 44.

Second differences: 1, 3, 5, 7, 9, 11 — increasing by 2 each time.

Next second difference = 13; next first difference = 44 + 13 = 57.

Next term = 152 + 57 = 209.

Answer: 209

Psychological Hook: A long series looks hard, but the extra terms actually help — they give you more differences to confirm the pattern. Length is intimidation, not difficulty.

Q7. Source: PYQ-style (SSC pattern)

Question: 4, 11, 20, 31, 44, ?

Options: (a) 55 (b) 57 (c) 59 (d) 61

Step-by-Step Solution:

Method 1 — differences: +7, +9, +11, +13 → next +15 → 44 + 15 = 59.

Method 2 — squares with offset: 4 = 2² + 0, 11 = 3² + 2, 20 = 4² + 4, 31 = 5² + 6, 44 = 6² + 8. The offset grows by 2, so next term = 7² + 10 = 59.

Both methods agree.

Answer: 59

Psychological Hook: The squares are masked by a growing offset. The plain difference method works fine; spotting the square structure is a bonus that saves a few seconds.

Q8. Source: PYQ-style (Banking pattern)

Question: 194, 232, 274, 320, ?

Options: (a) 430 (b) 380 (c) 370 (d) 350

Step-by-Step Solution:

Differences: 232 – 194 = 38, 274 – 232 = 42, 320 – 274 = 46.

Second differences: 42 – 38 = 4, 46 – 42 = 4 — constant.

Next difference = 46 + 4 = 50; next term = 320 + 50 = 370.

Answer: 370

Psychological Hook: Banking and SSC number series use the same logic. Don't expect exam-specific tricks — good series logic is universal.

Q9. Source: PYQ-style (SSC pattern)

Question: 2, 5, 11, 23, 44, ?

Options: (a) 77 (b) 63 (c) 51 (d) 65

Step-by-Step Solution:

First differences: 3, 6, 12, 21.

Doubling fails (12 × 2 ≠ 21), so go one level deeper.

Second differences: 6 – 3 = 3, 12 – 6 = 6, 21 – 12 = 9 — increasing by 3.

Next second difference = 12; next first difference = 21 + 12 = 33.

Next term = 44 + 33 = 77.

Answer: 77

Psychological Hook: The near-doubling (3, 6, 12 …) lures you into a ×2 assumption that breaks at the last step. When a pattern breaks even once, abandon it immediately and check the next level of differences.

Q10. Source: PYQ-style (Banking pattern)

Question: 0.5, 1.5, 4.5, 13.5, ?

Options: (a) 27.5 (b) 40.5 (c) 45.5 (d) 54

Step-by-Step Solution:

Ratios: 1.5 ÷ 0.5 = 3, 4.5 ÷ 1.5 = 3, 13.5 ÷ 4.5 = 3.

Each term is multiplied by 3.

Next term = 13.5 × 3 = 40.5.

Answer: 40.5

Psychological Hook: Decimals make candidates nervous and cause careless multiplication slips. Mentally strip the decimal (treat the series as 5, 15, 45, 135 — a clean ×3) and divide the answer by 10 at the end.

The 3 Universal Traps Every Examiner Uses

The "Familiar Face" Trap: Numbers near squares or cubes (4, 8, 9, 16, 25 …) anchor you to the square/cube family and hide the offset or the real operation underneath.

The Length Intimidation Trap: Longer series look harder, but the second or third layer of differences usually turns constant — the extra terms actually make the pattern easier to confirm if you work methodically.

The Decimal/Fraction Fear Trap: Dots and denominators create anxiety. Strip the decimal (multiply by 10 mentally) or separate numerator and denominator, and the clean integer pattern reveals itself.

How to Use This Analysis

Practise these 10 questions until you can name the pattern family within about 15 seconds.

For every new practice question, name the trap type before solving — this builds meta-awareness.

With a long series, don't flinch: differences of differences is your best friend.

With decimals or fractions, pause, strip the formatting, and solve the integer version.
