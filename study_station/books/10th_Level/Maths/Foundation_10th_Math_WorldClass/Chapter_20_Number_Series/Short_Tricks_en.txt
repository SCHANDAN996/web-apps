📦 Memory Trick 1: The Detective Onion (Difference of Differences)
Visualise an onion in a detective's hat peeling its own layers, muttering:
"First layer differences, second layer resolution!"
When first differences look messy, you peel to the second layer. That's the "Onion Peel" method.

📦 Memory Trick 2: The Cube-Eating Monsters (Cube Series)
"One ATE twenty-seven, sixty-four ATE one-twenty-five,
two-sixteen ate three-forty-three."
"ATE" sounds like 8. So 1, 8, 27, 64, 125, 216, 343.
Picture a monster named "One" gulping 27, "Sixty-four" gulping 125, and "Two-sixteen" gulping 343.

📦 Memory Trick 3: A Super Monkey Dances Crazy (Order of Checking)
Acronym: C – Cubes & Squares, M – Multiplication, D – Division, A – Addition, S – Subtraction.
Visualise a super monkey in a disco suit dancing wildly. This is the order you check when you first see any series: first ask "are these squares or cubes?", then "is something being multiplied or divided?", and only then "is something being added or subtracted?" — because powers and multiplication grow fastest, so they are easiest to spot first.

📦 Memory Trick 4: Snake Split (Alternating Series)
Two snakes, red and blue, tangled together. Odd‑position terms = red snake, even‑position terms = blue snake.
Pull them apart! Each snake has its own simple pattern, often +something. The "Snake Split" trick separates any alternating series instantly.

📦 Memory Trick 5: Square Dance Partner (Offset Squares)
Numbers near perfect squares (5, 10, 17, 26…) are squares doing a dance.
Imagine a square‑shaped person holding a "+1" balloon if the rule is n²+1,
or carrying a shovel "–1" for n²–1. Spot the balloon or the shovel, and you know the offset.

📦 Memory Trick 6: Bunny Multiplication (Multiplication Series)
When a series grows by a constant multiplier (like 3, 6, 12, 24 — each term ×2), imagine bunnies!
Bunnies multiply like … bunnies. If the multiplier changes (×2, ×3, ×4…), the bunnies wear caps with increasing multiplying signs.

📦 Memory Trick 7: The Fibonacci Family Tree
Each Fibonacci number is the sum of the two "parents" before it.
Draw a family tree: grandparents 1 and 1, child 2, child 3, child 5…
The child's age is always Mum's age + Dad's age. The "Family Tree" locks the pattern forever.

📦 Memory Trick 8: The Staircase of Differences
When the first differences are constant (e.g., +3, +3, +3, +3), you're climbing a staircase — every step is the same height.
When the first differences themselves grow by a constant (e.g., +3, +6, +9, +12), the steps get taller and taller — that's an escalator, and it means the second differences are constant.
The "Staircase / Escalator" image reminds you to check second‑level differences.

📦 Memory Trick 9: The Tricky Trio of Traps
Three villains:
1. Square Sam – wears a square mask, stands near squares (e.g., 5, 10, 17…), hides offsets.
2. Long Larry – a very long snake that looks terrifying but moves with a simple rhythm (long series = easy second differences).
3. Dotty Dolly – a ghost covered in decimal dots; the dots are just a mask. Multiply by 10 mentally and the integer pattern appears.

📦 Memory Trick 10: The Waiter's Tip (×2+1 Series)
For patterns like 5, 11, 23, 47, 95: double the previous number, then give a 1‑rupee tip.
"Double and tip one!" Imagine a waiter smiling, doubling your bill and you adding one coin. DTi1 = Double Then add 1.

📦 Memory Trick 11: Prime Squares Royal Court
The series 4, 9, 25, 49, 121 are squares of primes. Picture a royal court:
King 2²=4, Queen 3²=9, Prince 5²=25, Princess 7²=49, Duke 11²=121.
The royal order of primes (2, 3, 5, 7, 11…) yields the squares.

📦 Memory Trick 12: The Fraction Knife
For fractions like 1/2, 3/4, 5/8, 7/16, imagine a chef's knife slicing each fraction.
Top (numerator) is a running track of odd numbers. Bottom (denominator) is a doubling machine.
Separate top and bottom, find two tiny patterns. The "Fraction Knife" trick saves panic.

📦 Memory Trick 13: The Product Factory
For series 2, 5, 10, 50, 500 where each term is the product of the two previous terms,
imagine a factory conveyor: 2 and 5 go into a machine with a giant × sign, out comes 10.
Then 5 and 10 go in, out comes 50. The "Product Factory" keeps feeding numbers.

📦 Memory Trick 14: The Alternating Square‑Cube Ball
Visualise a ballroom: numbers in bow ties are squares (1², 3², 5², 7²…),
numbers in top hats are cubes (2³, 4³, 6³, 8³…). They alternate on the dance floor:
1², 2³, 3², 4³, … Spot the attire, and you'll predict the next dancer.

📦 Memory Trick 15: The Magnifying Glass Question
The first and most powerful question to ask a number series:
"What operation changed this number into the next?"
Imagine yourself as a detective holding a huge magnifying glass over the series,
repeating that single question. The "Magnifying Glass" trick is your default weapon.
