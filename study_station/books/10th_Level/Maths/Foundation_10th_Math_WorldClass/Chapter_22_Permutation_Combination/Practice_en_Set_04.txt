76. How many distinct arrangements can be made with the letters of the word 'FAILURE' such that all vowels are together?
(a) 288 (b) 720 (c) 144 (d) 576
Answer: (d)
Solution: Treating the 4 vowels (A, I, U, E) as one block gives 4 units (F, L, R + block) arranged in 4! = 24 ways, and the vowels arrange inside in 4! = 24 ways. The trap is 720, which is just 6! and ignores the block logic. Total = 24 × 24 = 576, so the answer is 576.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Mathematics

77. How many 3-digit numbers can be formed using the digits 0, 2, 4, 6, 8 with repetition not allowed?
(a) 48 (b) 60 (c) 100 (d) 120
Answer: (a)
Solution: The hundreds place cannot be 0, so it has 4 choices; then tens has 4 remaining choices and units has 3. The trap is 60 = 5 × 4 × 3, which wrongly allows 0 in the hundreds place. Total = 4 × 4 × 3 = 48, so the answer is 48.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Mathematics

78. A box contains 4 red, 5 blue and 3 green balls. In how many ways can 2 balls be chosen so that both are of the same colour?
(a) 20 (b) 18 (c) 19 (d) 22
Answer: (c)
Solution: Both red: 4C2 = 6; both blue: 5C2 = 10; both green: 3C2 = 3. The trap is 20, which comes from a slip in one of the combinations. Total = 6 + 10 + 3 = 19, so the answer is 19.
Source: PYQ-style

79. In how many ways can 5 keys be arranged on a key ring, given that the ring can be flipped over?
(a) 12 (b) 60 (c) 120 (d) 24
Answer: (a)
Solution: On a flippable ring, arrangements = (n − 1)! / 2. The trap is 24 = 4!, which forgets that flipping makes clockwise and anticlockwise arrangements identical. With n = 5: 4! / 2 = 12, so the answer is 12.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Mathematics

80. How many 4-digit numbers divisible by 2 can be formed from the digits 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6 if no digit is repeated?
(a) 240 (b) 180 (c) 360 (d) 120
Answer: (b)
Solution: The units digit must be even (2, 4 or 6): 3 choices; then the remaining three places fill in 5 × 4 × 3 = 60 ways. The trap is 360 = 6 × 5 × 4 × 3, which ignores the divisibility condition. Total = 3 × 60 = 180, so the answer is 180.
Source: PYQ-style

81. In how many ways can 5 boys and 4 girls be seated in a row so that the girls occupy the even places?
(a) 2880 (b) 5760 (c) 720 (d) 1440
Answer: (a)
Solution: Even places among 9 positions are 2, 4, 6, 8 — exactly 4 places for the 4 girls: 4! = 24 ways; the 5 boys fill the odd places in 5! = 120 ways. The trap is 5760, which comes from using 5! × 4! × 2 unnecessarily. Total = 120 × 24 = 2880, so the answer is 2880.
Source: PYQ-style

82. A student has to choose 4 subjects out of 8, but if Mathematics is chosen then Physics must also be chosen. In how many ways can the subjects be selected?
(a) 35 (b) 40 (c) 15 (d) 30
Answer: (d)
Solution: Either both Mathematics and Physics are chosen (then pick 2 more from the other 6: 6C2 = 15) or neither is chosen (pick 4 from the other 6: 6C4 = 15). The trap is 35 = 7C3, which counts only the case where Mathematics is excluded. Total = 15 + 15 = 30, so the answer is 30.
Source: PYQ-style

83. How many different arrangements can be made by taking 3 letters at a time from the word 'LOGARITHM' (9 distinct letters), without repetition?
(a) 84 (b) 720 (c) 504 (d) 168
Answer: (c)
Solution: Order matters, so use permutations: 9P3 = 9 × 8 × 7 = 504. The trap is 84 = 9C3, which ignores the order of the letters. Hence the answer is 504.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Mathematics

84. In how many ways can 6 men and 4 women be seated at a round table so that no two women sit together?
(a) 86400 (b) 43200 (c) 7200 (d) 120
Answer: (b)
Solution: Seat the 6 men around the table in (6 − 1)! = 120 ways, creating 6 gaps; place the 4 women in 4 of these gaps in 6P4 = 360 ways. The trap is 86400, which doubles the count by treating the circle as a row. Total = 120 × 360 = 43200, so the answer is 43200.
Source: PYQ-style

85. How many diagonals does a convex polygon with 12 sides have?
(a) 54 (b) 44 (c) 66 (d) 60
Answer: (a)
Solution: Diagonals = n(n − 3)/2 = 12 × 9 / 2 = 54. The trap is 66 = 12C2, which counts the 12 sides as well as the diagonals. Hence the answer is 54.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Mathematics

86. A team of 5 players is to be selected from 10 players, including 2 star players. How many teams can be formed if exactly one of the star players must be included?
(a) 112 (b) 140 (c) 56 (d) 70
Answer: (b)
Solution: Choose 1 of the 2 stars in 2C1 = 2 ways, then 4 more from the remaining 8 players in 8C4 = 70 ways. The trap is 70, which forgets the choice between the two stars. Total = 2 × 70 = 140, so the answer is 140.
Source: PYQ-style

87. How many 3-digit odd numbers can be formed using the digits 0, 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6 without repetition?
(a) 90 (b) 75 (c) 105 (d) 120
Answer: (b)
Solution: Units digit must be odd (1, 3, 5): 3 choices; hundreds digit can be any of the remaining digits except 0: 5 choices; tens digit: 5 remaining choices (0 allowed). The trap is 90, which comes from mishandling the zero restriction. Total = 3 × 5 × 5 = 75, so the answer is 75.
Source: PYQ-style

88. In how many ways can 8 books — 3 identical Economics books and 5 identical Mathematics books — be arranged on a shelf if all 3 Economics books must stay together?
(a) 56 (b) 336 (c) 21 (d) 6
Answer: (d)
Solution: Treat the 3 identical Economics books as one block; we then arrange 6 units of which 5 (the Mathematics books) are identical: 6!/5! = 6. The trap is 56 = 8C3, which counts positions without the "together" block logic. Hence the answer is 6.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Mathematics

89. How many 4-letter words (with or without meaning) can be formed from the letters of the word 'NUMBER' if repetition of letters is allowed?
(a) 1296 (b) 360 (c) 720 (d) 216
Answer: (a)
Solution: Each of the 4 positions can be filled by any of the 6 distinct letters: 6⁴ = 1296. The trap is 360 = 6P4, which wrongly assumes repetition is not allowed. Hence the answer is 1296.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Mathematics

90. If 10Cᵣ = 120, then r can be:
(a) 4 or 6 (b) 2 or 8 (c) 5 (d) 3 or 7
Answer: (d)
Solution: 10C3 = 120, and by the symmetry 10Cᵣ = 10C_{10−r}, also 10C7 = 120. The trap is "4 or 6", since 10C4 = 210, not 120. Hence r = 3 or 7, so the answer is 3 or 7.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Mathematics

91. In how many ways can 5 persons be seated in a row such that 2 particular persons never sit together?
(a) 120 (b) 48 (c) 96 (d) 72
Answer: (d)
Solution: Total arrangements = 5! = 120; arrangements with the two together (treated as a block) = 4! × 2! = 48. The trap is 48, which is the "together" count, not the "never together" count. Required = 120 − 48 = 72, so the answer is 72.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Mathematics

92. In how many ways can a captain and a vice-captain be chosen from a team of 11 players?
(a) 55 (b) 121 (c) 110 (d) 22
Answer: (c)
Solution: The two posts are distinct, so order matters: 11P2 = 11 × 10 = 110. The trap is 55 = 11C2, which treats the two posts as identical. Hence the answer is 110.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Mathematics

93. In a party, every person shakes hands with every other person exactly once. If there are 15 people, how many handshakes take place?
(a) 120 (b) 210 (c) 105 (d) 90
Answer: (c)
Solution: Each handshake is a pair of people: 15C2 = 15 × 14 / 2 = 105. The trap is 210 = 15 × 14, which counts every handshake twice. Hence the answer is 105.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Mathematics

94. How many 3-digit numbers divisible by 2 can be formed using the digits 0, 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6 without repetition?
(a) 120 (b) 90 (c) 105 (d) 75
Answer: (c)
Solution: Units digit even: if it is 0, hundreds has 6 choices and tens 5, giving 30; if it is 2, 4 or 6 (3 choices), hundreds has 5 choices (not 0, not the used digit) and tens 5, giving 75. The trap is 120, which comes from ignoring the zero restriction. Total = 30 + 75 = 105, so the answer is 105.
Source: PYQ-style

95. In how many ways can a committee of 5 persons be formed from 6 men and 4 women if it must contain at least 3 women?
(a) 45 (b) 55 (c) 66 (d) 75
Answer: (c)
Solution: 3 women and 2 men: 4C3 × 6C2 = 4 × 15 = 60; 4 women and 1 man: 4C4 × 6C1 = 6. The trap is 45, which results from a wrong combination value. Total = 60 + 6 = 66, so the answer is 66.
Source: PYQ-style

96. How many 5-letter words can be formed using all the letters of the word 'EXAMS' if the middle letter must be a vowel?
(a) 96 (b) 48 (c) 120 (d) 24
Answer: (b)
Solution: The vowels are E and A, so the middle position has 2 choices; the remaining 4 distinct letters fill the other 4 places in 4! = 24 ways. The trap is 120 = 5!, which ignores the vowel condition. Total = 2 × 24 = 48, so the answer is 48.
Source: PYQ-style

97. A rectangular table has 12 distinct fixed chairs around it. In how many ways can 12 people be seated on these chairs?
(a) 11! (b) 12 (c) 4! × 4! × 2! × 2! (d) 12!
Answer: (d)
Solution: Since the chairs are fixed and distinct, this is a linear arrangement, not a circular one: 12! ways. The trap is 11!, which applies only to a round table with identical rotation positions. Hence the answer is 12!.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Mathematics

98. How many distinct 6-digit numbers can be formed using the digits 1, 2, 3, 3, 4, 4?
(a) 180 (b) 120 (c) 360 (d) 720
Answer: (a)
Solution: With 3 repeated twice and 4 repeated twice, arrangements = 6! / (2! × 2!) = 720 / 4 = 180. The trap is 720 = 6!, which treats the repeated digits as distinct. Hence the answer is 180.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Mathematics

99. In how many ways can 3 identical red, 2 identical blue and 1 green ball be arranged in a row?
(a) 60 (b) 720 (c) 120 (d) 360
Answer: (a)
Solution: Total 6 balls with repetitions: 6! / (3! × 2!) = 720 / 12 = 60. The trap is 120 = 6!/3!, which forgets to divide for the 2 identical blue balls. Hence the answer is 60.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Mathematics

100. If ¹⁵C₆ = ¹⁵Cₓ and x ≠ 6, then x equals:
(a) 8 (b) 9 (c) 12 (d) 10
Answer: (b)
Solution: By symmetry, ¹⁵Cₓ = ¹⁵C_{15−x}, so 15 − x = 6 gives x = 9. The trap is 8, which comes from computing 15 − 7 by mistake. Hence the answer is 9.
