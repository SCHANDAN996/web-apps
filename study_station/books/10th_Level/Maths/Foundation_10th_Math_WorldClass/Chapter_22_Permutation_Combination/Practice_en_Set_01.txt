Practice Set 1: Permutation & Combination — Questions 1–20 Easy, Questions 21–25 Medium

1. How many 2-digit numbers can be formed using the digits 1, 2, 3 if repetition of digits is not allowed?
(a) 3 (b) 9 (c) 12 (d) 6
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is 3 × 3 = 9, which wrongly allows repetition. Tens place has 3 choices and units place has 2 remaining choices, so the answer is 6.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Mathematics

2. In how many ways can 4 different books be arranged on a shelf?
(a) 24 (b) 12 (c) 4 (d) 16
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is 4 × 4 = 16, which ignores that each position loses one choice. Arranging 4 distinct items in a row gives 4! = 24, so the answer is 24.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Mathematics

3. A restaurant offers 2 soups and 3 starters. In how many ways can a customer choose one soup and one starter?
(a) 5 (b) 8 (c) 6 (d) 9
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is 2 + 3 = 5, which uses addition instead of multiplication. By the fundamental counting principle, 2 × 3 = 6, so the answer is 6.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Mathematics

4. What is the value of 5P2?
(a) 120 (b) 10 (c) 60 (d) 20
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is 5C2 = 10, but order matters in a permutation. 5P2 = 5 × 4 = 20, so the answer is 20.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Mathematics

5. In how many ways can a captain and a vice-captain be chosen from 5 players?
(a) 20 (b) 25 (c) 5 (d) 10
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is 5C2 = 10, but the two posts are different, so order matters. 5P2 = 5 × 4 = 20, so the answer is 20.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Mathematics

6. A team of 2 students is to be selected from 4 students. In how many ways can this be done?
(a) 12 (b) 6 (c) 8 (d) 4
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is 4P2 = 12, but selection does not involve order. 4C2 = 6, so the answer is 6.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Mathematics

7. The value of nC0 is:
(a) 0 (b) 1 (c) n (d) n!
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is 0, but there is exactly one way to choose nothing from n objects. Hence nC0 = 1, so the answer is 1.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Mathematics

8. How many 3-letter words (with or without meaning) can be formed using the first 5 letters of the English alphabet if no letter is repeated?
(a) 125 (b) 10 (c) 15 (d) 60
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is 5³ = 125, which allows repetition. Without repetition, 5P3 = 5 × 4 × 3 = 60, so the answer is 60.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Mathematics

9. In how many ways can 6 people be seated in a row?
(a) 36 (b) 120 (c) 720 (d) 30
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is 5! = 120, which counts one person short. Seating 6 people in a row gives 6! = 720, so the answer is 720.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Mathematics

10. If nC8 = nC5, what is the value of n?
(a) 15 (b) 13 (c) 12 (d) 11
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is adding wrongly or assuming n = 8 + 5 is not needed. Since nCr = nC(n − r), we get n − 8 = 5, so the answer is 13.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Mathematics

11. In how many ways can a committee of 3 be formed from 7 people?
(a) 35 (b) 21 (c) 210 (d) 42
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is 7P3 = 210, but a committee is a selection, not an arrangement. 7C3 = (7 × 6 × 5)/(3 × 2 × 1) = 35, so the answer is 35.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Mathematics

12. A lock code consists of 3 digits, each from 0 to 9. If repetition is allowed, how many codes are possible?
(a) 720 (b) 504 (c) 1000 (d) 100
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is 10P3 = 720, which wrongly forbids repetition. Each of the 3 places has 10 choices, giving 10³ = 1000, so the answer is 1000.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Mathematics

13. How many even 2-digit numbers can be formed using the digits 1, 2, 3, 4, 5 without repetition?
(a) 12 (b) 10 (c) 8 (d) 6
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is 5 × 4 = 20 ÷ 2 = 10, a faulty halving. The units digit must be 2 or 4 (2 choices), then the tens digit has 4 choices, giving 2 × 4 = 8, so the answer is 8.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Mathematics

14. What is the number of diagonals in a regular hexagon?
(a) 6 (b) 9 (c) 12 (d) 15
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is 6C2 = 15, which counts the 6 sides as well. Diagonals = 15 − 6 = 9, so the answer is 9.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Mathematics

15. The value of 5P5 is:
(a) 120 (b) 60 (c) 5 (d) 25
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is 5 × 5 = 25, confusing permutation with multiplication. 5P5 = 5! = 120, so the answer is 120.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Mathematics

16. A man has 3 shirts and 4 ties. In how many ways can he choose one shirt and one tie?
(a) 7 (b) 24 (c) 4 (d) 12
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is 3 + 4 = 7, which adds instead of multiplying. By the counting principle, 3 × 4 = 12, so the answer is 12.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Mathematics

17. How many ways can 3 letters be chosen from the word COUNT?
(a) 60 (b) 10 (c) 20 (d) 15
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is 5P3 = 60, but only selection is asked, not arrangement. 5C3 = 10, so the answer is 10.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Mathematics

18. The value of 0! is:
(a) 0 (b) 1 (c) Undefined (d) Infinity
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is 0, since 0 multiplied gives 0. By definition 0! = 1, which keeps n! = n × (n − 1)! consistent, so the answer is 1.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Mathematics

19. How many different signals can be given by arranging 3 flags of different colours vertically on a pole, using all three flags?
(a) 6 (b) 9 (c) 12 (d) 3
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is 3 × 3 = 9, which reuses flags. Arranging 3 distinct flags gives 3! = 6, so the answer is 6.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Mathematics

20. If nP2 = 12, what is the value of n?
(a) 4 (b) 5 (c) 6 (d) 3
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is n = 3, since 3 × 4 = 12 reverses the factors. nP2 = n(n − 1) = 12 gives n² − n − 12 = 0, so n = 4, and the answer is 4.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Mathematics

21. In how many ways can the letters of the word MATH be arranged so that the first letter is a consonant?
(a) 12 (b) 24 (c) 6 (d) 18
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is 4! = 24, which ignores the restriction. The first place has 3 consonant choices (M, T, H) and the remaining 3 letters arrange in 3! = 6 ways, giving 3 × 6 = 18, so the answer is 18.
Source: PYQ-style

22. How many 3-digit numbers divisible by 5 can be formed using the digits 0, 1, 2, 3, 4, 5 without repetition?
(a) 36 (b) 20 (c) 15 (d) 24
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is counting only numbers ending in 0, which gives 20. Ending in 0: 5 × 4 = 20; ending in 5: hundreds digit has 4 choices (not 0 or 5), tens has 4, giving 16. Total = 36, so the answer is 36.
Source: PYQ-style

23. From 5 men and 3 women, a committee of 2 men and 1 woman is to be formed. In how many ways can this be done?
(a) 40 (b) 15 (c) 20 (d) 30
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is 5C2 + 3C1 = 13 or using permutations. Choose 2 men in 5C2 = 10 ways and 1 woman in 3C1 = 3 ways, giving 10 × 3 = 30, so the answer is 30.
Source: PYQ-style

24. In how many ways can 5 persons be seated around a circular table?
(a) 120 (b) 60 (c) 24 (d) 20
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is 5! = 120, which treats the arrangement as linear. In a circle only relative positions matter, giving (5 − 1)! = 24, so the answer is 24.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Mathematics

25. A box contains 2 white, 3 black and 4 red balls. In how many ways can 3 balls be drawn such that all are of different colours?
(a) 60 (b) 12 (c) 24 (d) 28
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is 9C3 = 84 or adding the choices. One ball of each colour gives 2C1 × 3C1 × 4C1 = 2 × 3 × 4 = 24, so the answer is 24.
Source: PYQ-style
