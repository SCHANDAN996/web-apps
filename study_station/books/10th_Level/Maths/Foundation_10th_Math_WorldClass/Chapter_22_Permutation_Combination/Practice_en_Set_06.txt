126. In how many ways can the letters of the word 'MATHEMATICS' be arranged so that all vowels are together, the two M's are together, and the two T's are together?
(a) 8640 (b) 17280 (c) 4320 (d) 12960
Answer: (a)
Solution: Vowels A, A, E, I form one block; the two M's and two T's each form a block. Units to arrange: M-block, T-block, H, C, S, vowel-block = 6 distinct units giving 6! = 720, and inside the vowel block 4!/2! = 12. The trap is arranging all 11 letters first and then dividing, which miscounts the blocks. Total = 720 × 12, so the answer is 8640.
Source: PYQ-style

127. In how many ways can 5 distinct prizes be distributed among 4 students so that each student gets at least one prize?
(a) 240 (b) 120 (c) 150 (d) 480
Answer: (a)
Solution: One student must get 2 prizes and the rest 1 each. Choose the student (4 ways), choose his 2 prizes (5C2 = 10), distribute the remaining 3 prizes (3! = 6): 4 × 10 × 6 = 240. The trap is computing 4⁵ = 1024, which allows students to get nothing. So the answer is 240.
Source: PYQ-style

128. How many 3-digit numbers have the sum of their digits equal to 10?
(a) 72 (b) 54 (c) 45 (d) 63
Answer: (b)
Solution: Solve a + b + c = 10 with a from 1 to 9 and b, c from 0 to 9. Put a' = a − 1, so a' + b + c = 9 gives C(11,2) = 55 solutions; subtract the one case a' = 9 (a = 10, invalid). The trap is forgetting the hundreds digit cannot be zero, which inflates the count. So the answer is 54.
Source: PYQ-style

129. In how many ways can 6 men and 4 women be seated at a round table so that all the 4 women sit together?
(a) 8640 (b) 17280 (c) 720 (d) 34560
Answer: (b)
Solution: Treat the 4 women as one block, giving 7 units around a circle: (7 − 1)! = 720. Arrange the women inside the block: 4! = 24. The trap is using 7! instead of 6!, forgetting that circular arrangements of n units are (n − 1)!. Total = 720 × 24, so the answer is 17280.
Source: PYQ-style

130. A committee of 5 is to be formed from 6 gentlemen and 5 ladies. In how many ways can it be done if the committee must contain at least 2 ladies and at least 2 gentlemen?
(a) 500 (b) 400 (c) 455 (d) 350
Answer: (d)
Solution: Valid splits are 2 ladies and 3 gentlemen: 5C2 × 6C3 = 10 × 20 = 200; or 3 ladies and 2 gentlemen: 5C3 × 6C2 = 10 × 15 = 150. The trap is including the 4 ladies and 1 gentleman split, which violates the condition on gentlemen. Total = 200 + 150, so the answer is 350.
Source: PYQ-style

131. How many different arrangements can be made using all the letters of the word 'INDEPENDENCE' such that all the vowels are together?
(a) 33600 (b) 8400 (c) 16800 (d) 50400
Answer: (c)
Solution: Vowels are I, E, E, E, E (5 letters, E four times); consonants are N, N, N, D, D, P, C. Treat vowels as one block: 8 units arrange in 8!/(3! × 2!) = 3360 ways, and inside the block 5!/4! = 5. The trap is ignoring the repeated N's and D's, which inflates the count. Total = 3360 × 5, so the answer is 16800.

132. In how many ways can 5 books be selected from 10 different books and then arranged on a shelf, if two particular books are never selected together?
(a) 23520 (b) 30240 (c) 15120 (d) 11760
Answer: (a)
Solution: Valid selections = 10C5 − 8C3 = 252 − 56 = 196 (subtract selections containing both particular books). Each selection arranges in 5! = 120 ways: 196 × 120 = 23520. The trap is subtracting only one of the two forbidden books or forgetting the final arrangement step. So the answer is 23520.

133. How many 5-digit numbers divisible by 3 can be formed using the digits 0, 1, 2, 3, 4, 5 without repetition?
(a) 240 (b) 216 (c) 300 (d) 180
Answer: (b)
Solution: Sum of all six digits is 15, so the omitted digit must be a multiple of 3: omit 0 or omit 3. Omitting 0 gives 5! = 120 numbers; omitting 3 leaves 0 among the digits: 5! − 4! = 96. The trap is omitting only the digit 3 and forgetting that omitting 0 also keeps the digit-sum divisible by 3. Total = 120 + 96, so the answer is 216.
Source: PYQ-style

134. In how many ways can 8 persons be seated at a round table if three particular persons insist on sitting together, but two other particular persons refuse to sit together?
(a) 576 (b) 432 (c) 288 (d) 720
Answer: (b)
Solution: With the three as a block: 6 units in a circle give 5! × 3! = 720. Subtract arrangements where the two rivals also sit together: treat both as blocks, 5 units in a circle give 4! × 3! × 2! = 288. The trap is stopping at 720 and ignoring the rivals' condition. Required = 720 − 288, so the answer is 432.
Source: PYQ-style

135. In how many ways can the letters of the word 'PERMUTATION' be arranged so that all consonants are together?
(a) 518400 (b) 129600 (c) 86400 (d) 259200
Answer: (d)
Solution: Vowels E, U, A, I, O are 5 distinct letters; consonants P, R, M, T, T, N form one block. Units = 6, arranged in 6! = 720 ways, and inside the consonant block 6!/2! = 360 (T repeats). The trap is treating the two T's as distinct inside the block, which doubles the count. Total = 720 × 360, so the answer is 259200.

136. How many 4-digit numbers divisible by 6 can be formed using the digits 0, 1, 2, 3, 4, 5 without repetition?
(a) 52 (b) 60 (c) 72 (d) 84
Answer: (a)
Solution: The number must be even with digit-sum divisible by 3. Unit digit 0: the other three digits come from {1,2,3,4,5} with sum a multiple of 3 — the subsets {1,2,3}, {1,3,5}, {2,3,4}, {3,4,5} give 4 × 3! = 24. Unit digit 2 or 4: each gives 14 valid numbers after discarding leading-zero arrangements. The trap is counting only even endings without checking the digit-sum condition. Total = 24 + 14 + 14, so the answer is 52.
Source: PYQ-style

137. In how many ways can 4 Indians, 3 Americans, and 2 Russians (all distinct individuals) be seated at a round table so that no two Russians sit together?
(a) 60480 (b) 15120 (c) 30240 (d) 7200
Answer: (c)
Solution: Seat the 7 non-Russians around the table first: (7 − 1)! = 720 ways, creating 7 gaps between them. Place the 2 distinct Russians in 2 different gaps: 7P2 = 42. The trap is using 7C2 instead of 7P2, forgetting the two Russians are distinct. Total = 720 × 42, so the answer is 30240.

138. In how many ways can 10 different toys be divided into three unlabelled groups containing 3, 3, and 4 toys?
(a) 2100 (b) 4200 (c) 3150 (d) 6300
Answer: (a)
Solution: Partitions = 10! / (3! × 3! × 4! × 2!) = 3628800 / 1728 = 2100, where the extra 2! removes the duplication of the two equal-sized groups. The trap is omitting the division by 2!, which gives 4200 and counts each split twice. So the answer is 2100.

139. How many arrangements can be made with the digits of the number 112233 such that no two identical digits are adjacent?
(a) 42 (b) 24 (c) 30 (d) 36
Answer: (c)
Solution: Total arrangements = 6!/(2! × 2! × 2!) = 90. By inclusion–exclusion over the events that a pair is together: subtract 3 × 30, add back 3 × 12, subtract 6, giving 90 − 90 + 36 − 6 = 30. The trap is subtracting only the single-pair cases and not adding back the overlaps. So the answer is 30.

140. In how many ways can 4 letters be selected from the word 'EXAMINATION'?
(a) 126 (b) 136 (c) 168 (d) 144
Answer: (b)
Solution: Letter types: A, I, N each appear twice; E, X, M, T, O appear once — 8 types with 3 pairs. All four distinct: 8C4 = 70; one pair plus two distinct: 3 × 7C2 = 63; two pairs: 3C2 = 3. The trap is treating all 11 letters as distinct and writing 11C4. Total = 70 + 63 + 3, so the answer is 136.

141. How many numbers greater than 1000000 can be formed by using all the digits 1, 2, 0, 2, 4, 2, 4?
(a) 360 (b) 480 (c) 420 (d) 300
Answer: (a)
Solution: Every 7-digit arrangement exceeds 1000000 unless it starts with 0. Total arrangements = 7!/(3! × 2!) = 420; those with 0 first = 6!/(3! × 2!) = 60. The trap is forgetting that 2 appears thrice and 4 twice, which inflates the count toward 7!. Valid = 420 − 60, so the answer is 360.
Source: PYQ-style

142. In how many ways can 4 different rings be worn on 5 fingers of one hand if any finger can hold any number of rings and the order of rings on a finger matters?
(a) 120 (b) 720 (c) 625 (d) 1680
Answer: (d)
Solution: Place the rings one by one: the first ring has 5 choices of finger, and each subsequent ring adds one more position (it can go above or below rings already on a finger), giving 5 × 6 × 7 × 8 = 1680. The trap is computing 5⁴ = 625, which ignores the order of rings on a finger. So the answer is 1680.

143. In how many ways can a 4-member committee be formed from 5 men and 5 women such that the committee contains at least as many women as men?
(a) 125 (b) 210 (c) 155 (d) 175
Answer: (c)
Solution: Valid splits: 2 women and 2 men: 5C2 × 5C2 = 100; 3 women and 1 man: 5C3 × 5C1 = 50; 4 women: 5C4 = 5. The trap is including the split of 1 woman and 3 men, which violates the condition. Total = 100 + 50 + 5, so the answer is 155.

144. How many 6-digit numbers can be formed from the digits 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6 without repetition that are divisible by 4?

From {1,2,3,4,5,6} the valid endings with distinct digits are 12, 16, 24, 32, 36, 52, 56, 64 — eight endings, and for each the remaining four digits fill the first four places in 4! = 24 ways. The trap is counting endings like 28 or 48 that use digits outside the given set. Total = 8 × 24, so the answer is 192.

146. How many 4-digit numbers divisible by 5 can be formed using the digits 0, 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6 without repetition?
(a) 240 (b) 260 (c) 220 (d) 180
Answer: (c)
Solution: The unit digit must be 0 or 5. Unit digit 0: the first three places fill from the remaining 6 digits in 6P3 = 120 ways. Unit digit 5: the thousands digit has 5 non-zero choices, then the middle two places fill in 5 × 4 = 20 ways, giving 100. The trap is allowing 0 in the thousands place in the second case, which inflates it to 120. Total = 120 + 100, so the answer is 220.

147. In how many ways can the letters of the word 'EQUATION' be arranged such that all five vowels appear in alphabetical order?

Answer: (d)

The trap is choosing vowel positions with 8C5 and then also permuting the vowels, which undoes the fixed-order condition. So the answer is 336.

148. In how many ways can 4 different letters be placed into 4 addressed envelopes so that no letter goes into its correct envelope?
(a) 8 (b) 12 (c) 9 (d) 24
Answer: (c)
Solution: This is a derangement of 4 items: D₄ = 4! × (1 − 1/1! + 1/2! − 1/3! + 1/4!) = 24 × (9/24) = 9. The trap is writing 4! = 24, which counts all placements including those where some letters land correctly. So the answer is 9.
Source: PYQ-style

149. In how many ways can 10 different books be distributed among 3 distinct students such that one student gets 4 books and the other two get 3 books each?
(a) 4200 (b) 6300 (c) 8400 (d) 12600
Answer: (d)
Solution: Choose which student receives 4 books: 3 ways. Choose his books: 10C4 = 210. Choose 3 of the remaining 6 for the second student: 6C3 = 20; the last student takes the rest. The trap is dividing by 2! as if the two 3-book students were identical — they are distinct, so no division applies. Total = 3 × 210 × 20, so the answer is 12600.
Source: PYQ-style

150. How many 5-digit numbers with all digits distinct can be formed using the digits 0 to 9 such that the number is a multiple of 5?
(a) 5712 (b) 5040 (c) 4032 (d) 6720
Answer: (a)
Solution: Unit digit 0: the first four places fill from the remaining 9 digits in 9P4 = 3024 ways. Unit digit 5: the first digit has 8 choices (not 0 or 5), then the middle three places fill in 8P3 = 336 ways, giving 2688. The trap is giving the first digit 9 choices when the unit digit is 5, which permits a leading zero. Total = 3024 + 2688, so the answer is 5712.
