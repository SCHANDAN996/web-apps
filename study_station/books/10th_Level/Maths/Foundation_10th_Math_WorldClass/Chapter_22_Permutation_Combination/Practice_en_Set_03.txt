51. How many ways can the letters of the word 'REMOTE' be arranged such that the vowels are always together?
(a) 72 (b) 144 (c) 36 (d) 120
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is treating the three vowels as all different, which gives 4! × 3! = 144. Vowels are E, O, E with E repeated, so inside the block there are only 3!/2! = 3 arrangements; the block plus R, M, T gives 4 units arranged in 4! = 24 ways, so the answer is 72.
Source: PYQ-style

52. From 5 boys and 4 girls, a team of 3 is to be selected such that exactly 2 are girls. The number of ways is:
(a) 30 (b) 20 (c) 40 (d) 60
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is computing 4C2 × 5C2 = 60, which selects 2 boys instead of 1. Exactly 2 girls means 2 girls from 4 and 1 boy from 5: 4C2 × 5C1 = 6 × 5, so the answer is 30.
Source: PYQ-style

53. In how many ways can 5 different keys be placed on a key ring?
(a) 24 (b) 12 (c) 60 (d) 120
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is using (n−1)! = 24, which counts arrangements where flipping the ring over is treated as different. On a key ring a flip gives the same arrangement, so we use (n−1)!/2 = 4!/2, so the answer is 12.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Mathematics

54. In how many ways can the letters of the word 'ARRANGE' be arranged so that the two R's are always together?
(a) 180 (b) 720 (c) 120 (d) 360
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is forgetting the repeated A's and answering 6! = 720. Treating RR as one block gives 6 units with A repeated twice, so arrangements = 6!/2! = 720/2, so the answer is 360.
Source: PYQ-style

55. How many 3-digit even numbers can be formed using the digits 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7 without repetition?
(a) 120 (b) 60 (c) 90 (d) 210
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is filling the hundreds place first and then losing track of which even digits remain. Fix the units place first: it must be 2, 4 or 6, giving 3 choices; then hundreds has 6 choices and tens has 5, giving 3 × 6 × 5, so the answer is 90.
Source: PYQ-style

56. A student must attempt 5 out of 8 questions in an exam. If the first 3 questions are compulsory, in how many ways can the student choose the questions?
(a) 10 (b) 20 (c) 56 (d) 36
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is computing 8C5 = 56, which ignores the compulsory condition. With the first 3 questions fixed, only 2 more must be chosen from the remaining 5, giving 5C2, so the answer is 10.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Mathematics

57. How many numbers between 100 and 1000 can be formed with the digits 0, 1, 2, 3, 4, 5 if no digit is repeated?
(a) 100 (b) 120 (c) 80 (d) 150
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is allowing 0 in the hundreds place, which gives 6 × 5 × 4 = 120 but includes 2-digit numbers. The hundreds digit has 5 choices (1–5), then tens has 5 (0 now allowed) and units has 4, giving 5 × 5 × 4, so the answer is 100.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Mathematics

58. In how many ways can 7 persons be seated in a row if 3 particular persons always sit together?
(a) 5040 (b) 240 (c) 120 (d) 720
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is arranging only the 5 units and forgetting the internal order of the block, which gives 120. The block of 3 plus the other 4 persons make 5 units arranged in 5! = 120 ways, and the 3 persons inside the block arrange in 3! = 6 ways, giving 120 × 6, so the answer is 720.
Source: PYQ-style

59. A man has 5 friends. In how many ways can he invite one or more of them to a party?
(a) 5 (b) 32 (c) 31 (d) 10
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is answering 2⁵ = 32, which includes the case where nobody is invited. Each friend is either invited or not, giving 2⁵ = 32 cases, and we remove the single case of inviting no one, so the answer is 31.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Mathematics

60. How many distinct permutations can be made from the letters of the word 'BANANA'?
(a) 120 (b) 60 (c) 45 (d) 720
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is answering 6! = 720, which treats repeated letters as distinct. BANANA has 6 letters with A repeated 3 times and N repeated twice, so permutations = 6!/(3! × 2!) = 720/12, so the answer is 60.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Mathematics

61. In how many ways can 4 different rings be worn on 3 fingers of one hand if any finger can hold any number of rings?
(a) 81 (b) 64 (c) 24 (d) 12
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is computing 4³ = 64 by assigning rings to fingers the wrong way round. Each of the 4 rings independently chooses any of the 3 fingers, giving 3⁴, so the answer is 81.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Mathematics

62. A committee of 4 is to be formed from 4 men and 5 women. In how many ways can it be done if the committee must contain exactly 2 women?
(a) 120 (b) 60 (c) 80 (d) 100
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is choosing only the women and answering 5C2 = 10, or pairing it wrongly. Exactly 2 women means 2 women from 5 and 2 men from 4: 5C2 × 4C2 = 10 × 6, so the answer is 60.
Source: PYQ-style

63. If ²ⁿP₂ = 30, then the value of n is:
(a) 6 (b) 4 (c) 5 (d) 3
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is solving n(n−1) = 30 and getting n = 6, which ignores that the permutation is on 2n objects. Here ²ⁿP₂ = 2n(2n−1) = 30, and testing n = 3 gives 6 × 5 = 30, so the answer is 3.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Mathematics

64. How many 4-digit even numbers can be formed using the digits 0, 1, 2, 3, 4, 5 without repetition?
(a) 312 (b) 156 (c) 120 (d) 180
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is treating all even endings alike and getting 3 × 5 × 4 × 3 = 180, which wrongly allows 0 in the thousands place. Split by units digit: ending in 0 gives 5 × 4 × 3 = 60; ending in 2 or 4 each gives 4 × 4 × 3 = 48; total 60 + 48 + 48, so the answer is 156.
Source: PYQ-style

65. In how many ways can 4 boys and 2 girls be seated in a row such that no two girls sit together?
(a) 240 (b) 120 (c) 720 (d) 480
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is subtracting from 6! = 720, which is messy and error-prone. Arrange the 4 boys first in 4! = 24 ways, creating 5 gaps; place the 2 girls in 2 of these gaps in 5P2 = 20 ways, giving 24 × 20, so the answer is 480.
Source: PYQ-style

66. A polygon has 10 sides. The number of its diagonals is:
(a) 45 (b) 40 (c) 35 (d) 30
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is computing 10C2 = 45, which counts the 10 sides as well. Diagonals = n(n−3)/2 = 10 × 7/2, so the answer is 35.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Mathematics

67. A club has 6 members. In how many ways can a president, a secretary and a treasurer be chosen if no member can hold more than one post?
(a) 20 (b) 60 (c) 120 (d) 240
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is using 6C3 = 20, which ignores that the three posts are distinct. Since the posts are different, order matters: 6P3 = 6 × 5 × 4, so the answer is 120.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Mathematics

68. How many 4-digit numbers can be formed from the digits 0, 2, 4, 6, 8 without repetition?
(a) 120 (b) 48 (c) 96 (d) 24
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is computing 5 × 4 × 3 × 2 = 120, which allows 0 in the thousands place and counts 3-digit numbers. The thousands digit has 4 choices (2, 4, 6, 8), then 4, 3 and 2 choices remain, giving 4 × 4 × 3 × 2, so the answer is 96.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Mathematics

69. How many 5-digit numbers can be formed using the digits 1, 2, 3, 4, 5 without repetition that are divisible by 5?
(a) 120 (b) 60 (c) 24 (d) 48
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is answering 5! = 120, which counts all arrangements without the divisibility condition. A number is divisible by 5 only if it ends in 5 here, so fix 5 at the units place and arrange the remaining 4 digits in 4! ways, so the answer is 24.
Source: PYQ-style

70. In how many ways can 8 persons be seated around a round table if two particular persons refuse to sit together?
(a) 2520 (b) 1440 (c) 720 (d) 3600
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is answering 1440, which is the number of arrangements where the two sit together, not apart. Total circular arrangements are 7! = 5040; together-arrangements are 6! × 2! = 1440; subtracting gives 5040 − 1440, so the answer is 3600.
Source: PYQ-style

71. If ⁿC₈ = ⁿC₅, then n equals:
(a) 15 (b) 12 (c) 10 (d) 13
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is concluding 8 = 5 is impossible or adding wrongly as 8 + 5 = 13 but then doubting it. By the property ⁿCᵣ = ⁿCₙ₋ᵣ, we get n − 8 = 5, so the answer is 13.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Mathematics

72. How many 3-digit numbers greater than 400 can be formed using the digits 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7 without repetition?
(a) 120 (b) 210 (c) 180 (d) 140
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is taking 3 choices for the hundreds digit (only 5, 6, 7) and forgetting that 4 also works. The hundreds digit can be 4, 5, 6 or 7, giving 4 choices; then tens has 6 and units has 5, giving 4 × 6 × 5, so the answer is 120.
Source: PYQ-style

73. A bag contains 3 red, 4 blue and 2 green balls. In how many ways can 2 balls be drawn such that both are of the same colour?
(a) 9 (b) 10 (c) 36 (d) 18
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is computing 9C2 = 36, which counts all pairs regardless of colour. Same-colour pairs are 3C2 + 4C2 + 2C2 = 3 + 6 + 1, so the answer is 10.
Source: PYQ-style

74. In how many ways can the letters of the word 'SUCCESS' be arranged such that no two S's are together?
(a) 120 (b) 96 (c) 60 (d) 24
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is arranging all 7 letters and subtracting, which double-counts because the three S's are identical. Arrange U, C, C, E first in 4!/2! = 12 ways, creating 5 gaps; place the 3 identical S's in 3 gaps in 5C3 = 10 ways, giving 12 × 10, so the answer is 120.
Source: PYQ-style

75. From 6 boys and 4 girls, a committee of 5 is to be formed with at least 3 boys. The number of ways is:
(a) 246 (b) 186 (c) 252 (d) 120
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is computing only the 3-boy case, 6C3 × 4C2 = 120, and missing the other valid cases. At least 3 boys means 3 boys + 2 girls, 4 boys + 1 girl, or 5 boys: 6C3×4C2 + 6C4×4C1 + 6C5×4C0 = 120 + 60 + 6, so the answer is 186.
Source: PYQ-style
