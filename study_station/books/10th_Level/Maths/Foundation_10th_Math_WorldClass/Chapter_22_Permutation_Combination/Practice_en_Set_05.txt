Set 5: Permutation & Combination — Foundation Level (Questions 101–118 Medium, 119–125 Hard)

101. How many words can be formed by using all the letters of the word ‘DAUGHTER’ such that the vowels always occupy odd positions?
(a) 2880 (b) 1440 (c) 720 (d) 4320
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is placing all 3 vowels in 3 fixed odd positions, which ignores that there are 4 odd positions (1, 3, 5, 7). Choose and arrange 3 vowels in 4 odd positions: 4P3 = 24; arrange the 5 consonants in the remaining 5 places: 5! = 120. Total = 24 × 120, so the answer is 2880.
Source: PYQ-style

102. In how many ways can 4 boys and 3 girls be seated in a row so that boys and girls sit alternately?
(a) 144 (b) 72 (c) 288 (d) 576
Answer: (a)
Solution: Since there are more boys, the only alternating pattern is B G B G B G B; starting with a girl is impossible, which is the trap behind the doubled value 288. Boys arrange in 4! = 24 ways and girls in 3! = 6 ways. Total = 24 × 6, so the answer is 144.
Source: PYQ-style

103. A committee of 4 is to be selected from 5 men and 4 women. In how many ways can this be done if the committee must contain at most 2 men?
(a) 105 (b) 126 (c) 81 (d) 60
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is computing only the case of exactly 2 men and 2 women, which gives 60. Use complement: total committees 9C4 = 126; subtract committees with 3 men (5C3 × 4C1 = 40) and 4 men (5C4 = 5). So 126 − 45, and the answer is 81.
Source: PYQ-style

104. How many 3-digit numbers greater than 200 can be formed using the digits 0, 1, 2, 3, 4, 5 if repetition of digits is allowed?
(a) 120 (b) 144 (c) 180 (d) 100
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is forgetting that repetition is allowed and using 5 × 4 for the last two places, giving 80-type results. Hundreds digit must be 2, 3, 4 or 5 (4 choices); tens and units each have 6 choices. Total = 4 × 6 × 6, so the answer is 144.
Source: PYQ-style

105. In how many ways can the letters of the word ‘MOTHER’ be arranged so that the relative order of the vowels remains the same as in the original word?
(a) 120 (b) 240 (c) 360 (d) 720
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is answering 720, the total arrangements, ignoring the order condition. The vowels O and E must stay in the order O before E, so divide 6! by the 2! possible orders of the vowels: 720 ÷ 2. The answer is 360.
Source: PYQ-style

106. How many 5-digit numbers can be formed using the digits 0, 1, 2, 3, 4 without repetition?
(a) 96 (b) 120 (c) 48 (d) 72
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is computing 5! = 120, which counts arrangements starting with 0 — those are not 5-digit numbers. First place has 4 choices (1–4), then the remaining 4 digits fill 4 places in 4! = 24 ways. Total = 4 × 24, so the answer is 96.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Mathematics

107. In how many ways can 6 persons sit at a round table if two particular persons must sit opposite each other?
(a) 48 (b) 120 (c) 240 (d) 24
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is treating it like a linear row or adding an extra factor of 2 for the pair. Fix one of the two particular persons (removing rotation); the other must take the unique opposite seat. The remaining 4 persons arrange in 4! ways, so the answer is 24.
Source: PYQ-style

108. How many different arrangements can be made by taking 4 letters at a time from the word ‘COMBINE’?
(a) 840 (b) 5040 (c) 210 (d) 2520
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is answering 210, which is only the selection 7C4 and ignores arrangement. Since arrangements are required, use 7P4 = 7 × 6 × 5 × 4. The answer is 840.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Mathematics

109. In how many ways can 5 different prizes be distributed among 4 students if each student can get any number of prizes?
(a) 625 (b) 1024 (c) 256 (d) 120
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is computing 5⁴ = 625 by giving each student a prize choice; here each prize goes to any of the 4 students. So total ways = 4⁵. The answer is 1024.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Mathematics

110. How many 4-digit numbers can be formed using the digits 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7 without repetition if the number must be divisible by 25?
(a) 20 (b) 30 (c) 60 (d) 40
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is considering only the ending 25 and getting 20. A number is divisible by 25 only if it ends in 25 or 75; both endings are possible here. For each ending, the first two digits come from the remaining 5 digits: 5P2 = 20. Total = 20 + 20, so the answer is 40.
Source: PYQ-style

111. How many different words can be formed using all the letters of the word ‘INSTITUTE’ such that all vowels are together?
(a) 720 (b) 1440 (c) 5040 (d) 120
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is ignoring the repeated letters: T occurs 3 times among consonants and I twice among vowels. Treat the 4 vowels as one block: 6 units arrange in 6!/3! = 120 ways, and the vowels inside the block arrange in 4!/2! = 12 ways. Total = 120 × 12, so the answer is 1440.
Source: PYQ-style

112. In how many ways can 5 Indians, 4 Americans and 3 Russians be seated in a row so that all persons of the same nationality sit together?
(a) 34560 (b) 103680 (c) 207360 (d) 51840
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is forgetting to arrange the three nationality blocks themselves, which removes the factor 3! = 6. Blocks arrange in 3! ways; within them: 5! × 4! × 3! = 120 × 24 × 6 = 17280. Total = 6 × 17280, so the answer is 103680.
Source: PYQ-style

113. A box contains 2 white, 3 black and 4 red balls. In how many ways can 3 balls be drawn such that all are of different colours?
(a) 48 (b) 24 (c) 12 (d) 36
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is adding instead of multiplying, giving 2 + 3 + 4 = 9-type errors, or doubling to 48. One ball of each colour is needed: 2C1 × 3C1 × 4C1 = 2 × 3 × 4. The answer is 24.
Source: PYQ-style

114. How many 3-digit even numbers can be formed using the digits 0, 2, 4, 6, 8 if repetition of digits is allowed?
(a) 80 (b) 64 (c) 100 (d) 120
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is imposing an extra condition on the units digit, but every given digit is even, so all numbers formed are automatically even. Hundreds place: 4 choices (not 0); tens and units: 5 choices each. Total = 4 × 5 × 5, so the answer is 100.
Source: PYQ-style

115. In how many ways can 8 persons be seated at a round table if two particular persons must always sit together?
(a) 720 (b) 5040 (c) 1440 (d) 2880
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is using linear arrangement 7! × 2 = 10080-type thinking instead of circular. Treat the pair as one block: 7 units around a circle arrange in (7 − 1)! = 720 ways, and the pair arranges internally in 2! = 2 ways. Total = 720 × 2, so the answer is 1440.
Source: PYQ-style

116. A student must answer 8 out of 10 questions in an examination. If the first 3 questions are compulsory, in how many ways can he choose the questions?
(a) 35 (b) 56 (c) 28 (d) 21
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is computing 10C8 = 45-type totals or choosing 8 from the remaining 7. Since 3 questions are fixed, he needs only 5 more from the remaining 7: 7C5 = 7C2. The answer is 21.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Mathematics

117. How many 4-digit numbers divisible by 4 can be formed from the digits 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6 without repetition?
(a) 84 (b) 120 (c) 144 (d) 96
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is checking divisibility of the whole number instead of only the last two digits. Valid endings divisible by 4 using distinct digits from the set are 12, 16, 24, 32, 36, 52, 56, 64 — 8 endings. For each, the first two places take 4P2 = 12 arrangements. Total = 8 × 12, so the answer is 96.
Source: PYQ-style

118. How many 3-digit numbers can be formed using the digits 0, 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6 without repetition?
(a) 180 (b) 210 (c) 150 (d) 120
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is computing 7 × 6 × 5 = 210, which allows 0 in the hundreds place. Hundreds place has 6 choices (1–6); then tens has 6 choices (0 now allowed) and units has 5. Total = 6 × 6 × 5, so the answer is 180.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Mathematics

119. A question paper has two sections: Section A with 5 questions and Section B with 7 questions. A student must attempt 8 questions, selecting at least 3 from each section. In how many ways can he select the questions?
(a) 350 (b) 560 (c) 490 (d) 420
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is missing one of the three valid splits. The splits are 3 from A and 5 from B (10 × 21 = 210), 4 and 4 (5 × 35 = 175), and 5 and 3 (1 × 35 = 35). Total = 210 + 175 + 35, so the answer is 420.
Source: PYQ-style

120. In how many ways can 4 persons be chosen from 6 married couples such that no husband and wife are both chosen?
(a) 480 (b) 120 (c) 360 (d) 240
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is choosing 4 persons from 12 and then subtracting cases, which is messy and error-prone. Instead, first pick 4 couples from 6: 6C4 = 15; from each chosen couple pick exactly one person: 2⁴ = 16. Total = 15 × 16, so the answer is 240.
Source: PYQ-style

121. In how many ways can 6 balls of different colours be distributed among 3 boxes of different sizes so that no box remains empty?
(a) 540 (b) 1080 (c) 150 (d) 720
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is answering 3⁶ = 729-type totals without excluding empty-box cases. By inclusion-exclusion: 3⁶ − 3 × 2⁶ + 3 × 1⁶ = 729 − 192 + 3. The answer is 540.
Source: PYQ-style

122. In how many ways can the letters of the word ‘PANDEMONIUM’ be arranged so that all vowels are together and the two M’s are also together?
(a) 21600 (b) 86400 (c) 43200 (d) 10800
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is forgetting that N is repeated twice among the units, which doubles the result wrongly to 86400. Treat the 5 vowels as one block and the two M’s as one block: 6 units with N repeated arrange in 6!/2! = 360 ways; the vowels arrange inside in 5! = 120 ways. Total = 360 × 120, so the answer is 43200.
Source: PYQ-style

123. How many different arrangements can be made using all the letters of the word ‘BANANA’?
(a) 120 (b) 60 (c) 720 (d) 360
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is answering 6! = 720, ignoring repetitions. In BANANA, A occurs 3 times and N occurs 2 times, so divide by 3! and 2!: 720 ÷ (6 × 2) = 720 ÷ 12. The answer is 60.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Mathematics

124. In how many ways can 5 boys and 5 girls be seated in a row so that no two girls sit together?
(a) 86400 (b) 43200 (c) 14400 (d) 28800
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is seating girls first; then gaps cannot be managed correctly. Seat the 5 boys first in 5! = 120 ways, creating 6 gaps (including ends); choose and arrange 5 girls in these gaps: 6P5 = 720. Total = 120 × 720, so the answer is 86400.
Source: PYQ-style

125. How many diagonals does a polygon with 10 sides have?
(a) 45 (b) 40 (c) 35 (d) 30
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is answering 10C2 = 45, which counts the 10 sides as well. Diagonals = total line segments minus sides = 10C2 − 10 = 45 − 10. The answer is 35.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Mathematics
