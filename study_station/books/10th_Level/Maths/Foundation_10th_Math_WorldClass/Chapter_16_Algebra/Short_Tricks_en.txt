🪄 Short Tricks – English

1. The "River Crossing" Rule for Transposition

Mnemonic:
Imagine the equals sign as a magical river. Numbers can cross it, but they must pay a toll:
"Plus flips to minus, minus flips to plus; Multiply turns to divide, divide turns to multiply."
Remember: First move the "lazy" numbers (add/subtract), then move the "busy" numbers (multiply/divide). Chant:
"Add/Subtract first, then Multiply/Divide – That's the river ride!"

2. (a+b)² – The "Adam and Bella's Big Wedding" Story

Mnemonic (Loci + Story):
Picture a grand wedding hall. Adam (a) and Bella (b) are getting married.

First, Adam builds his own palace: a².

Then, Bella builds her own palace: b².

Finally, they build two identical guest houses to connect the two palaces: one ab on the left, one ab on the right. That's 2ab.
So, (a+b)² = a² + b² + 2ab. The two guest houses are the often-forgotten middle term!

3. (a–b)² – "The Breakup Story"

Mnemonic (Story continuation):
After a fight, Bella leaves. The guest houses that once connected them become negative, cold spaces: –2ab.
Adam's palace (a²) and Bella's distant palace (b²) remain, but the connection is lost.
So, (a–b)² = a² + b² – 2ab. The negative sign is the sadness of separation.

4. a² – b² – "The Two Squares' Secret Tunnel"

Mnemonic (Visual imagery):
Two square gardens (a² and b²) sit side by side. Their difference is not just plain arithmetic; it's a magical tunnel that connects the sum and the difference of their sides:
"Difference of Squares = (Sum of the sides) × (Difference of the sides)."
Imagine a bridge with "a+b" written on one side and "a–b" on the other.
So, a² – b² = (a+b)(a–b).

5. (x+a)(x+b) – "The X-Men Meet Up"

Mnemonic (Acronym):
Think of x as a superhero. It meets two friends, a and b. The result:

x squares up first: x²

Then x teams up with each friend: the combined strength is (a+b)x

Finally, the two friends multiply their own powers: a·b
So, (x+a)(x+b) = x² + (a+b)x + ab.
Remember: "x², plus (sum of the friends)x, plus (product of the friends)."

6. The "Balancing Beam" for Solving Equations

Mnemonic (Kinesthetic):
Imagine yourself as a circus performer on a balance beam. The beam is the equation: left side equals right side.
If you take a weight off one side, you must take the same off the other, or you'll fall.
"Whatever you do to one side, do to the other – keep the balance or you'll crash!"
This mnemonic reinforces the golden rule of algebra: maintaining equality by operating on both sides.

7. Elimination Method – "The Boxing Match"

Mnemonic (Loci):
Two equations are two boxers facing off. You want one boxer (variable) to be knocked out.

Make their strengths (coefficients) equal, like giving both boxers the same gloves.

Then subtract (uppercut!) – one variable gets eliminated, leaving the other standing alone.
"Make 'em equal, then subtract – one goes down, the other's the fact!"

8. (a+b)³ – "The Cube of Two Builders"

Mnemonic (Acrostic):
A Pretty Big Cube
A³ + Plus 3a²b + Big 3ab² + Cube b³
But a more fun story:
Adam and Bella build a cube. Adam's volume a³, Bella's volume b³. They also create three square towers (3a²b) and three rectangular blocks (3ab²).
"Cube the first, cube the last, three times the square of first with second, three times the first with square of second."

9. (a–b)³ – "The Broken Cube"

Mnemonic (Story):
The cube from the wedding is now broken after the breakup. The signs alternate: positive, negative, positive, negative.
a³ – 3a²b + 3ab² – b³
"First cube is happy, then three square blocks fall away, then three rectangular blocks come back, then the last cube is gone."

10. The "Invisible 1" Trick (Coefficient of x)

Mnemonic (Visual):
When you see just x, y, or z, imagine a ghostly number 1 floating beside it.
"If you see a letter alone, 1 is its secret clone."
So, x + x = 2x, but x × x = x². That ghost 1 helps with adding and multiplying correctly.

11. Simplifying Fractions – "The Cancellation Twins"

Mnemonic:
In a fraction like (x+2)(x-3)/(x+2), you can cancel the (x+2) only if it's a factor of the whole numerator and denominator.
Think of it as identical twins – you can only remove them if they're fully cloned, not just parts.
"Cancel only factors; never cancel terms. Terms are like distant cousins, not twins."

12. The "Transpose Don't Cross" Rule

Mnemonic (Action):
Instead of "cross-multiplying" in all cases, use transposition. When you have a/b = c/d, cross multiply: ad = bc. But if you have a/b + c = d, never cross multiply indiscriminately.
"Transpose first, cross-multiply last – if you rush, you'll be aghast!"

13. Roots of Quadratic – "Sum and Product"

Mnemonic:
For x² – (sum of roots)x + (product of roots) = 0:
"Sum with a minus, product as it is, that's the quadratic's quiz."
So if roots are 3 and 5, equation is x² – (3+5)x + 3·5 = x² – 8x + 15.

14. The "Always Check Your Solution" Habit

Mnemonic (Rhyme):
"Solve with pride, then check – plug it in, to avoid a wreck."
Many algebraic errors are caught by substituting the answer back into the original equation. Make it a habit.

15. The Distributive Law – "Sharing is Caring"

Mnemonic:
a(b+c) = ab + ac.
Imagine a mother (a) distributing sweets to two children (b and c). Each child gets a sweet from the mother.
"Mother loves both kids the same, each gets a share in the multiplication game."
