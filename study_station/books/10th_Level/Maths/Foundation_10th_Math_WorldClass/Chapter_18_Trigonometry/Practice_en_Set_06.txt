Set 6: Trigonometry (Foundation) – Hard Questions (126–150)

126. If cosec θ − cot θ = 1/2, then cosec θ + cot θ equals:
(a) 4 (b) 1/2 (c) 1 (d) 2
Answer: (d)
Solution: The tempting slip is to think the sum is also 1/2, but the identity cosec²θ − cot²θ = 1 gives (cosec θ − cot θ)(cosec θ + cot θ) = 1, so the two factors are reciprocals. Hence cosec θ + cot θ = 1 ÷ (1/2) = 2, so the answer is 2.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Mathematics (Introduction to Trigonometry)

127. If sin⁴θ + cos⁴θ = 1/2, where 0° < θ < 90°, then θ is:
(a) 30° (b) 45° (c) 60° (d) 75°
Answer: (b)
Solution: Many students test 30° or 60° first, but sin⁴θ + cos⁴θ = 1 − 2sin²θcos²θ = 1 − (1/2)sin²2θ. Setting this equal to 1/2 gives sin²2θ = 1, so 2θ = 90°. Therefore θ = 45°, so the answer is 45°.
Source: PYQ-style

128. The value of sin²5° + sin²10° + sin²15° + … + sin²90° is:
(a) 9.5 (b) 9 (c) 10 (d) 8.5
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is to count 18 terms and guess 9, forgetting the unpaired middle terms. Pairing sin²θ + sin²(90° − θ) = 1 for θ = 5°,10°,…,40° gives 8 pairs summing to 8; the leftover terms are sin²45° = 0.5 and sin²90° = 1. Total = 8 + 0.5 + 1 = 9.5, so the answer is 9.5.
Source: PYQ-style

129. If sin θ + cos θ = √2, then sin⁶θ + cos⁶θ equals:
(a) 1/2 (b) 1/4 (c) 1 (d) 3/4
Answer: (b)
Solution: Squaring gives 1 + 2sinθcosθ = 2, so sinθcosθ = 1/2; the trap is to cube √2 directly. Using a³ + b³ with a = sin²θ, b = cos²θ: sin⁶θ + cos⁶θ = 1 − 3sin²θcos²θ = 1 − 3/4 = 1/4, so the answer is 1/4.
Source: PYQ-style

130. If cos θ + sec θ = 5/2, then cos²θ + sec²θ equals:
(a) 9/4 (b) 25/4 (c) 13/4 (d) 17/4
Answer: (d)
Solution: The common error is to square 5/2 and report 25/4, forgetting the cross term. Squaring: cos²θ + sec²θ + 2 = 25/4, so cos²θ + sec²θ = 25/4 − 2 = 17/4, so the answer is 17/4.
Source: PYQ-style

131. From the top of a 60 m high lighthouse, the angles of depression of two ships on the same side are 30° and 45°. The distance between the ships is:
(a) 60(√3 − 1) m (b) 60(√3 + 1) m (c) 60√3 m (d) 60 m
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is to add the two distances, but the ships are on the same side so we subtract. Distances from the foot are 60cot45° = 60 m and 60cot30° = 60√3 m, giving 60√3 − 60 = 60(√3 − 1) m, so the answer is 60(√3 − 1) m.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Mathematics (Some Applications of Trigonometry)

132. If sin θ + sin²θ = 1, then cos¹²θ + 3cos¹⁰θ + 3cos⁸θ + cos⁶θ equals:
(a) 0 (b) 2 (c) 1 (d) 3
Answer: (c)
Solution: The expression looks intimidating, and the trap is to try numeric angles. Note it is (cos⁴θ + cos²θ)³; from the given, cos²θ = 1 − sin²θ = sin θ, so cos⁴θ = sin²θ and the bracket becomes sin²θ + sin θ = 1. Hence the value is 1³ = 1, so the answer is 1.
Source: PYQ-style

133. The value of cos²1° + cos²2° + … + cos²89° is:
(a) 44 (b) 44.5 (c) 45 (d) 45.5
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is to pair all 89 terms and report 44 or 45, ignoring the middle term. Pairs cos²θ + cos²(90° − θ) = 1 for θ = 1° to 44° give 44, and the unpaired middle term is cos²45° = 0.5. Total = 44.5, so the answer is 44.5.
Source: PYQ-style

134. If sec θ + tan θ = 3, then sin θ + cos θ equals:
(a) 5/7 (b) 3/5 (c) 7/5 (d) 4/5
Answer: (c)
Solution: Since sec²θ − tan²θ = 1, we get sec θ − tan θ = 1/3; the trap is to stop after finding cos θ = 3/5 and report 3/5. Solving, sec θ = 5/3 and tan θ = 4/3, so sin θ = tan θ · cos θ = 4/5. Then sin θ + cos θ = 4/5 + 3/5 = 7/5, so the answer is 7/5.
Source: PYQ-style

135. If tan θ + cot θ = a, then tan³θ + cot³θ equals:
(a) a³ − 3a (b) a³ (c) a³ − 1 (d) a³ + 3a
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is to cube both sides and write a³, forgetting the expansion term. Cubing x + 1/x = a gives x³ + 1/x³ + 3(x + 1/x) = a³, so tan³θ + cot³θ = a³ − 3a, so the answer is a³ − 3a.
Source: PYQ-style

136. If x sin³θ + y cos³θ = sin θ cos θ and x sin θ = y cos θ, then x² + y² equals:
(a) 0 (b) 2 (c) 4 (d) 1
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is to assume x and y must involve a and b style constants. Let x sin θ = y cos θ = k; substituting into the first equation gives k(sin²θ + cos²θ) = k = sin θ cos θ, so x = cos θ and y = sin θ. Then x² + y² = cos²θ + sin²θ = 1, so the answer is 1.
Source: PYQ-style

137. A man on the top of a tower observes a car moving towards the tower at uniform speed. The angle of depression of the car changes from 30° to 45° in 12 minutes. The car will reach the tower in:
(a) 12 min (b) 6(√3 − 1) min (c) 6(√3 + 1) min (d) 12(√3 − 1) min
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is to assume equal angles mean equal times and answer 12 min, but distances are not proportional to angles. With height h, the car covers h√3 − h = h(√3 − 1) in 12 min and must still cover h. Time = 12h / [h(√3 − 1)] = 12/(√3 − 1) = 6(√3 + 1) min, so the answer is 6(√3 + 1) min.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Mathematics (Some Applications of Trigonometry)

138. If sin θ − cos θ = 0, then sin⁶θ + cos⁶θ is:
(a) 1/4 (b) 1/2 (c) 3/4 (d) 1
Answer: (a)
Solution: The condition gives θ = 45°, and the trap is to assume the sum of sixth powers is 1 like the squares. Here sin 45° = cos 45° = 1/√2, so each sixth power is 1/8 and the sum is 2/8 = 1/4, so the answer is 1/4.
Source: PYQ-style

139. If 2sin²θ − 7sin θ + 3 = 0 and 0° < θ < 90°, then θ is:
(a) 45° (b) 30° (c) 90° (d) 60°
Answer: (b)
Solution: Factorising gives (2sin θ − 1)(sin θ − 3) = 0; the trap is to accept sin θ = 3, which is impossible since sin θ ≤ 1. So sin θ = 1/2, giving θ = 30°, so the answer is 30°.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Mathematics (Introduction to Trigonometry)

140. If cosec θ − sin θ = m and sec θ − cos θ = n, then (m²n)^(2/3) + (mn²)^(2/3) equals:
(a) 1 (b) mn (c) m + n (d) (m + n)²
Answer: (a)
Solution: Simplify m = cos²θ/sin θ and n = sin²θ/cos θ; the trap is to leave the answer in terms of m and n. Then m²n = cos³θ and mn² = sin³θ, so the expression becomes cos²θ + sin²θ = 1, so the answer is 1.
Source: PYQ-style

141. A kite flies with a 100 m string inclined at 60° to the horizontal. If the string is replaced by one making 45° with the horizontal while the kite stays at the same height, the new string length is:
(a) 100√2 m (b) 100√3 m (c) 100/√2 m (d) 50√6 m
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is to scale 100 m by √2 directly without computing the height. Height = 100 sin 60° = 50√3 m, so new length = 50√3 / sin 45° = 50√3 × √2 = 50√6 m, so the answer is 50√6 m.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Mathematics (Some Applications of Trigonometry)

142. The value of tan 1° · tan 2° · tan 3° … tan 89° is:
(a) √3 (b) 0 (c) 1 (d) undefined
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is to fear tan 90° and call it undefined, but 90° never appears. Each pair tan θ · tan(90° − θ) = tan θ · cot θ = 1, and the middle term tan 45° = 1, so the whole product is 1, so the answer is 1.
Source: PYQ-style

143. If tan 15° = 2 − √3, then cot 75° equals:
(a) 2 + √3 (b) √3 (c) 1 (d) 2 − √3
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is to take the reciprocal 1/(2 − √3) = 2 + √3, but cot 75° = tan(90° − 75°) = tan 15°, not cot 15°. Hence cot 75° = 2 − √3, so the answer is 2 − √3.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Mathematics (Trigonometric Ratios of Complementary Angles)

144. If cos θ + sec θ = 2, then cos²⁰¹⁶θ + sec²⁰¹⁶θ is:
(a) 0 (b) 1 (c) 2²⁰¹⁶ (d) 2
Answer: (d)
Solution: The huge exponent tempts students toward 2²⁰¹⁶, but cos θ + 1/cos θ = 2 forces (cos θ − 1)² = 0, so cos θ = 1 and sec θ = 1. Then 1²⁰¹⁶ + 1²⁰¹⁶ = 2, so the answer is 2.
Source: PYQ-style

145. The shadow of a pole is 10 m longer when the sun's altitude is 30° than when it is 60°. The height of the pole is:
(a) 10√3 m (b) 5√3 m (c) 5 m (d) 10 m
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is to take the shadow difference as h√3 alone. Shadows are h cot 60° = h/√3 and h cot 30° = h√3, so h√3 − h/√3 = 2h/√3 = 10, giving h = 5√3 m, so the answer is 5√3 m.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Mathematics (Some Applications of Trigonometry)

146. If sin θ : cos θ = 3 : 4, then sec θ − tan θ equals:
(a) 1/3 (b) 1/4 (c) 1/2 (d) 3/4
Answer: (c)
Solution: From tan θ = 3/4 we get sec θ = √(1 + 9/16) = 5/4; the trap is to report tan θ = 3/4 itself. Then sec θ − tan θ = 5/4 − 3/4 = 1/2, so the answer is 1/2.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Mathematics (Introduction to Trigonometry)

147. If sin θ + cosec θ = 2, then sin⁴θ + cosec⁴θ is:
(a) 2 (b) 4 (c) 1 (d) 8
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is to square 2 and answer 4, but sin θ + 1/sin θ = 2 forces (sin θ − 1)² = 0, so sin θ = 1 and cosec θ = 1. Then sin⁴θ + cosec⁴θ = 1 + 1 = 2, so the answer is 2.
Source: PYQ-style

148. From the top of a cliff 200 m high, the angle of depression of a ship changes from 30° to 60° as it sails towards the cliff. The distance travelled by the ship is:
(a) 200√3 m (b) 200/√3 m (c) 400/√3 m (d) 400√3 m
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is to report only the initial distance 200√3 m. Initial distance = 200cot30° = 200√3 m and final distance = 200cot60° = 200/√3 m, so the ship travels 200√3 − 200/√3 = 400/√3 m, so the answer is 400/√3 m.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Mathematics (Some Applications of Trigonometry)

149. If sin θ + cos θ = (√3 + 1)/2, then sin θ cos θ is:
(a) 1/4 (b) √3/4 (c) √3/2 (d) 1/2
Answer: (b)
Solution: Squaring gives 1 + 2sinθcosθ = (4 + 2√3)/4 = 1 + √3/2; the trap is to report √3/2, forgetting the factor of 2. Then 2sinθcosθ = √3/2, so sinθcosθ = √3/4, so the answer is √3/4.
Source: PYQ-style

150. If x = a cos θ + b sin θ and y = a sin θ − b cos θ, then x² + y² equals:
(a) a² + b² (b) a² − b² (c) ab (d) a²b²
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is to think the minus sign in y produces a² − b², but squaring removes it. Expanding, the cross terms 2absinθcosθ cancel and x² + y² = a²(sin²θ + cos²θ) + b²(sin²θ + cos²θ) = a² + b², so the answer is a² + b².
Source: PYQ-style
