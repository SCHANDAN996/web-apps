101. If sin A = 1/2 and A is acute, then the value of 3 cos A − 4 cos³ A is:
(a) 0 (b) 1 (c) 3/2 (d) −3/2
Answer: (a)
Solution: The tempting slip is to compute 3 cos A − 4 cos A = −cos A, forgetting the cube. Here 3 cos A − 4 cos³ A = cos 3A, and sin A = 1/2 gives A = 30°, so cos 90° = 0, so the answer is 0.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Mathematics (concept); PYQ-style

102. If sin θ = 12/13 and θ is acute, then (1 + sin θ)/(1 − sin θ) equals:
(a) 25/169 (b) 169/25 (c) 144/25 (d) 25
Answer: (d)
Solution: Many students rationalise and stop at (1 + sin θ)²/(1 − sin² θ), picking a squared-looking option like 169/25. Directly, (1 + 12/13)/(1 − 12/13) = (25/13)/(1/13) = 25, so the answer is 25.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Mathematics

103. tan 47° / cot 43° equals:
(a) 0 (b) tan 4° (c) 1 (d) cot 4°
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is to subtract the angles and choose tan 4°. Since cot 43° = tan(90° − 43°) = tan 47°, the ratio is tan 47°/tan 47° = 1, so the answer is 1.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Mathematics

104. A ladder 10 m long just reaches the top of a wall and makes an angle of 60° with the wall. The height of the wall is:
(a) 5√3 m (b) 5 m (c) 10 m (d) 10√3 m
Answer: (b)
Solution: The common error is using the angle with the ground instead of the wall, giving 5√3. The height is adjacent to the 60° angle with the wall, so cos 60° = h/10 gives h = 10 × 1/2 = 5, so the answer is 5 m.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Mathematics, Exercise 9.1

105. If sin θ = x and sec θ = y, then cot θ is:
(a) 1/(xy) (b) y/x (c) xy (d) x/y
Answer: (a)
Solution: Students often write cot θ = cos θ/sin θ and then invert only one term, landing on xy. Since cos θ = 1/sec θ = 1/y, we get cot θ = (1/y)/x = 1/(xy), so the answer is 1/(xy).
Source: NCERT Class 10 Mathematics

106. If tan θ = a/b, then (a sin θ + b cos θ)/(a sin θ − b cos θ) is equal to:
(a) (a² − b²)/(a² + b²) (b) (a − b)/(a + b) (c) (a + b)/(a − b) (d) (a² + b²)/(a² − b²)
Answer: (d)
Solution: Dividing numerator and denominator by cos θ gives (a tan θ + b)/(a tan θ − b); the trap is substituting tan θ = a/b carelessly to get (a + b)/(a − b). Correctly, (a²/b + b)/(a²/b − b) = (a² + b²)/(a² − b²), so the answer is (a² + b²)/(a² − b²).
Source: PYQ-style

107. The value of sin(60° + θ) − cos(30° − θ) is:
(a) 2 cos θ (b) 2 sin θ (c) 1 (d) 0
Answer: (d)
Solution: Expanding both terms fully tempts students into a long simplification that seems to give 2 sin θ. But sin(60° + θ) = sin[90° − (30° − θ)] = cos(30° − θ), so the difference is 0, so the answer is 0.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Mathematics (complementary angles)

108. From the top of a building 15 m high, the angle of depression of a point on the ground is 30°. The distance of the point from the base of the building is:
(a) 15√3 m (b) 15/√3 m (c) 15 m (d) 30 m
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is putting the height in the numerator wrongly, getting 15/√3. By alternate angles the elevation from the point is 30°, so tan 30° = 15/d gives d = 15√3, so the answer is 15√3 m.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Mathematics, Exercise 9.1

109. If sin θ + cos θ = √2 cos θ, then cos θ − sin θ is:
(a) √2 cos θ (b) √2 sin θ (c) cos θ (d) sin θ
Answer: (b)
Solution: Squaring the given equation, 1 + 2 sin θ cos θ = 2 cos² θ. Then (cos θ − sin θ)² = 1 − 2 sin θ cos θ = 2 − 2 cos² θ = 2 sin² θ; the trap is assuming symmetry gives √2 cos θ again. Taking the positive root, the answer is √2 sin θ.
Source: PYQ-style

110. If cot θ = 1/√3, then (1 − cos² θ)/(2 − sin² θ) equals:
(a) 1/3 (b) 2/3 (c) 3/4 (d) 3/5
Answer: (d)
Solution: cot θ = 1/√3 gives θ = 60°, so cos² θ = 1/4 and sin² θ = 3/4. The trap is computing (1 − cos² θ)/(2 − cos² θ), which gives 3/7-like confusion; correctly the ratio is (3/4)/(5/4) = 3/5, so the answer is 3/5.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Mathematics

111. From a point on the ground the angle of elevation of the top of a pole is α; after walking a distance d towards the pole it becomes β. The height h of the pole is:
(a) d/(cot α − cot β) written as d tan α tan β/(tan α − tan β) (b) d tan α tan β/(tan β − tan α) (c) d/(tan β − tan α) (d) d/(tan α + tan β)
Answer: (b)
Solution: Let the initial distance be x; then x = h cot α and x − d = h cot β, so d = h(cot α − cot β). The trap is forgetting to convert cot into tan when solving for h. Since cot α − cot β = (tan β − tan α)/(tan α tan β), we get h = d tan α tan β/(tan β − tan α), so the answer is d tan α tan β/(tan β − tan α).
Source: NCERT Class 10 Mathematics (application)

112. The maximum value of 1/cosec θ is:
(a) 1 (b) −1 (c) 0 (d) not defined
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is thinking cosec θ ≥ 1 makes the fraction unbounded or zero. Since 1/cosec θ = sin θ and the maximum value of sin θ is 1, the answer is 1.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Mathematics

113. sin² 63° + sin² 27° is:
(a) 2 (b) 0 (c) 1 (d) 1/2
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is adding the angles and choosing sin² 90° logic loosely, or guessing 2. Since sin 27° = cos 63°, the expression becomes sin² 63° + cos² 63° = 1, so the answer is 1.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Mathematics

114. If sec θ − tan θ = 1/2, then sec θ is:
(a) 4/3 (b) 5/2 (c) 5/4 (d) 2
Answer: (c)
Solution: Using sec² θ − tan² θ = 1, we get sec θ + tan θ = 2. The trap is reporting this sum 2 as sec θ. Adding the two equations, 2 sec θ = 2 + 1/2 = 5/2, so sec θ = 5/4, so the answer is 5/4.
Source: PYQ-style

115. cos 18° / sin 72° equals:
(a) 1 (b) 0 (c) tan 18° (d) cot 18°
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is converting only one function and landing on tan 18°. Since sin 72° = cos(90° − 72°) = cos 18°, the ratio is cos 18°/cos 18° = 1, so the answer is 1.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Mathematics

116. If sin θ = 3/5 and cos φ = 12/13 (θ, φ acute), then sin(θ + φ) is:
(a) 63/65 (b) 33/65 (c) 16/65 (d) 56/65
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is using sin θ cos φ − cos θ sin φ, which gives 16/65. With cos θ = 4/5 and sin φ = 5/13, sin(θ + φ) = (3/5)(12/13) + (4/5)(5/13) = 36/65 + 20/65 = 56/65, so the answer is 56/65.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Mathematics (concept); PYQ-style

117. A rope connects a point on the ground to the top of a 10 m pole and makes an angle of 30° with the ground. The length of the rope is:
(a) 20 m (b) 10√3 m (c) 10 m (d) 10/√3 m
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is using cos 30° instead of sin 30°, giving 10/√3-type results. Here sin 30° = 10/L, so L = 10/(1/2) = 20, so the answer is 20 m.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Mathematics, Exercise 9.1

118. If tan θ + cot θ = 2, then tan¹⁰⁰ θ + cot¹⁰⁰ θ is:
(a) 0 (b) 2 (c) 2¹⁰⁰ (d) 1
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is assuming the large power makes the value huge, picking 2¹⁰⁰. From tan θ + 1/tan θ = 2 we get (tan θ − 1)² = 0, so tan θ = cot θ = 1, and 1¹⁰⁰ + 1¹⁰⁰ = 2, so the answer is 2.
Source: PYQ-style

119. (1 − tan² 30°)/(1 + tan² 30°) simplifies to:
(a) cot 30° (b) sin 60° (c) tan 60° (d) cos 60°
Answer: (d)
Solution: With tan 30° = 1/√3, the expression is (1 − 1/3)/(1 + 1/3) = (2/3)/(4/3) = 1/2. The trap is picking sin 60° = √3/2 by confusing the identity with a double-angle sine; the value 1/2 equals cos 60°, so the answer is cos 60°.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Mathematics

120. If sin θ = a/c (θ acute), then cos θ in terms of a and c is:
(a) √(c² − a²)/c (b) √(a² + c²)/c (c) a/√(c² − a²) (d) c/a
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is adding the squares, giving √(a² + c²)/c, which would exceed 1. Since cos θ = √(1 − sin² θ) = √(1 − a²/c²) = √(c² − a²)/c, the answer is √(c² − a²)/c.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Mathematics

121. If x = a sin θ and y = b tan θ, then a²/x² − b²/y² is equal to:
(a) 0 (b) a²/b² (c) 1 (d) b²/a²
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is using sec² θ − tan² θ = 1 directly on the given forms. Here a/x = cosec θ and b/y = cot θ, and cosec² θ − cot² θ = 1, so a²/x² − b²/y² = 1, so the answer is 1.
Source: PYQ-style

122. The angle of elevation of a cloud from a point 60 m above a lake is 30°, and the angle of depression of its reflection in the lake is 60°. The height of the cloud above the lake is:
(a) 60 m (b) 90 m (c) 120 m (d) 80 m
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is taking the reflection only 60 m below the observer instead of h + 60. If the cloud is h m above the lake, tan 30° = (h − 60)/d and tan 60° = (h + 60)/d; dividing gives 3 = (h + 60)/(h − 60), so h = 120, so the answer is 120 m.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Mathematics, Exercise 9.1 (harder variant)

123. If tan θ + sin θ = m and tan θ − sin θ = n, then m² − n² is equal to:
(a) mn (b) 2√(mn) (c) 4√(mn) (d) √(mn)
Answer: (c)
Solution: m² − n² = (m + n)(m − n) = 4 tan θ sin θ. Also mn = tan² θ − sin² θ = sin⁴ θ/cos² θ, so √(mn) = sin² θ/cos θ = tan θ sin θ; the trap is stopping at 4 tan θ sin θ without linking it to mn. Hence m² − n² = 4√(mn), so the answer is 4√(mn).
Source: PYQ-style

124. If sin θ + sin² θ + sin³ θ = 1, then cos⁶ θ − 4 cos⁴ θ + 8 cos² θ equals:
(a) 1 (b) 4 (c) 3 (d) 2
Answer: (b)
Solution: Writing s = sin θ, the condition gives s³ = 1 − s − s² and, multiplying by s and subtracting, s⁴ = 2s − 1; then s⁶ = 2s³ − s² = 2 − 2s − 3s². Substituting cos² θ = 1 − s², the expression expands to 5 − 3s² − s⁴ − s⁶ = 5 − 3s² − (2s − 1) − (2 − 2s − 3s²) = 4. The trap is guessing 1 by analogy with sin² + cos², so the answer is 4.
Source: PYQ-style (hard)

125. The shadow of a tower on level ground increases by 30 m when the sun's altitude changes from 60° to 30°. The height of the tower is:
(a) 30 m (b) 15√3 m (c) 30√3 m (d) 15 m
Answer: (b)
Solution: Shadows are h cot 60° = h/√3 and h cot 30° = h√3; the trap is taking the difference as h√3 alone, giving 30/√3 = 10√3-like confusion. The difference h√3 − h/√3 = 2h/√3 = 30 gives h = 15√3, so the answer is 15√3 m.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Mathematics, Exercise 9.1 (harder variant)
