Set 1: LCM & HCF MCQs (English)
Questions 1–20: Easy | Questions 21–25: Medium

Q1. The HCF of 12 and 18 is
(a) 3 (b) 6 (c) 9 (d) 12
Answer: (b) 6
Solution: 12 = 2² × 3 and 18 = 2 × 3². Common primes with least powers = 2 × 3 = 6.
Source: NCERT Class 6

Q2. The LCM of 8 and 12 is
(a) 16 (b) 24 (c) 36 (d) 48
Answer: (b) 24
Solution: 8 = 2³ and 12 = 2² × 3. LCM takes highest powers = 2³ × 3 = 24.
Source: NCERT Class 6

Q3. The HCF of 15 and 25 is
(a) 3 (b) 5 (c) 10 (d) 15
Answer: (b) 5
Solution: 15 = 3 × 5 and 25 = 5². The only common prime is 5, so HCF = 5.
Source: NCERT Class 6

Q4. The LCM of 4, 6 and 8 is
(a) 12 (b) 24 (c) 36 (d) 48
Answer: (b) 24
Solution: 4 = 2², 6 = 2 × 3, 8 = 2³. LCM = 2³ × 3 = 24.
Source: NCERT Class 6

Q5. The smallest number divisible by both 5 and 7 is
(a) 12 (b) 35 (c) 45 (d) 70
Answer: (b) 35
Solution: 5 and 7 are co-prime, so the smallest common multiple is 5 × 7 = 35.
Source: NCERT Class 6

Q6. The HCF of two co-prime numbers is always
(a) 0 (b) 1 (c) their product (d) their sum
Answer: (b) 1
Solution: Co-prime numbers share no common factor except 1, so their HCF is 1.
Source: NCERT Class 6

Q7. For any two numbers, LCM × HCF equals
(a) their sum (b) their difference (c) their product (d) their ratio
Answer: (c) their product
Solution: A fundamental rule: LCM × HCF = first number × second number.
Source: NCERT Class 8

Q8. The HCF of 24 and 36 is
(a) 6 (b) 8 (c) 12 (d) 18
Answer: (c) 12
Solution: 24 = 2³ × 3 and 36 = 2² × 3². Common primes with least powers = 2² × 3 = 12.
Source: NCERT Class 6

Q9. The LCM of 9 and 12 is
(a) 24 (b) 36 (c) 48 (d) 108
Answer: (b) 36
Solution: 9 = 3² and 12 = 2² × 3. LCM = 2² × 3² = 36.
Source: NCERT Class 6

Q10. The greatest number that divides both 60 and 75 exactly is
(a) 5 (b) 10 (c) 15 (d) 25
Answer: (c) 15
Solution: 60 = 2² × 3 × 5 and 75 = 3 × 5². Common primes = 3 × 5 = 15.
Source: NCERT Class 6

Q11. The HCF of two numbers is 8 and their LCM is 48. If one number is 24, the other is
(a) 12 (b) 16 (c) 18 (d) 32
Answer: (b) 16
Solution: Other = (HCF × LCM) ÷ given = (8 × 48) ÷ 24 = 384 ÷ 24 = 16.
Source: NCERT Class 8

Q12. The LCM of 10, 15 and 20 is
(a) 30 (b) 45 (c) 60 (d) 120
Answer: (c) 60
Solution: 10 = 2 × 5, 15 = 3 × 5, 20 = 2² × 5. LCM = 2² × 3 × 5 = 60.
Source: NCERT Class 6

Q13. Two numbers are in the ratio 3 : 4 and their HCF is 5. Their LCM is
(a) 40 (b) 50 (c) 60 (d) 75
Answer: (c) 60
Solution: The numbers are 15 and 20. 15 = 3 × 5, 20 = 2² × 5. LCM = 2² × 3 × 5 = 60.
Source: SSC CGL

Q14. The HCF of 18, 24 and 30 is
(a) 2 (b) 3 (c) 6 (d) 12
Answer: (c) 6
Solution: 18 = 2 × 3², 24 = 2³ × 3, 30 = 2 × 3 × 5. Common = 2 × 3 = 6.
Source: NCERT Class 6

Q15. The smallest number that is exactly divisible by both 12 and 16 is
(a) 24 (b) 36 (c) 48 (d) 96
Answer: (c) 48
Solution: This is the LCM. 12 = 2² × 3, 16 = 2⁴. LCM = 2⁴ × 3 = 48.
Source: NCERT Class 6

Q16. The LCM of the two co-prime numbers 7 and 9 is
(a) 16 (b) 63 (c) 1 (d) 126
Answer: (b) 63
Solution: For co-prime numbers, LCM = their product = 7 × 9 = 63.
Source: NCERT Class 8

Q17. The HCF of 36, 48 and 72 is
(a) 6 (b) 8 (c) 12 (d) 24
Answer: (c) 12
Solution: 36 = 2² × 3², 48 = 2⁴ × 3, 72 = 2³ × 3². Common = 2² × 3 = 12.
Source: SSC CHSL

Q18. The product of two numbers is 2028 and their HCF is 13. Their LCM is
(a) 26 (b) 156 (c) 169 (d) 2028
Answer: (b) 156
Solution: LCM = product ÷ HCF = 2028 ÷ 13 = 156.
Source: NCERT Class 8

Q19. Three bells ring at intervals of 9, 12 and 15 minutes. If they ring together now, after how long will they ring together again?
(a) 60 min (b) 120 min (c) 180 min (d) 200 min
Answer: (c) 180 min
Solution: LCM of 9, 12, 15. 9 = 3², 12 = 2² × 3, 15 = 3 × 5. LCM = 2² × 3² × 5 = 180 minutes.
Source: SSC CGL

Q20. The HCF of two numbers is 16 and their LCM is 240. If one number is 48, the other is
(a) 60 (b) 72 (c) 80 (d) 96
Answer: (c) 80
Solution: Other = (HCF × LCM) ÷ given = (16 × 240) ÷ 48 = 3840 ÷ 48 = 80.
Source: RRB NTPC

Q21. (Medium) The greatest number which divides 2011 and 2623 leaving remainders 9 and 5 respectively is
(a) 77 (b) 154 (c) 308 (d) 11
Answer: (b) 154
Solution: It must divide (2011 − 9) = 2002 and (2623 − 5) = 2618 exactly. 2002 = 2 × 7 × 11 × 13, 2618 = 2 × 7 × 11 × 17. HCF = 2 × 7 × 11 = 154.
Source: SSC CGL

Q22. (Medium) The least number which when divided by 6, 7, 8, 9 and 12 leaves remainder 1 each time is
(a) 253 (b) 505 (c) 1009 (d) 1513
Answer: (b) 505
Solution: LCM of 6, 7, 8, 9, 12 = 2³ × 3² × 7 = 504. Required number = 504 + 1 = 505.
Source: SSC CHSL

Q23. (Medium) The LCM of two numbers is 2079 and their HCF is 27. If one number is 189, the other is
(a) 297 (b) 189 (c) 231 (d) 351
Answer: (a) 297
Solution: Other = (LCM × HCF) ÷ given = (2079 × 27) ÷ 189 = 56133 ÷ 189 = 297. Check: 189 = 27 × 7, 297 = 27 × 11.
Source: SSC CGL

Q24. (Medium) The largest 4-digit number exactly divisible by 12, 15, 18 and 27 is
(a) 9720 (b) 9999 (c) 9540 (d) 9800
Answer: (a) 9720
Solution: LCM of 12, 15, 18, 27 = 2² × 3³ × 5 = 540. Largest 4-digit multiple: 9999 ÷ 540 = 18.5, so 18 × 540 = 9720.
Source: SSC CGL

Q25. (Medium) The HCF of two numbers is 11 and their LCM is 693. If one number is 77, the other is
(a) 88 (b) 99 (c) 66 (d) 110
Answer: (b) 99
Solution: Other = (HCF × LCM) ÷ given = (11 × 693) ÷ 77 = 7623 ÷ 77 = 99. Check: 77 = 7 × 11, 99 = 9 × 11, LCM = 7 × 9 × 11 = 693.
Source: SSC CGL
