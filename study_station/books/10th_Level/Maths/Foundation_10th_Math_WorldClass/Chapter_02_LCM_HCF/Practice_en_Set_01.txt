Set 1: LCM & HCF MCQs (English)
Questions 1–20: Easy | Questions 21–25: Medium

Q1. What is the Highest Common Factor (HCF) of 12 and 18?
(a) 6 (b) 3 (c) 9 (d) 12
Answer: (a) 6
Solution: Finding prime factorizations: 12 = 2² × 3 and 18 = 2 × 3². The common prime factors with their lowest exponents are 2¹ and 3¹, so HCF = 2 × 3 = 6. Thus, the HCF is 6. Hence, the correct option is (a).
Source: SSC CGL

Q2. What is the Least Common Multiple (LCM) of 8 and 12?
(a) 16 (b) 24 (c) 36 (d) 48
Answer: (b) 24
Solution: Finding prime factorizations: 8 = 2³ and 12 = 2² × 3. The LCM takes the highest power of each prime factor occurring in either number: LCM = 2³ × 3 = 8 × 3 = 24. Thus, the LCM is 24. Hence, the correct option is (b).
Source: SSC CHSL

Q3. What is the HCF of 15 and 25?
(a) 3 (b) 10 (c) 5 (d) 15
Answer: (c) 5
Solution: Prime factorizations: 15 = 3 × 5 and 25 = 5². The only common prime factor is 5, with lowest power 1. Therefore, HCF = 5. Hence, the correct option is (c).
Source: SSC MTS

Q4. What is the LCM of 4, 6, and 8?
(a) 12 (b) 16 (c) 36 (d) 24
Answer: (d) 24
Solution: Prime factorizations: 4 = 2², 6 = 2 × 3, and 8 = 2³. The highest power of 2 is 2³ and the highest power of 3 is 3¹. LCM = 2³ × 3 = 8 × 3 = 24. Hence, the LCM is 24. Hence, the correct option is (d).
Source: RRB NTPC

Q5. What is the smallest positive integer divisible by both 5 and 7?
(a) 35 (b) 12 (c) 45 (d) 70
Answer: (a) 35
Solution: Since 5 and 7 are prime numbers, they are mutually co-prime. The smallest number divisible by both is their LCM: LCM(5, 7) = 5 × 7 = 35. Therefore, the smallest number is 35. Hence, the correct option is (a).
Source: RRB Group D

Q6. The HCF of any two co-prime numbers is always equal to:
(a) 0 (b) 1 (c) Their product (d) Their sum
Answer: (b) 1
Solution: By definition, two numbers are co-prime if they share no common factor other than 1. Therefore, their Highest Common Factor (HCF) is always 1. Hence, the correct option is (b).
Source: SSC CGL

Q7. For any two positive integers, the product LCM × HCF is always equal to:
(a) Their sum (b) Their difference (c) Their product (d) Their ratio
Answer: (c) Their product
Solution: By the fundamental theorem of arithmetic for two numbers: LCM(a, b) × HCF(a, b) = a × b, which is Their product. Hence, the correct option is (c).
Source: SSC CHSL

Q8. What is the HCF of 24 and 36?
(a) 6 (b) 8 (c) 18 (d) 12
Answer: (d) 12
Solution: Prime factorizations: 24 = 2³ × 3 and 36 = 2² × 3². The common prime factors with lowest powers are 2² and 3¹. HCF = 2² × 3 = 4 × 3 = 12. Thus, the HCF is 12. Hence, the correct option is (d).
Source: SSC MTS

Q9. What is the LCM of 9 and 12?
(a) 36 (b) 24 (c) 48 (d) 108
Answer: (a) 36
Solution: Prime factorizations: 9 = 3² and 12 = 2² × 3. LCM takes the highest power of each prime factor: LCM = 2² × 3² = 4 × 9 = 36. Hence, the LCM is 36. Hence, the correct option is (a).
Source: RRB NTPC

Q10. What is the greatest positive integer that divides both 60 and 75 exactly without leaving any remainder?
(a) 5 (b) 15 (c) 10 (d) 25
Answer: (b) 15
Solution: The greatest number that divides both numbers is their HCF. Factoring gives 60 = 2² × 3 × 5 and 75 = 3 × 5². Common prime factors with lowest powers: 3¹ × 5¹ = 15. Thus, the greatest number is 15. Hence, the correct option is (b).
Source: RRB Group D

Q11. The HCF of two numbers is 8 and their LCM is 48. If one number is 24, what is the other number?
(a) 12 (b) 18 (c) 16 (d) 32
Answer: (c) 16
Solution: Using the product rule: Product of numbers = HCF × LCM ⇒ 24 × Other = 8 × 48 = 384. Dividing by 24 gives: Other number = 384 / 24 = 16. Therefore, the other number is 16. Hence, the correct option is (c).
Source: SSC CGL

Q12. What is the LCM of 10, 15, and 20?
(a) 30 (b) 45 (c) 120 (d) 60
Answer: (d) 60
Solution: Prime factorizations: 10 = 2 × 5, 15 = 3 × 5, and 20 = 2² × 5. Taking highest powers of each prime: LCM = 2² × 3¹ × 5¹ = 4 × 3 × 5 = 60. Hence, the LCM is 60. Hence, the correct option is (d).
Source: SSC CHSL

Q13. Two numbers are in the ratio 3 : 4 and their HCF is 5. What is their LCM?
(a) 60 (b) 40 (c) 50 (d) 75
Answer: (a) 60
Solution: Let the numbers be 3x and 4x. Since 3 and 4 are co-prime, the HCF is x = 5. The numbers are 3(5) = 15 and 4(5) = 20. The LCM is 3 × 4 × x = 12 × 5 = 60. Thus, their LCM is 60. Hence, the correct option is (a).
Source: SSC MTS

Q14. What is the HCF of 18, 24, and 30?
(a) 3 (b) 6 (c) 12 (d) 2
Answer: (b) 6
Solution: Prime factorizations: 18 = 2 × 3², 24 = 2³ × 3, and 30 = 2 × 3 × 5. Common prime factors with lowest powers are 2¹ and 3¹. HCF = 2 × 3 = 6. Hence, the HCF is 6. Hence, the correct option is (b).
Source: RRB NTPC

Q15. What is the smallest positive integer that is exactly divisible by both 12 and 16?
(a) 24 (b) 36 (c) 48 (d) 96
Answer: (c) 48
Solution: The smallest common multiple is the LCM. Prime factorizations: 12 = 2² × 3 and 16 = 2⁴. LCM takes the highest power of each prime factor: LCM = 2⁴ × 3 = 16 × 3 = 48. Therefore, the smallest number is 48. Hence, the correct option is (c).
Source: RRB Group D

Q16. What is the LCM of the two co-prime numbers 7 and 9?
(a) 1 (b) 16 (c) 126 (d) 63
Answer: (d) 63
Solution: For any two co-prime numbers, the LCM is simply their product: LCM = 7 × 9 = 63. Thus, the LCM is 63. Hence, the correct option is (d).
Source: SSC CGL

Q17. What is the HCF of 36, 48, and 72?
(a) 12 (b) 6 (c) 8 (d) 24
Answer: (a) 12
Solution: Prime factorizations: 36 = 2² × 3², 48 = 2⁴ × 3, and 72 = 2³ × 3². The common prime factors with lowest powers are 2² and 3¹. HCF = 2² × 3 = 4 × 3 = 12. Thus, the HCF is 12. Hence, the correct option is (a).
Source: SSC CHSL

Q18. The product of two positive numbers is 2028 and their HCF is 13. What is their LCM?
(a) 26 (b) 156 (c) 169 (d) 2028
Answer: (b) 156
Solution: Using the relationship LCM × HCF = Product of numbers: LCM × 13 = 2028 ⇒ LCM = 2028 / 13 = 156. Therefore, their LCM is 156. Hence, the correct option is (b).
Source: SSC MTS

Q19. Three bells ring at intervals of 9, 12, and 15 minutes respectively. If they ring together now, after how many minutes will they ring together next?
(a) 60 min (b) 120 min (c) 180 min (d) 200 min
Answer: (c) 180 min
Solution: The bells will toll together after an interval equal to the LCM of 9, 12, and 15 minutes. Prime factorizations: 9 = 3², 12 = 2² × 3, and 15 = 3 × 5. LCM = 2² × 3² × 5 = 4 × 9 × 5 = 180 minutes. Thus, they will ring together after 180 min. Hence, the correct option is (c).
Source: RRB NTPC

Q20. The HCF of two numbers is 16 and their LCM is 240. If one of the numbers is 48, what is the other number?
(a) 60 (b) 72 (c) 96 (d) 80
Answer: (d) 80
Solution: Using the product rule: 48 × Other = HCF × LCM = 16 × 240 = 3840. Dividing by 48 gives: Other number = 3840 / 48 = 80. Hence, the other number is 80. Hence, the correct option is (d).
Source: RRB Group D

Q21. What is the greatest positive integer that divides 2011 and 2623 leaving remainders of 9 and 5 respectively?
(a) 154 (b) 77 (c) 308 (d) 11
Answer: (a) 154
Solution: The required number must divide (2011 – 9) = 2002 and (2623 – 5) = 2618 exactly. Factoring the differences: 2002 = 2 × 7 × 11 × 13, and 2618 = 2 × 7 × 11 × 17. The common prime factors are 2, 7, and 11, so HCF = 2 × 7 × 11 = 154. (Trap: Taking the HCF of 2011 and 2623 directly is incorrect; we must first subtract each respective remainder). Thus, the greatest divisor is 154. Hence, the correct option is (a).
Source: SSC CGL

Q22. What is the least positive integer which when divided by 6, 7, 8, 9, and 12 leaves a remainder of 1 in each case?
(a) 503 (b) 505 (c) 1009 (d) 1513
Answer: (b) 505
Solution: The required number has the form LCM(6, 7, 8, 9, 12) × k + 1. Prime factorizations: 6 = 2 × 3, 7 = 7, 8 = 2³, 9 = 3², and 12 = 2² × 3. LCM = 2³ × 3² × 7 = 8 × 9 × 7 = 504. For the smallest positive integer (k = 1), the number is 504 + 1 = 505. (Trap: Subtracting the remainder gives 503, which would leave a remainder of 503, not 1). Thus, the least number is 505. Hence, the correct option is (b).
Source: SSC CHSL

Q23. The LCM of two numbers is 2079 and their HCF is 27. If one of the numbers is 189, what is the other number?
(a) 189 (b) 231 (c) 297 (d) 351
Answer: (c) 297
Solution: Using the product formula: 189 × Other = LCM × HCF = 2079 × 27 = 56133. Dividing by 189 gives: Other number = 56133 / 189 = 297. (Check: 297 = 27 × 11 and 189 = 27 × 7, where 11 and 7 are co-prime). Hence, the other number is 297. Hence, the correct option is (c).
Source: SSC MTS

Q24. What is the largest four-digit number that is completely divisible by 12, 15, 18, and 27?
(a) 9540 (b) 9800 (c) 9999 (d) 9720
Answer: (d) 9720
Solution: First find LCM(12, 15, 18, 27): 12 = 2² × 3, 15 = 3 × 5, 18 = 2 × 3², 27 = 3³. LCM = 2² × 3³ × 5 = 4 × 27 × 5 = 540. The largest 4-digit number is 9999. Dividing 9999 by 540: 9999 = 540 × 18 + 279 (remainder = 279). Subtracting the remainder: 9999 – 279 = 9720. Hence, the largest divisible four-digit number is 9720. Hence, the correct option is (d).
Source: RRB NTPC

Q25. The HCF of two numbers is 11 and their LCM is 693. If one of the numbers is 77, what is the value of the other number?
(a) 88 (b) 99 (c) 66 (d) 110
Answer: (b) 99
Solution: Using the product formula: 77 × Other = HCF × LCM = 11 × 693 = 7623. Dividing by 77 gives: Other number = 7623 / 77 = 99. (Check: 77 = 11 × 7, 99 = 11 × 9, where 7 and 9 are co-prime). Therefore, the value of the other number is 99. Hence, the correct option is (b).
Source: RRB Group D
