Flashcards

Front: What is the one question every factor must say “yes” to?
Back: “Do I divide the number perfectly, leaving zero remainder?” Factors are numbers that divide exactly.

Front: A number’s multiples are like its family members. What makes them different from factors?
Back: Multiples are what you get when you multiply the number by 1, 2, 3,… They go on forever, unlike factors which are limited.

Front: If HCF were a person, what would its life motto be?
Back: “I am the greatest (largest) common factor that fits into everyone perfectly.” HCF = Highest Common Factor.

Front: If LCM had a superhero catchphrase, what would it be?
Back: “I am the smallest common multiple that can shelter all the numbers under my umbrella.” LCM = Least Common Multiple.

Front: What’s the prime factorization secret that turns big numbers into tiny building blocks?
Back: Break any number into a product of only prime numbers. E.g., 12 = 2 × 2 × 3 = 2² × 3.

Front: You have 24 (2³ × 3¹) and 36 (2² × 3²). To find their HCF, do you take the highest or lowest power of common primes?
Back: Lowest power. HCF = 2² × 3¹ = 12. (Because it must divide both completely, so it can’t exceed what’s common.)

Front: 🐊 What’s the Hungry Crocodile memory trick for HCF?
Back: The crocodile thinks, “I can only eat the lowest power of common primes – otherwise I’ll choke!” So HCF = lowest exponent of common primes.

Front: 🐪 What’s the Lazy Camel memory trick for LCM?
Back: The camel thinks, “I’m too lazy to climb small dunes; I pick the highest power of each prime to cover everyone.” So LCM = highest exponent of all primes.

Front: Why is the LCM of 24 and 36 equal to 72, not 48?
Back: 48 = 2⁴ × 3, but 36 only has 2², so 48 isn’t a multiple of 36. LCM must borrow the highest power from each: 2³ from 24, 3² from 36 → 72.

Front: ❤️ What is the ‘Love and Hugs Multiply the Pair’ trick for two numbers?
Back: LCM × HCF = Product of the two numbers. (L for LCM, H for HCF, multiply them and get the pair back.)

Front: If HCF is 12 and product of two numbers is 1728, how do you instantly find their LCM without a list?
Back: LCM = Product ÷ HCF = 1728 ÷ 12 = 144. The ‘Love and Hugs’ relationship strikes again!

Front: In a real-life problem, which keywords tell you to use HCF?
Back: “Greatest,” “maximum,” “largest,” “exactly divide,” “equal groups with nothing left over.”

Front: Which keywords whisper “use LCM” in a word problem?
Back: “Least,” “smallest,” “next time together,” “simultaneously,” “minimum common length.”

Front: Three bells ring at 18 sec, 24 sec, 32 sec. You want the time when they all ring together again. HCF or LCM?
Back: LCM – because we are aligning a future simultaneous event. LCM(18,24,32) = 288 seconds.

Front: You have 40 apples and 60 oranges. You want to pack them into boxes, each with the same number of apples and same number of oranges, no fruit left. Maximum number of boxes?
Back: HCF(40,60) = 20 boxes. (The greatest number that divides both counts exactly.)

Front: What changes when you find HCF of three numbers instead of two?
Back: You still take common primes, but now you pick the lowest power that appears in all three numbers. If a prime is missing in any, it’s out.

Front: For three numbers, the LCM rule says “take all primes with the highest power.” Does a prime that appears in only one number still count?
Back: Yes! LCM is the biggest collector; it grabs every prime from every number at its maximum exponent.

Front: 🧠 What’s the quick English clue to never confuse ‘lowest’ and ‘highest’ between HCF and LCM again?
Back: H for HCF → huddle (bring lower, shorter stack). L for LCM → lofty (tall, highest stack). Short for HCF, Tall for LCM.
