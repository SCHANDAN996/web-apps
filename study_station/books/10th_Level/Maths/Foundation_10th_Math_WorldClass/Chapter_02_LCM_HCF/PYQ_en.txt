Question 1: Product Rule: LCM × HCF = Product of Numbers (SSC CGL)

Question: The LCM of two numbers is 22 times their HCF. If one of the numbers is 132 and the sum of the LCM and HCF is 276, what is the other number?

Options: (a) 36  (b) 48  (c) 12  (d) 24

<details> <summary><b>✅ View Solution</b></summary>

Step-by-step solution:
Let HCF = H, then LCM = 22H.
Given: LCM + HCF = 276
⇒ 22H + H = 276
⇒ 23H = 276
⇒ H = 12
Therefore, LCM = 22 × 12 = 264.

Using the product rule: LCM × HCF = Number₁ × Number₂
⇒ 264 × 12 = 132 × x
⇒ 3168 = 132x
⇒ x = 24

Correct Answer: (d) 24

</details>

🧠 Psychological Trap: This is a direct formula-based question. The main risk is an arithmetic miscalculation during the addition 22H + H = 23H, which leads to an incorrect HCF. Ensure clean arithmetic.

Source: SSC CGL

Question 2: Ratio + LCM → HCF (SSC CGL)

Question: Three numbers are in the ratio 2 : 3 : 5 and their LCM is 90. Find their HCF.

Options: (a) 9  (b) 1  (c) 6  (d) 3

<details> <summary><b>✅ View Solution</b></summary>

Step-by-step solution:
Let the numbers be 2x, 3x, and 5x.
Since 2, 3, and 5 are mutually co-prime, their LCM is 2 × 3 × 5 × x = 30x.
Given: 30x = 90 ⇒ x = 3.
The numbers are: 2(3) = 6, 3(3) = 9, and 5(3) = 15.
Finding the HCF:
6 = 2 × 3
9 = 3²
15 = 3 × 5
The only common prime factor is 3 with lowest power 1.
Therefore, HCF = 3.

Correct Answer: (d) 3

</details>

🧠 Psychological Trap: Candidates often calculate x = 3 and then either choose 3 directly without understanding why, or confuse the common multiplier with the LCM. For co-prime ratio terms, the multiplier x is precisely the HCF.

Source: SSC CGL

Question 3: Sum/Difference of LCM and HCF (SSC CGL / MTS)

Question: The HCF of two numbers is 1/20 of their LCM. If the difference between their LCM and HCF is 456, and one of the numbers is 96, find the other number.

Options: (a) 144  (b) 120  (c) 96  (d) 72

<details> <summary><b>✅ View Solution</b></summary>

Step-by-step solution:
Let HCF = H, then LCM = 20H.
Given: LCM – HCF = 456
⇒ 20H – H = 456
⇒ 19H = 456
⇒ H = 24
Therefore, LCM = 20 × 24 = 480.

Using the product rule: LCM × HCF = Number₁ × Number₂
⇒ 480 × 24 = 96 × x
⇒ 11520 = 96x
⇒ x = 120

Correct Answer: (b) 120

</details>

🧠 Psychological Trap: Misreading "HCF is 1/20 of LCM" as "LCM is 1/20 of HCF" is a frequent reading trap. Additionally, seeing 96 in the question lures students into choosing option (c) 96. Always follow the algebraic relationship.

Source: SSC CGL

Question 4: Bells Ringing Together (SSC CGL / RRB)

Question: Four bells ring at intervals of 8, 12, 16, and 28 seconds respectively. If they start tolling together at 1:00 AM, at what time will they toll together next?

Options: (a) 1:04:48 AM  (b) 1:06:12 AM  (c) 1:05:36 AM  (d) 1:05:12 AM

<details> <summary><b>✅ View Solution</b></summary>

Step-by-step solution:
Prime factorization of intervals:
8 = 2³
12 = 2² × 3
16 = 2⁴
28 = 2² × 7

LCM(8, 12, 16, 28) = 2⁴ × 3 × 7 = 16 × 21 = 336 seconds.
Converting seconds to minutes:
336 seconds = 5 minutes and 36 seconds.
Adding to 1:00:00 AM gives 1:05:36 AM.

Correct Answer: (c) 1:05:36 AM

</details>

🧠 Psychological Trap: "Toll together next" requires finding the elapsed time equal to the LCM. If the question asked "How many times do they toll together in 1 hour?", one would divide and add 1 for the initial toll. Here, only the next simultaneous toll time is required.

Source: SSC CGL

Question 5: Same Remainder Case (HCF of Differences) (SSC CGL)

Question: Find the greatest number that will divide 108, 124, and 156 so as to leave the same remainder in each case.

Options: (a) 16  (b) 12  (c) 8  (d) 4

<details> <summary><b>✅ View Solution</b></summary>

Step-by-step solution:
Theorem: When different numbers leave the same remainder when divided by a common divisor D, the pairwise differences of the numbers are exactly divisible by D.
Computing differences:
124 – 108 = 16
156 – 124 = 32
156 – 108 = 48

Taking the HCF of the differences:
HCF(16, 32, 48) = 16.

Correct Answer: (a) 16

</details>

🧠 Psychological Trap: The common blunder is computing the HCF of the original numbers directly: HCF(108, 124, 156) = 4, which is prominently placed as option (d). For "same remainder" problems, always take the HCF of the differences!

Source: SSC CGL

Question 6: LCM and HCF of Fractions (SSC CGL)

Question: Find the ratio of the LCM to the HCF of the fractions 2/7, 12/35, and 8/49.

Options: (a) 420 : 1  (b) 120 : 1  (c) 210 : 1  (d) 490 : 1

<details> <summary><b>✅ View Solution</b></summary>

Step-by-step solution:
Formulas for fractions:
LCM of fractions = LCM of numerators / HCF of denominators
HCF of fractions = HCF of numerators / LCM of denominators

1. Calculate LCM:
LCM(2, 12, 8) = 24
HCF(7, 35, 49) = 7
LCM = 24/7

2. Calculate HCF:
HCF(2, 12, 8) = 2
LCM(7, 35, 49) = 7² × 5 = 245
HCF = 2/245

3. Ratio of LCM to HCF:
Ratio = (24/7) ÷ (2/245) = (24/7) × (245/2) = 12 × 35 = 420 : 1.

Correct Answer: (a) 420 : 1

</details>

🧠 Psychological Trap: Mixing up the fraction formulas. Memory rule: what you want to find goes to the numerators (LCM → LCM of numerators; HCF → HCF of numerators), while the opposite operation is applied to denominators.

Source: SSC CGL

Question 7: Smallest n-Digit Number (SSC CHSL)

Question: Find the smallest 5-digit number which is completely divisible by 12, 15, and 18.

Options: (a) 10020  (b) 10080  (c) 10025  (d) 10180

<details> <summary><b>✅ View Solution</b></summary>

Step-by-step solution:
Prime factorization:
12 = 2² × 3
15 = 3 × 5
18 = 2 × 3²
LCM(12, 15, 18) = 2² × 3² × 5 = 4 × 9 × 5 = 180.

The smallest 5-digit number is 10000.
Dividing 10000 by 180:
10000 = 180 × 55 + 100 (remainder = 100).
To make it divisible and retain at least 5 digits, add (180 – 100) = 80:
Smallest divisible number = 10000 + 80 = 10080.

Correct Answer: (b) 10080

</details>

🧠 Psychological Trap: Adding the remainder (100) instead of (LCM – remainder) gives 10100, which is incorrect. For "smallest number greater than N", add (divisor – remainder).

Source: SSC CHSL

Question 8: Ratio + LCM → HCF / Numbers (SSC CGL)

Question: Three numbers are in the ratio 2 : 3 : 5 and their LCM is 90. What is their HCF? (Pattern verification)

Options: (a) 9  (b) 1  (c) 6  (d) 3

<details> <summary><b>✅ View Solution</b></summary>

Step-by-step solution:
Let the numbers be 2x, 3x, and 5x.
Since 2, 3, and 5 are co-prime, LCM = 30x = 90 ⇒ x = 3.
The common factor x is the HCF = 3.

Correct Answer: (d) 3

</details>

🧠 Psychological Trap: When the numbers are in ratio a : b : c with a, b, c co-prime, the common multiplier x is identical to the HCF. Do not recalculate from scratch.

Source: SSC CGL

Question 9: Co-prime Numbers and LCM (SSC MTS)

Question: The product of two co-prime numbers is 117. What is their LCM?

Options: (a) 9  (b) 13  (c) 39  (d) 117

<details> <summary><b>✅ View Solution</b></summary>

Step-by-step solution:
By definition, co-prime numbers have an HCF of 1.
Using the product formula:
LCM × HCF = Product of numbers
⇒ LCM × 1 = 117
⇒ LCM = 117.

Correct Answer: (d) 117

</details>

🧠 Psychological Trap: Over-complicating by finding factors of 117 (9 × 13) and then computing their LCM. Simply apply HCF = 1 directly.

Source: SSC MTS

Question 10: Measuring Rod / Tiling — Practical HCF (IBPS / RRB)

Question: A rectangular courtyard is 140 cm long and 525 cm broad. It is to be paved with square tiles of equal size. What is the largest possible length of the side of each tile?

Options: (a) 64 cm  (b) 35 cm  (c) 21 cm  (d) 28 cm

<details> <summary><b>✅ View Solution</b></summary>

Step-by-step solution:
The "largest side" that divides both dimensions evenly requires the HCF of 140 and 525.
140 = 2² × 5 × 7
525 = 3 × 5² × 7
Common prime factors: 5¹ and 7¹.
HCF = 5 × 7 = 35 cm.

Correct Answer: (b) 35 cm

</details>

🧠 Psychological Trap: Confusing keywords: "largest", "maximum", "divide evenly" require HCF; "smallest", "meet together", "simultaneously" require LCM.

Source: IBPS RRB
