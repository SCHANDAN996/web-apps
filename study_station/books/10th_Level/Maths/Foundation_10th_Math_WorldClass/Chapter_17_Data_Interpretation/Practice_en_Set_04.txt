76. The table below shows the number of participants in different events at a school sports day.

Event | Boys | Girls
Race | 45 | 30
Relay | 35 | 25
Jumps | 40 | 15
Throws | 30 | 20

What percentage of total participants were girls?
(a) 33.3% (b) 42.5% (c) 40% (d) 37.5%
Answer: (d)
Solution: Total boys = 45+35+40+30 = 150 and total girls = 30+25+15+20 = 90, so total participants = 240. The trap is dividing girls by boys only, which gives 60%; the correct base is all participants. Girls % = (90/240)×100, so the answer is 37.5%.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Mathematics

77. Refer to the table in Question 76. In which event is the ratio of boys to girls the highest?
(a) Race (b) Jumps (c) Relay (d) Throws
Answer: (b)
Solution: The ratios are Race 45/30 = 1.5, Relay 35/25 = 1.4, Jumps 40/15 ≈ 2.67, Throws 30/20 = 1.5. The trap is picking Race because it has the most boys, but ratio depends on both numbers. The highest ratio is in Jumps, so the answer is Jumps.

(a) 32.5% (b) 28.6% (c) 30% (d) 26.7%
Answer: (d)
Solution: Decrease = 300 − 220 = 80, and the base is July's 300, so % decrease = (80/300)×100 ≈ 26.7%. The trap is using September's 220 as the base, which wrongly gives 36.4%. So the answer is 26.7%.

79. A pie chart (total ₹1,20,000) shows expenses: Housing 90°, Food 72°, Transport 54°, Education 72°, Others 72°. What amount is spent on Housing?
(a) ₹25,000 (b) ₹30,000 (c) ₹40,000 (d) ₹35,000
Answer: (b)
Solution: Housing angle 90° is one-fourth of 360°, so the amount is (1/4)×1,20,000 = ₹30,000. The trap is treating 90° as 90% of the total. So the answer is ₹30,000.

82. Data Sufficiency: What is the total number of fruits in a basket?
Statement I: 30% of the fruits are apples.
Statement II: The number of oranges is 21, which is 35% of the total.
(a) I alone is sufficient (b) Neither is sufficient (c) Both together are sufficient (d) II alone is sufficient
Answer: (d)
Solution: From Statement II, 35% of total = 21, so total = 21/0.35 = 60. Statement I gives only a percentage with no absolute number, so it cannot fix the total; the trap is assuming a percentage alone gives a count. So the answer is that II alone is sufficient.
Source: PYQ-style

92. A line graph gives the temperature (in °C) at noon for 5 days: 30, 32, 31, 35, 33. If the temperature on the 6th day makes the average of all six days exactly 32°C, what is the temperature on the 6th day?
(a) 31°C (b) 30°C (c) 32°C (d) 33°C
Answer: (a)
Solution: Sum of five days = 30+32+31+35+33 = 161; required six-day total = 6×32 = 192, so the 6th day = 192 − 161 = 31°C. The trap is assuming the 6th day must equal the target average of 32°C. So the answer is 31°C.

94. Data Sufficiency: What is the percentage of defective items in a lot?
Statement I: 20 items were defective.
Statement II: The total number of items in the lot is 500.
(a) I alone is sufficient (b) II alone is sufficient (c) Both together are sufficient (d) Neither is sufficient
Answer: (c)
Solution: A percentage needs both the defective count and the total; Statement I gives only the count and Statement II only the total. Together, percentage = (20/500)×100 = 4%. The trap is thinking the defective count alone fixes the percentage. So the answer is that both together are sufficient.

100. Data Sufficiency: What is the average score of a batsman in the last 5 matches?
Statement I: In the last 5 matches, he scored 40, 60, 80, 100 and 120 runs.
Statement II: His highest score is 120 and lowest is 40.
(a) II alone is sufficient (b) I alone is sufficient (c) Both together are sufficient (d) Neither is sufficient
Answer: (b)
Solution: Statement I lists all five scores, so the average (400/5 = 80) follows directly. Statement II gives only the extremes, and the middle three scores can vary; the trap is assuming the average of highest and lowest equals the overall average. So the answer is that I alone is sufficient.
Source: PYQ-style
