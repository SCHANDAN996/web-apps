1. The table below shows the sales (in kg) of two shops over four months.
Month — Jan, Feb, Mar, Apr; Shop A — 120, 150, 130, 140; Shop B — 100, 110, 120, 130.
What was the total sales of Shop A in all four months?
(a) 440 kg (b) 460 kg (c) 540 kg (d) 520 kg
Answer: (c)
Solution: Adding Shop A's sales: 120 + 150 + 130 + 140 = 540 kg. The trap is 460 kg, which is Shop B's total, not Shop A's. So the answer is 540 kg.
Source: NCERT Class 8 Mathematics

2. The table below shows the sales (in kg) of two shops over four months.

(a) Jan (b) Apr (c) Mar (d) Feb
Answer: (d)
Solution: Differences: Jan = 20, Feb = 40, Mar = 10, Apr = 10. The trap is picking Jan because it is the first month with a visible gap, but its gap is only 20 kg. The maximum difference is 40 kg, so the answer is Feb.

3. A shop sold 100 kg of rice in January and 120 kg in March. The sales in March are what percent of the sales in January?
(a) 120% (b) 110% (c) 80% (d) 90%
Answer: (a)
Solution: Required percent = (120/100) × 100 = 120%. The trap is 80%, which comes from dividing January by March instead of March by January. So the answer is 120%.

25. Question: What is the total number of apples sold?
Statement I: 40% of the total apples were sold on Monday, which is 50 apples.
Statement II: On Tuesday, 60 apples were sold.
Choose the correct option:
(a) Both statements together are sufficient, but neither alone is sufficient (b) Statement II alone is sufficient, but Statement I alone is not sufficient (c) Statement I alone is sufficient, but Statement II alone is not sufficient (d) Statements I and II together are not sufficient
Answer: (c)
Solution: From Statement I, 40% of total = 50, so total = 50/0.40 = 125 apples; Statement I alone suffices. The trap is thinking Tuesday's figure is also needed, but Statement II gives no link to the total. So the answer is that Statement I alone is sufficient.
