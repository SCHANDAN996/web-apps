26. The table below shows the number of visitors to a museum from Monday to Friday: Monday — Adults 240, Children 160; Tuesday — Adults 300, Children 200; Wednesday — Adults 180, Children 240; Thursday — Adults 320, Children 120; Friday — Adults 280, Children 280. What is the ratio of total adults to total children during the five days?
(a) 33 : 23 (b) 33 : 25 (c) 37 : 24 (d) 37 : 26
Answer: (b)
Solution: Total adults = 240 + 300 + 180 + 320 + 280 = 1320; total children = 160 + 200 + 240 + 120 + 280 = 1000. Dividing both by 40 gives 33 : 25; the trap is to simplify only partially and pick a nearby-looking ratio, so the answer is 33 : 25.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Mathematics

27. In the museum table of Question 26, on which day was the percentage of children among total visitors the highest?
(a) Monday (b) Friday (c) Thursday (d) Wednesday
Answer: (d)
Solution: Children share: Monday 160/400 = 40%, Tuesday 200/500 = 40%, Wednesday 240/420 ≈ 57%, Thursday 120/440 ≈ 27%, Friday 280/560 = 50%. Friday's 50% tempts because its children count is highest, but the share is highest on Wednesday, so the answer is Wednesday.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Mathematics

28. A bar chart shows production (in tonnes) of wheat and rice: 2016 — 50, 40; 2017 — 60, 55; 2018 — 70, 60; 2019 — 65, 70; 2020 — 80, 75. In which year was the combined production of wheat and rice maximum?
(a) 2017 (b) 2018 (c) 2019 (d) 2020
Answer: (d)
Solution: Combined totals are 90, 115, 130, 135 and 155 tonnes respectively. 2019 tempts because rice alone peaked late, but the sum is greatest in 2020 at 155 tonnes, so the answer is 2020.

29. In the bar chart of Question 28, what is the percentage increase in rice production from 2016 to 2020?
(a) 87.5% (b) 80% (c) 75% (d) 90%
Answer: (a)
Solution: Rice rose from 40 to 75 tonnes, an increase of 35. Percentage increase = (35/40) × 100 = 87.5%; the trap is dividing 35 by 75 (the new value), which gives about 46.7%, so the answer is 87.5%.
Source: PYQ-style

30. A pie chart of total expenses ₹80,000 shows: Rent 72°, Food 108°, Education 90°, Savings 54°, Miscellaneous 36°. What is the amount spent on Food?
(a) ₹24,000 (b) ₹20,000 (c) ₹28,000 (d) ₹32,000
Answer: (a)
Solution: Food's share = 108/360 = 3/10, so amount = (3/10) × 80,000 = ₹24,000. The trap is treating 108° as 108% or using 90° (Education) by mistake, so the answer is ₹24,000.
Source: NCERT Class 8 Mathematics

31. In the pie chart of Question 30, what is the ratio of the amount spent on Rent to that on Savings?
(a) 4 : 3 (b) 3 : 2 (c) 5 : 4 (d) 6 : 5
Answer: (a)
Solution: Amounts are proportional to angles, so Rent : Savings = 72 : 54 = 4 : 3. The trap is reversing the ratio to 3 : 4 or computing actual amounts and mis-simplifying, so the answer is 4 : 3.

32. In the pie chart of Question 30, if total expenses rise by 25% but the angle for Education stays the same, what is the new amount for Education?
(a) ₹25,000 (b) ₹20,000 (c) ₹18,000 (d) ₹28,000
Answer: (a)
Solution: Education is 90/360 = 1/4 of the total. New total = 80,000 × 1.25 = ₹1,00,000, so Education = ₹25,000. The trap is keeping the old amount ₹20,000, forgetting the total also grew, so the answer is ₹25,000.

33. A line graph shows employees (in hundreds) of a company: 2010 — 10, 2011 — 12, 2012 — 15, 2013 — 18, 2014 — 20, 2015 — 22. What is the average increase per year in the number of employees from 2010 to 2015?
(a) 200 (b) 220 (c) 250 (d) 240
Answer: (d)
Solution: Total increase = 22 − 10 = 12 hundred = 1200 employees over 5 years, so average = 1200/5 = 240. The trap is dividing by 6 (counting years instead of intervals), giving 200, so the answer is 240.
Source: PYQ-style

34. In the line graph of Question 33, in which year was the percentage increase in employees over the previous year the highest?
(a) 2012 (b) 2011 (c) 2013 (d) 2014
Answer: (a)
Solution: Percentage increases: 2011 = 2/10 = 20%, 2012 = 3/12 = 25%, 2013 = 3/15 = 20%, 2014 = 2/18 ≈ 11%. The trap is picking 2013 or 2014 because the absolute rise looks steady, but the highest percentage rise is 25% in 2012, so the answer is 2012.

35. Marks out of 100 of two students: A — Math 80, Science 65, English 75; B — Math 72, Science 80, English 70. What is the difference between the average marks of A and B?
(a) 0 (b) 1.33 (c) 0.67 (d) 2
Answer: (c)
Solution: A's total = 220, average = 73.33; B's total = 222, average = 74. Difference = 0.67. The trap is assuming equal averages because the marks look similar, so the answer is 0.67.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Mathematics

36. Using the marks in Question 35, what is the overall percentage of student A (rounded to one decimal place)?
(a) 72.0% (b) 75.2% (c) 68.9% (d) 73.3%
Answer: (d)
Solution: A scored 220 out of 300, so percentage = (220/300) × 100 = 73.33% ≈ 73.3%. The trap is averaging the three subject percentages incorrectly or dividing by 100 per subject twice, so the answer is 73.3%.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Mathematics

37. A company's revenue (in ₹ lakh): 2016 — 40, 2017 — 48, 2018 — 54, 2019 — 60, 2020 — 72. What is the approximate compound annual growth rate (CAGR) from 2016 to 2020?
(a) 12% (b) 14% (c) 16% (d) 18%
Answer: (c)
Solution: CAGR = [(72/40)^(1/4) − 1] × 100 = [1.8^0.25 − 1] × 100 ≈ (1.158 − 1) × 100 ≈ 16%. The trap is using simple growth 80% ÷ 4 = 20%, so the answer is 16%.
Source: PYQ-style

38. A pie chart of ₹1,20,000 shows: Food 30%, Rent 25%, Education 20%, Entertainment 10%, Savings 15%. If the amount on Entertainment is doubled and all other items stay the same, by what percentage does the total expenditure increase?
(a) 5% (b) 10% (c) 12.5% (d) 15%
Answer: (b)
Solution: Entertainment = 10% of 1,20,000 = ₹12,000; doubling adds ₹12,000, so increase = 12,000/1,20,000 × 100 = 10%. The trap is saying 20% because Entertainment "doubled", so the answer is 10%.
Source: PYQ-style

39. A line graph shows a car's speed (km/h): 8 am — 40, 9 am — 60, 10 am — 55, 11 am — 50, 12 noon — 70. What is the average speed during these five hours?
(a) 52 km/h (b) 54 km/h (c) 55 km/h (d) 56 km/h
Answer: (c)
Solution: Sum = 40 + 60 + 55 + 50 + 70 = 275, so average = 275/5 = 55 km/h. The trap is averaging only the extremes (40 and 70) to get 55 by luck but with wrong method, or mis-adding to 270, so the answer is 55 km/h.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Mathematics

Answer: (c)
Solution: Ratios P : Q are 30/20 = 1.5, 40/30 ≈ 1.33 and 50/45 ≈ 1.11. The trap is picking 2018 because the gap of 10 thousand looks smallest, but the lowest ratio is in 2020, so the answer is 2020.

Solution: Total in 2018 = 30 + 20 = 50 thousand; in 2020 = 50 + 45 = 95 thousand. Increase = 45, so percentage = (45/50) × 100 = 90%. The trap is dividing 45 by 95 (the new total), giving about 47%, so the answer is 90%.

42. Data Sufficiency — What is the total number of students in a class? Statement I: 40% of the students are girls and the number of boys is 30. Statement II: The ratio of boys to girls is 3 : 2.
(a) Statement I alone is sufficient, but Statement II alone is not (b) Statement II alone is sufficient, but Statement I alone is not (c) Both statements together are needed (d) Neither statement is sufficient
Answer: (a)
Solution: From Statement I, boys are 60% of the class, so total = 30/0.60 = 50 — sufficient. Statement II gives only a ratio with no actual count, so it is not sufficient; the trap is assuming a ratio fixes the total, so the answer is that Statement I alone is sufficient.
Source: PYQ-style

43. A family's monthly expenditure: Food ₹5,000, Rent ₹3,000, Education ₹2,000, Clothes ₹1,500, Others ₹2,500. What percentage of total expenditure is spent on Food and Rent together?
(a) 40% (b) 57.14% (c) 53.33% (d) 50%
Answer: (b)
Solution: Total = ₹14,000; Food + Rent = ₹8,000, so percentage = (8,000/14,000) × 100 ≈ 57.14%. The trap is dividing 8,000 by 16,000 (a mis-added total) to get 50%, so the answer is 57.14%.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Mathematics

44. In Question 43, the family reduces Clothes by 20% and increases Education by 25%, keeping total expenditure constant. How must the amount for Others change?
(a) Decrease by ₹250 (b) Increase by ₹200 (c) Decrease by ₹200 (d) Increase by ₹250
Answer: (c)
Solution: Clothes falls by 20% of 1500 = ₹300; Education rises by 25% of 2000 = ₹500; net change = +₹200, so Others must decrease by ₹200. The trap is subtracting the two changes (500 − 300) and then adding it to Others, so the answer is decrease by ₹200.
Source: PYQ-style

46. Tickets sold for a play: Premium — ₹500 each, 70 tickets; Gold — ₹300 each, 120 tickets; Silver — ₹200 each, 140 tickets. What is the total revenue?
(a) ₹1,00,000 (b) ₹99,000 (c) ₹1,05,000 (d) ₹1,10,000
Answer: (b)
Solution: Revenue = 500 × 70 + 300 × 120 + 200 × 140 = 35,000 + 36,000 + 28,000 = ₹99,000. The trap is adding the prices first (500 + 300 + 200 = 1000) and multiplying by total tickets, so the answer is ₹99,000.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Mathematics

47. Using the ticket data of Question 46, what is the average price per ticket sold (weighted average)?
(a) ₹280 (b) ₹290 (c) ₹300 (d) ₹310
Answer: (c)
Solution: Total revenue = ₹99,000 and total tickets = 70 + 120 + 140 = 330, so average = 99,000/330 = ₹300. The trap is taking the simple average of the three prices, (500 + 300 + 200)/3 ≈ ₹333, so the answer is ₹300.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Mathematics

48. A line graph shows quarterly profit (in ₹ lakh): Q1 — 20, Q2 — 30, Q3 — 40, Q4 — 50. What is the percentage increase in profit from Q1 to Q4?
(a) 100% (b) 150% (c) 125% (d) 175%
Answer: (b)
Solution: Increase = 50 − 20 = 30 lakh, so percentage increase = (30/20) × 100 = 150%. The trap is computing 50/20 = 250% or 30/50 = 60%, so the answer is 150%.
Source: PYQ-style

49. Total sales: 2018 — ₹200 lakh, 2019 — ₹250 lakh. Product shares: 2018 — A 40%, B 35%, C 25%; 2019 — A 30%, B 40%, C 30%. What is the change in sales of product B from 2018 to 2019?
(a) Increased by ₹25 lakh (b) Increased by ₹40 lakh (c) Increased by ₹35 lakh (d) Increased by ₹30 lakh
Answer: (d)
Solution: B in 2018 = 35% of 200 = ₹70 lakh; B in 2019 = 40% of 250 = ₹100 lakh; change = +₹30 lakh. The trap is comparing only the percentages (40% − 35% = 5%) and ignoring the different totals, so the answer is increased by ₹30 lakh.
Source: PYQ-style

50. Data Sufficiency — What is the percentage of marks obtained by a student? Statement I: The student scored 80 in Mathematics and 70 in Science. Statement II: The maximum marks in each subject are 100 and there are 5 subjects.
(a) Statement I alone is sufficient (b) Neither statement is sufficient (c) Both statements together are sufficient (d) Statement II alone is sufficient
Answer: (b)
Solution: Statement I gives marks in only two subjects; Statement II gives the maximum total (500) but no marks in the other three subjects. Even together the total obtained is unknown, so the percentage cannot be found; the trap is assuming two subjects' marks are enough, so the answer is that neither statement is sufficient.
Source: PYQ-style
