### 📖 Content — Data Interpretation (Foundation)

**Chapter Outline & Learning Goals**  
By the end of this chapter, you will be able to:  
- Read and understand tables, bar charts, line graphs, and pie charts without fear.  
- Calculate percentages, ratios, and averages on the fly.  
- Spot patterns hidden in boring numbers.  
- Avoid the three deadliest traps examiners set.  
- Use one weird memory trick to remember formula relationships forever.

#### The shocking truth about DI

Imagine a bank PO exam where a student leaves the DI section completely blank — and still passes. How? Because the cut‑off for that section was very low. The examiners knew that many candidates would waste all their time on one DI set and fail the whole paper. DI is not about being a maths genius; it's about knowing **what to ignore**. The person who filters noise from signal wins.

#### The two‑minute rule that changes everything

Before touching your pen, spend two full minutes just *looking*. Ask three questions:  
1. What is the topic? (Sales, population, marks?)  
2. What is the unit? (Lakhs, crores, percentage?)  
3. Where is the biggest change? (A sudden jump? A flat line?)  

If you skip this, you will calculate the right answer for the wrong question. Always.

🧠 **Active Recall Prompt** – What are the three questions you must ask before solving any DI set?

#### The four main types you will face

Most DI questions in SSC, banking, and railway exams fall into one of these four main types (you may occasionally also see caselets — data given as a paragraph — or mixed graphs combining two types):  
| **Type**        | **What it asks**                     | **Your weapon**          |
|-----------------|--------------------------------------|---------------------------|
| Table           | Compare numbers across rows/columns  | Approximation + units     |
| Bar Chart       | Show differences visually            | Height difference first   |
| Line Graph      | Trend over time                      | Slope direction           |
| Pie Chart       | Parts of a whole                     | Convert degrees to %     |

Once you train yourself to label the type in the first ten seconds, your brain automatically knows which short‑trick to load.

#### The weird memory trick that saves you in exams

**BALT** – a funny name for a delicious dish, but here it means:  
**B**ars – **A**lways compare heights  
**L**ines – **T**rend direction first  

Imagine a waiter serving a plate of BALT to a line of customers, and the line is moving up and down like a graph. The waiter shouts, "Bars are tall, lines are dancing!" Ridiculous. Unforgettable. Now you will never mix up when to focus on height versus trend.

#### The three calculations behind the vast majority of questions

1. **Percentage change** = ((New – Old) / Old) × 100  
2. **Ratio** = Value A : Value B, simplify by HCF  
3. **Average** = Sum of all values / Number of values  

You don't need to memorise dozens of formulas. Just these three, and learning to twist them. If a question asks "What percentage of total sales came from Product X?", that's just ratio disguised as percentage.

---

#### The "approximation superpower"

Examiners design DI questions so that exact calculations kill your time. They want you to approximate.  
Example: 63.7% of 4893. Instead of long multiplication, round it: 64% of 4900 → 0.64 × 4900 = 3136. The exact answer? 3116. The options are usually far enough apart that 3136 will be correct. Practise rounding to the nearest 5% or 10%. Your speed will double in one week.

🧠 **Active Recall Prompt** – What is the mental trick to compute 63.7% of 4893 in under 10 seconds?

#### Trap alert: The "hidden average" trap

A bar chart shows five years of profits. Question: "What is the average profit?" You add all bars, divide by 5. Simple. But the examiner has given you a table with *net* profit, and a footnote says "excluding taxes". If you don't read the footnote, your average is wrong. Footnotes are the chain‑saws of DI sets. Never skip them.
