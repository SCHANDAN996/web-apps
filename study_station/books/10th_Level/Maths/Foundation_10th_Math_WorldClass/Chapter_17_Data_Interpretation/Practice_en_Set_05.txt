101. The table below shows the quarterly sales (in units) of two products, P and Q, in a year.
Quarter — Product P — Product Q: Q1: 240, 180; Q2: 300, 200; Q3: 200, 250; Q4: 260, 220.
What is the ratio of total annual sales of Product P to that of Product Q?
(a) 25:17 (b) 20:17 (c) 25:19 (d) 20:19
Answer: (b)
Solution: Total P = 240+300+200+260 = 1000 and total Q = 180+200+250+220 = 850, so the ratio is 1000:850 = 20:17. The trap is stopping the simplification at 100:85 or mis-adding one quarter, which leads to 25:17; the correct answer is 20:17.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Mathematics

102. Using the same table of quarterly sales of P and Q (Q1: 240, 180; Q2: 300, 200; Q3: 200, 250; Q4: 260, 220), in which quarter was the combined sale of P and Q the lowest?
(a) Q4 (b) Q2 (c) Q3 (d) Q1
Answer: (d)
Solution: Combined sales are Q1 = 420, Q2 = 500, Q3 = 450, Q4 = 480. The trap is picking Q3 because Product P alone is lowest there, but Q's high sale lifts the total; the lowest combined sale is 420 in Q1.

103. Using the same table, the sale of Product P in Q2 is what percent more than its sale in Q3?
(a) 40% (b) 50% (c) 45% (d) 55%
Answer: (b)
Solution: P in Q2 = 300 and in Q3 = 200, so the increase is 100 on a base of 200, giving (100/200)×100 = 50%. The trap is dividing by 300 instead of the base 200, which gives about 33%; the correct answer is 50%.

104. A bar chart shows the number of cars sold by a dealership in five months: Jan 45, Feb 50, Mar 60, Apr 55, May 70. What is the average monthly sale?
(a) 56 (b) 55 (c) 57 (d) 54
Answer: (a)
Solution: Total = 45+50+60+55+70 = 280, so the average is 280/5 = 56. The trap is taking the middle value 60 or mis-totalling as 275; the correct average is 56.

105. If the same dealership (sales 45, 50, 60, 55, 70 in the first five months) wants an average of 60 cars per month over six months, how many cars must it sell in June?
(a) 70 (b) 80 (c) 75 (d) 85
Answer: (b)
Solution: Required six-month total = 60×6 = 360, and the five-month total is 280, so June must be 360 − 280 = 80. The trap is averaging 60 with the current average 56, which suggests 64–75; the required June sale is 80.

108. A line graph plots the number of visitors (in thousands) to a museum from Monday to Friday: 3.5, 4.0, 3.0, 4.5, 5.0. What is the percentage increase in visitors from Wednesday to Friday?

109. A table gives the number of employees and average monthly salary in three departments: HR — 10 employees, ₹30,000; IT — 20 employees, ₹50,000; Finance — 20 employees, ₹40,000. What is the overall average salary of all employees?
(a) ₹40,000 (b) ₹41,000 (c) ₹42,000 (d) ₹44,000
Answer: (c)
Solution: Total salary = 10×30,000 + 20×50,000 + 20×40,000 = ₹21,00,000 over 50 employees, so the average is ₹42,000. The trap is taking the simple average of the three salaries, which gives ₹40,000; the correct weighted average is ₹42,000.

110. Data Sufficiency: What is the value of a car after 2 years? Statement I: The car was purchased for ₹5,00,000. Statement II: The car depreciates at 10% per annum on its current value.
(a) Statement I alone is sufficient (b) Statement II alone is sufficient (c) Neither statement is sufficient (d) Both statements together are sufficient
Answer: (d)
Solution: Statement I gives only the initial price and Statement II gives only the rate; neither alone fixes the final value. Together, value = 5,00,000 × (0.9)² = ₹4,05,000, so both statements together are sufficient.

111. A pie chart divides a budget of ₹1,80,000 among four heads with central angles: Education 100°, Health 80°, Infrastructure 120°, Others 60°. What amount is allocated to Infrastructure?
(a) ₹65,000 (b) ₹50,000 (c) ₹55,000 (d) ₹60,000
Answer: (d)
Solution: Infrastructure's angle 120° is one-third of 360°, so its share is (1/3)×1,80,000 = ₹60,000. The trap is using 100° (Education) as the base, which gives ₹50,000; the correct amount is ₹60,000.
Source: NCERT Class 8 Mathematics

112. In the same budget of ₹1,80,000 (Education 100°, Others 60°), the Education allocation is increased by 20% while the total budget stays the same, and the increase is taken from Others. What is the new central angle for Others?
(a) 20° (b) 30° (c) 40° (d) 50°
Answer: (c)
Solution: Education = (100/360)×1,80,000 = ₹50,000, and a 20% rise adds ₹10,000, so Others falls from ₹30,000 to ₹20,000. The new angle is (20,000/1,80,000)×360 = 40°; the trap is reducing the 60° angle by 20% directly, which gives 48°. The correct answer is 40°.
Source: PYQ-style

113. A bar graph shows wheat production (in tonnes) over six years: 2015: 50, 2016: 60, 2017: 75, 2018: 70, 2019: 80, 2020: 90. What is the approximate compound annual growth rate from 2015 to 2020?
(a) 12.5% (b) 10.5% (c) 11.5% (d) 13.5%
Answer: (a)
Solution: CAGR = [(90/50)^(1/5) − 1]×100 = [1.8^0.2 − 1]×100 ≈ 12.5%, since 1.125⁵ ≈ 1.80. The trap is dividing the total 80% growth by 5 years, which gives 16%, or averaging yearly increases; the correct answer is about 12.5%.
Source: PYQ-style

Answer: (b)
Solution: Monsoon 2019 = 60% of 120 = 72 cm and monsoon 2020 = 70% of 150 = 105 cm, so the difference is 33 cm. The trap is comparing only the percentages (10% of 150 = 15 cm) or taking 10% of 120; the correct difference is 33 cm.

118. Data Sufficiency: A, B and C can complete a piece of work alone in 10, 12 and 15 days respectively. How long will they take to finish the work together? Statement I: All three work together throughout. Statement II: A and B work for 3 days, then C joins.
(a) Statement I alone is sufficient (b) Statement II alone is sufficient (c) Both statements together are needed (d) The question itself provides enough information
Answer: (d)
Solution: The individual times already fix the combined rate: 1/T = 1/10 + 1/12 + 1/15 = 15/60, so T = 4 days, and no statement is needed. The trap is assuming a data-sufficiency question must need a statement; here the question itself is sufficient.

121. Data Sufficiency: A shopkeeper sells two items. What is his overall profit percentage? Statement I: The total cost price of the two items is ₹500. Statement II: One item is sold at a profit of 20% and the other at a loss of 10%.
(a) Statement I alone is sufficient (b) Statement II alone is sufficient (c) Neither statement is sufficient even together (d) Both statements together are sufficient
Answer: (c)
Solution: The overall profit percentage depends on how the ₹500 cost is split between the two items, which neither statement gives. The trap is assuming the profits and losses cancel to a fixed net figure; since the individual cost prices are unknown, neither statement suffices even together.

122. A family's total monthly expenditure is ₹40,000, shown in a pie chart. The expenditure on Food and Education together is ₹18,000, and the central angle for Food is 108°. What is the central angle for Education?
(a) 54° (b) 60° (c) 72° (d) 90°
Answer: (a)
Solution: Food = (108/360)×40,000 = ₹12,000, so Education = 18,000 − 12,000 = ₹6,000, and its angle is (6,000/40,000)×360 = 54°. The trap is halving 108° to get 54° by luck or taking 18,000's angle (162°) minus 108° wrongly; computed properly, the answer is 54°.
Source: PYQ-style

123. A factory's monthly production and defective percentage are: Jan 500 units, 5%; Feb 600, 5%; Mar 800, 5%; Apr 700, 5%; May 900, 5%. What is the total number of non-defective units produced in the five months?
(a) 3200 (b) 3325 (c) 3250 (d) 3400
Answer: (b)
Solution: Total production = 3500 and total defective = 5% of 3500 = 175, so non-defective units = 3500 − 175 = 3325. The trap is computing 5% of only some months or subtracting 175 twice; the correct total is 3325.
Source: PYQ-style

124. Data Sufficiency: What is the number of students in a class? Statement I: The average weight of the class is 40 kg. Statement II: If a student weighing 60 kg leaves, the average weight becomes 39.5 kg.
(a) Both statements together are sufficient (b) Statement II alone is sufficient (c) Statement I alone is sufficient (d) Neither statement is sufficient
Answer: (a)
Solution: Statement I alone gives only the average and Statement II alone links two unknowns, so neither works alone. Together, with n students, (40n − 60)/(n − 1) = 39.5 gives 0.5n = 20.5, so n = 41; both statements together are sufficient.
Source: PYQ-style

125. A company's total sales were ₹10 lakh in 2020 and ₹12 lakh in 2021. Product shares were: A 30% then 25%, B 40% then 50%, C 30% then 25%. Profit margins are: A 20%, B 10%, C 25%. What is the approximate percentage increase in total profit from 2020 to 2021?
(a) 9.5% (b) 14.2% (c) 12.8% (d) 11.4%
Answer: (d)
Solution: Profit in 2020 = 0.6 + 0.4 + 0.75 = ₹1.75 lakh and in 2021 = 0.6 + 0.6 + 0.75 = ₹1.95 lakh, so the increase is (0.20/1.75)×100 ≈ 11.4%. The trap is using the 20% sales growth or computing the increase on 2021's profit; the correct answer is about 11.4%.
Source: PYQ-style
