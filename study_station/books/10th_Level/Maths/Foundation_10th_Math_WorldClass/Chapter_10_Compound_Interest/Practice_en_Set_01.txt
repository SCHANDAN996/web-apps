1. Find the compound interest on ₹1000 at 10% per annum for 2 years, compounded annually.
(a) ₹210 (b) ₹200 (c) ₹220 (d) ₹190
Answer: (a)
Solution: Amount = 1000 × (1.1)² = 1000 × 1.21 = ₹1210, so CI = 1210 − 1000. The trap is ₹200, which is only the simple interest for 2 years and ignores interest on the first year's interest. The compound interest is ₹210.
Source: NCERT Class 8 Mathematics

2. What is the amount on ₹5000 at 8% per annum compound interest for 2 years?
(a) ₹5832 (b) ₹5808 (c) ₹5800 (d) ₹5820
Answer: (a)
Solution: Amount = 5000 × (1.08)² = 5000 × 1.1664. The trap is ₹5800, which treats the interest as simple interest (5000 × 8% × 2 = 800). The correct amount is ₹5832.
Source: NCERT Class 8 Mathematics

3. The difference between compound interest and simple interest on ₹2000 at 10% per annum for 2 years is:
(a) ₹20 (b) ₹40 (c) ₹10 (d) ₹30
Answer: (a)
Solution: For 2 years, CI − SI = P × (R/100)² = 2000 × (0.1)² = 2000 × 0.01. The trap is ₹40, obtained by wrongly doubling the difference or using the rate itself instead of its square. The difference is ₹20.
Source: PYQ-style

4. Simple interest on a sum at 5% per annum for 2 years is ₹100. The compound interest on the same sum at the same rate and time is:
(a) ₹100 (b) ₹105 (c) ₹102.50 (d) ₹110
Answer: (c)
Solution: From SI = 100 = P × 5 × 2/100, we get P = ₹1000. CI = 1000 × (1.05)² − 1000 = 1102.50 − 1000. The trap is ₹100, assuming CI equals SI, but CI exceeds SI by P(R/100)² = ₹2.50. The compound interest is ₹102.50.
Source: PYQ-style

5. A sum becomes ₹12100 in 2 years at 10% per annum compound interest. The sum is:
(a) ₹11000 (b) ₹10000 (c) ₹10500 (d) ₹12000
Answer: (b)
Solution: P = 12100 / (1.1)² = 12100 / 1.21. The trap is ₹11000, found by simply subtracting 10% of 12100 twice or dividing by 1.1 only once. The correct principal is ₹10000.
Source: NCERT Class 8 Mathematics

6. The compound interest on ₹8000 at 15% per annum for 1 year, compounded half-yearly, is:
(a) ₹1200 (b) ₹1260 (c) ₹1300 (d) ₹1245
Answer: (d)
Solution: Half-yearly compounding gives rate 7.5% per period over 2 periods: Amount = 8000 × (1.075)² = 8000 × 1.155625 = ₹9245. The trap is ₹1200, which uses annual compounding (8000 × 15%). The compound interest is ₹1245.

7. At what rate percent per annum will ₹20000 amount to ₹22050 in 2 years, compounded annually?
(a) 5% (b) 4% (c) 6% (d) 10%
Answer: (a)
Solution: 22050/20000 = 1.1025 = (1 + R/100)², and √1.1025 = 1.05. The trap is 10%, obtained by dividing the total interest ₹2050 by 20000 over 2 years as if it were simple interest. The rate is 5% per annum.
Source: PYQ-style

8. The present population of a town is 25000. It grows at 4% per annum. What will be the population after 2 years?
(a) 26000 (b) 27000 (c) 27040 (d) 28000
Answer: (c)
Solution: Population = 25000 × (1.04)² = 25000 × 1.0816. The trap is 27000, which adds a flat 4% of 25000 twice (simple growth of 2000). The population after 2 years is 27040.

9. A machine depreciates at 10% per annum. Its present value is ₹81000. What will be its value after 2 years?
(a) ₹64800 (b) ₹64000 (c) ₹72900 (d) ₹65610
Answer: (d)
Solution: Value = 81000 × (0.9)² = 81000 × 0.81. The trap is ₹64800, which subtracts 10% of the original value twice (81000 − 16200), ignoring that the second year's depreciation is on the reduced value. The value after 2 years is ₹65610.

10. What is the compound interest on ₹12000 at 5% per annum for 2 years?
(a) ₹1200 (b) ₹1260 (c) ₹1300 (d) ₹1230
Answer: (d)
Solution: Amount = 12000 × (1.05)² = 12000 × 1.1025 = ₹13230. The trap is ₹1200, the simple interest for 2 years; compounding adds 12000 × (0.05)² = ₹30 extra. The compound interest is ₹1230.

11. The difference between compound interest and simple interest on ₹1500 at 10% per annum for 3 years is:
(a) ₹46.50 (b) ₹45 (c) ₹48 (d) ₹50
Answer: (a)
Solution: For 3 years, CI − SI = P(R/100)²(3 + R/100) = 1500 × 0.01 × 3.1. The trap is ₹45, which uses only P(R/100)² × 3 and forgets the extra R/100 term in the 3-year formula. The difference is ₹46.50.
Source: PYQ-style

12. ₹5000 becomes ₹5618 in 2 years at compound interest. The rate per annum is:
(a) 5% (b) 6% (c) 8% (d) 7%
Answer: (b)
Solution: 5618/5000 = 1.1236, and √1.1236 = 1.06. The trap is 5%, which gives only (1.05)² = 1.1025, i.e. ₹5512.50, less than ₹5618. The rate is 6% per annum.
Source: PYQ-style

13. A sum of money doubles itself at compound interest in 10 years. In how many years will it become 8 times?
(a) 20 years (b) 40 years (c) 30 years (d) 80 years
Answer: (c)
Solution: Doubling in 10 years means the factor 2 is gained every 10 years; 8 = 2³ needs 3 such periods. The trap is 80 years, from wrongly multiplying 8 × 10 as in simple interest. The required time is 30 years.
Source: PYQ-style

14. Compound interest on ₹6250 at 8% per annum for 1 year, compounded half-yearly, is:
(a) ₹500 (b) ₹510 (c) ₹520 (d) ₹540
Answer: (b)
Solution: Half-yearly rate is 4% over 2 periods: Amount = 6250 × (1.04)² = 6250 × 1.0816 = ₹6760. The trap is ₹500, the simple interest at 8% for 1 year, which ignores the second half-year's interest on interest. The compound interest is ₹510.

15. What is the effective annual rate of interest corresponding to a nominal rate of 10% per annum compounded half-yearly?
(a) 10.75% (b) 10% (c) 10.5% (d) 10.25%
Answer: (d)
Solution: Effective rate = (1 + 5/100)² − 1 = 1.1025 − 1 = 0.1025. The trap is 10%, the nominal rate itself, which ignores the effect of compounding twice a year. The effective annual rate is 10.25%.

16. The simple interest on a sum for 2 years at 12% per annum is ₹720. The compound interest on the same sum for 2 years at the same rate is:
(a) ₹720 (b) ₹780 (c) ₹763.20 (d) ₹800
Answer: (c)
Solution: P = 720 × 100/(12 × 2) = ₹3000; CI = 3000 × (1.12)² − 3000 = 3763.20 − 3000. The trap is ₹720, assuming CI equals SI; the excess is P(R/100)² = 3000 × 0.0144 = ₹43.20. The compound interest is ₹763.20.
Source: PYQ-style

17. A certain sum amounts to ₹8820 in 2 years at 5% per annum compound interest. The sum is:

Answer: (a)
Solution: P = 8820 / (1.05)² = 8820 / 1.1025. The trap is ₹8400, from dividing 8820 by 1.05 only once. The correct sum is ₹8000.

Solution: Quarterly rate is 5% over 8 periods: Amount = 4000 × (1.05)⁸ = 4000 × 1.477455 = ₹5909.82. The trap is ₹1920, from annual compounding (4000 × 1.44 − 4000), which undercounts because quarterly compounding is more frequent. The compound interest is ₹1909.82.

(a) 300 cm (b) 280 cm (c) 250 cm (d) 240 cm
Answer: (c)
Solution: Initial height = 360 / (1.2)² = 360 / 1.44. The trap is 300 cm, from wrongly subtracting 20% of 360 twice or dividing by 1.2 only once. The height 2 years ago was 250 cm.

21. A sum of ₹8000 is invested at compound interest for 3 years. The rates for the first, second and third years are 5%, 10% and 20% respectively. Find the amount after 3 years.
(a) ₹11520 (b) ₹10800 (c) ₹12000 (d) ₹11088
Answer: (d)
Solution: Amount = 8000 × 1.05 × 1.10 × 1.20 = 8000 × 1.386. The trap is ₹10800, from adding the rates (35%) as simple interest on 8000 for the period. The amount after 3 years is ₹11088.

22. The compound interest on a sum for 2 years at 20% per annum is ₹2640. The simple interest on the same sum for 2 years at the same rate is:
(a) ₹2000 (b) ₹2600 (c) ₹2800 (d) ₹2400
Answer: (d)
Solution: CI = P × (1.44 − 1) = 0.44P = 2640, so P = ₹6000; SI = 6000 × 0.20 × 2. The trap is ₹2600, from guessing the principal as ₹6500 or misusing the ratio. The simple interest is ₹2400.
Source: PYQ-style

23. A certain sum of money at compound interest becomes ₹20000 in 2 years and ₹22000 in 3 years. Find the rate of interest per annum.
(a) 5% (b) 10% (c) 8% (d) 12%
Answer: (b)
Solution: The third year's growth factor is 22000/20000 = 1.10, so 1 + R/100 = 1.10. The trap is 5%, from dividing the ₹2000 increase by 20000 and then halving it as if over 2 years. The rate is 10% per annum.
Source: PYQ-style

24. A sum of ₹5000 is borrowed at 10% per annum compound interest, compounded annually. It is repaid in two equal annual installments. Find the amount of each installment (approximately).
(a) ₹2881 (b) ₹3000 (c) ₹2750 (d) ₹2500
Answer: (a)
Solution: 5000 = x/1.1 + x/1.21 = x × 1.73554, giving x ≈ 2880.95. The trap is ₹2750, from simply adding one year's interest (₹500) and halving ₹5500, which ignores the present-value discounting of installments. Each installment is approximately ₹2881.
Source: PYQ-style

25. The population of a town increases by 10% annually. If the present population is 66550, what was it 3 years ago?
(a) 55000 (b) 50000 (c) 60000 (d) 45000
Answer: (b)
Solution: Population 3 years ago = 66550 / (1.1)³ = 66550 / 1.331. The trap is 55000, from dividing by 1.21 (only 2 years of growth) instead of 1.331. The population 3 years ago was 50000.
