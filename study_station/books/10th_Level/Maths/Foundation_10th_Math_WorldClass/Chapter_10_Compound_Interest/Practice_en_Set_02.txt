26. The difference between compound interest and simple interest on a sum of ₹2500 at 12% p.a. for 2 years is:
(a) ₹24 (b) ₹30 (c) ₹48 (d) ₹36
Answer: (d)
Solution: The tempting option ₹30 comes from wrongly using P × R/100 × 2 × something small; the correct shortcut for 2 years is CI − SI = P(R/100)² = 2500 × (0.12)² = 2500 × 0.0144, so the answer is ₹36.
Source: NCERT Class 8 Mathematics

27. A sum of money amounts to ₹5832 in 2 years and to ₹6298.56 in 3 years at compound interest. The rate percent per annum is:
(a) 6% (b) 7% (c) 8% (d) 10%
Answer: (c)
Solution: The ratio of successive year amounts gives the multiplying factor: 6298.56/5832 = 1.08, so 1 + R/100 = 1.08. Choosing 10% is a trap from rounding 6298.56 carelessly; the exact ratio gives R = 8%.

28. The compound interest on ₹16,000 for 9 months at 20% p.a. compounded quarterly is:
(a) ₹2522 (b) ₹2500 (c) ₹2600 (d) ₹2400
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is ₹2400, which is simple interest (16000 × 20% × 3/4). With quarterly compounding, rate per quarter = 5% and periods = 3, so A = 16000 × (1.05)³ = ₹18,522 and CI = 18522 − 16000, so the answer is ₹2522.

29. A person invested ₹12,000 at compound interest. After 2 years, the amount becomes ₹14,520. Find the rate of interest.
(a) 5% (b) 8% (c) 10% (d) 12%
Answer: (c)
Solution: 14520/12000 = 1.21 = (1 + R/100)², so 1 + R/100 = √1.21 = 1.1. The trap is dividing the total interest 2520 by 12000 and 2 to get 10.5% or guessing 12%; the square root gives exactly R = 10%.

31. The simple interest on a sum for 3 years at 10% p.a. is ₹3000. Find the compound interest on the same sum for 3 years at the same rate.
(a) ₹3110 (b) ₹3200 (c) ₹3310 (d) ₹3300
Answer: (c)
Solution: From SI, P = (3000 × 100)/(10 × 3) = ₹10,000. The trap is ₹3300, which just adds a rough 10% to SI; the exact CI = 10000 × (1.1)³ − 10000 = 13310 − 10000, so the answer is ₹3310.

32. A sum becomes 3 times itself in 6 years at compound interest. In how many years will it become 9 times?
(a) 9 years (b) 18 years (c) 15 years (d) 12 years
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is 18 years, from assuming tripling the multiple triples the time. Since 9 = 3², the growth factor must be applied twice, so time = 2 × 6, and the answer is 12 years.

34. The difference between CI and SI on a sum for 3 years at 20% p.a. is ₹1280. The sum is:
(a) ₹18,000 (b) ₹12,000 (c) ₹15,000 (d) ₹10,000
Answer: (d)
Solution: For 3 years, CI − SI = P(R/100)²(3 + R/100) = P × 0.04 × 3.2 = 0.128P. The trap is using the 2-year formula P(R/100)², which gives a wrong, larger sum; solving 0.128P = 1280 gives P, so the answer is ₹10,000.
Source: PYQ-style

Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is ₹11200, which is annual compounding. Half-yearly means rate 6% per period and 2 periods: A = 10000 × (1.06)² = 10000 × 1.1236, so the answer is ₹11,236.

36. What is the effective annual rate of interest corresponding to 8% per annum compounded quarterly?
(a) 8% (b) 8.24% (c) 8.16% (d) 8.30%
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is 8.16%, from (1.02)² thinking only two periods. Effective rate = (1 + 8/400)⁴ − 1 = (1.02)⁴ − 1 = 1.08243216 − 1 ≈ 0.0824, so the answer is 8.24%.

37. A sum of money is borrowed at 10% p.a. compound interest. If the total interest for 2 years is ₹1050, find the sum.
(a) ₹4000 (b) ₹4500 (c) ₹5000 (d) ₹5500
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is ₹5250, from treating ₹1050 as simple interest (1050 = P × 0.20). For CI, interest = P[(1.1)² − 1] = 0.21P = 1050, so P = 1050/0.21, and the answer is ₹5000.

38. The CI on a certain sum for 2 years at 5% p.a. is ₹410. The SI on the same sum for the same time at the same rate is:
(a) ₹400 (b) ₹380 (c) ₹420 (d) ₹390
Answer: (a)
Solution: CI = P[(1.05)² − 1] = 0.1025P = 410, so P = ₹4000. The trap is assuming SI and CI are nearly equal and picking ₹390 or ₹420; SI = 4000 × 5% × 2, so the answer is ₹400.

41. A certain sum is invested at compound interest for 2 years at 10% p.a. If the interest is compounded half-yearly, the amount becomes ₹12,155. Find the sum.

44. An amount of ₹8000 is borrowed at 12% p.a. compound interest and is to be repaid in two equal annual installments. The approximate value of each installment is:

49. The present value of a machine is ₹1,00,000. It depreciates at the rate of 15% per annum. What was its value 2 years ago? (approx.)

50. A sum of ₹10,000 is invested at compound interest. After 2 years, the amount is ₹12,100. If the same sum is invested at the same rate but compounded half-yearly, what will be the amount after 2 years?
(a) ₹12,250 (b) ₹12,200 (c) ₹12,314 (d) ₹12,155
Answer: (d)
Solution: From 12100 = 10000(1 + R/100)², R = 10%. The trap is assuming half-yearly compounding doubles the benefit and picking ₹12,314; actually A = 10000 × (1.05)⁴ = 10000 × 1.21550625 ≈ 12155, so the answer is ₹12,155.
