126. A sum of money amounts to ₹4,200 in 2 years and ₹4,410 in 3 years at compound interest, compounded annually. Find the rate of interest and the sum.
(a) 5%, ₹3,809.52 (b) 10%, ₹3,471.07 (c) 5%, ₹3,800 (d) 10%, ₹3,500
Answer: (a)
Solution: The ratio of the 3-year amount to the 2-year amount is 4410/4200 = 1.05, so the rate is 5%, not 10% — the tempting 10% comes from wrongly doubling 5%. Principal = 4200/(1.05)² = 4200/1.1025, so the answer is 5% and ₹3,809.52.
Source: PYQ-style

127. The compound interest on a certain sum for 2 years at 20% p.a. is ₹1,320. Find the simple interest on the same sum for the same period at the same rate.
(a) ₹1,200 (b) ₹1,100 (c) ₹1,000 (d) ₹1,250
Answer: (a)
Solution: CI = P(1.2² − 1) = 0.44P = 1320, so P = 3000; the trap is treating 1320 as simple interest and halving it. SI = 3000 × 20% × 2, so the answer is ₹1,200.
Source: PYQ-style

128. The difference between compound interest and simple interest on a certain sum at 10% p.a. for 3 years is ₹310. Find the sum.
(a) ₹8,000 (b) ₹15,000 (c) ₹12,000 (d) ₹10,000
Answer: (d)
Solution: For 3 years, CI − SI = P(R/100)²(3 + R/100) = P × 0.01 × 3.1 = 0.031P = 310. Using the 2-year formula P(R/100)² gives the wrong ₹31,000-type result, so apply the 3-year formula: P = 310/0.031, so the answer is ₹10,000.
Source: PYQ-style

129. A sum of ₹1,00,000 is invested at compound interest. The rate for the first year is 10% p.a. compounded annually, for the second year 20% p.a. compounded half-yearly, and for the third year 15% p.a. compounded quarterly. Find the amount after 3 years (approx).
(a) ₹1,52,000 (b) ₹1,56,500 (c) ₹1,54,200 (d) ₹1,55,000
Answer: (c)
Solution: Year 1: 1,00,000 × 1.10 = 1,10,000. Year 2: 10% per half-year, factor 1.1² = 1.21, giving 1,33,100. Year 3: 3.75% per quarter, factor (1.0375)⁴ ≈ 1.15865; the trap is compounding the third year annually at 15%. So 1,33,100 × 1.15865 ≈ ₹1,54,200.
Source: PYQ-style

130. A sum of ₹1,20,000 is borrowed at 12% p.a. compound interest, compounded annually. It is to be repaid in three equal annual installments. What is each installment (approx)?
(a) ₹48,000 (b) ₹50,200 (c) ₹49,960 (d) ₹51,000
Answer: (c)
Solution: If each installment is x, then x/1.12 + x/1.12² + x/1.12³ = 1,20,000, i.e. 2.401831x = 1,20,000. Simply dividing 1,20,000 by 3 gives ₹40,000, which ignores interest entirely. So x ≈ 1,20,000/2.401831, and the answer is ₹49,960.
Source: PYQ-style

131. The value of a machine depreciates at 15% p.a. for the first 2 years and then at 20% p.a. for the next 2 years. If its present value is ₹3,46,800, what was its value 4 years ago?
(a) ₹7,50,000 (b) ₹7,00,000 (c) ₹8,00,000 (d) ₹6,50,000
Answer: (a)
Solution: Present value = Original × (0.85)² × (0.80)² = Original × 0.4624. The trap is applying a flat 15% or 20% for all 4 years. Original = 3,46,800/0.4624, so the answer is ₹7,50,000.
Source: PYQ-style

132. What is the effective annual rate of interest corresponding to a nominal rate of 20% p.a. compounded quarterly?
(a) 21% (b) 21.55% (c) 20.5% (d) 22%
Answer: (b)
Solution: Effective rate = (1 + 20/400)⁴ − 1 = (1.05)⁴ − 1 = 1.21550625 − 1. The trap is assuming quarterly compounding adds exactly 1% over the nominal rate. So the effective rate is 0.21550625, and the answer is 21.55%.
Source: NCERT Class 8 Mathematics

133. The compound interest on a certain sum for 2 years is ₹210 and for 3 years is ₹331. Find the sum and the rate percent per annum.
(a) ₹1,200, 10% (b) ₹1,000, 10% (c) ₹1,000, 5% (d) ₹1,500, 10%
Answer: (b)
Solution: Interest in the third year alone = 331 − 210 = 121, earned on the amount after 2 years. If P = 1000 at 10%, amount after 2 years = 1210 and 10% of 1210 = 121, which matches; the 5% option fails this check. So the answer is ₹1,000 at 10%.
Source: PYQ-style

134. A sum of ₹2,00,000 is invested at compound interest, with rates 5%, 10% and 20% for the first, second and third years respectively. Find the difference between this compound interest and the simple interest on the same sum at 10% p.a. for 3 years.
(a) ₹15,000 (b) ₹20,000 (c) ₹17,200 (d) ₹18,000
Answer: (c)
Solution: CI amount = 2,00,000 × 1.05 × 1.10 × 1.20 = 2,77,200, so CI = 77,200; SI = 2,00,000 × 10% × 3 = 60,000. The trap is averaging the rates to 11.67% and compounding that. Difference = 77,200 − 60,000, so the answer is ₹17,200.
Source: PYQ-style

135. A sum of ₹32,000 is invested at 25% p.a. compound interest, compounded half-yearly. What is the interest earned in the fifth half-year?
(a) ₹6,400 (b) ₹6,407 (c) ₹7,200 (d) ₹6,250
Answer: (b)
Solution: Half-yearly rate = 12.5%; amount after 4 half-years = 32000 × (1.125)⁴ = 32000 × 6561/4096 = 51,257.81. The trap is computing 12.5% of the original ₹32,000, which gives ₹4,000-type thinking, or using the amount after 5 half-years. Interest in the fifth half-year = 51,257.81 × 0.125, so the answer is ₹6,407.
Source: PYQ-style

136. The simple interest on a certain sum for 2 years at 12% p.a. is ₹1,200. What is the compound interest on the same sum for 2 years at the same rate, compounded half-yearly?
(a) ₹1,248 (b) ₹1,262 (c) ₹1,350 (d) ₹1,312
Answer: (d)
Solution: P = 1200/(0.12 × 2) = 5000. Half-yearly rate 6%, 4 periods: amount = 5000 × (1.06)⁴ ≈ 6312.38. The trap is compounding annually, which gives CI = 1272. So CI ≈ 6312 − 5000, and the answer is ₹1,312.
Source: PYQ-style

137. A sum of ₹1,00,000 is invested at 5% p.a. compound interest, compounded half-yearly. What is the difference between the compound interest and simple interest after 1.5 years?
(a) ₹190 (b) ₹200 (c) ₹189 (d) ₹180
Answer: (c)
Solution: SI = 1,00,000 × 5% × 1.5 = 7,500. CI: rate 2.5% per half-year, 3 periods, amount = 1,00,000 × (1.025)³ = 1,07,689.06, so CI = 7,689.06. The trap is rounding CI to 7,700 and reporting 200. Difference = 7,689.06 − 7,500, so the answer is ₹189.
Source: PYQ-style

138. The population of a town increases by 10% in the first year, decreases by 5% in the second year and increases by 20% in the third year. If the population after three years is 1,25,400, what was the population three years ago?
(a) 1,10,000 (b) 95,000 (c) 1,05,000 (d) 1,00,000
Answer: (d)
Solution: Net factor = 1.10 × 0.95 × 1.20 = 1.254. The trap is adding the percentages (10 − 5 + 20 = 25%) and dividing by 1.25, which gives 1,00,320 — close but wrong method. Population = 1,25,400/1.254, so the answer is 1,00,000.
Source: PYQ-style

139. A sum of ₹8,000 amounts to ₹9,261 in 3 years at compound interest, compounded annually. If the rate of interest had been 2% more, what would have been the amount?
(a) ₹10,000 (b) ₹9,500 (c) ₹9,800 (d) ₹9,900
Answer: (c)
Solution: 9261/8000 = 1.157625 = (1.05)³, so the original rate is 5% and the new rate is 7%. The trap is adding 2% of the principal to ₹9,261. New amount = 8000 × (1.07)³ = 8000 × 1.225043, so the answer is ₹9,800.
Source: PYQ-style

140. The difference between compound interest and simple interest on a sum at 20% p.a. for 3 years is ₹1,600. Find the sum if interest is compounded annually.
(a) ₹12,000 (b) ₹13,000 (c) ₹12,500 (d) ₹14,000
Answer: (c)
Solution: CI − SI for 3 years = P(0.2)²(3 + 0.2) = 0.128P = 1600. Using the 2-year formula 0.04P = 1600 wrongly gives ₹40,000-type results. P = 1600/0.128, so the answer is ₹12,500.
Source: PYQ-style

141. A loan of ₹1,00,000 is to be repaid in two equal half-yearly installments at 20% p.a. compound interest, compounded half-yearly. What is each installment (approx)?
(a) ₹57,600 (b) ₹60,000 (c) ₹55,000 (d) ₹52,500
Answer: (a)
Solution: Half-yearly rate is 10%; if each installment is x, then x/1.1 + x/1.1² = 1,00,000, i.e. 1.73554x = 1,00,000. The trap is using 20% per half-year or simply halving the loan to ₹50,000. So x ≈ 57,619, and the answer is ₹57,600.

142. The value of a machine depreciates at 20% p.a. in the first year, 25% p.a. in the second year and 50% p.a. in the third year. If its present value is ₹15,000, what was its value 3 years ago?
(a) ₹60,000 (b) ₹45,000 (c) ₹40,000 (d) ₹50,000
Answer: (d)
Solution: Present value = Original × 0.8 × 0.75 × 0.5 = Original × 0.3. The trap is averaging the depreciation rates. Original = 15,000/0.3, so the answer is ₹50,000.
Source: PYQ-style

143. A sum of money doubles itself in 5 years at compound interest, compounded annually. In how many years will it become 8 times itself at the same rate?
(a) 10 years (b) 15 years (c) 12 years (d) 20 years
Answer: (b)
Solution: Since 8 = 2³, the sum must double three times, needing 3 × 5 = 15 years. The trap is assuming simple proportionality (8 times = 4 times the period = 20 years), which holds for simple interest, not compound. So the answer is 15 years.
Source: NCERT Class 8 Mathematics

144. The compound interest on a sum for 2 years at 10% p.a. is ₹2,100. Find the simple interest on the same sum for 3 years at 15% p.a.
(a) ₹4,500 (b) ₹4,200 (c) ₹5,000 (d) ₹4,800
Answer: (a)
Solution: CI = P(1.1² − 1) = 0.21P = 2100, so P = 10,000. The trap is treating 2100 as simple interest and getting P = 10,500. SI = 10,000 × 15% × 3, so the answer is ₹4,500.
Source: PYQ-style

145. The difference between compound interest and simple interest on a sum for 2 years at 20% p.a. (compounded annually) is ₹50. What will be the difference if the interest is compounded half-yearly?
(a) ₹82 (b) ₹80 (c) ₹85 (d) ₹75
Answer: (b)
Solution: Annual case: P(0.2)² = 0.04P = 50, so P = 1250. Half-yearly: rate 10%, 4 periods, amount = 1250 × (1.1)⁴ = 1830.125, CI = 580.125; SI = 1250 × 0.4 = 500. The trap is assuming the difference stays ₹50. Difference = 580.125 − 500, so the answer is ₹80.
Source: PYQ-style

146. A sum of money becomes ₹6,050 in 2 years and ₹6,655 in 3 years at compound interest, compounded annually. Find the rate of interest.
(a) 5% (b) 15% (c) 12% (d) 10%
Answer: (d)
Solution: The one-year growth factor is 6655/6050 = 1.1, so 1 + R/100 = 1.1. The trap is computing (6655 − 6050)/6050 = 10% but then misreading it as the two-year rate. So the answer is 10%.
Source: PYQ-style

147. A sum of ₹50,000 is borrowed at 12% p.a. compound interest, compounded annually. The first equal installment is paid at the end of the second year and the second at the end of the third year. Find each installment (approx).
(a) ₹33,135 (b) ₹31,200 (c) ₹33,100 (d) ₹32,500
Answer: (a)
Solution: If each installment is x, then x/(1.12)² + x/(1.12)³ = 50,000, i.e. x(0.797194 + 0.711780) = 1.508974x = 50,000. The trap is discounting both installments from year 1. So x ≈ 50,000/1.508974, and the answer is ₹33,135.
Source: PYQ-style

148. A man invests ₹10,000 at 10% p.a. compound interest. At the end of each year he withdraws ₹2,000. What will be the amount left after 3 withdrawals?
(a) ₹7,000 (b) ₹7,200 (c) ₹6,500 (d) ₹6,690
Answer: (d)
Solution: Year 1: 10,000 × 1.1 − 2000 = 9,000; Year 2: 9,000 × 1.1 − 2000 = 7,900; Year 3: 7,900 × 1.1 − 2000 = 6,690. The trap is subtracting ₹6,000 from the 3-year compound amount 13,310 to get ₹7,310-type results. So the answer is ₹6,690.
Source: PYQ-style

149. The population of a town is 2,50,000. It increases by 5% in the first year and 10% in the second year, then 20,000 people migrate out at the end of the second year, and it increases by 20% in the third year. Find the population after three years.
(a) 3,22,500 (b) 3,30,000 (c) 3,15,000 (d) 3,20,000
Answer: (a)
Solution: After year 1: 2,50,000 × 1.05 = 2,62,500; after year 2: × 1.10 = 2,88,750; after migration: 2,68,750; after year 3: 2,68,750 × 1.20. The trap is applying the 20% growth before subtracting the migrants. So the answer is 3,22,500.

150. A sum of ₹1,00,000 is invested at 10% p.a. compounded half-yearly. After 1.5 years, the rate changes to 20% p.a. compounded quarterly. What is the total amount after 3 years from the start (approx)?
(a) ₹1,58,000 (b) ₹1,55,000 (c) ₹1,52,800 (d) ₹1,60,200
Answer: (b)
Solution: First 1.5 years: 3 half-years at 5%, amount = 1,00,000 × (1.05)³ = 1,15,762.5. Next 1.5 years: 6 quarters at 5%, factor (1.05)⁶ ≈ 1.34010. The trap is applying 20% per quarter instead of 5%. Amount ≈ 1,15,762.5 × 1.34010 ≈ 1,55,132, so the answer is ₹1,55,000.
Source: PYQ-style
