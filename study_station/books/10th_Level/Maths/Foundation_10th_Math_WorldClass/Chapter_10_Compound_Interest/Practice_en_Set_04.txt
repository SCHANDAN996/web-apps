76. A sum of money becomes ₹2880 in 2 years and ₹3456 in 3 years at compound interest. Find the rate percent per annum.
(a) 20% (b) 18% (c) 25% (d) 15%
Answer: (a)
Solution: The ratio of amounts in successive years gives the yearly growth factor: 3456 ÷ 2880 = 1.2, so 1 + R/100 = 1.2. The trap is treating ₹576 as simple interest on ₹2880 and misreading the period; here the one-year factor directly gives the rate, so the answer is 20%.
Source: PYQ-style

77. The difference between compound interest and simple interest on ₹8000 at 15% p.a. for 2 years is:
(a) ₹120 (b) ₹150 (c) ₹180 (d) ₹240
Answer: (c)
Solution: For 2 years, CI − SI = P × (R/100)² = 8000 × 0.0225 = 180. The tempting wrong value ₹240 comes from wrongly using P × R/100 × 2 style thinking on the difference, so the answer is ₹180.
Source: PYQ-style

78. What is the compound interest on ₹20,000 at 10% p.a. for 1 year 6 months, compounded half-yearly?
(a) ₹3,152.50 (b) ₹3,100 (c) ₹3,200 (d) ₹3,050
Answer: (a)
Solution: Half-yearly compounding gives rate 5% per period and 3 periods: A = 20000 × (1.05)³ = 23152.50, so CI = 3152.50. The trap is using 10% for 1.5 years directly, which ignores half-yearly conversion, so the answer is ₹3,152.50.
Source: NCERT Class 8 Mathematics

79. A tree increases in height by 10% each year. If its present height is 13.31 m, what was its height 3 years ago?
(a) 11 m (b) 10 m (c) 9 m (d) 12 m
Answer: (b)
Solution: Past height = 13.31 ÷ (1.1)³ = 13.31 ÷ 1.331 = 10. The trap is subtracting 30% of 13.31 instead of dividing by the cube of the growth factor, so the answer is 10 m.
Source: NCERT Class 8 Mathematics

80. A sum of money doubles itself in 6 years at compound interest. In how many years will it become 16 times?
(a) 24 years (b) 18 years (c) 30 years (d) 36 years
Answer: (a)
Solution: 16 times means 2⁴, i.e. four doublings, so time = 4 × 6 = 24 years. The trap is computing 16 × 6 ÷ 4 or simply scaling 6 by 16, so the answer is 24 years.
Source: PYQ-style

81. The simple interest on a sum for 2 years at 20% p.a. is ₹1,600. The compound interest on the same sum for 2 years at the same rate is:
(a) ₹1,840 (b) ₹1,800 (c) ₹1,760 (d) ₹1,900
Answer: (c)
Solution: SI = 0.4P = 1600 gives P = 4000; CI = 4000 × (1.2)² − 4000 = 5760 − 4000 = 1760. The trap is assuming CI equals SI at the same rate, so the answer is ₹1,760.
Source: PYQ-style

82. Find the amount on ₹10,000 after 2 years if the rate of interest is 5% for the first year and 10% for the second year, compounded annually.
(a) ₹11,450 (b) ₹11,500 (c) ₹11,550 (d) ₹11,600
Answer: (c)
Solution: A = 10000 × 1.05 × 1.10 = 11550. The trap is averaging the rates to 7.5% and compounding, which gives a wrong figure, so the answer is ₹11,550.
Source: PYQ-style

83. The effective annual rate of interest corresponding to a nominal rate of 12% p.a. compounded quarterly is:
(a) 12.36% (b) 12.55% (c) 12.48% (d) 12.60%
Answer: (b)
Solution: Effective rate = (1.03)⁴ − 1 ≈ 1.125509 − 1 = 0.1255, i.e. 12.55%. The trap is taking 12% + 4 × 0.03 style approximations or just quoting 12%, so the answer is 12.55%.
Source: PYQ-style

84. A sum of ₹6400 amounts to ₹8100 in 2 years at compound interest. Find the rate percent.
(a) 12.5% (b) 20% (c) 10% (d) 25%
Answer: (a)
Solution: (1 + R/100)² = 8100/6400 = 81/64, so 1 + R/100 = 9/8 = 1.125. The trap is dividing the total interest 1700 by 6400 over 2 years as if it were simple interest, so the answer is 12.5%.
Source: PYQ-style

85. A person invests ₹25,000 at 8% p.a. compound interest for 2 years. Calculate the compound interest if the interest is compounded half-yearly.
(a) ₹4,246.40 (b) ₹4,121.60 (c) ₹4,080 (d) ₹4,320
Answer: (a)
Solution: Rate per half-year = 4%, periods = 4: A = 25000 × (1.04)⁴ ≈ 29246.46, so CI ≈ 4246.46 ≈ 4246.40. The tempting ₹4,160-type annual-compounding result (here ₹4,160 is not listed, but ₹4,121.60 comes from a miscount of periods) is the trap, so the answer is ₹4,246.40.
Source: NCERT Class 8 Mathematics

86. The difference between CI and SI on a sum of ₹12,000 at 10% p.a. for 3 years is:
(a) ₹372 (b) ₹360 (c) ₹384 (d) ₹400
Answer: (a)
Solution: For 3 years, CI − SI = P × (R/100)² × (3 + R/100) = 12000 × 0.01 × 3.1 = 372. The trap is applying the 2-year formula P(R/100)², which gives only 120, so the answer is ₹372.
Source: PYQ-style

87. A sum becomes ₹12,100 in 2 years and ₹13,310 in 3 years at compound interest. Find the principal.
(a) ₹8,000 (b) ₹10,000 (c) ₹9,000 (d) ₹11,000
Answer: (b)
Solution: Growth factor = 13310 ÷ 12100 = 1.1, so R = 10%; then P = 12100 ÷ (1.1)² = 12100 ÷ 1.21 = 10000. The trap is treating the yearly increase ₹1210 as simple interest on the principal, so the answer is ₹10,000.
Source: PYQ-style

88. The population of a town increases by 5% in the first year and by 10% in the second year. If the present population is 23,100, what was it 2 years ago?
(a) 18,000 (b) 19,000 (c) 20,000 (d) 21,000
Answer: (c)
Solution: Past population = 23100 ÷ (1.05 × 1.10) = 23100 ÷ 1.155 = 20000. The trap is reducing 23100 by 15% directly instead of dividing by the combined factor, so the answer is 20,000.
Source: PYQ-style

89. A machine depreciates at the rate of 20% per annum. Its present value is ₹1,20,000. What will be its value after 2 years?
(a) ₹78,000 (b) ₹76,800 (c) ₹80,000 (d) ₹72,000
Answer: (b)
Solution: Value = 120000 × (0.8)² = 120000 × 0.64 = 76800. The trap is deducting 40% of the original value (giving 72000), which ignores that the second year's depreciation is on the reduced value, so the answer is ₹76,800.
Source: NCERT Class 8 Mathematics

90. At what rate percent per annum will a sum of ₹2,000 amount to ₹2,315.25 in 3 years, compound interest being compounded annually?
(a) 4% (b) 5% (c) 7% (d) 6%
Answer: (b)
Solution: 2315.25 ÷ 2000 = 1.157625 = (1.05)³, so R = 5%. The trap is dividing total interest 315.25 by 2000 over 3 years as simple interest, which gives about 5.25% and misleads, so the answer is 5%.
Source: NCERT Class 8 Mathematics

91. The simple interest on a sum for 3 years at 8% p.a. is ₹1,200. Find the compound interest on the same sum for 3 years at the same rate.
(a) ₹1,298.56 (b) ₹1,300 (c) ₹1,312.24 (d) ₹1,320
Answer: (a)
Solution: P = (1200 × 100) ÷ (8 × 3) = 5000; CI = 5000 × (1.08)³ − 5000 = 6298.56 − 5000 = 1298.56. The trap is assuming CI equals SI for the same rate and period, so the answer is ₹1,298.56.
Source: PYQ-style

92. A sum of ₹10,000 is invested at 10% p.a. compound interest, compounded half-yearly. What is the amount after 1 year?
(a) ₹11,000 (b) ₹11,100 (c) ₹11,050 (d) ₹11,025
Answer: (d)
Solution: Half-yearly rate = 5%, periods = 2: A = 10000 × (1.05)² = 11025. The trap is using annual compounding, which gives ₹11,000, so the answer is ₹11,025.
Source: NCERT Class 8 Mathematics

93. The difference between CI and SI on a sum for 2 years at 12% p.a. is ₹144. Find the sum.
(a) ₹8,000 (b) ₹12,000 (c) ₹14,000 (d) ₹10,000
Answer: (d)
Solution: CI − SI = P × (0.12)² = 0.0144P = 144, so P = 144 ÷ 0.0144 = 10000. The trap is dividing 144 by 0.12 instead of 0.0144, which gives 1200 and then gets mis-scaled, so the answer is ₹10,000.
Source: PYQ-style

94. A sum of ₹5,000 is lent at compound interest for 2 years. The rates for the first and second year are 10% and 20% respectively. Find the amount.
(a) ₹6,400 (b) ₹6,500 (c) ₹6,600 (d) ₹6,700
Answer: (c)
Solution: A = 5000 × 1.10 × 1.20 = 5000 × 1.32 = 6600. The trap is using an average rate of 15% for both years, which gives 6612.50-type confusion, so the answer is ₹6,600.
Source: PYQ-style

95. In how many years will ₹8,000 amount to ₹9,261 at 5% p.a. compound interest?
(a) 2 years (b) 4 years (c) 3 years (d) 5 years
Answer: (c)
Solution: 9261 ÷ 8000 = 1.157625 = (1.05)³, so the time is 3 years. The trap is dividing the interest 1261 by 400 (one year's interest) and rounding, which misleads toward 3.15 years, so the answer is 3 years.
Source: NCERT Class 8 Mathematics

96. A sum of money becomes 8 times itself in 18 years at compound interest. In how many years will it double?
(a) 15 years (b) 9 years (c) 12 years (d) 6 years
Answer: (d)
Solution: 8 times = 2³, so three doublings take 18 years and one doubling takes 18 ÷ 3 = 6 years. The trap is halving 18 to get 9, which confuses 8 times with 2 times, so the answer is 6 years.
Source: PYQ-style

97. A sum of ₹5,000 is invested at 10% p.a. compound interest for 2 years. The amount is then reinvested at 20% p.a. compound interest for 1 year. Find the final amount.
(a) ₹7,000 (b) ₹8,000 (c) ₹7,500 (d) ₹7,260
Answer: (d)
Solution: After 2 years: 5000 × (1.1)² = 6050; then 6050 × 1.2 = 7260. The trap is applying 20% to the original ₹5,000 for the last year, which gives 7050-type errors, so the answer is ₹7,260.
Source: PYQ-style

98. The compound interest on a sum for 2 years at 4% p.a. is ₹408. The simple interest on the same sum for 2 years at the same rate is:
(a) ₹390 (b) ₹380 (c) ₹420 (d) ₹400
Answer: (d)
Solution: CI = P × [(1.04)² − 1] = 0.0816P = 408 gives P = 5000; SI = 5000 × 4 × 2 ÷ 100 = 400. The trap is assuming SI equals CI, i.e. ₹408, so the answer is ₹400.
Source: PYQ-style

99. A sum of ₹2,000 is invested at compound interest at 5% p.a. for 3 years. Find the interest earned in the third year alone.
(a) ₹102.50 (b) ₹110.25 (c) ₹115.76 (d) ₹105.00
Answer: (b)
Solution: Amount after 2 years = 2000 × (1.05)² = 2205; third-year interest = 2205 × 0.05 = 110.25. The trap is taking 5% of the original ₹2,000 (₹100) or of the first-year amount (₹105), so the answer is ₹110.25.
Source: PYQ-style

100. The effective rate of interest for 20% p.a. compounded half-yearly is:
(a) 20% (b) 20.5% (c) 21.5% (d) 21%
Answer: (d)
Solution: Effective rate = (1.10)² − 1 = 0.21 = 21%. The trap is quoting the nominal 20% itself, ignoring the extra half-yearly compounding, so the answer is 21%.
Source: NCERT Class 8 Mathematics
