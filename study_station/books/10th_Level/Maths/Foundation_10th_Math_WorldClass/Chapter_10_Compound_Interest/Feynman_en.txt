You have two magic piggy banks. In one, a lazy elf adds ₹10 every New Year. In the other, a clever goblin multiplies whatever is inside by 1.1 each New Year. Both start with ₹100. After 10 years, the lazy elf has added 10 times ₹10, so you have ₹200. The goblin? After year 1: ₹110. Year 2: ₹121. Year 3: ₹133.10. Year 10: wait, let me use a trick. At 10%, doubling time is about 7.2 years, so in 10 years it doubles once and a bit — it becomes around ₹259. That extra ₹59 appears because the goblin earns interest on the interest. That's compound interest, and the hardest thing to really feel in your bones is that the growth speeds up all by itself, like a snowball rolling downhill.

Why is that so hard? Because our brains are wired for straight lines — the elf's ₹10 per year feels fair. But the goblin's bank curves upward, slowly at first, then exploding. Let me tell you a story.

Aryan and Zara are both 12 years old. Aryan puts ₹100 into a goblin bank paying 10% compound interest. Zara thinks, "I'll wait till I'm 22; ₹100 now won't matter." At 22, she puts in ₹100 as well. They both leave the money untouched until age 60. Aryan's money sits for 48 years; Zara's for 38 years. Here's the mind-bender: at 60, Aryan's ₹100 has doubled 48/7.2 ≈ 6.67 times, so it's around ₹100 × 2^6.67 ≈ ₹100 × 100 ≈ ₹10,000. Zara's ₹100 doubles 38/7.2 ≈ 5.28 times, about ₹100 × 40 ≈ ₹4,000. Just waiting 10 years cost Zara more than half the final amount! The hardest truth is: time is the most powerful ingredient.

The unforgettable trick: The Rule of 72. It's a secret decoder ring. Divide 72 by your interest rate, and you get the number of years it takes for your money to double. 10%? 72/10 = 7.2 years. 8%? 72/8 = 9 years. 6%? 72/6 = 12 years. Why 72? It's a handy approximation, and 72 is a nice number divisible by tons of small numbers (1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 8, 9, 12…). When you meet a rate, just ask: "How many times would 72 split into you?"

Now here's a question for you. Suppose you pop ₹500 into a goblin bank that pays 9% compound interest. How many years will it take to become ₹2,000? (Hint: How many doublings from 500 to 2000? And how long does each doubling take with the Rule of 72?)
