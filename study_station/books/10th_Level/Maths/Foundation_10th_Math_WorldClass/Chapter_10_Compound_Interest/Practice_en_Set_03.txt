Practice Set 3: Compound Interest — Questions 51–75 (All Medium difficulty)

51. What is the compound interest on ₹15,000 at 12% p.a. for 1 year, compounded quarterly?
(a) ₹1875.45 (b) ₹1890.00 (c) ₹1800.50 (d) ₹1882.63
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is using 12% directly for one year, which gives only ₹1800. With quarterly compounding, rate per quarter = 3% and periods = 4, so A = 15000 × (1.03)⁴ ≈ 16882.63, and CI = 16882.63 − 15000, so the answer is ₹1882.63.
Source: NCERT Class 8 Mathematics

52. The difference between compound interest and simple interest on a sum of ₹5000 at 10% p.a. for 3 years is:
(a) ₹150 (b) ₹165 (c) ₹155 (d) ₹175
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is applying the 2-year formula P(R/100)², which wrongly gives ₹50-type thinking, or just taking 3 × 50 = ₹150. For 3 years, CI − SI = P(R/100)²(3 + R/100) = 5000 × 0.01 × 3.1, so the answer is ₹155.
Source: PYQ-style

53. A sum of money amounts to ₹10,816 in 2 years and ₹11,248.64 in 3 years at compound interest. Find the rate percent per annum.
(a) 4% (b) 6% (c) 5% (d) 8%
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is computing interest on the original sum instead of on the 2-year amount. The ratio 11248.64/10816 = 1.04 equals 1 + R/100, so the answer is 4%.
Source: PYQ-style

54. A person borrows ₹6000 at 15% p.a. compound interest and repays it in three equal annual installments. What is the approximate value of each installment?
(a) ₹2700 (b) ₹2628 (c) ₹2800 (d) ₹2500
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is simply dividing 6000 by 3 and adding interest flat, which gives a round figure like ₹2700. Using present values, 6000 = x(1/1.15 + 1/1.15² + 1/1.15³) = x × 2.283225, so x ≈ 6000/2.283225, and the answer is ₹2628.
Source: PYQ-style

55. The population of a city is 2,00,000. It increases by 10% in the first year and decreases by 10% in the second year. What is the net change after 2 years?
(a) No change (b) 1% decrease (c) 1% increase (d) 2% decrease
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is assuming +10% and −10% cancel out to no change; the decrease acts on a larger base. Net change = 10 − 10 + (10 × −10)/100 = −1%, so the answer is 1% decrease.
Source: NCERT Class 8 Mathematics

56. A sum invested at compound interest becomes ₹13,230 in 2 years and ₹13,891.50 in 3 years. Find the sum.
(a) ₹12,000 (b) ₹11,000 (c) ₹10,000 (d) ₹11,500
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is treating ₹13,230 as the principal. The one-year interest is 13891.50 − 13230 = 661.50 on 13230, giving rate 5%; then sum = 13230/(1.05)² = 13230/1.1025, so the answer is ₹12,000.
Source: PYQ-style

57. Find the compound interest on ₹3200 at 25% p.a. for 2 years, compounded half-yearly.
(a) ₹1920 (b) ₹2100 (c) ₹2000 (d) ₹1925.78
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is using 25% for 2 years annually, which gives CI = 3200 × (1.5625 − 1) = ₹1800, or mis-halving the time. Half-yearly rate = 12.5%, periods = 4; A = 3200 × (1.125)⁴ = 3200 × 1.60180664 ≈ 5125.78, so the answer is ₹1925.78.
Source: PYQ-style

58. The simple interest on a certain sum for 2 years at 6% p.a. is ₹600. What will be the compound interest on the same sum for 2 years at the same rate?
(a) ₹612 (b) ₹618 (c) ₹624 (d) ₹600
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is thinking CI equals SI for 2 years, i.e., ₹600; CI exceeds SI by P(R/100)². Here P = 60000/12 = 5000, CI = 5000 × 1.1236 − 5000, so the answer is ₹618.
Source: NCERT Class 8 Mathematics

59. A sum of ₹8000 is invested at compound interest. The rate is 10% p.a. for the first year and 12% p.a. for the second year. Find the amount after 2 years.
(a) ₹9800 (b) ₹9856 (c) ₹9920 (d) ₹10000
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is averaging the rates to 11% and compounding, which gives 8000 × 1.2321 ≈ 9857, close but wrong in method, or adding simple interests to get ₹9760. Amount = 8000 × 1.10 × 1.12 = 8000 × 1.232, so the answer is ₹9856.

60. At what rate percent per annum will ₹5000 amount to ₹5618 in 2 years, compounded annually?
(a) 5% (b) 7% (c) 8% (d) 6%
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is dividing total interest ₹618 by 2 years to get 6.18% simple-style and picking 7%. Here 5618/5000 = 1.1236 = (1 + R/100)², and √1.1236 = 1.06, so the answer is 6%.

61. The effective annual rate of interest corresponding to a nominal rate of 10% p.a. compounded semi-annually is:
(a) 10% (b) 10.75% (c) 10.50% (d) 10.25%
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is assuming effective rate equals the nominal 10%. Effective rate = (1 + 5/100)² − 1 = 1.1025 − 1 = 0.1025, so the answer is 10.25%.

62. A sum of money becomes 4 times itself in 20 years at compound interest. In how many years will it become 16 times?
(a) 30 years (b) 50 years (c) 40 years (d) 60 years
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is scaling linearly: 4 times in 20 years, so 16 times in 80 years, or halving wrongly. Since 4 = 2², doubling time is 10 years; 16 = 2⁴ needs 4 × 10, so the answer is 40 years.
Source: PYQ-style

63. What sum will yield a compound interest of ₹420 in 2 years at 10% p.a.?
(a) ₹2050 (b) ₹1950 (c) ₹2100 (d) ₹2000
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is using the simple interest formula: 420 = P × 10 × 2/100 gives ₹2100. For CI, 0.21P = 420 since (1.1)² − 1 = 0.21, so P = 420/0.21, and the answer is ₹2000.
Source: NCERT Class 8 Mathematics

64. A chemical sample loses 5% of its mass every year due to decay. If the present mass is 800 grams, what was its mass 2 years ago? (approximate to nearest whole number)
(a) 886 g (b) 884 g (c) 880 g (d) 900 g
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is adding back 10% of 800, i.e., 880 g; decay percentages compound on changing bases. Mass 2 years ago = 800/(0.95)² = 800/0.9025 ≈ 886.42, so the answer is 886 g.
Source: NCERT Class 8 Mathematics

65. The difference between CI and SI on ₹16,000 for 2 years at 10% p.a. is:
(a) ₹150 (b) ₹160 (c) ₹180 (d) ₹200
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is computing full CI − SI by long calculation and slipping, or using P(R/100) = ₹1600. For 2 years the difference is P(R/100)² = 16000 × 0.01, so the answer is ₹160.

66. A sum of ₹6000 becomes ₹7260 in 2 years at compound interest. What will it become in 4 years at the same rate?
(a) ₹8800 (b) ₹8784.60 (c) ₹9000 (d) ₹8600
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is doubling the 2-year interest: 6000 + 2 × 1260 = ₹8520-type thinking, or doubling the amount. Since 7260/6000 = 1.21, rate is 10%; amount in 4 years = 6000 × (1.1)⁴ = 6000 × 1.4641, so the answer is ₹8784.60.

67. The compound interest on a sum for 3 years at 5% p.a. is ₹1576.25. Find the sum.
(a) ₹10000 (b) ₹8000 (c) ₹9000 (d) ₹11000
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is using simple interest: P = 157625/15 ≈ ₹10508, close to ₹11000 and misleading. For CI, P × [(1.05)³ − 1] = 0.157625P = 1576.25, so P = 1576.25/0.157625, and the answer is ₹10000.

68. A bank offers 12% p.a. compound interest. How much will a deposit of ₹20000 grow to in 2 years if interest is compounded quarterly?
(a) ₹25335.40 (b) ₹25400 (c) ₹25000 (d) ₹26000
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is compounding annually at 12% for 2 years, giving 20000 × 1.2544 = ₹25088, near ₹25000. Quarterly: rate 3%, periods 8; A = 20000 × (1.03)⁸ ≈ 20000 × 1.26677, so the answer is ₹25335.40.

69. A sum of money placed at compound interest doubles itself in 8 years. In how many years will it amount to 8 times itself?
(a) 24 years (b) 16 years (c) 32 years (d) 40 years
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is linear scaling: 2 times in 8 years so 8 times in 32 years. Since 8 = 2³, it needs 3 doubling periods of 8 years each, so the answer is 24 years.

70. The ratio of the amount for 3 years to the amount for 2 years at compound interest on a certain sum is 11:10. The rate percent is:
(a) 20% (b) 5% (c) 10% (d) 15%
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is taking 11 − 10 = 1 and guessing 5%, or doubling to 20%. The ratio A₃/A₂ = 11/10 = 1.1 = 1 + R/100, so the answer is 10%.

75. A machine depreciates at 20% per annum. If its present value is ₹1,28,000, what was its value 2 years ago?
(a) ₹1,80,000 (b) ₹2,50,000 (c) ₹2,00,000 (d) ₹1,60,000
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is adding back 40% of 128000, giving about ₹1,80,000; depreciation compounds on reducing value. Original value = 128000/(0.8)² = 128000/0.64, so the answer is ₹2,00,000.
