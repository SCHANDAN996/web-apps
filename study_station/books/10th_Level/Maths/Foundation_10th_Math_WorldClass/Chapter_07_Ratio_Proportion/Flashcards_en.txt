Flashcards: Ratio & Proportion
Level: Foundation
Language: English

Card 1

Front: What is the one golden rule you must never forget before writing any ratio?

Back: Convert both quantities to the same unit first. A ratio of 2 meters to 50 cm is NOT 2:50. It's 200:50, which simplifies to 4:1.

Card 2

Front: Is a ratio just another fraction? What makes it different?

Back: A ratio is a comparison of two quantities of the same kind — and it has no unit. A fraction can compare different kinds (like km/hour), but a ratio is a pure, naked number. 3:4 means "3 is to 4," nothing else.

Card 3

Front: 24:36:60 looks scary. What single weapon slashes it into simplicity?

Back: The HCF (Highest Common Factor) — which is 12. Divide every term by 12. You get the baby ratio 2:3:5. Always hunt for a common factor.

Card 4

Front: If a:b :: c:d, what is the one equation that unlocks the entire proportion?

Back: Product of Extremes = Product of Means. That is, a × d = b × c. If any one term is missing, plug the others into this equation and solve.

Card 5

Front: In a:b :: c:d, which two are the "extremes" and which two are the "means"?

Back: Extremes are the outer terms: a and d. Means are the inner terms: b and c. The proportion equality says their cross-products are identical.

Card 6

Front: Recite the weird mnemonic that helps you remember the Extremes and Means rule. What's the full story?

Back: "Extreme weather equals Mean comfort."
Imagine a freezing storm outside (Extremes) and a cozy, warm room inside (Means). The cold outside must equal the warmth inside. So: Product of Extremes = Product of Means. a × d = b × c.

Card 7

Front: "More workers finish a job in less time." Is this Direct or Inverse Proportion? How do you identify it instantly?

Back: It's Inverse Proportion. Ask yourself: "If one goes up, does the other go down?" Yes. In Inverse, the product (x × y) stays constant. More workers means fewer days — the product (Workers × Days) always equals the total work.

Card 8

Front: "More books cost more money." Is this Direct or Inverse Proportion? What stays constant here?

Back: It's Direct Proportion. Both move in the same direction. The ratio (Cost / Books) stays constant. If books double, cost doubles.

Card 9

Front: What is the foolproof 3-step "Total Parts Method" to divide ₹1200 between A and B in the ratio 3:5?

Back:

Add the parts: 3 + 5 = 8 total parts.

Find 1 part's value: 1200 / 8 = ₹150.

Multiply: A gets 3 × 150 = ₹450; B gets 5 × 150 = ₹750.
Think of it as packing the whole amount into 8 equal "boxes."

Card 10

Front: The "boxing model" is a mental trick for ratio distribution. What does the denominator physically represent?

Back: The denominator is the total number of equal "boxes" or parts into which the whole thing is broken. Each person simply gets the number of boxes the ratio tells them.

Card 11

Front: A mixture has milk and water in ratio 2:1. Total mixture is 60 liters. How much pure milk is present, and what's the mental shortcut?

Back: Total parts = 2 + 1 = 3. Milk's share = 2 out of 3 parts. Shortcut: (Milk's parts / Total parts) × Total quantity = (2/3) × 60 = 40 liters. No formula, just the boxing model applied instantly.

Card 12

Front: When combining A:B = 2:3 and B:C = 4:5 into A:B:C, what is the one trick that makes it effortless?

Back: Make the common term (B) equal. Find the LCM of the two B values (3 and 4), which is 12. Multiply the first ratio by 4 → 8:12. Multiply the second by 3 → 12:15. Now simply read them together: A:B:C = 8:12:15.

Card 13

Front: How do you instantly check if 3:5 and 9:15 are in proportion without complex calculation?

Back: Simplify 9:15. Divide both by their HCF (3). You get 3:5. Since both ratios simplify to the same thing, they are in proportion. Alternatively, cross-multiply: 3×15 = 45 and 5×9 = 45. Equal.

Card 14

Front: A map scale is 1:50,000. What does this ratio mean in plain English?

Back: It means 1 unit of anything on the map represents 50,000 of the same unit on the ground. 1 cm on map = 50,000 cm (or 500 meters) in reality. Ratios are scale-invariant — as long as units match.

Card 15

Front: What common trap catches students when comparing 50 paise and ₹2? How do you escape it?

Back: The trap is mixing units. Never write 50:2. Escape by converting both to the same unit — either both to paise (50 and 200) giving 1:4, or both to rupees (0.5 and 2) giving 1:4. Same-unit first, always.

Card 16

Front: The "Copycat Arrow" mnemonic is used for what kind of proportion problem? Describe the trick in one sentence.

Back: It's for solving a missing term in a proportion (a:b :: c:d). The trick: Ask "What was the move (multiply or divide) from a to c?" Then apply the exact same move to b to find d. Like a mirror dancer who copies every step.

Card 17

Front: A:B = 5:6 and B:C = 3:4. What is A:C?

Back: A:C is simply the combined effect. One method: imagine B as 6 and 3. Make them equal by taking LCM = 6. Then A:B = 5:6, B:C = 6:8. So A:C = 5:8. The bridge term B disappears.

Card 18

Front: If x:y = 3:4, is x necessarily 3 and y necessarily 4? Why or why not?

Back: No, absolutely not. The ratio 3:4 means x and y are in the simplest form relationship. They could be 6 and 8, 9 and 12, 300 and 400 — any multiple. The ratio only fixes the relative size, not the absolute values.
