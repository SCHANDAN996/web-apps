1. The Angry Gatekeeper of BODMAS
Imagine a huge, red-faced gatekeeper at the entrance of a math palace. His name is Bhola Of Delhi Mandir. He shouts: “Brackets kholo! Of lo! Divide karo! Multiply karo! Add karo! Subtract karo!” But whenever you reach Division and Multiplication together, he screams, “Left se Right chalo, nahi to bhool jaoge!” Same for Addition and Subtraction. The absurd shouting gatekeeper image anchors the entire BODMAS sequence and the left-to-right rule.

2. The Cannibal’s Dinner Table (Brackets Order)
Picture a cannibal eating his meal. He first eats the smallest dish ( ), then the medium dish { }, and finally the largest dish [ ]. The rule is: eat from the inside out. Innermost bracket always goes first, just like a hungry cannibal who won’t touch the big pot before finishing the small plate. You’ll never forget that () must be solved before {} and {} before [].

3. The “Of” Monster with Invisible Hands
Visualise a monster named “Of” that hides behind numbers. Whenever you see a phrase like “3 of 4”, the monster grabs those two numbers and wraps them in an invisible bracket, making them untouchable by division or multiplication until you solve it. Picture the monster roaring, “I am stronger than × and ÷!” This trick reminds you that “of” has higher priority than multiplication/division and must be solved immediately after brackets.

4. Left-Right Conveyor Belt (DM and AS Rule)
Imagine a factory conveyor belt carrying numbers and operators. When equal-strength operators (÷ and ×, or + and –) come in a row, the belt moves only from left to right. You can’t pick an item from the right side until it reaches the front. So for 18 ÷ 3 × 2, the belt brings ÷ first, then ×. You process in order. This vivid image kills the habit of blindly multiplying before dividing.

5. The Bhola-of-Delhi-Mandir Acronym Song
Sing this to the tune of a popular local rhyme:
B for Brackets, peel them like a banana,
O for Of, the hidden bracket planner,
D for Divide, M for Multiply, left to right they dance,
A for Add, S for Subtract, they never miss their chance.
The rhyme cements the sequence and the equal-priority left-to-right rule in a musical memory.

6. The Fraction-Decimal Marriage (Convert All to Fractions)
Imagine a wedding between a fraction boy and a decimal girl. The priest says, “For a happy life, always convert decimals to fractions first!” Visualise the decimal 0.5 taking off his disguise to reveal his true form 1/2. Then the couple lives happily as fractions. Whenever you see a mix of fractions and decimals, remember the wedding and convert all to fractions for a smooth calculation.

7. The Three-Headed Percentage Beast
Meet a three-headed dragon. Each head represents a common percentage: 50% (½), 33⅓% (⅓), and 12.5% (⅛). When you see these percentages, imagine the dragon breathing fire and shouting, “I am a fraction in disguise!” This helps you instantly convert percentages to fractions without pen and paper, speeding up simplification.

8. The Invisible Bracket Ghost around “Of”
Close your eyes and picture a ghost named “Of” who wears a bracket-shaped hat. Wherever “of” appears in an expression, the ghost drops an invisible pair of brackets around the two numbers. So 4 ÷ 2 of 2 becomes 4 ÷ [2 of 2] in your mind. The ghost’s whisper: “Solve me first, or I’ll haunt your answer!” This trick stops the common mistake of treating “of” as ordinary multiplication.

9. The BODMAS Family Tree (Loci Method)
Place each BODMAS family member in a room of your house:

Entrance: Brackets – open the door first.

Drawing room: Of/Orders – sit and talk.

Kitchen: Division & Multiplication – cook together, left to right.

Garden: Addition & Subtraction – stroll left to right.
As you mentally walk through your house, you recall the order without effort. The absurd idea of a family doing math around the house makes it stick.

10. The Bursting Firecracker (Surds and Powers First)
Imagine a firecracker with two parts: the root (√) and the power (²,³). Before you can light it, you must handle each part separately. √16 + 3² means you crack the root (4) and pop the power (9) first, then add them. If you light the whole expression at once, the firecracker fizzles. This reminds you that roots and powers are solved individually right after brackets and “of”.

11. The Twin Rule of DM and AS (Left-Right Handshake)
Visualise two pairs of twins: Divvy & Multi, and Addy & Subby. They always hold hands and walk in the order they are standing. The one on the left walks first. So if Divvy is standing to the left of Multi, Divvy goes first. If Addy is to the left of Subby, Addy goes first. The twin handshake image stops you from breaking the left-to-right sequence.

12. The Brackets Family (Eldest to Youngest)
Picture a family with three members: Child ( ), Mother { }, and Father [ ]. The rule is: the youngest (child) must finish eating before the mother can start, and the mother before the father. So solve ( ) first, then { }, then [ ]. The family pecking order ensures you never mess up the bracket order.

13. The Zero-Term Bomb Detector
In a long simplification, imagine you’re a bomb disposal expert. If you spot (a – a) inside any bracket, it’s a zero bomb – it destroys the entire multiplication chain it touches. You shout, “Zero detected! No need to calculate further!” This trick saves time and prevents unnecessary computations when a bracket evaluates to zero.

14. The “DM Left-Right” Arrow Tattoo
Imagine you have a glowing arrow tattoo on your forearm that always points from left to right. Whenever you see ÷ and × together (or + and – together), you glance at your tattoo and slide your finger along the arrow, solving as you move. The physical act of sliding your finger reinforces the left-to-right rule and prevents the common “multiply first” blunder.

15. The “Of is Boss” Office Hierarchy
In an office, the boss “Of” sits in a cabin. Division and Multiplication are employees. When an expression arrives, the boss “Of” demands, “I’ll take these two numbers first. You wait outside.” Only after the boss finishes does he let the employees in, from left to right. This hierarchy picture permanently fixes the priority of “of” over division and multiplication.
