The Great Math Race: Why Left-to-Right Is the Secret Rule

Imagine a relay race with four friends: Addy, Subby, Multi, and Divvy. Their rule: when two of them are in the same lane, they must run in the order they are standing — left to right. One day, Divvy and Multi are standing together. Divvy is on the left, Multi on the right. The coach blows the whistle. Multi shouts, "Me first! I'm more important!" But the judge says, "No, you are twin powers. When you're together, you run in the order you appear, left to right."

So 18 ÷ 3 × 2 means Divvy runs first (18 ÷ 3 = 6), then Multi takes the baton (6 × 2 = 12). If Multi had jumped ahead, 3 × 2 = 6, then Divvy would be left with 18 ÷ 6 = 3 — a completely different race, and the wrong winner.

This same rule applies to Addy and Subby. In 30 – 5 + 2, Subby is on the left, so he goes first: 30 – 5 = 25, then Addy runs: 25 + 2 = 27. If you let Addy go first (5 + 2 = 7) then 30 – 7 = 23, it's cheating.

The Never-Forget Trick: Draw a horizontal arrow over the expression. Whenever you see Division and Multiplication together, or Addition and Subtraction together, slide your finger along the arrow from left to right, doing each as you meet it. Think of it as a math conveyor belt — you can't grab an item from the right side first.

Story check: Once, a girl named Anju had to split 24 marbles among 4 boxes, then each box's marbles were multiplied into 3 shiny stones. She did 24 ÷ 4 × 3. Her friend said, "Multiply first! 4 × 3 = 12, then 24 ÷ 12 = 2 stones." Anju remembered the left-to-right relay, did 24 ÷ 4 = 6, then 6 × 3 = 18 stones. She got 18 beautiful stones; her friend got only 2. The rule saved her treasure.
