🔝 Top 10 High-Yield PYQs (2016–2025)
Q1. SSC CGL 2021 Tier-I (20 Aug, Shift 2)

Question: Simplify: 15 ÷ 3 of 2 × 4 + 9 ÷ 18 of 2 × 3 – 4 ÷ 8 × 2

Options: (a) 37/4 (b) 39/4 (c) 35/4 (d) 41/4

Solution:

Step 1: "of" first → 3 of 2 = 6, 18 of 2 = 36

Step 2: 15 ÷ 6 × 4 → (15/6) × 4 = (5/2) × 4 = 10

Step 3: 9 ÷ 36 × 3 → (1/4) × 3 = 3/4

Step 4: 4 ÷ 8 × 2 → (1/2) × 2 = 1

Step 5: 10 + 3/4 – 1 = 9 + 3/4 = 39/4

Answer: (b) 39/4 -
6

🧠 Psychological Trap — "The of Illusion": Your brain sees ÷ and × and instinctively goes left to right, treating "of" as just another multiplication sign. But "of" creates an invisible bracket. Students who skip "of" priority get 15 ÷ 3 × 2 × 4 = 40, then wonder why they failed. The exam doesn't test your calculation speed — it tests whether you'll spot the disguised bracket.

Q2. SSC CGL 2020 Tier-I (9 March, Shift 3)

Question: The value of (26 – 13 × 2) ÷ 2 + 1 is:

Options: (a) 0 (b) 1 (c) 2 (d) 3

Solution:

Step 1: Bracket first — inside: 13 × 2 = 26

Step 2: 26 – 26 = 0

Step 3: 0 ÷ 2 + 1 → 0 + 1 = 1

Answer: (b) 1 -
3

🧠 Psychological Trap — "The Zero Blindspot": After solving the bracket to zero, many students freeze. Their brain screams, "Zero divided by anything is zero!" and they write 0 + 1 = 0 (yes, really — some actually skip the +1 entirely). Others rush: 26 – 13 = 13, × 2 = 26, ÷ 2 = 13, + 1 = 14. The bracket is invisible in their mental model because they're reading left to right like a sentence.

Q3. SSC CHSL 2021 (6 August, Shift 3)

Question: Simplify: (3 2/3 – 5 7/9 ÷ 1/3 × 1 2/13) / (2/3 of 1 1/3 ÷ 1/3)

Options: (a) 2 (b) –2 (c) 1 (d) –1

Solution:

Numerator: 5 7/9 ÷ 1/3 × 1 2/13

52/9 × 3/1 × 15/13 = (52×3×15)/(9×1×13) = 2340/117 = 20

3 2/3 – 20 = 11/3 – 60/3 = –49/3

Denominator: 2/3 of 4/3 ÷ 1/3

2/3 × 4/3 = 8/9

8/9 × 3/1 = 8/3

Result: (–49/3) ÷ (8/3) = –49/8

Answer: Not listed among given options (requires verification against actual exam). The trap is the layered BODMAS within a fraction format. -
54

🧠 Psychological Trap — "Fraction Overload": Mixed fractions, "of", division, and a fraction-of-fractions structure create cognitive overload. The brain wants to simplify from left to right but the fraction bar acts as an invisible bracket around the entire numerator and denominator. Students solve numerator and denominator in parallel, mixing operations, and the minus sign gets lost in the chaos.

Q4. SSC CGL 2023 Tier-I (24 July, Shift 3)

Question: Simplify: 15 – (–5) × {4 – (7 – 3)} ÷ [3 × {5 + (–3) × (–6)}]

Solution:

Step 1: Innermost (7 – 3) = 4

Step 2: {4 – 4} = 0

Step 3: {5 + (–3) × (–6)} → 5 + 18 = 23

Step 4: [3 × 23] = 69

Step 5: 15 – (–5) × 0 ÷ 69 = 15 – 0 = 15

Answer: 15 -

🧠 Psychological Trap — "The Zero-Term Annihilator": When {4 – 4} produces zero, the entire multiplication chain becomes zero. Students who miss this continue grinding through [3 × 23] = 69 and then do (–5) × 0 ÷ 69, which is still zero — but the extra work costs 40 seconds. The exam rewards those who spot zero-producing terms early. Always scan nested brackets for identical subtraction before computing.

Q5. IBPS RRB PO (Previous Year)

Question: What will come in place of ?: (7294 – 3241 + 716) – (3267 + 2425 – 961) = ?

Options: (a) 38 (b) 42 (c) 45 (d) 50 (e) 56

Solution:

First bracket: 7294 – 3241 = 4053, then 4053 + 716 = 4769

Second bracket: 3267 + 2425 = 5692, then 5692 – 961 = 4731

Result: 4769 – 4731 = 38

Answer: (a) 38 -
71

🧠 Psychological Trap — "Sign Reversal on the Second Bracket": The minus sign before the second bracket applies to everything inside. Students often compute 3267 + 2425 – 961 = 4731 correctly, then write 4769 – 4731 = 38. But the real trap is when they do 3267 + 2425 = 5692 and then 5692 – 961 — the –961 inside the second bracket means subtracting a negative, effectively adding. This double-negative confusion is a classic banking exam pattern.

Q6. RRB NTPC (Previous Year)

Question: 3245 – 123 – 456 – 347 + x = 3650. Find x.

Options: (a) 2134 (b) 1331 (c) 1567 (d) 1467

Solution:

3245 – 123 = 3122

3122 – 456 = 2666

2666 – 347 = 2319

2319 + x = 3650

x = 3650 – 2319 = 1331

Answer: (b) 1331 -
21

🧠 Psychological Trap — "The Running Subtraction Cascade": Multiple subtractions in a row. The brain wants to club them: "123 + 456 + 347 = 926, then 3245 – 926 = 2319." This works for pure subtraction chains but becomes dangerous when addition is mixed in. Students who club everything get 3245 – 926 + x = 3650, then 2319 + x = 3650, x = 1331. That's correct here, but if the signs were mixed differently, clubbing would fail. The safe habit: always go left to right.

Q7. SSC CHSL 2021 (6 August, Shift 1)

Question: Simplify: 3/5 + 2 2/5 ÷ [(7/8 – 3/4) × 4 – 6 2/3 ÷ 4/3] × 1/3

Solution:

Innermost: 7/8 – 3/4 = 7/8 – 6/8 = 1/8

1/8 × 4 = 1/2

6 2/3 ÷ 4/3 = 20/3 × 3/4 = 5

Bracket: 1/2 – 5 = –9/2

2 2/5 ÷ (–9/2) = 12/5 × (–2/9) = –24/45 = –8/15

–8/15 × 1/3 = –8/45

Final: 3/5 + (–8/45) = 27/45 – 8/45 = 19/45

Answer: 19/45 -
51

🧠 Psychological Trap — "The Double Bracket Blindspot": The expression has an outer fraction structure, an inner square bracket, parentheses, and mixed fractions all competing for attention. Students solve the inner bracket correctly but forget that ÷ [result] means the entire bracket result is a divisor. They compute the bracket and multiply instead of divide, or lose track of the negative sign when 1/2 – 5 = –9/2.

Q8. SSC CGL 2023 Tier-I (Memory-Based)

Question: Simplify: (52 – 1170 ÷ 26 + 13 × 2) / (2 + (1 1/8 of 2) – 1 1/4)

Options: (a) 13 (b) 11 (c) 9 (d) 15

Solution:

Numerator: 1170 ÷ 26 = 45, 13 × 2 = 26, 52 – 45 + 26 = 33

Denominator: 1 1/8 of 2 = 9/8 × 2 = 9/4, 2 + 9/4 – 5/4 = 2 + 4/4 = 2 + 1 = 3

Result: 33 ÷ 3 = 11

Answer: (b) 11 -
63

🧠 Psychological Trap — "The 'Of' Inside a Fraction": The denominator contains 1 1/8 of 2. Students in a hurry read this as 1 + 1/8 + 2 and add everything. The word "of" is small, easy to overlook, but it changes the entire denominator value. 2 + 9/4 – 5/4 = 3 vs 2 + 1.125 + 2 – 1.25 = 3.875 — completely different paths.

Q9. IBPS RRB PO (Previous Year)

Question: 125% of 444 + (5/8) of 808 – 100 + ? = 1195

Options: (a) 225 (b) 235 (c) 245 (d) 255 (e) 265

Solution:

125% of 444 = (125/100) × 444 = 555

(5/8) of 808 = 5 × 101 = 505

555 + 505 – 100 + ? = 1195

960 + ? = 1195

? = 235

Answer: (b) 235 -
71

🧠 Psychological Trap — "The Percentage-Plus-Fraction Combo": Two different "of" formats in one expression — percentage and fraction. Students comfortable with one format but shaky on the other waste time converting. 125% = 5/4, so 5/4 × 444 = 555 is faster than (125/100) × 444. The exam rewards those who know their percentage-to-fraction conversions cold.

Q10. RRB Group D (Previous Year)

Question: 4 + (1/6) [–10 × (25 – 13 – 3) ÷ (–5)]

Options: (a) 8 (b) 9 (c) 6 (d) 7

Solution:

Innermost: 25 – 13 – 3 = 9

–10 × 9 = –90

–90 ÷ (–5) = 18

(1/6) × 18 = 3

4 + 3 = 7

Answer: (d) 7 -
22

🧠 Psychological Trap — "The Negative ÷ Negative = Positive Reflex": Students often stumble on –90 ÷ (–5). The double negative should yield +18, but under pressure, many write –18. Combined with the fraction 1/6 × 18 = 3, the entire answer shifts from 7 to 4 + (–3) = 1 (not even an option — and that's when panic sets in). Train your brain: two negatives in division/multiplication always make a positive. Always.
