51. In a triangle, the measures of two angles are 65° and 45°. The measure of the third angle is:
(a) 65° (b) 75° (c) 70° (d) 80°
Answer: (c)
Solution: The sum of the interior angles of a triangle is 180°. Third angle = 180° – (65° + 45°) = 180° – 110° = 70°. The trap is to halve 110° and pick 65°, but the third angle is found by subtraction, so the answer is 70°.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Mathematics

52. A chord of a circle of radius 15 cm is at a distance of 9 cm from the centre. The length of the chord is:
(a) 24 cm (b) 18 cm (c) 12 cm (d) 30 cm
Answer: (a)
Solution: The perpendicular from the centre bisects the chord. Half chord = √(15² – 9²) = √(225 – 81) = √144 = 12 cm. The trap is to stop at 12 cm, which is only half the chord; the full chord is 2 × 12 = 24 cm.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Mathematics

53. The angles of a quadrilateral are in the ratio 2 : 3 : 5 : 8. The difference between the largest and the smallest angle is:
(a) 120° (b) 140° (c) 160° (d) 100°
Answer: (a)
Solution: Sum of angles of a quadrilateral = 360°, so 18x = 360° and x = 20°. Largest = 160°, smallest = 40°. The trap is to pick 160°, which is only the largest angle; the required difference is 160° – 40° = 120°.

54. A parallelogram has sides 8 cm and 6 cm. The distance between the longer sides is 4 cm. The distance between the shorter sides is:
(a) 3 cm (b) 16/3 cm (c) 5 cm (d) 6 cm
Answer: (b)
Solution: Area = base × height = 8 × 4 = 32 cm². Using the 6 cm side as base, 6 × h = 32, so h = 32/6 = 16/3 cm. The trap is to assume the two heights are equal and pick 4-like values, but the area stays fixed while the base changes, so the answer is 16/3 cm.

55. An isosceles right triangle has hypotenuse 10 cm. Its area is:
(a) 50 cm² (b) 25 cm² (c) 75 cm² (d) 100 cm²
Answer: (b)
Solution: If each equal side is a, then 2a² = 100, so a² = 50. Area = ½ × a × a = ½ × 50 = 25 cm². The trap is to take ½ × 10 × 10 = 50 cm², treating the hypotenuse as a leg, so the answer is 25 cm².

Answer: (a)
Solution: Since chord² = 98 = 7² + 7², the chord subtends 90° at the centre. Sector area = ¼ × (22/7) × 49 = 38.5 cm²; triangle area = ½ × 7 × 7 = 24.5 cm². The trap is to give the sector area itself; the segment = 38.5 – 24.5 = 14 cm².

57. A cylindrical pipe has outer radius 5 cm and inner radius 4 cm. The cross-sectional area of the metal in the pipe is (π = 22/7):
(a) 22/7 cm² (b) 154/7 cm² (c) 198/9 cm² (d) 198/7 cm²
Answer: (d)
Solution: Metal area = π(R² – r²) = π(25 – 16) = 9π = 9 × (22/7) = 198/7 cm². The trap is to compute π(R – r)² = π, i.e. 22/7 cm², but the area of a ring uses the difference of squares, so the answer is 198/7 cm².

Solution: πr² = 154 gives r² = 49, so r = 7 cm and circumference = 2 × (22/7) × 7 = 44 cm. Side of square = 44/4 = 11 cm. The trap is to take the side as 12 cm from a wrong circumference, but the correct area is 11² = 121 cm².

Solution: The distance between the centres is r, and the common chord is bisected perpendicularly by the line of centres. Half chord = √(r² – (r/2)²) = √(3r²/4) = r√3/2. The trap is to take the chord equal to the radius, but the full chord is r√3.

61. A rectangular sheet of metal 44 cm × 20 cm is rolled along its length to form a cylinder. The volume of the cylinder so formed is (π = 22/7):
(a) 4620 cm³ (b) 1540 cm³ (c) 6160 cm³ (d) 3080 cm³
Answer: (d)
Solution: Rolling along the length makes the circumference 44 cm, so 2πr = 44 and r = 7 cm; height = 20 cm. Volume = (22/7) × 7 × 7 × 20 = 3080 cm³. The trap is to roll along the width, which halves the volume to 1540 cm³, so the answer is 3080 cm³.

62. In triangle ABC, AD is the bisector of angle A meeting BC at D. If AB = 6 cm, AC = 8 cm and BC = 10 cm, then BD is:
(a) 4 cm (b) 30/7 cm (c) 6 cm (d) 5 cm
Answer: (b)
Solution: By the Angle Bisector Theorem, BD : DC = AB : AC = 3 : 4, so BD = (3/7) × 10 = 30/7 cm. The trap is to bisect BC and pick 5 cm, but the bisector divides BC in the ratio of the sides, so the answer is 30/7 cm.

63. The diagonal of a rectangular field is 60 m more than the shorter side, and the longer side is 30 m more than the shorter side. The length of the shorter side is:
(a) 90 m (b) 60 m (c) 120 m (d) 150 m
Answer: (a)
Solution: Let the shorter side be b; then b² + (b + 30)² = (b + 60)², which simplifies to b² – 60b – 2700 = 0, i.e. (b – 90)(b + 30) = 0. The trap is to pick 60 m from the diagonal condition alone, but solving gives b = 90 m.

Answer: (c)
Solution: Equating volumes, 3³ = 1.5³ + 2³ + x³, so 27 = 3.375 + 8 + x³, giving x³ = 15.625. The trap is to add radii instead of volumes, but x = ∛15.625 = 2.5 cm.

71. In a trapezium ABCD, AB is parallel to CD, AB = 12 cm, CD = 8 cm and the height is 5 cm. Its area is:
(a) 40 cm² (b) 60 cm² (c) 100 cm² (d) 50 cm²

Answer: (c)
Solution: The angle in a semicircle is 90°, so angle ACB = 90°. Then angle ABC = 180° – 90° – 35° = 55°. The trap is to assume the triangle is isosceles and pick 35° or 45°, but the correct angle is 55°.
