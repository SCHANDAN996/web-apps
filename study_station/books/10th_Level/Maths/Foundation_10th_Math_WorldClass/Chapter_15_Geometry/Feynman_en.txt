Imagine you're holding a slice of pizza — a perfect triangle. You're not hungry (okay, maybe a little), but you're holding it by the crust, and the pointy tip is dripping cheese. Now, I challenge you to prove something magical about that triangle, without any ruler or protractor. Ready?

Once, a curious kid named Leo was eating a triangular sandwich. His friend said, "All triangles have angles that add up to 180 degrees." Leo didn't believe it. "Every triangle? Even a huge one, even a skinny one? How can that be true?" So Leo did an experiment.

He took his sandwich (which was a perfect triangle), and carefully tore off the three corners — just the tips. He put them side by side on his plate, with their points all touching. What do you think happened? The three torn corners, each a wedge from a different angle, fit together perfectly to make a straight line! A straight line is exactly 180 degrees. The three angles, no matter what shape the triangle was, always hugged each other to form that straight line.

That's the trick: The Triangle Hug Trick.
Take any triangle made of paper. Tear off its three corners. Place them so the vertices (the pointy bits) meet, and the edges touch. They will always, always make a straight line. Like three best friends who, when they come together, form a perfectly straight chain.

Why? Think of walking around the triangle. Start at one corner, face the direction of one side, walk to the next corner. Turn through that corner's angle to face the next side. Walk to the next corner, turn through its angle. Walk back to the start, and turn through the last angle to face the original direction. You've turned a full spin — 360 degrees — but that's the exterior turns. The interior turns? A beautiful geometry magic ensures they always sum to 180°.

But Leo asked a deeper question: "What if I draw a triangle on a ball? Like the surface of an orange?" Try it: on a globe, you can draw a triangle with two points on the equator and the third at the North Pole. Each angle at the equator is 90°, and the angle at the North Pole could be, say, 90°. That triangle would have angles adding up to 270°! Whoa. So the "straight line hug" only works on flat paper. On a curved surface, triangles go crazy.

So here's your trick to never forget: "Flat triangle corners always hug into a straight line."
You can do it with any scrap of paper, right now. Tear, arrange, and see the 180° magic.
