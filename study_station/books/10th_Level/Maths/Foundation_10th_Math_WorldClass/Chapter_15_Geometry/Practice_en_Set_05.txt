101. The angles of a triangle are in the ratio 1 : 2 : 3. The smallest angle is:
(a) 20° (b) 30° (c) 40° (d) 50°
Answer: (b)
Solution: Let the angles be x, 2x and 3x. Then x + 2x + 3x = 180°, so 6x = 180° and x = 30°. The trap is picking 60°, which is the middle angle, not the smallest. So the answer is 30°.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Mathematics

102. A chord of a circle of radius 13 cm is at a distance of 5 cm from the centre. The length of the chord is:
(a) 12 cm (b) 20 cm (c) 24 cm (d) 26 cm
Answer: (c)
Solution: The perpendicular from the centre bisects the chord, so half the chord = √(13² – 5²) = √144 = 12 cm. The trap is stopping at 12 cm, which is only half the chord. Full chord = 2 × 12 = 24 cm, so the answer is 24 cm.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Mathematics

103. A parallelogram has adjacent sides 12 cm and 8 cm. The distance between the longer sides is 5 cm. The distance between the shorter sides is:
(a) 10 cm (b) 6.5 cm (c) 8 cm (d) 7.5 cm
Answer: (d)
Solution: Area = base × height = 12 × 5 = 60 cm². Taking 8 cm as base, height = 60/8 = 7.5 cm. The trap is assuming both heights are equal to 5 cm. So the answer is 7.5 cm.

Answer: (b)
Solution: (√3/4)a² = 36√3 gives a² = 144, so a = 12 cm. The trap is taking a² = 36, which ignores the division by 4. So the answer is 12 cm.

105. Two tangents PA and PB from point P to a circle with centre O are inclined at 80° to each other. The measure of ∠AOB is:
(a) 80° (b) 90° (c) 100° (d) 120°
Answer: (c)
Solution: The radius is perpendicular to the tangent, so ∠OAP = ∠OBP = 90°. In quadrilateral OAPB, ∠AOB = 360° – (90° + 90° + 80°) = 100°. The trap is answering 80°, the angle between the tangents itself. So the answer is 100°.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Mathematics

106. A chord of a circle of radius 14 cm subtends a right angle at the centre. The area of the minor segment is (π = 22/7):
(a) 98 cm² (b) 112 cm² (c) 154 cm² (d) 56 cm²
Answer: (d)
Solution: Sector area = (90/360) × (22/7) × 14² = 154 cm²; triangle area = ½ × 14 × 14 = 98 cm². Segment = 154 – 98 = 56 cm². The trap is reporting 154 cm², which is the sector, not the segment. So the answer is 56 cm².
Source: NCERT Class 10 Mathematics

Answer: (a)
Solution: Diagonal = a√3 = 12√3, so a = 12 cm and volume = 12³ = 1728 cm³. The trap is using the face diagonal formula a√2, which gives a wrong edge. So the answer is 1728 cm³.

108. A cylindrical pipe has outer diameter 10 cm, inner diameter 8 cm and length 14 cm. The volume of metal used is (π = 22/7):
(a) 396 cm³ (b) 400 cm³ (c) 560 cm³ (d) 616 cm³
Answer: (a)
Solution: Outer radius = 5 cm, inner radius = 4 cm. Volume = π(5² – 4²) × 14 = (22/7) × 9 × 14 = 396 cm³. The trap is using the diameters 10 and 8 directly as radii. So the answer is 396 cm³.

109. In ΔABC, AD is a median and G is the centroid. If AG = 6 cm, the length of AD is:
(a) 9 cm (b) 12 cm (c) 15 cm (d) 18 cm
Answer: (a)
Solution: The centroid divides the median in the ratio 2 : 1, so AG : GD = 2 : 1 and GD = 3 cm. Hence AD = 6 + 3 = 9 cm. The trap is doubling AG to get 12 cm, which reverses the ratio. So the answer is 9 cm.

110. The ratio of the areas of two similar triangles is 4 : 9. If a side of the smaller triangle is 6 cm, the corresponding side of the larger triangle is:
(a) 12 cm (b) 13.5 cm (c) 9 cm (d) 15 cm
Answer: (c)
Solution: The ratio of sides is the square root of the ratio of areas: √(4/9) = 2/3. Larger side = 6 × (3/2) = 9 cm. The trap is using the area ratio directly, giving 13.5 cm. So the answer is 9 cm.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Mathematics

111. A right circular cone has height 24 cm and slant height 25 cm. Its volume is (π = 22/7):
(a) 1800 cm³ (b) 1256 cm³ (c) 1232 cm³ (d) 2200 cm³
Answer: (c)
Solution: Radius = √(25² – 24²) = √49 = 7 cm. Volume = ⅓ × (22/7) × 7² × 24 = 1232 cm³. The trap is using 25 cm as the radius instead of computing it from the slant height. So the answer is 1232 cm³.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Mathematics

112. A solid sphere of radius 6 cm is melted and recast into a solid cylinder of base radius 3 cm. The height of the cylinder is:
(a) 16 cm (b) 24 cm (c) 48 cm (d) 32 cm
Answer: (d)
Solution: Volume of sphere = (4/3)π × 6³ = 288π cm³. Cylinder: π × 3² × h = 288π, so h = 32 cm. The trap is forgetting the factor 4/3 in the sphere's volume, which gives 24 cm. So the answer is 32 cm.

113. The difference between the interior and exterior angles of a regular polygon is 90°. The number of sides is:
(a) 6 (b) 8 (c) 10 (d) 12
Answer: (b)
Solution: Interior + exterior = 180° and interior – exterior = 90°, so exterior = 45°. Number of sides = 360/45 = 8. The trap is dividing 360 by the interior angle 135°, which does not give an integer count directly. So the answer is 8.
Source: NCERT Class 8 Mathematics

(a) 38.5 m² (b) 77 m² (c) 154 m² (d) 308 m²
Answer: (b)
Solution: Perimeter = πr + 2r = r(22/7 + 2) = r × 36/7 = 36, so r = 7 m. Area = ½ × (22/7) × 49 = 77 m². The trap is using πr alone as the perimeter, which omits the diameter. So the answer is 77 m².

117. A chord of length 30 cm is drawn in a circle of radius 17 cm. Its distance from the centre is:
(a) 12 cm (b) 15 cm (c) 7 cm (d) 8 cm
Answer: (d)
Solution: Half the chord = 15 cm. Distance = √(17² – 15²) = √(289 – 225) = √64 = 8 cm. The trap is using the full chord 30 cm in the Pythagorean step. So the answer is 8 cm.

118. The base of an isosceles triangle is 16 cm and its area is 48 cm². Its perimeter is:
(a) 30 cm (b) 36 cm (c) 40 cm (d) 24 cm
Answer: (b)
Solution: 48 = ½ × 16 × h gives h = 6 cm. Each equal side = √(8² + 6²) = 10 cm, so perimeter = 16 + 10 + 10 = 36 cm. The trap is taking the equal side as √(16² + 6²), forgetting the height bisects the base. So the answer is 36 cm.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Mathematics

119. In a cyclic quadrilateral ABCD, ∠A = 65° and ∠B = 75°. Then ∠D is:
(a) 65° (b) 75° (c) 115° (d) 105°
Answer: (d)
Solution: Opposite angles of a cyclic quadrilateral are supplementary, and B is opposite D. So ∠D = 180° – 75° = 105°. The trap is computing 180° – 65° = 115°, which is ∠C, not ∠D. So the answer is 105°.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Mathematics

120. A right circular cone and a right circular cylinder have equal bases and equal volumes. The ratio of their heights (cone : cylinder) is:
(a) 1 : 3 (b) 2 : 1 (c) 1 : 2 (d) 3 : 1
Answer: (d)
Solution: ⅓πr²h₁ = πr²h₂ gives h₁ = 3h₂, so the ratio is 3 : 1. The trap is inverting the ratio to 1 : 3. So the answer is 3 : 1.

121. A solid sphere of radius 6 cm is melted and drawn into a wire of diameter 2 mm. The length of the wire is:
(a) 288 m (b) 144 m (c) 432 m (d) 576 m
Answer: (a)
Solution: Volume of sphere = (4/3)π × 6³ = 288π cm³. Wire radius = 0.1 cm, so length = 288π / (π × 0.01) = 28800 cm = 288 m. The trap is forgetting to convert millimetres to centimetres, which distorts the result by a factor of 100. So the answer is 288 m.

122. Two circles of radii 5 cm and 3 cm have centres 4 cm apart and intersect at two points. The length of their common chord is:
(a) 6 cm (b) 8 cm (c) 4.8 cm (d) 5 cm
Answer: (a)
Solution: The distance from the larger circle's centre to the chord is (5² – 3² + 4²)/(2 × 4) = 32/8 = 4 cm. Half chord = √(25 – 16) = 3 cm, so the chord is 6 cm. The trap is reporting 3 cm, which is only half the chord. So the answer is 6 cm.

124. An equilateral triangle is inscribed in a circle of radius 6 cm. The area of the triangle is:

125. A sphere is inscribed in a right circular cone of base radius 6 cm and height 8 cm. The radius of the sphere is:
(a) 3 cm (b) 4 cm (c) 5 cm (d) 2 cm
Answer: (a)
Solution: Slant height = √(6² + 8²) = 10 cm. The inscribed sphere's radius = (R × h)/(R + l) = (6 × 8)/(6 + 10) = 48/16 = 3 cm. The trap is using r = R × h / (2l), which gives 2.4 cm and is not the correct formula. So the answer is 3 cm.
