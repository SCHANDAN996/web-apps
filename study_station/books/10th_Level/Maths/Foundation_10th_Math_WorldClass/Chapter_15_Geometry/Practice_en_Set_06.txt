126. In a right-angled triangle, the hypotenuse is 25 cm and the inradius is 3 cm. The area of the triangle is:
(a) 168 cm² (b) 150 cm² (c) 84 cm² (d) 180 cm²
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is computing ab = 168 and reporting it as the area — but area is half of ab. For a right triangle, r = (a + b − c)/2, so a + b = 31 and semi-perimeter s = (31 + 25)/2 = 28. Area = r × s = 3 × 28, so the answer is 84 cm².
Source: PYQ-style

127. Two circles of radii 6 cm and 2 cm have their centres 10 cm apart. The length of the transverse common tangent is:
(a) 8 cm (b) 6 cm (c) 10 cm (d) 12 cm
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is using the difference of radii (direct tangent formula) instead of the sum. Transverse tangent length = √[d² − (r₁ + r₂)²] = √(100 − 64) = √36, so the answer is 6 cm.
Source: PYQ-style

128. A solid cone of base radius 6 cm and height 3 cm is melted and recast into a solid sphere. The radius of the sphere is:
(a) 3 cm (b) 4 cm (c) 6 cm (d) 5 cm
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is forgetting the 4/3 factor in the sphere's volume and getting R³ = 108. Cone volume = ⅓π × 36 × 3 = 36π, and ⁴⁄₃πR³ = 36π gives R³ = 27, so the answer is 3 cm.
Source: PYQ-style

129. The sum of the interior angles of a polygon is 1800°. The number of diagonals of the polygon is:
(a) 44 (b) 65 (c) 54 (d) 77
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is reporting n(n−3)/2 with n = 10 (using 1800/180 directly) instead of first finding n = 12 from (n − 2) × 180 = 1800. Diagonals = 12 × 9/2, so the answer is 54.
Source: PYQ-style

130. In triangle ABC, BC = 6 cm and angle A = 60°. The circumradius R is:
(a) 3√3 cm (b) 2√3 cm (c) 4√3 cm (d) 6 cm
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is using R = a·sin A instead of the sine rule a = 2R sin A. Here R = 6/(2 × √3/2) = 6/√3, so the answer is 2√3 cm.
Source: NCERT Class 11 Mathematics

131. A solid cylinder of base radius 6 cm and height 14 cm is melted and recast into a solid right circular cone of base radius 3 cm. The height of the cone is:
(a) 56 cm (b) 84 cm (c) 112 cm (d) 168 cm
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is equating volumes without the ⅓ factor for the cone, which gives 56 cm. Cylinder volume = π × 36 × 14 = 504π and cone volume = 3πh, so 3πh = 504π and the answer is 168 cm.
Source: PYQ-style

132. The length and breadth of a rectangle are in the ratio 5:3. If its perimeter equals the perimeter of a square of side 40 cm, the area of the rectangle is:
(a) 600 cm² (b) 750 cm² (c) 1500 cm² (d) 937.5 cm²
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is taking the perimeter as 40 cm instead of 160 cm. With 16x = 160, x = 10, giving sides 50 cm and 30 cm, so the answer is 1500 cm².
Source: PYQ-style

133. A solid hemisphere of radius 6 cm is melted and recast into a right circular cone of height 12 cm. The base radius of the cone is:
(a) 6 cm (b) 3 cm (c) 9 cm (d) 12 cm
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is using the full sphere volume ⁴⁄₃πr³ instead of the hemisphere's ⅔πr³. Hemisphere volume = 144π and cone volume = 4πR², giving R² = 36, so the answer is 6 cm.
Source: PYQ-style

134. A square and an equilateral triangle have equal perimeters. If the side of the square is 9 cm, the area of the triangle is:
(a) 27√3 cm² (b) 48√3 cm² (c) 36√3 cm² (d) 54√3 cm²
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is taking the triangle's side as 9 cm instead of dividing the perimeter 36 cm by 3. Side = 12 cm and area = (√3/4) × 144, so the answer is 36√3 cm².

135. In trapezium ABCD, AB is parallel to CD, AB = 20 cm, CD = 14 cm, and the non-parallel sides are 5 cm each. The area of the trapezium is:
(a) 68 cm² (b) 72 cm² (c) 85 cm² (d) 102 cm²
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is using 5 cm directly as the height. Dropping perpendiculars leaves base segments of 3 cm each, so height = √(25 − 9) = 4 cm and area = ½ × 34 × 4, so the answer is 68 cm².

136. A chord of a circle of radius 10 cm subtends an angle of 120° at the centre. The area of the minor segment is (take π = 3.14, √3 = 1.732):
(a) 61.37 cm² (b) 40 cm² (c) 80 cm² (d) 50.33 cm²
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is reporting the sector area 104.67 cm² without subtracting the triangle. Sector = ⅓ × 3.14 × 100 = 104.67 cm² and triangle = ½ × 100 × sin 120° = 43.3 cm², so the answer is 61.37 cm².
Source: NCERT Class 10 Mathematics

137. The distance between the centres of two circles of radii 5 cm and 3 cm is 10 cm. The length of the direct common tangent is:
(a) 12 cm (b) 8 cm (c) 10 cm (d) 4√6 cm
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is adding the radii (transverse formula) instead of subtracting them. Direct tangent = √[d² − (r₁ − r₂)²] = √(100 − 4) = √96, so the answer is 4√6 cm.
Source: PYQ-style

138. A cone and a hemisphere have equal base radii and equal volumes. The ratio of the height of the cone to the radius of the hemisphere is:
(a) 1:2 (b) 2:3 (c) 3:2 (d) 2:1
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is inverting the ratio at the end. Equating ⅓πr²h = ⅔πr³ gives h = 2r, so the answer is 2:1.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Mathematics

139. A circle is inscribed in an equilateral triangle of side 12 cm. The area of the circle is (take π = 3.14):
(a) 113.04 cm² (b) 37.68 cm² (c) 75.36 cm² (d) 150.72 cm²
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is using the circumradius a/√3 = 4√3 instead of the inradius a/(2√3) = 2√3. Area = 3.14 × (2√3)² = 3.14 × 12, so the answer is 37.68 cm².
Source: PYQ-style

140. The volumes of two cones are in the ratio 1:4 and their heights are in the ratio 1:2. The ratio of their base radii is:
(a) 1:2 (b) 1:4 (c) 1:√2 (d) √2:1
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is forgetting that volume depends on the square of the radius. From (r₁²/r₂²) × (1/2) = 1/4 we get r₁²/r₂² = 1/2, so the answer is 1:√2.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Mathematics

141. A solid cube of side 8 cm is painted on all faces and then cut into smaller cubes of side 2 cm. How many smaller cubes have exactly two painted faces?
(a) 12 (b) 24 (c) 36 (d) 48
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is counting 4 cubes per edge instead of excluding the two corner cubes, which have three painted faces. Each of the 12 edges contributes 4 − 2 = 2 cubes, so the answer is 24.
Source: NCERT Class 8 Mathematics

142. The sides of a triangle are 25 cm, 39 cm and 56 cm. The length of the altitude to the side of 25 cm is:
(a) 28 cm (b) 50.4 cm (c) 42 cm (d) 33.6 cm
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is stopping at the Heron area 420 cm² and forgetting to double it when finding the altitude. Altitude = 2 × 420/25, so the answer is 33.6 cm.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Mathematics

143. A solid cylinder of base radius 6 cm and height 14 cm is melted and recast into a right circular cone of height 21 cm. The base radius of the cone is:

144. In a right circular cone, the slant height is 13 cm and the total surface area is 90π cm². The base radius is:
(a) 6 cm (b) 5 cm (c) 9 cm (d) 10 cm
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is using only the curved surface area πrl = 90π, which ignores the base. From πr(13 + r) = 90π, r² + 13r − 90 = 0 gives r = 5 (rejecting −18), so the answer is 5 cm.
Source: PYQ-style

145. A sphere of radius 9 cm is melted and drawn into a cylindrical wire of radius 3 mm. The length of the wire is:
(a) 324 m (b) 216 m (c) 108 m (d) 432 m
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is mixing units — forgetting to convert 3 mm to 0.3 cm. Volume = ⁴⁄₃π × 729 = 972π cm³ and length = 972π/(0.09π) = 10800 cm, so the answer is 108 m.

146. Two circles of radii 8 cm and 5 cm touch each other internally. The length of their common chord is:
(a) 6 cm (b) 0 cm (c) 13 cm (d) 3 cm
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is applying the common-chord formula mechanically. Circles touching internally meet at exactly one point, and a chord needs two intersection points, so the answer is 0 cm.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Mathematics

147. The perimeter of a rhombus is 40 cm and one of its diagonals is 16 cm. The area of the rhombus is:
(a) 96 cm² (b) 80 cm² (c) 48 cm² (d) 64 cm²
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is using the full diagonal 16 cm in the Pythagoras step instead of its half, 8 cm. Side = 10 cm, other half-diagonal = √(100 − 64) = 6 cm, so diagonals are 16 and 12 and the answer is 96 cm².
Source: PYQ-style

148. Two parallel chords on the same side of the centre of a circle are 6 cm and 8 cm long, and the distance between them is 1 cm. The radius of the circle is:
(a) 8 cm (b) 6 cm (c) 7 cm (d) 5 cm
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is adding the distances of the chords from the centre, which applies only when the chords are on opposite sides. With same-side chords, √(r² − 9) − √(r² − 16) = 1, satisfied by r = 5 (distances 4 and 3), so the answer is 5 cm.
Source: PYQ-style

149. From an external point P, secants PAB and PCD cut a circle at A, B and C, D respectively. If PA = 3 cm, AB = 9 cm and PC = 4 cm, then CD is:
(a) 6 cm (b) 7 cm (c) 9 cm (d) 5 cm
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is reporting PD = 9 cm as the answer instead of subtracting PC. By the secant theorem, 3 × 12 = 4 × PD gives PD = 9 cm, so CD = 9 − 4 and the answer is 5 cm.
Source: PYQ-style

150. The sum of the squares of the sides of a rhombus is 1600 cm² and one diagonal is 20 cm. The area of the rhombus is:
(a) 200√3 cm² (b) 400 cm² (c) 300 cm² (d) 500 cm²
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is assuming the other diagonal is also 20 cm. From 4a² = 1600, side a = 20 cm; half-diagonals are 10 cm and √(400 − 100) = 10√3 cm, so area = ½ × 20 × 20√3 and the answer is 200√3 cm².
Source: PYQ-style
