26. In ΔABC, ∠A = 50° and ∠B = 60°. The measure of ∠C is:
(a) 50° (b) 60° (c) 70° (d) 80°
Answer: (c)
Solution: The angles of a triangle sum to 180°, so ∠C = 180° − (50° + 60°) = 70°. The trap is 80°, which comes from wrongly subtracting only one given angle from 180°. So the answer is 70°.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Mathematics

27. A chord of length 12 cm is drawn in a circle of radius 10 cm. The distance of the chord from the centre is:
(a) 6 cm (b) 8 cm (c) 10 cm (d) 12 cm
Answer: (b)
Solution: The perpendicular from the centre bisects the chord, giving a right triangle with hypotenuse 10 cm and one leg 6 cm. Distance = √(10² − 6²) = √64 = 8 cm. The trap is 6 cm, which is only half the chord, not the distance. So the answer is 8 cm.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Mathematics

28. The angles of a quadrilateral are in the ratio 1 : 2 : 3 : 4. The largest angle is:
(a) 144° (b) 150° (c) 160° (d) 120°
Answer: (a)
Solution: Let the angles be x, 2x, 3x, 4x; then 10x = 360°, so x = 36° and the largest angle is 4x = 144°. The trap is 120°, which comes from dividing 360° by 3 instead of using the full ratio sum 10. So the answer is 144°.
Source: PYQ-style

29. A rhombus has diagonals of length 16 cm and 12 cm. Its area is:
(a) 72 cm² (b) 192 cm² (c) 48 cm² (d) 96 cm²
Answer: (d)
Solution: Area of a rhombus = ½ × d₁ × d₂ = ½ × 16 × 12 = 96 cm². The trap is 192 cm², which forgets the factor ½. So the answer is 96 cm².
Source: NCERT Class 8 Mathematics

30. The area of an equilateral triangle of side 6 cm is:
(a) 9√3 cm² (b) 36 cm² (c) 18√3 cm² (d) 12√3 cm²
Answer: (a)
Solution: Area = (√3/4) × a² = (√3/4) × 36 = 9√3 cm². The trap is 18√3 cm², which comes from using (√3/2)a² instead of (√3/4)a². So the answer is 9√3 cm².
Source: NCERT Class 9 Mathematics

31. O is the centre of a circle and ∠AOB = 100°. If C is a point on the major arc AB, then ∠ACB is:
(a) 130° (b) 100° (c) 50° (d) 80°
Answer: (c)
Solution: The angle subtended by an arc at the centre is double the angle at any point on the remaining part of the circle, so ∠ACB = ½ × 100° = 50°. The trap is 100°, which copies the central angle without halving it. So the answer is 50°.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Mathematics

32. Two circles of radii 5 cm and 3 cm touch each other externally. The distance between their centres is:
(a) 2 cm (b) 4 cm (c) 15 cm (d) 8 cm
Answer: (d)
Solution: When two circles touch externally, the distance between centres equals the sum of the radii: 5 + 3 = 8 cm. The trap is 2 cm, which is the difference of the radii — that applies to internal touching. So the answer is 8 cm.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Mathematics

33. The volume of a cylinder with base radius 7 cm and height 10 cm is (take π = 22/7):
(a) 154 cm³ (b) 700 cm³ (c) 220 cm³ (d) 1540 cm³
Answer: (d)
Solution: Volume = πr²h = (22/7) × 7 × 7 × 10 = 1540 cm³. The trap is 154 cm³, which is the base area πr² without multiplying by the height. So the answer is 1540 cm³.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Mathematics

34. In a cyclic quadrilateral ABCD, ∠A = 70°. The measure of ∠C is:
(a) 110° (b) 130° (c) 70° (d) 180°
Answer: (a)
Solution: Opposite angles of a cyclic quadrilateral are supplementary, so ∠C = 180° − 70° = 110°. The trap is 70°, which wrongly assumes opposite angles are equal. So the answer is 110°.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Mathematics

35. A ladder 25 m long leans against a wall with its foot 7 m from the wall. The height it reaches up the wall is:
(a) 20 m (b) 22 m (c) 24 m (d) 26 m
Answer: (c)
Solution: By the Pythagoras theorem, height = √(25² − 7²) = √(625 − 49) = √576 = 24 m. The trap is 22 m, which comes from miscomputing 625 − 49. So the answer is 24 m.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Mathematics

36. The perimeter of a semicircle of radius 14 cm, including the diameter, is (take π = 22/7):
(a) 72 cm (b) 58 cm (c) 86 cm (d) 44 cm
Answer: (a)
Solution: Perimeter = curved part + diameter = πr + 2r = 14 × (22/7) + 28 = 44 + 28 = 72 cm. The trap is 44 cm, which is only the curved arc and forgets the diameter. So the answer is 72 cm.
Source: NCERT Class 7 Mathematics

37. ΔABC is right-angled at B, with AB = 6 cm and BC = 8 cm. The inradius of the triangle is:
(a) 3 cm (b) 5 cm (c) 4 cm (d) 2 cm
Answer: (d)
Solution: Hypotenuse AC = √(6² + 8²) = 10 cm; area = ½ × 6 × 8 = 24 cm² and semi-perimeter s = (6 + 8 + 10)/2 = 12 cm, so inradius = area/s = 24/12 = 2 cm. The trap is 4 cm, which comes from dividing the area by the semi-perimeter incorrectly as 24/6. So the answer is 2 cm.
Source: PYQ-style

38. A triangle has sides 5 cm, 5 cm and 6 cm. Its area is:
(a) 15 cm² (b) 12 cm² (c) 20 cm² (d) 24 cm²
Answer: (b)
Solution: The triangle is isosceles; the height to the base 6 cm bisects it, so height = √(5² − 3²) = 4 cm and area = ½ × 6 × 4 = 12 cm². The trap is 24 cm², which forgets the factor ½ in the area formula. So the answer is 12 cm².
Source: PYQ-style

39. The sum of the interior angles of a heptagon (7-sided polygon) is:
(a) 720° (b) 900° (c) 1080° (d) 1260°
Answer: (b)
Solution: Sum = (n − 2) × 180° = (7 − 2) × 180° = 900°. The trap is 1260°, which comes from using n × 180° instead of (n − 2) × 180°. So the answer is 900°.
Source: NCERT Class 8 Mathematics

40. A sphere and a cube have equal surface areas. The ratio of their volumes (sphere : cube) is:
(a) √π : √6 (b) √π : √3 (c) √3 : √π (d) √6 : √π
Answer: (d)
Solution: From 4πR² = 6a² we get a/R = √(2π/3); then V(sphere)/V(cube) = (4π/3) ÷ (2π/3)^(3/2) = √(6/π) = √6 : √π. The trap is √π : √6, which simply inverts the correct ratio. So the answer is √6 : √π.
Source: PYQ-style

41. The length of a rectangle is increased by 20% and its breadth is decreased by 10%. The percentage change in its area is:
(a) 8% decrease (b) 10% increase (c) 8% increase (d) 10% decrease
Answer: (c)
Solution: New area = 1.2 × 0.9 = 1.08 times the old area, an increase of 8%. The trap is 10% increase, which comes from simply subtracting the percentages (20 − 10) instead of multiplying the factors. So the answer is 8% increase.
Source: PYQ-style

42. A cone and a cylinder have equal base radii and equal heights. The ratio of the volume of the cone to that of the cylinder is:
(a) 1 : 3 (b) 1 : 2 (c) 3 : 1 (d) 2 : 1
Answer: (a)
Solution: V(cone) = ⅓πr²h and V(cylinder) = πr²h, so the ratio is ⅓ : 1 = 1 : 3. The trap is 3 : 1, which reverses the order asked in the question. So the answer is 1 : 3.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Mathematics

43. A circle is inscribed in a square of side 14 cm. The area of the circle is (take π = 22/7):
(a) 196 cm² (b) 154 cm² (c) 616 cm² (d) 44 cm²
Answer: (b)
Solution: The diameter equals the side of the square, so radius = 7 cm and area = (22/7) × 7² = 154 cm². The trap is 616 cm², which uses radius 14 cm instead of 7 cm. So the answer is 154 cm².
Source: NCERT Class 7 Mathematics

44. In ΔABC, AD is a median and E is the midpoint of AD. The area of ΔBED is what fraction of the area of ΔABC?
(a) 1/8 (b) 1/4 (c) 1/3 (d) 1/6
Answer: (b)
Solution: The median AD halves the triangle, so area of ΔABD = ½ of ΔABC; since E is the midpoint of AD, BE is a median of ΔABD, so area of ΔBED = ½ of ΔABD = ¼ of ΔABC. The trap is 1/8, which applies the halving one time too many. So the answer is 1/4.
Source: PYQ-style

45. Two circles of radii 9 cm and 4 cm touch each other externally. The length of their direct common tangent is:
(a) 13 cm (b) 12 cm (c) 15 cm (d) 6 cm
Answer: (b)
Solution: For externally touching circles, the direct common tangent = 2√(r₁r₂) = 2√(9 × 4) = 2 × 6 = 12 cm. The trap is 13 cm, which is the distance between the centres (9 + 4), not the tangent length. So the answer is 12 cm.
Source: PYQ-style

46. A polygon has 9 diagonals. The number of its sides is:
(a) 6 (b) 8 (c) 7 (d) 9
Answer: (a)
Solution: Number of diagonals = n(n − 3)/2 = 9, so n² − 3n − 18 = 0, giving (n − 6)(n + 3) = 0 and n = 6. The trap is 9, which just copies the number of diagonals as the number of sides. So the answer is 6.
Source: NCERT Class 8 Mathematics

47. The curved surface area of a cone with slant height 10 cm and base radius 7 cm is (take π = 22/7):
(a) 440 cm² (b) 110 cm² (c) 220 cm² (d) 154 cm²
Answer: (c)
Solution: Curved surface area = πrl = (22/7) × 7 × 10 = 220 cm². The trap is 154 cm², which is πr² (the base area) multiplied by nothing relevant — it ignores the slant height. So the answer is 220 cm².
Source: NCERT Class 9 Mathematics

48. AB and CD are parallel chords of a circle of radius 13 cm, lying on opposite sides of the centre, with AB = 10 cm and CD = 24 cm. The distance between the chords is:
(a) 17 cm (b) 12 cm (c) 5 cm (d) 7 cm
Answer: (a)
Solution: Distance of AB from centre = √(13² − 5²) = 12 cm; distance of CD from centre = √(13² − 12²) = 5 cm. Since the chords are on opposite sides, total distance = 12 + 5 = 17 cm. The trap is 7 cm, which is the difference 12 − 5 and would apply only if the chords were on the same side. So the answer is 17 cm.
Source: PYQ-style

49. A right circular cylinder of radius 7 cm and height 20 cm is melted and recast into a cone of base radius 14 cm. The height of the cone is:
(a) 10 cm (b) 5 cm (c) 15 cm (d) 20 cm
Answer: (c)
Solution: Volume of cylinder = π × 7² × 20 = 980π; volume of cone = ⅓π × 14² × h = (196π/3)h. Equating, h = 980 × 3/196 = 15 cm. The trap is 5 cm, which results from forgetting the factor 3 in the cone's volume formula. So the answer is 15 cm.
Source: PYQ-style

50. Each interior angle of a regular polygon is 156°. The number of its sides is:
(a) 12 (b) 18 (c) 20 (d) 15
Answer: (d)
Solution: Each exterior angle = 180° − 156° = 24°, and the number of sides = 360°/24° = 15. The trap is 12, which comes from dividing 360° by 30° (a miscomputed exterior angle). So the answer is 15.
Source: NCERT Class 8 Mathematics
