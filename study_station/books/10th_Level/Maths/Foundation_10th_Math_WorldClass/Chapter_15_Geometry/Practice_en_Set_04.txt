76. In an equilateral triangle of side 10 cm, the length of the altitude is:
(a) 10 cm (b) 10√3 cm (c) 5 cm (d) 5√3 cm
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is taking the altitude as half the side (5 cm), but the altitude splits the triangle into two 30-60-90 right triangles. Altitude = (√3/2) × side = (√3/2) × 10, so the answer is 5√3 cm.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Mathematics

77. A chord of a circle of radius 17 cm is at a distance of 8 cm from the centre. The length of the chord is:
(a) 15 cm (b) 30 cm (c) 34 cm (d) 20 cm
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is reporting the half-chord (15 cm) as the answer. The perpendicular from the centre bisects the chord, so half-chord = √(17² − 8²) = √225 = 15 cm, and the full chord is 2 × 15, so the answer is 30 cm.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Mathematics

78. Two adjacent angles of a parallelogram are in the ratio 2 : 3. The measure of the smaller angle is:
(a) 36° (b) 90° (c) 108° (d) 72°
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is stopping at x = 36°, which is only the common factor, not an angle. Adjacent angles are supplementary: 2x + 3x = 180° gives x = 36°, and the smaller angle is 2x, so the answer is 72°.
Source: NCERT Class 8 Mathematics

79. A rhombus has diagonals of lengths 24 cm and 10 cm. The perimeter of the rhombus is:
(a) 52 cm (b) 68 cm (c) 104 cm (d) 120 cm
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is adding the diagonals (34 cm) or doubling the side wrongly. Diagonals bisect each other at right angles, so side = √(12² + 5²) = 13 cm and perimeter = 4 × 13, so the answer is 52 cm.
Source: NCERT Class 8 Mathematics

80. The angle between a tangent to a circle and the radius drawn to the point of contact is:
(a) 90° (b) 45° (c) 180° (d) 0°
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is thinking the angle depends on the circle's size; it does not. The tangent at any point of a circle is perpendicular to the radius through the point of contact, so the answer is 90°.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Mathematics

81. A sector of a circle of radius 12 cm has a central angle of 60°. Its area is (take π = 3.14):
(a) 37.68 cm² (b) 150.72 cm² (c) 113.04 cm² (d) 75.36 cm²
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is using the full circle area 452.16 cm² or the wrong fraction. Sector area = (60/360) × 3.14 × 144 = (1/6) × 452.16, so the answer is 75.36 cm².
Source: NCERT Class 10 Mathematics

82. The sum of the interior angles of a decagon (10-sided polygon) is:
(a) 1440° (b) 1260° (c) 1080° (d) 1800°
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is using n × 180° = 1800° instead of (n − 2) × 180°. Sum = (10 − 2) × 180° = 8 × 180°, so the answer is 1440°.
Source: NCERT Class 8 Mathematics

83. A triangle and a parallelogram are on the same base and between the same parallels. If the area of the triangle is 30 cm², the area of the parallelogram is:
(a) 15 cm² (b) 60 cm² (c) 45 cm² (d) 30 cm²
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is halving 30 to get 15 cm², reversing the relation. On the same base and between the same parallels, the parallelogram's area is double the triangle's area, so the answer is 60 cm².
Source: NCERT Class 9 Mathematics

84. The volumes of two spheres are in the ratio 64 : 27. The ratio of their radii is:
(a) 8 : 3 (b) 16 : 9 (c) 4 : 3 (d) 2 : 1
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is taking the square root (8 : 3) instead of the cube root. Since volume varies as the cube of the radius, (r₁/r₂)³ = 64/27 = (4/3)³, so the answer is 4 : 3.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Mathematics

85. In a rectangle, the length is 20% more than the breadth. If the breadth is 25 cm, the length is:
(a) 30 cm (b) 20 cm (c) 35 cm (d) 40 cm
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is computing 20% of 25 = 5 and subtracting or misplacing it. Length = 25 + (20/100) × 25 = 25 + 5, so the answer is 30 cm.
Source: NCERT Class 8 Mathematics

86. A right circular cone has base radius 6 cm and height 8 cm. Its slant height is:
(a) 12 cm (b) 14 cm (c) 9 cm (d) 10 cm
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is adding radius and height (14 cm) instead of applying Pythagoras. Slant height l = √(r² + h²) = √(36 + 64) = √100, so the answer is 10 cm.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Mathematics

87. In ΔABC, D and E are points on AB and AC respectively such that DE is parallel to BC. If AD = 4 cm, DB = 6 cm and AE = 5 cm, then EC is:
(a) 6 cm (b) 10 cm (c) 7.5 cm (d) 8 cm
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is equating AE with EC or using AD/AB incorrectly. By the Basic Proportionality Theorem, AD/DB = AE/EC, so 4/6 = 5/EC gives EC = 30/4, so the answer is 7.5 cm.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Mathematics

88. The circumference of a circle is 88 cm. Its area is (take π = 22/7):
(a) 616 cm² (b) 2464 cm² (c) 154 cm² (d) 308 cm²
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is using r = 28 cm (forgetting the factor 2 in 2πr), which gives 2464 cm². From 2πr = 88, r = 14 cm, and area = (22/7) × 196, so the answer is 616 cm².
Source: NCERT Class 7 Mathematics

89. If the edge of a cube is doubled, the percentage increase in its volume is:
(a) 700% (b) 300% (c) 100% (d) 800%
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is answering 800%, which is the new volume as a percentage of the old, not the increase. New volume = 8a³, increase = 7a³, so the percentage increase is 700%.
Source: NCERT Class 8 Mathematics

90. A cylinder and a cone have equal bases and equal heights. If the volume of the cylinder is 300 cm³, the volume of the cone is:
(a) 100 cm³ (b) 200 cm³ (c) 300 cm³ (d) 150 cm³
Answer: (a)
Solution: The trap is halving the cylinder's volume (150 cm³). A cone's volume is one-third of a cylinder with the same base and height: 300/3, so the answer is 100 cm³.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Mathematics

91. Two circles of radii 12 cm and 3 cm touch each other externally. The length of their direct common tangent is:
(a) 15 cm (b) 12 cm (c) 9 cm (d) 6 cm
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is adding the radii (15 cm), which gives the distance between centres, not the tangent. For externally touching circles, direct common tangent = 2√(r₁r₂) = 2√36, so the answer is 12 cm.
Source: PYQ-style

92. A ladder is placed with its foot 5 m from a wall and reaches a height of 12 m on the wall. The length of the ladder is:
(a) 10 m (b) 13 m (c) 15 m (d) 17 m
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is adding 12 + 5 = 17 m. The ladder is the hypotenuse: √(12² + 5²) = √169, so the answer is 13 m.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Mathematics

93. The area of a trapezium whose parallel sides are 14 cm and 6 cm and whose height is 8 cm is:
(a) 100 cm² (b) 120 cm² (c) 80 cm² (d) 160 cm²
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is forgetting the factor ½, which gives 160 cm². Area = ½ × (14 + 6) × 8 = ½ × 20 × 8, so the answer is 80 cm².
Source: NCERT Class 8 Mathematics

94. A regular polygon has each exterior angle equal to 30°. The number of its sides is:
(a) 6 (b) 8 (c) 12 (d) 10
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is using the interior-angle formula with 30°. Exterior angles of any polygon sum to 360°, so n = 360/30, and the answer is 12.

95. A solid metallic sphere of radius 5 cm is melted and recast into a solid cylinder of radius 5 cm. The height of the cylinder is:
(a) 10/3 cm (b) 20/3 cm (c) 5 cm (d) 10 cm
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is forgetting the 4/3 factor in the sphere's volume, which gives 5 cm. Equating volumes: π × 25 × h = (4/3)π × 125, so h = 500/75, and the answer is 20/3 cm.
Source: NCERT Class 10 Mathematics

96. In a circle with centre O, the angle AOC is 130°. B is a point on the minor arc AC. The measure of angle ABC is:
(a) 65° (b) 130° (c) 115° (d) 180°
Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is halving 130° to get 65°, which is the angle from a point on the major arc. Since B lies on the minor arc, angle ABC subtends the major arc of 360° − 130° = 230°, giving half of 230°, so the answer is 115°.
Source: NCERT Class 9 Mathematics

97. A rectangular field has its length and breadth in the ratio 3 : 2 and an area of 216 m². Its perimeter is:
(a) 72 m (b) 60 m (c) 84 m (d) 96 m
Answer: (b)
Solution: The trap is taking x = 6 as a side and computing 2(3+2) × 6 = 60 incorrectly as 5 × 6 × 2 without finding actual sides — here it coincides, but the correct route is: 6x² = 216 gives x = 6, sides 18 m and 12 m, perimeter = 2(18 + 12), so the answer is 60 m.
Source: NCERT Class 8 Mathematics

98. A circle is drawn with the diagonal of a square of side 14 cm as its diameter. The area of the circle is (take π = 22/7):

Answer: (c)
Solution: The trap is taking the side 14 cm as the diameter, which gives 154 cm². The diagonal is 14√2 cm, so the radius is 7√2 cm and area = (22/7) × 98, so the answer is 308 cm².

99. In a right-angled triangle, the legs are 5 cm and 12 cm. The radius of the inscribed circle is:
(a) 1 cm (b) 3 cm (c) 4 cm (d) 2 cm
Answer: (d)
Solution: The trap is using Area divided by the full perimeter instead of the semi-perimeter. Hypotenuse = 13 cm, area = 30 cm², semi-perimeter s = 15 cm, and r = Area/s = 30/15, so the answer is 2 cm.

Solution: The trap is using the side 22 cm directly as the radius, which inflates the area. Perimeter of square = 88 cm = 2πr, so r = 14 cm and area = (22/7) × 196, so the answer is 616 cm².
