"चक्रवृद्धि ब्याज" सुनते ही दिमाग़ क्यों जाम हो जाता है?

अगर "चक्रवृद्धि" शब्द सुनकर आपको रसायन विज्ञान की प्रयोगशाला याद आती है, तो आप अकेले नहीं हैं। अधिकतर विद्यार्थी इसे साधारण ब्याज से गड्ड-मड्ड कर देते हैं क्योंकि सूत्र दिखने में एक जैसे लगते हैं। लेकिन ये डर बस एक पतली चादर है। इसके पीछे एक खूबसूरत पैटर्न है जो उतना ही आसान है जितना एक कागज़ को 50 बार तह करना – और उसकी मोटाई चाँद की दूरी से भी ज़्यादा हो जाना। मेरे साथ बने रहिए; मैं पक्का करूँगा कि आप फिर कभी इन दोनों को न मिलाएँ।

पहले, साधारण ब्याज की धूल झाड़ लें

साधारण ब्याज (SI) सिर्फ़ मूलधन पर ही मिलता है। इसे ऐसे समझो जैसे एक सुस्त मकान मालिक हर साल एक जैसा किराया वसूलता है।

सूत्र:

SI = (P × R × T) / 100

अगर आप ₹1000 उधार दें 10% दर पर 3 साल के लिए:

SI = (1000×10×3)/100 = ₹300

कुल रकम = ₹1300

🧠
हर साल आपको ठीक ₹100 मिलते हैं। बोरिंग, है न? अब ब्याज को दिमाग़ देते हैं।

चक्रवृद्धि ब्याज – वो ब्याज जो सोता नहीं

चक्रवृद्धि ब्याज (CI) में, ब्याज खुद ब्याज कमाता है। पहले साल में जो पैसा आप कमाते हैं, वो दूसरे साल में मूलधन से जुड़ जाता है। ये पहाड़ी से लुढ़कता बर्फ का गोला है, हर सेकंड और बर्फ चिपका लेता है।

वही ₹1000, 10% दर पर 3 साल के लिए, वार्षिक चक्रवृद्धि:

पहले साल बाद: ₹1000 + ₹100 = ₹1100

दूसरे साल: ₹1100 पर ब्याज = ₹110 → कुल ₹1210

तीसरे साल: ₹1210 पर ब्याज = ₹121 → कुल ₹1331

कुल CI = ₹1331 – ₹1000 = ₹331।

गौर कीजिए, आपने साधारण ब्याज से ₹31 ज़्यादा कमाए। यही अतिरिक्त रकम है "चक्रवृद्धि प्रभाव।"

🧠 सक्रिय स्मरण: आँखें बंद करें और अपने छोटे भाई-बहन को समझाएँ कि ₹1000 पर 10% CI से 3 साल में ₹331 ब्याज क्यों मिला, जबकि SI से सिर्फ़ ₹300 मिला।

सुनहरा सूत्र

वार्षिक चक्रवृद्धि के लिए:

A = P (1 + R/100)^T

CI = A – P

जहाँ

P = मूलधन (शुरुआती रकम)

R = प्रति समय अवधि दर (आमतौर पर वार्षिक)

T = समय अवधियों की संख्या (आमतौर पर साल)

हमारे उदाहरण में: A = 1000 (1 + 10/100)^3 = 1000 × 1.1³ = 1000 × 1.331 = 1331।

🧠 खुद को परखें: ₹5000 पर 8% वार्षिक चक्रवृद्धि दर से 2 साल में कितनी रकम होगी?

(हल करें: 5000 × 1.08² = 5000 × 1.1664 = 5832)

सूत्र कभी न भूलें – तोता टीटू की तरकीब

एक अजीबोगरीब तोता है चक्रधर तोता। वो आपके मूलधन से शुरुआत करता है। हर साल वो चिल्लाता है "एक-जमा-दर-बटा-सौ" और रकम को उस वाक्यांश से गुणा कर देता है। वो ऐसा ठीक T बार करता है। तो मिश्रधन

(A) बस P है, जिसे (1 + R/100) से बार-बार, T बार गुणा कर दिया गया है।

कल्पना कीजिए चक्रधर तोता, हरा रंग, छोटा सा कैलकुलेटर लिए, बार-बार "(1 + R/100)" रटे जा रहा है जैसे कोई टूटा रिकॉर्ड। T चीखों के बाद, उसके कैलकुलेटर पर A दिखता है। यही घातांक है – बार-बार गुणा, जोड़ नहीं।

कंपाउंडिंग की आवृत्ति – बढ़ने के और मौके

बैंक और भी उदार हो सकते हैं: वो अर्धवार्षिक, तिमाही या मासिक आधार पर भी ब्याज जोड़ सकते हैं। मतलब ब्याज ज़्यादा बार मूलधन में जुड़ता है, और आप ब्याज पर ब्याज तेज़ी से कमाते हैं।

A = 10000 (1 + 20/200)^(2×1) = 10000 (1 + 0.1)² = 10000 × 1.21 = ₹12,100।

(तिमाही: R/4 = 2.5%, समय = 4; A = 8000 × (1 + 2.5/100)^4 = 8000 × (1.025)^4। आप अनुमान लगा सकते हैं।)

प्रभावी वार्षिक दर – असली प्रतिशत

जब कंपाउंडिंग साल में एक से ज़्यादा बार होती है, तो असली सालाना बढ़त बताई गई दर से ज़्यादा होती है। इसे कहते हैं प्रभावी वार्षिक दर।

प्रभावी दर = (1 + R/n)^n – 1, जहाँ n = एक साल में कंपाउंडिंग अवधियों की संख्या।

20% अर्धवार्षिक के लिए: n=2 → (1 + 0.20/2)² – 1 = (1.1)² – 1 = 0.21 = 21%

20% तिमाही: (1.05)^4 – 1 ≈ 0.2155 = 21.55%

तो "20% अर्धवार्षिक कंपाउंडिंग" असल में 21% सालाना है। बैंक नाममात्र दरें बताना पसंद करते हैं – लेकिन अब आप सच्चाई जानते हैं।

दो और तीन साल के जादुई शॉर्टकट

अगर दर R% ही रहे 2 या 3 साल के लिए, तो कुछ सूत्र भारी गुणा-भाग से बचा लेते हैं।

2 साल के लिए:

CI और SI का अंतर = P (R/100)²

CI = P × [2

R/100 + (R/100)²] (अगर आप (1+R/100)² – 1 को खोलें)

उदाहरण: P=5000, R=10% → अंतर = 5000 × (0.1)² = 5000 × 0.01 = ₹50। मतलब CI, SI से ₹50 ज़्यादा देता है।

3 साल के लिए:

अंतर = P (R/100)² × (3 + R/100)

CI = P × [3

R/100 + 3(R/100)² + (R/100)³]

उदाहरण: P=5000, R=10% → अंतर = 5000 × 0.01 × (3 + 0.1) = 5000 × 0.01 × 3.1 = 5000 × 0.031 = ₹155।

🧠 स्मरण झटका: कबूतर नृत्य निमोनिक याद करें। 2 साल के लिए अंतर है "P-दर-वर्ग"। 3 साल के लिए ये बन जाता है "P-दर-वर्ग गुणा (3 जमा दर)"। एक कबूतर की कल्पना करें जो पार्टी में '3' के बड़े गुब्बारे और अपने पंख पर दर का टैग लगाए नाच रहा है।

जब दरें हर साल बदलें (क्रमिक प्रतिशत)

अगर ब्याज दर हर साल अलग-अलग हो – जैसे 10%, 20%, 15% तीन सालों के लिए – तो रकम ऐसे बढ़ेगी:

A = P × (1 + R₁/100) × (1 + R₂/100) × (1 + R₃/100)

क्रम मायने नहीं रखता। यह बस क्रमिक वृद्धि गुणकों को गुणा करना है।

उदाहरण: P=4000, दरें 5%, 10%, 20% तीन सालों में:

A = 4000 × 1.05 × 1.10 × 1.20 = 4000 × 1.05 = 4200; 4200 × 1.10 = 4620; 4620 × 1.20 = 5544।

CI = 5544 – 4000 = 1544।

🧠 इसे आज़माएँ: ₹5000 पर 8%, 10%, और 12% की दर से 3 साल। कुल CI निकालें।

(A = 5000 × 1.08 × 1.10 × 1.12 = 5000 × 1.08 = 5400; ×1.10 = 5940; ×1.12 = 6652.8; CI = 1652.8)

बढ़ोतरी और गिरावट – एक ही DNA

चक्रवृद्धि ब्याज सिर्फ़ पैसे के लिए नहीं है। जनसंख्या वृद्धि, मशीन का मूल्यह्रास, बढ़ते पौधे की ऊँचाई – सब यही तर्क अपनाते हैं।

वृद्धि (जैसे, जनसंख्या): A = P (1 + R/100)^T

मूल्यह्रास (जैसे, गाड़ी की कीमत): A = P (1 – R/100)^T

दोनों में, हर अवधि में मात्रा एक स्थिर गुणक से गुणा होती है।

उदाहरण: 20,000 की आबादी वाला कस्बा 10% सालाना से बढ़ता है। 3 साल बाद:

20000 × 1.1³ = 20000 × 1.331 = 26,620 लोग।

एक मशीन जिसकी कीमत ₹1,00,000 है, 15% वार्षिक दर से मूल्यह्रास। 2 साल बाद:

1,00,000 × (1 – 0.15)² = 1,00,000 × 0.85² = 1,00,000 × 0.7225 = ₹72,250।

A Penny That Becomes a Crorepati

Imagine I offer you a deal:

Option A – I hand you ₹10 lakh right now.

Option B – I give you a magical ₹1 coin that doubles in value every day, for 30 days.

Most people scream “Option A!” But wait…

On Day 1 you have ₹1.

Day 2: ₹2

Day 3: ₹4

Day 10: ₹512

Day 20: ₹5,24,288 (over 5 lakh!)

Day 30: the coin is worth ₹1,07,37,41,824 i.e., more than ₹10 crore!

That one rupee grew not by adding a fixed amount, but by multiplying itself. That’s compound interest – the force that turns a baby step into a giant leap.

Why Do Students Freeze at “Compound Interest”?

If the word “compound” makes your brain picture a chemistry lab, you’re not alone. Most learners confuse it with simple interest because the formulas look similar. The fear, though, is just a thin veil. Behind it lies a beautiful pattern that’s as easy as folding a paper 50 times – and ending up thicker than the distance to the moon. Stick with me; I’ll make sure you never mix them up again.

First, Refresh Simple Interest

Simple Interest (SI) is paid only on the original principal. Think of a lazy landlord who collects the same rent every year.

Formul

Back:

SI = (P × R × T) / 100

If you lend ₹1000 at 10% for 3 years:

SI = (1000×10×3)/100 = ₹300

Total amount = ₹1300

🧠
Every year you earn exactly ₹100. Boring, right? Now let’s give interest a brain.

Compound Interest – Interest That Works Without Sleeping

In Compound Interest (CI), interest earns interest. The money you earn in Year 1 joins the principal in Year 2. It’s a snowball rolling down a hill, picking up more snow every second.

Same ₹1000 at 10% for 3 years, compounded annually:

After Year 1: ₹1000 + ₹100 = ₹1100

Year 2: Interest on ₹1100 = ₹110 → total ₹1210

Year 3: Interest on ₹1210 = ₹121 → total ₹1331

Total CI = ₹1331 – ₹1000 = ₹331.

Notice you earned ₹31 more than Simple Interest. That extra is the “compound effect.”

🧠 Active Recall: Close your eyes and explain to your younger sibling why ₹1000 at 10% CI for 3 years gives ₹331, but SI gives only ₹300.

The Golden Formula

For annual compounding:

A = P (1 + R/100)^T

CI = A – P

Where

P = Principal (starting money)

R = Rate per time period (usually per year)

T = Number of time periods (usually years)

In our example: A = 1000 (1 + 10/100)^3 = 1000 × 1.1³ = 1000 × 1.331 = 1331.

🧠 Test Yourself: What is the amount on ₹5000 at 8% p.a. compounded annually for 2 years?

(Work it out: 5000 × 1.08² = 5000 × 1.1664 = 5832)

Never Forget the Formula – Parrot Tito’s Secret

Let a quirky parrot named Tito help you. Tito starts with your Principal. Every year he chirps “one‑plus‑rate‑over‑hundred” and multiplies the amount by that phrase. He does this exactly T times. So the Amount

(A) is just P, multiplied by (1 + R/100) again and again, T times.

Visualise Tito, a green parrot with a tiny calculator, repeating “(1 + R/100)” like a broken record. After T chirps, his calculator screen shows A. That’s exponentiation – repeated multiplication – not addition.

Effective Annual Rate – The Real Percentage

When compounding happens more than once a year, the actual yearly gain is higher than the stated rate. This is called the Effective Annual Rate.

Effective Rate = (1 + R/n)^n – 1, where n = number of compounding periods per year.

For 20% half‑yearly: n=2 → (1 + 0.20/2)² – 1 = (1.1)² – 1 = 0.21 = 21%

20% quarterly: (1.05)^4 – 1 ≈ 0.2155 = 21.55%

So “20% compounded half‑yearly” is actually 21% per year. Banks love to quote nominal rates – but now you know the truth.

Magic Shortcuts for Two and Three Years

For same rate R% over 2 or 3 years, a few formulas save you from heavy multiplication.

For 2 years:

Difference between CI and SI = P (R/100)²

CI = P × [2

R/100 + (R/100)²] (if you expand (1+R/100)² – 1)

Example: P=5000, R=10% → Difference = 5000 × (0.1)² = 5000 × 0.01 = ₹50. So CI gives ₹50 more than SI.

For 3 years:

Difference = P (R/100)² × (3 + R/100)

CI = P × [3

R/100 + 3(R/100)² + (R/100)³]

Example: P=5000, R=10% → Difference = 5000 × 0.01 × (3 + 0.1) = 5000 × 0.01 × 3.1 = 5000 × 0.031 = ₹155.

🧠 Recall Frenzy: Remember the Pigeon square‑dance mnemonic. For 2 years the difference is “P‑Rate‑Square”. For 3 years it becomes “P‑Rate‑Square times (3 plus Rate)”. Picture a pigeon dancing at a party with a big ‘3’ balloon and a rate tag on its wing.

When Rates Change Every Year (Successive Percentages)

If the interest rate is different each year – say 10%, 20%, 15% for three years – the amount grows as:

A = P × (1 + R₁/100) × (1 + R₂/100) × (1 + R₃/100)

Order does not matter. This is just multiplying successive growth factors.

Example: P=4000, rates 5%, 10%, 20% over 3 years:

A = 4000 × 1.05 × 1.10 × 1.20 = 4000 × 1.05 = 4200; 4200 × 1.10 = 4620; 4620 × 1.20 = 5544.

CI = 5544 – 4000 = 1544.

Growth and Decay – The Same DNA

Compound interest isn’t only for money. Population growth, machine value depreciation, height of a growing plant – all follow the same logic.

Growth (e.g., population): A = P (1 + R/100)^T

Depreciation (e.g., car value): A = P (1 – R/100)^T

In both cases, each period the quantity multiplies by a constant factor.

Example: A town of 20,000 grows at 10% per year. After 3 years:

20000 × 1.1³ = 20000 × 1.331 = 26,620 people.

A machine costing ₹1,00,000 depreciates at 15% per year. After 2 years:

1,00,000 × (1 – 0.15)² = 1,00,000 × 0.85² = 1,00,000 × 0.7225 = ₹72,250.

You have two magic piggy banks. In one, a lazy elf adds ₹10 every New Year. In the other, a clever goblin multiplies whatever is inside by 1.1 each New Year. Both start with ₹100. After 10 years, the lazy elf has added 10 times ₹10, so you have ₹200. The goblin? After year 1: ₹110. Year 2: ₹121. Year 3: ₹133. Year 10: wait, let me use a trick. At 10%, doubling time is about 7.2 years, so in 10 years it doubles once and a bit — it becomes around ₹259. That extra ₹59 appears because the goblin earns interest on the interest. That's compound interest, and the hardest thing to really feel in your bones is that the growth speeds up all by itself, like a snowball rolling downhill.
🧠
Why is that so hard? Because our brains are wired for straight lines — the elf's ₹10 per year feels fair. But the goblin's bank curves upward, slowly at first, then exploding. Let me tell you a story.

Aryan and Zara are both 12 years old. Aryan puts ₹100 into a goblin bank paying 10% compound interest. Zara thinks, "I'll wait till I'm 22; ₹100 now won't matter." At 22, she puts in ₹100 as well. They both leave the money untouched until age 60. Aryan's money sits for 48 years; Zara's for 38 years. Here's the mind-bender: at 60, Aryan's ₹100 has doubled 48/7.2 ≈ 6.67 times, so it's around ₹100 × 2^6.67 ≈ ₹100 × 100 ≈ ₹10,000. Zara's ₹100 doubles 38/7.2 ≈ 5.28 times, about ₹100 × 40 ≈ ₹4,000. Just waiting 10 years cost Zara more than half the final amount! The hardest truth is: time is the most powerful ingredient.

🧠
The unforgettable trick: The Rule of 72. It's a secret decoder ring. Divide 72 by your interest rate, and you get the number of years it takes for your money to double. 10%? 72/10 = 7.2 years. 8%? 72/8 = 9 years. 6%? 72/6 = 12 years. Why 72? Nobody's sure, but 72 is a nice number divisible by tons of small numbers (1,2,3,4,6,8,9,12…). When you meet a rate, just ask: "How many times would 72 split into you?"

1What is the simple yet powerful difference between simple interest and compound interest?
Simple interest pays only on the original principal. Compound interest pays on the principal and on the interest that has already been added – it’s interest on interest.
2₹1000 at 10% p.a. for 3 years. Simple interest vs. compound interest – what are the final amounts?
SI: ₹1300 (₹300 interest). CI: ₹1331 (₹331 interest). The extra ₹31 is the compound effect.
3Write the golden formula for compound interest when compounding annually.
Amount A = P (1 + R/100)^T, and then CI = A – P.
4Why is the exponent T so magical in A = P (1 + R/100)^T?
It stands for repeated multiplication. The money doesn't just grow by adding the same amount each year – it multiplies itself again and again.
5What does Parrot Tito teach us about the compound interest formula?
Tito starts with P and chirps "(1 + R/100)" exactly T times, multiplying as he goes. This reminds you that it's an exponent (repeated multiplication), not addition.
6How do you tweak the formula for half-yearly compounding?
Rate becomes R/2 per half-year, time becomes 2
7What changes for quarterly compounding?
Rate → R/4, time → 4
8A bank says 20% p.a. compounded half-yearly. What is the real annual rate?
Effective annual rate = (1 + 0.20/2)² – 1 = 0.21 = 21%. More frequent compounding gives a higher true return.
9What is the Rule of 72, and why does it feel like magic?
Divide 72 by the interest rate (in %) to find the number of years it takes for money to double. For 10%, 72/10 = 7.2 years. It’s a super-fast mental shortcut.
10If the interest rate changes every year (e.g., 5%, 10%, 20%), how do you calculate the final amount?
Multiply the principal by each year's growth factor in succession: A = P (1 + R₁/100)(1 + R₂/100)(1 + R₃/100). Order doesn't matter.
11What's the “pigeon dance” mnemonic for the difference between CI and SI for 2 years?
Difference = P × (R/100)². P-Rate-Square. Imagine a dancing pigeon holding a square with the rate written on it.
12How does the pigeon dance change for a 3-year CI–SI difference?
Difference = P × (R/100)² × (3 + R/100). The same pigeon now dances with a big ‘3’ balloon and a rate tag on its wing.
13A city’s population grows at 10% per year. Is this simple or compound growth?
Compound growth. Each year's population becomes the new base for the next year's growth, so we use A = P (1 + R/100)^T.
14A machine loses 15% of its value every year. How do you calculate its worth after 2 years?
Value = Original price × (1 – 15/100)². The minus sign captures depreciation instead of growth.
15₹5000 invested at 8% compound interest becomes ₹5832 after 2 years. What is the total compound interest?
CI = Amount – Principal = ₹5832 – ₹5000 = ₹832.
16How do you fairly compare two investments that compound at different frequencies?
Convert both to effective annual rates using (1 + r/n)^n – 1, then compare. Higher effective rate means better growth.
17Using the Rule of 72, how long will money take to double at 9% interest?
72 ÷ 9 = 8 years. The Rule of 72 is your pocket doubling-time calculator.
18You're unsure whether the formula is (1 + R/100) or just (R/100). How does Parrot Tito fix this?
Tito always says "one-plus" first. The "1+" means you keep the original principal. Without it, you'd lose the principal and the formula would be wrong.

एक आलसी निवेशक कहता है, "5 साल बाद निवेश करूँगा।" उसका जुड़वाँ भाई आज ही निवेश करता है। अंत में भाई का पैसा एक अतिरिक्त बार दोगुना हो चुका होता है। समय सबसे बड़ा जादू है।

ट्रिक: आपका भविष्य का स्वरूप चिल्ला रहा है: "आज शुरू करो, कंपाउंडिंग के जिन्न को खेलने दो!"

1. Parrot Tito’s Power Squawk

📦 Box 1

Imagine a bright green parrot named Tito. He sits on your shoulder and squawks “(1 + R/100)” exactly T times into your ear. Every time he squawks, the money multiplies. The amount is just P multiplied by that squawk repeated T times.

Formul

Back: A = P (1 + R/100)^T.

🧠
Mnemonic: Polly want a cracker? No, Polly wants an exponent! Tito’s repetitive squawk = exponentiation.

2. The Half-Yearly Halving Party

📦 Box 2

A birthday cake cut in half: for half-yearly compounding, you halve the rate (R/2) and double the periods (2

T). Picture a crazy baker shouting “Half the cream, double the layers!”

Formul

Back: A = P (1 + R/200)^(2

T).

Mnemonic: Half the percentage, double the dance steps.

3. The Quarterly Quarter Trick

📦 Box 3

Visualise a coin with four quarters. For quarterly compounding, the rate is divided by 4 (R/4) and the time multiplied by 4 (4

T). Think “Quarter of a pizza, but four times the slices.”

Formul

Back: A = P (1 + R/400)^(4

T).

Mnemonic: Quarter means split into 4, then times by 4.

4. The Pigeon Square Dance (CI – SI for 2 Years)

📦 Box 4

A fat pigeon dances in a square holding a sign that says “P × (R/100)²”. For 2 years, the difference between compound and simple interest is just that: P times the square of the rate (in decimal).

Formul

Back: CI – SI = P (R/100)².

Mnemonic: Pigeon’s square dance = P-Rate-Square.

5. The Pigeon’s Birthday Balloon (CI – SI for 3 Years)

📦 Box 5

The same pigeon now wears a party hat and holds a huge “3” balloon with the rate tag tied to its leg. The difference becomes P (R/100)² × (3 + R/100). Picture the pigeon struggling to fly because the balloon is pulling it up.

Formul

Back: CI – SI = P (R/100)² (3 + R/100).

Mnemonic: 3-year-old pigeon party: square the rate, then times (3 plus rate).

6. The Effective Annual Goblin

📦 Box 6

A little green goblin called Ef-fect lives in a bank vault. He gets angry if you ignore his true power. When compounding is more than once a year, he shouts: “(1 + R/n)^n – 1”. He grows a pointy hat for every compounding period.

Formul

Back: Effective rate = (1 + R/n)^n – 1.

Mnemonic: The goblin’s hat stacks up: more compounding periods, taller hat, higher true rate.

7. The Rule of 72 – Grandpa’s Magic Number

📦 Box 7

Grandpa always carries a walking stick with “72” carved on it. He taps it and says, “Divide 72 by your interest rate, son, and you’ll know when your money doubles.” For 10%: 72/10 = 7.2 years.

Formul

Back: Doubling time ≈ 72 / Rate (%).

Mnemonic: 72 taps of Grandpa’s stick = your money’s growing trick.

8. The Successive Rate Train

📦 Box 8

Three train cars each labelled with a growth factor: 1+R₁/100, 1+R₂/100, 1+R₃/100. The money just travels car to car, multiplying each time. No need to average the speeds; just ride the train.

Formul

Back: A = P (1+R₁/100)(1+R₂/100)(1+R₃/100)…

Mnemonic: Don’t average the tracks, just ride the factor cars.

9. The Popcorn Growth vs. Deflating Balloon

📦 Box 9

For population growth: the popcorn machine pops kernels, each kernel pops more. Use + R/100.

For depreciation: a balloon leaks air, each day losing some. Use – R/100.

Formulas: Growth: A = P(1+R/100)^T; Depreciation: A = P(1–R/100)^T.

Mnemonic: Popcorn (+) pops up; Balloon (–) sinks down.

10. The Half-Yearly Haircut

📦 Box 10

You go to a barber who says he will cut your hair “half-yearly”. You pay half the rate but you visit twice as often. The total haircut cost (amount) grows faster because of the extra visits.

Formul

Back: A = P (1 + R/200)^(2

T).

Mnemonic: Half the price, double the snips = more hair gone (more money).

11. The Quarterly Quarter Pounder

📦 Box 11

At a burger joint, a “Quarter Pounder” is served 4 times a day. The rate per meal is a quarter, but you eat 4 times more often. Your belly (the amount) expands quicker.

Formul

Back: A = P (1 + R/400)^(4

T).

Mnemonic: Quarter burger, 4 meals, exponential weight gain.

12. The One-Plus Mantra

📦 Box 12

Chant “One-Plus” before anything. Many forget the “1+” in (1+R/100) and end up with only the interest part, losing the principal. Picture a magician saying “You get your money BACK plus interest… so 1 + rate/100”.

Formul

Back: Growth factor = 1 + R/100.

Mnemonic: If you don’t say “One-Plus”, the magician makes your principal vanish!

13. The Doubling Genie and Power of 2

📦 Box 13

A genie grants a wish: your money doubles every Td years. To become 8 times, you need 3 doublings (2³=8). So total time = 3 × Td. For 16 times, 4 doublings. Always count the power of 2.

Formul

Back: If Td is doubling time, then n times requires log₂(n) × Td.

Mnemonic: Count the doublings on your fingers; each finger adds a Td.

14. The Installment Iceberg

📦 Box 14

When repaying in equal installments, the future payments are “discounted” back to today. Imagine each installment is an iceberg melting as it approaches the present. For 2 installments: P = x/(1+r) + x/(1+r)².

Formul

Back: PV = Σ x/(1+r)^t.

Mnemonic: Each iceberg (installment) gets smaller the further in the future it is; melt them with (1+r) power.

15. The Five-Year Flash – Saving with Time

📦 Box 15

A lazy saver says “I’ll wait 5 years to invest.” His twin invests now. The twin’s money doubles once extra because of the early start. Time is the secret sauce.

Mnemonic: Your future self is screaming: “Start today, let the compounding genie play!”

This trick reminds you: compound interest is about time, not just rate.
