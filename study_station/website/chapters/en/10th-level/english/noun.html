Chapter Notes

Noun

Look around your room — table, chair, book, window. Now look outside — Ram, Delhi, dog, tree. Every name you just said is a Noun. It is the backbone of every English sentence — any name at all, whether a thing, a person, a place, or a feeling.

Definition: A Noun is a word that gives the name of a person, place, thing, or idea.

  • Person: Ram, teacher, mother
  • Place: Delhi, school, market
  • Thing: book, table, phone
  • Ide

Back: *honesty, love, beauty*

🧠 Recall: "Delhi" and "city" are both nouns, but there is one big difference between them. What is it?

The 5 Types of Noun

1. Proper Noun — the name of one particular thing. Always starts with a capital letter.

Ram, Delhi, India, Monday, Ganga

2. Common Noun — the shared name of a whole class.

boy, city, country, river

> ⚡ Delhi is one particular city (Proper), while city is the shared name of all cities (Common).

3. Collective Noun — one name for a group of many.

team, family, crowd, army, bunch

4. Material Noun — the material things are made of. These are not counted.

gold, water, iron, wood, milk

5. Abstract Noun — what you cannot touch, only feel.

honesty, beauty, childhood, anger, freedom

🧠 Recall: Among *team*, *gold*, *honesty* — which is Collective, which Material, which Abstract?

Countable vs Uncountable

This is the most frequently tested distinction in SSC.

| | Countable | Uncountable |

|---|---|---|

| Examples | book, boy, apple | water, rice, advice, information |

🧠
| Plural? | Yes — books, boys | No — ~~waters~~, ~~advices~~ |
🧠
| Takes a/an? | Yes — a book | No — ~~an advice~~ |

| many/much | many books | much water |

⚠️ These are always Uncountable (examiners love them): advice, information, furniture, luggage, news, work, bread, hair, money, poetry, scenery

Wrong: *He gave me many advices.*

Right: *He gave me **much advice*** / *a piece of advice*

Rules of Number (Singular → Plural)

| Rule | Example |

|---|---|

| Usually add s | book → books |

| After s, sh, ch, x, o add es | box → boxes, hero → heroes |

| Consonant + y → ies | city → cities (but boy → boys) |

| f/fe → ves | knife → knives, leaf → leaves |

| Irregular | man → men, child → children, tooth → teeth, foot → feet |

| Same both ways | sheep → sheep, deer → deer, fish → fish |

🧠 Recall: The plural of *city* is *cities*, but of *boy* it is *boys* — why the difference?

Possessive Case (the 's rule)

  • For living beings use 's: Ram's book, the boy's bag
  • For plurals already ending in s, use only ': boys' school, students' union
  • For lifeless things use of: the leg of the table (not ~~table's leg~~)
  • For time/distance 's is allowed: a day's work, a mile's distance

The Examiner's Trap

⚠️ Trap 1 — plural of uncountables: advices, informations, furnitures — all three are wrong. This is the commonest trap in the exam.

⚠️ Trap 2 — verb with a Collective Noun: If the group acts as one unit, use a singular verb: The team is playing. But if members act separately, use plural: The team are arguing among themselves.

⚠️ Trap 3 — capitals for Proper Nouns: ram went to delhi is wrong — Ram went to Delhi.

Review this after 2 days.

In Simple Words

Feynman Technique: Noun in Simple Words

Tell your younger brother: "A game — say the name of anything you see in this room. Table! Chair! Fan! Well done — you just said a bunch of Nouns. A Noun simply means a name. Whether it is a thing, a person, or a place — its name is a Noun."

"Now the tricky one — the name 'happiness' is also a Noun, even though you cannot touch it. That kind is called an Abstract Noun."

Noun = name. That's all. Everything else is just its types.

The Biggest Trick: Ask "Can I count it?"

🧠
80% of the exam's traps live here. Ask every noun — "Can I count this as 1, 2, 3?"
  • book → yes (one book, two books) → Countable → many books, a book
  • water → no (~~one water, two waters~~) → Uncountable → much water, ~~a water~~

Line to remember: "Advice, news, furniture, luggage — never make these plural."

(Never write advices!)

Second Trick: Capital for Proper Nouns

There is only one Delhi — hence capital D. There are many cities — hence small c.

If it is "that one particular thing" → capital. If it is "any one of many" → small.

Short Tricks

Short Tricks: Noun

🪄
Trick: Ask every noun — "Can I count this as 1, 2, 3?" Yes → Countable (many, a/an); No → Uncountable (much, no a/an).
🪄
Trick: These 10 are always Uncountable — advice, information, furniture, luggage, news, work, bread, hair, money, scenery. Never make them plural.
🪄
Trick: To count an uncountable, add "piece/item" — a piece of advice, an item of furniture, a piece of news.
🪄
Trick: Proper Noun = "that one particular" → capital letter. Common Noun = "any one of many" → small letter.
🪄
Trick: Consonant + y → ies (city → cities); vowel + y → just s (boy → boys, key → keys). Look at the letter before the y.
🪄
Trick: The possessive of lifeless things uses of, not 's — the leg of the table, not table's leg. (But time/distance allows 's: a day's work.)
🪄
Trick: Collective Noun — if the group acts as one, use a singular verb (The team is playing); if members act separately, plural (The team are arguing).

Skip Strategy: If a plural rule escapes you, pick the option that "sounds most familiar" — irregular plurals (men, children, teeth) are usually recognisable.

💡Mnemonic (for uncountables): **"Advice-News-Luggage"** — plus furniture and information — these never take a plural.
