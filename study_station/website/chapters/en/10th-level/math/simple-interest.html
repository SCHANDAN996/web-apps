The ₹5 Lakh Car That Cost Me Nothing (Almost!)

🧠
Imagine this: You lend your friend ₹5,00,000 to buy a car. Your friend promises, “I’ll return the money in 2 years, plus ₹50,000 extra as thanks.” Sounds fair, right? You just earned ₹50,000 for doing nothing. But wait — is that a good deal? What if a bank would have paid you ₹80,000 for the same money? In both cases, the extra money is called interest. The simplest form of this deal — where interest is calculated only on the original sum, year after year, without adding it back — is what we call Simple Interest.
🧠
In exams like SSC and Banking, Simple Interest (SI) might seem like a tiny topic, but it’s the foundation for Compound Interest, Installments, and even Data Interpretation. Nail SI today, and you’ll breeze through harder topics tomorrow. And the best part? It’s just 4th-grade math wrapped in a formula.

But first, let’s be real…

Chunk 2: Time — The Shape-Shifter in Exams

Time (T) is always in years in the formula. But examiners love testing you with months and days. The fix is simple:

If time is given in months, convert to years by dividing by 12. (e.g., 6 months = 6/12 = 0.5 years)

If time is given in days, divide by 365. (e.g., 73 days = 73/365 = 0.2 years)

Sometimes they use 366 for a leap year — but 99% of the time, use 365 unless they scream “leap year.”

Even easier: remember “M÷12, D÷365” as a chant. (M for months, D for days.)

Example: ₹2000 at 5% for 3 months.

SI = (2000 × 5 × 3/12) / 100 = (2000 × 5 × 0.25) / 100 = 2500/100 = ₹25. Simple.

Chunk 3: When They Hide P, R, or T — The Detective Mode

Not every question will serve you P, R, and T on a plate. Sometimes they give you the SI and ask for the Principal. Or they say, “A sum doubles in 10 years, find the rate.” Don’t panic — it’s just rearranging the formula.

If a sum doubles, that means Amount = 2

P, so SI = Amount – P = 2

P – P = P.

So, SI = P. Plug into formul

Back: P = (P × R × 10) / 100.

Cancel P from both sides (P ≠ 0): 1 = (R × 10)/100 → R = 10%.

The secret: Whenever you see “doubles,” “triples,” or “n times,” subtract the original P to get SI. For “doubles,” SI = P; for “triples,” SI = 2

P; for “4 times,” SI = 3

P, and so on. Observation: SI is always (n-1) × P when the sum becomes n times.

This single insight solves 80% of the hard-looking SI problems in under 30 seconds.

Chunk 4: The Traps — Where Good Students Stumble

Trap 1: “Rate is 5% per annum, time is 6 months.” Many write T=6 and get a bloated SI. Remember: T is always in years. 6 months = 6/12 = 0.5.

Trap 2: “Amount after 3 years is ₹X, SI is given.” They ask for Principal, but some directly use X as P. Always: P = Amount – SI.

Trap 3: Two different principals, same rate and time, but SI difference is given. Just use the formula on the difference of principals. (SI1 – SI2) = (P1 – P2) × R × T / 100. This shortcut appears in nearly every banking exam.

Let’s frame an active recall for the difference trap.

🧠 Active Recall Prompt: If SI on ₹5000 for 2 years is ₹500, and SI on ₹8000 for same rate and time is ₹800, find the rate. Here the difference in SI is 300 on a difference of 3000. So 300 = (3000 × R × 2)/100. Solve to get R = 5%. This saves solving two separate equations. Keep this in your mental tool kit.

Chunk 5: The 5-Second Half-Yearly, Quarterly, and “Per Annum” Hack

Simple Interest is always calculated per annum unless the question tricks you by giving a rate for a different period. If it says “5% half-yearly,” it means you get 5% every 6 months, so the yearly rate becomes 10%. Similarly, “2% quarterly” means 2% every 3 months, so the annual rate is 8%. In SI, you can simply convert the rate to annual and use the same formula — no complex compounding. The only twist: time must still be in years aligned with that annual rate.

Example: ₹10000 at 4% quarterly for 1 year.

Annual rate = 4% × 4 = 16%. T = 1 year. SI = (10000 × 16 × 1)/100 = ₹1600. Done.

🧠
Remember: “Quarterly? Multiply rate by 4; Half-yearly? Multiply by 2.” But keep T in years.
🧠 Active Recall Prompt: What will be the simple interest on ₹5000 at 6% half-yearly for 2 years? (Convert rate: 6% × 2 = 12% per annum. Then SI = 5000×12×2/100 = ₹1200.) Confirm you didn’t forget to convert time.

Short Tricks

🪄
Trick 1: The Dirty Rat PRT/100

Visualize a giant, filthy rat named Professor Rat Tickles, holding a sign that reads “/100”. Whenever you need the simple interest formula, picture him screaming:

“P × R × T / 100 = Simple Interest!”

PRT lives on top, and the divider 100 is his stage. The weirder the image, the stickier it is.

🪄
Trick 2: Ape Sees Pie + Ice

To remember Amount = Principal + Simple Interest, imagine an ape at a birthday party. He sees a delicious Pie (P) and a scoop of Ice (SI) cream. He smashes them together and yells, "A = P + SI!"

This visual of a messy ape ensures you never write Amount without adding SI back.

🪄
Trick 3: Moo 12, Day 365

For time conversion, picture a cow (Moo) splitting the months into 12 equal nibbles, and a blazing sun (Day) counting every 365 rays. Chant:

"Moo divide 12, Day divide 365."

When you see months, hear the cow. When you see days, feel the sun’s 365 beams.

🪄
Trick 4: The Genie’s ‘n-1’ Rule

A genie offers to multiply your money n times, but he always takes one share as his fee. So the extra amount (the interest) is only (n – 1) times your original.

So if the sum becomes 5 times, the genie keeps 1, you get 4 — and that 4 is your SI in terms of P. Picture the greedy genie pocketing that ‘1’ and leaving you the rest.

🪄
Trick 5: Subtract the Cash, Then Apply the Hash

For two sums with the same rate and time, difference in interest = (difference in principals) × R × T / 100.

Imagine two money bags. First, subtract the cash (get the difference). Then, apply the hash (the same PRT/100 formula) to that difference.

Think “Cash minus Cash, then Rate-Time Hash”.

🪄
Trick 6: Executioner’s Rate Change

When the problem gives a half‑yearly rate, picture an executioner with an axe — he halves the year but doubles the rate to save you. When it says quarterly, he uses four axes and quadruples the rate.

🧠
Half‑yearly? Double it. Quarterly? Multiply by 4.

The executioner turns the confusing period into the simple annual rate.

🪄
Trick 7: Keep Time in Years, or Shed Tears

Imagine a crying face made of clock parts. Every time you see a problem with months or days, a voice whispers: “Convert to years, or you’ll cry like this clock‑face.”

The rule is absolute: T in the formula is always in years. Never use raw months or days.

🪄
Trick 8: Simple Sam Never Compounds

Simple Sam is a lazy landlord who charges rent only on the original property, never on the improvements. Every year he walks in, looks at the original principal, and takes exactly the same amount.

When you hear “simple interest”, think of Sam’s boring, constant rent. It never changes, never grows. That’s why it’s “simple”.

🪄
Trick 9: Doctor PT’s 100‑Rupee Note

To find the rate: R = (SI × 100) / (P × T).

Imagine a doctor named Dr. P.

T. who prescribes a treatment: Multiply your SI by a ₹100 note, then divide by the patient’s “P” and “T” vitals.

“See Eye” (SI) times 100, over P and T — Dr. P.

T.’s magic rate formula.

🪄
Trick 10: The Guard at the Bottom

In the formula SI = (P × R × T)/100, imagine three friends — P, R, T — standing on a stage. The security guard named 100 crouches at the bottom, checking that everything is multiplied before dividing.

This mental picture cements the order: multiply the top three first, then divide by the guard at the bottom.

🪄
Trick 11: Double the Money, Share the Century

For a sum to double, SI = P, so Rate = 100 / Time.

Imagine a century (100) being split equally into the years it takes to double. A “double‑century” celebration!

If doubling takes 8 years, 100/8 = 12.5%. That’s the “century sharing” trick.

🪄
Trick 12: The Salami Slice for Absolute vs. Additional

When a sum becomes so many times, remember the salami:

The whole salami is the final Amount.

The original salami stick is the Principal.

The slices you eat represent the Interest.

*If the salami is now 7 times, you ate 6 slices — SI = (7‑1)

P. Each slice is earned by the original stick, not by previous slices.*

Previous Year Questions

Q1. (SSC CGL) A sum of money at simple interest amounts to Rs. 1,200 in 3 years and to Rs. 1,350 in 4 years. The sum is:
Show Solution
✅ Answer:
💡 SI for 1 year = 1350 - 1200 = Rs. 150. SI for 3 years = 150 × 3 = Rs. 450. Principal = Amount after 3 years - SI for 3 years = 1200 - 450 = Rs. 750. 🧠 Psychological Hook: This uses the "Amount Progression" trap. Students often try to set up complex equations instead of using the simple logic that SI is constant each year.
Q2. (IDBI Bank PO) A earns an interest of Rs. 2880 on a certain sum invested for 3 years at R% SI. B earns Rs. 4000 on another sum for 5 years at the same R%. Sum invested by A is what percent more than that of B?
Show Solution
✅ Answer:
💡 P1 = (2880×100)/(3×R) = 96000/R. P2 = (4000×100)/(5×R) = 80000/R. Required % = ((P1 - P2)/P2) × 100 = ((16000/R) / (80000/R)) × 100 = 20%. 🧠 Psychological Hook: The "Variable Phobia" trap. The presence of an unknown variable R in the denominator frightens many, who then try to solve for R first, which is unnecessary.
Q3. (Railway JE) A lends Rs.15000 to B at 15% p.a. SI for 2 years. B immediately lends it all to C at 20% p.a. SI for 2 years. Find the profit of B.
B and his two transactions.
Show Solution
✅ Answer:
💡 SI paid by B to A = (15000×15×2)/100 = Rs. 4500. SI earned by B from C = (15000×20×2)/100 = Rs. 6000. B's profit = 6000 - 4500 = Rs. 1500. 🧠 Psychological Hook: The "Problem of Plenty" trap. Multiple people and numbers can overwhelm a student into inaction. The trick is to focus only on the middleman
Q4. (SSC MTS) A sum of Rs. 2,400 is invested for 4 years at R% p.a. simple interest and yields an SI of Rs. 1,152. Find the amount received after 3 years when the same sum is invested at (R+8)% p.a.
Show Solution
✅ Answer:
💡 Step 1: 1152 = (2400 × R × 4)/100 => R = 12%. Step 2: New rate = 12+8 = 20%. New SI = (2400 × 20 × 3)/100 = Rs. 1440. Amount = 2400 + 1440 = Rs. 3840. 🧠 Psychological Hook: The "Multi-Step Fatigue" trap. The question is long and can cause a student to lose focus halfway. Breaking it down into two independent, simple parts is the key.
Q5. (SBI PO Prelims) A person invested ₹ x at 10% p.a. SI for 7 years. If he had invested ₹ (x + 10,100) at 15% p.a. SI for 8 years, the interest would have been ₹ 34,620 more. Find x.
Show Solution
✅ Answer:
💡 SI1 = (x × 10 × 7)/100 = 0.70x. SI2 = ((x+10100) × 15 × 8)/100 = 1.20(x+10100). Equation: 1.20(x+10100) - 0.70x = 34620 => 0.5x + 12120 = 34620 => x = 45000. 🧠 Psychological Hook: The "Algebraic Panic" trap. Solving for a variable in a seemingly complex linear equation feels daunting. However, it simplifies neatly, and skipping it means losing an easy high-scoring question.
Q6. (SSC CGL) If the ratio of principal to simple interest for 1 year is 25:1, then the rate of interest is:
Show Solution
✅ Answer:
💡 P / SI = 25/1. We know SI = (P × R × 1)/100. So, R = (SI × 100) / P. From the ratio, SI/P = 1/25. Therefore, R = (1/25) × 100 = 4%. 🧠 Psychological Hook: The "Ratio-Relation" trap. Students often freeze when they see ratios instead of direct values. The key is to substitute the ratio directly into the formula.
Q7. (SSC CHSL) Simple interest on a sum for six months at 5% per annum is Rs. 65.5. What is the value (in Rs.) of the sum?
Show Solution
✅ Answer:
💡 Here, T = 6 months = 6/12 = 0.5 years. SI = (P × R × T) / 100 => 65.5 = (P × 5 × 0.5) / 100 => 65.5 = (2.5 P) / 100 => P = (65.5 × 100) / 2.5 = 6550/0.25? Wait. Let's re-calculate carefully. 65.5 = (P * 5 * 0.5) / 100 => 6550 = 2.5 P => P = 6550 / 2.5 = 2620. Check: (2620*5*0.5)/100 = (2620*2.5)/100 = 6550/100 = 65.5. Correct. 🧠 Psychological Hook: The classic "Time-Unit Trap". The most common mistake is using T=6 instead of T=0.5, which gives a principal that is 12 times smaller than the correct answer.
Q8. (RRB NTPC) A sum of money becomes double in 8 years at a rate of simple interest per annum. Find the rate.
Show Solution
✅ Answer:
💡 Sum doubles, so n=2. Using the n-1 rule, SI = (n-1) P = P. So, SI = P. Formul Back: P = (P × R × 8) / 100 => 1 = (8 R)/100 => R = 100/8 = 12.5%. 🧠 Psychological Hook: The "Missing Data" trap. Students panic thinking the principal is needed. The "n-1" rule elegantly removes the need for the principal, making the solution unexpectedly short.
Q9. (Insurance AO) What is the simple interest on Rs. 5000 at 6% half-yearly for 2 years?
Show Solution
✅ Answer:
💡 "6% half-yearly" means the annual rate is 6% × 2 = 12%. Time remains T = 2 years. Therefore, SI = (5000 × 12 × 2) / 100 = Rs. 1200. 🧠 Psychological Hook: The "Rate Definition" trap. The term "half-yearly" lures students into incorrectly adjusting the time instead of the rate for simple interest problems, leading them to a complex calculation.
Q10. (SSC CGL Tier 2) The simple interest on a certain sum at the rate of 12.5% per annum for 6 years is Rs. 13,500 less than the principal. Find the simple interest.
Show Solution
✅ Answer:
💡 Let Principal = P. SI = (P × 12.5 × 6) / 100 = (75 P)/100 = 0.75 P. The problem states: SI = P - 13500. So, 0.75 P = P - 13500 => 0.25 P = 13500 => P = Rs. 54,000. Therefore, SI = 0.75 × 54000 = Rs. 40,500. 🧠 Psychological Hook: The "Verbal Math" trap. Translating the phrase "interest is 13500 less than the principal" into the correct algebraic equation (SI = P - 13500, not P - SI = 13500, though they are the same) is where the error occurs. The trick is to write it down exactly as you read it.
