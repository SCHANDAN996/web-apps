4. Relative Speed – When Two Bodies Move

This is the heart of train and meeting problems. It is the speed at which one body sees the other moving.

Same direction: They seem to move at the difference of speeds.

Relative speed = Faster – Slower.

Imagine you are in a car at 60 km/h and a car beside you is at 50 km/h in the same direction. To you, it is going backward at 10 km/h.

Opposite direction: They rush towards each other. Speeds add up.

Relative speed = Speed1 + Speed2.

If you are at 60 km/h and a car heads straight at you at 50 km/h, your closing speed is 110 km/h. It feels faster, and it is.

Story detector for trains:

A train crossing a pole or a man – the distance to cover is the train’s own length.

A train crossing a platform – distance = length of train + length of platform.

Two trains crossing each other – distance = sum of their lengths; speed = relative speed (add if opposite, subtract if same direction).

Now, you are the detective. The train itself becomes a measuring tape.

🧠 Active Recall Prompt: A train travelling at 72 km/h crosses a pole in 12 seconds. Find its length. (Convert speed to m/s: 72×5/18=20 m/s. Distance = 20×12=240 m. That is the length.) Solve it in your head again.

5. The Train-Platform Tango (One Problem, Two Scenes)

Examiners’ favourite: A train crosses a pole in T1 seconds and a platform in T2 seconds. The difference in time is the time the train takes to cover the platform’s length (since the train’s own length is covered in both scenarios).

Method:

Let length of train = L, speed = v.

Pole crossing: L = v × t1.

Platform crossing: L + Platform = v × t2.

Subtract: Platform = v (t2 – t1).

From there, you can find v and then L.

Visualise the train’s nose as a point. When it touches the pole, timer starts; when the tail passes, timer stops. For the platform, nose touches start of platform, timer starts; tail leaves end of platform, timer stops. This is pure cinematography.

🧠 Active Recall Prompt: A train crosses a pole in 10 s and a 150 m long platform in 18 s. Can you smell the speed? (v = Platform / time difference = 150 / (18-10) = 150/8 = 18.75 m/s. Length = 18.75×10 = 187.5 m.)

In Simple Words

The Noodle, the Straw, and the Super-Fast Train

🧠
Let me tell you about a little girl named Anu who loved noodles. One evening, Anu was slurping a long, delicious noodle. Her dad, a clever engineer, smiled and said, “Anu, do you know that your noodle can teach you the toughest puzzle of trains, time, and distance?”
🧠
Anu giggled. “A noodle, Papa? How?”
🧠
“Watch,” he said. He picked up a single noodle from her bowl. “First, Anu, you just sucked that noodle straight into your mouth, right? The noodle’s tip started at your lips and travelled all the way in until its tail vanished. The distance the tip travelled was exactly the length of the noodle. That’s a train crossing a pole or a man. The train only has to cover its own length.”

Anu nodded.

“Now,” her dad continued, holding a straw, “imagine you slurp the same noodle through this straw. The noodle tip starts at one end of the straw, travels through the whole straw, and then still has to pull its own tail inside. So the tip travels the straw’s length plus the noodle’s length. That’s a train crossing a platform, a bridge, or a tunnel. The train has to cover its own length and the extra thing it’s crossing.”

Anu’s eyes widened. “So the extra time… is just for the extra distance!”

“Exactly. The difference between the two times is the time the train (or noodle) takes to cover the extra object’s length.”

The Never-Forget Trick

Whenever you see a train cross a pole in t₁ seconds and a platform in t₂ seconds, do this:

Subtract the times: (t₂ – t₁) seconds is the time the train needs to cover just the platform’s length.

Now you can find the speed: Speed = Platform length ÷ (t₂ – t₁).

Then the train’s length = Speed × t₁.

It’s like magic. The time difference always hands you the platform’s journey. You never need to panic.

The Secret Snapshot

Think of the train’s front nose as the noodle tip. When the nose touches the pole, the stopwatch starts. When the tail passes the pole, it stops — one noodle-length travelled.

When the nose enters the platform, the stopwatch starts again. It stops when the tail leaves the platform’s far end — one noodle-length PLUS one platform-length travelled. The extra time in the stopwatch is the gift that reveals the platform’s size.

Two apes, A and B, run a round trip. Their average speed is 2×A×B / (A+B).

4. Relative Speed – Same vs Opposite Direction

Mnemonic: “Sally Snake Subtracts, Ollie Owl Adds.”

Same direction → Sally Snake slithers after prey, she subtracts the slower speed.

Opposite direction → Ollie Owl flies head‑on, they crash and the speeds add up.

So simple: SSS (Same, Subtract, Snake) and OOA (Opposite, Add, Owl).

5. Train Crossing a Pole

Mnemonic: “Pole passionately kisses the train – time = length ÷ speed.”

Imagine a pole with big red lips hugging the train. The kiss lasts exactly Time = Train Length / Speed.

Acronym: P‑T KISS → Pole‑Train: KIss Speed = Length/Speed.

6. Train Crossing a Platform / Bridge

Mnemonic: “The train and platform have a PIT stop – Time = ( Platform + Iron‑train ) ÷ Speed.”

PIT = Platform + Iron‑Train (I = Train length, like an iron snake). At the pit stop, add their lengths and divide by speed to get the time.

“At the PIT stop, it’s P+I over S.”

7. Boat Upstream & Downstream Speeds

Mnemonic: “Uncle Boat Sucks upstream, Downstream he Adds.”

Upstream : B – S (Uncle Boat is slow, subtract the stubborn stream)

Downstream : B + S (Downhill, he joyfully adds the stream’s push)

Think of Uncle Boat in a bathtub; going upstairs he loses speed (subtract), going downstairs he gains speed (add).

“U‑B‑S” and “D‑B‑A‑D” (Down Boat Adds Delight).

8. Finding Boat’s Speed in Still Water & Stream Speed

Mnemonic: “Still boat is Average, Stream is Difference halved.”

Still‑water speed = ( Up + Down ) ÷ 2

Stream speed = ( Down – Up ) ÷ 2

Use the acronym SAD:

Still = Average (add and halve)

Stream = Difference (subtract and halve)

“SAD – Still Add, Stream Difference!”

9. Time Difference when Changing Speed

Mnemonic: “Discotheque DJs – D/ V₁ minus D/ V₂ equals the Jam time saved.”

When distance is same, the time gap = D/V₁ – D/V₂.

Picture a DJ spinning two discs – the difference between the two spinning speeds tells you how much time you save or lose.

“Divide Distance by each V, then Difference = time saved.”

10. Speed‑Time Proportionality (Constant Distance)

Mnemonic: “Duke Speed and Timer are enemies – when one goes up, the other dives.”

When distance is fixed, S ∝ 1/T. Imagine Duke Speed and Timer Timer on a seesaw; if Speed jumps up, Timer crashes down, and vice‑versa.

“Constant D → S and T are inversed‑seesaw pals.”

11. Meeting Time (Opposite Direction)

Mnemonic: “Meeting Opposites Divide Sum – MODS!”

When two objects start from opposite ends, the meeting time = Total Distance ÷ Sum of Speeds.

“MODS” – Meeting Opposites, Divide Sum. Imagine two rock stars running toward a giant mod‑style scooter; they crash into each other exactly when the distance is divided by the sum of their running speeds.

12. Train Overtaking Another Train (Same Direction)

Mnemonic: “Snake Overtake Slowpoke – Length Sum / Relative Gap.”

Time to completely overtake = (L₁ + L₂) ÷ (V₁ – V₂).

Picture a giant snake overtaking a slower snake; it must cover both lengths, and the effective speed is the gap between them.

Acronym: SOS – L / R (Overtaking: Sum of Lengths divided by Relative speed).

13. Boats: Time for a Round Trip in a River

Mnemonic: “Down‑Up‑Down Trip is D/(B+S) + D/(B‑S).”

Remember it as the “DUST” formula – Downstream Upstream Sum Time.

Picture a dust cloud as the boat goes down and then up; the cloud settles when you add the two time pieces.

“DUST = Down‑time + Up‑time.”

14. Conversion: Minutes → Hours

Mnemonic: “Mice Hide from the 60‑foot cat.”

Minutes are mice, an hour is a huge cat that chases them; 60 mice make one cat hideout. So divide minutes by 60 to get hours.

“60 Mice = 1 Cat – dividing by 60 turns mice into cats (minutes into hours).”

Pick the ones that make you smile – the more outrageous, the better they stick!

Previous Year Questions

Q1. SSC CGL 2020 (Shift 2) — The Deceptive Stoppage *Two cyclists, X and Y, start together from A to B at 6 km/h and 8 km/h. Y stops for 15 minutes en route but still arrives 10 minutes before X. Find the distance AB.* Options: a) 6 km b) 16.5 km c) 10 km d) 8 km Step-by-Step
Show Solution
✅ Answer: (c).
💡 Speed ratio X:Y = 6:8 = 3:4. Time ratio is inverse, so X:Y = 4:3. Let X's time be 4t, Y's ideal time be 3t. Y actually takes 3t + 15 min (stoppage). But Y still reaches 10 min earlier than X. So, 4t − (3t + 15) = 10 → t = 25 minutes. X's actual time = 4t = 100 min = 100/60 hours. Distance = Speed × Time = 6 × (100/60) = 10 km.
Q2. SSC CGL 2020 (Shift 1) — The Head-Start Mirage *Two cars A and B leave Delhi at 8:30 a.m. and 9:00 a.m. for Shimla at 40 km/h and 50 km/h respectively. How far from Delhi will they be together?* Options: a) 200 km b) 45 km c) 5 km d) 100 km Step-by-Step
Show Solution
✅ Answer: (d).
💡 From 8:30 to 9:00, A travels alone at 40 km/h for 0.5 h → covers 20 km. Relative speed when both run same direction = 50 − 40 = 10 km/h. B must close a 20 km gap at 10 km/h → time to catch = 20/10 = 2 hours. In 2 hours, B travels 50 × 2 = 100 km from Delhi.
