Chapter Notes

Cubes & Dice

🧠
Paint a cube and cut it into small pieces — how many pieces will have 3 painted faces? Always 8 (the corners)! And on a die, which number is opposite 1? 6 (because opposite faces sum to 7). Cubes-and-dice questions test this spatial thinking.

Definition: This chapter has two parts — (1) Cube: counting painted faces when a painted cube is cut; (2) Dice: identifying the number opposite a face.

🧠 Recall: On a standard die, what do opposite faces sum to?

Part 1: Painted Cube

Paint all faces of a cube and cut it into n×n×n small cubes:

  • 3 painted faces (corners): always 8 (the 8 corners of the cube).
  • 2 painted faces (edges): 12 × (n − 2).
  • 1 painted face (face-centre): 6 × (n − 2)².
  • 0 painted faces (inner): (n − 2)³.

Example (n = 3): corners 8, edges 12×1 = 12, face-centre 6×1 = 6, inner 1³ = 1. Total = 27 ✅.

Part 2: Dice

  • Standard die: opposite faces always sum to 7 (1↔6, 2↔5, 3↔4).
  • From two positions: if two pictures of a die are given, find the number common to both, then rotate the neighbouring numbers to find the opposite face.
🧠 Recall: In a 3×3×3 cube, how many pieces have exactly 1 painted face?

The Examiner's Trap

⚠️ Trap 1 — forgetting the sum 7: On a standard die, the opposite number = 7 − the given number.

⚠️ Trap 2 — corners always 8: Whatever n is, the pieces with 3 painted faces are always 8.

⚠️ Trap 3 — remembering (n−2): The edge and inner counts use (n−2); do not forget it.

Review this after 2 days.

In Simple Words

Feynman Technique: Cubes & Dice in Simple Words

Tell your younger brother: "Imagine a chocolate cube with all its surfaces painted. Now cut it into 27 small pieces (3×3×3). The 8 corner pieces will have 3 painted surfaces (a corner shows from three sides!). The 1 piece hidden inside is completely unpainted. That is the whole cube game."

Corner = 3 painted, edge = 2 painted, face-centre = 1 painted, inner = 0 painted.

The Biggest Trick: The Dice "Magic of 7"

On a standard die, opposite faces always sum to 7:

  • 6 is opposite 1 (1+6=7)
  • 5 is opposite 2 (2+5=7)
  • 4 is opposite 3 (3+4=7)

So the opposite number = 7 − the given number. That's it!

Short Tricks

Short Tricks: Cubes & Dice

🪄
Trick: Memorise the four painted-cube formulas — 3 painted = 8 (corners); 2 painted = 12(n−2); 1 painted = 6(n−2)²; 0 painted = (n−2)³.
🪄
Trick: The corner pieces (3 painted faces) are always 8 — whatever n is.
🪄
Trick: Total small cubes = n³. As a check, the four counts should add up to n³.
🪄
Trick: On a die, the opposite number = 7 − the given number (standard die).
🪄
Trick: Remember the pairs — 1↔6, 2↔5, 3↔4 (each pair sums to 7).
🪄
Trick: In a two-position die, treat the number common to both as fixed, then rotate the rest.

Skip Strategy: Skip tangled two-die puzzles; do the direct painted-cube formula questions first.

💡Mnemonic: "Corner 3, edge 2, face 1, inner 0" and "the magic of 7 on a die".
