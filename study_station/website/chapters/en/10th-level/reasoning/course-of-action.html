Chapter Notes

Course of Action

🧠
Dengue suddenly spreads in a city. What should be done? "Run a cleanliness drive" — this is a correct course of action. But "evacuate the whole city" — this is too extreme. Course-of-action questions test choosing this 'right step'.

Definition: In these questions, a problem or situation (statement) is given, then some suggested courses of action. We must decide which action is logical and practical for solving the problem.

🧠 Recall: What matters most when choosing a course of action — solving the problem, or punishment?

The Golden Rule: Three Checks

A course of action is correct only if it is:

1. Directly related to the problem (it solves or reduces it).

2. Practical — it can actually be carried out.

3. Not extreme — not too harsh, impossible, or unreasonable.

Key Ideas

Solution-oriented vs Extreme

  • "Disease has spread → run a vaccination drive" (correct, solution-oriented).
  • "Disease has spread → jail everyone" (wrong, extreme).

Prevention vs Punishment

A good course of action often prevents the problem, not just punishes.

🧠 Recall: Should a "too harsh or impossible" action be considered correct?

The Examiner's Trap

⚠️ Trap 1 — extreme steps: Overly harsh actions like "shut everything down", "remove everyone" are usually not correct.

⚠️ Trap 2 — unrelated action: An action not directly related to the problem is not correct.

⚠️ Trap 3 — assuming both are correct: Sometimes both actions are correct, sometimes neither — don't rush.

Review this after 2 days.

In Simple Words

Feynman Technique: Course of Action in Simple Words

🧠
Tell your younger brother: "Suppose your room has too many mosquitoes. What's the right step? 'Put up a net, don't let water collect' — that's sensible. But 'demolish the whole room' — that's crazy! Course-of-action questions just ask this — which step is sensible."

A course of action is correct only if it is related to the problem, practical, and not extreme.

The Biggest Trick: Cut the "Extreme" Immediately

Any option with harsh words like "shut down completely", "remove everyone", "ban forever" — is usually wrong. Such steps are not practical in the real world.

A good course of action solves or prevents the problem, not just punishes.

Short Tricks

Short Tricks: Course of Action

🪄
Trick: Apply three checks — the action must be (1) related to the problem, (2) practical, (3) not extreme.
🪄
Trick: Actions with harsh words like "shut down completely", "remove everyone", "ban forever" are usually wrong.
🪄
Trick: Prevention/solution actions are usually correct; punishment-only actions are often incomplete.
🪄
Trick: Immediately cut any action not directly related to the problem.
🪄
Trick: Check each action separately — one may be correct, another wrong.
🪄
Trick: Think about practicality — can this step actually be carried out?

Skip Strategy: For a tangled two-action situation, run the "three checks" on each; don't rush.

💡Mnemonic: "Related, practical, moderate" — the three qualities of a correct action.
