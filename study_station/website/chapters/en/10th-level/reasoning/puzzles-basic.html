Chapter Notes

Puzzles — Basic

A building has 5 floors and 5 people live there — Ram, Shyam, Mohan, Sita, Gita. A few clues are given: "Ram lives on the topmost floor", "Sita lives immediately below Gita". From just these clues you must fill the whole building. That is a puzzle — building the full picture from scattered clues.

Definition: In puzzle questions, relationships between some persons/objects and their attributes (floor, day, colour, age, etc.) are given as clues, and we must work out the correct matching.

🧠 Recall: What is the biggest weapon in solving puzzles — memory, or making a table?

The Golden Rule: Make a Table

Draw a grid/table on paper. Persons on one side, attributes on the other. Then:

1. Fill the direct clues first — "Ram on top".

2. Cross out cells using negative clues — "Shyam is not on the first floor".

3. Fill remaining cells from relative clues ("above/below", "between").

Main Types

1. Floor Puzzle

Who lives on which floor — from above/below clues.

2. Scheduling / Day Puzzle

Whose task/birthday on which day — matching to days of the week.

3. Matching Puzzle

Person ↔ city ↔ colour ↔ liking — matching two or more attributes.

🧠 Recall: Why is it important to fill the definite clues first?

The Examiner's Trap

⚠️ Trap 1 — guessing too early: Do not guess before filling the definite clues; often only one solution is possible.

⚠️ Trap 2 — "immediately above/below" vs "above/below": "immediately above" = the very next floor; "above" = anywhere higher.

⚠️ Trap 3 — ignoring negative clues: Clues like "X is not on Monday" are just as important for crossing out cells.

Review this after 2 days.

In Simple Words

Feynman Technique: Puzzles in Simple Words

🧠
Tell your younger brother: "A game — three friends live on three floors. Ram on top, Mohan at the bottom. So where is Shyam? In the middle! See, from just two clues the third fell into place by itself."

That is all a puzzle is. Keep filling each clue into a table — the cell left over is the answer.

The Biggest Trick: Definite First, Then the Rest

Not all clues are equal:

  • "Ram on top" — a definite clue, fill it at once.
  • "Shyam not on floor 1" — negative, cross it out there.
  • "Sita immediately below Gita" — a pair, keep them together.

After filling the definite clues, the rest usually fall into place on their own.

Short Tricks

Short Tricks: Puzzles

🪄
Trick: Always solve by making a table/grid — don't keep it in your head.
🪄
Trick: Order of filling clues — first the definite (direct), then the negative (cross out cells), then the relative (above/below/between).
🪄
Trick: In a floor puzzle, always fix one direction (top to bottom, or bottom to top) for the table — no confusion.
🪄
Trick: Keep "immediately above/below" pairs together — they never separate.
🪄
Trick: The more cells you cross out with negative clues ("X not on Monday"), the faster you reach the solution.
🪄
Trick: At the end, re-check every clue — if any clue breaks, there is a mistake somewhere.

Skip Strategy: Big puzzles with 7+ variables take a lot of time; do the small (3-5 person) puzzles first.

💡Mnemonic: "Make a table, fill definite first" — the whole method of puzzles.
