Let’s Build Empathy — Why Does Average Feel Tricky?

Most learners confuse average with the “middle number.” They think adding a new value will move the average by an equal amount.

The truth is, average is a balancing act — and every new value must lift the total weight of the entire group, not just itself. This chapter will gently erase that confusion forever.

The Golden Formula (Smallest Possible Chunk)

Think of average as equal sharing:If 5 friends have 100 rupees and share equally, each gets 20. That 20 is the average.

The formula in words:Average = Total Sum ÷ Number of Items

Numbers version: A = S / NFrom this one simple formula, three superhero versions emerge:S = A × N (Find total if you know average and number)

N = S / A (Find how many items if you know total and average)

A = S / N (The original)

If you memorize only these three, you can solve 80% of all average questions.🧠 Cover the screen. Say the three forms out loud like a chant: “A = S/N, S = A×N, N = S/A.”Crack the Batting Puzzle (Step-by-Step Chunk)

Old situation: 10 innings, average 45.→ Old total runs = 10 × 45 = 450.

New situation after 11 innings: average wanted 50.→ New total runs needed = 11 × 50 = 550.

So the 11th innings must add: 550 – 450 = 100 runs.

This is called an inclusion problem. Always use:New value = (New Avg × New Count) – (Old Avg × Old Count).

Weighted Average — The Trap That Hurts

Imagine a class: 4 boys weigh 50 kg on average, 6 girls weigh 40 kg on average.

Many students do (50+40)/2 = 45. Wrong! That would be true only if boys and girls were equal in number.

Here the “weight” (number of people) is different.

Correct weighted average:= (4×50 + 6×40) / (4+6)= (200 + 240) / 10 = 440/10 = 44 kg.

Always keep the weights in the numerator and the sum of weights in the denominator. Never fall for the 50–50 trap.🧠 *In a factory, 10 workers earn ₹300 avg, 5 earn ₹500 avg. Find overall average. (

For even count, e.g., 11 to 20 → average = (11+20)/2 = 15.5.

Correcting Mistakes (Error Questions)

If a student wrote 56 instead of 65 while summing, and average came out 40 for 20 items:Wrong sum = 40 × 20 = 800.

New average = 809/20 = 40.45.

In Simple Words

Feynman Explains the Toughest Part of Average

🧠
Imagine Rahul loves cricket. He’s played 10 innings and his batting average is 45. Now he thinks, “If I score 50 runs in the next innings, my average will become 50, right?”Nope. That’s where almost everyone trips. The real answer is 100 runs. Why? Let me tell you a cookie story.

There are 10 kids sitting in a circle. Each kid has a pile of cookies, but they have different amounts. They decide to share equally. After sharing, each kid gets 45 cookies. That’s the average — the equal share.

🧠
Now, an 11th kid arrives. He doesn’t have any cookies yet. The group wants the new equal share to be 50 cookies for everybody. So what happens? The new kid can’t just bring 50 cookies for himself. If he brings 50, then the total cookies is (10 × 45) + 50 = 450 + 50 = 500. Divide by 11 kids, each gets 500 ÷ 11 ≈ 45.45 — not 50. The average barely budges! Why?

Because the new kid’s cookies have to be shared among everyone, including himself. The new equal share of 50 means all 11 kids need 50 cookies each — a total of 550 cookies. They only had 450 before. So the new kid must bring 100 cookies — not just his own 50, but an extra 50 to raise everyone else’s share from 45 to 50. He’s lifting the whole group.

Here’s the trick that sticks: The new kid must pay for the entire party, not just his own plate.

Whenever you add one new number and want to raise the average, the gap (new average – old average) has to be multiplied by the total number of items after adding.

Formul

Back: New kid’s contribution = (Desired new average × New total count) − (Old average × Old count).

So: Rahul’s innings = (50 × 11) − (45 × 10) = 550 − 450 = 100.

Now it’s your turn: A fruit-seller sold 4 days with an average profit of ₹30 per day. How much profit must he make on the fifth day so that the average profit becomes ₹40'hink: Old total = 4×30 = 120. New total needed for 5 days at ₹40 = 5×40 = 200. So fifth day profit = 200 − 120 = ₹80. He needs ₹80, not ₹40. That’s the party trick in action.

Short Tricks

Box 1: SAD Panda (मूल औसत सूत्र)

The SAD Panda sits under a bamboo tree, repeating “Sum, Amount, Divide!”Sum = सबको जोड़ोAmount = गिनती करोDivide = भाग दोPanda cries if you forget to Divide at the end. To make him happy, always do the D step last. Reverse magic: Multiply Average × Amount to get Sum — Panda hugs his bamboo.

Box 2: Triangle of Power (तीन सुनहरे रूप)

Draw a triangle:           S      A    NCover the one you want:• S = A × N (multiply bottom)• A = S / N (top ÷ bottom)• N = S / A (top ÷ other bottom)

Think of it as a “SAN” sandwich — S is the king, A and N are the soldiers.

Box 3: NINO the Rhino (समावेश / Inclusion)

When a new number charges into the group like a rhino, use NINO:New value = ( New Avg × New Count) – ( Old Avg × Old Count)

Picture NINO the Rhino stomping: “New total minus old total!” The rhino’s horn points at the new value.

Box 4: WALT the Walrus (भारित औसत / Weighted Average)

WALT the Walrus balances buckets on his tusks. Buckets are not equal size!Weighted Average = (Weight₁ × Avg₁ + Weight₂ × Avg₂) / (Weight₁ + Weight₂)

WALT says, “Weight each group, not just the average!” If you just take (Avg₁+Avg₂)/2, WALT will splash water on you.

Box 5: Sew the Sum (त्रुटि सुधार / Error Correction)

When a wrong number sneaks in, don’t panic — Sew the Sum first.

Correct Sum = Wrong Sum – Wrong Value + Right Value

Then divide. Imagine a tailor stitching the torn total: “Sew first, then divide.” Remember, the wrong thread is pulled out, right thread is sewn in.

Box 6: Fist-Last Half (क्रमागत संख्याएँ / Consecutive Numbers)

For consecutive numbers, make a fist with one hand (first number) and last finger (last number), then half it.

Average = (First + Last) / 2

Even count: half the sum of two middles. Odd count: middle number is average. Fist-last is so fast, no addition needed!Box 7: NOVA (एक नई प्रविष्टि का प्रभाव / Effect of One New Entry)

New Old Value Adjustment

New Average = Old Average + (New Value – Old Average) / New Total Count

Imagine a NOVA star exploding — the new value blasts the old average, and the fragments spread equally among everyone. NOVA = New Old Value Adjustment.

🧠
Box 8: Not Out? No Divide! (बल्लेबाज़ी औसत / Batting Average)

In cricket, if a batsman remains NOT OUT, that innings does NOT count in the denominator.

Total runs / Number of times dismissed (NOT OUT innings are NOT counted)

Think: “Not Out = No Divide for that innings!” The denominator only includes completed innings.

Box 9: The Scale Trick (प्रतिस्थापन / Replacement)

When a person is replaced, total change = Number of people × Change in average.

Picture a weighing scale: if average rises by 2 kg for 8 people, the new person must bring 16 kg extra.

New person weight = Old person weight + (Number of people × change). Scale tip!Box 10: Ally the Gator (मिश्रण / Allegation)

Ally the alligator crosses her arms and chomps the differences!Write two averages on ends, overall average in middle.

Subtract diagonally to get ratio.    High Avg        \Overall Avg        /    Low Avg

Ratio = (Overall – Low) : (High – Overall)

Ally says, “Cross and chomp, then simplify!”Box 11: Hippo’s Harmonic (दो समान दूरी की औसत चाल / Average Speed)

When distance is same, Hippo runs at speed a and speed b.

Average Speed = (2 × a × b) / (a + b)

This is “Harmonic Hippo” — two speeds, one harmonic mean. If you just average speeds, Hippo will sit on you.

Box 12: Copycat Chameleon (स्थिरांक संक्रियाएँ / Constant Operations)

A chameleon copies whatever you do to each number!• Multiply each by k → Average multiplies by k• Divide each by k → Average divides by k• Add k to each → Average adds k• Subtract k from each → Average subtracts k

Remember: “Whatever you do to each, the average does too. Copycat chameleon never forgets!”

Q1. [SBI PO 2020] Inclusion - The "Just Add the Difference" Trap The average score of a cricketer for 13 matches is 42 runs. If the average score for the first 5 matches is 54, what is his average score for the last 8 matches?
A 37
B 39
C 34.5
D 33.5 Step-by-Step
Show Solution
✅ Answer: (c) 34.5🧠 Trap & Hook: The trap here is the "just add the difference" fallacy. A student might incorrectly think (42+54)/2 or use a shortcut without calculating totals. The hook is the intuitive but wrong idea that the overall average is simply the midpoint of the sub-averages.
💡 Total runs for 13 matches: 13 × 42 = 546 runs. Total runs for the first 5 matches: 5 × 54 = 270 runs. Total runs for the last 8 matches: 546 - 270 = 276 runs. Average for the last 8 matches: 276 / 8 = 34.5 runs.✅ Correct

B 3 : 2
C 3 : 1
D 4 : 3 Step-by-Step
Show Solution

B 35

Show Solution

Q6. [SSC CPO 2019] Linear Equations in Disguise The average of ten numbers is 72. The average of the first four numbers is 69 and that of the next three numbers is 74. The 8th number is 6 more than the 9th number and 12 more than the 10th number. What is the average of the 8th and 9th numbers?
A 74
B 75
C 76
D 77 Step-by-Step
Show Solution
✅ Answer: (d) 77🧠 Trap & Hook: This question looks terrifyingly complex at first glance, triggering test anxiety. The hook is the "wordy problem" that scares students away. The trick is to break it down, step-by-step, applying the golden formula "Sum = Average × Number" to each known segment until the mystery is solved.
💡 Total sum of 10 numbers: 10 × 72 = 720. Sum of first 4 numbers: 4 × 69 = 276. Sum of next 3 numbers (5th to 7th): 3 × 74 = 222. The remaining sum for numbers 8, 9, and 10: 720 - (276 + 222) = 222. Let the 9th number be y. Then the 8th is y+6, and the 10th is y-6. Sum of 8th, 9th, 10th: (y+6) + y + (y-6) = 3y = 222. Therefore, y = 74. The 8th number is 80. Average of 8th and 9th: (80+74)/2 = 77.✅ Correct
Q7. [RRB NTPC 2019] Replacement - The "Arithmetic Shortcut" Trap The average weight of 15 oarsmen in a boat is increased by 1.6 kg when one of the crew, who weighs 42 kg, is replaced by a new man. Find the weight of the new man.
A 67 kg
B 65 kg
C 66 kg
D 43 kg Step-by-Step
Show Solution
✅ Answer: (c) 66 kg🧠 Trap & Hook: The direct shortcut is the trap itself; many students simply add the increased average (42 + 1.6) and get it wrong. The hook is the logical leap from an "average increase" to a "total increase," which is the crucial step.
💡 The increase in total weight is the increase in average multiplied by the number of oarsmen: 1.6 kg × 15 = 24 kg. This total increase of 24 kg is the difference between the new man's weight and the replaced man's weight. Therefore, New man's weight = Replaced man's weight + Total increase = 42 kg + 24 kg = 66 kg.✅ Correct

Q9. [SSC CGL 2023] Consecutive Numbers What is the product of B and D, if the average of four consecutive numbers A, B, C, and D is 49.5?
A 2499
B 2352
C 2450
D 2550 Step-by-Step
Show Solution
✅ Answer: (a) 2499🧠 Trap & Hook: The hidden assumption is that the count of numbers is even. If it were odd, the average would be the middle number itself. This question elegantly hooks students who blindly apply the generic consecutive number formula without paying attention to the count.

Q10. [SBI Clerk 2021] Batting Average - The "Not Out" Illusion A batsman has scored 450 runs in 10 completed innings. His batting average is 45. In the next innings, he scores 55 runs and remains not out. What is his new batting average?
A 45.9
B 48.6
C 50.5
D 50.0 Step-by-Step
Show Solution
✅ Answer: (c) 50.5🧠 Trap & Hook: The most diabolical trap in cricket-based averages.
