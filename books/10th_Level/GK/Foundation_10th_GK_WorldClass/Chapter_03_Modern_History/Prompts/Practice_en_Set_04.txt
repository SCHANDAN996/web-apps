📝 Practice Set 4 (Questions 76–100)

All Medium Difficulty

76. Which of the following princely states was annexed by Lord Dalhousie in 1856 on the grounds of misgovernance, rather than under the Doctrine of Lapse?
(a) Satara
(b) Jhansi
(c) Awadh
(d) Nagpur

Answer: (c) Awadh
Step-by-step: While Satara, Jhansi, and Nagpur were annexed through the Doctrine of Lapse, Awadh was annexed in 1856 on the pretext of misrule, an accusation that its Nawab Wajid Ali Shah was incompetent. This distinction is often blurred in the exam.
Source: UPSC Prelims 2012
🧪 Trap Type: Reverse Causation/Policy Mix‑up (Dalhousie used different annexation methods; not all were under the Doctrine of Lapse.)

77. Who among the following was the first Indian to qualify for the Indian Civil Service?
(a) Surendranath Banerjee
(b) Satyendranath Tagore
(c) Romesh Chunder Dutt
(d) Dadabhai Naoroji

Answer: (b) Satyendranath Tagore
Step-by-step: Satyendranath Tagore cleared the ICS examination in 1863, becoming the first Indian to do so. Surendranath Banerjee passed in 1869 but was later disqualified.
Source: Railway NTPC 2019

78. The Rowlatt Act (1919) is also known as:
(a) The Seditious Meetings Act
(b) The Black Act
(c) The Vernacular Press Act
(d) The Defence of India Act

Answer: (b) The Black Act
Step-by-step: The Rowlatt Act, which allowed imprisonment without trial, was vehemently opposed by Indian leaders and was nicknamed the Black Act.
Source: SSC CHSL 2020

79. With reference to the Government of India Act 1935, which of the following is correct?
(a) It introduced provincial autonomy for the first time.
(b) It abolished dyarchy at the centre.
(c) It provided for an all‑India federation that came into effect fully.
(d) It separated Burma from India.

Answer: (d) It separated Burma from India.
Step-by-step: The 1935 Act separated Burma from British India and also introduced provincial autonomy, abolished provincial dyarchy, and introduced dyarchy at the centre. The all‑India federation never materialised.
Source: UPSC Prelims 2018

80. The Hunter Commission was appointed by the British government to:
(a) Investigate the Chauri Chaura incident.
(b) Inquire into the Jallianwala Bagh massacre.
(c) Recommend constitutional reforms.
(d) Enquire into the Kakori conspiracy.

Answer: (b) Inquire into the Jallianwala Bagh massacre.
Step-by-step: In the wake of the Jallianwala Bagh tragedy, the British government formed the Hunter Commission to investigate the actions of General Dyer and the Punjab authorities.
Source: IBPS Clerk 2019

81. Which of the following movements was the first to be led by Mahatma Gandhi on an all‑India scale?
(a) Champaran Satyagraha
(b) Kheda Satyagraha
(c) Non‑Cooperation Movement
(d) Ahmedabad Mill Strike

Answer: (c) Non‑Cooperation Movement
Step-by-step: Gandhi’s earlier satyagrahas (Champaran, Kheda, Ahmedabad) were localised. The Non‑Cooperation Movement (1920‑22) was his first nationwide mass movement.
Source: SSC CGL 2017

82. The resolution on Fundamental Rights and National Economic Policy was passed at which Congress session?
(a) Lahore (1929)
(b) Karachi (1931)
(c) Haripura (1938)
(d) Faizpur (1936)

Answer: (b) Karachi (1931)
Step-by-step: The Karachi session, presided over by Sardar Vallabhbhai Patel, adopted the famous resolutions on Fundamental Rights and Economic Policy, a landmark in the nationalist agenda.
Source: UPSC Prelims 2014

83. The book “Poverty and Un‑British Rule in India” was authored by:
(a) Dadabhai Naoroji
(b) R. C. Dutt
(c) Mahatma Gandhi
(d) Gopal Krishna Gokhale

Answer: (a) Dadabhai Naoroji
Step-by-step: Naoroji’s seminal work systematised the “Drain of Wealth” theory, explaining how India’s resources were being expropriated by colonial rule.
Source: Banking 2018

84. Who was the Viceroy of India during the Partition of Bengal (1905)?
(a) Lord Curzon
(b) Lord Minto
(c) Lord Hardinge
(d) Lord Elgin

Answer: (a) Lord Curzon
Step-by-step: Lord Curzon, as Viceroy, announced the partition of Bengal in July 1905, which took effect on 16 October 1905, sparking the Swadeshi Movement.
Source: SSC MTS 2019

85. The Vernacular Press Act was passed by which Viceroy?
(a) Lord Lytton
(b) Lord Ripon
(c) Lord Dufferin
(d) Lord Lansdowne

Answer: (a) Lord Lytton
Step-by-step: Lytton passed the Vernacular Press Act in 1878 to curb the growing criticism of the British government in the Indian‑language press.
Source: Railway Group D 2018

86. The Ahmedabad Mill Strike (1918) was primarily related to:
(a) Increase in wages
(b) Plague bonus
(c) Reduction in working hours
(d) Demand for trade union rights

Answer: (b) Plague bonus
Step-by-step: Gandhi intervened in the Ahmedabad textile mill strike demanding a “plague bonus” of 35% for workers who stayed back during the plague, eventually settling for a compromise.
Source: State PCS 2017

87. Consider the following pairs:

Bal Gangadhar Tilak  – Swaraj Party

C. R. Das     – Home Rule League

Annie Besant    – Theosophical Society

Motilal Nehru    – Indian National Army

Which of the pairs given above is/are correctly matched?
(a) 1 and 2 only
(b) 3 only
(c) 2 and 4 only
(d) 3 and 4 only

Answer: (b) 3 only
Step-by-step: Tilak was associated with the Home Rule League, not the Swaraj Party (founded after his death). C. R. Das co‑founded the Swaraj Party. Motilal Nehru was a key figure in the Swaraj Party and the Nehru Report; INA was Subhas Chandra Bose. Only pair 3 is correct.
Source: SSC CGL 2021
🧪 Trap Type: Similar Name/Association Trap (examiners often mix up the organisations led by prominent leaders of the same era.)

88. Who founded the Ramakrishna Mission in 1897?
(a) Ramakrishna Paramahamsa
(b) Swami Vivekananda
(c) Sister Nivedita
(d) Keshab Chandra Sen

Answer: (b) Swami Vivekananda
Step-by-step: The mission was founded by Swami Vivekananda to propagate the teachings of his guru Ramakrishna Paramahamsa and engage in social service. Ramakrishna himself did not found the organisation.
Source: Railway NTPC 2019
🧪 Trap Type: Nearly True Statement (students often attribute the founding to Ramakrishna, given the name.)

89. The Chittagong Armoury Raid (1930) was led by:
(a) Bhagat Singh
(b) Surya Sen
(c) Chandrashekhar Azad
(d) Batukeshwar Dutt

Answer: (b) Surya Sen
Step-by-step: Masterda Surya Sen, along with a band of revolutionaries including women like Pritilata Waddedar, carried out the raid on the police armoury.
Source: IBPS PO 2020

90. The Shimla Conference of 1945 was held to discuss:
(a) The Cripps Mission proposals
(b) The Wavell Plan
(c) The Mountbatten Plan
(d) The Cabinet Mission Plan

Answer: (b) The Wavell Plan
Step-by-step: Lord Wavell convened the Shimla Conference in June 1945 to secure acceptance of the Wavell Plan for an interim government with equal representation for high‑caste Hindus and Muslims. It failed.
Source: SSC CPO 2019

91. Who was the first Indian Governor‑General of independent India?
(a) Lord Mountbatten
(b) C. Rajagopalachari
(c) Dr. Rajendra Prasad
(d) Dr. S. Radhakrishnan

Answer: (b) C. Rajagopalachari
Step-by-step: Mountbatten was the first Governor‑General of the Dominion of India (Aug 1947‑June 1948). C. Rajagopalachari succeeded him as the first and last Indian Governor‑General, serving until India became a republic in 1950.
Source: SSC CGL 2020

92. The Ilbert Bill controversy (1883‑84) occurred during the viceroyalty of:
(a) Lord Lytton
(b) Lord Ripon
(c) Lord Dufferin
(d) Lord Curzon

Answer: (b) Lord Ripon
Step-by-step: Ripon’s Ilbert Bill sought to allow Indian judges to try European offenders, provoking a fierce backlash from the European community and leading to a compromise.
Source: UPSC Prelims 2015

93. Which of the following events occurred earliest?
(a) Formation of the Muslim League
(b) Partition of Bengal
(c) Surat split of the Congress
(d) Passage of the Morley‑Minto Reforms

Answer: (b) Partition of Bengal (1905)
Step-by-step: Partition of Bengal – 1905, Muslim League – 1906, Surat split – 1907, Morley‑Minto Reforms – 1909. Hence, 1905 is the earliest.
Source: Banking 2019

94. The slogan “Swaraj is my birthright and I shall have it” was popularised during:
(a) The Home Rule Movement
(b) The Non‑Cooperation Movement
(c) The Swadeshi Movement
(d) The Quit India Movement

Answer: (c) The Swadeshi Movement
Step-by-step: Bal Gangadhar Tilak gave this stirring slogan during the Swadeshi Movement that followed the Partition of Bengal, asserting the demand for self‑rule.
Source: SSC CHSL 2019

95. Who among the following presided over the Lahore Session of the Indian National Congress (1929) that declared Purna Swaraj?
(a) Mahatma Gandhi
(b) Jawaharlal Nehru
(c) Sardar Vallabhbhai Patel
(d) Subhas Chandra Bose

Answer: (b) Jawaharlal Nehru
Step-by-step: Nehru was the president of the Lahore session, which passed the historic Purna Swaraj resolution on 19 December 1929.
Source: SSC CGL 2018

96. The “Red Shirts” movement was launched in:
(a) Bengal
(b) Punjab
(c) North‑West Frontier Province
(d) Bihar

Answer: (c) North‑West Frontier Province
Step-by-step: Khan Abdul Ghaffar Khan (Badshah Khan) organised the Khudai Khidmatgars, also called Red Shirts, in the NWFP to support the national movement through non‑violence.
Source: UPSC Prelims 2017

97. The doctrine of “Subsidiary Alliance” was introduced by which Governor‑General?
(a) Lord Cornwallis
(b) Lord Wellesley
(c) Lord Hastings
(d) Lord Dalhousie

Answer: (b) Lord Wellesley
Step-by-step: Wellesley used the Subsidiary Alliance system to bring Indian princely states under British paramountcy without direct annexation.
Source: Railway NTPC 2018

98. The Indian National Army (INA) Memorial is located at:
(a) Singapore
(b) Moirang, Manipur
(c) Kolkata
(d) New Delhi

Answer: (b) Moirang, Manipur
Step-by-step: The INA hoisted the Indian tricolour for the first time on Indian soil at Moirang in Manipur in April 1944, and a memorial exists there.
Source: State PCS 2020

99. Who among the following was known as “Deshbandhu”?
(a) C. R. Das
(b) Bipin Chandra Pal
(c) Motilal Nehru
(d) Lala Lajpat Rai

Answer: (a) C. R. Das
Step-by-step: Chitta Ranjan Das, a leader of the Swaraj Party, was fondly called Deshbandhu (Friend of the Nation).
Source: SSC MTS 2020

100. Which of the following statements is incorrect regarding the Revolt of 1857?
(a) Mangal Pandey was executed at Barrackpore.
(b) Bahadur Shah Zafar was proclaimed the leader of the revolt in Delhi.
(c) Rani Lakshmibai fought against the British and died at Gwalior.
(d) The revolt led to the immediate grant of Dominion Status to India.

Answer: (d) The revolt led to the immediate grant of Dominion Status to India.
Step-by-step: The revolt resulted in the end of Company rule and the direct takeover by the Crown (1858), not Dominion Status. Dominion Status was granted only in 1947.
Source: SSC CPO 2019
🧪 Trap Type: Nearly True Statement (options (a)‑(c) are historically correct, but (d) is a glaring anachronism that can be missed if the student does not read carefully.)
