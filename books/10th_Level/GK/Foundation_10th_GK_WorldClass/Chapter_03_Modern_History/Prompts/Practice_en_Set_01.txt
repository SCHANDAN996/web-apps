📝 Practice Set 1 (Questions 1–25)

Easy (Q1–Q20)

1. The Battle of Plassey was fought in which year?
(a) 1756 (b) 1757 (c) 1764 (d) 1761
Answer: (b) 1757
Step-by-step: Plassey (1757) gave the East India Company a political foothold in Bengal.
Source: SSC CGL 2018

2. Who was the Governor-General of India when the Revolt of 1857 broke out?
(a) Lord Canning (b) Lord Dalhousie (c) Lord Wellesley (d) Lord Cornwallis
Answer: (a) Lord Canning
Step-by-step: Canning served from 1856 to 1862; the revolt took place in 1857.
Source: Railway NTPC 2019

3. The Indian National Congress was founded in the year:
(a) 1885 (b) 1905 (c) 1857 (d) 1919
Answer: (a) 1885
Step-by-step: INC was founded in 1885 with its first session at Bombay under W.C. Bonnerjee.
Source: IBPS Clerk 2017

4. Who gave the slogan “Do or Die”?
(a) Subhas Chandra Bose (b) Mahatma Gandhi (c) Jawaharlal Nehru (d) Bal Gangadhar Tilak
Answer: (b) Mahatma Gandhi
Step-by-step: Gandhi launched the Quit India Movement on 8 August 1942 with the slogan “Do or Die.”
Source: SSC CHSL 2019

5. The Doctrine of Lapse was introduced by:
(a) Lord Wellesley (b) Lord Dalhousie (c) Lord Cornwallis (d) Lord Hastings
Answer: (b) Lord Dalhousie
Step-by-step: Dalhousie (1848–1856) annexed Satara, Jhansi, Nagpur etc. under this policy.
Source: UPSC Prelims 2015

6. The first session of the Indian National Congress was held at:
(a) Calcutta (b) Bombay (c) Madras (d) Delhi
Answer: (b) Bombay
Step-by-step: Held in December 1885 at Gokuldas Tejpal Sanskrit College, Bombay.
Source: SSC CGL 2020

7. Who among the following founded the Arya Samaj?
(a) Raja Ram Mohan Roy (b) Swami Dayanand Saraswati (c) Swami Vivekananda (d) Jyotiba Phule
Answer: (b) Swami Dayanand Saraswati
Step-by-step: Arya Samaj (1875) was founded by Dayanand with the slogan “Back to the Vedas.”
Source: Railway Group D 2018

8. The Jallianwala Bagh massacre took place in the year:
(a) 1919 (b) 1920 (c) 1921 (d) 1918
Answer: (a) 1919
Step-by-step: On 13 April 1919, General Dyer ordered firing on an unarmed gathering.
Source: SSC MTS 2019

9. “Purna Swaraj” was declared at which Congress session?
(a) Calcutta (1928) (b) Lahore (1929) (c) Karachi (1931) (d) Haripura (1938)
Answer: (b) Lahore (1929)
Step-by-step: Under Jawaharlal Nehru’s presidency, the Congress declared complete independence as the goal.
Source: UPSC Prelims 2016

10. The Civil Disobedience Movement started with the Dandi March in:
(a) 1920 (b) 1930 (c) 1942 (d) 1922
Answer: (b) 1930
Step-by-step: Gandhi began the march from Sabarmati Ashram to Dandi on 12 March 1930 to break the salt law.
Source: IBPS PO 2019

11. The Cabinet Mission came to India in:
(a) 1942 (b) 1945 (c) 1946 (d) 1947
Answer: (c) 1946
Step-by-step: The Cabinet Mission (1946) proposed a plan for transfer of power and a federal structure.
Source: SSC CPO 2018

12. Who was the Viceroy of India when the Non‑Cooperation Movement was launched?
(a) Lord Chelmsford (b) Lord Irwin (c) Lord Reading (d) Lord Linlithgow
Answer: (a) Lord Chelmsford
Step-by-step: The movement was started in 1920 during Chelmsford’s tenure (1916–1921).
Source: State PCS 2017

13. The Home Rule Movement was started by:
(a) Bal Gangadhar Tilak and Annie Besant (b) Annie Besant and Gokhale (c) Tilak and Lajpat Rai (d) Gokhale and Tilak
Answer: (a) Bal Gangadhar Tilak and Annie Besant
Step-by-step: Tilak started the Home Rule League in April 1916; Besant started hers in September 1916.
Source: SSC CHSL 2019

14. The famous Kakori Train Robbery (1925) was associated with:
(a) Chittagong Armoury Raid (b) Hindustan Republican Association (c) Ghadar Party (d) Indian National Army
Answer: (b) Hindustan Republican Association (HRA)
Step-by-step: HRA members, including Ram Prasad Bismil and Ashfaqulla Khan, carried out the Kakori robbery.
Source: Railway NTPC 2019

15. The British officer who ordered the firing at Jallianwala Bagh was:
(a) General Dyer (b) Lord Curzon (c) Michael O’Dwyer (d) Lord Irwin
Answer: (a) General Dyer
Step-by-step: Reginald Dyer was the acting Brigadier‑General; O’Dwyer was the Lieutenant‑Governor of Punjab.
Source: Banking 2020

16. The Partition of Bengal (1905) was ordered by:
(a) Lord Curzon (b) Lord Hardinge (c) Lord Minto (d) Lord Chelmsford
Answer: (a) Lord Curzon
Step-by-step: Curzon partitioned Bengal on 16 October 1905, sparking the Swadeshi Movement.
Source: SSC CGL 2018

17. The Indian Independence Act was passed in:
(a) June 1947 (b) July 1947 (c) August 1947 (d) September 1947
Answer: (b) July 1947 (specifically, 18 July 1947)
Step-by-step: The Act received royal assent on 18 July 1947 and led to the creation of two dominions on 15 August.
Source: UPSC 2014

18. Who said “Swaraj is my birthright and I shall have it”?
(a) Lala Lajpat Rai (b) Bal Gangadhar Tilak (c) Bipin Chandra Pal (d) Aurobindo Ghosh
Answer: (b) Bal Gangadhar Tilak
Step-by-step: Tilak made this famous assertion during the Swadeshi movement.
Source: SSC MTS 2020

19. The Regulating Act of 1773 was passed during the governor‑generalship of:
(a) Robert Clive (b) Warren Hastings (c) Lord Cornwallis (d) Wellesley
Answer: (b) Warren Hastings
Step-by-step: The Act made Hastings the first Governor‑General of Bengal.
Source: UPSC 2013

20. The “Red Fort Trials” were held for the officers of:
(a) Indian National Army (b) Royal Indian Navy (c) Ghadar Party (d) HSRA
Answer: (a) Indian National Army
Step-by-step: The INA trials of 1945–46 at the Red Fort created widespread nationalist sympathy.
Source: Banking 2019

Medium (Q21–Q25)

21. Which of the following statements regarding the Morley‑Minto Reforms (1909) is correct?
(a) They introduced separate electorates for the Depressed Classes.
(b) They provided for a system of Dyarchy in the provinces.
(c) They introduced separate electorates for Muslims.
(d) They allowed Indians to enter the Central Legislative Assembly for the first time.

Answer: (c) They introduced separate electorates for Muslims.
Step-by-step: Option (a) is wrong (Depressed Classes got separate electorates under the Communal Award in 1932, later modified by the Poona Pact). Dyarchy (b) was introduced by the 1919 Act. Indians had been nominated to legislative councils earlier; the 1909 Act allowed election for the first time but (d) is misleading. Thus (c) is correct.
Source: UPSC Prelims 2015
🧪 Trap Type: Nearly True Statement (options (a) and (d) appear partially correct; the examiner tests precise knowledge of which community got separate electorates first.)

22. Arrange the following events in the correct chronological order:

Chauri Chaura incident

Gandhi‑Irwin Pact

Simon Commission

Jallianwala Bagh massacre
Codes:
(a) 4 → 1 → 3 → 2
(b) 1 → 4 → 3 → 2
(c) 4 → 3 → 1 → 2
(d) 3 → 4 → 1 → 2

Answer: (a) 4 (1919) → 1 (1922) → 3 (1928) → 2 (1931)
Step-by-step: Jallianwala (1919), Non‑Cooperation Movement started in 1920, Chauri Chaura occurred in Feb 1922 causing withdrawal, Simon Commission arrived in 1928, Gandhi‑Irwin Pact signed in 1931.
Source: SSC CGL 2019
🧪 Trap Type: Chronological Confusion (common error is swapping Chauri Chaura and Simon Commission or placing Jallianwala after Chauri Chaura).

23. The term “Drain of Wealth” was popularised by:
(a) Mahatma Gandhi (b) Dadabhai Naoroji (c) Gopal Krishna Gokhale (d) R. C. Dutt
Answer: (b) Dadabhai Naoroji
Step-by-step: Naoroji’s book “Poverty and Un‑British Rule in India” (1901) systematically argued that a large portion of India’s wealth was being drained to England. While R. C. Dutt also wrote on economic issues, the phrase is most associated with Naoroji.
Source: UPSC 2012

24. Which of the following is not correctly matched?
(a) Swadeshi Movement – Partition of Bengal
(b) Home Rule Movement – Annie Besant
(c) Quit India Movement – Subhas Chandra Bose
(d) Civil Disobedience Movement – Dandi March

Answer: (c) Quit India Movement – Subhas Chandra Bose.
Step-by-step: The Quit India Movement (1942) was launched by Mahatma Gandhi; Subhas Chandra Bose led the INA and gave the call “Dilli Chalo.”
Source: Railway Group D 2020
🧪 Trap Type: Similar Name/Association Trap (students sometimes confuse Bose’s militant nationalism with the Quit India movement).

25. The Poona Pact (1932) dealt with:
(a) Separate electorates for Muslims.
(b) Reservation of seats for the Depressed Classes in joint electorates.
(c) Partition of Bengal.
(d) Acceptance of the Cripps Mission proposals.

Answer: (b) Reservation of seats for the Depressed Classes in joint electorates.
Step-by-step: The Communal Award (1932) gave the Depressed Classes separate electorates. Gandhi’s fast led to the Poona Pact, which replaced separate electorates with reserved seats in joint electorates.
Source: SSC CGL 2017
🧪 Trap Type: Nearly True Statement (option (a) lures those who incorrectly associate “separate electorates” with the Poona Pact; the Pact actually modified the separate electorate provision.)
