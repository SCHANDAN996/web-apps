🪄 Memory Hooks & Short Tricks

Mnemonic 1: Borrowed Features – “A BIG CUP of Tea”
A – America (Fundamental Rights, Judicial Review)
B – Britain (Parliamentary system, Rule of Law)
I – Ireland (DPSP)
G – Germany (Emergency provisions, suspension of FRs)
C – Canada (Federation with strong centre, Residuary powers)
U – USSR (Fundamental Duties)
P – Australia (Concurrent List, Freedom of Trade)
Plus Japan (Procedure Established by Law).
Image: A giant teacup labelled “Indian Constitution” with these flags floating in it like tea bags!

Mnemonic 2: 6 Fundamental Rights – “Every Frog In Rainy Creek Croaks”
Equality
Freedom
Individual’s right against Exploitation
Religious freedom
Cultural & educational rights
Constitutional remedies
Frog = Fundamental Rights. Visualise six frogs croaking in a rainy creek!

Mnemonic 3: DPSP Classification – “SGL: Social, Gandhian, Liberal”
Socialist (Art. 39, etc.)
Gandhian (Art. 40, 43)
Liberal-Intellectual (Art. 44, 50)
Think SGL = “Single” – these three are the single family of DPSP ideas.

Mnemonic 4: Writs – “HB MP CQ” (Hindi film style)
Habeas Corpus – “Have the body” (body present)
Mandamus – “We command” (order to do duty)
Prohibition – “Stop it” (lower court stop)
Certiorari – “To be certified” (quash order)
Quo Warranto – “By what authority?”
Imagine a Bollywood hero named “HB MP CQ” who forces corrupt officers to present the person (Habeas Corpus) or stop proceedings (Prohibition).

Mnemonic 5: President’s Election – “PREM Members”
P – Parliament (elected)
R – Rajya Sabha (elected)
E – State Assemblies (Elected)
M – Members
“PREM” reminds: only elected members love (prem) the President? Yes, only elected!

Mnemonic 6: Vice‑President’s Election – “VIP = All PMs”
Vice‑President elected by All Parliament Members (elected + nominated).
“VIP” needs all Parliamentary Members’ approval.

Mnemonic 7: Constitutional Bodies – “E-Cup CAG Fin” (coffee time)
Election Commission – 324
Cup – UPSC (U sounds like “cup”) – 315

Mnemonic 8: Emergency Articles – “National 352, President 356, Financial 360”
National Emergency: 352 (2 looks like a swan – nation under attack, swan cries)
President’s Rule: 356 (6 looks like a monkey with a crown – state under monkey rule)
Financial Emergency: 360 (360° circle – economy spinning out)
Story: A swan (352) flies over, then a monkey king (356) takes over the state, and the whole economy spins 360°!

Imagine 22 cricket balls, later upgraded to 25 rupees. Original 22 parts, now 25.

Mnemonic 10: Original Schedules – “8, Now 12” (Eat 8, now 12)
Eat 8 chocolates, but now you want 12. Schedules: 8 originally, now 12.

Mnemonic 11: Supreme Court Strength – “34 Judges, CJI plus 33”
Think “34” as a speed limit. 1 Chief Justice and 33 others – like 33 bees following the queen bee.

Mnemonic 12: Retirement Ages – “65 Supreme, 62 High”
Supreme Court judge retires at 65; High Court at 62.
Supreme = Sixty-five. High = 62 – just remember “High 62”. Or “SC=65 (SIX-five), HC=62 (SIX-two)”.*

Mnemonic 13: 42nd vs 44th Amendments – “42 to Preamble, 44 to Property”
42nd Amendment: added words to Preamble (Socialist, Secular, Integrity) and Fundamental Duties.
44th Amendment: deleted Right to Property from Fundamental Rights.
*Think “42” is a couple (2 parents) adding words to the Preamble; “44” is double death (4+4) to property right.*

Mnemonic 14: Fundamental Duties Count – “42 gave 10, 86 gave 1 more”
42 (1976) → 10 duties; 86 (2002) → 11th duty.
42 is like a father (10 duties), and 86 is like a grandfather adding one more chore.

Mnemonic 15: Article 32 Heart & Soul – “Heart has 4 chambers, 32 is 4 times 8”
Heart has 4 chambers, 32 = 4×8. “Article 32 is heart and soul” – the heartbeat of the Constitution. Associate 32 with a heart shape.

🏃 Skip Strategy

Multi‑Name or Multi‑Year Confusion: If a question asks “Which of the following pairs (person–post) is correctly matched?” and you’re unsure about even one option among four, skip immediately. The examiner deliberately places similar names (Ambedkar–President of Constituent Assembly, etc.). A guess will likely fall into the trap.

“All of the above” with DPSP or Emergency: When a statement claims “All Fundamental Rights are suspended during National Emergency” or “All DPSPs are justiciable,” and you spot an extreme word like “all” or “never”, skip if you can’t immediately identify the exception. These are overgeneralization traps — extreme options are rarely correct.

Complex Article‑Number Questions: If a question lists multiple article numbers (e.g., “Article 32, 226, 136, 143, 227”) and asks which one empowers the Supreme Court to grant special leave to appeal, but you mix up their purposes, skip without spending time. These numerical lists are memory-intensive; skipping saves time for factual but easier questions on structure or functions.
