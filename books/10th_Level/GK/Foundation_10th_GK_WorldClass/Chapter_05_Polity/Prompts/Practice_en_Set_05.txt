Q101. Which one of the following is not a feature of the parliamentary system of government in India?
(a) Collective responsibility of the Council of Ministers to the Legislature
(b) Leadership of the Prime Minister
(c) Separation of powers between the executive and the legislature
(d) Presence of a nominal and real executive
Answer: (c) Separation of powers between the executive and the legislature
Solution: The parliamentary system in India is based on the fusion of powers between the executive and the legislature, not strict separation. The Council of Ministers is drawn from Parliament and is collectively responsible to it. Separation of powers is a feature of the presidential system (e.g., USA).
Source: UPSC Prelims 2017
Trap: Almost Correct Statement — option (c) looks plausible because India has some separation under the basic structure, but the defining feature of the parliamentary system is the fusion, not separation.

Q102. The President of India can return an ordinary bill for reconsideration of Parliament:
(a) Only once
(b) Any number of times
(c) Twice
(d) Only for Money Bills
Answer: (a) Only once
Solution: Under Article 111, if a Bill (other than a Money Bill) is presented to the President, he may return it to Parliament for reconsideration. If Parliament passes it again with or without amendments, the President must give his assent. He can return it only once.
Source: SSC CGL 2020

Q103. The concept of ‘Judicial Review’ in India is derived from the Constitution of:
(a) United Kingdom
(b) United States of America
(c) Canada
(d) Ireland
Answer: (b) United States of America
Solution: The power of the judiciary to review legislative and executive actions and declare them void if they violate the Constitution is borrowed from the US Constitution. The UK does not have judicial review in the same sense due to parliamentary sovereignty.
Source: UPSC Prelims 2016

Q104. Which Article of the Constitution gives special powers to the Supreme Court to do complete justice in any cause or matter pending before it?
(a) Article 141
(b) Article 142
(c) Article 143
(d) Article 144
Answer: (b) Article 142
Solution: Article 142(1) empowers the Supreme Court to pass such decree or order as is necessary for doing complete justice in any cause or matter pending before it. This is a unique power not available to any other court.
Source: UPSC Prelims 2019

Q105. The 42nd Amendment to the Constitution is significant because it:
(a) Deleted the Right to Property
(b) Added the words ‘Socialist’ and ‘Secular’ to the Preamble
(c) Lowered the voting age to 18 years
(d) Introduced the Panchayati Raj system
Answer: (b) Added the words ‘Socialist’ and ‘Secular’ to the Preamble
Solution: The 42nd Amendment (1976) added the words ‘Socialist’, ‘Secular’, and ‘Integrity’ to the Preamble and also added Fundamental Duties. Deletion of Right to Property was by 44th Amendment; lowering voting age by 61st Amendment; Panchayati Raj by 73rd Amendment.
Source: SSC CHSL 2021
Trap: Chronological Confusion — mixing amendments and their effects.

Q106. A person is eligible to contest the election of the President of India if he/she has completed:
(a) 25 years of age
(b) 30 years of age
(c) 35 years of age
(d) 40 years of age
Answer: (c) 35 years of age
Solution: Article 58 lays down the qualifications: citizen of India, completed 35 years of age, and qualified to be a member of the Lok Sabha.
Source: Railway NTPC 2020

Q107. Which of the following is a correct statement about the Rajya Sabha?
(a) It is dissolved every five years.
(b) The Vice‑President is an elected member of the Rajya Sabha.
(c) The term of a member of Rajya Sabha is six years.
(d) Members of Rajya Sabha are directly elected by the people.
Answer: (c) The term of a member of Rajya Sabha is six years.
Solution: Rajya Sabha is a permanent house, not dissolved. One-third members retire every two years, each member has a six-year term. The Vice‑President is the ex-officio Chairman, not a member. Members are elected indirectly by state legislative assemblies.
Source: UPSC Prelims 2018

Q108. The power to increase the number of judges in the Supreme Court is vested in:
(a) The President of India
(b) The Parliament
(c) The Chief Justice of India
(d) The Law Commission of India
Answer: (b) The Parliament
Solution: Parliament may by law increase the number of judges of the Supreme Court, as per Article 124(1).
Source: SSC CGL 2022

Q109. Which of the following is not a ground for reasonable restriction on the freedom of speech and expression under Article 19(2)?
(a) Security of the State
(b) Public order
(c) Public health
(d) Defamation
Answer: (c) Public health
Source: Article 19(2) lists eight grounds: security of the State, friendly relations with foreign States, public order, decency or morality, contempt of court, defamation, incitement to an offence, sovereignty and integrity of India. ‘Public health’ is not a ground, though it is a ground for restricting freedom of trade and profession under Article 19(6).
Source: UPSC Prelims 2021
Trap: Almost Correct Statement — mixing grounds from different clauses of Article 19.

Q110. The Chief Minister of a State is appointed by the:
(a) President of India
(b) Governor of the State
(c) Prime Minister
(d) Chief Justice of the High Court
Answer: (b) Governor of the State
Solution: Article 164(1) provides that the Chief Minister shall be appointed by the Governor. The other ministers are also appointed by the Governor on the advice of the Chief Minister.
Source: SSC CHSL 2019

Q111. The system of Public Interest Litigation (PIL) in India was introduced by:
(a) The Constitution
(b) Judicial activism
(c) A statute of Parliament
(d) A Presidential order
Answer: (b) Judicial activism
Solution: PIL is not mentioned in the Constitution or any statute; it was developed by the Supreme Court through judicial activism in the 1980s (e.g., Hussainara Khatoon case) to provide access to justice for the poor and marginalised.
Source: UPSC Prelims 2017

Q112. Which of the following provisions can be amended by a simple majority of Parliament?
(a) Fundamental Rights
(b) Directive Principles of State Policy
(c) Admission of new States into the Union
(d) Election of the President
Answer: (c) Admission of new States into the Union
Solution: Article 2 and Article 3 (admission/establishment and reorganisation of States) can be amended by a simple majority of Parliament. Other provisions require special majority or special majority plus state ratification.
Source: UPSC Prelims 2018

Q113. The salary of the Comptroller and Auditor General (CAG) is charged on the:
(a) Consolidated Fund of India
(b) Public Account of India
(c) Contingency Fund of India
(d) Consolidated Fund of the State
Answer: (a) Consolidated Fund of India
Solution: Article 148(6) provides that the salary and other conditions of service of the CAG shall be determined by Parliament and charged on the Consolidated Fund of India.
Source: SSC CGL 2021

Q114. Which part of the Constitution deals with citizenship?
(a) Part I
(b) Part II
(c) Part III
(d) Part IV
Answer: (b) Part II
Solution: Part II (Articles 5–11) deals with citizenship at the commencement of the Constitution. Parliament has also enacted the Citizenship Act, 1955.
Source: SSC CHSL 2020

Q115. The provision for the protection and improvement of environment and safeguarding of forests and wildlife is a:
(a) Fundamental Right
(b) Directive Principle of State Policy
(c) Fundamental Duty
(d) Both (b) and (c)
Answer: (d) Both (b) and (c)
Solution: Article 48A (DPSP) directs the State to protect and improve the environment, and Article 51A(g) (Fundamental Duty) imposes a duty on every citizen to protect the natural environment including forests, lakes, rivers, and wildlife.
Source: UPSC Prelims 2016

Q116. The resolution for removing the Vice‑President of India can be moved only in:
(a) Lok Sabha
(b) Rajya Sabha
(c) Either House of Parliament
(d) Joint sitting of Parliament
Answer: (b) Rajya Sabha
Solution: Article 67(b) provides that the Vice‑President may be removed from office by a resolution of the Council of States (Rajya Sabha) passed by an effective majority and agreed to by the House of the People (Lok Sabha). The resolution must be moved only in Rajya Sabha with at least 14 days’ notice.
Source: UPSC Prelims 2020

Q117. The original Constitution of India had how many Schedules?
(a) 7
(b) 8
(c) 10
(d) 12
Answer: (b) 8
Solution: The original Constitution had 8 Schedules. Currently, there are 12 Schedules (added by various amendments).
Source: SSC CGL 2019

Q118. The Attorney General of India is appointed by the:
(a) President
(b) Prime Minister
(c) Chief Justice of India
(d) Parliament
Answer: (a) President
Source: Article 76(1) states that the President shall appoint a person who is qualified to be appointed a Judge of the Supreme Court to be Attorney General for India.
Source: Railway NTPC 2021

Q119. In which year was the first amendment to the Constitution made?
(a) 1950
(b) 1951
(c) 1952
(d) 1956
Answer: (b) 1951
Solution: The Constitution (First Amendment) Act, 1951, was enacted to address issues like zamindari abolition, and added the Ninth Schedule, among other changes.

Q120. Which of the following emergencies has never been proclaimed in India?
(a) National Emergency
(b) State Emergency (President’s Rule)
(c) Financial Emergency
(d) All of the above have been proclaimed
Answer: (c) Financial Emergency
Source: National Emergency has been proclaimed three times (1962, 1971, 1975). President’s Rule has been imposed many times. Financial Emergency under Article 360 has never been proclaimed.
Source: UPSC Prelims 2019

Hard Questions (121–125)

Q121. Consider the following statements about the Preamble of the Constitution:

It is a source of power to the legislature.

It is a part of the Constitution.

It can be amended.

It is justiciable.
Which of the above statements are correct?
(a) 1 and 2 only
(b) 2 and 3 only
(c) 1, 2 and 3
(d) 2, 3 and 4
Answer: (b) 2 and 3 only
Solution:

Statement 1 is false: The Preamble is not a source of power; it is a key to open the minds of the makers. It does not grant substantive power.

Statement 2 is true: In the Kesavananda Bharati case, the Supreme Court held that the Preamble is a part of the Constitution.

Statement 3 is true: The Preamble can be amended under Article 368, provided the basic structure is not destroyed.

Statement 4 is false: The Preamble is non-justiciable; it cannot be enforced in a court of law.
Thus, 2 and 3 are correct.
Source: UPSC Prelims 2020 (multi-statement)

Q122. Which of the following writs lies against a person who has usurped a public office?
(a) Mandamus
(b) Certiorari
(c) Quo Warranto
(d) Prohibition
Answer: (c) Quo Warranto
Solution: Quo Warranto (‘by what authority’) is issued to inquire into the legality of a person’s claim to a public office and to oust him if the claim is not well-founded. It prevents illegal usurpation of public office.
Source: UPSC Prelims 2017

Q123. The Constitution (Ninety-seventh Amendment) Act, 2011, gave constitutional status to:
(a) Municipalities
(b) Co-operative Societies
(c) Tribunals
(d) Panchayats
Answer: (b) Co-operative Societies
Solution: The 97th Amendment inserted Part IXB (Articles 243ZH–243ZT) relating to co-operative societies. It gave a constitutional basis to the functioning of co-operatives.
Source: SSC CGL 2022 (concept)

Q124. With reference to the legislative powers of the President, consider the following:

He can promulgate ordinances when both Houses of Parliament are not in session.

An ordinance can be retrospective in effect.

The maximum life of an ordinance is six months and six weeks from the reassembly of Parliament.
Which of the statements given above is/are correct?
(a) 1 and 2 only
(b) 2 only
(c) 1 and 3 only
(d) 1, 2 and 3
Answer: (d) 1, 2 and 3
Solution:

Statement 1 is correct: Article 123 empowers the President to promulgate ordinances when Parliament is not in session.

Statement 2 is correct: An ordinance can have retrospective effect.

Statement 3 is correct: The ordinance must be approved by Parliament within six weeks of its reassembly, and ceases to operate after six weeks from reassembly (if not approved), or earlier; thus, effectively, the maximum life is six months and six weeks from reassembly.
All statements are correct.
Source: UPSC Prelims 2015 (multi-statement)
Trap: Almost Correct Statement — many students miss that an ordinance can be retrospective.

Q125. The concept of ‘Equality before law’ in Article 14 is borrowed from the Constitution of:
(a) United States
(b) United Kingdom
(c) France
(d) Canada
Answer: (b) United Kingdom
Solution: ‘Equality before law’ is a concept from the British (English) common law system. The Indian Constitution includes both ‘Equality before law’ (British) and ‘Equal protection of the laws’ (American) in Article 14.
Source: UPSC Prelims 2018
Trap: Similar Name Confusion — often students think the entire Article 14 is from the USA, but ‘Equality before law’ is specifically British; the combined phrase is unique to India. The question specifically asks “Equality before law”, which is British.
