Q1. Who was the permanent President of the Constituent Assembly of India?
(a) Dr. B.R. Ambedkar
(b) Dr. Rajendra Prasad
(c) Jawaharlal Nehru
(d) Sardar Vallabhbhai Patel
Answer: (b) Dr. Rajendra Prasad
Solution: The Constituent Assembly elected Dr. Rajendra Prasad as its permanent President on 11 December 1946. Dr. Ambedkar chaired the Drafting Committee.
Source: SSC CGL 2022
Trap: Similar Name Confusion – Ambedkar’s role is often mistaken as President.

Q2. The Indian Constitution was adopted by the Constituent Assembly on:
(a) 26 January 1950
(b) 26 November 1949
(c) 15 August 1947
(d) 26 January 1949
Answer: (b) 26 November 1949
Solution: The Constitution was adopted on 26 November 1949 (Constitution Day) and came into effect on 26 January 1950.
Source: UPSC Prelims 2018
Trap: Chronological Confusion – Enforcement date vs adoption date.

Q3. The idea of a Constituent Assembly for India was first put forward by:
(a) Mahatma Gandhi
(b) M.N. Roy
(c) B.R. Ambedkar
(d) Jawaharlal Nehru
Answer: (b) M.N. Roy
Solution: M.N. Roy first proposed the idea of a Constituent Assembly in 1934.
Source: UPSC Prelims 2015

Q4. Which of the following words was NOT originally present in the Preamble?
(a) Sovereign
(b) Socialist
(c) Republic
(d) Democratic
Answer: (b) Socialist
Solution: ‘Socialist’, ‘Secular’, and ‘Integrity’ were added by the 42nd Amendment in 1976.
Source: SSC CHSL 2020

Q5. The concept of ‘Judicial Review’ in the Indian Constitution is borrowed from:
(a) Britain
(b) USA
(c) Canada
(d) Ireland
Answer: (b) USA
Solution: Judicial Review, along with Fundamental Rights, was borrowed from the U.S. Constitution.
Source: UPSC Prelims 2017

Q6. Which Article of the Indian Constitution is known as the “Heart and Soul of the Constitution”?
(a) Article 19
(b) Article 21
(c) Article 32
(d) Article 44
Answer: (c) Article 32
Solution: Dr. Ambedkar described Article 32 (Right to Constitutional Remedies) as the heart and soul because it allows direct access to the Supreme Court for enforcement of Fundamental Rights.
Source: SSC CGL 2021

Q7. The Right to Property was removed from the list of Fundamental Rights by which Amendment?
(a) 42nd Amendment
(b) 44th Amendment
(c) 24th Amendment
(d) 86th Amendment
Answer: (b) 44th Amendment
Solution: The 44th Amendment (1978) deleted the Right to Property (Article 31) from Part III and made it a legal right under Article 300A.
Source: UPSC Prelims 2019

Q8. The Directive Principles of State Policy (DPSP) are enumerated in which part of the Constitution?
(a) Part III
(b) Part IV
(c) Part IV-A
(d) Part V
Answer: (b) Part IV
Solution: DPSPs are contained in Part IV (Articles 36–51). Part III is Fundamental Rights, Part IV-A is Fundamental Duties.
Source: UPSC Prelims 2016

Q9. Which of the following is a Gandhian Directive Principle?
(a) Uniform Civil Code
(b) Equal pay for equal work
(c) Organisation of village panchayats
(d) Protection of monuments
Answer: (c) Organisation of village panchayats
Solution: Article 40 (organising village panchayats) is a Gandhian principle. Uniform Civil Code (Article 44) is Liberal-Intellectual; equal pay (Article 39) is Socialist.
Source: SSC CGL 2019

Q10. Fundamental Duties were added to the Constitution by the:
(a) 42nd Amendment
(b) 44th Amendment
(c) 86th Amendment
(d) 1st Amendment
Answer: (a) 42nd Amendment
Solution: The 42nd Amendment (1976) added 10 Fundamental Duties under Article 51A. The 86th Amendment later added the 11th duty.
Source: Railway NTPC 2021

Q11. The minimum age for becoming a member of the Lok Sabha is:
(a) 21 years
(b) 25 years
(c) 30 years
(d) 35 years
Answer: (b) 25 years
Solution: Article 84 prescribes 25 years for Lok Sabha and 30 years for Rajya Sabha.
Source: SSC CGL 2020

Q12. Who is the ex-officio Chairman of the Rajya Sabha?
(a) President
(b) Vice‑President
(c) Prime Minister
(d) Speaker of Lok Sabha
Answer: (b) Vice‑President
Solution: The Vice‑President of India is the ex-officio Chairman of the Rajya Sabha (Article 89).
Source: UPSC Prelims 2021

Q13. The maximum strength of the Rajya Sabha is:
(a) 200
(b) 250
(c) 500
(d) 550
Answer: (b) 250
Solution: Article 80 sets the maximum strength at 250 – 238 elected from States/UTs and 12 nominated by the President.
Source: SSC CHSL 2022

Q14. A Money Bill can be introduced only in:
(a) Lok Sabha
(b) Rajya Sabha
(c) Either House
(d) Joint Session
Answer: (a) Lok Sabha
Solution: Article 110(1) states a Money Bill shall not be introduced in the Rajya Sabha. It can only be introduced in the Lok Sabha.
Source: UPSC Prelims 2020

Q15. The President of India can proclaim National Emergency under Article 352 on which of the following grounds?
(a) War, external aggression or armed rebellion
(b) Failure of constitutional machinery
(c) Financial instability
(d) Internal disturbance
Answer: (a) War, external aggression or armed rebellion
Solution: Article 352 permits emergency on war, external aggression, or armed rebellion. ‘Internal disturbance’ was replaced by ‘armed rebellion’ by the 44th Amendment. Failure of constitutional machinery is Article 356, financial instability Article 360.
Source: UPSC Prelims 2019
Trap: Almost Correct Statement – ‘Internal disturbance’ is a deliberate trap; the phrase was changed to ‘armed rebellion’.

Q16. The power to impeach the President of India lies with:
(a) Supreme Court
(b) Parliament
(c) Prime Minister
(d) Chief Justice of India
Answer: (b) Parliament
Solution: Article 61 provides that the President may be impeached by Parliament for violation of the Constitution.
Source: SSC CGL 2021

Q17. The retirement age of a Supreme Court judge is:
(a) 60 years
(b) 62 years
(c) 65 years
(d) 70 years
Answer: (c) 65 years
Solution: Article 124(2) sets the retirement age of Supreme Court judges at 65 years. High Court judges retire at 62 years.
Source: UPSC Prelims 2017

Q18. The writ ‘Habeas Corpus’ means:
(a) To be certified
(b) We command
(c) To have the body
(d) By what authority
Answer: (c) To have the body
Solution: Habeas Corpus (Latin) literally means ‘you may have the body’. It is a writ to produce a detained person before the court.
Source: SSC CGL 2020

Q19. Panchayati Raj was given constitutional status by which Amendment?
(a) 71st Amendment
(b) 73rd Amendment
(c) 74th Amendment
(d) 76th Amendment
Answer: (b) 73rd Amendment
Solution: The 73rd Amendment (1992) added Part IX and Article 243–243O, giving constitutional status to Panchayats. The 74th Amendment dealt with Municipalities.
Source: Railway NTPC 2020

Q20. The President of India is elected by an electoral college consisting of:
(a) Elected members of Parliament only
(b) Elected members of State Legislatures only
(c) Elected members of both Houses of Parliament and elected members of State Legislative Assemblies
(d) All members of Parliament and State Legislatures
Answer: (c) Elected members of both Houses of Parliament and elected members of State Legislative Assemblies
Solution: As per Article 54, the electoral college comprises the elected members of both Houses of Parliament (Lok Sabha and Rajya Sabha) and the elected members of the Legislative Assemblies of States and UTs (Delhi, Puducherry, J&K). Nominated members do not participate.
Source: UPSC Prelims 2023
Trap: Almost Correct Statement – Option (d) includes ‘all members’ (i.e., nominated) which is incorrect; option (a) and (b) omit essential parts.

Q21. (Medium) Which of the following statements is correct regarding the Vice‑President of India?
(a) He is elected by an electoral college consisting of elected members of both Houses of Parliament and State Legislatures.
(b) He can be removed by a resolution passed by the Lok Sabha alone.
(c) He acts as the ex-officio Chairman of the Rajya Sabha.
(d) He holds office for a term of 6 years.
Answer: (c) He acts as the ex-officio Chairman of the Rajya Sabha.
Solution:

The Vice‑President is elected by all members (elected + nominated) of both Houses of Parliament, not including State Legislatures (→ a wrong).

He can be removed by a resolution of the Rajya Sabha passed by an effective majority and agreed to by the Lok Sabha (→ b wrong).

He is ex-officio Chairman of Rajya Sabha (→ c correct).

His term is 5 years (→ d wrong).
Source: SSC CGL (Modified)
Trap: Almost Correct Statement – mixing electoral colleges of President and VP.

Q22. (Medium) The doctrine of ‘Basic Structure’ of the Constitution was propounded by the Supreme Court in which case?
(a) Golaknath v. State of Punjab
(b) Kesavananda Bharati v. State of Kerala
(c) Minerva Mills v. Union of India
(d) Maneka Gandhi v. Union of India
Answer: (b) Kesavananda Bharati v. State of Kerala
Solution: The Supreme Court in Kesavananda Bharati (1973) held that Parliament can amend the Constitution but cannot destroy its basic structure.
Source: UPSC Prelims 2017

Q23. (Medium) Which of the following is NOT a Fundamental Duty under Article 51A?
(a) To abide by the Constitution and respect its ideals and institutions.
(b) To defend the country and render national service when called upon.
(c) To pay taxes honestly and regularly.
(d) To promote harmony and the spirit of common brotherhood.
Answer: (c) To pay taxes honestly and regularly.
Solution: Paying taxes is a legal obligation, not listed as a Fundamental Duty. Duties include abiding by the Constitution, defending the country, promoting harmony, etc.
Source: SSC CHSL 2020
Trap: Negative Phrasing (NOT) and mixing legal duties with constitutional duties.

Q24. (Medium) The Comptroller and Auditor General (CAG) of India is appointed by the:
(a) President
(b) Vice‑President
(c) Prime Minister
(d) Parliament
Answer: (a) President
Solution: Article 148 provides that the CAG is appointed by the President by warrant under his hand and seal.
Source: Railway NTPC 2019

Q25. (Medium) During a Financial Emergency under Article 360, the President can:
(a) Suspend all Fundamental Rights.
(b) Issue directions to States to observe canons of financial propriety.
(c) Dissolve the Lok Sabha.
(d) Assume to himself all powers of the State Governments.
Answer: (b) Issue directions to States to observe canons of financial propriety.
Solution: Under Article 360, the President can direct reduction of salaries and can direct States to observe certain financial principles. Suspension of Fundamental Rights occurs under National Emergency (Article 358/359).
Source: UPSC Prelims 2018
Trap: Almost Correct Statement – option (a) relates to National Emergency, not Financial Emergency.
