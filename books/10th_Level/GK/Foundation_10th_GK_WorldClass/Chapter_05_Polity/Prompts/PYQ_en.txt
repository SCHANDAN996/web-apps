4. Top 10 High-Yield PYQs with Solutions, Trap Types & Time Rule

Q1. UPSC 2023: Consider the following statements about the Vice-President of India:

The Vice-President is elected by an electoral college consisting of elected members of both Houses of Parliament.

The Vice-President can be removed by a resolution passed by the Rajya Sabha and agreed to by the Lok Sabha.
Which is correct?

(a) 1 only

(b) 2 only

(c) Both 1 and 2

(d) Neither 1 nor 2

Answer: (d) Neither 1 nor 2
Solution:

Statement 1 is wrong: VP is elected by all members (elected + nominated) of both Houses, not only elected.

Statement 2 is also wrong: VP can be removed by a resolution of Rajya Sabha passed by effective majority and agreed to by Lok Sabha. But the statement misses “effective majority” and “Rajya Sabha only” – the procedure is complex; the statement is incomplete and incorrect as given.
Trap Type: Almost Correct Statement (both statements miss a crucial word – “all” vs “elected”, and resolution specifics).
Time Rule: 15-sec fact-based (recalling VP electoral college and removal procedure).

Q2. UPSC 2022: With reference to the writs issued by the Courts in India, consider the following pairs:

Mandamus – to command a public official to perform a duty

Prohibition – issued by a higher court to a lower court to stop proceedings

Quo Warranto – to inquire into the legality of a person holding a public office
Which pairs are correctly matched?

(a) 1 and 2 only

(b) 2 and 3 only

(c) 1 and 3 only

(d) 1, 2 and 3

Answer: (d) 1, 2 and 3
Solution: All three descriptions are correct.
Trap Type: None explicitly; pure factual. However, sometimes the exam flips Prohibition and Certiorari definitions.
Time Rule: 45-sec analytical (need to confirm each definition precisely).

Q3. SSC CGL 2021: Which Article of the Indian Constitution is known as the “Heart and Soul of the Constitution”?

(a) Article 19

(b) Article 21

(c) Article 32

(d) Article 44

Answer: (c) Article 32
Solution: Dr. Ambedkar called Article 32 (Right to Constitutional Remedies) the heart and soul.
Trap Type: Similar Name Confusion (often Article 21 is confused as soul, or 19, 44).
Time Rule: 15-sec fact.

Q4. UPSC 2020: In the context of the Indian Parliament, a Money Bill:

(a) Can be introduced only in the Lok Sabha.

(b) Can be introduced only in the Rajya Sabha.

(c) Can be introduced in either House.

(d) Is defined under Article 112.

Answer: (a)
Solution: Money Bill can only be introduced in Lok Sabha (Article 110).
Trap Type: Almost Correct Statement with Article number (Money Bill is Art 110, Annual Financial Statement is Art 112).
Time Rule: 15-sec fact.

Q5. UPSC 2019: The 44th Amendment to the Constitution of India:

(a) Restored the term ‘Socialist’ in the Preamble.

(b) Deleted the Right to Property from the list of Fundamental Rights.

(c) Added Fundamental Duties.

(d) Made the President bound by the advice of the Council of Ministers.

Answer: (b)
Solution: 44th Amendment removed Right to Property from Part III (made legal right under Art 300A).
Trap Type: Chronological Confusion – mixing 42nd (added words, Duties) and 44th Amendments. Option (d) was by 42nd and 44th clarified but the direct removal is (b).
Time Rule: 15-sec fact.

Q6. Railway NTPC 2020: Who among the following administers the oath of office to the Governor of a State?

(a) President of India

(b) Chief Justice of India

(c) Chief Justice of the concerned High Court

(d) Vice-President

Answer: (c)
Solution: The Governor takes oath before the Chief Justice of the High Court (or senior-most judge).
Trap Type: Similar Authority Confusion – often mistaken that President administers oath.
Time Rule: 15-sec fact.

Q7. UPSC 2018: The power to increase the number of judges in the Supreme Court of India is vested in:

(a) The President of India

(b) The Parliament

(c) The Chief Justice of India

(d) The Law Commission

Answer: (b) Parliament
Solution: Parliament can by law increase the number of judges.
Trap Type: Almost Correct – many think President does it by ordinance.
Time Rule: 45-sec analytical (need to distinguish between appointment and strength increase).

Q8. SSC CHSL 2019: Which of the following is NOT a Fundamental Duty under Article 51A?

(a) To uphold and protect the sovereignty, unity and integrity of India

(b) To safeguard public property and to abjure violence

(c) To pay taxes regularly

(d) To develop the scientific temper, humanism and the spirit of inquiry

Answer: (c) To pay taxes regularly
Solution: Paying taxes is a legal duty, not a Fundamental Duty.
Trap Type: Negative Phrasing – “NOT” in the question; also, mixing legal and constitutional duties.
Time Rule: 15-sec fact if you know the list.

Q9. Banking (IBPS PO 2020): The Finance Commission is constituted under which Article?

(a) Article 280

(b) Article 324

(c) Article 148

(d) Article 315

Answer: (a) Article 280
Solution: Finance Commission – Art 280; Election Commission – 324; CAG – 148; UPSC – 315.
Trap Type: Article Number Confusion.
Time Rule: 15-sec fact (recall).

Q10. UPSC 2017: In the Kesavananda Bharati case, the Supreme Court propounded the:

(a) Theory of Parliamentary Sovereignty

(b) Basic Structure Doctrine

(c) Doctrine of Harmonious Construction

(d) Doctrine of Colourable Legislation

Answer: (b) Basic Structure Doctrine
Solution: The case gave the Basic Structure doctrine (Parliament can’t alter the basic features).
Trap Type: None, but sometimes confusion with Golaknath case.
Time Rule: 15-sec fact.

5. 15/45-Second Rule Applied

15-second fact-based questions: Q3, Q4, Q5, Q6, Q9, Q10. Instantly recallable if facts are memorised. Skip if unsure and move on.

45-second analytical questions: Q2 (writs matching – need to cross-check all definitions), Q7 (power to increase judges – requires distinguishing appointment vs strength, may need rule knowledge). Q1 also analytical if the removal procedure is known, but still could be 15-sec if you remember VP election details. It’s borderline.

Trap Awareness: Most traps in Polity are “Almost Correct Statement” (missing word) and “Chronological Confusion” (amendments). In Q1, the trap was “elected” vs “all” in VP election, making the statement almost correct but wrong. Awareness of such traps saves time.
