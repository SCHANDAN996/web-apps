🕌 Medieval Indian History (मध्यकालीन भारतीय इतिहास)

Hook
Imagine a king who printed leather coins that people forged overnight. Imagine a 13-year-old girl who became a sultan and rode into battle wearing a male turban. Or a ruler who carried a golden chain of justice for his subjects to ring. Medieval India is not just a list of dates — it’s a blockbuster drama of ambition, art, and mind-blowing experiments. And the best part? 6 out of 10 questions in competitive exams come straight from these stories. Let’s make them unforgettable.

Learning Objectives

By the end of this chapter, you will be able to:

Recall the chronology of major dynasties and their founders.

Explain the administrative, economic, and cultural reforms of key rulers.

Differentiate between similar-sounding names and events.

Spot common examiner traps and avoid them.

Connect medieval developments with modern-day landmarks.

1. Delhi Sultanate (1206–1526 CE) – Five Families, One Throne

Think of the Delhi Sultanate as five back-to-back movies with almost the same set but different directors. The directors? Gulam, Khilji, Tughlaq, Sayyid, and Lodi — remember “Gul Khil Tug Say Lo”.

1.1 The Mamluk (Slave) Dynasty (1206–1290)

Qutb-ud-din Aibak – A slave who became a sultan. He began building Qutub Minar but only finished the first storey. Title: Lakh Baksh (giver of lakhs).

Iltutmish – The real organiser. He introduced the Iqta system (land revenue assignment) and issued the silver Tanka and copper Jital. He also formed the group of 40 Turkish nobles — Chalisa/Chahalgani.

Razia Sultan – First and only Muslim woman to sit on Delhi’s throne. She dressed like a man, led armies, but fell because the Turkish nobles refused to accept a woman.

Balban – A strict disciplinarian. He crushed the Chalisa and promoted the idea that the king is the Shadow of God (Zil-e-Ilahi). He introduced Sijda (prostration) and Paibos (kissing feet) in the court.

🧠 Active Recall
Who introduced the Iqta system and the silver Tanka? Which ruler started building Qutub Minar but didn’t finish it?

1.2 The Khilji Dynasty (1290–1320) – Expansion Over Everything

Jalaluddin Khilji – The first Khilji ruler. He was lenient with Mongols — a soft approach.

Alauddin Khilji – His nephew who killed him to grab power. Once in charge, Alauddin became India’s most ambitious early Sultan.

Market Reforms: Fixed prices of all goods; department called Shahana-i-Mandi to control supply.

Military Reforms: Introduced Dagh (branding of horses) and Chehra (descriptive rolls of soldiers).

Southern Campaigns: Sent Malik Kafur to Devagiri, Warangal, Dwarasamudra, and Madurai.

Architecture: Alai Darwaza, Siri Fort, Hauz Khas.

🧠 Active Recall
What was the Dagh system? Which general did Alauddin send to South India?

1.3 The Tughlaq Dynasty (1320–1414) – The Experimenters

Ghiyasuddin Tughlaq – Built Tughlaqabad Fort. Allegedly killed by a collapsing pavilion engineered by his son?

Muhammad bin Tughlaq (1325–51) – A genius misunderstood as mad. He tried three famous experiments:

Token Currency – Copper and brass coins with the same value as silver. People started minting coins at home; economy collapsed.

Capital Shift – Moved capital from Delhi to Daulatabad (Devagiri) to control the South. Ordered entire population to march; then revoked it.

Doab Tax Hike – Increased taxes during famine in the Ganga-Yamuna doab; revolts followed.

He received Moroccan traveller Ibn Battuta, who wrote Rihla.

Firoz Shah Tughlaq – The “welfare dictator”. Built canals, hospitals, and made Jizya a separate tax (earlier it was part of land revenue). Wrote his autobiography Futuhat-i-Firoz Shahi.

🧠 Active Recall
Which ruler introduced token currency? Who wrote Rihla?

1.4 Sayyid (1414–51) and Lodi (1451–1526) Dynasties

Sayyids – Short-lived, left no mark.

Lodis –

Sikandar Lodi – Founded Agra (1504); patronised Persian poetry and music; introduced Gaz-i-Sikandari for land measurement.

Ibrahim Lodi – His arrogance led to the First Battle of Panipat (1526), where Babur defeated him using gunpowder and Tulughma tactics.

2. The Mughal Empire (1526–1857) – A Six-Part Epic

Mnemonic for Mughal rulers: BHAJ SA (Babur, Humayun, Akbar, Jahangir, Shah Jahan, Aurangzeb).
BHAJ SA — like “भाज सा” — that’s all you need to keep the order.

2.1 Babur (1526–30) – The Adventurer

Descendant of Timur and Genghis Khan.

Four famous battles: Panipat (1526, defeated Ibrahim Lodi), Khanwa (1527, Rana Sanga), Chanderi (1528), Ghaghra (1529).

Wrote autobiography Tuzuk-i-Baburi in Turkish, later translated to Persian as Baburnama.

2.2 Humayun (1530–40; 1555–56) – The King Who Lost and Won Back

Defeated by Sher Shah Suri at Chausa (1539) and Kannauj (1540).

Spent 15 years in exile; Akbar was born in Amarkot.

Returned in 1555 with Persian help but died shortly after falling from library stairs.

2.3 Sher Shah Suri (1540–45) – The Intermission Star

Although not Mughal, he ruled in between. He’s the exam superstar:

Issued Rupiya (silver coin) and Dam (copper coin) — ancestor of today’s rupee.

Built Grand Trunk Road (Sadak-e-Azam) from Sonargaon to Peshawar.

Introduced Patta-Kabuliyat (land records agreement) and measured land using Sikandari Gaz.

2.4 Akbar (1556–1605) – The Great Integrator

Coronated at 13; won Panipat II (1556) against Hemu, the Hindu general.

Administration: Mansabdari system — every officer had a rank (Zat for personal status, Sawar for cavalry).

Land Revenue: Zabti system by Todar Mal; measurement standard Ilahi Gaz.

Religious Policy:

Abolished Jizya (1564) and pilgrimage tax.

Built Ibadat Khana at Fatehpur Sikri (1575) for debates.

Promulgated Din-i-Ilahi (1582) — not a new religion but a socio-spiritual code; very few disciples, most notably Birbal.

Navratnas in his court: Birbal, Tansen, Todar Mal, Abul Fazl (author of Akbarnama and Ain-i-Akbari), Raja Man Singh, etc.

🧠 Active Recall
What was Din-i-Ilahi? Name the revenue system introduced by Akbar. Who wrote Ain-i-Akbari?

2.5 Jahangir (1605–27) – The Chain of Justice

Started Zanjir-e-Adl (golden chain) for petitioners.

His reign saw two English visitors: Captain Hawkins and Sir Thomas Roe, who got trade concessions.

Famous for his autobiography Tuzuk-i-Jahangiri and love for painting.

2.6 Shah Jahan (1628–58) – The Builder King

Golden Age of Mughal architecture: Taj Mahal (by Ustad Ahmad Lahauri), Red Fort, Jama Masjid, Moti Masjid, Peacock Throne.

Capital shifted back to Delhi, founding Shahjahanabad.

His son Aurangzeb imprisoned him in Agra Fort.

2.7 Aurangzeb (1658–1707) – The Zealous Expansionist

Reimposed Jizya (1679), banned music and Sati celebrations at court.

Spent decades in Deccan fighting Bijapur, Golconda, and the Marathas.

Executed Sikh Guru Tegh Bahadur; later Guru Gobind Singh organised the Khalsa (1699).

After him, the empire fragmented — later Mughals ended symbolically with Bahadur Shah Zafar (1857 revolt).

3. Regional Powerhouses
3.1 Vijayanagar Empire (1336–1646)

Founded by Harihara and Bukka (with help of saint Vidyaranya).

Greatest ruler: Krishnadeva Raya (1509–29) — great patron of literature (Amuktamalyada in Telugu), built Hazara Rama Temple, Vitthala Temple at Hampi.

Famous for the Battle of Talikota (1565) — a disastrous defeat by the combined Deccan sultanates.

3.2 Bahmani Sultanate and Deccan Sultanates

Bahmani kingdom founded by Hasan Gangu Bahman Shah (1347).

Famous wazir Mahmud Gawan established a madrasa in Bidar.

Later split into five sultanates: Bijapur, Golconda, Ahmadnagar, Berar, Bidar.

3.3 The Marathas

Shivaji (1630–80) – Born at Shivneri; coronated at Raigarh (1674) with the title Chhatrapati.

Key events: Killing of Afzal Khan at Pratapgad (1659), Treaty of Purandar (1665) with Jai Singh, escape from Agra (1666).

Administration: Ashtapradhan (8 ministers); revenue systems Chauth (1/4th tax) and Sardeshmukhi (10%).

3.4 The Sikhs

Guru Nanak (1469–1539) founded Sikhism; the fifth guru Arjan Dev compiled Adi Granth and built Harmandir Sahib; he was executed by Jahangir.

Tenth guru Gobind Singh created the Khalsa (1699) at Anandpur Sahib.

🧠 Active Recall
Who founded Vijayanagar? What were Chauth and Sardeshmukhi?

4. Art & Architecture – What Survives, Speaks

Indo-Islamic architecture: Arches, domes, minarets, jaali work.

Key structures: Qutub Minar (Iltutmish completed), Alai Darwaza (first true arch), Siri Fort, Tughlaqabad, Fatehpur Sikri’s Buland Darwaza, Taj Mahal, Red Fort.

Music: Amir Khusrau (disciple of Nizamuddin Auliya) introduced qawwali, sitar, tabla, and developed Khari Boli. Tansen composed ragas like Miyan ki Malhar and Darbari.

⚠️ Caution: Examiner’s Trap

Trap 1: Founder Confusion
Question: Who was the founder of the Khilji dynasty?
Temptation: Alauddin Khilji (because he’s most famous).
Correct: Jalaluddin Khilji.
Trap Type: Similar Names / Chronological Confusion

Trap 2: “Not a Religion” Trick
Statement: Akbar founded a new religion called Din-i-Ilahi.
Verdict: False. Din-i-Ilahi was a socio-spiritual path, not a religion with rituals.
Trap Type: Almost Correct Statement (90% correct, one fatal word)

Trap 3: Razia’s Uniqueness Twist
Question: Who was the first and last Muslim woman to rule the Delhi Sultanate?
Answer: Razia Sultana.
Trap: Some options may include Chand Bibi, but she ruled Ahmednagar, not Delhi.
Trap Type: Geographic / Context Shift
