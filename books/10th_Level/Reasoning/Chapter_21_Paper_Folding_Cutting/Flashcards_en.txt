Front: One fold and one hole — how many holes when opened?
Back: 2 holes (folding doubles the hole).

Front: Two folds and one hole — how many holes?
Back: 4 holes (each fold doubles it).

Front: What is the formula for the number of holes?
Back: original holes × 2^(number of folds).

Front: How is each hole formed when opened?
Back: Like a mirror across the fold line — not a direct copy.

Front: What is the correct order of opening a paper?
Back: Reverse — open the last fold first.

Front: Three folds and one hole → how many holes?
Back: 8 holes (2³ = 8).

Front: If a hole is near the fold line, where is its mirror hole?
Back: On the other side, at the same distance from the line.

Front: What property does an opened paper show?
Back: Symmetric — balanced across the fold lines.

Front: Are holes copied directly or mirrored?
Back: Mirrored — reversed across the fold line.

Front: What does each fold do to the number of holes?
Back: Doubles it.

Front: A quick trick to solve options?
Back: First check the number of holes; cut any with the wrong count.

Front: What is the most common trap?
Back: Copying holes directly instead of mirroring them.

Front: When does the first fold made open?
Back: Last of all.

Front: 1 hole, 4 folds → how many holes?
Back: 16 (2⁴ = 16).

Front: What is this chapter's mnemonic?
Back: "Each fold doubles, mirror on the line".
