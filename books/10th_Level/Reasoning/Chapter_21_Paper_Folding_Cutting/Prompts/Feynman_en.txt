🧒 Feynman (The Magic Mirror in Folded Paper — As Told by a Curious Kid)

Ever watched a child make paper snowflakes? My niece Lily, age 7, once sat cross-legged on the floor with a pair of safety scissors and a square of white paper. She folded it in half, then in half again. Then she snipped triangles and curves along the edges. When she carefully unfolded it, her eyes went wide. “It’s a snowflake! But I only cut a few pieces — how did it become a whole design?”

I sat down beside her. “Lily, you just discovered the secret of paper folding and cutting. Every time you fold the paper, you create an invisible magic mirror along the fold line. Whatever you cut on one side of that mirror, the mirror instantly makes a copy on the other side. When you open the paper, you see the original cut and its mirror image joined together — that’s why it’s always symmetrical.”

She tilted her head. “But I folded it twice. Two mirrors?”
“Exactly,” I said. “And that’s the trickiest part — multiple mirrors. When you have more than one fold, you have to remove the mirrors in the reverse order you created them. Imagine you’re wearing a jacket over a sweater. To take them off, you don’t pull the sweater first — you remove the jacket first, then the sweater. Same with folds: always start unfolding from the last fold you made, and work backwards to the very first fold. ”

We tried a little experiment. I took a fresh square, folded it once (fold A), then folded it again (fold B). Now I cut a small triangle at the corner that was all open edges — not touching any fold line.
“Now watch, Lily. We’ll undo our last fold first — fold B.” I unfolded along the second fold line, and the single cut appeared on both layers, creating two separate triangles, mirror images across that fold line. “Now we undo the first fold — fold A.” I unfolded it completely, and she counted: “Four triangles! Two became four.”
“Yes, because the first mirror took those two triangles and reflected them across its fold line, making four. And because the cut never touched the fold lines, they all stayed separate — like four little islands.”

She clapped. “So if the cut had touched the fold line, would they join?”
“Bingo! If you cut along the fold line, the mirror makes the two halves join into one big shape — like cutting a half-heart on the fold gives a full heart.”

The Unforgettable Trick: The Reverse Unfolding Dance
I made Lily stand up. “Fold your arms across your chest. That’s fold 1. Now cross your legs while standing — tricky, but do it. That’s fold 2. To get back to normal, do you uncross your arms first or your legs first?” She uncrossed her legs first, then her arms. “That’s exactly how you unfold paper. Last fold, first undo. Every time you undo a fold, you put a mirror on that line and reflect everything you currently have.”

The magic phrase: “Last fold first, mirror the cut, repeat.” That’s the heart of it. Every fold line is a mirror; you just add reflections one by one in reverse order, and the final picture appears.
