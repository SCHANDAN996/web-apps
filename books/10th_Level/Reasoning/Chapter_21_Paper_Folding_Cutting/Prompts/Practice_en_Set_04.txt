Q76. (Medium)
A square paper is folded in half along the vertical centre line. On the folded paper, a small heart shape is cut out with its cleft touching the fold line and the pointed end away from the fold. After opening, what does the hole look like?

A. Two separate hearts
B. A single heart with two clefts
C. Two hearts joined at the cleft, forming a clover-like shape
D. A heart that is twice as wide

Correct Answer: C
Step-by-step:

The heart’s cleft lies on the fold line.

Mirroring across the vertical fold reflects the heart, creating a second heart attached at the cleft. The combined shape resembles two hearts joined at the top, like a clover or butterfly.

Option A would be true if the cleft did not touch the fold.
Source: SSC CHSL 2019

Q77. (Medium)
A paper is folded first along the vertical centre line, then along the horizontal centre line. A cut is made that removes a small rectangle from the open corner that is farthest from both fold lines. After fully opening, how many rectangular holes are present?

A. 1
B. 2
C. 3
D. 4

Correct Answer: D
Step-by-step:

The cut rectangle is at the free corner of the quadruple-layered packet, not touching any fold.

Undo horizontal fold: rectangle mirrored to the adjacent quadrant → 2 rectangles.

Undo vertical fold: each of those mirrored to the opposite side → total 4 rectangles, one at each corner of the original paper.
Source: SSC CGL 2020

Q78. (Medium)
A circular paper is folded into a semicircle, then into a quarter circle. A punch is made exactly at the midpoint of the straight edge (the radius edge) of the quarter circle, which is not a fold line. After full opening, how many holes are there?

A. 2
B. 4
C. 6
D. 8

Correct Answer: B
Step-by-step:

Two folds. The punch is on a straight cut edge, away from both folds.

Unfold last fold: hole mirrored across the second fold → 2 holes on the semicircle.

Unfold first fold: each of those 2 holes mirrored across the diameter → total 4 holes on the full circle.
Source: RRB NTPC 2019

Q79. (Medium)

A. A square hole in the centre
B. Two square holes, one on each side of the diagonal, symmetric
C. A single rectangular hole along the diagonal
D. Four square holes at the corners

Correct Answer: B
Step-by-step:

The square cut is on the open edge, not touching the diagonal fold.

Mirroring across the diagonal fold creates an identical square hole on the other half. They are separate and symmetric about the diagonal.

Q80. (Medium)
A paper is folded once horizontally. A small triangle is cut out such that its base lies on the fold line and the apex points upward, completely on the upper half. After opening, what is the shape of the hole?

A. A single triangle
B. A rhombus
C. A shape like two triangles joined at the base, forming a diamond
D. Two separate triangles, one above the fold and one below

Correct Answer: C
Step-by-step:

Triangle base on fold, apex up.

Mirroring across the horizontal fold reflects the triangle downward, creating a second triangle with apex down, attached along the base. Together they form a diamond (rhombus) shape.
Source: SSC CGL 2019

Q81. (Medium)
A paper is folded twice: first vertically, then horizontally. A punch is made at a point that lies exactly on the horizontal fold line but 2 cm to the right of the vertical fold line. After full unfolding, how many holes and where?

A. One hole on the horizontal centre line, 2 cm right of centre
B. Two holes, symmetric about the vertical centre line, both on the horizontal centre line
C. Two holes, both on the vertical centre line
D. Four holes forming a rectangle

Correct Answer: B
Step-by-step:

Punch is on the horizontal fold line, so unfolding the horizontal fold does not duplicate it (the hole remains on that line).

Then unfolding the vertical fold: the hole is 2 cm to the right of that fold, so its mirror appears 2 cm to the left of the vertical fold, also on the horizontal centre line. Total 2 holes.
Source: SSC CHSL 2020

Q82. (Medium)
A paper is folded along the vertical centre line. A cut is made that removes a shape resembling a 'T', with the vertical stem of the T lying exactly on the fold line and the crossbar to the right. After opening, the hole will be:

A. A 'T' shape that is twice as wide
B. Two separate 'T' shapes
C. An 'H' shape
D. A shape like a 'T' with a crossbar on both sides, forming a symmetric plus-like figure

Correct Answer: D
Step-by-step:

The T's stem is on the fold, crossbar to the right. Mirroring reflects the crossbar to the left, while the stem remains central. The resulting hole is a symmetric shape with a central stem and crossbars on both sides — like a plus sign with the vertical bar longer, or a 'T' with two crossbars. Often called a “double T” or “+ with a long stem”.
*Source: RRB Group-D 2019*

Q83. (Medium – Examiner’s Trap: Punch on fold vs off fold)
A square paper is folded once along the diagonal. Two punches are made: one exactly on the fold line at its midpoint, and another 1 cm away from the fold line on the left half. After unfolding, how many holes are there?

A. 2
B. 3
C. 4
D. 5

Correct Answer: B
Step-by-step:

Punch on the fold at midpoint → upon unfolding, this yields a single hole at the centre (the two mirrored holes coincide).

Punch off the fold on the left half → mirroring produces a second hole on the right half, giving 2 holes.

Total holes = 1 + 2 = 3.
Trap: Students often double both punches, giving 4, forgetting that the punch on the fold does not double.
Source: SSC CGL 2022

Q84. (Medium)
A paper is folded once. A cut is made that removes a narrow wedge starting from the open edge and ending exactly at the fold line, with the point of the wedge on the fold. After opening, the shape of the hole is:

A. A single wedge
B. Two wedges meeting at a point on the fold
C. A single triangular hole
D. A shape like a kite

Correct Answer: B
Step-by-step:

The wedge is a V-shape with its point on the fold. Mirroring creates an identical wedge on the other side, sharing the point. The combined hole is an hourglass or bow-tie shape, i.e., two wedges joined at the point.
Source: SSC GD 2017

Q85. (Medium – Examiner’s Trap: Asymmetrical cut, rotation vs reflection)
A paper is folded once along the vertical centre. An asymmetrical shape like a comma or a tadpole (a circle with a curved tail) is cut out such that the tail end touches the fold line. Which of the following is the correct unfolded hole?

A. Two separate commas, one on each side, both tails inward and touching at the fold
B. A comma on the right and its mirror image on the left, forming a shape like a paisley symmetric about the fold
C. A comma on the right and the same comma rotated 180° on the left
D. A single comma shape crossing the fold

Correct Answer: B
Step-by-step:

The comma touches the fold at its tail. Mirroring reflects the comma, resulting in a mirror-image comma on the left, attached at the tail. The two together form a symmetric paisley-like or yin-yang-like figure.

Option C is a rotation, which is a common error — students think folding flips the shape in a rotational manner, but it's a reflection.
Trap: Misleading option with rotation.
Source: SSC CGL 2021

Q86. (Medium)
A square paper is folded along both diagonals to form a small triangle (a quarter of the square). A punch is made through all layers at the midpoint of the hypotenuse of this small triangle. After complete opening, how many holes appear?

A. 2
B. 4
C. 6
D. 8

Correct Answer: B
Step-by-step:

Two diagonal folds. The punch is on the hypotenuse (open edge) of the small triangle, not on any fold.

Unfold the last fold (one diagonal): the hole reflects across that diagonal, giving 2 holes on the half-square triangle.

Unfold the first fold (other diagonal): each of those 2 holes reflects again, giving 4 holes on the full square, symmetrically placed.
Source: SSC CGL 2023

Q87. (Medium)
A paper is folded once vertically. A cut is made that removes a shape like a star with five points, with one point touching the fold line. After opening, how many points does the resulting hole have?

A. 5
B. 9
C. 10
D. 12

Correct Answer: B
Step-by-step:

The star has 5 points, one on the fold. Mirroring adds the other 4 points from the mirrored star, which are distinct from the original (except the shared point on the fold).

So total distinct points = 1 (shared) + 4 (from original star, not on fold) + 4 (from mirror star, not on fold) = 9 points. The hole is a 9-pointed star shape.
Source: SSC CHSL 2018

Q88. (Medium)
A paper is folded twice: first vertically, then horizontally. A cut is made that removes a small circle from the exact centre of the folded packet (the point that is the intersection of the two fold edges). After full opening, what is seen?

A. One circle in the centre
B. Four circles, one in each quadrant
C. One large circle
D. Two circles on the vertical centre line

Correct Answer: A
Step-by-step:

The centre of the folded packet is the point where the two fold edges meet, which is the intersection of the original vertical and horizontal fold lines. The circle is cut exactly at that corner, thus its centre lies on both folds.

When unfolded, the hole is reflected across both folds, but because it is on the fold lines, all reflections coincide. Only one hole appears, at the exact centre of the original paper.
Source: SSC GD 2021

Q95. (Medium – Examiner’s Trap: Double relationship, fold order)
A paper is folded first along the horizontal centre line, then along the vertical centre line. A punch is made at a point that is on the vertical fold line but 1 cm above the horizontal fold line (in the folded state). After full opening, how many holes and where?

A. One hole on the vertical centre line, 1 cm above the centre
B. Two holes, both on the vertical centre line, one above and one below the centre, symmetric
C. Two holes, both on the horizontal centre line
D. Four holes forming a cross

Correct Answer: B
Step-by-step:

The paper is folded first horizontally, then vertically. The last fold is vertical. The punch is on the vertical fold line but offset vertically from the horizontal fold line.

Unfold the last fold (vertical): the punch is on the vertical fold line, so mirroring across the vertical fold does not produce a new hole; the hole remains on that line. (But careful: The punch is on the vertical fold line, so it's a single hole after vertical unfold, still at the same coordinates relative to that fold.)

Now unfold the first fold (horizontal): the hole is 1 cm above the horizontal fold line (since the packet had the horizontal fold at one edge). Upon unfolding, that hole is mirrored across the horizontal fold to a point 1 cm below the horizontal fold line. So we get two holes, both on the vertical centre line, one above and one below the horizontal centre line, symmetric.
Trap: Students might unfold in the wrong order or think that because the punch is on a fold line, it's only one hole, forgetting the other fold can still duplicate it if it's not on that fold too.
Source: SSC CGL 2022

Q98. (Medium – Examiner’s Trap: Symmetry assumption)
A square paper is folded once vertically. A cut is made that removes a shape like a right-angled triangle with the right angle at the top open edge and the hypotenuse sloping down towards the fold line, but the cut does NOT touch the fold line. After opening, the hole will be:

A. Two separate right triangles, one on each half, with right angles at top, not touching
B. Two triangles joined along the fold line to form a larger triangle
C. One triangle on the right and its mirror on the left, touching at the fold line
D. A shape like a parallelogram

Correct Answer: A
Step-by-step:

The triangle is entirely on one half, not touching the fold. Mirroring creates a second identical triangle on the other half. They are separate, not joined.

Option C incorrectly assumes they touch because the shape might be close to the fold, but the question says "does NOT touch".
Trap: Misleading option C exploits the common mistake of assuming cuts near the fold automatically merge.
Source: SSC CGL 2021
