📝 Practice Set 3: Paper Folding & Cutting (Questions 51–75)

Instructions: In each question, a sheet of paper is folded and then cut or punched as described. Choose the answer figure that shows how the paper will appear when fully unfolded. All questions are of Medium difficulty.

Q51. (Medium)
A square paper is folded along the vertical centre line. On the folded paper, a small circle is cut out such that its circumference touches the fold line at exactly one point. When the paper is opened, what does the hole look like?

A. Two separate circles
B. A single figure-eight shaped hole
C. A single larger circular hole
D. Two circles that overlap slightly, forming a shape like a Venn diagram intersection

Correct Answer: B
Step-by-step:

The cut circle touches the fold line at exactly one point.

When the paper is opened, the circle is reflected across the fold. Because the circles touch at a single point on the fold line, they become connected at that point. The resulting hole is a single continuous shape — two circles joined at a point, resembling a figure-eight.

Option A would be if the circle did not touch the fold; Option C would be if the circle straddled the fold heavily; Option D is not accurate as the overlap is only at a point, not an area.
Source: SSC CHSL 2019

Q52. (Medium)
A paper is folded first along the vertical centre line and then along the horizontal centre line. A cut is made that removes a small square from the exact centre of the folded packet. After full opening, what pattern of holes is seen?

A. A single square hole in the centre of the paper
B. Four small square holes, one in each quadrant, symmetrically placed
C. Two square holes on the vertical centre line
D. One large square hole covering the centre

Correct Answer: B
Step-by-step:

The paper is quarter-folded. The centre of the folded packet is not on any fold line (it is away from both folds).

Unfolding the last fold (horizontal) mirrors the square to the opposite quadrant along the horizontal fold, giving two squares.

Unfolding the first fold (vertical) mirrors each of those two squares across the vertical fold, resulting in four identical square holes, one in each quadrant.
Source: SSC CGL 2020

Q53. (Medium)
A circular paper is folded into a semicircle, then into a quarter circle. A punch is made through all layers at the midpoint of the radius (i.e., halfway between the centre and the curved edge) on the line that bisects the quarter circle. How many holes will the paper have when fully opened?

A. 2
B. 4
C. 6
D. 8

Correct Answer: B
Step-by-step:

Two folds (semicircle then quarter). The punch is on the bisector line of the quarter circle, not on any fold line.

Undo the second fold (quarter to semicircle): the hole is reflected across that fold line, producing 2 holes on the semicircle.

Undo the first fold (semicircle to full circle): each of the 2 holes is reflected across the diameter fold, yielding a total of 4 holes symmetrically placed on the original circle.
Source: RRB NTPC 2019

Q54. (Medium)
A square paper is folded along a diagonal. A cut is made that removes a thin strip starting from the hypotenuse (the open edge) and going inwards, stopping exactly at the diagonal fold line. When the paper is unfolded, what is the shape of the cut-out area?

A. A single strip along the diagonal
B. A V-shaped cut that starts and ends on the edges
C. Two separate strips meeting at the centre
D. A straight line cut along the diagonal

Correct Answer: B
Step-by-step:

The strip is cut from the hypotenuse, perpendicular to the fold, and stops at the fold.

When mirrored across the diagonal fold, the strip is reflected, creating an identical strip on the other half. Because the strips meet at the fold line, they form a continuous V-shaped opening (like an elongated V) pointing towards the centre of the square.
Source: SSC GD 2018

Q56. (Medium)
A paper is folded twice: first along the vertical centre, then along the horizontal centre. A cut is made that removes a small triangle from the corner that is the intersection of the two folded edges (i.e., the inner corner where the folds meet). B. 2
C. 4

Correct Answer: C
Step-by-step:

The cut triangle is at the corner that lies on both fold lines (the meeting point of the two fold edges).

Unfold the horizontal fold: the triangle is mirrored across the horizontal fold, creating a second triangle attached along that fold line. Now we have a shape like two triangles sharing a side along the horizontal fold.

Unfold the vertical fold: that whole shape is mirrored across the vertical fold, resulting in four triangles total, one at each corner of the original paper's inner central square? Actually, they will be located symmetrically, forming four triangular holes around the centre.
Source: SSC CHSL 2020

Q57. (Medium)
A paper is folded along the vertical centre line. A cut is made in the shape of a crescent moon, with the inner curve of the crescent touching the fold line. After opening, what is the shape of the hole?

A. Two separate crescents
B. A single crescent shape
C. An oval-like shape formed by two crescents joined at their inner curves
D. A full moon shape

Correct Answer: C
Step-by-step:

The crescent has its inner concave edge touching the fold. Mirroring reflects the crescent, creating a symmetric shape where the two inner curves meet at the fold line, forming a pointed oval or lens-like shape (like two crescents back-to-back).

Option A would be if the crescent did not touch the fold; B is single; D is a full circle.
*Source: RRB Group-D 2019*

Q58. (Medium)
A square paper is folded diagonally. A punch is made exactly at the midpoint of the hypotenuse (the open edge) of the resulting triangle. After unfolding, how many holes are there and where are they located?

A. One hole at the centre of the square
B. Two holes, one at each end of the diagonal
C. One hole on the diagonal, halfway between centre and corner
D. Two holes symmetric about the diagonal, but not on the diagonal

Correct Answer: D
Step-by-step:

The punch is on the hypotenuse, which is the open edge, not the fold. The midpoint of the hypotenuse is offset from the diagonal fold line.

Mirror across the diagonal fold reflects that point to a symmetric location on the other half of the square. So we get two holes, one on each side of the diagonal, equidistant from it, near the opposite corners.
Source: SSC CGL 2018

Q59. (Medium)
A paper is folded once. A cut is made that removes a long narrow rectangle from the folded edge, but the cut does not extend to the fold line (it stops short). After opening, the paper will have:

A. One narrow rectangular hole
B. Two separate narrow rectangular holes
C. One wide rectangular hole
D. Two rectangular holes connected by a thin slit

Correct Answer: B
Step-by-step:

The rectangle is cut entirely on one side of the fold, not touching it. Mirroring creates an identical rectangle on the other half, separated by an uncut margin along the fold. So two separate holes.
Source: SSC GD 2020

Q60. (Medium)
A triangular paper (equilateral) is folded along its altitude. A small circle is punched on the folded triangle such that the circle's centre lies exactly on the fold line. When opened, how many holes are visible?

A. 1
B. 2
C. 3
D. 4

Correct Answer: A
Step-by-step:

Single fold, punch on fold line. The circle straddles the fold, so the two halves of the circle (one on each layer) combine into one circular hole when unfolded. Only one hole, centred on the altitude.
Source: SSC MTS 2019

Q61. (Medium)
A square paper is folded first along the vertical centre line, then along the horizontal centre line. Now a cut is made that removes a small square from the open corner farthest from both fold lines. After complete unfolding, how many square holes appear?

A. 1
B. 2
C. 3
D. 4

Correct Answer: D
Step-by-step:

The cut square is at the corner that has no fold lines (the free corner of the quadruple layer).

Undo horizontal fold: the square is mirrored to the adjacent quadrant, giving 2 squares.

Undo vertical fold: each of those is mirrored, giving 4 squares, one at each of the four corners of the original paper.
Source: SSC CHSL 2021

Q62. (Medium)
A paper is folded once vertically. A shape resembling a right-angled triangle is cut out, with the hypotenuse on the fold line. After opening, the hole will be:

A. A right-angled triangle
B. An isosceles triangle
C. A square
D. A shape like two right triangles joined along the hypotenuse, forming a rectangle

Correct Answer: D
Step-by-step:

The cut triangle has its hypotenuse on the fold. Mirroring across the fold reflects the triangle, resulting in two congruent right triangles attached along the hypotenuse. Together they form a rectangle (if the legs are perpendicular). So the hole is a rectangle.
Source: RRB NTPC 2018

Q63. (Medium)
A paper is folded along the vertical centre line. On the folded paper, a small semi-circle is cut out from the open edge (the edge opposite the fold), with the diameter of the semi-circle lying on that open edge. After opening, what is seen?

A. Two semi-circles at the centre of the paper
B. Two semi-circles at the left and right edges of the paper
C. One full circle at the edge
D. Two semi-circles, one on each half, both on the top edge

Correct Answer: B
Step-by-step:

The cut semi-circle is on the open edge, away from the fold. The fold is vertical; the open edge is the right edge of the folded paper.

Mirroring across the vertical fold places an identical semi-circle on the left side of the paper, on the original left edge. So the opened paper has two semi-circular cut-outs, one on the left edge and one on the right edge, symmetrically placed.
Source: SSC GD 2017

Q64. (Medium)
A paper is folded twice: first diagonally, then again along a line parallel to the first diagonal but not at the centre, producing a narrow strip. A punch is made at the centre of the folded strip. After complete unfolding, how many holes are there?

A. 2
B. 4
C. 8
D. 16

Correct Answer: B
Step-by-step:

Two folds (first diagonal, second parallel to diagonal). The punch is at the centre of the final folded shape, which is not on any fold line (since the second fold is offset).

Unfold second fold: hole reflected across that second fold line, giving 2 holes.

Unfold first fold: each hole reflected across the first diagonal, giving 4 holes in total on the original square.
Source: SSC CGL 2022

Q65. (Medium – Examiner’s Trap: Overlapping holes from punch on fold with multiple folds)
A square paper is folded along the vertical midline, then along the horizontal midline. A single punch is made exactly at the centre of the folded square (which is the point where the two fold edges meet). After fully opening, how many holes are there?

A. 1
B. 2
C. 4
D. 8

Correct Answer: A
Step-by-step:

The paper is quarter-folded. The centre of the folded square is the corner where the two fold edges meet — that is, the point that is the intersection of the original vertical and horizontal fold lines.

The punch is made exactly at that intersection. Because the point lies on both fold lines, when the paper is unfolded, the hole is reflected across each fold, but all reflections coincide at the same central point. Thus only one hole appears, at the exact centre of the original square.
*Trap: Many students apply the 2ⁿ formula (2²=4) blindly and choose 4, ignoring that the punch is on the folds and the holes merge.*
Source: SSC CGL 2023 (Tricky)

Q66. (Medium)
A paper is folded once along the vertical centre. A cut is made that removes a shape like a plus sign (+), with its centre exactly on the fold line and one arm lying along the fold. After opening, what does the hole look like?

A. A plus sign that is wider horizontally
B. Two separate plus signs
C. A shape like a plus sign with an extra horizontal arm
D. A single symmetrical plus sign, identical to the original cut but centred on the fold

Correct Answer: A
Step-by-step:

The plus sign has its vertical arm on the fold line, horizontal arm crossing at the centre. The centre is on the fold.

When mirrored, the half of the plus sign on one side is reflected to the other. Since the vertical arm lies on the fold, it remains single; the horizontal arm gets extended to both sides. The resulting hole is a plus sign that is twice as wide horizontally (because the horizontal arm now extends equally to left and right from the fold). So it appears as a wider plus sign.
Source: SSC CHSL 2022

Q68. (Medium)

Q75. (Medium – Examiner’s Trap: Asymmetrical shape with rotation)
A square paper is folded along the vertical centre line. An asymmetrical shape like a flag (a rectangle with a triangular cutout on one side) is cut from the folded paper, with the flagpole on the fold line. Which of the following correctly shows the unfolded hole?

A. A flag with two triangular cutouts on opposite sides
B. A flag with the triangular cutout rotated 90° on the other half
C. A flag and its mirror image forming a symmetric banner shape
D. Two separate flags, one on each half, both facing the same direction

Correct Answer: C
Step-by-step:

The flag has its pole on the fold. Mirroring reflects the entire shape, producing a mirror image on the other half. The two flags share the pole and face opposite directions, creating a symmetric banner-like shape.

Option B suggests rotation, which is a trap; option D suggests no mirroring (same direction).
Trap: Students often confuse reflection with rotation, selecting B because the shape "flips" orientation, but a mirror image is not a rotation.
Source: SSC CHSL 2023 (Medium)
