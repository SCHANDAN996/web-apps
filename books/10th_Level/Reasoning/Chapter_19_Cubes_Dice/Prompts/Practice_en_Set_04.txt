Question: Two views of a dice are given. View1: top 2, front 5, right 1. View2: top 2, front 6, right 3. Which number is opposite to 2?
Options: A) 1 B) 3 C) 4 D) 5
Answer: C) 4
Solution: Common top 2. Clockwise from 2 in View1 gives 5,1; View2 gives 6,3. So 5↔6, 1↔3. Remaining 4 is opposite 2.
Source: SSC CGL 2019

Question: A non-standard dice has opposite pairs (1,4), (2,6), (3,5). Which of the following views is possible?
Options: A) Top 1, Front 2, Right 3 B) Top 1, Front 4, Right 5 C) Top 2, Front 1, Right 6 D) Top 3, Front 5, Right 6
Answer: A) Top 1, Front 2, Right 3
Solution: Opposite pairs cannot appear together. B: 1 and 4 appear together, invalid. C: 2 and 6 appear together, invalid. D: 3 and 5 appear together, invalid. Only A has no opposite pair.
Source: SSC CHSL 2020

Question: A dice has numbers such that 1 opposite 3, 2 opposite 5, 4 opposite 6. If 1 is on top and 2 in front, what is the right face?
Options: A) 4 B) 5 C) 6 D) Cannot be determined
Answer: A) 4
Solution: Opposite pairs: (1,3), (2,5), (4,6). 1 top, 2 front. Right face must be adjacent to both 1 and 2. Adjacent to 1: 2,4,5,6. Adjacent to 2: 1,3,4,6. Common adjacent to both: 4,6. Orientation: with 1 top, 2 front, right could be 4 (then left 6) or 6 (left 4). In many such dice, the arrangement follows a right-hand rule. But given standard pattern, one possible answer is 4. (If not sure, could be "Cannot be determined", but exam often expects 4 based on typical spatial arrangement). Actually, with only opposite pairs, the exact right face can't be uniquely determined without seeing the dice's handedness. So trap: answer D) Cannot be determined.
Answer: D) Cannot be determined
