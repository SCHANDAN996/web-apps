🧒 फेनमैन तकनीक + ब्लर्टिंग शीट (Feynman’s Way: The Dice That Fooled Arjun)

Arjun wanted to build his own Ludo set. He took a piece of cardboard, drew six squares in a cross shape, and wrote numbers 1 to 6 on them. He was sure that after folding, 1 would be opposite 2, because they were at the ends of the cross. He folded, and… 1 ended up right next to 2. “How can that be?” he cried.

Here’s the thing, said his elder sister who loved puzzles. “Opposite faces are like ex-best friends who can’t stand being neighbors. In the flat net, they never share a side — they are separated by exactly one face in the same straight line.”

“Take the middle row. The first and third (2 and 4) become opposite. The second and fourth (3 and 5) become opposite. And the top and bottom lone faces (1 and 6) become opposite. Always.”

Arjun folded it again — and it worked. He never forgot that rule.

The Unforgettable Trick: The ‘Cross-Stretch’ Rule
In any cross-shaped dice net, find the row with four faces. Label them A, B, C, D. The faces that are one apart in that row are opposites: A ↔ C, B ↔ D. And the lonely face above the middle is opposite the lonely face below the middle. That’s it — you’ve unlocked all three opposite pairs instantly, no folding required.

One question to test yourself:
If a cross net has numbers: top lone face = 3, bottom lone face = 4, and middle row faces (left to right) = 5, 1, 2, 6. Which number is opposite to 6? (Try it before looking up. Answer: 6 is in the middle row, second from left? Wait, middle row: 5, 1, 2, 6. The second and fourth are 1 and 6. So opposite of 6 is 1. Check: The top and bottom are 3 and 4 opposite. First and third are 5 and 2 opposite.) Correct? Good. Now go and teach this to someone — that’s the true Feynman way.
