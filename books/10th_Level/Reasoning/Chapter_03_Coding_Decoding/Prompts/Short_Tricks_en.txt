🧠 Mnemonic 1: EJOTY – “Every Joker On Television Yells”
E = 5, J = 10, O = 15, T = 20, Y = 25.
Imagine a TV show where a joker screams these numbers every 5 seconds. Use these milestones to find any letter’s position instantly (e.g., M is 2 after J → 12). Never count from A again.

🧠 Mnemonic 2: 27 – The Magic Mirror
“27 minus your position, you see your reflection!”
To find the opposite letter (A ↔ Z, B ↔ Y, etc.), just subtract your position from 27.
Example: A = 1 → 27 – 1 = 26 = Z. K = 11 → 27 – 11 = 16 = P.
Picture holding up a number to a mirror and the reflection showing (27 – number).

🧠 Mnemonic 3: The Zoo of Opposite Couples
A-Z, B-Y, C-X, D-W, E-V, F-U, G-T, H-S, I-R, J-Q, K-P, L-O, M-N.
Story: “In a zoo, an Alligator and a Zebra Yelled at a Big X-ray of a Cat and a Dog Wrestling. An Elephant and a Fox were Under a Tree while a Goat and a Tiger Hugged a Snake. An Igloo Rabbit argued with a Joker Queen, a King Penguin danced with a Lion, and an Owl and a Monkey took a Nap.” Each pair adds to 27.

🧠 Mnemonic 4: +1 Shift – “One Step Forward, Everyone Moves”
If CAT becomes DBU, just imagine each letter taking one step forward in a marching band. For coding, +1 always. For decoding, -1. Like a conga line: just follow the leader.

🧠 Mnemonic 5: Reverse First, Then Shift – “Flip Before the Trip”
Some codes are like a roller‑coaster: first, the word is reversed (like a backflip), then a shift is applied. Example: RISE → ESIR → +1 → FTJS. Think: “Flip the word, then take a step.”

🧠 Mnemonic 6: Number Coding – “Alphabet = Number Plate”
A=1, B=2, …, Z=26. Imagine each letter wearing a jersey with its position number. BAT = 2, 1, 20 → 2120. It’s like reading a car’s number plate but for letters.

🧠 Mnemonic 7: Mixed Coding – “Alien Language Decoder”
When you see symbols, numbers, and letters mixed (A=@, B=2, C=#), imagine an alien dictionary. Just replace each symbol with its letter according to the given mapping. No panic – it’s just a substitution game.

🧠 Mnemonic 8: Vowel‑Consonant Trap – “Vowels are VIPs”
Sometimes vowels get special treatment (e.g., +2 instead of +1). Remember: Vowels are Very Important People – they might demand a different shift. Always check if vowels follow a separate rule.

🧠 Mnemonic 9: Positional Shift – “Staircase Coding”
If the shift increases by 1, 2, 3, 4… for each position, imagine climbing stairs. First step +1, second +2, third +3… So for word “HOT”: H+1=I, O+2=Q, T+3=W. It’s like taking bigger steps each time.

🧠 Mnemonic 10: Double‑Layer Trap – “The Onion Code”
Some codes have two layers – peel them one by one. Example: first reverse the word, then apply a shift. If you see a pattern that almost works but not quite, think “Is there another layer?” Like peeling an onion, you might cry but you’ll get to the core.

🧠 Mnemonic 11: The 27‑Sum Marriage
All opposite pairs (A+Z, B+Y, …, M+N) sum to exactly 27. Think of them as 13 perfect marriages. If you know one partner, you can find the other by subtracting from 27. A and Z are happily married with sum 27 forever.

🧠 Mnemonic 12: Elimination – “Kill the Options”
Don’t decode the whole word. Just code the first one or two letters and check which option matches. This “sniping” technique saves time. Imagine you’re a sniper picking off wrong options one by one.

🧠 Mnemonic 13: EJOTY’s Reverse Cousin – “VQLGA”
If you need the reverse letter positions quickly, remember V=5, Q=10, L=15, G=20, A=25 (the 5th letters from the end). But the 27–x formula is still easier. Just a backup.

🧠 Mnemonic 14: Complex Shift – “Keyboard Stuck Keys”
When shifts seem random (e.g., +2, +3, +2, +3…), imagine a keyboard with sticky keys that sometimes jump an extra step. Write the word and code one above the other; the pattern often pops out visually.

🧠 Mnemonic 15: The 15‑Second Flash Rule
If the code pattern doesn’t flash in your mind within 15 seconds, it’s likely a trap. Mark it, skip it, and return later. Like a traffic signal – if it’s not green quickly, move on to the next road.

🪄 Skip Strategy Tips

15‑Second Pattern Check: If you cannot identify any possible rule (shift, reverse, mirror) within 15 seconds, immediately mark the question and skip. Do not waste time staring.

45‑Second Hard Stop: Even if you found a pattern, if solving the whole code takes more than 45 seconds (including checking all letters), pause, eliminate options, and if still stuck, skip. You can revisit with fresh eyes.

One‑Letter Elimination: When skipping, quickly code the first letter of the given word and eliminate options that don’t match. This narrows down choices even without fully solving.
