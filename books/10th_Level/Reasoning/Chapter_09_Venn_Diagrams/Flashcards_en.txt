Front: What is a Venn diagram made of?
Back: Overlapping circles that show the relationship between groups.

Front: Who falls in the shared (overlap) part of two circles?
Back: Those who are in both groups (A ∩ B).

Front: What is the formula for n(A∪B)?
Back: n(A∪B) = n(A) + n(B) − n(A∩B).

Front: Why do we subtract "− n(A∩B)" in the formula?
Back: Because those in both are counted twice; they must be subtracted once.

Front: How do you find "only A"?
Back: Only A = n(A) − n(A∩B).

Front: How do you find "neither"?
Back: Neither = total − n(A∪B).

Front: What does the diagram for "all A are B" look like?
Back: A's circle fully inside B.

Front: The diagram for "some A are B"?
Back: The two circles overlap a little.

Front: The diagram for "no A is B"?
Back: Two completely separate (disjoint) circles.

Front: Dog, Cat, Animal — what does the diagram look like?
Back: Dog and Cat are separate circles, both inside the big 'Animal' circle.

Front: What is the union formula for three groups?
Back: n(A∪B∪C) = sum − (pairwise shared) + (all-three shared).

Front: 30 cricket, 20 football, 10 both — at least one?
Back: 30 + 20 − 10 = 40.

Front: Difference between "only A" and "A"?
Back: "Only A" = in A but not B; "A" = the whole A circle (including the overlap).

Front: The most common trap in numerical Venn?
Back: Forgetting to subtract the shared part (double counting).

Front: What is the Venn mnemonic?
Back: "Add both, subtract shared".
