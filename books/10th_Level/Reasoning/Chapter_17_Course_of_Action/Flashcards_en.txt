Front: What matters most when choosing a course of action?
Back: Solving the problem — not punishment.

Front: What are the three checks of a correct course of action?
Back: (1) related to the problem, (2) practical, (3) not extreme.

Front: Give an example of an extreme action.
Back: "Disease has spread → jail everyone" (too harsh, wrong).

Front: Give an example of a solution-oriented action.
Back: "Disease has spread → run a vaccination drive" (correct).

Front: Which is better, "prevention" or "punishment"?
Back: Prevention/solution — a good action prevents the problem.

Front: Should an unrelated action be considered correct?
Back: No — one not directly related to the problem is not correct.

Front: Is a too-harsh or impossible action correct?
Back: No — extreme actions are usually wrong.

Front: Can both actions be correct at the same time?
Back: Yes, sometimes both, sometimes neither — check each separately.

Front: Why is the practicality of an action important?
Back: Because one that can't actually be carried out is not a correct action.

Front: How is an action like "shut it all down"?
Back: Extreme — usually wrong.

Front: How do you check each action?
Back: Separately — applying the three checks (related, practical, moderate).

Front: Does a good action only punish, or also solve?
Back: Also solves — punishment alone is often incomplete.

Front: What is the most common trap?
Back: Treating an extreme (too harsh) action as correct.

Front: Which action should you cut first?
Back: One not directly related to the problem.

Front: What is the course-of-action mnemonic?
Back: "Related, practical, moderate".
