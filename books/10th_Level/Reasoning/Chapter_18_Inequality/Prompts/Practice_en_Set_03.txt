Q51. In a certain code language, the symbols represent the following meanings:
‘A @ B’ means A is not greater than B (A ≤ B)
‘A # B’ means A is not less than B (A ≥ B)
‘A B’ means A is neither greater than nor equal to B (A < B) ‘A % B’ means A is neither less than nor equal to B (A > B) ‘A * B’ means A is neither greater than nor less than B (A = B) **Statements:** X # Y Y @ Z Z A
A % B
Conclusions:
I. X % A
II. X * A
(a) Only I follows
(b) Only II follows
(c) Either I or II follows
(d) Neither I nor II follows

Answer: (d) Neither I nor II follows
Solution:
Decoded statements: X ≥ Y, Y ≤ Z, Z < A, A > B.
Combined chain: X ≥ Y ≤ Z < A.
The relation between X and A cannot be determined; all possibilities (X > A, X = A, X < A) exist.
Conclusion I (X > A) is not definite. Conclusion II (X = A) is not definite. Moreover, they do not form a complementary pair because the possibility X < A is missing. Hence, neither follows.
Trap: The presence of > and = tempts students to apply Either-Or without checking if all possibilities are covered.
Source: IBPS PO 2021

Q52. Statements:
A > B = C ≥ D < E; F ≤ D > G
Conclusions:
I. A > F
II. B > G
(a) Only I follows
(b) Only II follows
(c) Both follow
(d) Neither I nor II follows

Answer: (c) Both follow
Solution:
From A > B = C ≥ D ≥ F ⇒ A > F (I follows).
From B = C ≥ D > G ⇒ B > G (II follows).
Both are definite.
Source: SSC CGL 2020

Q53. In a certain code:
‘A α B’ means A > B
‘A β B’ means A < B
‘A γ B’ means A = B
‘A δ B’ means A ≥ B
‘A ε B’ means A ≤ B
Statements:
P α Q
Q β R
R γ S
S ε T
Conclusions:
I. P β T
II. P α T
(a) Only I follows
(b) Only II follows
(c) Either I or II follows
(d) Neither I nor II follows

Answer: (d) Neither I nor II follows
Solution:
Decoded: P > Q, Q < R, R = S, S ≤ T.
Chain: P > Q < R = S ≤ T.
The connection between P and T breaks at Q because of opposite signs. All relations between P and T (>, <, =) are possible. I (P < T) and II (P > T) are individually false and do not form a complementary pair (equality missing). Thus neither follows.
Source: SBI PO 2022

Q54. Which of the following expressions will make ‘M ≤ N’ definitely true?
(a) M > O ≥ N
(b) M < O ≤ N
(c) M = O > N
(d) M ≥ O < N

Answer: (b) M < O ≤ N
Solution:
In option (b): M < O ≤ N ⇒ M < N, which always satisfies M ≤ N.
Other options do not guarantee M ≤ N.
Source: RRB NTPC 2021

Q55. Statements:
M ≥ N > O = P
Q ≤ P
Conclusions:
I. M > O
II. M = O
(a) Only I follows
(b) Only II follows
(c) Either I or II follows
(d) Neither I nor II follows

Answer: (a) Only I follows
Solution:
Combine: M ≥ N > O ⇒ M > O (definite). Thus I is true.
Conclusion II (M = O) is false.
Trap: The statement M ≥ N > O might appear to give M ≥ O, tempting students to treat I and II as an Either-Or pair. However, the chain actually forces M > O, so II cannot be true.
Source: IBPS Clerk 2019

Q58. Statements:

Q60. Which set of symbols should replace the blanks in the expression ‘A _ B _ C _ D’ so that both ‘A ≥ C’ and ‘B > D’ definitely hold?
(a) ≥, >, =
(b) >, ≥, >
(c) =, ≥, <
(d) ≥, =, ≥

Answer: (a) ≥, >, =
Solution:
Expression: A ≥ B > C = D.
From A ≥ B > C, we get A > C, hence A ≥ C true.
From B > C = D, we get B > D true.
Other options do not satisfy both conditions simultaneously.
Source: SSC CGL 2022

Q61. Which set of symbols should replace the blanks in ‘M _ N _ O _ P’ so that both ‘M > O’ and ‘N ≤ P’ definitely hold?

Solution:
Expression: M > N = O ≤ P.

Other options fail to guarantee one or both conditions.
Source: IBPS Clerk 2021

Q65. Statements:
P = Q > R ≥ S; T < S; U ≥ T
Conclusions:
I. Q > U
II. R > T
III. P ≥ S
(a) Only II follows
(b) II and III follow
(c) I and II follow
(d) All follow

Answer: (b) II and III follow
Solution:
Q > R ≥ S > T and U ≥ T, but U could be larger than Q (e.g., Q=10, T=5, U=20), so I is false.
R ≥ S > T ⇒ R > T (II true).
P = Q > R ≥ S ⇒ P > S, so P ≥ S is true (III true).
Source: IBPS Clerk 2022
