Q1. Statement: A > B > C
Conclusions: I. A > C II. B < A
(a) Only I follows
(b) Only II follows
(c) Both follow
(d) None follows

Answer: (c) Both follow
Solution:
From A > B > C, we get A > C (True). Also B < A (True). Hence both follow.
Source: IBPS Clerk 2019

Q2. Statement: P ≥ Q = R
Conclusions: I. P > R II. P = R
(a) Only I follows
(b) Only II follows
(c) Either I or II follows
(d) Neither I nor II follows

Answer: (c) Either I or II follows
Solution:
P ≥ Q = R ⇒ P ≥ R. This allows both P > R and P = R. Neither is individually definite, but together they cover all possibilities. Hence either I or II follows.
Trap: Students often mark only I or only II, forgetting the double possibility hidden in ‘≥’.
Source: IBPS PO 2018

Q3. Statement: M > N < O
Conclusions: I. M > O II. M < O
(a) Only I follows
(b) Only II follows
(c) Either I or II follows
(d) Neither I nor II follows

Answer: (d) Neither I nor II follows
Solution:
Opposite signs meeting at N — no definite relation between M and O. Both conclusions are false.
Source: RRB NTPC 2018

Q4. Statements: S > T, T ≥ U
Conclusions: I. S > U II. S = U
(a) Only I follows
(b) Only II follows
(c) Either I or II follows
(d) Neither I nor II follows

Answer: (a) Only I follows
Solution:
Combining: S > T ≥ U ⇒ S > U (definite). S = U is not possible.
Source: SSC CGL 2020

Q6. Statement: A < B ≤ C = D > E
Conclusions: I. A < D II. C > E
(a) Only I follows
(b) Only II follows
(c) Both follow
(d) None follows

Answer: (c) Both follow
Solution:
A < B ≤ C = D ⇒ A < D. C = D > E ⇒ C > E. Both are definite.
Source: IBPS Clerk 2020

Q7. Statement: X ≥ Y
Conclusions: I. X > Y II. X < Y
(a) Only I follows
(b) Only II follows
(c) Either I or II follows
(d) Neither I nor II follows

Answer: (d) Neither I nor II follows
Solution:
X ≥ Y means X is greater than or equal to Y. Neither X > Y nor X < Y is definite individually. Moreover, the pair (>, <) is not complementary because the equality possibility is missing. Hence neither follows.
Trap: Many students mistake > and < for an Either–Or pair. A complementary pair must cover all possibilities (here = is not covered).
Source: SBI PO 2017

Q8. Statement: P = Q > R < S
Conclusions: I. P > R II. Q < S
(a) Only I follows
(b) Only II follows
(c) Both follow
(d) Neither I nor II follows

Answer: (a) Only I follows
Solution:
P = Q > R ⇒ P > R (True). Q > R < S ⇒ no relation between Q and S (False).
Source: RRB PO 2019

Q9. Statement: M ≤ N ≤ O
Conclusions: I. M < O II. M = O
(a) Only I follows
(b) Only II follows
(c) Either I or II follows
(d) Neither I nor II follows

Answer: (c) Either I or II follows
Solution:
M ≤ N ≤ O ⇒ M ≤ O. The possibilities are M < O or M = O. Neither is certain alone, but together they form an Either–Or pair.
Source: IBPS Clerk 2018

Q10. (Coded) Symbols: ‘A * B’ ⇒ A = B, ‘A @ B’ ⇒ A ≥ B, ‘A # B’ ⇒ A ≤ B, ‘A $ B’ ⇒ A > B, ‘A % B’ ⇒ A < B
Statements: P * Q, Q @ R
Conclusions: I. P @ R II. R # P
(a) Only I follows
(b) Only II follows
(c) Both follow
(d) Neither I nor II follows

Answer: (c) Both follow
Solution:
P = Q, Q ≥ R ⇒ P = Q ≥ R. Hence P ≥ R (I true) and R ≤ P (II true). Both follow.
Source: SSC CGL 2021

Q11. Statements: D > E ≥ F = G

Solution:
D > E ≥ F = G ⇒ D > G (True). E ≥ F = G ⇒ E ≥ G (True).

Q15. (Coded) Symbols: ‘A δ B’ ⇒ A ≥ B, ‘A φ B’ ⇒ A ≤ B, ‘A γ B’ ⇒ A > B, ‘A β B’ ⇒ A < B, ‘A θ B’ ⇒ A = B
Statements: X δ Y, Y θ Z
Conclusions: I. X γ Z II. X θ Z
(a) Only I follows
(b) Only II follows
(c) Either I or II follows
(d) Neither I nor II follows

Answer: (c) Either I or II follows
Solution:
X δ Y ⇒ X ≥ Y, Y θ Z ⇒ Y = Z. So X ≥ Y = Z ⇒ X ≥ Z. Thus either X > Z or X = Z. Hence Either I or II follows.
Source: IBPS PO 2016

Solution:
K > L ≥ M ⇒ K > M (True). L ≥ M ≤ N gives no relation between L and N (False).

Q18. Statements: P = Q > R; S ≤ R
Conclusions: I. P > S II. Q ≥ S
(a) Only I follows
(b) Only II follows
(c) Both follow
(d) Neither I nor II follows

Answer: (c) Both follow
Solution:

Source: IBPS Clerk 2019

Q19. (Coded) Symbols: ‘A + B’ ⇒ A is not less than B (A ≥ B), ‘A – B’ ⇒ A is not greater than B (A ≤ B), ‘A × B’ ⇒ A is neither greater than nor equal to B (A < B), ‘A ÷ B’ ⇒ A is neither less than nor equal to B (A > B), ‘A = B’ ⇒ A is neither greater than nor less than B (A = B)
Statements: P – Q, Q + R
Conclusions: I. P – R II. Q ÷ R
(a) Only I follows
(b) Only II follows
(c) Both follow
(d) Neither I nor II follows

Answer: (d) Neither I nor II follows
Solution:
Decode: P – Q ⇒ P ≤ Q; Q + R ⇒ Q ≥ R. Thus P ≤ Q ≥ R. No definite relation between P and R (I false). Q ÷ R means Q > R. From Q ≥ R we cannot definitely say Q > R (II false).
Source: SSC CGL 2018

(a) Only I follows
(b) Only II follows
(c) Both follow
(d) Neither I nor II follows

Answer: (a) Only I follows
Solution:
E > F ≥ G = H ⇒ E > H (True). F ≥ G = H ≤ I ⇒ no definite relation between F and I (False).
Source: IBPS PO 2017

Q21. (Coded) Symbols: ‘A @ B’ ⇒ A ≤ B, ‘A # B’ ⇒ A ≥ B, ‘A B’ ⇒ A > B, ‘A % B’ ⇒ A < B Statements: P # Q, Q % R, R S
Conclusions: I. P $ R II. P % R
(a) Only I follows
(b) Only II follows
(c) Either I or II follows
(d) Neither I nor II follows

Answer: (d) Neither I nor II follows
Solution:
Decode: P ≥ Q, Q < R, R > S. So P ≥ Q < R. The chain gives no relation between P and R. The conclusions P > R and P < R are both possible in different scenarios, but equality P = R is also possible. So neither is definite, and they do not form a valid Either–Or pair (missing =). Hence neither follows.
Trap: The presence of > and < tempts students to apply Either–Or without checking the equality possibility.
Source: SBI PO 2021

Q22. Which of the following expressions ensures ‘T ≤ W’ is definitely true?
(a) T < U ≥ V = W
(b) T ≤ U < V ≤ W
(c) T ≥ U > V = W
(d) T > U < V ≤ W

Answer: (b) T ≤ U < V ≤ W
Solution:
In (b): T ≤ U < V ≤ W ⇒ T < W, which certainly satisfies T ≤ W. All other options do not guarantee T ≤ W.
Source: SSC CGL 2019

Q23. Statements: M = N ≥ O < P; Q ≤ P > R
Conclusions: I. M > O II. M = O
(a) Only I follows
(b) Only II follows
(c) Either I or II follows
(d) Neither I nor II follows

Answer: (c) Either I or II follows
Solution:
M = N ≥ O ⇒ M ≥ O. Hence M > O or M = O are the only possibilities. Neither is definite alone, but they form a complementary pair.
Source: IBPS PO 2022

Q24. Statements: A ≥ B > C < D ≤ E; F > E ≥ G
Conclusions: I. A > C II. F > C
(a) Only I follows
(b) Only II follows
(c) Both follow
(d) Neither I nor II follows

Answer: (c) Both follow
Solution:
A ≥ B > C ⇒ A > C (True). C < D ≤ E < F ⇒ C < F, i.e. F > C (True).
Source: SBI PO 2020
