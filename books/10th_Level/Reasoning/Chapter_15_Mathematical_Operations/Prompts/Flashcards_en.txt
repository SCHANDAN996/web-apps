🃏 Flashcards | गणितीय संक्रियाएँ / Mathematical Operations

Card 1
Front: What is the first thing you must do when you see a question like “If ‘+’ means ‘–’, ‘–’ means ‘×’, then 8 + 4 – 2 = ?”
Back: Replace all symbols with their new meanings first. Then apply BODMAS. Never solve with the original symbols.

Card 2
Front: The moment you see changed meanings of +, –, ×, ÷ in a question, what mental habit should kick in automatically?
Back: Think “RDB” — Replace, Decode, BODMAS. Or remember “Rohan’s grandmother makes” (रोहन की दादी बनाती हैं). First replace all symbols, then decode, then BODMAS.

Card 3
Front: In a mathematical operation problem, does changing the meaning of a symbol change the BODMAS rule?
Back: No. The BODMAS hierarchy applies to the original mathematical operations, not to the code words. So after you replace symbols with their actual operations, BODMAS stays unchanged.

Card 4
Front: If ‘×’ means ‘+’, ‘÷’ means ‘–’, ‘+’ means ‘×’, and ‘–’ means ‘÷’, what does 12 ÷ 6 + 3 × 2 – 4 become after substitution?
Back: Replace: 12 – 6 × 3 + 2 ÷ 4. Then solve with BODMAS. (No need to solve fully here; just the transformed expression.)

Card 5
Front: What is the most common mistake students make in “sign interchange” problems where you need to balance an equation?
Back: They try to solve the equation from scratch instead of testing each option by swapping the two signs and checking which makes the equation true. Testing options is faster and more accurate.

Card 6
Front: What is the mnemonic “SIGN बदलो, BALANCE ढूँढो” used for?
Back: For “sign interchange” problems. It reminds you to swap the two signs given in each option and then check if the equation becomes balanced, rather than solving randomly.

Card 7
Front: In a question, it says “P means greater than, Q means equal to, R means less than.” What is the examiner’s favorite trap in such questions?
Back: They provide options that look almost true, like A > B and A ≥ B, where a slight difference makes one correct and the other wrong. Always write the full relationship chain instead of guessing.

Card 8
Front: Why is it dangerous to quickly mark an answer when the question uses weird symbols like ‘&’, ‘$’, ‘@’ with definitions like “one more than” or “half of”?
Back: Because you might misread the definition under exam pressure—e.g., mistake “& means one more than” as +1 when it might be “+1 if the number is even” (if tricky). Examiner uses heavy language to create confusion. Read each word slowly.

Card 9
Front: What is the “double-relation trap” in mathematical operations?
Back: When a question mixes mathematical operations with family relations or directions, e.g., “P # Q means P is father of Q, then A + B means?” Students often solve it as pure math, forgetting the defined mapping. Always first map symbols to relations.

Card 10
Front: After replacing all symbols in an expression, you get 20 – 4 + 6 ÷ 3 × 2. According to BODMAS, which operation do you perform first?
Back: Division: 6 ÷ 3 = 2, making it 20 – 4 + 2 × 2. Then multiplication: 2 × 2 = 4. Then addition and subtraction left to right: 20 – 4 + 4 = 20.

Card 11
Front: Why should you “write out the transformed expression” on rough paper even if you think you can do it mentally?
Back: Because under time pressure, it’s easy to mis-substitute signs or forget a step. Writing reduces errors, especially in sign interchange and symbol substitution problems.

Card 12
Front: What is the RDB rule?
Back: Replace all symbols according to the given code, Decode the new expression to ensure it’s correct, then apply BODMAS. The mnemonic: “Rohan’s grandmother makes” — Replace, Decode, BODMAS.

Card 13
Front: If a question says “Do not apply BODMAS” explicitly, what should you do?
Back: Simply operate left to right after replacing symbols with their new meanings. But such instructions are rare—always check the question’s direction.

Card 14
Front: In an equation balancing question like “15 × 3 + 6 – 2 = 25”, what mental shortcut can you use if options give pairs of signs to swap?
Back: Quickly test each option by interchanging only those two signs in the equation, then check if LHS = RHS. This “reverse engineering” saves time.

Card 15
Front: How do you handle a problem where a single expression like “A $ B” has multiple meanings depending on context (e.g., A is greater than B, A is less than B, etc.)?
Back: Treat each coded relation separately. Write down what each symbol means, then translate the entire statement into plain mathematical inequalities/equalities. Then evaluate truth/falsity. Double-check all given options.

Card 16
Front: What is a quick way to remind yourself that BODMAS is applied to the actual operations, not the coded ones?
Back: Think of the coded symbols as “masks.” You first remove the masks (replace) and then let BODMAS work on the real face of the operations.

Card 17
Front: Examiner’s Trap: If you see “+ means ÷, ÷ means –, – means ×, × means +,” what is a common silly mistake students make?
Back: They substitute one symbol but forget to change another, or they apply the new meaning twice. Write down the mapping before solving, and cross-check.

Card 18
Front: Which part of the chapter does the mnemonic “रोहन की दादी बनाती हैं” help you remember?
Back: The sequence for solving any coded operation: Replace, Decode, BODMAS. (RDB)
