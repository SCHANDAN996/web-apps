Front: What is the core idea of analogy in one sentence?
Back: The same relationship exists between two pairs. Identify the first pair's relationship and apply it to the second.

Front: What is the most reliable rule to solve an analogy?
Back: Connect the given pair in a sentence, then test that same sentence on every option.

Front: What is the answer to "Cow : Calf :: Horse : ?"
Back: Foal. Sentence — "The young one of a cow is a calf, of a horse is a foal."

Front: "Bee : Honey" is which type of analogy?
Back: Worker–Product — a bee makes honey.

Front: What relationship does "Wheel : Car" show?
Back: Part–Whole — a wheel is a part of a car.

Front: Number analogy "4 : 16 :: 5 : ?" — answer?
Back: 25. Rule — squaring (4²=16, 5²=25).

Front: Number analogy "2 : 8 :: 3 : ?" — answer?
Back: 27. Rule — cubing (2³=8, 3³=27).

Front: Letter analogy "AB : CD :: EF : ?" — answer?
Back: GH. Each time, the next two letters of the alphabet.

Front: What does the EJOTY mnemonic help with?
Back: Quickly finding letter positions — E=5, J=10, O=15, T=20, Y=25.

Front: Why does order matter in analogy?
Back: Because "Doctor : Patient" and "Patient : Doctor" are different relationships. Reversing direction changes the answer.

Front: What is the examiner's "near-miss option" trap?
Back: An option that looks almost right but does not fully match the relationship. Re-check with your sentence.

Front: "Day : Night" is which analogy?
Back: Antonym — the two are opposites.

Front: What relationship is "Rose : Flower"?
Back: Category–Member — a rose is a member of the flower category.

Front: When two options seem correct, what should you do?
Back: Choose the most exact and closest relationship, not the general one.

Front: What if you cannot catch the relationship within 40 seconds?
Back: Skip the question and move on (Skip Strategy), returning later if time allows.
