Chapter: Direction Sense (दिशा ज्ञान)

🎯 What You’ll Learn

By the end of this chapter, you will be able to:

Identify the four cardinal and four intercardinal directions instantly.

Track a person’s facing direction after every left/right turn.

Calculate the shortest distance from start using Pythagoras theorem.

Solve shadow‑based direction problems with time of day.

Spot the examiner’s favourite traps and avoid losing easy marks.

🔥 A Hook to Start With

Imagine you’re blindfolded, spun around ten times, and asked to point North. Sounds impossible? Our brains are not built to track rotation automatically – but with a simple mental compass, you’ll do it every single time. Direction Sense isn’t just a chapter; it’s a life skill that makes you the person who never gets lost, in puzzles or in parking lots.

🧩 Why We Struggle With Directions

Most of us mix up left and right the moment we’re not facing North on paper. The real reason is that we try to memorise turns instead of updating our mental facing direction. Once you learn to “become the person in the question”, everything clicks. Let’s build that skill from scratch.

📌 The Master Compass

There are eight directions you must know:

Cardinal: North (N), East (E), South (S), West (W)

Intercardinal: North‑East (NE), South‑East (SE), South‑West (SW), North‑West (NW)

Mnemonic Trick (Clockwise order):
Never Eat Soggy Waffles → North, East, South, West.
Once you have this, intercardinal directions automatically sit between them.

🧠 Active Recall Prompt: Close your eyes. Point to NE. Now point to SW. Can you see them on a clock face?

🚶‍♂️ The Golden Rule: Become the Person

Whenever a question says, “Ram is facing North. He turns left and walks 10 m,” do this:

Stand mentally in Ram’s shoes. You are facing North.

Your left hand is West. So Ram now faces West.

He moves – draw an arrow West.

Every time a turn comes, update the direction your face is pointing. Never turn the paper; turn yourself in your mind.

🧠 Active Recall Prompt: You are facing East. You take a right turn, then another right, then a left. Which way are you facing now? (Solve it without looking further. Answer: South.)

🌞 Shadow Questions: Let the Sun Guide You

The Sun rises in the East and sets in the West. At noon (in the northern hemisphere), the Sun is in the South.

Morning: Shadow falls West.

Evening: Shadow falls East.

Noon: Shadow falls North.

Now, if a person’s shadow is exactly to their left, and it’s morning, you can deduce their facing direction. Sketch a quick Sun and shadow stick figure – never guess.

🧠 Active Recall Prompt: It is 8 AM. Riya’s shadow falls exactly to her right. Which direction is she facing? (Sun in East → shadow West. Shadow to her right means West is on her right → she must be facing North.)

🔁 Review After 2 Days

Take a blank sheet. Draw the 8‑point compass without looking.

Ask yourself: Did I fall for any trap? Which one?

⚠️ Caution: Examiner’s Trap

Trap 1 – The Hidden Turn‑Back
Sometimes questions say “he turned around” or “took an about turn”. That’s a 180° flip, not a left/right. Always replace “about turn” with exact opposite direction.

Trap 2 – Distance vs. Displacement
The examiner may ask “how far has he walked?” (total path) vs. “how far is he from the start?” (shortest distance). Read the last line twice before calculating.

Trap 3 – Shadow + Time Mix‑up
A shadow falling “in front” at 4 PM means different directions than at 7 AM. Never ignore the time of day; quickly note Sun’s position first. Many students lose marks by applying morning logic to evening questions.

Trap 4 – Options That Look Alike
Multiple options might show similar distances but different directions (e.g., 10 m North‑East vs 10 m North‑West). Always draw a tiny compass beside your final diagram to avoid slipping at the last step.
