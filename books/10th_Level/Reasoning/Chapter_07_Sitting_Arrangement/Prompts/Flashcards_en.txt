Flashcards: Sitting Arrangement (Basic)

Card 1

Front: If five friends sit in a row all facing North, and you say “A is to the left of B”, who sees A on their left?

Back: Everyone does. When all face the same direction, their left and right are the same as yours.

Card 2

Front: In a circle where everyone faces the center, is the person on your clockwise side to your left or right?

Back: Left. Clockwise = Left for a person facing the center. Remember: Clock-L.

Card 3

Front: What flips when a person facing the center and a person facing outward sit side by side?

Back: Their left and right directions flip relative to each other. Each sees their own left differently.

Card 4

Front: What is the very first thing you must draw before touching any conditions in a sitting arrangement puzzle?

Back: A skeleton — empty boxes for a row or dots on a circle for a circular arrangement.

Card 5

Front: Why is it a golden rule to “fix one person first” in a circular arrangement?

Back: A circle has no fixed starting point, so placing one person anchors the whole arrangement.

Card 6

Front: If “A is third to the left of B” in a circle facing the center, how many people sit between them?

Back: Two. “Third to the left” means skip two people on the left and place A after that.

Card 7

Front: Which mnemonic helps you remember that in a row, when two people face each other, their left-right gets reversed?

Back: “Face to Face, Left-Right Reverse; Back to Back, Same Side Track.”

Card 8

Front: What does the “L” in Clock-L stand for?

Back: Left. For a person facing the center, the Clockwise direction is their Left side.

Card 9

Front: In a row where some face North and some South, how do you avoid left-right confusion?

Back: Draw a small arrow (↑ or ↓) above each person's name to show their facing direction.

Card 10

Front: Why is “A is exactly to the right of B” treated like a glued pair?

Back: Because they are immediate neighbours with a fixed order — you can place them as a single block and move it together.

Card 11

Front: What is the 4-step magic strategy to crack any basic sitting arrangement?

Back: 1. Sort info (Certain vs Relative). 2. Draw skeleton. 3. Apply conditions one by one. 4. Verify and eliminate possibilities.

Card 12

Front: If you get two possible arrangements while applying conditions, what should you do next?

Back: Keep both as branches and test further conditions. Discard the one that fails.

Card 13

Front: How does the examiner trap you by giving multiple seemingly correct options?

Back: They include answers that fit one of your possible arrangements but not the final correct one. Always verify all conditions before choosing.

Card 14

Front: What is the “tough-word trap” in sitting arrangement questions?

Back: Words like “सन्निकट नहीं” (not adjacent), “एकांतर क्रम में” (alternate), or “प्रतिवेशी दिशा” (opposite direction) sound difficult but just hide simple conditions. Translate them instantly to plain language.

Card 15

Front: If “A is second to the right of B” in a circle facing outward, which direction (clock/anti-clock) is their right?

Back: Clockwise. For outward-facing persons, right = clockwise (opposite of center-facing). Use Anti-Clock-R to recall that outward-facing left is anti-clockwise.

Card 16

Front: You see “B is not an immediate neighbour of D.” How should you mentally mark this?

Back: Put a ✗ between B and D — they cannot sit next to each other. Use it to eliminate false possibilities.

Card 17

Front: What is a common trap when a question asks “Who is at the extreme right end?” and you haven’t ruled out all possibilities?

Back: One arrangement may give Person X at the right end, another may give Person Y. Never pick an answer until only one arrangement survives.

Card 18

Front: Why is it important to “review after 2 days” in learning sitting arrangements?

Back: Spaced repetition moves the skill from short-term memory to permanent logical reflex, so you can solve puzzles faster in the exam.
