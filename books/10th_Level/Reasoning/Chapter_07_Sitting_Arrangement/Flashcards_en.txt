Front: What is the golden rule for solving sitting arrangement?
Back: Draw the arrangement (line/circle) on paper and fill conditions one by one; fixed conditions first.

Front: Round table, facing centre — a person's left is on which side of us?
Back: On our right (reversed).

Front: Round table, facing outward — a person's left is on which side of us?
Back: On our left (same).

Front: What are the three main types?
Back: Linear (row), circular (round table), and square/rectangular table.

Front: In two rows facing each other, which way do they face?
Back: One row towards the north, the other towards the south.

Front: What does "immediately to the left" mean?
Back: The very next neighbour, not just anyone on that side.

Front: What is the correct order of applying conditions?
Back: Fixed conditions first, uncertain ones later.

Front: What is the very first thing to decide?
Back: Each person's facing direction — then left and right.

Front: How do you count the gap between two people in a circle?
Back: Count both ways and choose the shorter path.

Front: What is the most common trap?
Back: Forgetting the facing direction and reversing left and right.

Front: Facing centre, "B to the right of A" — where is B in our picture?
Back: Anticlockwise from A, because a person's right is our left.

Front: 6 people at a round table — how many neighbours each?
Back: Two — one on the left, one on the right.

Front: In a linear row where all face the same way?
Back: Left and right stay the same as ours.

Front: Strategy for a complex (8+ people) puzzle?
Back: Solve the smaller and linear puzzles first; the big ones later.

Front: What is the sitting-arrangement mnemonic?
Back: "See the face, then left-right".
