Flashcards: Alphabet Questions

Card 1
Front: What is the "EJOTY" trick, and why will it save you 10 seconds per question?
Back: EJOTY gives you the forward positions: E=5, J=10, O=15, T=20, Y=25. Use them as anchors to quickly find any letter’s position without counting from A every time.

Card 2
Front: What phrase helps you remember the reverse alphabet anchors V, Q, L, G, B?
Back: "Very Quiet Lions Growl Boldly" — V=5, Q=10, L=15, G=20, B=25 in reverse order (Z=1). Also remember "VQLGB" and the Hindi jingle "विकल्प से पीछे हटो".

Card 3
Front: If A and Z are best friends forever, what number keeps them together?
Back: 27. The sum of the positions of opposite letters is always 27. So Opposite letter = 27 – Forward position.

Card 4
Front: You have the letter M (position 13). Who is its opposite buddy, and how did you find it instantly?
Back: N (14), because 27 – 13 = 14. M and N are opposite. The pair right in the middle of the alphabet flips to each other!

Card 5
Front: A, C, F, J, ? — What comes next, and what is the pattern’s secret?
Back: O. The gaps between positions increase by 1 each time: +2, +3, +4, then +5. Positions: 1, 3, 6, 10, 15.

Card 6
Front: ACE : GIK :: MOQ : ? — What's the missing link and why?
Back: SUW. Each letter moves +6 positions. A(1)+6=G(7), C(3)+6=I(9), E(5)+6=K(11). Apply +6 to M(13)→S(19), O(15)→U(21), Q(17)→W(23).

Card 7
Front: If CAT becomes DBU, what kind of shift is happening, and what would DOG become?
Back: +1 shift to each letter. D+1=E, O+1=P, G+1=H → EPH. Simple forward shifting.

Card 8
Front: How do you decode a word that first took the opposite letter and then moved forward by 2? (Example: M → ?)
Back: Reverse the steps: first move backward by 2, then take the opposite. M→K (13-2=11), opposite of K (11) is P (16). Always undo the last step first.

Card 9
Front: What is the "27 Rule" and how does it turn you into a letter-flipping ninja?
Back: For any letter, 27 minus its position gives the opposite letter. No memorization needed; just quick subtraction. Example: G=7, opposite = 27-7=20=T.

Card 10
Front: Quickly: what is the opposite of S, and which mnemonic anchor helps you calculate it without subtracting?
Back: H. S is 19 (T=20, so S is one back), 27-19=8=H. Anchor T=20 helps find S quickly. Alternatively, remember the pair: S and H are opposite (S=19, H=8).

Card 11
Front: Mnemonic trick: What three pairs of opposites should you learn by heart to save time?
Back: E↔V, J↔Q, O↔L, T↔G, Y↔B. These are the anchors. Learn them and you can find neighboring opposites quickly.

Card 12
Front: Examiner's Trap: You see a series A, C, F, J, ? with options O and P. Why might you wrongly pick P, and how do you avoid the trap?
Back: Because you might think the gaps are +2, +3, +4, +6 (choosing P) instead of the natural +2, +3, +4, +5 (O). Always test for the simplest consistent pattern first. If multiple patterns exist, look for the one that follows the easiest mathematical progression.

Card 13
Front: Examiner's Trap: In an analogy ACE : GIK, you find two possible answers: SUW and STV. How do you identify the correct one?
Back: Check if the relationship holds uniformly. A→G is +6, C→I is +6, E→K is +6. So apply +6 to M, O, Q → S, U, W. STV would be +6, +5, +6 – not uniform. Always verify the relationship applies to all pairs exactly.

Card 14
Front: Odd one out: B, C, D, E — why is E the odd one, and what trap might make you overlook it?
Back: E is the only vowel. The trap is overthinking: you might start looking at positions (2,3,4,5) and miss the obvious vowel/consonant distinction. Always check the simplest classification first.

Card 15
Front: In a letter classification question, all options have positions that are even numbers. Could one still be the odd one out?
Back: Yes. For example, positions 4 (D), 8 (H), 10 (J), 12 (L) – all even. But D is the only one with a vowel sound? No. Better: check if one is prime? 10 and 12 composite, 4 composite, 8 composite, so none prime. Maybe one is a vowel? D is consonant. Another pattern: D=4 is square number, others not. So always look for a secondary hidden pattern.

Card 16
Front: What is the quickest way to find the position of R if you know O=15?
Back: O=15, P=16, Q=17, R=18. Count forward from the EJOTY anchor O. Same for backward: to find L, O=15, N=14, M=13, L=12. Use anchors as starting points.

Card 17
Front: How can you crack a mixed letter-number series like A2, C4, E6, ?
Back: Treat letters and numbers separately. Letters: A, C, E — every other letter (gap of 2). Next is G. Numbers: 2, 4, 6 — +2 each. Next is 8. So answer is G8.

Card 18
Front: What is the one thing you must do two days after studying this chapter to lock in all these tricks forever?
Back: Practice the Blurting Sheet: write everything you remember on a blank paper without peeking, then fill in missing bits. And actively recall opposite pairs during idle moments.
