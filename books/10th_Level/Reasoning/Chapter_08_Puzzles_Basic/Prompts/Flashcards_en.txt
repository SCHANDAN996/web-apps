🃏 Flashcards – Simple Puzzles

Card 1
Front: What are the 4 main types of simple puzzles?
Back: Arrangement, Comparison/Ranking, Scheduling, Direction-Distance.

Card 2
Front: In a row arrangement where everyone faces North, which direction is "left"?
Back: West. (Everyone's left = West, right = East.)

Card 3
Front: Which mnemonic helps remember the solving flow? Spell it out.
Back: D.R.A.C. — Diagram, Read definite, Add relative, Cross-check.

Card 4
Front: What's the first thing you should do within 10 seconds of seeing a puzzle?
Back: Identify the puzzle type and draw a clean diagram.

Card 5
Front: Clue says "A is left of B." Is A immediately left of B?
Back: No. Without the word "immediately" (or "just/ठीक"), gaps may exist.

Card 6
Front: What is the golden rule about diagrams?
Back: Only one letter or symbol per position — keep it clean.

Card 7
Front: Which clues should you place first — definite or relative?
Back: Definite clues first. They fix positions directly.

Card 8
Front: A puzzle gives two possible placements for a person. What do you do?
Back: Create Case 1 and Case 2, then test further clues to eliminate the wrong one.

Card 9
Front: In a circular arrangement, a person faces the centre. Their left is which direction around the circle?
Back: Anticlockwise. (Trick: Centre → Left-Anticlockwise, Outside → Left-Clockwise.)

Card 10
Front: Recite the mnemonic for left-right in circular puzzles.
Back: C-LAC, O-LC — Centre: Left Anticlockwise, Outside: Left Clockwise.

Card 11
Front: How many cases should a simple puzzle rarely exceed?
Back: Two. If you have more, re-read — you likely missed a definite clue.

Card 12
Front: What is the 2-minute drill for a 3-question puzzle set?
Back: 0:00-0:20 diagram, 0:20-0:40 definite clues, 0:40-1:10 relative & cases, 1:10-1:40 eliminate, 1:40-2:00 answer.

Card 13
Front: Examiner's Trap: The clue says "A is left of B" but the question asks "Who is immediately left of B?" What's the trap?
Back: The word "immediately" wasn't in the clue. You assume it and get a wrong answer.

Card 14
Front: Examiner's Trap: A puzzle adds "A, who is tallest, likes blue." The question is about seating. What should you do?
Back: Ignore the irrelevant detail (colour). The examiner wants you to waste time.

Card 15
Front: What is the "ghost" trick for gaps in a row?
Back: Imagine a ghost in every gap. Only the word "immediately" removes the ghost — otherwise space is allowed.

Card 16
Front: In a circular puzzle, the facing direction is not given. Can you confidently use "left" clues to fix absolute positions?
Back: Not confidently. Without facing direction, "left" could mean opposite sides; stick to relative ordering.

Card 17
Front: How do you handle a clue like "P is second to the right of Q" when Q's position is unknown?
Back: Try possible positions for Q that keep P within the row/circle, make cases, then use other clues to eliminate.

Card 18
Front: What's the difference between a "definite" and a "relative" clue? Give an example.
Back: Definite: "A sits at position 4." Relative: "A is left of B." Definite pins, relative links.

Card 19
Front: In comparison puzzles, what kind of diagram is most useful?
Back: A vertical line with > symbols (e.g., A > B > C) showing taller/richer/faster order.

Card 20
Front: After finishing a puzzle, what final step must you never skip?
Back: Cross-check your arrangement against all original clues to catch any mistake.
