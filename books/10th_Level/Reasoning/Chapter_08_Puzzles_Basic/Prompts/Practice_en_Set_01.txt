Question 1.
Five persons A, B, C, D, E sit in a row facing north. A is immediate left of B. C is immediate right of D. E sits at the extreme right end. B is not at any end. Who sits at the extreme left end?
(A) A
(B) C
(C) D
(D) Cannot be determined

Answer: (D)
Solution:
Row positions 1 to 5. E = 5 (extreme right). A-B block (A left of B). B not at end → B can be 2, 3, or 4. Case 1: A=1, B=2. Then C-D block (D left of C) fits at 3-4 (D=3, C=4). Left end = A. Case 2: B=3, A=2. Then D=1, C=4 works; left end = D. Hence not unique.
Examiner’s Trap: The clue “A is immediate left of B” does not fix the block at an extreme; multiple possibilities exist.
Source: Based on SSC CGL 2022 pattern.

Question 2.
Six friends P, Q, R, S, T, U sit in a row facing south. P sits at one of the ends. R sits second to the left of T. Only one person sits between Q and U. S is immediate neighbour of T. Who sits third from the left end?
(A) Q
(B) R
(C) S
(D) U

Answer: (B) R
Solution:
Let positions 1–6 (left to right). Second to left: R = T – 2. P at an end. Test P=1. Possible T=5, R=3. S neighbour of T ⇒ S=4 or 6. If S=6, T=5, R=3. One person between Q and U ⇒ gap of 2. Remaining positions 2,4; Q and U can occupy 2 and 4 (one person at 3 between). Arrangement: 1:P, 2:Q/U, 3:R, 4:U/Q, 5:T, 6:S. Third from left is position 3 = R.
Source: RRB NTPC 2020 style.

Question 3.
Seven boxes A, B, C, D, E, F, G kept one above another (1 = bottom, 7 = top). A is at an odd-numbered position. B is three boxes above A. C is immediately below D. E is at the top. F is between A and C. Which box is at position 4?
(A) A
(B) B
(C) C
(D) F

Answer: (B)
Solution:
A odd (1,3,5,7). B = A+3. Possible A=1 ⇒ B=4; A=3 ⇒ B=6; A=5 ⇒ B=8 invalid; A=7 invalid. Case 1: A=1, B=4. E=7. C-D block, F between A and C. If C=5,D=6, then F between 1 and 5 ⇒ F=2 or 3 (but 3 free). Works, position 4 = B. Case 2: A=3, B=6. E=7. C-D block, F between 3 and C. Remaining positions 1,2,4,5. If C=5,D=6? B=6 conflict. C=4,D=5 ⇒ F between 3 and 4 impossible. C=2,D=3? A=3 conflict. So no valid placement. Thus only Case 1 works, pos4 = B.
Source: Banking Clerk 2023 type.

Question 4.
Six friends A, B, C, D, E, F sit around a circular table facing the centre. A sits opposite D. B sits immediate right of A. C sits second to the left of B. Who sits immediate left of D?
(A) E
(B) F
(C) C
(D) Cannot be determined

Answer: (D)
Solution:
Let A=1, D opposite = 4. B immediate right (clockwise) of A = 2. C second to left (anticlockwise) of B (2): left steps 2→1→6, so C=6. Remaining persons E, F at 3 and 5. Immediate left of D (4) is position 3 (E or F). Specific person undetermined.
Examiner’s Trap: “Immediate left” of D is not uniquely fixed; options list E and F separately, tempting a guess.
Source: SBI Clerk 2021 (adapted).

Question 5.
Five persons P, Q, R, S, T have different heights. P is taller than Q but shorter than R. S is taller than T. T is not the shortest. Who is the shortest?
(A) P
(B) Q
(C) T
(D) Cannot be determined

Answer: (B) Q
Solution:
R > P > Q. S > T. T not shortest ⇒ T > (someone). Since Q is already below R and P, if Q were taller than T, T would be shortest (contradiction). So T > Q. Thus Q is shortest.
Source: SSC CGL 2019.

Question 6.
Seven persons take leave Mon–Sun. R takes Friday. Only one person takes leave between R and S. T takes immediately before U. V takes Sunday. Who takes Wednesday?
(A) S
(B) T
(C) U
(D) Cannot be determined

Answer: (A) S
Solution:
R = Fri (5). One person between R and S ⇒ S = Wed (3) or Sun (7). V=Sun ⇒ S=3 (Wed). T before U block. Remaining days: Mon(1), Tue(2), Thu(4), Sat(6). T-U must be consecutive; possible (1,2) or (5,6) no, (6,7) no. Only (1,2) works: T=Mon, U=Tue. So Wednesday = S.
Source: IBPS RRB Clerk 2022.

Question 7.
A walks 6 m north from point X, turns left and walks 8 m, turns left and walks 6 m, turns left and walks 8 m. How far is he from X?
(A) 0 m
(B) 10 m
(C) 14 m
(D) 20 m

Answer: (A) 0 m
Solution:
Movement forms a rectangle returning to start. Path: N6, W8, S6, E8 → back to X. Distance 0 m.
Source: RRB Group D 2018.

Question 8.
A 5-floor building (1 = ground, 5 = top). A lives on an odd floor. B lives immediately above D. C lives on floor 1. E lives on the top floor. Who lives on floor 3?
(A) A
(B) B
(C) D
(D) Cannot be determined

Answer: (A) A
Solution:
C=1, E=5. Odd floors free: 3 only (1 and 5 taken). A must be on odd, so A=3.
Source: SSC CHSL 2020.

Question 9.
Six boxes P, Q, R, S, T, U stacked (1 bottom, 6 top). P immediately above Q. R exactly in middle (position 3). S at top. T immediately below U. Which box is at position 2?
(A) P
(B) U
(C) Q
(D) Cannot be determined

Answer: (D)
Solution:
S=6, R=3. P=Q+1; U=T+1. Two blocks to place in remaining positions 1,2,4,5. Possibility 1: Q=1, P=2, then T=4, U=5 → pos2 = P. Possibility 2: Q=4, P=5, then T=1, U=2 → pos2 = U. Hence not unique.
Examiner’s Trap: Students often fix the first block arbitrarily and pick P or U.
Source: Banking PO Pre 2020 style.

Question 10.
P, Q, R, S, T have different weights. P is heavier than Q but lighter than R. S is heavier than T. T is not the lightest. Who is the heaviest?
(A) R
(B) S
(C) P
(D) Cannot be determined

Answer: (D)
Solution:
R > P > Q. S > T. T not lightest ⇒ Q is lightest (Q < T). Heaviest could be R or S, as no relation between R and S is given.
Examiner’s Trap: Incomplete comparison; options (A) and (B) both possible.
Source: UPSC CSAT 2016.

Question 11.
Six persons sit in two parallel rows, 3 each. Row1 (A,B,C) faces south, Row2 (P,Q,R) faces north. A sits opposite the person who sits immediate left of R. B sits at the extreme left end of Row1. Who sits opposite C?
(A) P
(B) Q
(C) R
(D) Cannot be determined

Answer: (C) R
Solution:
Row1 left to right: B, C, A (since B extreme left, opposite arrangements). Row2 faces north; “immediate left of R” is the seat to R’s right (observer’s perspective). Let Row2 seats 1,2,3 (left to right). If R at 2, immediate left = 3. So opposite 3 is Row1 rightmost = A. Thus A opposite Row2-3. Then Row1: B=1, C=2, A=3. Opposite C (2) is Row2-2 = R.
Source: SSC CGL 2019.

Question 12.
Five seminars on Mon–Fri. English not Mon/Fri. Maths immediately after Science. Hindi on Thursday. Science before English. Which seminar is on Monday?
(A) Science
(B) Maths
(C) Hindi
(D) Cannot be determined

Answer: (A) Science
Solution:
Hindi=Thu. English Tue/Wed. Science before English. Maths immediately after Science (Sci–Maths block). If English Tue, block Sci Mon, Math Tue conflicts. If English Wed, block Sci Tue, Math Wed conflicts? Check: Sci Tue, Math Wed, then English Wed conflict. Instead, block Sci Mon, Math Tue, English Wed, then Hindi Thu, Fri free. So Monday = Science.
Source: RRB NTPC 2021.

Question 13.
From point P, Ravi walks 6 m east, turns left walks 8 m north, turns right walks 6 m east, turns left walks 8 m north. How far is he from P?
(A) 10 m
(B) 14 m
(C) 20 m
(D) 28 m

Answer: (C) 20 m
Solution:
Net displacement: east = 6+6=12 m, north = 8+8=16 m. Distance = √(12²+16²) = √400 = 20 m.
Source: SSC GD 2022.

Question 14.
Seven boxes A–G, bottom 1 top 7. A at 4. B three boxes above C. D immediately above E. F at top. G at an odd floor. Who is at floor 2?
(A) D
(B) E
(C) C
(D) G

Answer: (A) D
Solution:
A=4, F=7. B = C+3. C can be 2 (B=5) or 3 (B=6). C=3 ⇒ B=6; remaining 1,2,5. D above E (D=E+1). Place D=2, E=1 works; G odd, floor 5 left → G=5. So floor 2 = D. (C=2, B=5 fails as D-E block cannot fit.)
Source: IBPS Clerk 2023.

Question 15.
Six persons J,K,L,M,N,O in a row facing north. L at extreme left end. K immediate left of M. N second to right of J. Only one person between O and J. Who sits immediate right of L?
(A) J
(B) K
(C) O
(D) Cannot be determined

Answer: (C) O
Solution:
L=1. N = J+2, |O–J|=2. Try J=4 ⇒ N=6, O=2 (since gap 2). K-M block: K=3, M=5 works. Arrangement: 1:L,2:O,3:K,4:J,5:M,6:N. Immediate right of L is O.
Source: SSC CGL 2020.

Question 16.

Answer: (A) D

Question 17.
A, B, C, D, E have different heights. A is taller than D. B is shorter than C but taller than E. The person who likes red is the tallest. C does not like red. Who is the tallest?
(A) A
(B) B
(C) C
(D) Cannot be determined

Answer: (A) A
Solution:
C > B > E. A > D. Red = tallest. C doesn’t like red ⇒ C not tallest. If A were shorter than C, then C would be tallest (contradiction). Hence A > C. So A is tallest.
Examiner’s Trap: Extra information (likes red) is crucial to eliminate C; students may overlook it.
Source: Banking Mains style.

Question 18.
Seven persons P–V, Mon–Sun. P on Wednesday. Only three persons between Q and R. S immediately before T. U on Saturday. V on a day before P. Who is on Monday?
(A) Q
(B) R
(C) S
(D) Cannot be determined

Answer: (A) Q
Solution:
P=3 (Wed), U=6 (Sat). V < 3 ⇒ V=1 or 2. Q and R gap 4 (three persons between) ⇒ |Q–R|=4. Possible pairs: (1,5), (2,6), (3,7). 3 and 6 taken, so only (1,5) works. So Q,R=1 and 5. V before P, so V=2 (since 1 taken). S-T block. Remaining days 4,7. S-T must be consecutive ⇒ S=4,T=7 or vice versa (S immediately before T ⇒ S=4,T=7). Thus Monday (1) = Q or R? 1 is Q (say). So Monday is Q.
Source: SBI PO Pre 2023.

Question 19.
Six persons A–F around circle facing centre. A opposite D. B immediate right of A. C second to left of B. Who sits between E and F (counting clockwise from E)?
(A) A
(B) B
(C) C
(D) D

Answer: (D) D
Solution:
As in Q4: A=1, D=4, B=2, C=6. Remaining E,F at 3 and 5. Clockwise from E (say E=3): 3→4(D)→5(F)→... between E and F is D (if E=3, next is D=4, then F=5). So D sits between E and F.
Source: SSC CGL 2018.

Question 20.
A certain number of persons sit in a row facing north. A sits at one end. B sits third to the right of A. Only two persons sit between B and C. C sits at an extreme end. How many persons?
(A) 5
(B) 6
(C) 7
(D) Cannot be determined

Answer: (C) 7
Solution:
A at left end (pos1). B third right ⇒ pos4. Two persons between B and C ⇒ C can be pos1 (conflict) or pos7 (with persons 5,6 between). C at extreme end ⇒ pos7. Hence total 7.
Source: RRB ALP 2019.

Question 21.
Seven lectures Mon–Sun. Physics on Wed. Chemistry immediately before Biology. Maths immediately after Physics. English before Maths. History on Friday. Which lecture is on Monday?
(A) Chemistry
(B) Biology
(C) English
(D) Cannot be determined

Answer: (C) English
Solution:
Phy=3, Hist=5, Maths=4. Chem–Bio block. English before Maths ⇒ English 1 or 2. Chem–Bio block can’t be (1,2) because English needs 1 or 2. So block must be (6,7): Chem=6, Bio=7. Then English=1, remaining 2 for Geography. Monday = English.
Source: Banking Clerk 2022.

Question 22.
Seven floors 1–7. A lives on an even floor. B lives three floors above C. D lives immediately below E. F lives on top. G lives below A but above B. Who lives on floor 2?
(A) A
(B) D
(C) G
(D) Cannot be determined

Answer: (B) D
Solution:
A even (2,4,6). F=7. B=C+3. G between B and A (B < G < A). Try A=6 ⇒ B<G<6. B≥4 (since C≥1). So B=4, G=5 works. C=1. D below E: remaining 2,3. D=2, E=3. Floor 2=D. (Other A values fail.)
Source: SSC CGL Tier-2 2021.

Question 23.
Eight persons A–H around circle facing centre. A second to left of B. C opposite D. E immediate right of F. G third to right of H. H not immediate neighbour of A. Who sits opposite C?
(A) D
(B) E
(C) F
(D) G

Answer: (A) D
Solution:
Let B=1, A=7 (second left). H≠6,8. Try H=5 ⇒ G=8 (third right). E=F+1. Remaining positions 2,3,4,6. Block E=F+1 fits (3,4): F=3,E=4. Opposite pairs: (1,5),(2,6),(3,7),(4,8). Opposite C is D; remaining free pair (2,6) for C,D. Thus C=2, D=6 or vice versa. Opposite C is D.
Source: SBI PO 2023.

Question 24.
P, Q, R, S, T, U have different heights. P > Q, P < R. S > T, S < U. Exactly one person is between U and R in height order. T is not the shortest. Who is tallest?
(A) R
(B) U
(C) S
(D) Cannot be determined

Answer: (D)
Solution:
R > P > Q; U > S > T. One person between U and R. Case 1: U > X > R ⇒ X = S (since S < U and > R possible) ⇒ U > S > R > P > Q, and T < R (so T between R and Q). Tallest = U. Case 2: R > X > U ⇒ X = P (since P > U possible) ⇒ R > P > U > S > T > Q. Tallest = R. Both valid; T not shortest holds. So not unique.
Examiner’s Trap: Incomplete ordering leads to dual possibility; students may fix one and choose R or U.
Source: UPSC CSAT 2022 style.

(A) R
