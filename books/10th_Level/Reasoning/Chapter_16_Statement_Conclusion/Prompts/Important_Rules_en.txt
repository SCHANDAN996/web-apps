📋 Important Rules & Approaches
Rule Name	Description	Example
Robot Brain Rule	Treat the statement as the only available data. Delete all outside knowledge and assumptions.	"Some cats are black" → Do not assume other cats are not black.
Conversion – All	"All A are B" can be safely converted to "Some B are A".	"All pens are items" → "Some items are pens" ✅
Conversion – Some	"Some A are B" can be safely converted to "Some B are A".	"Some boys are players" → "Some players are boys" ✅
Conversion – No	"No A is B" can be safely converted to "No B is A".	"No tree is an animal" → "No animal is a tree" ✅
Some Not Trap	Never derive "Some A are not B" as a definite conclusion from "Some A are B".	"Some fruits are sweet" does not mean "Some fruits are not sweet".
Certainty vs. Possibility	A conclusion is valid only if it is forced 100% by the statement. A mere possibility is not a valid conclusion.	"All dogs are mammals" → "Some mammals are dogs" (certain). "All mammals are dogs" (not certain).
Venn Diagram Test	A conclusion is valid only if it holds true in every possible Venn diagram. If it fails in even one, it is invalid.	For "Some A are B", check both partial overlap and complete inclusion.
T.E.A. Test	T – True as per statement only, E – Eliminate outside knowledge, A – Avoid "Some Not". A quick mental checklist.	Use before marking any answer.
⏱️ Time Management
Rule	Description
15-Second Rule	For a direct single-statement-single-conclusion question, decide within 15 seconds. If you hesitate, mark it for review and skip immediately.
45-Second Rule	For questions with 2 statements and 3-4 conclusions, spend a maximum of 45 seconds. Never overanalyze or get stuck.
Skip Strategy	If you encounter ambiguous language or a tricky "some not" trap that confuses you, skip after 15 seconds. Return with a fresh mind later only if time permits.
