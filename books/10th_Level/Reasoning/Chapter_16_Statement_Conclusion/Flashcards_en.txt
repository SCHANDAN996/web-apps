Front: On what basis alone should a conclusion be drawn?
Back: Only on the basis of the given statement — not your general knowledge.

Front: What is the golden rule of statement & conclusion?
Back: Take the statement as 100% true, even if it seems false in the real world.

Front: From "All A are B", which conclusion is certain?
Back: "Some B are A".

Front: From "All A are B", does "All B are A" follow?
Back: No — that is not necessary.

Front: From "No A is B", what follows?
Back: "No B is A" (true in both directions).

Front: Difference between "possibility" and "conclusion"?
Back: A conclusion must follow with certainty; what only 'may be' is not a conclusion.

Front: Example of a chain (syllogism)?
Back: "All A are B; All B are C" → "All A are C" follows.

Front: From "Some A are B", which conclusion is certain?
Back: "Some B are A".

Front: When are "Either-Or" conclusions correct?
Back: When two conclusions are not true alone but are correct as "either this or that".

Front: If the statement is "All birds fly", what do you assume?
Back: Take it as true, even though an ostrich really doesn't fly.

Front: What is the diagram method to check a conclusion?
Back: Draw a Venn diagram — a conclusion true in every possible picture is correct.

Front: Why is reading "some" as "all" wrong?
Back: "Some A are B" never gives "All A are B".

Front: What is the most common trap?
Back: Mixing your outside knowledge into the statement.

Front: When does a conclusion "follow"?
Back: When it follows with certainty from the statement alone.

Front: What is the statement-conclusion mnemonic?
Back: "Statement true, knowledge aside, only the certain is a conclusion".
