# Feynman Technique: Statement & Conclusion in Simple Words

Tell your friend: "A game — I'll say something, and you assume it is completely true, even if it sounds crazy. I say 'All elephants fly'. Now tell me, are 'Some flying things elephants'? Yes! Because I assumed all elephants fly. Forget the real world — just draw the answer from my sentence."

**Take the statement as true, keep your knowledge aside — the conclusion must come only from the statement.**

## The Biggest Trick: "Certain" vs "May Be"

A conclusion is correct only when it follows **with certainty** from the statement.
- "All dogs are animals" → "Some animals are dogs" — definitely correct.
- But "All animals are dogs" — does not follow (may be, but not certain).

Never treat what 'may be' as a conclusion.
