1. The "Silly Old Harry" Mnemonic for Sin, Cos, Tan
Picture a wobbly old man named Harry, perched on a tiny boat in Alaska, catching a herring with a goofy grin.
Sentence: Silly Old Harry Caught A Herring Trawling Off Alaska.
Decode: S → Sin = Opposite/Hypotenuse | C → Cos = Adjacent/Hypotenuse | T → Tan = Opposite/Adjacent.
The more exaggerated the image (a fish flapping in his face!), the stronger the memory.

2. The √/2 Ladder Trick for Sin Standard Angles
Imagine a ladder with five rungs numbered 0, 1, 2, 3, 4. You climb it, and at each rung you take the square root of the rung number, then slide down by dividing by 2.

Rung 0° → √0/2 = 0

Rung 30° → √1/2 = 1/2

Rung 45° → √2/2

Rung 60° → √3/2

Rung 90° → √4/2 = 1
The bizarre image: a ladder stretching into a cloud where a square root monster halves every number.

3. The "Frog & Fly Pond" for sin²θ + cos²θ = 1
A lazy frog sits exactly at the centre of a perfectly circular pond of radius 1 m. An annoying fly lands on the edge, always exactly 1 m away.
The fly’s east distance = cosθ, north distance = sinθ. No matter where the fly sits, the distance from the frog is always 1, so cos²θ + sin²θ = 1² = 1.
Imagine the frog shouting, “Stop moving! Your squares always add up to 1 anyway!”

4. "All Snow Tigers Cuddle" – ASTC Quadrant Signs
In a snow-covered jungle, the seasons change quadrant by quadrant:

All (I): All animals are active – All ratios positive.

Snow (II): Only the Snow leopard (Sin) is visible – only Sin (cosec) positive.

Tigers (III): Only Tigers (Tan) prowl – only Tan (cot) positive.

Cuddle (IV): They Cuddle (Cos) to keep warm – only Cos (sec) positive.
Picture a snow tiger cuddling a cosine cushion!

5. The "Flipping Couples" for Reciprocal Pairs
Trigonometry has three inseparable couples:

Sin and Cosec

Cos and Sec

Tan and Cot
Each couple is the other’s upside-down: if one is 3, the other is 1/3.
Visualise them wearing T-shirts that are mirror-images of each other’s values.

6. Tan is Just a Beach Lover
tanθ is born when sinθ lies on top of cosθ at the beach.
tanθ = (sinθ) / (cosθ).
Picture a sunbather (tan) lying on a beach towel woven from a sin thread over a cos thread.
If you ever forget, see the tan lines forming a fraction!

7. The 90° Identity Swap Party
Whenever two angles add up to 90°, they swap their Trigonometric outfits:

sin(90°–θ) = cosθ

cos(90°–θ) = sinθ

tan(90°–θ) = cotθ

cot(90°–θ) = tanθ
Picture a “Freaky Friday” party where Sin and Cos exchange masks exactly at the 90° door.

8. The Security Guard & the Tangent Square
A tough security guard (Sec) always stands with a shield that reads “1 + Tan²”.
1 + tan²θ = sec²θ
If you ever see Sec, remember he’s protecting a squared Tan plus one.
Similarly, another guard, Cosec, holds “1 + Cot²” → 1 + cot²θ = cosec²θ.
Imagine a comic book panel: “Sec-Man: Shield of 1+tan²!”

9. The "Nose & Chin" for Elevation & Depression
Angle of Elevation: you tilt your head UP, your nose points upward → E for Elevation.
Angle of Depression: you look DOWN, your chin dips → D for Depression.
Picture a cartoon face shouting: “Ele-vation = Eyes to the sky!”

10. The "0, 1, 2, 3, 4" Finger Sin Table
Hold up your left hand. Assign angles to fingers from 0° (folded thumb) to 90° (little finger).
Count 0,1,2,3,4 across the fingers. Take √(finger number)/2 for sin.
For cos, just reverse the hand (start from the little finger as 0°).
Visualisation: a magic hand where each finger lights up with a square root symbol and a tiny “/2”.

11. The "Half-a-Cake" Story for Sin Values
A superhero’s cake-eating journey:

At 0°, he eats nothing (0).

At 30°, he eats half a cake (1/2).

At 45°, he eats a mysterious √2/2 cake.

At 60°, a richer √3/2 cake.

At 90°, he devours the whole cake (1).
The weird image: a superhero in a cape, growing fatter as the angle increases, with square roots sprinkling from the cake!

12. The "Cop & Robber" for Cosec & Sin
Cosec is the cop chasing Sin (the robber). Whenever Sin runs, Cosec catches him by inverting:
cosecθ = 1/sinθ.
Similarly, Sec chases Cos, and Cot chases Tan.
Picture a cartoon police chase where the “bad guy” flips upside down when caught.

13. The "Cot in the Boat" for Tangent-Cotangent Product
A fisherman named Cot always sits in a boat with Tan. Their weights multiply to exactly 1:
tanθ × cotθ = 1.
Visualise a seesaw on water, perfectly balanced, labelled “1”.

14. The "Twin Towers" for Height & Distance Shortcut
When you see a tower problem, remember: the tower and its shadow are in a tango dance.
tan = height / distance.
Picture two twin towers casting shadows that click together to form perfect tan lines – literally.
If the angle changes, the shadow stretches or shrinks, but the tan relationship never breaks.
