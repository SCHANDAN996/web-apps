🃏 Flashcards: Time & Work

Card 1

Front: If you can eat a whole pizza in 6 days, what fraction of the pizza do you eat in one day?

Back: 1/6 of the pizza. This is your "one day's work."

Card 2

Front: A takes 12 days to build a wall. B takes 18 days. What’s the first step of the LCM–Total Work method?

Back: Take the LCM of 12 and 18, which is 36. Assume total work = 36 units.

Card 3

Front: In the LCM method, if total work is 36 units and A takes 12 days, what is A’s efficiency (work per day)?

Back: 36 ÷ 12 = 3 units per day.

Card 4

Front: A and B work together with efficiencies 3 and 2 units/day. Total work is 36. How many days to finish?

Back: Combined efficiency = 5 units/day. Days = 36 ÷ 5 = 7.2 days.

Card 5

Front: A is twice as efficient as B. What is the ratio of their individual times to complete a job?

Back: Efficiency ratio A:B = 2:1 → Time ratio A:B = 1:2 (inverse).

Card 6

Front: If A alone takes 10 days and B alone takes 15 days, what fraction of work do they do together in one day?

Back: 1/10 + 1/15 = (3+2)/30 = 5/30 = 1/6. So together they take 6 days.

Card 7

Front: Which bizarre seesaw image helps you remember that efficiency and time are inverses?

Back: Imagine a fat cat ("Efficiency") on one side up high, and a tiny mouse ("Time") down low. When one goes up, the other goes down — they are inverse buddies.

Card 8

Front: What is the "Cookie Equation" mnemonic for man-days-work problems?

Back: Picture a layered cookie: M (Men) × D (Days) × H (Hours) / W (Work) is equal on both plates. Formula: M₁D₁H₁/W₁ = M₂D₂H₂/W₂.

Card 9

Front: 15 men can complete a job in 20 days. How many men are needed to do the same job in 30 days?

Back: Use M₁D₁ = M₂D₂ (work same). 15×20 = M₂×30 ⇒ M₂ = 10 men.

Card 10

Front: A works for 5 days on a job he normally finishes in 20 days, then leaves. B finishes the rest in 12 days. What is B's alone time?

Back: Total work = 20, A eff=1. Work done=5. Left=15. B eff=15/12=1.25. B’s time = 20/1.25=16 days.

Card 11

Front: A and B work on alternate days, starting with A. A takes 10 days alone, B takes 15 days. How much work is done in a 2-day cycle?

Back: A's 1-day work=1/10, B's=1/15. 2-day work = 1/10 + 1/15 = 1/6. So full cycles and remainder.

Card 12

Front: If A is three times as efficient as B, and together they finish a job in 10 days, how long would B take alone?

Back: Eff A:B = 3:1, time ratio A:B = 1:3. Combined eff=4. Total work = 4×10=40. B alone = 40/1=40 days.

Card 13

Front: Wages are always divided in the ratio of ______, not time spent.

Back: Work done (Efficiency × Time worked).

Card 14

Front: A and B have efficiencies 3 and 2. Together they finish a job in 5 days. Total wage ₹500. A’s share?

Back: Work ratio = 3:2. A’s share = (3/5)×500 = ₹300.

Card 15

Front: In the fraction method, if together 1 day's work is 1/4, how many days to complete the whole job?

Back: Days = 1 ÷ (1/4) = 4 days.

Card 16

Front: What is the "inverse rule" connecting time ratio and efficiency ratio?

Back: If efficiency ratio is a:b, then time ratio is b:a (flipped).

Card 17

Front: 20 workers can complete a job in 12 days. If only 8 workers show up, how many days will the same job take?

Back: M₁D₁ = M₂D₂ ⇒ 20×12 = 8×D₂ ⇒ D₂ = 30 days.

Card 18

Front: Why does the LCM method feel easier than adding fractions like 1/7 + 1/13?

Back: Because it turns all numbers into whole integers, avoiding messy fraction addition.

Card 19

Front: A builder says "If I have 3 more workers, the job gets done 2 days earlier." Which formula handles this best?

Back: The Cookie Equation (M₁D₁ = M₂D₂) with work constant, or M₁D₁H₁/W₁ = M₂D₂H₂/W₂.

Card 20

Front: True or False: In the man-days formula, you must always keep the work done on both sides equal.

Back: True. The ratio M₁D₁H₁/W₁ must equal M₂D₂H₂/W₂, so the "cookie" remains balanced.
