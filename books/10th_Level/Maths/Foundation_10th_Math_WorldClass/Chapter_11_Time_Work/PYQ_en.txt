Question 1 (Basic Together/Alone)

A and B can together complete a piece of work in 20 days. They worked together for 8 days, and then A left the job. In how many more days will B complete the remaining work?

(a) 18 days (b) 15 days (c) 12 days (d) 10 days

Source: SSC CHSL 2025 Pattern-
33

Question 2 (LCM–Total Work Method)

A can do a piece of work in 10 days, B in 15 days. They work together for 3 days. How much work is left?

(a) 1/2 (b) 7/10 (c) 3/10 (d) 1/5

Source: Previous Year Exams-
1

Solution:

Total work = LCM(10, 15) = 30 units.

A's efficiency = 30/10 = 3 units/day.

B's efficiency = 30/15 = 2 units/day.

Combined efficiency = 5 units/day.

Work done in 3 days = 3 × 5 = 15 units.

Work left = 30 - 15 = 15 units.

Fraction of work left = 15/30 = 1/2.

Answer: (a) 1/2.

🧠 Hook/Trap: Some students calculate "fraction of work done" and forget to subtract from 1. Others add fractions incorrectly (1/10 + 1/15 = 5/30 = 1/6, then 3 × 1/6 = 1/2, remaining = 1/2 — correct but error-prone for larger numbers).

Question 3 (Efficiency Ratio)

E is twice as efficient as F. If they both complete a work in 10 days by working together, how many days does E alone take to complete the same work?

(a) 15 days (b) 7.5 days (c) 30 days (d) 10 days

Source: SSC CGL PYQ-
2

Solution:

Efficiency ratio E : F = 2 : 1.

Combined efficiency = 3 units.

They complete work in 10 days → Total work = 3 × 10 = 30 units.

E alone: Time = Total work / E's efficiency = 30/2 = 15 days.

Answer: (a) 15 days.

🧠 Hook/Trap: The most common error is applying the inverse relationship incorrectly. Many think "twice efficient means half the time" and directly halve 10 days to get 5 days. But that would be if E worked alone at half the combined time. The trap here is forgetting that E and F work together, and the combined time is not simply E's or F's individual time. Always use the ratio method to avoid this.

Question 4 (Leaving/Joining)

40 men can complete a piece of work in 18 days. 9 days after they start working together, 5 more men join them. How many more days will the work now take to complete?

(a) 8 days (b) 9 days (c) 7 days (d) 6 days

Source: SSC CHSL PYQ-

Solution:

Total work = 40 × 18 = 720 man-days.

Work done in first 9 days = 40 × 9 = 360 man-days.

Remaining work = 720 - 360 = 360 man-days.

Now, number of men = 40 + 5 = 45.

Days required = 360 / 45 = 8 days.

Answer: (a) 8 days.

🧠 Hook/Trap: Students sometimes use the LCM method and get confused when the number of men changes midway. The man-days approach is cleaner here. Another trap: forgetting to add the 5 men and calculating with 40 men, yielding 360/40 = 9 days (option b).

Question 5 (Alternate Days)

A can do a work in 5 days. B can do the same work in 15 days. If they work on alternate days starting with B, in how many days will they complete thrice the work?

(a) 20 days (b) 21 days (c) 22 days (d) 23 days

Source: SSC CGL PYQ-
2

Solution:

Total work (original) = LCM(5, 15) = 15 units.

A's efficiency = 15/5 = 3 units/day.

B's efficiency = 15/15 = 1 unit/day.

Thrice the work = 3 × 15 = 45 units.

Work pattern (starting B): Day 1 (B): 1 unit, Day 2 (A): 3 units. 2-day cycle = 4 units.

Number of full cycles = 45 / 4 = 11 cycles (44 units) with remainder 1 unit.

11 cycles = 22 days.

Remainder 1 unit: Day 23 (B's turn, as the pattern starts with B). B does 1 unit. Total = 23 days.

Answer: (d) 23 days.

🧠 Hook/Trap: The trick is "thrice the work" — students often calculate for single work (15 units, 15/4 = 3 cycles + remainder = 8 days or similar). Also, starting with B means the remainder on the 23rd day is done by B. Misidentifying whose turn it is for the remainder is a classic error.

Question 6 (Wages)

Hari can do a piece of work in 30 days while Giri can do the same work in 20 days. Working together they complete the work for which they are paid Rs 1000 in total. What is Hari's share?

(a) Rs 400 (b) Rs 500 (c) Rs 600 (d) Rs 300

Source: Railway Group D PYQ-
21

Solution:

Total work = LCM(30, 20) = 60 units.

Hari's efficiency = 60/30 = 2 units/day.

Giri's efficiency = 60/20 = 3 units/day.

Ratio of efficiencies = 2 : 3.

Wages are divided in the ratio of work done. Since both work for the same duration (together), work ratio = efficiency ratio = 2 : 3.

Hari's share = (2/5) × 1000 = Rs 400.

Answer: (a) Rs 400.

🧠 Hook/Trap: Students sometimes divide wages in the inverse ratio of time (i.e., 30:20 = 3:2, giving Hari Rs 600) because they mistakenly think "more time means more money." The correct principle: wages are proportional to work done, which is proportional to efficiency when time is constant.

Question 7 (Men, Women, Boys — Man-Days Equation)

9 men and 12 boys finish a job in 12 days. 12 men and 12 boys finish it in 10 days. In how many days will 10 men and 10 boys finish the job?

(a) 8 days (b) 10 days (c) 12 days (d) None of these

Source: Banking Exams (Repeated)-
59

Solution:

Let 1 man's 1-day work = M, 1 boy's 1-day work = B.

Total work: (9M + 12B) × 12 = (12M + 12B) × 10.

108M + 144B = 120M + 120B.

144B - 120B = 120M - 108M.

24B = 12M → M = 2B.

Now, total work = (9M + 12B) × 12 = (18B + 12B) × 12 = 30B × 12 = 360B.

10 men + 10 boys = 10(2B) + 10B = 30B.

Days = 360B / 30B = 12 days.

Answer: (c) 12 days.

🧠 Hook/Trap: This problem cannot be solved by taking simple LCM of the given days because two different groups are involved. The trap is trying to use the standard LCM method without finding individual efficiencies first. Also, solving for M in terms of B (or vice versa) is essential, but many students struggle with setting up the equations correctly.

C is 40% less efficient than B → C's efficiency = 60.

Ratio A : B : C = 150 : 100 : 60 = 15 : 10 : 6.

Combined efficiency = 15 + 10 + 6 = 31.

Total work = 31 × 20 = 620 units.

Question 9 (Chain of Workers — Combined Equations)

A and C together can complete a work in 120 days, C and E together can complete the same work in 80 days, and A and E together can complete the same work in 160 days. In how many days can A, C and E together complete the same work?

(a) 120/13 days (b) 480/13 days (c) 240/13 days (d) 960/13 days

Source: SSC CGL PYQ-
3

Solution:

Let A, C, and E's 1-day work be a, c, and e.

a + c = 1/120

c + e = 1/80

a + e = 1/160

Adding all three: 2(a + c + e) = 1/120 + 1/80 + 1/160.

LCM of 120, 80, 160 = 480.

1/120 = 4/480, 1/80 = 6/480, 1/160 = 3/480.

Sum = 13/480.

So 2(a + c + e) = 13/480 → a + c + e = 13/960.

Combined time = 1 / (13/960) = 960/13 days.

Answer: (d) 960/13 days.

🧠 Hook/Trap: The mistake here is forgetting to divide the sum by 2. Many students add the three equations and directly take the reciprocal, yielding 480/13 days (option b). The sum is 2(a+c+e), so you must halve it.

Question 10 (M₁D₁H₁/W₁ Variation with Efficiency Drop)

A and B can do a piece of work in 16 days and 15 days respectively. As they were ill, they could do 80% and 60% of their efficiency respectively. How many days will they take to complete the work together?

(a) 10 days (b) 12 days (c) 15 days (d) 20 days

Source: SSC & Railway Exams-
68

Solution:

Total work = LCM(16, 15) = 240 units.

A's normal efficiency = 240/16 = 15 units/day.

A's reduced efficiency = 80% of 15 = 12 units/day.

B's normal efficiency = 240/15 = 16 units/day.

B's reduced efficiency = 60% of 16 = 9.6 units/day.

Combined reduced efficiency = 12 + 9.6 = 21.6 units/day.

Time = 240 / 21.6 = 2400/216 = 100/9 = 11.11 days ≈ 12 days.

Answer: (b) 12 days (approximately).

🧠 Hook/Trap: A common mistake is applying the percentage reduction to the number of days instead of efficiency. For example, making A take 16/0.8 = 20 days instead of reducing efficiency. Efficiency and time are inversely related, so reducing efficiency increases time, but the calculations must be done on efficiency, not directly on days.
