Feynman Explains the Toughest Part of AverageImagine Rahul loves cricket. He’s played 10 innings and his batting average is 45. Now he thinks, “If I score 50 runs in the next innings, my average will become 50, right?”Nope. That’s where almost everyone trips. The real answer is 100 runs. Why? Let me tell you a cookie story.There are 10 kids sitting in a circle. Each kid has a pile of cookies, but they have different amounts. They decide to share equally. After sharing, each kid gets 45 cookies. That’s the average — the equal share.Now, an 11th kid arrives. He doesn’t have any cookies yet. The group wants the new equal share to be 50 cookies for everybody. So what happens? The new kid can’t just bring 50 cookies for himself. If he brings 50, then the total cookies is (10 × 45) + 50 = 450 + 50 = 500. Divide by 11 kids, each gets 500 ÷ 11 ≈ 45.45 — not 50. The average barely budges! Why?Because the new kid’s cookies have to be shared among everyone, including himself. The new equal share of 50 means all 11 kids need 50 cookies each — a total of 550 cookies. They only had 450 before. So the new kid must bring 100 cookies — not just his own 50, but an extra 50 to raise everyone else’s share from 45 to 50. He’s lifting the whole group.Here’s the trick that sticks: The new kid must pay for the entire party, not just his own plate.Whenever you add one new number and want to raise the average, the gap (new average – old average) has to be multiplied by the total number of items after adding.Formula: New kid’s contribution = (Desired new average × New total count) − (Old average × Old count).So: Rahul’s innings = (50 × 11) − (45 × 10) = 550 − 450 = 100.Now it’s your turn: A fruit-seller sold 4 days with an average profit of ₹30 per day. How much profit must he make on the fifth day so that the average profit becomes ₹40?Think: Old total = 4×30 = 120. New total needed for 5 days at ₹40 = 5×40 = 200. So fifth day profit = 200 − 120 = ₹80. He needs ₹80, not ₹40. That’s the party trick in action.Go ahead, try it yourself: If a student’s average score after 6 tests is 70, what must he score on the 7th test to get an average of 75?
