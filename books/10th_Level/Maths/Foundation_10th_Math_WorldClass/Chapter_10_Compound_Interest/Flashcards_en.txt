Flashcard 1
Q: What is the simple yet powerful difference between simple interest and compound interest?
A: Simple interest pays only on the original principal. Compound interest pays on the principal and on the interest that has already been added – it’s interest on interest.

Flashcard 2
Q: ₹1000 at 10% p.a. for 3 years. Simple interest vs. compound interest – what are the final amounts?
A: SI: ₹1300 (₹300 interest). CI: ₹1331 (₹331 interest). The extra ₹31 is the compound effect.

Flashcard 3
Q: Write the golden formula for compound interest when compounding annually.
A: Amount A = P (1 + R/100)^T, and then CI = A – P.

Flashcard 4
Q: Why is the exponent T so magical in A = P (1 + R/100)^T?
A: It stands for repeated multiplication. The money doesn't just grow by adding the same amount each year – it multiplies itself again and again.

Flashcard 5
Q: What does Parrot Tito teach us about the compound interest formula?
A: Tito starts with P and chirps "(1 + R/100)" exactly T times, multiplying as he goes. This reminds you that it's an exponent (repeated multiplication), not addition.

Flashcard 6
Q: How do you tweak the formula for half-yearly compounding?
A: Rate becomes R/2 per half-year, time becomes 2T: A = P (1 + R/200)^(2T).

Flashcard 7
Q: What changes for quarterly compounding?
A: Rate → R/4, time → 4T: A = P (1 + R/400)^(4T).

Flashcard 8
Q: A bank says 20% p.a. compounded half-yearly. What is the real annual rate?
A: Effective annual rate = (1 + 0.20/2)² – 1 = 0.21 = 21%. More frequent compounding gives a higher true return.

Flashcard 9
Q: What is the Rule of 72, and why does it feel like magic?
A: Divide 72 by the interest rate (in %) to find the number of years it takes for money to double. For 10%, 72/10 = 7.2 years. It’s a super-fast mental shortcut.

Flashcard 10
Q: If the interest rate changes every year (e.g., 5%, 10%, 20%), how do you calculate the final amount?
A: Multiply the principal by each year's growth factor in succession:
A = P (1 + R₁/100)(1 + R₂/100)(1 + R₃/100). Order doesn't matter.

Flashcard 11
Q: What's the “pigeon dance” mnemonic for the difference between CI and SI for 2 years?
A: Difference = P × (R/100)². P-Rate-Square. Imagine a dancing pigeon holding a square with the rate written on it.

Flashcard 12
Q: How does the pigeon dance change for a 3-year CI–SI difference?
A: Difference = P × (R/100)² × (3 + R/100). The same pigeon now dances with a big ‘3’ balloon and a rate tag on its wing.

Flashcard 13
Q: A city’s population grows at 10% per year. Is this simple or compound growth?
A: Compound growth. Each year's population becomes the new base for the next year's growth, so we use A = P (1 + R/100)^T.

Flashcard 14
Q: A machine loses 15% of its value every year. How do you calculate its worth after 2 years?
A: Value = Original price × (1 – 15/100)². The minus sign captures depreciation instead of growth.

Flashcard 15
Q: ₹5000 invested at 8% compound interest becomes ₹5832 after 2 years. What is the total compound interest?
A: CI = Amount – Principal = ₹5832 – ₹5000 = ₹832.

Flashcard 16
Q: How do you fairly compare two investments that compound at different frequencies?
A: Convert both to effective annual rates using (1 + r/n)^n – 1, then compare. Higher effective rate means better growth.

Flashcard 17
Q: Using the Rule of 72, how long will money take to double at 9% interest?
A: 72 ÷ 9 = 8 years. The Rule of 72 is your pocket doubling-time calculator.

Flashcard 18
Q: You're unsure whether the formula is (1 + R/100) or just (R/100). How does Parrot Tito fix this?
A: Tito always says "one-plus" first. The "1+" means you keep the original principal. Without it, you'd lose the principal and the formula would be wrong.
