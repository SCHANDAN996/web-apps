Here is the PYQ analysis for Compound Interest at the Foundation level. The focus is on how examiners use psychology to trap students and how to avoid those traps.

Top 10 High-Yield Compound Interest PYQs (With Solutions & Psychological Trap Analysis)
1. The "Half-Yearly" Trap

Question: A bank offers compound interest at the rate of 10% per annum when interest is compounded half-yearly. A customer deposits Rs. 3,200 each on 1st January and 1st July of a year. At the end of the year, what will be the amount received by the customer as interest? (SSC GD Constable, 2022) -
56

Solution:

First deposit (Rs. 3,200 on 1st Jan, 1 full year = 2 half-years):
CI₁ = 3200 (1 + 10/200)² - 3200 = 3200 × 1.1025 - 3200 = 3528 - 3200 = Rs. 328

Second deposit (Rs. 3,200 on 1st July, 6 months = 1 half-year):
CI₂ = 3200 (1 + 10/200) - 3200 = 3200 × 1.05 - 3200 = 3360 - 3200 = Rs. 160

Total Interest = 328 + 160 = Rs. 488

Psychological Hook (The "Straight-Line" Trap): The brain is wired to think in straight lines. Most students calculate interest on the total Rs. 6,400 for 1 year and get Rs. 656. The trap is ignoring that the second deposit earns interest for only six months. The examiner knows you will treat both deposits equally.

2. The "Hidden Time" Trap

Question: A sum of Rs. 7500 amounts to Rs. 9075 at 10% p.a., interest being compounded yearly in a certain time. The simple interest (in Rs.) on the same sum for the same time and the same rate is: (SSC CGL, 16th August 2021 Shift-1) -
3

Solution:

9075 = 7500 (1 + 10/100)ⁿ → 9075/7500 = 1.21 = (1.1)² → n = 2 years.

SI = (7500 × 10 × 2) / 100 = Rs. 1,500

Psychological Hook (The "Incomplete" Illusion): The question deliberately withholds time 'T'. Less confident students panic. But the *ratio A/P = 1.21* is a classic "2-year signature" for 10%. Examiners use this to test whether you recognize patterns or get lost looking for a missing variable.

3. The "Mindless Formula" Trap

Question: The difference between the compound interest and simple interest on a sum of money at the rate of 10% per annum for 2 years is Rs. 50. The principal amount is: (SSC/ Railway, 2023) -
50

Solution:

For 2 years, CI – SI = P (R/100)²

50 = P (10/100)² → 50 = P × (0.1)² → 50 = P × 0.01 → P = Rs. 5,000

Psychological Hook (The "Jugglery" Trap): Examiners know students reflexively use A = P(1+R/100)ᵀ and drown in calculations. The intent is to test if you grasp the shortcut principle. If you don't know that CI – SI = P(R/100)² for 2 years, the exam loses you valuable minutes.

4. The "Double in T Years (Doubling Principle)" Trap

Question: A sum of Rs. 12,000 deposited at compound interest becomes double after 5 years. How much will it be after 20 years? (SSC CGL) -
5

Solution:

20 years = 4 periods of 5 years. Each period doubles the amount.

After 20 years: 12000 × 2⁴ = 12000 × 16 = Rs. 1,92,000

(Avoid the error: 12000 × 2 × 4 = Rs. 96,000 — this is the linear-thinking trap.)

Psychological Hook (The "Linear vs. Exponential" Trap): The human brain is linear. The first instinct is 5 years → double, so 20 years → quadruple. The examiner exploits the default assumption that growth is additive. This question separates thinkers who understand recursive multiplication from those who just multiply by 4.

5. The "Successive Rate Change" Trap

Question: What is the compound interest obtained on a sum of Rs. 1,200 after two years, if the rate of interest for the first year is 5% and for the second year is 10%? (IBPS PO, 2019) -
21

Solution:

A = 1200 × (1 + 5/100) × (1 + 10/100) = 1200 × 1.05 × 1.10 = 1200 × 1.155 = Rs. 1,386

CI = 1386 – 1200 = Rs. 186

Psychological Hook (The "Averaging" Trap): The mind wants to take the simple average (7.5%) and plug it into the formula. This gives a wrong answer. The examiner tests whether you understand that compound interest in successive rates is a chain of multiplications, not a single average rate.

6. The "Ratio Decoding" Trap

Question: A sum of money amounts to Rs. 16,900 in 2 years and to Rs. 17,576 in 3 years at compound interest. Find the rate of interest per annum. (RRB NTPC) -
33

Solution:

The ratio of amounts = A₃ / A₂ = 17576 / 16900 = 1.04

This means the amount grew by a factor of 1.04 in one year.

1 + R/100 = 1.04 → R = 4%

Psychological Hook (The "Need Principal" Trap): Students are conditioned to think they need P, R, and T to solve. The examiner removes P and T. The smart insight is that the ratio of consecutive amounts directly gives the growth factor. The question tests pattern recognition, not formula memory.

7. The "Tree Growth" Trap (Application)

Question: A tree increases annually by 1/8th of its height. By how much will it increase after 2.5 years, if it stands today 10 ft. high? (SSC, 2007) -
20

Solution:

Rate = (1/8) × 100 = 12.5% p.a.

For 2.5 years, use: A = P (1 + R/100)² × (1 + (R/2)/100) for the half-year.

A = 10 × (1.125)² × (1 + 12.5/200) = 10 × 1.265625 × 1.0625 = 13.45 ft.

Increase = 13.45 – 10 = 3.45 ft.

Psychological Hook (The "Money-Only" Trap): Students associate compound interest exclusively with money. When the same formula appears in a biology context ("tree height"), the brain fails to retrieve the relevant schema. Examiners use this to test abstraction power. Compound interest is just multiplicative growth; the substrate doesn't matter.

8. The "Installment Loan" Trap

Question: A sum borrowed at compound interest rate of 7% per annum is paid in two annual installments of Rs. 57,245 each. What was the principal? (SSC & Railway, 2024) -

Solution:

P = Installment₁/(1 + R/100) + Installment₂/(1 + R/100)²

P = 57245/1.07 + 57245/(1.07)²

P = 53500 + 50000 = Rs. 1,03,500

Psychological Hook (The "Simple Splitting" Trap): The untrained mind simply adds the installments: 57245 × 2 = Rs. 1,14,490. The examiner exploits the fact that most aspirants don't understand the time value of money. Money paid later is worth less today. This question tests the present value concept disguised as CI.

9. The "CI on CI (Reinvestment)" Trap

Question: A person invests Rs. 50,000 at 10% p.a. compounded annually for 3 years. He then reinvests the amount for 2 more years at 12% p.a. compounded annually. What is the total amount? (SSC Pattern) -

Solution:

Phase 1: A = 50000 (1.1)³ = 50000 × 1.331 = Rs. 66,550

Phase 2: A = 66550 (1.12)² = 66550 × 1.2544 = Rs. 83,488.92

Psychological Hook (The "Rate Switch" Trap): Students calculate both phases separately and add the interests, or worse, try to average the rates. The examiner wants you to understand that the amount from Phase 1 becomes the principal for Phase 2. This is a test of process-order logic.

10. The "Effective Rate" Trap

Question: A bank offers 12% compound interest on a sum of Rs. 10,000 compounded every quarter. What will be the approximate interest earned at the end of 1 year? (SSC CHSL) -

Solution:

Quarterly: R/4 = 3%, time = 4 quarters.

A = 10000 (1 + 3/100)⁴ = 10000 × 1.125509 = Rs. 11,255.09

CI = 11255.09 – 10000 = Rs. 1,255 (approx)

Psychological Hook (The "12% is 12%" Trap): The naive brain thinks 12% annual compounding quarterly means 3% per quarter × 4 = 12%, which seems the same as annual. But (1.03)⁴ – 1 = 12.55%. The extra 0.55% is the compounding on compounding within the year. Examiners love testing whether you mechanically divide R by 4 but forget the exponent changes the outcome entirely.
