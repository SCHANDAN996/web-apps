🪄 Short Tricks — Percentage (English)

Trick 1: Centipede’s Century Board
Picture a bright-yellow centipede with 100 tiny legs, each wearing a sock labeled 1/100. Whenever you see %, the centipede lifts a wooden board that says "/100". So 45% becomes 45/100 instantly. “Cent” = 100 (century, centipede). The weird image of a centipede lifting a board makes the word “percent” unforgettable.

Trick 2: The 100-Floor Elevator
To convert any fraction to percent, imagine the numerator is a person and the denominator is the elevator’s floor. The person wants to go to floor 100. The elevator multiplies the fraction by 100. Example: 3/5 — the person (3) takes the elevator: (3×100)/5 = 300/5 = 60%. Always “multiply by 100 and divide.” The elevator always reaches the 100th floor.

Trick 3: The Quarterback’s Throne
25% = 1/4. Imagine a quarterback (American football) sitting on a throne, because a quarter is 25 cents, and he’s the “quarter” back. So whenever you see 25%, you see a quarterback throwing a 1/4-shaped ball. Similarly, 50% = 1/2: a half-eaten cake at a 50th birthday party with “Half-century” written on it. 75% = 3/4: three quarters of a pizza, so a pizza with 3 slices left out of 4.

Trick 4: The Pizza Slicer for 12.5%
12.5% = 1/8. Imagine a giant pizza cut into 8 slices. You take one slice, and the waiter shouts, “You took 12.5 percent!” Because an 8-slice pizza makes each slice 1/8. Remember 12.5 is half of 25 (1/4), and twice 4 gives 8. So 12.5% = 1/8. If in doubt, picture the pizza slicer dividing the circle into 8.

Trick 5: The Three-Eyed Monster for 1/3 and 33.33%
1/3 = 33.33%. Visualize a three-eyed monster; each eye represents 33.33% of its face’s vision. Together they make 100%. When you think 1/3, immediately see that monster’s three floating 33.33s. And 2/3 = 66.67% — two of its eyes open.

Trick 6: “X’s of Y” Become a Multiplication Dance
“X% of Y” translates to (X/100) × Y. Imagine the percentage sign as a dance instruction: “Divide me by 100, then multiply with Y.” Picture a dance floor where the % symbol spins, cuts itself into a fraction over 100, and then grabs Y and multiplies. So 30% of 200: 30/100 × 200 = 30×2 = 60. The “of” becomes multiplication music.

Trick 7: The Jealous Original (for Increase/Decrease)
In % change, the denominator is always the original number. The original is “jealous” and demands that all changes are measured relative to it. If price goes from 50 to 70, change = 20, original = 50, % = (20/50)×100 = 40%. The story: “The original says, ‘You left me, but I’m still the reference point!’” Always base on the jealous original.

Trick 8: A + B + AB/100 — The “AB on the Hundredth Floor”
For two successive changes of A% and B%, net = A + B + (A×B)/100. Imagine A and B are two friends who meet on the 100th floor. First they add normally (A+B), then they multiply their initials (A×B) and put that product on floor 100 (divide by 100). If either is negative, the product will be negative. For +20% and -10%, net = 20 -10 + (20×-10)/100 = 10 -2 = +8%. The “AB elevator” always stops at the 100th floor.

Trick 9: Yo-Yo Trap — The r²/100 Ghost
If a value increases by r% and then decreases by the same r%, you always lose (r²/100)%. Think of a Yo-Yo that goes down and up with the same string length, but never lands exactly in your palm; it stops a tiny bit lower. For r=20, loss = 400/100 = 4%. The ghost of the square steals that percent. Every time you see equal up-down, whisper “r-square ghost steals 4 percent” (adjust to r).

Trick 10: Multiplier Masks
Instead of thinking in % changes, think in multipliers. An increase is a “growth mask”: (1 + p/100). A decrease is a “shrink mask”: (1 - q/100). To string them, just wear masks in sequence. For +25% put on mask 1.25, for -20% put on mask 0.80. Net mask = 1.25 × 0.80 = 1.00 = no change. Imagine a masquerade ball where each change gives you a mask; multiply to see your final face.

Trick 11: Decimal Tango — Two Steps Right, Two Steps Left
Decimal to percent: shift decimal two places right (like dancing two tango steps right). 0.45 → 45%. Percent to decimal: shift two steps left (back). 7% → 0.07. Add zeros if needed. Picture a couple dancing: “Right-right for % rise, Left-left for decimal disguise.”

Trick 12: 1% Lazy Walker
To find 1% of any number, the decimal point walks two lazy steps left. 1% of 2500: from 2500. → 25.00 (25). It’s as if the number goes to sleep and the decimal tiptoes two steps to the left. Then for 2%, double that, etc.

Trick 13: The Bounce-Back Boiler
If a price is reduced by p%, to restore it you must increase by not p%, but (p/(100-p))×100%. Think of a basketball: you compress it by a percentage, and to bounce back to original height, you need a bigger percentage because the new base is smaller. For 20% drop, bounce-back = (20/(80))×100 = 25%. So a “20% off” sticker means to undo the discount, you’d need a 25% hike. The ball bounces back higher on a percentage scale.

Trick 14: Apple-Orange Slow Dance (Different Bases)
You can’t average percentages with different bases; you must get actual values. Two dancers—Apple and Orange—hold cards with different “out of” numbers. They can only slow dance if you first convert to actual scores, sum them, then find total %. Otherwise, mixing them is a forbidden fruit salad. Exam traps: always find the real fruits.

Trick 15: Golden Key Rings
Memorize these fraction-percent pairs by putting them on imaginary key rings:

· 1/2 = 50% (half a century key)
· 1/4 = 25% (quarterback key)
· 1/8 = 12.5% (pizza slice key)
· 1/10 = 10% (dime key)
· 1/20 = 5% (nickel key)
· 1/12 = 8.33% (clock key: a clock has 12 hours, each hour is 8.33% of the day)
· 1/11 ≈ 9.09% (two large 9s like twin towers on a 11-shaped base).
  Whenever you see these percentages, grab the right key from your mental keychain.
