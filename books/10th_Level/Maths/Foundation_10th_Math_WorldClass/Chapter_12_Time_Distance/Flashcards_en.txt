Flashcard 1
Front: What are the three characters in the Time & Distance triangle, and what is the one master formula that rules them all?
Back: Distance (D), Speed (S), Time (T). Master formula: Distance = Speed × Time.

Flashcard 2
Front: If you cover 'D' with your thumb in the DST triangle, what do the remaining two letters tell you?
Back: Speed × Time. So, Distance = Speed × Time.

Flashcard 3
Front: You hide 'S' in the triangle. What operation do the visible D and T perform?
Back: D over T, i.e., D ÷ T. So, Speed = Distance ÷ Time.

Flashcard 4
Front: To convert km/h into m/s, what fraction do you multiply by?
Back: 5/18.

Flashcard 5
Front: What is the weird lizard-and-centipede trick to remember the km/h to m/s conversion?
Back: A 5-headed lizard (5) eats an 18-legged centipede (18). Multiply by 5/18 when going km/h → m/s. Reverse (spit out) for m/s → km/h: multiply by 18/5.

Flashcard 6
Front: Convert 90 km/h into m/s.
Back: 90 × (5/18) = 25 m/s.

Flashcard 7
Front: Convert 10 m/s into km/h.
Back: 10 × (18/5) = 36 km/h.

Flashcard 8
Front: Can you always find average speed by adding two speeds and dividing by 2?
Back: No! Only if the time spent at each speed is the same. The true formula is Average Speed = Total Distance ÷ Total Time.

Flashcard 9
Front: A car goes from A to B at 40 km/h and returns from B to A at 60 km/h. What formula gives the average speed for the whole trip?
Back: When distances are equal, Average speed = 2xy / (x + y). Here, 2×40×60 / (40+60) = 4800/100 = 48 km/h.

Flashcard 10
Front: Two cars are moving in the SAME direction. How do you find how fast one seems to be moving relative to the other?
Back: Relative speed = Speed of faster car – Speed of slower car.

Flashcard 11
Front: Two trains are moving in OPPOSITE directions towards each other. What is their relative speed?
Back: Relative speed = Speed₁ + Speed₂. Their speeds add up because they are closing the gap together.

Flashcard 12
Front: When a train crosses a pole or a man, what distance does it actually cover?
Back: Only its own length. Distance = Length of train.

Flashcard 13
Front: When a train crosses a platform, tunnel, or bridge, what distance does it cover?
Back: Distance = Length of train + Length of platform/tunnel/bridge.

Flashcard 14
Front: A train crosses a pole in 8 seconds. Which distance formula applies here?
Back: Length of train = Speed × 8.

Flashcard 15
Front: A train crosses a pole in t₁ seconds and a platform of length P in t₂ seconds. What magic does the time difference (t₂ – t₁) reveal?
Back: (t₂ – t₁) is the time the train takes to cover ONLY the platform's length. So, Speed = P ÷ (t₂ – t₁).

Flashcard 16
Front: What is the traffic signal mnemonic for recalling the DST relationship?
Back: Imagine a traffic signal — Red on top is D, Yellow in middle is S, Green at bottom is T. Cover the one you need; the other two show the formula.

Flashcard 17
Front: Two trains of lengths L₁ and L₂ are crossing each other in opposite directions. What total distance must be covered for them to completely pass each other?
Back: Distance = L₁ + L₂. The relative speed is S₁ + S₂.

Flashcard 18
Front: What is the single, eternal formula that never fails for Average Speed?
Back: Average Speed = Total Distance Travelled ÷ Total Time Taken. Use it everywhere, especially when distances or times are uneven.
