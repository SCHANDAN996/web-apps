Front: What do you get when you tear off the three corners of any paper triangle and place their tips together?
Back: A straight line — exactly 180°. The three angles of a flat triangle always add up to 180°.

Front: If two lines intersect, which angles are always equal?
Back: Vertically opposite angles. They're the ones facing each other directly.

Front: A line cuts across two parallel lines. What do we call the equal angles that sit in the same position at each intersection?
Back: Corresponding angles.

Front: When a transversal crosses two parallel lines, what's special about the pair of alternate interior angles?
Back: They are equal. Like a "Z" shape inside.

Front: What's the one-word memory trick to recall that an acute angle is smaller than 90°?
Back: "A-cute angel" — like a cute little angel, small.

Front: How do you quickly remember the difference between an obtuse angle and an acute one?
Back: "Obese-tuse" — obese means fat, so a large angle >90°.

Front: What is the mnemonic "I-saw-two-equal" used to remember?
Back: It reminds you that an isosceles triangle has two equal sides (I-saw-two-equal).

Front: All sides and angles equal — which triangle is this, and what's the memory trick?
Back: Equilateral. Mnemonic: "Every Edge Equal" — all three E's.

Front: A right-angled triangle has sides 3 and 4. Without calculating, what is the hypotenuse? Why?
Back: 5. Because of the 3-4-5 Pythagorean triple, the rope trick from ancient Egypt.

Front: What's the unforgettable formula for the area of a circle, and what's the weird memory image?
Back: Area = πr². Mnemonic: "Pi R squared, the circle's face; pi R squared, it takes that space." (Imagine a pie face.)

Front: How do you instantly recall the circumference formula in a funny way?
Back: "Two pie are round" — 2πr. Picture two round pies.

Front: A slice of pizza is part of a circle. How do you find its area if the central angle is 60°?
Back: Area of sector = (60/360) × πr² = (1/6) × πr².

Front: What single rule gives you the sum of interior angles of any polygon?
Back: (n – 2) × 180°, where n is the number of sides. Think of splitting into triangles.

Front: A rhombus has diagonals of 8 cm and 6 cm. What's its area?
Back: 24 cm². Area = ½ × d₁ × d₂ = ½ × 8 × 6 = 24.

Front: A cone and a cylinder have the same base and height. How much of the cylinder's volume is the cone's? What's the mnemonic?
Back: One-third. Mnemonic: "Cone is one-third of a cylinder."

Front: A cube and a sphere have the same surface area. Which one has greater volume, and how do you remember without math?
Back: The sphere. Because a sphere is the most efficient shape for holding volume — a "trap" question. No calculation needed.

Front: What's the fast way to check if a triangle with sides 6, 8, 10 is right-angled?
Back: Verify if 6² + 8² = 10² — i.e., 36 + 64 = 100. Yes, it's a right triangle. This is another multiple of the 3-4-5 triple.

Front: A trapezium has parallel sides of 10 m and 6 m, and height 5 m. How do you compute its area?
Back: Area = ½ × (10 + 6) × 5 = ½ × 16 × 5 = 40 m². Average of parallel sides times height.
