Q4. LCM of Decimals (SSC GD 2024)

Question: Find the LCM of 1.2, 1.8, 2, and 2.5.

Options: (a) 25 (b) 30 (c) 90 (d) 120

Solution:
Step 1 — Remove decimals: write as fractions with denominator 10.
1.2 = 12/10, 1.8 = 18/10, 2 = 20/10, 2.5 = 25/10

Step 2 — Find LCM of numerators: 12, 18, 20, 25
Prime factors:

12 = 2² × 3

18 = 2 × 3²

20 = 2² × 5

25 = 5²

LCM = 2² × 3² × 5² = 4 × 9 × 25 = 900

Step 3 — Divide by denominator (10): 900 ÷ 10 = 90

Answer: (c) 90

🧠 Psychological Hook: Many students compute LCM of 12, 18, 20, 25 = 900 and stop, picking option (d) 120 (which is wrong). The trap is forgetting to divide back by 10 because the original numbers were decimals. Others might pick 30 (half of 60) thinking LCM of decimals works like fractions.

Source: SSC GD 2024 (Feb 27, Shift III)-

Q5. Original Fraction — % Change (IBPS PO 2019)

Question: If the numerator of a fraction is increased by 80% and the denominator is increased by 120%, the resultant fraction becomes 5/11. Find the original fraction.

Options: (a) 5/9 (b) 3/7 (c) 5/12 (d) 7/16

Solution:
Let original fraction = N/D.
Increased numerator = N × 180/100 = 1.8N
Increased denominator = D × 220/100 = 2.2D

So, 1.8N / 2.2D = 5/11
⇒ 18N / 22D = 5/11
⇒ 9N / 11D = 5/11
⇒ 9N = 5D
⇒ N/D = 5/9

Answer: (a) 5/9

🧠 Psychological Hook: The percentage increase trap: "increased by 80%" means becomes 180% (1.8×), NOT 0.8×. Many students mistakenly use 0.8 and 1.2, getting N/D = (5/11) × (1.2/0.8) = wrong answer. Also, students often confuse "increased by" with "increased to" — a single-word difference that changes the multiplier entirely.

Q7. HCF of Decimals (SSC GD 2023)

Question: Find the HCF of 0.25, 2.08, and 8.1.

Options: (a) 0.01 (b) 0.02 (c) 0.05 (d) 0.1

Solution:
Step 1 — Equalise decimal places: 0.25, 2.08, 8.10
Step 2 — Multiply by 100 to remove decimals: 25, 208, 810
Step 3 — Prime factorisation:

25 = 5²

208 = 2⁴ × 13

810 = 2 × 3⁴ × 5
Common factor = 1

Step 4 — Adjust back: HCF = 1 ÷ 100 = 0.01

Answer: (a) 0.01

🧠 Psychological Hook: Students often compute HCF = 1 and pick option (d) 0.1, forgetting that the decimal needs to be scaled back. The trap lies in the option set — 0.1 is prominently placed as a distractor, and a hurried student will select it confidently. Another trap: not equalising decimal places first, leading to wrong integer conversion.

Source: SSC GD 2023 (Feb 13, Shift IV)-

Q8. Decimal of an Hour (SSC CHSL/MTS)

Question: What decimal of an hour is a second?

Options: (a) 0.0025 (b) 0.0256 (c) 0.00027 (d) 0.000126

Solution:
1 hour = 60 minutes = 3600 seconds.
1 second = 1/3600 of an hour.
1/3600 = 0.0002777... ≈ 0.00027

Answer: (c) 0.00027

🧠 Psychological Hook: The unit conversion trap: students use 1 hour = 60 seconds (wrong), getting 1/60 = 0.0167, then panic when the answer isn't in the options. Others divide 1 by 3600 correctly but misplace the decimal point, selecting 0.0025 (option a). The key is knowing that 1/3600 is a very small number — three zeros after the decimal.

Q9. Fraction Equation — Original Fraction (SSC CGL 2000 / CHSL)

Question: Sum of four times a fraction and 6 times its reciprocal is 11. What is the fraction?

Options: (a) 3/4 (b) 4/3 (c) 4/7 (d) 7/4

Solution:
Let the fraction = x.
Then, 4x + 6/x = 11
Multiply both sides by x: 4x² + 6 = 11x
⇒ 4x² – 11x + 6 = 0
⇒ 4x² – 8x – 3x + 6 = 0
⇒ 4x(x – 2) – 3(x – 2) = 0
⇒ (4x – 3)(x – 2) = 0
⇒ x = 3/4 or x = 2

Since x is a fraction (and typically less than 1 for such problems), x = 3/4.

Answer: (a) 3/4

🧠 Psychological Hook: The equation gives two possible answers — 3/4 and 2. Option (d) 7/4 tempts students who make an algebraic error in the quadratic. The trap is not checking which answer fits the context. Also, students forget that a reciprocal exists and try to solve by guessing, wasting time.

Source: SSC CHSL Number System Set-3-

🧠 Master Trap Matrix — 7 Psychological Patterns
#	Trap Name	How It Works	Defence
1	The Identity Blind Spot	Complex expression hides a simple identity (a³+b³, (a–b)²). You brute-force.	Before solving, scan for algebraic patterns.
2	The "of" Operator Ambush	"Of" means multiply and has high priority. You treat it as ÷ or ignore BODMAS.	VBODMAS: V→B→O→D→M→A→S. Circle every "of".
3	The Percentage Multiplier Switcheroo	"Increased by 80%" means ×1.8, not ×0.8. You use the wrong multiplier.	Increased by P% = (100+P)/100. Decreased by P% = (100–P)/100.
4	The Decimal Scaling Amnesia	You find LCM/HCF of integers correctly, then forget to scale the result back.	Before final answer, ask: "Did I multiply or divide earlier? Reverse it."
5	The Visual Size Illusion	13/20 looks bigger than 11/15 because numbers are larger. You judge by appearance.	Always convert to decimals or cross-multiply. Never trust visual intuition.
6	The 0.999... Equivalence Shock	0.3̄9̄ = 0.3999... = 0.4. You treat it as a regular mixed recurring decimal.	If the repeating digit is 9, the number simplifies to a terminating decimal.
7	The Option Mirror Trap	A distractor option matches a partial result. You stop early and pick it.	Solve fully, then match. Never reverse-engineer from options unless stuck.
📈 10-Year Trend: What's Changing?
Trend	Detail
Recurring decimals rising	From ~1 question/year (2015) to ~3 questions/year (2024–25). SSC especially favours mixed recurring.
BODMAS complexity increasing	Earlier: 2–3 operations. Now: 5–7 nested operations with mixed fractions.
Visual identity masking	Questions embed (a+b)², a³+b³, (a–b)² inside messy decimals. Pattern recognition is now more important than calculation speed.
Percentage-fraction hybrid	Banking exams increasingly combine fraction concepts with percentage change, testing two skills in one question.

Bottom line: In the last 10 years, 7 out of every 10 fraction-decimal PYQs test pattern recognition, not calculation skill.
